# Applying the steering and sailing rules — a repeatable decision sequence

**Goal:** for every vessel you meet, decide fast and correctly: is there risk of
collision, which rule applies, what's my role, and what action is early, substantial
and safe. Rule numbers below are from the International Regulations for Preventing
Collisions at Sea (COLREGs); the US Inland Rules (33 CFR Part 83) use the same rule numbers, and exceptions for the Great Lakes and Western Rivers apply only inland.

## 1. Detect (Rule 5) and use safe speed (Rule 6)
Proper lookout by all means. Safe speed considers visibility, traffic density,
manoeuvrability (stopping distance, turning), background lights, wind/sea/current,
draught vs depth, and radar limitations.

## 2. Assess risk (Rule 7)
- **Compass bearing not appreciably changing + range decreasing = risk.** Take
  bearings with a compass/repeater or EBL over a few minutes.
- Risk may exist even with a changing bearing — large ships or close range.
- Use radar plotting/ARPA properly; don't assume on scanty information.

## 3. Classify (sector of the other vessel's lights tells you a lot)
- **Overtaking (Rule 13):** you approach from more than 22.5° abaft her beam (you'd
  see only her stern light at night). The overtaking vessel keeps clear — always,
  whatever the vessel types.
- **Head-on (Rule 14):** reciprocal or nearly reciprocal courses (masthead lights
  in line or nearly, both sidelights). **Both alter to starboard.**
- **Crossing (Rule 15):** the vessel which has the other **on her own starboard
  side** gives way, and should avoid crossing ahead.
- Not in sight in restricted visibility → none of these apply; Rule 19 does.

## 4. Responsibilities between vessels (Rule 18)
Generally (except Rules 9, 10, 13): power-driven gives way to — not under command,
restricted in ability to manoeuvre, constrained by draught (avoid impeding), engaged
in fishing, sailing. In narrow channels (Rule 9) and TSS (Rule 10), small and sailing
vessels shall not impede vessels that can only navigate within them.

## 5. Act
**Give-way (Rule 16, 8):** early and substantial, readily apparent: a large
alteration of course (often ≥ 30°, commonly to starboard, passing astern of the
other) or reduce speed/stop. A series of small alterations is not apparent on radar.
Check the action works — re-check bearing and CPA until finally past and clear.
**Stand-on (Rule 17):** keep course and speed. **May** act when it becomes apparent the
give-way vessel isn't acting — then, if crossing, avoid altering to port for a vessel
on your own port side. **Must** take best action when collision can't be avoided by
the give-way vessel alone. Five short blasts (Rule 34(d)) to signal doubt.

## 6. Restricted visibility (Rule 19)
- Safe speed for the conditions; engines ready for immediate manoeuvre.
- Detected by radar only and risk exists → take avoiding action in ample time, but
  avoid: altering to port for a vessel forward of the beam (other than one being
  overtaken), and altering toward a vessel abeam or abaft the beam.
- Hearing a fog signal apparently forward of the beam → reduce to bare steerageway, or
  stop, until danger is over.
- Sound signals (Rule 35): power-driven making way: one prolonged every ≤ 2 min.

## 7. Lights and shapes quick check (Part C)
Masthead white(s) 225° forward; sidelights red port/green starboard 112.5°; stern light
135°. NUC: two all-round red; RAM: red-white-red; constrained by draught: three red
(optional, regional); at anchor: all-round white (shape: one ball); aground: anchor
lights + two red. Fishing (trawling): green over white; other fishing: red over white.

## Gotchas
- VHF agreements contrary to the rules ("let's pass starboard to starboard") have
  caused collisions — misidentified vessels; use VHF cautiously.
- Altering to port in a close-quarters crossing situation is a common fatal error.
- AIS heading/speed can be wrong — rely on radar plotting and visual bearings.

## Verify
- [ ] Risk assessed by compass bearing trend and CPA, not by guess.
- [ ] Situation classified and your role (give-way/stand-on) stated.
- [ ] Action large, early, and checked until past and clear.
- [ ] In fog: no port alteration for a vessel forward of the beam; safe speed and signals.

*Uncertainty:* "≥30°" is a common teaching figure, not in the rules; the rules require action to be readily apparent.
