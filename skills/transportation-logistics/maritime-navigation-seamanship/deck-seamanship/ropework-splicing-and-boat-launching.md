# Ropework, splicing and boat launching — the basics that keep working

**Goal:** tie the right knot first time, make a strong eye in rope, rig loads within
safe working limits, and launch and recover a ship's rescue boat or lifeboat safely.

## 1. Seven knots that cover most deck work
| Knot | Use | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Bowline | fixed loop at the end | easy to untie after load; can shake loose unloaded |
| Clove hitch | quick temporary hitch to a rail | slips under varying load — back it up |
| Round turn & two half hitches | secure a line to a ring/post under load | can be tied under tension |
| Rolling hitch | grip a line or spar along its length (e.g. take load off a mooring line onto a stopper) | pull toward the turns |
| Sheet bend (double for slippery/different sizes) | join two ropes | |
| Figure-of-eight | stopper knot | |
| Reef knot | binding only (sail ties, bundles) | never to join ropes under load |
Practise until you can tie each without looking and in the dark.

## 2. Three-strand eye splice
1. Unlay ~4 tucks' worth (≈ 3× rope circumference per tuck; whip or tape the strand ends).
2. Form the eye to the size you want. Lay the three strands on the standing part: middle
   strand on top.
3. Tuck the **middle** strand under one strand of the standing part, **against the lay**.
4. Tuck the **left** strand under the next strand to the left (over the one the middle
   went under).
5. Turn the splice over; tuck the **last** strand under the remaining strand — each
   strand now emerges from a different place.
6. Continue over-one-under-one: minimum **3 full tucks** for natural fibre, **5 for
   synthetic** (slippery). Taper last tucks if wanted, roll under foot to bed in.
A well-made splice keeps ~90% of rope strength; a knot can lose 40–50%.

## 3. Whipping
Common whipping: lay a loop of twine along the rope, wrap tightly toward the end for
a length ≈ rope diameter, pass the end through the loop and pull the loop under the
turns; trim. Synthetic rope: heat-seal as well.

## 4. Rigging loads safely
- Know **MBL** (minimum breaking load) and **SWL/WLL** (safe working load): SWL =
  MBL / safety factor (often 5–6 for wire/fibre slings, check the certificate).
- Every shackle, block, sling and wire has a certificate and marked SWL; condemn
  damaged gear (kinks, birdcaging, broken wires, cut/fused fibres, deformed shackles).
- Sling angles increase leg load: at 60° included angle each leg carries ≈ 58% of the
  load; at 120° ≈ 100% — keep included angles ≤ 90°.
- Mouse shackle pins on long-term rigging; never stand under a suspended load.

## 5. Launching a rescue boat / lifeboat (davit)
Follow the ship's posted instructions and SOLAS drills; typical sequence:
1. Crew in lifejackets/immersion suits as required; boat crew briefed; bridge
   informed; ship slowed/stopped, lee made.
2. Check boat: plug in, painter rigged forward and led well forward, engine starts
   (if allowed on deck), harness/securing gear released, gripes off.
3. Lower to embarkation deck (if applicable), embark, lower to water steadily with the
   brake; keep tension on the painter.
4. On water: release falls — **on-load release hooks** only per procedure (hydrostatic
   interlock indicates waterborne); off-load release normally. Release painter last
   and steer away from the ship's side.
5. Recovery: approach into the painter, connect falls/hook properly (check hooks
   fully reset and locked — incorrectly reset on-load hooks have killed crews),
   hoist, secure, stow.

## 6. Small-boat handling alongside the ship
Come alongside into wind/current at slow speed, use the painter to hold position,
keep hands and feet inside the gunwale when coming against the hull.

## Gotchas
- Reef knot used as a bend under load — it capsizes.
- Too few tucks in synthetic splice — it pulls out.
- Hooks not correctly reset before hoisting a lifeboat.

- [ ] Splice has the right number of tucks and holds a test load.
- [ ] All lifting gear certified, inspected and within SWL.
