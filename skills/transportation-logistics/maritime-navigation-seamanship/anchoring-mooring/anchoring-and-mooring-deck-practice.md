# Anchoring and mooring deck practice — hold the ship, keep the crew whole

**Goal:** anchor where the ship will hold and swing clear, detect dragging early, and
moor alongside with a balanced line plan while keeping people out of the path of
parting lines. One shackle of cable = 15 fathoms = 27.5 m (UK/international); the US
"shot" is 90 ft (27.4 m) — check your windlass markings.

## 1. Choosing the anchorage
- Charted anchorage or a spot with good holding (mud, sand, clay good; rock, weed, shell poor).
- Depth that suits the windlass (most ships safely recover in up to ~80–100 m with
  limited cable out; deeper anchoring needs walking back all the way).
- Swinging room: **swing radius ≈ length of cable out (horizontal) + ship's length**,
  plus a safety margin to dangers and other ships' circles.
- Shelter from forecast wind/swell; clear of cables, pipelines, fairways.

## 2. How much cable
Rules of thumb (companies vary):
- Fair weather, good holding: cable length ≈ 4 × depth, or shackles ≈ 1.5 × √depth(m) is
  sometimes used for deeper water.
- Rising wind/poor holding: 6–10 × depth.
The aim: enough catenary that the pull on the anchor is horizontal. Not so much that
the swing circle hits something.

## 3. Anchoring procedure
1. Approach into wind/current at slow speed, plan the final heading and drop position.
2. Anchor cleared away, brake on, gear disengaged — or **walked back** with the
   windlass engaged to just above the bottom (mandatory in deep water and often by
   company policy for large ships; letting go from the hawse in deep water can lose
   the anchor and cable).
3. At the position, ship stopped over the ground and starting to make **sternway**,
   let go/walk out; lay cable along the bottom as the ship drops back — don't pile it.
4. Report cable direction and tension ("leading 2 o'clock, moderate strain").
5. Anchor **brought up**: cable rises and then slackens as the ship comes forward
   onto it — anchor holding. Then brake on and stopper/guillotine secured.
6. Fix the anchor position immediately; record the **drop position** (not the bridge).

## 4. Anchor watch
- Set ECDIS/GNSS anchor watch circle on the anchor position (radius = cable + distance
  from GNSS antenna to hawse + margin).
- Check position by radar ranges/bearings and transits regularly; watch for a steady
  movement in one direction (dragging) rather than swing.
- Engines on short notice in rising wind; heave up or veer more cable early. A second
  anchor dropped underfoot can arrest a drag in an emergency.

## 5. Mooring alongside
- **Line plan:** headlines and sternlines resist longitudinal and off-berth forces;
  **breast lines** (perpendicular) hold the ship to the quay most efficiently;
  **springs** stop fore-and-aft surge. Same material and similar length for lines doing
  the same job — mixed lines share load badly (the stiffest takes it all).
- Brake holding capacity of winches set to ~60% of line MBL (OCIMF MEG4 guidance) so
  the brake slips before the line parts; test per schedule.
- Tend lines for tide and draught changes; avoid lines going slack then shock-loaded.

## 6. Snap-back zones
- A parting synthetic line recoils with lethal force along and around its lead —
  especially around fairleads and rollers. Painted zones on deck are **not** a guarantee
  (MEG4 now emphasises assessing the whole mooring deck as hazardous).
- Stand clear of the bight and lines under tension; never step over a line; communicate
  with the bridge before heaving.

## Gotchas
- "Short stay" mistaken for holding — wait for bring-up.
- Anchor watch circle centred on the GNSS antenna instead of the anchor.
- Mixed lines, or lines with different leads, overloading one line.
- HMPE lines have low stretch — shock loads go straight to fittings.

## Verify
- [ ] Depth, holding ground, swing radius and cable length planned before arrival.
- [ ] Anchor brought up, position fixed, anchor watch circle set correctly.
- [ ] Line plan balanced; winch brakes rendering set and tested.
- [ ] Crew briefed on snap-back hazards before mooring ops.

*Uncertainty:* scope ratios and windlass depth limits vary by ship and company; follow the anchoring and mooring procedures in your SMS.
