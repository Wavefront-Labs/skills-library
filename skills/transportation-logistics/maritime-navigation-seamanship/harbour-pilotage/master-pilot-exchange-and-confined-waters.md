# Master-pilot exchange and confined waters — one plan, one team

**Goal:** as a pilot (or as the ship's master/OOW working with one), get aboard safely,
create a shared plan within minutes, and conduct the ship through confined waters with
the bridge team monitoring and able to challenge. The master retains command; the pilot
has the con by agreement.

## 1. Boarding
- Pilot ladder rigged per **SOLAS V/23** and IMO A.1045(27): ladder from ~0.5–1.5 m
  above water per pilot boat request; secured to strong points (not to the bulwark
  rails); side ropes if requested; man-ropes; lifebuoy with light; heaving line;
  officer in radio contact attending. Climb > 9 m requires a combination arrangement
  (accommodation ladder + pilot ladder).
- Ship provides a lee, speed as agreed (often 6–8 kn).
- Pilot: inspect before stepping on — defective ladders are a leading cause of pilot
  deaths; refuse if unsafe and report.

## 2. Master-pilot exchange (MPX) — before the con is handed over
Ship hands the **pilot card**: draught, air draught, displacement, propulsion type,
propeller handedness, thrusters, engine limits/response time, anchors ready, defects,
manoeuvring characteristics.
Pilot briefs:
- Route and plan: courses, speeds, critical points, tide/current, UKC at critical points.
- Traffic expected, VTS communications.
- **Tug plan:** number, positions, where made fast, line (ship's or tug's), channel.
- Berth: side to, lines plan and order, fender/landing limits.
- **Abort plan:** where you can still turn back or anchor, and the cue to do so.
- Language and orders: pilot gives helm/engine orders in English (standard phrases),
  OOW repeats and checks; agree that the team will speak up on any doubt.
Both sign/acknowledge per company and port procedure. Keep it to a few minutes but
don't skip it — mismatched expectations cause accidents.

## 3. Conduct in confined waters
- Use transits, leading lights and the ship's heading to stay on the channel line;
  watch the **rate of turn** and start turns early (wheel-over from the turning data).
- Speed: enough for steerage, low enough for squat and interaction; slow before bends and
  when passing other ships or moored vessels.
- **PPU** (portable pilot unit) gives independent precise position/heading and ROT;
  use it with the ship's ECDIS — two independent pictures.
- Bridge team monitors: position vs plan, UKC, helm and engine response
  (repeat-back of every order, checking rudder angle indicator and RPM), traffic.

## 4. Working tugs
- Orders: clear, standard ("tug forward, pull 90 to port, half power"), with the tug
  acknowledging. Give tugs time to get in position before you need them.
- Keep ship speed within tug limits when making fast (e.g. ≤ 5–6 kn forward).
- Inform tugs before using the ship's engines/thrusters heavily near them.
  See transportation-logistics.maritime-navigation-seamanship.tugboats.ship-assist-and-girting-avoidance.

## 5. Berthing and handover
- Approach as planned; lines sent per plan; landing speed within berth limits.
- Disembarking (outbound): agree drop-off point and speed, ladder rigged again.

## Gotchas
- The team going silent: "the pilot knows best" — the master must intervene if the
  ship is standing into danger.
- Language: local-language VHF with tugs leaves the bridge team blind; ask for English summaries.
- Fatigue and night transits: double-check helm orders.
- Ship's pilot card out of date (draught, defects) — recheck on boarding.

## Verify
- [ ] Ladder inspected and compliant before boarding.
- [ ] MPX done before the con handed over; abort plan understood by master.
- [ ] Every helm/engine order repeated back and verified on indicators.
- [ ] Position cross-checked by PPU and ship's ECDIS/radar throughout.
- [ ] Tugs briefed, positioned in time, released safely.

*Uncertainty:* ladder dimensions and procedures follow SOLAS/IMO but national pilotage rules add specifics; check the port's pilotage directions.
