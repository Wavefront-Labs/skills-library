# Navigational watch routine — know where you are and what's around you

**Goal:** keep the ship safe through your watch — continuous lookout, position known
and cross-checked, traffic assessed early, the master called when the situation needs
it — following STCW (Code A-VIII/2), COLREGs and the company's bridge procedures.

## 1. Taking over (arrive ~10–15 min early, eyes adjusted at night)
Don't accept the watch during a manoeuvre or collision avoidance in progress. Check:
- Master's standing and **night orders** (sign them).
- Position, course, speed, draught; planned track and next waypoints/alterations.
- Traffic around, nav warnings, weather, visibility trend.
- Equipment status: steering mode (auto/hand), gyro/magnetic compass, radar settings,
  ECDIS alarms, engines, BNWAS on, lights correct.
- Anything unusual: ongoing work on deck, restrictions, expected calls.
Only say "I have the watch" when you're satisfied — the outgoing OOW stays until then.

## 2. Lookout (COLREG rule 5)
- Sight, hearing and all available means, all the time. At night have a dedicated
  lookout as required; the helmsman steering is not the lookout.
- Scan systematically: horizon in sectors, then radar/ECDIS, then back outside.
  Don't spend minutes heads-down at the chart table.

## 3. Position monitoring
- Fix interval guidance (adapt to SMS): open ocean ~hourly; coastal ~15–30 min;
  pilotage waters as often as needed — often every few minutes, with continuous
  parallel indexing.
- Cross-check GNSS with independent methods: radar ranges/bearings, visual bearings,
  depth sounder against charted depth, celestial offshore. A fix confirmed by two
  methods is a fix; a single GNSS readout is a claim.
- Apply alterations at the planned wheel-over; check that the ship actually follows.

## 4. Compass error
- Check gyro error at least once a watch and after major course changes: azimuth
  or amplitude of the sun, a transit, or a GNSS heading comparison.
- Compare gyro and magnetic compass and record deviation. Log all errors.

## 5. Traffic
- Acquire early, assess CPA/TCPA, decide which COLREG rule applies, and act early and
  substantially. See
  transportation-logistics.maritime-navigation-seamanship.colregs-collision-avoidance.applying-the-steering-and-sailing-rules.
- Use VHF only to clarify — not to negotiate manoeuvres contrary to the rules.

## 6. Call the master (standing orders usually list these; don't hesitate)
- Visibility reduces (e.g. below 2–3 nm), or traffic causes concern.
- Difficulty keeping course, failure to sight land/mark/sounding when expected,
  unexpected land or change in soundings.
- Equipment failure (steering, gyro, radar, engine) or alarms you can't clear.
- Heavy weather, ice, any doubt. Call early: the master would rather be woken
  than surprised. Record time and reason in the log.

## 7. Log and handover
- Log positions, courses, weather, compass errors, events, master's calls.
- Handover covers everything in section 1, plus what you intended to do next.

## Gotchas
- Complacency in autopilot and ECDIS — check the ship is where the screen says.
- Fatigue: rest-hour records are a safety tool, not paperwork.
- Distractions: paperwork, phones and cargo calls during critical periods.
- A single-person bridge at night requires conditions (STCW) — follow them strictly.

## Verify
- [ ] Night orders read and signed; equipment status checked at takeover.
- [ ] Fixes at the planned interval by at least two methods in coastal waters.
- [ ] Gyro error obtained and logged this watch.
- [ ] Master called per standing orders; calls logged.
- [ ] Handover complete with traffic, intentions and equipment status.

*Uncertainty:* fix intervals and call-the-master criteria are typical company values; your SMS overrides.
