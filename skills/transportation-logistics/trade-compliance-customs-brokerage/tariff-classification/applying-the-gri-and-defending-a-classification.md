# Applying the GRI and defending a classification

**Goal:** arrive at the correct tariff code by method, not keyword search — and leave
a paper trail that survives an audit.

## 1. Know the structure
- **HS** (WCO): 6 digits, shared by ~200 countries. Chapter (2) → heading (4) →
  subheading (6).
- National extensions: US HTSUS 8 digits + 2 statistical (10) (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked); EU CN 8 digits, TARIC
  10; UK tariff 10 digits (commodity code) (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
- **Legal text** = heading texts + section and chapter notes. The **Explanatory Notes**
  are not legally binding in every country but are persuasive and widely followed.
- HS is revised every ~5 years (HS 2022 current; the next edition, HS 2028, enters into force 1 January 2028 per the WCO) — check
  the version effective on the entry date.

## 2. The GRI, strictly in order
1. **GRI 1:** classify by the **terms of the headings and the section/chapter notes**.
   Most goods stop here. Read the notes *first* — they exclude and redirect (e.g.
   chapter notes excluding parts of general use).
2. **GRI 2(a):** incomplete/unfinished or unassembled goods classify as the complete
   article if they have its **essential character**. **2(b):** mixtures and composite
   goods extend headings for a material — then go to GRI 3.
3. **GRI 3** (goods prima facie in two or more headings):
   - (a) most **specific** description wins;
   - (b) mixtures, composite goods and **retail sets** by the component giving
     **essential character** (bulk, value, role in use);
   - (c) if still tied, the heading **last in numerical order**.
4. **GRI 4:** goods most akin (rarely used).
5. **GRI 5:** cases and packing (camera cases, ordinary packing go with the goods).
6. **GRI 6:** apply the same rules at subheading level, comparing only subheadings at
   the same level (one-dash vs. one-dash).
Never jump to GRI 3 if GRI 1 resolves it.

## 3. Research
1. Write a precise description: what it is, material, function, how used, how
   presented (retail set? bulk?), and spec sheets/photos.
2. Candidate headings from the legal text and index of the Explanatory Notes.
3. Search rulings: **CBP CROSS** (US), **EBTI** (EU binding tariff information),
   UK Advance Tariff Rulings; look for the same product type, not keyword matches.
4. Check for **classification regulations** (EU) and WCO classification opinions.
5. Check "parts and accessories" rules — Section XVI and XVII notes restrict them
   heavily.

## 4. Write the memo
- Product description and evidence; candidate headings; GRI applied at each level
  with the notes cited; rulings considered (supporting and distinguishing);
  conclusion with full code and duty rate on date; reviewer and date.
- For high-value or uncertain items, apply for a **binding ruling** (US: CBP rulings under 19 CFR Part 177;
  EU BTI; UK ATaR) before large shipments.

## Gotchas
- Classifying by the supplier's code: their export code may be from a different
  national extension or wrong.
- "Parts" of machines are not automatically classified with the machine — check
  section notes.
- Kits and sets: only "put up for retail sale" sets get GRI 3(b) treatment.
- Duty rate also depends on origin and trade remedies (anti-dumping, Section 301 in
  the US) — the code is necessary, not sufficient.

## Verify
- [ ] Section and chapter notes read for each candidate heading.
- [ ] GRI applied in order; reasoning written at heading and subheading level.
- [ ] At least one relevant ruling found or the absence noted.
- [ ] Code valid for the entry date's HS/national version.
- [ ] Memo stored with product evidence and reviewer sign-off.
