# Urban scooter commuting — short hops, done safely every day

**Goal:** use a scooter or moped (petrol 50–125 cc or electric equivalent) as daily city
transport: get through traffic efficiently, be seen at junctions, and still have the
bike there when you come back. Licensing (e.g. UK CBT and L-plates for learners,
moped categories AM/A1, US state moped rules) and filtering legality vary — check
local law.

## 1. Know the machine
- **Twist-and-go (CVT):** no clutch or gears; throttle closed = some engine braking,
  but less than a geared bike. Throttle opening can surge at low speed — be smooth.
- **Brakes:** left lever = rear (on most scooters), right = front. Many have
  **linked/combined braking (CBS)** where the left lever also applies some front.
  Use both levers together, squeeze progressively.
- **Small wheels (10–14 in):** quicker steering but more upset by potholes, tram lines,
  drain covers and kerb edges; cross rails at as close to 90° as possible.
- Low weight and low centre of gravity — easy to handle, but gusts and lorry wash push
  you around.

## 2. Daily checks
Tyre pressures (small tyres lose pressure fast — check weekly), lights, brakes,
battery charge (electric: plan range with ~20% margin; cold weather cuts range).

## 3. Road position
- Ride in the position where you are **seen and see most**: usually the centre to
  right-hand third of your lane (left-hand traffic: the right third; mirror for right-hand
  traffic), not hugging the gutter — that invites close overtakes and hides you
  from emerging drivers.
- Approaching side roads, move to give emerging drivers the best view of you; cover
  the brakes; assume they haven't seen you (the "SMIDSY" crash: car turns across you).
- Avoid riding in blind spots alongside vans and lorries; hang back or pass decisively.
- Left/near-side turning lorries: never go up the inside at a junction.

## 4. Filtering through queues (where legal)
- Filter only when traffic is slow or stationary; keep speed differential small
  (e.g. ≤ ~15–20 km/h faster than traffic, and slow overall).
- Watch for gaps where cars may pull out, doors opening, U-turning vehicles, pedestrians
  stepping between cars.
- Don't filter approaching junctions or across hatching/ASL entry where prohibited;
  rejoin the lane before the traffic starts moving.

## 5. Weather and visibility
- Wet: painted lines, manholes and leaves are slippery; brake earlier and gentler.
- Wear proper protective kit even for short trips — most crashes happen in town at
  low speed, and asphalt doesn't care about the trip length.
- Hi-vis vest or bright helmet; headlight on.

## 6. Parking and theft
Scooters are stolen by lifting into vans — weight is no protection.
- Use a **disc lock with reminder cable** plus a **ground anchor or chain** through
  the frame to a fixed object; park in view/CCTV, in dedicated motorcycle bays.
- Alarm or tracker for regular-commute bikes; mark parts; record VIN and keys.
- Remove valuables and charging cables (electric).

## Gotchas
- Forgetting the disc lock and riding off — use the reminder cable.
- Grabbing the left (rear) lever alone in an emergency: use both.
- Under-powered 50 cc on fast roads: plan routes that avoid 60+ km/h arterials.
- Under-seat storage gets hot near the engine — don't carry heat-sensitive items there.

## Verify
- [ ] Both levers used together on every stop; you know whether brakes are linked.
- [ ] Lane position chosen for visibility at every junction approach.
- [ ] Filtering only in slow traffic and only where legal.
- [ ] Locked to an immovable object with a disc lock every time parked.

*Uncertainty:* filtering legality and speed guidance differ between countries and states.
