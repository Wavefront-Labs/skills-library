# Braking, cornering and machine control — the physical skills under every ride

**Goal:** stop short, turn where you want, and handle the bike at walking pace, so the
machine never becomes the reason a situation goes wrong. Practise these in an empty
car park before you need them on the road. Training and licensing rules (CBT, A2,
MSF BRC etc.) vary by country — take the formal course.

## 1. Pre-ride check (T-CLOCS, ~2 minutes)
- **T**yres & wheels: pressures cold to the handbook (not the sidewall max), tread
  depth, cuts, spokes/rims.
- **C**ontrols: levers, cables, throttle snaps shut, brake fluid level.
- **L**ights & electrics: headlight, brake light from both brakes, indicators, horn.
- **O**il & fluids: oil level, coolant, leaks.
- **C**hassis: chain tension (typically 20–35 mm free play — see manual), lubrication,
  suspension.
- **S**tands: side stand springs up, kill switch works.

## 2. Braking
- The front brake provides most stopping power (70%+ in a hard stop on dry road)
  because weight transfers forward. Use both brakes; emphasise the front.
- **Progressive squeeze:** take up lever slack, let the fork compress (~¼ second),
  then squeeze firmly harder. Grabbing locks the front before load transfer builds.
- **With ABS:** in an emergency squeeze hard and hold; let ABS modulate. Keep eyes
  up and the bike upright.
- **Without ABS:** front lock → release immediately and reapply. Rear lock in a
  straight line → keep it locked until stopped (releasing at speed can high-side).
- Brake upright where possible. Mid-corner, cornering ABS helps but traction is shared:
  stand the bike up a little as you brake, or brake gently while leaned.
- Practise: stops from 30, 50, 70 km/h on dry, then wet, measuring distance.

## 3. Cornering
- **Countersteer:** above ~15–20 km/h, push the bar on the side you want to go
  (push right = lean right = turn right). Press harder = lean quicker.
- Sequence: **slow** (brakes/gears) before the bend → **look** through the turn to
  the exit / vanishing point → **press** to lean → **roll** on the throttle gently and
  steadily through the bend (stabilises the chassis).
- Late apex on blind bends: stay wide-ish on entry (as visibility and lane allow), turn
  in later, so you see the exit sooner.
- If running wide: look where you want to go, press the inside bar more, don't
  chop the throttle or grab the front brake — the bike almost always has more lean
  available than the rider believes.
- **Target fixation:** you go where you look. Look at the escape route, not the hazard.

## 4. Slow-speed control (filtering, car parks, U-turns)
- Use the **clutch friction zone** to control drive, slight constant throttle, and
  **light rear brake** to stabilise — avoid the front brake at full lock (bike falls).
- Head and eyes turned in the direction of the turn; counterweight by sitting upright
  while leaning the bike.
- U-turn drill: practise in a 6–7 m box both directions.

## 5. Riding position and gear
- Knees grip tank, relaxed arms, weight through the pegs, head up.
- Gear: helmet (certified, correct fit), gloves, abrasion-resistant jacket/trousers with
  armour, boots covering the ankle. Hi-vis improves conspicuity.

## Gotchas
- Cold tyres and wet paint/manholes/diesel reduce grip dramatically.
- Overinflated tyres cut the contact patch; underinflated tyres make steering heavy.
- Engine braking plus a hard rear brake can lock the rear on downshifts.
- "Survival reactions" (chop throttle, grab brake, stare at hazard) are trained out
  only by practice.

## Verify
- [ ] T-CLOCS check done before the ride.
- [ ] Can stop from 50 km/h on dry tarmac in roughly 12–15 m (ABS or controlled).
- [ ] Can countersteer an avoidance swerve both ways at 40–50 km/h.
- [ ] Can do a full-lock U-turn in a 6–7 m width without putting a foot down.

*Uncertainty:* braking distances and percentages vary with bike, tyres and surface; use them as practice targets only.
