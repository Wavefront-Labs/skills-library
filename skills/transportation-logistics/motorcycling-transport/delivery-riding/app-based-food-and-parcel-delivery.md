# App-based food and parcel delivery — profitable shifts, intact orders, no crashes

**Goal:** deliver for food/parcel platforms (Uber Eats, Deliveroo, DoorDash, Just Eat
and similar) on a scooter or motorcycle so that orders arrive hot and intact, you
choose work that actually pays, and you stay safe at the kerb. Platform features
change often — check the current app terms.

## 1. Setup before the first shift
- **Insurance:** hire-and-reward (food delivery) cover — some platforms provide
  on-shift cover, often only third party; check what it covers and when it starts.
- **Box:** rigid insulated delivery box on a rear rack (better than a backpack for
  weight, stability and food temperature). Inside: a flat insulated bag, dividers,
  and a cup holder for drinks.
- Phone mount with vibration damper (motorcycle vibration can damage phone camera
  stabilisers), USB charger, waterproof cover. Power bank backup.
- Personal kit for wet days; gloves that work with a touchscreen at stops.

## 2. Choosing orders
- Judge each offer by **earnings per hour**, not per order: (pay) ÷ (time to restaurant
  + wait + time to customer + return to a busy zone).
- Short pickup distances and restaurants with fast turnaround make better money.
- Note restaurants that routinely keep riders waiting 15+ minutes.
- Batching (two orders in one trip) is good when drops are on a line; it can make
  the first customer's food cold if not.
- Acceptance-rate rules vary by platform — know whether declining affects you.

## 3. Packing for the ride
- Hot and cold separated (divider or separate bags).
- **Upright and wedged**: soups, drinks and pizzas flat and immobile; fill gaps
  with a folded insulated bag/towel. Sealed drink lids, cups in a holder.
- Close the box fully; check it's locked on.
- Ride smoothly — hard braking or bumps tip sauces. Avoid kerb hops (also illegal).

## 4. Riding safely between jobs
- The app timer doesn't make you invincible: the fastest legal route is fine.
- Don't use the phone while moving; set navigation before leaving.
- Junctions, opening car doors and pedestrians stepping out are the main risks in
  dense restaurant streets. See transportation-logistics.motorcycling-transport.scooters-mopeds.urban-scooter-commuting.

## 5. Kerbside and handover
- Park legally and stably: stand on firm ground, not on a slope where the bike can roll
  off the side stand; engine off, key removed or steering lock on when leaving sight
  of the bike.
- Hazards on foot: cars reversing, slippery steps, dogs at doors.
- Handover: check name/order number, contactless drop if requested (photo where the
  app asks), ID check for age-restricted items (alcohol) — refuse if no valid ID and
  follow the platform's return process.
- Be polite and quick; ratings affect future offers on some platforms.

## 6. Hygiene and food safety
Clean the box inside daily; don't open sealed food bags; report spillages and
tampered seals rather than delivering them.

## 7. Track your real economics
Record each shift: hours online, hours active, gross pay, tips, fuel/charging, and
an allowance for tyres, maintenance and insurance. Calculate net per hour; shift
your hours to peak windows (lunch, dinner, rain) where pay rises.

## Gotchas
- Riding with a heavy backpack raises your centre of gravity and loads the spine.
- Leaving the bike unlocked "just for a minute" — common theft moment.
- Accepting every offer chasing acceptance rate while earnings collapse.
- Rain doubles orders and doubles crash risk — slow down, increase gaps.

## Verify
- [ ] Insurance covers delivery work for the hours you ride.
- [ ] Box mounted, clean, with dividers and drink holder.
- [ ] Offers judged by earnings per hour; weak restaurants noted.
- [ ] Bike parked and locked properly at each handover; ID checks done for restricted items.
- [ ] Weekly net earnings per hour calculated.

*Uncertainty:* platform policies on insurance, acceptance and ratings vary and change frequently.
