# Multi-day tour planning and packing — arrive every evening still enjoying it

**Goal:** cover long distances over several days without exhaustion, mechanical
surprises or paperwork trouble at borders, with the bike set up for the load it carries.

## 1. Plan days around fatigue, not distance
- A sustainable daily target for most riders: **300–500 km on main roads**, less on
  twisty or unpaved roads, with a hard stop time that leaves daylight margin.
- Break every **~1.5–2 hours** (fuel stops are natural breaks): get off, walk, drink
  water, eat light. Dehydration and cold are fatigue multipliers.
- Rest days every 4–6 days on long trips; build one spare day per week for weather or
  breakdowns.
- Fatigue warning signs: missed gear changes, sloppy lines, drifting thoughts,
  not remembering the last kilometres → stop now, coffee + 15–20 min nap if needed.

## 2. Weather routing
- Check forecasts the night before and morning: rain bands, wind (crosswinds on bridges
  and exposed plateaus), heat (>35 °C is dangerous in full gear — ride early, rest midday),
  mountain passes (snow closures into early summer).
- Have alternates: a lower valley route around a closed pass, a motorway to escape a
  storm.

## 3. Luggage and load
- Know your bike's **maximum payload** (gross vehicle weight minus kerb weight, in the
  manual) — rider + pillion + luggage + accessories must fit inside it. Touring loads
  easily exceed it on smaller bikes.
- Heavy items **low and forward** (panniers bottom, tank bag); light bulky items high
  and rear (tail bag). Keep each pannier similar in weight.
- Top boxes have low weight limits (often 3–10 kg) and lighten the front at speed.
- Soft luggage straps: check after the first 20 km and at every stop — they loosen.
- Packing list core: rain gear, warm layer, documents, phone/charger, first aid,
  tools, puncture kit, compressor, spare fuses/bulbs, chain lube, cable ties, duct tape.

## 4. Set the bike up for the load
- **Tyre pressures:** use the manual's "loaded/two-up" values (often +0.2–0.4 bar).
- **Rear preload:** increase so sag returns to normal with luggage; check headlight
  aim (heavy rear loads point it upward and dazzle oncoming drivers).
- Service before departure: tyres with enough tread for the whole trip (or plan a
  change), chain/sprockets, brake pads, oil.

## 5. Documents and border crossings
- Licence (with **International Driving Permit** where required), registration
  document, insurance valid for each country (Europe: green card where needed),
  passport/visas, breakdown cover with repatriation.
- Some countries require a **carnet de passages** for temporary vehicle import, or
  a temporary import permit at the border; check the specific country rules months ahead.
- Keep paper copies plus photos; carry local currency for tolls and fuel at remote stations.
- Know local equipment rules (e.g. hi-vis, spare bulbs, breathalyser kits, helmet
  reflective stickers) — they differ by country.

## 6. Fuel and range
Know the bike's realistic range loaded (often 10–20% less than solo); in remote areas
refuel at half tank. Carry a small spare fuel container where range is marginal.

## 7. Roadside fixes
- Tubeless puncture: plug kit + compressor; ride gently to a tyre shop.
- Tubed tyres: tyre levers, patches, pump — practise at home.
- Loose bolts: check torque points nightly (luggage racks, mirrors, chain adjusters).

## Gotchas
- Overloaded bikes brake and corner noticeably worse.
- Wet gear in the morning — pack waterproofs on top so they're reachable fast.
- Over-ambitious first day; jet-lag-like fatigue builds over days.
- Insurance not valid in a non-EU/transit country.

## Verify
- [ ] Daily plan ≤ comfortable distance with breaks and a stop time.
- [ ] Total load within payload; preload and tyre pressures set for it.
- [ ] Documents checked per country; insurance and breakdown cover confirmed.
- [ ] Repair kit packed and you've practised using it.

*Uncertainty:* daily distance targets and luggage limits vary with rider and bike; border rules change — verify for each trip.
