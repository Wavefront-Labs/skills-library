# Pre-use checks, load capacity and trailer entry

**Goal:** move loads without dropping them, tipping the truck or hitting a person — the
three ways forklift work kills. Requires formal training and authorisation on the
specific truck type (e.g. OSHA 1910.178(l), UK L117); this skill is a working
reference, not a licence.

## 1. Pre-use inspection (at least daily, and after each shift on round-the-clock trucks, per 29 CFR 1910.178(q)(7))
Visual and functional, with the truck off then on:
- Forks: cracks at the heel, bent tips, even height, locking pins in.
- Mast and chains: no damaged links, even tension, no leaks from lift/tilt cylinders.
- Tyres/wheels, overhead guard, load backrest, seat belt.
- Fluids/battery: LPG cylinder secured and no smell; battery charged, connector
  sound, electrolyte (for flooded batteries) only topped after charging.
- Functional: horn, lights/blue spot, brakes and parking brake, steering, lift/tilt/side
  shift, reverse alarm, seat switch.
Any safety defect → tag out, keys to supervisor, don't drive it.

## 2. Capacity plate and load centre
- The **data plate** gives rated capacity at a stated **load centre** (e.g. 2,500 kg at
  500 mm, or 5,000 lb at 24 in) and at given lift heights/attachments.
- A longer load (centre of gravity further out) reduces capacity. Rough estimate:
  capacity × (rated load centre ÷ actual load centre) — 2,500 kg at 500 mm → ~2,080 kg
  at 600 mm. *Approximation only; use the manufacturer's chart.*
- Attachments (clamps, rotators, extensions) and high lift heights derate further —
  check the plate for that configuration.
- Unknown load weight → find out (labels, scale), don't "try it".

## 3. Handling and stacking
- Forks fully under the load, spread for balance, load against the backrest.
- Travel with forks as low as practical (on grades OSHA permits raising only as far as necessary to clear the surface, 1910.178(n)(7)), mast tilted back, looking in the direction
  of travel; if the load blocks your view, travel in reverse.
- At the rack: stop square to the face, raise only when stopped, level forks before
  entering the pallet position, set down, then withdraw. Never lift or lower while
  travelling.
- Reach trucks: extend the reach only when stopped and squared; watch the rack beam
  and overhead clearances.
- **Stability triangle:** turning fast, turning on a slope, or travelling with a raised
  load moves the combined CG outside the triangle → lateral tip-over. Stay in the seat
  with the belt on if it tips — don't jump.

## 4. Ramps and grades
- Loaded: **load uphill** — drive forward up, reverse down.
- Unloaded: forks downhill.
- Never turn on a ramp.

## 5. Entering trailers and containers
1. Trailer secured: **dock lock engaged or wheels chocked** (OSHA 1910.178(k)(1) and (m)(7): brakes set and chocks under the rear wheels), brakes set; for detached
   trailers, a trailer stand/jack under the nose.
2. Dock plate/leveller properly placed and secured before driving over it (1910.178(n)(11)) and rated for the truck + load.
3. Check trailer floor for holes/weak boards and its height/headroom.
4. Enter slowly; the trailer can creep away from the dock ("trailer creep") under
   repeated braking — that's why the lock matters.

## 6. Pedestrians
- Separate aisles/walkways where possible; sound horn at intersections and blind
  corners; make eye contact before passing.
- No riders, no lifting people on forks (use an approved platform system).
- Park: forks lowered to the floor, controls neutral, brake on, key out.

## Gotchas
- Battery charging areas need ventilation and eye-wash; no smoking.
- Overloading "just this once" stresses the mast and wears the rear axle.
- Speed limits apply in buildings; most incidents are at low speeds near people.

## Verify

- [ ] Load weight and load centre within the plate for the configuration.
- [ ] Trailer secured (lock/chocks/jack) before entry.
- [ ] Travelling with forks low and visibility maintained.
- [ ] Truck parked forks down, key removed.
