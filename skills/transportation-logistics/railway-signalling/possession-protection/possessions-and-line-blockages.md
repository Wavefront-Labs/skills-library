# Possessions and line blockages

**Goal:** give engineering staff a piece of railway that no train will enter without
their permission, and take it back only when it's safe to run trains again.

*Draft: modelled on UK-style possessions/line blockages with generic analogues
(North American Form B/track and time, work blocks). Role titles (PICOP/PICOW, COSS,
ES) and forms are system-specific; follow the rulebook modules for your network.*

## 1. Before granting
- **Know the plan:** the published notice (weekly operating notice/work plan) giving
  limits, lines, times, who's in charge, any engineering trains to enter.
- **Clear the limits:** the last train must have passed clear of the limits (confirmed
  by track-circuit/axle-counter and, where required, tail lamp/train complete).
- **Identify the person in charge** and confirm their identity and competence
  (name, ID, what they're taking).

## 2. Granting
1. Put signals protecting the limits to danger and apply **blocking devices/reminders**
   (signal reminder collars, workstation blocks) so a route can't be set into the
   blocked area.
2. Agree the limits precisely — signal numbers, mile/km posts, lines — and the time.
   Use a formal message read-back.
3. Record in the occurrence book/log: who, what, limits, time granted.
4. The person in charge then places their own protection (marker boards, detonators,
   or as required) at the limits before work starts.

## 3. Managing trains inside a possession
- Engineering trains/on-track plant enter only with the person in charge's
  permission and under your instruction at the protecting signal (often passed at
  danger with authority).
- Track every movement into and out of the limits on your record.
- Multiple worksites inside one possession are managed by the person in charge, not
  by you — but you need the worksite count to confirm clearance at handback.

## 4. Handback
1. The person in charge confirms: all worksites closed, all staff and plant clear of
   the line, protection removed, and **the line safe for trains** — including any
   speed restrictions (e.g. after track renewal) and their locations.
2. Read back the conditions and restrictions; note TSRs and advise drivers/control.
3. Remove blocking devices only after confirmation and log the time.
4. First train over newly worked track may run at reduced speed per instructions;
   ask the driver to report anything unusual.

## 5. Common failure points
- Overruns: the possession doesn't end on time. Escalate early to control; trains
  must be re-planned.
- Ambiguous limits ("the down line by the bridge"): reject them and get precise
  references.
- Granting a line blockage on the wrong line when lines are similarly numbered.

## Gotchas
- A possession granted with a train still in the limits is a serious safety event —
  verify complete-train clearance.
- Blocking devices left off when "it'll only be a minute".
- Handback without the speed restriction notice reaching drivers.

## Verify
- [ ] Limits cleared of trains and confirmed complete before granting.
- [ ] Blocking devices applied before granting, removed only after handback.
- [ ] Every movement into/out of the limits logged.
- [ ] Handback conditions read back, restrictions published.
