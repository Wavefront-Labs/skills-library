# Tether protocol and EVA timeline management

**Goal:** do the planned work outside the vehicle and come back in with margin, without
ever being untethered or out of consumables. *Generic structure drawn from public ISS
practice; your programme's EVA checklist and suit limits govern.*

## 1. Before the hatch opens
- **Suit checkout:** pressure integrity (leak check), cooling loop, comm, displays,
  battery and oxygen levels, helmet lights/cameras. Any failed check is a No-Go —
  don't trade a checklist step for timeline.
- **Prebreathe** to wash nitrogen from tissues before dropping to suit pressure
  (~4.3 psi in the US EMU vs. ~14.7 psi cabin). Protocols combine oxygen breathing,
  exercise and/or lowered cabin pressure; skipping or shortening them risks
  decompression sickness.
- **Briefing:** worksite sequence, tools per task, tether points, get-ahead tasks,
  hard stop times, and the "no-go" conditions that end the EVA early.

## 2. Tether protocol — never untethered
- **Make before break:** attach the new tether/hook before releasing the old one,
  every time. Physically verify the gate is closed (look and tug) and call it.
- Safety tether reel stays connected to the vehicle from egress to ingress.
- **Tools tethered** at all times; a lost tool becomes debris and may need tracking.
- Body restraint: use the foot restraint or a body-restraint tether at the worksite to
  free both hands; work loads transmit through your body, not the handrails.
- SAFER (self-rescue jetpack) or equivalent is a last resort, not a plan.

## 3. Translation and worksite discipline
- Move hand over hand on handrails, slow, with three-point awareness; avoid kicking
  hardware (sharp edges cut gloves — inspect gloves at intervals for cuts).
- Know keep-out zones: thrusters, antennas radiating, hot/cold surfaces, robotic arm
  envelope.
- At the worksite: verbalise each step ("Torque set to 25 ft-lb, engaging"), confirm
  with IV (intravehicular) crew reading the procedure.

## 4. Timeline and consumables
- The IV crew member and ground track elapsed time vs. plan and the **consumables
  limit** (oxygen, CO2 scrubbing, battery, water). The suit's tightest consumable sets
  the hard stop.
- Have **get-ahead tasks** ready if ahead, and **deferrable tasks** identified if behind.
- Fatigue (especially hand fatigue in pressurised gloves) is real; plan rest pauses.
- Leave margin for ingress: return translation time plus contingency.

## 5. Stop and come in immediately if
- Unexpected water in the helmet (the 2013 water-intrusion event showed this can
  blind and threaten drowning in a suit — terminate EVA at the first sign).
- Suit pressure anomaly, CO2 high, comm loss that can't be restored, or a tether
  problem that can't be verified.
- Ground calls a debris conjunction or a station emergency.

## Gotchas
- Glove damage is cumulative — inspect after every abrasive task.
- Sun glare and darkness every ~45 minutes in LEO; tasks requiring vision should be
  timed with daylight passes where possible.
- Do not let schedule pressure override "make before break" — most near-misses come
  from hurry.

## Verify
- [ ] Suit checkout complete, prebreathe protocol fully executed.
- [ ] Every tether transfer called and confirmed, make-before-break.
- [ ] Hard stop time and consumable limit known to EV crew, IV crew and ground.
- [ ] Gloves inspected at the planned intervals.
- [ ] Termination criteria briefed and understood.
