# Loaded cargo-bike handling

**Goal:** carry 50–150 kg of kids or goods and still stop, steer and park with
confidence — because a cargo bike forgives less than a normal bike.

> Draft: general practice across front-loaders (box bikes like Urban Arrow, Bullitt),
> longtails (Tern GSD, Yuba) and trikes. Check your model's max system weight
> (rider + bike + cargo) and passenger rules; child-carrying laws vary by country.

## 1. Know the formats
| Format | Feel | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| **Two-wheel front-loader** (box) | Indirect linkage steering, long wheelbase | Twitchy at first under 5 km/h; wide turning circle |
| **Longtail** | Feels like a normal bike, load behind | Rear-heavy; lifting the front on steep climbs |
| **Trike (tadpole/delta)** | Stable at rest, no balancing | **Tips in fast corners**; camber pushes it to the gutter |

## 2. Load placement and securing
- **Heavy low and central**: in a box, heaviest items at the bottom, close to the
  rider; on a longtail, low in the side bags, balanced left/right.
- Total weight within the **max system weight** and the rack/box rating.
- Freight: cargo net or two cam straps crossing; nothing protruding past the box sides
  (catches on bollards and pedestrians).
- **Children**: the manufacturer's seat, harness buckled (lap + shoulder), helmets,
  feet clear of spokes (longtails need wheel guards/footrests). Check the child can't
  reach the wheel or the steering linkage.
- Rain cover and loose straps tucked in — straps in spokes stop the wheel.

## 3. Braking
- A loaded cargo bike may need roughly **double** a normal bike's stopping distance
  (order of magnitude, depends heavily on brakes and load). Start braking earlier,
  ride with fingers on levers.
- Hydraulic discs are the norm for a reason; check pads monthly with heavy use.
- **Front brake does most of the work** but on a front-loader the box loads the front
  wheel — smooth and progressive, not grabby. On wet cobbles or leaves, brake upright,
  before the corner.
- Downhill: control speed early; long descents overheat small rotors.

## 4. Steering and cornering
- Look where you want to go, far ahead; with a front-loader, don't watch the front wheel.
- First rides: an empty car park — slow figure-eights, tight U-turns, emergency stops.
- Corners: slower entry; on a trike, **lean your body into the turn** and keep the
  speed down — trikes can lift a wheel.
- Wide turning circle: plan junctions and bike-lane chicanes early; dismount and walk
  if a barrier is too tight.

## 5. Kerbs, ramps and tracks
- Avoid hopping kerbs; use dropped kerbs, approach **square-on** (90°), slow, weight
  off the saddle.
- Tram/rail tracks: cross as close to 90° as possible — long wheelbases don't recover
  from a wheel caught in a groove.
- Steep ramps on longtails: shift weight forward to keep the front wheel down.

## 6. Starting, stopping and parking
- Start in a **low gear** (or with e-assist on a low level) to avoid the wobbly first
  metres.
- Put the bike on the **centre stand** before loading or unloading — especially children.
  Many crashes happen stationary when a child climbs in and the bike tips.
- Park on level ground; turn the handlebar lock if fitted.

## Gotchas
- Wind pushes box covers like a sail — side gusts on bridges.
- The box hides potholes from view — scan further ahead.
- Tyre pressure: run near the maximum for heavy loads to avoid pinch flats and
  sluggish steering.

## Verify
- [ ] Total system weight is within the manufacturer's limit.
- [ ] Load is low, central and strapped; no loose straps.
- [ ] Children are harnessed, helmeted and clear of wheels/linkage.
- [ ] You've practised an emergency stop and tight U-turn loaded.
- [ ] Bike is always on the stand when loading or unloading.
