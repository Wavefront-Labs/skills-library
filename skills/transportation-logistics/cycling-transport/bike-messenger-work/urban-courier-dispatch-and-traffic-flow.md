# Urban courier dispatch and traffic flow

**Goal:** more completed jobs per hour, delivered with proof, without crashes, stolen
bikes or arguments at the front desk.

> Draft: general courier-company practice (radio and app dispatch). Pay models, legal
> status and city traffic laws vary; traffic-law compliance is assumed, not optional.

## 1. Taking jobs from dispatch
- Confirm each job: **pickup, drop, deadline/service level** (e.g. 1 hour, 3 hour,
  rush), contact name, package size, signature needed.
- Read back addresses on the radio ("copy, pick up 100 Main, suite 400, drop 22 Elm,
  one-hour"). On apps, open the job details — don't accept blind.
- Tell dispatch your ETA honestly; dispatch plans around it.

## 2. Stacking and routing
- **Stack** jobs that share a direction: pickups first along the route, drops in an
  order that respects deadlines (rush first).
- Keep a **manifest** (paper or app list) with deadlines in order; tick as you go.
- Learn the grid: one-ways, bike lanes, contraflow lanes, hills, building entrances on
  side streets, bridges and their busy times.
- Avoid the shortest path that stalls you (long lights, pedestrian plazas, freight
  zones at delivery hours).
- Push back when overloaded: a late rush job hurts the company more than a refused stack.

## 3. Reading dense traffic
- Look **two to three vehicles ahead** — brake lights, indicators, gaps closing.
- **Door zone**: ride at least ~1 m (a door's width) from parked cars, or slow down
  and watch for heads in driver seats and wheels turning out.
- **Right/left hook** (turning vehicles across you): never sit beside a large vehicle's
  front corner at a junction; stay behind it or well ahead where the driver sees you.
- Filtering past queued traffic (where legal): slow, ready to stop, expect doors and
  U-turns; don't filter up the inside of a bus or truck.
- Eye contact and position, not speed, keep you visible.

## 4. Locking fast and securely
- A **compact U-lock** through the frame and to a solid, fixed object; rear wheel
  inside the triangle if possible. Takes ~10 s with practice.
- Lock even for "one minute" drops — that's when bikes go.
- Small items (lights, bag) come with you.
- Note: some buildings let couriers bring bikes into a loading dock — ask once and
  remember which.

## 5. Building access routine
- Know whether the drop is **front desk, mailroom or direct** for each frequent building.
- At security: name, company, recipient, suite. Have the recipient's phone number
  ready (from the job) to call down.
- Use **freight elevators and loading docks** where required; follow the building's
  rules (helmets off, no bikes in lobbies).
- Waiting > a few minutes: inform dispatch.

## 6. Proof of delivery
- Signature + printed name, time; photo POD on apps (the package at the door with the
  unit number visible, or handed to reception).
- Undeliverable: call recipient and dispatch before leaving; never leave a
  signature-required item.

## 7. Bike and body
- Daily check: brakes, tyres, chain, lights. Carry a tube, levers, pump, multi-tool.
- Fixed-gear bikes still need a front brake (legally in many places, and practically).
- Hydrate and eat — 6–8 hours of riding burns serious energy.
- Rain gear and visible clothing; lights front and rear even in daylight in bad weather.

## Verify
- [ ] Every job was confirmed with pickup, drop, deadline and signature need.
- [ ] Stacks follow deadline order; manifest is current.
- [ ] You ride out of the door zone and never sit at a truck's front corner.
- [ ] The bike is locked with a U-lock every stop.
- [ ] Every drop has proof of delivery recorded.
