# Lane positioning and visibility (commuter roadcraft)

**Goal:** be where drivers look, never where they'll squeeze past or turn across you,
and arrive daily — dark, wet or icy — with the bike still there when you come back.

> Draft: based on standard roadcraft training (e.g. UK Bikeability, US League of American
> Bicyclists). Directions assume **riding on the right** (US/EU continental); swap left/right
> for left-hand-traffic countries. Local laws on lane use and lights apply.

## 1. Two positions
- **Secondary position**: about **1 m** to the side of the traffic flow from the kerb/edge
  (not in the gutter). Used when it's safe for traffic to overtake with a wide gap.
- **Primary position**: **centre of the lane** ("taking the lane"). Used when overtaking
  would be unsafe: narrow lanes, pinch points (islands, parked cars), approaching
  junctions, when going at traffic speed.
- Rule: if a driver can't pass you with ~1.5 m clearance in the lane, take primary so
  they don't try.
- **Door zone**: always ride ~1 m+ from parked cars (beyond an open door), even if
  it means primary.

## 2. Junctions
- **Going straight**: hold a position in the lane for straight-ahead traffic; don't
  ride up the right (kerb) side of a vehicle that may turn right.
- **Turning across traffic** (left here): look behind, signal, move to the left of the
  lane or the turn lane early, take primary, look again before turning.
- **Large vehicles**: never stop beside a truck or bus at a junction — the driver can't see
  you and the rear wheels cut in. Stop behind it.
- At lights, stop **in front** (bike box/advanced stop line) or behind the first car, not
  beside it.

## 3. Roundabouts (multi-lane caution)
- Take primary on approach and on the roundabout; signal exits; look over your shoulder
  before exits. Large multi-lane roundabouts: consider an alternative route or the bike
  path where provided.

## 4. Visibility and night riding
- Front light: a steady beam (e.g. **~200–800 lumens** for unlit roads; less in lit
  city streets) aimed so it doesn't dazzle. Rear light: steady red, plus a flashing
  one if allowed. Lights on in dusk, rain and fog, not just at night.
- **Retroreflective** material on moving parts (pedals, ankles) is most noticed by drivers.
- Hi-vis helps in daylight; reflectives work at night.
- Eye contact at junctions; assume you haven't been seen until the driver reacts.

## 5. Winter and wet commuting
- Brake earlier in rain; rim brakes lose bite when wet.
- Ice: studded tyres for regular icy commutes; lower pressure slightly for grip.
- Avoid painted lines, manhole covers and metal plates when wet; cross tram tracks at
  close to 90°.
- Mudguards/fenders and a dry layer at work make year-round commuting sustainable.

## 6. Route choice
- Quieter parallel streets and protected lanes beat the fastest arterial for most
  commuters. Test the route on a weekend.
- Know an alternative for roadworks or bad weather.

## 7. Secure parking
- A good **U-lock** (or folding lock) through the frame and rear wheel to a fixed
  stand; a cable for the front wheel/saddle if they're quick-release.
- Lock in busy, visible, covered places; avoid leaving it overnight on the street.
- Record the frame number and register it (e.g. national bike registers).

## Gotchas
- Riding in the gutter invites close passes and puts you in debris and drain covers.
- Undertaking a turning vehicle is the classic serious-injury collision.
- Lights mounted on a bag or helmet only can be blocked or pointed wrong.

## Verify
- [ ] You can say when you'd take primary and when secondary on your route.
- [ ] You ride clear of the door zone.
- [ ] You never wait alongside a truck or bus at a junction.
- [ ] Front and rear lights work and are on in poor light.
- [ ] Bike is U-locked through the frame to a fixed object every time.
