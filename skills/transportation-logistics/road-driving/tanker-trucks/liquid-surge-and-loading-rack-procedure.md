# Liquid surge and loading-rack procedure

**Goal:** move liquid without rolling the tanker or being shoved through an
intersection by surge — and load and unload without spills, fires or overfills.

*Draft: product-specific rules (petroleum, chemicals, food-grade, gases) and the
dangerous-goods regime (US HazMat/49 CFR, European ADR) govern details. Loading
racks have site-specific procedures that always take priority.*

## 1. Understand the load
- **Surge:** a partly filled tank lets liquid slosh. When you brake, liquid runs
  forward then *back*, pushing the truck forward after you think you've stopped, and
  then pulling it backward.
- **Baffled tanks** (perforated dividers) reduce front-back surge, not side-to-side.
  **Smooth-bore** tanks (no baffles — common for food-grade for cleaning reasons)
  surge the most. **Compartmented** tanks behave like several small tanks; load them
  to keep weight distributed.
- **High centre of gravity:** a full tank sits high — rollover risk on curves and
  ramps begins well below posted speeds. Partly loaded tanks combine both problems.
- **Outage:** leave expansion space (outage) as required by product and temperature;
  never fill to the brim.

## 2. Driving technique
- **Brake early and gently, and keep steady pressure** until stopped; release
  smoothly. Expect the surge push after stopping — hold the brake.
- **Curves and ramps:** take them well below the posted advisory speed; on/off-ramps
  are the classic rollover location. Don't brake hard *in* a curve.
- Increase following distance; give yourself room to slow gradually.
- Avoid sudden steering: an evasive swerve with surge can roll the vehicle.

## 3. Loading at a rack (typical bottom-loading sequence)
1. Park in the bay, engine off, brakes on, keys per site rule (often to the
   interlock box), wheel chocks if required.
2. **Connect the bonding/grounding** cable first — before opening anything. The rack
   permissive system won't allow flow without it.
3. Connect overfill-protection (optic/thermistor) sensors and **vapour recovery** hose.
4. Check each compartment's capacity and the order/quantity; connect the loading
   arm to the correct compartment adaptor (product grade match!).
5. Start flow, **stay with the vehicle** watching meters and the deadman control;
   stop at the planned quantity.
6. Disconnect in reverse order: product arm, vapour, overfill sensor, **ground last**.
7. Check for leaks, close valves and dome lids, collect paperwork, verify placards.

## 4. Discharging (e.g. fuel delivery)
- Check the receiving tank's **ullage** (space available) and the product grade
  before connecting — wrong-product deliveries contaminate a whole station tank.
- Bond the vehicle, connect hoses, open valves in the right order, attend the
  delivery throughout, keep spill kit ready.

## Gotchas
- Driving away connected: always walk around before moving.
- Static from splash loading or synthetic clothing near vapour.
- A "nearly empty" tank still surges and still holds explosive vapour.

## Verify
- [ ] Ground connected first and disconnected last at every rack.
- [ ] Compartment, product grade and quantity checked against paperwork.
- [ ] Curves taken well below advisory speed; no harsh braking events.
- [ ] Walkaround done before leaving the rack or delivery point.
