# Anticipation and fuel-efficient technique

**Goal:** use less energy per kilometre without being slower in practice — the gains
come from avoiding waste (braking, idling, high revs, drag), not from crawling.

*Draft: savings depend heavily on vehicle and duty cycle; 5–15% from technique is
commonly reported in fleet training programmes. Measure your own baseline.*

## 1. Baseline and feedback
- Record current consumption (L/100 km, mpg, or kWh/100 km) over a typical week.
- Use the vehicle's trip computer or telematics coaching (harsh braking, idle time,
  over-revving, speeding) as feedback.

## 2. Anticipation — the biggest win
- Look far ahead (12–15 s): red light, queue, roundabout, hill crest. **Lift off early
  and coast** instead of driving up to it and braking.
- Every brake application turns paid-for momentum into heat. Aim for fewer, gentler
  brake applications.
- Time arrival at lights so you roll through on the change instead of stopping.

## 3. Engine and gears (combustion vehicles)
- **Shift up early** — around 2,000 rpm for petrol and ~1,500–1,800 rpm for diesel in
  cars (*vehicle-dependent*). Trucks: keep in the **green band** of the rev counter;
  automated gearboxes do this if you don't force kickdown.
- Use moderate acceleration (about ½–¾ throttle) rather than gentle crawling or full
  throttle — reaching cruising gear promptly is efficient.
- **Engine braking:** coasting in gear on a modern fuel-injected engine typically cuts
  fuel flow to zero (overrun fuel cut-off).
- **Cruise control** on level motorways; on hilly roads it can waste fuel by
  holding speed uphill — predictive cruise (GPS-based) on trucks solves this.

## 4. Speed and drag
- Aerodynamic drag rises with the square of speed; 110 vs 130 km/h saves a meaningful
  share of fuel for little time lost on most trips.
- Remove roof boxes/racks when not used; keep windows closed at speed; trucks: trailer
  skirts and cab deflectors set to trailer height.

## 5. Idling
- Switch off when stopped for more than ~30–60 seconds (outside traffic queues) unless
  needed for safety/equipment. Trucks: use auxiliary heaters/APUs rather than idling
  overnight; idling regulations exist in many places.

## 6. Vehicle condition
- **Tyre pressures** checked monthly (cold) — under-inflation raises rolling resistance
  and wear.
- Remove unnecessary weight; keep service intervals; alignment right.

## 7. EV range management
- **Regenerative braking:** use one-pedal or high regen settings in town; still
  anticipate — regen recovers only part of the energy.
- **Precondition** cabin/battery while plugged in; heating from the grid, not the battery.
- Cold weather can cut range noticeably (often ~20–30%+); plan charging stops with
  buffer (arrive with ~10–20%).
- Speed on motorways affects EV range strongly; slowing a little often beats an extra
  charging stop.

## Gotchas
- Coasting in neutral is less efficient on modern engines and reduces control — keep it
  in gear.
- "Eco" driving that holds up traffic causes others' braking and complaints; stay at
  safe, normal speeds.
- Measuring over one trip is noise; compare weeks.

## Verify
- [ ] Baseline consumption recorded, and re-measured after 2–4 weeks.
- [ ] Brake applications and harsh events reduced on telematics.
- [ ] Tyres at correct cold pressure.
- [ ] Idle time tracked and falling.
