# Pre-trip inspection and coupling (tractor-trailer)

**Goal:** leave the yard with a combination that is mechanically sound and properly
coupled — the dropped trailer and the unnoticed brake defect are both avoidable.

*Draft: inspection sequences and air-brake test values follow the US CDL manual
style; UK/EU daily walkaround checks cover the same items with different paperwork.
Use your licence manual's exact figures.*

## 1. Walkaround (same order every time)
Pick a fixed route around the vehicle, e.g. front → left side → rear → right side →
in-cab, so nothing gets skipped.
- **Tyres:** pressure (gauge, not boot-kick), tread depth, cuts/bulges, duals not
  touching, nothing between duals. **Wheels:** nuts present and not rusted-streaked
  (loose-nut sign), hub oil level, no cracks.
- **Brakes:** chambers, slack adjusters (pushrod stroke within limits), hoses not
  chafed, drums/discs and linings.
- **Suspension:** springs, airbags, U-bolts, shock absorbers.
- **Lights and reflectors:** all working and clean, including marker lamps.
- **Coupling area:** see section 2.
- **Trailer:** doors/curtains secure, body damage, landing gear, load secure (see
  transportation-logistics.road-driving.cargo-securement.tie-down-working-load-limits).
- **In cab:** mirrors, wipers, horn, gauges, warning lamps, seatbelt, emergency kit.

## 2. Coupling
1. **Check the fifth wheel:** greased, jaws open, release handle in the unlocked
   position, plate tilted toward the trailer.
2. **Check the trailer height:** the trailer nose should be slightly lower than the
   fifth wheel plate so the plate lifts it slightly — too high and the kingpin can
   ride over the jaws ("high-hitching").
3. Back straight in until just short of the trailer and secure the tractor.
   **Connect air lines** (emergency/red, service/blue) and supply air to charge the
   trailer, then **lock the trailer brakes** (trailer air supply out) so the trailer
   can't roll away as you back under. (This is the US CDL-manual order; some
   operators chock and connect after locking jaws — pick your manual's order and do
   it the same way every time.)
4. Back until the jaws lock with a firm stop.
5. **Tug test:** trailer brakes applied, tractor in low gear, gently pull forward
   two or three times. It must not separate.
6. **Get out and look:** with a torch, under the trailer: **no gap** between upper and
   lower fifth-wheel plates, jaws closed *around the shank* of the kingpin (not the
   head), release handle in the locked position, safety latch engaged.
7. Raise landing gear fully, stow the crank. Check lines aren't tangled or rubbing.

## 3. Air brake checks (typical CDL sequence)
With the system at full pressure:
- **Leak test:** engine off, brakes released — pressure drop within the allowed limit
  in one minute; then with brakes fully applied — within a (higher) allowed limit.
- **Low-air warning** activates as pressure is pumped down.
- **Spring brakes** apply automatically as pressure drops further.
- **Build-up rate** within the allowed time when the engine runs.
- **Parking and service brake tests:** gently try to pull against each.

## 4. Uncoupling
Park on firm level ground, chock trailer wheels if required, lower landing gear until
it just takes weight, disconnect lines and stow them, unlock the fifth wheel, pull
forward slowly until the tractor is partly clear, stop and check the trailer is
stable, then drive away.

## Gotchas
- Kingpin "locked" over the head of the pin: the trailer drops on the first bump.
- Landing gear left down and torn off on the first dip.
- Glad hands swapped (red/blue) give odd brake behaviour — trace them.

## Verify
- [ ] Walkaround done in the same order; defects reported (DVIR / defect book).
- [ ] Tug test passed **and** visual check of jaws, gap and handle.
- [ ] Landing gear fully raised and lines secured.
- [ ] Air brake checks within manual limits before departure.
