# Oversize route survey and permits

**Goal:** move a load that exceeds legal dimensions or weights along a route that
has been proven to take it, with the right permits, escorts and axle distribution.

*Draft: thresholds for "oversize", permit processes, escort requirements and travel
curfews are set per state/province/country (e.g. US state DOTs, UK abnormal load
notification via the road authorities' online system). Figures below are examples.*

## 1. Measure the load as it will travel
- Final **height** on the trailer (deck height + load + any blocking), **width** at the
  widest point (including mirrors/projections), **length** and overhangs, **gross
  weight** and centre of gravity.
- Measure, don't trust the spec sheet. Write it on the paperwork and in the cab.

## 2. Choose the equipment
- **Axle loads drive equipment choice.** Spread weight with more axles: lowboys/RGNs
  with jeep/booster axles, modular trailers or SPMTs for very heavy loads.
- Work out load position so each axle group stays within its permitted weight; a
  load placed 30 cm forward can overload the drive axles.
- Lower deck height to gain clearance (lowboy/well deck) before choosing a longer
  detour.

## 3. Route survey
Drive the route (or have a surveyor do it) with a **height pole** set slightly above
load height and record:
- Bridges and overpasses (posted vs measured clearance), overhead signs, traffic
  signals, **utility lines** (sagging in heat).
- **Bridge weight ratings** and whether the load is permitted to cross.
- Turns: swept path at junctions and roundabouts (use swept-path software or
  templates), median islands, signs and lamp posts to remove/swing.
- Road works, low trees, narrow villages, steep grades, soft verges, rail crossings
  (low-ground clearance and crossing time).
- Places to pull over and let traffic pass, park overnight, and turn around if
  blocked.

## 4. Permits and notifications
- Apply for permits with exact dimensions, weights, axle spacings, route and dates;
  allow lead time — superloads often need engineering review.
- Note conditions: travel hours/days (often daylight only, no holiday weekends),
  escort requirements by width/length, flags/lights/signs, speed limits.
- Notify police/utilities/rail operators where required; book utility line lifts.

## 5. Escorts and the move
- Pilot cars/escorts: lead escort checks clearances ahead (often with its own height
  pole) and warns oncoming traffic; rear escort protects from behind.
- Pre-move briefing: route, radio channel, hand signals, stop points, who calls the
  turns.
- Move at the permitted speed; stop and re-check if anything differs from the survey
  (new road works, a diversion).
- Never take a "shortcut" off the permitted route — the permit covers only that route.

## Gotchas
- Height that changes with air suspension or a load that settles; re-measure after
  loading and securing.
- Heat sag on overhead lines and new resurfacing raising the road.
- Turning radius: the trailer cuts the corner; get the swing wide enough before the
  turn.

## Verify
- [ ] Loaded dimensions and axle weights measured and within permit values.
- [ ] Every structure on the route checked for height and weight capacity.
- [ ] Permit conditions (times, escorts, signage) printed and in the cab.
- [ ] Briefing held with escorts; communication plan tested.
