# Space cushion and hazard scanning

**Goal:** see hazards early and keep enough space that other people's mistakes
don't become your collisions. Jurisdiction: UK Highway Code (rules 103 to 158); other
countries set their own gaps and rules.

## 1. Following distance (time, not metres)
- Pick a fixed point such as a sign; when the vehicle ahead passes it, count the seconds.
  Rule 126: drive at a speed that lets you stop well within the distance you can see to be
  clear, and never get closer than your overall stopping distance. Allow at least a
  two-second gap on high-speed roads and in tunnels where visibility is reduced; at least
  double it on wet roads and up to ten times greater on icy roads. Large vehicles and
  motorcycles need a greater distance to stop; a large vehicle in a tunnel should allow a
  four-second gap. If you have to stop in a tunnel, leave at least 5 metres.
- Tailgating (a gap too small to stop if the vehicle ahead brakes) is dangerous and enforced
  by the police.
- When someone cuts into your gap, drop back and re-create the space.

## 2. Look ahead and around
- Look as far ahead as you can to see brake lights, merging traffic and stopped vehicles
  early; keep your eyes moving rather than staring at the car in front.
- Check mirrors before changing lane (rule 133), with a quick sideways glance if needed, so
  you do not force another road user to change course or speed.
- Avoid sitting in others' blind spots (beside a truck's cab, behind its trailer): if you
  cannot see the truck driver's mirrors, they cannot see you.
- Keep an **escape route**: an open lane or gap you could steer into if the traffic ahead
  stops suddenly.
- In slow-moving traffic (rule 151) never get so close that you cannot stop safely, and
  leave room to manoeuvre if the vehicle ahead breaks down or an emergency vehicle needs to
  get past.

## 3. Junctions and built-up areas
- Be prepared for road users emerging at junctions, and for unmarked junctions on side
  roads and country lanes (rule 146).
- Anticipate what pedestrians and cyclists might do; pedestrians looking the other way may
  step out without seeing you (rule 146).
- In residential streets look out for vehicles emerging from junctions or driveways,
  vehicles moving off, car doors opening, children running out from between parked cars,
  and cyclists and motorcyclists (rule 152).
- Approaching a green that has been green a while ("stale green"): expect amber and cover
  the brake.

## 4. Make yourself seen and predictable
- Give clear signals in plenty of time and cancel them after use (rule 103).
- Use dipped headlights at night in built-up areas and in dull daytime weather (rule 115).
- Use the horn only while moving and when you need to warn others of your presence; never
  aggressively (rule 112).

## 5. The driver
- Distraction (rules 148-150): avoid loud music, reading maps, adjusting the radio, arguing,
  eating, drinking and smoking; do not use a hand-held phone while driving; if you need to
  use a screen or navigation, find a safe place to stop. Driver assistance systems do not
  reduce your responsibility.
- Speed (rules 125, 146): the speed limit is an absolute maximum, not a target; reduce speed
  for bends, vulnerable road users, weather and night driving.

## Gotchas
- A speed limit does not make it safe to drive at that speed in all conditions.
- Rushing an amber because the driver behind is close.

## Verify
- [ ] Following gap checked by counting, at least once a trip.
- [ ] Mirrors checked regularly and before every lane change or manoeuvre.
- [ ] You know your escape route in traffic.
- [ ] Phone and navigation set up before moving.
