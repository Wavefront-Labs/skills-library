# Bus stop approach and passenger safety

**Goal:** every passenger — standing, elderly, in a wheelchair, with a buggy — gets on,
travels and gets off without falling; and the service runs to time.

*Draft: generic transit/coach practice. Local rules on ramps, kneeling, buggy
spaces and timing points come from your operator.*

## 1. Smooth driving is the job
- Passenger falls on buses mostly happen during **pulling away, braking and
  turning**, not collisions. Drive as if everyone is standing and holding nothing.
- Brake early and progressively; ease off just before stopping so there's no
  final jolt.
- **Wait until passengers are seated or holding on** before pulling away, especially
  elderly passengers who just boarded — check the interior mirror.
- Take corners and roundabouts slower than in a car: lateral force throws standing
  people.

## 2. Stop approach
1. Scan the stop early: who's waiting, any wheelchair user or buggy, obstacles at the
   kerb (bins, poles, trees, parked cars).
2. **Position parallel and close to the kerb** (~10–20 cm) so the step gap is small
   and the ramp/kneel works. Approach at a shallow angle and straighten before the
   stop.
3. **Front overhang:** the front of the bus swings over the kerb on the approach —
   watch pedestrians standing at the kerb edge, poles and shelters.
4. Stop with the doors at the boarding point; avoid stopping with the doors against
   a tree, pole or puddle.
5. **Kneel** the bus if needed; deploy the ramp for wheelchair users and wait until
   they're positioned and secured in the wheelchair space (with backrest/brakes as
   required) before moving.

## 3. Pulling out
- Check interior mirror (passengers settled), doors closed, nearside mirror (running
  passengers), offside mirror and blind spot for overtaking traffic.
- **Tail swing:** as you steer out, the rear overhang swings the other way — towards
  the kerb or a pole. Watch your nearside rear before steering hard.
- Signal and move off only when there's a safe gap; local rules may require traffic
  to give way to buses, but don't assume they will.

## 4. Schedule adherence
- **Running early** at a timing point is usually worse than slightly late — it
  strands passengers. Hold at timing points if early.
- Running late: recover through efficient boarding (have passengers tap/pay
  promptly), not faster driving.
- High-frequency routes run on headway: control may tell you to hold to space the
  service; follow it.

## 5. Coach touring specifics
- Luggage hold: heavy items low, doors locked; check before departure and after each stop.
- Seat belts: announce they must be worn where fitted and required.
- Driver hours: coaches typically run on tachograph/hours rules (see
  transportation-logistics.road-driving.trip-planning-compliance.hours-of-service-trip-planning).
- Head counts at every departure from a stop or attraction.

## Gotchas
- Pulling away while a passenger is still walking down the aisle.
- Stopping too far from the kerb so the ramp won't deploy.
- Passengers running into the road to catch you — watch the offside too.

## Verify
- [ ] No harsh braking or acceleration events on telematics/feedback.
- [ ] Every stop: parallel and close to kerb, door clear of obstacles.
- [ ] Wheelchair users secured before moving off.
- [ ] No early departures from timing points.
