# Blind-side reversing and alley docking

**Goal:** put a trailer square into a dock or bay in few corrections, without
touching anything — and with nobody walking behind you unseen.

*Draft: technique is universal; left/right below assumes a left-hand-drive vehicle
driving on the right (sight side = driver's side = left). Mirror the sides for
right-hand drive.*

## 1. Principles
- **Sight-side backing whenever possible:** set up so the trailer swings toward the
  driver's window side, where you can see it directly. Blind-side reversing relies on
  mirrors only — avoid it by choosing the approach.
- **Steering the trailer:** put your hand at the bottom of the steering wheel; move
  your hand the way you want the trailer's rear to go. Small inputs, then *follow* the
  trailer by steering back toward it to stop the swing.
- **Go slowly** — idle speed or less. You can't correct what's happening too fast.
- **GOAL: Get Out And Look.** Before starting and as often as needed. There is no
  prize for doing it in one go.

## 2. The 90° alley dock (sight side)
1. **Set-up:** drive past the dock with the target on your sight side, about 1–1.5
   trailer widths away from the dock line, and pull forward until the trailer's
   rear is past the dock opening.
2. **Swing:** turn the tractor away from the dock (toward the blind side) while moving
   forward to angle the trailer — or start reversing with steering toward the dock
   to start the trailer turning in. Watch the trailer's rear corner in your sight-side
   mirror/window.
3. **Chase the trailer:** as the trailer angles in, steer to follow it so the tractor
   lines up behind it. Too much angle → the tractor catches up toward a jackknife.
4. **Straighten:** once tractor and trailer are in line, back straight in, using both
   mirrors to keep equal distance from each side of the bay.
5. **Pull-up** is allowed: if the angle is wrong, pull forward to straighten and try
   again — far better than forcing a bad angle.

## 3. Blind-side and offset backing
- When it's unavoidable, get a spotter or GOAL more often; use the convex mirror and
  any camera.
- **Offset backing** (moving across one lane while reversing): steer to start the
  trailer moving toward the new lane, then counter-steer to bring the tractor in line.

## 4. Jackknife avoidance
- Warning sign: the angle between tractor and trailer is growing quickly and the
  trailer is swinging further than you want.
- Stop, pull forward to reduce the angle, then restart. Don't keep reversing hoping it
  will straighten.

## 5. Working with a banksman/spotter
- Agree signals before moving (stop, come back, left, right, distance). Use a
  standard set if your site has one.
- **If you lose sight of the spotter, stop.**
- Spotter stands where they can see the rear and you can see them in the mirror —
  never directly behind the vehicle.

## 6. Site safety
- Reversing alarms and cameras help; they don't make it safe. Check for pedestrians
  every time.
- Check dock height, dock bumpers, and that the dock plate/leveller is clear.
- After docking: handbrake, chock/restraint if the site uses vehicle restraints.

## Gotchas
- Setting up too close to the dock leaves no room for the swing.
- Over-steering: big hand movements equal big trailer swings.
- Looking only at one mirror — the tractor's front corner can hit something on the
  other side as it swings.

## Verify
- [ ] Set-up chosen for sight-side backing where possible.
- [ ] GOAL performed before reversing and whenever unsure.
- [ ] Spotter signals agreed; stopped whenever sight was lost.
- [ ] Docked square with no contact, wheels chocked/restraint applied.
