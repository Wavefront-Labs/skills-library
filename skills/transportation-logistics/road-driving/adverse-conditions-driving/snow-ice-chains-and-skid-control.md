# Snow, ice, chains and skid control

**Goal:** decide well whether to go, then drive so you never need maximum grip —
and recover calmly if you lose it.

*Draft: chain laws, winter-tyre requirements and commercial-vehicle rules vary by
jurisdiction (e.g. US western state chain controls). Check your route's rules.*

## 1. Go / no-go
Check forecast and road reports; ask: is the trip necessary? Do I have the right
tyres/chains, fuel, warm clothing, food, water, a shovel, a charged phone? Can I wait
for ploughing/treatment? For trucks: is the load light (empty trailers in wind) or
the route steep? "Not going" is a professional decision, not a failure.

## 2. Low-traction driving
- Everything **gently**: steering, throttle, braking. Grip is used up by combining
  them; do one at a time.
- Speed down, following distance up (snow: 8–10 s for cars, more for trucks).
- Brake in straight lines before curves; coast through, power gently out.
- On hills: keep momentum up the hill without spinning; descend in a low gear,
  slowly. Don't stop on an incline if you can avoid it.
- Cruise control **off** on wet/icy roads.

## 3. Black ice
- Appears as a wet-looking dark surface when it's at or near freezing; shaded spots,
  bridges and overpasses freeze first.
- Signals: spray from vehicles ahead stops, tyre noise goes quiet, steering feels light.
- On ice: no sudden inputs; keep the wheel straight, ease off the throttle, and let the
  vehicle slow.

## 4. Skid recovery
- **Rear-wheel skid (oversteer):** look and steer where you want to go; ease off
  throttle; don't brake hard. Be ready for the counter-swing.
- **Front-wheel skid (understeer):** ease off the throttle, reduce steering lock
  slightly so the tyres regain grip, then steer again.
- **With ABS:** brake firmly and hold — the pedal pulsing is normal; keep steering.
- **Without ABS:** threshold or cadence braking to keep wheels rolling.
- Trucks: jackknife risk when trailer or drive wheels lock — release brakes to
  regain traction, steer to straighten.

## 5. Chains
- Practise fitting at home in daylight; carry gloves, a mat, a torch.
- Fit on the **drive wheels** (and other axles where required by law for trucks).
- Lay chain out, drape over the tyre, connect inside then outside, drive forward a
  little, re-tension after ~1 km/0.5 mile. Speed limit with chains is low (often
  ~30 mph / 50 km/h — *check the chain maker*).
- Remove when on bare road; chains on dry tarmac break and damage tyres.

## 6. Rain, fog, wind, heat
- **Aquaplaning:** feel of lightness in the steering; ease off, don't brake or steer
  sharply until grip returns.
- **Fog:** dipped headlights and fog lights per law; slow to stop within visible
  distance; don't follow tail lights too closely.
- **Crosswinds:** empty high-sided vehicles are most at risk; slow down, grip firmly,
  watch for gusts at bridges and gaps.
- **Heat/dust:** check tyre pressures (hot) and coolant; in dust storms pull off fully,
  lights off, foot off the brake so others don't follow your lights into you.

## Gotchas
- 4WD helps you go, not stop.
- Bridge decks freeze before the road either side.
- Defrosted windscreen but snow on the roof — it slides onto the screen when braking.

## Verify
- [ ] Go/no-go decision made consciously with forecast and kit checked.
- [ ] Following distance at least doubled in poor conditions.
- [ ] Chains test-fitted before you need them.
- [ ] You can describe the right input for oversteer and understeer.
