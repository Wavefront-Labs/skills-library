# Urban multi-drop and bridge-strike prevention

**Goal:** complete a day of town deliveries without hitting a bridge, a cyclist, a
kerb-side pedestrian or your own back — and without fines for the wrong loading spot.

*Draft: units and sign conventions vary (UK signs show feet-inches and metres;
US clearance signs in feet-inches). Check your local regulations on loading
restrictions and tail-lift inspection intervals.*

## 1. Know your vehicle's numbers
- **Height:** measure it (or read the in-cab height indicator) *including* any
  roof unit, fridge or aerial — and remember empty vehicles ride higher on air
  suspension. Write it in both units on a card in the cab.
- **Width** with mirrors, **length**, **gross weight** and current axle loads.
- Set these in a **truck-specific sat-nav**; car sat-navs and phone maps route you
  under low bridges.

## 2. Bridge strikes
- Read the posted clearance on approach; signs can be conservative but not always.
  If your vehicle is the same height or higher: **do not try it**.
- Arched bridges: clearance is often only at the centre — the sign may show the
  side height or a centre-only height.
- Resurfacing raises roads: an old clearance sign can be optimistic.
- If you're committed under an unknown height: stop, hazards, get out and look, and
  reverse with a guide if needed. Report any strike immediately to the railway/
  road authority — a struck rail bridge must be examined before trains run.

## 3. The drop routine
1. **Plan the stop** before arrival: loading bay or legal loading place, access
   restrictions, time windows, delivery instructions.
2. Park so the tail-lift/rear doors open to a safe area, not into a live lane.
3. **Handbrake on, engine off, keys out, doors locked** when you leave the cab.
4. Use a pallet truck or sack barrow — don't carry heavy items up steps alone.

## 4. Tail-lift safety
- Check before use: platform level, controls working, no hydraulic leaks, safety
  rails/roll stops in place.
- Load positioned centrally, within the lift's rated capacity, roll stops up for
  cages.
- **Stand on the platform only if designed for it**, with feet clear of the edge;
  never put feet under the platform.
- Keep bystanders clear; lower/raise only when you can see the whole platform.
- Statutory inspections (e.g. UK LOLER thorough examination every 6 months for lifts
  used to lift people — *check your jurisdiction*).

## 5. Vulnerable road users
- Blind spots on a rigid truck: directly in front of the cab, the nearside (kerb
  side) alongside the cab, and behind. Use all mirrors and cameras; consider class V
  and VI mirrors and nearside sensors.
- **Left turns (UK) / right turns (US)**: check the kerb-side mirror *last* before
  turning; cyclists filter up inside you at lights.
- Pulling away from a stop: walk round or check front-view mirror for a child or
  pedestrian right in front of the cab.

## Gotchas
- Raising the tail-lift into the closed doors or the body.
- Forgetting a roof-mounted item added since you last measured.
- Overloading the front axle as rear drops are unloaded — weight shifts forward.

## Verify
- [ ] Vehicle height known in feet-inches and metres and set in a truck sat-nav.
- [ ] No route under a bridge at or below your height.
- [ ] Tail-lift checked before first use of the day.
- [ ] Mirror/camera check of nearside and front before every turn and pull-away.
