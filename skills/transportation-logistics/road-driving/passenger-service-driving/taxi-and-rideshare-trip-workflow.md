# Taxi and rideshare trip workflow

**Goal:** every passenger gets in the right car, arrives safely by a sensible route,
pays correctly, and leaves a good rating — and you get home safe.

*Draft: licensing (private-hire vs hackney/taxi), app features and fare rules vary
by city and platform. Check your licensing authority's conditions.*

## 1. Before the shift
- Vehicle check: tyres, lights, cleanliness, no odours, phone mount and charger,
  first-aid kit, wheelchair restraints if a WAV.
- Licence badge displayed where required; dashcam on (and disclosed if your rules
  require signage).
- Know the day's events: concerts, sports, airport peaks, road closures.

## 2. Pickup
1. Stop **legally and safely** — not in bus stops or on zig-zags; use pull-ins.
2. **Verify the passenger:** ask "who are you picking up?" style — *they* say the
   name; you don't announce it. Rideshare passengers should check your plate too.
3. Help with luggage; let them get in kerb-side.
4. Confirm the destination aloud before setting off.

## 3. Route and drive
- Use navigation, but apply local knowledge; offer the passenger a choice when
  routes differ meaningfully ("motorway or through town?").
- Drive smoothly: passengers judge you on braking and cornering more than speed.
- Keep conversation to the passenger's lead; no political or personal topics unless
  they start it, and even then, carefully.

## 4. Passengers needing assistance
- **Wheelchair users (WAV):** deploy the ramp on level ground, ask how they want to
  be assisted, secure the chair with the 4-point restraint and the occupant with
  the lap-and-diagonal belt — chair brakes on. Never push a wheelchair without
  asking.
- **Visually impaired passengers:** offer your arm, describe the car's door and seat.
- **Assistance dogs:** carrying them is a legal obligation in many places, even with
  allergies, unless you hold an exemption.
- Elderly passengers: wait until seated and belted before moving.

## 5. Fares and disputes
- Meter: start at the right time and tariff; apps: start trip only when the
  passenger is in.
- Explain extras (tolls, airport fees, waiting time) **before** they apply.
- Disputes: stay calm, show the app/meter, offer a receipt and the operator's
  complaints route; don't block someone from leaving.

## 6. Your safety
- Keep doors locked while driving; know your app's emergency button.
- Trust instincts: you can decline or end a trip if a passenger is threatening —
  stop in a busy, lit place and contact the operator/police.
- Cash: carry little, bank often.
- Fatigue: rideshare earnings tempt long shifts; set a hard stop (see
  transportation-logistics.road-driving.trip-planning-compliance.hours-of-service-trip-planning
  for fatigue principles).

## 7. After the trip
Check the back seat for lost property; report it via the app/operator. Note any
damage or cleaning needed with photos.

## Gotchas
- Pulling away before a passenger has fully closed the door.
- Cancelling on accessible-trip requests — often a licence violation.
- Arguing about the route mid-trip — offer the choice at the start instead.

## Verify
- [ ] Passenger identity verified the right way round.
- [ ] Destination confirmed before departure.
- [ ] Wheelchair and occupant both secured before moving.
- [ ] Extras explained before they applied.
- [ ] Back seat checked after each trip.
