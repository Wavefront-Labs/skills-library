# Harnessing and single-horse driving

**Goal:** a correctly fitted horse, put to and taken out of the vehicle in the order
that never leaves it attached by traces alone, and driven with quiet, consistent aids.

> Draft: general practice for a single horse/pony in breastcollar or full-collar
> harness with a two-wheeled or four-wheeled vehicle. Learn hands-on with an
> experienced whip; harness styles and national road rules vary.

## 1. Harness fit, part by part
- **Collar** (full collar): flat hand fits between collar and windpipe at the bottom;
  fingers slide along the sides. Too big rubs and chokes when pulling; too small pinches.
- **Breastcollar**: sits above the point of the shoulder and below the windpipe;
  traces level or rising slightly to the vehicle.
- **Saddle and girth**: saddle just behind the withers; girth snug like riding.
- **Backband/tugs**: shafts sit in the tugs at about the point of the shoulder height
  when the horse stands.
- **Breeching**: a hand's width behind the buttocks; with the horse standing in the
  shafts it should come into action before the vehicle hits the horse when slowing.
- **Crupper**: under the tail, not pulling it up.
- **Bridle**: blinker eye centred on the eye; bit with ~1–2 wrinkles; noseband
  two fingers. Throatlatch a hand's width.

## 2. Putting to (safe order)
Always with a header (assistant at the horse's head) when learning.
1. Bridle last-but-one before putting to: never tie up a horse by its driving bridle.
2. Bring the **vehicle to the horse**, shafts raised, not the horse backed into it.
3. Shafts through the tugs.
4. **Traces** attached to the vehicle.
5. **Breeching straps** around the shafts.
6. **Belly band** (girth/wrapper) last, loose enough to let the shafts move slightly.
7. Reins through terrets; driver picks up reins and mounts from the off/near side per
   local practice. Header stands clear only when the driver says so.

**Taking out: exact reverse** — belly band, breeching, traces, then shafts. The
golden rule: the horse is never attached by the traces while the breeching or shafts
are free; that is how runaways and wrecks start.

## 3. Rein and whip handling
- **Achenbach-style** (common in competitive and traditional driving): both reins in
  the left hand — near rein over the index finger, off rein between middle and ring
  finger — right hand free for the whip and to shorten/correct.
- Contact like holding a small bird: steady, elastic.
- Turns: rotate the left hand (knuckles toward you for left, turn wrist for right),
  supported by the right hand on the rein. Make turns wide — the vehicle tracks inside.
- **Whip** replaces the rider's leg: a light touch behind the pad, never above the
  collar. Carry it at all times.
- Voice: a consistent set ("walk on", "trot", "whoa"), with "whoa" reserved for stop.

## 4. Road work
- Check brakes, lights and reflectors. Drive with traffic on the correct side.
- Hand signals before turns; with a groom on board, they can signal for you.
- Keep a following distance behind other vehicles; horses spook at plastic bags,
  dogs and air brakes. Look ahead and talk to the horse before the hazard.
- Downhill: walk, use breeching and brakes; never trot downhill on the road.

## 5. Gotchas
- Never remove the bridle while the horse is still attached to the vehicle.
- Rotting leather and stitching fail without warning — inspect every outing.
- Shafts too long poke the horse in turns; too short let the vehicle hit the hocks.

## Verify
- [ ] Collar, breeching and bridle pass the hand/finger checks above.
- [ ] Putting to follows shafts → traces → breeching → belly band.
- [ ] Taking out is the exact reverse, with a header at the head.
- [ ] Reins are held in one hand with the whip in the other on every drive.
- [ ] Brakes, lights and harness were inspected before going on the road.
