# Team hookup and trail commands

**Goal:** get the team out of the yard without a tangle or runaway, steer it by
voice, stop it reliably, and bring every dog home sound.

> Draft: general practice for a small to mid-sized (4–12 dog) recreational or
> freighting team on snow. Learn with an experienced musher; trail rules and race
> rules vary.

## 1. Equipment check before hooking
- Sled: brake claw, drag mat, **two snow hooks** (one on a line to the sled, one
  backup), handlebar, brush bow, bag.
- **Gangline** — main line; each pair of dogs attaches with **tuglines** (from harness
  to gangline) and **necklines** (collar to gangline, keeps the pair together).
- Harness fit: X-back or similar; the harness should end near the base of the tail,
  neck opening snug enough not to slip over the head, no rub at the armpits.
- Booties for abrasive or icy snow, plus spares.

## 2. Positions
- **Leaders**: front; take commands and set direction. Single or double lead.
- **Swing dogs**: behind leaders, help pull the team round corners.
- **Team dogs**: middle, the engine.
- **Wheel dogs**: nearest the sled; strong, calm, take the jolts on turns.
Pair dogs that work well together; separate known fighters by at least one pair.

## 3. Hookup order (yard to trail)
1. **Anchor the sled** first: snub line tied to a solid post or vehicle, plus snow
   hook set. Dogs pull hard from the first moment.
2. Lay out the gangline straight.
3. **Leaders first** — hold the gangline tight so they keep it stretched (a good
   leader "lines out"). Then wheel dogs, then the middle; or lead → swing → team →
   wheel, depending on your yard routine. Leaders first keeps the line straight.
4. Clip **neckline and tugline** on each dog; check snaps are closed.
5. Last check walking back from leaders to sled, then pull the hook, release the snub
   and say "Hike!" (or "Let's go").

## 4. Trail commands
| Command | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Hike / Let's go** | Start / go faster |
| **Gee** | Turn right |
| **Haw** | Turn left |
| **On by** | Pass a distraction (another team, a trail junction, wildlife) |
| **Easy** | Slow down |
| **Whoa** | Stop (with brake) |
| **Come gee / Come haw** | 180° turn right/left back along the team |
Say the command once, clearly, *before* the junction — leaders need a couple of
seconds. Reward correct turns immediately with praise.

## 5. Braking and anchoring
- **Drag mat** for speed control (stand on it partially), **brake claw** for stopping.
- When you stop: set the snow hook firmly, stamp it in, and keep a foot on the brake.
  Never walk away from a hooked team without tying off to something solid —
  hooks pop out in soft snow.
- Downhill: ride the brake/drag early; a sled running into the wheel dogs injures them.
- **Never let go of the sled.** A loose team will run until it tangles or is injured.

## 6. Dog care on the trail
- Check feet at every stop (splits, ice balls between toes, sore wrists).
- Watch for dropping the tugline slack (tired or sore dog); move it to wheel or bag it.
- Water/snack at intervals; in cold, feed high-fat; in warm (above ~0 °C/32 °F)
  reduce distance and speed — overheating is a real risk.
- Untangle immediately; a leg over the line causes injuries.

## Verify
- [ ] Sled was snubbed and hooked before the first dog was attached.
- [ ] Every dog has both neckline and tugline closed, and harness fits.
- [ ] Leaders respond to gee, haw, on by and whoa before you go on unfamiliar trails.
- [ ] You never left the team on a hook alone without a solid tie-off.
- [ ] Feet were checked at every stop and sore dogs rested or bagged.
