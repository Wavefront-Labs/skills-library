# Mob control and stock-route staging

**Goal:** stock arrive in condition — calm, fed, watered, none lost — with the
drover moving them by position and pressure rather than noise and force.

> Draft: general low-stress stockmanship and long-distance droving practice (e.g.
> Australian stock routes, transhumance). Road-movement permits, travelling-stock
> rules and biosecurity requirements are jurisdiction-specific; check them before
> setting out.

## 1. Reading the mob: flight zone and point of balance
- **Flight zone**: the distance at which an animal moves away from you. Wild or
  stressed stock: large; quiet stock: small. Work at its **edge** — step in to move
  them, step back to stop.
- **Point of balance**: roughly the animal's shoulder. Behind it → it moves forward;
  in front → it turns or stops. Walking parallel to the mob in the *opposite* direction
  to travel, behind the point of balance, speeds them up; walking with them slows them.
- Animals follow the leaders; let natural leaders emerge and keep them going.
- Pressure off as soon as they respond — the release is the reward.

## 2. Positions in a moving mob
- **Lead** (point): one rider/walker ahead to the side, controls pace and direction.
- **Wings/flank**: along the sides, hold the shape and stop breakaways.
- **Tail** (drag): behind, keeps stragglers moving — the slowest, weakest stock are here;
  do not push them faster than they can go.
- Dogs: work in the wings or tail; a dog that "works the head" can stop the mob.
  Use them for short, controlled pressure, called off quickly.

## 3. Pace and daily stages
- Cattle on a long drive: rule of thumb ~10–15 km (6–10 mi) per day; sheep less,
  and in heat less again. Faster loses condition fast.
- **Water is the stage-setter**: plan each day to reach a reliable water point;
  in hot weather stock may need water twice a day.
- Allow **grazing time** (several hours) each day; walk them out to feed early,
  rest in the heat of the day, move again late afternoon.
- Overnight: a holding paddock, a portable electric fence, or a night watch on
  open country. Count the mob into and out of every holding area.

## 4. Road crossings and road movements
1. Permits and notification as required by the road authority.
2. **Signage** both directions well before the mob (e.g. "stock ahead"), and
   flaggers/vehicles with lights at front and rear on public roads.
3. Cross where sight lines are long. Hold the mob back, stop traffic, then let the
   lead go; keep the mob tight and moving — gaps invite drivers to push through.
4. Clear the road and count before reopening.
5. Never move stock on a road at night or in poor visibility unless required and
   lit to local standard.

## 5. Problems and responses
- **Breakaway**: go wide and get ahead of the leaders, quietly; chasing from behind
  drives them further.
- **Rushing/stampede** (cattle at night): ride to the lead, turn them into a mill
  (circling) to slow them.
- **Lame or weak stock**: drop them into a following vehicle or a holding paddock;
  don't drag the whole mob to their pace.
- **Mixed mobs** (meeting other stock): keep apart; identification and biosecurity.

## 6. Records
- Count at start, every night, and at delivery. Note deaths with cause.
- Note water points, feed, condition score and any treatment given.

## Verify
- [ ] You can move a small group by stepping in and out of the flight zone without
      shouting.
- [ ] Each day's stage ends at reliable water and allows grazing time.
- [ ] Road crossings had signage, front/rear warning and a count after.
- [ ] Counts reconcile at every holding point.
- [ ] Weak or lame stock were removed rather than driven.
