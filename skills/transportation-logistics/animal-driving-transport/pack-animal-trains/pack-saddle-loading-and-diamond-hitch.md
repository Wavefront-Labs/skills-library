# Pack-saddle loading and the diamond hitch

**Goal:** loads that stay balanced and tight all day, animals that finish without
sores, and a string that can't drag a person or another animal off a cliff.

> Draft: North American horse/mule packing practice (sawbuck and decker saddles).
> Weight limits are rules of thumb; check local regulations (e.g. land-agency limits)
> and your animals' condition. The diamond hitch is best learned hands-on — the
> description below is an outline, not a substitute.

## 1. Weigh and balance
- **Load limit**: common guidance is ~20% of body weight for a horse or mule over
  rough country (some packers use up to ~25% for fit mules). A 1,000 lb (450 kg)
  mule → ~200 lb (90 kg) including panniers. Llamas: much less (~25% of a lighter
  animal is common, check with your outfitter).
- **Pair the sides within ~2 lb (1 kg)** of each other. Use a hanging scale; guessing
  is how loads slip.
- Heavy, dense items low and close to the animal; bulky, light items on top.
- Nothing hard against the animal's side; pad with soft items.

## 2. Saddle fit
- **Sawbuck**: two crossed wooden "bucks"; panniers hang from them.
- **Decker**: metal hoops and a padded half-breed (canvas cover); loads are
  manty-wrapped bundles slung with rope.
- Pad (blanket) clean and wrinkle-free; saddle sits behind the withers and doesn't
  touch the spine.
- Cinch(es) snug, front first; **breast collar** stops slip on climbs, **breeching
  (britchen)** stops it on descents. Re-check cinches after 15–30 minutes of walking.

## 3. Manties and panniers
- **Manty** (mantie): canvas tarp wrapped around a load and tied in a neat bundle
  (often with a manty rope using half-hitches). Two matched manties per animal.
- **Panniers** (boxes or bags) hang on the sawbuck with straps adjusted so both
  sides sit at the same height.
- Top load: bedroll or duffel laid across the centre, then a tarp (top pack) over all.

## 4. Lashing: box and diamond hitch
Use a lash rope (~40 ft / 12 m) with a cinch hook.
- **Box hitch** (simpler): lash rope around each side load forming a box that
  traps it against the saddle; good for panniers.
- **Diamond hitch** (outline): cinch under the belly, rope over the top pack; twist
  a loop under the main rope on top, pull bights down around each side load, then
  tighten in sequence front to back so a diamond shape forms on top. The tension of
  every segment holds both side loads up and in. Finish with a secure tie.
- Test: grab the load and rock it — the animal should move, not the load.

## 5. Stringing the animals
- Tie each animal's lead to the saddle of the animal ahead using a **breakaway loop**
  (a loop of light twine/"pigtail") so a fall breaks one link, not the whole string.
- 4–6 animals per string is manageable for one packer.
- **Never tie the lead rope to yourself or your saddle horn hard**; dally it or hold it.
- Lead rope length: enough for the following animal's head to be just behind the
  front animal's tail.

## 6. On the trail
- Check loads and cinches at the first stop and every 1–2 hours.
- Watch for a load listing — fix it immediately, before it rolls.
- Water at every chance; rest on long climbs.
- At camp: unload promptly, check backs for heat or swelling (a sore spot under a
  saddle often shows as hot or dry patches), highline or hobble animals, feed and water.

## Verify
- [ ] Each animal carries within its weight limit, sides within ~2 lb.
- [ ] Breast collar and breeching are fitted and cinches were re-checked.
- [ ] The rock test shows the load moving with the animal.
- [ ] Every link in the string has a breakaway.
- [ ] Backs were checked for heat or swelling at unloading.
