# Bell run and lockout routine (saturation diving)

**Goal:** a run where the bell is ready, the working diver locks out to a known job
inside the excursion limits, the bellman can rescue them, and everyone returns to
storage with nothing forgotten.

> Draft: general offshore saturation practice. **Only for orientation** — sat diving
> requires certification, company procedures, the system's operations manual and the
> dive supervisor's instructions. Bell configurations (two- or three-person, bailout
> type) vary.

## 1. Living at storage depth
- **Hygiene** is safety: ear-drops routine, wash and dry thoroughly, keep the habitat clean —
  external ear infections (otitis externa) end sat tours.
- Sleep schedules with the other team; keep noise down for the resting shift.
- Report symptoms early (rashes, joint aches, headaches) to the LST/supervisor.
- Speech is distorted by helium; comms use an unscrambler. Speak slowly.

## 2. Pre-run bell checks (with the bellman)
Typical checklist themes (follow your company list):
1. **Trunk and hatches**: seals clean, hatches operate, mating clamp secure.
2. **Bell gas**: main supply from surface, onboard emergency gas (cylinders on the bell),
   pressures logged.
3. **Bailouts**: each diver's bailout (open-circuit cylinder or semi-closed/rebreather
   bailout) charged and checked.
4. **Hot water** to the bell and suits; bell heaters.
5. **Comms**: bell to surface (hard-wire and through-water backup), bellman to diver.
6. **Lights, CO₂ scrubber in the bell, emergency kit** (thermal protection, survival
   rations for a lost-bell scenario).
7. **Umbilicals** for the divers coiled correctly in the bell, length known.

## 3. Transfer and launch
- Divers enter via the trunk (transfer under pressure), close the bell's inner hatch; the
  trunk is vented by sat control; bell is unmated and launched by the LARS crew.
- During descent: report checks at intervals; the bell stays at storage pressure, and
  that internal pressure holds the bottom door sealed until outside water pressure
  matches it at working depth.
- At working depth: equalise bell with the water, open the bottom door.

## 4. Lockout (working diver)
1. Bellman dresses the diver: helmet, hot water suit, bailout, umbilical connected,
   **comms check, gas check, hot water check**.
2. Diver exits, reports "diver in the water", follows the downline/structure to the job.
3. Bellman **tends the umbilical** from inside the bell — feeding and taking slack,
   watching gas and hot water gauges, relaying comms.
4. Stay within **excursion limits** (maximum upward/downward depth change from storage set
   by the company's tables) — check the depth for every part of the job.
5. Umbilical routing: avoid sharp edges, cross-overs with ROV tethers, and thrusters.
   Return the same path.
6. Work in agreed intervals; swap with the bellman at the planned point if the plan
   uses two divers.

## 5. Emergencies (outline)
- **Diver on bailout**: diver returns to the bell; bellman ready to assist, surface
  informed.
- **Unconscious diver**: bellman locks out on bailout, recovers the diver to the bell
  (lifting system/winch in the bell if fitted), closes the door, starts first aid.
  Practise this drill regularly — it's the bellman's main reason for existing.
- **Lost bell / main umbilical failure**: onboard gas, thermal protection, conserve
  energy, wait for recovery per contingency plan.

## 6. Recovery and back to storage
- Diver back in the bell, clean up, close the bottom door, report "door closed".
- Bell recovered and mated; trunk equalised by sat control, hatches opened only
  when pressures match.
- Debrief the run; log job progress, gas and hot water issues, equipment faults.

## Gotchas
- The bellman is not a passenger: stay alert to the diver's breathing on comms.
- Hot water failures cool divers fast in heliox — report them immediately.
- Small leaks or seal grit on mating flanges can prevent a safe seal; clean and inspect.

## Verify
- [ ] Bell checks covered gas, bailouts, hot water, comms, scrubber and emergency kit.
- [ ] Working depth was within excursion limits for the whole task.
- [ ] Bellman tended the umbilical and monitored gas and hot water throughout.
- [ ] The unconscious-diver recovery drill has been practised this tour.
- [ ] Hatches opened only with equal pressure on both sides.
