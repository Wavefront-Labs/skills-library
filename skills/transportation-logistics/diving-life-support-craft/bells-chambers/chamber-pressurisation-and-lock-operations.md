# Chamber pressurisation and lock operations

**Goal:** take occupants to pressure and back exactly on schedule, with a breathable,
fire-safe atmosphere, and pass items through the locks without ever opening a door
under pressure.

> Draft: general DDC/recompression-chamber practice (US Navy Diving Manual / IMCA
> style). **Chamber operation is a qualified role.** Rates, oxygen limits and treatment
> tables come from your company manual, the chamber's manufacturer and your diving
> medical adviser; the figures here are illustrative.

## 1. Pre-use checks
- Doors, seals and dogs: clean seals, no damage; door locking mechanism and the
  **pressure interlock** (where fitted) works.
- Gas: supply to blow down (air bank) pressurised; **BIBS** (built-in breathing system)
  masks on oxygen and air test-breathe; overboard dump works (exhaled gas goes outside).
- Gauges: two depth gauges per lock that agree, in calibration; clock/timer.
- Comms (sound-powered or electric) and lights; fire suppression system (water deluge)
  charged; extinguisher/hand hose inside.
- **Fire safety**: only approved clothing and materials inside; no oils, lighters,
  battery devices unless approved.
- Chamber log ready: occupants, table, times, depths, gas, ventilation.

## 2. Blowdown (compression)
1. Brief the occupants: equalising ears, signal if pain, BIBS use.
2. Seal the lock, start the clock at **leaving surface**.
3. Compress at the rate specified (e.g. ~20 fsw/min, ~6 msw/min, on many US Navy
   treatment tables — check), slowing or holding if an occupant can't equalise.
4. Reach depth, note the time; start the table timing per its rules.
5. Watch temperature: compression heats the chamber, decompression cools it.

## 3. Atmosphere control
- **Ventilate** to control CO₂ and oxygen build-up, especially when occupants breathe
  oxygen on BIBS (overboard dump keeps most O₂ out, but leaks happen).
- Keep chamber oxygen below the limit set by your manual (commonly ~23–25% by volume)
  using an O₂ analyser; ventilate when it rises.
- Track CO₂ by analyser or a ventilation-rate rule from your manual.
- Log every ventilation and analyser reading.

## 4. Medical / equipment lock transfers
The **only** safe sequence:
1. Inner door closed and sealed (it is held shut by chamber pressure).
2. Outer lock at **surface (0)** — confirm on the lock gauge. Open the outer door, load
   items, close and seal the outer door.
3. **Pressurise the lock** to chamber depth (equalise), confirm gauges equal.
4. Occupant opens the inner door, removes items, **closes the inner door**.
5. Vent the lock to surface; confirm **gauge reads zero** before touching the outer door.
Never open or force a door with any pressure difference — lock doors have killed people.
Items: no pressurised containers or sealed items that can burst or implode.

## 5. Decompression
- Follow the table's stops and rates exactly; start each stop/ascent on time.
- Occupants breathe gases per table (oxygen periods with air breaks as specified).
- Watch for symptoms (joint pain, numbness, breathing difficulty) and report to the
  supervisor/diving doctor at once; the table may need to change.

## 6. Bell launch and recovery (outline)
- Wet bells and closed bells are launched by a **LARS** (launch and recovery system):
  main lift wire, guide wires/clump weight, umbilical.
- Pre-launch: bell gas supplies, onboard emergency gas, comms, through-water comms,
  heating, doors/hatches, and clump weight positioned.
- Recovery to a closed-bell mating flange (sat/DDC): seat and clamp, equalise trunk,
  only then open hatches.

## Gotchas
- A single depth gauge can lie — two gauges, cross-checked.
- Rapid temperature changes alter apparent pressure; don't chase small variations.
- Oxygen-enriched chambers make any spark catastrophic — fire is the main chamber killer.

- [ ] Compression rate followed and logged with leaving-surface time.
- [ ] Chamber O₂ stayed below the manual's limit, with ventilations logged.
- [ ] Every lock transfer followed: close inner → equalise → open inner → close → vent → zero.
- [ ] Decompression stops and gas periods ran to the table, with times logged.
