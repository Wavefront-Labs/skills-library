# Table selection and omitted-decompression response

**Goal:** every diver gets the schedule that matches the dive they actually did, runs it
exactly, and any departure from it — omitted stops or symptoms — gets the prescribed
response straight away.

> Draft: principles common to US Navy, DCIEM and similar air tables used commercially.
> **Never decompress a diver from this page.** Use the tables and procedures your
> company and regulator mandate, exactly as written, under the supervisor and diving
> medical adviser. Numbers here illustrate the logic only.

## 1. Defining the dive correctly
- **Maximum depth**: the deepest point reached, by pneumo — not the average, not the
  planned depth.
- **Bottom time**: as your table defines it (commonly leaving surface → leaving bottom).
- **Gas**: air vs nitrox (nitrox uses equivalent-air-depth or dedicated tables).
- **Prior exposure**: repetitive dive within the table's window (residual gas), altitude,
  or a recent flight.

## 2. Selecting the schedule
- Rule: **next deeper depth, next longer time** — if 23 m isn't listed, use 24 m (or
  the next deeper row); if 37 min isn't listed, use 40 min.
- Exceptionally cold or arduous dives: many tables/manuals direct the supervisor to
  take the **next longer** schedule again (conservatism). Follow your manual's rule.
- Repetitive dives: find the **repetitive group** after the first dive, apply the
  surface interval credit, and add **residual time** to the next dive's bottom time.

## 3. In-water decompression
- Ascent rate as specified (e.g. ~9 m/min / 30 fsw/min in many US Navy air procedures —
  check).
- Stop depths measured at the diver's chest (by pneumo); time starts per the table's
  rule (on arrival, or from leaving the previous stop, depending on the table).
- Topside keeps a **running timer and log** for every stop, called to the diver.
- Oxygen in-water stops (where used) require the proper system and procedures.

## 4. Surface decompression (SurDO₂)
- Diver ascends with any in-water stops, **surfaces, undresses quickly**, enters the
  chamber and is recompressed to the chamber stop depth (e.g. 15 msw / 50 fsw for US Navy
  SurDO₂) to breathe oxygen periods with air breaks.
- The **surface interval** (leaving the last water stop → reaching chamber depth) has a
  strict maximum (e.g. 5 minutes in US Navy SurDO₂ — confirm in your manual).
  Rehearse the undress and transfer until it's routine.
- If the surface interval is exceeded, the table's procedure for an exceeded interval
  applies (often a longer chamber schedule or treatment table).

## 5. Altitude and flying after diving
- Diving at altitude needs altitude-corrected tables or corrections.
- Flying after diving: common guidance (e.g. DAN) is at least **12 h** after a single
  no-decompression dive, **18 h** after repetitive or multi-day dives, and **longer**
  after decompression dives; company and table rules may be stricter. Include driving
  over high mountain passes.

## 6. Omitted decompression
If a diver misses stops (e.g. emergency surface, gas failure, equipment problem):
- **Asymptomatic** diver: follow the table's **omitted decompression procedure** at once
  (typically recompress in the chamber within a short time limit and decompress on the
  specified schedule, or return to the water if the procedure allows).
- **Symptomatic** diver: treat as **decompression illness (DCI)** — recompress per the
  treatment table (e.g. US Navy Treatment Table 6 for serious DCS/AGE) and call the
  diving medical adviser.

## 7. Recognising DCI
- **Joint pain** (type I), skin marbling, unusual fatigue; **neurological** signs
  (numbness, weakness, visual or speech changes, confusion), breathing difficulty
  (chokes), loss of consciousness (AGE suspected after rapid ascent).
- Ask every diver after every dive — and for hours afterwards. Denial is common.
- First aid: **100% oxygen**, lie flat, fluids if conscious, recompress.

## Gotchas
- Using the planned depth rather than the deepest pneumo reading.
- Rounding bottom time down — always round up.
- A diver who "feels fine" may still need omitted-decompression treatment.

## Verify
- [ ] Schedule chosen from actual max depth and bottom time, next deeper / next longer.
- [ ] Repetitive and altitude factors applied.
- [ ] Every stop timed and logged; SurDO₂ surface interval within limit.
- [ ] Omitted decompression handled by the table's procedure without delay.
- [ ] Every diver was questioned for DCI symptoms after the dive.
