# Remote-control shunting and coupling

**Goal:** couple, move and spot cars precisely at walking speeds without damaging
couplers or lading, and without anyone getting between moving equipment.

*Draft: coupling speed limits, protection procedures (e.g. "three-step protection"),
RCL handover rules and hand-signal meanings are railroad- and country-specific.
Shown here as typical practice — your rules govern.*

## 1. Before moving
- **Job briefing** with everyone in the crew: the switch list, moves in order, who
  has control, radio channel, where the point (leading end) protection comes from,
  and hazards (other crews, close clearances, derails, open platforms).
- RCL/belt pack: function test (tilt/man-down, vigilance, speed select, horn, brake),
  confirm it's linked to the right locomotive, and that only one operator has
  control at a time.
- Know the speed limits: yard speed commonly ~10 mph / ~15 km/h, coupling speed far
  lower (often ≤ 4 mph / ~6 km/h — *check your rules*).

## 2. Shoving (pushing) moves
- The leading end must be protected: you (or a crew member) must see the track ahead
  of the leading car is clear, switches are lined, derails are off, and no other
  equipment is fouling. If you can't see it, don't shove — walk it or stop short.
- Give distances on the radio in car lengths ("ten cars… five… two… one… half…
  that'll do"); the operator stops if radio contact is lost.

## 3. Coupling
1. Check the knuckles: at least one open, couplers aligned (adjust drawbars with
   proper tools — never by hand or foot with equipment that can move).
2. Approach slowly and steadily; let the knuckles make it. Hard couplings damage cars
   and lading and can shift loads.
3. **Stretch test** (pull test): move away gently to confirm the joint is made before
   anyone goes between.
4. Air hoses: connect only with **protection** in place (equipment stopped, brakes
   applied, reverser centred/RCL in a protected state, per your going-between rules).

## 4. Spotting and switching techniques
- **Spotting:** place a car at an exact location (a door, a pit, a hopper). Approach
  slowly; give "car-length" countdown; finish with gentle increments.
- **Flat switching:** pulling/shoving cuts into tracks, uncoupled while stopped.
- **Kicking** (cars released while moving so they roll into a track): only where
  allowed, with someone to ride or tracks clear and protected, never into a track
  where people are working. Some railroads restrict it heavily.

## 5. Leaving cars
Securement: apply enough handbrakes for the grade and number of cars per your
securement table, test them (release air/let the cut push against the brakes), and
protect against fouling the clearance point.

## Gotchas
- Fouling: cars left past the clearance point of a turnout get side-swiped.
- Free-rolling cars on a slight grade start moving slowly and quietly — always
  secure.
- Radio "step-ons": confirm the last instruction; if in doubt, stop.

## Verify
- [ ] Job briefing done and every move's leading end protected.
- [ ] Every joint stretch-tested before going between.
- [ ] Coupling speeds within limits (event recorder/RCL logs).
- [ ] Left cars secured per table, clear of fouling points.
