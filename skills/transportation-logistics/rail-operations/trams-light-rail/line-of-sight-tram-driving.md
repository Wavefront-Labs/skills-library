# Line-of-sight tram driving

**Goal:** always be able to stop within the distance you can see to be clear, in a
street full of people and cars who often forget a tram can't steer.

*Draft: signal aspects (e.g. white bar/dot "BOStrab"-style signals, letter/arrow
aspects), point-indicator conventions and braking figures differ by network; use
your network's rulebook and vehicle data.*

## 1. Speed = what you can see
- On-street running is **line-of-sight**: your speed must let you stop short of any
  obstruction within your sight distance, using *service* brake, not emergency.
- A tram's service braking is far weaker than a car's: roughly ~1.2–1.5 m/s² service;
  emergency with magnetic track brakes perhaps ~2.5–3 m/s² (*vehicle-dependent; check
  your fleet*). At 40 km/h that's a stopping distance of tens of metres after reaction.
- Reduce speed where sight lines shorten: curves between buildings, parked vans,
  crowds at stops, school exits.

## 2. Reading the street
- **Tram signals** at junctions are separate from road traffic lights — read yours, not
  theirs. A green road light is not an authority for the tram.
- **Point indicators:** check the indication *and* look at the point blades before
  running over facing points; split/half-thrown points derail trams.
- **Turning cars:** the most common collision is a car turning left/right across the
  tram from alongside. Watch indicators and front wheels of parallel cars, and cover
  the brake approaching side streets.
- **Pedestrians:** people stepping back from a kerb, people with headphones, people
  running for your tram. Use the gong/bell early and lightly — a horn blast at close
  range can freeze people.
- **Cyclists:** wheels caught in rail grooves at shallow angles fall; give space and
  expect falls on wet rails.

## 3. Braking technique
- Normal: progressive service braking, smooth release before stopping so standing
  passengers don't fall.
- Emergency: full emergency (with track brakes and sand) only when needed — it can
  injure standing passengers. **Decide early**: emergency applied late is worse than
  firm service applied early.
- Low adhesion (leaves, grease, first rain): brake earlier, use sand per your rules,
  and report the location.

## 4. Stop service
1. Approach at a speed that lets you stop short of anyone on the platform edge.
2. Stop at the marker, doors on the platform side; check mirrors/CCTV for wheelchairs,
   prams and people near the doors.
3. Close doors with warning; check for trapped items; check road/tram signal and
   traffic before moving off — kerb-side cars may try to overtake on departure.

## 5. Interface with road traffic
- Obey road rules where the tram shares the road; your priority is never absolute
  in practice — expect drivers to block the tracks at queues.
- Don't enter a junction box you can't clear; a tram stuck across a junction blocks
  everything.

## Gotchas
- Familiarity: the same crossing every 12 minutes breeds complacency. Cover the brake
  at known hotspots every trip.
- Turning trams sweep a wide arc — the rear swings into adjacent lanes and footways.
- Overhead line neutral sections and power gaps: coast through as your rules require.

## Verify
- [ ] Speed kept within sight distance at every curve, stop and junction.
- [ ] Tram signals and point blades checked before every facing point.
- [ ] Service brake used for normal stops; emergency only when necessary and early.
- [ ] Low-adhesion locations and near-misses reported.
