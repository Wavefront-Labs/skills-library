# Runway sequencing and incursion prevention

**Goal:** maximise runway use without ever having two things on it that shouldn't be —
and know what you'll do before the go-around happens.

> Draft: general practice. Phraseology differs (ICAO "line up and wait" is now also FAA
> usage; "cleared to land" vs "runway X, cleared to land"), and minima such as wake
> intervals and runway separation are set by your State's rules and local orders.

## 1. The runway is a resource with one owner
- **One clearance on the runway at a time** unless your rules explicitly allow
  multiple (e.g. reduced runway separation, intersection departures with approval).
- Before *any* clearance onto or across the runway, run the **full-length scan**:
  threshold → departure end → every entry taxiway → approach path out to ~4 NM.
  Look out of the window first, then the ground radar/ASDE, then the strips.
- Use a **physical memory aid** whenever something occupies the runway: a strip
  in a "runway occupied" bay, a red holder, a flag on the EFS. Remove it only after
  you see the aircraft or vehicle clear.

## 2. Sequencing departures and arrivals
1. Build a **gap plan**: from approach spacing (e.g. 5–6 NM between arrivals) decide
   which gaps take a departure. Rule of thumb to calibrate locally: a departure needs
   the next arrival at least ~2–3 NM out when it starts its roll for a single-runway
   mixed-mode operation.
2. **Line up and wait** the departure as the preceding arrival crosses the threshold
   or lands — only if you'll clear it to go within a short time (local limit, often
   ~90 s). Never leave an aircraft lined up with an arrival on short final unseen.
3. Departure spacing: apply **wake turbulence intervals** (e.g. 2 min behind a Heavy
   for a following Light/Medium from the same point, under ICAO PANS-ATM — confirm
   the RECAT scheme your unit uses) or route/speed-based intervals.
4. **Landing clearance** once the runway will be clear — anticipating separation is
   allowed only where your rules permit; otherwise wait.

## 3. Integrating the visual circuit
- Give each circuit aircraft a **number and who to follow** ("number two, follow the
  Cessna on base").
- Extend downwind or orbit to make gaps; don't let circuit traffic turn base inside
  an IFR arrival without a clear sequence.
- Touch-and-goes block the runway longer than you think — budget a full landing gap.

## 4. Go-arounds and missed approaches
Plan before it happens:
- What does the go-around fly (published missed approach or local instruction)?
- What is the **conflict with the departure** you just released? If both are
  airborne, the standard fix is a diverging heading for one (local "go-around
  vs departure" procedure).
- Phraseology: "Go around, I say again go around, [reason if time]". Tell approach
  immediately.

## 5. Incursion traps
- **Conditional clearances** ("behind the landing A320, line up behind") — only when
  both parties can see the subject; repeat the condition first.
- **Intersection departures** hide aircraft at full-length holding points. Scan them.
- **Vehicles**: runway inspections, birds/wildlife patrol. Same memory-aid rule.
- **Similar callsigns** on frequency: warn both, change one if needed.
- **Readback check** of every runway designator and "hold short": the hearback is the
  barrier.
- Stop bars: never instruct crossing a lit red stop bar; switch it off first.

## Verify
- [ ] You do a full-length scan before every runway clearance, every time.
- [ ] A memory aid marks every runway occupation and is removed only on sight of clear.
- [ ] Wake intervals applied match your unit's scheme (ICAO legacy vs RECAT).
- [ ] You can state the go-around plan for each departure–arrival pair you release.
- [ ] All "hold short" and runway designators were read back correctly.
