# Engine failure and forced landing (single-engine)

**Goal:** the engine stopping becomes a glide to a field you picked with time to spare,
not a stall-spin while troubleshooting.

> Draft: general light-aircraft training practice. Your POH emergency checklist and
> memory items are authoritative; best-glide speed and restart flows are type-specific.

## 1. First seconds: Aviate
1. **Pitch for best glide** (Vg from POH, e.g. ~65–70 KIAS in many four-seat trainers
   at max weight). Trim for it. This is the single most important action.
2. Note wind direction (you should already know it from the departure/ATIS).
3. **Pick a field** within gliding range — preferably into wind, ahead or to the side,
   below your normal glide angle (a field you're overshooting is easier to fix than one
   you can't reach).

## 2. Restart attempt (if height allows)
Typical flow (adapt to your type, fixed order, hands touching each control):
- **Fuel**: selector to the other tank / BOTH; fuel pump ON.
- **Mixture**: RICH.
- **Carb heat**: ON (carb icing is a common cause in carbureted engines).
- **Throttle**: as POH (e.g. ~half open).
- **Magnetos**: check L, R, BOTH; then **starter** if the prop has stopped.
- **Primer**: locked.
Keep flying best glide toward the field throughout. Don't let troubleshooting cost
height you need.

## 3. Communicate
"**MAYDAY MAYDAY MAYDAY**, [callsign], engine failure, [position], [altitude],
forced landing [location], [persons on board]." Squawk **7700**. Do this while gliding;
if workload is high, aviate first and transmit when possible.

## 4. The forced-landing pattern
- Aim to arrive at a **high key** point (e.g. ~1,500 ft AGL abeam the upwind threshold
  side, depending on the method you were taught) and a **low key** point (~1,000 ft
  abeam the touchdown on downwind), then a base turn you can adjust.
- **Aim for the first third** of the field; flap only once you're sure of reaching it.
- Too high: S-turns, flap, sideslip (if approved). Too low: no flap, shorten the base,
  accept a nearer field.
- Before touchdown (secure checklist): mixture idle cut-off, fuel off, magnetos off,
  master off after last flap use, doors unlatched (if POH says so), harnesses tight.
- Touch down at the **slowest safe speed** under control. Wings level; if obstacles are
  unavoidable, steer between them so wings absorb the impact and the cabin doesn't.

## 5. Engine failure after takeoff (EFATO)
- **Nose down immediately** to best glide (the airplane loses speed fast at climb attitude).
- Land **ahead**, within ~±30° of the runway heading, below a height you have trained and
  briefed (commonly several hundred feet AGL; the "impossible turn" back to the runway
  needs far more height than most pilots expect, and practised technique).
- Brief it before every takeoff: "Engine failure on the runway: close throttle, brake.
  After takeoff below X ft: land ahead. Above X ft: consider options."

## 6. Upset recovery (basic, light aircraft)
- **Push** (unload: reduce angle of attack), **roll** wings level the shortest way,
  **power** as appropriate, **stabilise** (PNPR-style sequence varies by authority —
  some teach power reduction first in a nose-low spiral).
- Spin (if inadvertent and type approved for spin recovery): the POH method — commonly
  power idle, ailerons neutral, full opposite rudder, stick forward until rotation stops.

## Gotchas
- Fuel exhaustion and mismanagement cause many "engine failures" — the fuel selector
  is first in the flow for a reason.
- Stretching the glide by raising the nose lowers range and leads to stalls.
- Choosing a field behind you or downwind uses height you don't have.

## Verify
- [ ] You can hit best glide and trim for it within ~3 seconds.
- [ ] Field selected within 10 s, into wind where possible.
- [ ] Restart flow done in order from memory without losing glide speed.
- [ ] Mayday and 7700 transmitted.
- [ ] Every takeoff included an EFATO brief with a land-ahead height.
