# Glassy- and rough-water landings, taxi and docking (seaplanes)

**Goal:** land on water whose surface you have actually read, control the airplane
afterwards like a boat, and arrive at the dock without a crunch.

> Draft: general seaplane-rating practice (e.g. FAA Seaplane, Skiplane and Float/Ski
> Equipped Helicopter Handbook-style). Speeds, power settings and attitudes vary
> by type; use your POH and instructor.

## 1. Reading the water (before you commit)
- Fly over the area at a safe height: **wind** from wind streaks, boat drift, smoke,
  the calm "lee" near the upwind shore, birds sitting facing wind.
- **Obstructions**: boats, swimmers, floating debris, logs, shallows (colour changes),
  wires across rivers and narrows.
- **Surface**: glassy (no waves — no depth perception), rippled (ideal), choppy,
  swell (long wavelength; land along the crests).
- Plan the takeoff too — enough run for your weight and density altitude.

## 2. Amphibian check (non-negotiable)
Landing gear **UP for water, DOWN for runway** — say the surface aloud on every
approach ("landing on water, gear up, four blue/up lights"). Gear-down water
landings flip the airplane.

## 3. Glassy-water landing
You can't judge height above mirror-smooth water.
1. Set up the landing attitude (slightly nose up, near the attitude for touchdown on
   the step) with power, **at a height you can judge** — e.g. over the last visual
   reference (shoreline, weeds) at ~100–200 ft.
2. Establish a **steady descent of ~100–200 fpm** (type dependent) at that attitude,
   using power.
3. **Hold attitude and rate until contact** — do not flare or look for the water.
4. On contact: power off, hold the attitude, let the airplane come off the step.
Use a long, clear area; this approach eats distance.

## 4. Rough-water landing
- Land into wind, with the **minimum safe speed** and a slightly nose-high attitude
  to keep the bows up.
- Touch down on the **back side of a wave** or along crests for swells.
- Power off immediately and keep the nose up as it slows. Don't let it skip into the
  next wave with flying speed.
- If it porpoises (rhythmic pitching on the step), **set a steady attitude**; if it
  grows, add power and go around.

## 5. On the water: three taxi modes
- **Idle taxi** (displacement): slowest, water rudders down, best for manoeuvring.
- **Plowing taxi**: high power, nose high, spray everywhere — avoid except briefly;
  hard on the prop and engine cooling.
- **Step taxi**: on the step at speed; fast but turns are risky — downwind step turns
  can capsize (centrifugal + wind). Turn from upwind to downwind at idle.
- **Sailing**: engine off or idle, use wind and water rudders/flaps/doors as sails to
  move backward or sideways into a tight dock.

## 6. Docking
- Approach the dock **slowly, downwind side last**, aiming to arrive at idle or engine off
  with momentum to coast in.
- Brief the passenger/dock helper: they take the float strut or cleat line, never
  fend off with a leg between float and dock.
- Water rudders up in shallow water; check for prop clearance from people.

## Gotchas
- Glassy water near a shoreline hides the shore distance — never descend toward it.
- Wind-lane estimates on rivers are distorted by current; observe drift on the water.
- Floats leak: pump them before every flight day (check each compartment).

- [ ] Gear position was called aloud for the landing surface.
- [ ] Glassy-water approaches set attitude and rate above a visual reference.
- [ ] Downwind turns were made off the step at idle.
- [ ] Floats pumped and dock helpers briefed before arrival.
