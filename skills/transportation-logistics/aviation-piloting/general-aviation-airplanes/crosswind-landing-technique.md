# Crosswind landing technique (light airplanes)

**Goal:** touch down with the longitudinal axis aligned with the runway, no sideways
drift, upwind main wheel first — and know before you start when to go elsewhere.

> Draft: standard flight-training technique (e.g. FAA Airplane Flying Handbook-style
> methods). Your aircraft's POH and your instructor's limits apply; "maximum
> demonstrated crosswind" in the POH is a tested value, not a structural limit.

## 1. Before the approach: is it within limits?
Crosswind component ≈ wind speed × sin(angle between wind and runway).
Quick rule (clock method): 15° ≈ ¼, 30° ≈ ½, 45° ≈ ¾, 60°+ ≈ full.
Example: wind 240/18G26, runway 27 → 30° off → ~9 kt steady, ~13 kt in gusts.
Compare with the POH demonstrated crosswind (often ~15 kt in light trainers) and your
personal minimum. Pick the runway most into wind if there's a choice.

## 2. Approach speed and configuration
- Add **half the gust factor** to normal approach speed: gusts 26 − 18 = 8 → +4 kt.
- Some pilots use less flap in strong crosswinds for better control authority; follow
  the POH and your training.

## 3. From crab to sideslip
1. On final, **crab** (heading into wind) to track the extended centreline.
2. Transition to the **wing-low (sideslip) method** by short final — many instructors
   use about 200–300 ft or the last few hundred metres:
   - **Rudder** aligns the nose with the runway (opposite rudder to the wind, i.e.
     downwind rudder).
   - **Aileron into wind** stops drift: lower the upwind wing just enough that the
     airplane tracks the centreline.
   - Rule: *rudder for alignment, aileron for drift*.
3. If you run out of rudder before the nose is aligned, the crosswind is beyond your
   aircraft/technique — **go around** and choose another runway or airport.

## 4. Flare and touchdown
- Hold the sideslip through the flare; as speed decays you need **more** aileron and
  rudder, not less.
- Touch down on the **upwind main wheel first**, then the other main, then the nose
  (or tail).
- Alternative for some types and pilots: "crab and kick" — hold the crab and kick it
  straight with rudder in the flare. Works but timing-critical; most GA training
  favours the sideslip.

## 5. Rollout
- **Keep flying it**: progressively increase aileron into wind as speed falls, to full
  deflection at the end of rollout.
- Rudder/steering to hold the centreline; tailwheels need more active feet and
  never relaxed until tied down.
- Taxi: aileron positions per wind quadrant (climb into headwind, dive away from tailwind).

## 6. Gusty conditions
- Small, quick corrections; don't chase every bump with big inputs.
- A gust in the flare can balloon you — be ready to add power and go around rather
  than force it down.
- Wind shear near hangars and trees on short final: expect sinks and lifts.

## Gotchas
- Relaxing the aileron after touchdown lets the upwind wing rise — a common cause of
  runway excursions and ground loops.
- Landing in a crab sideloads the gear and can cause a swerve.
- Fatigue and unfamiliar aircraft cut your personal limit — set it before flight.

## Verify
- [ ] You computed the crosswind component and gust increment before the approach.
- [ ] By short final the nose was aligned with rudder and drift stopped with aileron.
- [ ] Touchdown upwind main first with no visible sideways drift.
- [ ] Aileron increased into wind through the rollout.
- [ ] You had a go-around decision point if rudder ran out.
