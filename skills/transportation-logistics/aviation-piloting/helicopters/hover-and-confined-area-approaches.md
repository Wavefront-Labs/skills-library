# Hover work and confined-area approaches

**Goal:** get in and out of a tight off-airport site with power and escape route known
before you commit — not discovered at 20 ft.

> Draft: general helicopter training practice. Hover ceilings IGE/OGE, limits and
> recce procedures come from your RFM and operator; the recce mnemonic below is one
> of several used by schools.

## 1. The hover
- **Cyclic** controls disc tilt (position), **collective** height, **pedals** heading.
  Every input couples: collective up → more torque → yaw (nose right on main rotors
  turning counter-clockwise seen from above, e.g. US designs; nose left on French/
  Russian-style clockwise rotors) → opposite pedal needed.
- Look **well ahead** (20–50 m), not at the ground below; peripheral vision detects drift.
- Make small, early corrections; pressure, not movement.
- In wind, hold the nose into wind; crosswind or tailwind hovering needs more pedal and
  can approach tail-rotor limits.

## 2. Power check (know your margin)
At a hover at the site's elevation and conditions (or computed from charts):
- Note **hover power** (torque/MAP) at the planned hover height.
- Compare with **maximum available**. The difference is your margin.
- Use the RFM **HIGE/HOGE** charts: if the approach or departure requires out-of-ground-
  effect hover (tall trees, no run-on), you need HOGE capability at the weight and
  density altitude — or a different plan.

## 3. Reconnaissance (high and low)
**High recce** (e.g. 500 ft+ above the site, orbiting):
- Size, **shape and slope** of the site; surface (soft, debris, dust/snow brownout).
- **Obstacles**: wires (look for poles — you won't see wires), trees, buildings.
- **Wind** direction (smoke, water, trees, drift on orbit).
- **Approach and departure paths** into wind, clear of obstacles, with an **escape
  route** (where you go if you abort).
- Forced-landing options en route in.

**Low recce** (a slow pass on the approach path, ~ at obstacle height + margin):
confirm the surface, obstacles at the edge and the touchdown point.

## 4. The approach
1. Fly a constant-angle approach into wind (steeper than normal if obstacles require
   — but steeper means lower airspeed and more VRS risk).
2. **Commitment point**: decide where you can still abort (go around through the escape
   route). Past it, you're landing.
3. Maintain **> ~10–15 kt** effective translational lift as long as possible; transition
   to hover over the touchdown point.
4. Hover, check skids/clearance, set down gently; check slope limits.

## 5. Departure
- Hover check: power and controls at the departure point.
- Use the longest into-wind path; if obstacles demand a vertical climb, ensure HOGE
  margin, climb vertically to clear, then accelerate through translational lift.
- Never "drag" through obstacles hoping for translational lift.

## 6. Hazards
- **Vortex ring state / settling with power**: descending at low airspeed (<~30 kt)
  with significant power and descent rate (> ~300 fpm, order of magnitude). Avoid by
  keeping airspeed and limiting descent rate; recover with forward cyclic (or the
  Vuichard technique where trained) — not more collective.
- **LTE** (loss of tail-rotor effectiveness) with winds from the left and tail quadrants at
  low speed in US-rotation helicopters (unanticipated right yaw): apply full left pedal, increase forward speed, reduce power
  if altitude allows.
- **Dynamic rollover** on slopes: one skid on the ground + rolling moment; lower collective
  smoothly.
- **Brownout/whiteout**: plan a reference and a no-hover landing technique.

## Verify
- [ ] You have a computed or observed hover power margin for the site.
- [ ] High and low recce covered wires, wind, surface, approach/departure and escape.
- [ ] You stated a commitment point before the final approach.
- [ ] Descent rate below ~300 fpm at low airspeed on the approach.
- [ ] Departure path was into wind with the power for it confirmed.
