# Thermal soaring and outlanding decisions

**Goal:** stay up and go somewhere using rising air — and when the air stops cooperating,
land safely in a field chosen calmly at height, not grabbed at 300 ft.

> Draft: general gliding practice (BGA/SSA-style training). Decision heights below are
> typical club numbers; set your own with your instructor and adjust for terrain and
> glider type.

## 1. Finding thermals
- Cumulus: look for **firm, growing** bases with crisp edges; work the sunny/upwind side.
  Ragged, dissolving clouds are dying.
- Blue days: look for birds, other gliders circling, dust devils, sun-facing slopes,
  contrasting ground (dark fields, towns, quarries), and wind convergence lines.
- Thermals drift downwind with height — when you expect one under a cloud, search upwind.

## 2. Centering
1. On a surge on the vario (or felt lift), wait ~1–2 s, then roll into a **30–45°
   bank** turn.
2. Note where the strongest lift is in the circle (use a landmark or the vario/audio).
3. Shift the circle toward it: a common method — when the lift is strongest, **reduce
   bank (open the turn) for a few seconds**, then resume. Repeat until climb is steady.
4. Fly a steady speed ~5 kt above minimum sink at that bank (check your glider's polar);
   speed variations wreck centering.
5. Check FLARM and lookout: in a shared thermal, circle the same direction as gliders
   already in it.

## 3. Speed to fly between thermals (MacCready)
- Set the **MacCready (MC) value** to the climb rate you expect in the next thermal
  (e.g. 2 kt). The flight computer (XCSoar, LXNAV, etc.) or speed ring then shows the
  optimum cruise speed: faster in sink, slower in lift.
- Low on height or uncertain of the next climb → **lower MC** (slower, flatter glide,
  more range). Plenty of height and strong day → raise it.
- Take climbs weaker than the MC setting only when needed for safety.

## 4. Final glide
- Glide computer: set MC, ballast, bug factor (wing contamination; ~5–10% degradation
  is commonly assumed), wind, and **safety arrival height** (e.g. 500–1,000 ft above
  the destination).
- Start final glide only when the computer shows arrival at or above the safety height
  at a conservative MC; re-check every few minutes. Headwind increases on descent are
  common.

## 5. Outlanding decisions (decide early)
Typical decision ladder (adjust for terrain and your training):
- **~2,000 ft AGL**: actively select a group of landable fields in reach, while still
  searching for lift.
- **~1,500 ft AGL**: pick **one field** and stay within easy reach of it.
- **~1,000 ft AGL**: commit — fly the circuit; stop looking for thermals.
**Field selection (the 5 S's or similar)**: **Size** (several hundred metres, more for
high-performance gliders), **Shape**, **Slope** (land uphill, never across a slope),
**Surface** (short crop or pasture; avoid tall crops, standing water, plough), and
**Surroundings** (wires — look for poles, trees on approach, livestock). Also: wind
direction for the landing (smoke, crop movement, drift).

## 6. Flying the field landing
- A normal circuit with high key / low key points; land into wind, uphill if sloped.
- Approach speed per manual + wind/gust allowance; use airbrakes to control the angle.
- After landing: secure the glider, tell the retrieve crew position, and inform the
  landowner. Note damage to crops for reporting.

## Gotchas
- "Just one more thermal" below the commit height causes most outlanding accidents.
- Fields that looked fine from 2,000 ft often hide wires, slopes and crops — check
  again at circuit height and divert early if needed.
- Launch failures (winch/aerotow) are a separate drill — brief them before every launch.

## Verify
- [ ] Your circle moved toward the strongest lift and climb rate stabilised.
- [ ] MC was lowered when height or confidence dropped.
- [ ] Final glide was started only with your safety height shown at conservative MC.
- [ ] You had fields selected at ~2,000 ft and committed by ~1,000 ft AGL.
- [ ] The chosen field passed size, slope, surface and surroundings checks.
