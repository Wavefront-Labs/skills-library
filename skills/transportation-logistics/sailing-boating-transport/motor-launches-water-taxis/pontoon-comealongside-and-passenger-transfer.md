# Pontoon comealongside and passenger transfer

**Goal:** come alongside cleanly, keep the boat still while people step on and off,
and run the next hop without swamping anyone with wash.

*Draft: licensing (e.g. boatmaster/commercial endorsements), passenger limits and
harbour bylaws are local. Handling notes assume a single or twin outboard/inboard
launch.*

## 1. Plan the approach
- **Read the water:** which way is the tide flowing at the pontoon (look at moored
  boats, buoys, weed)? Where's the wind from?
- **Stem the stronger** of the two — approach heading into the tide (or wind, if it
  dominates). It gives steerage at low speed over the ground.
- Brief crew: which side, which line first (usually the **midships/spring line**),
  fenders set at the right height.

## 2. Coming alongside
1. Approach at a shallow angle (~20–30°), slow, aiming at the middle of the space.
2. Use short bursts of power; **neutral is a control** — coast in.
3. Just before contact, steer away and give a touch of astern to stop and swing the
   stern in (with a single screw, account for **prop walk** — the stern kicks to one
   side in astern; know which).
4. Get the spring/midships line on first; with engine ahead against a forward spring
   and the helm turned away from the pontoon, the boat sits pinned alongside.
5. Twin engines: use differential thrust to walk the boat sideways.

## 3. Passenger embarkation and landing
- **Boat secured or pinned before anyone moves.** Never let passengers jump while
  the boat is closing.
- Crew member on the pontoon or at the gunwale gives a hand — "one at a time, step
  down onto the deck, not onto the seat".
- Bags passed separately, not carried while stepping.
- Count passengers on and off against your licence limit.
- **Briefing** before leaving: lifejacket location/donning (or worn if policy), where
  to sit, keep hands inside, hold on in waves, what to do if someone goes overboard.

## 4. Short-hop pilotage
- Know the harbour speed limits and **wash** zones; your wash can capsize small craft
  and damage moored boats and banks. Watch your wake behind you, not just ahead.
- Keep a proper lookout for swimmers, kayaks, paddleboards — low and hard to see.
- **Kill cord** attached on open boats when running.
- Cross channels at right angles; give way per collision regulations and local rules
  (large vessels in narrow channels have priority).

## 5. Departing (springing off)
- To leave a pontoon with wind/tide pinning you: use a spring line and engine to swing
  the bow or stern out, then motor away.
- Check for lines in the water before engaging — props love ropes.

## 6. Man overboard (MOB)
Shout and point, throw a lifebuoy, press MOB on the plotter, turn back keeping the
casualty in sight, approach from downwind, **engine in neutral/stopped** near the
person. Practise recovery with your boat's ladder/sling.

## Gotchas
- Coming in down-tide: you arrive faster than you think and can't stop.
- Passengers stepping onto a cushion seat or slippery gunwale.
- Fenders too high/low for a fixed vs floating pontoon at different tide states.

## Verify
- [ ] Approach planned into the stronger of wind/tide with a line plan.
- [ ] No passenger moved until the boat was secured or pinned.
- [ ] Passenger count and briefing done every trip.
- [ ] Wash watched astern; speed limits kept.
