# Ferry berthing and vehicle-deck loading

**Goal:** a safe, repeatable cycle — berth, unload, load, secure, depart — many times
a day, in all weather, without the routine dulling the checks.

*Draft: procedures and limits are vessel-specific (Safety Management System, stability
booklet, cargo securing manual). Flag-state rules on passenger counts, door
indicators and drills apply. This is general practice, not a substitute.*

## 1. Approach and berthing
- **Know the vessel's limits** for the berth: maximum wind speed/direction for
  berthing, tidal height window for the linkspan, minimum under-keel clearance.
- Plan the approach for **set and drift**: tidal stream across the entrance and wind
  on the high sides of a ro-ro (huge windage). Approach slightly up-tide/up-wind of
  the berth line and let them carry you in.
- Reduce speed early; stop the vessel's way with engines, not with the berth fenders.
- Bow thrusters lose effect at speed (often above ~3–4 knots) — have the ship slowed
  before relying on them.
- **Abort point:** decide in advance the point at which, if not properly lined up,
  you go round again. Make the call early.
- Lines/automated mooring: confirm secured before lowering the ramp.

## 2. Ramp and door operations
- Lower the linkspan/ramp only when moored and the indicator panel shows the
  vessel secured; confirm ramp seated and level before traffic moves.
- **Bow/stern doors:** before departure, the bridge door indicators must show
  **closed and secured** (the Herald of Free Enterprise disaster is the classic case
  of sailing with bow doors open). Get positive confirmation from the deck crew, not
  just "nobody said they were open".

## 3. Vehicle deck loading
- Load per the **stowage plan**: heavy vehicles (trucks, coaches) in designated lanes,
  usually centreline/low, keeping the vessel upright and within trim limits.
- Dangerous goods vehicles in permitted locations only, with segregation and
  documentation.
- Spacing: leave walkways and fire-fighting access; keep clear of fire hydrants and
  escape doors.
- **Lashing:** heavy goods vehicles lashed per the cargo securing manual and weather
  forecast; cars typically handbrake on and in gear (passenger cars may need lashing
  in heavy weather).
- Calculate or check stability (draft marks, loading computer) before departure.

## 4. Passengers
- **Count** passengers boarded (ticketing/gate systems) and keep within certificate
  limits; the count is needed in an emergency.
- No passengers left on vehicle decks once at sea (usual rule) — sweep and close.
- Safety announcement, muster station signage, regular drills and lifesaving
  equipment checks.

## 5. Keeping the schedule
- Recover time in port turnaround (efficient discharge/loading plan) rather than
  pushing speed and berthing harder.
- When weather exceeds limits: cancel or divert early and communicate.

## Gotchas
- The 30th berthing of the week is when someone skips the door check.
- Wind gusts on high-sided ferries while the ramp is down — monitor moorings during
  loading.
- Overloading the vehicle deck forward without checking trim.

## Verify
- [ ] Berthing approach planned for wind/tide with an abort point.
- [ ] Doors confirmed closed and secured on the bridge before sailing.
- [ ] Stowage plan, DG placement and lashing completed; stability checked.
- [ ] Passenger count within certificate and recorded.
