# Lock working and river-current handling

**Goal:** pass locks, bridges and tunnels safely and efficiently, and handle a river
in flow — the inland hazards that catch out people used to open water.

*Draft: modelled on UK canal practice (hand-operated locks, windlass) with notes for
staffed/mechanised locks and European waterways (CEVNI rules). Local signage,
lock-keeper instructions and navigation authority rules always take priority.*

## 1. Locks — the cill is the danger
- Upper gates sit on a **cill** (a ledge) under the water. Going **down**, a boat too
  far back can catch its stern on the cill as water drops and be tipped or sunk.
  **Keep forward of the cill marker** throughout.
- Going **up**, a boat too far forward can catch its bow under the upper gate beams or
  be swamped by water from the paddles.
- Never hold a boat in a lock with a rope tied off — as the level changes it will hang
  the boat. Hold lines in hand or use a centre line loosely.

## 2. Working a hand-operated lock (going up, lock empty)
1. Check the lock is empty and lower gates open (or set it: close top gates and
   paddles, open bottom paddles to empty, open bottom gates).
2. Boat in, forward clear of the top gate but not too far forward; bottom gates
   closed, bottom paddles closed.
3. Open **top ground paddles first** (gentler), then gate paddles once the water is
   above them — slowly, watching the boat.
4. When levels equalise, open top gates; boat out.
5. Close gates and paddles behind you (check local rule — some waterways leave gates
   open).
- **Windlass:** never leave it on the spindle; if the ratchet slips, the spinning
  windlass injures people. Keep the pawl engaged.

Going down: fill the lock, enter, close top gates and paddles, check stern forward of
cill, open bottom paddles slowly.

## 3. Bridges and tunnels
- Swing/lift bridges: stop road traffic (barriers) as instructed, keep crew clear of
  moving parts, and close/lower behind you.
- Tunnels: headlight on, horn as required, check whether one-way working applies
  (timetable or signals); keep right (or per local rule) when passing.

## 4. River current
- **Check river conditions before leaving:** many authorities post stream
  warnings (e.g. yellow/red boards) — don't navigate on "red" or equivalent.
- Going **downstream** your speed over the ground is high and steerage poor; you need
  power to steer. **Turn to face upstream** before mooring or entering a lock
  approach — stop against the current, never with it.
- Weirs: keep well clear of the weir stream; lock approaches often sit next to them.
  Follow signs and buoys.
- Mooring on rivers: allow slack for level changes, moor facing upstream, use strong
  points.
- Carry an anchor ready to deploy on rivers — engine failure upstream of a weir needs it.

## 5. Waterway rules
- Keep to the right (CEVNI and UK rivers); pass oncoming craft port-to-port.
- Slow past moored boats (no wash, no breaking wave behind you).
- Commercial craft and large vessels have priority in narrow channels and locks; do as
  lock-keepers say.
- Learn signal lights at locks (red/green, often red-green for preparing).

## Gotchas
- Opening gate paddles on the uphill side before ground paddles — boat flooded.
- Forgetting the windlass on the spindle.
- Underestimating stopping distance going downstream.

## Verify
- [ ] Cill marker located and boat kept forward of it going down.
- [ ] No ropes tied off in locks.
- [ ] River stream warnings checked before departure.
- [ ] Moored and approached locks facing upstream on rivers.
