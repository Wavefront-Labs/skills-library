# Unfamiliar-boat delivery prep and watch system

**Goal:** get someone else's boat — whose faults you don't yet know — to its destination
safely, with a rested crew, and hand it over with a clear record.

*Draft: commercial delivery may require specific qualifications, coding/flag
rules and insurance endorsements; check before accepting. Checklist below is
generic for a 10–20 m yacht or motorboat.*

## 1. Before accepting
- Insurance: does the owner's policy cover a delivery skipper/crew and the route/
  season? Get it in writing.
- Documents: registration, VAT/tax status, radio licence, EPIRB registration, crew
  passports/visas, cruising permits.
- Scope: who pays fuel, berths, repairs, crew travel; what happens if you abandon for
  weather or defects.

## 2. Pre-delivery survey (assume nothing works until shown)
- **Rig:** go up the mast or binocular inspection — swage terminals for cracks,
  split pins taped, halyards and sheaves, furler operation, chainplates.
- **Hull/through-hulls:** every seacock opens and closes; hoses double-clipped where
  required; bilge pumps (electric and manual) tested; stern gland/shaft seal.
- **Steering:** cables/hydraulics, quadrant bolts, emergency tiller fits and works.
- **Engine:** oil, coolant, belts, impeller (carry spares), fuel filters (replace if
  doubtful — dirty tanks block filters in the first rough sea), run under load for
  an hour.
- **Electrics:** batteries' state, charging, nav lights, instruments, autopilot.
- **Safety:** liferaft service date, EPIRB/PLBs, flares, lifejackets with crotch
  straps and lights, jacklines, fire extinguishers/blanket, first-aid kit, grab bag.
- Make a defect list with photos; owner signs it off before departure.

## 3. Sea trial / shakedown
A few hours (or a short coastal hop) before the open-water leg: hoist and reef every
sail, run the engine hard, test autopilot, check for leaks under way. Many deliveries
are saved here.

## 4. Passage plan
- **Weather window:** look at multiple models (GRIB data and official forecasts) for
  the full passage plus margin; plan for the worst credible case on the longest open
  stretch.
- Route with waypoints, hazards, traffic schemes, tidal gates; **bail-out ports** along
  the way.
- **Fuel:** consumption at cruise × motoring hours + 20–30% reserve; jerry cans if
  needed.
- Food and water for the passage + several days' delay.
- Shore contact with your plan and check-in schedule.

## 5. Watch system
- **Three crew:** three-watch rota (e.g. 3 on, 6 off) gives decent sleep.
- **Two crew:** 3-on/3-off at night, flexible by day; fatigue builds — plan short legs.
- Fixed handover routine: position, course, traffic, weather, sail plan, anything odd.
- Night rules: tethered on deck, wake the skipper for any doubt, sail changes or
  ships closing.
- Log hourly: position, course, speed, wind, barometer, engine hours.

## 6. Arrival
Arrange berth and customs/immigration ahead; clear in as required; clean and secure
the boat; write a handover report (defects found, fixes, fuel used, engine hours).

## Gotchas
- Leaving on schedule pressure into a marginal window.
- Contaminated fuel stirred up by the first big sea.
- The emergency tiller that doesn't fit.

## Verify
- [ ] Insurance and documents confirmed in writing.
- [ ] Survey defect list agreed with the owner.
- [ ] Sea trial completed before the main leg.
- [ ] Weather window checked against multiple sources; bail-outs noted.
- [ ] Watch rota and handover routine briefed; hourly log kept.
