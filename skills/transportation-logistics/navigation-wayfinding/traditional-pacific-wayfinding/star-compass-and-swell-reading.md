# Star compass and swell reading — finding islands without instruments

**Goal:** understand and practise the main techniques of traditional Pacific
wayfinding: holding a heading from rising/setting stars, sun and swells; keeping a
mental reckoning of position; and expanding a small island into a large target with
natural signs.

**Respect note:** these are living knowledge traditions held by Pacific communities
(e.g. Micronesian navigators of the Carolines; the Hawaiian revival through Mau
Piailug, Nainoa Thompson and the Polynesian Voyaging Society). Systems and terms
differ by island group; learn from practitioners and their published teaching material.
This is a summary, not a substitute.

## 1. The star compass
- The Hawaiian star compass (as taught by the PVS) divides the horizon into **32
  houses** of 11.25°, each named, grouped into quadrants. Stars rise in a house on the
  east and set in the matching house on the west at the same angle from north/south.
- Each star rises and sets at the **same points on the horizon** all year (at a given
  latitude). Learn the rising/setting houses for a set of stars; as one star climbs
  too high to steer by, use the next star rising in the same house — a **star path**.
- Steer by keeping the chosen star at a fixed position relative to the canoe (over the
  bow, a stay, or a mark on the rail). Low stars (near rising/setting) are most useful.
- Sun: at sunrise and sunset its bearing is known from the season (amplitude);
  midday sun is hard to use for direction.

## 2. Swells
- Ocean swells generated by distant, persistent weather systems keep their direction
  for days. Identify the dominant swells (often 2–3 systems from different directions)
  at dawn/dusk when stars give direction.
- Feel them through the hull: the pitch and roll pattern changes when the canoe changes
  heading relative to the swell. At night and under cloud, hold course by keeping the
  swell feel constant.
- Recheck swell directions against stars whenever possible; swells change with weather.

## 3. Keeping track of position (mental DR)
- The navigator tracks direction steered, estimated speed (from wake, bubbles and
  experience) and time continuously, often hardly sleeping.
- Latitude cues: the altitude of known stars as they cross the meridian, and pairs of
  stars that rise or set together at specific latitudes; **zenith stars** pass directly
  overhead at the latitude of a given island.
- **Etak (Carolinian concept):** the canoe is imagined stationary while a reference
  island off to the side "moves" back along the star points of the horizon; progress is
  counted in segments as the reference island's bearing passes from one star point to
  the next.

## 4. Expanding the target (landfinding)
An island may be visible only ~10 nm away, but signs extend detection range:
- **Birds:** some species (e.g. white terns, noddies) fly out to fish by day and return
  to land at dusk — their evening direction points to land within a few tens of miles.
  Species ranges matter; frigate birds and others are less reliable.
- **Clouds:** clouds piling up and stationary over an island; a green tint under clouds
  over lagoons; a cloud "pinned" while others move.
- **Swell refraction and reflection:** islands bend and reflect swells, creating
  interference patterns felt in the hull.
- Floating vegetation, water colour, smell of land.
- **Screen of islands:** aim for a chain or archipelago rather than one dot; once inside
  the screen, sail along it to the target.

## 5. Practice on land and with modern backup
- Learn star rising/setting points from your latitude on clear nights; use a
  planetarium app to check.
- Sail short coastal legs steering by stars and swells, logging GNSS positions only
  afterwards to compare estimates.
- Modern voyaging canoes carry escort vessels and instruments for safety — the
  navigator does not use them; respect that discipline while still sailing safely.

## Gotchas
- Mistaking a high star's azimuth: steer by low stars or by their rising house.
- Swell direction changing with a new weather system and not noticed under cloud.
- Treating birds as certain — species and season matter.

## Verify
- [ ] Can name rising/setting points of 10+ stars at your latitude.
- [ ] Can hold a course by swell feel for an hour and check it against stars.
- [ ] DR estimate after a practice leg compared with GNSS track.
- [ ] Can list three landfinding signs and their limits.

*Uncertainty:* house names and counts follow the Hawaiian (PVS) system; Carolinian and other traditions use different structures; bird-range figures are approximate.
