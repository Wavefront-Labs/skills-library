# Event egress, contraflow and detour planning

**Goal:** move a large, sudden demand (a stadium emptying, a festival, an evacuation)
through the network without gridlock or unsafe crowding, using a plan agreed well
before the day.

## 1. Estimate demand
- Attendance × mode split (car, transit, walk, rideshare) → vehicles: e.g. 40,000
  attendees, 55 % by car at 2.5 per car = 8,800 vehicles.
- **Egress is the hard part:** most leave within 30–60 minutes of the end; ingress
  spreads over 2–3 hours.
- Add background traffic for the time and day.

## 2. Compare with capacity
- Car park exits: a single exit lane discharges very roughly 400–600 veh/h with
  manual control (*site-specific; observe or survey*); signalised road lanes ~1,800
  veh/h of green.
- 8,800 vehicles out in 1 hour needs on the order of 15+ effective exit lanes — if you
  have 6, plan for ~2.5 h or reduce demand (shuttles, park-and-ride, staggered
  activities, transit).
- Identify the **bottleneck** (often one junction or a parking exit), and design around
  it.

## 3. Build the plan
- **Routes:** inbound and outbound routes by origin direction, signed with event-specific
  signs; separate pedestrians from vehicles at crossing points.
- **Closures and detours:** for each closure, a detour route with capacity, signed at
  every decision point (advance, turn, confirm), avoiding residential streets and
  low bridges/weight limits; tell local residents and businesses.
- **Signals:** special timing plans for egress (long greens on outbound movements,
  pedestrian scramble phases near venues), manual control by police/officers at key
  junctions.
- **Transit:** shuttle bus loading areas with queuing space, bus priority lanes.
- **Emergency access:** a guaranteed blue-light route that stays open throughout.
- **Communications:** VMS, apps/navigation providers fed with closures, ticket emails
  with parking/route instructions.

## 4. Contraflow (evacuations and very large egress)
- Reverse the inbound lanes of a divided highway to outbound.
- Critical points: **entry crossover** (where traffic moves to the reversed side) and
  **termination** (where it merges back) — the termination is usually the bottleneck;
  design it with as many lanes as possible.
- Close and guard every on-ramp/access to the reversed carriageway; staff each one.
- Set start/end triggers and times in the plan; it takes hours to set up and clear.

## 5. Day-of control loop
- Command post with police, highway agency, transit, venue and stewards on one channel.
- Monitor queues by CCTV/drones/staff; trigger pre-agreed adjustments (open
  overflow car park, switch signal plan, hold crowds inside with entertainment).
- Record times: gates open, event end, last car out, roads reopened.

## 6. After-action review
Compare predicted vs. actual clearance time, complaints, incidents; update the plan.

## Gotchas
- Navigation apps route around closures through residential streets — engage
  providers early.
- Weather and early-leaving (lopsided game score) change egress shape.
- *Capacity figures are rules of thumb; calibrate from counts at your venue.*

## Verify
- [ ] Demand estimate compared with bottleneck capacity; clearance time stated.
- [ ] Every closure has a signed detour with capacity checked.
- [ ] Emergency access route defined and protected.
- [ ] Contraflow access points listed with staffing and termination capacity.
- [ ] After-action times recorded and plan updated.
