# Scene protection, lane closures and quick clearance

**Goal:** protect people at a road incident, keep the closure as small and short as
safely possible, and reopen without causing the next crash.

## 1. The first minutes
1. **Position the first vehicle as a block** upstream of the scene, angled to shield the
   work area, wheels turned away from workers, with a buffer of open space between it
   and the scene (many guides use roughly 15–30 m / 50–100 ft at speed; *follow your
   agency SOP*).
2. Lights: use enough warning to be seen but reduce forward-facing/dazzling lights at
   night once advance warning is established — glare hides responders.
3. High-visibility apparel for everyone outside a vehicle; walk and work facing traffic
   where possible; never turn your back to the flow.
4. Size up: casualties, fire/fuel/hazmat (placards, smell, leaks), lanes affected,
   resources needed. Report to the control centre so it can trigger upstream warning
   (VMS, lane signals).

## 2. Building the closure
- Upstream to downstream: **advance warning area → transition (taper) → buffer →
  activity area → termination**.
- Taper length (MUTCD merging taper, US units): **L = W·S²/60** for speeds ≤ 40 mph,
  **L = W·S** for ≥ 45 mph (L feet, W lane offset feet, S mph). For a 12 ft lane at
  60 mph: L = 720 ft. Shifting tapers are half; shoulder tapers a third.
  Incident closures are often shorter at first and extended as resources arrive.
- Cones/flares spaced roughly the speed in mph in feet along the taper (e.g. 60 ft at
  60 mph), closer in curves.
- Place advance warning far enough upstream that drivers see it **before the back of
  the queue** — and move it back as the queue grows.
- Close the minimum lanes needed; reopen lanes as soon as work allows.

## 3. Responder coordination
- Establish **unified command** for anything beyond a minor crash: police (scene and
  investigation), fire/EMS (life safety, hazmat), highway agency (traffic control,
  infrastructure), towing/recovery.
- Agree early on the **clearance plan and target time**; request the right recovery
  equipment the first time (heavy recovery for HGVs, cargo transfer, absorbent).
- Hazmat: isolate and deny entry upwind/uphill per the Emergency Response Guidebook
  initial isolation distances until identified.

## 4. Quick clearance
- **Move it** laws/policies: minor crashes with drivable vehicles move to the shoulder
  or off the road. Authority-removal laws let responders clear vehicles/cargo blocking
  lanes without owner consent.
- Investigation (photos, measurements, drone/total station) done efficiently; agencies
  with open-roads policies set clearance targets (e.g. 90 minutes).
- Spills: absorb and sweep; don't flush fuel into drains.

## 5. Reopening and queue protection
- Remove the closure **downstream to upstream** (reverse of placement), the
  activity area first, advance warning last.
- Keep a vehicle or VMS warning at the **back of the queue** until it dissipates —
  secondary crashes in the queue are a major risk.
- Tell the control centre lanes are open so signs are cleared promptly.

## Gotchas
- Parking responder vehicles on both sides of the road "for convenience" creates
  chicanes and gawking.
- Flares near fuel spills — use cones/LEDs there.
- *Distances and formulas above are US MUTCD values; other countries use different
  tables (e.g. Chapter 8 in the UK).*

## Verify
- [ ] Blocking vehicle positioned with a buffer; responders in high-visibility apparel.
- [ ] Taper length and device spacing appropriate to speed; advance warning ahead of the queue.
- [ ] Unified command and clearance target agreed and logged.
- [ ] Closure removed in reverse order; queue protection maintained until the queue cleared.
