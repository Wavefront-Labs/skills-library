# TTM plans, flagging and stop/slow operations

**Goal:** move traffic past roadworks safely and predictably, with the workers protected
and the layout matching an approved plan. Taper formulas and closure order are in
`transportation-logistics.traffic-transport-signalling.highway-incident-management.scene-protection-lane-closures-and-quick-clearance`;
this skill covers planned work.

## 1. Read the plan before you set out
A temporary traffic management (TTM / TTC) plan states: road and speed, layout type
(from a standard drawing/typical application), sign list and spacing, taper and buffer
lengths, lane widths kept, pedestrian/cyclist route, working hours, and who signs
off. Check on site that reality matches: sight lines, junctions and accesses inside
the zone, bus stops, hills or bends that hide the queue. If it doesn't fit, stop and
get the plan amended — don't improvise.

## 2. Set-up order
1. Park the works vehicle (with beacons) as protection upstream.
2. Place **advance signs first** ("Road works ahead", "One lane road ahead", "Be
   prepared to stop"), furthest upstream to nearest, facing traffic, on the side
   you're working.
3. Then the taper, then longitudinal cones along the buffer and work area, then
   end/termination signs.
4. Take it down in **reverse order** (last placed, first removed); advance signs
   last.
Typical sign spacing scales with speed (US MUTCD table: e.g. ~100 ft urban low speed
up to 1,000+ ft on freeways (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked); UK Chapter 8 tables by road type) — use the table, not
estimates.

## 3. Stop/slow flagging (one lane, two-way traffic)
- Station the flagger **visible from the stopping sight distance** for the speed,
  standing on the shoulder, never in the open lane, with an escape route.
- Equipment: STOP/SLOW paddle (US: at least 18 in wide with an octagonal STOP face in the 2009 MUTCD, Section 6E.03; 11th edition wording (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)) or Stop/Go board
  (UK); high-visibility class 2/3 garments; radio to the opposite flagger.
- Signals:
  - **Stop:** STOP face to traffic, free arm raised, palm facing traffic.
  - **Proceed:** SLOW face to traffic, free arm motions traffic on.
  - **Slow:** SLOW face held steady.
- Coordination: the two ends agree by radio *which vehicle is last* ("last car is a
  white van") before releasing the other direction.
- For longer sections use a **pilot car** to lead each platoon, or portable traffic
  signals with all-red clearance set for the full length (length ÷ slowest likely
  speed, e.g. 300 m at 15 km/h ≈ 72 s).
- Flaggers do not take on other tasks (phones, helping workers) while flagging.

## 4. Night works
- Light flagger stations and work areas without glare toward drivers (aim
  floodlights down and away).
- Retroreflective signs and devices clean; additional advance warning where queues
  are less expected.
- Plan for impaired and fatigued drivers — larger buffers where possible.

## 5. Inspection and records
- Inspect the layout at set-up, at intervals during the shift (e.g. every 2 h or per
  plan), and after any incident; drive through it at traffic speed.
- Log: time, condition, devices replaced, changes made and by whom.

## Gotchas
- Signs left up when the works are inactive ("cry wolf") teach drivers to ignore them —
  cover or remove.
- Queues longer than the advance warning: extend warning upstream.
- Pedestrian routes forgotten — provide an accessible route or a signed alternative.
- *Device sizes, sign spacing and colours vary by jurisdiction; follow the governing
  manual.*

## Verify
- [ ] On-site layout matches the approved TTM plan, or the plan was amended.
- [ ] Signs placed upstream-first and removed in reverse.
- [ ] Flaggers visible at stopping sight distance, with radios and escape routes.
- [ ] Portable signal all-red set for the section length.
- [ ] Inspection log complete for the shift.
