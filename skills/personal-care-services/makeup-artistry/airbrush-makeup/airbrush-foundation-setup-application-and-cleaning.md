# Airbrush foundation: setup, application and cleaning

**Goal:** lay down a very thin, even, long-wearing base (face or body) with an airbrush,
controlling paint and air separately so the result looks like skin, not paint — and
keep the gun clean so it doesn't spit mid-application.

## 1. The kit
- **Compressor** with adjustable pressure (PSI) and ideally a regulator and gauge.
  Makeup-specific compressors are quieter and lower-pressure than hobby units.
- **Gun:** usually **gravity-feed** (small cup on top) — uses little product.
  **Dual-action:** pressing the trigger down releases air; pulling it back releases
  makeup. This control is essential. Single-action guns are simpler but less controllable.
- Nozzle/needle size: makeup guns are typically ~0.2–0.35 mm. Bigger = more flow, coarser.
- **Product systems** (never mix in one gun without full cleaning):
  - **Water-based**: lightweight, natural finish, less durable; good for dry skin.
  - **Silicone-based**: very long-wear, water-resistant, buildable; standard for bridal
    and HD. Needs a silicone-compatible cleaner.
  - **Alcohol-based**: extremely durable (body, tattoo cover, SFX); harsh on dry/sensitive
    skin; must be well-ventilated.
- Cleaner matched to the system, lint-free tissues, a cleaning pot, small brush.

## 2. Pressure and distance
Common starting points (adjust for product and system manufacturer's guidance):
| Area | PSI (approx.) | Distance |
|------|---------------|----------|
| Face foundation | ~8–15 | ~10–15 cm |
| Detail (blush, brows, contour) | ~5–10 | ~3–8 cm |
| Body / tattoo cover | ~15–25 | ~15–20 cm |
Higher pressure = finer atomisation but more overspray; too low = spatter.

## 3. Application technique
1. Prep skin as for any base (moisturise; primer suited to system). Remove excess oil.
2. Shake the product, add **a few drops** to the cup — you need far less than a liquid
   foundation.
3. **Start air first, then pull back for product; return product before releasing air.**
   This prevents spatter at the start and end of each stroke.
4. Keep the gun **moving** in small circles or sweeping passes, perpendicular to the skin.
   Stopping in one place creates spots or drips.
5. **Build in thin layers**; wait a few seconds between passes. Coverage comes from
   multiple mists, not one heavy pass.
6. Ask the client to close eyes and hold their breath for passes over the nose/eyes area;
   spray around the eyes from a slightly greater distance with lower pressure.
7. Spot-conceal with a brush where heavier coverage is needed, then mist over to blend.
8. Some systems need setting (powder or fixing spray); silicone systems often don't.

## 4. Troubleshooting
| Problem | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Spitting/spatter | Dried product on needle tip, too low PSI, releasing product before air | Clean tip, raise PSI, correct trigger sequence |
| No product flow | Clog in nozzle | Back-flush (finger over tip briefly — check your gun allows it), cleaner soak |
| Streaks/drips | Too close, too much product, stopping in place | More distance, less product, keep moving |
| Heavy, masky look | Too many layers or too dark | Thin layers; match shade before spraying |

## 5. Cleaning (between clients and end of day)
- Between colours or clients: empty cup, add cleaner, spray through into a tissue or
  cleaning pot until it runs clear.
- End of day: more thorough clean; periodically disassemble the needle and nozzle per the
  manufacturer — **handle the needle gently; a bent tip ruins the spray pattern.**
- Hygiene: the gun doesn't touch the skin, but clean the cup and exterior; never reuse
  product left in the cup on the next client.

## Gotchas
- Overspray: cover hair and clothing; drape a cape. Work in ventilated space.
- Silicone systems on very dry or flaky skin can emphasise texture — moisturise first.
- Airbrush is less forgiving than brushes for colour matching; mix and test on the jaw first.
- Practise on a paper towel or your arm before a client; control comes with hours.

## Verify
- [ ] Correct cleaner and product system; no cross-system residue.
- [ ] Air-first/air-last trigger sequence used; no spatter on the face.
- [ ] Base built in thin layers; skin texture visible, no streaks at arm's length.
- [ ] Shade matched at the jaw before spraying the whole face.
- [ ] Gun flushed clear; needle undamaged; ready for the next client.
