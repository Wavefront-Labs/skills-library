# Camouflaging scars, pigmentation and tattoos

**Goal:** make a scar, birthmark, vitiligo patch, pigmentation or tattoo visually
disappear (or reduce as far as the client wants) with high-coverage camouflage
products that look like skin, last all day, and can be reproduced by the client at home.
This is cosmetic camouflage, not treatment; any open, changing or unexplained lesion
should be seen by a doctor first.

## 1. Products
- **Camouflage creams**: dense, highly pigmented, water-resistant creams (e.g. Dermacolor,
  Kryolan-type systems, Covermark, Dermablend). Far more opaque than foundation.
- **Colour correctors / neutralisers** (green, orange, peach, yellow, lavender).
- **Setting powder** — translucent or tinted; sets the cream so it doesn't move.
- **Fixing spray** for water/sweat resistance (for body work especially).
- Tools: spatula, mixing palette, small synthetic brushes, damp sponges for stippling.

## 2. Assess before you mix
- **Texture vs colour:** makeup covers **colour** well but cannot fully hide **texture**
  (raised or indented scars). Tell the client honestly: the aim for textured scars is to
  remove the colour contrast, which greatly reduces visibility.
- Scars should be **fully healed** (typically several months; red/pink immature scars
  still changing) and the skin unbroken.
- Note surrounding skin's depth and undertone, and check the client's lighting at home/work.

## 3. Neutralise the colour first
Complementary colours cancel:
- **Red** (new scars, port-wine stains, rosacea) → green.
- **Blue/green/black tattoo ink, dark under-eyes, bruising** → orange/red-orange (deeper
  skin) or peach/salmon (lighter skin).
- **Brown hyperpigmentation** on lighter skin → peach; on deeper skin → orange.
- **Vitiligo** (depigmented patches) → no neutraliser; match the surrounding skin directly,
  often with a warmer, deeper shade. Some clients prefer to blend edges only.
Apply neutraliser **thinly**, only on the area, set with a touch of powder.

## 4. Match and apply
1. Warm a little camouflage cream on the back of your hand; mix 2–3 shades to match the
   surrounding skin. Check in daylight.
2. **Stipple, don't rub** — press product on with a brush tip or damp sponge in thin
   layers; rubbing moves it off and leaves edges. Feather the edges outward.
3. Build in **thin layers**, setting each lightly with powder if needed. Thick single
   layers crack and look masky.
4. Press translucent powder generously, wait **~10 minutes**, then brush off the excess.
5. Add texture back: a light dusting of tinted powder or fine stippling with a darker
   shade (freckles, pores) so the patch matches surrounding skin rather than looking flat.
6. Fixing spray, especially for bodies, arms, legs.

## 5. Tattoos
- Black and dark ink needs a **red-orange neutraliser layer** first or the cover turns
  grey-blue. Then skin-matched cream in thin layers.
- Large tattoos: alcohol-activated palettes or airbrushed tattoo-cover systems are often
  more durable (common in film).
- Brightly coloured tattoos: may need a light opaque base before skin tone.

## 6. Teaching self-application
Clients wear it daily, so write down shades/ratios, demonstrate on them, then have them
do it while you watch. Supply removal advice (oil-based cleanser; camouflage creams are
designed to resist water).

## Gotchas
- Too much product looks like a "patch" — less, layered and feathered beats thick.
- Grey cast = missing neutraliser step on dark marks.
- **Clients may be emotionally attached to the issue** (burns, self-harm scars,
  surgical scars): be matter-of-fact and private, never comment on cause.
- Products for skin camouflage are sometimes available via medical/charity
  services; refer clients to those where relevant.

## Verify
- [ ] Area healed and medically appropriate to cover.
- [ ] Neutraliser chosen by complement and applied thinly.
- [ ] Coverage stippled and feathered; no visible edge at arm's length in daylight.
- [ ] Set with powder and fixing spray; passes a damp-tissue press test without transfer.
- [ ] Client can reproduce it and has shade notes.
