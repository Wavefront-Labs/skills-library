# Bridal: from trial to wedding-day long-wear application

**Goal:** agree a look the client loves at the trial, recreate it exactly on the day on
schedule, and make it last 12+ hours through tears, heat, kissing and flash
photography.

## 1. The trial (4–8 weeks before)
- Ask for: reference photos (looks they love and hate), dress style and colour, hair
  plan, venue/time of day, photographer style, skin concerns, allergies, lash
  preference, any planned skin treatments (avoid new peels/facials within ~1–2 weeks of
  the date).
- Do the full look on one side if undecided between options, or full face on both.
- **Photograph** it in daylight with and without flash; let the client wear it for a few
  hours and report back (how it wore, did it feel heavy, did lashes bother them).
- Record everything on a **face chart** or notes: every product, shade, mixing ratio,
  lash style and length.

## 2. Plan the morning
- Typical timings: bride ~60–90 min, each bridesmaid/mother ~30–45 min. Build in 15–30
  min of slack.
- Bride is usually done **last or second-to-last** so makeup is freshest, but not so
  late that photos are delayed. Coordinate with hair stylist (often alternating).
- Confirm in a written contract: start time, location, number of people, travel,
  deposit and cancellation terms.
- Ask for a spot with **daylight** (near a window, not in direct sun) and a table.

## 3. Long-wear layering method
1. **Skin prep:** cleanse, light hydrating moisturiser, wait; eye cream. Oily areas:
   mattifying primer only where needed. Rich or oily skincare under long-wear base
   causes slippage.
2. **Primer** suited to skin (gripping/tacky for long wear).
3. **Base in thin layers** — a long-wear foundation, built where needed, not one
   thick coat. Conceal, then **set lightly with powder** in creasing/oily zones
   (under-eye, T-zone) — a translucent powder low in flashback-prone ingredients.
4. **Eyes:** eyelid primer prevents creasing; waterproof or tubing mascara; tightline.
   Lashes: strip or individual clusters, glue set until tacky (~30 s) before applying.
5. **Brows** with gel or wax to hold.
6. **Cream products under powder products** for longevity (e.g. cream blush set with a
   touch of powder blush).
7. **Lips:** liner over the whole lip, then lipstick, blot, re-apply; or a long-wear
   liquid. Check the shade doesn't transfer badly during the kiss — some brides prefer a
   stain base.
8. **Setting spray** to melt powders into skin.

## 4. Photo checks
- Take a phone photo with flash: any white cast under eyes (flashback)? patchy powder?
- Blush and contour need slightly more intensity for photos than looks in the mirror —
  a common trial-day discussion. Don't overdo it in daylight.
- Check neck/chest match the face, especially with strapless dresses.

## 5. Touch-up kit (for the bride or a bridesmaid)
Blotting papers, pressed powder, the lipstick and liner used, cotton buds, a small
concealer, tissues, a mini setting spray. Waterproof mascara + cotton buds handles
tear streaks.

## Gotchas
- **Never test new products on the day.** Everything used must have been worn at the
  trial or on the client's skin before.
- Hygiene: separate disposable applicators for mascara and lip; clean brushes between
  people. Sanitise palettes.
- Heat and humidity: oil-control primer and lighter coverage outlast heavy layers.
- Mature skin: minimal powder, hydrating base — over-powdering cakes in photos.
- Have a backup plan for running late: drop contour or simplify eyes for bridesmaids.

## Verify
- [ ] Trial recorded as a face chart with exact products and shades; client approved in writing.
- [ ] Timeline sent and agreed with bride and hairstylist.
- [ ] Every layer applied thin; powders set only where needed; setting spray used.
- [ ] Flash photo shows no white cast and an even colour face-to-neck.
- [ ] Touch-up kit handed over with a 1-minute explanation.
