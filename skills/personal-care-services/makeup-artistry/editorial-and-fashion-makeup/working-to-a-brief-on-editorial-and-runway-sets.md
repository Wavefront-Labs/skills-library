# Working to a brief on editorial and runway sets

**Goal:** turn a creative brief into a makeup look that reads correctly under the
shoot's lighting and lens, fits the styling and hair, and can be repeated quickly —
on one model for an editorial, or 30 models in a runway show — while you collaborate
rather than freelance an idea of your own.

## 1. Before the day: read the brief
- Gather: mood board, references, garments/colour palette, hair concept, the
  photographer's lighting plan (hard light? soft? daylight? coloured gels?),
  the **medium** (print, digital, video, social), and whether retouching is planned.
- Pin down who signs off on the look: photographer, creative director, editor, or
  designer (runway). Usually there is one decision-maker — find out who.
- Make a **face chart** and a short product list; if possible do a **test** on a model or
  yourself and photograph it in similar light.

## 2. Design for the light and lens
- **Hard, direct light** shows texture — prep skin carefully, use less powder, avoid heavy
  glitter fallout on the cheeks.
- **Soft, broad light** flattens features; contour and definition may need slightly more
  strength.
- **Close-up beauty shots** reveal everything: individual lashes, lip line, peach fuzz,
  dry patches. Spend time on skin.
- **Coloured gels** change colours: red light washes out red lipstick; check on a
  test frame.
- Skin finish is often the main story: "dewy" (balms, highlight, minimal powder),
  "matte velvet", "no-makeup" (spot concealing only). Over-matte skin on camera often looks
  flat; strategic shine reads as healthy.

## 3. On set
1. Prep skin while the team sets up (it's the slowest part).
2. Build the look; get **sign-off from the photographer on the first frames** —
   look at the monitor or camera back. What looks right in the mirror can be too weak
   or too strong on camera.
3. **Stay on set** between shots with a set bag: blotting papers, powder, lip product,
   cotton buds, brushes, mirror. Watch for shine, smudges and stray hairs; fix between
   frames without slowing the shoot.
4. For looks changes, plan the order so you build up (natural → bold) rather than strip
   back.
5. **Continuity:** for multi-look or video work, photograph each finished look (front and
   profile) and note products.

## 4. Runway / backstage
- The **key artist** designs the look; assistants recreate it from a face chart and a
  demo face. Follow it exactly — the show needs every face consistent.
- Work fast: typical slots are ~15–20 min per model. Prep skin, then the "signature"
  element (e.g. graphic liner), then everything else.
- Models arrive late from other shows; prioritise per the running order and keep wipes
  and micellar water ready to remove previous looks.

## 5. Professional conduct
- Ask before touching a model's face; tell them what you're doing.
- Hygiene: disposable mascara wands and lip applicators, sanitised palettes, spatula
  for creams — one model's breakout or conjunctivitis can spread backstage.
- Respect different skin types and textures; carry products for deep skin tones and
  textured hair edges, not just a narrow range.
- Credit and usage: agree whether you're credited and whether images can go in your
  portfolio (often part of the agreement).

## Gotchas
- "Natural" in a brief rarely means no makeup — it means invisible makeup.
- Glitter and pigments may not be approved for use around the eyes in some markets;
  read the label.
- Retouchers can fix a lot, but not uneven colour across a series; consistency is on you.
- Don't argue taste on set; offer an option, then do what the decision-maker wants.

## Verify
- [ ] Brief, lighting and sign-off chain known before the day; face chart prepared.
- [ ] Photographer approved the look on camera, not just in the mirror.
- [ ] Look stayed consistent across frames/models; continuity photos and notes saved.
- [ ] Hygiene: no shared mascara or lip applicators.
- [ ] Usage/credit terms agreed.
