# Running a one-to-one makeup lesson

**Goal:** the client leaves able to do a look **themselves, at home, with mostly their own
products** — not just looking nice from your work. Success is measured by what they can
reproduce next Tuesday morning.

## 1. Before the lesson
Ask them to bring:
- Their **current makeup bag and brushes** (all of it).
- A photo of looks they like and dislike.
- How much time they actually spend on makeup each day, and the occasion (work,
  weekend, evening).
- Skin concerns, allergies, contact lenses, any vision or dexterity limits.
Let them know to arrive with a bare or near-bare face, and the lesson length
(commonly 60–120 min).

## 2. The makeup-bag edit (10–15 min)
- Lay everything out. Sort into **keep / replace / bin**.
- Bin by **expiry**: mascara and liquid liner ~3 months after opening, liquid
  foundation/concealer ~6–12 months, powders longer; check the PAO symbol (open-jar icon,
  e.g. "12M") on the packaging. Bin anything with a changed smell or texture.
- Check foundation/concealer shades against their jaw in daylight — wrong shade is the most
  common issue.
- Clean or advise replacing dirty brushes.
Keep it non-judgmental; most people have never been shown.

## 3. The half-face method
1. **You do one side of the face**, explaining each step and why (product amount, brush,
   direction, where to place).
2. **They do the other side**, using their own products where possible, with you
   coaching (hands off unless asked).
3. Compare sides in the mirror, adjust.
This gives a reference in the mirror right in front of them.

## 4. What to teach (prioritise ruthlessly)
Choose **3–5 core skills** for an everyday routine rather than 15 things:
- Skin prep and a thin base (or spot concealing).
- Brows: filling sparse areas with small hair-like strokes, brushing up with gel.
- One eye technique suited to their eye shape (e.g. a single crease shade + tightlining
  for hooded eyes; or a smudged liner).
- Mascara: wiggle at the root, comb through.
- Cheek and lip colour coordination.
For each: product amount ("a grain of rice"), tool, direction, and how to fix mistakes.
Teach **troubleshooting**: how to fix a too-heavy blush, wobbly liner (cotton bud + micellar
water, or a flat brush with concealer).

## 5. Take-home material
- A **face chart** or written step-by-step with products and brushes, in order, with timing.
- Photos of the finished look (front and side) on their phone.
- A short shopping list of **only the gaps** — suggest options at different price points;
  disclose if you earn commission on any product.

## 6. Adapting to the person
- **Mature skin:** less powder, creams over powders, lift brows and cheek placement slightly.
- **Low vision or dexterity issues:** magnifying mirror, chunkier tools, cream products
  applied with fingers.
- **Hooded eyes, monolids:** demonstrate with eyes open — shapes must show when eyes are
  open, not closed.
- **Teens:** simple, skin-friendly routines; consult a parent where required.

## Gotchas
- Doing too much yourself — they watched a beautiful demo but can't repeat it.
- Recommending a big new kit — clients leave feeling they must spend; work with what
  they have first.
- Hygiene: don't double-dip their products with your tools after using them on your demo;
  sanitise shared items.
- Ten-step routines don't survive a busy morning; time the routine with them.

## Verify
- [ ] Their bag was edited with expired and wrong-shade items flagged.
- [ ] They completed one full side themselves, within their target time.
- [ ] Routine limited to a handful of skills they could explain back to you.
- [ ] They left with a written routine, photos and a short gap-only shopping list.
- [ ] Follow-up offered (message with a photo after a week).
