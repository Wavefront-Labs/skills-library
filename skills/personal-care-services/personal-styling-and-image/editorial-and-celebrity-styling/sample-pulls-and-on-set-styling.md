# Sample pulls and on-set styling

**Goal:** the right looks reach the shoot or appearance on time, look perfect on camera,
are credited correctly, and every loaned piece goes back on time and undamaged — the
stylist's reputation with PR depends on the last part.

## 1. Brief to mood board
- Get the brief: publication or client, story/theme, photographer, talent and their
  sizes/measurements, shoot date and number of looks, budget, credit requirements
  (e.g. editorial credits only brands that are currently stocked or advertisers).
- Build a **mood board** and get it approved before pulling. For celebrities, include
  their team's preferences, brand obligations (ambassador contracts can bar other brands)
  and comfort needs.

## 2. Pulling samples
- Send a **pull letter / request** on the publication's or agency's letterhead to brand
  PR or showrooms: shoot details, talent, dates, credit/coverage, return date, and the
  stylist's contact and insurance details.
- Samples are often a **sample size**; for talent who aren't, you'll need to request
  specific sizes, borrow from retail, or arrange made-to-measure — plan ahead.
- Sign the **loan agreement / delivery note**; check every item on arrival against it and
  photograph condition. Note anything already damaged *before* it's yours.
- Keep a master spreadsheet: item, brand, PR contact, value, date in, date due, look number.
- Insurance: many stylists or productions hold cover for loaned goods; understand who's
  liable for loss or damage (uncertain: terms vary by brand and production).

## 3. Fittings
- Fit before the shoot day when possible: pin, clamp or plan tailoring with brand
  permission (never cut or permanently alter samples without written approval).
- Photograph each approved look for the call sheet and continuity.
- Red carpet: rehearse sitting, stairs, walking and car exit in the look; have a
  backup look.

## 4. On set
- **Kit:** steamer, lint rollers, clamps/clips, safety pins, fashion tape, shoe-sole
  protectors/tape (keeps soles unmarked so shoes can be returned), sewing kit, stain
  remover pen, deodorant shields, dress shields, nude underwear in range of sizes and
  tones, hangers, garment bags.
- Clamp and pin only where the camera can't see; re-check each set-up — angles change.
- Protect garments from makeup: face covers or scarves when dressing; dress after makeup
  where possible.
- Continuity: photograph each look from front, back and side for multi-day or reshoot.
- One assistant tracks items by look so nothing goes missing between set-ups.

## 5. Returns and credits
- Return everything by the due date in the same condition, with the delivery note; get a
  signed receipt or courier proof. Late or damaged returns are the fastest way to lose
  access to a brand.
- Send credits to the publication's fashion desk exactly as the brand requires (spelling,
  item description, price if required).
- Thank PR, share the published images.

## Gotchas
- Shoe soles: tape them before anyone walks in them.
- Couriers and customs for international samples take time; carnets may be needed for
  temporary export (uncertain: depends on countries involved).
- Talent's own team may override looks last minute — keep a backup on rail.

## Verify
- [ ] Brief, mood board and brand obligations approved.
- [ ] Every sample logged with condition photos and due date.
- [ ] Fittings done; no permanent alterations without written approval.
- [ ] Kit complete; continuity photos taken.
- [ ] All items returned on time with proof; credits sent correctly.
