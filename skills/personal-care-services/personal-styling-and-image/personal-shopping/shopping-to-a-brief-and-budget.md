# Shopping to a brief and budget

**Goal:** the client buys the right few pieces — ones that fill real gaps, fit, suit them
and stay within budget — in the least time, and trusts that your recommendations serve them,
not your commission.

## 1. The brief (written, agreed before shopping)
- Purpose: e.g. work capsule refresh, an event, a new job, post-pregnancy, travel.
- **Gap list** from a wardrobe audit if one was done (see wardrobe-styling); otherwise a
  short intake of what they own and wear.
- Sizes and fit notes per brand, colour palette (see colour-analysis), proportion notes
  (see fit-and-silhouette-analysis), fabric dislikes, ethical/sustainability preferences.
- **Budget:** a total and a split — spend more on high-wear items (coat, shoes, bag,
  trousers) and less on trend or low-wear items. Agree whether alterations come from the
  budget.

## 2. Preparation (pre-shop)
- Scout stores or sites in advance; build a shortlist by store with alternatives at two
  price points.
- Book **personal shopping suites / store appointments** where available — many department
  stores pre-pull items to a list for free.
- Pre-pull sizes either side of the client's usual; sizes vary by brand.
- Plan the route to minimise walking and finish at the most important store while energy
  is high.

## 3. The fitting session
- Start with the highest-priority item, not the most fun one.
- Edit fast: client tries, you assess fit and line, discuss briefly, decide yes/no/maybe.
- Build outfits on the spot with items from the session and things they already own
  (bring photos of key existing pieces).
- Keep a running total against budget (a phone note is enough); call the stop before the
  client overspends.
- Note alteration needs and get them quoted while in store if a tailor is on site.

## 4. After
- Summary to the client: what was bought, cost, outfits it makes, care notes, returns
  deadlines (photograph receipts).
- Online/virtual shopping: send a curated board with links, sizes and returns terms;
  schedule a try-on video call when deliveries arrive; track return windows.

## 5. Money and ethics
- Charge a clear fee (hourly, day rate or package). If you also receive **retailer
  commission, trade discount or affiliate payment**, **disclose it** in writing — advertising
  and consumer rules in many countries require disclosure of paid relationships when you
  recommend products, and trust depends on it.
- Never steer to a retailer for commission when an equal or better option exists elsewhere.
- Clients pay retailers directly unless agreed otherwise; avoid handling client card details.

## Gotchas
- Sale shopping: tempting, but final-sale items can't be returned — check before buying.
- Decision fatigue sets in after 2–3 hours; schedule breaks or split sessions.
- Watch for clients spending beyond their means under social pressure — the brief is your
  shield.

## Verify
- [ ] Written brief with gap list, sizes, palette and budget split.
- [ ] Appointments booked and items pre-pulled.
- [ ] Spend tracked against budget during the session.
- [ ] Summary sent with outfits, receipts and return deadlines.
- [ ] Any commission or affiliate relationship disclosed.
