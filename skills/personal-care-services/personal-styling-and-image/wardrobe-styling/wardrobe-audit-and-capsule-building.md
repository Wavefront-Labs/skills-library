# Wardrobe audit and capsule building

**Goal:** the client ends the session with fewer, better-working clothes, a set of
photographed outfits they can repeat without thinking, and a short, prioritised shopping
list — not a bag of impulse purchases.

## 1. Intake (before the session)
- **Lifestyle split:** rough percentage of time in work, casual, active, events, travel.
  This sets what the wardrobe should contain — a 70%-remote worker with a closet full of
  suits has the wrong proportions, not too few clothes.
- Dress codes at work; climate; budget; style words they like and dislike; any body
  changes, mobility needs or sensory preferences (fabrics, tags, waistbands).
- Ask for 3–5 photos of outfits they've felt great in — the evidence beats adjectives.

## 2. The audit (in the client's home, 2–4 hours)
1. Work one category at a time (tops, bottoms, dresses, outerwear, shoes, accessories).
2. Client tries on anything uncertain. Sort into four piles:
   - **Keep** — fits, suits, worn, fits the lifestyle split.
   - **Alter** — good piece, fixable fit (hem, take in, replace buttons). Be realistic:
     taking in is easy; letting out often isn't possible.
   - **Donate / sell** — doesn't fit, doesn't suit, not worn in 12+ months and no
     specific reason to keep. Offer resale/consignment for good pieces.
   - **Maybe** — box it, date it, revisit in 3 months.
3. Keep the pace; the stylist suggests, **the client decides**. Never shame a purchase.
4. Note everything that comes up repeatedly (e.g. "every navy item stays") — that's
   their core palette.

## 3. Outfit building
- Build outfits from **kept** items first. Use formulas: a third piece (jacket,
  cardigan, vest) lifts any top-and-bottom; one statement item per look.
- Photograph each outfit (front, full length, plain background), label by occasion,
  and share as a **lookbook** (shared album or app). Target 20–40 outfits.
- Show how one item works three ways — proves versatility before buying anything.

## 4. Capsule and gap list
- A capsule is a coordinated set (commonly ~25–40 pieces per season, shoes included or
  not by convention — numbers are a guide, not a rule) built on 2–3 **core neutrals**
  plus 1–3 **accent colours** (see colour-analysis).
- Gap list: only items that unlock several outfits ("black ankle boots: completes 9
  looks"). Prioritise by **cost per wear** and lifestyle split.
- Hand over to personal-shopping for sourcing if the client wants it.

## 5. Organising
- Hang by category then colour; visible storage of everyday items; seasonal rotation
  out of sight; matching hangers save surprising space.

## Gotchas
- Sentimental pieces: keep, but store separately from the working wardrobe.
- Clients may want to keep "goal size" clothes; don't fight it — move them out of daily view.
- Don't over-edit: a client left with nothing to wear on Monday won't rebook.
- Get written consent before photographing clients and their homes; store images privately.

## Verify
- [ ] Lifestyle split recorded and wardrobe compared to it.
- [ ] Every item sorted; alterations listed with tailor notes.
- [ ] Lookbook delivered with labelled outfits.
- [ ] Gap list is short, prioritised and tied to outfits unlocked.
- [ ] Client chose every exit; photos stored with consent.
