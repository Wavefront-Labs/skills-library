# Assessing proportion, fit and alteration needs

**Goal:** explain to a client *why* some clothes work on them, using proportion and fit
— and turn that into silhouettes to look for and alterations to order. Avoid fruit and
"flaw" vocabulary; describe lines and measurements neutrally.

## 1. Observe and measure
- Full-length front and side photos in fitted neutral clothes (with consent), or a mirror
  assessment.
- **Vertical proportion:** compare upper body (shoulder to natural waist) with lower body
  (waist to floor). Long legs/short torso vs long torso/shorter legs changes where waistlines,
  hems and jacket lengths should fall. The classic guide is a roughly 1:2 or 2:1 split
  (rule of thirds) rather than cutting the body in half.
- **Horizontal balance:** shoulder width vs hip width.
- **Scale:** bone structure and height (small, medium, large) — guides accessory size,
  print scale and lapel width.
- **Line:** straight/angular vs curved features — guides fabric (crisp vs fluid) and detail.
- Measure: bust/chest, waist, high and low hip, shoulder width, inseam, arm length.
  Clients rarely know their current measurements.

## 2. Reading fit (in the fitting room)
| Fault | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| Pull lines radiating from a point | Too tight there (buttons straining, X-lines at the zip) |
| Horizontal folds | Too long in that area, or too tight just below |
| Vertical folds / excess | Too wide there |
| Shoulder seam past the shoulder bone | Too big — shoulders are hard and costly to alter |
| Collar gaping off the neck | Posture or pattern mismatch; usually not an easy fix |
| Back of jacket vents splaying | Too tight at the seat/hips |
| Waistband gaping at the back | Common with a smaller waist-to-hip ratio; alterable |
Conventions (flex for fashion and preference): jacket sleeve shows ~1–1.5 cm of shirt
cuff; trouser break from none to slight for modern cuts; jacket length covers the seat for
classic tailoring.

## 3. Choosing silhouettes
- Place the waist or visual break where it creates the proportion the client likes (a
  high rise lengthens the legs; a shorter jacket usually suits a short torso better than
  a long one — but test on the client rather than assume).
- Column of colour (top and bottom in one tone) lengthens; strong horizontal contrast
  shortens and widens at that line.
- Match fabric weight and drape to the client's line and the outfit's purpose.
- Ask what the client *wants* to emphasise or play down — that's their call, not a rule
  that bodies must look hourglass or "balanced".

## 4. Alteration briefing
- Easy/cheap: hems, sleeve length (if no working cuff buttons), taking in side seams or
  waistbands, tapering trouser legs.
- Hard/expensive or not worth it: shoulders, letting out with no seam allowance, jacket
  length, rise changes.
- Pin in place or mark with chalk; write the brief for the tailor with measurements
  (see repair-maintenance.clothing-alterations-repair where relevant).
- Rule of thumb for clients: **buy to fit the largest part** of the body and alter the rest.

## Gotchas
- Size labels differ between brands and countries; trust measurements and try-ons.
- Adaptive needs (seated wearers, prosthetics, limited dexterity) change everything —
  seated fit is cut differently; refer to adaptive clothing lines.
- Weight change or pregnancy: plan for flexibility, not alterations that lock in a size.

## Verify
- [ ] Proportion, balance, scale and line recorded with current measurements.
- [ ] Fit faults named by cause, not by body judgement.
- [ ] Silhouette recommendations tied to client's own goals.
- [ ] Alteration brief written, realistic and costed.
