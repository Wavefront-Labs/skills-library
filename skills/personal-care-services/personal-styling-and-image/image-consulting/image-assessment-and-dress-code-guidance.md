# Image assessment and dress-code guidance

**Goal:** align how a client presents — clothes, grooming, posture and on-camera habits —
with what they want to achieve in their real context (a promotion, a board, a career change,
dating, public office), and leave them with a plan they can carry out without you.

## 1. Intake: goals and context first
- **What outcome** do they want, and **with whom**? ("Be taken seriously by the board",
  "look approachable to patients".) Image work without a goal is just a makeover.
- Context: industry norms, country/culture, role level, public visibility, budget, time
  they'll spend on grooming daily, religious or cultural dress requirements, disability or
  sensory needs.
- How do they want to be described in three words? How do they think others currently
  describe them? The gap is the brief.

## 2. Assessment
- Observe a typical working outfit and, if possible, a video call or recorded talk.
- Assess, neutrally and with permission: fit and condition of clothes (see
  fit-and-silhouette-analysis), colour (see colour-analysis), grooming (hair, nails,
  skin, facial hair, fragrance), accessories, posture, handshake/greeting, voice basics,
  camera framing and lighting.
- Benchmark against the **actual** norm in their setting — look at photos of senior people
  in their organisation or sector, not generic "power dressing" rules.

## 3. Dress codes decoded (common Western usage — varies by country and host)
| Code | Typical meaning |
|---|---|
| White tie | Tailcoat, white waistcoat and bow tie / full-length evening gown |
| Black tie | Dinner suit/tuxedo and black bow tie / floor-length or elegant cocktail gown |
| Cocktail | Dark suit, tie optional / cocktail dress, smart separates |
| Business formal | Dark suit and tie / suit or tailored dress |
| Business casual | Tailored trousers or chinos, collared shirt or knit, no tie / similar smart separates |
| Smart casual | Jacket optional, dark jeans may be fine — the most ambiguous; ask the host |
When in doubt: ask the host, check event photos from previous years, and err slightly
dressier. Cultural and religious dress is compatible with every code — advise on the
formal versions, never suggest abandoning it.

## 4. Grooming direction
- Give direction and refer to specialists (barber, hairdresser, makeup artist, dentist,
  dermatologist) rather than performing services outside your training.
- Low-maintenance routines the client will actually keep beat ambitious ones.

## 5. On camera
- Camera at eye level, face lit from the front (window or ring light), plain or tidy
  background, mid-tone solid colours (avoid fine stripes that strobe), frame head and
  shoulders with a little headroom.

## 6. Action plan
- Written plan: 3–5 priorities, what to keep, what to change, purchases with budget,
  grooming appointments, and a review date.
- Corporate group training: keep it about the organisation's standards and inclusive
  options; never single out individuals in a group.

## Gotchas
- Never comment on body size or features the client didn't raise; frame feedback around
  clothes and choices.
- Respect gender expression and cultural identity; the brief is theirs.
- Avoid claims that appearance changes will guarantee outcomes.

## Verify
- [ ] Goal, audience and context are written in the client's words.
- [ ] Assessment benchmarked to their real setting.
- [ ] Dress-code guidance specific to their events and culture.
- [ ] Grooming referrals made where outside your scope.
- [ ] Written action plan with priorities and review date delivered.
