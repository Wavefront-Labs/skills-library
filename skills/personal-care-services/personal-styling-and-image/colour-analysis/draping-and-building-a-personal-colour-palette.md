# Draping and building a personal colour palette

**Goal:** identify which colours make the client look healthy, even-toned and in focus,
and give them a palette they can actually shop and dress with. Colour analysis is a
practitioner's visual judgement within a chosen system; results differ between systems
and analysts — present it as guidance, not science-settled fact.

## 1. The three dimensions you are judging
- **Hue / undertone:** warm (yellow-based) vs cool (blue-based). Neutral-leaning clients
  are common.
- **Value:** light to deep; and the **contrast** between hair, skin and eyes.
- **Chroma:** clear/bright vs muted/soft.
Systems map these: the classic **4 seasons** (Spring warm-clear-light, Summer
cool-soft-light, Autumn warm-soft-deep, Winter cool-clear-deep) or a **12-season / tonal**
system that names the dominant dimension (e.g. Light, Soft, Deep, Clear, Warm, Cool).
Pick one system and use it consistently.

## 2. Setting up
- **Lighting is everything:** north-facing daylight or full-spectrum lamps around 5000–
  6500K, high CRI (90+). No coloured walls nearby; neutral grey or white surround.
- Client: clean face (no makeup, or minimal), hair colour covered with a white or grey
  cape/wrap if dyed; remove coloured clothing from the neckline (grey cape).
- Large mirror in front, client and analyst both looking.

## 3. Draping sequence
1. **Undertone test drapes:** pairs of the same colour in warm and cool versions
   (e.g. gold vs silver lamé, warm red vs cool red, cream vs pure white). Look at the
   face, not the fabric.
2. **Value:** light vs dark drapes in the winning temperature.
3. **Chroma:** bright/saturated vs muted/greyed versions.
4. Confirm with the full set for the candidate season(s), comparing best-of-each.

**What "good" looks like:** skin evens out, shadows under eyes and around the mouth
soften, eyes and lips look defined, face looks in focus. **Poor:** sallow or grey cast,
redness emphasised, shadows deepen, the colour arrives before the face.
- Have the client note their own reactions; agreement aids adoption.
- Photograph key comparisons under the same light (with consent) for the client's reference.

## 4. Handing over
- A **fabric swatch fan** or printed palette of 30+ colours: neutrals (for big pieces),
  core colours, accents, and best metals (gold/silver/rose).
- Translate into practice: which existing clothes to wear near the face, how to use
  off-palette colours below the waist or with a flattering scarf/collar between.
- Makeup and hair colour direction if within your training (hand off to makeup artistry
  otherwise).

## Gotchas
- Fake tan, recent sunburn, dyed brows and coloured contact lenses skew results — ask
  before booking.
- Screen colour is unreliable; online/virtual "analysis" from photos is approximate at
  best (uncertain: some analysts use calibrated photo methods; results still vary).
- Don't diagnose from hair or eye colour alone; drape.
- Analyst fatigue: your eye adapts — take breaks and re-compare.

## Verify
- [ ] Neutral, high-CRI lighting and neutral surround.
- [ ] Hue, value and chroma each tested with paired drapes.
- [ ] Season/tonal type stated within one named system.
- [ ] Client has a physical or printed palette with neutrals, cores, accents and metals.
- [ ] Practical guidance given for using their existing wardrobe.
