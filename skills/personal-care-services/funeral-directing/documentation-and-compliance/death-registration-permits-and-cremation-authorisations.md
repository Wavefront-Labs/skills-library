# Death registration, permits and cremation authorisations

**Goal:** no disposition happens without the complete, correct paperwork, the identity of
the deceased is controlled at every step, and records would satisfy an inspector. This is
a map of the chain — exact forms and deadlines change; check the current rules for your
jurisdiction every time something looks unfamiliar.

## 1. The chain (generic)
1. **Cause of death certified** by a doctor, or the death is **referred** (sudden,
   unexplained, violent, in custody, industrial disease...) to the coroner / medical
   examiner / procurator fiscal.
2. **Registration** with the civil registrar (or electronic death registration system).
3. **Disposal authority** — the permit that lets you bury or cremate.
4. **Cremation-specific authorisations** (where cremating).
5. **Disposition record** returned/kept.
Never book a final time as "confirmed" until you know which branch a death is on.

## 2. England and Wales (illustrative; verify current forms)
- Medical Certificate of Cause of Death (MCCD) completed by an attending doctor and
  scrutinised by the **medical examiner** service (statutory since 9 September 2024).
- Registration normally within **5 days** by an informant; the registrar issues the
  **Certificate for Burial or Cremation ("green form")**, or the coroner issues an order.
- Cremation: **Form Cremation 1** (application by the executor/next of kin); the
  crematorium's **medical referee** must authorise (Cremation 10). The 2024 reforms changed
  which medical forms are needed (uncertain: confirm the current set with the crematorium).
- Coroner cases: coroner's certificate for cremation or order for burial replaces the
  green form; inquests can delay release.
- Scotland and Northern Ireland have their own systems (e.g. 8-day registration in Scotland).

## 3. United States (illustrative; state law governs)
- Death certificate: medical portion by the certifier or ME/coroner; personal data by the
  funeral director, typically via the state **EDRS**; filed within a state deadline (often
  3–10 days).
- **Burial-transit permit** (name varies) before disposition or moving the body.
- Cremation: signed **cremation authorisation** from the legal authorising agent (per the
  right-to-control-disposition statute), ME/coroner cremation approval in many states,
  and a state **waiting period** (commonly 24–48 hours after death).
- Embalming: written or documented oral authorisation before embalming; the FTC Funeral
  Rule requires disclosure that it is not generally required by law.

## 4. Identity and custody control
- ID tag on the deceased at first call (wrist/ankle) and a matching tag on the
  coffin/container; check at every transfer, before embalming or preparation, before
  committal or cremation.
- Chain-of-custody log: who moved the deceased, when, from where to where.
- Personal effects: inventory, witnessed, signed for when returned.

## 5. Records and inspection
- One file per funeral: certificates, permits, authorisations, signed price statement,
  custody log, disposition confirmation.
- Retain for the period your regulator requires; many regimes allow unannounced inspection.
- Late or missing forms: escalate immediately — never "proceed and catch up".

## Gotchas
- Spelling of names/dates must match across every form; mismatches halt cremations.
- Authority disputes between relatives: stop until resolved; cremation is irreversible.
- Deaths abroad and repatriations have their own documents (see repatriation).
- Pacemaker/implant declarations sit on cremation paperwork — verify removal.

## Verify
- [ ] Death's route (certified vs referred) known before booking.
- [ ] Registration complete; disposal permit in hand.
- [ ] Cremation authorisations signed by the right person; referee/ME approval received.
- [ ] ID checked and logged at every transfer.
- [ ] Complete file retained for the regulator.
