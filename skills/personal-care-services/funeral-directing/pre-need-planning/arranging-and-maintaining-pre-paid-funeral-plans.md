# Arranging and maintaining pre-paid funeral plans

**Goal:** capture someone's funeral wishes accurately, fund them through a lawful and
secure route, explain exactly what is and isn't guaranteed, and make sure the plan is
found and honoured years later.

## 1. Two different things: wishes vs pre-payment
- **Recording wishes** (no money) — a wishes form held on file and copied to the client
  and their executor. Low risk, high value; offer it freely.
- **Pre-paying** — a regulated financial product in many places. Do not sell it unless
  your firm is authorised or appointed to.

## 2. Funding routes and rules (verify locally)
| Route | How it works | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| **UK funeral plan** | Since 29 July 2022 providers must be **FCA-authorised**; plans are funded by trust or whole-of-life insurance | Only FCA-authorised providers (or their appointed representatives) may sell; Consumer Duty; no cold-calling; cancellation rights |
| **US pre-need trust** | Payments held in trust per state law (percentage to be trusted varies by state, often 75–100%) | State licensing of the seller; trust reporting; refund rights on cancellation |
| **US pre-need / final-expense insurance** | Policy assigned to the funeral home | Growth may not keep pace with price inflation |
| **Irrevocable trust** | Can't be cashed in | Often used to protect assets for Medicaid eligibility (US); limits vary by state |

## 3. Guaranteed vs non-guaranteed
- **Guaranteed** items: the funeral firm's own services and goods listed in the plan,
  delivered at no extra cost whenever death occurs.
- **Non-guaranteed / cash-advance items (disbursements):** crematorium and cemetery fees,
  officiant, medical fees, flowers, notices. Many plans include only an allowance for
  these — **the shortfall can fall on the family**. Say this out loud and in writing.
- Burial plans rarely include the grave purchase or interment fees; make that explicit.

## 4. The consultation
1. Explore wishes with the same choice framework used at-need (see
   at-need-arranging) — disposition, ceremony, music, faith requirements, ashes.
2. Check affordability and suitability; don't sell instalment plans that run beyond
   likely affordability; explain what happens if payments stop.
3. **Vulnerable customers:** bereaved, elderly, cognitively impaired clients — slow down,
   suggest a family member or advisor attends, give a cooling-off reminder. Capacity
   doubts = don't proceed.
4. Provide the plan document, terms, what's included/excluded, cancellation rights, and
   how to contact the provider if the firm closes.

## 5. Keeping plans alive
- Register the plan with the provider and on your system; give the client a **plan card**
  and tell them to inform their executor and family — unfound plans lead to families
  paying twice.
- Review periodically (e.g. every few years, or when the client moves area): wishes,
  contact details, **portability** (transfer to another firm if they relocate).
- At need: search records by name and date of birth before quoting; convert the plan,
  then itemise any non-guaranteed shortfall for the family.

## Gotchas
- High-pressure or commission-driven selling caused the UK regulatory overhaul; avoid
  any practice that resembles it.
- Plans sold by firms that later failed left customers unprotected — check provider
  standing and trust arrangements.
- Changes to wishes must be dated and signed; old versions retained.

## Verify
- [ ] Firm is authorised/licensed to sell pre-payment in this jurisdiction.
- [ ] Wishes recorded and copied to client and executor.
- [ ] Guaranteed vs non-guaranteed items explained in writing.
- [ ] Vulnerability and affordability checked; cancellation rights given.
- [ ] Plan registered, card issued, review date set.
