# Planning and running funeral-day movements

**Goal:** every vehicle, bearer and family member is in the right place at the right
time, the cortege travels with dignity, and the service starts on the minute — with a
plan ready for when it doesn't.

## 1. Build the run-sheet
Work **backwards from the fixed slot** (crematorium or church time):
| Time | Movement |
|---|---|
| T−90 min | Staff and bearers report; vehicles checked, cleaned, fuelled |
| T−60 | Coffin placed in hearse; flowers loaded |
| T−X | Arrive at family home (X = drive time + 10–15 min buffer) |
| T−X+10 | Cortege departs |
| T−10 | Arrive at venue; conductor speaks to venue staff |
| T | Coffin enters |
Add drive times from a trial run or mapping at the actual hour and day — school-run and
market-day traffic differ. Note the **numbers to call** (venue, officiant, each driver).

## 2. Fleet and staffing
- Hearse + family limousines sized to who travels (seats per car); confirm who rides in
  which car with the family in advance.
- Back-to-back funerals: check fleet and bearer conflicts; never schedule a vehicle with
  less than a realistic gap.
- Specialist vehicles (horse-drawn, motorcycle, vintage) need weather and route checks
  and longer lead times; horses walk slowly — plan accordingly.

## 3. Cortege route and conduct
- Choose a route that avoids motorways, steep cambers and tight turns for long vehicles;
  include a pass of a meaningful place only if timing allows.
- **Paging/walking in front:** the conductor walks ahead of the hearse for a short
  distance on departure and arrival (a mark of respect); decide where it's safe.
- Keep the cortege together at a steady low speed; brief limousine drivers on the route
  and what to do if separated (meet at a named point, never race to catch up).
- Processions have **no automatic right of way** in many places (e.g. UK) — obey signals;
  some US states grant processions specific rights or allow escorts. Police or private
  escorts where permitted and arranged in advance.

## 4. Bearers
- Number: typically 4–6 for a shoulder carry, depending on coffin weight and size.
- Brief before every funeral: lift method (**shoulder carry** vs **waist/hand carry** vs
  trolley/bier), commands, positions by height, route through doors and steps, where to
  set down. Family bearers get a calm walkthrough and an option to bow out.
- Heavy or oversize coffins: trolley or extra bearers; don't risk a drop.

## 5. Contingencies
- Late running: call the venue early; shorten departure rather than speed.
- Breakdown: spare vehicle on standby or a nearby partner firm's number.
- Weather: umbrellas, mats at graveside, gritting for icy steps.
- A family member taken ill: keep the conductor's phone for medical calls; first-aider on team.

## Gotchas
- Mapping apps underestimate funeral speeds; add buffer.
- Floral tributes block the rear window or loading — check fit before the day.
- Parking at the venue for the hearse and cars must be reserved.

## Verify
- [ ] Run-sheet with times worked back from the slot and driver contacts.
- [ ] Fleet and bearers confirmed with no double-booking.
- [ ] Route trialled or checked for the day/time; drivers briefed.
- [ ] Bearers briefed on carry method and positions.
- [ ] Contingency contacts and equipment on board.
