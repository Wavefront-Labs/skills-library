# Preparing for natural burial without embalming

**Goal:** care for the deceased in a way that fits natural (green) burial and
other alternatives — keeping the body cool and presentable without chemical
preservation, dressing or shrouding in biodegradable materials, and meeting the
specific requirements of providers such as natural burial grounds, alkaline
hydrolysis facilities and human-composting operators.

## 1. What natural burial grounds usually require
Rules vary by site and certifying body (e.g. the Green Burial Council in the US, the
Natural Death Centre/ANBG in the UK). Common requirements:
- **No embalming**, or only non-toxic/formaldehyde-free methods if a site permits any.
- **Biodegradable containers:** untreated wood, wicker/willow, bamboo, cardboard, or a shroud
  on a board. No metal handles or plastic linings.
- **Natural fibres** in clothing and shroud: cotton, linen, wool, silk; no synthetics,
  zips or metal where possible.
- Sometimes depth and timing rules (burial within a set period).
Ask the burial ground for their written list before preparing.

## 2. Cold care (the core skill)
Without embalming, the main tool is temperature.
- **Refrigeration** at about **2–4 °C (36–39 °F)** as soon as possible after death slows
  decomposition substantially. *(Typical mortuary cold-room range; local regulations may
  specify.)*
- For home vigils or where refrigeration isn't possible, **dry ice** (wrapped in cloth or
  paper — never directly on skin, it causes freezer burn) or purpose-made **cooling
  blankets/plates** placed under the torso and on the abdomen; replace dry ice roughly
  every 24 h. Ventilate the room — dry ice releases CO₂ and can be dangerous in small,
  closed rooms.
- Keep rooms cool; close curtains; avoid heaters.
- Plan timing: many families hold viewings within a few days of death; length of time a
  body remains suitable for viewing without embalming depends on the person's condition,
  temperature and cause of death.

## 3. Washing, dressing and shrouding
- Wash the body gently with mild soap and water; families may wish to take part (some
  traditions require this). Set features as for any viewing (see restorative-art: eye
  caps and mouth closure — cotton or natural methods where the site prohibits plastics).
- Absorbent natural materials (cotton wadding, absorbent pads made of natural material)
  for leakage.
- **Shrouding:** lay the body on the shroud (often with a stiff board or carrying frame
  integrated) wrapped in layers, secured with ties; ensure enough handles or straps for
  bearers to carry and lower. Practise folds beforehand; families find a neat shroud
  deeply moving.

## 4. Alkaline hydrolysis (aquamation, water cremation)
The body is placed in a stainless-steel vessel with water and an alkali (potassium and/or
sodium hydroxide), heated (around 90–160 °C depending on low- or high-temperature systems),
for several hours. Bone remains are dried and processed like cremated remains.
Body-care requirements resemble cremation: authorisation, ID chain, removal of
pacemakers is often still requested, natural-fibre clothing or a shroud (wool/silk/
leather can behave differently — check the operator's list). Legal in some jurisdictions,
not others — check.

## 5. Natural organic reduction (human composting)
Body placed in a vessel with plant material (wood chips, alfalfa, straw) for roughly
4–8 weeks, producing soil. Operators specify: no embalming, natural-fibre shroud only,
removal of certain implants, and sometimes infectious-disease exclusions. Legal in a
growing number of US states and few other places — check local law.

## Gotchas
- Some families assume embalming is legally required; it usually isn't, though there may
  be rules for transport across borders or holding times.
- Natural burial sites may decline bodies with certain infectious diseases or after
  some medical treatments — check before promising.
- Dry ice in a sealed car or small room is a CO₂ hazard.
- Labelling: the ID tag must also be biodegradable or removed according to the site's
  protocol, but ID must be maintained up to the graveside.

## Verify
- [ ] Burial ground/operator's written requirements obtained and met.
- [ ] Body refrigerated or cooled promptly; dry ice wrapped and rooms ventilated.
- [ ] All materials natural/biodegradable; no metal or plastic left where prohibited.
- [ ] Shroud secure with enough carrying points; practised beforehand.
- [ ] ID maintained throughout; legal paperwork for the chosen method complete.
