# First-call removal from home or hospital

**Goal:** collect the deceased promptly, safely and with dignity — confirming identity
and legal release, treating the family with care, and moving the person without
injury to them or to staff — and document the chain of custody from the place of death
to the funeral home.

## 1. Taking the call
Record: name of the deceased, caller and relationship, address and access details
(stairs, lift, narrow hall, parking), approximate weight, whether death was expected,
whether a doctor/nurse/hospice has attended, **whether the coroner/medical examiner is
involved**, infection risks, and whether family will be present. Give a realistic
arrival time and call if it changes.

## 2. Release before removal
- **Expected death at home:** a doctor, hospice nurse or other authorised person usually
  needs to have verified/pronounced death; rules differ by country/state — know yours.
- **Unexpected/sudden death, violence, accident:** police/coroner/medical examiner have
  jurisdiction; **do not move the body** until released or instructed.
- **Hospital/care home:** collect release forms, check the hospital's identification
  against your paperwork, sign their mortuary register.

## 3. Arrival etiquette (home)
- Two staff minimum (more for stairs or larger persons). Clean vehicle, smart attire.
- Leave the stretcher in the vehicle at first; go in, introduce yourselves, offer
  condolences, explain briefly what will happen and how long it takes.
- Offer the family time to say goodbye before you start and the choice to leave the room
  during the transfer. Ask about items to stay with the person (rings, a blanket, a toy)
  and log them.

## 4. Identification
- Confirm identity with the family or staff and **attach an ID tag** (commonly ankle and/or
  wrist) before moving. In facilities, cross-check the facility's tags with your paperwork.
- Never rely on the room number or the family's description alone in a facility.

## 5. Moving the person
- Use a **transfer sheet/slide sheet** or board; lay the person on the sheet, then slide
  onto the stretcher (cot) at the same height as the bed. Avoid lifting where you can slide.
- Wrap in a clean sheet and a fitted cot cover or body bag (pouch) where needed (leakage,
  infection, police instruction); face covered for transport, but the family may wish to see
  the face uncovered while leaving the room.
- Strap the person securely. Head generally goes first up stairs and last down (feet
  first down stairs keeps the head higher) — confirm with your training.
- **Stairs and tight spaces:** use a stair chair, scoop stretcher or carry sheet as
  needed; plan the route, remove obstacles, and call for extra staff rather than risk a
  drop. Bariatric cases need a bariatric cot and more people.
- Use **PPE**: gloves always; aprons, masks, eye protection by risk.

## 6. Vehicle and transport
- Load and lock the cot into the vehicle's floor rails; check the restraint.
- Drive smoothly; the vehicle should be discreet and never left unattended with the
  deceased inside where avoidable.
- On arrival, log in: time, condition, ID tag check, personal effects, and place in
  refrigeration (or proceed to preparation) promptly.

## 7. Documentation
Removal record with: date/time of call, release authority, place of death, staff names,
ID check, items with the person, condition notes (wounds, skin tears, medical devices
like catheters or cannulas left in situ per instructions — in coroner cases devices are
usually left in place), and time of arrival at the home.

## Gotchas
- Moving a body before coroner/police release can obstruct an investigation — the biggest
  legal error at first call.
- Skin tears happen easily with older or fragile skin; slide, don't drag.
- Rings left on can be lost or cause disputes; log or return them on the spot with a signature.
- The family remembers how you carried their loved one out of the house — slow down.

## Verify
- [ ] Legal release confirmed before moving; coroner cases untouched until released.
- [ ] ID tag attached and checked against paperwork before leaving.
- [ ] Transfer done by slide sheet with enough staff; no drops, no skin tears.
- [ ] Cot locked in the vehicle; deceased never left unattended.
- [ ] Removal record and personal-effects log completed and signed on arrival.
