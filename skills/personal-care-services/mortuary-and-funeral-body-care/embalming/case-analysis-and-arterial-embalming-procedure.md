# Case analysis and the arterial embalming procedure

**Goal:** temporarily preserve, sanitise and restore a natural appearance to a deceased
person for viewing, by analysing the case first and then distributing preservative
fluid through the vascular system, followed by treatment of the body cavities. This is
reference content for mortuary-science students and licensed practitioners; embalming
is a regulated act performed by, or under supervision of, a licensed embalmer.

## 1. Before you begin
- **Authorisation:** written or recorded permission from the person with the legal right
  to authorise (family/next of kin, as defined locally). In many jurisdictions embalming
  without authorisation is unlawful, and a funeral home may not require embalming when
  the family declines it (e.g. US FTC Funeral Rule disclosures). Confirm any **coroner or
  medical-examiner hold** — do not embalm a case under investigation without release.
- **Identification:** check the ID band/tag against the paperwork before any procedure.
- **PPE and environment:** gown, gloves (double), eye/face protection, shoe covers;
  treat every body as potentially infectious (universal/standard precautions). Working
  ventilation with down-draft or local exhaust. In the US, the OSHA formaldehyde
  standard (29 CFR 1910.1048) sets a 0.75 ppm 8-hour TWA and 2 ppm STEL; employers must
  monitor exposure. Follow your local equivalent.

## 2. Case analysis (do not skip)
Record on the embalming report, before injection:
- Cause and manner of death, time since death, refrigeration, autopsy or organ/tissue
  donation (changes the approach entirely — see restorative-art and multi-point methods).
- **Body condition:** weight, oedema (fluid retention), dehydration, jaundice, skin
  slip, discolourations, rigor mortis, decomposition signs, medications (e.g.
  chemotherapy, high-dose steroids), lesions and wounds.
- Vascular condition: arteriosclerosis, clots.
This determines fluid **index** (formaldehyde percentage of the concentrate), solution
strength, volume, injection pressure/rate and whether supplemental chemicals are needed.

## 3. Pre-embalming steps
- Disinfect the body surface and orifices; wash.
- Relieve rigor gently by flexing and massaging joints.
- **Set features** (closing eyes with eye caps, mouth closure by suture or needle injector
  wires, natural expression) — easier before fluid firms tissue. See restorative-art.
- Position: head elevated slightly on a head block, arms at the sides or on the abdomen.

## 4. Arterial injection
- **Vessels:** the right common carotid artery (with the right internal jugular vein for
  drainage) is the most common single-point site; the femoral artery/vein is an
  alternative. Multi-point (e.g. six-point) injection is used when distribution is poor
  or after autopsy.
- **Solution:** arterial fluid diluted with water to a strength suited to the case, often
  with a co-injection chemical (to aid distribution, adjust pH, add humectant, or counter
  oedema/dehydration). Pre-injection fluid is sometimes used first to clear clots.
  Strength and volumes are case-specific — use manufacturer dilution charts and your
  training; a common teaching rule is roughly one gallon (~3.8 L) of solution per ~50 lb
  (~23 kg) of body weight, adjusted by condition. *(Treat this as a starting heuristic.)*
- **Pressure and rate of flow:** begin low and adjust; excessive pressure causes swelling
  (distension), especially of the face.
- **Signs of distribution:** colour returning (from dye in the fluid), firming, and
  clearing of discolourations. Massage limbs to aid distribution.
- **Drainage:** intermittent or alternate drainage helps distribution; watch for
  the face swelling (shut off, adjust) and check fingers, toes and the face.
- Areas showing no distribution need **sectional** injection via other vessels or
  hypodermic/surface treatment.

## 5. Cavity treatment
After arterial injection (timing varies by practice), a **trocar** is used to aspirate
fluids and gases from the thoracic and abdominal cavities and hollow organs, then
**cavity fluid** (high-index) is injected into the cavities. The trocar opening is
closed (trocar button or suture). Re-aspiration may be needed later if gases recur.

## 6. Post-embalming
Close incisions (sealing with adhesive or suture), wash and dry the body, apply
massage cream to exposed skin to prevent dehydration, place plastic garments where
leakage is a risk. Complete the embalming report. Clean and disinfect instruments,
table and room; dispose of waste per local rules (blood and fluids are typically
discharged to sanitary sewer only where permitted).

## Gotchas
- Jaundice cases: high-formaldehyde solutions can turn yellow skin greenish; use
  fluids formulated for jaundice.
- Oedematous bodies need stronger solutions and more drainage; dehydrated bodies need
  milder solutions and humectants.
- Autopsied cases require a six-point injection plus treatment of viscera.
- Late-stage decomposition may not be suitable for viewing; discuss options with the
  funeral director and family.

## Verify
- [ ] Authorisation, ID check and any legal hold confirmed before starting.
- [ ] Case analysis recorded; fluid choice matches the conditions found.
- [ ] Distribution confirmed in all areas, or sectional treatment completed.
- [ ] Cavities aspirated and treated; incisions sealed; no leakage.
- [ ] Embalming report completed; formaldehyde exposure kept within limits.
