# Cremation: the identification chain and retort operation

**Goal:** cremate the right person, with the right authority, one at a time, and return
**only** their remains to the right family — with every step documented. Identity
errors are the gravest failures in this work and almost always come from skipping a
check. Equipment details vary by manufacturer; follow your retort's operating manual
and local regulations.

## 1. Before cremation: paperwork and checks
- **Cremation authorisation** signed by the person with legal right to authorise
  (commonly next of kin), plus the required medical/legal certificates (in many places a
  medical referee or medical-examiner approval; a waiting period may apply).
- Confirm **implanted devices** are declared and removed: **pacemakers, implantable
  defibrillators** and some battery-powered devices can explode in the chamber and must be
  removed beforehand (a separate procedure, usually by the funeral home or mortuary).
  Radioactive implants (e.g. some brachytherapy seeds) require specific guidance.
- **Container:** combustible, rigid enough to handle; check for prohibited items (glass,
  some plastics, batteries). Ask what personal items are inside.

## 2. The identification chain
1. On receipt: check the name on the container, the ID tag on the body (where viewing
   the body is required or permitted by local practice), and the paperwork all match.
   Log in with a unique cremation number.
2. Assign a **metal (stainless or ceramic) ID disc** stamped with that number. The disc
   goes into the chamber with the person and stays with the remains through cooling,
   processing and packing.
3. Many operators use a **cremation log** and whiteboard/tag on the retort door showing
   the current case number.
4. Only one person per chamber at a time, except where law permits specific exceptions
   (e.g. a mother and infant, with written authority).

## 3. Retort operation (general sequence)
- Preheat the chamber to the manufacturer's operating range (secondary/afterburner
  chamber typically must be at temperature before charging to meet emission rules —
  modern retorts interlock this).
- **Charge** (load) the container using the loader or charging table quickly and
  smoothly; close the door. Note the time and case number.
- The cycle commonly takes roughly **1.5–3 hours** depending on body size, container and
  equipment; observe through the viewing port as trained, rotating/repositioning remains
  near the end of the cycle with tools only when the manual says it's safe.
- Watch emissions indicators (opacity/smoke) and adjust per the manual; repeated smoke
  usually signals overloading or wrong airflow.
- Let the chamber cool enough; then **sweep out** all remains into a cooling tray
  (every fragment — use the brushes for corners), with the ID disc.

## 4. Processing
- Once cool, remove metal (implants, prosthetic joints, casket hardware) with a magnet
  and by hand; metals are usually recycled via a programme as agreed in the paperwork —
  never kept or sold privately.
- Process the bone fragments in a **cremulator/processor** to a uniform consistency;
  clean the processor thoroughly between cases.
- Place remains in a sealed bag inside the temporary container or the family's urn, with
  the ID disc or a label bearing the cremation number. Typical adult remains weigh
  roughly **2–3.5 kg** (varies widely).

## 5. Release and return
- Release only to the authorised person or funeral home, with a signed receipt and
  matching paperwork. Shipping by post requires carrier-specific rules (e.g. USPS
  Priority Mail Express in the US — check current rules).
- Families who requested witness of the charge (common in some cultures, e.g. Hindu
  families) should have it arranged in advance and handled respectfully.

## Gotchas
- **Never batch** cases or process two at once in the same processor.
- Heat injuries: use full PPE (heat-resistant gloves, face shield, apron) when sweeping out.
- Mercury from dental amalgam is an emissions issue in many places; filtration
  requirements depend on local law.
- Late paperwork or a missing signature means you wait — do not cremate "on promise".

## Verify
- [ ] Authorisation, certificates and device-removal confirmation present before charging.
- [ ] Name/number on container, paperwork, log and retort tag all match.
- [ ] ID disc travelled with the remains at every stage.
- [ ] Chamber and processor fully cleared between cases.
- [ ] Remains released only with a signed receipt to an authorised person.
