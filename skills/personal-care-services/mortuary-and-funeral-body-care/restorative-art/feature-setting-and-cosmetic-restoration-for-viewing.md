# Feature setting and cosmetic restoration for viewing

**Goal:** give the family a natural, peaceful likeness of the person they knew — closed
eyes and mouth with a relaxed expression, any wounds or discolouration repaired, and
cosmetics that look like that person's own complexion. Restorative work is done with
the same respect and privacy as any care of the dead, and only within the family's
wishes and authorisation.

## 1. Start with the reference
Ask the family (through the funeral director) for a **recent photograph** and notes:
hairstyle, parting, glasses, usual makeup or none, facial hair, lipstick colour, jewellery.
Work towards how the person looked in life when well, not an idealised face.

## 2. Feature setting (before or early in embalming)
Features are much easier to set before arterial fluid firms the tissue.
- **Eyes:** clean, then place perforated plastic **eye caps** under the lids to restore
  the curve of sunken eyes and hold lids closed. The lid line should sit at about the
  lower third of the eye socket, as in natural sleep. A small amount of cream or adhesive
  may be used to hold them; avoid gluing visible lashes.
- **Mouth closure:** by a **needle injector** (barbs set into maxilla and mandible, wires
  twisted to close) or a **muscular/mandibular suture** through the septum and below the
  tongue. Then shape lips: natural line, lips just meeting, a slight relaxed form. Cotton
  or mouth formers restore fullness if teeth are absent — dentures, if provided, are
  usually replaced.
- **Expression:** relaxed, neither smiling nor frowning. Check from the viewing angle
  (the right side of the face is usually the side seen from a casket — confirm local
  practice).

## 3. Restoration techniques
- **Tissue building:** small amounts of tissue-building (filler) chemical injected
  hypodermically into sunken cheeks or temples after embalming. Easy to overdo — build
  slowly and compare to the photo.
- **Wounds and incisions:** dry and firm the area, clean, close with sutures (e.g. a
  hidden/intradermal stitch for visible areas) and/or adhesive, seal; then fill
  depressions with **restorative wax** in thin layers, sculpted to match surrounding
  contours and texture (stipple with a brush to imitate pores).
- **Larger trauma:** reconstruction with wax over an internal support structure may be
  possible; discuss honestly with the funeral director whether viewing is realistic.
  Partial viewing (e.g. hands only) or a closed casket with a photograph can be kinder
  options for the family.
- **Discolourations:** bruising, jaundice, or post-mortem stain — first treat chemically
  during embalming (bleaching agents, appropriate fluids), then cover with **opaque**
  mortuary cosmetics.

## 4. Cosmetics
- Work under lighting similar to the viewing room — warm/pink-tinted lights common in
  viewing rooms can mislead.
- Base: a mortuary cream cosmetic or regular cosmetics matched to the photo; thin layers;
  colour to restore warmth where blood colour is lost (lips, cheeks, ears, knuckles,
  fingertips).
- Set lightly with powder to remove shine.
- Lips: the person's own lipstick if provided; otherwise a natural tint.
- Hair: wash and style to the photo; the family's hairdresser may sometimes be invited.
- Nails: clean, trim and (if they wore it) polish.
- Men's facial hair: shave if the person was clean-shaven (shaving post-mortem can cause
  dark "razor burn" — moisten skin, use a sharp razor, apply massage cream after).

## Gotchas
- Massage cream on the face and hands after embalming prevents dehydration
  darkening ("leather" look) before viewing.
- Overfilling with tissue builder can't be undone easily.
- Waxes left in warm rooms soften; seal and set with powder.
- Never show the family unfinished work; let the funeral director present the
  person when ready, and invite feedback ("is there anything we can adjust?").

## Verify
- [ ] Photograph and family requests obtained before cosmetics.
- [ ] Eyes and mouth closed naturally with a relaxed expression from the viewing angle.
- [ ] Wounds and incisions sealed, waxed and textured; no leakage.
- [ ] Complexion matches the photo under viewing-room lighting.
- [ ] Hair, glasses and grooming match the family's wishes.
