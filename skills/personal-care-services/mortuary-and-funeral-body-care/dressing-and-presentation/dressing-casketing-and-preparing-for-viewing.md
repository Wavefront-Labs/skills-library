# Dressing, casketing and preparing for viewing

**Goal:** present the person peacefully and with dignity in the clothes the family
chose, positioned naturally in the casket or coffin, in a viewing room that feels calm
— with every personal item logged so nothing is lost or wrongly buried.

## 1. Check instructions first
From the arrangement notes: clothing supplied, underwear, jewellery to be **left on vs
returned**, glasses, rosary/religious items, letters or items to be placed, hair and
makeup notes, whether the family wants to help dress (some do — accommodate if possible).
Check for **cultural or religious requirements** — e.g. some traditions require same-gender
care, ritual washing by community members (such as Jewish *tahara* or Islamic *ghusl*),
shrouds rather than clothes, or no embalming/cosmetics. Ask; don't assume.

## 2. Washing and protective measures
- Wash and dry the body; shampoo and dry hair. Trim nails.
- Where leakage is a risk (oedema, after autopsy or donation, skin slip), use
  **plastic garments** — "unionalls", pants, stockings, sleeves — under clothing, with
  absorbent powder. Clothes ruined by leakage are distressing for families.
- Apply massage cream to hands and face to prevent dehydration.

## 3. Dressing technique
- Dress on the dressing table/tray, not in the casket. Two people make it easier and
  safer for your back.
- **Trousers:** roll each leg, slide over feet, pull up while rolling the body side to side.
- **Shirts/jackets/dresses:** commonly the garment is **cut up the back** (centre-back seam)
  so it can be put on from the front without forcing arms. Ask or confirm local practice
  and the family's wishes — it is standard but some families want garments intact
  (e.g. to be returned). Arms go in first, then smooth the halves under the body.
- Tight clothing: don't force — it can tear skin. Cut and tuck neatly.
- Shoes are optional; many families supply them, some don't.

## 4. Casketing
- Use a lift or at least two people (manual handling rules apply). Line up the body with
  the casket, lift together on a count.
- **Position:** body centred, head on the pillow raised so the face is visible from the
  kneeler/side, tilted very slightly (commonly ~10–15°) towards the viewing side. Shoulders
  level, not hunched; hands placed on the abdomen, often right over left (or as the
  family wishes), elbows tucked so they don't touch the casket sides.
- Adjust pillow height with blocks or by firming the bedding; check from the family's
  viewpoint — standing and kneeling.
- Smooth the clothing, collar and tie; arrange the overlay/blanket.
- Final cosmetic touch-up, hair, and glasses (if worn — check that lenses are clean and the
  frame doesn't press the face).

## 5. Personal effects
- Log every item **on the body, removed, and placed** with descriptions (e.g. "yellow-metal
  ring with clear stone", not "gold diamond ring" — don't assert value or material).
- Have the family confirm in writing which items remain with the person for burial or
  cremation (metal items must be removed before cremation in many crematoria; jewellery can't
  be retrieved afterward).
- Store removed items securely and return with a signature.

## 6. The viewing room
- Soft, warm-toned lighting, ideally a pink-tinted lamp over the casket; check how the
  complexion looks under it.
- Temperature cool. Tissues, chairs, quiet. Flowers placed so they don't hide the face.
- Before inviting the family in, **the funeral director checks the whole presentation**.

## Gotchas
- Mixing up personal effects or clothing is one of the most serious, avoidable errors —
  ID check at every stage (body tag, clothing bag tag, effects log).
- Pacemakers and some other implants must be removed before cremation (a separate
  procedure — check the cremation paperwork).
- Leaving the mouth or eyes slightly open shows up immediately in the casket — check features
  last thing.

## Verify
- [ ] Clothing, items and cultural/religious instructions confirmed against the arrangement.
- [ ] Protective garments used where leakage is possible.
- [ ] Body positioned centred, head tilted towards the viewer, hands natural.
- [ ] Personal-effects log complete and confirmed with the family.
- [ ] ID checked at each stage; viewing room checked before the family enters.
