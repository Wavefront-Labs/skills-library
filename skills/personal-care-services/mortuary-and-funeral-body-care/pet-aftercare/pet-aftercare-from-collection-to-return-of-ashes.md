# Pet aftercare: from collection to return of ashes

**Goal:** care for companion animals after death — collected from veterinary
practices or homes, handled gently, stored correctly, cremated (or otherwise
laid to rest) exactly as the owner chose, and returned with any keepsakes — with
tracking good enough that an owner's trust is never misplaced. The industry's
reputation rests on owners getting **their** pet's ashes when they paid for an
individual cremation.

## 1. Service types (be precise with words)
- **Individual (private) cremation:** one animal in the chamber; ashes returned. The only
  option that guarantees the ashes are only your pet's.
- **Partitioned/"individual-ish" cremation:** several animals in the chamber, separated
  by trays or bricks; some mixing is possible. Must be disclosed honestly as such.
- **Communal cremation:** several animals together, ashes not returned (often scattered
  in a garden of remembrance).
- Also: pet burial (pet cemetery or home burial where legal), and pet aquamation (alkaline
  hydrolysis) in some areas.
Mislabelling a partitioned service as "individual" has led to legal action and scandal.

## 2. After euthanasia (vet or home)
- Close the eyes if possible (they often stay open; a small amount of eye lubricant or
  gentle pressure can help but they may not close). Clean the mouth and any fluids;
  place an absorbent pad beneath — bladder and bowel may empty after death.
- **Position** naturally, curled as if sleeping, **before rigor mortis** sets in (it often
  starts within a few hours). Pets in an awkward position are hard to fit in a coffin or
  casket later.
- Remove collar and tags unless the owner wants them kept on; record either way.
- Offer keepsakes before cremation: **paw print** (ink or clay), **nose print**, a clipping of
  fur (from a discreet area), whisker. Ask first — some owners find this hard, others
  treasure it.

## 3. Collection and identification
- Each animal gets a **unique ID tag/number** at collection (often a tag on a leg or
  collar plus a tag on the bag), recorded with owner name, species, breed, colour,
  approximate weight and service type.
- Use leak-proof bags or a stretcher/pet carrier; larger dogs need two people or a
  stretcher (manual handling!).
- **Refrigerate** promptly (around 2–4 °C) or freeze if holding longer before cremation,
  per your facility's procedures.

## 4. Cremation and tracking
- Follow the same ID discipline as human cremation: tag on the bag → log → metal or
  ceramic ID tag in the chamber (for individual cremations) → cooling tray → processing →
  container label. One animal per chamber for individual cremation; clean chamber and
  processor fully between individual cases.
- Many crematoria offer owners the option to **witness** the start of an individual
  cremation — arrange in advance and treat it with the same care as a human service.
- Microchips do not survive cremation reliably; don't rely on them for ID during the process.

## 5. Returning remains
- Ashes in a sealed bag inside the chosen urn or scatter tube, with a certificate naming the
  pet and cremation number.
- Return by the owner's chosen route (vet practice, home delivery, collection). Include
  the keepsakes, and a short handwritten note if your practice does that — owners notice.
- Expect strong grief; acknowledge the loss by the pet's name. Have information on pet-loss
  support services available.

## Gotchas
- Small animals (hamsters, birds, reptiles) may produce very little or almost no visible
  ash; tell the owner beforehand to avoid distress or suspicion.
- Large dogs can exceed some small cremators' capacity — check before accepting.
- Pets treated with certain drugs (e.g. euthanasia solution, pentobarbital) must not be left
  where wildlife could eat them — relevant to home burials; advise deep burial or
  cremation.
- Keep records for audits — some regions have pet crematorium codes of practice or
  accreditation schemes.

## Verify
- [ ] Service type explained in plain words and recorded; owner consent for keepsakes.
- [ ] Pet positioned naturally before rigor; cleaned and bagged with absorbent pad.
- [ ] Unique ID tag at collection, log, chamber and container all match.
- [ ] Individual cremations truly one animal per chamber with full clean-out between.
- [ ] Ashes and keepsakes returned with certificate to the right owner.
