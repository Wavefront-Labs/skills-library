# Classic manicure: shaping, cuticles and polish

**Goal:** leave natural nails shaped evenly, cuticle area tidy without injuring live
skin, and polish smooth with clean edges that lasts — while keeping every tool hygienic
so you never spread infection between clients.

## 1. Inspect before you start
- Look at nails and skin: fungal infection (yellow, thickened, crumbling), bacterial
  infection (green discolouration, "greenies"), paronychia (red, swollen nail fold),
  warts, open cuts, nail separating from the bed (onycholysis).
- **Infection or broken skin → don't work on it.** Recommend seeing a doctor or
  pharmacist. Cosmetic services don't treat conditions.
- Ask what length/shape they want and lifestyle (typing, gardening).

## 2. Remove old polish and shape
- Remove polish with a remover pad, pressing and holding a few seconds before wiping
  (dissolves, doesn't smear).
- **Filing:** use a fine file on natural nails — **~180–240 grit** (lower numbers are
  coarser and are for enhancements). File from side towards centre in **one direction**,
  not sawing back and forth, which splits layers.
- Shapes: square, squoval (square with softened corners — strongest for most), round,
  oval, almond. Look down the finger from the tip to check symmetry; match all ten.
- Don't file deep into the sides (sidewalls) — it weakens the nail and invites breaks
  and ingrowns.

## 3. Cuticle work (the risky part)
Terminology: the **cuticle** is the dead, thin tissue stuck to the nail plate; the
**eponychium** is the living fold of skin at the base. Only remove the dead cuticle.
1. Soften: warm soak (~2–3 min) or a cuticle remover gel (follow product timing —
   these are alkaline and can irritate if left too long).
2. Gently **push back** the eponychium with a pusher held at a low angle; lift the dead
   cuticle off the nail plate.
3. **Nip only hanging dead tissue** and hangnails with sharp, clean nippers, in one
   clean bite. Do not cut the living eponychium — cutting live skin risks infection and
   is outside scope in many licensing rules.
4. Clean under the free edge with a wooden stick or a soft brush.

## 4. Hand treatment and massage (5 min)
Exfoliate if part of the service, then lotion and massage: effleurage up the forearm,
thumb circles in the palm, gentle finger traction. Afterward, **remove all oil from the
nail plates** with remover or alcohol — oil under polish causes peeling.

## 5. Polish application
1. **Base coat** — thin; it protects nail and improves adhesion.
2. **Colour, two thin coats** with the **three-stroke method**: wipe excess off one side
   of the brush; place the brush near the cuticle (not touching it), push slightly towards
   the cuticle to fan the bristles, then stroke down the centre, then each side. Leave a
   hairline gap at the cuticle and sidewalls.
3. **Cap the free edge** (run the brush lightly along the tip) to reduce chipping.
4. **Top coat**, also capped.
5. Clean mistakes with a small flat brush dipped in remover.
Drying: regular polish feels dry in minutes but stays soft for an hour or more; quick-dry
drops help the surface only.

## 6. Hygiene
- Single-use items (files, buffers, wooden sticks) get thrown away or given to the client.
- Metal tools: clean (scrub with soap/detergent) **then** disinfect in an appropriate
  hospital-grade disinfectant for the full contact time, or sterilise (autoclave) where
  required. Store in a clean, closed container. Regulations differ by state/country —
  follow yours.
- Clean the table and wash hands between clients.

## Gotchas
- Bubbles in polish come from shaking the bottle (roll it instead), thick coats or heat.
- Polish peeling off in one piece = oil or water left on the nail.
- Over-pushing the cuticle or aggressive nipping causes pain, bleeding and infection.
- Nails soaked too long absorb water and expand, then shrink under polish — chips.

## Verify
- [ ] Nails/skin inspected; any infection declined and referred.
- [ ] All ten nails a matching length and shape; no filed-in sidewalls.
- [ ] Only dead cuticle removed; no bleeding.
- [ ] Polish thin, even, free edge capped, clean margin at cuticle and sides.
- [ ] Tools cleaned and disinfected; single-use items discarded.
