# Gel polish, builder gel, dip powder and safe removal

**Goal:** apply gel and dip systems so they last 2–3+ weeks without lifting, cure them
properly (under-cured gel is the main driver of allergy), and remove them without
shredding the natural nail.

## 1. Know the systems
- **Gel polish (soak-off):** thin, flexible, cured under UV/LED; removes with acetone.
- **Builder gel** (incl. "BIAB"-type, builder in a bottle): thicker, self-levelling, adds
  strength or short extensions; usually soak-off or file-off depending on product.
- **Hard gel:** very durable, extensions; doesn't soak off — must be filed off.
- **Dip powder:** base liquid (cyanoacrylate-type adhesive) → powder → activator → top coat;
  no lamp.

## 2. Prep (identical importance across systems)
Push back and remove dead cuticle from the plate, lightly buff off shine (~180+ grit,
don't thin), dust, dehydrate, primer/bond if the system uses one. Keep everything off the
skin: product on skin is the commonest route to sensitisation.

## 3. Gel application and curing
1. **Thin coats.** Base coat thin, cure; colour 2 thin coats, cure each; top coat, cure.
   Thick coats cure poorly at the bottom → lifting, wrinkling and allergy risk.
2. **Cap the free edge** each coat.
3. **Match lamp and gel.** Use the lamp the manufacturer specifies (wavelength and
   power) and the stated cure time. A cheap or mismatched lamp can leave gel under-cured
   even if it feels hard. Replace UV bulbs as directed; LED lamps fade too.
4. **Inhibition layer:** a tacky film left after curing on many gels; wipe with cleanser
   at the end (or not, if using a no-wipe top coat).
5. **Heat spikes:** curing releases heat; thick builder layers or sensitive clients can
   feel a burning sensation. Use a "low-heat" mode or pull the hand out for a few seconds
   then return (if the brand allows "flash curing"), and keep layers thin.
6. Thumbs separately for even curing; keep fingers flat in the lamp.

## 4. Allergy: the serious issue
Methacrylate monomers (notably **HEMA**) cause allergic contact dermatitis — itchy,
swollen, blistered skin around nails, sometimes nail-bed damage. Once sensitised it is
usually lifelong and can affect future medical/dental treatments using acrylates.
- Avoid skin contact; wipe spills from skin before curing.
- Cure fully, using matched lamp and products.
- Consider HEMA-free products for sensitive clients (still not risk-free).
- Stop and refer any client (or yourself) with symptoms. Don't sell untrained home kits
  without warnings.

## 5. Dip powder
- **Hygiene:** dipping fingers into a shared jar spreads infection. Professionally, use
  the **pour-over method** (sprinkle powder over the nail above a dish; discard used
  powder) or a client-only jar.
- Thin layers of base, then powder, ~2–3 layers; stay off the cuticle area to avoid bulk.
- Activator, buff/shape, more activator if required, top coat.

## 6. Soak-off removal
1. **File off the top coat** (break the seal) with a medium file — just the shine, not down
   to the nail.
2. Acetone-soaked cotton on each nail, wrapped in foil or clips, **~10–15 minutes**
   (warmth speeds it). Dip powder often needs longer.
3. Gently push lifted product off with an orangewood stick or pusher. **If it doesn't lift,
   re-wrap** rather than scraping or peeling.
4. Buff lightly, hydrate with cuticle oil.
Builder gels may be "filed down" partially and rebalanced instead of fully removed (infill).
Hard gel: file off; an e-file speeds this, with training.

## Gotchas
- Peeling gel off at home strips nail layers — clients do it; educate them.
- Wrinkling = coat too thick or under-cured.
- Lifting: prep (cuticle left on plate), oily nails, product flooded onto skin.
- Mixing brands (base from one, top from another) can cure inconsistently — use the system.

## Verify
- [ ] Prep thorough; no product on skin.
- [ ] Thin coats cured with the manufacturer's lamp and time; no wrinkles or tackiness after cleanse.
- [ ] Client asked about itching/redness history; HEMA-free used where indicated.
- [ ] Dip by pour-over or single-client jar.
- [ ] Removal by soak, not scraping; nail surface intact afterward.
