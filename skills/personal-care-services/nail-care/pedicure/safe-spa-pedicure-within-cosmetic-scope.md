# Safe spa pedicure within cosmetic scope

**Goal:** soften, smooth and tidy feet and toenails and finish with polish and massage —
without causing infection, cuts or ingrown nails, and knowing exactly where cosmetic work
stops and podiatry (chiropody) begins.

## 1. Footbath hygiene — the big risk
Whirlpool footspas have been linked to outbreaks of skin infection (e.g. *Mycobacterium
fortuitum*) when not cleaned properly. **Pipeless** spas or disposable tub liners reduce
risk, but still need cleaning.
- **After every client:** drain, scrub surfaces (and remove/clean jets or impellers per
  manufacturer) with detergent, rinse, then fill/circulate with an EPA-registered (or
  locally approved) disinfectant for the **full contact time** on the label; drain and rinse.
- **End of day/weekly:** deeper cleaning of the jets/screens per your local regulation.
- Log each cleaning — inspectors may ask. Rules vary by state/country.
- Advise clients **not to shave or wax their legs** for ~24 h before a pedicure — tiny
  cuts are entry points.

## 2. Screening
Before soaking, look at the feet and ask:
- **Diabetes or poor circulation:** reduced sensation and slower healing mean even a small
  nick can become serious. Gentle work only: no cutting, no heavy filing, no hot water
  (test temperature yourself), and many technicians decline or refer to a podiatrist.
- Infections (athlete's foot, fungal nails, verrucas), open wounds, swelling, or a red,
  painful **ingrown toenail** → don't treat; refer.
- Blood thinners: be careful with any tool that could break skin.

## 3. The sequence
1. Remove polish. **Soak** in warm water (~5 min); longer soaking over-softens and makes
   overfiling likely.
2. **Toenail shaping:** clip **straight across**, keeping corners just visible beyond the
   skin; smooth with a file. Don't round the corners down into the sides or cut short —
   this is the classic cause of ingrown toenails.
3. **Cuticles:** gentle push back; nip only loose dead tissue. Don't dig down the sides
   of the nail.
4. **Calluses:** use a foot file or pumice to **smooth**, not remove — calluses protect
   pressure points. Stop at smooth; pink or tender skin means too far. **No credo blades or
   razors**: they're banned for cosmetic technicians in many jurisdictions and cause
   cuts. Callus softeners: follow time limits exactly.
5. **Exfoliate** legs and feet with a scrub; rinse.
6. **Massage** feet and lower legs with lotion (5–10 min): thumb circles on the sole,
   effleurage up the calf. Avoid deep pressure on varicose veins.
7. **Remove lotion** from toenails, then toe separators, base coat, two thin colour coats,
   top coat. Allow drying time; offer open-toe disposable slippers.

## 4. Scope boundaries
Within cosmetic scope: soaking, tidying nails, light callus smoothing, massage,
polish. Outside scope (refer to a podiatrist/chiropodist): ingrown nail treatment,
corn removal, thick fungal nails, cracked heels that bleed, diabetic foot care, any
wound or infection.

## 5. Tools
Single-use: files, buffers, pumice (if porous), toe separators, liners. Metal clippers and
pushers: clean then disinfect/sterilise as for manicure.

## Gotchas
- "Medical pedicure" is a term that may require specific training or podiatric supervision
  in your area — don't use it loosely.
- Very hot water feels luxurious but scalds clients with neuropathy.
- Overfiled heels thicken back faster — the body responds to friction with more callus.
- Smudged toes: wait longer or use quick-dry, and warn clients about closed shoes.

## Verify
- [ ] Footbath cleaned and disinfected for the full contact time before this client (logged).
- [ ] Feet screened; diabetic, infected or ingrown cases referred or adapted.
- [ ] Toenails cut straight across with corners intact.
- [ ] Calluses smoothed, not removed; no blades used; no broken skin.
- [ ] Polish neat; client left with drying guidance.
