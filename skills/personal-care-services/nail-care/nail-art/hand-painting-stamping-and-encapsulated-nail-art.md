# Hand painting, stamping and encapsulated nail art

**Goal:** add decoration — painted designs, stamped patterns, chrome, crystals,
encapsulated elements — that looks crisp, matches the client's brief, and survives
the 2–3 week wear cycle without snagging or chipping.

## 1. Plan the design
- Agree the design with references: number of feature nails (one accent per hand is
  quick; full sets take far longer — price by time), colour palette, scale (small nails
  need simpler designs).
- Sketch or do one nail first and check with the client before committing to all ten.
- Choose the **medium**: gel paints (don't dry until cured — best for detail, allow
  correcting), regular polish (fast to dry, unforgiving), acrylic paints (water-based, dry
  quickly, must be sealed with top coat).

## 2. Hand painting
- Tools: **liner/striping brush** (long, thin bristles for lines), **detail brush** (short,
  fine tip for small shapes), **dotting tools** (dots, flowers).
- Thin the paint to a flowing, ink-like consistency; thick paint gives blobby lines.
- Stabilise: rest your painting hand's little finger on the client's finger or the table;
  turn the client's hand rather than your wrist.
- Pull lines towards you in one continuous stroke; long brushes draw longer lines.
- Build designs light to dark; outline last (black liner over colour hides wobbles).
- Gel paint: **flash cure** in short bursts between elements to stop bleeding.

## 3. Stamping
- Kit: **stamping plate** (etched design), highly pigmented **stamping polish**,
  **scraper** (card or blade), **silicone stamper**.
- Steps: apply polish over the design, **scrape at ~45°** with one firm pass, roll the
  stamper over the design immediately (polish dries fast), roll onto the nail in one
  smooth motion from side to side.
- Clean the stamper and plate between uses with remover or sticky tape. Prime new stampers
  (lightly buff) if they don't pick up.
- Clean up excess around the cuticle with a brush and remover, then top coat gently —
  **"float" the top coat** (don't drag the brush) or regular polish stamps will smear.

## 4. Chrome, foils and effects
- **Chrome/mirror powders:** applied on a no-wipe gel top coat after curing (rub in with a
  silicone tool or applicator), then sealed with top coat. Cap the edges to prevent
  peeling.
- **Transfer foils:** use foil glue or tacky gel; press foil shiny side up, peel off.
- Ombre/gradient: sponge or airbrush; clean the cuticle area before sealing.

## 5. Crystals, charms and 3D
- Place on a thick layer of **builder gel or nail glue/gel adhesive**, cured.
- **Surround the stones with gel** to the girdle (the widest part) so edges can't snag
  hair or fabric; don't cover the top facets (kills sparkle).
- Keep 3D elements away from the free edge and cuticle; low-profile designs last longer.
- Check by running a cotton pad over the nail: if fibres catch, add more gel.

## 6. Encapsulation
Embedding art inside clear product — dried flowers, glitter, foil, painted designs — in
acrylic or builder gel.
- Apply a clear layer, place the element flat, then cover with more clear product.
- File and shape over the top without cutting into the embedded design.
- Make sure the element is **dry and flat**; air bubbles or moisture cause lifting and
  cloudiness.

## Gotchas
- Detailed hand-painting on every nail can double appointment time — price and schedule
  for it.
- Hygiene still applies: decontaminate dotting tools; don't reuse a dirty stamper on the
  next client.
- Copyrighted characters or brand logos: be cautious about posting work of others'
  trademarks for sale/promotion in some places.
- Bulky designs on short nails look cramped — scale down.

## Verify
- [ ] Design and number of feature nails agreed; first nail approved.
- [ ] Lines crisp, consistent across matching nails; stamping clean with no smears.
- [ ] Chrome/foil edges capped; stones surrounded by gel and pass the cotton-snag test.
- [ ] Encapsulated elements fully covered, flat, no bubbles.
- [ ] Final top coat smooth; cuticle area clean.
