# Studio hygiene and aftercare — a station that can't cross-contaminate

**Goal:** set up, work and break down a tattoo or body-art station so no client's blood
reaches another client or you, and send every client home with aftercare they'll follow.

Your local body-art regulation is the binding rulebook (in the US, OSHA's Bloodborne
Pathogens Standard plus state/county rules; elsewhere, national or council licensing).
This is the working method most regulations converge on.

## 1. The principle: clean zone vs dirty zone
Once your gloves touch broken skin, **everything you touch is contaminated** unless it is
barrier-covered or single-use. Plan the setup so that everything you'll need is within reach
and covered before you start, and so you never reach into clean supplies with dirty gloves.

## 2. Setup (in this order)
1. Wash hands (20+ s, soap, running water); dry with paper.
2. Disinfect the work surfaces, chair/bed, armrest, lamp handle and anything you'll touch with
   a hospital-grade disinfectant (in the US, EPA-registered against HBV/HIV), **respecting the
   label contact time** — the surface must stay wet for that time (often 1-10 minutes). Wiping
   dry immediately doesn't disinfect.
3. Glove up. Cover with barrier film/bags: machine (machine bag), clip cord or RCA cable
   (sleeve), power supply buttons, spray bottles, lamp handle, ink-cap holder area.
4. Lay out on a fresh barrier/dental bib: single-use ink caps, ink poured from the bottle
   (never dip into the stock bottle), ointment portioned out with a clean spatula, rinse cup,
   paper towels, razor, stencil, needles/cartridges still in sealed packaging.
5. Open sterile needles/cartridges **in front of the client** so they see the seal and date.

## 3. During the work
- Change gloves whenever they tear, after touching anything uncovered, and when leaving
  the station. Wash or sanitise hands at each change.
- If you must reach for an uncovered item, take gloves off first or use a fresh paper towel.
- Single-use: needles, cartridges, ink caps, ink, ointment portions, razors, barrier film, gloves.

## 4. Breakdown
1. With gloves on: needles/cartridges and razors straight into a **sharps container** —
   never recap needles by hand. Replace the container when it's 3/4 full.
2. Ink caps, towels, barriers and anything with blood into the appropriate waste stream
   (some places require biohazard bags for blood-soaked items — check local rules).
3. Remove barriers carefully, rolling them inward.
4. Clean and disinfect all surfaces again with full contact time; reusable items (grips,
   tubes if used) go to cleaning and the autoclave (see the autoclaves skill).
5. Remove gloves, wash hands.

## 5. Your own protection
- Hepatitis B vaccination (offered free by employers in the US under OSHA) and documented BBP
  training.
- An exposure plan: if you get a needlestick, wash the area with soap and water, report it,
  and seek medical assessment the same day — post-exposure treatment is time-sensitive.

## 6. Client aftercare (give it in writing too)
Methods vary; pick one, give it clearly, and match the advice of your licensing authority.
- **Wrap:** cling film or a breathable adhesive film (e.g. "second skin" products). Cling film:
  remove after a few hours. Adhesive films are often left on for several days — remove early if
  fluid builds up a lot or the skin reacts.
- **Wash** 2-3 times a day with clean hands, unscented soap and lukewarm water; pat dry with
  clean paper towel.
- **Moisturise** thinly with an unscented aftercare product; too much suffocates the tattoo.
- **Avoid:** soaking (baths, pools, sea, hot tubs) and direct sun for about 2-4 weeks; picking
  or scratching flakes; tight clothing rubbing it.
- **Red flags — see a doctor:** spreading redness, increasing pain or heat after day 3, pus,
  red streaks, fever. Allergic reactions (especially to red pigment) can appear weeks later.

## Verify
- [ ] Disinfectant used with its full label contact time, not wiped straight off.
- [ ] Every touched non-disposable item was barrier-covered before work.
- [ ] Sterile packages opened in front of the client; nothing recapped by hand.
- [ ] Sharps container below 3/4 full; waste separated by local rules.
- [ ] Client left with written aftercare including medical red flags.
