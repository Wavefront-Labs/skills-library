# Tattoo design and composition — drawings that still read in ten years

**Goal:** turn a client idea into a design that fits the body part, survives ink
spread and ageing, and transfers cleanly as a stencil.

## 1. Consultation: get the brief, not just the picture
- Ask for **meaning, must-have elements, size range, placement, colour or black-and-grey,
  and budget/time**. Reference images are mood, not a spec — never copy another artist's
  custom piece; redraw in your own hand.
- Photograph the placement on the client in the pose they usually hold (arm down,
  standing). Muscles and curves change the canvas.
- Agree what is negotiable. Clients often ask for too much detail at too small a size;
  this is the most common design failure and you must say so before drawing.

## 2. Design for skin, not paper
**Readability rules of thumb** (convention, not measured standards):
- **Size floor:** a small detail closer than about 1 mm to another line will merge as
  ink spreads over years. Fine-line text below roughly 5-6 mm cap height blurs fastest.
  If the client wants it smaller, simplify the drawing, not the size.
- **Line weight hierarchy:** a bold outer silhouette, medium interior lines, fine detail.
  A design that is all one line weight reads as a smudge from three metres.
- **Contrast and negative space:** leave skin breaks between dark areas. The
  "squint test" — view the design at thumbnail size or blurred; if it doesn't read, it
  won't on skin.
- **Value plan:** decide black, mid-grey and skin (highlight) areas before detailing.
  Aim for a full range; designs stuck in mid-grey age into mush.

## 3. Placement and flow
- Follow the long axis of the muscle (forearm, calf, upper arm). Designs fighting the
  anatomy look crooked when the limb moves.
- Wrap-around pieces: draw on a photograph or a tracing of the actual limb, or plan the
  design in sections; a flat drawing distorts when wrapped around a cylinder.
- High-flex areas (inner elbow, knuckles, ribs over breathing, feet/hands) blur and fade
  faster — warn the client and increase line weight.
- Text: check spelling with the client in writing, letter by letter, and keep it level
  with the body's horizon in the client's natural stance.

## 4. Cover-ups and reworks
- Photograph the old tattoo in good light and trace its darkest areas onto your sketch layer.
- The new design must put its **darkest values over the old darkest values**; you cannot
  cover black with lighter colour. Expect the cover-up to be roughly 2-3x the area of
  the original (rule of thumb — varies a lot with ink density).
- Organic textures (foliage, feathers, water, dark backgrounds) hide old lines better
  than clean geometry or lettering.
- Heavy old work may need laser lightening sessions first; refer out rather than promise
  the impossible.

## 5. Stencil preparation
- Clean, single linework only; shading becomes a few guide lines, not hatching.
- Thermal stencil paper through a thermal copier, or hand-trace with a stencil pen onto
  hectograph (spirit) paper. **Mirror check:** most thermal printers handle orientation
  automatically, but hand transfers can come out reversed — verify text reads correctly.
- Apply over clean, shaved skin with a stencil transfer solution; let it dry fully
  (often 10-20 minutes) before tattooing so it doesn't wipe away. Check placement in a
  mirror with the client standing, and re-apply rather than "eyeballing" a crooked stencil.

## Gotchas
- **Over-detailing:** what looks great on an iPad at 400% zoom disappears at 5 cm.
- **Symmetry illusions:** perfectly symmetrical designs on asymmetric bodies look off;
  centre on landmarks (spine, sternum), not ruler measurements alone.
- **Trademark/copyright:** reproducing characters, logos or another artist's art can
  carry legal risk; rules vary by country (uncertain — check local law).
- **Ageing:** colours like white and pale yellow fade first; don't build key contrast on them.

## Verify
- [ ] The design reads at thumbnail size and blurred (squint test).
- [ ] No detail is finer than the spacing ink spread will merge at that size.
- [ ] Placement photo shows the design following the muscle in the client's natural pose.
- [ ] Text spelling confirmed in writing by the client.
- [ ] Stencil is the right way round, fully dry, and approved in a mirror while standing.
- [ ] For cover-ups, the new darkest areas sit over the old darkest areas.
