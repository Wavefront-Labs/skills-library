# Henna and mehndi — safe paste, clean cones, dark stains

**Goal:** mix natural henna paste with good dye release, apply crisp freehand designs, and
get a deep stain — while never using "black henna".

## 1. Safety first: no "black henna"
- Natural henna (*Lawsonia inermis*) stains **orange to red-brown**, never black.
- "Black henna" is usually henna or dye adulterated with **PPD (para-phenylenediamine)**,
  a hair-dye chemical. On skin it can cause severe allergic blistering and lifelong
  sensitisation (later reactions to hair dye and some clothing dyes). Regulators such as the
  US FDA warn against it. Don't use it, and tell clients why.
- **Jagua** (from *Genipa americana*) gives a genuine blue-black stain and is the honest
  alternative for dark designs; patch-test it — allergic reactions are reported, though less
  commonly than with PPD (evidence is limited).
- Buy body-art-quality (BAQ) henna powder with a recent crop date and a supplier who lists
  no additives. Old or "compound" henna gives weak stains.

## 2. Mixing paste (a common working recipe — adjust to your powder)
- 20 g sifted henna powder.
- ~60-80 ml liquid: lemon juice or water (lemon is traditional; plain water or distilled water
  also releases dye, and is gentler on sensitive skin).
- ~1 tsp sugar (helps stringiness and adhesion).
- ~5 ml essential oil with high monoterpene alcohols ("terps"), e.g. tea tree, cajeput or
  lavender. **Skin safety:** keep essential oil under about 5% of the paste and avoid it on
  pregnant clients, babies and small children — use plain paste or skip them.
- Mix to a mashed-potato texture, cover with cling film touching the surface, and leave at
  room temperature until dye release: test by dabbing on your palm — an orange stain after
  5 minutes means it's ready. Typically **6-24 hours** depending on powder and temperature.
- Thin with a little more liquid to "toothpaste" consistency, beat out air, strain through
  fine fabric, and fill cones. Freeze extra cones; thaw just before use.

## 3. Cones and application
- Roll cellophane or mylar into a tight cone, tape it, fill, and snip a tiny tip; smaller tip
  = finer line.
- Hold the cone like a pen, lightly touching the skin, **squeezing gently and lifting
  slightly** so the line "drapes" onto the skin rather than being dragged.
- Clean, dry, lotion-free skin. Palms and soles take the darkest stain; the stain gets
  lighter towards the upper arm and torso (thicker, less keratinised skin there? — commonly
  observed, mechanism uncertain).
- Traditional vocabulary: Indian bridal (dense paisleys, mandalas, fill patterns, figures),
  Arabic/Khaleeji (bold floral trails with negative space), Moroccan (geometric). Learn the
  motifs in drills — lines, dots, paisleys, petals — before full designs.

## 4. Getting a dark stain
- Leave the paste on as long as possible — **4-8 hours or overnight**.
- Seal with a light dab of lemon-sugar solution once the paste is dry to keep it in contact;
  some artists wrap with medical tape or tissue overnight.
- Scrape off (don't wash); avoid water for 12-24 h. The stain starts orange and **oxidises
  darker over 24-48 hours**. Warmth helps.
- Bridal: apply 1-2 days before the event for the peak colour.

## Gotchas
- Paste too thick: lines break and skip; too runny: lines spread and bleed.
- Over-aged paste loses strength; frozen paste keeps for months.
- Clients with G6PD deficiency: henna has been linked to haemolysis in children with this
  condition — don't apply to infants or anyone known to have it.

## Verify
- [ ] Powder is BAQ with a recent crop date; no PPD or "black henna" products in the kit.
- [ ] Dye release confirmed with a palm test before cones are filled.
- [ ] Essential oil at or under ~5%, and none for pregnant clients or children.
- [ ] Lines are continuous with no skips at a consistent width.
- [ ] Client given aftercare: keep paste on long, scrape not wash, stain darkens over 48 h.
