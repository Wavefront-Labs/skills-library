# Tattoo application — lining, shading and packing without blowouts

**Goal:** deposit ink in the upper-to-mid dermis consistently, so lines are crisp, shading
is smooth, colour is solid, and the skin heals without scarring.

The numbers below are working ranges taught in studios, not lab measurements — every
machine, needle and skin differs. Calibrate on practice skin and with a mentor.

## 1. Before the needle touches skin
- Stencil dry, station set up (see studio-hygiene-and-aftercare skill), client positioned
  so **you** can stretch and see without twisting.
- **Stretch is half the technique.** Skin must be taut and flat under the needle — three-point
  stretch with your free hand and, where needed, the client's or an assistant's hand.
  Loose skin causes wobbly lines and blowouts.
- **Work order:** generally lines first, then black/grey shading, then colour from darkest to
  lightest, finishing with white highlights. Work from the bottom of the area up (if right-handed,
  often right to left) so you don't wipe the stencil or smear through ink and plasma.

## 2. Needle depth — the core skill
- Target: through the epidermis into the upper dermis. Commonly quoted needle hang/exposure is
  about **1-2 mm** beyond the tip; the felt cue matters more than the number.
- **Too shallow:** ink sits in the epidermis, the line looks good today and falls out patchy
  during healing.
- **Too deep:** ink spreads in the fat layer under the dermis — a **blowout**: a blurred blue
  halo around lines that appears in days and is permanent (only laser or cover-up fixes it).
- Cues: a correct line has a slight "tick" of resistance and a clean, sharp edge; the needle
  should not drag or bounce. Thin skin (inner arm, ribs, top of foot, elderly clients) needs
  less depth and a lighter hand.

## 3. Lining
- Use a **round liner** sized to the line weight (e.g. 3RL-9RL; larger for bold traditional).
- Dip, then **pull lines towards you** at a steady hand speed — speed and depth must stay
  constant or the line thickens and thins. Start moving just before contact and keep moving
  as you lift, to avoid blobs at line ends.
- Long lines in one confident pass; joining short segments shows as kinks.
- If a line is weak, wait and go over it once — repeated passes chew the skin.

## 4. Shading
- **Magnums / curved magnums (round mags)** for most shading; tight liners for small detail.
- Black-and-grey: build a **grey wash** set (e.g. 3-5 dilutions of black in a mixing solution)
  and lay lightest values first if you want control, or go darkest-first if that's your system —
  be consistent.
- Techniques: **smooth** (small circular or back-and-forth passes), **whip/pendulum** (flick out
  the stroke to make gradients), **pepper/stipple** (low hand speed, dotted texture).
- Lower needle angle (laid back, ~45°) for soft shading; upright for packing.

## 5. Colour packing
- Solid, overlapping passes until the area is evenly saturated; a well-packed area looks
  "wet" and opaque with no skin showing through.
- Stop when it's saturated. **Overworking** — many passes on the same spot — causes excess
  trauma, scabbing and patchy healing.
- Clean between colours; pale colours over dark don't cover, so plan values ahead.

## 6. Skin variables
- **Darker skin tones:** contrast comes from bold black and lines rather than pastel colour,
  which can heal low-contrast; don't go deeper or harder to "make it show". Some people are
  prone to keloids — ask about scarring history.
- **Scar tissue / stretch marks:** absorbs unevenly; test a small area or warn the client
  of patchy results.
- **Sunburnt, broken or irritated skin:** don't tattoo over it — reschedule.

## Gotchas
- Bleeding heavily is often over-working or a client who drank alcohol/took blood thinners;
  ask before starting.
- A swollen, puffy area loses stencil lines and takes ink poorly — finish that region, move on.
- Wiping too hard removes the stencil; dab with a damp paper towel and green soap solution.

## Verify
- [ ] Practice lines on synthetic skin are crisp, even width, no halo at day 0.
- [ ] Healed work (check at 4-6 weeks) shows no patchiness (too shallow) and no blowout (too deep).
- [ ] Shaded areas show a smooth gradient with no dark "rings" from overlapping passes.
- [ ] Colour areas healed solid with no skin peeking through.
- [ ] Client's skin history (keloids, medication, sunburn) was checked before starting.
