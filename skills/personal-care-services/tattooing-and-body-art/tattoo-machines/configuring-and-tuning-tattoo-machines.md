# Tattoo machines — choosing, configuring and tuning for the job

**Goal:** match machine, stroke, needle and voltage to the task (lining, shading, packing)
and recognise when a machine is out of tune before it shows in the skin.

All numbers are typical starting points; always begin from your machine maker's
recommended range and adjust with test lines on practice skin.

## 1. Machine types
- **Coil machines:** electromagnets pull an armature bar down, a contact screw breaks the
  circuit, the front spring returns it. Hard-hitting, adjustable, loud; tuned as a **liner**
  (fast, short stroke, crisp lines) or **shader** (slower, longer, softer hit). Need tuning skill.
- **Rotary machines:** a motor turns a cam/eccentric to drive the needle. Quieter, less
  vibration, consistent; one machine can line and shade if stroke and give are suitable.
- **Pen machines:** rotary in a pen-shaped body taking **cartridges**; most common today.
  Many have adjustable stroke and "give" (needle softness).
- **Wireless:** battery pen machines; convenient, but plan battery swaps for long sessions.

## 2. Stroke and give (rotary/pen)
- **Stroke** = how far the needle travels. Roughly: **short (~2.5-3.0 mm)** for soft shading and
  smooth greys; **medium (~3.5 mm)** all-rounder; **long (~4.0-4.5 mm+)** for lining and packing
  big groupings. (Model-specific — check the spec sheet.)
- **Give** = how much the needle yields on skin contact: more give is softer and gentler for
  shading; less give hits harder for lines and packing.

## 3. Needles and cartridges
Read a code like `1205RL`: **12** = needle diameter (#12 ≈ 0.35 mm), **05** = 5 needles,
**RL** = round liner. Common groupings:
- **RL** round liner — lines; tighter = crisper. **RS** round shader — loose round, small fill.
- **M1/MG** weaved magnum, **RM/CM** curved/round magnum — shading and packing; curved
  magnums are gentler at the edges.
- **F** flat — geometric, bold fills.
- **#10 "bugpin"** = thinner needles for fine detail and soft shading.
- **Taper** (long/short) affects how the point enters; long taper gives a finer point.
- Cartridges with a **membrane** reduce backflow into the machine; check cartridge
  compatibility with your pen.

## 4. Voltage and power
- Voltage controls speed (rotary) or hit (coil). Typical working range **~5-10 V**; coil liners
  are often run higher than shaders; rotary pens usually lower than coils.
- **Raise voltage** for big groupings (more needles = more drag) and lining; **lower** for soft
  shading. If the machine bogs down in the skin, it's underpowered or the stroke is too long for
  that grouping.
- Some power supplies show frequency (Hz) or duty cycle (%) for coil machines: a liner is often
  set to a shorter duty cycle than a shader (exact values vary by maker).
- Check clip-cord or RCA connections first when a machine cuts out; the cord fails more often
  than the machine.

## 5. Coil tuning basics
- Front-spring to armature gap and contact-screw gap set the stroke; the contact point should
  just touch the front spring when the armature is up.
- A **dime gap** (about the thickness of a coin) is a common starting point for a liner;
  shaders typically run a larger gap.
- Listen: a liner sounds fast and even, a shader slower and buzzier. Sparking at the contact
  point or uneven running means re-tuning, a worn spring, or a failing capacitor.

## 6. Needle hang
- Needle tips should protrude about 1-2 mm past the tube or cartridge tip at rest for lining,
  and slightly less for shading. Too much hang = hard to control depth; too little = can't reach.

## Gotchas
- Wrong cartridge-pen compatibility: needles wobble or don't reach full stroke.
- Running a big magnum on a short-stroke soft pen: skips and poor saturation.
- Heat: machines run hot over long sessions; check grip temperature and let coils cool.
- Never autoclave a machine; cover with barriers and wipe per the maker's instructions.

## Verify
- [ ] Machine, stroke and give suit the task (lining vs shading vs packing).
- [ ] Needle code read correctly and grouping matches the area size.
- [ ] Voltage set from the maker's range; practice lines are crisp and the needle doesn't bog.
- [ ] Needle hang checked before starting; no wobble in the cartridge.
- [ ] Cord and connections tested; machine barrier-bagged.
