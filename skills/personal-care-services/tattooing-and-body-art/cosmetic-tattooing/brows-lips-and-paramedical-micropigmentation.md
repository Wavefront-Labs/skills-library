# Cosmetic tattooing — brows, lips, liner and paramedical work that heals natural

**Goal:** place pigment shallowly and precisely on the face or reconstructive sites so the
healed result looks natural, matches the client's undertone, and fades gracefully.

Regulation differs widely (some places require a tattoo licence, some a medical setting for
paramedical work). Check your jurisdiction before offering any of this.

## 1. Consultation and screening
- **Contraindications / ask first:** pregnancy, active skin conditions or acne at the site,
  blood thinners, recent isotretinoin (commonly a 6-12 month wait is quoted — confirm with the
  client's doctor), keloid history, chemotherapy in progress, uncontrolled diabetes.
- **Lips:** history of cold sores (herpes simplex) — lip work can trigger an outbreak; many
  artists ask the client to discuss antiviral prophylaxis with their doctor beforehand.
- **Patch test** where required by local rules or the pigment manufacturer, ideally 24-48 h before.
- Photograph before, during design approval and immediately after; you will need them at touch-up.

## 2. Mapping
- **Brows:** map from facial landmarks — start in line with the side of the nose, arch roughly
  in line with the outer iris edge looking straight ahead, tail on the line from nostril
  through the outer eye corner. Then adjust to bone structure and the client's natural brow;
  symmetry is measured with a ruler/string, but faces aren't symmetrical, so balance by eye.
- Draw with a pencil, let the client see it in a mirror sitting **upright** (not lying down),
  and get approval before any pigment.
- **Lips:** follow the natural vermilion border; overlining more than about 1 mm tends to look
  obvious when healed.

## 3. Colour theory — undertone decides
- Healed pigment shifts cooler as the skin's red/yellow tones and the dermis show through.
  Black/dark-brown brow pigment can heal **blue-grey**, especially in cool or fair skin.
- Choose warmer (more orange-based) brow pigment for cool undertones; use **modifiers**
  (orange, yellow) to correct old grey/blue brows rather than adding more dark pigment.
- Lips: dark or cool lips can heal greyish; neutralise first with a warm corrector session.
- Healed result is typically **30-50% lighter** than what you see at the end of the session
  (rule of thumb). Plan a touch-up 6-8 weeks later.

## 4. Technique notes
- **Microblading:** manual blade makes hair strokes in the **superficial** dermis. Too deep = blurry,
  grey strokes; very oily or thin, mature skin often blurs — powder/ombre machine shading is the
  safer choice there.
- **Powder/ombre brows:** machine stippling, lighter at the front, denser at the tail.
- **Lip blush:** machine shading in layers; light hand, lots of passes would cause swelling and
  patchy healing.
- **Eyeliner/lash line:** shield the eye, client keeps eyes closed and still; never work while the
  client is anxious or moving. Pigment migration into the lid is permanent.
- **Paramedical — areola/nipple and scar camouflage:** work only on healed scars (often
  12+ months after surgery or radiotherapy, per the client's medical team). Scar tissue takes
  pigment unpredictably; test a small area. Skin-tone camouflage over white scars can look
  worse with tanning — warn the client.

## 5. Aftercare
- Keep clean and lightly protected per your protocol (dry-heal or thin ointment — artists
  disagree; follow the pigment maker's advice). No sweating, sauna, swimming, sun or makeup on
  the area for about 1-2 weeks; don't pick flakes.
- Laser, IPL, retinol and chemical peels near the area fade or alter pigment.

## Gotchas
- Iron-oxide pigments can darken under some laser treatments; flag this before the client books
  removal.
- MRI: rare reports of tingling/heat in tattooed areas; clients should tell the radiographer.
- Clients asking for very dark, heavy brows: heals harsher than expected; start lighter.

## Verify
- [ ] Contraindications and medication screened and recorded; cold-sore history asked for lips.
- [ ] Map approved in a mirror with the client sitting upright; before photo taken.
- [ ] Pigment chosen against the client's undertone with healing shift in mind.
- [ ] Touch-up booked 6-8 weeks out; healed photos compared with before photos.
- [ ] For paramedical work, written medical clearance and a healed-scar test patch.
