# Sculpting, flashing and running silicone appliances

**Goal:** a soft, translucent silicone appliance whose edges are thin enough to
disappear on the skin and whose texture matches the performer's pores — made from a
sculpt on their lifecast. Builds on
personal-care-services.sfx-and-prosthetic-makeup.lifecasting-and-moulding.face-lifecast-with-alginate-or-silicone-and-plaster-jacket.
Related clinical craft: handcrafts.medical-dental-appliance-crafting.maxillofacial-prosthetics.silicone-facial-prosthesis-sculpting-moulding-and-colouring.

## 1. Prepare the positive
- Duplicate the original lifecast for sculpting; keep the original untouched.
- Seal gypsum with a sealer compatible with your later mould materials.
- **Clay choice:** use a **sulphur-free** oil-based clay (e.g. Monster Clay, Chavant
  NSP sulphur-free) if silicone will touch the sculpt anywhere — sulphur inhibits the
  cure of platinum silicone (tacky, uncured patches). WED (water clay) is used on
  large sculpts but must stay damp.

## 2. Sculpt
- Block in forms at roughly the final thickness; **keep it as thin as the design
  allows** — thick appliances look rubbery and restrict expression. Areas that move a lot
  (around eyes and mouth) want to be thinnest, often under a few mm.
- **Respect anatomy:** new forms should sit over the real bone and muscle structure so
  they move correctly.
- **Texture:** match the performer's skin — use texture stamps (silicone pads cast
  from real skin or fruit), crumpled plastic film over the clay, fine brushes and
  solvent (99% isopropyl or a clay-thinner per clay type) to smooth. Pores are smaller
  around the eyes and larger on the nose.
- **Edges:** the sculpt edge should fade to nothing — you're aiming for a feather
  edge so thin it's translucent once run. Sculpt slightly beyond where you want the
  appliance to end.

## 3. Flashing and the mould
- Set a **flashing/overflow line**: a cutting edge ~3–6 mm outside the sculpt with a
  shallow trough around it so excess silicone escapes as a paper-thin flash that tears
  away. Without a cutting edge, edges come out thick.
- Add **keys** (registration bumps) and pour spouts/overflow channels as needed.
- Build the **front (negative) mould** over the sculpt in high-strength gypsum or
  epoxy (epoxy for repeated runs, more expensive). The original positive becomes the
  core; the two parts close to leave a gap the shape of the appliance.
- Clean every trace of clay from both halves; any residue shows on every appliance.

## 4. Run the silicone
- **Encapsulate:** coat both mould halves with a thin cap-plastic (vinyl, e.g. Baldiez
  type) layer first; this skin lets the edges be dissolved and blended with acetone
  later and lets adhesive grip the silicone.
- Mix a soft platinum gel silicone (Plat-Gel type) at the maker's ratio. **Deadener**
  makes it softer and more flesh-like; too much makes it weak and sticky.
- **Intrinsic colour:** tint with silicone pigments to the performer's base, slightly
  lighter/more translucent than skin; flocking adds opacity.
- Degas or pour carefully; fill the negative, close the mould, clamp evenly; excess must
  flow out the flashing.
- Cure per data sheet (gentle heat often accelerates platinum silicones — check).
- Demould slowly; powder lightly; trim flash with sharp scissors, never cutting the
  feather edge itself.

## Gotchas
- **Cure inhibition**: latex gloves, sulphur clay, some tapes and tin silicones inhibit
  platinum silicone. Wear nitrile and test unknown materials.
- Foam latex is an alternative (light, cheap, opaque, needs oven-baking and sealing); not
  covered in depth here.
- Specific product names and ratios vary by supplier — this skill is unverified against
  any single product line; follow the technical data sheet.

## Verify
- [ ] Sulphur-free clay used; mould cleaned with no clay residue.
- [ ] Cutting edge/flashing present; appliance edges translucent and tear-thin.
- [ ] Silicone fully cured, not tacky; colour sits close to skin tone.
- [ ] Appliance fits the lifecast without gaps at the edges.
