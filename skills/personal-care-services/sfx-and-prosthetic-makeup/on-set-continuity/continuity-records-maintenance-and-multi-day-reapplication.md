# Continuity records, maintenance and multi-day reapplication

**Goal:** an injury, ageing or creature effect that looks the same in every take and
on every day it appears — even though scenes are shot out of order and the effect is
rebuilt from scratch each morning. Continuity errors are the most visible makeup
mistakes on screen and the ones editors cannot always cut around.

## 1. Script breakdown (before the shoot)
- Break the script into **story days** (in-story timeline) per character, and list
  every scene number per story day.
- For each injury or effect, write its **progression**: e.g. "cut on left cheek: Sc 12
  fresh, bleeding; Sc 14 same day, dried blood; Sc 30 three days later, scabbed, bruise
  yellowing". Check with the director and the **script supervisor**, who keeps the
  official continuity.
- Map story days against the **shooting schedule** — note days where the same
  character jumps between progression stages and allow time for changes.

## 2. Continuity records (the "bible")
For each character and look, on every day it's applied:
- **Photos**: front, both profiles, three-quarter, and close-ups of each effect with a
  **scale reference** (ruler or coin) and a card naming character, scene and story day.
  Consistent, neutral light; same distance; no filters. Back of head and hands too.
- **Notes**: products, shades, brushes, adhesive, which appliance number (prosthetics
  degrade — use the same batch or a matched spare), where blood ran and in which
  direction, how much was used.
- Store per scene, backed up off the phone, organised so any team member can find
  "Sc 30, Character A, cut" in seconds. Production rules may restrict personal-device
  photos for confidentiality — follow them.

## 3. On set: last looks and maintenance
- **Set bag** with you at all times: powder, blotting papers, sponges, adhesive and
  remover, sealer, blood (each type used), the effect's paints, scissors, cotton buds,
  tissues, a mirror and the continuity photos.
- **Last looks** right before "action": check against the photo — blood position, sweat,
  shine, lifting edges. Step out of shot quickly when called.
- Between takes: fix lifting edges, re-seal, blot sweat, refresh blood to match the
  **end of the previous take** if the scene continues (or reset to the start position
  for a new take — ask the script supervisor which).
- Watch the monitor where possible; flag continuity problems to the 1st AD quietly and
  quickly.

## 4. Multi-day reapplication
- Rebuild from the photos, not memory: lay them next to the mirror and work
  feature by feature.
- **Templates and stencils** for repeatable shapes (a scar outline traced on acetate
  film, a tattoo transfer, a bruise stencil) make day-to-day matching far easier.
- Ageing or progression: plan the change between stages ("day 3 bruise" uses the same
  outline with yellow added), so the shape stays continuous while colour changes.
- Skin care between days: performers with daily adhesive need gentle removal and
  repair time; irritated skin can't hold the same effect.

## 5. Set etiquette (procedure)
- Know the call sheet: your performers, scenes and timings; arrive before call.
- Coordinate with hair, costume and the AD on timings; don't hold up the set without
  telling the AD why and how long.
- Keep chemicals labelled and capped; sharps and solvent rags disposed of properly.

## Gotchas
- Mirror images: photos from the performer's left look like their right in a mirror —
  label sides ("performer's left").
- Different lighting on the photos makes colours unreliable; shoot under consistent light.
- Practices above reflect typical film/TV procedure; department hierarchies and
  responsibilities vary by production and country.

## Verify
- [ ] Story-day breakdown done and agreed with the script supervisor.
- [ ] Every look photographed (all angles + scale + slate card) and noted each day.
- [ ] Last looks done before each take; set bag stocked for every effect in use.
- [ ] Reapplied effect matches the previous day's photos side by side.
