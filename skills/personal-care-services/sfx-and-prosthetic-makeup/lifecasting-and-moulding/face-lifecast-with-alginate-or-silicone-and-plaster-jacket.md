# Face lifecast with alginate or silicone and a plaster jacket

**Goal:** an accurate copy of the performer's face in stone, to sculpt prosthetics on —
taken with the performer safe, breathing comfortably and in control throughout.
A lifecast is the one SFX job where you are covering someone's face; plan it like it.

## 1. Planning and the performer
- Ask about claustrophobia, panic attacks, asthma, allergies (alginate is generally
  low-risk; check the silicone's skin-safe certification), skin conditions, and
  piercings/contacts (remove). Explain every step in advance and **agree a hand
  signal** (e.g. thumbs down = stop now) and that you will cut them out immediately
  if they use it. Never cast an unwilling or rushed performer.
- **Two people minimum**: one applying, one watching the performer's breathing and
  comfort the whole time. Have blunt-ended scissors on the table.
- **Neutral expression**, seated upright (head falls back when lying flat, distorting
  the jaw and neck). Practise "relaxed, slightly closed, not clenched".

## 2. Prep
- **Bald cap** or cling film/cap over hair; hairline and any long hair fully covered.
- **Facial hair, eyebrows, lashes:** coat with a thick layer of petroleum-based release
  or a water-based release recommended for the material; beards may need shaving or a
  thick barrier — alginate and silicone grab hair and pull.
- Draw cut lines on the cap/neck with a skin marker if you plan a multi-piece mould.
- Mark a **neckline/shoulder boundary** with cling film or a plastic sheet.

## 3. Applying the mould material
**Alginate (cheap, fast, single use):**
- Mix to the maker's ratio, cool water slows set, warm speeds it; working time is often
  only ~3–6 minutes. Mix lump-free, apply from the top of the head down, pressing into
  the skin to remove bubbles, **eyes closed and covered thinly last**.
- **Nostrils: always clear.** Work around them and clean the edge with a finger; never
  use straws pushed into the nose. Talk to the performer continually.
- Lay in burlap or cotton strips into the wet alginate to help the jacket lock on.

**Skin-safe platinum silicone (more expensive, detailed, reusable for a few casts):**
- Thin first coat for detail, thicker second, with **key bumps** (silicone blobs) so the
  jacket registers. Use the skin-safe grade only; check cure time — slower than alginate.

## 4. Jacket
- **Plaster bandages** dipped in warm water, 3–4 layers, overlapping over the
  alginate/silicone, keeping the nostrils and any breathing hole clear. Front half
  first; for full heads, a two-part jacket with a seam line along the side.
- Plaster gets warm as it sets — warn the performer. Wait until it's firm (typically
  10–15 min).

## 5. Demould
- Ask the performer to **wiggle their face** (scrunch, blink behind, move jaw) while
  you lift the jacket+mould forward off the face, with them leaning forward into your
  hands. Never lever it off the nose or chin.
- Clean the performer's face and hairline immediately; check skin and eyes.

## 6. Pour the positive
- Alginate shrinks and distorts as it dries — **pour within ~1 hour** of demoulding
  (silicone is stable). Patch any tears, support the mould in the jacket, nostrils
  plugged with clay.
- Splash coat of gypsum (Ultracal-30 or similar high-strength stone for durable
  positives; plaster of Paris for quick tests), working into detail, then build up.
  Allow full cure per maker before demoulding (often several hours).

## Gotchas
- **Never use plaster bandage directly on skin over the face** without a mould layer;
  never pour plaster onto skin — it heats while curing and has caused serious burns on
  body casts.
- A performer who starts to panic: stop, cut the nostril area clear or remove the
  mould. A ruined cast is fine.
- Curing times quoted here are typical; always follow the product data sheet.

## Verify
- [ ] Safety plan briefed: signal agreed, assistant watching, scissors ready.
- [ ] Hair, brows and lashes protected; nostrils clear throughout.
- [ ] Mould demoulded without pulling skin or hair; performer checked and comfortable.
- [ ] Positive poured promptly; detail (pores, eyelid creases) captured without bubbles.
