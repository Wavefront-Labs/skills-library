# Bald-cap application and laid-on facial hair

**Goal:** a bald head whose edges vanish against the forehead and neck, and facial hair
that grows convincingly out of the skin — two core hair-effects jobs.

## Part A — Bald cap
### Choosing the cap
- **Vinyl (cap-plastic, Glatzan-type)**: thin, edges dissolve with acetone so they
  blend best; standard for camera.
- **Latex**: cheap and strong, but thicker edges that must be filled; not for latex
  allergies.
- **Silicone**: most realistic movement, expensive, needs silicone adhesives.
Long or thick hair makes a lumpy cap — **short hair under a cap is far easier**.

### Flatten the hair
1. Wet the hair and slick it flat with a strong gel or hair-laying product; for longer
   hair, pin-curl it flat or wrap it in a wig cap, then gel the cap down.
2. Hairline hair: comb back and flatten firmly; no tufts poking at the edges.
3. Dry fully (cool dryer) — damp hair under the cap makes it lift and wrinkle.

### Fit and glue
1. Pull the cap on from the **front hairline to the back**, the front edge down over the
   forehead; mark where the ears are and **cut slits for the ears**, trimming carefully
   around them.
2. Glue the **front edge first**: lift the edge, apply adhesive (Pros-Aide-type or
   spirit gum for vinyl/latex) to skin and cap, press down. Then glue at the temples,
   behind the ears and finally the nape, pulling the cap snug (not tight) as you go.
3. Trim the cap to ~1–2 cm beyond the glue line.
4. **Edge blending:**
   - **Vinyl:** touch acetone on a cotton bud to the thin edge so it melts onto the
     skin (keep away from eyes; fan the fumes).
   - **Latex:** stipple liquid latex or a filler over the edge to bridge the step.
5. Colour: a rubber-mask grease or alcohol-activated base to match skin; mottle and add
   slight redness/veins on the scalp. A bald scalp is often a little lighter or pinker
   than the face — match a real reference. Powder.

## Part B — Laid-on facial hair
### Choose the hair
- **Crepe hair** (wool, comes braided): cheap, good for stage and background; straighten
  by steaming or ironing before use.
- **Yak hair / human hair**: finer and more realistic for camera; mix 2–3 shades —
  real beards have several colours (often greyer at the chin, redder in the moustache).

### Lay it in stages
1. Prep skin (clean, degrease; shave the area if real stubble fights the direction).
2. Apply a thin coat of spirit gum or hair-laying adhesive to a **small area**, let it
   go tacky.
3. Work **from the bottom up**: under the jaw and chin first, then up the cheeks and
   jawline, moustache last. Each layer overlaps the one below, like shingles.
4. Cut small clumps to length, **trim the base end straight**, and press the base into
   the adhesive with scissors or a damp towel, angled in the real growth direction
   (downward and out on the jaw, out and down from the philtrum on the moustache).
5. **Sparse at the edges**, denser in the middle; real beards thin toward the cheeks
   and neck.
6. Trim and shape the whole beard at the end with scissors; a light spray of hairspray
   or setting product holds it.
- Stubble: chop hair very finely and press onto a thin adhesive layer or stubble paste.

### Ready-made pieces
**Lace-backed beards/moustaches** (knotted hair on fine lace) are faster for multi-day
shoots: trim the lace close, glue edges, check the lace doesn't shine on camera.
Punched hair in appliances is a separate, slower craft.

## Gotchas
- Glue that's still wet when hair goes on = hair sliding down. Wait for tack.
- Cap wrinkles at the nape when the performer looks down — glue the nape with the head
  tilted forward.
- Removal: matched remover worked under the edges; never rip a cap or beard off.

## Verify
- [ ] Hair flat and dry under the cap; cap edges invisible on the monitor.
- [ ] Cap colour matched to face and neck under set light.
- [ ] Facial hair laid bottom-up in growth direction, multi-tone, sparse at edges.
- [ ] Everything removed with remover; skin cleansed and checked.
