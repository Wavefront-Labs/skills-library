# Gluing, blending, painting and removing appliances

**Goal:** an appliance that moves with the performer's face, has invisible edges on
camera, holds through a shooting day, and comes off at wrap without hurting the skin.
Assumes encapsulated silicone appliances (see
personal-care-services.sfx-and-prosthetic-makeup.prosthetic-sculpting-and-fabrication.sculpting-flashing-and-running-silicone-appliances);
foam latex follows the same order with different blending materials.

## 1. Before the day
- **Patch test** every adhesive, remover, paint and sealer on the performer (inner arm
  or behind the ear) at least 24–48 h before the shoot. Record products and results.
- **Test-fit** the appliance on the performer; trim if needed; mark the outline
  lightly.

## 2. Skin prep
- Clean with gentle cleanser, then **99% isopropyl alcohol** (or a skin prep the
  adhesive maker recommends) to degrease — oil is the No. 1 reason appliances lift.
  Avoid moisturiser under the glue area.
- Facial hair: shave the area or the edge will catch; flatten brows under a
  brow-blocking layer if covered.
- Barrier spray/film on sensitive skin if the adhesive allows.

## 3. Position and glue
- Prep the appliance's back: clean with alcohol; with encapsulated pieces, keep the
  cap-plastic edge intact.
- **Glue from the centre outward.** Common adhesives: water-based acrylic (Pros-Aide
  type) — apply to both surfaces, let it go tacky-clear, press; or **silicone
  medical adhesive** (Telesis type) for stronger, sweat-resistant hold. Do not glue the
  edges yet.
- Ask the performer to make expressions; adjust centre adhesion so the piece moves
  naturally.
- **Lift the edges** last: tack them with a fine brush, press with a powder puff or
  a cotton bud, working out any wrinkles toward the edge.

## 4. Blend the edges
- **Encapsulated silicone:** dissolve the thin cap-plastic edge into the skin with a
  tiny amount of acetone (or the maker's edge melter) on a cotton bud — sparingly, and
  never near eyes.
- **Fill gaps/steps** with a thin filler: Pros-Aide cream, "bondo" (Pros-Aide mixed with
  Cab-O-Sil), or a silicone edge filler; feather it onto the skin and stipple texture
  into it with a sponge while it's wet.
- Seal the area so paint sits evenly (sealer per your paint system).

## 5. Paint
- **Alcohol-activated palettes** (Skin Illustrator / PAX-type colours) are the standard:
  thin, transparent, sweat-resistant. Activate with 99% IPA; apply in layers.
- Work like real skin: **a translucent base**, then **mottling** (stipple sponge in
  warm red, yellow and blue-grey to break up flat colour), freckles and capillaries
  with a fine brush, then shadows and highlights. Paint **across the edge** onto skin so
  the colour carries over.
- Check on camera monitor or with a phone under set light — flat, opaque colour reads as
  "rubber" immediately.
- Powder lightly; translucent powder only where shine is wrong.

## 6. Removal (budget time for it)
- Never peel. Work the adhesive's matched remover (isopropyl myristate-based or the
  maker's silicone remover) under the edge with a soft brush, lift gradually, more
  remover as you go.
- Remove all adhesive residue, then cleanse, tone and moisturise. Check for redness,
  rashes, broken skin; record it. Repeated daily application needs a skin-care plan
  and rest days if irritation builds.

## Gotchas
- Acetone and solvent fumes near eyes: protect with a tissue and work slowly.
- Sweat breaks the seal: under hot lights or action scenes use silicone adhesive and
  refresh edges between takes.
- Product names are common examples, not endorsements; compatibility of a given
  adhesive with a given appliance is unconfirmed until you test it.

## Verify
- [ ] Every product patch-tested on this performer and logged.
- [ ] Appliance moves with expressions; no lifting edges after 15 minutes of talking.
- [ ] Edges invisible on the camera monitor under set lighting.
- [ ] Removal left skin intact, clean, and checked.
