# Assisted shaving and grooming — shaving, hair and nails for someone else

**Goal:** help a person look and feel like themselves — shaved (or not) as they choose,
hair as they like it, nails short and smooth — without cuts or infection.

Always follow the person's preferences and care plan. Some tasks (e.g. cutting nails for
people with diabetes) may be outside your role — check your employer's policy.

## 1. Preferences first
- Ask how they like it: clean-shaven, a beard or moustache kept a certain way, the side
  their hair is parted, nail length, products they use. Look at photos or ask family if the
  person can't tell you. Religious and cultural reasons may mean someone **should not** be
  shaved — never assume.
- Encourage them to do as much as they can; hand-over-hand guidance with their own razor or brush.

## 2. Shaving
**Choose the method:**
- **Electric shaver** is the default for assisted shaving, and strongly preferred for people
  on **anticoagulants** (blood thinners such as warfarin, apixaban), with low platelets, or with
  skin conditions — much lower cut risk. Check the care plan; some services require it.
- **Wet shave** with a razor only if trained, the person prefers it and the care plan allows.
- Each person has their **own** shaver or razor — never share (bloodborne infection risk).

**Electric:**
1. Clean, dry skin (some shavers work wet — check).
2. Stretch the skin gently with your free hand; move against the direction of hair growth with
   foil shavers (rotary heads in small circles).
3. Clean the head afterwards per the maker's instructions; charge it.

**Wet shave:**
1. Seat the person upright, towel round the shoulders, good light.
2. Soften the beard with a warm damp flannel for a minute, then apply shaving foam/gel.
3. Hold the skin taut, short strokes **with the grain** (direction of hair growth) first; going
   against the grain gives a closer shave but more irritation and cuts.
4. Rinse the blade often; take extra care around the jawline, chin, Adam's apple and under the nose.
5. Rinse, pat dry, apply an unscented moisturiser if they like.
6. Dispose of disposable blades into a sharps container.
**If you cut them:** apply gentle pressure with clean gauze; on anticoagulants, it may take
longer — report it if it doesn't stop within about 10 minutes.

## 3. Hair tidying
- Brush or comb from the ends upward to work out tangles, holding the hair above the knot so
  you don't pull on the scalp; a detangling spray helps.
- Style the way they like it; keep their hairbrush clean and personal.
- Check the scalp for flaking, sores, lice or pressure marks from lying (back of head) and report.

## 4. Basic nail care
- **Fingernails:** soak briefly in warm water to soften, clean underneath gently with a nail brush,
  **file** with an emery board in one direction to round off sharp edges. Many services prefer
  filing to clipping for carers.
- **Do not cut** the nails of someone with diabetes, poor circulation, very thick or fungal nails,
  or on anticoagulants unless trained and permitted — refer to a podiatrist/chiropodist. A small
  cut can turn into a serious foot ulcer.
- Toenails: straight across, not down the sides (ingrowing nails) — usually podiatry's job.
- Moisturise hands and feet, but **not between the toes** (moisture there encourages fungal infection).

## Gotchas
- Dentures out while shaving the mouth area changes the face shape — shave with them in if possible.
- Hearing aids and glasses off before shaving; replace afterwards.
- Shaving dry with a blade = rashes and cuts.
- Sharing clippers between residents: never.

## Verify
- [ ] The person's grooming preferences (and any cultural/religious requirements) are known and followed.
- [ ] Electric shaver used for anyone on blood thinners; equipment is theirs alone.
- [ ] Skin intact after shaving; any cuts managed and reported if prolonged.
- [ ] Hair styled as they like; scalp checked.
- [ ] Nails filed smooth; high-risk feet and nails referred to podiatry rather than cut.
