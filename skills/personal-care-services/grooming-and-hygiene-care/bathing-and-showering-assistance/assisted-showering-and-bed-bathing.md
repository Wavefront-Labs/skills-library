# Bathing and showering assistance — safe, warm, private and observant

**Goal:** help a person get clean — in bed or in the shower — without scalds, falls,
chills or embarrassment, and notice skin changes while you're there.

This builds on the broader human-services.aged-care.personal-care-assistance.dignified-personal-care-routine
skill; here is the task-level method. Always follow the person's care plan and your
employer's policies (some moves need two carers or equipment).

## 1. Before you start
- **Ask and explain:** "Would you like a shower now or later?" Consent is for this time, not
  forever. Offer choices: shower, bath, bed wash; their own products; male/female carer if possible.
- Check the care plan: mobility aids, transfers, skin issues, catheter or dressings to protect.
- Room warm (no draughts), everything within reach: towels, clean clothes, products, gloves,
  laundry bag. You should **never** have to leave the person alone mid-wash.
- Hand hygiene; gloves for contact with body fluids, broken skin and intimate areas.

## 2. Water temperature — scald prevention
- Test with a thermometer: commonly **around 37-40 °C** for showers and baths; many services set
  thermostatic valves to cap hot water at about **43-44 °C** to prevent scalds.
- Then let the person feel it with their hand too — but remember people with diabetes,
  stroke or dementia may not sense heat accurately; the thermometer wins.
- Run cold first, then add hot; re-check if the water has been running a while.

## 3. Assisted shower
- Shower chair or commode with brakes on; grab rails; non-slip mat; floor dry around the entry.
- Let the person do as much as they can (washing face, arms) — this keeps skills and dignity.
- Keep them covered with a towel over the lap or shoulders where not being washed.
- Wash **cleanest to dirtiest**: face (water only or as they prefer), arms, chest, abdomen,
  legs, feet, back, then genital and anal areas last, front to back.
- Dry thoroughly, especially **between toes, skin folds, under breasts and the groin** — damp
  folds lead to fungal infection and breakdown.

## 4. Bed bath
1. Bed at your working height (brakes on), side rail up on the far side if used; lower it again after.
2. Two basins or bags if your service uses them: one for body, a fresh change of water (and
   cloths) for intimate areas; or use no-rinse wash wipes per policy.
3. Expose only the part you're washing; keep the rest under a towel or blanket.
4. Same order: face, arms, chest, abdomen, legs, then turn (with correct technique/second carer)
   for back and buttocks, then intimate care with fresh water and cloths.
5. Change water whenever cool or soapy.

## 5. Look at the skin while you wash
Check pressure areas — heels, sacrum/tailbone, hips, shoulder blades, elbows, back of the head
— and skin folds for:
- redness that **doesn't fade** after pressure is relieved (possible pressure injury);
- breaks, blisters, bruises, rashes, swelling, moisture damage;
- any unexplained marks.
Report and record per your service's procedure; unexplained bruising may be a safeguarding concern.

## Gotchas
- Leaving someone "for a second" to get a towel is when falls happen.
- Bath oils make tubs slippery; avoid or use with great care.
- Shivering after a shower: dry and dress the upper body first while washing the lower body.
- Hearing aids, glasses and dentures: remove safely before, replace after.

- [ ] Person never left alone wet; equipment brakes on; floor dry.
