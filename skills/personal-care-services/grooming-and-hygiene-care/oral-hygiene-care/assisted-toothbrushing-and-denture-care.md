# Oral hygiene care — brushing for someone else, and denture care

**Goal:** keep the mouth of a person who needs help clean and comfortable, twice a day,
with low choking risk — because poor mouth care is linked to pain, poor eating and
aspiration pneumonia.

Follow the person's care plan and any speech-and-language therapist advice on swallowing.

## 1. Set up
- Ask and explain; offer to help rather than take over. Many people can brush with a
  thicker-handled or electric brush if you set it up.
- **Position:** person sitting upright (or as upright as possible, head slightly forward if in
  bed) — never brush someone lying flat; fluid and debris can be inhaled.
- You: seated or standing slightly to the side or behind, so you can see and support the head.
  Gloves, good light (a torch), soft small-headed brush, fluoride toothpaste, a cup, a bowl,
  towel or tissue.

## 2. Brushing someone's natural teeth
- **Pea-sized** smear of fluoride toothpaste — adult toothpaste commonly **1350-1500 ppm**
  fluoride, or higher-strength products a dentist prescribes (e.g. 5000 ppm).
- Soft brush at about **45° to the gumline**, small circular or short back-and-forth
  movements, a couple of teeth at a time: outer surfaces, inner surfaces, chewing surfaces.
  About **2 minutes** in total.
- Brush gently along the gums and, if tolerated, the tongue.
- **Spit, don't rinse:** have them spit out excess but not rinse with water, so fluoride stays on
  the teeth. (If they can't spit, wipe excess out with a gauze or use a low-foam toothpaste —
  ask the dental/SLT team.)
- Interdental brushes once a day if the person had them or the dentist recommends.

## 3. People who can't swallow safely or keep their mouth open
- Use a **low-foam (SLS-free) toothpaste** and minimal water; a suction toothbrush where
  available and trained.
- Don't put fingers between someone's teeth — they may bite involuntarily. Use a mouth prop
  or a second brush to hold the cheek if trained.
- For dementia or resistance: try a calm approach, show the brush, hand-under-hand guidance,
  a different time of day; stop and retry later rather than forcing.
- Moisten dry lips and mouth with water-based products; avoid lemon-glycerine swabs (they
  dry the mouth and can harm teeth — commonly advised against in care guidance).

## 4. Denture care
- **Remove** dentures for cleaning (lower first, then upper; reverse to insert). Partial dentures
  unclip from clasps — don't pull on the clasps.
- **Clean over a bowl of water or a towel** so a dropped denture doesn't break.
- Brush all surfaces with a denture brush and **non-abrasive** denture cleaner or plain soap
  — regular toothpaste is abrasive and scratches acrylic.
- Soak per the product instructions (not in hot water — it can warp acrylic; metal partials
  avoid bleach-based soaks).
- **Out overnight** in a labelled pot of water or soak unless the care plan says otherwise; this
  rests the gums and reduces thrush.
- Clean the gums, palate and tongue with a soft brush while dentures are out.
- **Label** dentures (dental or denture-marking kits) — lost dentures in care settings are common.

## 5. Check and report
Look for: red/swollen or bleeding gums, white patches (possible thrush), ulcers that don't
heal within ~2-3 weeks, broken teeth, loose or ill-fitting dentures, pain when eating, bad breath
that persists. Report per your service; ulcers that don't heal need a dentist or doctor.

## Verify
- [ ] Person sitting upright; consent and preferred help agreed.
- [ ] Teeth brushed 2 min with a pea-sized fluoride toothpaste; spit, don't rinse.
- [ ] Dentures cleaned over water with non-abrasive cleaner; out overnight in a labelled pot.
- [ ] Gums, tongue and palate checked; anything new reported.
- [ ] Swallowing precautions from the care plan followed.
