# Skin and hair hygiene routines — washing hair, moisturising and pressure-aware skin care

**Goal:** keep another person's hair clean and comfortable and their skin moisturised and
intact, as a daily routine that catches early pressure damage.

Follow the person's care plan and repositioning schedule; report concerns to the nurse or
manager per your service.

## 1. Hair washing
**Ask first:** how often they wash it (daily for some, weekly or less for others — many people
with **Afro-textured or coily hair** wash less often and need conditioner and oils rather than
frequent shampoo), their products and how they style it. Cultural head coverings and hair
practices must be respected.

**At a sink or in the shower:** seated, towel round the shoulders, head tipped *forward* over a
basin or back with a neck support; protect eyes with a dry flannel. Warm water tested with a
thermometer (~37-40 °C). Shampoo with fingertips (not nails), rinse well, condition if used.

**In bed:**
- **Inflatable hair-washing basin** with a drainage tube to a bucket, or a **rinse-free shampoo
  cap** (warm in the microwave per instructions; test temperature) — practical, low effort.
- Protect the bed with towels/waterproof sheet; position the person flat or slightly raised
  per the care plan; support the neck.
- Dry hair thoroughly — damp hair against a pillow is uncomfortable and chilling.

**Scalp:** check for dryness, flaking, sores, lice, and pressure marks on the back of the head in
people who lie a lot. Report sores.

## 2. Moisturising routine
- Older skin is thinner and drier; **emollients** reduce itching and splitting.
- Apply **after washing**, while the skin is slightly damp, in the **direction of hair growth**
  (downwards on limbs) to avoid blocking follicles (folliculitis). Gentle smoothing, never rubbing hard.
- Use the person's prescribed or chosen emollient; **don't share pots** between people (use a
  pump or a clean spatula).
- **Fire risk:** paraffin-based emollients soak into clothing and bedding and make them burn
  much more easily — warn smokers and anyone near open flames; launder fabrics regularly
  (washing reduces but may not remove residue).
- Not between the toes; dry skin folds carefully.

## 3. Pressure-area awareness
- Pressure injuries develop over **bony points**: sacrum/tailbone, heels, hips, ischial (sitting)
  bones, elbows, shoulder blades, ears and back of the head — and under devices (oxygen tubing,
  splints, catheter tubes).
- Look at these every time you wash or change someone. **Blanching test:** press the red area
  lightly with a finger; if it doesn't go pale and return to red, the redness doesn't blanch —
  report it as possible early pressure damage. On darker skin, redness may not show; look for
  **purplish/bluish discolouration**, and feel for warmth, firmness or bogginess compared to
  nearby skin.
- Support the repositioning schedule in the care plan; avoid dragging the person across sheets
  (shear); use slide sheets.
- Keep skin clean and dry; manage moisture from sweat and incontinence (see the
  continence-and-toileting-care skill).
- Don't massage reddened bony areas — this is old practice and can increase damage.

## 4. Fragile skin and skin tears
- Short nails and no rings/watches with sharp edges for carers; pad bed rails and wheelchair parts.
- Support limbs from underneath rather than gripping.
- If a skin tear happens: control bleeding, gently clean with saline or water, carefully lay the
  skin flap back into place if possible, and report for a dressing — don't use adhesive tape on
  fragile skin.

## Verify
- [ ] Hair washed in the person's preferred way and frequency, dried fully, scalp checked.
- [ ] Emollient applied downward on damp skin, from the person's own pot or pump; fire risk explained.
- [ ] All pressure areas looked at; non-blanching redness or dark-skin changes reported.
- [ ] Repositioning done without dragging; slide sheet used.
- [ ] Any skin tear cleaned, flap repositioned, reported, no tape on the skin.
