# Sauna and steam practice — running heat sessions, löyly and aufguss

**Goal:** run safe, enjoyable heat-bathing sessions: right temperature and humidity, good
pacing between heat and cooling, and rituals (löyly, aufguss, banya venik) done well.

Status is draft: temperatures are common ranges, not standards; follow your venue's
operating manual and health-and-safety rules.

## 1. Know your room
- **Finnish sauna:** typically **70-100 °C**, low base humidity; bathers raise humidity by
  throwing water on stones (**löyly**). Higher benches are much hotter than lower ones.
- **Banya (Russian):** often lower air temperature than a Finnish sauna with heavier, wetter
  steam, and the **venik** (birch/oak whisk) ritual.
- **Steam room:** ~40-50 °C at near 100% humidity; feels hotter than the thermometer.
- **Infrared cabin:** heats the body directly at lower air temperature (often 45-60 °C).
- Check thermometers and hygrometers at bench height before opening; heater stones must be
  free of cracks and arranged loosely so steam rises well.

## 2. Screening and briefing
Ask or post signage. People should talk to a doctor first, or avoid heat, with: unstable
heart conditions, recent heart attack, uncontrolled blood pressure, pregnancy, fever or
acute illness, recent alcohol, certain medications (some blood-pressure drugs, diuretics),
and those prone to fainting. Children need closer supervision and shorter sessions.
**Never** let anyone use a sauna after drinking alcohol — heat plus alcohol is a known risk for
collapse and arrhythmias.

## 3. Pacing a session
- A common rhythm: **8-15 min heat → cool down (cold shower, plunge or fresh air) → rest
  10-20 min**, repeated 2-3 times. Listen to your body; leave earlier if dizzy or uncomfortable.
- Shower before entering, sit on a towel, and drink water between rounds.
- Cool gradually if unused to cold (see cold-exposure-and-contrast skill).

## 4. Löyly
- Ladle water onto hot stones in small amounts — about a ladle at a time — and **ask the room**
  before throwing. Too much at once produces a scalding blast near the top bench.
- Wet stones briefly; good stones hiss and the steam reaches the top bench within a few
  seconds. If water pools on the stones, they aren't hot enough or the stones need re-stacking.

## 5. Aufguss (attended infusion)
- The **saunameister** pours water mixed with sauna-safe aromatic concentrates over the stones
  in stages, then uses a towel to **distribute and move heat** — waving towards each bather in
  turn. Typical aufguss: 10-15 min, 3 rounds of pouring and towel work, with a pause between.
- Announce it in advance: duration, scents, that people may leave at any time, that doors stay closed.
- Use only products designed for sauna infusion, diluted per label; undiluted essential oils
  can ignite or irritate skin and airways.
- Watch the guests the whole time; a good aufguss has nobody fainting.

## 6. Banya and venik
- Soak a dried birch or oak venik in warm water (some soak it briefly in hot), shake it over the
  stones to release steam, then use it on the lying bather: light patting and fanning to bring
  hot air, pressing to the skin, and gentle whipping. The venik moves heat — it isn't for hitting hard.
- Work from feet towards the back and shoulders; check in with the bather.

## Gotchas
- Heater stones degrade: crumbling stones cut steam quality and can block airflow; re-stack yearly-ish.
- Metal jewellery and glasses get hot; warn guests.
- Slip hazards: wet floors and benches need drainage and anti-slip surfaces.
- Electrical heaters need the guard rail intact; nothing (towels, clothes) drying on them — fire risk.

## Verify
- [ ] Room temperature and humidity are in range at bench height.
- [ ] Guests have been briefed on contraindications and no alcohol.
- [ ] Sessions alternate heat, cooling and rest, with water available.
- [ ] Aufguss products are sauna-rated and diluted per label; the room is supervised.
- [ ] Heater guard intact; nothing on the heater; floors not slippery.
