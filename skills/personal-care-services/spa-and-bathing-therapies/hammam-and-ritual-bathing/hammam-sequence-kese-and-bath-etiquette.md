# Hammam and ritual bathing — the attended wash, kese and bath etiquette

**Goal:** guide a guest through a hammam sequence (warm-up, kese scrub, foam wash, rest)
safely and with dignity, and brief guests on the etiquette of onsen and sento bathing.

Traditions vary by country, town and family; this is a common modern-spa sequence, not the
only correct one.

## 1. The space and the kit
- Hot room (traditionally 40-50 °C, very humid), a warm central platform (the Turkish
  **göbek taşı**), basins with hot and cold taps, bowls for pouring.
- **Peştemal** (flat-woven cotton wrap) for modesty; slippers or wooden clogs for the wet floor.
- **Kese** (coarse mitt — Turkish *kese*, Moroccan *kessa*); **black soap** (Moroccan *savon
  noir/beldi*, an olive-based soft soap); olive-oil soap and a cotton bag or pillowcase for foam;
  optional **rhassoul** (ghassoul) clay for hair and body.

## 2. The attended sequence
1. **Welcome and screen:** ask about heart conditions, blood pressure, pregnancy, skin
   conditions, recent sunburn, waxing or shaving (kese on freshly shaved or waxed skin stings
   and can abrade), and allergies to oils or clays. Explain each step and what the guest will
   wear; offer a same-gender attendant where the tradition or guest expects it.
2. **Warm and soften (10-20 min):** guest rests on the warm platform; pour warm water
   regularly. With black soap, apply it now and leave it 5-10 min to soften the skin.
3. **Rinse**, then **kese scrub:** firm, long strokes in one direction along the limbs and back,
   on wet skin. Dead skin rolls off in grey strips — the sign the skin was softened enough.
   Light pressure on the neck, inner arms, chest and face; skip the face entirely with a coarse mitt.
   Check pressure with the guest; red is fine, raw or pinpoint bleeding is too much.
4. **Rinse well.**
5. **Foam wash (Turkish köpük):** dip the cotton bag in soapy water, blow or swing air into it,
   then squeeze clouds of foam over the guest and give a light soapy massage. Rinse.
6. Optional rhassoul clay mask for skin or hair; optional hair wash.
7. **Cool rinse and rest** in a cooler room with water or tea; keep the guest warm and seated
   for a while — they may feel light-headed.

## 3. Hygiene
- Single-use kese mitts per guest, or launder and disinfect per your local spa rules.
- Bowls and platforms rinsed with hot water between guests; regular disinfection of floors and
  drains to control fungal growth and slip film.
- Peştemals and towels laundered hot for each guest.

## 4. Onsen and sento etiquette (to brief guests)
- **Wash thoroughly before entering**: sit on the stool at the washing station and soap and
  rinse your whole body. The bath is for soaking, not washing.
- Pour bath water over yourself (**kakeyu**) first to acclimatise.
- **No swimwear** in traditional baths; the small towel stays out of the water (on your head
  or the side).
- Tie up long hair so it doesn't touch the water; no swimming, splashing or noisy behaviour.
- **Tattoos:** many baths still refuse visible tattoos; some allow cover patches; check each venue.
- Dry off before returning to the changing area.

## Gotchas
- Guests often dehydrate; give water before and after.
- Fainting in heat is the main risk: never leave a guest alone in the hot room.
- Overly aggressive kese on darker or sensitive skin can cause post-inflammatory marks;
  adjust pressure.
- Black soap isn't for eyes; rinse immediately if it gets there.

## Verify
- [ ] Guest screened for heart issues, pregnancy, recent shaving/waxing and skin problems.
- [ ] Skin softened before kese; strips of dead skin roll off without rawness.
- [ ] Guest rested, warm and hydrated before leaving.
- [ ] Kese single-use or disinfected; textiles laundered hot.
- [ ] International guests briefed on wash-first, no-swimwear and tattoo rules where relevant.
