# Sectioning, elevation and guideline cutting

**Goal:** turn a consultation picture into a predictable shape by controlling three
variables — where the hair is held (elevation), which way it is pulled (over-direction)
and what you cut to (the guide) — then prove it with a cross-check.

## 1. Consult before you pick up scissors
- Agree the **finished length at a landmark** (jawline, collarbone, "a finger below the
  ear"), not in centimetres — clients misjudge both. Show it on their own hair, dry.
- Look at **growth patterns** dry: crown whorls, nape whorls, front cowlicks, hairline
  dips. A cowlick at the fringe needs extra length or it will stand up.
- Note **texture and shrinkage**. Curly and coily hair can shrink a lot when dry (often
  quoted as 25–75%, it varies widely by curl type); cut curls dry or leave much more
  length when wet. Fine hair shows every line; thick hair hides lines but adds bulk.

## 2. Section for control
Standard four- or five-section pattern: centre parting ear to ear over the crown, then
split front and back. Work in **subsections about 1–2 cm thick** — thin enough to see
the guide through. Keep hair evenly damp (re-spray as it dries; dry hair cuts shorter
and frizzier than wet hair under tension). Comb with consistent, **light tension** — on
curly hair and over the ears, heavy tension makes the hair spring up shorter.

## 3. Elevation and the shape it builds
| Elevation (from the head's natural fall) | Result |
|---|---|
| 0° — natural fall, no lift | One-length / blunt: maximum weight at the perimeter (bob, blunt long). |
| Low–medium (~15–60°) | Graduation: weight line builds up, stacked shape (graduated bob, wedge). |
| 90° from the curve of the head | Uniform layers: same length all over, follows head shape. |
| Above 90° / to a fixed point overhead | Increased layers: shorter on top, length kept at perimeter (long layers). |

**Over-direction** — pulling hair away from its natural position before cutting — makes
it *longer* toward where you pulled from. Pull side hair back to the ear to keep front
length; pull everything to the centre to create a face-framing V.

## 4. Guides
- **Stationary guide:** every section is brought to one fixed point and cut there.
  Builds length difference (used for long layers, face framing).
- **Travelling (mobile) guide:** each new subsection includes a slice of the previous
  cut piece. Keeps length uniform (layers, graduation).
Always see the guide before you cut. If you can't see it, the subsection is too thick
or you've lost it — go back a section.

## 5. Cutting and finishing
- Cut **parallel or perpendicular to the section** consistently; changing finger angle
  mid-haircut is the classic cause of uneven lines.
- **Point-cutting** (snipping into the ends vertically) softens a blunt line;
  **slide-cutting** removes weight along the shaft — easy to overdo on fine hair.
  **Thinning scissors** high up the shaft on coarse hair cause frizz and short pieces
  poking through; keep them in the mid-lengths to ends.
- Cut longer than the target on the first pass, especially at the fringe and neckline.

## 6. Cross-check
Re-section **in the opposite direction** to how you cut (horizontal cut → vertical
check) at the same elevation. Check both sides in the mirror for balance, and compare
**ear to ear** with fingers at equal points. Then dry and style and re-check: lines
change once hair falls naturally, and curls move a lot.

## Gotchas
- Head tilted by the client (looking at a phone) = uneven perimeter. Keep the head level.
- Ears push hair out: the line over the ear always lands shorter unless you cut with
  zero tension there.
- Left-handed vs right-handed stance changes how you angle fingers on the far side —
  rotate yourself, not the client's head.
- Guidance only: training standards (e.g. NVQ/state board) differ in elevation names
  and degrees; the numbers above are common teaching values, not a single standard.

## Verify
- [ ] Agreed length named by landmark and confirmed with the client before cutting.
- [ ] Guide visible in every subsection; elevation stayed consistent through each area.
- [ ] Cross-checked in the opposite direction; sides balanced ear to ear.
- [ ] Shape re-checked after drying and styling, with growth patterns behaving.
