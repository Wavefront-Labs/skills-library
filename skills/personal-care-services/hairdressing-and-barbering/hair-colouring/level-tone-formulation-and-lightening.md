# Level, tone, formulation and lightening

**Goal:** predict the colour you'll get before it goes on, by working out how far you
must move the hair (level), what warmth that exposes (underlying pigment) and what
neutralises it (tone).

## 1. Read the hair (in daylight or a daylight bulb)
- **Natural level** at the regrowth, on the 1–10 scale (1 black … 10 lightest blonde;
  some brands run to 12 for high-lift). Use the brand's shade chart swatches against
  the root, not the client's description.
- **Existing colour** on mid-lengths and ends — previous dye, box colour, henna, or
  grey percentage. Artificial colour does not lift with more colour ("colour doesn't
  lift colour"); it needs a lightener or colour remover.
- **Porosity and condition:** stretch a wet strand — if it stretches and doesn't
  return, it is over-processed and needs a gentler plan or bond-building additive.
- **Target level and tone**, agreed against a swatch or photo *in the same light*.

## 2. Tone and the colour wheel
Opposites neutralise: **violet cancels yellow, blue cancels orange, green cancels red.**
Shade numbers usually read `level.primary-tone secondary-tone` (e.g. 7.1 = level 7 ash),
but the tone digit codes **differ by brand** — always use that brand's chart.

## 3. Underlying (contributing) pigment when you lift
As natural hair lightens it passes through roughly: red (levels 3–4) → red-orange (5)
→ orange (6) → orange-yellow / gold (7) → yellow (8) → pale yellow (9–10). Whatever
level you stop at, that warmth is exposed and your formula's tone must control it. A
level-8 ash on a level-5 base usually reads golden unless you lighten first and tone.

## 4. Choose the product and developer
| Goal | Typical approach |
|---|---|
| Deposit only / blend grey, same level or darker | Demi-permanent with low-volume developer (brand-specific, often ~6–10 vol). |
| Lift 1–2 levels with tint | Permanent with 20 vol (6%). |
| Lift ~3 levels with tint | 30 vol (9%), or high-lift tints with 40 vol per maker. |
| More than ~3–4 levels, or lifting off artificial colour | Lightener (bleach), then tone. |
| Grey coverage | Mix a natural/neutral base into the formula; resistant grey may need longer time or pre-softening. |
Mix ratios (1:1, 1:1.5, 1:2) are **brand-specific** — weigh on scales, don't eyeball.
On the scalp, lightener at 30–40 vol is a burn risk: most on-scalp lighteners are
used at 20 vol or lower.

## 5. Application order
- **Virgin lift:** mid-lengths and ends first, roots last — body heat makes the root
  process faster ("hot roots" if applied at the same time).
- **Regrowth retouch:** regrowth only; pull through for the last 5–10 minutes only if
  ends have faded. Overlapping lightener onto previously lightened hair causes breakage
  lines.
- Foils / balayage: keep slices consistent; lightener bleeding out of foils gives spots.

## 6. Tone and rinse
Lighten to the **right underlying level** (e.g. pale yellow for a cool level-9 blonde)
before toning — a violet toner can't fix orange. Tone on towel-dried hair, watch it
visually; most toners act fast and cool shades can over-deposit to grey/lilac on
porous ends. Emulsify at the basin, rinse thoroughly, use an acidic/post-colour
conditioner.

## Safety
- **Allergy alert (patch) test** per manufacturer instructions — commonly 48 hours
  before an oxidative colour, including returning clients after a gap. PPD/PTD
  reactions can be severe. Do not proceed after a positive or uncertain result.
- **Strand test** on unknown history (box dye, henna with metallic salts can react
  violently with peroxide).
- Gloves for every mix; ventilate when using lightener powder.

## Gotchas
- Regulations on patch testing and permitted dye strengths vary by country (e.g. EU
  cosmetics rules); follow the product insert and local law, which take precedence.
- Darker colour on porous ends grabs much darker; add a filler or reduce time there.
- Developer volumes and "levels of lift" above are typical teaching values, not
  universal — unconfirmed for any specific brand.

## Verify
- [ ] Natural level, existing colour and target were all read against the brand chart.
- [ ] The formula names the underlying pigment you'll expose and the tone that controls it.
- [ ] Patch and strand tests done and recorded; results negative.
- [ ] Result checked dry in daylight; formula and timing written on the client card.
