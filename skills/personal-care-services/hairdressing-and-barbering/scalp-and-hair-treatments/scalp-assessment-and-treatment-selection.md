# Scalp assessment and treatment selection

**Goal:** decide, in a few minutes at the basin, whether a scalp/hair issue is a
cosmetic one you can treat, one you should work around, or one that needs a GP,
dermatologist or trichologist — then choose the right in-salon treatment.

## 1. Look before you wash
Part the hair in several places (crown, nape, temples, behind the ears) under good
light; a magnifier or scalp camera helps. Note:
- **Oil:** shiny scalp, hair limp at the roots within a day of washing.
- **Flakes:** small, dry, white and loose (often dry scalp or simple dandruff) vs
  larger, yellowish, greasy and stuck to red skin (suggests seborrhoeic dermatitis —
  a medical diagnosis, not yours to make, but a reason to refer if severe).
- **Redness, scale plaques, broken skin, pustules, bald patches, lice/nits.**
- **Hair:** porosity (rough, tangles, absorbs fast), elasticity (wet stretch test),
  breakage points, split ends, heat or chemical damage.

## 2. Stop, adapt, or refer
| Finding | Action |
|---|---|
| Head lice / nits | Don't proceed; advise pharmacy treatment; disinfect tools and capes. Discreet, not embarrassing. |
| Suspected ringworm (scaly patches, broken hairs, round areas) | Contagious — don't treat; refer to GP. |
| Open wounds, infection, pustules | Don't treat that area; refer if spreading or painful. |
| Thick plaques (possible psoriasis), severe flaking, itching that doesn't settle | Gentle service only; refer to GP/dermatologist. |
| Sudden or patchy hair loss, diffuse thinning | Cosmetic help only; recommend GP (thyroid, iron, hormones and medications are common causes — not for you to diagnose). |
These are typical salon contraindications; your training body's list takes precedence.

## 3. Choose the treatment
- **Oily scalp:** clarifying or balancing shampoo, light scalp massage (heavy massage
  can stimulate more oil — reported anecdotally, unconfirmed), lightweight conditioner
  on lengths only.
- **Dry/tight scalp, small flakes:** mild shampoo, hydrating scalp tonic or oil,
  gentle exfoliating scalp treatment; advise cooler water and less frequent washing.
- **Dandruff-type flaking:** anti-dandruff shampoo with an active such as zinc
  pyrithione, ketoconazole, selenium sulfide or salicylic acid — leave on for the label
  contact time (often a few minutes). Persistent cases: GP/pharmacist.
- **Hair conditioning — protein vs moisture:**
  - Hair stretches a long way and won't return, feels mushy/gummy → needs **protein**
    (e.g. hydrolysed keratin).
  - Hair snaps with little stretch, feels straw-like and stiff → needs **moisture**
    (humectants, emollients). Too much protein makes it worse.
  Apply to towel-dried hair, use gentle heat (steamer or heat cap) if the product
  allows, rinse at the stated time.

## 4. Scalp massage (part of the treatment)
Fingertip pads, not nails. Effleurage to start, then small circular rotary movements
and petrissage across the scalp from hairline to crown to nape, then
friction lightly; 5–10 minutes. Avoid massage on any contraindicated area; keep it
light for clients with long, fragile or newly coloured hair (tangling).

## 5. Home-care plan
Write it down: shampoo frequency, which product on the scalp vs the lengths, heat
protection, how long before review (often 4–6 weeks). Keep claims cosmetic — "helps
reduce visible flakes", not "cures dandruff" or "regrows hair".

## Gotchas
- A product sold "for hair growth" in a salon is a cosmetic claim minefield; avoid
  medical promises (advertising rules vary by country).
- Over-clarifying strips colour; ask about recent colour first.
- Tools that touched an infestation or infection: disinfect per local regulation.

## Verify
- [ ] Scalp inspected in several areas before washing; findings recorded.
- [ ] Contraindications checked; referrals made in writing where needed.
- [ ] Treatment matches the finding (oil/flake/protein/moisture), applied per label timing.
- [ ] Client leaves with a written home-care plan and review date.
