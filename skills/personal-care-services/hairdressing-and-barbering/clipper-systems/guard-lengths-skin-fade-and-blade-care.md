# Guard lengths, skin fade and blade care

**Goal:** a fade with no lines, from skin to length, using clippers, guards and the
taper lever — and a blade kept sharp, aligned and hygienic so it does that without
cutting or irritating the client.

## 1. Know the system
- **Guard numbers** on the common US/European salon standard (Wahl/Andis pattern):
  #0.5 ≈ 1.5 mm, #1 ≈ 3 mm, #1.5 ≈ 4.5 mm, #2 ≈ 6 mm, #3 ≈ 10 mm, #4 ≈ 13 mm,
  then +3 mm per number. **Brands differ** — check yours (some Asian/European ranges
  use mm directly; verify your guard set rather than trusting a chart).
- **Taper lever:** closed (lever up/towards blade) cuts shortest — roughly 0.5 mm on a
  typical adjustable clipper with no guard; open (lever down) cuts about 1.5–3 mm.
  Every guard therefore has a short and a long setting: "a 1 closed" vs "a 1 open".
  This half-step is the main blending tool.
- **Trimmer (zero-gapped or near-zero)** for outlines; **foil shaver** for skin.

## 2. Skin fade, step by step
1. **Clean up with a longer guard** (e.g. #3–4) on the sides and back to remove bulk.
2. **Set the guideline:** with the bare clipper lever open (or a #0.5), cut the lowest
   line where the fade starts — low (just above the ear), mid (temple), high
   (parietal ridge). Keep it level by checking ear to ear.
3. Below it, **foil-shave or trimmer to skin**.
4. **Work up the guards one step at a time**: lever open no guard → #0.5 → #1 → #1.5 →
   #2. Each new guard starts **about half-way up** the previous band and goes slightly
   higher; you're always removing the line above the last one.
5. Use a **flicking or "C-stroke" motion**: blade into the hair, tilt the clipper away
   from the head as you go up so it lifts off. Pushing straight up leaves a hard line.
6. **Erase lines** with the half-steps: lever half-open, the guard of the band
   above with lever closed, or clipper-over-comb across the line. Dark band = too much
   left; light band = too much taken — fix the band *above* first.
7. **Blend into the top** with the longest guard or scissor-over-comb at the
   parietal ridge.
8. Line up with the trimmer, then check in good overhead and side light; turn the
   chair to see the shadows.

## 3. Freehand and clipper-over-comb
Clipper-over-comb: comb lifts the hair at the angle you want, clipper blade runs flat
across the comb's teeth. Keep the comb moving and the clipper parallel to it.

## 4. Blade care and hygiene
- **Before every client:** brush out hair; spray with a clipper disinfectant registered
  for barber use and leave the stated contact time; let it evaporate.
- **Oil** on the blade teeth and the rails (2–3 drops, run for a few seconds, wipe off)
  every few cuts — dry blades heat up and drag.
- **Blade heat:** touch the blade to your wrist; if hot, swap blades or use cooling spray.
- **Alignment:** the cutting (moving) blade's teeth must sit behind the stationary
  blade's teeth — commonly about 0.5–1 mm back. Zero-gapping trimmers brings them
  closer for skin-tight lines but makes nicks and irritation much more likely —
  zero-gap only if trained, and recheck after any drop.
- Dropped clipper: check for chipped teeth before it touches a client.

## Gotchas
- Fading on the wrong guard because you forgot the lever was open is the No. 1 cause
  of a "hole" — check the lever before each band.
- Fine, light-coloured hair shows less line; very dark coarse hair on light skin shows
  every flaw — blend more gradually there.
- Disinfectant rules (contact time, immersion vs spray) are set by your licensing
  board; follow those over this guide.

## Verify
- [ ] Guard and lever sizes known for your actual kit.
- [ ] Guideline level ear to ear; each band started half-way up the previous one.
- [ ] No visible lines in overhead and side light.
- [ ] Blade oiled, cool, correctly aligned, and disinfected for the stated contact time.
