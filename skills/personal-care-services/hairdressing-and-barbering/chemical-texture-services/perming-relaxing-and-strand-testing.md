# Perming, relaxing and strand testing

**Goal:** re-form the hair's disulphide bonds into a new shape (curl or straight)
without breaking the hair or burning the scalp. Both services are irreversible on the
treated length; the only real undo is growing it out and cutting it off.

## 1. Before anything: history and tests
- **Ask about every chemical in the last ~2 years:** colour, lightener, previous perm or
  relaxer, keratin treatments, box dyes, henna. **Relaxers (sodium hydroxide, "lye") and
  perms (thioglycolate) are generally incompatible** — putting one over the other can
  dissolve the hair. Keratin/smoothing over relaxed hair is brand-dependent.
- **Strand test** on a hidden cutting from the nape: process with the actual product and
  timing, then check elasticity and texture. Hair that snaps or goes gummy = no service.
- **Scalp check:** no abrasions, recent scratching, sunburn or lesions; don't brush or
  vigorously shampoo before a relaxer (it opens micro-abrasions).
- Follow the product's skin/patch test instructions where the maker requires one.

## 2. Perming
1. **Shampoo lightly** without conditioner (it can block the lotion), towel-dry.
2. **Rod choice** sets curl size: the curl is roughly the rod's diameter; one rod
   size smaller than the target because perms relax. Section to the rod's length and
   width; base control (on-base vs off-base) sets root lift.
3. **Wrap** with end papers smoothing every end — fishhooks (bent ends) are the most
   common perm fault. Even, moderate tension; no stretching.
4. **Apply waving lotion** (alkaline for resistant/virgin hair; acid or "low pH" for
   tinted, fine or fragile hair — follow the maker). Protect the skin with cotton strips;
   change them once they're saturated.
5. **Test curl** from the start of the stated time: unwind 1.5 turns at several areas;
   when the hair forms an S that matches the rod size, rinse. Don't rely on the clock.
6. **Rinse thoroughly** (several minutes, rods in), blot, **neutralise** for the stated
   time — under-neutralising is why perms drop within a week.
7. No shampoo for ~48 hours is common aftercare advice (product-dependent).

## 3. Relaxing (lye or no-lye)
1. **Base the scalp** with protective cream (essential with lye; still advised for
   "no-base" formulas at the hairline and ears).
2. **Regrowth only on a retouch.** Overlapping relaxer onto previously relaxed hair is
   the main cause of breakage lines. Strong formula for coarse/resistant, mild for fine.
3. Apply to the most resistant area first (often the nape or crown) and the hairline
   last; smooth with the back of the comb or fingers, never pull.
4. **Watch the clock and the client** — any burning or stinging = rinse that area
   immediately. Do not exceed the maker's maximum time.
5. Rinse with warm water until no slip remains, then **neutralising shampoo** (often
   colour-indicating) repeatedly until it shows no residue.
6. Space retouches roughly 6–10 weeks apart (maker/stylist guidance varies).

## 4. Smoothing / keratin systems
These are mostly a heat-sealed coating plus bond change, not true relaxing. Many
products release **formaldehyde or similar aldehydes when flat-ironed** — ventilate,
use extraction, and check the product's safety data sheet and local regulations.
"Formaldehyde-free" labels have been contested in some regulatory reviews — read the
SDS ingredients yourself (unverified for any specific brand).

## Gotchas
- Colour after a relaxer or perm: wait — about 1–2 weeks is a common rule; consult the
  maker. Same-day services multiply damage.
- Very tight rods on fine hair look frizzy, not curly.
- A client who "just washed this morning" is a relaxer risk; reschedule rather than hurry.

## Verify
- [ ] Full chemical history recorded; incompatibilities ruled out.
- [ ] Strand test passed (elastic, intact) with the actual product.
- [ ] Perm: test curls matched rod size; full rinse and neutraliser time observed.
- [ ] Relaxer: scalp based, regrowth only, rinsed until neutralising shampoo showed clear.
- [ ] Aftercare and next-retouch timing written on the client record.
