# Straight-razor hot-towel wet shave

**Goal:** a close, comfortable shave with no cuts or razor burn, using a straight razor
(in most salons a **disposable-blade shavette** — single-use blades are the hygiene
norm and in many places a licensing requirement; a fixed-blade cut-throat is often
restricted for client use).

## 1. Consult and map the grain
- Ask about sensitive skin, ingrown hairs, acne, blood-thinning medication or
  conditions that bleed easily, and recent facial treatments (retinoids, peels).
  **Don't shave over active acne, cold sores, rashes or open lesions.**
- **Map the grain** with your fingertips on dry stubble: the direction it feels smooth
  is *with* the grain. Necks often have swirls and grow sideways or upward — map
  each area, don't assume "downwards".
- Clients with tight curly beard hair prone to **pseudofolliculitis barbae** (razor
  bumps) usually do better with with-the-grain passes only, or a close clipper finish
  instead of a razor.

## 2. Prep (the shave is mostly prep)
1. Trim long stubble with clippers first; a razor in long hair pulls.
2. **Hot towel**, 1–3 minutes: wring out until it's hot but bearable, **test it on your
   own inner wrist** first, leave nose clear. Repeat once for heavy beards.
3. Pre-shave oil (optional), then **lather** with a brush in circular strokes to lift
   the hairs — a thin, creamy lather, not foam that hides the skin.

## 3. Razor technique
- Insert a fresh blade in front of the client. Hold the razor at roughly **30° to the
  skin** (spine of the blade about two spine-widths off the face); flatter = gentler
  but less close, steeper = cuts.
- **Stretch the skin** with your free hand, flat and taut, opposite to the stroke.
  Most nicks happen on unstretched skin: jawline corners, chin dimple, Adam's apple,
  upper lip.
- Short, light strokes; let the blade's weight do the work. No pressure.
- Use the established holds: **freehand** (forehand), **backhand**, and the
  **push-stroke** for awkward angles. Rotate yourself and gently turn the client's
  head, rather than twisting your wrist into an unsafe angle.
- **First pass with the grain** on all areas. Re-lather. **Second pass across the
  grain.** Against-the-grain only on skin that tolerated the first two, and never on
  clients prone to ingrowns.
- Wipe the blade on a tissue or towel after each stroke, away from your fingers.

## 4. Finish
- Rinse residue with a warm damp towel, then a **cool towel** to calm the skin.
- Alum block or a gentle balm; alcohol aftershave stings on sensitive skin — offer
  alcohol-free.
- Line-ups (neck, beard edge) are done the same way: lather or clear gel so you can
  see the line, stretch skin, short strokes.

## 5. Nicks and hygiene
- Nick: firm pressure with a clean tissue; styptic (single-use pencil or liquid
  applied with a fresh cotton bud — never touch a shared styptic stick to skin).
- Wear gloves where policy requires; treat any blood as potentially infectious.
- **Used blades into a sharps container immediately**; disinfect the razor handle with
  a hospital-level disinfectant per local regulation.

## Gotchas
- Too-hot towel = burn and complaints; too-cool = no softening. Re-test each towel.
- Dry spots in the lather = drag and burn. Re-lather small areas rather than shaving dry.
- The angle "30°" is a common teaching value; feel matters more — if you hear scraping
  or see skin, stop and re-assess.
- Regulations on razor type, gloves and sterilisation differ by state/country; follow
  your licensing board.

## Verify
- [ ] Contraindications checked; grain mapped area by area.
- [ ] Towel temperature tested on your wrist before every application.
- [ ] Fresh blade shown to the client; skin stretched on every stroke.
- [ ] No nicks, no redness beyond mild warmth after the cool towel.
- [ ] Blade in sharps container; handle disinfected.
