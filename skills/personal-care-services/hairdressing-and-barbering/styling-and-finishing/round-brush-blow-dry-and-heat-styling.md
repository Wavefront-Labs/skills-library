# Round-brush blow-dry and heat styling

**Goal:** a blow-dry that is smooth, has the volume you planned, and still holds the
next day — without frying the ends with hot tools.

## Why it holds: the hydrogen-bond set
Water breaks the hair's hydrogen bonds; as the hair dries **in a shape and cools in that
shape**, those bonds re-form and hold it. So the shape is set at the moment the section
goes from damp to dry and then cools. Humidity re-breaks the bonds — that is why
blow-dries drop in damp weather.

## 1. Prep
- Towel-blot, don't rub. Detangle from ends upward.
- **Heat protectant** through mid-lengths and ends; add root lift product at the root
  only if wanted. Too much product = heavy, sticky hair that won't hold.
- **Rough-dry to ~70–80% dry** with fingers, directing the roots the opposite way to
  their fall for volume. Brushing soaking-wet hair stretches it and wastes time.

## 2. Section and dry
- Section into clips; work **from the nape up**, subsections no wider than the brush
  and about as deep as its bristles grip.
- **Brush size:** small barrel = more curl/bend; large barrel = smoothness and soft
  bend. Over-direct the section **up at 90°+ from the head** for root volume; pull
  down/flat for sleek roots.
- **Nozzle on, always, pointed down the hair shaft** (root to end) to flatten the
  cuticle — air blown up the shaft roughens it and causes frizz. Keep the nozzle
  moving, a few centimetres from the brush, never pressed into the hair.
- Keep **tension** on the section; rotate the brush and follow it with the dryer to the
  ends. Dry the section **completely** — a damp section frizzes and drops.
- **Cool shot** with the brush still in place, or roll the section and pin it (a
  "set") to cool. This is the step most people skip.
- Fringe first, while it's still damp, or cowlicks take over — dry it side to side
  (left, right, left) to neutralise the cowlick, then direct it.

## 3. Hot tools (dry hair only)
| Hair | Typical flat-iron/curling setting |
|---|---|
| Fine, lightened or damaged | ~150–170 °C (300–340 °F) |
| Normal / medium | ~170–190 °C (340–375 °F) |
| Coarse, resistant | ~190–210 °C (375–410 °F); rarely higher |
These are common guidance ranges, not lab-verified limits; start low and only go up if
the hair doesn't take the shape. **Steam or sizzling** means the hair isn't dry — stop.
- One slow pass beats several fast ones. Thin sections (1–2 cm) so heat goes through.
- **Curls:** wrap away from the face on the sides for an open look; alternate direction
  at the back for a natural look. Hold each curl in the palm or pin it until cool,
  then brush out.

## 4. Finish
- Light shine product on the ends only; hairspray from 25–30 cm away. Hairspray on hot
  hair can sizzle and crunch — let it cool first.
- Check the back in the hand mirror for flat spots and missed damp sections.

## Gotchas
- **Diffusing curly hair:** low speed, low/medium heat, cup the hair; don't touch it
  until dry or it frizzes. Brushing curls dry destroys the pattern.
- Dryer filter clogged with lint = overheating dryer and slower drying. Clean it weekly.
- Never leave a hot tool on a client's ear or neck — use a heat-resistant mat and keep
  the cable out of their lap.
- Bleached ends can melt or snap at temperatures virgin hair tolerates — always use the
  low setting there.

## Verify
- [ ] Every section was dried fully with the nozzle aimed root to tip.
- [ ] Each section cooled in shape (cool shot or pinned) before release.
- [ ] Iron temperature matched to hair condition; no steam or sizzle.
- [ ] Finished style checked from the back and still has shape after 10 minutes.
