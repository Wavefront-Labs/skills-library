# Hoist and sling transfers

**Goal:** move a person between surfaces with no injury to them or the carer, using the
right equipment chosen from their care plan — never improvised lifting.

Pairs with human-services.aged-care.mobility-and-falls-support.safe-transfers-and-falls-prevention
(assisted standing transfers and falls); this skill is the equipment side.

## 1. Choose the method from the assessment, not the day
| Person can... | Typical method |
|---|---|
| Stand and step with light help | Walking aid, verbal prompts, one carer |
| Bear weight but not step | Turntable or **stand-aid (sit-to-stand) hoist** |
| Sit balanced, not bear weight | **Slide/transfer board** (level surfaces, armrest off) |
| Not bear weight or sit balanced | **Full-body hoist** (mobile or ceiling) + sling |
| Lie in bed, needs repositioning | **Slide sheets**, two carers, bed at waist height |
The moving-and-handling assessment and care plan name the equipment, the sling size and
type, and how many carers. If the person's ability has changed today, **stop and
re-assess** rather than adapting on the spot.

## 2. Checks before every lift
- Hoist: in-date thorough-examination sticker (UK LOLER: patient hoists and slings
  every **6 months**), battery charged, emergency lowering known, safe working load
  (SWL) above the person's weight, brakes/legs working.
- Sling: the **named person's** sling (single-patient or labelled), correct type and
  size, label legible, no fraying, worn stitching or laundering damage.
- Route clear; destination prepared (chair brakes on, commode positioned).
- Explain each step to the person and get their agreement.

## 3. Full-body hoist sequence (two carers is the usual standard)
1. Roll the person (slide sheet) to position the sling — centre line on the spine,
   base of the sling at the coccyx, top at the shoulders/head as the type requires.
2. Bring the legs of the hoist under the bed; **open the legs** only when moving into
   a chair, not on the bed.
3. Attach loops: shoulder loops first, then leg loops, **same colour loop each side**
   (a mismatch tilts the person). Leg straps crossed or not crossed per sling type.
4. Raise slowly just clear of the surface; **stop and check** every attachment and the
   person's comfort before moving.
5. Move by pushing the hoist mast, not the person; mobile hoist **brakes off** while
   lifting/lowering (lets it find its centre) unless the manufacturer says otherwise.
6. Lower into the chair with one carer guiding the hips back; detach legs first, then
   shoulders; remove the sling unless the plan says leave in place.

## 4. Slide sheets and boards
- Slide sheets: two tubes or a tubular sheet under the person, carers in walk-stand,
  move on a count, friction only — **no lifting**. Remove the sheet afterwards (it
  creates a slip hazard and pressure risk).
- Boards: surfaces level or slightly downhill, armrest removed, person shuffles in small
  increments; carer guards the gap, doesn't pull.

## 5. Gotchas
- Wrong sling size: too big = slip-out risk; too small = pressure at the thighs and
  shoulders. Size by the manufacturer's chart, not by guess.
- Mixing sling and hoist brands: only with the manufacturer's confirmed compatibility.
- Never use a hoist to pick someone up from the floor without the floor-lift procedure;
  first check for injury.
- Drag lifts (under-arm hooks) are not an acceptable technique.
- One-carer hoisting is sometimes permitted after risk assessment; don't assume it is.

## Verify
- [ ] Method matches the current care plan; changes trigger re-assessment.
- [ ] Hoist and sling inspection in date; sling is the person's, correct size.
- [ ] Loops matched and checked at the "just clear" pause.
- [ ] No manual lifting of the person's weight at any point.
- [ ] Person asked about comfort; skin checked where sling contacts.
