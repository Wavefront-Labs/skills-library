# Coaching daily prosthesis and orthosis use

**Goal:** the person puts on, wears, cares for and uses their fitted device
independently every day, recognises a fit problem early, and knows when it is a
coaching issue and when it must go back to the prosthetist/orthotist.

**Scope line:** coaching is *use* of a device someone else prescribed and fitted.
Adjusting alignment, trimming sockets or changing componentry is the clinician's job
(see health-medicine.allied-health-technologies.orthotics-and-prosthetics.transtibial-socket-fitting-and-alignment-troubleshooting).

## 1. Donning and doffing (lower limb, the common case)
- Follow the **specific suspension system's** sequence — liner + pin lock, liner +
  seal-in/suction, sleeve, or socks only. Get the clinic's written instructions and
  copy them into a step card.
- Liner: turn fully inside out, place the end against the limb with no air pocket,
  roll on — never pull or drag (shears the skin). Wrinkles = pressure lines later.
- Pin-lock: line the pin straight down; listen for each click. Sitting, then stand to
  seat fully.
- **Sit to don and doff** until balance is proven; have the chair braked.
- Doffing: release the lock/valve first, never yank the limb out.

## 2. Sock-ply and volume
- Residual limbs shrink over the day and over the first months. The person adjusts with
  **sock ply** (1-, 3-, 5-ply socks). Teach the cue: pistoning, a looser feel, or the
  pin bottoming out = add ply; pain at the end or red marks high on the limb = remove ply.
- Keep a log of ply worn morning and evening. Needing more than about 10–15 ply total
  usually means the socket is too big — refer, don't keep stacking (uncertain: clinics
  quote different thresholds; use theirs).

## 3. Wear-time build-up and skin checks
- New users follow a **graded wearing schedule** from the clinic (often starting at
  1–2 hours, increasing daily). Don't let enthusiasm skip it.
- After every removal: inspect the whole limb with a long-handled mirror. Redness that
  fades within ~20–30 minutes is normally acceptable; redness that persists, blisters,
  broken skin or darkened areas = stop wearing and contact the clinic.
- Wash limb and liner daily with mild soap, rinse thoroughly, dry fully. No lotion
  before donning unless the clinic says so.
- Diabetic or poor-sensation users: daily checks are non-negotiable — they won't feel it.

## 4. Gait and daily-task practice
- Practise in parallel bars or with the walking aid the physio prescribed, then
  progress: level floors → turns → slopes → stairs (good leg up, prosthetic leg down)
  → uneven ground → carrying things.
- Coach equal step length and time on each side; a short prosthetic step often means
  fear or pain, not technique.
- Upper limb: practise real tasks (opening jars, dressing, cutlery) in short sessions;
  myoelectric users need electrode skin contact and a battery-charging routine.
- Orthoses (AFO/KAFO): correct shoe (often a half-size up, removable insole), straps
  in the order taught, check heel fully seated.

## 5. Gotchas
- Falls happen most when getting up at night without the device — plan a route,
  light, and a walking aid by the bed.
- Weight change of a few kilos changes fit.
- Never wear a damaged liner; a split liner causes skin breakdown fast.
- Heat and sweat loosen suction suspension; carry a cloth and spare socks.

## Verify
- [ ] Person dons and doffs independently using their system's sequence.
- [ ] Sock-ply log is kept; they can say when to add or remove ply.
- [ ] Skin check done at every removal; red-flag findings are known.
- [ ] Gait/task goals practised at the level the physio set.
- [ ] They know exactly which problems mean "call the prosthetist".
