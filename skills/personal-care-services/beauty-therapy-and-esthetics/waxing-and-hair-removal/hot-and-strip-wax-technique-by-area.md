# Hot and strip wax technique by area

**Goal:** remove hair from the root in as few passes as possible, with no burns, no
lifted skin and minimal breakage — choosing the wax for the area, not by habit.

## 1. Screen and prep
- **Contraindications:** oral isotretinoin (commonly no waxing for ~6 months after the
  last dose), topical retinoids or prescription exfoliants on the area (stop several
  days before — product guidance varies), recent peels/laser, sunburn, broken skin,
  varicose veins (avoid), moles and skin tags (protect, don't wax over), diabetes and
  blood thinners (caution: slower healing, bruising).
- **Hair length:** about **5 mm–1 cm** (roughly "grain of rice"). Shorter: wax can't
  grip. Much longer: trim first — pulling long hair hurts and breaks.
- Clean with a pre-wax cleanser/antiseptic; skin must be **completely dry** (light
  dusting powder on damp or oily areas for strip wax; a thin pre-wax oil for hard wax
  per maker).
- **Test the wax temperature on your inner wrist** every time you start, and after
  any refill.

## 2. Strip (soft) wax — large areas
Best for legs, arms, back: fast, low cost, grips fine hair well.
1. Spatula at ~45°, thin even layer **with the hair growth**.
2. Strip on, rub firmly 2–3 times with the growth.
3. **Hold the skin taut** just below the strip with your other hand.
4. Remove **against the growth, low and parallel to the skin**, fast. Pulling *up*
   and away from the skin is what bruises and lifts skin.
5. Only one application per area — strip wax removes some skin cells; a second pass
   on the same patch risks lifting. Tweeze strays instead.

## 3. Hot (hard / film) wax — sensitive and coarse hair
Best for bikini/intimate, underarms, face. It shrink-wraps the hair, not the skin, so
it's gentler and can (carefully) go over an area twice.
1. Apply **with the growth** in a thicker layer (about coin-thick), patches no bigger
   than the area you can hold taut — palm-sized or less.
2. Build a **thicker "lip" edge** at the end you'll pull from.
3. Wait until it's pliable but no longer tacky (seconds; it should click/dull).
   Too long = brittle and it snaps.
4. Hold the skin taut, flick the lip, remove **against the growth, parallel to the skin**.
5. Immediately press your palm on the area to calm the sting.

## 4. Area notes
| Area | Wax | Watch out |
|---|---|---|
| Underarm | Hard | Growth goes in 2–3 directions — work in small zones. |
| Bikini / Brazilian | Hard | Small patches; client helps hold skin; strict hygiene, gloves. |
| Upper lip / chin | Hard | Tiny patches; tension with fingers above/below the lip. |
| Eyebrows | Hard or strip | See brow services skill for mapping. |
| Legs, arms, back | Strip | Big strips; check ankle/knee angles for growth changes. |

## 5. Aftercare and hygiene
- Remove residue with post-wax oil; soothing lotion. Advise 24–48 h: no hot baths,
  sauna, sun, tight clothes, perfumed products, gym sweat.
- Ingrowns: gentle exfoliation from about 48–72 h after, a few times a week.
- **No double-dipping** — a fresh spatula every dip. Gloves on. Dispose of waxed strips
  as clinical/contaminated waste if blood spotting occurs (follow local rules).

## Gotchas
- Lifted skin (shiny weeping patch): stop, cool, apply a soothing antiseptic; no
  further waxing there. Record it.
- Wax that strings is too cool; wax that runs is too hot.
- Sugaring removes *with* the growth and is another method — not covered here.

## Verify
- [ ] Contraindications screened, hair length right, wax temperature wrist-tested.
- [ ] Applied with the growth, removed against it, parallel to the skin, skin held taut.
- [ ] No lifted skin; no repeat strip-wax passes on the same patch.
- [ ] Fresh spatula every dip; aftercare given.
