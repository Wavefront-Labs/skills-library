# Skin typing, contraindications and treatment planning

**Goal:** before any facial, peel, wax or machine treatment, know what the skin is,
what it's doing right now, what would make the treatment unsafe, and what you're
aiming for over the next weeks.

## 1. Paperwork first (it's a safety tool)
A written consultation form, updated **every visit**: medications (oral and topical),
medical conditions, allergies, pregnancy, recent procedures (laser, injectables, peels,
waxing, sunbed/sun exposure), current home routine, and the client's goals. Clients
forget that "the acne tablet" or a prescription retinoid matters — ask by name.

## 2. Look and touch (clean skin, magnifying lamp)
Cleanse first; makeup hides everything. Then assess under the mag lamp, and by touch:
- **Skin type** (largely genetic, stable): oily, dry (lacks oil), combination,
  "normal". Look at pore size and oil in the T-zone vs cheeks.
- **Skin condition** (changes; this is what you treat): dehydrated (lacks water —
  oily skin can be dehydrated), sensitised, congested (comedones, milia), acne
  (inflamed papules/pustules), pigmentation, redness/visible capillaries, ageing.
- **Fitzpatrick phototype** I–VI (burns always/never tans … never burns/deeply
  pigmented). Higher types carry **more risk of post-inflammatory
  hyperpigmentation (PIH)** from heat, aggressive exfoliation, peels and waxing trauma.
- A Wood's lamp can show surface dehydration and pigment depth on some systems;
  interpretation varies and it isn't diagnostic — treat as a hint.

## 3. Contraindications
| Category | Examples | Typical action* |
|---|---|---|
| Absolute / don't treat | Active infection (cold sore, impetigo, conjunctivitis), open wounds, undiagnosed lumps or changing moles, sunburn | Postpone; refer to GP where relevant. |
| Medication | Oral isotretinoin (often no waxing/peels for ≥6 months after stopping), topical retinoids (stop several days before waxing/peels), blood thinners, photosensitising drugs | Adapt or postpone; follow product/insurer rules. |
| Restrictive / adapt | Pregnancy (avoid some actives and machines), diabetes (slower healing; caution with extraction/heat), epilepsy (some machines, flashing LED), pacemaker/metal implants (no galvanic/electrical currents), rosacea (no heat or aggressive exfoliation) | Modify; GP note if unsure. |
*Timings vary between training bodies and product manufacturers — use your insurer's
and the product's guidance; numbers here are commonly taught values, unverified.

## 4. When to refer, not treat
Any mole or lesion that is **A**symmetric, has irregular **B**orders, mixed
**C**olour, **D**iameter over ~6 mm or is **E**volving (changing, bleeding, itching):
don't touch it, mention it calmly and recommend a doctor. Same for cystic or scarring
acne, suspected rosacea flares, rashes and anything you can't name. Esthetics treats
the cosmetic surface; diagnosis is not in scope.

## 5. Plan
Write: priority concern, treatment series (e.g. 6 facials, 2–4 weeks apart), what
each session will include, home-care (cleanser, SPF 30+ daily, one active at a time),
and when you'll review. Get **informed consent** for anything with downtime or risk
(peels, extractions, machines) and photograph in consistent light with consent.

## Gotchas
- Mixing up "oily" and "dehydrated" leads to stripping cleansers that make both worse.
- New active in the client's home routine since last visit = re-check before exfoliating.
- Sunbed or holiday sun in the last week: no peels or aggressive treatment.
