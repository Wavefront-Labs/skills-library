# Brow mapping, shaping, tinting and lamination

**Goal:** brows that look balanced on *this* face, keep the client's natural density,
and grow back well — mapped before anything is removed, because removing too much
takes 6–12 weeks (or longer) to recover, and over-plucked brows may never fully return.

## 1. Consult
Ask what they like and dislike about their brows, what they fill in, whether they're
growing them out, and the usual contraindications for wax and tint (retinoids,
isotretinoin, eye infections, cuts, previous tint reaction). **Brush the brows up and
look at them from in front and in the mirror with the client** before mapping.

## 2. Map with a pencil (or thread with mapping paste)
Use a straight edge (a long brow pencil or mapping string):
1. **Start:** pencil from the side of the nostril straight up past the inner eye
   corner. Brow starts here — typically aligned with the inner corner. Narrow-set eyes
   look better with a start slightly further out; wide-set slightly in.
2. **Arch (highest point):** pencil from the side of the nostril through the outer edge
   of the iris (client looking straight ahead) up to the brow.
3. **Tail:** pencil from the nostril through the outer corner of the eye. The tail ends
   where it meets; its end is usually level with or slightly above the start.
4. Mark top and bottom lines; check **symmetry by eye, not ruler** — faces aren't
   symmetrical, and brows that are measured equal can look uneven because of bone
   structure or eye position. Show the client the drawn map and get a yes.

These "golden ratio" landmarks are a convention, not a rule — adjust for face shape and
the client's preference.

## 3. Remove outside the map only
- **Wax** (usually hard wax): above, below and between, never inside the drawn shape;
  small patches, skin taut, against the growth.
- **Tweezers** for strays and refinement, one hair at a time in the growth direction.
- **Threading** where offered; great precision, avoids wax contraindications.
- Take less from the **top line** than the bottom; top-line removal changes expression
  and is the most common overdo.
- **Trim** long hairs by brushing up and cutting just the excess above the top line —
  conservatively.

## 4. Tint
- **Allergy alert / patch test** per manufacturer instructions — often 48 h before,
  including for returning clients after a gap. Many brow tints contain PPD or similar.
- Shade: for most, **one to two shades darker than the hair on the head** for fair
  hair, and matching or slightly lighter for dark hair; ash/brown mixes cancel warmth.
- Protect skin around with barrier cream; apply with a fine brush, starting at the
  tail and arches; the front last and lightest so the start stays soft.
- Process for the maker's time; remove with damp pads. Henna-style brow products also
  stain skin; time per product.

## 5. Lamination (brow lift)
Two-step perming-type chemistry that sets hairs upward.
1. Cleanse and degrease; brush hairs up into shape.
2. **Step 1 (lifting/perming lotion)** — time strictly by hair: fine/tinted hair needs
   the shortest time (often only a few minutes); coarse longer. Covering with cling film
   speeds it.
3. Remove; **Step 2 (setting/neutralising lotion)** for its time.
4. Nourishing oil/serum. Optional tint after (wait per maker — some recommend not on
   the same day).
5. Aftercare: no wetting for ~24 h, no steam or heavy makeup, brush daily.
Over-processing leaves frizzy, broken brow hairs — time on the low side and check.

## Gotchas
- Never map from the client's old, over-plucked shape; map from the bone and eye.
- Darker tint than the face = harsh look; err light, it's easier to go darker.
- Lamination too often (more than every ~6–8 weeks) dries and breaks hairs.

## Verify
- [ ] Map drawn and approved by the client before any removal.
- [ ] All removal outside the map; top line left largely intact.
- [ ] Patch test done and recorded for tint/lamination products.
- [ ] Brows look balanced in the mirror from straight on; aftercare given.
