# Classic lash extension, isolation and retention

**Goal:** one synthetic lash bonded to one healthy natural lash, set so it grows out
cleanly over the natural cycle — no stuck lashes, no adhesive on skin, no eye
irritation, and retention to a 2–3 week infill.

## 1. Consult and screen
- Contraindications: eye infections (conjunctivitis, styes, blepharitis flare), recent
  eye surgery, very sparse or fragile natural lashes, trichotillomania, chemotherapy,
  known allergy to cyanoacrylate or latex. Contact lenses out.
- **Patch test** where your insurer/brand requires it, typically 24–48 h before a first
  set (adhesive behind the ear or a few lashes). Sensitisation can still develop later
  — tell clients that swelling or itching means "come back and remove", not "wait it out".

## 2. Environment and adhesive
- Cyanoacrylate adhesive **cures with moisture in the air**. Most adhesives specify a
  working range such as ~40–60% relative humidity and ~20–24 °C — read yours; keep a
  hygrometer by the bed. Low humidity = slow cure, fumes, stickies; high = shock-curing
  and weak bonds.
- Shake the bottle well; dispense a **small fresh drop every ~20–30 minutes** onto a
  jade stone/glue ring; a skinned-over drop holds badly.
- Ventilate; fumes are the main cause of eye stinging. A fan or nebuliser after
  application aids curing — keep the client's eyes closed.

## 3. Prep and map
- Cleanse lashes with lash cleanser, rinse, dry; primer if your system uses one.
- **Under-eye gel pads or tape** across the lower lashes, not touching the waterline
  or the eye surface. If the client feels stinging from the pad, reposition at once.
- **Map** with a marker on the pad: inner corner short, building to the longest at the
  outer third for a cat-eye, or at the centre for a doll-eye.
- **Weight rule:** extension length no more than ~30–50% longer than the natural lash,
  and diameter suited to its strength (0.15 mm classic is common; 0.20 only on strong
  lashes). Overweighting causes early loss and damage.

## 4. Isolation and attachment
1. Isolation tweezers (non-dominant hand) separate **one natural lash** cleanly, with
   no neighbours touching.
2. Dip the extension's base about 1–2 mm into the adhesive; wipe off excess by pulling
   out slowly — a bead of glue is a sticky waiting to happen.
3. Place along the natural lash, base **about 0.5–1 mm from the lid** (never touching
   skin), attached along the first third to half of its length.
4. Hold a second or two, then release isolation slowly. **Check that lash moves
   freely** after every placement.
5. Work in zones, alternating eyes, so adhesive cures without lashes resting together.

## 5. Finish and aftercare
- Brush through and run isolation tweezers across the line to find **stickies** (two
  natural lashes glued together) — separate before finishing; they cause pain and
  premature loss.
- Aftercare: keep dry for the time the adhesive maker states (often ~24 h), no oil-based
  removers or mascara on the extensions, clean daily with lash foam, don't rub or pick.
  Infill at 2–3 weeks.

## Gotchas
- Adhesive touching the skin = irritation, inflammation and early shedding.
- Fumes or a drip in the eye: stop, flush with sterile saline, remove pads; if pain
  persists, the client needs urgent medical attention. Document it.
- Lash lifts and tints are separate services with their own patch tests.

## Verify
- [ ] Humidity/temperature inside the adhesive's range; fresh drop in use.
- [ ] Every extension on exactly one natural lash, off the lid, appropriate weight.
- [ ] No stickies on a final isolation check; no adhesive on skin.
- [ ] Client's eyes comfortable on opening; aftercare given and set recorded.
