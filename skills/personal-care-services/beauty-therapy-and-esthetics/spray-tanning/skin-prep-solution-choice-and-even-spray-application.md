# Skin prep, solution choice and even spray application

**Goal:** a believable tan with no streaks, orange palms or patchy knees — achieved
mostly by prep and a consistent spraying pattern, not by the solution.

## How it works
**DHA (dihydroxyacetone)** reacts with amino acids in the dead outer skin layer to
form brown melanoidins (a Maillard reaction). It develops over ~4–24 h and fades as the
outer skin sheds (roughly 5–10 days). **Erythrulose** develops more slowly and is often
blended in for a longer, softer fade. Most solutions include a temporary **guide
colour (bronzer)** so you can see coverage while spraying — the bronzer washes off; the
DHA colour stays.

## 1. Consultation
- Ask about allergies, asthma or respiratory conditions (the mist is inhaled), pregnancy
  (many salons ask for GP clearance or avoid — evidence is limited), skin conditions,
  and recent waxing or shaving.
- **Fitzpatrick type and undertone** set solution strength. Typical solution bands
  quoted by makers: light ~6–8% DHA, medium ~8–10%, dark ~10–14% — verify against your
  brand's guide. Too strong for fair skin = orange; violet-based guide colours reduce
  orange tone on fair skin, green-based on red undertones (brand-dependent).
- Explain: a spray tan **is not sun protection** — daily SPF still needed.

## 2. Client prep (given at booking)
- **Exfoliate 24 h before** (dry/flaky areas especially), shave or wax at least 24 h
  before (waxing ideally 48 h) — freshly opened follicles show as dots.
- On the day: clean skin, no moisturiser, deodorant, perfume or makeup; loose dark
  clothing for afterwards.

## 3. In the booth
- Extraction booth running; you wear a respirator/mask; offer the client **nose
  filters, eye protection** and lip balm. The FDA and others have noted DHA is not
  approved for inhalation or mucous membranes — protect eyes, lips and nose (this is a
  regulatory caution, not a confirmed harm finding).
- **Barrier cream** on palms, nails, cuticles, soles' edges, between fingers and toes,
  dry knuckles, elbows, knees, ankles and eyebrows — these grab colour.
- Hair tied back and covered; jewellery off.

## 4. Spray pattern
- HVLP gun about **15–30 cm** from the skin, perpendicular, moving continuously.
- **Overlapping horizontal passes**, each overlapping the last by about half, in a fixed
  order every time — e.g. front: face → neck → chest → arms → abdomen → legs; turn;
  repeat on the back; then sides with arms raised.
- **Feather** wrists, ankles, elbows, knees and hairline with a lighter, further pass.
- **Face:** eyes closed, breath held for each short pass; one light coat.
- Contour (optional, experienced only): a second light pass under cheekbones, abs,
  quad lines — easy to overdo.
- Hands: let the client make a loose claw, mist the backs lightly from a distance, then
  wipe palms and nails.

## 5. Aftercare
- Dry 5–10 min with fan or air before dressing.
- **Rinse time** per solution (often 1–8 h for rapid formulas, 8–12 h standard): lukewarm
  water only, no soap on the first rinse, pat dry.
- No sweating, swimming, long baths or oil-based products for the first day or two;
  moisturise daily thereafter to slow patchy fading.

## Gotchas
- Green tinge: often lots of guide colour + chlorine/high pH skin or old solution; check
  dates and store solution cool and dark.
- Streaks under bra lines, watch straps and in skin folds: client must wait and wear loose
  clothing; stand differently for folds.
- Rinse too soon = pale result; too late with a strong solution on fair skin = orange.

## Verify
- [ ] Solution strength chosen from skin type, not client request alone.
- [ ] Barrier cream applied to dry areas; eyes/nose/lips protected; booth extraction on.
- [ ] Even coverage visible via guide colour under good light, front, back and sides.
- [ ] Aftercare with rinse time given in writing.
