# Superficial peel selection, application and aftercare

**Goal:** controlled exfoliation of the outer skin (stratum corneum and upper
epidermis) for dullness, mild congestion, texture and superficial pigment — without
burns, blotchy pigment or cold-sore outbreaks. **Medium and deep peels (e.g. higher
strength TCA, phenol) are medical procedures** and outside esthetic scope; where the
line falls (acid %, pH) is set by local law and your insurer, and varies — check yours.

## 1. Choose the peel
Strength depends on **acid type, concentration, pH (free acid)** and contact time —
not concentration alone. A 30% glycolic at pH 3.5 can be milder than a 20% at pH 1.5.
| Acid | Character | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| Lactic (AHA) | Larger molecule than glycolic, hydrating | Dry, dull, first-timers, sensitive-ish |
| Mandelic (AHA) | Large molecule, slow penetration | Higher Fitzpatrick types, acne-prone, sensitive |
| Glycolic (AHA) | Smallest AHA, fast, penetrates unevenly | Texture, photo-ageing on robust lighter skin |
| Salicylic (BHA) | Oil-soluble, self-limiting, anti-inflammatory | Oily, congested, acne-prone |
| Enzyme (papain, bromelain) | Very gentle | Reactive skin, pre-peel conditioning |
| Jessner's / blends | Stronger, can frost | Experienced users; check scope |
**Higher Fitzpatrick types (IV–VI):** larger-molecule acids, lower strengths, longer
pre-conditioning — PIH risk is much higher.

## 2. Pre-peel
- Contraindications: oral isotretinoin in the last ~6–12 months (guidance varies),
  topical retinoids/exfoliants in the last ~3–7 days, recent waxing/laser/injections in
  the area, sunburn, active cold sores (history of cold sores → client may need GP
  antiviral cover), broken skin, pregnancy (avoid higher-strength salicylic), aspirin
  allergy for salicylic. Timings are commonly taught values, product inserts win.
- **Patch test** in front of the ear 24–48 h before a first peel or new product.
- Pre-condition for ~2 weeks with low-strength home acid and daily SPF where advised.

## 3. Application
1. Cleanse, then **degrease** with the peel's prep solution (or alcohol/acetone per
   maker) — uneven oil = uneven penetration.
2. **Protect** nostrils, lip corners, eye area and any broken skin with occlusive balm.
   Eye pads on; have an eyewash and water bottle within reach.
3. Apply with a fan brush or gauze, **fast and even**, from the least sensitive area
   (forehead) to most (cheeks, then around eyes/mouth carefully).
4. **Start the timer.** Watch the skin, not just the clock: even pink erythema is
   expected; **white frosting** (protein coagulation) is a sign of depth — with an AHA
   at esthetic strength it usually means neutralise now.
5. Use a fan for comfort; ask for a 0–10 stinging score every minute. Above ~7, or
   any sharp burning, neutralise immediately.
6. **Neutralise** AHAs with the maker's neutraliser (often a bicarbonate solution) or
   plenty of cool water, as directed. Salicylic and many blends are self-neutralising —
   just remove residue.
7. Cool compress, soothing serum, **SPF 30+**.

## 4. Aftercare
- No other acids, retinoids, scrubs, waxing or heat for about 5–7 days.
- Bland cleanser, moisturiser, strict daily SPF; don't pick flaking skin.
- Series: superficial peels often 2–4 weeks apart in a course of 4–6.

## Gotchas
- **Hot spots:** areas recently shaved, waxed or exfoliated take the acid much faster.
- Layering a second coat doubles depth faster than you'd expect — only per protocol.
- Any blistering, dark grey patches or severe swelling: cool, neutralise, refer medically,
  document.

## Verify
- [ ] Acid chosen for skin type and Fitzpatrick type; patch test clear.
- [ ] Skin degreased evenly; sensitive areas protected.
- [ ] Timed, watched, neutralised at the right endpoint; no frost unless protocol intends it.
- [ ] Aftercare and SPF advice given in writing; reaction recorded.
