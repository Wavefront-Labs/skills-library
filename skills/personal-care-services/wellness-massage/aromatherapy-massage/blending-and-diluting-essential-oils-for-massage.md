# Blending and diluting essential oils for massage

**Goal:** make a massage blend the client actually likes, at a dilution that is safe
for whole-body skin contact, and avoid the three common harms: sensitisation, phototoxic
burns and oils that are inappropriate for pregnancy, children or certain conditions.
Aromatherapy massage is a wellbeing service — don't claim oils cure or treat anything.

## 1. Dilution maths
Essential oils are never applied neat in massage. Rough working figure: **1 drop ≈ 0.05 ml**,
so 20 drops ≈ 1 ml. (Drop size varies with dropper and oil viscosity — this is
approximate; use a scale or graduated pipette for precision.)

| Dilution | Drops per 10 ml carrier | Typical use |
|----------|-------------------------|-------------|
| 1% | ~2 | Face, sensitive skin, older clients, pregnancy (where oils are used at all) |
| 2% | ~4 | Standard adult full-body massage |
| 3% | ~6 | Small local area, short-term |
A full-body massage uses roughly **20–30 ml** of blend, so for 2% in 20 ml use ~8 drops
total — across all oils combined, not per oil.

## 2. Carriers
- **Sweet almond** — good glide, classic; **nut allergy risk**, ask.
- **Jojoba** (a liquid wax) — stable, doesn't go rancid quickly, good for faces.
- **Fractionated coconut** — light, non-greasy, long shelf life.
- **Grapeseed, sunflower** — inexpensive, oxidise faster; buy small.
Store blends in dark glass, label with date, oils and dilution; use within a few weeks.

## 3. Building the blend
1. **Consult:** what does the client want (calm, uplift, "clear head")? Which scents do
   they like or hate? Let them smell 2–4 candidate oils on a scent strip, not their skin.
2. **Choose 2–4 oils.** A loose structure: a fresh "top" note (citrus, eucalyptus), a
   "middle" (lavender, geranium, marjoram), a "base" (cedarwood, vetiver, frankincense).
   Popular relaxing choices: lavender, sweet marjoram, Roman chamomile, cedarwood.
3. **Ratio:** e.g. 2% in 20 ml = 8 drops → 3 lavender, 3 marjoram, 2 cedarwood.
4. Mix in a small dish or bottle, re-smell, adjust.

## 4. Safety rules you must know
- **Phototoxic oils:** cold-pressed bergamot, lime (expressed), lemon (expressed), bitter
  orange, grapefruit at higher amounts — can cause burns/pigmentation if skin is exposed
  to sun or UV within ~12–18 h. Use "bergaptene-free" (FCF) bergamot, distilled lime, or
  keep under the recommended maximum, and warn the client.
- **Sensitisers:** oxidised oils (old citrus, pine, tea tree) and oils rich in certain
  aldehydes (cinnamon bark, lemongrass, may chang) — avoid in massage or use at very low
  levels. Old oils are a common cause of reactions: check dates, smell for "off" notes.
- **Pregnancy, babies/young children, epilepsy, asthma, people on multiple medications:**
  many oils have specific restrictions — use a recognised safety reference (e.g. Tisserand
  & Young's *Essential Oil Safety*) or skip essential oils entirely. Peppermint and
  eucalyptus near the face of young children are a known no.
- **Patch test** for new clients with sensitive skin: a little of the diluted blend on the
  inner forearm, check after ~24–48 h. Not always practical — at minimum ask about
  previous reactions.

## 5. In session
Warm the blend in your palms; keep the bottle out of the client's sightline but
ready. Ventilate between clients — a room soaked in oil vapour gives the next client
(and you) headaches. Wash hands before touching your own face.

## Gotchas
- "Natural" ≠ safe. Neat lavender on skin is still a sensitisation risk over time —
  therapists themselves develop contact dermatitis from daily exposure.
- Drop counts per oil add up — 8 drops total, not 8 of each.
- Labelling matters: botanical (Latin) name, chemotype where relevant, supplier batch.
- Keep oils away from eyes; if it happens, flush with carrier oil or milk then water,
  not water alone (oil doesn't mix with water).

## Verify
- [ ] Allergies (nuts, fragrance) and pregnancy/medical status asked before choosing oils.
- [ ] Total drop count matches the intended % for the volume.
- [ ] No unprotected phototoxic citrus if client will be in sun; warned if used.
- [ ] Blend labelled with oils, %, date; oils within shelf life.
- [ ] Client chose or approved the scent before it touched their skin.
