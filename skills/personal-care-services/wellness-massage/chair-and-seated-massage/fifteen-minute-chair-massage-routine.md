# Fifteen-minute chair massage routine (workplace and events)

**Goal:** deliver a consistent, clothed, oil-free 10–20 minute massage in a portable
massage chair — mostly upper back, shoulders, neck, arms and hands — that leaves people
relaxed but able to return to work, and keep a line of clients moving on schedule.

## 1. The chair
- Adjust **seat height** so the client's knees are slightly lower than hips and their
  chest rests fully on the chest pad. **Face cradle** angled so the neck is neutral —
  not flexed down or cranked up. The client should be able to breathe easily through
  the cradle. **Armrest** at a height where shoulders drop and relax.
- Place it where you can walk all the way round. Check the locks/knobs are tight
  before each client sits — a collapsing chair is a real incident.
- Face-cradle covers (disposable or wipe-clean) for each client.

## 2. Thirty-second intake
Short form or verbal: any injuries, surgery, pregnancy, neck problems, recent illness,
areas to avoid, and pressure preference. Skip anyone feeling unwell. Ask them to
remove glasses, lanyards, earrings that dig in, and empty chest pockets. Long hair:
offer a clip.

## 3. A 15-minute routine (clothed, no oil)
| Min | Region | Technique |
|-----|--------|-----------|
| 0–1 | Whole back | Connect: hands on shoulders, a few slow palm compressions down the back |
| 1–5 | Upper and mid back | Palm and forearm compressions either side of the spine (not on it); kneading of upper trapezius |
| 5–8 | Shoulders | Squeezing/kneading tops of shoulders, thumb work around the shoulder blade edge, forearm press on top of shoulder |
| 8–10 | Low back | Light palm compressions — the lumbar area is compressed in the chair; keep it gentle |
| 10–12 | Arms and hands | Squeeze down the arm, thumb circles in the palm, gentle finger traction |
| 12–14 | Neck and scalp | Gentle squeezes of the back of the neck, fingertip scalp work |
| 14–15 | Close | Light tapotement down the back to wake them, finish with still hands on shoulders |
Adapt: 10-minute slots drop arms; 20-minute slots extend shoulders and neck.

## 4. Technique through clothing
- Use **compression and kneading**, not gliding — gliding drags fabric.
- Lean in from your legs; the chair puts the client's back at roughly your waist-to-chest
  height, so kneeling on one knee or lunging helps for low-back work.
- Avoid deep thumb work in the neck — the client is in a fixed position and can't
  easily move away.

## 5. Hygiene and flow
- Wipe the face cradle, armrest and chest pad with an appropriate disinfectant between
  every client; fresh cover. Sanitise or wash hands between clients.
- Sign-up sheet or booking app in slots (15 min booked = ~12–13 min massage + turnover).
- Have water and a small mirror for people fixing their hair.

## 6. Ending well
Bring them up slowly: hand on the back, tell them to sit up in their own time —
standing straight up from a face-down position can make people lightheaded. Keep
aftercare short: drink water, roll shoulders.

## Gotchas
- **Over-deep pressure in a corporate setting** sends people back to their desks sore.
  Medium pressure is the default.
- Don't put pressure on the spine or the front/side of the neck.
- Pregnancy: the chest pad position may be unsuitable later in pregnancy — offer to work
  seated upright in a regular chair or skip.
- Employers sometimes ask for feedback forms; never share individual health info from
  intake with them.
- Back-to-back sessions for hours are hard on your hands: rotate techniques, use
  forearms, schedule breaks every ~2 hours.

## Verify
- [ ] Chair fitted to each client — neutral neck, relaxed shoulders, locks checked.
- [ ] Intake asked, unsuitable clients deferred politely.
- [ ] Routine covered back, shoulders, arms and neck within the slot with time to turn over.
- [ ] Surfaces disinfected and cradle cover replaced between every client.
- [ ] Clients rose slowly; no one left dizzy or sore.
