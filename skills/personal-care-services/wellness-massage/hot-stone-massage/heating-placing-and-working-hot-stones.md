# Heating, placing and working hot stones — warmth without burns

**Goal:** use heated basalt (and optional chilled marble) stones to deepen relaxation,
placing static stones and gliding with working stones, without ever burning the client.
Burns are the main liability in this modality and they happen to experienced
therapists who skip the temperature routine.

## 1. Kit
- **Stones:** smooth basalt (holds heat) in sets — large flat placement stones for the
  back/abdomen, palm-size working stones, small toe stones. Marble or other light stones
  for cold work.
- **Heater:** a purpose-made stone heater or a roaster with a lid, filled with water so
  stones are fully covered; a towel or mat on the base so stones don't sit on the
  element.
- **Digital thermometer** (do not trust the dial), slotted spoon or net, bowl of cool
  water, towels, extra oil.

## 2. Temperature — the non-negotiable routine
- Typical water temperature is about **50–55 °C (120–130 °F)**. Some trainings use
  slightly higher for experienced use; treat ~54 °C as a practical ceiling for direct-on-skin
  work. *(Figures vary between trainings — check your course and insurer.)*
- Heating from cold takes roughly 30–60 minutes; prepare before the client arrives.
- **Test every stone** on the inside of your own forearm before it touches the client.
  If you can't hold it comfortably in your hand, it is too hot.
- **Never place a stone directly on skin straight from the heater**; keep it moving in
  your hands, or place it over a sheet or towel. Static stones placed under or on the client
  go **over fabric**.
- Re-check temperature during the session; heaters drift up when stones are removed.

## 3. Session flow
1. Intake and screening (see contraindications below).
2. **Placement layout:** with the client supine, a line of stones under a towel along
   either side of the spine (placed on the table, client lies on them) and one under the
   sacrum; stones in palms, between toes (wrapped or over a sheet), and a covered stone
   on the abdomen. Ask about heat every time you add a stone.
3. **Working strokes:** oil the area; hold a stone flat in the palm and use it as an
   extension of the hand for effleurage and gentle petrissage. Begin with the stone's
   flat face and quick passes, slowing as it cools. Swap for a fresh stone as soon as it
   cools to skin temperature.
4. **Cold stones (optional):** brief passes over the face or tired areas for contrast.
   Do not alternate hot and cold aggressively in a relaxation session.
5. Remove static stones before turning the client; count them in and out.

## 4. Contraindications and cautions
Avoid or get medical clearance for: diabetes or any condition reducing sensation,
circulatory disorders, high or low blood pressure issues, pregnancy, recent sunburn or
inflamed skin, taking blood thinners, recent injury, varicose veins, and clients who
have had alcohol. Reduced sensation is the big one — a client who can't feel the heat
can't warn you.

## 5. Cleaning
After each client: wash stones in hot soapy water, rinse, then disinfect with a
product approved for your setting; dry. Oil residue in the heater goes rancid — drain
and clean it daily. Re-oil basalt occasionally to keep the surface from drying out.

## Gotchas
- Client says "it's fine" to be polite — watch for redness that doesn't fade within a
  few minutes; that is a warning sign.
- Stones placed under the client heat up the skin more than gliding stones because
  body weight presses them in — always over a towel.
- Dropped stones crack and chip; a chipped edge scratches — retire it.
- Don't let stones clank together near the client's ear; it shatters the mood.

## Verify
- [ ] Water temperature measured with a digital thermometer, within your training's range.
- [ ] Every stone tested on your own forearm before use.
- [ ] Static stones were over fabric; heat checked with the client after each placement.
- [ ] No lingering redness on the skin at the end.
- [ ] Stones counted out, cleaned, disinfected; heater drained if end of day.
