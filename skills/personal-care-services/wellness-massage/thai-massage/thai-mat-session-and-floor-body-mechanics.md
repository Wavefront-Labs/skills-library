# Thai mat session and floor body mechanics

**Goal:** give a flowing, clothed, mat-based Thai massage (nuad Thai) — rhythmic
compression and assisted stretching — using your body weight and leverage instead of
arm strength, and never forcing a joint past where the client can comfortably go.

## 1. Setup
- Firm futon or Thai mat (~5–8 cm thick) on the floor, enough space to walk all round it.
- Client in loose, comfortable clothes; no oil. You also wear loose clothes you can kneel
  and lunge in.
- Pillows or bolsters for side-lying and under knees.
- Session length is traditionally long — 90–120 minutes is common; 60 minutes requires
  dropping sections, not rushing them.

## 2. Compression technique
- **Palm press:** heel of the hand, arms straight, lean in from the hips. Walk palms
  alternately ("palm walking") along the legs, back and arms.
- **Thumb press:** for more specific lines; thumbs together or thumb-over-thumb, lean in,
  hold ~1–3 s, release, move a thumb-width. Use sparingly to protect your thumbs.
- **Sen lines:** traditional energy lines (commonly ten "main" lines in modern Thai
  teaching) used as routes for pressure. Teaching of their exact paths differs by school
  (Wat Pho, Old Medicine Hospital/Chiang Mai, etc.). Describe them to clients as
  traditional pathways, not anatomy.
- Rhythm: slow, rocking, like breathing. Press on the client's exhale for deeper work.

## 3. Assisted stretches (core repertoire)
Typical order: feet and legs supine → side-lying → prone → sitting.
- **Single-leg hamstring stretch:** client supine, lift a straight leg, support the heel
  on your shoulder or hand, lean slowly.
- **Hip opener ("tree" position):** sole of one foot against the other knee, gentle
  palm pressure down on the bent knee.
- **Spinal twist (supine):** knee across the body, opposite shoulder held down.
- **Cobra-style backbend (prone):** gripping wrists/forearms, lean back to lift the chest —
  advanced; only for flexible clients without back issues.
- **Seated shoulder/neck work** to close.
Hold stretches ~10–30 s with slow breathing; enter and leave slowly.

## 4. Body mechanics — the heart of Thai work
- **Use gravity:** position above the client and lean; straight arms transmit weight
  without effort.
- **Use your feet:** foot-pressing on legs is traditional and saves hands (only once
  trained, and with your weight controlled through your standing/kneeling leg).
- **Stay close**; reaching strains the low back. Move around the mat instead.
- Kneel on a soft surface or use a knee pad if your knees complain.

## 5. Safety and screening
- Avoid or modify for: pregnancy (many stretches inappropriate), osteoporosis, recent
  surgery or fractures, joint replacements (especially hips — avoid deep flexion and
  rotation), herniated discs, uncontrolled high blood pressure, varicose veins (no
  pressure on them), and **hypermobile** clients — they can be pushed too far without
  feeling much warning.
- Stretch to the client's comfortable end range, not further. Pain, held breath or
  pulling away = back off.
- Avoid sustained pressure on the inner thigh's femoral artery area for long (some
  traditional "blood stop" techniques do this — not recommended in modern wellness
  practice) and on the front of the neck.

## Gotchas
- Thai massage is often marketed as "lazy yoga" — clients expect stretches; the
  compression work is equally important.
- Strength ≠ depth; if your arms are tired you're muscling, not leaning.
- Hands on both sides of a joint when moving a limb: support, then move.
- Unclear communication through a language barrier is common in this tradition; agree on
  simple signals ("stop", "less") before starting.
