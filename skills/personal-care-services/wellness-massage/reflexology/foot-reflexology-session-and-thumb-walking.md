# Foot reflexology session and thumb-walking

**Goal:** give a relaxing, structured reflexology session on the feet (or hands) using
correct thumb- and finger-walking, working through the traditional reflex map in a
logical order, and describe it honestly: reflexology is a relaxation practice; there is
no good evidence that pressing a foot area treats the organ it is mapped to. Never
diagnose ("this tender spot means your kidneys…").

## 1. Setup and inspection
- Client reclining (reflexology chair or massage table with a bolster under the knees),
  feet at your chest height, you seated.
- **Inspect the feet first:** verrucas, athlete's foot, open cuts, swelling, bruising,
  hot red areas. Fungal infections and verrucas — cover or avoid the area, or postpone;
  unexplained swelling, especially one-sided with calf pain, means don't work and refer
  (possible DVT).
- Cleanse with a wipe or warm towel. Use little or no medium — a light powder or balm.
  Oil makes thumb-walking slip.

## 2. Thumb-walking technique
- Bend the thumb at the first joint (the one nearest the nail), press with the outer edge
  of the thumb pad, then partially straighten and re-bend to inch forward — a
  caterpillar motion of a few millimetres per step.
- The **other hand supports and holds** the foot, giving resistance; most of the work is
  leverage between the hands, not thumb strength.
- Always walk **forward**, never backward; lift and reposition to change direction.
- **Finger-walking** — the same motion with the index finger — for the top of the foot
  and bony areas.
- **Rotating on a point:** hook in with the thumb and rotate the foot onto it for deeper,
  specific pressure.

## 3. A typical session (45–60 min)
1. **Relaxation openers (5–10 min):** ankle rotations, foot rocking ("windscreen wiper"
   between palms), spinal twist (wringing the foot gently), metatarsal kneading.
2. **Work the right foot, then the left** (some traditions start left; consistency
   matters). On each foot, a common order follows the traditional map:
   - **Toes** → head/neck reflexes (tips = head, base = neck).
   - **Ball of the foot** → chest/lung area.
   - **Arch** (upper then lower) → digestive organs.
   - **Heel** → pelvic area.
   - **Inner edge (medial) of the foot** → spine, worked heel to big toe.
   - **Outer edge** → shoulder, hip, knee.
   - **Top of the foot** → lymphatic/chest area, finger-walked.
3. **Solar plexus hold** (just below the ball of the foot, centre) with both thumbs on
   both feet at once — a calming close.
4. Finish with slow strokes from toes to ankle, then cover the feet with a towel.

## 4. Pressure and communication
Firm but comfortable — tender spots are common (often just mechanical: calluses,
tight fascia, shoes). Ease off and keep moving. Ticklish clients do better with
firmer, slower contact.

## 5. Contraindications and cautions
Avoid or seek clearance for: suspected DVT or recent clot, acute infection/fever,
feet with open wounds or infection, recent foot/ankle injury or surgery, unstable
epilepsy, and early pregnancy (many practitioners avoid the first trimester as a
precaution despite limited evidence). Diabetic clients: gentle pressure only, inspect
carefully, and check for reduced sensation.

## Gotchas
- **Don't over-claim.** Advertising reflexology as treating named conditions can breach
  advertising rules (e.g. the UK's ASA has ruled against such claims). Say "relaxation",
  "wellbeing", "feeling of calm".
- Long nails ruin thumb-walking — keep them short.
- Thumb fatigue → alternate with knuckles and finger-walking; keep the thumb's knuckle
  bent, not hyper-extended.
- Maps differ between schools (Ingham method vs others); stick with one and keep it
  consistent.

## Verify
- [ ] Feet inspected before contact; anything infected covered or avoided, swelling referred.
- [ ] Thumb-walked forward only, with the support hand doing its share.
- [ ] Both feet worked through the same map order; session closed with a calming hold.
- [ ] No diagnosis or treatment claims made during or after the session.
- [ ] Client relaxed (slower breathing, heavier feet) by the end.
