# Running a safe and compliant companionship service

**Goal:** an independent companion or small agency that takes bookings predictably,
travels safely, stays inside the law where it operates, and keeps clean records.
Professional/industry reference; not legal advice.

## 1. Decide what the service legally is
- **Social companionship only** (time and company, no sexual services) is lawful in
  most places, but authorities and platforms may still treat "escort" advertising as
  sex-work advertising. Wording, platform and jurisdiction all matter.
- Where sexual services may be part of the arrangement, the **sex-work framework**
  applies (see sex-work-practice in this field). In many partial-criminalisation regimes
  the agency, driver or booker is the one committing the offence (third-party liability).
- Some places license escort agencies or require operator certificates (e.g. New Zealand
  requires a certificate to operate a business with more than four workers — verify
  current rules).

## 2. Advertising
- Know platform rules and local law; in the US, FOSTA-SESTA made many platforms remove
  adult ads, and payment processors may refuse adult-adjacent businesses.
- Advertise only truthful, recent photos and an accurate service description; misleading
  ads cause disputes and can breach consumer law.
- Never use images or names of others without consent.

## 3. Booking policy (publish it)
- Minimum notice, rates by duration, what's included (e.g. dinner bills paid by client),
  deposit amount and method, cancellation and no-show terms, overtime rules.
- **Deposits** for new clients and for any travel; full prepayment for travel bookings
  (flights and hotel booked in your name or refundable).
- Screening requirements stated up front (see boundaries-and-screening).

## 4. Outcall and travel safety
- Outcalls: verify the hotel/address and room in advance where possible; buddy holds
  address, time, client details and check-in time.
- Social settings: agree the itinerary in advance; any change of venue is your call.
- **Travel bookings:** your own return ticket and accommodation access; money for a
  taxi home; local emergency number and embassy contact if abroad; check the destination's
  law — some countries criminalise conduct that is lawful at home, and immigration
  officials may refuse entry to people suspected of travelling for sex work.
- Alcohol: decide your own limit beforehand; keep your drink in sight.

## 5. Agencies: contracts and status
- Read the contract for fees/commission, exclusivity, who owns the client list, image
  rights, and whether you can refuse any client (you must be able to).
- Employment status (employee vs independent contractor) affects rights; in
  decriminalised settings workers have used employment law against operators (uncertain:
  outcomes depend on jurisdiction).
- Warning signs: fines, debt for "advertising" or "accommodation", holding of ID, being
  unable to refuse bookings — these point toward exploitation.

## 6. Money, records, privacy
- Keep a booking log (date, duration, fee, method) and pay tax where required.
- Separate work phone, email, bank account and social accounts.
- Client discretion is part of the service: no names, photos or details shared.
