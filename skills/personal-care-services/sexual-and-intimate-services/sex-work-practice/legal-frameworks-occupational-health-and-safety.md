# Sex work: legal frameworks, occupational health and safety

**Goal:** an adult who has chosen sex work understands the law where they work, runs
routines that protect their health, safety and income, and knows where to get support.
This is an occupational-safety reference in the style of sex-worker-led and public-health
organisations — it contains no sexual instruction and is not legal advice.

## 1. Know the legal framework you are in
The same activity can be lawful, licensed, partly criminalised or fully criminalised
depending on place. The main models (check current local law — it changes):
| Model | What it means in practice | Examples (as of writing; verify) |
|---|---|---|
| **Decriminalisation** | Treated as work under ordinary employment, health and safety law | New Zealand (PRA 2003), New South Wales, Victoria, Northern Territory, Belgium |
| **Legalisation / regulation** | Lawful only within a licensing or registration regime; working outside it is an offence | Germany (ProstSchG registration), Netherlands, licensed Nevada brothels |
| **Partial criminalisation / "Nordic model"** | Selling not an offence, but buying, third-party involvement, and often working together are | Sweden, Norway, France, Ireland, Canada (PCEPA) |
| **Full criminalisation** | Selling and buying are offences | Most US states and many other countries |
Points people commonly miss:
- Under many partial-criminalisation regimes, **two workers sharing premises** for safety
  can count as a brothel, and a landlord or driver can be charged as a third party.
- Advertising law is separate: e.g. US FOSTA-SESTA (2018) led many platforms to drop
  adult ads; payment processors and banks may close accounts regardless of local law.
- Migrant workers can face immigration consequences even where the work itself is lawful
  (New Zealand's PRA excludes temporary-visa holders, for example).
Get advice from a sex-worker-led organisation or a lawyer who works with them.

## 2. Sexual and general health routines
- Barrier methods used consistently; keep supplies stocked and in date.
- **Regular STI testing** on a schedule agreed with a sexual-health clinic (many offer
  confidential or worker-specific services); HIV **PrEP** where appropriate.
- Know the **PEP** window: post-exposure prophylaxis must start as soon as possible and
  within 72 hours — know where to get it out of hours.
- Vaccinations: hepatitis B and HPV are commonly recommended; ask the clinic.
- Mental health and burnout are occupational risks; peer support helps.

## 3. Safety systems
- **Screening** before meeting (see boundaries-and-screening in this field).
- **Buddy/check-in system:** someone trusted knows location, time and client details,
  with an agreed check-in time and a plan if you miss it.
- Check **bad-date / "ugly mugs" alert lists** and report incidents to them; in the UK,
  National Ugly Mugs, elsewhere local sex-worker organisations run equivalents.
- At outcalls/incalls: know exits, keep the phone charged and reachable, control the
  money conversation before anything else, trust a bad feeling and leave.
- Violence and robbery can be reported to police; many organisations offer accompanied
  or third-party reporting where workers fear police contact.

## 4. Money and records
- Keep income records and pay tax where required — in decriminalised and legalised
  settings this is expected, and records protect you in disputes.
- Separate work and personal phone numbers, email and payment accounts.
- Uncertain: how banks and tax authorities treat this income varies widely; get local advice.

## 5. Coercion and trafficking
Sex work by consenting adults is distinct from trafficking and exploitation. Signs a
person is not working freely: someone else holds their ID or money, they can't refuse
clients, they owe an unpayable "debt", they live where they work under control. Point
them to anti-trafficking hotlines and sex-worker organisations, which are often the
first to spot exploitation.

## Verify
- [ ] You can state which legal model applies where you work and what it makes risky.
- [ ] Testing schedule, PrEP/PEP access and supplies are in place.
- [ ] A buddy/check-in system runs for every booking.
- [ ] Bad-date list checked and incidents reported.
- [ ] Work identity, money and records are separated and kept.
