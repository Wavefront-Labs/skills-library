# Negotiating consent and limits with clients

**Goal:** both parties know, before anything starts, what will and won't happen; either
can pause or stop at any time without friction; and nothing proceeds when one party
can't genuinely agree. Applies to paid intimate, touch-based and companionship work.
Written as professional communication practice — no sexual content.

## 1. The standard to meet
A widely used checklist (FRIES): consent is **Freely given** (no pressure, debt or
threat), **Reversible** (can be withdrawn at any moment), **Informed** (both know what
is proposed, including risks and any recording), **Enthusiastic** (a real yes, not
silence or reluctance) and **Specific** (yes to one thing is not yes to others).
It applies to *both* parties: the professional's limits count as much as the client's.

## 2. Before the session: the negotiation
1. **Restate the service frame** — what this booking includes and excludes (from your
   written policy). Do this before payment where possible.
2. **Ask about theirs:** "Is there anything you don't want, or anywhere you don't want
   to be touched? Any injuries, health conditions or triggers I should know about?"
3. **Yes / no / maybe:** for structured work, a short list sorted into yes, no and
   "only if we check in first" prevents assumptions.
4. **Agree stop signals** — a plain word ("stop", "pause") or a traffic-light system
   (green = fine, yellow = slow down/check, red = stop now); a non-verbal signal if
   speech may be difficult.
5. Confirm anything about **recording, photography or third parties** explicitly —
   default is no.
Keep it matter-of-fact. A calm, routine negotiation signals professionalism and makes
pushback stand out.

## 3. During: check-ins
- Brief, regular, neutral: "Still good?" / "Do you want to keep going?"
- Watch non-verbal cues — freezing, going quiet, pulling away — and check in rather than
  assume. Silence is not consent.
- **Any change** from what was agreed needs a new, specific yes. Don't renegotiate
  mid-session under pressure; "not today" is a complete answer.

## 4. Withdrawal of consent
- Stop immediately when either party signals it. No sulking, no persuasion, no penalty
  in tone.
- Professional to client: "I'm going to stop there. Let's take a moment." Then decide
  whether to resume with a new agreement or end.
- Refunds and partial payment should be pre-set in policy so stopping is never a money
  argument in the moment.

## 5. Capacity
Consent requires capacity. Do not proceed if someone is heavily intoxicated, appears
confused, or you suspect they are under the age of majority or being directed by someone
else. Age and identity verification sits in screening (see boundaries-and-screening).

## 6. Afterwards
- Short debrief: how was that, anything to change next time.
- For therapeutic modalities (e.g. surrogate partner therapy, sexological bodywork),
  note agreements and changes in session records kept to that profession's standards,
  and share with the supervising clinician where the model requires it.
- Note anything that felt off for your own records and screening.

## Gotchas
- Power imbalance runs both ways: paying clients may feel entitled; professionals may
  feel pressure to please for income. Policy defends both.
- Cultural and neurodivergent communication differences — be explicit, not hint-based.
- Language barriers: don't proceed on assumed understanding.

## Verify
- [ ] Inclusions, exclusions and stop signals agreed before starting.
- [ ] Recording/third parties explicitly addressed.
- [ ] Check-ins happened; any change got a new specific yes.
- [ ] A stop was honoured instantly with no penalty.
- [ ] Capacity doubts led to not proceeding.
