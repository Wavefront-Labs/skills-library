# Client screening and boundary setting

**Goal:** fewer unsafe bookings, clear expectations before any meeting, and a practised
way to end a session that goes wrong. Applies across paid intimate and companionship
work; written as safety practice, not legal advice — screening methods themselves can be
legally sensitive where the work is criminalised (see sex-work-practice).

## 1. Put your boundaries in writing first
- A short **policy page**: services offered in neutral terms, duration and rates,
  what is never offered, deposit and cancellation terms, hygiene expectations, and
  that intoxicated or abusive clients will be refused.
- Written boundaries do three jobs: they filter people who won't respect them, they
  make "no" impersonal ("that's not something I offer"), and they give you a record.
- Decide your non-negotiables while calm, not mid-booking.

## 2. Screening methods (use more than one)
| Method | What it checks | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Real name + verifiable info (e.g. work email, LinkedIn) | Identity | Store securely; delete per your policy |
| **Provider references** | History with other workers | Ask the reference directly through a known channel, not via the client |
| Deposit | Commitment, reduces no-shows | Use payment methods that don't expose your legal name |
| Phone/video call | Voice, demeanour, consistency | Short; note how they handle your rules |
| **Bad-date / ugly mugs list** check | Known dangerous people | Check number, email and description |
| Industry verification services | Varies by region | Legality and reliability vary — uncertain, research locally |

## 3. Red flags
- Pushes against stated policy before meeting ("just this once", haggling, asking for
  things you don't offer).
- Refuses any verification, or gives details that don't match.
- Insists on a location change at the last minute, or wants you to come to a remote or
  unknown place.
- Pressure about condom use or substances.
- Excessive personal questions about where you live or your real name.
- Gut feeling — it is data. Declining needs no reason.

## 4. On the day
- Buddy knows where, when, who, and the check-in time; agree a code word for "come now /
  call police".
- Collect payment at the start, out of the way of the rest of the meeting.
- Restate boundaries briefly; ask about theirs too (see consent-communication).
- Keep phone, keys and clothes within reach; know the exits.

## 5. Ending a booking that's going wrong
- Use a pre-planned, calm script: "I'm ending our session now." No debate.
- Move toward the exit or ask them to leave; your buddy's code word if needed.
- Afterwards: write down details while fresh, report to the bad-date list, consider
  police or third-party reporting, and talk to someone — incidents carry a mental load.

## 6. Privacy hygiene
- Separate work phone, email, payment and social accounts; strip photo metadata and
  avoid identifiable backgrounds.
- Reverse-image-search your own ads periodically for leaks.
- Don't keep more client data than you need, and secure what you keep.

## Verify
- [ ] Written policy exists and is shown before booking.
- [ ] At least two independent screening checks per new client.
- [ ] Bad-date list checked; buddy/check-in arranged every time.
- [ ] You have a rehearsed ending script and code word.
- [ ] Work and personal identities are separated.
