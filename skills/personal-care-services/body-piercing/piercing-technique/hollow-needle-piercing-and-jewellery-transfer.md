# Piercing technique — needle, clamp or freehand, and a clean transfer

**Goal:** pass a sterile, single-use hollow needle cleanly along the marked line and get the
jewellery in with minimum tissue trauma, every time.

This is a craft learned through a supervised apprenticeship; this skill sets out the method
and decision points, not a replacement for supervised practice. Numbers are typical, not rules.

## 1. Why a hollow needle, not a gun
- A **hollow, bevelled, single-use needle** cuts a small channel and removes a sliver of tissue;
  a stud "gun" pushes a blunt stud through, causing more blunt-force trauma, and many gun
  bodies can't be autoclaved. Professional bodies such as the APP advise against guns, and
  some jurisdictions restrict them to earlobes only.
- Needle gauge usually matches or is one size larger than the starting jewellery
  (e.g. 14 g needle for 14 g jewellery; some piercers go a half size up for easier transfer).

## 2. Preparation
- Hands washed, gloves on; sterile instruments and jewellery in sealed autoclave pouches with a
  passed indicator (see the autoclaves skill). Open in front of the client.
- **Skin prep:** clean the site with an antiseptic such as chlorhexidine or povidone-iodine per your
  protocol (check for iodine allergy), allowing it to dry.
- **Mark** entry and exit with a skin-safe marker, check from several angles and in a mirror with
  the client sitting upright (see anatomy-and-placement skill). Re-mark rather than accept a
  "close enough" dot.

## 3. Choosing a method
- **Freehand:** no clamp; you stabilise tissue with your fingers or with the client's natural
  tension. Least compression of tissue, best view of the marks, more skill required.
- **Clamp/forceps (e.g. Pennington, Foerster):** holds tissue still and guides the line. Easier
  for beginners and mobile tissue, but compresses and can distort the tissue, so placement may
  shift slightly when released. Some piercers use slotted clamps with the needle passed through.
- **Receiving tube:** a sterile tube held behind the exit point to receive the needle tip and
  protect the client and you (common for nostrils and some cartilage).
- **Needle blank / taper** for jewellery insertion where the needle can't carry the jewellery directly.

## 4. The pass
- Ask the client to breathe in, then pierce on a slow exhale — it relaxes them and keeps them still.
- One **smooth, steady, committed motion** along the line from entry to exit mark; hesitation or
  angle change mid-pass creates a crooked channel.
- Keep the needle on axis; check exit against your mark before going further.

## 5. Jewellery transfer
- **Butt the jewellery against the back of the needle** (threadless/push-pin ends or
  internally threaded bars fit into the needle's hollow end) and follow the needle through in
  one continuous motion, keeping contact so the channel isn't lost.
- Use a **fully sterile insertion taper** if needed; never force jewellery in.
- Close the jewellery: threadless ends should be firmly seated with the pin slightly bent per
  the manufacturer's instructions; check it can't be pulled out by hand.
- Clean off blood with sterile saline gauze. Dispose of the needle immediately into sharps.

## 6. Common situation notes
- **Earlobes:** soft, forgiving; freehand or with a clamp. Sit or lie the client down.
- **Cartilage (helix, conch, tragus):** firm, more pressure needed; often receiving-tube or
  cork-backed approach; swelling is greater — initial jewellery longer.
- **Nostril:** receiving tube inside the nostril; L-shapes or threadless studs with a flat disc back.
- **Septum:** pierce the "sweet spot" — the thin membrane just in front of the cartilage — not
  the cartilage itself.

## Gotchas
- Fainting: always seat or lie the client down; have water and sugar available.
- Don't pierce clients who are intoxicated, under the legal age without required consent, or ill.
- Bending a threadless pin too much makes it impossible to change later; too little and it falls out.

## Verify
- [ ] Sterile single-use needle and autoclaved jewellery opened in front of the client.
- [ ] Marks checked from multiple angles with the client upright.
- [ ] One continuous pass; exit landed on the mark.
- [ ] Jewellery followed the needle without losing the channel; ends secure.
- [ ] Needle went straight into sharps; client seated and stable before leaving.
