# Piercing aftercare and troubleshooting — irritation vs infection, bumps and rejection

**Goal:** give aftercare a client will actually follow, then diagnose the common problems
correctly — mostly irritation, occasionally infection — and know when to refer to a doctor.

## 1. Aftercare that works
Keep it short and in writing:
- **Clean with sterile saline wound wash** (0.9% sodium chloride, spray can) 1-2 times a day.
  Spray, let it soak a moment, gently dry with clean paper or gauze. Home-mixed salt water is
  easy to get too strong; the sterile spray is more consistent.
- **Don't touch, twist or turn the jewellery.** Old advice to rotate jewellery is out of date; it
  drags crusts into the channel and tears healing tissue.
- Avoid: alcohol, hydrogen peroxide, antibacterial ointments, tea tree oil (frequent cause of
  irritation), makeup and hair products on the site.
- No soaking in pools, lakes, the sea, hot tubs or baths during early healing.
- **Protect from pressure:** don't sleep on new ear piercings (a travel pillow helps), avoid
  hats, headphones, tight waistbands over navels.
- "Crusties" (dried lymph) are normal; soften with saline and let them fall off.
- Typical healing: earlobes 6-8 weeks; nostril 4-6 months; cartilage 6-12 months; navel
  6-12 months; tongue 4-6 weeks (ranges commonly quoted; individuals vary).

## 2. Irritation vs infection
Most problems are irritation.
- **Normal early healing:** some redness, swelling, tenderness, clear-to-whitish fluid and
  crusting for the first days to weeks.
- **Irritation:** renewed redness/swelling or a bump after it had been calming down, often after
  trauma — knocking it, sleeping on it, a too-long bar, tight clothing, a change of products.
- **Possible infection — refer to a doctor:** spreading redness and heat, increasing (not
  decreasing) pain and swelling after a few days, thick yellow/green pus with a bad smell,
  red streaks, fever or feeling unwell.
- **Leave the jewellery in** while an infection is treated, unless a doctor says otherwise:
  removing it can let the surface close and trap the infection (an abscess). Cartilage
  infections are taken seriously — they can damage cartilage; refer promptly.

## 3. The "bump"
Irritation bumps are the commonest complaint, especially on nostrils and cartilage.
- **Usually an irritation (hypertrophic) bump**, sitting right next to the hole, often
  reddish or fluid-filled. Fix the cause and it typically shrinks over weeks:
  - downsize a too-long bar, which lets it move and catch;
  - swap a ring for a flat-backed labret stud;
  - stop sleeping on it, stop touching it, check jewellery quality and material;
  - keep the saline routine; stop extra products.
- **Keloids** are firm, grow beyond the wound and persist; they are less common, more likely
  with personal or family history, and need a dermatologist — don't promise home fixes.
- Avoid "bump remedies" like aspirin pastes or harsh oils; they add irritation.

## 4. Embedding, migration and rejection
- **Embedding:** swelling swallows the jewellery end or disc. Refer back to the piercer for a
  longer bar urgently; do not let the client push it out themselves.
- **Migration/rejection:** the piercing shifts toward the surface, skin thins, more jewellery
  shows. Remove the jewellery early to limit scarring, then let it heal (see the advanced-piercings skill).

## 5. When the client asks "can I change it now?"
Not until fully healed — and for cartilage, healing is longer than it looks; the outside
can look healed months before the channel is. First changes are best done in the studio.

## Gotchas
- A client using several products at once: strip back to saline only.
- Allergic reactions to low-grade jewellery look like irritation; check the material.
- Nose piercings: blowing the nose, glasses and masks are frequent hidden irritation sources.

## Verify
- [ ] Client has written aftercare: saline, hands off, no rotating, no soaking.
- [ ] Problem assessed against irritation vs infection signs; cause of irritation identified.
- [ ] Infection signs → referred to a doctor with jewellery left in unless told otherwise.
- [ ] Bumps handled by removing the cause (downsize, stud, pressure), not by remedies.
- [ ] Embedding or migration seen in the studio promptly.
