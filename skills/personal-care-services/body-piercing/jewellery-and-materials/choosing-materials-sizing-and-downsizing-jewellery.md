# Body jewellery: materials, sizing and fitting through healing

**Goal:** put only body-safe metal in a fresh piercing, sized for swelling at first and
downsized at the right time, then help the client change jewellery without damage.

## 1. Materials for initial piercings
Use materials with a recognised implant standard and ask suppliers for **mill certificates**
that tie the batch to the standard.
- **Implant-grade titanium** — Ti-6Al-4V ELI to **ASTM F136**, or unalloyed titanium to
  **ASTM F67**. The default for most initial piercings: light, nickel-free in practice,
  can be anodised to colours.
- **Implant-grade stainless steel** — **ASTM F138** (316LVM). It contains nickel bound in the
  alloy; most people tolerate it, but people with nickel allergy are better in titanium.
- **Niobium** — commonly used and well tolerated, but as far as I know it has no specific
  implant ASTM grade comparable to F136 (uncertain — check the APP's current list).
- **Solid gold** (14k or 18k, nickel-free, solid not plated). Avoid white gold that uses nickel.
- **Glass and some polymers** may be acceptable under some standards; check before using.
- **Avoid in fresh piercings:** plated or "surgical steel" with no grade, sterling silver
  (tarnishes and can stain tissue), acrylic, wood and organic materials, unknown metals.

Surface finish matters as much as alloy: threads, ends and gems should be **mirror-polished**
with no burrs or scratches that trap bacteria.

## 2. Gauge and size
- Gauges (AWG): 20 g ≈ 0.8 mm, 18 g ≈ 1.0 mm, 16 g ≈ 1.2 mm, 14 g ≈ 1.6 mm, 12 g ≈ 2.0 mm,
  10 g ≈ 2.5 mm. Smaller number = thicker.
- **Thicker is often better initially**: very thin jewellery can "cheese-cut" through tissue
  under load. Typical initials: earlobe and cartilage 16 g, nostril 18-20 g, navel/nipple/
  tongue 14 g (practice varies).
- **Wear length / diameter:** measure tissue thickness at the marks and add room for swelling.
  Tongues and lips swell markedly, so initial bars are long; navel and nipple need room too.
- Rings vs studs: for most cartilage and nostril piercings, studs/labrets heal better
  initially than rings, which move and rotate in the channel.

## 3. Threading types
- **Internally threaded:** the thread is on the end, not the bar, so no rough threads pass
  through the piercing. Standard for initials.
- **Threadless (push-pin):** a pin end held by tension from a slight bend; easy changes, very
  common for ear and nostril studs.
- **Externally threaded:** threads on the bar pass through and scrape the channel — avoid for
  fresh piercings.

## 4. Downsizing
- Once **initial swelling settles** (often 2-6 weeks for ear cartilage and lips; tongues often
  around 2-4 weeks — individual), swap the long post for a shorter one. Too-long posts snag
  and get pushed around, causing irritation bumps and angle changes; lip and tongue bars that
  are too long can damage teeth and gums.
- The piercer should do the first downsize; the client should not change jewellery themselves
  until the piercing is fully healed.

## 5. Jewellery changes
- Change only with clean hands or gloves, with jewellery that has been cleaned and, for
  a not-quite-healed piercing, sterilised.
- Use an insertion taper for stubborn changes; never force jewellery. If it won't go in
  easily, stop — the channel may be shrinking or misaligned.
- When stretching, increase one gauge at a time with adequate healing between steps;
  forcing causes blowouts and scarring.

## Gotchas
- "Surgical steel" without an ASTM/ISO grade means nothing; ask for the certificate.
- Anodised colour wears and is not a quality marker.
- Clients buying cheap jewellery online for healing piercings is the commonest cause of
  irritation complaints; tell them to bring it in to be checked.

## Verify
- [ ] Initial jewellery is a certified implant-grade material with a mill certificate.
- [ ] Finish is mirror-polished; internally threaded or threadless.
- [ ] Gauge and length measured on the client, allowing for swelling.
- [ ] Downsize appointment booked for when swelling should settle.
- [ ] Client knows not to change jewellery themselves until healed.
