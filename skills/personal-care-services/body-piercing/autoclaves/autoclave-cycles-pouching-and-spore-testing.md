# Autoclaves and sterilisation — clean, pouch, cycle, prove, store

**Goal:** turn used instruments and jewellery into **demonstrably sterile** items, with
records that prove every load passed.

Always follow your autoclave manual and local regulations, which may set the test frequency
and log format. Numbers here are common settings, not substitutes for the manual.

## 1. The chain (each step depends on the one before)
**Clean → rinse/dry → inspect → pouch → load → cycle → verify → dry/cool → store.**
Sterilisation fails if items are still dirty: steam can't penetrate dried blood or tissue.

## 2. Cleaning
- Wear heavy-duty gloves, eye protection and an apron; tools may still carry blood.
- Pre-soak or spray with an enzymatic cleaner so debris doesn't dry on.
- **Ultrasonic cleaner:** fill with the manufacturer's solution, **lid on** while running
  (aerosols), instruments open/unlocked, not stacked; typically 5-15 min cycles. Change the
  solution at least daily or when visibly dirty. Ultrasonics clean, they do **not** sterilise.
- Rinse, dry, and inspect under light: no residue, rust, cracks or stiff hinges.

## 3. Pouching and loading
- Use **self-seal pouches** sized so items aren't crammed; one item or one kit per pouch.
- Hinged tools open/unlocked; sharp tips protected so they don't pierce the pouch.
- Include a **chemical indicator** (most pouches have a process indicator printed on; add a
  **class 5 integrator** in the load for stronger evidence).
- Label each pouch with date, load/cycle number and, if used, your initials.
- **Load** paper side to plastic side, on edge or in a single layer with space between pouches,
  so steam can circulate; never overfill the chamber.

## 4. Cycle types
- **Class B (vacuum / pre-vacuum):** pulls air out before steam enters; suitable for pouched,
  hollow and porous items (e.g. hollow needles, tubes). The safest choice for body-art studios.
- **Class N (gravity displacement):** for **unwrapped solid** items only; air pockets in pouches
  and hollow items may not be removed. Not suitable for pouched studio loads.
- **Class S:** specific loads as validated by the maker (cassette units like a Statim fall here).
- Typical parameters: **134 °C for at least 3-4 minutes** or **121 °C for 15-20 minutes** of hold
  time, plus a drying phase. Use the cycle the maker validated for your load type.

## 5. Proving it worked
- **Every load:** check the printout/display (time, temperature, pressure) and the colour change
  on every pouch indicator and integrator. Record them in the log.
- **Daily (for vacuum autoclaves):** air-removal test such as a **Bowie-Dick** or **helix test**
  at the start of the day, per the manual.
- **Spore test (biological indicator):** a strip or vial of *Geobacillus stearothermophilus*
  spores run in a load and then incubated (in-house or by a mail-in lab). Frequency is often
  **weekly to monthly** depending on the jurisdiction — check yours. A failed spore test means:
  stop using the unit, recall items since the last passing test, re-sterilise, service the unit.
- Keep logs and spore-test results for as long as your regulator requires (often years).

## 6. Storage and use
- Let pouches **dry completely** inside the autoclave; a wet pouch wicks contamination and is not sterile.
- Store in a clean, dry, closed cabinet away from the work area.
- **Event-related sterility:** a pouch stays sterile until it's torn, wet, dropped on a dirty
  surface or opened — many studios also re-process after a set time as a policy.
- Open pouches in front of the client.

## Gotchas
- Using tap water where the manual says distilled/demineralised: scale builds and the
  unit fails.
- Pouching wet instruments or loading too tight: wet loads and failed cycles.
- Indicator changed colour ≠ sterile: it only shows that conditions were reached at that spot.
  Biological tests are what prove sterilisation.
- Never autoclave machines, plastics or anything the maker doesn't rate for it.

## Verify
- [ ] Items cleaned (ultrasonic, lid on), rinsed, dried and inspected before pouching.
- [ ] Vacuum (Class B) cycle used for pouched and hollow items.
- [ ] Every pouch indicator changed and the cycle printout is logged.
- [ ] Daily air-removal and periodic spore tests passed and recorded.
- [ ] Pouches fully dry, intact, dated and stored in a closed cabinet.
