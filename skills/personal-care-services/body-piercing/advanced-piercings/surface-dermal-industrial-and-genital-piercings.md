# Advanced piercings — surface work, dermal anchors, industrials and genital piercings

**Goal:** judge whether an advanced piercing has a realistic chance of healing, choose the
right jewellery style for it, and set honest expectations about migration, rejection and
healing time.

This assumes you are already competent at standard piercings (see piercing-technique and
anatomy-and-placement skills). Advanced work should be learned under a mentor; some of it
(e.g. dermal punches, genital work) is restricted by law or licence in some places.
Healing times are typical ranges, not promises.

## 1. Surface piercings
A surface piercing goes in and out of a **flat** area rather than through a fold or edge
(nape, hip, sternum, anti-eyebrow, Madison at the base of the throat).
- The key failure is **migration and rejection**: the body pushes the jewellery to the surface
  over months. Expect a meaningful rejection rate even with good work — tell the client up front.
- **Jewellery:** use **surface bars / staple bars** (bar with 90° bends so only the ends exit the
  skin), not straight or curved barbells, which exert pressure on the channel ends.
- Pierce **perpendicular to the skin's tension lines** where possible, and only where the tissue
  can be lifted into a good ridge; shallow placements reject.
- Avoid high-movement areas, waistbands, bra lines and places that are leaned on.
- Healing: often 6-12 months or more; may never become fully "stable".

## 2. Dermal anchors (microdermals, single-point piercings)
- A small base plate sits under the skin with a threaded post exiting through one hole; a top
  screws on.
- Placed with a **needle** (creating a pocket for the foot) or a **dermal punch** (biopsy-punch
  style). Punch use is restricted in some jurisdictions — check before using one.
- Use an anchor with a **holed or "fenestrated" foot** so tissue can grow through it.
- Expect: snagging is the main risk; tops get lost; anchors can reject or become embedded.
  **Removal** requires skill (massaging the anchor out, or a small cut by a medical professional
  if tissue grew through); explain this before placing one.
- Avoid hands, fingers and areas that flex a lot.

## 3. Industrials and ear projects
- Two cartilage holes joined by a long bar. The line must suit the ear (see the anatomy skill).
- Pierce each hole for the bar; some piercers start with two separate studs and fit the bar
  after healing to reduce trauma and improve alignment.
- Healing often 6-12 months; the bar is easily knocked, so warn about sleeping on it.

## 4. Genital piercings
- These need the same anatomy-first approach, a private and professional setting, chaperone
  options, explicit verbal and written consent, and absolute respect for the client's comfort.
- Suitability depends heavily on anatomy, e.g. a **vertical clitoral hood (VCH)** needs a hood
  with enough depth for a receiving tube or needle to pass behind it (the "Q-tip test" is the
  common check); a **Christina** sits on a surface-like area and has higher rejection rates; a
  **Prince Albert** passes through the urethra and bleeds more initially.
- Healing ranges from a few weeks (some VCH, PA) to many months (Christina).
- Aftercare includes guidance on hygiene, barrier protection during sex during healing, and
  urinating after cleaning with PA piercings — give it in writing.

## 5. Consent and expectations
- Written consent that includes: expected healing time, likelihood of migration or rejection,
  scarring if it rejects, and what removal involves.
- Book a check-up and agree how you'll handle early signs of rejection: removing jewellery
  early leaves a smaller scar than waiting for it to grow out.

## Signs of migration or rejection
- Jewellery visibly closer to the surface, more of the bar showing, thin, shiny or translucent
  skin over the bar, the holes getting larger, persistent redness without infection signs.
- Action: remove the jewellery before it grows out completely, then let it heal.

## Verify
- [ ] Anatomy assessed for a genuine ridge/fold; client informed of realistic rejection risk.
- [ ] Correct jewellery style (staple/surface bar, fenestrated dermal foot) chosen.
- [ ] Local rules checked for punches and genital piercing.
- [ ] Written consent includes healing time, rejection and removal.
- [ ] Follow-up booked and client knows the signs of migration.
