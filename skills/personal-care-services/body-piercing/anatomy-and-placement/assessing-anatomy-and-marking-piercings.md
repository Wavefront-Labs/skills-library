# Anatomy and placement — deciding whether, where and at what angle

**Goal:** read the client's anatomy honestly, place marks that sit well in the body's
natural lines, and say no when a piercing won't heal or won't look right.

Anatomy-dependent piercings fail more often from bad placement than bad technique.
The suitability checks below are common practice; individual anatomy always overrides.

## 1. The core questions for every piercing
1. **Is there enough tissue** to hold the jewellery without excess pressure? (Pinch test: a
   defined ridge, fold or flap that the jewellery can sit through comfortably.)
2. **Will the jewellery sit naturally** — flat to the body, not pressed against another
   structure or pulled by clothing, glasses, phones or sleeping position?
3. **What is under it?** Blood vessels, nerves, teeth/gums, cartilage vs soft tissue.
4. **Can the client care for it** (reach it, keep it clean, avoid snagging at work/sports)?

## 2. Marking method
- Mark with the client **sitting or standing upright and relaxed**, head straight. Lying down
  shifts tissue (especially navels and nipples).
- Use a fine single-use skin marker or a sterile toothpick with gentian violet.
- Check marks from front, side, and in a mirror; ask the client to approve before you pierce.
- For pairs (nipples, nostrils, snakebites), measure symmetry, but place to the face/body,
  not just the ruler — most people are asymmetrical.

## 3. Placement notes for common sites
- **Navel:** suitable when there is a defined upper rim/ridge that doesn't collapse when the
  client sits. If the navel folds closed when sitting, a standard navel piercing is under
  pressure and likely to migrate — decline or suggest a floating navel design only if anatomy
  supports it.
- **Daith / rook / snug / conch:** need a defined fold of cartilage; check the jewellery won't
  be crowded by the tragus or antihelix.
- **Industrial:** the two holes must be in line *for that ear*; if the bar has to bend the ear
  or press on the anti-helix, decline or suggest two separate helix piercings.
- **Tragus:** enough thickness and a tragus that projects; small, flat tragi don't hold well.
- **Septum:** feel for the **"sweet spot"** — the thin membrane between the cartilage and the
  nostril tip. A deviated septum may shift it; the piercing must be through the sweet spot, not cartilage.
- **Nostril:** place on the crease of the nostril so the stud sits flat; account for the
  internal wall thickness.
- **Tongue (centre):** look under the tongue at the frenulum and the large veins either side;
  the piercing must sit centrally and clear of them. A short or tight frenulum (tongue-tie) may make it unsuitable.
- **Lip/labret:** consider gum and tooth contact; the disc behind will rub — initial post longer
  for swelling, downsized later. Warn about gum recession and enamel wear.
- **Eyebrow and surface sites:** need tissue that can be pinched perpendicular to the surface;
  shallow tissue = higher rejection (see advanced-piercings skill).
- **Nipple:** pierce at the base of the nipple, horizontally or vertically; clients must be told
  about possible effects on future breastfeeding (evidence is mixed).

## 4. Saying no
Decline (or redirect) when:
- There's not enough tissue or the jewellery would be under constant pressure.
- The client's lifestyle (contact sports, occupational helmets, CPAP masks, uniforms) will
  constantly traumatise the site.
- Skin at the site is broken, sunburnt, scarred or keloid-prone.
- The client is intoxicated, unwell, pregnant (many studios decline navel/nipple), or under age
  without the legally required consent.
Explain the reason and offer an alternative; a declined piercing is better than a migrated one.

## Gotchas
- Earrings, glasses and headsets: ask what the client wears every day.
- Marking with the client lying down: navels and nipples move when they sit up.
- Industrial bars that look fine on day one tilt and press within weeks if the line was forced.

## Verify
- [ ] Pinch test confirms adequate tissue for this jewellery.
- [ ] Marks placed with the client upright and approved in a mirror.
- [ ] Jewellery will sit without pressure from neighbouring structures or daily wear.
- [ ] Underlying structures (veins, teeth, cartilage) considered for this site.
- [ ] Any decline explained with an alternative offered.
