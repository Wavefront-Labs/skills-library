# Substrate assessment, filling, sanding and priming systems

**Goal:** a clean, sound, keyed surface with the right primer, so the finish coats
look good and stay stuck. Most paint failures are prep failures.

## 1. Assess first
- **What is it?** New plaster, old painted plaster, drywall, timber (bare/painted),
  MDF, metal (ferrous/galvanised/aluminium), masonry/render, tile or laminate.
- **Old coating sound?** Score a small cross-hatch with a knife, press tape firmly,
  rip it off: paint lifting = remove loose coatings back to sound edges.
- **Oil or water-based?** Rub a small area with methylated spirits/denatured alcohol
  on a rag: water-based acrylic softens and transfers; oil/alkyd doesn't. Acrylic
  over old gloss without priming/keying peels.
- **Moisture:** meter new plaster and masonry; new plaster typically needs weeks to
  dry (rule of thumb ~1 week per mm of thickness, conditions-dependent) before
  normal paint. Efflorescence (white salts) = still drying or water ingress.
- **Lead paint:** in older buildings (pre-1978 in the US, pre-~1960s–1990s in the UK
  depending on product), test before sanding. Use lead-safe methods (wet sanding,
  HEPA extraction, no dry sanding or burning; US RRP rules for contractors). Never
  dry-sand suspect paint.

## 2. Clean
- Wash with sugar soap/a TSP-substitute degreaser, especially kitchens, handles,
  skirting; rinse with clean water. Contaminants (grease, nicotine, silicone) cause
  cissing and adhesion failure.
- Mould: treat with a fungicidal wash, leave the contact time, rinse; fix the cause
  (ventilation, leaks).

## 3. Fill and caulk
- Fine surface filler for small dents; deeper holes in layers or with a two-pack
  filler (timber, exterior). Overfill slightly, sand flush.
- **Decorator's caulk** (paintable acrylic) for gaps between trim and walls — not
  silicone (paint won't stick). Smooth with a wet finger; let it skin before painting.
- Spot-prime fillers if the paint maker recommends (they "flash" through as dull patches).

## 4. Sand
- Grits: ~80–120 to remove nibs and level fillers; ~120–180 to key previous paint;
  ~180–240 between finish coats on trim. Too coarse leaves scratches that show
  through gloss.
- Key existing gloss thoroughly (dull it all over) or use a bonding primer.
- Extract dust (sander with extraction/vacuum), then wipe with a damp or tack cloth.

## 5. Choose the primer
- **New bare plaster:** a mist coat (thinned contract/trade emulsion per maker) or a
  dedicated plaster primer. Vinyl emulsions straight on new plaster can peel.
- **Stains (water, smoke, nicotine), knots:** stain-blocking primer — shellac-based or
  oil-based is the most reliable; knotting solution on resinous knots in bare timber.
- **Bare timber:** wood primer (oil or acrylic per system); end grain gets extra.
- **MDF:** MDF primer — water-based primers can raise fibres on edges; sand and re-prime.
- **Ferrous metal:** remove rust to bright metal, anti-corrosive primer. **Galvanised/
  aluminium:** degrease, etch/mordant or a dedicated adhesion primer.
- **Glossy tile/laminate/old gloss:** bonding (adhesion) primer.
- **Masonry/render:** stabilising solution on chalky surfaces; alkali-resistant primer
  on fresh render.

## Gotchas
- Acrylic over unkeyed oil gloss → peels off in sheets.
- Silicone used as filler → paint crawls away.
- Water stains bleeding through two coats of emulsion.
- Sanding lead paint dry.

## Verify
- [ ] Substrate and old coating identified; adhesion and oil/water tests done.
- [ ] Lead tested where the building's age warrants.
- [ ] Surface clean, degreased, dust-free.
- [ ] Fillers flush; caulk paintable; no visible sanding scratches.
- [ ] Primer chosen for the substrate and problem, compatible with the topcoat.
