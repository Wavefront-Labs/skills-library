# Staining and clear coating doors, floors and trim

**Goal:** even colour without blotches, a clear finish suited to the wear, and
coats that bond to each other — on joinery, doors and floors in buildings.

## 1. Prepare the timber
- Sand progressively, not skipping grits: e.g. 80 → 120 → 150 (→ 180 for fine
  joinery). Final sanding **with the grain**; cross-grain scratches show dramatically
  under stain.
- For water-based stains and finishes: **raise the grain** — wipe with a damp cloth,
  let dry, then denib lightly with the final grit — or the first coat will feel rough.
- Remove all dust (vacuum, then a lint-free cloth lightly dampened with the finish's
  solvent or a tack cloth compatible with the finish).
- Glue and filler squeeze-out blocks stain; find it with a solvent wipe before staining.

## 2. Staining
- **Test on offcuts or a hidden area of the same timber**, with the full system
  (stain + topcoat) — the topcoat changes the colour.
- Blotch-prone woods (pine, birch, cherry, maple) take stain unevenly: use a
  **pre-stain conditioner/washcoat**, a **gel stain**, or tint the finish instead.
- Apply liberally, work with the grain, and wipe off excess evenly after the
  maker's dwell time; keep a wet edge on a whole door face or a full board length.
- Doors: same order as painting panels (panels/mouldings, centre stiles, rails, outer
  stiles, edges). Finish both faces and **all edges, including top and bottom** —
  unequal finish lets moisture warp the door (many door warranties require it).
- Let stain dry fully (oil stains often overnight) before topcoating.

## 3. Choosing the clear finish
- **Water-based polyurethane:** low odour, fast dry, non-yellowing (stays pale); good
  for floors and trim; multiple thin coats.
- **Oil-based / oil-modified poly:** ambers the wood, levels well, slower to dry,
  more odour (VOC limits in some regions).
- **Hardwax oil** (e.g. Osmo-type products): penetrates, low sheen, natural look,
  easy spot repairs; needs periodic maintenance; thin coats only — excess must be wiped
  or buffed off or it stays tacky.
- **Lacquers** (nitrocellulose, pre-cat, 2K): mostly sprayed, shop or factory finishing.
- **Compatibility:** don't put water-based coats over fresh oil stain without the
  maker's guidance (dry time, or a sealer). When unsure, test adhesion on a sample
  (tape test after full cure).

## 4. Floors
1. Sand with a **drum or belt sander** in the direction of the boards (diagonal first
   pass only to flatten cupped floors), grits e.g. 36/40 → 60 → 80/100; **edger** for
   perimeters with the same grit sequence; scraper in corners.
2. Final pass with a **buffer/multi-disc sander** with a fine screen to blend drum and
   edger marks.
3. Vacuum thoroughly (boards, gaps, sills).
4. Stain if wanted (test!), then sealer + 2–3 coats of floor finish, screen-abrading
   lightly between coats per the maker. Respect recoat windows.
5. Light foot traffic only after the stated time; rugs and furniture much later (full
   cure can be a week or more).

## 5. Exterior timber
Use exterior oils or stains with UV protection (pigmented products last longer than
clear). Clear exterior varnishes fail by UV and peel — they need frequent maintenance.
Decking oils: clean and dry deck, thin coats, wipe excess.

## Safety
**Oily rags** (linseed, hardwax oils, oil stains) can **self-heat and ignite**. Lay
them flat to dry outdoors or put them in a sealed metal container with water.

## Gotchas
- Cross-grain scratches revealed by stain.
- Blotchy pine stained without conditioner.
- Door finished on faces only → warps.
- Hardwax oil applied thickly → tacky for weeks.
- Oily rags in a bin → fire.

## Verify
- [ ] Test sample with the full system approved.
- [ ] No cross-grain scratches; grain raised and denibbed for water-based products.
- [ ] Even stain colour; no blotches or lap marks.
- [ ] All six faces of doors finished; coats within recoat windows.
- [ ] Rags disposed of safely.
