# Airless spray setup, tip selection and fine-finish spraying

**Goal:** fast, even coats on walls and ceilings, and smooth furniture-like finishes on
doors and trim — without overspray on everything else or an injury.

## 1. Safety first
- **Injection injury:** airless pressure (often ~100–250 bar / 1500–3600 psi) can inject
  paint through skin — a surgical emergency even if it looks minor. Never put fingers
  near the tip, always engage the trigger lock when not spraying, relieve pressure
  before changing/cleaning tips, never remove the tip guard.
- Respiratory protection suited to the product (e.g. a half-mask with appropriate
  filters for solvents, particulate for water-based mist), eye protection, ventilation.
- Earth/ground the pump for solvent-based coatings.

## 2. Tips
Tip code is usually **3 digits**: the first × 2 ≈ fan width in inches at ~12 in
distance; last two = orifice size in thousandths of an inch. E.g. **517** = ~10 in fan,
0.017 in orifice. (Check your brand's chart — conventions are near-universal but confirm.)
- Fine finishes/lacquers/enamels: small orifice ~0.010–0.013 (e.g. 310, 312), or
  **fine-finish / low-pressure tips** (e.g. "FFLP"-type).
- Emulsion on walls/ceilings: ~0.015–0.019 (e.g. 515, 517, 519), bigger fans for big
  areas, smaller (e.g. 315) for cutting near edges.
- Heavy coatings (elastomerics, primers): ~0.019–0.025+.
- Pump must support the tip size (max tip rating on the sprayer). Tips wear: a worn tip
  sprays a narrower fan with heavy centre — replace.
- Match the gun and manifold **filter mesh** to the paint (finer mesh for fine tips).

## 3. Set pressure
Start low, spray onto card, increase until the fan is even with **no tails** (heavy
stripes at the edges) — then stop. Excess pressure = more overspray and tip wear, not
better finish. Thin only within the maker's limit if it won't atomise.

## 4. Masking and overspray
- Mask windows, floors, fittings with masking film and tape; remove door ironmongery.
- Overspray drifts far — cover adjacent rooms' doorways, cars outside, everything. On
  exteriors, check wind; don't spray in gusts.
- For walls, it's common to spray then **back-roll** the first coat on porous surfaces
  for penetration and uniform texture.

## 5. Technique
- Gun **perpendicular** to the surface, ~250–300 mm (10–12 in) away; move the arm, not
  the wrist (arcing = thick in the middle, thin at the ends).
- **Trigger** after starting the movement and release before stopping — no blobs at
  stroke ends.
- **50% overlap** each pass (aim the tip at the edge of the previous pass).
- Consistent speed; thin coats; sag = too slow or too close.
- Edges and corners: spray the inside corner with a vertical pass first, then the walls.

## 6. Fine finish on doors and trim
- Spray doors flat on stands or hanging (both faces in one go with a door hanger).
  Edges first, then panels/mouldings, then the flat faces with full passes.
- Two light coats, sand between (~240 grit), dust-free environment (damp the floor,
  tack-cloth), good lighting at a low angle to see the wet edge.
- Air-assisted airless or HVLP gives finer finish with less overspray on small items.

## 7. Clean up
Relieve pressure, remove tip, flush with the right solvent/water until clear, clean the
filters; for storage add the pump-protector fluid recommended by the maker.

## Gotchas
- Tails from too low pressure or a worn tip.
- Arcing wrist strokes → stripes.
- Forgetting the trigger lock while clearing a blocked tip.
- Overspray onto a neighbour's car.

## Verify
- [ ] Tip size matched to the coating and pump rating; filters matched.
- [ ] Fan even at minimum pressure, no tails.
- [ ] Masking complete; overspray zone controlled.
- [ ] Even film, no runs or dry spray; wet-film gauge reading at spec thickness.
- [ ] Rig flushed and stored; trigger lock and pressure-relief habits used.
