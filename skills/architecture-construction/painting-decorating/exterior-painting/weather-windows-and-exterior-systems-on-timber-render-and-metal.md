# Weather windows and exterior systems on timber, render and metal

**Goal:** an exterior finish applied in conditions it can cure in, with a system
matched to each substrate, done safely at height — so it lasts its rated life.

## 1. The weather window
Read the product data sheet; typical limits:
- **Air and surface temperature** above the minimum (often ~5–10 °C for acrylics; some
  low-temperature products to ~2 °C) and **rising**, through the drying period.
- **Surface temperature ≥ 3 °C above the dew point**: below that, condensation forms
  on the surface. Use a surface thermometer and a hygrometer/dew-point app.
- **Relative humidity** below the product max (often ~80–85%).
- **No rain** for the rain-resistance time on the sheet (often several hours).
- **Not in direct hot sun** on dark/sheltered surfaces: too-fast drying causes laps,
  blistering on timber, and poor film formation. **Follow the shade:** work on west
  elevations in the morning, east in the afternoon (northern-hemisphere logic — adjust
  where you are).
- Start late enough for dew to burn off; stop early enough that the film skins before
  evening dew.

## 2. Systems by substrate
- **Timber (cladding, windows, fascias):** sound and dry (moisture meter ~< 18–20%),
  knots treated, bare areas primed, **end grain sealed** (it's where water gets in).
  Microporous/flexible exterior systems that move with timber; oil-based gloss
  systems where traditional. Back-prime new boards before fixing if possible.
  Caulk joints with exterior paintable sealant, but leave drainage gaps (bottom edges
  of boards, window weepholes) open.
- **Render/masonry:** clean, stabilise chalky surfaces, allow new render to cure (often
  4+ weeks). Use **breathable** masonry paint (acrylic smooth/textured) or **silicate**
  mineral paints on mineral substrates; elastomeric coatings bridge fine cracks but are
  less breathable — avoid on solid-wall heritage buildings that must breathe. Lime
  render → limewash or silicate, not plastic coatings.
- **Ferrous metal (railings, gates):** wire-brush or grind rust to sound metal, rust
  converter/anti-corrosive (e.g. zinc phosphate) primer, undercoat, gloss — or a
  direct-to-metal system. Don't leave bare steel overnight (flash rust).
- **Galvanised:** degrease (ammoniacal wash or proprietary), etch/adhesion primer — or
  T-wash traditionally — then system. Never alkyd direct on galvanised (saponifies, peels).

## 3. Order of work
Top down: gutters/fascias → walls → windows and doors → downpipes → plinths and
ground-level elements. Finish whole elevations in one session to a natural break
(corner, downpipe) to avoid laps.

## 4. Access safety
- **Ladders:** for short-duration light work only; 1-in-4 angle (75°), footed or tied,
  3 points of contact, extend ~1 m above the landing point; never overreach.
- **Towers:** trained erector, outriggers/stabilisers, guardrails, brakes on; not in
  high wind.
- **Scaffold / MEWP** for sustained work at height or large elevations; check the
  scaffold tag/inspection. Comply with local work-at-height rules.
- Lead paint on older exteriors: wet methods, sheeting to collect debris (see prep skill).

## Gotchas
- Painting at 4 pm in autumn → dew on wet film → surfactant leaching (streaky, sticky marks).
- Vinyl/plastic coatings on a breathing solid wall → blistering and damp inside.
- Unsealed end grain → peeling at board ends within a year.
- Painting into the sun on south walls in summer.

## Verify
- [ ] Temperature, RH and dew-point margin recorded at start and checked during work.
- [ ] Each substrate has a compatible, breathable-where-needed system.
- [ ] End grain sealed; drainage gaps left open.
- [ ] Elevation completed to natural breaks; no laps.
- [ ] Access method appropriate and inspected.
