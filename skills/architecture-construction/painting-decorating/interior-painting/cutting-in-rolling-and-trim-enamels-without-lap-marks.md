# Cutting in, rolling and trim enamels without lap marks

**Goal:** walls with uniform colour and sheen, sharp lines at ceilings and trim, and
smooth doors and skirting — no laps, flashing, holidays or brush marks. Assumes prep
is done (see
`architecture-construction.painting-decorating.surface-preparation.substrate-assessment-filling-sanding-and-priming-systems`).

## 1. Sequence
1. **Ceilings** first (cut in, roll).
2. **Walls** (cut in, roll).
3. **Trim** last: windows, doors and frames, then skirting.
Do each surface's coats before moving down. Protect floors with dust sheets; mask only
where freehand isn't practical.

## 2. Pick the materials
- **Sheen:** matt hides imperfections (ceilings, walls in living spaces); eggshell/
  satin for wipeable walls (kitchens, halls, kids' rooms); satin/semi-gloss/gloss for
  trim. Higher sheen shows every flaw in prep.
- **Roller nap:** short (~4–6 mm / ¼ in) for smooth plaster and enamels, medium
  (~9–12 mm / ⅜–½ in) for typical walls, long for textured surfaces. Microfibre sleeves
  hold more paint and leave a fine texture.
- **Brush:** a good synthetic angled sash brush (~50–63 mm / 2–2½ in) for water-based
  paints; natural bristle traditionally for oils.
- Check **spread rate** (m²/L on the tin) to order enough and to apply enough —
  stretching paint thin is the main cause of patchy coverage.

## 3. Cutting in
- Load the brush a third of the bristle length, tap (don't wipe) off excess.
- Place the brush ~5 mm from the line, push to fan the bristles, and draw along —
  the bristles' tip makes the line. Steady hand, long strokes.
- Cut a band ~50–75 mm wide. Work **one wall at a time**: cut in, then roll that wall
  while the cut-in is still wet — otherwise the cut band "pictures-frames" (shows a
  different sheen).

## 4. Rolling with a wet edge
1. Load the roller evenly on the tray/grid — no drips.
2. Apply in a ~1 m wide section: roll a "W" or "N", then fill in without lifting, then
   **lay off** with light parallel strokes, top to bottom, in one direction.
3. Move to the next section **overlapping back into wet paint** — that's the wet edge.
   Finish a whole wall corner to corner without stopping.
4. Don't over-roll partially dried paint; don't press hard on the lay-off.
5. Light from a window: lay-off strokes towards the light reduce visible texture.

## 5. Trim enamels
- Sand lightly between coats (~180–240 grit), dust off.
- Brush in sections: apply across, then **lay off** along the length (grain direction
  on timber) with the tips, light pressure, from wet into dry.
- **Panel doors:** mouldings → panels → muntins (centre verticals) → rails
  (horizontals) → stiles (outer verticals) → edges. Keep a wet edge.
- Waterborne enamels dry fast: work smaller sections, don't go back; a conditioner/
  extender (per maker) helps flow. Alkyds level better but yellow and smell.

## 6. Two coats
Most systems need two finish coats (over primer/mist coat) for even colour and sheen,
especially strong colours. Observe recoat times; humidity and cold lengthen them.

## Gotchas
- Cutting in the whole room first, rolling later → picture framing.
- Stopping mid-wall → visible lap.
- Painting in direct sun or with heating blasting → too-fast drying, laps.
- Too thin a coat to "make the tin go further".

## Verify
- [ ] Inspect walls with a raking light (torch held flat): no laps, holidays, roller lines.
- [ ] Even sheen across cut-in bands and rolled areas.
- [ ] Straight lines at ceiling and trim.
- [ ] Trim smooth, no runs or brush ridges; edges painted.
- [ ] Spread rate matches the tin within reason.
