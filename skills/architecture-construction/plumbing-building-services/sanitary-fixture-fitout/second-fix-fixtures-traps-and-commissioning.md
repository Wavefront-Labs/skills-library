# Second fix: fixtures, traps and commissioning

**Goal:** fixtures fixed solid, sealed correctly, with accessible isolation, and a system
flushed and tested so the first user doesn't find the leak.

## 1. Before second fix
- Tiling and waterproofing complete and cured; painters ideally done in wet rooms.
- **Flush supply lines** before connecting anything: debris from first fix (solder, swarf,
  PEX shavings) wrecks cartridges and aerators. Open each stub into a bucket until clear.
- Check stubs are where the fixture needs them (measure against the data sheet). If not,
  fix it now, not with a contorted flexible hose.

## 2. Supply connections
- **Isolation valve on every fixture** (mini stop/angle valve) — required in many codes
  and always good practice.
- Braided flexible hoses: correct length (no tight loops or kinks), not twisted, rated for
  the pressure/temperature, and from a quality brand — cheap flex hoses are a leading
  cause of insurance water claims. Hand-tight plus the specified extra turn; don't crush
  seals.
- Threaded joints: PTFE tape *or* thread sealant per fitting type — never on compression
  olives or flat-washer joints.

## 3. Wastes and traps
- Every fixture trapped (unless an integral trap). Seal depth per code (typically
  50–75 mm).
- Basin waste: seat the waste with the specified washer or a thin bed of sanitary
  silicone/putty (not plumber's putty on natural stone — it stains).
- Align trap outlets with the waste stub without stressing joints; slip-joint nuts
  hand-tight plus a little.
- Overflow basins need slotted wastes; unslotted for basins without overflow.

## 4. Toilets
- Check pan connector type vs. the floor/wall outlet (S-trap, P-trap, set-out from wall).
- Set on a clean floor; fix to the floor with the supplied fixings (don't overtighten
  porcelain). Shim on uneven tiles — a rocking pan breaks its seal.
- Connect cistern, adjust float/fill valve to the water line, check the overflow.
- Many codes require the base to be left **unsealed at the back** (or partially) so a
  leak shows rather than rots the floor — local practice varies.

## 5. Showers and mixers
- Check the mixer body depth was set correctly at first fix (trim kit must fit).
- Install trims with the supplied seals; silicone where the trim meets tile as directed.
- Set temperature limit stops; in many jurisdictions sanitary hot water must be delivered
  at ≤ ~45–50 °C via a tempering/thermostatic valve — **check your code.**

## 6. Commissioning
1. Close all fixture valves, turn on the main slowly, bleed air.
2. Open each fixture valve; run each tap hot and cold. Check flow and temperature.
3. Fill each basin/bath to the overflow and release — watch traps under full flow.
4. Flush each toilet several times.
5. Dry paper towel around every joint, wait, check again.
6. Clean aerators (screens catch the remaining debris).
7. Record model numbers, warranties, and hand over manuals.

## Gotchas
- Not flushing lines → blocked cartridges in week one.
- Plumber's putty on stone or some plastics → staining/cracking.
- Over-tightened toilet bolts → cracked pans.
- Kinked flex hoses → reduced flow and later burst.

## Verify
- [ ] Every fixture has an accessible isolation valve.
- [ ] All traps full and no drips after full-flow tests (paper towel dry after 15 min).
- [ ] Hot water at outlets within the code's delivery limit.
- [ ] Toilets solid (no rock), fill level correct, no running.
- [ ] Handover pack complete.
