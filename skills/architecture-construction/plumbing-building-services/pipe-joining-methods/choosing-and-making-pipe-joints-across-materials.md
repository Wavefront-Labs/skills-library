# Choosing and making pipe joints across materials

**Goal:** a joint that's right for the material, pressure, temperature and location, made
so it passes the pressure test first time and lasts.

## Choosing
| Material | Joints | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Copper | Soft solder, brazing, press-fit, compression | Solder for water; brazing for refrigerant, gas (where required) and high temp. |
| PEX | Crimp/clamp ring, expansion (PEX-a), press sleeve, push-fit | System-specific: fittings and tools must match the pipe brand/standard. |
| PVC/CPVC/ABS | Solvent weld, threaded adapters | ABS and PVC use different cements; transition fittings between them. |
| PE/HDPE | Butt fusion, electrofusion, mechanical compression | Water mains, gas service lines. |
| Steel | Threaded, grooved, welded | Gas, fire, heating. |

Concealed joints in walls/slabs: many codes restrict or ban some mechanical joints there
(compression, push-fit). Check before burying any fitting.

## Soft soldering copper (water)
1. Cut square (tube cutter), **ream the burr** inside — burrs cause turbulence/erosion.
2. Clean pipe and fitting to bright metal (abrasive pad/brush).
3. Thin coat of flux (lead-free water-rated) on pipe; assemble fully into the socket.
4. Heat the **fitting**, not the pipe, until flux sizzles; touch solder (lead-free) to the
   joint opposite the flame — capillary action pulls it in. A ring of solder all the way
   round = done. Don't keep feeding; excess drips inside.
5. Let it cool undisturbed; wipe flux residue (flux is corrosive).
- **Water in the line** stops a joint taking solder — drain it, or use a pipe plug/bread
  trick (dissolves later) or freeze kit.

## Brazing copper
Higher temp (~600–800 °C+, red heat) with silver or phos-copper rods. Phos-copper rods
are self-fluxing copper-to-copper only; brass/steel needs flux. Purge refrigerant lines
with nitrogen while brazing to stop internal oxide scale.

## Press-fit (copper/stainless/multilayer)
- Correct jaw for the size and system. Cut square, deburr, **mark insertion depth**, insert
  to the mark, press.
- Most systems have a "leak-before-press" feature — unpressed fittings leak on test,
  which is why you test.
- Damaged or missing O-rings = leaks. Inspect each fitting.

## PEX
- Crimp: copper ring 3–6 mm from the pipe end, crimp, check with **go/no-go gauge**.
- Expansion (PEX-a): expand pipe + ring, push onto fitting, it shrinks back — slower in
  cold; don't over-expand.
- Never mix crimp rings, fittings and tools between brands/standards without the
  manufacturer saying it's allowed.

## Solvent welding (PVC/CPVC/ABS)
1. Cut square, deburr and chamfer the outside edge (so it doesn't wipe cement off).
2. Dry-fit; mark alignment.
3. Primer where required (purple in many PVC codes), then cement on pipe and socket.
4. Push home with a quarter turn, **hold ~15–30 s** (it pushes back out).
5. Respect cure time before testing (depends on size and temperature — can be hours).

## Threading
Cut and ream threads; PTFE tape (wrap with the thread, ~3–4 turns) or a rated compound.
Gas: use gas-rated tape/sealant. Don't over-tighten fittings into cast valves.

## Fusion (PE)
Butt and electrofusion are procedure-controlled: clean, scrape the oxide layer, align,
correct heat/time/pressure, and cool without disturbance. Usually requires certification.

## Verify
- [ ] Every joint visibly complete (solder ring, insertion mark at socket, gauge passed).
- [ ] No mixed-brand PEX components outside approvals.
- [ ] Solvent cure time respected before pressure test.
- [ ] System passed its pressure test with no joint weeping.
