# Laying drains to grade, with venting and access

**Goal:** waste leaves every fixture without siphoning traps, blocking or smelling, and
every length of drain can be reached for clearing.

## 1. Sizing and fall
- Size by **fixture units** (DFU in IPC/UPC, "fixture unit ratings" in AS/NZS 3500,
  discharge units in BS EN 12056) summed down the system — tables give the pipe size.
  Common results: basins DN32–40, showers DN40–50, WCs DN80–100 (3–4 in), house drain DN100.
- **Fall:** typical minimums ~1:40 for DN100 house drains in some codes, 1:60 in
  others; small branches steeper (~1:40–1:50). Too flat → solids settle; very steep on
  small pipe → water outruns solids. Use the local table, not a rule of thumb.
- Set grade with a pipe laser or a level on a fall board; check every length.

## 2. Bedding and trench
- Firm, uniform bedding (granular material per spec), no rocks touching the pipe,
  haunching to the sides, and a cover layer before backfill. Point loads crack PVC.
- Maintain cover under driveways (or protect with slab/sleeve).

## 3. Junctions and changes of direction
- Junctions enter **in the direction of flow** (sweep/Y junctions), never against it.
- Long-radius bends on horizontal runs; avoid tight 90° bends in horizontal drains.
- Don't connect a branch directly opposite another at the same level (cross flow
  pushes waste up the other branch) — use offset junctions.

## 4. Venting — why traps hold their water
Water flowing down a pipe drags air; without a vent it pulls the water out of traps
(induced siphonage) or a falling slug blows them out (back pressure).
- Each trap needs a vent within a maximum distance (trap-to-vent distance, set by pipe
  size and code), or a fixture branch sized/configured to be self-venting under the
  local system (e.g., "single stack" systems).
- Main vent/stack terminates above the roof clear of windows and openings (distances in
  code).
- **Air admittance valves (AAVs)** are accepted in many places for branch venting
  (one-way: let air in, not out) but not as the system's only vent in most codes, and
  must stay accessible. Check local acceptance.

## 5. Access for clearing
- Inspection openings / cleanouts at: the head of each run, changes of direction,
  junctions with long branches, base of stacks, and at maximum intervals along straight
  runs (code sets spacing). Bring them to finished surface level or an accessible pit.
- Boundary trap / sewer connection per the utility's rules — often they inspect.

## 6. Testing
- Water test: plug the low end, fill to a head (e.g., top of the highest inlet or a set
  height) and hold, checking joints. Or air test per code.
- Many authorities now accept/require CCTV survey for new drains under slabs.

## Gotchas
- Back-fall from settlement → "belly" that holds solids; usually poor bedding.
- Venting omitted on a long basin run → gurgling and smell.
- Cleanouts buried under paving or cabinetry.
- Solvent-weld joints not pushed home or not primed (PVC) → leaks under test.

## Verify
- [ ] Every length checked for fall with a level/laser; no back-falls.
- [ ] All junctions point with the flow.
- [ ] Each trap within the code's distance of a vent (or valid self-venting branch).
- [ ] Cleanouts at every required point, accessible at finished level.
- [ ] Water/air test held for the full duration; inspection passed before backfill.
