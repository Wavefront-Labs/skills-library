# Installing and hydrostatically testing sprinkler pipework

**Goal:** a system built exactly to the approved hydraulic design, with heads placed so
the spray isn't blocked, pipe properly supported, and a documented pressure test.
Sprinkler work is usually restricted to licensed/certified contractors and always
inspected (NFPA 13/13R/13D, AS 2118, BS EN 12845 / BS 9251).

## 1. Work to the design — don't improvise
- The drawings come from hydraulic calculations: pipe sizes, head types (K-factor,
  temperature rating, response type), and positions. Changing a head type or pipe size
  on site can invalidate the calculation. Changes go back to the designer.
- Check the delivered heads match the schedule (K-factor and temperature stamped on the
  head/frame; bulb colour indicates temperature — e.g., red ~68 °C).

## 2. Head placement
- Spacing and coverage area per head per the design and standard (light hazard
  coverage typically up to ~20 m² per standard spray head; sidewalls and extended-coverage
  heads have their own listings).
- **Distance below ceiling** (deflector position) within the listed range — too low and
  heat collection is delayed.
- **Obstruction rules:** beams, ducts, lights and cable trays can block the spray
  pattern. Standards give minimum distances from obstructions relative to their depth;
  wide ducts (>~1.2 m) usually need heads beneath them. Walk the ceiling with the
  services coordination drawings.
- Coordinate with the ceiling contractor so pendent heads land centred in tiles.
- Never paint, tape or overspray heads; replace any that get contaminated.

## 3. Pipe and supports
- Materials per design: black/galvanised steel (grooved or threaded), CPVC (listed,
  light hazard, with compatible products only — some cable sheaths and sealants attack
  CPVC).
- Hangers at the standard's maximum spacings and within the required distance of
  heads (armover restraint) so heads don't lift on activation.
- **Seismic bracing** where required (sway braces lateral and longitudinal).
- Grooved couplings: correct gasket lubricant, pipe ends fully engaged, bolts torqued
  evenly until pads meet.
- Slope for drainage (dry systems especially), with auxiliary drains at trapped sections.

## 4. Valves and alarms
- Main control valve supervised (tamper switch or locked open).
- Alarm valve / flow switch, pressure gauges, test and drain valve, **inspector's test**
  connection with an orifice equal to the smallest head.
- Fire department/brigade inlet where required, with check valve.

## 5. Hydrostatic test
- Typically 200 psi (~13.8 bar) for 2 hours, or 50 psi above working pressure where
  that's higher, under NFPA 13 for new systems — **confirm the standard in force where you
  are** (AS 2118 and BS EN 12845 specify their own values).
- Fill slowly, vent air at high points, pressurise, hold with a calibrated gauge, no drop
  allowed (aside from what the standard permits for temperature), and inspect every
  joint. Dry systems also get an air test.
- Record on the contractor's test certificate, signed by witness/inspector.

## 6. Commissioning
- Flush underground/lead-in mains before connecting.
- Flow test at inspector's test: alarm activates within the required time.
- Tamper and flow switches report to the fire alarm panel.
- Spare head cabinet with the correct heads and wrench.

## Gotchas
- Heads relocated "a little" around a new duct without checking obstruction rules.
- CPVC damaged by incompatible firestop or cutting oil.
- Missing armover restraints → heads move during discharge.
- Test gauges uncalibrated → invalid test.

## Verify
- [ ] Head types/positions match approved drawings; deviations approved in writing.
- [ ] Obstruction distances checked for every beam/duct/light.
- [ ] Hydrostatic test passed and certificate signed.
- [ ] Inspector's test triggers alarm within the required time; valves supervised.
- [ ] Spare heads cabinet stocked.
