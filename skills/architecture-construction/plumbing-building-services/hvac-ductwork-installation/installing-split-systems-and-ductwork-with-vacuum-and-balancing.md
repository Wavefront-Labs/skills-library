# Installing split systems and ductwork, evacuation and balancing

**Goal:** the design airflow at every outlet through tight ducts, and a refrigerant circuit
that's clean, dry and correctly charged. Refrigerant handling requires certification in
most countries (EPA 608 in the US, F-gas in the UK/EU, ARCtick in Australia).

## 1. Ductwork
- Follow the design (sizes matter — a smaller duct isn't "close enough").
- **Hang** at the specified spacing (often ~1.2–1.5 m for flex, ~2.4–3 m for rigid —
  check SMACNA/local spec) with straps that don't pinch flex ducts.
- **Flex duct:** pull it fully extended, no sags beyond a small amount per metre, no tight
  bends or kinks; compressed flex can lose a large share of its airflow.
- **Seal every joint** with mastic or rated tape (UL 181) — not cloth "duct tape". Leaky ducts
  in unconditioned spaces waste a lot of capacity.
- Insulate ducts outside the thermal envelope to the code value; vapour barrier intact on
  cooling ducts.
- Fire dampers where ducts cross rated walls/floors — installed with access.

## 2. Indoor and outdoor units
- Indoor: level (with slight fall to the drain side for wall units), on a solid fixing,
  clearances per manual. Condensate drain with continuous fall; trap where required;
  secondary drain/pan where overflow would cause damage.
- Outdoor: on a level pad or brackets, clearances for airflow and service, away from
  bedroom windows (noise), out of snow drift/dense leaf fall.

## 3. Refrigerant pipework
- Correct sizes and maximum lengths/lifts from the manual; insulate both lines on most
  splits (suction always).
- **Flares:** cut square, deburr facing down so swarf falls out, slide flare nut on *first*,
  flare to the tool's gauge, a drop of refrigerant oil on the flare face (where
  permitted), torque with a torque wrench to the manufacturer's value. Under-torqued =
  leak; over-torqued = cracked flare.
- **Brazed joints:** flow dry nitrogen through while brazing to prevent oxide scale.
- Keep pipe ends capped until connection (moisture is the enemy).

## 4. Pressure test and evacuation
1. Pressure test with dry nitrogen to the manufacturer's test pressure; hold and watch
   for drop (allow for temperature change).
2. Release, connect a vacuum pump through short, large-bore hoses (core removal tools
   speed it up).
3. Evacuate to a deep vacuum — commonly **below ~500 microns**, measured with a micron
   gauge at the system, not at the pump.
4. **Decay test:** isolate the pump; a rise that levels off below ~1,000 microns suggests
   dry and tight; a continuous rise to atmosphere = leak; a rise that levels high =
   moisture (keep evacuating). (Thresholds vary by manufacturer.)
5. Open service valves (pre-charged systems) or weigh in additional charge per line
   length as the manual specifies.

## 5. Commissioning and balancing
- Run in cooling and heating; check supply/return temperature split, and superheat or
  subcooling against the manufacturer's charging chart.
- **Balance airflow:** measure at each outlet (flow hood or anemometer × area), adjust
  dampers until each matches the design within tolerance (often ±10%). Start from the
  furthest outlets.
- Record readings in a commissioning sheet.

## Gotchas
- Flex duct crushed in roof spaces after other trades walk on it.
- Venting refrigerant is illegal; recovery equipment needed.
- Measuring vacuum at the pump reads deeper than the system really is.
- Condensate drains with no fall → water damage ceilings.

## Verify
- [ ] Every duct joint sealed with rated product; flex fully extended.
- [ ] Nitrogen pressure test held; vacuum <~500 microns and passed decay test.
- [ ] Charge matches the manual (weighed or by superheat/subcooling).
- [ ] Measured airflow at each outlet within tolerance of design.
- [ ] Condensate drains tested by pouring water.
