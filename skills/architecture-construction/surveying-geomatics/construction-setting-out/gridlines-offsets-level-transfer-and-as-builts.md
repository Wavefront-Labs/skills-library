# Construction setting-out — gridlines, offsets, levels and as-builts

**Goal:** put the design on the ground in the right place, keep it there while the
site is dug up, and catch errors while they're still cheap.

## 1. Before setting out anything
- Get the **current revision** of the setting-out drawing and confirm the gridline
  coordinates/dimensions match the architect's and structural engineer's drawings.
  Discrepancies between the two are common — raise them (RFI), don't pick one.
- Confirm the site control and TBMs (and that the coordinate system matches design).
- Agree **tolerances** from the spec (e.g. gridline position ±5 mm, column position,
  level ±5–10 mm). Write them on your check sheets.

## 2. Gridlines first, then everything from gridlines
1. Set out main gridline intersections from control with a total station (or tape and
   theodolite on small jobs).
2. **Check geometry**: measure diagonals of rectangles (they must be equal), check
   distances between all set points against design, not just from the instrument.
   Small jobs: 3-4-5 triangle (or multiples, 6-8-10) for right angles.
3. Everything else — walls, columns, bolts — is dimensioned from gridlines, so
   errors don't accumulate from a chain of measurements.

## 3. Protect it: offsets and profile boards
- Excavation and plant destroy pegs. Set **offset pegs** a known distance (e.g. 1 m or
  2 m) back along the gridline, outside the working area, both ends.
- **Profile (batter) boards** across trench lines with nails for wall face and
  foundation edges; string lines between them re-create the line at any time.
- Paint and label everything: gridline, offset distance, direction. An unlabelled
  nail is a hazard.
- Reference key gridlines onto permanent features (kerbs, existing walls) for later floors.

## 4. Transferring levels
- Establish **site TBMs** from the benchmark by closed level loop (see digital-level skill).
- Mark a **datum line** (e.g. 1.000 m above FFL) on walls/columns so trades work from
  a common reference.
- Rotating laser: check against a TBM at the start of each use; levels drift with
  knocks and temperature.
- Upper floors: transfer levels up by tape from the datum on a column/core, checked by
  a second independent route.

## 5. Plumbing up to upper floors
Transfer gridlines vertically through slab holes with a laser plummet or zenith
plummet, or set out each floor from external control by total station. Check plumb of
cores and columns at each level — errors stack up storey by storey.

## 6. As-built checks
- After formwork, before the pour: check positions and levels of shutters, holding-down
  bolts and cast-in items — this is the cheapest moment to fix anything.
- After the pour/erection: survey as-built positions and record deviation from design.
- Report any out-of-tolerance item immediately with the measured value; the next trade
  needs to know before they build on it.

## Gotchas
- Setting out from a superseded drawing: check the revision every time.
- Tape errors: tension, sag, temperature and reading the wrong end (some tapes start at
  the hook, some don't). Use a calibrated steel tape.
- "Building lines" and boundary offsets set by planning conditions — confirm against
  the boundary survey before excavation.
- One person setting out and checking their own work misses their own blunders; get an
  independent check on critical work.

## Verify
- [ ] Drawing revision and design coordinates confirmed; discrepancies raised.
- [ ] Diagonals and inter-point distances checked against design.
- [ ] Offsets/profiles set, labelled and recorded.
- [ ] Levels transferred by closed loop; datum lines marked.
- [ ] As-built checks recorded against tolerances, before and after pours.

*Uncertainty:* tolerance values are illustrative; project specification and
standards (e.g. national tolerance standards for building) govern.
