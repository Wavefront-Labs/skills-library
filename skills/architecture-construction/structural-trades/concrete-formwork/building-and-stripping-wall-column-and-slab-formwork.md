# Building and stripping wall, column and slab formwork

**Goal:** forms that hold their shape and position under wet concrete pressure, give the
specified finish, and come off at the right time without damaging the concrete or
dropping a slab.

## 1. Formwork is temporary works — design it
- Falsework and formwork are designed (by the system supplier or a temporary works
  engineer). Build to that design: tie spacing, waler/soldier sizes, prop spacing and
  loads, bracing.
- A **temporary works coordinator** (UK term; similar roles elsewhere) checks and permits
  loading — pour only after the formwork has been inspected and signed off.

## 2. Concrete pressure — why pour rate matters
- Wet concrete pushes like a fluid: full hydrostatic pressure ≈ density × g × height
  (about **24 kPa per metre** of head for normal concrete). A 3 m wall poured fast can
  approach ~72 kPa at the base.
- Pressure is reduced as concrete stiffens, so design pressure depends on **rate of rise**,
  concrete temperature and mix (retarders, SCC and admixtures increase pressure — SCC
  often assumed at full hydrostatic).
- Respect the rate of rise the design assumes. Pumping a wall full in minutes is a classic
  blow-out cause.

## 3. Walls and columns
- Faces: formply/system panels, cleaned and coated with release agent (thin film, not on
  reinforcement).
- **Ties** through the wall at the designed spacing take the pressure; walers and
  soldiers spread it. Tie type per spec (e.g., she-bolts, cone ties with water-stop
  where watertightness matters).
- Column boxes clamped at the spacing the design gives (closer at the bottom).
- Brace forms for plumb and wind; check plumb with a level/laser before and during the
  pour.
- Seal joints and the base (grout loss leaves honeycombing); chamfer strips on arrises if
  specified.
- Cleanout opening at the base of tall forms to remove debris before closing up.

## 4. Slab decks
- Props/shores on firm bearing (sole plates on prepared ground; check the slab below can
  take loads — back-propping through floors below per design).
- Bearers and joists per design; deck level set to the specified soffit level plus any
  **camber/pre-set** the engineer specifies.
- Edge forms at the right height for slab thickness; set-downs and penetrations boxed out.
- Check every prop is plumb, pinned and with head/base plates engaged.

## 5. Pre-pour checks
- Dimensions, levels, plumb, line; ties and clamps tight; bracing complete.
- Reinforcement and cast-ins inspected (hold point).
- Clean (no sawdust, tie wire, water); joints sealed; release agent applied.
- Access and edge protection for the pour crew; formwork watcher during pour to look for
  movement or leaks.

## 6. Stripping (striking)
- Vertical forms (walls, columns, beam sides) usually strip earlier — often 1–2 days in
  mild weather — once concrete can hold its shape and won't be damaged.
- Soffit forms and props stay until concrete reaches the strength the engineer specifies
  (often a % of design strength confirmed by cylinder/cube tests or maturity data),
  then back-propping per design. **Time alone is not a criterion** in cold weather.
- Strip in the designed sequence; never remove props from below loaded slabs above.
- Clean, repair and stack panels; record reuse counts.

## Gotchas
- Pour rate faster than design → blow-out.
- Props on soft ground or unchecked slabs below → deflection or collapse.
- Release agent on rebar → poor bond.
- Stripping soffits by the calendar in winter.

## Verify
- [ ] Formwork built to design drawings and signed off as permit-to-load.
- [ ] Pour rate/sequence agreed with the concrete gang and pump operator.
- [ ] Formwork watcher during the pour; levels/plumb checked after.
- [ ] Stripping only after specified strength confirmed; back-propping per design.
