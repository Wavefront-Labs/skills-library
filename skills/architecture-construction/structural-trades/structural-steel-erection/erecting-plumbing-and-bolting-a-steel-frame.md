# Erecting, plumbing and bolting a steel frame

**Goal:** the frame goes up in a sequence that's stable at every stage, ends up plumb and
in position within tolerance, and every bolt is tightened to the specified method.
(Erection regulations: e.g., OSHA 1926 Subpart R in the US; AS/NZS 5131; BS EN 1090-2.)

## 1. Before steel arrives
- **Survey the anchor (holding-down) bolts:** position, projection, plumbness and level
  of bearing surfaces against the drawings. Fix errors before the crane day — the
  engineer must approve any field modification.
- Concrete strength adequate for loading (often a specified % of design strength, from
  test results — not guessing).
- Erection drawings and the **erection sequence/method statement** reviewed: crane
  positions and lifts, temporary bracing, connection requirements before release of the hook.
- Laydown in sequence order; members marked/piece-marked to match drawings.

## 2. Sequence to a stable bay
- Start with a **braced bay** (two columns + beams + permanent or temporary bracing), so
  the first frame stands on its own.
- Extend from there, connecting each new member to the stable structure.
- Columns: set on shims/levelling nuts, minimum number of anchor bolts with nuts
  (OSHA requires at least four anchor bolts for columns in most cases), guyed/braced
  until beams tie them.
- **Don't release the crane hook** until the member has the minimum bolts per the
  method statement (commonly at least two bolts per connection, wrench-tight).

## 3. Temporary bracing
- Per the engineer's temporary works design: cables/turnbuckles or temporary members.
- Leave in place until permanent bracing, floor diaphragms or decking provide
  stability. Removing temporary bracing early is a known cause of frame collapses.

## 4. Plumbing and aligning
- Plumb columns with total station/laser or plumb bob and tape on two faces; adjust with
  guys/turnbuckles and shim packs at bases.
- Typical tolerances are small (e.g., H/500 to H/1000 for column plumb, with cumulative
  limits — **use the specified standard**).
- Plumb section by section and tighten bolts in plumbed bays before moving on;
  re-check after tightening.
- Grout base plates once the frame is plumbed and approved (non-shrink grout, fill fully).

## 5. Bolting
- **Snug-tight:** full effort of an ironworker with a spud wrench or a few impacts —
  plies in firm contact. Many connections only need this.
- **Pretensioned/slip-critical** (HSFG): tension by a specified method —
  *turn-of-nut* (snug, match-mark, rotate a specified amount), *tension-control (TC)
  bolts* (spline shears at tension), *direct tension indicators (DTIs)* (gaps closed to
  feeler-gauge criterion), or calibrated wrench. Pre-installation verification
  of the bolt assembly on site, per specification.
- Tighten from the stiffest part of the joint outward. Never reuse pretensioned bolts
  (galvanised especially).
- Faying surfaces on slip-critical joints: correct prep, no paint where not permitted.

## 6. Decking
- Lay decking per sequence, fix to beams immediately (puddle welds or fasteners) to
  stop wind lift; shear studs welded and tested (bend test).
- Controlled decking zones / fall protection per regulations.

## Gotchas
- Anchor bolts out of position discovered with the column on the hook.
- Releasing the hook with one bolt.
- Bracing removed to "make room" for another trade.
- Bolts tightened before plumbing → can't adjust.

## Verify
- [ ] Anchor bolt survey recorded and accepted.
- [ ] Each bay plumb within tolerance, recorded, before bolting up.
- [ ] Bolt tightening method verified and inspected (marks, DTI gaps, sheared splines).
- [ ] Temporary bracing in place until permanent stability achieved.
- [ ] Bases grouted; decking fixed down at end of each shift.
