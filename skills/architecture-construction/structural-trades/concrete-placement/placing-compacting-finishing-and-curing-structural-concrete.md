# Placing, compacting, finishing and curing structural concrete

**Goal:** dense, fully compacted concrete of the specified strength and finish, with no
cold joints, honeycombing or plastic cracking — because curing was planned, not an
afterthought.

## 1. Pour plan
- Volume (measure from drawings, add a few % for waste/deflection), truck frequency and
  pump size to place at the rate the formwork design allows.
- Sequence and direction; where construction joints go if the pour must stop (agreed with
  the engineer, not wherever the concrete runs out).
- Crew: placers, vibrator operators (spare vibrators and a backup pump plan), finishers.
- Weather: check forecast for heat, wind, rain or frost and have protection ready.
- Hold points closed (formwork, rebar, embedded services).

## 2. Receiving concrete
- Check every docket: mix/strength grade, slump, max aggregate size, admixtures, batch
  time. Reject concrete beyond the specified time from batching (often ~90 min, check spec).
- Slump test (or flow test for SCC) and make test cylinders/cubes per the sampling
  plan — by a competent tester.
- **No water added on site** unless the spec/supplier permits a recorded, controlled
  amount. Water raises slump and *lowers strength*.

## 3. Placing
- Place as close to final position as possible; don't use vibrators to move concrete
  sideways (it segregates).
- Free-fall limit (often ~1.5–2 m without a tremie/elephant trunk in congested forms)
  to avoid segregation.
- Layers ~300–500 mm in walls/columns; each new layer placed while the one below is still
  workable so they can be knitted together — otherwise a **cold joint**.

## 4. Vibration
- Insert the poker vertically, quickly, to penetrate ~100 mm into the layer below;
  withdraw slowly. Insertions at spacing about 8–10× poker diameter (so zones overlap).
- Stop when air bubbles stop rising and the surface glistens — over-vibration brings
  paste up and can segregate; under-vibration leaves honeycombing (far more common).
- Avoid touching the formwork face (marks) and avoid vibrating rebar to transmit
  (can cause voids in set concrete below).

## 5. Finishing slabs
1. **Screed** to level (screed rails or laser screed).
2. **Bull float** to close the surface and embed aggregate.
3. **Wait for bleed water to evaporate** — never trowel bleed water back in (weak, dusty
  surface, later scaling). Don't sprinkle cement on wet surfaces.
4. **Power float** when footprints are ~3–6 mm deep; then power trowel passes with
  increasing blade pitch as it hardens, for the specified finish (broom, float, steel
  trowel).
5. **Joints:** tool or saw-cut control joints at the spacing on the drawings (commonly
  within 4–12 hours, as soon as the cut won't ravel — early cut beats random cracking),
  to ~1/4 depth of slab.

## 6. Curing
- Begin as soon as finishing allows. Keep moist and at reasonable temperature for the
  specified period (often ≥7 days for normal cement, longer with slag/fly ash):
  wet hessian, plastic sheeting, ponding, or curing compound (check compatibility with
  floor finishes).
- Hot/windy: evaporation retarder, windbreaks, sunshades, pour early morning — plastic
  shrinkage cracks appear within hours otherwise.
- Cold: insulated blankets, heated enclosures; protect from freezing until a minimum
  strength (often ~5 MPa) is reached.

## Gotchas
- Water added at the truck "to help it pump".
- Finishing over bleed water.
- Late saw cuts → random cracks.
- Curing ignored on hot windy days.

## Verify
- [ ] Every docket checked; slump and cylinders recorded.
- [ ] No cold joints; construction joints only where agreed.
- [ ] Walls/columns stripped show no honeycombing (or repaired to approved method).
- [ ] Saw cuts done at the right time and depth.
- [ ] Curing method and duration recorded; test results reviewed at 7/28 days.
