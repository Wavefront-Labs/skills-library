# Lifting, bracing and connecting precast and tilt-up panels

**Goal:** each panel lifts without cracking, lands in position, is braced against wind
until the permanent structure takes over, and is connected and grouted to the design.
(Standards/guides: AS 3850 in Australia; Tilt-Up Concrete Association and PCI guidance in
the US; manufacturer's lifting system data everywhere.)

## 1. Panel and lift design come first
- Shop/panel drawings show: dimensions, lifting insert type and positions, brace insert
  positions, rigging configuration, minimum concrete strength at lift, panel weight, and
  connection details. **Lifting design is by an engineer** — inserts are positioned so
  bending stresses in the panel stay within limits during rotation.
- Crane selected for the heaviest panel at the worst radius, with ground bearing
  checked (outrigger pads).

## 2. Tilt-up casting (on site)
- Casting bed (often the floor slab) flat, clean, cured; slab joints filled/taped so they
  don't print through.
- **Bond breaker** applied evenly per manufacturer — insufficient bond breaker welds the
  panel to the slab (dangerous suction/bond on lift).
- Edge forms, openings, reinforcement, inserts (lifting and bracing) and embedded plates
  positioned and inspected as a hold point; recheck insert positions to the drawing — they
  are life-critical.
- Pour, finish and cure; take cylinders to confirm **lift strength**.

## 3. The lift
- Confirm lift strength from test results (not age) before lifting.
- Rigging per the design: spreader beams/sheaves so loads share as designed; correct
  lifting clutches for the insert system, checked engaged.
- Exclusion zone: nobody under or beside the panel's fall zone; tag lines for control.
- Break the bond slowly (crane takes up load gradually); stop if the panel doesn't release
  evenly — don't "jerk" it free.
- Rotate to vertical smoothly; land on prepared **shims/levelling pads** at the specified
  positions and heights.

## 4. Temporary bracing
- Braces (usually at least two per panel; number and type per design) fixed to panel
  inserts and to brace footings/slab anchors **before the crane releases**.
- Brace anchors into slabs require slab strength and edge distance as designed.
- Check plumb and adjust braces; record.
- Wind limits: braced panels are vulnerable — inspect braces daily and after storms, and
  don't remove until the roof/diaphragm and permanent connections are complete and the
  engineer allows.

## 5. Connections and grouting
- Connections per design: grouted dowels into sleeves, bolted cleats, welded embedded
  plates. Welding near panels follows a WPS; protect from heat damage.
- **Grouting:** non-shrink grout mixed to manufacturer instructions, pumped/poured from one
  side until it emerges at the other (no trapped air); dry-pack under bases where specified.
  Cylinders/cubes for grout strength where specified.
- Joint sealants between panels after alignment (backing rod + sealant to correct
  depth-to-width ratio).

## 6. Precast deliveries
- Check each element on arrival for damage, mark, orientation and insert condition.
- Lift from the delivery truck in erection order where possible ("just in time"
  erection) to avoid double handling.

## Gotchas
- Lifting on age rather than strength → cracked panel or pulled insert.
- Bond breaker missed → panel won't release.
- Braces removed early to let another trade through.
- Shims in the wrong position → stress cracking at bearings.

## Verify
- [ ] Insert positions inspected against drawings before pour.
- [ ] Lift strength confirmed by test; rigging matches design.
- [ ] Each panel braced before crane release; plumb recorded.
- [ ] Brace inspections logged daily and after wind.
- [ ] Connections and grouting complete and inspected before brace removal.
