# Framing walls and floors from plans, with bracing and tie-downs

**Goal:** a frame that's square, plumb and straight, with every opening, brace and
tie-down where the engineer or code says — because the linings and finishes can only be
as good as the frame.

## 1. Read the plans first
- Framing plans/structural drawings: stud size, grade and spacing; lintel/header sizes;
  bracing wall locations and types; tie-down schedule; joist sizes and spacing.
  Span tables (code or manufacturer) govern where the plan is silent — **don't oversize
  or undersize by eye.**
- Check the slab/floor is square and level; measure diagonals before starting.

## 2. Wall layout
1. Snap chalk lines for each wall's plate edge on the slab/floor.
2. Pair top and bottom plates and mark them together: stud centres (e.g., 400/450/600
   mm), openings (trimmer/jack studs, king studs), intersections, and noggin/blocking
   heights. Start layout so board edges land on studs (centre of the stud at 1,200 mm etc.).
3. Mark lintel sizes and opening rough-opening dimensions from the window/door schedule
   (not from the drawings alone — the supplier's rough opening is the one that matters).

## 3. Assemble
- **Crown up**: sight each stud and joist and put the crown (bow) the same way — up on
  joists, consistently out on walls.
- Nail per schedule (two nails per stud end at plates as a minimum in many codes; check).
- Lintels on jack studs with king studs beside; cripples above/below openings at layout.
- Noggins (blocking) at mid-height or as required, plus extra for fixtures (basins,
  grab rails, cabinets, TV brackets) — put them in now.

## 4. Stand, plumb and line
- Stand walls, fix bottom plate to slab/floor per schedule (anchors/bolts at specified
  spacing and near ends of plates).
- **Plumb** corners and ends with a long level or laser; **line** the top plate with a
  string and spacer blocks; temporary diagonal braces until permanent bracing goes in.
- Second top plate lapped at corners/intersections to tie walls together.

## 5. Bracing
- Bracing resists racking from wind and earthquake: structural sheathing (ply/OSB) with
  specified nailing, metal strap bracing tensioned in X, or let-in timber braces —
  whatever the bracing plan says, in the walls it names.
- Sheathing nailing schedule is engineering: edge and field spacing, nail type, distance
  from panel edges. Over-driven nails reduce capacity.

## 6. Floor framing
- Joist size/spacing per span tables; crown up; joists doubled under parallel walls and
  around openings (trimmer/header joists with hangers).
- Rim/boundary joists and blocking at supports; bearing lengths per code.
- Don't notch or drill joists outside the allowed zones (typically holes in the middle
  third of depth, away from supports; manufacturer rules for I-joists are strict).

## 7. Tie-downs
- Load path from roof to foundation: rafter/truss ties, stud-to-plate ties, hold-downs at
  bracing panel ends, anchors into the slab. In high-wind/cyclone areas this is the
  difference between a roof staying on and not.
- Use the specified connector and **every nail hole** the manufacturer requires, with the
  specified nails (not screws unless rated).

## Gotchas
- Using window drawing sizes instead of supplier rough openings.
- Missing noggins for fixtures → cutting into linings later.
- Drilling joists for services wherever convenient.
- Connectors half-nailed.

## Verify
- [ ] Walls plumb within tolerance (e.g., ~3 mm over the height — check spec), top plates straight.
- [ ] Diagonals of the floor/wall layout equal.
- [ ] Bracing walls and nailing match the bracing plan.
- [ ] Every tie-down per schedule, fully nailed.
- [ ] Frame inspection booked and passed before cladding/lining.
