# Service location and trench support

**Goal:** dig a trench for new pipes or ducts without striking an existing cable or gas
main, and without the trench walls collapsing on anyone. Both hazards kill workers
every year and both are mostly prevented by procedure.

> Legal requirements differ (OSHA in the US, HSE/CDM in the UK, state/territory rules
> elsewhere). Follow your jurisdiction's rules and your company's permit-to-dig system.

## Part A — Finding existing services

### 1. Records
- Obtain utility records/plans from every asset owner; in many countries submit a
  **one-call** request (e.g. 811 in the US, "Before You Dig" in Australia) a set number
  of days before digging, and wait for markings.
- Treat records as **indicative only** — positions and depths are often wrong, and
  private services (site lighting, private drains) aren't on them.
- For design-stage certainty, commission a utility survey to a defined **quality level**
  (PAS 128 / ASCE 38).

### 2. Detect on site
- Sweep the route with a **cable avoidance tool (CAT)** in all modes (power, radio) and a
  **signal generator (genny)** clamped/connected to traceable services. Plastic pipes
  without tracer wire are invisible to CAT — GPR may detect them, with limits in clay
  and wet ground.
- Mark located services with paint/flags in the agreed colour code.
- Re-sweep frequently as the dig progresses, and sweep the bottom of the excavation
  as you go deeper.

### 3. Expose before machine digging
- Within the tolerance zone of a marked service (e.g. 0.5 m in the UK guidance; state
  laws in the US often define ~18–24" each side), **hand-dig or vacuum excavate** to
  expose it. Use insulated tools; no picks or spikes on suspected cables.
- Once exposed, support and protect services across the trench; record positions.
- Damage of any kind (even a scraped sheath or coating) → stop, keep clear, report to the
  owner. Gas smell → evacuate and call the emergency number.

## Part B — Trench support

### 4. Choose a protective system
A trench can collapse with little warning; 1 m³ of soil weighs well over a tonne.
- US OSHA: a **protective system** is required at **5 ft (1.5 m)** depth or more (unless
  entirely in stable rock), and a **competent person** may require it at shallower depths.
  Trenches 20 ft+ need an engineer-designed system.
- UK: no "safe depth" — support or batter any excavation where collapse could injure.
Options:
- **Sloping/battering** to a safe angle by soil type (OSHA soil classes A/B/C give
  maximum slopes, e.g. Type C 1.5H:1V).
- **Benching** (not in Type C / granular soils).
- **Trench boxes/shields** — workers stay inside the box; check box rating vs depth.
- **Hydraulic shoring / sheeting** for urban trenches next to structures.

### 5. Daily operation
- Spoil and plant at least **0.6 m (2 ft)** back from the edge (US rule) or as assessed;
  surcharge loads collapse trench walls.
- Access/egress: ladders within **7.6 m (25 ft)** of workers in trenches ≥ 1.2 m / 4 ft
  (OSHA).
- Inspect the excavation **daily, before each shift and after rain** or any event;
  water, fissures, bulging and spalling are warning signs.
- Test the atmosphere in deep trenches or near gas/sewers; guard edges against falls.

### 6. Laying and reinstatement
Lay pipe/duct with bedding and warning tape/tracer wire above new services;
backfill in compacted layers; reinstate surface to the authority's specification.

## Verify
- [ ] Records and one-call markings obtained; route swept with CAT/genny (and GPR if needed).
- [ ] All services in the tolerance zone exposed by hand/vacuum before machine digging.
- [ ] Protective system suits depth and soil; competent person inspected today.
- [ ] Spoil set back; ladder access in place.
- [ ] New services have tracer/warning tape and are recorded as-built.
