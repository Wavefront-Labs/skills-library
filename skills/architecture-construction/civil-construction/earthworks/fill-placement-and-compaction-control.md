# Fill placement and compaction control

**Goal:** fill that won't settle under the slab or road built on it — achieved by
controlling material, layer thickness and moisture, and proving it with tests rather
than counting roller passes and hoping.

> The project earthworks specification and geotechnical engineer set the numbers
> (material classes, % compaction, lift thickness, test frequency). Values below are
> typical illustrations.

## 1. Know the material
- Get the specification's acceptable fill classes and the geotechnical report.
- Run **laboratory compaction** (Proctor) on each fill source: **standard** (ASTM D698 /
  BS light) or **modified** (ASTM D1557 / BS heavy) as specified. Result: **maximum dry
  density (MDD)** and **optimum moisture content (OMC)**.
- Reject or treat unsuitable material: organics, topsoil, high-plasticity clays where
  not allowed, frozen soil, oversize rock, contaminated soil.

## 2. Prepare the foundation
- Strip topsoil and organics; stockpile separately for reuse.
- **Proof-roll** the exposed subgrade with a loaded truck or heavy roller; watch for
  rutting or pumping (soft spots). Excavate and replace soft areas as directed.
- On slopes steeper than about 1V:4H (spec-dependent), **bench** into the existing ground
  so new fill keys in and doesn't slide.
- Control water: drains, crossfall on each layer so rain runs off; seal the surface
  (smooth roll) at the end of the day before rain.

## 3. Place in lifts
- Loose lift thickness commonly **150–300 mm** depending on roller and material — thin
  enough that compaction reaches the bottom of the lift.
- Spread evenly with a dozer/grader; break up clods; remove oversize.
- Extend fill beyond the final batter line and trim back, so the edges are compacted too.

## 4. Moisture conditioning
- Aim within a band around OMC (often −2% to +2%, or as specified). Too dry → won't
  compact; too wet → pumps, won't reach density, and loses strength.
- Add water with a water cart and mix (disc, grader); dry wet material by spreading and
  turning in sun/wind, or lime-treat where allowed.
- Check moisture quickly with the nuclear gauge or oven/microwave samples.

## 5. Choose the roller
- **Cohesive soils (clays/silts):** padfoot/sheepsfoot rollers knead the soil.
- **Granular soils (sands/gravels):** smooth-drum vibratory rollers.
- Rock fill: heavy vibratory rollers, method specification (passes per lift).
- Do a **trial section** to establish the number of passes that reaches the target density
  for that material, lift and roller; then use it as the method, with tests confirming.

## 6. Test density
- **Relative compaction** = field dry density ÷ MDD × 100. Typical targets: 95% standard
  Proctor under structures/pavements, 90–95% for general fill — *spec governs*.
- Field tests: **nuclear density gauge** (fast; needs licence, calibration and radiation
  safety), **sand replacement/sand cone**, or plate load / LWD for stiffness specs.
- Frequency per spec (e.g. per lift per X m² or m³), at random locations plus suspect spots.
- Failed test → rework that lift (moisture/passes) and retest; don't place the next lift
  on a failed one.

## Gotchas
- Comparing field density to the wrong Proctor (standard vs modified, or the wrong
  material source) gives false passes or failures.
- A dry, hard crust can test well while wet material below doesn't — test depth matters.
- Weather: wet periods wreck clay earthworks — plan seasonal sequencing.

## Verify
- [ ] Proctor results on file for every fill source used.
- [ ] Subgrade proof-rolled and soft spots treated; benches cut on slopes.
- [ ] Lift thickness and moisture within the specified band.
- [ ] Density tests at specified frequency, all passing or reworked.
- [ ] Records (test locations, levels, lift numbers) give an auditable trail.
