# Asphalt paving and mat compaction

**Goal:** an asphalt layer at the specified thickness, density and smoothness, bonded
to the layer below, with joints that don't open up. Most asphalt failures trace back
to low density, poor bond or bad joints — all controllable on the day.

> Temperatures, densities and tolerances depend on the mix (dense-graded, SMA, porous,
> warm-mix), binder and the specification. Treat numbers here as typical, not authoritative.

## 1. Before paving
- Underlying layer clean, dry, to level and accepted (proof-rolled base or milled surface).
- **Tack/bond coat** sprayed evenly at the specified rate on every surface the new layer
  touches, including vertical faces of joints and kerbs; allow emulsion to **break**
  (brown → black) before paving. Skipped or tracked-off tack = layer slippage and fatigue cracking.
- Check weather limits: minimum air/surface temperature, no standing water, wind (thin
  layers cool very fast).
- Confirm truck supply rate matches paver output — **continuous paving** is the aim.

## 2. Paving
- Set the screed to the required **loose** thickness (roll-down allowance ≈ 20–25% for
  dense mixes; check on the first section).
- Keep paver speed constant and a **steady head of material** in front of the screed
  (auger ~half to two-thirds covered). Stopping and starting the paver leaves bumps.
- Use automatic grade/slope control (string line, ski, sonic or 3D) for ride quality.
- Watch for **segregation** (coarse, open patches — often at truck changeovers or from
  emptying wings too often). Don't fold the hopper wings every load.
- Check mat temperature with an infrared thermometer/thermal camera behind the screed;
  cold spots don't compact.

## 3. Compaction — rolling pattern
Most density is gained while the mix is hot; compaction must finish above the
**cessation temperature** (roughly 80–90 °C / 175–195 °F for many dense mixes;
mix-specific).
1. **Breakdown:** steel-drum vibratory roller close behind the paver, drive wheel toward
   the paver. Where to start (unsupported edge or joint side) varies by specification
   and local practice — *check yours*; then keep the pattern consistent.
2. **Intermediate:** pneumatic-tyred or vibratory roller to knead and close the surface.
3. **Finish:** static steel roller while still warm to remove roller marks.
- Establish the pattern on a **test strip** with a density gauge: number of passes per
  roller to reach target, then keep rollers on that pattern.
- Don't stop rollers on the hot mat; change direction gradually; keep drums wet to
  prevent pick-up (but not flooding).
- SMA and polymer-modified mixes: usually no pneumatic roller (pick-up) and limited
  vibration — follow the mix designer.

## 4. Joints
- **Longitudinal:** overlap the screed ~25–50 mm onto the cold lane, bump back the
  overlap ("luting"), roll the joint from the hot side with most of the drum on the hot
  mat, or use echelon paving to avoid cold joints.
- **Transverse:** cut back to full-depth, full-thickness material at a straight line,
  tack the face, start the paver on the joint with screed at correct height (shims),
  roll across the joint first.
- Offset joints between layers (e.g. ≥ 150 mm longitudinal, more for transverse).

## 5. Checks
- **Density:** cores or calibrated nuclear/non-nuclear gauge; typical target ~92–97% of
  theoretical maximum density (i.e. ~3–8% air voids) — spec governs.
- **Thickness** by cores/levels; **surface level** to design within tolerance;
  **regularity** with a 3 m straightedge or rolling profilometer.

## Verify
- [ ] Tack applied, broken, and covering joints and kerb faces.
- [ ] Paver ran continuously at constant speed; no segregation streaks.
- [ ] Rolling pattern from test strip followed and finished above cessation temperature.
- [ ] Joints overlapped/cut back and rolled correctly.
- [ ] Density, thickness, level and regularity tests pass.
