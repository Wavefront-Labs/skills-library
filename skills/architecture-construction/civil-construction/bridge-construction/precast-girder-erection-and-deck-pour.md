# Precast girder erection and deck pour

**Goal:** get prestressed precast girders from truck to bearings safely and in the right
place, keep them stable until the deck makes them a structure, and pour a deck at the
designed profile and thickness — usually inside a short road or rail possession.

> This is high-consequence work governed by the designer's erection scheme, the temporary
> works designer, and lifting regulations. The notes below describe what to check, not
> design values.

## 1. Before girders arrive
- **Substructure survey:** bearing plinth/pedestal positions and levels, span lengths
  (pier-to-pier at bearing centrelines), skew. Compare with girder shop drawings;
  plinths are usually cast slightly low and set to final level with grout or shims.
- **Bearings:** correct type per location (fixed/guided/free), orientation arrows, preset
  for temperature if specified, clean and level seating.
- **Girder inspection on delivery:** cracks, damage to ends and strand, measured camber
  vs predicted (camber grows with age — a girder much older than planned may have more
  camber than the deck haunch can absorb).

## 2. Lift planning
- An **engineered lift plan**: girder weight (from the precaster, plus rigging), crane(s)
  chosen for radius and capacity with margin, outrigger loads and ground bearing
  (crane mats on verified ground), lifting points (the cast-in loops/ positions — lifting
  elsewhere changes stresses and can crack the girder), rigging and spreader beams,
  exclusion zones, wind limit.
- **Tandem lifts** (two cranes) need each crane's share computed and a lift supervisor
  coordinating; derate capacities as your rules require.
- Possession/closure plan: sequence, timings, contingencies if a crane fails.

## 3. Setting and stability
- Land girders on bearings at marked centrelines; check position and level before
  releasing the crane.
- **Lateral stability:** long, slender girders can roll over or buckle laterally before
  the deck is cast. Install the designed **temporary bracing** (ties to adjacent girders,
  props, end bracing) before the crane releases — the first girder in a span is the most
  vulnerable. Wind and walkway loads on unbraced girders have caused collapses.
- Then install permanent diaphragms/cross-frames per sequence.

## 4. Deck profile control
- **Haunch** (the concrete between girder top and deck soffit) absorbs differences
  between girder camber and the design road profile. Survey girder tops along their
  length after erection; compute haunch at points; check minimums (shear connectors
  must embed) and maximums.
- Set **screed rails** to deck finished levels including predicted **dead load
  deflection** of the girders under wet concrete — the design gives deflection values.
- Permanent formwork (precast planks, GRP, steel) or falsework between girders, sealed
  against grout loss.

## 5. Deck pour
- **Pour sequence** per the designer (e.g. positive-moment regions first, then over
  supports on continuous spans) to control cracking; or continuous pour with retarder
  if specified.
- Pre-pour: reinforcement and cover checks, dry run of screed over rails, pump/standby
  plant, concrete supply rate vs finishing capacity, weather (hot/cold/rain plans).
- During: check slump/temperature and make test cubes/cylinders; monitor deflections at
  reference points.
- **Curing** immediately (wet hessian, curing compound, or membranes) for the specified
  period; early drying causes plastic shrinkage cracking on bridge decks.

## 6. After
As-built survey of deck levels and thickness; bearing positions recorded; temporary
bracing removed only when allowed by the sequence.

## Verify
- [ ] Plinth levels and bearing types/orientation checked against drawings.
- [ ] Lift plan approved, ground bearing confirmed, lifting points as designed.
- [ ] Temporary bracing in place before each girder is released.
- [ ] Haunch calculated from surveyed camber; screed rails include deflection.
- [ ] Pour sequence followed; curing started immediately; tests recorded.
