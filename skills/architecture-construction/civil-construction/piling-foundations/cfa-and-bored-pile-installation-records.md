# CFA and bored pile installation and records

**Goal:** piles of the designed diameter, length and concrete quality, in the right
place, with a record for each one that proves it — because once a pile is in the
ground, the record is the only evidence of what was built.

> Pile design, lengths and tolerances come from the geotechnical/pile designer and the
> piling specification. Numbers below are typical illustrations.

## 1. Working platform and set-out
- Rigs are heavy and top-heavy: build a **designed working platform** (granular, proven
  by tests) and maintain it — rig overturning is a major piling hazard. Keep a platform
  certificate on site.
- Set out each pile from control; typical position tolerance ~75 mm at platform level
  and verticality ~1 in 75 (specification-dependent). Check pins aren't disturbed by
  plant before boring.
- Locate services and obstructions before boring.

## 2. CFA (continuous flight auger) piles
1. Auger drilled to design depth in one continuous operation; record depth, torque,
   penetration rate (the soil is supported by the spoil on the flights).
2. Concrete (or grout) **pumped down the hollow stem** while the auger is withdrawn,
   keeping positive concrete pressure at the tip. **Never extract faster than the
   concrete fills** — the rig instrumentation shows concrete volume vs extraction;
   under-supply means necking or soil inclusions.
3. Cage plunged into the fresh concrete after auger removal (vibrated or pushed). Use a
   suitable mix workability so the cage reaches depth.
4. Rig instrumentation produces a **per-pile record**: depth, auger rotation, concrete
   pressure and volume by depth. Review it before the next pile.

## 3. Bored (rotary/cased) piles
1. Bore with temporary **casing** through unstable soils, or **support fluid** (bentonite
   or polymer) maintained above groundwater level; check fluid properties (density,
   viscosity, sand content, pH) per spec.
2. At toe level: check depth by tape, **base cleanliness** (cleaning bucket/airlift), and
   for rock sockets, confirm rock with the engineer (logging from cuttings).
3. Lower the cage with spacers for cover; hang it at correct level.
4. Concrete by **tremie** from the base, tremie always embedded in the concrete
   (several metres in practice); never free-fall through fluid or water. Pour
   continuously; withdraw casing with sufficient concrete head inside.
5. Over-pour above cut-off level to remove contaminated top concrete.

## 4. Concrete over-consumption
Compare actual concrete volume with theoretical. Some over-consumption is normal (e.g.
10–30% depending on soils); **under**-consumption suggests necking or collapse, and very
high over-consumption suggests voids or soft layers. Plot it per pile and investigate
outliers.

## 5. Testing
- **Integrity testing** (low-strain / PIT) on a proportion of piles after curing, or
  **cross-hole sonic logging** (CSL) via access tubes cast in larger piles.
- **Load testing**: preliminary piles (to verify design) and working-pile tests —
  static (maintained load, kentledge or reaction piles), dynamic, or bi-directional
  cells, as specified.

## 6. Cut-down and caps
Break down to cut-off level (hydraulic pile croppers or ring-crack methods; avoid
damaging the pile below), expose reinforcement for lap into the cap, and survey as-built
positions. Out-of-tolerance piles go to the designer for cap redesign.

## Verify
- [ ] Working platform certified and maintained.
- [ ] Each pile set out within tolerance; depth and toe conditions recorded.
- [ ] CFA instrumentation shows continuous positive concrete supply during extraction.
- [ ] Concrete volumes compared with theoretical; anomalies investigated.
- [ ] Integrity/load tests done per spec; as-built positions sent to the designer.
