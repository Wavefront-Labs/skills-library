# Soil nailing and shotcrete walls

**Goal:** support a cut (basement excavation, road cutting, slope repair) by building
the wall from the top down — excavate a lift, nail it, drain it, spray it, repeat —
without the exposed face ever standing unsupported longer than the ground allows.

> The wall is designed by a geotechnical engineer (nail length, spacing, inclination,
> capacity, facing). The constructor's job is to build exactly that and prove it with
> tests. Values here are typical illustrations.

## 1. Before excavation
- Check the ground suits nailing: it must stand unsupported for a lift height for a
  day or two. Running sands, soft clays and high groundwater are poor candidates —
  stop and raise it with the designer if the ground differs from the report.
- Locate services and neighbouring foundations within the nail zone; nails often extend
  under neighbouring property (consent may be needed).
- Install **monitoring** before cutting: survey prisms on the crest and nearby structures,
  inclinometers if specified, with trigger levels (alert/action).

## 2. Excavate a lift
- Lift height to match nail vertical spacing, commonly 1–2 m; excavate in plan **panels**
  (alternating) if the ground's stand-up time is short.
- Trim the face to line; don't over-excavate below the next nail row.
- Never leave a lift unsupported over a weekend or heavy rain without the designer's OK.

## 3. Nails
- Drill to design length, angle (often 10–20° below horizontal) and spacing; cased or
  self-drilling hollow bars in collapsing ground.
- Insert the bar with **centralisers** so grout cover is even; corrosion protection
  (galvanising, encapsulation) as specified for permanent walls.
- **Grout** from the bottom of the hole up (tremie) until clean grout returns at the
  collar; top up as it settles.
- Record each nail: ID, length drilled, ground conditions, grout volume, date.

## 4. Drainage
Before shotcrete: **vertical geocomposite drainage strips** against the face between
nails, connected to weep holes or a toe drain. Water pressure behind an undrained face is
a common cause of soil nail wall distress.

## 5. Shotcrete facing
- Place reinforcement mesh (and waler bars at nails) with spacers for cover; overlap
  mesh between lifts.
- Spray with the nozzle **perpendicular to the face** at roughly 0.6–1.8 m distance
  (ACI-style guidance), building up from the bottom. Encase bars fully — **shadowing**
  (voids behind bars) weakens the facing; blow out **rebound** and don't spray over it.
- Use a certified/experienced nozzleman; shoot test panels for strength (cores) at the
  start and at set frequencies.
- Install **bearing plates** and nuts on nails once the shotcrete is placed, tighten as
  specified; finish face/permanent facing later as designed.
- Cure the shotcrete (it's thin and dries quickly).

## 6. Nail testing
- **Verification (sacrificial) tests** before production on separate nails to
  confirm bond strength assumptions.
- **Proof tests** on a percentage of production nails (often ~5%) to a load above design,
  with creep checks. Failures → designer reviews length/spacing.

## 7. Monitoring during work
Read monitoring after each lift and daily; compare movement with triggers. Crest
movement increasing between lifts or cracks behind the crest → stop, inform designer.

## Verify
- [ ] Ground matches the design assumption; stand-up time adequate for lift height.
- [ ] Every nail logged with length, angle, grout volume.
- [ ] Drainage strips installed before each lift's shotcrete.
- [ ] Shotcrete cores meet strength; no shadowing at inspected bars.
- [ ] Verification and proof tests pass; monitoring within trigger levels.
