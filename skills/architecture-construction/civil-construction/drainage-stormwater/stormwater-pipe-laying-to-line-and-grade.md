# Laying stormwater pipes to line and grade

**Goal:** a gravity drain that flows as designed — at the right invert levels and
gradient, with no back-falls, open joints, or deformed pipe — that passes the CCTV
inspection first time.

## 1. Before digging
- From the drawings, tabulate each run: upstream and downstream **invert levels**,
  length, gradient, pipe size/material/class, bedding class.
- **Check the outfall first**: survey the actual downstream connection invert. If the
  existing outfall is higher than designed, the whole network may need redesign —
  find out before laying anything.
- Locate and expose existing services crossing the route (see utility-trenching-pipelaying).
- Plan trench support (shoring/boxes) for the depth — trench collapse is the main killer
  on drainage jobs.

## 2. Set out and control grade
- Peg the pipe line with offsets; record invert depths at each pit.
- **Pipe laser** set in the downstream pit/manhole on the pipe centreline, dialled to the
  design gradient (in %), aimed at a target in the pipe. Check the laser's grade against
  levels taken at the far end before relying on it.
- Without a laser: sight rails/boning rods with a traveller; levels checked every few pipes.

## 3. Trench and bedding
- Excavate to the formation level below invert (invert − pipe wall − bedding depth).
- Remove soft spots; don't let water soften the formation — pump and work in short
  sections.
- **Bedding:** granular material (e.g. single-size gravel) compacted to the specified
  depth (often 100–150 mm); scoop out **socket holes** so the barrel, not the socket,
  bears on the bedding.

## 4. Laying
- Lay from the **downstream end upward**, with **sockets facing upstream** (spigot
  pushed into the socket of the previous pipe).
- Clean spigot and socket, lubricate the seal with the manufacturer's lubricant,
  align, and push home to the witness mark (bar and block, or excavator with timber
  protection — never shove with the bucket teeth on the pipe).
- Check each pipe with the laser target: centred left/right and on grade. Adjust bedding,
  don't wedge with stones.
- Cut pipes square and chamfer spigots per the maker's instructions.
- Protect open ends at end of shift (debris, animals, groundwater).

## 5. Haunching and backfill
- **Rigid pipe** (concrete, clay): bedding class determines haunch/surround — often
  granular to half or full pipe height.
- **Flexible pipe** (PVC, PE, twin-wall): support comes from the side fill — compact
  granular embedment in layers (e.g. 150 mm), equally on both sides, up to above the
  crown (often 150–300 mm cover). Poor side compaction → ovalised pipe.
- No heavy compaction directly over the pipe until minimum cover is reached; no large
  stones within the pipe zone.
- Remaining trench backfill to spec (road vs verge).

## 6. Pits, culverts and testing
- Connect to pits/manholes at design inverts; flexible joint near the structure.
- Culverts: bedding and compaction under the haunches, headwalls and scour protection
  at inlet/outlet.
- **CCTV survey** after backfill: checks joints, deformation, debris, back-falls (water
  ponding). Flexible pipes may need a **mandrel/deflection test** (commonly ≤ 5%
  deflection at a set time — *spec governs*).
- **As-built**: surveyed inverts, cover levels and positions.

## Gotchas
- Gradients specified as 1 in X vs %: 1 in 100 = 1% — double-check what the laser wants.
- A pipe laid flat or with a sag ("belly") collects silt; small errors on flat grades are
  proportionally large.

## Verify
- [ ] Outfall invert surveyed before laying.
- [ ] Laser grade checked against levels; each pipe on target.
- [ ] Sockets upstream, joints pushed to witness marks.
- [ ] Embedment compacted equally both sides (flexible pipe).
- [ ] CCTV/deflection passed; as-built inverts recorded.
