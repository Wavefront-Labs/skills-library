# From brief to massing options

**Goal:** turn a client brief and a site into a small set of genuinely different,
numerically honest building concepts the client can choose between — before anyone
gets attached to a pretty sketch that doesn't fit the brief.

## 1. Interrogate the brief → area schedule
- List every space with **net area, quantity, occupancy and special needs**
  (daylight, acoustics, security, floor loading, ceiling height).
- Group into **departments/zones**; total the **net** area.
- Apply a **net-to-gross** factor for walls, circulation, plant, risers and cores.
  Typical starting assumptions (verify per building type and jurisdiction):
  offices ~80–85% net-internal efficiency of gross internal; housing blocks ~75–85%;
  hospitals and labs much lower. State the assumption on the schedule.
- Write down what the brief does *not* say (growth, phasing, budget per m²) and ask.

## 2. Relationships
- **Adjacency matrix**: must be adjacent / should / neutral / must be separate.
- **Bubble diagram** sized roughly to area, showing public → private gradient,
  service routes, and entrances. This is the plan's DNA before it has walls.

## 3. Site analysis (one sheet)
Sun path and overshadowing, prevailing winds, views in and out, noise sources,
access and servicing points, levels/topography, trees, utilities, neighbours' rights
to light/privacy, heritage constraints, flood zones.

## 4. Envelope and capacity
- From zoning/planning: height limits, setbacks, plot ratio/FAR, site coverage,
  daylight planes, parking ratios.
- **Capacity check:** maximum GFA = site area × FAR; compare with the gross area from
  step 1. If the brief doesn't fit, say so now.
- Model the permissible envelope as a 3D volume — options must sit inside it.

## 5. Generate options (aim for 3, genuinely different)
Vary the *organising idea*, not the cladding: e.g. a courtyard block, a linear bar,
a tower-on-podium; or single- vs double-loaded corridor. For each:
- Massing within the envelope; floor plate sizes and number of storeys.
- **Plan diagram**: zones, core position, entrances, vertical circulation, fire-escape
  travel logic (rough — see building-codes-compliance for egress).
- Structural grid hint (e.g. 7.5–9 m for offices with parking below — the parking
  module often drives the grid).
- Daylight depth check: rooms lit from one side rarely work deeper than about
  2–2.5 × window head height (rule of thumb).

## 6. Appraise fairly
Score each option against the same criteria in a table: area achieved vs brief,
efficiency (net/gross), façade-to-floor ratio (a cost proxy), daylight, orientation,
phasing, planning risk, structural simplicity, cost range. Show numbers, not adjectives.
Recommend one, but present the others honestly.

## Gotchas
- **Circulation is not free** — options that "fit" usually forgot corridors, cores or
  plant. Draw cores and stairs at real size even at concept.
- Floor-to-floor heights compound: 0.3 m extra per floor over 10 floors loses a storey
  under a height limit.
- An option the client can't distinguish from another is not an option.
- Rules of thumb above are starting points; *confirm with local codes and engineers.*

## Verify
- [ ] Area schedule totals net and gross with the stated efficiency assumption.
- [ ] Each option's GFA is within the zoning envelope and FAR.
- [ ] Each plan diagram shows cores, stairs and circulation at real size.
- [ ] Appraisal table uses identical criteria for all options with numeric values.
- [ ] Open questions to the client are listed with the options.
