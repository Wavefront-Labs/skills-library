# Top-down demolition with a high-reach excavator — eat the building from the top, in control

**Goal:** take a multi-storey concrete or steel frame down floor by floor from the top,
keeping the remaining structure stable, the machine inside its duty chart, and all
falling material inside the drop zone.

## 1. Pre-conditions (do not start without)
- Approved method statement and sequence from the demolition engineer
  (`architecture-construction.demolition.demolition-planning.writing-a-demolition-method-statement`).
- Hazardous materials removed and cleared; soft strip done; services isolated in writing.
- Machine chosen for the **working height plus margin**, with the high-reach (long-front)
  configuration rated for the attachment you will hang on it. High-reach boom duty is
  usually far lower than a standard excavator's — read the manufacturer's chart for that
  boom, that attachment, that counterweight (never extrapolate).
- Ground under the tracks checked or matted; basements, voids and drains located.

## 2. Set up the machine
- Position so the cab looks along the face, not under it; the operator should see the
  bite point. Use cameras on tall rigs.
- **Stand-off distance:** keep the machine back far enough that collapsing material
  cannot reach it — a widely used planning rule is about half the building height,
  but your engineer and manufacturer set the real figure (unverified as a universal rule).
- Falling object protective guard (FOGS) and impact-rated front screen fitted; cab
  doors closed; dust suppression running before the first bite.
- Level the machine; tilt limits on high-reach rigs are tight and an unlevel base eats
  your stability margin.

## 3. Choose the attachment
- **Crusher / pulveriser (concrete):** the default for slabs, beams and columns in RC frames.
- **Shear:** steel sections, rebar, light steel frames.
- **Multi-processor:** one body, swappable jaws — fewer machine moves.
- **Hammer/breaker:** generally avoided at height — vibration and shock loads are hard
  on the boom and the remaining structure; used at low level instead.
- Check attachment weight against the duty chart at the reach you need.

## 4. The bite sequence
1. **Top floor first, working back from the edge** in bays, so the structure ahead of
   the face stays braced.
2. Within a bay: **slab → secondary beams → primary beams → columns**. Never leave a
   column taller than the engineer's free-standing limit.
3. **Pull material inward,** onto the floor below or into the building footprint —
   not out over the street.
4. Keep the working face roughly level; don't leave a tall tower next to a cleared area.
5. Break columns near the floor level so they fall short, not as long lever arms.
6. Bring the face down one storey, then the next; clear debris piles before they
   overload floors (engineer sets the limit).
7. Once within reach of a standard machine, step down to it — it is quicker and more
   stable.

## 5. Debris and environment
- Debris falls into the footprint; a separate machine sorts and loads (concrete, steel,
  mixed). Stockpiles never block the high-reach's escape route.
- Water/misting at the bite point and on stockpiles; stop if dust leaves site beyond
  permit limits.
- Monitor noise and vibration at neighbours where the permit requires.

## 6. Stop rules
- Wind above the manufacturer's limit for that boom (high-reach rigs are wind-sensitive).
- Unplanned cracking, leaning or movement of the remaining structure → withdraw, call
  the engineer.
- Anyone in the drop zone → stop. The banksman/spotter controls entry.
- Hidden structure (transfer beam, post-tensioned slab, unexpected tanks) → stop and
  revise the method.

## Gotchas
- **Post-tensioned slabs** look like ordinary slabs; cutting tendons can whip and drop
  spans. Confirm the structure type before biting.
- **Precast floors** can lose bearing suddenly when a support beam goes.
- Overconfident reach: the longest bite is the least stable — reposition instead.
- Steel frames can store energy in members under load; shear in an order that
  lets the member fall where you expect.

## Verify
- [ ] Machine config (boom, counterweight, attachment) is on the manufacturer's chart for the reach used.
- [ ] Stand-off and drop zones marked, controlled and respected throughout.
- [ ] Structure stays braced at each stage; no column or wall left above the engineer's limit.
- [ ] Material falls inward; nothing left the site boundary.
- [ ] Dust, noise and vibration within permit limits; records kept.
- [ ] Operator holds the local high-reach qualification (e.g. CPCS/NPORS in the UK).
