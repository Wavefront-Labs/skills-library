# Removing a load-bearing wall section with temporary propping — take out a piece, keep the rest up

**Goal:** form a new opening in a loaded wall (or slab) inside an occupied or retained
building, transferring load onto temporary supports, then onto the permanent beam,
without cracking, settlement, or dust spreading through the rest of the building.

## 1. Design first, cut later
- A **structural engineer** designs the permanent beam, its bearings (padstones) and
  the **temporary works**: needle size and spacing, prop type and safe working load,
  what the props stand on. Under UK practice (BS 5975) a temporary works coordinator
  checks and signs off before loading; elsewhere use your equivalent sign-off.
- Find out what the wall actually carries: floors, roof, chimney breast, point loads
  from beams above, or a wall stacked over it on upper storeys.
- Check what is below the props: a suspended timber floor or thin slab may need
  **back-propping** down to solid ground or a capable slab.
- Building control / permit and party-wall notices where required (jurisdiction-specific).

## 2. Protect and contain
- Isolate and reroute services in the wall (cables, pipes, gas) — scan and verify dead.
- Seal the work area: floor protection, dust sheeting, zipper doors, negative-pressure
  air scrubber if the building is occupied. Protect retained finishes, windows, lifts.
- Plan noisy and dusty steps for agreed hours; tell occupants.

## 3. Install the temporary supports
1. Mark the opening and the needle positions to the engineer's drawing.
2. Cut needle holes above the future beam level; insert needles (steel or timber
   to design) through the wall.
3. Stand props under each end of each needle on **sole plates/spreaders**, plumb, and
   tighten evenly — snug, not jacked up hard (overjacking lifts and cracks the work above).
4. Where floors bear on the wall, add props under the floor joists/slab on both sides.
5. The responsible person inspects and signs the permit-to-load before any cut.

## 4. Remove the wall section
- **Masonry:** cut the opening outline with a saw (wet cutting or on-tool extraction),
  then remove from the **top down** in small pieces. Don't hammer against retained work.
- **Concrete:** diamond wall-saw or wire-saw the perimeter, core-drill stitch holes at
  corners (sharp corners crack), lift sections out in pieces sized for the lifting kit.
- Keep debris off the props and needles; never use a prop as a step or a lifting point.

## 5. Install the permanent beam and transfer the load
1. Bed the padstones, check level and bearing length to the engineer's figures.
2. Lift in the beam (mechanical handling for heavy sections); pack tight between
   beam and masonry above with dry-pack or approved shims.
3. Let mortar/dry-pack gain strength for the specified period before **striking**
   (removing) props — the engineer sets the time (unverified rule of thumb is days, not
   hours).
4. Strike props evenly, in the sequence specified; then remove needles and make good.

## Gotchas
- **Hidden structure:** flues, a buried beam, or a wall that is thicker than drawings
  show. Stop and re-check design.
- **Vibration from breakers** cracks retained plaster and decorative ceilings — saw and
  crush instead of hammer where fabric matters.
- Silica dust from cutting masonry/concrete is a serious respiratory hazard: wet cutting
  or on-tool extraction plus suitable RPE.
- Props on a timber floor with no back-propping can punch straight through.
- Props knocked during removal — fence them off and keep a person watching.

## Verify
- [ ] Engineer's temporary and permanent works design exists and was followed.
- [ ] Props on spreaders, plumb, even, inspected and signed off before cutting.
- [ ] Services in the wall isolated and verified dead.
- [ ] No new cracks or deflection in work above (check with a level/tell-tales).
- [ ] Beam bearings and packing match the design; props struck only after the specified cure.
- [ ] Dust contained to the work area; retained fabric undamaged.
