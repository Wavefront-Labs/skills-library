# Reviewing an explosive demolition plan — oversight, not blast design

**Goal:** as a client, principal contractor, engineer or regulator's reviewer, check that
an explosive felling or implosion is planned by the right licensed people, that the
collapse logic and pre-weakening are sound, and that the public, neighbours and
retained structures are protected. This skill deliberately covers **no** charge design,
explosive quantities, formulations or initiation methods — that work belongs solely to
the licensed blaster and their specialist engineer under local explosives law.

## 1. Confirm the roles and licences
- **Licensed blaster / shotfirer / blaster-in-charge** holding the jurisdiction's
  explosives licence (e.g. in the US, storage and handling under ATF rules plus state
  blaster licensing; in the UK, explosives certificates via the police and HSE
  regimes; in Australia, state shotfirer licences — check locally).
- A **specialist demolition engineer** responsible for the collapse design, independent
  of the client's structural engineer, plus an independent check for high-risk jobs.
- Police, fire, highways/traffic authority, rail or utility operators and any airspace
  authority engaged early; permits obtained in writing.
- One named person has final go/no-go authority on the day.

## 2. Review the collapse logic (the "why it falls that way")
Ask the specialist to explain, in plain terms and with drawings:
- The **intended mechanism**: fall in a direction (felling, typical for chimneys and
  towers) or inward onto its own footprint (implosion), and why the site suits it.
- Which supports are removed in what order, and how the timing delay **between
  stages** makes the structure hinge, rotate or progressively collapse as intended.
- The **failure modes considered**: partial collapse ("hang-up"), fall in the wrong
  direction, debris throw beyond the footprint, kick-back of the base.
- The **post-event plan** for a hang-up: who approaches, when, and by what means
  (usually mechanical, from a safe distance, after the specialist's inspection).

## 3. Review pre-weakening
Pre-weakening is the conventional removal of non-critical walls, bracing and stair
cores so the structure will respond as designed. Check that:
- Every pre-weakening cut is on the engineer's drawing, and its effect on **stability
  before the event** has been assessed (the building must stand safely — including in
  wind — between pre-weakening and the blast day).
- There's a limit on how long the structure stays pre-weakened and a monitoring regime
  (tell-tales, survey) in that period.
- Hazardous materials (especially asbestos) were removed **before** pre-weakening.

## 4. Exclusion and evacuation zones
- Zone radius comes from the specialist's assessment of debris throw, structure height
  and fall direction, then is often enlarged by the authority — do not accept a figure
  without its basis.
- Map: hard exclusion zone (no one), evacuation zone (buildings emptied), public viewing
  area, roadblocks, marshals' positions, communications plan, and **sweep and clear**
  procedure with a sign-off before the countdown.
- Weather criteria: wind direction for the dust plume, lightning, visibility — with a
  postponement rule.

## 5. Protection and monitoring
- Protection of the charged areas (screening, wrapping, fencing) is the blaster's
  design; the reviewer checks it exists and is inspected.
- **Ground vibration (PPV) and air overpressure** monitors at the nearest sensitive
  receptors, with limits agreed with the authority and neighbours; pre-event condition
  surveys of nearby properties (photos, crack records).
- Protection for buried services and adjacent structures from the impact of the
  collapsing mass (cushion/debris berms as the engineer specifies).
- Dust monitoring and a post-event clean-down plan for streets and neighbours.

## Gotchas
- Pre-weakened structures have collapsed early; treat the pre-weakened building as a
  high-risk site, not "nearly finished".
- Spectators move — marshals and a hard perimeter beat signs.
- Post-event: no one re-enters until the blaster-in-charge declares all clear (misfire
  check), then the engineer inspects any standing remains.

## Verify
- [ ] Licensed blaster and specialist engineer named, licences checked, independent check done.
- [ ] Collapse mechanism, failure modes and hang-up plan explained and documented.
- [ ] Pre-weakening drawn, assessed for interim stability, monitored, time-limited.
- [ ] Exclusion/evacuation zones mapped with their basis; sweep-and-clear sign-off defined.
- [ ] Vibration, overpressure and dust monitoring at receptors; condition surveys done.
- [ ] All permits and authority approvals in writing; one go/no-go authority named.
