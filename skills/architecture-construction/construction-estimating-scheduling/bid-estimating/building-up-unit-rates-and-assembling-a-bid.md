# Building up unit rates and assembling a bid

**Goal:** a bid price where every number traces to a build-up, a quote or a stated
allowance — low enough to win, and with enough risk and margin to survive the job.

## 1. All-in labour rate first
Build an hourly cost per trade, not the wage:
- base wage × paid hours (including non-productive time, travel, inclement weather
  allowances where awarded),
- \+ statutory on-costs (employer taxes/insurance, holiday pay, pension/superannuation,
  workers' comp/employer's liability — percentages are jurisdiction-specific),
- \+ training levies, PPE, small tools,
- ÷ **productive** hours. Typical build-ups land at 1.3–1.7× the base wage
  (rule of thumb, varies widely by country and agreement).

## 2. Unit rate build-up
For each measured item: `rate = labour + plant + materials + sub-items`.
Example, 200 mm RC slab per m³:
| Component | Basis |
|---|---|
| Concrete | supplier price/m³ × (1 + 4% waste) + pump/hire share |
| Place & finish labour | gang (e.g. 1 foreman + 4 labourers) cost/hr ÷ output m³/hr |
| Plant | vibrators, power float hours per m³ |
| Curing | compound or sheeting per m² ÷ 5 m²/m³ |
- Output (productivity) is the biggest lever and the biggest risk. Use your own
  historical outputs, then published guides, then judgment — and record which.
- Check rates against your cost history and published price books (e.g. Spon's,
  RSMeans, Rawlinsons, depending on region).

## 3. Subcontract quotes: level them
1. Send enquiries with the same scope package, drawings, programme and contract terms.
2. On receipt, build a **levelling sheet**: columns per bidder, rows per scope item,
   plus exclusions, qualifications, programme and payment terms.
3. **Plug the gaps**: price each bidder's exclusions with your own allowance so
   you compare like for like. Lowest headline is often not lowest levelled.
4. Check plant, access (scaffold, hoists), attendance, cleaning, waste removal and
   design responsibility — classic exclusions.
5. Rule of thumb: be wary of a quote more than ~15–20% below the median; confirm scope
   in writing.

## 4. Preliminaries / general conditions
Price **time-related** items (site staff, cabins, hoists, cranes, security, utilities,
temporary works hire) from the programme: weekly cost × weeks. Price **fixed** items
(setup, mobilisation, removal) as lump sums. On building jobs prelims are commonly
10–15% of construction cost (wide variation; unverified as a norm). A prelims number
without a programme behind it is a guess.

## 5. Risk, overhead and margin
- Keep a **risk register**: each risk with probability × cost, priced or excluded.
  Ground conditions, design development, access, weather windows, supply lead times,
  liquidated damages exposure.
- Separate **contingency** (for priced risks) from **margin** (profit). Overhead
  (head-office) recovery and profit are usually applied as percentages at adjudication.
- Commercial terms matter: retention, payment cycle, bonds, LDs — they cost money.

## 6. Bid review (adjudication)
Present to a reviewer: scope, programme, top 10 cost items (80% of value usually sits
in ~20% of items), levelled subcontracts, risks, qualifications, comparison to similar
past jobs (cost/m² GFA) and the proposed margin. Then lock: final arithmetic check,
form of tender completed exactly as requested, qualifications in the cover letter
(minimise them in a compliant bid).

## Gotchas
- Pricing the drawings and missing the specification (or addenda).
- Double counting: plant both in rates and in prelims; scaffold both in the sub and
  in your own allowance.
- Escalation omitted on long jobs; quotes with short validity periods.
- Spreadsheet errors: hidden rows, hard-coded totals, ranges not extended.
- Using a winning but loss-making historical rate as "the market".

## Verify
- [ ] All-in labour rates documented per trade.
- [ ] Every priced item has a build-up, quote or labelled allowance.
- [ ] Subcontract quotes levelled with exclusions plugged.
- [ ] Prelims tied to programme durations.
- [ ] Risk register priced or excluded; contingency separate from margin.
- [ ] Independent arithmetic check and reasonableness check (cost/m² vs history) done.
