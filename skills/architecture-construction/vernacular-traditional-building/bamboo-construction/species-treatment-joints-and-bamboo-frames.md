# Bamboo construction — species, treatment, joints and frames

**Goal:** a bamboo structure that lasts decades rather than a few seasons. Untreated,
wet bamboo can be destroyed by beetles and fungi within a few years; treated, dry,
well-detailed bamboo lasts far longer.

**Standards:** ISO 22156 (structural design) and ISO 19624 (grading) exist, plus national
codes (e.g. Colombia NSR-10 Title G for guadua, India's National Building Code on bamboo).
Engineered structures need design to these.

## 1. Species and culm selection
- Structural species include *Guadua angustifolia* (Latin America), *Dendrocalamus
  asper*, *Bambusa blumeana*, *Phyllostachys* spp., among many others. Use a known
  structural species of your region.
- **Harvest age:** mature culms, commonly around 3–6 years old depending on species —
  young culms are weak and starchy. Mark culms by year in the grove.
- Harvest in the dry season and, in many traditions, at times of low sap, to reduce
  starch (which attracts beetles).
- Straight, undamaged, without cracks; check wall thickness and diameter consistency.

## 2. Treatment
- **Borate (boric acid + borax)** solutions are the common, relatively low-toxicity
  treatment. Methods:
  - *Vertical soak diffusion* or *Boucherie* (pressure-pushing solution through fresh culms).
  - *Soaking* in a tank for days to weeks, with internal node diaphragms pierced so
    solution reaches inside (except where you want closed sections).
- Borates **leach out in water**: treated bamboo must still be kept dry.
- Dry slowly in shade, standing or well-supported, to avoid cracking. Air-dry to a low
  moisture content before building.
- Traditional methods (smoking, water-leaching of starch) give partial protection.

## 3. Design by protection
- **Keep it off the ground:** columns on concrete/stone plinths with a steel connector,
  not buried.
- **Keep the rain off:** wide overhangs; UV and rain degrade exposed bamboo quickly.
- Ventilated details so wetted members dry.

## 4. Joints
Bamboo is hollow and splits easily along the grain, so joints are the critical point.
- **Fish-mouth (saddle) joint:** the end of one culm is cut to a concave saddle to sit
  on another, then fixed with bolt, dowel or lashing.
- **Bolted joints with mortar infill:** bolts placed **near nodes** (nodes resist
  splitting); the internode where the bolt passes is filled with cement mortar to stop
  crushing. Common in engineered guadua structures.
- **Lashings:** rattan, rope or steel wire in traditional work; tighten and check.
- Avoid nails through unfilled culm ends — they split it. Pre-drill everything.
- Cut ends near a node so the node closes the end, or plug ends.

## 5. Frames and walls
- Brace in every plane (diagonal members/trusses); bamboo frames are light and rack under wind or earthquake.
- **Bahareque** (Colombia and region): bamboo frame with split bamboo (*esterilla*) or
  lath, covered by earth or cement plaster — performs well in earthquakes when built
  well and kept dry.
- Split bamboo for matting, slats, flooring and lath.

## Gotchas
- Splitting from drying cracks or over-tight bolts: use washers/plates and pre-drill.
- Untreated bamboo in contact with soil fails fast.
- Variable dimensions mean every connection needs fitting; grade culms by diameter.
- Fire: bamboo burns; plaster and separation help.

## Verify
- [ ] Species known; culms mature and graded.
- [ ] Treatment method and solution documented; culms dried before use.
- [ ] No bamboo in ground contact; all members under cover.
- [ ] Joints near nodes, pre-drilled, mortar-filled where bolted.
- [ ] Frame braced in all planes; engineering per ISO/national code where required.

*Uncertainty:* harvest ages and treatment details vary by species and region; check
local guidance (e.g. INBAR technical reports).
