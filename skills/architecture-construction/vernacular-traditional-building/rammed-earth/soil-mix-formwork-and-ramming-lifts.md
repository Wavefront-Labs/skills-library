# Rammed earth — soil mix, formwork and ramming lifts

**Goal:** dense, true walls with consistent strength and appearance, built lift by lift
from damp soil compacted in formwork.

**Codes:** rammed earth is covered by specific standards in some places (e.g. NZS 4297–4299,
Australian earth building handbooks, New Mexico code). Structural design, especially in
seismic zones, needs an engineer.

## 1. Soil
- Well-graded mix: gravel, sand, silt and enough clay to bind. A commonly quoted target
  is roughly 50–70% sand/gravel, with clay ~10–20% (rough guidance; test locally).
- Too much clay → shrinkage cracks; too little → weak, crumbly.
- Screen out stones larger than about a third of lift thickness (and smaller for fine faces).
- Blend sources (e.g. crusher dust + clayey subsoil) to correct grading.

## 2. Stabilised or not?
- **Unstabilised** (traditional pisé): lower embodied carbon, fully recyclable, needs
  a good roof and plinth; lower strength.
- **Stabilised (SRE):** typically ~5–10% cement by weight (lime on clayey soils), for
  higher strength, erosion resistance and code compliance in many countries. Cement
  greatly raises embodied carbon; use the least that meets the spec.
- Mix stabiliser thoroughly and use stabilised mixes within a limited working time
  (cement begins setting); don't ram mix that's been sitting for hours.

## 3. Moisture — the critical control
Compact near **optimum moisture content** (OMC), usually somewhere around 8–12% for
typical mixes. Field **drop test:** squeeze a handful into a ball and drop it from about
1 m onto hard ground:
- shatters into fine dust → too dry;
- breaks into a few pieces → about right;
- stays in one flattened lump → too wet.
Calibrate the drop test against lab Proctor results if you have them.

## 4. Formwork
- Rigid, braced formwork (ply or steel panels with walers and through-ties or clamps):
  ramming forces are high. Bulging forms give bellied walls.
- Check plumb and line before each lift. Fix inserts (window/door boxes, conduit,
  timber nailing blocks) securely.
- Release agent if needed — check it doesn't stain.

## 5. Ramming
- Place loose soil in layers about 100–200 mm deep; compact to roughly half to two-thirds
  of loose depth.
- Ram with a pneumatic rammer (or hand tampers), working systematically, edges and
  corners first. Sound changes to a ringing note when compacted.
- Lift by lift to the top of the form; move or raise forms. Vertical joints between
  panels need a key or are detailed as control joints.
- **Aesthetic layering:** colour variation between lifts is part of the look; deliberate
  oxide or soil changes need consistent batching.

## 6. Curing, finishing, testing
- Stabilised walls: protect from rapid drying (cover with hessian/plastic, mist) for
  the early days.
- Make **test cylinders or blocks** from the same mix and compaction for compressive
  strength testing at the specified age (often 28 days).
- Sealers: if used, choose vapour-permeable ones; test on a sample panel.
- Build a **sample panel** first to agree finish and colour with the client.

## Gotchas
- Inconsistent moisture between lifts → weak "cold" layers and colour banding.
- Ramming against a too-flexible form → dimensional errors you can't fix later.
- Water from the wall top during construction: cover overnight and in rain.
- Efflorescence (white salts) from salty soils or water.

## Verify
- [ ] Soil grading and clay content tested; mix recorded and consistent.
- [ ] Stabilisation choice justified; cement content minimised to spec.
- [ ] Moisture checked by drop test each batch.
- [ ] Forms rigid, plumb and braced; inserts fixed.
- [ ] Sample panel approved; strength test specimens made and tested.

*Uncertainty:* percentages and moisture contents are typical published ranges, not
design values; follow testing and your local standard.
