# Bracket setout and unitised panel erection

**Goal:** a façade line that's true in plan, level at each floor, watertight at every
joint, and built within the tolerances the system was designed to absorb.

## 1. Survey the structure first
- Get the as-built slab edge survey (from the site surveyor, using the project
  gridlines and datum). Compare against the façade drawings: slab edges are often
  out by 20–30 mm or more. Report anything outside the bracket's adjustment range
  **before** fabrication or install — fixing it later costs far more.
- Confirm embeds / cast-in channels are where shown; misplaced embeds need an
  engineered remedial fix (post-drilled anchors), signed off by the façade engineer.

## 2. Bracket setout
- Establish a **façade offset line** from gridlines on each floor (total station or
  piano-wire lines dropped from the roof with plumb-bobs / lasers).
- Set brackets in **three axes**: in/out (to the offset line), left/right (to module
  centres from gridlines, not accumulated from the last bracket), and height (to a
  floor datum). Typical bracket adjustment is ±25 mm in each direction (system specific).
- Torque bolts to the specified value and mark them (paint pen) once checked; weld
  serrated washers or locking plates only if the system calls for it.
- Record bracket positions in a floor-by-floor check sheet — the client will ask.

## 3. Unitised panel erection
1. Panels arrive in stillages, floor by floor in install sequence. Inspect for damage
   and missing gaskets before lifting.
2. Hoist with the system's lifting clamp/bar, by floor crane, monorail on the roof, or
   tower crane. Tag lines on every lift; stop in wind above the set limit (often ~
   9–11 m/s, confirm with the lift plan).
3. Lower the panel so its hooks engage the bracket and its **male/female stack joint**
   and side **split mullions** interlock with the neighbours. Never force — if it
   doesn't drop in, something is out of tolerance.
4. Level with the bracket's jacking screws; check panel joint widths and the head
   height against the setout. Lock off.
5. Install **stack joint** seals (splice sheets, gaskets, sealant at corners) per the
   system — the stack joint is where unitised façades leak.

## 4. Stick-system differences
Mullions fixed to brackets floor to floor with expansion splices (gap sized for
thermal and live-load movement), transoms fixed between, then glass glazed in from
outside with pressure plates and caps. More site labour and site sealing, but
tolerant of irregular structures.

## 5. Perimeter and testing
- **Slab-edge fire stopping** (mineral wool + smoke seal) between façade and slab
  edge, per the tested detail.
- Field **water test** a sample area: common practice is a calibrated hose nozzle
  test (e.g. per AAMA 501.2 in North America) on joints — confirm the spec's method
  and pass criteria.

## Gotchas
- Accumulated setout error from measuring bracket to bracket instead of to gridlines.
- Panels out of sequence block access to the next bay.
- Stack joint seals missed on one panel — water tracks down several floors.
- Lifting in wind — big panels are sails.

## Verify
- [ ] Slab survey reviewed; out-of-tolerance items raised before install.
- [ ] Brackets set to gridlines, torqued, marked, recorded.
- [ ] Panel joints within width tolerance; heads level per floor.
- [ ] Stack joints and perimeter seals complete; firestop installed.
- [ ] Water test passed on the sample area.
