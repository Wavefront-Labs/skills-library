# Installing IGUs with setting blocks and beads

**Goal:** a sealed unit that sits on the right blocks, never touches the frame,
drains its rebate, and keeps the sash square for its whole life.

## 1. Measure and order
- Measure the **rebate (sight-to-sight plus rebate depth)** at top, middle and bottom,
  both ways; take the smallest. Order the unit at rebate size **minus edge clearance**
  on each side — typically ~3 mm per edge for small units, more for large or heavy
  ones (check the system or unit maker's table; edge clearance rules differ by
  standard and frame material).
- Note unit build-up (e.g. 4-16-4), required spec (toughened/laminated where safety
  glazing is required — near doors, low level, bathrooms; check local code), low-E
  surface position, and spacer colour.
- Check the rebate depth gives the required **edge cover** on the seal (commonly
  ~ the depth of the edge seal) so UV doesn't reach the sealant.

## 2. Prepare the rebate
- Clean out debris; confirm **drainage/weep slots** in the bottom rebate are open
  (drained-and-ventilated glazing). Never block them with blocks or sealant.
- On timber, prime and seal the rebate before glazing.

## 3. Setting and location blocks
- **Setting blocks** (hard plastic/EPDM, unit thickness wide) carry the weight: two
  on the bottom edge, usually at about a quarter of the width in from each corner (or
  per maker — some say ~L/10 from corners on large units). Place them on bridges so
  water can pass under.
- **Location blocks** on the sides and top stop the unit moving in the frame.
- **Opening sashes: toe-and-heel.** Pack diagonally so the glass props the sash
  square — on a side-hung sash, block the bottom corner on the hinge side and the top
  corner on the lock side (the glass holds the sash up against sag). Get this wrong
  and the sash drops and binds.
- **Distance pieces** (face packers) keep face clearance between glass and the
  upstand and bead, so the unit is never pinched.

## 4. Fit and bead
1. Lift the unit in (suction cups), sit it on the setting blocks, centre it.
2. Check for any direct glass-to-frame contact — there must be none.
3. Fit internal or external **beads** with gaskets or glazing tape as the system
   specifies; wedge gaskets go in last, pushed in from a corner (don't stretch them —
   they shrink back and leave gaps at corners).
4. Close and operate the sash; it should swing and lock without binding.

## 5. Putty glazing a timber sash (single glass or slim units)
- Rake out old putty, remove sprigs, prime bare timber (linseed putty on bare wood
  dries out). Back-putty the rebate, bed the glass pressing at the edges (not the
  centre), sprig it, then front-putty and strike a clean bevel with a putty knife,
  stopping just short of the sightline. Linseed putty must skin (often 1–3 weeks)
  before painting; paint should lap ~2 mm onto the glass to seal it.
- Note: standard linseed putty is not compatible with IGU edge seals; use a
  unit-compatible glazing compound if you must putty-glaze a sealed unit (verify
  with the unit maker).

## Gotchas
- Blocks across the drainage slots → water sits on the edge seal → misted unit.
- Unit ordered to rebate size with no clearance → cracks when the frame moves.
- Wrong toe-and-heel corners → sash drops in months.
- Solvent sealants not compatible with the unit seal or PVB laminate.

## Verify
- [ ] Clearances all round; no glass-to-frame contact.
- [ ] Setting blocks in correct positions; drainage open.
- [ ] Opening sash operates freely, square, after glazing.
- [ ] Beads/gaskets continuous, corners closed; putty bevel clean.
- [ ] Safety glazing marked where required.
