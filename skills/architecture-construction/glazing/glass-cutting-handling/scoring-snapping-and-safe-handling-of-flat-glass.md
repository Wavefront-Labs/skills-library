# Scoring, snapping and safe handling of flat glass

**Goal:** a clean, square cut on site or in the shop, safe edges, and glass moved
from rack to opening without breakage or injury.

## 1. Know what you're holding
- **Annealed float** — the only common glass you can score and snap.
- **Toughened / tempered** — **cannot be cut** after toughening; any score or edge
  damage makes it shatter into dice. Look for the permanent stamp in a corner. All
  holes, notches and cut-outs must be done before toughening, so measure carefully.
- **Laminated** — two annealed lites with a PVB interlayer: score both faces, snap
  both, then separate the interlayer (heat with a torch or hot-air gun and cut, or a
  dedicated laminate cutting table). Practice varies by shop; confirm with the supplier.
- **Wired, patterned, coated (low-E)** — cut from the uncoated or smooth face where
  possible; low-E coatings can be damaged by the wheel (check the maker's guidance).

## 2. Scoring and snapping annealed glass
1. Glass clean and dry, flat on a felt- or carpet-covered table with full support.
2. Mark the line; use a **T-square or straightedge** held firmly, allowing for the
   cutter head offset (typically ~2–3 mm, but measure your own cutter).
3. **Oil-fed carbide wheel cutter**, lubricated with cutting fluid. Hold it upright,
   one steady pass edge to edge with even pressure — listen for a consistent fine
   "zip". **Never go over a score twice**: it wrecks the wheel and gives a ragged break.
4. Run the cut promptly (the score "heals" over minutes — run it while fresh):
   - Thin glass: lift the glass and snap it over the table edge or a straightedge
     placed under the score.
   - Thicker glass (6 mm+): tap the underside along the score with the cutter ball,
     or use **running pliers** centred on the line.
5. Small strips (< ~20 mm): break off with **grozing / breaker pliers** working from
   the end.

## 3. Arrissing edges
A fresh cut edge is razor sharp and has micro-cracks that can start a thermal crack
later. **Arris** (bevel) both corners with a wet carborundum stone or diamond hand
pad at ~45°, ~1 mm. In units and frames, an arrised clean-cut edge is generally the
minimum; polished or ground edges come from the factory if specified.

## 4. Handling and transport
- **PPE:** cut-resistant gloves (cut level per EN 388 / ANSI A4+ for glass work),
  arm sleeves, safety boots, safety glasses; no loose clothing at the cutting table.
- **Carry vertically, on edge**, never flat — a lite carried flat sags and breaks.
  Hold with one hand low, one high; walk on edge with the leading edge down.
- **Suction cups / vacuum lifters:** check the pad is clean, the glass is dry and
  free of dust, and the vacuum indicator (red line) holds. Re-check pumped cups every
  few minutes. Know the rated load of the device and add glass weight: float glass is
  ~2.5 kg per m² per mm thickness (a 2 × 1 m sheet of 10 mm ≈ 50 kg).
- **Rack and A-frame:** lean lites at ~3–5° from vertical on rubber-padded frames,
  strap them, and load both sides evenly so it can't tip.
- Two-person lifts above roughly 25 kg; mechanical lifting beyond that. Plan the route
  (doors, stairs, wind on scaffolds) before you pick up.

## Gotchas
- Trying to cut toughened glass — it explodes. Check the stamp every time.
- Second pass over a score, or a dry wheel.
- Corners left unarrised → cuts and later edge cracks.
- Suction cups on dusty or wet glass slip without warning.
- Wind: a large lite acts as a sail; don't carry big glass on an exposed scaffold in gusts.

## Verify
- [ ] Glass type identified (annealed/toughened/laminated) before cutting.
- [ ] Cut runs cleanly, square, to size within ±1 mm.
- [ ] Both edge arrises taken off; no shells or vents along the edge.
- [ ] Lifting device rated for the calculated weight; vacuum holding.
- [ ] Glass stored on edge, strapped and padded.
