# Frameless glass balustrades and patch-fitted doors

**Goal:** frameless toughened glass that's correctly specified for its load, held
by tested hardware, aligned to a millimetre, and safe if a pane breaks.

## 1. Spec first — you can't change toughened glass on site
- **Every hole, notch and corner is cut before toughening.** Template the actual
  site (floor spring positions, ceiling heights, wall out-of-plumb) and order from
  survey dimensions, not drawings.
- **Balustrades:** the glass thickness and fixing come from the system's tested data
  or an engineer's calc for the required line load (e.g. 0.74 kN/m domestic,
  1.5–3 kN/m public/crowd in UK practice; values differ by code — check yours).
  Where a pane is the barrier with no separate handrail, codes typically require
  **laminated toughened** glass so it stays in place after breakage, or an
  interlinking handrail. Stiff interlayers (SGP-type) retain more post-breakage
  strength than PVB.
- **Heat-soaked** toughened glass reduces the risk of spontaneous breakage from
  nickel-sulphide inclusions — worth specifying overhead and at height.

## 2. Base-shoe (channel) balustrade
1. Fix the aluminium shoe to structure with the specified anchors at the specified
   centres (this is the structural element — don't improvise anchors).
2. Shim and level the shoe; it should be straight within a couple of mm over its run.
3. Lower glass onto setting pads in the channel, insert the clamping system (wedge
   gaskets or mechanical clamps), and **plumb each pane** with a long level. Adjust
   so the top edges line through and joint gaps are even (often 5–10 mm).
4. Fit the cover cladding, and handrail/cap rail if specified.

## 3. Spigots and standoffs
- Spigots (posts) or standoff bolts through pre-drilled holes: use the supplied
  isolating sleeves and gaskets — **no metal-to-glass contact** ever. Tighten to the
  maker's torque; over-torque cracks glass at holes.

## 4. Patch-fitted doors
- **Floor spring** set in a recessed box concreted level in the floor, its spindle
  exactly plumb under the **top pivot** in the transom/ceiling patch. Plumb with a
  laser — a few mm out and the door won't stay centred.
- Bottom and top **patch fittings** clamp the door at the corners; check gasket
  inserts are in, tighten evenly.
- Set the spring's closing speed and back-check; adjust hold-open as required.
- Door edge gaps typically 3–5 mm to fixed panes; check the leaf hangs centred at
  closed position.

## 5. Finish and safety
- **Manifestation** (visible marking) on large glazed areas people could walk into,
  at two heights (UK guidance around 850–1000 mm and 1400–1600 mm; check local rules).
- Silicone joints between fixed panes: compatible neutral-cure silicone, tooled flush.
- Keep toughened stamps visible where code requires.

## Gotchas
- Ordering glass from drawings, not a site template.
- Monolithic toughened used as a barrier where code needs laminated.
- Metal contact at a hole — the classic origin of a breakage.
- Floor spring not plumb under the pivot → door drifts open.

## Verify
- [ ] Glass type, thickness and interlayer match the engineered/tested spec.
- [ ] Shoe/spigot anchors as specified; torques recorded.
- [ ] Panes plumb, top edges aligned, gaps even; no metal-to-glass contact.
- [ ] Door self-centres and closes smoothly; spring adjusted.
- [ ] Manifestation and stamps in place.
