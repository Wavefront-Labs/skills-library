# Structural silicone joint prep, application and QC

**Goal:** a structural silicone bond that carries wind load and glass weight for
decades. The silicone maker's project-specific approval and the façade engineer's
joint design govern — this is the method around them.

## 1. What's designed, and what you control
- The engineer/silicone maker sets **bite** (contact width on glass and frame) and
  **glueline thickness** (the gap, set by the spacer tape) from wind load and
  movement — commonly a minimum of ~6 mm each, bite usually larger. You must build
  exactly that. Measure your spacer and frame with a gauge, not by eye.
- Every material that touches the silicone (spacer tape, setting blocks, gaskets,
  frame finish, IGU secondary seal) must have passed the maker's **compatibility and
  adhesion testing** for this project. Don't substitute a "similar" tape.
- Work in a controlled shop environment where possible (factory glazing beats site
  SSG) — dust, humidity and temperature matter.

## 2. Surface preparation: two-rag wipe
1. Use the approved solvent (often isopropyl alcohol or a specific cleaner) in a
   clean dispensing bottle — never dip rags into the can.
2. Wipe with solvent on one clean, lint-free white cloth; **immediately** wipe dry
   with a second clean cloth **before the solvent evaporates**, lifting the
   dissolved dirt off. Change cloths frequently.
3. Apply primer where the maker requires it (often on some frame finishes, rarely on
   glass), and observe its open time window.
4. Glaze within the time limit after cleaning set by the maker (often an hour or so;
   re-clean if exceeded).

## 3. Application
- Two-part silicone: check the mixer with a **butterfly test** (fold a bead in paper,
  open it — uniform colour, no streaks) and a **snap/cure time** test (a cup sample
  should reach snap time within the maker's range) at start-up, after breaks, and
  when changing drums.
- Gun the joint full in one direction, pushing the bead ahead of the nozzle to drive
  out air; no voids, no skins. Tool firmly to press silicone against both substrates
  and leave a flush or slightly concave surface.
- Do not move units until the maker's minimum cure time; unitised panels typically
  sit some days before transport (one-part silicones far longer — they cure from
  moisture inward).

## 4. QC records (expect an auditor to ask)
- Batch numbers, mix tests, cleaning and primer times per unit.
- **Adhesion tests:** a sample bead on the actual substrate, cured, then peeled at
  90°/180°; result must be **cohesive failure** (silicone tears, leaving silicone on
  the substrate), not adhesive failure (lifts clean).
- **Deglazing** a random sample unit (per spec frequency) to check fill, bite and
  adhesion in the real joint. Practice references include ASTM C1401 guidance and
  EAD/ETAG 002 in Europe — confirm which your project uses.

## Gotchas
- Solvent left to evaporate on the surface → dirt redeposited → adhesive failure.
- Using the wrong spacer tape → incompatibility discolouring or loss of adhesion.
- Air entrapped behind the bead; a joint that looks full and isn't.
- Moving panels too soon.

## Verify
- [ ] Bite and glueline measured to design on every unit sampled.
- [ ] Only project-approved materials touch the silicone.
- [ ] Two-rag clean and primer times logged; within open time.
- [ ] Butterfly and snap tests pass at each start-up.
- [ ] Adhesion peel tests show cohesive failure; deglaze checks recorded.
