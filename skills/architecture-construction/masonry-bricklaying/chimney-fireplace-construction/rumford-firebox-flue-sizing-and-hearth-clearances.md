# Rumford firebox, flue sizing and hearth clearances

**Goal:** an open fireplace and chimney that draws cleanly, doesn't set the house on
fire, and passes inspection. Codes rule here: US IRC R1001–R1003 (and local
amendments), UK Approved Document J, etc. Numbers below are typical — always check your
code and the flue liner maker's data. (Lining and sweeping existing flues is covered in
`repair-maintenance.chimney-sweeping.flue-liner-repair.stainless-liner-sizing-and-installation`.)

## 1. Firebox
- **Firebrick** (refractory) lining laid in thin refractory mortar joints (~3–6 mm),
  backed by solid masonry. IRC-type rules call for minimum total back/side wall
  thickness (e.g. 8 in / 200 mm including a 2 in / 50 mm firebrick lining or 10 in
  solid masonry) — verify.
- **Rumford proportions** (tall, shallow, splayed — radiates more heat into the room):
  - Opening height ≈ opening width (up to about the same; never much less than ~⅔).
  - Firebox depth ≈ ⅓ of opening width (and at least ~12 in/300 mm on small ones).
  - Back wall width ≈ depth; sides splayed so they reflect heat out.
  - **Throat** depth only ~3–4 in (75–100 mm), with a streamlined (rounded) breast.
- Conventional fireplaces have deeper fireboxes with sloping back; whichever, build to a
  proven template — improvised geometry smokes.

## 2. Throat, smoke chamber, damper
- **Damper** at or above the throat (throat damper), or a top-sealing damper at the
  chimney top.
- **Smoke shelf** (conventional) below the smoke chamber; Rumfords use a streamlined
  throat with minimal shelf.
- **Smoke chamber:** corbelled or parged smooth; sides slope inward no steeper than
  ~45° (IRC limits the slope, ~45° from vertical) to the flue. Parge it smooth to
  reduce turbulence and creosote build-up.

## 3. Flue sizing
- Flue area relative to fireplace opening area: commonly **~1:10** for round flues and
  **~1:8** for square/rectangular (rectangular corners don't flow), up to 1:12 for tall
  chimneys — IRC gives tables by chimney height. Rumfords are often sized at ~1:10 of
  the opening.
- Use **clay flue liners** (or listed liner systems) jointed with refractory mortar,
  with an air space (not packed solid) between liner and surrounding masonry to allow
  expansion, per code.
- Offsets: minimise; limits on angle (often ≤ 30° from vertical).

## 4. Chimney height and top
- The **3-2-10 rule** (US): chimney top at least 3 ft above where it passes through the
  roof and 2 ft higher than anything within 10 ft horizontally. UK rules differ (e.g.
  heights relative to ridge and openings in Approved Document J).
- **Crown** (wash) sloped to shed water with an overhang/drip and an expansion joint
  around the liner; **cap** with spark arrestor where required. Flashing and a
  **cricket** behind wide chimneys on the upslope side.

## 5. Hearth and clearances
- **Constructional hearth** of non-combustible material in front of and under the
  fire. Typical IRC: extends ≥ 16 in (400 mm) in front and 8 in (200 mm) each side for
  openings < 6 ft², more for larger; UK figures similar but defined differently — check.
- **Clearances to combustibles:** wood framing kept off the chimney by an air space
  (e.g. 2 in / 50 mm interior, 1 in / 25 mm exterior walls, per IRC), firestopped with
  non-combustible material; mantels and trim kept away from the opening (e.g. 6 in /
  150 mm, plus more for projecting parts).

## Gotchas
- Flue too small for a big feature opening → smokes into the room.
- Sharp throat edges and rough smoke chambers → poor draft.
- Timber built tight against the chimney breast.
- Liners packed solid in mortar → crack from expansion.

## Verify
- [ ] Firebox geometry matches the chosen template; firebrick in refractory mortar.
- [ ] Flue area ratio checked against the opening and chimney height.
- [ ] Chimney height meets code rule; crown, cap, flashing done.
- [ ] Hearth extensions and combustible clearances met.
- [ ] Draft tested (smoke test) before handover; inspection signed off.
