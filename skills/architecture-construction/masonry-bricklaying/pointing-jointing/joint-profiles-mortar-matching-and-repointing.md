# Joint profiles, mortar matching and repointing

**Goal:** joints that shed water, match the wall, and are softer than the masonry, so
the joints — not the bricks or stones — take the weathering.

## 1. Joint profiles (new work)
- **Bucket handle / concave** (tooled with a round jointer): compresses the surface,
  sheds water well — the default for most facing brickwork.
- **Weather-struck:** sloped from slightly recessed at the top to flush at the bottom,
  shedding water out.
- **Flush:** cut flush and rubbed; common in heritage and with soft bricks.
- **Recessed (raked):** strong shadow, but forms a ledge that holds water — avoid in
  exposed walls and with frost-susceptible bricks.
- Tool joints when mortar is **thumbprint hard** (firm, takes an impression without
  sticking). Too wet = smeared faces; too dry = burnished, crumbly.
- Consistency: same time after laying, same tool, same pressure — otherwise joint
  colour varies across a wall.

## 2. Diagnose before repointing
- Repoint when joints are recessed beyond ~5–10 mm, cracked, loose or missing, not
  just because they look old.
- Find the cause: leaking gutters, failed copings, rising damp, or **hard cement
  pointing** on soft masonry (cement pointing makes brick faces spall around it).

## 3. Matching the mortar
- For heritage work: get the old mortar **analysed** (lab) or at least break a sample,
  dissolve the binder in dilute acid to see the sand, and match **sand** colour,
  grading and texture — sand is what you see.
- Binder: lime (NHL 2/3.5, lime putty, hot-mixed lime) for traditional masonry; must be
  weaker and more permeable than the masonry. Modern masonry: match the original mix
  designation.
- Make **trial panels** and let them dry for days (mortar lightens a lot as it dries)
  before approval.

## 4. Raking out
- Rake to **~2–2.5 × the joint width** deep (typically ~15–25 mm), square-backed, to
  sound mortar. Hand tools (plugging chisel, joint raker) for soft or heritage work;
  angle grinders only on bed joints by skilled operators, never on perps or narrow
  joints of historic masonry — one slip cuts the arris. Consider oscillating tools.
- Don't widen joints or chip arrises. Brush and flush out dust; dampen the joint
  (not soaking) so the masonry doesn't suck the water out of the new mortar.

## 5. Repointing
1. Fill deep joints in **layers** (~10 mm each), letting each firm up before the next.
2. Press mortar in hard with a pointing iron from a hawk, perps first then beds.
3. Finish to the agreed profile; on lime, often slightly recessed and **brushed**
   (beaten with a stiff bristle brush when firm) to expose sand and compact the surface.
4. Keep mortar off the faces; clean smears when firm, not wet.
5. **Cure:** protect from sun, wind, rain and frost; mist lime work and cover with damp
   hessian for several days. Don't work lime in frost conditions (~ below 5 °C).

## Gotchas
- Cement pointing on soft brick → spalled faces.
- Wet-looking trial panel approved — dries two shades lighter.
- Grinder overcuts ruining arrises.
- Shallow raking (5 mm) — new pointing falls out in a winter.

## Verify
- [ ] Cause of joint failure identified and fixed first.
- [ ] Mortar softer than the masonry; sand and colour matched on a dried trial panel.
- [ ] Raked to depth, square, without damaged arrises.
- [ ] Filled in layers; profile consistent across the wall.
- [ ] Curing protection used; no frost exposure.
