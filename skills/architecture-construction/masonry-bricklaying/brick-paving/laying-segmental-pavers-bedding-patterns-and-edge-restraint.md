# Laying segmental pavers: bedding, patterns and edge restraint

**Goal:** a paved area that drains, doesn't rut or creep, and keeps its pattern
lines straight for decades. (Pavement design choices — build-up depth by traffic,
permeable systems — are in
`architecture-construction.landscape-architecture.hardscape-detailing.paving-build-ups-edges-steps-and-retaining-walls`.)

## 1. Levels and excavation
- Set **finished levels** with falls (typically ~1:40–1:60) away from buildings to a
  drain or soft edge; finished paving ~150 mm below DPC except at detailed thresholds.
- Excavate to formation = finished level − paver thickness − bedding − sub-base.
  Remove topsoil and soft spots; compact the formation.
- Use a geotextile on soft clay subgrades if specified.

## 2. Sub-base
- Well-graded crushed stone (Type 1/MOT or equivalent): ~100 mm for patios/footpaths,
  ~150–200 mm+ for driveways (more on weak ground). Lay in layers of ~100 mm max,
  each compacted with a plate compactor (several passes) — layers too thick don't
  compact at the bottom.
- Shape the sub-base to the final falls; its surface tolerance should be within ~±10 mm.

## 3. Edge restraints first
Pavers lock together only if they can't spread. Lay kerbs/edging on and haunched in
concrete (or a mortared soldier course on a concrete bed) **before** bedding, at
finished level. Every free edge needs one, including against lawns.

## 4. Bedding sand
- Sharp, well-graded sand (grit sand / concrete sand), consistently damp.
- Screed to a loose depth of ~30–50 mm (it compacts ~10 mm), using **screed rails**
  (pipes or battens) and a straightedge. Don't walk on the screeded bed; lay from the
  paved surface working forward.
- Never use the bedding to fix sub-base levels — uneven bedding = uneven settlement.

## 5. Laying the pattern
- **Herringbone at 45° or 90°** is the best interlock for vehicles; stretcher bond
  laid across the traffic direction works for light use; basketweave and stack are
  decorative/pedestrian.
- Start from a straight edge or a string line, at a corner, with the pattern set out
  square. Lay pavers **hand-tight** with the joint (2–5 mm, many pavers have spacer
  nibs) — don't hammer them into contact.
- Check pattern lines with string lines every few rows; nudge them straight with a
  lump hammer and bolster *before* compaction.
- Cuts: lay full pavers first, then cut infill pieces with a block splitter or
  diamond saw (wet cut — silica dust). Avoid pieces smaller than about a third of a
  paver at edges; plan a border course to take cuts.

## 6. Compaction and jointing
1. Sweep clean, then **plate-compact** with a rubber mat on the plate (avoids scuffing),
   2–3 passes in different directions; keep ~1 m back from unrestrained edges.
2. Brush **kiln-dried jointing sand** into the joints, compact again, refill —
   repeat until joints are full. Polymeric sands are an option (activate exactly as the
   maker says, or they haze the surface).
3. Remove broken units after compaction and replace.

## Gotchas
- No edge restraint → pavers creep and joints open.
- Thick bedding sand compensating for a bad sub-base → ruts.
- Pattern lines wandering because no string line was checked.
- Small slivers at edges that work loose.

## Verify
- [ ] Falls checked with a level and straightedge; no ponding after a hose test.
- [ ] Sub-base compacted in layers to the right depth.
- [ ] Edges restrained everywhere before laying.
- [ ] Pattern lines straight; no cuts < ~⅓ paver at edges.
- [ ] Joints full after compaction; no rocking pavers.
