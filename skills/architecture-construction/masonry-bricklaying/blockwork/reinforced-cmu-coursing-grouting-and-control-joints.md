# Reinforced CMU: coursing, grouting and control joints

**Goal:** a block wall that's straight and to height, with reinforcement where the
engineer put it, cores fully grouted, and control joints where cracks would otherwise
choose for themselves. Reinforcement, grout and joint locations come from the
structural drawings — this is how to build them.

## 1. Units and coursing
- Common formats: US nominal 8 × 8 × 16 in (actual 7⅝ × 7⅝ × 15⅝ in, ⅜ in joints → 8 in
  course); UK/EU 440 × 215 mm face (10 mm joints → 225 mm course).
- Lay out the first course dry to check bond and opening positions; block cuts are
  expensive and ugly — adjust openings to block modules at design stage where you can.
- First course on a **full mortar bed** to take out footing irregularities; above
  that, **face-shell bedding** (mortar on the two face shells only) is common for
  hollow units, but **webs are bedded around cores that will be grouted** so grout
  can't leak sideways (and full bedding per spec where required).

## 2. Laying
- Corner leads, gauge (storey pole) and line as in brickwork (see
  `architecture-construction.masonry-bricklaying.bricklaying.building-corner-leads-and-cavity-walls-to-line-and-gauge`).
- Keep vertical cores aligned for reinforced cells — check with a light or plumb line
  down the cell. Clean mortar **fins** projecting into cells that will be grouted.
- **Bed-joint (ladder/truss) reinforcement** at the spacing specified (e.g. every
  second or third course) and in the courses above and below openings.
- Large-format and dense blocks are heavy (a dense 440 × 215 × 140 mm block is ~20 kg+):
  follow handling limits, use lighter blocks or lifting aids where required, and build
  to safe lift heights per day (often ~1.5 m for fresh blockwork in wind; check spec).

## 3. Reinforcement
- **Vertical bars** in the specified cells, lapped to starter bars from the footing
  by the lap length shown (often ~40–60 bar diameters), held centred with **rebar
  positioners**.
- **Bond beams** with lintel/bond-beam units at specified levels and over openings,
  horizontal bars continuous round corners.

## 4. Grouting
- Use **grout**, not mortar: a fluid mix with high slump (~200–275 mm / 8–11 in) so it
  flows round bars. Fine or coarse grout per cell size.
- **Low-lift:** grout as you go, in pours up to ~1.5 m (5 ft), stopping ~40 mm below the
  top of a course so the next pour keys in.
- **High-lift** (full storey height in one go, in lifts) requires **cleanout openings**
  at the base of each grouted cell, cleaned and inspected before closing. Rules vary by
  code (e.g. TMS 602 in the US) — follow the spec.
- **Consolidate** each lift by mechanical vibration (or rodding for small pours), and
  reconsolidate after initial water loss.

## 5. Control (movement) joints
- Vertical joints at spacing set by the designer (commonly ~6–7.5 m in long walls, at
  changes in height or thickness, near corners, and at openings) — a continuous
  vertical joint through the wall, with bed-joint reinforcement stopped either side
  (unless detailed to cross), a shear key or debonded ties for lateral restraint, and
  backer rod + sealant at the face.

## Gotchas
- Mortar fins and droppings blocking cells → voids in grout.
- Grouting with mortar or a stiff mix → honeycombing round bars.
- Missing cleanouts on high-lift pours.
- Control joints omitted to "look tidier" — the wall cracks there anyway, crookedly.

## Verify
- [ ] Coursing on module; openings at block dimensions.
- [ ] Bars in the right cells, lapped, centred.
- [ ] Cells clean before grouting; lift heights within limit; consolidated.
- [ ] Bed-joint reinforcement at spec spacing.
- [ ] Control joints continuous and sealed at the drawn positions.
