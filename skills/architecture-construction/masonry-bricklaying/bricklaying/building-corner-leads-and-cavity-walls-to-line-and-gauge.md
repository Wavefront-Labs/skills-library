# Building corner leads and cavity walls to line and gauge

**Goal:** a facing wall that's plumb, level, to gauge (consistent course heights),
with even perps and a bond that works round openings — and a cavity that stays dry.
Dimensions below are for UK/European metric bricks (215 × 102.5 × 65 mm, 10 mm
joints, 75 mm gauge); US modular brick works on the same principles with different numbers.

## 1. Set out dry
- Lay the first course **dry** along the foundation/DPC line with perp gaps (use a
  10 mm spacer). Adjust so openings, piers and corners fall on whole or half bricks;
  close any cut (a "broken bond") under a window, not at a corner.
- Mark brick positions on the footing and the openings' widths.

## 2. Gauge
- Make a **gauge (storey) rod**: marks at 75 mm per course from DPC, plus heights of
  sills, lintel bearings, floor joists and wall plate. Every corner is checked to it.
- Levels: transfer a datum from the site TBM to each corner.

## 3. Mortar
- Mix designation from the spec (e.g. 1:1:6 cement:lime:sand, or a premix/plasticised
  mix to the stated class). **Consistent batching by gauge box or bucket**, not shovels —
  colour changes otherwise. Retemper only within the working life; discard stiffening mortar.
- Bed: spread, furrow lightly, lay to the line; don't fill the cavity side with snots.

## 4. Corner leads
1. Lay the first course of the corner on a full bed, level and square (3-4-5 check).
2. **Rack back** (step) the lead — typically 5–7 courses high at the corner, one brick
   less each course along each wall.
3. Each course: **level** along the top, **plumb** the corner and each face at the
   ends, **gauge** with the rod, and check the rack is straight by laying the level
   diagonally along the stepped ends (the "racking" check). Correct while mortar is soft.
4. Build both corner leads before the wall between them.

## 5. Running in to the line
- Set **line and pins** (or corner blocks) at the top arris of the course; brick
  just touches the line (1 mm behind — never pushes it). Lay from both ends to the
  middle; the last brick is the closer, fully buttered.
- Keep perps aligned vertically every other course (plumb-pointing the perps with
  the level occasionally).
- Joint finish when "thumbprint hard" — see
  `architecture-construction.masonry-bricklaying.pointing-jointing.joint-profiles-mortar-matching-and-repointing`.

## 6. Cavity walls
- **Wall ties** to spec type and spacing (commonly ~900 mm horizontally × 450 mm
  vertically, staggered, with extra ties within ~225 mm of openings and movement
  joints at ~300 mm vertical spacing). Ties slope slightly to the outer leaf, drip
  centred in the cavity.
- **Cavity insulation** boards held tight against the inner leaf with retaining clips.
- **Keep the cavity clean:** a cavity batten lifted course by course, or clean-out
  bricks at the base. Mortar droppings bridge the cavity and carry damp across.
- **Cavity trays** with stop ends over lintels and openings and at abutments, with
  **weep vents** at ~900 mm max centres (at least two per opening) above them.
- DPC continuous, lapped, ~150 mm above finished ground.

## Gotchas
- Corners built without the gauge rod → courses don't line up at the second corner.
- Pushing the line out with each brick → a bowed wall.
- Cavity full of droppings → damp patches inside.
- Lime-rich mortar laid in frost; protect new work (hessian/insulated covers) and
  don't lay below about 3 °C on a falling thermometer.

## Verify
- [ ] Bond set out dry; cuts in agreed positions.
- [ ] Corners plumb, level, to gauge; rack straight.
- [ ] Wall within tolerance (e.g. ±8 mm plumb per storey height — check spec).
- [ ] Ties at spec spacing; cavity clean; trays and weeps in place.
- [ ] Mortar colour consistent; joints finished uniformly.
