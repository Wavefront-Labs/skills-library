# Master planning — structure, land budget, yield and staging

**Goal:** a plan for a large site or precinct that fits the land, works as a place,
delivers the required yield, and can actually be built in sequence.

## 1. Site analysis (constraints and opportunities)
Map, as layers on one base:
- Hard constraints: flood extents, steep slopes, contamination, high-voltage easements,
  pipelines, protected habitat and trees, heritage, aircraft noise, buffers to industry.
- Edges and connections: existing streets, likely access points, public transport,
  neighbouring uses and their frontages.
- Assets: views, watercourses, mature trees, landmarks, solar orientation, prevailing wind.
- Ownership and parcel boundaries — fragmented ownership affects staging.
Overlay to find the **developable envelope**. Be honest about it; yield figures built on
land that floods won't survive scrutiny.

## 2. Structure before buildings
Fix the long-lived elements first, in this order:
1. **Movement network:** connections to the surrounding grid at every reasonable point;
   a street hierarchy (main street, connector, local, lanes); walkable block sizes
   (often in the range ~80–150 m sides for permeable urban areas); public transport route
   and stops with walk catchments.
2. **Open-space and blue-green network:** drainage corridors as linear parks, a
   hierarchy of parks with catchment distances, retained trees.
3. **Centres and community uses:** at the junction of movement routes and transit,
   within walking distance of most homes.
4. **Development blocks** and density gradient — higher near centres and transit.

## 3. Land budget (gross to net)
Build a table:
- Gross site area
- minus undevelopable land (constraints)
- minus primary infrastructure (arterials, drainage reserves, major parks, schools)
- = **Net developable area (NDA)**
- minus local streets and local open space
- = **Net residential/commercial land**
Local streets typically consume roughly 20–30% of a net area; check against your own
layout, not a percentage assumption.

## 4. Yield testing
- Assign typologies to blocks (detached, terraces/townhouses, apartments, mixed-use)
  with realistic lot sizes, heights and parking solutions.
- Calculate dwellings and floorspace per block; sum to totals and density (dwellings
  per net hectare).
- **Test a few blocks in detail** (lot layouts, building footprints, parking) — a block
  that looks fine at 1:5000 often fails at 1:500 because of parking or corner lots.
- Run options (e.g. low/medium/high density scenarios) and report yield, open space per
  capita, and infrastructure demand for each.

## 5. Staging
- Stage 1 must work on its own: an access, connection to trunk services, a first piece of
  park, and enough frontage to sell or let.
- Sequence along trunk infrastructure (sewer, water, road) to avoid stranded investment.
- Deliver community facilities and transit early enough to shape behaviour — a common
  failure is homes first, bus service a decade later.
- Show interim conditions (temporary turning heads, temporary drainage) and who funds
  what (developer contributions, public capital works).

## 6. Documentation
Structure plan, land budget table, yield schedule, staging plan, infrastructure
schedule, and design principles/codes for later development applications.

## Gotchas
- Overlapping deductions (counting a drainage corridor as both constraint and open space) inflate yield.
- Ignoring land ownership: a perfect street on someone else's land never gets built.
- Density targets without typology testing produce undeliverable plans.

## Verify
- [ ] Constraints mapped and the developable envelope defined.
- [ ] Movement and open-space structure fixed before blocks.
- [ ] Gross-to-net land budget reconciles to total site area.
- [ ] Yield tested in detail on representative blocks.
- [ ] Stage 1 functions standalone; infrastructure sequencing and funding shown.

*Uncertainty:* block-size and street-percentage figures are typical ranges from
practice, not standards; local guidance governs.
