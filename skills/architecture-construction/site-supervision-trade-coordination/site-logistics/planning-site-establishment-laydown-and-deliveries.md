# Planning site establishment, laydown and deliveries

**Goal:** a site where people, vehicles and materials move without conflict, materials
land close to where they're used, and the neighbours aren't your biggest risk.

## 1. Site establishment plan
Draw the site plan (to scale) with:
- **Boundary/hoarding** and gates; security and signage.
- **Access:** vehicle gates sized for the largest delivery (articulated trucks, concrete
  pumps, mobile cranes) with turning circles checked; **separate pedestrian entry**.
- **Welfare:** site office, toilets, drying/lunch rooms, first aid, sized for peak
  headcount (local rules give ratios).
- **Services:** temporary power, water, drainage, lighting, connection points.
- **Plant:** crane position and reach radii, hoists, pump positions, wash-out area for
  concrete trucks (contained).
- **Laydown and storage**, **waste area**, **parking** (often off-site with shuttles in
  cities).
- Emergency routes and assembly point.
Update the plan at each phase — the structure, façade and fit-out phases need different
layouts.

## 2. Traffic management
- One-way circulation where possible; avoid reversing (banksman where unavoidable).
- Physical separation between pedestrians and plant (barriers, not paint alone).
- Gate managed during deliveries; public footpaths protected or diverted per permit.
- Wheel wash/road sweeping to keep public roads clean (often a permit condition).

## 3. Crane and laydown
- Tower crane position decided from the heaviest lift at the furthest radius and from
  delivery and laydown positions. Check over-sail of neighbours and rail/roads (permissions
  often needed).
- Laydown inside the crane's hook coverage at the capacity needed, on firm level ground,
  dunnage under materials, separated by trade and labelled.
- Minimise double handling: plan where each package lands and how it gets to its floor.

## 4. Deliveries
- **Delivery booking system:** each trade books a slot (e.g., 30–60 min windows) a day or
  more ahead; no slot, no entry. Prevents queuing trucks on the street and crane conflicts.
- Just-in-time for bulky items in tight sites; check lead times in the lookahead.
- Receiving check: quantity, damage, correct spec vs order; record delivery dockets.
- Respect local delivery-hour restrictions.

## 5. Waste
- Separate streams (timber, metal, concrete/masonry, plasterboard, general, hazardous)
  for recycling targets and cost; plasterboard often must be separated from general
  waste.
- Skips positioned for crane/forklift access; chutes from upper floors with enclosed
  drops.
- Hazardous waste (asbestos, chemicals) handled by licensed contractors with records.

## 6. Neighbours and the public
- Notify neighbours before works start and before noisy phases; give a contact number.
- Working hours, noise, dust and vibration limits per permit; monitor where required.
- Condition survey of adjoining properties before work (photos) — protects against
  claims.

## Gotchas
- Gate too narrow for the crane delivery → road closure scramble.
- Laydown outside crane reach → double handling for months.
- No delivery booking → trucks queued in the street and police complaints.
- Concrete washout into stormwater → environmental offence.

## Verify
- [ ] Current site plan posted at gate and office, matching the phase.
- [ ] Swept-path check done for the largest vehicle.
- [ ] Pedestrian/vehicle separation physically in place.
- [ ] Delivery schedule for this week issued; laydown inside crane coverage.
- [ ] Waste streams separated and recorded; neighbours notified.
