# Running daily site safety: inductions, toolbox talks and permits

**Goal:** everyone on site knows today's hazards and controls, high-risk work only
happens under a checked plan and permit, and incidents are handled and learned from.

## 1. Inductions
- Nobody works before induction: site rules, emergency procedures (assembly point,
  alarm, first aid, nearest hospital), site-specific hazards (live services, crane zones,
  traffic routes), PPE, sign-in/out.
- Check tickets/licences relevant to their work (e.g., working at height, plant,
  scaffolding, rigging — names vary by country). Copy and record.
- Language and literacy: use interpreters/visual materials; confirm understanding
  with questions, not just a signature.

## 2. Toolbox talks
- Short (10–15 min), at the work area, about **this week's** work: "tomorrow we're
  pouring L3; here's the pump exclusion zone and the edge protection changes."
- Ask the crew what worries them; they know where it's going wrong.
- Record topic, date, attendees. Repeat key ones when crews change.

## 3. SWMS / method statements / RAMS
- Required for high-risk work in most regimes (e.g., AU/NZ SWMS for HRCW; UK RAMS under
  CDM; US JHA/JSA practice).
- Check before work starts: is it **specific to this job** (not a generic template), does it
  follow the hierarchy of controls (eliminate → substitute → isolate/engineer → admin →
  PPE), and do the people doing the work know it?
- Then **check on the job** that the work matches it. If the method changes, stop and
  revise.

## 4. Permit-to-work
Use permits for: hot works, confined space entry, excavation near services, working on
live systems / isolations (lockout-tagout), working at height in some sites, crane lifts
over occupied areas.
- Permit names the work, location, time window, hazards, controls, isolations and the
  competent person.
- Physically check controls before signing (gas test results, isolations locked, fire
  extinguisher present).
- Close out the permit: area made safe, fire watch done (hot works typically 30–60 min
  after), isolations removed in order.

## 5. Hazard walks
- Daily walk, weekly formal inspection with a checklist: edges and openings, scaffold
  tags, housekeeping and access, electrical leads/RCDs, plant exclusion zones,
  excavations (shoring, edge setback), hazardous substances, PPE use.
- Fix on the spot if quick; otherwise record with owner and date.
- Lead by example: if the supervisor walks past an unguarded edge, the standard becomes
  "unguarded is fine."

## 6. Stop-work authority
Everyone can stop unsafe work. Make it real: thank people who use it, and never punish a
good-faith stop.

## 7. Incident response
1. Make the area safe; first aid; call emergency services if needed.
2. Preserve the scene for serious incidents; notify the regulator where required
   (notifiable incidents have strict timelines in most jurisdictions).
3. Record facts, photos, witness statements.
4. Investigate for root cause (not "worker careless"): what control failed or was missing?
5. Share the lesson in the next toolbox talk. Record near misses too — they're free lessons.

## Gotchas
- Generic SWMS that nobody read.
- Permit signed in the office without checking the site.
- Toolbox talks as a signature-collecting exercise.
- Not notifying the regulator on time after a serious incident.

## Verify
- [ ] Everyone on site today inducted and signed in.
- [ ] High-risk activities have job-specific, current SWMS/RAMS and the crew can explain them.
- [ ] Permits physically checked, displayed and closed out.
- [ ] Hazard walk done today; open actions have owners and dates.
- [ ] Incidents/near misses recorded and investigated.
