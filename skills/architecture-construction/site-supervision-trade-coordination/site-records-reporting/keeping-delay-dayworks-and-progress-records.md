# Keeping delay, dayworks and progress records

**Goal:** a contemporaneous record that lets anyone reconstruct what happened, when and
why — the evidence for extensions of time, variations and dispute defence. Records made
later from memory carry little weight.

## 1. The site diary (daily)
Whatever the tool, each day record: weather (and its effect), labour by trade and
numbers, plant, deliveries, work done by area, inspections, visitors, instructions
received (verbal included), problems, delays, accidents. Write facts in neutral language
and sign/date. (See the Procore skill for the app version.)

## 2. Delay records
When something holds up work:
1. **Record it the same day:** what was delayed, where, start time, cause (late
   information, client instruction, weather, utility, another trade, unforeseen ground),
   resources affected (crew of 6 standing, crane idle).
2. **Photograph** the condition with a date/time stamp.
3. **Notify** under the contract — most contracts have strict notice periods (sometimes
   days) and notice-of-delay requirements; missing them can forfeit the entitlement.
   Raise it to the PM immediately; the PM sends the formal notice.
4. Record the **end** of the delay and what was done to mitigate (re-deploying crews).
5. Keep a delay register: event, dates, notice sent, programme activity affected.

## 3. Dayworks (time and materials)
For work instructed to be paid on a dayworks basis:
- Daywork sheet per day per instruction: instruction reference, description, names/trades
  and hours, plant and hours, materials with quantities and dockets.
- **Get it signed by the client's representative the same day or next morning** — signed
  sheets confirm the resources, not necessarily entitlement; unsigned ones get disputed.
- Separate dayworks crews from contract work in the diary so hours aren't double-counted.

## 4. Instructions and variations
- Log every instruction (written or verbal): who, when, what. Confirm verbal instructions
  in writing within the contract's time ("confirmation of verbal instruction").
- Don't proceed with significant changes without the instruction recorded.

## 5. Progress photos
- **Structured:** same fixed points photographed weekly (e.g., from crane, each elevation,
  each floor plan grid), plus targeted photos of anything about to be covered.
- File by date and location; caption with grid/area. A photo whose location can't be
  identified is weak evidence.
- Drone surveys monthly on large sites where permitted.

## 6. Weekly progress report
- Percent complete by area/package vs planned; key milestones and forecast dates.
- Look-ahead summary, critical issues, RFIs/submittals overdue, delays recorded,
  safety stats, weather days.
- Update the **as-built programme** (actual start/finish dates) — critical for delay
  analysis later.

## 7. Storage
Back up records off site/cloud; never edit past entries — add a dated correction instead.
Keep for the contract's limitation period (often 6–12 years).

## Gotchas
- Delay noted in the diary but no contractual notice → claim lost.
- Daywork sheets signed weeks later → disputed hours.
- Photos with no date or location.
- Backdated edits to diaries destroy their credibility.

## Verify
- [ ] Today's diary complete and signed.
- [ ] Every delay event in the register has a notice date within the contract period.
- [ ] Daywork sheets signed within a day.
- [ ] This week's fixed-point photos taken and filed by location.
- [ ] As-built programme dates updated in the weekly report.
