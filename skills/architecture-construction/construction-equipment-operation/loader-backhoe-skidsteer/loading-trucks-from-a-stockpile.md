# Loading trucks from a stockpile

**Goal:** fill trucks quickly to their legal payload, evenly and without spillage,
damage or anyone in the loader's path — using a short, repeatable cycle.

Operate only with the licence/ticket and site induction your jurisdiction requires,
belt on, and the manufacturer's rated operating capacity respected.

## 1. Set up the loading area
- Level, firm floor at the face; grade it with the bucket between trucks if it ruts.
- Plan the **V-pattern**: truck positioned at about 45–60° to the face, so the loader
  backs out of the pile, turns through a short "V" and drives straight at the truck.
  The legs should be short — about one to two machine lengths.
- Trucks approach from the operator's side so you can see them; agree a horn/light
  signal for "move up", "stop" and "done". Drivers stay in the cab (or in a marked
  safe zone, per site rules).
- Pedestrians: exclusion zone around the loading area; no one walks behind a reversing
  loader.

## 2. Filling the bucket
1. Approach the pile square, bucket flat and just skimming the floor.
2. Enter in a low gear at steady speed; as the bucket penetrates, **raise the boom
   slightly and roll the bucket back in stages** ("crowd and curl"). This keeps
   the front wheels gripping and stops wheel spin.
3. Don't spin the tyres — spinning wears tyres fast and digs the floor; ease off the
   throttle and lift.
4. Aim for a heaped bucket without a spill-over lip. Typical fill factors: ~100–115%
   for loose sand/gravel, less for blasted rock (unverified — varies by material and
   bucket).
5. Back out with the bucket rolled back and low (about axle height) for stability.

## 3. Dumping into the truck
- Raise the boom as you travel toward the truck so it's at height on arrival — don't
  stop and lift at the side.
- First bucket goes to the **front** of the body (near the cab, but not against it),
  the next ones toward the rear, spreading the load over the axles.
- Dump slowly, shaking material out rather than dropping it from height; drop rock
  on a cushion of fines to protect the body.
- Count buckets: bucket payload × buckets should land on the truck's payload. Use
  the loader's onboard scale if fitted; otherwise calibrate by weighing a few trucks
  at a weighbridge.

## 4. Machine-specific notes
- **Skid steer / compact track loader**: rated operating capacity is commonly rated at
  50% of tipping load for wheeled machines and 35% for tracked ones (per the usual
  SAE/ISO rating basis; check the machine's decal). Carry low; travel up and down slopes
  with the heavy end uphill.
- **Backhoe loader**: stabilisers up and the backhoe stowed and locked (boom lock
  and swing lock) before loader work; it is rigid-framed, so plan wider turns than
  an articulated loader needs.
- **Wheel loader**: articulated steering reduces stability when turned hard with a
  raised load; straighten before lifting high.

## Gotchas
- Overloaded trucks are illegal on public roads (axle and gross limits) and damage the
  haul road; underloaded trucks waste money. Weigh, don't guess.
- Material stuck in the bucket corners (wet clay) silently reduces payload.
- Long, curved cycles double the cycle time — keep the V tight.
- Undercutting a tall face can bring it down on the machine; keep the pile height
  within the reach of the raised bucket and bench it if not.
- Reversing is where most loader strikes happen: use mirrors, cameras and the alarm,
  and look before every reverse.

## Verify
- [ ] V-pattern legs short; truck within the operator's sight line.
- [ ] Bucket filled with crowd-and-curl, no tyre spin, consistent heap.
- [ ] Load placed front-first and spread; no spillage over sides or tailgate.
- [ ] Truck payload checked (scale or weighbridge) within legal limits.
- [ ] Exclusion zone kept clear; no reversing near people.
