# Positioning a boom lift and planning rescue

**Goal:** get the platform to the work face on firm ground, without crushing the
operator against a structure or tipping the machine, and know exactly how someone
on the ground will bring you down if you can't.

Operators need the training/licence your jurisdiction requires (e.g. IPAF PAL card,
ANSI A92 training, an EWP ticket or licence for some booms in Australia). Follow the
manufacturer's manual and site rules; figures here are typical.

## 1. Plan before you drive
- Choose the right machine: scissor for vertical reach over firm, level floors; boom
  (telescopic for reach, articulating/knuckle for up-and-over access).
- Check working envelope: platform height **and** outreach to the work, plus the
  platform capacity (people + tools + materials). Many platforms are rated around
  200–250 kg (unrestricted) — read the plate, including the reduced zone on some booms.
- Overhead hazards: power lines (keep the exclusion distance for the voltage),
  beams, ducts, and anything you could be crushed against.
- Exclusion zone below for falling objects; barriers and signs.

## 2. Ground assessment
- Look for: trenches, covers, voids, soft verges, recent backfill, slab edges, slopes.
- Wheel or outrigger point loads on booms can be several tonnes; on suspended slabs
  get confirmation from the engineer of the slab's capacity (unverified — check the
  machine's ground pressure data and the slab design).
- Use spreader pads under outriggers where fitted.
- Stay within the machine's rated slope for travel and the level tolerance for
  elevating; most machines alarm and cut out beyond it — never bypass the tilt alarm.

## 3. Pre-use checks (each shift)
- Walk round: tyres, hoses, structural damage, oil leaks, guardrails, gate.
- Function test from the **ground controls first**, including emergency stop and
  emergency lowering, then from the platform.
- Check the load-sensing, tilt alarm and any secondary guarding (anti-entrapment bars,
  pressure-sensing switches) work.
- Logbook/inspection tag current.

## 4. Fall protection and approach
- Boom lifts: wear a full-body harness with a short **restraint** lanyard clipped to the
  designated anchor in the basket (not the guardrail). Restraint is meant to
  stop you reaching the edge, so keep the lanyard short enough that you cannot be
  thrown out (catapult effect) if the boom is struck or a wheel drops. Scissor lifts: requirements vary
  by jurisdiction and site (unverified — check local rules).
- Never climb on the rails or stand on boxes in the basket.
- Approach the work slowly with single functions, looking in the direction of travel
  and **above** you. Final approach on lowest speed.
- Driving with the platform elevated: only if the manufacturer allows it, at creep
  speed, on firm level ground.
- Entrapment: a person pinned against a structure may be unable to release the
  controls. Secondary guarding and the ground person are your mitigations.

## 5. Rescue plan (before going up)
Someone competent must be on the ground who:
1. Knows where the ground controls and the emergency stop are.
2. Can operate the ground-override and the emergency/auxiliary lowering (hand pump,
   battery auxiliary power or manual descent valve) — practised, not just read.
3. Has a way to call emergency services and knows the site address and access.
4. Knows the plan for a pinned operator: reverse the last function from the ground,
   carefully, as the manual describes.
Never work alone at height on an EWP.

## Gotchas
- Wind: most outdoor machines have a limit around 12.5 m/s (28 mph) (unverified —
  check the plate); indoor-only machines have zero wind rating.
- Overloading with materials (plasterboard, glass) is common and dangerous.
- Hidden ground hazards under grass or gravel.
- Battery low at height: plan charge levels before a long elevated task.

## Verify
- [ ] Machine suits the height, outreach and load; capacity plate read.
- [ ] Ground assessed, pads used, level within tolerance.
- [ ] Ground and platform controls, emergency stop and emergency lowering tested.
- [ ] Harness worn with restraint lanyard on the designated anchor (booms).
- [ ] Trained ground rescuer present and briefed; exclusion zone set below.
