# Drilling a bored pile to position and verticality

**Goal:** bore a pile at the set-out position, plumb (or to the design rake), to the
specified toe level, with a clean base and a complete record — without tipping the rig
on a weak platform.

Piling rigs are high-centre-of-gravity machines; work only with the right
licence/competency, the manufacturer's manual, the piling contractor's method
statement and the project specification. Numbers below are typical, not authoritative.

## 1. Working platform first
- Rig tracks must sit on a designed working platform with a certificate (in the UK a
  platform design and certificate is common practice following BR 470 guidance).
  Check the certificate covers **this** rig and its track pressures, including the
  worst case of drilling/extracting casing.
- Walk the route: backfilled trenches, soft spots, previous pile bores, slopes and
  edges. A rig tracking over a fresh pile bore is a classic overturn.
- Keep the platform maintained; ruts and puddles concentrate load.

## 2. Setting out over the peg
1. Surveyor sets out the pile position with offset pegs (typically two, at right
   angles, at a known distance) so the position can be recovered after the centre peg
   is lost.
2. Track the rig on so the mast is square to the pile; mast lowered for travel, raised
   only once in position and on level ground (per manual).
3. Lower the auger tip to the centre peg and check offsets with a tape from both
   reference pegs.
4. Typical position tolerance is around 75 mm in plan for onshore piles (unverified —
   check the specification; EN 1536 and project specs set the value).

## 3. Verticality
- Use the rig's mast inclinometer/display to bring the mast plumb in both axes (or to
  the design rake). Cross-check at the start with a spirit level or plumb on the
  kelly/auger, as displays drift.
- Typical verticality tolerance for bored piles is in the order of 1 in 75 to 1 in 100
  (around 1.3–2%) (unverified — your spec rules).
- Recheck after the first metre or two: the mast can settle as the platform loads up.
- Don't correct a drifting bore by pulling the mast over at depth — withdraw, re-plumb
  and redrill.

## 4. Drilling
**Kelly (rotary bored) piles:**
- Install temporary casing through unstable or water-bearing ground; drill inside it.
- Drill in passes, emptying the auger/bucket each time; spin off spoil away from the
  bore and the rig's path.
- Clean the base with a cleaning bucket; check depth with a weighted tape against the
  design toe level.
**CFA piles:**
- Drill continuously to toe; avoid excessive rotation for little advance ("flighting"),
  which draws soil up and loosens the ground around the pile.
- Concrete is pumped through the hollow stem as the auger is withdrawn; maintain
  positive concrete pressure and keep the auger tip embedded in concrete. Withdrawal
  rate is coordinated with the concrete volume shown on the rig instrumentation.
- Insert the reinforcement cage immediately after concreting.

## 5. Record
Pile log: pile number, position check, platform level, toe level, casing depth,
strata encountered, water, verticality reading, times, concrete volume vs theoretical
(over-break), and anomalies. Many rigs export this automatically; still sign it off.

## Gotchas
- Open bores are a fall hazard: cover or fence them immediately.
- Obstructions (old foundations, boulders) deflect the auger; stop and report rather
  than forcing.
- Spoil heaps near the rig track path create soft, uneven ground.
- Overhead lines and slewing mast clearance: check before raising the mast.
- Concrete volume much lower than theoretical on CFA suggests necking — report it.

## Verify
- [ ] Platform certificate valid for this rig; route checked.
- [ ] Position checked from reference pegs within tolerance.
- [ ] Mast plumbed in both axes and rechecked early in the bore.
- [ ] Toe level confirmed by measurement; base cleaned (bored) or concrete pressure kept (CFA).
- [ ] Pile log complete and bore made safe.
