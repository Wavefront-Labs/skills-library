# Placing a load at height using the load chart

**Goal:** put a pallet of blocks, a bundle of timber or a crate onto a slab edge,
loading bay or scaffold platform at height and reach, staying inside the machine's
rated capacity at every point of the movement.

Use only the load chart in the cab for that machine and attachment, follow
manufacturer instructions, your licence/training requirements and site lift plans.
Figures below are illustrative.

## 1. Know the load
- Weight from the delivery docket or packaging — not "it's a pallet of blocks".
  Wet materials weigh more.
- **Load centre**: the chart assumes a standard load centre (commonly 500 mm / 24 in
  from the fork heel). A long or deep load whose centre of gravity is further out
  has a lower real capacity.
- Check the attachment fitted: forks, bucket, jib, platform — each has its own chart.

## 2. Read the chart
Charts plot boom height (vertical) against forward reach (horizontal), with zones or
arcs marked by capacity, and boom angle/extension markings that match decals on the
boom.
1. Find the **landing point**: height above ground and horizontal distance from the
   front tyres or stabilisers to where the load centre will sit.
2. Locate that point on the chart and read the capacity zone it falls in. If it falls
   on a line, use the lower value.
3. Use the right chart: stabilisers down vs up, and on some machines tyres vs
   stabilisers and rotating vs fixed.
4. Plan the boom angle and extension letter/number you will need, then read it off the
   boom decals during the lift.
Example: if the slab edge is at 8 m height, 4 m out, and the chart zone there reads
1,500 kg, a 1,200 kg pallet is about 80% — workable, but a 1,600 kg one is not.

## 3. Set up
- Ground firm and level; check for trenches, backfilled services and slab edges
  under wheels and stabiliser pads.
- Use the **frame levelling** (sway) to bring the chassis level side-to-side **before**
  raising. Most manufacturers prohibit levelling with the boom raised.
- Lower stabilisers if the chart requires them; tyres just clear or as the manual says.
- Check the load moment indicator (LMI) is working; never override or bypass it.

## 4. Lifting sequence
1. Pick the load with the boom retracted and low; tilt the carriage back.
2. Travel to the set-up point with the load low (around 300 mm off the ground), boom
   retracted.
3. Stop, apply the park brake, neutral, then **raise first, extend last**. The
   capacity falls rapidly as you extend out.
4. Watch the LMI as you extend; if it moves into the warning zone, stop and retract —
   don't push it to cutout.
5. Lower onto the landing gently, level the forks, and withdraw by retracting rather
   than reversing the machine.
6. Retract fully and lower before moving the machine.

## Gotchas
- Driving with the boom raised or extended is a common cause of overturns.
- On a slope, capacity and stability fall; many machines are not rated to lift on
  slopes beyond a small percentage — check the manual.
- Wind on large sheets (plasterboard, formwork) adds side load; stop in strong wind.
- Lifting people requires an approved work platform and a man-basket chart, not forks.
- Suspended loads from forks or a jib need a specific chart and rigging, and often a
  rigger/dogman.
- The LMI warns about forward tipping; it does not protect against sideways tipping.

## Verify
- [ ] Load weight and load centre known; correct chart for attachment and set-up.
- [ ] Landing point plotted on the chart with margin; boom decal targets noted.
- [ ] Frame levelled and stabilisers down before lifting.
- [ ] Raised first and extended last; LMI never in the red.
- [ ] Load landed level and the boom stowed before travel.
