# Digging a trench to grade near buried services

**Goal:** cut a trench on line and to the design invert without striking a service,
undermining the machine or burying the crew, and leave a clean, shored, inspectable
excavation for the pipe gang.

Always work under your site's permit-to-dig system, local excavation regulations and
your operator licence/ticket. Figures below are typical, not authoritative.

## 1. Before the bucket touches the ground
- **Get the records.** Utility plans via the one-call service (811 in the US, Before You
  Dig Australia, utility searches in the UK). Plans are indicative only — depth and
  position are often wrong by a metre or more.
- **Locate on site.** Cable avoidance tool and signal generator ("cat and genny") or a
  professional locator; mark services with paint/flags along the whole run.
- **Pothole every crossing and every service within the tolerance zone** by hand or
  vacuum excavation. A common rule is no machine digging within about 500 mm (UK) or
  the "tolerance zone" of 18–24 in (US, varies by state) either side of a marked line
  (unverified for your jurisdiction — check the local code and the asset owner's
  conditions). Overhead lines need their own exclusion distance from the boom.
- **Agree the plan**: line, invert levels at each end, trench width, spoil side,
  shoring method, access/egress ladders, and who is the spotter.

## 2. Set up the machine
1. Track the excavator in line with the trench, tracks square, with the final drives
   (sprockets) at the rear so you dig over the idlers — better stability, and you can
   track straight back as the trench advances.
2. Spoil on the opposite side to the pipe stockpile and the shoring delivery. Keep the
   spoil heap back from the edge — 0.6 m (2 ft) is the US minimum; many sites require
   at least 1 m or the trench depth (check your site rules).
3. Fit a digging/trenching bucket matching the trench width; a toothless ditching bucket
   is kinder near services once you are close.
4. Set up grade control: a rotating laser with a mast receiver on the stick, or
   2D/3D machine control. Benchmark the system at a known level before cutting and
   recheck it every few hours and after any knock.

## 3. Digging sequence
1. Strip topsoil/surfacing first and stockpile separately.
2. Dig in bays of a bucket length or two, pulling toward the machine. Keep the bucket
   vertical in the trench to get square walls; curling early leaves a bellied trench.
3. Take the last 100–150 mm (4–6 in) carefully with a flat pass to the receiver's
   on-grade indication — overdigging means imported bedding and lost time.
4. Check depth with a grade rod/staff from the pipe gang at each bay, not just the
   machine display.
5. Install shoring (trench box, hydraulic struts, or batter/bench) **before anyone
   enters**. US rule of thumb: protection required at 5 ft (1.5 m) and deeper unless
   in stable rock; a competent person decides below that. Other jurisdictions differ
   (unverified — follow your local regulation and geotechnical advice).
6. Leave a ramp or ladder within reach of workers (US: within 25 ft of lateral travel
   in trenches 4 ft or deeper).

## 4. Near a located service
- Expose it by hand/vac; once visible, you may machine-dig alongside at a controlled pace
  with a spotter who can see the service and the bucket teeth.
- Never use the bucket to lever, hook or "test" for a service.
- Support exposed pipes and cables across the trench — a sagging gas main can fracture.
- Unexpected tape, sand, tile, concrete slab or a colour change in the soil means
  **stop**: these are often warning markers above a service.

## Gotchas
- Laser bumped or receiver loose on the stick = an entire run off grade. Recheck benchmark.
- Water in the trench softens walls fast; pumping fines out undermines them.
- Vibration from the machine and traffic loads near the edge cause collapses; keep the
  excavator tracks back from an unsupported edge.
- Rain washes service marks away — re-mark before resuming.
- A struck service: stop, move people away, do not try to repair; follow the emergency
  procedure and notify the owner. Gas: no ignition sources, evacuate upwind.

## Verify
- [ ] Permit to dig signed; services located, marked and potholed at crossings.
- [ ] Grade system benchmarked at the start and rechecked during the run.
- [ ] Invert checked by staff at each bay, within the specified tolerance.
- [ ] Spoil set back from the edge; shoring/battering in place before entry.
- [ ] Ladders/ramps in place; exposed services supported and protected.
