# Fine grading a pad to level and slope

**Goal:** take a bulk-earthworked area down to the design level and crossfall within
the specified tolerance, with a uniform, compacted surface that drains and does not
need re-handling.

Operate only machines you are trained and licensed/ticketed on, follow site traffic
management and the manufacturer's manual. Tolerances below are typical examples.

## 1. Know the target
- From the drawings and the surveyor: finished level, falls/crossfall (e.g. 2–3% on a
  road subgrade, 1–2% on a pad for drainage), and tolerance — often ±20–25 mm on
  subgrade and tighter on subbase (unverified — your spec governs).
- Grade control: stakes/hubs with cut/fill marked ("blue tops" in the US), string lines,
  rotating laser, or 2D/3D GPS/total station machine control. Check machine control
  against a known benchmark at the start of each shift.

## 2. Dozer: getting close
1. Work with moisture near optimum — dry material won't hold a surface, wet material
   pumps and ruts.
2. Push in the direction of the long axis where possible; overlap passes by about a
   third of a blade width.
3. Keep the blade **loaded but not overloaded** — a full, rolling load carries the
   surface; an empty blade digs or skips. Ease the blade up as the load builds.
4. Feather small, early corrections. A large correction sets the tracks pitching and
   leaves waves at track-length spacing.
5. For final passes, use a light blade load in a low gear and float slightly; let the
   tracks run on finished grade where possible.
6. Six-way (PAT/VPAT) blades: tilt for crossfall, angle to drift material sideways.

## 3. Grader: trimming to finish
Blade setup for fine trim (starting points, adjust to material):
- Moldboard pitch: roughly vertical to slightly back for cutting; lean back for mixing
  and spreading; forward for a smoother finish in loose material.
- Blade angle (turntable): around 30–45° for spreading and moving a windrow; flatter
  for finishing so the material rolls rather than spills.
- Articulation: straight frame for finishing; crab only to keep wheels off the
  windrow or finished edge.
- Front wheel lean toward the side the material is pushed, to counter side draft.
Sequence:
1. Cut a reference edge first (along a kerb line, stake line or GPS edge).
2. Work from the high side to the low side, carrying a small windrow off the blade end.
3. Keep travel speed steady and slow (typically first or second gear) — speed changes
   show as waves.
4. Make your last pass a light "skim" with the toe cut minimal and the heel carrying a
   thin windrow, then pick that up or blade it to the edge.

## 4. Compact and check
- Roll as the surface is trimmed; a trimmed but uncompacted pad settles out of tolerance.
- Check with a level/staff or GPS rover on a grid (e.g. every 10 m and at changes of
  grade) and record cut/fill. Water test small pads: puddles mean birdbaths.

## Gotchas
- Chasing the stakes one by one creates a wavy surface; look several stakes ahead.
- Leaving a windrow overnight in rain softens the subgrade underneath it.
- Dozer tracks on loose fill re-disturb finished grade; finish with a grader or a
  smoothing pass in reverse.
- GPS base station moved or wrong calibration file → whole area off by a constant.
- Blade wear (worn cutting edges, rounded corners) prevents a crisp finish — check edges.

## Verify
- [ ] Machine control or stakes checked against a benchmark before trimming.
- [ ] Spot levels on a grid within the specified tolerance; crossfall measured, not assumed.
- [ ] Surface compacted after trimming and re-checked.
- [ ] No ponding, waves or loose windrows left on the finished area.
