# Setting up a boom pump and clearing blockages

**Goal:** get a boom pump set up stable and clear of hazards, deliver concrete
continuously to the placing crew, and deal with a blockage without a line burst or a
whipping end hose injuring anyone.

Operators must hold the licence/certification your jurisdiction requires and follow the
pump manufacturer's manual, which sets outrigger loads, boom limits and wind limits.
Figures below are illustrative.

## 1. Site set-up
- **Position**: within boom reach of the furthest point plus a margin, with the truck
  mixers able to reverse to the hopper without crossing outriggers. Check the boom's
  reach diagram including vertical clearance.
- **Overhead lines**: plan a set-up where no part of the boom can enter the
  exclusion distance for the line voltage; use a spotter. Line contact is the
  deadliest boom-pump event.
- **Ground**: outrigger loads on large pumps can be many tonnes per leg. Use the
  manufacturer's figure and pads/mats sized for the ground's bearing capacity. Keep
  outriggers away from trench edges, basement walls and backfill — a rule of thumb is
  at least the trench depth back from an unsupported edge (unverified — your local
  guidance governs).
- **Level**: within the manufacturer's tolerance (commonly around 3°), outriggers fully
  extended where the set-up chart requires. Never use partial outrigger set-ups unless
  the pump has a certified short-rig mode.
- Unfold the boom in the manufacturer's sequence, normally first section up first.

## 2. Prime and start
1. Wet the hopper, pipeline and boom; pump a primer (slurry or proprietary primer) so
   the first concrete doesn't lose paste to the dry line and block.
2. Discard the primer where the engineer allows — never into a structural element.
3. Agree communication with the hose hand: radio or hand signals for start, stop, more,
   less. The operator usually runs a belly-box remote standing where they can see the
   end hose.
4. Start slowly; build output once concrete is flowing steadily.

## 3. During the pour
- Keep the hopper above the agitator shaft/grill level: letting it run low pulls air into
  the line, which causes the end hose to whip and spray.
- Watch pressure: a sudden rise with stroke slowing is the classic early sign of a
  blockage. Stop, reverse a few strokes, then try forward again.
- The end hose is controlled by the hose hand, but never "kinked" and never stood
  astride. Nobody stands under the boom.
- Don't use the boom to drag hoses or lift anything. Keep the boom clear of formwork
  props and reinforcement.
- Stop in winds above the manufacturer's limit.

## 4. Clearing a blockage
1. **Stop pumping.** Reverse a few strokes to relieve pressure; check the gauge reads
   zero/pressure relieved before any clamp is touched.
2. Locate the blockage: tap the line — a blocked section sounds dull; check reducers and
   bends first, they block most.
3. Clear the exclusion zone around the joint to be opened; lower the boom section to
   ground level if possible.
4. Open the clamp with face and body to the side, never in line with the pipe, wearing
   eye protection.
5. Clear the plug, reassemble, check clamp pins and gaskets, re-prime if needed.
Never use compressed air to clear a boom without the manufacturer's procedure — a
compressed air ball shot is a known cause of fatalities.

## Gotchas
- Harsh, gap-graded, or low-slump mixes block; check the mix is pumpable before the pour.
- Worn pipes and couplings burst; inspect wall thickness per the maintenance schedule.
- Hot weather and long waits stiffen concrete in the line — keep it moving or wash out.
- Washout: catch water and slurry; don't discharge into drains.

## Verify
- [ ] Set-up clear of power lines with a spotter; outriggers on adequate pads, level.
- [ ] Line primed and primer disposed of correctly.
- [ ] Hopper kept above the minimum level; pressure watched throughout.
- [ ] Any blockage cleared only after pressure relief, out of line with the pipe.
- [ ] Boom folded in sequence, pipeline washed out and slurry contained.
