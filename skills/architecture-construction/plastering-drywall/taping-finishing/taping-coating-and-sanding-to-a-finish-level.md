# Taping and finishing drywall to a finish level

**Goal:** joints, fasteners and corners invisible under the final paint and lighting,
in three coats where possible, without over-sanding the face paper.

## 1. Agree the finish level first
Finish levels (Gypsum Association GA-214 in North America; AS/NZS 2589 uses Levels 3–5
similarly) set the job:
- **Level 4** — normal for flat/eggshell paint under ordinary light: tape + two further
  coats on joints, three coats on fasteners, all sanded.
- **Level 5** — Level 4 plus a thin skim of compound (or a high-build primer) over the
  *whole* surface. Specify it for gloss paint, long walls with raking light from windows,
  or dark colours. Asking for Level 5 after the job is done is expensive — raise it early.

## 2. Materials
- **Setting (chemical) compound** ("hot mud", 20/45/90-minute): hardens by reaction,
  shrinks little, strong. Use for bedding tape, filling gaps >3 mm, fast turnarounds.
  Mix only what you'll use in the working time — it will not wait.
- **Drying (premixed) compound**: all-purpose for bedding and coats; lightweight/topping for
  finish coats (sands easily). Shrinks as it dries; needs full drying between coats.
- **Paper tape** is strongest for flat joints and internal corners. **Mesh tape** only with
  setting compound (it cracks in premixed).
- Never bed tape in topping compound; it's too weak.

## 3. Sequence
1. **Pre-fill** gaps over 3 mm and damaged edges with setting compound. Let it set.
2. **Corner bead** on external corners: metal (nail/crimp), paper-faced metal (bedded in
   compound — less cracking), or vinyl. Check it's plumb and not proud of the wall plane.
3. **Bed the tape:** a full bed of compound on the joint, tape centred, wipe firmly
   with a 150 mm knife to squeeze out excess — leave enough under it that no dry spots
   remain (dry spots = bubbles later). Internal corners: fold tape, bed, wipe one side
   at a time or use a corner tool.
4. **Fasteners:** first coat at the same time.
5. **Second coat** (dry/set first): 250 mm knife on tapered joints; on butt joints feather
   wide — 300–400 mm each side, because there is no taper to hide the tape.
6. **Third coat**: 300–350 mm knife, thin, feathered to nothing at the edges.
7. **Sand** with 150–220 grit (or a pole sander/vacuum sander); sand the compound, not the
   paper. Fuzzy paper = scuffed; seal with primer, don't sand further.
8. **Prime** with a drywall primer-sealer. Painting straight on unsealed compound gives
   "flashing" (dull patches over joints).

## 4. Drying and conditions
- Premixed coats: typically 24 h between coats at ~20 °C / 50% RH; longer when cold or
  humid. Thick coats crack and shrink — several thin coats beat one fat one.
- Keep the room above ~10–13 °C during and after (check the product sheet) and ventilate,
  but don't blast heaters straight at fresh joints.

## Gotchas
- **Ridging / photographing joints:** usually under-feathered butts or coats applied
  before the last one dried.
- **Bubbles in tape:** not enough compound under it. Cut out, re-bed.
- **Nail pops** months later: framing shrinkage or fasteners not pulled tight. Re-drive a
  screw beside it, then recoat.
- Cracks at internal corners where wall meets ceiling: framing movement; some trades
  use a flexible or "floating" corner detail. (Uncertain which is best for your region.)

## Verify
- [ ] Hold a work light flat against the wall (raking light): no ridges, tape edges,
      craters or sanding scratches.
- [ ] Run a straightedge across butt joints: no hump over ~1–2 mm.
- [ ] Corner beads straight and the compound feathered past their flange.
- [ ] Whole surface primed before topcoat; Level 5 skim done if specified.
