# Hanging gypsum board — layout, fastening and the details that make finishing easy

**Goal:** get board on the frame so the taper does the work for the finisher: few butt
joints, none on the same stud as the next row, tight-but-not-forced fits, and every
fastener dimpled just below the paper without breaking it.

## 1. Before the first sheet
- **Check the frame**, not the board: run a 2 m straightedge across studs and joists.
  Over ~3 mm bow on a stud will telegraph through; plane, pack or replace it now.
- Confirm services are roughed in, inspected and protected (nail plates over pipes and
  cables within ~30 mm / 1¼ in of the stud face — check your local code figure).
- Confirm the **board type per room** from the drawings: standard, moisture-resistant
  (wet areas, but not as a tile substrate in showers unless the system says so), fire-rated
  (Type X / "fire" board), impact or acoustic board. Fire-rated walls are a *tested system*:
  board type, layers, stud gauge, screw type and spacing all have to match the listing.
- Stack board flat, dry and off the floor. Damp board sags on ceilings.

## 2. Layout — think about the joints first
- **Ceilings before walls**, so wall sheets support the ceiling edges.
- Run sheets **perpendicular to framing** where possible (stronger, bridges stud bow).
- Use the longest sheets you can handle to cut out butt joints. A wall 3.9 m long gets
  one 3.6 m sheet plus a cut, or better, a 4.2 m sheet trimmed.
- **Stagger butt joints** between rows — never on the same stud. Keep butts away from
  the centre of a wall that will be lit by a window (raking light shows everything).
- **No joints in line with door and window corners**: run the sheet past the opening
  and cut it out (an L-shaped piece). Joints at corners of openings crack.
- Top row first on walls, tight to the ceiling; leave the 10–12 mm gap at the floor
  (skirting covers it, and board wicks water from wet slabs).

## 3. Cutting and fitting
- Score face paper with a sharp knife, snap, cut the back paper. Rasp edges.
- Fit **loose, not forced**: about 3 mm gap. A forced sheet bulges or cracks at butts.
- Cut boxes with a spiral saw or keyhole saw; measure from the last sheet edge, not
  the room corner.

## 4. Fastening
- Screws over nails (fewer pops). Coarse thread for timber, fine thread self-drilling for
  light-gauge steel. Length: board thickness + ~20 mm into timber, ~10 mm through steel.
- Typical spacing (confirm against the manufacturer and code — fire-rated systems are
  often tighter): ceilings ~200 mm (8 in) on edges, ~300 mm (12 in) field; walls ~300 mm
  edges, ~400 mm (16 in) field. Keep 10–15 mm from paper edges.
- **Depth:** set the screw-gun clutch so the head sits just below the surface in a
  shallow dimple, paper unbroken. A broken face paper has no holding power — add another
  screw 40–50 mm away.
- Many manufacturers specify adhesive plus screws on walls to reduce fasteners;
  some fire systems forbid substituting adhesive. Follow the system sheet.

## 5. Ceilings
- Use a board lift or two people and T-props ("deadmen"). Never one person.
- Check the ceiling framing spacing against board thickness limits — 10 mm board
  on 600 mm centres will sag; that span usually needs 13 mm sag-resistant board
  (figures vary by brand).

## Gotchas
- Butt joints on the same stud = a ridge every finisher hates.
- Joints at window corners always crack later.
- Screw guns set too deep tear paper on every fastener.
- Mixing board types inside a rated wall voids the rating.

## Verify
- [ ] No butt joint lines up with the one in the row above or below.
- [ ] No joint starts at a corner of an opening.
- [ ] Run a hand over every screw: dimpled, no broken paper, none proud.
- [ ] Spacing and board type match the system sheet for any rated wall/ceiling.
- [ ] 10–12 mm gap at the floor; boards fitted loose at butts.
