# External render systems and movement joints

**Goal:** a render that stays bonded, sheds water and doesn't crack across the wall — by
matching the system to the substrate and putting movement where you want it.

## 1. Pick the system for the substrate
| System | Typical use | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Sand-cement (traditional stucco) | Brick, block | 2–3 coats, ~15–20 mm total. Cheap; cracks if mix too rich or cured badly. |
| Polymer-modified / acrylic | Block, cement board, over base coats | Thinner, more flexible, through-coloured texture finishes. |
| Monocouche (one-coat) | Block | Machine-sprayed, through-coloured; system-specific. |
| EIFS / ETICS | Over insulation boards | Adhesive + mechanical fixings, base coat with embedded mesh, thin finish. |
| Lime render | Solid-wall heritage masonry | Breathable; never cement over lime. |

Use the manufacturer's **whole system** (primer, base coat, mesh, beads, finish) —
warranties and fire classifications are for the system, not individual products.

## 2. Substrate preparation
- Sound, clean, free of efflorescence, paint and laitance. Rake out soft joints.
- Control suction (dampen high-suction blockwork; bonding agent/spatterdash on dense
  concrete) — same logic as internal plastering.
- Mixed substrates (block + concrete beam) get mesh across the junction, lapped ≥100 mm
  either side.

## 3. Coat strengths and the cement-rich trap
- Each coat equal or weaker than the one below. A traditional 3-coat might be 1:3 →
  1:4 → 1:5 or 1:6 (cement:sand, often with lime added). **Over-rich render shrinks and
  crazes.**
- Use clean, well-graded sharp sand; a dirty, single-size sand cracks.
- Key the base coat (scratch/comb) and let it cure (often ~3–7 days for cement) before
  the next coat; acrylic systems have shorter intervals — follow the sheet.

## 4. Beads and edges
- **Bell-cast / drip bead** at the base, above the damp-proof course — render must not
  bridge the DPC or touch the ground.
- Angle beads on corners, stop beads at openings and abutments. Use stainless or PVC
  externally (galvanised steel rusts through in coastal areas).
- Head of openings: drip detail so water doesn't run back under.

## 5. Movement (control) joints
- Put them where the structure moves: over substrate joints, at changes of
  substrate, at floor lines in timber frame, and at regular spacings on long walls
  (often quoted ~5–6 m for cement render on block; EIFS systems state their own).
  **Unverified rule of thumb** — always follow the system and structural engineer.
- Carry existing building movement joints straight through the render; never render over
  them.
- Panels roughly square (avoid long thin panels); add mesh diagonals at opening corners.

## 6. Weather and curing
- Not below ~5 °C or with frost forecast, not in direct hot sun, wind or rain.
- Cement render: keep damp (mist) for several days; protect with scaffold sheeting.
- Acrylic: protect from rain until cured (often 24–48 h).

## Gotchas
- Render bridging the DPC → rising damp in the wall.
- Diagonal cracks from opening corners = no mesh diagonals / no joint.
- Hard cement render on soft brick or lime mortar traps moisture and spalls the brick.
- EIFS impact damage at ground level — specify a high-impact mesh there.

## Verify
- [ ] Bell-cast bead sits above DPC with a clear gap to ground/paving.
- [ ] Movement joints line up with substrate and structural joints.
- [ ] Mesh diagonals at every opening corner.
- [ ] Tap test after cure: no hollow areas; no crazing under oblique light.
- [ ] System data sheet kept on file with batch numbers.
