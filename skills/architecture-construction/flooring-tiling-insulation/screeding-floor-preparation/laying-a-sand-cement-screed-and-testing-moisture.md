# Laying a sand-cement screed and testing moisture — flat, sound, dry enough

**Goal:** a sand-cement screed that is level to tolerance, well compacted, crack-free
and proven dry enough for the floor finish going on top. Key references: BS 8204-1
(screeds) and BS 8203 / BS 8201 (finishes) in the UK, ASTM F2170 (in-situ RH) and
ASTM F1869 (calcium chloride) in the US, AS 1884 for resilient floors in Australia.
Proprietary screed and flooring adhesive data sheets override these figures.

## 1. Choose the screed type and depth
| Type | Typical minimum thickness (sand-cement) |
|---|---|
| Bonded to a prepared slab | ~25 mm, up to about 40 mm |
| Unbonded (on a membrane) | ~50 mm |
| Floating (on insulation) | ~65 mm domestic, ~75 mm commercial |
| Over underfloor heating pipes | ~25 mm cover over pipes minimum (often more) |

(Approximate BS 8204 figures — unverified against the current edition.)
Thinner than this and it curls, cracks or hollows; for thinner fixes use a levelling
compound instead (see self-levelling products).

## 2. Prepare
- **Bonded:** mechanically remove laitance (shot blast or grind) to expose sound
  aggregate; vacuum; damp down the slab the day before (no standing water).
- **Unbonded/floating:** lay a polythene slip membrane or DPM with lapped, taped joints,
  turned up the walls; perimeter strip (5–10 mm foam) on floating screeds.
- Set **datum**: transfer a level line round the walls with a laser; set screed rails
  (or mortar "dots" and "screeds") to finished level at ~2–3 m centres.

## 3. Mix and lay
- Mix roughly **1:3 to 1:4.5 cement:sharp sand** by weight (1:4 common), semi-dry —
  a squeezed ball holds shape without free water (the "snowball" test). Too wet =
  shrinkage cracks and a weak, dusty top.
- Use a forced-action mixer; keep batches consistent.
- **Bonded only:** brush a cement or SBR/PVA slurry bond coat onto the slab just ahead
  of the screed and lay into it wet. A dried bond coat is a debonding layer.
- Spread proud of the rails, **compact hard** (tamp or heavy float, especially at edges and
  corners), rule off with the straightedge across the rails, fill rails' voids, then
  close with a steel or plastic float. Under tiles a wood-float finish is usually fine.
- Bay joints: form day joints square; on floating screeds add joints at doorways and
  roughly every 40 m² (unverified — follow the design).

## 4. Cure
- Cover with polythene for about **7 days** to cure (keep it damp, out of drafts and
  sun), then uncover to dry. Light foot traffic after ~24–48 h.
- Underfloor heating: do not use to "force dry" until ~21–28 days, then ramp gradually
  per the heating and screed supplier.

## 5. Drying and moisture testing
- Old rule of thumb: **~1 day per mm for the first 40 mm**, slower beyond that, in good
  conditions — a 65 mm screed can take months. Never trust the calendar; test.
- **Hygrometer hood (BS 8203/8201 method):** seal a hood on the surface, read after
  equilibrium (often 72 h). Typical limit **≤ 75% RH** for resilient/wood; tiles often
  tolerate more with suitable adhesive. Check the flooring maker's limit.
- **In-situ RH probe (ASTM F2170):** drill to ~40% of slab depth (one side drying),
  equilibrate 24 h, read; many flooring manufacturers cap at 75–85% RH.
- **Carbide (CM) test:** common in Europe, gives % moisture by weight; limits roughly
  2.0 CM% for cement screed under resilient floors (unverified).
- Test in several places, including the coldest, least-ventilated corner and thickest area.
- Surface moisture meters are for **comparison only**, not acceptance.

## 6. Surface regularity
- Check with a 2 m straightedge: e.g. SR1 ≈ 3 mm, SR2 ≈ 5 mm, SR3 ≈ 10 mm max gap
  under the edge (BS 8204 classes — confirm). Tile and resilient floors generally want
  SR1 or a levelling compound on top.

## Gotchas
- Hollow/drummy screed is almost always poor compaction or a dry bond coat.
- Curling at edges comes from overly wet mix and fast drying.
- Accelerated or proprietary screeds have very different drying and RH limits; read the sheet.
- Gypsum (anhydrite) screeds are different: sand off laitance and use compatible primers.

## Verify
- [ ] Thickness meets the minimum for bonded/unbonded/floating type throughout.
- [ ] Tap/drag-chain test finds no hollow areas; surface not dusty when scratched.
- [ ] Straightedge readings meet the specified SR class or finish tolerance.
- [ ] Moisture tested by an accepted method at several locations; results recorded
      and under the flooring manufacturer's limit.
