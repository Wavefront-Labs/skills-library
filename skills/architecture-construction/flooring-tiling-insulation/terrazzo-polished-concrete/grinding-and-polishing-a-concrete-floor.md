# Grinding and polishing a concrete floor — one grit at a time

**Goal:** a mechanically polished concrete (or ground terrazzo) floor with even aggregate
exposure, no scratch patterns from earlier grits, a densified hard surface and the agreed
gloss and slip resistance. Industry guidance: the Concrete Polishing Association of America
(CPAA) and ACI 310R (US), the Concrete Society (UK), and equipment/chemical makers' system
sheets — which take precedence over the numbers here.

## 1. Agree the finish before starting
- **Exposure class:** cream (surface paste only, almost no grinding), **salt-and-pepper**
  (fine aggregate, ~1–2 mm removed) or **full/large aggregate** (~3–6 mm removed). Each
  needs a different first grit and the slab must have been placed and finished to suit
  (flat, well-compacted, ideally no curing compound).
- **Gloss level:** matte, satin, semi-gloss or high gloss (CPAA classes 1–4 use rough
  final grits ~100 / 400 / 800 / 1500–3000 — unverified mapping).
- Sample the actual slab: grind a 1 m² test area and approve it with the client.

## 2. Prepare
- Slab cured (commonly ≥ 28 days) and dry enough for chemicals; remove sealers, glue,
  paint and curing compounds.
- Repair cracks and spalls with a polishable repair mortar or tinted epoxy — they will
  show; tell the client.
- **Dust:** run grinders with a HEPA dust extractor (or wet with slurry control).
  Respirable crystalline silica is a serious hazard; follow local silica rules (e.g.
  OSHA 1926.1153 Table 1 in the US) and wear the stated respirator.

## 3. Grit sequence (typical)
| Stage | Tooling | Typical grits |
|---|---|---|
| Grind / exposure | Metal-bond diamonds | 16/20 or 30/40 → 70/80 → 120/150 |
| Transition | Hybrid or copper-bond | 50–100 |
| Hone / polish | Resin-bond pads | 100 → 200 → 400 → 800 → (1500 → 3000) |

- Match metal bond hardness to the concrete: **soft bond for hard concrete**, hard bond
  for soft/abrasive concrete. Glazing (tools stop cutting) = bond too hard.
- **Never skip more than one grit step.** Each grit must fully remove the previous
  grit's scratches; check with a raking light or flashlight held low.
- Overlap passes by ~1/3; change direction between grits (e.g. north–south, then east–west).
- Edges: hand grinder or edger with the same grit sequence so borders don't look different.

## 4. Densify and grout
- After the first or second metal grit (or after the 100 resin), clean the floor and
  apply a **grout coat** (slurry or proprietary filler worked into the surface) to fill
  pinholes and air voids, then grind it off with the next grit.
- Apply **densifier** (lithium, sodium or potassium silicate) usually around the
  100–200 resin step: flood evenly, keep wet for the dwell time, then squeegee or
  scrub off excess so it doesn't leave a white residue (dwell and coverage per product;
  often ~10–20 m²/L — unverified).
- Allow cure (often a few hours to overnight) before the next resin grit.

## 5. Guard and burnish
- Apply a penetrating stain guard/impregnator in thin coats, then **burnish** with a
  high-speed burnisher and a suitable pad to raise gloss.
- Guards improve stain resistance against oil, wine and acids but are not a coating;
  acidic spills still etch.

## 6. Terrazzo note
- Cast-in-place terrazzo follows the same sequence after the matrix has cured: rough
  grind to expose chips and level divider strips, grout to fill voids, then progressive
  hone/polish and seal. Keep divider strips flush, not proud.

## Gotchas
- Swirl marks or "grinder halos" = skipped grits or too-fast passes.
- Curing compound left in the surface causes blotchy densifier uptake.
- Densifier applied too thick crystallises white; you have to grind back.
- High gloss on a wet or sloped area may fail slip requirements — test.

## Verify
- [ ] Test area approved for exposure and gloss before full production.
- [ ] Each grit's scratch pattern gone under a raking light before moving on.
- [ ] Pinholes filled; densifier evenly absorbed with no residue.
- [ ] Gloss/DOI readings at agreed points recorded (if specified).
- [ ] Slip resistance meets the specification (e.g. pendulum/DCOF test) for the use.
