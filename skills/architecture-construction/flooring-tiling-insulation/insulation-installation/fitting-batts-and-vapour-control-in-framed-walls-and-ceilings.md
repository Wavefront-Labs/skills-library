# Fitting batts and vapour control in framed walls and ceilings

**Goal:** insulation that actually delivers its labelled R-value: batts filling every cavity
edge to edge, uncompressed, with no gaps around services, a vapour control layer on the
correct side and an airtight lining. References: AS 3999 and NCC Part H6/Section J (AU),
IECC and the RESNET installation grading (US), BS 5250 and Approved Document L (UK).
Local code, the energy design and the insulation maker's instructions take precedence.

## 1. Before you open the packs
- Confirm the **specified R-value / thickness** for each element (walls, ceiling, floor)
  and that the frame depth fits it: a batt thicker than the cavity gets compressed and
  loses R-value.
- Check widths match stud/joist spacing (e.g. 430/580 mm batts for 450/600 mm centres;
  US 15"/23" for 16"/24" o.c.).
- Wear gloves, long sleeves, eye protection and a P2/N95 dust mask; mineral fibres itch
  and irritate. Ventilate the space.
- Services (wiring, plumbing, ducts) roughed in and inspected first.

## 2. Fit wall batts
1. Friction-fit each batt snugly between studs, **top to bottom**, with no gap at the top
   plate or bottom plate. Cut lengths about 10–20 mm over-long so they press in.
2. **Cut with a long knife** against a straightedge, compressing the batt with a board;
   cut on a scrap of timber, not the floor.
3. Around **cables**, split the batt in its thickness and wrap half behind, half in front
   — don't stuff it behind the cable.
4. Around **pipes and boxes**, cut the batt to fit tightly; fill small voids with offcuts
   rather than leaving air paths.
5. Odd-width bays: cut to width + ~10 mm.
6. Faced batts (US kraft-faced): stapling flanges to the stud face vs inset depends on
   the code — follow the product's instructions.

## 3. Fit ceiling / loft batts
- Lay batts tightly butted between joists, ends pushed together; where two layers are
  needed, lay the second **across the joists** to cover the timber thermal bridges.
- Don't block **eaves ventilation**: use rafter vents/baffles so air can flow over the
  insulation from the soffit.
- **Downlights and fans:** keep clearances unless the fitting is rated for insulation
  contact (e.g. IC/IC-4 rated in AU; check AS/NZS 60598.2.2 and the fitting label).
  Non-rated fittings near insulation are a fire risk.
- Keep clearances from flues and chimneys per code.
- Fit a dam around the loft hatch and insulate the hatch lid.

## 4. Vapour control layer (VCL) and airtightness
- In **heating-dominated (cold) climates**, the VCL usually goes on the **warm (room) side**
  of the insulation, behind the plasterboard/drywall. In hot-humid climates it may be
  omitted or go outside; a VCL in the wrong place traps condensation. Follow the
  design (and BS 5250 / local guidance) — when in doubt, ask.
- Lap polythene or membrane joints ~100–150 mm and tape with the system tape; tape to
  frames at perimeters.
- Seal every **penetration** (cables, pipes, boxes) with grommets, tape or airtight
  boxes. A single gap leaks more moisture by air movement than diffusion through the
  whole sheet.
- Often a service void (battens inside the VCL) keeps the membrane unpierced.

## 5. Other types — quick notes
- **Rigid board** (PIR, XPS, EPS): cut tight, foam-fill or tape joints, stagger in two
  layers.
- **Blown-in loose fill:** install to the depth markers for the required R-value and
  allow for settling per the coverage chart.
- Never compress insulation behind cupboards or under boards to make it fit.

## Gotchas
- Gaps matter disproportionately: a few percent uncovered area can cost a large share of
  the R-value (unverified figure — often quoted at ~5% gaps ≈ 30–50% loss in ceilings).
- Fluffing batts after unpacking: let them recover to full thickness before installing.
- Wet insulation loses most of its R-value; keep stock dry and the frame closed in.

## Verify
- [ ] Each cavity filled edge to edge, top to bottom, no compression or voids.
- [ ] Insulation split around cables; tight cuts at boxes and pipes.
- [ ] Ceiling: second layer crosses joists; eaves vents clear; downlight clearances met.
- [ ] VCL on the correct side, joints lapped and taped, every penetration sealed.
- [ ] Photos taken before lining for the inspector or energy assessor.
