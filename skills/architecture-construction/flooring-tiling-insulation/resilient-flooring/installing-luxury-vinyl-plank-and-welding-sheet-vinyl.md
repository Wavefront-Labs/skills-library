# Installing luxury vinyl plank and welding sheet vinyl

**Goal:** a resilient floor with no telegraphed bumps, no lifting edges, tight plank joints
and watertight welded seams. References: ASTM F710 (preparing concrete for resilient
flooring, US), BS 8203 (UK), AS 1884 (AU). The flooring and adhesive manufacturer's
instructions set the actual limits and govern the warranty.

## 1. Substrate (vinyl shows everything)
- **Flatness:** commonly ~3 mm in 3 m (3/16" in 10') for LVP/LVT, tighter for thin
  glue-down and sheet; no bump or hollow over ~1 mm in 150 mm (unverified — check the
  product). Any nail head, trowel ridge or grain of grit will show through thin vinyl.
- Skim-coat or self-level and sand smooth; vacuum thoroughly.
- **Moisture:** concrete RH limits are often 75–85% (in-situ, ASTM F2170) for glue-down;
  some rigid-core floating products allow more with a vapour barrier. Test, don't guess.
- Remove old cutback adhesive or encapsulate per the adhesive maker — solvent-based
  residues can bleed through and stain vinyl.

## 2. Acclimatise
- Store cartons/rolls flat in the room at **~18–27 °C (65–80 °F)** for about **24–48 h**
  before and keep it there during and 24–48 h after install (unverified — check sheet).
- Unroll sheet vinyl and let it relax; cold sheet cracks when coved and won't lie flat.

## 3. LVP — floating (click) method
1. Set out so the first and last rows are at least ~1/3 to 1/2 a plank wide; stagger
   end joints ≥ ~150–200 mm (6–8"), random, no H-joints.
2. **Perimeter gap ~6–8 mm (1/4"–5/16")** for rigid-core; larger rooms (often > ~15–25 m
   run) need transition breaks per the maker.
3. Angle the long edge in, then tap/press the end joint closed; use a tapping block, not
   a hammer on the edge. Check every joint is flush — a half-engaged lock peaks later.
4. Don't pin under kitchen islands or heavy fixtures; fit cabinets first or leave gaps.

## 4. LVT/LVP — glue-down (dryback)
- Use the specified trowel (often a fine **1/32"×1/16"×1/32" U-notch** or similar for
  pressure-sensitive adhesive — unverified, read the adhesive sheet).
- Respect **open (flash) time**: PSA must go tacky (often 15–45 min) before laying; laying
  too wet causes squeeze-up and plank drift.
- Lay within the working time, then **roll with a 45–50 kg (100 lb) three-section roller**
  both directions; roll again after ~1 h.

## 5. Sheet vinyl and welding
1. Pattern/scribe the sheet to walls (or flash-cove with a cove former and capping strip
   to ~100 mm up the wall in hygienic or wet rooms).
2. Overlap and **double-cut** seams in one pass so edges match exactly.
3. Adhere (wet-lay or per adhesive), roll seams with a hand seam roller, then the 45 kg
   roller. Wait for adhesive to set (often 24 h) before welding.
4. **Groove** the seam with a grooving tool to about 2/3 of the wear-layer depth,
   roughly 3 mm wide (typical for 2 mm sheet; check).
5. **Hot-air weld** with the speed nozzle: tune temperature on a scrap until the rod
   flows and a small bead forms both sides (typical settings are in the welder/rod data;
   unverified). Weld at a steady pace.
6. Trim in two stages: first pass with a **spatula/Mozart knife** and trimming plate while
   warm, final flush cut once the rod has fully cooled — trimming hot leaves a dip.
- Linoleum uses different rod and lower temperatures; rubber is often cold-welded or
  sealed instead of hot-welded — follow its system.

## Gotchas
- Telegraphing: one missed screw-head or trowel line becomes a visible bump.
- Temperature swings (sun through glass) make floating LVP gap or buckle; use blinds or a
  glue-down product rated for heat.
- Rolling too early with wet adhesive pushes adhesive up through joints.
- Welding before adhesive has set pulls seams open.

## Verify
- [ ] Substrate flatness and moisture readings recorded within the manufacturer's limits.
- [ ] Materials acclimatised; room in the temperature range.
- [ ] Floating floors: perimeter gap everywhere, all locks flush, no fixed loads.
- [ ] Glue-down: adhesive transfer checked on a lifted tile; floor rolled with a 45 kg roller.
- [ ] Welds continuous, flush after final trim, no voids or scorching.
