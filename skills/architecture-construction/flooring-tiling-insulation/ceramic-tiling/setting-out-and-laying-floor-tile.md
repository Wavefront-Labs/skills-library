# Setting out and laying floor tile — square, flat, fully bedded

**Goal:** a tiled floor with balanced cuts, straight joints, no hollow (drummy) tiles,
lippage within tolerance and movement joints where the substrate needs them.
Standards to check locally: ANSI A108/A118 and the TCNA Handbook (US), BS 5385-3 (UK),
AS 3958.1 (AU). The adhesive and tile manufacturer's data sheets take precedence over
any number below.

## 1. Check the substrate before buying adhesive
- **Flatness:** typical requirement is about 3 mm in 2 m (1/8" in 10 ft) for normal tiles;
  for tiles with any edge ≥ 15" / ~380 mm, TCNA asks for about 1/8" in 10 ft *and*
  1/16" in 24" (unverified — check the current TCNA/ANSI edition). Fix it with
  levelling compound, not with thick adhesive.
- **Sound and clean:** no laitance, curing compound, paint or dust. New concrete slabs
  normally need ~28 days before tiling unless the adhesive/membrane system says otherwise.
- **Deflection:** timber floors need adequate stiffness (L/360 for ceramic, L/720 for
  natural stone are the common US rules of thumb) plus a cement board or uncoupling layer.

## 2. Set out from the room, not the wall
1. Snap two perpendicular centre lines; check square with a 3-4-5 triangle (or 0.6-0.8-1.0 m).
2. **Dry-lay a row** each way with spacers. Shift the grid so cuts at the walls are at
   least half a tile, and so the doorway and the most visible wall get full or equal cuts.
3. Mark a working grid every 3–4 tiles so errors cannot accumulate.
4. Plan movement joints now (section 5) so they land on grout lines.

## 3. Trowel and bed
| Tile size (largest edge) | Starting notch (square/U) |
|---|---|
| up to ~200 mm (8") | 6 mm (1/4") square |
| 200–400 mm (8–16") | 10 mm (3/8") square |
| > 400 mm / large format | 12 mm (1/2") square or slant/U notch, plus back-buttering |

These are starting points (unverified — confirm with the adhesive data sheet).

- Key a skim coat into the substrate with the flat side, then comb with the notched side
  **in straight parallel lines**, not swirls, so air can escape.
- On large format, comb the floor and the tile back in the same direction, and press
  the tile across the ridges with a slight slide to collapse them.
- **Coverage targets:** ≥ 80% for dry interior floors, ≥ 95% for wet areas, exterior and
  natural stone, with all corners and edges supported. Lift a tile every few square
  metres early in the job to check; if coverage is low, change notch or technique.
- Respect **open time**: touch the combed adhesive — if it doesn't transfer to your
  finger, scrape it off and re-spread.
- Use levelling clips/wedges on tiles ≥ 600 mm or with any bow. Offset long rectangular
  tiles by no more than ~1/3 of their length to limit lippage from warpage.

## 4. Cut and finish
- Wet saw or rail cutter for straight cuts; angle grinder with diamond blade for notches.
- Leave a perimeter gap (commonly 6 mm / 1/4") at walls and upstands, hidden by skirting.
- Clean adhesive out of joints to at least 2/3 tile depth before it sets.

## 5. Movement joints
- At perimeters, changes of plane, over structural/control joints in the slab (never
  bridge them), and in field areas: TCNA EJ171 suggests about every 8–12 ft (2.4–3.6 m)
  interior and 8 ft exterior or sun-exposed; BS 5385-3 commonly uses bays of about
  40 m² or 8 m length (unverified — check current editions). Fill with flexible sealant,
  not grout.

## 6. Grout
- Wait for adhesive initial set (often 24 h; rapid-set 2–4 h).
- Sanded/cementitious for joints ≥ 3 mm, unsanded or epoxy for narrow joints and
  polished stone (sand can scratch it). Pack diagonally across joints with a rubber float.
- First wash with a damp sponge once the grout firms (typically 10–30 min); haze off later.
- Keep traffic off for 24 h and avoid wet cleaning for about 72 h (follow the grout sheet).

## Gotchas
- Thickening adhesive to fix a bad floor gives hollow tiles and cracking — level first.
- Swirled trowel marks trap air: a common cause of drummy large-format tiles.
- Dark grout on porous unglazed tile can stain; seal or test first.
- Underfloor heating: switch off before tiling and bring up slowly after full cure.

## Verify
- [ ] Substrate flatness measured and recorded against the tile size requirement.
- [ ] Wall cuts ≥ half tile; layout centred on the key sightline.
- [ ] Lifted test tiles show the target coverage with edges supported.
- [ ] Lippage checked with a straightedge (roughly ≤ 1 mm for narrow joints; per standard).
- [ ] Tap test finds no hollow-sounding tiles.
- [ ] Movement joints at perimeter, changes of plane and bay spacing, sealant-filled.
