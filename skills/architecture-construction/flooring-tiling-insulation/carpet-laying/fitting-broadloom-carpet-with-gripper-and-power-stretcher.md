# Fitting broadloom carpet with gripper and a power stretcher — tight, flat, invisible seams

**Goal:** wall-to-wall carpet that is stretched tight enough not to ripple for years,
with seams that don't show and neat edges at walls and doorways. References: CRI 105
(residential) and CRI 104 (commercial) in the US, BS 5325 (UK), AS/NZS 2455.1 (AU).
UK "gripper" = US "tackless strip"; UK "underlay" = US "pad/cushion". The carpet maker's
instructions (especially stretch direction and seam method) come first.

## 1. Measure and plan the cut
- Measure each room at its widest/longest plus **~75–100 mm (3–4")** excess each side
  for trimming.
- Broadloom is usually 3.66 m / 4 m / 5 m (12'/13'2"/15') wide; plan seams to be:
  out of main traffic paths and doorways, **parallel to the main light source** (window
  light across a seam shows it), and with all pieces' **pile running the same way**.
- Avoid seams that run through doorways (heavy pivoting traffic); where a room-to-room
  join is unavoidable, put it under the closed door, not on either side.

## 2. Gripper (tackless) strip
- Nail or glue strip round the perimeter, pins angled toward the wall.
- **Gully gap** between strip and wall/skirting: slightly less than the carpet thickness,
  typically **~6–10 mm (1/4–3/8")**, so the edge can be tucked.
- Concrete: use masonry-nailed strip or adhesive; around radiator pipes and in doorways
  use short pieces; in front of fireplaces or curved walls cut into short lengths.
- At doorways the strip stops under the door bar.

## 3. Underlay (pad)
- Lay underlay inside the strip, butted to it, not over it. Joints butted (not lapped)
  and taped; run joints at right angles to carpet seams where possible.
- Staple to timber floors; spray adhesive or tape on concrete.
- Use the density/thickness the carpet maker allows — too soft or thick a pad voids some
  warranties and makes seams fail (e.g. CRI suggests ≤ 7/16" for residential cut pile;
  unverified for your product).

## 4. Seams
1. Trim both edges straight with a row-runner/seam cutter along a row of backing, so
   the cut runs between tufts, not through them.
2. Centre hot-melt **seam tape** (adhesive up) under the join.
3. Run the **seaming iron** slowly (roughly 300 mm per ~30 s, per tape instructions),
   pressing the edges down into the melted glue behind it; keep edges tight with no
   pile trapped. Roll with a star/seam roller and place a weight while it cools.
4. **Seal the cut edges** (latex/seam sealer) on cut-pile and loop carpets, or loops can
   zipper out along the seam.

## 5. Stretch in sequence (power stretcher, not just the knee kicker)
1. Hook the carpet on the gripper along one corner's two walls with the **knee kicker**.
2. Use the **power stretcher** from that corner diagonally to the opposite corner, then
   along each wall in turn, bracing the stretcher's tail block against the opposite
   wall (protect the wall with a block). Typical target stretch is around 1–1.5% of
   length (unverified — follow the carpet maker's figure).
3. Hook each run onto the strip as you go; work toward the last corner.
4. Knee kickers are for positioning and short runs/closets only. Kicked-only carpet
   in a big room is the classic cause of ripples a year later.

## 6. Trim and finish
- **Wall trimmer** set to the carpet thickness, then tuck the edge into the gully with a
  stair tool/bolster.
- Cut round door frames with relief cuts, then trim.
- Fit a **door bar / transition strip** at every edge that isn't against a wall
  (gripper-type bars bite the carpet; Z-bars for carpet to hard floor).
- Check doors clear the finished carpet; plane or trim doors before the client moves in.

## Gotchas
- Pile direction mismatched across a seam reads as a different colour.
- Stretching wet or newly shampooed carpet: it shrinks back and can tear off the strip.
- Ripples after months = insufficient stretch or the wrong pad; re-stretch with the power stretcher.
- Watch for underfloor services before nailing strip (heating pipes, cables).

## Verify
- [ ] Seams out of traffic and parallel to the main light; pile direction consistent.
- [ ] Seam edges sealed and tape fully bonded; no peaking or visible gap.
- [ ] Power stretcher used across the room; carpet drum-tight with no ripples.
- [ ] Edges tucked neatly into the gully; no visible backing at walls.
- [ ] Door bars fitted and doors swing freely.
