# Acclimatising and laying timber floorboards — fit the wood to the building

**Goal:** a timber floor that stays flat and tight through the seasons: boards at the right
moisture content, fixed by a method that suits the subfloor, with room to move at the
edges. References: NWFA installation guidelines (US), BS 8201 (UK), AS 1684 / AS 4786
and the Australian Timber Flooring Association guides (AU). The board manufacturer's
instructions govern warranty and override the figures below.

## 1. Moisture first (most failures start here)
- Building must be **closed in**, wet trades finished and dried, heating/cooling running
  at normal living conditions for several days before boards arrive.
- Measure the **subfloor**: timber subfloor with a pin meter; concrete by RH test (see
  screed moisture testing). Typical limits: concrete ≤ 75% RH for glue-down without a
  moisture barrier (varies by adhesive).
- Measure **boards** (10+ boards from different packs). Rule of thumb for solid strip:
  board and timber subfloor within **~2% MC** of each other; wider boards (> 3"/75 mm)
  within ~1–2% (unverified — NWFA tables give regional targets).
- **Acclimatise** by moisture reading, not by days. Open or stack boards with spacers in
  the room until readings stabilise near the expected in-service MC (typically 6–12%
  depending on climate). Engineered boards often need little or none — follow the maker.

## 2. Pick the fixing method
| Method | Suits | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Secret nailing** (cleats/staples through the tongue at ~45°) | Solid and thick engineered over ply/OSB or joists | Nails every ~200–300 mm, 25–50 mm from ends; use the gun's correct adapter plate |
| **Full-spread glue-down** | Engineered (and some solid) on concrete or ply | Trowel notch per adhesive sheet; elastic MS/PU adhesives; weight boards while curing |
| **Floating** (click or glued joint) | Engineered on an underlay over almost any flat floor | Not fixed to the subfloor; needs flatness ~3 mm in 2 m and clear perimeter |

Direct-to-joist solid boards need a thickness suited to the joist spacing (e.g. ~19 mm
over 450 mm centres — check AS 1684 or local span tables).

## 3. Lay out
1. Run boards along the longest dimension or perpendicular to joists (nailed floors over
   joists must cross them).
2. Snap a straight **starter line** off the most visible long wall, allowing the
   expansion gap. Walls are rarely straight; work off the line, not the wall.
3. **Rack** several rows dry: mix packs for colour, keep end joints at least ~150 mm
   (solid strip) or 2–3× board width apart, avoid H-joints and stair-step patterns.
4. Face-nail or glue the first 1–2 rows until the nailer fits.

## 4. Expansion gaps
- Leave around **10–15 mm** at all walls, columns, thresholds and fixed objects; wider for
  large rooms or wide solid boards — some makers ask about 1.5 mm per metre of floor width
  (unverified — follow the manufacturer).
- Break long floating runs (often over ~8–12 m or through doorways) with expansion
  profiles as the maker specifies.
- Hide gaps under skirting/baseboard or scotia; **never** fix the skirting into the floor.

## 5. Finishing touches
- Undercut door jambs and architraves with a jamb saw so boards slide under.
- Wood-filler only the face; keep filler out of expansion gaps.
- Site-finished floors: sand in sequence (e.g. 36/40 → 60 → 80/100 grit), vacuum between,
  then seal/oil per the coating system.

## Gotchas
- **Cupping** (edges high) = moisture from below; **crowning** = sanded while cupped, then
  dried. Fix the moisture source before touching the floor.
- Gaps in winter are normal for solid wood in heated homes; large ones mean boards were
  installed too wet.
- Nailing too close to the board end splits the tongue; set the nailer pressure on scrap.
- Floating floors pinned by kitchen islands or heavy built-ins buckle — fit cabinets
  first or leave the floor free around them.
- Underfloor heating: use engineered boards rated for it and keep surface temperature
  within the maker's limit (often ≤ 27 °C).

## Verify
- [ ] Board and subfloor moisture readings recorded and within the required difference.
- [ ] Fixing method matches the board type, subfloor and manufacturer's instructions.
- [ ] Starter line straight; end joints staggered with no clusters or H-joints.
- [ ] Expansion gap present at every wall, pipe, threshold and fixed object.
- [ ] Floor flat underfoot with no bounce, squeaks, or lifted joints.
