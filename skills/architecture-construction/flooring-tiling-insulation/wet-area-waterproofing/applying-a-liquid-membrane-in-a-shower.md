# Applying a liquid membrane in a shower — continuous, thick enough, tested

**Goal:** a seamless waterproof layer under the tiles of a shower and bathroom floor that
drains to the waste, survives building movement at corners and joints, and is proven
by a flood test before any tile goes down. References: AS 3740 (AU), ANSI A118.10 for
membranes (US), BS 5385-1/-3 and "tanking" practice (UK). The membrane system's own
data sheet and local plumbing/building code override every number here.

## 1. Prepare the substrate
- **Falls first.** Screed or form the floor so water reaches the waste: commonly
  1:60–1:80 inside the shower and around 1:80–1:100 elsewhere when the floor drains
  (unverified — AS 3740 and local codes set the exact figures; US practice is often
  1/4" per foot ≈ 1:50). No ponding: pour water and watch.
- Substrate dry, clean and sound: cement sheet fixed and joints taped as specified;
  screeds cured (often 7+ days, check the membrane sheet for moisture limits).
- Fit the **puddle flange / waste outlet** first so the membrane can be dressed into it.
  Linear drains come with their own membrane flange — use it.
- Prime with the membrane maker's primer; let it go tack-free.

## 2. Bond breakers and reinforcement (where leaks start)
1. At every internal wall/floor junction and wall/wall corner, run a **bond breaker**:
   a flexible sealant fillet (roughly 6–10 mm leg) or a bond-breaker tape. Its job is
   to let the membrane stretch over a gap instead of tearing where it is glued on both sides.
2. Embed **reinforcing tape or fabric** into a wet first coat over the bond breaker,
   sheet joints, the waste flange and around pipe penetrations; use preformed corners
   and pipe collars where the system has them.
3. Board joints and screws/nails: tape or spot-coat per system.

## 3. Coat to the specified thickness
- Apply **at least two coats**, the second at right angles to the first, each after the
  previous is touch-dry (often 1–4 h; cold, damp rooms take far longer).
- Measure with a **wet-film gauge** as you go. Typical systems call for a dry film of
  roughly 0.5–1.0 mm (unverified — read the sheet; it gives litres per m² per coat and
  wet thickness). Thin spots at edges and over tape are the usual weak point.
- Colour change between coats (or contrasting coats) makes missed areas visible.

## 4. Extent: how far it must go
Common minimums (verify against AS 3740 / local code — these are approximate):
- **Shower floor:** whole floor, turned up the wall **≥ 150 mm**, and the walls to at
  least 1800 mm high inside the shower area.
- **Rest of a bathroom floor** where water can escape: membrane or water-resistant
  substrate with a perimeter upturn (often 25–100 mm) and a **waterstop angle** at the
  doorway, the membrane lapped onto it.
- Hobless/level-entry showers need the membrane carried across the threshold and the
  floor falling back to the waste.
- Around taps, mixers and shower roses: collar or seal every penetration.

## 5. Cure and flood test
- Cure per sheet before loading — often 24–48 h, longer in cold weather.
- **Flood test:** plug the waste, dam the doorway, fill to about 25–50 mm at the high
  point (below the top of upturns), mark the level, leave ~24 h. Check the level and
  inspect ceilings/rooms below. Any drop → find, repair, retest.
- Photograph membrane, upturns and the test level for the record; some jurisdictions
  need a certificate from a licensed waterproofer.

## Gotchas
- Membrane applied straight over a sharp corner without a bond breaker cracks with the
  first seasonal movement.
- Falls created in the tile bed over a flat membrane trap water under the tiles; build
  falls *under* the membrane (or use a system designed for it).
- Some liquid membranes need a primer or slurry before tile adhesive; check adhesive
  compatibility (polyurethane vs cementitious).
- Never pierce the cured membrane with fixings; plan grab rails and niches before coating.

## Verify
- [ ] Falls checked with water: no ponding, all water reaches the waste.
- [ ] Bond breakers and reinforcement at every junction, corner, flange and penetration.
- [ ] Wet-film gauge readings meet the system's thickness, two coats minimum.
- [ ] Upturns and wall heights meet the code minimums; doorway waterstop lapped.
- [ ] 24 h flood test passed with no level drop, recorded with photos.
