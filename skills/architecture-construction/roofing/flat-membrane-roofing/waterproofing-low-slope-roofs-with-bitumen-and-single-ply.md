# Waterproofing low-slope roofs with bitumen and single-ply membranes

**Goal:** water leaves the roof quickly, the membrane is continuous at every lap,
upstand and outlet, and condensation can't build up inside the build-up.

## 1. Falls first
- "Flat" roofs need a designed fall — commonly a **minimum finished fall of 1:80**
  (achieved with a design fall of ~1:40 to allow for deflection) in UK practice; ~2% (¼ in
  per ft) in US codes. Ponding water accelerates membrane ageing and voids many warranties.
- Achieve falls in the structure, with firrings, or with **tapered insulation**. Check with
  a level and string before membrane goes down.

## 2. The build-up (warm roof, most common)
Deck → vapour control layer (VCL) → insulation → (cover board) → membrane.
- The VCL goes on the **warm side** of the insulation; laps sealed and turned up at
  perimeters. Missing VCL in a heated building → interstitial condensation that looks like a
  leak.
- Insulation fixed or bonded per wind uplift design.
- Cold roofs (insulation below a ventilated deck) are more condensation-prone; avoid in new
  work unless designed.

## 3. Bitumen membranes
- **Torch-on (SBS/APP):** base layer and cap sheet, laps typically ~100 mm side and
  ~150 mm end (check product), staggered between layers. Heat the roll's underside until
  bitumen flows, and roll forward so a small bead of molten bitumen shows at the lap
  edge — that bead is your proof of fusion.
  - **Hot works**: permit, fire extinguisher, no torching near combustible upstands,
    timber or insulation edges (use self-adhered or cold-applied at those details), and a
    fire watch for an hour after (many insurers require it). Many roof fires start at
    upstands and eaves timber.
- **Self-adhered/cold-applied:** primer on the substrate, warm conditions, pressure rolled;
  less fire risk, more sensitive to cold and dust.

## 4. Single-ply (EPDM, TPO, PVC)
- EPDM: often large sheets bonded with adhesive; seams with tape and primer.
- TPO/PVC: seams **hot-air welded**; test with a probe along every seam and do test welds
  each morning (peel test) to set temperature/speed.
- Mechanically fixed, adhered or ballasted per design; fasteners in laps per pattern.

## 5. Details that leak
- **Upstands:** at least ~150 mm above finished roof surface (UK common rule; check code),
  with termination bar/counter-flashing; fillet or angle at the base.
- **Outlets/drains:** at the low points (not just where convenient); membrane clamped or
  bonded to the outlet flange; leaf guards; **overflow** outlet or scupper for blocked
  drains.
- **Penetrations:** proprietary pipe boots or sleeves; avoid clusters of penetrations
  too close to weld round.
- **Thresholds:** door thresholds need the upstand height too, or a drainage channel with
  a designed detail.

## 6. Testing
- **Flood test** (block outlets, fill to a modest depth for 24–48 h) — only where the
  structure is designed to take the load.
- **Electronic leak detection** (low/high voltage) on membranes, increasingly specified
  instead of flooding.

## Gotchas
- Insufficient fall → ponding and early failure.
- Missing or broken VCL → condensation mistaken for leaks.
- Torching near timber → roof fires.
- Outlets at high points due to deflection — check falls after loading.

## Verify
- [ ] Falls checked; no ponding deeper than a few mm 48 h after rain.
- [ ] VCL continuous and sealed at edges.
- [ ] Every torch-on lap shows a bitumen bead; every single-ply weld probed.
- [ ] Upstands to height with terminations; outlets at low points with overflows.
- [ ] Leak test passed; hot-works records completed.
