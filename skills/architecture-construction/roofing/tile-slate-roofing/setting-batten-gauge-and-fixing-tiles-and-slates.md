# Batten gauge, setting out and fixing tiles and slates

**Goal:** even courses from eaves to ridge with the minimum headlap the product needs at
that pitch, and every tile/slate fixed to resist wind uplift.

## 1. Check pitch vs. product
- Each tile/slate has a **minimum pitch** and a **minimum headlap** for it (and both
  tighten in exposed locations). E.g., some interlocking concrete tiles go down to
  ~15–22.5°; plain tiles ~35°; natural slates vary with size (larger slates for
  lower pitches). If the roof is near the limit, use a lower-pitch product, don't guess.
- Underlay (breathable membrane or felt) laid with correct laps and support; counter
  battens where specified.

## 2. Batten gauge
For **double-lap** slates/plain tiles:
`gauge = (slate length − headlap) ÷ 2`
e.g., 500 mm slate, 90 mm headlap → (500 − 90) / 2 = 205 mm.

For **single-lap** interlocking tiles: gauge = tile length − headlap, within the product's
min–max gauge range (e.g., 310–345 mm).

## 3. Setting out
1. Fix the **eaves batten** so the first course overhangs into the gutter correctly
   (often ~50 mm, or a third into the gutter).
2. Fix the **top batten** below the ridge (allowing the ridge tile overlap).
3. Measure the distance between them, divide by the max gauge, round up to the next
   whole number of courses, then divide the distance by that number for the actual gauge.
   That gauge must fall within the product's allowed range.
4. Mark the gauge on a rod/storey rod or both rafters at each end and snap chalk lines.
5. **Horizontal (perpend) setout:** for interlocking tiles, lay a dry row along the eaves
   and the ridge to find the "shunt" (tolerance in the interlocks) so you finish with
   full or half tiles at verges, not slivers.

## 4. Battens
- Grade and size per standard (e.g., graded battens to BS 5534 in UK — check marking),
  fixed into every rafter, joints over rafters, staggered.

## 5. Fixing
- Wind-uplift calculation (BS 5534, AS 2050/AS 4055, manufacturer software) decides how
  many tiles are nailed/clipped. Modern codes commonly require **every tile mechanically
  fixed** in many zones, plus all perimeter tiles (eaves, verges, ridge, hips, around
  openings). Mortar alone is not a fixing.
- Slates: two nails each (copper or stainless), at the right distance from the tail so
  the next course covers them; or hooks (slate hooks) where permitted.

## 6. Verges, hips, ridges, valleys
- Dry-fix systems (mechanical verges, ridges and hips) are now required for ridges/hips
  in UK BS 5534 practice; mortar-bedded ridges also need mechanical fixing.
- Hips and valleys: cut tiles to a straight line, clip and fix cut pieces (small cuts tied
  to the tile beside or using hip irons); no tiny unfixed slivers.
- Valleys: GRP/metal valley trough or laced/swept valleys in traditional work.

## Heritage slating
Reuse sound slates where possible, match size and thickness, sort slates by thickness
(thicker ones lower on the roof), and follow the original course diminishing pattern.

## Gotchas
- Gauge exceeding the product maximum → inadequate headlap → leaks in driven rain.
- Mortar-only verges and ridges → they come off in storms.
- Slivers at verges from not dry-laying the shunt.
- Walking on tiles in the middle rather than the headlap → breakages.

## Verify
- [ ] Pitch and headlap within the product's limits for the exposure.
- [ ] Measured gauge constant and within range; courses parallel to eaves.
- [ ] Fixing schedule matches the wind-load calculation.
- [ ] Verges finish in full/half tiles; cut pieces fixed.
- [ ] No broken tiles after the job (walk the roof in safe access).
