# Laying asphalt shingles: underlayment, valleys and ridges

**Goal:** a roof that meets the shingle maker's warranty conditions: correct deck,
underlayment, exposure, nailing and ventilation. Those instructions on the bundle wrapper
*are* the spec — read them for the product you're laying.

## 1. Deck and minimum pitch
- Solid deck (plywood/OSB) with H-clips or blocking as required; replace delaminated
  boards; fasteners flush.
- Asphalt shingles generally need ≥ 2:12 pitch; 2:12–4:12 is "low slope" and needs
  double underlayment or a full ice barrier (check manufacturer).

## 2. Edges and underlayment
1. **Drip edge at the eaves** goes on *under* the underlayment; **at rakes** it goes *over*
   it (water on the underlayment then drains over the eave drip).
2. **Ice barrier** (self-adhered membrane) in cold climates from the eave up to at least
   ~600 mm (24 in) inside the interior wall line (code language), and in valleys and
   around penetrations everywhere.
3. Underlayment (felt or synthetic) the rest of the roof, laps per manufacturer
   (often ~100 mm / 4 in horizontal, ~150 mm / 6 in end laps), shingle-fashion so upper
   courses lap lower ones.

## 3. Starter and coursing
- **Starter strip** along eaves (and often rakes) with sealant strip at the edge, so the
  first course's tabs/gaps are covered and sealed down.
- First course flush with the starter, small overhang (~6–19 mm / ¼–¾ in) past the drip
  edge.
- **Exposure:** set by the product (commonly ~143 mm / 5⅝ in for metric-size laminated).
- **Offset:** stagger joints between courses by the product's minimum (often ≥ 100–150 mm)
  so no joint lines up with the one below. Use chalk lines every few courses to keep
  courses straight and parallel.

## 4. Nailing
- Nails in the **manufacturer's nailing zone** (usually a marked strip), not above it —
  high nails miss the shingle below and void wind warranties.
- Typically 4 nails per shingle, 6 in high-wind zones; nails driven flush, not cut into
  the mat, not crooked. Calibrate the nail gun pressure.
- Correct nail length to penetrate the deck (or ~19 mm into planks).

## 5. Valleys
- **Closed-cut:** shingle one side across the valley, then lay the other side over and cut
  it back ~50 mm from the centreline, clipping the top corners and bedding in cement.
  Run the lower-slope/lower-volume side first.
- **Woven:** interlace courses across the valley (for 3-tab; laminates generally not).
- **Open metal:** a metal valley liner exposed between cut shingles.
- No nails within ~150 mm of the valley centreline.

## 6. Penetrations and walls
- **Step flashing** at roof-to-wall junctions: one piece per course, lapped, nailed to
  the deck only (not the wall), with counter-flashing or housewrap/siding over.
- Pipe boots and vent flashings shingled in: upper course over, lower course under.

## 7. Ridge and ventilation
- Ridge vent if designed for balanced intake/exhaust ventilation (soffit intake needed).
- Ridge cap shingles at the specified exposure, longer nails, starting at the end facing
  away from prevailing wind.

## Gotchas
- High nailing → shingles blow off in the first storm.
- Drip edge over underlayment at the eaves → water behind the fascia.
- Aligned joints between courses → leaks.
- Laying in cold weather without hand-sealing → tabs lift until warm weather seals them.

## Verify
- [ ] Bundle wrapper instructions followed for exposure, offset, nailing.
- [ ] Lift a few shingles: nails in the zone, flush.
- [ ] Ice barrier where climate/code requires; drip edge layering correct.
- [ ] Valleys clear of nails near the centre; step flashing one-per-course.
- [ ] Balanced ventilation (intake ≈ exhaust).
