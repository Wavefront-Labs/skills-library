# Flashing wall junctions, chimneys and parapets

**Goal:** at every junction, water meets a lap pointing downhill and running *over*
the next material, never into it. Sealant is a backup, never the primary seal.

## 1. The one rule: shingle-lap everything
Trace a raindrop from the top of the wall to the gutter. Every piece it crosses must lap
over the piece below. If a drop can run behind a layer, the detail is wrong, however much
sealant is on it.

## 2. Materials
- **Lead** (traditional, malleable, long-lived): thickness by "code" (UK Code 4 for
  most step/apron work, Code 5 for some); limit piece lengths (often ~1.5 m) for
  thermal movement; patinate to avoid staining.
- **Zinc, copper, aluminium, pre-painted steel**: folded on a brake; match to the roof
  metal to avoid galvanic corrosion (no copper upstream of zinc-coated steel).
- Keep lead and copper off aluminium/zinc-coated roofs or isolate them.

## 3. Roof-to-wall: sloping abutment
- **Step flashings** (soakers) under each tile/slate/shingle course, turned up the wall.
- **Counter (cover) flashing** over them, let into the masonry joint (raggle/reglet)
  ~25 mm deep, wedged and pointed, stepped with the courses. On clad walls, the wall
  membrane/cladding laps over the upstand instead.
- **Kickout flashing** at the bottom of a sloping wall junction where it meets an eave —
  diverts water into the gutter instead of down the wall (classic rot spot in the
  US/clad houses).

## 4. Horizontal abutment (top of a lean-to)
- **Apron flashing**: upstand ~75–150 mm, cover onto the roofing ~150 mm (more on
  low pitch), dressed to the profile; let into the wall joint or behind cladding;
  on brick cavity walls a **cavity tray** above stops water running down inside the cavity
  onto the roof.

## 5. Chimneys
- **Front apron** at the downslope face.
- **Step + counter flashings** on the sides.
- **Back gutter** on the upslope face; for wide chimneys (often >~750 mm) build a
  **cricket/saddle** to split the water around it.
- Counter-flashing laps over the step flashings and the back gutter upstands.
- Check the chimney itself — porous brick and a missing cap or cavity tray leak water that
  gets blamed on the flashing.

## 6. Parapets and box gutters
- **Parapet capping:** falls to the roof side, drip edges both sides held off the wall
  ~25–40 mm, laps sealed and riveted or proper joints, with expansion joints on long runs;
  membrane under the capping on top of the wall.
- **Box gutters:** sized for the higher design rainfall, with a sump/rainwater head and
  overflow (see roof-plumbing skill). Sole fall and expansion joints per manufacturer;
  the roofing turned down into the gutter with a drip, not short of it.

## 7. Fabrication tips
- Fold drips/hems on every exposed edge (stiffens and stops water tracking back).
- Measure in place; fold returns rather than cut-and-seal corners where possible
  ("dog-ear" folded corners).
- Fasten so the metal can move; clips rather than face-nailing where possible.

## Gotchas
- Counter-flashing face-fixed and sealed only → fails as soon as sealant cracks.
- No cavity tray above an abutment → "roof leak" that's really cavity water.
- Missing kickout → rotten wall framing below the junction.
- Overlong lead pieces → fatigue cracking.

## Verify
- [ ] Raindrop trace: every lap downhill, no reverse laps.
- [ ] Counter-flashings let into joints or lapped under cladding, not just sealed.
- [ ] Chimney has back gutter/cricket as needed; cavity trays where required.
- [ ] Drips on all exposed edges; capping falls to the roof.
- [ ] Hose test from the top of the junction downwards, checking inside.
