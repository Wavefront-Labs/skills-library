# Slope, hydrology, microclimate and context mapping

**Goal:** understand what the land is already doing, so the design works with it —
captured as a set of analysis maps and a single opportunities-and-constraints (O&C)
plan that the whole team designs from.

## 1. Gather before you visit
- Topographic survey (or LiDAR DTM from public sources), aerial imagery, geology and
  soil maps, flood maps, utility records, planning/zoning designations, tree
  preservation orders, heritage and ecological designations, historic maps.
- A GIS (e.g. QGIS) is ideal for overlays; CAD works for small sites.

## 2. Site visit (more than once if you can)
Walk the boundaries and the middle. Note and photograph: where water sits or flows,
soil colour and wetness, vegetation (indicator species tell you about wetness/acidity),
tree condition, desire lines worn in grass, noise, smells, views in and out, overlooking,
wind exposure, how neighbours use their edges. Visit in rain if you can — drainage
reveals itself.

## 3. Analysis maps
- **Slope:** classify into bands relevant to use — e.g. < 1:20 (≈ 5%) easy for
  paths and play; 1:20–1:12 (5–8%) needs ramp design for accessible routes; 1:12–1:3
  needs terracing or steps; > 1:3 (~33%) generally beyond mowing and stable soil without
  engineering. Derive from contours or the DTM.
- **Aspect:** which way slopes face — governs sun, frost and planting.
- **Hydrology:** high points, ridges, valleys, flow paths, ponding areas, outfalls,
  flood extents. Note where the site receives water from upslope neighbours.
- **Soils:** texture, depth, compaction, contamination risk; dig trial pits and run
  **infiltration/percolation tests** where SuDS/green infrastructure is likely (to
  BRE 365 or local equivalent).
- **Microclimate:** sun path and shading by buildings/trees (seasonal), prevailing
  and funnelling winds, frost pockets in low ground, heat from paved areas.
- **Context:** access points, pedestrian/vehicle desire lines, adjacent uses, views
  to keep and block, noise sources, landmarks.
- **Ecology and trees:** habitats, trees with category/condition and root protection
  areas, protected species signs (escalate to an ecologist).
- **Legal/planning:** easements, rights of way, utilities and their offsets, setbacks.

## 4. Synthesis
- Overlay the maps (the McHarg "sieve" approach) into an **O&C plan**: areas good
  for buildings/active use, areas to protect, water routes to keep, views to frame,
  barriers to fix. Keep it to one sheet with a clear legend.
- Write 5–10 key findings in plain sentences — they become design principles.

## Gotchas
- Analysing only the red-line site, not the catchment and context around it.
- Summer-only visit missing winter wetness and low sun angles.
- Slope bands that don't relate to any use.
- Pretty maps with no synthesis — the design team ignores them.

## Verify
- [ ] Desk data gathered, sources listed with dates.
- [ ] At least one visit in wet conditions, photographs geolocated.
- [ ] Slope, aspect, hydrology, soils, microclimate, context, ecology and legal maps done.
- [ ] Infiltration tested where needed.
- [ ] Single O&C plan plus written key findings.
