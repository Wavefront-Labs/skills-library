# Sizing rain gardens and bioswales

**Goal:** green infrastructure that captures and treats a defined volume of runoff,
drains down in time, overflows safely in big storms and stays planted. Local
stormwater manuals (e.g. the UK CIRIA SuDS Manual, state/city LID manuals in the
US) set the design storms and rules — use them; the numbers below are typical.

## 1. Catchment and target volume
1. Measure the **catchment area** draining to the feature (roofs, paving, lawns).
2. Choose the design event the manual requires — often a **water-quality volume**
   (e.g. first ~10–25 mm of rainfall, or a percentile storm) rather than the flood event.
3. Volume ≈ catchment area × rainfall depth × runoff coefficient (roofs/paving ~0.9,
   lawn much lower). Example: 200 m² roof × 0.02 m × 0.9 ≈ 3.6 m³.

## 2. Test infiltration
- Dig a test pit at the proposed location and depth; run an infiltration test (e.g.
  BRE 365 in the UK, double-ring or borehole methods elsewhere). Clay soils may
  infiltrate at < 1 mm/hr — then you need an underdrain.
- Check groundwater (seasonal high) — keep the base well above it (often ≥ 1 m) — and
  distance from foundations (commonly ≥ 3–5 m unless lined; follow the manual).
- No infiltration on contaminated land without a risk assessment.

## 3. Size the rain garden
- **Ponding depth** typically ~150–300 mm (shallower near paths and play).
- **Area** ≈ volume ÷ (ponding depth + storage in media voids). With 3.6 m³ and 200
  mm ponding, ~18 m² before media storage; a common rule of thumb is a rain garden
  of ~10–20% of its impervious catchment on moderately draining soils.
- **Drawdown:** surface water should drain within ~24–48 hours (mosquitoes, plant
  health, readiness for the next storm). Check: ponding depth ÷ infiltration rate.

## 4. Build-up
- Surface mulch (if any) — shredded hardwood or gravel; avoid floating bark.
- **Filter media / engineered soil** ~450–900 mm: sandy loam with limited fines and
  organics, specified by permeability (the manual gives a mix).
- Optional transition layer, then **gravel drainage layer** with a perforated
  **underdrain** to the network where soils are slow.
- **Inlet** with energy dissipation (stone apron) and a sediment forebay for easy
  cleaning; **overflow** (raised grate or spillway) set at max ponding depth, routed
  safely to the drainage system.

## 5. Bioswales
- Linear, shallow, vegetated channels: longitudinal slope ~1–4%; side slopes ~1:3–1:4;
  base width ~0.6–2.4 m. Steeper gradients need **check dams** to slow flow and pond
  water. Flow velocity kept low to avoid erosion (manual gives limits).
- Plant dense grasses or sedges that tolerate inundation and drought; mowable if turf.

## 6. Planting
Zones: the **base** (wet periods and drought — tough sedges, rushes, grasses), the
**side slopes** (moisture-tolerant perennials/shrubs) and the **rim** (drier).
Avoid trees with invasive roots near underdrains. Plant densely (≥ ~6–9/m² herbaceous)
for fast cover and root channels.

## Gotchas
- Sited where infiltration was never tested.
- No overflow — the garden floods the building in a big storm.
- Construction traffic compacting the base before it's built; protect the footprint.
- Sediment-laden runoff during construction clogging new media — bring online last.

## Verify
- [ ] Catchment, design depth and target volume calculated and recorded.
- [ ] Infiltration test done at depth; groundwater and setbacks checked.
- [ ] Ponding depth and drawdown time within limits.
- [ ] Inlet, forebay, overflow and (if needed) underdrain detailed.
- [ ] Planting zoned for wet base, slopes and rim.
