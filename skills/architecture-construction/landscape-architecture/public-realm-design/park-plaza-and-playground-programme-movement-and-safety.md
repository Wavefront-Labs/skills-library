# Park, plaza and playground: programme, movement and safety

**Goal:** a public space people actually use at many times of day, that feels safe,
works for all ages and abilities, and the council or owner can maintain.

## 1. Programme from people, not precedent photos
- Observe the site and its context: who passes, when, where they're going, what's
  missing nearby (shade? toilets? play for teens?). Count people at different times
  and days.
- Engage: workshops, on-site pop-ups, schools, and especially groups usually unheard
  (older people, teenagers, disabled people). Record what you heard and how the design
  responds.
- Write a **programme**: activities, capacity, times of use, events, and the
  management/maintenance regime it implies.

## 2. Movement
- Plot **desire lines** (worn grass, observed routes) and design main paths on them —
  people won't detour; they'll cut corners and wear new paths.
- Hierarchy: primary routes wide enough for two-way flow and maintenance vehicles
  (often 3 m+ in parks), secondary paths narrower.
- Step-free access to every main space; level resting points on long ramps/slopes.
- Wayfinding: clear sightlines to destinations, landmarks, consistent signage.

## 3. Places to stay
- **Edges** get used first (William Whyte's observations): seating along edges with
  views of activity and a back to something. Provide moveable chairs where management allows.
- Mix of seating heights, backs and armrests (older people need them to rise), in sun
  and shade; shade from trees for summer heat.
- Active frontages, cafés, kiosks and events bring people at more hours.

## 4. Safety: CPTED basics
- **Natural surveillance:** sightlines from windows, paths and roads; low planting
  (under ~0.6–1 m) and high-canopy trees (clear stem ~2 m+) near paths.
- Lighting on main routes after dark at uniform levels (avoid glare and dark pools);
  dark-sky-friendly fittings where ecology matters.
- Clear ownership and maintenance — broken things signal neglect.
- Avoid entrapment spots (dead-end recesses beside main routes).

## 5. Playgrounds
- Equipment to recognised standards (e.g. **EN 1176** in Europe, **ASTM F1487** /
  CPSC guidance in the US), installed and inspected by competent persons
  (post-installation inspection before opening).
- **Impact-attenuating surfacing** under equipment sized to the **critical fall
  height** (EN 1177 / ASTM F1292 tested), with use zones kept clear and the required
  extent around each piece.
- Zones by age; inclusive play pieces and routes for wheelchair users; seating for
  carers with sightlines over the whole area; fencing/gates where dogs or roads demand.
- Risk-benefit thinking: some risk is valuable in play; document the assessment.

## 6. Maintenance
Choose robust furniture and surfaces with replaceable parts, standard sizes, and the
owner's approved palette. Cost the maintenance per year and get it agreed.

## Gotchas
- Paths that ignore desire lines.
- Benches in the middle of open space with nothing to look at.
- Dense shrubs beside paths creating hiding places.
- Play surfacing not tested for the equipment's fall height.

## Verify
- [ ] Programme traceable to observation and engagement.
- [ ] Main paths follow desire lines; step-free routes to all key spaces.
- [ ] Seating at edges, in sun and shade, with backs and arms provided.
- [ ] CPTED review done (sightlines, lighting, no entrapment).
- [ ] Play equipment and surfacing to standard; inspection planned.
