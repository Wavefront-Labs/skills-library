# Paving build-ups, edges, steps and retaining walls

**Goal:** hardscape details that a contractor can build, that drain, don't move, and
last. Structural walls above low heights and heavily trafficked pavements need an
engineer — this is the designer's toolkit for the rest.

## 1. Paving build-ups
Design from the bottom up: **subgrade** (tested or assumed strength), **sub-base**,
**bedding**, **surface**, **jointing**.
- **Flexible** (unit paving on sand or grit bedding): pedestrian — e.g. ~100–150 mm
  compacted crushed-stone sub-base (Type 1/MOT in the UK), ~30–50 mm bedding sand,
  pavers; light vehicles — sub-base ~150–250 mm+. Needs **edge restraint**.
- **Rigid** (bound): mortar or concrete bed on a concrete base — required for many
  natural stone flags in vehicle areas and for thin setts; movement joints needed in
  the base and surface (spacing per standards and material).
- **Permeable**: open-graded aggregate sub-base acting as storage (depth from
  hydraulic and structural design), no fines, geotextile where needed; paving with
  wide joints or porous units. Check infiltration test results.
- Weak/clay subgrade: deeper sub-base, possible geogrid, or capping layer — get the
  subgrade CBR from site investigation for vehicle areas.

## 2. Edges
- Every flexible pavement needs a restraint: concrete-haunched kerb or edging,
  steel/aluminium edging for gravel and lawns, or a mortared soldier course.
- Haunch to roughly half the edging height on the outer side; the haunch shouldn't
  show at the surface.
- Edges are where water, roots and wheels attack — specify them as carefully as the paving.

## 3. External steps
- Use the comfort rule **2R + G ≈ 600–700 mm** (Blondel's formula), with external
  steps gentler than internal: e.g. riser ~120–150 mm, going ~350–450 mm.
- Keep risers equal in a flight (tolerance a few mm); single steps are a trip hazard —
  use at least two, or a ramp.
- Fall each tread slightly (~1:60) to the front to shed water; contrasting nosings for
  visibility; handrails per code (often required at 2+ risers in public spaces — check).
- Long flights: landings at intervals per code (e.g. after a set number of risers).

## 4. Ramps
Gradient, length between landings and landing size come from accessibility codes
(e.g. ADA, UK Approved Document M/BS 8300): typically ≤ 1:12, with steeper gradients
allowed only for short going; handrails both sides; level landings at top, bottom and
turns.

## 5. Low retaining walls
- **Gravity walls** (masonry, gabion, segmental blocks) for low retained heights; beyond
  roughly 0.6–1.0 m retained (or any surcharge — vehicles, buildings, slopes above) get
  an engineer and check local permit thresholds.
- **Drainage is the design:** free-draining aggregate behind the wall, perforated
  drain at the base to an outlet, filter fabric to keep soil out, and weep holes. Most
  wall failures are water pressure.
- Batter (lean back) segmental walls per the maker; footing below frost depth; movement
  joints in long rigid walls.

## Gotchas
- Same build-up used for footpath and driveway.
- Flexible paving with no edge restraint — it creeps outward.
- Steps with uneven risers or a single step.
- Retaining wall with no drainage, built "temporarily".

## Verify
- [ ] Build-up specified per use, with subgrade assumption stated.
- [ ] Edge restraint on every flexible surface.
- [ ] Step geometry satisfies 2R+G and code limits; risers equal.
- [ ] Ramps and landings meet the named code.
- [ ] Retaining walls have drainage; engineer engaged above threshold.
