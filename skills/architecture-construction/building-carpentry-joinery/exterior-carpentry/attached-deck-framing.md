# Framing an attached deck

**Goal:** a deck that stays attached to the house, drains, doesn't rot the house's rim
joist, and doesn't rust through its connectors. Ledger failure is the leading cause of
deck collapses — most of this skill is about not being that statistic.

> Member sizes, spans, footing sizes and fastener schedules come from your code's
> prescriptive tables (e.g. IRC R507 / AWC DCA 6 in the US) or an engineer. Numbers
> below are typical illustrations only.

## 1. Plan
- Height above ground decides guardrails (commonly required above ~30" / 600 mm — check),
  bracing and permits; most jurisdictions require a permit for attached decks.
- Locate buried services before digging footings.
- Decide: **attached** (ledger) or **free-standing** (extra beam near the house). Free-
  standing is often better where the house rim/structure is unknown, it's brick veneer,
  or there's cantilevered floor framing — **never** fix a ledger to brick veneer or a
  cantilevered floor.

## 2. Ledger — the critical connection
1. Remove cladding at the ledger zone; expose the house **rim joist/band** (must be solid,
   sound timber of adequate size).
2. **Flash**: self-adhered membrane on the sheathing, then a metal or plastic Z-flashing
   over the top of the ledger, lapped under the house's weather barrier so water runs
   outside. Rot in the house rim behind an unflashed ledger is hidden and progressive.
3. Optional spacers/washers to create a drainage gap behind the ledger (common practice).
4. Fix with **through-bolts or approved structural screws / lag screws** in the
   prescribed staggered pattern and edge distances — **never nails** for the ledger.
5. Lateral load connectors (tension ties into the floor joists) where required.

## 3. Footings, posts, beams
- Footings below frost depth and on undisturbed soil, sized to tributary load.
- Posts on **post bases** that keep end grain off concrete; posts not embedded in concrete
  (end-grain rot) unless rated and designed for it.
- Beams (bearers) notched into or bolted to posts per the tables; splice only over posts.

## 4. Joists
- Joist size and span from tables for the timber species/grade and spacing
  (often 16" / 400 mm centres for timber decking, closer for diagonal boards or composite
  per manufacturer).
- Hang on the ledger with **joist hangers** — fully nailed with the hanger maker's
  specified nails/screws (not deck screws, which can shear).
- Crown up; block at mid-span or as specified; rim joists square — measure diagonals.
- Joist tape on top edges helps shed water at board fixings (practice, not usually code).

## 5. Timber and corrosion
- Use **preservative-treated** timber rated for the exposure: above ground vs ground/
  water contact (e.g. US UC3B vs UC4A/4B; UK Use Class 3 vs 4). Posts and anything within
  ~150 mm of the ground → ground-contact rating.
- Treat cut ends with an end-cut preservative.
- Modern copper-based treatments corrode plain steel: use **hot-dip galvanised (G185/Z275
  or heavier)** or **stainless** fasteners and connectors as the treatment supplier/
  connector maker specifies; stainless near the coast or pools. Don't mix metals.

## 6. Boards and guards
- Gaps between boards for drainage and movement: e.g. ~5–6 mm for timber (depends on
  board moisture at installation — wet treated boards shrink); composite per manufacturer.
- Two fixings per board per joist; pre-drill near ends.
- Guard posts: their attachment is a structural connection — use tested hardware or the
  prescribed bolted detail; infill must not pass a 4" / 100 mm sphere.

## Verify
- [ ] Ledger on solid rim (not veneer), flashed under the weather barrier, bolted to schedule.
- [ ] Footings below frost depth; posts on bases, not buried end grain.
- [ ] Joists in fully nailed hangers with specified fasteners.
- [ ] Timber treatment class and fastener coating suit the exposure.
- [ ] Guard height and posts meet code; permit inspections passed.
