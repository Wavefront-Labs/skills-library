# Hanging an internal door

**Goal:** a door that swings freely, stays where you leave it (no self-opening or
closing), shuts on the latch without slamming, and shows even gaps all round.

## 1. The lining must be right first
A badly set lining cannot be fixed by planing the door.
1. Assemble the lining (head housed into legs) and stand it in the rough opening.
2. Pack the **hinge-side leg** plumb in both directions with folding wedges/shims at
   hinge positions and the latch height, fixing through the packing.
3. Pack the head level, then the latch leg parallel to the hinge leg — measure the
   width at top, middle and bottom (should match).
4. Check for **twist (wind)**: stretch two strings diagonally across the lining
   corners; they should just touch where they cross. A twisted lining makes the door
   hit the stop at one corner.

## 2. Size the leaf
Typical gaps (confirm for the door type; fire doors have specified maximum gaps):
- Hinge side and latch side: **2–3 mm**.
- Head: **2–3 mm**.
- Bottom: to clear the finished floor covering — often ~8–10 mm over hard floors,
  more for carpet; for fire doors follow the certification (often ≤ ~10 mm, and smoke
  seals may require less).
Trim:
- Plane the hinge edge square; plane the latch edge with a slight **bevel** (~2–3°)
  toward the stop side so the trailing edge clears as it closes.
- Take equal amounts off each side of solid doors where possible; hollow-core doors have
  only narrow solid lippings (~25–35 mm) — don't plane through them.
- Cut the bottom with a track saw or a scored line to avoid splintering.

## 3. Hinges
- Position: commonly ~150 mm from the top and ~225 mm from the bottom (UK habit);
  heavy or fire doors take a third hinge mid-way. *Follow door manufacturer.*
- Wedge the door in the opening with the gaps set; mark hinge positions on door and
  lining simultaneously.
- Cut recesses to exactly the leaf thickness — with a hinge jig and router or
  chisel: score the outline, chop across at 3–5 mm intervals, pare flat.
- Fix one screw per hinge, test-swing, then drive the rest. Use screws supplied/specified;
  for fire doors, steel CE/UKCA-marked hinges as certified.

## 4. Latch and keep
- Latch height usually ~1000–1050 mm to centre of handle (or match other doors).
- Drill/mortise the edge and face per template, keeping square to the door face.
- Close the door onto the latch, mark the tongue position on the lining, cut the keep
  recess, and fit the strike plate so the door pulls against the stops without rattle.

## 5. Stops
Fit door stops last: close the door to the latch, hold it flush with the lining edge
(or set back as designed), and pin stops with a **~1–2 mm gap** (a coin thickness) to the
leaf on the latch side, tight-ish on the hinge side. Then the door shuts softly onto the
stop without bouncing.

## Fault-finding
- **Door swings open/closed on its own** → hinge leg not plumb; repack.
- **Binds at top latch corner** → head gap too tight or top hinge recess too shallow;
  deepen recess or pack bottom hinge.
- **Hinge-bound (springs open)** → recesses too deep or screw heads proud; shim behind
  hinge leaves.
- **Rattles** → adjust keep tongue or stop.

## Fire doors
A fire door is a certified *assembly*: leaf, frame, gaps, hinges, intumescent seals,
glazing and closer. Any deviation (wrong hinges, oversize gaps, unapproved cuts) can
void certification — follow the manufacturer's installation instructions exactly.

## Verify
- [ ] Hinge leg plumb both ways; lining out of twist.
- [ ] Gaps even all round within the stated tolerance.
- [ ] Door stays at any open position without drifting.
- [ ] Latches positively with no rattle; stops set.
- [ ] Fire doors match their certification label and instructions.
