# Fitting skirting and architrave

**Goal:** trim that looks crisp at arm's length: tight joints, consistent reveals and no
gaps along walls or floors — in rooms where nothing is square, flat or level.

## Tools
Compound mitre saw with a sharp fine-tooth blade, coping saw (or a coping jig/router),
digital or sliding-bevel angle finder, block plane, files, pin/brad nailer
(15–18 gauge) and/or adhesive, compass/scribing tool, sharp pencil, fine filler and
decorator's caulk.

## Architraves (door casing) first
1. **Reveal/margin:** mark a consistent setback from the inside face of the door lining,
   typically ~5–6 mm (≈ 3/16–1/4"), at each corner and along the legs with a combination
   square or margin gauge. This line hides lining irregularities.
2. Cut the **head** first: mitre one end, hold it to the reveal marks, mark the other,
   cut. Long point to long point = lining width + 2× reveal.
3. Legs: mitre the top to match, mark length at the floor (allow for floor finish to come
   — leave above if carpet/floating floor isn't down; check the plan).
4. If the lining isn't plumb or the head isn't level, the mitre won't be 45°: test-fit
   offcuts and trim one side (a few degrees on the saw, or a block plane) until closed.
5. Glue the mitres, **pin across the mitre** (one pin through the top edge) to lock it,
   then fix to the lining (small pins) and to the stud (longer pins) at ~300–400 mm.
6. Out-of-plane walls: if the wall stands proud of the lining, back-bevel the architrave
   or plane the lining edge — don't force it, it twists the mitre open.

## Skirting
### Order of work
Plan the run so the visible joints are well made: start on the wall opposite the door
(both ends coped or square into corners), finish on walls where cut joints are least seen.
### Internal corners: cope, don't mitre
Internal walls are rarely 90°, and mitres open as timber shrinks. Instead:
1. Butt the first piece square into the corner.
2. On the second piece, cut a 45° internal mitre to reveal the profile line.
3. Cut along that profile with a coping saw, back-cutting a few degrees so only the face
   edge touches. Clean with files. The cope slides over the first piece and stays tight
   even if the corner moves.
(For square MDF profiles, a simple mitre may be acceptable.)
### External corners: measure and split
Measure the actual angle with an angle finder; set each piece to half (a 92° corner →
46° each). Glue the mitre, pin through it, and keep the pieces slightly long first to
sneak up on the length.
### Lengths over a run
Join long runs with a **scarf joint** (two opposing ~45° bevels in the thickness) over a
stud, glued — not a butt joint, which opens.
### Scribing to floors
Where the floor dips, hold the skirting level, set the compass to the largest gap, run it
along the floor to transfer the line, then plane/jigsaw to it. On hard floors, scribe;
on carpet, leave ~6–10 mm up for the carpet to tuck under (check with the layer).

## Fixing and finishing
- Fix into studs/grounds (wood walls) or with adhesive + pins/screws to masonry.
- Punch pins, fill holes with fine filler; caulk only the paint-grade wall line, never
  the joints in stained work.
- Prime MDF cut ends before painting — they drink paint.
- Let timber acclimatise in the room for a few days before fitting.

## Gotchas
- Mitre saw settings drift — test-cut and check with a square before each job.
- Measuring "short point to short point" vs "long point to long point" mixups cost
  more lengths than anything else; write which on the measurement.

## Verify
- [ ] Architrave reveals equal all round (check with gauge).
- [ ] All mitres closed with no visible gap at 1 m.
- [ ] Internal corners coped (for profiled stock); externals glued and pinned.
- [ ] No gap under skirting on hard floors larger than finish/caulk can hide.
- [ ] Holes filled, ends primed, ready for decorator.
