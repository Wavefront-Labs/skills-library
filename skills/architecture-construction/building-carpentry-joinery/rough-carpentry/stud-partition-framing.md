# Framing a timber stud partition

**Goal:** a straight, plumb, flat non-structural partition that boards cleanly, carries
whatever will be hung on it, and has its door openings the right size the first time.
(Load-bearing walls are a structural-trades job designed by an engineer — don't use this
for them.)

## Materials
- Timber: typically 89×38 / 2×4 or 63×38 for thinner partitions; structural graded,
  **dry** (kiln-dried, ≤ ~19% moisture). Sight every length; set aside bowed or twisted
  stock for blocking. Wet timber shrinks and pops screws through the board finish.
- Fixings: 90 mm / 3½" nails (framing nailer) or screws; masonry anchors or concrete
  nails/powder-actuated fasteners for plates on slabs (check for underfloor heating
  pipes and services first).

## 1. Set out
1. Mark the wall line on the floor from the drawing — measure from two fixed points, and
   check room squareness with 3-4-5 or diagonals.
2. Snap a chalk line for one face of the bottom plate.
3. Transfer to the ceiling with a **rotary or cross-line laser** (or plumb bob) and snap
   the top line. Mark door openings on the floor line.

## 2. Plates
- Fix the bottom (sole) plate at ~600 mm centres and within ~150 mm of each end; cut it
  out at door openings *after* the frame is up (leave it continuous for alignment).
- Fix the top (head) plate to joists where they cross; where joists run parallel, add
  noggins between joists above to fix to.
- Under a concrete slab ceiling with expected deflection (commercial), use a **deflection
  head** detail instead of a tight fit — *check the engineer's requirements.*

## 3. Stud layout
- Mark stud centres on both plates together so studs land plumb. Centres suit the board:
  **400 mm / 16"** or **600 mm / 24"**, so every board edge lands on a stud
  (1200 mm board → 400 or 600; 4' board → 16" or 24"). Start the layout so the first
  board edge lands on stud centre.
- Measure each stud individually (floors and ceilings are rarely parallel); cut for a
  firm push fit. Skew-nail (toe-nail) two each side at top and bottom, or screw.
- Crown all studs the same way (usually toward one face) and swap out any that bow.

## 4. Door openings
- Rough opening = door leaf + lining/jamb thickness ×2 + packing allowance. Example
  (UK-style): 826 leaf + 2×(lining 15–18) + ~5 mm each side packing → ≈ 870–880 mm
  between studs. *Confirm with the door/frame being supplied.*
- Full-height studs (king studs) each side, a trimmer, and a head (lintel) at
  door height + lining head + packing; cripple studs above to the top plate at layout centres.
- Height allowance: leaf height + head lining + floor finish + clearance under door.

## 5. Noggins and backing
- Noggins mid-height (stagger for nailing) on walls over ~2.4 m or as the board system
  requires; also at board horizontal joints.
- **Backing for everything heavy**: wall cupboards, basins, grab rails, TV brackets,
  handrails, radiators, door stops. Photograph and measure before boarding — you will
  forget otherwise.
- Leave service routes: drill holes on stud centreline, within limits (a common
  guide: no more than ~25% of stud depth for non-load-bearing), and fit steel plates
  where pipes/cables are within 50 mm of the face (wiring regulations often require this).

## 6. Check
- Straightedge/laser along both faces; plane or pack proud/shy studs (±3 mm over 1.8 m is
  a common tolerance for board finishes — *check spec*).
- Plumb every king stud with a level.
- Acoustic partitions: insulation between studs and sealed perimeters; flanking at the
  head is where sound leaks.

## Gotchas
- Forgetting the extra stud at internal corners/junctions leaves no board fixing.
- Fixing plates over underfloor heating — check drawings or use a thermal camera.

## Verify
- [ ] Wall line matches drawing within tolerance and is square to the room.
- [ ] Every board edge lands on a stud; studs plumb.
- [ ] Door rough openings measured against the actual door set.
- [ ] Backing installed and recorded for every heavy fixture.
- [ ] Face flat to straightedge before boarding.
