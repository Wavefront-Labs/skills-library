# Installing kitchen cabinets

**Goal:** a kitchen whose cabinets are dead level and in line, with tight scribes to
walls, evenly gapped doors, and a worktop that fits first time — on floors and walls that
are neither level nor straight.

## 0. Before starting
- Check the delivery against the plan (every carcass, panel, filler, plinth, hinge pack).
- Confirm services are in the right place: water, waste, gas, electrical outlets and
  isolation switches, extract duct route. Fitters get blamed for first-fix errors.
- Mark appliance clearances and ventilation needs from the appliance manuals
  (built-in ovens and fridges often need specific vents in the plinth or carcass).
- Assemble flat-pack carcasses square (measure diagonals) on a clean surface.

## 1. Datum line
1. Find the **floor high point** along all runs (laser and staff, or a long level).
2. From it, set the worktop height: commonly **~900–910 mm** to top of worktop (check
   the design and appliance heights — dishwashers and under-counter fridges need a minimum).
3. Shoot a **laser datum** around the room at top-of-base-carcass height and mark it.
   Also mark wall-unit underside (often ~450–500 mm above worktop, more above a hob per
   the hood/hob manufacturer).
4. Mark stud/fixing positions and pipe/cable zones on the walls before drilling.

## 2. Base units
- Start from a **corner or a fixed point** (tall unit, appliance housing) and work out.
- Adjust legs to bring every carcass to the datum, front-to-back and side-to-side.
- Clamp neighbouring carcasses with their fronts flush, then screw together through the
  sides (connector bolts or screws, positioned behind hinge plates so they're hidden).
- Fix to the wall through the back rail into studs/plugs — carcasses must not move
  when the worktop is loaded.
- Leave appliance gaps exactly at the specified width (e.g. 600 mm): measure at front
  and back.

## 3. Wall units
- Fix a **hanging rail** level to the datum into solid backing (studs, noggins installed
  at framing, or masonry with suitable plugs); plasterboard alone is not enough.
- Hang units on adjustable hangers, adjust height and depth, clamp and join neighbours
  as for bases, then lock the hangers.
- Tall units: plumb both ways, then fix to the wall and neighbours.

## 4. Fillers and scribes
Walls are out of plumb and wavy. Where a unit meets a wall, use a filler or end panel:
hold it in position, set a compass to the largest gap, scribe, cut with a jigsaw/plane
(back-bevel for a tight face line), then fix from inside the carcass.

## 5. Worktops
- Laminate/wood worktop joints: **mason's mitre** cut with a router and jig, or a
  postformed/edge-strip joint; seal the joint edges and use bolts underneath.
- Hob and sink cutouts from the appliance template; keep at least the manufacturer's
  distance from joints and ends; seal cut edges (water swells particle board).
- Stone/quartz/solid-surface worktops are templated after bases are fixed and installed
  by specialists — bases must be level within their tolerance (often ~1–2 mm over 1 m).
- Wood worktops need expansion gaps and slotted fixings.

## 6. Doors, drawers, plinths
- Fit hinges, then adjust (most European concealed hinges have side, height and depth
  adjustments) for **even gaps** (~2–3 mm) and aligned tops along the run.
- Drawer fronts aligned to doors; soft-close checked.
- Plinths cut to height below the carcasses, scribed to the floor, with vents where needed.

## Gotchas
- Leveling from the low point puts the high-point unit's legs below minimum.
- Corner units: check the door/drawer clearance with handles before fixing (handles clash).
- Hinge adjustment ranges are limited — a carcass out of level can't be fixed at the door.

## Verify
- [ ] Base run on datum within ~1 mm per metre; wall units level and in line.
- [ ] All units fixed to wall and to each other into solid fixings.
- [ ] Appliance gaps correct; ventilation provided per appliance manuals.
- [ ] Scribes tight; cut edges sealed.
- [ ] Doors and drawers evenly gapped, soft-close working.
