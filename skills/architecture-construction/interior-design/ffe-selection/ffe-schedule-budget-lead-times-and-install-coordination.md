# FF&E schedule, budget, lead times and install coordination

**Goal:** every piece of furniture, lighting and equipment specified, approved,
ordered in time, delivered undamaged and installed on the right day — on budget.

## 1. The FF&E schedule is the master record
One row per item type, with a unique code (`CH-01`, `TB-03`, `LT-05`):
- Image, manufacturer, model, dimensions, finish/fabric (with COM details), quantity,
  location(s) by room number.
- Unit cost, extended cost, freight, tax/duty, installation cost — **landed cost**,
  not list price.
- Supplier contact, quote reference, lead time, order date, expected ship date,
  delivery date, status.
- Contract-grade/fire requirement (e.g. commercial seating flammability standards
  for the jurisdiction) and warranty.
Keep it in a spreadsheet or FF&E software; the drawings' FF&E plan uses the same codes.

## 2. Budget
- Build the budget bottom-up from the schedule; hold a **contingency** (often 5–10%)
  for freight surprises, price rises and damage replacement.
- Track **committed vs paid vs remaining** per item and in total. Report to the
  client at fixed intervals, flagging variances early.
- Be transparent about pricing model (trade discount passed on vs procurement fee) —
  this is a common source of disputes; put it in the appointment.

## 3. Lead times drive the programme
- Collect lead times at selection, not at order. Custom upholstery, lighting and
  imported pieces often run 8–16+ weeks; stone and bespoke joinery longer.
- Work backwards from the install date: order date = install − lead time − shipping −
  receiving buffer (~2 weeks). Long-lead items get client approval first.
- **COM (customer's own material):** order the fabric early, ship it to the maker,
  confirm yardage with the maker's calculation (pattern repeat and railroading change
  it) — usually with ~10% overage.

## 4. Custom pieces
Brief with drawings: overall dims, construction, materials, finishes with control
samples, hardware, load and use (e.g. commercial banquette), fixing to structure.
Require **shop drawings** for approval and, for big runs, a prototype or finish sample.

## 5. Receiving and install
- Use a receiving warehouse for consolidated delivery when there are many suppliers;
  inspect on arrival and photograph damage immediately (claims windows can be days).
- Install day plan: site clear and clean (trades finished, floors protected), access
  and lift booked, placement plan per room, team for assembly, waste removal.
- Walk through with the client after placement; record a **snag/punch list** with
  photos and owners; close it out with suppliers.

## Gotchas
- Budgeting at list price with no freight, tax or install.
- Selecting first, asking lead times later.
- COM yardage short because the pattern repeat was ignored.
- Delivering furniture before the painters have finished.

## Verify
- [ ] Every item coded, priced at landed cost, with lead time and status.
- [ ] Committed/paid/remaining reconciles to budget plus contingency.
- [ ] Order dates back-calculated from the install date; long-lead flagged.
- [ ] Shop drawings approved for custom work.
- [ ] Damage inspected at receipt; snag list closed out.
