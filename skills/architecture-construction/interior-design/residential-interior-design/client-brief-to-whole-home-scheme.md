# Client brief to whole-home scheme

**Goal:** a home scheme the client signs off with confidence, that holds together
room to room, fits their real life and budget, and doesn't unravel during the build.

## 1. The briefing visit
Ask about life, not just looks:
- **Who lives there**, ages, pets, how often guests stay, who cooks, who works from
  home, routines (morning bathroom rush, where bags and shoes land).
- **What stays:** furniture, art and heirlooms you must design around — photograph
  and measure them.
- **What bothers them** now (dark hall, no storage, cold room) — these are the
  measurable wins.
- **Taste:** ask for images they love *and* hate; hate lists are often more precise.
  Ask why they chose each.
- **Budget and timescale**, and who decides. Get the budget stated as a number,
  separated into building work, furnishings and fees.
Measure and photograph every room; note orientation (light direction matters for colour).

## 2. Write the brief back
A one- to two-page brief listing needs, constraints, keep-list, priorities ranked,
budget and programme. The client signs it. Every later decision gets checked against it.

## 3. Budget reality check
Early, before design: rough-cost the wish list (rule-of-thumb £/$/€ per m² for
works, allowances per room for furnishings). If wishes exceed budget, agree
priorities or phasing **now**. Clients rarely object to a hard conversation at
stage one; they object to it at tender.

## 4. Concept that flows
- Pick a **unifying thread** — a restricted base palette, a consistent floor through
  connected spaces, repeated materials (one timber tone, one metal finish), a
  consistent door/ironmongery style — then let each room vary within it.
- Look from room to room through open doors: adjacent spaces should relate in tone
  and undertone even if colours differ (see
  `architecture-construction.interior-design.colour-schemes.building-a-palette-that-works-in-real-light`).
- Present a **concept board** first (mood, palette, key materials), get agreement,
  then go room by room. Don't show finished schemes before the direction is agreed.

## 5. Room schemes and sample boards
For each room: plan with furniture, elevations where needed, a sample board of
**real** samples (paint drawdowns, fabric, flooring, tile, hardware), and a priced
item list. Show samples in the room's own light, morning and evening if possible.

## 6. Staged sign-off
Sign-offs at concept, room schemes, and final specification/FF&E before ordering.
Record changes in writing with cost/time impact. Late changes on order items are the
main source of overspend and disputes.

## Gotchas
- Designing for the Pinterest image, not the family with a dog and two kids.
- Budget discussed only after the scheme is loved.
- Each room beautiful alone, jarring together.
- Samples approved under showroom lights.

## Verify
- [ ] Written brief signed, with ranked priorities and a number for budget.
- [ ] Keep-list items placed in the scheme.
- [ ] Unifying thread visible across the plan and boards.
- [ ] Samples viewed in situ under real light.
- [ ] Sign-off recorded at each stage; change log kept.
