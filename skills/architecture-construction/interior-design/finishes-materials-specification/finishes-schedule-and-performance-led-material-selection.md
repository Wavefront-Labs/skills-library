# Finishes schedule and performance-led material selection

**Goal:** finishes that look right on day one and still perform in year ten, written
down so precisely that the contractor installs exactly what was approved.

## 1. Start from performance, then aesthetics
For each surface, list what it must withstand before looking at samples:
- **Wear / traffic:** domestic vs commercial; carpet and LVT use-class ratings (EN
  ISO 10874 classes like 33/34 in Europe); upholstery abrasion (Martindale rub
  counts — roughly 25,000+ for general domestic, 40,000+ for heavy commercial seating;
  check the supplier's recommendation for the use).
- **Slip resistance** in wet or entrance areas: pendulum test value (PTV; UK guidance
  treats ≥ 36 as low slip potential in wet) or ramp R-ratings (R10–R11+ for wet
  commercial floors). Methods aren't interchangeable — specify the test as well as the value.
- **Fire:** wall/ceiling linings must meet the code class for the space and escape
  routes (Euroclass B-s3,d2 / Class 0-type requirements in UK; ASTM E84 Class A/B/C
  in the US). Fabrics in commercial seating/curtains may need specific fire tests.
- **Moisture and cleaning:** what chemicals will cleaners use? Natural stone and oiled
  timber hate acidic and strong alkaline cleaners.
- **Health:** low-VOC paints, adhesives and boards; certifications where the project
  targets WELL/LEED/BREEAM.
- **Maintenance and replacement:** can a damaged tile/plank be replaced? Order
  attic stock (spare material) — typically a few % of each finish.

## 2. Finish codes and the schedule
- Give every finish a **code** by type: `PT-01` paint, `FL-02` flooring, `WC-01` wall
  covering, `TL-03` tile, `JN-01` joinery finish, `MT-01` metal.
- The **finishes schedule** row: code, product/manufacturer, range/colour/reference,
  size/format, finish/texture, installation method/pattern, grout or trim, location
  (room numbers), performance requirement (slip, fire, wear), supplier and lead time.
- Use the same code on plans, elevations and the room-by-room finishes matrix
  (room × floor/skirting/walls/ceiling).

## 3. Specification clauses
- Write to your office system (NBS clauses in the UK, CSI MasterFormat sections in
  North America): product, performance, substrate prep, installation standard,
  tolerances (e.g. floor flatness for large-format tile), protection and handover.
- **Substitution rule:** "or approved equivalent" only with the performance criteria
  stated, and with submission of samples and data for approval. Without criteria,
  "equivalent" means cheaper.

## 4. Samples
- Approve **physical samples**; keep a signed control sample on site. Large-batch
  materials (stone, tile, timber) vary by batch: ask for range samples and specify
  one batch per area.

## Gotchas
- Choosing a lovely honed marble for a busy café counter.
- Slip values quoted without the test method.
- Fire class met by the board but not by the decorative finish applied to it.
- Long-lead items specified last.

## Verify
- [ ] Every surface has a code, a product, and stated performance criteria.
- [ ] Codes consistent across drawings, schedule and spec.
- [ ] Fire, slip and wear requirements checked against the code/brief for each room.
- [ ] Lead times logged; long-lead items flagged.
- [ ] Signed control samples on site; attic stock specified.
