# Circulation, clearances and furniture layout

**Goal:** a layout where people move without squeezing, furniture fits with room to
use it, and the plan meets accessibility and egress rules — checked by numbers, not feel.

## 1. Brief → adjacency
1. List spaces and activities with their size needs (from the brief) and who uses
   them when.
2. Draw an **adjacency/bubble diagram**: strong links (kitchen–dining), weak links,
   and things that must be apart (bedroom and noisy plant, toilets opening onto dining).
3. Overlay on the plan's fixed constraints: windows/daylight, structure, wet-service
   risers, entrances, fire exits.

## 2. Circulation first, furniture second
- Draw **routes** (door to door, entrance to destinations) as bands before placing
  furniture. Keep primary routes straight and clear; don't route through
  conversation groups or work areas.
- Rule-of-thumb widths (check your local code for minimums):
  - Main walkway in a home: ~900–1000 mm; secondary: ~600–750 mm.
  - Commercial corridors and accessible routes: often 1200 mm+ clear; many codes
    set an absolute minimum around 900–915 mm (ADA: 36 in) with passing spaces.
- **Wheelchair turning:** a 1500 mm (ADA: 60 in) diameter circle, or a T-turn, in
  accessible rooms and at the ends of routes.
- Door swings: show them; nothing within the arc. Accessible doors need clear space
  on the latch side (ADA push/pull side clearances; UK Approved Document M has its
  own figures).

## 3. Furniture clearances (common rules of thumb)
- **Dining:** ~900 mm from table edge to wall/obstacle to pull out a chair; ~1100–
  1200 mm if people walk behind seated diners. Allow ~600 mm table width per diner.
- **Living:** coffee table ~400–450 mm from sofa; seating within ~2.5–3 m across for
  conversation; TV viewing distance roughly 1.5–2.5 × screen diagonal (preference varies).
- **Bedroom:** ~600–750 mm beside and at the foot of a bed; ~900 mm in front of a
  wardrobe/drawers to open them.
- **Desks:** ~1000–1200 mm behind a desk for the chair and passage.
- **Kitchen** clearances: see
  `architecture-construction.interior-design.kitchen-bathroom-design.kitchen-work-zones-and-bathroom-clearances`.

## 4. Codes and egress (commercial especially)
- Occupant load (area ÷ code load factor) drives the **number and width of exits**
  and travel distances. Never reduce exit widths with furniture.
- Keep fire doors, extinguishers, hose reels and alarm call points accessible.
- These numbers vary by jurisdiction — note the code and edition you used on the drawing.

## 5. Test-fit options
- Produce two or three layouts at the same scale; score each against the brief
  (capacity, adjacencies met, daylight, cost of moving walls). Present options with
  furniture at true size — clients misjudge scale from symbols.

## Gotchas
- Furniture placed first, then circulation squeezed into what's left.
- Furniture symbols smaller than the actual pieces specified.
- Forgetting door swings, radiators, sockets and windowsills.
- Accessibility "added later" when it requires moving walls.

## Verify
- [ ] Every route at or above its target width; door arcs clear.
- [ ] Dining, bed, wardrobe and desk clearances checked with a tape on the plan.
- [ ] Turning circles shown in accessible spaces.
- [ ] Egress widths/travel distances checked against the named code.
- [ ] Layout tested with the client's actual furniture dimensions.
