# Workplace brief to Cat A / Cat B fit-out package

**Goal:** a fit-out that matches how the organisation actually works, builds only
what the tenant needs on top of the landlord's base, and tenders cleanly.

## 1. Understand Cat A vs Cat B (UK terms; elsewhere "shell and core" vs "tenant improvement")
- **Shell & core:** structure, lifts, risers, base services to the floor.
- **Cat A:** landlord-standard finished floor — raised floor, suspended ceiling,
  lighting, base HVAC, toilets. Open, empty, lettable.
- **Cat B:** the tenant's fit-out — partitions, meeting rooms, kitchen, reception,
  furniture, IT/AV, branding, and any changes to Cat A (moving ductwork for rooms).
Read the **lease and landlord's fit-out guide** early: what you may alter, reinstatement
obligations at lease end, approved contractors, working hours, and the consent process.

## 2. Workplace brief from data, not opinions
- **Utilisation study:** sample occupancy (sensor data or walk-through counts several
  times a day for ~2 weeks). Offices commonly show far lower peak desk use than
  headcount suggests — but measure, don't assume.
- **Staff survey / workshops** per team: work modes (focus, collaboration, calls,
  learning), adjacencies, storage, hybrid patterns by day of week.
- Translate into a **space budget**: desk ratio (e.g. 0.6–0.8 desks per person for
  hybrid — set by data and policy), number and size of meeting rooms (count
  bookings and actual attendees — most bookings are 2–4 people), focus rooms, phone
  booths, breakout, amenities, storage.

## 3. Test-fit
- Lay the space budget onto the floor plate. Respect the Cat A grid: ceiling grid
  and lighting module, raised-floor grid, mullion spacing (partitions must land on
  mullions), and HVAC zones. Rooms on the core keep the perimeter daylight for open
  work.
- Check means of escape and fire compartmentation with the fire engineer; enclosed
  rooms may need extra smoke detection, sprinkler heads and separate cooling.
- Present 2–3 options with area schedules and an order-of-cost estimate.

## 4. Retail and hospitality notes
- Retail: customer journey from threshold, sightlines to feature walls, back-of-house
  stock flow, landlord's shopfront rules.
- Hospitality: covers per area, kitchen and bar workflow with the operator,
  acoustics, durable finishes for heavy wear and cleaning chemicals.

## 5. The fit-out package
Drawings (setting-out, partition types, RCP, finishes, joinery, furniture, power and
data positions), finishes and FF&E schedules, outline specification, a clear scope
split (Cat A changes vs Cat B new, tenant-direct items like IT and furniture), and
landlord consent drawings. Coordinate M&E design for rooms that affect services.

## Gotchas
- Designing meeting rooms by gut — usually too many big rooms, too few small.
- Partitions that hit the middle of a mullion or a ceiling grid.
- Missing landlord consent or reinstatement costs.
- Enclosed rooms with no cooling — they overheat by afternoon.

## Verify
- [ ] Lease/fit-out guide reviewed; constraints listed.
- [ ] Space budget traced to measured utilisation and survey.
- [ ] Partitions on mullions and grids; escape routes checked.
- [ ] Scope split between Cat A alterations, Cat B and tenant-direct is explicit.
- [ ] Package ready for tender with schedules matching drawings.
