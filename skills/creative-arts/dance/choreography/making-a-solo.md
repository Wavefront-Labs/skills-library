# Making a solo

**Goal:** a solo that holds attention on one body for its whole duration.

## Method
1. **Start from the performer:** their strengths (jump, flexibility, groundedness, acting),
   personality and the concept.
2. **Arc:** solos need development - a change in state or a journey (from contained to
   expansive, from searching to finding).
3. **Space:** one dancer can't fill a stage by standing centre; use travel, diagonals, corners,
   levels, and proximity to the audience to create variety.
4. **Dynamics:** contrast is crucial - sudden and sustained, strong and light; stillness is a
   powerful tool in a solo.
5. **Pacing:** most solos work at 2-5 minutes; longer requires strong material and arc.
6. **Outside eye:** self-choreographers must film and get feedback; you cannot see yourself.

## Self-choreographing tips
- Film every session; watch as an audience member.
- Work from improvisation and tasks to get past personal habits.
- Rehearse performance quality early, not only steps.

## Verify
- [ ] Solo has a clear arc and change.
- [ ] Spatial plan uses more than centre stage.
- [ ] Feedback from an outside eye incorporated.
