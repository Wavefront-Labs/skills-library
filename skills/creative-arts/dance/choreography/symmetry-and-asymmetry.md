# Symmetry and asymmetry

**Goal:** choose symmetric or asymmetric shapes - in a single body and in group stage pictures
- to create the intended feeling.

## Humphrey's view
- **Symmetry:** stable, calm, formal - but quickly becomes static and dull.
- **Asymmetry:** dynamic, exciting, unstable - more expressive, but can look chaotic if
  unbalanced.
- Use symmetry for moments of rest or ritual, asymmetry for drama and motion.

## Body shapes
- Symmetric: both sides the same (arms in 5th, feet in 1st).
- Asymmetric: different on each side (arabesque, a twisted reach); opposition adds life.

## Stage pictures
1. **Symmetric:** groups mirrored across the centre line - formal, balanced (corps de ballet lines).
2. **Asymmetric balance:** a large group on one side balanced by a single dancer or a strong
   action on the other.
3. **Shifting:** move from symmetric to asymmetric to show disruption, or back for resolution.

## Method
1. Take photos/stills of key moments from the front; check balance.
2. Mark which moments should feel stable vs unstable; design accordingly.
3. Avoid accidental symmetry (everyone in identical lines) unless intended.

## Verify
- [ ] Key stage pictures photographed and assessed for balance.
- [ ] Symmetric moments used deliberately, not by default.
- [ ] Asymmetric pictures balanced in weight and focus.
