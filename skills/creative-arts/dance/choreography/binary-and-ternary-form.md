# Binary and ternary form

**Goal:** give a dance a clear, satisfying overall shape using two classical structures
borrowed from music.

## Binary (AB)
- Two sections that contrast but share a link (a motif, a dancer, a spatial idea).
- Use when the dance moves from one state to another (calm to chaos, solo to group).
- Contrast tools: tempo, dynamics, number of dancers, level, music.

## Ternary (ABA)
- A, contrasting B, then A returns. The return feels like a homecoming.
- The second A is usually **developed** (A'): same material, changed by what happened in B -
  smaller, in canon, with new dancers, or in a new space.

## Method
1. Write a one-line intention for each section.
2. Make A's material first and define its identity (motif, quality, formation).
3. Make B by contrasting at least two dimensions with A while keeping one link.
4. Design the transition A to B and B to A' (see choreographic transitions).
5. Time it: sections roughly balanced, or deliberately unequal for effect (short return).

## Verify
- [ ] Viewer can identify the sections without being told.
- [ ] B contrasts A in at least two dimensions while keeping one link.
- [ ] A' recognisably returns and shows development.
