# Building and developing a movement phrase

**Goal:** get from a blank studio to a few minutes of coherent, original material:
invent a short phrase with a task, then multiply and vary it with composition devices so
the dance grows from its own material instead of a string of unrelated steps.

> **Status: draft.** These devices are standard composition teaching (Smith-Autard,
> Blom & Chaplin and most dance degree programmes). Times and counts are suggestions.

## 1. Generate raw material with a task (20–40 min)
Tasks beat "making something up" because they give the body constraints. Examples:
- **Body writing:** write a word or your name in space with different body parts (elbow,
  hip, head), at different sizes.
- **Gesture source:** take 3–4 everyday gestures (checking a phone, pushing a door) and
  exaggerate, slow, or relocate them to another body part.
- **Image or text:** five verbs from a poem — *scatter, fold, hover, crack, drift* — one
  movement per verb.
- **Spatial path:** a floor pattern drawn on paper, travelled with changes of level.

Improvise each task for a few minutes, **film it**, then pick the moments that surprised
you. Set those into a fixed sequence: your **motif phrase**, typically 8–32 counts or
20–40 seconds. Make it repeatable exactly — same facing, same counts, same weight shifts.

## 2. Give it dynamic life
A phrase in one speed and weight reads as flat. Run it through Laban's effort factors:
- **Time:** sudden vs sustained. **Weight:** strong vs light. **Space:** direct vs indirect.
  **Flow:** bound vs free.
- Also change **level** (floor, mid, high), **size**, and **facing**.
Mark the phrase's internal highs and lows: where it accelerates, where it suspends,
where the weight drops. Phrasing — a breath shape of start, build, peak, resolve — is
what makes movement read as a sentence.

## 3. Develop: the motif devices
Apply these to the motif to get new but related material:

| Device | What you do |
|---|---|
| Repetition | same phrase again — clarity, emphasis |
| Retrograde | perform it backwards |
| Inversion | flip it — up becomes down, right becomes left |
| Augmentation / diminution | make it bigger/slower or smaller/faster |
| Fragmentation | use only a part (e.g. counts 3–5) and loop it |
| Accumulation | 1; 1-2; 1-2-3 … building up the phrase gesture by gesture |
| Instrumentation | do the arm phrase with the legs, or the whole thing seated |
| Embellishment | add a turn, a jump, a pause within it |

With more than one dancer: **unison, canon** (staggered starts), **complement** (shapes
that answer each other), **contrast** (one still while the other moves), and
**question-and-answer**.

## 4. Structure the section
Choose a form and know why: **binary (AB)**, **ternary (ABA)**, **rondo (ABACAD…)**,
**theme and variations**, or narrative. Contrast between sections (tempo, density, number of
dancers) keeps attention; return of the motif gives unity.

## 5. Musicality
Decide the relationship to the score: **on the music** (counts, accents hit), **against**
it (contrapuntal phrasing that crosses bar lines), or **independent** (shared time only).
Count the music before choreographing; mark sections and accents on the track's timeline.

## Gotchas
- Too much new material and not enough development — the audience never learns
  anything to recognise. Reuse more than feels comfortable.
- Everything facing front, everything at standing level.
- Making the phrase on your own body only: check it reads on your dancers' bodies (see
  `creative-arts.dance.ensemble-staging.cleaning-unison-and-spacing`).
- Not filming: you will forget it overnight.

## Verify
- [ ] A motif phrase that can be repeated identically, filmed and counted.
- [ ] At least three development devices applied, visibly related to the motif.
- [ ] Clear dynamic contrast (time/weight) within the phrase.
- [ ] A named structure for the section, with a return of the motif.
- [ ] A conscious, stated relationship to the music.
