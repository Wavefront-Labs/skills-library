# Choreographic copyright and licensing

**Goal:** choreography is protected by copyright in most countries, but only in specific
ways. Knowing what is protected, how to prove it and how to license it lets
choreographers earn from their work and control how it is shown.

> **Status: draft.** General information, not legal advice. Based on US Copyright Office
> Circular 52 and Compendium chapter 800, and UK copyright guidance. Law differs by
> country; consult a lawyer for contracts.

## 1. What is protected
- **US:** "choreographic works" are protected (Copyright Act of 1976). The Copyright Office
  treats choreography as a related series of dance movements and patterns organised into
  a coherent whole. **Not protected:** individual steps, simple routines, social dances
  and ordinary exercise or sports moves.
- **UK:** a work of dance or mime is a dramatic work (Copyright, Designs and Patents Act
  1988).
- **Fixation:** the work must be recorded (video, notation, detailed written
  description). Unrecorded choreography is hard or impossible to protect.
- The music and the choreography are separate works with separate owners.

## 2. Ownership
The choreographer usually owns it unless made as an employee in the course of employment
(US work made for hire) or assigned by contract. Commissions should say who owns it.
Moral rights (credit, integrity) exist in the UK and many other countries; in the US
they are limited.

## 3. Register and document
- In the US, registration with the Copyright Office is required before suing for
  infringement of a US work and makes statutory damages available; deposit a video or
  notation.
- Elsewhere there is usually no registry: keep dated video, scores and records of who
  made what.

## 4. Licensing performances
A licence states: the work, the licensee, venues and dates, number of performances,
territory, fee or royalty, who stages it (an approved stager), casting approval,
changes allowed, credit wording, recording rights, and term. Trusts and foundations
license many major choreographers' works on this model (see staging-licensed-works).

## Gotchas
- No recording of the work: nothing to prove.
- Commissioning contract silent on ownership.
- Licensing the choreography but forgetting the music rights (see
  music-rights-and-licensing-for-dance).

## Verify
- [ ] Work fixed in video and/or notation, dated.
- [ ] Ownership stated in any commission or employment contract.
- [ ] Licences list venues, dates, fee, stager, changes and credit.
