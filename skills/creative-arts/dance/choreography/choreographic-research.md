# Choreographic research

**Goal:** ground a new work in real research so the movement has depth and the concept holds
up - without letting research replace making.

## Method
1. **Frame the question:** "What does it feel like to wait?" is more productive than "a dance
   about waiting rooms".
2. **Gather sources:**
   - Reading: books, articles, poetry.
   - Archives: photographs, film, historical documents.
   - People: interviews with experts or communities (with consent).
   - Places: site visits, observing movement in context.
   - Embodied: try the activity itself (learn a craft, a sport, a ritual - respectfully).
3. **Translate into tasks:** for each finding, write 1-2 movement tasks (see choreographic
   tasks). Research that never becomes movement is a reading list.
4. **Sketchbook:** keep notes, images, task results, video stills in one place (physical or
   digital) - it becomes the source for programme notes and funding reports.
5. **Studio R&D:** test tasks with dancers; keep what works.

## Ethics
- Cultural material: consult and credit source communities; avoid appropriation.
- Personal stories: consent for use, the right to withdraw.

## Verify
- [ ] Research question written.
- [ ] Sources logged with references.
- [ ] Each key finding linked to at least one movement task.
