# Music visualisation and counterpoint

**Goal:** decide deliberately how the dance relates to its music, rather than defaulting to
dancing on every beat.

## Relationships
| Approach | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Visualisation** | dance mirrors the music's structure - melody lines, instruments, phrasing (Balanchine, Mark Morris) |
| **Counterpoint** | dance has its own rhythm that interlocks with the music (e.g. dancers in 3 against music in 4) |
| **Contrast** | dance quality opposes the music (slow movement over fast music) |
| **Independence** | dance and music share duration only (Cunningham/Cage) |
| **Mickey-mousing** | literally hitting every accent - usually to avoid except for comedy |

## Visualisation technique
1. Study the score: identify voices (melody, bass, inner parts) and their entrances.
2. Assign voices to dancers or groups (e.g. a canon in the music becomes a canon in the dance).
3. Match phrasing and dynamics, not every note.

## Counterpoint technique
1. Create a dance phrase with its own count structure (e.g. 5-count phrase over 8-count music).
2. Let it cycle until it realigns; place the realignment at a structural moment.
3. Or let one group follow the music and another counterpoint it.

## Verify
- [ ] Music-dance relationship chosen for each section and written down.
- [ ] Visualised sections map identifiable musical lines.
- [ ] No unintended mickey-mousing.
