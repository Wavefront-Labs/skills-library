# Making a duet

**Goal:** a duet where the relationship between two dancers is the subject, readable and
developing.

## Define the relationship
- Who are they to each other (lovers, rivals, mirror images, strangers)? Even abstract duets
  have a relationship of energy.

## Tools
| Relationship type | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Spatial** | near/far, facing/away, side by side, one behind the other, one high one low |
| **Action** | unison, mirroring, canon, question-and-answer (call and response), complement, contrast |
| **Contact** | touch, weight sharing, lifts, counterbalance, leading/following |
| **Focus** | looking at each other, at the same point, never at each other |

## Method
1. Make each dancer's individual material (or a shared phrase).
2. Combine it using relationship tools; e.g. one does the phrase, the other answers.
3. Build contact sections with care: rehearse weight-sharing safely.
4. Develop the relationship through the duet - it should change.

## Gotchas
- Too much unison makes a duet two solos side by side.
- Contact needs rehearsal time and safety (see contemporary partnering).

## Verify
- [ ] Relationship described in one sentence.
- [ ] At least three relationship tools used.
- [ ] Relationship changes from start to end.
