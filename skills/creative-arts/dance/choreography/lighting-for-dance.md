# Lighting for dance

**Goal:** work effectively with a lighting designer so the light reveals the dance, supports
the concept, and keeps dancers safe.

## Why dance lighting differs
- **Side light** is the backbone: lanterns on vertical poles (booms) in each wing, at
  **shin** (low, "shin busters"), **mid** and **head** height, sculpt the body in 3D and leave
  the floor less lit.
- Front light flattens bodies; used sparingly for faces.
- Top/back light separates dancers from the background.
- The wings must stay clear for side light; set and masking are planned around booms.

## Briefing the designer
1. Share concept, references, music and video of full runs early.
2. Mark key moments and moods per section; name sections the same in all documents.
3. Indicate spatial needs: where are the important moments; special areas (a pool of light
   downstage left).

## Tech and plotting
- At plotting, the designer builds cues while dancers or walkers stand in positions.
- Cue placement: give cue points by music time or a visible movement; the stage manager calls them.
- Keep notes short and specific ("cue 14 fade slower, 8 counts").

## Safety
- Booms in the wings are a collision hazard: tape and mark them, glow tape on bases.
- Blackouts and haze: rehearse exits in the dark; agree on running lights in the wings.

## Verify
- [ ] Designer briefed with video and section list.
- [ ] Wings plan includes booms and safe exits.
- [ ] Cue list matches the choreography's section names.
