# Music rights and licensing for dance

**Goal:** use music legally in performances and videos. This is general guidance, not legal
advice; rules differ by country and by work.

## Key concepts
- **Two copyrights in a recording:** the composition (composer/publisher) and the sound
  recording (record label/performer).
- **Small (performing) rights:** non-dramatic public performance, licensed via collecting
  societies (PRS in UK, ASCAP/BMI/SESAC in US) - venues usually hold blanket licences.
- **Grand rights (dramatic rights):** music used in a dramatic or narrative stage work
  (ballet, story dance) may need direct permission from the publisher; collecting societies
  often exclude these. Many dance works fall into a grey area - ask.
- **Sync licence:** putting music onto video (screendance, online posts) requires permission from
  the publisher (composition) and the label (master recording).
- **Public domain:** compositions can be out of copyright (e.g. composer died over 70 years ago
  in UK/EU), but a specific recording usually still has rights.

## Process
1. List every track with composer, publisher, performer and label.
2. Check the venue's licence covers performing rights; ask the society about grand rights.
3. For dramatic works or edits/remixes, contact the publisher for permission and a fee.
4. For film/online: get sync and master licences, or use commissioned or licensed library music.
5. Keep written permissions in the production file.

## Verify
- [ ] Music cue sheet with rights holders.
- [ ] Written permissions where needed (grand rights, sync, master).
- [ ] Venue licence confirmed.
