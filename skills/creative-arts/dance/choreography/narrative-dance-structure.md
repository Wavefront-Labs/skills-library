# Narrative dance structure

**Goal:** tell a story in dance that an audience can follow without program notes, while
still being dance rather than mime.

## Method
1. **Scenario:** write the story scene by scene (one paragraph per scene) - who, where, what
   happens, what changes.
2. **Arc:** set-up, inciting incident, rising action, climax, resolution. Mark turning points.
3. **Characters:** define each one's movement vocabulary - weight, tempo, preferred level,
   gestures - so they are recognisable when they enter.
4. **Relationships:** use duets and spatial relationships to show them (closeness, avoidance,
   lifting, mirroring).
5. **Clarity tools:** costumes, props and lighting carry information; a motif can represent an
   idea (e.g. a gesture for "home").
6. **Pacing:** alternate narrative scenes with "divertissement" or ensemble dances that
   express mood, giving the audience breathing space.

## Gotchas
- Complex plots don't translate; simplify to the core relationships.
- Avoid miming every action; abstract gestures (see abstracting-from-gesture).
- Test with a viewer who hasn't read the scenario: what did they understand?

## Verify
- [ ] Written scenario with an arc and turning points.
- [ ] Each character has a distinct movement vocabulary.
- [ ] A naive viewer can retell the main story.
