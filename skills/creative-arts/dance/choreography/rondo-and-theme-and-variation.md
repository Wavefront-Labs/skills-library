# Rondo and theme and variations

**Goal:** two more complex forms for longer dances, giving coherence through recurrence or
transformation.

## Rondo (ABACADA)
- A recurring section (A, the "chorus") alternates with contrasting episodes (B, C, D).
- A grounds the audience; the episodes provide variety.
- A can return exactly or slightly varied each time; keep it short so it doesn't tire.
- Good for group works with soloists in the episodes, or community pieces.

## Theme and variations
- A theme (a phrase or section) is presented, then transformed in successive variations,
  each changing elements: tempo, dynamics, dancers, space, body part, music.
- Variations stay recognisably linked to the theme; the order should build (e.g. growing
  complexity or intensity).
- Classical example: Balanchine's *Theme and Variations*; musical models abound.

## Other forms to consider
- **Episodic:** a series of linked scenes, common in narrative works.
- **Narrative:** follows a story arc (see narrative dance structure).
- **Collage:** unrelated sections juxtaposed for effect.

## Method
1. Make A (or the theme) strong and distinctive.
2. List variation/episode ideas; order them for an arc.
3. Check proportions: the return/theme mustn't dominate or disappear.

## Verify
- [ ] Theme/chorus recognisable each time it appears.
- [ ] Episodes/variations each have a distinct identity.
- [ ] Overall order builds to a climax.
