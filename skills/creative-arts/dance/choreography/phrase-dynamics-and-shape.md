# Phrase dynamics and shape

**Goal:** make a phrase feel like a sentence: it starts, builds, peaks and resolves, with
dynamic contrast that keeps the audience's attention.

## Phrase shapes (Blom and Chaplin)
| Shape | Where the accent sits |
|---|---|
| **Impulsive** | accent at the start, then dissipates |
| **Impactive** | builds to an accent at the end |
| **Swing** | accent in the middle (drop and rebound) |
| **Even/sustained** | no strong accent |
| **Explosive/vibratory** | sudden bursts or shaking |

## Method
1. Write the phrase as a line on paper: where is the peak?
2. **Vary effort:** use Laban's factors - time (sudden/sustained), weight (strong/light),
   space (direct/indirect), flow (bound/free) - to contrast moments.
3. **Breath:** where does the phrase breathe? Place pauses or suspensions.
4. **Climax:** one main peak per phrase; secondary accents support it.
5. **Resolution:** how does it end - a stop, a dissolve, a transition into the next?

## Tips
- Humphrey: a phrase is "a sequence with a shape"; monotone dynamics are the main cause of
  boring dances.
- Rehearse the phrase in silence to check it has its own rhythm.
- Contrast between consecutive phrases matters as much as within one.

## Verify
- [ ] Phrase has a clear climax and resolution.
- [ ] At least two effort contrasts.
- [ ] Phrase shape can be drawn and matches what is seen.
