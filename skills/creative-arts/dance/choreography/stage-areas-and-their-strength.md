# Stage areas and their strength

**Goal:** place movement where it has the intended impact - the same phrase reads differently
downstage centre and upstage left.

## The nine areas (proscenium)
Upstage right, upstage centre, upstage left; centre right, centre, centre left; downstage
right, downstage centre, downstage left. Right and left are from the performer's view
facing the audience.

## Area characteristics (Humphrey and common practice)
| Area | Typical effect |
|---|---|
| Downstage centre | strongest, most intimate - climaxes, direct address |
| Centre | strong, balanced, formal |
| Upstage centre | regal, remote, ceremonial |
| Downstage corners | intimate, confessional, informal |
| Upstage corners | distant, weak, mysterious - good for entrances/exits |
| Diagonal (upstage corner to downstage corner) | the strongest path - travelling, energy |

## Using it
1. Plan where key moments happen; save downstage centre for important ones.
2. Use upstage areas for background action or distance.
3. Travel on diagonals for drive; along the front line (horizontal) for presentation.
4. Check sightlines: corners and extreme downstage may be cut off in some venues.

## Gotchas
- In thrust or in-the-round stages, these rules change; there is no single "front".
- Overuse of centre makes a work static.

## Verify
- [ ] Floor plan with key moments placed by area.
- [ ] Climax located in a strong area.
- [ ] Sightlines checked in the actual venue.
