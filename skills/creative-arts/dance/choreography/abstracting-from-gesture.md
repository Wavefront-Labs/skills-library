# Abstracting from gesture

**Goal:** take a literal, recognisable gesture (waving, wiping a table, praying) and transform
it into dance movement that keeps its essence but loses its literalness.

## Method
1. **Choose a gesture** related to the work's theme. Perform it literally a few times.
2. **Find the essence:** what's the core shape, rhythm or dynamic? (wiping = sweeping arc,
   pressure, repetition).
3. **Transform** with one or more operations:
   - **Size:** enlarge to the whole body or shrink to a finger.
   - **Body part:** do it with the hip, the head, the foot.
   - **Tempo/rhythm:** slow motion, staccato, accelerating.
   - **Level and plane:** on the floor, in the air, in a different plane.
   - **Repetition and fragmentation:** repeat part of it, cut it up.
   - **Quality:** change effort (light to strong, direct to indirect).
4. **Combine** transformed versions into a phrase.

## Tips
- Keep a trace of the original - abstraction that loses all connection to source loses meaning.
- Humphrey warns against overly literal gesture; aim for evocation.
- Test with an outside eye: is it "dance" now, and does it still evoke the source?

## Verify
- [ ] Original gesture and its essence written down.
- [ ] At least three transformations created.
- [ ] Phrase reads as dance but keeps a recognisable trace.
