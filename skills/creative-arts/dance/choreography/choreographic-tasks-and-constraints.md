# Choreographic tasks and constraints

**Goal:** give dancers (or yourself) tasks that generate material specific to the work, quickly
and in a usable form.

## Anatomy of a good task
| Part | Example |
|---|---|
| **Constraint** | "write your name in space with your elbow, then your knee" |
| **Time limit** | "10 minutes" |
| **Output** | "a phrase of about 16 counts you can repeat" |
| **Showing** | "show in pairs, then I'll choose" |

## Task examples
- **Body-part writing:** spell a word with different body parts in different planes.
- **Spatial grid:** move only along a 3x3 floor grid; each square has a rule.
- **Gesture source:** take three everyday gestures and enlarge, slow or fragment them.
- **Word list:** five verbs (melt, slice, gather, collapse, flick) - make one movement each,
  join them.
- **Image:** "you are being pulled through a narrow gap".
- **Copy and change:** learn a partner's phrase, change its level and tempo.

## Tips
- Tie every task to the work's concept; generic tasks give generic material.
- One constraint usually; add a second only when the first is mastered.
- Watch for the dancers who solve tasks unusually - follow those directions.

## Verify
- [ ] Each task written with constraint, time, output and showing.
- [ ] Material from tasks is fixed and filmed.
- [ ] Material visibly connects to the concept of the piece.
