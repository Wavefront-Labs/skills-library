# Counting and phrasing music

**Goal:** understand a piece of music's structure well enough to set choreography on it and
communicate counts to dancers.

## Method
1. **Find the pulse:** tap along. Decide whether dancers will count the beat or half-time.
2. **Find the metre:** where does the strong beat fall? 4/4 (1-2-3-4), 3/4 (1-2-3, waltz),
   6/8 (two groups of three, felt as 2), 5/4 or 7/8 (count as 3+2, 2+2+3, etc.).
3. **Group into dance counts:** in 4/4 popular music dancers count "8s" (two bars). In 3/4,
   count in bars of 3 or "1-2-3, 2-2-3...".
4. **Find the musical phrases:** usually 4 or 8 bars; mark verse, chorus, bridge, intro,
   breaks, and irregular extensions (an extra bar or a 2-count tag).
5. **Write a music map:** timestamps, sections, number of 8s per section, notable accents
   ("hit on 5 of 8-3").
6. **Upbeats:** note pickups ("and" or "a" before 1) - many movements start on the "and".

## Tips
- Musicians count bars; dancers count 8s. Translate when talking to musicians.
- Live tempo varies; agree on cues as well as counts.
- Odd metres: count them consistently and drill the grouping with clapping.

## Verify
- [ ] Music map with timestamps, sections and 8-count totals.
- [ ] Odd bars and tags identified.
- [ ] Dancers can clap the counts of each section.
