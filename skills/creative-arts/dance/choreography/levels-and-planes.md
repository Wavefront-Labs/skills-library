# Levels and planes

**Goal:** vary where in vertical space movement happens and in which plane it travels, to
avoid monotony and to build relationships between dancers.

## Levels
| Level | Content |
|---|---|
| Low | floor work, rolls, kneeling, crawling |
| Medium | standing with bent knees, most gestures, pliés |
| High | relevé, extensions, reaches upward |
| Elevation | jumps, lifts |

## Planes (Laban)
| Plane | Image | Movement |
|---|---|---|
| **Frontal (vertical)** | door | side to side, up and down - cartwheel shapes; presentational |
| **Sagittal** | wheel | forward and back, up and down - advancing/retreating, rolls |
| **Horizontal** | table | side to side, forward and back - turning, sweeping, encircling |

## Using them
1. Map the levels of each section on a timeline; make sure the dance travels through levels.
2. Group contrast: one dancer high, others low creates focus and hierarchy.
3. Change the plane of a motif to transform it (a frontal arm circle becomes a horizontal sweep).
4. Use level changes at climaxes (sudden drop to the floor, a lift).

## Verify
- [ ] Each section uses at least two levels.
- [ ] Motif presented in more than one plane.
- [ ] Group sections use level contrast for focus.
