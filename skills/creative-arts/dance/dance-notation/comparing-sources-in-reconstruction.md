# Comparing sources in reconstruction

**Goal:** most older dances survive in several partial, conflicting sources. A
reconstructor compares them, decides which to follow at each point, and documents the
decision so others can see what is evidence and what is choice.

> **Status: draft.** Based on reconstruction practice discussed in Jordan (ed.),
> Preservation Politics, and archive practice.

## 1. List the sources
For each: type (score, film, notes, photographs, reviews, music score, oral account),
date, who made it, which cast or version, how complete, where held. Note provenance.

## 2. Rate reliability
- **Primary and close to creation:** the choreographer's notes, a score notated during
  creation, film of the premiere cast.
- **Later or second-hand:** later revivals, memories decades later, reviews.
- Every source has a bias: a film may show a damaged cut; a dancer remembers their own
  role best; a score may record one revival.

## 3. Compare section by section
Make a table for each section: what each source shows, agreements, differences. Where
they agree, follow them. Where they differ, decide by reliability, by fit with the music
and with the choreographer's known style, and by consulting surviving dancers or the
trust.

## 4. Fill gaps honestly
Where nothing survives, decide whether to leave a gap, reconstruct in the style, or
commission a new section, and say so. Never present an invention as original.

## 5. Document
Write a reconstruction report: sources used, decisions with reasons, and gaps. Include it
in the programme note in short form and in the archive in full.

## Gotchas
- Treating the most watchable film as the truth.
- One former dancer's memory overriding everything.
- Undocumented choices that become "the original" in the next generation.

## Verify
- [ ] Sources listed with date, type and provenance.
- [ ] Comparison table for each section.
- [ ] Each divergence decided with a reason.
- [ ] Reconstruction report written.
