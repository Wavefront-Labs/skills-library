# Benesh travel, direction and rhythm

**Goal:** add space and time to Benesh positions so a sequence can be reconstructed with its
travel, facings and timing.

## Below the stave: space
- **Direction faced:** signs indicate which way the dancer faces relative to the stage
  (front, diagonals, sides, back).
- **Location:** stage position signs place the dancer on the stage.
- **Travel:** signs show the path and direction of travel between positions (straight lines,
  curves, circling).
- **Turns:** turn signs show direction (clockwise/anticlockwise) and amount.

## Above the stave: time
- **Rhythm signs** above the stave indicate timing of frames relative to beats; bar lines
  align with the music's bars.
- Movement lines (curves between frames) show the path of a limb between positions.

## Group work
- Separate staves or a group plan show formations and relative positions.

## Practice
1. Notate a simple travelling sequence (e.g. glissade-assemblé to the side) with direction and
   travel signs.
2. Add timing aligned to 2/4 music.
3. Reconstruct a short phrase written by someone else.

## Gotchas
- Benesh is economical: what is not written is assumed unchanged. Missing a direction sign
  changes the whole facing.
- Formal training (RAD Benesh Institute) is needed for professional scores.

## Verify
- [ ] Short phrase notated with facing, travel and timing.
- [ ] A second reader reconstructs it correctly.
- [ ] Bars align with the music.
