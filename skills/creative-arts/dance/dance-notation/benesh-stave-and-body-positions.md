# Benesh stave and body positions

**Goal:** read and write body positions on the Benesh stave - the basis of Benesh Movement
Notation (BMN), the system widely used by ballet companies' choreologists.

## The stave
- Five horizontal lines, read left to right like a music stave, often written beneath the
  music score.
- The lines correspond (top to bottom) to the **top of the head, shoulders, waist, knees and
  floor (feet)**.
- Each **frame** (a segment of the stave) shows a moment, as if the dancer is seen **from
  behind** - so the dancer's right is on the reader's right.

## Plotting positions
- Hands and feet are marked as points in the frame at their height and side.
- **Three signs** show depth relative to the body:
  - a horizontal dash: level with the body (in the body's plane),
  - a vertical stroke: in front of the body,
  - a dot: behind the body.
- Lines connecting points show limb positions (e.g. bent knee marked with a cross at the knee).

## Practice
1. Notate five positions of the feet with arms in bras bas.
2. Notate arabesque and attitude (note depth signs for the back leg).
3. Read back positions and perform them.

## Gotchas
- "Seen from behind" is the most common reading error for new students.
- Full proficiency requires a course (RAD Benesh Institute certificates); use this as orientation.

## Verify
- [ ] Student identifies what each stave line represents.
- [ ] Correctly uses the three depth signs.
- [ ] Reads and performs ten static positions accurately.
