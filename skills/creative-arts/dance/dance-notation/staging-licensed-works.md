# Staging licensed works (trusts and foundations)

**Goal:** many major choreographers' works are licensed through trusts or foundations (for
example The George Balanchine Trust, the Martha Graham Dance Company and Center, the Limón
Foundation, the Merce Cunningham Trust). They control who performs the works and how, to
keep them as the choreographer made them.

> **Status: draft.** General process; each trust has its own application, fees and rules.
> Read their current guidelines.

## 1. Apply early
Contact the trust or licensing office well ahead - often a year or more for major
works. Expect to provide: company profile, dancers' level, venue, dates, number of
performances, and sometimes video of the company.

## 2. Meet the conditions
Typical conditions in a licence:
- **Stager / repetiteur** assigned or approved by the trust; the company pays fee,
  travel and accommodation.
- **Rehearsal time** minimums with the stager.
- **Casting approval** by the stager or trust.
- **Production elements:** approved costumes, set and lighting designs (sometimes
  rented from the trust), specified music and orchestration, stage size and floor.
- **Credit** wording and copyright notices in programmes and publicity.
- **Recording** usually prohibited or tightly limited.
- **Final approval** before the first performance.

## 3. Working with the repetiteur
- Prepare the dancers in the style beforehand (classes in the technique).
- Provide the rehearsal schedule and studios the licence requires.
- Do not change choreography, music or design without written approval.

## 4. Music
The music is licensed separately (publisher or rights society) unless included.

## Gotchas
- Announcing a season before the licence is signed.
- Cuts or tempo changes made locally without approval.
- Underestimating stager costs and rehearsal weeks.

## Verify
- [ ] Licence signed before announcement.
- [ ] Stager, rehearsal time and casting approved.
- [ ] Design, music and credits as specified.
- [ ] Final approval obtained.
