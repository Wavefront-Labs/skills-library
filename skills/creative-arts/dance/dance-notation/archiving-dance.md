# Archiving dance

**Goal:** a dance disappears unless its traces are kept. Archiving gathers and describes
the materials so a stager, scholar or the company itself can find and use them decades
later.

> **Status: draft.** Summarises dance archive guidance (Dance Heritage Coalition, NYPL
> Jerome Robbins Dance Division) and general archival practice.

## 1. What to keep
For each work: notation scores, record video and rehearsal video, music scores and
recordings, the production bible, spacing charts, costume and set designs, lighting plots
and cue sheets, programmes, reviews, photos, the choreographer's notes, contracts and
rights documents.

## 2. Describe it (metadata)
Use a consistent scheme (Dublin Core fields are a simple start; repositories may use
their own). For each item at least:
- Title of the work and item; creator(s) and roles.
- Date (premiere, recording date).
- Format and extent (video, 1h 12m; score, 84 pages).
- Cast and version; venue.
- Rights and access restrictions.
- Identifier and location.
Keep a finding aid or catalogue listing all items for the work.

## 3. Store it
- Paper: acid-free folders and boxes, cool, dry, stable. Remove metal clips.
- Video and audio: migrate old tapes before they fail; keep digital masters and access
  copies; at least two copies in separate places; check integrity (checksums) regularly.
- Costumes: padded hangers or flat storage, no plastic dry-cleaning bags.

## 4. Place it
For long-term access, deposit with a repository (national library, dance division,
university archive) under a deposit agreement covering access and rights. Keep copies
for company use.

## Gotchas
- Video on a personal hard drive only.
- No rights information: archive material nobody can use.
- Materials sorted by format with no link to the work they belong to.

## Verify
- [ ] Materials for each work listed in a finding aid.
- [ ] Each item has the core metadata fields.
- [ ] Digital masters in two places with checksums.
- [ ] Deposit agreement in place or planned.
