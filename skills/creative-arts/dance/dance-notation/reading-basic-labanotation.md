# Reading basic Labanotation

**Goal:** be able to read a simple Labanotation passage — steps, arm gestures and
timing — well enough to reconstruct it in the studio or to check a score against a
video, and know where Labanotation fits beside Benesh and video documentation.

> **Status: draft.** The core conventions below (staff, columns, direction and level
> symbols, duration by length) are stable; the full system is vast and has detailed
> rules this page doesn't cover. Learn from a certified teacher or the textbook for real
> reconstruction work.

## 1. The staff
- A **vertical three-line staff**, read **from bottom to top** (time flows upward).
- The **centre line** divides the body's left (left of the line) from right.
- Horizontal **bar lines** cross the staff, matching musical bars; short **tick marks**
  show beats. A **double line** at the bottom separates the **starting position** (below)
  from the movement (above).

## 2. Columns: which body part
From the centre outward, each side (mirror image):
1. **Support column** (just beside the centre line) — what carries weight: steps,
   transfers of weight, jumps (a gap in both support columns = no support = in the air).
2. **Leg gesture column** — leg movements that don't take weight.
3. **Body/torso column** — torso movements.
4. **Arm column** — arm gestures.
Further out: the head (outside the staff on the right, with a pre-sign), and extra columns
added as needed for hands, etc., labelled with body-part symbols.

## 3. Direction symbols
The basic symbol is a **rectangle** modified in shape to show direction:
- **Place** (straight down / on the spot) — plain rectangle.
- **Forward / backward** — rectangle with a notch/point at the top or bottom
  (forward points up, toward the direction the body faces; backward points down).
- **Side** — a symbol pointing left or right (a triangle-like shape).
- **Diagonals** — rectangles with a corner cut off on the diagonal.
The **column** tells you which side's limb; the **shape** tells you where it goes.
*Unconfirmed detail:* the shape descriptions above are simplified from memory —
check the exact forward/backward and diagonal glyphs against the direction-symbol chart
in Hutchinson Guest before reading a real score.

## 4. Level by shading
- **Striped/hatched** = **high**.
- **Dot in the centre** = **middle** level.
- **Solid/blacked in** = **low**.
For steps, level relates to the supporting leg: low = bent knees (plié), middle = normal,
high = on the ball of the foot (relevé).

## 5. Length = duration
The **vertical length** of a symbol is how long the movement takes. A step symbol spanning
one beat is a one-count step; stretched over two beats it's slow. Symbols side by side
happen at the same time. This is Labanotation's strength: timing is read directly off
the page.

## 6. Reading practice
1. Start below the double line: find the starting position (feet together, arms down, etc.).
2. Read one bar at a time, bottom to top. Speak it: "count 1: step forward right, middle
   level; count 2: step forward left..."
3. Watch for **hold signs** (keep weight / position) and **turn signs** (pins, turn
   symbols) and **path signs** alongside the staff.
4. Walk it through slowly before adding music; check the floor plan diagrams beside the
   score for location.

## Labanotation vs Benesh vs video
- **Labanotation** (Laban, 1928): vertical staff, strong on direction and timing;
  widely used for modern dance and in US/German archives (Dance Notation Bureau).
- **Benesh Movement Notation** (1955): a horizontal five-line stave like music, a
  "picture" of the body from behind; standard in many ballet companies (Royal Ballet and
  others) and taught via the Benesh Institute.
- **Video:** fast, captures style and dynamics, but depends on one performance, one angle,
  and dancers' mistakes become "the choreography". Best practice is **both** — score plus
  video plus choreographer's notes.

## Gotchas
- Reading top-down by habit (it's bottom-up).
- Confusing the body's left/right with stage left/right — the staff is from the dancer's
  point of view.
- Ignoring symbol length and dancing everything at the same speed.

## Verify
- [ ] Can identify starting position, bars and beats on a score.
- [ ] Can name which column is supports vs legs vs arms.
- [ ] Can read direction and level of a step symbol.
- [ ] Can tell duration from symbol length and spot simultaneous movements.
- [ ] Can explain when to use Labanotation, Benesh or video.
