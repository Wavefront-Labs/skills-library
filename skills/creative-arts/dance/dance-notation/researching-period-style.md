# Researching period style

**Goal:** steps alone do not make a period piece; the way of dancing - posture, epaulement,
dynamics, use of the floor, relation to music - does. Researching style lets a
reconstruction look like its era rather than like today's dancers in costume.

> **Status: draft.** Performance-practice method used for baroque, Romantic ballet and
> early modern dance reconstructions.

## 1. Sources for style
- **Treatises and manuals:** for example Feuillet notation (1700) and Rameau's Le Maître
  à danser (1725) for baroque; Blasis and Bournonville sources for nineteenth-century
  ballet.
- **Notation and scores** of the period or of later notated revivals.
- **Iconography:** paintings, prints, photographs; read carefully - poses were staged.
- **Film:** early film of dancers of the era or their pupils (early twentieth century).
- **Music:** period performance tempos and phrasing.
- **Dress and footwear:** corsets, heeled shoes, long skirts and early pointe shoes all
  shaped what the body could do.
- **Living lineage:** teachers in a direct line (for example Bournonville style in Danish
  ballet, Graham or Limón technique via their companies).

## 2. Turn research into rules
Write a style sheet: posture and carriage, arm positions, head and focus, foot
articulation, jump and turn qualities, dynamics, relationship to music, partnering
conventions, gestures of manners.

## 3. Coach it
- Teach a period class (exercises in the style) before rehearsing the work.
- Rehearse in practice skirts, heeled shoes or period shoes early.
- Correct modern habits: today's extensions and turnout often exceed the period's.

## Gotchas
- Modern extensions and high legs in a Romantic ballet.
- Treating one painting as proof of how a step was done.
- Style researched but never taught as a class; dancers revert.

## Verify
- [ ] Treatises, iconography, film and music consulted.
- [ ] Written style sheet.
- [ ] Period class taught; rehearsals in period-appropriate shoes and dress.
