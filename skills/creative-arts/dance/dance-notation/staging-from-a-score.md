# Staging from a score

**Goal:** reconstruct and teach a dance from its notation score, so the work is
staged as recorded rather than as remembered by whoever last danced it. The Dance
Notation Bureau and the Benesh Institute train stagers who do this.

> **Status: draft.** Practice of Labanotation and Benesh stagers (Dance Notation Bureau,
> RAD Benesh Institute). Requires fluent score reading (see reading-basic-labanotation and
> reading-a-benesh-score).

## 1. Study the score
- Read the front matter: notator, date, cast and version recorded, glossary of special
  signs, floor plans, music references.
- Map the score to the music: bar numbers and counts, repeats, tempo markings.
- Read the floor plans first to understand the structure and traffic.

## 2. Learn it before rehearsal
- Read and dance each role yourself; mark difficult passages.
- Prepare a rehearsal plan by section and cast, and teaching notes (counts, key
  images, cues).
- Note ambiguities and how you will resolve them (another source, the trust, a
  former dancer).

## 3. Teach from the page
- Teach the structure and floor patterns, then the steps with counts, then the style
  details the score indicates (dynamics, effort signs, timing nuances).
- Keep the score open in the room; check details as you go.
- Use the score to settle disagreements about "how it used to be done".

## 4. Video as a check
Video shows one cast's performance, which may differ from the score. Use it for style and
energy, not to override the score without a reason recorded.

## 5. Record changes
Any change for this production (a venue, a cast limitation) is noted in the stager's
report and, where appropriate, reported to the rights holder.

## Gotchas
- Reading the score in the rehearsal room for the first time.
- Overriding the score with a video because it is quicker.
- Skipping the glossary and misreading special signs.

## Verify
- [ ] Score front matter, glossary and floor plans studied.
- [ ] All roles learned before rehearsal.
- [ ] Ambiguities listed with resolution.
- [ ] Stager's report notes changes.
