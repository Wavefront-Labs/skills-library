# Common dance injuries and first response

**Goal:** a rehearsal director or teacher who recognises likely injuries, responds correctly
in the room, and refers appropriately. Not a substitute for medical assessment.

## Most common sites
Lower limb (foot, ankle, knee, hip) and lower back dominate dance injury statistics.

| Injury | Typical signs |
|---|---|
| **Lateral ankle sprain** | rolled ankle on landing, swelling outside the ankle |
| **Achilles tendinopathy** | pain at the back of the heel, worse in the morning or with jumps |
| **FHL tendinopathy ("dancer's tendinitis")** | pain behind the inner ankle on pointe/relevé |
| **Stress fracture** (metatarsal, tibia) | gradually increasing localised bone pain, pain with hopping |
| **Patellofemoral pain** | pain around the kneecap in plié and jumps |
| **Hip impingement/snapping** | front-of-hip pain or clicking in extensions |
| **Low back pain (incl. spondylolysis)** | pain in arching, especially adolescents |

## First response (acute injury)
1. **Stop dancing** that dancer; don't "walk it off" or finish the run.
2. Assess safely; call medical help for suspected fracture, dislocation, head injury.
3. Soft tissue: modern guidance (e.g. PEACE and LOVE / POLICE) - protect, elevate, compress,
   optimal loading as advised by a clinician; ice may be used for pain relief.
4. Record the incident (time, mechanism, response).
5. Refer to a dance-experienced physiotherapist or doctor.

## Red flags - refer urgently
Inability to bear weight, deformity, numbness, night pain, persistent bone pain, pain that
increases over weeks.

## Verify
- [ ] Incident book and referral contacts available in every studio.
- [ ] First aider on site during rehearsals.
- [ ] Dancers encouraged to report early, without penalty.
