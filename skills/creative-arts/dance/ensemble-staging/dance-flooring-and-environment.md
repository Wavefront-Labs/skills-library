# Dance flooring and environment

**Goal:** dance on surfaces and in conditions that reduce injury risk, in the studio and on tour.

## Floors
- **Sprung (resilient) subfloor:** absorbs impact and returns energy; essential for jumping.
  Area-elastic or point-elastic systems; must be uniform (no dead spots).
- **Concrete or concrete-backed floors:** high impact load - limit jumps or bring a touring
  sprung floor.
- **Surface (vinyl/"Marley"):** consistent friction, slightly sticky; laid with seams running
  upstage-downstage (so dancers travel along, not across, seams), taped with vinyl floor tape.
- **Friction:** too slippery = falls; too sticky = knee/ankle torque in turns. Clean with
  manufacturer-recommended products; avoid polish. Rosin on Marley only sparingly if at all.

## Environment
- **Temperature:** cold studios increase injury risk; aim for a comfortable warm room (often
  cited ~20-24 C) and warm up longer when cold.
- **Ventilation and hydration:** water available, air flow.
- **Ceiling height** for lifts and jumps; mirrors and barres secure.

## Touring checks
1. Ask venues for floor specs in advance (sprung? surface?).
2. On arrival, walk and jump-test the floor; check seams and tape.
3. Clean the floor before rehearsal and show.

## Verify
- [ ] Floor type confirmed for each venue.
- [ ] Vinyl seams taped and running up-downstage.
- [ ] Studio temperature monitored.
