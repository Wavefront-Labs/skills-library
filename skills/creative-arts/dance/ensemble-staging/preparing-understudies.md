# Preparing understudies

**Goal:** when a dancer is off, someone ready goes on safely and the show looks the same.
This is the rehearsal director's or dance captain's side; the performer's side is in
swing-and-understudy-tracks.

> **Status: draft.** Practice across ballet and musical theatre; contracts may require
> understudy rehearsals and set pay for going on.

## 1. Assign covers early
Decide covers when casting (see casting-dancers): every principal role and ensemble
track has at least one named cover or swing. Record it in the casting grid.

## 2. Give them materials
- Track sheets and spacing charts for each role covered.
- Reference video from the front for that role.
- Notes of style and intention, partner names and lifts.

## 3. Teach and rehearse
- Teach the role, not just the steps: partner contact, entrances, quick changes.
- Hold **understudy rehearsals** regularly during the run (often weekly), with the
  partners where lifts are involved.
- Let covers watch from the wings and front; let them mark along at the back during
  rehearsals.

## 4. Before going on
- **Put-in rehearsal:** the cover runs the role on stage with the company, under work
  light, focusing on partnering, traffic and quick changes.
- Brief the company and stage management on who is on and any changes.
- Check costume fits.

## 5. After
Give the cover notes; record what needs work; thank them.

## Gotchas
- Covers who have never rehearsed with their partner going on in a pas de deux.
- Materials out of date.
- No put-in because "they know it".

## Verify
- [ ] Every role and track has a named cover.
- [ ] Track sheets, charts and video available to covers.
- [ ] Regular understudy rehearsals happen.
- [ ] Put-in held before going on.
