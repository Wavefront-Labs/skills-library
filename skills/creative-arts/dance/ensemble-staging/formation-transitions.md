# Formation transitions

**Goal:** audiences notice the transitions more than the formations. A good transition
arrives on the count, without collisions, with every dancer taking the shortest clear path,
and looks designed rather than like people walking to their next spot.

> **Status: draft.** Standard rehearsal-director and commercial choreographer practice.

## 1. Plot it on paper (or an app) first
- Draw formation A and formation B with stage numbers and depth (see spacing-charts).
- Assign each dancer an end spot that minimises total travel. A quick method: match
  dancers left-to-right in A to left-to-right in B, then check for crossings.
- Draw each path. Where two paths cross, either swap end spots, stagger timing, or make
  the crossing a feature (weave, pass right shoulder to right shoulder).
- Note the counts: start count, arrival count, and how many counts each dancer has.

## 2. Choose a transition style
- **Walk/travel on counts:** simple, effective for commercial and musical theatre.
- **Choreographed travel:** the transition is itself movement (a travelling phrase, a
  turn sequence) - best for concert dance.
- **Peel-off / ripple:** dancers leave in sequence (canon), revealing the next shape.
- **Hidden:** move during a blackout, behind a featured dancer, or behind scenery.
- **Explode and gather:** everyone scatters then lands in the new shape on one accent.

## 3. Traffic rules
- Agree passing sides (usually pass right shoulder to right shoulder, or upstage
  dancer passes behind).
- Whoever travels furthest leaves first or gets more counts.
- Keep lanes for entrances and exits clear; know which wing each dancer exits to.
- With props, costumes or partnering, add counts.

## 4. Rehearse it like choreography
- Walk it at half tempo with counts out loud, then at tempo.
- Run transition plus the eight counts before and after, not just the transition.
- Check from the front: the in-between moments should look intentional.
- Film it from the audience view and check arrivals land on the same count.

## Gotchas
- Two dancers aiming for the same spot because the chart was not updated.
- Everyone arriving early and waiting: looks like a mistake. Use all the counts.
- Running backwards into someone: assign who looks and who yields.

## Verify
- [ ] Every dancer has a path, start count and arrival count.
- [ ] No unplanned crossings; passing rules agreed.
- [ ] Transition arrives on the count in a video from front of house.
