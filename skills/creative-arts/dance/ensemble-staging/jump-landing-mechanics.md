# Jump landing mechanics

**Goal:** landings that absorb force through the whole leg and keep joints aligned, reducing
injury risk in ankles, knees and back.

## Good landing
1. **Roll through the foot:** toes, ball, then heel touches down.
2. **Heels down:** every landing, even in fast allegro - lifted heels overload Achilles and calf.
3. **Plié:** hips, knees and ankles flex together to absorb; depth matches jump height.
4. **Alignment:** knees track over the toes; no knee falling inward (valgus).
5. **Torso:** stacked over the base; no collapsing forward.
6. **Quiet:** loud landings signal poor absorption.

## Coaching
- Drill landings from small jumps before height: e.g. sautés freezing in the landing plié.
- Single-leg landings: hold the landing for 2 counts to check alignment.
- Film side and front views.
- Strength: calf raises, single-leg squats and plyometric progressions support landings.

## Managing volume
- Count jumps per session for heavy jump repertoire; spread across the day.
- Landings deteriorate with fatigue - put big jumps before the end of long rehearsals.
- Floor surface matters: avoid jumping on concrete or unsprung floors (see dance flooring).

## Verify
- [ ] Heels down and knees over toes on landing (video check).
- [ ] Landing holds stable for 2 counts on one leg.
- [ ] Jump volume planned for heavy repertoire.
