# Spacing calls on tour

**Goal:** in each new venue, the spacing call (a rehearsal on the stage before the first
show) puts the work into the new space quickly: dancers learn the new distances, the
lighting is checked against where they actually are, and dangerous moments are tested.

> **Status: draft.** Standard touring practice for dance and musical theatre; union
> contracts often limit how long a call can be on show days.

## 1. Before the call
- Tape stage numbers (centre 0 and outward) and depth marks; check wing edges are taped.
- Revised spacing charts in hand (see adapting-a-dance-to-a-new-venue).
- Agree the running order of the call with stage management and lighting.
- Dancers warmed up; the floor swept and checked.

## 2. Running the call
1. **Walk the stage:** dancers find their number, the wings, the edges, the pit.
2. **Section by section at marking pace:** count out loud, check formations and
   entrances; stop and fix.
3. **Lighting marks:** check special positions (spotlit solos, pools) with the lighting
   designer or relighter; move the dancer or refocus the light.
4. **Full-out the risky parts:** lifts, jumps, fast entrances, floor work, any trick, on
   the actual floor.
5. **Quick changes and props:** walk the route offstage.

## 3. Relighting on tour
A touring production may use a relighter who refocuses the plot for each venue. The
spacing call is where dancer marks and light marks are reconciled; agree which moves
when they disagree.

## 4. After the call
Announce all changes to the company before the show; update charts; dance captain
watches the first show from the front.

## Gotchas
- Too much time on the opening number and nothing left for the finale.
- Not testing lifts and jumps full out on a new floor.
- Changes not passed to swings.

## Verify
- [ ] Numbers and wing edges taped.
- [ ] Every section walked; risky moments full out.
- [ ] Lighting marks checked.
- [ ] Changes announced and charts updated.
