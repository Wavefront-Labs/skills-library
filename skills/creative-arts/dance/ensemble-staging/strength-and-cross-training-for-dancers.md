# Strength and cross-training for dancers

**Goal:** build the fitness that technique class alone doesn't develop - research shows dance
class is often insufficient for aerobic fitness and strength demands of performance.

## Components
| Component | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Strength** | squats, single-leg deadlifts, calf raises (slow, heavy enough), hip abductor/rotator work, upper body for partnering |
| **Power/plyometrics** | progressive jumps, hops, bounds - for elevation and landing control |
| **Aerobic** | cycling, swimming, interval training - stamina for long sections |
| **Core/stability** | Pilates, plank variations, anti-rotation work |
| **Mobility** | as in flexibility training |

## Principles
- Strength training does not make dancers "bulky" with typical programmes; it supports jumps,
  landings and injury resilience.
- 2-3 sessions per week, 20-45 minutes, progressive overload.
- Periodise: build in the off-season/early rehearsals, maintain during shows, taper before
  premieres.
- Single-leg work mirrors dance demands.

## Gotchas
- Avoid heavy new training in tech or performance weeks.
- Get a qualified strength coach or physio to design for injury history.

## Verify
- [ ] Weekly plan with strength, aerobic and plyometric sessions.
- [ ] Periodised around the schedule.
- [ ] Progress measured (e.g. calf raise count, jump height, recovery heart rate).
