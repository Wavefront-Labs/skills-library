# Working with hypermobile dancers

**Goal:** use the advantages of hypermobility (range, lines) while protecting dancers from
instability, pain and injury.

## Screening
- **Beighton score (0-9):** little fingers bending back beyond 90 degrees, thumbs to forearm,
  elbows and knees hyperextending beyond 10 degrees, palms flat to floor with straight knees.
  Higher scores indicate generalised hypermobility; many dancers score high.
- High score alone is not a diagnosis. Pain, fatigue, frequent subluxations or other symptoms
  suggest referral (hypermobility spectrum disorder or hEDS assessment).

## Training priorities
- **Strength and control in range**, especially at end range - not more stretching.
- **Proprioception:** balance work, eyes-closed exercises, slow controlled movement.
- **Joint alignment cues:** "straight not locked" knees and elbows (see controlling
  hyperextended knees), neutral spine.
- **Endurance:** hypermobile dancers often fatigue faster; build gradually.

## Rehearsal considerations
- Watch for "hanging" in joints at rest (locking knees, sinking into hips).
- Partnering: grips on hypermobile wrists/shoulders need care.
- Plan rest; fatigue increases instability.

## Red flags
Recurrent dislocations/subluxations, widespread pain, dizziness on standing, unusual bruising
- refer to a clinician experienced in hypermobility.

## Verify
- [ ] Dancers screened or self-identified, with individual plans.
- [ ] Strength/control programme in place for hypermobile dancers.
- [ ] Teachers cue "straight not locked" consistently.
