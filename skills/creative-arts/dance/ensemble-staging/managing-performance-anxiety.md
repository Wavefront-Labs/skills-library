# Managing performance anxiety

**Goal:** dancers who channel nerves into performance energy rather than being hindered by them.

## Understand it
- Some arousal improves performance; too much tightens muscles, shortens breath and narrows
  attention.
- Reframing "I'm anxious" as "I'm excited/ready" can shift its effect.

## Tools
1. **Pre-performance routine:** the same warm-up, make-up, mental rehearsal and timing every
   show - routine reduces uncertainty.
2. **Breathing:** slow exhale-focused breathing (e.g. 4 in, 6-8 out) for a few minutes in the
   wings.
3. **Imagery:** mentally rehearse the piece with full sensory detail, including recovering from
   a mistake.
4. **Cue words:** short task-focused words for hard moments ("tall", "breathe", "push the floor").
5. **Focus on process:** attention on the movement and intention, not on the audience or outcome.
6. **Mistake plan:** agree how to recover from errors so they don't spiral.

## Rehearsal director's role
- Provide stable call times and clear information; uncertainty raises anxiety.
- Run full dress runs and invited audiences before opening.
- Normalise talking about nerves.

## Refer when
Anxiety causes avoidance, panic attacks, sleep problems or persists beyond performances -
refer to a performance psychologist or BAPAM-type service.

## Verify
- [ ] Each dancer has a pre-performance routine.
- [ ] Breathing and imagery practised in rehearsal, not first tried on opening night.
- [ ] Referral options known.
