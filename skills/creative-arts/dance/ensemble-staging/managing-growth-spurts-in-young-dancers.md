# Managing growth spurts in young dancers

**Goal:** keep adolescent dancers training safely through rapid growth, when injury risk rises
and technique can temporarily regress.

## What happens
- **Peak height velocity:** fastest growth, typically around 11-13 for girls, 13-15 for boys
  (wide individual variation).
- Bones lengthen faster than muscles and tendons adapt: temporary **loss of flexibility,
  coordination, balance and strength-to-height ratio**.
- Growth plates (apophyses) are vulnerable to traction injuries.

## Common growth-related conditions
- **Osgood-Schlatter:** pain and bump below the kneecap, worse with jumps and kneeling.
- **Sever's:** heel pain at the back of the heel in younger dancers.
- **Back pain** with arching, which needs medical assessment (spondylolysis risk).

## Practical management
1. **Track height** every 1-3 months; rapid change (e.g. several cm per term) flags a spurt.
2. **Modify load:** fewer jumps, less extreme range work, more technique and strength basics.
3. **Explain:** reassure dancers that clumsiness is temporary and not loss of talent.
4. **Avoid forcing flexibility** to regain lost range; it returns as tissues adapt.
5. **Refer** persistent pain to a clinician.
6. Delay starting pointe or demanding repertoire during a spurt.

## Verify
- [ ] Height tracked regularly for students in the age range.
- [ ] Load modification plan for dancers in a spurt.
- [ ] Students and parents informed about growth effects.
