# Cleaning unison and spacing

**Goal:** turn choreography that everyone "knows" into a section that reads as one
organism — same timing, same paths, same shapes, same energy, everyone in the right
place — and keep it that way through a run.

> **Status: draft.** Reflects widespread practice of rehearsal directors and dance
> captains; specific numbering systems differ between venues and companies (centre = 0
> with numbers outward is the common one).

## 1. Spacing first: give everyone an address
- **Stage numbers:** numbers along the downstage edge (and often on the back wall), usually
  **0 at centre**, then 2, 4, 6, 8… outward each side at ~60 cm–1 m intervals (venue
  dependent). Dancers say "I'm on 4 left" or "right of 2".
- **Depth:** wing numbers (wing 1 downstage to wing 4 upstage) or named lines ("first line,
  second line"), and quarter/half/three-quarter depth marks.
- **Spike marks:** small coloured tape marks for key positions (a colour per dancer or per
  number) — use sparingly; too many confuses everyone.
- Write a **spacing chart** per formation: name, number, depth, facing. Keep it updated
  digitally (spreadsheet or formation app) for swings and restaging.
- Check spacing in the actual venue: a studio is never exactly the stage width. Rescale
  from the centre outward.

## 2. Clean in this order
Work in short chunks (one eight or one phrase), always the same order — earlier layers
make later ones fixable:
1. **Timing.** Every movement hits on the same count and the same subdivision ("on the
   and of 3"). Clap or count aloud; agree **where the accent is** (the arm reaches full
   extension on 3, not starts on 3).
2. **Path / placement.** Which foot, which direction, how far the travel, where the
   formation lands. Feet and floor patterns before arms.
3. **Shape.** Arm heights, angles, head and focus, levels (how deep the plié, how high the
   kick). Use concrete references: "hand at eye level", "elbow in line with shoulder".
4. **Dynamics and quality.** Sharp vs sustained, attack and release, breath. Two dancers
   hitting the same shape with different energy still look out of unison.

## 3. Tools for cleaning
- **Mirror off sometimes:** dancers fixed on the mirror lose spatial sense and facings.
- **Video from the audience view**, then watch it together or send notes: frame by frame
  shows timing disagreements nobody can see live.
- **Pairs/small groups:** run the section two at a time; differences jump out.
- **Count-and-say:** dancers say the action words out loud with counts.
- **Lines and diagonals:** stand on the end of a line and sight down it; one person ahead
  or behind breaks it.
- **Notes:** give them specific and actionable ("Sam, count 5: right arm, palm down, finish
  at shoulder height") and record them for the next rehearsal.

## 4. Maintaining through a run
- Dance captain watches from front of house regularly and gives notes after shows.
- **Swings and understudies** learn track by track from the spacing chart and video,
  with a clean rehearsal before going on.
- Re-clean after any break, cast change or new venue — unison drifts within a couple of
  weeks.

## Gotchas
- Cleaning shape before timing: perfect arms at different moments still look messy.
- Correcting one dancer to match another who is also wrong — define the reference
  (choreographer's video, the dance captain) explicitly.
- Everyone watching the front person ("following") — results in a ripple. Dancers must own
  the counts.
- Spacing taped only in the studio; first stage call wastes an hour.

## Verify
- [ ] Each formation documented: dancer, number, depth, facing.
- [ ] Spacing checked and rescaled in the venue.
- [ ] Each section cleaned in order: timing → path → shape → dynamics.
- [ ] Video from front shows lines straight and accents together.
- [ ] Notes recorded; swings have chart and video.
