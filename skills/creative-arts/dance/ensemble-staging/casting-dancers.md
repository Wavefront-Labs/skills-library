# Casting dancers

**Goal:** match dancers to roles so the work looks as intended and the cast is safe and
covered. For restagings, a trust or the original choreographer may approve casting.

> **Status: draft.** Common company and producer practice; licensed repertory often
> requires the stager's or trust's approval of casting (see staging-licensed-works).

## 1. Define the role
For each role list: technique and style demands (pointe, partnering, floor work, tap,
specific style), physical demands (stamina, jump, lifts), musicality, acting, and any
partnership heights. Note what is essential and what can be coached.

## 2. Choose how to see dancers
- **Company class and rehearsal:** for in-house casting, watch class and a short teach of
  the actual material.
- **Audition:** a class, then a combination from the piece, then a featured section for
  callbacks (see musical-theatre-dance-calls for the dancer's side).
- **Partnering:** try partnerships together; heights and chemistry decide pas de deux.

## 3. Build the cast
- First cast plus at least one cover for each principal role; swings or understudies for
  ensemble tracks.
- Double or triple casting for long runs or demanding roles, with rotation planned.
- Consider workload across the programme: the same dancer in three heavy works on one
  night is an injury risk.
- Keep a casting grid: roles vs dancers, with first cast, second cast, cover.

## 4. Communicate
- Post casting at a set time; tell dancers in person when it is a big change.
- Give feedback to those not cast where possible; note what would get them there.
- Keep casting confidential until published.

## Gotchas
- Casting by type alone, ignoring who can actually do the steps.
- No covers for principal roles until someone is injured.
- Casting a licensed work without the trust's approval.

## Verify
- [ ] Role demands written for each part.
- [ ] Casting grid with first cast and covers.
- [ ] Workload checked across the programme.
- [ ] Approvals obtained where required; casting communicated.
