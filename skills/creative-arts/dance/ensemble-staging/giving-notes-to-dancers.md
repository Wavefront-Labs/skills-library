# Giving notes to dancers

**Goal:** a note is only useful if the dancer understands it and can change something next
time. Good notes are specific, few, timely and given with respect.

> **Status: draft.** Rehearsal-director, choreographer and dance-captain practice.

## 1. Make each note actionable
- **Where:** section, count or music cue ("bar 33, count 5").
- **What:** what happened ("your arm arrives late").
- **Target:** what it should be ("full extension on 5, palm down").
- If it is an image or quality note, add one concrete check ("reach through the
  fingertips to the exit sign").

## 2. Prioritise
1. Safety (lifts, collisions, unsafe landings).
2. Spacing and traffic that affect others.
3. Timing and big shapes.
4. Detail and style.
Give the top few; carry the rest. A dancer can fix three things, not thirty.

## 3. When and where
- **Group notes** to everyone together, at the start of the next call or straight after a
  run when people are still warm.
- **Individual notes** privately or quietly, never as a public dressing-down.
- During a creation process, interrupt less; during cleaning, interrupt more.
- After a show: brief and positive first; the main notes next day if the company prefers.

## 4. Tone
State what you saw and what you want. Name what is working too. Avoid notes about a
dancer's body shape; note the movement.

## 5. Follow up
Write notes down (see video-review-notes). At the next run, check the noted moments.
If a note keeps coming back, re-teach or find out why (physical limit, confusion,
choreography that does not work).

## Gotchas
- Vague notes ("more energy").
- Personal or body-shaming comments.
- Contradicting the choreographer's notes without agreeing them first.

## Verify
- [ ] Every note has where, what and target.
- [ ] Notes prioritised; safety first.
- [ ] Individual notes given privately.
- [ ] Notes checked at the next run.
