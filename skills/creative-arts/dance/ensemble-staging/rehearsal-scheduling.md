# Rehearsal scheduling

**Goal:** get the work learned and clean in the hours available, with every dancer used
well, no one called to sit for hours, and the rules on hours and breaks kept.

> **Status: draft.** Common practice; the hour and break limits come from your contract
> (Equity, AGMA, company agreement) and law. Check the current agreement.

## 1. Build the master plan
- List every section with cast, length and difficulty. Estimate hours to teach (often
  several hours per minute of complex choreography) and to clean.
- Work back from first tech: learn everything, then runs, then cleaning, then
  stage-specific work.
- Mark fixed dates: costume fittings, photo calls, press, musician rehearsals, venue access.

## 2. Use a cast grid
A table of sections against dancers shows who is needed when. Sequence the day so:
- Dancers in many sections are called continuously; those in one are called for that
  slot only.
- Partnering and lifts come when dancers are warm, not right after a long break or at the
  end of a long day.
- The most demanding sections are spread across the week.

## 3. Rules and bodies
- Keep the contract's limits: maximum rehearsal hours per day and week, required breaks
  (often a short break every hour or so and a meal break), turnaround between calls, and
  days off.
- Company class or warm-up before rehearsals.
- Watch total load: a dancer called for every heavy section every day is an injury risk.

## 4. Publish the daily call
The daily schedule (call sheet) goes out by the agreed deadline (often the evening before):
time, room, section, who is called, what to bring (shoes, kneepads), and contacts. Update
and announce changes; never move a call earlier without notice.

## 5. Track it
Note each day what was finished; adjust the plan. Keep a record of hours worked for
payroll and compliance.

## Gotchas
- Calling the full company all day for a section that needs four.
- Lifts scheduled cold, at 9 a.m. or after lunch.
- Schedule changes posted late.

## Verify
- [ ] Master plan works back from first tech.
- [ ] Cast grid used to sequence each day.
- [ ] Hours and breaks within the contract.
- [ ] Daily call published on time.
