# Adapting a dance to a new venue

**Goal:** every stage differs in width, depth, wing space, floor, rake and sightlines. Plan
the changes before you arrive so the spacing call fixes details rather than redesigning.

> **Status: draft.** Standard touring and production practice.

## 1. Read the venue's technical specification
Get the tech spec and a ground plan (drawn to scale). Note:
- Proscenium width, stage depth from setting line to back wall, wing widths each side.
- **Floor:** sprung or not, surface (marley/vinyl, wood), whether you must bring your own.
- **Rake** (slope) if any; raked stages change balance, turns and travel.
- Obstacles: pillars, fly lines, booms, orchestra pit position, stage-door distance.
- Crossover route and quick-change areas.

## 2. Compare with the home stage
Overlay the work's spacing chart on the new ground plan. Decide:
- **Rescale:** keep relationships, scale distances from centre. If narrower, reduce
  width of formations first, then cut the outside dancers' travel.
- **Depth:** shallow stage means fewer lines; combine back lines.
- **Entrances and exits:** reassign wings if one side has less space.
- **Set and props:** what fits and what is cut.

## 3. Floor and safety
- A dance needs a sprung floor; if the venue has none, bring a sprung floor system or
  reconsider jumps. Lay a dance floor if the surface is unsuitable.
- On a rake, warm up and mark on the stage first; lifts and turns need extra rehearsal.
- Check temperature and backstage warm-up space.

## 4. Agree changes before arrival
Send the revised spacing and requirements to the venue and the cast. Brief dancers on
changes at the last home rehearsal.

## Gotchas
- Assuming the spec is accurate; ask for a photo or a site visit.
- Discovering no sprung floor at get-in.
- Re-spacing during the show call because nobody read the ground plan.

## Verify
- [ ] Tech spec and ground plan read; floor and rake confirmed.
- [ ] Revised spacing drawn and shared.
- [ ] Floor safe for the choreography.
