# Dance warm-up

**Goal:** prepare dancers' bodies for rehearsal or performance by raising tissue temperature,
mobilising joints and activating the patterns about to be used.

## Structure (15-25 minutes)
1. **Pulse raiser (5-8 min):** continuous, low-impact whole-body movement - walking, jogging,
   gentle bouncing, travelling steps - until breathing and heart rate rise and dancers feel warm.
2. **Joint mobility (3-5 min):** spine rolls, hip circles, ankle and wrist circles, shoulder
   rolls - through comfortable range.
3. **Dynamic stretching (5 min):** controlled leg swings, lunges with reaches, pliés, arm
   swings. Long static holds (over ~30-60 seconds) right before explosive work can reduce power;
   keep static stretches short in the warm-up.
4. **Dance-specific activation (5-7 min):** rehearse the movement types of the session at lower
   intensity - jumps progressing in height, turns, floorwork entries.

## Timing
- Finish no more than ~15-20 minutes before the dancing starts; temperature drops quickly.
- Before a show: plan the warm-up to end near the half; keep warm with layers until on stage.
- Re-warm after long breaks (e.g. interval).

## Gotchas
- A ballet barre is a warm-up for ballet only; contemporary or commercial shows may need
  different activation.
- Cold venues and early calls need longer pulse raisers.

## Verify
- [ ] Dancers report feeling warm and mildly breathless after the pulse raiser.
- [ ] Session-specific movements rehearsed at low intensity.
- [ ] Timing ends close to the start of dancing.
