# Nutrition and energy availability for dancers

**Goal:** a rehearsal environment where dancers eat enough to train and perform, and where
under-fuelling is recognised and handled with care. Refer individuals to qualified dietitians
and doctors.

## Key concept: energy availability
Energy availability is the energy left for body functions after training. When it is too low
for long, **RED-S (Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport)** can develop: hormonal disruption,
menstrual irregularity, low bone density (stress fractures), reduced performance, poor
recovery, mood changes. Affects all genders.

## Warning signs
- Repeated stress fractures or slow-healing injuries.
- Missed or irregular periods (not "normal" for dancers).
- Fatigue, dizziness, frequent illness, mood changes, weight loss.
- Comments or behaviour around food restriction.

## Practical fuelling (for scheduling)
- Schedule real meal breaks on long days (not just 10-minute breaks).
- Carbohydrate is the main fuel for dance; snacks between calls (fruit, sandwiches, yogurt).
- Water available; electrolytes on hot or long days.
- Show days: main meal 3-4 hours before; light snack an hour before.

## Raising concerns
- Speak privately, focus on health and performance, not weight or appearance.
- Never comment on bodies or weight in rehearsals.
- Have referral routes (company physio, GP, specialist eating disorder services).

## Verify
- [ ] Rehearsal schedules include meal breaks on long days.
- [ ] Staff know RED-S warning signs and referral routes.
- [ ] No body/weight comments policy in place.
