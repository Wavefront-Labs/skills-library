# Grand jeté

**Goal:** a big travelling leap from one leg to the other, showing a split at the top and an
apparent suspension.

## Preparations (run-up)
- Common: glissade, chassé (or pas couru), or a small run; the final step is a deep demi-plié
  that loads the take-off leg.
- The last step is a strong, straight-line push forward and up, not a stamp down.

## Method
1. **Brush:** the front leg passes through the floor (glissé) and throws forward like a grand
   battement - straight leg, leading with the heel.
2. **Take-off:** push off the back leg through the whole foot; the back leg extends behind.
3. **Peak:** both legs reach the split at the top of the arc. Torso lifted, arms usually in
   arabesque line or 3rd, eyes up and forward.
4. **Landing:** onto the front leg, toe-ball-heel into fondu, the back leg held in arabesque
   then lowered or continued into the next step.

## Teaching points
- Height first, then distance: aim up, let the travel come from momentum.
- The split is achieved by both legs, not by throwing the front leg higher and dropping the back.
- Arms: lift with the jump; flailing destroys the line.

## Faults and fixes
| Fault | Fix |
|---|---|
| Bent front knee in the air | brush through the floor, lead with the heel |
| Back leg drops early | engage the back of the leg; arabesque height maintained |
| Heavy landing on a straight leg | land into a deep fondu, heel down |
| Looking at the floor | eyes up and out |

## Gotchas
- Land on sprung floors; limit repetitions when fatigued - most landing injuries happen late
  in class.
- Over-split "tilted" jetés are a stylistic choice; check the repertoire before teaching.

## Verify
- [ ] Split shape at the peak (film side-on).
- [ ] Soft, controlled landing on the front leg with heel down.
- [ ] Clean on both legs.
