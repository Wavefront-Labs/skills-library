# Structuring and teaching a ballet class

**Goal:** a class that warms the body progressively, builds technique logically towards jumps,
and ends with the dancers better than they began - safely.

## Typical order (approx. 90 minutes)
| Section | Exercises | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Barre | pliés, tendus, glissés, ronds de jambe à terre, fondus, frappés, ronds en l'air, adage/développés, grands battements, stretch | 35-45 min |
| Centre | port de bras, centre tendus, adage | 15 min |
| Turns | pirouettes, travelling turns | 10 min |
| Allegro | petit allegro, batterie, medium jumps, grand allegro | 15-20 min |
| End | reverence | 2 min |

Pointe or partnering follows class, not before it.

## Planning principles
- Each barre exercise prepares a centre step (glissé -> petit allegro; développé -> adage).
- Pick a focus for the week (e.g. épaulement, use of plié) and thread it through.
- Progression: simple to complex, slow to fast, small jumps before big.
- Balance left and right; finish exercises on both sides.

## Working with the pianist
- Give metre and tempo clearly: "a slow 3/4, 4 bars intro", or demonstrate with counts and
  a sung rhythm. Common: pliés slow 3/4 or 4/4, tendus 2/4, frappés sharp 2/4, adage slow
  3/4 or 4/4, petit allegro bright 2/4, grand allegro big waltz or march.
- State the introduction length and whether to repeat for the other side.
- Thank the pianist; they are part of the teaching.

## Corrections
- One correction at a time, positive and specific ("knee over the second toe") rather than
  vague ("better turnout").
- General corrections to the whole class, then individual.
- Hands-on corrections only with consent and clear explanation.

## Verify
- [ ] Class plan shows barre-to-centre links and a weekly focus.
- [ ] Time budget leaves at least 15 minutes for allegro.
- [ ] Pianist cues given with metre, tempo and intro length.
