# Grand jeté en tournant (tour jeté)

**Goal:** a turning leap where the legs pass (or scissor) in the air and the dancer lands
facing the opposite way in arabesque.

## Method
1. **Preparation:** usually a chassé or pas couru, then the travelling step onto the take-off
   leg in demi-plié, body turning to face the direction of travel's opposite as the step
   finishes (back to travel in many versions).
2. **Battement:** the free leg swings through 1st with a straight grand battement forward
   (in the direction of travel).
3. **Turn:** push off; the body makes a half turn in the air around the vertical axis while the
   take-off leg rises behind. The legs pass each other at the top (the "entrelacé" - interlaced).
4. **Landing:** on the battement leg, in fondu, the other leg in arabesque; arms usually
   finish in an arabesque line or 5th.

## Teaching points
- Keep the turn on the vertical axis: the body rotates, it does not lean to the side.
- The battement must go straight up and forward; a swinging battement to the side causes a
  sideways tilt.
- Learn it first as a walking half-turn with battement, then a small sauté, then full height.

## Faults and fixes
| Fault | Fix |
|---|---|
| Body tilts sideways (cartwheel look) | battement straight forward; axis vertical |
| Lands with bent back leg | arabesque leg straight and lifted at landing |
| Under-rotation | turn the shoulders and head with the battement |
| Hard landing | land through the foot into fondu |

## Verify
- [ ] Legs pass at the top of the jump.
- [ ] Lands facing the correct direction in a held arabesque.
- [ ] Repeated in a series without travelling off the line.
