# Battement frappé and petits battements

**Goal:** sharp, quick lower-leg action from a fixed thigh. Frappé builds the explosive
extension for jumps; petits battements build the speed for beats (batterie).

## Battement frappé
1. **Start:** working foot flexed or wrapped sur le cou-de-pied (RAD lower grades use a
   flexed foot touching the floor on the way out; Cecchetti and higher grades strike the
   floor with the ball of the foot, or go straight to the air).
2. **Strike:** the lower leg extends sharply to a fully pointed foot just off the floor (or
   brushing the floor) in front, 2nd or back. Accent out.
3. **Return:** the foot comes back quickly to cou-de-pied; the thigh stays still.
4. Doubles: the foot beats front-back (or back-front) at the ankle before extending.

## Petits battements sur le cou-de-pied
1. Working foot sur le cou-de-pied, thigh turned out and still.
2. The lower leg opens slightly and the foot changes from front to back of the ankle (and
   back) in quick even beats, accent usually in front or back as set.
3. Only the lower leg moves; keep it small and fast.

## Faults and fixes
| Fault | Fix |
|---|---|
| Thigh moving | reduce speed; hold the knee out to the side |
| Soft, unpointed extension in frappé | accent the stretch; full point at the end |
| Beats too wide | smaller action, rhythm first |
| Supporting leg bouncing | supporting leg pulled up and steady |

## Gotchas
- Different schools strike the floor differently: follow the syllabus you are examined in.
- Petits battements are the raw material for entrechats and brisés; tidy ankles here mean
  readable beats in the air.

## Verify
- [ ] Accent clearly out (frappé) at the set rhythm.
- [ ] Thigh still in both exercises.
- [ ] Even, quick beats with the foot in place at the ankle.
