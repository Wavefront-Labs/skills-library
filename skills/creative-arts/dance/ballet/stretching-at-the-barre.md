# Stretching at the barre

**Goal:** use the barre stretch (usually after grand battements or at the end of class) to
gain range safely, without destabilising joints or stretching cold tissue.

## When
- After the body is warm - end of barre or end of class. IADMS guidance: long static stretches
  immediately before explosive work can temporarily reduce power; keep pre-performance
  stretching dynamic and short.
- Static holds of roughly 30 seconds per position, repeated 2-3 times, build range over weeks.

## Leg on the barre (front)
1. Heel on the barre at a height where the supporting leg stays straight and the pelvis square.
2. Both legs rotated (or both parallel - be consistent).
3. Fold forward over the leg from the hips with a long spine; then side bend away and towards.
4. Plié on the supporting leg for a deeper hamstring stretch only if the pelvis stays square.

## Side and back
- **Side:** leg à la seconde on the barre, hips facing front; side bend over the leg with the
  arm in 5th.
- **Back:** face away from the barre, the foot or knee on the barre behind (beginners use a lower
  surface); pelvis stays square, the stretch goes into the front of the hip of the back leg;
  cambré back only within control.

## Traps
| Trap | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Over-splits between chairs/blocks | stresses hip capsule and ligaments, little benefit |
| Bouncing into stretches | ballistic loading without control |
| Forcing turnout in frog/butterfly with weight on top | knee and hip risk, especially growing dancers |
| Stretching a hypermobile joint further | adds range without control; strengthen instead |
| Partner pushing a stretch | dancer loses control of the endpoint |

## Verify
- [ ] Stretching happens warm, not first thing.
- [ ] Supporting leg straight and pelvis square in each position.
- [ ] Holds are steady, no bouncing, breathing continues.
