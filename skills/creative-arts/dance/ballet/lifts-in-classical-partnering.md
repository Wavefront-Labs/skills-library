# Lifts in classical partnering

**Goal:** lifts in which the lifted dancer jumps and holds her shape, and the partner lifts
with his legs and keeps her close to his centre - so lifts look effortless and nobody is hurt.

## Shared principles
- **Both plié together; she jumps, he lifts.** A lift on a dead weight is twice the work.
- **Close is light:** the farther her centre is from his, the heavier she becomes.
- **She holds her shape:** a firm torso and set position make her a solid object; a soft
  body folds and becomes impossible to control.
- Rehearse with a spotter or a third person for new lifts.

## Waist lift (simple jump lift)
1. He stands behind, hands on her waist above the hip bones, thumbs on her back.
2. Count "and" plié together, she jumps from both feet; he straightens his legs and extends his
   arms to carry her up and slightly forward.
3. Lower her with control; she lands through her feet into demi-plié.

## Overhead press
- From a waist lift, he bends his arms and presses her straight overhead, arms locked, her
  centre directly over his. Her body stays rigid in the set shape (e.g. arabesque).

## Shoulder sit
- She jumps; he lifts her to sit on his shoulder, his hand securing her thigh or waist; her
  free leg in the set position.

## Faults and fixes
| Fault | Fix |
|---|---|
| He lifts with a rounded back | bend the knees, back vertical, lift with the legs |
| Late jump (dead weight) | count the "and" and plié together |
| She softens in the air | hold the torso and legs firm |
| Lowering too fast | he controls the descent; she lands in plié |

## Gotchas
- Fatigue causes most partnering injuries; schedule big lifts early in rehearsal, not late.
- Overhead lifts need clearance: check flies, scenery and lighting bars.

## Verify
- [ ] Lift timed with a shared plié and an active jump.
- [ ] Lifter's back stays upright.
- [ ] Controlled landing with the lifted dancer in plié.
