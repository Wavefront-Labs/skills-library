# Spotting

**Goal:** keep orientation and reduce dizziness during turns by keeping the eyes on one point
as long as possible and returning to it as early as possible.

## Method
1. **Choose the spot:** a fixed point at eye level in the direction you face at the end of the
   turn (on stage: an exit sign or the edge of the dress circle; in class: the mirror or a mark).
2. **Hold:** as the body begins to turn, the head stays facing the spot.
3. **Whip:** when the head can no longer stay, it turns quickly - the fastest part of the turn
   - and finds the spot again before the body arrives.
4. **Level:** the head turns on a level plane; no tilting or dropping the chin.
5. Drills: walking quarter-turns spotting each wall; slow "head only" spots standing; then
   half turns on relevé; then full turns.

## Travelling turns
- For chaînés and piqués travelling on a line or diagonal, spot the **end point of the
  travel** (the corner), not the front.
- For manèges (circles), spot is continuously changing; spot the direction of travel ahead.

## Dizziness
- Repeated practice reduces post-turn dizziness (vestibular habituation).
- Fix the eyes on a stable point immediately after the last turn.
- If dizziness persists or comes with nausea outside of turning, refer for assessment.

## Faults and fixes
| Fault | Fix |
|---|---|
| Head travels with the body | hold it back deliberately; turn it last |
| Chin drops on the whip | keep the eyeline level |
| Spotting with eyes but not head | the whole head turns |

## Verify
- [ ] Head arrives at the spot before the shoulders on every turn.
- [ ] Ends a set of chaînés travelling straight on the line.
