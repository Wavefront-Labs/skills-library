# Classical ballet mime

**Goal:** tell the story with the conventional gesture language of 19th-century ballet,
phrased to the music so the audience reads it at the back of the house.

## Core vocabulary (common conventions)
| Meaning | Gesture |
|---|---|
| I / me | hands touch the chest |
| you | open hand gestures towards the person |
| dance | arms circle around each other above the head |
| love | both hands over the heart |
| marry | point to the ring finger of the left hand |
| beautiful | hand circles the face |
| queen / king | hand traces a crown over the head; then point |
| die / death | arms crossed and lowered, fists clenched |
| swear / promise | two fingers raised towards the sky |
| no / never | arms crossed then uncrossed sharply downward |
| see | point to the eyes |
| why? | hands open upward, shoulders lifted |

Productions vary slightly; learn the exact version from the répétiteur.

## Method
1. **Phrase it like dance:** each gesture lands on a musical phrase - mime is choreography,
   not acting improvised over the music.
2. **Size:** gestures big and clear, eyes leading; hold the end of each "word".
3. **Address:** look at the character you are addressing, then out to the audience on key words.
4. **Character:** a queen mimes differently from a peasant girl - weight, speed, posture.

## Repertoire examples
- Swan Lake Act II: Odette's story of the sorcerer's curse and the lake of tears.
- Giselle Act I: Berthe warns of the Wilis.
- Sleeping Beauty Prologue: Carabosse's curse.

## Verify
- [ ] Gestures readable from the back of the room.
- [ ] Each gesture placed on the music as staged.
- [ ] Character consistent with the role.
