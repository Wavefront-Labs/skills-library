# Épaulement, head and eyeline

**Goal:** the slight rotation of the shoulders against the hips and the placing of the head
that turn correct steps into classical dancing. Examiners mark it under artistry/presentation.

## Method
1. **Shoulders:** in croisé and effacé the shoulder nearest the audience comes slightly
   forward; the rotation happens in the upper spine, the hips stay on their diagonal.
2. **Head positions:** erect, inclined (tilted to one side), turned, lifted, lowered - and
   combinations. In croisé devant the head is usually turned towards the downstage
   shoulder; the exact inclination in each pose differs between schools and set exercises,
   so take it from the syllabus or the teacher, not by rule of thumb.
3. **Eyeline:** eyes follow the hand in port de bras; otherwise look out over the audience
   (just above the heads), not at the floor or the mirror in performance.
4. **Adding it:** learn the legs en face first, then add épaulement to the same exercise -
   e.g. tendus in croisé and effacé with the head moving on the close.

## Faults and fixes
| Fault | Fix |
|---|---|
| Hips twist with the shoulders | rotation from the upper spine only |
| Head tilts but chin juts | lengthen the back of the neck, then incline |
| Eyes drop to the floor | pick a point at audience-eye-level |
| Stiff, held head | the head breathes with the port de bras |

## Gotchas
- Mirror habit: dancers who always look at the mirror lose épaulement when the mirror is
  covered (exams, stage). Practise facing away regularly.
- Vaganova épaulement is generally fuller than RAD/Cecchetti; match the style asked.

## Verify
- [ ] Hips stay on the diagonal while the shoulders shade.
- [ ] Head position correct for the set exercise in each direction.
- [ ] Eyeline out, not down, in centre work.
