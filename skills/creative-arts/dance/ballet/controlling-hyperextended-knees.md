# Controlling hyperextended knees

**Goal:** dancers whose knees extend past straight (genu recurvatum) get a curved leg line
but tend to lock back, sink into the heels and strain the knee and lower back. Teach a
straight, supported leg instead of a locked one.

## Recognise it
- In profile, the locked knee sits behind the line from hip to ankle.
- Heels cannot touch in 1st or 5th without the knees overlapping or locking.
- Weight drifts onto the heels; the pelvis often tips forward to compensate.

## Method
1. **Find straight:** side-on to the mirror, lock the knees, then release until hip, knee and
   ankle line up. That is the working position; it feels slightly bent to the dancer at first.
2. **Engage, don't lock:** quadriceps and hamstrings co-contract; the kneecap lifts but the
   joint is not pressed back.
3. **Weight forward:** over the balls of the feet with three-point contact. Hyperextension
   usually lives with weight in the heels.
4. **Positions:** in 1st and 5th accept a small gap between the heels (or a slightly
   uncrossed 5th) rather than forcing heels together and locking.
5. **Extensions:** the curved line can be shown on the working leg in tendu and développé;
   it is the supporting leg that must be held.

## Gotchas
- Hyperextension often comes with generalised hypermobility; refer to a dance physio if there
  is pain or instability (see the IADMS hypermobility guidance).
- "Pull up your knees" makes hyperextenders lock harder. Cue "lengthen up the thigh, knee soft".
- Landing on locked knees sends force straight into the joint - check every jump.

## Verify
- [ ] Side view: hip, knee and ankle in line on the supporting leg.
- [ ] Weight over the balls of the feet, heels light.
- [ ] Dancer can feel and describe engaged versus locked.
