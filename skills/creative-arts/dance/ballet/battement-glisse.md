# Battement glissé (dégagé)

**Goal:** a quick, light brush of the foot off the floor that trains the speed and precision
needed for petit allegro.

Terminology: RAD and Cecchetti say *battement glissé*, Vaganova *battement jeté*, American
schools *dégagé*. Same action.

## Method
1. Start as a tendu, brush through the floor, and let the pointed foot leave the floor to a
   low height (around 25 degrees; some syllabi allow up to 45).
2. **Accent out:** the foot arrives at height sharply and holds for an instant. Some set
   exercises accent in instead - follow the set.
3. Return by brushing back through the floor into a clean 5th.
4. Variations: glissé with pointé (tapping the floor), piqué, en cloche through 1st, with
   demi-plié on closing.

## Faults and fixes
| Fault | Fix |
|---|---|
| Foot flicks without passing through the floor | brush through the whole foot every time |
| Too high, hip lifts | lower the height; height belongs to grand battement |
| Bouncing supporting leg | supporting knee pulled up, weight forward |
| Soft foot at height | full articulation before speed |

## Gotchas
- This is the barre exercise most directly tied to jumping: the brush of glissade, jeté and
  assemblé is this movement. Lazy brushes show up as weak petit allegro.
- At fast tempi dancers cheat the closing into 3rd; correct it immediately.

## Verify
- [ ] Each brush visibly passes through the floor.
- [ ] Consistent low height, foot fully pointed.
- [ ] Hips level; supporting leg steady.
- [ ] Accent matches the music as set.
