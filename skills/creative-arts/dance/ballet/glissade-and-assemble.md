# Glissade and assemblé

**Goal:** the most used linking step (glissade) and the first jump from one foot to two
(assemblé), plus the combination that strings them together.

## Glissade
1. From 5th demi-plié, the leading foot brushes out along the floor (glissé) to the side.
2. Push off the back leg; both legs stretch briefly just off or close to the floor.
3. Land on the leading leg in demi-plié, the other foot slides in to close 5th in plié.
4. Variants: sideways with or without change of feet (dessus/dessous - the syllabus defines
   which foot leads and which finishes in front), en avant, en arrière.
- Rhythm: usually "and-1", the closing on the count.

## Assemblé
1. From 5th demi-plié, the working foot brushes out through the floor (like a glissé).
2. Push off the supporting leg; both legs **join in 5th in the air** (assembled) before
   landing together.
3. Land in 5th demi-plié on both feet, heels down.
4. Variants: dessus (back foot brushes out, lands front), dessous (front brushes, lands back),
   devant, derrière, porté (travelling), battu (beaten).

## Combination
Glissade - assemblé: the glissade's plié is the assemblé's preparation; no stop between.

## Faults and fixes
| Fault | Fix |
|---|---|
| Legs land one after the other in assemblé | join legs in the air first |
| Glissade too big/jumped | a gliding step near the floor |
| No brush through the floor | brush like battement glissé |
| Ending in 3rd or open 5th | precise closing every time |

## Verify
- [ ] Legs meet in the air on assemblé and land simultaneously.
- [ ] Glissade travels low and closes on the count.
- [ ] Dessus/dessous correct on request.
