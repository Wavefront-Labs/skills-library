# Chaînés and piqué turns

**Goal:** fast, travelling turns on a straight line or circle, used in diagonals and codas.

## Chaînés (déboulés)
1. Legs together in 1st on demi-pointe (or pointe), knees straight and close together.
2. Each turn is two half-turns: step onto one foot (half turn), bring the other beside it
   (half turn). Small steps - travel comes from many turns, not big steps.
3. Arms close to 1st (or held low) - closing them speeds the turn.
4. Spot the end of the diagonal.

## Piqué turn en dedans
1. From a small preparation (tendu or dégagé to the side with a fondu), step directly onto a
   straight leg on demi-pointe (the piqué) in the direction of travel.
2. The other leg snaps to retiré (or coupé) as you turn towards the supporting leg.
3. Land the working foot in front or behind as set (often lowering into fondu and dégagé for
   the next).

## Piqué turn en dehors ("lame duck" variants)
- Step onto the supporting leg and turn away from it, the other leg in retiré or
  coupé; a quicker, trickier rhythm.

## Manège
- Piqués or chaînés travelling in a large circle around the stage; lean slightly into the
  circle and spot the next point of travel.

## Faults and fixes
| Fault | Fix |
|---|---|
| Chaînés with knees bent and legs apart | straight legs, feet close, small steps |
| Drifting off the diagonal | spot the corner, not the front |
| Piqué lands on a bent leg | step onto a straight leg, onto demi-pointe directly |
| Arms flailing | keep them close and still |

## Verify
- [ ] 8 chaînés travelling in a straight line to the corner.
- [ ] Piqué en dedans series with a straight supporting leg.
- [ ] Finishes cleanly at the end of the diagonal.
