# Arabesque lines

**Goal:** the long line from fingertips to the extended back leg, with a lifted spine and
square-enough hips, in each numbered arabesque.

## Building the shape
1. **Supporting leg** straight (or in fondu as set), fully rotated, weight over the ball of the
   foot.
2. **Working leg** extended behind, rotated (the knee faces the side, not the floor), foot
   pointed in line with the leg.
3. **Back:** the spine lifts up and slightly forward as the leg rises above about 45 degrees -
   the pelvis tilts a little and opens slightly; the upper back spirals back towards the
   audience-side, shoulders level, not hunched.
4. **Arms:** reach long, palms facing down, through the line of the body; eyes over the front
   hand in most arabesques.

## Numbering (check your school)
The numbered arabesques differ in three variables: which arm is forward (same side as the
supporting leg, or in opposition), whether one or both arms reach forward, and how the body is
oriented to the audience (profile, effacé or croisé, with the 4th usually spiralling the back
towards the audience). Cecchetti has five arabesques, Vaganova four, and RAD uses its own set
within the syllabus. The same number means a different shape across schools, so learn the
arabesques from the syllabus book of the school you are taught or examined in, and ask which
system a choreographer means.

## Faults and fixes
| Fault | Fix |
|---|---|
| Collapsed back to raise the leg | lower the leg, lift the sternum |
| Working knee faces the floor | rotate from the hip, heel down |
| Hip of working leg swings up and out | square the hips a little more, lower height |
| Weight back on the supporting heel | weight forward over the ball of foot |

## Verify
- [ ] Rotated working leg with a straight knee.
- [ ] Lifted, spiralled upper back; level shoulders.
- [ ] Correct arms for the numbered arabesque in the school asked.
