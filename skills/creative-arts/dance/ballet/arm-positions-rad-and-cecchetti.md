# Arm positions across RAD, Cecchetti and Vaganova

**Goal:** know the arm positions of the school you teach or are examined in, and translate
correctly when a choreographer or guest teacher uses another system.

## Comparison
| Shape | RAD (English) | Cecchetti | Vaganova |
|---|---|---|---|
| arms low, rounded in front of thighs | bras bas | 5th en bas | preparatory position |
| rounded in front at navel/diaphragm height | 1st | 5th en avant | 1st |
| open to the side, slightly forward and below shoulder | 2nd | 2nd | 2nd |
| rounded above and slightly in front of head | 5th | 5th en haut | 3rd |
| open low to the side, between bras bas and 2nd | demi-seconde | demi-seconde | - |

The mixed positions (one arm in one place, the other elsewhere: RAD 3rd and 4th, Cecchetti 1st,
3rd and 4th en avant/en haut) are defined differently in each school and have changed between
syllabus revisions. Confirm them in the current syllabus book of the school you are examined in
rather than translating from memory.

## Shape of the arm and hand
- Rounded but not broken: elbows softly lifted (supported from the back), the curve continues
  through the wrist into the fingers.
- Hand: fingers grouped, thumb near the middle finger, no stiff "claw" or broken wrist.
- Shoulders down; the arm lives in front of the body so the dancer sees it with peripheral vision.

## Gotchas
- "5th" means low arms in Cecchetti (en bas) and high arms in RAD and Vaganova contexts.
  Ask which system before setting counts with arms.
- Dropped elbows are the most common fault; cue "elbows float, arms supported from the back".

## Verify
- [ ] Dancer can name and show each position in their syllabus and translate to one other.
- [ ] Elbows supported, wrists unbroken, shoulders down in every position.
