# Cambré and port de bras at the barre

**Goal:** stretch and mobilise the spine with control, usually after pliés or within tendu
exercises, without dumping into the lower back.

## Method
1. **Lengthen first:** lift the ribs away from the pelvis, then begin the bend.
2. **Forward:** fold from the hips with a long spine to about 90 degrees, then release head and
   neck; the arm follows through bras bas. Return by restacking the spine or with a flat
   back as set.
3. **Back:** hips stay over the feet (do not push them forward), lift the sternum up and
   back, bend through the thoracic spine; arm in 5th, eyes follow the hand. Depth is limited
   by upper-back mobility, not by how far the lower back can hinge.
4. **Side (towards or away from the barre):** stay in the frontal plane; no twist, the hip
   does not push out.
5. **Circular port de bras** (higher grades): forward, side, back, side in one breath-like
   circle with the legs completely still.

## Faults and fixes
| Fault | Fix |
|---|---|
| Hinging at the lumbar spine | lift the sternum, engage lower abdominals, shallower bend |
| Hips shooting forward | weight over the balls of the feet, thighs vertical |
| Knees releasing in forward bend | keep legs pulled up; fold from the hips |
| Holding breath | exhale into the bend, inhale to return |

## Gotchas
- Back pain after cambrés usually means lumbar hinging. Repeated lumbar hyperextension is
  associated with spondylolysis in adolescent dancers - refer persistent low-back pain.
- Hypermobile dancers can bend very far and still be unsupported; control beats range.

## Verify
- [ ] Hips stay over the feet in every direction (side mirror).
- [ ] The back bend visibly comes from the upper back.
- [ ] Return to vertical is controlled, not a bounce.
