# Free enchaînement and unseen work

**Goal:** learn a combination you have never seen (set by the examiner or audition teacher)
quickly and dance it accurately and musically - a test of vocabulary, memory and musicality.

## Method
1. **Listen to the terms:** the setter often names steps in French. Knowing terminology cold is
   most of the battle.
2. **Count structure:** most enchaînements are 8 or 16 bars; identify the phrase shape (e.g.
   2 x 8 repeated on the other side).
3. **Chunk:** remember patterns, not single steps - "glissade-assemblé twice, then sissonne,
   changement" is three chunks.
4. **Mark with counts:** mark it small while the setter demonstrates, saying counts and
   direction changes to yourself.
5. **Listen to the music played:** waltz, march, mazurka? The quality changes the attack
   and ballon.
6. **Dance full out first time:** commit, add épaulement and presentation. A small error
   danced musically scores better than a hesitant correct version.

## Teacher preparation
- Set short unseen combinations in every class from the level's vocabulary.
- Vary music and time signatures.
- Ask students to repeat a combination on the other side without re-demonstration.

## Faults and fixes
| Fault | Fix |
|---|---|
| Freezing on a forgotten step | keep the rhythm going; improvise a linking step |
| Ignoring the music style | match quality to the time signature |
| Over-marking with no épaulement | add the head and arms on the first full run |

## Verify
- [ ] Student repeats a new 16-bar enchaînement after one demonstration.
- [ ] Performs it on the other side unaided.
- [ ] Musical quality matches the accompaniment.
