# Learning and coaching a classical variation

**Goal:** a variation performed with the correct version, steps, musicality and character,
with enough stamina to finish as strongly as it started.

## Choose the right variation and version
- Match to the dancer's strengths (jump vs adage vs turns) and the competition/exam rules -
  some competitions publish a fixed list with required versions and music cuts.
- Versions differ between companies (Petipa as staged by the Royal Ballet vs Mariinsky vs
  ABT). Pick one and stay consistent; mixing versions is marked down.

## Learning process
1. Listen to the music alone; mark phrases and accents on a copy of the score or a timed list.
2. Learn from a coach, a notated score or a licensed staging where possible; use several
   videos of the chosen version, never one YouTube clip alone.
3. Learn in sections (entrance, middle, diagonal/manège, finish). Mark first, then full out.
4. Map every step to counts and music cues; note where the tempo changes.

## Stamina
- Run sections full out, then pairs of sections, then the whole variation; add one extra run
  per week. Finish with the hardest section when fresh, then in fatigue.
- The coda section is usually the hardest - rehearse it after the opening, not only at the end.

## Character and artistry
- Know who the character is and what the variation means in the ballet (e.g. a fairy's gift,
  a peasant celebration). Adjust épaulement, eyes and attack to match.
- Plan the entrance and the exit/reverence as part of the variation.

## Verify
- [ ] Version identified and consistent; steps match the chosen staging.
- [ ] Two consecutive full-out runs without loss of quality.
- [ ] Music cues and tempo agreed with the recording or pianist.
