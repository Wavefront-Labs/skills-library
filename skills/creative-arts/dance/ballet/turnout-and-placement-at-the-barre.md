# Turnout and placement at the barre

**Goal:** build the foundation of ballet — rotation of the legs from the hip joint, on a
stable, upright pelvis and spine — so that every barre exercise trains strength and line
rather than strain on knees and ankles.

> **Status: draft.** Principles below match mainstream teaching across Vaganova, RAD and
> Cecchetti schools and dance-medicine guidance. Individual anatomy varies a lot; an
> experienced teacher's eye beats any written cue.

## 1. Turnout is rotation at the hip
Turnout is **external rotation of the femur in the hip socket**, powered largely by the
deep hip rotators and the inner thighs, with the knees and feet following. It is
**limited by bone structure** (hip socket orientation, femoral neck angle) as much as by
flexibility. Few dancers have a "perfect" 180°; most professionals work somewhere well short
of it and look turned out through placement and strength.

**Find your functional turnout:** stand in parallel, heels together. Without moving the
pelvis, rotate both legs out from the top of the thigh — feel the back of the hips work —
and let the feet follow. Where the feet land, with knees directly over the second toes,
is **your** first position. Work there.

## 2. Placement checklist (standing at the barre)
- **Feet:** weight spread across big toe joint, little toe joint and heel. Arches lifted,
  not collapsed.
- **Knees:** pulled up (quadriceps engaged), aligned over the toes, not locked back hard
  into hyperextension.
- **Pelvis:** level and neutral — not tipped forward (arched lower back, bottom sticking
  out) or tucked under (flattened back, gripping glutes).
- **Torso:** ribs closed (not flared), abdominals gently drawn in and up, shoulders down,
  neck long.
- **Hand on the barre:** resting lightly, slightly in front of the body. If you'd fall
  without it, you're leaning.

## 3. How it shows up in the first exercises
- **Pliés:** knees travel **directly over the toes** as you bend. In demi-plié heels stay
  down; in grand plié (except in second) the heels release only at the bottom. If knees fall
  inside the toes, the turnout at the feet is more than your hips can hold.
- **Tendu:** the working leg slides out from the hip with rotation maintained; heel leads
  forward, toes lead back. Supporting leg stays rotated too — it's half the work.
- **Rond de jambe:** the pelvis stays still; the leg circles in the hip socket. Watch the
  hip of the working leg doesn't hike.
- **Relevé:** rise straight up over the second/third toes; no sickling.

## 4. Faults and fixes
| Fault | Sign | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Forcing turnout** from the feet | feet at 180°, knees pointing forward, arches rolling in | reduce foot angle until knees track over toes |
| **Rolling in** (pronation) | inner arch collapses, big toe side bears weight | lift arch, spread weight, less turnout |
| **Sickling** | pointed foot curves inward at the ankle | line through ankle — imagine heel forward on pointe |
| **Tucking** | lower back flat, glutes clenched | find neutral; lengthen spine up, not under |
| **Swayback** | lower back arched, ribs open | engage lower abdominals, close ribs |
| **Sitting in the hip** | weight shifts into supporting hip on one leg | lift out of the supporting side, stay over the ball of the foot |

## Gotchas
- Forcing turnout at the feet is the classic cause of knee pain (twisting at the knee)
  and foot/ankle injuries — it may look "more" now and cost a career later.
- Turnout varies left vs right; work each side honestly.
- Turnout strength fades in centre without the barre — that's where you learn if it's real.
- Children and teenagers: don't push rotation with stretching tricks (frog stretch with
  weight on top) — growth plates are vulnerable.

## Verify
- [ ] First position set by hip rotation, knees over second toes.
- [ ] Pelvis neutral and level throughout pliés and tendus (check in the mirror side-on).
- [ ] Knees track over toes in every plié.
- [ ] No rolling in or sickling on relevé and pointed feet.
- [ ] Supporting leg kept rotated, not just the working leg.
