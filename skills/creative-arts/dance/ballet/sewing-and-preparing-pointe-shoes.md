# Sewing and preparing pointe shoes

**Goal:** a shoe that stays on, supports and fits the dancer's foot, prepared without
weakening it more than necessary.

## Ribbons
1. Fold the heel of the shoe forward onto the insole; the crease marks where ribbons go
   (just in front of the fold, on the inside of the canvas).
2. Cut ribbon into two lengths per shoe (about 55-60 cm each, depending on the ankle).
   Seal ends with a flame or clear nail varnish.
3. Sew each ribbon on the inside of the shoe, angled slightly forward, catching the lining
   and canvas but **not the drawstring channel**. Use strong thread or dental floss,
   small box-stitch.

## Elastics
- Sew a loop of elastic from heel to heel across the ankle front (or a cross-over), placed so
  it holds the heel on without digging into the Achilles tendon.

## Breaking in (minimal)
- Bend the shank only where the dancer's foot naturally bends (often at the 3/4 point), by hand.
- Soften the box sides slightly by pressing; avoid crushing it or hammering the box flat.
- Avoid shutting shoes in doors or standing on the box - it shortens shoe life drastically.

## Darning and platform
- Darning the platform (traditional) or adding suede/glue tips gives grip and reduces wear.
- Some dancers apply shellac or jet glue inside the box/shank to extend life: let it cure fully.

## Tying ribbons
- Wrap inside ribbon over the instep and around the ankle, then the outer ribbon the opposite
  way; tie a small knot on the inside of the ankle (not on the Achilles), tuck the ends in.
- Ribbons should be snug, not tight enough to restrict the Achilles.

## Verify
- [ ] Ribbons and elastics hold the heel without slipping.
- [ ] Knot on the inside ankle, ends tucked.
- [ ] Shank bends where the foot bends; box intact.
