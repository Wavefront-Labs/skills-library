# Relevé lent

**Goal:** raise a straight, rotated leg slowly from tendu to height and hold it. Unlike
développé there is no folding: the whole leg lifts as one lever, which makes it the most
honest test of extension strength.

## Method
1. Start in tendu (devant, à la seconde or derrière), supporting leg rotated.
2. Lift the straight leg slowly - typically 2-4 counts - to 90 degrees, keeping the full
   rotation and point.
3. Hold at the height for the set counts.
4. Lower slowly through 45 degrees to tendu, then close.

## Teaching points
- Initiate from under the thigh (hamstring/deep rotator connection), not from a gripping
  hip flexor at the front of the hip.
- Devant: heel forward, rotation increases as the leg rises. 2nd: the leg rises slightly in
  front of true side for most dancers so the hip does not hike. Derrière: the pelvis may
  tilt forward slightly; the spine lifts to compensate.
- Lowering is the strength builder - spend as long down as up.

## Faults and fixes
| Fault | Fix |
|---|---|
| Hip hiking to get height | accept a lower leg; level pelvis first |
| Bent knee at height | lower height until the knee stays straight |
| Front-of-hip pain/clicking | lower height, release gripping; see a dance physio if persistent |
| Supporting side sinks | lift out of the supporting hip, weight over the ball of foot |

## Gotchas
- Snapping or clicking at the front of the hip in relevé lent is common (iliopsoas over the
  hip). Painless clicking is often benign; painful clicking needs assessment.
- Don't let students hand-lift their leg to "show" height in exams; examiners mark control.

## Verify
- [ ] Straight leg rises without bending or hip hiking.
- [ ] Height held for the set counts without dropping.
- [ ] Lowering at least as slow as the rise.
