# Grand battement

**Goal:** a big, free throw of the straight leg that builds the dynamic flexibility and
attack used in grand allegro, while the body stays calm.

## Method
1. Brush out through tendu and glissé (the leg passes through the floor, never lifts from
   5th directly).
2. Throw the leg to its full height with the accent up; the leg is straight and turned out,
   foot fully pointed.
3. **Lower with control** back through tendu into 5th. The lowering is resisted; the leg
   does not fall.
4. Usual sequence: en croix, often finishing with a balance.

## Variations
- **Battement en cloche:** the leg swings like a bell through 1st, front to back, continuously.
  Torso counter-balances minimally; hips stay square as long as possible.
- **Grand battement développé (enveloppé):** throw to height, then fold back to retiré and
  close.
- **Grand battement pointé:** throw, lower to touch pointe on the floor, throw again.

## Faults and fixes
| Fault | Fix |
|---|---|
| Torso jerks back or forward | keep the spine vertical; height comes from the leg |
| Leg drops to the floor | resist the lowering, count it |
| Supporting knee bends | supporting leg pulled up, weight over the ball of the foot |
| Hip hikes in 2nd | throw slightly in front of 2nd; working hip down |
| Rotation lost on the way up | lead with the heel (front) and the toes (back) |

## Gotchas
- Grand battements are ballistic; place them after the body is fully warm (end of barre),
  and reduce the number for dancers with hip flexor or hamstring issues.
- Height is not the goal; a controlled 90 degrees beats a wild 150.

## Verify
- [ ] Every battement passes through tendu out and in.
- [ ] Accent up, controlled lowering.
- [ ] Shoulders and pelvis still (front and side mirror).
