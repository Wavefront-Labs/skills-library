# Swing and understudy tracks

**Goal:** know several ensemble tracks (sometimes all of them) well enough to go on in any
of them, often with a few minutes' notice. An **understudy** covers a principal role; a
**swing** covers ensemble tracks and is not normally in the show nightly.

> **Status: draft.** Contracts and naming (swing, standby, alternate, universal swing) vary
> by country and agreement.

## The swing book
For each track, keep:
- **Track sheet**: entrances, exits, positions per number, props, costume changes.
- **Spacing charts** per number, with your track highlighted.
- **Counts and choreography notes**, singing parts and harmony line.
- **Traffic notes**: who passes behind you, wing assignments.

## Learning tracks
1. Learn the choreography from the dance captain; record it.
2. Watch the show from the wings and front of house, focusing on one track at a time.
3. Walk each track on stage in understudy rehearsals.
4. Learn harmonies per track; ensemble parts differ by track.

## Going on
- Confirm which track, costume, wig and mic.
- Brief the people around you ("I'm on for Sam tonight").
- Run the book's quick checklist: changes, props, key spacing.

## Verify
- [ ] Swing book complete and updated for every covered track.
- [ ] Each track walked on stage.
- [ ] Quick-call checklist ready.
