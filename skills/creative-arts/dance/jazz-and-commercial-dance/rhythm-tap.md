# Rhythm tap

**Goal:** dance tap as a percussion instrument - the hoofer tradition of John Bubbles,
Baby Laurence, Jimmy Slyde, Gregory Hines and Savion Glover - where the sound is the point
and the body serves it.

> **Status: draft.** Built on rhythm tap teaching practice; individual teachers have their
> own terminology for the same sounds.

## Body and sound
- **Low and relaxed**: knees soft, weight centred over the balls, upper body quiet, arms
  natural. Big presentational arms are Broadway tap, not rhythm tap.
- **Heel drops** are a full voice: drop the heel with weight for bass sounds; ball sounds
  are higher. Use heel and toe to create melody.
- **Clarity**: every sound clean and even before any speed.

## Musicality
- **Swing vs straight:** swung eighths (long-short) in jazz; straight in funk and
  contemporary music. Match the band.
- **Accents and dynamics:** vary volume - not every sound at the same level. Ghost notes
  (very soft) make accents stand out.
- **Syncopation:** accent the "and"s and the "a"s; place phrases across the bar line rather
  than always starting on 1.
- **Phrasing:** think in 2-, 4- and 8-bar phrases and in the song's form (12-bar blues,
  32-bar AABA). Know where you are in the chorus.
- **Silence** is a sound: leave space.

## Practice routine
1. Metronome on 2 and 4 (jazz feel) at 60-80 bpm.
2. Paradiddles and rudiments with feet (heel-toe, toe-heel patterns) for even sound.
3. Take a known phrase and re-accent it four ways.
4. Play along with recordings (Count Basie, Thelonious Monk) and transcribe a drummer's
   phrase into feet.

## Faults and fixes
| Fault | Fix |
|---|---|
| All sounds same volume | practise ghost notes; record yourself |
| Rushing | metronome on 2 and 4; sit back on the beat |
| Arms flailing | hands relaxed; focus on the floor sound |

## Verify
- [ ] Clean, even sounds at slow and fast tempo.
- [ ] Clear accents and dynamic range in a recorded phrase.
- [ ] Can say where in the song form they are at any point.
