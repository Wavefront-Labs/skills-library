# Musical theatre dance style

**Goal:** match the dance style of a specific show and era. A revival of *Oklahoma!*,
*West Side Story* or *A Chorus Line* asks for a different body from a modern pop musical.

> **Status: draft.** Each original choreography is staged by its rights holders or by
> the new production's choreographer; this skill is about recognising and adapting style.

## Key choreographers and signatures
| Choreographer | Shows | Signature |
|---|---|---|
| Agnes de Mille | *Oklahoma!* (1943), *Carousel* | ballet-based, the "dream ballet" that tells story; Americana |
| Jerome Robbins | *West Side Story*, *Fiddler on the Roof* | ballet and jazz fused, character-driven, grounded athletic jumps, finger snaps |
| Jack Cole | film musicals; *Kismet* | "father of theatrical jazz": isolations, knee slides, Indian and Latin influences, low crouch |
| Bob Fosse | *Pippin*, *Chicago*, *Sweet Charity* | turned-in, isolations, hats, stillness |
| Gower Champion | *Hello, Dolly!*, *42nd Street* | presentational, tap and big ensemble staging |
| Michael Bennett | *A Chorus Line*, *Dreamgirls* | jazz with precise unison; cinematic staging |

## Working method
1. Research the period: film clips, original cast recordings, the published script and
   (where available) the stage manager's or dance captain's records.
2. Identify base technique (ballet, jazz, tap) and posture of the era.
3. Learn the character first: who is dancing, and why do they dance here? Robbins' Jets
   move as a gang, not as a chorus line.
4. Match costume and shoes in rehearsal early - long skirts, character heels and boots
   change the movement.

## Gotchas
- Modern extension and contemporary flourishes creep into period pieces; keep lines to the era.
- Character drops in the dance break. The dance is the scene continuing.

## Verify
- [ ] Base technique and posture of the original identified and drilled.
- [ ] Character intention clear in every dance section.
- [ ] Rehearsed in period shoes and costume pieces.
