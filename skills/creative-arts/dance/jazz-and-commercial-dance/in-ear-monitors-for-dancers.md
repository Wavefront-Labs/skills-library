# In-ear monitors for dancers

**Goal:** dance in time on a big tour or TV show where the stage sound is unusable, using
in-ear monitors (IEMs) fed from the monitor engineer.

> **Status: draft.** Practice-based; equipment and conventions vary by production.

## Kit
- **Earpieces**: universal-fit buds or custom moulds (best seal and stays in).
- **Beltpack receiver**: worn in a pouch or costume-built pocket, cable run under costume,
  secured with tape or medical tape behind the ear.
- **Spare pack** side of stage; costume team knows the routing.

## Your mix
- Ask the monitor engineer for: music, a **click** (metronome), **count-ins** or a
  dance-captain talkback, a little of the artist's vocal.
- Set level at the lowest comfortable volume; louder is not safer.
- Request a soundcheck where you dance full out to test that the pack stays on.

## Hearing safety
- A good seal lets you run quieter mixes; don't take one ear out (you'll turn the other up).
- Annual hearing checks; earplugs when not on IEMs at loud venues.

## When it fails
- If the pack drops out, keep dancing to the visual cues (other dancers, lights, the
  artist), and use the stage monitors if available.
- Tell the monitor engineer or dance captain after the number, not mid-show.

## Verify
- [ ] Pack secured and tested in full-out rehearsal.
- [ ] Mix includes music, click and count-ins as needed at safe level.
- [ ] Spare pack and failure plan known.
