# Jazz walks and travelling steps

**Goal:** move across the floor with jazz style - grounded, rhythmic, with plié - using the
basic travelling vocabulary.

## Steps
| Step | How |
|---|---|
| **Jazz walk (low)** | deep plié, step through the ball of the foot, hips released, torso slightly forward, often with isolations |
| **Jazz walk (high/strut)** | on demi-pointe, legs crossing slightly, hips swinging, chest lifted |
| **Ball change** | quick weight transfer: ball of one foot behind ("and"), then the other foot ("1") |
| **Pas de bourrée** | back-side-front (behind, side, front) - three quick weight changes, "1-and-2" |
| **Jazz square** | cross front, step back, step side, step front (or together) in a square |
| **Chassé** | step-together-step travelling, one foot chases the other |
| **Triplets** | down-up-up waltz rhythm (plié-relevé-relevé) travelling across the floor |
| **Step touch / kick ball change** | social-dance-derived basics used in combos |

## Teaching points
- Plié is the engine: knees soft, weight low unless the style calls for height.
- Accents matter: many steps hit on the "and" or have syncopation.
- Arms and focus give style; practise with and without them.

## Faults and fixes
| Fault | Fix |
|---|---|
| Stiff, upright walking | lower centre, plié, release hips |
| Pas de bourrée on flat feet | use the balls of the feet, quick changes |
| Losing timing in triplets | clap down-up-up first |

## Verify
- [ ] Each step performed across the floor in time.
- [ ] Low and high walks show distinct styles.
- [ ] Combinations of steps flow without stops.
