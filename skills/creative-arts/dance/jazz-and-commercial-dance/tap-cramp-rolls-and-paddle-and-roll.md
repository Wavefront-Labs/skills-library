# Cramp rolls and paddle and roll

**Goal:** produce fast, even, rolling sequences of sounds - the basis of rhythm-tap texture.

## Cramp roll
- Four sounds: **ball (R), ball (L), heel (R), heel (L)** - usually after a small jump or step.
- Counted "e-and-a-1" or "1 e and a". The ending heel is often the accent.
- Variations: reverse cramp roll (heels first), cramp roll with change, travelling.

## Paddle and roll
- A continuous four-sound pattern on one foot: **dig (heel), spank, step (ball), heel drop** -
  repeated as a "paddle" while travelling or turning (paddle turns).
- Speed and evenness matter more than volume.

## Heel and toe drops
- **Heel drop:** with weight on the ball, drop the heel for a sound.
- **Toe drop:** with weight on the heel, drop the toe.
- Used to fill rhythms and make rolls.

## Practice
1. Say the sounds slowly: "ball-ball-heel-heel".
2. Metronome; play with accents on different sounds.
3. Build to sixteenth notes at moderate tempo.
4. Record and listen for even spacing.

## Faults and fixes
| Fault | Fix |
|---|---|
| Sounds clumping (flam) | separate each sound, slow down |
| Weight on heels | stay forward, balls first |
| Losing the accent | choose and mark the accent deliberately |

## Verify
- [ ] Four evenly spaced sounds in cramp rolls both sides.
- [ ] Paddle and roll sustained 4 bars, even.
- [ ] Accent placed where intended.
