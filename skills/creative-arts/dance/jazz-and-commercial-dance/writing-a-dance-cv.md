# Writing a dance CV

**Goal:** a single page that a choreographer or casting director scans in 10 seconds and
knows your look, your styles, your level and your credits.

> **Status: draft.** Conventions differ by country and sector (UK Spotlight vs US resume,
> concert vs commercial); follow your agent's template if you have one.

## Layout (one page, A4/Letter)
1. **Name** large; contact (or agent's contact), Equity/union membership if any.
2. **Stats**: height, hair, eyes, playing age range if theatre; sometimes clothing/shoe
   sizes for commercial work. Keep them true and current.
3. **Credits**, grouped by sector (Theatre, Tour/Live, TV/Film, Music Video,
   Commercials, Concert/Company), most recent or most impressive first:
   `Production | Role/Track | Company/Artist | Choreographer/Director`.
4. **Training**: school, course and dates; key teachers or intensives.
5. **Skills**: styles with honest level (ballet, jazz, tap, contemporary, commercial,
   heels, breaking...), acrobatics, singing range, instruments, accents, licences.
6. **Links**: showreel (2 minutes max, best work first), Spotlight/agency page, Instagram if
   professional.
7. **Headshot**: attached or printed on the back, recent and looking like you now.

## Rules of thumb
- Never list a skill you cannot perform at the audition on the spot.
- Choreographer names are currency: list them.
- Student work goes under Training or a separate "Training productions" heading.
- Save as PDF named `Firstname-Lastname-CV.pdf`.

## Verify
- [ ] One page, readable at a glance.
- [ ] Credits in consistent format with choreographers.
- [ ] Stats, headshot and reel current.
- [ ] Every listed skill performable today.
