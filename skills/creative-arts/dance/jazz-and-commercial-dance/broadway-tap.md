# Broadway tap

**Goal:** perform show tap - the presentational, upright style of *42nd Street*, *Anything
Goes* and *Crazy for You* - where the look and the line matter as much as the sound.

> **Status: draft.** Practice-based; style differs between productions and choreographers.

## Style
- **Upright**: lifted chest, shoulders open, head up; more balletic carriage than rhythm tap.
- **Arms and presentation**: set arm lines, jazz hands, smiles out to the house; dancing to
  the audience is part of the job.
- **Sound**: clean and unison - 30 dancers hitting the same sound is the effect. Fewer,
  louder sounds rather than intricate rhythms.
- **Vocabulary**: time steps, shuffles, flaps, cramp rolls, buffalos, maxie fords, shim
  sham, wings, traveling grapevines and kick lines.

## Unison work
1. The dance captain sets the reference sound recording or count.
2. Learn the sounds by saying them ("shuf-fle step, ball change") in rhythm.
3. Clean with eyes closed in small groups: listen for flams (two dancers' sounds
   slightly apart).
4. Spacing: tap lines are often in tight rows; agree on arm spacing.

## Selling the number
- The button (final pose) lands with the band's last hit, held until the applause starts.
- Energy rises through the number; the last chorus is bigger, not tired.

## Faults and fixes
| Fault | Fix |
|---|---|
| Flams in unison | small-group cleaning by ear |
| Looking at the floor | spot a point in the house; trust the feet |
| Heavy, low posture | lift through the sternum; think ballet carriage |

## Verify
- [ ] Unison sounds clean with no flams.
- [ ] Presentation and focus out to the house throughout.
- [ ] Button hit on the music and held.
