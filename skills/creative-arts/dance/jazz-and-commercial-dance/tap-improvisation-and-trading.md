# Tap improvisation and trading

**Goal:** make up tap in real time - solo over a band, or trading phrases with other
dancers and musicians at a jam - while staying in the music's form.

> **Status: draft.** From jazz and rhythm tap jam practice; jam etiquette varies by scene.

## Building material
- **Vocabulary**: a set of solid steps you can play at any tempo (paddle and rolls, cramp
  rolls, riffs, time-step variations).
- **Rhythmic ideas**: a short motif (e.g. three-note pickup) you can repeat, displace,
  augment (slower), diminish (faster) and answer.
- **Space and dynamics**: rests and soft passages make the loud passages count.

## Trading fours and eights
- In a jam or with a band, players take turns: 4 bars each ("trading fours") or 8.
- Count bars: always know when your turn ends; land a clear ending on the last bar.
- Respond to what came before: echo a rhythm, then develop it. This is the conversation.

## A practice ladder
1. Improvise only with a single step and change only the accents (2 minutes).
2. Improvise with only two steps, adding rests.
3. Trade 4s with a recording of a drummer (leave 4 bars blank on a backing track or use a
   loop).
4. Solo a full 12-bar blues chorus; record and listen.

## Jam etiquette
- Wait to be invited or for the circle to open; one soloist at a time.
- Do not exceed your turn; listen when others play.
- Acknowledge the musicians.

## Verify
- [ ] Can solo one chorus without losing the form.
- [ ] Trades 4s cleanly, ending on time.
- [ ] Solo shows development of an idea, not a list of steps.
