# Picking up choreography fast at auditions

**Goal:** in a typical open call you get one teach of a 32–64 count combination, at
speed, in a crowded room, then perform it in small groups. Getting kept depends on
learning it quickly *and* still having attention left for style and performance.

> **Status: draft.** Based on common audition practice in musical theatre and commercial
> (music video, tour, TV) dance. Conventions vary by city, director and production;
> treat timings as typical, not guaranteed.

## Before the teach
- **Warm up properly** before you arrive or in the corridor — rooms often go straight
  into the combination.
- **Stand where you can see the choreographer** and a mirror, ideally second or third row
  behind a gap. Not hidden at the back; not in the front row if you're a slow picker-upper
  (people copy the front row's mistakes).
- **Know the brief:** the style (jazz, theatre, hip-hop-commercial, heels), the show's
  vocabulary, what to wear and shoes. Bring options (jazz shoes, heels, trainers, kneepads).

## During the teach: technique for retention
1. **Count the music first.** Notice tempo and where "5-6-7-8" falls. Clap the phrase in
   your head when they demonstrate it at tempo.
2. **Chunk by eight-counts.** Treat each 8 as one unit. When the choreographer says
   "that's the first eight", run it once in your head with counts.
3. **Label moves with words.** "Step-ball-change, kick, pivot, hit, hit, chest pop, roll
   down." Words stick when bodies are tired. Sing them in rhythm.
4. **Watch the weight changes and directions first, arms second, details third.** If you
   have feet and facings right you can fake an arm; the reverse isn't true.
5. **Mark** (small, low-energy version) while learning, with full timing and facings —
   save the energy, but don't mark so small you don't learn the spacing.
6. **Ask one precise question** if at all: "Is count 6 on the left or right?" — not
   "Can you go over the whole thing again?"
7. **Connect the joins.** Mistakes happen at the transitions between eights; practise the
   end of each eight into the start of the next.

## Between teach and performing
- Run it **full out** at least once when the room is given time to practise.
- Run it **from different starting points** (from the second eight, from the third) so a
  blank doesn't end your run.
- Watch earlier groups: they reveal where the tricky counts are and what the panel responds to.

## Performing in groups
- **Commit to every move**; a confident wrong arm reads better than a hesitant right one.
- **Performance and style are the point**: faces, focus, attitude, musicality — the panel
  already assumes most people roughly know it by now. Dance it "as the show", not as
  a class exercise.
- If you blank: keep moving in style, catch up on the next eight. Never stop and walk off.
- Change rows when asked; know the combination facing different walls.

## Callbacks and cuts
Keep the combination in your head (and phone notes) through the day — callbacks often
revisit it with additions, or ask you to perform it in another style, or with singing.
Cuts aren't personal: casting is about height, type and the whole company picture.

## Gotchas
- **Learning only from the mirror** flips left and right when you're turned around.
  Learn the combo in your body's left/right.
- Copying the dancer next to you copies their mistakes.
- Overdoing tricks (extra turns, flips) you weren't asked for — the panel wants to see
  you can dance *their* choreography.
- Energy drained by running it full out twelve times before your group.

## Verify
- [ ] Can say the combination's counts and movement words in rhythm, start to end.
- [ ] Can start from any eight without restarting.
- [ ] Facings and weight changes correct when facing a different wall.
- [ ] Performed full out once before your group, with style and face, not just steps.
- [ ] Notes saved for callbacks.
