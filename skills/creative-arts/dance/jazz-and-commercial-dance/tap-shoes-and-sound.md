# Tap shoes and sound

**Goal:** equipment and conditions that make taps sound clear and safe to dance in.

## Shoes
- **Types:** Oxford lace-ups (most common, secure), Mary Janes (younger students), heeled
  character taps (theatre), professional rhythm-tap shoes with thicker soles/build.
- **Fit:** snug, no heel slip, toes not cramped - sloppy fit kills clarity.
- **Taps:** metal plates on toe and heel; quality taps (with a sound board/resonator) sound richer.

## Adjusting taps
- Taps are attached with small screws; loosening slightly can produce a jinglier sound,
  tightening a crisper sound. Check screws regularly - lost screws scratch floors and change sound.
- Replace worn taps; uneven wear changes tone.

## Floors and boards
- Wood floors (sprung) sound and feel best; avoid concrete (impact) and carpet.
- Portable tap boards (plywood/hardwood) for home practice and stages with vinyl.
- Tap on Marley/vinyl can damage it and sounds dull - lay boards or wood over it.

## Stage sound
- Floor mics (boundary/PZM) or contact mics; sometimes mics in shoes for big shows.
- Sound check with the full cast, adjusting for volume and room.

## Verify
- [ ] Shoes fit snugly; taps tight or tuned deliberately.
- [ ] Suitable floor or board available for practice and performance.
- [ ] Mic plan agreed with sound designer for shows.
