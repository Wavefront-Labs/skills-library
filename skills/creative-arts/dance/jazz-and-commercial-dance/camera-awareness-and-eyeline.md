# Camera awareness and eyeline

**Goal:** dance for the lens rather than a stage audience: the camera is the audience, and its
position, lens and shot size change what reads.

## Knowing the shot
- Ask: which camera is on me, what shot size (wide, mid, close-up), is it moving?
- On multi-camera TV, the **tally light** (red) shows the live camera.

## Adapt to shot size
| Shot | Performance |
|---|---|
| Wide | full-body clarity, lines, formations, travel |
| Mid | upper body, arms, groove and attitude |
| Close-up | face and eyes; small, precise movements; less is more |

## Eyeline
- **Into the lens:** direct address (common for lead artists and in music videos); feels
  intimate - only when directed.
- **Off-lens eyeline:** look at a point just beside the lens, or at another dancer, for a
  natural, observed feel.
- Agree on eyeline per section with the director.

## Facial performance
- Faces read big on camera; exaggerated stage faces look false in close-up.
- Keep energy in the eyes; avoid "dead face" when concentrating on counts.
- Check playback when allowed.

## Verify
- [ ] Dancer knows shot size and live camera for each section.
- [ ] Eyeline agreed and consistent.
- [ ] Playback shows appropriate scale and facial performance.
