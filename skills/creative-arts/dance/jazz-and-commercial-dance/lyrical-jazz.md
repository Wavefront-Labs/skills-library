# Lyrical jazz

**Goal:** dance in the lyrical style - jazz and ballet technique driven by the music's
melody and words, with continuous flow, breath and visible emotion. It is the staple of
competition and studio dance and of many commercial ballads.

> **Status: draft.** "Lyrical" is a studio/competition label, not a codified technique;
> definitions vary between teachers and competitions.

## What makes it lyrical
- **Flow:** movements connect through the transitions; no dead stops except chosen ones.
- **Breath:** inhale on rises and reaches, exhale into releases and falls; the breath is
  visible in the chest and shoulders.
- **Technique underneath:** ballet extensions, pirouettes and leaps; jazz parallel work and
  contractions; contemporary-style floor work and falls.
- **Interpretation:** the movement follows the vocal line and the sense of the lyric - not
  word-for-word gesture.

## Choreographing a lyrical piece
1. Map the song: verses, choruses, bridge, the climax. Mark the vocal phrases, not only the
   counts (lyrical phrases often run across the 8).
2. Decide the emotional arc per section and one physical idea per section (reach, collapse,
   spiral).
3. Place the tricks (turn sequence, leap, extension) at the musical peaks - usually the
   last chorus or key change.
4. Avoid literal mime ("heart" hands on "love"); show the feeling through quality instead.

## Performing it
- Let the movement finish: extend through fingertips and toes before the next one starts.
- Use dynamics: suspension before a fall, acceleration into a turn.
- Eyes and face follow the intention, not the mirror.

## Faults and fixes
| Fault | Fix |
|---|---|
| Choppy, count-by-count | dance to the vocal line; sing it while marking |
| Overacting the face | let the body carry it; ask for "less face" in run-throughs |
| Tricks without context | connect each trick into and out of the phrase |

## Verify
- [ ] Phrases connect without dead stops.
- [ ] Breath visible on rises and releases.
- [ ] Peaks of the choreography sit on peaks of the song.
