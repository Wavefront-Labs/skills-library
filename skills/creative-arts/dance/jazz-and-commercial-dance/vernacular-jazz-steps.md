# Vernacular jazz steps

**Goal:** dance the African American social and stage steps of the 1920s-1940s that
underpin all later jazz dance - with the swing feel that makes them right, and knowing
where they come from.

> **Status: draft.** Step names and exact versions vary between teachers and regional scenes;
> learn the version the class or show uses. Stearns' *Jazz Dance* is the classic history.

## Swing feel first
Swing music (around 120-200 bpm) has uneven eighth notes ("long-short") and an accent on
2 and 4. Bodies are relaxed, knees soft, a pulse (bounce) on every beat. Without the pulse
the steps look like a drill.

## Core steps
| Step | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Charleston** | swivel on the supporting foot while the other kicks forward and back; 1920s; also in tandem and side-by-side partner versions |
| **Shim Sham Shimmy** | a 1920s-30s tap routine (credited to Leonard Reed and Willie Bryant) danced as a line dance in swing scenes: shim sham, push and cross, tacky annie, half break |
| **Suzie Q** | heel-toe swivel travelling sideways with crossed feet |
| **Shorty George** | knees together, low, walking with knees swinging side to side (named for George "Shorty" Snowden) |
| **Truckin'** | shuffling walk with a wagging index finger raised |
| **Boogie Back / Boogie Forward** | low, rocking, travelling backward or forward on the bounce |
| **Apple Jacks** | heel and toe swivels with the knees, travelling |
| **Fishtail, Tacky Annie, Fall off the Log, Gaze Afar** | common chorus steps |

## Learning them
1. Pulse on every beat to the music for 16 bars before any step.
2. Learn each step slowly, then at tempo, then string 4-6 together for a chorus.
3. Learn a routine (Shim Sham, Tranky Doo, Big Apple) to fix the vocabulary in phrases.

## Context
These dances come from Black communities (Harlem's Savoy Ballroom, Black vaudeville and the
TOBA circuit). Name originators and the culture when teaching or staging them.

## Verify
- [ ] Pulse and swing feel present throughout.
- [ ] Each step both sides and at tempo.
- [ ] Origins credited in class or programme notes.
