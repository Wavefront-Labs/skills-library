# Consistency across takes

**Goal:** film is cut together from many takes and angles, so a dancer must do the same
thing every time - the same arm on the same beat, the same hair, the same prop hand - or
the editor cannot cut.

> **Status: draft.** From film and TV set practice; departments and terminology vary.

## What must match
- **Choreography and timing** to the playback track (the music played on set).
- **Marks**: start and end positions; where you are at key beats.
- **Props and costume**: which hand holds the hat, jacket on or off, hair up or down.
- **Eyeline and facing**: matched across angles (see camera-awareness skill).
- **Energy**: a wide shot and its close-up must have the same intensity.

## How to deliver
1. Lock the choreography before the shoot; no "improving" between takes unless directed.
2. Ask the script supervisor or continuity to note anything you change.
3. Count from the playback's start cue every take; know the bar you start in when
   picking up mid-song.
4. Mark the energy you can sustain: 10-20 takes of a chorus is normal - pace yourself; full
   out when the camera is rolling, marking between.
5. Watch the monitor playback when invited to check matching.

## Slow-motion shoots
Slow-motion music videos are filmed at a higher frame rate with the track played faster
(e.g. 2x speed for 50 fps on a 25 fps timeline), so the movement looks in time when slowed.
Rehearse to the fast track before the shoot day.

## Verify
- [ ] Choreography locked and recorded before the shoot.
- [ ] Props, hair and costume details noted.
- [ ] Timing matches playback every take.
