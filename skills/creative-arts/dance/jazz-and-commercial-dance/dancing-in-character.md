# Dancing in character

**Goal:** in a book musical, dance is the scene continuing - every ensemble member is a
person with a reason to move, not a dancer doing steps.

> **Status: draft.** From musical theatre acting and dance practice.

## Build the character
- Who are they: name, age, job, status, relationships, what they want in this scene.
- How their body works: posture, weight, tempo, walk. A farmhand moves differently from a
  showgirl.
- Period clues: costume, shoes, social dances of the era.

## Per section
1. Why does the character dance now? (celebration, courtship, threat, dream).
2. What changes from start to end of the number?
3. Where is the focus - at whom are they looking?

## Execution
- Keep choreography exact; character lives in the quality and the face, not in changed steps.
- Reactions between dancers are real: listen and respond.
- Transitions in and out of dance are part of the scene; no "dancer face" switch on.

## Long runs
- Refresh the "why" before each show; find small truthful variations in reaction (within
  the set staging).

## Verify
- [ ] Character profile written for the track.
- [ ] Each dance section has a stated intention and focus.
- [ ] Steps exact; character visible in quality and reactions.
