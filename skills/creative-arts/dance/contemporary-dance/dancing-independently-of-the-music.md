# Dancing independently of the music

**Goal:** perform choreography that coexists with music (often heard for the first time in
performance) without following it - the Cage/Cunningham principle that dance and music share
time but not structure.

## Method
1. **Learn the dance in counts or clock time** without the score. Cunningham works were often
   rehearsed in silence with counts or a stopwatch.
2. **Internal tempo:** practise phrases against a metronome until the tempo is stable, then
   without it; check durations with a stopwatch.
3. **Cues:** use visual cues from other dancers, clock cues (a visible clock in the wings or
   held stopwatch), or agreed spatial cues - not musical ones.
4. **Ignore accents:** when the music arrives (live, electronic, sometimes unpredictable),
   don't react to its accents or dynamics. Keep the dance's own rhythm.
5. **Duration:** the only shared fact is the total duration; finish on time.

## Rehearsal tips
- Rehearse with random sound (radio, noise) to train non-reaction.
- Dancers count individually or in agreed groups; overlapping counts are normal.
- Keep a timing sheet: section start times in minutes:seconds.

## Faults and fixes
| Fault | Fix |
|---|---|
| Speeding up with loud music | metronome training, stopwatch checks |
| Reacting to sound accents | rehearse with distracting sound |
| Drifting off group timing | visual cues between dancers |

## Verify
- [ ] Sections hit their timed durations within a few seconds.
- [ ] Dancer stays in tempo with unrelated sound playing.
- [ ] Cues are visual or clock-based, documented.
