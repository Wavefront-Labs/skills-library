# Floor slides and spins

**Goal:** use the floor's slipperiness to travel and rotate without effort - a key texture
in contemporary floorwork.

## Preparation
- **Surface:** test the floor (vinyl/Marley vs wood). Friction changes everything; adapt the
  momentum.
- **Clothing:** long sleeves and trousers slide; bare skin sticks and burns. Costume fabric
  must be tested on the actual stage floor.
- **Contact surfaces:** outer thigh, side of the hip, back, shoulder blade - large, fleshy areas.

## Slides
1. **Side slide:** from a low lunge, lower onto the outer thigh and push with the standing leg;
   slide out to side lying.
2. **Back slide:** sitting, lean back and push with the feet; slide on the back.
3. **Knee slide (performative):** only on suitable floors with knee pads or trousers, landing on
   the shin, leaning back to unload the knees - never drop onto the kneecap.

## Spins
1. **Seated/back spin:** on the back or hip, legs tucked to spin faster (angular momentum),
   extended to slow.
2. **Spin initiated by leg swing:** swing the legs around to create rotation, then fold.

## Safety
- Check for splinters, tape seams and dirt before sliding.
- Warm up hips and shoulders; slides can jar if friction catches.

## Faults and fixes
| Fault | Fix |
|---|---|
| Friction burns | cover skin, use fabric |
| Slide stops short | more push, earlier contact |
| Spin wobbles | tuck tight, then extend to control |

## Verify
- [ ] Slides travel at least a body length smoothly.
- [ ] Spins controlled with tuck/extend.
- [ ] Costume tested on performance floor.
