# Horton hinge

**Goal:** the body leans back as one straight line from the knees to the top of the head,
pivoting at the knees, on demi-pointe - a test of thigh and core strength.

## Method
1. Stand in parallel 1st, rise to demi-pointe.
2. Bend the knees forward while the body from knees to head stays in a straight line and
   lowers backward - like a door hinged at the knees.
3. Arms reach forward (parallel to the floor) to counterbalance.
4. Go only as far as the line can be held; hold, then return by straightening the knees with
   the same straight line.
- Advanced: hinge to the floor, lowering until the back of the body lies on the floor (knees
  folded), then rising back up.

## Teaching points
- Never break at the hips: the line from knees to head is straight - glutes and abdominals
  engaged.
- Thigh strength (quadriceps, eccentric) controls the descent.
- Start small: 15-20 degrees of lean; increase over weeks.

## Safety
- Dancers with knee pain (patellofemoral) should avoid deep hinges.
- Always on a sprung floor; avoid hinging when fatigued.

## Faults and fixes
| Fault | Fix |
|---|---|
| Hips bend (sitting back) | squeeze glutes, push hips forward |
| Heels drop | maintain demi-pointe |
| Arching the lower back | abdominal support, straight line |

## Verify
- [ ] Straight line knees-to-head visible side-on.
- [ ] Controlled descent and return at the same speed.
- [ ] No knee pain during or after.
