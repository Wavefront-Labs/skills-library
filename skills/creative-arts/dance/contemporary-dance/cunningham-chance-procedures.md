# Chance procedures (Cunningham)

**Goal:** remove habitual choices from composition by letting chance determine the order,
direction, timing or combination of movement material - then dance the result with full
commitment.

## Method
1. **Build gamuts (charts):** lists of choices for each parameter, e.g.
   - movements (numbered 1-20 short phrases or positions),
   - directions/facings (8),
   - space (stage areas or a grid),
   - durations (counts or seconds),
   - number of dancers (1-6), levels, tempos.
2. **Decide by chance:** coin tosses (Cunningham used the I Ching's 64 hexagrams via coin
   tosses), dice, or a random number generator. Record each result.
3. **Assemble:** write the score - who does which movement, where, facing where, for how long.
4. **Solve the problems:** chance produces awkward transitions; the dancers and choreographer
   find ways to make them physically possible - this is where new movement appears.
5. **Rehearse to fixed form:** in most Cunningham works, chance decides composition but the
   result is set and rehearsed exactly. ("Events" recombine existing material for each
   performance.)

## Teaching points
- Chance applies to choices; the technique demanded is precise.
- Keep a log of decisions so the piece can be restaged.
- Try chance on one parameter first (order only) before all.

## Faults and fixes
| Fault | Fix |
|---|---|
| Overriding chance when results look odd | accept and solve; that's the point |
| Gamut too small | more options give more surprising results |
| Sloppy execution "because it's random" | rehearse to precision |

## Verify
- [ ] Gamuts and decision log documented.
- [ ] Score assembled entirely from recorded chance results.
- [ ] Transitions solved and rehearsed until repeatable.
