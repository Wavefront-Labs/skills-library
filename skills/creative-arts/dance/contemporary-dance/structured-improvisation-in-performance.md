# Structured improvisation in performance

**Goal:** perform work where some parts are improvised within agreed structures, so it is
different every night but always coherent and technically manageable for crew.

## Building the structure
1. **Map the piece:** a running order of sections - set, open (improvised within a score),
   semi-set (fixed material, free order or timing).
2. **Define each open section's score:** space, rules, who is on stage, duration and the
   ending signal.
3. **Cues for crew:** lighting and sound need predictable triggers. Use time (stopwatch), a
   visual dancer cue ("when X lies down centre"), or a stage manager calling on sight.
4. **Safety rules:** no unrehearsed lifts or speed near the edge; known exits.

## On stage
- **Compositional awareness:** watch the whole stage picture - balance space, contrast or
  join what is happening; leave space for others.
- **Clear choices:** commit to an idea for long enough to be readable; avoid random flitting.
- **Listening:** to music, partners and audience energy.
- **Endings:** recognise or create endings; finish sections on the agreed signal.

## Rehearsal
- Run open sections many times with feedback from an outside eye.
- Note what works and add it to the score as optional "anchors".
- Film runs to check overall shape.

## Faults and fixes
| Fault | Fix |
|---|---|
| Everyone busy all the time | include stillness and exits |
| Missed lighting cues | unambiguous cues, rehearsed with crew |
| Sections running long | stopwatch/time limits |

## Verify
- [ ] Running order with each open section's score and ending.
- [ ] Crew cue sheet with improvisation-safe triggers.
- [ ] Two runs within the agreed total duration.
