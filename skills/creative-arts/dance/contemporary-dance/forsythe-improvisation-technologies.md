# Forsythe improvisation technologies

**Goal:** apply William Forsythe's geometric operations to generate movement, either
improvising live or creating material for choreography.

## Core operations
| Operation | What you do |
|---|---|
| **Point-point-line** | imagine a line between two points on/near the body; move the body to trace, follow or fold around it |
| **Inscription** | draw shapes (lines, circles, curves) in space with any body part |
| **Extrusion** | take a shape or line and push it through space (like extruding material) |
| **Dropping curves** | let an imagined curve drop and follow it through the body |
| **Avoidance** | move body parts to avoid imagined lines or shapes |
| **Isometric transfer** | perform the same shape with a different body part or in a different plane |
| **Rotation of planes** | rotate an action around different axes (e.g. a port de bras in the sagittal plane becomes horizontal) |
| **Reorganisation** | take a ballet step and redistribute its components across the body |

## Method
1. Watch the lecture demonstrations (Improvisation Technologies).
2. Practise one operation at a time on simple inputs (a straight line from shoulder to hand).
3. Combine operations: inscribe a circle with the elbow, then extrude it with the hip.
4. Apply to a phrase: take a 8-count phrase and transform it by two operations.

## Teaching points
- Precision in imagination produces precision in movement - define exact points and lines.
- Operations work at any speed; Forsythe's dancers often perform them very fast.
- Ballet-trained dancers: use ballet positions as the input to deconstruct.

## Verify
- [ ] Dancer demonstrates three operations clearly on request.
- [ ] Phrase transformed with two operations, recognisable relationship to the original.
- [ ] Can explain the imagined geometry behind a movement.
