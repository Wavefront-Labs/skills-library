# Floorwork and release-technique fundamentals

**Goal:** move into, along and out of the floor with continuity, using gravity and
momentum rather than muscle — the backbone of most contemporary classes — without
bruising bones or loading joints badly.

> **Status: draft.** Principles below are common across release-based, Limón-derived and
> contemporary floorwork classes. "Release technique" is an umbrella term; teachers vary.
> Check pain-related questions with a dance physio.

## 1. The core idea: give weight, don't brace
- **Release** means letting the floor take your weight rather than lowering yourself with
  tight muscles. Bracing makes you land like a plank: noisy, bruising, slow.
- **Fall, suspend, recover** (Limón/Humphrey): give in to gravity, catch the moment at the
  top of the breath, rebound out. Breath leads — exhale into the fall, inhale to rise.
- **Spiral**: twisting through the spine lets you lower progressively from one surface to
  the next instead of dropping straight down.

## 2. Use the padded surfaces
Land and roll on the **soft, broad** parts of the body: outside of the thigh, side of the
hip/bottom, back of the shoulder blade area, forearm flat. Avoid landing on **bony points**:
kneecaps, hip bones, the point of the elbow, spine tips, the top of the head.

## 3. Basic pathways to practise
1. **Spiral down from standing:** step one foot behind, bend both knees, spiral toward
   the back foot side, lower to the outside of the back thigh, then the hip, then the
   forearm/side. Reverse it to come up.
2. **Side roll (log/pencil roll)** and **curled roll**: roll across the back on the soft
   area beside the spine, not over the vertebrae.
3. **Shoulder roll (back diagonal):** roll over the **back of one shoulder**, head turned
   away to the side — never over the neck. Learn on mats with a teacher.
4. **Rise using momentum:** from sitting, swing the legs (or a spiral) to build energy,
   transfer weight through the hands to the feet, and arrive standing with the motion
   still going. If you have to push hard, the timing is late.
5. **Hand weight-bearing:** fingers spread, weight through the whole palm, elbow soft —
   preparing for inversions and transitions through the hands.

## 4. Sliding and travelling
Sliding on clothes (long sleeves, socks or bare skin depending on the floor) along the
side of the leg or the back. A sprung, clean dance floor matters: dirt and splinters
grab skin, and concrete under vinyl hurts.

## Gotchas
- **Knees:** don't drop onto them; lower through a squat with the shin and foot first.
  Kneepads are normal in floor-heavy rehearsals and auditions (many companies allow them).
- **Shoulders and wrists:** catching a fall on a straight arm transmits the shock to the
  wrist and shoulder. Keep the elbow soft and roll it out.
- **Neck:** no weight on the head in rolls unless specifically trained for it.
- **Muscling through:** if you hear yourself land, there's tension. Exhale and give weight.
- Cold body on a cold floor = strains. Warm up spine and hips before floor sequences.

## Training notes
- Practise slow first: slow floorwork exposes every brace and gap.
- Film yourself: continuous floorwork looks like one breath; jerky floorwork shows exactly
  where you went rigid.
- Contact improvisation classes are excellent for learning to give and receive weight.

## Verify
- [ ] Can spiral to the floor and back to standing on both sides without hands slamming down.
- [ ] Landings and rolls on soft surfaces — no knee, hip-bone, elbow or spine impacts.
- [ ] Shoulder rolls go over the back of the shoulder, head turned, never over the neck.
- [ ] Rises use momentum and arrive standing without a hard push.
- [ ] Movement through the floor is quiet and continuous with breath.
