# Graham contraction and release

**Goal:** the foundational action of Graham technique - a deep, dramatic curving of the spine
initiated from the pelvis and powered by the breath, and its release back to length.

## Contraction
1. Start seated in the Graham first position on the floor (soles of the feet together,
   knees open, spine lifted) or standing.
2. On the **exhale**, the pelvis initiates: the lower abdominals pull back towards the spine
   and the pelvis tilts under; the curve travels up the spine into a C.
3. The back stays **lifted and wide**, not collapsed - the contraction is a powerful hollow,
   with energy shooting up through the back of the head and out through the knees.
4. Shoulders stay down, the neck continues the curve, the eyes look out (not at the floor).

## Release
1. On the **inhale**, the pelvis returns to neutral and the spine lengthens back to vertical
   in sequence from the base, the chest lifts.
2. Release is active - a return to height, not a slump.

## Teaching points
- The breath and pelvis start it; the shoulders and head are the last to move.
- Contraction is not crunching the abdominals forward; it is a hollow with an opposing lift.
- Use percussive (sharp) and sustained contractions as set by the class.

## Faults and fixes
| Fault | Fix |
|---|---|
| Collapsing the chest | lift the back while contracting |
| Starting from the head/shoulders | initiate low in the pelvis |
| Holding breath | exhale into the contraction, inhale to release |
| Shoulders rising | keep them wide and down |

## Verify
- [ ] Contraction visibly initiated from the pelvis.
- [ ] C-curve with a lifted back and steady eyeline.
- [ ] Breath audible/visible in sync with contraction and release.
