# Successional movement

**Goal:** let movement travel through the body joint by joint, like a wave, instead of moving
all parts at once. Central to Limón technique and to most contemporary phrasing.

## Types
- **Successional (sequential):** adjacent joints move one after the other (e.g. head - neck -
  each vertebra - pelvis).
- **Simultaneous:** all parts move at once (a sharp contrast).
- **Isolation:** one part moves while others stay still.

## Exercises
1. **Spinal roll down/up:** release the head, then each vertebra in turn, to a hanging fold;
   rebuild from the pelvis up, the head arriving last.
2. **Arm succession:** initiate from the shoulder blade; the energy travels through upper arm,
   elbow, wrist, fingers. Reverse from the fingertips.
3. **Leg succession:** hip - knee - ankle - toes, e.g. in a développé that unfolds sequentially.
4. **Whole-body wave:** forward and backward waves passing from knees to pelvis to chest to head.

## Teaching points
- Speed varies: a slow succession is lyrical; a fast one becomes a whip.
- Breath carries the succession; keep it continuous, not segmented.
- Pair with opposition: as the torso curves forward, energy flows out the back.

## Faults and fixes
| Fault | Fix |
|---|---|
| Body moves as a block | slow down; name each joint as it moves |
| Rolling up with the head first | build from the pelvis; head last |
| Jerky segments | continuous flow, overlapping timing |

## Verify
- [ ] Spinal roll visibly vertebra by vertebra.
- [ ] Arm succession starts in the back and ends at the fingertips.
- [ ] Dancer can switch between successional and simultaneous on cue.
