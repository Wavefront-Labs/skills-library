# Horton flat backs

**Goal:** a straight, lengthened spine hinged from the hip joints - forward and to the side -
building core strength, hamstring length and the long lines characteristic of Horton.

## Flat back forward
1. Stand in parallel 2nd, feet hip-width (or wider), knees straight but not locked.
2. Arms overhead in a long V or parallel; lengthen up.
3. Hinge from the hips to 90 degrees: the spine, head and arms form a straight line parallel
   to the floor (a table top). Pelvis tips forward; no rounding of the back.
4. Return by hinging back up with the spine straight.

## Flat back side (lateral)
- From parallel 2nd, tilt the straight torso directly sideways from the hip, arms extending
  the line. Both sides of the waist stay long - it is a hinge, not a side bend.

## Deep forward flat back
- Continue past 90 degrees towards the legs while keeping the back flat, then release into a
  hanging position as set.

## Teaching points
- Hamstring range limits depth; bend the knees slightly rather than round the back.
- Engage abdominals to support the spine; the neck stays in line (eyes to the floor).
- Arms extend from the back - they are part of the spine line.

## Faults and fixes
| Fault | Fix |
|---|---|
| Rounded upper back | lower the hinge height, lengthen the crown |
| Arched lower back (sway) | abdominal support |
| Head dropped or lifted | neck in line with spine |
| Locked knees | micro-bend if hyperextended |

## Verify
- [ ] Side view: spine, head and arms in one straight line at 90 degrees.
- [ ] Lateral flat back without side bending.
- [ ] Controlled return to vertical.
