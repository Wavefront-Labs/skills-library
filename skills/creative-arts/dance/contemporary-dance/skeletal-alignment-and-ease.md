# Skeletal alignment and ease

**Goal:** stand and move with the bones carrying the weight and muscles doing only what is
needed - the foundation of release-based contemporary technique.

## Constructive rest (Sweigard)
1. Lie on the back, knees bent and resting against each other, feet on the floor, arms folded
   across the chest (or resting).
2. Do nothing with muscles; let the floor take the weight.
3. Visualise the **lines of movement** (Sweigard's nine lines), e.g. "the spine lengthening
   downward from the head to the tailbone", "the centre of the knee moving forward",
   "the thighbone head sinking into the hip socket".
4. 10-20 minutes; imagery, not active effort, changes the habitual holding patterns.

## Standing alignment with imagery
- Weight through the centres of the feet; imagine the leg bones as columns.
- Pelvis like a bowl balanced on the thigh bones, not tipped.
- Head floating on top of the spine (atlas joint between the ears).
- Release the jaw, shoulders hanging from the spine like a coat on a hanger.

## In class
- Begin with floor work and constructive rest before standing.
- Cue images, not muscle commands ("let the sitting bones widen" vs "squeeze your glutes").
- Use touch (with consent) on bony landmarks to guide awareness.

## Faults and fixes
| Fault | Fix |
|---|---|
| "Placing" with tension | use imagery and breath, reduce effort |
| Sleeping in constructive rest | keep the attention on the image |
| Collapsing (mistaking release for slump) | release is efficient support, not collapse |
