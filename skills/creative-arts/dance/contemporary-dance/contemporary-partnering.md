# Contemporary partnering

**Goal:** perform set partnering material - lifts, catches, counterbalances, contact
transitions - precisely, repeatably and safely, often with roles not tied to gender.

## Learning a partnered phrase
1. Learn each dancer's track separately first (where each body goes).
2. Walk it slowly together, naming **contact points** and **grips** (forearm grip, hand on
   sacrum, shoulder support).
3. Identify **who carries weight when**, and on which count.
4. Run at half speed, then full speed; film and review.

## Principles
- **Shared timing:** breath and plié together; agree on counts or a physical cue (a squeeze, a
  look).
- **Centre to centre:** lifts and supports work best with the partners' centres close.
- **Skeletal support:** stack bones under weight; muscles alone tire fast.
- **Active partners:** the supported dancer stays toned and helps the lift (jump, push).

## Safety
- New lifts with a spotter and on mats if needed.
- Talk: either partner can stop. Note any pain or fear immediately.
- Fatigue kills partnering; place big partnering early in rehearsal.
- Costume test: fabrics can be slippery or snag.

## Faults and fixes
| Fault | Fix |
|---|---|
| Timing mismatch | agree a physical cue, count out loud |
| Grip slipping | change grip (forearm vs hand), use chalk/rosin if allowed |
| Supporter strains the back | get under the partner's centre, use legs |

## Verify
- [ ] Contact points and grips documented for each partnering moment.
- [ ] Phrase repeated identically 3 times.
- [ ] No pain reported; spotter used for new lifts.
