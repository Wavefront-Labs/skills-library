# Graham spiral

**Goal:** a rotation of the torso around the vertical axis of the spine, initiated low and
travelling upward, used throughout the Graham floor work and in standing and travelling phrases.

## Method (seated, in Graham fourth position on the floor)
1. Sit lifted, sitting bones grounded, legs folded with the front leg in front and back leg
   to the side/behind (Graham 4th).
2. **Spiral:** rotate the back towards the back knee (or front, as set) beginning from the
   base of the spine; the rotation travels up through the ribs, shoulders and finally the head.
3. The spine stays **lifted and vertical** throughout - it rotates, it does not lean or
   contract.
4. **Return** in sequence from the base, or release into a contraction as the set phrase
   demands.

## Teaching points
- The pelvis remains anchored to the floor; the spiral is the spine twisting above it.
- Breath: often inhale to lift, exhale into the spiral; follow class phrasing.
- The arms are carried by the back, not leading.
- Combinations: spiral with contraction (the "spiral contraction") is a later step.

## Faults and fixes
| Fault | Fix |
|---|---|
| Leaning sideways | stay vertical, lift through the crown |
| Rotation only in the shoulders | start at the base of the spine |
| Hip lifting off the floor | keep both sitting bones grounded |

## Verify
- [ ] Rotation sequenced from base to head.
- [ ] Spine vertical and lifted throughout.
- [ ] Pelvis stays grounded.
