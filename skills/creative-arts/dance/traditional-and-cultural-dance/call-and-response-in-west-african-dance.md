# Call and response in West African dance

**Goal:** call and response - a leader calls, the group answers - organises song, drumming
and dance across much of West Africa and the diaspora. In dance it shows up as the
singer's call and the dancers' reply, the soloist and the circle, and the way classes are
taught. Using it well keeps dance social rather than a performance at people.

> **Status: draft.** Practice varies by community and occasion; this is the general
> pattern taught in West African dance classes.

## 1. Forms of call and response
- **Song:** a lead singer calls a line, everyone answers with the refrain while dancing.
  Learn the response line; it often marks where the step changes.
- **Drum and dance:** the lead drum's break is a call; the group's step change is the
  answer (see west-african-drum-and-dancer-dialogue).
- **Soloist and circle:** a dancer enters the circle (call), the circle responds by
  clapping, singing, shouting encouragement or copying the move; then the next dancer.
- **Leader and line:** a lead dancer in front of lines performs a move, lines answer with
  the same or a complementary move.

## 2. Running a circle
- Form a circle with drummers at one point of it. Keep clapping or a simple step going
  so the circle stays alive.
- Invite soloists in turn or let them self-select; two at once is common.
- The circle's response matters: cheering, ululation, mirroring. Model it for beginners.
- Close with the break and a final group phrase.

## 3. Teaching by call and response
- Teacher demonstrates a phrase facing or alongside the class, class repeats immediately,
  in time with the drums, with no stopping to explain. Repeat until it lands.
- Break long phrases into a call (first half) and answer (second half) so the group
  learns the structure while moving.
- Keep the drums going between repeats; silence breaks the energy.

## 4. On stage
Stage the circle as an arc open to the audience so soloists and responders both read.
Leave space for real response from the cast; planned shouts at the same place every
night start to look mechanical.

## Gotchas
- Treating the response as decoration and leaving the group silent and still.
- Soloists ignoring the drummers and the circle.
- Explaining at length instead of letting the class learn by answering.

## Verify
- [ ] Dancers know the song response and where step changes fall.
- [ ] Circle keeps a live response going during every solo.
- [ ] Class learns a phrase by repeat-back with drums continuous.
