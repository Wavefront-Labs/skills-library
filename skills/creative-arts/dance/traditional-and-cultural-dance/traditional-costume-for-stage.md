# Traditional costume for the stage

**Goal:** traditional dress carries meaning - region, village, marital status, occasion,
rank. Stage versions must keep those signifiers so the costume tells the truth, while
surviving lights, sweat, quick changes and touring.

> **Status: draft.** General costume practice for folkloric staging; each tradition has
> its own codes and specialist makers. Confirm details with tradition-bearers.

## 1. Research the signifiers
For each garment record: region and era, who wears it (age, gender, marital status,
occasion), colours and their meaning, embroidery motifs, how it is put on and tied,
jewellery and headdress, and the footwear. Photograph originals with permission, front,
back and detail.

## 2. Decide what to keep
Agree with consultants which features are essential (motifs, colours, silhouette, how a
scarf is tied) and which can be changed for the stage (fabric weight, closures, lining,
hem length). Do not mix regions in one costume unless the piece says so.

## 3. Make it danceable
- **Movement test:** full range in the actual choreography - turns, kicks, floor work,
  partnering. Lift arms overhead; skirt hem and sleeves must not catch.
- **Fabric:** lighter or stretch-backed versions of heavy wool or velvet; breathable
  under lights. Stage-weight embroidery or printed copies for heavy originals.
- **Closures:** hooks, poppers or Velcro hidden under traditional ties for quick changes.
- **Headdresses and jewellery:** secured with pins, elastic or sewn to a cap; nothing
  that can fly off into the audience or a partner's face; sharp edges covered.
- **Skirts with turns:** check what the skirt does in a spin - lift and fullness are
  often part of the dance (folklórico, sevillanas).

## 4. Footwear
Shoes affect technique and sound: tap-nailed or hard shoes for footwork forms, specific
soft boots or sandals for others, barefoot where tradition requires. Match the floor (a
footwork form on marley needs a board). Rehearse in show shoes early.

## 5. Care and touring
Heritage originals stay off stage or are worn only for short display. Label every piece,
keep a costume bible with photos of correct dressing, and allow for washing and drying
between shows.

## Gotchas
- Generic "national costume" that no region would recognise.
- First wearing at dress rehearsal; something always catches.
- Jewellery noise drowning footwork or getting caught in partnering.

## Verify
- [ ] Signifiers documented and approved by consultants.
- [ ] Costume tested in the full choreography, including turns and floor work.
- [ ] Quick changes timed and achievable.
- [ ] Dressing guide with photos exists for touring.
