# Dancer and drum dialogue in West African dance

**Goal:** in Mande (Guinea, Mali, Senegal, Côte d'Ivoire) and related West African forms
the dance and the drums are one conversation. The lead drummer watches the dancer and
plays their movements; the dancer listens for the signals that change the step. Dancing
to the rhythm without that exchange misses the form.

> **Status: draft.** Drawn from the djembe-ensemble tradition as taught in diaspora
> classes. Rhythms (Kuku, Lamban, Sinte, Dununba, Soli...) each have their own steps,
> breaks and meanings; learn them from a teacher of that lineage.

## 1. Know the ensemble
- **Dundun family**: three bass drums - **dundunba** (lowest), **sangban** (middle),
  **kenkeni** (highest, the steady timekeeper) - often with bells. Together they give the
  rhythm its identity; the sangban part tells you which rhythm it is.
- **Accompanying djembes**: fixed interlocking parts.
- **Lead (solo) djembe**: plays the break (call), improvises solos and "plays the dancer".

## 2. Listen for the break
- The **break (call)** is a fixed phrase the lead plays to start the piece, end it, or
  change section. Dancers learn it like a word: hearing it means "change step now" or
  "stop".
- Count from the break, not from the first downbeat you think you hear: the downbeat of a
  West African rhythm is often not where a European ear expects it.
- Step changes in class are usually cued by the break, sometimes repeated a set number of
  times ("four times, then change").

## 3. Solo in front of the drums
- In a circle or line, a dancer comes forward to solo. The lead djembe **follows and
  accents** the dancer's movements - jumps, stamps, shoulder hits.
- To end a solo, dancers often give a clear physical cue (a final accent, a turn toward
  the drums, a stamp) that the lead answers with a closing phrase.
- Make eye contact with the lead drummer; acknowledge the drums before and after.

## 4. In class and on stage
- Learn the **song** too where there is one; singing locates the rhythm.
- Rehearse with the actual drummers. A recording cannot answer you.
- On stage, place the drummers where dancers can see the lead (often upstage or a side
  arc); a drummer who cannot see the dancer cannot accompany them.

## Gotchas
- Dancing on top of the drums as if to a fixed track: no exchange, no form.
- Missing the break because you counted from the wrong pulse.
- Turning your back on the drums for a whole solo.
- Not paying or crediting the drummers as artists equal to the dancers.

## Verify
- [ ] You can recognise the sangban pattern and the break of the rhythm you dance.
- [ ] Step changes land on the break, not late.
- [ ] Solo ends with a cue the lead drummer answers.
- [ ] Drummers placed with clear sightline to the dancers.
