# Working with live musicians

**Goal:** most traditional dance is made with live music - the dancer and musicians
respond to each other. Bringing them onto a stage takes planning so the exchange survives
the lights, the distance and the sound system.

> **Status: draft.** General staging practice across flamenco, Irish, Indian classical,
> West African and folkloric forms; follow each form's own conventions on leading.

## 1. Agree who leads
In some forms the dancer leads and musicians follow (a flamenco dancer's llamada, the
lead djembe following the soloist); in others the music leads and dancers follow (many
social and ensemble dances). Write it down per section so nobody waits for the other.

## 2. Map the structure
Make a shared running order: sections, repeats, number of bars or cycles, tempos, cues
(visual or musical), and endings. Mark which sections are fixed and which are open for
improvisation, and how an open section ends (a signal, a set number of repeats).

## 3. Rehearse together early
- At least some full rehearsals with the musicians before tech, not only on the day.
- Set tempos together; record a metronome reference for each piece and agree a range
  (live energy pushes tempos up).
- If you must rehearse to recordings, record the actual musicians.

## 4. Place them on stage
- Musicians need to see the dancers' feet and faces and the dancers need to see the lead
  musician: common options are an upstage arc, a side platform, or on stage as part of
  the scene.
- Leave clear lanes so dancers never collide with instruments or cables.
- Light them enough to read cues; agree whether they are part of the stage picture.

## 5. Sound
- Sound check with dancers moving: footwork forms need floor mics or a miked board;
  balance between drums, voice and feet.
- Musicians need monitors to hear each other and the feet; dancers may need foldback.

## 6. Contracts and credit
Musicians are artists, not accompaniment: fee for rehearsals and shows, names in the
programme, a bow.

## Gotchas
- Tempos at the show far faster than rehearsal; agree a signal to pull back.
- Musicians placed where they cannot see the dancer they follow.
- No plan for how an improvised section ends.

## Verify
- [ ] Running order with leader, cues and endings for each section shared.
- [ ] Rehearsals with musicians held before tech.
- [ ] Sightlines checked in both directions.
- [ ] Sound check done with dancers moving.
