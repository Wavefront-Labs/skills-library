# Polyrhythmic movement in West African dance

**Goal:** in many West African forms the body carries several rhythms at once - feet on
one, shoulders or hips on another, arms on a third - answering different drums in the
ensemble. Scholars call this polyrhythm and polycentrism (several centres of movement in
the body). It is the main step up from "doing the steps" to dancing the music.

> **Status: draft.** Based on the descriptions in Welsh-Asante and Chernoff and on
> common teaching practice. Each dance's layering is specific; learn it in context.

## 1. Find the layers in the music
- **Pulse:** the steady beat you could walk to (in 12/8 rhythms, four dotted-quarter beats
  per cycle).
- **Timeline:** the bell or kenkeni pattern that repeats and orients everyone, often a
  7-stroke 12/8 pattern or similar asymmetric figure.
- **Drum parts:** dundun and djembe parts that fit between each other.
Learn to clap the pulse while singing the timeline before adding movement.

## 2. Assign body parts to layers
1. Feet on the pulse (step-touch or a basic travelling step). Make this automatic.
2. Add one centre on another layer: shoulders on the off-beats, or the hips on a
   triplet feel, or arms on the timeline.
3. Only when two layers run without thinking, add a third (head, chest or hands).
Practise each layer alone, then pairs, then the full body.

## 3. Cross-rhythm drills
- **3 against 2:** feet step in 2 while hands clap 3 over the same span. Say "pass the
  gol-den but-ter" or count 1-2-3-4-5-6 with the 2s on 1 and 4 and the 3s on 1, 3, 5.
- **12/8 duple-triple:** the same bar felt as four groups of three or six groups of two;
  shift the arms between the two feels while the feet stay.

## 4. Polycentrism
Movement can start from different centres - pelvis, chest, shoulders, head - and centres
can move independently. Isolations practice (from jazz or classes) helps, but keep the
movement connected to the ground and the drums, not as isolated tricks.

## Gotchas
- Feet drifting off the pulse once the upper body starts a second layer: drop back to
  feet only and rebuild.
- Counting everything in straight 4/4 when the rhythm is in 12/8.
- Doing layers mechanically with a tense face and torso; aim for ease and play.

## Verify
- [ ] Can clap the pulse and sing the timeline of the rhythm.
- [ ] Feet hold the pulse while shoulders or arms keep a second pattern for a full phrase.
- [ ] 3-against-2 drill clean at slow and medium tempo.
