# West African dance posture: grounded stance

**Goal:** much West African dance is earth-directed - the body moves toward and with the
ground rather than lifting away from it as in ballet. Getting the base posture right is
what makes the steps look and feel correct, and what keeps knees and back safe.

> **Status: draft.** Posture varies between regions and dances (some Senegalese sabar
> movement is very upright and fast; many Guinean dances are deeply bent). This is the
> common class base, not a rule for every form.

## 1. The base
- **Feet:** parallel, about hip width, whole foot on the floor; weight through the heels
  and balls together. Barefoot is usual.
- **Knees:** softly bent, tracking over the second and third toes. Never locked.
- **Hips:** hinge forward at the hip joint so the torso inclines; the back stays long, not
  rounded. The pelvis is free, not tucked.
- **Torso:** responsive - it will undulate, contract and pulse with the rhythm. Do not
  brace the abdomen rigidly.
- **Head and gaze:** neck long; gaze forward or toward the drums, not down at the feet.
- **Arms:** free and full-range, often driving the movement; shoulders loose.

## 2. Working the ground
- Push into the floor on stamps and steps ("give your weight to the earth"); the rebound
  feeds the next move.
- Bounce comes from knees and ankles in time with the pulse, small and continuous.
- Stay low through travelling steps; avoid popping up between counts.

## 3. Safety
- Deep knee bend for a long class loads knees and quads: build depth gradually, keep the
  knee over the foot, and come out of the hinge between pieces.
- Hinge from the hips, not by rounding the lower back; strengthen glutes and hamstrings.
- Barefoot on a hard floor: a sprung floor is better; warm up the feet and calves.

## 4. Coming from ballet or contemporary
Release the pulled-up, turned-out habit: parallel feet, weight down, chest not lifted and
held. The common correction for trained dancers is "lower, looser, give it to the floor".

## Gotchas
- Bending only the knees with an upright torso: looks stiff, overloads the knees.
- Rounding the spine instead of hinging at the hip.
- Locking the upper body while the legs work; the torso should answer the rhythm.

## Verify
- [ ] Knees soft and tracking over toes throughout a phrase.
- [ ] Torso hinged from the hip with a long back.
- [ ] Weight audibly and visibly into the floor on accents.
- [ ] Gaze up and out, not at the floor.
