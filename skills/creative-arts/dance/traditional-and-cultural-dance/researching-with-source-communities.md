# Researching with source communities

**Goal:** a traditional dance belongs to people. Before staging, teaching or adapting it,
work with the community that holds it so you learn it accurately and use it with consent.
This is the research stage that comes before staging-a-traditional-dance-for-an-audience.

> **Status: draft.** Reflects the UNESCO 2003 Convention and its Ethical Principles for
> Safeguarding Intangible Cultural Heritage (communities' central role, free prior and
> informed consent, fair benefit, respect for customary restrictions). Local law and
> community rules apply first.

## 1. Find the right people
- Start with community organisations, cultural associations, recognised teachers and
  elders rather than the first video online.
- Ask who has the authority to teach this dance and to agree to its staging. In some
  traditions that is a lineage holder, a family or a council.
- Expect several voices; regions and families disagree on the "right" version.

## 2. Agree terms before taking anything
Discuss and write down (letter or simple agreement):
- **Purpose:** what you want to do (class, performance, tour, film) and for whom.
- **Consent:** free, prior and informed - they know the uses, can say no, and can withdraw.
- **Restrictions:** sacred, gendered, seasonal or initiation-only material that must not be
  shown; who may perform which roles.
- **Recording:** whether you may film or photograph; who keeps copies; whether recordings
  may be published.
- **Payment:** fees for teaching, consulting, music and travel at professional rates.
- **Credit:** names, communities and titles as they want them in programmes and media.

## 3. Doing the research
- Learn in context: attend social occasions or classes where invited, not only private
  sessions.
- Record context, not only steps: occasion, meaning of gestures, music, costume, who dances.
- Keep field notes with dates, names and permission given for each recording.
- Learn the music and song texts; ask for translations and their meaning.

## 4. Check back
Show the staged draft to the consultants before it is public. Agree changes. After the
run, share recordings and press as agreed and keep the relationship.

## Gotchas
- Treating one informant as speaking for a whole community.
- Recording first and asking later.
- Unpaid "consultation" over coffee.
- Publishing restricted material because it was available online.

## Verify
- [ ] Authority to teach and approve identified and involved.
- [ ] Written terms covering purpose, consent, restrictions, recording, pay and credit.
- [ ] Field notes record permission per recording.
- [ ] Consultants have seen and approved the staged version.
