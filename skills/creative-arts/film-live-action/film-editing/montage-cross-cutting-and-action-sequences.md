# Montage, cross-cutting and action sequences

**Goal:** sequences where the *cutting itself* is the storytelling - time compressed
into meaning, two places made one event, a fight made legible and felt.

## 1. Montage (time and meaning)
Montage compresses a process (training, falling in love, a season) or juxtaposes images
to create a meaning neither carries alone (Kuleshov/Eisenstein).
- Decide the **arc** of the montage before cutting: beginning state, change, end state.
  Order shots so something *progresses* (skill improves, days get darker) - a list of
  pretty shots is a slideshow.
- Pick a **rhythmic spine**: music (cut on phrase, not every beat), a sound motif, or a
  visual rule (every shot starts on a hand). Vary shot length inside the rule.
- Entry and exit matter most: enter the montage on a motivated cut from the scene before
  (a look, a sound), exit onto a shot that restarts real time (someone speaks).
- 20-45 seconds is typical; longer needs internal structure (two movements).
- Graphic matches (shape, movement, colour) across cuts make a montage feel designed.

## 2. Cross-cutting / parallel action
Intercut two or more lines of action to create **suspense** (the bomb and the dinner),
**comparison** (two lives), or **convergence** (they will meet).
- Establish each line clearly before intercutting fast; the audience needs to know the
  geography and the stakes of both.
- **Rhythm of alternation**: segments shorten as the lines converge (classic Griffith
  acceleration); vary lengths so it isn't mechanical.
- Cut on **questions**: leave line A on an unresolved beat (hand reaching for the
  door) and return to it only after line B raised its own. Each return should answer
  and pose.
- Keep **time logic** plausible: the audience assumes simultaneity; if line A is
  minutes and line B hours, bridge with ellipsis cues.
- Sound bridges (a phone ringing in A heard in B) stitch lines together; a shared score
  cue signals convergence.

## 3. Action: fights and chases
- **Geography first**: a wide or a clear establishing beat tells the audience who is
  where and which direction "escape" is. Reinforce it after every big change.
- **Screen direction**: the pursuer and pursued travel the same direction across cuts;
  a reversal means a reversal in the story (turned around, cornered). Head-on and
  tail-away neutral shots are your reset tools.
- **Cut on impacts**: hit frames - cut a frame or two *before* the punch lands in the
  incoming shot so the impact plays in the new angle with its sound; the wind-up in one
  angle, the hit and reaction in another. Sound sells the hit, not the frame.
- **Shot size rhythm**: wide for choreography, medium for exchanges, tight for pain and
  decision; return to the wide often enough that the audience never loses the dance.
- **Duration**: action shots run short (8-20 frames) but the clearest shots are held
  longer; incomprehensible speed is just noise. Audience must read *cause -> effect*.
- **Speed ramps / overcrank**: use for the one beat that needs weight; constant slow-mo
  drains tension.
- Stunt doubles: cut around the face swap - tight on the double's back, wide on the
  actor's face.
- Build in **breaths**: a beat of stillness (a look, reload, a fallen weapon) resets
  the audience before the next burst.

## 4. Sound leads the cut
In all three, cut the **sound first** (or at least with) the picture: a music spine, the
sync of impacts, a rising ambience. Temp with real sound design early; action cut mute
is always cut wrong.

## Gotchas
- Montage of equal-length shots on every music beat - the music video trap.
- Intercutting before either line is established; the audience waits to understand instead of fearing.
- Fights where the geography is never re-established and the audience stops caring who's winning.
- Cutting away from the impact instead of into it.
- Speed-ramping everything because the footage allows it.

## Verify
- [ ] Montage has a stated arc, a rhythmic rule and motivated entry/exit.
- [ ] Cross-cut lines are each established before alternation; segments shorten toward convergence.
- [ ] Action geography and screen direction readable after every cut; impacts cut into, not away.
- [ ] Sound/music spine laid before fine-cutting the picture.
