# Restructuring, screenings and notes

**Goal:** when a cut "doesn't work" and trims won't fix it, change the order, lose what
doesn't belong, and learn from an audience before the film is locked - without losing a
version that was better.

## 1. Diagnose before you move anything
- Watch the whole cut and write down, per scene, what the audience *knows* and *wants* at
  the end of it. Gaps between what we want them to feel and what the cut delivers point
  to structure (information arrives too early/late, a question never posed) rather than
  to execution.
- Build **scene cards** (index cards or a spreadsheet/Avid bin in "script" order): scene
  number, one line, running time, what it sets up, what it pays off, who's in it. Lay them
  out on a wall or a board; colour-code storylines.
- Mark each card: *essential / useful / nice*. Anything in "nice" is a lift candidate.
- Running time versus genre expectation is a structural fact, not a trim problem: a
  130-minute comedy is wrong by 25 minutes of scenes, not 25 minutes of air.

## 2. Try the reorder on paper first
Move cards, not clips: swap act-two sequences, bring a reveal forward, delay an
explanation, open on a later scene. For each candidate, trace the consequences:
- what information is now missing (a line referencing something not yet seen);
- what emotional beat loses its setup;
- costume/time-of-day/location continuity across the new join (a "night" scene next to
  the "same day" scene);
- whether a bridging shot or ADR line is needed.
Then build it in the timeline from the **paper cut**, quickly and roughly, and watch it
through. Don't fine-cut an experiment.

## 3. Lifts and the lift bin
Remove whole scenes to see what's missed. Keep every lift in a **lifts bin** with the
version it came from; several come back in a different place. A scene that nobody misses
after a week is gone. Also try the opposite: a scene the editor hates but the structure
needs.

## 4. Versioning so nothing is lost
`FILM_v12_2026-10-15` style sequence names; duplicate before every structural experiment;
a changelog text file (what changed, why, running time); never work in the only copy of
a cut. Export a reference movie of every screened version. Resolve/Premiere/Avid all
make this cheap - the discipline is the thing.

## 5. Screenings
- **Friends-and-family / filmmaker screenings** (early): 6-15 people who know story; watch
  the room (where phones come out, where laughs don't land). Short discussion after,
  led by the director: "where were you confused? bored? what did you think the film was
  about?" Not "what would you change".
- **Test screenings** (later, larger, recruited audience): questionnaires with 1-5 scales
  on overall, start/middle/end, characters, pace, "would recommend", plus free text and
  "scenes you liked least". A facilitated focus group of 15-20 afterwards. Ignore
  individual fixes; look for **clusters** (if 40% say the middle dragged, it did).
- Screen a cut with temp sound/music and a near-final grade when possible; audiences
  rate unfinished sound as "boring".
- Before any screening, decide what you want to learn and note your own predictions.
  The gap between prediction and result is the information.

## 6. Turning notes into a plan
Notes from producers, financiers, directors and audiences will conflict. Sort them:
*symptom* (what they felt - always valid) vs *prescription* (their fix - optional).
Group symptoms by location in the film; find the structural cause that explains the
most symptoms; propose one change per cause and test it. Present the plan with the
expected effect and the cost (days). "Note taken, not addressed, because..." is a valid line.

## Gotchas
- Fine-cutting scenes that will be lifted.
- Restructuring in the timeline without a paper pass - the cut becomes a mess of half-versions.
- Treating every note as a mandate, or none of them.
- Test-screening a cut with scratch sound and no music and reading the results literally.
- Not exporting the previous version before the "big" change.

## Verify
- [ ] Scene cards exist with runtimes and set-up/pay-off; lift candidates marked.
- [ ] Each reorder tested on paper for information/continuity gaps before building.
- [ ] Every screened version exported and logged with running time.
- [ ] Notes sorted into symptoms vs prescriptions; plan lists cause -> change -> expected effect.
