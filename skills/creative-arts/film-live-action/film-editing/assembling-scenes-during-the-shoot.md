# Assembling scenes during the shoot

**Goal:** within a few days of wrap, a complete assembly of the film exists; during the
shoot, set learns quickly about anything missing.

## 1. Keep up with camera
Cut each scene within 1-3 days of its dailies arriving ("**keeping up to camera**"). Fall
behind and you lose the chance to flag problems while the set/actors are still available.

## 2. Assembling a scene
- Cut **to the script** - include every scripted line and moment, even if you'd cut it
  later; the assembly shows what was shot.
- Use the best performances and readable coverage; don't over-polish or add elaborate
  sound/music yet (temp effects/music only where the scene needs it to play).
- Leave a slightly long, complete version; note alternatives with markers.
- Name sequences by scene number and version (`Sc024_v01_assembly`).

## 3. Feedback to set
Report to the director/producers (and the script supervisor) promptly:
- **Coverage gaps**: a missing reaction, an insert needed for clarity, a cutaway to bridge a
  jump.
- **Technical problems**: soft focus, boom in shot, a continuity error that won't cut.
- **Things that work** too - set morale matters.
Keep it brief and specific (scene, set-up, what's needed). Pick-up shots are cheap while
the location and cast are there.

## 4. The assembly
- Join scene assemblies in script order; add scene markers and temp titles.
- **Length**: assemblies typically run long - 30-50% over the target (a 100-minute film
  may assemble at 2:30+). That's normal; it's a document of the shoot.
- Watch it alone first; fix only glaring issues.

## 5. Showing the assembly
Under DGA rules the editor's assembly is delivered to the director (who may choose
not to watch with others); it is not a cut for producers. Set expectations: long, rough,
temp sound. Screen it in one sitting if possible; take notes on the director's
reactions, not just their comments.

## Gotchas
- Falling behind camera; gaps discovered after wrap.
- Cutting scenes down to your ideal in the assembly - the director can't see what was shot.
- Showing the assembly to producers before the director.
- Panicking at the assembly's length.

## Verify
- [ ] Scenes cut within days of dailies; versioned sequences.
- [ ] Coverage and technical gaps reported to set promptly.
- [ ] Full assembly in script order with markers.
- [ ] Assembly shown to the director first, with notes taken.
