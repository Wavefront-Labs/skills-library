# Locations, permits and the tech scout

**Goal:** locations that serve the scene, can be lit and recorded, are legal to shoot in,
and are handed back in the state you found them - with the paperwork that keeps the
insurer and the council happy.

## 1. Scouting against needs
From the breakdown, write a **location brief** per set: script requirements (period,
window direction for the "morning" scene, a staircase), scene count and shoot days,
night/day, crew size and parking, special needs (water access, drone, vehicles).
On the scout (bring the director/DP later, not first):
- Photograph with a wide lens from the corners and a 360° pano; note **compass direction**
  and use a sun app (Sun Seeker, Helios) to know where the sun is at the planned call time.
- **Listen** for 5 minutes: traffic, flight paths, HVAC, schools, church bells, construction.
  Ask what's scheduled (bin day, markets, roadworks).
- Power: available amps, where the breaker is, or where a **generator** can sit (noise distance).
- Access: load-in path, lift sizes, stairs, truck parking, unit base within walking distance,
  holding areas for cast/extras, toilets, catering space.
- Ownership: who can sign, who the neighbours are, residents' associations, listed status.
Shortlist with photos, pros/cons and estimated fee for the director/DP/designer.

## 2. Permits and permissions
- **Public land / streets**: apply to the city film office (FilmLA, Film London boroughs,
  Screen Scotland, etc.) typically 5-10 working days ahead; longer for road closures,
  police, drones, pyro or large units. Permit fees scale with unit size and impact.
- **Private property**: a **location agreement** (contract): dates (prep/shoot/strike),
  fee, hours, areas included, right to photograph and use the images in perpetuity,
  restoration terms, cancellation and weather-day terms, insurance requirements (they'll
  ask for a certificate naming them as additional insured), indemnity, who's the contact.
- **Specials**: drones (licensed pilot, airspace permission), firearms (notify police with
  times, even replicas), animals, children, water, night shooting (noise curfews), road
  closures (traffic management plan), railways and airports (their own film units).
- **Neighbour letters** 7+ days ahead for residential streets: dates, hours, what to expect
  (lights, generator, parking suspension), a phone number. Courtesy payments where usual.
- Carry copies of permits and the insurance certificate on set; authorities ask.

## 3. The tech scout (tech recce)
One visit, all HODs, one or two weeks before the shoot, in shooting order:
1st AD leads; director describes the blocking per scene; DP states camera positions,
lighting and power needs; gaffer/key grip plan rigs and cable runs; sound flags noise;
designer notes dressing and what gets removed; location manager confirms boundaries,
parking, no-go areas, neighbour deals; PM notes costs. Output: a **tech scout report**
per location (map with unit base, parking, generator, holding, toilets, circuits;
per-scene notes), and a prep/strike schedule.

## 4. On the day and after
Floor protection, corner guards, furniture photographed before moving (for reset),
"no smoking" and clean-up crew, a locations assistant at the door. Walk-through with
the owner at wrap; sign off restoration. Pay invoices and send thank-you notes - you'll
want the location (and the film office) again.

## Gotchas
- Scouting at the wrong time of day; the window light is gone at call.
- Verbal agreements with the owner's son. Get the signatory and a signed contract.
- Permit covers the sidewalk, not the parked trucks - parking suspensions are separate.
- Generator placed by the sound mixer's worst nightmare (under the window).
- No weather cover location booked for the exterior day.

## Verify
- [ ] Each location has a brief, scout photos with compass, sound notes and power plan.
- [ ] Signed location agreement and permits in hand before the tech scout.
- [ ] Insurance certificate naming the owner issued; neighbour letters delivered.
- [ ] Tech scout report distributed; restoration signed off at wrap.
