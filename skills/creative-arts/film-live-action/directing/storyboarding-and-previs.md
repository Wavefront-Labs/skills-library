# Storyboarding and previs

**Goal:** make the expensive decisions on paper. Boards and previs exist to find shots that
don't work, to tell forty departments the same thing, and to estimate time - not to
decorate the production office.

## 1. What to board
Board what cannot be improvised on the day: action, stunts, VFX, crowd scenes, complex
camera moves, montages, anything with a split screen or a rig. Dialogue scenes usually need
an overhead and a shot list, not boards. A full-film board is a luxury; a boarded
third act is often the difference between finishing and not.

## 2. Panel conventions
- Frame in the **delivery aspect ratio** (1.85, 2.39, 16:9). Draw a frame template.
- One panel per shot; extra panels for a change *within* the shot (move, reframe).
- **Arrows**: subject movement = arrow on the subject; camera movement = arrow outside
  the frame or a second "end frame" panel linked by an arrow. Push-in/pull-out: a smaller
  inner frame with arrows to the corners.
- Under each panel: shot number (match the shot list), size (WS/MS/CU), lens if decided,
  camera move (static, pan, dolly in, Steadicam), one line of action, dialogue cue.
- Number panels `12A, 12B...` for shot 12 - renumbering when a shot is cut is pain.
- Stick figures are fine. The information is composition, size, direction and eyeline.

## 3. Tools
- **Paper / iPad** (Procreate with a frame template) - fastest for a director's own boards.
- **Storyboarder** (free) - panels, timing, exports to PDF and animatic.
- **Photoboards** - shoot stills on location with the lens app (Artemis, Cadrage) and
  stand-ins; most accurate for real locations.
- **3D previs**: FrameForge (lens-accurate, floorplans), Blender, Unreal Engine (for VFX-heavy
  work, shared with the VFX supervisor). Previs lenses and camera heights should be real
  values so the result translates to set.
- **Animatic**: boards cut in the NLE with temp audio and intended durations. This is the
  only way to test pace and running time before shooting.

## 4. What boards feed
- **Shot list and schedule**: count setups per boarded sequence; the 1st AD times the day.
- **VFX**: plate requirements, where the greenscreen edge is, what's practical vs digital.
- **Stunts/SFX**: the stunt coordinator breaks the sequence from boards and may redesign
  for safety - treat their version as the shooting version.
- **Art department and locations**: what direction the set must dress for.
- Shoot the **animatic timing**: if a board shot holds 3 s in the animatic, that's the
  target on set; overcover is wasted light.

## Gotchas
- Boards drawn with impossible lenses (a close-up with wide-angle background) - previs with
  real focal lengths or photoboard on location.
- Boarding every dialogue scene and then ignoring the boards on the day; crew stops trusting them.
- Panels without eyelines or screen direction - the main thing boards are for.
- Not updating boards after the tech scout changes the set; departments build the old version.

## Verify
- [ ] Every action/VFX/stunt sequence has boards numbered to the shot list.
- [ ] Panels carry size, move, direction and eyeline; delivery aspect ratio used.
- [ ] An animatic exists for the sequences with timing-critical cuts.
- [ ] Stunt/VFX supervisors signed off their sequences from the boards.
