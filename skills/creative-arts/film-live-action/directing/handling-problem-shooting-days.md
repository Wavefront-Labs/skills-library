# Handling problem shooting days

**Goal:** when the plan breaks, still finish the day with the scenes the film most
needs - and protect tomorrow.

## 1. Plan for it in prep
- **Cover sets**: interior scenes held ready (dressed, cast available on standby) for
  exterior days at weather risk. The schedule marks them; call sheets list "weather cover".
- **Priorities**: for each day, the 1st AD and director agree A/B/C shots.
- **Simplified versions** of complex scenes (a oner that can become 3 shots, a master
  that can be dropped).

## 2. Decision order when behind
1. **Combine set-ups**: cover two beats from one position; cross-shoot with two cameras.
2. **Drop C-priority** shots, then B.
3. **Rethink coverage**: a developing master or a oner instead of full coverage.
4. **Move a scene** to another day (AD and producers - checks cast and location
   availability).
5. **Cut or rewrite** the scene with the writer/producer (last resort).
Decide by lunch, not at wrap. Never sacrifice the key performance close-up to save a
transitional wide.

## 3. Specific problems
- **Weather**: rain on an exterior - go to cover set, or embrace it if continuity allows
  (rain covers whole scenes). Wind kills sound - plan ADR or move to tighter shots.
- **Losing light**: shoot the sun-dependent reverse first; plan the "golden hour" shots
  with the DP; prepare lighting to match the late side; use the last light on wides.
- **Sick or absent actor**: shoot their scenes' reverse angles with a stand-in reading,
  their doubles in wides; reshuffle the day.
- **Equipment failure**: second body / backup kit; simplify moves (dolly to handheld).
- **Location loss**: production has a backup; rewrite blocking to the new space.

## 4. Communicate
The 1st AD announces changes to the crew; the director tells actors personally about
script or scene changes. Producers are informed immediately of any schedule change.
Keep calm visibly - the set takes its mood from the director.

## Gotchas
- Pressing on with the original shot list until the last hour, then dropping a whole scene.
- Cover sets not dressed or cast not on standby, so they can't actually be used.
- Overtime decisions made without producers.
- Blaming crew in front of everyone.

## Verify
- [ ] Cover sets and A/B/C priorities in the schedule.
- [ ] Progress check at lunch; decisions taken early.
- [ ] Specific contingency for weather, light, cast and kit known.
- [ ] Changes communicated through the AD and to producers.
