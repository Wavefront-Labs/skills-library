# Working with experienced actors and stars

**Goal:** a collaboration where the actor trusts that you see them, protect the film,
and will make them look good - so they give you their best instead of protecting themselves.

## 1. Earn trust in prep
- Meet before the deal closes if possible; talk about the **character and the film**,
  not logistics. Show you've thought hard about their role specifically.
- Ask how they work: rehearsal or not, takes, how they like notes, what they need on set.
- Share your **vision** clearly and briefly - stars commit to directors who know what
  they want.

## 2. Their ideas and script notes
Experienced actors often have script notes. Listen fully; separate the **feeling** from
the suggested fix (as with any note). Take good ideas openly and credit them. For notes
you decline, explain the **story reason** privately. Bring the writer in early on
significant character changes. Contracts may give approvals (script, stills, hair/make-
up, co-stars) - know exactly what they're entitled to via producers.

## 3. On set
- **Notes privately**, at their side, short and specific. Never in front of crew or
  co-stars. Never through an AD or the monitor speaker.
- Give them freedom early (let them show you their take first); then adjust.
- Watch for "one for me" requests - give it; it costs one take and buys trust; then
  "one for me" back.
- Hold the line on things that matter to the film (tone, continuity, pace), concede on
  things that don't.
- Protect equal treatment of co-stars and day players; the set watches.

## 4. Protocol and logistics
Producers manage the deal; ADs manage their time (trailer calls, set calls, breaks);
first-team-only rehearsals when they arrive; stand-ins for lighting. Respect
turnaround times in their contract. Don't keep them waiting on set unnecessarily - it's
expensive and erodes goodwill.

## 5. When there's conflict
Talk privately, early. Ask questions ("what's bothering you about the scene?") before
asserting. If a conflict persists, involve the producer - never argue on set.

## Gotchas
- Deferring entirely - the film loses its director; stars notice.
- Over-explaining craft to someone with 40 years' experience; ask instead.
- Notes given within earshot of others.
- Unequal treatment that alienates the rest of the cast.

## Verify
- [ ] Working preferences and contractual approvals known before day one.
- [ ] Script notes discussed and resolved with the writer before shooting.
- [ ] Notes given privately; "one for me" handled.
- [ ] Conflicts raised privately and early.
