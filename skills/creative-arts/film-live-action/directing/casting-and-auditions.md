# Casting and auditions

**Goal:** choose actors who can *do the role on the day*, not actors who gave the best
first read - and build the relationship that makes directing them on set possible.

## 1. Breakdown
For each role: name, age range (playing, not actual), one line of who they are, the
role's function in the story, size (lead / supporting / day player / featured), shoot
dates and location, pay/contract type, any specific requirements (language, skill, nudity,
stunts) stated upfront. Avoid physical descriptors that aren't story-essential; describe
quality ("carries authority without raising her voice"). Post via a casting director or
breakdown services (Spotlight, Backstage, Casting Networks, Mandy).

## 2. Sides
Pick two contrasting scenes per role, 1-3 pages each: one where the character wants
something badly, one that shows the opposite register. Avoid the climactic monologue - it
tests nerve, not range. Send sides at least 48 h before; include a short note on tone and
the "world" so self-tapes aren't guessing.

## 3. Self-tapes (first round)
- Ask for: slate (name, height, agent), the scene read with an off-camera reader, mid-shot,
  neutral background, decent sound. Don't punish production value.
- Watch the first 20 seconds of each tape for the shortlist, then watch the shortlist whole.
  Listen for whether they *listen* - reaction between lines is the tell.
- Shortlist 4-8 per lead role for callbacks.

## 4. The room (callbacks)
Run it the same way for everyone:
1. Greet, two minutes of talk - you're also auditioning as someone they'd want to work with.
2. First read as prepared. Say thank you, nothing else.
3. **Redirect** - one adjustment, specific and playable ("this time you've already decided
   to leave; the whole scene is you being polite about it"). The redirect is the audition:
   you're testing whether they can take a note and change, which is what set requires.
4. Optional: a second scene, or an improvised moment to see them without text.
5. Record every callback on a locked-off camera; note taker in the room.
Use a good reader (ideally an actor), not the casting assistant reading flat.

## 5. Chemistry reads
For pairs that must carry the film, bring the final two or three for each role together
and mix combinations. Watch the scene *between* them, not each actor. Try a redirect that
makes one of them lead and the other follow, then swap.

## 6. Deciding
- Rewatch tapes with the sound off, then with the picture off.
- Ask: can I see every scene in the script with this person? Which scenes worry me?
- Check **availability and conflicts** before falling in love; check references from a
  recent director for how they are on day 12 at 2 a.m.
- Make the offer through the agent with dates, fee, billing and nudity/rider terms in writing.
- Tell the people you didn't cast, promptly, via the casting director.

## Gotchas
- Casting the best audition instead of the best actor for the whole arc.
- No redirect: you learn nothing about directability.
- Over-directing in the room (three adjustments in a row) - one clear note.
- Rewriting the role to fit a charismatic read without checking the story still works.
- Non-actors: cast for presence and the ability to repeat; audition by *doing* (a task,
  a conversation) rather than reading.

- [ ] Every callback got the same structure including one redirect.
- [ ] Chemistry reads done for the central pairing.
- [ ] Availability, references and offer terms confirmed before announcing.
