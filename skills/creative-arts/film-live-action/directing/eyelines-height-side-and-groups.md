# Eyelines: height, side and groups

**Goal:** every look lands on the right person in the edit - side, height and distance
all consistent - so the audience never wonders who is looking at whom.

## 1. Side of lens
With the line established, character A always looks **camera left** of the lens and B
**camera right** (or vice versa) in their singles. The script supervisor logs it per
set-up ("A looks L-R").

## 2. Height
- Match **relative heights**: if A stands and B sits, A's single looks **down** below
  lens, B's looks **up** above lens. Put the off-camera actor (or their mark) at the
  correct height - an apple box or a stand-in kneeling.
- Height difference between actors (tall vs short) is matched the same way; camera height
  for each single usually sits near the subject's eye height unless deliberately
  high/low.

## 3. Distance from lens (intimacy)
The closer the eyeline to the lens, the more intimate and engaged the shot feels; a
wide eyeline reads distant. For key close-ups, put the off-camera actor **right beside
the matte box**. Keep both sides of a conversation at similar distances so the
intensity matches. Looking **into** the lens breaks the fourth wall (only for POV or
deliberate address).

## 4. Group eyelines
- Map positions on the floor plan; for each single, list where **every** other character
  sits relative to lens (A: B is close camera left, C is far camera left, D is camera
  right).
- Give actors **marks for each look** (pieces of tape on the matte box or C-stands at
  the right positions when the real actor isn't there).
- In wide group singles, keep the order of looks consistent with seating (a look
  further left = further down the table on that side).
- Moving camera positions requires recalculating - the script supervisor checks.

## 5. Off-camera readers and stand-ins
Best: the real actor off camera at the right position. If unavailable: a reader at the
correct eyeline; or a mark (tennis ball, tape X) for VFX/creatures - give the actor a
height and movement for the eyeline (VFX supervisor provides creature height).

## Gotchas
- Off-camera actor sitting on a low chair beside camera for a standing scene.
- Wide eyelines for one side, tight for the other - one character seems detached.
- Group scenes where a look crosses the lens to reach someone on the "wrong" side.
- VFX eyelines that drift between takes; use physical marks.

## Verify
- [ ] Side of lens logged per character per set-up.
- [ ] Heights matched with boxes/stand-ins.
- [ ] Key close-ups have eyelines close to lens on both sides.
- [ ] Group look map on the floor plan; marks placed for absent actors.
