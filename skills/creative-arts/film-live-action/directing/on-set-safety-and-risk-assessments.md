# On-set safety and risk assessments

**Goal:** nobody is hurt making the film. The schedule, the shot and the budget come after
that, and the system below is how a producer and 1st AD make sure the order holds when
the day is going badly.

## 1. Responsibilities
- **Producer / production company** is legally responsible (employer duties).
- **1st AD** runs safety on the floor: safety meetings, enforcing control measures,
  authority to stop. In the UK the production appoints a competent person; larger shoots
  hire a **safety advisor / officer**. A **set medic** is standard for any stunt, SFX,
  remote location or large crew; know the nearest A&E (on the call sheet).
- Every HOD is responsible for their department's practice (grips for rigging, gaffer for
  electrics, stunt coordinator for stunts, armourer for weapons, SFX supervisor for pyro).
- Anyone can stop work for a safety concern without penalty. Say it at the first safety meeting.

## 2. Risk assessments (RA)
Written per **location** and per **hazardous activity**, before the tech scout is over:
1. Identify hazards (working at height, electricity, vehicles, water, weather, firearms,
   animals, crowd, night work, fatigue, lone working, confined spaces, noise, dust, lifting).
2. Who is at risk (crew, cast, public).
3. Rate likelihood x severity; decide **control measures** (eliminate, substitute, engineer,
   procedure, PPE - in that order).
4. Name the responsible person and the residual risk.
5. Distribute: attached to the call sheet; departments sign they've read it.
Template from your broadcaster/funder or HSE; generic "office" RAs don't count.
Hazardous activities (stunts, pyro, firearms, drones, water, aerial work, vehicles in
shot, extreme weather) get their own method statement from the specialist contractor.

## 3. The safety bulletins (what the specialist does and the AD checks)
The US Industry-Wide Safety Bulletins (CSATF) are the de facto standard worldwide:
#1 firearms (no live ammo, armourer control, no pointing at people, announce "hot/cold"),
#2 special effects/pyro (licensed supervisor, fire watch), #8 vehicles/insert cars
(precision drivers, lock-ups), #16 (helicopters) / #36 (drones), #17 water work (safety
divers, lifejackets, water quality), #29 multiple dwelling / #35 scaffolding and heights
(fall protection above ~6 ft/2 m), #23 lighting/electrical (GFCI/RCD on exteriors,
HMI UV and ballasts), #4 stunts (rehearsed, coordinator-controlled). Read the relevant
bulletin before the tech scout for any such day.

## 4. Safety meetings
- **First day**: whole crew; introduce the medic, hospital, radio channel for emergencies,
  muster point, fatigue policy, right to stop.
- **Before every hazardous setup**: 1st AD gathers everyone involved, the specialist walks
  through the action, the controls, and who gives the go. Log it on the production report.
- Daily when conditions change (weather, new location).

## 5. Fatigue and hours
Fatal accidents on film sets cluster around driving home after long days. Controls:
turnaround respected (10-12 h), a hard cap on day length (12-14 h including lunch), no
more than ~6 consecutive days, transport or hotel for crew after nights, a named person
empowered to call the day. Treat "one more setup" past the cap as the hazard it is.

## 6. Incidents
Any injury, near miss or damage: treat first, secure the scene, record on an **incident
report** (who, what, where, witnesses, photos), note on the DPR, notify the producer,
insurer and (for serious injuries) the regulator within the legal window (RIDDOR in the
UK, OSHA in the US). Near misses are reported too - they're free lessons.

## Gotchas
- RA written for the permit, never read by the crew.
- Specialist activity (a "small" fall, a car driving past camera) improvised without the specialist.
- Rain on a lighting exterior with no RCDs/GFCIs.
- Heat/cold with no water, shade, warm-up or break plan for extras.
- Drones flown by "a crew member with a drone".

## Verify
- [ ] RA per location and per hazardous activity attached to the call sheet; HODs signed.
- [ ] Safety meeting before every stunt/firearm/pyro/vehicle/water/height setup, logged.
- [ ] Medic and hospital details on the call sheet; emergency radio channel known.
- [ ] Day-length cap and turnaround policy published and kept.
