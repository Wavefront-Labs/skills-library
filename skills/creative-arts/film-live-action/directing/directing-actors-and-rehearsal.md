# Directing actors and rehearsal

**Goal:** performances that are alive on take 7 and match on take 1 of the reverse -
produced by notes an actor can *act on* instead of results they have to fake.

## 1. The grammar of a playable note
| Don't say (result) | Say instead (playable) |
|---|---|
| "Be angrier" | "Make him take it back" (verb/objective) |
| "More vulnerable" | "You already know he's lying; you're hoping he'll stop" (fact + hope) |
| "Faster" | "You've got a train to catch and he's making you miss it" (circumstance) |
| "Sadder" | "As if you're telling this to your mother" (adjustment / as-if) |
| "Be natural" | "Do less; just tell her" |

Five tools, in order of strength: **verbs** (to persuade, to punish, to test), **facts**
of the given circumstances, **objectives** (what they want from the other person),
**adjustments / as-ifs**, and **physical tasks** (pour the wine during the line). Emotion is
the result of these, never the instruction. One note per take.

## 2. The rehearsal period
Even a day helps. Structure:
1. **Read-through** of the whole script, no performance - actors learn the shape; you listen
   for scenes that don't read.
2. **Table work** on key scenes: facts, questions, what each character wants. Decide the
   assumptions together so they're owned. Don't "set" line readings.
3. **Up on feet** with rough blocking in a taped-out space: find the physical life - where
   people stand says what they feel. Keep what the actors discover; note it.
4. **Don't rehearse to the finish.** Stop when the scene is understood, not polished -
   you want the discovery on camera. Especially don't over-rehearse comedy or first kisses.
5. Agree a **shared vocabulary** for set: what "again, same" and "one for you" mean.

## 3. On the day
- Private notes, always. Walk to the actor; never shout direction across the set.
- Watch the actor, not the monitor, for at least one take; use the monitor to check frame.
- After a take: say *something* - "good, we have that; now try..." Silence reads as failure.
- Protect first takes of emotional scenes: shoot the close-up early if the actor is a
  "first three takes" performer; shoot it late if they build.
- Give the other actor the off-camera performance you want (eyelines, full energy); if a
  star is absent for a reverse, read with the same verbs, not a flat line.
- Continuity of performance across setups: remind them of the beat they were in, not the gesture.
- When nothing works: change the *task* (give them something to do), change the
  *circumstance*, or change the staging. Don't repeat the same note louder.

## 4. Safety and trust
Intimacy and violence are choreographed with an intimacy coordinator / stunt coordinator
and agreed before the day. Closed sets, no surprises, no "just try something" on those
scenes. Trust is the directing instrument; it's built by keeping every promise about what
will be asked of them.

## Gotchas
- Line readings ("say it like this") - occasionally fine for a comic beat, poison as a habit.
- Giving a note to the wrong actor: if a scene is flat, often the *listener* needs the
  note, not the speaker.
- Notes in front of crew or other actors that embarrass.
- Over-explaining the psychology. One sentence, then "let's go".
- Rehearsing blocking so tightly that performance becomes hitting marks. Marks serve
  the actor, not the other way round - have the DP adapt when a better move appears.

## Verify
- [ ] Every note you plan to give is a verb, fact, objective, adjustment or task.
- [ ] Rehearsal left the key emotional beats undiscovered on purpose.
- [ ] You said something to the actor after every take.
- [ ] Intimacy/stunt scenes were choreographed and agreed before the shoot day.
