# Action, stunts and intimacy coordination

**Goal:** sequences where the danger - physical or emotional - is designed, rehearsed and
consented to in prep, so that on the day everyone is bored by how safe it is and the
only thing left to direct is storytelling.

## 1. Action and stunts
**Brief the stunt coordinator early** (prep, not the week before): the beat-by-beat story
of the fight/chase (who's winning, where the turn is, what the audience must feel),
character skill levels, the location, the schedule. The coordinator designs the
choreography and the rig; you own the story and the frame.
- **Stunt-vis**: the stunt team shoots a rehearsal video with the intended shots. Cut it.
  If the sequence doesn't work on the stunt-vis, it won't work with lights.
- **Shoot for the cut**: fights cut on impacts; each hit needs the setup (the wind-up),
  the hit, and the reaction, often from different angles. Frame rate: 24 for weight,
  slightly overcranked (30-36) only for specific beats. Camera *moves with* the action's
  direction to sell energy; crossing the line within a fight is routine if direction of
  attack stays readable.
- **Performers**: actors do what the coordinator says they do and nothing more; doubles do
  the rest. Face replacement is cheaper than an injury.
- **Protocols** (Industry-Wide Safety Bulletins): a **safety meeting** on set before any
  stunt, pyro, firearms, vehicle or water work, attended by all; the 1st AD runs it.
  **Firearms**: licensed armourer, no live ammunition on set ever, chamber checks shown to
  actors and 1st AD, no pointing at people unless specifically blocked and approved,
  consider non-firing replicas + muzzle flash in post. **Vehicles**: a precision driver,
  closed road, lock-ups, spotters on radio. **Pyro/fire**: licensed SFX supervisor, fire
  safety officer, extinguishers, medic. **Water/heights**: safety divers, riggers, harness checks.
- Nobody "just tries" a stunt. If it wasn't in the risk assessment, it doesn't happen today.

## 2. Intimacy and nudity
An **intimacy coordinator (IC)** is a department head for scenes with simulated sex,
nudity, intimate touch, or sexual violence. Under SAG-AFTRA standards and most broadcaster
policies:
- The IC reads the script, meets actors individually in prep to establish **boundaries
  and consent** per scene, and agrees a **nudity rider** in writing (what, how much, who
  sees it, no changes on the day).
- The IC choreographs the scene with you like a stunt: beats, positions, duration, what's
  seen. Rehearse clothed. Modesty garments, barriers, and a closed set are standard.
- **Closed set**: essential crew only, no monitors outside the set, no stills, phones away.
  The call sheet says "closed set" and the 1st AD enforces it.
- Actors can withdraw consent to anything at any time; "we already shot it" is not an argument.
- Footage handling: limited access in post; agreed in the rider.
- Minors: never nudity or simulated intimacy; a chaperone/studio teacher present always.

## 3. Directing in these scenes
Give the same kind of notes as any other scene - objectives and beats - but through the
choreography agreed. Changing the choreography means going back through the coordinator
with the performers' agreement, even if it costs a setup.

## Gotchas
- Scheduling an intimate scene on day one before trust exists; schedule it mid-shoot.
- Letting a fight run as one wide take with no impacts covered - uncuttable.
- Improvised "spontaneous" slaps, kisses or grabs between actors. Everything is agreed.
- Stunt doubles whose wardrobe and hair don't match because the costume department
  wasn't told the double's measurements in prep.
- Guns handed to actors by anyone other than the armourer.

## Verify
- [ ] Stunt-vis cut and approved before the shoot day; risk assessment signed.
- [ ] Safety meeting held and recorded before every stunt/firearm/vehicle/pyro setup.
- [ ] Nudity riders signed; IC present; closed set on the call sheet.
- [ ] Every hit in the fight has wind-up, impact and reaction covered.
