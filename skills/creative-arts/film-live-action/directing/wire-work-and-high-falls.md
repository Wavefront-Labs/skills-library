# Wire work and high falls

**Goal:** shots of people flying, being thrown or falling that look dangerous and
aren't, designed with the stunt rigging team and finished cleanly in post.

## 1. Rig types (the stunt rigger's toolbox)
- **Ratchet / jerk-back** (pneumatic or mechanical): yanks a performer backwards for
  explosions or hits.
- **Descender** (decelerator): controlled high fall that slows near the ground - no bag
  in shot; allows falls into frame-tight landings.
- **Flying rigs**: 2D/3D track systems or cable cams that move a performer through space;
  **pick points** on a harness (hips, shoulders) set the attitude.
- **Pendulum / swing** rigs for swings and arcs.
- **Airbags / box catchers** for free falls; size per height.
All designed by the stunt coordinator and rigging team, with engineering for loads.

## 2. Plan it as shots
Storyboard the action; for each shot specify **start, end, attitude, speed**. Agree which
parts are rig, which are performer, which are digital. Techvis for big flights.

## 3. Rehearsal and safety
- Rigger tests with sandbags, then stunt performer at reduced speed, then full.
- Height work: harness inspections, rescue plan, exclusion zones; the coordinator
  calls go/no-go. Weather (wind) limits.
- Actors on wires need training days and comfortable harness fittings (harness
  shows under costume - costume builds in openings).

## 4. Camera
- **Sell height**: shoot from above for falls (the drop visible), low for flights against
  sky; long lenses compress, wide lenses exaggerate.
- Hide wires: angles where wires go out of frame or against busy backgrounds; thin wires
  (painted, or matte) reduce paint work; shoot a **clean plate** for each set-up.
- Speed: a fall over-cranked (e.g. 30-48 fps) adds weight; ask the DP.

## 5. Post
VFX wire and rig removal: provide clean plates, tracking markers if needed, and a list of
shots with rigs in frame. Budget per shot; it's cheaper to frame out wires on the day.

## Gotchas
- Changing the gag on the day without rigger re-testing.
- Harness lines visible through costume.
- No clean plate; removal becomes expensive paint work.
- Wide lens close to performer making a 10 m fall look short.

## Verify
- [ ] Rig type, start/end and attitude defined per shot with the stunt team.
- [ ] Test sequence (sandbag -> slow -> full) scheduled; rescue plan written.
- [ ] Camera angles chosen to sell height and hide rigs.
- [ ] Clean plates and a VFX removal list delivered.
