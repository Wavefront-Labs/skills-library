# Crossing the line legally

**Goal:** get to the other side of the action axis - for a better background, light or
staging - while the audience keeps its sense of who is where.

## 1. Legal crosses
1. **Camera moves across the line on screen** - a dolly, Steadicam or crane move that
   crosses in a continuous shot; the audience sees the reorientation. Cut freely on the
   new side afterwards.
2. **Actor movement creates a new line** - a character walks to a new position (crosses
   the room, sits on the other side); the new line is set in the shot. Cover the move.
3. **Neutral (axial) shot** - a shot on the line itself (head-on or tail-on, character
   moving toward/away from camera, or a frontal close-up looking straight down the lens
   axis). Cut A side -> neutral -> B side.
4. **Cutaway / insert** - cut to a detail or a third character, then return on the new
   side. Weakest method; works in fast scenes, risky in slow ones.
5. **Wide re-establish** - a master that clearly shows the geography from the new side.
6. **New eyeline anchor** - a third character's entrance creates a new pair and a new
   line.

## 2. Plan crosses during staging
Draw the line on the floor plan for each beat. Where you want the reverse background
(the window, the view), stage a **move** that brings the actors across. Tell the script
supervisor where the line changes - they track eyelines and direction take by take.

## 3. Deliberate crosses
A "jump" across the line can express disorientation, a rupture in a relationship, or a
moment of shock. It must be **one clear choice** at a turning point, not drift.
Directors such as Ozu worked with their own consistent spatial system - consistency is
what reads as intention.

## 4. Check in the cut
Ask the editor/script supervisor to assemble quickly when unsure. Field monitors with a
frame-grab overlay (comparing last shot's eyeline) help confirm on the day.

## Gotchas
- Crossing between two singles in a calm dialogue scene with no reorientation shot.
- An "axial" neutral that's slightly off-axis - looks like a cross.
- Forgetting the line moved after an actor's cross; later coverage uses the old side.
- Cutaways that are too short to reset the viewer's map.

## Verify
- [ ] Line drawn per beat on the floor plan, including where it changes.
- [ ] Each cross uses a move, a new line, a neutral or a re-establish.
- [ ] Script supervisor informed; eyelines logged.
- [ ] Deliberate crosses limited to meaningful moments.
