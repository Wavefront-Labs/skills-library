# Film budgeting and the top sheet

**Goal:** a budget that is a *plan* - derived from the schedule, defensible line by line to
a financier or bond company, and tracked weekly so the film finishes without surprises.

## 1. Inputs before a single number
- Locked script, breakdown, and a **schedule** (shoot days, locations, nights, company
  moves). Shoot days drive most below-the-line costs; budget without a schedule is a guess.
- Crew list and rate card (union agreement or local market rates), cast deals, the union
  status of the production.
- Post plan (editing weeks, VFX shot count, sound mix days, grade days, deliverables list).

## 2. Structure: the chart of accounts
Industry budgets use a standard account structure (Movie Magic Budgeting ships templates;
studios have their own):
- **Above the line (ATL)**: story/rights, writer, producers, director, principal cast
  (plus their fringes, agents' fees, travel/per diem).
- **Below the line - production (BTL)**: extras, production staff (AD, PM, coordinators),
  camera, electric, grip, sound, art/set construction/set dressing, props, wardrobe,
  hair/make-up, locations, transport, catering, stunts/SFX, production office, insurance
  in some templates, stock/media.
- **Post-production**: editorial, VFX, music, sound post, colour/finishing, titles,
  deliverables, QC.
- **Other**: insurance, publicity/stills, legal/accounting, completion bond, finance costs,
  **contingency** (10% of ATL+BTL+post is standard; bonds insist on it).
Each account has **detail lines**: `quantity x units x rate` (e.g. `1st AC - 6 weeks x
1 x 2,000`). Prep, shoot and wrap weeks are separate columns for crew.

## 3. Fringes
Payroll fringes (employer taxes, union health/pension, holiday pay) are applied as
percentages to labour lines - 20-35% is common and is the most frequently omitted cost.
Set fringe groups once in the software and attach them to every labour line.

## 4. The top sheet
A one-page summary: each account group's subtotal, totals for ATL / BTL / post / other,
contingency, grand total, with shoot days, locations and union status in the header.
Financiers read the top sheet first and the ratio ATL:BTL second.

## 5. Incentives and financing
Tax credits/rebates (UK AVEC, Georgia, Ireland Section 481, etc.) are a percentage of
*qualifying* spend - budget in a way that lets the accountant tag qualifying lines from
day one. The financing plan lists each source (equity, pre-sales, incentive, gap) and
when it cash-flows; a **cash-flow schedule** by week shows when money is needed vs available.

## 6. Tracking during production
- **Cost report** weekly: per account `budget | cost to date | estimate to complete |
  estimated final cost | variance`. The EFC is the number everyone argues about.
- **Hot costs** daily during the shoot: actual labour hours, overtime, meal penalties,
  extras counts vs budget for that day - catches an overrun inside 24 hours.
- Changes > contingency burn rate trigger a conversation with the bond/financier before,
  not after.

## Gotchas
- Budgeting the schedule you hope for rather than the one the AD produced.
- Missing lines: fringes, overtime allowance, wrap weeks, kit rentals for crew-owned gear,
  hard drives, archive/delivery, residuals, E&O insurance, bank/legal fees, music licensing.
- Contingency used as a slush fund in week one.
- Post budget as a lump sum - it's where under-budgeting hides because it's furthest away.

## Verify
- [ ] Every labour line has fringes applied; prep/shoot/wrap weeks separated.
- [ ] Contingency 10% and listed below the line totals.
- [ ] Top sheet totals reconcile with detail; shoot days match the schedule.
- [ ] Weekly cost report with EFC during production; hot costs during the shoot.
