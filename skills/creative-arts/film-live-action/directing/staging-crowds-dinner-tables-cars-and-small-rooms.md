# Staging crowds, dinner tables, cars and small rooms

**Goal:** complicated configurations that read clearly on screen and can be covered in
the time available.

## 1. Dinner tables and group conversations
- Pick the **dominant axis**: usually along the table between the two characters in
  the main conflict, or across it. All singles respect that axis.
- Split the table into **sub-groups** (each end) with their own mini-axes; move between
  them via a master or a character's look.
- Keep **heads at different heights/depths**; seat the main characters where they'll
  face camera without cheating (often the far side of the table, or at the ends).
- Cover efficiently: one master along the table, then a camera position from each side
  shooting **several singles** by panning/re-framing (no relighting), plus inserts.
- Continuity: food, drink levels and cutlery are a nightmare - limit eating to key moments;
  script supervisor photographs plates after every take.

## 2. Crowds and large groups
- Give the crowd an **activity and direction of flow**; principals move against or across
  it so they stand out.
- Use **layers**: foreground pass-bys, mid-ground principals, background flow.
- Fake size: tight lenses compress; repeat background through frame on separate takes;
  keep the widest shot short.
- Plan with the 2nd AD: crowd cues ("background action"), resets, and where principals
  enter.

## 3. Cars
- Decide **process trailer/low-loader, tow, poor man's process, LED volume or actor
  driving** (safety first - see driving-shots skill).
- Standard set-ups: two-shot through windscreen, singles from the hood/windows (hostess
  tray or suction mounts), POV out, side profile. Back seat passengers need their own
  plan.
- Eyeline: characters face forward; looks to each other cross the cabin - agree which side
  of camera each looks for consistency.

## 4. Lifts, corridors, small rooms
- Remove walls ("wild" walls) on stages; on location, choose lenses and positions in
  advance with the DP - the tech scout should test every set-up.
- Stage **in depth** along the long dimension; use mirrors and doorframes.
- Minimise crew in the room: remote focus, monitors outside, small lights.

## Gotchas
- Dinner-scene axes switching between angles; viewers lose who's beside whom.
- Crowds standing still around principals.
- Car scenes where the actor drives while performing complex dialogue.
- Discovering on the day that the camera doesn't fit in the room.

## Verify
- [ ] Dominant axis and sub-groups drawn on the floor plan.
- [ ] Coverage grouped by camera side to avoid relights.
- [ ] Crowd activity, flow and layers planned with the 2nd AD.
- [ ] Car and small-room set-ups tested on the tech scout.
