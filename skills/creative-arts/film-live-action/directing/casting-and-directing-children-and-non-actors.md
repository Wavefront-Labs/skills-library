# Casting and directing children and non-actors

**Goal:** truthful performances from people who aren't trained to repeat them,
within the law and without harming anyone.

## 1. Legal first (children)
- **UK**: children under school-leaving age need a **child performance licence** from
  the local authority (apply ~21 days before), a **licensed chaperone**, and limits on
  hours on set, performance time and breaks by age band; education must be provided
  on long shoots.
- **California**: an **Entertainment Work Permit** for the minor, a **studio teacher**
  on set, and age-based maximum hours (e.g. infants 15 days-6 months: 20 minutes of
  work in a 2-hour window); Coogan trust account for earnings. Other states/countries
  have their own rules - your production office checks.
- Schedule child days around these limits (see scheduling) - it affects coverage plans.

## 2. Casting
- **Street/open casting** for non-actors: schools, clubs, workplaces related to the role.
  Explain the project to parents/guardians and the person in plain language.
- Auditions as **conversation and play**: talk about their life; use improvisation
  around the scene's situation rather than reading lines. Film it.
- Look for **listening and presence** on camera, not line delivery.
- Callback with the other principal to test chemistry; meet the parents (they come to set).

## 3. Preparing the script
- Simplify dialogue; allow paraphrase. Some directors never give the full script to
  young children - give the scene situation verbally instead.
- Build scenes so key moments can be **captured** (play, a real task) rather than
  repeated on cue.

## 4. Directing on set
- Give **actions and tasks**, not emotions ("find the red block before she counts to ten").
- Use **real reactions**: surprise them with something new (safely and ethically, with
  guardian consent - never frighten a child for a reaction).
- Keep takes few and early in the day; children's energy drops fast. Shoot their
  coverage first.
- Roll before calling action; keep crew small and quiet around non-actors.
- With non-actors, let them do their real job/skill on camera; dialogue grows from it.
- Adult actors opposite children must be patient and play off what happens.

## Gotchas
- Missing licence or exceeding hours - production can be shut down.
- Exhausting a child with repeated takes for the adult's coverage; shoot the child first.
- Coaching a non-actor until they "act" - the authenticity disappears.
- Using deception that causes distress; ethical and safeguarding failure.

## Verify
- [ ] Licences/permits, chaperone/studio teacher booked; hours built into the schedule.
- [ ] Auditions recorded as play/improv; parents met.
- [ ] Scenes adapted for capture; dialogue simplified.
- [ ] Child coverage scheduled first; safeguarding brief given to crew.
