# Staging two- and three-person scenes

**Goal:** a staging that expresses the relationship and the beats, gives the camera
good angles and keeps continuity simple.

## 1. Two-person patterns (Arijon/Katz)
| Pattern | Expresses | Coverage |
|---|---|---|
| **Face to face** (across a table) | Confrontation, negotiation | Classic OTS/singles on reverse axes |
| **Side by side** (car, bar, bench) | Shared purpose, avoidance, intimacy | Two-shot profile, frontal two-shot, singles from the front at angles |
| **L-shape** (at right angles) | Awkward, informal | Singles at 90 degrees; an angle catches both |
| **One behind the other** (front/back) | Hidden reaction, power | Deep-focus two-shot; one face to camera, other turned away |
| **Standing vs sitting** | Status | High/low angles motivated by the staging |
Pick the pattern from the **relationship in the beat**, not the furniture.

## 2. Movement marks beats
Change the staging at each beat change: one stands, crosses, turns away, sits. Each
move creates a new composition and can shift who dominates the frame. Give the move a
**motivation** (get a drink, look out the window) the actor can play. Plan where each
move lands so coverage can match.

## 3. Three-person patterns
- **Triangle** (most common): open to camera; any two can be in OTS of the third. Lines
  of action multiply - pick the dominant line between the two main antagonists.
- **Linear** (three in a row - a bench, a car's back seat): profile shots or a frontal
  three-shot; singles from in front.
- **Two vs one**: two side by side facing one - expresses alliances; cover as group
  vs single with a clear axis.
- Shift the **apex** of the triangle when power shifts (who stands where).
Plan which character's eyeline is "camera left/right" for each pair (see eyeline skill).

## 4. Coverage implications
Write the coverage before blocking is final: does each beat have a strong angle? Does
the pattern force a line cross? Simplify if your schedule allows only 3-4 set-ups.

## 5. On the day
Block with actors first (they find natural moves), then adjust to camera. Mark actors'
positions (tape marks) for focus and continuity.

## Gotchas
- Face-to-face in every scene; visual monotony.
- Movement without motivation - actors wander.
- Three-person scenes with an unclear line of action; eyelines confuse in the edit.
- A staging that requires 12 set-ups on a 3-set-up schedule.

## Verify
- [ ] Pattern chosen per beat to express the relationship.
- [ ] Each move motivated and landing on a mark.
- [ ] Line of action defined for each pair; eyelines planned.
- [ ] Coverage list fits the time available.
