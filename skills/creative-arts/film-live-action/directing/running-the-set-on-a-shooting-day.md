# Running the set on a shooting day

**Goal:** get the scenes that matter at the quality that matters, finish the day, and have
the crew turn up tomorrow wanting to work for you.

## 1. Chain of command
- You decide *what* and *why*; the **1st AD** decides *when* and runs the floor (calls,
  safety, schedule). Never call "quiet" or "rolling" yourself; never bypass the AD to give
  a department an order - tell the AD, they'll make it happen.
- Your conversations on set: actors, DP, 1st AD, script supervisor. Everyone else gets
  your decisions through their HOD.
- **Script supervisor** is your memory: tell them which takes you liked (circle takes)
  and why; note coverage holes before moving on.

## 2. The cycle per setup
1. Block (marking rehearsal) - 10-20 min. 2. Crew lights; you're with actors or checking
the next scene. 3. "First team in." Rehearse on camera once. 4. **"Roll sound" -> "speed"
-> "roll camera" -> "rolling/set" -> slate -> "Action"** (you call action, or delegate
to the 1st AD; agree in advance). 5. "Cut." 6. Note to actor, note to DP, "going again" or
"check that / that's a print" -> 7. "Moving on" (AD), or "that's a wrap on [actor]".
Give the AD a clear "we have it" after every setup; hedging ("maybe one more") silently
burns the schedule.

## 3. Protecting performance inside the schedule
- Know the **scene's event**; get that moment right before anything else. The rest can be
  adequate.
- Shoot the emotionally hardest setup when the actor is freshest - sometimes before the master.
- Don't do takes for comfort; do a take for a reason and tell the actor what it is.
- Reserve the right to "one more for me" but spend it rarely - the AD will stop believing it.

## 4. When you're behind (you will be)
Ask the AD for the numbers (setups left, hours left). Then, in order:
1. **Combine** setups: one developing shot instead of two sizes.
2. **Drop** inserts and cutaways (the editor can live without them; note it for the pickup list).
3. **Simplify** a camera move to a static.
4. **Cut** a scene fully and move it to another day - never shoot half of it.
Never shave the scene that the film is about. Decide fast and say it out loud.

## 5. Habits that keep a crew
- Be the first to know what the next setup is. The crew waits for your decision more than
  anything else; indecision at the monitor is the most expensive thing on set.
- Watch at least one take from beside the camera, not the village.
- Thank departments by name at wrap; take the blame publicly, give notes privately.
- Eat lunch with the crew sometimes. Attend the end-of-day meeting with the AD and DP
  about tomorrow; look at tomorrow's call sheet before it goes out.
- Safety overrides schedule, always, and the AD has the authority to stop anything.

## Terms you'll hear
**Abby Singer** second-to-last shot of the day; **martini** last shot; **company move**
relocation; **turnaround** reversing the camera for the reverse angles, and also the
legally required rest between wrap and next call; **MOS** no sound; **pickup** restarting
a take from a point; **series** several takes without cutting ("roll a series").

## Gotchas
- Giving direction via the monitor's talkback or across the set.
- Re-blocking after lighting because you didn't watch the marking rehearsal closely.
- Letting video village (producers, clients) note actors - all notes go through you.
- Extending days without the AD/producer agreeing; overtime and turnaround cascade into tomorrow.

## Verify
- [ ] You can name the event of every scene on the call sheet before call.
- [ ] A clear "we have it" or "going again" after every setup.
- [ ] A drop list prepared in prep for each day's scenes.
- [ ] Tomorrow's call sheet reviewed with the AD before wrap.
