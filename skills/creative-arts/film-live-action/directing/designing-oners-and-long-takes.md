# Designing oners and long takes

**Goal:** a continuous shot that does the work of a sequence of shots - each beat gets
its "shot" inside the move - and that the crew can execute repeatably.

## 1. Justify it
Use a oner when **real time, immersion or a performance's continuity** matters, or the
geography is the story (a chase through a building, a party). If the beats need
different emphasis that cutting gives better, cut. Oners cost rehearsal time and
remove options in the edit.

## 2. Map beats to frames
Write the scene's beats, then design a **path** where each beat lands on a composed
frame (wide for arrival, MCU for the reveal, OTS for the argument). Draw the floor plan
with camera path, actor paths, and numbered **frame stops**. Like a shot list, but
along a single line.

## 3. Choose the tool
Steadicam (long distances, stairs), dolly with track (precise, repeatable),
gimbal on a ride-on/ crane (big transitions), handheld (intimate, forgiving), or a
combination with **handoffs** (gimbal passed from operator to a car mount to a crane - plan
the handoff points). Lens choice is fixed for the take - usually wider (24-35mm) so
frames work at different distances; focus puller works with marks or a rangefinder.

## 4. Choreograph everyone
- Actors' **marks** at each frame stop; background cues timed to the camera.
- **Lighting**: everything visible; rig overhead or hide units; lighting cues on a dimmer
  board for moments the camera turns.
- **Sound**: lavs plus a boom that tracks or hidden plant mics.
- **Crew**: everyone not on camera hides; walkie routes; doors opened by grips off-frame.

## 5. Hidden stitches
For impossible or long takes, plan **stitch points**: whip pans, wipes past a dark
foreground (a pillar, a passer-by), camera passing through a wall or into darkness. Shoot
each piece overlapping by a few seconds for the VFX/edit blend.

## 6. Rehearse and protect
Rehearse in stages: walk-through, camera-only, cast plus camera at half speed, full speed.
Budget the day for **many takes** (10-30). Shoot a **safety coverage** piece of the most
important beat if the schedule allows, in case no take is perfect throughout.

## Gotchas
- A oner as a stunt that ignores the scene's beats.
- Lens too tight - frames only work at one stop.
- Lighting or crew reflections in windows and mirrors along the path.
- No safety coverage and no take is fully clean.

## Verify
- [ ] Reason for the oner written; beats mapped to frame stops on the plan.
- [ ] Tool, lens and any handoffs/stitches defined.
- [ ] Lighting, sound and crew hiding planned along the full path.
- [ ] Staged rehearsals scheduled; safety coverage decided.
