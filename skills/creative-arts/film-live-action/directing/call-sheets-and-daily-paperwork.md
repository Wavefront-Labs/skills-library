# Call sheets and daily paperwork

**Goal:** everybody arrives at the right place, at the right time, knowing what is being
shot and what they need - and at wrap the day is documented well enough to pay people and
defend the schedule.

## 1. Who and when
The **2nd AD** drafts the call sheet from the 1st AD's schedule; the 1st AD, PM/line
producer and director approve. It goes out at wrap of the previous day (never later than
~12 hours before call). Any later change is a **revised call sheet**, clearly marked, with
calls made by phone to anyone affected.

## 2. The call sheet, top to bottom
1. **Header**: production, day X of Y, date, **unit call** (general crew call), weather
   (temp, rain chance, sunrise/sunset), **nearest hospital** with address and phone,
   key contacts (1st AD, PM, coordinator, set medic), unit base / parking / breakfast.
2. **Schedule** table: scene number, set/description (`INT. KITCHEN - Marta confronts Dev`),
   D/N, cast numbers, pages (eighths), location. In shooting order. Include company moves
   and estimated lunch.
3. **Cast**: number, name, character, status (SW start work, W work, SWF start-finish, H
   hold, etc.), pickup time, make-up/wardrobe call, on-set time. Transport notes.
4. **Stand-ins / extras / minors** with calls and chaperone/tutor notes.
5. **Crew by department** with individual calls where they differ from unit call
   (pre-calls for grip/electric, camera prep). Walkie channel plan.
6. **Special requirements**: stunts, SFX, animals, vehicles, playback, intimacy
   coordinator, closed set, firearms/armourer, drones, water, weather cover.
7. **Advance schedule**: the next one or two days so departments can prep.
8. Notes: safety (riggers, no smoking, hearing protection), parking, catering numbers,
   wardrobe reminders, "no photography on set".
Attach: location map/movement order, **sides** (the day's script pages, reduced), any
risk assessments.

## 3. Deriving call times
- Work back from the **first shot**: unit call = first shot time - (blocking + lighting
  time + camera build). Typically 60-90 min on a drama.
- Cast calls = on-set time - (make-up + wardrobe + travel). Hair/make-up gives durations
  per actor (a lead with prosthetics may need 3 h).
- **Turnaround** (union minimum rest between wrap and next call, e.g. 10-12 h for crew,
  12 h for cast in many agreements): yesterday's wrap + turnaround sets the earliest call.
  A late night pushes tomorrow; the week drifts. Plan night shoots at the end of the week.
- Pre-calls for departments that must be ready at unit call; note them individually.

## 4. The production report (DPR / daily progress report)
Closed by the 2nd AD after wrap, signed by the 1st AD and PM:
- Scenes scheduled vs **scenes shot** (completed / partial / not shot), pages shot today
  and to date, setups, **scheduled vs actual** days ahead/behind.
- Times: crew call, first shot, lunch, first shot after lunch, camera wrap, last out.
- Cast: arrival, make-up, on set, dismissed, meals - from which payroll is computed.
- Crew hours, extras count, meals served, equipment/vehicles used, media shot, stills count.
- **Incidents**: injuries, damage, delays and their cause (weather, equipment, late cast).
  This is the record insurers and completion bonds read.
Time cards / Exhibit G (SAG) are collected with it.

## Gotchas
- Call sheet without the nearest hospital or with last week's weather.
- Missing cast status codes - payroll and the DOOD break.
- Not flagging a closed set or minors' hours; both are enforced by law/agreement.
- Advance schedule missing: departments can't pre-rig, so tomorrow runs late.
- Forgetting the pre-call for the location (unlock, power, generator).

## Verify
- [ ] Unit call derived from first shot and respects turnaround from yesterday's wrap.
- [ ] Every cast member has status, pickup, M/U and on-set times.
- [ ] Hospital, contacts, special requirements and advance schedule present.
- [ ] DPR completed and signed at wrap with times, pages and incidents.
