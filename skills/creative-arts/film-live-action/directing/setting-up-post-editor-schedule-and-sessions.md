# Setting up post: editor, schedule and sessions

**Goal:** the right editor, a realistic post calendar, and productive sessions with
sound, music and colour where the director's intent is clearly expressed.

## 1. Choosing the editor
- Watch their films for **rhythm and performance choices** - not just genre.
- Meet: talk about the script; ask what they'd cut. You want honest disagreement.
- Check workflow: NLE (Avid is common on features/TV; Premiere and Resolve also),
  assistant editor, availability for the whole schedule including director's cut.
- Brief: tone references, cutting style, how you like to receive cuts (scenes as shot,
  weekly assemblies), whether they cut during the shoot (usual).

## 2. The post calendar (feature, typical)
| Phase | Duration (typical) |
|---|---|
| Editor's assembly (during shoot + after) | Shoot + 1-2 weeks |
| Director's cut | DGA features: at least **10 weeks** after the assembly (or one day per two shooting days if greater); episodic TV gets a few days (e.g. 4 for a one-hour episode) - check the current agreement |
| Producer/studio cuts, screenings | 4-10 weeks |
| Picture lock | - |
| VFX completion, sound editorial, music | 8-12 weeks after lock |
| Mix | 1-3 weeks |
| Grade / online | 1-3 weeks (parallel) |
| Deliverables, QC | 2-4 weeks |
Work backwards from the delivery or festival date; add buffers for VFX.

## 3. Spotting sessions
- **Music spotting** with composer and music editor: play the locked cut reel by reel;
  decide where music starts/stops, its job in each cue ("tension under, no melody"),
  and temp-score references - and where you **don't** want music.
- **Sound spotting** with supervising sound editor: effects, ADR candidates, foley
  needs, backgrounds, design moments.
Take notes into a cue list; agree before work begins.

## 4. Mix and grade sessions
- **Mix**: attend the final mix; review reel by reel at the target level (cinema at
  reference level, TV near-field). Give notes as feelings and priorities ("I lose her
  line under the train"); the mixer finds the fix. Budget playbacks and fix days.
- **Grade**: brief the colourist with references and the DP's intent (DP usually
  attends); approve hero shots per scene first, then the rest; review on the
  deliverable-grade display (HDR and SDR trims).

## Gotchas
- Hiring an editor who agrees with everything.
- Compressing the director's cut to make a date - breaches DGA rights on covered shows.
- No music spotting; temp love becomes the score brief.
- Approving the mix on laptop speakers.

## Verify
- [ ] Editor chosen with a brief and agreed review rhythm.
- [ ] Post calendar built backwards with director's cut period and buffers.
- [ ] Music and sound spotting notes in cue lists.
- [ ] Mix and grade attended on reference systems.
