# Travel direction, chases and intercut calls

**Goal:** movement and looks that stay consistent across shots and places, so audiences
read journeys, pursuits and conversations instantly.

## 1. Travel direction
- Pick a direction for a journey and **keep it** (left-to-right for going out/west, right-
  to-left for returning is a common convention - many Western films map left-to-right
  as "forward").
- A **change of direction** must be shown (character turns on screen, or a neutral
  head-on/tail-on shot between).
- Entrances and exits: exiting frame left means entering the next shot frame right.
- **Map logic**: if the story has a geography (east to the border), keep screen
  direction faithful to it throughout the film.

## 2. Chases
- Pursuer and pursued travel the **same direction**; that reads as chase. Opposite
  directions read as approaching each other (collision course).
- Vary shot types (side, head-on, tail-on, overhead, POV) but keep the lateral direction.
- Use neutral (head-on/tail-on) shots to allow direction changes at corners.
- Mark the gap between them: closing distance through framing and cutting rate.
- Give each party a **colour or silhouette** identity so geography reads at speed.

## 3. Phone calls and intercut locations
- Two people in different places talking: they should **look in opposite directions**
  (A faces frame right, B faces frame left) as if facing each other across the cut.
- Keep framing sizes matched for equal emphasis; vary deliberately to show power.
- For split screens, look toward the split line.
- Video calls: decide whether characters look at the screen (slightly off lens) or into
  the lens; keep consistent.
- Script supervisor notes the direction for scenes shot weeks apart (each side of a call
  is often shot on different days or by different units).

## 4. Across scenes
A character leaving one location frame left and arriving at the next frame right keeps
the flow; reverse it to signal a return or reversal.

## Gotchas
- Both sides of a phone call looking the same direction - seems they're talking to someone else.
- Chase where a pursuer suddenly runs the other way after a cut.
- Second unit shooting direction-blind; give them the direction sheet.
- Direction flipping between day and night portions of the same journey.

## Verify
- [ ] Direction chart for journeys and chases shared with all units.
- [ ] Direction changes shown on screen or via neutral shots.
- [ ] Phone-call sides look opposite ways; framing sizes agreed.
- [ ] Script supervisor logs direction for split-shot scenes.
