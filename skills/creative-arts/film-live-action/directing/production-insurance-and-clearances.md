# Production insurance and clearances

**Goal:** the film can survive a dropped camera, a sick lead, a lawsuit from someone whose
name appears on a tombstone in shot, and a distributor's lawyer asking for the chain of
title - because each was handled before it became a problem.

## 1. The production insurance package
Arranged through an entertainment broker (not a general insurer) before prep starts;
permits and equipment rentals require certificates.
| Cover | What it pays for |
|---|---|
| **Negative / faulty stock / digital media** | Loss or damage of footage (including data loss, faulty cards, lab errors). Requires 3-2-1 data practice; claims are denied for single-copy losses. |
| **Equipment (hired-in and owned)** | Camera, lighting, sound kit - rental houses demand it with values stated. |
| **Props, sets, wardrobe** | Damage/theft of art department items. |
| **Public / general liability** | Injury or damage to third parties and their property - locations and councils require it (typical £5-10m / $1-2m per occurrence). |
| **Employers' liability / workers' compensation** | Legally required cover for crew injury. |
| **Cast insurance** | Abandonment or delay if a named cast member (or director) can't work - requires medicals for the insured people. Essential for anything with a bond or a star. |
| **Extra expense / civil authority / weather** | Cost of delays from events outside control; weather cover is specific and expensive. |
| **Hired-in vehicles / non-owned auto** | Picture cars, unit vehicles. |
| **Drones, stunts, pyro, water, animals** | Declared separately; undeclared activity voids cover. |
Give the broker the schedule, budget, cast list, locations and a list of hazardous
activities; keep the **certificate of insurance** and know the deductibles.

## 2. Errors and omissions (E&O)
Covers claims of defamation, privacy invasion, copyright/trademark infringement arising
from the content. Distributors and broadcasters require it (typically $1m/$3m or
£1m/£3m, 3-year term) before delivery. Underwriters will ask for:
- **Chain of title**: the documents proving you own the rights - option/purchase
  agreements, writer agreements (work-for-hire or assignment), life-rights agreements,
  underlying material (book, article) licences, copyright registration.
- **Title report** (the title is clear of confusingly similar works) and **copyright report**.
- A **script clearance report** (below) and evidence that flagged items were changed or cleared.
- Releases: cast, crew, locations, anyone identifiable on screen.
- Music licences (sync and master) or composer agreements.
- A production attorney's letter confirming review for defamation/privacy if real people/events.
Start E&O in prep: it's cheaper to change a character's name than to re-shoot a scene.

## 3. Script clearance
A clearance house (or the production attorney) checks the script for: character names
that match real people in the story's setting/profession, business names, phone numbers
(use 555-01xx in the US; Ofcom-reserved ranges in the UK), addresses, licence plates,
product names, song lyrics quoted, artwork and book titles, real events and people.
The report flags each item as clear, caution or change. Keep the report and the
resulting changes for the E&O underwriter.

## 4. On-screen clearances
- **Logos / products**: incidental background use of trademarks in public is usually
  acceptable; prominent or disparaging use needs permission (or greek/dress it out).
  Product placement deals are written contracts.
- **Artwork, posters, photographs, book covers, TV footage on a screen in shot** are
  copyrighted; license, replace with cleared art, or avoid.
- **Music**: anything audible - radios, musicians in a bar, a hummed tune - needs
  sync + master licences or is replaced in post. Decide in prep; "we'll fix it later"
  is the most expensive sentence in production.
- **People**: appearance releases from anyone recognisable; crowd notices at public
  events; minors' releases from guardians.
- **Locations**: the location agreement includes the right to depict the property.

## 5. Completion bond
Financiers of larger films require a **completion guarantor** who guarantees delivery
to the distributor; they review script, schedule, budget (with 10% contingency),
key crew and cast insurance, and take over if the production goes badly over. Budget
the bond fee (around 2-3%, often with a rebate) and expect weekly cost reports to them.

## Gotchas
- Shooting a stunt or a drone that wasn't declared to the insurer.
- No cast medicals, so cast insurance doesn't attach to the lead.
- Chain of title with a gap (an early co-writer who never signed anything).
- Real phone numbers and licence plates on screen.
- Buying E&O at delivery with no clearance report - premium doubles and exclusions appear.

## Verify
- [ ] Certificate of insurance in hand before first rental/permit; hazardous activities declared.
- [ ] Chain of title documents collected and reviewed by a production attorney.
- [ ] Script clearance report obtained in prep; flagged items changed or cleared.
- [ ] Every audible piece of music and visible artwork/logo has a licence or a plan.
