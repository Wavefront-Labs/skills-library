# Directing emotional scenes

**Goal:** the truest performance is on camera in the shot that matters - not spent on
rehearsal, a wide master, or the off-camera side.

## 1. Prep with the actor
Talk privately in prep: how do they like to prepare (quiet time, music, isolation,
staying in character, chatting until the last second)? Do they want to talk about the
scene's emotional content or keep it private? Never ask an actor what personal memory
they use. Agree on **signals** ("I'll give you a minute before rolling").

## 2. Direct playable actions, not results
Not "be devastated" or "cry here" (result direction). Instead:
- the **objective** ("make him admit he knew"),
- the **action verb** ("beg", "punish", "accuse"),
- the **fact or image** ("he's packed her mother's things into bin bags").
Tears are a side-effect of fighting not to cry; many actors play *against* the emotion.
Never demand tears; if story needs them, discuss tools (menthol tear stick from
make-up as a backup) in advance.

## 3. Plan the coverage around the peak
- Shoot the **emotional actor's coverage first**, or the angle that matters most, while
  the performance is fresh. Turn around later for the other side.
- Consider **two cameras** cross-covering so the peak is captured once on both.
- Keep the master short or shoot it last; many directors pre-light all set-ups so the
  actor isn't waiting during relights.
- Fewer takes: tell the AD this scene gets time, but expect peaks on takes 1-4.
- Roll long; don't cut between takes if the actor wants to stay in it (resets in-roll).

## 4. The set
- **Closed or minimal set**; quiet before rolling; no chatter at the monitor.
- AD holds the crew; no "checks" from hair/make-up unless asked.
- Off-camera actor gives the full performance for the on-camera one.
- After a strong take, a short, specific acknowledgement; don't over-talk.

## 5. Aftercare
Give the actor space after the scene; check in privately; schedule easier work after.
For traumatic content (abuse, death), consider a mental-health support contact on
call and discuss with the intimacy coordinator if relevant.

## Gotchas
- Result direction ("more emotional") makes actors push and indicate.
- Spending the peak on the wide or on rehearsal.
- Crew noise and relights between takes breaking concentration.
- Asking for "one more for safety" after the actor is empty.

## Verify
- [ ] Actor's preparation preferences known; signals agreed.
- [ ] Direction framed as objectives, verbs and facts.
- [ ] Coverage order puts the key angle first; set-ups pre-lit.
- [ ] Closed set; aftercare planned.
