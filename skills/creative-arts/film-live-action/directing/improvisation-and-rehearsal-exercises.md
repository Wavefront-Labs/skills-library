# Improvisation and rehearsal exercises

**Goal:** actors who know their relationships and the scene's stakes in their bodies,
without the scene being worn out before the camera rolls.

## 1. Choose the right exercise for the problem
| Problem | Exercise |
|---|---|
| Relationship feels thin | **Unwritten scene improv**: improvise a scene from the characters' past (first meeting, last fight) |
| Lines sound recited | **Paraphrase read**: play the scene in their own words, then return to text |
| Actors not listening to each other | **Repetition** (Meisner): repeat each other's observations; behaviour changes the line |
| Emotion forced | **As-if**: a personal substitute situation the actor keeps private |
| Physical life missing | **Task rehearsal**: play the scene while doing a real task (cooking, packing) |
| Stakes too low | **Raise the given circumstance** in improv ("she leaves for good tonight") |
| Monotone tactics | **Verb exercise**: play each beat with a different action verb (to accuse, to charm) |

## 2. Running an improv
- Give each actor **separate private information** (objectives, secrets) to create real
  conflict; brief one at a time.
- Set **place, relationship, situation**; no ending.
- Let it run until something happens, then stop; ask what they discovered, not what they
  felt. Don't judge.
- Note discoveries that could enter the script (with writer's agreement).

## 3. Rehearsing the scene
- Read for meaning, then **block loosely** (rehearsal space taped out, or the location).
- Rehearse **up to but not including** the emotional peak of big scenes - save the
  peak for camera (Weston: don't burn it in rehearsal).
- Stop each beat when it works; don't polish to a fixed reading.

## 4. Logistics
Rehearsal time in the schedule (often 1-2 weeks for features, sometimes none). Union
rules govern paid rehearsal days. Rehearse in a private room; film rehearsals only
with agreement.

## 5. When not to rehearse
Some actors (and some scenes) are best fresh; some performers rely on technique and
dislike improv. Ask each actor how they prefer to work; adapt per actor.

## Gotchas
- Improv that becomes therapy - stay with the characters.
- Rehearsing until the scene is mechanical.
- Forcing improvisation on actors who don't work that way.
- Improvised discoveries that contradict continuity or the script without the writer knowing.

## Verify
- [ ] Problem diagnosed and matching exercise chosen.
- [ ] Private information given separately for improvs; discoveries noted.
- [ ] Emotional peaks saved for camera.
- [ ] Each actor's preferred process asked and respected.
