# Running the table read

**Goal:** the whole cast hears the whole film once, the writer and director learn what
works and what doesn't, and actors leave knowing the tone - without anyone being
"directed" in front of the group.

## 1. When and who
Once principal cast is set, ideally 1-3 weeks before shooting (late enough that the
script is near locked, early enough to rewrite). Attendees: cast, director, writer,
producers, 1st AD, script supervisor (times it), often heads of department; studio/network
executives on TV. Keep observers to the edges.

## 2. Set-up
- Tables in a rectangle; actors seated in the order they appear or by relationship;
  name cards. Director and writer at the head.
- A **stage directions reader** (often an actor or the AD) reads scene headings and
  essential action - **brief edit** the action for reading (cut camera and minor detail).
- Day players for small roles can be read by cast doubling or the reader.
- Water, scripts printed with names, current colour.
- Record audio (with consent) for the writer.

## 3. Director's opening (2-3 minutes)
Welcome; introduce everyone; one paragraph about the film's spirit and tone; set the
expectation: "No acting required, just read and listen; no notes today." Then read.

## 4. During the read
- **Don't stop** the read except for real confusion. No line notes.
- Listen for: where laughter comes (or doesn't), where attention drifts, unclear
  information, lines actors stumble over (often a writing problem), scenes that feel
  long, character voices that blur.
- Script supervisor **times** the read (a read runs faster than screen time; their
  timing estimate comes later).
- Writer marks the script.

## 5. After
- Thank everyone; producers and executives give notes **privately** later, not in the room.
- Within 24 hours: director and writer debrief, a list of script fixes and rehearsal
  priorities. Questions actors raised go into individual rehearsal sessions.
- New pages from the read go out in the next revision colour.

## Gotchas
- Directing actors in front of everyone - creates defensiveness.
- Executives giving notes in the room.
- Reading every line of action; the read drags and loses tone.
- Treating the read's timing as the film's running time.

## Verify
- [ ] Attendees, reader, seating and printed scripts arranged.
- [ ] Opening sets tone and "no notes" expectation.
- [ ] Notes taken on laughs, drift, stumbles and length; read timed.
- [ ] Debrief and script-fix list within 24 hours.
