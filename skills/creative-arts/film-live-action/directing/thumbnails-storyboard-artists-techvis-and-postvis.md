# Thumbnails, storyboard artists, techvis and postvis

**Goal:** the right level of visualisation for each scene - quick thumbnails for most,
full boards for complex ones, techvis where physics and equipment matter, postvis to
cut VFX scenes before the shots exist.

## 1. Director's thumbnails
Stick figures in small frames (6-12 per page) drawn while breaking down a scene - the
point is composition, size and direction, not art. Mark arrows for movement, frame
lines for camera moves, and shot numbers. Photographing actors/stand-ins on location
with a phone at the intended focal length (or a viewfinder app such as Artemis or
Cadrage) is a faster "photo-board".

## 2. Briefing a storyboard artist
- Provide: script pages, your thumbnails/photos, floor plans, location photos, character
  references, lens intent, aspect ratio (draw frames to the shooting ratio).
- Walk through the sequence **shot by shot** in a session (1-2 hours per complex
  sequence); the artist sketches roughs live or after.
- Rounds: **roughs** -> director notes -> **cleans** (shaded, with arrows and numbering
  matching the shot list) -> revisions after the tech scout.
- Version and date every board set; mark revised panels.
- Boards go to DP, 1st AD (scheduling), stunts, VFX, art department.

## 3. Techvis
Technical visualisation built in 3D (usually by the previs/VFX vendor) from accurate
set/location measurements: it answers **"can we actually do this?"** - crane reach, track
length, lens focal length and sensor size giving the framing, camera speed for a car
rig, sun position at a time of day. Commission it for stunts, vehicle work, big crane or
motion-control shots, and VFX plates. Deliverables: top-down diagrams with camera
positions, heights, lens, moves and timings.

## 4. Postvis
After shooting, VFX artists composite **temp CG** into the plates so the editor can
cut the sequence with timing that represents the final - essential for VFX-heavy
films' screenings and for locking VFX shot lengths (and so cost). Brief postvis with
the cut, the plates and the previs; update as the cut changes.

## 5. Choose the level
| Scene | Visualisation |
|---|---|
| Dialogue | Thumbnails / shot list |
| Action, stunts | Boards, often animatic |
| VFX-heavy | Previs + techvis + postvis |
| Vehicle/rig-dependent | Techvis |

## Gotchas
- Boards drawn to the wrong aspect ratio or impossible lenses.
- Beautiful boards nobody on set has seen.
- Previs without techvis - shots that can't be built physically.
- Postvis neglected; VFX shot lengths lock late and budgets overrun.

## Verify
- [ ] Thumbnails or photo-boards for every scene.
- [ ] Artist briefed with references; roughs and cleans versioned and distributed.
- [ ] Techvis commissioned for rig-dependent shots; diagrams on set.
- [ ] Postvis plan for VFX sequences agreed with editor and VFX supervisor.
