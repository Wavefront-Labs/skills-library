# Loop group and walla sessions

**Goal:** every crowd, restaurant, office and battlefield sounds populated with
voices that are specific to the place and period, recorded so they can be mixed,
cleared and removed for the foreign version.

## 1. Spotting
With the director/supervising sound editor, list per scene:
- **Walla**: general murmur, unintelligible or barely intelligible.
- **Specifics / callouts**: lines audible over the crowd ("Table four's ready!",
  a nurse paging a doctor), off-screen PA announcements.
- **On-screen extras** whose mouths move: they need sync lines.
- **Efforts**: breaths, grunts, laughs, reactions for background characters.
Note era, location, accents, ages and gender mix, emotional temperature.

## 2. Casting and prep
- The **loop group coordinator** casts a group (often 6-12) with the right ages and
  accents, and writes scene-appropriate lines in advance: jargon for a hospital,
  period slang, local language.
- **Clearance**: avoid real names, brands, phone numbers, real businesses; the
  clearance check covers group lines too.

## 3. Recording
- Record in **layers**: close group around one mic, then the same group further off,
  plus a stereo pair (or LCR) for spread. Perspective at record beats faking it later.
- Multiple passes with different people/voices so the crowd seems larger; vary energy
  with the scene's beats.
- Record specifics and sync lines individually to picture, like ADR.
- Keep the room dead; the mixer adds space.

## 4. Editing and delivery
- Split tracks: **group sync lines**, **intelligible specifics**, **walla beds**,
  **efforts**. They live in the dialogue stem.
- For the **M&E**, intelligible language must come out; efforts and truly
  unintelligible walla may stay per the distributor's spec. Keeping them on separate
  tracks makes that a mute, not a re-edit.
- Thin out during the mix: walla under principal dialogue is the first thing the mixer drops.

## Gotchas
- Generic "rhubarb" murmur from libraries in a specific setting (modern English
  walla in a 1940s Paris cafe).
- An audible line naming a real company that wasn't cleared.
- All walla recorded close: the crowd sits on top of the actors.
- Walla mixed into the same tracks as specifics: M&E needs a costly split later.

## Verify
- [ ] Spotting list with every crowd scene, specifics and on-screen extras.
- [ ] Lines cleared and written for period/location.
- [ ] Close, mid and wide layers recorded.
- [ ] Tracks split for the M&E.
