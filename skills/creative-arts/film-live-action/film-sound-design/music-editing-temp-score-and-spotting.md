# Music editing, temp score and spotting

**Goal:** the right music in the right place at the right length - temp that
communicates, a score commissioned from a clear spotting session, cues cut and
conformed invisibly, and paperwork that gets the composer and rights holders paid.

## 1. Temp music (during the edit)
- Purpose: communicate tone and pace to the director, editor, test audiences and the
  composer. Choose temp that suggests *function* (tension, release, scale) rather than
  a specific famous theme; mix sources (library, other scores, the composer's own
  earlier work) and avoid the single iconic cue the composer can't replace - **temp
  love** is the main reason scores get rejected.
- Keep temp in a consistent level and format (stereo, 48 kHz, named `TEMP_` with the
  source and the scene); track in a music bin with a **temp log** (where from, what it
  does, who chose it). Never let temp leak into a festival cut without a licence.
- Share the temp with the composer early with notes on what *works about it*, not
  "write this".

## 2. Spotting session
With the director, composer, music editor, editor (and music supervisor if there's
source music), watch the locked (or near-locked) cut reel by reel and decide for each cue:
- **In/out timecodes** (where music starts/ends, with the kind of start - sneak, hit,
  hard cut), **function** (what it must do emotionally), **hit points** (events the
  music should mark or deliberately avoid), source vs score, and relation to the temp.
- Number cues `1M1, 1M2...` (reel-cue) and log in a **spotting notes / cue sheet**
  document: cue ID, title, in/out, duration, description, notes. The music editor
  prepares **timing notes** (every relevant picture event with timecode and seconds
  from the cue start) for the composer.
- Decide the **tempo/click** handling for on-camera musical performances and
  playback material.

## 3. Cutting music to picture
- Cut on **bars/beats** and phrase boundaries; use crossfades at zero crossings or on
  downbeats; shorten by removing whole phrases (verse 2) rather than mid-phrase; extend
  by looping a bar at the same bar position. Keep a **tempo map** in the DAW for the
  cue (tap tempo or import from the composer's session) so edits snap.
- Hit points: slide the cue to land the hit, then fix the start with an edit upstream;
  pitch/time stretch only tiny amounts (±3%) unless it's a design choice.
- Source music: match **perspective** (room/radio/PA) with the mixer; ensure the
  licensed version/edit is what plays at the mix.
- Deliver score stems from the composer (e.g. strings/brass/perc/synth/choir) so the
  mix can rebalance under dialogue without asking for a remix.

## 4. Conforming after picture changes
Picture re-cuts after spotting are normal. From the **change list**, the music editor
re-conforms every affected cue in the DAW (Pro Tools **Conform / EdiLoad**, Nuendo
ReConform, or manually: slip, trim, re-loop, or re-request from the composer when a
cue's structure no longer fits). Report to the composer which cues changed and by how
much; some need rewriting before the recording session. Keep cue versions dated.

## 5. Licensing and cue sheets
- **Licensed songs**: need both **sync** (composition/publisher) and **master** (the
  recording) licences, per territory/media/term; the music supervisor negotiates;
  budget early (festival licence vs all-media perpetuity differ by orders of
  magnitude). Library/production music comes with blanket or per-cue rates.
- **Score**: composer agreement covers writing fees, recording budget, rights (work-for-
  hire or licence), soundtrack album and performing-rights share.
- The **PRO cue sheet** (PRS/ASCAP/BMI/APRA…) lists every cue: title, composer,
  publisher, usage (background/visual vocal/theme), duration, timecode - the
  broadcaster/streamer files it; without it, nobody is paid. Export from the DAW
  session markers or the cue sheet document; verify against the final mix.

## 6. Delivery to the mix
All cues as stems and stereo/5.1 premixes at the reel's timecode with 2-pops, labelled
by cue ID; source music with clean and futzed versions; a cue list with in/outs
matching the picture lock; temp fully removed (or clearly marked if a temp cue was
licensed for use).

## Gotchas
- Temp cue so loved it is licensed at a price that eats the music budget.
- Spotting on an unlocked cut without a conform plan; every cue drifts.
- Mid-phrase cuts with crossfades that pump; cut on bars.
- Score delivered as a stereo mix only; dialogue fights the strings and nobody can fix it.
- No cue sheet filed; the composer's royalties vanish.

## Verify
- [ ] Temp log maintained; temp chosen for function; composer briefed with what works.
- [ ] Spotting notes with cue IDs, in/outs, function, hit points; timing notes to composer.
- [ ] Cues cut on bars with tempo maps; conformed per change list with versions.
- [ ] Sync and master licences and composer agreement in place; PRO cue sheet complete and verified.
