# Designed sounds and synthesis

**Goal:** sounds for things that don't exist or need to be bigger than reality
(creatures, weapons, vehicles, magic, sci-fi interfaces, signature impacts) that
feel real, carry emotion and sit in the mix.

## 1. Start from the story
Ask what the sound must *mean* (threatening, sad, alien, mechanical, old) and where it
sits against dialogue and music. Collect references; record or pull candidate
sources. Organic sources (animals, materials, voices) give life; synthesis gives
control and the impossible.

## 2. Layer by job
Build in bands, each layer doing one thing:
- **Sub/low** (body, weight): pitched-down impacts, sine/synth sub sweeps.
- **Mid** (identity): the character sound - an animal, a metal scrape, a voice.
- **High** (detail, air): sizzle, debris, breath, mechanical clicks.
- **Transient** (attack): a sharp element aligned to the visual hit.
High-pass and low-pass each layer so they don't fight; align transients sample-accurately.

## 3. Manipulation
- **Pitch/time**: pitching down adds size; varispeed (pitch + time together) keeps
  natural character; formant-preserving shift changes size without "chipmunking".
- **Reverse**, convolution with odd impulses (metal, pipes), spectral smearing,
  granular stretching (pads, textures from tiny sources).
- **Synthesis**: subtractive/FM synths for sweeps, risers, sci-fi tones; modular or
  Kyma for complex controllable systems; Doppler and pitch automation for passbys.
- **Creature voices**: blend several animals by role (body from big animals,
  articulation from vocal performers, texture from small animals), drive with a
  performed human vocal via tools like Dehumaniser. Classic example: the Jurassic Park
  T. rex blended baby elephant, tiger and alligator recordings.

## 4. Make it mixable
- Deliver designs as **split layers** (or stems: low/mid/high) on the FX tracks, not
  one printed file - the mixer needs to thin them under dialogue.
- Leave headroom; watch the sub so the LFE isn't the only thing carrying it.
- Keep a **signature sound** consistent every time it appears (same base design, varied).
- Save sessions/presets so designs can be revised after picture changes.

## Gotchas
- Stacking layers in the same band: louder but muddier, not bigger.
- Designs that only work in the edit room at loud levels; check at mix level and on TV speakers.
- Sources with copyright or library licence limits used in a signature sound.
- Fighting dialogue frequencies (1-4 kHz) at story-critical moments.

## Verify
- [ ] Each layer's role and band defined; nothing redundant.
- [ ] Delivered split for the mix; session saved for revisions.
- [ ] Reads at low level and on small speakers.
- [ ] All sources licensed or original.
