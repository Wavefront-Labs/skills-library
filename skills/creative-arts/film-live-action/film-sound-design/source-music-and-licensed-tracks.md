# Source music and licensed tracks

**Goal:** songs and source cues that sound like they live in the scene (or play as
needle drops over it), edited within what the licence allows and reported accurately.

## 1. Source vs score
- **Source (diegetic)**: music characters can hear - radio, club, band, car stereo.
- **Needle drop**: a licensed recording used as score-like music over the scene.
- Know which it is per cue; it drives treatment and the cue-sheet usage type
  (background/visual vocal/visual instrumental, etc.).

## 2. Licences dictate the edit
- Two rights: **sync** (the composition, from the publisher) and **master use** (the
  recording, from the label). Quotes specify duration, media, territory, term and
  usage type.
- Edit within the licensed **duration**; cutting out a verse or looping a chorus is
  normally fine, but changing lyrics' meaning, remixing or using the song in a way the
  licence excluded is not. Ask the music supervisor when unsure.
- Re-records or sound-alikes need their own clearance.

## 3. Editing songs
- Cut on downbeats and phrase boundaries; crossfade within the same musical moment
  (same chord, same instrumentation) so the edit is inaudible.
- Plan the out: the song ending with the scene (backtimed so the final chord lands on
  the cut) or fading with scene change.
- For scene cuts that interrupt source music, keep continuity: the song keeps
  playing in real time across angles within a scene.

## 4. Making source sound like source
- **Futzing**: EQ/distortion for the playback device (phone, transistor radio, car).
- **Worldizing**: play the music through a speaker in a real space and re-record it
  (Murch, *American Graffiti*), or emulate with convolution reverb of a similar space.
- Perspective: change EQ/level/reverb as the camera moves closer to or away from the
  source; through a wall = lows and muffled.
- Playback on set (lip sync, dancing) must match the version used in post - same mix,
  same tempo.

## 5. Delivery
- Source music lives in the MX stem; separate source from score tracks for the mixer.
- **Cue sheet**: exact in/out timings, usage type, writers, publishers, label. Update
  after every picture change; PROs pay from it.

## Gotchas
- Editing a song to a length longer than the licensed duration.
- Temp needle drops the director loves that can't be cleared - flag early.
- Futz printed into the only copy; keep a clean version for revisions.
- Cue-sheet timings from an old cut.

## Verify
- [ ] Each cue's licence terms (duration, usage) checked against the edit.
- [ ] Edits inaudible; continuity across cuts in-scene.
- [ ] Source treatment matches device and perspective.
- [ ] Cue sheet matches the final picture.
