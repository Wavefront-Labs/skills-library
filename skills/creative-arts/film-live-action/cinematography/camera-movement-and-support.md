# Camera movement and support

**Goal:** the camera moves when the story moves and is still when stillness means
something - with the tool that gives the right *feel* of movement at a cost the day can
afford.

## 1. Movement has a grammar
- **Static**: observation, stability, formality; lets performance carry it. Default.
- **Pan / tilt** (on a fluid head): follow, reveal; cheap and invisible when motivated by movement in frame.
- **Push-in** (dolly in): entering a character's thought; the slower, the more internal.
- **Pull-out**: isolation, consequence, ending.
- **Tracking / lateral**: travel with the subject, parallel energy; backgrounds stream.
- **Arc / orbit**: tension, reorientation, "the world turns around them"; overused.
- **Handheld**: subjectivity, instability, documentary truth; degree matters (Easyrig +
  shoulder = "alive" not "shaky").
- **Steadicam / gimbal float**: dreamlike, continuous space, following through doors.
- **Crane / boom**: scale, omniscience, the god view; openings and endings.
- **Drone**: establishing scale cheaply; dangerous as a cliché.
Pick one movement language per film or per character; a mixed vocabulary reads as noise.

## 2. Motivate and time the move
A move begins on a cue (a line, a look, a step) and ends on a frame that is itself a
composition. Design moves as **start frame -> cue -> end frame**; rehearse the actors to
the cue, not the camera to the actors. Speed of a push-in is set by the line it lands on.
Combine: a dolly-in with a slight boom-down feels different from the same dolly flat - test.

## 3. Choosing the support
| Tool | Feel | Needs | Cost/time |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dolly on track** (Fisher, Chapman, PeeWee) | Precise, heavy, repeatable; boom arm adds vertical | Dolly grip, track laying, level ground or wedges | Moderate; track is the time sink |
| **Dolly on floor / dance floor** | Freer paths, crab moves | Smooth floor (lay dance floor), skilled grip | Moderate |
| **Slider** (1-2 m) | Small parallax, product/insert, subtle drift | One person | Cheap, fast |
| **Steadicam** | Floating, long takes, stairs, through doors | Specialist operator, time to balance; lines matched | Day rate for operator + rig; worth it for the right shot |
| **Gimbal** (Ronin, Movi) | Similar float; more "machine" feel at speed; remote operation on vehicles/cranes | Balancing, a focus puller on wireless, battery discipline | Cheaper than Steadicam; fatigue on long takes |
| **Crane / jib / Technocrane** | Big vertical/horizontal moves, telescoping | Crane crew, remote head (Libra/Scorpio), safety zone | Expensive; schedule it as its own half-day |
| **Handheld / Easyrig** | Immediate, reactive | Operator stamina, appropriate lens (wider) | Free, but blocking must be loose |
| **Vehicle rigs** (hostess tray, hood mount, process trailer, Russian arm) | Driving scenes | Grips, precision driver, permits | See car-rig practice |
| **Drone** | Aerials | Licensed pilot, permissions, weather | Half-day |
| **Motion control** | Identical repeated moves for VFX/plates | Mocon tech, programming time | Expensive, slow |

## 4. Working with the team
- Brief the **key grip** on the move's start/end frames and cue the night before: they
  decide track vs floor and pre-lay.
- Give the **Steadicam/gimbal operator** the blocking and lens; they'll want a wide-ish
  lens (18-35 mm S35) and marks; long lenses on a moving rig show every wobble.
- The **dolly grip** is a performer: rehearse the speed curve ("ease in on 'you knew'").
  Call the cue; they'll find the timing.
- Focus moves with the camera; plan marks or use a rangefinder (Cine Tape/Light Ranger)
  for continuous moves.
- Monitor the move for **horizon and headroom** at the end frame; moves that end badly
  are the ones that get cut around.

## Gotchas
- Moving because the scene is static, instead of fixing the scene.
- A dolly track visible in the reverse because nobody planned the second angle.
- Handheld with a long lens; unusable.
- Steadicam booked for a day with no time to balance; the first shot is at lunch.
- Drone establishing shots in every act; the audience leaves the story each time.

## Verify
- [ ] Each move has a start frame, a cue and an end frame written in the shot list.
- [ ] Support chosen per move and the grip/operator briefed the day before.
- [ ] Lens and focus plan compatible with the rig.
- [ ] Movement language consistent within the scene/film.
