# Lighting and exposing diverse skin tones

**Goal:** every face reads with depth, detail and its true colour - especially in scenes
where very light and very dark skin share the frame.

## 1. Meter the skin, not a formula
Spot-meter each actor's skin under the key. Darker skin may read 1-3 stops under
a grey-card exposure; decide where it should sit (often at or slightly below key for
richness, not dragged up to "Caucasian +1"). Check false colour on the actual face; don't
let auto exposure or a reflective meter "average" it.

## 2. Shape and sheen
Darker skin reflects more **specular** light relative to diffuse: large, soft sources
produce a beautiful sheen that defines the face's shape; small hard sources create hot
spots. Techniques widely used by DPs:
- Use **large soft sources** at a slightly raking angle; let the sheen model the face.
- Make-up: a light moisturiser or oil (not powder) to control the sheen, agreed with
  the make-up department.
- **Negative fill** to keep shape rather than adding more light from the front.
- Avoid flat front fill that greys the skin.

## 3. Colour
Warmer light (amber/bastard amber, CTO fractions) often enriches darker skin; very cool
or green-spiked LED sources can make it look ashen. Check LED fixture **TLCI/SSI**
quality; poor spectrum hurts darker skin first. Bounce off warm surfaces (gold/ bleached
muslin) for fill.

## 4. Mixed skin tones in one frame
- Light each actor separately where staging allows (separate keys, flags or nets to
  reduce light on the lighter face, or add a touch on the darker).
- Expose for the darker skin's detail and protect the lighter skin's highlights - log
  formats have room; check both on the waveform.
- In wide shots where separate control is impossible, position actors relative to the
  source (darker skin slightly closer to the key).

## 5. Reference for post
Shoot a chart plus each actor's face in neutral light at the start (a "skin tone
reference"); note intended placement. Tell the colourist and dailies colourist so dailies
don't "correct" skin toward a generic target.

## Gotchas
- Exposing every face to the same IRE.
- Powdering out the sheen and losing shape.
- Cheap LEDs with poor spectral quality.
- Dailies auto-balance brightening and desaturating darker skin.

## Verify
- [ ] Each actor's skin metered and placement decided.
- [ ] Large soft sources and negative fill used; sheen controlled with make-up.
- [ ] Fixture spectral quality checked; warm fill where it serves.
- [ ] Skin-tone references shot and communicated to dailies/grade.
