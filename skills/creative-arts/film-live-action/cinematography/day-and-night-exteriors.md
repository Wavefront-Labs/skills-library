# Day and night exteriors

**Goal:** the sun and the night are treated as sources you place, shape and schedule -
so a scene shot over six hours cuts as a single moment.

## 1. Day exteriors: schedule first
- Plot the **sun path** for the location and date (Sun Seeker, Helios, PhotoPills). Choose
  the camera direction so the sun is **behind or beside** the subject (backlight/side light)
  for the longest part of the day; shoot the wide when the sun is where you want it and
  turn into the coverage as it moves.
- Shoot the reverse that faces the sun first or last, when the angle is low and softer, or
  silk it.
- Overcast = a giant soft source: flat but kind to faces; add contrast with negative fill
  and a hard backlight (HMI) if the sky allows. Mixed cloud is the enemy - wait for a
  consistent state or shoot the close-ups under a silk that makes the state irrelevant.
- **Golden hour** lasts ~20-40 min; rehearse fully, shoot the one shot that needs it,
  shoot the rest earlier under control. Blue hour after sunset gives a few minutes of
  ambient "night" with detail.

## 2. Day exterior toolkit
- **Overhead silk / diffusion** (12x12, 20x20 on a frame, or a butterfly) over the actors
  turns the sun into a soft top-key and removes cloud variation. Rig with ballast and
  spotters; wind is the hazard.
- **Backlight the sun** and **bounce** (Ultrabounce, muslin, beadboard) or a hard fill
  (HMI through 250) into the face. Keep fill below key for shape.
- **Negative fill** on the shadow side of faces to restore contrast under open sky.
- **Solids/floppies** to cut skylight from the top, **nets** to knock the background down.
- **ND and polariser** to hold the stop and control sky/reflections; IR-ND on cameras that
  need it to avoid far-red contamination at heavy ND.
- Big HMIs (M90, 18k) through diffusion to compete with sun on a face in a wide - or
  accept silhouettes and plan it.
- Rig practical shade for crew and monitors; a video village under a tent is not vanity.

## 3. Continuity across the day
Decide the look (sun direction, contrast) per scene and fight to keep it: as the sun
moves, move the silk and bounce, swap backlight to an HMI, or re-block subtly. Keep a
reference frame on the monitor (A/B wipe) and check the sky's exposure, not just faces.
Record the time and sun position per setup for the colourist.

## 4. Night exteriors
Real darkness has no detail; night on screen is an *ambient base* plus motivated sources:
- **Ambient / moon**: a large soft source from high (condor with 18k HMI through a 20x20,
  or balloon light, or an array of LED panels), usually from the side/back, cool but not
  blue cartoon (4500-6500 K vs tungsten/warm practicals). Keep it 2-4 stops under key.
- **Motivated practicals**: street lamps (replace bulbs with controllable LED/sodium look),
  shop windows, car headlights, phone screens - these are the keys. Add hidden units to
  extend what the practical "does".
- **Wet-down** the street so sources reflect and the ground isn't a black hole.
- **Backlight/rim** separates people from darkness; haze or smoke carries beams and depth.
- Expose for faces at the stop; let the night fall off. Fast lenses and higher EI help but
  noise in shadows is the trade - test the camera's clean range.
- **Day-for-night** (underexpose 2-3 stops, cool balance, no sky in shot, grade) works for
  wides and landscapes; faces usually need real night or blue hour.

## 5. Logistics
Night shoots: condor/cranes need riggers and permits; generators placed far with cable
runs; crew safety lighting away from frame; turnaround flips the schedule - group night
exteriors at the end of a week. Day exteriors: weather cover scene planned; sunscreen,
water and shade on the call sheet.

## Gotchas
- Shooting the wide at noon with top sun and raccoon eyes, then lighting beautiful close-ups that don't match.
- Blue night with no warm contrast - "day with a blue filter".
- Forgetting the sky in the frame when exposing faces; blown-out or inconsistent.
- Silk rigged without spotters/ballast in wind.
- Not rehearsing before golden hour and losing the light mid-take.

- [ ] Night exteriors have an ambient base, motivated practicals, rim and a wet-down plan.
