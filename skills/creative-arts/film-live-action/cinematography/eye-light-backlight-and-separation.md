# Eye light, backlight and separation

**Goal:** faces with life in the eyes and subjects that stand off the background -
with sources that feel motivated, not "three-point lighting".

## 1. Eye light and catchlights
- A **catchlight** is the reflection of a source in the eye; it gives life. Often the key
  already provides it - check in the monitor (zoom the image).
- If eyes are dead (deep sockets, low-key, hats): add a small **eye light** near the
  lens axis - an LED panel or a dimmed small fresnel through diffusion, a ring of LED
  around the matte box (e.g. an "obie"-style light), or a bounce card under the lens.
- Keep it **low intensity** (2-3 stops under key) so it only shows in the eye, not on the
  face; dimmer or ND gel.
- Shape: a square softbox reads as a window; a ring reads as a ring - choose deliberately.
- Remove double catchlights from fill sources that look artificial.

## 2. Backlight and rim (edge) light
- **Backlight** from behind and above: separates hair and shoulders from the background.
- **Rim / edge**: from behind at the side, drawing a line on the cheek/shoulder - often
  motivated by a window, practical, sun or street light behind the actor.
- **Kicker**: from 3/4 behind on the shadow side, catching the cheek edge.
- Intensity: start around key level for hard sun-like rims; 1-2 stops under for subtle
  separation. Check that it doesn't flare the lens (flag it from camera, use a matte box).
- Avoid hot tops of heads in close-ups; adjust height/angle.

## 3. Separation without backlight
- **Tonal**: light background darker (or brighter) than the face.
- **Colour**: warm faces, cool backgrounds.
- **Depth of field** and **atmosphere** (haze reveals light shafts and separates layers).
- Pools of light on backgrounds rather than flat wall light.

## 4. Motivate
Place backlights where a real source exists in the story world (a window behind, a
lamp, the sun). In night exteriors, a back/edge light can be "moonlight" or street lights
- consistent direction across coverage.

## Gotchas
- Eye light too bright: a flat, front-lit face.
- Backlight in every shot regardless of motivation - "TV look".
- Rim light flaring the lens.
- Catchlights from different sources in reverse shots that don't match.

## Verify
- [ ] Catchlights present in key close-ups; eye light dimmed so it doesn't light the face.
- [ ] Backlight/rim motivated and flagged off the lens.
- [ ] Separation achieved tonally/by colour where backlight isn't motivated.
- [ ] Directions consistent across coverage.
