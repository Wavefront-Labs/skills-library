# Drone cinematography and regulations

**Goal:** aerial shots that look like cinema, flown legally by qualified operators with
a safe, approved plan.

## 1. Legal first
- **US**: commercial flight under **FAA Part 107** - remote pilot certificate, registered
  aircraft, max 400 ft AGL, visual line of sight, no flight over people unless the
  aircraft category allows or a waiver; airspace authorisation (LAANC) in controlled
  airspace; night operations with training and anti-collision lighting.
- **UK**: CAA Open/Specific categories - film work commonly needs an **Operational
  Authorisation** (Specific category) with an operations manual, insurance, and
  distances from uninvolved people; Flyer ID and Operator ID.
- Other countries differ; local permits, national parks and cities have extra rules.
- Landowner permission for take-off/landing; notify the film office and police where required.

## 2. Hire the right team
A licensed drone company: **pilot** (flies) and **camera operator** (controls gimbal/
camera in dual-operator mode) for complex moves; a **visual observer/spotter**. Ask for
their authorisations, insurance certificate and risk assessment.

## 3. Aircraft and camera
- Integrated (DJI Inspire 3 with X9 full-frame) for most work; heavy-lift drones (Alta,
  Freefly) for cinema cameras (ARRI/RED) - bigger permissions and crew.
- FPV drones for dives and fly-throughs - specialist pilots; risk assessment for
  proximity.
- Match codec/log profile to the main cameras; flight time 15-25 min per battery - plan
  shots per battery.

## 4. Flight planning
Survey the site (maps, obstacles, power lines, birds, RF interference), take-off/landing
zone, **safety perimeter** with marshals, weather limits (wind, rain), and emergency
procedures (return-to-home, landing spots). Brief everyone on set; radio contact with
the 1st AD.

## 5. Moves that work
Reveal (rise over an obstacle), slow push toward subject, top-down (God's eye), orbit,
tracking a vehicle (coordinate speeds), pull-back from close to vast. Smooth, slow
moves read as cinema; fast, jerky ones as "drone footage". Shoot at higher frame rates
for smoothing if needed.

## Gotchas
- Unlicensed operators - insurance void, production liable.
- Flying over crowds or uninvolved public.
- Not checking airspace (airports, heliports, temporary restrictions).
- One battery left when the light is perfect.

## Verify
- [ ] Authorisations, insurance and landowner permissions in hand.
- [ ] Pilot, camera operator and observer booked.
- [ ] Site survey, perimeter and emergency plan briefed.
- [ ] Shot list mapped to batteries and light.
