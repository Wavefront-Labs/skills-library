# Effect filters, rear filters and nets

**Goal:** optical effects that can't be faked convincingly in the grade - flares,
streaks, halation and texture - chosen by testing and applied consistently.

## 1. Colour filters
- **Warming** (81 series, Coral 1/4-1, Antique Suede) and **cooling** (82 series): subtle
  overall shifts; with digital, mostly replaced by white balance and grade, but still
  used for in-camera mood on single-camera looks.
- **Grads** (ND grads soft/hard edge, colour grads like tobacco or blue): darken or tint
  the sky - must be positioned in a rotating/ sliding matte box stage; the line shows on
  camera moves (tilt reveals the edge).
- **Polarisers** (see filters skill) for reflections and skies.

## 2. Effect filters
- **Streak filters** (blue/amber/clear streaks, e.g. Tiffen Streak, Schneider True-Streak):
  anamorphic-style horizontal flares from point sources. Orientation set by rotating the filter.
- **Star / cross** filters: points on highlights (very stylised).
- **Diffusion** variants (Black Pro-Mist, Glimmerglass, Hollywood Black Magic): halation
  around highlights and softening of skin - strength 1/8 to 1.
- **Low-con / ultra-con**: lift shadows by spreading light.
Effects grow with highlight brightness and lens length: test at the real focal lengths
and contrast.

## 3. Rear filters and nets
- **Nets** (fine silk or nylon stocking) stretched behind the lens on the rear element
  (rear mount) - classic glamour diffusion: soft halation, maintains sharpness better than
  front nets and avoids reflections. Some lenses/mounts have rear gel holders (ARRI LPL/PL
  adaptors, Cooke and Zeiss with rear filter slots, or tape-on mounts).
- Rear **gels** (ND, colour) in rear slots on lenses that support them; avoids large
  front filters on wide zooms.
- Caveats: changes the back focus slightly (re-check focus marks/collimation); the net
  pattern can show at small apertures (T8+) - shoot wider open; affects all focal lengths
  differently.
- Front nets on the matte box: bigger effect, pattern visible if not out of focus enough.

## 4. Test and log
Shoot a test with faces, practicals and a backlit highlight for each filter and strength;
grade it as you would. Log filters per set-up in camera reports so matching shots
match; tell VFX (filters complicate plates - shoot clean plates without heavy effects).

## Gotchas
- Grad lines visible on tilts.
- Different diffusion strength between shots in a scene.
- Rear net pattern visible at deep stops.
- Effect filters on VFX plates without clean versions.

## Verify
- [ ] Filters tested at real focal lengths and stops.
- [ ] Logged per set-up in camera reports.
- [ ] Rear filter/net focus checked; stop range kept wide enough.
- [ ] VFX informed; clean plates where needed.
