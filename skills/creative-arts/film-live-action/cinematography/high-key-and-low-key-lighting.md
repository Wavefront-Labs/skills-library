# High-key and low-key lighting

**Goal:** a contrast style chosen for the story, executed consistently across a scene
and the whole film.

## 1. Definitions
- **High-key**: low contrast (key:fill around 2:1 or less), bright overall, open
  shadows, soft sources, light backgrounds. Comedy, commercials, sitcom, optimism, clinical
  or institutional worlds.
- **Low-key**: high contrast (4:1 to 8:1+), large dark areas, hard or directional
  sources, faces partially in shadow. Noir, thriller, horror, drama with secrets.
- **Chiaroscuro**: strong light/dark modelling on faces and objects (from painting) -
  usually one dominant soft-ish source from the side with little fill.

## 2. Building high-key
- Big soft sources (book lights, large diffusion frames, bounced light) frontal-ish to
  wrap the face; fill from camera side; backgrounds lit to within 1 stop of key.
- Keep **direction** so faces still have shape - high-key isn't flat.
- Control spill with flags; avoid shadows on walls from multiple sources.

## 3. Building low-key
- One **motivated** key (window, lamp, street light) from the side or behind
  (**far-side key**: the key on the side of the face away from camera gives modelling).
- **Negative fill** (black flags/floppies) on the shadow side to deepen contrast instead
  of reducing the key.
- Backgrounds darker than faces; pick out depth with small pools and practicals.
- Keep the eyes readable (see eye light) unless darkness is the point.
- Meter the shadow side: where do you want detail? Log cameras keep 5-6 stops under key;
  decide which shadows go black.

## 4. Consistency
Write the ratio and key direction per scene in a lighting diary; match it across
coverage (singles must have the same ratio as the master). The grade can push contrast
but can't invent shadow shape.

## 5. Genre and story
Style can shift within a film to track the story (warm high-key home, low-key once the
secret is out). Agree the arc with the director and production designer (walls and
costumes affect the key too - dark walls make low-key easy).

## Gotchas
- "Low-key" achieved by underexposure - noisy, muddy blacks.
- High-key with no direction - flat TV look.
- Ratios changing between master and singles.
- Dark set walls fighting a high-key brief.

## Verify
- [ ] Style and ratio per scene agreed and logged.
- [ ] Low-key built with negative fill and a motivated key, exposure correct for faces.
- [ ] High-key has soft, directional key and controlled backgrounds.
- [ ] Coverage matches the master's ratio.
