# Lens selection, focal length and framing

**Goal:** the lens is chosen for how it renders *space and faces*, the frame is chosen for
the story, and both are consistent across a scene so the edit cuts invisibly.

## 1. Focal length is perspective, size is distance
Shot size comes from camera-to-subject distance; focal length then sets how much
background is in the frame and how compressed it looks. For the same close-up:
- **Wide (18-25 mm on S35):** camera close, background expansive, faces distort near the
  edges and in the nose; movement toward camera feels fast. Intimate-but-uneasy, or spatial.
- **Normal (32-50 mm S35):** roughly human perspective; the default for dialogue.
- **Long (75-135+ mm):** camera far, background compressed and soft, faces flattering,
  movement toward camera feels slow; isolates subjects in crowds.
Decide the scene's *perspective language* first (one lens family for a character or a
world is a real tool), then set sizes by distance.

## 2. Sensor format changes everything
Field of view depends on focal length *and* sensor width. Rough equivalence: full-frame
(36 mm) needs ~1.4-1.5x the focal length of Super 35 (~24.9 mm) for the same view -
a 50 mm on FF frames like a 35 mm on S35, with shallower depth of field at the same stop.
Large format (Alexa 65, 54 mm) multiplies again. Check lens **coverage** (image circle)
for the sensor/mode in use - S35 lenses vignette on FF in many focal lengths; camera
makers publish illumination guides. Decide format before building the lens list.

## 3. Spherical vs anamorphic
- **Anamorphic** (2x squeeze, sometimes 1.3x/1.5x/1.8x): oval bokeh, horizontal flares,
  wider horizontal field for a given focal length, "cinema" look; costs: slower stops,
  close-focus limits, breathing and distortion at wide focal lengths, more light needed,
  desqueeze in monitoring and post. 2x squeeze on a 4:3 sensor yields 2.39:1 natively.
- **Spherical** with a 2.39 crop is cleaner, faster and cheaper; choose anamorphic for
  the artefacts, not the ratio.

## 4. Aspect ratio
1.85:1 (standard flat), 2.39:1 (scope), 1.78 (16:9 TV), 2:1 (Univisium, popular for
streaming), 1.33/1.37 (Academy, deliberate period or intimacy), 1.19 and 4:3 for
stylistic choices. Decide early: it determines framing, set build, lens choice, and
the **framing chart** shot on day one (frame leader with the delivery ratio and safe
areas) so editorial and VFX know the intended frame. Shoot with frame lines for the
delivery ratio *and* a common-top or common-centre protect for 16:9 if TV delivery
needs it.

## 5. Framing conventions (to keep or break on purpose)
- Headroom shrinks as the shot tightens; eyes about a third from the top in a CU.
- **Lead room / nose room** in the direction of look or movement.
- Shot sizes named consistently (WS, MWS, MS, MCU, CU, ECU) and matched across the
  scene's reverses: same size, same focal length, same height, same distance.
- Camera height: eye level neutral; low = power/looming; high = diminished/observed.
- Compose in layers (foreground, subject, background) to use the lens's depth rendering.

## 6. Choose the set and test it
Modern "clean" (Signature, Zeiss Supreme, Cooke S7) vs vintage/character (Cooke Speed
Panchro, Canon K35, Baltar, rehoused stills lenses) vs anamorphic (Panavision C/E/G,
Atlas, Cooke). Test on the actual camera with skin, a backlight for flares, a chart for
distortion and breathing, close focus and the stop you'll shoot at - and **match sizes
with the editor in mind**: a vintage set with mismatched colour across focal lengths
costs grade time. Keep a lens list with T-stop, close focus, weight and front diameter
(matte box, filters).

## Gotchas
- "Zooming in" with the lens instead of moving the camera, changing the scene's perspective mid-sequence.
- Mixed formats on one show without a conversion plan (crop/scale) for VFX.
- Not testing coverage; vignetting discovered in dailies.
- Shooting anamorphic without desqueezed monitoring - everyone frames the squeezed image.
- Reverses on different focal lengths or heights; the scene feels "off" in the cut.

## Verify
- [ ] Format and aspect ratio decided; framing chart shot and sent to post.
- [ ] Each scene has a lens plan; reverses matched in size, focal length, height, distance.
- [ ] Lens coverage checked for the sensor mode in use.
- [ ] Lens test viewed on a calibrated monitor before the lens order.
