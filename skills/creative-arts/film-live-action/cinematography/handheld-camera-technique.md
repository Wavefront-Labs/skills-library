# Handheld camera technique

**Goal:** handheld movement that serves the scene - invisible and breathing, or
urgent and raw - sustainable through a 12-hour day.

## 1. Build and balance
- Shoulder-mount build: baseplate with shoulder pad, the camera's **centre of gravity
  over the shoulder** (move battery/accessories to counterbalance a heavy lens).
- Viewfinder at eye position with your head upright; handgrips at a natural elbow angle.
- Keep it as light as practical: small lenses, compact wireless, minimal matte box.
- Smaller cameras held at chest level (or on a cage) are less stable - use a shoulder rig
  or a monopod/Easyrig.

## 2. Body mechanics
- Stance: feet shoulder-width, knees soft; move from the legs and hips, not the arms.
- Walk heel-toe ("Groucho walk") with bent knees to absorb steps; move sideways crossing
  feet carefully.
- Breathe out slowly on still moments; brace an elbow against the body or a wall.
- An assistant spots you when walking backwards and manages cables.

## 3. Lens and focus
Wider lenses (18-35 mm S35) hide shake; long lenses amplify it - above ~50 mm handheld
looks nervous. Focus puller with wireless follow focus; or operator focuses in doc work
(autofocus aids on some cameras).

## 4. Style
- **Invisible handheld**: soft, breathing frames, operator anticipates; used in many
  intimate dramas.
- **Observational**: reacts to action, reframes after it happens (doc vérité feel).
- **Urgent**: aggressive moves, whip pans, crash zooms; reserve for chaos.
Discuss with the director what motivates movement: the character's emotional state, the
camera as an observer, or a participant.

## 5. Long days (docs especially)
- **Easyrig / Flowcine Serene**: a vest with an arm that carries the camera's weight
  from above - transfers load to hips; saves backs on 10-hour days.
- Rest the camera on a beanbag or high-hat between takes; shoulder-pad and back care.
- Battery and media swaps without losing moments - keep accessories on the body.

## Gotchas
- Front-heavy build pulling the shoulder forward; fatigue in an hour.
- Long lenses handheld.
- Shaky "style" added where the scene doesn't motivate it.
- No spotter when backing up.

## Verify
- [ ] Build balanced on the shoulder; weight minimised.
- [ ] Lens choice suits handheld; focus plan set.
- [ ] Style motivated and agreed with the director.
- [ ] Support (Easyrig/ rest) planned for long days; spotter assigned.
