# Interview setup and technique for documentary

**Goal:** an interview that gives the edit real, specific, emotionally true material in
clean picture and sound — and leaves the subject feeling respected. In most documentaries
the sit-down interview is the spine the verite footage and archive hang on.

## 1. Before the day
- **Pre-interview** (phone or in person, often without camera): learn their story, the
  vivid moments, and how they talk. Don't rehearse the answers — you want first tellings
  on camera — but know where the gold is.
- Write questions as a **map**, not a script: key topics in story order, specific
  memories to reach, and 3–5 must-get moments.
- Explain clearly what the film is, where it may be shown, and what being in it means.
  Get a signed **release** (appearance and, where relevant, location/material releases);
  consider vulnerable subjects' capacity to consent and agree any conditions in writing.
  Legal requirements differ by country and broadcaster — check with the producer.

## 2. Frame and eyeline (decide on purpose)
- **Off-axis:** the subject looks at the interviewer seated just beside the lens. The
  closer the interviewer's eyes are to the lens, the more intimate it feels. Keep the
  interviewer on the side that gives the subject "looking room" in frame.
- **Direct-to-camera:** the subject looks into the lens (via a teleprompter-style
  mirror system such as an Interrotron, or simply looking at the lens). Confrontational
  and intimate; harder for non-performers.
- **Two cameras** (a medium and a tight, or a profile) give cut points to remove
  ums and condense answers.
- Background with depth and meaning (their workshop, their kitchen), subject several
  metres in front of it if possible.

## 3. Light and sound
- A soft key on the far side of the face (away from camera) with low fill gives shape; a
  backlight separates from the background. Avoid lights in their eyes for hours — they
  will squint and tire.
- Consistent light for the whole interview: if relying on daylight, it will change over
  2 hours — block windows and use controlled light, or shoot in short periods.
- **Sound:** a lav plus a boom (or a fixed overhead mic) on separate tracks. Kill fridges,
  aircon, phones; record room tone at the end.

## 4. Asking
- Start with easy, concrete questions to warm up; the real material often comes after
  20–40 minutes, once they forget the camera.
- Ask **open, specific** questions: "Take me to the morning you got the call — where were
  you standing?" beats "How did you feel?".
- Ask them to **answer in complete sentences** that include the question, because your
  voice will usually be cut: "When I got the call, I was…". Explain this once at the start;
  gently re-ask if needed.
- **Silence:** after an answer, wait 3–5 seconds without speaking. People often fill
  the pause with the most honest part.
- Don't make noise while they speak (no "mm-hm"); nod instead.
- Follow the unexpected; go back to the map later.
- End with "Is there anything I didn't ask that I should have?"

## 5. During and after
- Keep an eye on continuity (hair, glasses, mic visible) and on the time — tired subjects
  give thin answers.
- Log key moments with timecode right after, while you remember them; transcribe for the edit.
- Shoot **b-roll** linked to what they said (hands at work, places mentioned).
- Ethics in the edit: condensing answers is normal; changing their meaning is not.
  Some filmmakers show subjects a cut of their sections — decide your policy and
  communicate it honestly.

## Gotchas
- Leading questions that put words in their mouths.
- Over-rehearsed pre-interviews that make the on-camera telling flat.
- Interviewer talking over answers — unusable audio.
- No release before shooting — footage you may not be able to use.

## Verify
- [ ] Pre-interview done; question map with must-get moments.
- [ ] Release signed; subject understands the film and its distribution.
- [ ] Eyeline, frame and background chosen deliberately; light consistent.
- [ ] Lav + boom on separate tracks; room tone recorded.
- [ ] Answers in complete sentences; silences used; log with timecodes made after.
