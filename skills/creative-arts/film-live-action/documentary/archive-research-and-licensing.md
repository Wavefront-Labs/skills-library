# Archive research and licensing

**Goal:** the right footage and photos, found early enough to shape the edit,
licensed for where the film will actually be shown, and documented so delivery and
E&O don't stall at the end.

## 1. Where to look
- **Commercial libraries**: Getty Images, AP Archive, Reuters, British Pathe, ITN/
  ITV Archive, BBC Motion Gallery (via Getty), NBCUniversal, CNN, Periscope, Pond5/
  Shutterstock/Adobe Stock (modern stock), Bridgeman/Alamy (stills).
- **Public/national**: Library of Congress, National Archives (NARA/TNA), BFI
  National Archive, Imperial War Museums, Prelinger/Internet Archive (much public
  domain), NASA, state broadcasters' archives, university film archives, local-history
  societies and regional film archives (often cheaper and unique).
- **Personal**: home movies, photo albums, VHS/camcorder tapes, letters, newspapers -
  owned by your participants or their families; the most valuable and the cheapest, if
  you ask early.
- **Press and newspapers**: publishers' archives for front pages/clippings; photos may
  be agency-owned (AP/Getty) even if printed in a newspaper.
- Hire an **archive producer/researcher** (FOCAL members) for archive-heavy films;
  they know the libraries' catalogues, what isn't online, and the going rates.

## 2. Research and screeners
Search by event/date/place/person; many catalogues have only partial metadata, so ask
the librarian with a precise brief (dates, names, formats, what you've already found).
Request **screeners / low-res** (watermarked H.264 or stills proofs) - usually free or
a nominal fee; log every clip with library, clip ID, timecode, description, duration
used, rights holder, and rate. Edit with low-res, then order masters for exactly the
seconds used (plus handles) after lock.

## 3. Licence terms
Price = seconds (often with a **minimum licence**, e.g. 10-30 s) x rate, where the rate
depends on:
- **Media**: festival only, theatrical, TV (one country/region/world), streaming/VOD,
  educational, **all media** - all media is the standard ask for a film with a sales agent.
- **Territory**: single country, Europe, **worldwide**.
- **Term**: 1 year, 5-7 years, 10 years, **in perpetuity**.
- Exclusivity (rarely needed), promotional use (trailer/stills - ask for it included),
  format/delivery (HD/4K masters cost extra; scans from film are a service charge).
Negotiate: a **festival licence** first (cheap) with a pre-agreed **step-up** to all
media worldwide in perpetuity when the film sells; package deals for multiple clips
from one library; "most favoured nation" terms across libraries. Get everything in
a **written licence** naming the production, the film title, the clips, the terms;
pay before delivery or per the invoice; keep invoices.

## 4. Personal archive
Agree a **written licence** (or release) from the owner - and confirm *they* own it
(the photographer owns photos; the person who shot the home movie owns it; a dead
relative's footage belongs to the estate). Handle originals carefully: **scan** stills at
600+ dpi TIFF; film (8mm/16mm) via a professional scanning house (2K/4K flat scans,
keep the originals in their boxes); VHS/Hi8 via a capture service (ProRes, with the
noise, not a "cleaned" MP4). Return everything with a thank-you and a copy of the scans.
Photos of photos on set (a hand holding a print) are a design choice, not a workaround
for a scan.

## 5. Rights and fair use/dealing
Public domain isn't a licence to the *copy* - a library may charge an access fee for
its scan of a PD film. **Fair use** (US) can cover brief, transformative use for
commentary/criticism; **fair dealing** (UK) is narrower (criticism/review, quotation,
news reporting). A lawyer's opinion letter per use is what the E&O insurer wants; don't
rely on it for the emotional archive montage. **Orphan works** (unknown owner): document
a diligent search; some jurisdictions have schemes; insurers may exclude it.

## 6. Delivery paperwork
An **archive log / clearance report** for delivery: for every archive element - source,
clip ID, in/out timecode in the film, duration, licence terms, licence document ref,
invoice ref, credit line required. Compile credits as the libraries require. Keep
licences in the delivery folder with the E&O application.

## Gotchas
- Cutting with archive that can't be licensed for all media; recutting after the sale.
- Assuming a newspaper owns the photo it printed.
- Licence for 5 years; the film is still streaming in year 6.
- Family tapes "cleaned" by a transfer house into deinterlaced mush.
- Credits missing a library's mandated credit line; delivery rejected.

## Verify
- [ ] Archive log with every clip's source, ID, timecodes, duration used, terms and documents.
- [ ] Licences written for the actual media/territory/term (or festival + step-up) and promotional use.
- [ ] Personal archive licensed from the actual rights holder; originals scanned properly and returned.
- [ ] Fair use/dealing items covered by a legal opinion; clearance report delivered with E&O.
