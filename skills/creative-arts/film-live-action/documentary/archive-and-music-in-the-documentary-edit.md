# Archive and music in the documentary edit

**Goal:** archive that feels like storytelling, not illustration, and music that supports
rather than tells the audience what to feel - all logged for clearance.

## 1. Archive as story
- Build **archive sequences** with their own shape (setup, escalation, payoff), not one
  shot per sentence of narration.
- Let archive play with its own sound where possible; use interview audio over archive
  sparingly - layered meaning (what we see vs what's said) is powerful.
- Respect the material: don't misrepresent context (a protest from another year presented
  as this one) - ethical and legal risk.

## 2. Technical handling
- **Aspect ratio**: present 4:3 archive pillarboxed (preferred by most broadcasters'
  guidelines) rather than cropping/ stretching; if reframing, do it deliberately and
  consistently.
- **Frame rate**: archive at 25i/ 29.97i/ film 16-18 fps - convert properly (deinterlace,
  frame-rate conversion with motion compensation); speed-correct silent film.
- **Quality**: order the best available master (not YouTube rips) once the cut is close -
  use low-res temps in the edit with **watermark-free placeholders** clearly labelled.
- Restoration (dust, scratches, stabilisation) budgeted per minute.

## 3. Log everything
For each archive clip in the cut: source/ licensor, ID, TC in/out, duration used, rights
needed (territory, media, term). An archive log/ cue sheet drives licensing and E&O
clearance (see archive licensing skill). Update it every recut.

## 4. Sound for archive
Silent archive often needs **sound design** (period-appropriate effects, crowd walla) -
mark it clearly as added; avoid creating misleading "actuality". Clean noisy archive audio
(restoration tools).

## 5. Music
- Temp music in the edit helps pacing but causes "temp love" - limit it; prefer cutting
  scenes dry first so they work without music.
- Spot with the composer (or music supervisor for licensed tracks) like a drama: purpose
  of each cue, and places of silence.
- Licensed tracks: check budget and availability before falling in love; log cues
  (title, composer, publisher, label, duration, usage) for the **music cue sheet**.
- Avoid manipulating emotion over testimony (sad music under grief) - often stronger dry.

## Gotchas
- Archive used out of context.
- Cropped 4:3 archive losing information; stretched images.
- Unlogged archive discovered at clearance stage.
- Temp music unlicensable and irreplaceable in the director's mind.

## Verify
- [ ] Archive sequences structured; context accurate.
- [ ] Aspect and frame-rate handling consistent; masters ordered.
- [ ] Archive log and music cue log current.
- [ ] Music spotted with intent; dry versions tested.
