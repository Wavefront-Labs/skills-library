# Fixers, translators and working abroad

**Goal:** a foreign shoot where access, logistics and language work, the film's
material is accurately understood, and nobody local is put at risk by your film.

## 1. Fixers
- A fixer is local producer, researcher, negotiator and often interpreter. Hire through
  trusted referrals (other filmmakers, foreign correspondents, film commissions).
- Agree in writing: day rate, prep days, overtime, expenses, kit/vehicle costs,
  credit, and what they'll arrange (permits, locations, contributors, transport,
  accommodation, security).
- Brief them fully on the film, including sensitive angles - their safety depends on
  understanding the risk.
- Their exposure lasts after you leave. Discuss credit (or anonymity), how the film
  portrays local authorities, and an emergency plan.

## 2. Paperwork
- **Visas**: many countries require a journalist/media visa for filming; shooting on a
  tourist visa can mean arrest and confiscated media.
- **Filming permits** and location permissions (national and local; drones separate).
- **ATA Carnet** (where accepted) for temporary import of kit without duties; kit list
  with serial numbers.
- Insurance covering the territory, kit, and kidnap/medical evacuation where relevant.
- **Risk assessment**, and hostile environment (HEFAT) training for high-risk areas.

## 3. Interpreting on the shoot
- Brief the interpreter on vocabulary and the questions; ask for **direct first-person
  interpretation** ("I was..."), not summaries.
- For interviews: let the contributor speak uninterrupted in their language; interpret
  in breaks or via a whispered/earpiece feed to the director to keep the audio clean.
- Avoid the fixer interpreting when they have a stake in the story or community.

## 4. Translation for the edit
- Commission **timecoded** transcripts plus translations (timecode every line or
  every 10-30 s), done by someone other than the shoot interpreter for accuracy.
- Mark uncertain words; get key lines double-checked before they go in subtitles.
- Translate release forms and consent explanations into the contributor's language.

## Gotchas
- Fixer unpaid for prep/waiting days - reputations travel; pay fairly and promptly.
- Interpreter softening or editorialising answers; check with a second translator.
- Data: devices searched at borders - encrypt, back up out of country, minimise
  contributor data carried.
- Leaving local crew exposed to reprisals after broadcast.

## Verify
- [ ] Written fixer agreement with rate, scope and credit.
- [ ] Visas, permits, carnet, insurance and risk assessment complete.
- [ ] Timecoded translations independently checked for key lines.
- [ ] Post-release safety discussed with local staff and contributors.
