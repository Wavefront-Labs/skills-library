# Licensing stills, newspapers and documents

**Goal:** photographs, newspaper pages and documents on screen that are legally cleared,
look sharp at full resolution, and are logged well enough to pass E&O review.

## 1. Sources
- **Photo agencies** (Getty, AP, Reuters, Alamy and specialist archives): rights-managed
  licences quoted by media, territory, term, number of uses and screen time.
- **Newspapers**: the publisher owns the layout and articles; individual photos may
  belong to a separate agency or photographer. Historic papers via digitised archives
  (Chronicling America, British Newspaper Archive, newspapers.com).
- **Personal collections**: family photos, letters - license from the owner (often the
  copyright holder is the photographer, not the family).
- **Government documents**: US federal works are public domain; other countries vary
  (UK Crown copyright, etc.).

## 2. Clearing
- Ask for "all media, worldwide, in perpetuity" quotes for documentaries headed to
  broadcast/streaming; cheaper festival-only quotes need upgrading before sale.
- Check **public domain** by jurisdiction and date; in the US, works published more
  than 95 years ago are public domain - but a modern scan or restoration may carry its
  own claimed rights and the archive's access terms.
- People in photos: private individuals in sensitive contexts may raise privacy or
  defamation issues even if the image is licensed.
- **Fair use** (US) or fair dealing (UK etc.): legitimate for commentary/critique when
  applied per the Documentary Filmmakers' Statement of Best Practices; get a lawyer's
  opinion letter for E&O.

## 3. Capturing
- Request the **highest-resolution** original; for moves on a 4K timeline you need
  several times the frame size in pixels (e.g. 8K+ wide for slow pushes).
- Scanning yourself: flatbed at 600-1200 dpi (higher for small prints/negatives),
  TIFF, no auto-sharpening; scan whole page plus details.
- Documents and newspapers: shoot on a copy stand with even, cross-polarised light to
  kill glare; or scan.
- Moves (rostrum/"Ken Burns"): motivated, slow, ending on the detail that matters;
  keep text readable for the time needed to read it.

## 4. Log
Clearance log per item: source, ID/URL, rights holder, licence terms and cost, invoice,
where it appears (TC), credit wording. E&O insurers and distributors ask for it.

## Gotchas
- Using the agency's watermarked comp in the final.
- Newspaper licensed but a photo within it isn't.
- Festival licence only, then a streamer sale - re-licensing costs spike once the film
  has buzz.
- Upscaling a 600-pixel web image for a full-screen push.

## Verify
- [ ] Every still/page/document in the cut has a log entry and licence.
- [ ] Resolution adequate for the largest move.
- [ ] Public-domain and fair-use calls documented.
- [ ] Credits match licence requirements.
