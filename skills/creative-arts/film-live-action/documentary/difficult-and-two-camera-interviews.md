# Difficult and two-camera interviews

**Goal:** truthful interviews from people in pain or under scrutiny - conducted ethically
and covered so the editor has options.

## 1. Trauma-informed interviews (Dart Center practice)
- **Informed consent** that's real: explain the film, how the interview will be used, and
  that they can stop, skip questions or withdraw (within agreed limits).
- Let the subject choose location and companions; give them some control (breaks,
  order of topics).
- Start with safe, factual ground; approach the hardest material in the middle, not the
  end - leave time to come back to calmer territory.
- Ask open, simple questions; allow silence; don't push for tears.
- Afterwards: check in, explain next steps, provide support contacts; consider your
  crew's wellbeing too.

## 2. Hostile/ accountability interviews
- Prepare evidence and a clear list of allegations to put; follow your broadcaster's
  **right of reply**/ fairness rules (give notice of allegations and time to respond).
- Keep calm, specific questions; repeat the question if evaded; note non-answers.
- Record everything (two cameras so no "selective editing" claims); keep the unedited
  recording.
- Legal review before broadcast.

## 3. Two-camera interview setup
- **A camera**: the main angle, MCU, eyeline just off lens toward the interviewer.
- **B camera**: a different size and angle (wider, or a tight CU from 30-45 degrees off
  A) - lets the editor cut within answers without jump cuts.
- Match colour (same model or matched settings; shoot a chart), frame rates, and
  timecode (jammed); same lens style.
- Eye-to-lens interviews (Interrotron-style teleprompter devices) for direct-address
  intimacy.

## 4. B-roll and coverage
- Shoot **cutaways** in the session: hands, objects they mention, details of the room, the
  subject listening/ thinking (with consent - not fake reactions to manipulate meaning).
- Gather B-roll tied to what they talk about (places, activities) the same day while
  access exists.

## Gotchas
- Pushing a traumatised subject for the "big moment".
- Accountability interviews without legal/ fairness preparation.
- Mismatched cameras that won't cut together.
- No cutaways; every edit is a visible jump cut.

## Verify
- [ ] Consent explained; trauma-informed structure and aftercare planned.
- [ ] Right of reply/ fairness steps followed for accountability interviews.
- [ ] Two cameras matched, synced and framed differently.
- [ ] Cutaways and B-roll gathered.
