# Available light and minimal lighting for documentary

**Goal:** good-looking images in real places with no lighting crew and no time, without
changing the behaviour of the people you film.

## 1. Read the room first
- Find the main source: windows, doorways, overhead fixtures, screens, lamps.
- Decide where people will naturally be; place yourself so the source falls
  **side-on or three-quarter** to them rather than flat from behind you.
- Avoid putting subjects with their back to a bright window unless you want a
  silhouette.

## 2. Shape with subtraction
- **Negative fill**: a black cloth, dark coat or flag on the shadow side deepens
  contrast in rooms where light bounces everywhere.
- Turn off overhead fluorescents/LED panels if you can (they flatten faces and add
  green/magenta casts); keep practical lamps on for depth.
- Draw a curtain partly to narrow a window into a softer, directional source.

## 3. Add with minimum kit
- **Bounce**: a collapsible reflector or white card (or a white wall) to fill the
  shadow side of the face from a window.
- **Swap practical bulbs** for high-CRI bulbs of a known colour temperature; dimmable
  if possible - invisible to contributors.
- One small battery LED panel (bi-colour, high CRI) with diffusion: as a soft key
  bounced off a ceiling or wall, or a low eyelight. Set it once and leave it.

## 4. Exposure and colour
- Modern cameras' **dual native ISO** and log profiles make low light usable; expose to
  protect highlights (windows) and accept some noise; denoise in post.
- Mixed light (daylight window + tungsten lamps): pick one white balance for the
  scene, usually for faces, and let other sources fall warm/cool as motivation; or
  gel/swap sources to one temperature when you control the room (interviews).
- ND filters (variable ND) for exteriors and bright windows to keep the aperture you want.

## 5. Verite discipline
- Light the space, not the shot: set any added light so people can move freely.
- No new lights mid-scene; changes break continuity and the moment.

## Gotchas
- Faces underexposed against bright windows; meter for skin.
- Flicker from cheap LEDs/fluorescents at certain shutter speeds: use 1/50 in 50 Hz
  countries, 1/60 in 60 Hz, or check flicker before rolling.
- Bi-colour LED set to a different temperature than the room - patchy skin.
- A visible light stand making contributors self-conscious.

## Verify
- [ ] Main source identified and subject positioned to it.
- [ ] Skin exposure correct; windows not clipped beyond use.
- [ ] One white balance decision per scene.
- [ ] No flicker at the chosen shutter.
