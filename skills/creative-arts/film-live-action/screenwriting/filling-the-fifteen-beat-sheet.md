# Filling the fifteen-beat sheet

**Goal:** one page with fifteen beats that prove the story has a shape before you
spend a month on cards or pages.

## 1. The beats and their page targets (110-page script)
| # | Beat | Page | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Opening Image | 1 | Snapshot of the "before" world |
| 2 | Theme Stated | 5 | Someone states the lesson; hero doesn't get it yet |
| 3 | Set-Up | 1-10 | Hero's world, flaws, "six things that need fixing" |
| 4 | Catalyst | 12 | Life-changing event |
| 5 | Debate | 12-25 | Should I go? |
| 6 | Break into Two | 25 | Hero chooses and enters the upside-down world |
| 7 | B Story | 30 | Relationship carrying the theme |
| 8 | Fun and Games | 30-55 | The promise of the premise - the trailer moments |
| 9 | Midpoint | 55 | False victory or false defeat; stakes raised |
| 10 | Bad Guys Close In | 55-75 | Pressure from outside and inside |
| 11 | All Is Lost | 75 | Worst point; "whiff of death" |
| 12 | Dark Night of the Soul | 75-85 | Hero wallows, then finds the answer |
| 13 | Break into Three | 85 | A and B stories cross; the solution |
| 14 | Finale | 85-110 | Hero applies the lesson; old world dismantled |
| 15 | Final Image | 110 | Opposite of the opening image - proof of change |
Scale pages to your target length (beat page = target x proportion).

## 2. Order to fill them
1. **Opening and Final Image** - define the change first.
2. **Catalyst, Break into Two, Midpoint, All Is Lost, Break into Three** - the five
   structural pillars.
3. **Theme Stated** (once you know the change, you know the lesson).
4. Fill **Fun and Games** with 4-6 set-piece ideas; **Bad Guys Close In** with the
   escalation, inside (team fractures) and outside.
5. Everything else.
Write each beat as one or two sentences in the present tense with a specific event,
not a function ("Catalyst: she finds a job offer in her dead sister's email", not
"something changes her life").

## 3. Check the sheet
- Does the Midpoint mirror All Is Lost (up/down) ?
- Does the Finale use something set up in act one?
- Is Fun and Games actually the premise (would it make the trailer)?
- Is the Break into Two a **choice**, not something that happens to the hero?

## 4. Use it as a tool, not a template
Beats are a diagnostic. Many great films hit them loosely; page numbers move ±5. Use
the sheet to find the hole ("I have no All Is Lost") then solve it with story logic,
not by inserting a death because the sheet asked.

## Gotchas
- Filling beats with abstractions; the sheet "works" but no scene exists.
- Debate that lasts 25 pages of nothing - combine with set-up action.
- Theme stated by a character who sounds like the writer.
- Forcing a "whiff of death" that's literal and unearned.

## Verify
- [ ] All fifteen beats written as concrete events in one page.
- [ ] Opening and final images are visibly opposite.
- [ ] Five pillars sit near their target pages.
- [ ] Break into Two is a protagonist choice.
