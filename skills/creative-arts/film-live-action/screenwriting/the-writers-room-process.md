# The writers' room process

**Goal:** a room that turns a season plan into approved episode outlines on schedule,
with every writer's best ideas captured.

## 1. Phases
1. **Blue sky** (week 1-2): open discussion of characters, themes, world, possible
   stories; pitch freely, write everything down.
2. **Season break**: the season grid (see season-arc skill).
3. **Episode breaking**: one episode at a time on the board until every scene beat exists.
4. **Outlines**: an assigned writer turns the board into a 10-20 page outline; notes
   from showrunner, then studio/network.
5. **Scripts**: writer drafts (1-3 weeks); showrunner rewrites/polishes.

## 2. Breaking an episode on the board
- Columns for acts (teaser + 4-6 acts in network drama; a streaming episode uses
  sequences instead). Act-outs (cliffhangers) first.
- Cards per scene: who, where, what turns. Colour per story line (A/B/C).
- Pitch alternatives aloud; the showrunner (or room leader) decides. Don't argue
  past a decision - pitch something better.
- Check each story line has beginning, middle, end in the episode and hands off to the next.

## 3. The writers' assistant (WA)
Takes near-verbatim **room notes** of every pitch (ideas rejected today are often used in
episode 7), maintains the board photos, the season bible, and the outline templates.
Notes are sent at end of day.

## 4. Assigning and writing
Scripts are assigned by the showrunner (WGA rules govern pay for staff vs freelance
episodes). The writer's job is to execute the break; changes to the break go back to the
showrunner before pages.

## 5. Etiquette that matters
- "Yes, and" pitching; criticise the idea, never the person.
- Don't hold a good pitch for your own episode.
- Personal stories shared in the room stay in the room.
- Be concise; time is the room's budget.

## Gotchas
- Room breaking with no decision-maker; boards stall for days.
- Outlines that drift from the agreed break.
- No WA notes; good ideas lost.
- Breaking episodes out of order with the serial plot unmapped.

## Verify
- [ ] Phases scheduled against the production calendar.
- [ ] Each episode broken to scene level with act-outs before outlining.
- [ ] Room notes and board photos archived daily.
- [ ] Outline approved before script assignment.
