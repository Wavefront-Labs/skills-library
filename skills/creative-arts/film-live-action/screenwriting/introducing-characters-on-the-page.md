# Introducing characters on the page

**Goal:** the reader knows who matters, what they're like, and why to watch them -
within three lines of their first appearance.

## 1. Format
- Name in **CAPS** the first time it appears in action (`MARTA (42)` or `MARTA, 42,`);
  afterwards normal case. Never caps a character again in action (that implies new).
- Age as a number or decade ("40s"). Exact ages for kids.
- Minor roles: functional names (`NURSE`, `GUARD #2`) - don't name characters who
  speak once; named characters signal importance to the reader and to casting.

## 2. The description
One sentence, two at most, about **character, not cataloguing**:
- Bad: "MARTA (42), brown hair, blue eyes, wearing jeans."
- Good: "MARTA (42), a night nurse who stopped expecting good news years ago, eats her
  sandwich standing up."
Include physical detail only if it matters to story or reveals attitude. Avoid ethnic
or body description that limits casting unless it is story-relevant.

## 3. The entrance action
Show the defining trait in the **first thing they do**: a choice under mild pressure.
Protagonists ideally enter doing something that reveals both their skill and their flaw.
Antagonists enter showing competence. Let the action carry the description; the
adjectives then confirm what we saw.

## 4. Management
- Introduce no more than **3-4 new speaking characters per scene**; readers lose track.
- Space introductions of principals across act one.
- Avoid names that look or sound alike (Mark/Mike, Jan/Jen) or start with the same letter.
- Groups: introduce as a unit then single out one ("THREE TEENAGERS - the loudest,
  DANNY (16), ...").
- When a character is first heard before seen: `MARTA (V.O.)` / `(O.S.)` then caps on
  first visual appearance in action.

## Gotchas
- Novelistic interior description ("she remembers her father") the camera can't film - keep it attitude, not backstory.
- Every character described as "attractive".
- Main character introduced late and quietly; reader attaches to the wrong person.
- Caps used for sound effects and props inconsistently with names.

## Verify
- [ ] Caps and age on first appearance only; consistent names throughout.
- [ ] Each principal's description reveals character in one or two sentences.
- [ ] Entrance action dramatises the trait.
- [ ] No scene introduces more than four speaking characters cold.
