# Writing monologues and speeches

**Goal:** a long speech that plays as a scene - it has a goal, turns, and changes the
listener - so an actor can't wait to perform it and an editor can't cut it.

## 1. Earn it
A monologue is justified when the speaker **must** talk at length to get something
(persuade, confess, threaten, seduce, rally) and the listener is **unable or unwilling
to interrupt**. If the listener could reasonably cut in, let them - it becomes dialogue.
Limit: one or two big speeches per feature, placed at turning points.

## 2. Structure inside the speech
McKee: a monologue is a scene in one voice. Write it in **beats**, each a new tactic:
1. Opening hook - a concrete image or startling line, not throat-clearing.
2. 3-5 beats, each changing tactic (story -> accusation -> plea -> threat). Mark beats
   with line breaks in your draft.
3. A **turn** - the speaker reveals something, or realises something mid-speech.
4. A final line that lands the action: a demand, a decision, a door shut.
Tell a **specific story** rather than generalising - "the summer my brother drowned"
beats "loss changes people".

## 3. The listener
Cut to the listener (in action lines) at the turns - the scene's real change is in them.
`She doesn't look up. He keeps going.` One line of listener reaction every ~6-8
lines of speech gives the director coverage and the reader breath.

## 4. On the page
- Break long blocks with **parentheticals or action** only where the behaviour changes
  (`(beat)`, `He sits.`); don't stage every gesture.
- Keep any single dialogue block under ~6-8 lines; continue after action with the
  character cue and `(CONT'D)`.
- Vocabulary and rhythm specific to the speaker - read it in their voice.

## 5. Cut it
Read aloud with a stopwatch. Most first-draft monologues lose 30-40%: cut the
explanation of the image, the second example, the summary at the end. Target 1-2
minutes; anything longer needs very strong justification.

## Gotchas
- The theme speech: the character says what the film means.
- Speech delivered to someone who'd obviously interrupt.
- No turn; it's a list of grievances at one pitch.
- Monologue as exposition of backstory nobody asked for.

## Verify
- [ ] Speaker's goal and the listener's resistance named.
- [ ] Beats marked; at least one turn; final line is an action.
- [ ] Listener reactions placed at turns.
- [ ] Timed aloud and trimmed under two minutes (or justified).
