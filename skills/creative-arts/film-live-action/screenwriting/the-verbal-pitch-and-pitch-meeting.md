# The verbal pitch and pitch meeting

**Goal:** in ten minutes, make a development executive see the film, care about the
person in it, and want to read the script - and leave the room having learned what
they actually want to buy.

## 1. Structure (feature, ~10 minutes)
1. **Why you / why this** (30 s): the personal or topical reason the story is yours.
   One sentence, not a biography.
2. **Title, genre, logline** (30 s): say it, then stop - let it land.
3. **The world and the protagonist** (1 min): who they are at the start, the flaw,
   the want.
4. **The story** (6-7 min): act one to the inciting incident quickly; act two with
   its escalations and the midpoint; the crisis; the climax **and the ending** - never
   withhold. Tell it as scenes ("she opens the door and the kitchen is empty"), not
   as summary. Keep three or four named characters.
5. **Tone and comps** (30 s): "it feels like X with the scale of Y"; the audience.
6. **Close** (30 s): what you're asking for (a read, development money, a meeting
   with a producer).
Series pitches: engine and world first, then pilot story, then season shape (three or
four beats), then why it runs for years. Fifteen minutes maximum.

## 2. Prepare
- Write it out, then reduce to **index cards or a single page of beats**; don't read.
- Rehearse aloud to a timer with someone who stops you when they're bored. Cut there.
- Prepare the **two-minute version** (the elevator pitch) and the **30-second** one;
  rooms run late.
- Know the answers to: budget range, who'd direct/cast, what's the ending, why now,
  what else have you written, what happens in episode two.
- A **leave-behind**: one-pager or short treatment (see the treatment skill), not the
  script unless asked.

## 3. In the room
- Open with two minutes of conversation; read the room's energy and shorten if needed.
- Make eye contact with everyone, not only the senior person.
- If they interrupt with questions, answer briefly and return to the story; questions
  mean engagement.
- If they say "we have something similar", ask what - it tells you their slate.
- Don't defend; note objections and say "that's useful" - the meeting is also research.
- Never pitch two projects unless asked; have a second ready if they ask "what else".

## 4. The general meeting
A "general" (no project to sell) is about you: your voice, what you love, what you'd
want to write. Bring three ideas at one-paragraph length; listen more than you talk;
ask what they're looking for. Follow up within 48 hours with a short email and the
one-pager for whatever they responded to.

## 5. After
Write down what they said within the hour: what they liked, what worried them, who
they mentioned. Executives move; the notes travel with them. A pass is often "not
now" - stay in touch quarterly with news, not nagging.

## Gotchas
- Pitching theme ("it's about forgiveness") instead of story.
- Withholding the ending to "keep them interested" - they're deciding whether to pay for it.
- Running 25 minutes; nobody has heard the last five.
- Reading from the page; eye contact is the pitch.
- Arguing with a note in the room.

## Verify
- [ ] Ten-minute pitch rehearsed to a timer, with 2-minute and 30-second versions.
- [ ] Ending and all standard questions prepared; one-pager leave-behind ready.
- [ ] Notes written within an hour of the meeting; follow-up sent within 48 hours.
