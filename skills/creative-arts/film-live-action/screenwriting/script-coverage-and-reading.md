# Script coverage and reading

**Goal:** turn a 110-page read into a two-to-three page document that lets someone who
will never open the script decide whether to spend more time on it - and gives the writer
(if shared) notes they can use.

## 1. Read like a reader
- Read the whole thing in one sitting; note the page where you first wanted to stop and why.
- Track page numbers for: inciting incident, end of act one, midpoint, low point, climax.
  Compare to expected ranges (roughly 10-15, 25-30, 55-60, 75-85, 95-110 for a feature).
- Note every question you had that the script never answered.
- Note any line of dialogue you'd quote - good or bad.

## 2. The coverage document
**Header:** title, writer, draft date, format (feature/pilot), genre, pages, budget range
(low/mid/high), submitted by, reader, date.

**Logline (1-2 sentences):** the reader's own, not the writer's - protagonist, goal, obstacle, stakes.

**Synopsis (1 page):** the story as written, in present tense, including the ending. No
evaluation here. If the synopsis is hard to write, say so in comments - it's a structure note.

**Comments (1-1.5 pages)** in this order:
1. *Premise and concept* - is it fresh, is it a movie, is it castable/marketable?
2. *Structure and pacing* - where it sags, where the acts land, what the engine is.
3. *Character* - clarity of want/need, arc, distinctiveness, antagonist strength.
4. *Dialogue* - voice, subtext, exposition.
5. *Tone, world, theme* - consistency; does it deliver on its genre promise?
6. *Producibility* - budget flags (period, VFX, crowds, animals, children, water).
7. *Writer* - separate from the script: is this a voice worth tracking?
Lead each paragraph with the strongest observation; give page references.

**Grid:** premise, story, structure, characters, dialogue, writing style - each
Excellent / Good / Fair / Poor.

**Verdict:** `PASS` (not for us), `CONSIDER` (merits, with reservations - say which), or
`RECOMMEND` (rare; you'd be happy to defend it). Separate verdicts for *script* and *writer*.

## 3. Writing useful notes
- Describe the *effect* before suggesting a fix: "I lost track of what Ana wanted between
  pp. 40-60" beats "add a scene where Ana states her goal".
- Prioritise: three big notes outrank twenty small ones. Put line notes in a list at the end.
- Be specific and kind; be honest about the verdict. Vague praise helps nobody.

## Gotchas
- Rewriting the film you'd have written. Judge the script against its own intentions.
- Grading on effort or on the writer's bio.
- Synopsis that drifts into opinion, or comments that retell the story.
- Missing the ending because you skimmed act three - the end is what gets quoted upstairs.

## Verify
- [ ] Logline is yours, one sentence, contains stakes.
- [ ] Synopsis includes the ending and no opinions.
- [ ] Each comment paragraph has a page reference.
- [ ] Verdict for script and for writer, with one sentence justifying each.
