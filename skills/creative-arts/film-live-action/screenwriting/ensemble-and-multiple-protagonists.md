# Ensemble and multiple protagonists

**Goal:** several characters share the lead without the film losing focus - one
question unifies them, each has a turn, and they converge.

## 1. Pick the model
- **Dual protagonist / two-hander** (buddy, romance): two people with opposing views
  forced together; the relationship is the plot. Each needs a want, and they change
  each other.
- **Group protagonist** (McKee's plural protagonist): the group wants one thing (the
  heist, the mission); members differ by method and flaw. Write the group's goal as if
  it were one character's.
- **Multiple protagonist / ensemble**: separate goals linked by theme, place or an event
  (Aronson). Each strand is a mini-film with its own act structure.

## 2. The unifying device
Name it in one line: the **shared goal** (group), the **relationship** (dual), or
the **theme + time/place container** (ensemble - "one wedding weekend", "one night in
the city"). If there isn't one, it's an anthology.

## 3. Arcs and balance
- Each lead needs an inciting incident, a midpoint turn and a climax beat. In an
  ensemble of 4-6, some arcs can be shorter (3 beats) but none can be absent.
- Count screen time by pages per character per act in a spreadsheet; intended
  imbalances are fine, accidental ones are not.
- Differentiate leads by **want and method**, not just personality ("the planner, the
  muscle, the con" - each solves problems differently).
- Give one character the audience's point of view (often the newcomer) for orientation.

## 4. Cutting between strands
Cut on **rhymes and contrasts** (one triumph against another's loss); leave each strand
on a question. Keep strand absences under ~10-12 pages. Introduce strands quickly in
the first 15 pages, or one by one with a clear handoff.

## 5. Convergence
Ensembles need a **convergence** event where strands collide (the party, the storm, the
heist itself). Climaxes can be sequential but should resolve in the same sequence. In
group stories, the group fractures at the all-is-lost beat and reforms for the climax.

## Gotchas
- Equal characters, no point of view - the audience has nobody to hold.
- Strands that never touch; it reads like short films.
- The "everyone gets a speech" act three that stalls the climax.
- Two protagonists with the same flaw - nothing to resolve between them.

## Verify
- [ ] Model chosen and unifying device written in one line.
- [ ] Page-count table per character per act.
- [ ] Every lead has inciting, midpoint and climax beats.
- [ ] A convergence sequence resolves all strands.
