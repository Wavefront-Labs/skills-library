# Writing set pieces and action on the page

**Goal:** the reader experiences the sequence at screen speed and a director can
shoot it - without the page becoming a shot list.

## 1. Design before writing
A set piece is a mini-story: write in one paragraph
- **Goal** within the sequence (get the drive, reach the car).
- **Obstacle and escalation**: 3-5 complications, each worse, ideally using the location
  (the elevator, the market, the rising water).
- **Reversal**: the moment the plan fails and the hero improvises.
- **Outcome** that changes the story (win at a cost, or lose).
- **Character**: what the action reveals or tests (the coward who goes back).
Set pieces in the Fun and Games stretch should deliver the premise's promise.

## 2. Vertical writing
- Paragraphs of **1-3 lines**; each paragraph roughly one shot or beat. White space =
  speed.
- Present tense, active verbs, specific nouns ("The Volvo clips the cart - melons
  explode across the windscreen").
- Implied shots instead of camera directions: a new paragraph starting with a detail
  implies a close-up ("Her hand. Inching toward the gun.").
- Sound in CAPS sparingly for key effects (BANG) per your format convention.
- Use `--` and `...` for rhythm; short fragments for impact.

## 3. Shootability
- Keep geography clear: where everyone is relative to each other and the exit.
  Establish it before the action starts.
- Don't choreograph every punch; describe the shape and the turn
  (`They fight - brutal, graceless - until he gets the cord around her neck.`).
  The stunt coordinator will design the moves.
- Note genuine requirements only (rain, night, a crowd, a crash) - producers budget off
  the page.
- Length: a page is roughly a minute even in action; a two-page set piece will run
  longer on screen, so don't pad.

## 4. Reread for pace
Read it aloud at speed. Where you stumble, the reader will. Cut adjectives, "begins to",
"starts to", "we see".

## Gotchas
- Blocks of 8-line action paragraphs; readers skim and miss the turn.
- Camera directions (`ANGLE ON`, `DOLLY IN`) in a spec script.
- Action with no character stakes - spectacle without meaning.
- Unclear geography; the reader doesn't know who's winning.

## Verify
- [ ] Goal, escalations, reversal, outcome written before the pages.
- [ ] No action paragraph over four lines.
- [ ] Geography established; fights described by shape, not move by move.
- [ ] The sequence changes the story.
