# Dialect, slang and period dialogue

**Goal:** characters sound like they come from a specific place, class and time,
without the reader decoding spelling or an anachronism pulling them out.

## 1. Syntax and vocabulary, not phonetics
Convey dialect through **word choice, word order, idiom and rhythm** - not respelled
words. "Where's he to?" (Newfoundland/West Country) does the work; "Wheer's 'e tew?"
just slows the reader and insults the character. Allow at most a few signature
contractions (`gonna`, `y'all`) and keep them consistent. Note the accent once in the
character introduction ("a broad Glaswegian accent") and let the actor do it.

## 2. Source real speech
- Record or transcribe people from the community (with permission); read their writing,
  forums, court transcripts, oral histories.
- Build a **voice sheet** per character: 10-20 signature words or phrases, how they
  swear, how they address people, sentence length.
- Get a native reader to check; slang ages fast and errors read as mockery.

## 3. Slang
Slang dates a script the day it's written. Prefer slightly understated, enduring forms;
invent group-specific slang only if consistent and clear from context. Teen slang in
particular - consult current users or keep it sparse.

## 4. Period dialogue
- Avoid **anachronisms**: check first-use dates in the OED or Online Etymology
  Dictionary for suspect words ("okay" 1839, "teenager" 1940s).
- Don't overdo archaism ("prithee"); aim for the *feel* of formality - fuller sentences,
  fewer contractions, period forms of address - in contemporary readable English.
- Keep idiom period-consistent per class; servants and aristocrats spoke differently.

## 5. Foreign-language dialogue
- If the audience should understand: write in English with a note `(in Spanish;
  subtitled)` and keep it consistent, or set it once: `NOTE: All dialogue in Spanish
  is subtitled in English.`
- If the audience should NOT understand: write the line in the language (or
  `(in Mandarin, unsubtitled)` with the English meaning in action for the reader).
- Italicise or bracket subtitled lines per your format guide - be consistent.

## Gotchas
- Phonetic spelling that makes the script hard to read and the character a caricature.
- Every character speaking the writer's idiom regardless of origin.
- Period scripts with modern therapy-speak ("I need closure").
- Untranslated foreign dialogue the reader needs to follow the plot.

## Verify
- [ ] Voice sheet per principal; accent noted once in the introduction.
- [ ] No more than light phonetic spelling, used consistently.
- [ ] Suspect words checked for period accuracy.
- [ ] Foreign-language convention stated and applied consistently.
