# Character biography and backstory

**Goal:** know your character well enough that every choice on the page feels
inevitable - while the audience sees perhaps 5% of what you know.

## 1. Interior and exterior (Field)
- **Interior life**: birth to the first page - formative events, the wound, beliefs.
  Write it as 3-5 pages of free prose in the character's own voice or third person.
- **Exterior life**: first page to last - professional, personal and private life
  during the story. List these as three short columns; conflict lives where they collide.

## 2. Egri's three dimensions (a checklist, not a form to fill)
- **Physiology**: age, health, appearance, how the body affects behaviour.
- **Sociology**: class, education, work, family, religion, politics, community.
- **Psychology**: moral standards, ambitions, frustrations, temperament, complexes.
Answer only what the story will test. A heist film needs her relationship to risk and
loyalty; it doesn't need her favourite colour.

## 3. The ghost / wound and the lie
The **ghost** is the past event that still haunts; it produces the **lie** the character
believes about the world (Weiland), which produces the **want** (protective) that
blinds them to the **need**. Write the chain in four lines:
`Ghost: father left after the fire -> Lie: anyone you rely on leaves -> Want: total
self-sufficiency (her own business) -> Need: to let someone in.`

## 4. Story-specific questions
- What does she want in scene one, and what would she never do? (Then make her do it.)
- What is she ashamed of? What does she lie about, and to whom?
- How does she behave under pressure: attack, flee, joke, freeze?
- What does she know that other characters don't (dramatic irony fuel)?
- Specific speech traits: vocabulary, rhythm, what she never says.

## 5. Keep it off the page
Backstory is revealed through **behaviour and choices**, and in dialogue only when a
character is forced to use it as a weapon or a confession under pressure. No scene of
two people explaining their shared past ("As you know, Bob..."). Allow at most one
memory speech per film and make it a turning point.

## Gotchas
- 30-page questionnaires that never change a scene.
- Wound revealed in a flashback in act one, killing the mystery of behaviour.
- Biography that contradicts on-page behaviour; actors will find it.
- Backstory used to excuse passivity.

## Verify
- [ ] Interior (pre-story) and exterior (in-story) lives written.
- [ ] Ghost -> lie -> want -> need chain in four lines.
- [ ] Story-specific questions answered; at least one behaviour on page traces to the wound.
- [ ] No exposition dumps of backstory.
