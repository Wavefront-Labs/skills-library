# Designing the antagonist

**Goal:** opposition strong enough to force the protagonist to their limit. McKee: a
protagonist and story can only be as fascinating as the forces of antagonism make them.

## 1. Map all forces of antagonism
Three levels of conflict (McKee): **inner** (the character's own mind, body, emotions),
**personal** (family, lovers, friends), **extra-personal** (institutions, society,
environment). List what opposes the protagonist at each level; the strongest scripts
have pressure at all three, with one antagonist personifying the main one.

## 2. Give the antagonist a real goal
Truby: the best opponent wants **the same thing** as the hero (the same prize, person,
territory or truth) - so they can't both win. Write the antagonist's:
- **Goal** (concrete, in-story) and **why they believe they're right**.
- **Plan** - what they are actually doing off-screen while the hero acts. Track it scene
  by scene; the hero's surprises should come from this plan.
- **Strength** - what they're better at than the hero.
- **Weakness** - one the hero can only exploit after changing.

## 3. Mirror design
Design the antagonist as the protagonist's **shadow**: same wound, opposite choice; or
the protagonist's lie taken to its conclusion. Write one line: "she is what the hero
becomes if he never learns X". Their confrontation is then thematic as well as physical.

## 4. Escalate (the negation of the negation)
Take the story's core value to its limit: justice -> unfairness (contrary) ->
injustice (contradictory) -> **tyranny / injustice disguised as justice** (the negation
of the negation). The act-three antagonist pressure should reach that fourth level.
Each act, the antagonist's pressure should increase; never let them lose ground they
can't regain until the climax.

## 5. On the page
- Introduce the antagonist with an action that shows competence and values.
- Give them at least one scene where they're right.
- Avoid monologues explaining the plan; show the plan's effects.
- In man-vs-nature/system stories, personify the force in a character (the official,
  the company man) to have someone to confront.

## Gotchas
- Villain whose only motive is "evil".
- Antagonist absent for act two; tension sags.
- Opponent defeated by luck or a third party rather than the hero's change.
- An antagonist so weak the hero never has to change.

## Verify
- [ ] Opposition listed at inner, personal and extra-personal levels.
- [ ] Antagonist goal, justification, plan, strength and weakness written.
- [ ] Mirror line stated; climax tests the theme.
- [ ] Pressure escalates every act.
