# Limited series and serialised structure

**Goal:** one closed story told across 4-10 episodes where every episode is
satisfying on its own terms and compels the next.

## 1. Is it a limited series?
A limited series has a **definite ending** (the case solved, the event over, the life
told) and typically 4-8 episodes (UK serials 3-6; US limited 6-10). Ask: why not a film?
Answer must involve **perspective shifts, time span, or a mystery with layers** that
need hours. If the story is one through-line with no new questions per hour, it's a film.

## 2. Map the whole first
Write the full story as a feature-style outline with acts, then split into episodes at
natural **reversals** - not at equal page counts. Each episode needs:
- A **question** of its own (who was in the car? will she testify?).
- A **turn** mid-episode.
- An **ending** that answers that question and raises a bigger one (the hook).
Common shapes: episode 1 = the event and the world; middle episodes = investigation /
complications with a POV shift; penultimate = biggest reversal; final = confrontation
and aftermath.

## 3. Episode identity
Give each episode a **distinct flavour**: a POV character (episode per perspective), a
time period, a setting, or a set piece. Viewers remember "the episode in the courtroom".

## 4. Serialised hooks
End on a **revelation, a decision or a threat**, not just danger. Avoid fake
cliffhangers resolved in the first minute of the next episode. On streaming, the
first 5 minutes of each episode must pay the previous hook quickly.

## 5. Pilot and pitch documents
Pitch with: logline, the whole story's ending, an episode-by-episode paragraph
breakdown, character arcs, and episode 1 script. For an open-ended serialised show,
also the **series engine** (how it generates future seasons).

## Gotchas
- A film stretched to six hours: episodes 3-4 repeat the same beats.
- Episode breaks at page counts rather than turns.
- The answer to the central mystery withheld with no interim payoffs.
- Episodes indistinguishable from each other.

## Verify
- [ ] "Why not a film?" answered in one line.
- [ ] Full story outlined, then split at reversals.
- [ ] Each episode has its own question, mid turn, answer and hook.
- [ ] Episode breakdown and ending in the pitch documents.
