# Scene transitions and scene flow

**Goal:** each scene is caused by the one before and causes the one after, and the
reader slides across the cut without noticing it.

## 1. The causal chain
Write the scene list as one sentence per scene joined by **BUT** or **THEREFORE**
(Parker/Stone). Wherever the joint is **"and then"**, the story is episodic: either
find the causal link, reorder, or cut the scene. Cause-and-effect also runs on emotion -
the outcome of scene 12 is the attitude the character brings into scene 13.

## 2. Enter late, leave early
Start the scene at the last possible moment that still sets up its turn; end it
right after the turn, before characters say goodbye or summarise. Ending on an open
question or an unfinished action pulls the reader into the next slug.

## 3. Transitional hooks (McKee)
Link the end of one scene to the start of the next with something in common or in
contrast:
- **Repeated element**: a word, sound or image ("Who'd do that?" -> cut to the
  answer's face).
- **Contrast**: loud to quiet, day to night, wealth to poverty.
- **Question/answer**: one scene asks, the next opening image answers.
- **Pre-lap** of dialogue or sound: `JACK (V.O. PRE-LAP)` to start the next scene's
  audio early.
- **Match cut** described in action (`MATCH CUT TO:` or simply describe the matching
  shapes) - use rarely, when the match carries meaning.

## 4. Transition formatting
Modern spec scripts mostly omit `CUT TO:` (every slugline implies a cut). Use
explicit transitions only when the transition is a storytelling choice: `SMASH CUT TO:`
(abrupt contrast), `MATCH CUT TO:`, `DISSOLVE TO:` (time passing), `FADE OUT.` at the
end of acts in TV. Right-aligned, caps, followed by a colon.

## 5. Rhythm across scenes
Alternate scene lengths and intensities; three long dialogue scenes in a row drag
regardless of quality. Read the slug list alone: the INT/EXT and DAY/NIGHT pattern
shows visual monotony quickly.

## Gotchas
- Scenes that begin with arrivals and end with departures.
- Transition hooks that are clever but meaningless - a gimmick every scene.
- `CUT TO:` after every scene, wasting lines.
- Two scenes in a row with the same emotional outcome.

## Verify
- [ ] Scene list joined by but/therefore with no "and then" joints left unaddressed.
- [ ] Each scene starts after its set-up and ends on or just after its turn.
- [ ] Explicit transitions used only where they carry meaning.
- [ ] Slug list shows varied locations, time and length.
