# Breaking the season arc

**Goal:** a grid showing every episode's A/B/C stories and the season's serial arcs
climbing to a finale, agreed before any episode outline is written.

## 1. Season question and endpoint
Write the **season question** (will she clear her name? will the family keep the
farm?) and the **finale image** - where each principal ends. Then the **premiere**
endpoint and the **midseason turn** (episode 4-5 of 8-10). Work backwards from the finale.

## 2. Arcs per character
For each principal: start state, end state, 3-5 turns across the season. Plus the
**mythology/serial plot** (the case, the conspiracy) as its own row.

## 3. The episode grid
Rows = story lines (serial plot, each character arc, standalone/case-of-the-week);
columns = episodes. Fill tentpoles first: **premiere, midseason, penultimate
(the biggest event), finale** (consequences, new question). Then distribute turns so
each episode advances 2-3 rows. Spreadsheet, whiteboard or index cards per cell.

## 4. Episode allocation
Each episode gets a **title/engine sentence**, an A-story (often the serial or a
standalone), B and C stories, and an **ending hook**. Check: does every episode
change the status quo in a way the next episode must deal with? Is any character
missing for more than two episodes? Do two episodes feel the same?

## 5. Season document
1-2 pages of season overview + one paragraph per episode + character arc summaries.
This goes to the network/streamer for approval and becomes the room's map. Mark
which episodes can be shot as blocks (shared locations/directors) once production weighs in.

## Gotchas
- Season with no midseason turn; episodes 4-6 sag ("the streaming sag").
- Finale that only sets up season two without resolving season one's question.
- Arcs that move in big jumps between episodes with no on-screen turn.
- Standalone episodes that don't touch the serial rows at all.

## Verify
- [ ] Season question, premiere, midseason, penultimate and finale defined.
- [ ] Grid filled; every episode advances 2-3 rows and ends on a hook.
- [ ] Character presence checked across episodes.
- [ ] Season document written and approved.
