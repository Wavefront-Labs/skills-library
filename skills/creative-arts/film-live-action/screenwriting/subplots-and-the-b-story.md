# Subplots and the B-story

**Goal:** every subplot earns its pages by complicating, contradicting or resonating
with the main plot - and lands its own ending before or with the climax.

## 1. Decide what the B-story is for
McKee lists four jobs a subplot does; name which one each of yours does:
- **Contradicts** the controlling idea of the A-plot (ironic counterpoint).
- **Resonates** - repeats the theme in another key.
- **Complicates** the protagonist's pursuit (the love interest who is also the suspect).
- **Sets up** a multiplot film (ensemble structures).
Snyder's B-story is usually a **relationship** (love interest, mentor, rival) that
starts at the break into act two (~p.30 of 110) and is where the theme is *talked about*
while the A-story is where it is *acted*.

## 2. Give each subplot a spine
For every subplot write one line: *character wants X, obstacle Y, outcome Z*. If you can't,
it's texture, not a subplot - fold it into scenes of the A-story. A subplot needs at
least three beats: **set-up, complication, resolution** (most need 5-7).

## 3. Weave on a grid
Lay the A-story sequences across columns (8 sequences, Gulino) and place B, C beats
under them. Rules of thumb:
- Introduce the B-story **after** the A-story's inciting incident is clear.
- B beats land between A beats - they give the audience a breath after big turns.
- The B-story's crisis should **feed** the A-story's climax (Snyder: "A and B stories
  cross" at the break into three - the B character gives the protagonist the insight).
- Resolve subplots **before** or **inside** the climax, never after it (except a brief
  tag scene).
- Combine beats: one scene that advances A and B at once is worth two scenes.

## 4. Runners
Tiny recurring threads (a running gag, a dog that needs walking) need a set-up, two or
three repeats with variation, and a payoff. Track them on the grid so they don't vanish.

## 5. Audit
For each subplot, delete it in your head: does the climax lose meaning? If not, cut it
or merge it. Three subplots is plenty for a 100-minute feature.

## Gotchas
- B-story with no connection to the theme - feels like a second film.
- Subplot introduced in act three.
- Subplot resolution after the climax drains energy.
- Characters who exist only to service a subplot nobody pays off.

## Verify
- [ ] Each subplot has a one-line spine and a named function (contradict/resonate/complicate).
- [ ] Grid shows beats spaced through act two; none untouched for 25+ pages.
- [ ] B-story crosses the A-story at the act-two/three break.
- [ ] All subplots resolved by the end of the climax.
