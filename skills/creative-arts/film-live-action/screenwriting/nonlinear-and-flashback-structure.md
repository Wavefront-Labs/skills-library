# Nonlinear and flashback structure

**Goal:** a story told out of order because order creates meaning or suspense - never
because the linear version was weak and scrambling hid it.

## 1. Choose the form (Aronson's families)
| Form | Shape | Example pattern |
|---|---|---|
| **Flashback (preview/bookend)** | Present-day frame; past story is the main action | Open near the end, return, finish |
| **Flashback as interrogation** | Present action poses questions; flashbacks answer them | Detective/biography stories |
| **Parallel timelines** | Two eras, each its own linear story, cut together | Thematic rhyme between eras |
| **Tandem / ensemble** | Several protagonists of equal weight in one time/place | Interlocked stories on one theme |
| **Consecutive stories** | Sequence of stories handing off a protagonist or object | Chapters |
| **Fractured / multi-perspective** | Same events from different viewpoints | Each retelling adds a reveal |

## 2. Rules that make it work
- **Each timeline is a full linear story** with its own protagonist goal, turning points
  and climax. Outline each on its own first, in chronological order.
- A flashback must be **motivated by a present-tense question** the audience is
  asking right now (McKee: only flash back when the audience wants to know). Cut to the
  past at the moment of maximum curiosity; return at a moment of new tension.
- The past must **change** the present - new information that turns the next present scene.
- Converge timelines so their climaxes **land together** or the past's climax
  triggers the present's.
- Ensemble/tandem: unify by a **single theme, place or event** (a wedding, a city, a
  crash); give each strand the same number of turning points; cut between strands on
  rhyming beats.

## 3. Build it
1. Linear outline per strand on cards (one colour per timeline).
2. Interleave on the board; read only the card order and check suspense at each cut.
3. Mark the *question* each cut poses and the *answer* placement.

## 4. Format
`FLASHBACK - INT. FARMHOUSE - NIGHT (1987)` and `BACK TO PRESENT` / `END FLASHBACK`;
for long or recurring eras, put the year in the slugline or a `SUPER: 1987` and drop
"flashback" labels. Keep one consistent convention.

## Gotchas
- Opening with a flash-forward to the climax just to "hook" - spoils the climax if the rest doesn't reframe it.
- Flashbacks that only illustrate what a character already said.
- One timeline much stronger than the other; audiences resent returning to the weak one.
- Viewers lose their place: no visual/aural anchor for each era.

## Verify
- [ ] Each strand works as a linear outline on its own.
- [ ] Every cut to the past answers or raises a present question.
- [ ] Climaxes converge; era conventions are consistent on the page.
