# Cutting pages and tightening the script

**Goal:** a script at the target length (feature spec 90-110, comedy/horror often 90-100,
one-hour TV 50-60, half-hour 25-35) with nothing that matters lost.

## 1. Know why length matters
Roughly a page a minute; readers and producers judge length before reading, schedulers
budget by eighths of pages. A 130-page spec signals a writer who can't cut.

## 2. Cut in order of size
1. **Scenes**: reverse-outline; any scene whose `+/-` value change repeats another's, or
   whose information is delivered elsewhere, goes. Typical yield: 5-10 pages.
2. **Combine** two scenes in the same location or with the same characters into one.
3. **Enter later / leave earlier** in every scene: cut the first and last third of a
   page where it's arrival, small talk or summary.
4. **Subplots**: trim the C-story to its essential beats.
5. **Speeches**: cut first/last lines, repeated arguments.
6. **Action lines**: cut adjectives, stage directions the actor will invent, redundant
   descriptions of things already established.

## 3. Widows and orphans (the last few pages)
Search for paragraphs whose last line is one or two words; rewording to pull them up
saves a line each. Dialogue blocks splitting with `(MORE)` across pages cost two lines -
trim the speech to fit. Twenty of these is a page or more.

## 4. Don't cheat
Don't shrink margins, font (Courier 12pt is standard), or line spacing, or set
"tighter" element spacing in software - readers and production notice and scheduling
breaks. Final Draft's default templates are the standard; leave them.

## 5. Check after cutting
Re-read for continuity breaks: a payoff whose setup was cut, a character referred to
who no longer appears, a time-jump that now confuses.

## Gotchas
- Cutting the quiet character moments and keeping plot, leaving a mechanical script.
- Cheating format; production re-formats and the "real" length reappears.
- Cutting the setup of a payoff.
- Trimming only dialogue when the problem is redundant scenes.

## Verify
- [ ] At target length in standard format and font.
- [ ] Scene-level cuts made before line-level ones.
- [ ] Widows/orphans pass done.
- [ ] Setup/payoff continuity checked after cuts.
