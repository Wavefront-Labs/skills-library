# The midpoint reversal

**Goal:** a scene halfway through the film after which the story cannot go back -
the audience's understanding of the problem changes, the protagonist stops reacting and
starts driving, and the second half is a different film from the first.

## 1. What a midpoint does (choose the ones you need)
- **False victory or false defeat**: the want appears won (the heist worked, they
  kissed) or lost (the plan collapsed). Whichever it is, the end of act two will be
  its opposite - a false victory sets up a real low point; a false defeat sets up a
  darker victory.
- **Reaction to action**: before the midpoint the protagonist responds to the problem;
  after it, they pursue a plan of their own.
- **Reveal**: new information reframes act one (the mentor is the villain; the
  diagnosis was wrong). The audience re-reads earlier scenes.
- **Stakes raise**: the cost of failing goes up - personal (a loved one is now
  involved), public (the town knows), mortal.
- **The clock starts** or tightens: a deadline that structures the second half.
- **Want meets need**: the first moment the audience (not yet the character) sees
  that getting the want won't be enough.
Pick two or three; a midpoint doing all six is noise.

## 2. Place it
Around page 50-60 of 110, but the real test is proportion: equal pressure on both
sides. If the midpoint lands on page 40, the first half is thin or the second is
bloated; if on page 70, act one overstayed.

## 3. Build the scene
- Make it a **scene**, not a montage or a phone call: a confrontation, a discovery
  in a place, a public event. The biggest set piece in the first two acts often belongs here.
- Give it an **image** the audience remembers (the reversal visible in a picture).
- Let the protagonist **choose** something in it, even if the reveal is external -
  choice is what shifts reaction into action.
- Pay off a plant from act one; plant something for the climax.
- End the scene on the new question the second half will answer.

## 4. Check the halves
List what the protagonist wants and believes at page 1, at the midpoint, at the
crisis. If the belief column doesn't change at the midpoint, you have an event, not a
reversal. List every act-two scene after the midpoint: each should escalate under the
new condition; any scene that could play before the midpoint unchanged belongs there.

## Gotchas
- Midpoint as "something big happens" with no change in what the protagonist does next.
- A reveal the audience already guessed in act one.
- Two midpoints (page 45 and page 65) competing; merge or pick.
- Stakes raised by adding a new villain rather than deepening the existing conflict.
- The character learns the lesson at the midpoint; nothing left for act three.

## Verify
- [ ] Midpoint lands near the centre by page count; both halves carry weight.
- [ ] Stated in one line as false victory/defeat plus what shifts (plan, information, stakes).
- [ ] Protagonist makes a choice in the scene; the second half's question is posed by its end.
- [ ] Every post-midpoint scene depends on the midpoint's change.
