# High-volume reading and competition judging

**Goal:** read 10-20 scripts a week (or hundreds in a contest round) consistently,
giving each a fair read and a defensible score.

## 1. Reader workflow
- Read in **blocks** at the same time of day; no more than 3-4 features in one sitting
  before quality of attention drops.
- Read **in one pass** without stopping for notes; keep a scratch sheet of page numbers
  with a word (`p23 twist`, `p47 lost`). Write coverage/scores immediately after.
- Track time: an experienced reader reads a feature in 60-90 minutes; coverage 20-40.
- Log every script: title, writer, date read, score, logline - in a spreadsheet, so
  rereads and resubmissions are recognised.

## 2. Stopping rules (where the brief allows)
Some open calls (e.g. BBC Writersroom) read a first sample (10-15 pages) and only
continue if it engages. Apply the rule **consistently**; note why you stopped.
Contests that pay for full reads require you to read to the end.

## 3. Scoring rubrics
Typical criteria (weight per the competition's rubric): **premise/concept,
structure, character, dialogue, voice/originality, craft (format, typos)**, often 1-10
each. Write the comment **before** the number. Calibrate:
- Read the competition's anchor scripts or past winners at the start of the round.
- Use the whole scale; most scripts are 4-6, finalists 8+.
- Compare your average with other readers'; the administrator will flag outliers.

## 4. Fairness
- Declare conflicts (you know the writer, you've read it before) and recuse.
- Don't let genre preference move scores; judge the script against its intent.
- Blind reads: ignore any identifying info that slips through and report it.
- Never share or discuss submitted material (contest NDAs).

## 5. Fellowship-specific
Fellowship reads (e.g. Nicholl, studio programmes) weigh **voice and potential**
alongside the sample; the personal statement and a second sample may matter. Note
what's distinctive even in flawed scripts.

## Gotchas
- Scores drifting downward late in a session (fatigue); stagger difficult reads.
- Comments that don't match the score.
- Bias toward the first script in a batch (anchoring).
- Skimming the second half of every script.

## Verify
- [ ] Reading log with dates and scores.
- [ ] Rubric applied with comments preceding scores.
- [ ] Calibration against anchors and other readers done.
- [ ] Conflicts declared; confidentiality kept.
