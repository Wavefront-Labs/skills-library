# Revision colours and locked pages in Final Draft

**Goal:** once a script is in prep, hundreds of people schedule, budget and break down
from its page and scene numbers. Changes must arrive as clearly marked replacement
pages that slot into everyone's copy without renumbering anything. Final Draft's
production features do this; the conventions are industry-wide (WriterDuet, Fade In and
others offer similar tools).

Menu locations below are from recent Final Draft versions (12/13) and may differ in
yours; look under the **Production** menu. Not tested against every version — confirm.

## 1. Lock the shooting script
1. **Number scenes** (Production › Scene Numbers). Once numbered for production,
   keep existing numbers on future changes — never renumber.
2. Save a copy labelled as the white production draft with its date.
3. **Lock pages** (Production › Lock Pages). From now on, page breaks are frozen:
   added material creates **A-pages** (e.g. pages 23, 23A, 24) instead of pushing
   everything down; deleted material leaves short pages or combined pages
   ("24-25") rather than reflowing.

## 2. Revision mode and colours
Turn on revision tracking (Production › Revision Mode / Mark Revisions) and choose the
revision set. Changed lines get a revision mark (by default an asterisk) in the right
margin, and changed pages get a header with the revision colour and date.

The common US sequence of revision colours:
1. White (original locked draft)
2. Blue
3. Pink
4. Yellow
5. Green
6. Goldenrod
7. Buff
8. Salmon
9. Cherry
…then often Double White, Double Blue, and so on. Productions sometimes customise this
order — use whatever the production office/1st AD specifies.

Each revision round = one colour. Send **only the changed pages**, printed (or PDF
pages labelled) in that colour, with a cover note listing page numbers changed.

## 3. Scene changes after lock
- **Added scene** between 14 and 15 becomes **14A** (scene numbers locked).
- **Deleted scene:** leave the number with "OMITTED" so 22 still exists in everyone's
  breakdown.
- **Moved scene:** keep its number in its new position; the schedule follows the number.
- Final Draft handles these automatically when scene numbers are locked; check the result
  in the Scene Navigator before issuing.

## 4. Before issuing revisions
- Compare against the previous colour: are all changes marked, and nothing marked that
  didn't change? Formatting-only reflows can falsely mark lines.
- Check page headers show colour and date.
- Check A-pages and combined pages read in order.
- Issue a full "collated" script only when the production asks; otherwise pages only.
- Optionally **clear revision marks** when beginning the next colour, per production
  preference (some keep cumulative marks).

## 5. Beat Board and outlines
Before production, Final Draft's **Beat Board** (free-form cards) and **Structure
View/Outline** help plan structure; beats can be linked to scenes. Useful during
development; irrelevant once pages are locked — don't restructure a locked script there.

## Gotchas
- Editing a locked production draft with revision mode off: changes go unmarked and
  departments miss them.
- Changing page formatting (margins, font, element styles) after lock breaks every page.
- Mixing up which colour is current — put the colour and date in the filename.
- Sharing .fdx files with people on other software: export PDF for distribution; .fdx
  import elsewhere may drift formatting.

## Verify
- [ ] Scenes numbered and kept; pages locked on the white draft.
- [ ] Revision mode on with the correct colour before any edit.
- [ ] Added scenes show A-numbers; removed scenes show OMITTED.
- [ ] Only changed pages issued, with colour/date headers and a change list.
- [ ] Filename and title page show the current revision colour and date.
