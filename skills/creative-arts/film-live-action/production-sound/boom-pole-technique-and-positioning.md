# Boom pole technique and positioning

**Goal:** the mic as close as the frame allows, on axis with whoever is speaking, with
no shadows, no handling noise and no dips through a 12-hour day.

## 1. Holding the pole
- **Overhead hold**: arms extended up, pole horizontal, hands shoulder-width or wider -
  the weight stacks through straight arms onto the skeleton; less muscle fatigue than
  bent arms.
- Extend only as much pole as needed; every extra section multiplies the weight at the tip.
- Rest between takes (pole on shoulder or a boom stand); stretch; switch hand positions.
- Gloves (thin, quiet) help grip without noise.

## 2. Handling noise and cable
- Internal-cable poles: secure the cable so it doesn't slap inside (coiled cable or
  tension); or wireless boom transmitter at the base.
- Rotate the mic by twisting the pole smoothly; avoid sliding hands during takes.
- Shock mount (Rycote Lyre type) correctly fitted; windjammer/ blimp outdoors.

## 3. Finding the frame
- Ask the operator/ AC for the **frame line** - or watch a monitor; lower the mic until it
  dips into frame in rehearsal, then raise a few centimetres for safety (and for
  operators' reframing).
- Watch for **shadows**: from key lights, move to the side opposite the key or boom from
  below; ask the gaffer to flag a light if needed.
- Wide shots: plant mics/ lavs take over; the boom grabs what it can.

## 4. Above vs below
- **From above** (standard): natural perspective, less body/ clothing noise, room tone
  consistent; risk of shadows and frame dips.
- **From below** (pointing up from under frame): useful in tight sets with low ceilings
  or shadow problems; picks up more of the chest and footsteps; risk of camera seeing the
  mic in tilts; more low-frequency room. Match perspective between angles where possible.
- Aim the mic at the **mouth**, roughly 45 degrees down from above, at 30-60 cm.

## 5. Cueing
- Learn the script; know who speaks next; **swing** the mic to each speaker just before
  they speak (a beat early). With overlapping dialogue, favour the important line or use
  a second boom.
- Track movement with the actor; walk with them, keeping the cable clear (cable person on
  big moves).

## Gotchas
- Mic in frame on the take after the operator reframes.
- Boom shadow on the actor's face or a wall.
- Late cueing clipping the first syllable.
- Fatigue causing the mic to drift down late in long takes.

## Verify
- [ ] Frame line and shadow positions found in rehearsal.
- [ ] Above/below decision per set-up; perspective matched across coverage.
- [ ] Script known; cues planned; cable managed.
- [ ] Rest routine for long days.
