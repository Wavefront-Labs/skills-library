# Noise control on location

**Goal:** dialogue tracks that need no rescue - because the mixer walked the location
before the shoot, told the right people what to turn off, and the set has a routine for
holding when the world gets loud.

## 1. Hear the location before you commit
On the tech scout, stand where the scene plays, close your eyes for a minute, and list:
- Continuous: HVAC, fridges/freezers, fluorescent/LED ballast hum, computer fans, fish
  tanks, water heaters, traffic, river, wind in trees, distant motorway.
- Intermittent: flight paths (check Flightradar and the schedule), trains, school bells,
  church clocks, bin collection, construction, lifts, neighbours, dogs.
- Acoustic: hard floors and bare walls (reverb), glass, long corridors, low ceilings.
Report to the location manager and 1st AD with fixes and costs. A location that can't
be made quiet should be heard by the director *as an ADR decision* now, not at the mix.

## 2. Kill the continuous sources
- **HVAC/AC**: find the switch/thermostat; get permission to turn off per take (sign
  "DO NOT TOUCH" for the day); in offices arrange with building management for the
  floor's air handler. Tape a "HVAC OFF/ON" radio call into the AD's routine.
- **Fridges/freezers**: unplug for the scene; put a crew member's name on the plug and
  a **note on the call sheet** to plug back in at wrap (frozen food is a claim).
- **Lighting**: dimmer buzz on tungsten (ask the gaffer for a different dimmer or full
  up + ND), LED driver whine (swap unit/firmware, DMX dimming rather than mains), HMI
  ballast hum (move the ballast out of the room, blankets over it).
- **Camera/kit**: fan noise on cameras/monitors near the actor (fan settings "off while
  recording"), follow-focus motors, the DIT's cart.
- **Practicals**: neon transformers, clocks ticking (remove batteries), aquarium pumps.
- **Generators**: place downwind, behind a building, as far as cable allows; use a
  "movie-quiet" generator (Whisperwatt/MQ Power) and blankets/baffles; test from the
  set with the recorder before locking the position.
- **Phones**: on silent *and* airplane mode (lavs pick up GSM bursts).

## 3. Treat the room
- **Sound blankets / furniture pads** on C-stands behind the actor and opposite
  reflective walls; on the floor under the boom op; over windows (double as blackout).
- **Carpet / rugs** off camera on hard floors; **felt or rubber pads** on chair legs.
- Squeaky floorboards: locate, screw down or shim, or re-block.
- Close doors/windows (with gaffer tape for seals); hang blankets in open doorways.
- Reverb cannot be removed convincingly in post; dry it now.

## 4. Props, wardrobe and action
- **Wardrobe**: silk/nylon rustles; ask for cotton/wool; use Moleskin/Undercovers on
  lavs; remove jangling jewellery and keys; soften new leather jackets.
- **Props**: ice in glasses (use acrylic ice), paper (spray with water/wax), coins,
  cutlery on plates (felt under the plate, pre-set), bags of chips (replace with
  something quiet), guns (dry-fire with click, add in post).
- **Shoes**: rubber soles or foam/felt on soles for the master; footsteps added in Foley.
- Block noisy business away from lines, or ask for the line to be spoken *between*
  actions; the editor can overlap sound from another take only if the ISOs are clean.

## 5. Manage the intermittent
- **Lock-ups**: PAs with radios at corridors/doors/streets; "quiet please/rolling/cut"
  relayed; signs; polite traffic holds with permit and police if required.
- **Hold for sound**: the mixer calls "hold for the plane" to the 1st AD before action
  if a jet is overhead; a two-minute hold saves a scene of ADR. Agree the protocol on
  day 1 and don't abuse it.
- Keep a **noise log** per take (what hit, where) for the sound report; offer a wild
  line immediately after the take when a key line was hit.
- **Room tone**: 30-60 s with everyone still, lights on as in the scene, after each
  scene/setup change - the fill that hides the cuts.
- Neighbours: ask (and pay) the roofer next door to break for two hours; schedule
  exteriors around bin day.

## Gotchas
- Fridge unplugged and nobody plugged it back in - insurance claim.
- Generator placed by the electricians for cable convenience, under the open window.
- Blankets hung after lighting; shadows and flags fight for space - hang during pre-rig.
- Hold-for-sound never agreed; the AD rolls through aircraft and the mixer is blamed at the mix.
- Room tone recorded with the fridge back on.

## Verify
- [ ] Noise list from the tech scout with fixes assigned (HVAC, fridges, lighting, generator, locks).
- [ ] Reflective surfaces treated during pre-rig; props/wardrobe/shoes silenced in rehearsal.
- [ ] Hold-for-sound protocol and lock-ups in place; noise log per take.
- [ ] Room tone recorded per setup; fridges and HVAC restored at wrap (on the checklist).
