# Shadow screen materials and framing

**Goal:** an even, wrinkle-free screen that shows sharp shadows to the back row.

## Materials
- **White cotton or muslin**: traditional, soft look, cheap; dampen and stretch to remove wrinkles.
- **Tracing or drafting paper or vellum**: crisp small-scale screens, fragile.
- **Rear-projection fabric or fine white ripstop**: brightest and most even, best for large screens and projection.
- Avoid visible weave, seams or shiny coatings that create hot spots.

## Framing
- Stretch on a frame (timber, aluminium or PVC pipe) with staples or a keder channel, pulling evenly from the middle of each side.
- Add a **dark border** (black cloth) round the lit area so the audience sees a clean rectangle; extend the masking down to the floor so the performers are hidden.
- Size: the screen should be about 1/3 to 1/2 of the room width for a small hall; test legibility from the back row.

## Checks
- Look at the screen with the lamp on and no figures: no creases or bands, no hot spot in the middle.
- Check sag after an hour; re-tension if needed.
