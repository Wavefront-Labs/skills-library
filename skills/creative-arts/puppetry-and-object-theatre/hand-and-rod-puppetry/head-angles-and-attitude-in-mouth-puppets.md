# Head angles and attitude in mouth puppets

**Goal:** thoughts and feelings shown by head angle, not extra movement.

## Principles
- A puppet looks **where its eyes point**. Angle the head so the eyes point at the other character or object; a half-turn off target reads as inattention.
- **Chin down** reads as sullen, sceptical or sad; **chin up** as proud or hopeful; a **slight tilt** as curiosity or friendliness; a **quick turn** away as rejection.
- Shift attitude **before speaking**: look, then think, then talk.
- Avoid constant micro-movement; stillness makes movements read.

## Practice
- Eye-line drill: set targets on the wall at different heights and look at each in turn without moving the body.
- Attitude drill: say "hello" six times with a different head attitude (shy, bold, bored, suspicious, thrilled, sorry).
- In two-puppet scenes alternate looking at the speaker and at the audience.

## Check
Someone watching with sound off can tell the emotional state from head angle alone.
