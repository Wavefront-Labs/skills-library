# Holding consistent puppet height

**Goal:** a puppet whose height does not creep during a long scene.

## Why it drifts
Arm fatigue, looking at your own puppet, and reacting to the other performer all drop the arm. Audiences read lowering height as the character losing energy or leaving.

## Set a height
- Find the puppet's **character height**: the point where its eyes sit at the top of the frame, or its body base sits just above the playboard. Mark it on the set (a tape line, a hand-held marker) or at the edge of the monitor.
- Rest the elbow on a **pad or the rear of the playboard** to reduce drift.
- Keep your own head still; looking down at your hand pulls the shoulder with it.

## Scene variety
- Use deliberate height changes as acting choices: rising for pride, sinking for shame, bouncing for excitement.
- Keep both puppets in a two-hander at **similar heights** unless there is a size relationship to show.

## Check
Over a two-minute scene the puppet's eye line stays within a fist's width of the marker except where you choose otherwise.
