# Central rod and head control

**Goal:** head and body that move as one character with clean head turns.

## Anatomy
A typical rod puppet has a **central rod** from the head or shoulder block to the operator's hand, with a free-turning **neck** (a dowel or string on a swivel) and two thin **hand rods** held by the free hand.

## Control
- Hold the central rod near the lower end with a loose grip; turn the head with the **thumb and finger on the head control** (a cord or a short head stick) while the body stays still.
- Keep the central rod **vertical**; leaning shows the stage side and tilts the figure.
- Support the weight: the rod is a post, not a handle; hold it so the puppet's body hangs from the shoulder block, not the neck.

## Practice
- Turn head left and right on count, with the body still.
- Bow by tilting the rod forward from the wrist and returning, never by bending your whole arm.
- Practise walking and turning the whole figure by moving your feet and rotating the rod.

## Check
The head can turn 90 degrees while the body stays facing the audience, with the rod vertical.
