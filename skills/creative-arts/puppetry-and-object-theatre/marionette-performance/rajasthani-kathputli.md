# Rajasthani kathputli

**Goal:** understand the kathputli form and how its figures are worked.

## The figure
Kathputli figures are carved wooden heads with painted faces, a cloth body with long skirt and little or no leg articulation, and strings from the head and hands (often two or three per figure) to the operator's fingers or a simple control.

## Performance
- One or two performers sit or stand behind a small cloth screen; the operator works two figures at once with strings to the fingers of each hand.
- A **reed voice device** held in the mouth (the *boli* or *bir*) gives the squeaky, high-pitched character voices; a drum (dholak) and songs accompany.
- Story cycles use folk heroes (Amar Singh Rathore) and the dancing of the Rajasthani dancer figure.

## Handling notes
- Movement is created by short jerks of the string to the head and hands; the skirt hides the legs.
- The figures are often kept in storage in the same colourful dress and cloth.

## Respect
Learn from the Bhat community's performers; do not claim repertoire you have not been taught.

## Check
You can describe the string layout, the voice device and the staging of a kathputli show in your own words.
