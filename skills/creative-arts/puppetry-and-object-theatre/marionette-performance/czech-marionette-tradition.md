# Czech marionette tradition

**Goal:** place Czech marionettes in history and recognise their style.

## Background
- Czech marionette theatre grew from itinerant troupes who played **Faust, Don Juan and Kasparek** plays in villages from the 18th century.
- Josef Skupa designed **Spejbl** (carved by Karel Nosek in Plzen, first staged in 1920); **Hurvinek** joined him on stage on 2 May 1926. The father-and-son pair became an emblem of Czech puppetry.
- Prague, Plzen and other cities host puppet theatres, museums and festivals.

## Style
- **Long-string marionettes** worked from a bridge, often worked with a **control bar** (the exact Czech control type, paddle or cross, was not confirmed) and a strong comedic physical style.
- Heads of **Kasparek** and other stock characters are carved, with exaggerated features.
- Satire and wordplay are central in modern repertoire.

## Practice note
- Many figures use **wooden controls** and rigging adapted for fast comic movement.
