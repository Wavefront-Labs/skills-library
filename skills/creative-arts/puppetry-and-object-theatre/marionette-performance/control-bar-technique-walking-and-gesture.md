# Control-bar technique, walking and gesture

**Goal:** make a marionette appear to carry its own weight, walk with intention and
gesture clearly — from a bridge or on open stage. Building and stringing the figure is
covered in `handcrafts.stage-props-scenery-costume.puppet-making.stringing-a-marionette-and-building-its-control`.

## 1. Know your control
- **Horizontal ("airplane") control:** a main bar with head/shoulder strings, a
  detachable or rocking **leg bar**, and hand strings. Rocking the leg bar lifts
  alternate legs.
- **Vertical (paddle) control:** a vertical bar, often with shoulder/head strings at
  the base and back string at the top; tilting it bows or nods.
- Before rehearsal, **identify every string** and what it does; practise picking the
  right string without looking. Layouts differ between makers — learn *this* puppet.
- Hold the main control in your non-dominant hand at a steady height; the other hand
  plucks individual strings or works the leg bar.

## 2. Weight and contact with the floor
- The single most important skill: **the feet must touch the floor** with weight.
  A marionette floating a few millimetres high reads as a ghost.
- Find the height where the puppet just settles and the knees flex slightly; then keep
  the control at that height. Use a mark on the bridge rail or a sightline reference.
- Movement starts in the **body/shoulders**, not by swinging the whole control.

## 3. Walking
1. Shift the puppet's weight slightly forward (tilt the control a touch).
2. Rock the leg bar: one leg lifts, **move the whole control forward** as the foot
   lands — the body travels *over* the stepping leg.
3. Small, regular steps; the head stays level (no bobbing like a yo-yo).
4. Stops: last step lands, a beat of settling (knees flex), then stillness.
- Character changes gait: old = short steps, bent forward; proud = taller, slower.
- Practise along a straight tape line on the floor, then turns (pivot on one foot).

## 4. Gesture
- **Look first, then gesture:** tilt the head toward the target, pause, then lift the
  hand string.
- Gestures need **slower, bigger, simpler** movements than real life — the strings
  amplify jitter.
- Use **gravity**: let a hand drop and swing to rest rather than lowering it.
- Keep one action at a time; simultaneous head, hand and walk usually blurs.

## 5. Bridge and stage work
- On a **bridge** (raised platform behind a backdrop), lean on the rail for stability;
  keep the control steady while moving along the bridge for travel.
- Watch the puppet, not your hands; your sightline is from above, so check stage
  pictures in a mirror or on video.
- Plan **string paths**: puppets passing each other must cross without tangles; agree
  which operator passes "over".

## 6. Tangles
- Prevent: hang puppets on hooks immediately offstage, controls separated; never set
  a control down on the floor.
- Clear: hold the puppet up so it hangs freely, rotate the *puppet* (not the
  control) until strings untwist, then work out individual knots gently.

## Gotchas
- Arm fatigue drops the control and sinks the puppet; build stamina, use rests.
- Stage lighting can reveal strings — dark strings against dark backgrounds, check
  from the house.
- Uncertain: control types and terms vary across traditions (e.g. Czech, Burmese,
  English); learn the one in your hands.

## Verify
- [ ] Every string named and found without looking.
- [ ] Feet carry weight throughout a scene (video check).
- [ ] Walk along a line with level head and weight transfer.
- [ ] Gestures preceded by a look and finished with a settle.
- [ ] Controls hung safely offstage; no tangles in a full run.
