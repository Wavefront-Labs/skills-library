# Burmese yoke thay marionettes

**Goal:** recognise how Burmese marionette theatre is structured and operated.

## The figures
- Roughly 60-90 cm tall carved and costumed figures with jointed limbs and **many strings** (sources give 16 to 19 per figure, for example 18 for male and 19 for female characters; an earlier "up to 60" figure could not be confirmed), hung from a control bar and worked by one puppeteer per figure.
- A traditional troupe has a standard cast of about 28 figures: royal figures, ministers, a hermit, the nat spirits, a horse and elephant, animals and clowns.

## Performance
- Performed on a **small stage with a curtain**; operators stand behind and above, working strings from a bar.
- Movement is drawn from **classical Burmese dance**, with a strict set of poses and entrances.
- A **hsaing waing orchestra** (drum circle, gong circle, oboe) accompanies; narrators and singers voice the characters.

## Learning
- The art is passed down within families and schools; a figure's movements are learned by long apprenticeship.

You can name the main figure types and say why the strings and movements follow dance rather than realistic walking.
