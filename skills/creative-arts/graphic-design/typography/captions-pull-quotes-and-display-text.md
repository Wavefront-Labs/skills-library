# Captions, pull quotes and display text

**Goal:** secondary text that pulls readers into an article and explains images, distinct from body text
but in the same family of voices.

## The pieces (magazine and newspaper practice)
- **Standfirst / deck:** 20-40 words under the headline summarising the story; 1.3-1.6x body size, lighter
  weight or italic.
- **Pull quote:** a short, strong line repeated from the text (or a quote from the interviewee), 2-3x body
  size. Edit to under ~25 words. Place it on the same page or spread as the original, never before the
  reader reaches it on the next page (spoils the text).
- **Captions:** smaller than body (often 0.8x), different weight or a sans; start with the subject in bold
  or a lead-in. A caption should add information, not describe the obvious.
- **Credits:** smallest text on the page, rotated or along the image edge.
- **Kickers / section labels:** small caps or tracked caps above the headline.

## Pull quote styling
- **Hanging punctuation:** the opening quotation mark sits outside the text block so the letters align
  (InDesign Story > Optical Margin Alignment, or a negative left indent for the mark).
- Use real curly quotes or a large decorative quote glyph, not straight marks.
- Rules above and below, or a colour, separate it from body text; wrap body text around it with consistent
  text wrap offsets on the baseline grid.
- Do not repeat the same quote design style for captions; captions and quotes need different voices.

## Captions placement
- Under or beside the image, aligned to the image edge and the grid; keep caption width at or below image
  width. Multiple images: key captions with position words (left, above) or numbers.
- Sit captions on the baseline grid or a fractional grid aligned to it.

## Gotchas
- Pull quotes that invent wording not in the text breach editorial standards; quote exactly or paraphrase as a
  standfirst.
- Too many display elements per spread compete; one pull quote per spread is plenty in most layouts.
- Long captions set full-width under wide images become hard to read; break into columns.

## Verify
- [ ] Each spread has one clear entry point and at most one or two display elements beyond the headline.
- [ ] Pull quotes appear after or near their source in the text and are word-accurate.
