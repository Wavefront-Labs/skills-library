# Manual kerning of display type

**Goal:** headlines and wordmarks where the space between every pair of letters looks equal - space as an even volume, not equal distances.

## Why display type needs it
Kerning pairs are designed for text sizes. At 48 pt and up, small irregularities (gaps around A, T, V, Y, L, quote
marks, punctuation) become obvious. Logos and headlines are seen first and longest.

## Method
1. Set the word at final size and weight; set kerning to Metrics (or Optical) as the starting point and tracking to the intended value.
2. Think in **areas**, not distances: the white space between letters should look equal in volume. Straight-to-straight
   (HI) needs the most space, round-to-round (OO) less, diagonals (AV) least.
3. Work in **groups of three letters**: adjust the middle letter until it looks centred between its neighbours,
   then move along. Cover the rest of the word to focus.
4. **Shortcuts (InDesign/Illustrator):** cursor between letters, **Alt/Option + left/right arrow** = -/+ 20/1000 em
   (default; set in Preferences > Units & Increments); add Ctrl/Cmd for 5x steps. Alt/Option+Ctrl/Cmd+Q resets.
5. Tests: turn the word **upside down** (reads as shapes, not words), **blur your eyes** or view small, and check
   from across the room. Revisit after a break.

## Typical problem spots
- Capitals with open sides: L, T, P, F, V, W, Y, A next to each other or lowercase.
- Quotes, apostrophes, commas and periods after T, V, Y.
- Numbers (1 often too spaced) and mixed case (To, Ty, Wa).
- All-caps settings need tracking first (see letterspacing capitals), then kerning.

## For logos
Kern in live type first, then outline (see create outlines skill). Record kern values if the logo may be rebuilt.
Optical adjustments may go beyond spacing: shortening crossbars, adjusting overshoots (see logo optical refinement).

## When to stop
When no pair stands out in the upside-down and small-size tests. Over-kerning (letters touching, tight "designer"
spacing) hurts legibility.

## Verify
- [ ] Upside-down and small-size tests: no pair visibly looser or tighter.
- [ ] Values kept modest; letters never touch unintentionally.
