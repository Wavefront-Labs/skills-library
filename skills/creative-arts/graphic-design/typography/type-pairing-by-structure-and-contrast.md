# Type pairing by structure and contrast

**Goal:** two (rarely three) typefaces that clearly play different roles yet look like they belong together.

## Three relationships
- **Concord:** one family (or a superfamily) - safe, harmonious (see superfamilies skill).
- **Contrast:** clearly different in at least one dimension (serif vs sans, heavy vs light, roman vs script) while
  sharing something underneath - this is the goal of pairing.
- **Conflict:** faces similar but not the same (two humanist sans, two old-style serifs) - reads as a mistake. Avoid.

## What to share
- **Skeleton / construction:** humanist with humanist (calligraphic stress, open apertures: e.g. a Garalde serif with a
  humanist sans like Frutiger/Source Sans); geometric with geometric or rational (Futura with Bodoni/Didone);
  grotesque with transitional/realist (Helvetica-like with Baskerville-like) - Bringhurst's historical kinship idea.
- **Proportions:** similar x-height ratios and widths, so mixed in a line or stacked they feel matched.
- **Era and mood:** a designer's era match (a 1920s geometric sans with a Didone) or deliberate tension.

## What to contrast
Pick one main axis: classification (serif/sans), weight, width or style. Do not contrast everything at once.

## Roles
- Headline/display vs body; or body vs UI/data. Assign roles and stick to them: changing roles midway breaks hierarchy.
- Limit: 2 families; a third only for a specific role (code, data, a logotype-like accent).

## Test the pair
- Set a real page: H1, H2, body, caption, pull quote, numbers. Mix the faces in one line (inline emphasis).
- Check **x-height match** when mixed in a sentence; adjust sizes so x-heights align (e.g. sans 0.5 pt smaller).
- Look at both weights at text size and at large size; check figures and italics of both.

## Gotchas
- Pairing by "they look nice on a font pairing website" without checking the actual roles and languages needed.
- Two strong display personalities fight; let one lead.

## Verify
- [ ] Each face has one role; contrast clear in one dimension; no near-miss conflict.
- [ ] Mixed-in-line test shows matched x-heights and colour.
