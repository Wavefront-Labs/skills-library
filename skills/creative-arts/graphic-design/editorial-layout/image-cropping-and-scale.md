# Image cropping and scale in editorial layout

**Goal:** pictures that carry the story and set the rhythm of the pages, not just fill boxes.

## Crop for the story
- Decide what the picture says (a face, a gesture, a place) and crop to that. A tight crop
  on a face gives intimacy; a wide crop with small figures gives scale and isolation.
- **Remove dead edges** - empty sky, irrelevant objects - unless the emptiness is the point.
- Do not crop through joints (wrists, knees, ankles, neck): crops between joints look
  intentional, crops at joints look like amputations.
- Leave **lead room**: space in front of where a person looks or moves.
- Horizon and verticals: straighten in Photoshop first, not with the frame.

## Scale contrast
Muller-Brockmann and most editorial designers use **one dominant image per spread**, with
others clearly subordinate. Equal-sized images compete; a big/small pairing creates hierarchy
and pace. Across a feature, alternate scale from spread to spread.

## Fit the grid
- Crop edges align to column and module edges (and to the baseline grid at top/bottom where
  text sits alongside).
- Image width spans whole columns + gutters; heights snap to grid fields or the baseline grid.
- A **full-bleed** image runs to the bleed (3 mm past trim) on the edges it touches. Keep
  faces and text out of the gutter on spreads, and allow ~5 mm away from trim for important
  details (trim tolerance).

## Respect the source
- Editorial and documentary photographs: agree crops with the photographer or picture
  editor; some licences or credits forbid cropping, flipping or compositing.
- Never flip (mirror) photos containing text, known people or places.
- Check effective resolution after scaling: >= 300 ppi at final size for offset print; a
  big crop of a small file becomes soft.

## Workflow in InDesign
Place, Fill Frame Proportionally, then use the content grabber to reposition; set
crops on the grid. For alternatives, duplicate the frame on the pasteboard.

## Verify
- [ ] Each spread has a clear dominant image; crops say one thing each.
- [ ] No joint amputations, no faces in the gutter, effective ppi acceptable.
- [ ] Crop edges sit on grid lines.
