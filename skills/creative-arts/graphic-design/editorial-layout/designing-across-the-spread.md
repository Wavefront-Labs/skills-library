# Designing across the spread and the gutter

**Goal:** a reader opens a magazine or book and sees one composed image across two pages, with nothing
important lost in the fold.

## Think in spreads
- Set up facing pages (InDesign: Facing Pages on) and always view and approve spreads, not single pages.
- Treat left and right as one composition: a big image on the left balances a text block on the right;
  a headline can start on one page and an image complete it on the other.
- Mirror margins: inside margins are narrower or wider per the binding; outer margins give thumb room.

## Crossing the gutter
- Images across the fold: keep faces, eyes, product details and text away from the central 10-15 mm.
- Headlines across the gutter: break the word spacing at the fold (split between words), never mid-word.
- Allow the fold to absorb a little image (perfect binding hides 3-6 mm per side; lay-flat bindings less).

## Binding-specific rules
- **Saddle stitch:** pages near the centre of a thick booklet push outward (**creep**); the printer
  compensates in imposition, which shifts content toward the fold. Keep text and folios at least 5-6 mm
  from the trim and do not rely on exact gutter alignment.
- **Perfect binding:** glue takes roughly 3 mm each side of the spine, and the book does not open flat.
  Increase inside margins (15-20 mm minimum) and avoid text crossing the gutter.
- **Lay-flat, wire-o, section-sewn:** most forgiving for crossing images; wire-o needs hole allowance.

## Checks
- Print spreads at 100% and fold them, or view on a real dummy from the printer.
- Ask the printer for creep values and gutter recommendations before final layout.

## Gotchas
- Spreads that look great on screen (flat) lose their centre in thick perfect-bound books.
- Cross-gutter images on pages that are not printed as a printer spread (e.g. the centre of a signature
  versus across signatures) may shift in colour and alignment; check imposition.

## Verify
- [ ] Nothing important within the gutter safety zone; headlines split between words.
- [ ] Binding allowances agreed with the printer and applied in the margins.
