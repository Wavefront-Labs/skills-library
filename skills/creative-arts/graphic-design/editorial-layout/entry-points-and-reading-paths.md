# Entry points and reading paths

**Goal:** the reader enters the page where you planned, understands the story's point, and finds the start
of the text without searching.

## Entry points
- An **entry point** is any element that catches the eye first: a large image (especially faces looking
  out), a big headline, a number, a pull quote. Poynter EyeTrack studies found readers enter print pages
  mostly at the dominant image or headline, then scan display text before reading.
- Design **one dominant** entry point per page or spread, and a few **secondary** entries (subheads,
  captions, pull quotes, sidebars) for scanners who do not read the whole story.

## Reading paths
- Western readers scan top-left to bottom-right; print layouts often follow a **Z pattern**: entry top-left
  (or the image), across, diagonally down, to the text start.
- Text-heavy screen pages follow an **F pattern** (Nielsen Norman Group): users read the first lines and
  scan down the left edge; front-load headings and first words.
- Images direct attention: faces and gaze, arrows, vanishing lines. Have subjects look **into** the page,
  toward the headline or text.

## Sequence to design
1. Image or headline (entry) ->
2. Standfirst (what the story is) ->
3. Text start (drop cap or bold first words help) ->
4. Secondary entries along the way (subheads every 4-6 paragraphs, captions, pull quotes).

## Methods to check
- **Squint/blur test:** what is seen first, second, third?
- Ask someone unfamiliar to point to where they would start reading.
- Remove the dominant image temporarily; if the page has no clear entry, the headline is too weak.

## Gotchas
- Two equally big elements compete; readers bounce.
- Text start hidden at the bottom of a column or after a sidebar loses readers.
- Right-to-left languages mirror the reading path; adjust layouts, not just the text direction.

## Verify
- [ ] Each page or spread has one obvious entry point and a clear route to the text start.
- [ ] Captions and subheads let a scanner get the gist in 10 seconds.
