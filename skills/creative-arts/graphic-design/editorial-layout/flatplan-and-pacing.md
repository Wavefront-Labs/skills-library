# Flatplan and pacing

**Goal:** a page-by-page map of the issue that the editor, ad sales, production and designers all work
from, with a rhythm that keeps readers turning pages.

## The flatplan
- A grid of thumbnails, one per page, in spreads (page 1 alone as the cover, then 2-3, 4-5...).
- Total pages must be a multiple of the printer's **signature** (usually 8, 16 or 32 pages for web offset;
  4 for saddle-stitched small runs). Ask production first.
- Mark each page: section, story slug, ad (full page, half, sold position), colour or mono if the press
  allows mixed, special positions (inside front cover IFC, inside back IBC, outside back OBC).
- Tools: spreadsheet, InDesign page thumbnails, or dedicated systems (Woodwing, Adobe InCopy workflows).

## Standard magazine structure
1. **Front of book (FOB):** contents, editor's letter, letters, news, short departments; ads dense.
2. **Feature well:** the long stories, usually ad-free or ad-light consecutive spreads in the middle.
3. **Back of book (BOB):** reviews, listings, regular columns, last-page feature.

## Pacing
- Alternate dense pages (news, listings) with open pages (big image openers).
- Separate similar features (two portrait-led interviews) with a different type of story.
- Put the strongest feature first in the well, but not on the first spread after the contents.
- Right-hand pages are seen first: premium ads and feature openers often take them.
- Check the flatplan zoomed out as a strip of spreads for rhythm in colour and density.

## Ad placement rules
- Avoid ads competing with editorial on the same theme next to each other (or follow agreed adjacencies).
- Advertorials must be labelled and styled differently from editorial (many codes of practice require it).

## Gotchas
- Late ad sales change pagination; keep features in multiples that can move as spreads.
- A spread across signatures may shift in colour; avoid critical cross-gutter images there.
- Page count changes cost money; agree locking date for the flatplan.

## Verify
- [ ] Page count is a valid signature multiple; every page has an owner and content type.
- [ ] Strip view shows varied rhythm, no two near-identical spreads adjacent.
