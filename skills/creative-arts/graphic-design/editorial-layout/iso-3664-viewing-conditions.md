# ISO 3664 viewing conditions

**Goal:** everyone judging a proof or print sees the same colours, because they look under the same light.

## The standard
**ISO 3664:2009** defines viewing conditions for graphic arts:
- **Illuminant D50** (5000 K daylight simulation) with high colour rendering (CRI Ra >= 90) and a defined UV content
  (important for papers with optical brighteners).
- **P1 (critical comparison):** 2000 lux +/- 500 at the viewing surface - comparing proofs and press sheets.
- **P2 (practical appraisal):** 500 lux +/- 125 - judging the print as a reader would see it.
- Neutral grey surroundings (Munsell N8 or similar, matt), no strong coloured objects or windows nearby.

## Equipment
- **Viewing booth** or overhead viewing station (Just Normlicht, GTI, etc.) meeting ISO 3664:2009 with M1 UV content;
  lamps replaced at the manufacturer's hours (output and colour drift).
- Press consoles have built-in D50 booths - use the same standard in the studio so judgments agree.

## Metamerism check
Two colours that match under D50 may differ under shop lighting (LED 4000 K, tungsten). For packaging or retail
displays, also check under the light where the product will be seen (booths often switch between D50, D65, TL84, A).

## Monitor matching (soft proofing)
**ISO 12646**: calibrate the monitor to D50 white point (or adjust to visually match the booth), luminance ~80-160 cd/m2
matched to the booth's P2 level, gamma 2.2/L*. Then a soft proof next to the booth sheet is meaningful (see soft
proofing skill).

## Practical habits
- Never approve colour under office fluorescent light or by a window.
- Let eyes adapt for a minute; remove coloured clothing reflections; view at a 45-degree angle to avoid gloss glare.

## Verify
- [ ] Proofs and prints compared in a D50 booth at P1 level.
- [ ] Booth lamps within rated hours; monitor calibrated to match for soft proofs.
- [ ] Metamerism checked under end-use lighting for packaging.
