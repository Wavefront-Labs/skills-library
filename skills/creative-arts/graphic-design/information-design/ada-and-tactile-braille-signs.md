# Tactile and braille sign requirements (US ADA Standards)

**Scope:** US federal accessibility design standards (2010 ADA Standards for Accessible Design). Other countries and building codes have different rules. **Verify against the current text, the state and local building code, and consult an accessibility specialist; this is design guidance, not legal advice.**

## Which signs
Signs for permanent rooms and spaces (room numbers, restrooms, exits) generally need tactile characters and braille; temporary signs and signs for directions or information (overhead directories, directions) have different, visual-character rules. Check the standard's section on signs for the full list.

## Key requirements for tactile signs (typical, check the current text)
- **Raised characters:** upper case, a sans serif or simple serif typeface, not italic, oblique, script or highly decorative; stroke thickness and width within the standard's limits; character height commonly 5/8 in (16 mm) to 2 in (51 mm) measured on the capital I; raised about 1/32 in (0.8 mm).
- **Braille:** Grade 2 braille, dots rounded, placed below the corresponding text; braille is usually not required on text that is not tactile.
- **Finish and contrast:** non-glare finish; characters and background in contrasting colours (light on dark or dark on light).
- **Pictograms:** where used, a pictogram field with a stated minimum height, and a text description (tactile and braille) below it.
- **Mounting:** on the wall adjacent to the latch side of the door, with a clear floor space; baseline of the lowest braille cells at least 48 in (1220 mm) above the floor and of the highest tactile characters no more than 60 in (1525 mm), per the standard.

## Production
- Sign makers produce raised copy by photopolymer, machined or moulded acrylic with applied braille beads; specify the process and request a sample or proof.
- Brailled text must be translated by a braille translation tool or a qualified transcriber, with spacing checked; do not set braille as ordinary text.

## Design practice
- Keep the sign family consistent (see `creative-arts.graphic-design.information-design.wayfinding-sign-family`).
- Add visual information (larger characters) separately from tactile copy as the standard allows.

## Self-check
Mark the standard section for each rule on the sign drawing; verify mounting heights and location on the plan, and obtain a physical proof for tactile and braille checks.
