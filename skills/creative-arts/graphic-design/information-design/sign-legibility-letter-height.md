# Sign letter height and viewing distance

**Goal:** sign text readable at the intended distance by the intended audience, including people with low vision.

## Rule of thumb (check against the governing standard)
Common sign-industry guidance is roughly **25 mm (1 in) of letter (cap) height per 3 m (10 ft) of viewing distance** for good reading conditions. It is a starting point for lettering you want to read comfortably, not a legal minimum.

## Adjust for conditions
- **Faster approach** (driving, walking through a station) needs larger type: the reader has fewer seconds. Vehicle signs are sized by speed and distance and governed by road-sign standards.
- **Oblique viewing angles, low light, glare, glass and clutter** all reduce legibility; increase size or contrast.
- **Importance:** emergency exits, hazard and entry signs larger than secondary information.

## Typeface and style
- Use typefaces designed for distance reading: clear letterforms, open counters, moderate weight (not extra-light), and generous letter and word spacing. Upper and lower case reads faster than all capitals for longer words.
- Space more loosely at small sizes and for light-on-dark text.

## Contrast
- Aim for strong tonal contrast between letters and ground; light-on-dark and dark-on-light both work if the contrast is high. Matte finishes reduce glare.
- Accessibility guidance in many jurisdictions asks for a minimum contrast percentage between character and background; look up the one that applies.

## Legal standards
- Accessibility regulations (for example the US ADA Standards and equivalents elsewhere) define character height and viewing distance rules for specific sign types, with mounting heights; some are covered in `creative-arts.graphic-design.information-design.ada-and-tactile-braille-signs`. Where a regulation applies, it overrides the rule of thumb.
- Seek the local code officer or an accessibility consultant for compliance; this skill is design guidance, not legal advice.

## Testing
1. Print the sign at full size (tile pages), pin it where it will be mounted, and walk the approach path.
2. Test with someone with low vision if possible, and at dusk if the sign is exterior.

## Self-check
Compute required height from the longest viewing distance, compare with the sign face, and confirm a mock-up reads from that distance.
