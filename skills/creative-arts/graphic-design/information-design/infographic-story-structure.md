# Infographic story structure and reading order

**Goal:** a reader knows where to start, where to go next, and what to remember.

## 1. Fix the message and the audience
- One sentence for the main finding, one for who needs it and what they should do or believe afterwards.
- List the facts that support it and cut everything else; an infographic that answers three questions answers none.

## 2. Pick a structure
- **Linear narrative** (top to bottom): context, problem, evidence, consequence, action. Good for scrolling web pages and long posters.
- **Comparison:** two columns or paired panels with the same measures.
- **Hub and spokes:** one central figure with branches for sub-points.
- **Timeline or process spine:** see the timeline and process-graphic skills.

## 3. Control reading order
- Put the **entry point** (title, key number or hero graphic) at the largest scale and highest contrast.
- Use a single dominant flow direction: top-to-bottom, with left-to-right inside rows. Number the sections if the order is not obvious.
- Chunk into 3-7 sections with consistent headings, sized by importance, divided by space or rules instead of boxes where possible.
- Align to a grid so sections look related; use numbers, arrows or connectors only where order matters.

## 4. Close the loop
- End with the conclusion or action, repeated in one line.
- Add **source and date** line, methodology notes for derived figures, and the author or organisation.

## 5. Build and check
1. Storyboard on paper as thumbnails; test reading order by asking someone to say what they read first, second, third.
2. Keep text short: labels and one-sentence annotations; long prose belongs elsewhere.
3. For digital delivery provide a text alternative and keep text live where possible (see `creative-arts.graphic-design.information-design.alt-text-for-graphics`).

## Failure modes
- Several equally loud focal points; no entry.
- Decorative icons that carry no data.
- Missing sources.

## Self-check
Cover the title: does the structure still make the finding clear? Can a new reader tell the main point in five seconds?
