# Process and how-it-works graphics

**Goal:** a reader can follow a process and explain it back after one pass.

## 1. Define the steps
- Write the process in plain language, list steps in order, then merge until there are roughly 3-7. If a step has branches or decisions, use a flowchart instead (see `creative-arts.graphic-design.information-design.flowcharts-and-decision-trees`).
- Each step: a verb phrase title, one short sentence, optional who/when.

## 2. Layout
- Choose **one** flow direction: left to right (horizontal strip), top to bottom, or a loop for repeating processes. Avoid snaking unless the format forces it, and then mark direction with numbers.
- Number the steps; arrows are only needed when numbers are not enough.
- Equal-sized step blocks (cards) on a common grid; vary size only to emphasise the one critical step.
- Show **inputs and outputs** at the ends so the reader sees what goes in and what comes out.

## 3. Pictures
- Use a consistent icon or illustration style (stroke weight, corner radius, palette) across steps; see `creative-arts.graphic-design.information-design.icon-grid-and-keylines`.
- For physical mechanisms use a simplified schematic with labels and leader lines; use colour to track a substance or signal through the process.
- Avoid decoration that does not explain.

## 4. Text
- Keep step text short and in the same grammatical form. Define jargon once.
- Time or cost per step can be placed in a consistent slot (bottom of each card).

## 5. Check
1. Ask a newcomer to explain the process from the graphic alone; note where they stall.
2. Check that arrows or numbers cannot be read in two ways.
3. Provide a text version of the steps for accessibility and search.

## Failure modes
- Too many steps, tiny text.
- Mixed icon styles.
- Ambiguous loops (where does it end?).
