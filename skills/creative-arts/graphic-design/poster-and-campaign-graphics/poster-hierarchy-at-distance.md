# Poster hierarchy at distance — one idea, read in seconds

**Goal:** a poster or campaign graphic that stops a passer-by, delivers its message in
a few seconds, and rewards a closer look with details. Same principles apply to
billboards, key art and social thumbnails.

## 1. Reduce to one message
Write the poster's job in one sentence ("Jazz night, Friday 8 pm, free"). Then rank
every piece of content into **three levels**:
1. **Hook** — the image or headline seen from far away (one thing only).
2. **Message** — what/when/where in a few words.
3. **Details** — address, URL/QR, sponsors, fine print. Read only up close.
If the client insists everything is level 1, nothing will be.

## 2. Size for viewing distance
- Decide the realistic distance (corridor wall ~2–5 m; street poster ~5–10 m;
  billboard 50 m+) and how long people look (walking past: a few seconds; bus stop: longer).
- A rough legibility rule used in signage: about **25–30 mm of capital letter height
  per metre** of viewing distance for comfortable reading (some guides quote ~1 inch
  per 10 feet for maximum distance). Treat as a starting point and test.
- Billboards: famously ~7 words or fewer is a common guideline.

## 3. Compose around one focal point
- **Contrast** creates the focal point: size, value (light/dark), colour, isolation.
  The hook should be the highest-contrast element; give it space.
- **Lead the eye** from hook → message → details with alignment, scale steps and
  directional cues (a figure's gaze, lines in the image).
- Limit typefaces (one or two) and colours (two or three plus neutrals) so the hook
  isn't competing.
- **Value structure first:** design in greyscale. If the hierarchy works in grey,
  colour will reinforce it.

## 4. Tests before print
- **Squint/blur test:** what's left visible is your hierarchy. Is it the hook?
- **Thumbnail test:** shrink to ~5% (stamp size) — this simulates distance and also
  approximates a social feed thumbnail. Can you still tell what it's for?
- **Three-second test:** show it to someone for three seconds, take it away, ask what
  it was about and when. Fix whatever they couldn't recall.
- **Print a tiled proof** at 100% and pin it up at real distance.

## 5. Campaigns and series
- Keep a **system**: fixed logo position, type, colour logic and grid; vary the image
  or headline per poster. Each poster must still work alone.
- Build a template with locked elements, and a version list per size/aspect ratio
  (A-series, 24×36 in, digital screens 9:16 and 16:9). Recompose for each ratio — don't
  just scale; text may need relocating away from crops and safe areas.

## Production checklist
- Bleed (commonly 3 mm / 0.125 in), safe area margins, CMYK or printer's profile,
  rich black for large black areas if the printer recommends it, images at adequate
  resolution for viewing distance (large-format posters seen from metres away can use
  lower ppi than a magazine; confirm with the printer).
- QR codes: big enough to scan from the intended distance, high contrast, tested with
  multiple phones; include a short URL fallback.

## Gotchas
- **Busy backgrounds** behind text kill legibility; add a solid panel, overlay or move the text.
- **Thin and light type** disappears at distance and in bad lighting.
- **Too much information** is the commonest failure — move details to a URL.
- Distance rules above are approximations from signage practice and vary with
  typeface, lighting and contrast — test a real print.

## Verify
- [ ] One-sentence message; content ranked into three levels.
- [ ] Hook is the highest-contrast element; greyscale version still reads.
- [ ] Passes squint, 5% thumbnail and three-second tests.
- [ ] Type sizes checked against real viewing distance with a printed proof.
- [ ] Series: each format recomposed, shared system intact.
