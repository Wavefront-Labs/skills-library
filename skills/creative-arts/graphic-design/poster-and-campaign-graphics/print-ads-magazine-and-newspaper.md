# Magazine and newspaper print ads

**Goal:** an ad that reproduces well on the publication's paper and passes preflight first time.

## 1. Get the spec first
Every publication's media kit lists trim size, bleed, safety margin, accepted PDF standard, colour profile, maximum ink coverage and deadline. **Use theirs; do not assume.** Sizes differ widely.

## 2. Layout
- **Trim**, **bleed** (commonly 3 mm or 0.125 in, but follow the spec) and a **safety area** inside trim for text and logos.
- Spreads: keep key content out of the centre fold.
- Newspaper ads are often sold in column-width and depth units; build to the exact dimension rather than scaling.

## 3. Paper changes the file
- **Coated magazine stock** holds fine detail and higher ink limits; use the profile the publisher names.
- **Newsprint** is absorbent and low-contrast: use larger, sturdier type (avoid thin light type and small reversed text), avoid very fine screens, expect lower total ink limits than on coated stock, and expect images to print darker. Take the ink limit and profile from the publication and lighten images accordingly.

## 4. Export
- Export the **PDF/X** variant the publisher names (PDF/X-1a and PDF/X-4 are typical) from InDesign: **File > Adobe PDF Presets**, set marks and bleeds, convert to the destination profile; run **Window > Output > Preflight** first.
- Fonts embedded, images around 300 ppi (line art higher), no RGB unless the spec allows it.

## 5. Copy and legal
Proof phone numbers, URLs, offers and any disclosures with the client. Disclosure rules vary by jurisdiction and product; seek compliance or legal advice.

## Self-check
Preflight clean, spec checklist ticked, a proof or the publisher's reference to compare.
