# Stakeholder interviews

**Goal:** understand the organisation's goals, politics and perceptions before designing, and earn the
buy-in of the people who will approve the work.

## Who
- Decision makers (CEO, founders, marketing lead), people who live with the brand daily (sales, customer
  service), and outliers (a sceptic, a long-serving employee). Typically 6-15 interviews.
- Customers and partners are separate research, but interview a few if budget allows.

## Interview guide (adapt per role)
1. Your role and how you interact with the brand.
2. In one sentence, what does the organisation do and for whom?
3. What makes it different from competitors? Who are the real competitors?
4. Where will the organisation be in 3-5 years? What could stop that?
5. Three words for how the brand is seen today; three words for how it should be seen.
6. What must not change? What should go?
7. What would make this project a success for you?
8. Who else should we talk to?

## Conducting
- 30-45 minutes, one-to-one (people speak more freely than in groups), with a note-taker or recording with
  permission. Promise anonymity of quotes in the report.
- Ask open questions and follow-ups ("why?", "can you give an example?"); do not show designs or pitch ideas.

## Synthesis
- Transcribe key quotes to cards; cluster into themes (affinity mapping) within 48 hours.
- Note agreement, contradictions (leadership says premium, sales sell on price) and recurring words.
- Write a findings summary: 5-8 themes with anonymous quotes, implications for the brief, open questions.
- Present findings back before concepts; contradictions resolved here save rounds of revisions later.

## Gotchas
- Interviewing only leadership gives an aspirational view the frontline does not recognise.
- Leading questions ("Don't you think the logo is dated?") produce useless agreement.
- Missing the real decision maker means late surprises; ask who signs off.

## Verify
- [ ] Interview list covers decision makers and daily users; findings report shared and agreed.
- [ ] Brief references the themes and resolves the contradictions.
