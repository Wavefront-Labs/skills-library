# Brand positioning and attributes

**Goal:** an agreed, written statement of who the brand is for, what it offers and why it is different,
plus a few attributes the design can be judged against (Wheeler, Designing Brand Identity: Clarifying strategy).

## Positioning statement (classic template)
> For **[target audience]** who **[need or problem]**, **[brand]** is the **[category / frame of reference]**
> that **[key benefit / point of difference]**, because **[reason to believe]**.
- Write it with the client from research findings, not from the design team alone.
- It is an internal tool; the tagline and copy come later.

## Perceptual map
- Pick two axes that matter to customers (price vs specialist/generalist, traditional vs progressive).
- Plot competitors from the audit; look for credible, valuable empty space.
- Use as a discussion tool in the strategy workshop; agree where the brand should sit.

## Brand attributes
- Agree **3-5 attributes** (adjectives) with definitions: "Precise: we show our working and never round up".
- Pair attributes with what they are **not** ("confident, not arrogant") to avoid misreadings.
- Convert each into design criteria: "Precise" -> tight grid, measured type, restrained palette. These
  criteria judge concepts in the presentation and steer feedback.

## Process
1. Research and audit inputs (stakeholder interviews, customer data, competitive audit).
2. Workshop: positioning options, map, attribute card sort (start with 30-50 adjectives, cut to 5).
3. Write the creative brief from the result; get sign-off before concepts.

## Gotchas
- Attributes everyone claims (innovative, trusted, quality) differentiate nothing; push for specifics.
- Positioning the client cannot deliver on is a promise that damages the brand.
- Skipping sign-off makes concept feedback subjective ("I don't like green").

## Verify
- [ ] Signed-off positioning statement and attribute list with definitions.
- [ ] Each design concept can be explained against the attributes.
