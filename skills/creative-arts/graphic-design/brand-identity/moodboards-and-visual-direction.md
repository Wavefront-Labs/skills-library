# Moodboards and visual direction

**Goal:** agree the look and feel with the client before detailed design, so concepts are judged against
a shared picture instead of personal taste.

## Build routes, not one board
- Make **2-3 distinct routes**, each named and tied to brand attributes ("Quiet precision", "Warm craft").
- Each board, 10-20 images, curated across: colour, typography, photography or illustration style,
  texture and materials, layout energy, and one or two "touchpoint feel" images (signage, packaging).
- Add 2-3 lines of text per route: what it says about the brand, why it fits the positioning.

## Sources
- Photography libraries, design archives, physical materials photographed in the studio, non-category
  references (architecture, fashion, product design). Avoid boards made of competitors' work or famous
  identities: the client will ask for a copy.
- Tools: FigJam, Milanote, Figma, or InDesign/Keynote for presentation. Keep a source list (usage of
  references is internal only).

## Presenting
- Present routes side by side after recapping the brief and attributes.
- Ask the client to react with reasons tied to the attributes; capture what they like in each route
  (colours from A, photography from B) but agree **one direction** to develop.
- Some studios go further with **style-scapes**: rough compositions mixing type, colour and image in route
  styles, without a logo.

## Gotchas
- Boards that are all similar get the answer "a bit of each": make routes genuinely different.
- Images with strong subjects distract (a dog in a photo makes "dog brand"); pick for tone, not content.
- Copyrighted images may not be used beyond internal concept work; never put them in final assets.

## Verify
- [ ] Each route is distinct and explained against the attributes.
- [ ] Client sign-off on one direction (written) before logo or system design.
