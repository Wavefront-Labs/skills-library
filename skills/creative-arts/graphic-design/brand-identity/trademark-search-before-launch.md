# Trademark search before launch

**Goal:** catch obvious conflicts with existing trademarks before a client invests in a name or logo, and
know when a lawyer's clearance search is needed. Designers do preliminary screening; legal clearance is
a lawyer's job.

## 1. Define scope
- Goods and services: identify **Nice Classification** classes (45 classes; e.g. 9 software, 25 clothing,
  35 advertising and business, 42 technology services).
- Markets: the countries where the brand will trade now and within a few years.

## 2. Search names
- **WIPO Global Brand Database** (international, many national offices), **TMview** (EUIPO and partner
  offices), **USPTO** trademark search, national offices (UKIPO, IP Australia, CIPO).
- Search exact, phonetic and spelling variants, translations, and plural forms; filter by class and live status.
- Also search the web, app stores, domain registries and company registers (use without registration can
  create rights in common-law countries).

## 3. Search logos
- WIPO Global Brand Database **image search** (upload the mark) and Vienna Classification codes for figurative
  elements; TMview also supports image search.
- Reverse image search (Google Lens, TinEye) for unregistered lookalikes.

## 4. Record and advise
- Document search date, databases, terms, classes and notable hits.
- Report "no obvious conflicts found in preliminary search" or flagged risks; recommend the client's
  trademark attorney runs a full clearance before launch and filing.

## Gotchas
- An identical name in an unrelated class may be acceptable; a similar name in the same class may not. Judgment
  belongs to a lawyer.
- Famous marks have wider protection across classes.
- Searches go stale: new filings appear daily; repeat close to filing.

## Verify
- [ ] Search log with databases, terms, classes and date.
- [ ] Client informed in writing that legal clearance is required before launch.
