# Presenting design concepts to clients

**Goal:** the client chooses a route for the right reasons and leaves with agreed next steps.

## Before the meeting
- Confirm **who decides**. Present to the decision maker, not only the marketing contact who
  then re-presents (badly) upstairs. If you must, give them a self-explaining deck.
- Show **2-3 routes**, each genuinely different and each one you would be happy to build.
  Never include a weak "filler" option - clients pick it.
- Present **in context**: mockups on the real touchpoints (sign, app icon, packaging, social
  post, business card) at realistic size. Logos alone on white invite subjective reactions.

## Deck structure
1. **Recap the brief and strategy** (objectives, audience, attributes) - one slide; get a nod.
2. For each route: the idea in one sentence, the mark, how it is built (colour, type,
   graphic elements), then 4-6 applications. Explain how it meets the brief.
3. Comparison slide: routes side by side against the brief's criteria.
4. Next steps and timeline.

## In the room
- Present in person or on video with screens shared; do not email the deck first.
- Talk about the brief, not about what you like. "This route signals precision, which you
  said your customers value" beats "I love this colour".
- Present all routes before taking comments; ask for reactions to the brief, not personal taste.
- Silence after you finish is fine - let them think.

## Handling feedback
- Ask "what problem do you see?" when you hear a solution ("make it bigger/blue"). Fix the
  problem, maybe differently.
- Write feedback down, read it back, and agree the **single consolidated feedback** owner.
- Do not redesign live in the meeting.
- Close with: chosen route (or combination rules), changes, who approves, date of next round.

## Gotchas
- "Can we combine route A's symbol with route B's colours?" - sometimes good, often a compromise
  that loses both ideas. Show it if needed, but give your recommendation.
- The CEO's partner's opinion appears late; plan for it by asking who else needs to see it.

## Verify
- [ ] Decision maker present; brief recapped and agreed at the start.
- [ ] Each route shown in at least 4 real applications.
- [ ] Written summary of decisions and next steps sent within 24 hours.
