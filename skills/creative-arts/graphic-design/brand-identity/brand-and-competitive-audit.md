# Brand and competitive audit

**Goal:** evidence of where the brand is now and what the category looks like, so design
decisions are argued from facts, not taste.

## 1. Collect the current brand (the audit wall)
Gather every touchpoint you can find, physical and digital: logo files and variants in use,
stationery, website, social profiles, app, signage, vehicles, packaging, ads, presentations,
email signatures, uniforms, invoices. Photograph or screenshot each, **as found** (including
the bad ones). Wheeler calls this the audit; put it on a wall or a Miro/FigJam board grouped by type.

Note for each: logo version used, colours (measured), typefaces, imagery style, tone of copy,
consistency with the others, and quality.

## 2. Findings from the internal audit
- How many logo versions exist? Which colours are actually in use vs the "official" ones?
- What is **equity worth keeping** (a colour or symbol customers recognise)?
- Where does inconsistency cost money (re-done artwork, confusing signage)?

## 3. Competitive audit
- List 8-15 competitors and adjacent/aspirational brands (direct, indirect, and the brands
  customers compare you with).
- Collect their logos, colours, typography, imagery, taglines, key messages.
- Lay logos side by side at the same size, then in greyscale, then as colour swatches only.
- Identify **category codes**: what everybody does (e.g. fintech = blue + geometric sans;
  organic food = green + hand-drawn). Codes signal category membership; breaking them signals
  difference. Decide deliberately which to follow and which to break.
- Plot on a **positioning map** (two axes from strategy, e.g. traditional-modern x
  premium-accessible) to show white space.

## 4. Present
A short audit deck: current state (with the wall), equity to keep, problems, competitor
landscape, codes, white space, and 3-5 design implications that feed the brief.

## Gotchas
- Auditing only what the client sends you misses the rogue versions staff actually use - go find them.
- Do not mock competitors in the deck; clients often know and respect them.
- Trademark: note similar marks you find; full clearance is a legal job (see trademark search skill).

## Verify
- [ ] Every touchpoint category collected; logo variants counted.
- [ ] Competitive board viewed at equal size and in greyscale.
- [ ] Audit ends in written design implications, not just pictures.
