# Graphic devices and patterns

**Goal:** a visual language recognisable even without the logo (think of a brand's stripes, a cropped
shape, a frame), flexible enough for campaigns and strict enough to stay consistent.

## Types of devices
- **Derived shapes:** a part of the logo (a curve, an angle, a dot) used as a frame, crop mask or divider.
- **Patterns:** repeated marks or shapes for packaging, backgrounds, textiles. Build as seamless tiles
  (Illustrator Pattern Options) in brand colours.
- **Supergraphics:** oversized, cropped forms on signage, vehicles and walls.
- **Frames and containers:** shapes that hold images and headlines (a corner tab, a speech shape).
- **Lines and rules:** a consistent line weight, angle or motif for dividers and charts.

## Design rules (write them down)
- Construction: derived from the mark's geometry and grid, in a specified unit.
- Scale and crop: allowed crop amounts, minimum visible portion, never crop through the logo itself.
- Colour: which colours and combinations; device colour vs background colour.
- Placement: anchor to edges or the grid; relationship with the logo (keep clear space; never both large).
- Quantity: one device per layout, or a stated maximum.

## Build assets
- Master vector files (AI, SVG), tile files for patterns, Figma components with variants for frames,
  InDesign object styles or CC Library assets for staff templates.
- Test across real touchpoints: a social post, a brochure cover, a vehicle, a website hero.

## Gotchas
- Devices that are too similar to the logo dilute it, or read as extra logos.
- Overly complex patterns fail at small sizes and in embroidery; provide a simplified version.
- Freedom without examples leads to misuse; show 6-10 correct examples in the guidelines.

## Verify
- [ ] Each device has construction, colour, crop and placement rules with examples.
- [ ] Devices tested on at least five touchpoints and at small and large scales.
