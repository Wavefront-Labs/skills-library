# Brand architecture models

**Goal:** a clear, consistent relationship between a parent brand and its divisions,
products and acquisitions, visible in names and logos.

## The models (Wheeler; Aaker's brand relationship spectrum)
| Model | Parent visibility | Examples | Visual expression |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Monolithic / branded house** | Parent is the brand everywhere | FedEx (Express, Ground), Google products | One logo + descriptor; colour codes for divisions |
| **Endorsed** | Sub-brand leads, parent endorses | Courtyard by Marriott, Polo by Ralph Lauren | Sub-brand logo + "by Parent" / small parent mark |
| **Pluralistic / house of brands** | Parent invisible to consumers | P&G (Tide, Pampers), Unilever | Each brand independent; parent appears only on legal/corporate |
| **Hybrid** | Mix (often after acquisitions) | Many conglomerates | Explicit rules per tier needed |

## Choosing
- **Monolithic** when products share audience and reputation; cheapest to market; every
  failure touches the parent.
- **Endorsed** when sub-brands need their own personality but benefit from parent trust (new
  launches, acquired brands in transition).
- **Pluralistic** when audiences conflict (premium vs budget) or brands may be sold.
- Decide with the strategy team; designers express it, but the identity system makes it real.

## Designing the system
1. Map the portfolio: every entity, its audience, revenue role, and future (keep, merge, sell).
2. Assign each a tier and a naming convention: `Parent + descriptor` (Parent Payments),
   `Sub-brand by Parent`, or freestanding name.
3. Build **lockup rules**: position of the parent mark, relative size (e.g. endorsement mark
   at 50% of sub-brand cap height), clear space between, colour behaviour.
4. Document a decision tree: "new product -> does it serve the same audience? -> descriptor;
   different audience/price tier -> endorsed sub-brand; ...".

## Gotchas
- Too many sub-brands in a monolithic system dilutes it; descriptors are cheaper than logos.
- Endorsements that change size and position per product look like accidents - fix them in templates.
- Acquisitions need a migration plan (endorsed for 1-2 years, then folded in) - see rebrand rollout.

## Verify
- [ ] Every portfolio entity has an assigned tier and naming rule.
- [ ] Lockup specs (size ratio, spacing, position) documented with examples.
- [ ] A decision tree exists for future products.
