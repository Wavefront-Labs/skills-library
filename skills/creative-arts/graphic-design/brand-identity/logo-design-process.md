# Logo design process — from brief to a mark that survives use

**Goal:** a distinctive mark that works at favicon size and on a billboard, in one
colour and in full colour, delivered with the files and rules to use it correctly.

## 1. Brief and research
Get answers in writing before drawing:
- What the organisation does, for whom, and how it wants to be perceived (3–5 adjectives).
- Competitors and their marks — you need to look **different** from them.
- Where the logo will live: app icon, signage, embroidery, social avatars, packaging.
  This decides constraints (embroidery hates fine detail; app icons are square).
- Name length and whether the name or a symbol must carry recognition.
Collect a mood board of references for tone, not to copy.

## 2. Choose the logo type
| Type | Example form | When |
|---|---|---|
| **Wordmark / logotype** | the name in custom-adjusted type | distinctive name, new brand needing name recognition |
| **Lettermark / monogram** | initials | long names |
| **Symbol / pictorial mark** | icon alone | established brands, small square spaces |
| **Abstract mark** | non-literal shape | broad or changing offerings |
| **Combination mark** | symbol + wordmark lockup | most new brands — flexible |
| **Emblem** | name inside a badge | heritage, institutions; weak at small sizes |

## 3. Ideate wide, on paper
- **Thumbnails:** dozens of small, fast pencil sketches (aim for quantity — 50+ is
  common for a serious project). Explore concepts (meaning), not finish.
- Pick a handful of concepts that answer the brief in different ways.
- Avoid clichés for the sector (lightbulbs for ideas, globes for "international",
  swooshes) unless executed in an unexpected way.

## 4. Refine in vector
- Rebuild in Illustrator (or Affinity Designer/Inkscape) with clean geometry: few
  anchor points, consistent stroke-to-space ratios. Consider building from a grid or
  circles, but adjust **optically** — mathematically equal shapes often look unequal
  (round shapes overshoot the baseline slightly to look the same size).
- For wordmarks, start from a typeface but customise letterforms and kerning so it's
  ownable. Check the font licence permits logo use.
- Work in **black only** first. If it fails in black, colour won't save it.

## 5. Stress tests
- **Small:** view at 16 px (favicon) and ~10 mm in print. Details that fill in must go
  or a simplified small-size version is needed.
- **One colour, reversed** (white on dark), and on a photo.
- **Distinctiveness:** next to competitors, and a reverse image search for unintended
  look-alikes.
- **Unintended readings:** rotate, mirror, and show it to others — hidden shapes and
  letters nobody intended are a common embarrassment.

## 6. Present and deliver
- Present 2–3 directions in context mockups (signage, app icon, letterhead), each with
  the rationale tied to the brief.
- Deliver: vector master (AI/EPS/PDF and SVG), PNGs at several sizes with transparency,
  versions (horizontal, stacked, symbol only), colour versions (full colour,
  one-colour black, reversed), colour values (Pantone, CMYK, RGB, HEX).
- A short guideline page: clear space (often defined by a unit of the mark, e.g. the
  height of a letter), minimum size, colour usage, don'ts (stretching, recolouring,
  effects).

## Gotchas
- **Trademark:** distinctiveness isn't legal clearance. Advise the client to run a
  trademark search in their markets; that's a legal task, not a design one.
- **Thin lines and gradients** die in embroidery, fax, newsprint and tiny sizes; supply a flat version.
- **Converting text to outlines** in the master is essential — never send live type.
- **Committee feedback** ("make it pop") — tie feedback back to the brief adjectives.
- Process steps reflect standard studio practice; thumbnail counts are illustrative.

## Verify
- [ ] Written brief with audience, adjectives, competitors, uses.
- [ ] Wide sketch exploration before any vector.
- [ ] Works in black, reversed, at 16 px and on a photo.
- [ ] Checked against competitors and for unintended shapes.
- [ ] Full file set plus clear-space/minimum-size/colour rules delivered; type outlined.
