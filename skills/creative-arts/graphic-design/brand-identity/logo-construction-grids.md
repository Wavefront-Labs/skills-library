# Logo construction grids

**Goal:** a mark whose curves, angles and weights relate to each other, making it coherent and reproducible.

## Choose a system
- **Unit grid:** a square grid (e.g. 1 unit = stroke width). All strokes, gaps and radii are multiples of the unit.
  Good for monograms, geometric wordmarks and icon-like symbols.
- **Circle system:** a small set of circle sizes in a fixed ratio (e.g. Fibonacci 1, 2, 3, 5, 8 or golden ratio
  1:1.618) whose arcs define the curves (famous in published construction drawings of tech logos).
- **Angle set:** one or two angles (e.g. 45 and 22.5 degrees) for all diagonals and terminals.
- **Typographic basis:** the wordmark's x-height, cap height and stem width drive the symbol's proportions.

## Build in Illustrator
1. Draw the grid or circles on a locked `Construction` layer (guides or thin strokes).
2. Draw shapes snapping to the grid (Smart Guides, View > Snap to Grid / Point). Use Shape Builder to merge and cut
   (see shape builder skill).
3. Keep construction shapes for documentation; refine on a separate layer.
4. Apply optical corrections afterwards (see optical refinement) - note where you deviated.

## Document it
A **construction drawing** for the brand guidelines: the mark over its grid/circles with unit annotations and the
**clear space** defined in the same unit (e.g. clear space = 2 units = height of the "x"). It helps future
reproduction and shows rigour to clients.

## Gotchas
- Golden-ratio overlays drawn after the fact to justify a logo are a cliché and often inaccurate - use the system to
  design, or do not claim it.
- Pure geometry can look mechanical; the grid serves the idea.
- Snapping strokes to whole units can clash with optical sizes - prioritise the eye at small sizes.

## Verify
- [ ] Every curve and stroke traceable to the system (or noted as an optical deviation).
- [ ] Construction drawing and clear-space unit ready for the guidelines.
