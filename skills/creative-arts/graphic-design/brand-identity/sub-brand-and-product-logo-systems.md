# Sub-brand and product logo systems

**Goal:** a family of logos for divisions, products and services that is clearly related to the master
brand, scales to new additions, and does not multiply into a mess of one-off marks.

## Decide the level first (after brand-architecture-models)
| Level | Expression | Example pattern |
|---|---|---|
| **Descriptor** | master logo + plain descriptor text | `Brand` + `Consulting` |
| **Sub-brand** | master brand plus a named sub-brand with its own word | `Brand Pay` |
| **Endorsed** | product name leads, "by Brand" or endorsement mark | `Product by Brand` |
| **Independent** | own logo, no visual link | rare, for house of brands |
- Default to the lowest level that meets the business need; each new mark costs money and recognition.
- Criteria for a distinct sub-brand mark: different audience, a need for its own equity, long life, budget to
  support it.

## Systematic lockups
- Fixed relationship between master and sub-brand names: same baseline or stacked, spacing in units, the
  sub-brand set in the brand typeface at a defined weight.
- Generate lockups from a **template** (Illustrator artboards or a Figma component) so new products follow
  the rule automatically.
- Colour coding by division or product line if it helps navigation; avoid one colour per product when there
  will be dozens.

## Naming rules
- Agree name structure: descriptive (`Brand Analytics`), suggestive, or coined; capitalisation; whether the
  master name is repeated.
- Check trademarks for new sub-brand names (see trademark-search-before-launch).

## Gotchas
- Every team wants its own logo; a written request and approval process stops proliferation.
- Long descriptors break lockups; set a maximum character count and a two-line rule.
- Acquired brands: plan a migration path (independent -> endorsed -> master) with dates.

## Verify
- [ ] A new product lockup can be made from the template and rules without design involvement.
- [ ] Portfolio overview shows every mark at the same scale; family resemblance is obvious.
