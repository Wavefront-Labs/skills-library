# Logo optical refinement

**Goal:** a mark that looks balanced, even and solid at every size - the difference between a decent and a professional logo.

## Corrections type designers use (apply them to logos)
- **Overshoot:** round and pointed shapes extend beyond the baseline/cap height (about 1-3% of height) so they look
  the same size as flat shapes. A circle exactly as tall as a square looks smaller.
- **Optical centre:** the visual centre sits slightly **above** the geometric centre. Centre symbols in containers
  (circles, badges) a little high; centre triangles by their visual mass, not their bounding box.
- **Horizontal vs vertical strokes:** horizontals look thicker than verticals of the same width - make horizontals
  slightly thinner (5-10%).
- **Thinning at joins:** where strokes meet (n, m shoulders, crossings), thin them so the junction does not form a dark spot.
- **Ink traps:** small notches at acute joins keep counters open at small sizes and on absorbent print - optional, stylistic.
- **Visual weight balance:** elements of a symbol + wordmark should feel equal in darkness; adjust stroke weights.
- **Spacing:** letter spacing in wordmarks by area (see manual kerning skill); symbol-to-wordmark gap defined by
  a unit (e.g. cap height of a letter).

## Process
1. Build from geometry (see construction grids skill), then deviate deliberately where the eye disagrees.
2. Compare at many sizes: 8 px favicon, 2 cm print, billboard. Small sizes may need a simplified version (see responsive logos).
3. Check **reversed** (white on black): white shapes on dark look bolder - a reversed version may need slightly thinner strokes.
4. Flip horizontally and turn upside down to see imbalances fresh.
5. Print it - screens flatter.

## Gotchas
- Perfect geometric construction often looks wrong (circles small, junctions heavy); geometry is a starting point.
- Over-refining into many tiny details hurts reproduction - keep refinements subtle.

## Verify
- [ ] Round and pointed parts overshoot; centred elements sit at the optical centre.
- [ ] Even colour (darkness) across the mark at small size; reversed version checked separately.
