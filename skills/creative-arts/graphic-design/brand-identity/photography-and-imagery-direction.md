# Photography and imagery direction

**Goal:** photographs and illustrations from many sources (commissioned shoots, stock, staff phones) that
all feel like the same brand.

## Define the style in principles
- **Subject and story:** what we show (real customers in use, products in context, details of craft).
- **Composition:** crop tightness, camera height, space for type (negative space side), candid vs posed.
- **Light:** natural, soft daylight vs studio hard light; time of day.
- **Colour and grade:** warm/cool, saturation, contrast, how brand colours appear in scenes. Supply a
  Lightroom/Camera Raw preset or LUT for consistency.
- **Casting and representation:** diversity of age, ethnicity, body type and ability that reflects the audience;
  model releases.
- **Illustration** (if used): style rules per commercial-illustration skills.

## Show it
- 8-12 "this" examples and 4-6 "not this" examples per category (people, product, place).
- Short captions explaining why each fits.

## Photographer brief and shot list
- Objectives, usage (channels, crop ratios: 16:9, 4:5, 1:1, 9:16), deliverables count, file specs (RAW + edited
  TIFF/JPEG, colour space), shot list with must-haves and nice-to-haves, styling and props, locations, schedule.
- Ask for horizontal and vertical versions and room for text.

## Stock selection rules
- Avoid obvious stock clichés (handshakes, people pointing at screens). Check licence (royalty-free vs
  rights-managed, sensitive-use restrictions) and model releases.
- Grade stock with the brand preset so it fits.

## Gotchas
- Images that only work with text in one position limit layouts; shoot with options.
- AI-generated imagery: check the client's policy and the tool's licence before use.

## Verify
- [ ] Imagery guide section with principles, examples and preset delivered.
- [ ] A test set from mixed sources looks consistent side by side.
