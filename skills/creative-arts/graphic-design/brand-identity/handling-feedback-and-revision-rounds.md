# Handling feedback and revision rounds

**Goal:** feedback that improves the work and arrives in a controlled number of rounds, without the project
drifting or the fee evaporating.

## Set it up in the contract
- State the number of **revision rounds** per phase (commonly two to three) and what a round is: one
  consolidated set of changes, delivered together.
- Changes beyond the rounds, or that change the brief, are **change requests** billed at an agreed rate
  (AIGA Standard Form of Agreement includes change and additional services clauses).

## Ask for useful feedback
- One **consolidated** feedback document from one owner, by a date. Not ten people emailing separately.
- Provide a simple format: what works, what does not, against which brief objective or attribute.
- Ask for problems, not solutions: when you hear "make the logo blue", ask "what is the concern?"
  (perhaps it feels aggressive). Solve the problem, possibly differently, and explain.

## Respond
- Acknowledge each point in a numbered list: will do / alternative proposed / not recommended because...
- Push back with the brief and research, not taste. Offer a quick test if a disagreement is factual
  (legibility, contrast).
- Show changes clearly (before and after).
- Track rounds openly: "This is round 2 of 3."

## Approvals
- Get sign-off in writing (email or approval tool) at the end of each phase; later changes to approved work are
  change requests.

## Gotchas
- Late new stakeholders restart debates; at kickoff, ask who must approve and involve them early.
- Unlimited rounds produce worse work, not better.
- Contradictory feedback from different people goes back to the feedback owner to resolve.

## Verify
- [ ] Each round has one consolidated feedback doc and a numbered response.
- [ ] Written approval exists for every phase.
