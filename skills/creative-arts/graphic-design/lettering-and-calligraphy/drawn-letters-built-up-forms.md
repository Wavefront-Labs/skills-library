# Drawn letters (built-up forms)

**Goal:** letters that are constructed rather than written, with consistent weight, terminals and proportions.

## 1. Skeleton first
- Sketch each letter as a **single-line skeleton** on a grid (baseline, x-height, cap height). The skeleton fixes proportions, widths and the rhythm of curves.
- Keep round letters slightly larger than flat ones to look the same height (**overshoot**).

## 2. Add weight
- Choose a **weight rule** (vertical stems thicker than horizontals, or monoline) and apply it to every letter.
- Build the thickness outward or inward from the skeleton consistently; keep joins clean: where stems meet bowls and arms, thin the joint slightly so it does not look darker than the rest of the letter.
- Decide on terminals: flat, round, angled, flared, or serif; use one family of terminals.

## 3. Dimension and effects
- **Drop shadow or extrusion:** pick one light direction and extrude by a fixed distance.
- **Inline, outline, shading:** add after the form is finished; keep line widths consistent.

## 4. Spacing
After the letters are drawn, adjust spacing by eye; compare areas between letters rather than distances. Tracing paper overlays or a mirrored view help find problems.

## 5. Finish
- Ink with a fine pen or a monoline brush, or digitise (Illustrator Pen tool or Image Trace; see `creative-arts.graphic-design.lettering-and-calligraphy.vectorising-hand-lettering`).
- Clean outlines: smooth curves with few nodes, consistent stroke ends.

## Failure modes
- Heavy joints and awkward overlaps where strokes meet.
- Round letters looking small.
- Spacing set before the shapes are final.
