# Lettering composition and layout

**Goal:** a piece of lettering that reads in the right order with balanced shape, rhythm and emphasis.

## 1. Plan on paper
- Write the text; mark the **keyword(s)** that carry the meaning.
- **Thumbnail** at least 6 compositions in rough shapes before drawing letters: stacked lines, arc, banner, circular badge, diagonal. Choose one shape and work within it.

## 2. Hierarchy
- Give the keywords the most size, weight or colour; the rest become support.
- Use 2-3 lettering styles at most (for example script plus bold sans plus a small serif): one dominant, one secondary, one accent; change only one attribute at a time between pairings (size, weight, style).

## 3. Line structure
- Set baselines and cap heights as guidelines; for **arcs and envelopes** draw guide curves and keep letters perpendicular to the arc (see the envelope tools in Illustrator: **Object > Envelope Distort > Make with Warp**).
- Fill the shape: scale lines to the same width (justified look) or deliberately ragged.
- Keep leading tight but with clear separation; avoid collisions of ascenders and descenders unless intentionally interlocked.

## 4. Spacing
Adjust the spacing of every letter pair optically (kerning is manual in lettering); check by turning the sketch upside down or mirroring it.

## 5. Embellishment
- Ornaments, banners, underlines and flourishes should support the reading order; place them to fill awkward gaps and balance the visual weight.
- Keep the same line weight and corner style.

## 6. Refine
Trace the best thumbnail to a clean pencil sketch, adjust, ink or digitise. Review at a small size: the main words must read.

## Failure modes
- All words equal weight.
- Too many styles.
- Awkward gaps and rivers.
