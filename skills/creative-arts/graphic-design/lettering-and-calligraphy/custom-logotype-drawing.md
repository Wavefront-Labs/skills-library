# Custom logotype drawing

**Goal:** a wordmark with distinctive letterforms, balanced spacing and clean vectors that works from favicon to signage.

## 1. Brief and approach
- Define the brand traits and where the logotype will appear (sizes, backgrounds, single colour, reversed).
- Choose the approach: **customised type** (modify a typeface), **drawn from scratch** (built-up letters, see `creative-arts.graphic-design.lettering-and-calligraphy.drawn-letters-built-up-forms`), or **hand-lettered** (see vectorising skill).

## 2. Start
1. Sketch several directions on paper; pick two.
2. For customised type, set the name in 3-5 candidate fonts. **Check each font licence:** some licences exclude logos or require an extended or trademark licence; confirm before using. A commissioned custom drawing avoids that problem but is not immune to similarity claims.
3. Outline the type (Illustrator **Type > Create Outlines**) on a duplicate.

## 3. Modify letters
- Identify the 1-2 distinctive letters (a ligature, a cut, an altered terminal) and tweak them; leave the rest calm.
- Keep stroke contrast consistent; fix joins, overshoots and curve nodes (use few nodes, at extrema).
- Use the **Pen tool** and **Direct Selection** to adjust; apply the same modifications consistently across letters.

## 4. Spacing
- Optical spacing: equalise the white area between letters; consider rounded and straight pairs; check with the word turned upside down and at small sizes.
- Kern all pairs manually after the shapes are finished.

## 5. Test
- Reduce to favicon size and 16 px height; view in black, reversed and a single colour; display on light and dark backgrounds.
- Check against competitors for similarity.

## 6. Deliver
Vectors (AI, SVG, PDF), outlined; the construction guide (grid, clear space); colour specs; usage rules.

## Legal note
Trademark and copyright issues around typefaces and logos vary by country; consult an intellectual property professional for registration and clearance.
