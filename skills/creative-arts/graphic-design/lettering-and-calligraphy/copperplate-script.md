# Copperplate script

**Goal:** even, rhythmic copperplate with consistent slant, shade and spacing.

## Tools
- Flexible pointed nib: Nikko G (sturdy, good for learning), Hunt 101, Leonardt Principal
  (very fine, advanced). Prepare new nibs by cleaning off the factory oil (saliva, or a quick
  wipe with alcohol/toothpaste).
- **Oblique holder** for right-handers (see oblique holder skill).
- Ink: sumi or walnut ink thinned to flow; iron gall for crisp hairlines.
- Smooth paper that does not bleed (Rhodia, laser paper 90-100 gsm).

## Guidelines
- Proportion commonly **x-height : ascender : descender = 1 : 2 : 2** (some exemplars 2:3:2).
  Start with x-height 8-10 mm.
- **Slant 55 degrees** from horizontal (35 degrees from vertical). Draw slant lines every 5-10 mm.

## Pressure
- **Upstrokes = hairlines** (no pressure). **Downstrokes = shades** (pressure spreads the tines).
- Shades start and end gradually (swell) on ovals; on straight stems they are square-ended:
  press at the top, release at the bottom.
- Move the whole arm, not the fingers; go slowly - copperplate is drawn, not dashed off.

## The basic strokes (practise each as a row)
1. Hairline upstroke (entrance/exit).
2. Underturn (u-shape: shade down, hairline up).
3. Overturn (n-shape: hairline up, shade down).
4. Compound curve (overturn + underturn: n then u).
5. **Oval** (shade on the left side, hairline on the right) - the basis of a, c, d, g, o, q.
6. Ascending stem loop (b, h, k, l) and descending stem loop (f, g, j, y).
7. Reverse oval (capitals, x).

## Letter families
- Underturn: i, u, w, t. Overturn: n, m, v, x. Oval: a, c, d, g, o, q. Ascending loops: b, h, k, l.
  Descending loops: g, j, y, f.
- Many letters are built from separate strokes with **pen lifts**; the join is then a hairline.
  Spacing: equal space between letters, roughly the width of the counter of n.

## Practice routine
15 minutes of drills (ovals, compound curves) then words using the family you drilled
("minimum", "aqua"). Check slant with the slant lines; shades should be parallel.

## Gotchas
- Railroading (two thin lines in a shade) = not enough ink or too much speed.
- Snagging upstrokes = paper too rough, nib too sharp, or holder flange misaligned.

## Verify
- [ ] Shades parallel at 55 degrees across a full line.
- [ ] Hairlines unbroken; oval letters same width; even spacing.
