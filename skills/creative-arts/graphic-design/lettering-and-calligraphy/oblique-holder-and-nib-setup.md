# Oblique holder and nib setup

**Goal:** a pointed nib that lies in line with the slant so both tines touch the paper evenly and ink flows without scratching.

## Why an oblique holder
A straight holder forces right-handed writers to turn the paper or the wrist to get the slant. An **oblique holder** has a metal **flange** that holds the nib off-centre so the tines lie along the slant line while the hand stays in a natural position. It is standard for copperplate and Spencerian.

## Fit the nib
1. Choose a holder with a flange sized for the nib (check manufacturer notes; flanges differ).
2. Slide the nib in until firmly held, with the **slit** (the tine split) lined up with the flange direction; do not force it, since this can crack the nib shoulder.
3. The flange should hold the nib so the tines sit in a straight line with the pen hand and the slant. Test: make an upstroke along the slant lines; both tines should contact the paper.
4. Adjust the flange: some can be moved or bent slightly; for a right-hander, the point is usually aligned so the tip is centred over the slant line when the holder is held at the preferred angle. Make small adjustments, and only bend a flange with the appropriate tool or advice from the maker.

## Prepare a new nib
- New nibs have a protective oil coating that repels ink. Remove it by wiping with alcohol, a mild abrasive such as toothpaste on a soft cloth, or other cleaning; rinse and dry.
- Dip to just below the vent hole so the nib reservoir fills; wipe the extra against the inkwell.
- Ink flow: if ink does not flow, reclean; if it floods, wipe and reduce ink thickness.

## Left-handed writers
- Left-handers often use a **straight holder** or an oblique holder in reverse arrangements, with the paper rotated; many manuals explain these adaptations. Experiment, as there is no single standard.

## Care
- Wipe the nib clean after use and dry it to prevent rust; replace worn nibs when the tines spread or the line gets scratchy.
- Do not leave a nib in ink.

## Self-check
Draw a line of slanted downstrokes: shading should be consistent, hairlines clean, and the nib should not catch on paper.
