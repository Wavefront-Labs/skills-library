# Roman capitals with the broad pen

**Goal:** well-proportioned capitals with consistent stroke weight, serifs and spacing, in the tradition of Roman inscriptions.

## Model
The classical reference is the capitals carved on the Trajan Column in Rome (dedicated AD 113). Calligraphers write them with a broad-edged pen as a pen-made version of the carved forms. Study good exemplars from a teacher or a reputable calligraphy manual rather than copying one image.

## Setup
- Broad-edged nib (Brause, Mitchell, Speedball C-series or a Pilot Parallel pen for practice) and ink or gouache; slightly sloped board; guidelines ruled on layout paper.
- Measure letter height in **nib widths** by stacking small squares or "ladder" marks with the nib; choose a ratio from your exemplar and keep it for the whole line. Many teachers use roughly 7-9 nib widths for capitals; the exact figure varies by model, so confirm against yours.
- **Pen angle:** commonly taught at about 30 degrees to the baseline for Roman capitals; the angle is adjusted slightly for letters like M, N and diagonal strokes in some models.

## Letter widths (relative)
- **Round and wide:** O, C, D, G, Q are close to a circle or wide oval; the letter O is the pattern for round forms.
- **Wide:** H, N, U, V, A, T, K, X, Y, Z are roughly three-quarters of the height or wider.
- **Narrow:** E, F, L, P, R, S, B are about half the width of O.
- **Widest:** M; **I** is a single stem.
Use these as a spacing map: widths vary by letter, which gives Roman capitals their rhythm.

## Strokes and serifs
- Stem thickness comes from the pen width; thin strokes (diagonals) arise at the pen angle.
- Serifs: add small controlled hairline finishes at stem ends, either carved-style bracketed or as simple pen-made feet; keep serifs consistent across the line.
- Stroke order and direction follow your exemplar (generally top to bottom, left to right).

## Spacing
Space by **area, not distance**: round-round pairs closer, straight-straight pairs further apart, so the space between letters looks equal; word spaces about the width of an O.

## Practice and check
1. Practise stems, round forms and diagonals in rows.
2. Write a word with H, O and N first (they define the proportions).
3. View from distance; adjust spacing and rhythm; check consistent pen angle and baseline.
