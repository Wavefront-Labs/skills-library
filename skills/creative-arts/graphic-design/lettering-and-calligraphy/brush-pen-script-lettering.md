# Brush pen script

**Goal:** confident script lettering with flexible-tip brush pens, using pressure to make thick downstrokes and thin upstrokes.

## Tools
- **Small-tip brush pens** (for example Tombow Fudenosuke and Pentel Pocket Brush) for small and medium lettering; **larger flexible markers** (such as Tombow Dual Brush Pen) for bigger work. Test several; hardness and tip size vary.
- Smooth paper that does not fray tips (marker or layout paper).
- Ruled guidelines: baseline, x-height, ascender and descender lines, and slant lines.

## Technique
1. **Grip and angle:** hold the pen at roughly 45 degrees, lightly, with the tip pointing back at you; use the whole arm for big strokes.
2. **Pressure rule:** press on downstrokes for thick lines, release on upstrokes for hairlines; keep the tip moving smoothly rather than pushing.
3. **Drills:** practise upstrokes, downstrokes, ovals, underturns and overturns, and compound curves; then letters.
4. **Letters:** write lower-case first; connect letters with hairline strokes and keep consistent slant and x-height.
5. **Spacing:** equal visual space between letters; words slightly more space than a letter gap.

## Finishing (faux calligraphy alternative)
- Write the word with a standard pen, then thicken downstrokes by adding a second line and filling; this gives control without pressure.

## Digital scaling
- Scan at 600 ppi or more, then vectorise (Illustrator Image Trace) or trace manually; see `creative-arts.graphic-design.lettering-and-calligraphy.vectorising-hand-lettering`.
- On a tablet use pressure-sensitive brush presets (Procreate, Photoshop) to practise the same motion.

## Failure modes
- Fraying tips from pushing too hard or using the pen on rough paper.
- Shaky hairlines from drawing too slowly or from the wrist.
- Inconsistent slant.
