# Modern pointed-pen calligraphy

**Goal:** expressive, personal scripts with reliable pressure control and consistent rules, used for invitations, envelopes and branding.

## What it is
Modern calligraphy is a loose term for pointed-pen scripts that borrow the pressure principle of copperplate (thin up, thick down) but relax slant, proportions and joins for personality. The style freedom still needs rules: pick them and apply them throughout.

## Tools
Pointed nib (Nikko G, Zebra G, Hunt 101 or Leonardt Principal), an oblique or straight holder, ink (sumi, gouache for light on dark), smooth paper. See `creative-arts.graphic-design.lettering-and-calligraphy.oblique-holder-and-nib-setup`.

## Core skills
1. **Pressure:** thick on downstrokes, thin on upstrokes and releases.
2. **Style decisions** to make once: slant (often 55 degrees or more relaxed), x-height, ratio of ascender to x-height, thickness of the shade, connection style, entry and exit strokes.
3. **Bounce:** letters deliberately shift above and below the baseline for a playful look; keep the shifts controlled and rhythmic.
4. **Flourishes:** extend entry and exit strokes in oval curves that follow the slant; cross strokes smoothly; do not overload.
5. **Spacing:** keep similar space between letters; check the whole word at a distance.

## Workflow for client work
1. Collect exact text, spelling of names, quantities, paper and colour of ink.
2. Draft in pencil or layout paper with guidelines; test on the final paper type (ink absorbency).
3. Write a trial run, then the final; allow spare envelopes or cards.
4. For logos or print versions, scan at 600 ppi or more and vectorise (see `creative-arts.graphic-design.lettering-and-calligraphy.vectorising-hand-lettering`).

## Failure modes
- Inconsistent slant, which looks sloppy rather than relaxed.
- Excessive flourishes on every letter.
- Ink that bleeds on the chosen paper.
