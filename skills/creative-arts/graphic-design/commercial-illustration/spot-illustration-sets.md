# Spot illustrations as a set

**Goal:** a family of small illustrations (articles, onboarding, packaging) that look like one hand made them and can be extended later.

## 1. Define the rules first
Write a one-page style sheet:
- **Canvas and safe area:** a fixed artboard size, such as a square, with consistent margins.
- **Line:** stroke weight, cap and corner style, or no outlines at all.
- **Shape:** corner radius, level of detail, level of distortion.
- **Palette:** 4-8 swatches with defined roles (main, accent, shadow, highlight, background).
- **Light and depth:** a single light direction, or flat with no shading.
- **Point of view:** front, isometric or top-down, but the same for all.

## 2. Build for reuse (Illustrator)
- Put all spots as artboards in one document; keep a **Libraries** panel or shared swatches for the palette.
- Make **Graphic Styles** for stroke and fill combinations, and **Symbols** for repeated elements (leaf, hand, laptop).
- Name layers consistently; use a template file with the guide grid.

## 3. Review the set together
- View all spots on one board at actual display size; check weight, saturation and level of detail.
- Replace outliers rather than adjusting the palette for one piece.
- Check readability at the smallest size used.

## 4. Deliver
- SVG and PNG at needed sizes, a PDF contact sheet, the style sheet and the palette with codes; label any fonts or textures.
- Consider alt text for each spot on the web (decorative ones may take empty alt; see `creative-arts.graphic-design.information-design.alt-text-for-graphics`).

## Failure modes
- Mixed stroke weights from scaling artwork (check **Scale Strokes & Effects** in preferences, and rebuild at the final size).
- Palette creep as new spots are added.
- Different levels of detail across the set.
