# Editorial illustration for articles

**Goal:** an illustration that adds an idea to the article, delivered to a fast deadline.

## 1. Brief
- Get the article (or summary), word count, slot (spread, half page, spot, cover), orientation and size, colour or black and white, deadline for sketches and finals, fee and usage terms.
- Read the whole piece, note the central argument and any sensitive content.

## 2. Find the idea
1. Underline the key tension or claim, then write 5-10 visual ideas as quick thumbnails (literal, metaphorical, visual pun, symbolic).
2. Prefer an image that adds meaning rather than repeating the headline; avoid clichÃ©s (lightbulb, handshake) unless you twist them.
3. Check cultural readings of symbols and avoid stereotypes.

## 3. Sketch round
- Send two or three roughs (a clear sketch, not polished) with a line on each idea; ask the art director to choose one.
- Leave **room for the headline** and allow flexibility in cropping (extra bleed at the edges and a version with the focal point central).
- Keep the style consistent with your portfolio, since that is why they commissioned you.

## 4. Finish and deliver
- Make the finish at the agreed size and colour mode; test it at the likely reproduction size.
- Deliver on time or earlier; editorial deadlines are short, and lateness damages relationships.
- Be ready for one round of small revisions; keep the layered file.

## 5. Terms
- Confirm in writing: fee, kill fee for cancellation, rights (first publication in a named title, territory, term), payment date, and whether the publisher may re-use the art online or in promotion. Seek legal advice on contracts.

## Failure modes
- Producing a finished drawing before approval.
- Sensitive or legal subjects illustrated without checking with the editor.
- No space for the text.
