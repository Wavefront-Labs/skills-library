# Limited colour and value structure

**Goal:** illustration that reads clearly at a glance and survives reproduction in few colours.

## 1. Value first
- Thumbnail in 3-4 values: **light, mid-light, mid-dark, dark** (notan is a two-value design of dark against light). Place the darkest dark and lightest light at the focal point.
- Group objects into value masses; do not let neighbours share the same value unless you want them to merge.
- Squint, or work at thumbnail size, to see masses rather than details.

## 2. Check in greyscale
- Photoshop: add a **Hue/Saturation** layer with saturation at minus 100, or **Black & White** adjustment, above the art to check. In Illustrator use **Edit > Edit Colors > Convert to Grayscale** on a duplicate or View > Proof Setup.
- If the focal point disappears in greyscale, fix values, not hue.

## 3. Limited palette
- Assign colour by role: background, dominant mass, secondary, accent, outline or dark. Fix the palette (3-5 colours plus black or paper) before rendering.
- Make them global swatches (Illustrator) or a swatch set (Photoshop, Procreate) to keep to the limit.
- Use value variations of the same hues for depth; add one accent.
- **Recolour:** Illustrator **Recolor Artwork**; Photoshop **Gradient Map** or **Selective Color** adjustment layers.

## 4. Reproduction
- Limited-colour jobs (screenprint, riso, packaging) often print with spot inks; separate each colour on its own layer or channel and use Multiply for overlaps.
- Check that adjacent colours separate when converted to CMYK or to the printer's inks.

## Failure modes
- Hues of identical value (a red on a green) vibrating or merging.
- Too many mid-values: the picture looks washed out.
- Colour added late that breaks the value plan.

## Self-check
Greyscale at thumbnail size: focal point, secondary shape and background each distinct.
