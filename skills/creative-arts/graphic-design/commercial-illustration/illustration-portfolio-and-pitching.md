# Illustration portfolio and pitching art directors

**Goal:** a portfolio that makes a specific kind of commission obvious, and a pitching routine that is polite, targeted and repeatable.

## 1. Portfolio content
- **Focus:** show the work you want more of. An art director should see in seconds what you would be hired for (editorial, children's books, packaging, etc.).
- **Quantity:** a tight selection of your best pieces (many illustrators keep roughly 10-20 on the front page, with more in sections); one weak piece weakens the set.
- **Consistency:** a recognisable approach (line, colour, subject matter) across pieces. If you work in two styles, separate them into sections.
- **Show process sparingly:** a sketch beside a finished piece demonstrates concept thinking.
- Include **in-context mock-ups** (a cover, a magazine page) only for real or clearly labelled self-initiated work.
- Add a short about section, clients (only what you may name), and an obvious contact route.

## 2. Format
- Own website with fast-loading, uncropped images, readable on phones; each piece with title, client or "personal project", and year.
- Social channels support the site; keep a consistent handle and link.
- A PDF of 10-15 pages for emailing on request, under a few MB.

## 3. Pitching
1. Research: pick publications, publishers or brands whose illustration you would fit; read their recent commissions and find the art director or art buyer, normally via the company site or a professional directory.
2. Email: short, personal, one sentence on why them, three images in the body or a link, and your site. No big attachments.
3. Follow-up: a short note a few weeks later; then a small promotional mailer or new-work email on a regular schedule (every few months, with new work only).
4. Keep a spreadsheet of contacts, dates and replies. Respect unsubscribe requests and data-protection law for marketing email.

## 4. Pricing and rights
Quote with the usage described (medium, territory, duration, exclusivity); agree it in a written contract. Rates and standards vary; professional bodies and guild resources publish guidance. Seek legal advice on contracts.

## Failure modes
- Showing everything you have ever done.
- Unlabelled client work or confidential projects.
- Mass emails with no personalisation.
