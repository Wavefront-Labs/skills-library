# Moving between frames and in fashion film

**Goal:** produce frames (or footage) full of life — garments in motion, natural
transitions — while staying controlled enough to repeat a move, hit a mark and keep
continuity. Movement work rewards rhythm and repetition, not random energy.

## 1. Movement for stills
- **Flow, don't freeze-and-jump.** Move slowly and continuously through a sequence of
  related poses; the photographer shoots in bursts and picks the peak. Fast flailing
  gives blur and awkward in-between frames.
- **Peak moments:** build a movement with a clear apex (top of a step, the end of a
  turn, the moment a skirt flares fully) and pause or slow very briefly at it.
- **Make the fabric work:** a quarter-turn and sudden stop throws a skirt out; flicking
  a coat open from the hip shows lining; walking into wind (or a fan) lifts loose
  fabric. Ask the stylist what the garment should show.
- **Hair:** a head turn followed by a stop, or a flip, gives movement; agree with the
  hair stylist first — some looks must stay perfect.
- **Repeat to get it:** the same move 5–10 times, refining one element each time, beats
  improvising 10 different moves.

## 2. Jumps, spins and safety
- Warm up (ankles, calves, hips) before jump or dance sequences.
- Check the floor surface and the shoes. Jumping in heels on a hard floor risks ankles;
  ask for flats out of frame, a mat, or a cheat (shooting from low so a small jump reads big).
- Spin on the ball of the foot with a fixed spot to avoid dizziness; limit repeats.
- Land softly through the knees, and say no to stunts that are unsafe — sets have
  insurance and safety rules for a reason (rules vary by country and production).

## 3. Fashion film and video
- **Ask the frame rate.** Footage shot for slow motion (e.g. 60–120 fps played back at
  24/25 fps) exaggerates every micro-movement: blinks, adjusting hands and breathing
  become obvious. Move with more intention and slightly smoother than for real-time.
- **Hit marks.** Tape marks on the floor show where focus is set; land on them without
  looking down — rehearse until you feel them.
- **Continuity:** for multiple takes and angles, repeat the same action at the same
  moment (which hand moves first, where the bag is, when you look up). The editor
  needs takes that cut together.
- **Eyeline:** know whether to engage the lens (direct address) or never look at it.
- **Energy across a long day:** pace yourself; save peak energy for takes, not rehearsals,
  but rehearse at full speed once so camera and focus can learn it.
- **Social/vertical video:** framing is tighter and vertical; keep movement within a narrow
  frame and closer to the camera axis.

## 4. Working with the crew
Talk to the camera operator: where the frame edges are, how wide the shot is, and
whether they're following you or static. Moving out of a static frame wastes takes;
moving too fast for a follow focus ruins them.

## Gotchas
- Mouth open or tense face during exertion — keep the face relaxed even when the body works hard.
- Big movement in a tight, structured garment can tear seams; ask about limits.
- Slow-motion reveals shoe slips and adjustments — fix wardrobe before rolling, not during.

## Verify
- [ ] Sequences flow continuously with a clear peak you can repeat on cue.
- [ ] The garment's key feature (flare, lining, drape) is visible in the peak frame.
- [ ] Warm-up done, surface and shoes checked before jumps or spins.
- [ ] Frame rate, frame edges and marks known before video takes.
- [ ] Repeated takes match for continuity.
