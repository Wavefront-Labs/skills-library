# Fit-model sessions and garment feedback

**Goal:** a fit model is a measuring instrument with a voice. The brand builds its
patterns on your body and grades every size from it, so your value is **consistency**
of body and **precision** of feedback. Showroom work adds presenting the finished
collection to buyers.

## 1. Measurements upkeep
- The brand records a measurement set (bust/chest, waist, high hip, low hip, inseam,
  shoulder width, arm length, rise, etc.). Their patterns assume you stay within a small
  tolerance — often around a centimetre on key girths, but ask the brand what they need.
- Measure yourself at the same time of day with the same tape method and keep a log.
  Report changes early; a fit model whose waist drifted silently can skew a season's fit.
- Wear the agreed foundation garments to every fitting (same bra type/size, seamless
  underwear, or what the brand specifies) — they change measurements and fit.

## 2. Standing for the fitting
- Stand naturally, weight even on both feet, arms relaxed at the sides — **not** your
  posing posture. The pattern maker needs your real stance.
- Keep still while pinning; warn before moving. Hold positions you're asked for.
- Then do the movement test when asked: raise arms, reach forward, sit, squat, walk,
  bend. Many problems only show in movement.

## 3. Feedback: say what you feel, where, and when
The team sees the garment; only you feel it. Report in specific terms:
- **Location:** "at the back armhole", "across the high hip", "at the front crotch".
- **Sensation:** tight, pulling, digging, rubbing, loose, gaping, riding up, sliding off.
- **Trigger:** standing, sitting, arms up, walking.
- Example: "When I reach forward the back across the shoulder blades feels tight and the
  hem rides up about two fingers."

Useful vocabulary the team uses (learn it so you can follow comments):
- **Drag lines / pull lines** — diagonal wrinkles pointing to a tight spot.
- **Gaping** — an edge (neckline, armhole, waistband) standing away from the body.
- **Ease** — room beyond body measurement; "needs more ease through the hip".
- **Balance** — how front and back hang relative to each other (hem dipping at front/back).
- **Rise** — crotch depth on trousers; "rise feels short in the back".
Avoid design opinions ("I'd prefer it in blue") unless asked; comment on fit, comfort and
function (can you sit, lift a bag, fasten it yourself?).

## 4. Confidentiality
Fit models see unreleased collections. Expect to sign an NDA; don't post photos from
fittings or showrooms.

## 5. Showroom and buyer presentations
- Learn each style's name/number, fabric, colourways and key selling points before the
  appointment; buyers ask.
- Change quickly and neatly between looks; garments are often single samples.
- Walk close to buyers, turn slowly to show back and details, stop to let them touch fabric,
  and step back when the sales team is talking numbers.
- Long days, many repeats: keep energy and grooming consistent from the first appointment
  to the last.

## Gotchas
- Posing during a fitting — it hides fit faults.
- Saying "it's fine" to be polite; an unreported problem gets graded into every size.
- Inconsistent undergarments or shoes between fittings (heel height changes trouser length).

## Verify
- [ ] Current measurements logged; changes reported to the brand.
- [ ] Agreed foundation garments and shoes brought to every session.
- [ ] Feedback states location, sensation and trigger for each issue.
- [ ] Movement test done (sit, reach, walk) on every garment.
- [ ] For showroom days: style names, fabrics and colourways known.
