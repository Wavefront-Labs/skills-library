# Conceptual and character walks

**Goal:** perform a designer's idea (a character, a mood, a story) through the walk, consistently.

## Getting the brief
Ask the designer or movement director: who is the character, what are they feeling,
where are they going? Get two or three words ("bored aristocrat", "running late",
"floating"). Ask for a reference (a film, a dancer, a previous show).

## Translating it
| Element | Change |
|---|---|
| Speed | dawdle, hurried, stop-start |
| Stride | long, tiny, uneven |
| Upper body | slumped, very upright, leaning into the walk |
| Eyes | down, wandering, piercing |
| Arms | crossed, in pockets, carrying, swinging |
Change one or two, not all; the clothes still need to be visible.

## Consistency
Rehearse it until it is repeatable; a whole cast doing the same character needs to match.
Movement directors often run the cast in groups.

## Safety and comfort
Unusual direction (walking backwards, running, crawling, blindfolds, stairs) needs a
rehearsal on the actual set. You can ask for changes if something is unsafe; councils'
welfare guidance backs this.

## Verify
- [ ] Character summarised in a few words agreed with the director.
- [ ] Repeated identically in two rehearsal runs.
