# Head and chin line on the runway

**Goal:** a head that floats level and still from the top of the runway to the pit, so
the photographers get the same strong face in every frame.

## Set the line
1. Stand against a wall: heels, buttocks, shoulder blades and the back of the head touch.
2. Think of a thread lifting the crown straight up; the neck lengthens, the chin drops a
   fraction without tucking into the throat.
3. Step off the wall and keep that length.

## Chin angle
- **Level to very slightly down** reads strong and editorial; the eyes look out under the
  brow. This is the default for most shows.
- **Raised** shows nostrils and the underside of the jaw to the pit (photographers sit
  low) and reads arrogant. Only if the brief asks.
- **Dropped too far** creates a double chin in the pit shot and makes you look at the floor.
- Push the face very slightly forward (towards the lens) to sharpen the jawline; small,
  or it becomes a chicken-neck.

## Keep it still
The head is the camera mount: the hips and legs move, the head does not bob or sway.
Head bob usually comes from bouncing up on the toes or overlong strides; fix the stride,
not the neck. Do not tilt the head side to side with each step.

## Common faults
- Tension: clenched jaw, raised shoulders. Exhale, unclench the teeth, keep lips softly closed.
- Chin rises as you near the pit (nerves). Rehearse the last 5 m specifically.
- Turning the head to look for your mark. Learn the mark in rehearsal.

## Verify
- [ ] Filmed from the front, a fixed point on the forehead stays on one horizontal line.
- [ ] Pit-height photo (camera at hip height) shows no nostrils and no double chin.
- [ ] Jaw relaxed for the whole walk.
