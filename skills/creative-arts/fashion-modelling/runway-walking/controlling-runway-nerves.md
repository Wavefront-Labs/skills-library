# Controlling runway nerves

**Goal:** walk the show you rehearsed, not the faster, stiffer version that adrenaline produces.

## What nerves do to a walk
- Speed up (the most common fault); spacing collapses.
- Shorten the breath, raise the shoulders and the chin, clench the jaw and fists.
- Make you look at the floor or the audience.

## Backstage routine (2-3 minutes before your go)
1. **Breathe out long**: in for 4 counts, out for 6-8, five times. Long exhales slow the heart rate.
2. **Shake out** hands and shoulders, roll the neck gently, unclench the jaw.
3. **Run the walk** in your head: release, pace, end mark, pause count, turn, passing side.
4. Pick your focus point and your intention (the mood word from the brief).
5. Feet planted, weight centred, on the entrance mark.

## On the runway
- Consciously walk slightly slower than feels right for the first few steps; nerves make
  "right" feel slow.
- At the pit, exhale through the pause; it relaxes the face.

## After a bad walk
It is over. Reset with the same routine before your next look. Nobody remembers a
slightly fast walk; they remember a model who stays upset on the next exit.

## When it is more than nerves
Persistent panic, dread or sleeplessness about work is worth talking to a professional
about; see the `rejection-resilience` and `model-health` skills in this field.

## Verify
- [ ] Routine rehearsed before the show, not invented on the day.
- [ ] Show-day walk timed within the rehearsal pace.
