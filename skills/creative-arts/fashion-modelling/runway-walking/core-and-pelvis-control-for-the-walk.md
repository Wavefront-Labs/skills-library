# Core and pelvis control for the walk

**Goal:** a still upper body riding over legs that do all the work. Most "bad walks" are a
pelvis problem.

## Neutral pelvis
- Stand, find full anterior tilt (arched lower back, bottom out) and full posterior tilt
  (tucked); settle in the middle. That is neutral.
- Light core engagement: draw the lower belly in about 20 percent, keep breathing. Do not suck in hard; it shows in the ribs and stops breathing.

## Leading the walk
- The leg swings from the hip joint, the knee follows, the foot lands under the hip line.
  The hip moves because the leg moves; do not throw the hip sideways.
- Many coaches cue "lead with the hips": the pelvis arrives slightly before the shoulders,
  which keeps you from leaning back. The opposite fault, shoulders ahead of hips, makes
  you look like you are falling forward.
- Avoid the **lean back**: in high heels many beginners arch the lower back and push the
  hips forward. Re-find neutral, bend the knees very slightly, and let the core hold you.

## Upper body stillness drill
Walk 20 m holding a book flat on the head, or with hands lightly touching the hip bones to
feel the pelvis rotate while the ribcage stays square. Then remove the aid and repeat.

## Signs it's wrong
- Bobbing head (bouncing up and down) - pelvis is lifting each step.
- Excessive side sway or a "model wiggle" the brief did not ask for.
- Lower-back ache after rehearsal - anterior tilt; check in heels specifically.

## Verify
- [ ] Side video: ear, shoulder and hip roughly stacked through the step cycle.
- [ ] Front video: shoulders stay level; hip rotation visible but no lateral throw.
- [ ] Can talk normally while holding the core (still breathing).
