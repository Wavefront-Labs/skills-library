# Runway eyeline and focus point

**Goal:** eyes that hold the line of the walk and give intensity to the pit, without
looking down, around, or vacant.

## Pick the point
- In rehearsal, choose a fixed point at the far end at your eye height or very slightly
  above the photographers (an exit sign, a seam on the back wall, a light). Not the floor.
- In dark venues with strong front light you may not see beyond the lights; aim at the
  middle of the light bank, not at the floor in front of you.
- On the way back, pick a second point at the entrance (often the top of the backdrop).

## Hold it with life
- Think of a specific thought or intention (the brief's mood) so the eyes are engaged,
  not glazed. Blink normally; holding the eyes wide open makes them water and look startled.
- Peripheral vision handles the runway edges and the model passing you. Do not look at
  them.
- Do not look at the audience, the front row, or friends unless the show briefs it.

## At the pit
Eyes go to the photographers' lenses, typically straight ahead into the centre of the
pit. A small slow eye move across the pit is allowed in some shows; ask. Keep the eyes on
the lenses through the start of the turn (see `pivot-turn` and `pause-hold` skills).

## Faults
- Looking down at the edge of the runway (fear of falling). Rehearse the venue and trust
  peripheral vision.
- Eyes drop the moment the turn starts. Head and eyes should be the last thing to leave.

## Verify
- [ ] Filmed walk: eyes do not drop below the horizon at any point.
- [ ] Pit photos show eyes into lens, not past it.
