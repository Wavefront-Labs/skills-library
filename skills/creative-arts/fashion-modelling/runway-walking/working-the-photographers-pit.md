# Working the photographers' pit

**Goal:** give the pit what it needs, because those pictures are what the world sees of
the show.

## What the pit is
- A tiered riser at the end of the runway, facing it, with photographers on long lenses
  (often 70-200 mm and longer) shooting the whole final stretch, not just the stop.
- Video crews usually sit in the centre. Designers' official photographers have the best
  positions.
- Some shows also have side photographers along the runway shooting profiles.

## What that means for you
- The last 10-15 m of the walk is photographed continuously: posture, face and eyeline
  must be at their best from well before the end mark.
- Shooting from a low riser at long range compresses perspective; a raised chin shows nostrils.
- Eyes to the centre of the pit (where the lenses are), not over their heads.
- Flash is mostly not used; the show lighting is set for cameras. Find the light on your
  face by staying on the centre line.

## Avoid
- Playing to one photographer.
- Stopping short of the mark (the light and focus are set to it).
- Breaking expression the instant you start the turn.

## Verify
- [ ] Peak posture held from the last 15 m, not only at the stop.
- [ ] Eyes into the lens bank in pit images.
