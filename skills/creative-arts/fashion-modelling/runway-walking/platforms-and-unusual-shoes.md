# Platforms and unusual shoes

**Goal:** stay upright in whatever the designer puts on your feet, and know when to say no.

## Platforms
- The sole does not flex: you cannot roll heel to toe. Lift the knee more and place the
  whole foot; shorter stride.
- Ankle has less feedback from the floor; go slower on the turn, prefer the two-step turn.
- Very high platforms: walk with slightly more weight forward and keep the core firm.

## Mules and backless shoes
- Grip with the toes very lightly, keep the foot flexed so the heel stays in; shorter
  stride. Ask for clear tape or a gel grip on the footbed.
- Slingbacks: check the strap is not slipping down; ask for elastic or a stitch.

## Sculptural, heelless, unusual heels
- Practise in them before the show - always ask for the shoes at the fitting.
- Balance point may be far forward; find it standing still before walking.

## Wrong size
Sample shoes are often one size (commonly EU 38-39 / UK 5-6 for women, EU 42-43 for men).
Too big: heel grips, toe inserts, tissue. Too small: tell the stylist early; do not walk a
show in shoes you cannot stand in.

## Saying it's unsafe
If you cannot walk safely after trying fixes, tell the shoe or show producer at the
fitting or rehearsal, not on the runway. Welfare guidance from fashion councils supports
models raising safety concerns.

## Verify
- [ ] Walked the full runway route in the actual show shoe before the show.
- [ ] Fit fixes applied (grip, tape, inserts) and tested.
