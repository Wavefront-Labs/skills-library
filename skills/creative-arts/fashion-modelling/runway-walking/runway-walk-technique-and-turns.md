# Runway walk technique and turns

**Goal:** show the clothes, not the walk. A good runway walk is consistent, on time,
and makes the garment move the way the designer intended. Each show briefs its own
style (fast and hard, slow and soft, smiling or not) — the technique below is the base
you adapt to the brief.

## 1. Posture and line
- Stand tall from the crown of the head; shoulders down and slightly back, not pinned.
- Neck long, chin roughly level (slightly lowered reads stronger; raised reads arrogant
  and shows nostrils to photographers at the end).
- Core engaged so the upper body stays quiet; the movement comes from the hips and legs.
- Arms relaxed and swinging naturally, close to the body, hands soft (no fists, no
  splayed fingers). Some briefs want arms still — practise both.

## 2. Feet and stride
- Place feet along a single line, or slightly crossing for a stronger hip swing
  (womenswear), or on two close parallel lines (typical menswear). Over-crossing looks
  forced and is a trip risk.
- Lead with the leg; the heel strikes slightly before the ball in flats, closer to
  ball-first in high heels. Keep knees soft, not locked.
- Consistent stride length; don't shuffle in heels.
- Eyes forward to a fixed point at the end (often just above the photographers' pit),
  not down at the floor.

## 3. Pace and music
Most shows set pace by spacing: the next model goes when the previous one reaches a mark.
Count the music's beat in rehearsal and match steps to it if asked. Stay at the briefed
speed even when nervous — nerves speed people up.

## 4. End of runway: pause and turn
At the end is the photographers' pit; this is the money shot.
1. Arrive on your mark, plant feet (one slightly in front, weight settled).
2. **Pause** for the briefed count (often a beat or two; some shows want no stop).
   Hold the face and eyeline; small angle change only if allowed.
3. **Turn:** a common clean turn is to step and pivot on the balls of the feet through
   180°, keeping the head on the camera until the body has turned, then walk back.
   Keep the turn small and quick; big theatrical turns are only for briefs that ask.
4. On the way back, keep to the side you're told (usually passing on one side of
   the incoming model) and keep walking to the end — the show isn't over at the turn.

## 5. Shoes and garments
- Practise in shoes as high and as unstable as you'll be given. Scuff new soles (or
  ask the dresser for grip tape) — runways can be slippery.
- Get the shoes as early as possible; report fit problems before the show, not after a fall.
- Long gowns: kick the hem forward slightly with each step or hold as directed; don't
  step on trains on the turn. Coats: let them open and move unless told otherwise.
- If something goes wrong (shoe lost, strap breaks), keep walking calmly; carrying the
  shoe and finishing professionally is remembered better than a stop.

## 6. Backstage discipline
Arrive on time, hair and make-up ready to start (clean face/hair if asked). Learn your
running order, your look numbers and your dresser. Quick changes between looks can be
very fast; keep still and let the dresser work. No food or drink near the clothes.

## Practice drill
Film yourself from the front at the end of a hallway: 10 walks, pause, turn, walk back.
Watch for bobbing head, stiff arms, uneven stride, looking down, and wobbling turns.

## Verify
- [ ] Upper body stays quiet; head level; arms relaxed.
- [ ] Consistent stride and pace across 10 filmed walks.
- [ ] Clean pause and turn that keeps eyeline on camera.
- [ ] Practised in the highest/most difficult shoes you might be given.
- [ ] You know the brief, running order and passing side before the show.
