# Heel-to-toe roll and knee use

**Goal:** a quiet, smooth foot contact: no stomping, no clacking, no locked knees.

## Flats and low heels
- Heel lands first, the foot rolls through the outer edge to the ball, toes push off.
- The knee is slightly bent at contact and straightens through the step. A locked knee at
  contact makes a jarring, stiff walk.

## High heels (about 8 cm and over)
- Contact is close to simultaneous heel and ball, or ball fractionally first for very high
  heels; a heel-first strike on a stiletto can skid.
- Lift the knee a little more than normal so the shoe is placed, not dragged; dragging the
  toe catches on runway seams.
- Keep the knee soft at all times. Straight-knee heel walking is what makes models look
  like they are wading.

## Sound check
A loud strike means too much heel first or too much weight dropped through the leg.
Venues are often quiet between tracks; the audience hears it.

## Drill
Walk barefoot on a line, then in flats, then in heels, focusing on rolling silently.
Record audio as well as video.

## Verify
- [ ] No audible stomp on a hard floor at show pace.
- [ ] Knees never lock at contact (side video).
- [ ] No toe-drag in heels over a taped seam.
