# Crossover walk and single-line track

**Goal:** the womenswear foot placement that narrows the silhouette and adds hip
movement, at the right amount for the brief.

## The single line
- Imagine a line down the centre of the runway (tape one at home). Each foot lands with
  the inner edge of the heel on or touching the line, toes pointing straight ahead or
  very slightly out.
- The result: the legs pass close together, the pelvis rotates more, and the garment swings.

## The crossover
- Each foot lands **just over** the line (the heel lands on the far side of it). This
  adds a stronger hip sway and a more dramatic, editorial look.
- Keep it to about half a foot-width past the line. More than that and the knees knock
  together, ankles roll in heels, and long hems wrap round the shins.

## When to use which
| Situation | Placement |
|---|---|
| Most womenswear shows | Single line |
| Strong editorial brief, short or fitted skirts | Light crossover |
| Long gowns, narrow skirts, wide-leg trousers | Single line or slightly parallel (crossover catches fabric) |
| Very high or unstable shoes | Single line; never cross |
| Commercial and bridal | Single line, softer |

## Drill
Tape a 10 m line. Walk 5 lengths on the line, 5 crossing, filming from the front. Check
the head stays still and the knees do not collide.

## Verify
- [ ] Feet land consistently (check footprints with chalk or a damp sole).
- [ ] No knee knock or ankle roll at full show speed in show shoes.
- [ ] Can switch between line and crossover on request.
