# Stride length control

**Goal:** the same step length every step, adjusted for the look, so you arrive on the
mark without a stutter step.

## Set it
- Start from your natural stride in flats. In heels it is shorter; in a narrow skirt it is
  limited by the hem; in wide trousers or a long coat it can open up.
- Over-striding causes head bob, heel slips and a lean back. Under-striding (shuffle) kills
  the garment's movement. The right length is the longest you can take with the head still.

## Garment limits
| Garment | Adjustment |
|---|---|
| Pencil or hobble skirt | Short steps, knees close; let the hips do more |
| Long gown | Medium steps; kick the hem slightly forward |
| Wide-leg trousers, maxi coats | Fuller stride to make the fabric move |
| Shorts, mini | Natural; watch the crossover |

Test every look in rehearsal or the fitting: walk five steps in it before the show.

## Counting to the mark
In rehearsal count the steps from the top of the runway to the end mark at show pace.
Note it per look (gowns may take more steps). If you are going to miss the mark, adjust
over the last several steps by a small amount each, never one big or small step at the end.

## Verify
- [ ] Same step count to the mark on three rehearsal runs.
- [ ] No stutter step before the pause.
- [ ] Stride tested in every look and shoe.
