# Arm carriage and shoulders

**Goal:** arms that frame the clothes and never pull the eye away from them.

## Shoulders
- Down and slightly back, as if sliding the shoulder blades into back pockets. Not
  pinned (that lifts the chest and stiffens the walk).
- Level: one shoulder rising with each step usually means the bag or the arm on that
  side is tense.
- Menswear briefs often want a little more width and stillness through the shoulders.

## Arm swing options
| Brief | Arm action |
|---|---|
| Default | Natural swing from the shoulder, opposite arm to leg, arms close to the body, small range (hand travels roughly hip to just past the thigh) |
| Still / strong | Arms hang long and almost still beside the thigh; swing comes only from the body's motion |
| Commercial | Slightly fuller, relaxed swing; can include a hand on the hip at the pause |
| Hands occupied | Bag, pocket, holding a hem: the free arm swings or hangs still, matched to the rest of the cast |

Ask in rehearsal which one the show wants; casts are usually expected to match.

## Hands
Soft and relaxed: fingers together and slightly curved, thumb resting along the index
finger. No fists, no splayed or flat "paddle" hands. Hands in pockets only if the stylist
says so (it changes the garment's line).

## Fixing faults
- **Robot arms** (stiff, same side): walk slowly, let the arms go completely limp, then speed up.
- **Flapping / chicken wings**: elbows drifting out; brush the inner wrist past the hip.
- **Crossing arms in front of the body**: swing forward-back, not across.

## Verify
- [ ] Filmed front and side: arms swing in a straight line, opposite to legs, no elbow flare.
- [ ] Both "natural" and "still" versions available on request.
- [ ] Hands soft in every frame of a pit-shot burst.
