# Posing: angles, hands and working to direction

**Goal:** give the photographer a steady stream of usable, varied frames that show the
garment and match the brief, while responding instantly to direction. Posing is
small, controlled changes — not big shapes held for a long time.

## 1. Know the brief type
- **E-commerce:** the garment is the product. Neutral face, consistent poses brand to
  brand (front, three-quarter, back, detail), nothing hiding the clothes. Speed matters:
  many looks per day.
- **Commercial / catalogue / advertising:** approachable, warm, often smiling; the person
  sells a feeling of "I want that life".
- **Editorial:** a story or mood; stronger shapes, unusual angles, expression can be
  intense or strange. Clothes may be styled rather than displayed.
Ask at the start: "Is this more product or more mood?"

## 2. Body
- **Angle to camera:** square-on reads wide and confrontational; turning the body ~30–45°
  away and the face back toward the lens slims and adds depth.
- **Weight shift:** put weight on the back leg; relax the front knee. This creates the
  S-curve (contrapposto) and avoids a stiff, soldier stance.
- **Create negative space:** gaps between arm and torso, between legs, show the
  silhouette of the garment. Arms pressed flat against the body widen it.
- **Elongate:** neck long, push the face slightly forward and down to define the jaw
  (it looks odd in the mirror and good on camera).
- **Show the garment:** a pocket detail, a sleeve, the drape of a skirt — pose to it
  (e.g. turn to show a back detail, lift a hem, put a hand in a pocket for trousers).

## 3. Hands
Hands give poses away. Rules of thumb:
- Show the side or edge of the hand, not the flat palm or back of the hand to camera.
- Soft fingers with small gaps; no claws, no fists unless asked.
- Give hands a job: touching a collar, holding a strap, in a pocket (thumb out often
  reads better), resting lightly — not gripping.
- Keep hands away from blocking the product (logos, closures) on commercial work.

## 4. Face and eyes
- Expression comes from the eyes; practise intensity without widening them
  ("smiling with the eyes" without moving the mouth).
- Relax the mouth — lips slightly parted reads softer than pressed.
- Small changes between frames: chin up/down a few degrees, eyes to lens then just off it.
- Blink between shots, not during — learn the photographer's rhythm.

## 5. Find the light
Feel where the key light is (warmth on the face, the brightest point). Keep the face
turned so the light falls across it as the photographer set it; if your nose shadow
crosses your cheek badly or eyes go dark, turn slightly toward the light. Ask if unsure.

## 6. Working to direction
- Move **one thing at a time** between frames: head, then hands, then weight — every
  click a new but related frame.
- When given direction ("chin down, more energy"), change only what was asked; don't
  reset the whole pose.
- Mirror direction correctly: "your left" vs "camera left" — confirm once.
- Check in occasionally ("Is this working?") and look at a few frames on tether if
  offered — it calibrates you fast.

## Practice drill
In a mirror or on a phone camera: 20 frames from one standing position, changing one
element each frame. Review for repeated poses, hidden hands, and stiff knees.

## Verify
- [ ] You can state the brief type and adapt the energy to it.
- [ ] Weight on one leg; body angled; negative space where the garment needs it.
- [ ] Hands soft, side-on, with a purpose; product not blocked.
- [ ] Face finds the key light; expression changes in small steps.
- [ ] Directions are applied precisely without resetting the pose.
