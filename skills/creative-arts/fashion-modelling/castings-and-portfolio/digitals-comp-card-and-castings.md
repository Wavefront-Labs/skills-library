# Digitals, comp card and castings

**Goal:** make it easy for an agent or client to say yes: accurate current pictures,
a short, strong book, and reliable behaviour at castings and on set.

## 1. Digitals (polaroids/"polas")
Agencies and clients want to see you as you are today.
- **Look:** clean face (at most light skincare and brow grooming), hair natural and
  away from the face for some frames, no heavy styling.
- **Clothes:** fitted plain basics — black or neutral fitted top and jeans or leggings;
  swimwear or fitted underwear if the agency requests it (only for legitimate agencies
  and never with pressure).
- **Shots:** full length front, side and back; three-quarter length; head-and-shoulders
  front and both profiles; one smiling, one neutral. Hands at sides, relaxed stance.
- **Light and background:** soft daylight from a window facing you, plain light wall,
  camera at about chest height for full length (lower angles distort height). Phone
  quality is often acceptable; do not filter or retouch.
- Update every few months or after any visible change (hair cut or colour, weight,
  tattoos).

## 2. Comp card
Front: your strongest image (usually a clear headshot or beauty shot) and your name.
Back: 3–4 images showing range (full length, editorial, commercial), plus measurements
(height, bust/chest, waist, hips, shoe size, hair and eye colour) and agency contact.
Measurements must be honest — clients cast and dress you from them, and a mismatch on
the day costs everyone.

## 3. Portfolio ("book")
- **Edit hard:** typically something like 10–20 images in a digital book; fewer strong
  images beat many average ones. Your agent normally has final say.
- **Sequence:** open and close with your strongest; alternate close-up and full length;
  show range (editorial, commercial, beauty, movement) without repeating the same
  pose or look.
- **Tear sheets** (published work) and campaign images go in as soon as you have them.
- **Test shoots** build a new book: collaborations with photographers. Agree usage and
  credits in writing first, even for unpaid (TFP) tests.

## 4. Castings and go-sees
- Bring: phone with digital book, comp cards, the right shoes (heels for womenswear
  runway castings are common), plain outfit, ID if required.
- Be punctual, polite and brief. Say your name and agency, hand over the card/book, do
  what's asked (walk, pose, digitals, try on a look), thank them, leave.
- You will hear "no" far more than "yes"; castings are about fit to a specific brief,
  not a verdict on you.
- **Self-tapes:** follow the brief exactly (slate with name, height, agency; the asked
  angles; a walk), shoot horizontal or vertical as requested, in good light, quiet room.

## 5. On-set etiquette
Arrive on time with what you were told to bring; confirm call time the night before.
Phone away while working. Respect the clothes (no food, deodorant/make-up marks, sit
carefully). Ask before leaving set. Raise concerns about nudity, safety or changes to the
agreed job with your agent, not by quietly going along.

## Scam awareness
Legitimate agencies earn commission from paid work. Be wary of anyone who requires
large up-front fees for "portfolio packages", courses or "registration" as a condition of
representation; check the agency's existing models and reputation. Never meet unknown
"photographers" alone at private locations; bring someone or confirm via the agency.
(Licensing and regulation of agencies differs by country — check your local rules.)

## Verify
- [ ] Current, unfiltered digitals covering all standard angles.
- [ ] Comp card with honest, current measurements and agency contact.
- [ ] Book edited to strong images with range, best first and last.
- [ ] Casting kit packed; self-tape brief followed to the letter.
- [ ] No up-front fees paid to "agencies"; unknown shoots verified.
