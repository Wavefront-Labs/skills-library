# Employment status and misclassification

**Context:** many models are treated as self-employed or independent contractors, but the right label depends on law and facts, not on what a contract says. The label affects pay, rights (such as minimum wage or holiday), tax and benefits.

## Why it matters
- **Employees and some workers** may have rights to minimum pay, holiday, limits on hours, and protection from discrimination and dismissal, depending on the country.
- **Independent contractors** usually have fewer statutory protections and handle their own tax (see the model-tax skill).
- **Misclassification** happens when someone is treated as a contractor but the relationship looks like employment in law.

## Factors courts and agencies often consider (varies by place)
- How much **control** the agency or client has over how and when you work.
- Whether you can **refuse jobs** or send a substitute.
- Whether you are **in business on your own account.**
- The **degree of financial risk** you carry.

## What to do
- **Read your contract** and compare it with how work actually happens.
- **Ask your agent** how you are classified and why.
- **Get advice** from a union (such as Equity in the UK), a legal-aid or advocacy service, or a lawyer. Specific laws such as New York's Fashion Workers Act affect how some models are treated; check current law.
