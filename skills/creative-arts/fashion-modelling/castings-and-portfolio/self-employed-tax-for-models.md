# Self employed tax for models

**Warning:** tax rules differ by country and change. This is general information. Ask a qualified accountant or tax adviser, especially if you work in more than one country.

## Common principles
- **Many models are self-employed or independent contractors** and must report their own income and pay tax. Some jurisdictions treat some model work as employment; see the worker-status skill.
- **In the UK,** self-employed people generally report through Self Assessment (HMRC). **In the US,** independent contractors usually receive forms such as the 1099-NEC and pay income and self-employment tax, often through quarterly estimated payments (IRS). Check current rules and deadlines.
- **Keep records:** statements, invoices, receipts, mileage and dates of work.
- **Expenses:** some professional costs may be deductible (for example travel, agency-approved costs, portfolio, work-related clothing and grooming where allowed), but rules are specific; ask an accountant.
- **Working abroad:** you may owe tax in more than one country and may be covered by a tax treaty.
- **Set money aside** from each payment for tax.

## Habit
Use a spreadsheet or app to track income and costs from your first job, and consult a professional early.
