# Eating disorder awareness

**Important:** if you or someone you know may have an eating disorder, contact a doctor or a qualified mental-health professional. In an emergency, use local emergency services.

## Why the risk is higher
- Appearance-based work, measurements and size pressure, rejection, irregular schedules and comparison all increase risk.

## Warning signs (not diagnostic)
- Preoccupation with weight, food or body shape; restricting, bingeing or purging; excessive exercise; avoiding meals; mood changes, tiredness or dizziness; withdrawal; denial of problems.

## What to do
- **Speak to a doctor** or an eating-disorder specialist, and consider a trusted friend or family member.
- **Tell your agent** if you are able; welfare guidance such as the CFDA Health Initiative, BFC guidance and the LVMH-Kering charter emphasises health, education and access to support.
- **If you see signs in someone else,** speak privately and kindly, avoid commenting on appearance, and encourage professional help.
- **Say no** to pressure to lose weight or change your body in unhealthy ways, and see the size-policy and healthy-stability skills.
