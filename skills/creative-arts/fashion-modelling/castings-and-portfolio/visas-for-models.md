# Visas for models

**Warning:** immigration rules differ by country and change often. Check the official government site of the country you will work in, and ask your agent and an immigration adviser before you travel.

## Key points
- **Do not work on a tourist or visitor visa** unless the rules explicitly allow your activity. Working without permission can lead to refusal, deportation and bans.
- **Common routes** include sponsored work or creative-worker categories. In the US, for example, fashion models of distinguished merit and ability have been eligible for H-1B status, and some models use the O-1 category for individuals of extraordinary ability; confirm current categories and criteria.
- **Who applies:** the agency or client often sponsors, and may recoup costs from earnings (see the advances skill).
- **Documents:** passport validity, contracts or invitation letters, portfolio and evidence of work, insurance and funds.
- **Timelines:** applications can take weeks or months; plan well ahead.
- **Short visits:** some places allow limited paid engagements or have specific rules; check the exact conditions.

## Practice
- **Ask the agency** to confirm in writing what visa you need and who pays.
- **Keep copies** of all documents.
- **Never share your passport** with anyone other than official processes, and do not let anyone hold it.
