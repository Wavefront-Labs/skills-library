# Stage combat safety framework

**Goal:** make violence on stage look real while every move is choreographed,
rehearsed and repeatable, so nobody is hurt in the 1st or the 200th show. This is
a production-safety framework — *how* fights are set, rehearsed and maintained —
not a technique manual. Specific moves must be taught in person by a qualified
fight director or instructor.

## 1. Who is responsible
- **Fight director** (fight choreographer): sets every move, decides what is
  safe for these performers in this space, and signs off the fight. Look for a
  recognised certification body in your country (e.g. national stage-combat
  societies); qualification schemes vary, so check what the venue's insurer
  accepts. Unconfirmed: whether a given jurisdiction mandates certification.
- **Fight captain:** a company member who runs fight calls, watches for drift
  and reports to the fight director and stage manager.
- **Stage manager:** schedules fight time, keeps the fight notation, logs
  incidents.
- **Actors:** perform only what is set, report any change or discomfort, never
  improvise in a fight.
The director decides the story of the fight; the fight director decides how it is
made safe. If they conflict, safety wins.

## 2. Core principles
- **The victim controls.** In most illusions the person "receiving" a move leads
  it (e.g. controls a hair pull or a push), so force is never actually applied.
- **Distance.** Strikes aimed at the body or head are thrown **out of distance**
  — they miss by a set margin — and the audience angle hides the gap. Moves
  that make contact are deliberately soft, to large muscle groups, never the head,
  neck, spine, joints or face. The fight director sets exactly which moves
  touch and how.
- **Eye contact before every move.** No eye contact, no move. It confirms both
  partners are ready and in place.
- **Pace.** Learn at slow motion, then increase only as the fight director allows.
  Performance pace is usually slower than it looks; the sound and reaction sell
  the speed.
- **Sound is separate.** The impact sound ("knap") is made by a body part or
  off-stage, not by the hit.
- **Reaction sells the hit.** The receiver's reaction, timed to the knap, is
  what the audience believes.

## 3. Weapons safety
- Use only **stage-combat-rated** weapons (blunted, rebated blades with
  correct tangs; no decorative or "wall-hanger" swords). Inspect before every
  use: blade nicks/burrs, loose hilts, cracks.
- A named person controls weapons: they are signed out and in, stored locked,
  and never handled off-stage for play.
- **Firearms** (even blank-firing or replica) need a qualified armourer and
  follow local law and venue policy; never point at anyone, even unloaded.
  Many productions avoid blanks entirely. Treat this as a specialist area.
- Never practise weapon moves without the fight director's sign-off.

## 4. Rehearsal process
1. **Assess** performers (experience, injuries, flexibility) and the space
   (floor, set, sightlines, costume).
2. **Teach** components slowly, out of context.
3. **Set** the sequence and **notate** it (stage management keeps a copy).
4. **Build speed** gradually over sessions.
5. **Integrate** with acting, then with set, costume, lighting and sound — each
   change can alter safety (skirts, heels, haze, darkness, raked stage). Re-check at tech.
6. **Fight call** before *every* performance: run each fight at half speed then
   performance speed, on stage, in costume where relevant.

## 5. Falls, lifts and intimacy
Falls and lifts follow the same framework: taught by a qualified instructor,
built slowly, with mats until set. Scenes with intimacy use an intimacy
coordinator — same consent, choreography and notation principles.

## Gotchas
- **Drift**: fights creep faster and closer over a run. The fight captain must
  call it out; the fight director may need to return.
- **Adrenaline** in performance makes everyone faster and stronger than in rehearsal.
- **Ad-libbed additions** ("I added a shove tonight") are never acceptable.
- Injury, illness or an understudy going on means the fight is re-run before the
  show, at minimum.
- Anything that goes wrong in a show: stop if needed, report, log, and fix before
  the next show.

## Verify
- [ ] A qualified fight director set and signed off every fight.
- [ ] Each fight is notated and held by stage management.
- [ ] Every move has eye contact; no contact lands on head, neck, spine or joints.
- [ ] Weapons are combat-rated, inspected and logged out/in.
- [ ] A fight call happens before every show; incidents are logged.
