# Distance and targeting in stage combat

These principles underpin every unarmed and armed technique taught in British
Academy of Stage and Screen Combat (BASSC) and Society of American Fight Directors
(SAFD) syllabi. Learn them from a certified teacher; this is a reference.

## Distance (measure)
- **Out of distance**: attacks are aimed so that, if the defender did nothing,
  they would miss. For unarmed strikes, typically the fist passes several inches
  in front of the target; for blades, the point stops short of the body.
- **Measure** is the distance between partners; it's set in rehearsal and
  re-established by footwork before each attack (adjusting with small steps).
- **Close distance** techniques (grabs, chokes, hair pulls) are designed so the
  **victim controls** the force (see
  `creative-arts.theatre.stage-movement-and-combat.hair-pulls-and-stage-chokes`).

## Targeting
- Attacks travel to **specific targets that are off the body**: e.g. a punch
  aimed past the chin line, a sword cut aimed to a point beside the head.
  Agreed targets let the defender's reaction match.
- Common target terms in sword syllabi: head, flank, hip, shoulder;
  thrusts to belly/chest are delivered off-line or out of distance.

## Eye contact and cueing
- Partners make **eye contact before each sequence** (and often each move) —
  "check in".
- **Preparation/cue**: the attacker shows the attack (the "prep") clearly
  before delivery so the partner can respond; speed comes later.
- **Action-reaction**: the defender reacts on the attacker's cue; the attacker
  waits for the partner's readiness.

## Masking and sightlines
- Non-contact strikes are hidden by the angle to the audience (the
  **upstage/downstage** relationship or body masking). Check from the house.
- Knaps (the sound) are made out of sight (see the stage combat safety
  framework).

## Speed and intensity
Learn at **slow motion**, then half speed, then performance speed — usually
60-75% of real speed. Speed never exceeds the point where both can stay safe.

## Gotchas
- Distance shrinking over a run as actors relax — re-check at fight calls.
- Raked stages or new set pieces altering footing and measure.
- Eye contact lost under bright light or masks — adapt cues.

## Verify
- [ ] Every attack has an off-body target and set measure.
- [ ] Eye contact/cue points marked in the fight notation.
- [ ] Sightlines checked from multiple seats.
