# Alexander Technique for actors

F.M. Alexander (an Australian actor who lost his voice, c. 1890s) developed a method
of noticing and stopping habitual tension. Taught in most UK and US drama schools,
usually by certified teachers (STAT in the UK, AmSAT in the US) through hands-on
lessons. Feldenkrais Method (Moshe Feldenkrais) is a related somatic practice.

## Core concepts
- **Primary control**: the relationship of head, neck and back — when the neck
  is free and the head poised, the whole body organises better.
- **End-gaining**: rushing for the result (e.g. "projecting") and tensing to
  get it.
- **Inhibition**: pausing the habitual response before acting.
- **Direction**: thinking (not doing) instructions: "let the neck be free, to
  let the head go forward and up, to let the back lengthen and widen".
- **Use affects functioning**: how you use yourself shapes voice, breath and
  movement.

## Self-practice
1. **Semi-supine / constructive rest** (10-20 min daily): lie on your back,
   knees bent, feet flat, head on a few paperback books (height so the neck is
   neither tipped back nor squashed). Give directions; notice and release.
2. **Chair work**: sitting down and standing up while inhibiting the habit of
   pulling the head back, allowing the head to lead and the back to lengthen.
3. **Monkey (position of mechanical advantage)**: knees bent, torso inclined
   forward with a long back — useful for lifting and grounding.
4. **Whispered "ah"**: Alexander's breath and voice procedure — lips and jaw
   free, tongue resting, exhale on a whispered "ah".
5. **Applying to performance**: notice habitual reactions under stress
   (shoulders up, jaw clench); inhibit and redirect before entrances.

## Feldenkrais (Awareness Through Movement)
Slow, small, exploratory movement sequences (often lying down) to find easier
patterns. Useful for injury recovery and refining movement range.

## Gotchas
- Treating it as "good posture" (holding up straight) — that's more tension.
- Learning solely from books; hands-on lessons are central.
- Expecting instant change; it's a gradual re-education.
