# Rapier and dagger (and single sword) for the stage

Rapier and dagger (Elizabethan/Jacobean period: *Romeo and Juliet*, *Hamlet*) is a
core SAFD and BASSC weapon form; single sword (rapier alone) and broadsword
(medieval, heavier, two-handed or with shield) are separate tests. Train with a
certified teacher.

## Weapons
- **Stage combat steel** from specialist makers: blunt edges, rounded/buttoned
  tips, tempered blades designed for edge-to-edge contact. Never use
  decorative/wall-hanger swords — they snap.
- Check blades (bends, burrs, loose tangs/pommels) before every rehearsal and
  show; file burrs; tighten pommels; retire cracked blades.

## Fundamentals
- **En garde**: balanced stance, weapon point toward partner's face/upper
  body area but out of distance.
- **Footwork**: advance, retreat, lunge, pass — the main tool for keeping
  measure.
- **Parries** — commonly numbered 1-8 (fencing-derived, e.g. 1 prime, 4 quarte,
  6 sixte) protecting areas of the body; stage parries are made with the
  **forte** (strong part near the hilt) meeting the attacker's **foible**
  (weak, tip end), edge to edge, at 90°.
- **Cuts**: to head, shoulders, flanks, hips; aimed at a target point beside
  or above the body, out of distance. **Thrusts**: off-line (beside the body) or
  out of distance; the defender parries anyway.
- **Dagger**: used to parry and to attack at close range; in the off hand.

## Choreography practice
1. Learn phrase by phrase, slow motion, with **eye contact** before each phrase.
2. Attacker **shows the preparation** (cue), defender parries before the cut
   arrives at the target, at a pace that allows reaction.
3. Notate (e.g. "A: cut 2 to flank; B: parry 2; A: pass, thrust off-line to belly...").
4. Build speed gradually to performance speed (still well below real speed).

## Broadsword note
Heavier weapons use larger movements, more body involvement, and different
parry geometry (hanging guards); shields add blocks.

## Gotchas
- Flat-of-blade contact (bends blades) instead of edge.
- Parrying with the foible.
- Speeding up in performance adrenaline — fight captain watches for it.

## Verify
- [ ] Blades inspected and logged before each show.
- [ ] Each phrase notated with target, parry and cue.
- [ ] Performance speed agreed and maintained.
