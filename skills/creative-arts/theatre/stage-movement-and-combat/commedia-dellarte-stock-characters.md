# Commedia dell'arte stock characters

Italian professional theatre (c. 16th-18th centuries): improvised performances from a
**scenario** (plot outline) using stock characters, half masks and set comic
routines (**lazzi**). Taught in movement curricula (Lecoq, many drama schools)
for physical precision and status play.

## Main characters (common features)
- **Zanni** (servants) — low status, hungry, simple, quick. Low centre, bent
  knees, big shifts of weight.
- **Arlecchino (Harlequin)** — acrobatic servant, patched costume, playful,
  hungry, quick-thinking yet foolish; light, bouncing, animal-like (cat/monkey)
  movement; black half mask.
- **Brighella** — scheming servant/innkeeper, cunning and opportunistic;
  smoother, feline, sly.
- **Pantalone** — old Venetian merchant, miserly, lecherous; hooked nose
  mask, stooped back, chest forward, knees bent, quick small steps, hands
  guarding his purse.
- **Il Dottore** — pompous Bolognese pedant who talks endless nonsense Latin;
  round belly forward, short half mask or painted face, leaning back.
- **Il Capitano** — boastful cowardly soldier; long-nosed mask, expansive
  chest, wide stance, big gestures that collapse in fear.
- **Innamorati** (the lovers) — no masks, elegant, self-absorbed, poetic,
  upright and romantic.
- **Colombina** — witty maidservant, often the cleverest; usually unmasked.

## Working method
1. **Mask etiquette** (see neutral mask skill): on/off facing away, face
   the audience frontally so the mask reads ("show the mask").
2. Find each character's **stance and walk** first; then gestures and voice.
3. **Status**: masters high-status but foolish; servants low-status but
   clever — comedy comes from reversals.
4. **Lazzi**: rehearse set routines (eating a fly, falling asleep standing,
   a ladder gag) to insert into scenes.
5. **Scenario**: a simple plot (lovers kept apart by Pantalone; servants
   scheme) improvised within character rules.
6. **Direct address** to the audience is normal.

## Gotchas
- Turning masks sideways/profile — masks die in profile; keep them
  frontal.
- Playing commedia as generic slapstick without character logic.
- Neglecting physical training — it's extremely demanding.

## Verify
- [ ] Each character's stance, walk and status defined.
- [ ] Masks read frontally.
- [ ] At least two lazzi rehearsed and placed in a scenario.
