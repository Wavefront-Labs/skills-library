# Partner lifts and weight-sharing for actors

## Principles
- **Lift with the legs**, back neutral; the **base** gets under the flyer's centre
  of gravity (around the pelvis) rather than lifting at arm's length.
- **The flyer helps**: jumps at the right moment, engages core, holds their own
  shape; most "lifting" is the flyer's jump plus timing.
- **Counterbalance** before lifting: partners lean away holding wrists, or
  back to back, sharing weight.
- **Communication**: counts ("1, 2, 3 — up" on an agreed beat), eye contact.
- **Spotters** for every new lift; spotters' job is to protect head and neck.

## Progression
1. **Weight sharing / counterbalance**: holding wrists, lean away and down to
   sitting and back up; back-to-back sitting and rising.
2. **Contact improvisation basics** (Steve Paxton, 1970s): a rolling point of
   contact between two bodies, giving and receiving weight, following momentum;
   start with small weight transfers and **surfing** on a partner's back when
   they're on all fours (table position).
3. **Simple lifts**:
   - **Hip lift / "sack of potatoes"**: flyer jumps, base catches at hips on
     shoulder, carefully.
   - **Front lift**: base's hands at flyer's hips, flyer jumps, base
     extends legs with arms locked near the body.
   - **Seated lift onto thigh**: base in lunge, flyer steps onto thigh near the
     hip crease (never on the knee), supported by hand-holds.
4. Add travelling, turns and descents only once stable.

## Safety
- Warm up thoroughly; no lifts with injuries.
- Clear space; mats for learning; costume checks (slippery fabrics, belts).
- Raked stages and lighting changes require re-rehearsal.
- Don't lift beyond what the pair can control in a show-tired state.

## Gotchas
- Lifting with a rounded back.
- Bases catching at the waist/ribs instead of hips.
- Partner changes (understudies) without re-teaching.

## Verify
- [ ] Every lift has a count, a spotter during learning, and a planned descent.
- [ ] Base under the flyer's centre of gravity.
- [ ] Rehearsed in costume on stage surface.
