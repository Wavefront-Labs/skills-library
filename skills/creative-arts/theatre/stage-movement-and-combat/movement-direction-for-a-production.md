# Movement direction for a production

The movement director shapes how bodies move across a whole production — distinct
from a choreographer setting dances or a fight director setting fights, though
roles overlap.

## Pre-rehearsal
1. Meet the director: the physical world (period, social codes, stylisation
   level), key physical sequences (dreams, transitions, crowd moments),
   characters' physical lives.
2. Read the design: costume restrictions (corsets, heels, masks), set
   (stairs, rake, revolve), space.
3. Research references: period movement and etiquette, footage, paintings,
   animal or element imagery.

## In rehearsal
- **Company movement warm-ups** (15-30 min daily) that build the show's
  vocabulary and ensemble: body awareness, rhythm, partnering.
- **Character physicality** sessions with individual actors (centres, tempo,
  efforts — see `creative-arts.theatre.stage-movement-and-combat.laban-efforts-for-character`).
- **Sequences**: stage non-naturalistic moments (slow motion, flashbacks,
  transformations), transitions (with the director and SM shift plot), crowd
  movement.
- **Period movement**: bowing, curtseys, fans, sitting in period costume,
  social distance; teach in rehearsal skirts/shoes.
- Record sequences on video and in writing (counts, cues) for the SM/book.

## Tech and the run
- Adjust sequences to set, light and costume realities.
- Leave a **movement captain** and documented warm-up for the run.
- Return visits for maintenance; brush up understudies.

## Gotchas
- Working without costume rehearsal items — sequences fail in tech.
- Overlap disputes with fight/intimacy directors — define who owns which moment.
- Movement style inconsistent across scenes.

## Verify
- [ ] Physical world described in a short document agreed with director.
- [ ] All sequences recorded with counts/cues.
- [ ] Movement captain and maintenance plan in place.
