# Suzuki method training

Developed by Tadashi Suzuki (Suzuki Company of Toga; *The Way of Acting*, 1986) and
taught in the US by SITI Company alongside Viewpoints. It trains the "grammar of
the feet": the actor's connection to the floor, centre, breath and energy
under physical extremity.

## Core exercises (as commonly taught)
1. **Stomping**: rhythmic stomping to music, knees bent, the upper body still
   and centred, then stillness (collapse to the floor, then rise slowly).
   Builds lower-body energy and centre.
2. **Walks**: a series of stylised walks — e.g. walking on the outsides of the
   feet, on the insides, on tiptoes, knees bent low ("sliding"), and others —
   with an upright, still upper body.
3. **Slow-tenteketen**: walking extremely slowly across the space with
   continuous movement and a quiet upper body, energy projected forward.
4. **Statues**: on a music cue, freeze in a strong shape; hold with internal
   energy; can add text.
5. **Shakuhachi** and seated exercises: holding positions with breath control.
6. **Text under load**: speaking a speech while in a demanding position or
   moving, maintaining vocal support and stillness.

## Principles
- Upper body quiet, lower body active; energy "rooted" through the feet.
- The **centre** (lower abdomen/pelvis) controls movement.
- Stillness is active, not resting.
- Discipline: precise starts/stops on music cues; ensemble unison.

## Safety
- Proper sprung or wooden floor; avoid concrete. Bare feet or split-soles.
- Build up stomping gradually — shin and knee strain is common.
- Warm up; stop with pain in knees/hips.

## Gotchas
- Stomping with locked knees — joint injury.
- Copying external form without the internal energy.
- Running full sessions with untrained groups — progress over weeks.

## Verify
- [ ] Floor suitable; warm-up done.
- [ ] Upper body remains still in stomping and walks.
- [ ] Text can be spoken clearly under physical load.
