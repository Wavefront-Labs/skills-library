# Cutting Shakespeare

## 1. Base text
Choose an edition (Folger, Arden, RSC, Oxford, New Cambridge) and note where
Folio and Quarto differ (e.g. *Hamlet*, *King Lear*); conflation choices matter.
Folger Digital Texts give line numbers useful for cut lists.

## 2. Running-time target
- Rule of thumb: about **900-1,000 lines per hour** of performance for
  Shakespeare spoken at a brisk modern pace (it varies with style; time a
  read-through). Uncut *Hamlet* (~3,900-4,000 lines in conflated texts) runs
  ~4 hours.
- Target: e.g. 2h30 including an interval of 20 min → ~2h10 of text → roughly
  2,100-2,300 lines. Add time for songs, fights and transitions.

## 3. Cut by priority
1. **Concept and cast**: cut characters you can't cast or that the concept
   doesn't need (doubling plan constraints).
2. **Repetition**: messengers recapping events we've seen; double reports.
3. **Topical jokes and obscure references** that need a footnote.
4. **Long similes and rhetorical extensions** — keep the first image, cut
   elaborations.
5. Sub-plots peripheral to your production's story.
Never cut the information the plot depends on (who knows what, when).

## 4. Repairing verse
- When cutting within a speech, try to cut in **whole lines** or from a
  mid-line caesura to another mid-line break so the iambic pentameter stays
  intact. A cut that leaves a 7-syllable line will be audible.
- Check shared lines (two speakers completing one pentameter line); cuts
  can break the cue rhythm.
- Keep rhyming couplets that end scenes; they signal exits.

## 5. Document
Cut list by act/scene/line numbers, plus a clean rehearsal script. Leave cuts
"open" in week 1; actors may plead for lines. Freeze cuts by end of week 2.

## Gotchas
- Cutting soliloquies first — usually the audience's lifeline into character.
- Losing exposition and confusing the plot.
- Inconsistent edition between actors' scripts.

## Verify
- [ ] Edition and line counts recorded; target line count calculated.
- [ ] No broken verse lines left unintentionally.
- [ ] Read-through timed within 5% of target.
