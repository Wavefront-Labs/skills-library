# Adapting a novel for the stage

## 0. Rights
If the novel is in copyright, secure **dramatic adaptation rights** from the
author/estate or publisher (usually via their agent) *before* writing:
option period, territory, media (stage only), approvals, royalties. UK/EU term:
life of author + 70 years; US: works published 1929 or earlier are public
domain as of 2025 (rolling forward each year), later works vary. Translations
of public-domain novels can still be in copyright.

## 1. Find the spine
- Reread the novel and write: what is it *about* (one sentence), whose story
  is it, what is the central dramatic question?
- List the **events** — things that happen in scene, not description.
- Identify what you must keep for fans and what you can lose.

## 2. Choose the narrative device
- **Narrator** (a character remembering, the author, a chorus).
- **Direct dramatisation** (no narration, everything in scene).
- **Ensemble storytelling** (actors narrate and play multiple roles, e.g.
  Shared Experience, Kneehigh, *Nicholas Nickleby* (RSC, 1980)).
- **Framing device** (a story told within a later situation).
The device decides how interiority and time jumps reach the audience.

## 3. Compress
- Merge minor characters; cut subplots that don't serve the spine.
- Compress time: one scene can carry several of the novel's chapters.
- Decide the cast size early (doubling plan) — it shapes everything.

## 4. Dramatise interiority
- Turn thoughts into actions and confrontations; give characters someone to
  tell.
- Use direct address sparingly and purposefully.
- Visual/physical theatre for what words can't do (Lecoq-style ensemble images).

## 5. Develop
Workshop readings (see
`creative-arts.theatre.dramaturgy.running-a-new-play-development-workshop`),
then revise. Get estate approval if required by the contract.

## Gotchas
- Including every favourite scene → 4-hour first drafts.
- Narration that describes what we can see.
- Starting before rights are clear.

## Verify
- [ ] Rights secured in writing (or public domain confirmed).
- [ ] One-sentence spine and event list.
- [ ] Narrative device chosen; cast size fixed.
