# Analysing a play with Aristotle and Freytag

## Aristotle, Poetics (c. 335 BCE)
Six elements of tragedy, in his order of importance:
1. **Plot (mythos)** — the arrangement of incidents; must be a whole with
   beginning, middle and end, causally connected ("one thing because of
   another", not "after another").
2. **Character (ethos)** — moral choices revealed through action.
3. **Thought (dianoia)** — arguments and ideas expressed.
4. **Diction (lexis)** — the language.
5. **Song (melos)** — music/chorus.
6. **Spectacle (opsis)** — the visual.
Key plot terms: **peripeteia** (reversal), **anagnorisis** (recognition),
**pathos** (suffering), **hamartia** (error), **catharsis**.
The "three unities" (action, time, place) are mostly a later Renaissance/
neoclassical codification; Aristotle insisted on unity of action and noted
tragedy tends to a single day.

**Use**: list the causal chain of incidents; mark reversal and recognition;
check which element the play prioritises (Chekhov often puts thought and
atmosphere over plot).

## Freytag's pyramid (Technique of the Drama, 1863)
Five parts (based on five-act drama):
1. **Exposition** (with the "exciting force"/inciting moment at its end).
2. **Rising action** — complications.
3. **Climax** — the turning point (Freytag placed it mid-play, often act 3).
4. **Falling action** — with a "moment of final suspense".
5. **Catastrophe/denouement**.
**Use**: map scenes onto the shape; identify inciting incident and climax; note
where modern plays deviate (climax late, open endings).

## Well-made play (pièce bien faite; Scribe, Sardou; Ibsen adapted it)
Mechanics: a secret known to audience but not some characters; exposition
through servants or confidants; escalating complications driven by letters,
misplaced objects, entrances; a **scène à faire** (obligatory confrontation);
a logical resolution. **Use**: identify the secret and the obligatory scene;
check the play's plants and payoffs.

## Output
A one-page structure map: incident chain, inciting incident, reversal(s),
recognition, climax, resolution, with scene/page numbers.

## Gotchas
- Forcing episodic or non-Western plays into the pyramid — see
  `creative-arts.theatre.dramaturgy.epic-and-episodic-structure`.
- Treating Aristotle as prescription rather than description.
