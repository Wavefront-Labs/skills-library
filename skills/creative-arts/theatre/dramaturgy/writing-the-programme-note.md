# Writing the programme note

## Brief first
Confirm with the producer/marketing: word count (commonly 500-1,000 per article),
deadline (often 3-4 weeks before first preview for print), image rights, who
approves (director, sometimes playwright or estate).

## Procedure
1. **One angle.** Don't summarise the plot. Choose a single entry point that
   enriches watching: the real event behind the play, the author's life at
   the time of writing, a key idea, why it matters now.
2. **Hook in the first sentence** — a story, a quotation, a striking fact.
3. **Accessible language** — written for a general audience reading in the
   interval or before curtain; avoid academic jargon and footnotes.
4. **No spoilers** — many read it before the show. Hint, don't reveal.
5. **Connect to this production** without explaining the director's concept
   in a way that tells audiences what to think.
6. **Check facts** and attribute quotations accurately. Cite sources lightly in
   the text ("as the critic Kenneth Tynan wrote in 1956...").
7. **Approval round** with the director; edit to length.
8. Supply a short **contributor bio** and image credits.

## Other audience texts
- Lobby displays: images and captions (50-100 words per panel).
- Online "about the play" pages and social posts reuse the note's angle.
- Glossaries for complex plays can go in the programme.

## Gotchas
- Writing about the dramaturg's research process instead of the play.
- Overlength: designers will cut it awkwardly.
- Contradicting the production's choices.

## Verify
- [ ] One clear angle, hooked opening, no plot summary.
- [ ] Within word count, facts checked, quotations attributed.
- [ ] Approved by director and producer.
