# Running a post-show talkback

## Set-up
- Agree with producer and company which performance (often a mid-week show),
  who's on the panel (actors, director, writer, an expert guest) and length
  (20-30 minutes).
- Announce before the show and at curtain call; give audiences a minute to
  leave or stay.
- Chairs onstage, a roving or handheld mic for the audience if the house is
  large; check accessibility (captioning, BSL if offered).
- Actors may stay in costume or change; give them 5 minutes.

## Facilitation
1. **Welcome and frame**: introduce panel, say how long, what the session is for
   (sharing responses, not giving notes).
2. **Open with a question to the audience** that anyone can answer: "What
   moment has stayed with you?" Liz Lerman's Critical Response Process starts
   with **statements of meaning** — what was meaningful, evocative, striking.
3. Have **2-3 seed questions** ready for the panel in case the room is quiet
   (process, research, surprising rehearsal discoveries).
4. **Turn-taking**: call on hands across the room; one question per person;
   repeat questions into the mic.
5. **Redirect** "notes" disguised as questions ("why didn't you...?") into
   neutral questions ("what led to that choice?"), or answer briefly yourself.
6. **Protect the artists**: actors aren't required to answer about personal
   experiences; shut down hostile comments politely and move on.
7. **Close on time** with thanks and any upcoming events.

## After
- Note recurring audience questions (they may reveal clarity issues to feed back
  to the director, discreetly).

## Gotchas
- Letting one audience member dominate.
- Spoilers in talkbacks before press night or in previews.
- Holding talkbacks after very emotionally demanding performances without
  checking with the cast.

## Verify
- [ ] Panel and length agreed; announced at curtain.
- [ ] Opening question and seed questions prepared.
- [ ] Ended on time; feedback themes recorded.
