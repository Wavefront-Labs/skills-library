# French-scene breakdown and pre-production paperwork

## French scenes
A **French scene** begins and ends with any entrance or exit — a new combination of
characters onstage. Breaking the play into French scenes lets the director
schedule only the actors needed.

## Building the breakdown
1. Read the play twice. Then go through and mark every entrance and exit.
2. Number French scenes within each act/scene (e.g. 1.1a, 1.1b...).
3. For each French scene record: pages (start-end), page count in eighths (as in
   film) or lines, characters present, setting, time of day, key props, special
   effects (sound, light, fights, songs).
4. Produce a **character-scene chart**: characters as rows, French scenes as
   columns, X where present. Totals show who's in most; gaps show doubling
   options and quick changes.
5. Share with the director (for scheduling), costume (for changes), and the
   production manager.

## Preliminary props list
From the breakdown, list every prop mentioned or implied:
| Prop | Act/Scene/Page | Character | Notes (consumable? practical? breakable?) | Source (build/buy/borrow/stock) |
Include set dressing and furniture separately. Update as rehearsals add/cut
props.

## Contact sheet
- Every company member: name, role/department, phone, email, (emergency
  contact kept separately and confidential).
- Respect data protection (GDPR in UK/EU): share only with consent and only as
  needed; mark "confidential"; destroy at the end of the production.

## Gotchas
- Missing silent characters or offstage voices in the chart.
- Not updating the breakdown after cuts.
- Circulating personal data too widely.

## Verify
- [ ] Every entrance/exit produces a new French scene.
- [ ] Character-scene chart shared with director and wardrobe.
- [ ] Props list includes page refs and notes.
- [ ] Contact sheet circulated with consent and marked confidential.
