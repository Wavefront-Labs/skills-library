# Prompting and giving line notes

## Prompting ("on book")
- Once the company is off book, the DSM/ASM follows the script and gives
  **prompts** when an actor calls "line" (or the agreed word).
- Give the prompt: the **first few words** of the line, clearly, in a neutral
  voice, loud enough to hear — don't perform it.
- If the actor needs more, give the next phrase.
- Don't prompt during deliberate pauses; agree with the director which pauses
  are intentional (mark them "P" in the book).
- In performance, prompting is rare; it's usually agreed in advance whether a
  prompt corner or the DSM gives lines.

## Line notes
Record every deviation from the script during runs, per actor.
**Common codes** (vary by company; agree and explain on the form):
- **D** dropped (missed) words/lines
- **A** added words
- **P** paraphrased / wrong words
- **T** transposed (words in wrong order)
- **CL** called for line
- **J** jumped cue / missed cue
- **W** wrong word

**Form**: actor name, page, the correct text with the error marked (strike out
added, underline dropped, write the paraphrase above). Spreadsheet or line-note
template; email after the run, or print and hand out.

## Process
1. Take notes from the first off-book run until the end of previews (on long-
   running shows, spot-check periodically).
2. Deliver promptly (same day).
3. Be neutral — line notes aren't criticism.
4. For new writing, check with the director/writer which deviations matter
   (playwright may accept some).
5. Track recurring errors; suggest a line run for those scenes.

## Gotchas
- Vague notes ("p.12 wrong") — actors can't fix them.
- Prompting actors who are acting a pause.
- Giving line notes publicly in the rehearsal room.

## Verify
- [ ] Agreed code key on the form.
- [ ] Every note includes page and correct text.
- [ ] Notes delivered within 24 hours of the run.
