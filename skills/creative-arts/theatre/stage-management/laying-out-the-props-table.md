# Laying out the props table

## Layout
1. **Location**: one table per side of stage (or more), near the entrances where
   props are collected, out of traffic and sightlines, with blue/backstage
   working light.
2. Cover with paper or fabric; **outline each prop** with tape or marker and
   **label** the outline (prop name, character, scene). An empty outline shows
   instantly that a prop is missing or in use.
3. **Group** by scene/order of use, left to right or by character.
4. Fragile, sharp or valuable items in boxes or a locked area; weapons are signed
   out by the armourer/ASM.
5. Separate area for consumables and food (see
   `creative-arts.theatre.stage-management.consumable-and-food-props`).
6. Return rule: actors return props to the outline after use, or hand them to
   the ASM — agree which.

## Preset check (every performance)
- A written **preset list**: every prop's preset location (onstage positions
  spiked, props tables, actors' personal props, quick-change areas).
- ASMs check against the list before the half (typically before the house
  opens), tick and sign; report anything missing to the DSM.
- Also check working condition: practical lamps light, phones ring, bottles
  filled to the line.
- Interval reset list for Act 2.

## Sourcing and tracking (rehearsal to run)
- **Props list** with status column: source (stock/borrow/buy/build), rehearsal
  substitute, ETA, cost, notes.
- Rehearsal props early — actors need weight and size.
- Borrowed items logged with lender details and return date; credit in the
  programme if agreed.
- In the run: note damage in the show report; maintain spares for breakable or
  much-handled props.

## Gotchas
- Moving the table between shows — everyone's muscle memory breaks.
- Actors taking props to dressing rooms.
- Unlabelled outlines — no one knows what's missing.

## Verify
- [ ] Every prop has a labelled outline or preset position.
- [ ] Preset checklist signed before every show.
- [ ] Props list status current, borrowed items logged.
