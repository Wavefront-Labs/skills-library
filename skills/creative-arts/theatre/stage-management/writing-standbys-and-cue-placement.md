# Writing standbys and cue placement

Companion to `creative-arts.theatre.stage-management.calling-the-show-from-the-prompt-book`.
This is about getting cues into the book accurately, usually during the lighting
plot (plotting session) and tech.

## Before the plotting session
- Clean calling script: one script page per sheet with wide margin, or script on
  the left and a blank cue page on the right. Pencil only.
- Columns or colours by department: e.g. LX red, SQ blue, Fly green, Auto purple,
  VQ orange (any scheme, consistent).
- Get the designers' provisional cue lists in advance.

## Placement
1. As the designer names a cue ("LX 23 on 'goodbye'"), draw a **line from the cue
   box in the margin to the exact word/syllable** in the text, or to a described
   action ("on door close", "as A sits").
2. Write in the cue box: department, number, and any note (time, "visual",
   "auto-follow from 22").
3. **Visual cues** (on action): write the action precisely and where you'll look.
4. **Musical cues**: mark bar numbers/beats from the MD's score; for long numbers
   keep a vocal score with cues marked.

## Standbys
- Place the standby **roughly half a page to a page before** (or 20-60 seconds of
  stage time; more for complex sequences or fly/automation cues where operators
  need longer).
- One standby covers a **sequence** of close cues: "Standby LX 23 through 27,
  Sound 14."
- Mark "warning" lines for very complex sequences earlier.

## Numbering
- Designers number; insertions use decimals (LX 23.5) or letters (SQ 14A). Never
  renumber mid-tech without the operators updating simultaneously.
- **Auto-follows** (cues triggered by the previous cue) are marked but not called.

## After each session
- Read back changed cues with each operator; reconcile your book with the desk
  cue list and the QLab list.
- Date the page revision.

## Shadow calling and handover
- A second caller (e.g. relief DSM) calls along silently or into a dead channel
  during several shows, then calls with the regular caller monitoring.
- Handover notes cover personal timing quirks of difficult cues.

## Gotchas
- Pencilling cues to the wrong line when the script has been cut — keep script
  revisions current.
- Ambiguous visual cues ("on the move") — specify which move.
- Standbys separated from GOs by a page turn without a reminder.

## Verify
- [ ] Every cue has a placement line, department, number and standby.
- [ ] Book matches each operator's cue list after each session.
- [ ] Relief caller has shadowed and called under supervision.
