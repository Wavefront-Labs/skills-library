# Hagen substitution and object exercises

Uta Hagen (*Respect for Acting*, 1973; *A Challenge for the Actor*, 1991; HB Studio).
**Substitution** (later "transference"): replace the fictional people, places and
events with specific elements from your own life so they matter to you, while
playing the character's actions.

## Substitution
1. Identify what in the scene must matter (the partner as your rival, a letter
   from your father, the house as home).
2. Find a personal equivalent — a person, place or object with a matching
   relationship (not identical events). Privately "see" the partner through it.
3. Endow details: give the fictional object/partner the specific properties of
   your substitution (how it smells, its history).
4. Test in rehearsal: does it produce the right need and behaviour? If not,
   change the substitution, not the action.
5. Over time the fictional reality takes over; the substitution may fall away.

## Object exercises (Hagen's sequence, A Challenge for the Actor)
Done as solo studies, typically 5-10 minutes, in a re-created real place:
1. **The basic object exercise** — "Who am I, where am I, what do I want" in a
   simple daily task (preparing to go out). Real objects; no audience
   acknowledgement.
2. **Destination** — you physically go somewhere in the room with a purpose;
   inner and outer destination.
3. **The fourth side** — creating the imaginary fourth wall with specific
   objects you look at.
4. **Endowment** — giving props qualities they don't have (cold coffee is hot;
   a stage bottle is heavy and full).
5. **Re-creating a physical sensation** (heat, cold, illness, tiredness).
6. **Three entrances** — what you were doing before, coming into the room.
7. **Talking to yourself / outdoors / conditioning forces / historical
   imagination** in later exercises.

## Gotchas
- Substitution can make actors play their own life rather than the character's
  situation; keep the character's actions.
- Never require actors to reveal substitutions.
- Endowment must be consistent with what the audience sees (don't sip a "hot"
  drink then gulp it).

## Verify
- [ ] Each important relationship/object has a private substitution.
- [ ] Object exercise can be performed without acknowledging observers.
- [ ] Endowed props behave consistently every run.
