# Choosing and preparing audition monologues

## Selection criteria
1. **From a published play** (not film, novel or self-written) unless the
   panel says otherwise. Read the whole play.
2. **Castable for you now**: within ~10 years of your playing age, a part you
   could be cast in. Panels judge you, not your range in stunt casting.
3. **Active**: the character needs something from someone *now* (persuade,
   confess, refuse), not a reminiscence or a story to nobody.
4. **Length**: usually **1.5-2 minutes**; never exceed the stated limit (many
   UK drama schools say 2 minutes; some US conservatory pre-screens say 1 minute
   per piece).
5. **A journey**: a change happens in the speech (a decision, a discovery).
6. **Not overdone** for that panel (each school's FAQ sometimes lists pieces to
   avoid); not shock material used for shock.
7. **Contrast** when two are asked: classical (often Shakespeare or
   Jacobean verse) + contemporary (often post-1980); different tone, status or
   tempo.

## Preparation
1. Script analysis: given circumstances, the partner, the objective, the event
   (see `creative-arts.theatre.acting.breaking-a-scene-into-units-and-beats`).
2. Action the speech (`...actioning-a-script-with-transitive-verbs`).
3. **Place the partner**: imaginary listener just above the panel's heads or to
   one side, never in a panel member's eyes unless invited.
4. Learn word-perfect early; rehearse in different rooms and with
   distractions.
5. Prepare a **simple staging**: start in neutral, a few motivated moves at
   most; a chair if the room has one.
6. **Introduction**: "My name is ___, this is ___ from *Play* by Author."
   Then a breath, then begin. Hold a beat at the end before stepping out of it.
7. **Redirect readiness**: rehearse it three ways (different objective, partner,
   circumstance) so you can adjust when asked.

## Gotchas
- Accents not native to you unless the panel asks — play it in your own voice.
- Comedy that relies on audience laughter in a silent room.
- Shouting/crying as the main event.
- Cutting a speech by merging lines from different scenes: some panels allow
  small cuts; say so if asked, never misrepresent.

## Verify
- [ ] Timed under the limit at performance pace.
- [ ] You can explain the play's plot and your character's situation in 30s.
- [ ] Two contrasting pieces ready, plus a spare.
- [ ] Three alternative readings rehearsed for redirects.
