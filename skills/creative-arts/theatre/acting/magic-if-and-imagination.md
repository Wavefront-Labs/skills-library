# The magic if and imagination

Stanislavski (*An Actor's Work*, ch. "Action, 'If', Given Circumstances"): the actor
cannot believe the fiction is real, but can sincerely answer **"What would I do
*if* I were in these circumstances?"**. The "if" is a lever to action, not to
emotion.

## Procedure
1. **Collect the given circumstances** from the text: who, where, when (year,
   season, time of day), what has just happened, what you want, relationships,
   and the production's choices (set, period, director's concept). Separate facts
   from inferences; keep a list.
2. **Ask the if as a question of action**: "If I had just found this letter in my
   husband's coat, and he is due home in five minutes, what would I *do*?"
   Answer with verbs: hide it, reread it, check the window.
3. **Make it specific.** Replace generalities with concrete detail. Not "a
   house" but *this* kitchen: the cracked tile by the sink, the clock that ticks
   loud. Stanislavski's "film of inner images" — you should be able to "see" what
   you talk about.
4. **Build the moment before.** Where have you come from, what did you just do,
   what do you want as you enter? Play the first seconds from that, not from a
   neutral start.
5. **Test with physical action**: do the simple tasks (pour, look, search) as if
   under the circumstances. If the action feels true, the circumstances are
   working.
6. **Update as rehearsal adds circumstances** (the director moves the period,
   the set adds a staircase) — re-ask the if.

## Exercises
- **Changing one circumstance**: play a simple action (setting a table) three
  times changing one fact (a guest is late; the guest is dead; the guest is your
  ex). Note how action changes.
- **Image monologue**: describe a place in the play in detail to a partner who
  asks questions; anything you can't answer is homework.

## Gotchas
- Using the if to *feel* ("if I were sad") produces generalised emotion.
  Ask what you would *do*.
- Substituting your own life entirely loses the character's circumstances
  (that's a different tool — see Hagen substitution).
- Invented backstory that contradicts the text: text facts win.

## Verify
- [ ] Written list of given circumstances, facts separated from inferences.
- [ ] Each scene has a defined moment-before.
- [ ] Every place/person you mention has a specific inner image.
- [ ] Your if-answers are verbs, not feelings.
