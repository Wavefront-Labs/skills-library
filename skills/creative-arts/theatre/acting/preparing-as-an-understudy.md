# Preparing as an understudy

An understudy (UK also "cover"; US "standby" is off-stage-only) may go on with a
day's or an hour's notice. Preparation must be complete before you're needed.

## Steps
1. **Learn the text word-perfect early**, before rehearsals for covers start.
2. **Get the blocking** from the DSM/SM's prompt book and by watching every
   rehearsal you can. Note entrances, exits, spike marks, crosses and which
   side of the stage you exit for the next entrance.
3. **Track sheet**: write the role's running order — every entrance, exit,
   quick change, prop pick-up and preset (ask wardrobe and props for their
   plots). It's your survival map.
4. **Watch the show from the wings and the house** repeatedly. Note business
   and timing, cue lights, follow-spot points, where to look for the MD.
5. **Understudy rehearsals** (run by the SM or resident director) — come
   off-book, ask questions about safety-critical moments: fights, lifts, flying,
   trap doors, intimacy.
6. **Fight/intimacy calls**: learn choreography with the fight/intimacy
   director and do the fight call before any performance you go on for.
7. **Costume fittings** for the role's costumes; know quick-change setups.
8. **Put-in rehearsal** when possible: a run with the company/stage crew.

## The call
- Stay reachable during show times per contract; the SM calls you.
- On arrival: fight call, quick-change walk-through with dressers, check props,
  talk through key moments with scene partners.
- Play the show as rehearsed (the agreed blocking), with your own truth inside
  it. Huge new choices endanger partners.

## Gotchas
- Learning from the lead's interpretation rather than the director's blocking.
- Not knowing which side to exit — the most common understudy mistake.
- Missing a safety call.

## Verify
- [ ] Off-book, track sheet written, blocking checked against the book.
- [ ] All fight/intimacy/flying moments rehearsed with the right people.
- [ ] Costumes fitted and quick changes walked.
