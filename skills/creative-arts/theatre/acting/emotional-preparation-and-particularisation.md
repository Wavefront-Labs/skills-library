# Emotional preparation and particularisation

Meisner: emotional preparation is "the device that permits you to start your
scene or play in a condition of emotional aliveness". It is used **before
entering** — once the scene starts, you work off the partner.

## Emotional preparation
1. **Identify the state needed at entry** from the given circumstances
   (elation after good news, dread before a confrontation).
2. **Daydream, don't remember.** Meisner preferred *imaginary* circumstances
   that move you over recalled trauma: invent a fantasy in specific detail
   ("I've just been told I've won a scholarship to Paris; I see the letter...").
   Imagination is safer and renewable night after night.
3. **Find what works personally**, then test it repeatedly in class: some
   fantasies wear out; keep two or three per emotional colour.
4. **Use it just before the entrance** (a minute or two in the wings). Enter
   with it and then **let it go**: the partner's behaviour now leads.
5. A small preparation is enough. Meisner: "you only need to be full at the
   start; the scene does the rest."

## Particularisation
When the text references something that must matter, find a personal "as if"
that makes it concrete for you: "It's as if my best friend told everyone my
secret". It is private (never shared in the room unless you choose) and
specific — an image, a person, an object.

## Safety and wellbeing
- Avoid fresh, unresolved personal trauma. If it hasn't healed, it isn't
  usable craft material.
- Have a deliberate exit after the show (see
  `creative-arts.theatre.acting.de-roling-and-cool-down`).
- Nobody — teacher or director — can require you to disclose a
  particularisation.

## Gotchas
- Over-preparing and "playing the emotion" after entering — the classic
  result-acting trap.
- Using the same fantasy too often until it goes dead: rotate.
- Preparing for the wrong state because the moment-before was misread; check
  circumstances first.

## Verify
- [ ] Each emotionally loaded entrance has a defined preparation.
- [ ] Preparations are imaginary/as-if, not raw recent trauma.
- [ ] You can drop the preparation once the scene begins.
