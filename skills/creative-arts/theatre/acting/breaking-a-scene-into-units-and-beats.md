# Breaking a scene into units and beats

Stanislavski's *kusok* ("bit", translated "unit"; US slang "beat") is a section of a
scene in which one objective is pursued. A new unit starts when the objective
changes — usually because something happens (an **event**: a discovery, an
entrance, a decision, a refusal).

## Procedure
1. **Read the whole play twice** before marking anything. Units only make sense
   against the arc.
2. **Mark events.** Go through the scene and draw a line across the page at each
   moment that changes what someone wants: news arrives, a secret is revealed,
   a person enters/leaves, an offer is refused.
3. **Title each unit** with a short noun phrase ("The proposal", "The money
   question"). Titles force you to say what the section is *about*.
4. **Write your objective for the unit** as "I want (the partner) to ___" with an
   active verb the partner can do or feel: *I want him to admit he lied*. Avoid
   "I want to be happy" — unplayable and not aimed at anyone.
5. **Identify the obstacle** in each unit (what stops you): the partner, a
   circumstance, yourself.
6. **Link up.** Write the scene objective (what you want across all units), then
   the **super-objective** (what the character wants across the whole play) and the
   **through-line of action**: the single verb-chain connecting every scene
   objective to the super-objective. If a scene objective doesn't serve the
   through-line, reconsider one of them.
7. **Test in rehearsal.** Play unit by unit; the director may re-draw beat lines.
   Pencil only.

## Layout tips
- Brackets in the margin with unit numbers (1, 2, 3…) and the objective above.
- Keep a separate one-page "spine" sheet: super-objective at top, each scene
  objective below — the shortest map of your performance.
- Units are usually 1/2 to 2 pages in naturalistic plays; very short units signal
  high-pressure scenes.

## Gotchas
- **Every line a new beat** — too fine to play; collapse to where the *want*
  really changes.
- **Objectives that are emotions** ("I want to feel loved") — convert to what
  you need the other person to do.
- **Ignoring the partner's beat changes**: their event is often your new unit.
- Directors and actors may use "beat" also to mean "pause" (from stage
  directions) — clarify which in the room.

## Verify
- [ ] Every unit has a title, an objective aimed at a partner and an obstacle.
- [ ] Each unit boundary is justified by an event in the text.
- [ ] Scene objectives line up under one super-objective via a through-line.
- [ ] You can summarise the scene as a list of unit titles in under 30 seconds.
