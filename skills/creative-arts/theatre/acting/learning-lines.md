# Learning lines

Goal: **word-perfect, on cue, without effort**, by the off-book date the
director sets (often end of week 2 in a 4-week rehearsal).

## Method
1. **Understand before memorising.** Know what each thought means and why the
   character says it now. Meaning-based learning sticks; rote doesn't survive
   pressure.
2. **Chunk by thought**, not line. Learn one thought, then add the next, then
   recite from the top of the unit (cumulative).
3. **First-letter method** for exactness: write the first letter of each word
   with punctuation (`T b, o n t b: t i t q.`). Recite from the letters; it
   forces exact wording without the full text.
4. **Learn the cues.** Cover your lines and drill: read the cue line, speak your
   line. The cue's last words trigger yours. Apps (e.g. LineLearner, Rehearsal Pro,
   Scene Partner) or a recorded cue track help: record cues with silence gaps
   for your lines.
5. **Spaced repetition**: revisit at increasing intervals (same day, next
   day, 3 days, a week). Short daily sessions beat one long cram.
6. **Connect to movement**: once blocked, run lines while walking the blocking
   (even in your kitchen). Body memory reinforces text.
7. **Line runs** ("Italian run": fast, flat, accurate) with the cast before
   off-book day and before tech.
8. **Check against the script** for paraphrases. Ask the DSM for line notes and
   fix every one (see `creative-arts.theatre.stage-management.giving-line-notes`).

## On off-book day
- Call "line" (or the company's word) clearly, stay in character, take the
  prompt and continue.
- Note every prompt you needed; drill those that night.

## Gotchas
- Learning with a fixed delivery ("line readings" baked in) — learn neutrally,
  then play.
- Learning from an outdated script version; confirm the current draft.
- Paraphrasing in new writing — the writer will notice; in verse it breaks
  the metre.

## Verify
- [ ] Can run every scene from cues only, word-perfect.
- [ ] Line notes from DSM are zero or corrected.
- [ ] Can start from any cue mid-scene.
