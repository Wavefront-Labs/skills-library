# Meisner repetition exercise

Sanford Meisner (*Sanford Meisner on Acting*, Neighborhood Playhouse): acting
is "living truthfully under imaginary circumstances". The repetition exercise
trains actors to take their attention off themselves and respond to what the
partner actually does.

## Stages
1. **Mechanical repetition.** Partners sit facing each other. A makes a simple
   observation about B ("You're wearing a blue shirt"). B repeats exactly ("You're
   wearing a blue shirt"). Repeat back and forth. No line readings, no comedy —
   just listen and repeat.
2. **Repetition from your own point of view.** B changes pronouns: "I'm wearing
   a blue shirt." A: "You're wearing a blue shirt." Still about the observation.
3. **Observing behaviour.** Observations move from objects to behaviour: "You're
   smiling." "I'm smiling." "You're smiling." The *way* each line is said now
   changes according to what you see.
4. **Picking up changes (point-of-view repetition).** When the partner's behaviour
   changes, the words change to name it: "You're smiling" → "You stopped smiling"
   → "You're annoyed with me". Words change only when something real changes in
   the other person — not to be interesting.
5. **Repetition with independent activity and knock at the door** (next stages,
   see `creative-arts.theatre.acting.meisner-independent-activity`).

## Rules the teacher enforces
- **Don't do anything until something happens to make you do it** (Meisner).
- **What you do doesn't depend on you; it depends on the other person.**
- No politeness: if you feel irritation, the repetition carries irritation.
- No physical violence, no leaving the chair unless the exercise allows it.
- Stop exercises that become about clever wording — that's "in the head".

## Running a session
- Pairs of 3-5 minutes in front of the class, then brief notes ("you
  anticipated", "you were reading the line", "you missed when she laughed").
- Daily practice over weeks; the stages take months in a full Meisner course.

## Gotchas
- Actors "performing" variety in reading → note: you're working on yourself,
  not on the partner.
- Treating it as a game of insults; the observations must be true.
- Skipping stage 1 because it seems trivial: it builds listening.

## Verify
- [ ] Each change in wording traces to a real observable behaviour change.
- [ ] Reactions come instantly, not after deciding.
- [ ] The observer can describe the partner's behaviour from the repetitions alone.
