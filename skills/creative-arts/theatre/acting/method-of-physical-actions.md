# Method of physical actions

Stanislavski's late approach (1930s): emotion cannot be commanded, but a simple,
truthful **physical action** under given circumstances can, and feeling follows.
The role is built as a **score** of such actions.

## Procedure
1. **Retell the scene as actions.** In plain words, list what your character
   physically *does* in sequence: enters, looks for the key, finds the drawer
   locked, forces it, hears footsteps, hides the knife.
2. **Make each action simple and doable** — something you could do truthfully
   now: open, search, listen, wait, pour. No psychological verbs here.
3. **Perform the sequence for real** in the rehearsal space (with or without
   text). Do it at the speed and with the care the circumstances demand. Each
   action must have its logic: *why* now, *how* exactly.
4. **Check logic and sequence.** If an action feels false, find the missing link
   ("I can't just force the drawer — I first check the door").
5. **Add the text** onto the score. The words ride on the actions.
6. **Fix the score.** Repeat the sequence identically until it is reliable; the
   score is what you return to each night. Feelings may vary; actions don't.
7. **Pare down** in later rehearsal: some actions become inner (a glance instead
   of a search), but keep their logic.

## Exercise: imaginary objects
Counting money, threading a needle, writing a letter with no props. The detail
required (weight, resistance, order of steps) trains truthful sequence. Compare
with the real object, then repeat without.

## Gotchas
- Adding too many "business" actions that pull focus from the scene — the score
  serves the objective, it is not mime decoration.
- Confusing with "physical theatre": this is psychological realism via action.
- Skipping the given circumstances: physical actions are true only in
  circumstances.

## Verify
- [ ] Written score of physical actions per scene, in sequence.
- [ ] Each action has a reason tied to the objective.
- [ ] The score repeats reliably run to run.
- [ ] Removing the text, the scene's story still reads from the actions.
