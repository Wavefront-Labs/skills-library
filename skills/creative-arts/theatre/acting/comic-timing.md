# Comic timing on stage

## Principles
1. **Play it for real.** Characters in comedy don't know they're funny;
   they have urgent objectives and high stakes. The audience laughs at the gap
   between want and result.
2. **The laugh word goes last.** Land the operative (funny) word at the end of
   the line with a clean stop. Don't step on it with a gesture or the next breath.
3. **Set-up must be heard.** If the set-up line is lost, the punchline dies.
   Clear diction and focus on the set-up.
4. **One thing at a time:** move, *then* speak; or speak, *then* move. A
   simultaneous gesture splits focus.
5. **Rhythm**: comedy often runs on threes (two establish, third breaks
   pattern) and on pace — pick up cues tight between laugh lines.

## Holding for laughs
- When a laugh comes, **freeze the action, stay in character, keep the
  energy**. Don't say the next line into the laugh.
- Come back in **as the laugh peaks and starts to fall** (not at total silence
  — the energy drops — and not at the peak — the line is lost).
- If a laugh is long, add a small motivated piece of behaviour to fill.
- New laughs appear in previews; rehearse what to do if they don't come too
  (don't wait for a laugh that isn't there).

## Takes and topping
- **Take**: a reaction look (to partner or out front). Double-take: look, look
  away, snap back. Keep them short and motivated.
- **Topping**: each line in a building exchange slightly higher in pitch,
  volume or intensity than the last; or deliberately **undercut** (drop low) for
  contrast.

## Farce specifics
- Precision: doors, props and entrances to the beat; rehearse as choreography.
- Panic must escalate logically; every lie forces another.

## Gotchas
- "Playing for the laugh" (winking at the audience) kills it.
- Changing a working rhythm because it "feels stale" in the run — refresh
  intentions, not timing.
- Laughs vary with house size; don't chase last night's laugh.

## Verify
- [ ] Each laugh line has a clear set-up and a final operative word.
- [ ] Cast agrees what to do on and off laughs.
- [ ] Moves and lines separated where a laugh is expected.
