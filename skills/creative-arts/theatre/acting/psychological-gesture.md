# Psychological gesture

Michael Chekhov (*To the Actor*, ch. 5): a single, strong, whole-body gesture
that condenses the character's (or a scene's) main desire. Performed physically
in rehearsal, then done **inwardly** while acting, it awakens will and feeling.

## Finding a PG
1. **State the core want** as a simple archetypal verb: *to grasp, to push, to
   pull, to penetrate, to lift, to open, to close, to tear, to embrace, to
   throw, to smash*.
2. **Do the gesture with the whole body**, big and clear, from a neutral stance
   to a held end position. Example: Lady Macbeth "to seize": reach up and out,
   clench, pull down to the belly.
3. **Add direction and quality**: up or down, toward or away, fast or slow,
   with one of the qualities (moulding, floating, flying, radiating — see
   `creative-arts.theatre.acting.chekhov-qualities-and-imaginary-body`).
4. **Archetypal, not naturalistic.** It's not something the character would
   do in life; it's the *shape* of the want.
5. **Repeat** several times with full energy, then hold the end position and
   sense what it awakens (sensation, tempo, feeling).

## Using it
- **Before a scene:** do it fully in the wings, then enter carrying its after-
  image ("radiating" it).
- **While speaking:** do it inwardly — the body is still, the gesture continues
  invisibly. Speak lines "through" the PG.
- **Per speech or beat:** a scene can have a sequence of PGs (one per unit).
- **Directors** can give a PG instead of a psychological note ("try it with
  *pulling*, not *pushing*").

## Gotchas
- Small, polite, half-committed gestures don't work — it must be bold and fully
  extended in rehearsal.
- Mixing two verbs ("grab and push") muddles it; choose one.
- Leaving the gesture visible in performance — it should be internal unless
  the director wants stylised movement.
- PG that contradicts the objective: test against the script analysis.

## Verify
- [ ] The PG is one archetypal verb with clear direction and quality.
- [ ] Performed fully, it produces a recognisable tempo/feeling change.
- [ ] You can run the scene with the PG internal and the effect remains.
