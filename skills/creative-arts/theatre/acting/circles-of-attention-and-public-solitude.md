# Circles of attention and public solitude

From *An Actor's Work*, chapter "Concentration and Attention". The audience pulls
the actor's attention out of the play; the cure is a definite **object of
attention** inside the fiction, held within a chosen circle.

## The circles
- **Small circle** — you and an object within arm's reach (a letter, your
  hands, a cup). Intimate, private scenes; useful when nervous.
- **Medium circle** — you plus a partner or a small group, a few metres.
  Most dialogue.
- **Large circle** — the whole stage world, a room, the horizon. Arrivals,
  addresses to crowds, outdoor scenes.
- **Public solitude** — being private while watched: the ability to stay in a
  small circle with an audience present.

## Training sequence
1. **Relaxation scan** first (tension blocks attention): lie down, release
   jaw/shoulders/hands, then stand and keep only the tension the task needs.
2. **Object study**: give full attention to one object for 2 minutes — weight,
   texture, temperature, marks. When attention drifts, notice and return.
3. **Grow the circle**: lamp on one object; then include the partner; then the
   room. Shrink back. The teacher can literally light circles of increasing size.
4. **Private moment under observation**: perform a real private task (sorting
   a drawer, making tea) while the group watches; aim for no change in tempo or
   self-display.
5. **Switching**: in scene work, assign circles per unit and switch on a cue
   (an entrance widens the circle, a whispered line narrows it).

## Applying in performance
- When you feel the audience ("I'm being watched"), drop to a small circle:
  a specific object or detail of the partner's face. Then widen again.
- Mark circles in your script for big transitions.
- Direct address / soliloquy: the audience becomes a defined object in the
  circle rather than a vague mass — choose specific seats/sections.

## Gotchas
- Staring at an object ≠ attention. Attention is active (noticing, needing
  something from it).
- Narrowing so far that you stop listening to the partner.
- A small circle still needs sightlines and projection; it is mental, not a
  licence to mutter.

## Verify
- [ ] You can name the object and circle for each unit.
- [ ] Under observation, your private-task tempo matches your unobserved tempo.
- [ ] You have a known "reset object" for when focus breaks in performance.
