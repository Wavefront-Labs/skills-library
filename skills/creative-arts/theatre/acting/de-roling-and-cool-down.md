# De-roling and cool-down

De-roling (from drama therapy) is the deliberate act of leaving a character's
emotional and physical state when the work ends. Essential for roles involving
violence, abuse, grief or intimacy, and good practice for every show.

## Individual routine (3-10 minutes)
1. **Physical release**: shake out hands, arms, legs; big exhale through the
   mouth; roll shoulders and neck. Undo the character's posture deliberately.
2. **Change something concrete**: remove a costume item, wash your face, change
   shoes — a ritual marker of "the character stays here".
3. **Name yourself**: say aloud "I am [your name], it is [day], I'm in [place],
   tonight I'm going to [real plan]". Re-anchors identity.
4. **Breath**: slow breathing with longer exhales (e.g. in 4, out 6) for
   1-2 minutes to bring arousal down.
5. **Voice cool-down**: gentle hums/sirens downward, lip trills — especially
   after shouting or crying scenes.
6. **Transition activity** before going home: a snack, a short walk, a chat
   about something else.

## Group / rehearsal-room practices
- **Closing circle**: each person says one word about how they're leaving, or
  one thing they're looking forward to.
- **Partner de-role** after violent/intimate scenes: face each other, name
  each other by real names, a check-in ("anything you need from me?").
- Director/intimacy director builds 5 minutes for this into the schedule after
  heavy scenes.

## When it's not enough
Persistent intrusive thoughts, sleep disruption, or mood changes lasting days
need support: company welfare officer, Equity's support services, a GP or
therapist. Many productions now provide a mental-health first aider.

## Gotchas
- Skipping it because the show "went fine" — accumulation happens across a
  run.
- Using alcohol as the de-role.
- Staying "in character" off stage (method myth) to colleagues' detriment.

## Verify
- [ ] A named routine you do after every heavy show/rehearsal.
- [ ] Scene partners have agreed a check-in after violent/intimate scenes.
- [ ] You know who the company's welfare contact is.
