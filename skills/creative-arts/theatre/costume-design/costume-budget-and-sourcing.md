# Costume budget and sourcing

## Sourcing options per item
- **Pull from stock** (theatre's own wardrobe store): cheapest, alteration
  costs; limited choice.
- **Hire/rent** (costume houses, e.g. National Theatre Costume Hire, Angels
  in the UK; regional theatres' rental departments): period pieces, uniforms;
  pay weekly/run fees; alterations restricted.
- **Buy** (retail, vintage, online): modern dress; check returns policy.
- **Build** (made-to-measure in the costume shop or by makers): unique,
  period-accurate, best fit and durability; highest labour cost.

## Estimating
1. From the **piece list** (see
   `creative-arts.theatre.costume-design.costume-piece-list-and-rendering-media`),
   assign a sourcing method and cost to every item.
2. **Materials**: fabric (metres x price), trims, notions, dye.
3. **Labour**: maker hours (or quotes from freelancers), wigs and make-up
   labour, dyeing/breaking down.
4. **Hire** fees x weeks (including rehearsal and run), cleaning/repair
   charges.
5. **Consumables** for the run: tights, blood, laundry, replacements.
6. **Contingency** 10-15%.
Compare to the budget from the production manager; adjust sourcing (more
stock/hire, fewer builds) before compromising the key looks.

## Tracking
- Spreadsheet: item, method, estimate, actual, supplier, receipt reference,
  return date for hires/purchases.
- Petty cash and receipts reconciled weekly with the supervisor/PM.
- Return unwanted purchases promptly; return hires on time and clean.

## Gotchas
- Forgetting underwear, shoes, and duplicates.
- Hire items you can't alter for the actor's fit.
- Late design changes after builds start.

## Verify
- [ ] Every piece has a sourcing method and estimate.
- [ ] Total within budget (with contingency).
- [ ] Spend tracked weekly with receipts.
