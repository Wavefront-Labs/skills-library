# Lighting cue timing and the plotting session

## Cue synopsis (before plotting)
A table of planned cues: number, page, placement (line or action), what changes
(state description), purpose, approximate timing. Share with the director and
DSM. It frames the plotting session and lets the DSM pre-mark the book.

## The plotting session
- Usually in the theatre after focus, before tech: LD, programmer, director,
  DSM (to write cues), sometimes designers; stand-ins walk positions.
- Build cues in order: each state at the right levels, then record with times.
- The DSM writes placement and number into the book (see
  `creative-arts.theatre.stage-management.writing-standbys-and-cue-placement`).
- Work in "tracking" mode on most consoles (changes carry forward until
  changed) — know where changes will track to.

## Timing vocabulary
- **Up/down (in/out) times**: separate fade times for increasing and decreasing
  channels (e.g. "3 up / 5 down" — the decreasing light lingers to avoid a dip).
- **Delay**: wait before the fade starts (per cue or per parameter).
- **Part cues**: within one cue, different channels/parameters with different
  times (e.g. backlight 2s, front light 8s).
- **Follow / auto-follow**: the next cue triggers automatically after a time or
  when the previous completes — for sequences the DSM shouldn't call
  individually.
- **Hang/wait time** and **link** for loops.
- **Point cues**: inserted cues (12.5) without renumbering.

## Timing craft
- **Snap** cues (0s) for shocks, comic punctuation, blackouts.
- **Long imperceptible fades** (45-120s) for sunsets and slow mood shifts.
- Transitions to music: match fade to musical phrase.
- Lead with what the audience should look at next — the eye follows the light.
- "Bump" isolations quickly; restore with slower fades.

## Gotchas
- Equal up/down timing causing a visible dip in crossfades.
- Tracking changes ruining later cues — use cue-only/block cues where needed.
- Too many cues called individually — use follows.

## Verify
- [ ] Cue synopsis agreed before plotting.
- [ ] Every cue has times recorded and placement in the DSM's book.
- [ ] Crossfades have no dips; follows run correctly.
