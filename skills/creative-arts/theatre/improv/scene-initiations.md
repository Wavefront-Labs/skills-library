# Scene initiations

The first 3-5 lines decide whether a scene has a solid **base reality** (who,
what, where — see the UCB *Comedy Improvisation Manual*, ch. 1-3).

## Initiation types
1. **Relationship + emotion + location in one line**:
   "Dad, I can't believe you sold the car on my birthday." (who: child/father,
   what: sold car, emotional stake.)
2. **Endowment**: give your partner a trait, job or history: "Doctor, you've
   had your hands in my chest for six hours, could you at least look at me?"
3. **Object/environment work first**: silently establish an activity (washing
   dishes, fixing an engine) with specific space work, then speak.
4. **Premise initiation** (more advanced, UCB "premise-based"): start with an
   unusual idea already in play, often from an opening.
5. **Emotional/physical**: enter with a strong, specific emotion or posture and
   let the partner name it.

## Rules of thumb
- **Specific > general**: "Brenda, this is the third time you've parked in my
  space at the morgue" beats "Hi, how are you?".
- **Name each other** early.
- **Start in the middle** — skip greetings and arrivals.
- **Partner's job**: accept and add the next most obvious detail ("I told you,
  the hearses get priority, Gary").
- Avoid questions that hand the work back ("Who are you?").
- Keep the first beat grounded; the unusual thing comes when it comes.

## Drills
- **Three-line scenes**: A initiates, B responds, A adds — then stop. Check:
  do we know who, what, where and how they feel?
- **Initiation lists**: write 20 initiations of each type before class.
- **Silent 10 seconds** of object work before any line.

## Gotchas
- Transaction scenes (selling/buying) with no relationship.
- Teaching scenes ("Let me show you how to...") — low stakes.
- Starting with conflict over facts ("No, we're not in a bank") — that's blocking.

## Verify
- [ ] Within 3 lines: relationship, location, activity and emotion are clear.
- [ ] Names given early.
- [ ] No questions in the initiation.
