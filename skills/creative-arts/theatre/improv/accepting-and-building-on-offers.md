# Accepting and building on offers

An **offer** is anything a scene partner does or says that could define
reality: a line, a gesture, a mimed object, an emotion, a sound. **Yes, and**
means accepting it as true (*yes*) and adding information that builds on it
(*and*).

## What counts as an offer
- Verbal: "Your hands are cold."
- Physical: partner mimes holding a baby.
- Emotional: partner enters trembling.
- Environmental: lights change, music plays, the audience laughs at
  something.

## Accept-and-add procedure
1. **Listen and look** — fully, until the partner finishes.
2. **Accept** the reality literally ("Yes, I've been in the freezer.").
3. **Add** something connected and specific that raises stakes or deepens
   relationship ("...because you locked me in after I ate your yoghurt.").
4. Don't add a new unrelated idea — build *on* the last thing.

## Common failures
- **Blocking**: denying an offer ("That's not a baby, it's a bag.").
- **Wimping**: accepting without adding ("Yeah, okay." "Uh-huh.").
- **Gagging**: undermining the scene for a cheap laugh.
- **Bulldozing**: ignoring the partner and driving your own idea.
- **Questioning**: asking questions instead of making statements.
- **Waffling**: talking without making a definite choice.

## Drills
- **"Yes, and" story**: pairs build a story, each line starting "Yes, and...".
- **"Yes, but" contrast**: same story with "Yes, but" — feel how it stalls.
- **Gift-giving**: A mimes giving a box; B names what it is and is delighted;
  A adds why they chose it.
- **Endowment circle**: each person endows the next with a trait they must
  accept.

## Nuance
"Yes, and" applies to reality, not to the character's agreement: a character
can refuse a request ("No, I won't marry you") while the improviser accepts the
offer that a proposal happened.

## Verify
- [ ] No offers denied in a 3-minute scene.
- [ ] Each line adds information connected to the previous one.
- [ ] Physical and emotional offers acknowledged, not only verbal ones.
