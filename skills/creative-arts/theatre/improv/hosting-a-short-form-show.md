# Hosting a short-form show

The host (MC) runs the show: sets energy, explains games, collects suggestions
and controls pace. A 60-90 minute show might have 10-15 games.

## Before the show
- **Running order** with the team: alternate scene games, guessing games,
  musical games, high-energy and quiet games; open and close strong.
- Agree **signals**: how the host ends a game (ring a bell, "and scene", lights
  out), and which games need specific suggestion types.
- Check with tech: lights and music cues.

## Opening
1. Big welcome, quick explanation of improv ("everything is made up from your
   suggestions").
2. **Audience warm-up**: practise shouting suggestions ("give me a household
   object!"), cheering; maybe a quick participation game.
3. Introduce the team.

## Taking suggestions
- Ask for **specific categories**: "a location", "an occupation", "a
  relationship", "a film genre", "an object you'd find in a garage".
- **Pick the first clear, usable suggestion you hear**, repeat it loudly into
  the mic. Speed keeps energy.
- Filter: avoid offensive, overly niche, or "sex/toilet" suggestions unless
  the show's tone wants them — "let's go with something else, I heard..."
- Vary who you take from (not just the loud front row).
- For games needing multiple suggestions, collect them all before players
  start.

## During games
- Explain the rules in one or two sentences; demonstrate if needed.
- End games **on a laugh** or as soon as they peak — earlier is better than late.
- Keep transitions under ~30 seconds; fill with banter, not dead air.

## Gotchas
- Long explanations killing momentum.
- Taking the same audience member repeatedly.
- Letting a dying game run on.

## Verify
- [ ] Running order balanced and agreed.
- [ ] Suggestions taken in under 10 seconds each.
- [ ] Every game ended at or near its peak.
