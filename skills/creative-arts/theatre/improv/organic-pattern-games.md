# Organic pattern games (group games)

In the Harold and other long forms, **group games** (the "second beat" group
scene in a Harold) involve most of the ensemble playing a single repeatable
pattern. Not to be confused with pre-set short-form games.

## How a pattern game forms
1. **An idea or move** is offered — from the opening, from a scene, or a
   physical/verbal action that invites repetition (e.g. "everyone in
   line confesses something to the priest").
2. **Establish the rule**: a second person repeats the move with a variation;
   now there's a pattern.
3. **Heighten round the group**: each new player adds a bigger or more
   surprising instance, in rhythm.
4. **Twist**: break the pattern once it's fully established (a reversal
   or the pattern applied to its originator).
5. **End**: a strong button — often a callback to an earlier scene — then edit.

## Structures that support it
- **Line-up / interview** formats (a press conference, a line at a counter).
- **Repetitive tasks** (a production line, a choir).
- **Mirror/echo** moves (everyone copies a sound or movement and evolves it).

## Craft points
- Short contributions; keep rhythm.
- Everyone watches the edges — the best instance often comes from a quiet
  player.
- Don't stack three people speaking at once; pace entries.
- Support: if a move fails, the next player can justify or reset the pattern.

## Drills
- "Pattern circle": group stands in a circle; one makes a sound+move; next
  repeats and alters slightly; continue until it transforms.
- "Group game from a word": coach calls a word; team has 2 minutes to find and
  play a pattern.

## Gotchas
- Too many players entering with unrelated ideas.
- Pattern established but never heightened.
- No ending — edits should land on a laugh or callback.

## Verify
- [ ] Rule recognisable after 2-3 contributions.
- [ ] Each contribution heightens.
- [ ] Clear button/edit at a peak.
