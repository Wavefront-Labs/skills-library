# Theatresports format

Created by Keith Johnstone at Loose Moose Theatre, Calgary (late 1970s), inspired by
the energy of professional wrestling. The name and format are licensed via the
International Theatresports Institute (check licensing for public shows).
Related Johnstone formats: **Maestro** (individual elimination) and **Gorilla
Theatre** (players take turns directing).

## Roles
- **Two teams** (3-5 players each).
- **Judges** (traditionally three) score each scene, e.g. holding up cards 1-5,
  and can stop boring scenes.
- **Host/MC** introduces challenges and manages time.
- **Scorekeeper** and often a **musician** and lighting operator.

## Flow
1. Teams take turns issuing **challenges** to the other: "We challenge you to
   the best scene with a dog", "...the best love scene in gibberish".
2. Challenged team accepts (or the challenger may accept a counter).
3. Both teams perform their version of the challenge; judges score each.
4. Judges can call **"warning for boring"** (traditionally a hand signal/horn);
   continuing to be boring gets the scene stopped ("honk").
5. Penalties for gagging or offensive material (e.g. a player wears a basket
   or bag on head for a time — the "basket" penalty in some versions).
6. Highest total wins after a set number of rounds; the spirit is
   playful, the competition mock-serious.

## Craft points
- Challenges should inspire good scenes, not traps.
- Teams cheer each other; the audience is encouraged to support or "boo the
  judges".
- Failure is part of the show — embrace it with good humour.

## Gotchas
- Real competitiveness spoiling the generosity.
- Judges who never warn — the warning is what keeps the show sharp.
- Using the name commercially without checking licensing.

## Verify
- [ ] Roles assigned: teams, judges, host, scorekeeper.
- [ ] Challenge list prepared as backup.
- [ ] Warning/penalty signals agreed with judges and players.
