# The Armando (monologue form)

Named after Armando Diaz, who popularised the monologue-based long form in
Chicago and New York (UCB's "Armando Diaz Experience").

## Structure
1. Audience suggestion (one word).
2. **Monologist** (often a guest) tells a **true personal story** inspired by
   the word, 1-3 minutes.
3. Team performs **scenes inspired by ideas** in the monologue — not
   re-enactments. Several scenes, edited normally.
4. Monologist returns (often prompted by team, or edits in) with another
   story, possibly sparked by the scenes.
5. Repeat for the show length (25-40 minutes).

## Pulling premises from a monologue
While listening, note:
- **The unusual/funny idea** ("my mother labels every food container with
  the date AND her mood").
- **Themes** ("family control", "fear of waste").
- **Relationships and dynamics**.
Turn one into a **premise**: take the idea and put it in a new context or
heighten it ("a NASA mission control where every decision is labelled with the
director's mood").

## Scene craft
- **Initiate with the premise already in play** (premise-based scenes).
- Avoid reenacting the monologist's story — the audience has heard it.
- Several players may "see" the same idea; the first to initiate gets it,
  others support.
- Callbacks across monologues build the show's world.

## Monologist tips
- Real, specific stories; details over jokes.
- Let the stories wander thematically from the suggestion and from the scenes.

## Gotchas
- Scenes that only mock the monologist.
- Everyone waiting to initiate — someone must go.
- Monologues that are prepared stand-up bits.

## Verify
- [ ] Each scene traceable to an idea from a monologue.
- [ ] No straight re-enactments.
- [ ] Multiple monologues, each feeding new scenes.
