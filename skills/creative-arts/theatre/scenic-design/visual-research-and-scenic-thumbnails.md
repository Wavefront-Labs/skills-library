# Visual research and scenic thumbnails

Pamela Howard (*What is Scenography?*): scenography integrates space, text,
research, art, performers, director and spectators. The early phase is
research and spatial thinking, not finished pictures.

## 1. Script breakdown for space
For each scene: location, time, required entrances/exits, essential furniture
and objects, actions that need specific scenery (a balcony, a door slammed,
rain), number of people onstage, transitions. Note what is *essential* vs
*suggested* by stage directions.

## 2. Visual research
- **Primary**: period photographs, architecture, paintings of the era, real
  places (site visits), objects, textures.
- **Evocative/abstract**: art, photography, sculpture, film stills,
  colour and light references that catch the play's atmosphere.
- Collect widely, then **edit** into a **mood board** (physical or digital —
  Pinterest, Miro, PureRef) grouped by idea: world, textures, palette, key images.
- Keep sources and credits for each image.

## 3. Thumbnails
- Small, fast sketches (pencil/pen or tablet), 5-10 cm wide, of the space from
  the audience view and in plan.
- Explore **many spatial options** quickly (open space vs enclosed room, one
  unit set vs changing scenery, raked/tilted floor, levels).
- Indicate scale with tiny figures. Note how each option handles transitions
  and the key moments.
- Rough **white card sketch models** can follow promising thumbnails (see
  `creative-arts.theatre.scenic-design.ground-plans-and-white-card-models`).

## 4. Choosing with the director
- Present research and 3-5 thumbnail directions; discuss what each does to
  the story and actor-audience relationship.
- Agree one or two to develop; capture decisions in writing.

## Gotchas
- Literal illustration of every stage direction.
- Falling in love with one image before exploring alternatives.
- Ignoring the venue's real dimensions in thumbnails.

## Verify
- [ ] Spatial breakdown for every scene.
- [ ] Edited mood board with sourced images.
- [ ] Multiple thumbnail options shown; direction chosen.
