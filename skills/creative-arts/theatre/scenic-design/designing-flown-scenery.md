# Designing flown and moving scenery

## Flying
1. **Know the fly system**: counterweight (manual) vs hemp vs powered/automated
   bars; number of linesets, their positions, bar length, **max load per bar**,
   grid height (needs ~2x proscenium height to fly a full-height piece out of
   sight), arbor capacity.
2. **Assign linesets** to masking, LX bars and scenery on a **lineset schedule**
   (number, distance from setting line, item, weight, in/out trims).
3. **Weight**: give the shop and flys a target weight; lightweight construction
   (timber frames, soft cloths, aluminium) keeps within limits.
4. **Pick-up points**: designed into the piece for balanced lifting.
5. **Clearances**: adjacent flown pieces need clearance to pass; check against
   LX bars and borders on the section.
6. **Trims**: "in" trim (playing position) and "out" trim (hidden by borders).
7. Flying performers is a separate specialist discipline (Foy, ZFX) — never improvised.

## Revolves, trucks and automation
- **Revolve**: built-in or temporary (sits on the stage floor, raising the
  deck). Specify diameter, speed, direction, loads, and power/control.
- **Trucks/wagons**: platforms on castors moved by crew or automation; tracked
  (guide channels) or free; brakes; heights; how actors get on/off.
- **Automation**: motorised flying, trucks, lifts, revolves controlled by systems
  (e.g. Stage Technologies/TAIT/Waagner-Biro); requires risk assessment,
  E-stops, operator sightlines, and rehearsal time.
- Coordinate with production manager early; moving scenery drives budget and tech
  schedule.

## Documentation
Lineset schedule, section showing trims, moving-scenery plan with travel paths,
speeds and positions, and change list for the storyboard.

## Gotchas
- Designing a piece taller than the grid allows to fly out.
- Underestimating weight (steel, MDF) beyond bar capacity.
- Trucks with no space to store offstage.

## Verify
- [ ] Every flown item on the lineset schedule with weight and trims.
- [ ] Clearances checked on section.
- [ ] Automation scope agreed and costed with the PM.
