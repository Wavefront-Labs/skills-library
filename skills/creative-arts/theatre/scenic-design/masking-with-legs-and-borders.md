# Masking with legs and borders

## Elements
- **Legs** (UK also "wings"/"tabs" when hung as pairs): vertical drapes at the
  sides hiding the wings.
- **Borders**: horizontal drapes hiding the fly tower and lighting bars.
- **Black masking flats** (hard masking) where soft drapes won't work.
- **Tabs/travellers**: full-width curtains (mid-stage tabs, back tabs).

## Positioning legs
1. Plot **horizontal sightlines** from the extreme side seats (see
   `creative-arts.theatre.scenic-design.centre-line-section-and-sightline-checks`).
2. Set the **opening** (distance between onstage edges of a pair) so the
   sightlines land on the leg, not beyond it. Openings usually stay constant or
   narrow slightly upstage.
3. Space leg pairs typically every 1.5-3 m (5-10 ft) of depth, aligned with
   **lighting bar positions** so sidelight booms sit in the wings between legs.
4. Legs can be angled ("raked" or "hard-masked") to improve masking with fewer
   pieces.

## Positioning borders
- From the centre-line section: front-row sightlines under each border to the
  next upstage lighting bar; set **trim heights** so bars and flown scenery are
  hidden.
- Each border usually sits just downstage of a lighting bar.

## Materials
- **Wool serge** or cotton velour, black, inherently or treated
  flame-retardant (certificate required — see flame-retardancy skill).
- Flat-hung (no fullness) is common for modern masking; 50-100% fullness for
  a softer, traditional look.
- Bottom chain/pipe pockets to keep legs straight.

## Black box and studio
Use free-standing masking flats, pipe-and-drape or tab tracks; masking is about
defining entrances and hiding offstage clutter more than flies.

## Gotchas
- Masking that hides the lighting positions the LD needs.
- Leg openings so narrow the side seats lose the action.
- Grey, faded or dusty masking reflecting light.

## Verify
- [ ] Legs and borders positions derived from sightlines.
- [ ] Trims recorded in the lineset schedule.
- [ ] Fabrics' flame certificates on file.
