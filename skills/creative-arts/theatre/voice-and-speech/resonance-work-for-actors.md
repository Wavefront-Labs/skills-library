# Resonance work for actors

Resonance amplifies and colours the sound made at the vocal folds. Good
resonance carries in a large house **without pushing** — the voice "rides"
rather than shouts. Drawn from Linklater (*Freeing the Natural Voice*, resonating
ladder chapters) and Berry (*Voice and the Actor*).

## Prerequisites
Released breath and free throat (see
`creative-arts.theatre.voice-and-speech.projection-and-vocal-health-for-the-stage`).
Resonance work on a tight throat creates strain.

## Exercises
1. **Chest resonance**: on a relaxed low pitch, hum or sigh "huh" with a hand
   on the sternum; feel vibration. Speak a line low and feel it in the chest.
   Pat the chest lightly while sounding.
2. **Mouth/pharynx**: open "ah" sigh with a soft, released jaw and a spacious
   throat (yawn-sensation without depressing the larynx). Explore vowels
   moving through the mouth.
3. **Mask resonance (front of face)**: hum "mmm" with lips lightly closed and
   teeth apart; feel buzzing on lips, nose, cheekbones. Open to "mmm-ah",
   "mmm-ee", "nnn-oh" keeping the buzz forward.
4. **Nasal consonants into speech**: "many men, many men, many many men..." then
   a text line keeping the forward buzz.
5. **The resonating ladder** (Linklater): slide from low (chest) through middle
   (mouth) to high (head/skull) on sighs, sensing where vibration moves.
6. **Calling**: "Hey!" to a point across the room on a forward-placed vowel —
   send the sound, don't push from the throat.
7. Apply to text: speak a speech to the back wall at 3/4 volume and feel the
   resonance do the work.

## Diagnosing
- **Thin/small**: lacks chest; work low hums and open vowels.
- **Dull/swallowed**: sound pulled back; work mask resonance and forward
  vowels.
- **Hypernasal** (too much nose on vowels): pinch the nose on a non-nasal
  sentence ("Bob ate the big apple") — if vibration changes a lot, the soft
  palate isn't lifting; practise "ng-ah" (lowered then lifted palate).
- **Hyponasal** (blocked nose sound): check congestion; work "m/n/ng".

## Gotchas
- Pushing for "chest voice" by depressing the larynx — creates false depth and
  strain.
- Over-forward "buzzy" placement becoming harsh in comedy roles.
- Doing resonance work cold — warm up breath first.

## Verify
- [ ] You can feel vibration shift chest → mask → head on the ladder.
- [ ] A line spoken to the back row carries without throat tension.
- [ ] Nasal pinch test shows vowels not hypernasal.
