# Scanning iambic pentameter

## The pattern
Ten syllables, five **iambs** (da-DUM): *"If MU-sic BE the FOOD of LOVE, play
ON"*. The heartbeat rhythm. Shakespeare varies it constantly; variations are
clues to thought and feeling.

## Procedure
1. **Count syllables** of the line (use the edition's spellings; note
   apostrophes like *o'er*, *th'*, *-'d*).
2. **Mark stresses** naturally as you'd say the words (/ for stressed, x for
   unstressed), then compare to the iambic grid.
3. **Identify variations**:
   - **Trochee** (DUM-da) — commonly at line start or after a caesura:
     *"NOW is the WIN-ter OF our DIS-con-TENT"* — inversion gives a jolt.
   - **Spondee** (DUM-DUM) — two stresses, weight: *"NEV-er, NEV-er..."*.
   - **Feminine ending** — an 11th unstressed syllable: *"To BE, or NOT to BE,
     that IS the QUES-tion"* — often signals unease or an unresolved thought.
   - **Headless / short lines** — missing syllables can signal a pause
     (a silent beat) or a broken moment.
   - **Alexandrines** (12 syllables, six stresses) — occasional.
4. **Elision and expansion**: make syllables fit — *"heaven"* as 1 syllable
   (heav'n) or 2; *-ed* pronounced as a separate syllable (*"belovèd"*) in some
   editions marked with an accent; *"-tion"* sometimes 2 syllables
   (*"na-ti-on"*) in older verse.
5. **Shared lines**: when two characters split one pentameter line, the second
   picks up the cue immediately (no pause) — a sign of urgency or intimacy.
6. Decide which irregularities to **play** (an actor's choice) and mark them.

## Using it in performance
- Don't sing the da-DUM; let the metre support natural speech — it sits
  underneath like a pulse.
- The stresses often land on operative words; the metre helps you find them.
- Irregular lines often mark the emotional peaks.

## Gotchas
- Forcing stress onto wrong syllables to fit (e.g. stressing "of").
- Ignoring the edition's elision marks.
- Treating prose passages as verse (see the prose/verse switch).

## Verify
- [ ] Every line of your speech counted and stress-marked.
- [ ] Variations identified with an acting reason.
- [ ] Shared lines picked up without pause in rehearsal.
