# Operative words, Berry exercises and second circle

## Operative words
The words that carry the new information or the argument of a sentence.
1. Underline the **nouns and verbs** that carry meaning; ignore most pronouns,
   articles, auxiliaries.
2. **New vs given**: stress new information; words already established
   usually lose stress ("I asked for *tea*, not *coffee*" vs "...and the
   *tea* was *cold*").
3. **Antithesis and lists** dictate stress (see
   `creative-arts.theatre.voice-and-speech.line-endings-caesuras-and-antithesis`).
4. Say the sentence with only operative words, then fill in lightly.
Stress via a slight lift, length or energy — not by hammering volume.

## Berry text exercises (Cicely Berry, *The Actor and the Text*; RSC)
- **Walking the punctuation**: walk, changing direction at each punctuation
  mark — feel the thought structure.
- **Pushing against resistance**: partner(s) hold you back while you speak
  and try to reach a point — releases need into the voice.
- **Kicking/throwing**: kick a chair or throw a ball on each operative word.
- **Whispering**: whisper the speech intensely to a partner — clarifies intention
  and consonants.
- **Echo**: group echoes key words the speaker says.
- **Speak while doing a task** (tidying chairs): de-performs the text.

## Rodenburg's three circles of energy (*The Second Circle*, 2008)
- **First circle**: energy withdrawn inward — self-absorbed, mumbling, not
  reaching the listener.
- **Second circle**: energy focused on and exchanged with the other person or
  the audience — present, receptive, giving and taking. The aim on stage.
- **Third circle**: energy pushed outward indiscriminately — broadcasting,
  bluster, not listening.
Exercise: speak a line in each circle to a partner; partner reports how
connected they felt. Return to second circle and keep it as volume increases
for large houses.

## Sight-reading aloud
- Read ahead with the eyes by a phrase; keep the voice on the current phrase.
- Look for the operative word in each phrase; land it.
- Punctuation guides breath; breathe at full stops and colons.
- Read at a pace that allows sense, not speed.

## Gotchas
- Stressing every other word ("acting the words").
- Mistaking third circle for "projection".
- Exercises as games without returning to the text plainly.

## Verify
- [ ] Operative words marked in your speech; new vs given checked.
- [ ] Partner confirms second-circle connection.
- [ ] Unseen text read aloud with sense on first attempt.
