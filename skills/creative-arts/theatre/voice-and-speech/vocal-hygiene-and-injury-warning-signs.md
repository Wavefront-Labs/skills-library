# Vocal hygiene and injury warning signs

## Daily hygiene
- **Hydration**: drink regularly through the day (systemic hydration takes
  hours). Pale urine is a practical check.
- **Steam / nebulised saline**: surface hydration of the folds; 10 minutes of
  steam (bowl or personal steamer) helps after shows and in dry theatres or
  planes.
- **Sleep**: fatigue shows in the voice first.
- **Reflux**: laryngopharyngeal reflux causes hoarseness, throat clearing and
  morning roughness. Avoid eating 2-3 hours before lying down; limit late
  alcohol, caffeine and spicy foods; see a doctor if persistent.
- **Environment**: dry air conditioning, dust (haze/smoke effects), cold
  dressing rooms — use humidifiers, ask about haze fluid types.
- **Avoid**: smoking/vaping, shouting over noise (bars, after-show parties),
  habitual throat clearing (swallow or sip water instead), whispering (it can
  strain more than soft voice).
- **Medication**: decongestants and antihistamines dry the folds; some
  inhaled steroids affect the voice — ask a doctor or pharmacist.

## Warm-up and cool-down
Warm up before every show and rehearsal involving heavy use (see
`creative-arts.theatre.voice-and-speech.projection-and-vocal-health-for-the-stage`);
cool down after with gentle sirens and semi-occluded vocal tract exercises
(straw phonation, lip trills).

## Vocal rest
- **Relative rest** (minimal, gentle speaking) on days off and when tired.
- **Absolute rest** only on medical advice (e.g. after a haemorrhage).

## Warning signs → see a laryngologist (ENT voice specialist)
- Hoarseness lasting more than **2-3 weeks**.
- Pain when speaking or singing.
- Sudden loss of voice or of range (especially after a forceful sound —
  possible vocal-fold haemorrhage: **stop using the voice and get seen**).
- Reduced top range, breathy or "two-note" voice, increased effort.
- Coughing blood.
UK: BAPAM provides free clinics for performers; many theatres have access to
voice clinics. US: performing-arts laryngologists in major cities.

## Gotchas
- Pushing through a cold with laryngitis — swelling + force = injury risk.
- Lozenges with menthol that numb pain and mask damage.
- Self-diagnosing "nodules".

## Verify
- [ ] Daily hydration/steam routine.
- [ ] Throat clearing replaced with swallow/sip.
- [ ] Know where to get a laryngoscopy quickly.
