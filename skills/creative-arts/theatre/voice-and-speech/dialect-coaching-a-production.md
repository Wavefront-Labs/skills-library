# Dialect coaching a production

## Pre-rehearsal
1. Meet the director: which accents, how strong, for which characters?
   Consistency of the world (e.g. all Irish characters from the same county?)
   and **intelligibility** priorities for the audience.
2. Research and prepare **dialect packets** per accent: breakdown sheet
   (oral posture, lexical sets, consonants, prosody), source clip links with
   timestamps, practice sentences, and the script's key words transcribed (see
   `creative-arts.theatre.voice-and-speech.dialect-breakdown-with-lexical-sets`).
3. Record audio guides of the script's tricky words/lines for actors (or
   source-speaker recordings if possible).

## In rehearsal
- **Company session** in week 1: introduce each accent's features and
  oral posture; listen to sources together.
- **Individual sessions** (20-30 min) per actor, prioritised by accent load.
- **Attend runs**; take notes per actor: page/line, word, issue, fix
  ("p.12 'car' — dropped the r, keep rhotic").
- Deliver notes via SM or in person; keep notes short and specific.
- Coordinate with the director: accent notes shouldn't contradict acting notes;
  if an accent obscures a key line, agree a softer version.

## Tech and previews
- Check accents in the space (mics change perception).
- Note at previews; audience comprehension feedback (talkbacks, front-of-house
  comments) may prompt adjustments.

## Maintenance in the run
- Accents drift (towards actor's own, or exaggerated) over a long run.
- Return visits (e.g. monthly) or written notes from SM/resident director.
- Understudy coaching before they go on.

## Gotchas
- Strong authentic accent that the audience can't follow — clarity first.
- Inconsistent accents across a family on stage.
- Coaching too late (after lines are learned in the wrong sounds).

## Verify
- [ ] Accent packets delivered before rehearsals.
- [ ] Every actor with an accent has an individual session.
- [ ] Notes from runs logged and checked.
- [ ] Maintenance plan agreed for the run.
