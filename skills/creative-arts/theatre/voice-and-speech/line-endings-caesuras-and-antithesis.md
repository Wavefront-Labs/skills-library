# Line endings, caesuras and antithesis

Peter Hall (*Shakespeare's Advice to the Players*), Cicely Berry (*The Actor and the
Text*) and Patsy Rodenburg (*Speaking Shakespeare*) all treat the verse line as
the unit of thought and energy.

## Line endings
- **End-stopped lines** (punctuation at the end) close a thought.
- **Enjambed (run-on) lines** carry the thought onward. Don't stop dead, but
  **honour the line end** — a tiny suspension or lift (Hall: a "sense break"
  without a breath) that keeps the shape audible.
- Often the most important word sits at the **end** of the line; keep energy
  through to it instead of falling off.
- Exercise: walk the speech, changing direction at every line ending; then at
  every punctuation mark. Feel how thought and line interact.

## Caesuras
- A **caesura** is a mid-line break — usually at punctuation — where the thought
  pivots. Typical place: after the 4th, 5th or 6th syllable.
- A line split by a strong caesura may contain two contrasting thoughts or a
  change of addressee.
- Shared lines often split at the caesura between speakers.

## Antithesis
Shakespeare thinks in opposites: *"To be, or not to be"*, *"Fair is foul, and
foul is fair"*, *"I come to bury Caesar, not to praise him"*.
1. **Find the pairs**: underline opposing words/ideas.
2. **Point them**: give each side of the antithesis its own energy — often by
   lifting the second term or contrasting pitch.
3. The audience follows argument through antithesis; missing it muddies sense.
Related rhetoric: lists (build each item), repetition (each repeat different),
questions (real questions to someone).

## Exercise sequence (Berry-style)
1. Speak the speech emphasising only the line-end words.
2. Speak only the antithetical pairs, then fill in.
3. Throw a ball to a partner on each antithesis term.
4. Speak normally keeping the discoveries.

## Gotchas
- Breathing at every line end (choppy) or never honouring them (prose-like).
- Stressing pronouns instead of the antithetical content words.
- Ignoring punctuation differences between editions.

## Verify
- [ ] Every antithesis in your speech underlined and audibly pointed.
- [ ] Line endings honoured without breaking sense.
- [ ] Caesuras marked with what pivots there.
