# Linklater: touch of sound and channel release

Kristin Linklater, *Freeing the Natural Voice* (1976; revised 2006). The progression
removes habitual tensions so the voice responds directly to thought and feeling.
Designated Linklater teachers teach it over months; this is a practice outline.

## 1. Physical awareness and spinal release
- Stand with feet hip-width; **drop down the spine** vertebra by vertebra,
  head heavy, knees softening; hang; build back up from the base, head last.
- Notice and release unnecessary tension (shoulders, jaw, belly).
- Find the "**spine supporting, body relaxed**" stance.

## 2. Natural breath
- Observe breath without controlling it. Allow the belly and lower ribs to
  release out on the in-breath.
- **Sigh of relief**: breathe in with an impulse of relief; release on a sigh
  with no sound. The breath responds to an impulse, not muscle effort.

## 3. Touch of sound
- On the sigh of relief, add a small **"huh"** sound — vibration "touching"
  the breath at the centre of the body (Linklater images it starting from the
  diaphragm area). Effortless, low-mid pitch, short.
- Then "hu-uh" longer; then let vibrations spread ("huh-mmm") into lips.
- Aim: sound originates from impulse + breath, not throat effort.

## 4. Freeing the channel
- **Jaw**: massage jaw hinges; let the jaw hang loose with hands; "huh-ahh" with
  the jaw dropped and released, without pushing it open.
- **Tongue**: tongue tip resting behind lower teeth; roll the middle of the
  tongue forward out of the mouth and back, loosening the root; then "hee-yah"
  with tongue rolling.
- **Soft palate**: yawn-stretch to sense lifting; "k-ah"/"ng-ah" to exercise
  lifting and dropping; whispered "ka-ka".
- Result: a wider open channel where vibration travels freely.

## 5. Into range and resonance
The progression continues into the resonating ladder, range (a three-octave
"stairway" for many voices), articulation and text (see
`creative-arts.theatre.voice-and-speech.resonance-work-for-actors`).

## Gotchas
- Doing it as exercise routine rather than responsive to impulse.
- Pushing the jaw open or the tongue out forcefully.
- Expecting quick results; habits change over weeks of daily practice.

## Verify
- [ ] Spine drop and build-up without holding breath.
- [ ] Touch of sound produced with no throat effort.
- [ ] Jaw and tongue release possible while sustaining sound.
