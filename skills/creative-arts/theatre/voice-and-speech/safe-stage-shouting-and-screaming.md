# Safe stage shouting and screaming

"Vocal violence" — shouts, screams, sobbing, choking sounds — must be
**choreographed** like a fight: repeatable, supported, and less than it sounds.

## Principles
1. **Support from the body**: engage the abdominal wall and back; breath under
   pressure from below, not squeezed at the throat.
2. **Open throat**: retract the false folds (the sensation of a silent laugh
   or the start of a yawn) to avoid constriction — Estill calls this
   **retraction**.
3. **Twang and forward placement**: a bright, "nasal-ish" ring (aryepiglottic
   narrowing, Estill's "twang") makes sound carry at lower effort than raw
   loudness.
4. **Call, don't shout**: use an elongated vowel on a higher pitch ("Hey-y!",
   "No-o!") — the calling voice — rather than a low, pressed, glottal attack.
5. **Illusion**: the audience perceives intensity from pitch, the body, and
   the moment *before* the sound. Use 70-80% of actual volume with full physical
   commitment. Breath sounds and hard consonants add perceived violence.
6. **Avoid hard glottal attacks** on loud vowels — start with a slight "h" or
   onset on a consonant.

## Choreographing a scream
- Fix pitch, length, vowel, and where in the phrase it peaks.
- Practise at half-volume first, then build.
- Sound designer can help: reinforcement or a sound effect for the most
  extreme screams (or the scream offstage on a mic).

## Crying and sobbing
- Breath-driven, not throat-squeezed: rhythmic diaphragm spasms on
  exhalation with an open throat.
- Lines through tears must still be intelligible — keep consonants.

## Recovery
- Cool down after the show: gentle sirens downward, lip trills, straw
  phonation (semi-occluded vocal tract exercise), humming.
- Hydration, steam, rest days with minimal talking.
- Warning signs: persistent hoarseness >2 weeks, pain, loss of top range, voice
  breaks → see an ENT/laryngologist and a voice specialist.

## Gotchas
- Learning the scream "for real" in rehearsal and only fixing later.
- Shouting while turned upstage — compensating by pushing.
- Smoking/vaping on stage scenes plus shouting — compound strain.

## Verify
- [ ] Every shout/scream has fixed pitch, vowel and volume level.
- [ ] No throat pain after a full run.
- [ ] Cool-down routine done after each show.
