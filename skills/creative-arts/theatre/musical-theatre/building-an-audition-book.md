# Building an audition book

**Goal:** a binder (or tablet) of prepared songs so that, for any musical theatre
audition, you can pull a cut that shows the right style, range and acting in
16 or 32 bars, with music an unfamiliar pianist can sight-read cleanly.

## 1. Categories to cover
A working book usually has one or two strong pieces in each:
- **Golden Age / legit** — classic book musicals, a legit or classical sound.
- **Contemporary musical theatre** — the last few decades of story songs.
- **Pop/rock** — for jukebox and rock musicals; a pop song in a theatrical arrangement.
- **Uptempo** and **ballad** in each style you'll be seen for.
- A **comedic** song, and a song in a **specialty** style you sell (country, R&B,
  patter, operetta) if your type calls for it.
Sixteen to twenty songs is common for a working performer; students start with
five or six. Unconfirmed: local casting fashions vary by city and year — ask
teachers and casting workshops what's current.

## 2. Choosing songs
- **Show your type and the role.** Sing something a casting director can picture
  you doing in that show, in a similar style and vocal range — not usually a song
  from the show itself unless asked.
- **Acting first.** Pick songs with a clear objective and a change within the cut.
- **Avoid overdone songs** for your type — panels hear the same few hits all day.
- Choose material that sits in your reliable range on a bad day; show the top note
  only if you hit it every time.

## 3. Making cuts
- **16 bars** (often 16 bars plus a pickup) and **32 bars** are the standard asks;
  "16" usually means ~30 seconds — end on a complete musical thought, even if
  it's 18 bars. Some rooms are strict; be ready to trim.
- Cut from a point with a clear start and build to a strong finish — typically the
  last section of the song.
- Keep a **full version** prepared too; some auditions ask for the whole song.

## 4. Preparing the sheet music
- Use a **legal, clean copy** in your key. Transpose with a professional or a notation
  program; pianists shouldn't transpose at sight.
- **Mark the cut clearly**: bracket start and end, write "START" and "END", cross out
  (lightly) what's skipped, and mark repeats and codas explicitly. Highlighting is
  helpful; avoid making the page unreadable.
- Put pages in a binder in **sheet protectors** (non-glare) or tape them
  accordion-style so they lie flat. Avoid double-sided pages where turns occur.
- Write the tempo and feel at the top ("bright 2", "rubato then in 4 at bar 9").

## 5. In the room
- At the piano: show the start, end, any tricky spot, and **tap or sing the tempo**
  quietly. Be brief and polite — the pianist is your partner.
- Walk to centre, slate if asked, give a breath, nod to start.
- If the tempo is wrong, adjust rather than stopping, unless it's unworkable.
- Act the song to a point just above the panel's heads unless told otherwise.

## Gotchas
- Last-minute changes to your book — never bring something you haven't sung with
  a pianist.
- Songs from the show being cast can be banned; check the breakdown.
- Lyrics for pop songs can be awkward out of context; set up the story briefly in
  your head, not to the panel.
- Copyright: photocopies for auditions are common practice but technically vary
  by licence; buy the sheet music you use.

## Verify
- [ ] At least one strong 16- and 32-bar cut in each category you're cast in.
- [ ] Each cut is in your key, clearly marked and readable at a glance.
- [ ] Every song in the book has been sung with a pianist recently.
- [ ] You can state the objective and change in each cut.
- [ ] The book lies flat and pages turn without fumbling.
