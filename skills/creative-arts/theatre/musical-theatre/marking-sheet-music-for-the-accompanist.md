# Marking sheet music for the accompanist

The audition pianist sight-reads your music. Clear music = a good accompaniment.

## The music itself
- **Full piano-vocal score** (vocal line + piano accompaniment), not a lead
  sheet or chord chart — unless the pianist is known to accept it.
- **Your key**: if you transpose, get a properly engraved transposition (e.g.
  Musicnotes in your key, or a professional transposition) — never ask the
  pianist to transpose at sight.
- Pages **double-sided in a binder** (or taped accordion-style) so there are
  minimal page turns; no plastic sleeves that glare (or use non-glare sleeves);
  pages in order with no loose sheets.

## Marking the cut
- Mark **start** clearly: bracket and write "START" (with any intro: "play
  bell tone + 2 bars intro" or "intro from bar 23").
- Mark **end**: "END" with a bracket; if you cut out the middle, cross out
  skipped bars and use clear arrows/"jump to" notes.
- Cut at musical phrases; bar numbers help.
- Typical asks: **16 bars** (often closer to 16-24 bars of music to end on a
  phrase) or **32 bars**; follow the casting notice.

## Tempo and style notes
- Write the **tempo** (metronome marking) at the top and any changes
  ("ritard", "a tempo", "colla voce" where you'll lead freely).
- Note **style**: "swing", "legato ballad", "pop-rock — drive".
- Circle fermatas you want held; mark breath pauses.

## In the room
1. Greet the pianist, show the start and end.
2. **Give the tempo** by quietly singing/tapping a bar or two — never conduct
   aggressively; point to tempo changes.
3. Thank the pianist afterwards.

## Gotchas
- Complex, unidiomatic arrangements (pop songs with dense piano parts) — find
  a pianist-friendly arrangement.
- Unmarked repeats and D.S./coda routes — rewrite as linear.
- Taping music with shiny tape that reflects light.

## Verify
- [ ] Correct key, clean engraving, double-sided in binder.
- [ ] Start/end and cuts clearly marked.
- [ ] Tempo and style noted; you can give tempo in one bar.
