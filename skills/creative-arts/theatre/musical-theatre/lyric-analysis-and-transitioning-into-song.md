# Lyric analysis and transitioning into song

From Joe Deer and Rocco Dal Vera (*Acting in Musical Theatre*) and common
conservatory practice: characters sing when speech is no longer enough — the
stakes rise beyond words.

## 1. Lyric as monologue
1. Type out the lyric as prose, removing repeats only where they don't add
   meaning.
2. **Speak it** as a monologue to a specific partner: who are you talking to,
   what do you want, what's the obstacle?
3. Mark **beats** (thought changes) and **action** each beat with a transitive
   verb (see `creative-arts.theatre.acting.actioning-a-script-with-transitive-verbs`).
4. Note **repeated lines**: each repetition must mean something new
   (insistence, realisation, pleading).
5. Find the **moment of change**: where does the character arrive somewhere new
   by the end?

## 2. Use the music
- The composer has already made acting choices: tempo, key changes (a modulation
  often = heightened stakes), rests (thought moments), dynamics, accompaniment
  texture. Read the score as stage directions.
- Breathe with the thought, and use rests as impulse moments.

## 3. Transition into song
- **The moment before** the song: what happens in the scene/dialogue that forces
  the character to sing?
- **Intros and vamps**: use the instrumental intro for a thought or decision —
  don't wait passively for your entry.
- Dialogue **over underscore**: speak in rhythm with the music without singing;
  know the cue bar for your entry; don't rush ahead of the band.

## 4. Focus and address
- **Focal points**: to a scene partner, to an imagined person (placed specifically
  just above the audience's heads), to the audience directly (some numbers are
  direct address), or inner (soliloquy). Choose deliberately per section.
- Avoid scanning vaguely; shift focus on beat changes.

## 5. The button
The final musical moment ("button") — land the final thought/action, hold
the final pose or look through the button, then allow applause before moving on
(the director decides how scenes flow out of numbers).

## Gotchas
- "Singing pretty" with no objective.
- Playing the mood of the music instead of the character's action.
- Unmotivated arm gestures — move when the thought requires it.

## Verify
- [ ] Lyric spoken as a monologue with objectives and beats.
- [ ] The moment-before and intro thought defined.
- [ ] Focal points chosen per section; button landed.
