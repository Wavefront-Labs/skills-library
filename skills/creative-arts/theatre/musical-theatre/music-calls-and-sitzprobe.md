# Music calls and sitzprobe

## Music calls (note-bashing)
- Early in rehearsals, the **musical director (MD)** teaches the score: ensemble
  numbers by part (sopranos, altos, tenors, basses), principals individually.
- **Bring**: the vocal score (or your part), pencil, recorder (if permitted).
- **Mark your score**: breaths (comma or tick), cut-offs ("cut on 3"),
  dynamics, vowel modifications, harmony switches, who sings what in divisi,
  consonant placements (e.g. final "t" on beat 4).
- Learn notes and rhythms accurately first; style and acting later.
- Ask the MD for **part-predominant tracks** for home practice.
- In company numbers, listen across sections for blend and tuning.

## Integrating with staging
- Music must be secure before choreography (it's hard to fix notes once
  moving); revisit with the MD after staging to check accuracy under movement.

## Sitzprobe
"Seated rehearsal" (German): the **first time the cast sings with the full
band/orchestra**, typically in a rehearsal room or the theatre, seated or standing
at mic stands, no staging.
- Focus entirely on music: balance, tempos, cues from the conductor, how the
  orchestration changes what you hear (cues you relied on from piano may be
  gone).
- Note new cue points ("my entry is after the oboe line").
- It's often emotional — the score comes alive — but stay focused.

## Wandelprobe
"Wandering rehearsal": the cast **performs the staging with the orchestra** for
the first time (on stage, no full tech), allowing the MD to adjust balance and
tempos with movement. Often happens around the start of tech.

## Gotchas
- Learning wrong notes from the cast recording (different keys, arrangements).
- Not marking breaths — running out in the staged version.
- Losing cues when the orchestra replaces the rehearsal piano.

## Verify
- [ ] Vocal score marked with breaths, cut-offs, harmony assignments.
- [ ] Notes and rhythms secure before staging.
- [ ] New orchestral cues noted at sitzprobe.
