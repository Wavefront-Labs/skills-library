# Legit singing and eight-show stamina

## Legit
"Legit" = classically influenced musical-theatre singing (Rodgers and
Hammerstein, Lerner and Loewe, Bernstein, Sondheim's legit roles, operetta):
- Balanced registration with head-dominant production for women (legit soprano
  roles often sit high, to around G5-C6), a rounded, resonant tone for men.
- **Lower larynx**, more vertical vowel space, **consistent vibrato**,
  legato line.
- Diction still crisp: consonants clear but not interrupting the legato.
- Style: phrasing to the lyric (not operatic), honour the composer's markings.
Exercises: legato vowel slides, messa di voce (swell/diminish), arpeggios on
pure vowels, Italian art-song style études; keep speech-clear text.

## Eight-show week stamina
West End and Broadway commonly run **eight shows a week** (two matinee days).
- **Pacing**: map the vocally heaviest moments; sing them fully, but avoid
  pushing elsewhere; agree with the MD where you can "mark" in rehearsals
  (not in shows).
- **Marking in rehearsal**: singing lightly/an octave down when allowed
  (tech, spacing calls) to save the voice.
- **Daily routine**: warm-up 15-30 min before the show; cool-down after; steam;
  hydration; sleep 7-9 hours; manage speaking on two-show days (minimal
  backstage chatter).
- **Days off**: relative vocal rest, not loud socialising.
- **Illness**: tell the company manager early; know understudy procedures;
  follow medical advice — singing through laryngitis risks injury.
- **Physical fitness**: cardio and core strength support stamina.

## Gotchas
- Pushing to match the first week's adrenaline levels for months.
- Over-singing in sound checks and rehearsals on show days.
- Neglecting the speaking voice (it fatigues the singing voice).

## Verify
- [ ] Legit tone with consistent vibrato and clear text.
- [ ] Written show-day routine (warm-up, cool-down, rest).
- [ ] Heavy moments mapped with pacing plan.
