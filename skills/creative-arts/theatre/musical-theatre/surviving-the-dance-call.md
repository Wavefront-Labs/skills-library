# Surviving the dance call

## Preparation
- Read the notice: style (jazz, tap, ballet, contemporary, hip-hop), shoes
  required (jazz shoes, character heels, tap shoes, sneakers), "bring a
  ballet barre-ready outfit"?
- **Clothes**: fitted, shows body lines; a pop of colour helps the panel
  remember you; hair secured off the face.
- Warm up thoroughly before the call; arrive early.
- Headshot/CV, your number pinned where asked.

## Learning the combination fast
1. **Stand where you can see the choreographer/dance captain** — mid-rows,
   rotate positions between run-throughs if allowed.
2. Learn in **counts**: "5, 6, 7, 8 — 1 and 2..." Say the counts and
   steps in your head ("ball change, kick, turn on 3").
3. Learn **feet and direction first**, then arms, then style/dynamics.
4. **Mark** (small, low energy) while learning to save energy; go full out
   when asked.
5. Ask a brief question if something is unclear when the choreographer invites
   questions — not mid-teach.
6. Repeat in your head during breaks; watch the other groups' runs to
   reinforce.

## Performing it
- Groups of 3-6 perform; **perform** to the panel: face, character, energy —
  musical theatre wants storytelling, not just steps.
- If you go wrong, **keep going** with conviction; recover on the next count of 8.
- Style matters: show the style asked (Fosse isolation, ballet line, hip-hop
  groove).

## Cuts and callbacks
- Panels make cuts after groups; thank them and leave gracefully if cut.
- Those kept may learn a second combination, do a turn/kick/leap check
  (across the floor), or be asked to sing.

## Gotchas
- Standing at the back behind tall dancers.
- Going full out early and exhausting yourself.
- Wrong shoes for the style (sneakers for a tap call).

## Verify
- [ ] Shoes and outfit match the notice.
- [ ] Combination learned in counts with direction changes.
- [ ] Performed with character and recovered from any mistake without stopping.
