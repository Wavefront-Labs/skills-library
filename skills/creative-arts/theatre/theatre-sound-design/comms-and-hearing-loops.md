# Comms and hearing loops

## Intercom (comms)
- **Partyline** systems (Clear-Com, Tecpro, RTS) on 2-wire/4-wire; digital/IP
  matrix (Clear-Com Eclipse/Arcadia, Riedel) and **wireless beltpacks** (Clear-Com
  FreeSpeak, Riedel Bolero).
- Channel plan example: Ch A — DSM/LX/Sound/Fly/Auto (cue calling); Ch B —
  stage crew/SM; Ch C — followspots; separate channel for automation if busy.
- Positions: DSM desk, LX and sound ops, fly floor, automation, each wing,
  followspot booths, wardrobe/quick-change areas.
- **Wireless** beltpacks: coordinate RF with mics; licences.
- **Show relay**: stage mic feed to dressing rooms and backstage speakers; DSM
  **paging** (calls) over it (see half-hour calls).
- Cue lights system as backup for noisy positions.

## Paperwork
- **Speaker plot** and **system block diagram**: every source, console,
  processor, amp, speaker, network; signal flow; IP addresses (Dante);
  patch lists.
- Comms channel sheet and beltpack allocation.

## Assistive listening
- **Induction (hearing) loop**: a wire loop around the auditorium (or areas)
  driven by a loop amplifier; hearing aids switched to T-setting receive the
  feed. Install to IEC 60118-4 performance; test with a loop listener.
- **Infrared** systems (Sennheiser, etc.) with headsets/neck loops issued at
  FOH; good for privacy and directional zones.
- **RF/Wi-Fi assistive listening** (e.g. app-based).
- Feed: a dedicated mix (clear dialogue, reduced music/effects) from the console.
- Legal context: UK Equality Act 2010 reasonable adjustments; US ADA requires
  assistive listening systems in assembly areas.
- Signage at FOH and daily test.

## Gotchas
- Too many people on the cue-calling channel.
- Loop spill into adjacent rooms or interference with guitar pickups.
- Assistive feed just a copy of the main mix (music too loud).
