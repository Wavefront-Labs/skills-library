# Music licensing for theatre

Not legal advice; rules vary by country and contract. Check with the producer and
the rights holders.

## Two copyrights in a recording
1. **The composition** (music and lyrics) — owned by songwriters/publishers.
2. **The master recording** — owned by the record label/artist.
Using a commercial recording needs permission for **both**.

## Background vs dramatic use
- **Non-dramatic ("small rights") use** — music played as background (e.g.
  preshow, interval) may be covered by the venue's blanket licences from
  performing-rights organisations: **PRS for Music** (compositions) and **PPL**
  (recordings) in the UK (now jointly via "TheMusicLicence"); **ASCAP/BMI/SESAC/
  GMR** (compositions) in the US — sound recordings in US live venues aren't
  covered by a public performance right in the same way; check.
- **Dramatic use ("grand rights")** — music woven into the drama (underscore
  to action, a song a character sings, choreography to a track) usually falls
  outside blanket licences and needs **direct permission** from the publisher
  (and label for recordings). PRS in the UK has specific rules for "dramatico-
  musical" works and theatrical use of music; check the venue's licence terms.
- Recorded/streamed performances need **sync** licences.

## Process
1. List every piece of music with title, composer, publisher, recording,
   duration and how it is used (cue sheet).
2. Contact publishers (via PRS/ASCAP databases) and labels for permissions;
   expect fees and time (weeks).
3. Keep written permissions; credit in the programme as required.
4. Submit **music cue sheets/returns** to the PRO where required (the
   venue often reports usage).

## Alternatives
- **Commission original music** (composer contract: fee, rights, royalties,
  credit; who owns the recordings).
- **Production music libraries** with clear theatre licences.
- Public-domain compositions recorded by your own musicians (the recording is
  then yours).

## Gotchas
- Assuming the venue's PRS licence covers a song a character sings.
- Licensing the composition but not the master.
- Touring: permissions must cover all territories and dates.

## Verify
- [ ] Music cue sheet with use type for every track.
- [ ] Written permissions for dramatic uses and masters.
- [ ] Programme credits and returns completed.
