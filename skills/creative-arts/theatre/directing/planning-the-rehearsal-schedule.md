# Planning the rehearsal schedule

## Work backwards
Fix dates first: first rehearsal, move into the theatre, tech start, first
preview, press night. Then allocate phases (example for 4 weeks):
- **Week 1**: table work, first blocking of Act 1.
- **Week 2**: block Act 2; off-book date at end of week.
- **Week 3**: work scenes in detail; **stagger-through** (first stop-start run)
  mid-week; first full run end of week.
- **Week 4**: runs (at least 3-4 full runs), notes, fight/music/dance polish;
  designer and LD at a run.
Musicals add music calls (week 1 heavy), dance calls, and a sitzprobe before
tech.

## Allocation procedure
1. Use the **French-scene / character-scene chart** from the SM (who is in
   each unit) to group scenes by cast — avoids calling actors who sit idle.
2. Estimate hours per scene by density, not page count (fights, crowd scenes,
   songs take longer). Rough guide for blocking in straight plays: about 1 hour
   per 2-4 pages.
3. Run parallel calls (AD on a scene, MD on music, fight director on a fight)
   when rooms and people allow.
4. Schedule specialists early (fight director, intimacy director, dialect coach,
   movement director) — they're often booked for specific days.
5. Leave **buffer** sessions every week for catching up.
6. Respect union rules: maximum hours per day and week, breaks, rest between
   calls, days off (check current Equity/UK Theatre or AEA agreement).
7. Publish a weekly provisional plan and the **daily call** the day before via
   the SM.

## Gotchas
- Leaving Act 2 under-rehearsed because Act 1 overran — protect time.
- First run too late (no time to fix what the run reveals).
- Calling the whole company all day "just in case" — costly and demoralising.

## Verify
- [ ] Dated phases from day one to press night.
- [ ] Every scene has blocking, detail and run time allocated.
- [ ] Specialist calls booked.
- [ ] Schedule checked against union hours.
