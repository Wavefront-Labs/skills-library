# Concept statement and design meetings

## The concept statement (one page)
Answer, in plain language:
1. **Why this play now** — what it says to this audience in this place.
2. **The central question or image** of the production (one sentence).
3. **World**: period, place, how realistic or abstract; the rules of the world
   (e.g. "the house is always present", "no props are mimed").
4. **Actor-audience relationship**: proscenium distance, direct address,
   immersion.
5. **Tone and tempo** references (paintings, films, music) — not a list of
   effects.
6. **Non-negotiables** (doubling, live music, a flood in Act 2) that drive
   budget and design.
Avoid design solutions in the statement; leave room for designers.

## Design process milestones (typical, 12-16 weeks before tech)
1. **First meeting** — director + designers (set, costume, lighting, sound,
   video, movement) + production manager. Share the statement and research;
   designers respond with their readings.
2. **Research and response** — mood boards, sketches; director picks
   directions, not finished answers.
3. **White card / preliminary model** — set designer presents; PM costs it.
   Check sightlines, entrances/exits, scene changes, storage, budget.
4. **Final model and costume drawings** — sign-off date (often 6-10 weeks before
   rehearsals start) so build can begin. Changes after this cost money.
5. **Model showing** to company on day one of rehearsal.
6. **Production meetings** weekly through rehearsals.

## Running a design meeting
- Agenda circulated in advance; one decision list at the end.
- Speak in terms of the story and the actor's need, not "make it blue".
- Ask "what does this cost us in time/money/safety?" with the PM present.
- Write and circulate decisions within 24 hours.

## Gotchas
- Changing the concept after model sign-off — the most expensive mistake in
  theatre.
- Designing for a different venue's sightlines; insist on venue plans early.
- Ignoring lighting and sound until late; involve them at first meeting.

## Verify
- [ ] Concept on one page; every designer has read it.
- [ ] Dated milestones agreed with the PM.
- [ ] Final model signed off and costed before build.
