# Viewpoints Composition assignments

Bogart and Landau (*The Viewpoints Book*, part 2): **Composition** is the practice of
creating short theatre pieces quickly in groups, using a list of required
ingredients. Viewpoints is the alphabet; Composition is writing with it.

## Building an assignment
1. **Source**: a scene, a theme, an image or a question from the play.
2. **Ingredients list** (5-15 items), e.g.:
   - A revelation of space.
   - 15 seconds of unison movement.
   - An object used in three different ways.
   - A broken expectation.
   - A sound made by the group.
   - Three lines of text from Act 2, one repeated.
   - A moment of stillness lasting at least 10 seconds.
   - A specific architectural feature used.
3. **Constraints**: duration (2-5 minutes), group size (3-6), time to build
   (often 30-60 minutes; short time prevents over-discussion).
4. Optional structure: beginning-middle-end; a set piece of music.

## Running it
- Groups build simultaneously in separate spaces.
- **Showings**: each group performs; viewers note what moments struck them
  (not what they meant).
- **Harvest**: the director identifies moments to keep or develop for the
  production — specific images, transitions, physical vocabularies.
- Repeat with new ingredients targeting the scenes that need staging ideas.

## Vocal viewpoints
The vocal counterparts — pitch, dynamic (volume), acceleration/deceleration,
silence, timbre — plus tempo, duration, repetition, shape, gesture,
architecture applied to sound. Use them as ingredients ("a section where pitch
rises continuously", "silence as an event").

## Gotchas
- Too many ingredients for the time → rushed, literal pieces.
- Groups explaining their pieces before showing — let the work speak.
- Harvesting nothing: always record (video or notes) what to keep.

## Verify
- [ ] Assignment has a source, an ingredients list and a duration.
- [ ] Build time is limited and enforced.
- [ ] At least one moment from each showing recorded for possible use.
