# Training the physical Viewpoints

From Anne Bogart and Tina Landau, *The Viewpoints Book* (2005), developed from
Mary Overlie's Six Viewpoints. An ensemble improvisation vocabulary for time and
space; used for training, generating staging and building ensemble.

## The nine physical viewpoints
**Time**
1. **Tempo** — how fast or slow a movement happens.
2. **Duration** — how long a movement or state continues before changing.
3. **Kinesthetic response** — a spontaneous reaction to movement outside you
   (someone passes, you start walking).
4. **Repetition** — repeating something inside (your own movement) or outside
   (someone else's).

**Space**
5. **Shape** — the contour of the body (lines, curves, angles), alone or with
   others.
6. **Gesture** — *behavioural* (everyday, realistic) or *expressive* (abstract,
   inner feeling, symbolic).
7. **Architecture** — the physical environment: walls, floor, doors, light,
   texture.
8. **Spatial relationship** — distance between bodies and objects.
9. **Topography** — the floor pattern, the landscape you draw moving through space.

## Training sequence (first sessions)
1. **Soft focus**: eyes relaxed, taking in peripheral vision; the ensemble
   becomes aware of the whole room.
2. **The grid**: walk only on straight lines (like a city grid), turning at
   right angles. Group starts and stops together without a leader (listening).
3. Introduce viewpoints **one at a time** on the grid: e.g. vary tempo only
   (1-5 scale), then duration (change only when the "group sense" says),
   then kinesthetic response (move only in response).
4. **Lanes** exercise: participants in parallel lanes running/walking/jumping/
   falling while practising kinesthetic response and tempo.
5. Add shape and gesture, then architecture (use the room's surfaces),
   spatial relationship (proximity), topography (draw patterns).
6. **Open Viewpoints**: all nine available, 10-20 minutes; a facilitator
   side-coaches ("notice the architecture", "duration — let it last").
7. Debrief: what moments were compelling? Why? (Usually: clear
   spatial relationship, sudden tempo change, strong repetition.)

## Applying to a production
- Use Open Viewpoints on the set or a taped floor plan to discover staging,
  then fix what works.
- Combine with Composition assignments (see
  `creative-arts.theatre.directing.viewpoints-composition-assignments`).

## Gotchas
- Self-indulgent movement: keep attention outward on the group.
- Skipping soft focus/grid: the ensemble listening never develops.
- Side-coaching too much; let the group find things.

## Verify
- [ ] Group can start/stop together on the grid without a leader.
- [ ] Each viewpoint practised in isolation before Open Viewpoints.
- [ ] Debrief names viewpoints that made compelling moments.
