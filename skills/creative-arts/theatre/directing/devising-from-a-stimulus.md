# Devising from a stimulus

Devised theatre builds the performance text through the ensemble rather than
from a pre-written script. Practices drawn from Frantic Assembly (*The Frantic
Assembly Book of Devising Theatre*), Complicite and Forced Entertainment.

## 1. Choosing the stimulus
A stimulus is the seed: a photograph, a news story, a poem, an object,
a piece of music, a question ("what do we owe our parents?"), a place.
Good stimuli are **specific and open**: specific enough to anchor, open enough
for multiple readings.

## 2. Research phase
- Each member brings material: facts, interviews, images, personal
  testimony (by consent), songs.
- Build a shared wall (physical or digital) of material and questions.

## 3. Generative tasks
Set short, constrained tasks — constraints produce invention:
- "In threes, make a 60-second piece showing the moment before the photograph,
  using no words and one chair."
- "Each write a letter from a character in the story; read them over this
  music."
- "Create a sequence of six gestures for 'waiting'; teach it to another group."
- "Hot-seat": interview a performer in role.
- Physical tasks (e.g. Frantic's chair duets, see
  `creative-arts.theatre.stage-movement-and-combat.frantic-assembly-chair-duets`).
Time-box (10-30 min), show back, discuss what was striking.

## 4. Record everything
Video every showing, label it (date, task, group), keep a "material bank"
document with one line per fragment.

## 5. Sorting
Group fragments by theme, character, image or tone; identify what the piece
seems to be *about* (often not the original stimulus). This feeds structuring
(see `creative-arts.theatre.directing.structuring-devised-material`).

## Gotchas
- Endless discussion instead of tasks — get on the floor.
- Personal testimony used without consent or care.
- Losing material because nobody recorded it.

## Verify
- [ ] Stimulus is specific and agreed.
- [ ] Each session includes timed generative tasks and showings.
- [ ] Material bank exists with labelled recordings.
