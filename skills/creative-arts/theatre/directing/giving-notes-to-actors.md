# Giving notes to actors

## Taking notes during a run
- Sit where you can see; use a notebook or laptop with a light hood; an
  assistant can write while you dictate.
- Format each note: **time/page — actor — note** ("p.34 — Jo — the slap lands
  late").
- Note positives too; they keep work you want.

## Shaping the notes session
1. **Prioritise**: storytelling and safety first, then clarity, then polish.
   A run might produce 80 notes; give 20-30.
2. **Group**: general company notes first (energy, pace), then scene by scene in
   order.
3. **Make notes playable** (actionable verbs, circumstances, objectives):
   - Result note: "Be angrier." → Playable: "She has just insulted your
     mother — make her apologise."
   - "Faster" → "Pick up your cues; you already know what you'll say."
4. **Short and specific.** One idea per note; refer to the moment precisely.
5. **Private notes privately** — anything personal, sensitive or about
   behaviour.
6. **Technical notes** (light, sound, set) go to the creative team in a separate
   session or written via SM.
7. **Invite questions** but avoid debating every note in the full company; offer
   a one-to-one.

## Late process (tech and previews)
- Notes get shorter; often written notes via SM email plus a 30-minute
  session before the next show.
- Fix — don't change — after freeze (see
  `creative-arts.theatre.directing.cue-to-cue-and-previews`).

## Gotchas
- Line readings ("say it like this") — usually counterproductive; offer a
  reason instead. Exception: exact rhythm in comedy or verse, by agreement.
- Contradicting another creative's note publicly — align first.
- Notes that are really your own unease — check whether the story is unclear.

## Verify
- [ ] Notes are prioritised and grouped.
- [ ] Every note is a playable action, circumstance or concrete technical fix.
- [ ] Private notes delivered privately.
- [ ] Previous notes checked in the next run.
