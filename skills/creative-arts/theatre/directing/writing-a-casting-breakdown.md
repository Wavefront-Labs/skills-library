# Writing a casting breakdown

A breakdown is the document agents and actors see. It is published through
casting platforms (Spotlight, Backstage, Casting Networks) or sent by the
casting director to agents.

## Contents
**Production header**
- Title, author, company, director, casting director.
- Rehearsal dates, performance dates, venue(s), tour dates.
- Contract and pay (e.g. Equity/UK Theatre or ITC rates, AEA contract type);
  say if it's unpaid/profit-share — many platforms require pay info.
- Audition dates and format (in person, self-tape first).

**Per role** (3-5 lines each)
- Name, **playing age range** (e.g. 30-40), gender if the role requires it.
- One-line character essence: who they are and what they want in the story.
- Skills required (accent, singing range and style, instrument, stage combat,
  dance level, mover). Be specific: "strong legit soprano to B5".
- Content notes: nudity, intimacy, violence, smoking — must be disclosed in
  advance.
- Doubling.

**Inclusion**
- State which roles are open to actors of any ethnicity, gender, or with
  disabilities; describe characteristics only where the story needs them.
- Offer access support for auditions (step-free, BSL interpreter, extra time).

## Process with a casting director
1. Director and CD read the play and discuss each role (who could play it,
   wish lists).
2. CD drafts, director approves.
3. CD releases to agents; submissions close on a stated date.
4. CD shortlists; director reviews self-tapes or attends first round.

## Gotchas
- Vague essences ("quirky, fun") — they attract everyone.
- Hidden requirements (nudity revealed at callback) — breaches trust and often
  union rules.
- Unrealistic age ranges ("20-25 playing 15") without saying so.
- Missing dates — actors can't confirm availability.

## Verify
- [ ] Every role has age range, essence, skills and content notes.
- [ ] Dates, venue and pay are present.
- [ ] Inclusion and access statements included.
