# Composition and picturisation

From Alexander Dean and Lawrence Carra, *Fundamentals of Play Directing*: 
**composition** is the arrangement of bodies for clarity and emphasis;
**picturisation** is composition that tells the story — each stage picture shows
the relationships at that moment, readable with the sound off.

## Tools of emphasis (proscenium)
- **Body position** relative to audience, strongest to weakest: full front,
  one-quarter, profile, three-quarter back, full back.
- **Area**: downstage centre is strongest; down right/left next; upstage
  weaker (but upstage centre gains strength if others face it).
- **Level**: standing over sitting over lying; risers and stairs.
- **Plane**: closer to the audience reads stronger, unless isolated upstage.
- **Contrast**: one person standing among seated, one still among moving.
- **Focus lines**: everyone looking at one actor gives them the stage.
- **Space**: an actor separated from a group gains emphasis.
- **Triangles**: three actors at different depths and levels make a dynamic
  picture; avoid straight lines parallel to the footlights.

## Picturisation procedure
1. For each beat, write the relationship in one sentence ("she's isolated;
   the family closes ranks").
2. Find the picture that shows it: distance between bodies, who is higher, who
   faces whom, who's in the strongest area.
3. Move between pictures with **motivated movement** — every cross happens
   because the character wants something.
4. Check from several seats (centre, far side, balcony).
5. "Freeze test": stop the scene at random; does the picture tell the story?

## Gotchas
- Lines of actors across the stage ("washing line") — flat, nobody has focus.
- Upstaging: an actor further upstage forces others to turn their backs;
  sometimes useful, usually accidental.
- Over-composition: pretty pictures without motivation look posed.
- Thrust/round need different rules — see
  `creative-arts.theatre.directing.staging-for-thrust-and-in-the-round`.

## Verify
- [ ] Each beat has a picture you can describe in one sentence.
- [ ] Focus is unambiguous at key moments.
- [ ] Every move is motivated.
- [ ] Freeze test passes from at least three seats.
