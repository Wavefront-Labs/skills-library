# Table work and the first read-through

## Day one (typical order)
1. **Meet and greet** — company, creative team, production staff, venue staff.
   Name round with roles and pronouns.
2. **Housekeeping** by the SM: schedule, contact sheet, fire exits, rehearsal
   room rules, Equity/AEA deputy election if applicable.
3. **Company agreement** (increasingly common): how we work together, how
   to raise concerns, the reporting route.
4. **Director's introduction** — why this play, the world, the key question (5-10
   minutes; don't lecture).
5. **Model showing** by the set designer; costume designs shown.
6. **First read-through** — seated round the table. Ask actors to read simply,
   not perform. Stage directions read by the ASM/AD.
7. Short reactions afterwards; then end on time.

## Table work (days 1-5 depending on play)
Options to mix:
- **Read and stop**: read a scene, stop to discuss facts, questions, events
  (see `creative-arts.theatre.directing.facts-and-questions-pre-rehearsal-analysis`).
- **Research presentations** — actors or dramaturg present short research on
  period, place, issues (5 min each).
- **Paraphrase reading** — actors say each line in their own words; reveals
  misunderstandings.
- **Actioning** round the table (see
  `creative-arts.theatre.acting.actioning-a-script-with-transitive-verbs`).
- **Glossary** — dramaturg's word list for unfamiliar terms.

## How long?
- Naturalistic dense plays (Chekhov, Ibsen): often a week or more.
- Comedies, musicals: often 1-2 days; get up quickly.
- Signs to stand up: the discussion becomes speculative and repetitive; actors
  get restless.

## Gotchas
- Letting the read-through become a performance judged by producers.
- One actor dominating discussion: use rounds, ask quieter actors directly.
- No decision record: AD or SM notes agreed facts for the rehearsal file.

## Verify
- [ ] Day-one running order circulated in advance.
- [ ] All agreed facts and decisions written up and shared.
- [ ] Every scene read and discussed before first blocking.
