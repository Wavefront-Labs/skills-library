# Facts and questions: pre-rehearsal play analysis

From Katie Mitchell, *The Director's Craft* (2009), part 1 "Preparing the text",
and David Ball, *Backwards and Forwards* (1983).

## 1. Facts and questions lists
Go through the play scene by scene and list:
- **Facts**: anything the text states as true and unarguable ("It is
  winter", "Masha is 22", "The house will be sold").
- **Questions**: anything uncertain that the facts raise ("How long has Masha
  been married?", "Who told her about the sale?").
Keep separate lists for **the backstory** (facts before the play starts) and
**the immediate circumstances** of each scene.
Then **research** the questions — period, place, social context — and decide
answers as a company. Mitchell does a version with the actors in week 1 so
everyone shares one reality.

## 2. Backstory timeline
Turn backstory facts into a dated timeline (years, months, the day of the play).
This fixes ages, durations and "what just happened" for each entrance.

## 3. Place and time per scene
For each scene: exact location (and the rooms around it), date, time of day,
weather, how much time has passed since the last scene.

## 4. Events and intentions
- **Event**: the moment something changes for everyone onstage (a news
  delivery, an arrival, a decision). Mark them; title each section between events.
- **Intention**: what each character wants in each section, phrased as
  "to get X to do Y". Intentions change at events.
This gives the rehearsal structure: you rehearse event to event.

## 5. Backwards and forwards (Ball)
Start from the **last event** of the play and ask "what caused this?" Trace
back event by event to the first; each event must be triggered by the one
before (the "domino" chain). Gaps in the chain show what you don't understand
yet. Then read forwards to check momentum: each event makes the next one
necessary.

## Output for rehearsals
A rehearsal file: facts/questions lists, timeline, scene place-and-time sheets,
an event list per scene. Share the factual parts with designers and the company.

## Gotchas
- Mixing opinion into facts ("she's manipulative" is an interpretation).
- Answering every question alone — leave some for the company to answer.
- Too much analysis delaying getting up on the floor; time-box.

## Verify
- [ ] Every scene has facts, questions, place/time and events listed.
- [ ] Backstory timeline is consistent with stated ages and dates.
- [ ] The backwards chain links the last event to the first with no gaps.
