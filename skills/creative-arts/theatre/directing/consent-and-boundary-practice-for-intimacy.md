# Consent and boundary practice for intimacy

Based on frameworks from Intimacy Directors and Coordinators (IDC), Theatrical
Intimacy Education (TIE) and Equity guidance. Use a qualified **intimacy director**
for any scene with nudity, simulated sex, or sustained intimate contact; the
director still sets the culture.

## Before rehearsal
1. **Disclose in casting** — breakdowns state nudity/intimacy content.
2. Contracts or riders spell out what is agreed (e.g. nudity scope).
3. Book the intimacy director for every session the scenes are worked, and
   for the cast's first boundary conversation.

## Boundary practice (each session)
1. **Context**: describe what the scene requires and why (story purpose).
2. **Boundary check-in**: each actor indicates areas of the body available
   or off limits to touch *today*. Methods: verbal, or touching their own body
   ("fence" gesture). Boundaries can change day to day.
3. **Pause signal**: an agreed word or gesture (TIE uses "**button**") that
   anyone can use to pause without explanation. Honour immediately, no
   questions required.
4. **Ask before touch** even for non-intimate contact in early rehearsals.
5. **Closing**: a brief check-out and de-role (see
   `creative-arts.theatre.acting.de-roling-and-cool-down`).

## Closed rehearsals and protection
- Only essential people in the room for nudity/intimate work (director,
  intimacy director, SM, actors). Signs on doors; no photos.
- **Modesty garments** (thongs, pads, flesh-coloured coverings) and **barriers**
  (cushions between bodies) provided by wardrobe.
- Robes available immediately offstage.
- Photography/filming of intimate scenes only with explicit written consent.

## Ongoing consent
- Choreography is fixed (see
  `creative-arts.theatre.directing.choreographing-intimacy`); changes require
  re-consent.
- Intimacy captain or SM runs **intimacy calls** before shows; replacements
  and understudies go through the whole process again.

## Gotchas
- "Just be natural" instructions — the opposite of safe practice.
- Treating a refusal as a casting problem; it's a staging problem.
- Power dynamics: actors may not feel able to say no to a director — the
  intimacy director provides a separate channel.
