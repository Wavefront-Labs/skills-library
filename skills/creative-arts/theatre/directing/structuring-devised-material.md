# Structuring devised material

## Procedure
1. **Scene cards**: write each fragment from the material bank on a card
   (title, length, who's in it, tone, key image).
2. **Find the spine**: what is the piece about now? Write one sentence. Possible
   structures:
   - Narrative (a story with a protagonist).
   - Thematic montage (variations on a question).
   - Journey (a place or time sequence).
   - Framed (a framing device — a lecture, a game show, a ritual — holding
     fragments).
3. **Lay out a running order** on a table or wall. Look for rhythm: alternate
   tempo, scale (solo/ensemble), text/physical.
4. **Run it** rough. Watch for what's missing (a bridge, an ending) and what's
   redundant.
5. **Cut** — most fragments, including favourites, won't make it. Keep a cut
   list in case.
6. **Write the connective tissue**: transitions, repeated motifs, a recurring
   image or song that ties the piece.
7. **Fix the script**: once structure is stable, transcribe text and a movement
   record. The SM's book becomes the authoritative version.
8. **An outside eye** (dramaturg or director not in the room) at a run gives
   fresh perspective on clarity.

## Ensemble building alongside
- Daily ensemble warm-up (games, physical work) keeps trust and shared
  vocabulary as structure firms up.
- Agree how decisions are made (director final say vs consensus) at the start.

## Timeline example (4-week devise)
Week 1 research and generation; week 2 generation and first sort; week 3
structure and runs; week 4 fixing and tech prep.

## Gotchas
- Structuring too late (week 4) leaves no time to rehearse the final shape.
- A collage with no spine — audiences need a reason to follow.
- Unclear authorship credits; agree in writing early.

## Verify
- [ ] One-sentence spine written and agreed.
- [ ] Running order run at least three times before tech.
- [ ] Fixed script/movement record exists for the SM.
