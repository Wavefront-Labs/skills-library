# Montage sequences

Goal: a sequence of short shots that compresses time (training, relationship over years,
building something) or builds an emotional idea, usually to music.

## Structure
1. **One idea**: what changes across the montage (skill improves, relationship grows, decline).
2. **Progression**: each shot shows a further step. Order by change, not by randomness.
3. **Repeated motif**: a recurring setup (the same location, action or framing) shown at
   different stages makes the change visible (Up's tie-straightening and savings jar).
4. **Turn**: many montages end with a turn (a failure, a death, a success) that resolves the idea.

## Boarding
- Write the beat list first (10-25 beats), one change per beat.
- Board each beat as a single, very clear image (montage shots are short - 1-3 s).
- Use match cuts and graphic matches between beats for flow.
- Mark music sync points if the track exists (cuts on beats or phrases).

## Timing
- Start with longer shots and shorten toward the climax for acceleration, or the reverse for
  a slowing, melancholy montage.
- Leave the final image longer.

## Failure modes
- Random collection of nice shots with no progression.
- Shots that need explanation (montage shots must read instantly).
- Too long - montages lose energy beyond about 1-2 minutes unless they are the film's core.

## Verify
- [ ] Beat list shows a clear change from first to last shot.
- [ ] A recurring motif or framing tracks the change.
- [ ] Each shot reads in under 2 seconds.
