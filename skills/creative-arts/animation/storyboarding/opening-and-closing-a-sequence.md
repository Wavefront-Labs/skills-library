# Opening and closing a sequence

Goal: sequences that start oriented and end with a clear button, linking smoothly to what
comes next.

## Openings
- **Orient**: where, when, who - establishing shot, or a close detail that is then opened up.
- **Contrast with the previous ending**: a loud ending into a quiet opening (or vice versa)
  refreshes attention.
- **Hook**: a question or unusual image that makes the audience lean in.

## Closings
- **Button**: a final beat that completes the sequence - a joke, a reaction, a decisive image.
- **Hook out**: a question or turn that pulls into the next sequence.
- **Bookend**: the closing image echoes the opening (same framing, changed situation) to show
  change.

## Transitions between sequences
- Match cuts, sound bridges (next sequence's sound starts early), or a hard cut for contrast.
- Fades for big time jumps or act breaks.

## Method
1. Look at the previous sequence's last panel and the next sequence's first panel (coordinate
   with the artists on neighbouring sequences).
2. Design the opening for orientation and the closing for a button or hook.
3. Check the transition in the reel.

## Failure modes
- Openings that leave the audience disoriented for too long.
- Sequences that end by fading out on nothing.
- Repeating the same transition type every time.

## Verify
- [ ] First 1-2 shots orient (or deliberately withhold with a payoff).
- [ ] Clear button or hook at the end.
- [ ] Transition into the next sequence checked in context.
