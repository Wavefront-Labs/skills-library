# Power staging

Goal: the audience reads who is in control from the staging, and sees control change hands.

## Levers
| Lever | Dominant | Subordinate |
|---|---|---|
| Height | higher in frame, standing | lower, seated, kneeling |
| Size | larger (closer to camera) | smaller |
| Camera angle | shot from below | shot from above |
| Facing | facing camera, open | turned away, profile, back |
| Space | owns open space, centre | cornered, edge, boxed in |
| Light | lit, clear | in shadow (or reversed for a menacing figure in shadow) |
| Movement | still, controlled | fidgeting, moving around them |

## Shifts
- Mark the moment in the scene where power changes; change at least two levers there (the
  subordinate stands up, the camera angle flips, a character steps into light).
- Gradual shifts: move characters across the plan over the scene.

## Method
1. Identify the status relationship at the start, middle and end.
2. Choose levers for each phase.
3. Block and board; test by removing dialogue - does the staging tell who wins?

## Failure modes
- Static staging in a scene where power changes.
- Conflicting levers (dominant character small, low and in shadow without intent).
- Overdoing it (melodramatic angles on every shot).

## Verify
- [ ] Power relationship readable with sound off.
- [ ] Turning point has a staging change.
- [ ] Levers consistent within each phase.
