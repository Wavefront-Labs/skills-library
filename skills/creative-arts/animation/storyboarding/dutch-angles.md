# Dutch angles

Goal: tilt the horizon to make the audience feel something is wrong - unease, madness, danger,
disorientation, or energetic dynamism in action.

## Choosing the tilt
- **Subtle (5-10 degrees)**: something is off; tension rising.
- **Strong (15-30 degrees)**: disorientation, villain's lair, a character losing control.
- **Extreme (45 degrees)**: action dynamism, comic-book energy (common in action anime and
  Spider-Verse).
- **Direction**: alternating the tilt direction across a shot/reverse dialogue heightens conflict
  (each character tilted toward or away from the other).

## Boarding
- Draw the frame rectangle level and tilt the world inside it - the horizon and verticals tilt.
- Note the tilt degree and direction on the panel.
- If the camera rolls during a shot (tilting into dutch), mark it as a camera move.

## When to use
- Tied to a story state: the world becomes unstable as the character does.
- Escalation: start level, introduce slight tilt, increase as tension builds.
- Return to level for resolution.

## Failure modes
- Dutch angles on every shot: the effect becomes noise and looks amateurish.
- Tilted shots cut against level shots of the same moment without a reason.
- Tilt that makes the important action exit or crowd the frame.

## Verify
- [ ] Each dutch shot is tied to a story state or action intent.
- [ ] Tilt degree escalates/resolves with the scene.
- [ ] Composition still has a clear focal point.
