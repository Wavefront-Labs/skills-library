# Beat boards and key story moments

Goal: before boarding hundreds of panels, illustrate the 5-15 moments that define a sequence
so everyone agrees on what it is about.

## Method
1. List the sequence's beats (from script or outline).
2. Pick the **key moments**: where the story turns, the emotional peak, the visual set-piece,
   the ending image.
3. Draw each as a single, well-composed panel (more finished than rough boards, often with
   value or color).
4. Caption each with the beat in one line.
5. Pitch them in order as a mini-story; get direction before boarding.

## What makes a strong beat panel
- Clear staging of the moment; readable emotion.
- Composition that could be a real shot.
- Mood (lighting/value) that suggests the sequence's tone.

## Uses
- Aligning director and story team on tone and structure.
- Feeding visual development and color script.
- Pitching development projects.

## Failure modes
- Too many beats (becomes a rough board).
- Illustrations that are pretty but not story moments.
- Skipping beat boards on complex sequences and boarding the wrong idea in detail.

## Verify
- [ ] 5-15 panels that tell the sequence's story in order.
- [ ] Each captioned with its beat.
- [ ] Director approves the beats before full boarding.
