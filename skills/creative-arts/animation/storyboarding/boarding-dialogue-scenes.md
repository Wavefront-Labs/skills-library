# Boarding dialogue scenes

Goal: dialogue scenes that are visually structured, not a monotonous back-and-forth of talking heads.

## Method
1. **Read for structure**: find the scene's turning point (McKee: the moment the value changes)
   and its beats.
2. **Open**: establish geography (wide or two-shot).
3. **Build**: move into OTS, then singles, then closer singles as tension rises.
4. **Turning point**: break the pattern - a new angle, a reveal, a character moves, the camera
   crosses (with a bridge), or a hold on a reaction.
5. **Resolve**: open back up (two-shot, wider) as the scene settles.

## Choosing who we see
- Not always the speaker: show the listener when their reaction matters more.
- Hold on one character across multiple lines when they are the scene's emotional centre.
- Use OS dialogue to keep a reaction on screen.

## Staging movement
- Give characters business or movement motivated by the scene (moving away when avoiding,
  stepping closer when pressing). Movement creates new setups naturally.

## Failure modes
- Alternating singles on every line from the first panel - nowhere to escalate.
- Constant cutting that chops performances.
- Identical setups for the entire scene with no turn.

## Verify
- [ ] Shot sizes escalate with the scene's intensity.
- [ ] The turning point has a visual change.
- [ ] Reaction choices follow the emotional centre, not just who speaks.
