# Leading lines

Goal: lines within the image that carry the viewer's eye to the subject. Mateu-Mestre's Framed
Ink treats this as a primary composition tool.

## Sources of lines
- Perspective lines converging to a vanishing point (place the subject near it).
- Architecture: roads, rails, fences, corridors, rooflines.
- Natural: rivers, ridges, branches, shadows.
- Characters: limbs, pointing, eyelines, a row of heads.
- Implied: the gaze of crowd characters toward the focal character.

## Line character (Block's line and shape)
- **Horizontal**: calm, stability.
- **Vertical**: strength, formality, confinement.
- **Diagonal**: energy, tension, movement - action scenes use more diagonals.
- **Curved**: softness, flow.

## Method
1. Decide the focal point.
2. Find or add lines in the environment that point to it (change layout, move the camera, add a
   prop line).
3. Remove or soften lines that lead out of frame or to unimportant areas.
4. Check: squint at the panel - do the strong lines converge on the subject?

## Failure modes
- Strong lines leading out of frame - the eye exits.
- Lines converging on empty space.
- A tangent where a line just touches the subject's outline - flattens depth (move it to clearly
  overlap or clear).

## Verify
- [ ] Main lines in the panel point to the focal point.
- [ ] No strong line leads the eye out of frame unintentionally.
- [ ] Line character (horizontal/vertical/diagonal) matches the mood.
