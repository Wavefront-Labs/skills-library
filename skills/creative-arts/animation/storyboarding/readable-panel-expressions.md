# Readable panel expressions

Goal: expressions that read instantly in small panels and on a pitch wall.

## Simplify to the essentials
- **Brows** carry most emotion: angle and height. Inner brow ends up = sadness/worry; down and
  in = anger; both up = surprise.
- **Eyes**: lid height (wide vs narrow), pupil direction.
- **Mouth**: corners up/down, open/closed, shape (Faigin's mouth types).
- Drop everything else (wrinkles, detail) unless the panel is a close-up.

## Support with the body and head
- Head tilt and angle: down and forward = shame or threat; back = pride or surprise.
- Shoulders: raised = fear/tension; dropped = defeat.
- At small sizes, the body pose often reads before the face - make them agree.

## Match shot size to subtlety
- Subtle emotions (doubt, suppressed anger) need a close-up to read; in a wide shot rely on
  pose.
- If an expression is essential to the story and the shot is wide, add a cut-in or push in.

## Practice tools
- Faigin's expression charts; draw each character's six basic emotions plus blends at thumbnail size.

## Failure modes
- Ambiguous mixed brows/mouth (reads as confused when the intent was angry).
- Expression only in the eyes in a medium-wide panel (invisible).
- Every character's expression identical in style despite different designs.

## Verify
- [ ] Shrink the panel to 25 %: expression still readable.
- [ ] Brows, eyes and mouth agree on the emotion.
- [ ] Shot size appropriate to the expression's subtlety.
