# Establishing shots

Goal: tell the audience where and when they are, and where characters stand relative to each
other, so following shots can be closer without confusion.

## What an establishing shot must show
1. **Location** - recognisable, with a landmark element that recurs in closer shots.
2. **Time and mood** - day/night, weather, lighting key.
3. **Geography** - positions of the characters and key props; the line of action for later
   coverage is set here.
4. **Scale** - human-size reference.

## Patterns
- **Classic**: extreme wide (exterior) -> wide (interior) -> medium -> close.
- **Re-establish**: return to a wide when characters move, someone enters, or after a long
  run of close-ups (the audience loses the geography).
- **Delayed establishing**: open on a close detail and pull back/cut wide later - creates
  curiosity or a reveal (the location is the punchline).
- **Establishing by movement**: a character crossing the space shows layout.

## Boarding tips
- Hold long enough to read (36-72 frames for a complex exterior); include camera notes if it
  drifts or pans.
- Draw the key landmark the same way in every board that sees it.
- Keep it compositionally simple; one focal point (the building, the character).

## Failure modes
- Skipping establishing shots in a new location: disorientation.
- Establishing shots so busy the focal point is lost.
- Establishing a geography and then breaking it in coverage.

## Verify
- [ ] A viewer can sketch a floor plan of the scene after the establishing shot.
- [ ] Characters' positions in closer shots match it.
- [ ] Re-establish after big moves or long close-up runs.
