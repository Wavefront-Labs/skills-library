# Editing the story reel

Goal: shape the timed boards into a reel whose pacing works; in feature animation the story
reel (historically the "leica reel") is the whole film in boards and is updated continuously
as boards are replaced by layout and animation.

## Pacing passes
1. **Watch through without stopping**; note where attention drifts and where confusion occurs.
2. **Cut redundancy**: beats that repeat information, establishing shots that are not needed,
   double reactions.
3. **Trim panel durations**: shorten setups, keep holds on emotional beats and jokes.
4. **Check rhythm**: alternate intensity (fast/slow, loud/quiet) across scenes; avoid long
   stretches at one tempo.
5. **Check transitions**: sequence endings into the next sequence's opening.

## Working with editorial
- Editors suggest structural changes (reordering scenes, cutting a sequence) - the reel is
  where the film is rewritten cheaply.
- Request specific new panels from story to fix cuts (a bridging shot, a reaction).

## Versioning
- Save each reel cut with a version and date; keep an EDL/sequence change log.
- Replace boards with layout and animation as they arrive; the reel's length becomes the
  production's shot-length budget.

## Failure modes
- Treating the reel as fixed and cutting only for length.
- Reels padded to feel "complete" - pace is judged too slow later.
- Losing track of which version the director approved.

## Verify
- [ ] A fresh viewer follows the story without explanation.
- [ ] Reel runtime within target.
- [ ] Versioned with a change log.
