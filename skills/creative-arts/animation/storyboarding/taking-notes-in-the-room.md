# Taking notes in the room

Goal: leave the pitch knowing exactly what to change and why. The handling of notes in the
moment determines whether the next version is better or merely different.

## In the room
1. **Listen first**; do not defend or explain unless asked a direct question.
2. **Write against panel numbers** ("p.34-38: confusing who has the key").
3. **Find the note behind the note**: a suggested fix ("add a close-up") often points to an
   underlying problem ("I didn't see the key"). Ask: "Is the issue that the key isn't clear?"
4. **Clarify priorities**: which notes are must-fix, which are ideas to try.
5. **Repeat back** a short summary at the end.

## Types of notes
- **Clarity** (audience confused): highest priority.
- **Emotion/character** (doesn't feel right).
- **Pacing** (too long/short).
- **Taste/ideas** (try a different gag) - try it, keep the old version available.

## After
- Type up notes the same day; send to the director/story supervisor for confirmation.
- Plan revisions by sequence order; flag knock-on effects to other sequences.

## Failure modes
- Arguing in the room.
- Implementing the literal suggestion and missing the underlying problem.
- Notes without panel references - lost context.

## Verify
- [ ] Notes recorded with panel numbers.
- [ ] Underlying problems identified for prescriptive notes.
- [ ] Summary confirmed with the director.
