# Cutting on action

Goal: cuts that the audience does not notice because their attention is carried by the movement
across the cut.

## Rules
1. **Cut during the movement**, not before or after: e.g. a character starts to sit in the wide,
   finishes sitting in the medium.
2. **Typical cut point**: about one-third into the action (after the start is seen, while the
   motion is fast), not at the extreme.
3. **Match direction and speed** across the cut.
4. **Change shot size or angle enough** (at least ~30 degrees or a clear size change) so it reads
   as a deliberate cut, not a jump.

## Animation-specific
- Boards should indicate the action continues: "CONT'D" or arrows carrying across.
- **Overlap frames**: animators often animate a few frames of the action beyond the cut point
  on both shots (handles) so editorial can adjust the cut by a frame or two.
- Hookup poses: the last frame of shot A and the first frame of shot B describe the same moment
  (or with a 1-2 frame advance for a crisper cut).

## Failure modes
- Cutting after the action completes - the new shot starts static and the cut is noticed.
- Pose mismatch at the cut (arm up in A, down in B).
- Repeating too much action (the audience sees the same move twice) unless it is a deliberate
  emphasis double-cut.

## Verify
- [ ] Each cut on movement falls during the action.
- [ ] Poses and directions hook up.
- [ ] Shot size/angle change sufficient to avoid a jump cut.
