# Rule of thirds placement

Goal: place subjects so the frame feels balanced and directional, and break the rule
deliberately when centred framing serves the story.

## The rule
- Divide the frame into thirds horizontally and vertically.
- Put key subjects (eyes, the focal character) on the lines or intersections.
- Place horizons on the upper or lower third, not the middle, unless symmetry is intended.

## Applying it in boards
- Draw a thirds overlay in the panel template (most boarding tools - Storyboard Pro, Krita,
  Photoshop - support grids or guide overlays).
- In close-ups put the eyes on the upper third line.
- Give the subject's look direction more space (look room) - subject on the left third looking right.

## When to break it
- **Centred / symmetrical**: power, formality, confrontation, isolation, or a deadpan
  graphic style (Kubrick, Wes Anderson).
- **Extreme edge placement**: tension, isolation, something about to enter.
- **Middle horizon**: reflections, symmetry, stillness.

## Aspect ratio
- Wide formats (2.39:1) give more horizontal space; thirds placement and negative space matter more.
- Vertical/social formats (9:16): thirds on the vertical axis dominate; keep faces upper third.
- Board on the delivery aspect ratio with the safe areas visible.

## Failure modes
- Everything dead centre without intent: static.
- Rigid thirds everywhere: formulaic.
- Subjects cut off by the delivery safe area.

## Verify
- [ ] Focal points on thirds or deliberately centred.
- [ ] Horizon placement intentional.
- [ ] Boards drawn at the delivery aspect ratio with safe areas.
