# Line and shape

Goal: control the lines and shapes in frames so they express mood and build visual structure.

## Lines
- **Direction**: horizontal (calm), vertical (stable, formal, oppressive when dense), diagonal
  (dynamic, intense, unstable).
- **Quality**: straight (rigid, mechanical), curved (organic, soft), irregular (chaotic).
- **Sources**: edges of objects, contrasts of tone, character movement paths, perspective lines.

## Shapes (Block's three basic shapes)
- **Circle**: least intense - soft, safe, innocent, friendly.
- **Square/rectangle**: stable, ordered, rigid.
- **Triangle**: most intense - aggressive, dynamic, dangerous.
Any frame's arrangement of objects, characters and negative space reduces to these.

## Contrast and affinity
- Affinity (same shape family across shots) = calm, unified.
- Contrast (circles in one place, triangles in another; or a triangle intruding in a circle
  world) = tension. Classic use: the hero's world built on circles, the villain's on triangles.

## Method
1. Decide the shape family for each location/character/sequence.
2. Pull the environment design, staging and composition toward it.
3. Increase diagonals and triangles toward intense moments.

## Failure modes
- Diagonals and triangles everywhere - no room to escalate.
- Shape language of a set contradicting the scene's mood unintentionally.

## Verify
- [ ] Shape family defined per location/sequence.
- [ ] Line direction intensity follows the story's intensity.
- [ ] Contrast used at turning points.
