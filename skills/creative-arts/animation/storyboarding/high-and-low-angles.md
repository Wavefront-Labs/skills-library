# High and low angles

Goal: use camera height deliberately - it changes how powerful or vulnerable a character feels.

## Meanings
| Angle | Horizon line | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Eye level | through the character's eyes | neutral, equal, identification |
| Low angle (camera below, looking up) | low in frame or below the character's knees | power, threat, heroism, scale |
| High angle (camera above, looking down) | high in frame | vulnerability, smallness, being watched |

Mateu-Mestre stresses placing the horizon line consciously: it is the camera height.

## Uses
- A villain introduced from low angle, the hero from eye level.
- Power shifts in a scene shown by switching angles (the bully filmed from low, then from high
  once defeated).
- Children's POV of adults: low angle.
- Isolation: high wide shot of a lone character.

## Drawing it
1. Place the horizon line first (camera height).
2. Figures: if the camera is at eye level, all standing adults' eyes sit on the horizon regardless
   of distance. In low angle, the horizon cuts lower on the body.
3. Verticals converge (up for low angle, down for high angle) when the camera tilts - three-point
   perspective.

## Failure modes
- Random angle changes with no story reason.
- Inconsistent horizon placement for the same setup.
- Extreme angles on every shot (dilutes the effect).

## Verify
- [ ] Each angle choice has a story reason noted.
- [ ] Horizon line consistent across panels of the same setup.
- [ ] Power dynamics of the scene visible in angle choices.
