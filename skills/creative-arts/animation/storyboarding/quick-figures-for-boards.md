# Quick figures for boards

Goal: figures that communicate pose, attitude and action in seconds. Story artists draw hundreds
of panels per sequence; speed with clarity matters more than finish.

## The board figure
1. **Line of action** - one stroke.
2. **Bean or box torso** - the "bean" (ribcage + pelvis as a bendable bean) shows twist and lean.
3. **Head** - a circle with a centre line and eye line for direction.
4. **Limbs** - single lines or tubes, with the hands and feet as simple shapes (mittens, wedges).
5. **Silhouette check** - is the action readable?

## Tips
- Draw the pose, not the anatomy - exaggerate attitude.
- Keep characters' signature shapes (head shape, hair silhouette, costume shape) so they are
  identifiable even rough.
- Draw at the panel's final size; tiny details are wasted.
- Use one brush and a fixed line weight; no erasing - redraw instead.
- Copy-paste and adjust repeated figures (same setup, minor change) in digital boards.

## Practice
- Daily 1-minute gestures from life or photo reference.
- Draw the same character in 20 attitudes on one page.

## Failure modes
- Over-rendering (slow, and it hides problems).
- Stiff, symmetrical figures standing straight.
- Characters indistinguishable from each other in rough form.

## Verify
- [ ] Each figure's action reads in silhouette at panel size.
- [ ] Characters identifiable by shape.
- [ ] Drawing time per panel fits the schedule (TV boards often 50-100+ panels per day in rough).
