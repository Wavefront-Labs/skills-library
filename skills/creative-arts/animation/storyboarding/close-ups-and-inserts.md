# Close-ups and inserts

Goal: bring the audience close when the emotion or a detail matters most, and save that power
for the moments that need it.

## Shot sizes
| Size | Frame | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Medium close-up (MCU) | chest up | dialogue, standard coverage |
| Close-up (CU) | head and shoulders / face fills | emotion, reaction, decision |
| Extreme close-up (ECU) | eyes, mouth, part of face | peak intensity, tension, comedy |
| Insert | object or hand detail | information the audience must see (a note, a key, a button) |

## When to cut in
- The emotional peak of a scene (escalate shot size as intensity rises).
- A decision or realisation the face must show.
- Information that cannot be read in a wider shot (insert).
- To isolate a character from the group (loneliness, threat).

## Framing
- CU: eyes on the upper third line; leave look room on the side they face.
- Headroom shrinks as shots get closer; in an ECU, crop the top of the head freely.
- Inserts: simple background, prop large and readable; match lighting and screen direction of
  the master.
- Hold inserts long enough to read text (about 1 frame per 3-4 letters, min 1.5 s).

## Failure modes
- Close-ups everywhere: no escalation left for the climax; the audience loses geography.
- Close-up of a reaction that the audience could read in a medium shot (wasted cut).
- Inserts that break continuity (hand, prop, lighting mismatch).

## Verify
- [ ] Close-ups cluster at the emotional peaks of the sequence.
- [ ] Every insert carries necessary information.
- [ ] Eyeline and screen direction match the surrounding shots.
