# Tonal contrast

Goal: use brightness (tone) to control intensity and attention, shot by shot and across the film.
Block treats tone as a component separate from color.

## Within a shot
- **High contrast** (blacks and whites, few mid-tones): intense, dramatic, graphic.
- **Low contrast** (narrow range): calm, soft, or oppressive (all dark) / airy (all light).
- **High-key** (mostly light), **low-key** (mostly dark).
- The eye goes to the highest contrast - place it at the focal point.

## Across shots and sequences
- Affinity (similar tonal range throughout) = steady intensity.
- Contrast between consecutive shots or sequences = jolt, intensity.
- Plan: e.g. low-contrast grey opening, rising contrast through conflict, peak contrast in the
  climax, softer resolution.

## Controlling tone
- Light placement and size (boards indicate light sources and shadow shapes).
- Set and costume values (production design: light vs dark surfaces).
- Atmosphere (fog lowers contrast).
- Grade in post.

## Method
1. Chart the intended tonal range per sequence against story intensity.
2. Board in values; pass the chart to color script and lighting.

## Failure modes
- Uniform mid-grey boards with no tonal intention.
- Maximum contrast in every shot.
- Tone that fights the scene's mood.

## Verify
- [ ] Tonal range chart exists and matches the boards.
- [ ] Highest contrast at the focal point.
- [ ] Contrast peaks where the story peaks.
