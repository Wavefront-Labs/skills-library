# Fight choreography in boards

Goal: fights the audience can follow and feel, which also advance story and character.

## Principles
1. **Story inside the fight**: who wants what, who is winning, and the turning point(s). A fight
   is a scene with physical tactics.
2. **Cause and effect**: every action gets a visible reaction; show the attack, then the result.
3. **Character through style**: a brawler, a trained fighter, a trickster fight differently;
   the hero's style may change after growth.
4. **Rhythm**: flurries (fast cuts, 1-2 s shots) alternate with pauses (circling, a held look,
   a breath) so the audience can recover and anticipate.
5. **Clarity of impacts**: frame the hit so contact and direction read - often a wider or
   side-on angle for the impact itself, with the close-up on reaction.

## Method
1. Write the fight as beats (8-20): exchange, advantage shifts, escalation, turn, finish.
2. Plan geography (overhead diagram with positions per beat).
3. Board each beat with a primary action panel and its result.
4. Mark screen direction: keep the attacker's direction consistent within an exchange; change it
   only when advantage shifts (a visual cue).
5. Add impact frames, camera shake notes and SFX notes.

## Failure modes
- Unreadable hits from too-close angles.
- No advantage shifts: monotonous exchange.
- Screen direction flipping randomly.

## Verify
- [ ] Beat list shows advantage shifts and a turn.
- [ ] Every attack has a visible result.
- [ ] Viewer can say who is winning at any moment.
