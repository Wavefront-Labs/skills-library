# Timing panels to a scratch track

Goal: turn boards into a timed animatic using temporary dialogue so the sequence's pacing can
be judged before final voice recording.

## Scratch track
- Record temp dialogue with story artists, editors or staff (Pixar famously used crew voices,
  some of which became final). Clean, close mic; one line per file or a full scene take.
- Read for intention and timing, not polish.
- Include breaths and pauses where the scene needs them.

## Timing panels
1. Lay the scratch audio on the timeline (Storyboard Pro, Premiere, Avid, Resolve, or the
   board tool's own timeline).
2. Place each panel at the start of its beat; hold it until the next beat.
3. Dialogue panels: change panels on thought/acting changes, not every word.
4. Action without dialogue: act it out with a stopwatch, or count frames for each action (e.g.
   a walk across frame at 12 frames per step).
5. Add **pauses** where reactions land - silence is timing too.
6. Rough SFX for impacts and doors to judge rhythm.

## Rules of thumb
- Minimum on-screen time for a panel the audience must read: about 1 s; complex: 2-3 s.
- Comedy holds: leave space after the joke.
- Cut points on action, as in the final edit.

## Failure modes
- Panels changing on every syllable.
- Action timed far shorter than it can be animated (a 1 s panel for a 4 s action).
- No pauses - wall-to-wall dialogue.

## Verify
- [ ] Animatic plays at intended total length.
- [ ] Action panels' durations match how long the action takes to animate.
- [ ] Reaction pauses present.
