# Jump cuts

Goal: understand jump cuts so they only appear when wanted.

## What a jump cut is
Two consecutive shots of the same subject from nearly the same angle and size, with a time gap -
the subject appears to jump. The **30-degree rule** says to change camera angle by at least 30
degrees (or change shot size substantially) to avoid it.

## Deliberate uses
- **Time compression**: a character pacing, waiting, searching - several jump cuts compress hours.
- **Comedy**: a character in different absurd positions in the same frame (the waiting gag).
- **Agitation / energy**: music videos, frantic states.
- **Sudden appearance**: a jump that makes a character pop in (horror).

## Boarding deliberate jumps
- Keep the camera **locked** (identical framing) so the jump is clearly intended.
- Vary the subject's position strongly between jumps.
- Note "JUMP CUT" in the panel.

## Fixing accidental jumps
- Change the angle (30+ degrees) or shot size.
- Insert a cutaway (reaction or insert) between the two shots.
- Combine the two into one continuous shot.

## Failure modes
- Near-identical framing across cuts in a normal dialogue scene.
- Deliberate jump cuts with slightly drifting framing - reads as an error.

## Verify
- [ ] Consecutive shots of the same subject differ by angle or size unless intended.
- [ ] Deliberate jump cuts use locked framing and are labelled.
