# Pans and tilts

Goal: rotate the camera horizontally (pan) or vertically (tilt) with a story reason, and board it
so layout and animation can build it.

## Types
- **Follow pan**: tracks a moving character (in 2D, a pan background slides behind a character
  animated in place or progressing).
- **Reveal pan**: moves from one subject to another to reveal information (the hero, then the threat).
- **Scenic pan/tilt**: shows scale (tilt up a tower).
- **Whip pan**: very fast pan with blur, often as a transition.

## Boarding
1. Draw an **extended panel** (wider or taller than the frame) with the start frame and end
   frame rectangles marked and an arrow between them, or
2. Draw two panels - start and end - with "PAN R" / "TILT UP" and an arrow.
3. Note duration and ease (e.g. "slow pan R, ease in/out, 4 s").
4. Mark character positions at start and end.

## Speed and strobing
- Slow pans of high-contrast vertical detail strobe at 24 fps. A common rule of thumb: an
  object should take at least ~5-7 seconds to cross the frame for smooth motion on a wide lens;
  faster pans need motion blur or should be whip pans.
- In 2D, pans on twos with characters on twos keep sync; mismatched rates jitter.

## Motivation
- Follow the character's movement or gaze; lead slightly ahead of a moving subject.
- Avoid pans with no subject - the audience waits for something.

## Failure modes
- Panning across nothing.
- Too fast for the detail level (strobing).
- Panning while the important acting happens off-centre.

## Verify
- [ ] Start and end frames drawn and labelled.
- [ ] Duration and ease noted.
- [ ] Motivation clear (follow, reveal, scale).
