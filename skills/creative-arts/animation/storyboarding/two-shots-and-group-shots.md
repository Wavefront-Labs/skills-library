# Two-shots and group shots

Goal: shots with several characters that show their relationship at a glance and keep focus clear.

## Two-shot variants
- **50/50 profile two-shot**: equal weight, confrontation or partnership (both in profile facing
  each other).
- **Depth two-shot**: one character near, one far; the near one dominates by size, the far one can
  dominate by facing camera (an actor's "upstage" position).
- **Side by side**: shared direction or goal (walking together, watching something).
- **Back-to-back / turned away**: estrangement.

## Group shots
- Arrange in triangles or varied heights; avoid a line-up of equal heads.
- The focal character gets the strongest position: facing camera, higher, isolated by space or
  light, at a thirds point.
- Others' eyelines and body angles point to the focal character.

## Holding vs cutting
- Hold the two-shot when the relationship itself is the subject or when timing between the
  characters matters (comedy beats).
- Cut to singles when one character's emotion needs isolating or the conversation intensifies.
- Return to the two-shot for the resolution.

## Animation cost
Two-shots with both characters acting cost twice; boards should flag which character is acting
and which is on a hold in each panel.

## Failure modes
- Equal emphasis when one character's reaction matters.
- Characters overlapping in silhouette.
- Group shots with flat, evenly spaced line-ups.

## Verify
- [ ] Focal character identifiable in every multi-character panel.
- [ ] Silhouettes separated.
- [ ] Relationship readable from placement alone.
