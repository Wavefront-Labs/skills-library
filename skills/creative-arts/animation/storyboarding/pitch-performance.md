# Pitch performance

Goal: make the director and team experience the sequence as it will play. A board pitch
(standing in front of the boards on a wall or a screen) is a performance; Glebas devotes a
section to it and Pixar describes pitching as a core story artist skill.

## Before the panel flips
- One sentence of context: where we are in the story, what the sequence must accomplish.
- Do not explain the whole thing - let the boards play.

## During
1. **Pace like the film**: hold panels as long as the moment would last; speed up action, slow
   down emotional beats. Flipping too fast is the most common fault.
2. **Perform**: read dialogue with character voices (not great acting, but clear attitude);
   make sound effects; describe camera moves briefly ("we push in...").
3. **Point** with a pointer or cursor at action and focal points when needed.
4. **Sell the emotion and the jokes** - commit; a timid pitch makes good boards look weak.
5. **Don't apologise** for drawings.

## Reading the room
- Watch faces: confusion means a clarity problem; silence on a joke means the joke or its timing.
- Note where attention drops.

## After
- Stop; let the director respond. Take notes (see notes in the room).

## Failure modes
- Narrating every panel in detail instead of letting it play.
- Rushing.
- Defending the work during the pitch.

## Verify
- [ ] Pitch duration roughly matches the sequence's intended screen time.
- [ ] Key emotional beats and jokes land with the audience.
- [ ] Notes captured immediately after.
