# Drawing speed and shorthand

Goal: produce many panels quickly - story artists iterate sequences repeatedly, and TV schedules
demand high panel counts.

## Visual shorthand
- **Symbols**: speed lines, impact stars, sweat drops, question marks, motion arcs, "shake"
  lines - widely understood and faster than drawing the effect.
- **Simplified BGs**: a horizon line and two shapes indicate a location once it is established.
- **Faces**: eye direction and brows carry most expression; mouths simple.
- **Crowds**: a few drawn figures in front, scribbled masses behind.

## Digital efficiencies
- **Reuse BGs**: draw the setup's background once on its own layer; duplicate panels and change
  only the characters.
- **Character layers** separate from BG and FX so poses can be redrawn without the set.
- **Templates**: panel templates with safe areas, thirds and the show's aspect ratio.
- **Brush set**: one sketch brush, one fill/value brush, one eraser; avoid fiddling with tools.
- **Copy-paste and transform** for minor pose changes (a head turn).
- **Shortcut keys** for new panel, duplicate panel, flip, and next/previous panel.

## Workflow
1. Thumbnail the sequence first (planning saves more time than drawing speed).
2. Rough at speed; clean only what will be pitched or sent.
3. Batch similar panels (all shots from setup A, then setup B).

## Failure modes
- Shorthand the director or overseas studio does not read - agree conventions.
- Over-reused BGs that hide camera changes.
- Spending time on panels that will be cut after the first pitch.

## Verify
- [ ] Panels per hour meets schedule.
- [ ] Shorthand symbols are on the production's convention list.
- [ ] BG reuse does not misrepresent camera positions.
