# Overhead blocking diagrams

Goal: a top-down plan of the scene that keeps staging, continuity and camera coverage
consistent - Glebas recommends drawing one for any scene with movement or several characters.

## What to draw
1. **Floor plan** of the set: walls, doors, furniture, landmarks (simple shapes).
2. **Character marks**: circles with a nose tick for facing, labelled; numbered positions for
   each beat (A1, A2...).
3. **Movement paths**: arrows between marks.
4. **Line of action** for each exchange.
5. **Camera positions**: small camera icons/triangles with field-of-view lines, labelled by shot number.

## Method
1. Read the scene; list beats and movements.
2. Block movement on the plan first (where would they go, motivated by the scene).
3. Place cameras to cover each beat; check the 180 line.
4. Board from the plan; update the plan when boards change.
5. Share with layout - they build cameras from it.

## Tools
- Paper, or a layer in the boarding app; or a quick 3D blockout (Blender, SketchUp) viewed
  from top, which can also generate the panels' perspective.

## Failure modes
- Boarding complex scenes without a plan - continuity errors and impossible geography.
- Plans not updated after revisions.

## Verify
- [ ] Every shot's camera appears on the plan.
- [ ] Character positions per beat match the boards.
- [ ] Line crossings marked and bridged.
