# Acting key poses in boards

Goal: boards that carry the performance - the audience (and the animator) can follow the
character's emotional journey through the panels.

## Method
1. Break the scene into **acting beats** (each change of thought, tactic or emotion).
2. Draw **one panel per beat minimum**; add panels for important transitions within a beat.
3. Each pose shows:
   - attitude (body language: open/closed, leaning in/away),
   - thought (eye direction, brows),
   - action (what they are physically doing).
4. Vary poses beat to beat - repetition reads as a static performance.
5. Write the beat's intention in the panel notes ("realises he is lying").

## Panel count guide
- Feature story boards: often a panel for every pose change - many panels per shot.
- TV: enough key poses that a layout/animation artist can follow; typical TV dialogue shots
  have 1-4 panels per shot.

## Avoid
- Boarding the dialogue line-by-line with a gesture per word (illustrative gesture).
- Neutral "talking heads" with no attitude.
- Poses that only work from an angle the camera cannot reach.

## Verify
- [ ] Every acting beat has a distinct pose.
- [ ] Muting dialogue, the panel sequence still shows the emotional progression.
- [ ] Panel notes state the intention of each beat.
