# Reel screenings and story fixes

Goal: use full-reel screenings to find story problems early and fix them in boards, where it
is cheap. Pixar's "Braintrust" process (Catmull, Creativity, Inc.) - candid notes on story
reels every few months - is the best-known model.

## Running a screening
1. Screen the whole film (or a large act) as it currently stands - mixed boards, layout,
   animation.
2. Audience: an internal group of story-literate peers (braintrust) and occasionally
   outside test audiences.
3. Collect notes immediately: what was confusing, when attention dropped, which characters
   people cared about, what they expected to happen.
4. The director decides; notes diagnose problems, they do not prescribe fixes.

## From notes to fixes
- Group notes by underlying problem (motivation unclear, stakes too low, second act sags).
- Fix the cause, which is often earlier in the film than the symptom (a weak payoff usually
  means a weak setup).
- Assign sequences to story artists; schedule re-boards; rework the reel.

## Pipeline management
- Lock sequences that are stable and in production; avoid re-boarding animated shots unless
  essential.
- Track sequence status (story, layout, animation) so changes are costed before approval.

## Failure modes
- Screening only polished sections - the problems hide in the rough ones.
- Treating the loudest note as the biggest problem.
- Fixing symptoms late in the film instead of setups early.

## Verify
- [ ] Notes recorded and grouped by underlying problem.
- [ ] Fix list maps each problem to a sequence and owner.
- [ ] Downstream cost reviewed before changing sequences in production.
