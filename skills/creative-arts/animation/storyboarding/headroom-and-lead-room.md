# Headroom and lead room

Goal: characters feel comfortably framed, with space where they look or move - unless you want
the opposite.

## Headroom
- Space between the top of the head and the frame top.
- Wide/medium shots: a small amount (about 1/10 of frame height).
- Close-ups: shrinks; eyes on the upper third, top of the head may touch or be cropped.
- Too much headroom makes the character look like they are sinking; too little feels cramped.

## Lead room (look room / nose room)
- More space in front of a character than behind, in the direction they look or move.
- Moving characters need even more lead room the faster they move.

## Breaking it deliberately
- **No lead room** (character looking toward the near edge): trapped, something behind them,
  unease - a classic horror and tension frame.
- **Excess headroom**: isolation, smallness.
- **Cropped forehead in a wide shot**: aggressive, claustrophobic.

## Safe areas
- Keep heads and key action inside the action-safe area of the delivery format; titles inside
  title-safe. Boarding templates should show both.

## Failure modes
- Characters centred in close-ups with their gaze hitting the frame edge.
- Inconsistent headroom across matched shot/reverse shots.
- Heads cut off by safe-area crops on delivery.

## Verify
- [ ] Look/move direction has more space unless tension is intended.
- [ ] Headroom consistent across matching shots.
- [ ] Key elements inside action-safe.
