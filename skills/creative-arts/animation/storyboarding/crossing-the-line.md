# Crossing the line

Goal: move the camera to the other side of the axis without disorienting the audience - or
disorient them on purpose.

## Legitimate ways
1. **Camera moves across on screen**: a dolly or crane move that visibly crosses the line within
   the shot; the audience sees the relationship change.
2. **Neutral shot on the axis**: a shot looking straight down the line (head-on or tail-away) acts
   as a bridge; after it, you can cut to the other side.
3. **Character moves**: a character walks to a new position, creating a new line; cut on the new line.
4. **Cutaway / insert**: a cut to something else (an insert of an object, a wide establishing
   shot) can reset - weaker than the above but common.
5. **Re-establishing wide shot** from the new side, showing both characters.

## Deliberate crosses
- Abrupt line jumps can signal a shift in power, confusion, a character's mental break, or a
  turning point (the "line cross" at the moment a relationship flips).
- Use once, at a strong story moment; otherwise it reads as an error.

## Boarding
- In the overhead diagram, draw the old line and the new line, and mark the bridging panel.
- Note camera moves that cross with arrows and "camera crosses line" text.

## Failure modes
- Crossing via a cut with no bridge in a dialogue scene.
- Chase sequences flipping direction without a neutral or a visible turn.
- Using "deliberate" crosses repeatedly.

## Verify
- [ ] Every line change has a bridge (move, neutral, character movement, re-establish).
- [ ] Deliberate crosses coincide with a story turn.
