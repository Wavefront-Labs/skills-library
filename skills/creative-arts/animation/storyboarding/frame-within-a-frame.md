# Frame within a frame

Goal: a secondary frame inside the panel - a doorway, window, arch, mirror, tree branches,
characters' bodies - that encloses the subject.

## What it does
1. **Focus**: the inner frame isolates the subject and points at it.
2. **Depth**: a near frame and a far subject create depth planes.
3. **Meaning**: trapped, isolated, watched (voyeuristic), separated from others, or protected.
4. **Transition**: a door frame that a character steps through marks a threshold moment.

## Method
- Look in the set for natural frames; ask layout for one if the moment needs it.
- Keep the framing element darker or less detailed than the subject.
- Asymmetric inner frames feel more natural; centred inner frames feel formal or confining.
- In a shot/reverse dialogue, framing one character and not the other expresses who is trapped.

## Failure modes
- A busy, detailed frame that competes with the subject.
- Overuse: every shot through a doorway feels mannered.
- Inner frame tangents cutting through the subject's head.

## Verify
- [ ] The inner frame reinforces the panel's story point.
- [ ] Subject reads first, frame second.
- [ ] No tangents between frame edges and the subject.
