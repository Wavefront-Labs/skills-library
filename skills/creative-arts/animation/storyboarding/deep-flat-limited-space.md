# Deep, flat and limited space

Goal: choose how much depth each shot shows, and use changes in space as a structural tool.
Bruce Block (The Visual Story) defines four types.

## The types
| Space | How | Feel |
|---|---|---|
| **Deep** | strong perspective, diagonals, size change, overlap, atmospheric perspective, movement in depth, wide lens | dynamic, immersive, intense |
| **Flat** | frontal planes, no convergence, no size change, horizontal/vertical lines, long lens, movement across frame | graphic, calm or stylised, detached |
| **Limited** | flat planes but with some depth cues (overlap, size difference) and little perspective | controlled middle ground |
| **Ambiguous** | depth cues contradict; the audience cannot read scale/space | disorientation, unease, dreams |

## Depth cues (to add or remove)
Perspective convergence, size difference, overlap, atmospheric perspective (contrast and
saturation loss with distance), textural diffusion, up/down position, camera movement
(parallax), focus.

## Structural use
- **Contrast** (deep vs flat across cuts or sequences) increases intensity; **affinity** (same
  space throughout) reduces it.
- Example plan: flat space in the calm opening, increasing depth as conflict grows, deepest
  space at the climax, back to flat for the resolution.

## Method
1. Decide the space type for each sequence in a chart against the story's intensity.
2. Board each panel to that type by including/excluding depth cues.
3. Tell layout/lighting the plan (lens choices, atmosphere).

## Failure modes
- Mixed space choices with no plan - visual noise.
- "Deep" shots that lose clarity.
- Ambiguous space used accidentally (scale cues missing).

## Verify
- [ ] Space type assigned per sequence.
- [ ] Panels use the cues of their assigned type.
- [ ] Space contrast peaks at the climax (or the chosen peak).
