# Eyeline match

Goal: when we cut between two characters, they appear to look at each other (or at the object
we cut to).

## The three components
1. **Direction**: A looks frame right -> B looks frame left (180 rule).
2. **Height**: if B is taller, A looks up and B looks down by matching amounts. A child talking
   to an adult: child's eyes up, adult's eyes down; camera heights may match the looker.
3. **Proximity to lens**: the closer the eyeline is to the camera (just off the lens), the more
   intimate the shot; matching shots should have similar eyeline offsets unless emphasising a
   difference.

## Looking at objects
Character looks off at something -> cut to the object from roughly that direction and height ->
cut back. If the eyeline is high left, the object should appear as if seen from low right.

## Boarding tips
- Draw small arrows on the panel for the eyeline in complex scenes.
- With several characters, keep a seating chart; each character's eyeline to each other
  character is consistent across all panels.
- Avoid direct-to-lens looks unless breaking the fourth wall or POV.

## Failure modes
- Both characters looking up at each other (height mismatch).
- Eyelines drifting across panels of the same setup.
- Group scenes where characters seem to look at empty space.

## Verify
- [ ] Each look direction and height matches its counterpart across the cut.
- [ ] Group seating chart and eyelines consistent.
- [ ] Object cutaways match the looker's angle.
