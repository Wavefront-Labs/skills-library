# Visual rhythm

Goal: control rhythm - the alternation of elements in space and time - as Block's final
visual component.

## Rhythm in space (within a frame)
- Repeated elements (columns, windows, fence posts, a row of characters) create a beat.
- Regular repetition = calm, order; irregular = energy; accents break a pattern to create a focal point.

## Rhythm in time
- **Editing tempo**: shot length; regular lengths feel steady, accelerating cuts build tension.
- **Movement beats**: footsteps, repeated actions, flashes, objects passing camera.
- **Camera movement rhythm**: a pan across columns creates a visual beat (watch strobing).

## Contrast and affinity
- Affinity: the same rhythm throughout a sequence (hypnotic or steady).
- Contrast: rhythm change at turning points (sudden stillness after fast cuts).

## Method
1. In the animatic, chart shot lengths for the sequence; look at the pattern.
2. Shape tempo to the story's intensity curve; plan a rhythm change at the turn.
3. Use in-frame repetition to set a location's character (orderly vs chaotic).
4. Coordinate with music tempo where scored.

## Failure modes
- Shot lengths all equal (mechanical) or random (no shape).
- No rhythm change at the climax.
- Repetitive patterns that strobe on camera moves.

## Verify
- [ ] Shot-length chart shows an intentional shape.
- [ ] Rhythm changes at turning points.
- [ ] In-frame repetition supports the location's mood.
