# Reaction shots

Goal: show the impact of an event or line on a character - often more powerful than the event.

## Principles
- **The story is often in the listener**: the person receiving bad news carries the emotion;
  the speaker delivers information.
- **Whose reaction**: the character with the most at stake, or the one whose change matters to
  the story.
- **Audience guide**: reactions tell the audience how to feel (laugh, fear, sympathy).

## When to cut
- Immediately after the key line or event for shock.
- During the line (over the speaker's voice, OS) when the listener's response matters more than
  seeing the speaker deliver it.
- Delayed (hold on the speaker, then cut) for a slow-burn realisation.

## Comedy reactions
- Deadpan reaction holds (a beat of nothing) get laughs.
- Group reactions: cut to several faces in quick succession, building.
- The "look to camera" reaction breaks the fourth wall (use per show style).

## Boarding
- Draw the reaction as its own panel/shot with the expression clearly readable (CU or MCU).
- Note "OS" for the speaker's dialogue playing over the reaction.
- Indicate hold length for comedic reactions.

## Failure modes
- Missing the key reaction (audience does not know how to take an event).
- Reactions from characters who do not matter, diluting focus.
- Too many reaction cutaways breaking the speaker's performance.

## Verify
- [ ] Each key event/line has the important character's reaction.
- [ ] Reactions readable at their shot size.
- [ ] Comedic holds noted.
