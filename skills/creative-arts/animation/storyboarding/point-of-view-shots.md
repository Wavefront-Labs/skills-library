# Point-of-view shots

Goal: put the audience in a character's eyes so they see what the character sees and share
the reaction.

## Look - see - react
1. **Look**: shot of the character looking off-screen (eyeline clear).
2. **See**: the POV shot - what they see, from their eye position.
3. **React**: back to the character's reaction.
The Kuleshov effect: the same neutral face reads differently depending on what is cut in
between - the POV gives the face its meaning.

## Making the POV read
- **Height and angle** match the looker: a child's POV is low and looks up; a giant's looks down.
- **Direction** matches the eyeline: if they looked frame left, the POV looks toward where they
  were facing.
- **Subjective cues** (optional): handheld drift, blur, a vignette, a frame shaped like
  binoculars or a keyhole; character's hands entering the frame.
- **Motion**: if the character walks, the POV moves at walking pace with a slight bob.

## Uses
- Discovery and reveal.
- Threat (the monster's POV stalking the hero - audience sees through the threat's eyes).
- Identification with the protagonist.

## Failure modes
- POV at the wrong height (adult eye level for a dog).
- POV that does not match the eyeline direction.
- Long subjective sequences without returning to the character: the audience forgets whose
  eyes they are in.

## Verify
- [ ] Each POV is bracketed by the looker's shots (or clearly tied to them).
- [ ] Height and direction match the looker.
- [ ] Reaction shot follows.
