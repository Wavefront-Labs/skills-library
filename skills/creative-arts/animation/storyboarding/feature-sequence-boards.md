# Feature sequence boards

Goal: develop a feature sequence from script pages, an outline or idea into boards that play in
the reel, knowing it will be rebuilt many times.

## How feature differs from TV
| | Feature | TV |
|---|---|---|
| Source | script pages, beat outline, or just an idea | locked script |
| Panel density | very high (every acting change) | key poses |
| Finish | rough | rough to clean |
| Versions | many (sequences are re-boarded through production) | few |
| Primary user | director and story reel | production studio |

## Workflow
1. **Assignment**: director and head of story brief the sequence's purpose, emotional goal,
   and where it sits in the film.
2. **Beat board / thumbnails**: plan the sequence (see beat boards).
3. **Rough boards**: board fast; explore staging and acting options.
4. **Pitch** to head of story, then director.
5. **Revise** and cut into the reel with editorial.
6. **Repeat** as the film changes; keep earlier versions.

## Habits
- Don't marry panels - expect sequences to be cut or merged.
- Collaborate: story artists often trade ideas and gags; credit is shared.
- Keep track of continuity with neighbouring sequences boarded by others.

## Failure modes
- Over-rendering early versions.
- Ignoring the sequence's purpose in the film for a set piece.
- Losing older versions that the director later wants back.

## Verify
- [ ] Sequence purpose stated and served.
- [ ] Versions saved and labelled.
- [ ] Sequence cut into the reel and screened in context.
