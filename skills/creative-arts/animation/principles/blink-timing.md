# Blink timing

Goal: blinks that keep a character alive and punctuate thought, without becoming noise.

## Anatomy of a blink (24 fps)
| Blink | Close | Hold closed | Open |
|---|---|---|---|
| Normal | 1-2 frames | 0-1 | 2-4 frames |
| Fast/startled | 1 | 0 | 2 |
| Slow/tired/thinking | 3-4 | 2-6 | 4-8 |
Lids close faster than they open. Upper lid does most of the travel; lower lid rises slightly.
A half-closed in-between on the way down, often with a tiny squash of the brow/cheek.

## Where to place blinks
1. **On thought changes** - a blink on the beat where the character gets an idea or changes
   attitude (the strongest use).
2. **On head turns** - blink as the head starts a big turn, open when it lands.
3. **Before/after dialogue phrases** - not in the middle of a word.
4. **Holds** - one blink in a long hold keeps the character alive; avoid metronome regularity.
5. **Never on a key story look** - a blink at the exact moment of a reveal or emotional stare
   kills the beat.

## Shape and acting
- Lids follow the eyeball shape and the pupil direction (looking down = lids lower).
- Asymmetric blink (one lid slightly delayed by 1 frame) reads more natural in close-ups.
- Slow blinks read as affection, contempt or fatigue depending on the brows.

## Failure modes
- Regular interval blinks (every 2 seconds) - mechanical.
- Blinks during accents or on emotional stares.
- Lids passing through or floating off the eyeball in CG (check corrective shapes).

## Verify
- [ ] Each blink is tied to a reason (thought, turn, phrase, keep-alive).
- [ ] Close faster than open.
- [ ] No blink on the shot's key look.
