# Sizing anticipation

Goal: every main action is preceded by a move in the opposite direction that is just big and
long enough for the audience to see it coming and for the action to feel powered.

## Three things set the size
1. **Force of the action** - bigger action, bigger and longer anticipation. A throw needs a
   long backswing; a finger tap needs almost none.
2. **Weight of the character/object** - heavy things need more time to load (a sumo wind-up
   is long and low); light characters can anticipate in a few frames.
3. **Audience attention** - if the action is fast or small on screen, anticipate longer so the
   eye arrives first (Thomas and Johnston: "tell the audience what is going to happen"). If
   the audience is already looking, it can be shorter.

## Working ranges at 24 fps
| Action | Anticipation | Hold at the extreme |
|---|---|---|
| Blink, small head turn | 1-3 frames, tiny | none |
| Point, reach | 3-6 frames | 0-2 |
| Jump, punch, throw | 6-12 frames, large | 2-4 (moving hold) |
| Zip-out cartoon exit | 8-16 frames, extreme | 4-8, then 1-3 frames to exit |

The ratio matters more than the numbers: anticipation is usually slower than the action it
precedes. A 4-frame punch with an 8-frame wind-up feels powerful; the reverse feels weak.

## Method
1. Key the action's start and end poses.
2. Add the anticipation extreme opposite the action direction (down before up, back before
   forward, a squint before a wide-eye).
3. Push the anticipation pose until the action pose looks strong by contrast, then trim back
   to the style.
4. Ease into the anticipation, hold briefly, then snap out with wide spacing into the action.

## Failure modes
- Anticipation as large as the action: the move looks like a hesitation, not preparation.
- Same anticipation on every action: mechanical rhythm.
- No anticipation on a big move: the action pops and the audience misses it.
- Anticipating with the whole body equally: lead with the hips/centre of mass, let
  extremities drag.

## Verify
- [ ] Cover the action frames: can a viewer predict the direction from the anticipation alone?
- [ ] Anticipation frame count is larger than action frame count for forceful moves.
- [ ] The extreme pose is in the opposite direction on a clear arc.
