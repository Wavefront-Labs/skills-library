# Acting subtext

Goal: the audience reads more than the words. McKee's principle that characters rarely say
exactly what they mean applies directly to animation: the performance carries the subtext.

## Finding subtext
For each line, answer:
1. What does the character **say**?
2. What do they **mean** or want?
3. What do they **feel** and what are they hiding?
If 1 and 2/3 are the same, there is little subtext - play it simply.

## Playing it
- **Contrast**: say "I'm fine" with tense shoulders and an averted gaze. The body tells the truth.
- **Leaks**: let the hidden emotion leak in brief moments (a micro-expression of 4-8 frames, a
  glance away, a clenched hand) - not throughout.
- **Timing**: a hesitation before a lie, a too-quick answer, a delayed smile.
- **Masks**: a polite smile that does not reach the eyes (cheeks and lower lids do not rise -
  the non-Duchenne smile).
- **Point of view**: frame for the audience to catch the leak (close enough to read it).

## Method
1. Write the subtext under each line on the blocking notes.
2. Choose where it leaks (one or two moments per shot).
3. Animate the surface performance, then the leak.
4. Review with someone who has not read the notes: what do they think the character feels?

## Failure modes
- Playing the text literally.
- Over-signalling the subtext (melodrama): subtext becomes text.
- Leaks too small to see in the shot size.

## Verify
- [ ] Each line has written subtext where relevant.
- [ ] At least one visible leak per subtextual moment.
- [ ] Fresh viewer correctly identifies the hidden feeling.
