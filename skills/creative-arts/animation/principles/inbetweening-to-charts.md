# Inbetweening to charts

Goal: produce the in-between drawings exactly as the animator charted them, so the motion
matches the key animator's intent. The inbetweener is an Animation Guild classification and
the traditional entry job.

## Method
1. **Read the chart** on the key: which drawing numbers, where they fall (halves, thirds).
2. **Superimpose** the two drawings you are inbetweening (light box or onion skin, or the
   Williams method: line up the two drawings so the shapes overlap on the pegs/registration,
   then draw the middle).
3. **Find the arc**: points travel on arcs - put the inbetween point on the arc, not halfway on
   a straight line (Williams: "the inbetween is not in the middle of a straight line").
4. **Volume**: the inbetween's forms are the same size as the keys' (unless squash/stretch charted).
5. **Overlapping parts**: parts that lead/drag in the keys keep their offsets; inbetween
   each part separately.
6. **Detail**: same costume and feature placement.

## Superimposing (Williams)
When the two keys are far apart, slide them so the part being inbetweened overlaps (e.g. the
heads), draw the in-between head, then shift back. Avoids "shrinking" from averaging distant
shapes.

## Failure modes
- Straight-line inbetweens on arcing motion.
- Averaging shapes so volume shrinks (a common inbetween fault on heads and hands).
- Ignoring the chart and placing drawings evenly.

## Verify
- [ ] Inbetween positions match the chart's ticks.
- [ ] Points sit on the arcs between keys.
- [ ] Volumes equal to keys.
