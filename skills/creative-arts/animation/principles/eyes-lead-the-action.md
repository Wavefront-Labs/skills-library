# Eyes lead the action

Goal: a character looks before it moves. The eye change is the cheapest, clearest
anticipation there is - it tells the audience where the action is going and that the
character decided to do it.

## Order of movement
Eyes -> head -> shoulders/chest -> hips -> feet. Each part starts a few frames after the
previous one and arrives later.

## Offsets at 24 fps (starting points)
| Part | Starts | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Eyes | frame 0 | dart in 2-3 frames, often with a blink on a big turn |
| Head | +2-4 | travels on an arc, slight dip at the middle |
| Chest | +4-6 | |
| Hips/feet | +6-10 | only if the body re-orients |

## Method
1. Key the target pose of the whole body.
2. Move the eye key earlier, 2-4 frames ahead of the head start.
3. On turns over about 90 degrees, add a blink that closes on the start of the head move and
   opens near the end (the eyes "land" when they reopen).
4. Head turns dip slightly in the middle (the arc), not a flat swivel.
5. Let the body follow, finishing last with a small settle.

## Failure modes
- Eyes and head arriving together: looks robotic, intention unclear.
- Eyes leading by too much (> 8 frames on a normal turn): reads as hesitation or suspicion -
  only use deliberately.
- Pupils drifting during the head move: lock the gaze to the target once the eyes arrive.
- Forgetting the eye lead on exits: the character walks off without having decided to.

## Verify
- [ ] Step through: the eye change is the first visible change.
- [ ] Turns have a dip on the head arc.
- [ ] Gaze stays locked to the target while the head finishes.
