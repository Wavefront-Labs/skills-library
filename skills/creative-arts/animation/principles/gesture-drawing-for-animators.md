# Gesture drawing for animators

Goal: capture the action, force and attitude of a pose in seconds. Every animation curriculum
(CalArts, Sheridan, Gobelins) runs weekly life and gesture drawing; Walt Stanchfield's
Disney gesture classes ("Drawn to Life") are the canonical teaching.

## Practice routine
- Warm up: 10 x 30-second poses, then 5 x 1-minute, then 2-3 x 5-minute.
- Sources: life model, croquis sessions, timed photo reference sites, people in public.
- Draw with a tool that does not allow erasing (marker, brush pen) to force decisions.

## Method per drawing
1. **Find the line of action** - the one curve the whole pose hangs on - first.
2. **Head, ribcage, pelvis** as simple masses and their tilts relative to each other.
3. **Weight**: where is the support? Draw the supporting leg straight under the weight.
4. **Limbs** as directional strokes; hands and feet as simple shapes.
5. Ask "what is the pose *doing*?" (pushing, waiting, sulking) and exaggerate that.
6. Stop before details.

## Applying it to animation
- Thumbnail keys as gestures before constructing them.
- When CG posing, draw a gesture over the viewport screenshot (Grease Pencil / overlay) and
  pose the rig to match the curve.
- Keep the gesture through construction - most poses die during cleanup; compare the final
  key to the gesture thumbnail.

## Failure modes
- Contour-copying the model: accurate but lifeless.
- Starting with the head detail and running out of time.
- Stiff straight spines; the spine is always a curve.

## Verify
- [ ] Every drawing has a visible line of action.
- [ ] A viewer can name the action from a 30-second gesture.
- [ ] Final keys preserve the gesture thumbnail's curve.
