# Sneaks and tiptoes

Goal: a character trying not to be heard. Williams details two classic cartoon sneaks and
the tiptoe; they rely on long holds and quick, careful moves.

## Cartoon sneak (two variants)
- **Front sneak**: body leans forward, arms raised and bent in front (hands "creeping"),
  big lifted knee, foot placed toe first, slowly.
- **Back sneak**: body leans back, arms up and hands near chest, leg reaches far forward
  before weight transfers.
- Timing: slow reach (long ease), quick weight transfer, hold. Typical step 16-32 frames with
  most time spent in the reach and a fast plant.
- Head stays level or turns to look around - head holds still while body moves sells stealth.

## Tiptoe
- Weight always on the balls of the feet, heels never touch.
- Small, quick steps (6-8 frames) with holds between, or high, slow lifts (comic).
- Arms often out slightly for balance, shoulders raised.

## Realistic stealth
- Low centre of gravity, knees bent, feet placed heel-to-toe on the outer edge, weight tested
  before transfer; much smaller exaggeration.

## Readability
- Exaggerate the lift and placement; the audience must see the care.
- Put contrast in timing: slow-slow-quick-hold. Even timing kills the sneak.
- Silhouette: profile staging is clearest.

## Failure modes
- Bouncy vertical motion: sneaks have minimal bounce.
- Fast reaches: loses the tension.
- Feet slapping down (heel strikes) in a tiptoe.

## Verify
- [ ] Timing has visible slow-quick contrast with holds.
- [ ] Heels never touch in tiptoes.
- [ ] Head stable or deliberately scanning.
