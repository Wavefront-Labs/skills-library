# Shooting video reference

Goal: reference that gives real timing and body mechanics for a specific shot. Standard
practice at feature studios: animators act out shots on camera before blocking.

## Setup
- Phone or camera at **24 fps** if possible (or 30/60 and retime); shutter fast enough to avoid
  heavy blur.
- Frame the **same angle and lens feel** as the shot camera - reference from a different angle
  is much less useful.
- Even, bright light; plain background; fitted clothes so joints are visible.
- Props or stand-ins at the right height (a chair, a box for a table).
- Play the dialogue track while acting so timing matches.

## Shooting
1. Warm up; know the beats and the thought per beat.
2. Shoot **many takes** (10-20); vary choices deliberately (big, small, different tactics).
3. Act the character, not yourself - adjust for size and age (a heavy character moves heavier).
4. Shoot alternates for body mechanics (a fall, a lift) separately from acting.

## Using it
1. Pick the take with the most honest beat, or combine takes per beat.
2. Load into a frame-accurate player (SyncSketch, an NLE, Blender's sequencer, Keyframe MP).
3. Step through, mark key poses and their frame numbers.
4. Draw over key frames: line of action, weight, arcs.
5. **Caricature** - do not copy; push poses and timing (see caricature of action).

## Failure modes
- Wrong angle; reference unusable for posing.
- One take only - first takes are usually generic.
- Rotoscoping timing exactly, giving floaty results.
- Animator's own mannerisms ending up in every character.

## Verify
- [ ] Reference matches shot camera angle and dialogue.
- [ ] Key poses and frame numbers extracted.
- [ ] Final animation is clearer and more pushed than the footage.
