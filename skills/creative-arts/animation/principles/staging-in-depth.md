# Staging in depth

Goal: use the space toward and away from camera so shots feel dimensional, while keeping
the action clear. Flat side-on staging is the default habit; depth staging is a choice.

## Method
1. **Pick the axis**: decide whether the action reads better across frame (clear, flat,
   graphic - Block's "flat space") or into frame (dynamic, immersive - "deep space").
2. **Block on diagonals**: move characters on lines that recede to a vanishing point rather
   than straight toward lens; it shows both depth and profile.
3. **Use size change**: an approaching character grows; time that growth with the action
   (a threat looming in on its key line).
4. **Overlap**: let a near character partly overlap a far one - the strongest depth cue.
5. **Frame with foreground**: a dark, out-of-focus foreground element gives a depth plane
   and a frame (keep it simple and low contrast).
6. **Spacing on depth moves**: motion straight toward lens has little screen movement;
   increase scale change and add a slight lateral drift so it reads.

## 2D specifics
- Perspective guides in layout; characters scaled per depth on the guide.
- Depth moves cost drawings and keep perspective accurate - check with a horizon line.

## CG specifics
- Check focal length: wide lenses exaggerate depth moves, long lenses flatten them.
- Watch for characters crossing the near clipping plane or filling the frame unexpectedly.

## Failure modes
- Important acting placed too far back to read.
- Approaching characters that seem to hover because their size change is not timed to steps.
- Foreground framing elements that are busier than the subject.

## Verify
- [ ] The key action is readable at its depth (face at least ~1/10 of frame height for acting).
- [ ] Depth moves show a scale change you can measure across the shot.
- [ ] Silhouettes do not merge where characters overlap.
