# Calibrating exaggeration to the style

Goal: every animator on a project exaggerates by the same amount. Inconsistent exaggeration
(one shot realistic, the next broad) breaks the film's world faster than any single bad shot.

## The ladder (write where the project sits)
1. **Photoreal** - no deformation; push timing and clarity only (VFX creatures).
2. **Grounded feature** - subtle squash in faces, overlap, believable physics with cheats.
3. **Stylized feature** - clear squash and stretch, pushed poses, smears on fast action.
4. **Broad TV/theatrical cartoon** - large squash/stretch, takes, cartoon physics.
5. **Extreme/surreal** - anything goes (Tex Avery, Ren and Stimpy), but still internally consistent.
Tone can move a step within a film (a comedic sidekick a step broader than the lead), but
the range must be documented.

## Method
1. **Test shots**: animate the same 3-5 second action at two or three ladder levels with the
   actual rig/model; director chooses.
2. **Write the limits** in the animation bible: max squash (e.g. 20 % volume-preserved), are
   smears allowed, do characters hang in the air, how big are takes, is the face allowed
   off-model on extremes.
3. **Per-character rules**: lead vs comedic characters.
4. **Reference reel**: approved shots that show the limits; new animators watch it first.
5. **Review against the bible** in dailies, not only by taste.

## Failure modes
- Different supervisors setting different levels.
- Escalation over production (each animator pushing a little further than the last approved shot).
- Physics rules broken only once (a hang-time gag in an otherwise grounded film) - unless it
  is the point.

## Verify
- [ ] Animation bible states the ladder level and numerical limits.
- [ ] A reference reel exists.
- [ ] Cutting shots from different animators together shows no jump in exaggeration.
