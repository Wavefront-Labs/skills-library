# Caricature of action

Goal: use live-action reference as a source of truth, then distil it into animation that
is clearer and more alive than the footage. Disney's 1930s action-analysis classes taught
this: study the real thing, then caricature it.

## Method
1. **Watch at speed** several times without stopping; write down what the action feels like
   in one sentence.
2. **Find the essential poses**: step through and mark the frames that tell the story (usually
   3-6 for an acting beat). Discard the rest.
3. **Identify the noise**: small hesitations, double-moves, nervous bobbing, and
   performer-specific tics that do not serve the idea. Remove them.
4. **Exaggerate the carriers**: the part that carries the idea (a slump, a lean, the head) is
   pushed; contrast between poses increased.
5. **Retime**: real timing is often too even. Make the fast parts faster and holds longer.
6. **Re-check against the sentence**: does the animation feel like the one-line description more
   than the footage does?

## Not rotoscoping
Tracing the footage frame by frame (rotoscope, or mocap applied raw) gives floaty, weightless
motion in animation because cartoon proportions and frame rates amplify small noise. Use
footage as reference, then re-pose.

## Failure modes
- Copying every frame: mushy timing.
- Keeping the performer's tics.
- Exaggerating everything, losing the hierarchy.

## Verify
- [ ] Side by side with footage, the animation is clearer at a glance.
- [ ] Number of key poses much smaller than footage frames with movement.
- [ ] Timing has more contrast (fast vs held) than the footage.
