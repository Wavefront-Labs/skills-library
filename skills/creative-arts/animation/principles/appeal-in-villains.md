# Appeal in villains

Goal: the audience wants to watch the villain. Thomas and Johnston stress that appeal applies to
villains: a repulsive or confusing villain loses the audience; a charismatic one carries the film.

## Design
- **Strong, clear shape language**: triangles, sharp angles, long verticals; a readable,
  memorable silhouette (Maleficent's horns, Scar's angular head).
- **Contrast with the hero** in shape, color and proportion.
- **Elegance or flamboyance**: refined design details (cape, nails, costume) read as power.
- Avoid pure ugliness unless the character's ugliness is the story; even grotesque villains
  need design rhythm.

## Performance
1. **Control**: villains often move less and more precisely than heroes; stillness is menace.
2. **Theatricality**: big, confident gestures in specific moments (the "showman" villain).
3. **Intelligence in the eyes**: thinking, scheming beats - the audience sees the plan form.
4. **Humour**: dry wit makes villains enjoyable (Thomas and Johnston on Captain Hook's vanity).
5. **Motivation visible**: a want the audience understands.

## Failure modes
- Villain who only snarls: one-note and tiring.
- Messy, unreadable design: the audience cannot read intent.
- Villain acting copying the hero's vocabulary: no contrast.

## Verify
- [ ] Villain silhouette is recognisable and contrasts with the hero's.
- [ ] Performance includes still, controlled beats and specific big accents.
- [ ] Test viewers describe the villain with words like "fun to watch".
