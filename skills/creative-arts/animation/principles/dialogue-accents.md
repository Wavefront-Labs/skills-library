# Dialogue accents

Goal: the body supports the line's rhythm and meaning by hitting a few chosen accents -
Williams' advice is to find the main accents and not to accent everything.

## Finding accents
1. Listen for stressed syllables: louder, higher pitch or longer.
2. Mark all of them on the sheet.
3. Rank them: one or two **main accents** per phrase carry meaning; the rest are minor.

## Hitting them
- **Main accent**: a head or body move that **arrives** on the accent frame - head down/up, a
  hand beat, an eyebrow lift. The anticipation happens before.
- **Minor accents**: small brow, a slight head change, or nothing.
- Put the move's **extreme** on (or 1-2 frames before) the stressed syllable so it reads as
  driving the word.
- Vary direction: not all accents downward nods. Up, sideways, a lean, a hand.

## Phrasing
- Group accents into phrases; let a phrase be one gesture with an accent inside it rather than
  a new gesture per accent.
- Use pauses: a still beat before a key word makes the accent stronger.

## Failure modes
- "Nodding dog": head bobbing on every syllable.
- Accents landing after the sound: looks like the character is reacting to its own voice.
- Big body moves on unimportant words.

## Verify
- [ ] One or two main accents per phrase; each arrives on or just before its syllable.
- [ ] Accent directions vary.
- [ ] Muting the audio, the body rhythm still suggests the line's emphasis.
