# Comedic timing

Goal: get the laugh by controlling when the audience understands the joke. Most animated
comedy timing is about the pause before the payoff and the hold after it.

## Beats of a visual gag
1. **Set-up** - establish the normal situation clearly (enough time to read it).
2. **Build/anticipation** - the audience senses something coming; slow down slightly.
3. **Pause** - a beat of stillness right before the payoff (6-12 frames often). The pause lets
   the audience predict, which makes the surprise land.
4. **Payoff** - fast. Few frames, snap timing, often a hard cut or a 1-2 frame action.
5. **Hold for the laugh** - hold the aftermath (12-36 frames) so the audience can laugh
   without missing the next beat.
6. **Topper (optional)** - a second, smaller gag on the aftermath.

## Timing devices
- **Delayed reaction**: the character realises late (a hold, then a take).
- **Rule of three**: two repeats establish rhythm, the third breaks it - keep the first two
  identical in timing so the break is visible.
- **Snap vs slow**: fast payoff for slapstick; slow, inevitable payoff (watching the anvil
  fall for seconds) for dread comedy.
- **Off-screen action**: the sound or reaction instead of the impact - often funnier.

## Failure modes
- Rushing the pause: the audience has not caught up.
- Short hold after the payoff: laughter covers the next line.
- Payoff animated slowly: kills surprise.
- Over-milking: three toppers on a weak gag.

## Verify
- [ ] Each gag has identifiable set-up, pause, payoff and hold frames.
- [ ] Test screening: laughs land at the payoff, not before.
- [ ] Dialogue after a gag starts after the laugh hold.
