# Head and hand arcs

Goal: choose the arc shape of heads and hands deliberately in acting, since these are where
the audience looks and where flat motion is most noticeable.

## Head turns
- **Dip** (head goes down in the middle of the turn): natural, relaxed, thoughtful. Default.
- **Rise** (head goes up in the middle): alert, surprised, proud.
- **Flat** (no arc): robotic or deliberately controlled (a soldier, a machine).
- Add a slight tilt at the middle and a blink on big turns.

## Nods and shakes
- A nod is not up-down on a line: the nose travels a narrow arc (pivot at the top of the neck),
  ending slightly lower with a small settle.
- A head shake has a slight downward smile-shaped arc on each side.

## Hand gestures
- Hands describe loops: a gesture rises, accents, and returns on a different path (an oval), not
  back along the same line.
- The wrist leads in on approach and drags on departure (overlap).
- Pointing: the hand arcs up to the point and overshoots slightly past it before settling.

## Meaning through arcs
| Arc | Reads as |
|---|---|
| Large, open, rising | confidence, enthusiasm |
| Small, close to body, dipping | timidity, thought |
| Sharp, straight | anger, precision, mechanical |

## Method
1. Decide the arc per acting beat (write "dip" or "rise" on the blocking notes).
2. Set the breakdown to create it.
3. Track nose and wrist trails in polish.

## Failure modes
- All turns dipping the same amount: becomes a mannerism.
- Hands returning along the exact path they came: looks rewound.
- Head arcs fighting body arcs (head dips while body rises without reason).

## Verify
- [ ] Nose path on every turn is a deliberate curve.
- [ ] Gesture loops are ovals, not lines.
- [ ] Arc choices support each beat's emotion.
