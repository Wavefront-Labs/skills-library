# Anticipating a thought change

Goal: the audience sees the character *think* before acting. Thomas and Johnston describe the
best Disney acting as showing the thought that causes the action; in practice the thought is
an anticipation of the action, staged in the face.

## The beat structure
1. **Old thought** - the character holds the previous attitude (a moving hold).
2. **Trigger** - something is seen/heard; eyes shift to it or defocus.
3. **Processing** - a held beat: small eye darts, a blink, brows begin to change. 6-20 frames
   depending on how big the realization is.
4. **Decision** - a clear accent (a brow lift, a small head up, an intake of breath) - this
   is the anticipation of the new action.
5. **New action** - the body executes.

## Practical tools
- **Blink on the change**: a blink placed exactly on the thought change reads as "new idea".
- **Breath**: a chest lift before speaking or acting reads as decision.
- **Eye defocus**: pupils drift slightly apart/upward while recalling something.
- **Brow before mouth**: the brows change 2-6 frames before the mouth shape changes.

## Failure modes
- Acting on the line instead of before it: the character seems to react to its own words.
- Processing beat too short: realizations feel unmotivated.
- Every beat processed the same way (always blink + look down): becomes a tic.
- Too many thought changes in a short shot: pick the one that matters.

## Verify
- [ ] Muted playback: can a viewer point to the frame where the character changed its mind?
- [ ] The face changes before the body.
- [ ] Each thought change has a different physical vocabulary.
