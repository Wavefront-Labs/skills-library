# Heavy lift

Goal: the object's weight is communicated entirely through the character's body and timing -
a classic Survival Kit weight exercise.

## Beats
1. **Assess** - look at the object; maybe a pause, a breath, a hand wipe. Tells the audience it
   is heavy before any effort.
2. **Set-up** - feet planted wide, close to the object; squat (back straight-ish for realism,
   curved for cartoon strain); grip.
3. **First try (optional)** - pull, nothing moves. Body strains, shakes, releases. This sells weight
   better than anything.
4. **The lift** - hips drive up first, arms stay straight (they are cables, not levers), object
   rises late and slowly. Long ease out of the ground.
5. **Get under it** - for overhead lifts, a quick drop under the bar (the "clean") then a slow
   push up.
6. **Hold/carry** - body leans back to keep the combined centre of gravity over the feet;
   short, careful steps; knees slightly bent; shakes.
7. **Put down or drop** - controlled lower (slow) or a release with a big impact and relief.

## Timing contrast
Heavy objects: slow to start moving, slow to stop. The character's body moves first and
the object lags behind. Light objects move with the hands immediately.

## Body cues
- Shoulders pulled down by the load, neck tense, face strained (squint, teeth).
- Spine compresses.
- Feet do not slide; weight pushes into the ground.

## Failure modes
- Object rising with the hands from frame one: weightless.
- Arms bending to lift (bicep curl) on a heavy object: wrong mechanics.
- Combined centre of gravity outside the feet with no fall.

## Verify
- [ ] Object starts moving after the body begins the lift.
- [ ] Centre of gravity (body + object) over the support base during the carry.
- [ ] Audience guesses the object is heavy before it moves.
