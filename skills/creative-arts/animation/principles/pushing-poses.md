# Pushing poses

Goal: poses that read instantly. Thomas and Johnston: exaggeration is not distortion but making
the essence of an idea clearer. Real poses copied from reference usually read weak in animation.

## What to push
1. **Line of action** - curve it more; a C or S through the whole body.
2. **Contrast between consecutive poses** - a pushed anticipation makes the action pose look
   bigger. The difference matters more than either pose.
3. **Extremities** - hands further out, head tilted more, feet planted wider.
4. **Weight** - hips further over the supporting foot.
5. **Expression** - brows and mouth corners further.

## Method
1. Pose from reference or instinct.
2. Duplicate the pose and push it until it looks too much.
3. Step between the two in the shot context; settle somewhere between, usually closer to the
   pushed version than feels comfortable.
4. Check from the shot camera - foreshortening eats exaggeration, so push poses that point
   toward camera even more.
5. Check the next pose: push in the contrasting direction.

## When to pull back
- Realistic styles (VFX creatures, photoreal humans): push timing and line of action, not
  joint angles or proportions.
- Subtle acting close-ups: push clarity (eye direction, brow) rather than size.

## Failure modes
- Pushing every pose equally: no hierarchy.
- Pushing a pose so far it breaks the model or the rig.
- Pushing in the wrong direction (more pose, less clarity).

## Verify
- [ ] Each key pose reads in silhouette and at thumbnail size.
- [ ] Consecutive poses contrast clearly.
- [ ] Compared side by side with the reference, the animation pose is clearer, not just bigger.
