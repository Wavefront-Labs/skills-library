# Single-idea staging

Goal: at any moment the audience knows exactly what to look at and what it means. Thomas and
Johnston define staging as "the presentation of any idea so that it is completely and
unmistakably clear"; the working rule is one idea at a time.

## Method
1. **Name the idea** of each beat in one sentence ("she sees the letter", "he lies").
   If you need two sentences, it is two beats.
2. **Pick the single carrier**: the face, the hands, a prop, the whole body. Make that the
   biggest, highest-contrast, most-moving thing in frame.
3. **Freeze everything else** for the duration of the beat: background characters go to moving
   holds, secondary action quiets down.
4. **Sequence, do not overlap**: if two characters have reactions, stagger them by at least
   6-12 frames so the eye can go from one to the other.
5. **Choose the shot size for the carrier**: a hand action needs a closer shot or a clear
   silhouette; a body action needs the full figure.

## Failure modes
- Two important actions at once (one character talks while another does a gag): both lose.
- Detail without staging: subtle acting in a wide shot that nobody can see.
- Busy background animation competing with the story beat.
- Camera move during a key beat drawing attention to itself.

## Verify
- [ ] Each beat can be named in one sentence.
- [ ] At the beat, the carrier is the clear point of contrast (movement, value, size).
- [ ] Simultaneous actions are deliberately staggered.
- [ ] A fresh viewer can describe each beat after one viewing.
