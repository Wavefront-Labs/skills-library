# Takes and double takes

Goal: a surprise reaction that reads instantly and with the right size for the style.

## The take
1. **Normal** - character in a neutral or prior state.
2. **Anticipation (squash)** - the face and body compress: eyes squeeze shut or narrow, head
   drops into shoulders. 2-6 frames, often held a frame or two.
3. **Take (stretch)** - fast: 1-3 frames to the extreme. Eyes wide, brows up, jaw down, head up,
   body stretched; extreme cartoon takes leave the ground or the eyes pop out.
4. **Hold / vibration** - hold the extreme or vibrate it (staggers) for 6-24 frames.
5. **Settle** - come down into a held surprised pose.

Size ladder: subtle (eyes widen and brows lift, CG feature) -> medium (head pops back) -> broad
(full-body stretch) -> extreme (Tex Avery, eyes and jaw detach).

## The double take
1. Character sees something, it does not register; looks away (back to what they were doing).
2. A beat (8-24 frames) - the realisation building.
3. Snap back to look (fast head turn, often with the squash).
4. Full take.
Timing of the beat in step 2 is the comedy - too short and it reads as one take, too long and
the audience gets ahead.

## Failure modes
- No anticipation squash: the take pops weakly.
- Slow take extreme: loses the shock.
- Take size inconsistent with the show's exaggeration level.

## Verify
- [ ] Squash before stretch.
- [ ] Extreme reached in 1-3 frames.
- [ ] Hold long enough to read.
- [ ] Double take has a clear look-away and delayed snap-back.
