# Straights against curves

Goal: contours with rhythm. A pose or shape where every contour is a curve looks soft and
blobby; all straights looks stiff. Placing a straight against a curve gives direction and
tension - a principle taught throughout Disney and in Bancroft's design chapter.

## Rules
1. **Opposite sides differ**: on a limb or torso, one side is a straighter line (often the side
   under stretch or bearing load), the opposite side is the curve (the compressed side).
2. **Stretch side straight, squash side curved**: a bent leg has a straight-ish front of the shin
   and a curved calf; a leaning body has a straight stretched side and a curved compressed side.
3. **Lines flow across forms**: the end of one curve leads into the start of the next (back of
   the neck into the spine into the leg) - rhythm through the whole figure.
4. **Vary the curve size**: not all curves the same radius.

## Applying it
- Posing: check the contour of the torso and limbs on the key; replace mirrored curves with a
  straight/curve pair.
- Design: build character shapes with a straight side (often the back or the front of a face)
  and a curved side.
- Cleanup: preserve the straights; inbetweeners tend to soften everything into curves.

## Failure modes
- "Sausage limbs": both sides of an arm curved the same way.
- Straights everywhere on a cartoony character: stiff and mechanical.
- Rhythm lines that break at joints instead of flowing.

## Verify
- [ ] Each major form has one straighter and one curved side.
- [ ] Contours flow from form to form.
- [ ] Cleanup has not rounded the straights away.
