# Secondary action hierarchy

Goal: secondary actions support and reveal more about the primary action without stealing
attention. Thomas and Johnston: the secondary action is always kept subordinate to the primary.

## Choosing a secondary action
Ask: what does this character do, while doing the main thing, that reveals how they feel?
- A sad walk (primary) with a hand wiping a tear (secondary).
- Arguing (primary) while stabbing at food with a fork (secondary).
- Waiting (primary) while tapping a foot (secondary).
A good secondary action carries emotion or character, not just motion.

## Timing it
1. Animate the primary action fully first.
2. Place the secondary so its accents **do not land on the primary's accents** - stagger by at
   least a few frames, or put the secondary in the primary's quiet moments.
3. If the face carries the primary (an expression change), keep the secondary small and away
   from the face, or pause it during the expression change (Thomas and Johnston: an expression
   change can be missed if it happens during a big body move).
4. Keep secondary amplitude lower than the primary's.

## Failure modes
- Secondary actions bigger or faster than the primary.
- A secondary that contradicts the primary's emotion without intent.
- Secondary on the same frames as dialogue accents: muddy.
- "Busy" animation: several secondaries at once.

## Verify
- [ ] Hide the secondary part: the primary still tells the story.
- [ ] Show only the secondary: it adds information (emotion, character).
- [ ] No secondary accent coincides with a primary accent unless intended.
