# Appealing poses

Goal: poses the audience enjoys looking at. Thomas and Johnston define appeal as charm,
pleasing design, simplicity, communication and magnetism - not prettiness; villains need
appeal too.

## Ingredients
1. **Clarity** - readable silhouette and one idea.
2. **Rhythm** - lines flow into each other; a curve on one side answered by a straighter line
   on the other (straights against curves).
3. **Weight** - balance over the support, believable centre of gravity.
4. **Asymmetry** - no twinning; a tilt in head and shoulders.
5. **Design** - shapes have variety (big, medium, small); tangents avoided.
6. **Intent** - the pose shows what the character thinks.

## Diagnosing an unappealing pose
| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Stiff | straight spine, twinning | curve line of action, offset sides |
| Confusing | limbs overlapping body | open silhouette, rotate to camera |
| Ugly shapes | tangents, even spacing of parts | vary sizes, move tangencies |
| Off balance | CoG outside support | move hips over foot |
| Bland | no intent | push the thought, tilt the head |

## Method
1. Thumbnail 3-5 variations of the key pose.
2. Choose by silhouette and gesture at small size.
3. Refine with straights against curves and asymmetry.

## Verify
- [ ] Silhouette passes.
- [ ] Line of action continuous.
- [ ] No tangents or twinning at key parts.
- [ ] A viewer can describe the character's attitude from the pose.
