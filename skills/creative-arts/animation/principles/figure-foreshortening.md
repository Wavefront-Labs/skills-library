# Figure foreshortening

Goal: draw limbs and bodies pointing toward or away from the viewer so they read as deep
rather than short and stubby.

## Method
1. **Think in cylinders and boxes**: a forearm pointing at camera is a short cylinder seen
   end-on - its ellipse is nearly a circle.
2. **Overlap is the main cue**: the near form overlaps the far one (hand overlaps forearm,
   forearm overlaps upper arm). Draw overlaps as clear T-junctions.
3. **Size change**: near parts larger (a fist toward camera can be bigger than the head).
4. **Ellipse direction**: cross-section ellipses curve consistently toward the viewer's side.
5. **Cross-contours** on the forms (sleeve folds, belt line, muscle wraps) curve around the
   depth direction.
6. **Shorten lengths** - measure apparent length, not known length.

## In animation
- Foreshortened poses are weak in silhouette - check readability and consider cheating the
  angle a little toward profile.
- Spacing in depth: a punch toward camera covers little screen distance but grows in size;
  time the size change.
- In CG, wide lenses exaggerate foreshortening (good for impact, bad for faces); long lenses
  flatten it.

## Failure modes
- Drawing the full known length - limbs look broken or extremely long.
- Missing overlaps - the arm looks flat and short.
- Ellipses inconsistent between connected forms.

## Verify
- [ ] Overlaps clear at every joint pointing at the viewer.
- [ ] Near parts bigger in proportion to depth.
- [ ] Silhouette still communicates the action (or a cheat is applied).
