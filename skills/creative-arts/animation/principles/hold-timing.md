# Hold timing

Goal: give important poses and thoughts enough screen time to register, and keep the
character alive while held.

## Minimum read times at 24 fps (starting points)
| Content | Hold |
|---|---|
| Simple pose change (accent) | 4-8 frames |
| Clear expression the audience must read | 12-18 frames |
| Reaction the story depends on | 18-36 frames |
| Text/prop detail the audience must read | 1 frame per ~3-4 letters, minimum 36 |
Wider shots and complex compositions need longer holds than close-ups.

## Moving hold vs dead hold
- **Moving hold**: the pose keeps a slight drift (2-5 % of the move), plus breathing, blink or
  eye darts. Standard in CG and feature 2D - an exact freeze looks like the render stopped.
- **Dead hold** (exact freeze): legitimate in limited/anime styles and for comic freezes
  (a character frozen in shock). Make it intentional.

## Method
1. Mark the beats that must read and assign hold lengths first, then fit the moves between.
2. Create moving holds by keying the hold start and end with a small offset or a subtle settle.
3. Hold lengths vary: give the main beat the longest hold; trim minor ones.

## Failure modes
- No hold on the key pose: the audience never sees it.
- Holds padded "for safety": the scene drags. If a hold has no new thought, cut it.
- CG holds with drift that is too large: floaty.
- Every hold the same length: mechanical rhythm.

## Verify
- [ ] Fresh viewer can describe each key expression after one viewing.
- [ ] No exact-freeze frames unless stylistically intended.
- [ ] Each hold has a reason (read, thought, comedic beat).
