# Weight shift in standing poses

Goal: a standing character moves weight from one foot to the other believably - the most
common action in dialogue shots and a frequent animation-test subject.

## Mechanics
1. **Anticipation** - a small dip or a tiny move toward the current support foot before going
   the other way.
2. **Hips travel** laterally over the new support foot; they dip slightly in the middle of the
   travel (an arc, not a straight line).
3. **Hip tilt** flips: the new unweighted side drops.
4. **Shoulders counter-tilt**; head stays near level (the head stabilises).
5. **Unweighted leg** bends at the knee, may slide or reposition its foot.
6. **Settle** with a small overshoot.

## Timing at 24 fps
- Casual shift: 12-20 frames for hip travel, plus settle.
- Impatient shift: 6-10 frames, sharper.
- Tired: 20+ frames, heavier dip.

## Motivation
Weight shifts happen on thought changes or emphasis: shifting toward someone (engagement),
away (doubt), from foot to foot (impatience). Place them on beats in the dialogue, not at random.

## Failure modes
- Hips sliding sideways in a straight line: no weight.
- Both legs straight throughout.
- Feet sliding while planted.
- Head moving the full hip distance (head should travel less).

## Verify
- [ ] Hip path is an arc with a dip.
- [ ] Hip tilt flips with the support change.
- [ ] Planted foot stays locked; free leg bends.
- [ ] Shift timed to a beat in the performance.
