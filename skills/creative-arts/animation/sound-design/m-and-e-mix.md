# M and E mix for international

**Goal:** a mix with everything except the original-language dialogue, so dubbing territories add their own
voices and get a programme that sounds complete.

## Why animation is easier (and where it is not)

Live action needs heavy fill because production dialogue tracks carry footsteps and props. In animation all
foley and effects are built separately, so M&E is mostly the final mix minus DX. The exceptions:

- **Efforts and non-verbals** (laughs, grunts, breaths) recorded on the dialogue track: they are language-specific
  in performance; usually dubbed, but list them.
- **Walla:** intelligible walla is language-specific, general murmur is not
  (see `creative-arts.animation.sound-design.walla-and-crowd-loops`). Keep both on separate stems.
- **Songs:** sung vocals may be dubbed; deliver the song's instrumental and the vocal separately.
- **Effects processed with dialogue** (a robot voice's servo layer on the DX track) must move to FX.

## Building it

1. Mix with clean stems from the start: DX, MX, FX, FOL, BG, walla (see `creative-arts.animation.sound-design.final-mix`).
2. M&E = MX + FX + FOL + BG + general walla, mixed at the same levels as the domestic mix (the domestic mix minus DX
   should already sound right).
3. Fill any gaps where the DX removal leaves a hole (a scene relying on dialogue for room sound gets a matching BG).
4. Deliver alongside a DX stem and an optional/stem split M&E (music separate from effects) per the distributor spec.

## Deliverable checks

- Platform specs (e.g. Netflix) require a fully filled M&E for originals, in the same channel configurations as the
  primary mix (5.1 and/or stereo), plus stems.
- QC: listen to M&E end to end; no original-language words should remain (except songs or signs listed as
  approved).

## Verify

Domestic mix and (M&E + DX stem) summed in phase null to near silence (residual under about -60 dBFS), proving
stems are complete; the M&E played alone contains no audible original dialogue.
