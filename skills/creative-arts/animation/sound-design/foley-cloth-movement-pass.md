# Cloth and movement pass

**Goal:** a continuous, low-level layer of body movement that makes characters feel physically present. Animation
has no production sound, so without this pass characters sound like floating cut-outs.

## Fabric kit

| Costume | Performed with |
|---|---|
| Cotton T-shirt, casual | cotton shirt rubbed against itself or jeans |
| Leather jacket | an actual leather jacket or a leather bag |
| Nylon/sport, raincoat | nylon windbreaker |
| Armour, robots | small metal parts in a pouch, chain, keys (or a separate props layer) |
| Heavy coat/cape | wool blanket, canvas |

Keep one "character cloth" per main character for the whole series so they are consistent across episodes.

## Performing

- One pass per main character, performed to picture following the whole body: arms, sits, turns, stands. Start
  1-2 frames before visible motion (anticipation reads better than late).
- Mic close (30-60 cm), as quiet a room as possible; cloth is low level and noise shows.
- Do not perform every tiny move; follow the gestures that matter, keep a soft continuous bed for walking.

## Editing and level

- Sits well under dialogue: typically 10-20 dB below dialogue; the audience should notice it only when it is gone.
- Cut out cloth that masks dialogue consonants; high-pass around 100-150 Hz to avoid rumble.
- In a stylized show, cloth can be minimal, but robot or armoured characters need their movement layer (servo,
  rattle) as part of their identity (see `creative-arts.animation.sound-design.foley-design`).

## Verify

Play the scene with and without the cloth track: with it, characters feel weighted and present; dialogue
intelligibility is unchanged (no consonant masking) at the final mix level.
