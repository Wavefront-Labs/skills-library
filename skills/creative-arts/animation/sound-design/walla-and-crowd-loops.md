# Walla and crowd group loops

**Goal:** crowds and background characters that sound alive, are believable for the location, and do not
compete with the principal dialogue.

## Kinds

| Kind | Content |
|---|---|
| General walla | unintelligible murmur of a crowd (restaurant, classroom, stadium) |
| Specific walla | short audible lines from background characters ("Excuse me", "Two coffees") |
| Callouts / efforts | reactions, cheers, gasps, efforts on cue |

## Recording a loop group

- 4-10 actors, recorded in a group to picture; build crowd size by doubling/tripling passes with actors moving
  position relative to the mic.
- Give the group a **walla sheet**: scene, location, who the crowd is, era/region, what they are talking about, and
  any scripted specifics with timecodes.
- Record clean of the main dialogue; mic at a distance appropriate to the scene's perspective.

## Building from library

Layer library crowd beds (sized to the room) plus a few specific lines; loop the bed with a long crossfade so the
repeat point is not obvious (see `creative-arts.animation.sound-design.background-tracks-cutting`).

## Rules

- **Intelligibility:** general walla must not contain clear words; audiences lock on to them and lose the main
  dialogue. Specific walla goes in the gaps of the principal dialogue.
- **Language:** walla in the original language may need to be re-recorded for dubs if intelligible; keep it on a
  separate stem so the M&E carries a language-neutral version (see `creative-arts.animation.sound-design.m-and-e-mix`).
- **Clearances:** no real names, brands or anything defamatory in specifics.

## Verify

In the full mix, principal dialogue stays fully intelligible over the walla, no word of general walla can be
transcribed, and specifics sit only in dialogue gaps.
