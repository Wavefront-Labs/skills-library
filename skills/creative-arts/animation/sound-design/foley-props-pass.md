# Props pass

**Goal:** every object a character handles or touches (cups, doors, keys, phones, paper, weapons, food) has a
sound in sync with the contact.

## Cue list

Spot from the cut: shot, timecode, prop, action (pick up, set down, open, drop), material. Group by prop so the
same object is recorded once for all its uses in the episode and sounds the same each time.

## Sourcing objects

- Choose what **sounds** right: a cartoon sci-fi gadget may be a stapler plus a camera shutter; a sword draw is a
  metal ruler on a file; an egg crack is a stalk of celery or an actual egg.
- Keep a props room catalogue (photo + description + what it has been used for).
- For hero props, record several distinct performances and pick in the edit.

## Recording

- Close mic (20-50 cm), performed to picture; one prop per take; slate it.
- Split contacts: record the pick-up and the set-down as separate takes if they happen far apart.
- Record clean (no handling noise of other objects); cloth goes on the cloth pass, not here.

## Editing

- Sync contact transients on the contact frame (animation gives exact frames).
- Layer when one take lacks weight: e.g. a mug set-down = ceramic tap + a low wooden thud.
- Props that are part of a gag may be replaced by designed effects or cartoon sounds
  (see `creative-arts.animation.sound-design.cartoon-sfx-vocabulary`); decide in spotting
  (`creative-arts.animation.editorial.spotting-sessions`) so foley and effects do not both cover the same prop.

## Verify

Solo the props track against picture: each listed cue is present, in sync within one frame, and the same prop
sounds consistent across the episode. Overall foley craft: `creative-arts.animation.sound-design.foley-design`.
