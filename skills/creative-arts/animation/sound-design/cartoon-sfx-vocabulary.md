# Cartoon sound effects vocabulary

**Goal:** comedic, non-literal sound that tells the audience how to feel about an action, used consistently with
the show's style.

## Core vocabulary

| Action | Classic sound |
|---|---|
| Fast exit, zip off | zip/whistle up, then a pop or a ricochet |
| Head bonk, hit | wood block, frying pan "bong", bonk with a high ring |
| Fall | descending slide whistle, then a crash |
| Take, double-take | "boing" (jaw harp/spring), orchestral hit |
| Sneaking/tiptoeing | pizzicato strings, xylophone single notes per step |
| Eye blink | small tick or "plink" |
| Stretch/squash | rubber stretch, balloon squeak |
| Dizzy | birds tweeting, ringing bell |
| Spring/bounce | "boing" (spring door stop) |

Many classic studio libraries (Hanna-Barbera, Warner) exist in commercial collections; check licences. Synthesis
(slide whistle with a sine sweep, wood block with a short filtered click) covers much of it.

## Mickey-mousing

Matching music or sound exactly to every movement (named after early Disney shorts). Strong for slapstick and
children's shows; tiring and dated in dramatic or adult animation. Decide per show in the **sound bible**:
literal (realistic), heightened (realistic but exaggerated), or cartoon (full vocabulary).

## Style rules

- One style per show: mixing realistic gun sounds with a cartoon boing works only as a deliberate gag.
- Keep a show palette: the same 20-50 signature sounds reused across episodes builds identity (the way a
  character's footstep or a gadget sound becomes recognisable).
- Comedic timing: the sound often lands a frame or two **after** the impact, or after a pause, for the laugh.

## Verify

Spot a scene with the director using the sound bible: each cartoon cue is listed as intent, and a review of three
episodes shows signature sounds used consistently. Related: `creative-arts.animation.sound-design.sfx-design`.
