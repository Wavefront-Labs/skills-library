# Music licensing (sync and master rights)

**Goal:** every piece of music in the final mix is licensed for the uses, territories and term the distributor
requires. Not legal advice; involve a clearance specialist or lawyer for commercial releases.

## Two rights per recording

| Right | Who owns it | Licence |
|---|---|---|
| Composition (melody, lyrics) | songwriter/publisher | **sync licence** to synchronise with picture |
| Sound recording (that specific recording) | label / master owner | **master use licence** |

Using a commercial song needs both. Re-recording the song yourself (a cover) still needs sync from the publisher,
but not master use. Public-domain compositions can still have copyrighted recordings.

## Options, cheapest risk first

1. **Original score** commissioned for the show under a work-for-hire or licence agreement that grants all needed
   rights (see `creative-arts.animation.sound-design.scoring`). Register cues with the PRO for performance royalties.
2. **Production (library) music:** pre-cleared both rights from one licensor; check the licence tier covers
   broadcast/streaming/theatrical, worldwide, in perpetuity, and trailers/promos if needed.
3. **Commercial songs:** negotiated sync + master; expensive and slow; quote requests list scene description, use
   (background vocal, visual vocal, main title), duration, media, territory, term.

## Terms to check

Media (all media, or only streaming), territory (worldwide), term (perpetuity), MFN clauses, step deals for
festival-only vs commercial release, and whether promos/trailers are covered.

## YouTube

Library tracks registered in Content ID may claim your upload even when licensed; whitelist the channel with the
library before release, or use libraries that do not register in Content ID. Keep licence certificates per track.

## Verify

The music cue sheet (`creative-arts.animation.sound-design.music-cue-sheets`) lists every cue with a matching licence
document covering the release's media, territory and term; zero temp tracks remain.
