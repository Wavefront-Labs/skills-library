# Scaling figures with the horizon line

Goal: characters at the correct size anywhere in a perspective environment.

## Core rule
The horizon line is the camera's eye height. Every object of the same real height
standing on the same ground plane is cut by the HL at the same proportion of its height.
- Camera at a standing adult's eye height: every standing adult's eyes sit on the HL,
  near or far.
- Camera at waist height: the HL crosses every standing adult at the waist.
- Seated camera: HL at chest level of standing figures.

## Transferring a height
1. Draw one reference figure at the desired size.
2. From the reference's feet and head, run lines to any VP on the HL.
3. Slide along those lines to a new depth; drop a vertical; the lines give the height there.
4. To move sideways, first move along a horizontal (parallel to the HL), then into depth.

## Uneven ground and different heights
- Figures on stairs or hills: find where they would stand on the base plane, scale there,
  then raise them.
- Children and giants: scale from the adult reference by ratio, not by HL alone.

## Failure modes
- Distant figures drawn too big or small relative to the near ones (floating giants).
- HL drawn high on the page but characters drawn as if seen at eye level.
- Crowds where heads wander above and below the HL randomly.

## Verify
- [ ] HL cuts every same-height figure at the same body point.
- [ ] Transferred heights built with VP lines.
- [ ] Figures on raised ground scaled from the base plane.
