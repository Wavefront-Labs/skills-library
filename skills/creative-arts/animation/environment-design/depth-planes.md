# Depth planes

Goal: environments organised into clear depth planes so the viewer reads space instantly and
the acting plane is obvious.

## The three (or more) planes
- **Foreground (FG)**: closest elements - framing shapes, often darkest (or lightest), least
  detail, may be out of focus.
- **Midground (MG)**: the acting plane - where characters are; highest detail and the strongest
  contrast near the focal point.
- **Background (BG)**: distant elements - lighter, lower contrast, cooler/bluer in daylight, least
  saturation.
Complex shots may use 4-5 planes (near FG, FG, MG, far MG, sky).

## Method
1. Thumbnail the shot in three flat values, one per plane.
2. Assign each plane a value range that does not overlap with its neighbours (e.g. FG 70-90 %
   dark, MG 30-70 %, BG 10-30 %).
3. Put the characters in the MG plane's contrast range so they separate from both FG and BG.
4. Reduce detail and edge sharpness with distance.
5. Keep planes as separate layers (2D) or depth groups (3D) for parallax and comp.

## Failure modes
- Planes merging (same values in FG and BG) - flat, confusing.
- Character in a value that matches the BG behind it.
- Uniform detail everywhere.

## Verify
- [ ] Three-value thumbnail reads the planes.
- [ ] Characters separate from both adjacent planes.
- [ ] Detail and contrast decrease with distance.
