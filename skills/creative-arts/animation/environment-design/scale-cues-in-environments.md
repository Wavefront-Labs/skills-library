# Scale cues and human-scale references

Goal: viewers instantly know if a space is cosy or colossal. Without known-size objects
an environment has no scale.

## Known-size cues
- Doors (about 2 m), steps (about 18 cm rise), windows, railings, chairs, cars.
- People and animals: tiny figures in the distance establish monumental size.
- Repeating elements (bricks, floor tiles, windows per storey) - their count gives size.
- Atmospheric perspective: huge things far away get hazy.
- Detail density: a giant structure shows many small details; a model looks too simple.

## Method
1. Decide the intended scale feeling (intimate, normal, vast, impossible).
2. Put at least one human-scale element in every establishing frame.
3. For huge spaces: keep the scale cue small and in the lower frame, use a low camera
   and haze for distance, add repetition (hundreds of windows).
4. For miniatures or giant characters: reverse it - oversized everyday objects (a pencil the
   size of a tree for a tiny character).
5. Keep the scale consistent across shots (same door height to character ratio).

## Failure modes
- Large structures with no reference - reads as a toy.
- Doors and steps sized wrong for the characters.
- Too much haze on near objects or none on far ones, confusing distance.

## Verify
- [ ] Every establishing shot has a known-size object.
- [ ] Door and step heights match the size comparison sheet.
- [ ] Repetition and haze support the intended scale.
