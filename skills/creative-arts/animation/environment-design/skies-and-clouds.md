# Skies and clouds

Goal: skies that are designed composition elements with correct light logic.

## Sky gradient
- A clear sky is darkest and most saturated overhead or 90 degrees from the sun, lighter
  and less saturated toward the horizon (more atmosphere to look through).
- Near the sun it brightens and warms; at sunset the horizon goes warm and the zenith
  stays blue.
- The sky is the light source for shadows (blue fill), so its value controls shadow values.

## Clouds
- **Types**: cumulus (puffy, flat bottoms), stratus (layers), cirrus (high wisps),
  cumulonimbus (storm towers). Each implies weather and mood.
- **Perspective**: clouds lie on a ceiling plane; they get smaller, flatter and closer
  together toward the horizon. Flat cumulus bottoms line up on one height.
- **Light**: lit tops and sun side, cooler shadow sides, often lighter in shadow than the
  ground. Sunlight through thin edges glows (silver lining); thick cores go dark.
- **Edges**: varied - hard on lit sides, soft and lost toward the sky.

## Composition
- Use cloud shapes as leading lines and framing (diagonals point at the focal area).
- Keep the sky quieter than the subject; strongest cloud contrast away from character heads
  or deliberately framing them.

## Method
1. Paint the gradient from the sun position.
2. Block cloud masses on the perspective ceiling.
3. Light them as forms; vary edges.
4. Check composition and value against the characters.

## Failure modes
- Cotton balls stuck on a flat blue.
- Clouds the same size from top of frame to horizon.
- Sky competing with characters.

## Verify
- [ ] Gradient follows sun position.
- [ ] Clouds diminish toward the horizon.
- [ ] Sky value supports the subject.
