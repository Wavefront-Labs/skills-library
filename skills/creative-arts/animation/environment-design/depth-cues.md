# Depth cues

Goal: create the illusion of depth on a flat screen deliberately, using the cues the visual system
relies on.

## The cues
1. **Overlap (occlusion)**: a near object partly hides a far one - the strongest cue. Use clear
   T-junctions; avoid tangents.
2. **Size diminution**: same-size objects (trees, posts, people) shrink with distance.
3. **Texture gradient**: texture becomes finer and denser with distance (cobbles, grass, crowds).
4. **Vertical position**: below the horizon, farther things sit higher in frame; above it (clouds,
   ceilings), lower.
5. **Linear perspective**: converging parallel lines.
6. **Atmospheric perspective**: lower contrast and saturation with distance.
7. **Focus/depth of field**: blurred FG/BG around a sharp subject.
8. **Motion parallax**: near layers move faster than far ones during camera moves.

## Method
1. Decide how much depth the shot needs (deep vs flat - see Block's space types).
2. Add cues for depth or remove them for flatness.
3. Use repeated elements of known size (fence posts, windows) to make diminution and texture
   gradient obvious.

## Failure modes
- Tangent edges that flatten overlaps.
- Repeated elements not diminishing consistently (scale errors).
- Contradicting cues (a far object overlapping a near one) - unintentional ambiguity.

## Verify
- [ ] At least three cues reinforce the intended depth.
- [ ] No tangents at overlaps.
- [ ] Repeated elements diminish consistently with the perspective.
