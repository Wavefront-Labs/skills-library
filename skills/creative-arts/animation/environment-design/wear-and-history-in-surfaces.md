# History and wear in surfaces

Goal: surfaces that show age and use logically, so a viewer understands the past of a place
without exposition.

## Where wear goes (logic, not noise)
- **Touch points**: door handles, light switches, stair centres, chair arms - polished, darkened
  by hands, paint rubbed off.
- **Traffic paths**: worn floor tracks between door and desk; grass trampled into a path.
- **Weather**: streaks under window sills and roof edges, rust bleeding downward from metal,
  moss on the shaded/damp side, sun fading on the sunny side.
- **Edges and corners**: chipped first; flat centres stay intact.
- **Repairs**: patches, mismatched replacement boards, tape - show care or poverty.
- **Layers**: old paint under new, posters over posters, ghost signs.

## Method
1. Write the place's history in three lines (who built it, who uses it, what happened).
2. Decide the wear level from that story (new, maintained, neglected, abandoned).
3. Mark touch points, traffic paths and weather faces on the layout or blockout.
4. Paint or texture wear only there; leave areas untouched for contrast.
5. Keep wear scaled to the style: graphic styles use a few shapes, not grunge brushes.

## Failure modes
- Uniform grunge overlay on everything - reads as a filter, not history.
- Wear contradicting use (a pristine door handle on a centuries-old door).
- Detail noise competing with characters.

## Verify
- [ ] History written and wear level chosen.
- [ ] Wear follows touch, traffic and weather logic.
- [ ] Clean areas left for contrast and character read.
