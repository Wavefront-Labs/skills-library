# One-point perspective

Goal: environments where the main planes face the camera and all depth lines converge
on one vanishing point (VP) - corridors, roads, rooms seen head-on, symmetrical shots.

## Setup
1. Place the horizon line (HL) at the camera's eye height. Low HL = low camera, high HL = high camera.
2. Place the VP on the HL. Centre VP = formal, symmetrical, confrontational (Kubrick
   corridors); off-centre VP = more natural, still directional.
3. Frontal planes stay true rectangles (verticals vertical, horizontals horizontal).
4. All lines perpendicular to the picture plane run to the VP.

## Spacing equal intervals in depth
1. Draw the first interval (e.g. a door width or floor tile) on a receding wall.
2. Find its centre by crossing its diagonals; draw a line from the VP through that centre.
3. Draw a diagonal from the top corner through the midpoint of the far edge; where it hits
   the base line is the next interval. Repeat. Intervals shrink correctly.
4. Alternatively use a measuring point (diagonal VP) on the HL at the distance of the
   station point from the VP.

## When to use
- Strong pull into depth, tunnel vision, endless corridors, train tracks.
- Staging a character dead centre for confrontation or isolation.
- Flat graphic styles that want frontal shapes.

## Failure modes
- Objects rotated relative to the picture plane drawn in one-point (they need two VPs).
- VP too close to the frame centre when the shot needs asymmetry.
- Wide-angle distortion at the frame edges when the VP is far outside the image area.

## Verify
- [ ] All depth lines hit the single VP.
- [ ] Frontal planes are true rectangles.
- [ ] Repeated intervals shrink by diagonal construction, not guesswork.
