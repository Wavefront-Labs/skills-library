# Ellipses in perspective

Goal: wheels, cups, columns, round tables, towers and portholes that sit in the same
space as the rest of the environment.

## Rules
1. **Inscribe in a square**: a circle in perspective is the ellipse that touches the four
   edge midpoints of its perspective square.
2. **Minor axis = axis of the cylinder**: the short axis of the ellipse lines up with the
   cylinder's central axis (a wheel's axle, a column's vertical line). This is the most
   common error.
3. **Degree changes with distance from the HL**: ellipses near the horizon are thin
   (low degree); further above or below they open up. A stack of plates: tops near eye
   level almost a line, lower ones wider.
4. **Perspective centre is not the ellipse centre**: the true centre (from the square's
   diagonals) sits slightly behind the ellipse's geometric centre. Spokes and handles
   attach at the true centre.
5. The ellipse is always symmetrical; never draw egg shapes or pointed ends.

## Method
1. Draw the perspective square; cross its diagonals.
2. Mark edge midpoints; draw the ellipse through them, smooth and symmetric.
3. Check the minor axis against the cylinder axis.
4. For cylinders, draw both end ellipses; the far one is more open if further from the HL.
5. Use ellipse guides or a digital ellipse ruler for clean final lines.

## Failure modes
- Pointed or lopsided ellipses (football shapes).
- Same ellipse degree at top and bottom of a tall cylinder.
- Wheels whose minor axis does not point along the axle.

## Verify
- [ ] Minor axis aligned to the cylinder axis.
- [ ] Degree opens away from the HL.
- [ ] Ellipses symmetrical, tangent to the square.
