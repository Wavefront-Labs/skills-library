# Landmarks and wayfinding

Goal: an audience that always knows where characters are and where they are going.
Kevin Lynch's five elements of a city image apply directly: paths, edges, districts,
nodes and landmarks.

## Elements
- **Landmarks**: unique, visible from far away (a tower, a giant tree, a mountain peak, a castle).
  Distinct silhouette and often a unique colour or light.
- **Paths**: roads, rivers, corridors the characters travel; consistent direction on screen.
- **Edges**: walls, coastlines, cliffs - boundaries.
- **Districts**: zones with their own palette and shape language (market, slum, palace).
- **Nodes**: junctions, plazas where paths meet and scenes happen.

## Method
1. Draw a map of the world/set with the five elements before painting keys.
2. Give each district a distinct palette and shape vocabulary.
3. Place one or two landmarks visible from most locations; show them in establishing shots
   to orient the audience (e.g. the castle always on the horizon to screen-left).
4. Keep screen direction consistent: home is always to one side.
5. Use the landmark as a progress meter in journeys (growing larger as they approach).

## Failure modes
- Every street looks the same; the audience is lost.
- Landmark seen from impossible angles relative to the map.
- Districts sharing palettes.

## Verify
- [ ] Map with paths, edges, districts, nodes, landmarks.
- [ ] Landmark position consistent across shots.
- [ ] Districts distinguishable in greyscale and colour.
