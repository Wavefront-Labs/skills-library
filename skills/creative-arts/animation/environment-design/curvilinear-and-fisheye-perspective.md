# Curvilinear and fisheye perspective

Goal: extreme wide-angle and fisheye views where straight lines bow, used for
comic distortion, POV shots, skate/action energy and tight interiors.

## Systems
- **Four-point**: horizon and centre vertical straight; other horizontal and
  vertical lines become arcs through two horizontal VPs and two vertical VPs (zenith and nadir).
  Used for panoramic 180-degree views.
- **Five-point (fisheye)**: a centre VP for lines pointing straight away, plus four VPs on a
  circle at 180 degrees. Every line not passing through the centre bows outward like a
  sphere's meridian.

## Method
1. Draw a circle for the 180-degree field; mark the four VPs on it (left, right, top, bottom)
   and the centre VP.
2. Build a grid of arcs: meridians through the top and bottom VPs, parallels through the
   left and right VPs. Lines through the centre stay straight.
3. Block forms on the grid; the closer to the edge, the more they curve and compress.
4. Use digital help: Clip Studio and Procreate have curvilinear/fisheye rulers; or
   render a 3D blockout with a fisheye/panoramic camera and paint over it.

## Uses
- Comedic close-ups (nose bulging at camera), dizzy or drunk POV, music-video energy.
- Showing a whole room in one frame.

## Failure modes
- Some lines curved, others straight inconsistently.
- Characters not distorted to match the environment; they look pasted on.
- Overuse: distortion stops meaning anything.

## Verify
- [ ] Only lines through the centre are straight.
- [ ] Characters follow the same distortion.
- [ ] Used for a stated story reason.
