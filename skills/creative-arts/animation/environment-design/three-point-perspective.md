# Three-point perspective

Goal: shots where the camera tilts up or down, so verticals converge to a third VP -
skyscrapers from the street, cliffs from above, giants.

## Setup
1. Two VPs on the HL as in two-point.
2. The third VP (VP3) sits on a vertical line through the centre of vision: above the
   frame when looking up (worm's-eye), below it when looking down (bird's-eye).
3. The three VPs form a triangle; the centre of vision lies inside it.
4. When the camera tilts up, the HL moves down in the frame (possibly out of it).

## Rules of thumb
- The further VP3 is from the frame, the gentler the convergence (telephoto feel).
  Close VP3 = extreme wide-angle drama.
- Keep the VP triangle large relative to the frame to avoid distortion.
- Heights still measure in proportion along converging verticals; use diagonals to
  split floors evenly.

## Uses
- Making a character or building feel huge (worm's-eye) or vulnerable (bird's-eye).
- Vertigo and falling shots.
- Establishing cities and canyons.

## Failure modes
- Mixing straight verticals and converging ones in the same shot.
- VP3 too close: buildings look like they are falling over.
- Tilt in perspective but characters drawn with no matching foreshortening.

## Verify
- [ ] All verticals converge on VP3.
- [ ] Centre of vision inside the VP triangle.
- [ ] Characters foreshortened to match the camera tilt.
