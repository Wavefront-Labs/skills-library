# Shape language for environments

Goal: places whose shapes say how to feel about them - the same grammar used for
characters applied to architecture and landscape.

## Vocabulary
| Shape | Reads as | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Round, organic | safe, warm, natural, playful | hobbit holes, village of a gentle culture |
| Square, rectilinear | order, stability, bureaucracy, oppression when overused | offices, fortresses, dystopian blocks |
| Triangle, spikes | danger, aggression, dynamism | villain lairs, thorny forests, crystal caves |

## Method
1. Map each location to an emotional role in the story (sanctuary, threat, institution).
2. Assign a dominant shape and a secondary accent (a round cottage with a few triangular
   roof spikes = cosy with a hint of danger).
3. Echo the owner's character shapes in their space (villain's angular design and angular lair).
4. Shift shapes with the story: a home that becomes threatening gets more angular dressing,
   broken and leaning forms.
5. Check silhouettes of locations side by side - each should be distinct.

## Failure modes
- Mixed shapes everywhere; no emotional read.
- Shape language fighting the colour script (cosy rounds lit as horror without intent).
- Only the hero building styled; filler architecture breaks the vocabulary.

## Verify
- [ ] Every location has a dominant shape tied to its story role.
- [ ] Owner and space share shape language where intended.
- [ ] Location silhouettes distinct.
