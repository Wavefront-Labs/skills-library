# A space that reveals its owner

Goal: the audience learns who someone is from their room before they appear (Carl's house in
Up, Andy's room in Toy Story, Rey's AT-AT home).

## Questions to answer
- What do they value (objects displayed prominently)?
- What do they hide (closed drawers, covered items)?
- How orderly are they (alignment, clutter, symmetry)?
- What is their history (photos, trophies, inherited objects, wear)?
- What is their state now (unpacked boxes, abandoned hobby, empty chair)?
- Money and era: furniture age, quality, repairs.

## Method
1. Write 5 character facts; translate each into an object or arrangement.
2. Design the room's shape and colour from the dominant trait (cramped and warm for a
   hoarder; open, cool and symmetrical for a control freak).
3. Choose one hero object that tells the core story; give it a clear spot and light.
4. Arrange clutter density by personality, but keep the acting area clear.
5. Plan how the space changes with the arc (a later version of the room).

## Failure modes
- Generic room dressed from a catalogue.
- Story objects too small or too dim to read.
- Clutter that blocks the staging.

## Verify
- [ ] Each object traces to a character fact.
- [ ] Hero object is readable in the establishing shot.
- [ ] Arc version planned if the story needs it.
