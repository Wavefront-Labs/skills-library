# Controlling idea

Goal: a single sentence that says what the story means, used to decide what stays in and what gets cut.

## 1. Form (McKee)
**Controlling idea = value + cause.**
- Value: the positive or negative charge of the core value at the climax.
- Cause: the chief reason the world arrives at that value.
- Examples: "Justice is restored because the protagonist is more violent than the criminals."
  "Love triumphs when we sacrifice our own needs." "Friendship is lost when pride wins."

## 2. Types
- **Idealistic** (up-ending): optimism, positive value wins.
- **Pessimistic** (down-ending): the negative value wins; cautionary.
- **Ironic** (up/down): both at once ("he got what he wanted and lost what he needed").

## 3. Counter-idea
- The story argues both sides: the counter-idea (the opposite) must be strongly dramatised so the ending is
  earned. Scenes alternate between the idea and the counter-idea.

## 4. Using it
- It is found during development and rewriting; write it after the first draft, refine it, then cut or
  reshape scenes that don't argue for or against it.
- Distinguish from a premise (the "what if" that started the idea) and from theme words ("love").

## 5. Test
- Is it a sentence with a cause, not a single word?
- Does the climax dramatise exactly this sentence?

## Verify
- [ ] Controlling idea written as value + cause.
- [ ] Counter-idea identified and dramatised.
- [ ] Climax expresses the idea.
