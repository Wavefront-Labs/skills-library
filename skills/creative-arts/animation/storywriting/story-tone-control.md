# Tone control

Goal: the audience always knows how to feel, and shifts between comedy and drama feel intended.

## 1. Define the tonal range
- Write the tone as a range: "warm comedy with real stakes", "absurd but never cruel".
- List what the show/film never does (e.g. no deaths on screen, no meta jokes).
- Pick reference films for each end of the range.

## 2. Set the tone early
- The opening scenes promise the tone; the audience calibrates on them. If you will go dark later, hint
  early (a sad note in a comic opening).

## 3. Transitions
- Shift tone at scene or sequence boundaries, not mid-beat, unless it's intentional whiplash.
- Bridge with a character reaction: a laugh that fades into a realisation.
- After a sincere moment, give it a beat of silence before any joke.

## 4. Undercutting
- Jokes immediately after emotional beats can feel defensive; use deliberately (to show a character's
  avoidance) or not at all.
- Comic characters in serious scenes can stay in character but play the scene straight.

## 5. All departments
- Tone is carried by design, colour, music and acting too; share the tonal range in the bible.

## Verify
- [ ] Tonal range and "never" list written.
- [ ] Opening establishes the range.
- [ ] Emotional beats protected from undercutting jokes.
