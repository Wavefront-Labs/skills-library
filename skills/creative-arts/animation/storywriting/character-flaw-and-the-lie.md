# Flaw and the lie the character believes

Goal: a specific inner problem the story is built to test.

## 1. Build the chain
**Wound (ghost) -> lie -> flaw -> behaviour.**
- **Wound**: a past event that hurt the character (rarely shown in full; hinted).
- **Lie**: a false belief about themselves or the world they formed to protect themselves:
  "If I rely on anyone, I'll be abandoned."
- **Flaw**: the behaviour pattern the lie produces: controlling, refuses help, lies, arrogance.
- **Behaviour**: what we see on screen in the setup.

## 2. Make the plot attack the lie
- Design the inciting incident and complications to hit the flaw directly.
- The B-story character often embodies the truth that counters the lie.
- The all-is-lost moment is the flaw's cost made unavoidable.

## 3. Show, don't explain
- Introduce the flaw through a choice in the first scenes (the hero turns down help).
- Keep backstory exposition minimal; one well-placed image or object can stand for the wound.

## 4. Resolution
- Positive arc: the character recognises the lie and acts on the truth at the climax.
- Negative arc: the character doubles down on the lie and loses.
- Flat arc: the character already knows the truth and changes the world around them.

## Verify
- [ ] Wound, lie, flaw, behaviour written as a chain.
- [ ] Flaw shown through a choice in the setup.
- [ ] Climax choice confirms or overcomes the lie.
