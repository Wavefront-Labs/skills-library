# Progressive complications

Goal: a middle that escalates, so each sequence costs the hero more than the last.

## 1. Principles (McKee)
- **Law of conflict**: nothing moves forward in a story except through conflict.
- Complications progress: each step involves more risk, more forces of antagonism, and harder choices.
- **Point of no return**: complications build until the hero cannot go back to an earlier, easier action.
- Avoid repetition: the same value at the same charge twice in a row reads as the story stalling.

## 2. Levels of conflict
| Level | Source |
|---|---|
| Inner | the hero's own mind, body, emotions (fear, doubt) |
| Personal | intimate relationships (family, friends, lovers) |
| Extra-personal | society, institutions, environment, antagonists |
Escalation often moves between levels; the strongest climaxes engage all three.

## 3. Method
1. List the hero's attempts to get what they want, in order.
2. For each, write the opposition and the cost; check each is bigger than the previous.
3. Introduce new forces of antagonism (or reveal the antagonist's strength) as the story advances.
4. Mark the point of no return.

## 4. Test
- Can you reorder two act-two sequences without loss? Then they don't progress; rewrite one.
- Is the hero risking more each time?

## Verify
- [ ] Attempts listed with rising cost.
- [ ] At least two levels of conflict engaged.
- [ ] Point of no return identified.
