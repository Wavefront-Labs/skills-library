# Breaking story in a writers room

Goal: turn an episode idea into a scene-by-scene outline the assigned writer can script.

## 1. Before the room
- A **springboard**: one paragraph with the premise, the lead's problem, and the hook.
- Showrunner confirms it fits the series engine and season arc.

## 2. In the room
1. **Blue sky** (15-60 min): throw out ideas, set pieces, jokes, emotional moments, no judging. A writers'
   assistant records everything.
2. **Find the ending** early: what changes for the lead, what's the climax.
3. **Beat the A plot** on a whiteboard or cards: scene-by-scene, each card a one-line turn.
4. **Act breaks**: place turns at breaks; strongest at the last break.
5. **B (and C) plot**: beat them, then weave them into the A plot.
6. **Pressure test**: walk the board top to bottom aloud. Check motivation, cause and effect, act breaks, theme.
7. Pitch the board to the showrunner; adjust.

## 3. Out of the room
- The writer turns the board into an outline (beat outline, 3-10 pages); notes; then script.
- Animation-specific: early board artists may join or receive the outline for visual input.

## 4. Room etiquette
- "Yes, and" during blue sky; criticise ideas, not people; build on others' pitches.
- Keep the room moving: if stuck more than 20 minutes, park it and move on.

## Verify
- [ ] Ending found before detailed beating.
- [ ] Every card is a turn.
- [ ] Board pitched end to end before the outline.
