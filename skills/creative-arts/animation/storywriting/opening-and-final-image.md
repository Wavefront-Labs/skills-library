# Opening image and final image

Goal: one image at the start and one at the end that, side by side, show how the story changed the hero
and the world.

## 1. What they are
- **Opening image** (beat 1, page 1 of ~110): a snapshot of the hero's world and problem before the story
  starts. Sets tone, genre, style and scale.
- **Final image** (beat 15, last page): the mirror of the opening - same subject or composition, changed.
Snyder treats them as a pair: if they are not opposites, the story has no arc.

## 2. Design the pair together
1. State the change in one line: "from alone to belonging", "from fear to courage".
2. Pick a visual situation that can show both states: the same place, action or composition.
3. Opening: show the "before" state plainly (the hero eats alone at a big table).
4. Final: same situation with the "after" state (the table is full; the hero is passing food).
5. For animation, make it read without dialogue - composition, colour and staging carry it.

## 3. Use the visual tools
- Repeat the camera angle or composition so the audience notices the rhyme.
- Change colour key, space or framing to show the shift (enclosed -> open, cool -> warm).

## 4. Failure modes
- Opening that is generic action unrelated to the hero's problem.
- Final image that is just a happy group shot with no link to the opening.
- Change stated in dialogue rather than shown.

## Verify
- [ ] Change line written.
- [ ] Opening and final images share subject or composition.
- [ ] The difference is readable with the sound off.
