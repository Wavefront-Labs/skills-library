# Dramatic irony

Goal: tension and comedy from the audience knowing more than the character.

## 1. Mechanism
- The audience learns something (a threat, a secret, a misunderstanding) that a character does not.
- Every action the character takes is now watched with that knowledge: suspense ("don't open the door")
  or comedy (mistaken identity).

## 2. Uses
| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| Suspense | the audience sees the trap the hero walks toward |
| Comedy | the character boasts while the audience sees the disaster behind them |
| Empathy | we know a character's secret sacrifice others don't |
| Tragedy | the audience knows the ending; the character doesn't |

## 3. Design
1. Decide what the audience knows and when (an information map per scene).
2. Reveal to the audience early through a scene the character isn't in, a POV shot or a cutaway.
3. Plan the **discovery scene** where the character learns it; it's a turning point.
4. Keep the gap open long enough to enjoy but close before it frustrates.

## 4. Boards and staging
- Stage the irony visibly: the threat in the background, the character in the foreground facing away.

- [ ] Information map shows what audience vs characters know.
