# Discarding the first obvious idea

Goal: story choices that surprise the audience without feeling random.

## 1. The rule (Coats, rule 12)
"Discount the 1st thing that comes to mind. And the 2nd, 3rd, 4th, 5th - get the obvious out of the way.
Surprise yourself." The first ideas are the ones the audience will also predict.

## 2. Method
1. Frame the problem as a question: "How does she escape the kitchen?" "What's the villain's motive?"
2. Write 10-20 answers fast, without judging.
3. Cross out the first 3-5.
4. From the rest, pick the answer that is both **surprising** and **inevitable in hindsight** - it should
   come from the character, world and theme.
5. Check it against setups: can it be planted earlier?

## 3. Techniques to go beyond obvious
- Invert: what's the opposite of the expected choice?
- Use the character's flaw or specific skills.
- Use the world's rules or setting specifically.
- Ask what would make it worse for the hero.

## 4. Caution
- Surprise for its own sake breaks logic; the choice must still serve the story.

## Verify
- [ ] At least 10 options listed per key story problem.
- [ ] Chosen idea not among the first few.
- [ ] Chosen idea grows from character or world.
