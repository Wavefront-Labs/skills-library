# All is lost and dark night of the soul

Goal: the hero's lowest point, followed by the moment of reckoning that makes the finale possible.

## 1. All is lost (about 68-75%; page 75 of 110)
- The opposite of the midpoint: if the midpoint was a false victory, here everything collapses.
- Include a **whiff of death**: something dies - a mentor, a relationship, a plan, a dream, a plant on the
  windowsill. It signals the old self or old world ending.
- It should be caused, at least partly, by the hero's flaw, so the loss feels earned.

## 2. Dark night of the soul (about 75-80%)
- The hero sits with the loss. Short in animation (a few shots, often wordless, music-led).
- The hero confronts the lie they believed and the need they ignored.
- End with the **insight**: a new understanding, often triggered by the B-story character or a
  remembered object, which leads to Break into Three.

## 3. Staging for animation
- Isolate the hero visually: wide shot, small figure, low-contrast palette, rain or night.
- Hold longer than feels comfortable; silence works.

## 4. Mistakes
- Low point caused by pure bad luck (no flaw involvement).
- Dark night skipped or rushed so the insight feels arbitrary.
- Insight delivered by someone else's speech without the hero choosing it.

## Verify
- [ ] Loss includes a whiff of death and links to the flaw.
- [ ] Dark night leads to a specific insight.
- [ ] Insight connects to the theme stated earlier.
