# Exposition as ammunition

Goal: information the audience needs, delivered without stopping the story.

## 1. Principles (McKee)
- **Show, don't tell**: dramatise information in action and images.
- **Convert exposition to ammunition**: characters use facts as weapons in conflict to get what they want
  ("You'd know, if you'd been home once in ten years").
- **Pace it**: give information only when the audience wants and needs it; withhold to create curiosity.
- **Avoid "As you know, Bob"**: characters telling each other what both know.

## 2. Techniques
- Conflict scenes where a secret is revealed under pressure.
- Visual exposition: objects, settings, photos, graphics.
- New character in the world as audience surrogate (use sparingly).
- Flashbacks only if they are dramatised scenes with their own turn.

## 3. Animation specifics
- The world's rules can often be shown in one demonstration (how magic works, what the robot does).
- Opening narration is a fallback; use when stylised and short.

## 4. Test
- Mark every line of exposition; ask if the character would say it to that listener in that moment.
- Can it be shown instead? Can it be delayed?

## Verify
- [ ] No "as you know" dialogue.
- [ ] Exposition inside conflict or action.
- [ ] Information timed to audience curiosity.
