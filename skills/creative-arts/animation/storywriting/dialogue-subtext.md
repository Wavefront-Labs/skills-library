# Dialogue subtext

Goal: scenes where the important things are understood, not said.

## 1. Principle (McKee)
- Text: what is said and done. Subtext: what is thought and felt underneath.
- People rarely say exactly what they feel, especially in emotional moments. "On the nose" dialogue
  (characters stating their feelings) kills scenes.

## 2. Techniques
- **Displaced topic**: argue about the dishes when the real issue is the marriage.
- **Action against words**: "I'm fine" while breaking the plate.
- **Avoidance**: changing the subject, jokes, silence.
- **Questions instead of statements**.
- **Context**: the audience knows something the character doesn't say (dramatic irony).

## 3. Writing method
1. For each scene write the subtext first: what each character really wants and feels.
2. Write what they would say if they couldn't say that.
3. Add visible actions that betray the subtext.
4. Brief the voice actor and animator on the subtext so the performance carries it.

## 4. Test
- Highlight lines where characters state feelings directly; rewrite most.
- Does the audience still understand the real issue? If not, add one clearer signal.

## Verify
- [ ] Subtext written per character per scene.
- [ ] Few on-the-nose emotional statements.
- [ ] Action or behaviour reveals subtext.
