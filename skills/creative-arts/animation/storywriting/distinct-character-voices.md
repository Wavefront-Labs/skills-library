# Distinct character voices

Goal: you can tell who is speaking without the character names.

## 1. Dimensions of voice
| Dimension | Variation |
|---|---|
| Vocabulary | education, jargon, slang, age, region |
| Sentence length and rhythm | clipped vs rambling, questions vs statements |
| Formality | polite, blunt, crude |
| Verbal habits | catchphrases (sparingly), filler words, rhetorical patterns |
| Worldview | what they notice, joke about, worry about |
| Avoidance | what they never say (feelings, names, apologies) |

## 2. Method
1. Write a short voice sheet per main character: 5-10 sample lines and the dimensions above.
2. Draft scenes, then do a pass per character reading only their lines.
3. Cover the names and read the script; fix lines that could belong to anyone.

## 3. Animation considerations
- Voice casting and performance amplify the voice; give casting notes from the voice sheet.
- Avoid overusing catchphrases (fatigue in series).
- Comedy characters need a consistent comic attitude (literal-minded, overconfident).

## Verify
- [ ] Voice sheet per main character.
- [ ] Cover-the-names test passed.
- [ ] Casting notes derived from voice sheets.
