# Character-driven comedy

Goal: comedy rooted in who characters are, so jokes build investment instead of interrupting it.

## 1. Comic attitude
- Give each comic character a strong, consistent attitude and blind spot: the overconfident fool, the
  literal-minded one, the anxious planner, the vain one.
- The blind spot keeps causing problems (the character cannot see what the audience sees).

## 2. Situation pressure
- Put characters in situations that push directly on the attitude: the anxious one must improvise;
  the vain one must look foolish publicly.
- The comedy comes from consistent behaviour in an extreme situation.

## 3. Pairings
- Pair opposites (the straight man and the comic; the worrier and the reckless one).
- Each scene: who is the straight man here?

## 4. Test
- Swap the joke to another character: if it still works, it's a generic joke; rewrite for this character.
- Character jokes reveal something about the character or move the arc.

## 5. Arc compatibility
- Comic characters can change slowly; their attitude may soften but must remain recognisable.

## Verify
- [ ] Attitude and blind spot written for comic characters.
- [ ] Situations designed to pressure the attitude.
- [ ] Jokes pass the swap test.
