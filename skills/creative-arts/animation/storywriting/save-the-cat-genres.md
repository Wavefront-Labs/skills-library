# Save the Cat genres

Goal: classify a story by its structural genre to find what it must contain.

## 1. The ten genres (Snyder)
| Genre | Core | Examples of the shape |
|---|---|---|
| Monster in the House | a monster, a house (confined space), a sin that invited it | horror, creature films |
| Golden Fleece | a road, a team, a prize; the journey changes the hero | quests, heists, road movies |
| Out of the Bottle | magic/wish granted to a hero who learns a lesson | body swap, wish stories |
| Dude with a Problem | an ordinary person in extraordinary trouble | survival, disaster |
| Rites of Passage | life problem (puberty, death, divorce); the wrong way then acceptance | coming-of-age |
| Buddy Love | two who need each other, incomplete apart; a complication | romance, buddy films |
| Whydunit | a detective, a secret, a dark turn | mystery |
| The Fool Triumphant | an underdog fool, an establishment, a transmutation | underdog comedies |
| Institutionalized | a group, a choice, a sacrifice | family, workplace, team stories |
| Superhero | an extraordinary hero, a nemesis, a curse | origin stories, chosen ones |

## 2. Use
- Identify the genre; check the story has each core element.
- Find comparable films in the same genre (not the same subject) to study structure.
- Genre also suggests theme territory (Rites of Passage = acceptance).

## 3. Caveats
- Some stories mix genres; pick the dominant one for the A story.
- These are structural genres, not marketing genres (comedy, horror).

## Verify
- [ ] Genre chosen with core elements checked.
- [ ] Two or three comparable films identified.
- [ ] Missing elements added or deliberately omitted.
