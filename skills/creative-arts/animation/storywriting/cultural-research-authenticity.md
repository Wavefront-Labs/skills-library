# SKILL: Cultural research & authenticity (the story-trust practice)

**Goal:** a story set in a real culture, region, era or profession that people from
inside it recognise as *theirs*, not as set dressing. At feature scale this is a
credited department (Moana's Oceanic Story Trust — a standing panel of Pacific
anthropologists, navigators, linguists, choreographers and elders with real veto
power); at any scale it is a discipline, not a vibe.

## 1. Decide what the story actually borrows

List every element taken from the real world: language, names, clothing, music,
ritual, craft, geography, food, profession, era. For each, classify:
- **Load-bearing** — the story breaks if it is wrong (navigation in Moana, a
  procedure in a medical drama). These get research AND consultation.
- **Texture** — detail that signals place. Research is enough; get it right or cut it.
- **Invented** — your fiction. Mark it clearly as invented IN the design docs so later
  departments don't "correct" it toward a stereotype.

## 2. Research before consultation

Consultants' time is the scarce resource; spend it on judgement, not on facts a
library settles. Primary sources over summaries: practitioners' own accounts, museum
collections, field recordings, dictionaries — not other fiction about the culture
(that copies the previous fiction's errors; this is how stereotype chains form).

## 3. The trust loop (consultation that can change the work)

- Recruit people **inside** the culture/profession, plural — one voice is a sample
  of one, and cultures disagree internally. Pay them.
- Bring them **work in progress** (boards, designs, script pages), not finished work;
  feedback on a locked cut can only be cosmetic, and everyone knows it.
- Give the panel **standing**: an agreed list of element types they can block
  (names, sacred/ritual content, language use), not just advise on. Authenticity
  theatre — consultation with no power — reads as such in the result.
- Log every note and its disposition (adopted / adapted / declined-with-reason).
  The log is the audit trail that distinguishes consultation from decoration.

## 4. What changes in practice

Expect notes to move story, not just surfaces. On Moana the trust changed character
designs, removed a trickster-coconut sequence, and reshaped how navigation is
depicted — structural changes, accepted mid-production. Budget schedule for this:
a trust loop that cannot change anything after boards is already too late.

## 5. The boundary with invention

Fiction set in a real culture is still fiction. The rule that holds up: **invent
inward from what is true, never across it** — extrapolate from the real practice's
own logic rather than overwriting it with an outsider's convenience. When the story
needs to contradict the real thing, that is a conscious, logged decision with the
panel's eyes on it, not an accident.

## Verify

- [ ] Borrowed-elements list exists with load-bearing / texture / invented classes.
- [ ] Load-bearing elements each have a primary source AND a consultant pass.
- [ ] Consultants are plural, from inside, paid, and saw work in progress.
- [ ] A notes log exists with a disposition per note; at least one structural note
      was adopted (if none ever is, the loop has no power).
- [ ] Invented elements are flagged in the design docs read by later departments.
