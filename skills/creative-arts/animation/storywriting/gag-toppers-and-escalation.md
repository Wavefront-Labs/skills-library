# Toppers and escalation

Goal: get a second (and third) laugh from a gag that has already landed.

## 1. The topper
- After the main payoff, add a further beat that tops it: the door falls off, then the frame falls on the
  character, then the wall behind them.
- The topper must be bigger, weirder or more ironic than the first payoff, or a reversal of it.
- The reaction shot is often the topper (the deadpan look at the camera).

## 2. Escalation chains
- Each attempt to fix a problem makes it worse (snowballing).
- Escalate in size, consequence, or absurdity; never repeat at the same level.
- End the chain with a final big payoff or a quiet anticlimax for contrast.

## 3. Running gags
- A gag that recurs through a film or episode, with variations each time; the final occurrence tops or
  reverses all previous ones.
- Three to five occurrences in a 22-minute episode is typical; the last one is the button.

## 4. Knowing when to stop
- Stop when the gag stops escalating; a topper that is weaker than the payoff kills the laugh.
- Test in the animatic with fresh viewers; cut the weakest topper.

## Verify
- [ ] Each topper bigger or different than the previous beat.
- [ ] Running gag varies each occurrence.
- [ ] Weakest toppers cut after testing.
