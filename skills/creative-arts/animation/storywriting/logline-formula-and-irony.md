# Logline formula and irony

Goal: one sentence that sells the story and proves the idea has a strong engine.

## 1. Components
- **Hero type with an adjective**: "a neurotic clownfish", "a rat who dreams of cooking".
- **Inciting situation**: what happens to start the story.
- **Goal**: what the hero must do.
- **Antagonist or obstacle**: who or what stands in the way.
- **Stakes**: what happens if they fail.
- **Irony**: the hook - the contradiction that makes it interesting (a rat in a kitchen; a monster scared of
  a child). Snyder calls irony the first thing a logline needs.

Template: "When [inciting incident], a [adjective] [hero] must [goal] before/despite [antagonist/stakes]."

## 2. Snyder's tests
- **Irony**: is there an ironic twist?
- **Compelling mental picture**: can you see the whole movie, including time frame?
- **Audience and cost**: does it suggest who it's for and its scale?
- **Killer title**: a title that says what it is.

## 3. Process
1. Write 10-20 variations.
2. Read aloud; cut to 25-35 words.
3. Test on people unfamiliar with the project: do they ask "and then what?" (good) or "what is it?" (bad).

## 4. Mistakes
- Naming characters (nobody knows them); listing plot events; theme words instead of situation.

## Verify
- [ ] Hero with adjective, goal, obstacle, stakes all present.
- [ ] Irony identifiable.
- [ ] Under ~35 words; tested on cold listeners.
