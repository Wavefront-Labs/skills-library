# Plants and payoffs

Goal: every important solution or reveal was set up earlier, and every setup is used.

## 1. Principle
- **Chekhov's gun**: if a gun is on the wall in act one, it must go off later. Conversely, anything that
  pays off must be planted.
- McKee: setups and payoffs make endings feel inevitable and the story feel designed.

## 2. What to plant
- Objects (a key, a tool), skills (the hero can whistle loudly), rules of the world, character traits,
  lines of dialogue, relationships, locations.

## 3. Disguise the plant
- Plant inside another purpose: a joke, a character moment, a conflict.
- Plant early enough to be forgotten, clearly enough to be remembered on payoff.
- Repeat important plants once in a different context.

## 4. Payoff
- Use the planted thing in an unexpected way (surprising yet inevitable).
- Pay off near the climax for the biggest plants.
- Reverse payoffs (the thing that helped now hurts) add depth.

## 5. Tracking
- Keep a table: plant, scene planted, scene paid off, how. Unpaid plants are cut or paid off;
  unplanted payoffs get a plant.

## Verify
- [ ] Every payoff has a plant.
- [ ] Every major plant pays off.
- [ ] Plants disguised within other beats.
