# Foreshadowing

Goal: hints early in the story that make later events feel destined rather than arbitrary.

## 1. Foreshadowing vs planting
- A plant sets up a specific element used later (an object or skill).
- Foreshadowing hints at a future event or outcome (danger, a death, a betrayal).

## 2. Techniques
| Channel | Example |
|---|---|
| Dialogue | an offhand line that becomes true ("nothing could ever separate us") |
| Image | a broken object, a storm on the horizon |
| Staging | the future traitor framed apart or in shadow |
| Colour | the villain's colour appearing near a character before they turn |
| Music | the motif of a later event played softly earlier |
| Parallel scene | a smaller version of the climax earlier (a rehearsal) |

## 3. Calibrate
- Too subtle: no one notices on rewatch; too obvious: the audience predicts the twist.
- Aim for "noticed in hindsight": strong enough to be remembered when the event happens.
- Test with fresh viewers: did they predict the event? If most did, soften.

## 4. Animation advantage
- Every frame is designed; production design, colour and composition can carry foreshadowing without dialogue.
- Coordinate with art direction (colour script) and layout.

## Verify
- [ ] Each major turn has at least one foreshadowing hint.
- [ ] Hints spread across channels.
- [ ] Fresh viewer test checked predictability.
