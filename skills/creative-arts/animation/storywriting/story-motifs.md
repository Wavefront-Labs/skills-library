# Visual and story motifs

Goal: recurring elements that bind the story together and carry meaning without explanation.

## 1. Kinds of motif
| Kind | Example |
|---|---|
| Object | a scarf passed between characters |
| Image | hands reaching, doors closing |
| Line of dialogue | a phrase repeated with new meaning |
| Action | a ritual repeated (a handshake) |
| Music | a theme tied to a relationship |
| Colour/shape | coordinated with production design |

## 2. Rules
- Introduce the motif early in a neutral or positive context.
- Repeat it at turning points, each time with changed meaning or context.
- Pay it off at the climax or final image.
- Three to five appearances in a feature, two or three in a short.

## 3. Link to theme
- The motif should embody the theme's question (e.g. a door = choosing to open up).
- Its change mirrors the hero's arc.

## 4. Coordination
- Tell production design, colour and music so the motif is carried visually and aurally.

## Verify
- [ ] Motif list with each appearance and meaning.
- [ ] Meaning changes across appearances.
- [ ] Payoff at the climax or final image.
