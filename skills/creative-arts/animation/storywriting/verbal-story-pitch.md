# Verbal story pitch

Goal: listeners leave able to retell the story and wanting to see it.

## 1. Structure (5-15 minutes for a feature or series; 2-5 for a short)
1. **Why you**: a personal connection to the story (one or two sentences).
2. **Hook**: logline or a vivid opening image.
3. **World and hero**: who, where, what they want and what's wrong with them.
4. **Story**: inciting incident, two or three big set pieces, midpoint, low point, climax, ending.
5. **Theme**: what it's about, in one line, at the end.
6. For series: the engine and two or three example episodes.

## 2. Delivery
- Tell, don't read. Rehearse aloud 5-10 times; know the order cold; use cards only as a safety net.
- Use visuals (a few key art pieces or boards) to anchor characters and world.
- Perform characters briefly; enthusiasm sells.
- Keep names few; refer to roles ("the grandmother").

## 3. Room management
- Watch the listeners; skip detail if attention drops; stop before the time limit.
- Take questions as story notes, don't argue; write them down.

## 4. Mistakes
- Too much backstory before the story starts.
- Explaining the theme before telling the story.
- Not telling the ending.

## Verify
- [ ] Hook in the first 30 seconds.
- [ ] Ending and theme delivered.
- [ ] Rehearsed and timed.
