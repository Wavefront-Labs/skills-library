# Physical comedy and slapstick

Goal: physical gags that are funny, readable and consistent with the world's rules.

## 1. Cartoon physics with rules
- Define the show's rules: can characters hang in the air until they look down? Do injuries persist?
  How exaggerated are squash and stretch?
- Consistency makes slapstick funny; random physics feels arbitrary.
- Chuck Jones's Road Runner rules are the classic example: the Road Runner never harms the Coyote except
  by going "beep beep"; the Coyote's failures come from his own plans and products; gravity is his
  greatest enemy.

## 2. Structure of a physical gag
1. **Wind-up**: the character prepares (anticipation for the audience).
2. **Impact**: fast, clear contact (often off-screen or in silhouette for timing).
3. **Aftermath**: the result (flattened, accordioned, ringing like a bell).
4. **Reaction**: the character's look - often the biggest laugh.

## 3. Taste
- Pain is funny when it's not lasting and the victim deserves it or is undignified but unharmed.
- Avoid realistic injury; keep consequences cartoon.
- Know the audience and broadcaster standards (imitable behaviour in kids' content).

## 4. Writing on the page
- Describe actions clearly and briefly; leave timing to boards. Indicate key beats and the reaction.

## Verify
- [ ] Physics rules written in the bible.
- [ ] Gag has wind-up, impact, aftermath, reaction.
- [ ] Standards checked for imitable behaviour.
