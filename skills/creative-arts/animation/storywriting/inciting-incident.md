# Inciting incident

Goal: the event that begins the story proper by throwing the hero's life out of balance.

## 1. Definition (McKee, Story)
- It **radically upsets the balance of forces** in the protagonist's life, positively or negatively.
- The protagonist reacts, wanting to restore balance; this arouses a **conscious desire** (object of desire)
  and often an unconscious one that conflicts with it.
- It projects the **obligatory scene** (the climax the audience expects to see).

## 2. Rules
- It must happen on screen, not as backstory.
- It must involve the protagonist directly (by action or decision, or happening to them).
- Placement: within the first quarter; as early as possible once the audience cares enough. In shorts,
  within the first 30-60 seconds.
- One per story (subplots have their own).

## 3. Designing it
1. Define the hero's status quo and its balance.
2. Choose an event that breaks that balance at the hero's weak point.
3. State the desire it creates in one sentence ("to get the egg back to its mother").
4. Imagine the obligatory scene it promises; check the climax will deliver it.

## 4. Test
- Could the hero ignore it and life continue unchanged? Then it isn't the inciting incident.
- Ask "what does the hero now want?" - if unclear, sharpen it.

## Verify
- [ ] On screen, involves the protagonist.
- [ ] Desire stated as one sentence.
- [ ] The climax delivers the scene it promises.
