# Gap between expectation and result

Goal: every turn of a story surprises the character (and audience) and forces a bigger choice.

## 1. The mechanism (McKee)
- A character takes the minimum, conservative action they expect will get a useful reaction from the world.
- The world (an opponent, a partner, their own self) reacts differently, more powerfully or unexpectedly.
- A **gap** opens between expectation and result. The character must take a new action, with more risk
  and willpower, to get what they want.
- Gaps are where the audience learns the truth about the character (under pressure) and the world.

## 2. Applying it
- **Beat level**: in a dialogue, each attempt (flatter, threaten, plead) gets a reaction that is not
  what was expected.
- **Scene level**: the scene's turning point is a gap - the hero enters expecting one result and leaves
  with another.
- **Story level**: the climax's result differs from what the hero (and audience) anticipated yet feels inevitable.

## 3. Writing exercise
For each scene, write: Action -> Expected reaction -> Actual reaction -> New, riskier action.
If expected and actual match, there is no turn; change the reaction.

## 4. Mistakes
- Gaps that come from coincidence instead of character choices.
- Reactions that are surprising but implausible.

## Verify
- [ ] Each scene has an action/expectation/result line with a gap.
- [ ] New actions escalate in risk.
- [ ] Reactions are surprising and plausible.
