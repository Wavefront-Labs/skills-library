# Five-point finale

Goal: an act three that proves the hero has changed, instead of just a big fight.

## The five points
1. **Gathering the team** - the hero assembles allies, tools and a plan. Mend relationships broken in act two.
2. **Executing the plan** - the plan starts working; allies may fall away as each does their part.
3. **High tower surprise** - the plan fails because of a twist: the villain was ready, the princess isn't in
   the tower. The old approach is exhausted.
4. **Dig deep down** - with no plan, the hero must find the new truth inside (the need, the theme) and
   trust it. This is the real climax of the arc.
5. **Execution of the new plan** - acting from the new self, the hero wins (or loses in a way that is a
   moral win).

## Why it works
- The original plan represents the old self; it failing forces the change on screen.
- Point 4 links the action climax to the emotional climax.

## Adapting length
- Feature: 15-25 minutes. TV 22-min: 4-6 minutes, compress points 1-2. 11-min: points 3-5 can be one scene.

## Mistakes
- The plan works first time (no high tower surprise): no test of change.
- Dig deep down handled by luck or a rescue (breaks the coincidence rule).

## Verify
- [ ] The first plan fails because of a reveal or reversal.
- [ ] The winning plan comes from the hero's new understanding.
- [ ] Final action is taken by the hero, not by someone else.
