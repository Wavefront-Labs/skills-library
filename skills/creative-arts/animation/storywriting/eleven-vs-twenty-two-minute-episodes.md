# 11-minute vs 22-minute episode structure

Goal: a story sized and shaped for the slot it airs in.

## 1. 11-minute (half-hour slot holds two)
- One A plot, simple premise, fast setup (problem within the first minute).
- Often two acts around a mid-break, or one continuous act for streaming.
- Shape: setup (1-2 min) -> escalation (6-7 min) -> climax and button (2 min).
- Usually 2-4 locations, few new characters.
- Comedy-led shows favour this format (escalating gag structure).

## 2. 22-minute
- Cold open/teaser (1-3 min) often unrelated or a hook; main title; acts 1-2 or 1-3 around breaks; tag.
- A plot plus a B plot (and C runner).
- Room for an emotional arc and a midpoint turn; act breaks end on cliffhangers or turns.

## 3. Planning numbers (calibrate per show)
- Script: roughly one page per screen minute as a starting estimate; verify against the show's
  recorded dialogue and timed animatics.
- Boards: TV boards often run in the hundreds to over a thousand panels per 22 minutes depending on style.
- Pace: 11-minute stories need almost no debate beat; 22-minute stories can breathe.

## 4. Choosing format
- One idea, gag escalation, kids' comedy -> 11 minutes.
- Character arcs, ensemble, serialised -> 22 minutes.

## Verify
- [ ] Problem stated in the first minute (11) or by the end of act one (22).
- [ ] Act breaks land on turns.
- [ ] Timing estimates checked against the animatic.
