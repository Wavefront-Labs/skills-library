# Character dimension and contradiction

Goal: characters who feel real and surprising because they contain consistent contradictions.

## 1. Characterization vs true character (McKee)
- **Characterization**: the observable surface - age, looks, job, speech, habits, home.
- **True character**: who they are underneath, revealed only by **choices made under pressure**.
- Strong characters reveal a true character that contrasts with the characterization (the gruff old man
  who is secretly tender, the cheerful hero who is ruthless).

## 2. Dimension
- A dimension is a **contradiction**, either in deep character (ambitious but guilty) or between
  characterization and deep character (a timid-looking person with nerves of steel).
- Contradictions must be consistent (they recur), not random behaviour changes.
- Protagonists need the most dimensions; supporting characters fewer; a gag character may have one.

## 3. Method
1. List the characterization traits.
2. Write one or two contradictions.
3. Design scenes where pressure forces a choice that reveals each side of the contradiction.
4. Make sure the climax choice reveals the deepest layer.

## 4. Animation note
- Design and acting can carry the contradiction: a big, intimidating silhouette with delicate hand acting.
- Write the contradiction in the character brief for the designer and animators.

## Verify
- [ ] At least one consistent contradiction for each main character.
- [ ] Revealed by choices, not explained in dialogue.
- [ ] Climax reveals true character.
