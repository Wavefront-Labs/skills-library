# Midpoint

Goal: a turn at the middle of the story that stops act two sagging and changes its direction.

## 1. Two kinds (Save the Cat)
- **False victory**: the hero seems to get what they wanted (they win the contest, get the date). The
  second half takes it away.
- **False defeat**: everything seems lost early (the plan collapses). The second half claws back.
Snyder's rule: the all-is-lost beat is the opposite of the midpoint. A false victory at the midpoint leads to
a real low at all-is-lost; a false defeat at the midpoint leads to a (false) high there instead.

## 2. What the midpoint must do
- **Raise stakes**: what was personal becomes public, or the cost doubles.
- **Start a clock**: a deadline or ticking threat appears.
- **Shift the hero** from wanting what they want to beginning to face what they need; often where
  A and B stories cross (the kiss, the confession).
- Fun and games end; bad guys close in begins.

## 3. Placement
- At about 50% of the runtime (page 55 of 110; minute 5-6 of an 11-min episode).

## 4. Tests
- Could you swap the first and second halves? If yes, there is no midpoint.
- Does the midpoint change what scenes are possible afterward?

## Verify
- [ ] Midpoint labelled false victory or false defeat.
- [ ] Stakes raised and a clock introduced.
- [ ] It is visibly the opposite of the all-is-lost beat.
