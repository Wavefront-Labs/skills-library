# One-idea premise sized to runtime

Goal: a short with one clear idea fully told, rather than a feature squeezed into three minutes.

## 1. The one-idea rule
A strong animated short usually has:
- **One main character** (two at most, plus a foil).
- **One want or problem**, set up in seconds.
- **One location or a simple journey**.
- **One change** or reversal, often with a twist.
- **One emotional note** (funny, touching, eerie) done well.

## 2. Size to runtime
| Runtime | What fits |
|---|---|
| 30-60 s | a single gag or reversal |
| 1-3 min | setup, two or three escalations, payoff |
| 4-7 min | a small arc with a turning point |
| 8-15 min | a full but simple three-act story |
Rough production check: student 2D/3D teams average around 1-5 seconds of finished animation per artist-day;
estimate frames and shots before committing.

## 3. Tests
- Can you tell it in one sentence with a twist?
- Does it need backstory or world explanation? If yes, cut or simplify.
- Can it end within the runtime without a sequel hook?

## 4. Common traps
- World-building intros; prophecies; epic battles; multiple POV characters.
- Ideas that rely on dialogue-heavy explanation.

## Verify
- [ ] Premise in one sentence with one character, want and change.
- [ ] Runtime-to-content table checked.
- [ ] Production estimate fits the team and schedule.
