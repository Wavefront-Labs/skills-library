# Audience empathy for the hero

Goal: within the first minutes, the audience is on the hero's side, even if the hero is unpleasant.

## 1. Snyder's save-the-cat moment
- Early in the setup, the hero does something kind or decent (saves a cat, helps a stranger) that makes us
  like them. For flawed or abrasive heroes this is essential.

## 2. Other routes to empathy
| Tool | Example |
|---|---|
| Undeserved misfortune | the hero is mistreated, overlooked, hurt |
| Effort | Coats' Pixar rule 1: we admire a character for trying more than for their successes |
| Skill | the hero is very good at something |
| Humour | wit and self-awareness |
| Relatable want | a desire everyone shares (home, belonging, recognition) |
| Vulnerability | a private moment showing fear or longing |
| Worse opponents | surrounding the hero with people worse than them |

Use two or three together; McKee notes empathy (identification: "like me") matters more than sympathy (likeability).

## 3. Placement
- First 10% of runtime (first minute of a short). Repeat lightly before the hero's worst behaviour so the
  audience stays.

## 4. Mistakes
- A hero who is only perfect and nice: no empathy, no arc.
- Empathy beat that feels inserted and unrelated to the story.

## Verify
- [ ] At least two empathy tools used in the setup.
- [ ] The empathy beat is organic to the world and the want.
- [ ] Audience test: viewers can say why they root for the hero.
