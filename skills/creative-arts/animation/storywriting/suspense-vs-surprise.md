# Mystery, suspense and surprise

Goal: decide deliberately how much the audience knows compared with the characters.

## 1. McKee's three modes
| Mode | Knowledge | Emotion |
|---|---|---|
| Mystery | audience knows less than the character | curiosity |
| Suspense | audience knows the same as the character | concern + curiosity |
| Dramatic irony | audience knows more than the character | dread, comedy, empathy |

## 2. Hitchcock's bomb
- Surprise: a bomb under the table goes off - 15 seconds of shock.
- Suspense: the audience sees the bomb and the timer; the characters chat. Minutes of tension.
- Suspense generally holds attention longer; surprise is a single jolt. Use surprise for punctuation.

## 3. Design steps
1. For each sequence decide the mode.
2. Decide what to show the audience (the bomb, the clock) and when.
3. For mystery, plant fair clues; the answer should be gettable.
4. For surprise, control information so it's unexpected yet logical.

## 4. Animation and boards
- Suspense needs clear visual information (show the timer, the cracking ice) and cross-cutting.
- Surprise needs misdirection in staging (attention elsewhere).

## Verify
- [ ] Mode chosen per sequence.
- [ ] Information the audience needs is shown clearly.
- [ ] Surprises logically supported.
