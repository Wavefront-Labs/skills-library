# Twist and button endings

Goal: a short or episode that lands its last beat.

## 1. Twist ending
- A reveal that recontextualises what came before: the audience must be able to re-read earlier scenes with
  new meaning.
- Plant clues fairly; the twist must be surprising yet inevitable.
- Tie the twist to the theme or character, not just plot trickery.
- Test: tell the story without the twist; is it still worth watching? The twist should deepen, not rescue.

## 2. Button
- A short final beat after the main resolution: a callback gag, a reaction, a small reversal.
- Classic in TV animation and shorts (the tag scene).
- Keep it 3-10 seconds; one shot or a few.
- Ideal buttons call back to the opening or a running gag, or flip the theme lightly.

## 3. Choosing
- Twist for shorts built around a reveal or mystery.
- Button for comedy and character pieces; can follow a twist as a final laugh.

## 4. Mistakes
- Twist out of nowhere (no planting) - feels cheated.
- Button that undoes the emotional ending (a joke over a sincere moment unless the tone allows it).
- Ending dragging after the climax.

## Verify
- [ ] Twist has fair clues and reframes earlier scenes.
- [ ] Button is short and connected to earlier material.
- [ ] Ending tested on fresh viewers.
