# Coincidence rule (trouble yes, rescue no)

Goal: the hero earns every solution; the audience never feels cheated.

## 1. The rule (Coats, rule 19)
"Coincidences to get characters into trouble are great; coincidences to get them out of it are cheating."
Audiences accept bad luck as the start of a story. They reject good luck as its solution.

## 2. Find cheats
For every problem solved in the story, ask who solved it and how:
- Solved by chance (the key happens to fall), by an outside rescuer (deus ex machina), or by a new power
  introduced at the moment of need = cheat.
- Solved by the hero's choice, skill or growth, using something set up earlier = earned.

## 3. Fixes
- Plant the solution earlier (a setup that pays off), so it is not coincidence.
- Make the hero create the lucky break through an earlier action.
- Move the coincidence to the start of the problem rather than its resolution.
- If a rescuer is needed, have the rescue be the payoff of the hero's earlier choice (they helped the
  rescuer in act one).

## 4. McKee's view
- Coincidence early can launch a story; coincidence late destroys meaning. Avoid it in the climax.

## Verify
- [ ] Every solution listed with who solved it and how.
- [ ] No climax solution by chance or outside rescue.
- [ ] Solutions planted earlier.
