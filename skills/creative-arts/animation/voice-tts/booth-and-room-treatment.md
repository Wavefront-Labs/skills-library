# Booth and room treatment for voice recording

Two separate problems get confused:
1. **Isolation (soundproofing)** keeps outside noise out and keeps the actor from disturbing neighbours. It needs mass and sealed gaps, and is expensive.
2. **Treatment (absorption)** stops the room's reflections from colouring the voice. It is cheap and is usually the bigger win for dialogue.

## What treatment does
- A bare, square room adds early reflections and flutter echo that sound hollow and cannot be removed in post. Absorption on the surfaces closest to the mic and behind the actor reduces them.
- Thick, porous absorbers (mineral wool or acoustic foam panels, at least 5 cm / 2 in, thicker is better for low frequency) work at mid and high frequencies. Thin foam alone does little for low mids.
- Soft furnishings help: a clothes-filled wardrobe, heavy curtains, rugs, a sofa behind the mic.
- Put absorption **behind the mic** (to catch reflections from the actor's voice), to the side walls at mouth height, and ideally also above and in front of the actor. Leave a parallel-wall pair uncovered only if the room is already dead.

## Budget booths
- A reflection filter or a three-sided mic shield helps but does not replace a treated room.
- A closet full of hanging clothes, or a blanket cloth over a rigid frame, is a standard budget vocal booth.
- If you use a small booth, watch for a boxy tone from standing waves; test and adjust the actor position and mic distance instead of piling on more material.

## Noise sources to hunt
- Computer fans: move the machine out of the room or use long cables.
- Air conditioning, fridge hum, fluorescent lights, traffic, plumbing.
- Floor creaks and mic-stand rumble: use a shock mount and isolation under the stand.
- Phones, smartwatches, and stomach rumbles: ask the actor to remove or silence.

## Test the room
1. Clap sharply in the room: a ringing flutter echo means more absorption is needed.
2. Record 30 seconds of silence with preamp gain set for the loudest line, then listen on headphones at high volume, looking for hum, buzz, and hiss.
3. Record a read of a test line and compare it to a previous session's file in the same chain.

## Tip
Match the room, not just the mic, between sessions. If one session used a deader room, matching pickups in a live room will not intercut cleanly, and no EQ will fully fix it.

## Verify
- [ ] Clap test shows no ringing.
- [ ] Silence recording is free of mains hum and fan noise.
- [ ] Same room set-up recorded for a character's whole run.
