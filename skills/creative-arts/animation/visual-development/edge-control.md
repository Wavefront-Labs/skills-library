# Edge control (hard, soft, lost)

Goal: using edge quality as a compositional tool, as important as value and colour.

## Edge types
- **Hard**: sharp transition - focal areas, cast shadow near contact, man-made forms,
  silhouettes against strong contrast.
- **Soft**: gradual - turning forms, distant objects, out-of-focus areas, soft materials,
  diffuse shadows.
- **Lost**: no visible boundary - two areas of equal value and similar colour merge (a dark
  coat into a dark background). Lets the viewer's eye complete the shape.
- **Found**: an edge that reappears after being lost; lost-and-found contours feel alive.

## Rules
- Hardest edge + highest contrast at the focal point.
- Soften edges as forms turn away from the light and as distance increases.
- Edges of same-value shapes can be lost to group them into one mass.
- Depth of field: in film-style keys, edges outside the focal plane soften consistently.

## Method
1. Paint shapes first with medium edges everywhere.
2. Decide the focal point; sharpen edges there.
3. Soften edges on turning forms and in the background.
4. Lose edges where values match and the shape need not read separately.
5. Squint and view at thumbnail size to check the eye path.

## Failure modes
- All edges equally hard: cut-out, sticker look.
- All soft: mushy, no focus.
- Losing edges on the silhouette that tells the story.

## Verify
- [ ] Hardest edges at the focal point.
- [ ] Soft edges on turning and distant forms.
- [ ] Story silhouettes keep readable edges.
