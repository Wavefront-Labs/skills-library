# Skylight fill and blue shadows

Goal: outdoor shadows that are lit by the sky dome, as the Impressionists observed.

## Physics
- Rayleigh scattering makes the sky a big blue area light. Where the sun is blocked,
  this skylight is what lights the shadow side.
- Shadow planes facing **up** see the most sky - bluest and lightest.
- Shadow planes facing **sideways or down** see less sky and more ground bounce - warmer and darker.
- Under overhangs (eaves, chin, hat brim) both are blocked - darkest, often warm from bounce.

## Method
1. After establishing the warm key, paint shadows on up-facing planes with a blue/violet
   tint derived from the sky colour.
2. Shift side-facing shadows toward neutral; downward-facing toward the ground's colour.
3. Use cast shadows on the ground as the most visibly blue areas on clear days.
4. Adjust intensity by sky: deep blue sky = strong blue shadows; hazy sky = mild; overcast = none.
5. Check the shadow value; a bright sky lifts shadows; a dark storm sky deepens them.

## When not to use
- Interiors without sky view, night scenes, overcast days, or scenes under a big warm surface
  (canyon walls reflect red into shadows).

## Failure modes
- Shadows uniformly blue regardless of orientation.
- Pure blue painted over local colour, killing it.
- Blue shadows in scenes with no sky.

## Verify
- [ ] Up-facing shadows cooler than down-facing ones.
- [ ] Shadow colour relates to the sky.
- [ ] Local colour still visible in shadow.
