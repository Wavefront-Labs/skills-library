# Time of day

Goal: an audience that feels the hour of the day from colour and light alone.

## Reference table
| Time | Sun | Light colour | Sky | Shadows |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dawn / blue hour | below horizon | none direct | deep blue to pink at horizon | none; soft cool light |
| Sunrise | very low | warm orange-pink | warm horizon, cool zenith | very long, blue-violet |
| Morning | low-mid | warm-neutral, clear | clean blue | long-ish, crisp |
| Midday | high | neutral white, harsh | deep blue overhead, pale horizon | short, under objects, dark |
| Golden hour | low (under ~10 degrees) | strong orange-gold | warm, glowing haze | long, saturated blue |
| Sunset | at horizon | red-orange, light low and dim | red/orange band, violet above | very long; shadows dissolve into sky fill |
| Dusk / blue hour | below horizon | none direct | deep blue; artificial lights turn on | soft, cool |

## Method
1. Pick the hour from the story beat (hope at dawn, menace at dusk).
2. Set sun elevation and azimuth; derive shadow length and direction.
3. Choose key and sky colours from the table, then adjust for weather and location.
4. Note practical lights (windows, streetlamps) appearing at dusk.
5. Keep time-of-day continuity within a sequence; changes must be motivated.

## Failure modes
- Long shadows with midday colours.
- Golden-hour orange on everything including the sky zenith.
- Time of day drifting between shots of one scene.

## Verify
- [ ] Shadow length matches sun elevation.
- [ ] Light and sky colours match the hour.
- [ ] Continuity across the sequence.
