# Color accents

Goal: drawing the eye with a small amount of contrasting colour - the red coat in a grey
crowd, the warm window in a blue night.

## Rules (Gurney)
- An accent is small relative to the field: usually under 5-10% of the image.
- It contrasts in hue (often complementary to the dominant), saturation, or both.
- Use one main accent and maybe a weaker echo or two to lead the eye through the frame.
- Accents lose power when repeated too often; an image full of accents has none.

## Uses in animation
- Character costume accents that pull focus in crowd shots (a signature colour item).
- Props that matter to the story (the glowing key, the red balloon).
- Lighting accents: a lamp, a neon sign, a fire.

## Method
1. Establish the dominant palette with restrained saturation.
2. Choose the accent hue (complement or out-of-gamut tip) and place it at the focal point.
3. Add 1-2 smaller echoes along the path you want the eye to travel.
4. Check at thumbnail size: the accent should be the first thing seen.
5. Keep accent colours reserved in the style guide so other elements do not use them.

## Failure modes
- Accent colour reused on background props, splitting focus.
- Accent too large; it becomes the dominant.
- Accent placed away from the story point.

## Verify
- [ ] Accent occupies a small area at the focal point.
- [ ] No competing accents.
- [ ] Accent colour reserved in the palette.
