# Warm and cool contrast

Goal: depth, light and focus from temperature shifts rather than value alone.

## Uses
- **Light vs shadow**: warm light/cool shadow (sunlight) or cool light/warm shadow
  (overcast skylight with warm bounce, fluorescent interiors). Pick one per scene.
- **Near vs far**: warm advances, cool recedes; distance turns cooler through atmosphere
  (except at sunset haze).
- **Focus**: a warm focal area in a cool surround (or the reverse) pulls the eye.
- **Story**: temperature shifts across a sequence carry emotion (warm home, cool threat).

## Rules
- Temperature is relative: a grey looks warm next to blue and cool next to orange.
  Judge in context, not on the swatch.
- A green or violet can be warm or cool depending on its neighbours (yellow-green vs blue-green).
- Keep one temperature dominant; 50/50 splits split the image in two.

## Method
1. Decide the light temperature relationship for the scene.
2. Paint big areas in their temperature families before detail.
3. Check the focal point for the strongest temperature contrast.
4. Push distance cooler (or toward the sky colour).
5. Review in sequence to make sure temperatures follow the colour script.

## Failure modes
- Warm light with warm shadows (no temperature contrast) in a sunlit scene.
- Temperature flips between shots of the same scene.
- Equal amounts of warm and cool everywhere.

## Verify
- [ ] Light/shadow temperature rule stated and followed.
- [ ] One temperature dominates.
- [ ] Focal point has the strongest temperature contrast.
