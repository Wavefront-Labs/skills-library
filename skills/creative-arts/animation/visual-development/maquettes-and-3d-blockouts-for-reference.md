# Maquettes and 3D blockouts for reference

Goal: physical or digital models that let artists observe how an invented subject looks under
real light from any angle (Gurney's dinosaur maquettes; Disney's character maquettes
since the 1930s; quick Blender blockouts today).

## Physical maquettes
- Sculpt in oil-based clay (Monster Clay, Chavant) over an armature at a scale where a lamp
  makes a convincing sun.
- Paint grey or in local colours; photograph under the planned lighting (single lamp for sun,
  a diffuser for overcast).
- Use for characters and creatures where the anatomy is invented.

## Digital blockouts
- Primitive shapes and quick sculpts in Blender/ZBrush; no topology or UV care needed.
- Set the camera to the planned lens and height; render clay or flat-shaded passes, plus a
  shadow-only pass, for several light directions.
- Use as the base for paintovers or as perspective guides for 2D layouts.

## Method
1. Decide what question the model answers (cast shadow shapes, scale, a tricky angle).
2. Build the minimum needed - ignore surfaces the camera will not see.
3. Light it to match the scene; shoot/render several variations.
4. Paint from it, not over it blindly: redesign where the model reveals problems.
5. Keep the model for later shots and turnarounds.

## Failure modes
- Over-building the model (production quality) when a blockout would answer the question.
- Painting a render too literally; the image looks like CG.
- Scale or lens mismatch with the board.

## Verify
- [ ] Model answers a stated question.
- [ ] Lens/camera match the shot.
- [ ] Final paint redesigns rather than traces.
