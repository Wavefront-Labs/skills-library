# Reference gathering and field study

Goal: designs grounded in observed truth. Studios send artists on research trips (Pixar's
Scotland trip for Brave, Disney's Norway trip for Frozen) because reference beats memory.

## Types of reference
- **Photo reference**: shot for a purpose (materials, light, scale), not tourist snapshots.
  Shoot details, wide context and a scale reference in the same set.
- **Field sketches**: quick drawings that force understanding of structure and design.
- **Plein-air colour studies**: 20-60 minute paintings capturing light and colour relationships
  cameras flatten (Gurney).
- **Film/video**: for motion, weather, behaviour.
- **Objects and materials**: physical samples (fabric, bark, stone).
- **Expert and archival sources**: museums, historians, natural history collections.

## Method
1. Write a research brief: what questions must reference answer (how does moss grow on this
   stone, what colour is fog at dawn here)?
2. Plan the shot list or trip; capture for each question.
3. Sketch and paint on location; note time, weather, direction.
4. Organise: tagged folders or boards (PureRef, Milanote, shared drive) by category, with
   source and licence noted.
5. Distil: a one-page "findings" sheet of the 10 features that will drive the design.

## Failure modes
- Random image dumps from search engines with no question behind them.
- Copying a reference photo directly into final art (rights and stiffness).
- Reference collected but never distilled into design rules.

## Verify
- [ ] Research brief written.
- [ ] Reference labelled with source/licence.
- [ ] Findings sheet produced.
