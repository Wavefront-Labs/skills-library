# Lighting keys per sequence

Goal: a target image for each sequence that tells the lighting department exactly
what the shots should look like.

## What a lighting key specifies
- Key light direction, colour and hardness; fill colour and ratio.
- Time of day, weather and atmosphere density.
- Character lighting vs environment lighting (how the character separates).
- Focal point brightness and where the image falls off to dark.
- Practical lights and their colours.
- Mood notes from the colour script.

## Method
1. Start from the colour script thumbnail for the sequence.
2. Choose the representative shot(s): usually the establishing wide and the key close-up.
3. Paint over a layout or 3D blockout render at the final camera.
4. Annotate: source list, ratios, colour values (hex or swatches), atmospheric notes.
5. Review with the art director and lighting supervisor; adjust for feasibility.
6. Lighting artists build a master light rig for the sequence matching the key and propagate it.

## Failure modes
- Keys painted from imagined cameras that do not match the layout.
- Beautiful but unbuildable effects (impossible bounce, unmotivated glows) without a note on how.
- Keys missing for transitional sequences, leading to drift.

## Verify
- [ ] One key per sequence (two for complex ones) at final cameras.
- [ ] Annotated sources, ratios and swatches.
- [ ] Lighting rig render matches the key side by side.
