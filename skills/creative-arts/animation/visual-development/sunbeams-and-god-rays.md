# Sunbeams and god rays

Goal: light shafts that look physical - through windows, trees, clouds, dust or water.

## Conditions
- Requires **particles** in the air (dust, smoke, mist, spray) and a **strong source partially
  blocked** (gaps in clouds, leaves, window frames). Without both, no shafts.
- Shafts are lighter than the surrounding air in the same direction, never solid white.
- Rays are parallel for the sun, but **converge in perspective** toward the sun (or its
  anti-solar point for anticrepuscular rays).
- Shafts are brightest when looking toward the light; nearly invisible with the sun behind the viewer.
- Edges soft and slightly irregular; intensity varies along the shaft.

## Method
1. Place the source and the blockers; draw guidelines from the source through the gaps.
2. Paint the shafts with a light, low-opacity value of the air colour (not pure white),
   using a gradient that fades with distance from the gap.
3. Let shafts fall on surfaces as matching light patches.
4. Keep the number small (3-6 strong shafts); vary widths.
5. Animate subtle drift of particles, not of the shafts, unless blockers move.

## Failure modes
- Shafts without a visible gap or atmosphere.
- Rays pointing in inconsistent directions.
- Overuse: the frame washed out by light shafts.

## Verify
- [ ] Gaps and particles justify the rays.
- [ ] Rays converge on the source.
- [ ] Shafts land on surfaces as light patches.
