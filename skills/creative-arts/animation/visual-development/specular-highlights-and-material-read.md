# Specular highlights and material read

Goal: highlights that tell the viewer what a surface is made of.

## Rules
- A highlight is a reflection of the light source; it sits where the surface's angle
  mirrors the source to the eye. It moves when the viewer moves.
- **Glossy** (polished, wet, lacquered): small, sharp, bright highlight; strong reflections.
- **Satin/semi-gloss** (skin, leather, plastic): broader, softer highlight.
- **Matte** (chalk, felt, unglazed clay): almost none; the light side is just diffuse.
- **Colour**: dielectrics (plastic, skin, water) reflect the light's colour (white highlights
  on red plastic). Metals tint highlights with their own colour (gold gives gold highlights)
  and have dark diffuse.
- Highlight shape mirrors the source (window shape on a glossy ball).
- Fresnel: reflectivity rises at grazing angles on non-metals.

## Method
1. Decide the material list for the shot and each one's gloss level.
2. Paint diffuse form first; add highlights last.
3. Size and edge each highlight by gloss; tint by metal/non-metal rule.
4. Place highlights where the surface turns to face the source and the viewer.
5. Keep highlight hierarchy: the brightest highlight near the focal point.

## Failure modes
- Same white blob highlight on every material.
- Highlights painted in the centre of every form regardless of light direction.
- Coloured highlights on plastic or white ones on gold.

## Verify
- [ ] Highlight size/sharpness matches material.
- [ ] Metal vs non-metal colour rule followed.
- [ ] Highlight positions consistent with the source.
