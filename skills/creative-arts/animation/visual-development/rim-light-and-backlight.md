# Rim light and backlight

Goal: separating subjects from backgrounds and creating drama with light from behind.

## Characteristics (Gurney)
- **Rim light**: light from behind/side grazing the edge; a thin bright line on the silhouette.
  Widest where the form turns away gradually; on hard edges it is a sliver.
- **Fuzzy edges glow most**: hair, fur, peach fuzz, fabric fibres scatter backlight into a halo.
- **Backlight translucency**: thin materials (ears, leaves, fabric, petals) glow warm
  when lit from behind.
- **Silhouette**: with strong backlight, the front of the subject falls into shadow and the
  image reads as a silhouette with a rim - powerful for drama, poor for facial acting unless filled.

## Method
1. Place the backlight on the side where the subject meets a darker background.
2. Paint the rim on the edge planes that face the light only; keep it thin.
3. Make it brighter and softer on hair and fuzzy surfaces.
4. Add translucency glow on ears and thin parts.
5. Fill the front with bounce/ambient enough for the acting to read.
6. In NPR shading, use a facing-ratio/fresnel rim in the light's direction, not a uniform outline glow.

## Failure modes
- Rim on every edge regardless of light direction (glow outline).
- Rim as bright as the key, flattening the light hierarchy.
- Front left too dark for expressions.

## Verify
- [ ] Rim only on edges facing the back source.
- [ ] Silhouette separation improved.
- [ ] Faces readable.
