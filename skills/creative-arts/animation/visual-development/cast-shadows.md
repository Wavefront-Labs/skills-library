# Cast shadows

Goal: shadows thrown by objects onto surfaces that are correctly shaped, edged and valued.

## Construction
- **Sunlight (parallel rays)**: pick the light's direction and its ground point (the point on
  the ground plane directly under the sun, on the horizon line). Shadow of a vertical edge
  runs from its base away from (or toward) that point; the ray from the light through the top
  of the edge sets the shadow length.
- **Point light (lamp, candle)**: rays radiate from the source; its foot point is on the floor
  directly below it. Shadows splay out and grow longer with distance.
- Shadows follow the surfaces they fall on: up a wall, over steps, across a curved form.

## Edges and values
- **Contact**: sharpest and darkest where the object touches the surface.
- **Penumbra**: edges soften with distance from the object and with larger light sources
  (overcast = very soft, noon sun = crisp but not razor-like at distance).
- **Value and colour**: set by the fill (sky = blue-ish shadows outdoors); cast shadows on a
  surface are usually darker than that surface's own form shadow near contact.

## Method
1. Locate the source and its foot/ground point.
2. Project key corners to get the shadow shape; simplify complex objects to boxes and cylinders.
3. Paint the shadow as one flat shape in the shadow family; then soften the far edges.
4. Add an occlusion accent at the contact.

## Failure modes
- Shadows pointing in inconsistent directions within one scene.
- Uniform soft edges everywhere (nothing sits on the ground).
- Shadows painted black instead of the surface colour darkened and tinted by fill.

## Verify
- [ ] All shadows trace to the same source.
- [ ] Edges sharpen at contact.
- [ ] Shadow colour matches the fill light.
