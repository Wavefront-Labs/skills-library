# Color harmony schemes

Goal: palettes that feel unified, chosen deliberately for each scene or design.

## Schemes
| Scheme | Construction | Feel |
|---|---|---|
| Monochromatic | one hue, varied value/saturation | unified, moody, minimal |
| Analogous | 2-4 neighbouring hues | calm, natural, warm or cool |
| Complementary | two opposites | high energy, contrast; risk of vibration |
| Split-complementary | one hue + the two neighbours of its complement | contrast with less tension |
| Triadic | three equally spaced hues | lively, cartoony when saturated |
| Tetradic/square | four hues | rich but hard to balance |

## Rules
- **Dominance**: one hue covers most of the image, a second supports, a third accents
  (about 60/30/10 as a starting point).
- **Saturation unity**: lower the saturation of all but the accent.
- **Value structure overrides hue**: harmony fails if values do not organise the image.
- **Mixing a common hue** into every colour (glazing or a colour-balance layer) unifies
  any scheme.

## Method
1. Choose the scheme from the scene mood and colour script.
2. Pick the dominant hue and its share.
3. Build a 5-7 swatch palette with value range.
4. Paint big areas with dominant hues; reserve the accent.
5. Check greyscale and thumbnail.

## Failure modes
- Equal amounts of complementary colours: visual tug-of-war.
- Schemes chosen from a generator without regard to light.
- All colours at full saturation.

## Verify
- [ ] Scheme named and dominant hue clear.
- [ ] Proportions unequal.
- [ ] Greyscale structure holds.
