# Rain and snow

Goal: weather that is felt through surfaces and light, not just falling particles.

## Rain
- **Wet surfaces** carry the effect: darker and more saturated materials, mirror-like
  reflections of lights and sky on roads, vertical streaked reflections under lamps.
- Light is usually overcast; contrast low except for practical lights at night.
- **Rain streaks** visible mainly against dark backgrounds; lighter than what is behind them.
  Layer them by depth (near = long, blurry; far = thin, faint), all with the same wind angle.
- Splashes, drips from edges, puddle ripples sell interaction.

## Snow
- Snow is a highly reflective white: shadows on snow go strongly blue from skylight; lit snow
  takes the sun's warmth.
- Cover rounds over shapes, piles on top planes, leaves verticals bare; drifts on the
  windward side.
- Falling flakes: larger and softer near the camera, small dots far away; snowfall acts like fog,
  reducing contrast with distance.
- Footprints and contact marks show weight and story.

## Method
1. Paint the scene's wet or snow-covered state first (surfaces, light).
2. Add atmosphere (rain haze/snow fog) per depth.
3. Add falling particles as separate layers for animation (FG, MG, BG), with wind direction noted.
4. Hand FX notes: particle size, speed, angle per layer.

## Failure modes
- Dry-looking surfaces with rain lines painted over.
- White snow shadows with no colour.
- Particles flattened into one layer, no depth.

## Verify
- [ ] Surfaces show wetness or snow cover logic.
- [ ] Particle layers by depth with one wind angle.
- [ ] Snow shadows coloured by the sky.
