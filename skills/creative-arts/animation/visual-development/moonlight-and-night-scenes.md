# Moonlight and night scenes

Goal: night that reads as night while characters stay readable.

## Observations (Gurney)
- Moonlight is reflected sunlight - physically slightly warm - but human night vision
  (Purkinje shift) perceives it as blue-green and desaturated. Paint it cool and low chroma.
- Values compress into the dark range; contrast between moonlit planes and shadows is modest
  but present; shadows are crisp (the moon is a small source).
- Sky is often lighter than the land at the horizon; silhouettes read against it.
- **Practicals** (windows, lamps, fire) become warm, saturated accents and the brightest values.

## Animation conventions
- **Day for night**: keep the scene lighter than reality so faces and acting read; rely on
  hue (blue), lowered saturation and warm practicals to say night.
- Rim or edge light from the moon to separate characters from the dark background.
- Keep eye whites and key facial planes readable at viewing size; test on the target display.

## Method
1. Set the moon direction and a cool desaturated key.
2. Paint values low-key but keep a readable 3-value structure.
3. Silhouette important shapes against a lighter sky or lit areas.
4. Add warm practicals as accents; let them light small areas with falloff.
5. Check on a dim display/phone to ensure readability.

## Failure modes
- Pure black frames where nothing reads.
- Saturated bright blue everywhere - cartoon "night filter".
- Practicals that do not light anything.

## Verify
- [ ] Three-value structure visible in low key.
- [ ] Characters read at target screen size.
- [ ] Warm practicals light their surroundings.
