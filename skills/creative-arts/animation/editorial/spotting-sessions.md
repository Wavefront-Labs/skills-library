# Spotting sessions

**Goal:** a shared, written plan for where music plays and what sound does, scene by scene, agreed by the
director with the composer and sound supervisor before they start work.

## Before

- Locked (or near-locked) cut with burn-in timecode, temp music and temp SFX on separate tracks so they can be
  muted (`creative-arts.animation.editorial.editorial-track-layout`).
- Copies of the cut for the composer and sound team (QuickTime with TC, plus AAF/stems for sound).
- A cue sheet template: cue number, TC in, TC out, length, description, intent.

## Music spotting

For each potential cue:
- Start and end points (often on a cut, an action, or a line), with timecode.
- Function: emotion, pace, comedy hit, transition, source/diegetic.
- Temp reference (what the temp track does right and wrong) and any themes/leitmotifs to use.
- Hits to catch (sync points: a door slam, a reveal).
Number cues by reel/act: `1M3` = reel/act 1, music cue 3.

## Sound spotting

- Key designed sounds (signature character sounds, magic, vehicles), cartoon effects style (realistic vs
  cartoony), ambience per location, Foley needs, ADR/additional dialogue lines, and places that should be silent.
- The director's sound priorities for the mix (what must be heard).

## After

Spotting notes distributed within a day; cue list becomes the composer's working list and later feeds the
music cue sheet (`creative-arts.animation.sound-design.scoring` and the cue sheet for licensing).

## Gotchas

- Recut after spotting shifts every timecode; tie notes to shot IDs and lines as well as TC.
- Temp love: if the director is attached to temp music, say so explicitly in the notes and plan a conversation;
  it shapes the composer's brief.

## Verify

The spotting document covers every scene, every cue has in/out TC and intent, and the composer's first pass
matches the cue list's starts and ends.
