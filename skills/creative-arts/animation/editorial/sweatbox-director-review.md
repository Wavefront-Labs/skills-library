# Sweatbox director review

**Goal:** the director sees work **in context of the cut** (unlike shot-by-shot dailies), gives decisions
that stick, and every note reaches the right artist the same day. "Sweatbox" is the Disney-era name for the
screening room where animation was reviewed with the director.

## Before the session

- Editor builds a review timeline: the sequence with new versions cut in, plus a playlist of the specific
  shots up for approval, each with the previous version available for A/B.
- Producer/coordinator lists what decisions are needed (approve, retake, cut changes).
- Burn-ins on: shot name, version, frame counter (`creative-arts.animation.editorial.review-burn-ins`).

## During

- Editor drives playback: full sequence once at speed, then shot by shot, loops on request.
- Note taker (coordinator or assistant editor) logs per shot: version reviewed, decision (approved / approved
  with notes / retake), notes with frame numbers, who owns the fix.
- Draw-overs captured from the review tool or as stills.
- Timing notes that affect the cut ("hold two more frames", "trim the end") are fixed live by the editor where
  possible, so the director approves the result.

## After

- Notes published within hours: into the tracker per shot/task, with the clip of the reviewed version attached.
- Editor applies approved cut changes and issues a change list (`creative-arts.animation.editorial.editorial-change-lists`).
- Approved versions become the editorial versions of record.

## Gotchas

- Vague notes ("make it better") waste a round; the note taker asks for the specific frames or the reason.
- Reviewing without sound hides timing problems; play with dialogue and temp music.
- Keep sessions short and decision-focused; big creative discussions go to a separate story meeting.

## Verify

Every shot in the session has a recorded decision in the tracker within the day, and the next session's
playlist shows those shots' new versions with the notes addressed.
