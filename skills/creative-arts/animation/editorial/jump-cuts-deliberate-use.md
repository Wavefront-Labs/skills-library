# Jump cuts

**Goal:** never ship an accidental jump cut, and use deliberate ones with intent.

## Definition

A jump cut joins two shots of the same subject from nearly the same angle and size, so the subject appears to jump
in place. The **30-degree rule**: change the camera angle by at least 30 degrees, or the shot size by at least one
step (wide to medium), between consecutive shots of the same subject.

## Accidental jump cuts in animation

- Removing a line from the middle of a held shot in the animatic: the pose jumps.
- Reusing the same layout for consecutive shots after a sequence was restructured.

Fixes, cheapest first: cut away to a reaction or insert over the gap
(see `creative-arts.animation.editorial.reaction-shots-and-performance-cutting`); push in 10-20% on one side as a temp
and request a different field from layout; animate a bridging move. A dissolve hides it only if time passes.

## Deliberate jump cuts

- **Comedy:** the same framing, the character in a new pose each cut (waiting, boredom, repeated failed attempts).
  The sameness of framing is the joke; keep it identical, locked off.
- **Time compression:** a montage of a task in progress, same angle.
- **Shock/disorientation:** jumping in closer on the same axis (an axial cut) in steps for emphasis; this keeps the
  angle but changes size, which is the accepted form.

## Craft notes

- Deliberate jumps need a clear rhythm, often on the beat of music or with an SFX on each cut.
- In limited animation, a jump with the same held background and a changed character pose is cheap to produce,
  which makes it a useful budget device.

## Verify

Step through every cut in the lock: any consecutive same-subject shots that fail the 30-degree/size rule are listed
in the cutting notes as intentional, or fixed.
