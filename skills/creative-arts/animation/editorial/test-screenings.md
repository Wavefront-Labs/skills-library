# Test screenings

**Goal:** learn whether a fresh audience follows the story, laughs where intended, and stays engaged, while
the edit can still change. Animation studios screen story reels and work-in-progress cuts internally and,
for features, with recruited audiences.

## Prepare the screening version

- Mix of finished shots, animation, layout and boards is normal; make the levels consistent enough not to
  distract (label unfinished sections if needed).
- Temp mix (`creative-arts.animation.editorial.temp-mix-screenings`): clean dialogue, temp music and key SFX;
  bad sound tanks comprehension scores.
- Burn-in watermark per screening; collect devices if the material is sensitive.

## Audience

- Internal: crew outside the production (fresh eyes), at each act lock.
- External: recruited to match the target demographic (age band for kids' content, with guardian consent and
  child protection rules), typically 20-100 people.

## Measure

- **Observation**: an editor/director in the room logging laughs, restlessness, confusion moments with timecode
  (a seat-side timecode log or a second person with a TC watch).
- **Questionnaire** right after: overall rating, favourite/least favourite scenes, characters liked/disliked,
  "anything confusing?", would-recommend; keep open questions few and specific.
- **Discussion group** (10-15 people) led by a neutral moderator, not the director.

## Turning feedback into edits

- Look for patterns, not individual comments: confusion reported by many at the same point is a story/clarity
  problem; laughs missing at a gag is timing or setup.
- Separate "what" (the audience problem) from "how" (their suggested fix); the fix is the filmmakers' call.
- Prioritise problems in act structure and comprehension over polish notes.

## Gotchas

- Audiences judge unfinished animation harshly; brief them that it's a work in progress.
- Screening too often with the same internal people creates familiarity bias.

## Verify

A short report per screening: top three problems with timecodes, changes made in response, and whether the next
screening shows improvement on those points.
