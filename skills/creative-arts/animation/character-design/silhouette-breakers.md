# Signature accessories and hair as silhouette breakers

Goal: give each character one or two shapes that break out of the basic body
outline - hair, ears, hat, tail, weapon, scarf - so they are identifiable in black.

## Principles
- A silhouette breaker extends the outline in a direction no other cast member
  uses (Ariel's hair mass, Mickey's ears, Link's cap tip, Naruto's spikes).
- One strong breaker beats many small ones. More than two compete.
- Breakers that move (hair, tail, scarf) add overlapping action and show
  direction and speed.
- The breaker must read from front, side and 3/4. Ears that vanish in profile fail.

## Method
1. Black-fill the bare body; it should already read, but generic.
2. List breaker candidates from personality and world (job tools, culture, era).
3. Try 3-5 versions, each extending in a different direction (up, back, sideways, down).
4. Pick the one most distinct from the rest of the cast line-up in black.
5. Check animation cost: long hair and loose cloth need follow-through in every shot;
   flag for rigging/sim or simplify for 2D.
6. Lock it on the model sheet with its rest shape and motion range.

## Failure modes
- Breakers that sit inside the body outline (a chest badge) - no silhouette gain.
- An accessory the story removes with no replacement read.
- Expensive moving breakers on a low-budget TV schedule.

## Verify
- [ ] Character identifiable in black from front, 3/4 and profile.
- [ ] Breaker direction unique within the cast.
- [ ] Motion cost agreed with animation/rigging.
