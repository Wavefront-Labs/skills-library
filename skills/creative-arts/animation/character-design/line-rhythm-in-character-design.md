# Line rhythm in character design

Goal: contours that flow into each other so the eye travels smoothly through the
design, with straights set against curves for tension.

## Principles
- **Continuation**: a line ending on one form is picked up by a line on the next
  (back of neck flows into the spine curve, arm contour into the hip).
- **Straight against curve**: pair a straight (structural, hard) side with a curved
  (fleshy, soft) side on limbs and torso. Classic leg: straight shin front, curved calf back.
- **Alternating rhythm**: contour bulges alternate sides down a limb (bicep outer,
  forearm inner) rather than mirroring; mirrored bulges look like sausages.
- **Line weight**: heavier on the shadow side and under overlaps, thinner on the lit side.

## Method
1. Draw the line of action, then a few long rhythm lines through the figure before
   anatomy.
2. Build contours that run tangent off these rhythm lines.
3. For each limb, mark the straight side and the curved side.
4. Trace the outer silhouette in one stroke: kinks and stalls show where the flow breaks.
5. Flip the drawing horizontally to catch lopsided or stiff passages.

## Failure modes
- Symmetrical parallel contours (tube limbs).
- All-curve designs: soft mush without structure.
- Lines that stop dead at joints instead of flowing across them.

## Verify
- [ ] Rhythm lines connect head to feet.
- [ ] Each limb has a straight side and a curved side.
- [ ] The flipped image reads as well as the original.
