# Cast contrast in a line-up

Goal: an ensemble where every character occupies a distinct visual slot, like voices
in a choir.

## Axes of contrast
- **Height**: tall / medium / short.
- **Mass**: thin / average / heavy.
- **Dominant shape**: circle / square / triangle.
- **Silhouette breaker direction**: up, sideways, back, down.
- **Value and colour**: light / mid / dark, warm / cool, saturated / muted.
- **Energy**: still vs fidgety (affects how the design is posed).

## Method
1. Lay the cast in a line-up, all in black fill at true scale.
2. Build a table: one row per character, one column per axis.
3. Any two rows that match on 3+ axes are a problem; change one of them.
4. Pair contrast for duos (Laurel and Hardy, Woody and Buzz, Shrek and Donkey):
   opposite on height, shape and energy.
5. Colour-test: convert the coloured line-up to greyscale; neighbours should differ in value.

## Failure modes
- Same-height teenagers differentiated only by hair colour.
- A sidekick who duplicates the lead's shape and size.
- Contrast that ignores story roles (villain softer and rounder than the hero without intent).

## Verify
- [ ] No two characters match on 3+ axes.
- [ ] Black-fill line-up: every character named by a cold viewer.
- [ ] Greyscale line-up separates neighbours.
