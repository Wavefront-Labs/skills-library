# Realistic character design

Goal: characters at the right end of the spectrum (Disney princes, adult-drama anime,
action-series leads) that look anatomically convincing yet animate cleanly.

## Rules
- **Anatomy is mandatory**: 7-8 heads, correct landmarks (pit of neck, iliac crest,
  knee about 2 heads above the floor at 8 heads). Errors a cartoon hides are
  glaring here.
- **Simplify with intent**: remove wrinkles, nasolabial folds and muscle separations
  that would need drawing every frame; keep only the planes that sell structure.
- **Stylize something**: eyes slightly larger, jaw cleaner, hands simplified. Pure
  realism animated by hand or CG lands near the uncanny valley; a small stylization
  pushes it back to appealing.
- **Restrict exaggeration**: squash and stretch is limited to flesh areas (cheeks, belly);
  see `stretch-limits-on-rigid-subjects`.

## Method
1. Shoot or gather reference of a model or actor matching age and build.
2. Build a proportion grid in head units; mark landmarks.
3. Draw the figure realistic, then do a simplification pass: list every line and keep
   the ones that change the form read.
4. Decide 1-3 stylizations (eye size, nose simplification, hair as big shapes) and
   apply them consistently across the cast.
5. Test with an expression sheet and an action pose; if acting looks stiff,
   loosen construction rather than adding detail.

## Failure modes
- Detail creep: a beautiful illustration nobody can draw 24 times a second.
- Eyes too small and realistic - dead stare, uncanny.
- Inconsistent realism across the cast (one realistic, one cartoon) without a story reason.

## Verify
- [ ] Landmarks match the head-unit grid.
- [ ] Simplification list documented on the model sheet.
- [ ] Expression and action tests do not read stiff or uncanny.
