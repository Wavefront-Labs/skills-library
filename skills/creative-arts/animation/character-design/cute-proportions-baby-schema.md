# Cute proportions (baby schema)

Goal: trigger the "aww" response deliberately, using Konrad Lorenz's Kindchenschema
traits that audiences read as young, harmless and lovable.

## The traits
- Head large relative to body (1.5-3 heads tall total).
- Cranium large relative to face: features sit in the **lower half** of the head.
- Eyes big, round, set low and wide; large irises/pupils.
- Small nose and mouth, round cheeks, small chin.
- Short, chubby limbs; small hands and feet; round belly.
- Soft round silhouette, no sharp corners.

## Method
1. Draw the head circle; place the eye line below the midpoint (around 55-60% down).
2. Make the body 1-2 head units; limbs short with tapered, rounded ends.
3. Round every corner; use circles and ovals as primary shapes.
4. Increase eye size in steps and stop just before it looks bug-eyed or uncanny.
5. Test cuteness against role: a cute villain is a deliberate subversion; a cute hero in
   a drama may undercut stakes.
6. Check animation: short limbs limit reach and contact poses - plan how they pick
   things up, sit, cross arms (often they cannot; that is a gag source).

## Failure modes
- Eyes on the head's midline: reads as small adult, not baby.
- Realistic detail (knuckles, eyelashes everywhere) breaking the soft read.
- Limbs too short to perform the story actions.

## Verify
- [ ] Eye line below head centre.
- [ ] Total height 3 heads or fewer.
- [ ] Key actions (grab, sit, run) drawable with these limbs.
