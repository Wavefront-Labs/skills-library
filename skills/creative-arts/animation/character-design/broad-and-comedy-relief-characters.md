# Broad and comedy-relief characters

Goal: characters built to be pushed - big takes, squash and stretch, slapstick - without
breaking model.

## Traits on the spectrum
- **Broad**: classic cartoon (Goofy, Bugs Bunny, Genie). Exaggerated proportions,
  rubbery construction, a very wide expression and pose range.
- **Comedy relief / sidekick**: broad design living next to a more restrained lead
  (Timon, Mushu, Olaf). Must contrast the lead in shape, size and energy.

## Method
1. Start from the gag role: what physical comedy will this character do? Falls, takes,
   fast talk, eating? Design the anatomy that enables it (long neck for takes, big
   mouth for yelling, loose body mass for wobble).
2. Use soft, squashy primitives (circles, pears, beans) for the main masses so they
   deform without losing volume.
3. Exaggerate one proportion hard (huge head, tiny legs, giant nose) and keep a
   readable simple construction underneath (Blair-style circles and centre lines).
4. Design the extremes on day one: full squash, full stretch, biggest take, widest
   mouth. If the design breaks at the extremes, fix the design, not the animation.
5. Contrast with the lead: if the lead is tall and angular, make the sidekick small
   and round (and vice versa).
6. Group facial features low or high to leave a big cranium or a big jaw to
   squash - the space between features is what animators stretch.

## Failure modes
- Broad in design but stiff construction - animators cannot squash it.
- Sidekick that matches the lead's silhouette and energy - redundant on screen.
- Gag anatomy with no personality anchor: all mugging, no character.

## Verify
- [ ] Extreme squash/stretch/take drawings exist and stay on model.
- [ ] Silhouette and size clearly contrast the lead.
- [ ] Construction can be redrawn from basic shapes.
