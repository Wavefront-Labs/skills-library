# Construction sheet

Goal: show how the character is built, not just what it looks like. A construction
sheet lets a new animator rebuild the character from primitives in 4-6 steps.

## Contents
- Step 1: primary masses as simple volumes (sphere head, bean torso, cylinder limbs).
- Step 2: centre lines and eye line wrapped around the volumes.
- Step 3: secondary forms (jaw, muzzle, hair mass, hands as mitten shapes).
- Step 4: features placed on the guidelines.
- Step 5: final clean line.
- The same build at front, 3/4 and profile, plus a tilted head.
- Measurements: eye spacing in eye-widths, head width vs height, and key ratios.

## Method
1. Analyse the approved design: reduce each part to the simplest 3D form that
   still captures it.
2. Draw the forms as volumes with overlap (not flat shapes), so they rotate.
3. Show construction lines in a contrasting colour (blue or red) under the clean line.
4. Label proportions and relationships ("ear top level with brow", "muzzle starts at eye line").
5. Test it: give the sheet to another artist and have them draw an unseen angle.

## Failure modes
- Flat circle-and-line construction that cannot be rotated.
- Skipping steps; jumping from circles to final face.
- Construction that does not match the final design (built after the fact, wrong).

## Verify
- [ ] 4-6 steps from volumes to clean line.
- [ ] Three angles plus a tilt.
- [ ] Another artist reproduces an unseen angle on model.
