# Storytelling poses

Goal: a pose that tells one beat of the story on its own - a still frame an audience
understands with the sound off.

## Principles
- **One idea per pose**: "she has just realised he lied". Not two.
- **Clarity over realism**: choose the moment and angle that make the idea unmistakable.
- **The whole body acts**: feet, hips, shoulders and hands all support the emotion, not just the face.
- **Props and gaze**: the eyes and any prop point toward what matters in the story.
- **Contrast with context**: the pose reads against other characters and the environment.

## Method
1. Write the beat in one sentence (subject, verb, feeling).
2. Thumbnail 5-10 versions at small size; vary angle and moment (before, peak, after).
3. Pick the one that reads fastest; check its silhouette.
4. Push the line of action and the key gesture.
5. Remove anything not serving the beat (fussy hand detail, extra props).
6. Test: show it to someone without context and ask what is happening.

## Failure modes
- Mid-action poses that are ambiguous (between two ideas).
- Face carries the emotion while the body is neutral.
- Pose hidden by camera angle or overlapping characters.

## Verify
- [ ] Beat sentence written and matched.
- [ ] Silhouette communicates the action.
- [ ] Cold viewer describes the beat correctly.
