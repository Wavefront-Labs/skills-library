# Avoiding tangents

Goal: no two lines just touching. A tangent is where contours kiss without
overlapping or separating; the eye snags and depth collapses.

## Common tangents
- Hand edge exactly on the hip contour (in front or behind?).
- Hat brim kissing the top of the frame or a BG line.
- Shoulder line continuing straight into a horizon or table edge.
- Two limbs touching end to end; finger tips meeting.
- Character head aligned with a door frame or pole behind them.
- Corner to corner: one shape's corner landing exactly on another's.

## Fix
1. **Overlap clearly**: move one form so it sits decisively in front (hand overlaps hip
   by a visible amount).
2. **Separate clearly**: open a gap of negative space (hand away from hip).
3. **Shift alignment**: break a continuous line by raising or lowering one element.
4. In layouts, move the camera or the prop rather than the character's acting.

## Method
1. Squint or view at thumbnail size; tangents read as flat spots.
2. Trace every contour endpoint and ask: overlapping, separate, or kissing?
3. Fix each kiss with one of the three moves above.
4. Recheck in silhouette - fixing one can create another.

## Failure modes
- Fixing tangents only in the design pose; animators reintroduce them in key poses.
- Overlap so slight it still reads as a tangent.

## Verify
- [ ] No contour endpoints touching another contour.
- [ ] Character lines do not continue into BG lines.
- [ ] Model sheet carries a tangent callout for known trouble spots.
