# Iconic and simple characters

Goal: a character built from the fewest possible shapes that still carries personality,
the far-left end of Bancroft's character spectrum (iconic -> simple -> broad -> comedy relief
-> lead -> realistic).

## Where it sits
- **Iconic**: symbol-level design (Mickey's head as three circles, Hello Kitty, Pac-Man).
  Dot eyes or none, minimal mouth, almost no anatomy. The audience projects onto it
  (McCloud's "amplification through simplification").
- **Simple**: slightly more information - pupils, a nose, simple hands - but still
  few lines (Charlie Brown, preschool TV leads). Limited expression range, broad poses.

## Method
1. Pick the dominant primitive (circle/square/triangle) from the personality, and
   build the whole body from 2-4 of them.
2. Choose one memorable feature and push it (ears, hair tuft, antenna). That feature is
   the logo.
3. Remove every line that does not change the read. Test by deleting it: if nobody
   misses it, it stays deleted.
4. Design features as symbols: eyes as dots or ovals, mouth as a single line that
   changes shape. Expression comes from shape change, not wrinkles.
5. Draw it at 1 cm tall and at full page. It must read at both.
6. Draw it from 5 angles. Iconic designs often only work front-on (Mickey's ears
   cheat in 3/4); decide and document the cheats on the model sheet.

## Failure modes
- Simple without a hook: a generic blob anyone could have drawn.
- Adding realistic detail (knuckles, eyelids) that breaks the symbol language.
- Off-model drift: with so few lines, a 5% change in eye spacing is a different character.

## Verify
- [ ] Fewer than ~15 lines/shapes in the full figure.
- [ ] Recognisable as a black silhouette and at thumbnail size.
- [ ] Angle cheats documented.
