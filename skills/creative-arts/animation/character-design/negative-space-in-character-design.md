# Negative space in character design

Goal: design the shapes of the holes - between arms and torso, between legs, around
props - so the silhouette reads and carries a distinctive pattern.

## Principles
- Negative shapes are as much part of the design as positive ones. A triangle of
  space between arm and waist reads as "hand on hip" instantly.
- Varied negative shapes beat uniform gaps (a big gap and a small gap, not two
  identical slots).
- Long thin slivers of space read as mistakes; open them or close them.

## Method
1. Fill the design black. Look only at the white shapes around and inside it.
2. Make sure each limb that matters has at least one window of space showing its
   shape (arms away from body in the neutral pose, legs apart enough to show stance).
3. Design props and accessories with internal negative space (handle loops, gaps
   in a cape) to add character-specific shapes.
4. Give the negative shapes big-medium-small variety.
5. In turnarounds, check the side view - arms often collapse into the body there;
   offset them slightly forward or back.

## Failure modes
- Arms pinned to the body in the default pose: the silhouette becomes a column.
- Identical symmetrical gaps on each side: stiff.
- Slivers between limbs that create tangents.

## Verify
- [ ] Black fill shows readable windows for arms and legs.
- [ ] Negative shapes vary in size.
- [ ] No slivers or tangents in the negative space.
