# Handing the color script to lighting and BG

Goal: the color script stops being a pretty strip and becomes measurable targets for the people who light
and paint the shots.

## 1. Package per sequence
For each sequence deliver one sheet containing:
- The approved thumb(s) enlarged, plus 1-3 sequence keys if painted.
- **Swatches**: key light colour, shadow/fill colour, ambient, sky, accent - with hex or linear RGB values.
- **Value range**: darkest and brightest allowed values and where the brightest value belongs (focal point).
- **Light direction and story time**, sun or practical source marked on an overhead plan.
- **Mood words** (2-3) and what must change from the previous sequence.
- **Character treatment**: how the hero separates (rim, warmer, brighter).

## 2. Brief the departments
- Lighting: walk the sequence keys against the layout; agree a master shot per sequence that is lit
  and approved first, before the rest of the sequence.
- Background painters (2D): give the key per BG group plus the characters' colour models so contrast is
  checked against them.
- Compositing / grade: the target look of the sequence so the grade pushes toward it, not away.

## 3. Close the loop
- Review lit or painted master shots side by side with the key at the same size.
- Note deviations as a push or pull on a single variable (warmer, darker, less saturated).
- Update the color script if a shot reveals a better choice; the script follows the film.

## Verify
- [ ] Every sequence has a sheet with swatch values, value range, light direction.
- [ ] Master shot per sequence approved before wide lighting or painting starts.
- [ ] Reviews compare against the key at matched size.
