# Dressing a set for a character

Goal: the audience learns who lives here before the character speaks.

## 1. Write the occupant's dressing brief
- Who: age, job, income, hobbies, habits, relationships, what they hide, what they're proud of.
- How long they have lived there; whether they are tidy or messy, nostalgic or new.
- What has changed recently in their life (a breakup, a new job) - show the evidence.

## 2. Convert to objects
| Trait | Dressing evidence |
|---|---|
| Hobby | tools, half-finished projects, worn work surface |
| Status / income | quality and age of furniture, mismatched items |
| Habits | mug rings, a worn path in the rug, a sofa dent |
| Relationships | photos (who's in them, who's been removed), two toothbrushes |
| Secrets | an object half hidden that pays off later |

## 3. Arrangement
- Placement reveals personality: alphabetised shelves vs piles; everything facing the TV; a shrine corner.
- Keep evidence in the frames the camera sees, near acting areas but not behind faces.

## 4. Story props and change
- Plant objects that will pay off (coordinate with the story team).
- Plan the set's later state if the character changes (cleaned up, packed boxes, broken).

## Verify
- [ ] Dressing brief written per occupant.
- [ ] Each key trait has visible evidence in camera.
- [ ] Planted story props logged with their payoff scene.
