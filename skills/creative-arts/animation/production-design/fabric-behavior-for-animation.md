# Fabric behavior for animation

Goal: costumes move in a way that supports the performance and is affordable to animate or simulate.

## 1. Know the fabric
| Fabric type | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| Heavy (wool coat, leather) | slow, few large folds, lags and settles |
| Medium (cotton, denim) | moderate folds, follows body |
| Light (silk, chiffon) | many small folds, floats, long overlap |
| Stiff (canvas, armour) | holds shape, hinges at seams |
Specify the intended behaviour on the costume sheet with a fold drawing in rest and in action.

## 2. Fold vocabulary
Draw folds where they really form: pipe folds from a hanging point, zigzag at bent joints,
spiral folds around twisted limbs, half-lock at elbows and knees, drop folds from a support.

## 3. Animation cost
- Long, loose cloth (capes, skirts, scarves) needs simulation in CG or many drawings in 2D;
  budget it or shorten it.
- Cloth that covers key acting areas (hands, face, silhouette line of action) hides performance.
- 2D: decide what moves on secondary action and design it to be simple shapes.

## 4. Design for the sim
- Separate layers (shirt under jacket) are expensive; combine or fake where possible.
- Provide a target: a reference video of the real fabric in a similar movement.

## Verify
- [ ] Fabric weight and behaviour specified with fold drawings.
- [ ] Loose cloth has a budget (sim or hand-animated).
- [ ] Costume does not hide key acting areas.
