# Period costume research

Goal: costumes that place a character in time, place and class, accurately enough to be believed.

## 1. Define the parameters
For each character: year/decade, region, social class, occupation, age, occasion, and how fashionable they are
(ahead of, on, or behind trend). People rarely wear the newest fashion; older and poorer characters lag.

## 2. Sources, in order of reliability
- Surviving garments in museum collections (e.g. The Met Costume Institute online collection).
- Photographs and paintings of real people from the period (not fashion plates, which show the ideal).
- Pattern books, catalogues, tailoring manuals for construction.
- Costume history references; consultants for cultural and religious dress.
Film and other animation are the least reliable sources.

## 3. Extract the design-relevant facts
- Silhouette: shoulder width, waist position, skirt/trouser shape, hat shapes. This is what reads on screen.
- Fabrics and how they hang; colours achievable with period dyes; patterns.
- Fastenings and layering (what goes over what).

## 4. Stylise
- Keep the silhouette and two or three signature details; drop seams and trims the line style can't carry.
- Check against the show's design rules; note deliberate anachronisms.

## Verify
- [ ] Parameters per character (year, region, class, occupation).
- [ ] At least two primary sources per costume.
- [ ] Silhouette preserved after stylisation.
