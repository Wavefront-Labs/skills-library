# Clutter density and focal clearing

Goal: a set that feels full of life while the character and action stay instantly readable.

## 1. Density map
- From the layout or camera, mark three zones: **focal** (where acting happens and the eye must land),
  **transition**, and **periphery** (frame edges, corners, high/low areas).
- Dress the periphery densely, the transition moderately, and keep focal areas calm.
- Detail creates attention; the eye is drawn to busy areas, so busy areas near the subject should point to it.

## 2. Clear the acting space
- Nothing busy directly behind a character's head, hands or silhouette edges.
- Leave floor and surfaces clear where props will be put down or picked up.

## 3. Grouping
- Cluster objects into groups with a big-medium-small rhythm rather than evenly scattered items; even
  scattering reads as noise.
- Groups lead the eye along lines toward the subject.

## 4. Check
- Blur the frame: focal area should be the simplest shape region next to the highest contrast.
- Squint test with the character in place; the character pops first.
- Adjust per shot by hiding items from camera (cheats), logged so continuity is not broken.

## Verify
- [ ] Density map made per key camera.
- [ ] Nothing busy behind heads and hands.
- [ ] Objects grouped, not scattered.
