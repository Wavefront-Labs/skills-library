# Vehicle design

Goal: a vehicle that reads in silhouette, belongs to its world, and looks like it would move.

## 1. Brief
- Function (carry what, how many, on what terrain), era/technology level, owner's personality and status.

## 2. Silhouette and stance
- Thumbnail 20-40 side-view silhouettes first; a vehicle is identified mostly by its side profile.
- Stance: wheelbase vs body length, ride height, wheel size relative to body. Large wheels pushed to the
  corners read sturdy and capable; a low, long body reads fast.
- Mass distribution: show where the engine/weight is; the shape should lean toward its direction of travel.

## 3. Logic
- Wheels: track width wide enough for stability; steering wheels need clearance to turn.
- Wings/rotors: area plausible for size; thrust sources visible.
- Doors, hatches, seats placed where people can get in; character scale in the drawing.

## 4. Character and world
- Apply the world's shape language (rounded for friendly, angular for military/villain).
- Wear, repairs and modifications tell the owner's story (patched panel, oversized engine).

## 5. Build package
- Side, front, rear, top orthos at one scale; 3/4 hero view; callouts for moving parts (doors, suspension,
  rotors) and their ranges.
- Check silhouettes at the smallest size it appears on screen.

## Verify
- [ ] Side silhouette distinct from other vehicles in the show.
- [ ] Character figure in the drawing for scale and access.
- [ ] Orthos with moving parts and ranges.
