# Signage and in-world graphics

Goal: signs, posters, packaging and screens that make the world feel real and never distract or cause legal trouble.

## 1. Graphic language of the world
- Pick typefaces and lettering styles that belong to the period and culture; custom-letter key signs.
- Define the world's brands: logos, colours, slogans that echo the story or theme (and can carry gags).

## 2. Legibility and focus
- Decide per graphic whether it must be read (plot information), noticed (gag, world texture) or ignored.
- Must-read: large, high contrast, held on screen long enough (about 2x the time to read it aloud).
- Ignore: low contrast, broken words, simplified to shapes so it does not pull focus.

## 3. Legal and localisation
- No real brands, logos or trademarks unless licensed; avoid near-copies.
- Avoid real phone numbers, URLs and addresses (use reserved fictional formats).
- Plan localisation: keep text on separate layers or textures so it can be swapped per language;
  prefer icon-based signs where possible.

## 4. Library
- Store graphics as layered sources plus flattened textures with IDs; reuse across sets.

## Verify
- [ ] Each graphic classed must-read / notice / ignore.
- [ ] No real brands or real contact details.
- [ ] Text kept on swappable layers.
