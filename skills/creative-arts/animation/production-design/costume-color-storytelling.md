# Costume color storytelling

Goal: the audience reads who a character is and how they change partly from what colours they wear.

## 1. Colour identity
- Give each main character a dominant hue family that is distinct from the others in hue and value.
- Choose with meaning for this film (not universal symbolism): e.g. the outsider in the only warm colour
  in a cool town.

## 2. Relationships
- Allies can share a secondary colour; opponents use complementary or clashing families.
- Show influence: a character slowly picks up another's colour as they bond or are corrupted.

## 3. Arc changes
- Plan costume colour per act on the color script: muted to saturated, dark to light, or a new accent
  appears at the turning point.
- Make the change visible in silhouette-level areas (jacket, dress), not small trims.

## 4. Read in context
- Test every costume against the backgrounds and lighting states where it appears; the hero colour must
  separate from the main locations.
- Check colour-blind accessibility for colour-coded story information (red/green pairs).

## Verify
- [ ] Each main character's colour identity distinct in hue and value.
- [ ] Arc colour changes mapped to sequences.
- [ ] Costumes tested on their BGs and lighting states.
