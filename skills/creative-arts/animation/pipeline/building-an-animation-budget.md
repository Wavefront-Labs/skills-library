# Building an animation budget

A budget turns the show into costs by department and by time. Producers build it before production and track against it all the way.

## Structure (a typical breakdown)
- **Above the line**: story rights, writers, director, producers, key voice talent in many structures.
- **Below the line**: pre-production (design, storyboard, animatic), production (layout, animation, FX, lighting, rendering), post (editorial, sound, music, colour, delivery).
- **Other**: insurance, legal, completion bond where required, office and equipment, software and licences, render and storage costs.
- Producers and studios use their own chart of accounts; follow the financier's format (for example a TV network or distributor) when one is required.

## Building it
1. **Start from the script and format**: runtime, number of episodes, style (2D, 3D, hybrid), and in-house vs outsourced work.
2. **Build crew and schedule**: for each department, roles x weeks x weekly rate (`crew-planning-and-ramp-up.md`).
3. Add **fringes and payroll taxes** to labour (they vary by country and union; use your accountant's rates).
4. Add **non-labour items**: software seats, workstations, render farm or cloud render, storage, travel, insurance, voice sessions and music.
5. Add **overhead** (studio overhead) and a **contingency** (commonly around 10%, set by financier and risk).
6. Compute **cost per minute** and **cost per shot** as sanity checks and compare with similar productions.
7. Include outsourcing quotes at actual rates, including supervision and retakes (`outsourcing-service-studios.md`).

## Tracking
- Maintain a **cost report**: budget, committed, actuals to date, estimate to complete, variance (`production-tracking.md`).
- Update monthly or per milestone; flag departments trending over.
- Link to the schedule: a delay costs crew-weeks.

## Gotchas
- Rendering and storage are real costs; do not forget the farm and archive (`render-time-estimation-and-budgets.md`, `storage-tiers-and-project-lifecycle.md`).
- Retakes and revisions are the main reason animation goes over; include a revision allowance.
- Keep the budget version-controlled with dates and approvals.

## Verify
- [ ] Every line has a basis (rate x quantity).
- [ ] Contingency and overhead are explicit.
- [ ] Cost per minute is reasonable against comparable shows.
