# Animator quotas and footage tracking

A **quota** is the expected amount of finished work per artist per period, often seconds (or feet, in film terms) of animation per week. **Footage tracking** records what is actually delivered.

## Setting quotas
1. Start from **history** on similar shows: a lead's experience and past data. A published industry average is not a substitute; style, complexity and tools change throughput a lot.
2. **Grade shots** by complexity (`bidding-shots-and-sequences.md`) and assign a weight, so that a hard dialogue shot counts for more than a simple walk.
3. Adjust for **artist level** and experience with the show's rig.
4. Revisit after the first weeks of production using real data, then lock a working quota.

## Tracking
- Record per shot: assigned artist, status (blocking, spline, polish, approved), approved footage in seconds or frames, and dates, in the tracker (`production-tracking.md`).
- Report weekly: footage approved this week, cumulative vs plan, and the forecast to completion.
- Count **approved** work, not started work, to avoid false progress.
- Track **retakes**: how many rounds per shot and why. High retakes often reveal upstream problems (unclear direction, late changes).

## Using the data
- Compare cumulative footage to the plan to see if the **schedule** or **crew** needs to change (`crew-planning-and-ramp-up.md`).
- Feed it into bids and budgets (`building-an-animation-budget.md`).
- Use quotas as **planning tools**, not as personal punishment; unrealistic targets reduce quality and retention.

## Gotchas
- Different measures (shots, seconds, frames) must not be mixed in one chart.
- Dialogue vs action vs crowd have different rates; split reports.
- Revisions after approval should be logged as new work.

## Verify
- [ ] Weighted quota agreed with leads.
- [ ] Weekly footage report is produced from the tracker.
- [ ] Retake counts are logged.
