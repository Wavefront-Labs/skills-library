# Pre-production lock list

Pre-production prepares everything production needs. A **lock** is a decision that stops being changed freely, because downstream work depends on it. The **lock list** tracks every item that must be locked, who signs off, and when.

## Typical locks
- **Script lock**: dialogue and action final for the episode or sequence.
- **Design locks**: characters (model sheets, turnarounds, expressions), props, environments, colour keys and style guide.
- **Storyboard and animatic lock**: timing, shots and sequence order; the base for layout and animation (`animatic-assembly.md`, `story-reel-editorial.md`).
- **Voice recording lock**, or scratch-track lock when final voices come later.
- **Rig and asset locks**: asset build complete and approved, in the library (`asset-management.md`).
- **Pipeline and naming locks**: shot and asset naming, folder structure, delivery specs (`shot-episode-structure.md`).

## The list
Track per item: name, owner, sign-off person, planned date, actual date, status (open, in review, locked) and the downstream departments affected. Keep it in the production tracker.

## Change control after a lock
1. Any change after lock goes through a **change request**: who asks, what, why.
2. Assess **impact**: shots affected, cost, schedule (`editorial-change-lists.md`).
3. The director/producer approves or declines; approved changes are communicated with a revision number to everyone.
4. Record changes so cost overruns can be explained (`building-an-animation-budget.md`).

## Practice
- Do not release work to production on unlocked inputs unless risk is accepted in writing.
- Stagger locks by sequence so production can start on early sequences while later ones are in boards.

## Verify
- [ ] Every lock has an owner and sign-off.
- [ ] Changes after a lock have a recorded request and decision.
- [ ] Downstream teams are told when a lock changes.
