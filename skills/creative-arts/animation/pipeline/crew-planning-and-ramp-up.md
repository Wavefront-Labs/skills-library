# Crew planning and ramp-up

Crew planning answers: who do we need, from when to when, to hit delivery inside the budget.

## Steps
1. **Work backwards from delivery** through the phase schedule (`production-phases-and-deliverables.md`): when do assets need to be ready, when do shots start, when does final render lock.
2. **Define roles by department** (story, design, layout, animation, rigging, FX, lighting, compositing, editorial, sound, production management) with a count for each phase.
3. **Estimate throughput** using your own history, such as seconds of animation per animator per week (`animator-quotas-and-footage-tracking.md`), and divide the total by the available weeks to get headcount.
4. **Plot the staffing curve**: a ramp up as work becomes available, a peak during production, and a ramp down in post. Avoid hiring everyone at day one: departments downstream cannot start before upstream delivers.
5. Allow **lead time**: recruiting, contracts, onboarding, equipment and licences. A late ramp-up costs more than an early one in lost time.
6. Plan **supervision**: leads and supervisors per number of artists (ratios depend on the studio), plus production coordinators for tracking.
7. Add **buffer** for sick leave, holidays and learning curves on a new show.

## Onboarding
- A new artist typically produces less in their first weeks: style guide, templates, pipeline training and test shots.
- Provide accounts, seat licences, hardware and storage before the start date.

## Remote and outsourced teams
- Plan time zones, review cadence and secure file transfer (`secure-file-transfer-and-screeners.md`).
- Decide which work goes to vendors and when, and include their ramp-up (`bidding-shots-and-sequences.md`).

## Verify
- [ ] Staffing curve matches the schedule and budget (`building-an-animation-budget.md`).
- [ ] Each phase has the right roles ready on the right dates.
- [ ] Onboarding and licences planned before start dates.
