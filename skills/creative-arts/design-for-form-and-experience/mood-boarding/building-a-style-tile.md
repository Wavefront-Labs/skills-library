# Building a style tile

Samantha Warren introduced style tiles as a deliverable that communicates the visual direction for a web or interface project without committing to layouts. It sits between a mood board and a full design (see `...mood-boarding.building-a-mood-board-and-style-tiles`).

## Contents of a tile
- **Adjectives:** 3-5 words describing the direction (from the brand attributes; see `...mood-boarding.translating-brand-attributes-into-visual-keywords`).
- **Colour palette:** primary, secondary, accent and neutral values, with proportions.
- **Typography:** typefaces for headings, body and UI with sample text and sizes.
- **Interface elements:** a button, link, input, card, icon style, borders and radii in the chosen look.
- **Imagery and texture:** photo or illustration style, patterns, graphic motifs.
- **Logo or wordmark** if relevant.

## Steps
1. **Gather inputs:** brand words, audience, competitors, constraints (accessibility, tech).
2. **Draft elements** into a one-page frame (a Figma or Sketch file works) at screen size.
3. **Check legibility and contrast** (see `...interaction-design.checking-colour-contrast`).
4. **Write a rationale** line under each decision tied to an adjective.
5. **Iterate** to a tile that holds together as a whole.

## Verify
- [ ] Every element can be traced to an adjective.
- [ ] Text and UI elements meet contrast targets.
- [ ] The tile can be understood without a page layout.
