# Brand attributes to visual keywords

Brand attributes ("trustworthy", "playful") are abstract; boards and design need concrete visual equivalents.

## Steps
1. **Start with 3-5 attributes** from the brand strategy, and define each in a sentence ("approachable means rounded forms and warm light, never corporate stock handshakes").
2. **Ask for specifics:** what does this look like as colour, shape, texture, type, photography, motion, sound, space?
3. **Build a mapping table:**
   | Attribute | Colour | Form | Texture | Type | Imagery |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | Precise | cool neutrals | straight lines, tight radii | smooth, matte | geometric sans | macro detail, grids |
4. **Add anti-keywords:** what the brand is not ("not sterile", "not childish") with example images to avoid.
5. **Resolve conflicts:** "premium" and "friendly" can pull opposite ways; set priority and where each applies.
6. **Collect images by keyword** (see `...mood-boarding.sourcing-reference-imagery`), and test whether others read them as intended (see `...mood-boarding.measuring-attributes-with-semantic-differential-scales`).
7. **Convert keywords to a board** (see `...mood-boarding.composing-and-curating-a-mood-board`) and later to style tiles (see `...mood-boarding.building-a-style-tile`).

## Gotchas
- Generic adjectives that every brand claims ("innovative") carry no information.
- Cultural differences change meaning of colours and symbols.

## Verify
- [ ] Each attribute has at least three concrete visual translations.
- [ ] Anti-keywords are written down with examples.
- [ ] Others interpret your board as the intended attributes.
