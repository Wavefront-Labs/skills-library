# Physical material boards

A physical board lets people touch and compare real materials, colours and finishes, which screens cannot show.

## Gather
- **Samples** from suppliers (request swatches and chips), trade-show libraries, hardware and fabric stores; include paint chips, fabrics, woods, metals, plastics, leather, glass and finishes.
- Record the **maker, code, finish and colour reference** for each (and cost or lead time if relevant).
- Print supporting images and keywords on matte paper.

## Build
1. **Plan the layout** on paper at the board size (A2 or 24 x 36 in foam board is common): hero material large; supporting samples grouped by role (primary, secondary, accent).
2. **Mount** with double-sided tape, adhesive dots, or hook-and-loop for movable samples; use a neutral background (white, grey or a board colour that does not shift perceived colours).
3. **Label** each sample with a code and name in small consistent type; add a legend if codes are long.
4. **Keep proportions honest:** show samples in roughly the share they will be used, or call out accents as small.
5. **Check the light:** view under daylight and the project's artificial light (see `...concept-design.specifying-colour-and-matching-across-materials`).
6. **Protect:** a clear cover or a portable case; keep away from sunlight that fades materials.
7. **Photograph** the board for records and remote review (photos are not a colour reference).

## Verify
- [ ] Every sample has a traceable code and source.
- [ ] Proportions reflect the intended usage.
- [ ] The board was reviewed under project lighting.
