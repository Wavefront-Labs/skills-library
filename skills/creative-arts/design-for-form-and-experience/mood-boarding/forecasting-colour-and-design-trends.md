# Trend forecasting

**Goal:** an evidence-based view of where tastes are heading, early enough to design for the market 12-24 months ahead.

## Process
1. **Define scope and horizon:** category (consumer electronics CMF, interiors, UI), market, 12-24 months.
2. **Scan for signals** in many sources: runway, street style and social (Instagram, TikTok, Pinterest Predicts), trade fairs (Milan Salone, CES, Heimtextil, Maison&Objet), galleries, film, material fairs, patents, search-trend data (Google Trends), retail new-arrival feeds, subculture and niche communities, and subscription reports (WGSN, Pantone View, Coloro).
3. **Log signals** in a database: image, source, date, geography, tags.
4. **Cluster into themes** with affinity mapping (`creative-arts.design-for-form-and-experience.ux-design.affinity-diagramming-research-notes`); name each theme and write the **driver** (cultural, economic, technological, environmental).
5. **Place on a lifecycle** (Rogers adoption curve): niche signal, early adopters, mainstream, saturation; designers target the transition from early adopter to early majority.
6. **Validate:** signal volume growth, cross-industry appearance (colour showing in fashion, automotive and packaging), spend and sales data, expert interviews.
7. **Output a trend brief:** theme name, mood images, one-paragraph narrative, palette (with codes), materials and finishes, forms and graphics, shelf-life estimate, and what it means for the product line.
8. **Review quarterly;** retire dead trends and record hit rate for learning.

## Gotchas
- A single viral image is not a trend; seek repeated, independent appearances.
- Trends do not override brand; filter through brand fit and user need.
- Colour forecasts reflect economic mood (soft after crises, bright when optimistic) as well as fashion.

## Verify
- [ ] Each trend backed by several independent signals and a driver.
- [ ] Brief includes palette, materials and shelf life.
- [ ] Hit rate tracked over time.
