# Divergent design directions

Presenting several really different visual directions helps stakeholders understand options and decide, and avoids the "make it more like ... " drift of a single option.

## Steps
1. **Define the spectrum** from the brand attributes (see `...mood-boarding.translating-brand-attributes-into-visual-keywords`): for example along "classic - contemporary" and "quiet - bold".
2. **Pick three positions:** one close to the brief's safe centre, one stretching toward one extreme, one toward another. Two options invite a coin-flip; more than three overwhelm.
3. **Make them different in more than colour:** vary typography, imagery, layout, shape language and tone. Test the difference by blurring each to see if they still differ.
4. **Name each** with a short title and a rationale that ties to goals ("Warm Workshop: approachable and handcrafted").
5. **Keep comparable:** use the same format (style tile or element collage) and the same elements, such as a header, button, card and photo, so differences are the point.
6. **Check each against constraints:** accessibility, tech, brand guidelines, cost.
7. **Prepare a recommendation** and the reasoning, even if you present all options evenly.

## Gotchas
- Two options that are clones with different colours.
- A strawman option included only to make another look good.

## Verify
- [ ] The three directions are distinguishable at a glance.
- [ ] Each has a name, a rationale and a constraint check.
- [ ] They use the same elements for comparison.
