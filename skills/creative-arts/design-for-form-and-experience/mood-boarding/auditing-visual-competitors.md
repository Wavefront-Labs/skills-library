# Visual competitor audit

A visual audit compares how competing brands and products look so you can find conventions to meet and territory to own. It differs from a UX audit (see `...ux-design.running-a-competitive-ux-audit`) by focusing on visual language.

## Steps
1. **Choose the set:** direct competitors, adjacent categories, and aspirational brands (8-15).
2. **Capture consistently:** logo, homepage or hero, product shots, packaging, social posts, store or environment; screenshot at the same size and date them.
3. **Tabulate** attributes per brand: dominant and accent colours (record values), typefaces and style (serif, geometric sans, script), imagery style (photo or illustration, people or product, light or dark), layout density, tone of voice, shape language (round or sharp), motion.
4. **Lay out a grid** (a board with all logos or hero images at one scale) to see patterns.
5. **Spot conventions** (for example every brand in the category uses blue, or uses a particular mark type) and **gaps** (unused colours, textures or tones).
6. **Plot a perceptual map:** choose two meaningful axes (for example playful-serious, premium-accessible) and place the brands.
7. **Conclude:** what you must match to be understood, what you can differentiate on, and the risks.
8. **Use it:** feed into mood boards (see `...mood-boarding.translating-brand-attributes-into-visual-keywords`) and style direction.

## Gotchas
- Differentiate on a dimension the audience cares about, not only to be different.
- Copying the category leader is not a strategy.

## Verify
- [ ] Every brand was captured at the same time and format.
- [ ] Findings state conventions and gaps with examples.
- [ ] The map's axes tie to the brand strategy.
