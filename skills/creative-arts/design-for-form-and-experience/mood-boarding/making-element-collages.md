# Element collages

Dan Mall describes element collages as a way to explore visual direction by composing fragments (type, colour, imagery, UI pieces, textures) into a rough, loose composition, rather than designing full page comps. They tend to start conversations about look and feel quickly.

## Steps
1. **Collect elements** from the brand and references: logo, product photos, a candidate typeface, colour swatches, texture crops, icon samples, buttons and cards, screenshots, quotes or headlines.
2. **Compose** them in a single canvas (Figma, Photoshop or Sketch): overlap, crop, scale, and arrange for balance and rhythm; use whitespace; do not worry about realistic page structure.
3. **Make it intentionally unfinished** so stakeholders react to look and feel and not to layout details.
4. **Annotate** with a few notes: what each part signals (confident, warm) and why.
5. **Create two or three collages** with different leanings (see `...mood-boarding.exploring-divergent-design-directions`).
6. **Collect reactions** by asking what the collage says about the brand and which elements feel right or wrong (see `...mood-boarding.presenting-design-directions-and-gathering-feedback`).
7. **Translate** the selected collage into a style tile (see `...mood-boarding.building-a-style-tile`) and a component design.

## Gotchas
- A collage is exploratory; do not present it as final.
- Without a message, a collage becomes decoration.

## Verify
- [ ] Each collage has a stated message.
- [ ] Elements come from real brand assets and references.
- [ ] Feedback is recorded by element.
