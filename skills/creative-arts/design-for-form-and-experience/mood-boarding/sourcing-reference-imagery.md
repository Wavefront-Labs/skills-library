# Sourcing reference imagery

Good boards come from deliberate research, not the first page of an image search.

## Steps
1. **Write the questions** the imagery should answer: mood, materials, colour, composition, use context, competitors, historical precedents.
2. **Search widely.** Mix sources so the board is not a copy of everyone else's feed:
   - Museum and library open-access collections (for example the Met's Open Access programme offers many public-domain images).
   - Wikimedia Commons and other open repositories with stated licences.
   - Stock and free photo sites (note each site's licence).
   - Curation tools (Are.na, Pinterest) for discovery; trace images back to their originals.
   - Design archives, books, film stills, product catalogues, trade shows.
   - **Your own photography:** materials, shadows, details, places. These are original and licence-free.
3. **Search by concept and by words other than the obvious:** synonyms, other languages, adjacent fields (architecture for packaging, textiles for UI).
4. **Capture provenance immediately:** source URL, creator, licence, date saved (see `...mood-boarding.licensing-and-clearing-reference-images`).
5. **Save at usable resolution** and keep the original.
6. **Cull as you go:** keep the strongest 3x more than you will show.

## Gotchas
- Pinterest-sourced images often lack credit; find the source before publishing anything.
- AI-generated images may have unclear rights and provenance; label them and check licence terms of the tool.

## Verify
- [ ] Every saved image has a source and licence note.
- [ ] Sources span at least four different types.
- [ ] The research answers the written questions.
