# Board composition and curation

A mood board communicates a feeling and direction; its composition should do the same.

## Steps
1. **State the message** in one sentence ("calm, tactile, precise").
2. **Curate hard:** from your library, keep only images that carry the message; typically a dozen to 25 on a single board. Remove anything that merely looks nice.
3. **Choose a hero image** that carries the central idea, and place it larger.
4. **Vary scale and role:** a few large images for atmosphere, medium for context, small for detail (texture, material). Include non-photo items: swatches, type samples, a drawing.
5. **Balance:** distribute colour and visual weight; avoid clusters of the same subject; allow some tension so it is not monotonous.
6. **Group by theme** (materials, colour, form, environment) with clear spacing, or let images flow in an organic layout if the mood is about feeling; be deliberate either way.
7. **Use negative space** so the eye can rest; do not fill every gap.
8. **Label minimally:** keywords or short captions for intent; credit sources if shared externally (see `...mood-boarding.licensing-and-clearing-reference-images`).
9. **Test with fresh eyes:** ask someone to describe the mood in three words and compare with your intent (see `...mood-boarding.measuring-attributes-with-semantic-differential-scales`).

## Verify
- [ ] The board communicates the intended words without explanation.
- [ ] There is one clear focal image.
- [ ] Every image earns its place.
