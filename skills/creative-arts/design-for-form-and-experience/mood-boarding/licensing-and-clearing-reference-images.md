# Image rights and licensing for reference work

This is a practical overview, not legal advice. Copyright law varies by country; ask a lawyer before publishing anything uncertain.

## Think by use
- **Internal reference** (private board used inside the team) carries lower risk than anything **shown to a client, published, or sold**. Boards that go outside the team need cleared images or originals.
- Copying a reference image's composition into final work is a separate risk from displaying it.

## Licence types
- **Public domain and CC0:** no restrictions (check the claim is reliable and applies in your country).
- **Creative Commons:** CC BY (credit required), BY-SA (credit and share alike), BY-NC (non-commercial only), BY-ND (no derivatives), and combinations. Follow the stated terms and attribute as required.
- **Royalty-free** means a one-time licence fee with no per-use royalty; it does not mean free or unrestricted, so read the licence for permitted uses (print runs, resale, merchandise).
- **Rights-managed** licences are per use.
- **Editorial-use-only** images cannot be used commercially or in advertising.
- Stock sites' own licences (for example free photo sites) differ; read them.

## Releases
- Recognisable people need a **model release** for commercial use; private property and trademarks can need releases or clearance; artwork and design visible in a photo may be protected.

## Practice
1. Keep a **source log** (URL, creator, licence, date, permitted use).
2. Prefer your own photographs, commissioned imagery or openly licensed images for anything public.
3. Credit creators on boards shown outside the team.
4. Replace unlicensed images before the board becomes a deliverable.
5. Do not remove watermarks or credits.

## Verify
- [ ] Anything leaving the team has a licence you can cite.
- [ ] Attribution requirements are met.
- [ ] Uncertain cases went to legal advice.
