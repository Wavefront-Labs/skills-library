# Bodystorming

Bodystorming is brainstorming with the body: teams act out use of a product or service in a physical setting using rough props, to discover problems and ideas that talking and sketching miss. The term and approach are described in the 2003 paper by Oulasvirta and colleagues, and similar enactment methods appear in service design.

## Prepare
1. **Pick a scenario** (a storyboard moment; see `...concept-design.storyboarding-use-scenarios-for-concepts`) and the questions you want answered.
2. **Choose the setting:** ideally the real place or a mock-up (a corridor as a ward, tables as a shop counter).
3. **Make rough props:** cardboard, paper screens, sticky-note interfaces, a phone as a stand-in; speed beats polish.
4. **Assign roles:** customer, staff, system (a human acting as the system, see `...prototyping.wizard-of-oz-prototypes`), observers.

## Run (30-90 minutes)
1. Brief the roles and the starting situation.
2. **Act through the scenario** in real time; let things go wrong; the "system" responds from a prepared script.
3. **Pause and change:** stop, adjust the setup or the idea, and replay; try variations and extreme cases.
4. **Observers record** sticking points, workarounds, emotions, and ideas; use cameras with permission.
5. **Debrief** immediately: what surprised us, what felt awkward, what we would change.

## After
Update the journey map or flows; turn insights into requirements; decide what to prototype next.

## Verify
- [ ] The scenario and questions were written before starting.
- [ ] At least two variations were acted out.
- [ ] Insights are logged with who observed them.
