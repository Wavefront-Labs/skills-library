# Video prototypes

A video prototype shows a concept in use through a short film, without building the product. It is useful for products that are expensive or impossible to build early, or for explaining a service to stakeholders. Bill Buxton's work on sketching user experiences discusses showing interactions before they exist.

## Steps
1. **Goal and audience:** what should viewers understand or decide after watching? Keep to one scenario (30-90 seconds is common).
2. **Storyboard** (see `...concept-design.storyboarding-use-scenarios-for-concepts`): 6-12 frames with action and caption; include the problem, the moment of use, and the outcome.
3. **Script and shot list:** short lines, no technical jargon; list props, locations, people.
4. **Prepare props:** a paper or Figma prototype on a real phone, a foam model, a cardboard mock-up, or screen overlays to add later.
5. **Shoot** on a phone: steady (tripod or stable surface), good light, landscape, clear audio; get close-ups of hands and interface moments; shoot extra.
6. **Edit** in any editor: cut tightly, add titles, overlay UI animations (a screen recording or After Effects) where a prop cannot show it, and clean audio.
7. **Show honestly:** label what is simulated; avoid implying capabilities that do not exist.
8. **Test:** show to a few people and ask them to explain what the product does and for whom; refine.

## Verify
- [ ] A viewer can restate the concept after one viewing.
- [ ] Simulated parts are labelled.
- [ ] People appearing in the video gave consent.
