# Brand form DNA across a product family

Form DNA is the small set of repeatable visual decisions that make different products recognisable as one brand even when function, size and price differ. Automotive design uses the term design DNA; consumer electronics, appliances and furniture use the same logic.

## Build the DNA (from existing products or for a new brand)
1. **Audit:** line up top, front and side views of 5-10 current or admired products at one scale. Mark what repeats.
2. **Extract features** into a short list (aim for 5-8):
   - Signature graphic or light signature.
   - Silhouette and proportion rules (stance, ratio of glasshouse to body, height to width).
   - Surface language: crisp lines vs soft volumes, convex vs concave, curve tension.
   - Radii family: the set of corner and fillet radii allowed.
   - Parting-line and gap logic; interface and control placement.
   - CMF vocabulary (see `...concept-design.writing-a-cmf-specification-sheet`).
3. **Write rules as testable statements:** "Front face is flat with a single 3 mm radius to the side" beats "friendly look."
4. **Separate fixed from flexible:** what must stay constant from smallest to largest family member, and where variants may differ.
5. **Test coherence:** put new concepts in the line-up at silhouette scale; if you cannot tell the brand with the logo hidden, the DNA is not carrying.

## Evolution
Plan a refresh cycle; change one DNA element per generation so recognition carries over.

## Verify
- [ ] Every DNA rule can be checked on a drawing.
- [ ] A new family member passes the logo-hidden test.
- [ ] Rules distinguish the fixed from the variable.
