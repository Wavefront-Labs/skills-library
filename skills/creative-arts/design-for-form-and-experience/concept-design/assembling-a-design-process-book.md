# Process book

A process book shows how a design was reached, not only the result. It serves clients, portfolios and award entries.

## Structure (typically 20-40 pages or 10-20 landscape spreads)
1. **Cover and one-page summary:** product, problem, outcome.
2. **Brief and context:** users, constraints, goals (see `...concept-design.writing-and-decoding-a-design-brief`).
3. **Research and insights:** photos, quotes, 3-5 key findings, opportunity statements.
4. **Ideation:** thumbnail walls, SCAMPER results, early models.
5. **Selection:** criteria and chosen directions; why others were dropped (a Pugh matrix).
6. **Development:** sketches to CAD to models, ergonomic tests, CMF and engineering decisions.
7. **Final design:** hero renders, details, exploded view, use scenarios, specification.
8. **Reflection:** what you learned, what you would change, next steps.

## Page rules
- One idea per page or spread, with a headline that states the point ("Large grip tested best with small hands").
- Show real artefacts (photos of messy models) beside polished renders.
- Consistent grid, two typefaces at most, restrained colour.
- Captions give date, medium and what to notice.

## Delivery
Export PDFs for print and screen; keep file size reasonable for email; keep the editable source.

## Verify
- [ ] Skimming only the headlines tells the story.
- [ ] Every decision shows a reason.
- [ ] Failures and pivots are included.
