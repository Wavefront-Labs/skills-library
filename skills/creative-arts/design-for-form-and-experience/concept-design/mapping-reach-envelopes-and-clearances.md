# Reach envelopes and clearances

Reach envelopes show where a user can touch without moving the body much. Clearances are the minimum spaces that let a user fit, move or put a hand in.

## Rules of thumb
- **Design reach for the small user (for example 5th percentile) and clearance for the large user (95th percentile),** using data for the target population (see `...concept-design.sizing-products-with-anthropometric-percentiles`).
- Separate **functional reach** (arm reach with shoulders fixed) from **extended reach** (leaning). Use functional reach for frequent controls.
- Seated: frequent controls inside the arc in front of the body at about elbow height; occasional controls at the edge; emergency controls reachable without changing posture.
- Standing: frequent items between roughly hip and shoulder height of the smallest user; avoid below the knees and above the shoulder.
- **Hand clearance:** allow for gloves and tools; give finger access around knobs, handles and connectors.
- Include **knee and leg clearance** under work surfaces.

## Process
1. Draw the user body (a manikin) in plan and side views, or use a digital human model.
2. Draw reach arcs for the small user and plot controls.
3. Draw clearance volumes for the large user and check collisions.
4. Test with a quick mockup (see `...concept-design.building-ergonomic-bucks-and-sizing-mockups`).

Take numeric values from the source tables for your population; do not carry numbers from one dataset to another.

## Verify
- [ ] Frequent controls are inside the small-user reach arc.
- [ ] Clearances fit the large-user body and hands.
- [ ] A physical test with real users agrees with the drawing.
