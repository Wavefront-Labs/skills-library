# Wall thickness, ribs and sink marks

For injection-moulded parts. Values are typical guidelines that vary by resin, geometry and tool; confirm with the resin supplier and moulder before release.

## Principles
- **Uniform wall thickness** avoids differential cooling that causes warp and sink. Where thickness must change, transition gradually.
- **Typical range:** many parts in common thermoplastics sit around 1.5-3 mm; thinner walls need higher injection pressure.
- **Ribs add stiffness without thick walls.** A common starting guideline is a rib base thickness of roughly 50-60 percent of the adjoining wall (lower on glossy visible faces) and height up to about three times the wall, with draft on the rib sides and a small radius at the base. Check against the resin supplier's data.
- **Bosses:** keep the wall around a screw hole similar to the main wall; connect to walls with ribs or gussets; avoid solid thick bosses.
- **Radii:** avoid sharp internal corners; a radius of roughly half the wall is a common start.
- **Draft** on all faces parallel to the pull direction.

## Sink marks
Sink marks are depressions on the visible face opposite a thick feature (rib or boss). Reduce with thinner ribs, coring out thick sections, texture on the visible face (hides sink), gating near the thick section and adequate packing. Gloss surfaces show them most.

## Check
Run a mould-flow simulation or ask the moulder for a design review; inspect first-shot samples.

## Verify
- [ ] Wall thickness is uniform and transitions gradual.
- [ ] Rib and boss geometry follows supplier guidance for the resin.
- [ ] A DFM review is recorded with the tooling vendor.
