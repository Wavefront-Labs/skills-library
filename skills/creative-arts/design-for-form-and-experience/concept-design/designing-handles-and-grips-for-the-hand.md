# Handles and grips

**Goal:** a grip that is comfortable for the target hands, transmits force without hot spots,
and keeps the wrist straight.

## Identify the grip
| Grip | Description | Typical handle |
|---|---|---|
| Power grip | Fingers wrap, thumb closes over | Hammer, pan, drill; diameter roughly 30-50 mm, 40 mm a common sweet spot |
| Precision grip | Fingertips and thumb pinch | Pen, scalpel, stylus; roughly 8-16 mm |
| Hook grip | Fingers hooked, thumb idle | Bag handles, drawer pulls; clearance at least 35-50 mm for fingers (more with gloves) |
Handle length for power grip: at least 100 mm, 120 mm or more for gloved use (palm breadth
plus margin).

## Shape rules
1. Cross-section: slightly oval or rounded-rectangular resists rotation better than a pure
   circle; avoid sharp edges and deep finger grooves (they fit only one hand size and create
   pressure points).
2. **Bend the tool, not the wrist:** angle the handle so the wrist stays neutral in the main
   task (pistol grips for horizontal pushing, straight handles for vertical force).
3. Spread pressure over the palm: avoid handles that end in the middle of the palm (pressure on
   nerves at the palm centre).
4. Surface: medium friction texture or soft overmould where torque is applied; smooth where the
   hand must slide.
5. Two-handed or high-force tasks: provide a second grip point.

## Process
1. Start with 3-5 foam or 3D printed grip variants spanning the diameter range.
2. Test with users at both hand-size extremes doing the real task (with gloves if relevant).
3. Record comfort ratings and hot spots (ask users to mark pressure points on a hand diagram).

## Verify
Users from the 5th to 95th percentile hand length complete the task with a neutral wrist and no
reported pressure points; the chosen diameter is within the range for the grip type.
