# Ellipses and cylinders in perspective

**Goal:** round parts (wheels, knobs, bottles, lenses) that look like circles sitting in
the right plane, not tilted coins.

## The one rule: minor axis = axle
A circle in perspective is an ellipse. Its **minor axis** (the short one) always lines up
with the **axis of rotation** of the cylinder (the axle). Draw the axle line toward its
vanishing point first, then draw the ellipse with its minor axis on that line. Every
"wonky wheel" in a sketch breaks this rule.

## Ellipse degree
The degree is how open the ellipse is: 0 = a line, 90 = a full circle.
- A face square to the viewer at the horizon is near 0 degrees; it opens as it moves away
  from the horizon (a cup below eye level shows a fuller top ellipse further down).
- On one cylinder, the ellipse **further from the horizon is more open**. A bottle seen
  from above: base ellipse rounder than the top.
- For a circle on a perspective square, inscribe it: the ellipse touches the midpoints of
  the four sides (found by diagonals). The ellipse's widest point sits slightly in front of
  the square's diagonal centre, not on it.

## Drawing technique
1. Ghost the motion above the paper 2-3 times, then commit from the shoulder in one stroke;
   go round twice and keep the better line.
2. Practise sheets: rows of ellipses at 15, 30, 45, 60 degrees through a drawn minor axis.
3. Ellipse guides (templates in 5 degree steps) for presentation drawings; pick the degree
   by matching a test circle in the box.

## Cylinders and wheels
- Cylinder: axis line, two ellipses on it (more open one further from horizon), then
  tangents joining the ellipse extremes, perpendicular to the axis visually.
- Wheels: draw the wheel axle to the car's side VP; both wheels' minor axes on parallel
  axles to the same VP. Tyre width = a second concentric-axis ellipse offset along the axle.
- Turned parts (knobs, lenses): stack ellipses on one axis, then connect the profile.

## Gotchas
- An ellipse is symmetric front-to-back; do not draw the near half fatter (egg shape).
- Ellipses have rounded ends, never pointed.
- In three-point perspective the vertical axis tilts, so the minor axis tilts too.

## Verify
Draw the axle through the finished ellipse: it should bisect the ellipse along its short
dimension. Rotate the page 90 degrees; a correct ellipse still looks symmetric.
