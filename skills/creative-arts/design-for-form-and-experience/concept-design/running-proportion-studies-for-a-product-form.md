# Proportion studies

**Goal:** find the overall proportions that make the object read right (stable, light,
premium, sporty) before any surface detail exists. Details cannot rescue bad proportions.

## Method
1. **Fix the hard constraints** in a simple orthographic side view: internal package
   (battery, board, motor), contact points with the user or ground, fixed dimensions.
   Draw them as grey blocks.
2. **Variation grid:** duplicate the side view 9-16 times. Change one variable per row:
   overall height, top-to-base ratio, where the widest point sits, overhang front/back,
   corner radius size. Keep line weight and style identical so the eye compares only
   proportion.
3. **Silhouette fill:** fill each variant black. Proportion reads more clearly as a solid
   silhouette than as a line drawing.
4. **Key ratios:** note the ratios of the favourites (height:length, base:top, volume
   above vs below a break line). Common tricks: a visual break near (not at) the centre;
   stacking volumes with the heavier one at the bottom for stability; tapering toward the
   top to look lighter.
5. **Context overlay:** place candidates at true scale next to competitor silhouettes and
   human hands or bodies. Proportions that look elegant alone can look toy-like at scale.
6. **Pick and lock:** choose 2-3, convert to front and top views, check they still work in
   3/4 perspective (side-view elegance sometimes vanishes in 3/4).

## Gotchas
- Do not change two variables at once in the grid; you will not know which one helped.
- Proportions read differently at small screen size than on the wall; review printed at
  life size or on a large monitor at 1:1.
- Golden-ratio claims are rarely why something looks right; compare against real references.

## Verify
Reviewers shown the silhouette grid blind pick the same favourites; the chosen proportion
still contains the package with required wall thickness.
