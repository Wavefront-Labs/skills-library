# Ergonomic evaluation with users

Goal: collect evidence that a product is comfortable, safe and efficient across target users.

## Plan
1. **Hypotheses and tasks:** what must be comfortable (a long grip, a repetitive reach)? Choose realistic tasks and durations.
2. **Participants:** cover the size range (small and large), handedness, age and relevant abilities; a handful to about a dozen for qualitative formative work, more for quantitative claims.
3. **Prototypes:** working or appearance models at real weight and size.
4. **Measures:** subjective comfort or discomfort ratings by body area (a body map with a rating scale), task time and errors, posture from video; grip force or EMG only when equipment and expertise exist.

## Run
- Brief, consent and warm-up; randomise task or option order when comparing.
- Video hands and posture from two angles; photograph the grip.
- Ask after each task and at the end: comfort rating, where it hurt, what they would change.
- Look for compensating behaviours (changing grip, resting the wrist).

## Analyse
Combine ratings with video; list issues by body region, severity and frequency; relate them to design parameters (grip diameter, handle angle, weight). Write changes as measurable requirements.

## Verify
- [ ] Participants span the user range.
- [ ] Ratings and observations agree, or differences are explained.
- [ ] Findings trace to changes with dimensions.
