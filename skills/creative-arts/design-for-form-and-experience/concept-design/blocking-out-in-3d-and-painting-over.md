# 3D blockout and paintover

Use simple 3D geometry as an accurate perspective and lighting underlay, then paint over it.

## Workflow
1. **Block out** with primitives in Blender, SketchUp, Fusion or similar. Use real-world scale (people, doors, cars). Do not model detail.
2. **Camera:** set focal length and framing for the story; keep the horizon line intentional. Save several angles.
3. **Lighting:** add a sun or HDRI and a couple of lights; render a **clay render** (one neutral material), plus ambient occlusion or depth passes if available.
4. **Export** the render at the final aspect ratio as PNG or EXR.
5. **Paint over** in Photoshop, Krita or Procreate: put the render at the bottom, paint colour and detail above on separate layers, and redefine silhouettes that look too CG.
6. **Add textures and photo elements** carefully (see `...concept-design.photobashing-for-concept-art`).
7. **Iterate cheaply:** move the camera or light in 3D and repaint instead of redrawing.

## Pitfalls
- Over-modelled blockouts waste time; stay rough until the design is decided.
- Painting over every pixel hides the benefit of the 3D perspective; keep the main lines.
- A clay render has no design in it; the paintover supplies that.

## Verify
- [ ] Perspective lines in the painting match the blockout.
- [ ] The paintover adds shapes and story, not only texture.
- [ ] A changed camera or light regenerates the base easily.
