# Use-scenario storyboards

**Goal:** show a product in the life of a user, so reviewers judge the experience, not just a
render.

## Structure (6-9 frames)
1. **Context:** who, where, when (one frame; establishing shot).
2. **Trigger:** the moment a need arises.
3. **Discovery / reach:** how they find or pick up the product.
4. **Key interactions (2-4 frames):** the moments the concept improves. Close-ups on hands and
   the interface.
5. **Outcome:** need met; show the emotion (relief, delight).
6. **Afterwards:** storage, charging, cleaning, return to rest. Often forgotten and often where
   concepts fail.

## Drawing it
- Simple figures and environments; keep the product drawn accurately and consistently.
- Vary shot size like film: wide for context, medium for action, close-up for interaction.
- One idea per frame; a caption of one line under each.
- Colour or highlight only the product so it stays the focus.

## Uses
- Concept reviews: compare 2-3 concepts with storyboards of the same scenario.
- Testing: show storyboards to users to check whether the scenario is real for them.
- Edge cases: draw a second storyboard for a bad day (dropped, used with gloves, dark room).

## Verify
Someone outside the project can retell the story and name the product's key benefit from the
storyboard alone; every frame's caption relates to a requirement or research finding.
