# CMF strategy and palette for a product line

CMF means colour, material, finish. A line-level strategy decides palette and material families once, so individual products inherit them. For a per-product specification see `...concept-design.writing-a-cmf-specification-sheet`.

## Inputs
- Brand attributes and form DNA; target users and regions (colour meaning varies by culture).
- Price tiers and channels; manufacturing options per tier (moulded-in colour, paint, anodise, fabric).
- Trend and competitor scan (see `...mood-boarding.forecasting-colour-and-design-trends`).
- Constraints: cost per part, recycled-content goals, material regulations, finish durability.

## Steps
1. **Define 2-3 CMF themes** as short stories ("calm natural", "technical dark"), each with keywords and imagery.
2. **Set the palette structure:** a neutral base (2-3), one hero colour that carries the brand, 2-3 accents, and seasonal or limited colours. Keep unique colours few; each new colour carries tooling, inventory and quality-control cost.
3. **Allocate by tier:** entry gets the fewest colours and cheapest finishes; premium gets richer materials and special finishes.
4. **Define material families** (for example one soft-touch polymer, one metal finish, one textile) and where each is allowed, so touch points feel consistent.
5. **Plan colourways** as a matrix of product x colour x tier, and check each cell is manufacturable and consistent across materials (see `...concept-design.specifying-colour-and-matching-across-materials`).
6. **Plan the calendar:** core colours stay, accents rotate by season or generation.
7. **Document** in a CMF strategy deck with swatches, sample references and usage rules.

## Verify
- [ ] The palette fits on one page and every colour has a role.
- [ ] Each colourway is feasible in each material of that product.
- [ ] Cost impact of each finish was reviewed with sourcing.
