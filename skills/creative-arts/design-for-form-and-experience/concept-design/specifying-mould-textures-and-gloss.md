# Mould textures and gloss

**Goal:** choose a texture that delivers the intended look and touch, can actually be
released from the tool, and is called out unambiguously.

## Reference systems
| System | What it describes | Example callout |
|---|---|---|
| SPI finish (A-1...D-3) | Polish/blast grade of the tool steel; A = diamond polish (gloss), B = paper, C = stone, D = blast (matte) | "SPI B-2" |
| VDI 3400 | EDM spark-erosion roughness, ref 12 (fine) to 45 (coarse); number = 20 x log of Ra in micrometres | "VDI 27" |
| Mold-Tech codes | Photo-etched patterns (leather, geometric, stipple) from a library | "MT-11020" |
Also give target gloss in gloss units at 60 degrees for low-gloss textures.

## Draft per texture
Textured walls need extra draft so the texture does not scrape on ejection. Rule of thumb from
texture houses: about 1 degree extra per 0.025 mm (0.001 in) of texture depth, on top of base
draft; fine VDI 18-24 often needs 1-2 degrees total, coarse leather grains 3-5 degrees or more.
Check the texture supplier's chart for the specific pattern.

## Choosing
- Matte, fine textures hide sink marks, weld lines and fingerprints; gloss shows everything.
- Coarse textures read darker and lower in saturation; finer reads lighter.
- Touch surfaces: fine stipple or soft-touch paint; grip areas: deeper texture or TPE overmould.
- Keep texture consistent across mating parts; mismatched gloss is the most visible CMF failure.

## Sampling
Order texture plaques (or a texture paddle) in the production resin and colour. Review in a
D65 light booth and at an angle; texture gloss changes with view angle. Record the approved
plaque ID on the CMF sheet.

## Gotchas
- Texturing is applied after tool trials; leave tool steel untextured until geometry is frozen.
- Texture on curved or deep-draw surfaces stretches in photo-etching; ask for a test area.

## Verify
Approved plaque exists per texture; drawings show texture code and area boundaries; draft on
textured faces meets the supplier's minimum.
