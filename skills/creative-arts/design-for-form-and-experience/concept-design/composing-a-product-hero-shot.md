# Product hero-shot composition

**Goal:** one image that sells the product's character at a glance, consistent with the brand's
photography style.

## Camera
- **Focal length:** 50-100 mm equivalent for most products. Wide lenses (under 35 mm) distort and
  make products look toy-like; long lenses (over 135 mm) flatten form. Small objects photographed
  close in reality are shot with macro lenses around 90-105 mm; match that look.
- **Height:** slightly above the product's centre for a natural "on the table" view; low camera
  (below centre) for heroic, large-looking vehicles and appliances.
- **Angle:** classic three-quarter front (about 30-40 degrees off the main face) shows two faces
  and the top. Keep the face that carries the interface or logo facing the camera more.

## Framing
- Product fills about 60-75% of the frame width for a hero; leave negative space where copy will go.
- Rule of thirds for the product's visual centre, not the bounding box.
- Keep verticals vertical (KeyShot: Camera > Shift for 2-point perspective correction) unless the
  dynamic tilt is intentional.

## Depth of field
Shallow DOF makes small things look small (macro look) and big things look like miniatures.
Use a little for jewellery and earbuds, almost none for furniture and cars.

## Series consistency
Save the camera as a named camera; reuse the same height, focal length and lighting for every
colourway so a lineup compares fairly.

## Verify
Thumbnail the render at 200 px wide: the product is recognisable, the key feature is visible, and
the silhouette is clean against the background.
