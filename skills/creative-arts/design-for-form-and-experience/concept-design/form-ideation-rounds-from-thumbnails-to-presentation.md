# Form ideation rounds: from thumbnails to presentation

**Goal:** design the *look* of a product, vehicle, prop, creature or environment by
generating many options cheaply, narrowing deliberately, and refining only the
strongest — instead of polishing the first idea. The deliverable is form, communicated
clearly enough that a client, art director or modeller can act on it.

> **Status: draft.** The round structure is standard in entertainment concept art and
> industrial-design sketching courses; the numbers (how many thumbnails, how many
> directions) are typical studio habits and scale with schedule.

## Round 0: brief to constraints (30 min)
Turn the brief into a short list you can test drawings against:
- **Function and use:** what it does, who uses it, scale (draw a human for scale always).
- **World/brand language:** existing shapes, materials, era, manufacturer or faction.
- **Must-haves and must-nots.**
- **Three adjectives** for the feeling ("heavy, agricultural, trustworthy").
Collect references (see `creative-arts.design-for-form-and-experience.mood-boarding.building-a-mood-board-and-style-tiles`).
Include real-world mechanical/structural references so forms are believable.

## Round 1: silhouettes and thumbnails (1–2 h)
- Work **small** (thumbnail ~3–5 cm) and **fast**: 30–100 options. Small scale forces big
  decisions — proportion, gesture, mass distribution — not detail.
- **Silhouettes first:** filled black shapes. A design that reads in silhouette reads at
  any distance. Vary deliberately: top-heavy vs bottom-heavy, long vs compact, organic vs
  faceted, symmetric vs asymmetric.
- Use generation tricks: lasso-shape tools in Photoshop/Procreate, random shape
  scribbles then "find" the object, combining two references, kitbash blocks in 3D.
- No erasing; next thumbnail instead.

## Round 2: select and push (half a day)
- Pick **3–5 directions** that are genuinely different, not five versions of one.
  Pick against the constraints and adjectives, not "which looks coolest".
- Draw each larger in **perspective** (a simple box in two-point perspective underneath),
  with a clear **big-medium-small** hierarchy of shapes: one dominant mass, a few
  secondary, details clustered rather than evenly spread.
- Get a review now — art director/client choose or combine directions. Cheap changes happen here.

## Round 3: refine one direction (1–3 days)
- Establish **design language**: repeated shape motifs, consistent radii/bevels, a few
  materials. Inconsistent detail is the common tell of amateur concepts.
- **Rendering:** marker or digital value pass first (light from one side, form shadows,
  cast shadows), then colour and materials. Many studios block the form in 3D (Blender,
  Gravity Sketch, ZBrush) and **paint over** a render for accurate perspective and lighting.
- **Line weight:** heavier outside contour and at overlaps/shadows, lighter for surface
  details — helps form read.

## Round 4: present
- **Hero image** at a flattering three-quarter view, plus **callouts** (material,
  function, scale notes), and for production: **orthographics/turnarounds**
  (front, side, back, top) so a modeller can build it.
- Show the path: a sheet of thumbnails and the rejected directions builds trust and
  justifies the choice.

## Gotchas
- **Polishing too early**: a rendered bad idea is still a bad idea, just expensive.
- **Detail noise**: details everywhere flatten the read; rest areas are as important.
- **No scale figure**: everything looks like a toy.
- Perspective errors hidden by rendering: check ellipses (wheels, cylinders) align to
  their axes.
- Pretty but unbuildable: check with modellers or engineers mid-way if it's going to production.

## Verify
- [ ] Written constraints and three adjectives exist and each direction is checked against them.
- [ ] ≥30 thumbnails with real variation in silhouette.
- [ ] 3–5 distinct directions reviewed before refinement.
- [ ] Final has consistent design language, clear shape hierarchy, scale figure.
- [ ] Presentation includes hero view, callouts and, for production, orthographics.
