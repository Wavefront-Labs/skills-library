# Colour specification and matching

Goal: the colour on the approved sample appears on every part, in every material, every batch.

## Specify
- Name the **reference standard**: a Pantone or RAL code, a physical master, or CIELAB L*a*b* values measured with a spectrophotometer (state illuminant and observer; confirm the supplier's convention).
- State **finish and gloss** beside the colour (matte, satin, gloss, with a gloss target if the supplier measures it). A colour looks different at different gloss.
- State **tolerance** as a delta E limit in CIELAB, agreed with the supplier; tighter for adjacent visible parts, looser for hidden ones.
- Name the **material and process** per part: the same colour code in moulded plastic, paint, anodise and textile will not match without separate formulation.

## Match process
1. Choose a **master sample** per colour and material; sign and date it; keep a copy with each supplier.
2. Suppliers produce **colour chips on the actual material**; measure and compare.
3. View in a **light booth** under at least two sources (daylight and a retail-type source) to catch metamerism, where colours match under one light but not another.
4. Check **adjacent parts assembled together**, not only separately.
5. Approve in writing; re-measure incoming batches against the master.

## Gotchas
- Screen colour is not a spec; do not send RGB values as the standard.
- Textures change perceived colour: match with the final texture.
- Metallic and pearl finishes shift with viewing angle; specify the angle and add visual review.

## Verify
- [ ] Spec lists standard, finish, tolerance, material and process.
- [ ] A master sample exists at each supplier.
- [ ] Assembled parts were judged side by side under two light sources.
