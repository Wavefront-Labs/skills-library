# Split lines and assembly gaps

Related: `...concept-design.placing-parting-lines-on-moulded-products` covers tool parting; this skill covers the visible seams between assembled parts.

## Principles
- **Make the split line deliberate:** align it with form changes, colour breaks or a recess so variation looks intended. A line wandering across a continuous surface exposes every tolerance.
- **Gap and flush:** gap is the width between adjacent parts; flush (step) is the height difference. Consistency matters more than smallness; tiny gaps expose variation.
- **Add a shadow gap** (a recessed groove) or step to mask misalignment.
- **Locate by features:** pins, ribs and lips (tongue and groove) hold position and block dust and light.
- **Allow for thermal and moisture movement** in plastics and for assembly tolerances in metal and glass.

## Process
1. Decide split lines at concept stage with engineering.
2. Choose a nominal gap consistent with process capability and visibility; ask suppliers for the achievable range. Typical values differ widely by industry and are not stated here.
3. Run a **tolerance stack-up** on gap and flush for worst-case combinations.
4. Prototype and measure real assemblies; adjust nominals and locators.
5. Put gap and flush targets and tolerances on the drawings.

## Verify
- [ ] Split lines coincide with form features.
- [ ] Stack-up result is within the visual limit.
- [ ] Measured prototypes match targets.
