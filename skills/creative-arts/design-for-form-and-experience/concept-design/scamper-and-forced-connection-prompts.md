# SCAMPER and forced-connection prompts

SCAMPER is a checklist of seven idea-provoking verbs (popularised by Bob Eberle, building on Alex Osborn's earlier list of idea-spurring questions). Use it when a concept exists but feels derivative.

## The prompts, applied to a physical product
- **Substitute:** another material, power source, user, process or context? (Moulded pulp for foam; a hand crank for a battery.)
- **Combine:** merge two functions, two parts or two products? (Lamp plus speaker.)
- **Adapt:** what else is like this that we could borrow from, in another industry or in nature?
- **Modify:** change colour, shape, size or overall structure.
- **Put to another use:** other users, other places, extreme users, reuse of waste?
- **Eliminate:** remove a part, a step, a control, packaging. What if it had no screen, no screws?
- **Rearrange (sometimes called Reverse):** reorganise the components or steps, change the sequence or layout.

## Forced connection
Pick a random object or word (a deck of cards, a photo of a plant, a verb) and force a relationship to your problem: "How is this a charger? What does its structure suggest for the hinge?" The value is in the combination, not the random object.

## Running it
1. Define the problem or challenge clearly before starting, and state it on the wall.
2. Work through each letter in turn; one idea per sticky or thumbnail; hold judgement until later.
3. Cluster, vote, and take the strongest sparks into thumbnail rounds (see `...concept-design.form-ideation-rounds-from-thumbnails-to-presentation`).
4. Filter afterwards with a Pugh matrix (`...concept-design.selecting-concepts-with-a-pugh-matrix`).

## Verify
- [ ] At least one idea per letter, with sketches not just words.
- [ ] Judgement was held until clustering.
- [ ] Selected sparks trace back to the prompts that produced them.
