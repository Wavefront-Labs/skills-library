# Writing and decoding a design brief

A brief is a short agreement about what problem is being solved, for whom, within what limits, and how success will be judged. Decode a client's brief before sketching; write one when the client has none.

## Sections of a working brief (one to two pages)
1. **Background and problem:** the situation, the opportunity, what was tried.
2. **Users and context of use:** who, where, how often, what they do now.
3. **Objectives:** 2-4 outcomes, ideally measurable (target cost, volume, a usability target).
4. **Constraints:** budget, schedule, target retail price, manufacturing process and volume, standards and certifications, parts to reuse, brand guidelines.
5. **Scope and deliverables:** sketches, renders, CAD, models, specs; and what is out of scope.
6. **Competitors and references:** products to beat or avoid resembling.
7. **Stakeholders and decision-makers:** who approves each milestone.
8. **Success criteria and review points.**

## Decoding checklist (questions to ask)
- Is the stated solution (a "better handle") hiding the real problem?
- Which constraints are fixed and which are preferences? Who can change them?
- What does the client believe about the user, and what evidence supports it?
- What would make this a failure even if everything in the brief is delivered?
- What is the real timeline for decisions, tooling and launch?

## Output
Restate the brief in your own words (a one-page confirmation) and get written agreement; log assumptions as risks. Keep a list of open questions and revisit at the first review.

## Verify
- [ ] Problem is separate from solution.
- [ ] Every constraint has an owner and a source.
- [ ] Success can be judged at the review without new debate.
