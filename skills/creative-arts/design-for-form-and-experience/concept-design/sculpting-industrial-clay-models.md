# Industrial clay modelling

**Goal:** a surface you can push and pull in real time with a review group, at full or scale
size, then capture by scanning. Clay is how automotive and premium product teams judge
highlights before CAD is final.

## Setup
- **Core:** foam or wood buck 15-25 mm under the final surface so clay stays thin and stable.
- **Clay:** sulphur-free industrial clay; warm it in a clay oven to about 55-60 C so it spreads.
- **Tools:** rakes (toothed loops) for roughing, steel and plastic slicks for finishing, sweeps
  (flexible splines) for long curves, templates cut from section data, tape for character lines.

## Process
1. Apply warm clay in thin layers pressed hard into the core (no air pockets), overbuild by a few mm.
2. Let it cool to working hardness; rake in crossing directions to remove high spots.
3. Bring key sections to template, then join sections with sweeps for long surfaces.
4. Slick to a smooth finish; check highlights with a long fluorescent or line light passed
   along the model, or apply reflective film (Di-Noc type) to read reflections.
5. Mark character lines with fine tape, cut against it, and sharpen or soften the edge.
6. Work one side, then mirror via templates or a scan-and-mill of the other half.
7. Scan (structured light or laser) to feed surfacing; record which version was approved.

## Gotchas
- Clay moves with temperature; keep the studio stable and do not leave it near heaters.
- Thick clay sags; that is why the core matters.
- Reviews under one light lie; rotate the model and change lighting before signing off.

## Verify
Both halves match within agreed tolerance on the scan; highlight lines flow without breaks
under a moving line light; the scan registers to the vehicle or product coordinate system.
