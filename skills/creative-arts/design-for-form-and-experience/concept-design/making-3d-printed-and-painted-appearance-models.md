# 3D printed and painted appearance models

An appearance model looks like the production product but need not work. It is used for reviews, photography and consumer clinics.

## Choose the print process
- **SLA/resin or PolyJet:** smooth surfaces, fine detail; best for small hero parts. Resin parts may need support removal and UV post-cure.
- **SLS nylon:** strong, grainy surface; needs more filling and sanding before paint.
- **FDM:** cheapest for large parts; layer lines need much more filling. Use a fine layer height and orient so visible faces are not on supports.

## Workflow
1. **Split the model** so there are no seams on key surfaces; plan joins along real parting lines.
2. Print, remove supports, wash or cure as the process needs.
3. **Fill and sand:** thin coats of filler primer or body filler; sand through grits (roughly 220, 400, 600, then finer by hand, wet) until reflected light looks smooth; repeat prime-sand cycles as needed.
4. **Mask** for multi-colour parts and sharp split lines with low-tack tape; burnish edges. Test the paint on a spare print.
5. **Paint:** primer, colour coats and clear coat in several light coats; match the CMF sample (see `...concept-design.specifying-colour-and-matching-across-materials`). Gloss shows every flaw; matte hides some.
6. **Add real details:** actual screens or lenses, vinyl graphics, etched or printed metal sheet, real buttons and fasteners.
7. **Assemble** with consistent gaps; compare to the intended gap and flush.

## Safety
Spray paint and resin: ventilate, wear a respirator rated for organic vapours and gloves, and follow the product safety data sheets.

## Verify
- [ ] Under a raking light, no layer lines or sanding scratches show.
- [ ] Colours and gloss match the CMF samples.
- [ ] Gaps match the design intent.
