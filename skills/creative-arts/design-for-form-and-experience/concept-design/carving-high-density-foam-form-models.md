# High-density foam form models

**Goal:** a quick, cheap, true-to-size 3D form you can hold, photograph and compare,
usually within a day, before investing in CAD or prints.

## Materials and tools
- Polyurethane modelling foam / tooling board, 10-20 lb/ft3 (160-320 kg/m3) for detail;
  lower density for big volumes. Avoid blue XPS insulation foam for anything you sand finely.
- Band saw or hot-wire (EPS/XPS only, never polyurethane: toxic fumes), rasps, surform,
  sanding blocks 80-400 grit, spray adhesive or PU glue for blocking, filler primer.
- Templates: print the side and top views at 1:1, spray-mount to card.

## Steps
1. **Block up** a blank slightly larger than the bounding box; glue layers with PU glue and
   clamp. Mark a centreline on every face.
2. **Profile cut:** trace the side view on the blank, band-saw it, tape the offcuts back on,
   then cut the top view. You now have the intersection of the two silhouettes.
3. **Rough sections:** use cut-out section templates (from the cross-section sketch) to check
   key stations as you rasp.
4. **Refine** with sanding blocks, working symmetric halves alternately; recheck templates.
5. **Detail** lines with tape and a scalpel; small radii with sanding sticks.
6. **Prime** with filler primer, sand 320-400, repeat; then paint the intended colour or a
   neutral grey to judge form.

## Safety
Dust mask (FFP2/N95 minimum) and extraction when sanding PU foam; eye protection on the band
saw; no hot-wire on polyurethane.

## Gotchas
- Glue lines are harder than foam and stand proud after sanding; stagger them away from key surfaces.
- Foam dents easily; keep the model on a padded board and handle primed models with gloves.
- Make two or three variants rather than perfecting one.

## Verify
Model matches templates at each section within about 1 mm; held in hand it confirms or
changes the size and proportion decision (record which).
