# Perspective grids for product sketching

**Goal:** a grid that lets you place a product in believable space so proportions read
correctly, before you commit any line work.

## Choose the perspective type
| Type | Use | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| One point | Front-on interiors, dashboards, a product viewed square-on | Depth lines converge to one VP on the horizon; widths and heights stay parallel. |
| Two point | The default for products (3/4 view) | Two VPs on the horizon; verticals stay vertical. |
| Three point | Tall or very close objects, dramatic hero views | Third VP above or below; verticals converge. Use sparingly for consumer products. |

## Build a two point grid
1. **Horizon line.** Draw it first. Its height sets the eye level: above the object you see
   the top (most product sketches look slightly down, horizon roughly 1/3 down from the
   top of the page); at the object's middle you see no top or bottom face.
2. **Vanishing points far apart.** Put VP1 and VP2 on the horizon, ideally off the page
   (tape extra paper or use a long ruler). Close VPs = wide-angle distortion: cubes look
   stretched and corners sharper than 90 degrees.
3. **Leading edge.** Draw the nearest vertical edge of a bounding box. Connect top and bottom
   to both VPs.
4. **Depth by eye, checked by cube.** Pick widths that make the box look like the real
   ratio, then check with a cube: the front corner of the box should read as close to 90
   degrees in plan. If the angle at the near corner on paper is under 90 degrees, VPs are too close.
5. **Diagonals for midpoints.** Cross the diagonals of any face to find its perspective
   centre; use this to place the product centreline, wheel axes, buttons.
6. **Subdivide and repeat** with diagonals, never by measuring equal spacing with a ruler
   (equal spacing does not stay equal in perspective).

## Distortion control
- Keep the object inside a cone of vision of roughly 60 degrees around the centre of vision;
  anything outside looks warped.
- Mixing VP sets inside one object is the most common error: every parallel set of edges
  must go to the same VP.
- For rotated parts (a lid at an angle) find new VPs on the same horizon for the rotated
  edge set; tilted planes get VPs on a vertical line through the original VP.

## Digital shortcut
Procreate (Drawing Guide > Perspective), Photoshop (Vanishing Point filter or a
perspective-grid brush), Sketchbook (perspective guides) and Concepts all snap strokes to
VPs. Set VPs wide, then turn snapping off once the box is in, so line quality stays loose.

## Verify
- Extend three random edges past the object: each lands on its VP.
- Flip the canvas horizontally: distortion that you had stopped seeing becomes obvious.
- Draw a cube at the edge of the object: if it looks like a brick, widen the VPs.
