# Ergonomic bucks and sizing mockups

A buck is a rough, cheap physical model made to test fit, reach and posture with real people. A sizing mockup tests overall size, weight and proportions.

## Materials
Foam board, high-density foam, cardboard, hot glue, clay, dowels, 3D prints for critical interfaces, weights (washers, sand) to imitate mass.

## Steps
1. **Decide the question:** hand-span fit? Reach to the farthest control? Seated posture? Write it before building.
2. **Build the critical geometry only,** at full scale, with adjustable parts where uncertain (buttons on tape, shim layers for thickness).
3. **Add weight and centre of mass** if carried or held; people judge comfort by weight at the grip.
4. **Recruit a range of users:** at minimum small and large cases (see `...concept-design.sizing-products-with-anthropometric-percentiles`), plus left-handers and gloved users if relevant.
5. **Task test:** have them do realistic actions for several minutes; watch grip changes, wrist angles, reaching, fatigue; ask for comfort ratings.
6. **Mark up the buck:** record where thumbs and fingertips land; photograph or trace.
7. **Iterate in hours:** cut, add, re-test; record final dimensions as requirements for CAD.

## Gotchas
- Static fit is not use: test actions, not just holding.
- Your own hand is not the population.

## Verify
- [ ] A question and a pass/fail criterion existed.
- [ ] Both small and large users tried it.
- [ ] Measured dimensions were passed to the CAD brief.
