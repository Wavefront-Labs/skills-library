# Concept selection with a Pugh matrix

**Goal:** a defensible, discussable choice between 4-12 concepts, without pretending a
spreadsheet decides for you.

## Screening (Pugh)
1. **Criteria** from the brief and research: 6-12 of them, each phrased so that "better" is
   unambiguous (ease of one-hand use, perceived quality, part count, fits 200 mm shelf, brand
   fit). Avoid duplicates (cost and part count overlap).
2. **Datum:** pick a reference concept (current product or the median idea). It scores 0 on
   everything.
3. **Score** each concept against the datum per criterion: `+` better, `-` worse, `S` same.
   Do this as a group, discussing each cell; the discussion is the value.
4. **Sum** pluses and minuses; do not let a net score alone eliminate.
5. **Improve and hybridise:** look at what makes the strong concepts lose (a row of minuses)
   and whether another concept's strength could fix it. Combine.
6. **Rerun** with the best concept as the new datum until a clear leader emerges.

## Scoring (weighted, second round)
Weights summing to 100% per criterion, ratings 1-5 relative to a reference, weighted total.
Run a sensitivity check: change the top two weights by 10 points; if the winner flips, the
decision is close and should go to a user or prototype test instead.

## Gotchas
- Aesthetic criteria get under-weighted because they are hard to score. Score them from a
  quick preference test or a silhouette review, not opinion alone.
- Do not score concepts drawn at different fidelity: a rendered concept beats a thumbnail
  unfairly. Normalise to the same sketch level first.
- Pugh is for reducing, not for picking one final; keep 2-3 to develop.

## Verify
Every eliminated concept has a recorded reason (the criteria rows that sank it); the chosen
concepts have no unaddressed minus on a must-have criterion.
