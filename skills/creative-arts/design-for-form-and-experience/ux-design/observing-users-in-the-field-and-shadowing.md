# Field observation and shadowing

Observation shows what people do, which differs from what they say. Shadowing follows one person through a task or shift; field observation watches a place (a store, ward, workshop). For a structured master-apprentice interview see `...ux-design.running-a-contextual-inquiry`.

## Prepare
1. Define the **focus** (a task, a handoff, a place) and a few questions; get permission from site managers and participants, and agree what you may record (see `...ux-design.obtaining-informed-consent-and-running-ethical-research`).
2. Learn the setting's rules: safety gear, confidentiality, quiet hours.
3. Bring a notebook, camera if allowed, a small kit; dress to blend in.

## Do
- **Be a quiet presence;** let events unfold. Observe artefacts, workarounds, interruptions, handoffs, paper notes, sticky notes, who talks to whom.
- **Take notes in three layers:** what happened (facts, times), what you inferred (marked), and questions to ask later.
- **Ask lightly in the moment:** "What are you doing now?" "Why that one?" without leading.
- Sketch layouts and flows of movement; photograph items (with permission).
- Observe at different times (peak and quiet) and with several people.

## After
- Write up within a day; tag observations (pain points, workarounds, tools).
- Debrief with the team; feed insights into maps and personas.

## Verify
- [ ] Notes separate observation from interpretation.
- [ ] More than one participant or time slot was observed.
- [ ] Privacy and consent rules were followed.
