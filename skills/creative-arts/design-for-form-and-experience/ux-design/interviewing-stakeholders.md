# Stakeholder interviews

Goal: understand business goals, constraints, history, assumptions and how success will be judged before designing. Stakeholders can be executives, product owners, engineering, support, sales, legal and operations.

## Prepare
- Map who to talk to (see `...ux-design.mapping-stakeholders-and-the-service-ecosystem`); speak to stakeholders one on one, ideally at the start of the project.
- Set a goal for the interview, then draft a discussion guide (see `...ux-design.writing-an-interview-guide`). Stakeholder interviews are usually semistructured. NN/g suggests four topics: success metrics, priorities, history and expertise (constraints, solutions tried before), and process and workflow (how they want to be involved and kept informed).

## Run
1. Introduce yourself and the purpose, ask for honesty, and say that comments will not be attributed back to them if that is the case.
2. Stay neutral and listen actively; do not interrupt.
3. Probe with open prompts such as "Tell me more about that", "Can you give me an example?" and "Why is that important to you?".
4. Close with "Is there anything else that would be helpful for me to know?" and "Who else should I speak to?".
5. Send a thank-you follow-up.

## Synthesise
- Analysis can be informal for about five or fewer stakeholders; with more, NN/g suggests thematic analysis (tag observations with codes to find themes such as concerns and success metrics mentioned by several people).
- Note where stakeholders **agree, disagree or hold unverified beliefs**; list assumptions to test later in user research.
- Share a short readout to align the team on the problem and success measures; keep individual comments anonymous if promised.

## Verify
- [ ] Goals and constraints are written down with owners.
- [ ] Contradictions between stakeholders are visible.
- [ ] Assumptions are queued for validation in user research.
