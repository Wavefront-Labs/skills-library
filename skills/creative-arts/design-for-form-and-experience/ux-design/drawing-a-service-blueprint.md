# Service blueprint

**Goal:** show how a service is delivered end to end, including everything the customer never sees.

## Elements
1. **Customer actions:** steps, choices and interactions the customer performs to reach a goal, from research or a journey map (`creative-arts.design-for-form-and-experience.ux-design.mapping-a-current-state-customer-journey`).
2. **Frontstage actions:** actions in the customer's view, either human-to-human (contact employee) or human-to-computer (self-service).
3. **Backstage actions:** behind-the-scenes steps that support frontstage actions.
4. **Processes:** internal steps and interactions that let employees deliver the service.
5. **Evidence:** props and places that anyone in the blueprint interacts with; the last layer.

## Lines
- **Line of interaction:** direct interactions between customer and organisation.
- **Line of visibility:** separates visible (frontstage) from non-visible (backstage) activity.
- **Line of internal interaction:** separates contact employees from staff who do not directly support customer interactions.

Optional extras: arrows (single for one-way exchange, double for codependency), time, regulations or policy, employee emotion, metrics.

## Approach
- Choose one customer journey and its user goals; separate scenarios need separate blueprints.
- Align the blueprint to a business goal (for example reducing redundancies, improving employee experience, converging siloed processes).
- Take customer actions from research or a journey map, then map frontstage, backstage, processes and evidence, and draw the lines.

## Gotchas
- Not every customer touchpoint has a frontstage actor.
- A department measured only on the touchpoint it owns may miss organisation-level objectives.

## Verify
- [ ] All elements and the three lines are present.
- [ ] One journey per blueprint, tied to a stated business goal.
