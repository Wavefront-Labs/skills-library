# Touchpoint and moments of truth analysis

A touchpoint is any point of contact between a person and the offering (an ad, an app screen, a call, a package, a staff member). A moment of truth is a pivotal instant that shapes an overall judgement. The phrase comes from Jan Carlzon of SAS ("moments of truth") and was later used in marketing, for example by Procter & Gamble (first moment of truth at the shelf) and Google (zero moment of truth, before purchase); definitions vary by author.

## Steps
1. **List touchpoints** per journey stage and channel (digital, physical, human), including offline and third-party ones. Source: the current-state journey map (see `...ux-design.mapping-a-current-state-customer-journey`).
2. **Rate each touchpoint** from research: importance to the person, performance today, effort, emotion; use a simple scale and the evidence from interviews and metrics.
3. **Identify moments of truth:** where emotion peaks or drops, where the person decides to continue or leave, where trust is built or broken, handoffs and first or last contacts.
4. **Find gaps and inconsistencies:** tone, information or promises that differ across touchpoints.
5. **Prioritise** moments with high importance and poor performance for redesign (see `...ux-design.building-a-ux-roadmap-with-impact-effort-prioritisation`).
6. **Assign owners** and metrics per touchpoint (for example resolution time for a support call).

## Output
A touchpoint table (stage, channel, owner, rating, evidence) and a short list of three to five critical moments with proposed actions.

## Verify
- [ ] Every rating cites evidence, not opinion.
- [ ] Critical moments are tied to specific stages.
- [ ] Each priority moment has an owner and a metric.
