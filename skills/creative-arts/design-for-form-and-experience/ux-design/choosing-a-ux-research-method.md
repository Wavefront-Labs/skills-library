# Choosing a research method

A common aid is Christian Rohrer's landscape published by Nielsen Norman Group, which places methods on three dimensions: attitudinal vs behavioural (what people say vs what they do), qualitative vs quantitative (why and how vs how many), and context of use (natural, scripted, limited, or not using the product).

## Steps
1. **Write the question first** (see `...ux-design.writing-research-questions-and-a-research-plan`): "why do users abandon step 3?" is not the same as "how many do?"
2. **Place the question on the axes.**
   - Mostly behavioural: A/B testing, analytics, clickstream analytics, eyetracking.
   - Mostly attitudinal: card sorting, surveys, focus groups, interviews.
   - Mixed, leaning behavioural when possible: usability studies and field studies.
   - Qualitative methods answer why or how to fix a problem; quantitative methods answer how many and how much.
3. **Add the phase:** strategize (generative: field studies, diary studies, interviews, surveys, participatory design, concept testing), design (formative: card sorting, tree testing, usability testing, remote testing), launch and assess (summative: usability benchmarking, unmoderated testing, A/B testing, analytics, surveys).
4. **Check the constraints:** time, budget, access to users, sample size needed, ability to prototype.
5. **Combine methods:** a qualitative method to find reasons, a quantitative one to size them.
6. **Record the choice** and why alternatives were rejected in the research plan.

## Gotchas
- Surveys and interviews report opinion; they do not substitute for observation of behaviour.
- A/B tests tell you what won, not why.

## Verify
- [ ] The method answers the question as stated.
- [ ] Data type (what vs why) matches the decision to be made.
- [ ] The sample and time fit the constraints.
