# Jobs to be done statements

Jobs to be done (JTBD) frames what customers are trying to accomplish ("hire" a product for) in a situation, independent of the product. Several schools exist; the formats below are common starting points.

## Job statement
- **Job story format** (popularised by Alan Klement): "When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [expected outcome]."
  Example: "When I get to the airport with bags and little time, I want to drop my bags quickly, so I can relax before boarding."
- **Functional job** plus **emotional and social jobs** (how I want to feel or be seen).
- Keep the **verb** that describes progress, not a feature ("keep my team up to date", not "use a dashboard").

## Add the forces
For adoption, note the four forces: push (problem with the current situation), pull (attraction of the new), anxiety (about the new) and habit (of the old).

## Desired outcomes (Ulwick's outcome-driven approach)
Express outcomes as measurable statements with a direction and a metric, such as "minimise the time it takes to ..." or "reduce the likelihood that ...", then rate importance and satisfaction in a survey.

## Get the data
Use switch interviews (recent purchases, timeline of events from first thought to use) and observation; do not make statements from imagination.

## Verify
- [ ] The statement contains a situation and an outcome, not a solution.
- [ ] Evidence from at least several interviews supports it.
- [ ] Competing alternatives (including doing nothing) are named.
