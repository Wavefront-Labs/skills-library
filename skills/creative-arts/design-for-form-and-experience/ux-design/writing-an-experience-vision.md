# Experience vision

**Goal:** a shared picture of the experience you are building toward, so many small decisions point the same way.

## What it is
A short statement and supporting artefact describing the future user experience in 3-5 years: who it serves, what it feels like and what changes for them. It is a **north star**, not a feature list or a roadmap.

## Build it
1. **Ground it:** research insights, the biggest pains and opportunities, business strategy, technology trends.
2. **Draft the statement** (1-3 sentences) in user terms: "Anyone can see, understand and control their energy use at a glance, without a bill surprise." Test it against: Does it inspire? Is it specific enough to say no to things? Does it avoid feature names?
3. **Add 3-5 experience principles,** each with a short rationale and an example choice they settle ("Show the answer first, the detail on request").
4. **Create a vision artefact:** a narrative storyboard, a day-in-the-life story, or a concept video or clickable prototype that shows the experience.
5. **Align stakeholders:** run a workshop to critique and co-own it; revise.
6. **Use it:** reference it in roadmap reviews, design critiques and hiring; check each initiative against the principles.

## Gotchas
- A vision with no research reads as marketing.
- A vision nobody uses is wallpaper; tie it to prioritisation criteria and review it yearly.

## Verify
- [ ] Statement is user-centred, specific and inspiring.
- [ ] Principles are decision-ready with examples.
- [ ] The vision is referenced in prioritisation and critiques.
