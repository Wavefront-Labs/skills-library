# Moderating think-aloud sessions

In a think-aloud test, participants use the system while continuously thinking out loud, verbalizing their thoughts as they move through the interface, so you hear expectations and confusion. Nielsen calls it the number-one usability tool.

## Opening
- Welcome, consent and recording (see `creative-arts.design-for-form-and-experience.ux-design.obtaining-informed-consent-and-running-ethical-research`).
- Explain what is expected; a short video demo of a think-aloud session is one way to show it. Keeping up a running monologue is harder than it sounds, so expect to coach.

## During
- Give one task at a time (see `creative-arts.design-for-form-and-experience.ux-design.writing-task-scenarios`).
- Typically you have to prompt users to keep them talking, but prompts must be neutral: an untrained facilitator's interruptions can easily change user behaviour, and putting words in participants' mouths biases them.
- Otherwise let the users do the talking.
- Do not accept an edited commentary after the fact, and do not let users wait to speak until they have thought the situation through.
- If you realise you have biased a participant, identify that part of the session and discard it.

## Limits
- It is an unnatural situation, and participants may filter what they say to seem smart.
- It does not suit detailed statistics, serves many but not all purposes, and is tricky with speech interfaces.

## Closing
Ask for overall impressions, optionally a questionnaire (see `creative-arts.design-for-form-and-experience.ux-design.measuring-usability-with-sus-and-seq`), thank the participant and debrief the team.

- [ ] The same script was followed for every participant.
- [ ] Prompts were neutral; any biased segment is flagged and excluded.
