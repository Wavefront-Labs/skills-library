# Informed consent and research ethics

Goal: participants know what they are agreeing to, can leave at any time and are not harmed. Legal requirements vary by country and employer (for example privacy law and institutional review); check with your legal or privacy team. This skill is not legal advice.

## Consent form (plain language, one page)
- Who is running the study and who to contact.
- Purpose, in neutral terms, and what the participant will do and how long it takes.
- What will be recorded (audio, video, screen, notes) and who will see it.
- How the data will be used, where it is stored, how long it is kept, and whether quotes or clips will be shared.
- Voluntary participation: they may skip questions or stop at any time without penalty, and keep any incentive stated.
- Risks and benefits, and how compensation works.
- Signature (paper or e-sign) or recorded verbal consent where appropriate.

## Practice
1. Send the form before the session; at the start, go through it verbally and give time for questions.
2. Ask permission to record separately; stop recording on request.
3. Do not push a hesitant participant; the facilitator's job is to protect them.
4. **Minors and vulnerable users:** get guardian consent and follow institutional rules; use more caution with sensitive topics.
5. **Data handling:** pseudonymise participant IDs, keep the key separate, restrict access, delete on schedule, avoid personal data in shared repositories.
6. **Deception:** avoid it; if a design requires withholding a purpose (for example not saying which version is yours), debrief afterwards.
7. **Observers:** brief them to stay silent and not identify participants.

## Verify
- [ ] Signed or recorded consent exists for every participant.
- [ ] Anyone can find the retention period and contact.
- [ ] Files are stored with pseudonymised names.
