# Planning and facilitating a design workshop

**Goal:** a group leaves with a concrete output and shared understanding, not just good conversation.

## Plan
1. **Outcome first:** one sentence ("a prioritised list of five onboarding problems and one agreed concept to prototype"). If you cannot state it, do not run a workshop.
2. **Participants:** decision makers plus people who know the problem; 5-8 for working sessions, more only with breakouts.
3. **Agenda:** opening (goals, rules, 10 min), warm-up (5-10), diverge, converge, decide/commit, close (actions). Time each exercise and name the output (sketches, clusters, a ranked list). Build in breaks every 60-90 minutes. Total under about 3.5 hours for live sessions.
4. **Materials:** pre-read, templates (Miro or printed), markers, sticky notes, dot stickers, timer, a wall. Prepare a dry run.

## Run
- Start with purpose, agenda and **ground rules** (one conversation, phones away, build on ideas, parking lot for tangents).
- Use silent, individual work before sharing (brainwriting, sketching) so quiet people contribute; then pair or small groups; then plenary.
- Keep time visibly; stop on time. Stay neutral; ask open questions; paraphrase; protect quiet voices.
- Park off-topic points on a visible board and revisit.
- Capture photos of walls and a decision log as you go.

## Close
Review outputs, confirm **decisions, owners, deadlines**, and send a summary within 24 hours with photos and next steps. Collect quick feedback.

## Gotchas
- No named decider means the output dies after the workshop.
- Over-packed agendas skip the converge and decide steps; cut exercises instead.

## Verify
- [ ] Outcome and deliverable written before invitations.
- [ ] Agenda timed with named outputs; dry run done.
- [ ] Decisions, owners and dates sent in the follow-up.
