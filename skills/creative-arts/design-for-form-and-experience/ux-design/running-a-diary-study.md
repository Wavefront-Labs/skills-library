# Diary studies

**Goal:** learn about behaviour that unfolds over days or weeks by having participants record entries as they happen.

## Design
1. **Reporting period:** long enough to give enough data points; base it on how often the behaviour occurs and how long a typical user takes to complete the activity.
2. **Entry type:**
   - event-based: record an entry when an event of interest occurs;
   - interval-based: report at regular intervals (when you do not need detail on each interaction);
   - signal-based: respond when prompted by a signal (useful when activities vary within a day or people may forget).
   Studies often combine them.
3. **Questions:** decide whether each participant answers specific questions about each interaction they report.
4. **Participants:** NN/g's rough guide is about 5-12 for small discovery projects, 12-30 for large ones and 30-50 for large or academic research. Longer studies lose more participants, so over-recruit.
5. **Tool:** choose on data complexity, data security, participant convenience and cost. Free tools often take more work; specialised tools cost more but cut overhead.

## Running it
- **Pre-study brief:** a short meeting before the period to walk through the schedule, answer questions and confirm tool familiarity.
- Review entries as they arrive and ask follow-up questions; send periodic reminders each day or every few days, tailored to engaged, less engaged and disengaged participants.
- **Incentives:** higher than for a typical usability session; set a realistic minimum number of responses and a maximum to prevent fabricated entries; pay in instalments at milestones. Check the NN/g article for its current pay guideline.
- **Post-study interview** to clarify responses and ask reflection questions; it is the last chance to get insights from participants.

## Gotchas
- Diary studies are costly and resource-intensive.

## Verify
Each participant has entries across the whole period; findings cite entries by participant and date.
