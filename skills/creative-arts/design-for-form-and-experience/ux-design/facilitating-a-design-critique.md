# Facilitating a design critique

**Goal:** get feedback that evaluates the design against its objectives, not against reviewers' taste, and leave with notes and action items.

## Roles
- **Presenter** shares the design; **critiquers** give informed, ideally cross-disciplinary feedback.
- **Facilitator** manages time, keeps the conversation on track and resolves tension; rotate the role between critiques.
- **Recorder** takes notes publicly in a collaborative tool (the facilitator or another team member can do it).

## Before
1. Set scope and goals: what the critique should and should not cover, plus the design objectives and user needs.
2. Send the agenda and the work in advance so people can think first.
3. Choose participants on purpose, ideally from several disciplines.

## Run
1. Presenter repeats the objectives (personas, pain points, user tasks) and tells the story of the work.
2. Presenter shares concisely and asks for specific feedback: what works, what does not, where input is needed. Avoid over-explaining.
3. Run feedback with a structure so everyone contributes: **round robin** (each person in turn) or **quotas** (a set number of comments each, for example two positives and one improvement; use only to start the conversation).
4. Recorder documents the discussion live.
5. Facilitator follows up with notes and action items by email or in a shared space.

## Facilitator rules
- Keep to the agreed scope and keep it a conversation, not commands.
- Reframe opinions as questions tied to goals: not "Yikes, that layout" but "How does this layout make it easier for the user to accomplish their task quickly and efficiently?"
- Do not rush to solve problems in the room, and do not discuss only the negatives.
- Agree personas and objectives beforehand. Avoid scheduling overly long sessions; a weekly 30-minute round-robin is a starting point for small teams.
