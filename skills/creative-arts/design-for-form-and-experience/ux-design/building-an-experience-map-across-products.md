# Experience map across products

A journey map follows a person through your product or service. An experience map is broader: it follows a human activity (planning a trip, managing a chronic condition) across all products, channels and services, including competitors and non-digital ones. Jim Kalbach's Mapping Experiences covers the family of maps and how to choose among them.

## Steps
1. **Choose the activity and the person,** not your product: "organising a family move".
2. **Research** with interviews, diaries and observation about everything they do, in any channel or tool (see `...ux-design.running-a-diary-study`).
3. **Lay out the map:** a horizontal axis for phases or time of the whole activity; lanes for actions, thoughts, feelings, tools and channels used, people involved, and pain points.
4. **Add the context** (environment, time pressures, other people) and where your offer appears (if at all).
5. **Mark opportunity areas:** gaps, repeated frustrations, handoffs, places where the person works around tools.
6. **Share and use:** run a workshop to choose strategic opportunities; link to portfolios or roadmaps.

## Distinctions
- Current-state journey map: one touchpoint set, often your service.
- Experience map: the whole experience, independent of your products.
- Service blueprint: adds frontstage and backstage operations.

## Verify
- [ ] The map shows non-owned products and channels.
- [ ] Phases come from the user's view, not your funnel.
- [ ] Opportunities cite research evidence.
