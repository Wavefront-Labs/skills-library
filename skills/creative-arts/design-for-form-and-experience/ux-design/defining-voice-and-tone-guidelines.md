# Voice and tone guidelines

**Goal:** the product sounds like one consistent personality while adapting to what the user is feeling.

## The four dimensions (NN/g)
Each is a spectrum; copy can sit at either pole or between.
- Formal vs casual (casual and conversational are not synonyms)
- Serious vs funny (measures an attempt at humour, not whether it lands)
- Respectful vs irreverent (irreverence targets the subject matter, not the reader)
- Matter-of-fact vs enthusiastic

## Building it
1. Define the tone with the four dimensions first, then refine with specific tone words. Keep to a handful of tone words so the guidelines stay easy to follow; optionally add anti-tone words (tones to avoid).
2. Keep brand personality consistent but vary tone to the user's emotional state and the topic: a financial report should sound different from a careers page for students.
3. Avoid extreme tones; emphasise the chosen qualities without letting them dominate.
4. Test: humour may irritate frustrated users or people who see an error often. Evaluate tone with product-reaction testing of tone words, or Likert scales with each pole at an end.

Small wording changes move tone: "apologize" versus "sorry", or adding "Oops!" or a joke, in an error message.
