# Crazy Eights and dot voting

## Crazy Eights (diverge)
1. Each participant folds a sheet of paper in half three times to make eight sections.
2. Set a timer for eight minutes (one minute per section) and sketch an idea in each section. Rough is fine.
3. Pens down when the timer goes off.
4. Each person presents their eight ideas; then run a dot vote on which ideas and idea components the team likes most, and consolidate into a single idea to flesh out into a testable prototype.

## Dot voting (converge)
1. Gather options (sticky notes or a list) and dot stickers or pen marks.
2. State the purpose of the vote and set the number of votes: roughly a quarter of the number of options per person. Optionally limit voters to criteria they own or know well.
3. Vote quietly, with no lobbying during voting.
4. Review the collective ranking and discuss it. The board resembles a heat map, with dots concentrated on popular options.
5. To break ties, revote on only the top two to four options.
- **Ranked dots:** label dots 1, 2, 3 and assign weights to compute a score.
- **Colour coding:** colours stand for criteria, for example feasibility and user impact; add shapes so colour-blind participants can read results.

## Gotchas
- Known failure modes: one person campaigns for an option, votes split evenly between the top two, and groupthink ("everyone else voted here").
- Mitigations: give time to research, vote digitally (a survey tool, or Mural or Miro built-in voting), dictate voting order, or use out-and-back voting.
- Beware the HIPPO effect, where a senior person's opinion sways votes.

## Verify
- [ ] Eight sections sketched per person in eight minutes.
- [ ] Voting constraints stated, votes cast quietly, outcome discussed.
