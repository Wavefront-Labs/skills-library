# Usability test plan

A plan fixes what you are testing and how, so findings are comparable and stakeholders agree beforehand.

## Contents (one to two pages)
1. **Purpose and objectives:** what decisions the study will inform; 3-5 research questions.
2. **What is tested:** product, version, prototype fidelity and its limits.
3. **Method:** moderated or unmoderated, in person or remote, think-aloud or not (see `...ux-design.moderating-a-think-aloud-session` and `...ux-design.running-a-remote-unmoderated-usability-test`).
4. **Participants:** user types, number (a handful per user group for qualitative rounds is a common starting point; more for quantitative studies), screening criteria (see `...ux-design.recruiting-participants-with-a-screener`), incentives.
5. **Tasks:** list with success criteria (see `...ux-design.writing-task-scenarios`).
6. **Measures:** task success, time, errors, satisfaction (see `...ux-design.measuring-usability-with-sus-and-seq`), and observation categories.
7. **Environment and equipment:** location or tool, device, recording, observer room.
8. **Roles:** facilitator, note-taker, observers, tech support; observer rules.
9. **Schedule:** pilot date, session slots with buffers, analysis, readout.
10. **Ethics and consent:** see `...ux-design.obtaining-informed-consent-and-running-ethical-research`.
11. **Deliverables:** report format, who gets it.

## Verify
- [ ] Every task maps to a research question.
- [ ] A pilot session is scheduled before the first real participant.
- [ ] Stakeholders signed off on objectives and tasks.
