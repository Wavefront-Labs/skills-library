# Research questions and plan

**Goal:** a one-to-two page plan that ties a study to a decision, so findings get used.

## Plan sections
1. **Background and decision:** what the team must decide and by when ("whether to rebuild
   onboarding in Q3").
2. **Research questions (3-6):** what we need to learn, not the questions we ask participants.
   Good: "How do new admins currently set up permissions, and where do they get stuck?"
   Bad: "Do users like our permissions page?"
3. **Assumptions / hypotheses** to test, written as statements.
4. **Method:** chosen to fit the questions (see method selection); include why.
5. **Participants:** criteria, number, recruiting source, incentives.
6. **Logistics:** dates, sessions, tools, observers, consent and data handling.
7. **Outputs:** what will be delivered (report, workshop, journey map) and when.
8. **Stakeholders:** who reviews the plan, who observes sessions.

## Turning goals into research questions
- Start from the decision; ask "what would we need to know to decide?"
- Split vague goals: "understand users" becomes questions about behaviour, context, pain and
  current workarounds.
- Prefer questions about past behaviour and current practice over opinions about future features.
- Check each question can be answered by the method chosen.

## Gotchas
- Too many questions for one study; prioritise and park the rest.
- Questions that already assume the solution.
- No named decision owner, so the report sits unread.

## Verify
Stakeholders sign off that answering these questions would change their decision; every interview
guide or task traces to a research question.
