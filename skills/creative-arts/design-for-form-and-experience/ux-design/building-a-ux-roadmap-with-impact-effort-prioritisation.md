# Impact-effort prioritisation and a UX roadmap

**Goal:** a short, honest list of what the UX team does next and why.

## Step 1: build the backlog
Collect opportunities from research, audits, analytics and support (`creative-arts.design-for-form-and-experience.ux-design.running-a-ux-audit`). Write each as a problem plus evidence, not a solution.

## Step 2: score
- **Impact:** effect on a goal metric or user pain (frequency x severity x number of users). Use evidence: funnel drop-off, severity ratings.
- **Effort:** design plus engineering plus research time (S/M/L or t-shirt to person-weeks), and risk.
- Do it with the whole team in a workshop; discuss disagreements rather than averaging.

## Step 3: plot
2x2: high impact / low effort = **quick wins** (do now); high impact / high effort = **big bets** (plan and research); low impact / low effort = **fill-ins**; low impact / high effort = **avoid**. Consider also RICE (reach, impact, confidence, effort) when numbers exist.

## Step 4: roadmap
Use **Now / Next / Later** columns (commitment decreases to the right), not dates for uncertain work. Each card carries: problem, outcome metric, evidence, owner, dependencies, status. Align with the product roadmap and engineering capacity; leave capacity for fixes and research.

## Step 5: maintain
Review monthly: move cards, retire stale ones, record decisions and why. Publish where stakeholders can see.

## Gotchas
- Impact scores are guesses without data; label confidence.
- A roadmap of solutions locks teams in; keep problems and outcomes visible.

## Verify
- [ ] Each item has evidence, impact, effort and an outcome metric.
- [ ] Roadmap shows now/next/later with owners and dependencies.
- [ ] A review date is set.
