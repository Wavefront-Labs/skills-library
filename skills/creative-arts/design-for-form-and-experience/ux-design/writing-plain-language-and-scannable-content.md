# Plain language and scannable content

**Goal:** readers find and understand what they need on the first pass.

## Structure
- **Inverted pyramid:** the answer or action first, detail after.- Users mostly scan rather than read word by word, so write for scanning.
- Clear, literal headings, not clever ones, so a heading scan tells the story.
- One idea per short paragraph; bulleted lists to break up information.
- Highlight key terms (bold, links); avoid promotional exaggeration and keep text short.

## Words
- Prefer shorter, familiar words.
- Active voice makes clear who does what; present tense is the simplest, strongest verb form.
- Avoid hidden verbs (nominalisations): "we analyze the data", not "we conduct an analysis of the data".

## Check
1. Test with target readers that they can find and understand what they need.
2. Check scannability: can a reader state the page's point from headings and first words alone?

## Verify
- [ ] Answer in the first screen; headings tell the story.
- [ ] Sentences short; common words; active voice.
- [ ] A reader completes a find-the-fact task quickly.
