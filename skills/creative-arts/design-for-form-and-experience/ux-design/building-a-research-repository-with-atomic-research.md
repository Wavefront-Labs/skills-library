# Research repository and atomic research

Goal: keep research findings findable and reusable by future teams, not buried in slide decks.

## Atomic research
The atomic research idea splits research into small, linkable pieces. A common version is: **experiments or studies** (what was done), **facts or observations** (what was seen or said, with a source), **insights** (patterns drawn from several facts), and **recommendations** (what to do). Each atom links to its evidence so any claim can be traced back to a quote or clip.

## Build it
1. **Choose a tool** you already have: a database or wiki, a spreadsheet and shared drive, or a dedicated research tool; fit and access matter more than features.
2. **Define the schema:** study (date, question, method, participants, owner), observation (text, source, participant ID, timestamp or link), tags (user type, product area, journey stage, theme), insight, recommendation, status.
3. **Tag consistently:** a controlled vocabulary with one owner; avoid free-form tags that drift.
4. **Template every study:** plan, discussion guide, notes template, readout with links to atoms.
5. **Add the habit:** each study is filed within a week; stale items marked.
6. **Govern:** retention schedule, access control, anonymisation of personal data (see `...ux-design.obtaining-informed-consent-and-running-ethical-research`).
7. **Promote use:** a short index page, a slack or email digest, and linking insights from design and product docs.

## Verify
- [ ] A new teammate can find the last three studies on a topic in five minutes.
- [ ] Every insight links to at least two observations.
- [ ] Personal data is removed or access is restricted.
