# Evaluating designs against requirements

The last HCD activity checks whether the design meets the user requirements (see `...ux-design.writing-user-requirements`) and the context of use (see `...ux-design.specifying-the-context-of-use`).

## Steps
1. **Build a traceability table:** one row per requirement, with the measure, target value, and planned method (for example task completion in a usability test, a heuristic review, a questionnaire).
2. **Pick methods by stage and question:** early, expert reviews (heuristic evaluation, cognitive walkthrough) and paper tests; later, task-based tests with representative users, and field or longitudinal feedback for real use.
3. **Evaluate on the closest-to-real prototype that answers the question.**
4. **Record results per requirement:** met, partly met, not met, with evidence (video clips, counts, quotes).
5. **Judge:** requirements with measurable targets are checked against the target; qualitative ones need a stated acceptance rule agreed beforehand.
6. **Feed back:** unmet requirements become design changes or an explicit change to the requirement signed off by the owner.
7. **Re-test** changed areas.

## Gotchas
- Testing what is easy to measure instead of what the requirement says.
- Rewriting the requirement after the fact to make it pass.

## Verify
- [ ] Every requirement has a recorded result and evidence.
- [ ] Failed items have an owner and a next action.
- [ ] Requirement changes are logged with who approved.
