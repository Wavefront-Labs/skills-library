# Interview guide

**Goal:** a guide that keeps the interview on the research goals while leaving room to follow the participant's story. Research goals shape the questions and stay fixed during the study.

## Structure
1. **Warm-up:** explain the purpose, the kinds of questions and how the information will be used; speak slowly; open with easy questions ("Tell me a bit about yourself"). Avoid anything personal or judgemental early.
2. **Main questions:** a few well-designed, open-ended questions, each with follow-ups nested beneath. The interviewer may skip, reorder or add questions.
3. **Follow-ups:** prepare detail-gathering ones: when it happened, how long it took, whether it has happened before, how the participant felt.

## Question craft
- Ask participants to describe experiences ("Walk me through a typical day"), and ask about specific past events rather than general processes; a particular incident jogs memory. Memory is flawed, so distant events may be recalled inaccurately.
- Avoid leading questions; they compromise data validity.
- Use unscripted probes to uncover motivations and attitudes ("Tell me more about that", "Why is that important to you?"). Write them on the guide only if you fear forgetting them.

## Pilot
Pilot with a colleague (for a non-specialised topic) or with one or two target users. It reveals confusing questions, gaps, unnecessary questions and awkward ordering. Minor tweaks can continue during the study, but the research goals should not change.

## Gotchas
- Reading the guide verbatim kills rapport; treat it as a checklist.
- Showing your product too early anchors the rest.

## Verify
Every guide section maps to a research goal; the pilot ran without confusing questions; no question suggests its own answer.
