# Proto-personas

A proto-persona is a quickly written persona based on the team's current beliefs, not on research. It is a hypothesis to test. The term and method are associated with Jeff Gothelf's Lean UX.

## Workshop (60-90 minutes)
1. **Silent write:** each member writes who they think the user is: role, goals, tasks, pain points, tools, context, what success means for them.
2. **Share and cluster** the answers; agree on one to three target users and one primary.
3. **Fill a one-page template:** name and photo placeholder, short description, goals, behaviours, needs, pain points, the product's value for them, and **assumptions to validate**.
4. **Mark confidence:** circle what is known (from data) and underline what is guesses.
5. **Turn guesses into research questions** (see `...ux-design.writing-research-questions-and-a-research-plan`).

## Use
Keep them lightweight on a wall; use them to align language and to pick whom to recruit for interviews. When research arrives, **update or retire** them and produce research-based personas (see `...ux-design.building-research-based-personas`).

## Gotchas
- Do not present proto-personas as research findings; label them "hypothesis".
- Avoid stereotypes: do not invent demographics unless relevant to behaviour.
- Do not leave them untested.

## Verify
- [ ] Each proto-persona lists assumptions to validate.
- [ ] A plan exists to test the top assumptions.
- [ ] They are labelled as hypotheses.
