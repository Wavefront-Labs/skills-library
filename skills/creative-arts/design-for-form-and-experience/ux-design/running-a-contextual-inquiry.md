# Contextual inquiry

**Goal:** understand how people actually work by observing them where they normally work, and discussing what you see as it happens.

## The four principles
1. **Context:** observe users where they normally work, not in a lab or conference room.
2. **Partnership:** researcher and user share control of the conversation.
3. **Interpretation:** build a shared interpretation of the work and refine it with the user's feedback ("So you print this because...?").
4. **Focus:** know the project's purpose and the information you need; it guides what you watch and ask.

## Session structure
1. **Primer:** casual start, build rapport, explain goals, cover confidentiality and recording consent, introduce the topic.
2. **Transition:** explicitly move the user from interview mode to working mode and say you will watch and may interrupt. Do not skip this.
3. **Contextual interview:** cycle between watching the user work and stopping to discuss when something is unclear or you want to confirm an interpretation. Use open-ended questions and limit validation requests so they do not bias later behaviour.
4. **Wrap-up:** final questions, summarise your interpretations and let the user correct them.

Sessions run from an hour or two to several days depending on the work. Use a small sample of users qualified in the area. If participants cannot be interrupted, use direct observation instead: watch quietly and ask clarifying questions afterwards.

## What to capture
Steps, breakdowns, workarounds and the artefacts people use (photograph with permission), plus the physical environment.

## Gotchas
- Turning it into an interview at a desk with no real work happening.
- Not getting permission to photograph screens with private data.
- Observing only power users.

## Verify
Every interpretation was confirmed or corrected by the participant; notes record the observed workarounds or breakdowns.
