# Insight statements and how-might-we questions

**Goal:** a short set of insights that explain behaviour, each turned into questions the team can
ideate on.

## Insight statement
An insight is not a fact. It says what users do, why, and the tension that creates.
Template: **[User] [does/needs X] because [motivation], but [tension/barrier].**
Example: "New team leads build their own spreadsheets for rota planning because they don't trust
the system to handle swaps, but that means their manager never sees the real schedule."
Checks:
- Based on several participants (cite them).
- Explains a "why", not just a "what".
- Non-obvious to the team, or confirms with evidence something they were unsure about.

## How-might-we (HMW)
1. Take one insight and write several HMW questions from different angles. NN/g's tips: start from the problems you found, do not embed a solution, keep it broad, focus on the desired outcome, and phrase it positively ("increase", "create") rather than with "reduce" or "prevent":
   - Outcome: "HMW make team leads feel safe to do swaps in the system?"
   - Question an assumption: "HMW let the manager see the real schedule wherever it lives?"
2. **Scope check:** too broad ("HMW improve scheduling?") gives no traction; too narrow ("HMW add
   a swap button?") is a solution in disguise. Aim for a question that admits many solutions.
3. Write and choose the HMWs together with your team, then take the chosen ones into ideation.

## Gotchas
- Insights written as feature requests.
- HMWs that include the answer.
- Too many insights: keep only the ones that matter.

## Verify
Each insight cites the research evidence behind it (or flags weaker evidence); each chosen HMW
admits many different solutions and does not name one.
