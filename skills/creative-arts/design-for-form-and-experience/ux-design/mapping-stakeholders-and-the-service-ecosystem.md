# Stakeholder and ecosystem maps

**Goal:** see everyone and everything that affects or is affected by the service, and how value flows between them.

## Stakeholder map
1. **Brainstorm actors** on sticky notes: users, customer-facing staff, back-office teams, managers, regulators, partners, suppliers, critics. Include non-human actors where they matter (devices, systems).
2. **Group** them in rings: core (direct service users and providers), involved (second-order), peripheral (influencers, regulators).
3. Optionally plot a **power-interest grid:** high power and high interest = manage closely; high power, low interest = keep satisfied; low power, high interest = keep informed; low-low = monitor.
4. Add **relationships:** lines for strong or weak ties, dotted for conflict. Mark who you have spoken to.

## Ecosystem (system) map
1. Put the focal user or service in the centre.
2. Add actors, places, tools and organisations around it.
3. Draw **exchanges** between them as labelled arrows (money, information, goods, permission, emotion). A missing return flow marks a fragile relationship.
4. Highlight bottlenecks, dependencies, and opportunities for new links.

## Uses
- Choose research participants across the map, not just the loudest group.
- Plan engagement and the order of workshops.
- Spot single points of failure and who must be in the room for change.

## Verify
- [ ] Peripheral and non-obvious actors included.
- [ ] Relationships and exchanges drawn and labelled, not just a list.
- [ ] Map used to plan research or engagement, then updated.
