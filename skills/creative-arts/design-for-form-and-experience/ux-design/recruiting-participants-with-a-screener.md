# Participant recruiting and screeners

**Goal:** sessions with people who match the target users, who show up, and who are not
professional testers gaming the screener.

## Criteria
- Behavioural criteria first ("has set up payroll for a company of 10-200 staff in the last 12
  months"), demographics second, attitudes rarely.
- Quotas for important segments (e.g. 3 new admins, 3 experienced).
- Exclusions: employees, competitors, UX/market research professionals.

## Writing the screener
1. Start with exclusion and qualifying questions so unqualified people exit early.
2. Do not reveal the target answer: ask "Which of these tools have you used in the last month?"
   with a list including decoys, not "Do you use Product X?".
3. Use frequency and recency ranges, not yes/no.
4. One open-ended articulation question ("Describe the last time you ...") to check fluency and
   honesty.

## Logistics
- Sources: customer lists, in-product intercepts, or a recruiting agency (NN/g suggests small or
  new teams consider one).
- Incentives scale with the profile: in NN/g's survey high-level professionals were paid several
  times what non-professionals were. NN/g recommends fair incentives partly to limit no-shows.
- Plan for no-shows: NN/g's survey found an average rate of 11% (about one in nine) that varies
  widely, so a five-user test can lose one or two. Recruit spares or have a backup plan.
- Track participants in a panel sheet (contact, segment, last session, consent) with access limited.

## Gotchas
- Screeners that are too broad bring in people who cannot answer the research questions.
- Over-recruiting the same friendly customers biases results.

## Verify
Each scheduled participant meets every must-have criterion with a recorded screener response;
quota segments are filled before sessions start.
