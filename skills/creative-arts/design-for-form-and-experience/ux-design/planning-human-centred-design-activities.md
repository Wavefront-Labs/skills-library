# Planning human-centred design activities

ISO 9241-210 describes human-centred design (HCD) as an iterative process: understand and specify the context of use, specify user requirements, produce design solutions, and evaluate against requirements, repeated until the requirements are met. Planning decides which of these happen, when, and with whom. (The standard is paywalled; this is a paraphrase of its structure, so check the clause text before citing numbers.)

## Steps
1. **Confirm the principles apply:** design based on explicit understanding of users, tasks and environments; users involved throughout; design driven and refined by user-centred evaluation; iterative; addresses the whole user experience; multidisciplinary team.
2. **List the activities** for this project: context-of-use study, requirements, design options, prototypes, evaluations. Size each to the risk: a known domain needs less discovery than a new one.
3. **Set the sequence and iterations:** plan at least two evaluation rounds so findings can change the design; reserve time between rounds.
4. **Assign roles and users:** who runs each activity; how users and stakeholders are recruited and compensated.
5. **Plan resources and links:** budget, tools, labs or remote setup, how HCD outputs feed engineering milestones (for example a requirements baseline before build).
6. **Define the evidence you will keep:** research plan, context-of-use description, requirements, prototypes, evaluation reports.
7. **Review at each milestone** and adjust the plan.

## Output
A one-page HCD plan (activity, purpose, owner, dates, deliverable) attached to the project plan.

## Verify
- [ ] Every activity ties to a decision it informs.
- [ ] At least two evaluation-and-redesign cycles are scheduled.
- [ ] Users are involved before and after design, not only at the end.
