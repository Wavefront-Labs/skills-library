# Research-based personas

**Goal:** personas grounded in user research that help the team decide who they design for, not decorative invented profiles.

## Method (NN/g)
1. Base them on user research: field studies, surveys, longitudinal studies or interviews.
2. Create them early, as part of the research phase, and preferably as a team so members trust and use them.
3. Group observed user attributes into clusters, merge overly similar groups, and drop groups less relevant to the product or business.
4. Add details to each distinct role.

## Profile contents
Common elements: name, age, gender and a photo; a tagline; experience level; usage context; goals and concerns; a representative quote.
- Keep taglines from being too witty, which makes the persona seem unserious.
- Every detail needs a purpose: if it would not affect design or help a decision, remove it. A name and photo are valid because they aid memorability.

## Keep them alive
Personas are living documents: validate and refine them over time, for example by segmenting analytics data for a live product. Other uses include briefing agencies, guiding expert reviews (not a replacement for user testing) and as participant-screening templates.

## Verify
Each persona traces to real research; every detail has a purpose; the team uses them to settle real decisions.
