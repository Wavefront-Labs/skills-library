# Writing unbiased survey questions

**Goal:** every question is understood the same way by every respondent and does not steer the answer.

## Rules
- **One concept per question.** "How easy and fast was checkout?" evaluates two concepts (double-barrelled); ask them separately.
- **Neutral wording.** Wording changes can shift answers a lot. Avoid terms that may be viewed as biased or offensive. To detect wording effects, test two versions on random halves of the sample.
- **Plain language:** simple, concrete wording; avoid double negatives, jargon and unfamiliar abbreviations; consider the audience's education level.
- **Closed-ended lists should be exhaustive.** Randomise the order of unordered options (respondents favour later items by phone, top items when self-administered); keep ordinal scales in their natural order, because the order conveys information.
- **Question order matters:** earlier questions can supply context for later ones (contrast and assimilation effects). Keep the order consistent when tracking trends.
- **Open-ended questions** let people answer in their own words and are useful in pilots to build closed-ended options.

## Process
1. Write the research questions first and delete any survey question that does not feed one.
2. Pretest new questions, for example with cognitive interviews or a small opt-in sample, before the production survey.
