# Card sorting: open and closed

Card sorting asks participants to group content items into categories, revealing their mental models of how content should be organised.

## Types
- **Open sort:** the most common; participants create and name their own groups, giving a view of their mental models.
- **Closed sort:** participants sort into your predefined categories, mainly for validation. NN/g usually recommends tree testing instead.
- **Hybrid:** predefined plus user-created categories. NN/g generally advises against it unless you are confident in one or two categories.
- **Moderated vs unmoderated:** moderated suits qualitative insight because you can ask follow-up questions; unmoderated suits quantitative data collection.

## Prepare
1. Select 30-50 cards; beyond 50, fatigue pushes participants to dump cards into a large "Miscellaneous" group.
2. Recruit representative participants: at least 15 for a qualitative sort, at least 30-50 for a quantitative one (more if consistent patterns do not emerge).
3. Pilot with a smaller group to check the number of cards and that labels are clear.

## Run
- Ask them to group by what makes sense to them and to name groups.
- Add post-sort questions (optional but highly recommended), for example which items were hard to place.

## Analyse
- A **similarity matrix** shows how often two cards were grouped together.
- Read group names for vocabulary.
- Combine with tree testing (see `...ux-design.running-a-tree-test`) to validate the resulting structure.

## Verify
- [ ] Participants were representative users, not stakeholders.
- [ ] Results show agreement patterns, not just a final list.
- [ ] Proposed structure is checked with a tree test.
