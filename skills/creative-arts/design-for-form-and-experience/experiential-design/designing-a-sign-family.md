# Sign family design

A sign family is the set of sign types that together deliver the wayfinding plan with a consistent look.

## Define types by function
- **Identification** (room or destination names, building signs).
- **Directional** (arrows and lists at decision points).
- **Orientation** (maps and directories, you-are-here).
- **Regulatory and safety** (rules, exits, hazards; often governed by codes).
- **Informational** (hours, services) and **interpretive**.
Add sizes (small, medium, large), mounting (wall, projecting, ceiling, freestanding, door) and illumination.

## Design the system
1. **Visual rules:** typeface and sizes, colour system (backgrounds, text, category colours), grid, margins, arrow style, pictogram set, corner radii and edge details (tie to brand and architecture).
2. **Legibility:** large sans-serif with generous spacing, high contrast, a limited number of messages per sign, readable at the intended distance (check with a mock-up at full scale).
3. **Modularity:** standard panel widths and heights, interchangeable inserts, and clear rules for message layout.
4. **Materials and finish:** durable, low-glare, appropriate to environment (indoor, exterior), vandal resistance, maintenance and replacement.
5. **Accessibility:** tactile and braille where required (see `...experiential-design.designing-ada-tactile-and-braille-signs`), contrast, mounting heights.
6. **Mounting and structure:** foundations or fixings, power and data, access for updates.
7. **Prototype** each type at full size, in place, in daylight and at night; review with users and the building owner.
8. **Document** in a sign standards manual: drawings, specifications, message layout templates and approved suppliers.

## Verify
- [ ] Each type has a function, size range and layout template.
- [ ] A full-scale mock-up passed a legibility test.
- [ ] Codes for safety signs were checked.
