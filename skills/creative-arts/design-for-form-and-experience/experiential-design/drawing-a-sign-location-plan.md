# Sign location plan

A location plan shows where each sign goes. Together with the message schedule it is the core of a signage document set.

## Steps
1. **Use current architectural plans** (and reflected ceiling plans) as the base; show levels separately and keep a key plan for large buildings.
2. **Place signs from the decision-point analysis** (see `...experiential-design.analysing-wayfinding-and-decision-points`): at junctions, before turns, at entries and arrival points.
3. **Mark each with its sign ID and type symbol** (matching `...experiential-design.writing-a-sign-message-schedule`), with arrow showing facing direction and single or double sided.
4. **Add elevations or details** for complex locations: mounting height, distance from corners, centring over doors.
5. **Mounting heights and positions:** follow the sign standards and codes. For example, in the 2010 ADA Standards, tactile characters on room signs are located between 48 and 60 inches above the floor (measured to the baseline of the lowest and highest tactile characters), and typically on the latch side of the door; check the current text and local code.
6. **Coordinate:** ceiling heights and grids, light fixtures, sprinklers and alarms, door swings, glazing, structure, power and data; check protruding objects clear route requirements (see `...experiential-design.planning-accessible-routes-and-clearances`).
7. **Check sightlines** on site, in daylight and at night, and from the user's approach.
8. **Walk the site** with a printed plan and tape or stickers to verify positions; update the plan.

## Verify
- [ ] Every sign ID appears exactly once on a plan.
- [ ] Heights meet the code and are dimensioned.
- [ ] Coordination with ceilings, lights and doors was checked.
