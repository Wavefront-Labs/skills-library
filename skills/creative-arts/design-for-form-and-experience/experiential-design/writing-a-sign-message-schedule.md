# Message schedule

A message schedule lists every sign and its exact content, so owners can approve copy and fabricators can build. It pairs with the location plan (see `...experiential-design.drawing-a-sign-location-plan`).

## Columns (spreadsheet)
- **Sign ID** (unique, such as D-1.04, I-2.15; tied to the location plan).
- **Sign type** (from the sign family; see `...experiential-design.designing-a-sign-family`).
- **Location** (building, level, area, grid reference) and **mounting** (wall, door, ceiling, freestanding) and side (single or double).
- **Message copy** exactly as it should appear, by line, with capitalisation, and for bilingual signs the second language line.
- **Arrows and pictograms** (direction and symbol IDs).
- **Tactile and braille** flag and braille text if required.
- **Quantity**, dimensions, material, finish, illumination.
- **Status** (draft, approved, fabricated, installed) and revision, approver and date.
- **Notes** (existing sign to remove, power needed).

## Process
1. Start from the wayfinding plan's decision points and destination list (see `...experiential-design.analysing-wayfinding-and-decision-points`).
2. Write copy to the rules: consistent destination names, a limited number of lines, same order of listing (for instance by importance then alphabetical), arrows aligned with the route.
3. Check that every destination appears at decision points in the right sequence and that room numbers follow the owner's numbering.
4. **Review and sign off** with the owner and operations, and keep version control.
5. Issue with the location plan and shop drawings; after install, field-check against the schedule.

## Verify
- [ ] Every sign on the plan has a row and every row has a location.
- [ ] Destination names are identical everywhere.
- [ ] Owner sign-off is dated.
