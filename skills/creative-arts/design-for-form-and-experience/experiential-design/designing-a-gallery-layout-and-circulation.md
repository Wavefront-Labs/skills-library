# Gallery layout and circulation

Layout turns the interpretive plan into a physical route (see `...experiential-design.writing-an-interpretive-plan-and-big-idea`).

## Circulation types
- **Linear or sequential:** one path, a story told in order; strong narrative, but crowds and skipped sections are a problem.
- **Hub and spoke (or open plan):** visitors choose; good for varied interests, but orientation needs clear signage.
- **Loop with branches:** mixes guidance and choice.
Pick by content: chronology suits linear; thematic content suits open.

## Steps
1. **Place the entry and threshold** with orientation (map, big idea) and a decompression space.
2. **Set the key moments** (a "wow" object, immersive room) and pace them, with quieter areas between.
3. **Plan decision points** and keep sightlines to the next area, to landmarks and to exits (see `...experiential-design.analysing-wayfinding-and-decision-points`).
4. **Provide rest:** seating at intervals, as visitors tire; place seating where it adds viewing, not blocks flow.
5. **Calculate capacity and flow** for peak times (see `...experiential-design.planning-guest-flow-and-capacity`); allow queueing space for popular exhibits.
6. **Accessible routes:** accessible route minimum clear width in the 2010 ADA Standards is 36 inches (with passing spaces where narrower), turning space of 60 inches in diameter, and objects protruding limits; reach ranges; plan more generous clearances in galleries (see `...experiential-design.planning-accessible-routes-and-clearances`). Verify against current standards and local code.
7. **Fire egress** and exits (see `...experiential-design.planning-egress-and-occupant-load`).
8. **Prototype:** tape the plan on the floor or walk a 3D model; observe flow.

## Verify
- [ ] The route supports the story and the choice you intended.
- [ ] Peak flow does not block exits or accessible routes.
- [ ] Rest places exist at intervals.
