# Interpretive plan and big idea

An interpretive plan is the document that links an exhibition's content to what visitors will experience, before design begins. Beverly Serrell's work popularised the big idea as the organising statement of an exhibition.

## Contents
1. **Audience:** who the exhibition is for; what they know, expect and want; time and group types (families, school groups, adults alone). Use visitor research and front-end evaluation.
2. **Big idea:** one clear sentence that states the exhibition's subject, an active message and the consequence or meaning (Serrell recommends keeping it brief; verify specifics in her book). Example: "Everyday objects hold the history of how a city fed itself."
3. **Goals** across knowledge, emotion and behaviour (what visitors should know, feel and do) with measurable outcomes.
4. **Content outline:** primary, secondary and tertiary messages organised in sections; link objects and media to each message (see `...experiential-design.writing-exhibit-labels-and-text-hierarchy`).
5. **Experience strategies:** objects, interactives, media, immersion, programmes; choose by the message and by audience.
6. **Layout intent:** sequence and key moments (see `...experiential-design.designing-a-gallery-layout-and-circulation`), and the visitor journey (see `...experiential-design.designing-an-exhibition-visitor-journey`).
7. **Constraints:** budget, collection care, accessibility, schedule.
8. **Evaluation plan:** front-end, formative (test mock-ups) and summative (after opening).

## Process
Workshops with curators, designers, educators and audience representatives; sign off the big idea before design.

## Verify
- [ ] The big idea fits in one sentence and the team agrees on it.
- [ ] Every exhibit supports a stated message.
- [ ] An evaluation plan exists.
