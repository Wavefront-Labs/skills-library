# Casework and conservation lighting

Work with the object's conservator or registrar; they set requirements. Numbers below are commonly cited starting values, to be confirmed for the collection and the standard in force.

## Case design
- **Materials:** use inert, low-emission materials (sealed or specified boards and adhesives, tested with an Oddy test where required) so off-gassing does not damage objects.
- **Environment:** control relative humidity and temperature as the collection requires; sealed cases with buffering material (such as silica gel) stabilise humidity.
- **Security and safety:** laminated or security glazing, locks, access for installation, and fire and electrical rules.
- **Visibility:** low-reflectance glass, sightlines for seated and standing visitors, no glare from room lights, mounts that hold the object safely and hide supports.
- **Access and dust:** reach for maintenance, and filtered ventilation.

## Lighting
- **Light is cumulative damage** for sensitive materials (textiles, works on paper, dyes, some paints): the damage depends on illuminance, time and spectrum.
- **Commonly cited illuminance limits:** around 50 lux for highly light-sensitive objects and around 200 lux for moderately sensitive ones, per museum lighting guidance (IES RP-30, CIE 157); less sensitive objects can take more. Confirm with the conservator.
- **UV:** remove or filter ultraviolet; a limit around 75 microwatts per lumen has long been cited, and white LEDs emit little UV.
- **Source:** high-quality LEDs with good colour rendering (see `...experiential-design.setting-illuminance-targets-and-colour-quality`); dimmable; low heat in cases (fibre optics or remote drivers if heat is a concern).
- **Aiming:** avoid glare and veiling reflections on glass; accent the object, not the case (see `...experiential-design.aiming-accent-lighting-and-beam-angles`).
- **Track exposure** and rotate objects on a schedule.

## Verify
- [ ] Case materials were checked for emissions.
- [ ] Light levels and UV were measured and agreed with the conservator.
- [ ] No reflections obscure the object from planned viewing positions.
