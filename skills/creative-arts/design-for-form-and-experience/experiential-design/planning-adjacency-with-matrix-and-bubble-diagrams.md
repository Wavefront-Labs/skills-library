# Adjacency matrix, bubble diagrams and block plans

**Goal:** decide how functions relate and fit in the plan before drawing walls.

## Steps
1. **Programme:** list every space with function, headcount, equipment, area target, and special needs (acoustic, daylight, privacy, services). Build an **area schedule** with net areas, circulation factor (typically 15-30 percent) and a gross total versus available area.
2. **Adjacency matrix:** triangular (half-diamond) matrix with every space on both axes. Code each pair: **1 = must be adjacent, 2 = desirable, 3 = neutral, 4 = undesirable (separate)**; add reasons (noise, privacy, flow, shared services). Weight by importance.
3. **Bubble diagram:** circles sized by area, lines for relationships (thick = must be adjacent, thin = desirable, dashed = separated). Iterate topologically ignoring walls; arrange to keep strong links short; resolve clusters (public, semi-public, private; front-of-house, back-of-house).
4. **Block (stacking and blocking) plan:** place scaled blocks of each space on the base plan (or floors in the building). Respect structure (columns, cores), services (wet areas stacked), daylight, entries, and egress. Show circulation spines.
5. **Test-fit** two or three options; evaluate against matrix (score unmet adjacencies), area efficiency (net-to-gross), flexibility, cost; mark the preferred option and why.
6. Move to space plan with furniture, partitions, ADA and egress checks (`creative-arts.design-for-form-and-experience.experiential-design.planning-egress-and-occupant-load`, `creative-arts.design-for-form-and-experience.experiential-design.planning-accessible-routes-and-clearances`).

## Gotchas
- Bubble diagrams that already look like floor plans were designed too early; stay diagrammatic.
- Do not forget unprogrammed spaces: storage, mechanical, toilets, circulation.

## Verify
- [ ] Area schedule reconciles with available area including circulation.
- [ ] Matrix coded with reasons; options scored.
- [ ] Block plan respects structure, services and egress.
