# Boxing the figure in perspective

**Goal:** turn the body's main masses into boxes so their direction in space is
unambiguous, then build the anatomy on top. Loomis' mannequin and Hampton's box
constructions are standard methods.

## Why boxes
A box shows **which way a form faces** (front, side, top planes) and its
**perspective**, which an outline or oval does not. Corners and centre lines give
places to attach limbs and features.

## Steps
1. Draw the gesture (line of action) and decide eye level.
2. **Head box**: a box slightly taller than wide; centre line down the face
   plane, brow line across.
3. **Ribcage box**: wider than deep, tapering to the top; front plane with centre
   line; set its tilt and twist (see ribcage-and-pelvis-masses).
4. **Pelvis box**: shorter, wider; front plane with centre line; hip sockets at
   its lower side corners.
5. Check all three boxes share the same eye level and plausible vanishing
   directions; boxes facing the same way converge to the same VPs.
6. Attach limbs as cylinders or boxes (see limb-construction-with-cylinders).
7. Round off corners into anatomy: chest, abdomen, glutes.

## Practice
- Draw a single pose from 6 imagined camera angles using boxes only.
- Box over photos: trace boxes over figures in photos to train seeing orientation.

## Gotchas
- Boxes drawn without perspective (flat rectangles): they add nothing.
- Making boxes too rigid in the final: they are scaffolding; soften into flesh.
- Inconsistent eye level between boxes makes parts look seen from different heights.

## Verify
- [ ] Each mass's facing is unambiguous.
- [ ] Boxes are consistent in perspective.
- [ ] Final figure keeps the gesture after anatomy is added.
