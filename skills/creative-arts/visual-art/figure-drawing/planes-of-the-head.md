# Planes of the head

**Goal:** see the head as a set of flat planes so each plane gets one value under
a given light. John Asaro's planar head model (and Reilly's earlier plane
abstractions) are the standard study tools.

## The main planes
- **Forehead**: front plane and two side planes (temples), turning at the
  frontal eminences.
- **Brow ridge** and **eye sockets**: the socket planes face down and forward,
  usually darker.
- **Cheek**: front-of-cheek plane (facing forward-down) and the side plane of
  the face under the cheekbone, which turns sharply; the cheekbone is the
  ridge between them.
- **Muzzle**: the barrel-shaped area around the mouth (the dental arch), with
  a front and two side planes.
- **Nose**: bridge, sides, base.
- **Chin and jaw**: chin front plane, under-jaw plane (in shadow under top light),
  the jaw side plane.
- **Side of the head**: a large flat plane from temple to jaw where the ear sits.

## Using it
1. Get a plane head model (resin cast, or a 3D model you can light, such as
   the Asaro head scans many artists use).
2. Light it from one source. Draw it in 3-4 values, one per plane group.
3. Move the light; note which planes change family.
4. When drawing a model, decide for each plane: light family or shadow family, which
   value? Then soften the edges between planes where the form is round.

## Gotchas
- Leaving the planes hard in a finished portrait: they are a stage, not a style
  (unless chosen as a style).
- Over-dividing into tiny planes before the big ones are right.

## Verify
- [ ] Each plane group has a single value at the massing stage.
- [ ] Turning planes (side of face, under-jaw) separate clearly from front planes.
