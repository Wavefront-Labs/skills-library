# Skeletal landmarks on the surface

**Goal:** locate the places where bone lies just under the skin, because they do
not change shape with muscle or fat and give fixed points for measuring and
construction (Goldfinger, *Human Anatomy for Artists*, "subcutaneous bony landmarks").

## Front
| Landmark | What you see |
|----------|--------------|
| Pit of the neck (jugular notch) | Hollow at the top of the sternum between clavicle heads |
| Clavicles | S-shaped ridges to the shoulders |
| Acromion | Bony point at the top of the shoulder |
| Sternum / thoracic arch | Flat strip down the chest; inverted V where ribs part |
| ASIS (anterior superior iliac spines) | Hip-bone points at the front of the pelvis |
| Pubic symphysis | Base of the abdomen |
| Patella | Kneecap, moves with leg extension |
| Tibia (shin) | Sharp ridge down the front of the lower leg |
| Malleoli | Ankle bones: inner higher, outer lower |

## Back
| Landmark | What you see |
|----------|--------------|
| C7 (vertebra prominens) | Bump at the base of the neck, especially with head bent |
| Spine of the scapula and medial border | Ridges of the shoulder blade, change with arm motion |
| Spinous processes | Groove/bumps down the back |
| Iliac crests | Top edge of the pelvis at the waist |
| PSIS / sacral dimples | Two dimples above the buttocks; triangle with the top of the gluteal cleft |

## Limbs
Olecranon (elbow point), medial and lateral epicondyles of the humerus, ulna
ridge along the forearm, styloid processes at the wrist, greater trochanter
on the side of the hip.

## Using landmarks
1. On the model, locate the pit of the neck, C7, iliac crests, and ASIS first.
2. Use them to set ribcage and pelvis tilt (line across ASIS = pelvis tilt;
   line across acromions = shoulder tilt).
3. Draw them as small, sharp shapes; bone gives the crisp accents in soft forms.

## Gotchas
- Missing malleoli asymmetry (inner ankle higher).
- Drawing the scapula as fixed: it slides and rotates with the arm.

## Verify
- [ ] Shoulder and pelvis tilts drawn from acromion and ASIS lines.
- [ ] Bony points rendered with sharper edges than surrounding flesh.
