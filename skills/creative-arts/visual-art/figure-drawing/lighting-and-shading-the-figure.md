# Lighting and shading the figure

**Goal:** a figure that reads as solid and lit, using the same light-shadow
principles as cast drawing but on a living, curving form.

## Lighting the model
- One main light, high and to one side (about 45 degrees), like cast lighting; avoid
  overhead fluorescent lights that flatten forms and put eyes in black.
- A weak fill or a reflector is acceptable for long poses to keep shadows readable.
- Background dark or mid; avoid white walls next to the model that flood shadows.

## Shadow shapes on the figure
1. Squint; find the big terminator running down the body: it links head, torso
   and limbs into one connected shadow shape on the shadow side.
2. Look for cast shadows: under the chin, breast/pectoral, arm on torso, between legs.
3. **Group**: merge small shadows into larger shapes; ignore minor ones in the light.

## Rendering
1. Fill the shadow family with one value.
2. Halftones in the light: skin turns softly, so the terminator is mostly soft,
   firmer over bony areas (knees, shins, elbows, clavicle).
3. Core shadow along the terminator, reflected light in the shadow (often warm
   light from the floor or other body parts).
4. Highlights small: on oily or bony points (forehead, nose, shoulders, knees).
5. Occlusion accents where forms touch (arm against torso, fingers together).

## Gotchas
- Shading each muscle separately: "bag of potatoes". Keep the big light-shadow
  split; muscles are halftone variations within it.
- Hard terminators on soft forms.
- Reflected light brighter than halftones.

## Verify
- [ ] One connected shadow shape on the shadow side when squinting.
- [ ] Muscles subordinate to the big light-shadow design.
- [ ] Edges firmer over bone, softer over flesh.
