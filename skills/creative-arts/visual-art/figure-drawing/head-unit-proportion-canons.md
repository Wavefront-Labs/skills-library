# Head-unit proportion canons

**Goal:** a reliable starting framework for figure proportions, measured in
head heights (chin to top of skull), from Loomis' *Figure Drawing for All It's Worth*.

## Common canons
| Canon | Use |
|-------|-----|
| ~7-7.5 heads | Average adult; realistic figures |
| 8 heads | Idealised fashion/academic figure (Loomis' "ideal") |
| 8.5-9 heads | Heroic (comics, fashion illustration) |
| ~6 heads | Youth around 12 years; ~4 heads at 1 year |

## Eight-head landmarks (standing, front view; Loomis)
1. Top of head to **chin** (1)
2. **Nipples** (2)
3. **Navel** / just above (3)
4. **Crotch** / pubic arch, the body's halfway point (4)
5. Mid-thigh (5)
6. Just below the **knee** (6)
7. Mid-shin / calf (7)
8. **Sole** of the foot (8)
Width: shoulders about 2 heads wide on a male (a little less on a female);
hips about 1.5-2 heads.

## Using it
1. Mark top and bottom of the figure on the page; divide into 8 (or 7.5).
2. Place the landmarks; adjust for the pose (bent legs, foreshortening shorten
   units; always measure along the form).
3. With a live model, **measure the model**, not the canon. The canon is a check
   when a figure looks wrong, and a default when inventing.

## Gotchas
- Applying 8 heads to a real model who is 7 heads: the likeness and gesture die.
- Counting heads on a foreshortened or seated figure as if standing.
- Hands are about the length of the face (chin to hairline); feet about one head
  long; small hands and feet are a common error.

## Verify
- [ ] Crotch near the halfway point of a standing adult.
- [ ] Hand ~ face length, foot ~ one head.
- [ ] Canon overridden by measurements when drawing a real model.
