# Limb construction with cylinders and rhythms

**Goal:** limbs that are solid in 3D and flowing in rhythm.

## Step 1: cylinders
- Upper arm, forearm, thigh, lower leg = tapered cylinders. Draw a centre line
  then ellipses at each end (degree changes as they turn toward/away from you).
- Joints: shoulder and hip as spheres (ball and socket); elbow and knee as
  smaller hinge joints, wider side to side than front to back.
- Forearm: flattens and twists toward the wrist (radius rotates around ulna);
  draw it as a box-like cylinder flattening at the wrist.
- Lower leg: the calf bulges high at the back; the shin is a straight line at the front.

## Step 2: opposing rhythms
- Contours on opposite sides of a limb are **offset**: where the outer contour
  peaks, the inner contour peaks higher or lower, not directly opposite.
  E.g. lower leg: the inner calf (medial gastrocnemius) bulge sits lower than the outer.
  Forearm: the outer mass (brachioradialis, extensors) bulges higher than the inner.
- This alternation creates the rhythmic "S" flow through limbs (Hampton;
  Loomis).

## Step 3: overlaps
- Where one form passes in front of the next at a joint (calf over the back of
  the knee seen from the side, deltoid over the biceps), draw overlapping
  contours with T-junctions.

## Foreshortening note
A limb pointing at the viewer becomes a series of overlapping ellipses; see
foreshortening-the-figure.

## Gotchas
- Parallel contours on both sides: sausage limbs.
- Joints too small: knees and elbows read weak.
- Forgetting the forearm twist when the palm turns down.

## Verify
- [ ] Each segment reads as a cylinder with correct ellipse direction.
- [ ] Opposing contour peaks are offset.
- [ ] Overlaps at joints are drawn as T-junctions.
