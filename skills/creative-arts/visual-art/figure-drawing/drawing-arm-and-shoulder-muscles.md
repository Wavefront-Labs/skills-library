# Drawing arm and shoulder muscles

**Goal:** draw arms that look correct in any rotation, by understanding the
shoulder girdle and the major muscle groups (Goldfinger; Bridgman).

## Shoulder girdle
- Clavicle (front) and scapula (back) form a yoke that **moves** on the ribcage:
  shrugging raises it, reaching forward slides the scapula around the ribs, raising
  the arm above shoulder level rotates the scapula. The shoulder's silhouette
  changes accordingly.
- **Deltoid**: caps the shoulder from clavicle, acromion and scapular spine,
  inserting mid-way down the outer upper arm in a V. Front, side and rear heads.

## Upper arm
- **Biceps brachii** (front): long oval, inserts into the forearm; peaks when the
  elbow flexes with the palm up (supination).
- **Brachialis**: under the biceps, visible on both sides of its lower end.
- **Triceps** (back): horseshoe shape with a flat tendon plate above the elbow;
  makes the back of the arm longer and the long head joins under the deltoid.

## Forearm
- Two main groups: **flexors** (inner side, from the medial epicondyle) and
  **extensors** (outer side, from the lateral epicondyle), plus
  **brachioradialis**, the high outer bulge from upper arm to thumb side of wrist.
- **Pronation/supination**: the radius crosses over the ulna when the palm turns
  down. The forearm's shape twists; the ulna stays as a straight ridge to the
  little-finger side of the wrist.
- Forearm is round near the elbow, flat and box-like near the wrist.

## Drawing approach
1. Place the girdle (clavicle and scapula) for the arm position.
2. Draw the deltoid wrapping over the shoulder ball.
3. Upper arm cylinder with biceps/triceps masses; then forearm with the flexor and
   extensor masses, noting rotation.
4. Use opposing rhythms (see limb-construction-with-cylinders).

## Gotchas
- Shoulder drawn the same height for a raised arm.
- Forearm drawn untwisted while the palm faces down.

## Verify
- [ ] Girdle position matches arm elevation.
- [ ] Forearm twist matches palm direction.
