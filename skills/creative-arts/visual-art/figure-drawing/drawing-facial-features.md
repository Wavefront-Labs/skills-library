# Drawing facial features

**Goal:** features that look three-dimensional and sit on the head, not symbols
pasted on (Loomis; Goldfinger's head chapter).

## Eye
- The eye is a **ball** in a socket; lids wrap over it. The upper lid is thicker
  and casts a shadow onto the eyeball; its highest point is toward the inner side,
  the lower lid's lowest point toward the outer side.
- Iris and pupil are ellipses when not facing you; the upper lid usually covers the top of the iris.
- The white of the eye is **not white**: it is in shadow from the lid and turns away;
  keep it at a halftone.
- Draw the brow ridge and the socket's plane change first.

## Nose
- A wedge with planes: bridge (front), sides, base. Tip (ball), wings (alae) and
  nostrils. Bony upper part (nasal bone) gives a hard edge; cartilage below is softer.
- The base plane is usually in shadow; nostrils are dark accents inside that shape,
  not outlined holes.

## Mouth
- The lips wrap around the cylindrical dental arch, so the mouth curves in 3D;
  in 3/4 view the far half is shorter.
- Upper lip: planes angled down, usually darker; lower lip: angled up, lighter,
  with a shadow beneath. The line between lips is the darkest accent; its corners
  are small dark pits.
- Avoid outlining the lips; define by value and plane changes.

## Ear
- Rim (helix), inner ridge (antihelix), bowl (concha), tragus and lobe.
- Top roughly level with the brow, bottom with the base of the nose; tilts back
  parallel to the jaw line.

## Method
1. Place features with the head construction (see loomis-head-construction).
2. Draw each as planes and shadow shapes, not outlines.
3. Compare relationships between features more than detail within them.

## Gotchas
- Eyes floating on the surface with no socket.
- Outlined lips and nostrils.

## Verify
- [ ] Each feature has a light and shadow side consistent with the light source.
- [ ] Whites of eyes are in halftone.
