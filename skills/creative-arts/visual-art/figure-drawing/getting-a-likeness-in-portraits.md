# Getting a likeness in portraits

**Goal:** a portrait recognisable as the person. Likeness lives mostly in the
**big proportions and shapes**, then in features' spacing, and only last in
the features' details.

## Order
1. **Head shape and tilt**: the overall envelope including hair and the angle of the
   head on the neck. Measure height vs width.
2. **Feature placement**: brow line, eye line, nose base, mouth line, chin.
   Distances between them are highly individual.
3. **Shadow shapes**: the shape of the shadow on the side of the face, under the
   nose and lower lip, and in the eye sockets. These carry likeness strongly.
4. **Features' shapes**: shape of the eyelids, nostril wings, lip line.
5. **Details** last.

## Key checks
- Eye line relative to the whole head.
- Distance from the bottom of the nose to the chin vs the length of the nose.
- Width between the eyes vs eye width.
- Mouth corners relative to pupils (sighted with a vertical).
- Ear position relative to the eye and nose lines.
- Jaw angle and chin width.

## Diagnosing a wrong likeness
- Looks older/younger: feature spacing vertically wrong, or lines and edges too
  hard/soft.
- Looks like a relative: generally close, with one relationship off; measure the
  key checks one by one.
- Looks like you (common): you defaulted to your own features. Use the mirror check.

## With a sitter
- Fix the pose and light; mark positions. Short breaks every 20-25 minutes.
- Talk to the sitter to get a natural expression; draw the expression's resting state.

## Gotchas
- Perfecting one eye before the head shape is right.
- Copying a photo's lens distortion (wide-angle phone selfies enlarge noses);
  use 85 mm+ equivalent photos.

## Verify
- [ ] The two-value shadow pattern alone is recognisable.
- [ ] Key measurements match the sitter.
