# Gesture drawing from the model

**Goal:** record the action, weight and energy of a pose quickly, so longer
figure drawings start from a body that looks alive instead of a stiff assembly of
parts. Gesture is about what the pose is *doing*, not what the body looks like.

## Materials and setup
- Large cheap paper (newsprint, A2/18×24") so you draw from the shoulder, not the wrist.
- Willow charcoal, a soft graphite stick, a brush pen or a marker — something that
  makes bold marks fast.
- Stand at an easel if possible; you'll see the whole figure and paper at once.

## 1. Find the line of action
Before drawing, look for **one line** that runs through the whole pose — usually
from the head, through the spine, to the weight-bearing foot. Draw it first, in one
stroke, exaggerating its curve slightly. A C-curve or S-curve is almost always
better than a straight line; straight reads as stiff.

## 2. Tilts and weight
- Mark the **shoulder line** and the **hip line**. In a standing pose with weight on
  one leg (**contrapposto**), the hip over the weight-bearing leg goes up and the
  shoulders usually tilt the opposite way.
- Find the **centre of gravity**: in a balanced standing pose, a plumb line from the
  pit of the neck falls to the weight-bearing foot (or between the feet if weight is shared).
  If the drawn figure's balance is off, it looks like it's falling.
- Show compression on one side of the torso and stretch on the other.

## 3. Rhythm across the body
Limbs and torso alternate **straight against curve**: a straight side opposite a
curved side, flowing from one form to the next. Look for long sweeping lines that
connect, e.g. from the raised arm through the side of the torso down the leg.

## 4. Session plan (typical life class)
- 10 × **30 seconds** — line of action and tilts only.
- 10 × **1 minute** — add head, ribcage and pelvis as simple masses, limbs as lines.
- 5 × **2 minutes** — add the shapes of limbs, hands and feet as simple forms.
- Then 5- and 10-minute poses, still starting with the gesture.
When drawing from photos, use timed reference tools and choose candid,
action-filled images (posed fashion photography is often stiff).

## 5. Habits that help
- Look at the model more than the paper — roughly 70/30.
- Draw the whole figure in each pose, even if roughly: no unfinished heads with
  no legs. Size the figure on the page first (mark head and feet).
- Use continuous strokes; avoid hairy, scratchy re-stating lines.
- Exaggerate the pose a little; drawings tend to lose movement as they develop.

## Gotchas
- **Starting with the head outline and adding parts**: produces correct-looking
  but lifeless figures. Line of action first.
- **Copying contours** instead of the movement inside them.
- **Symmetry**: drawing both shoulders level and both legs the same out of habit.
- **Foreshortening**: in limbs pointing at you, draw overlapping forms (the thigh
  overlaps the calf) rather than outlines.
- **Life-class etiquette**: never photograph the model without explicit consent;
  follow the studio's rules on breaks and comments. Only the facilitator directs
  the model.

## Verify
- [ ] Each drawing has a clear line of action you can trace in one stroke.
- [ ] Shoulder and hip tilts are visible and correct for the pose.
- [ ] The figure stands (or sits) with believable balance.
- [ ] The whole figure fits on the page in the 1-minute drawings.
- [ ] Comparing a 30-second drawing with the pose, someone else could copy the pose from it.
