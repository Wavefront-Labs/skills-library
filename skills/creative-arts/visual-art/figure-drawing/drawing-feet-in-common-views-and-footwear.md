# Drawing feet in common views and footwear

**Goal:** feet that sit on the ground correctly in the views you meet most, with
or without shoes.

## Views
- **Side (inner)**: arch clear; big toe leads; malleolus high.
- **Side (outer)**: flat outer edge on the ground; small toes visible; malleolus low.
- **Front**: strongly foreshortened; draw as overlapping masses: ankle, the
  slope of the top of the foot, toes as a row of small boxes in an arc. The inner
  ankle is higher; the foot widens toward the toes.
- **Back**: heel block and Achilles tendon dominate; toes hidden or peeking out at sides.
- **Three-quarter**: combine; place the ball and heel contact points first.

## Ground plane
- Both feet must sit on the same ground plane in perspective. Draw a small
  perspective rectangle (a "footprint") under each foot first, consistent with the
  scene's eye level.
- Contact shadow under the outer edge and toes plants the foot.

## Footwear
1. Draw the foot first, lightly.
2. Build the shoe as a shell around it: sole thickness, toe box, heel height.
   High heels raise the heel and steepen the arch; the ball still carries weight.
3. Add construction lines (seams, laces) following the shoe's surface
   (cross-contour).
4. Wrinkles form at the flex point across the ball.

## Gotchas
- Front-view feet drawn as long side-view shapes (no foreshortening).
- Shoes drawn without a foot inside: shapes that cannot hold a foot.
- Feet "floating" without contact shadows.

## Verify
- [ ] Each view shows the correct inner/outer profile.
- [ ] Feet sit on a consistent ground plane.
- [ ] Shoes contain a believable foot.
