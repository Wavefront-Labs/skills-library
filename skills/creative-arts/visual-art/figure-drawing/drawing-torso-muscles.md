# Drawing torso muscles

**Goal:** understand the torso muscles that shape the surface, enough to read and
design them on any model, not to label every fibre (Goldfinger; Bridgman).

## Front
- **Pectoralis major**: fan from clavicle and sternum to the upper arm (humerus),
  twisting under the deltoid. Forms the front wall of the armpit. Its shape
  changes completely as the arm rises (fibres straighten toward the arm).
- **Rectus abdominis**: vertical band from the ribcage (5th-7th costal cartilages)
  to the pubis; three tendinous intersections give the "six pack" above the navel,
  usually one fainter below. Linea alba runs down the middle.
- **External oblique**: from the lower ribs down to the iliac crest; forms the
  "flank pad" bulging over the hip bone.
- **Serratus anterior**: finger-like slips on the side of the ribcage under the
  armpit, interlocking with the external oblique; visible on lean, raised-arm poses.

## Back
- **Trapezius**: diamond from skull base and spine to clavicle and scapula; shapes
  the neck-to-shoulder slope.
- **Latissimus dorsi**: from the lower back and pelvis up and around to the arm;
  forms the back wall of the armpit and the V shape.
- **Erector spinae**: two vertical columns either side of the spine, most visible
  in the lower back.
- **Infraspinatus, teres major/minor, rhomboids**: shape the scapula area.

## Drawing approach
1. Start with ribcage and pelvis masses (see ribcage-and-pelvis-masses).
2. Add muscles as **groups and shapes** that follow their attachments; check the
   direction of pull for the pose.
3. Contracted muscles bulge and shorten; stretched muscles flatten and lengthen.
4. On most models only some forms show; draw what is visible, informed by the rest.

## Gotchas
- Drawing a textbook muscle chart onto every figure: over-defined, plastic look.
- Pectoral drawn as flat plate unaffected by arm position.
- Rectus abdominis with equal-sized boxes; the upper segments are wider and the
  lowest is longest.

## Verify
- [ ] Muscle shapes follow true attachments for the pose.
- [ ] Definition matches the model's actual leanness.
