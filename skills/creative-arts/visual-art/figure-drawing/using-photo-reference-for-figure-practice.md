# Using photo reference for figure practice

**Goal:** get useful figure practice when there is no life class, without
picking up the habits photos teach (copying contours, flattened forms).

## Sources
- **Timed pose sites and apps**: Line of Action, Quickposes, SenshiStock,
  Croquis Cafe (filmed life-model sessions, closer to the real thing),
  Proko and New Masters Academy reference packs.
- **Pose apps / 3D mannequins** (DesignDoll, Magic Poser, Posemaniacs):
  good for odd angles and anatomy, but bodies look generic; use them for
  construction, not for life studies.
- **Your own photos**: shoot friends or yourself with a timer.

## Choose good reference
- Natural light from one main source, clear shadow shapes.
- Full figure in frame, minimal clothing or fitted clothing.
- Candid, action, sport and dance poses over posed fashion images (stiff, retouched).
- Taken with a **normal or longer lens** from a few metres. Wide phone lenses
  close up exaggerate near parts (huge feet, small head).
- Avoid heavily retouched or "liquified" images: their anatomy is wrong.

## Session structure
Mirror a life class: 10 × 30 s, 10 × 1 min, 5 × 2 min, 2 × 5 min, then one 10-20 min.
Set the timer and stop when it ends. For gesture, look for line of action and
tilts; for long poses, construct masses first.

## Avoid photo habits
- Do not trace or grid-copy for practice; it trains copying, not seeing form.
- Draw through the forms (construction) even when the photo hides them.
- Exaggerate the action a little; photos freeze and flatten movement.
- Alternate with life drawing when possible: photos cannot show you depth by
  shifting your head.

## Licensing
Practice studies from any image are fine privately. For finished work you will
show or sell, use reference you shot, licensed stock, or reference packs whose
licence allows derivative artwork. Follow the photographer's terms.

## Verify
- [ ] Reference has one clear light source and a sensible lens distance.
- [ ] Timed sequence used, not open-ended copying.
- [ ] Rights checked before using a photo for a finished piece.
