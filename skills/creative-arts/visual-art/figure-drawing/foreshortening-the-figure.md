# Foreshortening the figure

**Goal:** a limb or body that points toward or away from the viewer reads as
going into space, not as a short, flat stump.

## Why it goes wrong
You know an arm is long, so you draw it long. Foreshortened, it may measure
shorter than the hand at its end. Drawing what you **measure** beats drawing what
you know.

## Measure first
- Sight the visible length with a pencil at arm's length against your unit
  (head height). A thigh pointing at you might be under one head long.
- Measure **widths too**: the near end of a foreshortened form is larger than
  the far end (knee bigger than ankle when the foot points away).
- Use negative shapes around the limb to check the silhouette.

## Build with forms
1. Draw limbs as **cylinders or boxes**. The end facing you becomes a nearly full
   ellipse or square face; ellipses get rounder as the form points more toward you.
2. **Overlap**: the nearer form overlaps the farther one. A foreshortened arm reads
   as hand overlapping forearm overlapping upper arm overlapping shoulder. Draw the
   contour lines as T-junctions, not one continuous outline.
3. **Cross-contours** curve around the form in the direction of the ellipses,
   telling the eye which way the form turns.
4. **Size change**: exaggerate the difference between near and far parts (big
   hand, small head) for dynamic poses.
5. **Tone**: the near end is usually higher in contrast and sharper-edged; the far
   end softer.

## Whole-figure foreshortening
For a reclining figure seen from the feet, box the ribcage and pelvis in
perspective first (see boxing the figure), then hang the limbs on them. The
proportion canon (head units) no longer applies along the depth axis.

## Gotchas
- Outlining a foreshortened limb with two long parallel lines: flat, no depth.
- Continuous contour along the limb: hides the overlaps that create depth.
- Correct measurement but no overlaps or tone: reads as a short limb, not a
  receding one.

## Verify
- [ ] Lengths and widths measured against a unit, not assumed.
- [ ] Overlaps drawn as T-junctions at each joint.
- [ ] Ellipses and cross-contours agree with the direction of the form.
