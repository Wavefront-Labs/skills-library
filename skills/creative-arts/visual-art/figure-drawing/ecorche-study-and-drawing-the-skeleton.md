# Ecorche study and drawing from the skeleton

**Goal:** internalise anatomy by drawing what lies under the skin. An *ecorche*
("flayed") is a figure showing muscles without skin; Houdon's ecorche (1767) is a
classic studio cast.

## 1. Drawing the skeleton
- Use a full-size articulated skeleton (plastic medical models are adequate) or
  good photos. Draw: skull (front, 3/4, profile), ribcage, pelvis, scapula, and
  the limb bones with their joints.
- Draw each part as simplified form first (ribcage as egg, pelvis as bowl), then
  its landmarks. 30-60 minute studies; repeat until you can draw from memory.

## 2. Ecorche cast drawing
- Draw a cast of Houdon's or another ecorche in charcoal like any cast drawing;
  name each muscle you draw on a side sheet.

## 3. Overlay method
1. Take a finished life drawing or a good photo of a pose.
2. Lay tracing paper over it; draw the skeleton in place (ribcage tilt, pelvis
   angle, limb bones), matching the landmarks visible on the surface.
3. On another sheet, overlay the muscles, following attachment points.
4. Compare with an anatomy reference; correct errors.
This builds the link between surface forms and structure.

## 4. Ecorche sculpture
Ateliers often have students model a small (30-60 cm) ecorche in oil clay over an
armature or skeleton model, adding muscles one by one (see sculpture armature skill).

## Gotchas
- Memorising names without drawing forms; the goal is shape and function.
- Skeleton models have rigid joints and generic proportions; check against living models.

## Verify
- [ ] Can draw ribcage, pelvis, skull from memory in three views.
- [ ] Overlay skeleton aligns with surface landmarks on a real pose.
