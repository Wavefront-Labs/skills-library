# Long-pose figure drawing

**Goal:** a finished academic figure drawing over many hours (ateliers run poses
of 20-60+ hours over weeks), without losing accuracy or life.

## Before the first session
- Pose agreed with the model; marked with tape on the stand (feet, hands, props),
  photographed for reference of the pose (not for drawing from).
- Your easel position taped; the light fixed.

## Stages (typical time split)
1. **Gesture and envelope** (session 1): big movement, overall proportions,
   plumb from the pit of the neck.
2. **Block-in** (1-3 sessions): straight-line structure, landmarks, ribcage and
   pelvis, check with measurements and plumb lines.
3. **Shadow shapes** (1 session): terminator and cast shadows mapped.
4. **Massing** (1-2 sessions): shadow average value; big halftones.
5. **Modelling** (most of the time): turning forms, working across the whole
   figure each session.
6. **Finish**: accents, edges, highlights.

## Model drift
- Models settle and drift after breaks (25 minutes posing, 5 rest is common).
  After each break, check the main landmarks against your drawing before drawing more.
- Small drift: wait or ask the model to adjust to the tape marks. Do not chase
  every new position; pick the drawing's version.
- Breathing and fatigue lower the head and shoulders over a session.

## Keeping life
- Restate the gesture lines lightly at the start of each session.
- Compare the whole drawing at distance often; render in passes.

## Gotchas
- Spending all sessions on the head: finish nothing else.
- Chasing the model's changes and ending with a hybrid of poses.

## Verify
- [ ] Pose marked; drift checked after every break.
- [ ] Drawing progressed in whole-figure passes.
- [ ] Gesture still reads in the finished drawing.
