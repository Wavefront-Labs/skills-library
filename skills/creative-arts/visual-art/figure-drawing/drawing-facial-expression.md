# Drawing facial expression

**Goal:** readable, believable expressions built from what facial muscles
actually do (Gary Faigin, *The Artist's Complete Guide to Facial Expression*;
Ekman's basic emotions).

## Expression signals by area
| Emotion | Brows | Eyes | Mouth |
|---------|-------|------|-------|
| Happiness | Relaxed or slightly lowered | Lower lids raised, crow's feet, eyes narrowed | Corners pulled up and back (zygomaticus) |
| Sadness | Inner ends raised and pulled together (oblique) | Upper lids drooping | Corners pulled down; chin may bunch |
| Anger | Lowered and drawn together; vertical furrows | Glaring, lids tense | Lips pressed thin or squared open |
| Fear | Raised and drawn together | Wide open, white above iris | Stretched horizontally |
| Surprise | Raised high, curved; horizontal forehead lines | Wide open | Jaw dropped, relaxed open |
| Disgust | Lowered | Narrowed | Upper lip raised, nose wrinkled |

## Genuine vs posed smile
A genuine (Duchenne) smile involves the eye muscle (orbicularis oculi): cheeks
lift, lower lids bunch, crow's feet appear. A mouth-only smile looks posed.

## Drawing approach
1. Construct the head neutrally.
2. Move the features' masses as the muscles would: cheeks lift as volumes, not
   just lines; the nasolabial fold deepens with a smile.
3. Add creases perpendicular to the muscle pull.
4. Combine for nuance: sadness brows with a smiling mouth reads bittersweet.

## Gotchas
- Drawing only lines (brow tilt) without moving the soft tissue masses.
- Symmetrical expressions everywhere: real ones are often slightly asymmetric.
- Over-exaggeration in portraits; subtle signals carry more in fine art.

## Verify
- [ ] Brows, eyes and mouth agree on one emotion (or a deliberate mix).
- [ ] Soft tissue masses moved, not just lines added.
