# Contrapposto and weight shift

**Goal:** a standing figure that carries its weight convincingly on one leg,
the classical pose from Polykleitos's Doryphoros onward. Most standing life
poses are some form of contrapposto.

## Read the pose before drawing
1. **Which leg bears the weight?** It is the straighter leg, and the foot lies
   under the head: a plumb line from the **pit of the neck** falls to or near the
   inside ankle of the weight-bearing foot.
2. **Hip line**: the pelvis rises on the weight-bearing side (the hip is pushed
   up and out). The free leg's hip drops; its knee bends and the foot often rests
   forward or to the side.
3. **Shoulder line**: usually tilts the **opposite** way to the hips, dropping on
   the weight-bearing side. The angle is smaller than the hip tilt.
4. **Torso**: the side over the weight-bearing leg is **compressed** (curves in,
   folds at the waist); the other side is **stretched** (long, straighter).

## Drawing sequence
1. Line of action, then a plumb line from pit of neck to the ground.
2. Draw the shoulder line and hip line as two converging (not parallel) lines;
   exaggerate slightly.
3. Place the weight-bearing foot on the plumb line first, then the free foot.
4. Draw the ribcage and pelvis masses tilted to match their lines, connected by
   the compressed and stretched sides.
5. The weight-bearing leg: a strong diagonal from the raised hip inward to the
   ankle (femurs angle inward). The free leg relaxes.

## Variations
- Weight shared on both feet: plumb line falls between the feet; tilts are small.
- Leaning on a support (staff, wall): the support takes weight and the plumb line
  falls between support and foot.
- Arm placement often counterbalances: the arm on the free-leg side is often the
  active one in classical poses (chiastic balance).

## Gotchas
- Parallel shoulder and hip lines: the figure looks stiff or tipping.
- Weight-bearing foot not under the neck pit: figure looks like it is falling.
- Both knees locked straight: no weight shift reads.

## Verify
- [ ] Plumb line from the neck pit lands on the weight-bearing foot.
- [ ] Hips and shoulders tilt in opposite directions.
- [ ] One side of the torso compressed, the other stretched.
