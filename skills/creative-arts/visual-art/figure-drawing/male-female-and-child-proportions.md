# Male, female and child proportions

**Goal:** draw convincing figures across sexes and ages, using averages from
Loomis and anatomy texts as starting points, not rules.

## Adult male vs adult female (averages)
| Feature | Male tendency | Female tendency |
|---------|---------------|-----------------|
| Shoulders vs hips | Shoulders clearly wider than hips | Shoulders and hips closer in width; hips can be wider |
| Pelvis | Taller, narrower | Wider, shallower, more forward tilt |
| Waist | Higher, less defined | Defined, often slightly higher relative to the hip |
| Thighs | Straighter line from hip | Thigh angles inward more to the knee (Q angle) |
| Rib cage | Larger, deeper | Smaller |
| Fat distribution | Abdomen | Hips, thighs, buttocks, breasts |
| Neck | Thicker, prominent larynx | Slimmer |
Real people span both columns: use as starting points.

## Children (Loomis' age chart, approximate)
| Age | Height in heads | Notes |
|-----|-----------------|-------|
| 1 year | ~4 | Midpoint near navel; short legs, large head |
| 3 years | ~5 | |
| 6 years | ~5.5-6 | |
| 10-12 years | ~6-7 | Midpoint drops toward crotch |
| 15 years | ~7 | Near adult proportions |
- Children's facial features sit lower on the head: eyes below the halfway line,
  large cranium. Necks are short; limbs soft with little muscle definition.

## Body types
Vary head counts slightly, limb thickness, fat and muscle distribution, posture.
Draw from many models; avoid one generic figure.

## Gotchas
- Drawing children as small adults (adult head-to-body ratio): they look wrong
  instantly.
- Exaggerating sex differences into caricature.

## Verify
- [ ] Shoulder-hip ratio matches the intended figure.
- [ ] Child head ratio and feature placement match the age.
