# Balance and the plumb line in figures

**Goal:** figures that stand convincingly on the ground. Balance errors make an
otherwise accurate figure look as if it is falling.

## The rule
In a standing figure at rest, the centre of gravity (roughly in the pelvis, just in
front of the sacrum) sits over the **base of support** (the area under and between
the feet). A plumb line from the **pit of the neck** (jugular notch, top of the
sternum) in front view falls:
- onto the **inner ankle/foot of the weight-bearing leg** when weight is on one leg
  (contrapposto);
- **between the feet** when weight is equally shared.
In side view the plumb line from the ear passes roughly through shoulder, hip
(greater trochanter), behind the kneecap, and in front of the ankle.

## Steps
1. Before drawing, find the pit of the neck on the model and sight a vertical down
   with a pencil or plumb line. Note what it passes through and where it lands.
2. Draw the same vertical on the paper first, then build the figure around it.
3. Draw the weight-bearing foot directly under its line.
4. For loads (carrying a bag), the body leans away from the load to keep the
   combined centre over the feet.
5. For motion, the centre of gravity leaves the base: running and falling figures
   are deliberately off balance in the direction of travel.

## Gotchas
- Weight leg drawn off to the side of the plumb line: the figure tips.
- Drawing the head first and placing feet wherever they land.
- Seated and leaning figures also need support: check where the weight goes
  (chair, wall, elbow).

## Verify
- [ ] Plumb from pit of neck lands on the support foot or between feet as on the model.
- [ ] Figure reads as stable (or deliberately unstable for motion).
