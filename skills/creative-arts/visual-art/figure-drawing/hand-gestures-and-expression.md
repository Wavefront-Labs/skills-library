# Hand gestures and expression

**Goal:** hands that express emotion and action, not just correct anatomy;
second only to faces in carrying meaning in a figure.

## Gesture first
1. Draw a single **gesture line** from the forearm through the palm to the
   middle finger: it sets the action (relaxed curve, tense straight, reaching).
2. Place the palm mass along it, then fingers as **groups**: often the index
   finger separates, the other three move together, and the thumb opposes.
3. Only then refine joints and nails.

## Common expressive configurations
- **Relaxed**: fingers in a gentle progressive curl, little finger most curled.
- **Tense / clenched**: knuckles prominent, tendons on the back visible.
- **Pointing**: index straight, others curled, thumb along the curled fingers.
- **Open, offering**: palm up, fingers fanned slightly.
- **Holding**: the shape of the object dictates finger wrap; draw the object first
  then wrap the fingers around its volume with consistent contact points.

## Story and design
- Silhouette: make sure the hand's outline reads; separate fingers where their
  shape matters (pointing, counting) and merge them where it does not.
- Hands near the face draw attention; place deliberately in portraits.
- Study hands in master paintings (Durer, Holbein, Sargent) for economy.

## Practice
- Photograph your own hand in 30 expressive poses; draw 1-minute gestures, then
  pick five for 15-minute studies.

## Gotchas
- Spreading all fingers evenly in every pose (looks stiff).
- Holding an object without contact shadows or pressure deformation.

## Verify
- [ ] Hand gesture reads in silhouette.
- [ ] Finger grouping matches the action.
- [ ] Objects held have consistent contact and occlusion.
