# Straights against curves rhythm

**Goal:** contours that feel both structured and alive by opposing a straighter
line on one side of a form with a curved line on the other (taught by Hampton and
Proko for gesture and contour design).

## The principle
- On a bending form, the **stretched** side becomes straighter and tauter; the
  **compressed** side curves and bunches. Exaggerating this difference reads as
  energy and weight.
- A figure drawn all in curves looks soft and boneless; all in straights looks
  stiff and robotic. Alternate.

## Where to use it
- **Torso**: in a side bend, the stretched side of the waist is nearly straight,
  the pinched side curves.
- **Back of the leg vs front**: the shin is a straight; the calf a curve.
- **Arm**: the straight line along the outer forearm against the curve of the
  inner forearm in some poses.
- **Neck**: the straight line from behind the ear to the shoulder vs the curve of the
  throat.

## In gesture
1. Draw the line of action.
2. For each major form, choose: which side is stretched (straight), which side
   compressed (curve)?
3. Place them so the straight on one form leads into the curve of the next,
   forming an alternating chain from head to foot.

## In finished drawing
Use straights for bony, hard areas (shin, skull, wrist) and curves for muscle and
fat, maintaining the design while adding anatomy.

## Gotchas
- Applying the rule against the pose: check which side is truly stretched.
- Over-stylising: subtle in academic work, stronger in illustration.

## Verify
- [ ] Each bending form has a straight stretched side and curved compressed side.
- [ ] Straights and curves alternate along the figure.
