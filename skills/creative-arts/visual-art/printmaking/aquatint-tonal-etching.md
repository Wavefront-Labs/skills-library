# Aquatint (tonal etching)

An aquatint is a fine, even dot ground: the mordant bites tiny pits between the dots, which hold ink and print as tone. Longer bite = darker tone.

## Lay the ground
- **Rosin aquatint**: in an aquatint box, stir up powdered rosin, wait a few seconds for the coarse dust to fall, slide the plate in and let fine dust settle. Aim for about 40-60% coverage - looks like a light frost. Then **fuse** by heating the plate from below (hotplate or torch) until the rosin turns clear/glassy.
- **Spray aquatint** (safer, no rosin dust): fine mist of spray enamel or acrylic airbrush ground from about 50-60 cm, building an even dot pattern; let dry. Commonly used with ferric chloride.
- Wear a dust mask; rosin dust is explosive in quantity and a respiratory irritant.

## Step test
Before the real plate, make a **test strip**: stop out a band, bite, stop out the next band, bite again, until you have a scale of roughly 6-10 steps (e.g. 15 s, 30 s, 1 min, 2 min, 4 min, 8 min... in your bath). Print it - this calibrates your bath and ground.

## Biting values
1. Stop out pure whites (paper areas) first.
2. Bite the lightest tone; rinse, dry, stop out what should stay that tone.
3. Bite again; stop out the next; continue to the darkest.
Like a value map built in steps. Burnish to lighten small areas afterward.

## Variations
- **Sugar lift**: paint with a sugar + ink/gum solution, cover with a thin hard ground, soak in warm water - the sugar swells and lifts, exposing brushmarks; aquatint and bite them. Painterly marks.
- **Spit bite**: brush mordant directly onto an aquatinted plate for soft washes.

## Gotchas
- **Too sparse ground** bites open "foul" areas that won't hold ink and print grey.
- **Too dense ground** prevents biting; tones come out weak.
- Under-fused rosin washes away; over-fused merges into a solid resist.
- Very long bites break down the dot structure - the darkest tone gets lighter.

## Verify
- [ ] Step test printed for this bath and ground.
- [ ] Whites stopped out before the first bite.
- [ ] Dark tones hold ink evenly when wiped.
