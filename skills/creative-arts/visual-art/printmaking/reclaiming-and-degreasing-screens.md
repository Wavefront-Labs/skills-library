# Reclaiming and degreasing screens

## Order
1. **Remove ink** first: scrape excess back into the pot; wash water-based ink out with water and a screen wash; solvent inks need their solvent. Emulsion remover won't work through dried ink.
2. **Emulsion remover** (sodium periodate based): apply both sides with a brush or sprayer, scrub with a soft brush, let it act about a minute - **don't let it dry** on the mesh, which can lock the emulsion in permanently.
3. **Pressure wash** or hose both sides until the mesh is clear; hold the screen to light.
4. **Ghost images / stains**: if a faint image remains and would print, use a haze remover (caustic paste) per instructions, then rinse thoroughly. Light staining that doesn't block mesh is cosmetic.
5. **Degrease**: a dedicated mesh degreaser (not household detergents, which leave residues) scrubbed both sides, rinsed well.
6. Dry in a dust-free place, horizontal or vertical, before coating new emulsion.

## Why degrease
Grease, fingerprints and residues cause **fisheyes** and pinholes in the emulsion and stencil breakdown during printing. Even a new screen should be degreased.

## Care
- Don't scrub with abrasive pads unless the mesh supplier recommends abrading.
- Check mesh tension; slack screens give poor prints.
- PPE: gloves and eye protection; haze removers are caustic.

## Gotchas
- Reclaiming with dried ink in the mesh.
- Emulsion remover left to dry: emulsion becomes permanent.
- Handling the degreased mesh with bare fingers before coating.

## Verify
- [ ] Mesh clear when held to light.
- [ ] Water sheets evenly off the degreased mesh (no beading).
- [ ] Screen fully dry before coating.
