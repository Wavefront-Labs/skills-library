# Reduction linocut printing

One block, many colours: print a colour on the whole edition, carve away what should stay that colour, print the next colour, and so on. The block is destroyed as you go, so the edition is **fixed at the first colour** ("suicide print").

## Plan
1. Make a colour separation sketch: list colours from **lightest to darkest** (each later colour mostly covers earlier ones). Typically 3-8 colours.
2. Decide the edition plus **extras** (at least 20-30% more sheets than the edition) - misprints can't be replaced later.
3. Cut paper all the same size; set up registration (a jig or pin system) before the first print.

## Process
1. Clear the areas that should remain **paper white**. Print colour 1 on every sheet.
2. Clean the block. Carve away everything that should remain colour 1.
3. Print colour 2 over all sheets in exact registration.
4. Repeat: carve what stays colour 2, print colour 3, until the darkest colour (often the key/line work) is printed from what remains.
5. Dry between colours: oil-based inks need hours to a day per layer; water-based dry fast but can cockle the paper.

## Registration
- **Jig**: an L-shaped corner on a board; block sits in a recess, paper butts against the stops each time.
- **Pin registration**: tabs taped to each sheet, pins fixed to the jig (e.g. Ternes-Burton style) - most accurate.
- Mark a test sheet (newsprint) to check each colour's position before the edition.

## Ink
- Transparent extender lets colours mix optically; opaque ink covers cleanly. Keep a record of mixes so a misprint can be matched.
- Thin even ink films; heavy layers stay tacky and later colours won't take ("trapping" problems).

## Gotchas
- Carving something away by mistake can't be undone - mark areas with a coloured wash or marker on the block before each carving stage.
- Too few spare sheets.
- Ink building up on the paper over many layers gives a shiny, slippery surface; keep layers thin.

## Verify
- [ ] Colour order planned light to dark.
- [ ] Edition count and spares printed at colour 1.
- [ ] Registration tested on newsprint at each stage.
