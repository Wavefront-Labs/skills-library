# Drawing on litho stone or plate with tusche and crayon

Lithography works because grease and water repel: the drawing is made in greasy materials, which will later attract ink while the wet, gum-treated stone repels it.

## Prepare the surface
- **Stone** (Bavarian limestone): grain with carborundum grits (e.g. 100 then 180 then 220/240) and a levigator, in figure-eight passes, until the previous image is gone and the surface is level (check with a straightedge). Finer grain = finer, more delicate marks.
- **Aluminium plate**: pre-grained; handle by the edges only - finger grease prints.
- Paint a **gum arabic border** around the image area to keep margins clean.

## Drawing materials (greasiness decreases with number)
- **Litho crayons and pencils** (e.g. Korn's): grades from #00/0 (softest, greasiest) to #5 (hardest). Soft crayons give darks and rich texture; hard crayons for delicate greys.
- **Rubbing ink**: a greasy block rubbed on with a finger or chamois for soft smoky tones.
- **Tusche**: liquid (water-based) or stick tusche dissolved in water or solvent (e.g. lithotine/mineral spirits). Water tusche washes give reticulated, granular tones; solvent washes give smoother, grainier tones. Concentration affects darkness.
- Scratching (with a blade) and erasing (pumice, abrasive stick) can create whites before processing.

## Care while drawing
- Rest your hand on a bridge (a board over the stone) - no skin contact with the surface.
- Avoid saliva, sweat, fingerprints - all are greasy and will print.
- Tusche washes look dark when wet; they print lighter and granular - test on the margin or a test stone.

## Gotchas
- Very thin tusche washes may not hold through processing and printing.
- Grease-free areas must stay clean: dust, talc, cosmetics on hands all print.
- Strong solvent near crayon drawing dissolves and spreads it.

## Verify
- [ ] Surface grained level and clean (or plate untouched).
- [ ] Gum border in place.
- [ ] Image drawn only in greasy materials, no stray contact.
