# Registration systems for multi-colour prints

Registration means each colour lands in the same position on every sheet. Two things must be fixed: where the **printing matrix** sits and where the **paper** sits.

## Systems
| System | How | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Corner jig** | Board with an L-shaped recess for the block and stops for the paper | Relief by hand, reduction prints |
| **Pin and tab** | Punched tabs taped to each sheet; registration pins (e.g. Ternes-Burton style flat pins) fixed to the bed or jig | Relief, monotype, precise multi-block work |
| **Plate template on the press** | Mylar or paper sheet on the press bed marked with the plate outline and paper position; plates placed on the marks; paper edge lined to marks | Multi-plate intaglio |
| **Pinning the paper under the roller** | Paper end held under the press roller between plates (printing the next plate while the sheet stays trapped) | Multi-plate intaglio, colour monotype |
| **Acetate overlay + tabs** | Print on hinged acetate, align paper underneath, set tabs | Screenprint |
| **Kento** | Notches carved in each block | Mokuhanga |
| **Registration marks** | Crosshairs on the matrix outside the image, trimmed later | Litho, screen, checking any system |

## Practice
- **Square, same-size paper** - cut sheets precisely or the paper-edge reference fails. Mark the reference edges (top-left corner) on the back of every sheet.
- Same-size plates or blocks with square corners.
- Acclimatise paper; dampened paper expands - keep dampness consistent through all colours.
- Print a **registration proof** on newsprint or acetate for each new colour.

## Gotchas
- Using different edges of the paper for different colours.
- Paper shrinking between colours (oil inks dried for days on damp paper) - re-dampen equally, or use dry-paper processes.
- Pins that wobble; fix them firmly.

## Verify
- [ ] Reference corner marked on every sheet.
- [ ] Registration proof for each colour.
- [ ] Moisture state of paper consistent across runs.
