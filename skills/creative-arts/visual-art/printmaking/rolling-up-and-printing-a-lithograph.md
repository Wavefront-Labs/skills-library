# Rolling up and printing a lithograph

## Press setup
- A litho press drags a **scraper bar** (leather-covered or polyethylene-edged wooden blade) across a greased **tympan** sheet that sits on the paper over the stone/plate.
- The scraper must be **narrower than the stone** and longer than the image. Set start and stop points so the scraper never runs off the stone edge (it can crack the stone).
- Set pressure by the screw/lever: start firm, test, adjust. Grease the tympan with grease or a lubricant so the scraper glides.

## Rolling up
1. Sponge the stone with water; keep a thin even film - no puddles, no dry patches.
2. Charge the **leather or rubber/composition roller** with litho ink rolled thin on the slab.
3. Roll over the image in several directions with light, quick passes, re-dampening between passes. Speed and pressure matter: fast, light rolling deposits less; slow, heavy deposits more.
4. Bring the image up gradually over several proofs to **full strength**; first proofs are usually weak.

## Printing
1. Fan the stone almost dry (just a faint dampness). Lay the paper (dry or slightly damp per paper) with registration marks, then backing sheets and the tympan.
2. Lower the scraper, pull the bed through under pressure, release.
3. Lift the paper and compare with the proof (BAT, "bon a tirer") - the standard for the whole edition.
4. Re-damp and re-ink for each print; keep rolling count consistent.

## Problems
- **Scumming** (ink in non-image areas): too little water, too much or too soft ink, or under-etched stone. Clean with water and a gum rub, adjust ink stiffness (add magnesium carbonate), re-etch if needed.
- **Filling in**: darks clog and greys merge - too much ink or pressure, under-etched.
- **Weak image**: over-etched, roller undercharged, or ink too stiff.
- Uneven pressure: re-level the stone, check backing sheets.

## Verify
- [ ] Scraper narrower than stone, start/stop set.
- [ ] BAT proof agreed; inking routine consistent.
- [ ] Stone gummed up at end of session.
