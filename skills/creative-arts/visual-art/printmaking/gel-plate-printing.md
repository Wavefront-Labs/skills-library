# Gel plate printing

A gel plate (gelatin, or a reusable synthetic plate like Gelli Arts) is a soft, slightly tacky surface that picks up and releases thin paint by hand pressure - fast monoprinting without a press.

## Basics
1. Squeeze a little **acrylic paint** (or water-based printing ink) on the plate and roll a thin, even layer with a soft rubber brayer.
2. Make marks: draw into the paint with a cotton bud, comb, or the brayer end; press textures (bubble wrap, leaves, lace, stencils) into it and lift them away.
3. Lay paper on top and rub firmly by hand or with a clean brayer; peel.
4. **Ghost print**: pull a second print from the residue; textures and stencils left on the plate print as well.

## Masks and stencils
- Paper masks laid on the paint block it; the area under them prints as paper (or the previous layer's colour).
- Lift the mask and print the "stencil" paint left on it onto another sheet.

## Layering
- Let each print dry (acrylic dries in minutes), then print another colour on top, building complex images.
- Transparent layers (with glazing medium) overprint to make new colours; opaque layers cover.
- Light colours then darks, or a final dark layer with masks for strong shapes.

## Open time
- Acrylic dries fast on the plate in warm rooms; work quickly or add an open/retarder medium (e.g. an open acrylic line or retarder) for longer working time.
- If paint dries on the plate, print it off immediately with a scrap sheet or clean with water.

## Plate care
- Clean with water or baby wipes; avoid sharp tools and solvent.
- Store flat in its cover, away from heat and dust. Small dents and scratches will print.
- Gelatin plates (homemade) are biodegradable but spoil; synthetic plates last years.

## Gotchas
- Too thick paint: blobs and smears, no texture.
- Paper too thick or heavily sized: poor pickup. Smooth medium-weight papers work best.

## Verify
- [ ] Thin even paint film.
- [ ] Ghost print taken after each main print.
- [ ] Plate cleaned and stored flat.
