# Litho gum arabic etch and processing

The "etch" is **gum arabic with a little acid** (nitric for stone; phosphoric/tannic for aluminium plates). It doesn't bite like intaglio; it makes the non-image areas chemically water-receptive and grease-repellent, and stabilises the drawing.

## Sequence (stone)
1. **Rosin and talc**: dust powdered rosin, then talc, over the drawing; brush off excess. Rosin protects the drawing from the acid; talc helps the etch spread.
2. **First etch**: brush the gum etch over the whole stone. Strength is chosen by **how greasy each area is**: delicate greys get a weak etch (few drops of nitric per ounce of gum), heavy darks and tusche get stronger. Apply stronger etch locally where needed. Tamarind publishes etch tables by drops of acid per ounce of gum.
3. Leave on (e.g. several minutes), then **buff** a thin layer down with cheesecloth until dry and tight.
4. Rest the stone - ideally hours or overnight - so the etch acts and the image stabilises.
5. **Washout**: with the gum layer still dry, wash out the drawing with lithotine/solvent - the image disappears but the grease remains on the stone. Then rub in **asphaltum** (a greasy image base) and fan dry.
6. Sponge with water, then **roll up** with a leather or composition roller and stiff black ink until the image returns at full strength.
7. **Second etch**: rosin, talc and etch again (often slightly stronger), buff, rest. This makes the image durable for an edition.

## Aluminium plates
- Use plate-specific etches (e.g. TAPEM or a gum + tannic/phosphoric etch) - stone etches don't suit aluminium.
- Plates are more sensitive and less forgiving of correction.

## Judging the etch
- **Under-etched**: the image fills in, greys darken, scum forms in non-image areas.
- **Over-etched**: delicate greys and fine lines vanish; the image goes weak and won't take ink.
- Corrections (deletions with abrasives, additions after counter-etching) are possible but need re-etching.

## Verify
- [ ] Etch strength matched to drawing areas (note it on a record sheet).
- [ ] Rest period after each etch.
- [ ] Image rolled up to full strength before the second etch.
