# Chine-colle

Thin paper pieces are laid onto the inked plate with adhesive facing up; the press both prints the image and bonds the thin paper to the backing sheet in one pass. Adds colour and texture, or a fine paper surface for detail.

## Materials
- Thin papers: gampi, kozo, Chinese/Japanese tissues, coloured thin papers; usually lighter and thinner than the backing.
- **Adhesive**: wheat starch paste (cooked, sieved), methyl cellulose, or a dry adhesive (pre-coated, e.g. paste brushed on and dried, reactivated by the damp backing paper).
- Backing paper: damp etching paper (e.g. Hahnemuhle Copperplate, Somerset).

## Method
1. Cut or tear the chine-colle pieces to shape. They will print **reversed** in position relative to the plate (plate image is mirrored) - plan with a tracing.
2. Ink and wipe the plate as usual.
3. Place each piece on the inked plate **face down** (good side toward the plate).
4. Brush or sift the adhesive onto the **back** of the pieces (the side facing up), or use pre-pasted dried pieces.
5. Lay the damp backing paper over, blankets down, print with normal pressure.
6. Dry under weight (between blotters and boards) to prevent cockling from the two papers shrinking differently.

## Pre-pasting (cleaner)
- Brush methyl cellulose or wheat paste on the back of the thin paper, let it dry; put it paste-side up on the plate. Moisture from the damp backing sheet reactivates it.

## Gotchas
- Too much paste squeezes out and shows as shiny margins; too little and pieces lift.
- Very thin paper wrinkles when wet - handle with tweezers or a dry brush.
- Grain direction of the two papers different: curling on drying.
- Pieces shifting on the plate: tack with a dot of paste or use the dry method.

## Verify
- [ ] Placement planned for the reversal.
- [ ] Paste on the back (up) side only.
- [ ] Dried flat under weight.
