# Soft-ground etching

Soft ground never fully hardens, so anything pressed into it lifts the ground and exposes the metal. It records soft, grainy pencil-like lines and real textures.

## Lay the ground
- Degreased, bevelled plate (as for hard ground).
- Warm the plate on a hotplate; melt a little soft ground on and roll it out thin and even with a roller. Liquid soft grounds are also available.
- Let it cool; it stays tacky - **don't touch it**, any contact marks.

## Drawn soft ground
1. Lay a thin sheet of paper (tissue, newsprint, or smooth drawing paper) over the plate. Tape at the edges, without the tape touching the image area.
2. Draw on the paper with a pencil or stylus. Where you press, the ground sticks to the back of the paper.
3. Lift the paper - the line on the plate has the grain of the paper and the pressure of your hand. Harder pressure = more exposed metal = darker line.

## Textures
- Lay fabrics, lace, leaves, feathers, paper textures on the ground, cover with tissue and a sheet of newsprint, and run **through the etching press with reduced pressure** (much lighter than printing pressure). Lift the items.
- Combine with hand-pressed marks (fingerprints, sponges).

## Bite
- Bite as for hard ground, but soft-ground marks are shallow and open: bite times are usually short-to-medium; test with a strip. Stop-out to vary depth.
- Soft ground is fragile in the bath - handle the plate carefully and don't brush the surface.

## Clean and proof
- Remove with solvent or the ground maker's remover; proof.
- Soft ground often combines with aquatint for tone.

## Gotchas
- **Accidental marks** from hands, the press blanket, or plate edges.
- Press pressure too high crushes the item and lifts all the ground.
- Overheated ground gets too runny; underheated won't roll thin.

## Verify
- [ ] Ground even and untouched before marking.
- [ ] Texture pass done at reduced press pressure.
- [ ] Bite time tested.
