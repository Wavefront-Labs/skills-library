# Editioning, signing and documenting prints

**Goal:** turn a finished block, plate, stone or screen into a consistent, honestly
documented edition that buyers, galleries and later cataloguers can trust. This
applies across relief, intaglio, lithography and screenprint (for carving and
printing relief blocks, see
handcrafts.hand-printing.relief-block-printing.carving-and-registering-multi-block-relief-prints).

## 1. Proofing to a standard
- **State proofs / trial proofs (T.P.)**: prints pulled while the image is still being
  developed, to see changes. Mark them in pencil as they come off ("1st state").
- When the image and colour are final, pull the **bon à tirer (B.A.T.)** — "good to
  print": the reference impression the edition must match. Sign it B.A.T. and keep it
  beside the press.

## 2. Pulling the edition
- Fix every variable: same paper (and grain direction), same dampening time (intaglio
  and some relief), same ink batch mixed in enough quantity for the whole run, same
  press pressure, same wiping method.
- **Register** each sheet with marks or a jig so placement and margins are identical.
- Compare each print with the B.A.T.; reject impressions that differ (smudges, weak
  ink, misregistration). Expect rejects and print extra sheets — a reject rate of
  10% or more is not unusual for hand-printed work (unconfirmed; varies by process).
- Dry flat between blotters or on racks; interleave with tissue to avoid offset.

## 3. Edition size and categories
Decide the edition size before printing and **never add to it later**. Common
conventions (exact allowances vary by country and studio — note your practice):
- **Edition prints**: numbered *n/total*, e.g. `7/30`. The number is a sequence
  identifier, not necessarily printing order.
- **A.P. / E.A.** (artist's proofs, épreuve d'artiste): kept by the artist; a
  common guideline is up to about 10% of the edition.
- **P.P.** (printer's proof): for the printer, when a separate printer was used.
- **H.C.** (hors commerce, "not for trade"): for exhibition or reference.
- **Unique** impressions (monotypes, deliberately varied editions — "E.V.") are
  numbered as such and described honestly.

## 4. Signing and numbering
Pencil (graphite) in the bottom margin, below the image:
- **Left**: edition number (`7/30`, `A.P. 2/3`).
- **Centre** (optional): title.
- **Right**: signature and often the year.
Use pencil, not ink: graphite is permanent, hard to forge convincingly, and the
established convention. Some printers add an embossed **chop mark** (studio seal).

## 5. Cancelling the matrix
After the edition, **cancel** the block or plate so it can't be reprinted: score a
line across, drill a hole, or deface it, then pull a **cancellation proof** as a
record. Many artists keep the cancelled plate. Screens are reclaimed; lithographic
stones are grained down.

## 6. Documentation
Keep an edition record: title, date, technique, matrix, paper (name, weight), image
and sheet size, edition size, proof counts, printer, cancellation date. Provide a
**certificate of authenticity** or a printed documentation sheet with each sale if
your market expects it. Some jurisdictions have legal disclosure rules for
multiples (unconfirmed for yours — check if selling commercially).

## Gotchas
- **Digital reproductions** (giclée prints of a painting) are not original prints;
  label them as reproductions. Mixing the two misleads buyers.
- Numbering out of order as you sign is fine; numbering beyond the declared total is not.
- Paper that wasn't torn/cut identically gives uneven margins — size all sheets
  before printing.
- Store unsold prints flat, interleaved with acid-free tissue, away from light.

## Verify
- [ ] A signed B.A.T. exists and every edition print matches it.
- [ ] Edition size and proof counts are recorded and match what's signed.
- [ ] Each print is signed and numbered in pencil, in consistent positions.
- [ ] The matrix is cancelled and a cancellation proof kept.
- [ ] Rejected impressions are destroyed or clearly marked, not sold.
