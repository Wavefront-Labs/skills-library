# Hand printing relief with a baren or spoon

## Ink up
1. Squeeze a line of ink on a glass or acrylic slab. Roll the brayer back and forth in several directions until the film is even and makes a fine "velvet" hiss, not a sticky crackle.
2. Roll onto the block in several passes in different directions, recharging the roller. Thin coats - you can always add; too much ink fills fine lines.
3. Wipe ink off the block edges and from clearing areas if they picked up ink.

## Paper
- Thin, strong papers print best by hand: Japanese papers (kozo, Hosho), Somerset Newsprint for proofs, thin printmaking papers. Thick cotton papers need a press or a lot of pressure.
- Lay the paper down **once** onto the block (use registration stops or pins). Don't shift it after contact.

## Burnish
- **Baren** (Japanese disc with a coiled cord or ball bearings) or the back of a **wooden spoon**: work in small overlapping circles with firm pressure, covering every area.
- Lay a sheet of tracing or thin card on top to protect the print paper from tearing and from a shiny burnish mark.
- Hold the paper down with one hand while burnishing with the other.
- Peel back a corner, keeping the rest in place, to check density; burnish more where it is pale.

## Water-based vs oil-based
- Water-based relief inks dry fast on the slab - work quickly in thin layers; retarder helps.
- Oil-based (or water-washable oil) inks give richer blacks and longer open time.

## Gotchas
- **Moving paper** causes a doubled image (ghost): use stops, keep one hand fixed.
- **Ink in the cleared areas** prints as noise; clean the gouged areas or cut them deeper.
- Too little pressure at the edges: corners print pale.

## Verify
- [ ] Even satin ink film on the block.
- [ ] Corner peeled and checked before full lift.
- [ ] No slip or double image.
