# Trace monotype

A drawing method (used by Gauguin and Tracey Emin): pressure from drawing on the back of the paper picks up ink from an inked slab, giving a soft, irregular, printed line.

## Setup
- Smooth slab: glass, acrylic, or a smooth plate.
- Ink: oil-based or water-washable oil relief/litho ink rolled into a **very thin**, even film. Too much ink and the whole sheet picks up a dirty tone. Some artists let the film sit a few minutes so it tacks up slightly.
- Paper: thin to medium smooth paper (newsprint for tests, thin Japanese papers, smooth cartridge).

## Process
1. Roll the ink film with a brayer.
2. Lay the paper gently on the ink without pressing.
3. Place your drawing or a photocopy on top as a guide if wanted (tape at the edge).
4. Draw firmly on the back of the paper with a pencil, ballpoint, stick or stylus. Rest your hand on a **bridge** or a sheet of card so it doesn't press tone into the paper.
5. For tone: rub with fingertips, the side of a pencil, or a soft roller over areas.
6. Peel the paper back carefully - the drawn lines are in ink on the underside, which becomes the front of the print. It is a **mirror image** of the drawing as you saw it on the back, so reverse text and any guide drawing beforehand.

## Variations
- Ink part of the slab in different colours for colour changes.
- Mask areas with paper to keep them clean.
- Combine with a painted monotype layer or second trace pass when dry.

## Gotchas
- **Smudgy, toned background**: ink film too thick or hand pressure; thin the film, use a bridge.
- **Faint lines**: ink too dry or too thin; re-roll.
- Lifting the paper to check loses registration - lift only a corner.

## Verify
- [ ] Test sheet on newsprint before the good paper.
- [ ] Hand never rests directly on the paper.
- [ ] Corner-lift check before peeling.
