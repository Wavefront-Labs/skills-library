# Cutting lino and wood blocks

**Goal:** a cleanly cut relief block that prints crisp lines and solid areas
(Grabowski and Fick, *Printmaking*, relief chapter).

## Blocks
- **Lino**: traditional battleship-grey hessian-backed lino (cuts crisply; warm it
  to soften), softer vinyl "easy-cut" blocks (beginners, less crisp).
- **Wood**: plank wood (cherry, pear, shina/basswood/lime ply) cut along the grain
  for woodcut; end-grain boxwood for wood engraving (gravers, not gouges).

## Transferring the image
- The print is a **mirror** of the block. Reverse the design: draw on tracing
  paper, flip and transfer with carbon/graphite, or photocopy transfer.
- Ink in what stays (prints black) with a marker; anything cut away prints white.
- Tone the block lightly (thin ink wash) to see cut marks clearly.

## Tools
- **V-gouge**: lines, outlines, fine detail.
- **U-gouge** (small and large): textures, clearing.
- **Knife**: wood outlines (cut at an angle on both sides of a line).
- Good tools (Pfeil, Flexcut) stay sharp; strop often.

## Cutting
1. Use a **bench hook** to hold the block; **always cut away from your other hand**.
2. Hold the gouge low (about 15-30 degrees); push with the palm, guide with fingers.
3. Turn the block, not your wrist, for curves.
4. Cut lines with **sloped shoulders** (wider at the base) so fine lines are supported.
5. Clear large white areas deeply enough not to pick up ink (2-3 mm on lino), and
   leave some ridges in big open areas only if you want texture.
6. Proof often (see hand-printing) and recut.

## Gotchas
- Undercutting fine lines: they break off.
- Cold lino chips; warm it on a radiator or hotplate briefly.
- Blunt tools slip: the main cause of cuts. Strop and sharpen.

## Verify
- [ ] Image reversed on the block.
- [ ] Lines have supported shoulders; cleared areas deep enough.
- [ ] Bench hook used; no cutting toward the hand.
