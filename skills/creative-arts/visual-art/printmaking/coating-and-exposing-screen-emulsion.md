# Coating and exposing screen emulsion

**Goal:** a sharp, durable photo stencil on a screen (Grabowski and Fick,
screenprint chapter; Speedball/Ryonet emulsion guides).

## Mesh
- Mesh count (threads per inch): about 110-160 for textiles and heavy ink, 160-230
  for paper prints and fine detail. Higher counts hold detail but pass less ink.

## Steps
1. **Degrease**: scrub both sides with screen degreaser; rinse; dry in a dust-free
   place. Oils cause pinholes and emulsion failure.
2. **Coat** (in safe light, e.g. yellow bulbs): pour emulsion (diazo or
   photopolymer, sensitised per instructions) into a scoop coater; hold the screen
   tilted; draw the coater up the print side with steady pressure, then the squeegee
   side. One coat each side is typical; extra coats on the print side for thicker
   stencils.
3. **Dry** flat, print side down, in the dark (fan helps), until completely dry.
4. **Expose**: place the film positive (opaque black on clear) **right-reading**
   on the print side in contact (glass or vacuum), expose to UV. Exposure time
   depends on light source, distance, emulsion and mesh.
5. **Find the time**: do a step test (expose strips for increasing times by
   covering them progressively, or use an exposure calculator film); choose the
   shortest time that gives hard, non-slimy emulsion with crisp detail.
6. **Wash out**: wet both sides with cool water, then spray the print side until
   the image areas clear; dry.
7. **Check**: hold to light; block pinholes with screen filler or tape; optional
   post-exposure in sunlight for durability.

## Gotchas
- Underexposure: emulsion washes off or breaks down during printing.
- Overexposure: fine details do not wash out.
- Positives not dense enough: light leaks; double up transparencies or print darker.
- Emulsion stored warm or old: unreliable.

## Verify
- [ ] Step-test chosen exposure time recorded.
- [ ] Image areas fully open, emulsion hard.
- [ ] Pinholes blocked.
