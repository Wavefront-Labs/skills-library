# Aluminium and polyester plate lithography

## Aluminium plates
- Pre-grained aluminium plates (ball-grained or brush-grained) take the same crayons, pencils and tusche as stone, but are lighter, cheaper and fit in any litho or offset press.
- **Handle by the edges**; skin oils print. Keep plates in interleaving paper.
- Processing: plate-specific gum etches (e.g. gum with tannic or phosphoric acid, or commercial plate etches) - **not** the nitric/gum etches used for stone, which attack aluminium.
- Washout with solvent, then a plate image base (asphaltum or lacquer base); roll up; second etch.
- Corrections are harder than on stone; deletions use abrasive erasers and counter-etch.
- **Presensitised (photo) aluminium plates** expose through a film positive in a UV unit and develop chemically.

## Polyester plates (e.g. Pronto plate)
- A coated polyester film with a water-receptive surface. Image the plate with:
  - **Laser printer or photocopier** (toner is the greasy image): print digital drawings or photos directly. Check the printer's heat and toner compatibility; some modern printers need the plate's recommended settings.
  - **Hand drawing** with permanent markers, ballpoint, litho crayon, special tusche.
- **No etching**: wipe with a fountain solution (plate maker's or water + a little gum arabic), keep damp, roll up with litho ink and print. Ideal for teaching and quick editions.
- Can print on an etching press with a smooth backing, or on a litho press; even by hand with a baren for small sheets.

## Limits
- Polyester plates wear after tens of prints and can scum if allowed to dry while inked; edition sizes are modest.
- Toner density limits fine tone; adjust printer darkness.
- Aluminium plates can oxidise; store gummed.

## Gotchas
- Plate drying out while inked: scum sticks permanently.
- Too much water: ink won't take; too little: background scums. Keep a thin even film.

## Verify
- [ ] Plate type matched to the right etch or fountain solution.
- [ ] Laser test print on a scrap plate before imaging the good one.
- [ ] Plate never left inked and dry.
