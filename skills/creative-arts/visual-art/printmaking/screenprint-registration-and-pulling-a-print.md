# Screenprint registration and pulling a print

## Bed setup
- Screen in **hinge clamps** on a flat table or vacuum bed. Set **off-contact**: the mesh sits about 2-5 mm above the paper, touching only under the squeegee, so it snaps away cleanly.
- Tape off the open mesh around the stencil.

## Register each colour
1. Tape a sheet of **acetate** (clear film) hinged along one edge of the bed.
2. Print the new colour onto the acetate.
3. Slide a print with previous colours (or the first sheet) under the acetate and move it until the images align.
4. Hold it there, lift the acetate, and fix **registration tabs** (card or tape stops) against two sides of the paper - typically two on the long edge and one on a short edge (three-point registration).
5. Every sheet then goes against the same tabs. Check the first print against the key.

## Pull
1. **Flood stroke**: with the screen lifted slightly, push ink across the stencil lightly to fill the mesh without printing.
2. Lower the screen; **print stroke**: squeegee at about 60-75 degrees, firm even pressure, one steady pull. Pressure enough to clear the mesh, not to squash ink under the stencil.
3. Lift, remove the print to a drying rack, flood again immediately so ink doesn't dry in the mesh.

## Squeegee
- Blade width at least 2-3 cm wider than the image each side.
- Durometer: 70 (medium) is the general choice; softer deposits more ink, harder gives crisper detail.
- Keep the edge sharp and clean.

## Drying and ink
- Water-based screen inks dry fast in the mesh: flood after every pull, use retarder, work in a cool humid room, and clean promptly when pausing.
- Dry prints in a rack; most water-based inks touch-dry in minutes.

## Gotchas
- No off-contact: smudged, blurred prints; paper sticks to the screen.
- Too much pressure or a rounded squeegee edge: ink bleeds under.
- Paper not against all stops: misregistration.

## Verify
- [ ] Off-contact set; tape around stencil.
- [ ] Acetate registration and three tabs for each colour.
- [ ] Flood after every print.
