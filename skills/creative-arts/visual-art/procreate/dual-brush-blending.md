# Dual brushes in Procreate

**Goal:** build richer marks by combining a primary brush with a second brush that modulates it.

Availability and exact control names depend on your Procreate version; check the Handbook's Brush Studio section for the current names, and note that this skill does not confirm which release introduced the feature.

## Steps
1. Duplicate the brush you want to modify (swipe left > Duplicate), then open it in Brush Studio.
2. Find the **Dual Brush** section (or tab) and choose a second brush from your library to act as the secondary.
3. Choose how the two combine using the blend mode offered there, and adjust the secondary brush's size, spacing and scatter so its texture reads through the primary stroke.
4. Test in the drawing pad. Try a soft round primary with a rough grain-like secondary for chalk, or a hard bristle secondary for dry brush.
5. Save, name it clearly (for example "Dry bristle - dual") and test on a layer with a few strokes at different pressures.

## Gotchas
- Both brushes stack their own settings (spacing, jitter, dynamics), so results can get noisy; change one parameter at a time.
- Dual brushes cost performance; very large sizes on big canvases lag.
- Sharing a dual brush as a `.brush` file should carry the secondary with it, but confirm by re-importing on another device.

## Verify
- [ ] The combined stroke has the texture of the secondary and the form of the primary.
- [ ] Performance remains acceptable at your normal canvas size.
