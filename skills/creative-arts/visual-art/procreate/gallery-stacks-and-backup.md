# Gallery, stacks and backup in Procreate

**Goal:** keep artwork findable and recoverable. Procreate stores artworks in the app on the device; it does not autosave to a cloud account on its own, so backup is your job.

## Organising
- **Stacks:** drag one artwork onto another to create a stack; drag more in; rename by tapping the stack title. Stacks can be opened and reordered like folders.
- **Select:** tap Select, tick artworks, then Stack, Preview, Share, Duplicate or Delete.
- **Swipe left** on an artwork thumbnail for Share, Duplicate and Delete.
- Rename an artwork by tapping its title text below the thumbnail.
- **Import** brings in .procreate, PSD, JPEG, PNG, TIFF and other supported files from the Files app; **Photo** brings in from the Photos library.

## Backup
1. **Share > Procreate** exports the editable .procreate file (full layers and history thumbnails). Save to Files, iCloud Drive, a USB drive or a computer.
2. Select several artworks and share them together to produce a bundle export; check that the destination reports the right file sizes, as large files can fail silently on low storage.
3. Keep a second export format for safety: a flattened PNG or layered PSD (see `creative-arts.visual-art.procreate.exporting-psd-and-layered-files-from-procreate`).
4. Whole-device backups (iCloud or a computer) usually include app data, but treat that as unconfirmed for your setup until you have restored from one; an explicit .procreate export is the reliable copy.
5. Routine: export finished or milestone work, not only finals, and name files with date and version.

## Gotchas
- Deleting an artwork in the Gallery is permanent in the app; there may be no recycle bin.
- Duplicate before risky edits (a flatten, a large canvas resize).
- Low device storage causes failed saves and slow brushes; keep several GB free.

## Verify
- [ ] An exported .procreate file imports into a clean Procreate install with all layers.
- [ ] Backup copy exists in a second location outside the iPad.
