# Encaustic painting

**Goal:** paint with molten pigmented wax. Encaustic medium is traditionally beeswax with damar resin (commonly in a ratio somewhere near 8 or 9 parts wax to 1 part resin by weight; recipes differ). Pre-made medium and paint blocks are sold.

## Safety first
- Heating wax creates fumes; overheating produces irritating and harmful smoke. Keep temperatures **low and thermostat-controlled**, never leave heaters unattended, use good ventilation (and local extraction if possible), and keep a suitable fire extinguisher nearby.
- Do not breathe pigment dust; wear gloves, and be aware that some pigments contain toxic metals (such as cadmium or cobalt). Check the safety data sheet. Never use kitchen equipment later for food.

## Equipment
- A heated palette (griddle) with a thermostat and a thermometer; natural-hair or heat-resistant brushes; a heat gun or small torch for fusing (take care); palette knives; metal pans and a work surface that tolerates wax.

## Support
- Use a **rigid, absorbent** panel (birch or other wood board, cradled; clay-coated panels), not a flexible canvas. Prime with a traditional gesso or encaustic ground that leaves the surface absorbent.

## Process
1. Melt medium and pigmented paint on the palette (commonly kept around 200 to 220 degrees Fahrenheit, about 93 to 104 degrees Celsius; follow your paint maker).
2. Brush layers of molten wax quickly onto the warm panel.
3. **Fuse** each layer with a heat gun or torch so it bonds with the one below; this is essential to prevent delamination.
4. Scrape, incise and inlay between layers; add oil sticks or pigment into fused wax for lines.
5. Let cool and cure; polish with a soft cloth for a sheen. No varnish is needed.

## Gotchas
- Skipped fusing results in peeling layers.
- Wax can crack in cold; do not store in extreme temperatures.
- Do not use cold wax as a substitute; it is a different medium (see `creative-arts.visual-art.collage-and-mixed-media.cold-wax-medium-with-oil-paint`).

## Verify
- [ ] Layers are fused and cannot be peeled.
- [ ] Ventilation and temperature controls are in place before heating.
