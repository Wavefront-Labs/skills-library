# Image transfers: gel medium, solvent and packing tape

**Goal:** move a printed image onto paper, board or canvas. Mirror your image first, because the printed face goes against the surface and reads backwards otherwise.

## Gel medium transfer
- Works best with **laser or photocopier prints** (toner); many inkjet inks smear or dissolve, so test first. Magazine pages vary.
1. Brush an even coat of acrylic gel medium (soft or regular, gloss or matte) over the image side and onto the surface.
2. Place image face down, burnish firmly with a bone folder or spoon from the centre out, and let dry fully (hours to overnight).
3. Dampen the back with a sponge, wait briefly, then rub gently with wet fingers to remove paper fibre, leaving the image in the gel. Let dry; repeat the rubbing until clean.
4. Seal with another gel layer.

## Packing-tape transfer
1. Stick clear packing tape over a toner print or magazine clipping, smooth and burnish.
2. Soak in water for several minutes and rub off the paper from the back; the ink stays on the tape, giving a translucent image.
3. Press the tape onto the surface or seal into a layered piece.
- Results are translucent and show the surface through them; the tape's adhesive ages, so it is not archival.

## Solvent transfer
- Citrus-based solvents (d-limonene products, such as Citra Solv) can release toner or some magazine inks: lay the print face down, brush the solvent on the back, burnish firmly with a spoon, and peel.
- **Ventilation and safety:** solvents are flammable, irritate skin and release fumes. Work with good ventilation, wear nitrile gloves, keep away from flames, read the safety data sheet, and dispose of rags safely. Avoid harsher solvents such as xylene; they are more hazardous.

## Gotchas
- Not every ink transfers; test every source. Thick coated paper leaves a ghost of its coating.
- Uneven pressure leaves gaps in the image; burnish evenly.
- Transferred images are fragile until sealed; protect with medium or varnish.

## Verify
- [ ] Text reads correctly (after mirroring the original).
- [ ] The paper fibre is fully removed and the image sealed.
