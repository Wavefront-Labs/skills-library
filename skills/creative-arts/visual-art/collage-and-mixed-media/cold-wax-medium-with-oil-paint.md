# Cold wax medium with oil paint

**Goal:** build matte, buttery, scrapable layers by mixing oil paint with cold wax medium. Cold wax medium is a wax paste (beeswax in solvent, sometimes with resin) that is mixed with oil paint at room temperature; it is not heated like encaustic.

## Safety
- Most cold wax media contain mineral spirits or similar solvents. Use good ventilation, avoid open flames, wear nitrile gloves, read the safety data sheet, and dispose of solvent-soaked rags safely (oily rags can self-heat; lay them flat to dry outdoors or store in a closed metal container with water).

## Support
- Use a **rigid, absorbent** surface: wood panel with a thin ground, or sealed heavy paper mounted on board. Avoid unprimed canvas.

## Process
1. Mix oil paint with medium on a palette to the consistency you want; ratios range from mostly paint to several parts medium, and more wax gives a more translucent, matte mix. Manufacturer instructions vary; test.
2. Apply with a palette knife, brush, squeegee or brayer, then **scrape back** with a squeegee, card or knife to reveal underlayers; texture comes from tool marks and incision.
3. Layer thinly and allow layers to set between sessions; the wax sets but oil beneath dries slowly (days to weeks per layer to touch).
4. After weeks to months, once the surface is dry to the touch and firm, **buff** with a soft lint-free cloth for a soft sheen. No varnish is needed, and varnishes are not recommended over wax.

## Gotchas
- Adding more than the recommended proportion of wax makes soft, cheesy films that mark easily.
- Do not put acrylic over cold wax (see `creative-arts.visual-art.collage-and-mixed-media.layering-media-compatibly`).
- Works can show fingerprints and fade shine if handled; frame under glass or with a spacer.

## Verify
- [ ] The finished surface is firm and not tacky before buffing.
- [ ] Studio ventilation and rag disposal are sorted before you mix.
