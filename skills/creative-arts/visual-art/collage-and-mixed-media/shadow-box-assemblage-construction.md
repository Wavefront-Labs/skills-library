# Shadow box assemblage construction

**Goal:** a glazed box containing arranged objects and images that reads as one
composed work, after Joseph Cornell's boxes (MoMA, Art Institute of Chicago
collections).

## The box
- Buy a deep frame/shadow box, or build one: 9-12 mm hardwood or birch ply, butt or
  rebated joints glued and pinned; a rebate at the front for glazing.
- Inside depth: at least the deepest object plus 1-2 cm clearance from the glass.
- Seal raw wood (acrylic medium, shellac) to reduce acids reaching paper contents.

## Composition
1. Lay out objects on a table within the box's footprint; arrange in **depth**:
   back panel image, middle-ground objects, front elements.
2. Compartments (thin wood dividers), shelves, rods and rings create a theatre-like
   structure.
3. Design for the frontal view; check in the lighting it will be displayed in.
4. Use repetition and a limited palette to unify disparate objects.

## Mounting
- Light items: archival adhesives (PVA, acrylic gel) on clean surfaces.
- Heavier objects: mechanical fixing: small screws, wire through drilled holes,
  brass pins, clear monofilament ties, brackets hidden behind objects
  (see attaching-heavy-and-odd-objects).
- Keep paper items off direct contact with wood: mount on acid-free board.

## Finishing
- Line the back with paper, fabric or painted panel.
- Glazing: UV-filtering acrylic (lighter, safer) or glass; seal the box edges with
  framer's tape to keep out dust and insects.
- Hanging: D-rings and wire rated for the weight, screwed into the box sides.

## Gotchas
- Objects shifting in transit: test by turning the finished box on each side before glazing.
- Glue blobs visible; use small amounts, mechanical fixing for weight.
- Rusting metal objects staining neighbours: seal with microcrystalline wax or lacquer.

## Verify
- [ ] Everything stays in place with the box turned on each side.
- [ ] Clearance from the glazing; box sealed.
- [ ] Hanging hardware rated for weight.
