# Archival adhesion for paper collage

**Goal:** a collage whose pieces stay stuck, flat and unfaded for years — rather than
lifting, bubbling, yellowing or rippling the support.

## 1. Choose the support
- **Rigid** supports resist warping: cradled wood panel (sealed/primed first, as wood
  acids migrate), museum/mount board, heavy (300 gsm+) cotton paper.
- Thin paper or canvas supports cockle (ripple) when wet glue goes on; if you must
  use them, glue the back with a matching layer or work with very little moisture.

## 2. Choose the adhesive
| Adhesive | Use | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Acrylic gel/matte medium** | General collage, mixed media, acrylic painting over | Flexible, clear, archival grade available; can be brushed over as a sealer too |
| **PVA (archival, pH-neutral)** | Board and heavier papers | Strong and fast; ordinary craft PVA may yellow — buy a conservation grade |
| **Wheat starch paste / methyl cellulose** | Delicate papers, reversible work | Reversible with water (conservation-friendly), slower, more moisture |
| **Glue sticks, spray mount, rubber cement** | Sketchbook/maquettes only | Most fail or stain over time |
"Archival" labels aren't a regulated term everywhere — look for pH-neutral and
stated longevity. Unconfirmed for individual brands.

## 3. Laying pieces down without wrinkles or bubbles
1. **Pre-plan**: lay out pieces dry and photograph the layout.
2. Apply adhesive thinly and evenly to **the support** for thin papers (paper
   expands when wetted directly) and to **the back of the piece** for thick ones.
3. Place, cover with a sheet of baking/silicone release paper, and roll from the
   **centre outward** with a soft rubber **brayer** to push out air and excess glue.
4. Wipe squeeze-out immediately with a damp cloth.
5. Dry **under weight** (boards and books on top of release paper) — several hours
   or overnight.
For very thin or printed papers (magazine, tissue), pre-coat both sides with
gel medium and let dry first; it stops ink bleeding and reduces stretch.

## 4. Found papers and permanence
- **Newsprint and most magazines are acidic** and yellow/fade quickly. Use them knowingly,
  seal them with medium, or make a high-quality scan and print on archival paper
  instead.
- Inkjet prints can bleed when wet adhesive goes over them — test, or seal with a
  spray fixative first.
- Lay a final coat of medium or a **UV-protective varnish** suited to acrylic over
  the finished surface (for non-reversible collage). Frame behind UV-filtering
  glazing for display.

## 5. Mixed-media layering
Work **flexible over rigid** and **water-based under oil**, never oil-based paint
under acrylic layers. Heavy objects (found objects, fabric with weight) need
mechanical fixing (stitching, small screws, epoxy) — medium alone won't hold them
long-term.

## 6. Copyright on source images
Using recognisable copyrighted photographs or illustrations can raise legal issues,
especially for commercial work. "Transformative" use is judged case by case, and the
rules vary by country (fair use, fair dealing). Safer choices: your own photographs,
public domain material (check dates and the source's rights statement), licensed
images, or material altered beyond recognition. Unconfirmed legal advice — consult
local guidance for commercial work.

## Gotchas
- Too much glue: causes wrinkles, slow drying and shiny patches.
- Rolling from one edge: traps air in the middle.
- Unsealed wood panels: brown stains (support-induced discoloration) come through
  light papers.
- Double-sided printed pages: the back image shows through when wet.

## Verify
- [ ] Support is rigid or backed so it stays flat when dry.
- [ ] Adhesive is pH-neutral and matched to the paper weight.
- [ ] No bubbles or lifting edges after drying under weight.
- [ ] Acidic found papers are sealed or substituted.
- [ ] Source images are your own, public domain or licensed (for commercial use).
