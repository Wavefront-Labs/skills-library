# Texture pastes and modelling paste

**Goal:** add physical relief under or within a painting or collage. Product names vary; Golden, Liquitex and others each make light, regular and heavy molding or modelling pastes, plus fibre and coarse pastes. Follow the maker's technical sheet for thickness and drying.

## Choosing
- **Light molding paste:** very lightweight, flexible, can be carved or sanded when dry, absorbent. Good for deep relief with low weight.
- **Heavy or regular modelling paste:** dense, strong, holds sharp edges and sands smooth.
- **Coarse or fibre pastes:** visible grit or fibre for rough surfaces.
- **Gels** (heavy, extra heavy) are translucent, useful for embedding items.

## Use
1. Use a **rigid support** (wood panel, board) for thick work. Canvas can flex and crack heavy paste.
2. Stir gently to avoid bubbles; apply with a palette knife, spatula or brush. Build in layers and let each layer dry fully before adding more; very thick single layers can crack or stay wet inside. Follow the label for maximum layer thickness.
3. Press stencils, fabrics or objects into wet paste, or carve and score when leather-hard.
4. Allow to dry fully (often a day or more for thick areas).
5. Pastes dry absorbent and matte; seal with gel or paint over with acrylics, or stain. Oil paint can be used over fully cured acrylic paste on a sealed surface, but not the other way around (see `creative-arts.visual-art.collage-and-mixed-media.layering-media-compatibly`).

## Gotchas
- Shrinkage and cracking if applied too thick or dried too quickly.
- Light paste is soft; protect from impact.
- Sanding dried paste releases dust; wear a dust mask.

## Verify
- [ ] The relief is hard, fully dry and free of cracks before painting.
- [ ] Paint and glazes sit evenly (seal absorbent areas first).
