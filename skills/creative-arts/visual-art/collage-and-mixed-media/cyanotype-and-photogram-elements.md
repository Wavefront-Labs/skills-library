# Cyanotype and photogram elements

**Goal:** produce blue-and-white prints by contact exposure, to use as collage pieces. Cyanotype was introduced by Sir John Herschel in 1842, and Anna Atkins used it for her book of algae photograms (published 1843 onwards).

## Chemistry and safety
- The classic sensitiser is two solutions mixed in equal parts: **ferric ammonium citrate (green)** and **potassium ferricyanide**. Premixed kits are widely available.
- Wear nitrile gloves and eye protection, avoid creating dust from powders, keep away from acids (strong acids can release toxic gas from ferricyanide), and do not pour large volumes down the drain; follow local rules and the supplier's safety data sheet.

## Process
1. Work in dim, non-UV light (tungsten or yellow-filtered; not daylight).
2. Coat acid-free, **unbuffered or lightly sized** cotton paper or fabric with a thin even layer using a foam brush; dry in the dark.
3. Place objects (leaves, lace, cut paper) or a **transparent negative** printed on film in contact with the paper under glass.
4. Expose to sunlight or a UV source. Times vary widely with light, from a few minutes in strong sun to much longer under UV lamps or overcast skies; test strips are the way to find yours.
5. Rinse in running water until the water runs clear (yellow-green fades away), then dry. Hydrogen peroxide dilutions in the rinse deepen the blue quickly.
6. Optional toning with tea or other tannins shifts blue to brown or grey.

## In collage
- Print on thin paper, then glue with neutral-pH adhesive. **Alkaline** materials (some buffered papers, washing soda, strongly alkaline adhesives) can bleach cyanotype; test.
- Fugitive in strong light for long periods; display with moderate light.

## Gotchas
- Pale prints usually mean under-exposure or too thin a coat; very dark prints in light areas mean over-exposure.
- Heavy sizing on some papers repels the sensitiser.

## Verify
- [ ] A test strip shows deep blue at the right time with clear whites.
- [ ] Final prints are fully washed and dry before use.
