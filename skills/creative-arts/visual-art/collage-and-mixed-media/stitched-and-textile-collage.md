# Stitched and textile collage

**Goal:** add fabric and sewn marks to a collage reliably.

## Fabrics and stabilising
- Cotton and linen are stable and easy to glue; avoid fraying silks and stretchy knits without backing. Pre-wash fabric you intend to wash or fade-test.
- Stabilise thin or fraying fabrics with **fusible web** or iron-on interfacing; follow the manufacturer's heat and steam instructions, and protect the iron with baking parchment.
- Seal raw edges with fabric glue or acrylic medium if they will not be stitched.

## Joining fabric to paper and board
- Use acrylic gel, matte medium or PVA to adhere fabric to paper or board; apply thin even coats and press under weight.
- To prevent staining, avoid dark dyes bleeding through; test a scrap.

## Stitching through paper
1. **Pre-punch holes** with a pin or awl along a marked line; this prevents tearing and gives regular spacing.
2. Hand-sew with embroidery thread and a sharp needle, knotting on the back.
3. **Machine stitching** through thin paper works with a larger stitch length and a sharp or denim needle; test on scrap first. Heavy card needs hand sewing.
4. Reinforce with a backing layer so the stitches do not pull through.

## Safety and care
- Use a thimble and keep needles in a pin cushion; dispose of broken needles safely.
- Wet fabric glues can stain fabrics and warp paper; use minimal moisture.

## Gotchas
- Fabric dyes may bleed when wet; test.
- Many fabrics are acidic or contain synthetic finishes (see `creative-arts.visual-art.collage-and-mixed-media.planning-longevity-and-inherent-vice-in-mixed-materials`).
- Heavy thread and tight stitching distort thin paper; keep tension light.

## Verify
- [ ] Fabric edges are sealed or stitched and do not fray.
- [ ] Stitched paper lies flat without tearing.
