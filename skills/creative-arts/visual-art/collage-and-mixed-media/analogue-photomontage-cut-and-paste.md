# Analogue photomontage (cut and paste)

**Goal:** assemble photographic fragments into one image with scissors, scalpel and glue, as practised by the Dada photomontagists such as Hannah Hoch and John Heartfield.

## Materials
- Magazines, prints or photocopies (copy originals you care about), self-healing cutting mat, sharp craft knife or scalpel with spare blades, metal ruler, tweezers, a glue of your choice (PVA, matte acrylic medium or a stick adhesive), and a bone folder or roller.

## Process
1. **Select and match.** Pick images with similar light direction, colour temperature and grain; mismatch is the usual reason montage looks fake (unless the clash is intentional).
2. **Photocopy or scan** precious or double-sided images so you can cut them freely, and print at several sizes to try scale changes.
3. **Cut cleanly.** Use a fresh blade, cut slowly on a mat, turning the paper rather than the blade for curves. For a subtle edge, bevel the cut slightly (blade angled away from the keep) so the edge thins out when glued down; a tiny edge shadow or a white rim shows.
4. **Dry-fit** on the support, then glue back to front with thin, even adhesive, burnishing from the centre to the edges to remove air.
5. **Unify.** Rephotograph or scan the finished piece and adjust contrast, or add a uniform grain or a coloured glaze so the pieces share a tone.

## Safety
- Spray adhesives release solvent vapour; use outdoors or with good ventilation and a suitable respirator, and keep away from flames.
- Replace dull blades; they slip and cause cuts. Cut away from the hand and keep fingers behind the ruler.

## Gotchas
- Glue shows through thin paper; use thin glue, or glue the thicker piece with the other dry-mounted.
- Edges lifting years later suggest poor adhesion; burnish and seal.
- Montage assembled from published images carries the same copyright questions as collage (`creative-arts.visual-art.collage-and-mixed-media.sourcing-papers-and-found-imagery-and-copyright`).

## Verify
- [ ] Light direction and scale agree across fragments, or the clash is intentional.
- [ ] Edges are flat, with no lifted corners or glue marks.
