# Composing a collage

**Goal:** make a collage that reads as a designed image rather than a pile of pieces.

## Process
1. **Gather and sort** material by colour, value and texture. Limit the palette on purpose; five or six related hues beat twenty.
2. **Dry-lay** everything on the support without glue. Photograph each arrangement on a phone so you can return to earlier versions.
3. **Establish value structure first.** Squint or take a greyscale photo; check there is a clear dark, mid and light mass and that the focal area has the strongest contrast.
4. **Choose a focal point** and place the highest contrast, sharpest detail and most saturated colour near it; use quieter shapes elsewhere.
5. **Vary scale** (one large element, medium supports, small accents). Equal-sized pieces look like wallpaper.
6. **Control edges.** Torn edges soften and feel organic; cut edges feel crisp and graphic. Mix them deliberately.
7. **Use overlap** for depth: larger and lower pieces read as nearer; transparent papers (tissue, glassine) tint what sits below.
8. **Leave negative space** so the eye can rest; unglued areas of plain support are part of the design.
9. **Glue back to front,** photograph again, then adjust with paint, pencil or a final unifying glaze if needed.

## Gotchas
- Adding detail to fix a weak structure rarely works; fix values and shapes first.
- Strong directional lines (a street, a figure's gaze) lead the eye; check they point inward, not off the edge.
- A single over-saturated scrap can dominate; tone it down with a glaze or swap it.

## Verify
- [ ] A greyscale photo of the dry-lay reads clearly from across the room.
- [ ] The eye lands on the focal point first, then travels.
