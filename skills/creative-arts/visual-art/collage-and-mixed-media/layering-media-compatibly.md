# Layering media compatibly

**Goal:** know which media may go over which so mixed-media work stays stable.

## The rules of thumb
1. **Acrylic over oil is unsound.** Water-based acrylic does not bond durably to an oil film and tends to peel; keep oil above acrylic or apart from it.
2. **Oil over acrylic is acceptable** on a fully cured, slightly toothy acrylic layer (acrylic ground or paint), and is the common order in mixed-media painting.
3. **Within oil: fat over lean.** Each layer should have more oil (be more flexible) than the one below, or the surface cracks as it dries.
4. **Watercolour, gouache and inks over acrylic** work on matte or absorbent acrylic grounds; they bead on gloss, so use a matte medium or absorbent ground. Seal water media before oil or acrylic goes on top if you want to avoid lifting.
5. **Oil on paper or unprimed cotton** needs a size or ground, or the oil rots the fibre over time.
6. **Wax is last.** Wax surfaces (cold wax, encaustic) repel water-based paint and give later layers little to grip, so nothing but more wax belongs on top of them.
7. **Dry pastels, charcoal and oil pastel:** use fixative with care; oil pastels stay greasy and should be on top of acrylic, not under it.
8. **Collage adhesives:** acrylic mediums and PVA bond well under acrylic; avoid gluing wet water-soluble items on oily surfaces.

## Practical checks
- Test a sample board of your intended stack and age it for several weeks. Scratch and flex it to check adhesion before building a large piece.
- Cure times: acrylic touch-dry in minutes to hours but fully cured in days; oil takes weeks to months to dry through.
- Manufacturers publish compatibility guides; consult them for specific products (for example Golden's technical notes).

## Gotchas
- A glossy surface can reject anything that follows; sand or use a tooth-giving medium.
- Alkyd mediums count as oil for compatibility.
