# Watercolour brushes and palette setup

**Goal:** a minimal, high-performance watercolour kit laid out for fast, clean mixing
(Mayer, watercolour chapter; maker technical leaflets).

## Brushes
| Brush | Use |
|-------|-----|
| Round (sable or good synthetic), sizes 6, 10, 14 | Most work: washes and detail from one brush if it holds a point |
| Squirrel mop / quill | Large wet washes; holds lots of water; soft edges |
| Flat 2.5 cm (1 in) | Skies, straight edges, lifting |
| Rigger / liner | Branches, rigging, fine lines |
- Kolinsky sable: best spring, point and capacity; expensive. Modern synthetics and
  sable-synthetic blends are good and cheaper.
- Test a round: wet it, flick it: it should snap to a sharp point.

## Paint: pans vs tubes
- **Pans/half pans**: compact, travel; re-wet each session. Fill empty pans from
  tubes and let dry (most honey-based or glycerine paints re-wet well; some
  pigments crack).
- **Tubes**: fresh, juicy paint for large washes; squeeze into palette wells.

## Palette layout
- A palette with deep wells and large flat mixing areas (white enamel, porcelain or
  plastic; porcelain beads water less).
- Arrange colours warm to cool or by family, consistently: yellows, oranges, reds,
  violets, blues, greens, earths. 8-12 colours is plenty, e.g. a split-primary set
  plus burnt sienna, raw sienna, and a dark (neutral tint or indanthrone blue).
- Leave the mixing areas clean; wipe between paintings.

## Water and accessories
- Two water jars (one to clean, one clean for mixing); spray bottle; paper towels or
  a sponge to control brush moisture; masking tape; scrap test paper of the same
  paper as the painting.

## Gotchas
- Lots of tiny brushes: fussy painting. Use the largest brush possible.
- Leaving brushes standing in water: bent tips, loosened ferrules.
- Mixing in a cramped palette: too little paint, washes run out mid-area.

## Verify
- [ ] Rounds hold a point; one large wash brush available.
- [ ] Palette laid out in a consistent order with large mixing space.
- [ ] Test sheet of the same paper at hand.
