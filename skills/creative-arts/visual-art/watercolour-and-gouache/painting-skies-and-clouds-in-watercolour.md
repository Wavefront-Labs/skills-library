# Painting skies and clouds in watercolour

**Goal:** fresh, luminous skies painted quickly in one or two passes; the sky
often sets the light of the whole landscape.

## Clear graded sky
1. Board tilted 10-20 degrees; tape edges.
2. Pre-mix plenty of blue (e.g. cerulean or cobalt for the horizon,
   ultramarine/phthalo blue for the top).
3. Lay a **graded wash**: strongest blue at the top, adding water each stroke
   toward the horizon (sky is lighter and warmer near the horizon; a touch of
   yellow ochre or rose low down warms it).
4. Leave it alone to dry.

## Wet-in-wet clouds
1. Wet the sky area evenly with clean water (paper should have an even sheen,
   no puddles).
2. Drop in blue around cloud shapes, leaving white paper for clouds.
3. While still damp, drop greys (ultramarine + burnt sienna, or + light red) into
   cloud undersides; tilt to let them diffuse.
4. Timing: very wet paper = very soft spreading; damp (sheen gone) = softer but
   controlled; dry = hard edges. Use a mix: soft cloud bases, a few crisp tops.

## Lifting clouds
On a still-wet blue wash, press a crumpled tissue or a thirsty brush to lift soft
white clouds. Staining blues (phthalo) lift less than cerulean or cobalt.

## Storm skies
Darker, stronger greys dropped boldly wet-in-wet; allow backruns where they help
(see controlling backruns); keep some light near the horizon for drama.

## Rules
- Sky usually stays lighter than the land masses (sky is the light source).
- Paint the sky in one go; repainting kills freshness.
- Clouds get smaller, flatter and closer together toward the horizon (perspective).

## Gotchas
- Puddles of water on the paper cause uncontrolled blooms.
- Overworking a drying sky: streaks and cauliflowers.
- Clouds all the same size and shape.

## Verify
- [ ] Sky graded overhead to horizon.
- [ ] Mixed edges: soft bases, some crisp tops.
- [ ] Painted in one pass with no reworked streaks.
