# Painting trees and foliage in watercolour

**Goal:** trees that read as masses of light and shadow with lively edges, not
thousands of leaves.

## See the mass
- Squint: a tree is 2-4 big masses (clumps) with a light top/side and a shadow
  underside, plus the trunk. Paint those, not leaves.
- Note the species' silhouette (oak broad and lumpy, poplar tall, pine layered).

## Greens
- Mix greens rather than using tube green alone: yellows + blues (lemon + phthalo:
  bright; raw sienna + ultramarine: olive; burnt sienna + phthalo green or blue:
  deep dark greens).
- Warm greens in sunlit tops (more yellow), cool and dark in shadows (more blue, a
  touch of red to grey). Vary green across trees for depth.

## Painting a tree
1. **First wash**: the light green of the whole mass, edges broken by dragging the
   **side of the brush** across the paper (dry-brush texture at the edges), leaving
   a few gaps.
2. While damp, drop darker green into the shadow side to blend softly.
3. When dry, add **darker clumps** with hard edges in the shadow areas for structure.
4. **Sky holes**: leave or later lift small gaps of sky in the mass, fewer and smaller
   than you see; they make the tree airy.
5. **Trunk and branches**: trunk darker where in shadow; a rigger for thin branches
   tapering out into the mass; let branches disappear behind foliage and reappear.

## Distant trees
Simpler, bluer, lighter shapes painted wet-in-wet; no detail (see atmospheric-perspective).

## Gotchas
- Lollipop trees (round ball on stick): vary the silhouette.
- Uniform tube green: flat, artificial.
- Painting individual leaves everywhere.

## Verify
- [ ] Each tree reads as light masses and shadow masses.
- [ ] Edges broken; a few sky holes.
- [ ] Greens varied warm to cool.
