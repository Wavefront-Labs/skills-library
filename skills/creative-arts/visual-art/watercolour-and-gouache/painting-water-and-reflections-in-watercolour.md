# Painting water and reflections in watercolour

**Goal:** water that lies flat and reflects convincingly (reflection principles
from Gurney, *Color and Light*, "reflections").

## Reflection rules
- A reflection appears **directly below** the object (vertically), whatever the viewpoint.
- Its length is measured from the point where the object would meet the water
  surface, not from the visible bank; objects set back on a bank may show only
  partly or not at all.
- Reflections of light objects are usually **slightly darker** than the object;
  reflections of dark objects slightly lighter; colours a little duller.
- Still water: mirror-like. Rippled water: reflections stretch vertically and break
  into horizontal zigzags.
- The water surface also reflects the **sky**, lighter toward the distance (more
  oblique angle).

## Method
1. Paint the sky's reflection in the water first (graded, matching the sky).
2. While still damp, drop in reflections as **vertical** strokes: soft, slightly
   darker and simpler than the subjects above.
3. Let dry partially; add stronger, crisper reflection accents near the shoreline.
4. **Ripples and wind lines**: when dry, lift thin horizontal lines with a damp flat
   brush edge and blot, or leave white paper streaks; in disturbed water, break
   reflections with horizontal gaps.
5. A few sharp horizontal strokes of dark near the bank anchor objects to the water.

## Gotchas
- Reflections angled with perspective: they are always vertical.
- Reflections painted as detailed as the object: they should be simplified.
- Water painted lighter than the sky everywhere: usually it is slightly darker.

## Verify
- [ ] Reflections directly below their objects.
- [ ] Reflections simpler and value-shifted.
- [ ] Horizontal breaks/lines make the surface lie flat.
