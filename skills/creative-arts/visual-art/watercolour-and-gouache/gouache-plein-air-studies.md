# Gouache plein air studies

**Goal:** small, colour-accurate outdoor studies in gouache, a method popularised by
James Gurney (videos and blog) for its speed and opacity.

## Kit
- Tubes of gouache (a limited palette plus white) squeezed into a small sealed
  palette; water cup; 2-4 brushes (flats and rounds).
- Watercolour sketchbook (cotton, 300 gsm) or toned paper; clips.
- A lightweight sketch easel or clipboard on a tripod; stool; umbrella or hat to
  shade the paper.

## Process (about 45-90 minutes)
1. **Thumbnail** with pencil (values and big shapes).
2. **Light underdrawing** in pencil or a thin wash.
3. **Transparent wash stage** (optional): thin washes like watercolour for big areas
   and darks.
4. **Opaque block-in**: big shapes with opaque mixtures, generally dark/mid first,
   then lights over them (gouache allows light over dark).
5. **Refine**: edges, accents, highlights, a few details at the focal point.
6. Sign and note place, date, time, light.

## Outdoor conditions
- Sun and wind dry paint fast: mist the palette, work in smaller areas, keep paper
  in shade.
- Cold/humid: slower drying; reactivation increases; fewer layers.
- Paint the shadows' shapes early, before the light moves.

## Colour judgement
- Gouache dries lighter/darker by colour; test swatches on the paper's margin.
- Toned paper (mid-grey or warm) helps judge values outdoors in bright light.

## Gotchas
- Thick paint cracking in a sketchbook when it bends.
- Glare on white paper fooling values.

## Verify
- [ ] Value thumbnail made first.
- [ ] Shadows painted early; paper shaded.
- [ ] Study completed in one sitting.
