# Lifting and scrubbing out watercolour

**Goal:** remove pigment to create soft lights (clouds, rays, highlights) or to
correct mistakes, without damaging the paper surface.

## Know what will lift
- **Staining pigments** (phthalo blues and greens, quinacridones, Prussian blue,
  alizarin) dye the fibres and barely lift. **Non-staining / sedimentary** pigments
  (ultramarine, cerulean, earths, cobalt) lift easily. Manufacturers' charts mark
  staining; test your palette on a swatch sheet.
- **Paper**: well-sized cotton papers (e.g. Arches, Saunders Waterford) lift
  cleanly and tolerate scrubbing. Gelatine-sized papers lift more readily than
  heavily surface-sized ones; cellulose papers pill.

## Lifting wet paint
- While the wash is damp, press a crumpled tissue, a squeezed natural sponge or a
  **thirsty brush** (rinsed and blotted) into it. The thirsty brush must be drier
  than the paper or it backruns.
- For clouds, dab and twist tissue in the sky while still shiny-damp; edges stay soft.

## Scrubbing out dry paint
1. Wet the area with clean water and wait 10-30 seconds.
2. Agitate gently with a **stiff synthetic flat** (an "oil" brush or a dedicated
   scrubber/fritch brush) or a damp melamine foam ("magic eraser") corner.
3. Blot immediately with tissue. Repeat rather than scrubbing harder.

## Hard-edged lifts
Cut a stencil from acetate or use low-tack tape to mask around the shape, then
sponge through the opening. Good for window highlights and light shafts.

## After lifting
Let the area dry completely. Scrubbed paper absorbs more, so further washes go on
darker and can bloom; glaze lightly or leave it.

## Gotchas
- Scrubbing too long breaks the size and raises fibres; the area then looks woolly.
- Lifting from a wash with a staining pigment leaves a tinted ghost, not white.
- A sponge with paint residue re-deposits colour; rinse often.

## Verify
- [ ] Pigments tested for staining before relying on lifts.
- [ ] Lift done with blotting, not rubbing to destruction.
- [ ] Surface still intact (no pilling) after drying.
