# Acrylic gouache versus traditional gouache

**Goal:** pick the right gouache for the job and combine them safely.

## Differences
| Property | Traditional gouache | Acrylic gouache (e.g. Holbein Acryla, Turner Acryl Gouache) |
|----------|---------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------|
| Binder | Gum arabic (re-soluble) | Acrylic polymer (permanent when dry) |
| Layering | Under-layers re-wet and lift | Layers stay put; unlimited layering |
| Finish | Very matte, velvety | Matte, slightly less chalky |
| Corrections | Re-wet and lift, soften edges later | Paint over only |
| Palette | Re-wet dried paint | Dried paint is wasted; stay-wet palette |
| Brushes | Easy to clean | Rinse often; dried paint ruins brushes |
| Flexibility | Thick layers crack | More flexible; still keep layers moderate |
| Varnish | Usually framed under glass | Can be varnished more readily |

## Which for what
- **Traditional**: plein air sketches, soft edges, corrections, illustration where
  re-wetting is useful.
- **Acrylic gouache**: many flat layers (graphic illustration, murals on paper/
  board), mixed media, painting over collage, when you need permanence.

## Combining
- Acrylic gouache underneath, traditional on top: good (base layers do not lift).
- Traditional underneath, acrylic over: possible but wet acrylic can lift the
  traditional layer; apply lightly.

## Gotchas
- Leaving acrylic gouache in good brushes: hardens; use synthetic brushes and rinse.
- Expecting acrylic gouache to re-wet on the palette.

## Verify
- [ ] Gouache type chosen for layering and correction needs.
- [ ] Layer order respects the binder differences.
