# Masking fluid and tape for watercolour

**Goal:** keep small or complex whites clean while you paint loose washes over
them, without tearing the paper or leaving cut-out-looking shapes.

## Materials
- **Masking fluid / frisket / drawing gum**: liquid latex in ammonia. Tinted
  versions (e.g. Winsor & Newton coloured, Schmincke blue) show where you put it;
  colourless versions can stain less on white paper but are hard to see.
- **Applicators**: an old or synthetic brush coated first in soap (rub on a bar of
  soap), a silicone colour shaper, a ruling pen or dip nib for fine lines, a
  toothbrush for spattered sparkles. Latex ruins good brushes.
- **Low-tack masking tape** (or artist's tape) for straight edges and borders.
- **Rubber cement pickup** (crepe eraser) to lift dried fluid.

## Paper matters
Use 300 gsm (140 lb) or heavier **internally and externally sized** cotton paper.
Soft or lightly sized papers tear when the latex is removed. Test on an offcut
of the same sheet first.

## Applying
1. Draw the design lightly; mask only what must stay white or very light.
2. Shake (do not stir in bubbles) the bottle; decant a small amount into a cap.
3. Apply in one confident pass. Rinse/re-soap the applicator often; latex sets in
   minutes.
4. Let it **dry completely** (it turns from glossy to matt and is no longer tacky,
   typically 10-20 minutes). Never use a hair dryer on hot setting: heat bonds latex
   to the fibres.

## Painting over
Paint washes freely over the mask. Pigment pooling against the mask edge dries as
a hard line; blot it if you do not want it.

## Removing
- Remove once the paint is **bone dry**, and ideally within a day or two. Fluid left
  for weeks or in sunlight becomes hard to remove and can yellow or stain.
- Rub from the edge with the crepe eraser or a clean finger, rolling it off.
- Tape: peel slowly at a low angle, back over itself; warm stubborn tape slightly.

## Softening the result
Masked whites have unnaturally hard edges. Afterwards, soften some edges with a
damp brush, glaze a tint into parts of the white, or vary the mask with spatter
and dry-brush application so the lights look painted, not cut out.

## Gotchas
- Applying fluid onto damp paper or over a wash that is not fully dry: it sinks in
  and tears the surface on removal.
- Old, thickened fluid will not flow and leaves ridges; replace it.
- Masking too much leads to a stiff, stencilled picture. Plan whites with painting
  around first; mask only fine sparkles, ropes, highlights and complex edges.

## Verify
- [ ] Tested fluid removal on the same paper.
- [ ] Mask dry before painting; paint dry before removal.
- [ ] No torn fibres or stains; hard edges softened where needed.
