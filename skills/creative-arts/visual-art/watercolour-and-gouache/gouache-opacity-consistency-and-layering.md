# Gouache opacity, consistency and layering

**Goal:** flat, opaque, velvety colour with gouache: water-based paint with more
pigment and often an opaque filler, giving a matte finish (Mayer, gouache).

## Consistency
- For opaque flats: thin the paint with water to roughly **single cream** or
  yoghurt consistency; it should flow off the brush and cover in one coat.
- Too thick: brush drag, ridges, and cracks when dry. Too thin: streaky, transparent.
- Mix enough of each colour for the whole area; gouache colour is hard to remix exactly.

## Flat even coverage
1. Use a soft flat or round, well loaded.
2. Lay strokes in one direction, overlapping while wet; do not scrub.
3. For large flats, two thin coats beat one thick.

## Layering
- Gouache can go **light over dark** (unlike watercolour), as long as the layer
  beneath is dry and the top layer is opaque enough.
- Work **thin to thick**: thin transparent washes early (like watercolour), opaque
  passages later.
- Each new layer re-wets the one below (see reactivation skill); apply with light,
  decisive strokes.

## Value shift
- Gouache dries **lighter** in darks and sometimes **darker** in lights (varies by
  brand); test swatches dried before matching.

## Surfaces
Hot or cold press watercolour paper, illustration board, or toned paper.

## Gotchas
- Thick layers on flexible paper: cracking and flaking.
- Too much white for every tint: chalky colour; lighten with lighter pigments where possible.

## Verify
- [ ] Even opaque flats without streaks or ridges.
- [ ] Colours checked dry before large areas.
