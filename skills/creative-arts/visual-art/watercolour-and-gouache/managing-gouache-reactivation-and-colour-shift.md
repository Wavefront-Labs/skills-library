# Managing gouache reactivation and colour shift

**Goal:** stop unwanted lifting and muddiness when layering traditional gouache,
and use its re-wettability as a feature.

## Why it happens
Traditional gouache uses gum arabic binder, which stays water-soluble after drying.
A wet brush over a dry layer dissolves it, mixing the two layers (Mayer).

## Painting over without lifting
1. Let the under-layer dry fully.
2. Use paint of a slightly thicker consistency on top, with a soft brush.
3. Lay each stroke **once**, lightly, without scrubbing or going back.
4. Work quickly in an area; avoid wetting large regions twice.
5. If a colour bleeds through (staining pigments like phthalos, some reds), isolate
   with a thin coat of fixative or switch to acrylic gouache for the base layer.

## Using reactivation
- Soften edges later by re-wetting with a damp brush.
- Lift highlights or correct small areas by re-wetting and blotting.
- Reuse dried paint on the palette: re-wet with a few drops of water.

## Palette and storage
- Palettes with lids or a stay-wet palette keep paint usable; dried gouache
  re-wets, though some brands crack or become chalky when re-wet.
- Keep a small water spray to dampen the palette.

## Colour shift
- Darks often dry lighter and mid-values may shift; paint a test swatch, dry it with
  a hair dryer, and compare before painting big areas.

## Gotchas
- Scrubbing a top layer: mud and lifted under-layers.
- Mixing traditional and acrylic gouache layers in the wrong order: traditional on
  top is fine; acrylic over traditional may lift it while wet.

## Verify
- [ ] Top layers laid without disturbing the ones beneath.
- [ ] Dry swatch checks done for critical colours.
