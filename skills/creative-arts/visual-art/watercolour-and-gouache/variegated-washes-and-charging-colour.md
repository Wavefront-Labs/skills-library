# Variegated washes and charging colour

**Goal:** washes that move smoothly from one colour to another, and colour dropped
into wet passages to create glowing, mixed-on-paper transitions.

## Variegated wash
1. Tilt the board 10-20 degrees; pre-mix **separate pools** of each colour, enough for
   the whole area.
2. Load the brush with colour A; lay horizontal strokes, each overlapping the
   **bead** of paint at the bottom of the previous stroke.
3. Switch to colour B partway, picking up the bead so the colours mingle.
4. Continue to the bottom; pick up the final bead with a damp, squeezed brush.
5. Do not go back into it.

## Charging colour
- Paint a passage with one colour; while it is still **wet** (shiny), touch a brush
  loaded with a second colour into it; the colours flow and mix on the paper.
- Paint consistency: the charged colour should be **as wet or slightly drier** than
  the area; wetter paint pushes into damper paint and causes backruns.
- Great for skin, petals, stones, skies: colour variety within one shape.

## Mixing on paper vs palette
- Colours mixed on paper keep more luminosity and variety than premixed;
  complementary pairs charged together can grey nicely or go to mud if overworked.
- Granulating pigments (ultramarine, burnt sienna, some earth blues) separate on
  the paper for texture.

## Gotchas
- Pools mixed too small: running out mid-wash leaves a line.
- Brushing back and forth: streaks and mud.
- Charging into a drying (matte) area: blooms.

## Verify
- [ ] Transitions smooth with no hard lines.
- [ ] Colours stay clean where they meet.
