# Dry brush, spatter and salt textures

**Goal:** describe surfaces (bark, sparkle on water, stone, snow) with texture
that is controlled and placed, not sprinkled everywhere.

## Dry brush
- Load the brush, then blot most water on tissue until paint only catches the
  **tops of the paper tooth**. Rough or cold-press paper gives the broken effect;
  hot-press barely does.
- Hold the brush low, nearly flat to the paper, and drag quickly with the side of
  the hairs. A splayed round or a worn flat gives grass and wood grain.
- Leaves white sparkle between strokes: excellent for light on water, rough
  stone, weathered wood. Apply over a dry first wash.

## Spatter
- Load a stiff brush or old toothbrush with fairly wet paint; flick with a finger or
  tap the loaded brush against another handle.
- **Mask** the areas you want clean with paper scraps. Spatter on wet paper gives
  soft dots; on dry paper, crisp dots.
- Clean water spatter into a damp wash makes small blooms (good for lichen, foam).

## Salt
- Sprinkle coarse or fine salt into a wash at the **damp-shiny stage** (just losing
  its gloss). Too wet: salt dissolves with no pattern. Too dry: no effect.
- Each crystal draws pigment and water, leaving pale starbursts as it dries.
  Coarse salt makes larger stars; table salt makes fine snow texture.
- Leave until completely dry, then brush all salt off. Residual salt is
  hygroscopic and can damage paper over time.
- Granulating pigments (ultramarine, cobalt, earths) give the strongest effect.

## Other resists
Wax candle or oil pastel before painting (permanent resist), alcohol drops into a
damp wash (rings), plastic wrap pressed into a wet wash (crystal facets).

## Gotchas
- Texture everywhere flattens the picture. Keep it to focal surfaces and leave
  quiet areas for contrast.
- Salt results vary with humidity and paper sizing; test on an offcut first.
- Spattered dots outside the intended area are hard to remove; mask generously.

## Verify
- [ ] Dry brush used on dry underwash with rough or cold-press paper.
- [ ] Salt added at damp-shiny stage and fully brushed off once dry.
- [ ] Texture confined to the areas that need it.
