# Travel watercolour kit and location painting

**Goal:** a kit small enough to always carry, which still makes proper washes.

## Palette
- A metal or plastic travel tin with 8-12 half pans (or fill empty pans from tubes
  and let dry 2-3 days).
- Suggested set: a cool and warm yellow, a cool and warm red, ultramarine,
  cerulean or phthalo blue, burnt sienna, raw sienna/yellow ochre, a dark (indigo,
  neutral tint or a mix), maybe a green (sap or perylene).
- Mixing areas in the lid; wipe after use; leave open to dry before closing.

## Brushes
- **Water brush** (barrel holds water): no water jar needed; good for sketching;
  limited flow for big washes; clean by squeezing on a tissue.
- **Travel brushes** (sable/synthetic rounds whose handle covers the hair):
  better washes; need a water container.
- One large and one small brush are enough.

## Paper
- Watercolour sketchbook (cotton, 300 gsm) or a watercolour block glued on four sides
  (stays flat). Mixed-media books (200 gsm+) for lighter washes.

## Water and extras
- Collapsible water cup with clip, small water bottle, paper towel or sponge, bulldog
  clips (wind), pencil, waterproof pen, small stool, a hat to shade the page.

## On location
1. Small format (A5-A4) and short time (20-60 minutes).
2. Big washes first (sky, ground), shadows second, details last (see
   urban-sketching-on-location).
3. Drying: shade slows, sun speeds; hold the book flat to avoid runs; in humid
   weather plan fewer layers.
4. Let the sketchbook pages dry before closing (or interleave tissue).

## Gotchas
- Too many pans: a heavy kit you leave at home.
- Water brush flooding wet areas: squeeze gently.

## Verify
- [ ] Kit fits a small bag and is used.
- [ ] Paper takes washes without buckling badly.
