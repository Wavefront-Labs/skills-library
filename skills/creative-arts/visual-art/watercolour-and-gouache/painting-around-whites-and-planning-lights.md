# Painting around whites and planning lights

**Goal:** keep the paper white where the brightest lights are, because transparent
watercolour cannot add a true white later (Mayer, watercolour chapter).

## Plan first
1. Make a small value sketch: mark the **whites** (paper), lights, mid-tones, darks.
2. Lightly pencil the outlines of white shapes on the watercolour paper.
3. Decide each white's edge: hard (clean painting around) or soft (painting around
   then softening with a damp brush).

## Painting around (reserving)
- Lay the first, lightest wash **around** the white shapes, leaving them untouched.
- Use the brush tip along the edge, keeping the wash moving to avoid hard-drying lines
  elsewhere.
- Small sparkles: leave tiny gaps by dragging the side of a dry-ish brush.

## Negative painting
- Instead of painting objects, paint the **space around them** a darker value so the
  objects emerge as lighter shapes (e.g. light branches against a darker background).
- Build layered depth: each layer of negative painting reveals another shape lighter
  than the new background.

## Light to dark
- Work from lightest washes to darkest; each glaze reduces light, so reserve every
  light area at the stage it should stop.

## When to mask instead
- Many small, complex whites in a big wet wash (snowflakes, sparkles on water,
  fine grasses) are easier with masking fluid (see masking fluid skill); painting
  around preserves softer, more natural edges.

## Gotchas
- Losing a white by a careless wash: correction options (lifting, scraping, gouache)
  never look quite as fresh.
- Whites everywhere: they must be few, to stay bright.

## Verify
- [ ] Whites planned in a value sketch before painting.
- [ ] Paper white remains at the planned spots with intended edges.
