# Choosing water, oil or polymer clay

The clay is chosen by **what happens to the sculpture next**, not by what is on the shelf.

## Decision table
| End use | Clay | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Fired ceramic piece (finished work is the clay) | Water-based clay body (earthenware, stoneware, paper clay, grogged sculpture body) | Only ceramic clay can be fired; grog (20-40% for big work) opens the body so thick walls dry and fire without cracking |
| Original for a mould (plaster, silicone, bronze) | Oil-based plastilina (Chavant NSP / Le Beau Touche, Monster Clay, Roma) or water clay kept damp | Oil clay never dries, so you can work for months and mould it at leisure; water clay is cheaper for big pieces but must be moulded before it cracks |
| Small detailed figure, maquette, hobby piece, no kiln | Polymer clay (Sculpey, Super Sculpey, Fimo, Cosclay) | Cures in a home oven around 110-135 C (follow the pack), holds fine detail, takes an internal foil/wire armature |
| Large life-size figure for moulding | Water clay on armature, or oil clay over foam core | Oil clay at that volume is costly and heavy; water clay is cheap and recyclable |

## Working properties
- **Water clay**: soft and responsive, quick to block in large masses; dries and shrinks (5-12% total), so it cracks around armatures. Keep it under plastic and mist it; cover detail with damp cloth.
- **Oil clay** (sulphur-free grades if moulding with platinum silicone): firmness grades soft / medium / hard; warm it (heat lamp, warming box, around 40-50 C) to soften and build, cool it to carve detail. Smooth with a solvent such as naphtha or odourless mineral spirits sparingly.
- **Monster Clay / wax-based clays**: melt and pour for quick bulking, re-meltable, sulphur-free.
- **Polymer clay**: condition by kneading before use; blend Super Sculpey with Sculpey Firm for a stiffer feel; bake in stages for complex pieces; it can be sanded and carved after curing.

## Gotchas
- **Sulphur-bearing plastilina inhibits platinum (addition-cure) silicone** - the mould stays tacky at the surface. Use sulphur-free clay or a tin-cure silicone, or test first.
- **Water clay over a permanent armature cannot be fired**; plan removable armature or hollow construction.
- **Polymer clay burns** if the oven runs hot; use an oven thermometer and ventilate.
- **Mixing clay bodies** with different shrinkage in one fired piece causes cracks at the joins.

## Verify
- [ ] The end use (fire, mould, keep) is fixed before buying clay.
- [ ] For silicone moulding, clay is confirmed sulphur-free or the silicone is tin-cure.
- [ ] For firing, the body is grogged for the wall thickness and no armature stays in.
