# Faux finishes on plaster and resin

## Prepare
- **Plaster**: must be fully dry (days to weeks). Seal with thinned shellac (2-3 coats) or acrylic sealer so paint doesn't soak in unevenly.
- **Resin**: wash off release with detergent; light scuff with fine abrasive; prime with a plastic-compatible primer.
- **Jesmonite**: sealer recommended by the maker, or acrylic.

## Faux bronze
1. Base coat: dark brown/black (acrylic or oil) all over.
2. **Metallic layer**: bronze or gold metallic wax (e.g. gilding wax / Rub 'n Buff type) or metallic acrylic, applied with a finger or rag on the high points only.
3. **Patina glaze**: a thin green-blue (verdigris) or brown glaze into the recesses; wipe back high points so metal shows.
4. Buff high points with soft cloth; seal with paste wax or a matt/satin varnish.

## Faux stone (marble, sandstone, granite)
- Base colour; sponge or stipple 2-3 related colours; spatter fine dark and light dots with a toothbrush for granite; feather veins with a liner brush and soften for marble.
- Sandstone: mix fine sand or texture paste into the base coat.
- Matt varnish; marble may get a satin wax.

## Faux terracotta
- Warm orange-red base; dry-brush lighter tones on the high points; dirty glaze (raw umber) in recesses; matt finish.

## General method
- Work **dark to light**: recesses dark, high points light - mimics wear and how light falls.
- Dry-brushing: nearly dry brush dragged over texture catches only the high points.
- Test on an offcut cast first.

## Gotchas
- **Paint on damp plaster** blisters and flakes.
- **Too-shiny metallic** reads as paint; break it with glaze and wipe back.
- Uniform colour looks fake - real materials vary.

## Verify
- [ ] Surface sealed and dry.
- [ ] Highlights on high points, darks in recesses.
- [ ] Protective topcoat appropriate for handling or display.
