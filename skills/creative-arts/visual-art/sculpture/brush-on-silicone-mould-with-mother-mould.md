# Brush-on silicone mould with a mother mould

For pieces larger than a hand, a thin (6-12 mm) rubber "glove" supported by a rigid **mother mould** (jacket) uses a fraction of the rubber of a block mould.

## Plan
- Decide the **parting line** where the jacket will split (usually two pieces; more for complex forms). The rubber may be one piece cut open later, or divided by a clay wall.
- Rubber: a brushable silicone (e.g. Smooth-On Rebound series) or regular silicone thickened with a thixotropic additive (e.g. THI-VEX).

## Steps
1. Seal and release the original as for any silicone mould; inhibition-test platinum rubber.
2. **Clay wall** along the parting line (if splitting the rubber): about 2-3 cm wide with a ridge or keys running along it so the halves register.
3. **Print coat**: a thin first coat of unthickened rubber brushed into every detail, blown into crevices to remove bubbles.
4. Further coats (thickened) once the previous coat is tacky but not fully cured, building to about 6-12 mm; thicker at edges and high points. Add **registration keys** (rubber cones or blobs) on the outside so the rubber locks into the jacket.
5. **Mother mould**: plaster bandage and plaster, a fibreglass-and-resin layup, or a brushable plastic paste (e.g. Plasti-Paste) over the rubber, with flanges along the parting line. Drill and bolt the flanges.
6. Remove the clay wall, release the exposed rubber flange and jacket edges, and repeat for side two.
7. Unbolt, demould the jacket, peel the rubber, clean, and reassemble rubber into jacket to cast.

## Gotchas
- **Coats applied after full cure** may delaminate; follow recoat windows.
- **Undercuts in the jacket** trap it on the rubber - fill deep hollows in the rubber with clay before jacketing.
- **Thin spots** at sharp edges tear first; build them up.
- Plaster jackets are heavy and can break; resin jackets are light and durable but need ventilation and PPE.

## Verify
- [ ] Rubber at least ~6 mm everywhere, thicker at edges.
- [ ] Jacket parts release cleanly and bolt up square.
- [ ] Keys hold the rubber in place when the mould is turned over.
