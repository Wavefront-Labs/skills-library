# Chemical patinas on bronze

**Goal:** colour a bronze surface with controlled chemical reactions, as done in
foundries after chasing (Penny; foundry patina practice).

## Preparation
- Clean bare metal: sandblast or glass-bead blast for an even tooth; or scotch-brite.
  Remove all oils (do not touch with bare hands; wear nitrile gloves).

## Common chemicals and results (typical, vary with alloy and heat)
| Chemical | Usual result |
|----------|--------------|
| Liver of sulphur (potassium sulphide) | Browns to black base |
| Ferric nitrate | Warm browns, reds, golden-browns (hot) |
| Cupric nitrate | Greens and blue-greens (hot); can turn grey-green |
| Ammonium chloride / cupric chloride mixtures | Verdigris-type greens (cold, slow) |
| Bismuth nitrate | Whites/creams (hot) |
| Sodium thiosulphate + ferric nitrate | Reds/oranges |

## Hot patina method
1. Heat the bronze evenly with a propane/MAPP torch until water just sizzles on it.
2. Apply the solution with a brush (stippling or brushing) or spray; it should
   sizzle and evaporate on contact.
3. Build colour in many thin layers, heating between; base colours first (brown
   from liver of sulphur), then over-colours (ferric for warmth, cupric for green).
4. Keep the torch moving; overheating burns the patina black or flaky.

## Cold patinas
Apply solutions to room-temperature bronze, sometimes wrapped (sawdust/plastic) for
days. Slower and less predictable; often for greens.

## Sealing
- While warm (not hot), apply clear paste wax (e.g. Trewax, Renaissance Wax) and
  buff; or a clear lacquer (Incralac) for outdoor works, then wax.
- Outdoor bronzes need annual washing and re-waxing.

## Safety
- Ventilated area or outdoors; respirator for fumes; nitrile gloves; eye protection.
- Some chemicals are oxidisers or toxic; store separately; dispose per local rules.

## Gotchas
- Fingerprints and grease: blotchy colour.
- Applying to a too-cool surface: runs and streaks.

## Verify
- [ ] Even, intended colour without blotches.
- [ ] Sealed with wax or lacquer for the display environment.
