# Found-object sculpture and joining

**Goal:** a sculpture built from existing objects that holds together, travels and
lasts (assemblage tradition as described by MoMA; adhesive maker guidance;
conservation advice).

## Sourcing and preparing
- Collect with intent: themes, materials, colours. Note provenance (some objects
  may have rights or hazards).
- Clean: remove grease, rust (wire brush, rust converter), old flaking paint;
  check for lead paint, asbestos, mould, sharp edges, pressurised containers.
- Stabilise: seal rusty metal (rust converter, lacquer, wax), consolidate rotten
  wood (epoxy consolidant), freeze or treat textiles for pests.

## Joining by material
| Materials | Join |
|-----------|------|
| Wood to wood | Screws, glue + screws, dowels |
| Metal to metal | Welding/brazing (if compatible metals), bolts, rivets (pop rivets) |
| Dissimilar materials | Bolts through drilled holes, machine screws, epoxy with mechanical backup |
| Plastics | Mechanical fastening; solvent cement only for same plastic; most glues fail on PE/PP |
| Fragile/irregular items | Wire, cable ties (temporary), custom brackets, hidden pins |
- Prefer **mechanical joins** for anything load-bearing; adhesives alone fail over time
  and with temperature changes.
- Two-part epoxy for gap-filling; roughen and degrease surfaces first.

## Structure
- Build a hidden **armature or core** (steel rod, plywood box, timber frame) and
  attach objects to it, rather than objects bearing each other's weight.
- Base: heavy enough or fixed to the floor/plinth; check stability by pushing at the top.
- Design for transport: removable parts, bolt-together sections.

## Gotchas
- Glue-only joins of heavy objects: they let go.
- Galvanic corrosion: steel bolts in aluminium outdoors; use isolating washers or
  stainless fixings.
- Untreated organic materials attracting pests.

## Verify
- [ ] All load-bearing joins mechanical.
- [ ] Objects cleaned and stabilised; hazards removed.
- [ ] Sculpture stable under a push test and transportable.
