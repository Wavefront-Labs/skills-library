# Plinths, bases and mounting sculpture

## Height and proportion
- Set the plinth so the work's key feature (a face, the top of a form) sits at the intended view height - often around eye level (about 1.5-1.6 m) for small heads, lower for larger works viewed from above.
- The plinth top is usually a little larger than the footprint of the base; too big competes, too small looks precarious.
- Plinth as part of the work (Brancusi) versus neutral white display plinth - decide deliberately.

## Bases (attached to the work)
- Materials: stone (marble, granite, slate), hardwood, steel plate, Jesmonite/concrete.
- **Mounting**: drill and tap the sculpture (or set a threaded insert/stud epoxied into a drilled hole) and bolt through the base with threaded rod, washer and nut recessed underneath. Bronze is typically tapped; stone and plaster get a stud epoxied in.
- Felt or cork pads under the base to protect furniture.

## Display plinths
- MDF or plywood boxes, painted (matt emulsion), with an internal ballast shelf; height and top size matched to the work.
- Acrylic covers (vitrines) for fragile or handleable work.
- **Stability**: if a push at the top could tip it, add ballast, wider base, or fix the work to the plinth (museum wax for small items, mechanical fixings for heavier) and the plinth to the floor.

## Outdoor and public work
- Concrete foundation sized by an engineer; stainless steel anchor bolts or resin anchors; stainless steel fixings to avoid rust staining and galvanic corrosion (isolate dissimilar metals).
- Allow drainage under hollow works; tamper-resistant fixings.
- Levelling: shims or levelling nuts on threaded rod, then grout.

## Gotchas
- Mild-steel rods in bronze or marble rust and stain.
- Tapped holes in thin cast walls strip; add an internal boss or plate.
- A plinth too short for children's reach invites handling.

## Verify
- [ ] View height tested with the work on the plinth.
- [ ] Push test at the top: no rocking or tipping.
- [ ] Corrosion-safe fixings outdoors.
