# Modelling relief sculpture

## Types
- **Low relief (bas-relief)**: forms project a small fraction of their real depth; nothing is undercut.
- **High relief**: forms project half or more of their depth; parts can be undercut or free-standing.
- **Rilievo schiacciato** (Donatello's "squashed" relief): almost drawing - depth of a few millimetres, perspective by modelling.

## Setup
1. Plywood board, sealed (shellac or varnish) so water clay doesn't warp it; screw in a few battens or nails to key the clay.
2. Lay a clay slab of even thickness (e.g. 2-3 cm) using wooden guide sticks and a rolling pin or straightedge.
3. Work the relief **upright on an easel** at its final viewing angle, lit from above at around 45 degrees, as it will be displayed.
4. Transfer the drawing by pricking through paper or incising lightly.

## Modelling
- **Decide the depth scale** (e.g. 1:10 of real depth for low relief). The nearest plane is the clay's top surface; the furthest is the ground.
- Establish the **planes in order**: foreground, middle, background, with clear steps between them.
- Contours: in low relief, cut the edge of a form nearly perpendicular to the ground or slightly undercut-looking, then soften. A crisp edge makes a shadow line that reads as depth.
- Compress depth progressively: a forearm going back in space flattens toward the ground.
- Model the background as a deliberate surface (smooth, textured, or with perspective lines).

## For moulding
- **No undercuts in low relief** if you want a simple one-piece mould; keep a slight draft (walls leaning outward).
- High relief with undercuts needs flexible (silicone) moulds or piece moulds.
- Medals: work at large size (e.g. 20-30 cm) and reduce by pantograph, 3D scan or reducing machine.

## Gotchas
- Working flat on a table, then hanging it: the lighting changes everything. Model vertical.
- Uniform depth everywhere reads flat; vary the projection.

## Verify
- [ ] Viewed under display lighting, planes separate clearly.
- [ ] No unintended undercuts for the chosen mould type.
