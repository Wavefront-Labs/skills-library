# Lost-wax bronze casting process

Most artists use a **foundry** for the pour, but you must understand every step to prepare work and judge quality.

## Stages
1. **Original** in clay, plaster, wax, wood or 3D print.
2. **Mould**: flexible silicone with a mother mould so many waxes can be pulled.
3. **Wax positive**: slush or brush hot casting wax into the mould to a wall of about 3-6 mm (thicker for large work), with a core space inside; or 3D-print in castable resin/PLA for burnout.
4. **Wax chasing**: remove seam lines, fix bubbles, sign and number the wax with a heated tool. Every mark on the wax will be in the bronze.
5. **Spruing (gating)**: attach wax rods - a pouring cup, main runners, gates feeding thick areas, and vents at high points - so metal flows downhill without trapping air. A poor sprue layout gives misruns and porosity.
6. **Investment**:
   - **Ceramic shell**: dip in slurry (colloidal silica + fine refractory), stucco with grit, dry, repeat 7-10 coats.
   - **Solid investment** (plaster-silica) poured round the wax in a flask - older method, still used for small work.
   - Core: the inside of hollow waxes is filled with investment; **core pins** (bronze or steel rods) hold the core in place when the wax is gone.
7. **Burnout (dewax)**: flash-fire shells in a kiln or autoclave; wax melts out ("lost"); shells are then fired to around 800-1000 C.
8. **Pour**: silicon bronze (common in art foundries) or traditional tin bronze at roughly 1100-1200 C into the hot shell.
9. **Devest**: break off shell (hammer, sandblast), remove core.
10. **Metal chasing**: cut off sprues, grind gates flush, weld sectioned pieces, fix porosity, re-create texture with chasing tools, then patina.

## Working with a foundry
- Ask: alloy, minimum/maximum wall thickness, whether they chase waxes, sectioning plan, edition numbering, patina, and lead times.
- Large works are cut into sections at the wax stage and welded after casting.
- Provide a stable original with any armature removable; mark the edition plan.

## Gotchas
- **Thin wax** (under ~3 mm) misruns; **very thick** areas shrink and porosity forms.
- **Undercut cores** without pins shift and leave holes or blowouts.
- Unchased seam lines in the wax become hours of metal grinding.

## Verify
- [ ] Wall thickness even and within the foundry's range.
- [ ] Waxes chased and signed before investing.
- [ ] Sprue plan agreed with the foundry.
