# Pointing machine and enlarging from a maquette

Pointing copies a model (usually plaster) into stone or another material by transferring measured points, so carving removes stone down to known depths.

## Same-size pointing (pointing machine)
1. Fix **three reference points** (crosses or metal pins) on the plaster model and the matching three on the block, arranged as a triangle around the work.
2. The pointing machine hangs from the reference points; its adjustable arm carries a **needle** on a sliding rod.
3. Set the needle to touch a point on the model (a pencil cross on a high point). Lock the arm.
4. Move the machine to the block; **drill** in along the needle direction until the needle, slid forward, reaches the same setting. Leave a few millimetres of "skin" over the point.
5. Repeat point by point - high points first, then hundreds of points over the surface (traditionally marked as crosses on the model). Carvers rough to the points with point chisel and claw, then finish freehand.

## Enlarging
- **Proportional callipers / dividers**: set the ratio (e.g. 1:3); measure on the maquette, read the enlargement on the long end. Works for key points; fill between by eye.
- **Three-point (triangulation) method**: from three fixed points on a base, measure distances to each feature point and scale them; a frame or plumb lines from a grid on a board help for large clay enlargements.
- **Frame and plumb bob**: a scaled frame over maquette and enlargement, with plumb lines at grid positions; measure depth from the frame. Common for big clay figures over an armature.
- **3D scan + CNC or 3D print**: scan the maquette, scale in software, mill a foam or stone rough, then the sculptor reworks it. Standard in modern foundries and stone yards.

## Gotchas
- Scaling multiplies errors: a 2 mm error on a maquette is 1 cm at 5x.
- Leave a margin over each point for finishing; you can't put stone back.
- Enlargements look different at scale - details and stance usually need revising, not just magnifying.

## Verify
- [ ] Reference points identical on model and block.
- [ ] High points located first.
- [ ] Margin left at every point for finishing.
