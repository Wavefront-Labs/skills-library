# Chasing and finishing casts

"Chasing" is reworking the surface of a cast so it matches the original: removing every trace of moulding and casting.

## Metal (bronze, aluminium)
1. **Cut sprues and gates** with a cut-off wheel or bandsaw, leaving a few millimetres proud.
2. **Grind flush** with a die grinder and carbide burrs or flap discs, working with the form, never flattening a curve.
3. **Seam lines** (flashing) from mould joins: grind and file away.
4. **Porosity and pits**: drill out, TIG weld with matching rod (silicon bronze rod for silicon bronze), or peen small pits closed.
5. **Re-texture**: match the surrounding surface using chasing tools (punches, matting tools, gravers) and a chasing hammer, burrs, or rotary files. Blend welded joins the same way.
6. **Final surface**: sandblast (bead blast) for an even tooth before hot patina, or polish to the required finish.

## Plaster, resin, Jesmonite
- Trim seams while the cast is "green" (fresh, slightly soft) with a scalpel, riffler files and surform.
- Fill pinholes: plaster with a thin plaster slurry; resin with resin + filler or a polyester filler; Jesmonite with Jesmonite paste.
- Sand from medium to fine grit (wet for resin); match tool texture if the original had one.

## Tools
- Riffler files (curved ends), needle files, scalpels, burnishers, chasing punches.
- Die grinder, flap wheels, carbide burrs, cut-off discs.
- PPE: eye protection, dust mask/respirator, hearing protection; bronze dust is harmful and resin dust is irritant.

## Gotchas
- **Over-grinding** removes the artist's surface: compare constantly with the original or photos.
- **Shiny polished patches** around repairs show through a patina - blast the whole piece evenly.
- Welds in the wrong alloy patinate a different colour.

## Verify
- [ ] No seam lines, sprue stubs or pits visible under raking light.
- [ ] Texture consistent across repairs.
- [ ] Even surface prep before patina or paint.
