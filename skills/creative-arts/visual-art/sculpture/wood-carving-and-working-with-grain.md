# Wood carving and working with grain

## Choose the wood
- **Lime (linden) / basswood**: fine even grain, soft, holds detail - the classic carving wood.
- **Walnut, cherry, oak, mahogany**: harder, richer colour; oak is coarse and splits.
- **Seasoned** (dry) wood: stable, won't crack further. **Green** wood carves easily but checks (cracks) as it dries - rough out, then dry slowly, or bore out the pith.
- Avoid the **pith** (centre of the log) in a solid piece; most radial cracks start there.

## Read the grain
- Wood cuts cleanly **downhill** with the grain - from high to low along the fibres. Cutting uphill tears and splits ahead of the tool.
- Rule: on a curve, carve from the high point down on each side, changing direction where the grain changes.
- End grain cuts best with a very sharp tool and a slicing (skewing) motion.
- Thin parts across the grain (fingers, beaks) snap - orient the design so long thin elements run along the grain.

## Tools and sequence
- **Gouges** by sweep (1 = flat chisel, up to 11 = deep U) and width; V-tool (parting tool) for lines.
- Rough out with large deep gouges and a mallet, or saw and rasp; refine with shallower sweeps; finish with flat and skew chisels, rifflers and scrapers.
- Hold the work: a carver's screw into the base through the bench, a vice, or a bench hook - never in your hand when using a mallet.
- Keep tools razor-sharp (see the sharpening skill).

## Finishing
- Leave tool marks as texture, or scrape and sand through grits (150 to 320).
- Oil (Danish, linseed), wax, or shellac then wax. Outdoor work needs durable species and penetrating oils, re-applied.

## Gotchas
- **Splitting off a section** by levering with the gouge: cut, don't pry.
- **Cracking** from rapid drying: keep green carvings in a paper bag or coat end grain with wax.
- **Cuts toward your hand**: keep both hands behind the cutting edge.

## Verify
- [ ] Design long thin elements oriented with the grain.
- [ ] Every cut leaves a clean surface (otherwise reverse direction).
- [ ] Work secured, hands behind the edge.
