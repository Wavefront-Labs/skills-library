# Hollowing and firing clay sculpture

Solid clay thicker than about 2-3 cm traps water and air and can crack or burst in the kiln. Model solid (freely), then hollow.

## Hollowing
1. Let the piece reach **leather-hard**: firm enough to hold its shape when cut, still damp enough to join.
2. Cut it into sections with a taut wire or fine knife along places that are easy to rejoin (e.g. around the head behind the ears, or horizontally through a torso). Mark registration lines across each cut first.
3. Scoop out with loop tools to an **even wall**, typically 1-2 cm for a life-size head (follow the clay body's guidance). Leave internal ribs in large spans if needed.
4. Rejoin: score both edges, apply slip (or magic water), press together, then compress and blend the seam inside and out; add a coil inside the seam.
5. Make **vent holes** so every closed cavity can breathe (a pencil-sized hole in a hidden place, e.g. under the chin or the base).

## Drying
- Dry **slowly and evenly**: loosely cover with plastic for days or weeks, uncovering gradually. Thin parts (ears, fingers) dry first - wrap them.
- Bone-dry means the piece feels room temperature, not cool, to the cheek.

## Firing (bisque)
- **Candle** first: hold at about 80-95 C for many hours (overnight or longer for thick work) with the kiln vented to drive off the last water.
- Climb slowly (e.g. 50-60 C/hour) through to around 600 C (quartz inversion near 573 C and burnout), then faster to bisque temperature for the body.
- Cool naturally; don't open until below about 100 C.
- Glaze or stain and refire if wanted; terracotta is often left unglazed or waxed.

## Gotchas
- **Air pockets** from adding clay in lumps explode; wedge clay well and compress additions.
- **Uneven wall thickness** cracks at the transition; aim for consistency.
- **Bases**: a closed hollow base needs a vent; large flat bottoms can crack - set on grog or sand in the kiln.
- Ask the kiln owner for its schedule and maximum piece size.

## Verify
- [ ] Walls of even thickness, all cavities vented.
- [ ] Bone-dry before firing; candle held.
- [ ] Slow ramp through 573 C.
