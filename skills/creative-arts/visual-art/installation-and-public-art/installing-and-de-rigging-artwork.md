# Installing and de-rigging artwork

**Goal:** install without damage and leave the venue as found.

## Before
1. **Condition report** each work on arrival, with photographs, noting existing damage. Both parties sign.
2. **Handling:** clean hands or nitrile gloves for most objects (cotton gloves can snag and reduce grip; follow the conservator's advice for specific items), two people for large pieces, never carry by frames or fragile edges, and use trolleys and padding.
3. **Plan hanging:** centre height commonly cited as around 57 inches (about 145 cm) to the work's visual centre; follow the curator's plan.

## Hanging and fixing
- **French cleat:** two interlocking angled strips, one on the wall and one on the work; strong and levelling.
- **Z-clips and D-rings:** for lighter panels; use two hangers with rated wall anchors.
- **Picture wire or cable systems:** use wire rated well above the weight, secured properly.
- Use **wall anchors rated** for the wall type (stud, masonry, drywall) and weight, with a safety factor.
- **Overhead rigging:** only by a qualified rigger; use rated hardware (working load limit marked), proper knots or fittings, and a **secondary safety line**. Check the structure's load capacity.

## De-rigging
1. Reverse the install: remove work, photograph condition again, and compare to the report.
2. Pack with the right materials (acid-free tissue, padding, crates) and label.
3. **Make good** the venue: fill holes, repaint to match, remove tape residue, clear waste, and return borrowed equipment.

## Gotchas
- Never trust a single fixing for heavy items.
- Spirit levels and laser levels are quicker than measuring by eye.
- Loan agreements may require specific handling; read them.

## Verify
- [ ] Every work has a condition report before and after.
- [ ] Fixings are rated, and the venue signs off the make-good.
