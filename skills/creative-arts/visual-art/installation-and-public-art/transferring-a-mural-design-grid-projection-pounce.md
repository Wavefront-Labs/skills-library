# Transferring a mural design to the wall

**Goal:** get the sketch to full scale accurately. Pick the method by wall size, access and light.

## Grid
1. Draw a grid over the sketch (for example 5 cm squares) and number the rows and columns.
2. Snap a chalk line to make a matching, larger grid on the wall (for a 2 m by 1 m wall using 20 cm squares), and copy the contents of each square.
3. A **doodle grid** is a variation: draw an irregular network of lines over the sketch, mark the points where lines meet or cross the sketch's edges, and measure those points on the wall. It suits organic or irregular compositions and avoids the mechanical look of squares.

## Projection
1. Use a bright projector (high lumens for daylight; dusk or dark gives better results). Place it square-on to the wall, level and at a distance that gives the right image size.
2. Correct **keystone** distortion by aligning the projector, not just by digital correction, or you will skew proportions.
3. Trace key outlines with a chalk, pencil or water-based paint marker; avoid permanent lines that show through light paint.
4. Mark registration points and re-project if the projector moves.

## Pounce
1. Prick holes along the outlines of a full-size paper cartoon with a pounce wheel or pin.
2. Tape the cartoon to the wall and tap a pounce bag of chalk or charcoal dust along the holes to leave dotted lines. The method is the same as the fresco-era "spolvero".
3. Join the dots with chalk or a paint marker.

## Gotchas
- Wind and sun make projection hard to see; check conditions.
- Wall surfaces are rarely flat, so measure from reference points and not only from the edges.
- Chalk lines wash off in rain; paint over them promptly.

## Verify
- [ ] Key measurements on the wall match the scaled drawing.
- [ ] Marker or chalk lines are visible but will not show through the final paint.
