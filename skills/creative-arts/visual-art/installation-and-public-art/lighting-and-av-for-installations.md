# Lighting and AV for installations

**Goal:** show the work as intended, reliably, and without damaging it.

## Lighting
- **Lux levels:** light-sensitive items (paper, textiles, watercolour, some dyes) are commonly limited to about 50 lux in museum practice; moderately sensitive works such as oil and acrylic paintings are often shown at roughly 150 to 200 lux. These are common guideline figures; follow the lender or conservator's instructions.
- **LED fixtures** with a high colour rendering index (CRI 90 or above) show colours well; check for flicker on camera. Use spots with barn doors or snoots to shape light, and avoid washing out projections with ambient light.
- Use **dimmers or DMX controllers** for scenes; test cabling and addressing early.
- Eliminate UV; use UV-filtering where daylight or UV-emitting lamps are present.

## Projection
- Choose **lumens** to suit ambient light; gallery rooms with blackout need less, daylight spaces much more. Consider throw ratio, lens shift and resolution.
- **Projection mapping:** align content to surfaces using software such as MadMapper, Resolume Arena, TouchDesigner or HeavyM. Use warping and masking, and calibrate after projector placement.
- Watch heat and fan noise; ventilate projectors and check mounting is safe.

## Playback and sound
- Use reliable **media players** (dedicated hardware or a robust computer with auto-start) set to loop and restart after power cuts; label cables; keep a spare.
- **Audio:** use directional speakers to limit bleed, set levels with the venue, and protect visitors' hearing; check local noise rules.

## Power and cables
- Plan sockets, dedicated circuits and cable runs; cover cables to avoid trips; use cable protection and tag every device with an inventory.

## Gotchas
- Lamp heat can damage nearby materials; measure.
- Software updates mid-show break playback; freeze the setup.
- Projector bulbs and LEDs degrade; plan maintenance.

## Verify
- [ ] Lux meter readings match the work's limit.
- [ ] The system restarts unattended after a power cut.
