# Anti-graffiti and UV protective coatings for murals

**Goal:** protect a finished mural from sun, weather and vandalism, while keeping it maintainable.

## Two kinds of anti-graffiti coating
- **Sacrificial:** a clear wax or polymer film is removed along with the graffiti (typically by hot-water washing or a manufacturer's cleaner) and then reapplied. The mural itself is spared; ongoing cost and visible sheen are the downsides.
- **Permanent (semi-permanent):** hard, low-surface-energy coatings (some polyurethane or fluoropolymer systems) let graffiti be cleaned off with solvents or washing without reapplication. They are less reversible, so test carefully on the paint and wall before committing to the whole wall.

## UV protection
- Exposure to sunlight fades pigments and degrades binders. Use an exterior varnish formulated with UV light stabilisers (UVLS) and ultraviolet absorbers. Some paint makers offer mineral spirits-based and water-based acrylic varnishes with UVLS; read the technical data sheet and the application limits (for instance the maker's minimum temperatures and number of coats).
- A commonly followed conservation principle: separate the paint from the varnish with an isolation or barrier coat so the varnish can be replaced without damaging the painting. Check your coating system supports this.

## Application
1. Let the paint cure fully; the data sheet gives the minimum wait, often days or weeks.
2. Test a small area for compatibility, gloss and adhesion; check it does not alter colours or cause blushing.
3. Apply in dry, mild weather, out of direct sun, with a roller or low-pressure spray, in thin even coats.
4. Record the product, batch and date for future maintenance.

## Safety
- Solvent-based coatings and two-component products (for example isocyanates) are hazardous; use the respirator and gloves the data sheet specifies, ensure ventilation, and avoid ignition sources. Cartridges must match the hazard.
- Dispose of waste and rags as required.

## Gotchas
- Gloss varnish can create glare; choose a satin or matt finish where visibility matters.
- Wrong solvent cleaners can attack the paint beneath; test cleaners first.
- Coatings do not repair a failing substrate.

- [ ] A test patch shows no colour change after cure and cleans graffiti marker.
