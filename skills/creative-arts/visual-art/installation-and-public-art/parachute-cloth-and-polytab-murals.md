# Parachute cloth and polytab murals

**Goal:** paint a mural off site on cloth, then adhere it to a wall (the marouflage approach) when direct painting is impractical. The terminology is inconsistent: "parachute cloth" usually means lightweight nylon fabric, while "polytab" is a name used in some mural programmes for a polyester cloth. Confirm the exact material, weight and recommended adhesive with your supplier and programme; the details below are general practice, not a specification.

## Painting the cloth
1. Cut the cloth to size with extra margin. Hang or tack it flat and taut (on a frame or wall), not stretched so far that it distorts.
2. Prime or apply a thin coat of acrylic medium or gesso if the fabric is slick; test that paint adheres and flexes without cracking when rolled up.
3. Transfer the design (grid, projection) and paint with acrylic paints, using outdoor-rated, lightfast pigments.
4. Seal with a UV-protective varnish (see `creative-arts.visual-art.installation-and-public-art.anti-graffiti-and-uv-coatings-for-murals`) as required.
5. Roll face-out onto a large tube for transport; do not fold.

## Installing
1. Prepare the wall as for a painted mural (clean, sound and primed; `creative-arts.visual-art.installation-and-public-art.mural-wall-assessment-and-surface-preparation`).
2. Mark the wall and set up access safely (`creative-arts.visual-art.installation-and-public-art.access-equipment-and-working-at-height`).
3. Apply adhesive recommended by your supplier to the wall in short working sections, press the cloth into it, smooth air out with a squeegee or roller, and trim the edges. Overlap or butt seams as specified.
4. Seal edges and seams, and touch up paint.

## Gotchas
- Adhesive type matters; wrong glues peel or stain through light cloth. Test on a sample on the same wall type.
- Textured walls such as rough brick are hard for cloth to follow.
- Works installed on cloth can be removed and re-installed, but only with the right adhesive and technique; confirm with the supplier.

## Verify
- [ ] A sample piece sticks and flexes on a sample of the actual wall.
- [ ] Seams and edges are flat and sealed.
