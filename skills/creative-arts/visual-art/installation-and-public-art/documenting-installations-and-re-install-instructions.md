# Documenting installations and re-install instructions

**Goal:** let a future technician or curator rebuild the work without you.

## Contents of an installation record
1. **Photographs and video** of the installed work from planned viewpoints, plus close-ups of details, and the installation process.
2. **Plans, elevations and measurements** as built, with tolerances.
3. **Parts and materials list:** product names, suppliers, specifications, quantities.
4. **Equipment and software:** models, firmware and software versions, settings, backups of files, and the player hardware's configuration.
5. **Lighting and sound:** lux levels, colour temperature, volume, positions and angles.
6. **Step-by-step installation and de-installation instructions,** including tools needed, time and the number of people.
7. **Behaviour and variability:** what should change (for example a plant or a time-based loop) and what must stay fixed.
8. **Replaceable versus authentic parts:** which components can be substituted when obsolete (for example a particular model of monitor), and who must be consulted.
9. **Contact and approval:** who can sign off a re-installation, and how to reach the artist or estate.
10. **Artist interview** capturing intent, which materials matter, and the viewing experience; museums often use structured methods such as the Variable Media Questionnaire developed by the Guggenheim and the Variable Media Network.

## Storage
- Keep the record with the sale or loan paperwork and a **certificate of authenticity** where used. Store digital files in open formats with multiple backups and review them periodically.

## Gotchas
- Equipment becomes obsolete; record functional requirements, not just model numbers.
- Photos without scale or location notes are less helpful.
- Documentation updates should be dated and versioned.

## Verify
- [ ] Someone unfamiliar with the work could follow the instructions to re-install it.
- [ ] Digital files are backed up in two places.
