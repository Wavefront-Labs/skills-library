# Public art budgets and fees

**Goal:** a realistic budget that a commissioner can approve and you can deliver. Figures below are commonly cited rules of thumb; programmes and countries vary, so confirm them locally.

## First questions to the commissioner
- Is the stated figure the **all-in** budget (artist fee, fabrication, installation, insurance, permits, engineering, lighting, site works, documentation, maintenance reserve) or only the artist's share?
- Is it inclusive of **sales tax or VAT**, and who pays for site preparation, power, foundations and traffic management?
- How is it paid, and when (milestones; deposits; retention)?
- Many US cities and some other jurisdictions run **percent-for-art** programmes that set aside a small share of capital project costs (commonly in the low single digits of a percent); check the specific programme.

## Budget lines
1. **Artist fee** for design and project management. The proposal-writing skill in this folder cites 15-20 percent of the total as commonly quoted guidance; confirm what your region's arts council or public art network recommends.
2. **Design development and drawings,** models and renderings.
3. **Engineering and approvals** (structural engineer, foundations, planning and building permits, traffic management).
4. **Fabrication** by quote: materials, finishing, coatings, tooling; get at least two written quotes.
5. **Transport and installation:** crane, scaffold or lift hire, labour, site works and safety.
6. **Insurance:** public liability, professional indemnity and fabricator cover as required.
7. **Lighting and power** if used, with ongoing running costs.
8. **Documentation and signage,** photography, plaques, community events.
9. **Maintenance and conservation** plan and, if the commissioner requires, a maintenance fund.
10. **Contingency:** often around 10 percent, higher for novel techniques or complex sites.

## Payment structure
- Tie payments to milestones (contract signed, design approved, fabrication start, installation, completion) with a first payment on signing, and keep a record of each approval.

## Gotchas
- Quotes expire and prices move; state the validity of your budget.
- Underpricing your own time is the most common error; log real hours on past projects.
- Budget changes due to client scope changes should be handled through a written change order (see `creative-arts.visual-art.installation-and-public-art.commission-contracts-vara-and-moral-rights`).

## Verify
- [ ] Every line has a source (quote, estimate) and the total includes contingency and tax.
- [ ] The artist fee is visible and not buried in fabrication.
