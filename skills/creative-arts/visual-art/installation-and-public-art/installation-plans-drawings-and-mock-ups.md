# Installation plans, drawings and mock-ups

**Goal:** make an installation deliverable and buildable before arriving on site.

## Site survey
- Measure floor plan, ceiling heights, door widths, stair and lift sizes (for delivery), column positions, power outlets, lighting and fire equipment. Photograph the space and note wall finishes, flooring, daylight and noise.
- Ask the venue about floor load limits, ceiling fixing points, fire exits and the permitted use of nails, rigging or adhesives.

## Drawings
1. **Floor plan** at a convenient scale (commonly 1:50 or 1:20) showing the work, sight-lines, visitor routes and egress.
2. **Elevations and sections** with heights.
3. **Detail drawings** for fixings, joins and structures, with materials and fastener sizes.
4. **Electrical and AV plan:** power, data, positions, cable runs.
5. **Bill of materials and quantities** with suppliers and lead times.
6. A **schedule** for installation and a method statement (see `creative-arts.visual-art.installation-and-public-art.risk-assessment-for-public-installations`).

## Models and mock-ups
- **Scale models** (cardboard or digital, such as SketchUp) test composition and viewing routes.
- **Full-scale mock-up** of any risky element (a hanging structure, a unique joint, a projection) tests strength and appearance before the final build.
- Use a scale figure in the drawings to judge proportion.

## Gotchas
- Missing door or lift dimensions are the most common reason works do not arrive on site.
- Drawings without tolerances cause fit problems; note allowances.
- Show who is responsible for each task on the schedule.

## Verify
- [ ] Every element can pass through the venue's access route.
- [ ] The venue has approved the drawings.
