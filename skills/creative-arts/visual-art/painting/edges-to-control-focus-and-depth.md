# Edges to control focus and depth

**Goal:** guide the viewer's eye and create space through edge hierarchy, as in
the work of Sargent, Zorn and Velazquez.

## Principles
- The eye sees sharply only in a small central area; the periphery is soft. A
  painting that mimics this feels natural: **sharpest edges at the focal point**,
  softer edges away from it.
- Distance softens edges (atmosphere, smaller detail).
- Edge sharpness combined with value contrast is one of the strongest attractors.

## Method
1. Decide the focal point (see focal-point-and-eye-path).
2. Paint most edges soft or firm in the first passes.
3. Toward the end, **sharpen selectively** at the focal point: a few crisp edges
   where highest value contrast meets.
4. Lose edges deliberately in the periphery and in shadow areas.
5. Step back and check that nothing outside the focal area competes.

## Depth
- Near objects outside the focus (foreground grass) can be soft as well:
  painting is not a camera, but a soft foreground pushes attention into the middle ground.
- Distance: progressively softer silhouettes.

## Photographic clichés to avoid
- Uniform Gaussian blur or bokeh circles copied from photos do not look like
  human vision; vary softness with value and form instead.

## Gotchas
- Sharpening everything at the end "to finish": the focal point vanishes.
- A hard edge at the picture's corner pulling attention away.

## Verify
- [ ] The sharpest edges cluster at the focal point.
- [ ] Peripheral and distant edges are softer.
