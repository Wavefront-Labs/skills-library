# Acrylic mediums and gels

**Goal:** change acrylic paint's consistency, sheen and transparency without
weakening the film (Golden and Liquitex medium guides).

## The family
Mediums are essentially paint without pigment: the same acrylic polymer binder.
| Type | Viscosity | Uses |
|------|-----------|------|
| Fluid/polymer medium (gloss, matte, satin) | Liquid | Glazing, thinning while keeping binder, sealing, collage adhesive |
| Gel medium (soft, regular, heavy, extra heavy) | Paste | Extending paint, impasto, transparency, strong adhesive |
| Pastes (molding/modelling paste, light paste, fibre paste) | Thick, opaque | Texture, sculptural surfaces, drawing grounds |
| Special (glazing liquid, retarder, flow release, pouring medium) | Various | Open time, flow, pours |

## Sheen
- Gloss media: transparent, saturated darks.
- Matte media: contain matting agents, slightly cloudy in thick layers; darks
  lighten and look chalky if overused.
- Mix gloss and matte for satin; unify sheen with a final varnish.

## Water dilution limits
- Acrylic paint can be thinned with water, but heavy dilution (makers often cite
  beyond about 25 percent water on non-absorbent surfaces) weakens the binder and
  adhesion: thin with **medium** instead for glazes.
- On absorbent surfaces (raw canvas, paper), very watery washes are acceptable.

## Common recipes
- **Glaze**: a little paint in gloss or glazing medium.
- **Impasto**: heavy gel + paint, knife application.
- **Texture underlayer**: modelling paste on a rigid support (inflexible in thick
  layers; avoid on flexing canvas).
- **Collage adhesive**: soft gel (matte or gloss) on both surfaces.

## Gotchas
- Thick matte gel layers dry cloudy.
- Thick pastes on loose canvas crack.
- Mixing in oil products: acrylic will not accept oil.

## Verify
- [ ] Glazes thinned with medium rather than lots of water.
- [ ] Sheen chosen deliberately and unified at the end.
