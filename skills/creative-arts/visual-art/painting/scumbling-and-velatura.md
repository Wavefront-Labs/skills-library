# Scumbling and velatura

**Goal:** modify a dry layer with a thin, **lighter, semi-opaque** veil, making
colours cooler, hazier or more luminous without repainting them. A glaze is the
opposite: transparent and usually darker over light.

## Definitions
- **Scumble**: a light, opaque or semi-opaque colour scrubbed or dragged thinly
  over a darker dry layer so the underlayer shows through in broken patches.
- **Velatura**: a thin, even semi-opaque veil (often with white) laid over a
  passage; used to unify, cool and soften, as in skin turning into shadow or
  distant landscape. Some writers use the terms interchangeably.

## Optics
A thin light layer over dark scatters light and looks **cooler and bluer**
than the paint mixed thickly (turbid medium effect). Thin white over a dark
background reads blue-grey; over warm umber it reads cool. Use this deliberately
for atmosphere, bloom on fruit, smoke, haze and soft skin transitions.

## Paint and medium
- Semi-opaque mixtures: colour plus a little white, or naturally semi-opaque
  pigments (earths, cadmiums thinned).
- Little or no medium for a dry scumble; a small amount of fat medium for
  velatura so it flows thinly (follow fat over lean: more oil than the layer below).
- The underlayer must be **touch dry**, ideally dry enough not to lift.

## Application
1. **Dry scumble**: stiff hog brush, little paint, scrub or drag in circles so the
   paint catches on texture and leaves gaps.
2. **Velatura**: brush on thinly, then even it out with a soft brush or rub with
   a lint-free rag or the heel of the hand; wipe back highlights.
3. Assess when dry; repeat in several thin layers rather than one heavy one.
4. Finish with glazes for depth if needed (glaze darks, scumble lights).

## Gotchas
- Too thick: it becomes opaque repainting and the effect is lost.
- Over a wet underlayer: mixes into mud.
- Overuse makes the whole painting chalky; keep darks clear of white veils.

## Verify
- [ ] Underlayer dry before scumbling.
- [ ] Each veil thin enough that the underlayer still shows.
- [ ] Layer slightly fatter than the one beneath.
