# Alla prima still-life painting

**Goal:** a complete still-life painting in one sitting (2-4 hours), painted wet
into wet, with fresh brushwork and correct values and colour.

## Set-up
- 3-5 objects with variety of size, shape, colour and surface (one reflective,
  one matt, one cloth). Group them in overlapping clusters, not a row.
- **Shadow box** or a corner draped with neutral cloth to control reflected light.
  One light source (north window or a single high-CRI lamp, around 5000 K) from
  above and to one side, so each object has clear light, shadow and cast shadow.
- Easel next to the set-up, palette lit by the same light (or a matching lamp).
- Do thumbnails first: test format and crop.

## Materials
- Panel or canvas, toned with a thin mid-value wash (dry or wipe-tacky).
- Limited palette is fine: titanium white, a yellow, a red, ultramarine, burnt umber.
- Several hog brushes (filberts, flats), a palette knife, rags, minimal medium.
  Tube paint is enough; too much medium makes wet layers slide.

## Sequence
1. **Drawing**: brush drawing in thin umber/ultramarine: placement, big shapes,
   ellipses for rims. Keep it loose but correct.
2. **Block-in**: cover the whole canvas quickly with the big shapes of light and
   shadow in approximately correct value and colour, darks thin, lights thicker.
   Start with the darkest dark and the lightest light to set the range.
3. **Develop**: mix each passage on the palette to match (compare against
   neighbours, squint), then place it with one stroke. Work from shadow to light,
   thin to thick.
4. **Edges**: decide hard (focal, turning edges in light) and soft (shadows, far
   edges). Soften with a clean dry brush.
5. **Accents**: highlights and darkest accents last, thick and placed once.

## Wet-into-wet handling
- Lay strokes next to each other rather than over, to avoid mud.
- To paint over a wet passage, use a loaded brush and a light touch, or scrape
  back with the knife first.
- Wipe brushes on a rag between colours; keep a clean brush for lights.

## When to stop
When the big relationships are right and the focal area reads, stop. Adding
detail everywhere kills the alla prima freshness.

## Gotchas
- Mixing too little paint: you remix, never match, and the passage goes patchy.
- Highlights placed too early get dirtied by later strokes.
- Lamp changes colour temperature or someone opens a curtain: values shift.

## Verify
- [ ] Single controlled light source and a thumbnail first.
- [ ] Canvas covered in the block-in before any detail.
- [ ] Highlights and accents placed last, unmixed by other strokes.
