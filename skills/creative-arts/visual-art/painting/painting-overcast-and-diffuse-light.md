# Painting overcast and diffuse light

**Goal:** paint the quiet, even light of an overcast sky or a north window
convincingly (Gurney, *Color and Light*, "overcast light").

## Characteristics
- The source is the whole sky: huge and soft. Light comes mostly **from above**.
- **Upward-facing planes** are lightest; vertical planes mid; downward-facing
  planes darkest. Form is described by plane orientation, not by a terminator.
- **Shadows are soft** and low contrast; cast shadows are vague except right under objects.
- **Occlusion shadows** (where objects meet the ground, in crevices) become the
  main dark accents.
- **Local colour** is at its most saturated and readable, because no strong light
  bleaches it and no dark shadows hide it. Wet surfaces deepen colours further.
- Colour temperature is cool-neutral; lights cooler than shadows by comparison.

## Painting approach
1. Value plan with a **narrow range**: sky often the lightest; land masses close
   in value. Use local colour differences to separate shapes.
2. Paint plane changes by orientation: top planes lighter, sides darker.
3. Place occlusion accents firmly.
4. Keep edges soft overall; hard edges only where contrasts are real (wet road
   reflections, dark against sky).

## Gotchas
- Inventing sun shadows "for drama": contradicts the light.
- Too much contrast: the scene loses its quietness.
- Over-greying colours: overcast light is soft, not colourless.

## Verify
- [ ] No strong directional cast shadows.
- [ ] Upward planes lightest, downward planes darkest.
- [ ] Occlusion accents anchor objects.
