# Painting sunlight and skylight

**Goal:** make a painting feel like a sunny day. Gurney (*Color and Light*,
"direct sunlight", "skylight") describes outdoor light as two sources at once.

## The two sources
- **Sun (key)**: a small, very bright, warm-ish point source (yellow-white midday,
  orange at golden hour) producing hard-edged cast shadows.
- **Skylight (fill)**: the whole blue dome, a large, soft, cool source that lights
  everything the sun does not reach. Shadows are lit by it, so shadows on upward-facing
  planes look **blue/cool**; planes facing away from the sky (under eaves) get
  darker and warmer (bounce from the ground).

## Value structure
- Sunlight creates a **large value gap** between lit and shadow planes; keep the
  sunlit family high and compact and the shadows clearly darker. This gap is what
  reads as "sunny"; overcast scenes lack it.
- Sunlit whites are not paper white; reserve the very top values for the brightest
  planes facing the sun and highlights.

## Colour
- Lit planes: local colour shifted toward the sun's warmth.
- Shadows: local colour shifted toward sky blue, lower value, often slightly higher
  chroma than expected at the edge.
- Cast shadows on the ground: cool, with warm reflected light near objects (bounce).

## Time of day
- Midday: short, dark shadows under objects; overhead light flattens vertical planes.
- Golden hour: long shadows, strongly warm light, cooler and more violet shadows,
  backlit glows and rim lights.

## Gotchas
- Shadows too light (copying a phone photo's HDR) lose the sunlight effect.
- Grey shadows outdoors; they carry sky colour.
- Bright, saturated colour everywhere: sunlight bleaches the lights slightly.

## Verify
- [ ] Clear value gap between sunlit and shadow families.
- [ ] Upward-facing shadow planes cooler than downward-facing ones.
- [ ] Cast shadows have hard edges near the object.
