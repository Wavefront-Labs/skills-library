# Brush handling for edges and marks

**Goal:** control marks and edges with the brush rather than fighting it.

## Brush shapes
| Shape | Use |
|-------|-----|
| Flat / bright | Crisp edges, planes, blocking in; bright = shorter, stiffer |
| Filbert | Versatile: soft edges at the curve, sharp with the side; organic forms |
| Round | Lines, detail, drawing with paint |
| Fan | Softening, textures (sparingly) |
| Mop / blender | Soft blending of large areas |

## Hair types
- **Hog bristle**: stiff, carries thick oil paint, leaves brush marks; for
  block-in and impasto.
- **Synthetic** (many stiffnesses): good in acrylic and oil; holds shape.
- **Sable/mongoose**: soft, for smooth passages and glazes.

## Handling
1. Hold the brush further back on the handle for loose, larger strokes; closer for
   control.
2. **Load fully**: paint on both sides of the brush, mixed thoroughly; scrubbing
   with a dry brush creates thin, broken marks (use only on purpose).
3. **One stroke, one decision**: place a stroke and leave it. Re-stroking wipes
   texture and muddies.
4. Use the brush's shape: a flat's chisel edge for thin lines, flat side for planes.
5. **Soften** an edge by dragging a clean, dry (or lightly oiled) brush along the
   boundary once; wipe it between passes.

## Care
- Oils: wipe, rinse in solvent or oil (safflower), then wash with soap and water;
  reshape and dry flat or bristles up.
- Acrylics: never let paint dry in the brush; rinse often, wash with soap.
- Never stand brushes on their hairs in a jar.

## Gotchas
- Using tiny brushes early: fussy, tight painting. Use the biggest brush that fits
  the shape.
- Dirty brushes: mud in every stroke.

## Verify
- [ ] Brush size matched to the shape being painted.
- [ ] Strokes placed and left; edges softened with clean brushes.
