# Hard, soft and lost edges

**Goal:** make each edge describe what is happening at that boundary
(Gurney, *Color and Light*, "edges"; atelier teaching).

## Edge types
| Edge | Looks like | Means |
|------|-----------|-------|
| Hard | Abrupt change | Sharp corner, overlap of distant forms, contrasting materials, cast shadow near its object |
| Firm | Slight transition | Defined but rounded form |
| Soft | Gradual blend | Turning form (terminator on round form), out-of-focus area |
| Lost | No visible boundary | Two areas of the same value and colour merging |

## Edge rules from light and form
- **Terminator**: soft on round forms, hard on angular forms.
- **Cast shadows**: hard near the casting object, softer further away; softer
  under large/diffuse light, harder under small sources.
- **Silhouettes**: edges against the background vary along their length; where
  values are similar, lose them.
- **Highlights**: sharp on shiny surfaces, soft on matt.

## Painting edges
- Paint the two adjacent areas, then decide the edge: leave it hard, drag a clean
  dry brush across to soften, or paint an intermediate value between.
- Lose an edge by painting both sides with the same mixture across the boundary.
- In oils, soften wet-into-wet; in acrylic, work quickly or scumble a transition.

## Checking
Squint: edges that disappear when squinting should be soft or lost; edges that
remain sharp should be hard.

## Gotchas
- All edges hard: cut-out, sticker look.
- All edges soft: blurry, no structure.
- Softening with a dirty brush: muddy halo.

## Verify
- [ ] All four edge types used deliberately.
- [ ] Edge choices follow form and light, not habit.
