# Isolation coat and varnishing acrylics

**Goal:** protect an acrylic painting with a removable varnish that can be
cleaned off by a conservator later, without dissolving or clouding the paint
(Golden technical bulletin on isolation coats).

## Why an isolation coat
- Acrylic paint films are porous and soft; varnish removers can reach the paint.
- An isolation coat (a clear, permanent acrylic layer) seals the paint, evens out
  sheen and absorbency, and acts as a barrier when the removable varnish is later
  taken off.

## Isolation coat (Golden recommendation)
1. Painting fully dry (acrylics: several days; thick areas longer).
2. Mix **Soft Gel Gloss** 2 parts to 1 part water (by volume); stir gently
   and let bubbles settle (or use a dedicated product like GAC 500 or
   ready-made isolation mixtures).
3. Apply with a wide soft brush in one thin, even coat, painting flat; fix
   drips immediately. Matte surfaces may need two coats.
4. Dry at least 24 hours.

## Varnish
- **Removable varnishes**: mineral-spirit-soluble acrylics with UV light
  stabilisers (e.g. Golden MSA Varnish, Polymer Varnish (ammonia-removable)).
- **Sheen**: gloss (most saturated), satin, matte (matting agents; thick coats
  can fog dark areas). Mix gloss and matte for satin.
- Apply 2-3 thin coats by brush or spray, letting each dry per maker; brush in
  one direction, alternate direction per coat.

## Problems
| Problem | Cause |
|---------|-------|
| Foggy darks | Too-thick matte varnish; water-based varnish over cool/humid conditions |
| Streaks | Overbrushing, uneven sheen; varnish too thick |
| Bubbles | Shaking the jar; brush too fast |

## Gotchas
- Varnishing a still-curing painting: traps moisture, clouding.
- Using oil varnish (dammar) on acrylic: not recommended.

## Verify
- [ ] Isolation coat dry and even before varnish.
- [ ] Removable varnish recorded on the back (product, date).
- [ ] Uniform sheen, no fogging.
