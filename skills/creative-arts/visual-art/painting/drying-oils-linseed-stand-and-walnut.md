# Drying oils: linseed, stand and walnut

**Goal:** pick the right oil for binding, mediums and specific layers, knowing
how each affects drying, film strength, flow and yellowing (Mayer, *Artist's
Handbook*, oils chapter).

## How they dry
Drying oils cure by **oxidation and polymerisation**, taking oxygen from the air
over days to months. They never evaporate. More oil means a more flexible but
slower, more yellow-prone film.

## The oils
| Oil | Drying | Film | Yellowing | Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Refined linseed** | Medium (2-5 days touch dry) | Strong, flexible | Moderate | General medium, most tube colours |
| **Cold-pressed linseed** | Faster than refined | Strongest | Moderate | Grinding paint, quality mediums |
| **Stand oil** (heat-polymerised linseed, no oxygen) | Slow | Tough, levelling, glossy | Less than raw linseed | Glazing media, smooth enamel-like passages; always thinned |
| **Sun-thickened oil** | Faster, thick | Glossy | Some | Adds body and speeds drying |
| **Walnut oil** | Slower than linseed | Slightly weaker | Low | Whites and pale colours, solvent-free cleaning |
| **Safflower / poppy** | Slow | Weaker, can crack in thick layers | Lowest | Whites only; avoid in underlayers |

## Practical use
- **Lean underlayers**: little added oil; solvent-thinned or alkyd.
- **Upper layers**: add oil in small increments (fat over lean). A classic
  medium is stand oil thinned with solvent (e.g. 1 part stand oil to 2-3 parts
  odourless mineral spirits), plus optional alkyd for speed.
- Do not exceed what the paint needs: too much oil causes **wrinkling**, sagging
  and darker yellowing. A few drops per pile of paint is usually enough.
- Yellowing of linseed is worst in the dark; paintings partly bleach back in light.
- Store oils in full, closed bottles; skinned or gummy oil has partly polymerised.

## Safety
**Oily rags can self-heat and ignite** as the oil oxidises. Spread them flat to
dry outdoors, or store in a sealed metal container with water, and dispose
per local rules. This applies to all drying oils, not just solvents.

## Gotchas
- Poppy or safflower in lower layers under linseed upper layers: slow-under-fast,
  risk of cracking.
- Stand oil used neat: too viscous, wrinkles and stays tacky.
- Kitchen walnut oil: may contain additives or antioxidants; use art-grade.

## Verify
- [ ] Oil chosen for the layer and colour (walnut/safflower for whites only).
- [ ] Oil content increases layer by layer and stays modest.
- [ ] Oily rags handled to prevent spontaneous combustion.
