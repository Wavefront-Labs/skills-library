# Impasto and palette knife painting

**Goal:** expressive, textured paint applied thickly with brush or knife,
staying clean and stable.

## Knives
- **Palette knife** (straight or slightly cranked, blunt): for mixing.
- **Painting knife** (trowel-shaped, cranked handle, flexible thin blade):
  pear/diamond shapes in many sizes, for applying paint.

## Technique
1. Mix thoroughly on the palette; impasto shows every streak.
2. Pick up paint on the underside of the blade near the tip.
3. Lay it with the knife nearly flat, pressing and sliding; lift cleanly.
   Use the edge for lines, the tip for small dabs, flat drag for broken colour
   over dry paint.
4. **Wipe the knife** between colours: knife painting stays crisp only if clean.
5. Place colour notes side by side; avoid going back over wet areas (mud).

## Impasto mediums
- Oil: cold wax medium, Gamblin Cold Wax, alkyd impasto mediums (Liquin Impasto,
  Galkyd gel) add body and speed drying.
- Acrylic: heavy gel (gloss or matte), modelling paste for texture under colour.

## Drying and stability (oil)
- Thick oil paint dries from the surface down and takes weeks to months; varnish
  only when fully dry (see final varnish skill).
- Keep impasto to the **final layers** (thick over thin, see fat-over-lean).
- Very thick paint with lots of oil can wrinkle; alkyd or wax mediums reduce this.
- Rigid supports (panels) reduce cracking of heavy impasto vs flexing canvas.

## Gotchas
- Buttery colours mixed on canvas by repeated knife strokes: grey mud.
- Impasto in the underpainting: later layers crack.
- Using expensive pigments by the kilo: buy large tubes of the main colours.

## Verify
- [ ] Knife wiped between colours; notes clean.
- [ ] Impasto only in top layers.
- [ ] Drying time planned before varnish or shipping.
