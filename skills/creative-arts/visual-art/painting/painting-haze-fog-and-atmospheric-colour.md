# Painting haze, fog and atmospheric colour

**Goal:** paint the air itself (Gurney, *Color and Light*, "atmosphere" chapters:
sky colour, aerial perspective, fog, light beams).

## Sky gradation
- A clear sky is **darker and more saturated overhead**, lighter and paler near the
  horizon (light passes through more air there). Away from the sun it is deeper blue;
  near the sun, lighter and warmer.
- Paint a gradient, not a flat blue.

## Haze and fog
- Fog/mist scatters light: everything moves toward the **colour and value of the
  fog** with distance, much faster than in clear air. In thick fog, objects a short
  distance away become flat silhouettes.
- Value range compresses; darks lighten first. Shapes simplify to silhouettes.
- Fog is often light and cool grey by day, but lit fog at night or sunrise takes
  the light's colour.

## Atmospheric colour shifts
- Clear air: distance shifts blue (Rayleigh scattering).
- Dust, smoke, pollution: distance shifts yellow-grey or brown; sunsets redder.
- Golden hour and sunset: the atmosphere itself glows warm near the sun.

## Light beams
- Visible rays (crepuscular rays, light through a window in a dusty room) appear
  only when the air contains particles and the background is dark. Paint them as
  slightly lighter, slightly warmer veils with soft edges that widen away from
  the source; do not make them brighter than the source area.

## Gotchas
- Dark, sharp objects in fog.
- Blue distance in a dusty desert scene.
- Rays painted as bright opaque stripes.

## Verify
- [ ] Sky graded overhead to horizon.
- [ ] Distant forms shift toward the air's value and colour.
- [ ] Beams are subtle veils over darker backgrounds.
