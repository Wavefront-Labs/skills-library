# Mixing flesh tones

**Goal:** natural skin colour across all complexions, in light and shadow.

## Start with a limited palette
- **Zorn-type**: yellow ochre, cadmium red light (or vermilion), ivory black,
  titanium white. Mixes a wide range of skin, from pale to deep; black gives
  cool greys for transitions.
- Add burnt sienna, raw umber, alizarin/quinacridone rose and ultramarine for
  deeper or more chromatic complexions.

## Base mixtures (adjust to the sitter)
- **Light skin, light family**: white + yellow ochre + a little cad red; greyed
  with a touch of the complement or black in halftones.
- **Medium/olive skin**: more ochre and raw sienna/umber, less white; a touch of green-grey in halftones.
- **Deep skin**: burnt umber, burnt sienna, ultramarine, alizarin; white sparingly
  (it chalks deep skin quickly). Deep skin is often more reflective: highlights can
  be cool and bright.

## Colour zones of the face (Gurney)
- Forehead: more yellow/golden.
- Cheeks, nose, ears: redder (more blood vessels near the surface).
- Mouth to chin: cooler, bluish/greenish, especially on men with beard shadow.

## Light vs shadow
- Shadows on skin are often warmer and more saturated near the terminator
  (subsurface scattering), then cooler where reflected sky or cool light enters.
- Keep shadow values in the shadow family (see separating-light-and-shadow-families).

## Gotchas
- Chalky skin: too much white in halftones; lower value with colour instead.
- Orange skin: too much cadmium; balance with greys and cool notes.
- One "flesh" mix for the whole face: no zones, no life.

## Verify
- [ ] Face shows yellow/red/cool zones subtly.
- [ ] Halftones contain cool or grey transitions.
- [ ] Shadows hold value and colour temperature consistent with the light.
