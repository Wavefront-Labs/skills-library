# Pigment lightfastness and toxicity

**Goal:** paintings that do not fade, and a studio that does not poison you
(Mayer, *The Artist's Handbook*, pigments chapter; ASTM D4302 for oil paints).

## Reading the label
- **Colour Index Generic Name**: PB29 (ultramarine), PY35 (cadmium yellow),
  PR83 (alizarin crimson). The name on the tube can be marketing; the code tells
  you the pigment.
- **Lightfastness**:
  - ASTM D4302/D5098: **I** excellent, **II** very good (both suitable for
    permanent art), III fair (avoid), IV-V poor.
  - Blue Wool Scale (ISO 105-B, used by European makers): 7-8 excellent, 6 very
    good, below 6 not recommended.
  - Some makers use their own star ratings; look up the pigment.

## Known fugitive or problematic pigments
- Alizarin crimson (PR83): ASTM III in tints; use quinacridone or "permanent
  alizarin" replacements.
- Some lakes and dye-based colours (e.g. older magentas, many cheap student
  colours, fluorescent pigments): fade fast.
- Dyes in markers and many inks: often not lightfast.

## Testing yourself
Paint a strip of each colour (mass tone and a tint with white), cover half with
card, place in a sunny window 1-3 months, compare.

## Toxicity
- **Toxic pigments**: cadmium (PY35, PR108), cobalt (PB28, PV14), lead white
  (PW1), manganese (PB33/PV16), chrome (PY34), arsenic-based historic colours (avoid).
- Main routes of harm: **ingestion** (hand-to-mouth, eating while painting),
  **inhalation** of dry pigment or sanding dust, spraying.
- Practice: no eating/drinking/smoking while painting; nitrile gloves or barrier
  cream; wash hands; wet-wipe surfaces; never sand dry paint without extraction
  and a P100/FFP3 mask; handle dry pigment in a fume hood or with a respirator.
- Dispose of paint residues as hazardous waste per local rules, not down the drain.

## Gotchas
- "Hue" paints replacing toxic pigments may also differ in lightfastness; check.
- Lightfast ratings apply to the pigment in that binder; watercolour ratings differ.

## Verify
- [ ] All palette pigments rated ASTM I/II or Blue Wool 7-8.
- [ ] Studio hygiene rules in place for toxic pigments.
