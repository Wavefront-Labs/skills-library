# Palette layout and tube colour choice

**Goal:** a small, reliable set of paints laid out the same way every time, so
mixing becomes automatic.

## Choosing tube colours
- Prefer **single-pigment** paints: check the Colour Index pigment code on the
  tube (e.g. PB29 ultramarine, PY35 cadmium yellow, PR101 iron oxide red, PW6
  titanium white). Single pigments mix cleaner; convenience mixtures with 3+
  pigments make mud faster.
- Check **lightfastness** (ASTM I or II, or Blue Wool 7-8) and **opacity**
  (printed on many tubes).

## A versatile split-primary palette (oil or acrylic)
- Titanium white (PW6) (oil: or a titanium-zinc mix)
- Cadmium yellow lemon / light (PY35) or hansa yellow (PY3, PY74) - cool yellow
- Cadmium yellow medium/deep or yellow ochre (PY42/PY43) - warm yellow
- Cadmium red light (PR108) or pyrrole red (PR254/255) - warm red
- Quinacridone magenta/rose (PR122/PV19) or alizarin permanent - cool red
- Ultramarine blue (PB29) - warm blue
- Phthalo blue (PB15) or cerulean (PB35/36) - cool blue
- Burnt sienna (PBr7) and/or burnt umber - earths for neutralising
- Optional: viridian/phthalo green (PG18/PG7), ivory black (PBk9)

## Layout
- Place paints around the **edge** in a fixed order, e.g. white near your hand,
  then yellows, reds, earths, blues, greens, black (warm to cool). Same order
  every session.
- Squeeze generous piles; skimpy piles cause stingy, thin painting.
- Keep the centre clear for mixing; scrape it clean regularly.
- Palette colour: mid-grey or the canvas tone helps judge mixtures; a white
  palette makes mixtures look darker than they will on a toned canvas.

## Gotchas
- Twenty colours: you never learn any of them.
- Hue paints ("cadmium red hue") behave differently from the real pigment; know which you have.
- Toxic pigments (cadmium, cobalt, lead): no eating, wash hands, do not sand.

## Verify
- [ ] Every tube's pigment code and lightfastness known.
- [ ] Layout identical between sessions.
