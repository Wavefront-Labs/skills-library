# True gesso panel preparation

**Goal:** a brilliant white, absorbent, smooth chalk-glue ground on a rigid panel,
the traditional ground for egg tempera and water gilding (Cennini via
Thompson's *The Practice of Tempera Painting*; Mayer).

## Materials
- Rigid panel: MDF, birch ply or hardboard, sanded and degreased (true gesso is
  brittle and cracks on flexible supports).
- Rabbit-skin glue (RSG) granules; chalk (calcium carbonate, whiting) or gypsum
  (calcium sulphate, the Italian *gesso sottile*); optional titanium white for extra white.
- Double boiler, wide soft brush, fine sandpaper, steel scraper.

## Glue
- Soak glue overnight in cold water, roughly 1 part glue to 10-15 parts water by weight
  (makers vary; a stronger first "size" coat, weaker for gesso).
- Warm gently in a double boiler; never boil (it weakens).
- Size the panel (and its back and edges to balance stress) with warm glue; dry.

## Gesso
1. Sift chalk slowly into the warm glue until it is like thin cream (roughly equal
   volumes of chalk and glue solution; adjust). Stir gently to avoid bubbles.
2. Keep it warm in the double boiler throughout.
3. Apply the first coat rubbed in with fingers or a brush to bond.
4. Apply **8-12 thin coats**, each as soon as the previous loses its gloss (still
   damp); alternate brushing direction. All in one session for good inter-coat bond.
5. Dry slowly for several days, flat, away from heat.

## Finishing
- Sand progressively (220-600 grit) or scrape with a steel scraper for an ivory
  surface. A slightly damp cloth can polish.
- Test absorbency: a drop of water should soak in fast but evenly.

## Gotchas
- Gesso too strong (excess glue): cracks; too weak: powdery, rubs off.
- Pinholes from bubbles: rub in the first coats; skim later coats.
- Gesso heated too hot or kept too long: weakens.

## Verify
- [ ] No cracks, pinholes or powdering.
- [ ] Surface smooth and evenly absorbent.
