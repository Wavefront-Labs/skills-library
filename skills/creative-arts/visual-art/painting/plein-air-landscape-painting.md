# Plein air landscape painting

**Goal:** a small, truthful landscape painted on site, typically 20x25 to 30x40 cm
in 1-2 hours, capturing light before it changes.

## Kit
- Pochade box or lightweight easel with palette; tripod.
- Panels (gessoed hardboard/MDF or canvas panels), toned in advance.
- Limited palette (e.g. white, cad yellow light, cad red, alizarin or quinacridone,
  ultramarine, + a green or earth), solvent in a sealed container (or
  solvent-free: oil to clean brushes), rags, rubbish bag.
- Wet panel carrier; hat, umbrella or shade, sun cream, insect repellent.
- Neutral/dark clothes; white shirts reflect onto the canvas.

## Process
1. **Pick the motif** within 10 minutes. Look for a strong light/shadow pattern,
   a clear big-shape design, one centre of interest.
2. **Thumbnail** a value plan (2-5 minutes).
3. **Block in** with thin paint: horizon, big masses.
4. **Shadows first**: lock in shadow shapes in the first 20-30 minutes; the sun
   moves about 15 degrees an hour and shadows change fast.
5. **Big colour notes**: sky, ground, masses of trees as single shapes in correct value.
6. **Refine** focal area; leave peripheral areas suggestive.
7. Stop at 90-120 minutes; correct at home only sparingly, or not at all.

## Simplifying nature
- Group trees into masses; paint the sky holes in foliage last and smaller than
  they look.
- Greens: mix many greens from yellows and blues plus reds to grey them; avoid
  tube green straight.
- Sky is the light source: usually the lightest value plane; ground planes
  (flat) next; slanted planes (hills) next; vertical planes (trees) darkest.

## Gotchas
- Painting in direct sun on the canvas/palette: everything looks lighter, and the
  painting is too dark indoors. Shade the panel.
- Chasing light changes: commit to the first effect.

## Verify
- [ ] Shadows reflect the first 30 minutes' light.
- [ ] Planes ordered sky > flat > slanted > upright in value (usual daylight).
- [ ] Panel shaded while painting.
