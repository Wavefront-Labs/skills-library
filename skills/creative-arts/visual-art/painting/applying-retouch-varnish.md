# Applying retouch varnish

**Goal:** temporarily restore the depth and even sheen of a touch-dry oil painting
before it is ready for final varnish (Mayer, varnishes; manufacturer guidance).

## What it is
- A very thin, dilute varnish (traditionally dammar or ketone resin in solvent;
  many modern ones synthetic) that lets solvent evaporate and oxygen pass, so the
  paint can continue to cure underneath.
- Uses: even out **sunken, dull patches** for photography or exhibition; or as an
  intermediate layer to "oil out" before continuing painting (some painters prefer
  a thin medium rub instead).

## When
- Paint **touch dry** (days to weeks, depending on thickness).
- Some modern varnishes (Gamblin Gamvar) are marketed as applicable once the
  painting is touch dry and firm (thin films) because they are formulated not to
  trap the curing paint; follow the maker's stated times rather than assuming.

## Application
1. Dust the painting with a soft brush.
2. Spray (aerosol retouch) in light, even passes 30 cm away, or brush very thinly
   with a soft wide brush.
3. One thin coat; dull areas should come back to match glossy areas.
4. Ventilate; wear a respirator for spray.

## What it is not
- Not a permanent protective layer; final varnish still needed later.
- Not a fix for structural problems (wrinkling, cracking).

## Gotchas
- Heavy coats: trap solvent and can soften or crack paint later.
- Applying over tacky paint: dull, cloudy or sticky finish.

## Verify
- [ ] Painting touch dry before application.
- [ ] Sheen even; no pooling.
- [ ] Reminder noted for final varnish later (date on the back).
