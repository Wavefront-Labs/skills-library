# Making egg yolk tempera paint

**Goal:** fresh egg tempera paint for each session: pigment bound in egg yolk
(a natural emulsion), as in Cennini and Thompson's *The Practice of Tempera
Painting*.

## Pigment pastes
- Grind or mix dry pigment with distilled water to a thick paste (a muller on glass
  helps). Store in small jars covered with water. Some pigments need a drop of
  alcohol to wet (phthalos, some organics).

## The yolk medium
1. Crack a fresh egg; separate the yolk; roll it between palms or on a paper towel to
   remove all the white.
2. Hold the yolk sac by pinching its skin over a jar; pierce it with a knife so the
   liquid yolk runs out; discard the empty sac (it does not dissolve).
3. Dilute with distilled water, about equal volume (some painters use more water,
   e.g. 1:2), and stir. A drop of vinegar or clove oil is sometimes added as
   preservative; refrigerate and discard after a day or two.

## Mixing paint
- On the palette, mix roughly equal volumes of pigment paste and yolk medium; adjust
  per pigment (earths and lean pigments need more).
- **Test**: paint a stroke on glass or a gesso sample and let it dry: if it peels in a
  rubbery film, too much egg; if powdery, too little. A good film is hard and slightly
  satiny when buffed.

## Painting notes
- Paint is thinned with water; dries in seconds; built up in many thin strokes
  (see tempera-hatching-and-layering).
- Fully cures (harder, more water-resistant) over months.

## Gotchas
- Yolk sac fragments: lumpy paint.
- Old or warm medium: smells and grows mould.
- Too much yolk: cracking, yellowing.

## Verify
- [ ] Test film neither peels nor powders.
- [ ] Medium fresh (same day or refrigerated a day).
