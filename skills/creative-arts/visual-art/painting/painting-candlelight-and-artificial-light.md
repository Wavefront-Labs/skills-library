# Painting candlelight and artificial light

**Goal:** paint scenes lit by small, warm, local sources: candles, fires,
lamps, streetlights (Gurney, *Color and Light*, "candlelight and firelight",
"electric light"; see Georges de La Tour and Joseph Wright of Derby).

## Falloff
- Small sources follow the **inverse-square law**: brightness drops quickly with
  distance (twice as far = one quarter the light). Nearby faces are bright;
  objects a few metres away fall into darkness.
- Paint this as a **gradient across the scene**, not just shadows on objects.

## Colour
- Candle and firelight: very warm (about 1800-2000 K), a narrow orange-yellow
  gamut. Cool colours almost disappear; blues read as dark greys.
- Tungsten lamps: warm (about 2700-3200 K). Fluorescent and LED: vary; some
  greenish or cool.
- **Mixed light**: warm interior light against cool twilight or moonlight from a
  window is a powerful contrast; keep each source's zone consistent.

## The source itself
- A visible flame or bulb is the lightest value; surround it with a halo/glow
  (gradual values into the darkness), keeping the halo's colour warmer and less
  bright than the core.
- Light sources look whiter at the core, more saturated at the edges of the glow.

## Value plan (nocturnes)
- Most of the picture sits low (values 1-4); reserve 7-9 for the source and the
  nearest lit planes. A mid-key nocturne loses the effect.

## Gotchas
- Using the same light intensity everywhere: no falloff.
- Strong blue shadows in a candle scene: there is no blue source.
- Cast shadows from a nearby small source spread outward (radiate from the light).

## Verify
- [ ] Brightness falls off visibly with distance from the source.
- [ ] Gamut matches the source's temperature.
- [ ] Shadows radiate from the source position.
