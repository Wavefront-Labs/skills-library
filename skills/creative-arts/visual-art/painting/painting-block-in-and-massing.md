# Painting block-in and massing

**Goal:** establish the whole painting's drawing, value and colour structure in
big shapes before any detail (Schmid, *Alla Prima II*; atelier practice).

## Steps
1. **Drawing with paint**: thin, lean paint (e.g. burnt umber or ultramarine/burnt
   sienna thinned with solvent or a little medium) and a small brush. Place the big
   proportions, a few angles, and the shadow shapes. Correct by wiping with a rag.
2. **Mass the shadows**: fill all shadow areas with one or two thin, darkish
   mixtures, as connected shapes. Squint to keep them simple.
3. **Lay in the big lights and colours**: with bigger brushes, place the largest
   colour notes in the correct **value and temperature**: background, major
   planes of the subject, light family. Moderately thick paint.
4. **Key the picture**: place the darkest dark and the lightest light (not
   necessarily final) early, so every other note can be judged against them.
5. **Cover the canvas**: no white canvas left; white surroundings make all
   colours look too dark.
6. Step back, compare to the subject; fix big errors now (cheap) rather than later.

## Rules
- Biggest brush that fits the shape.
- No detail, no edges refined yet.
- Colour notes placed **side by side** rather than blended.

## Gotchas
- Thick paint in the drawing stage makes corrections muddy.
- Painting each object fully one by one: values drift apart.
- Leaving the background until last: edges and values around the subject are wrong.

## Verify
- [ ] Canvas fully covered with big notes.
- [ ] Shadows massed as connected shapes.
- [ ] Darkest dark and lightest light keyed.
