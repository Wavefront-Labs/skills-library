# Diagnosing sinking-in, blooming and cracking

**Goal:** recognise what has gone wrong with a painting's surface, why, and what
you (versus a conservator) should do (Mayer, defects of oil paintings).

## Defects table
| Defect | Looks like | Cause | Fix / prevention |
|--------|-----------|-------|------------------|
| Sinking-in | Dull, matte patches; darks look grey | Absorbent ground or under-layer pulled oil from paint | Oil out (rub on very thin medium, wipe off excess) or retouch varnish; size/ground properly next time |
| Varnish bloom | White/bluish haze in varnish | Moisture trapped (humid application, damp storage) | Sometimes clears with gentle warmth; often needs conservator to remove/replace varnish |
| Drying cracks (traction cracks) | Wide cracks, island-like, often in thick dark areas | Fast-drying layer over a slow one (lean over fat), bitumen, too much medium | Prevent: fat over lean; nothing simple to fix |
| Age cracks (craquelure) | Fine network through all layers | Brittleness with age, canvas movement, humidity | Normal over decades; stabilise environment |
| Wrinkling | Skin-like puckering | Thick oil-rich paint or too much oil/medium | Prevent; scrape back if fresh |
| Yellowing | Overall yellow, especially whites | Excess linseed oil, darkness, dammar varnish ageing | Expose to daylight (oil yellowing from dark storage partly reverses); conservator for varnish |
| Delamination / flaking | Paint lifting from ground | Incompatible layers (acrylic over oil), poor ground, damp | Conservator; keep flat; do not touch |

## Process
1. Look in raking light (light from the side) to see texture changes.
2. Note where: only some colours or layers? Over the whole painting?
3. Ask what changed: storage, humidity, varnish date.
4. Reversible surface issues (sinking-in) you can fix; structural ones go to a
   conservator (find through AIC or Icon directories).

## Gotchas
- Repainting over flaking paint: makes conservation harder.
- Using household products to "clean" a painting.

## Verify
- [ ] Defect identified with its probable cause.
- [ ] Structural issues referred, not self-treated.
