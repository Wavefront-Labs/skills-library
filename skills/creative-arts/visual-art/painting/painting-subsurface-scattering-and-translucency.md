# Painting subsurface scattering and translucency

**Goal:** make materials look like what they are by how light enters them
(Gurney, *Color and Light*, "subsurface scattering", "transmitted light").

## Subsurface scattering
- Light enters a translucent material (skin, wax, marble, grapes, milk), scatters
  inside, and exits nearby with the material's colour, more saturated.
- Visible as:
  - A **warm, saturated band** along the terminator on skin (the light that enters
    the lit side emerges just into the shadow).
  - **Softer terminators** than an opaque material would have.
  - Glowing thin parts (ear rims, nostrils, fingers) when lit from behind.
- Paint the terminator edge on skin with a slightly higher-chroma, warmer note,
  not just a darker grey.

## Transmitted light
- Thin materials (leaves, petals, fabric, paper lanterns) lit from behind glow with
  saturated colour, often brighter than their front-lit version.
- Backlit leaves: yellow-green glowing, much higher chroma than front-lit leaves,
  with veins as dark lines.
- Value: transmitted light can be bright but never as bright as the source.

## Sheen
- Cloth like velvet and satin, and hair, show **anisotropic** highlights: bands
  of light running across the fibres' direction. Velvet: lightest at the edges
  turning away (rim sheen), darker facing you.
- Hair: a highlight band across the direction of strands, broken by clumps.

## Gotchas
- Grey, dead terminators on skin: looks like plaster.
- Backlit leaves painted the same green as front-lit ones.

## Verify
- [ ] Skin terminators carry warmth and chroma.
- [ ] Backlit thin forms glow with raised chroma.
- [ ] Sheen bands follow fibre direction.
