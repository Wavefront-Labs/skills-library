# Managing acrylic drying and colour shift

**Goal:** work acrylics with control despite drying in minutes and darkening as
they dry (Golden and Liquitex technical guides).

## Why
- Acrylic dries by water evaporation; the polymer particles coalesce into a film.
- Wet acrylic binder is milky white; it turns clear on drying, so colours look
  lighter/cooler wet and **darker** when dry (the wet-to-dry shift), especially in
  dark and mid values and with lots of medium.

## Keeping paint workable
- **Stay-wet palette**: a sponge or wet paper towel under a sheet of baking/
  palette paper in a lidded tray keeps paint wet for hours to days.
- **Mist** palette and canvas lightly with a spray bottle.
- **Retarder** (Golden Acrylic Glazing Liquid/Retarder, Liquitex Slow-Dri): add
  in small amounts (often up to about 10-15 percent; follow the maker), too much
  leaves a tacky film.
- **Slow-drying acrylics**: Golden OPEN and similar open-time lines stay wet
  for hours; blend wet-into-wet like oils. Do not mix heavily with fast acrylics.
- Humidity and cool rooms slow drying; heaters and fans speed it.

## Compensating the colour shift
1. Mix the colour and paint a swatch on scrap; dry it with a hair dryer; compare.
2. Mix slightly **lighter** than the target to land correctly when dry.
3. Matte media shift less than gloss.

## Blending strategies
- Work small areas at a time; wet blending with retarder or OPEN paints.
- **Layered blending**: build gradients with thin, semi-transparent layers or
  scumbles over dry paint.
- Hatching/broken colour reads as a blend at distance.

## Gotchas
- Dried paint in brushes ruins them: rinse constantly.
- Over-retarding gives soft, sticky films that collect dust.

## Verify
- [ ] Palette kept wet; brushes rinsed.
- [ ] Colour checked on dry swatches before large areas.
