# Mixing chromatic greys and neutrals

**Goal:** greys that carry subtle warmth or coolness and harmonise with the
painting, rather than dead black-and-white greys.

## Complementary pairs
- Mix two near-complements to approach neutral; add white for lighter values.
- Classic pairs:
  - **Ultramarine + burnt sienna** (or burnt umber): from warm brown through neutral
    to cool blue-grey; a near-black at full strength.
  - **Cadmium red + viridian/phthalo green**.
  - **Cadmium orange + ultramarine / cobalt**.
  - **Violet (quinacridone violet) + yellow ochre**.
- Shift the ratio slightly to make a grey lean warm or cool.

## Greys from the painting's own colours
- Mix the leftovers of a painting's main colours into a "mother" grey; it will
  harmonise with everything (common studio practice).
- Grey down a colour by adding a little of the colour opposite it on the palette.

## Black-and-white greys
Ivory black + white makes a slightly bluish grey (black acts as a dull blue,
see the Zorn palette). Useful, but can look flat beside chromatic greys.

## Avoiding mud
- Mud = unintended low chroma + wrong value, usually from too many pigments
  or overmixing on the canvas.
- Keep to 2-3 pigments per mix; mix on the palette, not by scrubbing on canvas.
- Clean brushes between unrelated colours.
- Be clear what neutral you want (warm grey for stone in sunlight, cool grey for
  shadow).

## Gotchas
- "Neutral" greys on an otherwise warm painting can look strongly blue by
  simultaneous contrast; judge in context.

## Verify
- [ ] Greys lean deliberately warm or cool.
- [ ] Each grey mixed from at most three pigments.
