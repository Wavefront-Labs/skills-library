# Water gilding and gold leaf

**Goal:** apply gold leaf to panels or frames, burnished to a mirror shine
(water gilding) or matte (oil gilding), as on medieval tempera panels (Thompson).

## Tools
Gilder's cushion (suede pad with a parchment screen), gilder's knife, gilder's
tip (thin squirrel-hair brush), agate burnisher, soft mop brush, loose-leaf gold
(23-24 ct for water gilding; transfer/patent leaf for oil gilding in drafts).

## Water gilding (on true gesso)
1. **Bole**: clay (often red, yellow or black) mixed with weak rabbit-skin glue;
   apply 4-6 thin coats on the gesso where gold goes; dry; burnish lightly with
   a cloth.
2. **Gilder's liquor**: water with a little alcohol (sometimes a trace of glue).
3. Tip a leaf onto the cushion; cut with the knife; pick up with the tip (rubbed on
   your cheek or arm for a tiny bit of static/grease).
4. Flood a small area of bole with liquor; lay the leaf onto it; it is drawn down.
5. Overlap leaves slightly; let dry to the right point (minutes to hours: test with
   the burnisher on a corner; it should glide with a slight squeak).
6. **Burnish** with the agate: firm, smooth strokes; the bole beneath gives the warm
   glow and allows the mirror finish.
7. Tooling and punching designs is done before/after burnishing per tradition.

## Oil (mordant) gilding
- Brush oil gold size (quick: 1-3 hours; slow: 12 hours) on a sealed surface; wait
  until tacky ("whistle" test: knuckle drags with slight resistance).
- Lay leaf; tamp with the mop; brush off the excess (skewings) after drying.
- Cannot be burnished; matte to satin finish; more durable outdoors.

## Gotchas
- Drafts and breath blow leaf away: work in still air, hold breath when transferring.
- Burnishing too early (tears) or too late (no shine).
- Imitation leaf (Dutch metal) tarnishes; seal it with shellac or varnish.

## Verify
- [ ] Leaf adhered without gaps (holidays) or fill them (faulting).
- [ ] Burnished areas uniform mirror; imitation leaf sealed.
