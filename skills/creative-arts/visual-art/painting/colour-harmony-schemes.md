# Colour harmony schemes

**Goal:** a coherent colour plan for a painting, chosen before painting.

## Schemes
| Scheme | Hues | Character |
|--------|------|-----------|
| Monochromatic | One hue, varied value and chroma | Calm, unified, moody |
| Analogous | 3-4 neighbours on the wheel | Harmonious, natural (sunset, foliage) |
| Complementary | Two opposites | Vibrant; needs one dominant |
| Split-complementary | One hue plus the two beside its complement | Contrast with less clash |
| Triadic | Three evenly spaced | Lively; mute two of them |

## Rules that make any scheme work
1. **Dominance**: one hue (or temperature) dominates by area, roughly 60-70 percent;
   a secondary 20-30 percent; an accent 5-10 percent.
2. **Chroma hierarchy**: few areas at full chroma; most areas muted. High chroma
   everywhere reads garish.
3. **Shared ingredient**: mix a little of one colour into all others (a "mother
   colour") to unify.
4. **Value structure first**: a scheme cannot rescue a weak value design.
5. **Complements placed for focus**: put the strongest complementary contrast at
   the focal point.

## Choosing
- Start from the light (warm evening light suggests analogous warm with muted cool
  accents) and the mood.
- Test on small colour thumbnails (see thumbnail-sketches-and-value-plans), 3-4 schemes.
- Gamut mapping (see gamut-mapping-and-limited-palettes) is a precise way to implement them.

## Gotchas
- Equal areas of complements: visual vibration with no dominance.
- Treating the wheel as fixed: printer's (CMY), painter's (RYB) and Munsell wheels
  differ in where complements sit; Munsell's are the most perceptually accurate.

## Verify
- [ ] One dominant hue by area.
- [ ] Highest chroma limited to accent/focal areas.
