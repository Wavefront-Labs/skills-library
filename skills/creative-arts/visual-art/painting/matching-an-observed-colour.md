# Matching an observed colour

**Goal:** mix exactly the colour you see, quickly and without mud.

## The questions (ask in this order)
1. **Value**: how light or dark, compared with the white of the canvas and the darkest dark?
2. **Hue**: which colour family; is it warmer or cooler than its neighbours?
3. **Chroma**: how intense compared with the pure tube colour; how grey?

## Mixing procedure
1. Choose the **dominant pigment** closest in hue (e.g. for a muted olive: yellow
   ochre).
2. Adjust **hue** with a neighbour on the wheel (a touch of blue to green the ochre).
3. Adjust **value**: lighten with white (it cools and lowers chroma), or with a
   lighter pigment of similar hue (lemon yellow to lighten a yellow); darken with a
   darker pigment of similar hue (burnt umber, ultramarine) rather than black, unless
   you want black's greying.
4. Adjust **chroma**: lower it with the complement or grey of the same value.
5. Mix with a knife thoroughly (streaks hide the true colour).

## Testing
- Load a palette knife with the mixture, hold it up next to the subject (in the
  same light), squint, and compare. Correct; repeat.
- Or dab it on the canvas next to related colours; colours look different in context.

## Correction table
| Problem | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Too light | Add a darker paint of similar hue |
| Too dark | Add white or lighter paint of similar hue |
| Too intense | Add complement or a matching-value grey |
| Too dull | Restart with a higher-chroma base; you cannot add chroma back |
| Too warm/cool | Add a neighbour hue on the other side |

## Gotchas
- Fixing everything at once: change one property per step.
- Mixing too small a pile: you cannot remix the same colour later.

## Verify
- [ ] Knife test against the subject matches when squinting.
- [ ] No more than 3-4 pigments in the mixture.
