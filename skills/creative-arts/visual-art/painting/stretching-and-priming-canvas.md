# Stretching and priming canvas

**Goal:** a taut, square, properly primed canvas ready for oil or acrylic
(Mayer, supports and grounds chapters; Golden/Gamblin technical sheets).

## Materials
- **Fabric**: linen (strong, stable, less reactive to humidity, expensive) or
  cotton duck (#10/#12 weight, cheaper, more humidity movement). Raw or pre-primed.
- **Stretcher bars**: keyable bars with a raised bevelled lip (so the canvas does not
  touch the inner edge). Add a cross-brace for sides over about 90-100 cm.
- Canvas pliers, staple gun with stainless or galvanised staples (or tacks),
  set square/tape measure, keys (wedges).

## Assembly
1. Push the bars together at the mitred corners (rubber mallet). Check square by
   measuring both diagonals: they must match.
2. Cut fabric about 5-8 cm larger than the frame on all sides, aligned with the weave.

## Stretching
1. Lay the frame bevel-side down on the fabric, weave parallel to the bars.
2. Staple the **centre** of one long side; pull with pliers and staple the centre
   of the opposite side; then the centres of the short sides.
3. Work outward from the centres in pairs (opposite sides alternately), staples
   5-8 cm apart, pulling evenly. Keep the weave straight.
4. **Corners**: fold the excess into a neat hospital/envelope fold; staple on the back
   (not on the side where it shows if the work is unframed).
5. Insert keys loosely at corners; tap in only to tighten later if the canvas sags.

## Priming
- Raw canvas: size first if using an oil ground (see sizing skill). For acrylic
  "gesso", no separate size needed (it seals).
- Apply 2-4 thin coats of acrylic gesso, each dry before the next, alternating brush
  direction; lightly sand between later coats for a smoother surface.
- Oil grounds: lead or titanium alkyd grounds over size; dry for days to weeks.

## Gotchas
- Stapling one side fully first: diagonal wrinkles.
- Over-tight cotton: shrinks further when primed and can warp bars.
- Keying too hard: cracks the paint later on old canvases.

## Verify
- [ ] Diagonals equal; weave straight.
- [ ] Even drum-like tension with no ripples.
- [ ] Priming even, sealed, dry before painting.
