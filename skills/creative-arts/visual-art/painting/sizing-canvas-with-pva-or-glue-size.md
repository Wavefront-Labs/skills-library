# Sizing canvas with PVA or glue size

**Goal:** seal raw fabric fibres so oil from the ground and paint cannot reach and
embrittle them ("oil rot"), before applying an oil ground (Mayer; Gamblin and
Golden technical notes).

## Size options
| Size | Notes |
|------|-------|
| PVA size (e.g. Gamblin PVA Size) | Neutral pH, stays flexible, not moisture-sensitive; modern standard under oil grounds |
| Acrylic sizes (Golden GAC 400 stiffens; GAC 100 seals) | Seal fibres; GAC 400 makes fabric stiff and reduces sagging |
| Rabbit-skin glue (RSG) | Traditional; swells and shrinks with humidity, contributing to cracking in old paintings; brittle with age |

## Applying PVA or acrylic size
1. Stretch the raw canvas (see stretching-and-priming-canvas).
2. Apply with a wide brush or knife, thinly, working into the weave; it should
   seal, not form a thick film.
3. One to two coats; let dry fully (hours).
4. Test: a drop of water should bead rather than soak in.

## Applying rabbit-skin glue (if required)
- Soak glue granules in water (around 1 part glue to 15-20 parts water by weight,
  per supplier), then warm in a double boiler without boiling; apply warm and thin.
- Cools to a jelly; a thin coat is enough. Too thick cracks.

## Not needed when
- Using acrylic gesso directly on raw canvas (acrylic seals the fibres), or
  painting with acrylics.

## Gotchas
- Skipping size under an oil ground: fibres degrade over years.
- Thick glue layers: brittle and humidity-reactive.
- Overheating RSG: weakens it.

## Verify
- [ ] Water beads on the sized fabric.
- [ ] Oil ground applied only over size.
