# Final removable varnish for oil paintings

**Goal:** a clear, even, removable protective layer that saturates colours and can
be taken off by a conservator decades later without harming the paint (Mayer;
Gamblin and Golden guidance).

## When
- Traditional advice: wait **6-12 months** for normal oil layers (longer for thick
  impasto) so the paint is dry enough not to be harmed by solvents and to stop
  moving. Some modern varnishes are formulated for earlier use; follow the maker.
- Test: press a fingernail gently into a thick area out of sight; no give.

## Choosing a varnish
| Varnish | Notes |
|---------|-------|
| Dammar (natural resin) | Traditional, glossy; yellows and becomes less soluble with age |
| Ketone and synthetic low-molecular-weight resins (e.g. Regalrez in Gamvar) | Non-yellowing, removable with mild solvents (odourless mineral spirits) |
| Acrylic MSA varnish | Removable with mineral spirits; UV stabilisers |
Avoid permanent polyurethane or household varnish.

## Sheen
Gloss saturates darks; matte reduces glare but can fog darks in thick coats;
satin is common. Some brands let you mix sheens.

## Application
1. Clean the surface: soft brush, or lightly with a damp lint-free cloth; dry fully.
2. Conditions: dust-free room, 18-25 C, moderate humidity; lay painting flat.
3. **Brush**: wide soft brush; thin coats; work in one direction in sections, then
   lightly cross-brush to level; do not go back over drying areas.
4. Or **spray**: several light passes, 30 cm away, overlapping; respirator.
5. Two thin coats beat one thick; dry between coats per maker.
6. Tilt to the light to find missed spots while wet.

## Records
Write on the back: varnish product, date, and solvent for removal.

## Gotchas
- Varnishing too early: cracking, wrinkling, or varnish mixing into soft paint.
- Dust and hair: work in clean conditions; lift hairs with a brush tip immediately.
- Thick matte coats: blooming/fogging.

## Verify
- [ ] Paint fully dry per test.
- [ ] Even sheen, no holidays or drips.
- [ ] Varnish details recorded on the back.
