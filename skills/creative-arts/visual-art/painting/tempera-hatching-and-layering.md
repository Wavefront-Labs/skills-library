# Tempera hatching and layering

**Goal:** build an egg tempera painting from many small, fast-drying strokes:
the technique of Duccio, later Wyeth and others (Thompson; Mayer).

## Why hatching
Egg tempera dries in seconds and cannot be blended wet like oil. Gradations are
built optically with **fine parallel or cross-hatched strokes**, and with
**thin translucent layers** laid over each other.

## Sequence
1. **Drawing** transferred to the gesso panel; reinforce lines with thin ink or
   tempera.
2. **Underpainting**: tonal modelling in thin tempera, e.g. verdaccio (green-grey)
   under flesh, or a monochrome ink wash.
3. **Local colour layers**: thin, semi-transparent; each layer only slightly
   different from the one below.
4. **Hatching**: small soft round brush (sable, size 0-2), nearly dry: load, then
   touch off most of the paint on a cloth. Lay parallel strokes following the form;
   cross at angles for darker values. Many passes.
5. **Highlights**: lighter mixtures with more white in fine strokes; darkest accents
   last.
6. Optional: buff with silk or a soft cloth after curing for a satin sheen; varnish
   if desired once well cured (months).

## Rules
- Work light, thin layers; heavy layers crack and lift.
- Do not overload the brush; wet strokes can lift the layer below.
- Keep consistent stroke direction in an area to avoid visual noise.

## Gotchas
- Trying to blend: muddy, lifting paint.
- Thick impasto: tempera is a thin-layer medium.
- Too much water in strokes: they bead or create tide marks.

## Verify
- [ ] Gradations built optically with hatching.
- [ ] No lifting or cracking; layers thin.
