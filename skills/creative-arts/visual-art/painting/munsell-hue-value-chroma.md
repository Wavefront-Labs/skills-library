# Munsell hue, value and chroma

**Goal:** describe any colour by three independent properties so you can judge
and correct a mixture one dimension at a time (Gurney, *Color and Light*, uses
Munsell's terms).

## The three dimensions
- **Hue**: the colour family. Munsell has 10 principal hues: R, YR, Y, GY, G, BG, B,
  PB, P, RP, each split into steps (2.5, 5, 7.5, 10). 5R is a central red.
- **Value**: lightness from 0 (black) to 10 (white). Independent of hue.
- **Chroma**: intensity / distance from grey. 0 is neutral grey; high chroma
  pigments (cadmium red) reach 14+; different hues reach different maximum
  chromas at different values (yellow peaks at high value, blue at low).
- Notation: **Hue Value/Chroma**, e.g. 5YR 6/4 = a medium-light, moderately muted
  orange-brown (a typical Caucasian skin light).

## Using it while painting
1. When a mixture looks wrong, ask in order: is the **value** right? Then is the
   **chroma** (too bright/dull) right? Then is the **hue** right (too red/yellow)?
   Value errors matter most.
2. To change value without changing chroma much: add a lighter or darker paint
   of the same hue, not just white (white lowers chroma and cools many colours) or black.
3. To lower chroma: add the complement or a grey of the same value.
4. To raise chroma: you cannot exceed the strongest pigment in the mix; start with
   a higher-chroma paint.

## Tools
- Munsell Book of Color or student charts; paint your own value/chroma chart for
  one hue (rows of value, columns of chroma) as an exercise.
- Gurney's "colour wheel masks" use the same space (see gamut-mapping-and-limited-palettes).

## Gotchas
- Confusing chroma with value: a dark colour can be high chroma (ultramarine), a
  light one low chroma (pale grey-pink).
- Adding white to "brighten": it lightens but lowers chroma.

- [ ] You can estimate value and chroma of a colour separately.
