# Hand-grinding oil colour

**Goal:** make your own oil paint from dry pigment and oil, as described in
Mayer's *Artist's Handbook* (oil colours, grinding), for control over texture,
oil content and pigment choice.

## Equipment
- Thick glass slab (frosted/sandblasted surface) about 40x40 cm, and a glass
  muller.
- Palette knives (steel), measuring spoons, empty aluminium tubes and a
  tube-filling knife or spatula.
- Refined or cold-pressed **linseed oil** (walnut or safflower for whites).
- Dry pigments from an artist supplier (Kremer, Natural Pigments, etc.).
- Respirator (P100/FFP3) and nitrile gloves; work away from drafts.

## Method
1. Put a small heap of pigment (a tablespoon) on the slab; make a well.
2. Add oil a few drops at a time; work with the knife into a stiff paste.
   Each pigment needs a different amount (**oil absorption**): earths and
   ultramarine need more, cadmiums and lead white less.
3. **Mull**: move the muller in figure-eights or circles with moderate
   pressure over a small amount of paste; gather with the knife; repeat. Mulling
   disperses agglomerates rather than "grinding" particles smaller.
4. Aim for the stiffest paste that is smooth: a buttery, short consistency.
   Too much oil yellows, wrinkles and sags.
5. Optional additives: a little beeswax (1-2 percent) for stability in tubes,
   or a stabiliser like aluminium stearate (used by manufacturers) to prevent oil
   separation.
6. Fill tubes from the back with a knife; fold the end 3 times and crimp.

## Testing
- Draw down a sample beside a commercial paint: compare tinting strength, texture
  and drying.
- Label tubes with pigment code, oil and date.

## Gotchas
- Dry pigment dust is the main hazard: never mull toxic pigments without a
  respirator; wet-wipe everything afterward.
- Under-mulled paint: streaky, gritty, weak tinting strength.
- Oil-rich homemade paint breaks fat-over-lean expectations; note oil content.

## Verify
- [ ] Paste smooth with no grit under the knife.
- [ ] Minimal oil; consistency holds a peak.
- [ ] Tubes labelled.
