# Preparing rigid painting panels

**Goal:** a flat, stable, archival panel for oil or acrylic: ideal for small
works, plein air, detailed painting and heavy impasto.

## Panel materials
| Material | Notes |
|----------|-------|
| Tempered/untempered hardboard (e.g. Masonite) | Cheap, smooth; untempered preferred (tempered contains oil); warps above ~40 cm without cradling |
| Birch plywood (exterior/marine grade, 6-12 mm) | Stable, light; grain may telegraph; seal well |
| MDF | Flat, heavy; edges absorb water; seal all sides |
| Aluminium composite (ACM, Dibond) | Very stable, archival; sand and prime with a suitable primer |
| Pre-made gessoed panels (Ampersand etc.) | Convenient; check ground type |

## Steps
1. **Cut** to size; sand edges.
2. **Cradle** panels over about 40x50 cm: glue a wooden frame (strip) to the back to
   prevent warping and for hanging.
3. **Lightly sand** the face (180-220 grit) to give tooth; wipe dust with a damp cloth.
4. **Seal** all sides with an acrylic size or gloss medium (Golden GAC 100) to block
   **support-induced discolouration** (SID): water-soluble impurities in wood
   products migrate into the ground and paint as yellow-brown stains. Two coats.
5. **Ground**: 3-6 thin coats of acrylic gesso, alternating direction, sanding between
   later coats for smoothness; or oil ground over size; or true gesso for tempera
   (see true-gesso-panel-preparation).
6. Optional: glue fine linen or cotton to the panel with acrylic medium for a fabric
   texture with rigid support.

## Gotchas
- Unsealed hardboard or MDF: yellowing through light paint layers.
- Priming one side only on thin panels: they bow toward the primed side; coat the
  back too.
- Respiratory protection: MDF dust is harmful; use extraction and a mask.

## Verify
- [ ] Panel flat; large ones cradled.
- [ ] All faces sealed before gesso.
- [ ] Ground smooth/tooth as intended.
