# Form shadow, cast shadow and reflected light

**Goal:** name each zone of light on a form so you can see it, place it and give
it the right value and edge. Covered in Gurney's *Color and Light* (light and form).

## The zones, light to dark
| Zone | Where | Value and edge |
|------|-------|----------------|
| Highlight | Mirror reflection of the source; position moves with your eye | Lightest; small; sharp on shiny, broad on matt |
| Light (centre light) | Planes facing the source | Light family, brightest after highlight |
| Halftone | Planes turning away from the source | Light family, gradually darker |
| Terminator | Where surface turns parallel to the light | Edge between families; soft on round forms, sharp on angular ones |
| Core shadow | Just past the terminator, before bounce light reaches | Darkest part of the form shadow |
| Reflected light | Shadow side lit by nearby surfaces | Shadow family; never as light as halftones |
| Cast shadow | Shadow the form throws on another surface | Darker than form shadow near the contact; sharp near the object, softer and lighter further away |
| Occlusion shadow | Where forms touch or nearly touch and no light enters | Darkest accents in the picture |

## Drawing procedure
1. Find the terminator and cast shadow shapes first (two-value map).
2. Set the darkest accent: occlusion shadows at the contact point.
3. Add core shadow as a slightly darker band inside the shadow along the terminator.
4. Model halftones in the light.
5. Add reflected light faintly; place the highlight last.

## Gotchas
- Outlining cast shadows with a hard edge all round: their edges soften with
  distance from the object (penumbra).
- Reflected light too bright: makes the form look lit from two sources.
- Missing occlusion: objects seem to float above the surface.

## Verify
- [ ] Each zone can be pointed to on the drawing.
- [ ] Occlusion accents anchor objects to surfaces.
- [ ] Cast shadow edges soften with distance.
