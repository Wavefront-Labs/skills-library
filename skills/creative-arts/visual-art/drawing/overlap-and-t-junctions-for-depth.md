# Overlap and T-junctions for depth

**Goal:** the strongest depth cue in a line drawing is **occlusion**: one form
cutting in front of another. Draw overlaps so the order of forms is unambiguous.

## The T-junction
- Where two contours meet, the line of the **front** form continues unbroken (the
  top bar of a "T"); the line of the **behind** form stops at it (the stem).
- In figure drawing: the calf's contour passes in front of the thigh behind the
  knee; the near shoulder's line cuts across the neck. Draw these as T's, not
  as two lines touching end to end.

## Steps
1. Decide the order of forms front to back before committing lines.
2. Draw the front form's contour fully through the junction.
3. Stop the rear contour exactly at it; a small gap or overrun ruins the read.
4. Strengthen the front line slightly at the junction (see line weight).
5. Where possible, let a contour of the rear form **re-emerge** on the other side
   along a consistent path; the eye infers it continued behind.

## Avoid tangents
- **Tangent**: two contours, or a contour and the picture edge, just touching.
  It flattens space and creates visual tension. Either overlap clearly or
  separate clearly.
- Common tangents: a head touching the frame top, a hand touching a table edge, a
  horizon meeting a shoulder.

## Gotchas
- Drawing every form complete and then erasing hidden parts gives soft,
  uncertain junctions; plan the overlap.
- Overlaps also need value support in a tonal drawing: the front form's edge
  against the background should be crisper.

## Verify
- [ ] Every junction reads clearly as front or behind.
- [ ] No accidental tangents with other forms or the frame.
