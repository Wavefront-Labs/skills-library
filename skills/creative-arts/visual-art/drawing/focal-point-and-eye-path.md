# Focal point and eye path

**Goal:** make the viewer look first where you intend, then travel through the
picture along a route you designed, returning rather than escaping (Poore,
*Pictorial Composition*; Gurney, *Imaginative Realism*).

## Tools of emphasis (stack several at the focal point)
- **Value contrast**: the strongest light-dark junction.
- **Edges**: the sharpest edges.
- **Detail**: the most information and texture.
- **Colour**: the highest chroma or a colour contrast (warm against cool).
- **Isolation**: the one shape that differs (a vertical among horizontals).
- **Faces and text** draw eyes regardless; place them deliberately.

## Placement
Avoid the exact centre and the edges; somewhere near (not exactly on) a third
line usually works. Test placement in thumbnails.

## Eye path
1. **Leading lines**: roads, limbs, gazes, edges of shadows pointing at the
   focal point.
2. **Stepping stones**: secondary accents of lesser contrast leading back to it.
3. **Gaze**: viewers follow where figures look and point.
4. **Exits**: lines that run into corners lead the eye out. Interrupt them
   (a tree, a darker value at the edge, a turning curve).

## Subordination
Everything else is quieter: lower contrast, softer edges, less detail. A picture
with many equal accents has no focal point.

## Gotchas
- Two competing focal points of equal strength.
- A bright highlight or a hard edge in a corner pulling the eye away.
- Leading lines pointing to nothing.

## Verify
- [ ] Ask someone where they look first; it matches your plan.
- [ ] Contrast, edges and detail peak at the focal point.
- [ ] Corners and edges hold the eye in.
