# Soft pastel technique and surfaces

**Goal:** build rich, layered colour with soft pastel, which is almost pure pigment
with a little binder (Mayer covers pastel as a painting medium).

## Pastel types
- **Hard pastels and pastel pencils** (more binder): drawing, early layers, detail.
- **Soft pastels** (e.g. Rembrandt medium-soft; Unison, Schmincke, Sennelier very
  soft): later layers. Rule: **hard under soft**; very soft pastel fills tooth fast.
- Oil pastels are a different medium (non-drying binder); do not mix freely.

## Surfaces
| Surface | Character |
|---------|-----------|
| Laid/Ingres paper (e.g. Canson Mi-Teintes) | Limited layers; textured; use the toned colour |
| Sanded paper (UART, Pastel Premier, Sennelier La Carte) | Many layers, takes wet underpaintings (most brands) |
| Pastelmat (Clairefontaine) | Velvety, many layers, holds fine detail |
Toned surfaces (mid value, often a complement to the subject) unify the colour.

## Process
1. Light drawing with hard pastel or charcoal.
2. **Underpainting** (optional): block in big shapes with hard pastel, then wash
   with water or alcohol on sanded paper, or use watercolour/gouache. Gives a
   colour base without filling tooth.
3. Mass in with the **side** of a broken stick; work dark to light, big to small.
4. **Layer** with light pressure; let underlayers show. **Scumble** (light
   veil over a dry layer) and broken colour (strokes side by side) rather than
   blending with fingers.
5. Final accents with the softest pastels, heavy pressure, only where needed.

## Health and safety
- Pastel dust contains pigment; avoid inhaling. Work upright, tap dust into a
  trough, wet-wipe surfaces (no dry sweeping), avoid cadmium/cobalt pastels
  if you blow or scrub; wear nitrile gloves or barrier cream.

## Framing
Frame behind glass with a spacer or mat so the surface does not touch the glass;
a gap at the bottom of the mat lets loose dust fall out of sight. Fixative changes
colours; most pastellists use none or very little.

## Gotchas
- Filling the tooth early with soft pastel: further layers slide.
- Over-blending: muddy, flat colour.

## Verify
- [ ] Hard-under-soft order kept; tooth remains for final accents.
- [ ] Framed with spacer; no glass contact.
