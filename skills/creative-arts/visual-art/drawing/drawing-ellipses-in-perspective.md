# Drawing ellipses in perspective

**Goal:** draw circular forms (cups, bottles, wheels, arches) with ellipses of
the right **degree** and **orientation**.

## Rules
- A circle in perspective is always a true, symmetrical **ellipse** with rounded
  ends; never pointed (a "lemon") or egg-shaped when drawn freehand as a guide.
- **Degree** (how open the ellipse is) changes with distance from eye level:
  near the horizon, thin; far above or below, rounder. On a glass, the top rim
  (near eye level) is thinner than the base.
- **Minor axis** aligns with the axis of the cylinder or wheel: for an upright
  bottle, the minor axis is vertical; for a wheel, it points along the axle.
- The perspective centre of the circle is **not** the ellipse's centre; it sits
  slightly behind (further from viewer). Matters when placing spokes or a handle.

## Drawing freehand
1. Draw the minor axis line first, then the major axis perpendicular to it.
2. **Ghost**: move the arm through the ellipse above the paper 2-3 times, then
   lower the pencil and draw 2 loops in one confident motion from the shoulder.
3. Keep ends round; check symmetry across both axes.
4. For accuracy, construct a square in perspective, draw diagonals, and fit the
   ellipse touching the midpoints of the four sides.

## Gotchas
- Flat, straight base on a bottle: the base is an ellipse; draw the hidden back
  half lightly to get it right.
- Top and bottom ellipses of a cylinder with the same degree when the eye level
  is between them.
- Drawing from the wrist gives wobbly ellipses; use the shoulder.

## Verify
- [ ] Ellipses are symmetrical with round ends.
- [ ] Degree opens up moving away from eye level.
- [ ] Minor axis matches the object's axis.
