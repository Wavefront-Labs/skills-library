# Graphite grades and layering

**Goal:** get a full value range in graphite with clean, even tones and no
unwanted shine.

## The grading scale
- **H** = hard (more clay binder, lighter, holds a point); **B** = black (more
  graphite, softer, darker, smudges). **HB** and **F** sit in the middle.
- European scale runs roughly 9H to 9B (some makers to 12B). Grades are not
  standardised between brands; an HB in one brand can match a B in another.
  Test and label your own set.
- Woodless graphite sticks and mechanical leads (0.3-0.9 mm, HB-2B) are useful
  for lines and fine control.

## A working set
2H (light construction, very light tones), HB (general), 2B (middle darks),
4B or 6B (darkest accents). More grades rarely add range; layering does.

## Layering method
1. Start light with harder grades; build tone in multiple light passes
   rather than pressing hard.
2. Move to softer grades only for the darker areas.
3. For the deepest darks, layer: HB, then 2B, then 4B, in different hatch
   directions. Finishing a dark with a harder pencil over a soft one can reduce
   shine and lift the value slightly (test on scrap).

## Shine and burnishing
- Soft graphite polished by pressure reflects light; darks look silvery and light
  at an angle. Avoid one heavy 8B pass. Charcoal or carbon pencil can be mixed in
  for matt darks (works over graphite, less well under it).
- Hard pencil pressed hard **dents and burnishes** paper: later layers skate over.

## Paper
- Smooth (Bristol, hot press) gives fine, even tone; medium tooth (drawing paper
  90-160 gsm) gives grain and takes more layers.

## Sharpening
Long, exposed lead (knife plus sandpaper block) for side-of-lead tone and points;
keep several sharpened pencils per grade.

## Gotchas
- Using 6B for everything: muddy, shiny, smudged drawings.
- Smudging with fingers (skin oil leaves marks).

## Verify
- [ ] Full value range achieved with no silvery shine at an angle.
- [ ] No dented lines from early heavy pressure.
