# Dividing and multiplying in perspective

**Goal:** place repeated or equally spaced elements (windows, fence posts, floor
tiles, columns) correctly receding, without guessing.

## Find the centre of a plane
Draw both diagonals of the plane (corner to corner). They cross at the
perspective centre. A vertical (or line to the VP) through it halves the plane.
Repeat for quarters, eighths.

## Divide into unequal or odd numbers
1. On the near vertical edge, mark the divisions you want at true scale
   (e.g. five equal marks).
2. Draw lines from each mark to the VP of the plane.
3. Draw one diagonal across the plane; where it crosses those lines, drop
   verticals. The plane is now divided into five equal receding parts.

## Repeat (multiply) a space
1. Have one rectangle in perspective (e.g. a fence panel) with its vertical edges.
2. Find the midpoint of its far vertical edge and draw a line from it to the VP
   (a centre line).
3. Draw a line from the near top corner through the midpoint of the far edge and
   extend it to the baseline. Where it lands is the next vertical. Repeat.

## Measuring points (exact depths)
- For a line receding to VP1, its measuring point (MP1) is found on the HL by
  swinging an arc from VP1 with radius VP1-to-station-point. True measurements
  marked on a horizontal measuring line through the near corner, projected to MP1,
  give exact receding lengths. (Norling and Robertson give step-by-step setups.)

## Gotchas
- Using equal page spacing for receding equal spaces: spaces must shrink.
- Small construction errors grow when multiplying many times; re-check every few repeats.

## Verify
- [ ] Repeated elements get closer smoothly toward the VP.
- [ ] Diagonals of repeated units are parallel in perspective (meet at a common VP).
