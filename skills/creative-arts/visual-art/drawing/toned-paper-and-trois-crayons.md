# Toned paper and trois crayons

**Goal:** a fast, luminous drawing where the paper provides the middle value, dark
chalk the shadows and white chalk the lights. *Trois crayons* (black, red and
white chalk) was used by Rubens and Watteau; the Met's Heilbrunn timeline
describes its history.

## Materials
- Paper: mid-value toned paper (grey, tan, blue-grey), e.g. Canson Mi-Teintes,
  Strathmore toned. Value around step 5-6 of 9.
- Chalks: Conte a Paris sticks or pencils in black, sanguine (red-brown), and
  white; or charcoal pencil and white charcoal / pastel pencil.

## Principle
- The paper **is** the halftone. Every mark either darkens (shadow family) or
  lightens (light family). Do not cover the paper uniformly.
- White marks are only for the light family: planes facing the light and
  highlights. Keep white out of the shadows entirely.

## Order
1. Block in lightly with sanguine (warm, forgiving, gives life to flesh).
2. Shadows: sanguine for warm shadows, black for the darkest accents and cool darks.
3. Lights last with white, sparingly: brightest on the planes most directly facing
   the light, highlights smallest and brightest. Leave the paper showing between.
4. Do not blend white into darks: it becomes chalky grey.

## Gotchas
- Too much white: drawing looks chalky and flat. White should occupy far less
  area than the dark marks.
- Choosing paper too light (white chalk does not read) or too dark (shadows do not read).
- White charcoal pencils smear differently from pastel pencils; test.

## Verify
- [ ] Paper tone visible as halftone across much of the form.
- [ ] White used only in the light family, smallest at highlights.
- [ ] No grey mud from mixing white and black.
