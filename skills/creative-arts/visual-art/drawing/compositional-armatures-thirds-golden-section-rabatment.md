# Compositional armatures: thirds, golden section, rabatment

**Goal:** place major divisions and the focal point by an underlying geometric
structure when it helps, while knowing these are aids, not laws.

## Rule of thirds
Divide width and height into thirds. Horizons and main verticals on the lines;
focal point near an intersection. Simple; prevents dead-centre placement.

## Golden section
- Divide a side in the ratio 1 : 1.618 (about 0.382 / 0.618 of its length).
  Close to thirds but slightly nearer the centre.
- Construct: on a side of length L, mark 0.618 x L from one end. A golden
  rectangle (sides 1:1.618) can be subdivided into a square and a smaller golden
  rectangle repeatedly, giving the spiral.
- Historic use is often overstated; treat it as one option.

## Rabatment of the rectangle
- Fold the short side onto the long side: in a horizontal rectangle, draw a
  vertical line at a distance equal to the height from the left edge, and again
  from the right edge. These lines (squares inside the rectangle) are strong
  placements for verticals and for the focal point.

## Harmonic armature
- Draw both main diagonals, then diagonals from each corner to the midpoints and
  rabatment points of the opposite sides. Intersections and lines give a web for
  placing edges and gestures at related angles.

## Use
1. Make a thumbnail by eye first.
2. Overlay the armature (tracing paper or a digital layer).
3. If key elements nearly align, nudge them on; if not, consider whether the
   picture works anyway before forcing it.

## Gotchas
- Forcing a subject onto a spiral after the fact.
- Using a square-format armature on a different aspect ratio.

## Verify
- [ ] Armature drawn for the actual aspect ratio.
- [ ] Main divisions avoid the exact centre unless chosen for symmetry.
