# Grid transfer and enlargement (squaring up)

**Goal:** copy a study or reference onto a larger (or smaller) surface with
accurate proportions, as painters have done since the Renaissance.

## Steps
1. **Match the aspect ratio.** Source 20 x 30 cm -> target must be 2:3 (e.g.
   60 x 90 cm). If not, crop the source first; drawing a diagonal across both
   rectangles shows whether they share a ratio (the diagonals of similar
   rectangles at a common corner overlap).
2. **Grid the source** without damaging it: draw on a clear acetate overlay, or
   a tracing-paper sheet, or digitally on a copy. Use 6-12 divisions on the short
   side; finer for detailed areas only.
3. **Grid the target** with the same number of divisions, lightly (hard pencil,
   charcoal, or chalk line for walls). Number rows and columns on both.
4. **Add diagonals** in the busiest cells; they give extra reference points.
5. **Transfer cell by cell**: note where each contour crosses the cell edges
   (e.g. "a third of the way along the top edge") and join the points, looking at
   shapes, not features.
6. **Check and erase**: stand back; correct the contours as a whole drawing,
   then remove grid lines (kneaded eraser for graphite; water for chalk on walls).

## Tools
- **Proportional dividers** set to the enlargement factor transfer individual
  distances without a grid.
- Projectors are faster but distort if not square-on; see the mural design
  transfer skill for walls.

## Gotchas
- Mismatched ratios produce stretched figures; check before gridding.
- Heavy grid lines show through thin paint or watercolour; keep them faint.
- Copying cell by cell can lose the gesture; restate the big lines afterwards.
- Copyright: transferring someone else's image is still copying it.

## Verify
- [ ] Source and target share an aspect ratio.
- [ ] Contours cross cell edges at the same proportional points.
- [ ] Grid removed or hidden before rendering.
