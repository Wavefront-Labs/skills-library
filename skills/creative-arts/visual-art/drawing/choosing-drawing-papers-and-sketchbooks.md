# Choosing drawing papers and sketchbooks

**Goal:** match paper to medium so marks behave and work lasts.

## Key properties
- **Weight**: gsm (grams per square metre) or lb (US ream weight; not comparable
  across paper types). 90-120 gsm sketch; 150-200 gsm drawing/mixed media; 300 gsm
  watercolour.
- **Tooth / surface**: smooth (Bristol plate, hot press) for ink and detailed
  graphite; vellum/medium tooth for graphite, coloured pencil, charcoal; rough
  or laid for charcoal, pastel.
- **Sizing**: internal/external gelatin or synthetic size controls absorbency.
  Wet media need well-sized paper; otherwise washes soak and buckle.
- **Permanence**: acid-free, lignin-free, buffered papers do not yellow quickly;
  100 percent cotton rag is the most durable. Newsprint yellows within months.

## Medium-to-paper guide
| Medium | Suitable paper |
|--------|----------------|
| Graphite, fine detail | Bristol smooth/vellum; Strathmore 400/500 drawing |
| Charcoal | Charcoal paper (laid), Canson Mi-Teintes, Arches/Ingres-type |
| Ink (pen) | Bristol smooth, hot-press, marker paper |
| Ink wash, line and wash | Watercolour paper (hot or cold press) 300 gsm, mixed media 200 gsm+ |
| Pastel | Sanded papers, Pastelmat, Mi-Teintes |
| Gesture/practice | Newsprint pads (cheap, not archival) |

## Sketchbooks
- **Binding**: spiral (lies flat, pages tear out), sewn/stitched (lies flat for
  double spreads, durable), glued (cheap, can crack).
- **Format**: landscape for panoramas; A5 for pocket use; A4/A3 for studies.
- Test a page with your main medium before committing to a brand; paper
  formulations change.

## Gotchas
- Watercolour in a 100 gsm sketchbook: buckling and bleed-through.
- Pigment liners on soft paper: feathering.
- Assuming "acid-free" means lightfast colour; that depends on the medium.

## Verify
- [ ] Paper weight and sizing suit any wet media used.
- [ ] Archival paper for works meant to last.
- [ ] Binding suits how you sketch (spreads, tear-outs).
