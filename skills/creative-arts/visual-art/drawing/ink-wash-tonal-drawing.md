# Ink wash tonal drawing

**Goal:** model light and form with diluted ink applied by brush, alone or with
pen line, as in Rembrandt's and Claude's wash drawings and East Asian ink painting.

## Materials
- Ink: India ink (waterproof once dry), sumi or Chinese stick ink (ground on a
  stone, adjustable), or sepia/brown inks. Non-waterproof ink re-wets; waterproof
  stays put.
- Brushes: a round (size 6-10) and a flat or mop for large areas.
- Paper: watercolour paper 300 gsm or heavier (or stretched lighter paper); for
  East Asian technique, xuan/rice paper (very absorbent, no corrections).

## Prepare a value scale of dilutions
In a palette or small jars, mix 3-4 dilutions, e.g. a few drops of ink per
spoon of water (light), more ink (medium), more (dark), and pure ink. Test each
on scrap and label them. Mixing on the fly gives uneven values.

## Method
1. Light pencil or pen drawing (waterproof ink if washes follow).
2. Lay the **lightest** wash over all areas except highlights; let it dry.
3. Add the medium wash over all areas in halftone and shadow; dry.
4. Dark wash for shadows; final darks and accents last.
5. **Wet-in-wet** softening: drop darker wash into a still-wet area for soft
   transitions (skies, rounded forms); use a damp clean brush to soften an edge.
6. Pen line can go last to restate key edges.

## Gotchas
- Working into half-dry washes: blooms and hard rings.
- Diluted India ink can look grey-brown and granular depending on brand; test.
- Washes dry lighter than they look wet; judge on dry tests.
- Buckling paper: stretch or tape it, or use heavier paper.

## Verify
- [ ] Dilutions mixed and tested before starting.
- [ ] Washes laid light to dark, each dry before the next.
- [ ] Highlights are untouched paper.
