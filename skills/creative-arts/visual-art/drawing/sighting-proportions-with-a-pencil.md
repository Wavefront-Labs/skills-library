# Sighting proportions with a pencil

**Goal:** get relative sizes right when drawing at any scale (not sight-size),
by comparing every length to one chosen unit.

## The method
1. **Stand or sit in one position** and keep it. Close one eye (always the same one).
2. **Lock your elbow** fully straight; the pencil must be the same distance from
   your eye for every measurement, or every comparison changes.
3. Hold the pencil (or a thin knitting needle / skewer, better because it is
   straight and thin) **perpendicular to your line of sight**, not tilted
   toward you.
4. **Choose a unit**: a clear, medium-sized length, e.g. head height of a figure,
   height of a bottle, width of a window. Align the pencil tip with one end and
   slide your thumbnail to the other.
5. **Compare**: how many units tall is the whole subject? Where does the halfway
   point fall? Write ratios down in a corner of the page ("total = 3.5 units").
6. Transfer the ratios to the paper at whatever size you chose; measure the
   drawing with a ruler or the pencil held flat on the paper, never by sighting
   the drawing from a distance.

## Useful checks
- **Halfway point**: find the midpoint of the subject first; it catches the
  biggest errors fast.
- **Height vs width**: the overall envelope ratio before any details.
- Compare in both directions (vertical units against horizontal units).

## Gotchas
- Bent elbow: the single most common cause of wrong measurements.
- Pencil tilted toward you: it foreshortens and measures short.
- Changing the unit halfway through; keep one unit per drawing.
- Measuring tiny things: errors are larger than the feature. Measure big
  intervals, judge small ones by eye.
- Moving your head or chair between measurements.

## Verify
- [ ] Unit noted and all major lengths recorded as ratios.
- [ ] Overall height:width of the drawing matches the subject.
- [ ] Halfway point lands on the same feature in both.
