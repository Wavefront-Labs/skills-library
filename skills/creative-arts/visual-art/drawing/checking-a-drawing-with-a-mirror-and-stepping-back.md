# Checking a drawing with a mirror and stepping back

**Goal:** see your drawing with fresh eyes so errors you have stopped noticing
become obvious. Three tools, each for a different kind of error.

## 1. Stepping back (silhouette and proportion)
- Walk back to the viewing spot (sight-size) or at least 2-3 m every few minutes.
- At distance, small marks vanish; silhouette, gesture and the light-shadow pattern
  remain. Compare only those against the subject.

## 2. Hand mirror (asymmetry and tilt)
- Turn your back to the drawing and look at it over your shoulder in a hand
  mirror, or hold the mirror beside it. The reversed image breaks familiarity:
  tilted eyes, a leaning figure or a drifting vertical jump out.
- Look at the **subject** in the mirror too and compare the two reflections.
- Digital equivalent: flip the canvas horizontally (one shortcut in most apps).

## 3. Black mirror (values)
- A black mirror ("Claude glass": black glass, or a dark phone screen) lowers
  brightness and colour, compressing halftones so the big value groups show.
- Use it to check that light and shadow families read as two groups and that
  nothing in the light is as dark as the shadow.

## How to act
1. Name the error in one sentence ("the chin is too far left").
2. Confirm it with a plumb line or measurement before erasing.
3. Fix the biggest error first; small errors often move with it.

## Gotchas
- Checking only at the end; check every 10-15 minutes because errors compound.
- Correcting from the mirror without re-measuring: the mirror finds, the
  measurement confirms.

## Verify
- [ ] The drawing has been compared at distance, reversed and darkened.
- [ ] Each correction was confirmed with a measurement.
