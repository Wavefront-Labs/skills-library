# Atmospheric perspective

**Goal:** make distance read through the effect of air (moisture, dust) between
viewer and object, described by Leonardo and in Gurney's *Color and Light*
("atmospheric perspective").

## What happens with distance
1. **Contrast drops**: darks lighten more than lights darken; everything tends
   toward the value of the sky near the horizon.
2. **Detail and texture vanish**: far trees become flat silhouettes.
3. **Edges soften**.
4. **Colour shifts**: in clear daylight, distant darks turn bluish (sky-light
   scattering) and saturation drops. In smog, mist or at sunset the shift can go
   toward grey, yellow or warm.

## Plan with planes
Divide the landscape into 3-5 planes: foreground, middle ground, distance, far
distance, sky. Assign each plane a narrower value range, closer to the sky value
the further it is. Example on a 9-step scale (sky 8):
- Foreground: 1-7 (full contrast)
- Middle ground: 3-7
- Distance: 5-7
- Far mountains: 6-7

## In drawing
- Use harder leads, lighter pressure and fewer marks for distance; keep the darkest
  accents and sharpest edges in the foreground.

## In paint
- Mix the sky colour into distant colours progressively; reserve the strongest
  chroma and warmest darks for the foreground.

## Gotchas
- Dark, sharp distant mountains: they jump forward.
- Applying blue everywhere far away regardless of light conditions; observe the
  actual atmosphere (fog = grey, golden hour = warm).
- Indoors the effect is weak over short distances; do not overdo it in interiors.

## Verify
- [ ] Each receding plane has less contrast than the one before.
- [ ] Darkest darks and sharpest edges are in the foreground.
- [ ] Distant colour shifts match the stated atmosphere.
