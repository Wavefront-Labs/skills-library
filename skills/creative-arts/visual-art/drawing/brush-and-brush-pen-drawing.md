# Brush and brush-pen drawing

**Goal:** expressive line with large thick-thin range from one tool, used in
figure sketching, comics inking and East Asian calligraphic drawing.

## Tools
- **Sable round** (e.g. Winsor and Newton Series 7 size 2-3, Raphael 8404) with
  India ink. Best line quality and point; needs care.
- **Brush pens**: Pentel Pocket Brush (real bristles, cartridge ink, waterproof
  with Pentel cartridges, refillable), Kuretake No. 8/No. 13, felt-tip "brush" markers
  (Tombow, Faber-Castell PITT B) that are easier but less responsive.

## Technique
1. Hold the brush higher on the handle than a pencil and nearly vertical for fine
   lines; drawing from the elbow or shoulder for long strokes.
2. **Load** to about two thirds of the hair; shape the point on the jar rim or
   a scrap.
3. **Pressure** controls width: tip only = hairline; press = swell. Taper at both
   ends by landing and lifting while moving.
4. Pull strokes; rotate the paper to keep pulling direction comfortable.
5. **Dry brush**: blot most ink off, then drag lightly for broken, textured
   strokes (fur, wood, rough edges).
6. **Feathering**: fast tapering strokes laid side by side to make gradients, a
   comics technique for rounded forms.

## Care
Rinse a sable in clean water often during inking (shellac ink dries in the hairs),
wash with mild soap at the end, reshape the point and store flat or upright
tip-up. Never leave standing in water.

## Gotchas
- Splayed point from pressing too hard or ink drying in the ferrule.
- Wavering long lines from drawing with the wrist.
- Non-waterproof brush pen ink smears under markers or washes.

## Verify
- [ ] Clean tapered strokes at both ends.
- [ ] Controlled thick-thin variation along single strokes.
- [ ] Brush cleaned and point restored after use.
