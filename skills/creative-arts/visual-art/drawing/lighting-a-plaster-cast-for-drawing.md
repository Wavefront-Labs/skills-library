# Lighting a plaster cast for drawing

**Goal:** a single stable light giving a clean split into light and shadow
families with readable halftones, so the drawing teaches form.

## Light source
- Traditional ateliers use **north light** from a high window: soft, steady, no
  sun moving across. Artificial alternative: one daylight LED (5000-5600 K,
  CRI 90+) in a reflector or small softbox.
- Position it **above and to one side**, roughly 45 degrees up and 30-45 degrees
  to the side, so about a third of the form falls into shadow.

## Shadow box
- A three-sided box (top, back, one side) painted matt mid-grey or black, open
  toward you and the light. It stops room light bouncing into the shadows and
  gives a plain dark background.
- Put the cast at eye level, or at the height the drawing intends.

## Controls
- Remove every other light from the cast: switch off or flag overhead lights.
- Light the **drawing** separately at a similar colour temperature so values can be
  compared; it must not spill onto the cast.
- Tape the cast's position and photograph the light position.

## Check the setup
1. Squint: is there one obvious shadow shape with a clear terminator?
2. Multiple cast shadows mean multiple lights; remove the extras.
3. If reflected light makes the shadow merge with halftones, darken the box or
   move the cast away from white surfaces.

## Gotchas
- Windows that also get sun change the light through the day; draw at the same
  hours or go fully artificial.
- A bare point source gives harsh edges and few halftones; diffuse it a little.

## Verify
- [ ] One light, one set of cast shadows.
- [ ] Clear terminator, readable halftones, modest reflected light.
- [ ] Positions marked so the setup is identical next session.
