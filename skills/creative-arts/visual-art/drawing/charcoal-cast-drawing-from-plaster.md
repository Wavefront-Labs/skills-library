# Charcoal cast drawing from plaster

**Goal:** a finished academic drawing of a white plaster cast (foot, hand, mask,
head or figure), typically 40-120 hours, used by ateliers to train value and form
before painting.

## Materials
- Paper: Canson Mi-Teintes, Strathmore charcoal paper or an Ingres-type laid paper,
  taped to a rigid board.
- Charcoal: vine/willow for the block-in; charcoal pencils (General's, Nitram,
  Ritmo or similar) soft to hard for rendering; a soft brush or feather; kneaded eraser.
- Sandpaper block to keep long sharp points.

## Stages
1. **Setup**: cast lit by one source from high side (see lighting-a-plaster-cast-for-drawing);
   sight-size or a fixed scale.
2. **Block-in** with vine charcoal: envelope, then smaller straight lines, checked
   with plumb lines and angles. Expect about a quarter of total time here.
3. **Shadow map**: draw terminator and cast shadows as shapes; fill the shadow with a
   flat light tone.
4. **Massing**: bring the shadow to its true average value, brushing lightly to
   even it. Lay a very light halftone over planes turning away.
5. **Turning the form**: model from the terminator into the light in small steps,
   comparing value to value. Use harder pencils for the lights.
6. **Accents and finish**: darkest accents in crevices, sharpen key edges, lift
   highlights by pressing (not rubbing) a kneaded eraser.

## Gotchas
- Charcoal dust: tap the board, never blow on it, and keep a slip sheet under your hand.
- Over-blending with fingers or stumps fills the tooth; a full tooth will not take
  more dark.
- White plaster bounces a lot of light into shadows; resist overstating reflected light.
- Heavy fixative darkens values. Use none, or a light workable spray only at the end.

## Verify
- [ ] Squinted drawing matches squinted cast.
- [ ] Nothing in the light side is as dark as the shadow side.
- [ ] Edges vary like the cast's (crisp cast shadows, soft turns).
