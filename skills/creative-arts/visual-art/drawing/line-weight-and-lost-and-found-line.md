# Line weight and lost-and-found line

**Goal:** make a line drawing describe light, depth and importance through
variation of line, rather than an even wire outline.

## Rules of thumb (choose deliberately, not all at once)
- **Light**: thinner/lighter lines on the lit side of a form, heavier on the
  shadow side and underneath.
- **Depth**: nearer forms get heavier lines; receding forms thinner and lighter.
- **Overlap**: thicken the line of the front form at the point where it overlaps
  another (a T-junction) to make the overlap read.
- **Weight**: heavier lines where a form presses down or carries load (under
  a foot, a sagging belly of fabric).
- **Focus**: give the area of interest the crispest, most varied line; let
  peripheral areas be simpler.

## Lost and found
- Where an edge merges with a similar value or the form turns softly, **lose
  the line**: lift the pencil and let the viewer complete it. Where the edge is
  sharp and contrasting, **find** it with a firm mark.
- Squint at the subject: edges that disappear when squinting are candidates to lose.

## Technique
- Vary pressure through a single stroke (press, ease off) instead of going back
  over lines to fatten them.
- With pencils, a chisel point gives thick and thin from one lead; with pens,
  flexible nibs or brush pens give swell.
- Keep the line confident; hairy, repeated strokes read as uncertainty.

## Gotchas
- Uniform heavy outline: flattens everything into a colouring-book look.
- Random variation that contradicts the light direction.
- Losing too much: key structural junctions must still read.

## Verify
- [ ] Line weight changes have a reason (light, depth, overlap, focus).
- [ ] Some edges are deliberately lost and still read.
- [ ] The area of interest has the most considered line.
