# Shading the sphere, cube and cylinder

**Goal:** know exactly how light behaves on the three primary forms, because
every complex form is shaded as an assembly of them (Loomis, Bridgman, Gurney).

## Setup
Real objects are best: a white ball, a white box, a white tube or rolled paper on
a mid-grey cloth, lit by one lamp from high side (see lighting-a-plaster-cast-for-drawing).

## Sphere
- Value changes **gradually** around the form; no flat planes.
- The terminator is a curve (part of an ellipse), soft.
- Core shadow just past the terminator, reflected light near the bottom edge of the
  shadow side, an oval cast shadow on the ground with a dark occlusion contact.

## Cube
- Three visible planes, each **one flat value**: top (often lightest), lit side,
  shadow side. Changes happen only at edges, which are sharp.
- Slight gradation within planes from distance or reflected light, but keep the
  three planes distinct.

## Cylinder
- Value changes **across** the axis like the sphere, but stays constant **along**
  the axis: draw values as bands parallel to the axis.
- Ends are ellipses: the top ellipse behaves like a cube plane (flat value).

## Cone
- Bands converge to the tip; the terminator is a straight line from tip to base.

## Practice
Draw each form three times with the light in different positions (front-side,
side, back-side/rim). Note where the terminator moves.

## Gotchas
- Shading the cube with gradients like a sphere: it loses its planes.
- Cylinder bands curving like contour lines: they run parallel to the axis.
- Forgetting cast shadows, so objects float.

## Verify
- [ ] Sphere: gradual; cube: three flat planes; cylinder: bands along the axis.
- [ ] Each has terminator, core, reflected light, cast and occlusion shadow.
