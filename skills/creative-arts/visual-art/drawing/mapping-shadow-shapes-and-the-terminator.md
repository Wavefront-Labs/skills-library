# Mapping shadow shapes and the terminator

**Goal:** after the block-in is accurate, divide the drawing into exactly two
families, light and shadow, by drawing the **terminator** (the line where form
turns away from the light) as a precise shape. This is the second stage of every
Bargue plate and cast drawing; rendering comes only after it.

## Why it matters
The shadow shape carries most of the likeness and the sense of light. A
beautifully rendered drawing with a wrong shadow shape reads as wrong; a flat
two-value drawing with the right shapes already reads as a lit form.

## Method
1. **Squint** at the plate or cast until halftones merge; only the shadow mass
   remains. Bargue's later plates print the first stage (line and shadow map)
   beside the finished stage; study that first stage, it is the target.
2. **Draw the terminator with straight lines first**, just like the envelope.
   Find its highest point, lowest point and main changes of direction; check
   each against plumb and level lines from landmarks already placed.
3. **Separate form shadow from cast shadow.** The terminator on a turning form is
   soft; the edge of a cast shadow (e.g. under the nose) is sharper near its
   source and softer further away. Note this with a light or firm line now.
4. **Fill the shadow in one flat value**: hatch lightly in graphite or a thin
   charcoal tone. Do not model inside the shadow yet; reflected light comes last
   and must stay darker than any halftone in the light.
5. **Compare shapes, not features:** check shadow widths against the negative
   spaces beside them, and the angle of each segment with a held-out needle.
6. **Step back** to 3x the viewing distance (or use a mirror) and compare the
   two-value drawing to the squinted model.

## Gotchas
- Placing the terminator where the halftone is darkest rather than where light
  stops; it is usually a little further into the light than beginners put it.
- Smooth curves too early hide errors; keep it angular until the shape is right.
- Darkening the shadow fully before the shape is approved makes corrections dirty.
- Bargue plates are lithographs; do not copy printing artefacts (dot pattern,
  foxing) as form.

## Verify
- [ ] Squinting at drawing and model gives the same two-value pattern.
- [ ] Terminator high/low points line up with plumb lines from the model.
- [ ] Cast shadows are distinguished from form shadows by edge quality.
- [ ] Shadow is one flat value, lighter than final, ready for rendering.
