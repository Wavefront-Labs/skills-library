# Thumbnail sketches and value plans

**Goal:** decide the composition and value pattern of a picture before investing
hours, by trying many small versions fast (Gurney, *Imaginative Realism*).

## Thumbnails
1. Draw a set of small boxes in the **same aspect ratio** as the final, about
   5 x 7 cm or smaller. Small size forces big shapes.
2. Spend 1-3 minutes each. Make 6-12. Vary: viewpoint, horizon height, crop,
   placement of the focal point, format (vertical vs horizontal).
3. Use a broad marker or soft pencil; no detail, no outlines inside shapes.

## Value plan
1. Pick the best 2-3 thumbnails and redo each with **3 or 4 flat values**: white,
   light grey, dark grey, black (grey markers, e.g. 30 percent and 60 percent cool greys).
2. Every area of the picture must belong to one of these values.
3. Check the plan reads at arm's length and upside down. The focal point should
   have the strongest value contrast.
4. Note the light direction and where the darkest dark and lightest light meet.

## Scaling up
- Grid or proportionally enlarge the chosen thumbnail to the final surface
  (see grid-transfer-and-enlargement), keeping its proportions.
- Keep the thumbnail pinned beside the work and compare often: if the final stops
  reading like the thumbnail, the big design has been lost to detail.

## Gotchas
- Thumbnails too large or too detailed: they become drawings, not plans.
- Wrong aspect ratio: the design will not fit the final canvas.
- Value plan with too many middle greys: the picture will look flat.

## Verify
- [ ] At least six alternatives tried.
- [ ] Chosen plan uses 3-4 values and reads at a distance.
- [ ] Final work still matches the plan when squinted.
