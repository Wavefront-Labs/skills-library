# Sight-size setup and viewing position

**Goal:** arrange subject, easel and viewing spot so the drawing appears the
**same size** as the subject from one fixed position. Every comparison is then
direct and side by side, not a mental scaling.

## The geometry
- The drawing surface stands **beside** the subject (cast, still life or model),
  in roughly the same plane, so your glance moves sideways, not in depth.
- You judge from a fixed spot **behind** the easel, far enough to see subject and
  drawing whole at one glance. A common rule is at least **three times the
  largest dimension of the subject** (a 40 cm cast -> 1.2 m or more).
- From that spot the subject's top and bottom align with the drawing's top and
  bottom, checked with a plumb line or straight edge at arm's length.

## Steps
1. Place the subject and fix the light first (see lighting-a-plaster-cast-for-drawing).
2. Set the easel so the paper's edge sits just beside the subject's silhouette as
   seen from the spot. Make the board **vertical**, perpendicular to your line of
   sight, or the drawing foreshortens.
3. Tape the floor for your feet. Every judgement is made from this spot, never
   from the easel.
4. Hang a plumb line (thread and weight) or use a long knitting needle; mark key
   heights (top, bottom, widest points) on the paper by walking between the spot
   and the easel.
5. Walk forward to draw, walk back to judge. Many trips are the method.

## Variations
- Drawing larger or smaller than sight-size is comparative measurement; moving the
  easel off the subject's plane gives a fixed scale factor you must account for.
- For a live model, tape the stand and the model's feet as well.

## Gotchas
- Judging from the easel instead of the spot defeats the method.
- A board leaning back makes everything look vertically short.
- Moving the light, subject or easel mid-drawing invalidates every mark; tape all three.

## Verify
- [ ] From the floor mark, the subject's top/bottom align with the drawing's.
- [ ] Board is vertical and beside the subject.
- [ ] Easel, subject and feet positions are taped.
