# Three-point perspective

**Goal:** draw tall subjects seen from below (worm's eye) or looking down from
above (bird's eye), where verticals converge to a third VP.

## Setup: the vanishing triangle
1. Place the HL and two VPs as for two-point, far apart.
2. Place the **third VP (VP3)** far below the HL (looking down) or far above
   (looking up), roughly below/above the middle between VP1 and VP2.
3. Lines VP1-VP2, VP2-VP3 and VP3-VP1 form the vanishing triangle. The centre of
   vision (where you are looking) is near the triangle's centre; keep the subject
   inside the triangle and close to that centre.

## Construction
1. Draw the nearest vertical edge as a line toward VP3.
2. From its ends, draw lines to VP1 and VP2.
3. Place the far verticals as lines toward VP3, between those converging lines.
4. Complete the box with lines to the opposite VPs.

## Judgement
- VP3 far away gives gentle convergence (normal looking up at a house). Close VP3
  gives a dramatic, wide-angle look. Use as close as the mood needs, no closer.
- When looking down, the horizon may be off the top of the page; when looking up,
  off the bottom. It still governs the horizontal VPs.

## Gotchas
- Keeping verticals parallel while horizontals use a tilted view; mixes
  two-point and three-point in one object.
- Putting VP3 under one side VP: the triangle collapses and boxes shear.
- Figures in three-point must foreshorten too (smaller feet when looking down).

## Verify
- [ ] All verticals meet at VP3.
- [ ] Subject sits within the vanishing triangle near its centre.
- [ ] Near corners do not look acute.
