# Projecting cast shadows in perspective

**Goal:** draw shadows that sit correctly on the ground and on other objects,
instead of guessed smudges.

## Two pieces of information
For every shadow you need: (1) the **direction of light rays** and (2) the
**direction of the shadow on the ground** (the light's footprint direction).
The shadow of a point is where the ray through that point meets the ground line
through that point's foot.

## Point light (lamp, candle)
1. Mark the light source L and drop a vertical to the ground: the **foot of light** F
   (in perspective, on the ground plane).
2. For a vertical pole with top T and base B: draw a line from F through B
   along the ground; draw a line from L through T. Where they cross is the tip of
   the shadow. Shadows radiate away from F.

## Sunlight (parallel rays)
- **Sun behind the viewer or in front**: rays converge to a point; the shadow
  direction lines converge to a **shadow VP on the horizon** directly below (or
  above) the sun's VP. Sun in front of you -> shadows come toward you.
- **Sun to the side (parallel to picture plane)**: shadow lines on the ground are
  horizontal; rays are parallel lines at the sun's angle.
1. Choose the sun VP (above HL if sun in front, below HL if behind viewer).
2. Drop it vertically to the HL: the shadow VP.
3. From each base point draw toward/away from the shadow VP; from each top point draw
   toward the sun VP. Intersections give shadow corners.

## Shadows on walls and steps
When a ground shadow hits a vertical wall, it climbs the wall vertically (for
sunlight, toward the sun's ray direction); find where the ray from the top meets
the wall plane.

## Gotchas
- Mixing a point-light construction with sun shadows in one scene.
- Ignoring that shadow edges soften with distance from the object (penumbra).

## Verify
- [ ] All ground shadows converge consistently (to F or to the shadow VP).
- [ ] Shadow tips sit on rays through the objects' tops.
