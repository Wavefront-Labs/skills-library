# Fixative and framing dry media

**Goal:** keep dry-media drawings from smudging and fading without damaging their
values.

## Fixative types
- **Workable fixative** (e.g. Krylon Workable Fixatif, Lascaux): reduces smudging and
  restores some tooth so you can keep drawing. Light coats between stages.
- **Final fixative**: stronger hold, for finished works. More darkening.
- All fixatives can darken charcoal and pastel and dull lights; spray fixative
  "test strips" on scrap with the same medium first.

## How to spray
1. Outdoors or with extraction; wear a respirator rated for organic vapours.
2. Drawing vertical or flat; can held 30-60 cm away.
3. Start spraying off the edge and sweep across in one continuous pass; then
   cross-pass. Two or three **light** coats beat one heavy one.
4. Never let droplets pool; heavy wet spray dissolves and darkens marks.
5. Some pastellists fix intermediate layers only and leave the final layer unfixed
   to keep its brilliance.

## Storage
- Interleave with glassine or acid-free tissue (not newsprint). Store flat in
  folders or a plan chest; do not roll charcoal or pastel.

## Framing
- **Mat** (acid-free, 4-ply or thicker) or spacers keep the drawing off the glass;
  pastel needs a deeper gap and a dust-catching gap at the bottom.
- **Glazing**: UV-filtering glass or acrylic. Do **not** use acrylic (Plexiglas)
  for unfixed charcoal or pastel: its static charge lifts pigment off the paper.
- Hinge the paper to the backing with Japanese paper and wheat starch paste or
  archival tape; never tape all edges (paper must expand).
- Hang away from direct sun and damp walls.

## Gotchas
- Hairspray as fixative: yellows over time; not archival.
- Spraying in cold or humid air: blooms or uneven spotting.

## Verify
- [ ] Test strip checked for darkening before fixing the work.
- [ ] Drawing does not touch glazing; correct glazing type for medium.
- [ ] Hinged, not taped all round.
