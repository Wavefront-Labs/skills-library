# Choosing format and cropping

**Goal:** decide the shape and edges of the picture so the subject has the right
space and emphasis. The frame is the first compositional decision.

## Format
- **Horizontal**: calm, panoramic, landscape. Wide ratios (2:1) for vistas.
- **Vertical**: height, figures, trees, buildings; more dynamic.
- **Square**: static, centred; harder to make dynamic, good for designed patterns.
- Standard ratios simplify framing: 3:4, 4:5, 2:3 (common stretchers e.g.
  30x40, 40x50, 60x90 cm; 12x16, 16x20, 24x36 in). Check sizes your framer stocks.

## Finding the crop
1. Use a **viewfinder**: a card window in the final ratio, or two L-shaped card
   brackets you can slide. Hold it at different distances to frame tighter or wider.
2. In thumbnails, draw the frame first, then the subject inside it.
3. For an existing drawing or photo reference, lay L-brackets over it to test crops.

## Cropping rules of thumb
- Do not cut figures at joints (wrists, knees, ankles, neck); crop mid-limb instead.
- Avoid tangents with the frame (a head just touching the top edge).
- Leave more space in the direction a figure faces or moves (lead room).
- A tight crop raises intimacy and drama; a wide crop gives context.
- Allow for the frame or mat covering 3-6 mm of each edge.

## Gotchas
- Starting a large canvas without deciding the ratio: the design must be squeezed.
- Cropping out the element that gave scale.

## Verify
- [ ] Format chosen deliberately for the subject.
- [ ] Ratio matches an available support or frame.
- [ ] No joint cuts or edge tangents.
