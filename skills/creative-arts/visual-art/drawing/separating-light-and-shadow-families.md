# Separating light and shadow families

**Goal:** a drawing or painting where every value in the lit side belongs to a
**light family** and every value in the shadow side to a **shadow family**, with
a clear gap between them. This is what makes form read as solidly lit.

## The rule
- The darkest halftone in the light is **lighter** than the lightest reflected
  light in the shadow (Gurney, *Color and Light*, "light and form").
- Local colour changes (a dark shirt vs pale skin) shift values up or down
  *within* each family; they do not cross the gap.

## Method
1. **Squint** until only two shapes remain. Draw that pattern flat (two values,
   no halftones). Check it reads as a lit subject; if not, the shapes are wrong.
2. Choose values: e.g. lights between steps 6 and 9 of a 9-step scale, shadows
   between 1 and 4. Keep step 5 mostly empty as the gap.
3. Render halftones only within the light; reflected light only within the shadow.
4. Compare the darkest halftone and the brightest reflected light side by side
   with a value finder or by squinting.

## Fixing a patchy drawing
- Patchiness usually means halftones are too dark (drifting into the shadow range)
  or reflected light too bright. Lift lights with a kneaded eraser; darken
  reflected lights by toning the whole shadow.
- If the subject has very weak light (overcast), the gap is smaller but still
  present; exaggerate it slightly for clarity.

## Gotchas
- Cast shadows on light surfaces can look "light"; they still belong to the shadow
  family relative to that surface.
- Rendering inside shadows with as much detail as the lights: keep shadows
  simpler and lower contrast.

## Verify
- [ ] Squinted, the work separates into two clear shapes.
- [ ] Darkest halftone is lighter than brightest reflected light.
- [ ] Detail and contrast are concentrated in the light family.
