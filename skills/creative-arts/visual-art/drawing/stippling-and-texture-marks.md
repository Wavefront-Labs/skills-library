# Stippling and texture marks

**Goal:** build smooth tone or specific surface textures from dots and small
marks; standard in scientific illustration (Guild of Natural Science Illustrators)
and pen rendering.

## Stippling
- **Tools**: technical pens (Rotring Isograph, Micron/Pigma 005-08); a 0.25-0.35 mm
  point gives good control. Smooth Bristol or drafting film.
- **Value = dot density**, not dot size. Keep dots round: hold the pen vertical and
  lift straight up. A dragged pen leaves "tails" that read as hatching.
- Lay out the lightest areas first with sparse dots, then build density toward
  the darks. Darkest areas can begin with a tight hatch underlayer then be dotted
  over, saving hours (traditional illustrators do this with care).
- **Randomise** placement. Rows or clusters create visible patterns.
- Edges: define form edges by density change, not outlines, for a soft look.

## Other texture marks
- **Scribble / squirkle**: small looping marks for foliage, fur, rough stone.
- **Broken line**: wood grain, bark.
- **Parallel short dashes**: grass, hair direction.
- **Cross-hatching**: smooth surfaces, cloth.
Make a texture swatch sheet of 10-20 surfaces before a big drawing.

## Practice
Draw a 1 x 6 inch value strip in stipple from white to near black; then a sphere
in stipple only.

## Gotchas
- Time: stippling is slow; plan the area. Large darks can take hours.
- Dot gain on soft paper: dots spread and merge.
- Inconsistent pen size changes the texture across the drawing.

## Verify
- [ ] Dots are round with no tails.
- [ ] Value transitions are smooth and pattern-free.
- [ ] Texture marks differ clearly between materials.
