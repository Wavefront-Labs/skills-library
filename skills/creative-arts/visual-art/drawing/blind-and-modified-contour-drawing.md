# Blind and modified contour drawing

**Goal:** train the eye to lead the hand by drawing edges slowly while looking
mostly at the subject, as set out in Nicolaides' *The Natural Way to Draw* and
taught in BFA foundation courses.

## Blind contour
1. Subject: your own hand, a shoe, a crumpled paper bag, a plant.
2. Fix the paper with tape so it cannot move. Place the pen on the paper.
3. Look **only at the subject**, never at the paper. Imagine the pen point is
   touching the edge you are looking at.
4. Move the eye slowly along an edge and the pen at exactly the same speed.
   Include inner edges (folds, creases), not only the outline.
5. 5-10 minutes per drawing. Do not lift the pen; do not peek.

## Modified contour
- Same slow, continuous line, but you may glance at the paper briefly at
  junctions to re-place the pen; about 90 percent of the time eyes stay on the subject.
- Proportions now begin to hold together; it is a bridge to observational drawing.

## Assessment
The result will be distorted; that is expected. Judge by:
- Is the line sensitive (changes in direction recorded, no generic curves)?
- Did the time spent match the complexity of the edge?
- Are interior contours included?

## Gotchas
- Drawing fast "to finish": the exercise is about slowness.
- Drawing what you know (symbolic eye shapes) instead of the edge seen.
- Lifting the pen and starting a new outline each time.

## Verify
- [ ] Eyes stayed on the subject almost the whole time.
- [ ] Line speed was slow and continuous.
- [ ] Interior contours as well as silhouette were recorded.
