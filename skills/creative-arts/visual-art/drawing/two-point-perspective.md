# Two-point perspective

**Goal:** draw objects seen corner-on, like a building at a street corner, where
two sets of horizontal edges recede to two VPs.

## Setup
1. Draw the HL at eye level.
2. Place **two VPs on the HL, far apart**, often off the page. Tape extra paper
   or mark them on the table. VPs too close together cause the classic
   distortion: the near corner becomes an acute angle (sharper than 90 degrees).
3. For a box whose sides are at 90 degrees to each other, the two VPs and the
   station point (your eye) form a right angle (Robertson, *How to Draw*). In
   practice: the closer the box sits to one VP, the further the other VP is.

## Box construction
1. Draw the nearest vertical edge.
2. Connect its top and bottom to both VPs.
3. Place the two far verticals by eye (or measured), between the converging lines.
4. Connect their tops/bottoms to the opposite VPs to find the back edges.
5. Check: the near corner, top and bottom, must look **90 degrees or more**.

## Buildings
- Windows, doors and rooflines on each face share that face's VP.
- Inclined planes (roofs) vanish to points directly above or below the face's VP
  on a vertical line (vanishing trace).

## Gotchas
- VPs on the page edge for a big object: extreme distortion.
- Verticals converging accidentally: in two-point, verticals stay parallel
  (use three-point if you want convergence).
- Different objects rotated differently need their own pair of VPs on the same HL.

## Verify
- [ ] Each face's horizontal edges meet at its VP.
- [ ] Near corners are not acute.
- [ ] All VPs lie on one horizon.
