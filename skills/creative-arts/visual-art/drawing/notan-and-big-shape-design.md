# Notan and big-shape design

**Goal:** an abstract arrangement of dark and light shapes that is pleasing on
its own, under any subject. *Notan* (Japanese for light-dark harmony) was brought
into Western art teaching by Arthur Wesley Dow's *Composition* (1899).

## Two-value notan
1. In a small box, reduce the subject to **black and white only**. Decide for
   every area: light or dark. No outlines, no grey.
2. Look at it upside down. Does it make an interesting, balanced pattern of
   shapes? Are positive (object) and negative (background) shapes both designed?
3. Make several versions, changing which areas join into a single dark or light.

## Grouping shapes
- **Link** adjacent shapes of similar value into one larger mass (e.g. a dark
  figure merges into a dark doorway). Fewer, bigger shapes read more strongly.
- **Vary** shape sizes: one dominant mass, a few medium, some small accents.
  Avoid equal-sized, evenly spaced shapes.
- **Interlock** light and dark like puzzle pieces; let edges of masses be varied
  (some straight, some irregular).

## Three-value notan
Add a mid grey. Typical structure: dominant mid-grey, a significant dark, a
small amount of light (or any other proportion, but one value must dominate).

## Gotchas
- Thinking in objects instead of shapes; reduce by squinting.
- Checkerboard patterns: values alternating evenly look busy.
- Focal point lost because the strongest contrast is elsewhere.

## Verify
- [ ] Two-value version reads as a strong design upside down.
- [ ] One value dominates in area; shapes vary in size.
- [ ] Highest contrast junction is at the intended focal point.
