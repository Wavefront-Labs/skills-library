# Vine and compressed charcoal and erasing

**Goal:** choose the right charcoal for each stage and use erasing as a drawing tool.

## Types
| Type | Made from | Behaviour |
|------|-----------|-----------|
| Vine / willow | Burnt sticks of vine or willow | Light, grey-black, very erasable, dusty; ideal for block-in |
| Compressed | Charcoal powder plus binder, pressed | Rich velvety black, hard to erase, more permanent |
| Charcoal pencil | Compressed charcoal in wood, hard to soft | Controlled lines and detail; sharpen with knife and sandpaper |
| Powdered | Loose powder | Broad toned grounds applied with cloth or brush |

## Order of use
1. Block in with **vine**: adjustable, wipes off with a chamois or cloth.
2. Establish shadows with vine, then firm them with **compressed** or soft
   charcoal pencil once shapes are right.
3. Detail and edges with **charcoal pencils**.
Compressed over vine works; vine over compressed barely shows.

## Erasing and subtractive drawing
- **Kneaded eraser**: press and lift (blot) to lighten without smearing; shape to a
  point or edge to draw light lines into tone. Knead clean often.
- **Chamois / cloth**: wipes vine back to a ghost; good for big corrections.
- **Plastic/vinyl eraser**: removes more, but can damage paper and smear compressed.
- **Electric eraser or eraser pencil**: precise highlights.
- **Subtractive method**: tone the whole sheet with powdered or vine charcoal to a
  mid value, then pull out lights with erasers and add darks. Fast way to think
  in masses.

## Dust and health
Tap the board over a bin, do not blow (inhaling fine carbon dust is unpleasant
and irritating). Work upright so dust falls away. Use a slip sheet under your hand.

## Gotchas
- Starting with compressed: hard to correct.
- Rubbing with fingers fills the tooth and leaves skin oil.
- Heavy fixative darkens and kills the lights; see fixative skill.

## Verify
- [ ] Block-in done in an erasable medium.
- [ ] Lights pulled with a clean kneaded eraser without smears.
