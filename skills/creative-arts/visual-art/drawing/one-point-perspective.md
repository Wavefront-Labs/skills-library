# One-point perspective

**Goal:** draw scenes viewed square-on (a corridor, a road, a room's back wall)
where one set of edges goes straight away from the viewer.

## Elements
- **Horizon line (HL)** = the viewer's eye level. It is always at eye height,
  whatever the land does.
- **Vanishing point (VP)** on the HL, directly in front of the viewer (the
  centre of vision).
- Three sets of lines: verticals stay vertical; horizontals parallel to the
  picture plane stay horizontal; **depth lines** (perpendicular to the picture
  plane) converge to the VP.

## Construction
1. Choose the eye level: low HL gives a worm's-eye mood, high HL shows more ground.
2. Place the VP on the HL. Off-centre VPs make more natural pictures.
3. Draw the frontal face of a box as a true rectangle.
4. Draw lines from its corners to the VP.
5. Cut the box's depth with a second horizontal/vertical line parallel to the front.
   Judge depth by eye or use measuring points (see dividing-and-multiplying-in-perspective).
6. Erase hidden lines; boxes above the HL show their underside, below show their top.

## Cone of vision
Keep the main subject within roughly a 60-degree cone around the VP. Objects drawn
far out to the side in strict one-point stretch badly; if you need them, switch to
two-point.

## Gotchas
- Horizon placed at the top of the ground instead of eye level.
- Depth lines from objects parallel to the picture plane drawn to the VP.
- Spacing equal-sized objects equally on the page: they should get closer as they recede.

## Verify
- [ ] All depth edges hit the single VP.
- [ ] Frontal faces are true rectangles.
- [ ] Eye-level figures' heads sit on the HL (if viewer and figures stand on the same ground).
