# Safety and calibration tools — intense play, without real harm

**Goal:** let participants play hard emotional or physical scenes while every person
can adjust, pause or leave instantly, and leave the event in good shape.
Works for LARP, immersive theatre and (scaled down) tabletop.

## 1. Before the game: publish expectations
- Event description states themes and content (violence, romance, horror, oppression),
  physicality level and any physical contact rules. People can't consent to what they
  weren't told.
- **Lines and veils** collected in advance or at the workshop: *lines* are content that
  won't appear at all; *veils* happen off-scene ("fade to black").
- Name one or more **off-game contacts / safety team** people can go to, with a
  visible sign (armband, lanyard) and a quiet off-game room.

## 2. The pre-game workshop (non-negotiable for intense games)
Typically 30–90 minutes, depending on game length. Cover:
1. **Why** the tools exist — using them is normal, not failure. Organisers model it.
2. **Each mechanic, practised physically** in pairs, not just explained.
3. **Calibration exercises:** pairs play a short scene and turn intensity up and down
   using the brake/escalate cues so everyone feels the range.
4. Physical contact rules (e.g. hand on shoulder only, ask first) and combat safety if relevant.
5. How to leave the game and where to go.

## 3. Common in-play mechanics
Exact names and gestures vary by community; teach whichever your event uses, consistently.
- **Cut** (spoken "cut" or a defined phrase): stop the scene immediately, out of
  character. Anyone may call it; nobody asks why in the moment.
- **Brake** (or "slow down"): continue, but de-escalate now. Allows nuance without stopping.
- **Escalate/"more"** (optional): consent to raise intensity.
- **Tap-out / walk-away:** a gesture (e.g. hand raised, or tapping the partner twice)
  meaning "I'm leaving this scene"; the player may leave without explanation.
- **Look-down:** hand over eyes means "I'm not here", so others ignore the player as
  they cross a space.
- **OK check-in:** an OK hand sign as a question; answer thumbs up (fine), flat hand
  (unsure — check in), thumbs down (get me out). Non-answers count as needing a check-in.
- **X-card** (tabletop or small groups): tap a card to remove the current content, no reasons needed.

## 4. Facilitation during play
- Organisers and NPC crew watch for distress the player can't signal, and check in
  off-game discreetly.
- Respect every call immediately, every time. One ignored brake undermines the system for everyone.
- Keep the safety room staffed and stocked (water, snacks, quiet).

## 5. After: de-roling and debrief
- **De-roling:** a short ritual to step out of character (removing a costume piece,
  stating your own name).
- **Debrief:** structured sharing in small groups — what happened, what stood out,
  how you feel — facilitated so one person doesn't dominate. Offer it right after the
  game, and point to follow-up contact for later.
- **Bleed** (emotions or relationships crossing between character and player) is
  expected after intense games; normalise it and remind people to check in days later.

## Gotchas
- **Explaining without practising** — people don't use tools they haven't rehearsed.
- **Tools aren't a licence** for organisers to skip content design; remove content that
  doesn't serve the game.
- **Mixed communities** may have learned different gestures. Always restate yours.
- **Physical safety** (weapons, stunts, terrain) needs its own rules and possibly
  first-aid cover; this skill covers emotional/social safety primarily.
- Mechanic names above are those common in Nordic-influenced larp; local variants
  differ and some details here may not match a given community.

## Verify
- [ ] Content and physicality were disclosed before sign-up.
- [ ] Workshop included physical practice of every mechanic and a calibration exercise.
- [ ] A visible safety contact and off-game room exist throughout.
- [ ] Every cut/brake during play was honoured immediately.
- [ ] De-roling and a facilitated debrief happened; follow-up contact shared.
