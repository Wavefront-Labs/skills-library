# Edu-larp design

**Goal:** use role-play to help participants learn concepts, perspectives or skills.

Educational larp (edu-larp) is used in schools and training; the Nordic scene has influential practitioners and schools that use it, and Nordic resources are a good place to read more.

## Procedure
1. **State learning goals:** what should participants understand or be able to do afterwards? Tie them to the curriculum or training outcomes.
2. **Choose a scenario** where the learning goals naturally arise: a historical negotiation, a science dilemma, a workplace conflict, a language situation.
3. **Keep the design simple:** short character sheets, clear objectives, a simple structure, and a time limit that fits the lesson.
4. **Prepare materials:** role cards, information sheets, props and a facilitator guide.
5. **Brief participants:** explain purpose, rules, safety tools and that it is voluntary or can be adapted for those who prefer observing.
6. **Run the game:** facilitators watch for learning moments, safety concerns, and time.
7. **Debrief thoroughly:** give as much time to reflection as to play. Ask about what happened, what they felt, what they learned, and how it connects to the goals (see larp-debrief-formats).
8. **Assess learning** through reflection tasks, discussion, or follow-up work, not only participation.
9. **Adapt for age and context:** younger groups need shorter games, clearer rules and less emotionally heavy content. Follow school safeguarding policies.
10. **Iterate** from feedback.

## Gotchas
- Too much game and too little reflection weakens learning.
- Do not grade students on emotional performance.
- Avoid roles that make students play oppressors or victims without careful framing and debrief.

## Verify
- [ ] Learning goals written and tied to the scenario.
- [ ] Debrief time planned and facilitated.
- [ ] Safety and age adaptations applied.
