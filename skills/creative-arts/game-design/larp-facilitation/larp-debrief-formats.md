# Larp debrief formats

**Goal:** help participants step out of character, process what happened, and leave in
good shape, with a structure that respects people who want to talk and people who don't.

## Components
1. **De-roling:** a short ritual to leave the character behind (remove a costume piece,
   say your own name, shake off the character physically).
2. **Small-group debrief:** groups of 4-8 (often by character group or with a facilitator);
   each person gets uninterrupted time.
3. **Plenary:** short whole-group closing (thanks, highlights, logistics). Large plenaries
   for emotional processing tend to be dominated by a few voices.
4. **Follow-up:** contact info for organisers / safety team after the event; optional
   online debrief days later.

## Guided questions (pick a few)
- What is one moment you want to remember?
- What is something your character did that you want to leave behind?
- Who would you like to thank, and for what?
- What will you take with you from the larp?

## Rules
- Participation is voluntary; passing is always fine.
- Talk about your own experience; don't critique others' play.
- What is shared stays in the group (agree confidentiality).
- Facilitators watch for distress and offer private follow-up.

## Post-larp blues
Some participants feel low or disoriented days after intense larps ("post-larp
depression" or blues). Prepare them in the debrief: it's common, reach out to others,
talk to organisers, get rest.

## Gotchas
- Skipping debrief to pack up is tempting; schedule it as part of the larp.
- "War stories" sharing is fun but different from processing; give space for both.
- Debriefs are not therapy; know when to refer someone to professional support.

## Verify
- [ ] De-roling step scheduled.
- [ ] Small-group debrief with facilitators and guided questions.
- [ ] Follow-up contact shared; online debrief offered for intense larps.
