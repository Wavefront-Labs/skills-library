# Larp runsheet and timeline

**Goal:** organisers and crew know what happens when, who does it, and what to do when
it slips, so the larp runs smoothly and plot events land.

## Runsheet columns
| Time | Duration | Event | Location | Owner | Cue / trigger | Crew needed | Notes / fallback |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 09:00 | 30 | player arrival, check-in | hall | Ana | - | 2 | late arrivals to info desk |
| 10:00 | 120 | workshops | hall | Ben | - | 3 | cut warm-up if late |
| 13:00 | - | game on | all | lead | gong | all | - |
| 15:30 | 15 | messenger arrives with news | gate | NPC team | after council scene ends | 1 NPC | if council runs long, hold until 16:00 |
| 18:00 | 60 | dinner (in-game feast) | hall | kitchen | - | 4 | dietary list |

## Procedure
1. Draft the **macro timeline** (arrival, workshops, acts, meals, end, debrief, cleanup).
2. Add **scheduled plot injections** with cues rather than fixed times where possible
   ("after the council scene" with a latest time).
3. Add **crew/NPC calls**: who must be where, in costume, when; briefing times.
4. Add **logistics**: meals, set changes, lighting/sound cues, safety team shifts.
5. Assign an **owner** for every line.
6. Walk through the runsheet with the team; time transitions.
7. Print copies; keep one master with a designated runner who tracks timing on the day.

## On the day
- A **game control** point (room or person) where changes are decided and communicated.
- Track slips; apply fallbacks; communicate to crew (radios/messaging).
- Log what actually happened for the post-mortem.

## Gotchas
- Workshops and check-in almost always run long; build in buffer.
- Fixed-time plot events can land in the middle of a player-driven scene and ruin it;
  use cue-based triggers with windows.
- NPC crew need breaks and meals scheduled too.

## Verify
- [ ] Every line has an owner and a cue or time.
- [ ] Plot injections have windows and fallbacks.
- [ ] Crew calls and breaks scheduled.
- [ ] Walkthrough done with the team.
