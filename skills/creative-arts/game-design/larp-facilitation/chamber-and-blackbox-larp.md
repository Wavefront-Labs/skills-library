# Chamber and blackbox larp

**Goal:** an intense, compact larp that runs in a plain room in a few hours, easy to repeat or tour.

Chamber larp comes from the Nordic larp tradition; the term describes games for small groups in small, simple spaces, often with a focus on character and emotion over spectacle. The format is well described on Nordic Larp resources.

## Design principles
1. **Keep it small:** a modest number of players (a handful to a dozen or so) and a single room.
2. **Use a blackbox space:** a neutral room with minimal props; lighting, sound and a few chairs and tables suffice. Players imagine the rest.
3. **Rely on workshops:** a pre-game session builds the characters, relationships and trust; it is part of the experience, not an extra.
4. **Use meta-techniques** (see larp-meta-techniques) to depict what the room cannot: inner thoughts, flashbacks, imagined locations.
5. **Control time:** a firm start and end; scenes can be cued by sound or light.
6. **Leave room for debrief:** short games are intense; schedule time to decompress.
7. **Write a portable package:** character sheets, a facilitator guide, music list, lighting plan and safety instructions, so others can run it.
8. **Keep logistics light:** tickets, venue and costume needs are far simpler than for field larps.

## Gotchas
- Intense content in a short time needs careful safety design (see safety-and-calibration-tools).
- Sparse sets mean the facilitator must describe the world well.
- Do not mistake small for easy; precision matters more with fewer players.

## Verify
- [ ] Group size and room suit the design.
- [ ] Workshop, meta-techniques and debrief scheduled.
- [ ] Package complete for another facilitator.
