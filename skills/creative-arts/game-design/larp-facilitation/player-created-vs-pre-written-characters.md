# Player-created vs pre-written characters

**Goal:** choose a character approach that fits the game, the players and your workload.

## Compare
- **Pre-written characters:** the organiser writes characters, relationships and secrets. Strong plot integration and tight webs of conflict; heavy writing workload; players may not connect with an assigned role; needs casting.
- **Player-created characters:** players build their own from a setting guide. Strong ownership and fit; less control for the organiser; risk of weak ties and unbalanced hooks; needs workshop time to weave connections.
- **Hybrid:** organisers provide archetypes, roles, or skeleton characters; players fill in details and add ties.

## Procedure
1. **Match to game type:** tight chamber games and plot-driven mysteries suit pre-written characters; open sandbox games suit player-created ones.
2. **Estimate workload:** pre-written characters cost many hours per character; plan time.
3. **Cast pre-written roles** using player questionnaires (see larp-casting).
4. **For player-created,** give a setting primer, character questions, and required ties (each player adds ties to two others), then review.
5. **Allow tweaks:** players should be able to adjust pre-written characters to their comfort.
6. **Check content limits** in either model against lines and veils.
7. **Gather feedback** after the game to improve the approach next time.

## Gotchas
- Players may feel locked in by heavy pre-written details; keep room to improvise.
- Player-created characters often cluster in popular archetypes; set quotas.
- Late sign-ups complicate pre-written casting.

## Verify
- [ ] Approach justified by game type.
- [ ] Workload and casting time planned.
- [ ] Character adjustment process defined.
