# Larp character sheet formats

**Goal:** sheets players will actually read, remember and play from.

## Structure
1. **Header:** name, age or role, a one-line concept, and the contact for questions.
2. **Quick summary:** three or four lines that capture who they are and what they want.
3. **Background:** short and relevant; skip things that cannot affect play.
4. **Goals and conflicts:** what the character wants, what stands in the way.
5. **Relationships:** a short list of ties from the relationship map, one or two lines each.
6. **Secrets:** place them in a separate section or sealed page, so players can choose when to reveal.
7. **Play advice:** how to portray the character, scenes to seek out, and what to avoid.
8. **Practical info:** costume suggestions, props, skills, and any mechanics.
9. **Content notes:** flag themes that touch lines and veils.

## Layout tips
- Aim for one to two pages; very long sheets are often unread.
- Use headings and bullets; put the most important information first.
- Send sheets early enough to read before the workshop.
- Offer accessible formats: large print, plain text, screen-reader friendly files.
- Keep a short version for use during the game.

## Gotchas
- Walls of backstory hide the hooks.
- Spoiling other players' secrets in a shared document; separate documents per player.
- Prescribing emotions too strictly removes player choice.

## Verify
- [ ] Sheet fits on one or two pages with a summary at the top.
- [ ] Hooks and relationships clear.
- [ ] Secrets and content notes separated.
