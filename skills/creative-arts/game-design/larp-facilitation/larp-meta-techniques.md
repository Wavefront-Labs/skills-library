# Larp meta-techniques

**Goal:** tools that let players express inner life, past events or alternate scenes
that ordinary in-character play can't show, without breaking the experience.

The Nordic Larp Wiki defines **meta-techniques** as techniques that communicate
information to other players outside ordinary in-character interaction, e.g.
**monologues** (a character speaks thoughts aloud that others "don't hear") and the
**black box** (a separate space where scenes outside the larp's time and place - dreams,
flashbacks, futures - are played with lights and direction).

## Common techniques
| Technique | What it does | Signal |
|---|---|---|
| Monologue | speak inner thoughts aloud; others hear as players, not characters | a gesture or "monologue" call |
| Inner voice | another player voices a character's thoughts | standing behind the character |
| Black box | play flashbacks, dreams, future scenes in a separate room | going to the black box |
| Bird-in-ear | an organiser whispers information or prompts | quiet approach |
| Act breaks / time jumps | skip time; change the situation | announced by organisers |
| Ars amandi / ars ordo | abstracted touch for intimacy / violence | defined touch zones |

## Procedure
1. Pick only the techniques the vision needs (each adds cognitive load).
2. **Workshop each technique** before the larp: demonstrate, let players practise, and
   agree signals (see pre-larp workshop skills).
3. Define who can trigger it (anyone, organisers only) and how often.
4. For black box: staff it (a director), prepare lighting/sound, and prepare scene
   prompts.
5. Debrief afterwards on whether the techniques supported or broke immersion.

## Gotchas
- Techniques introduced without practice get ignored or misused.
- Overuse of monologues interrupts scenes; give guidance on timing.
- Abstract intimacy/violence techniques need clear consent rules and opt-outs.

## Verify
- [ ] Chosen techniques listed with purpose and signals.
- [ ] Each practised in a workshop.
- [ ] Black box staffed and scene prompts prepared (if used).
