# Larp casting

**Goal:** each participant gets a character that fits what they want to play and what
they're comfortable with, so the whole larp works.

Pre-written-character larps (common in Nordic and chamber larp) typically cast from a
questionnaire rather than letting players pick characters directly.

## Casting questionnaire (examples of questions)
- What do you want to experience? (themes, intensity, leadership vs follower, action vs
  drama)
- Play preferences: lots of interaction or quieter play; public vs private conflict.
- Content limits: themes or actions they don't want to play (lines).
- Physical/accessibility needs relevant to roles.
- Requests: play with/against a friend; avoid playing with an ex.
- Gender play preferences (if the larp allows playing any gender).
- Experience level with this style of larp.

## Procedure
1. **Character list with casting notes:** for each character, what it demands (intensity,
   centrality, physicality, themes).
2. **Collect questionnaires** with a deadline.
3. **First pass:** match on hard constraints (limits, accessibility, requests).
4. **Second pass:** match on desires; balance centrality so experienced players don't
   take all lead roles unless intended.
5. **Review as a team**; check group dynamics (who is in each faction/family).
6. **Send casting** with the character and an invitation to raise concerns early; allow
   swaps within reason.

## Gotchas
- Casting by "who we know" disadvantages new players; use the questionnaire for
  everyone.
- Late dropouts break character webs; keep a list of which characters are essential and
  have reserves.
- Players' stated preferences may differ from what they enjoy; offer guidance in the
  questionnaire with examples.

## Verify
- [ ] Every character has casting notes.
- [ ] No casting violates a stated limit or accessibility need.
- [ ] Group compositions reviewed as a team.
- [ ] Dropout plan for essential characters.
