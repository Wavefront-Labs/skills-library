# Alternate reality game design

**Goal:** an interactive story played across real-world media (websites, email, phone
lines, physical locations, social media), solved collectively, and run live by the
designers.

ARGs are a form of pervasive game (Montola, Stenros and Waern, *Pervasive Games: Theory
and Design*, 2009, discuss expanding play into everyday life). Well-known examples
include *The Beast* (2001, for the film A.I.) and *I Love Bees* (2004, for Halo 2).

## Building blocks
| Element | Notes |
|---|---|
| Rabbit hole | the first clue that draws players in (hidden URL, odd ad, mailed object) |
| Trail | puzzles and content across media, each leading to the next |
| Puppet masters | the designers, who run the game live and respond to players |
| Curtain | the convention that the game doesn't acknowledge it's a game ("This is not a game") |
| Community | players organise in forums/Discord; collective intelligence solves puzzles fast |
| Live events | phone calls, real-world meetups, timed drops |

## Procedure
1. **Story bible** with the backstory, characters, timeline and planned reveals.
2. **Trail map:** dependency chart of clues/media (see puzzle dependency charts); plan
   parallel threads because communities solve linear trails very fast.
3. **Pacing:** schedule content drops; prepare buffer content for when players solve
   faster than expected (they usually do).
4. **Live operations:** puppet-master team, monitoring community channels, the ability
   to adapt or improvise.
5. **Boundaries and safety:** no instructions that could lead players to trespass or
   endanger themselves; clear real-world contact rules; privacy for players; review
   legal issues (impersonation, data collection, minors).
6. **Ending and archive:** a satisfying conclusion; publish or allow community archives.

## Gotchas
- Collective puzzle-solving makes hard puzzles fall in minutes; design for crowds.
- Fiction that is too convincing can alarm the public or authorities; use subtle signals
  that it's fiction where needed.
- Live ARGs are labour-intensive; scope the run time to the team's capacity.
