# Player character design

**Goal:** a protagonist the player can inhabit, whose motives, abilities and voice match
what the player is doing with the controller.

## The spectrum
| Type | Example | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| Blank slate / silent | Gordon Freeman, Link | maximum projection; weak dramatic presence |
| Customisable avatar | Skyrim, Mass Effect Shepard | player identity; huge VO and animation cost |
| Defined protagonist | Kratos, Aloy, Geralt | strong story; player may disagree with the character |
| Ensemble / switching | GTA V, Final Fantasy | variety; diluted attachment |

Schell's point: players identify most with avatars that are idealised and somewhat blank,
so even defined characters leave room for the player.

## Procedure
1. **Pick the point on the spectrum from the pillars.** A game about player expression
   needs a blank or custom avatar; a game about a specific person's journey needs a defined one.
2. **Align goals:** at every chapter, write the character's goal and the player's goal.
   When they diverge (the character hesitates, the player wants to rush), expect frustration.
3. **Abilities express the fantasy:** list the verbs; each should be something this
   character would do. A pacifist character with a headshot mechanic is ludonarrative dissonance.
4. **Decide voice rules:** never speaks, barks only, speaks in cutscenes, fully voiced
   dialogue choices. Document when the character comments on play (hints, reactions).
5. **Silhouette and readability:** distinct silhouette, readable from behind at gameplay
   camera distance; animation sells weight and personality more than face detail.
6. **Growth:** match mechanical progression to character change where possible.

## Gotchas
- Defined protagonists who comment on puzzles can nag; cap hint barks and let players disable them.
- Customisable avatars in cutscenes: test the extremes of the character creator (size, colour, gear).
- Player-character backstory revealed late can contradict choices the player already made.

## Verify
- [ ] Spectrum choice written in the narrative doc with reason.
- [ ] Per-chapter table of character goal vs player goal shows no unplanned divergence.
- [ ] Silhouette test at gameplay distance passes.
