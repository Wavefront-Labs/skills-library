# Character arcs in games

**Goal:** characters who change believably over a game, with the change visible in play
and not only in cutscenes.

## Arc anatomy
- **Want:** the external goal the character pursues (revenge, escape, the throne).
- **Need:** the internal truth they lack (trust, forgiveness, letting go).
- **Flaw / lie:** the belief that blocks the need.
- **Arc type:** positive (lie overcome), negative (lie embraced), flat (character stays
  true and changes the world around them).

## Mapping to the game
| Beat | Example placement |
|---|---|
| Establish want and flaw | prologue, tutorial |
| First test of the flaw | end of act 1 |
| Midpoint shift (want reframed) | midpoint boss / revelation |
| Dark moment (flaw costs something) | late act 2 |
| Choice that proves change | climax, ideally a player action |
| New normal | epilogue, post-game dialogue |

## Show change through play
- **Mechanics change:** Kratos in God of War (2018) learning restraint parallels the
  axe recall and Atreus commands.
- **Barks and idle lines change** after the midpoint; cheap and highly noticed.
- **Companion behaviour** in combat or traversal changes (they cover you, refuse orders).
- **Player choice as the arc's proof:** let the player perform the decisive act.

## Optional characters
- Companion arcs gated by approval or side quests need a minimum version visible on the
  critical path, or most players never see the character change.
- Track arc state per character in a table: beat, trigger, where, required or optional.

## Gotchas
- Arcs advanced only in cutscenes stall when the player spends 10 hours in side content;
  add ambient beats.
- Player choices that let the protagonist reject the arc need a written alternative ending
  for the arc, not just the plot.
- Do not resolve the arc before the final mechanic test, or the climax feels hollow.

## Verify
- [ ] Each major character has want, need, flaw and arc type written down.
- [ ] Every arc beat mapped to a chapter and a delivery method.
- [ ] At least one arc beat expressed by mechanics or player action.
