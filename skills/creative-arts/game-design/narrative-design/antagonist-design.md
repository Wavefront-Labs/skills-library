# Antagonist design

**Goal:** an opposing force the player understands, feels throughout the game and is
satisfied to confront.

## Build the antagonist
1. **Goal:** what they want, stated in one sentence that they would agree with.
2. **Reason:** why they believe it is right; the best antagonists have a point.
3. **Method:** the means that make them the opposition (cruelty, control, sacrifice).
4. **Mirror:** how they reflect the protagonist or player (same goal, different method;
   the hero's possible future). Rogers and Toolbox both stress the mirror for resonance.
5. **Power curve:** they must stay ahead of the player until the end; plan what keeps
   them out of reach (armies, distance, legitimacy, a mystery identity).

## Presence (the hardest part in games)
Players spend most of the game away from the villain. Plan presence touchpoints:
| Method | Example |
|---|---|
| Direct contact | radio / broadcast taunts (Andrew Ryan, SHODAN, Handsome Jack) |
| Consequences | towns burned, allies taken, the world visibly changed |
| Agents | lieutenants with their own mini-arcs as mid-bosses |
| Environment | propaganda, statues, laws, graffiti |
| Personal stakes | they take something from the player character |

Aim for a touchpoint at least once per chapter.

## The confrontation
- The final fight should test the skill the game taught and express the conflict
  thematically (e.g. a boss using the player's own abilities to show the mirror).
- Give a conversation or choice before or after the fight; the player wants to answer them.
- Reveals (the villain was right, was someone else) need clues planted earlier; list them.

## Gotchas
- A villain introduced only in the final act reads as a random boss.
- Constant taunting without consequence makes the antagonist toothless.
- Sympathetic villains need the player to see their reason in play, not only be told.

## Verify
- [ ] Goal, reason, method and mirror written down.
- [ ] Presence touchpoint table covers every chapter.
- [ ] Final confrontation tested for both mechanical and narrative payoff.
