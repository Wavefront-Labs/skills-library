# Environmental storytelling vignettes

**Goal:** players reconstruct stories from the arrangement of objects and spaces,
without text or dialogue, and feel clever for doing it.

Harvey Smith and Matthias Worch's GDC 2010 talk "What Happened Here? Environmental
Storytelling" frames it as staging the world so players infer past events. Henry
Jenkins ("Game Design as Narrative Architecture") describes **embedded narratives**:
story information placed in the space for players to discover.

## Building a vignette
1. **Write the micro-story in one or two sentences:** "A family barricaded the kitchen;
   the father went for help and never returned; the child hid in the cupboard."
2. **List the evidence** that implies each beat: barricaded door, chairs stacked, a
   child's drawing in the cupboard, one coat missing from the hooks, footprints outside.
3. **Stage cause and effect:** objects arranged so the sequence reads (the barricade
   faces inward, scratches on the outside).
4. **Place on sightlines:** the key prop should be visible from the entry point; details
   reward closer inspection.
5. **Use contrast:** one out-of-place object in an otherwise orderly room draws the eye.
6. **Keep it consistent with the world bible.**

## Rules of thumb
- One clear story per space; overlapping vignettes muddy.
- Leave gaps; the player's inference is the payoff.
- Pair with light (see level-design lighting guidance) to direct attention.
- Notes and audio logs can confirm, but should not replace, the staged evidence.

## Testing
After a playtest, ask: "What do you think happened in the kitchen?" Compare answers to
the micro-story. If most players can't reconstruct it, add or strengthen evidence;
if everyone states it identically from one note, the staging isn't doing the work.

## Gotchas
- Clichéd vignettes (skeleton on a toilet) become jokes; know when you want humour.
- Art passes may move or remove props; mark story-critical props.
- Highly detailed vignettes are expensive; prioritise on the critical path.

## Verify
- [ ] Micro-story and evidence list written for each vignette.
- [ ] Key prop visible from entry.
- [ ] Most testers reconstruct the core story.
