# Narrative design document

**Goal:** a single, maintained document that tells the team what the story is, how it is
delivered through play, and how much content it requires.

The Game Narrative Toolbox covers narrative documentation (outlines, character
documents, world documents) alongside writing for games.

## Sections
1. **Premise and themes:** one paragraph premise; 2-3 themes; tone references.
2. **Narrative pillars:** what the story must deliver (aligned with design pillars).
3. **Story outline:** act/chapter/level breakdown with key beats and the player goal
   for each; links to the level beat chart.
4. **Characters:** sheet per major character (want, need, arc, voice sample, gameplay
   role).
5. **World summary:** key facts; full detail lives in the world bible.
6. **Delivery methods:** where story is told (cutscenes, in-game dialogue, barks,
   environmental storytelling, collectibles, UI text) and the rules for each (e.g. no
   cutscene over 2 minutes; exposition only in safe spaces).
7. **Branching overview:** major choices, consequences, endings (flowchart).
8. **Content budget:** estimated word count / VO lines / cinematic minutes per chapter.
9. **Open questions** with owners.

## Procedure
1. Draft sections 1-3 early, before level briefs.
2. Review with design leads: does every beat have a gameplay home?
3. Keep the outline in sync with level changes (version and date the doc).
4. Use the content budget to plan VO and localisation costs.

## Gotchas
- Narrative docs that ignore gameplay produce stories that need cutscenes to deliver.
- Word counts grow silently; track against budget each milestone.
- A narrative doc nobody updates is worse than none; assign an owner.

## Verify
- [ ] Premise, pillars, outline, characters, delivery rules and budget present.
- [ ] Every story beat mapped to a level or system.
- [ ] Content budget tracked against actuals.
