# Playtester recruitment

**Goal:** sessions with people who resemble the intended audience, in enough numbers that one odd tester does not steer the design.

## Procedure
1. **Write the target player** in two lines (genre habits, tabletop/console experience, age band if relevant). Test questions differ by stage: early rules tests can use experienced hobbyists; onboarding and usability tests need newcomers.
2. **Write a short screener** (5-8 questions): games played recently, hours per week, familiarity with comparable titles, availability slots. Hide the "right" answer so people cannot guess their way in.
3. **Pick sources by stage:** friends and colleagues for first fumbles only; local game groups, game-store demo nights, conventions, university clubs, Discord/community posts, and paid panels or recruiters for blind tests.
4. **Over-recruit** by roughly a quarter to a third; no-shows are normal, especially for unpaid sessions.
5. **Incentives:** thank-you gifts, credits, a copy of the game, or paid slots for recruited sessions. State incentives up front and keep them modest enough not to bias answers.
6. **Consent and NDAs:** tell testers what is recorded, who sees it, and that they may stop. Get written consent before recording; for minors see the child-playtesting skill.
7. **Log each tester** (anonymised ID, experience band, source) so later analysis can separate novice from veteran results.

## Gotchas
- Friends say nice things; treat their praise as noise, their confusion as signal.
- Reusing the same testers teaches them the game; track how many prior sessions each has had and rotate in fresh players for onboarding tests.
- Self-selected volunteers skew enthusiastic. Mix sources.

## Verify
- [ ] Target player written down and screener matches it.
- [ ] At least one source outside your personal network.
- [ ] Consent and incentive terms stated before each session.
