# Asymmetric faction balance

**Goal:** factions that play differently yet give each a fair chance and a distinct fantasy.

## Procedure
1. **Define fairness:** usually equal chance to win between comparably skilled players, not equal power. State a target band for win rates (many teams aim near parity in the middle of the skill range, for example somewhere around 45 to 55 percent per faction; the exact figure is a team choice).
2. **Give each faction a different win condition shape and different resources**, then identify the main axes (tempo, economy, board presence, information) and keep the totals comparable.
3. **Build a matchup matrix** (every faction against every other, both seats or sides) and collect playtest or telemetry results. Look for pairings far from the band.
4. **Tune with faction-appropriate levers,** keeping identity: change a cost, start resource, or speed before removing a signature ability.
5. **Test across skill levels.** A faction easy to play can top low-skill win rates and fall off at high skill; a complex one does the reverse. Balance to the audience that matters for the game, and note the gap.
6. **Test seat or side advantage** separately (who moves first, attackers vs defenders).
7. **Document the balance intent** so later changes keep the design promises.

## Gotchas
- Equalising every number makes factions feel the same.
- Small samples in a 6-faction matrix: 30-plus games per cell is a rough minimum for a first read.
- Players' perception of imbalance often outruns data; share the numbers.

## Verify
- [ ] Matchup matrix filled with game counts.
- [ ] Skill-level and side/seat effects checked.
- [ ] Changes preserve each faction's identity.
