# Post-session playtest interviews

**Goal:** understand what players felt and why, in the 10-20 minutes after play.

## Procedure
1. **Interview separately** from other participants where possible, so answers are not coloured by the group.
2. **Start broad:** "Tell me about your game. What stood out?" before any specific topic.
3. **Anchor to moments** from your observation notes: "At the point where you passed the card, what were you thinking?" Memory of concrete moments beats opinions in general.
4. **Ask open, neutral questions** (what, how, tell me about). Avoid leading ones such as "Did you find the combat fun?"; ask "How did the combat feel?"
5. **Probe** with "why", "what did you expect", "what would you do instead" and silence; the best material often comes after a pause.
6. **Ask the standard closers:** the best moment, the worst moment, what they would change, would they play again and with whom.
7. **Do not defend or explain the design** during the interview. Take notes, thank them, and collect quotes verbatim.
8. **Write up the same day** while memory is fresh, separating quotes, observations and your interpretation.

## Gotchas
- Players suggest solutions; record the underlying problem, not the proposal.
- Social politeness inflates praise; ask for the worst moment explicitly.
- Do not rely on a questionnaire alone when you can ask "why" live.

## Verify
- [ ] Questions open and non-leading.
- [ ] Moments from observation used as anchors.
- [ ] Notes separate quotes from interpretation.
