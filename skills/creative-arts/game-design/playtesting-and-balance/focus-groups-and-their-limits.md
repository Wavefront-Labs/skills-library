# Focus groups and their limits

**Goal:** gather reactions to concepts, themes, art and positioning from a small moderated group.

## Procedure
1. **Pick the question.** Focus groups suit "how do people talk about this idea, what do they expect, what confuses them". They do not suit "will it be fun to play" or "which mechanic is better".
2. **Recruit 6-8 people per group** from the target audience (see playtester recruitment), and run more than one group so a single group's dynamic does not decide the result.
3. **Write a moderator guide:** warm-up, open questions, stimulus (concept art, one-page pitch, short video), probes, and a wrap-up. Keep it to about 60-90 minutes.
4. **Moderate neutrally:** invite quiet participants, redirect dominant ones, never defend the game, and use a fixed order for "write your answer first, then share" to limit conformity.
5. **Capture** audio/video with consent and a second person taking notes.
6. **Analyse for themes and vocabulary** (affinity mapping), noting what several groups said unprompted.

## Limits to state in the report
- People report what they think they would do; actual play behaviour often differs. Back concepts up with hands-on playtests.
- Groupthink and social desirability bias.
- Small, non-random samples cannot give percentages.
- Hands-on play beats talking about a game for most design questions.

## Verify
- [ ] Question suits a discussion method.
- [ ] More than one group, written answers before discussion.
- [ ] Findings labelled as opinion, with a follow-up behavioural test planned.
