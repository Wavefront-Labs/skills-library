# Playtesting with children

**Goal:** get honest signal from child players while protecting them and keeping sessions comfortable.

## Procedure
1. **Consent first:** written consent from a parent or guardian before any session, plus the child's own agreement on the day ("you can stop whenever you like"). A guardian should usually be nearby or in the room. Follow local safeguarding rules and the policies of any school or venue you use; schools and clubs often have required checks and permissions.
2. **Match sessions to age:** a few years of difference changes reading ability, attention span and abstract thinking. Test each target age band separately.
3. **Keep blocks short** (roughly 15-30 minutes of play for younger children, with breaks) and make the setting friendly; one adult facilitator and one note-taker.
4. **Prefer observation to asking.** Children may say they liked everything. Watch for where they re-read, ask for help, lose interest, or ask to play again, and use simple prompts ("show me your favourite part").
5. **Use age-appropriate tools** such as pictorial scales (smiley faces) rather than long questionnaires.
6. **Handle data carefully:** keep recordings and personal details minimal, anonymise notes, and get explicit consent for recordings. If you collect personal information online from children under 13 in the United States, the COPPA rule applies; in the EU and UK, child data has additional requirements under GDPR and the Age Appropriate Design Code. Check current rules for your market with a qualified adviser.
7. **Let children be children:** rules will be bent; note which bends tell you the rule is unclear.

## Gotchas
- A pleased parent can answer for the child; direct questions to the child.
- Do not test frustrating moments to the point of distress.
- Testers drift toward pleasing the adult; stay neutral.

## Verify
- [ ] Guardian consent and child assent recorded.
- [ ] Session length and tasks fit the age band.
- [ ] Recordings and personal data minimised and stored securely.
