# Designer contracts and royalties

**Goal:** a licence that pays fairly, returns the rights if the game stalls, and leaves
nothing ambiguous. Not legal advice: have an IP lawyer read any contract you sign.

## Key terms
| Term | Typical / what to look for |
|---|---|
| Royalty base | net receipts (what the publisher is paid), not retail price; get "net" defined |
| Royalty rate | commonly cited around 5-8% of net receipts for tabletop; check what deductions are allowed |
| Advance | an advance against royalties, paid on signing or publication; recoupable, not refundable |
| Rights granted | territories, languages, formats (physical, digital, expansions); narrow is better for you |
| Sublicensing | share of foreign-edition and digital licence income (often split 50/50 of what the publisher receives) |
| Term and reversion | rights revert if not published within a set period (often 1-2 years) or if out of print for a period |
| Expansions | first right to design expansions; royalty on expansions using your design |
| Credit | name on box front or back, rulebook credit |
| Reporting and audit | royalty statements quarterly or semi-annually; right to audit |
| Copies | free designer copies; discount on more |
| Changes | publisher may develop the game; consultation on major changes |

## Procedure
1. Before pitching, know your minimums: rate, reversion period, credit.
2. When an offer arrives, ask for the contract draft and read every definition.
3. Compare to published industry discussions (designer forums, publisher blogs such as
   Stonemaier's) for norms.
4. Negotiate the reversion clause first; it is what protects you if the publisher stalls.
5. Keep records: prototype versions, dates, who saw it.

## Red flags
- Royalties based on "profit" (easily zero) rather than net receipts.
- Perpetual rights with no reversion.
- Upfront fees charged to the designer.
- Rights to all your future designs.
- Vague "net" definition with unlimited deductions.

## Gotchas
- Crowdfunded sales count as sales; confirm they carry royalties.
- Digital adaptation rights are often valuable later; do not give them away by default.
- Co-designers need their own written agreement on splits.

## Verify
- [ ] Royalty base, rate and reporting schedule defined.
- [ ] Reversion clause with dates present.
- [ ] Lawyer review done before signing.
