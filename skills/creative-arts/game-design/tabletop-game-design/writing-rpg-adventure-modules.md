# Writing RPG adventure modules

**Goal:** a GM who skimmed the adventure once can run it from the page, finding what they
need in seconds mid-session.

## Structure
1. **Overview page:** premise, what is really going on (the secret), how it likely ends,
   level/tier, session count, content warnings.
2. **Hooks:** 3+ ways to get the party involved, for different motivations.
3. **Background** in under a page; GMs skip long histories.
4. **The adventure body**: keyed locations, events/timeline, NPCs.
5. **Appendices:** stat blocks, handouts, maps, random tables.

## Keyed location format
```
12. COLLAPSED CHAPEL
Rubble fills the nave; a bell rope hangs from the dark.
- Bell rope: pulling rings the bell; ghouls in area 14 arrive in 2 rounds.
- Altar (DC 13 Investigation): hidden drawer, silver key to area 18.
- Ghoul under rubble (ambush, see stats p.40).
Exits: north door (area 13), crawlspace under altar (area 15).
```
- **Information hierarchy:** first line is what the players see; bold or bullet the
  interactive elements; mechanics in the bullet; exits last.
- **Read-aloud text** short (2-3 sentences), describing only what is obvious, never the
  characters' actions or feelings.
- Put stats or a page reference at the point of use.

## Writing for usability
- Use the Three Clue Rule for investigations and node-based structure for non-linear plots.
- NPCs: name, role, want, one mannerism, what they know; a line of sample dialogue.
- Timelines for active villains: what happens if the party does nothing.
- Maps labelled with the same keys; include a player-safe version.
- Layout: one location per column break where possible; avoid page flips mid-room.

## Testing
- Have someone else run it from the manuscript; note every time they search for information.
- Play at least two groups; record where players went off the expected path.

## Gotchas
- Boxed text that assumes player actions ("you draw your sword") gets mocked; describe the world only.
- Hidden essential information in paragraph 4 of a room description: move it to the top.
- Stat blocks for a different edition or untested CR: verify encounter difficulty with the system's budget.

## Verify
- [ ] Overview states the secret and the likely ending.
- [ ] Every room key follows the same hierarchy.
- [ ] A GM unfamiliar with the text ran it without stopping to reread more than briefly.
