# Player motivation models (Quantic Foundry)

**Goal:** base audience decisions on an empirically derived model of why people play,
rather than designer intuition or the Bartle types.

## The model
Quantic Foundry's Gamer Motivation Model, built by factor analysis of survey responses
from over a million gamers (per Quantic Foundry), groups **12 motivations into 6 pairs**:
| Cluster | Motivations |
|---|---|
| Action | Destruction, Excitement |
| Social | Competition, Community |
| Mastery | Challenge, Strategy |
| Achievement | Completion, Power |
| Immersion | Fantasy, Story |
| Creativity | Design, Discovery |
The motivations cluster further into broader groupings (e.g. Action-Social, Mastery-
Achievement, Immersion-Creativity). Quantic Foundry's blog publishes breakdowns by age,
gender and game title (e.g. how motivation profiles vary with age).

## Using it
1. **Define the target profile:** which 3-4 motivations your game serves strongly.
2. **Check comparables:** Quantic Foundry publishes motivation profiles for popular
   games; compare yours with the games your audience plays.
3. **Feature audit:** tag each major feature with the motivation it serves. Features
   serving motivations your audience scores low on are candidates for cuts.
4. **Recruit playtesters by profile:** the free public Gamer Motivation Profile survey
   lets testers (or you) get a profile; use it in screening questionnaires.
5. **Watch tensions:** some motivations are in tension for many players (e.g. strong
   Competition vs strong Community audiences want different multiplayer).

## Gotchas
- Survey-based motivations describe stated preferences; verify with behaviour data.
- The model describes gamers who took the survey (self-selected, skewed to engaged
  players); casual audiences may differ.
- Commercial reports and detailed data are paid; the public blog has much of the core
  material.

- [ ] Feature list tagged by motivation; mismatches flagged.
