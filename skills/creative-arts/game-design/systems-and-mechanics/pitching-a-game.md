# Pitching a game

**Goal:** a pitch that makes a decision-maker believe the game will be good, can be
made by this team for this budget, and will find an audience.

Schell (2nd ed.) ch30 (The Designer Gives the Client a Pitch) gives tips including: get in the
door, show you are serious, be organised, be passionate, assume their point of view,
design the pitch, know all the details, exude confidence, be flexible, rehearse, and
get them to co-own it. Rogers' Level Up! covers pitch structure and the one-sheet.

## Deck structure (aim ~10-15 slides)
1. **Hook:** title, high concept, key art (see `creative-arts.game-design.systems-and-mechanics.high-concept-and-x-statement`).
2. **Gameplay:** core loop, what the player does, 30-60 s gameplay video or GIFs.
3. **Hooks/USPs:** 3 things that differentiate it.
4. **Audience and market:** who, comparables with performance data, why now.
5. **Status:** what exists (prototype, vertical slice), playable build link.
6. **Team:** relevant shipped titles and skills; gaps and how they will be filled.
7. **Scope, schedule, budget:** milestones, team size over time, total ask.
8. **Business model:** price point, platforms, monetisation if any.
9. **The ask:** what you want (funding, publishing services, marketing) and what they get.
10. **Risks and mitigations** (shows maturity).

## Procedure
1. **Research the audience:** publisher portfolio, deal sizes, genres they avoid;
   tailor slides 4 and 9.
2. **Lead with proof:** a playable build or gameplay video beats any slide.
3. **Rehearse** to time (often 20-30 minutes including questions); prepare answers to
   predictable questions (why this team, why this budget, what if it slips?).
4. **Leave-behind:** PDF deck + one-sheet + build link with access instructions.
5. **Follow up** within a few days with answers to anything you could not answer live.

## Gotchas
- Lore-heavy pitches lose rooms; gameplay first.
- Budgets that look too low signal inexperience as much as budgets that are too high;
  show your working.
- Pitching an idea with no prototype is much harder than pitching a game in progress.

## Verify
- [ ] Deck covers gameplay proof, team, budget, schedule and ask.
- [ ] Tailored to the specific recipient's portfolio.
- [ ] Rehearsed to time; question list prepared.
- [ ] Leave-behind package ready.
