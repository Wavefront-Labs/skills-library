# Gamification design

**Goal:** change a real behaviour (learning, exercise, form completion) by adding game
structure where it supports intrinsic motivation, not by bolting on points.

## Steps
1. **Name the target behaviour** and a measurable outcome (lessons completed per week,
   not app opens).
2. **Know the players:** survey or interview; map to a user-type model (Marczewski
   HEXAD: achiever, socialiser, free spirit, philanthropist, player, disruptor) or
   Octalysis core drives.
3. **Find the activity's own fun:** competence, autonomy, relatedness (self-determination
   theory). Design to amplify those.
4. **Choose mechanics per motivation:** progress bars and levels (competence), choice of
   path (autonomy), teams and sharing (relatedness), streaks used carefully.
5. **Feedback loop:** immediate, specific feedback on the real activity.
6. **Pilot and measure** against a control group; judge by the behaviour, not engagement metrics.

## Ethics checks
- Extrinsic rewards can crowd out intrinsic motivation (overjustification effect); do not
  reward activities people already enjoy.
- Global leaderboards demotivate the bottom; use friend or near-rank boards.
- Streaks create anxiety; allow freezes and do not punish breaks hard.
- Avoid dark patterns (fake urgency, loss-aversion abuse), especially in workplace or health contexts.

## Gotchas
- Points-badges-leaderboards-only designs spike, then decay within weeks.
- People optimise the score, not the outcome; reward the outcome.

## Verify
- [ ] Target behaviour and outcome metric defined.
- [ ] Each mechanic mapped to a motivation.
- [ ] Pilot with a control group, measured on the outcome.
- [ ] Ethics checklist reviewed.
