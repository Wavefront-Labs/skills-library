# Climax and ending design

**Goal:** the highest point of the interest curve comes near the end, uses everything the
player has learned, and resolves the player's goals.

Schell's interest curve: hook, rising peaks and valleys, a climax, then resolution. People
judge an experience largely by its peak and its end (Kahneman's peak-end rule), so a weak
or mistimed climax colours the memory of the whole game.

## Steps
1. **Plot the interest curve** for the whole game (one point per level or chapter); confirm
   the climax is the global maximum and sits in roughly the last 10% of play time.
2. **Final test = synthesis:** the climax should require combining mechanics taught
   earlier, not a new mechanic. New twists only as small variants.
3. **Raise stakes on every axis:** narrative (what can be lost), mechanical (difficulty,
   tempo), audiovisual (music, set piece).
4. **Quiet valley before the climax:** a safe room, last shop or farewell scene gives
   contrast and lets players prepare.
5. **Resolution:** show the consequences of the player's choices; keep cutscenes short and skippable.
6. **Post-game:** decide whether the world persists (cleanup, new game+), where credits sit,
   and what the save does after the end.

## Gotchas
- Difficulty spikes at the final boss cause abandonment just before the credits (check
  completion funnels); add mid-fight checkpoints and assist options.
- Endings that ignore earlier choices break the branching contract.
- A point of no return must be clearly warned, with a save prompt.
- Long unskippable credits block the post-game; allow skipping on repeat.

## Verify
- [ ] Interest curve shows the climax as the peak near the end.
- [ ] Climax uses at least three previously taught mechanics.
- [ ] Point-of-no-return warning present.
- [ ] Completion rate of the final encounter tracked in playtests.
