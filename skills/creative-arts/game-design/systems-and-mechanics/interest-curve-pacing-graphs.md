# Interest curve pacing graphs

**Goal:** a planned shape for how interesting the experience is over time, checked
against what players actually report.

Schell (2nd ed.) ch16 (Experiences Can Be Judged by Their Interest Curves) describes a good
interest curve: start with an interesting **hook**, drop to a lower level while the
experience is set up, then a series of **rising peaks and valleys** leading to a
**climax**, followed by a short **resolution**. He notes interest curves are **fractal**:
the whole game, each level, and each encounter can have the same shape.

## Procedure
1. **Draw the planned curve** for the whole game: x = time, y = interest/tension.
   Mark hook (A), setup dip, peaks, climax, resolution.
2. **Draw curves at smaller scales** for each level and key encounter.
3. **Identify the content that creates each peak** (new mechanic, boss, reveal, set
   piece) and each valley (rest, exploration, story).
4. **Collect actual data:** after a playtest, ask testers to sketch their own interest
   curve (Schell suggests this), or ask them to rate interest per section 1-10; overlay
   with telemetry (quit points).
5. **Fix flat stretches** (no peaks for too long) and **early peaks that out-shine the
   climax**.

## Interest factors (Schell)
Schell lists three components of interest: **inherent interest** (some events are
naturally more interesting - risk, drama), **poetry of presentation** (aesthetics), and
**projection** (empathy and imagination that put the player into the experience).
Raise interest at a point by working on any of the three.

## Gotchas
- A hook that is not representative (a spectacular intro then hours of tutorial)
  creates a big dip that loses players.
- Constant high intensity is fatigue, not excitement; valleys are part of the design.
- Players' curves differ; look for consensus patterns across 5+ testers.

## Verify
- [ ] Planned curves drawn at game, level and encounter scale.
- [ ] Each peak and valley linked to concrete content.
- [ ] Tester-drawn or rated curves overlaid on the plan; mismatches have actions.
