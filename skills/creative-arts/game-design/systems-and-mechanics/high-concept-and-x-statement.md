# High concept and X-statement

**Goal:** a single sentence that makes the game understandable and wanted, used at the
top of every pitch, one-sheet, store page and design doc.

## Formats that work
- **X-statement** (EA tradition, cited in many design texts): *"The X of this game is
  ..."* - the one experience it delivers better than anything else.
  "The X is: you are the heist crew's getaway driver, and every escape is improvised."
- **Logline:** *[Genre] where [player role] must [goal] by [distinctive mechanic], in
  [setting].*
- **Comparison ("X meets Y"):** "Into the Breach meets Overcooked." Fast, but only for
  audiences who know both games, and it shows derivation; use it as a support line, not
  the headline.

## Procedure
1. Write 10 variants quickly in different formats.
2. For each, underline the **hook** (the one thing not in other games). If there is no
   hook, the concept needs work, not the wording.
3. Remove adjectives that any game could claim ("epic", "immersive", "unique").
4. Keep it under ~25 words; it must survive being said aloud.
5. **Test on 5 strangers to the project:** say it once, then ask them to tell you what
   they would do in the first minute of play. If they cannot, it is not clear.
6. Pair it with 3 supporting bullets (the pillars or key features) for the one-sheet.

## Gotchas
- **Story-first loglines** for a game whose hook is mechanical hide the hook. Lead with
  what the player *does*.
- A high concept that needs the comparison games to make sense collapses for buyers or
  publishers who have not played them.
- The X-statement is a design tool too: if a proposed feature does not serve the X, it
  is a candidate for cutting (see
  `creative-arts.game-design.systems-and-mechanics.writing-design-pillars`).

## Verify
- [ ] One sentence, under ~25 words, naming role, action and hook.
- [ ] No unfalsifiable adjectives.
- [ ] 4 of 5 strangers correctly describe the first minute of play.
- [ ] The same line heads the pitch deck, one-sheet and GDD.
