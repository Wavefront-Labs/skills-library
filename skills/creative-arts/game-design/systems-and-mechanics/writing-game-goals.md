# Writing game goals

**Goal:** players always know what they are trying to do now, next and eventually, and
believe they can do it.

Schell (2nd ed.) ch12 (Rules: the goal is the most important rule) lists three qualities of good
goals: **concrete** (players can state them clearly), **achievable** (players believe
they can succeed), **rewarding** (achieving them feels worthwhile and the reward has
value). He also stresses balancing short-term and long-term goals.

## Procedure
1. **Write goals at three horizons:**
   | Horizon | Example | Player should be able to answer |
   |---|---|---|
   | Immediate (seconds-minutes) | reach the ledge, kill these 3 | "what am I doing right now?" |
   | Medium (session) | clear the temple, finish the chapter | "what am I working toward tonight?" |
   | Long (game) | defeat the tyrant, win the season | "why does it matter?" |
2. **Check concreteness:** can a playtester say the goal back in one sentence? If they
   say "I'm not sure, exploring?", the goal is not concrete (unless exploration is the
   designed goal, in which case make *that* concrete: "find the 3 shrines").
3. **Check achievability:** show progress (bars, counters, map), and show the path's
   first step.
4. **Check reward:** what changes when the goal is met? New abilities, story, area,
   status. A goal whose completion changes nothing is a chore.
5. **Layer them so one always overlaps the next**: before an immediate goal ends, a new
   one is visible. Gaps cause the "what now?" stall.
6. **Allow self-set goals** for expressive games (sandbox), but give a scaffold of
   suggested goals for players who need them.

## Gotchas
- Too many simultaneous goals (quest-log bloat) dilute focus; highlight one primary.
- Goals stated only in dialogue get missed; repeat in UI or world.
- In tabletop, the win condition must be checkable at the table without ambiguity
  (tie-breakers included).

## Verify
- [ ] Goals written at all three horizons.
- [ ] 4 of 5 playtesters state the current immediate and medium goal when asked mid-play.
- [ ] No observed "what now?" stall longer than ~30 seconds in the critical path.
