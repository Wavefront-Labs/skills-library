# Handouts and battle maps

**Goal:** props that make scenes tangible and maps that make combat clear.

## Handouts
1. **Use handouts for information players can hold:** letters, maps, clue cards, wanted posters, riddles, and item cards.
2. **Keep them short and readable:** a clear font, a size that is legible, and only the information the characters have.
3. **Separate player-facing text from GM notes;** never put the GM answer on the same page.
4. **Give them out at the moment of discovery** so the reveal is dramatic; keep a stack ready in an envelope per scene.
5. **Allow reading time,** and read aloud for players who prefer it.
6. **Provide an alternative format** (large print, read aloud) when needed for accessibility.
7. **For online play,** share images or documents to specific players.

## Battle maps
1. **Match scale to your miniatures:** a common standard is one inch per square for tabletop miniatures (and 5 feet per square in many D&D editions); check your system.
2. **Choose the medium:** wet-erase or dry-erase grid mats, printed tiles, hand-drawn maps, digital screens or projected maps.
3. **Draw only what matters:** walls, doors, cover, hazards, elevation; label areas that your rules reference.
4. **Reveal gradually:** cover with paper or draw as the party explores.
5. **Reuse maps** by writing variants (flooded, on fire, at night).
6. **Pre-draw the first encounter's map** and sketch the rest during play.
7. **Mark initiative and conditions** with small tokens.

## Gotchas
- Pretty maps take prep time; clarity beats beauty.
- Players need a clear key to symbols.
- Do not over-rely on handouts; use them where they add atmosphere.

## Verify
- [ ] Handouts contain only what the characters know.
- [ ] Map scale matches minis.
- [ ] Reveal order decided.
