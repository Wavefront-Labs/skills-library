# GM improvisation

**Goal:** stay calm and creative when players leave the script.

## Habits
1. **Prep situations, not outcomes.** Know the people, wants, secrets and pressures; let play decide the story.
2. **Say yes, or yes with a cost,** or ask for a roll when failure is interesting. Avoid a flat no unless the fiction makes it impossible.
3. **Fail forward:** a failed roll changes the situation (a cost, a complication, a worse position) instead of stalling play.
4. **Use "yes, and" and "yes, but"** as improv tools to build on player ideas; adopt a good player idea as truth.
5. **Ask questions back:** "What does your character notice?", "Who owes you a favour here?" Players supply detail and you gain thinking time.
6. **Keep ready lists:** twenty names, a few complications, a table of street scenes, a rumour list. See random-tables-for-improvisation.
7. **Take a breath:** pause, ask a short break, or say "let me think about that" rather than rushing a bad ruling.
8. **Make quick rulings, note them, and fix later.** Rule of thumb: keep the game moving, look it up after.
9. **Log what you improvised** (names, facts promised) in session notes for continuity.

## Gotchas
- Improvising everything leads to a flat world; mix prep and improv.
- Do not retcon players' discoveries to match your plan.
- Avoid adjudicating by "what would be dramatic" without explaining stakes.

## Verify
- [ ] Situation prep exists, not a fixed story.
- [ ] Failure produces change, not a dead stop.
- [ ] Improvised facts recorded for later sessions.
