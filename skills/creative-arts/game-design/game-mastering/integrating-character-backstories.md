# Integrating character backstories

**Goal:** make each character feel rooted in the world, and give players hooks they want to play.

## Procedure
1. **Ask for short backstories** (half a page at most) or three bullet hooks: a person who matters, an enemy or debt, an unfinished goal. Systems often have built-in prompts (bonds, relationships or connections); use them.
2. **Hold a collaborative session zero:** tie characters together and to the setting; ask players to name each other's contacts or rivals.
3. **Map hooks to the campaign:** link backstory people and organisations to factions, locations, and the main threats.
4. **Plant hooks gradually:** introduce a character's contact or enemy early in a scene, then return with consequences later.
5. **Offer, do not force:** a hook is an invitation. Keep alternatives if a player ignores it.
6. **Use hooks for spotlight:** schedule a scene for each character over a few sessions (see spotlight management).
7. **Let players change their story:** update backstories as play changes the character; record revisions.
8. **Respect boundaries:** do not use sensitive backstory material without checking with the player (see the consent checklist skill).

## Gotchas
- Long backstories rarely get used; short ones with hooks do.
- Do not punish players by killing their backstory NPCs without discussion.
- Connect hooks to the shared story, not only to the individual.

## Verify
- [ ] Each character has at least two usable hooks.
- [ ] Hooks tied to factions or threads.
- [ ] Consent and boundaries checked for sensitive material.
