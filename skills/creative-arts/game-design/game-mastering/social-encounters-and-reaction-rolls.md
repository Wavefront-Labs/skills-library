# Social encounters and reaction rolls

**Goal:** a believable NPC response that rewards smart play and keeps conversation from collapsing into one die roll.

## Procedure
1. **Know the NPC's goals, fears, and leverage** before the scene. A guard wants to keep his job; a merchant wants profit.
2. **Set the starting attitude** by fiction first (they have a grudge or an alliance). If it is uncertain, make a reaction roll. The classic old-school form is a 2d6 roll modified by charisma or circumstances, read on a band from hostile to friendly; check your system's table (the numbers vary by game) or use a simple custom one:
   - low band: hostile or refuses
   - lower middle: unfriendly, wants something
   - middle: uncertain, needs convincing
   - upper middle: friendly, helpful
   - top: enthusiastic ally
3. **Let players talk.** Their offer, argument, and leverage move the NPC along the attitude scale.
4. **Use dice for uncertainty,** not to replace roleplay: roll a social check only when the outcome is open and the stakes matter; say what a failure costs.
5. **Show the attitude** through voice, posture and what the NPC reveals.
6. **Offer consequences, not blocks:** a refused favour can become a price.
7. **Track relationships:** note changes in attitude for later scenes.

## Gotchas
- One roll to "persuade the king" bypasses play; ask what they say and why it works.
- Do not make NPCs follow players' attitudes without cost; reactions are not a mind-control button.
- Respect player agency: a character's feelings are chosen by the player; the roll is for NPCs.

## Verify
- [ ] NPC goals and leverage prepared.
- [ ] Starting attitude set by fiction or reaction roll.
- [ ] Consequences recorded for later scenes.
