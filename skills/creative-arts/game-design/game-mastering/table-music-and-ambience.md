# Table music and ambience

**Goal:** atmosphere that supports play without competing with conversation.

## Procedure
1. **Choose the mood palette:** a handful of playlists such as tavern, travel, tension, combat, mystery, calm. Fewer, longer playlists beat constant switching.
2. **Prefer instrumental music** without lyrics, which compete with speech; film scores, game soundtracks and ambient tracks work well.
3. **Set volume low,** so players can talk without raising their voices. Test from the farthest seat.
4. **Use ambient loops** (rain, crowd, forest, ship) as a bed under music or alone.
5. **Cue deliberately:** switch tracks at scene changes, not mid-sentence; use stingers for dramatic moments sparingly.
6. **Organise in advance:** build playlists per scene or per location; label them. Many GMs use a music player or a dedicated app; pick what your table can control quickly.
7. **Use light and props:** low lights, candles (electric ones are safer), or coloured lamps can add mood; keep reading light adequate for sheets and handouts.
8. **Ask your players:** some find music distracting; adjust or switch it off.
9. **Mind licensing for streams:** background music in streamed or recorded sessions may trigger copyright claims; use licensed or royalty-free sources for broadcast.

## Gotchas
- Tracks with sudden loud sections break the mood.
- Searching for the right track mid-scene stalls play; prepare it.
- Silence is a tool for tense moments.

## Verify
- [ ] Playlists prepared by mood.
- [ ] Volume tested from the farthest seat.
- [ ] Players consulted about preferences.
