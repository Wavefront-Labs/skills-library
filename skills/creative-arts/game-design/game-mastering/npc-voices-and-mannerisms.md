# NPC voices and mannerisms

**Goal:** make NPCs memorable and easy to switch between, without needing acting talent.

## Procedure
1. **Start with a want and an attitude** (what does this person need from the party, how do they treat strangers). Voice follows motive.
2. **Pick two or three dials:**
   - **Pitch** (high or low) and **pace** (fast or slow).
   - **Volume** (whisper, boom).
   - **One verbal tic** or catchphrase (clearing throat, "you see", repeating the last word).
3. **Add one physical gesture** (tapping the table, leaning in, folding arms). Players see it even with no stage skills.
4. **Anchor to a real or fictional reference** you can remember (a teacher, a news presenter) rather than inventing from nothing.
5. **Keep a one-line cheat sheet:** name, want, tic, voice. Update it when the NPC returns.
6. **Use third person when stuck:** "She leans in and says she will not betray her brother." Players still get the character.
7. **Switch cleanly:** move your body or tone when switching NPCs so the table knows who is speaking.
8. **Rest your voice** and avoid exaggerated voices for long scenes; clarity beats caricature.

## Accents
- Avoid stereotyped accents of real cultures; they can turn comic or offensive. Prefer pace, vocabulary, and rhythm over accents.
- If you use accents, keep them consistent and understandable and stop if players are uncomfortable.

## Verify
- [ ] Each major NPC has want, pitch/pace and one tic.
- [ ] Cheat sheet available at the table.
- [ ] Accents chosen with care or avoided.
