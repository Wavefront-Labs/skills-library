# Handling problem players

**Goal:** resolve disruptive behaviour so the whole table has a good time, with the
least drama and respect for everyone involved.

## Diagnose first
Common patterns and likely causes:
| Behaviour | Common cause |
|---|---|
| Spotlight hogging | enthusiasm; unclear turn-taking |
| Derailing / joking constantly | boredom, nerves, different expectations |
| Rules lawyering | wants fairness/predictability; distrust of rulings |
| Playing "my guy" (disruptive in-character) | mismatched expectations about genre/tone |
| Phone/absent | disengagement; scene not for them |
| Making others uncomfortable | content boundaries, social issues |
Different expectations about the game are the root of many problems; session zero is
where they get aligned.

## Escalation ladder
1. **In-game levers:** structured turn-taking, frame scenes around others, give the
   bored player a hook, set clear ruling procedures ("I'll rule now, we check after").
2. **Table-wide reminder:** restate expectations for everyone, not singling out.
3. **Private conversation** away from the session: describe the behaviour and its effect
   ("When X happens, others don't get to act"), ask what they want from the game, agree
   on something concrete.
4. **Revisit the social contract** (session zero redo) if expectations differ widely.
5. **Part ways** respectfully if the mismatch can't be fixed; not every player fits every
   table.

## Content and safety issues
Behaviour that makes others feel unsafe (harassment, ignoring stated lines) isn't a
"style" problem: stop the scene, use safety tools (TTRPG Safety Toolkit), and address it
directly and promptly.

## Gotchas
- Calling someone out in front of the group usually escalates.
- Ignoring the problem costs you the other players, who leave quietly.
- Assume good faith first; many "problem players" are misaligned, not malicious.

## Verify
- [ ] Behaviour and probable cause written down before acting.
- [ ] In-game levers tried before personal confrontation (except safety issues).
- [ ] Private conversation produced a concrete agreement.
