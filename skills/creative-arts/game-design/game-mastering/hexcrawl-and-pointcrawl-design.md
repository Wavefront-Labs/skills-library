# Hexcrawl and pointcrawl design

**Goal:** a wilderness that players explore on their own terms, with procedures that
make travel interesting and content that rewards discovery.

Justin Alexander's "Hexcrawl" series (The Alexandrian) sets out the structure: a keyed
hex map, navigation and travel procedures, encounter checks, and tracking of what the
players know.

## Hexcrawl components
| Component | Notes |
|---|---|
| Map scale | a common choice is 6-mile hexes (roughly what you can see from a hex) |
| Keyed content | every hex (or most) has something: lair, ruin, landmark, settlement, feature |
| Travel speed | hexes per day by terrain and pace |
| Navigation | chance of getting lost in difficult terrain; drift direction |
| Encounters | check frequency (e.g. per watch), terrain-based tables |
| Player map | players map what they've seen; GM tracks revealed hexes |
| Watches | the day divided into watches for travel, rest, foraging |

## Pointcrawl alternative
A **pointcrawl** maps locations as nodes connected by paths (roads, rivers, trails)
rather than a hex grid. Travel happens along edges; it's quicker to run, fits road-bound
or underground travel, and focuses choices on routes rather than grid squares.

## Procedure
1. Pick scale and region size (e.g. 10x10 hexes for a starting region).
2. Key content: mix of obvious landmarks (seen from afar), hidden locations (found on
   entering/searching), and lairs.
3. Write travel and navigation procedures on a one-page GM sheet.
4. Build encounter tables per terrain; include non-combat encounters (travellers, signs,
   weather).
5. Seed rumours in the starting town pointing to 3+ locations so players have goals.
6. After each session, update the revealed map and the campaign log.

## Gotchas
- Procedure overload (tracking rations, weather, navigation, encounters every watch)
  slows play; automate or simplify to what makes choices.
- Empty hexes everywhere make travel dull; empty-but-flavoured is fine.
- Players need reasons to explore (rumours, goals); a sandbox without hooks stalls.

## Verify
- [ ] Map scale and keyed hexes/nodes prepared for the starting region.
- [ ] One-page travel and encounter procedure.
- [ ] At least three rumours leading to locations.
- [ ] Revealed-map tracking in place.
