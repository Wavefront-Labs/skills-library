# Three Clue Rule

**Goal:** mystery and investigation scenarios where players reliably reach the
conclusions they need, while still feeling they solved it themselves.

Justin Alexander's "Three Clue Rule" (The Alexandrian): "For any conclusion you want the
PCs to make, include at least three clues." Players will miss the first, ignore the
second and misinterpret the third - redundancy keeps the scenario moving.

## Procedure
1. **List the conclusions (revelations)** players must reach to progress: "The butler was
   in the garden", "The cult meets at the old mill".
2. **For each conclusion, write at least three clues** in different places/forms:
   | Revelation | Clue 1 | Clue 2 | Clue 3 |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | cult meets at the mill | flour on the robes | a miller's receipt in the victim's pocket | a child saw lanterns at the mill |
3. **Vary the access:** clues reachable by different methods (searching, talking,
   magic, research) so no single failed roll blocks progress.
4. **Never gate an essential clue behind a single roll.** If a clue is essential, they
   find it; rolls determine extra information or cost.
5. **Proactive clues:** if players stall, bring a clue to them (an NPC approaches, an
   event happens). Alexander calls these "bash them on the head" fallbacks.
6. **Track found clues** during play; at each scene end, check whether each revelation
   is still reachable.

## Permissive clue-finding
Let players reach conclusions by unexpected routes; if their reasoning is sound, it
works even if you didn't plan that clue.

## Gotchas
- Red herrings consume clue bandwidth; use sparingly in TTRPGs.
- Three clues for the same conclusion in the same room is one clue; spread them.
- Players can draw the wrong conclusion from correct clues; let consequences guide them
  back rather than overriding.

## Verify
- [ ] Every required revelation listed with 3+ clues.
- [ ] Clues spread across locations and methods.
- [ ] No essential clue behind a single roll.
- [ ] Proactive fallback prepared for each revelation.
