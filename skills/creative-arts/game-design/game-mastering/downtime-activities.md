# Downtime activities

**Goal:** give players meaningful things to do between adventures that advance personal and campaign goals.

## Procedure
1. **Set the downtime window** (days, weeks or a season) and the cost of living if any.
2. **Offer a menu** of activities: research a rumour, craft or repair, work a trade, train a skill, carouse for contacts, run an errand for a faction, recover from injury, manage property.
3. **Define each activity** with time cost, resource cost (gold, favours), a check or roll (or a clock), and outcomes by degree (full success, partial success with a cost, failure with a setback).
4. **Add complications:** a rival interferes, a patron asks a favour, a rumour proves false. Complications give downtime hooks into future adventures.
5. **Use clocks for long projects:** a segmented clock advances with each activity; a milestone event triggers when it fills.
6. **Resolve fast:** each player states one or two activities, you resolve them in a few minutes, and tell a short scene for each.
7. **Tie to the world:** faction attitudes, prices and rumours change in response, so downtime feels real.
8. **Prevent tedium:** do not treat downtime as a spreadsheet; allow narration and skip when everyone is eager to adventure.

## Gotchas
- Open-ended downtime can balloon prep; use a standard menu.
- Rewards from downtime must not outpace adventures.
- Some systems include official downtime rules; use those when you run that game and adapt them as needed.

## Verify
- [ ] Menu with costs and outcomes written.
- [ ] Each activity can generate a hook.
- [ ] Time and resource accounting kept.
