# Factions and fronts

**Goal:** a world that acts on its own, so the players' choices land in a changing
situation and threats escalate if ignored.

Apocalypse World (and Dungeon World after it) uses **fronts**: groups of related threats
with a **countdown** of escalating events (grim portents) toward an impending doom.
Blades in the Dark uses faction status, tiers and progress **clocks** to track what
factions do.

## Faction sheet
```
NAME / one-line identity
GOAL          what they want right now (concrete)
MEANS         resources: money, soldiers, magic, information, territory
METHODS       how they act (bribery, violence, law, secrecy)
LEADER + 2 NPCs with names and one trait each
RELATIONSHIPS ally / rival / enemy with other factions
ATTITUDE TO PCs  -3 .. +3
CLOCK         progress toward the goal (4/6/8 segments)
```

## Front (threat) structure
1. **Threats** (people, monsters, forces) each with an impulse ("to consume").
2. **Grim portents:** 3-6 escalating events, from early warning to disaster.
3. **Impending doom:** what happens if nothing stops it.
4. **Stakes questions:** things you want play to answer ("Will the baron betray the
   village?").

## Between sessions
1. Advance clocks for factions based on what happened (and what the PCs ignored).
2. Roll or decide one notable faction action; show its consequence in the next session
   (rumour, changed prices, new guards, a body).
3. Update attitudes from PC actions.

## Gotchas
- Too many factions blur together; 3-5 active is plenty.
- Off-screen events must be visible to players or they don't matter.
- Don't pre-script outcomes: portents are what happens *if no one intervenes*.

## Verify
- [ ] 3-5 faction sheets with concrete goals and clocks.
- [ ] At least one front with escalating portents.
- [ ] Faction actions advanced and shown after each session.
