# Treasure and milestone levelling

**Goal:** a reward rhythm that keeps characters growing at a pace the table enjoys.

## Levelling methods
- **Experience points (XP) for defeating monsters or completing goals:** the standard in many games; tracking required.
- **Gold-for-XP** (as in older D&D editions): treasure recovered also gives XP, rewarding exploration and clever play over fighting.
- **Milestone levelling** (an option in 5th-edition D&D's guidance): characters level when the party hits story milestones; no tracking, but the GM controls pace.
- **Session-based or time-based levelling** (level every few sessions): simple pacing for fixed-length campaigns.

## Procedure
1. **Pick the method that suits the group** and announce it at session zero.
2. **Set a target pace** (for example, a level every two to four sessions) and adjust if players want faster or slower growth.
3. **Size treasure with the system's tables or guidance,** which vary by game; check treasure-by-challenge rules for your system rather than inventing amounts.
4. **Mix reward types:** coin, magic or special items, information, allies, property, favours. Not everything must be gold.
5. **Distribute fairly:** let players discuss splits; offer items that fit different characters; avoid single-character gear flooding.
6. **Control power spikes:** be careful with items that break balance; remove or curse if they do.
7. **Review pace** at the end of arcs.

## Gotchas
- Milestones can feel arbitrary if not announced; share them.
- Late-game gold can lose meaning; give players sinks (property, followers, research).
- Do not stall play with long loot arguments; set a time limit.

- [ ] Treasure sized from system guidance.
