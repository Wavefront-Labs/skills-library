# Theatre of the mind vs grid

**Goal:** pick the combat presentation that suits the system, group and encounter.

## Compare
- **Theatre of the mind:** describe the scene aloud; no minis. Fast, flexible, cinematic; risks disagreement about positions and ranges and weaker tactics.
- **Grid:** a battle map with miniatures or tokens. Precise positioning, area effects, cover and opportunity attacks work cleanly; slower to set up and can feel more like a board game.

## Choose
1. **Rules fit:** systems built on ranges, templates and attacks of opportunity (many d20 games) reward a grid; fast narrative systems work well without.
2. **Group preference:** ask at session zero.
3. **Encounter type:** simple brawls or chases can be abstract; complex terrain, flanking, and area spells benefit from a map.

## Run theatre of the mind well
1. Describe the arena with 3-4 key features (high ground, door, fire, crowd).
2. Use **range bands or zones** such as engaged, near, far (the idea behind zone maps in Fate, and range bands in many other games) instead of feet.
3. Say who is where after every round and keep a quick sketch or notes of positions.
4. Settle disputes in favour of the described fiction; players can ask "can I reach X?" and you answer yes, no, or yes with a cost.
5. Reward creative use of terrain.

## Hybrid
Use a rough sketch map or zones on paper: boxes for areas, dots for people. This keeps most of the speed with fewer arguments.

## Gotchas
- Switching to a grid mid-fight is fine when positions matter; announce it.
- Do not remove tactical options players built characters around.

## Verify
- [ ] Mode chosen with the rules and group in mind.
- [ ] Positions recorded after each round.
- [ ] Ranges use bands or zones if no grid.
