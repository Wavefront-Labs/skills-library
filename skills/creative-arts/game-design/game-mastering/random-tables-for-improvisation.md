# Random tables for improvisation

**Goal:** usable prompts at the table in seconds that stay inside your setting's tone.

## Procedure
1. **Choose the table's job:** names, rumours, wandering encounters, weather, loot, complications, NPC quirks. One job per table.
2. **Pick the die by need:**
   - d6 or d8 for short lists (fast to read).
   - d66 (roll two d6, one for tens and one for ones) for 36 entries without a d100.
   - d100 (or two d10) for large lists.
   - 2d6 for bell-curve results where the middle entries should be common and the ends rare.
3. **Write entries as situations, not just nouns.** "A merchant begs the party to escort a locked chest" beats "merchant". Each should suggest an action.
4. **Weight by rarity:** repeat common results or give them more slots; reserve extremes for ends of the curve.
5. **Make results usable twice:** pair two tables (who, wants what) or pair a result with a complication roll to generate more content.
6. **Customise to your setting** with local names, factions, and regional colour; generic tables produce generic play.
7. **Interpret the roll:** treat the result as a prompt; if it does not fit the situation, reroll or reinterpret.
8. **Keep tables at hand,** on one page per theme, and revise entries that never get used.

## Gotchas
- Entries that need a rules lookup break the flow; keep them self-contained.
- Rolling in front of players for secrets undermines mystery; roll behind a screen when needed.
- Never let a roll override a clue you already decided the players must find.

## Verify
- [ ] Table has one job and a die that matches its length.
- [ ] Each entry suggests an action or hook.
- [ ] Table tested in play and pruned.
