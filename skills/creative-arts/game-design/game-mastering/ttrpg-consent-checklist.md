# TTRPG consent checklist

**Goal:** before a campaign starts, everyone knows which content is welcome, which needs
care, and which is off the table, without anyone having to explain why.

The TTRPG Safety Toolkit (Kienna Shaw and Lauren Bryant) collects safety tools including
the RPG Consent Checklist (by Jason Cordova / Kienna Shaw). Monte Cook Games' free
*Consent in Gaming* booklet includes a similar checklist.

## The checklist
A list of content categories (e.g. horror elements, violence types, relationships,
specific phobias, social issues), each rated by each player:
| Rating | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Green | enthusiastically OK |
| Yellow | OK with care / off-screen / discuss first |
| Red | not at the table |
Players can add their own categories.

## Procedure
1. **Distribute before session zero** (paper or a form); allow **anonymous** answers so
   no one has to justify a red.
2. **Aggregate:** any red = a line (content doesn't appear); any yellow = a veil (happens
   off-screen / handled carefully) unless discussed otherwise.
3. **Share the combined result** with the table (not individual answers).
4. **Record in the campaign notes** and in prep checklists.
5. **Pair with in-play tools** (X-card, script change, open door) because people discover
   limits during play.
6. **Revisit** at intervals or when the campaign's direction changes; anyone can update
   their answers.

## Gotchas
- A checklist doesn't replace conversation; discuss tone and genre expectations too.
- Don't make red items into "challenges" to overcome; they're out.
- New players joining mid-campaign need to fill it in too.
- Respect privacy: never reveal who marked what.

## Verify
- [ ] Checklist collected (anonymously option offered) before play.
- [ ] Combined lines and veils shared with the table.
- [ ] In-play safety tools agreed.
- [ ] Review date set.
