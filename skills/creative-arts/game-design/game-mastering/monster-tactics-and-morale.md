# Monster tactics and morale

**Goal:** enemies that behave like living creatures with goals and self-preservation,
making fights varied and believable, and giving fights natural endings other than
"everyone dies".

Keith Ammann's *The Monsters Know What They're Doing* (blog and books) derives tactics
from each creature's stat block: abilities, ability scores and senses suggest how it
fights. Many older-edition rules (and OSR games) use morale checks.

## Derive tactics from the stat block
| Stat block clue | Tactic |
|---|---|
| High DEX, low CON | hit-and-run, avoid prolonged melee |
| Darkvision, light sensitivity | ambush in darkness, flee light |
| Pack tactics / advantage when allied adjacent | gang up on one target |
| Ranged attacks, low HP | stay at range, use cover |
| Low INT | straightforward; doesn't adapt |
| High INT/WIS | targets casters, uses terrain, retreats wisely |

## Morale
- Most creatures don't fight to the death. Triggers to check morale or decide retreat:
  leader killed, half the group down, creature reduced below ~half HP, surprising
  display of power.
- Options: flee, surrender, bargain, call for help, feign death.
- A simple house rule: at a trigger, roll (e.g. 2d6 vs a morale score as in older
  editions) or decide by personality.
- **Retreat creates story:** survivors warn others, return later, or become informants.

## Procedure
1. Before the session, write one line of tactics per enemy type and its morale trigger.
2. During combat, play to the creature's goals (eat, guard, steal, survive), not
   optimal damage.
3. Telegraph intentions ("the goblins glance at the exit") so players can use morale
   (intimidation, offering terms).

## Gotchas
- Ruthlessly optimal monsters can be deadlier than CR suggests; scale accordingly.
- Monsters that always flee teach players to chase; vary behaviour.
- Mindless creatures (undead, constructs) don't check morale - an intentional contrast.

## Verify
- [ ] One-line tactics and morale trigger per enemy type.
- [ ] At least one fight per session can end by retreat/surrender.
- [ ] Enemy intentions are telegraphed in description.
