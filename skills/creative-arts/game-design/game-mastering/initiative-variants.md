# Initiative variants

**Goal:** a turn order that fits the game's tone and keeps combat moving.

## Common methods
- **Individual roll each combat** (for example d20 plus a modifier in 5th-edition D&D): fixed order for the fight; quick to track; slower with many combatants.
- **Side-based** (each side rolls once; old-school D&D editions used this): fast for large fights, easy tracking; players on a side act in any order.
- **Popcorn:** the acting player picks who goes next; fosters teamwork and player agency; needs rules for the last actor and for NPCs.
- **Card-based** (Savage Worlds draws action cards each round): order reshuffles every round, adding surprise and fast "who acts next" resolution.
- **Count-up or speed-based systems:** actions have costs and the next to act is whoever reaches their next count; rich tactics, more bookkeeping.
- **Fixed order** by a stat or a seat: fastest, least tension.

## Procedure
1. Decide the effect you want: tension, speed, tactics, or agency.
2. Pick the method and run a test fight; time the first three rounds.
3. Use a visible tracker: a whiteboard, index cards in order, or a digital tool.
4. Handle surprise: grant a free round or penalise unprepared sides as the system describes.
5. Handle delays and held actions consistently.
6. Keep monster groups on a single initiative slot when numbers are large.

## Gotchas
- Round-by-round reshuffling invalidates "ready" tactics unless rules support them.
- Popcorn can neglect shy players; track who has gone.
- House-ruling initiative changes ability value; check specials that rely on it.

## Verify
- [ ] Method matches the intended pacing.
- [ ] Order is visible to everyone.
- [ ] Surprise and delay rules decided in advance.
