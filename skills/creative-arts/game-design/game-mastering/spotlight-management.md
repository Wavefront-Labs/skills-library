# Spotlight management

**Goal:** every player gets meaningful moments over a session or arc.

## Procedure
1. **Track spotlight loosely:** note after each scene who drove it. A line of initials on a notepad is enough.
2. **Frame scenes around hooks:** each character's goals, bonds or backstory (see backstory hooks) give you a ready-made scene for whoever is under-served.
3. **Ask directly:** "What does X do?" works better than waiting; direct the question, then wait through the silence.
4. **Break up the party's spotlight:** parallel scenes give different characters the lead, with time limits so others are not idle.
5. **Design for multiple solutions:** challenges that need different skills let different characters shine.
6. **Handle dominant players** with structure rather than confrontation: go around the table, use turn order outside combat, give short time limits, and talk privately if the pattern persists.
7. **Handle shy players kindly:** check that they are comfortable; some prefer less spotlight but want meaningful choices when it comes.
8. **Ask for preferences** in session zero or at breaks: what kind of scene each player wants more of.

## Gotchas
- Equal time is not always the goal; equal impact is.
- Overly rigid round-robin kills flow; use it as a fallback.
- Do not punish a player for a good plan with endless time for only them.

## Verify
- [ ] Spotlight tracked across the session.
- [ ] Each character has at least one hook scene prepared.
- [ ] Quiet and dominant players have specific handling in place.
