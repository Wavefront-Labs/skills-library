# Recognising aerial injury and fatigue signs

**Goal:** stop the session before fatigue causes a fall, and recognise the injuries that
need rest or a referral rather than "pushing through".

## Fatigue signs (stop or drop the difficulty)
- Shoulders creeping up in hangs; loss of active shoulder.
- Shaky climbs, extra rests, slower descents.
- Sloppier wraps, missed counts, needing reminders of known sequences.
- Breath held or ragged, pale or flushed face, irritability.
Rule: when fatigue appears, switch to conditioning on the floor or end the session.

## Common aerial injuries
- **Shoulder** (rotator cuff, impingement, labrum): ache at the front or side,
  pain in hangs or overhead. Rest from hangs, refer if more than a week.
- **Wrist and elbow**: tendon overload from grip and hoop; medial or lateral elbow pain.
- **Rib and intercostal**: sharp pain breathing or twisting after wraps or beats;
  possible rib stress injury - stop and refer.
- **Lower back**: from arching inversions and back balances.
- **Fabric burns and bruises**: expected in small doses; deep burns mean the wrap path
  is wrong.
- **Head knocks** on hoop or bar: any concussion symptom (headache, dizziness, confusion,
  nausea) - stop training that day and follow a medical return protocol.

## Return to training
- Pain-free daily life first, then floor conditioning, then low-height apparatus,
  then full height. Each stage at least one session without symptom increase.
- Anything worsening, recurring or affecting sleep: physio or sports doctor.

## Verify
- [ ] Coaches know the fatigue signs and have a stop rule.
- [ ] Injuries logged with the return-to-train stage.
- [ ] Concussion protocol posted in the studio.
