# Mapping an aerial act to grip and endurance

**Goal:** an aerial act must be safe on the worst day: tired, sweaty, second show. Map
where grip and strength are spent and design rests so the hardest element has enough
reserve.

## Build a load map
1. For each figure, note the main load: grip (hands), shoulders, core, legs/wraps.
2. Score each 1-3 for intensity and note its duration.
3. Plot as a timeline. Long runs of hand-grip-dependent figures are red flags.

## Design rests
- Insert rests where hands are free (footlock, hip key, seated in the hoop) every
  45-60 s of grip-heavy work.
- Alternate load types: a leg-based pose after a hand-hang sequence.

## Place the hardest element
- Usually around two-thirds through, after warm-up but before grip fatigue. Never put
  a drop that needs a strong hand release at the very end of a grip-heavy sequence.

## Test tired
- Do a full run after a hard training session, or run the act twice back-to-back.
- Any figure that fails under fatigue is moved, simplified, or preceded by a rest.

## Verify
- [ ] Load map drawn and agreed with the coach.
- [ ] Two consecutive full runs completed with no grip failures.
