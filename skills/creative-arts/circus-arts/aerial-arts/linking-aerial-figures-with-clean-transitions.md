# Linking aerial figures with clean transitions

**Goal:** an aerial act is judged on the moments between tricks as much as the tricks.
Clean transitions hide re-wrapping, save grip and keep the audience's attention.

## Map positions
1. List every figure with its **entry position** (where you start) and **exit
   position** (where you end): e.g. "hip key right, fabric pole A front".
2. Draw arrows only between figures where exit matches the next entry or a simple
   move connects them.
3. Mark sides: many wraps are side-specific; a left hip key cannot lead to a right one
   without a rebuild.

## Wrap economy
- Count wrap actions between figures. If a transition takes more than two wrap actions,
  either reorder or design the wrap as visible choreography.
- Prefer transitions that pass through a rest (footlock, hip key) so the act has
  built-in recovery.

## Making the in-between visible
- A slow roll, a reach, a line held for a count turns a re-wrap into a moment.
- Keep eyes and focus deliberate during wraps; looking down at feet reads as "working".

## Rehearsal
- Rehearse transitions as separate drills, then in pairs of figures, then in full runs.
- Film from the audience side.

## Verify
- [ ] Every figure in the act has a known entry and exit position.
- [ ] No transition longer than about 5 s without intentional choreography.
