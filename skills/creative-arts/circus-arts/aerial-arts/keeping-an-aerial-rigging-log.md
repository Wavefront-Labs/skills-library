# Keeping an aerial rigging log

**Goal:** a record that answers, for any piece of hardware or soft goods on any day:
what is it, where is it, who checked it, and when it leaves service.

## Two documents
1. **Equipment register** - one row per item:
   ID (engrave or tag: `C-014`), type, manufacturer, model, rating (MBS/WLL), serial or
   batch, purchase date, first-use date, location/point, retirement date + reason.
2. **Inspection log** - one row per check:
   date, point or item IDs, check type (pre-use / periodic / post-incident), result
   (OK / defect), inspector name, signature, action taken.

## Check types and frequency
- **Pre-use** (every session, by a trained user or coach): visual and functional,
  gates lock, swivel turns, soft goods sound. Two minutes, tick-box.
- **Periodic thorough inspection** (by a competent rigger): full disassembly look,
  rating tags, wear on connectors, anchor condition. Frequency set by the rigger;
  UK LOLER practice for people-lifting equipment is at least every 6 months.
- **Post-incident**: after any fall arrest, dropped hardware, unusual noise or
  overload. Item quarantined until inspected.

## Practical setup
- Paper sheet at each point *plus* a shared spreadsheet; paper gets filled because it is
  next to the apparatus.
- Colour-coded tags per inspection period so an out-of-date item is visible.
- A red "quarantine" box: anything suspect goes in it, not back in the kit bag.

## Gotchas
- Untraceable items: if it has no ID, it does not get rigged.
- Touring: the log travels with the kit; venue points get their own sign-off sheet from
  the house rigger.
- Logs that are always "OK" in the same handwriting are a warning sign, not proof.

## Verify
- [ ] Pick any carabiner on the floor - its ID is in the register.
- [ ] Today's pre-use line is filled for every rigged point.
- [ ] Periodic inspection is in date for every item.
- [ ] Quarantined items have a matching post-incident entry.
