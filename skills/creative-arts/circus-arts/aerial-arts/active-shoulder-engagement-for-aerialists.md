# Active shoulder engagement for aerialists

**Goal:** every hang on silks, hoop, trapeze or straps starts with the shoulder blades set,
so load goes through muscle rather than the passive capsule and labrum.

## What "active" means
From a passive dead hang (ears buried in shoulders), pull the shoulder blades **down and
slightly together** without bending the elbows. The body rises a few centimetres. That
lift is the scapular pull-up; holding the top is an active hang.

## Drill progression
1. **Standing cue**: arms overhead against a wall, slide the blades down; feel the lats.
2. **Scapular pull-ups on a bar**: 3 x 8, pause 2 s at top, slow lower.
3. **Active hang holds**: 3 x 20-30 s; if the shoulders creep up, end the set.
4. **One-arm active hang** with feet supported, then free.
5. **Transfer**: before every climb or inversion, coach cues "set" and checks.

## Coaching cues
- "Long neck, shoulders in back pockets."
- "Push the bar away" (for straps and hoop pull-overs).
- For inversions: shoulders stay set as the hips come over; if they shrug, the kick is
  doing the work.

## Red flags
- Clicking, catching, or pain at the front of the shoulder in hangs.
- One side visibly higher in the hang (asymmetry - often the non-dominant side).
- Students who can only hold passively for any length: limit time at height.
Refer persistent pain to a physio who knows aerial loads.

## Verify
- [ ] Student can do 8 controlled scapular pull-ups with a visible rise.
- [ ] 30 s active hang with no creep.
- [ ] Coach sees shoulders set at the start of every climb and inversion.
