# Checking the wrap before a silks drop

**Goal:** never release into a drop on a wrap you have not verified. Most serious silks
accidents are a correct drop performed from an incorrect wrap.

## The four-step check (every drop, every time, including shows)
1. **Narrate**: in training, say each wrap aloud in order ("left pole in front, right
   pole behind, two wraps around waist, tails between legs"). Narration exposes the
   moment memory and reality diverge.
2. **Look**: find the critical feature with your eyes: the fabric crossing the hip, the
   pole that must be on top, the number of waist wraps.
3. **Feel**: weight the wrap slightly before release - a correct wrap holds; a wrong
   one usually slides or feels loose in a specific place.
4. **Confirm** (training): coach says "yes" before release. No answer means no.

## Abort options
Decide them before you climb:
- Climb out of the wrap upward and re-stand in footlock.
- Hold the poles above the wrap and unwind slowly to a seated or standing position.
- Call for the coach and hold a rest position.
Make "abort" a normal, praised choice in your studio.

## In performance
Choreograph a visible "settle" moment before each drop (a look, a breath) that is the
check. Audiences read it as suspense; you use it to verify.

## Gotchas
- Fatigue and adrenaline in shows cause wrap errors in moves done hundreds of times.
- Costume changes (new fabric, sleeves) change how a wrap feels; rehearse in costume.
- Fabric with different stretch (new silk vs old) changes drop length; recheck height.

## Verify
- [ ] Student can narrate every wrap in their sequence from memory.
- [ ] Coach has observed at least one deliberate abort in practice.
- [ ] Every drop in the act has a choreographed check moment.
