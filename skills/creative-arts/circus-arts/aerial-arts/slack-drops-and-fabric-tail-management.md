# Slack drops and fabric tail management

**Goal:** understand slack drops - where gathered fabric gives the performer a short
free fall before the wrap catches - well enough to plan rigging, measure the fall and
keep tails out of the way.

> Advanced, coach-led, and a rigging conversation first. Slack drops produce the highest
> dynamic loads on silks.

## Load and rigging
- The free fall ends in a sudden arrest. Peak loads are much higher than in unrolling
  drops (see dynamic-load-factors-for-aerial-points). Tell the rigger the act includes
  slack drops and the free-fall length.
- Fabric stretch absorbs some of the shock; low-stretch fabric is harsher.
- Inspect the rescue-8 and swivel more often in acts with slack drops.

## Measuring slack
- The slack held in the hands (or gathered in loops) equals roughly twice the fall
  distance for a loop of fabric. Mark the gather point with tape so the amount is the
  same every time.
- Start with very small slack at low height; add slack in small increments.

## Fabric tail management
- Long tails can wrap a foot or neck during rotation. Before the drop, place the tails
  deliberately (between legs, behind, or held).
- Tails on the floor: in choreography, avoid stepping on or tangling the tail before
  climbing; assign stagehands to dress tails in shows if needed.

## Faults
- Uneven slack between the two poles: twisting landing.
- Catch on the neck or wrist: stop and rebuild the wrap.

## Verify
- [ ] Rigger informed and point rated for slack-drop loads.
- [ ] Slack amount marked and consistent across reps.
- [ ] Tail placement is part of the narrated wrap check.
