# Inspecting and retiring aerial soft goods

**Goal:** catch a damaged silk, sling, strap or rope before it fails, and retire it on a
rule rather than on a feeling.

## What counts as soft goods
Aerial fabric (nylon tricot), round slings / span sets (polyester core yarns in a
cover), aerial straps (webbing or cotton-wrapped), rope (corde lisse, Spanish web) and
any webbing in safety lines.

## Inspection routine (before every session, full detail weekly)
1. Run the whole length through your hands, both faces. Fabric: watch for **runs and
   pulls**, holes, thin glazed patches (friction burns), cuts.
2. Round slings: check the cover; if the **red core yarns or warning tell-tale** are
   visible, retire immediately. Feel for lumps or flat spots (broken core).
3. Webbing straps: edge cuts, fraying stitching, stiff or melted areas, discoloured
   patches from chemicals.
4. Rope: core shots (core showing through the sheath), soft or stiff sections, glazing.
5. Check the label is legible: synthetic slings without a readable rating tag are
   removed from service under ASME B30.9 practice.

## Retirement rules (any one = retire)
- Cut, hole or run that cannot be stopped (fabric) or any cut in load-bearing webbing.
- Heat or friction damage: glazing, melting, hardening.
- Chemical contamination (acids, bleach) or unknown stains that changed feel.
- Knots that were shock-loaded and will not release, or a fabric that has been in a
  serious fall arrest.
- Unreadable rating tag (slings).
- Manufacturer's age limit reached. Fabric life varies hugely with use; many studios
  retire teaching silks on a fixed schedule (often 1-3 years) regardless of look.
  *Exact schedule is a venue policy; record it.*

## Storage and care
- Out of direct sunlight (UV degrades nylon and polyester).
- Dry before bagging; no storage near solvents or batteries.
- Wash fabric only per manufacturer instructions; no fabric softener (slippery).

## Gotchas
- Fabric damage often hides in the knot or the section inside the rescue-8: untie and
  look on the weekly inspection.
- Sharp jewellery and nails are the main cause of pulls - enforce removal.
- A retired item must be **destroyed or cut** so it cannot drift back into use.
