# Silks and lyra — foundations and safe progression

**Goal:** build an aerial vocabulary on fabric (silks/tissu) and hoop (lyra) without
the two things that end aerial careers: a rigging failure and a drop that unrolls
wrong. Technique comes second to both.

> **Safety first, honestly stated.** Aerial work causes falls from height, shoulder and
> neck injuries, and fabric burns. This skill is a structure for learning under a
> qualified coach, not a substitute for one. Never self-teach drops or rig your own
> point from a ceiling, beam or tree without a qualified rigger/engineer signing it off.

## 1. Rigging: the non-negotiables (check every session)
- **Rated point only.** The anchor must be assessed by a qualified rigger or structural
  engineer for dynamic aerial loads. Drops and beats generate peak forces several times
  body weight; figures in the 5–10× body-weight range are commonly quoted, and riggers
  then apply large safety factors on top (often 10:1 on hardware). *Exact design factors
  vary by country and rigger — treat these as orders of magnitude, not a spec.*
- **Hardware chain:** rated anchor › rated sling/span set › locking steel carabiner or
  rated shackle (gate locked, loaded along its spine) › swivel (for silks/lyra spin)
  › rescue-8 or silk knot › apparatus. No climbing-gym "keylock" toys, no hardware-store
  hooks, no aluminium where your rigger specifies steel.
- **Pre-flight inspection:** gates locked; no cuts, burns or pulls in fabric; lyra
  tabs/welds not cracked; swivel turns freely; nothing rubbing on a sharp edge.
- **Mat under the apparatus**, sized for where you might land (drops travel), typically
  a thick crash mat for skill work, not a yoga mat.
- **Log it.** Date, inspector, hardware serials, fabric age. Fabric and soft goods are
  retired on schedule or on damage, whichever comes first.

## 2. Prerequisites before leaving the ground
Common coaching gates (adjust per coach):
- Dead hang 30 s with active shoulders (scapulae pulled down — never hang "in the
  sockets" for long).
- 3–5 strict pull-ups or a controlled flexed-arm hang + negatives.
- Hanging knee-to-chest ×10, working toward toes-to-bar (the inversion engine).
- Hollow body hold 30 s on the floor.
Without these, people muscle inversions with a kick and arched back — that is the
classic route to a shoulder or lower-back strain.

## 3. The foundation sequence
**Silks:** basic climb (Russian or French climb) › footlock (single, then double) as
your rest position › straddle-up / inversion from standing on the floor › inverted
straddle on the fabric › hip key › first poses from footlock and hip key. Only after a
reliable inversion from the floor learn **inversion in the air**.

**Lyra:** pull-over/mount from below › sitting the bar › back balance and front
balance › hocks/knee hang (arrive with control, never fall into it) › mermaid, man-in-the-moon
› beats and spins *only* once static shapes are solid.

## 4. Wraps before drops
Every drop is a wrap that unrolls under gravity. Progression:
1. Learn the wrap **low** (feet near the mat) and walk through it at slow speed.
2. Coach checks the wrap by eye; you narrate each wrap ("left leg front, fabric over
   hip, one wrap around waist").
3. Do the drop **low, over the mat, with a coach spotting** the release.
4. Raise height gradually across sessions, never in the same session you first did it.
**Wrong wrap = no drop.** If anything feels different from rehearsal, abort by
climbing out or re-standing in footlock. Make "say no" normal in your class.

## 5. Spotting
- Spotters stand outside the fall line, hands ready at the hips/torso, and know which
  way the drop travels.
- On lyra, spot under the hoop for new knee hangs and the transition from sit to hang.
- Agree the bail-out cue before the attempt.

## Gotchas
- **Grip fatigue is sudden.** Plan the session so the hardest skills come early.
- **Fabric burns** behind knees/armpits: wear leggings and long sleeves while learning.
- **Spin** on a swivel builds dizziness; limit spin work early and stop when vision blurs.
- **Jewellery and long nails** damage fabric and you — remove them.
- Heat and humidity make fabric slippery; rosin use is a coach/venue decision.
- Coming down: climb down or use a controlled slide, never "jump the last bit".

- [ ] Mat is under the full drop path.
- [ ] You can meet the strength gates above before working at height.
- [ ] Every new drop was first done low, with a spotter, after the wrap was checked.
- [ ] You can name each wrap aloud and would abort if one felt wrong.
