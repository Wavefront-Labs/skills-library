# Matting layout under aerial apparatus

**Goal:** a mat surface that covers everywhere a falling aerialist can land, with no gaps,
edges or soft holes that turn a fall into an ankle or neck injury.

## Mat types
- **Crash mat** (soft foam, 20-40 cm): skill learning and drops. Absorbs falls but can
  roll ankles on a standing landing.
- **Landing/safety mat** (firmer, 10-20 cm): general aerial work, walking under the
  apparatus.
- **Panel mats** (5 cm folding): floor protection and joining, not fall protection.
Choose thickness for the height you train at; higher work needs thicker, softer mats.

## Coverage
- Centre the mat on the apparatus line, then extend for **travel**:
  - Silks drops: in the drop direction (often 1.5-2 m beyond the line).
  - Lyra and static trapeze beats: in the swing plane on both sides.
  - Spin: radius of the furthest limb at full extension.
- Swinging apparatus needs mats along the whole arc or a net.

## Joining
- Butt mats tightly; Velcro edges together where available.
- Cover seams with a thin topper or panel mat so a foot cannot drop between them.
- No mats overlapping in a step: tripping edges cause more injuries than gaps.

## Daily checks
- Mats pulled back into place after every drop (they creep).
- Covers not torn; foam not crushed into a permanent dip in the landing zone.

## Gotchas
- People stand on crash mats to spot and sink in; spotters stand on firm ground or
  firm mats.
- Under a moving apparatus, mats shift with each swing; re-check between turns.

## Verify
- [ ] Mats cover drop travel and swing arc measured for today's skills.
- [ ] No exposed seams or step edges in the landing zone.
- [ ] Mat thickness matches the working height.
