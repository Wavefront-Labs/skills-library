# Wrist loops and wrapping on aerial straps

**Goal:** a straps act lives or dies on the connection at the wrist. Correct loops and
wrapping protect the wrist and make the hold secure without cutting circulation.

> Coach-led. Straps load the wrist and shoulder hard; the coach checks every wrap.

## Setup
- Straps are two separate lengths of webbing or cotton strap, each with a loop, hung
  from separate rated points or a spreader. Loops are sized so the hand passes through
  and the strap sits around the wrist, not the forearm.
- Many artists add padding or a wrap inside the loop; keep it secured so it cannot
  slide out mid-act.

## Wrapping
1. Hand goes through the loop from the side that lets the strap tighten as the arm
   turns (the "wrap" direction).
2. Rotate the wrist to take one turn of strap around the wrist and hand; grip the strap
   above the loop.
3. Load the hang. The strap should lock without pinching skin at the base of the thumb.
- Practise wrapping and unwrapping without looking; transitions depend on it.

## Hand protection
- Skin toughens over weeks; burns and blisters early are common. Tape or wrist wraps
  help; avoid anything that changes grip feel mid-act.
- Check for numbness or colour change in the hand; that means the wrap is too tight or
  sitting too long.

## Inspection
- Webbing: no cuts, glazing, fraying, or stitch damage at the loop. Retire on damage.
- Log strap age and use with the rest of the rigging record.

## Faults
- Loop too big: hand slides through on a dynamic move.
- Loop too small: circulation cut in sustained holds.

## Verify
- [ ] Student wraps both hands without looking.
- [ ] No numbness after a 30 s hang test.
