# Building the aerial hardware chain

**Goal:** connect a rated overhead point to a silk, hoop or strap with a chain in which
every link is rated, correctly oriented and inspectable, so the weakest part of the
system is still far stronger than any load a performer can generate.

> Assessing the *anchor itself* (beam, truss, ceiling) is a qualified rigger's or
> structural engineer's job. This skill starts at a point that someone competent has
> already rated and signed off.

## The standard chain (top to bottom)
1. **Rated anchor / beam** - signed off with a working load limit (WLL).
2. **Span set (round sling) or wire rope basket** around the beam, protected from sharp
   edges by a wear pad or sleeve. Choke hitches reduce capacity (often to ~80%); baskets
   double it only if the legs are near vertical.
3. **Steel screw-gate / auto-locking carabiner or bow shackle** - rated for life-safety
   use, MBS stamped in kN. Shackle pins moused or tightened then backed a quarter turn
   with a seizing wire if it will not be checked daily.
4. **Rotating swivel** (ball-bearing, rated) for silks and lyra spin. Omit only on
   apparatus designed not to spin (some straps setups, static trapeze).
5. **Second connector** (carabiner) below the swivel.
6. **Rescue-8 / silk knot** (silks) or **lyra spanset / tabs** (hoop).
7. **Apparatus.**

Every connector must sit with the load running along its **spine** (major axis). A
carabiner loaded across the gate or in three directions loses most of its strength.

## Choosing parts
- Steel over aluminium for permanent points: aluminium wears grooves from fabric and
  fails with less warning. Aluminium climbing kit is fine only if the rigger specifies it.
- Avoid hardware-store hooks, quick links without a rating stamp, keylock "toy"
  carabiners and anything without a traceable manufacturer and rating.
- Match swivel rating to the rest of the chain; cheap swivels are the most common
  weak link.
- Keep the chain short: every extra link is another thing to inspect.

## Assembly sequence
1. Lay all parts on the floor and check stamps, gate action, swivel rotation.
2. Rig the sling, then the top connector; close and lock the gate, gate facing away
   from the beam and down so it cannot be rolled open.
3. Add swivel and lower connector; lock.
4. Tie the rescue-8 on silks (dress the knot, tails even, tie-off on the top loop).
5. Hoist or lift into place; check nothing rubs a sharp edge when it swings or spins.
6. **Load test**: a person hangs from low height while the rigger watches every link.

## Failure modes
- Gate unscrews under spin: screw-gates loosen with rotation. Re-check before every
  session or use triple-action auto-locking gates.
- Cross-loading when the swivel sits sideways in a carabiner.
- Sling abrasion on an unpadded steel edge - invisible from the floor.
- Mixing rated and unrated parts "just for today".

## Verify
- [ ] Every link carries a legible rating and is listed in the rigging log.
- [ ] All gates locked and loaded on the spine; swivel turns freely under load.
- [ ] No contact between sling and sharp edges at full swing or spin.
- [ ] A competent person signed the point and the chain today.
