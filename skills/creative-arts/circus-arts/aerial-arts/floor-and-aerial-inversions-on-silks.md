# Floor and aerial inversions on silks

**Goal:** go from upright to inverted straddle with the fabric between the legs, first
from the floor, then from a climb. This is the entry to most silks vocabulary.

> Coach-led. The aerial inversion is not attempted until the floor version is clean and
> controlled for several sessions.

## Prerequisites
- Hanging knee raises to chest, working toward toes-to-bar.
- 20-30 s hollow hold; 3 strict pull-ups or a controlled flexed-arm hang.

## Floor inversion (straddle-up)
1. Hands on both poles at about head height, one above the other, thumbs down.
2. Arms bent, shoulders active; tuck knees to chest, then open to straddle as the hips
   rise above the hands.
3. Fabric sits between the legs, on the inner thigh; legs open wide, toes pointed.
4. Lower with control the same way (hips first, then legs together).

## Aerial inversion
- From a climb or a standing footlock, hands at chest height, same tuck-straddle
  action. The coach spots under the hips/back from the floor at low height.
- Mat under the apparatus; height below a safe fall height set by the coach.

## Coaching points
- Initiate by lifting the hips, not by throwing the head back.
- Keep arms bent; straight-arm inversions are a later strength skill.
- Exhale on the lift; no breath holding.

## Faults
- Kicking up: momentum replaces control and loads the lower back.
- Arched back when inverted: abdominals off, shoulders strained.
- Hands too low on the fabric: no leverage, body falls through.

## Verify
- [ ] 5 controlled floor inversions, pause 2 s inverted, controlled return.
- [ ] Coach-spotted aerial inversion at low height before unspotted attempts.
