# Flexibility training for aerial lines

**Goal:** lines that read from the back of the house - full splits, open backbends and
overhead shoulder range - available *in the air*, where you must hold them with muscle.

## Passive vs active
Passive range (on the floor with gravity helping) sets the ceiling. Aerial needs
**active** range: the position you can reach and hold with your own muscles. Train both,
and expect active range to lag by 10-30 degrees.

## Weekly structure
- Passive: 2-3 sessions, after training or as a separate warm session, 3 x 45-60 s
  holds per position (front splits both sides, middle split, pancake, bridge, shoulder
  extension).
- Active: every session, short and hard: leg lifts at end range (3 x 8, 2 s hold),
  pike and straddle lifts seated, active bridge push-ups.
- Transfer: practise the line on the apparatus at low height: splits in hip key,
  back-balance on hoop with arms overhead.

## Oversplits
Front splits with the front heel raised on a block are common in training. Keep them
modest, warm, and never bounced. Hypermobile students often need strength, not more range.

## Gotchas
- Cold static stretching right before heavy aerial work can reduce power; warm up
  dynamically first and keep long holds for later.
- Lower back takes the bend when the thoracic spine and shoulders are stiff: train
  thoracic extension and shoulder flexion, not just lumbar bridges.
- Hamstring or adductor tweaks from forcing a split mid-act are common; never chase
  range on the apparatus when tired.

## Verify
- [ ] Measure front and middle splits (hand-to-floor or block height) monthly.
- [ ] Active leg lift height recorded and rising.
- [ ] Line held clean for 3 s in the air at low height.
