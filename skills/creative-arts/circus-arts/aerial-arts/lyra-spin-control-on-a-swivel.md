# Spin control on a lyra swivel

**Goal:** use spin as an effect without losing orientation, grip or the audience. Spin
multiplies difficulty; it is added only to poses that are already secure.

> Coach-led. Introduce spin with a ground spinner and short durations; stop on any
> nausea or disorientation.

## Rig check
- A rated swivel in the chain (sealed bearing, rated for the dynamic load) and no
  twist in spansets above it. A swivel that grinds or sticks is retired.
- Clearance: hoop diameter plus extended limbs plus margin to any obstacle.

## Starting and controlling spin
- **Ground spinner** walks the hoop to start rotation, then releases; can slow or stop
  the hoop by catching the bottom bar with an open hand on command.
- **Self-spin:** push off the floor or throw the legs; less consistent.
- **Speed control by body shape:** pulling limbs towards the axis speeds up (angular
  momentum is conserved); opening out slows it. Plan poses in this order.

## Dizziness management
- Spot a fixed point where possible; look at the hoop instead of the room when
  rotating fast.
- Build tolerance over weeks in short bouts (10-20 s), not in one long session.
- After spinning, wait seated before standing on the floor.

## Coaching points
- Agree spinner calls: "more", "hold", "stop".
- Never let go to "enjoy" the spin; grip stays loaded.

## Faults
- Opening into a pose at full speed: the hoop decelerates sharply and grip is torn.
- Spinner stops the hoop abruptly: whiplash on the performer.

## Verify
- [ ] Performer can name which poses speed and slow the spin.
- [ ] 30 s spin sequence completed without disorientation before staging.
