# Rescuing a stuck aerialist

**Goal:** have a rehearsed plan for getting a person down when they cannot climb down
themselves, before you ever need it.

## Situations
- Fabric tangled around a limb or (worst case) neck.
- Grip or strength exhausted mid-air.
- Injury or fainting at height.

## Plan in advance
1. **Access**: a ladder, scaffold, or mobile elevated work platform that reaches the
   working height, stored nearby, with someone trained to use it.
2. **Lowering**: the best option is a point rigged on a **lowering system** (rescue
   descender or hand winch) so the whole apparatus can be lowered. Ask the rigger.
3. **Cutting**: a rescue hook knife or trauma shears for fabric - only after the person
   is supported. Never cut a load-bearing fabric holding the person's weight.
4. **Roles**: one leader, one on access, one supporting the body, one calling help.
5. **Rehearse** the rescue twice a year and in each new venue.

## Doing it
1. Talk first: calm voice, ask what is stuck and whether they can take weight on a
   footlock or sit.
2. Have them reach a rest position if possible (footlock, sit on the hoop bar).
3. Bring access to them or lower the apparatus.
4. Support the body weight, then free the tangle.
5. If fabric is around the neck: support the body upwards *immediately* to take load off
   the neck, then free it. This is the one case for speed over planning.

## What not to do
- Climbing up the same fabric to help: doubles load and tangles further.
- Pulling the person's legs from below.
- Letting the person "just drop" onto mats from height.

## Verify
- [ ] Access equipment for the working height is on site and someone is trained.
- [ ] Rescue plan written in the risk assessment and rehearsed this season.
- [ ] Knife or shears kept at the apparatus.
