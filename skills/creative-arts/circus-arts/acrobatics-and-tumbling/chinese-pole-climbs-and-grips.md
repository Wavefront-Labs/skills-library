# Chinese pole climbs and grips

**Goal:** climb and descend a rubber-coated Chinese pole efficiently and with locks that
let you hold position hands-free.

## The pole
Steel pole, typically 4-9 m, coated with rubber for grip, fixed at the base and guyed or
attached at the top. Grip comes from friction: skin and textured fabric against rubber.

## Grips and locks
- **Hand grip**: hands wrap the pole, thumbs around, pulling in and down.
- **Foot grip**: soles of the feet press the pole from either side (shoes or bare feet
  depending on tradition).
- **Thigh lock**: pole between the inner thighs, squeezing.
- **Shin/instep lock**: pole between the shin of one leg and the instep of the other.
- **Elbow hook**: pole in the crook of the elbow for one-arm holds.

## Climbs
- **Caterpillar**: hands reach up, knees tuck and feet grip higher, legs push, repeat.
- **Arm-only climb**: hand over hand with legs held in a straddle or L (strength goal).
- **Spiral climb**: climbing around the pole for visual effect.

## Descents
- Controlled slide: friction from thighs and hands, speed regulated by squeeze.
- Hand-over-hand down.
- Never free-slide without training the stop; friction burns and speed build quickly.

## Protection
- Long sleeves and full-length legs in grippy material while learning; experienced
  performers often wear specialised suits with grip patches.
- Skin: burns on inner thighs and arms are common - clean and dress them.

## Verify
- [ ] Caterpillar climb to 3 m and controlled descent, 3 reps.
- [ ] Hands-free hold in thigh lock 10 s at low height.
