# Choosing tumbling surfaces

**Goal:** the surface changes what a skill feels like and how hard the impacts are.
Training, rehearsing and performing surfaces are chosen on purpose.

## Air track (inflatable tumbling track)
- Soft, bouncy; good for learning and high volume with low impact.
- Inflation pressure changes rebound; set and record it for consistency.
- Overuse risk: skills learned only on air track feel very different on a hard floor.

## Sprung floor
- Gymnastics-style floor with springs or foam blocks under panels; standard for
  training tumbling. Predictable rebound.

## Mats
- Landing mats (thick, soft) for new skills; thinner tumbling mats for passes.
- Joins between mats cause rolled ankles; mats must be joined (velcro) and placed with
  no gaps.

## Stage floor
- Usually hard (wood over concrete or a dance floor); no rebound and high impact.
- Check for slipperiness (haze, paint), splinters, traps and joins.
- Skills performed on a stage should have been solid for months on harder surfaces.
- A portable sprung floor or tumbling strip is often in the tech rider.

## Progression of surfaces
- New skill: air track or soft mats.
- Consolidation: sprung floor.
- Performance prep: performance surface in rehearsal.

## Verify
- [ ] Tech rider lists the required surface.
- [ ] Skills rehearsed on the performance surface before the show.
