# Mat stacking and landing zones for acrobatics

**Goal:** a landing surface matched to the skill: soft enough to absorb a fall, firm enough
not to sprain an ankle on a good landing.

## Types
- Panel mats (firm, 3-5 cm): rolls, cartwheels, floor work.
- Landing mats (10-20 cm): handsprings, saltos.
- Crash/soft mats (20-40 cm): learning new skills, falls from height.
- Sting mat (thin, firm, 5-10 cm) on top of a crash mat: spreads load so feet do not
  sink and twist.
- Incline (cheese) mat: rolls and walkovers.

## Landing zones
- Size the zone for the skill's travel plus margin: a back tuck can land a metre or more
  behind the take-off; partner throws land far from the base.
- In partner and group work, mat where the flyer falls if a base collapses - usually all
  around the build.

## Stacking for learning rotations
- Raised landing (stacked mats or a block) lets students land rotations before full
  rotation is available. Lower the stack as they improve.
- Always firm on top of soft, never soft on top of a slippery firm mat.

## Seams and edges
- Butt mats tight, Velcro them, cover seams with a panel mat.
- No mat edges in the landing area; step edges cause ankle injuries.

## Gotchas
- Very soft mats under standing landings cause knee and ankle sprains; use a sting mat.
- Mats creep after every landing; reset them.
- Spotters standing on soft mats lose stability.

## Verify
- [ ] Landing zone measured for the skill's travel.
- [ ] Sting mat on any crash mat used for standing landings.
- [ ] Seams covered, edges outside landing area.
