# Hand-to-hand base position and stacking alignment

**Goal:** in hand-to-hand the base holds the flyer's handstand on their hands. The base
lasts a career only by stacking the load through the skeleton, not holding it with
muscle.

> Coach-led. New pairs work with a spotter and mats; the base's alignment is checked
> before every new balance.

## Stacking
- The flyer's centre of mass, the base's hands, wrists, elbows, shoulders and (standing)
  hips and feet form one vertical line.
- Elbows locked straight, shoulders pushed up into the load (elevated, like a press
  lockout), head neutral looking up at the flyer between the arms.

## Hand shape and grip
- **Hand-in-hand grip:** base's palm under the flyer's palm, fingers wrapping the
  flyer's hand/wrist, thumbs aligned. Base fingers do the corrections.
- Base hands slightly turned out; the flyer's fingers point forward.

## Base positions
- **Lying base (low hand):** base on the back, arms vertical; the easiest load path.
- **Standing base (high hand):** feet hip-width, knees soft then locked, ribs down,
  glutes tight; load through the legs to the floor.

## Corrections
- Small corrections in the fingers and wrists; big corrections are steps by a standing
  base. Never correct by bending the elbows.

## Faults
- Arching the lower back to take the weight (standing base).
- Hands drifting forward of the shoulders: the line breaks and muscles take over.

## Verify
- [ ] Base holds a vertical weighted press (or flyer) 10 s with a straight line.
- [ ] Coach checks the line from the side.
