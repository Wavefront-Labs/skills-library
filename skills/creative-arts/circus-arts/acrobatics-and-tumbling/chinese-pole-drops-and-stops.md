# Chinese pole drops and stops

**Goal:** understand the signature head-first drop and how performers stop it, so a coach
can progress it safely.

> Coach-led only. A failed stop means a head-first impact. Learn under a pole coach.

## The stop
The drop is a controlled slide; the stop is friction created by clamping:
- Feet or shins clamp around the pole (usually the shin/instep lock),
- Thighs squeeze,
- Hands may add friction.
The stop must be fully reliable before any drop length is added.

## Progression
1. Head-up slides with stops at low height.
2. Inverted hang in thigh lock, release slowly a short distance and re-clamp.
3. Short head-first drops (tens of centimetres) over a crash mat, coach at the pole.
4. Increase length gradually over weeks; final drops stop well above the floor.
5. Always train drops over mats; perform with matting or sufficient stop margin.

## Clothing
Grip suits or thick fabric on shins and thighs both protect skin and provide predictable
friction. A change of fabric changes stopping distance: re-test after any costume change.

## Failure modes
- Sweat or dust on the pole reducing friction: clean the pole, dry skin.
- Fatigue reducing clamp strength: drops early in the act.
- New costume material.

## Verify
- [ ] Stop distance measured and consistent at each drop length.
- [ ] Drop never trained without crash mat and coach present.
