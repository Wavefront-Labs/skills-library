# Chinese pole base and guying

**Goal:** a pole that stays plumb and rigid while performers climb, flag and drop on it.

## Two installation types
- **Top-fixed**: pole base on the floor, top attached to a rated overhead point or truss.
  Requires a rigger's sign-off for the top point; lateral loads from flags are large.
- **Freestanding guyed**: base plate on the floor, three or four guy lines from a collar
  near the top to floor anchors or ballast. Common in tours and outdoors.

## Base
- Base plate on a level, load-spreading surface. On stage, spread loads over joists
  (plywood or steel spreader) and confirm floor capacity with the venue.
- Base must not walk under lateral load: pinned, ballasted or tied back.

## Guying
- Typical guy angles around 45-60 degrees from vertical; manufacturer spec rules.
- Equal tension on opposite guys; use ratchets or turnbuckles, locked.
- Anchors: stage weights / ballast, floor anchors, or ground stakes outdoors - each
  rated for the guy load the manufacturer gives.
- Flag at head height where people walk.

## Checks
- Plumb in two directions with a spirit level after tensioning.
- Climb test by an experienced performer: shake and flag at low height, re-check plumb.
- Daily: tensioners locked, anchors secure, rubber coating intact (tears catch skin).

## Gotchas
- Two poles in one act: their guys must not cross the performance paths.
- Temperature changes alter guy tension outdoors; check before each show.

## Verify
- [ ] Floor capacity and anchor ratings documented.
- [ ] Plumb checked after tension and after test climb.
- [ ] Daily check logged.
