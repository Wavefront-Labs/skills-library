# Partner acrobatics and tumbling — progressions with spotting

**Goal:** add tumbling passes and partner balances to circus work in a sequence where
each skill is earned by the one before, with a spotter who knows what they are catching.

> **Safety.** Head and neck injuries in acrobatics come from under-rotation, falling
> flyers and unspotted "just one try" attempts. Learn inverted and flight skills only with
> a qualified coach. Spotting is a trained skill; an untrained friend is not a spotter.

## 1. Roles in partner acrobatics
- **Base:** supports weight. Works with stacked joints (wrist over elbow over shoulder,
  hips over heels) so bones carry the load, not muscles.
- **Flyer:** is lifted. Keeps a tight, rigid body ("hollow", glutes and core engaged) so
  the base moves one solid object, not a wobbly one.
- **Spotter:** stands where the flyer will fall, eyes on the flyer's hips and head,
  never part of the trick. One spotter per flyer while learning; more for pyramids.
- Agree the **bail rule** before every attempt: who calls it ("down!"), and how the
  flyer exits (usually step down forward or be caught at the waist).

## 2. Partner progression (roughly in order)
1. Counterbalances (both partners leaning, hands held) — teaches trust and alignment.
2. Low bases: base lying down, flyer in **front bird** on the base's feet, then
   throne, then **star** — all near the floor.
3. Standing two-highs: thigh stands, then shoulder stand with spotter behind.
4. Hand-to-hand: low-to-low hand balances, then high-to-hand (flyer in handstand on
   standing base's hands) — years of handstand prerequisites first (see
   creative-arts.circus-arts.hand-balancing-and-wire.handstand-training-and-wire-walking-basics).
5. Dynamic: pitches, throws, banquine (flyer thrown from bases' joined hands) — only
   with an experienced coach and a spotting belt/lunge for new rotations.

## 3. Tumbling progression
- Floor basics: forward and backward roll (chin tucked), cartwheel, handstand, bridge.
- **Round-off** — the most important tumbling skill; converts run into backward power.
- Back walkover and handspring drills on a wedge/barrel.
- **Back handspring (flic-flac)** spotted, then **round-off back handspring**.
- **Back tuck**: first spotted on a trampoline or into a pit/thick mat, with a spotting
  belt; then off a raised surface; then from standing on the floor.
- Combine only skills that are clean individually.

**Gate rule:** a skill moves to the next surface (pit › mat stack › sprung floor › stage)
only when ten in a row are clean with the spotter's hands *not* needed.

## 4. Surfaces
Stage floors are hard. Train new skills on a sprung floor or mats; a performance venue
needs either matting that suits the act or skills that have been bulletproof for months.

## Spotting technique (brief)
- Back tuck: one hand on the low back, one under the back of the thighs, lifting and
  turning — spotter walks with the athlete.
- Handspring: hand on low back, the other guiding the thighs over.
- Partner stands: spotter behind the flyer, hands ready at the waist, never grabbing ankles.
- The spotter calls off attempts when tired; fatigue is when spots fail.

## Gotchas
- **Under-rotation** lands on the neck; a skill that "sometimes lands low" is not ready.
- **Bases' lower backs** suffer from leaning; teach legs-do-the-work lifting.
- **Ego reps** at the end of a session cause most injuries.
- Fear balks mid-skill are dangerous; a balk means go back a step, not "try harder".
- *Specific spotting holds vary by school (gymnastics vs cheer vs circus); follow your coach's.*

## Verify
- [ ] Roles, bail call and exit agreed before each partner attempt.
- [ ] Every new inverted/flight skill had a trained spotter and suitable surface.
- [ ] Ten clean reps before changing surface or removing the spot.
- [ ] Bases stacking joints; flyers holding a rigid body line.
- [ ] Session stops before fatigue, not after the first fall.
