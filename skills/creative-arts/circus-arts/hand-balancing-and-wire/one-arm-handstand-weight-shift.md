# One-arm handstand: the weight shift

**Goal:** move the whole body's centre of mass over one hand without collapsing the line,
the core motor pattern of the one-arm handstand.

## Prerequisite
A freestanding straight handstand of 60 s and a straddle handstand of 30 s. Without
these, OAH training mostly trains bad habits.

## Mechanics
- Start in a **straddle** handstand: it lowers the centre of mass and widens control.
- Shift the **hips sideways** over the working hand; the shoulder of the working arm
  stays open and pushing tall, the body leans slightly.
- Free hand becomes lighter until only fingertips touch (see fingertip drills), then
  lifts.
- Working hand: fingers spread, slightly turned out; many balancers rotate the hand
  outward so the fingers can correct both directions.

## Drills
1. **Wall straddle shifts**: back or side to the wall, straddle, shift weight to one
   hand and lighten the other, 5 x 10 s each side.
2. **Freestanding shifts**: straddle, shift to 70/30, then 90/10, hold 5 s.
3. **Shift and return**: shift to one side, back to centre, to the other side.
4. **Side-wall OAH**: working side near a wall for support.

## Faults
- Shoulder collapse (working shoulder sinking): back to two-arm shrug strength.
- Twisting hips: square them to the hand.
- Shifting the shoulders instead of the hips.

## Verify
- [ ] 90/10 weight shift held 5 s each side, freestanding.
- [ ] Working shoulder visibly pushing tall in video.
