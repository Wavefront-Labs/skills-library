# Cane base stability and stage setup

**Goal:** a cane is only as stable as its base. Stage floors vary, so setup and checks are
routine before every rehearsal and show.

## Base
- A heavy steel base plate (often a large flat plate) keeps the cane from tipping; some
  bases are bolted, some weighted.
- Levelling feet or shims adjust for uneven floors.

## Fixing
- On a stage, canes may be screwed into the floor (with the venue's permission) or used
  on a base plate on a level floor.
- Check that the floor is level; a spirit level on the base.

## Spacing
- Two-cane positions: canes spaced for the artist's shoulder width.
- Multi-cane setups: measure and mark positions on the floor (spike marks).

## Inspection
- Check every joint, screw and block before use.
- Look for cracks in welds.

## Checks before the show
- Level checked; canes tight; spike marks visible; nothing slippery near the canes.

- [ ] Spike marks recorded in the tech rider.
