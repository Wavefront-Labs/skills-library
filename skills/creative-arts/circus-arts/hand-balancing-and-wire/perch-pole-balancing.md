# Perch pole balancing

A perch act has a base (carrier) who supports a long pole on the shoulders, in a harness, or on a belt, while the top performer works at the upper end. It is safety-critical: train only with an experienced perch coach and rigging professionals.

## Roles
- **Carrier:** holds the pole low, controls its balance, walks and sometimes turns. Needs a strong, stable stance and the ability to read the top performer.
- **Top performer:** climbs, balances and moves on the pole.
- **Spotters and rigging crew:** monitor the whole act.

## Safety principles
- Use poles built for the purpose (aluminium or carbon fibre, rated). Do not improvise with construction poles.
- Fall protection for the top performer is normally provided by an independent overhead safety system or line; the design and loads must be signed off by a qualified rigger or engineer, not guessed.
- Check the harness or belt socket, pole joints and all attachments before every session; retire damaged items.
- Mats and spotters on the floor; the exit route for a fall must be planned and marked.
- Stage lights, truss and ceilings must not be within the reach of the pole plus performer.

## Communication
Agree short calls ("ready", "go", "stop", "down") and a unique emergency word. Practise the calls at ground level before height.

## Learning progression
Begin with the pole at low height with a safety line, build the carrier's stance and the top performer's balance separately, then combine at low height before raising.

## Verify
- [ ] Qualified rigger or engineer has approved the safety system for the venue.
- [ ] Pole, harness and lines inspected and logged.
