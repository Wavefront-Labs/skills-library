# Balance props on tightwire (fan, umbrella, pole)

**Goal:** props are both balance aids and theatre. Each works by a different mechanism,
so the walker trains with the exact prop they will perform with.

## How each prop helps
- **Balance pole:** a long, often weighted pole held low and across the body. It adds
  rotational inertia, so a tip to one side happens more slowly and there is time to
  correct; a pole that droops at the ends lowers the effective centre of mass further.
  High-wire walkers use long, heavy poles; low-wire acts use short ones or none.
- **Fan:** small and light; corrections come from moving the fan (and arm), and from
  a little air resistance. Mostly a hand-and-arm counterweight and a visual flourish.
- **Parasol / umbrella:** held overhead or to the side; larger air resistance, slows
  sway, and is very readable from a distance.

## Choosing
- Weight and length decided in training, not on the day. A heavier or longer pole
  changes the timing of every correction.
- Parasol canopy size versus wind outdoors: a big canopy becomes a sail.

## Handling drills
1. Walk the floor line with the prop, then the low wire.
2. Open and close fan/parasol while standing still on the wire, then while walking.
3. Pass the prop hand to hand; drop and recover (assistant hands up a spare).

## Staging
- Colour and finish chosen for the lighting; metallic finishes can flash into the
  walker's eyes from front light.
- Plan where the prop is set down or handed off; a prop dropped onto the stage below
  is a hazard to others.

## Verify
- [ ] Performance prop used in all rehearsals for at least two weeks before the show.
- [ ] Spare prop and hand-off point agreed with the stage manager.
