# Rigging sag and anchors for slackwire

**Goal:** a slack wire is rigged with deliberate sag. Sag controls feel and, critically,
the force on the anchors. The artist chooses the feel; a qualified rigger checks the
loads.

> Rigging decisions are signed off by a qualified rigger.

## Sag and load
- For a wire loaded at its centre, anchor tension rises sharply as sag decreases:
  tension is roughly load divided by (2 × sin of the wire angle at the anchor). A wire
  at a small angle (little sag) can put many times the performer's weight into each
  anchor. Generous sag keeps loads modest; that is part of why slack wire is slack.
- Dynamic swinging adds further load at the bottom of each swing.

## Supports
- **Free-standing frame** (A-frames joined by a ground bar or guyed): common for touring;
  the frame must resist the inward pull and not tip when the performer swings.
- **Fixed anchors:** rated points on structure, approved for the horizontal component.

## Wire or chain
- Steel wire rope with thimbles and rated terminations, or a chain (heavier, swings more
  slowly, different feel). Rated shackles at each end.

## Inspection
- Broken strands, kinks, worn thimbles, corroded links; frame welds and pins.
- Log inspections like any rigging.

## Verify
- [ ] Rigger has the span, sag, performer weight and swing amplitude.
- [ ] Frame or anchors signed off; clearance around the swing marked.
