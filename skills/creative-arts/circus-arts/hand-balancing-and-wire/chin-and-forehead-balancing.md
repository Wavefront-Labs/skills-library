# Chin and forehead balancing

**Goal:** balance a long, light object on the chin or forehead and walk, turn or shift with it.

## Principles
- A long object has a higher centre of mass and falls more slowly, so it is easier to control than a short one. Start with a long, light pole or a feather, and shorten only later.
- The body moves under the top of the object. Never watch the base; look at the top (or the middle, as you develop) and move the contact point toward the direction of the fall.
- Corrections are small and early. Large late corrections cause over-travel.

## Progression
1. Hand balance first: balance the object upright on the palm and fingertip to learn the correction pattern.
2. Move to the chin or forehead with the head tilted back slightly, neck strong but not locked.
3. Add controlled movement: stand still, take a step, turn, kneel and stand.
4. Build to heavier, shorter and more awkward objects (chairs, ladders and swords are advanced skills for qualified teachers only).

## Safety
- Clear a landing zone; use a soft floor for the first attempts.
- Do not use sharp, hard or heavy objects until you can balance a light one reliably; falling objects strike the face and neck.
- Warm up and strengthen the neck gradually; stop at any neck pain or tingling.
- For public performance, risk-assess the object, the audience position and the drop plan.

## Verify
- [ ] 30 seconds still balance with the sight line on the top of the object.
- [ ] Controlled drop to the side without catching the object near your face.
