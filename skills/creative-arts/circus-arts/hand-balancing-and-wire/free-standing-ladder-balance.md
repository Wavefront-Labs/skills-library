# Free-standing ladder balance

This is a height skill with a rigid, rated apparatus. Learn only with a qualified instructor, with a spotting team and a purpose-built ladder.

## Apparatus
- Use a purpose-built or engineer-approved act ladder. Do not use a hardware-store ladder as performance apparatus; the rung joints, base width and weight rating are not designed for the loads or movements.
- Inspect the welds, rung joints, feet and any base plates before every session; remove it from service at any crack, bend or loose joint.
- Record the rated load and keep a maintenance log with dates.

## Floor and set-up
- Hard, level, non-slip surface; soft mats around (not under) the ladder so the feet are on a stable surface; the mat edge must not tilt the ladder.
- Clear overhead (no lights or rigging within reach) and measure the working height.

## Progression
1. Climb with all four spotters touching or ready to hold.
2. Learn the sensation of lean: the ladder is controlled by moving the whole body over the base, not by pulling on the sides.
3. Add height in small stages, always with spotters.
4. Move the spotters out only after the performer is consistent at the previous height.

## Spotting
Spotters take positions on both sides and front and back, hands ready, calling with one agreed voice. The lead spotter calls "up" or "down" and "stop" for the whole team; any member can call "stop".

## Falls
Plan the exit. A tipping ladder is abandoned by stepping or jumping clear, never by clinging to it. Practise the planned fall at the lowest height.

## Verify
- [ ] Engineered ladder, load rating and inspection log on file.
- [ ] Spotting team briefed, with the agreed call words rehearsed.
