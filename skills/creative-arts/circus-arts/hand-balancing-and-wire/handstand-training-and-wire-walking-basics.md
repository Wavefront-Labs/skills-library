# Handstand training and wire walking basics

**Goal:** build a stable freestanding handstand and first steps on the wire and rola
bola, with a bail-out you have practised for each before you need it.

> **Safety.** Wrists, shoulders and falls are the risks. Wire and rola bola work
> starts low (knee height or lower) over mats. Never raise a wire or put a rola bola on
> an elevated surface without a coach and spotters. Pain in the wrist is a stop signal.

## 1. Handstands

### Wrist preparation (every session, 5 minutes)
Wrist circles, palms-down and palms-up rocks on the floor, finger pulses, then weight
shifts in plank. Wrists tolerate load slowly; volume grows over weeks, not days.

### The line
Hands shoulder-width, fingers spread, push the floor away (shoulders by the ears,
"elevated"), ribs in, glutes and legs squeezed, toes pointed. Balance is controlled
mostly through the **fingers and heel of the hand**: falling toward your back, press
fingertips; falling toward your face, press heels of hands.

### Progression
1. **Chest-to-wall** handstand holds (belly faces wall): teaches the straight line.
   Build to 60 s.
2. **Toe pulls** off the wall: find balance for 1–5 s, then return.
3. **Kick-ups** in open space with a spotter at your hips or legs.
4. Freestanding holds: count clean holds, not attempts. 30 s is a common performance-readiness marker.
5. Shapes (straddle, tuck, flag) then **press to handstand** (needs compression and
   shoulder strength; months to years).
6. **Canes** (raised handstand blocks/posts): first low and wide-based, with mats and spotter,
   because a fall from canes is higher and less controlled.

### Bail out: the cartwheel exit
Before you kick up freely, practise the **pirouette out**: walk one hand in a quarter
turn and step down sideways like a cartwheel. Never let yourself collapse onto your back
or into a bridge you can't control.

## 2. Wire walking

### Tightwire vs slackwire
- **Tightwire** is tensioned and rigid; posture is upright, small foot movements.
- **Slackwire** swings; balance comes from moving the wire under you, a different skill.
Start on a low **tightwire** (or a training wire/beam at knee height or lower).

### Technique
- Feet: wire between the big toe and second toe, running along the sole toward the
  heel; soft leather slippers. Stand on one foot and place the other on top in a line.
- Eyes on the end of the wire (a fixed point), not your feet.
- Arms out wide (or a balance pole/fan) to slow rotation; bend the knees slightly.
- Walk with slow, deliberate steps; turn on the balls of the feet.

### Falling off
Step off, don't fall off: when balance is lost, deliberately step down to the side,
landing on both feet, knees soft. Practise stepping off on purpose until it is reflexive.
Never grab the wire as you fall; it is a common way to injure fingers or a groin landing.

### Rigging note
A wire carries large tensions and needs proper anchor points, a tensioner and cavalettis
on longer spans. It is a rigger's job; a home-built wire between two posts is not safe
above floor height. *Specific tension and cable specs vary; I have not verified numbers to give here.*

## 3. Rola bola (board on roller)
- Start with the roller **wedged** or on a carpeted surface, holding a bar or a partner.
- Feet wide, knees soft, hips stay over the cylinder; move the board under you with the legs.
- Bail out: step off the back or front with both feet, kicking the board away from people.
- Add tubes/stacking only after minutes of free balance, and always with spotters.

## Gotchas
- Kicking up too hard is the most common handstand mistake; lift with the hips.
- Banana back (arched line) feels easier but is unstable and stresses the lower back.
- On the wire, looking down is the fastest way off it.
- The rola bola board flies when you fall — clear the area.

## Verify
- [ ] Wrist warm-up done; no wrist pain during or after.
- [ ] Pirouette exit practised before freestanding work.
- [ ] Wire/rola bola work is at knee height or below over mats unless coached and rigged.
- [ ] You can deliberately step off the wire and the board on both feet.
