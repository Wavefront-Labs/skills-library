# One-arm handstand training timeline and plateaus

**Goal:** set honest expectations and a structure that keeps progress going over the
months to years the one-arm handstand takes.

## Realistic timeline
- From a solid 60 s freestanding handstand, many dedicated adults need **one to several
  years** of regular training to hold a reliable one-arm handstand. Circus school
  students training daily under a coach are faster. *Ranges vary widely; treat claims of
  weeks with suspicion.*

## Weekly structure
- 4-6 handstand sessions; OAH work early in each session when fresh.
- Per session: two-arm warm-up line work, straddle and shift drills, fingertip
  progression, attempts, then conditioning (shoulder shrugs, side planks).
- One lighter week every 4-6 weeks.

## Logging
For each session: best hold per side, finger support level, attempts, wrist status.
Progress is visible over months, not days.

## When progress stalls
- **Two-arm line degrading**: go back to straight-line work for a block.
- **Weak side lagging**: start sessions with the weak side, extra attempts.
- **Wrist pain**: reduce volume, use canes or blocks for some sessions.
- **Too few quality attempts**: more rest between attempts beats more attempts.
- **Life stress and sleep**: balance suffers first; adjust expectations.

## Verify
- [ ] Training log kept for at least 8 weeks.
- [ ] Deload week scheduled.
- [ ] Weak side gets equal or more attempts.
