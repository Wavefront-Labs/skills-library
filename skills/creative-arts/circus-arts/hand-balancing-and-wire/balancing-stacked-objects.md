# Balancing stacked objects

Equilibrists build towers of chairs, cylinders, boards or blocks and balance on top. The risk grows rapidly with height.

## Principles
- The combined centre of mass must be above the contact area at every moment. A wider base for the lowest tier gives more tolerance.
- Add each tier only after the previous one is settled. Wait for movement to stop.
- Keep the stack's centre of mass low by using the heaviest elements at the bottom.
- Use purpose-built interlocking chairs or blocks with grooves; do not use furniture that cannot take the point loads.

## Build and learn
1. Start with a two-tier stack at low height beside a wall or with spotters.
2. Climb with three points of contact; keep the stack tied (loosely, for the practice) to a safety line if the height justifies it.
3. Add height in small increments, with a spotter at each side and a call word.
4. Only remove a spotter after repeated clean runs.

## Safety and checks
- Inspect every element; remove any item with a crack or loose joint.
- Mats around the stack, with edges clear of the tower's footprint.
- A risk assessment should set the maximum height and the fall plan; a qualified instructor signs it off.
- Measure the venue ceiling and clear the zone above you.

## Falls
Plan the exit before building: step away from the stack and land flat-footed with knees bent, rolling if needed. Never try to save the stack.

- [ ] Maximum height, spotting plan and exit plan are written down.
- [ ] Each element passes the pre-use inspection.
