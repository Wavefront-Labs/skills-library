# Planning a circus load-in and rigging schedule

**Goal:** a get-in schedule where overhead rigging happens safely first, everything is
tested and rehearsed at height, and the performers are not exhausted before the show.

## Typical order
1. **Arrival and site walk** with the venue technical manager: points, access, power,
   floor loading, emergency routes.
2. **Rigging** (overhead points, bridles, motors) - before lighting and scenery, with an
   **exclusion zone** below. Nobody under overhead work.
3. **Load test and inspection**: each point weighted or test-hung, rigger signs off.
4. **Lighting and sound** focus and check; lighting must not foul aerial paths.
5. **Floor**: mats, stage, ground anchors.
6. **Apparatus hang** and performer checks at height.
7. **Technical rehearsal**: cues with performers on the apparatus.
8. **Dress run** if time.
9. **Break** for performers (at least 1-2 hours before the show).
10. **Warm-up and pre-show checks**.

## Timing estimates
- Simple single-point act in a theatre with house points: 1-2 hours rigging and checks.
- Multi-act show with bridles and motors: a full day or more.
- Always add contingency (around 25%) for venue surprises.

## Coordination
- One person (production or rigging lead) calls the schedule and the exclusion zones.
- Radio or clear verbal calls ("Heads!" for anything falling, "Flying in/out").
- Daily schedule sheet posted at stage door.

## Gotchas
- Lighting crews and riggers wanting the same air space at the same time: schedule it.
- Performer fatigue: avoid rigging work by performers right before a show.

## Verify
- [ ] Schedule sheet with times, owners and exclusion zones.
- [ ] Rigging signed off before any performer goes up.
- [ ] Performer rest window protected.
