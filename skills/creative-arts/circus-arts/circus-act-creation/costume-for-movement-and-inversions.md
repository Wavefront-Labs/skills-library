# Costume for movement and inversions

A costume has to stay in place when the performer is upside down, in a split, or spinning.

## Cut and fabric
- Choose stretch fabric with four-way stretch where the body bends fully.
- Add gussets at the underarm and crotch so that the garment moves with the body.
- Place seams away from points of friction and from the lines of the body that are loaded.

## Coverage
- Think of how the costume looks and behaves upside down and in the splits, from the audience's angles.
- Use opaque fabric or lining where the design needs it.
- Add under-layers (shorts, tights, supportive tops) for coverage and security.

## Staying in place
- Use a close cut and secure straps rather than loose fabric.
- Tape or sew in place where a garment tends to ride up.
- Avoid heavy items at the hem that pull the garment away when inverted.
- Check that waistbands and straps do not dig in when the body hangs.

## Testing
1. Wear the costume and do the act's inversions, splits and spins at low height.
2. Film the test to check from the audience's angle.
3. Test in the heat of a performance: sweat changes the fit.
4. Adjust and repeat.

## Care
- Keep spare costumes and repair kits.
- Wash and inspect costumes after use.

## Verify
- [ ] Costume stays in place through every inversion and split in the act.
- [ ] The test was filmed from the audience's angles and reviewed.
