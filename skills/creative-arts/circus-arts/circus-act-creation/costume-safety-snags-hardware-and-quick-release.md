# Costume safety: snags, hardware and quick release

A costume that catches on equipment can injure the performer and endanger the act. Treat costume design as part of the safety plan, and have the costume reviewed by the act's rigger or safety lead.

## Hazards
- Loose fabric, ribbons, fringe, long cuffs and trailing parts near rope, fabric or moving apparatus.
- Hardware: zips, hooks, buckles, snaps, metal studs.
- Jewellery, rings, necklaces, watches, hair accessories.
- Cords or straps that could tighten around the neck, limbs or fingers.

## Design rules
1. Keep the costume close to the body in areas that touch or pass the apparatus.
2. Place any hardware where it will not contact the apparatus or the performer's skin.
3. Avoid anything around the neck that could tighten.
4. Remove all jewellery.
5. Secure hair away from the apparatus.

## Quick release
- Where an entrapment risk exists, use breakaway or quick-release fastenings and test them.
- Make sure the release can be operated one-handed, and cannot open accidentally under load.
- Agree who releases it and when, as part of the rescue plan.

## Checks
- Before each show: check for loose threads, damaged seams and loose hardware.
- Run the act in the costume at low height first.
- After each show: inspect the costume and the equipment for damage.

## Fire-related costumes
- If flame is used anywhere, take fire-safety advice for fabrics and treatments (see the fire safety skill).

## Verify
- [ ] The costume has been reviewed by the safety lead or rigger.
- [ ] Any quick-release elements are tested and written into the rescue plan.
