# Venue structural sign-off and roof loads

**Goal:** confirm that the building or structure can carry your aerial loads, with a
signed statement from someone competent, before anyone flies.

## Who signs
- **Permanent venues**: the house rigger, using roof load data from the venue's structural
  engineer. Unusual loads go back to the engineer.
- **Non-theatre spaces** (warehouses, churches, gyms): a structural engineer must assess
  the beams or trusses. Never accept "it held a punch bag".
- **Temporary structures** (big top, ground-support truss): the manufacturer's
  calculations plus a competent person's inspection of the build.

## What to send
- Rigging plot with point positions and static + peak loads (see rigging plot skill).
- Load direction (vertical, horizontal components from swinging or bridles).
- Duration (static hang vs dynamic daily use) and number of shows.
- Any other loads you add: lighting, sound, scenery.

## Roof load concepts
- **Point load capacity**: maximum load at a single point on a beam.
- **Total/cumulative roof load**: all points together, often the real limit.
- **Distributed load**: loads per metre on a beam or grid.
- Dynamic aerial loads must be stated as such; engineers will apply their own factors.

## Big top and truss
- Big tops are designed with a rigging ring or king poles rated for specific loads;
  follow the tent maker's load table.
- Ground-support truss: check the truss rating tables (span, load type) and the base
  ballast for lateral loads from swinging acts.
- Wind load on temporary structures changes the available capacity; follow wind
  management plans.

## Verify
- [ ] Written sign-off listing points and loads, with signer's name and competence.
- [ ] Total cumulative load within the venue limit.
- [ ] Temporary structure inspected after build and before first rehearsal.
