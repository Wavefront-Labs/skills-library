# Editing a track to act length

A track edited to the act's structure makes the act feel finished. Edit in any audio editor (for example Audacity, which is free).

## Before editing
- Check you have the right to use and edit the track (see the licensing skill).
- Keep the original lossless file untouched; work on a copy.
- Note the act's sections and their lengths.

## Edit points
1. Mark bar lines using the waveform and by listening.
2. Cut on the beat, preferably at the start of a bar, where the transient of a drum hit helps hide the edit.
3. Remove or repeat whole bars or phrases rather than parts of bars.
4. Crossfade across the edit for a few milliseconds up to a fraction of a second, depending on the material.
5. Listen to each edit several times, and listen to the whole track.

## Endings
- End on a musical resolution, or use a fade out if the act ends with a blackout.
- Build the ending in time with the final trick: add a pause before the final hit if needed.

## Export
- Export a lossless file (WAV) at the sample rate of the source or the venue's usual rate (commonly 44.1 or 48 kHz).
- Also make an MP3 backup, and keep both on separate devices.
- Name files clearly: act name, version and length.

## Check
- Play the edit in the venue sound system if possible.
- Time the act with the final edit and adjust.

## Verify
- [ ] The edit has no audible click or jump.
- [ ] The final length matches the act and backup copies are on two devices.
