# Writing a circus act risk assessment

**Goal:** a short, specific document (risk assessment plus method statement, "RAMS") that
shows a venue you have thought through what can go wrong in your act and what you do
about it.

## Structure
1. **Act description**: apparatus, performers, duration, heights, rigging points,
   any fire, water, animals, audience participation.
2. **Phases**: get-in and rigging, rehearsal, warm-up, performance, strike.
3. **Hazard table** per phase:
   | Hazard | Who | Controls | Residual risk | Responsible |
   e.g. "Fall from height during drops | aerialist | rated point and chain, pre-use
   check, crash mat in rehearsal, act rehearsed at height, abort procedure | medium |
   performer + rigger".
4. **Rescue / emergency plan**: how a stuck or injured performer is brought down,
   equipment, roles (see rescuing-a-stuck-aerialist).
5. **Competence**: training and experience of performers and rigger.
6. **Method statement**: step-by-step rigging and de-rigging sequence, inspection
   points, exclusion zones under rigging work.

## Controls, in order of preference
Eliminate (change the trick), substitute (safer apparatus), engineer (rated hardware,
nets, mats, guards), administrate (checks, exclusion zones, briefings), PPE
(helmets for riggers).

## Rating risk
A simple 5x5 likelihood x severity matrix is common; be honest - aerial falls are
low-likelihood, high-severity. Show how controls reduce likelihood.

## Gotchas
- Generic copy-paste RAs get rejected by experienced venues; be specific to your act.
- Include the audience: front-row proximity, thrown objects, fire.
- Update after any incident or act change; date and version it.

## Verify
- [ ] Every phase has hazards with named controls and responsible people.
- [ ] Rescue plan included.
- [ ] Version and date on the document; signed by the act lead.
