# Ensemble scenes and group choreography

Ensemble scenes use the whole company to establish the show's world, link acts and create spectacle.

## Plan the scene
1. State the purpose: introduction, transition, climax.
2. Choose the structure: a series of formations, a flow across the stage, a canon of movement.
3. Map the floor with a diagram: positions, paths and the apparatus.
4. Pick the music and count it in eights.

## Staging tools
- **Unison:** all do the same, giving power.
- **Canon:** the same movement in sequence, giving a ripple.
- **Contrast:** different groups doing different things.
- **Layering:** adding performers or actions gradually.
- **Levels:** use platforms, apparatus and floor for depth.

## Safety
- Give each performer a clear space and path, and keep clear zones around apparatus that is in use.
- Group acrobatics need spotters, mats and a clear call system; do not rush the build in a crowded space.
- Avoid unrehearsed entrances and exits near moving rigging.

## Rehearsal
- Teach the scene in sections with counts and floor marks.
- Run slowly, then with the music at full speed.
- Rehearse with the costumes, shoes and any apparatus.

## Faults
- Crowded stage with no visual focus.
- Performers who do not know the counts.
- No clear start and end.

## Verify
- [ ] The scene has a floor diagram and count sheet.
- [ ] A full run with costumes and props was done before opening.
