# Transitions and set changes

Transitions keep the show moving while crew and equipment change on stage.

## List each changeover
For each transition: what is struck, what is set, how many people, how long it takes, and what the audience sees.

## Covering the change
- **Ensemble or clown:** a performer or group holds the stage while the change happens behind or beside them.
- **Music and light:** a change of look can focus attention away from the work.
- **Blackout:** quick, but needs glow marks and a rehearsed crew; make sure everyone is clear before aerial apparatus moves.
- **Built-in change:** integrate the change into the show's story (the crew are part of the world).

## Crew safety
- Assign roles and rehearse the change in the dark.
- Mark floor positions with glow tape and keep paths clear.
- Aerial apparatus should only be moved, lowered or attached under the supervision of the rigger, with a clear call system; no one stands under a moving load.
- Check mats and equipment positions before the performer enters.

## Calls
The stage manager calls "standby", "go" and "clear". Everyone in the crew knows the call words and the meaning.

## Rehearsal
- Rehearse the transitions at the same priority as the acts, including the worst-case changeover.
- Time them and note the total.

## Verify
- [ ] Each transition has a written plan and an owner.
- [ ] Rigging changes are signed off by the rigger before they run in a show.
