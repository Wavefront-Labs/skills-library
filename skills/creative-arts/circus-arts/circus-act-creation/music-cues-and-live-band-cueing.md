# Music cues and live band cueing

Clear cues keep the music and the act together.

## The cue sheet
Write a table with:
- Cue number
- Time or trigger in the act (for example "after the first drop")
- What happens (start track, fade, change section)
- Who calls it
- Notes (level, backup plan)
Give a copy to the sound operator and the stage manager.

## Recorded music
1. Mark the track with the cue points: bar numbers or time stamps.
2. The operator follows the cue sheet and watches the act.
3. If a trick is delayed, the operator holds the track, or loops a section if agreed beforehand.
4. Test the cue sheet in technical rehearsal.

## Live musicians
1. Agree a count-in so the band can start together: usually a visual count from the performer or conductor.
2. Give the band a clear visual cue for each section change (a gesture, a pose, a look).
3. Agree a way to hold or extend a section if a trick takes longer.
4. Rehearse with the band at least once with the full act.

## Dropped cues
- Agree a backup plan: a vamp (repeated section) or a silent hold.
- Choose a stop signal.

## Verify
- [ ] The cue sheet has a trigger and a name for the person who calls each cue.
- [ ] The cues have been run in a technical rehearsal.
