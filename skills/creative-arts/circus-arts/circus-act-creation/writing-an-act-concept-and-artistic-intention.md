# Writing an act concept and artistic intention

A strong circus act has a single clear idea behind the technique. Writing the concept down turns a collection of tricks into an act.

## The one-page concept
1. **Central idea:** one sentence. What is the act about? (For example: a performer trying to hold on to something.)
2. **Intention:** what you want the audience to feel at the end.
3. **Apparatus and discipline:** why this apparatus serves the idea (a rope can mean a climb, a lifeline or a trap).
4. **Arc:** how the act starts, changes and ends, in three to five beats.
5. **Constraints:** length, venue, rigging needs, music, cast.

## Using it
- Choose each trick because it serves a beat in the arc; remove tricks that do not.
- Name each section by what is happening, not by trick name.
- Use the concept to say no to ideas that are good in themselves but do not fit.

## Testing the concept
- Show a short run to someone who does not know the plan and ask what they thought the act was about.
- Compare their answer with your sentence; adjust the act or the sentence.

## Revising
- The concept can change in rehearsal. Update the page when it does, with the date.
- Keep the earlier versions so you can see where the act came from.

## Faults
- Concept too large: it cannot be shown in the act's time.
- Concept hidden in programme notes only: it must be visible in the act.
- Technique first, concept added afterwards.

## Verify
- [ ] The concept fits on one page, with the central idea in one sentence.
- [ ] A fresh viewer's description of the act is close to the intention.
