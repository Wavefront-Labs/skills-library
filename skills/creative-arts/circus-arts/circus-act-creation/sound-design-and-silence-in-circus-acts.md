# Sound design and silence in circus acts

Sound is more than music. Effects, silence and the performer's own sounds shape how the audience feels.

## Elements
- **Music:** gives pace and mood.
- **Effects:** add detail (a heartbeat, a creak, a drop).
- **Apparatus sounds:** rope creaks, hand slaps, the clatter of props.
- **Voice and breath:** live breath or voice gives intimacy.
- **Silence:** a held silence puts the audience's attention on the performer.

## Using silence
1. Choose the moments where silence adds meaning: before a big trick, after a fall, at the entrance.
2. Plan the transition: music fades or stops cleanly, not abruptly (unless the abruptness is the point).
3. Keep the silence long enough to be felt, and rehearse it.
4. Make sure the operator and the performer agree who ends the silence.

## Layering
- Build the sound from a small number of elements and add more rarely.
- Check that effects do not mask the performer's cues (a spotter's call, a count).
- Keep levels balanced so that the effects are heard but do not hide the music.

## Safety
- Do not let loud sound hide safety calls.
- Make sure the performers can hear the cue for the act in the venue's conditions.

## Venue testing
- Test levels in the venue with an audience-sized space if possible: empty rooms sound different.

## Verify
- [ ] A written plan of sound elements, including intentional silences.
- [ ] Safety calls are audible over the sound design.
