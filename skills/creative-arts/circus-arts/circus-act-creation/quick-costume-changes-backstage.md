# Quick costume changes backstage

A quick change is a rehearsed sequence with a time budget. Safety and clarity matter as much as speed.

## Planning
1. Write the change down: from what to what, in how many seconds, in which place.
2. Decide who helps (a dresser) and where each item is placed.
3. Design the costume for the change: layers worn underneath, tear-away pieces, pre-threaded fastenings.

## Fastenings
- Use hook-and-loop, snaps, magnets or zips with a long pull; avoid small buttons and ties.
- Do not mix too many different fastenings in one costume.
- Do not use fastenings that could open on the stage, and check quick-release rules with the safety lead.

## The changing area
- Light the area enough to see; mark the floor with glow tape for the route.
- Keep the area clear of cables and equipment; fix a mirror and a chair.
- Lay items out in the order of use on a table or hook.

## Rehearsal
- Practise the change repeatedly with a stopwatch, then in the dark.
- Rehearse with the dresser and the music.
- Rehearse what to do if the change fails: skip the item, add a cover, or hold the cue.

## Safety
- Never rush onto apparatus with an unfastened costume, loose hair or missing safety items.
- The stage manager is told if the change is not complete.

## Verify
- [ ] The change is timed three times in a row within the budget.
- [ ] A fallback for a failed change is written down and agreed.
