# Costume fabric grip and friction for apparatus

The costume sits between the performer and the apparatus at every contact point. It affects grip, comfort and the wear on the equipment.

## Grip
- Bare skin often grips better than fabric on rope, silks and hoops; many aerialists leave skin exposed at the contact points (for example the legs on silks, the hands on a hoop).
- Slippery fabrics (satin, some sequins, glossy lycra) can reduce grip and increase the risk of a slip. Test fabrics on the real apparatus before the show.
- Grippy or textured fabrics increase friction and can cause friction burns on the skin.

## Equipment wear
- Beading, sequins, rhinestones and metal hardware can abrade and cut soft goods such as silks and rope.
- Check the inside of the costume as well as the outside: seams, hooks and boning can snag.

## Skin comfort
- Place seams away from contact areas.
- Choose fabrics that stretch with the body and do not bunch.
- Use skin protection or tape in high friction zones where appropriate.

## Testing
1. Wear the costume in a full run-through on the real apparatus.
2. Check after the run for new scuffs, snags or loose pieces on the costume and the equipment.
3. Test with sweat: a damp costume grips differently.
4. Test the costume at the height at which the act will be performed, with spotters and mats as usual.

## Record
Keep a note of the fabrics and the apparatus for each act, with a photograph of the costume.

## Verify
- [ ] The costume has been run in a full rehearsal on the real apparatus.
- [ ] No decoration touches the soft goods or the contact surface.
