# Music licensing for circus performance

This is a practical overview, not legal advice. Rules differ by country; check with the venue, the producer or a lawyer.

## Different rights
- **Performing rights:** the right to perform or play a work in public. In many countries these are administered by performing rights organisations (for example ASCAP and BMI in the US, PRS for Music in the UK). Venues and festivals commonly hold blanket licences from these bodies, but check this for each venue.
- **Mechanical/reproduction rights:** relate to copying a recording.
- **Synchronisation rights:** needed when music is combined with moving images, such as a video of your act posted online.
- **Master rights:** the rights to the particular recording, often held by a record label.

## Practical steps
1. Ask the venue or festival whether it holds a performing rights licence that covers your act.
2. If you are producing the show yourself, find out which licences you need and obtain them.
3. For video of the act, get a sync licence or use music you have the rights to.
4. Keep copies of all licences and permission emails.

## Alternatives
- Commission original music and agree the rights in writing.
- Use royalty-free or Creative Commons music, checking the licence terms for performance and video.
- Work with a composer or musician who owns the rights.

## Common mistakes
- Assuming a purchased track includes performance and video rights.
- Posting a performance video with unlicensed music.
- Not keeping records.

## Verify
- [ ] The venue's licence situation is confirmed in writing.
- [ ] Rights for any video use are cleared separately.
