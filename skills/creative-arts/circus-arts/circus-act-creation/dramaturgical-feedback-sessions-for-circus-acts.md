# Dramaturgical feedback sessions

Feedback on a work in progress is most useful when the structure of the session protects the artist and the questions are specific.

## Preparing the showing
1. Decide what stage the act is at (idea, rough, nearly done) and tell the viewers.
2. Choose two or three viewers who understand the form; include one who does not know the act.
3. State what you want feedback on (the opening, the music, the transitions).
4. Make sure the space is safe for the showing: rig, mats and spotters as in a rehearsal.

## A structured format
Liz Lerman's Critical Response Process has four steps, led by a facilitator:
1. **Statements of meaning:** viewers say what was meaningful or interesting to them.
2. **Artist as questioner:** the artist asks questions about the work.
3. **Neutral questions:** viewers ask neutral questions about the work, with no opinion hidden inside.
4. **Opinion time:** viewers may offer opinions, with the artist's permission.

## Recording
- A scribe records the notes in a shared document or on paper.
- The artist reads them after a day, and chooses what to act on.

## Using the notes
- Group the notes into themes.
- Test one or two changes at the next rehearsal.
- Do not change everything at once; check each change against the concept.

## Faults
- Too many viewers, or viewers who do not have a stake.
- Feedback about taste instead of effect.
- The artist defending instead of listening.

## Verify
- [ ] The session has a facilitator, a scribe and a time limit.
- [ ] Notes are grouped and one or two changes are tested.
