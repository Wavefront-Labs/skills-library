# Bows and presentation moments

Presentation moments tell the audience when to applaud and how the act ends. In circus a held finishing pose is often called the ta-da.

## Finishing the trick
- End the trick with a clear, held pose; hold it long enough for the audience to see it.
- Do not drop out of the pose before the applause begins.
- If the finish is dangerous, plan a safe stop and a clean exit from the pose.

## Acknowledging the audience
- Look at the audience, breathe and let the response arrive.
- Use a simple gesture to include them: an open arm, a bow, a nod.
- Decide in advance how many bows you take and how you will exit.

## Presentation moments in the act
- Mark the high points of the act with brief holds or gestures so that applause can happen.
- Do not let presentation interrupt the flow in a way that costs safety, such as holding a fatiguing position longer than is safe.

## Technical support
- Agree the light and music cues for the bow: for example, music hits the final note, light holds, then fades.
- Rehearse the bow as part of the act; time it.
- Plan how you reach the ground and the exit route from the apparatus after the final trick.

## Faults
- Skipping the bow because of nerves.
- A bow that is too long or too short for the applause.
- A bow that breaks the character without a reason.

## Verify
- [ ] The finishing pose, bow and exit are rehearsed with lighting and music.
- [ ] The performer knows how many bows are planned and the exit route.
