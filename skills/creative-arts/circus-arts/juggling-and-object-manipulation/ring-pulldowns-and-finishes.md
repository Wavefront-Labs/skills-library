# Ring pulldowns and finishes

A strong finish makes the end of a ring routine read as deliberate. Terms vary among jugglers: a "pulldown" generally means bringing the pattern to a close by catching rings down into a stack or onto the arm. Confirm the exact meaning with the juggler or source you learn from.

## Principles
- Plan the finish before the pattern. Know which ring ends in which hand and in what order.
- Rehearse the final three throws at a slower tempo than the main pattern.
- Count the throws backward from the finish so that the ending is automatic.

## Finish types
1. **Stack in the hand:** each ring is caught onto the hand over the previous one.
2. **Stack on the arm:** catch rings on the forearm at the end of the pattern.
3. **Throw-up finish:** the last throws go to a clean position and the hands open.

## Practice
1. Start with three rings and a simple stack on one hand.
2. Add the other hand; then use the finish at the end of a long run.
3. Repeat 20 clean finishes, in both directions.

## Faults
- Finishing at the end of fatigue: the throws are rushed; finish at the first sign of drift.
- Rings bouncing off the arm: practise on a soft, steady arm position.

## Verify
- [ ] 10 clean finishes in a row.
- [ ] The finish is rehearsed with the routine music or count.
