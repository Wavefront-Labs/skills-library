# Arm-crossing three-ball patterns

Arm-crossing patterns are three-ball patterns in which the hands exchange sides, so the arms overlap at the wrists. The best-known example is the Mills Mess (attributed to the juggler Steve Mills), a hand-crossing pattern that looks like a woven tangle of arms.

## Prerequisites
- Solid cascade and reverse cascade.
- Ability to throw to a consistent height and keep the pattern in one plane.

## Crossed-arm cascade (starter)
1. In the cascade, cross the arms at the forearms as you throw so that the right hand throws from the left side and the left hand throws from the right, keeping the throws going to the other hand.
2. Keep the elbows loose and the throws wide enough that the catches are not blocked by the forearm.
3. Throw slightly higher than a normal cascade; the extra airtime absorbs the crossing.
4. Uncross, repeat, then cross in both directions (right over left, left over right).

## Mills Mess (general approach)
Learn each hand's job in the pattern separately: one hand makes the crossing, the other hand makes the plain throw and catches. Break the pattern into the three-throw cycle and learn it as a repeating loop. Use a slow tempo and a high throw height first.

## Burke's barrage
Burke's barrage is a named crossing pattern in the same family. I am not certain of its exact sequence, so confirm it in a reliable reference such as the Library of Juggling (https://libraryofjuggling.com) before teaching it.

## Faults
- Elbows pinned: the forearms cannot pass; keep them relaxed.
- Throwing too low: the crossing needs airtime.
- Shoulders twisting: keep the torso still.

## Verify
- [ ] 20 catches of a crossed-arm cascade.
- [ ] A slow, clean full cycle of the crossing pattern in both directions.
