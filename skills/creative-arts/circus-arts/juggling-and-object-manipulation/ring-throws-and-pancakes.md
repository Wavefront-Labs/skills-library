# Ring throws and pancakes

Juggling rings are flat, thin hoops. They fly most stably when thrown with spin, like a flying disc.

## The pancake throw
A pancake is a ring thrown flat (parallel to the floor), spinning about its vertical axis. In the standard ring cascade, each ring travels from hand to hand in this flat orientation, so that the receiving hand can meet it as it comes down.

## Holding and throwing
1. Hold the ring on its edge between the thumb and the fingers (grip details vary between jugglers; find the one that gives a clean flat spin).
2. Throw with a small upward arm movement plus a snap of the wrist so that the ring spins flat.
3. Aim the throw to peak at about eye height, at the side of the head opposite the throwing hand.

## Catching
1. Receive each ring with the hand open and the palm facing it.
2. Let the ring land flat in the hand; cushion by letting the hand move down a little.
3. Keep the thumb out of the ring's path.

## Cascade with three rings
- Learn the one-ring toss first, then two rings (one in each hand), then three.
- Keep the throws consistent in height and flatness; a tilted ring will fly off course.

## Faults
- Not enough spin: the ring wobbles; add wrist snap.
- Throwing too far from the body: the pattern widens; keep it near.
- Catching on the edge: use the open palm, not a pinch.

## Verify
- [ ] 20 consecutive catches of a flat ring from hand to hand.
- [ ] Ten rounds of the three-ring cascade.
