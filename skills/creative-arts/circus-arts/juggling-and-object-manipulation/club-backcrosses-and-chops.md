# Club backcrosses and chops

## Backcross
A backcross is a club throw made from behind the back so that the club travels across behind you to the other hand's catch position, and is caught in front.
1. With one club, learn the throw in each hand: swing the club behind your back with a loose elbow, release it upwards, and catch it with the other hand.
2. Throw higher than a cascade throw; the throw needs the extra airtime.
3. Slot the trick into three-club juggling on one throw, then repeat it on a regular beat.
4. Watch the spin: it must be consistent so the handle arrives in the catching hand.

## Chop
A chop is a club throw made from a high, overhand position with a downward chopping motion so that the club continues into an upward arc. The exact definition varies between jugglers, so work from a reliable video example and your coach; check the Library of Juggling (https://libraryofjuggling.com) for descriptions.
1. Learn it with one club first, using a soft club.
2. Add it to a cascade as one throw in the cycle.

## Common faults
- Late release: the club continues behind you; time the release earlier.
- Rigid shoulder: keep the arm loose and the wrist crisp.
- Looking at the trick instead of the pattern; keep your eyes on the main arc.

## Safety
Use a light, forgiving club for first attempts, leave space behind you, and warm up the shoulder and wrist.

## Verify
- [ ] 10 backcrosses in a row with one club.
- [ ] 5 slotted backcrosses in a three-club cascade.
