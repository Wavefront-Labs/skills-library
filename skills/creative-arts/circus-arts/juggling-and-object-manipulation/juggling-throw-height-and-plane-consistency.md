# Juggling throw height and plane consistency

**Goal:** every throw the same height and in the same plane, which is what makes higher
numbers possible.

## Why it matters
In numbers juggling the time each ball spends in the air is fixed by height
(flight time is proportional to the square root of height). Inconsistent heights break
the rhythm; drift forward or back makes you chase.

## Drills
- **Wall drill**: stand close to a wall facing it. Balls that drift forward hit the wall.
- **Target height**: a mark on the wall or a line of tape at peak height; peaks should
  touch it.
- **Single-ball and two-ball accuracy**: throw one ball from hand to hand 20 times without
  moving the catching hand.
- **Eyes on peaks**: look at the top of the pattern, not the hands.

## Release and hands
- Throw from a consistent point (hands roughly at waist height, elbows close to sides).
- Throws come from a scoop from inside to out (cascade), released at the same point.
- Catch on the outside and carry inwards.
- Relaxed shoulders; tension shrinks and shortens throws.

## Diagnosing drift
- Forward drift: release too late, or leaning. Wall drill.
- Sideways widening: throws too far across; narrow the pattern with a target.
- One side higher: weak-hand throws overpowered; drill weak hand alone.

- [ ] 20 throws hitting the target height mark in a wall drill.
