# Under-the-leg and behind-the-back throws

These are body throws inside the three-ball cascade: one throw per cycle goes under a raised leg, or behind the back, before landing in the other hand.

## Under the leg
1. With one ball, lift the leg on the same side as the throwing hand and throw the ball from under the thigh, aiming at the other hand's usual catch position.
2. Learn the ball's height: slightly higher than a cascade throw, so the leg has time to come down.
3. With two balls, replace one throw per cycle with the under-the-leg throw; the other hand keeps its normal throws.
4. In a three-ball cascade, add the trick on one throw, count the cycle, then repeat on a regular beat (every third throw, then every second, then each throw).

## Behind the back
1. With one ball, throw from the hand behind the back so that the ball arcs over the shoulder on the opposite side into the other hand, or round the body at waist height. Use the style you can control.
2. Keep the elbow loose and the wrist flicking forward; the throw feels like a push from behind.
3. As with under-the-leg, slot the trick into one cascade throw and keep the rhythm.

## Faults
- Throw too low: the trick takes time; add height.
- Looking at the body: keep eyes on the top of the pattern, trust the hands.
- Losing the cycle: return to the cascade rhythm between tricks.

## Safety
Clear the floor so you do not trip on a dropped ball; do not combine body throws with other props or elevated surfaces until the base trick is reliable.

## Verify
- [ ] 10 consecutive trick throws inside a three-ball cascade.
- [ ] You can stop on the trick and return to a plain cascade without breaking rhythm.
