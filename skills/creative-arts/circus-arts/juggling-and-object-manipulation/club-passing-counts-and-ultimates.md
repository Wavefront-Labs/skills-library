# Club passing counts and ultimates

Two jugglers each hold three clubs and throw some of their throws to each other. The *count* names how often the passes occur in the repeating cycle of throws. A smaller count means more passes per cycle; patterns with more passes are faster and need tighter timing.

I am giving the working method, not a throw-by-throw definition of each count, because the lists differ between sources. Get the exact throw sequences for 4-count, 6-count and the ultimates from a reliable reference (Library of Juggling, https://libraryofjuggling.com) and confirm them with your partner before you start.

## Before passing
- Each juggler has a solid three-club cascade (self throws) with consistent height.
- Both can throw a single-spin club to a target at partner distance (about 2 to 3 metres apart).

## Learn one count at a time
1. Stand facing, apart enough that a thrown club needs about one comfortable single spin to arrive.
2. Agree who passes first and which hand. A common convention is that the passes go hand-to-hand across the gap, aimed at where the receiver's hand would normally catch; whatever you use, say it aloud.
3. Start with one club each: walk through the passes slowly without juggling.
4. Add a second club each (the self throw and the pass).
5. Add the third club and the count's full cycle.

## Calls
Use the same call words each time: "ready" (both set), "go", "stop" or "hold" (any juggler can call it). The caller counts the first cycle aloud until both have the pulse.

## Common faults
- Pass throws are not the same height as self throws; keep them at the same top height.
- Pass thrown at the partner, not at the target hand.
- Losing the count: say it aloud, "pass, self, self..." (whatever the count), until it is automatic.

## Verify
- [ ] 30 seconds of a basic count without drops, and both jugglers can call a stop at any time.
- [ ] You can name the count by the position of the pass in the cycle.
