# Staff contact and rotations

A spinning staff (or baton) is manipulated by hand, by contact with the body, or with the fingers.

## Choosing a staff
- Length: commonly roughly from the floor to shoulder or chin height; try a few before you decide.
- Weight and balance: a staff that balances at the centre, with a firm grip surface.
- Practise props: padded or lighter staves are best at the start.

## Basic rotations
1. Hold the staff near the centre; spin it in a figure-eight in front of the body.
2. Change hands by passing the staff through the palm as it rotates.
3. Keep the wrists loose; the spin comes from the fingers, wrists and forearm rather than the shoulder.

## Contact
1. Roll the staff along the forearm: keep the staff horizontal and make a wave.
2. Roll it over the shoulders and behind the neck: keep the contact smooth, not pulled.
3. Learn to catch and re-grip: always know where the next contact is.
4. Spin in the planes (wall, wheel, floor) first so that contact moves have a rotation to attach to.

## Progression
1. Basic rotations in each hand.
2. Pairs of moves (rotate, roll, rotate).
3. Strings of three or four moves with a set rhythm.

## Safety
- Clear ceiling height and a safe radius around the performer.
- Keep the staff low near other people.
- Avoid fast neck wraps until the staff is under complete control.

## Verify
- [ ] Figure-eight rotations with each hand and a hand change, 20 turns without a drop.
- [ ] A shoulder roll sequence repeated 5 times.
