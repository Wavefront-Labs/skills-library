# Ring turnovers and colour changes

Rings are as much a visual prop as a technical one. A turnover turns the ring from its flat plane into a vertical plane (or the other way round) during the throw. Colour changes use the ring order, so that colours repeat or alternate in a deliberate sequence.

## Turnovers
1. Throw a ring flat, then add a small tilt with the wrist so the ring turns toward a vertical position at the top of its arc.
2. Catch the ring while it is vertical, or let it return flat.
3. Practise the one-ring turnover, then slot it into a cascade on a single throw.

## Colours
- Decide a colour order before the pattern (for example, red, blue, yellow) and keep the throw order the same.
- A clean cascade repeats the same colour order automatically; the visual effect appears from the repeat.
- Use rings with consistent weight and thickness, and check that colours are matched.

## Stagecraft
- Lighting: reflective or bright rings read best against a dark background.
- Rehearse the effect without looking at it: look at the apex.

## Faults
- Over-tilting: the ring loses its spin and wobbles.
- Different ring weights: pattern becomes uneven; use a matched set.

## Verify
- [ ] Single-ring turnover 10 times in a row.
- [ ] Colour order is repeatable across two or three runs.
