# Choreographing juggling to music

Juggling is a rhythmic activity. Musical alignment makes a routine read as a performance rather than a run of tricks.

## Choose the music
- Pick a track whose tempo matches the speed of your pattern. Many juggling patterns suit a steady beat; a track in the range of roughly 100 to 130 beats per minute is workable for three-ball patterns, but this depends on your pattern and style.
- Prefer clear phrasing (eight-bar or four-bar sections) so you can map tricks to phrases.

## Map the track
1. Count the track in eights and write the structure: intro, verse, build, chorus, break, ending.
2. Mark accents (drum hits, changes) where the audience will feel them.
3. Place your biggest tricks on strong accents and the plain pattern in quieter parts.

## Throw timing
- Align throws or catches with the beat or the half-beat. Pick one and keep it consistent.
- Use siteswap pulse: each beat of the notation can equal a beat of the track.

## Rehearsal
- Learn each section against a click or a metronome first.
- Record the run and check the timing against the track.
- Practise the ending until the last catch lands on the final beat.

## Faults
- A drop breaks the sync: plan a recovery that rejoins at a phrase boundary.
- The tempo drifts as you tire: use the track to correct, and cut a trick if you cannot hold it.

## Verify
- [ ] The sections are written down by bar number.
- [ ] The ending lands on the final beat 5 times in a row.
