# Unicycle free mount and first riding

**Goal:** get from standing beside the unicycle to riding forward without holding a wall, and learn to do it repeatably.

## Set-up before you start
- **Seat height:** sit on the seat with the pedal at its lowest point; the leg should be almost straight with a slight bend at the knee. Too low is tiring and unstable; too high makes the pedals impossible to reach. Raise it gradually as you improve.
- Equal, firm tyre pressure; check the cranks, pedals and wheel nuts are tight before every session.
- Protective kit for learners: helmet, wrist guards, knee and elbow pads, flat closed shoes. Learn on a smooth indoor floor (sports hall) with a rail or wall to hold.

## Learning to ride (supported)
1. Hold a rail or wall, sit, and pedal slowly along it. Weight on the seat, not on the hands.
2. Sit upright, eyes up and forward (not at the wheel), shoulders relaxed, arms out to the sides for balance.
3. Pedal in a smooth, steady rhythm. Balance forward and back by speeding up or slowing the pedals; the wheel must stay under the body. Falling forward is corrected by pedalling faster, backward by easing off.
4. Reduce the support to fingertips, then to a few unsupported metres, then to long unsupported lines.

## Free mount (the standard static mount)
1. Stand behind or beside the wheel with the unicycle in front of you, hold the front of the seat (the handle or the front edge) with one hand.
2. Set one pedal in the rear-lower position (roughly the four o'clock position) and put your stronger foot on it.
3. Press down on that pedal and step up and over with the other foot onto the other pedal in one movement, keeping pressure forward so the wheel rolls under you rather than away.
4. Let go of the seat as you gain balance and begin pedalling immediately.
The rolling mount (stepping onto a moving wheel) comes afterwards.

## Common faults
- Hands gripping the seat tightly: the unicycle cannot be steered by the body.
- Looking at the wheel: look at a fixed point ahead.
- Stiff upper body: rider and wheel need to move independently; keep the hips loose.
- Starting too slowly: the first half revolution must be committed.

## Verify
- [ ] You can mount without support three times in a row and ride 20 m in a straight line.
- [ ] You can dismount in a controlled way by stepping forward off the pedal.
Learning time varies a great deal between people; do not promise a fixed number of hours. Learn with a coach or an experienced rider where possible.
