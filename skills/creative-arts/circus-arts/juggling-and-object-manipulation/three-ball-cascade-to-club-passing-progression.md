# From the three-ball cascade to club passing

**Goal:** build toss juggling in a sequence that removes the usual plateaus, then take
it to clubs and partner passing, and understand enough siteswap to learn patterns from
notation.

## 1. The cascade, step by step
1. **One ball:** throw hand to hand in an arc peaking just above eye level, crossing
   in front of the face. Elbows by your sides, hands at waist height, scooping
   throws from the inside. The arc is the whole skill; make it identical both ways.
2. **Two balls ("exchange"):** throw A from right; as A peaks, throw B from left
   *underneath* A's path; catch both. Then start from the left. Stop. Don't chain yet.
3. **Three balls:** two in the right, one in the left. Throw right, at the peak throw
   left, at the peak throw right… Count catches: a **flash** (3 throws, 3 catches) is
   the first milestone, then 6, 10, 20, then continuous.
4. Practise facing a wall or over a bed so drops don't roll; balls drift forward,
   so the wall teaches a flat plane.

**Gotchas:** throwing *at* the other hand instead of up (collapses height); rushing
throws (each throw waits for the peak); walking forward to chase the pattern.

## 2. Reading siteswap (vanilla)
Each number is how many beats until that object is thrown again; even numbers return to the
same hand, odd numbers cross. `3` = cascade, `4` (with 4 balls) = fountain,
`441` and `531` are three-ball tricks: the average of the digits equals the number of
objects (4+4+1 = 9, /3 = 3). Juggling apps/simulators animate any valid siteswap —
use one to see a pattern before trying it.

## 3. Clubs
- Hold near the knob end, thumb along the handle; the club is thrown with a slight
  wrist lift so it makes **one turn (single spin)** and comes back handle-first.
- Drill one club single spins hand to hand until every catch lands at the handle, then
  two-club exchanges, then three-club cascade. Doubles (two turns) come later.
- Stand on a carpet/grass: clubs bounce off hard floors and get damaged.

## 4. Passing (two jugglers, six clubs)
- Partners face each other ~2–3 m apart, juggling in time ("in sync": same hand throws
  at the same moment). Count "1-2-3-pass" out loud.
- **6-count** (pass every third right-hand throw) › **4-count** (every other right)
  › **every others** (every right-hand throw is a pass) › **ultimates** (every throw from both hands is a pass). Passes are thrown from
  the right hand to the partner's left, a straight, slightly higher **single** or
  **double** spin, aimed at their left shoulder.
- Start with the call "ready, hup!" and a shared start count; stop together on a call.
- *Terminology varies between countries and festivals; check with your passing partner.*

## 5. Other manipulation families (one line each)
- **Contact juggling:** acrylic ball rolled over hands/arms; slow, fluid, isolation-based.
- **Diabolo:** spun on a string between two sticks; accelerate with the dominant
  hand, correct tilt by moving the sticks forward/back.
- **Poi:** weights on cords, planes and timing (same/split, together/opposite).

## Practice structure
- 20–30 min focused sessions beat long tired ones; drops climb with fatigue.
- Log your **catch counts**; "qualify" a trick at a set number of catches (e.g. 3× the
  number of objects for a flash, 50+ for performance readiness — choose your own bar).
- Learn every trick on the weaker side too.

## Stage gotchas
- Lighting: overhead/backlight can blind you at the peak; rehearse under show light.
- Low ceilings and rigging bars cap throw height. Measure before the show.
- Plan a recovery: a drop is part of the act if you pick up with style; keep a spare
  object in reach.

## Verify
- [ ] One-ball arcs peak at the same height on both sides.
- [ ] You can flash 3 balls from either hand, then run 20+ catches.
- [ ] You can explain why 441 is valid and 432 isn't (432 makes 2 balls land on the same beat).
- [ ] Club singles come back handle-first both sides before cascading.
- [ ] You and a partner can hold a 6-count, then a 4-count, 20 passes each.
