# Shower and half shower

## Shower
The three-ball shower is a circular pattern: one hand throws high arcs to the other, the other hand makes very fast, low throws back. In vanilla siteswap it is 51 (a 5 throw and a 1 throw alternating), three balls. The high hand throws about the height of a five-ball throw, so the other hand has time to pass its ball over.

## Learning it
1. Practise the cascade first and make sure you can throw consistent heights.
2. One hand becomes the thrower of the high arcs; the other receives and passes each ball quickly to the thrower with a short, flat push.
3. Throw the first ball as a cascade throw, the second as a quick pass, and learn to count: high, quick, high, quick.
4. Keep the high throws narrow and at one height; use a mirror or video to check.
5. Reverse the shower direction once the first one is stable.

## Common faults
- The quick throws are too high, making a collision: keep them flat, short and quick.
- The high throws drift forward; stand next to a wall or use a taped line on the floor.
- Speed rises as errors accumulate: take a breath, slow the quick passes.

## Half shower
The half shower is the related shower-like pattern in which the hands move in a circle with one hand's throws lower and flatter than the other's. Treat it as its own pattern. I am not certain of its exact siteswap representation, so check the pattern's entry at the Library of Juggling (https://libraryofjuggling.com) before teaching it.

## Verify
- [ ] 30 consecutive throws of the shower in each direction.
- [ ] You can stop on a catch in either hand without a drop.
