# Contact juggling isolation

Isolation is the illusion that the ball is hovering in a fixed place while the body moves around it. It is the signature effect of contact juggling.

## Principle
The ball does not move; the contact points do. The performer shifts the supporting surface (the hand, then the fingers, then the other arm) so that the ball stays at the same point in space. The movement should be continuous, with no sharp changes of speed.

## Learning
1. Choose a single point in front of you and hold the ball there with the fingertips.
2. Move the hand slowly around the ball while the ball remains in place, tilting the wrist and shifting weight.
3. Practise with the ball held between the two hands; shift the hands around the ball as the body moves.
4. Add walking: the ball stays at the same height while the body moves forward.
5. Use a mirror or filming to check that the ball does not move in the frame.

## Tips
- Hands relaxed; keep the ball near the centre line of the body.
- Eyes on the ball, never on the hand.
- Fingers close together: gaps let the ball slip.

## Faults
- Hurrying: isolation is slow; the speed is the effect.
- Visible bounce at hand changes: the transfer point must be smooth.
