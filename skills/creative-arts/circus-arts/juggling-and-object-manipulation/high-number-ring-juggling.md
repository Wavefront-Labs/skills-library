# High-number ring juggling

Rings are flat and low in drag when thrown flat, so they pack tightly in the air; many number jugglers favour them for five, six and seven objects.

## Prerequisites
- A reliable three-ring cascade, then four rings in a fountain, with consistent throw height and flat spin.
- General fitness and shoulder warm-up for the repeated high throws.

## Progression
1. Learn the five-ring cascade as five throws in a row (a flash), then five catches after a fixed number of throws.
2. Repeat flashes until you can repeat them reliably, then extend to a qualifying run.
3. Build to six rings (fountain), then seven.
4. Keep detailed notes: object count, number of catches, date.

## Technique notes
- Throws are higher and slightly narrower than in a three-ring cascade; rings should land into the cup of the hand.
- Keep the thumbs out of the path and the elbows close to the sides to keep the pattern stable.
- Throw heights are consistent: peak each ring at the same level; use an overhead marker.

## Training
- Train in short bursts to avoid shoulder strain; rest days matter.
- Use shared terms: a flash is one throw of each object with all caught; a qualifying run is a longer, agreed number of catches. Fix the criterion before you start.

## Verify
- [ ] Log of attempts, flashes and runs with dates.
- [ ] Shoulder warm-up complete before each session.
