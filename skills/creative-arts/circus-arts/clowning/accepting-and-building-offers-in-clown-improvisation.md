# Accepting and building offers in clown improvisation

**Goal:** see every event on stage - a partner's action, an audience sound, a prop
falling - as an offer, accept it, and build on it instead of shutting it down.

## What counts as an offer
- Anything a partner does or says.
- Audience reactions: a laugh, a cough, a child shouting.
- Accidents: a dropped prop, a squeaky chair, a light cue arriving early.
- Your own impulses and emotions.

## Accept and build ("yes, and")
- **Accept**: acknowledge it openly, usually with a look to the audience.
- **Build**: add one step. The chair squeaks -> clown looks at it -> sits again to make
  it squeak -> conducts the squeak like an orchestra.
- Keep each step small; big leaps lose the audience.

## Blocking (what kills scenes)
- Ignoring an offer the audience saw.
- Contradicting a partner ("That's not a dog, it's a hat").
- Hurrying back to the planned material.

## Exercises
- **Yes, and** story in pairs: each sentence starts "Yes, and...".
- **Object offers**: a random object is placed on stage; clown must find three uses,
  each building on the last.
- **Interruptions**: coach throws in sounds while a clown performs a simple task.

## Gotchas
- Accepting does not mean doing everything: choose offers that serve the clown's want.
- Over-acknowledging (milking) every tiny event slows the show.

## Verify
- [ ] In a 3-minute improv, the clown visibly picks up and builds at least two
  unplanned offers.
- [ ] No blocks noted by the observer.
