# The open look and sharing with the audience

Complicity is the bond between clown and audience. It is built most directly by the open look: the clown looks out, lets the audience see their face, and shares what has just happened.

## What the open look is
- The clown stops, turns the face to the audience and lets the feeling show.
- The look takes time. It is a moment in which the audience can answer.
- It is not a wink or a performance of a face; it is a real response to what has happened.

## When to use it
- After a discovery, a failure or a success.
- After a partner does something surprising.
- Before a decision: the audience seems to say what to do.

## How to do it
1. Finish the action before you look out.
2. Look at one person, then another, then the whole room.
3. Hold until a reaction arrives (laughter, a sound, a smile) and let it land.
4. Return to the action only when the moment has finished.

## Exercises
- Stand in front of a group and do nothing but look at them until you feel the connection.
- Do a simple action (drop a hat), then use the look to share what happened.
- Work in pairs: one clown acts, the other sits as audience and says when they feel included.

## Faults
- Looking too early: the audience has not seen the event yet.
- Looking for the laugh: this reads as pleading.
- Looking at the floor, the ceiling or the back of the room.

## Verify
- [ ] An observer can say when the clown shared and what they shared.
- [ ] The look lasts long enough for a reaction.
