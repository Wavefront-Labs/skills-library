# Clown walk and physical silhouette

How a clown walks tells the audience who they are before they speak.

## Centre of gravity
- Explore different centres: the head leading, the chest, the belly, the pelvis, the knees, the feet.
- Try walking with the centre in each place and observe the character that emerges.

## Rhythm and pace
- Find a pace: slow, brisk, hesitant, bouncy.
- Add a rhythm: steady, syncopated, or stuttering.
- A strong clown walk has a recognisable pattern that the audience can read.

## Posture and shape
- Notice the shape of the silhouette: wide, narrow, tall, low.
- Costume amplifies silhouette: shoes, hat and padding change how the walk reads.
- Keep the shape comfortable: avoid positions that cause pain or strain.

## Exercises
1. Walk across the room with each centre of gravity.
2. Repeat the walk with a different pace.
3. Add a prop (a bag, a hat) and see how the silhouette changes.
4. Film and watch only the shadow: is the silhouette interesting and readable?

## Use in performance
- Use the walk at every entrance.
- Use a change of walk to show a change of feeling.

## Verify
- [ ] A named centre of gravity, pace and rhythm for the character.
- [ ] A silhouette check on video, from the side and the front.
