# Stage slaps and knaps

A stage slap looks like a real slap but does not make contact. The impact is shown by the slapper's hand sweeping past the face and a sound made separately (the knap), with the receiver's head reacting at the sound. Train it with an experienced teacher or fight director.

## Principles
- The slapper's hand never touches the receiver's face. It passes at a safe distance, in a line the receiver can see.
- The sound (the knap) is made by the slapper's other hand, for example by clapping against the chest or thigh, or by a clap of both hands. The sound is produced out of sight of the audience.
- The receiver moves the head on the sound, as if the contact had happened.

## Set-up
1. Agree the type of slap, the side, and the distance between the partners.
2. Walk the movement slowly without sound.
3. Add the sound in slow motion.
4. Add the head reaction.

## Safety
- Clear eye contact between partners at the start of every move.
- The receiver always has the right to say stop.
- Never rehearse or perform while tired or after drinking; fatigue ruins distance.
- Use a stage-fight check before each performance.

## Repeating in performance
- Rehearse the slap as a precise sequence.
- Check before each show; walk through at half speed.
- Confirm the audience angle: the trick only works from the front and in a limited range of angles.

## Faults
- Too close: contact occurs.
- Late sound: the audience sees the trick.
- The receiver's head reaction anticipates the sound.

## Verify
- [ ] The slap and the sound are rehearsed at slow, medium and full speed.
- [ ] Both partners have agreed signals for stopping.
