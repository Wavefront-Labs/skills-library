# Silent clowning and nonverbal comedy

**Goal:** a clown that communicates entirely through body, face, rhythm and sound -
readable by any audience, in any language.

## Principles
- **One thing at a time**: action, then reaction, then the next action. Audiences can read
  a single clear action; simultaneous ones blur.
- **Fixed points (toc)**: pause at the start and end of important gestures so they land.
- **Look - react - act**: the clown sees, thinks visibly, then does.
- **Rhythm**: silence and stillness are the punctuation; vary tempo.
- **Exaggerated clarity**, not exaggerated size: a small, clear reaction beats a big vague
  one.

## Tools without words
- **Gibberish**: nonsense language with clear intonation conveys emotion and intent.
- **Vocal sounds**: sighs, gasps, whistles, hums.
- **Props as partners**: objects that "talk back".
- **Music cues** as dialogue (the band answers the clown).

## Silent improvisation exercises
- Coach gives a task (make a sandwich, catch a fly) - no words, audience must follow.
- **Mirror**: partner copies every move; then leads secretly change.
- **Discovery**: clown enters, finds an object, discovers what it can do.

## Gotchas
- Mumbling halfway between gibberish and words confuses; commit to one.
- Facing upstage hides the face; open out.

## Verify
- [ ] An observer can retell the story of a 2-minute silent improv.
- [ ] Each key action ends on a clear fixed point.
