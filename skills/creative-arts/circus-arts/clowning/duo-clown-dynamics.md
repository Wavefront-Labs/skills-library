# Duo clown dynamics

A duo clown act relies on contrast. The classic circus pairing is the whiteface and the auguste: the whiteface is the authoritative, neat one; the auguste is the foolish, messy one who upsets the order.

## What makes a duo work
- Contrast: in status, energy, tempo and want.
- A clear relationship: boss and worker, teacher and pupil, two friends who want the same thing.
- Trust: each partner supports the other's moment and does not compete for the laugh.

## Status
- High status: still, controlled, takes time.
- Low status: busy, fast, apologetic, trying to please.
- Status can shift during the scene; a reversal is a strong payoff.

## Rhythm
- Treat the scene like a rally: an offer, a response, an escalation.
- Do not talk over a partner's moment; pause and let the laugh come.
- Vary the rhythm: fast exchanges, then a long silence.

## Rehearsal
1. Improvise a simple scene (two clowns share one chair).
2. Name the relationship and each clown's want.
3. Repeat, with the high-status clown becoming the low.
4. Fix the structure: beats, reversal and ending.

## Faults
- Both clowns competing for attention.
- No relationship: the scene becomes two solos.
- Hesitating to let the partner win the moment.

## Verify
- [ ] Partners can state each clown's want and status in a sentence.
- [ ] The scene has a clear reversal or payoff.
