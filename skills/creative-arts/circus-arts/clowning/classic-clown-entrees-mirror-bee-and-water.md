# Classic clown entrees: mirror, bee and water

Classic entrees survive because they have a clear structure. Learn the structure, then play it in your own way.

## Mirror gag
Two performers face each other and one imitates the other as a mirror. The fun comes from the mismatch: the mirror lags, a prop breaks the symmetry, or the original discovers the trick.
- Setup: the mirror is established and the audience sees the rule.
- Escalation: the actions become more complex and the mirror starts to cheat.
- Payoff: the rule breaks (the mirror refuses, or the original ends up as the mirror).

## Bee gag
A clown is bothered by an imaginary bee (or fly) that the audience follows through the clown's reactions.
- Setup: the clown notices the buzzing and tries to ignore it.
- Escalation: the bee follows the clown; the clown's attempts to get rid of it become larger.
- Payoff: the clown catches it, or it lands on someone else.
The bee must be completely believable through the clown's eyes and body.

## Water gag
A bucket or glass of water is set up as a threat. The audience expects it to be thrown.
- Setup: the clown shows the bucket; the audience waits.
- Escalation: the clown threatens, hesitates, or swaps.
- Payoff: a surprise, such as confetti instead of water, or a different victim.
Water on a stage is a slip risk; use a safe floor, towels and a plan for clean-up, and clear any water use with the venue.

## Using the structures
- Keep setup, escalation and payoff clear.
- Add your own want and character.
- Find the real moment in the gag rather than copying someone else's timing.

- [ ] You can describe the setup, escalation and payoff of each gag in one sentence.
