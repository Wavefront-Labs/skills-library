# Drawing a circle show crowd

**Goal:** turn passers-by into a seated or standing circle of audience that stays for a
20-40 minute show.

## Pitch choice
- Foot traffic that can stop: a square or wide pavement, not a narrow rush corridor.
- A backdrop (wall, fountain) so the audience forms a half circle, not a full one.
- Shade, no competing noise, permitted (many cities license pitches - check rules and
  time slots).
- Sightlines: steps, slopes and benches help people at the back see.

## The draw
1. Set up visibly: props, rope or chalk circle - people are curious about setup.
2. Make noise and small bits: a whistle, music, a quick trick, talking to passers-by.
3. Get the first few people to commit: ask them to sit at the front ("the VIP area").
4. Once a few are watching, others join (crowds attract crowds).

## Setting the edge
- Mark the circle with rope, chalk or props; invite kids to sit at the edge, adults to
  stand behind.
- Bring people forward early: "Everyone take two steps forward." Tight crowds laugh more
  and leave less.

## Holding them
- A promised finale announced early ("At the end I'll juggle fire on this unicycle").
- Short bits with pay-offs every minute or two.
- Use volunteers to keep the crowd invested.

## Gotchas
- Building too long kills energy: start the show when the crowd is big enough to sustain
  itself, often 20-30 people.
- Blocked shopfronts and pathways annoy local businesses; keep a walkway clear.

## Verify
- [ ] A clear walkway and legal pitch.
- [ ] The crowd has moved forward at least once during the draw.
- [ ] Finale promised early and delivered.
