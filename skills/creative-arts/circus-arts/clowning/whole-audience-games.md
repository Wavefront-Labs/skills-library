# Whole-audience games

A whole-audience game uses the entire crowd rather than a single volunteer. It can open a show, reset a restless audience, or build a climax.

## Types
- **Call and response:** the performer makes a sound or action; the audience repeats it.
- **Waves and rhythms:** the audience claps or stamps to a pattern.
- **Simple movement games:** raise a hand, turn a head, stand and sit (only when it is safe).
- **Voting or choosing:** the audience decides between two options.

## Leading
1. Give one instruction at a time, in a short sentence, with a demonstration.
2. Make the first round easy so that everyone succeeds.
3. Build gradually: faster, louder, more complex.
4. End decisively with a clear final gesture and applause.

## Safety and access
- Avoid anything that has the audience standing on seats or moving in a crowded space.
- Offer ways to join in that do not require movement: voice, clapping or a small gesture.
- Do not force participation; make joining optional.
- For children, check the space and supervision with the organisers.

## Control
- Use a stop signal that the audience knows (for example a raised hand and silence).
- Keep your own energy slightly above the audience's.
- Be ready to cut the game if it is not working.

## Verify
- [ ] Instructions fit in one sentence each.
- [ ] A stop signal is agreed and used at the end.
