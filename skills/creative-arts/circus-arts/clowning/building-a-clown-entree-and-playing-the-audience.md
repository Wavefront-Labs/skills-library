# Building a clown entrée and playing the audience

**Goal:** make a clown act that is funny because of the audience relationship, not
because of the costume, and structure it so it lands every night.

## 1. The core engine: want, try, flop
A clown **wants something simple** (to sit down, to play the trumpet, to impress the
ringmaster), **tries** with complete sincerity, and **fails** — the "flop". The comedy is
in how the clown experiences the failure and shows it to the audience.
- Play the want 100% seriously. A clown who "acts funny" isn't.
- After each flop, **look at the audience** (the take): share the failure with them.
  This look is the clown's main tool; it creates the complicity (*complicité*).
- The clown never knows the joke is coming; the audience often does.

## 2. Comic mechanics worth drilling
- **The take / double-take:** see it, look away, *realise*, look back, then look at the
  audience. Time the pause before the realisation.
- **Rule of three:** set up, repeat, break. Third time the expected thing doesn't happen.
- **Escalation:** each attempt bigger than the last.
- **The "button":** a clean final beat that tells the audience the bit is over.
- **Stillness:** a frozen moment after a disaster usually gets more than more business.

## 3. Entrée structure
An entrée is a short self-contained clown scene (classically between acts, often
2–8 minutes). A dependable skeleton:
1. **Entrance & want** — establish who you are and what you want within ~20 s.
2. **Obstacle** — a prop, partner or rule stops you.
3. **Attempts** — three or more escalating tries, each with a flop and a take.
4. **Turn** — an unexpected solution, a reversal, or the obstacle wins absurdly.
5. **Button and exit** — strong final image; exit while they're still laughing.
In a duo the classic pairing is a high-status figure (whiteface tradition) and a
low-status one (auguste) who undermines them. *Historic definitions vary between
traditions; treat the labels loosely.*

## 4. Working with volunteers
- **Choose:** look for someone already engaged and smiling, not someone hiding.
  Ask with a gesture; a "no" is accepted cheerfully and immediately.
- **Protect:** the volunteer is never the butt of the joke. The clown takes the
  humiliation; the volunteer is the hero. Applaud them off.
- **Make it easy:** give simple tasks with clear physical instructions; take them by
  the hand to where they stand.
- **Never touch** without signalled consent; avoid children's volunteers being lifted
  or isolated; return everyone to their seat.

## 5. Physical comedy — safely
- Pratfalls and trips are **controlled falls**: land on the fleshy back of the hips and
  roll back onto the shoulders with chin tucked; slap the floor with palm/forearm to
  sell sound and spread impact. Learn them on mats with an instructor first.
- Slaps and hits are **knaps** (the sound made by the receiver or a third party out of
  sightline) — never real contact to the face.
- Water, foam and slippery props: plan the clean-up and floor traction for the next act.

## 6. Developing a clown
- Work in front of people early; a clown only exists in front of an audience.
- Record shows and mark where laughs happen and where they don't; cut dead beats.
- Keep a "bit book": gags, props, flops that worked and the exact timing.

## Gotchas
- Mugging (pulling faces at the audience) instead of reacting truthfully kills laughs.
- Too much plot; an entrée is one want and one obstacle.
- Ignoring a disruptive moment (a dropped prop, a crying baby) — the clown must
  acknowledge what everyone has seen, then play with it.
- Running long; cut 20% after the first shows.

## Verify
- [ ] You can state the clown's want in one sentence.
- [ ] Each attempt has a flop and a take to the audience.
- [ ] The entrée follows entrance › obstacle › attempts › turn › button.
- [ ] Volunteers are chosen with consent, made to look good, and applauded off.
- [ ] Every fall and slap has been rehearsed as a technique, never real impact.
