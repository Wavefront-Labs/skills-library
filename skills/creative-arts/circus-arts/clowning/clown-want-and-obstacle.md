# The clown's want and obstacle

A clown on stage is most readable when they want something simple and something stands in the way.

## A clear want
- Choose a simple objective: to sit down, to get the hat, to impress the audience.
- State it physically: the audience should see what the clown wants without words.
- Keep the objective single and clear at any moment.

## The obstacle
- The obstacle can be a prop, another clown, the audience, or the clown's own body.
- The clown tries, fails, tries again; the audience follows the attempts.
- Each failure should be different: the clown learns something, then tries a new approach.

## Escalation
1. First attempt: simple and direct.
2. Second attempt: more effort.
3. Third attempt: invention.
4. Resolution: success, a bigger failure, or a surprising turn.

## Honest failure
- The clown does not know the outcome in advance.
- Real disappointment and real joy read to the audience.
- Avoid playing for the laugh; let the want drive the play.

## Exercises
- Pair exercise: one clown wants something that the other holds.
- Solo exercise: a clown wants to leave through a door that will not open.

## Verify
- [ ] The want can be stated in one sentence by an observer after viewing.
- [ ] Three different attempts, each with a different tactic.
