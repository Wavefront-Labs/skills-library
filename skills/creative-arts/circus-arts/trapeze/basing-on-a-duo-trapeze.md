# Basing on a duo trapeze

**Goal:** be the stable, locked structure your partner moves on, while managing a bar
that wants to tip and swing under two bodies.

## Bar and rig
- Duo work uses a wider bar (often 80-100 cm or more) and a point rated for two people
  with dynamic loads (see dynamic-load-factors-for-aerial-points).
- A double trapeze (two bars stacked or a long bar) changes the base's options.

## Base positions
- **Seated on the bar** holding the ropes: for lifts and balances above the bar.
- **Hocks hang** (knees over bar): the base holds the flyer below. Hocks must be deep,
  feet flexed and pushing on the ropes or crossed for security.
- **Catcher's lock / ankle lock in the ropes**: legs wrapped in the ropes so the base
  is secure without using hands, freeing both arms for the flyer.
- **Standing on the bar**: for counterbalance figures.

## Taking weight
- Before the flyer commits weight, the base says "ready" and is locked.
- Absorb by bending the arms slightly, then lock; never catch with straight locked arms.
- The bar rotates as weight shifts; the base counters by adjusting the hips or the lock.

## Conditioning for bases
- Hanging leg and hip-flexor strength for long hocks hangs.
- Grip and forearm endurance holding a partner's wrists.
- Core anti-rotation (pallof presses), and neck comfort inverted for long periods.
- Inverted time tolerance: build gradually, come up between attempts.

## Gotchas
- Hocks slipping when the bar is wet or costume is slick: chalk/grip fabric behind knees.
- Fatigue in the base is the main danger; plan the act so the base has rests.

## Verify
- [ ] Base can hold the main lock 60 s with a partner's weight in training.
- [ ] Partner agrees the base's "ready" call is reliable.
