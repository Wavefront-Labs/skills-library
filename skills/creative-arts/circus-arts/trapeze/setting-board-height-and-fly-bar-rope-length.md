# Setting board height and fly bar rope length

**Goal:** a geometry where the flyer's swing reaches the catcher at the right moment and
height, and the flyer can take off and return safely.

## The relationships
- **Fly bar rope length** sets the swing period (longer ropes = slower swing) and the
  lowest point of the swing.
- **Board height** relative to the bar's rest position sets how much potential energy
  the flyer starts with and the swing amplitude. The board is above the bar's hanging
  height so the flyer drops into the swing.
- **Catch-trap distance** from the fly bar must match where the flyer is at the peak of
  the forward swing. The catcher's own swing adds or subtracts distance.

## Adjustments
- Flyer consistently short of the catcher: move the catch trap closer, raise the board
  slightly, or have the catcher swing bigger.
- Flyer arriving early or too high: move catch trap away or lower the board.
- Return to the board too low: board height or fly bar rope length off; adjust the bar
  height or have a board hand use a hook to retrieve the bar.
Make one change at a time and test with a staff flyer.

## Testing
1. Staff flyer swings with no trick; watch where the knees or hands are at the forward
   peak relative to the catcher's hands.
2. Catcher swings in time; rehearse catches with an experienced flyer before students.
3. Record board height, rope length and catch-trap distance for the rig.

## Gotchas
- Different flyers' body lengths and swing styles shift the peak; adjust the catcher's
  swing rather than the rig for each student.
- Ropes stretch over time and with humidity; re-measure periodically.

## Verify
- [ ] Settings recorded and repeatable after a rig rebuild.
- [ ] Staff flyer reaches catch position consistently in 5 test swings.
