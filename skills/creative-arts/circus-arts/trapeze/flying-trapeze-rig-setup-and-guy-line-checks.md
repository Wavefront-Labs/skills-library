# Flying trapeze rig setup and guy-line checks

**Goal:** a freestanding rig that is plumb, properly guyed and anchored, so swing loads
from the fly bar and catch trap go into the ground through a known path.

## Main components
Two pairs of uprights (A-frames or masts) at the board end and catch-trap end, a top
cable or beam, guy lines out to ground anchors, the pedestal board with ladder, the fly
bar and catch trap, and the net.

## Setup order (follow the manufacturer manual; this is the shape)
1. Survey ground: level, soil type for anchors, overhead hazards, wind exposure.
2. Lay out anchor positions with a tape from the rig centreline. Guy angles as specified
   (often around 45 degrees).
3. Drive anchors (stakes, screw anchors, or ballast blocks on hard ground). Pull-test if
   the manufacturer specifies.
4. Raise uprights, attach guys loosely, plumb with a level or plumb bob, then tension
   opposing guys evenly.
5. Hang board, fly bar and catch trap; hang net and tension it.

## Guy lines
- Even tension on opposite pairs keeps uprights plumb under swing loads.
- Ratchet or turnbuckle tensioners, safety-wired or locked.
- Mark the ground line around anchors to keep public away; flag lines at head height.

## Daily walk-round
- Uprights plumb (re-level after rain or ground movement).
- Anchors not lifted; soil not soft around stakes.
- Guy tensioners locked, no fraying at thimbles or clips (wire rope clip saddles on the
  live side, "never saddle a dead horse").
- Fly bar and catch-trap ropes, swivels and shackles inspected.

## Wind and weather
Follow the manufacturer's wind limit for flying and for leaving the rig standing. Suspend
flying in lightning and when gusts exceed the limit.

## Verify
- [ ] Rig plumb and all guys tensioned evenly; record checked.
- [ ] Anchors meet manufacturer pull-out spec for the ground type.
- [ ] Wind limit posted and an anemometer on site.
