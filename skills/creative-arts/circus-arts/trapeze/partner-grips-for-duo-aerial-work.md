# Partner grips for duo aerial work

**Goal:** choose and train grips that do not slip when loaded dynamically.

## The grips
- **Wrist-to-wrist (catcher's grip)**: each partner grasps the other's wrist; four hands
  form a lock. The strongest grip for swinging and catches; standard in flying trapeze.
- **Hand-to-hand (palm to palm, fingers interlaced or clasped)**: for balances where
  hands push (handstand on hands). Poor for hanging loads.
- **Forearm grip**: the base holds the flyer's forearm near the elbow - used in some
  low-load transitions.
- **Ankle grip**: base holds above the ankle bones; flyer flexes the foot to stop the
  hand sliding off.
- **Hip / waist holds**: in transitions; slippery with costumes.

## Training sequence
1. On the floor: practise the grip, then pull against each other to feel slip points.
2. Low bar or partner hanging from a low bar with feet near the floor.
3. Full height once the grip holds under deliberate bounces at low height.

## Grip hygiene
- Chalk both partners' hands and wrists; no lotion before training.
- Wristbands or tape if sweat is a problem (agree, because they change the feel).
- Nails short.

## Gotchas
- Watches, rings and bracelets: never.
- Wrist-to-wrist with uneven hand sizes: the smaller partner grips the forearm a little
  higher. Practise.
- Under fatigue grip changes happen without noticing; agree a "re-grip" call.

## Verify
- [ ] Every grip in the act tested at low height with a bounce.
- [ ] Both partners chalk and check hands before each run.
