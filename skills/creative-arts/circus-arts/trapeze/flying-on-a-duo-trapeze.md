# Flying on a duo trapeze

**Goal:** be easy to hold: predictable weight, constant body tension, and transitions the
base can feel coming.

## Principles
- **Tension is lightness**: a tight hollow body is easier to hold than a floppy one of the
  same mass. Keep core and glutes engaged in every hold.
- **Transfer weight gradually**: move from bar to partner in stages, hands first, then
  hips, never jumping your full weight into the base without a call.
- **Keep connection points** where the base expects them (wrists, ankles, hips).
- **Active shoulders** in every hang from the base's hands.

## Typical transitions
- From seated beside the base to hanging from the base's hands below the bar.
- From hanging to a hip or foot balance on the base.
- From the base back to the bar: re-grip the bar or ropes before releasing the base.

## Communication
- Use agreed calls for "taking weight", "ready", "down" and "stop".
- Flyer calls stop when grip or tension fails; the base brings the flyer to the bar
  or a hang rather than letting go.

## Conditioning
- Grip and hollow body endurance.
- Flexibility for lines while held (splits in hands-to-ankles holds).
- Spatial awareness work on trampoline for inversions in a partner's hold.

## Gotchas
- Sweat on wrists ruins grips: chalk and towels at the bar, wristbands as costume.
- A flyer who goes limp when nervous is the most dangerous flyer to hold; train relaxed
  tension in rehearsal.

## Verify
- [ ] Base rates the flyer's transitions as predictable.
- [ ] Every transition has a named call.
