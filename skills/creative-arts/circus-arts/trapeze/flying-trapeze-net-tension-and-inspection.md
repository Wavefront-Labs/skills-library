# Flying trapeze net tension and inspection

**Goal:** a net that catches flyers softly without bottoming out or bouncing them off,
inspected so a broken mesh or border rope is found before someone lands on it.

## Net geometry
- Net is typically 1.5-2.5 m above the floor and extends well beyond the swing path
  front, back and sides (commonly several metres past the catch trap and board).
- It is held by a border rope tensioned to the rig frame with side lines and end lines
  through tensioners (ratchets, turnbuckles or block-and-tackle).

## Tension target
- **Too tight**: flyers bounce, risk being thrown off the edge, and land harder.
- **Too loose**: a flyer landing seat-first can bottom out on the floor.
- Practical check used by schools: a staff member drops seat-first from standing height
  at centre; the net should give substantially but leave a clear margin (often around
  half a metre or more) above the floor. Set your number from the manufacturer's manual
  and write it down. *Exact sag targets vary by rig; this is a field check, not a spec.*
- Re-tension after temperature or humidity changes and after rain on outdoor rigs
  (rope nets shrink when wet).

## Inspection (daily visual, weekly hands-on)
- Walk the net perimeter: border rope, attachment ties, tensioners, shackles.
- Look for broken or worn meshes, especially in the landing zone and at the edges.
- Check for UV fade and stiffness on outdoor nets.
- Any broken mesh in the landing zone: repair with matching twine by a competent person
  or take the net out of service.

## Gotchas
- Edges are where flyers get hurt: ensure the net extends far enough that a long
  release does not land on the border.
- Tension changes overnight: check before the first flyer, not after the first landing.

## Verify
- [ ] Centre drop test done today and recorded.
- [ ] No broken meshes in landing zone; border and ties secure.
- [ ] Net dimensions cover the longest trick trajectory with margin.
