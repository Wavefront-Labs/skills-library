# Dove magic and animal welfare

**Goal:** if you perform with doves (ring-necked doves are typical), put their welfare above
the act and comply with local law. Consider whether a non-animal alternative serves
your act.

## Legal
- Many countries require registration or a licence to exhibit performing animals (e.g.
  in England, a licence under the Animal Welfare (Licensing of Activities Involving
  Animals) regulations). Check local rules.
- Venues and cruise lines may ban live animals; ask early.

## Welfare basics
- **Housing**: a proper aviary or large cage at home, not the travel case.
- **Diet and water**: dove seed mix, grit, clean water daily.
- **Vet**: an avian vet; regular checks.
- **Handling**: calm, gentle; trained doves are habituated gradually.
- **Load time**: time a dove spends in a hidden load must be short (minutes, not longer)
  with adequate air; overheating is the main risk under stage lights.
- **Rest days** and limits on shows per day.

## In the act
- Loads designed with ventilation and padding; never squeezing.
- Doves return to a perch or the assistant immediately after production.
- No flying in venues where they cannot be recovered safely.

## Alternatives
Silk or mechanical dove productions, or other visual productions, avoid all welfare
concerns and travel restrictions.

## Verify
- [ ] Licence or registration current where required.
- [ ] Maximum load time measured and respected.
- [ ] Vet contact and care log kept.
