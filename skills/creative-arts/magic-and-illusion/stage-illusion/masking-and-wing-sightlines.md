# Masking and wing sightlines

**Goal:** the audience never sees backstage preparation, crew or loaded props in the wings,
from any seat sold.

## Principle
Theatres use **masking** (black legs at the sides, borders above, tabs and travellers
across the stage) to frame the stage and hide the wings and fly space. A magic show needs
more masking than most theatre, because a single glimpse of an assistant waiting, a
duplicate prop or a loading position can expose a method.

## Method
1. Draw sightlines from the extreme seats (front-row ends, side boxes, balcony) on plan and
   section.
2. Place legs so the sightline from each extreme seat lands on masking, not the wing.
3. Borders trimmed so the balcony cannot see lighting bars and flown props.
4. Mark "no-go" zones in the wings where crew must not stand during illusions.
5. Check from every extreme seat with the lighting on.

## Touring
Every venue differs. Carry your own masking where possible, and walk the house at each
venue before rehearsal.

## Common faults
- Crew standing in a sightline during an illusion.
- Wing lighting (blue lights, running lights) silhouetting people.
- Masking set for the stalls only.

## Verify
- [ ] Sightline drawing completed for each venue.
- [ ] Extreme seats walked with lights on.
- [ ] Crew no-go zones taped and briefed.
