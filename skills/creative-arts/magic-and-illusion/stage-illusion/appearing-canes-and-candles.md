# Appearing canes and candles

**Goal:** use spring-loaded and telescopic props (appearing canes, appearing candles,
vanishing canes) cleanly and safely in a manipulation act.

## How they work (general)
- **Appearing cane**: a long spring-steel band or telescoping tube rolled or collapsed in
  the hand; released, it extends into a rigid cane.
- **Vanishing cane**: the reverse - a cane collapses (often into a silk or the hand).
- **Appearing candle**: a telescoping tube with a flame (real or electric) that extends
  from a small load. Fantasio-style designs are the classic.
Follow the specific manufacturer's instructions.

## Loading
- Small loads held in the hand, sleeve or behind a silk; the release happens during a
  sweeping gesture so the extension is a "flash" of appearance.
- Retracting: some canes are manually re-rolled offstage; keep a spare set.

## Maintenance
- Spring steel kinks: handle and store as instructed; replace when it no longer locks
  rigid.
- Check telescopic joints for wear.

## Fire safety (real flames)
- Venue permission and fire officer approval for open flame.
- Fire extinguisher or fire blanket in the wings.
- No flames near drapes, hair or loose costume.
- Many performers use flicker LED candles instead.

## Using them
Canes and candles are strong openers: an appearing cane at the first music beat
establishes the manipulator.

## Verify
- [ ] Cane extends and locks rigid in 10 consecutive tests.
- [ ] Flame permission and extinguisher in place, or LED substitute used.
