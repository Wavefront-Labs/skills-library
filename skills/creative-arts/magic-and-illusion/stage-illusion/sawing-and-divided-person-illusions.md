# Sawing and divided-person illusions

**Goal:** a person appears to be cut or separated into sections, and is restored unharmed.

## Principle
P.T. Selbit introduced sawing through a woman in 1921; Horace Goldin popularised a
version with the head and feet visible. Robert Harbin's **Zig Zag** (1965) displaced the
middle section of a standing cabinet sideways while the assistant's face, hand and foot
remained in view. These illusions depend on body displacement and on the audience's
belief that the visible head, hands and feet belong to one person in one place.

## What makes it convincing
- Visible extremities throughout (hand waving, feet moving).
- Thin-looking dividing blades and sections.
- A clear separation moment, ideally with space visibly between sections.
- Clean restoration and the assistant stepping out unharmed.

## Safety
- Blades never sharp; guard edges; blades checked before each show.
- Pinch points around moving sections identified and guarded.
- Assistant's position rehearsed; an abort signal agreed.

## Common faults
- Sections handled roughly, suggesting weight.
- Extremities stop moving at a key moment.
- Blades that look like props.

## Verify
- [ ] Extremity movements choreographed at every phase.
- [ ] Blade and pinch-point checklist signed each show.
- [ ] Separation moment visible from all seats sold.
