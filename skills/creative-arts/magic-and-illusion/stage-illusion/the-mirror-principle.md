# The mirror principle and the Sphinx

**Goal:** understand how a mirror set at an angle can make a space look empty, and the
staging needed for it to deceive.

## Principle
A mirror placed at 45 degrees between two identical surfaces reflects one side into the
place of the other. The audience sees what looks like the space behind the mirror (the
back wall), but is actually a reflection of the side wall. Anything behind the mirror is
hidden. The **Sphinx** (Thomas Tobin, 1865) used mirrors under a table to show a living
head on an apparently empty table. Mirror boxes and many cabinet illusions use the same
idea.

## Staging requirements
- Matched, plain surfaces: the reflected side must look exactly like the back.
- Even lighting on both surfaces, so the reflection is the same brightness.
- **Audience directly in front:** at side angles the mirror reflects the audience or the
  stage.
- No objects or performers moving where the mirror would reflect them.
- Clean, high-quality mirror; dust and fingerprints betray it.

## Common faults
- Performer walking across a reflected area.
- Floor or wall patterns that do not repeat symmetrically.
- Lighting change during the illusion that affects one side only.

## Verify
- [ ] Viewed from the extreme seats under show light.
- [ ] No reflection of moving people or audience at any viewing angle sold.
- [ ] Mirror surfaces cleaned before each show.
