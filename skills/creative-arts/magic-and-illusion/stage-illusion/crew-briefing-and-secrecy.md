# Crew briefing and secrecy

**Goal:** venue and touring crew do their jobs safely and well while methods stay
protected.

## Principle
Crew need to know **what** to do and **where not to be**, not necessarily **how** an
illusion works. Secrets spread through casual talk and photographs; safety depends on
crew knowing hazards. A clear briefing balances both.

## Briefing content
- Running order and each cue they operate.
- Safety: pyro, blades, abort signals, who watches for them.
- No-go zones in the wings during illusions.
- Rules: no photos backstage, no discussing the show on social media.
- Contact: stage manager for all questions.

## Need-to-know
- Venue crew learn their cue, not the method.
- Touring crew who load and reset props learn what they must, under a signed secrecy
  agreement.
- Cover props when not in use; load in and out with covers on.

## Common faults
- Methods explained casually to venue staff.
- Props left uncovered in the dock.
- Briefing done only verbally.

## Verify
- [ ] Written briefing sheet issued to each crew member.
- [ ] Secrecy agreements signed by touring crew.
- [ ] Props covered at all times off stage.
