# Black art staging

**Goal:** make objects and people in black appear, vanish or float against a black
background, using the publicly documented black art principle.

## Principle
The eye cannot separate dead-black matte material from a dead-black background when the
foreground is lit brightly and the background is not. Black velvet absorbs light so well
that a black-covered object in front of it disappears. Bright light shining **into the
audience's eyes** (footlights or front-of-house light in the old staging) further reduces
their ability to see into the dark. Max Auzinger's 19th-century "black art" theatre is the
historical model.

## Staging requirements
- **Surround:** a full black velvet box (back, sides, floor, often ceiling), no seams
  catching light.
- **Light:** bright light on the performer and the "white" objects; none on the velvet.
  Side light at a low angle, shuttered off the background.
- **Covers:** matte black velvet bags and assistants in black velvet hoods.
- **Contrast:** performers in light or bright costumes, props in white or metallic.
- **Distance:** works best when the audience is several metres away; front rows see
  more texture.

## Common faults
- Light spill onto the backdrop or floor.
- Shiny black fabric (satin, polyester) instead of matte velvet.
- Dust and lint on velvet, visible under light.

## Verify
- [ ] Viewed from front-row ends and the back under show light.
- [ ] Velvet clean and seams closed.
- [ ] Light meter or camera check shows no spill on the backdrop.
