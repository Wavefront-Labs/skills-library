# Haze, smoke and pyro for reveals

**Goal:** use atmospheric effects and pyrotechnics to strengthen appearances and vanishes
without exposing methods or creating hazards.

## What each does
- **Haze:** a fine, even atmosphere that makes light beams visible. Good for big-light
  looks. Dangerous for black art, because it reveals beams and lit areas you meant to keep
  dark.
- **Smoke / fog:** denser clouds for covering moments or creating mood. Low fog hugs the
  floor.
- **Pyrotechnics:** flashes, gerbs, concussions. A flash at the moment of appearance
  dazzles and covers.

## When to avoid haze
Any illusion that depends on darkness or black art. Test with haze on in technical
rehearsal before committing.

## Safety and permissions
- Pyrotechnics are regulated: in the US, NFPA 1126 governs pyro before a proximate
  audience; most jurisdictions require licensed operators and permits.
- Fog fluids should meet published standards (ANSI E1.5 in North America).
- **Fire alarms:** smoke and haze trigger detectors. Agree an alarm isolation procedure
  with the venue's fire officer; never isolate without permission.
- Flash effects and strobes need audience warnings for photosensitive people.

## Common faults
- Haze lingering into the next illusion.
- Pyro placed near a curtain or an assistant's path.
- Fire alarm triggered mid-show.

## Verify
- [ ] Every pyro effect has a licensed operator and permit.
- [ ] Alarm isolation agreed in writing with the venue.
- [ ] Each illusion tested in the planned haze state.
