# Illusion materials and road cases

**Goal:** build or specify illusions that are rigid enough to bear a person, light enough to
tour, and finished so construction details reveal nothing.

## Materials
- **Plywood** (birch or marine ply, 9-18 mm): classic for boxes and cabinets. Easy to work,
  heavy. Thin ply over a frame saves weight.
- **Aluminium** extrusions and sheet: light, rigid frames; common in modern touring
  illusions. Needs welding or bolted brackets.
- **Steel** tube: bases and load-bearing parts (where a person stands or lies).
- **Foam board / composites**: lightweight panels for non-load-bearing parts.
- **Fabric and velvet**: masking, black art surfaces, drapes.

## Design principles
- **Load paths**: where a person sits, lies or stands, the structure must carry dynamic
  loads (stepping in, jumping out). Over-build those parts.
- **Thin looks honest**: audiences judge capacity by visible thickness; thin edges and
  bevels make boxes look too small to hide anyone.
- **Hide joins**: decoration, paint patterns and trim conceal secret panels and seams.
- **Quiet operation**: felt, rubber stops, and nylon guides stop clunks.

## Road cases
- Each illusion packs into fitted cases or onto a wheeled base with a cover.
- Foam-lined cases for delicate parts; corners and edges protected.
- Label cases with contents and assembly order; keep secret parts out of sight of venue
  crew (lockable cases).
- Weight per case suited to the crew who will move it.

## Verify
- [ ] Load-bearing parts tested with more than the heaviest performer, dynamically.
- [ ] Illusion packs into labelled cases within the get-out time.
