# Pepper's ghost

**Goal:** make a transparent "ghost" appear and fade in a live scene, using reflection in a
large sheet of glass.

## Principle
A large sheet of clear glass (or modern stretched foil) is angled between the audience and
the stage. A hidden scene, out of the audience's direct sight (below or to the side), is
lit. Its reflection in the glass appears in the visible scene. Raising the hidden light
makes the ghost appear; lowering it makes it vanish. Henry Dircks invented the principle;
John Henry Pepper popularised it in 1862.

## Staging requirements
- Glass (or foil) large enough to cover the area where the ghost appears.
- **Hidden scene** in black, with only the ghost figure lit.
- **Light balance:** the visible stage must be dim enough that the reflection shows; the
  ghost's light sets its opacity.
- The glass must be invisible: no edges, frames or reflections of stage lights.
- Modern versions use special foils and projected video (used for live "hologram" shows).

## Common faults
- Edges or seams of the glass catching light.
- Visible stage reflecting audience or exit signs.
- Ghost and live performer misaligned in depth.

## Verify
- [ ] Glass edges invisible from every seat sold.
- [ ] Ghost fades in and out on a lighting cue.
- [ ] Performers' marks set so the ghost interacts correctly.
