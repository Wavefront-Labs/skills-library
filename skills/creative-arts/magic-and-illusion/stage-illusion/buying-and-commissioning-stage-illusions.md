# Buying and commissioning stage illusions

**Goal:** get an illusion that fits your show, your assistants and your venues, from a
builder you can trust, with the right to perform it.

## Choosing a builder
- Look at their recent work in live shows, not just photos.
- Ask other performers about reliability, finish and after-sales support.
- Check whether they build original designs (with rights) or copies.

## What to specify
- **Effect** and the presentation you want.
- **Venue types**: sightline range (side seats, balconies), stage depth, ceiling.
- **Assistant size and flexibility**: many illusions are built to the assistant.
- **Transport**: max case dimensions and weights, set-up time and crew.
- **Finish**: style, colours, decoration.
- **Safety** features required (see pinch-point and blade safety).

## Used illusions
- Inspect in person; check structure, mechanisms and finish.
- Confirm it fits your assistants.
- Ask about provenance and rights; copies of a living designer's illusion are a known
  ethical problem.

## Rights
- Many illusion designs are owned by their inventors (e.g. published plans sold with
  performance rights). Buy plans or illusions with clear permission.

## Acceptance testing
On delivery: assemble, run the illusion with your assistants under stage lighting from
all sightlines, check safety, and agree a fix list before final payment.

## Verify
- [ ] Specification signed with the builder.
- [ ] Rights to perform confirmed in writing.
- [ ] Acceptance test done under show conditions.
