# Appearances and vanishes of people

**Goal:** make a person appear in, or vanish from, an apparently empty space on stage.

## Principle
Every person-vanish or appearance hides the person somewhere the audience accepts as
empty: inside a prop's hidden space, behind a mirror or black-art cover, beneath the
stage (trap), or through a quick exit masked by a distraction. The **method category**
chosen sets the staging requirements.

## Approaches
- **Cabinet:** a box shown empty, closed, opened to reveal the person.
- **Platform or trap:** depends on a raised stage or stage machinery.
- **Cover:** a cloth, a flash, a moment of darkness.
- **Open stage:** the strongest and hardest, often black art or lighting-based.

## Choosing for the venue
- Traps need a theatre with a working stage floor.
- Black art needs controlled lighting and depth.
- Cabinets tour anywhere but must be shown convincingly.

## Staging the reveal
- Show the space empty in a way that is memorable and simple.
- Make the appearance sudden: light, music hit, gesture on one count.
- Hold the reveal still for applause.

## Common faults
- Showing the cabinet with fussy handling.
- Reveal without a clear moment.
- Method that depends on equipment the venue cannot provide.

## Verify
- [ ] Method category matched to each venue on the tour.
- [ ] Empty-showing sequence takes under fifteen seconds and is clear.
- [ ] Reveal on a single count with music and light.
