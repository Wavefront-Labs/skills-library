# Levitations

**Goal:** a person rises and floats in the air, often with a hoop passed around them to
show nothing supports them.

## Principle
Every levitation hides its support, by placing it where the audience does not look,
making it invisible (black art, thin structures), or by removing the person altogether
(the **Asrah**, created by Servais Le Roy, in which a covered person floats and then
vanishes as the cloth is pulled away). Hoop passes are planned around the hidden support
so the hoop appears to pass completely.

## Families
- **Supported** from behind or below via a concealed mechanism.
- **Suspended** from above, hidden by lighting.
- **Black art** levitation of objects or people.
- **Asrah-type**: a covered form floats, then vanishes.

## Staging
- Levitations are slow and graceful; music sets the tempo.
- The hoop pass must look full and unhurried.
- Angles are usually strict; check the house plan.

## Safety
- Any device lifting a person needs engineering certification, load testing and a safe
  height limit.
- Assistant briefed on positions and abort signal.
- Rigging done by competent riggers only.

## Common faults
- Hoop pass that clearly avoids an area.
- Support catching light.
- Rushing the float.

## Verify
- [ ] Device load-tested and documented.
- [ ] Hoop pass rehearsed from audience view.
- [ ] Angles checked against seats sold.
