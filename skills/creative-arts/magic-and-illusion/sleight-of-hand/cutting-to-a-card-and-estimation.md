# Cutting to a card and estimation

**Goal:** with a memorised deck, cut directly to a named card or position, or close enough
to correct invisibly.

## Principle
Knowing the position of every card turns any named card into a number. A practised hand
can cut within one or two cards of a target thickness. The correction (spreading a few
cards, a count, a double lift) is built into the presentation so a near miss is never
visible.

## Method (outline)
1. Named card is converted to its position via the stack.
2. Cut at the estimated thickness (calibrate with practice: 10, 20, 26, 40 cards).
3. Glimpse the card at the cut and calculate the difference.
4. Correct: spread or deal the needed cards, or use a double/triple lift, or "have the
   spectator deal" a number you set.

## Practice
- Cut to random targets and count how far off you are. Track error over weeks.
- Thickness changes with deck wear and humidity; recalibrate with your performing deck.

## Common faults
- Correction looks like counting: build it into the patter.
- Calibrating with a new deck and performing with a worn one.

## Verify
- [ ] Average error under two cards across fifty cuts.
- [ ] Corrections invisible to a test audience.
- [ ] Recalibrated on the performance deck.
