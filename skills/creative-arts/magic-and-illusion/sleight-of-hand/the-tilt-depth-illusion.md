# Tilt and the depth illusion

**Goal:** apparently push a card into the middle of the deck while it really goes in just
below the top card, ready to appear on top.

## Principle
Lift the back edge of the top card to form a wedge-shaped gap. A card inserted into the
gap at the front looks, from the front and above, as if it enters the middle because the
deck's thickness is visible beneath it. This is the depth illusion credited to Dai Vernon
and developed by Ed Marlo as Tilt. It is the classic engine of the ambitious card.

## Method (outline)
1. Get a break under the top card at the inner end and lift its back edge slightly
   while holding the deck tilted towards the audience.
2. Insert the card from the front into the gap, at mid-height of the deck's visible edge.
3. Push it flush; lower the top card. The inserted card is now second from top.
4. Usually followed by a double lift or a top-card reveal.

## Angle limits
Works from the front at eye level or slightly above. Fails from the sides and from
seated spectators looking upward. Tilt the deck downward slightly to the audience.

## Common faults
- Lifting the top card too high: the gap shows.
- Inserting the card too high on the edge.
- Doing it to spectators at the side.

## Verify
- [ ] Recording from audience eye level shows the card apparently mid-deck.
- [ ] Angle limits known and staged around.
- [ ] Tilt and the following reveal flow without a pause.
