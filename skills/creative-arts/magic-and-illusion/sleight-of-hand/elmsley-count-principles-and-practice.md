# Elmsley count: principles and practice

**Goal:** count four cards one at a time as four while one card is never seen - so a
packet with one reversed or different card looks uniform.

## What it does
The Elmsley count (Alex Elmsley's "ghost count") displays a packet of four cards as four
cards while concealing the face (or back) of one of them, and changes the order of the
packet as it goes. It is the engine of countless packet tricks: Twisting the Aces,
"Oil and Water" variants, and many commercial packet effects.

## The essential mechanic (study it from the sources)
The count works by taking two cards as one at a specific moment while the previously
counted card is secretly returned under the left-hand packet, then counting the rest
singly. Learn the exact handling from Card College vol 3 or Elmsley's Collected Works -
each describes the grip and finger action precisely, and small differences matter.

## Rhythm is the whole trick
- Do a **genuine** count of four cards first, many times, and note its rhythm and the
  motion of each card.
- The Elmsley count must copy that exactly: same speed, same pauses, same hand positions.
- The "take" moment (where two cards move as one) must not be slower than the others.

## Using it
- Plan which position in the packet the hidden card must be in before you count;
  the count changes the order.
- Combine with a genuine count elsewhere in the routine to make both look the same.
- Use as few counts as possible; repetition invites scrutiny.

## Tells
- Hesitation or tension at the take.
- Edges of the double card visible from the side: square the two well.
- Flashing the hidden face when the packet is tilted.

## Verify
- [ ] Genuine count and Elmsley count indistinguishable on side-by-side video.
- [ ] You can predict packet order after the count.
