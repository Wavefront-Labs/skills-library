# Memorising a stack

**Goal:** know instantly, in both directions, which card is at each of the 52 positions
and the position of any named card.

## Principle
A memorised deck turns every card into a known position, so a single glimpse tells you
everything. The well-known stacks (Tamariz **Mnemonica**, the **Aronson** stack) are
designed so the order looks random and contains built-in tricks. Learning uses a
**mnemonic link**: number pegs (1 = tie, 2 = Noah, etc., from the major system) linked
to card images.

## Method (outline)
1. Learn a peg word for each number 1 to 52 and an image for each card.
2. Learn 10 positions a day, each as a vivid scene linking number peg and card image.
3. Drill both directions: card to number and number to card, with flashcards or an app.
4. Drill random access: draw a card, say its position; call a number, name the card.
5. After a week, practise with a physical deck: cut, glimpse, name neighbours.

## Maintenance
Short daily drills for months. Stacks fade fast without use.

## Common faults
- Learning in order only: random access is what you need.
- Mixing two stacks.
- Performing before recall is under one second.

## Verify
- [ ] Random card to position under one second, 52/52.
- [ ] Random position to card under one second, 52/52.
- [ ] Recall survives a week without drilling.
