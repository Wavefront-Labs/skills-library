# Testing on real spectators

**Goal:** find out whether a routine works on laypeople before it goes into a paid set.

## Principle
Magicians and mirrors judge method; laypeople judge experience. A routine is only finished
when real spectators react the way you designed. Testing early, in low-stakes settings,
shows confusing moments, weak climaxes and angle problems that practice cannot.

## Method
1. **Choose low-stakes audiences:** friends of friends, an open-mic, a casual walk-around
   slot, a club night. Avoid testing at the most important gig.
2. **Sandwich it:** put the new piece between two proven ones.
3. **Watch faces, not hands:** where do they look? When do they react? Is the reaction at
   the moment you planned?
4. **Record audio or video** (with permission) to review reactions.
5. **Ask afterwards, indirectly:** "What did you think happened?" Their account shows what
   they understood and remembered. Avoid "did you see anything?"
6. Revise and test again; most routines need several cycles.

## Readiness test
The reaction comes at the designed moment, people can retell the effect clearly, and no
one offers the same solution twice.

## Common faults
- Testing only on magicians.
- Explaining the method when someone guesses.
- Changing three things between tests, so you cannot tell what helped.

## Verify
- [ ] Routine tested on at least five lay groups.
- [ ] Retellings show the effect is clear.
- [ ] One change per revision cycle, noted.
