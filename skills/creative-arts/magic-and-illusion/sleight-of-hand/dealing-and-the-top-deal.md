# Dealing and the top deal

**Goal:** an honest deal so consistent that any later false deal (second, bottom, glide)
can be built to match it exactly. The honest deal is the reference the audience learns.

## Principle
Every false deal is judged against the real one the audience saw a moment before. If the
honest deal has an inconsistent rhythm, sound or hand position, there is nothing stable
for the sleight to imitate. Lock the honest deal first.

## Method (outline)
1. Hold the deck in the **mechanic's grip** (first finger at the outer end, other fingers
   along the side, thumb on top, deck angled slightly to the right).
2. The left thumb pushes the top card a short distance to the right; it moves the card,
   it does not throw it.
3. The right hand takes the card with thumb on top and fingers below, at the outer right
   corner, and either deals it face down to the table or turns it face up (a "stud" deal,
   turned towards the audience as it travels).
4. Return the left thumb to the same resting point on every card.

Choose **one** style (pushing with the thumb and taking, or the right hand stripping the
card) and never mix them inside a routine; a change of style reads as a change of action.

## Common faults
- Thumb travels too far, so cards fan before they are taken.
- Rhythm speeds up on the important card (the counted or forced one).
- Squeezing: the deck bows and the deal sounds different.
- Looking at the deck: look at the pile or the spectator.

## Fine mechanics
Erdnase gives the dealing grip in detail; Giobbi's Card College vol 1 builds the top deal
as the basis of the glide and later false deals.

## Verify
- [ ] Twenty cards dealt on video at a constant tempo, no fanning at the left thumb.
- [ ] The dealt pile lands squarely in one place.
- [ ] Same grip and rhythm whether dealing one card or a stud deal.
