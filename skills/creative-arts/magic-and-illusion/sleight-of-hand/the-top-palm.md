# The top palm

**Goal:** secretly take one or more cards from the top of the deck into the palm of the
right hand while apparently just squaring or handing the deck over.

## The palm position
The card lies along the palm with its outer right corner pressed by the base of the little
finger and its inner left corner against the base of the thumb (the fleshy pad). The hand
curls gently; the card is held by slight pressure, not by gripping fingers. The back of
the hand should look relaxed and natural.

## The two-hand top palm (Royal Road style)
1. Deck in the left hand in dealing position. Push the top card slightly to the right with
   the left thumb, or obtain a little finger break under it.
2. The right hand comes over the deck as if to square it, fingers at the front end, thumb
   at the back.
3. The left little finger (or fourth finger) presses up on the right side of the top
   card(s), pivoting them up into the right palm, which closes slightly to receive them.
4. The right hand continues the squaring action, then moves away naturally, often to
   pick up a prop or gesture, while the left hand offers the deck for shuffling.

## Covering action and misdirection
- The palm happens during a reason to touch the deck: squaring, cutting, handing it over.
- Attention is on the spectator ("Would you shuffle?") at the moment of the palm.
- Do not freeze the palming hand; let it hang or rest naturally.

## Angles
Top palm is safe from the front and above; the palm is visible from the right side and
below. Keep the palm facing the floor or your body.

## Practice
In front of a camera: compare the "real square" and the palmed square side by side. The
palming hand must move at the same speed and rhythm as the real action.

## Verify
- [ ] The palmed hand looks identical to an empty hand on video from the front.
- [ ] You can palm 1, 2 and 4 cards with the same action.
