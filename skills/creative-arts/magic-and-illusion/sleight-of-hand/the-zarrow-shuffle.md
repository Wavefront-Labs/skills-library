# The Zarrow shuffle

**Goal:** a table riffle shuffle that looks completely fair yet leaves every card in its
original position.

## Principle
The two halves really do interweave at their inner ends, but one half is **slipped back
through** the other and placed back on top instead of being squared into it. The right
hand's half is carried over the left half under cover of the squaring action. Because the
cards were seen to interlace, the audience accepts that the deck was mixed.

## Method (outline)
1. Cut the deck into two halves and riffle normally, but keep the cards of one half at the
   back corner riffled *above* the corresponding cards of the other half at the near end.
2. As the hands push the halves together, the top half's interlaced cards slide through
   and over, rather than meshing.
3. Square with the same motion you use for an honest riffle.
4. Often finished with a false cut, so the deck "looks shuffled and cut".

## Common faults
- Gap or step visible at the near end at the moment of strip-out.
- A hesitation when the halves are pushed together.
- Practising only on one surface; the shuffle depends heavily on the mat.

## Fine mechanics
The full handling, including corner positions and the exact strip-out, is in Card College
vol 3 and David Ben's biography of Zarrow; learn it from one of those with video.

## Verify
- [ ] Full-deck order retained ten times in a row.
- [ ] Recording from audience eye-line shows no step at the strip-out.
- [ ] Shuffle tempo identical to your honest riffle.
