# Classic palm for coins

**Goal:** hold a coin in the centre of the palm by muscle contraction alone, with the hand
looking relaxed and empty from the back.

## Where it goes
Press a coin into the centre of the palm and contract the thumb's base (thenar) and the
little-finger base (hypothenar) muscles slightly towards each other. The coin is gripped
by its edges between them. The fingers and thumb are free.

## Getting it there
- Place the coin on the second-phalanx area of the middle two fingers.
- Curl the fingers in, pressing the coin into the palm centre, contract slightly, and
  extend the fingers again.
- Practise until you can do it with one smooth curl and no adjusting.

## Holding naturally
- The hand should hang or rest with the fingers slightly curled, as an empty hand does.
- Not too tight (the hand looks clawed), not too loose (the coin talks or drops).
- Check: can you pick up an object with the fingers while palming? You should.

## Coin size
Larger coins (US half dollar, around 30 mm) are standard in coin magic; very small coins
are harder to palm classically. Hands differ; choose coins you can hold securely.

## Exercises
- Palm, shake the hand, gesture - the coin stays.
- Palm while the other hand takes a coin from the palming hand's fingertips.
- Do everyday tasks for 5 minutes with a coin palmed (open doors, hold a cup).

## Angles
The coin may show between the fingers from below or the side if the hand is flat. Keep
the palm facing down or towards you.

## Verify
- [ ] Coin stays palmed during 5 minutes of daily activity.
- [ ] Back of the hand looks natural on video from three angles.
