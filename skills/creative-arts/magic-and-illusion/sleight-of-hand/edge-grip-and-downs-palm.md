# Edge grip and Downs palm

**Goal:** hold coins for productions in ways that free the fingers and allow coins to be
released one at a time.

## Edge grip
- A coin (or stack of coins) is held by its edge near the base of the fingers / heel of
  the hand, the coins' faces roughly perpendicular to the palm, gripped by the palm's
  muscle contraction against the edges.
- Lets you release coins one at a time to the fingertips: the basis of "coin productions
  at the fingertips" and multiple coin routines.
- The hand must stay relatively closed; it is not for open-hand displays.

## Downs palm
- Named after T. Nelson Downs. A coin lies flat in the crotch of the thumb, held by the
  thumb pressing against the side of the hand, so the fingers are entirely free and the
  hand can be shown palm-front with the coin hidden behind the thumb crotch from the
  front (in a narrow angle).
- Useful for productions at the fingertips: the first finger and thumb pull the coin up.
- Angle-sensitive: visible from the side and behind.

## Practice
- Edge grip: load four coins, release one at a time to the fingertips with no sound.
- Downs palm: practise the production motion in front of a mirror and a camera from
  multiple angles.

## When to use them
Stage and parlour manipulation (coin productions, Miser's Dream), where the audience
is in front. Close-up surrounded, prefer finger and classic palm.

## Verify
- [ ] Four coins released one at a time from edge grip silently.
- [ ] Downs palm hidden on a front camera with the palm shown.
