# The classic pass

**Goal:** secretly transpose the two halves of the deck, bringing a returned selection
from the middle to the top or bottom in a fraction of a second.

## Principle
A card is returned to the middle and a little-finger break held above it. The two hands
grip the halves so that the lower half is lifted up and over the upper half while the
upper half drops beneath it. The halves pass through each other; the deck looks unchanged.

The pass is never invisible on its own. It works because it is done **under cover**:
a turn of the body, a gesture, a moment of relaxation, or while the hands are moving.
Many professionals regard a pass that needs a frozen pause as worse than a simple
double undercut.

## Method (outline)
1. Hold a break above the selection.
2. Right hand covers the deck from above in end grip; left fingers curl around the top
   half.
3. Halves are moved past each other with minimal travel, the right hand masking the
   action from the front.
4. Done during a larger motion (turning to the next spectator, swinging the deck down).

## Common faults
- Audible snap or a "flash" of the halves at the side.
- Freezing before or after the move.
- Treating the pass as the only control; it should be one option.

## Fine mechanics
Erdnase's two-handed shift is the classic text; Royal Road and Card College vol 2 teach
the pass step by step. Expect months of practice.

## Verify
- [ ] Recording at normal speed from front and both sides shows no flash.
- [ ] Pass done while turning the body, with no pause.
- [ ] Simpler alternative control ready when angles are poor.
