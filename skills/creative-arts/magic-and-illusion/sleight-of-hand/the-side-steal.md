# Side steal

**Goal:** extract a single card from the middle of the deck, without any cut, and bring it
to the top or into the palm.

## Principle
The selection is inserted only partly into the deck, angled slightly. Under cover of the
right hand, the left little finger or right fingers pivot it out the side of the deck and
into the right palm. Because the deck is never cut, the spectator's memory is "my card went
in the middle and stayed there". Erdnase's **diagonal palm shift** is the classic version.

## Method (outline)
1. Selection is pushed into the deck, stopping slightly out of square at an angle.
2. The right hand covers the deck from above as if squaring.
3. The card is levered out of the side into the right palm (or to the top).
4. The right hand moves away naturally, or the palmed card is added to the top.

## Common faults
- Card's edge flashing at the side or front.
- Right hand covering the deck for longer than a squaring action.
- Gripping the palmed card stiffly.

## Fine mechanics
Read Erdnase's diagonal palm shift, then the modern side steal in Card College vol 3 or
Expert Card Technique. Angles are front-only; use it seated or with spectators in front.

## Verify
- [ ] Card stolen with no visible edge at normal speed on video.
- [ ] Squaring action identical in time to an honest square.
- [ ] Palmed card held with a relaxed hand.
