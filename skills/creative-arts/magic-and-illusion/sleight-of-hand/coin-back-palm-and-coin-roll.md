# Coin back palm and coin roll

**Goal:** the knuckle roll as a skill-display flourish, and the back palm as a way to show
the hand empty front and back.

## The coin roll (knuckle roll)
1. Coin balanced on the back of the first finger, held by the thumb at the edge.
2. Lift the second finger slightly: the coin tips over onto the back of the second finger,
   clipped between first and second fingers.
3. Continue to the third and then little finger.
4. At the little finger, the coin drops down between the third and little fingers, and
   the thumb catches it under the hand and brings it back to the first finger.
Practise slowly, each step separately, then join. Do both hands.

## The back palm
- The coin is clipped by its edges between the first and fourth fingers on the back of
  the hand (behind the middle two fingers), so the front of the hand can be shown empty.
- Transfer to the front: fingers curl in, carrying the coin around to the front palm while
  the hand turns, so both sides appear empty in turn. Takes a lot of practice.
- Very angle-sensitive: the audience must be in front only.

## Drills
- Coin rolls: 50 rolls each hand daily; then roll while walking or talking.
- Back palm: start with a playing card's equivalent (card back palm) to understand the
  clipping, then coins.

## Gotchas
- Coins with milled edges grip better than smooth ones.
- Sweaty hands: talc or let them dry.
- A coin roll that is too slow looks laboured; too fast looks frantic. Match music.

- [ ] 20 continuous rolls each hand.
