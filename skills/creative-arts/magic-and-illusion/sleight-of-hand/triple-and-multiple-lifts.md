# Triple and multiple lifts

**Goal:** show three or more cards as a single card, or push off a block of cards that
behaves as one.

## Principle
The same as a double lift, with a thicker packet. Thickness is the enemy: hide the edges by
keeping the packet low over the deck and by bevelling it slightly so the side view shows a
single edge. A **block push-off** moves several cards as one off the top, often used to
deal or display a "single" card.

## Method (outline)
1. Count the required number by feel with the right thumb at the inner end, then take a
   pinky break below them.
2. Lift as one in end grip, squaring hard at the ends.
3. Turn over close to the deck, matching your single-card turnover.
4. Block push-off: the left thumb pushes the block over as one; ends squared by the right
   fingers as it is taken.

## Common faults
- Edge thickness visible from the side.
- Cards buckle in the middle under pressure.
- Wrong count: drill counting by feel.

## Fine mechanics
Card College vol 2 covers triple lifts and block push-offs with routines that need them.

## Verify
- [ ] Triple turned as one with no visible thickness from the side.
- [ ] Block push-off of four cards dealt as one.
- [ ] Correct card count by feel twenty times in a row.
