# Biddle steal and Biddle count

**Goal:** count a packet so that it appears to contain one more card than it does, or
secretly get a card back under the packet - the method behind many "card vanishes from
the packet" effects.

## The Biddle grip
The right hand holds the packet from above, thumb at the inner end, fingers at the outer
end, face down. The left hand is below, palm up.

## The genuine peel
The left thumb peels the top card of the right-hand packet into the left hand. Repeat
for each card, counting aloud.

## The steal
1. Peel the first card into the left hand ("one").
2. As the right hand returns to peel the second card, the left thumb pushes the first card
   (now in the left hand) back up under the right-hand packet, where the right fingers
   and thumb secretly take it, while the left thumb simultaneously peels the next top card
   ("two").
3. Continue peeling normally.
The result: one card has been counted twice, so a packet of three counts as four.

## Uses
- Packet counts that show four cards when only three remain (one is already elsewhere,
  e.g. in a pocket or under a spectator's hand).
- The classic Biddle trick: a selected card vanishes from a counted packet.

## Rhythm and cover
- The steal happens under the cover of the right hand's packet.
- Count at an even pace; the steal must not create a pause.
- Keep the right packet angled down so the stolen card's edge is not visible.

## Verify
- [ ] Count three cards as four, ten times, indistinguishable from a genuine count on
  video.
- [ ] Audience in front sees no extra card movement.
