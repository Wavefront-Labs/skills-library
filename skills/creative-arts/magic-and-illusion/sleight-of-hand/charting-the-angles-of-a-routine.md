# Charting the angles of a routine

**Goal:** know, for every secret moment in a routine, from which directions it can be
seen, and stage the routine so no audience member stands there.

## Principle
Every sleight or gimmick has an **angle envelope**: front only, front and right, all round,
and so on, plus a height limit (seated spectators look up; a raised stage looks down).
A routine's angles are only as good as its worst moment. Charting turns this into a
decision: cut the move, change it, or control the audience layout.

## Method
1. List every secret moment in order (palm, break, load, steal, gimmick on view).
2. For each, test with a camera or helper from 0, 45, 90, 135 and 180 degrees, at
   standing and seated eye heights.
3. Draw a simple top-down chart: you at the centre, safe arcs marked for each moment.
4. The **intersection** of all safe arcs is the routine's playing position.
5. Decide how to enforce it: a wall behind you, a table, a semicircle of spectators,
   or replacing the weak move.

## Common faults
- Testing only from the front.
- Forgetting height (children, seated guests, balconies).
- Assuming a gimmick is angle-proof because the dealer said so.

## Verify
- [ ] Written angle chart exists for each routine.
- [ ] Venue position chosen to fit the narrowest envelope.
- [ ] Worst moment rechecked whenever the routine changes.
