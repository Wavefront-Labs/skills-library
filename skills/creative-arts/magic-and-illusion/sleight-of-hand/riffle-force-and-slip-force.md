# Riffle force and slip force

**Goal:** the spectator says "stop" anywhere while you riffle, and the card at that point is
apparently the one they stopped at - in fact your force card.

## Riffle force (with a break)
1. Force card is controlled to the middle with a **little finger break** above it.
2. Deck held in the left hand; the left thumb riffles down the outer left corner,
   towards you, as the spectator watches.
3. On "stop", the right hand lifts off **all the cards above the break**, not above the
   riffle point. The spectator assumes it is where they stopped.
4. The force card is now the top card of the left-hand packet. Have it taken or peeked.
Cover: riffle at a steady pace, and lift away cleanly without looking at the deck.

## Slip force (from the top)
1. Force card on top of the deck.
2. Riffle as above; on "stop", the right hand takes the upper portion **from above**.
3. As it moves away, the left fingers press on the top card of the upper portion, so the
   force card **slips** off it onto the lower packet.
4. The force card is now on top of the lower packet.
Cover: the upper packet moves away to the right with a slight upward motion; the slip
happens under it.

## Making it fair
- Riffle slowly enough that they can stop anywhere.
- If they say stop very early or late, it still works: that is the beauty of both.
- Avoid letting them see the face of the stopped-at cards; tilt the deck down.

## Angles
Both are fine for audiences in front; spectators to your left can see the slip if the
deck is tilted badly. Test with a camera at 45 degrees left.

## Verify
- [ ] Ten slips in front of a camera with no visible lateral movement.
- [ ] Spectator can stop at any point and you still deliver the card.
