# Deck switch and the cold deck

**Goal:** swap the deck in use for a prepared one (stacked, gaffed or duplicate) without
the audience realising a second deck exists.

## Principle
A deck switch is a **timing** sleight. The audience must believe the routine is over or
not yet begun, so they are not watching the deck. Common covers: putting the deck away,
reaching for a pen, putting on a jacket, or handing out an object. The "cold deck" is
the gambling term for a stacked deck switched into a game.

## Methods (outline)
- **Pocket switch:** the used deck goes into a pocket during a reason to reach into it;
  the hand comes out with the stacked deck.
- **Case switch:** a prepared deck in a gimmicked or double case.
- **Lap switch:** seated at a table, the deck drops to the lap and the replacement is
  brought up during an off-beat.

## Common faults
- Switching at a moment of attention.
- Visible bulk in pockets before the switch.
- Prepared deck looks different (wear, colour, case).

## Fine mechanics
Ortiz's Cardshark discusses switches and their timing; Card College vol 5 covers several
practical deck switches.

## Verify
- [ ] Switch done during an off-beat and unnoticed by a test audience.
- [ ] Both decks match in wear and back design.
- [ ] Clean pockets afterwards for a final display.
