# Deliberate practice schedule for sleights

**Goal:** learn sleights faster and more reliably than by repeating them in front of the
TV, using the principles of deliberate practice.

## Principle
Ericsson's research on expertise distinguishes **deliberate practice** (focused work on a
specific weakness, at the edge of ability, with immediate feedback) from simple repetition.
Sleights improve when you isolate the moment that fails, fix it slowly, and test it in
context.

## A working schedule
- **Daily, 15 to 30 minutes** of focused work beats a weekly marathon; hand skills
  consolidate between sessions.
- **One to three target moves** per block; rotate others in maintenance (a few clean
  repetitions).
- **Slow, correct, then up to speed.** Speed reveals what is not yet automatic.
- **Feedback:** camera review at the end of each session, a note of what to fix next.
- **Context:** finish each session by doing the move inside its routine with patter.
- **Weekly review:** compare clips, retire moves that are ready, add the next one.

## Common faults
- Practising what you can already do because it feels good.
- Practising while distracted, which trains distracted performance.
- No record, so no sense of progress.

## Verify
- [ ] Written practice log with dates, targets and notes.
- [ ] Each target move has a clear "ready" test (for example ten clean on video).
- [ ] Sessions end with in-context performance, not isolated reps.
