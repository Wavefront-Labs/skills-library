# Finger palm and thumb palm for coins

**Goal:** two of the most used coin holds: the finger palm (most natural looking) and the
thumb palm (fast, accessible for productions).

## Finger palm
- Coin rests on the base of the middle two fingers (across the second and third
  phalanges), held by slightly curling those fingers.
- The hand looks like a relaxed, half-closed hand. Most natural of all coin holds.
- Obtained easily: the coin is just allowed to rest in the curled fingers.
- Ideal for retention vanishes, French drop, and holding a coin while the hand rests on a
  table or holds another object.

## Thumb palm
- The coin is gripped edgewise in the crotch of the thumb (between the base of the thumb
  and the side of the first finger).
- Obtained by the first and second fingers carrying the coin to the thumb crotch and
  the thumb clipping it.
- Fast to get to and from the fingertips, so used for productions (e.g. the coin
  appears at the fingertips) and in Miser's Dream style work.
- Visible from the side if the hand opens too far; keep the fingers together.

## Transfers
- Finger palm to thumb palm: fingers curl, carry coin up to the thumb crotch.
- Thumb palm to fingertip production: first and second fingers curl in, take the coin
  and extend it.

## Naturalness checks
- With a coin finger-palmed, gesture and point - the hand should look the same as without.
- With a thumb palm, avoid spreading fingers wide.

## Verify
- [ ] Finger palm held while picking up and using a pen.
- [ ] Thumb palm to fingertip production in a single smooth motion.
