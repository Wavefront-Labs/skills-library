# Key card location

**Goal:** find a freely chosen card after the deck has been cut, without any sleight, by
using a known card positioned next to it.

## Principle
Complete cuts do not change the cyclic order of a deck. If a known card (the **key**) sits
directly above or below the selection, any number of complete cuts keeps them together.
Spread the deck, find the key, and the selection is beside it.

## Method (outline)
1. Glimpse the bottom card while squaring or shuffling.
2. Spectator places the selection on top; cut, completing the cut. The key now sits on the
   selection.
3. Spectator may give further complete cuts.
4. Spread through the faces "looking for vibrations"; the card after the key is theirs.

## Variations
- **Physical keys:** a short card, a crimped card or a thick double card can be found by
  feel and cut to. Useful for cutting directly to a selection.
- **Multiple keys** for several selections.
- **Spectator shuffles** destroy the relationship; only allow complete cuts.

## Common faults
- Glimpsing the bottom card in a way the audience notices.
- Letting a spectator do an overhand shuffle after the selection returns.
- Revealing the selection by looking straight at the key.

## Verify
- [ ] Selection found after five complete cuts by the spectator.
- [ ] Bottom-card glimpse done during a natural squaring or display.
- [ ] One physical key (crimp or short card) can be cut to blind.
