# Squaring and the casual display of the deck

**Goal:** square and show the deck so it looks ordinary while the squaring actions quietly
hide breaks, jogs and steps.

## Principle
Squaring is the most frequent action in card magic, so the audience stops seeing it. That
makes it the best cover for secret work, but only if you square the same way when nothing
is happening. A squaring action that appears only before a sleight becomes the tell.

## Method (outline)
- **Standard squaring:** right hand above the deck in end grip; right thumb and fingers
  square the ends while the left fingers square the sides. Done briefly and always the same.
- **Squaring with a break:** the left little finger holds a small break at the inner right
  corner; the right hand squares the ends over it, the back of the right hand covering the
  break from the front.
- **Hiding jogs and steps:** an injogged card is squared away during a natural squaring
  stroke, not with a separate push.
- **Casual display:** hold the deck face up in the left hand and let the faces be seen,
  or briefly spread a few cards between the hands. Never announce "an ordinary deck".

## Common faults
- Over-squaring: several fussy squares before a move.
- Squaring only when holding a break.
- Gripping hard: tension shows in the knuckles.

## Fine mechanics
Card College vol 1 treats squaring as a technique in its own right; Vernon's notes on
naturalness explain why consistency matters more than perfection.
