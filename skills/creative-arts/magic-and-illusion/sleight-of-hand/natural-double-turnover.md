# Natural double turnover

**Goal:** turn two cards face up as one and turn them back, so that the action looks
exactly like turning a single card.

## Principle
Decide how you turn **one** card, and make the double copy it. Vernon's teaching: if a
single card is turned casually by its edge, the double must be turned casually by its
edge. A beautifully precise double that differs from your single-card handling is a tell.

## Method (outline)
1. From the get-ready break, the right hand takes the two cards at their inner end in end
   grip (thumb at the back, fingers at the front), squaring them as one.
2. Turn them over sideways or end-over-end onto the deck, deliberately matching your
   single-card turnover in speed and grip.
3. Show the face, then turn the pair back down the same way.
4. Deal off the top (single) card.

## Common faults
- **Splay:** the two cards separate at the edges. Squeeze lightly at the ends and keep the
  turnover close to the deck.
- Gripping the double tighter than a single.
- Turning a single card differently elsewhere in the routine.

## Fine mechanics
Card College vol 1 gives several double turnovers; Vernon's handling is the model for the
"natural" style.

## Verify
- [ ] Single turnover and double turnover side by side on video look identical.
- [ ] No splay visible from the side at table height.
- [ ] Double done ten times in a row with no misfire.
