# The classic force

**Goal:** the spectator reaches into a spread deck, takes "any card", and gets the one you
wanted - with no apparent restriction at all.

## Set-up
- Bring the force card to a position near the centre of the deck and hold a **little
  finger break** above it (e.g. after a cut or control).
- Deck in the left hand, ready to spread from left to right hand.

## The action
1. Ask the spectator to take a card; start spreading at a relaxed, steady pace as their
   hand begins to move.
2. Watch the hand, not the cards. **Time** the spread so that the force card arrives under
   their fingertips exactly as they reach. The break lets you know where it is without
   looking.
3. As their fingers arrive, the force card is slightly exposed (a little more of it than
   its neighbours), and the spread slows to a near stop.
4. They take it. Close the spread naturally.

## Psychology
- Spread at waist height towards their dominant hand.
- Say "take any card" casually, not as a challenge.
- Do not look at the card you want; look at the person.
- People tend to take a card from the centre; that is why the force card is there.

## Outs (when they take another card)
Always have an out: switch to a different effect, use a multiple-out ending, or force
again with a different method later ("let's use two cards"). Never show disappointment.

## Practice
Practise with friends who are told to try to avoid it; it trains timing. Vernon and
others recommend learning it early and performing it often; confidence is half the method.

- [ ] 8 of 10 successful forces on cooperative but unbriefed people.
- [ ] A ready out for every effect that uses it.
