# The French drop

**Goal:** apparently take a coin from one hand with the other, while it stays behind in the
first hand - the oldest and most exposed coin vanish, which still fools when done with
proper timing and attention.

## The handling
1. Hold the coin horizontally by its edges between the left thumb (below) and the
   fingertips (above), left palm up and fingers slightly cupped, coin clearly visible.
2. The right hand approaches, thumb going under and fingers over the coin, as if to grip
   it. The right fingers hide the coin from the front.
3. At the moment of apparent grip, the left thumb releases: the coin **drops** into the left
   fingers (finger palm position).
4. The right hand closes as if holding the coin and moves away; the left hand drops
   naturally to the side or points at the right hand.

## What sells it
- **Eyes follow the right hand**: you look at the hand that "has" the coin. The audience
  follows your eyes.
- **The right hand moves away with intent** and continues to act as if holding a coin
  (it has weight, it is held carefully).
- **Delay**: open the right hand after a beat, not immediately.
- The left hand must relax, not freeze.

## Common faults
- Looking at the left hand.
- Right hand closing before it reaches the coin.
- Left hand jerking down after the drop (the "guilty hand").
- Doing it too fast.

## Compare with the real take
Practise the genuine take first, then make the French drop match it exactly.
