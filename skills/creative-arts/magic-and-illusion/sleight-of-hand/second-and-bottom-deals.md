# Bottom deal and second deal

**Goal:** apparently deal the top card while really dealing the second card or the bottom
card, matching your honest deal exactly.

## Principle
Both deals hide in the honest deal's rhythm. In the **second deal** the left thumb pushes
the top card slightly, the right fingers take the second card from beneath it, and the top
card is drawn back. In the **bottom deal** the right fingers take the bottom card while
the left thumb's push-off of the top card provides the visible motion. Sound and tempo
must match the top deal you have already established.

## Uses
- Magic: holding back a selection, switching a card, delivering a force.
- Gambling demonstrations: dealing yourself good hands (presented as exposure of cheats).

## Common faults
- Different sound (seconds "click").
- Thumb travel that differs from the honest deal.
- Practising too fast before the action is clean.

## Ethics
Gambling sleights demonstrated as entertainment are a respected genre. Using them at a
real game is cheating and illegal; Ortiz's books are explicit about this boundary.

## Fine mechanics
Erdnase is the classic text; Ortiz's Gambling Scams and Cardshark give modern guidance.
Expect years, not months.

## Verify
- [ ] Recorded deals of top and second sound and look identical.
- [ ] Twenty consecutive seconds without a misfire.
- [ ] Used only in performance or demonstration.
