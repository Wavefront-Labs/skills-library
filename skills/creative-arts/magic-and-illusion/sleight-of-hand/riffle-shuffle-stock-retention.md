# Riffle shuffle retaining top and bottom stock

**Goal:** riffle shuffle the deck on the table and keep a block of cards at the top or
bottom in exactly the order you set.

## Principle
In a table riffle the two halves interlace one card at a time. You control **which half
releases first and last**. Release the bottom stock's half first and it stays on the
bottom; let the top stock's half release last and its cards fall on top unmixed. The
middle mixes genuinely, which is why the shuffle looks honest.

## Method (outline)
1. Cut the deck so the stock is in a known half (top stock usually in the right half).
2. Begin the riffle by releasing several cards from the half holding the bottom stock.
3. Riffle the rest normally.
4. Hold back the top stock with the thumb and drop it last as a block.
5. Square by pushing the halves together; for a fuller look follow with a false cut.

## Common faults
- Releasing the held block too visibly: the last drop should sound like the rest.
- Uneven halves give away the cut position.
- Squaring that telescopes the halves with a separate push looks different from your
  honest shuffle; use the same squaring for both.

## Fine mechanics
Erdnase's blind riffles and Card College vol 2 give the exact releases and the full
"push-through" style of false riffle, a more advanced shuffle that does not mix at all.

## Verify
- [ ] Top ten and bottom five cards retained in order through two riffles.
- [ ] Sound and tempo match an honest riffle on recording.
- [ ] Combined with a false cut, a spectator sees only a normal shuffle.
