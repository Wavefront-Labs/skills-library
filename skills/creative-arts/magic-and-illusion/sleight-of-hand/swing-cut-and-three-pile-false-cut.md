# Swing cut and three-pile false cut

**Goal:** apparently cut the deck to mix it while keeping the full order, either in the
hands (swing cut) or on the table (three-pile cut).

## Principle
A cut that is really a reassembly in the original order. In the hands, the swing cut is
the basic honest cut that many controls later disguise. On the table, piles are put down
in an order that looks random but is picked up so the deck re-forms exactly.

## Method (outline)
- **Swing cut:** right hand holds the deck in end grip; right first finger swings the top
  half to the left, where the left hand takes it. The right hand's half is then placed on
  or under it. Used honestly or as a base for holding a break.
- **Three-pile false cut:** place three piles on the table in an order that looks like a
  normal cut, then reassemble them in the order that restores the deck. Practise one
  sequence until you never have to think about it.
- **Combine** with a false shuffle; a false cut alone looks thin.

## Common faults
- Hesitation over which pile to pick up: drill one sequence.
- Piles placed so neatly that the pattern is obvious.
- Different tempo from your honest cut.

## Fine mechanics
Card College vol 1 gives the swing cut and several table false cuts; many later false
cuts (Zarrow, Vernon) build on them.

## Verify
- [ ] Order retained through twenty three-pile cuts.
- [ ] Laypeople describe the cut as "she cut it into piles", not as a pattern.
- [ ] Swing cut runs at the same speed as honest cutting.
