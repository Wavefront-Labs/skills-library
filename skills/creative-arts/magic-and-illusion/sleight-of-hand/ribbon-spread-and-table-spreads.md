# Ribbon spread and table spreads

**Goal:** spread a deck along the table in an even line, turn it over as a wave, and use
the spread to show the cards are ordinary and mixed.

## Principle
A spread is both a flourish and a proof. An even ribbon tells the audience "a normal
shuffled deck" without words; it also exposes a stack or a reversed card, so know what a
spread will show before you do it.

## Method (outline)
- **Ribbon spread:** deck face down, held from above with the right hand, first finger
  resting lightly on the left long edge. Lay the deck down at the left and draw it to the
  right in one smooth stroke, the finger controlling how much each card overlaps.
- **Even overlap** comes from constant pressure and constant speed. Pressing harder at the
  end gives a clump; slowing gives a gap.
- **Spread turnover:** lift the first card at the left end and push it over; the rest
  follow in a wave. Control it with a card held at the right end, or reverse the wave.
- **Pressure fan/table fan:** for a selection, spread in a gentle arc so every card is
  offered.

## Surfaces and decks
A close-up mat (soft, slightly textured) is almost essential; bare wood or cloth breaks
spreads. A clean deck with good finish spreads well. Humidity and new glossy decks both
make clumps, so fan and break in the deck before the show.

## Common faults
- Cards bunch at the start because the deck is set down at an angle.
- Turnover stalls halfway: spread too thin or surface too rough.
- Spreading a stacked deck face up and revealing the order.

## Verify
- [ ] Ten consecutive spreads with even overlap across the whole deck.
- [ ] Spread turnover completes and reverses on your mat.
- [ ] You know which routines must not have a face-up spread.
