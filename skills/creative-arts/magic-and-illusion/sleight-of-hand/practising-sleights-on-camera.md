# Practising with a camera instead of a mirror

**Goal:** see your sleights as a spectator sees them, without the distortions a mirror
introduces.

## Principle
A mirror reverses the image and, worse, lets you watch yourself while you move, so you
unconsciously adjust your hands to look right and you train yourself to look down at
them. A camera records what an audience actually sees, from a fixed point, and lets you
review afterwards without the feedback loop.

## Method
1. Put the camera (a phone is fine) at the **eye-line of a real spectator**: seated across
   a table, standing at a walk-around distance, or the front row of a stage.
2. Add a second angle from the side (45 to 90 degrees) for flash checks.
3. Perform the move inside its routine, looking at where you would look in performance.
4. Review at normal speed first: does anything look odd? Then frame by frame to find
   exactly where it flashes.
5. Keep dated clips to compare progress.

## Use the mirror for
Learning the initial shape of a grip. Switch to camera once the move works mechanically.

## Common faults
- Camera too high (above the eye-line), hiding problems.
- Reviewing only in slow motion; magic is watched in real time.
- Practising the sleight alone, not in context.

## Verify
- [ ] Each new sleight reviewed from two camera angles before performance.
- [ ] Clips dated and compared over at least four weeks.
- [ ] Eyes in the recording look where the routine requires, not at the hands.
