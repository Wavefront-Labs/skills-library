# Overhand jog shuffle control

**Goal:** shuffle overhand while keeping a chosen card (or the top stock) under control,
using a jogged card as a marker.

## Principle
In an overhand shuffle each small packet is run off the top of the deck. If one card is
stepped slightly towards you (an **injog**) it marks a place in the deck; the shuffle can
then be "undone" at that point. The audience sees a loose, honest-looking shuffle; the jog
is hidden by the right hand.

## Method (outline)
1. To control the top card to the top: run it singly, injog the next card, shuffle off.
2. Push up on the injogged card to form a break, and shuffle off to the break, throwing
   the remaining block on top. The original top card is back on top.
3. To control a card returned to the middle: undercut half, injog the first card, shuffle
   off; cut at the jog to bring the selection to the top.
4. **Running cards** (one at a time with the left thumb) is the engine; practise it until
   it is fast and quiet.

## Common faults
- Jog too large, so it shows on the far side.
- Changing tempo at the jog: keep the shuffle's rhythm unbroken.
- Looking down at the jog.

## Fine mechanics
The Royal Road to Card Magic devotes its opening lessons to the overhand shuffle,
running cards and jog controls; Card College vol 1 adds refinements.

## Verify
- [ ] Top card retained through three shuffles, five times running.
- [ ] Jog invisible from the front and from the left side.
- [ ] Single-card runs of ten cards at a constant pace.
