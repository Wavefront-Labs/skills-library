# Double backers and double facers

**Goal:** use gaffed cards to achieve effects impossible with a normal deck, while
keeping them out of the spectators' hands.

## Common gaffs
- **Double backer**: backs on both sides. Shows a card that "has no face"; used for
  transformations (a card turns over to reveal its back again).
- **Double facer**: faces on both sides (e.g. 3C/QH). One card shows two identities;
  powerful for transformations and transpositions.
- **Blank face / blank back**: for "printing" and "blank deck" effects.
- Commercial **gaff decks** include a selection; the box tells you the contents.

## Using them
1. Plan a routine where the gaff does the hard work and the handling is simple.
2. Bring the gaff into play secretly (it sits at the top or bottom of a stack, or is
   palmed in), and remove it before spectators handle the cards.
3. **Clean up**: the deck must be examinable at the end; ditch the gaff into a pocket or
   switch it out.

## Gotchas
- Spectators grab cards. Control handling: never put a gaff in a spectator's hand.
- Double facers flash both sides when the card is turned carelessly; practise the turn.
- Wear: gaffs from different deck batches look slightly different; replace with the
  deck.

## Ethics
Gaffs are fine; letting spectators examine a gaffed deck while claiming it is normal is
the line most magicians do not cross.

## Verify
- [ ] Every routine with a gaff has a written clean-up step.
- [ ] No gaff visible on camera during turns.
