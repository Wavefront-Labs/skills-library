# The miser's dream and coin productions

**Goal:** apparently pluck coin after coin from the air, from spectators' clothing and
hair, dropping each into a bucket or hat with an audible clink.

## Principle
The routine combines a few real coins with the **sound** of many. Coins are produced from
a palm, dropped in, and secretly recovered or replaced from a load held inside the bucket.
Downs made the routine famous; it scales from close-up to stage because the clink carries
the effect.

## Structure (outline)
1. Show the bucket empty.
2. Produce a coin from the air; drop it in. Repeat with variation (elbow, knee, from a
   spectator's ear).
3. Build pace; produce coins in quick succession.
4. Climax: a shower of coins poured out of the bucket.

## Design choices
- Use coins that are large enough to be seen at distance.
- Involve one or two spectators for the "from their clothing" phases.
- Control the clink: one coin dropped from height sounds like one coin.

## Common faults
- Producing from the same place each time.
- Stiff palming hand.
- Ending with a whimper instead of a pour.

## Fine mechanics
Downs's Modern Coin Manipulation and Bobo's chapter teach the palms and the bucket work.

## Verify
- [ ] At least six productions, varied in location.
- [ ] Climax pour lands on a visible beat.
- [ ] Clink audible at the back of the room.
