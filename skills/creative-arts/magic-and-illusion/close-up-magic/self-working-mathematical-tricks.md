# Self-working mathematical tricks

**Goal:** perform effects that need no sleight, because a mathematical principle does the
work, while hiding that any arithmetic is involved.

## Principle
Many card and number principles guarantee an outcome regardless of the spectator's
choices.
- **Gilbreath principle:** riffle shuffling together the two halves of a deck set in alternating
  colours (so the two halves end in different colours) leaves every adjacent pair one
  red and one black.
- **Kruskal count:** two people following "count by the value of the card" chains through
  a dealt-out deck tend to land on the same card.
- **Parity and complementary sums:** card and dice totals that always sum to known values.
- **Number forces:** 1089, digital roots.

## Disguising the method
- Never let the audience perform visible arithmetic you could do yourself; they reverse
  engineer it.
- Add "free choices" that do not matter.
- Present a reason (a story, a test of intuition) rather than "a math trick".
- Combine with a small sleight or a key card so the principle is not the only explanation.

## Common faults
- Long, tedious counting procedures.
- Explaining the procedure as a rule.
- Kruskal-type probabilistic effects failing without an out.

## Verify
- [ ] Principle tested fifty times to know its failure rate.
- [ ] Presentation hides every counting step's purpose.
- [ ] Out prepared for probabilistic principles.
