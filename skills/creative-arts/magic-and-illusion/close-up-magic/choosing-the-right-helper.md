# Choosing the right helper

**Goal:** pick a spectator who will make the effect work and be enjoyable to watch.

## Principle
The helper is part of the show. A good helper is engaged, follows instructions, reacts
openly, and is liked by the group. A poor choice (drunk, hostile, shy to the point of
distress, or desperate to catch you) can sink a routine.

## Reading the group
- Watch reactions to the opener: who laughs, who leans in, who glances at friends.
- The person introducing you, or the host, is often a good first helper.
- Avoid: the person with arms folded and a smirk, the very drunk, someone on the phone,
  the friend who announces they "know this one".
- For effects that need a reaction, pick an expressive face.
- For effects that need precision (writing, remembering), pick someone attentive and
  sober.

## Matching to the effect
Rotate helpers within a set so more people feel involved. Use quieter people for simple
roles (holding, signing) rather than demanding ones.

## Asking
Ask directly and kindly: "Could you help me? You look like you have good hands for this."
Accept a refusal at once and choose someone else.

## Common faults
- Choosing the most attractive or loudest person by default.
- Insisting after a refusal.
- Using the same helper for everything.

## Verify
- [ ] Selection criteria noted per effect.
- [ ] At least two different helpers used in a set.
- [ ] Refusals accepted gracefully every time.
