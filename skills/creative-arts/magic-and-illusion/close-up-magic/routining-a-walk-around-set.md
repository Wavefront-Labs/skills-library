# Routining a walk-around set — strong, resettable, angle-proof

**Goal:** a close-up set you can perform to a standing group of 3–8 people at an event,
repeat 20+ times in an evening, and reset in seconds between groups.

## 1. Set structure (6–10 minutes per group)
1. **Opener (≤60 s):** quick, visual, needs no explanation and no volunteer — e.g. a
   sponge-ball or coin production, a signed card to the top. Its job is permission:
   it tells the group "this is worth stopping your conversation for".
2. **Middle (2–3 effects):** the interactive pieces — a chosen card, a borrowed object.
   Vary the *kind* of effect (vanish, transformation, prediction) so it doesn't feel
   like the same trick three times. Ortiz's point: variety of effect, not of prop.
3. **Closer:** your strongest, ideally ending with something the spectators can keep
   or a visual "impossible object" moment (a card in a sealed place, a final load).
   Leave on the applause line; don't add "one more".

## 2. Pocket management and reset
- Every prop has **one fixed pocket**. Write a pocket map and never deviate.
- Prefer effects that **end clean** (nothing left to hide) or **reset in-pocket**.
- Carry duplicates for anything given away or destroyed (signed cards, torn items).
  Count how many you need: groups per hour × hours, plus 20%.
- Between groups, reset out of sight (step away 5 m, back to the room) — never in front
  of the next group.

## 3. Approaching a group
- Approach groups that are **standing, not mid-meal, not in intense conversation**.
  At weddings, avoid the couple's family during speeches or photos.
- Open with a one-line introduction ("Hi, I'm the magician for tonight — can I show you
  something quick?"), then go straight into the opener. Don't ask permission at length.
- Position yourself so the group forms a **semicircle in front of you**; take one step
  back if people drift to your sides. A wall or pillar behind you protects angles.

## 4. Spectator management
- Choose volunteers who are engaged and sober-ish; avoid the person who is clearly
  testing you for the key prop handling.
- **Give hands something to do:** a spectator holding the deck can't grab your hands.
- With a heckler: stay friendly, give them a job (hold something, be the "witness"),
  or switch to an effect where they can't interfere. Never humiliate a spectator — the
  rest of the group sides with them.
- Learn and use names; refer back to them in the closer.

## 5. Table work variations
Seated tables shift angles: spectators are lower and see more of your lap and palm.
Avoid lapping, tighten palm angles, and work at table height or above.

## Gotchas
- **Noise:** live bands drown patter; choose visual effects for loud rooms.
- **Lighting:** dim rooms hide colour (red/black suits) — use high-contrast props.
- **Borrowed items:** return them immediately and visibly; lost jewellery ends gigs.
- Uncertain: set length norms vary by market and booker; 6–10 min is a common
  working range, not a rule.

## Verify
- [ ] Opener needs no explanation and runs under one minute.
- [ ] Every prop has a fixed pocket; full reset takes under 30 seconds.
- [ ] Duplicates for consumables cover the full booking plus margin.
- [ ] Set run five times in a row with a timer stays within your target length.
- [ ] Filmed from both sides of a semicircle, no secret action shows.
