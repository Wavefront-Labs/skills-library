# Shells and gimmicked coins

**Goal:** use specially made coins to achieve effects no sleight alone can, while hiding
that any special coin exists.

## Principle
A gimmicked coin does one thing perfectly, and is handled so it is never examined.
- **Expanded shell:** a hollow coin face that fits over a real coin; two coins can be shown
  as one, or a coin "vanishes" when the shell is lifted.
- **Folding coin:** cut and hinged with elastic; it can pass through a bottle neck.
- **Magnetic and steel-cored coins:** for use with hidden magnets.
- **Coin-through-hand / flipper coins** and others, each for one effect.

## Handling principles
- Switch the gimmick in and out at the start and end, so spectators can examine the real
  coin.
- Show only the safe side; a shell's back is open.
- Keep shells and their matching coins together; a mismatched date or wear shows.

## Buying
Machined shells vary greatly in quality. Buy from reputable makers, matching your coin
type exactly (US half dollar, a current local coin).

## Common faults
- Letting a gimmick be handled.
- Clinking: shells sound different from solid coins.
- Mismatched wear between the shell and the coin.

## Verify
- [ ] Gimmick never handled by a spectator.
- [ ] Switch in and out at clean moments.
- [ ] Shell and coin match on close view.
