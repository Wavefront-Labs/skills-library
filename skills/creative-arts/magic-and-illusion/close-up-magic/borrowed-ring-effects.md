# Borrowed ring effects

**Goal:** a borrowed finger ring passes onto a string, a pencil or a key, or travels to a
sealed place.

## Principle
Rings work because they are personal and cannot be duplicated. Classic methods include:
- **Ring on string:** a duplicate or a loop of string threaded so the ring appears to
  penetrate it; or a release move so the ring falls off and on.
- **Ring on pencil / key:** the ring is retained in the hand while the hand slides along
  the object, then let go to spin onto it.
- **Ring flight:** the ring vanishes and appears on a keychain, using a reel or a
  switch (commercial versions exist).

## Safety for the ring
- Ask the owner first, and never take wedding or engagement rings unless offered freely.
- Hold the ring in view; avoid tossing or dropping it on hard floors.
- Return it to the owner's hand, not the table.

## Common faults
- Using a ring that is too large or small for the method.
- Losing track of the ring in a crowd.
- Damaging delicate rings with a reel clip.

## Verify
- [ ] Method works with rings from small to large.
- [ ] Ring always returned to the owner's hand.
- [ ] Owner's consent asked explicitly.
