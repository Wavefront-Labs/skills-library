# Seated angles and table height

**Goal:** know exactly what each spectator can see when you perform seated, and arrange or
choose material accordingly.

## Seating arrangements
- **Across the table** (ideal): spectators opposite you, slightly lower eye line; lapping
  and many palms are safe.
- **Beside you**: they can see your lap and the sides of your hands. Avoid lapping;
  watch palms.
- **Surrounded** (dinner party): treat as walk-around; use angle-proof material.

## Table and chair height
- Standard dining tables (around 72-76 cm) suit seated close-up.
- If the spectators' eyes are high above the table (they stand, you sit), they look down
  into your hands - problematic for palms and lapping.
- Low coffee tables: everything is seen from above; use material that works on the open
  palm.
- Your own chair: high enough that your forearms rest comfortably on the table edge.

## Checking angles
1. Set up the room as it will be.
2. Film from each spectator's seat at their eye height.
3. Run the routine; note every flash.
4. Change seating, material or handling.

## Gotchas
- Mirrors and glass behind spectators.
- People getting up and walking behind you mid-trick: have an angle-proof filler.

## Verify
- [ ] Filmed from every seat at eye height.
- [ ] Material choice matches the seating plan.
