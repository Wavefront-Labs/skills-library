# Sponge ball routine

**Goal:** sponge balls multiply in a spectator's own closed hand, finishing with a larger
load.

## Principle
Sponge balls compress to almost nothing, so two can be held as one, and a palmed ball can
be secretly added to a spectator's hand as you place a visible ball there. The magic
happens **in their hand**, which makes it strong. Goshman built a career on this.

## Structure (outline)
1. Show one ball, then two. Simple vanishes and reappearances establish the props.
2. Place a ball in the spectator's hand (secretly adding a second); one goes into your
   pocket; they open their hand and find two.
3. Repeat with escalation (three in their hand).
4. Climax: a large number of balls, or a different-shaped sponge, appears from their hand.

## Handling principles
- Finger palm the extra ball naturally; red balls on a dark mat show less.
- Squeeze both balls together as you press them into the hand.
- Close the spectator's hand for them, firmly and politely.

## Common faults
- Revealing two balls when the hand is opened too early.
- Rushing the phases.
- No climax after the multiplication.

## Verify
- [ ] Two balls added as one, unnoticed by test spectators.
- [ ] Each phase escalates.
- [ ] Final load is a different scale or kind.
