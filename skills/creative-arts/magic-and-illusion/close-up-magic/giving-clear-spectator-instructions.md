# Giving clear instructions

**Goal:** spectators do exactly what you need, first time, without feeling ordered about.

## Principle
Confused spectators ruin effects: they take the wrong card, shuffle when you meant cut, or
show their card to the wrong person. Most confusion is created by vague or compound
instructions. Clear instructions are short, one action at a time, and often **shown** as
well as said.

## Techniques
1. **One action per sentence.** "Take a card." Pause. "Show it to your friends." Pause.
2. **Show with a gesture** while you say it (hold out the deck, mime the cut).
3. **Name the spectator** you are addressing.
4. **Use their language**, not magician's jargon ("put it back anywhere", not "replace the
   selection").
5. **Check:** "Got it? Great."
6. **Script every instruction** in the routine and test it on laypeople; replace any that
   cause hesitation.

## Polite authority
Use a warm but definite tone. "Would you mind taking a card?" is fine; a long question is
not.

## Common faults
- Two instructions in one sentence.
- Asking a spectator to remember and also to do something simultaneously.
- Instruction given while they are looking away.

## Verify
- [ ] Every instruction in the script is one action.
- [ ] Test spectators follow each without hesitation.
- [ ] Gesture accompanies each physical instruction.
