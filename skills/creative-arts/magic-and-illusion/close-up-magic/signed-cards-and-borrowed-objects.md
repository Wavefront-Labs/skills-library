# Having cards signed and borrowing objects

**Goal:** use signatures and borrowed items to make effects unarguable, without losing,
damaging or embarrassing anything.

## Principle
A signed card cannot be a duplicate; a borrowed ring cannot be a prop. Both raise
**conviction** (Ortiz). The cost is logistics: pens, ink drying, the risk of loss, and
people's attachment to their property.

## Signed cards
- Carry a fine permanent marker; uncap it for them.
- Ask them to sign across the face, large enough to read.
- Allow drying time before any palm or slide (fast-dry ink helps).
- Plan whether they keep the card at the end (a souvenir is a strong finish).

## Borrowed objects
- Ask for something specific ("a ring, any ring you are comfortable lending").
- Avoid heirlooms and very valuable items; take the second offer if needed.
- Keep the object in view as much as possible, and say where it is when it is not.
- **Return it visibly** and say thank you. Never pocket it, even briefly, without saying
  so.

## Common faults
- Smudged signatures.
- Forgetting to return an item before moving on.
- Damaging a note or ring with a gimmick.

## Verify
- [ ] Marker tested on your deck stock for drying time.
- [ ] Return of every borrowed item scripted.
- [ ] No borrowed item leaves the spectator's sight without explanation.
