# Invisible thread and reels

**Goal:** make small objects float, move or jump using thread the audience cannot see.

## Thread types
- **Invisible thread** (very fine nylon or polyester filament, often sold "split" from a
  multi-strand thread): nearly invisible at close range under the right conditions.
- **Loops**: small elastic thread loops worn on the wrist for close-up animation.
- **ITR (invisible thread reel)**: a spring-loaded reel worn in clothing; the thread pulls
  out and retracts.
- Heavier thread for stage (black thread against black background).

## Invisibility conditions
- **Background**: thread disappears against busy or dark backgrounds; it shows against
  plain light backgrounds and in backlight.
- **Lighting**: avoid strong side or back light, which makes thread glint.
- **Distance**: close-up thread works at arm's length but not in a spectator's hands.

## Handling
- Anchor points: a button, a ring, a pin under the table edge, or an assistant offstage
  (stage).
- Keep slack controlled; sudden tension snaps fine thread.
- Practise with a mirror and a camera under performance lighting.

## Breaks
- Fine thread breaks. Carry spare loops and a pre-loaded spare reel.
- Have an out: if the thread breaks, switch to a different effect smoothly.

## Gotchas
- Static and humidity make thread tangle; store loops in a small case.
- Spectators touching the object can feel the thread; control handling.

## Verify
- [ ] Thread invisible on camera under show lighting and background.
- [ ] Spare thread and an out effect ready.
