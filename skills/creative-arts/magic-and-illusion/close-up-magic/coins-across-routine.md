# Coins across

**Goal:** four coins visibly travel one at a time from one closed hand to the other.

## Principle
Each passage is a simple switch of where one coin really is. The work is a combination of
palms (classic or finger palm), a hidden extra coin or a shell in some versions, and
counting the coins in each hand openly at each phase so the audience tracks the totals.
Clarity is everything: the audience must always know how many coins are in each hand.

## Structure (outline)
1. Show four coins. Establish the hands as separate and empty apart from the coins.
2. Close the hands. One coin "travels"; open and count three and one.
3. Repeat, varying the manner (slow, near the spectator's hand, fast).
4. Final coin passes cleanly, ideally with the hands visibly empty at the end.

## Design choices
- **Methods:** extra coin, shell, palms and a retention move; choose by your angles.
- **Variety:** make each passage feel different (Ortiz's point on repetition).
- **Kicker or clean-up:** finish with nothing to hide.

## Common faults
- Hands too close together: the audience suspects a simple pass between them.
- Confusing counts.
- Tension in the palming hand.

## Fine mechanics
Bobo's chapter on coins across and Roth's Expert Coin Magic give the classic routines and
handlings.

## Verify
- [ ] Coin totals are clear at every phase.
- [ ] Each passage looks different from the last.
- [ ] Hands can be shown empty at the end.
