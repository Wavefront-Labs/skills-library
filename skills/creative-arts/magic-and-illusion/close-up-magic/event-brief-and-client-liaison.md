# Event brief and client liaison

**Goal:** arrive at an event knowing exactly what the client wants, when and where you are
working, and who to speak to, so nothing surprises you on the night.

## Principle
Most problems at close-up gigs (wrong timing, no access, playing during speeches) come
from a vague brief. A written brief, confirmed back to the client, turns expectations into
a shared plan.

## Questions to ask
- **Event:** type, venue, room layout, number of guests, age range, language.
- **Timings:** arrival, start, breaks, finish, key moments to avoid (speeches, first
  dance, photos, dinner service).
- **Where:** drinks reception, tables between courses, specific VIPs to see.
- **Restrictions:** no fire, no cards (some religious groups), dress code, brand
  requirements at corporate events.
- **Logistics:** parking, load-in, a place to change and reset, food and water.
- **Contact on the night:** name and phone of the person who can make decisions.

## Confirming
Send the brief back in writing with the contract. Re-confirm a week before. Bring a copy.

## On the night
Find the contact on arrival; confirm timings again. Note any changes.

## Common faults
- Relying on the booker, who is not at the event.
- Not asking what to avoid.
- No written confirmation.

## Verify
- [ ] Written brief with timings and contact sent and acknowledged.
- [ ] Moments to avoid listed.
- [ ] Contact found within ten minutes of arrival.
