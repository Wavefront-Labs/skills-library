# Close-up mat and lapping

**Goal:** use the seated table position - mat, lap and table edge - to vanish and load
objects in ways impossible standing.

## The close-up mat
- Soft surface (neoprene or felt over foam), typically around 30 x 40 cm or larger.
- Makes cards and coins easy to pick up, quiets sounds, defines the performing area.
- Dark, plain colours hide threads and contrast with cards and coins.

## Lapping
Secretly dropping an object off the table edge into your lap.
1. Sit close to the table; knees together so the lap catches objects (or use a
   napkin / lap servante).
2. An object is brought to the table edge naturally (e.g. a coin slid back towards you
   while apparently being picked up).
3. It is released over the edge into the lap while the hand continues the action of
   taking it.
4. Retrieval: later, a hand rests at the table edge and secretly picks up the object from
   the lap (e.g. for a reappearance or final load).
Slydini built entire routines on lapping with large, open movements and strong
misdirection.

## Servantes
A small shelf or bag hung at the back edge of the table, used similarly to lapping. Not
possible when spectators sit around the table.

## Timing
Lapping happens on the off-beat, when attention is on the other hand or a revealed
object. Movement towards the edge must look motivated.

## When not to lap
- Spectators beside you can see your lap.
- Low tables or tablecloths that drape (objects get caught).
- Standing or walk-around performances.

## Verify
- [ ] Lapping a coin invisible to spectators across the table on video.
- [ ] Retrieval from the lap with a natural hand position.
