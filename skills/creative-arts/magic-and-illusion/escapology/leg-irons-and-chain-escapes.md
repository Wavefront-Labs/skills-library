# Leg irons and chains

**Goal:** an escape from chains and leg irons that looks severe but is planned for a
reliable, safe release.

## Principle
Chains look formidable; the weak points are the **locks** and the **way the chain is
wrapped**. Burling Hull's book documents classic chain releases in which slack is gained
during the wrapping and the chain is slipped rather than unlocked. Leg irons are cuffs for
ankles with longer chains and the same mechanism.

## Planning
1. Choose the chain and locks yourself where possible.
2. Rehearse the wrapping sequence with volunteers so you can gain slack naturally.
3. Plan the release order: hands first, then body chain, then legs.
4. Stand only when the legs are free.

## Safety
- Falls are the main hazard; perform on a soft surface or seated when ankles are chained.
- No chain around the neck.
- Safety officer holds keys and bolt cutters.

## Common faults
- Volunteers wrapping too tight around joints.
- Trying to walk with leg irons on.

## Verify
- [ ] Release sequence rehearsed fifty times.
- [ ] Bolt cutters and keys at the safety position.
- [ ] Wrapping script given to volunteers.
