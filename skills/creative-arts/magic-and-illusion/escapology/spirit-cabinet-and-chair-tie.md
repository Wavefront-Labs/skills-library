# Spirit cabinet and chair tie

**Goal:** a performer is tied to a chair inside a cabinet; when the curtain closes,
"spirit" phenomena occur, and the performer is found still tied when it opens.

## Principle
The Davenport brothers made the spirit cabinet famous in the 1860s. The performer
releases a hand (or more) during the tie, produces phenomena, and returns to the tied
position before the curtain opens. Houdini exposed the method in A Magician Among the
Spirits as part of his campaign against fraudulent mediums.

## Staging
- Committee tie with a fixed rope length.
- Short curtain closures for each phenomenon (bells ringing, instruments played, objects
  thrown).
- Open the curtain immediately after each phenomenon to show the performer tied.
- Build to a final phenomenon, then release.

## Framing
Present as theatre or as a demonstration of how fraudulent mediums worked. Do not claim
genuine spirit contact.

## Common faults
- Curtain closures too long.
- Visible slack when the curtain opens.

## Verify
- [ ] Release and re-tie timed under the curtain duration.
- [ ] Framing script avoids claims of spirit contact.
- [ ] Committee procedure rehearsed.
