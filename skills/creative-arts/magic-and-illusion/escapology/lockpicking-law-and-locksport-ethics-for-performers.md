# Lockpicking law and locksport ethics for performers

**Goal:** carry and use picks, shims and keys for an escape act without legal trouble or
harming anyone's security.

## Core ethics (locksport rules)
1. Only pick or bypass locks you own, or have explicit permission to open.
2. Never pick a lock that is in use (a door, a bike lock someone relies on).
3. Do not open locks for strangers who claim they are theirs.

## Law (varies by country and state)
- In many jurisdictions, owning picks is legal, but carrying them **with intent** to commit
  a crime is an offence (e.g. "going equipped" in the UK, Theft Act 1968 s.25).
- Some US states treat possession of picks as prima facie evidence of intent unless you
  are a locksmith - check where you live and perform.
- Airlines and borders: carry tools in checked luggage with act props; be ready to explain.

## Performance practice
- Store picks and shims with the act kit, not on your person in daily life.
- Keep a letter or contract showing you are a performer if touring.
- In challenge escapes, accept locks under clear conditions; inspect them before the show
  (see challenge locks).

## What not to do
- Do not publish bypass methods for current security products to the public.
- Do not demonstrate opening audience members' locks on their property.

## Verify
- [ ] Local law on picks checked for each touring country/state.
- [ ] Tools stored with the act kit and documented.
