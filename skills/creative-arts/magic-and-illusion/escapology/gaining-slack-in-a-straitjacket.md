# Gaining slack when strapped in

**Goal:** be strapped into a straitjacket by volunteers while securing enough slack to
escape.

## Principle
The straitjacket escape is largely won during the strapping. By holding the body and arms
in positions that take up space (expanded chest, arms held slightly away, shoulders
raised) the escapologist gains slack when relaxing afterwards. Gibson's account of Houdini's
methods describes this and the arm positions that make the later release possible.

## Method (outline)
1. Put on the jacket with arms crossed in front; the arm on top is the one to free first.
2. As the volunteers strap, breathe in and expand the chest; raise the shoulders slightly.
3. Hold the arms a little away from the body; grip the sleeve material to gain room.
4. Relax after strapping to feel the slack.

## Volunteer handling
- Brief volunteers to pull straps firmly but never around the neck.
- Watch the tension yourself; your safety officer watches the volunteers.

## Common faults
- Relaxing during strapping.
- Jackets that are too small for your build.

## Verify
- [ ] Slack measurable when relaxed after strapping.
- [ ] Volunteer briefing scripted.
- [ ] Jacket size correct for your build.
