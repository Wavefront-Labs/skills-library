# Shimming handcuffs

**Goal:** understand the shim principle as it applies to ratchet handcuffs, so you can
choose and prepare your own performance cuffs safely.

## Principle
A ratchet cuff closes by a toothed arm passing a spring-loaded pawl. A thin flat shim
inserted between the teeth and the pawl lifts it clear, letting the arm open. This is a
well-known weakness; the **double lock** blocks the pawl and defeats simple shimming,
which is why it exists. Ollam describes shimming as a general bypass class; Houdini
documented its period equivalents.

## Performer practice
- Use **your own cuffs**, bought for the act, single-locked by design where the routine
  allows.
- Practise only on your own equipment.
- Shim concealment and the escape choreography come from the published sources; learn
  them there and rehearse with your safety officer.

## Legal and ethical rules
- Never practise on, or demonstrate against, cuffs in service use.
- Carrying shims or cuff keys may be restricted in some jurisdictions; check local law
  (see the lockpicking law skill).

## Common faults
- Relying on the shim with double-locked cuffs.
- Damaging the ratchet teeth with heavy shims.
