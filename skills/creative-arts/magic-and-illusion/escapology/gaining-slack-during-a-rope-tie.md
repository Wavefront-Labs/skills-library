# Gaining slack during the tie

**Goal:** let volunteers tie you firmly with rope while keeping enough slack to escape.

## Principle
Rope escapes are mostly won during the tying. Slack is gained by **taking up space**
(tensed muscles, expanded chest, wrists held slightly apart or angled) and relaxing
afterwards. Hull's classic book documents positions and crossings that leave usable slack
even when the tie looks tight.

## Method (outline)
1. Offer wrists crossed or angled so a relaxed position leaves room.
2. Tense forearms and expand the chest while ropes are pulled.
3. Turn the body subtly as rope is wound to create loose turns.
4. Relax once tying is done; feel for the loosest point.

## Volunteer briefing
- Ask for firm but not painful ties; never near the neck.
- Safety officer watches knots near joints and circulation.

## Common faults
- Relaxing during tying.
- Accepting rope that is too thin and cuts in.

## Verify
- [ ] Slack measurable after relaxation in each standard tie.
- [ ] Volunteer briefing scripted.
- [ ] Rope type tested for comfort and slip.
