# Identifying cuff types and mechanisms

**Goal:** recognise the handcuffs offered for an escape, how they lock, and what that means
for the act's safety and method.

## Principle
Houdini's Handcuff Secrets catalogued the cuffs of his era; the principle remains that
every cuff design has a **mechanism** (ratchet, pawl, lock) and a **key type**, and the
escape plan depends on identifying both. Modern cuffs are chain-linked, hinged or rigid;
most have a **double lock** that stops the ratchet tightening further.

## Inspection
- Type: chain, hinged, rigid; maker and model.
- Lock: single or double locked; keyhole position.
- Condition: worn ratchets, bent bows, damage.
- Tightness: the double lock protects circulation; a cuff left single-locked can tighten
  painfully.

## Safety
- Always have the matching key on your safety officer.
- Never allow cuffs to be applied over the wrist bones tightly; nerve damage from cuffs is
  well documented.
- Refuse cuffs you cannot identify (see the challenge locks skill).

## Common faults
- Accepting unfamiliar cuffs in front of an audience.
- Cuffs left single-locked.

- [ ] Inspection checklist for every cuff in the act.
- [ ] Matching keys held by the safety officer.
- [ ] Double lock engaged on every application.
