# Padlock shims and lock bypass for escape acts

**Goal:** understand why many padlocks can be opened without the key, so an escape artist
can choose, prepare and practise with their **own** padlocks - and know which locks a
challenger might bring that will not open that way.

## The principle
Many inexpensive padlocks hold the shackle with a **spring-loaded latch** that is pushed
back when the shackle is pressed in. A thin shim slipped down beside the shackle into
the body can push the latch back, releasing the shackle without touching the lock
cylinder. This is a bypass, not picking.

## Locks that resist shimming
- Padlocks with **ball-bearing locking** (the shackle is held by balls driven by the
  cylinder) - common in better padlocks.
- Shrouded or close-tolerance shackles.
- These need picking or the key, and are what an experienced challenger may bring.

## In the act
- Use padlocks you own and have prepared and tested.
- Shims are concealed on the body or in props.
- Practise until release is fast and silent under a cloth or in a cabinet.

## Legal and ethical
- Only open locks you own or have explicit permission to open.
- Carrying shims and picks is legal in many places but restricted in some, especially
  with intent; know your local law.
- Do not teach bypass of security locks to the public; escapology is entertainment.

## Verify
- [ ] Act padlocks tested 20 times each.
- [ ] Challenge-lock policy written (which locks you accept).
