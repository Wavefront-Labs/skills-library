# Pin-tumbler picking for escapologists

**Goal:** a working understanding of pin-tumbler picking, practised as locksport on your own
locks, so escape acts can include genuine picking and you can assess challenge locks.

## How the lock works
A plug (that turns with the key) sits inside a housing. Pin stacks (key pins below,
driver pins above, springs on top) cross the gap between plug and housing (the shear
line). The right key lifts every stack so the gap between key pin and driver pin aligns
with the shear line, letting the plug turn.

## Single-pin picking (SPP)
- A **tension wrench** applies light rotational force to the plug.
- Because of manufacturing tolerances, one pin "binds" first. Lift it with a **pick**
  until it sets at the shear line (a slight click and plug movement).
- Find the next binding pin; repeat until all are set and the plug turns.
- Light tension is the most common lesson; too much tension prevents pins from setting.

## Practice progression
1. Clear acrylic practice lock to see the pins.
2. Repinnable practice locks with 2, then 3, 4, 5 pins.
3. Common padlocks you own.
4. Locks with security pins (spools, serrated) - harder; feel "false sets".

## For the act
Genuine picking is slow and undramatic for audiences; escapologists usually combine it
with other methods and present it inside a cabinet or with time pressure.

## Legal ground rules
- Two locksport rules: only pick locks you own; never pick locks in use.
- Know local laws on possessing picks.

- [ ] Opened a 5-pin practice lock 10 times.
