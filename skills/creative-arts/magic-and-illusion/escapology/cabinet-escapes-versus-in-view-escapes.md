# Cabinet escapes versus in-view escapes

**Goal:** decide when the escape happens hidden (behind a curtain, inside a cabinet or
"ditty box") and when in full view, and stage each well.

## Cabinet / screen escapes
- **Hides the method** (tools, keys, techniques that look unimpressive).
- **Builds suspense** through time and sound: the audience waits and imagines.
- Risk: audiences suspect anything is possible behind a curtain. Counter with
  examination, committee, and a reveal still partly restrained.
- Houdini's early handcuff escapes were done in a cabinet; when audiences grew
  suspicious, he moved key escapes into view.

## In-view escapes
- The **struggle is the show**: straitjackets (Houdini found the in-view straitjacket far
  more popular than the hidden one), rope ties, suspended escapes.
- Requires methods that look like effort, not tricks.
- Audiences credit strength and flexibility.

## Mixed approach
- Begin in view to show the restraint is real, go behind a screen for the critical moment,
  reappear partly free and finish in view.
- Or show a time-limited in-view struggle on a big screen.

## Staging details
- Cabinet: small, simple, examined; lit so the audience sees shadows/movement if wanted.
- In-view: elevated position, lighting from front, music that builds, clock visible.

## Verify
- [ ] Each escape has a reason for its hidden/in-view choice.
- [ ] Safety access to the performer maintained even in a cabinet.
