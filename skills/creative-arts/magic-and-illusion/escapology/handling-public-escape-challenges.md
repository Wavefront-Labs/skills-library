# Handling public escape challenges

**Goal:** turn a challenge from a local business, police force or audience member into a
memorable show moment, without losing control of safety.

## Issuing or accepting
- **Issued challenge**: you invite local firms or individuals to bring a restraint
  (straitjacket from a hospital, packing crate from a factory, handcuffs from police).
- **Accepted challenge**: someone challenges you; accept on your published terms.

## Written terms
- What may be brought, in what condition, with key or method of release.
- Inspection rights before the show (see challenge-lock inspection).
- Time limit, abort right, safety officer authority.
- Media use: photos and names.

## The committee
- A few audience members or representatives to inspect and witness.
- Brief them on their role; give them something to do (check knots, hold the key).
- Choose people who will be fair and audible.

## Story
- The challenger's story ("our factory's crate has never been opened") is the drama.
- Let the challenger speak; introduce them with respect.
- Build tension with the time and visible struggle (see pacing).

## Protecting reputation
- If a challenge item is refused on safety grounds, explain clearly and offer an
  alternative; refusing is better than an injury.
- If the escape fails, end safely and with grace; history remembers grace.

## Verify
- [ ] Written terms agreed with the challenger before announcing.
- [ ] Committee briefed.
- [ ] Safety officer with authority to stop.
