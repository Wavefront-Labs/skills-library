# Suspension safety for upside-down escapes

**Goal:** hang upside down for an escape with engineering, timing and rescue planning that
keep the real risk low.

## Principle
Inversion stresses the body: blood pressure rises in the head, breathing is harder, and
disorientation sets in. The suspension system is life-safety equipment. **Inverted hang
time** must be limited and the performer must be lowerable at any moment.

## Requirements
- **Rigging** designed and installed by a qualified rigger; load-rated hardware.
- **Ankle harness** padded and rated; inspected before each use.
- **Hang time limit** agreed with medical advice; the safety officer calls it.
- **Lowering plan:** hoist or crane operator ready; manual backup.
- **Weather:** outdoors, wind limits for cranes; abort in high wind.
- **Rescue drill** before each performance day.

## Common faults
- Crane operator distracted by the crowd.
- Using climbing equipment not rated for the application.
- Skipping the rescue drill.

## Verify
- [ ] Rigging documented and inspected.
- [ ] Hang time limit set and enforced.
- [ ] Lowering plan rehearsed with the operator.
