# Barnum statements

**Goal:** understand and use general statements that most people accept as accurate
descriptions of themselves, in an entertaining character reading.

## Principle
In 1949 Bertram Forer gave students a "personal" personality profile that was actually the
same for all of them; they rated it highly accurate. This **Forer effect** (also called
the Barnum effect) shows people accept vague, positive, double-sided statements as
personal. Rowland catalogues the forms: the **Barnum statement** ("you have a great need
for other people to like you"), the **Rainbow ruse** (both sides of a trait), the **fine
flattery**.

## Writing them
- Make them positive and double-sided.
- Add specificity of tone, not of content ("you sometimes put others first, even when it
  costs you").
- Deliver with warmth and pauses for agreement.

## Ethics
- Present readings as entertainment.
- Avoid health, death and money advice.
- Never use them privately on vulnerable people.

## Common faults
- Overusing generic statements, which becomes obvious.
- Delivering without watching the response.

## Verify
- [ ] Ten statements written for a character.
- [ ] Each tested for positivity and double-sidedness.
- [ ] Disclaimer present in the reading's framing.
