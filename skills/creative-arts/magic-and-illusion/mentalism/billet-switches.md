# Billet switches

**Goal:** exchange the spectator's folded billet for an identical-looking blank or dummy
billet, so you can read theirs at leisure while they hold the dummy.

## Preparation
- Use billets cut to the same size and paper; fold the dummy in the same way you will
  instruct the spectator to fold theirs.
- The dummy is **finger palmed** (or held in a pocket ready).

## The basic hand switch
1. Take the folded billet from the spectator with the hand holding the dummy.
2. In the act of placing it somewhere (on the table, in their hand, into a glass), the
   fingers switch: the real billet is retained in finger palm and the dummy is released.
3. The real billet is later read under cover and ditched.

## Other switches
- **Envelope switch**: the billet goes into an envelope with a secret compartment or a
  duplicate envelope is switched.
- **Pad and clipboard switches**: the billet or writing goes onto a gimmicked pad.
- **Bag or bowl switch**: billets dropped into a container that delivers dummies.

## Timing
- The switch should happen on a natural action ("Hold this in your hand").
- Do not look at the switching hand; look at the spectator.
- Delay the reading until much later.

## Gotchas
- Different paper texture or fold is noticed by the spectator holding the dummy. Match
  paper and fold exactly.
- The spectator opening the dummy too early: give them a reason to keep it closed
  (hold it to the forehead, keep it in a fist).

## Verify
- [ ] Switch invisible on front camera, ten times.
- [ ] Dummy identical in feel and fold.
