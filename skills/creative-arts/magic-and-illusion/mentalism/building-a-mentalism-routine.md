# Building a mentalism routine

**Goal:** a sequence of mental effects that builds credibility and impossibility, ending
with a climax that ties the act together.

## Principle
Mentalism acts persuade over time. Early effects establish the performer's apparent
skill (reading body language, intuition); later effects stretch it further; the final
piece often reveals that a prediction made at the start foresaw everything. Variety of
**mental faculty** (reading thoughts, predicting, influencing) matters more than variety
of prop.

## Structure
1. **Opener:** quick, direct thought-reading with one person.
2. **Build:** effects with more participants and stronger conditions.
3. **Contrast:** a lighter, comic piece; a muscle-reading or influence piece.
4. **Climax:** a prediction revealed, often visible from the start.

## Credibility
- Consistent explanation ("psychology, observation, suggestion").
- Occasional hesitation and small misses corrected (they suggest real process).
- Fewer props than a magic act.

## Common faults
- Too many similar effects (three card predictions).
- No arc for the participants.
- Mixing obvious magic tricks into a mentalism act.

## Verify
- [ ] Act includes at least three different mental faculties.
- [ ] Climax connects back to the opening.
- [ ] Framing and disclaimer consistent throughout.
