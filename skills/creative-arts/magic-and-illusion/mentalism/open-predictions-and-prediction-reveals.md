# Open predictions and the prediction reveal

**Goal:** announce or display the prediction openly at the start, then have the spectator
arrive at it freely.

## Principle
An open prediction removes the most common solution ("the prediction was written
afterwards"). The challenge moves to the selection: forces, equivoque, or a procedure
that guarantees the outcome. Paul Curry's "open prediction" problem in card magic is the
famous example; mentalists use it with words, objects and numbers.

## Structure
1. Show the prediction (a card face up on the table, a word on a board).
2. Explain the challenge: "you will choose this, without knowing it".
3. Conduct a process that appears completely free.
4. Arrive at the prediction slowly; the spectator reveals the last step.

## Reveal pacing (any prediction)
- Recap the freedom of the choice before the reveal.
- Slow down at the final step; let tension build.
- Let the spectator see or read it first; then the audience.
- Hold for the reaction before speaking.

## Common faults
- Process so long that interest drops.
- Rushing the final step.
- Performer touching the props at the last moment.

## Verify
- [ ] Prediction visible from the start.
- [ ] Spectator makes the final reveal.
- [ ] Recap and pause scripted.
