# Word divination presentation

**Goal:** reveal the word a spectator is thinking of so it feels like mind reading, not a
trick.

## Principle
Knowing the word is the method; the **reveal** is the effect. A mentalist who announces
the word instantly looks like a trickster. A gradual reveal (images, letters, feelings,
then the word) feels like a genuine process and allows the audience to share the tension.

## Reveal structure
1. Ask the spectator to concentrate on the word and visualise it.
2. Describe sensory impressions first ("something you can hold... something warm").
3. Reveal a letter or two, or the number of letters.
4. Write the word on a board or pad before saying it.
5. Ask the spectator to say their word; then show what you wrote.

## Writing on a board
Big enough for the room; turn it round on the beat after they speak.

## Common faults
- Revealing too fast.
- Too many vague impressions; keep it under a minute.
- Misses that are not acknowledged gracefully.

## Verify
- [ ] Reveal sequence scripted with sensory impressions first.
- [ ] Written before the spectator says it.
- [ ] Under a minute from concentration to reveal.
