# Reading the room and drawing duplication

**Goal:** reproduce a drawing a spectator made in secret, choosing the right participant
and pacing the reveal so it plays as genuine thought transmission.

## Principle
Drawing duplication obtains the drawing by one of the standard information methods (an
impression device, a peek, a billet principle, pencil reading as in Corinda's first step)
and then **performs** the reveal as a gradual sensing. Reading the room means choosing a
participant whose reactions will carry the effect and noticing which audience members are
engaged.

## Reading the room
- Watch for engaged faces during the opening; choose an expressive participant.
- Avoid people who seem anxious about being "read".
- Notice group dynamics: a friend group reacting together amplifies the effect.

## The reveal
1. Ask the spectator to draw something simple, in secret.
2. Obtain the information discreetly.
3. Narrate impressions: shapes, lines, size.
4. Draw on a large pad, slightly imperfectly; real duplications are rarely exact.
5. Ask them to show their drawing first; then turn yours.

## Common faults
- A duplication that is too perfect, suggesting a method.
- Obtaining the information while the audience watches the pad.
- Choosing a shy participant who freezes.

## Verify
- [ ] Participant selection criteria noted.
- [ ] Drawing reproduced with natural imperfection.
- [ ] Spectator shows theirs first.
