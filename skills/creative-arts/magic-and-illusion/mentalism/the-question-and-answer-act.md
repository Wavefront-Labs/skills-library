# The question and answer act

**Goal:** a stage act in which audience members write private questions, and the
mentalist reveals the questions and offers answers, seemingly without reading them.

## Principle
The Q and A was a staple of vaudeville mentalism (Alexander, "The Man Who Knows"). The
method gathers the written questions through a billet system (envelopes, a one-ahead
principle, switches, or an assistant), so the mentalist knows the content. The
**performance** is in revealing names, initials and details, and in giving answers that
are warm, witty and safe.

## Structure
1. Questions written and sealed or folded during the interval or the opening.
2. Collection by assistant or the mentalist.
3. Revelation one by one: name, initials, details, then the answer.
4. Close with a strong final question or a prediction.

## Responsible answers
- Never answer questions about health, death, legal matters or investments seriously;
  deflect with humour or kindness.
- Disclaimer at the start of the act.
- Screen questions; skip anything that would distress the writer or audience.

## Common faults
- Too many questions; the act drags.
- Answers that sound like fortune telling.
- Revealing private content that embarrasses someone.

## Verify
- [ ] Collection method rehearsed with the assistant.
- [ ] Screening rule written and applied.
- [ ] Act runs under fifteen minutes with eight to ten questions.
