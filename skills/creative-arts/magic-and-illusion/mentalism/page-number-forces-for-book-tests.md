# Page number forces

**Goal:** a spectator opens an ordinary-looking book at a page you know, believing the
page was chosen freely.

## Principle
The book test has two parts: getting to a known page, and knowing the word on it. The page
force covers the first part. Common approaches: a forced card or dice total, an arithmetic
procedure, a forced number from a list, or a bookmark force. Combining a page force with
an ordinary unprepared book is strong because the book can be examined.

## Choosing a force
- **Card force:** a forced card's value gives the page or line (simple, familiar).
- **Arithmetic:** a spectator's own number leads to a fixed total.
- **Dice:** loaded or with a forcing procedure.
- **Bookmark / insertion force:** a card inserted at a page you know.

## Disguising
- Separate the page force from the book: force the number first, introduce the book
  later.
- Use the spectator's own phone calculator.
- Allow them to "change their mind" if your structure has outs.

## Common faults
- A page force that feels like arithmetic.
- Number that only fits a short book.

## Verify
- [ ] Page force tested with ten spectators.
- [ ] Target word memorised on the forced page and line.
- [ ] Book can be handed out at the end.
