# Forces, predictions and mentalism presentation

**Goal:** perform apparent mind reading and prediction that feels like a genuine
experience for the audience, built on reliable forces and staged with integrity.
Methods mentioned here are the standard published ones from the books in `sources`.

## 1. Mentalism is magic that must not look like tricks
The audience must believe the *process* (reading body language, intuition) even if
they know it's entertainment. That means: fewer props, no "magic" gestures, slower
pacing, and effects that look like they could fail. A mentalist who never hesitates
looks like a magician.

## 2. Forces — giving the illusion of free choice
- **Cross-cut force:** spectator cuts the deck, you cross the halves and later "mark
  the cut". The top card was preset. Relies on time misdirection — talk for 10–20 s
  before the reveal of "where they cut".
- **Equivoque / magician's choice:** spectator chooses between items; your wording
  interprets any choice to reach the target ("you keep that one / you give me that
  one"). Never repeat the same phrasing twice for one person; write a decision tree
  and rehearse every branch until the interpretation sounds natural.
- **Psychological forces:** "think of a number between 1 and 10" etc. They skew the
  odds but fail a meaningful fraction of the time. Only use them with an *out* — a
  second effect that works for the other answers. Results vary by culture and
  language; test your own audiences.
- **Number/word forces using printed material:** book tests rely on gimmicked or
  memorised books — buy the published ones; don't improvise.

## 3. Predictions
- Make the prediction **visible early and isolated** (sealed envelope, held by a
  spectator, in view all along). The memory of "it was there the whole time" is the
  effect.
- **Recap** before the reveal: summarise how free each choice was. Audiences remember
  your summary, not the actual procedure — this is the reconstruction principle.
- Reveal slowly; let the spectator read it aloud.

## 4. Billets (written thoughts)
A billet is a small paper the spectator writes on. Classic principles include the
**centre tear** (folding so the written centre is retained when the paper is torn) and
**peeks**. Learn one from a published source; the skill is in handling it casually.
Practise until you don't glance at your hands.

## 5. Presentation and ethics
- Give **"pseudo-explanations"**: "people give away more than they realise in how they
  hold their hands". It frames success.
- **Cold-reading style** lines (Barnum statements) are useful for character but should
  not be presented as contact with the dead or genuine psychic diagnosis.
- **Disclaimer:** most contemporary performers say something like "I use psychology,
  suggestion and showmanship" — it protects vulnerable audience members and you.
  Never do private "readings" for people in grief or crisis.

## Gotchas
- A force that fails is fine if you have an out; a force with no out that fails is a
  lost routine. Every effect needs a plan B.
- Over-claiming invites challenge. Under-claim and let the result speak.
- Uncertain: success rates of specific psychological forces are folklore-level data;
  don't trust published percentages without testing.

## Verify
- [ ] Each force has a written out for every non-target outcome.
- [ ] Equivoque branches all rehearsed aloud until fluent.
- [ ] Prediction is in plain view from the start and recapped before reveal.
- [ ] A clear, honest disclaimer is part of your script.
- [ ] Filmed run-through shows no glances at billets or gimmicks.
