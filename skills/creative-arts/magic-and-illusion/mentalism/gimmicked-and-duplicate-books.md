# Gimmicked and duplicate books

**Goal:** use a prepared book (or a set of duplicates) for a book test that can withstand
casual inspection.

## Principle
Book tests buy reliability with preparation. Common published approaches include books
where certain words repeat across pages, books with a structured layout the performer has
memorised, and **duplicate books** where the performer holds an identical copy. Each has
an inspection limit; choose the one whose limits match your performing situation.

## Choosing
- **Stage:** large books readable from a distance; inspection unlikely.
- **Close-up:** books that survive flipping through; ordinary-looking covers and spines.
- **Language and market:** a book appropriate to the audience and culture.

## Handling
- Introduce the book casually, as if picked from a shelf.
- Let a spectator flip it if the method allows; know exactly what can be shown.
- Switch to an ordinary copy at the end if possible.

## Maintenance
Covers wear, pages yellow; keep duplicates the same condition.

## Common faults
- A book with a cover that looks like a magic prop.
- Letting a spectator read too long.

## Verify
- [ ] Inspection limits listed and staged within them.
- [ ] Duplicates kept in matching condition.
- [ ] Book fits the character and setting.
