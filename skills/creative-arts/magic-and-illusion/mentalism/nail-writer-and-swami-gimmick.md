# Nail writer and swami gimmick

**Goal:** write a "prediction" secretly, after the information is known, so it appears to
have been written beforehand.

## Principle
A **nail writer** (a small writing point attached under the thumbnail or a finger) lets
you write on a card or pad held in the hand without any visible pen. The **swami gimmick**
is the classic thumb version. You learn the target information, then write it in a few
strokes while attention is on the spectator. Corinda devotes a full step to this.

## Technique
- **Short:** a number, a single word, a letter, a simple drawing. Long writing is slow
  and messy.
- **Card held** in a natural grip with the thumb on the writing surface.
- **Write while the spectator is talking** or concentrating, with your eyes on them.
- **Practise blind** until letters are legible without looking.
- Several types exist (thumb, fingertip, pencil lead, ink); choose one and stay with it.

## Common faults
- Looking at the card while writing.
- Writing too much.
- Gimmick visible when the hand is shown.

## Verify
- [ ] Number and short word written legibly without looking.
- [ ] Writing done in under five seconds while talking.
- [ ] Gimmick ditched or concealed when hands are shown.
