# Contact mind reading: grip and technique

**Goal:** be led to a hidden object or chosen location by reading the tiny involuntary
movements (ideomotor responses) of a spectator who is thinking about it.

## Principle
When a person concentrates on a target, their muscles make small unconscious movements
towards it and resist movement away. Through physical contact you can feel these signals.

## The grip
- Hold the spectator's **wrist** (their hand loosely in front of your body or on your
  shoulder), or have them hold your wrist - both are used.
- Light, steady contact; too tight and you feel only your own tension.

## Setting focus
- Ask them to think **only** of the target and to "send" directions: "Think left, right,
  forward, stop."
- Tell them not to help or hinder, just think. Most people unconsciously guide you.

## Reading
- Start moving slowly in a direction. **Resistance** (a slight drag) means wrong way; a
  **relaxation or pull** means right way.
- Change direction in small arcs, testing.
- Near the target, movements become smaller; a slight stop or tension shift marks it.
- Move at a walking pace that lets you feel changes; speed comes with skill.

## Practice
- Cooperative partner, large room, simple targets (one of four corners).
- Graduate to hidden objects, then specific items.

## Gotchas
- Sceptical or nervous spectators send weak signals; choose an engaged, relaxed helper.
- Not every spectator works; have an out.

## Verify
- [ ] 8 of 10 correct corners with a cooperative partner.
- [ ] Hidden object found within 2 minutes in practice.
