# Number and word forces

**Goal:** a spectator arrives at a number or word you know in advance, believing the
choice was free.

## Principle
Forces hide a fixed outcome inside a procedure that feels open. Three families:
- **Arithmetic forces:** procedures whose result is fixed regardless of the starting
  number (the 1089 force, digital-root forces, calendar-square totals).
- **Positional forces:** a forced number becomes a page and line, giving a forced word.
- **Verbal and equivoque structures:** choices interpreted to lead to the target.

## Disguising the procedure
- Use a calculator or let the spectator write, so the steps feel theirs.
- Separate the steps in time; do other things between them.
- Present a reason for each step ("to make it truly random").
- Combine forces: a forced number then a forced page in a borrowed-looking book.

## Outs
Arithmetic forces fail with arithmetic errors. Ask spectators to check their working, or
use a calculator.

## Common faults
- Long procedures that make the audience suspect maths.
- Explaining the rule.
- Presenting the forced result as a choice only you could know.

## Verify
- [ ] Force tested with fifty random starting values.
- [ ] Procedure takes under a minute.
- [ ] Each step has a stated reason in the script.
