# Pendulum and ideomotor effects

**Goal:** let a spectator experience their own hand moving "by itself" - a pendulum swinging
in answer to their thoughts - and use it as a strong interactive mentalism piece.

## The Chevreul pendulum
1. Give the spectator a pendulum (a ring on a thread, a crystal on a chain), elbow resting
   on a table, hand still.
2. Draw a cross and a circle on a card, or define "yes" as a line and "no" as a circle.
3. Ask them to watch the pendulum and **think** of it moving along the line.
4. It starts to swing that way: tiny unconscious muscle movements amplify into a visible
   swing.

## Suggestion wording
- Expectation drives it: describe what will happen ("You'll notice it begins to move,
  slightly at first...").
- Ask them to **think**, not to move; tell them to keep the hand still.
- Give it time; some people take 30 seconds.

## Stage uses
- "Lie detector": the pendulum answers yes/no questions.
- Group demonstration with many pendulums handed out.
- Ouija-style planchette under several hands moving to letters.

## Framing
Explain afterwards (or frame from the start) that this is the ideomotor effect - the
mind moving the body without awareness. This is honest and still astonishing; avoid
presenting it as spirits or genuine divination.

## Verify
- [ ] Wording rehearsed and works on most volunteers within 30 seconds.
- [ ] Presentation includes an honest frame.
