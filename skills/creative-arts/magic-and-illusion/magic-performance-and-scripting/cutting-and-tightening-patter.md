# Cutting and tightening patter

**Goal:** a script with no wasted words, where every line moves the effect forward,
builds character or gets a reaction.

## Principle
First drafts of patter are long. They narrate the obvious ("I'll now shuffle the cards")
and repeat information. Weber's advice is to cut everything that does not serve the
audience's experience. Nelms recommended writing a full script and then editing it
ruthlessly.

## Method
1. Record a performance and transcribe it.
2. Mark lines as: **essential** (instructions, premise, reveals), **builds** (character,
   laughs), **dead** (narration, filler, apology).
3. Cut dead lines first.
4. Shorten essential lines to the fewest words.
5. Keep builds that earned reactions; cut those that did not.
6. Time the result; repeat after more performances.

## Common faults
- Cutting instructions spectators need.
- Keeping jokes because you like them.
- Never recording.

## Verify
- [ ] Transcript marked and edited.
- [ ] Running time reduced without losing reactions.
- [ ] Revised script tested on audiences.
