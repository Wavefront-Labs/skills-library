# Comedy with volunteers

**Goal:** use a volunteer to create laughs that the audience shares with them, not at
their expense.

## Choosing
- Look for a willing, relaxed person who smiles; avoid anyone who looks reluctant or has
  been drinking heavily.
- Ask for permission with a clear, light invitation and make clear what will be asked of
  them (no surprises that embarrass).

## Setup and play
1. **Status**: keep the volunteer at least your equal; you can play the fool while they
   are the straight one.
2. **Give them lines** they can say easily ("say your name", "what do you do?") and
   build jokes from their answers.
3. **React honestly**: reactions to their answers get big laughs; do not talk over them.
4. **Use callbacks** to their earlier answers later in the act (see
   `creative-arts.magic-and-illusion.magic-performance-and-scripting.running-gags-and-callbacks-in-magic`).
5. **Protect them**: if they seem uncomfortable, simplify the job and finish gently.

## Exit
Thank them by name and give them applause or a small keepsake. Never leave them standing
without a clear instruction to return to their seat.

## Cautions
- No jokes about appearance, health or vulnerable topics.
- Avoid physical contact unless clearly consented and necessary.
- With children, follow the safeguarding guidance in
  `creative-arts.magic-and-illusion.magic-performance-and-scripting.child-volunteers-and-kids-crowd-control`.
- A volunteer who tries to expose a method needs a calm line and a move on, not a fight.

## Verify
- [ ] The volunteer has a simple job and clear exit.
- [ ] The jokes are at the performer's expense or shared with the volunteer.
- [ ] Applause for the volunteer at the end.
