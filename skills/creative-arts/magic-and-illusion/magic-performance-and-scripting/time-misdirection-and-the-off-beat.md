# Time misdirection and the off-beat

**Goal:** do the secret work when nobody is attending to it, and put enough time between
the method and the effect that the audience cannot connect them.

## Principle
Attention rises and falls in a rhythm. It peaks at moments the performer signals as
important (a selection, a reveal) and drops just after them, during laughter, applause, a
question, or a change of props. These low points are **off-beats**. Work done in an
off-beat is rarely seen; work done a while before the effect is rarely remembered.
**Time delay** erodes memory: after a minute, audiences reconstruct what happened from the
gist, not the detail.

## Applying it
1. Mark the routine's beats: where attention is high (on-beats) and low (off-beats).
2. Move secret actions into off-beats: just after a laugh, after the reveal of a previous
   effect, while a spectator is looking for something.
3. **Do the work early.** Set the method before the effect is announced.
4. Add delay between method and effect: a short story, another phase, a different
   spectator.
5. Recap the conditions before the reveal so the audience's memory is the version you
   want.

## Common faults
- Doing the move at the moment you announce something important.
- Rushing from method to effect.
- Off-beat moments that are too obviously "dead time".

## Verify
- [ ] Every secret action in the routine falls in a marked off-beat.
- [ ] Measurable delay (seconds, phases) between method and effect.
- [ ] Test spectators' retelling omits the moment of the method.
