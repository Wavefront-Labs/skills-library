# Working with a magic director or outside eye

**Goal:** get useful direction from someone who sees the show as an audience member,
without losing your own artistic control or your secrets.

## Who and why
A director or outside eye (often a theatre director, actor or comedian, not necessarily a
magician) sees **what the audience sees**: pacing, clarity, character, emotion. Magicians
often get too close to method to notice.

## Briefing
1. Tell them the **intended effect and audience** for each routine, not the method, unless
   they truly need it (for example to manage safety or cues).
2. Share the **running time and constraints** (venue, props, budget).
3. Agree how to give notes: after a full run, written or spoken.
4. If method must be disclosed, use a trusted person and an agreement on confidentiality;
   see `creative-arts.magic-and-illusion.magic-performance-and-scripting.exposure-and-secrecy-rules-in-magic`.

## Taking notes
- Do not defend or explain during notes; write them down.
- Sort afterwards: **must fix** (clarity, safety), **try** (worth testing), **discard**
  (against your character or intent).
- Test changes in rehearsal before accepting or rejecting.
- Thank them; a director who feels heard gives better notes next time.

## Warnings
- A director who does not understand the audience for your market may push the wrong
  style; weigh their notes against your audiences.
- The performer remains responsible for safety; a director does not replace a safety
  officer.

## Verify
- [ ] The director knows the intended effect, not just the method.
- [ ] Notes were sorted into must fix, try and discard.
- [ ] Safety responsibilities are still clearly yours or a named officer's.
