# Clarity of effect in magic

**Goal:** the audience knows exactly what happened - and therefore why it is impossible.
Confused audiences are not amazed.

## The one-sentence test
Write the effect as the audience would describe it, in one sentence: "She signed a card,
it vanished from the deck and was inside a sealed envelope that had been on the table
the whole time." If you cannot, the effect is unclear.

## Establish conditions before the magic
- What is where, what is normal, what is the situation - all clear before the magic moment.
- Ortiz: the audience must understand the "before" to appreciate the "after".
- Recap briefly just before the climax ("Your card was lost in the deck, the envelope
  never moved").

## Remove clutter
- Extra phases, extra props, unnecessary procedures dilute the effect.
- Every action must serve the effect or be needed for the method; if the latter, make it
  look natural or motivated.
- Too many spectators involved at once divides attention.

## Make the magic moment visible
- Know exactly when the magic happens and show it (see magic moment placement in effect
  design).
- Pause after the reveal for the audience to process.

## Check with real audiences
After a show, ask lay friends to describe the tricks. Where their descriptions differ from
your one-sentence effect, fix the clarity.

## Verify
- [ ] One-sentence effect written for each piece.
- [ ] Lay audience recaps match the intended effect.
