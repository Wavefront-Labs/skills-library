# Running gags and callbacks

**Goal:** make the act feel connected and funnier as it goes by returning to an earlier
joke, prop or phrase.

## Terms
- **Running gag:** a joke that returns several times through the act, changing each time.
- **Callback:** a later reference to something earlier (a line, a volunteer's answer, a
  prop) that gets a laugh because the audience remembers it.

## Building one
1. **Plant** something small and clear early: a catch-phrase, a prop that keeps
   misbehaving, a volunteer's remark.
2. **Return** to it at least once or twice more; a third return usually pays off more
   than a second.
3. **Vary** each return (a bigger version, a smaller version, a reversed version) so it
   rises rather than repeats.
4. **Pay off** with the biggest version near the end, often on the closing effect.
5. **Stop** while it still works; if the laugh weakens, cut it.

## Using volunteers' contributions
Remember a volunteer's name or funny answer and refer to it later. Write it down in your
show notes if you tend to forget. Never mock them cruelly; the callback should include
them in the joke.

## Cautions
- Do not let a gag cover for a weak effect.
- Do not plant a gag that depends on the audience seeing something they cannot see from
  the back row.
- Record shows and count what got laughs; keep those, drop the rest (see
  `creative-arts.magic-and-illusion.magic-performance-and-scripting.cutting-and-tightening-patter`).

## Verify
- [ ] Each running gag has a planted first appearance, variations and a payoff.
- [ ] A recording confirms the laughs rise rather than fall.
