# Directing gaze and where you look

**Goal:** make the audience look where you want, at the moments that matter, using your
own eyes, voice and body.

## Principle
People follow other people's gaze automatically. Eye-tracking studies of magic (Kuhn and
Tatler, 2005) show spectators watch the performer's face and look where the performer
looks, especially at moments of social interaction. A performer who looks at the hand that
"holds" the coin drags the audience's eyes there. Looking at the hand doing the secret
work drags them there too.

## Techniques
- **Look at the apparent location** of the object, never the real one.
- **Look at a spectator** and ask a question at the moment of the secret move; people
  look back at you or at the person addressed.
- **Point** with the hand that is not working.
- **Speech:** a direct question pulls attention to faces.
- **Movement:** a moving object attracts the eye more than a still one, and a new object
  more than a familiar one.

## Limits
- Gaze cueing is strongest in the performer's direct, social moments; it is weaker when
  the audience is suspicious and watching hands deliberately.
- Attention can be elsewhere while the eyes are on the hands (inattentional blindness);
  looking is not the same as noticing.

## Common faults
- Glancing at the secret hand.
- Directing gaze too forcefully, which reads as "look away from that".

## Verify
- [ ] Your eyes on video go to the apparent location at every secret moment.
- [ ] A question or name is scheduled at each key move.
- [ ] Test audiences recall your face and words, not your hands, at that moment.
