# The comedy of failure

**Goal:** get laughs from apparent failure while the audience still believes you control
the magic.

## Why it works
Failure comedy plays on **status**: a performer who seems to fail is lower status and
likeable, then the surprise of success flips it. Tommy Cooper is the classic example of a
performer whose clumsy persona hid precise technique; watch recordings to study timing
rather than copying lines.

## The rule
The failure is **planned and rehearsed**. The audience should laugh at the apparent
fumble and gasp at the real magic. If a trick truly fails by accident, treat it as a
free gag but have a recovery ready.

## Building a failure gag
1. **Pick the beat**: a vanish that "doesn't work", a wrong card, a prop that falls.
2. **Time it**: pause so the audience sees it, react (silent look to the audience,
   confident wrong line), then fix it.
3. **Escalate**: each failure a little bigger, then the real effect happens cleanly.
4. **Protect the final effect**: do not let the persona's clumsiness cast doubt on the
   closing miracle; the real climax should be clean and strong.
5. **Keep technique perfect**: the funniest failures are the ones executed with full
   control.

## Cautions
- Failure on a dangerous prop (blades, fire, escapes) is never a gag; use qualified
  safety guidance for those.
- Do not blame the volunteer; the failure is the performer's.
- Avoid copying a famous performer's exact gags; write your own (see
  `creative-arts.magic-and-illusion.magic-performance-and-scripting.crediting-and-intellectual-property-in-magic`).

## Verify
- [ ] Every apparent failure is scripted and rehearsed.
- [ ] The performer is the butt of the joke.
- [ ] The final effect lands cleanly.
