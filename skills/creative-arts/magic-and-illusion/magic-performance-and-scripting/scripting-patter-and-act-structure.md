# Scripting patter and act structure

**Goal:** turn a collection of tricks into a performance: each effect has a reason to
exist, the words are written and rehearsed, and the act rises to a climax. The old
maxim (attributed to Robert-Houdin) — a magician is an actor playing the part of a
magician — is the principle.

## 1. Script every word, then make it sound unscripted
- Write the routine as a **two-column script**: left column words, right column
  actions/secret moves. You'll see where a secret move falls on a weak line — move it
  to a line that pulls attention elsewhere.
- Cut **"process patter"** that narrates what the audience can see ("now I'll shuffle
  the cards"). Replace it with *why*: a story, a premise, a stake, a joke.
- Read it aloud with a timer. Typical close-up routines run 3–6 minutes; trim anything
  that doesn't build to the effect.
- After ~20 performances, revise: keep lines that got laughs, cut ones that didn't.
  Record every show (audio is enough).

## 2. Character
- Define your character in one sentence ("a slightly anxious scientist who keeps
  surprising herself", "a smooth con-man who admits the con"). It decides wardrobe,
  vocabulary, music and which effects fit.
- An effect that fights the character goes, however strong. A gentle storyteller
  doing aggressive "gotcha" magic confuses the audience.
- Exaggerate one trait for the stage; audiences read character at a distance.

## 3. Act running order
- **Opener:** fast, visual, establishes you and the character in the first 30 s.
- **Build:** alternate pace and tone — comic/serious, solo/volunteer, small/big.
- **Second-strongest piece second-to-last**, **strongest last** (standard variety-act
  wisdom).
- **Applause cues:** finish each effect with a clear end pose, music hit or a line that
  signals "now you clap". Audiences need permission.
- Transitions are part of the act: plan how props come on and off; dead time kills
  momentum.

## 4. Magic for camera
- The camera is **one fixed spectator**; angles are easier, but every cut makes
  viewers suspect editing. Use **long single takes** for key moments and include
  real spectators' reactions, which carry the effect for remote viewers.
- Shoot wider than you think; tight framing invites "it happened just off-screen"
  suspicion.
- Many magicians add a statement like "no camera tricks or edits were used"; it is only
  worth saying if it's true (some broadcasters have rules on this — check yours).
- For live streaming, test latency and the mirror flip in your software so that
  writing and card faces read correctly.

## 5. Rehearsal and direction
Rehearse in full costume with music and props from the opening line. Bring in an
outside director or "show doctor" who isn't a magician — they see the theatre, not the
method.

## Gotchas
- Jokes at volunteers' expense lose the room; jokes at your own expense win it.
- Patter written for one language/culture rarely translates; rewrite, don't translate.
- Uncertain: broadcaster editing rules for magic vary and change; confirm per project.

## Verify
- [ ] Two-column script exists for every routine.
- [ ] Character defined in one sentence; every effect fits it.
- [ ] Running order ends with the strongest piece; each effect has an applause cue.
- [ ] A full-dress run timed within ±10% of target length.
- [ ] Camera versions shot in long takes with visible reactions.
