# Filming street magic and capturing reactions

**Goal:** record a street effect and the spectator's reaction in a way that is credible,
legal and respectful. Rules on filming people in public and on commercial use differ by
country and venue; check local law and the platform's or broadcaster's rules, and take
advice from a qualified source if you plan to publish commercially.

## Equipment and set-up
- Two cameras help: one on the **hands and effect**, one on the **spectator's face**. A
  single camera can work if the framing includes both.
- Shoot wide enough that the audience can see the whole action (see
  `creative-arts.magic-and-illusion.magic-performance-and-scripting.scripting-patter-and-act-structure`
  for camera angle guidance).
- Use a clip-on microphone or a nearby mic; wind and traffic ruin sound.
- Keep a spare battery and card.

## Consent and law
1. Ask people for their **consent to be filmed and published**, and get a signed or
   recorded release for anything you intend to publish.
2. Do not film children without a parent's consent, or anywhere people expect privacy.
3. Check whether the location (a mall, station, private property) needs a permit or
   forbids filming.
4. Honour requests to delete or not publish; being filmed is a choice.

## Reactions
- Let the reaction **breathe**: keep the camera on the spectator for a few seconds after
  the climax.
- Capture a **natural** response; do not coach a reaction.
- Reactions can be cut with the effect, but keep the **effect itself uncut** where
  credibility matters.

## Credibility
Do not misrepresent a staged effect as spontaneous if a platform or law requires
disclosure. See
`creative-arts.magic-and-illusion.magic-performance-and-scripting.exposure-and-secrecy-rules-in-magic`.

## Verify
- [ ] Release or consent recorded for every person published.
- [ ] Location rules checked.
- [ ] The effect shot is continuous; the reaction is captured.
