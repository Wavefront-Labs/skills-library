# Exposure and secrecy rules in magic

**Goal:** protect the secrets that make the art work, follow the codes of the societies you
belong to, and know where teaching ends and exposure begins.

## What exposure is
Revealing the method of an effect to the **general public** (not to students of magic)
without good reason - e.g. explaining a trick to an audience, posting reveal videos, or
selling methods disguised as entertainment.

## What the societies say
- Magic societies (The Magic Circle, IBM, SAM) have codes against exposure; members can be
  disciplined or expelled.
- The traditional "magician's oath" commits members not to reveal secrets to non-magicians
  and to practise before performing.
Read your society's current code; wording and enforcement differ.

## Teaching versus exposure
- Teaching students who are learning magic (books, lessons, lectures, magic shops) is how
  the art is passed on - accepted and encouraged.
- The test: is the audience learning magic as an art, or just being told how a trick works?

## Social media
- Videos that show a method to millions are exposure even if labelled "tutorial".
- Performance videos are fine; avoid angles that reveal methods.

## "How did you do it?"
- Smile and deflect: "Very well, thank you", or "Can you keep a secret? So can I."
- Offer resources if they want to learn magic (a book, a club).

## Verify
- [ ] You know your society's code and have read it.
- [ ] Your online content shows performances, not methods to the public.
