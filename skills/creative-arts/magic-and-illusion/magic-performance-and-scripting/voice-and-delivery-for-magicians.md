# Voice and delivery

**Goal:** be heard clearly, use the voice to shape the magic, and keep the voice healthy
across long performance days.

## Principle
Actors train the voice for clarity, projection and stamina; magicians need the same.
Berry's Voice and the Actor is a standard text. Delivery shapes the effect: **pauses**
before reveals build tension, a change of pace signals a new phase, and **volume** draws
focus.

## Technique
- **Breath support:** breathe low (diaphragm), not high in the chest.
- **Projection:** aim at the back row; open vowels, clear consonants.
- **Pace:** slow down at key moments; pause before and after reveals.
- **Pitch:** vary it; a monotone kills interest.

## Microphones
- Headset or lavalier for hands-free magic; test placement and rustle.
- Do not shout into a mic; let it amplify.
- Know where the mute is during asides.

## Voice care
- Warm up before shows; hydrate.
- Rest the voice between walk-around sets.
- Avoid shouting over loud rooms; choose visual material instead.

## Common faults
- Rushing reveals.
- Dropping volume at the end of sentences.

## Verify
- [ ] Recorded show audible at the back of the room.
- [ ] Pauses scripted before each reveal.
- [ ] Warm-up done before every performance.
