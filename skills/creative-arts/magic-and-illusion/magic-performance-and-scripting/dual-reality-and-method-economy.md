# Dual reality and method economy

**Goal:** two design tools for choosing and shaping methods.

## Dual reality
- The on-stage helper experiences one thing; the audience experiences another, and both
  find it impossible. Example structures: the helper is asked a question the audience does
  not hear; the helper sees a different prediction than the audience.
- Common in mentalism (e.g. effects where the helper's choice seems free to them but the
  audience sees a fair procedure).
- **Risk**: if the helper later describes their experience, the methods may come out. Use
  only where the helper's version also seems magical to them.
- **Ethics**: never humiliate the helper or make them a secret accomplice without consent.

## Method economy
- Choose the **simplest method** that achieves the effect under the conditions you want to
  show. Simpler methods fail less and leave the performer free to perform.
- But economy is not the goal in itself: a complex method that produces clean conditions
  can beat a simple one with suspicious conditions (see layering methods).
- Ask: does each part of the method earn its place in the effect?

## Choosing between methods
1. List possible methods.
2. For each: conditions it allows, risks, angles, preparation, reset, skill needed.
3. Pick the one that gives the strongest effect with acceptable risk for your venues.
