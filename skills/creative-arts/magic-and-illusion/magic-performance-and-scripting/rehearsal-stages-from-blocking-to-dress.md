# Rehearsal stages from blocking to dress

**Goal:** move an act from "works in the practice room" to "works in front of an
audience, in the venue, with every cue" in a sequence that finds problems early.

## Stages
1. **Script and method check**: read the script aloud with props; fix method problems
   before staging.
2. **Blocking**: decide where you stand and move for each moment, angles included.
   Mark positions on the floor with tape. Test angles with a camera or an honest
   observer (see
   `creative-arts.magic-and-illusion.sleight-of-hand.charting-the-angles-of-a-routine`).
3. **Run-throughs without stopping**: perform the whole act, record it, then review. Stop
   for notes only after the run.
4. **Technical rehearsal**: add lights, sound, projection and crew cues in the venue. For
   illusions, this is where safety checks and sightline checks happen.
5. **Dress rehearsal**: full costume, props, music and timing as in the show, with
   nobody stopping the run. Invite a small audience if possible.
6. **Preview or try-out**: perform for a friendly audience, then adjust.
7. **Opening**: after opening, schedule a review of the first shows.

## Rules of thumb
- Do the hard technical bits of a stage show on a day separate from performance day.
- Rehearse **resets and prop changes**; they are where acts fall apart.
- Rehearse **what to do when it goes wrong** (dropped prop, missed cue).
- Keep notes of changes so the crew knows the current version.

## Safety
Anything with blades, fire, restraint, water or suspension needs a qualified safety
person at technical and dress rehearsals. See
`creative-arts.magic-and-illusion.stage-illusion.illusion-maintenance-and-preshow-checks`.

## Verify
- [ ] Each stage has a goal and a finish point.
- [ ] A full uninterrupted run was recorded before dress rehearsal.
- [ ] Technical and dress rehearsals were done in the venue or a close copy.
