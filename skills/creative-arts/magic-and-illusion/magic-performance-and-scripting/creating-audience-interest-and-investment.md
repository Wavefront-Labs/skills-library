# Creating audience interest and investment

**Goal:** an audience that wants to know what happens next and cares about the outcome -
the difference between a puzzle and an experience.

## Sources of interest
- **Personal stakes**: the spectator's own object (borrowed ring, signed note), their
  choice, their memory.
- **Challenge**: "can he do it?" - gambling demonstrations, escape attempts, a bet.
- **Story and theme**: a narrative or a meaningful subject (memory, luck, love, chance).
- **Character**: the audience cares because they like the performer.
- **Novelty**: something they have never seen.

## Building investment
- Let spectators make choices that feel free and important.
- Use real objects that matter (a wedding ring beats a coin from your pocket).
- Raise the stakes progressively in a routine (from a card to a signed card to their
  borrowed phone).

## Pacing interest
- Open with a hook (fast, visual, clear).
- Vary emotion: surprise, laughter, wonder, tension.
- Do not explain everything up front; let curiosity work.

## Warning signs
- Spectators checking phones or chatting.
- Audience reacting to the method ("how?") rather than the effect.

## Verify
- [ ] Each routine has an identified stake for the audience.
- [ ] At least one personal-object effect in the set.
