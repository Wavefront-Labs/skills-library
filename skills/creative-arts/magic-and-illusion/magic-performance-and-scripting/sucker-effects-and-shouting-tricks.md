# Sucker effects and shouting tricks

**Goal:** use the audience's belief that they have spotted the secret, and their urge to
shout, to produce a loud, happy reaction rather than confusion.

## Two related structures
- **Shouting trick:** the performer does something and the children shout a correction
  ("it's behind you!", "it's in your hand!"). The performer pretends not to hear or looks
  the wrong way, so the children get louder.
- **Sucker trick:** the audience is allowed to think they know how the effect works; the
  twist shows the expected outcome was wrong or lets them be right in a surprising way.

Both rely on the performer playing slightly foolish, so the children feel clever.

## Building one
1. **Set the rule** clearly: "If you see me do something silly, shout STOP."
2. **Let them be right** first: give the audience true information they can shout.
3. **Escalate**: repeat once or twice, building volume.
4. **Pay off** with a surprise or a laugh, and let the shouters win in the end.
5. **Close** by thanking them for helping.

## Cautions
- Do not use scary shouting (pretend danger) with under-fives.
- Never make a child feel stupid; the fool is the performer.
- Have a calm-down signal before the shouting starts (see
  `creative-arts.magic-and-illusion.magic-performance-and-scripting.child-volunteers-and-kids-crowd-control`).
- Test in front of real children; some rooms shout, some do not.

## Verify
- [ ] The audience has a clear rule and a clear payoff.
- [ ] The performer is the fool, not a child.
- [ ] A calm-down signal exists before the shouting starts.
