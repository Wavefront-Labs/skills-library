# One-liners and heckler lines

**Goal:** have short, tested lines for the moments the script does not cover, and use them
without turning a show into an argument.

## One-liners
- Structure: setup, then a short punch word at the end. Cut every word that is not
  needed.
- Write them to fit your character (see the character section of
  `creative-arts.magic-and-illusion.magic-performance-and-scripting.scripting-patter-and-act-structure`).
- Test each on real audiences and drop what gets no laugh.
- Pause after a laugh; do not speak over it.

## Heckler lines
Hecklers and burners come in many types: the drunk, the know-it-all, the shouting child,
the friend showing off. Plan a **ladder** of responses:
1. **Ignore or smile** if it is mild and brief.
2. **Light line** that is funny at your expense or neutral ("I'll deal with you in a
   moment").
3. **Redirect**: give the heckler a job or move to a different spectator.
4. **Firm but calm**: a short request to keep it down, preferably with a laugh.
5. **Stop and escalate** to the organiser if there is aggression or a safety concern.

## Cautions
- Do not punch down: avoid mocking someone's body, background or vulnerability.
- Prepared lines should be reusable and not tied to a particular person.
- Keep the room's mood in mind; a joke that works at a pub gig can fail at a
  corporate dinner.
- Never let a line escalate into a real confrontation; get a venue manager if needed. See
  `creative-arts.magic-and-illusion.close-up-magic.handling-hecklers-and-burners`.

## Verify
- [ ] Each line has been tested on real audiences.
- [ ] A ladder of responses exists, from ignore to escalate.
- [ ] No line mocks a vulnerable person.
