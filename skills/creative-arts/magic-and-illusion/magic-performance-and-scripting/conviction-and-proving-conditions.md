# Conviction and proving conditions

**Goal:** the audience is convinced the conditions were fair, so they cannot dismiss the
effect as "he switched it" or "it was a trick deck".

## List the theories
For each effect, write every explanation a smart spectator might think of: duplicate,
switch, stooge, trick deck, it was in his pocket all along, magnets. This is the list you
must defeat.

## Convincers
- **Implicit** proof: let the spectator shuffle, sign, hold the object, without announcing
  "this is ordinary". Ortiz favours implicit proof: it convinces without arousing
  suspicion.
- **Explicit** proof: showing hands empty, examining a prop. Useful but signals that
  there is something to look for.
- **Spectator-held** objects and **spectator actions** convince more than anything the
  performer does.

## Overproving
- Showing the hands empty five times invites the question "why does he keep doing that?"
- Prove only what is necessary to rule out the theories that would otherwise occur.
- If a proof raises a new suspicion, it is wrong.

## Timing
- Convincers before the magic moment build conviction.
- Some proof after (examining the vanished object's location) confirms it.

## Verify
- [ ] Theory list written for each effect, each with a convincer.
- [ ] No proof repeated without a reason.
