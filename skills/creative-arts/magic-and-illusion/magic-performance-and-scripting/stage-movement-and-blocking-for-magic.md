# Stage movement and blocking

**Goal:** every movement on stage has a purpose, draws focus where you want it, and keeps
methods within their angles.

## Principle
Movement draws the eye. Purposeless wandering dilutes focus; **stillness** at the moment
of magic concentrates it. Blocking (where you stand and when you move) must also respect
each effect's sightlines. Nelms applies theatre blocking principles to magic; Lecoq's
movement training builds physical expressiveness.

## Principles
- **Move with intention:** to a prop, to a volunteer, to a new area for a new phase.
- **Freeze for the magic:** stillness at the reveal.
- **Downstage centre** is the strongest position; save it for big moments.
- **Open to the audience:** turn the body out when talking to volunteers.
- **Marks:** tape positions for effects with tight angles.

## Blocking a routine
1. Draw the stage and mark the angles for each effect.
2. Plan your path between effects.
3. Rehearse with marks, then with lights.

## Common faults
- Pacing while talking.
- Turning your back to the audience.
- Moving during the reveal.
