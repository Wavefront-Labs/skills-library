# Tamariz's Magic Way and false solutions

**Goal:** use Tamariz's framework to make an effect leave no explanation standing in the
spectator's mind.

## The idea
Tamariz describes the spectator's mind as constantly generating possible solutions to
what they see. The magician's path - the "magic way" - must close off each solution
before or as it arises, so that at the end the spectator has none left.

## Theory of false solutions
1. List all solutions a spectator might think of (correct method and false ones).
2. For each, plan how to **cancel** it: a convincer, a condition, a procedure that rules
   it out.
3. The **real method** must also be cancelled in the spectator's mind - ideally by a
   false solution the audience already rejected, or by the method being disguised as
   something that is ruled out.
4. Avoid introducing new suspicions with your cancellations.

## Using it to evaluate routines
- Walk through the routine as a spectator, beat by beat, noting which theories are alive.
- At the climax, no theory should be alive. If one is, add a cancellation earlier.

## Related ideas
- Tamariz's emphasis on the **emotional** experience and the moment of magic.
- His work on memorised decks (Mnemonica) shows how a method can be invisible because the
  audience never imagines it.

## Verify
- [ ] For a routine, a table of theories with where each is cancelled.
- [ ] No theory alive at the climax in a test performance (ask spectators).
