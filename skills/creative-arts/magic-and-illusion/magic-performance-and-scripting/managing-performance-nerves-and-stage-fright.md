# Managing performance nerves and stage fright

**Goal:** perform steadily when nervous. This is general practical guidance, not medical
advice; if anxiety is severe or affects your health or work, speak to a doctor or a
qualified therapist.

## Understand it
Some nerves are normal and even useful: the body is alert. The aim is to channel
arousal, not to remove it.

## Preparation (reduces most nerves)
- Rehearse to the point that the sequence is automatic (see
  `creative-arts.magic-and-illusion.magic-performance-and-scripting.rehearsal-stages-from-blocking-to-dress`).
- Do at least one full run in costume and in the venue if possible.
- Have a **recovery plan** for the usual failures (dropped prop, forgotten line) so they
  are not frightening.
- Arrive early; leave time so the show does not start with a rush.

## Pre-show routine
1. Same warm-up each time: stretch, voice warm-up, hand warm-up.
2. Slow breathing: breathe in through the nose for about four counts, out for about six;
   repeat for a minute or two.
3. Check props and cues once, then stop checking.
4. Say your first line out loud once.

## On stage
- Focus on the **audience and the effect**, not on yourself.
- Slow down; nervous performers speed up.
- Treat the first minute as the hardest and plan the opening to be your most secure
  piece.
- If you make a mistake, carry on; audiences usually do not know the plan.

## After
Note what helped and what did not in your show notes (see
`creative-arts.magic-and-illusion.magic-performance-and-scripting.show-reports-and-post-show-notes-for-magicians`).

## Verify
- [ ] A fixed pre-show routine exists.
- [ ] The opening piece is the most secure in the act.
- [ ] A recovery plan exists for the common failures.
