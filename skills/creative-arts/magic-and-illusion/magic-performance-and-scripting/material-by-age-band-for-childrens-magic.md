# Material by age band for children's magic

**Goal:** pick effects and pacing a given age group can actually follow, so the show
works instead of being "too young" or "too old".

The bands below are rules of thumb from working entertainers, not research findings.
Always ask the booker for the real age range and the number of children.

## Bands (adjust to the room)
| Age | What tends to work | What tends to fail |
|---|---|---|
| Under 5 | Very short bits (1-2 min), big visible props, bubbles, puppets, songs, repeated catch-phrases, things that appear and vanish, lots of movement | Long stories, anything needing memory of an earlier step, card tricks, quiet moments |
| 5-7 | Simple stories, "magic words", colour changes, vanishing and reappearing toys, sucker effects, participation | Subtle effects, irony-heavy patter, waiting turns |
| 8-11 | Mystery and puzzles, "how did that happen", bigger reveals, mind-reading style effects, escapes, competition, jokes with a twist | Baby talk; being talked down to |
| 12+ | Closer to a teen or adult close-up style: cards, coins, mentalism, cool factor | Gimmicky "magic words", forced participation |

## Mixed ages
- Aim the effect at the middle of the range and add a layer for the older ones (a joke
  they get, a surprise ending) while the younger ones enjoy the visual.
- Give older children the tricky or "expert" role rather than only simple helper jobs.

## Pace and structure
1. Open with something fast and visual within the first minute; small children decide
   quickly whether to watch.
2. Ask the booker what running time they want; shorter suits younger children.
3. Alternate sitting-still moments with moving, shouting or counting moments.
4. Keep a spare filler (a song, a quick vanish) for a restless room.

## Verify
- [ ] Booker gave the real age range and group size.
- [ ] Every effect in the set suits the youngest present.
- [ ] A short filler is ready for a restless room.
