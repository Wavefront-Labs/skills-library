# Crediting and intellectual property in magic

**Goal:** act ethically with other people's tricks: credit creators, buy rather than copy,
and understand what protection magic ideas have.

## Why it matters
Magic methods are hard to protect by law (copyright protects the written or filmed
expression, not the method; patents reveal the secret). The community relies on **norms**:
crediting, buying, not copying. Loshin's study describes this norm-based system.

## Researching credits
- Conjuring Archive and the literature (Genii, Linking Ring, books) list origins and
  variations.
- Ask creators or dealers when unsure.

## Crediting
- In publications, lectures and tutorials, credit the originator and those whose variations
  you use.
- In performances for the public, crediting is not expected, but never claim a known
  creator's signature piece as your own invention.

## Performance rights
- Buying an effect usually grants a right to perform it; some (especially illusions and
  signature pieces) require explicit permission or exclusive licensing.
- Recorded and TV performances may need separate permission.
- Read the instructions and licence of any product.

## Knock-offs
Do not buy or sell pirated copies of effects or books; the community regards it as theft
and it harms creators.

## Verify
- [ ] Sources and credits recorded for each routine in your repertoire.
- [ ] Performance rights confirmed for TV or signature pieces.
