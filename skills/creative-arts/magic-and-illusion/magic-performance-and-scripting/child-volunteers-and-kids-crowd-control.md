# Child volunteers and crowd control

**Goal:** use children as helpers without losing the room, hurting feelings or putting
a child in an uncomfortable position.

## Choosing helpers
- Pick a child who is already looking at you and smiling, not the loudest one jumping up
  (they will take over) and not the shyest (they may freeze).
- Choose for the task: an older child for jobs needing memory or reading, a younger one
  for simple "hold this" jobs.
- A birthday child works well as guest of honour with a special role, but share roles so
  nobody is left out.
- Give every child some part over the show: counting, magic words, holding the wand.

## At the front
1. Ask their name and use it, then place them beside you facing the audience so the group
   can see.
2. Give one simple instruction at a time.
3. Protect them from failure: the effect works whatever they do, and they leave to
   applause.
4. If a child freezes or becomes upset, thank them warmly and let them return; do not
   coax.

## Controlling the crowd
- Use a signal rather than volume: a call-and-response phrase, a clap pattern or
  "freeze". Teach it in the first minute.
- Keep the whole group busy: counting, saying the magic word, pointing at where they
  think something is.
- A game ("everyone sit like a statue") gets more than a request to sit.
- For the loud child: acknowledge once, give a short job, then move on.
- Watch for any child at risk (stage edges, props, doorways) and stop if needed.

## Contact and conduct
Avoid physical contact with children and never take a child out of sight of adults. See
`creative-arts.magic-and-illusion.magic-performance-and-scripting.safeguarding-and-parent-liaison-in-childrens-magic`.

## Verify
- [ ] Signal taught early and used.
- [ ] Every helper gets applause and a clear exit.
- [ ] No child is left without a role.
