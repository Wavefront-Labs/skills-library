# Draping trousers and crotch shaping

**Note:** trousers are usually drafted, then fitted. Draping is used for unusual volumes
(pleated, draped, harem, wide palazzo) and to understand the crotch. It needs a **pant form**
(form with legs).

## Front
1. Mark the lengthwise grain (crease line) down the centre of the front leg, from the waist
   through the knee to the ankle; pin it vertical.
2. Mark a cross-grain at hip level; keep it level from CF to side.
3. Smooth over the abdomen; take waist excess into darts or pleats in line with the crease.
4. Clip into the front crotch carefully and shape the **front crotch curve** around the body;
   the front extension is short (roughly a quarter to a third of the back extension).

## Back
1. Back grain down the centre of the back leg. The back crotch seam (CB) slopes: the
   **seat angle** tilts the CB line outward at the top for room when sitting.
2. Shape the longer **back crotch extension** under the seat; ease over the seat.
3. Back waist darts toward the seat's fullest point.

## Join and check
- Inseams front and back roughly equal length (back slightly shorter and stretched in on
  wovens is a tailoring convention); outseam matched.
- The crotch point sits about 2-3 cm below the body crotch for trousers with ease.
- Check: smile lines at the front = crotch too short/tight; drag lines under the seat = back
  crotch too short or seat angle too small.

## Verify
- [ ] Crease lines vertical front and back.
- [ ] Crotch curves smooth, back extension longer than front.
- [ ] No drag lines at front crotch or under the seat on a live fit.
