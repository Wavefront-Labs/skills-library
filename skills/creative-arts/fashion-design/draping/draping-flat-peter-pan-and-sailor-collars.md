# Draping flat Peter Pan and sailor collars

**Goal:** a flat collar that lies on the shoulders with just enough roll to cover the
neckline seam, in a rounded (Peter Pan) or square-backed (sailor) shape.

## How roll works
- A collar whose neck edge matches the bodice neckline exactly lies dead flat and the seam
  shows. Shortening the outer edge relative to the neck curve makes it roll up.
- Pattern rule of thumb (Armstrong): overlap the front and back bodice shoulders at the
  armhole by ~1.3 cm for slight roll; 2.5-4 cm for more roll. Draping gives the same result.

## Drape
1. Neckline marked with style tape; CF and CB marked. For a sailor collar the front neckline
   is a V; mark the V depth first.
2. Muslin piece large enough to cover the shoulder area; straight grain at CB.
3. Pin CB, smooth the muslin over the shoulder toward CF, letting it lie on the bodice.
4. Clip the neck edge seam allowance as you go; pin on the neckline tape.
5. To add roll, lift the neck edge slightly and let the outer edge tighten — 3-6 mm stand at
   CB is enough to hide the seam on a Peter Pan.
6. Draw the outer edge: Peter Pan = 6-8 cm wide, rounded at CF; sailor = square flap at CB
   (roughly 20-25 cm wide, falling to mid-shoulder-blade), tapering to the V point.

## True
- Smooth the neck curve; it must equal the bodice neckline length.
- Outer edge: smooth curve, square at CB fold so there is no point or dip.
- Undercollar 2-3 mm smaller at outer edge so the seam rolls under.

## Gotchas
- A two-piece Peter Pan (split at CB) needs both halves to meet at CB without a gap.
- Sailor collars on a V can gape at the V; tape the neckline or add a modesty panel/dickey.
- Heavy fabric adds roll by itself; check in final fabric.

## Verify
- [ ] Neckline seam hidden all round.
- [ ] Collar lies flat with no rippling at the outer edge.
- [ ] Left and right sides match when folded at CB.
