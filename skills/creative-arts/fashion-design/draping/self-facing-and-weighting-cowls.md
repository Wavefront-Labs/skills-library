# Self-facing and weighting cowls

## Self-facing (grown-on facing)
- Cowl edges are rarely faced with a separate piece: the cowl edge is folded back on itself
  so the fold is clean and the inside does not show.
- Extend the pattern above the cowl line by the facing depth (typically 8-12 cm, and deeper
  than the lowest fold so the facing edge never flips out).
- Cut the facing in one with the cowl on the same bias.
- Finish the facing edge with a narrow overlock or roll hem and tack it at the shoulder seams.

## Weights and anchors
- **Chain or lead weights** (covered drapery weights, 3-10 g) stitched inside the lowest
  fold hold the cowl down in light fabrics.
- **Thread tacks** between folds keep them in place; or a small bead stitched inside.
- On low-cut cowls, a **lingerie stay** or hidden elastic from shoulder to shoulder stops slip.

## Testing
- Move the form arm or get a model to lean forward: the facing must not show.
- Check in the final cloth; heavier crepes may not need weights, satins usually do.

## Gotchas
- Shallow facings flip out on the first wear.
- Pressing a crease into the fold kills the soft drape.
- Weights sewn too tight drag; let them hang free in a small pocket.

## Verify
- [ ] Facing deeper than the lowest fold.
- [ ] Weights or tacks specified on the pattern.
- [ ] Facing invisible when bending forward.
