# Bias seams and twist control

## Why bias twists
Woven cloth stretches differently in warp and weft. On true bias both act at 45 degrees and
the piece drops evenly; if a piece is a few degrees off-bias, one direction dominates and the
garment **spirals** around the body.

## Rules
1. Mark true bias accurately (fold so warp lies on weft, or measure 45 degrees off the selvedge
   with a large set square). Off by 2-3 degrees is visible.
2. **Mirror the grain at a seam**: the two pieces of a bias seam must meet with grains in
   matching but opposite directions (chevron), so each side stretches the same amount.
   A side seam joining "bias one way" to "bias the other way" hangs straight.
3. Avoid joining a bias edge to a straight-grain edge unless planned (it ripples).
4. Cut wide seam allowances (2-2.5 cm); hang the toile 24 hours before truing (see letting
   bias hang).
5. Sew with a slight stretch and a small zigzag or a stretch stitch so the seam stretches with
   the fabric; French seams on sheers.

## Stabilising
- Stay necklines and armholes with silk organza strips or fusible stay tape cut on the straight.
- Leave hems until the garment has dropped.

## Gotchas
- Different fabric batches stretch differently; cut all pieces from one layout.
- Prints on bias look rotated; check motif direction on the cut.
- Narrow overlocked seams pucker on bias; use wider allowances and finish after hanging.

## Verify
- [ ] Bias line within about 1 degree on each piece.
- [ ] Grains mirrored at seams (chevron).
- [ ] Garment hung before truing hem and seams.
