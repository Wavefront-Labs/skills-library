# Negative ease and stretch reduction

**Goal:** close-fitting knit garments that hug the body comfortably, by making them
smaller than the body by a controlled amount.

## Reduction amounts (indicative, circumferences)
| Fabric stretch | Reduction for close fit |
|---|---|
| Stable (~20%) | 0-5% |
| Moderate (25-50%) | 5-10% |
| High (50-75%) | 10-15% |
| Swim/activewear (75%+) | 15-25% widthways, plus length reduction |
Reduce widthways in the stretch direction; length usually reduced less (0-5%) or not
at all unless the fabric stretches 4-way.

## Draping knits on the form
1. Measure stretch (see the stretch-percentage skill).
2. Drape the knit itself (not muslin). Mark grain (wales) vertical on CF.
3. Smooth with **slight, even stretch** around the body, not pulled at one point; pin
   along seams only, pins at a low angle.
4. Mark seams with chalk or thread tracing; knits don't take pencil well.
5. Remove and lay **relaxed** before truing: the shape will shrink back; this is the
   pattern.
6. Darts are rarely needed; shaping comes from stretch and seams.

## Bodycon check
Toile in the real knit; check for: horizontal pull lines (too tight), sagging at seat/
bust (too loose or low recovery), show-through of underwear, seam strength (use stretch
stitches: overlock 514, zigzag).

## Gotchas
- Same reduction for all knits ignores their stretch; measure every fabric.
- Pins pulling on knit distort the drape; use plenty of pins with even tension.
- Over-reduction causes transparency and seam failure.

## Verify
- [ ] Reduction percentage based on measured stretch.
- [ ] Drape traced from relaxed fabric.
- [ ] Toile passes movement and transparency checks.
