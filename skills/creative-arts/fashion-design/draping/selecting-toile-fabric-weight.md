# Selecting toile fabric weight

**Rule:** the toile must imitate the final fabric's weight, drape and stretch, or the shape you
approve on the form will not survive in the real cloth.

## Muslin/calico grades (US muslin = UK calico)
| Toile | Use for |
|---|---|
| Light muslin | blouses, shirting, light dresses |
| Medium muslin | general bodices, skirts, dresses in cotton/wool suiting |
| Heavy muslin / calico | jackets, trousers, structured dresses |
| Canvas / duck | coats, heavy outerwear, structured volume |

## Substitutes when muslin lies
- **Silk, chiffon, crepe, bias garments**: drape in a cheap fabric of similar weight and fluidity
  (rayon/viscose challis, poly crepe, silk habotai) because muslin is too stiff.
- **Knits**: use a jersey with the same stretch percentage and recovery as the final fabric.
- **Heavy wool coating**: felt or melton offcuts for collars and volume checks.
- **Leather**: vinyl or heavy non-woven to test seam placement.

## Prep
- Tear to find grain, press flat, mark grain and cross-grain with pencil.
- Pre-shrink if the toile will be steamed heavily during fitting.

## Gotchas
- A full skirt draped in light muslin looks floaty but collapses in wool.
- Stiff muslin on a cowl gives a rigid fold that never appears in silk.
- Note the toile fabric on the pattern so the fit is interpreted correctly.

## Verify
- [ ] Toile weight and drape comparable to final fabric.
- [ ] Knit toile stretch tested and matching.
- [ ] Toile fabric recorded with the drape.
