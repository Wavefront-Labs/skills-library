# Walking seams and matching notches

**Goal:** every seam that sews together has the same length (or a deliberate ease amount)
and notches that tell the sewer exactly how to match them.

## Walking a seam
1. Place the two pattern pieces on top of each other at the seam line start, seam lines
   overlapping (not cutting edges).
2. Use an awl or pencil point as a pivot; "walk" one piece along the other in short
   steps, pivoting so the seam lines stay together.
3. At the end, compare: lengths equal, or note the difference.
4. Typical intended differences: back shoulder 0.3-0.6 cm longer than front (eased over the
   shoulder blade); sleeve cap 2-4 cm longer than the armhole in tailored sleeves (cap ease),
   less in shirts; zero for most side seams.

## Notches
- Single notch on front seams, **double notch** on back seams (industry convention), so
  pieces can't be swapped.
- Place notches where alignment matters: waist on side seams, apex level on princess seams,
  shoulder point on sleeves, ease start/end points.
- Walk to place matching notches on both pieces at the same distance along the seam.

## Distributing ease
Mark the zone where ease goes (e.g. sleeve cap between front and back notches) and the
zone with none (underarm). The sewer eases only between the notches.

## Gotchas
- Walking cutting lines instead of seam lines gives false lengths (seam allowances differ).
- Missing notches on curves = misaligned seams and twisted sleeves.
- Too much cap ease in fabrics that don't shrink (synthetics) puckers.

## Verify
- [ ] All seam pairs walked; differences intentional and recorded.
- [ ] Notches placed with front/back convention.
- [ ] Ease zones marked between notches.
