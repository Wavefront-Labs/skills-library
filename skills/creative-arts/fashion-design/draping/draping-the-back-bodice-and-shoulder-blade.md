# Draping the back bodice and shoulder blade

## Prepare
Muslin about 30 cm wide plus allowances, length nape to waist plus 10 cm. Draw the
lengthwise grain 2.5 cm from the edge (CB) and a cross-grain at the **shoulder blade level**
(about a quarter of the way from nape to waist).

## Drape
1. Pin the grainline to CB at nape and waist; keep it vertical.
2. Align the cross-grain with the shoulder blade line, level to the armhole; pin at the blade.
3. Smooth up over the blade to the neck; clip the neckline allowance and pin the neck curve.
4. Smooth toward the shoulder: the excess between the blade and the shoulder is the
   **shoulder blade dart** (typically 1-1.5 cm on a standard form). Either pin a dart
   (about 7-8 cm long, aimed at the blade) or leave it as **ease** to be eased into the
   front shoulder seam (common on blouses and jackets up to about 0.6-1 cm).
5. Below the blade, smooth toward the waist; pin a **back waist dart** roughly in line with
   the blade point, leaving ease (pinch 0.5-1 cm).
6. Pin the side seam from underarm to waist without stretching; keep the cross-grain level.

## Mark and check
- Mark neck, shoulder, armhole (shoulder point, mid-armhole notch, underarm), side seam,
  waist, darts. Crossmark at side seam to match the front.
- Back armhole notch is a double notch by convention.
- Side seams of front and back must be the same length; shoulder lengths match except the
  planned ease.

## Gotchas
- Pulling the cross-grain up at the side gives a back that rides up.
- Overlarge waist darts give a swayback look; leave ease.

## Verify
- [ ] CB on grain, cross-grain level at the blade.
- [ ] Shoulder dart or ease amount recorded.
- [ ] Front and back side seams equal.
