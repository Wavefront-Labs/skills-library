# Back and armhole cowls

## Back cowl
1. Cut a large square on **true bias**; the bias line becomes CB.
2. Fold the top edge down to make a self-facing (see cowl facings) and pin at the shoulders.
3. Lower the centre to the desired depth; folds form as the fabric drops. Adjust the
   shoulder pins outwards/upwards to add folds.
4. Check exposure from the side: deep back cowls show the bra line; agree depth with the design.
5. Pin the side seams and waist; the cowl hangs from the shoulders only.

## Armhole cowl
1. Bias placed so folds fall from the shoulder point toward the underarm.
2. Lift fabric at the shoulder to create folds that hang into the armhole.
3. The underarm stays fitted to avoid exposure; anchor with a stay.

## Control
- Number of folds is set by how much fabric is lifted at the anchor points.
- Small hand tacks or a weight in the lowest fold keep folds consistent.
- Use drapey fabrics (silk crepe, satin, jersey); stiff fabric folds rigidly.

## Gotchas
- Bias off-angle makes the cowl hang to one side.
- Cowl too deep at CB slides off the shoulders; add a hidden strap or elastic stay.
- Muslin exaggerates stiffness; test in the real cloth.

## Verify
- [ ] CB on true bias; folds symmetrical.
- [ ] Depth and exposure checked from side and back.
- [ ] Anchors or weights noted on the pattern.
