# Straight and A-line skirt drape

**Goal:** fitted skirt foundations with correct balance, from which flared and panelled
skirts derive.

## Prepare
Style tape: waist, hip line (about 18-20 cm below waist, level all round), side seams,
CF, CB. Muslin length = waist to hem + 10 cm; width = CF to side seam + 10 cm at the hip.
Mark grain (CF/CB) and the hip cross-grain line.

## Straight skirt (front)
1. Grain on CF; pin at waist and hip.
2. **Cross-grain level along the hip line** to the side seam; pin at the side hip.
3. Add ease: pinch about 0.5-1 cm in the front panel at hip (quarter of total hip ease).
4. Hang straight down from the hip to hem; side seam vertical (perpendicular to floor).
5. Above the hip, take the excess at the waist into **1-2 darts** (front darts shorter,
   about 9-10 cm; back darts longer, 12-14 cm), the rest into the side seam curve.
6. Mark waist, darts, side seam, hip notch.
Back: same, with larger darts (more seat shaping).

## A-line
1. Pin grain at CF and the waist.
2. Instead of folding darts, **release some dart fullness downward**: rotate the fabric so
   the excess drops below the hip as flare, keeping grain on CF.
3. Typically keep a small waist dart and release the rest; side seam flares out evenly.
4. Check the side seams of front and back flare by the same amount so the skirt doesn't
   swing to one side.

## Gotchas
- A drooping hip cross-grain = skirt swings forward or back; re-balance.
- Too little hip ease: skirt rides up when walking and sitting.
- Walking ease for straight skirts: add a vent or kick pleat (hem circumference needs room).

## Verify
- [ ] Cross-grain level at the hip, side seams vertical.
- [ ] Darts point toward the fullest parts and are marked.
- [ ] A-line flare equal at front and back side seams.
