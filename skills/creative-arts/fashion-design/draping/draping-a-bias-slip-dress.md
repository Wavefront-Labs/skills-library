# Bias slip dress draping

**Goal:** a dress cut on the true bias that clings and drapes over the body with few or no
darts, in the tradition of Madeleine Vionnet's 1920s-30s bias cutting.

## Prepare
- Fabric: muslin behaves poorly on bias; drape in a soft fabric close to the final
  (crepe, satin-back crepe, or soft rayon).
- Mark the **true bias** (45 degrees to the selvedge) as the centre line on each piece; also
  mark the straight grain for reference.

## Drape
1. Pin the bias line on CF; pin at neck/bust and at the hip.
2. Smooth across the bust; on the bias the fabric shapes itself; minimal or no darts.
   Fullness can become soft drape at the neckline (cowl) or be eased.
3. Do not stretch the fabric down; let it hang. Bias **grows in length and narrows in width**
   as it hangs.
4. Pin side seams loosely; mark neckline, strap points, side seams.
5. Back the same way, with bias on CB.

## Stabilise
- Neckline: stay with a narrow straight-grain strip or silk organza tape to stop it growing.
- Straps: bias rouleau or straight-grain straps; attach after the dress has hung.

## After draping
- Hang the dress (or the toile in real fabric) for **24-48 hours**, then remark length and
  side seams; the hem will have dropped unevenly.
- True side seams so front and back are on the same bias angle; mismatched angles twist.

## Gotchas
- Side seams with front and back on opposite bias directions can spiral.
- Pressing bias edges stretches them.
- Bias consumes much more fabric than straight-grain layouts.

## Verify
- [ ] True bias on CF and CB.
- [ ] Dress hung and remarked after 24+ hours.
- [ ] No twist visible at side seams in motion.
