# Front cowl neckline

**Goal:** soft, controlled folds falling at the front neckline, formed by bias fabric
draping between the shoulders.

## Prepare
- Fabric with drape (crepe, jersey, satin, soft wool); muslin is too stiff to judge.
- A square of fabric; draw the true bias line diagonally - it becomes CF.
- Fold the top edge down to make a self-facing (see below).

## Drape
1. Pin the bias line on CF at the waist.
2. Take the upper corners to the shoulders: pin at the neck point on each side, letting
   fabric fall in a U between them.
3. **Depth**: lower the neckline drop by bringing the fabric higher at the shoulders
   (pivoting at the shoulder): the more excess between shoulder points, the deeper the
   cowl.
4. **Number of folds**: create additional folds by lifting and pinning more fabric at the
   shoulder line in pleats (each pleat = another fold below). 2-4 folds usually read well.
5. Smooth the rest to the side seams and waist; take remaining fullness to waist gathers or
   pleats if needed.
6. Mark shoulder seam with each pleat, armhole, side seam, waist.

## Facing and weight
- Self-facing: extend the cowl edge upward by 8-15 cm and fold it inside so the
  underside never shows when the cowl moves.
- A small weight or bead at the CF fold can keep the cowl hanging.

## Gotchas
- Cowls in straight grain fall stiffly in angular folds.
- Too deep cowls expose underwear; check on a moving body.
- Shoulder pleats must be secured (stitched) so the cowl doesn't migrate.

## Verify
- [ ] CF on true bias.
- [ ] Depth and fold count match the design.
- [ ] Self-facing deep enough not to show in movement.
