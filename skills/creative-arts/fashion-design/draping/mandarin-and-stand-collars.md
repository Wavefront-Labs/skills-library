# Mandarin and stand collars

**Goal:** a collar that stands up from the neckline at the angle you want: hugging the
neck, vertical, or flaring away.

## Prepare
- Finished bodice drape (or toile) on the form with the neckline marked and trued.
- Muslin strip about 10 cm wide x half neckline + 5 cm; grain line perpendicular to the
  long edge, placed at CB.

## Drape
1. Pin the grain line at CB, neck seam line aligned with the bodice neckline.
2. Work toward the front, **clipping** the collar's seam allowance and pinning it to the
   neckline every 1-2 cm.
3. Control the lean:
   - Clip and pull the lower edge **down** (more curve) -> collar **leans in** toward the
     neck (classic mandarin).
   - Keep the lower edge straighter -> collar stands **vertical** or flares out (funnel).
4. Mark the stand height (e.g. 3-4 cm for mandarin) and the front shape (rounded or square).
5. Mark the neckline seam and CF/closing point.

## Truing
The collar's neck edge is a curve that must equal the bodice neckline length (half);
the more curve, the closer it hugs. Add notch at shoulder seam position.

## Gotchas
- Collars that touch the neck all round feel tight; leave a finger's space.
- High stand collars need interfacing to stand; muslin overestimates support.
- Unequal curves at left/right halves; drape one half and mirror.

## Verify
- [ ] Collar neck edge length equals bodice neckline.
- [ ] Lean matches the design.
- [ ] Shoulder notch and CF marked.
