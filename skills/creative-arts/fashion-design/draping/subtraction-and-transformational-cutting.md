# Subtraction and transformational cutting

**Goal:** generate unexpected volume and form by working with negative space and redrawn
style lines rather than conventional block shapes.

## Subtraction cutting (Julian Roberts)
- Garments are built from large fabric sheets with **holes** (subtracted shapes) through which
  the body passes. Sewing the edges of a hole creates a tunnel; fabric hangs and spirals around it.
- Workflow: cut two large rectangles; mark the "plug" points (neck/shoulder points) from a
  basic block placed on them; cut tunnel shapes between holes; join outer edges.
- The size and position of the tunnel controls drape: longer tunnel = more spiral; circular
  holes = cowling; elongated ovals = cascading falls.
- Work quarter scale first (cheap, fast), then full scale in muslin on the form.

## Transformational reconstruction (Shingo Sato)
1. Fit a basic bodice toile on the form.
2. Draw new design lines freely across it with tape; the lines must pass through or near the
   bust point so dart volume can migrate into them.
3. Mark and number every piece, add balance marks, cut the toile apart.
4. Close darts; the volume moves into the new seams. Add 3D features (flutes, twists,
   pleats) by slashing and spreading the pieces.
5. Trace new pieces, sew, refit.

## Gotchas
- Number and notch every piece before cutting the toile apart; reassembly is otherwise guesswork.
- Results are fabric-dependent: test in final weight; jersey and crepe behave very differently.
- Photograph each stage; these methods are hard to reproduce without a record.

## Verify
- [ ] Every piece numbered, grain-marked and notched.
- [ ] Toile photographed front, side, back.
- [ ] Pattern traced and trued before production.
