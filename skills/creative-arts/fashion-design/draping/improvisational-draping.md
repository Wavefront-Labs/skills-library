# Improvisational draping on the stand

**Goal:** find shapes that drawing wouldn't produce, by manipulating fabric directly on the
stand, then capture them as reproducible patterns.

## Setup
- Uncut lengths (2-3 m) of fabrics in different behaviours: soft jersey, crisp cotton,
  wool, organza. The fabric decides much of the outcome.
- Camera on a tripod at a fixed position; timer.

## Method
1. Set a **constraint** per session (e.g. "only one seam", "fabric must stay one piece",
   "only pins at the shoulders", "start from a rectangle"). Constraints drive invention.
2. Work fast: pin, wrap, twist, fold, pleat; 5-10 minutes per experiment.
3. **Photograph each state** front/side/back before changing it.
4. Choose the strongest; refine: decide which parts become seams, darts, pleats; which
   fabric edges become hems.
5. Mark everything on the fabric: pin points, fold lines, grain direction, match points.
6. Remove, lay flat, trace (the shape is often surprising: asymmetric, non-garment-like).

## Making it reproducible
- Add seam allowances and notches; cut a second version from the traced pattern; adjust.
- Keep the rectangle/one-piece logic visible in the pattern for zero-waste potential.

## Gotchas
- Unmarked drapes can't be reproduced; mark before unpinning.
- Shapes that hold by pins may not hold by stitches; test the closure method.
- Body movement: try the drape on a person, not only the form.

## Verify
- [ ] Each session has a stated constraint and photo record.
- [ ] Chosen drape marked and traced.
- [ ] A second cut from the pattern reproduces the shape.
