# Flared and circle skirts on the form

**Goal:** place flare (cones of fabric) exactly where wanted, in the amount wanted, with a
level hem.

## Releasing flare
1. Grain on CF; pin at CF waist.
2. Working toward the side, clip the waist seam allowance and **pivot** the fabric down
   at a chosen point: the waist edge rises, a cone of flare drops below that point.
3. Pin at the pivot; repeat at regular intervals (e.g. every 4-6 cm) for evenly
   distributed flare, or concentrate pivots for flare at sides/back only.
4. Amount: the more the waist edge is lifted at each pivot, the deeper the flare.

## Circle and half-circle
- A full circle skirt makes the waist edge a circle; drafting by radius (waist / 2 pi) is
  more accurate than draping, but draping a quarter on the form shows how the fabric
  falls.
- Hem width needs fabric width: a full circle in 150 cm fabric fits only short lengths
  without piecing.

## Gored skirts
Drape panels (6, 8 gores) separately, adding flare at the gore seams from the hip down;
each gore's grain down its centre.

## Hem
- Let the skirt **hang for 24 hours** (bias areas at sides stretch).
- Level the hem with a hem marker or by measuring up from the floor, all round.
- Remove the excess that dropped on bias sections.

## Gotchas
- Flare on the bias drops more than on the straight; hem is uneven until it hangs.
- Too much flare at the CF looks bunchy over the stomach; place flare where body
  allows.
- Heavy fabrics (wool) and light fabrics (chiffon) flare differently; test in real cloth.

## Verify
- [ ] Flare cones positioned as designed and even where intended.
- [ ] Skirt hung before hem levelling.
- [ ] Hem measured level from floor all round.
