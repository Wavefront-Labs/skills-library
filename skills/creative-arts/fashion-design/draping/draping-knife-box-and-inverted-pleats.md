# Draping knife, box and inverted pleats

**Goal:** pleats that hang on grain, with even spacing and depth, designed on the form and
transferred precisely.

## Types
- **Knife pleat**: folds all one direction; one fold edge visible.
- **Box pleat**: two folds facing away from each other; flat panel on top.
- **Inverted pleat**: two folds meeting at the centre (box pleat reversed); common at CB
  of skirts and coats.
- Variants: kick pleat (short inverted pleat at a hem), stitched-down, released below hip.

## Calculations
- Each pleat consumes **2 x depth** of fabric (the underlay).
- Total fabric width = finished width + (number of pleats x 2 x depth).
- Example: 70 cm finished hip, 14 knife pleats of 4 cm depth -> 70 + 14 x 8 = 182 cm.
- Spacing: finished width / number of pleats (5 cm here).

## On the form
1. Mark pleat fold lines and placement lines on the muslin before draping (in pencil), with
   the grain parallel to the folds.
2. Fold and pin pleats at waist and hip; check folds hang vertical (on grain).
3. If shaping waist to hip: take the shaping inside the pleat (deeper at the waist than at
   the hip) and stitch down to the hip line.
4. Mark stitch-down length, fold direction (arrows), and hem; hem before pleating
   permanently.

## Gotchas
- Pleats off-grain twist and flare open.
- Too-deep pleats in thick fabrics bulk at the waist; trim underlays above stitching line.
- Pleats in synthetics can be heat-set permanently; natural fibres need edge-stitching.

## Verify
- [ ] Fabric width calculated from depth and count.
- [ ] Fold lines on grain and hanging vertically.
- [ ] Pattern marks show fold and placement lines with direction arrows.
