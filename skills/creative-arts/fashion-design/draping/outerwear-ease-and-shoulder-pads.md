# Ease over under-layers and shoulder pads

**Goal:** jackets and coats that fit over what will be worn beneath them, with the shoulder
structure the design needs.

## Set up the form
- **Dress the form** in the under-layers: a shirt or knit for jackets; a jacket for coats.
  Or add a layer of batting over the torso to simulate them.
- Pin **shoulder pads** at the design position: the pad extends 1-1.5 cm beyond the
  natural shoulder point for a tailored look, more for strong shoulders; raglan pads
  for raglan sleeves.

## Ease guidelines (over body)
- Jacket: chest ease about 10-16 cm; coat over jacket about 18-26 cm (see the ease
  table skill). On the form, pinch out ease evenly instead of pulling the muslin tight.
- Armhole: lower about 1.5-2.5 cm for jackets vs a bodice, more for coats.
- Shoulder: extend to the edge of the pad.

## Drape
1. Muslin heavier than for bodices (or canvas) so it simulates wool.
2. Grain on CF and CB, cross-grain level at chest.
3. Keep ease pinched in vertical tucks at front and back as you drape, then release when
   marking.
4. Mark the armhole around the pad, the lower armhole, the side seams.

## Check
Sew a toile with pads; try over the real under-layers; reach forward, raise arms, sit.

## Gotchas
- Draping on the bare form produces jackets too tight over a shirt.
- Pads change the shoulder line and length; always drape with the final pads.
- Heavy coatings add bulk at seams; allow extra ease for thickness.

## Verify
- [ ] Form dressed in under-layers and pads during draping.
- [ ] Chest and armhole ease measured against targets.
- [ ] Toile tested in movement over the real layers.
