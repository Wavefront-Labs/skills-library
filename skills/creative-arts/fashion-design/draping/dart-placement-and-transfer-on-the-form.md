# Dart placement and transfer on the form

**Goal:** control bust fullness wherever the design wants it, knowing all darts are the
same fullness moved around the apex.

## Principle
Fullness between the bust and the narrower areas above and below the bust can be taken
up anywhere on a line that radiates from the **bust apex**. The amount stays the same;
only the position changes.

## Positions
Shoulder, neckline, armhole, side seam (underarm), French dart (from lower side seam
toward apex), waist, CF (bust dart into CF), and combinations (two smaller darts).

## On the form
1. Drape the front with grain on CF and cross-grain level at the bust line.
2. Smooth fabric from CF over the bust; push all excess toward the chosen position.
3. Fold the dart; legs stop **short of the apex** (roughly 2-3 cm for shoulder/waist darts,
   about 2.5-4 cm for side darts, more on fuller busts); the point aims at the apex.
4. Pin along the fold; mark both legs and the point.
5. To move the dart, unpin, smooth toward the new position, re-fold. Compare on the stand.

## Converting darts
- **To seams**: a dart running into a seam (princess, yoke) can be absorbed into the seam
  (see princess draping).
- **To gathers/tucks**: spread the fullness as gathers along a seam or yoke.
- **Partly to ease**: distribute a small dart as ease across a seam (e.g. 0.5 cm in the
  back shoulder).

## Gotchas
- Dart points ending on the apex produce a pointed bust; stop short.
- Very large single darts in thick fabric bubble; split into two.
- Plaids/stripes: dart position affects pattern matching; choose positions that
  keep stripes level.

## Verify
- [ ] Every dart points at the apex and stops short.
- [ ] Cross-grain stays level at the bust after dart folding.
- [ ] Dart legs marked and equal length for truing.
