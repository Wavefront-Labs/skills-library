# Oversized and dropped-shoulder draping

**Goal:** deliberate volume that still hangs straight and balanced, with a dropped shoulder
that reads as intent rather than a size too big.

## Decide the numbers first
- **Shoulder drop:** distance the armhole seam sits below/beyond the natural shoulder point:
  3-5 cm (relaxed), 8-12 cm (strong drop), 15 cm+ (near kimono).
- **Body ease:** 15-25 cm at chest for relaxed, 30-50 cm for oversized.
- **Armhole depth** increases with drop: lower the underarm 2-6 cm or the sleeve binds.

## Drape
1. Pad the form or add an arm; volume needs something to hang from.
2. Pin CF/CB grain vertical. Extend the shoulder line straight past the form's shoulder point
   to the chosen drop; pin a style tape marking the new armhole.
3. Let the body fall from the shoulder; pin side seams with the excess split evenly front and
   back. Check from the side: side seam vertical, hem level.
4. Sleeve: a dropped shoulder needs a flatter, lower cap (cap height 5-10 cm instead of
   14-16 cm) so the arm can lift.

## Balance and swing
- Volume tends to swing back. If the hem kicks back, add length at the CB hem or take a little
  from CF shoulder; if it swings forward, the reverse.
- For trapeze volume, add flare at the side seam evenly, not only at CF.

## Gotchas
- Dropped shoulder with a high-cap sleeve pulls the arm down.
- Oversizing scales badly across sizes; grade drop and ease deliberately, not proportionally.
- Heavy fabric collapses the volume; drape in a muslin of similar weight.

## Verify
- [ ] Side seams vertical, hem level.
- [ ] Arm lifts to 45 degrees without lifting the hem dramatically.
- [ ] Drop, ease and armhole depth recorded on the spec.
