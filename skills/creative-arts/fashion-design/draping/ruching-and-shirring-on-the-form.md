# Ruching and shirring on the form

## Ruching (gathered fabric over a fitted base)
1. Drape or draft the **fitted underlayer** first; it controls fit.
2. Cut the outer layer longer (ruching ratio 1.5:1 to 2:1 along the gathered direction; 1.3:1
   for a soft effect).
3. Pin the outer layer at the seams, gathering it along side seams or CF; distribute folds by
   hand on the form until the rhythm looks right.
4. Baste or tack the folds to the underlayer at intervals so they hold.
5. Mark gather start/stop points and the ruched length vs the base length on both pattern pieces.

## Shirring
- Rows of gathers across a panel; on the form, test by gathering a sample panel to the target width.
- **Elastic shirring** (shirring elastic in the bobbin) gives stretch; rows 1-1.5 cm apart;
  finished width roughly 1/2 to 2/3 of the cut width depending on fabric weight.
- Channel shirring: elastic or cord through casings, for adjustable fit.

## Fabric
Light, soft fabrics (jersey, chiffon, crepe, poplin for elastic shirring). Heavy fabrics
bulk up.

## Gotchas
- Ruching without an underlayer distorts the fit and twists seams.
- Elastic shirring shrinks differently on cotton vs viscose; sample the exact fabric.
- Ruched side seams in jersey need a clear elastic stay to keep the gather.

## Verify
- [ ] Ratio recorded on the pattern.
- [ ] Base fits independently of the ruching.
- [ ] Shirring sample at final width approved.
