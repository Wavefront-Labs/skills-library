# Letting bias hang and drop before marking

**Goal:** account for the lengthening of bias areas under gravity before the hem and seams
are finalised.

## Why
Woven fabric on the bias has no yarn running in the direction of the pull, so it
**lengthens and narrows** under its own weight. Amounts vary by fabric: from under 1 cm
in firm cloth to several cm in heavy crepes and charmeuse over a long skirt.

## Procedure
1. After draping and rough-truing, cut the toile (or real fabric) with extra hem allowance
   (5-10 cm on bias sections).
2. Sew or pin side seams loosely; hang on the form or a padded hanger for **24-48 hours**.
   Heavy fabrics: longer, or add a weight at the hem.
3. Re-mark hem level from the floor all round with a hem marker.
4. Re-check side seams: grown seams may ripple; re-pin them hanging, then mark.
5. Transfer the corrections to the pattern: add length to on-grain areas or remove from
   bias areas.

## Stabilising
- Stay-stitch or tape necklines, armholes and waist seams on bias pieces right after
  cutting.
- Sew seams with slight stretch or with a narrow zigzag so seams can move with the bias.
- Hem with a narrow rolled hem or a hand hem after the drop is corrected.

## Gotchas
- Marking the hem immediately after draping: it will be uneven later.
- Different fabrics drop differently; a muslin toile doesn't predict the silk.
- Storing finished bias garments folded rather than hung may change their hang.

## Verify
- [ ] Toile hung 24+ hours before hem marking.
- [ ] Hem level from the floor at all points.
- [ ] Corrections transferred to the pattern.
