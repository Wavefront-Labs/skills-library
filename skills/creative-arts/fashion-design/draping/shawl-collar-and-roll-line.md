# Shawl collar and roll line

**Goal:** a continuous lapel-and-collar that rolls smoothly from a break point at the front
to the back neck.

## Concepts
- **Break point**: where the lapel turns back on the CF edge (usually at the top button).
- **Roll line**: the fold line from the break point to the neck, continuing round the back
  neck.
- **Stand**: height the collar rises at CB before folding (2.5-3.5 cm typical).
- **Fall**: width of the visible collar at CB; must exceed the stand to cover the neck seam.

## Drape
1. Front bodice with CF extension (button stand) draped; mark break point.
2. Extend the front muslin above the neckline, enough for the collar.
3. Fold back along a line from the break point toward the neck point: the **roll line**.
   Pin the fold.
4. Shape the outer edge of the shawl on the folded-back fabric (width at the widest
   point, curve toward the break).
5. At the back neck, drape a separate collar piece (or the extension, if cut in one
   with a CB seam): stand up to the stand height, fold over to fall width, pin.
6. Mark the roll line, outer edge, neckline seam and CB.

## Undercollar and facing
- The front facing is cut as one with the shawl's upper collar; the undercollar is
  part of the front bodice with a CB seam, often cut on the bias in tailoring.
- Upper collar slightly larger than undercollar (turn of cloth: ~2-3 mm) so the seam
  rolls under.

## Gotchas
- Roll line not continuous: collar breaks or buckles at the shoulder.
- Fall too small: neck seam shows at back.
- Thick fabrics need more turn-of-cloth allowance.

## Verify
- [ ] Smooth roll line from break point round CB.
- [ ] Fall covers stand seam by at least 0.5-1 cm.
- [ ] Turn-of-cloth allowance on the upper collar/facing.
