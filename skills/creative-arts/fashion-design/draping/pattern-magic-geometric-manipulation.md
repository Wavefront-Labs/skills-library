# Pattern Magic style geometric manipulation

**Goal:** sculptural volumes and surfaces created by geometric operations on a basic
block, in the manner of Tomoko Nakamichi's *Pattern Magic* books.

## Operations
- **Insertions**: add triangles, squares, circles into slashes in the bodice; the inserted
  shape stands out as a 3D form.
- **Slash and spread**: cut lines from an edge toward a pivot and open them for volume.
- **Folding and tucking**: fold the pattern before cutting to create tucks that pop out as
  shapes.
- **Twists**: cut and rotate sections so seams spiral around the body.
- **Stretch tricks**: knits allow shapes that hold by tension.

## Process
1. Start from a fitted block (bodice, skirt) as a reference.
2. Work at **half scale** on a half-scale form with paper or light muslin for speed.
3. Draw the operation lines on the block; cut, insert or rotate; tape.
4. Make the 3D test in paper/muslin; photograph; iterate.
5. Scale to full size; toile in a fabric with comparable body.

## Material choice
- Crisp fabrics (organza, scuba, taffeta, felted wool) hold geometric volumes.
- Soft fabrics collapse inserted shapes into drapes.

## Gotchas
- Effects that work in paper often collapse in fabric; test in real cloth early.
- Complex insertions demand precise sewing; mark every notch.
- Grain changes across inserted pieces affect the hang; mark grain on each.

## Verify
- [ ] Half-scale test proves the effect.
- [ ] Full-scale toile in comparable fabric holds the shape.
- [ ] All inserted pieces have grain lines and notches.
