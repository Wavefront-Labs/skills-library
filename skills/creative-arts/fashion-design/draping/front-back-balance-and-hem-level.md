# Front-back balance and hem level

**Goal:** a garment that hangs straight from the body: side seams plumb, CF and CB
vertical, hem parallel to the floor (or to the intended line).

## Check
- Stand the model or form square; look from the side at eye level.
- Side seam should drop straight down from underarm to hem, not swing forward or back.
- Measure hem height from the floor at CF, sides and CB with a hem marker or ruler.

## Read the faults
| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Hem rises at CF, side seam swings forward | Front too short (bust/abdomen needs length) | Add length to front at shoulder or waist; or take from back |
| Hem rises at CB, side seam swings back | Back too short (round back, prominent seat) | Add length at CB neck/shoulder or seat |
| Skirt side seam pulls forward | Abdomen | Lower back waist or raise front waist |
| Garment slides back off the shoulders | Back neck too short or front too long | Shorten front shoulder, raise back neck |

## Correct
1. Fix balance before anything else; other faults often disappear.
2. Lift or lower at the shoulder line (tops) or waist line (skirts/trousers) rather than at the
   hem: adjusting only the hem leaves the side seam swinging.
3. Re-mark the hem level from the floor after balance is right.

## Gotchas
- Flared and bias garments drop unevenly; let them hang 24 hours before levelling the hem.
- Posture varies; balance to the fit model, and note it on the spec.
- Heel height changes balance at the hem.

## Verify
- [ ] Side seams plumb from the side view.
- [ ] Hem height equal all round from the floor (or as designed).
- [ ] Changes applied to the pattern at shoulder/waist, not only the hem.
