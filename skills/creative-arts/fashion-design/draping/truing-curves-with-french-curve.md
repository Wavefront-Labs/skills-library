# Truing curves with French curve and hip curve

**Goal:** turn the dotted marks from the drape into clean, smooth curves that sew and fit as
the drape did.

## Tools
- **French curve**: tight curves (armholes, necklines, crotch).
- **Hip curve (vary form curve)**: long gentle curves (side seams over the hip, hems,
  princess seams).
- Clear grid ruler for straight lines; L-square for right angles.

## Method
1. Lay the drape flat, press lightly, and pin to the table on grain.
2. Straight seams: connect marks with the ruler (shoulders, CF, side seams above hip).
3. Curves: find the part of the French curve that touches **at least three consecutive dots**;
   draw between the first two, move the curve to cover the next three; continue so the line
   blends.
4. Ignore single stray dots that break the flow (marking errors), unless they're notch points.

## Blending across seams
- Place adjoining pieces together at the seam (e.g. front and back shoulder) and draw the
  neckline and armhole as one continuous curve across the join.
- **Right angles**: necklines and armholes meet CF/CB and shoulder/side seams at about 90
  degrees, so the curve doesn't form a point or a dip when sewn. Same for hems at side
  seams and waistlines at CF/CB.

## Gotchas
- Drawing curves freehand produces wobbles that show when sewn.
- Not blending across seams creates peaks at shoulder or side seam points.
- Truing without darts folded closed gives wrong seam shapes (see truing darts).

## Verify
- [ ] All curves smooth with no peaks at seam junctions.
- [ ] Right angles at CF/CB and seam ends.
- [ ] Trued pattern laid back on the form matches the drape.
