# Draping a bodice toile and truing it to flat

**Goal:** find a silhouette in three dimensions on the stand, then capture it as a flat
pattern accurate enough to sew a second toile that looks like the first. The draping
itself is the easy part; keeping grain and transferring markings honestly is the skill.

## 1. Prepare the stand and fabric
- **Style tape** the stand: centre front (CF), centre back (CB), bust line, waist,
  shoulder seam, side seam, armhole, neckline. Most stands have seams marked; tape makes
  them visible through muslin. Pad the stand to the target body if needed.
- **Muslin/calico** close in weight and drape to the final fabric. A light muslin for a
  wool coat lies; use a heavier calico or a cheap version of the real fabric.
- **Block** the fabric: tear (don't cut) along the grain so edges are on-grain, then
  steam and gently pull until warp and weft are at true right angles.
- Rough-cut pieces generously (5–8 cm extra beyond the area to cover).
- Draw the **grainline** (lengthwise, parallel to selvedge) and the **cross-grain**
  (bust or hip level) in pencil on each piece before it touches the stand.

## 2. Drape the front bodice
1. Pin the lengthwise grain line exactly on CF tape; pin at neck and waist.
2. Match the cross-grain line to the bust line, level, across to the side seam. If it
   dips or climbs, the grain is twisting and the toile will hang wrong.
3. Smooth up over the bust to the shoulder/neck; clip the neckline seam allowance
   so it lies flat; pin along the neck.
4. Smooth excess toward where the dart or style line will be — e.g. into a shoulder
   dart, a waist dart, or a princess seam. Pin the dart folded toward its intended
   direction; dart point stops short of the bust apex (often ~2–3 cm; adjust to taste).
5. Pin the side seam and waist without stretching. Leave wearing ease — pinch, don't pull.
6. **Mark** everything with pencil or dots while still on the stand: neckline, shoulder
   seam, armhole (key points: shoulder point, mid-armhole, underarm), side seam, waist,
   both legs of each dart. Use cross-marks across seams that will join (match points).

Back follows the same steps from CB, usually with a shoulder blade dart or ease.

## 3. Truing to flat
Unpin, lay flat, and press lightly (no steam stretching):
- Connect dots with a ruler for straight seams and a French curve for neck/armhole.
- **Fold darts closed** before truing the seam line that crosses them, then cut along
  the seam — this gives the correct dart "flange" shape.
- Walk adjoining seams (front vs back shoulder, side seams) against each other; lengths
  should match unless ease is intended. Note any ease amount.
- Check the armhole and neckline curves flow smoothly across shoulder and side seams
  when the pieces are placed together.
- Add seam allowances, grainlines, notches, CF/CB labels, piece names.

Then **cut a fresh toile from the trued pattern** and put it on the stand. That second
toile is the real test — the first one only proves the idea.

## Gotchas
- Grain drift is the number-one failure: the drape looked right because pins held it.
- Over-fitting a stand that is smaller or more rigid than a body — add ease and do a
  live fitting before committing.
- Bias areas stretch when pinned and when pressed; staystitch or mark immediately.
- Unmarked drape = lost work. Mark more than you think you need.

Production pattern making, grading and sewing sit with handcrafts/garment-construction
skills; this skill stops at a trued design pattern.

## Verify
- [ ] Grain and cross-grain lines sit on CF and bust line without twist.
- [ ] All seams, darts and match points were marked on the stand.
- [ ] Adjoining seams walk to equal length (or a stated ease).
- [ ] A second toile from the flat pattern reproduces the original silhouette.
