# Truing darts and dart flanges

**Goal:** darts that sew flat and true: equal legs, a clean point, and a seam allowance
("flange" or "dart cap") that matches the seam it is sewn into.

## Steps
1. Mark the dart point and both legs from the drape marks. Draw each leg as a straight line
   from the seam to the point (slightly convex for a fitted bust dart is acceptable).
2. Measure both legs: they must be equal length. If not, adjust the point or the seam end of
   the shorter leg; never leave one leg longer.
3. Pull the point back 1.5-3 cm from the apex (bust darts) so it does not end on the fullest point.
4. **Fold the dart closed** in the direction it will be pressed (vertical darts toward CF/CB,
   horizontal darts downward).
5. With the dart folded, true the seamline across it (blend with a French curve) and cut along
   the seam/cutting line. Unfold: the projecting point is the dart flange.
6. Add drill holes 1-1.5 cm inside the point and notches at the dart legs on the seamline.

## Wide darts
- Darts over ~3 cm intake: split into two darts or convert part to ease/gathers; or cut the
  dart open in thick fabric and press it flat.

## Gotchas
- Cutting the seam before folding the dart leaves a notch or step at the sewn seam.
- Pressing direction changes the flange shape; fold the way it will be pressed.
- Curved darts need extra notches along the leg to sew accurately.

## Verify
- [ ] Legs equal length.
- [ ] Seam line smooth across the folded dart.
- [ ] Drill hole and notches marked.
