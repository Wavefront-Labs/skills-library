# Godets and bias insets

**Godet:** a triangular or sector-shaped piece set into a seam or slit to add flare at the hem.

## Design on the form
1. Drape the base skirt or dress fitted to the point where flare should start.
2. Mark godet positions with style tape: in seams (panelled skirts) or in slashes.
   Typical heights: knee to hem, or high hip for dramatic flare.
3. Pin a muslin sector into the opening and adjust its angle until the flare looks right.
   Angles from 30 to 90 degrees; a half-circle godet for maximum swing.

## Grain
- Usually the godet's **centre line on straight grain** so both edges are slightly bias and
  hang evenly; or the whole godet on bias for maximum fluidity in light fabric.
- Edges of the godet must be the same length as the opening edges after the godet has hung.

## Setting the point
- Reinforce the slash point with a small square of fusible or organza.
- Sew each side from the point down, pivoting with the needle exactly at the point; never
  sew across the point in one pass.
- Press seams toward the main garment; the godet rolls under otherwise.
- Hang before levelling the hem; the godet drops.

## Gotchas
- Points that pucker: stitching not ending exactly at the point, or no reinforcement.
- Godets in a slash (not a seam) are weaker; use them in seams on heavy fabrics.

## Verify
- [ ] Godet lengths match opening lengths.
- [ ] Point flat with no pleat or hole.
- [ ] Hem levelled after hanging.
