# Padding the form to a fit model

**Goal:** a dress form that reproduces the measurements and posture of the body you
design for, so drapes fit the person without endless corrections. You can only pad up,
so start with a form slightly smaller than the target.

## 1. Measure
Measure the fit model/client and the form at the same points: bust, underbust, waist,
high hip, full hip, across front, across back, shoulder length, nape to waist,
front waist length, bust point to bust point, armhole depth. Note posture (forward
shoulders, sway back, high hip on one side).

## 2. Build up
- Bust: use commercial bust pads or shaped wadding; position apex at the model's bust
  point height and separation.
- Hips/seat: layers of polyester batting cut in decreasing ovals, largest at the widest
  point, feathered edges; build the seat projection separately from side hip.
- Shoulders: shoulder pads or batting to match slope and width; fill the hollow above the
  bust/collarbone if needed.
- Back: batting at the shoulder blades for a rounded back.
- Pin layers loosely, measuring repeatedly; aim within about 0.5 cm of target.

## 3. Cover
- Make a fitted **cover** from calico or a stable knit, drafted to the target measurements,
  or use a stretch cover; it compresses padding into smooth contours.
- Re-tape style lines (CF, CB, bust, waist, hip, side seams, armhole) on the cover.

## Gotchas
- Lumpy padding shows through drapes; feather edges and use a firm cover.
- Forms don't move or breathe; padding to measurements still needs a live fitting.
- Asymmetry: pad one side only when the client is asymmetric, and drape both sides.

## Verify
- [ ] Form measurements within ~0.5 cm of the model at key points.
- [ ] Style tape re-applied on the cover, level and vertical.
- [ ] Silhouette photographed beside the model in profile matches.
