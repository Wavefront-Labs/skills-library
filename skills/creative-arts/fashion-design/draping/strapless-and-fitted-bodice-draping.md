# Strapless and fitted bodice draping

**Problem:** a strapless bodice hangs from nothing. It has to grip the ribcage and the top
edge, so it is draped tighter than a basic bodice and built in layers.

## Drape
1. Drape with **panel seams** (princess from the top edge or a 6-8 panel corset layout)
   rather than darts: more seams give more shaping points.
2. Remove nearly all ease at the ribcage and waist; the top edge sits about 1-2 cm smaller
   than the form at the upper bust/top edge so it hugs.
3. Shape the top edge on the form with style tape: straight across, sweetheart, or curved;
   pin over the bust with the apex in the panel seam.
4. Extend below the waist to the high hip if the bodice stands alone; it anchors on the hip.
5. Mark boning lines: CF, each panel seam, side seams, and CB beside the closure.

## Build (inform the pattern and sample room)
- Shell, interlining (coutil or canvas), boning channels on the interlining, lining.
- Waist stay (grosgrain ribbon ~2.5 cm, fitted tight at the waist) carries weight off the bodice.
- Bra cups or a bust pad as needed.
- Spiral steel for curved seams, flat steel at CB and closure; plastic boning only for
  light support.

## Fit check
- Ask the model (or test on the form) to raise arms and sit: the bodice must not slide.
- Top edge must not gape at the bust or dig into the underarm.

## Gotchas
- Fitting a strapless bodice on a padded form with a hard bust gives gaping on a real body;
  always fit live.
- Too much ease at the top edge is the main cause of slipping.

## Verify
- [ ] Top edge with negative ease and no gape.
- [ ] Boning lines and waist stay specified.
- [ ] Live fitting passed sitting and arms-raised tests.
