# Draping a bodycon knit dress

**Goal:** a close-fitting dress that grips the body smoothly in stretch knit, without
cutting in or bagging after wear.

## Before draping
- Test the knit: stretch % across and lengthwise and recovery (see measuring stretch skill).
  Bodycon needs 4-way stretch or at least 50%+ widthways with elastane.
- Choose negative ease: commonly 5-10% under body measurement at bust/waist/hip for medium
  stretch, up to 15-20% for powernet-type fabrics.
- Drape in the final fabric (or an identical-stretch test knit); muslin cannot do this.

## Drape
1. Pin CF, greatest stretch running around the body.
2. Stretch the knit gently and evenly across the front, pinning side seams on the form at the
   intended reduction. Do not stretch lengthwise; it makes the dress ride up.
3. Shape with seams, not darts: princess or side panels let you control the waist; darts in
   knit pucker.
4. Pin the back the same way; match stretch tension front and back.
5. Mark neckline and hem; bodycon hem usually ends where stretch tension is still even.

## True and test
- Remove and lay flat: the flat pattern will be visibly smaller than the form.
- Sew a quick toile with stretch stitch or overlock; wear-test 20-30 minutes for creep at hip
  and seat.

## Gotchas
- Uneven stretch while pinning shows as diagonal ripples.
- Too much negative ease shows underwear lines and seams that roll.
- Elastane content under 3% often loses recovery at seat and knees.

## Verify
- [ ] Even tension, no diagonal drag lines.
- [ ] Sitting and walking test passed.
- [ ] Negative ease % recorded on the spec.
