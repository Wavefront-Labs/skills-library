# Measuring stretch percentage and recovery

**Goal:** know exactly how much a knit stretches and whether it comes back, because it
decides ease, negative ease, seam method and whether a pattern will fit.

## Stretch test
1. Lay the knit relaxed; mark 10 cm along the course (crosswise, usually the greater
   stretch) with pins, away from the edge.
2. Hold one pin at a ruler's zero; pull gently until the fabric resists (not to the
   limit), read the length.
3. **Stretch % = (stretched - 10) / 10 x 100**. 10 cm to 15 cm = 50%.
4. Repeat lengthwise (wale direction).
Lab methods: ASTM D2594 (stretch properties of knitted fabrics with low power).

## Recovery
- Hold stretched for ~30 s, release, measure after 1 minute.
- Good recovery: back to ~10 cm. Poor recovery (stays 10.5+ cm) = bagging at knees,
  elbows, seat.
- Elastane (spandex) content improves recovery.

## Classes (indicative)
| Stretch | Example | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Stable, under 20% | Ponte, double knit | Tailored knit garments, little negative ease |
| Moderate, 25-50% | Jersey, interlock | Tees, dresses |
| Stretch, 50-75% | Cotton-spandex | Fitted tops, leggings |
| Super stretch, 75%+ | Lycra blends | Swim, activewear |
| 2-way vs 4-way | Stretch in one or both directions | 4-way for bodywear |

## Gotchas
- Measuring at the cut edge gives false values (edges curl).
- A pattern made for 50% stretch fits tight in a 25% knit.
- Rib knits stretch much more widthways than they look.

## Verify
- [ ] Stretch and recovery recorded in both directions.
- [ ] Fabric class noted on the pattern.
- [ ] Pattern ease chosen to the measured stretch.
