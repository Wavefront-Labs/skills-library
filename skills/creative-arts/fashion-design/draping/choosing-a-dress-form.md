# Choosing a dress form

## Types
- **Professional draping forms** (e.g. Wolf, Kennett & Lindsell, Stockman, Siegel & Stockman):
  linen-covered, fully pinnable, with seamlines taped at CF, CB, princess, side, shoulder and
  neck. The standard for design studios.
- **Adjustable dial forms**: cheap, dials change girth but leave gaps; not pinnable deep, useful
  only for home fitting.
- **Full-body / pant forms**: with legs for trousers and jumpsuits; costlier.
- **Torso to hip** is standard; longer **ankle-length stands** for dresses and coats.

## Features that matter
- **Collapsible shoulders**: let you put fitted garments on and off without stretching them.
- Detachable arm(s) and magnetic or pinnable armhole plates.
- Height-adjustable stand and a heavy, wheeled base.
- Hem marker compatibility (stand base clear for a hem gauge).

## Size and shape
- Match the **brand's base size and fit model**, not a nominal size label; check bust, waist,
  hip, back length and shoulder against the size chart.
- Choose one size smaller where measurements differ and pad up to the fit model (padding
  adds, it cannot remove).
- Market-specific forms exist: missy, petite, plus, maternity, menswear, childrenswear, and
  industry standards (ASTM-based or ISO-based shapes) by region.

## Gotchas
- Old forms from different eras have different posture and bust shapes; don't mix in one range.
- Covers wear and tape lines lift; re-tape before a season.

## Verify
- [ ] Form measurements recorded and compared to the size chart.
- [ ] Style lines taped and straight.
- [ ] Padding plan written for gaps to the fit model.
