# Twisted and knotted drapes

## Twist
1. Drape the two halves of the garment (e.g. bodice fronts) with extra width extending
   toward the twist point (CF at bust, waist, or shoulder).
2. Cross the extensions over each other and twist them once (half turn) at the point; the
   twist creates folds radiating from it.
3. Adjust where the extensions are pinned beyond the twist: further away = tighter, more folds.
4. Pin and mark; the pattern is often **one piece with a slash and interlocking extension**
   (Pattern Magic style), cut so the twist forms when sewn.

## Knot
- A real knot needs an allowance: tie the actual fabric to measure the length used
  (often 25-40 cm per band for a simple overhand knot depending on width and weight).
- Fake knots (a wrapped tube) look cleaner and are sewn on; specify which.

## Grain
- Twisted bands behave best partly on bias; straight-grain bands crease sharply.
- Jersey twists hold well; crisp wovens look stiff.

## Pattern
- Unpin systematically, mark each fold's direction and crossmarks.
- Test: cut the pattern in a second toile and sew; does the twist reappear without hand
  arranging? If not, add tacks or a stay.

## Gotchas
- Twists at CF bust add bulk exactly where it shows; use lightweight fabric.
- Knots tied by the factory differ every time; specify a sewn construction.

## Verify
- [ ] Second toile from the pattern reproduces the twist.
- [ ] Fabric allowance for knots measured in final fabric.
- [ ] Construction (tacks, stays) specified.
