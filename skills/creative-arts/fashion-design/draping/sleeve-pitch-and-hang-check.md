# Sleeve pitch and hang check

**Goal:** a set-in sleeve that hangs following the natural forward angle of the arm, with no
twist lines across the sleeve, especially in jackets and coats.

## What good looks like
- Arm relaxed: the sleeve hangs slightly forward; the front crease (if any) sits over the
  thumb-to-centre line, the hem covers the wristbone evenly front and back.
- No diagonal drag from the sleeve head toward the elbow.

## Reading faults
| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Diagonal folds from back armhole to front elbow, sleeve hits the back of the hand | Sleeve pitched too far back | Rotate the sleeve head forward |
| Folds from front armhole to back elbow, sleeve sits in front of hand | Pitched too far forward | Rotate the sleeve head back |
| Horizontal folds under sleeve head | Cap too tall / too much ease | Lower cap height |
| Drag across the cap | Cap too short or ease too low | Raise cap, add ease |

## Correction on the stand or model
1. Unpin the sleeve from about notch to notch over the cap; leave the underarm pinned.
2. Rotate the whole sleeve in the armhole 0.5-1 cm at a time; re-pin and check from the side.
3. When it hangs clean, mark new pitch point on sleeve and armhole (the point aligned to the
   shoulder seam), and new front and back notches.
4. Transfer to the pattern: move the shoulder point mark on the sleeve; do not redraw the cap.

## Tips
- Check with a dress-form arm attached or on a fit model; pitch cannot be read on a bare form.
- Two-piece tailored sleeves pitch more forward than one-piece shirt sleeves.
- Shoulder pads change pitch; fit with the final pad in place.

## Verify
- [ ] No twist lines with the arm relaxed.
- [ ] Hem even around the wrist.
- [ ] New pitch and notches recorded on pattern pieces.
