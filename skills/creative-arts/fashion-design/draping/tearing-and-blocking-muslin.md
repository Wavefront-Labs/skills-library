# Tearing and blocking muslin on grain

**Goal:** fabric pieces whose lengthwise and crosswise grains are exactly perpendicular,
so grain lines drawn on them mean something on the stand.

## Steps
1. **Measure** the area to drape and add 5-8 cm all round (e.g. front bodice: CF to side
   seam plus 8 cm by neck-to-waist plus 8 cm).
2. **Snip and tear**: snip the selvedge, tear lengthwise and crosswise. Muslin/calico tears
   along a single yarn, giving a true grain edge. (Fabrics that won't tear: pull a thread
   and cut along the gap.)
3. **Remove selvedges** (they shrink and pull differently).
4. **Check squareness**: lay the piece on a gridded table or against an L-square. If the
   corner isn't 90 degrees, the weave is skewed.
5. **Block**: pull diagonally on the short corners (bias direction) to bring the grains
   square, then steam press along the grain (not on the bias) until it lies square.
6. **Pre-shrink** if the toile will be pressed heavily: steam the whole length first.
7. Press out tear-edge distortion; mark grain and cross-grain with pencil.

## Gotchas
- Cutting with scissors along a drawn line rarely follows the grain exactly.
- Pressing on the bias stretches the piece out of square again.
- Some calico is printed or finished off-grain; check with a pulled thread.

## Verify
- [ ] Each piece has torn or thread-pulled edges.
- [ ] Corners measure 90 degrees on a gridded mat.
- [ ] Grain and cross-grain lines drawn before draping.
