# Runway, street and retail trend shopping

**Goal:** turn looking at shows and shops into counts and price facts that a design and
merchandising team can act on.

## Runway analysis
1. Choose the set: e.g. 30 shows across Paris, Milan, London, New York for the season.
2. Build a tally sheet with columns per item: silhouette (oversized, fitted, A-line...),
   length, colour family, fabric, key detail, print type.
3. Go look by look (Vogue Runway gallery view). Tally, don't describe.
4. Report percentages: "oversized tailoring in 41% of shows, up from 22% last season".
   Comparisons over seasons matter more than single counts.

## Street style
Photograph or collect street images by location; tally the same columns. Street data
shows what is actually worn vs what is shown; the gap tells you timing.

## Comp shopping (store walks)
- Pick 5-8 competitors at your market level plus 1-2 above it.
- Per store record: category, item, fabric content (from label), price, colour,
  quantity on the floor, markdown status, fitting room queues.
- **Markdowns early in the season = poor sell-through**; full-price items at deep
  sizes late in the season = strong sellers.
- Photograph only where allowed; otherwise sketch quick flats and note details.
- Buy 1-3 key items to study construction and fabric; return policies vary, budget for it.

## Report format (one page per finding)
Finding, evidence (counts, photos, prices), implication for our line, recommendation
(add / adjust / avoid).

## Gotchas
- Counting only memorable looks inflates statement trends.
- Comparing prices across market levels without noting fabric/content misleads.
- Store-walk photos without permission can get you removed; use a notebook.

## Verify
- [ ] Each trend claim has a count or price behind it.
- [ ] Season-over-season comparison is included.
- [ ] Every finding ends with a concrete recommendation for the line.
