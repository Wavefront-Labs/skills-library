# Competitor and white-space analysis

**Goal:** find a position nobody owns that your customer cares about, and design into it.

## 1. Choose competitors
10-15 brands your customer actually buys (from surveys, social follows, store
neighbours), across price levels just above and below yours.

## 2. Positioning map
- Plot each brand on two axes that matter for buyers, e.g. price (x) vs
  classic-to-directional (y); or sustainability vs price; or fit inclusivity vs trendiness.
- Use evidence: average price of 5 key items; share of trend items in their range.
- Draw 2-3 maps with different axes; white space must hold on more than one.

## 3. Assortment audit
For each competitor tally: categories, number of styles per category, size range,
fabric quality (content labels), price per category. Spreadsheet, not impressions.

## 4. Find and test the gap
A white space is valid when:
- customers express the need (reviews, search terms, interviews),
- nobody serves it well (assortment gap or poor reviews),
- you can deliver it at a viable cost.
Example: "tall sizing in contemporary tailoring at mid price" - check search volume and
competitor size ranges.

## Gotchas
- Empty space on a map may be empty because nobody wants it.
- Using only aesthetic axes ignores price, which decides most purchases.
- Competitors move each season; refresh twice a year.

## Verify
- [ ] Maps use measured positions, not guesses.
- [ ] Gap is supported by customer evidence and competitor assortment data.
- [ ] Cost feasibility is checked before designing.
