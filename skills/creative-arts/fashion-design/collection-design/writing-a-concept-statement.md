# Writing the concept statement and collection narrative

**Goal:** a short written concept that filters decisions. If a design cannot be defended
by the statement, it is cut.

## Structure (150-250 words)
1. **One-line idea** (under 15 words): "Uniforms of night-shift workers, softened for
   off-duty life."
2. **Source**: the research that started it (primary research named specifically).
3. **Tension**: two forces the collection plays against each other (utility vs intimacy,
   rigid vs collapsing). Tension creates variety inside a coherent line.
4. **Customer**: who wears it and when.
5. **Design rules**: 3-5 concrete rules that every look obeys, e.g.
   - "Every garment has one functional pocket placed where it is not expected."
   - "No colour brighter than safety orange, which appears in one look only."
   - "Only natural fibres; nylon only in hardware tape."
6. **Mood words**: 3-5 adjectives, used to judge styling and photography later.

## Process
- Draft after research, before designing; rewrite after the first iteration grids.
- Read it aloud to someone outside fashion; if they can't picture anything, add specifics.
- Pin it to the line-up wall. Use it in editing: "which rule does this look follow?".

## Gotchas
- Abstract statements ("exploring identity and memory") steer nothing; name objects,
  places, materials.
- Too many rules strangle variety; 3-5 is enough.
- A narrative written after the collection to justify it shows; assessors and buyers notice.

## Verify
- [ ] One-line idea under 15 words.
- [ ] 3-5 checkable design rules.
- [ ] Each final look can be mapped to at least one rule.
