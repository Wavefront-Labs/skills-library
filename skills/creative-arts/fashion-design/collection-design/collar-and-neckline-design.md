# Designing collars, necklines and lapels

**Goal:** choose and specify the neck detail that frames the face and carries much of a
garment's character, in terms a pattern cutter can make.

## Types
- **Necklines** (no collar): jewel/crew, scoop, V, square, boat/bateau, sweetheart,
  halter, cowl, keyhole, off-shoulder.
- **Flat collars**: lie on the garment (Peter Pan, sailor). Little or no stand.
- **Stand collars**: stand up (mandarin, band, funnel).
- **Rolled collars**: stand then fall over (shirt collar with stand, convertible, polo).
- **Lapelled**: notched, peak, shawl; roll line determines how the lapel turns.

## Variables that change the look
- **Stand height** (e.g. shirt collar stand ~3-3.5 cm; higher = more formal).
- **Fall width** (must cover the stand seam by about 0.5-1 cm).
- **Point shape and length** (spread, point, button-down, rounded).
- **Lapel width and gorge height**; peak lapels look more formal.
- **Neckline depth/width**: wide boat necklines widen shoulders; deep V lengthens.

## Proportion
Relate collar size to shoulder width and garment scale. Oversized coats need larger
collars; tiny collars on big coats look accidental unless deliberate.

## Spec for the pattern cutter
Front and back flat views; collar point length, stand height, fall width, neck drop
(front/back), lapel width, break point position; note fabric for undercollar (often
cut on bias, or melton in tailoring).

## Gotchas
- A drawn collar that floats off the neck can't be made; show how it sits.
- Fall too narrow exposes the neck seam at the back.
- Wide necklines need a stay or facing to stop gaping.
