# Silhouette exploration: collage and photo-draping

**Goal:** generate many unexpected silhouettes quickly, bypassing the habits of the
drawing hand.

## Collage on croquis
1. Print 20-30 blank croquis (same pose) at small scale (about 12-15 cm tall).
2. Cut shapes from research images (architecture, objects, photos), magazines, coloured
   paper. Don't cut garment shapes; cut interesting forms.
3. Place shapes on the figure: rotate, enlarge, crop. Glue only when the shape suggests
   a garment.
4. Draw over with a fine liner to interpret: where are the seams, closures, how does it
   attach to the body?
5. Do 20+ in a session; select 5 and redraw clean.

## Photo-draping
1. Take a length of fabric (1-3 m of the intended weight: jersey, calico, wool) to a dress
   form or a person.
2. Pin, wrap, fold, tie in 5-minute experiments. Photograph each from front, side and
   back at the same distance and height.
3. Print photos small, trace the silhouette, then refine into a garment drawing.
4. Note the pin points; they become seams or darts later.

## Digital variant
Photoshop/Procreate: drop research images on a croquis layer, transform (Ctrl/Cmd+T),
mask, then draw on a top layer. Faster, but physical collage gives more accidents.

## Gotchas
- Choosing only the "nice" collages early; keep the odd ones for a second round.
- Drape photos at different camera distances distort proportion; fix a tripod spot.

## Verify
- [ ] 20+ explorations per session, with a selection marked.
- [ ] Each selected exploration has an interpretive drawing showing seams/attachment.
- [ ] Photos are shot from fixed position for comparability.
