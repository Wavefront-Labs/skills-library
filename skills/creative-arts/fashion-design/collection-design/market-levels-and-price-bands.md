# Market levels and price bands

**Goal:** know which level the line sits in, because level dictates fabric, construction,
production volume, calendar and price.

## The levels
| Level | What it means | Production | Typical design implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| Haute couture | Legally protected label in France (Federation de la Haute Couture et de la Mode criteria: made-to-order, Paris atelier, hand work, two shows a year) | One-offs, many fittings | Unlimited hand work, toiles per client |
| Luxury / designer RTW | Designer label ready-to-wear | Small runs, Italy/France/Japan factories | Premium fabrics, complex construction, show pieces |
| Diffusion / bridge | Second lines of designer brands | Medium runs | Simplified signature details |
| Contemporary | Between designer and high street | Medium runs, mixed sourcing | Fashion-forward but wearable |
| High street / mass | Large retailers, fast cycles | Large runs, offshore | Cost-engineered, trend-led |
| Value / discount | Lowest price | Very large runs | Minimal trims, simple cuts |

Price ranges vary by country and year; check current comparable retail prices when
positioning rather than relying on fixed numbers.

## Positioning a line
1. Pick 5 comparable brands; record retail prices for a shirt, trouser, knit, coat, dress.
2. Place your target price for each category within that range.
3. Work back to target cost (retail / markup; see the costing skill).
4. Check that your fabric and construction ambitions fit that cost.

## Gotchas
- "Couture" is a protected term in France; calling RTW couture misleads.
- A designer-level look at high-street price is a costing fantasy; decide which to give up.
- Mixing levels inside one line (a few luxury pieces in a contemporary line) needs a
  clear reason, e.g. image pieces.

## Verify
- [ ] Line has a named level and 5 comparable brands.
- [ ] Target retail per category is stated and inside the comparable range.
- [ ] Fabric/construction ambition fits target cost.
