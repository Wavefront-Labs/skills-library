# Design for durability, repair and disassembly

**Goal:** garments that stay in use longer and come apart cleanly at end of life.

## Physical durability
- Fabrics: check pilling (ISO 12945/ASTM D4970), abrasion (Martindale ISO 12947),
  tear strength, seam slippage, colourfastness. Set minimum values in the spec.
- Construction: reinforce stress points (bartacks at pocket corners, gussets at crotch
  and underarm), adequate seam allowances (1.5 cm side seams allow letting out).
- Avoid fragile embellishment in high-wear zones.

## Emotional durability
Timeless cuts, quality hand, versatility (wear multiple ways), personalisation and
storytelling make people keep clothes longer.

## Repair
- Spare buttons and patches; repair instructions on the label or online.
- Avoid fused/bonded construction where repair is impossible.
- Modular designs: detachable hoods, lining, sleeves.

## Disassembly and recycling
- **Mono-material** where possible (fabric, thread, trims all polyester or all cotton).
- Removable hardware (no rivets in 100% cotton jeans destined for recycling; Ellen
  MacArthur Jeans Redesign guidelines limit metal rivets, require easy removal).
- Avoid elastane blends where recyclability is a goal; mixed fibres are hard to recycle.
- Dissolvable thread or easy-unpick seams where available.

## Gotchas
- Durability for fast-trend items nobody keeps is wasted; match durability to use.
- Mono-material can compromise performance; decide per product.

## Verify
- [ ] Minimum physical test values in the spec.
- [ ] Stress points reinforced; repair plan exists.
- [ ] Material and trim composition checked for recyclability goal.
