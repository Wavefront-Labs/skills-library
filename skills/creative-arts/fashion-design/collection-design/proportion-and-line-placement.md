# Proportion and line placement

**Goal:** decide where horizontal and vertical lines cross the body so that a look feels
balanced (or deliberately unbalanced).

## Divisions
- **Equal halves** (waist exactly between shoulder and hem) look static.
- **Thirds / golden ratio** (~1:1.618, roughly 3:5) look dynamic. A cropped jacket ending
  at about a third of the total look length over a long skirt reads elegant.
- Unequal divisions can be strong statements (very dropped waist, micro-crop).

## Body landmarks to place lines on
Neck base, shoulder point, bust line, natural waist, high hip, full hip, crotch,
mid-thigh, knee, mid-calf, ankle. Hemlines landing exactly on the widest part of the
calf or hip widen it visually; a few cm above or below changes perception noticeably.

## Method
1. Draw the look on a 9-head croquis; mark landmarks.
2. Try 3 versions of the main horizontal (waist seam, jacket hem, skirt hem) at
   different landmarks; compare side by side at small scale.
3. Check verticals: centre front closures, princess seams and long lapels elongate.
4. Check the ratio of top to bottom garment lengths across the whole line for variety.

## Live check
Pin a tape or ribbon at the line on a fit model or dress form and photograph from 3 m;
croquis proportions (9 heads) exaggerate leg length versus a real body (~7.5 heads).

## Gotchas
- Croquis legs are long; a proportion that works on paper can look stumpy on a person.
- Heels change proportion; decide styling footwear before finalising hems.

## Verify
- [ ] Main horizontals placed on deliberate ratios or landmarks.
- [ ] Checked on a real body or form, not only on the croquis.
- [ ] Hem/length variety exists across the line.
