# Ease allowances by garment type

**Goal:** convert a body measurement into a garment measurement with the right amount of
ease for comfort, movement and the intended look.

## Two kinds of ease
- **Wearing (functional) ease**: minimum to breathe, sit and move.
- **Design (style) ease**: extra for the silhouette (relaxed, oversized).

## Indicative bust/chest ease over body circumference (wovens)
| Garment | Slim | Regular | Relaxed | Oversized |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fitted dress/blouse | 5-8 cm | 8-10 cm | 12-16 cm | 20 cm+ |
| Shirt | 8-12 cm | 12-18 cm | 18-24 cm | 25 cm+ |
| Jacket | 10-12 cm | 12-16 cm | 16-20 cm | 25 cm+ |
| Coat (over jacket) | 16-20 cm | 20-26 cm | 26-32 cm | 35 cm+ |
Hip ease for skirts/trousers: roughly 4-6 cm fitted, 6-10 regular. Knits may use **zero or
negative ease** depending on stretch. Aldrich's *Metric Pattern Cutting* tables give block
ease values; adjust by brand fit intent.

## Steps
1. Choose fit intent per style (written on the spec).
2. Garment measurement = body + ease (circumference), then halve for flat specs.
3. Check layering: an outer layer's chest and armhole must exceed the inner layer's
   garment measurement plus fabric thickness allowance.
4. Confirm on a fit model in motion: sit, reach, cross arms.

## Gotchas
- Ease that suits a light shirting is too tight in a thick wool.
- Oversized fits still need controlled shoulders/armholes or they look like the wrong size.
- Different ease in tops and bottoms at the same size breaks coordinated outfits.

## Verify
- [ ] Each style has a stated fit intent and ease value per key POM.
- [ ] Layering checked between inner and outer pieces.
- [ ] Movement test passed on a fit model.
