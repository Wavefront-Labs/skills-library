# Volume and structure strategies

**Goal:** get the volume you drew to actually stand up in fabric, with acceptable weight,
comfort and cost.

## Sources of volume (cheapest to most complex)
1. **Cut**: flare (circle, gores, godets), added width with gathers or pleats, cocoon
   seams curving outward. Costs fabric only.
2. **Fabric body**: choose stiff or lofty fabrics - taffeta, duchess satin, scuba/neoprene,
   double-face wool, felted knit, organza (crisp but light).
3. **Interfacing/underlining**: fusible or sew-in canvas, hair canvas, organza underlining,
   horsehair braid in hems (gives a flared hem shape).
4. **Padding**: wadding/quilting, shoulder pads, sleeve heads, hip pads.
5. **Support layers**: tulle petticoats, crinolines/hoops, boned bodices (spiral steel
   or synthetic whalebone/Rigilene), internal corsetry, wire.

## Choosing
- Test small: make a 1/2-scale or panel sample with the chosen fabric + support.
- Weigh it: heavy volume pulls shoulders and seams; consider distributing load (waist stay,
  internal straps).
- Movement: sitting, walking, getting through doors and car seats.
- Care: fusibles can bubble when washed; wire and boning complicate cleaning.

## Gotchas
- Drawing volume with no plan for support produces flat toiles.
- Volume in soft fabric collapses at the bottom; horsehair braid or a facing helps hems.
- Stiff fabrics crease; check crush recovery for shipping and wear.

- [ ] Each volume look has a named volume strategy.
- [ ] A sample proves the shape holds in the real fabric.
- [ ] Weight and movement tested on a body.
