# Deconstruction and reconstruction of existing garments

**Goal:** use real garments as raw material and teachers: take them apart to learn their
cut, then rebuild the parts into new designs.

## Deconstruct to learn
1. Photograph the garment (front, back, inside) before cutting.
2. Unpick (seam ripper, small scissors) seam by seam; label each piece with chalk or tape
   (name, grain, notches, which seam it joined).
3. Press pieces flat, trace onto paper: this is a pattern of a proven garment.
4. Record construction order as you go; it shows how the factory built it.

## Reconstruct to design
- Swap parts between garments (a shirt collar on a coat, trouser legs as sleeves).
- Rotate a piece 90 or 180 degrees; let the original details land in new places.
- Keep evidence of the original (labels, plackets, hems) as design marks.
- Drape the pieces on the stand and pin into new forms; photograph each option.

## Deconstruction as an aesthetic
Exposed seams, raw edges, visible interfacing, basting threads left in, inside-out
construction (associated with Maison Margiela, Comme des Garcons). Make it intentional:
choose finishes that will hold up (raw edges fray; overlock or use non-fraying fabrics
if longevity matters).

## Gotchas
- Second-hand garments vary; one-off reconstructions don't scale to production without
  a repeatable pattern and supply.
- Raw-edge looks in woven fabrics can fray beyond the intended look after washing; test-wash.

## Verify
- [ ] Original garment documented before cutting.
- [ ] Pieces traced as patterns with grain and notches.
- [ ] Reconstruction concepts photographed on the stand.
