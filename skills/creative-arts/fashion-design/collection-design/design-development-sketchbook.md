# Building a design-development sketchbook

**Goal:** a book that lets someone else follow the path from research to final looks;
in art-school assessment and design interviews, the process is graded as much as the result.

## Structure (A3 or A4 landscape, bound or ring)
1. **Opening pages** - starting point, concept words, 2-3 key images.
2. **Research spreads** - primary drawings/photos beside secondary images (with sources).
3. **Translation spreads** - collage on croquis, photocopy-and-cut, drape-on-stand photos,
   detail studies. This is where design happens.
4. **Iteration grids** - 12-30 small figures per spread exploring one variable at a time
   (length, volume, closure).
5. **Fabric and colour tests** - swatches stapled in, prints, manipulation samples.
6. **Selection pages** - circled/starred designs with a short note on why.
7. **Final line-up preview** - small figures of the chosen looks.

## Techniques that show thinking
- **Collage on templates**: cut research shapes and lay them on a printed croquis.
- **Photocopy distortion**: enlarge/stretch a detail, trace the result as a new shape.
- **Photo-draping**: drape fabric on the stand or yourself, photograph, draw over the photo.
- **Annotations**: short, specific ("drop the shoulder 6 cm, keeps the boxy line from p.12").

## Practical rules
- Work in it daily; dating spreads helps you and assessors see progression.
- Mix media: pencil, marker, tape, stapled swatches, prints. Avoid polishing every page.
- Leave nothing unexplained: if a page has no annotation, add one line of intent.
- Scan or photograph spreads weekly; the scans become the digital portfolio.

## Gotchas
- A "pretty" book of finished illustrations hides the process; assessors mark that down.
- Skipping iteration grids leads to a collection of 8 one-off ideas, not a family.
- Glue sticks fail over months; use double-sided tape or PVA for swatches.

## Verify
- [ ] A reader can trace each final look back to a research page.
- [ ] At least one spread of 20+ variations exists per key garment.
- [ ] Every spread carries at least one annotation.
