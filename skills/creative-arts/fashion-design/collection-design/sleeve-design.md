# Designing sleeves and cuffs

**Goal:** pick a sleeve construction and shape that gives the silhouette and the movement
you want, and specify it clearly.

## Construction types
| Type | Look | Movement | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Set-in (high cap) | Sharp, tailored shoulder | Limited arm lift | Classic jackets; needs cap ease |
| Set-in (low cap) / shirt | Relaxed | More lift | Shirts, casualwear |
| Drop shoulder | Shoulder seam on upper arm | Relaxed | Oversized looks; drag lines at underarm |
| Raglan | Diagonal seam neck to underarm | Good lift | Sportswear, coats; easy to fit shoulders |
| Kimono / dolman | Cut in one with bodice | Good, with gusset | Underarm folds; gusset adds lift |
| Two-piece | Curved, follows arm | Tailored | Jackets/coats |

## Shape variations
Puff (fullness at cap), bishop (fullness at wrist gathered to cuff), leg-of-mutton,
bell/flared, lantern (seamed fullness mid-arm), cap sleeve, cuffed vs uncuffed.

## The movement trade-off
Higher sleeve cap + narrower armhole = cleaner look, less lift. Lower cap + deeper
armhole = more lift but the sleeve drapes with folds when the arm is down. Decide per
garment use (a coat must cross arms; an evening jacket can be restrictive).

## Cuffs and finishes
Shirt cuff (barrel, French), rib cuff (knit), vents with buttons (working cuffs in
tailoring), elastic, drawstring, raw. Spec cuff depth and opening circumference.

## Spec
Sleeve type, length (from shoulder point or CB neck for raglan), bicep width, elbow and
cuff circumference, cap height if critical, gathers/pleat counts.

## Gotchas
- Drop shoulders on tailored fabrics look sloppy unless the volume is intentional.
- Kimono sleeves without a gusset tear at the underarm when fitted.
- Drawn sleeves often ignore back view; draw the back armhole.

## Verify
- [ ] Construction type chosen for movement need.
- [ ] Key sleeve measurements specified.
- [ ] Arm-raise test done on toile.
