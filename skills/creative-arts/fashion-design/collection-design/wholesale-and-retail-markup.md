# Wholesale and retail markups and margin

**Goal:** set wholesale and retail prices that cover cost, markdowns and overhead, and
know the difference between markup and margin.

## Definitions
- **Markup** (on cost) = (price - cost) / cost.
- **Margin** (on price) = (price - cost) / price.
- 50% margin = 100% markup = price is 2x cost ("keystone").

## Typical chain (indicative multipliers)
- Brand selling wholesale: **wholesale price = landed cost x ~2-2.5**.
- Retailer: **retail = wholesale x ~2-2.7** (keystone or more).
- So retail is often ~4-6x landed cost for wholesale brands.
- DTC brand: retail often ~3-5x landed cost; margin must cover marketing,
  fulfilment, returns.
Check multipliers against your own P&L; these vary by category and market level.

## Steps
1. Landed cost from the cost sheet.
2. Apply target margin -> required wholesale and retail.
3. Compare with the target retail from the price architecture. If it's above, value
   engineer or accept lower margin intentionally.
4. Round to price points (e.g. 79, 89, 129) and check the ladder across categories.

## Markdowns and returns
- Initial markup must allow for markdowns: plan the average achieved margin, not the
  full-price margin.
- DTC returns (often 20-40% in apparel online) cost shipping and handling; include them.

## Gotchas
- Confusing margin and markup: "50% markup" is far less than "50% margin".
- Pricing from cost alone ignores what customers will pay; check comparables.
- Currency changes erode margins on imported goods; review per season.

## Verify
- [ ] Wholesale and retail computed from landed cost with stated multipliers.
- [ ] Margin after planned markdowns meets target.
- [ ] Prices fit the line's price architecture.
