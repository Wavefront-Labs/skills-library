# Building a brand size chart from standards

**Goal:** a body measurement chart (not garment measurements) that defines who each size
fits, used by pattern makers, fit sessions and customers.

## Sources
- **ISO 8559-1** (anthropometric definitions for body measurement) and **ISO 8559-2**
  (primary and secondary dimension indicators for size designation).
- **ASTM D5585** (women's misses sizes 00-20), **ASTM D6240** (men's), ASTM D6960
  (women's plus), ASTM D7878 (curvy), children's charts.
- National size surveys (SizeUSA, SizeUK) and your own customer/returns data.
- European EN 13402 (size designation, pictogram) for labelling.

## Steps
1. Choose the **primary dimensions** per garment type: tops = bust/chest; bottoms =
   waist and hip; trousers also inside leg; height for long/short ranges.
2. Pick a **base size** near the middle of your customer distribution (sales size curve).
3. Set **intervals** between sizes (e.g. bust 4-5 cm in misses mid-range, larger in plus).
4. Fill secondary dimensions (shoulder, neck, arm length, rise) from the standard tables
   or scan data at the same size.
5. Define labels per market (UK/EU/US numbers, letter sizes) and a conversion table.
6. Publish the customer-facing chart (bust, waist, hip, height) with how-to-measure.

## Gotchas
- Mixing body and garment measurements in one chart confuses everyone; keep separate.
- Copying a competitor's chart copies their customer, not yours.
- Vanity sizing drift across seasons breaks repeat purchases; freeze the chart and
  version it.

## Verify
- [ ] Chart cites its source standards and data.
- [ ] Base size justified by sales/customer data.
- [ ] Intervals and conversions documented; chart versioned.
