# Secondary research and visual referencing

**Goal:** widen a primary idea with books, films, photography and online archives while
keeping every reference traceable and turning it into your own design language.

## Where to look (in order of originality)
1. **Library special collections and photo books** - documentary photography, subculture
   studies, architecture monographs. Less used by peers than the web.
2. **Film and music video stills** - pause, screen-grab, note timecode.
3. **Online museum catalogues** (V&A, Met, Kyoto Costume Institute) - high-res, dated.
4. **Runway archives** (Vogue Runway) - only for checking that your idea is not already a
   recent collection; never as a starting point.
5. **Social image platforms** - last; images circulate without credit.

## Keep it traceable
- One reference log (spreadsheet or the back pages of the sketchbook): image id, source,
  photographer/author, date, page or URL, why it matters.
- Name files `src-<topic>-<nn>.jpg` and keep the log in the same folder.
- Cite in the sketchbook in the school's style (UAL uses Harvard). Tutors and employers
  check; unreferenced images read as copying.

## Transform, don't transcribe
- For each strong image, write three extracted qualities (e.g. "collapsed volume",
  "frayed edge", "uniform pocket repetition"), then design from the qualities, not the image.
- Combine two unrelated references (a 1950s workwear pocket + a Japanese packaging fold).
- Shift scale, material or placement; a detail taken literally is a copy.

## Gotchas
- Research that is all runway images produces derivative work.
- Pinterest results converge for everyone searching the same words; go deeper with
  specific terms (era, place, trade, process).
- Copyright: research use is fine; reproducing a photographer's image in a printed
  lookbook or textile print is not.

## Verify
- [ ] Every secondary image has a logged source.
- [ ] Runway images are under 10% of the research.
- [ ] Each key reference has written extracted qualities and at least one design response.
