# Balancing fabric weights across a collection

**Goal:** a range of fabric weights that suits the season, lets pieces layer, and gives
visual and tactile variety.

## Reference weights (g/m2, approximate)
| Fabric use | Light | Mid | Heavy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shirting/blouse | 60-100 (voile, georgette) | 100-140 (poplin, oxford ~130) | 150+ |
| Jersey (tops) | 120-160 | 160-220 | 220-300 (heavy tees) |
| Dress fabrics | 80-150 | 150-250 | 250+ |
| Suiting | 200-250 (summer) | 250-300 | 300-400 (winter) |
| Trousers/denim | 200-280 | 280-400 (denim ~9-12 oz/yd2) | 400+ (14 oz+) |
| Coating | 350-450 | 450-600 | 600-800+ (melton, double-face) |
| Fleece/sweat | 240-300 | 300-400 | 400+ |
Denim is quoted in oz/yd2 (1 oz/yd2 ~ 33.9 g/m2).

## Planning
1. List styles by layer: base (shirts, tees), mid (knits, dresses, trousers), outer.
2. Check each layer has suitable weights for the season and climate of the main market.
3. Make sure outerwear can go over midlayers (sleeve and armhole room grows with weight).
4. Include contrast in hand: crisp vs fluid, flat vs textured.

## Gotchas
- AW collections of only heavy fabrics fail in transitional months and indoor heat.
- Heavy fabrics change silhouette (bulk at seams, darts); simplify construction.
- Weight alone doesn't tell drape; check hand and structure.

## Verify
- [ ] Each layer has at least one suitable weight for the season.
- [ ] Outerwear fitted over the heaviest midlayer.
- [ ] Weights recorded on the fabric board.
