# Silhouette families

**Goal:** choose and vary the overall outline of each look on purpose. The silhouette is
what reads first from across a room or a runway.

## The families (Dior's 1950s letter lines popularised the vocabulary)
| Family | Shape | Emphasis | Typical garments |
|---|---|---|---|
| A-line | Narrow top, flares to hem | Shoulders/neck, hides hips | A-line skirt/dress, swing coat |
| H-line / column | Straight from shoulder to hem | Length, verticality | Shift dress, straight coat |
| X / hourglass | Wide shoulder and hem, defined waist | Waist | New Look, fit-and-flare |
| Y / V | Broad shoulders, narrow below | Shoulders | 1980s power shoulder, tapered trousers |
| O / cocoon / barrel | Volume at the middle, narrow at ends | Mass, softness | Cocoon coat, balloon skirt |
| Trapeze | Like A but from the shoulder, unfitted | Movement | Trapeze dress (Saint Laurent for Dior 1958) |

## Working method
1. Draw the silhouette as a solid black shape over the croquis; ignore details.
2. For a collection, choose 2-3 families as the language; more reads unfocused.
3. Vary within a family by **where the widest point sits** and **how much volume**.
4. Balance volume: big top + narrow bottom or vice versa; full-volume both reads as
   costume unless that is the concept.

## Silhouette and fabric
- A-line and trapeze need fabric with body (gabardine, taffeta, scuba) or a bias cut in
  soft fabric to flare by drape instead of structure.
- Cocoon shapes need weight and loft (boiled wool, double-face) or interior support.

## Gotchas
- Silhouette sketches with details distract; check shape as filled shapes first.
- The walking body changes the silhouette; drape or 3D-simulate in motion.

## Verify
- [ ] Each look is assigned a silhouette family.
- [ ] Line uses 2-3 families with clear variation.
- [ ] Fabric choices support the intended volume.
