# Lower-impact material selection and certifications

**Goal:** pick materials that genuinely reduce impact and be able to prove it with
certificates that trace the material to your order.

## Certifications and what they cover
| Standard | Covers |
|---|---|
| GOTS (Global Organic Textile Standard) | Organic fibre (min 70%/95% labels) plus chemical and social criteria in processing |
| OCS (Organic Content Standard) | Organic content tracking only |
| GRS (Global Recycled Standard) / RCS | Recycled content (GRS min 20% for chain, 50% for label) plus processing criteria |
| RWS / RMS / RAS | Responsible wool, mohair, alpaca: animal welfare and land |
| OEKO-TEX Standard 100 | Product tested for harmful substances (not a sustainability fibre claim) |
| bluesign | Chemical management at mills |
| FSC / PEFC (for viscose, lyocell feedstock) | Forest sourcing; Canopy's Hot Button ranks producers |
| Better Cotton | Farming practices; mass balance, not physical traceability |

## Proof
- Ask for the supplier's **scope certificate** (valid, listing the product) and for each
  order a **transaction certificate (TC)**. Without a TC you cannot make the claim.
- Check certificate validity in the standard's public database.

## Selection logic
- Fibre is only part of the impact; dyeing and finishing (wet processing) and garment
  lifespan matter a lot. Use LCA data (Higg MSI, Textile Exchange) carefully.
- Mono-material designs (100% polyester including trims) recycle more easily than blends.

## Claims
EU and UK rules (Green Claims guidance, CMA Green Claims Code, FTC Green Guides in the US)
require specific, substantiated claims; avoid "eco-friendly" without evidence.

## Gotchas
- OEKO-TEX is about harmful substances, not organic or recycled content.
- "Recycled polyester" from bottles is a downcycle; not circular textile-to-textile.

## Verify
- [ ] Scope certificate valid and covering the product.
- [ ] Transaction certificates per order filed.
- [ ] Marketing claims reviewed against green-claims guidance.
