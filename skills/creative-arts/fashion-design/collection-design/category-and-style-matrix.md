# Category and style matrix

**Goal:** a spreadsheet that fixes how many styles in which categories and colours the
designers will create, before sketching starts, so design effort matches sales potential.

## Inputs
- Last 2-4 seasons' sales by category (units, revenue, margin, sell-through).
- Target revenue for the season and average selling price per category.
- Production minimums per style and per colour.

## Build the matrix
| Category | Sales share | Styles | Colours/style | Style-colours | New / carryover |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Outerwear | 20% | 6 | 2 | 12 | 3 / 3 |
| Knitwear | 18% | 8 | 3 | 24 | 5 / 3 |
| Shirts | 15% | 7 | 2-3 | 17 | 4 / 3 |
| Trousers | 15% | 6 | 2 | 12 | 3 / 3 |
| Dresses | 12% | 6 | 2 | 12 | 5 / 1 |
| Tees/jersey | 10% | 5 | 3 | 15 | 2 / 3 |
| Accessories | 10% | 5 | 2 | 10 | 3 / 2 |

1. Start from sales share; set style counts roughly proportional, adjusted for strategy
   (growing categories get more).
2. Style-colours (SKUs before sizes) = styles x colours. Multiply by size count for
   total SKUs.
3. Check minimums: projected units per style-colour must exceed the factory/fabric MOQ;
   otherwise drop colours.
4. Fill each cell with price tier (good/better/best) and delivery.

## Gotchas
- Designers over-design the categories they enjoy; the matrix protects the business.
- Too many style-colours dilute buys below minimums and raise costs.
- New categories need a test size (few styles) before a full allocation.

## Verify
- [ ] Style counts tie to sales shares and strategy notes.
- [ ] Each style-colour meets MOQ at projected volume.
- [ ] Total SKUs (with sizes) is within the team's capacity to develop.
