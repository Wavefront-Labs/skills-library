# Iterating design variations from one idea

**Goal:** turn one good idea into 20-50 variations, then into a family of coherent styles,
instead of 10 unrelated ideas.

## The variation grid
1. Draw the base idea small on a croquis or flat (one per box, 4x5 grid on A3).
2. Change **one variable per row**:
   - length (crop, hip, knee, ankle),
   - volume (fitted, relaxed, oversized),
   - neckline/collar,
   - sleeve,
   - closure position and type,
   - placement of the key detail (front, back, side, sleeve),
   - fabric/colour block.
3. Fill each row with 4-5 options; don't judge until the grid is full.

## Combine and refine
- Star the strongest per row; combine stars into 5-8 new designs.
- Redraw these larger with front and back.
- Repeat a second round if the result still looks like the first idea.

## Select with criteria
Score each design 1-3 on: concept fit (follows the design rules), customer need,
newness, feasibility (cost/make), relation to the line (does it sit with others?).
Discuss the scores; the numbers start the conversation, they don't end it.

## Gotchas
- Changing many variables at once makes it impossible to see what works.
- Iterating only in front view misses back interest and side silhouette.
- Falling for the first idea: force 20 variations before judging.

## Verify
- [ ] Grid shows single-variable changes.
- [ ] Selected designs carry scores against stated criteria.
- [ ] Front and back are drawn for finalists.
