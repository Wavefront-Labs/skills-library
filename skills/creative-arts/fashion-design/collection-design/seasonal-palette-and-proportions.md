# Building a seasonal palette with proportions

**Goal:** a palette of 8-14 colours where each colour has a job and a planned share of the
line, so the rail looks intentional and sells.

## Structure
| Role | Count | Typical share of units | Where it goes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Neutrals (black, navy, ecru, grey, camel) | 3-5 | 50-70% | Core basics, outerwear, bottoms |
| Core/seasonal colours | 3-5 | 20-35% | Tops, knits, dresses |
| Accents | 1-3 | 5-15% | Small items, prints, accessories, one hero look |
Shares are a starting point; set them from your own sales history.

## Steps
1. Pull colours from the concept research (sample with an eyedropper, then match to
   physical standards).
2. Place them on a strip in proportion (wide band for neutrals, thin for accents).
3. Map each colour to styles in the line plan: which styles come in which colours.
   A style rarely needs more than 3-4 colours.
4. Check pairings: every top colour should go with at least one bottom colour in the line.
5. Check category coverage: outerwear in at least one neutral; accents mostly in low-risk
   categories.

## Gotchas
- Too many colours per style explodes SKUs and minimums (each colour has a dye MOQ).
- Accents chosen at equal weight to neutrals make a rail look like a sample sale.
- Colours behave differently on different fabrics; one "rust" on cotton vs wool will not match.

## Verify
- [ ] 8-14 colours, each with a role and target share.
- [ ] Every style-colour combination appears in the line plan.
- [ ] Cross-category pairings checked.
