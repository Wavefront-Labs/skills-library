# Seasonal colour forecasting

**Goal:** decide which colours, in which shades and proportions, the line will use 12-24
months ahead, with a reasoned path from what sells now.

## 1. Know the inputs
- **Industry forecasts**: Color Marketing Group, WGSN/Coloro, Pantone Fashion Color Trend
  Reports, Premiere Vision colour range (announced ~12 months ahead of the season).
- **Your own sales data**: best and worst colours by category for the last 2-4 seasons.
- **Cultural drivers** from macro scanning (e.g. outdoors interest pushes earthy greens).

## 2. Think in shifts, not new colours
Colour moves gradually. For each colour family plot last season's shade and decide the
shift along three axes:
- **Hue** (red going warmer toward coral or cooler toward berry),
- **Value** (lighter/darker),
- **Chroma** (saturated vs muted/dusty).
Example: "green: from sage toward a deeper, slightly yellower olive; chroma unchanged".

## 3. Build the forecast board
- 4-6 colour stories, each with 4-8 colours as physical standards (Pantone TCX chips or
  dyed fabric), not only screen swatches.
- Include **neutrals** (often 50-70% of sales units), **core colours** and **accents**.
- Note reference codes and the image or driver behind each story.

## 4. Validate
- Check against your best sellers: never drop a top-3 colour without a replacement.
- Check dye feasibility on the main fabrics (some brights are costly or unstable on cotton).

## Gotchas
- Screen colours lie; approve on physical standards in D65 lighting.
- Accent colours sell less than you think; keep them to small quantities and accessories.
- Copying a forecast service's palette unchanged makes you look like everyone else.

## Verify
- [ ] Each colour has a physical standard and code.
- [ ] Shifts are explained relative to last season and to sales data.
- [ ] Neutral/core/accent split is stated.
