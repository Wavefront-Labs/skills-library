# Muse and character development

**Goal:** a specific imagined (or real) person who makes design decisions easier: "would
she wear this, and how?". Different from the commercial customer persona; the muse is
creative fuel, the persona is market fact.

## Build the character (one page)
- **Name, age, place, occupation** - specific ("Ines, 34, night-shift radiographer, Lisbon").
- **Daily routine**: where she goes, what she carries, climate, transport.
- **Wardrobe habits**: what she keeps for years, what she alters, how she layers,
  what she refuses to wear.
- **Contradictions**: two opposing traits (practical but sentimental). Contradictions
  give design tension.
- **Objects and references**: 5 objects she owns, music, a film she rewatches.
- **Image references**: 3-6 photos of real people (cast, street, archive) that capture her.

## Use it
- Design: write three garments she would need for a specific day; design those first.
- Styling: decide how she wears pieces (sleeves pushed up, coat over shoulders).
- Casting and show: her attitude drives model choice and walk.

## Real-person muses
- If basing a muse on a real public figure, use them as inspiration only; never imply
  endorsement in marketing.

## Gotchas
- A generic muse ("strong confident woman") adds nothing; detail is the point.
- Muse and target customer drifting far apart produces an unsellable line; check both.

## Verify
- [ ] Character page has routine, habits, contradictions and images.
- [ ] At least three designs trace directly to a muse scenario.
- [ ] Styling notes reference the muse.
