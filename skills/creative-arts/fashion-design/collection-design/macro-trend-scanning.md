# Macro trend and consumer-shift scanning

**Goal:** explain *why* customers will want something in 18-24 months, so trend choices
rest on evidence rather than on what is already on the runway.

## 1. Scan with STEEP
Collect signals in five buckets (social, technological, economic, environmental,
political). Sources: news, demographic reports, retail earnings calls, tech launches,
subcultures, art and music, search-interest data (Google Trends), resale platforms.
Log each signal: date, source, one-line summary, link.

## 2. Cluster signals into drivers
Every 2-4 weeks, print signals as cards and group them. A **driver** needs at least
5 independent signals from 3+ buckets (e.g. "repair culture": rising repair-cafe numbers,
EU right-to-repair rules, resale growth, visible-mending hashtags, brands offering repairs).

## 3. Turn drivers into design directions
For each driver write:
- **Consumer need** ("wants clothes that last and show history").
- **Product expression** (reinforced seams, patches as design, mono-material).
- **Colour, material and silhouette implications.**
- **Timing**: emerging (niche, 2-3 seasons out), growing (adopted by forward retailers),
  mainstream (mass market now). Most brands act at "growing".

## 4. Check against the brand
Discard drivers your customer will never touch. Keep 2-3 per season.

## Gotchas
- Confusing a fad (one viral item) with a driver; drivers have causes that persist.
- Echo chamber: if every signal comes from fashion media, you are tracking other forecasters.
- Paid services (WGSN, Trendstop) are a useful baseline, but everyone else has them too.

## Verify
- [ ] Each driver has 5+ dated, sourced signals across 3+ STEEP buckets.
- [ ] Each driver has a stated consumer need and timing stage.
- [ ] Design directions are filtered through the brand's customer.
