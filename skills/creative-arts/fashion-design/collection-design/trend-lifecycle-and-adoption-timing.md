# Trend life-cycle and adoption timing

**Goal:** decide *when* to act on a trend and how big a bet to make, based on where it sits
on the adoption curve and where your customer sits.

## The curve
Rogers' diffusion of innovations applied to fashion:
innovators (2.5%) -> early adopters (13.5%) -> early majority (34%) -> late majority (34%)
-> laggards (16%). In fashion terms: introduction (runway, subculture), rise (forward
retailers, influencers), peak (mass market, high street), decline (markdowns), obsolescence.

## Locate a trend (evidence checklist)
| Stage | Signals |
|---|---|
| Introduction | A few designers/subcultures; no high-street versions |
| Rise | Several brands at multiple price levels; growing search interest; sell-outs |
| Peak | Everywhere at mass level; heavy marketing; lots of colour/fabric variants |
| Decline | Markdowns, off-price, "how to style it" content fades |

## Match to market level and customer
- Luxury/designer brands introduce; contemporary brands act at early rise; high-street
  at late rise to peak; value retail at peak.
- **Bet size by stage**: rise = test (1-3 styles, shallow buys); confirmed rise = expand
  (more colours, more categories); peak = protect (carry what sells, no new depth);
  decline = exit (no reorders).

## Classic vs fad vs trend
- **Fad**: steep rise and fall within one season; act only with fast production.
- **Trend**: multi-season curve.
- **Classic**: plateau; becomes core/carryover.

## Gotchas
- Lead times: if development + production is 9 months, act when the trend is 9 months
  before your target stage.
- Your customer may be a laggard even when the trend is at peak elsewhere; check sales.

## Verify
- [ ] Each trend has a stage call backed by the evidence table.
- [ ] Bet size and number of styles match the stage and lead time.
- [ ] Exit criteria (markdown rate, search decline) are written down.
