# Care, content and labelling requirements

**Goal:** labels that are legally compliant in each sales market and give care
instructions that the garment actually survives.

## Fibre content
- **EU**: Regulation (EU) No 1007/2011 - use only listed fibre names, percentages by
  weight, in the language(s) of the member state of sale; trims/decoratives under 7%
  may be excluded under rules; "non-textile parts of animal origin" must be stated.
- **US**: Textile Fiber Products Identification Act (FTC) - generic fibre names,
  percentages, country of origin, manufacturer identity (name or RN number).
- **Wool Products Labeling Act** (US) for wool.
- Order: descending by weight. Linings shown separately ("Shell: 100% wool; Lining:
  100% cupro").

## Care instructions
- **ISO 3758** symbols (washing tub, bleach triangle, drying square, iron, professional
  care circle) in that order; GINETEX owns the symbols in many countries.
- **US FTC Care Labeling Rule**: care must be given (words or ASTM D5489 symbols), and
  the manufacturer must have a **reasonable basis** (testing) for the instructions.
- Base care on testing (shrinkage AATCC 135/ISO 6330, colourfastness) of the actual garment,
  including trims and prints.

## Placement and form
- Permanent label, legible for the garment's life; usually left side seam or CB neck.
- Size label, country of origin, RN/company ID; some markets require importer address.
- Consider heat-transfer labels for sportswear comfort.

## Gotchas
- Care given for the shell only when the trim bleeds or shrinks.
- "Dry clean only" by default is not a reasonable basis; test it.
- Fibre names like "bamboo" for viscose made from bamboo are mislabelling (FTC action).

## Verify
- [ ] Content labels follow the target market's fibre naming rules.
- [ ] Care instructions backed by test results.
- [ ] Origin, ID and language requirements checked per market.
