# Cutting looks and editing criteria

**Goal:** remove looks so that the remaining collection is stronger; editing is a design
act, and the line-up is judged by its weakest look.

## Criteria (score each look)
1. **Concept fit**: does it follow the design rules and story?
2. **Redundancy**: does another look say the same thing better?
3. **Role**: statement (press/show), commercial (sells), bridge (connects stories). The
   line needs all three; cut where a role is over-supplied.
4. **Quality of execution**: proportion, fabric, finish of the sample.
5. **Feasibility**: cost, production risk, delivery.

## Process
1. Start 20-50% over the final number (designing 30 looks for a 20-look show).
2. First pass: remove obvious weak/duplicate looks.
3. Second pass with a fresh eye (next day) and an outside voice (tutor, merchandiser).
4. Try "subtract to strengthen": remove one element from a look (a layer, a detail)
   before cutting the whole look.
5. Final pass in look order on the wall or on models.

## Things to protect
- The opening and closing looks.
- At least one look per key story.
- Pieces that sell (commercial core) even if less exciting.

## Gotchas
- Keeping looks because of the effort invested (sunk cost).
- Cutting all quieter looks makes the collection exhausting; rhythm needs calm.
- Editing alone; designers can't see their own repetition.

## Verify
- [ ] Each cut is recorded with the reason.
- [ ] Final line covers statement, commercial and bridge roles.
- [ ] Final count matches the show/line plan.
