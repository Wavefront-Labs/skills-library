# Primary research: archives, museums and objects

**Goal:** collect first-hand material no other designer has, so the collection starts from
something specific rather than from the same Pinterest images as everyone else.

## 1. Plan the visit
- Pick 2-3 sources tied to the starting idea: a costume archive (V&A Clothworkers' Centre,
  The Met Costume Institute study room, a university dress collection, a regimental or
  workwear archive), a museum gallery, a flea market, your own family wardrobe.
- Study rooms need **booking weeks ahead** and a list of object numbers. Search the online
  catalogue first, note accession numbers, ask for 5-10 objects per session.
- Bring: pencils only (no pens or ink near objects), sketchbook, measuring tape (ask before
  touching), phone or camera without flash, nitrile gloves if supplied.

## 2. Look like a maker, not a tourist
For each garment, record in this order:
1. **Overall silhouette** - front, side, back photos at body height; quick thumbnail sketch.
2. **Cut** - where the seams run, grain direction (look at the weave), how fullness is
   controlled (darts, gathers, pleats, ease).
3. **Construction** - inside if allowed: seam finishes, interfacing, hand stitches, linings,
   weights, boning.
4. **Details** - closures, pockets, collars, trims, at 1:1 scale drawings with measurements.
5. **Surface** - fabric close-ups (macro), colour notes against a neutral grey card.
Write object number and source on every page so you can cite and re-request later.

## 3. Non-garment objects
Architecture, tools, packaging, natural forms: photograph the **shape, the joint, the
texture** separately. A pleat idea often comes from a fold in a map or a lampshade,
so draw the mechanism, not just the look.

## 4. Process the haul the same day
- Print key photos small, paste beside drawings, annotate with "what could this become"
  (e.g. "button stand 4 cm, could become the whole front panel").
- Do 10 fast drawings per object exploring scale shifts (detail enlarged to a full garment).

## Gotchas
- Archives ban flash, ink and sometimes photography; confirm rules on booking.
- Photographing only the front loses the cut; the back and inside teach more.
- Research that never gets drawn stays research. Translate within 48 hours.

## Verify
- [ ] Every image/drawing is labelled with source and object number.
- [ ] At least one cut or construction idea is extracted per object, not just mood.
- [ ] The sketchbook shows translation pages, not only collected images.
