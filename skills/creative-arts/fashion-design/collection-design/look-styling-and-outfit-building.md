# Styling and outfit building for the lineup

**Goal:** combine pieces into looks that communicate the concept and help sell
individual items, and record the styling so shoots and shows repeat it exactly.

## Build looks
- Start from the hero piece; add pieces that support it (don't compete).
- Vary layering depth across the lineup: some single-layer, some 3-4 layers.
- Proportion: balance volume top vs bottom; use tucks, half-tucks, rolled sleeves,
  belts to change proportion without new garments.
- Accessories and footwear: choose a **family** (one shoe type in colourways, one bag
  shape) so they support, not distract.
- Hair and make-up: one direction for the whole show; it frames the concept.

## Styling tricks (state them so they're repeatable)
"Sleeves pushed to elbow", "coat worn off shoulders", "shirt buttoned to the top, no tie",
"socks visible 3 cm above shoe". Photograph each look front/side/back with notes.

## Look sheet
Per look: number, model, each garment (style number, colour, size), accessories,
styling notes, photo. Dressers at shows work from these.

## Gotchas
- Over-accessorising hides the garments buyers need to see.
- Clips and pins used to fit samples on models must not show in photos.
- Styling the commercial pieces only with show pieces means buyers can't imagine them alone;
  include some simple looks.

## Verify
- [ ] Every look has a look sheet with photo and notes.
- [ ] Accessories follow one family.
- [ ] Commercial pieces appear in at least one simple, wearable look.
