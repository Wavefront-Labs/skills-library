# Line plan and range building — from mood board to a lineup that holds together

**Goal:** convert research and a concept into a collection that is (a) visually one
story, (b) balanced across categories and prices so a buyer can order it, and (c) small
enough to actually sample. Most student and first-label collections fail on (b) and (c),
not on ideas.

## 1. Fix the frame before drawing
Write these on one page and keep it pinned:
- **Customer** — one specific person: age range, where they wear it, what they already own.
- **Season and drop** (e.g. SS / AW, or monthly drops) and the selling window.
- **Size of the range** — number of *styles* (designs) and *options* (style × colour).
  A first independent collection is often 12–25 styles; a show collection 25–45 looks.
  These are rules of thumb, not standards — set yours from the sampling budget.
- **Price ladder** — entry, core and statement price points (good/better/best).

## 2. Build the line plan grid
A spreadsheet, one row per style slot, before any sketching is final:

| Category | Slot | Role | Fabric | Colours | Target cost | Target retail | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Outerwear | OW-01 | Hero | wool melton | 2 | | | sketch |
| Tops | TP-03 | Core | cotton jersey | 4 | | | toile |

- **Roles:** *hero/statement* (the image pieces, 10–20%), *core/key* (the sellers, often
  the majority), *basics/carryover* (proven blocks re-coloured). A range of all heroes is
  a show nobody can buy; all basics is a range nobody photographs.
- **Category balance:** check tops vs bottoms vs dresses vs outerwear against how the
  customer actually dresses — a buyer needs things to put *with* the heroes.
- **Block reuse:** design several styles off one shared pattern block or fabric to cut
  sampling cost and minimums. Fabric minimums (often hundreds of metres per colour at
  mills; less from stock services) can kill a colour that appears on only one style.

## 3. Stories that tie it together
- **Colour story:** a palette of roughly 5–9 colours: anchors (neutrals that go across
  categories), mid-tones, and 1–2 accents used sparingly. Assign each slot's colours in
  the grid so every colour appears on more than one style.
- **Fabric story:** a short list of base fabrics in different weights; each style uses
  one of them. Too many fabrics = too many minimums and a range that reads as unrelated.
- **Silhouette story:** 2–3 recurring proportions (e.g. cropped-over-wide, long-over-lean)
  repeated with variations. Repetition is what makes a collection look intentional.

## 4. Lineup editing
Print every look at the same scale and pin them in walk order (or lay out digitally):
1. Squint — do colour blocks flow, or jump randomly? Group by colour or build a gradient.
2. Cover the names — could two looks be swapped without anyone noticing? Cut or
   differentiate one.
3. Count roles against the grid; if a category has no core pieces, the range has a hole.
4. Remove the weakest 10–20%. Editing is the most skipped step and the most visible.
5. Opening and closing looks carry the concept most clearly; put the purest statement first.

## Gotchas
- Designing looks first and costing later — the line plan exists to stop this.
- A "story" that lives only in the mood board; check that the concept is visible in the
  garments themselves (cut, fabric, detail), not just in styling.
- Forgetting the size run and grading cost — every extra size multiplies sampling and stock.
- Calendar: sampling, fabric lead times and sales appointments run months ahead of
  delivery; confirm the actual dates with your mills and factory rather than trusting a
  generic calendar (lead times vary hugely by region and fabric type — unverified here).

## Verify
- [ ] One page states customer, season, style count and price ladder.
- [ ] Every slot in the grid has a role, fabric, colours and target cost/retail.
- [ ] Every colour and fabric appears on more than one style.
- [ ] Heroes are a minority; every category has sellable core pieces.
- [ ] The lineup has been edited down at least once, with cuts recorded.
