# Value engineering a style to target cost

**Goal:** bring a style that costs too much down to its target cost while keeping what
makes customers want it.

## 1. Decide what must not change
List the 2-3 features that carry the design (e.g. the silhouette, a signature pocket,
the hand of the fabric). Everything else is negotiable.

## 2. Levers by size of saving
| Lever | Typical saving | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Fabric price (alternative quality, mill, blend) | Large | Fabric is often 40-60% of FOB |
| Fabric consumption (pattern changes to nest better, fewer pieces, narrower flare) | Medium-large | Ask for marker efficiency |
| Construction (fewer seams, panel joins, simpler finishes, remove lining or half-line) | Medium | Check with factory SAM/minute rate |
| Trims (standard vs custom buttons, plastic vs metal zip, fewer labels) | Small-medium | Custom trims have MOQs |
| Wash/embellishment simplification | Medium | Embroidery stitch count, print colours |
| Order quantity / factory / country | Varies | Consolidate fabrics across styles |

## 3. Process
1. Get a cost breakdown from the factory (fabric, trims, CMT minutes).
2. Attack the biggest line first.
3. Price each change and keep a change log; review the sample after changes.
4. Consolidate: same fabric/trim across several styles raises volume and lowers price.

## Gotchas
- Death by small cuts: each change looks fine, together they cheapen the product.
- Changing fabric changes fit and drape; re-fit after swaps.
- Factory quotes assume a quantity; confirm cost at your real volume.

## Verify
- [ ] Key features list written and preserved.
- [ ] Change log with saving per change.
- [ ] Revised sample approved after changes.
