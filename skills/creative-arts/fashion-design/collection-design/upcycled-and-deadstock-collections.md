# Upcycled and deadstock collections

**Goal:** make collections from existing materials (end-of-roll deadstock, surplus,
second-hand garments, household textiles) that can still be produced repeatably.

## Sourcing
- **Deadstock fabric**: mill and brand surplus via jobbers, deadstock marketplaces, or
  direct from brands. Quantities are finite: buy enough for the whole run plus samples.
- **Post-consumer garments**: sorting facilities and rag merchants sell graded bales by
  type (e.g. denim jeans, men's shirts, wool knits).
- Record source, composition (test if unlabelled: burn test or lab), quantity.

## Design for variability
- Design around a **type** of input (men's shirts, army blankets) rather than a single item.
- Use modular panels so pieces from different inputs combine (patchwork, colour blocking).
- Keep a fixed silhouette and pattern; let fabric vary within rules (colour range,
  weight range).
- Brands like Marine Serre (regenerated household linens, scarves) and Bode (antique
  textiles) show how to make variation a feature.

## Production
- Pre-processing: wash, sanitise, unpick, press, sort by size and colour.
- Cutting is often single-layer and manual; cost CMT accordingly (higher).
- Each piece may be unique: photograph and list individually for e-commerce.

## Communication
Explain variation to customers ("each piece unique; colours vary") and avoid
overclaiming impact.

## Gotchas
- Deadstock may carry unknown chemical finishes; test for restricted substances.
- Labour-heavy processing makes upcycling expensive; price for it.

## Verify
- [ ] Input sources and quantities logged.
- [ ] Design rules handle variation.
- [ ] Unit cost includes pre-processing and single-ply cutting.
