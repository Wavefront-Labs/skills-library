# Delivery and drop planning

**Goal:** split the season into deliveries that land when customers want to buy them, each
complete enough to sell, and derive the deadlines for design and production.

## Decide the cadence
- **Traditional wholesale**: 2 main seasons (SS, AW) + pre-collections (resort/cruise,
  pre-fall), often 2-3 deliveries each.
- **Direct-to-consumer**: monthly drops or more frequent small drops.
- **Fast fashion**: continuous replenishment and weekly newness.

## Content per drop
- Weather-appropriate categories (transition layers early, heavy coats when temperatures
  fall, party in November/December, swim from spring).
- A complete outfit set: tops, bottoms, outer, accessory.
- A hero/marketing story per drop (campaign images need hero styles).

## Example AW (DTC, northern hemisphere)
| Drop | In store | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | late July | transition: shirts, light knits, trousers, jackets |
| D2 | early Sept | knitwear, heavier trousers, first coats |
| D3 | October | coats, boots, heavy knits |
| D4 | mid Nov | party/holiday, gifting, accessories |

## Work back the deadlines
In-store date - shipping (2-6 weeks sea, 1 week air) - production (4-10 weeks) - fabric
(4-12 weeks) - sampling and approvals (8-16 weeks) = design handover date. Put each in the
critical path.

## Gotchas
- Fabric lead times usually set the true design deadline.
- Dropping heavy outerwear in September in a warm autumn = early markdowns.
- Each extra drop multiplies photography, marketing and logistics work.

## Verify
- [ ] Every style is assigned to one drop.
- [ ] Each drop contains complete outfits.
- [ ] Deadlines for each drop worked back to design handover.
