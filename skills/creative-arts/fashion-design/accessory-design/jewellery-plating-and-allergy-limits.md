# Base metals, plating and allergy limits

**Goal:** fashion jewellery that looks right, lasts, and complies with restricted-substance
laws.

## Base materials
| Material | Notes |
|---|---|
| Brass | Easy to cast and plate; common base; tarnishes if plating wears |
| Zinc alloy (zamak) | Cheap casting; heavier; poor if plating wears |
| Stainless steel (316L) | Durable, hypoallergenic for most; harder to work |
| Sterling silver (925) | Precious; tarnishes; hallmarking rules in some countries (UK) |
| Titanium, niobium | Hypoallergenic piercing jewellery |
| Gold-filled | Thick bonded gold layer (by weight, e.g. 1/20 14K) |

## Plating
- Electroplated gold, rhodium, silver; specify **thickness in microns**: flash (~0.1 um or
  less) wears quickly; **vermeil** (US) = sterling silver with at least 2.5 um of 10K+ gold.
- PVD coating on stainless steel: durable colours.
- Lacquer/e-coat over plating slows tarnish.

## Compliance
- **Nickel (EU REACH Annex XVII entry 27)**: release limit 0.2 ug/cm2/week for items in
  piercings, 0.5 ug/cm2/week for prolonged skin contact (tested by EN 1811). Similar rules
  elsewhere.
- **Lead and cadmium**: REACH limits (lead 0.05% by weight in jewellery parts; cadmium
  0.01%); US CPSIA lead limits for children's products (100 ppm substrate); state laws
  (e.g. California).
- Test via accredited labs; keep certificates per batch.

## Gotchas
- Nickel underplating beneath gold can still release nickel when worn through.
- Cheap zinc alloys may contain lead or cadmium; test castings.
- "Hypoallergenic" claims need evidence.

## Verify
- [ ] Base metal and plating thickness specified.
- [ ] Nickel, lead, cadmium test reports per market.
- [ ] Wear test (sweat, abrasion) on plated samples.
