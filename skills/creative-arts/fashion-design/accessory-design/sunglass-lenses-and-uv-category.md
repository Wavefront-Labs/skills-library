# Sunglass lenses and UV category

**Goal:** sunglasses that protect, look as designed and are legal to sell.

## Filter categories (ISO 12312-1)
| Category | Light transmission | Use |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 80-100% | Fashion/clear, overcast |
| 1 | 43-80% | Low sun |
| 2 | 18-43% | Medium sun, general use |
| 3 | 8-18% | Strong sun, most sunglasses |
| 4 | 3-8% | Very strong (mountains, glaciers); **not for driving** |
- Category must be marked on the frame or label; category 4 needs the "not suitable for driving"
  warning.

## UV
- **UV400** blocks wavelengths up to 400 nm; standard claim for UV protection.
- UV protection is independent of tint darkness; a dark lens without UV filter is harmful.

## Lens types
- **Polarised:** cut glare from horizontal surfaces (water, roads); can hide LCD screens.
- **Gradient:** darker at top; driving, fashion.
- **Mirror** coatings: reduce transmission, fashion look.
- **Photochromic:** darken in UV.

## Materials
- **Polycarbonate:** light, impact resistant, scratches more (hard coat).
- **CR-39** (allyl resin): good optics, lighter than glass.
- **Nylon/TAC** (triacetate, common in polarised fashion lenses).
- **Glass:** best optics and scratch resistance, heavy.

## Compliance
- EU/UK: CE/UKCA mark under PPE Regulation (EU) 2016/425 as category I PPE; ISO 12312-1.
- US: FDA 21 CFR 801.410 impact resistance (drop ball) for sunglasses.
- Australia AS/NZS 1067.

## Gotchas
- Lens colour affects colour perception (traffic lights); some tints not allowed for driving.
- Category marking missing is a common retail rejection.

## Verify
- [ ] Category, UV400 and markings on product.
- [ ] Test report to ISO 12312-1 / FDA drop-ball.
- [ ] Lens material and coating specified.
