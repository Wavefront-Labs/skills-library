# Findings and closures

**Goal:** the functional parts of jewellery - clasps, rings, wires, chains - chosen so the
piece is easy to wear and doesn't break or get lost.

## Clasps
| Clasp | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| Lobster | Secure, common | Fiddly to open one-handed for bracelets |
| Spring ring | Small, cheap | Weak spring over time |
| Toggle | Decorative, easy | Can slip open on light chains |
| Box clasp with safety | Very secure | Costly |
| Magnetic | Easy for limited dexterity | Opens under pull; add safety |
| Hook and eye / S-hook | Simple | Can open |

## Rings and connectors
- **Jump rings**: open rings joining parts; choose thicker gauge for load; **solder** them
  closed (or use split rings) on heavy or valuable pieces.
- Wire gauge (AWG or mm): thicker for bracelets and heavy pendants.

## Earrings
Ear wires (French hook; add stoppers), posts with butterfly or screw backs, hoops with hinged
closure, clip-ons for non-pierced; materials compliant with piercing nickel limits.

## Chains
Cable, curb, box, rope, snake, figaro, ball; specify link size, wire thickness, length
(necklaces 40-45 cm choker/princess, 50-60 cm matinee, 70-90 cm opera), extender chains.

## Checks
- Pull test at the weakest connection (clasp, jump ring).
- Wear test: snagging on hair and knit garments, ease of opening.
- Proportion: clasp size relative to chain.

## Gotchas
- Open jump rings on heavy pieces pull apart; solder or double.
- Clasps heavier than the pendant rotate to the front.
- Mixed metals in findings tarnish differently.

## Verify
- [ ] Clasp and connectors chosen for load and wearer.
- [ ] Pull test results recorded.
- [ ] Earring findings meet piercing compliance.
