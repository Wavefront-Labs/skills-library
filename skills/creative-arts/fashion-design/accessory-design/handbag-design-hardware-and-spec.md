# Handbag design: form, hardware and specification

**Goal:** a bag is a small piece of engineering that has to carry weight, open easily
and keep its shape, while being the most recognisable object in a collection. This
covers handbags; the same logic (form → structure → hardware → spec) applies to hats
and eyewear, with their own fit standards.

## 1. Form and proportion
- **Use first:** what must fit (phone, laptop size, water bottle) — write the
  internal dimensions before the external look.
- **Strap drop:** the distance from the top of the strap to the top of the bag.
  Rough guide (verify on bodies): handheld/top-handle ~8–15 cm; shoulder ~20–30 cm;
  crossbody adjustable, often ~50–60 cm. Test on several heights of person.
- **Gusset** (the side/bottom panel giving depth) shapes the silhouette: a separate
  gusset gives crisp edges; a "bellows" or folded gusset expands; no gusset gives a flat pouch.
- **Sightlines:** look at the bag at hip and shoulder height, and in the hand — it is
  almost never seen straight on as in a flat drawing.

## 2. Structure
Structure is mostly invisible materials:
- **Stiffeners/reinforcement** between leather and lining (board, foam, bonded
  reinforcement) where the bag must hold shape; a **base board** keeps the bottom flat.
- **Feet** (metal studs) protect structured bases.
- **Reinforcement behind every hardware attachment** — handles tear out from thin
  leather without a backing patch.
Unstructured (slouchy) bags rely on leather weight and temper; a thin soft leather
alone will collapse.

## 3. Hardware
- Choose for **load and use**: handle rings and swivel clips carry the full weight;
  cheap cast zinc parts can snap. Ask suppliers about material (brass, zinc alloy,
  stainless) and plating durability; this varies by supplier and isn't standardised here.
- Closures: zip (smooth, secure; specify gauge and pull), magnetic snap (fast, weaker),
  turn-lock, push-lock, flap with no closure (only for low-value contents).
- **Consistent finish** across the range: one hardware colour family (e.g. pale gold,
  gunmetal) per capsule. A **signature hardware** element repeated across styles is the
  strongest coherence tool an accessory line has.
- Size hardware to strap width: a 25 mm strap needs 25 mm inner-width rings/buckles.
- Custom hardware needs moulds and minimum quantities; stock hardware first for samples.

## 4. Edges and finishes
- **Turned edge** — leather folded over; clean, soft, luxurious, more labour.
- **Raw/painted edge** — cut edge sanded and coated with edge paint; crisp, modern;
  needs several coats for durability.
- **Bound/piped edge** — a binding strip; casual or structured looks.
Specify edge type per seam; mixing without intent looks unfinished.

## 5. Prototype and spec
1. Build a **paper/card mock-up** at full size; tape it, load it with real contents,
   carry it on the shoulder. Revise proportions now — leather samples are expensive.
2. Then a cheap-material sample (e.g. canvas or offcut leather) before the real one.
3. Tech pack: front/side/back/top/interior views, all dimensions (external, internal,
   strap drop, handle length), materials per part, hardware with supplier codes and
   finish, edge finish, stitch per cm, lining and pocket layout, logo placement.

## Gotchas
- Handles that look elegant but cut the hand when loaded — test with ~2–3 kg inside.
- Zip pulls that hit the hand or scratch adjoining leather.
- Interior pockets forgotten until sampling.
- Strap too short for winter coats on crossbody styles — make it adjustable.

## Verify
- [ ] Internal dimensions fit the stated contents; mock-up carried with weight.
- [ ] Strap drop tested on more than one body height.
- [ ] Every hardware attachment backed with reinforcement; hardware sized to straps.
- [ ] Hardware finish and edge finish consistent across the capsule.
- [ ] Tech pack lists every material, part and dimension.
