# Exotic skins and CITES compliance

**Goal:** legally source and sell products in exotic skins, or decide not to.

## CITES basics
- CITES (Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species) lists species in appendices:
  - **Appendix I:** trade generally prohibited (e.g. some crocodilians wild populations).
  - **Appendix II:** trade allowed with export permits; most commercial crocodile, alligator,
    python and lizard skins.
  - **Appendix III:** listed by individual countries.
- Check species against the CITES checklist/Species+; listings change at each Conference of the
  Parties.

## Paperwork
- Export permit from the country of origin; re-export certificates when finished goods cross
  borders again; import permits for some markets (EU requires import permits for Annex A/B under
  Reg. 338/97, stricter than CITES).
- Crocodilian skins carry **universal tags**; keep tag numbers traceable to products.
- Pre-convention or personal-effects exemptions are narrow; do not rely on them commercially.

## Sourcing
- Farmed (captive-bred or ranched) skins are typical in luxury; ask for farm audits and animal
  welfare standards (e.g. ICFA, SAFE).
- Python and lizard supply chains carry welfare and legality risks.

## Retail
- Some US states (e.g. California) ban sale of certain species products; check local laws.
- Many brands and retailers have exotic-free policies; check buyer policy.
- Alternatives: embossed croc/snake on calf, stamped or printed leathers, labelled as such.

## Gotchas
- Shipping samples without permits leads to seizure at customs.
- Mislabelled "croc embossed" vs real skin is misleading; label clearly.

## Verify
- [ ] Species listing and permits checked per market.
- [ ] Tags/traceability recorded.
- [ ] Retailer policy confirmed.
