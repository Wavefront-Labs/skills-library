# Fascinators and headpieces

**Goal:** headpieces that stay secure, sit at a flattering angle and suit the occasion.

## Types
- **Fascinator:** small decorative piece (feathers, flowers, net) on a comb, clip or headband.
- **Hatinator:** larger, hat-like base (often 10 cm+ diameter) on a headband; meets dress codes
  that require "hats" (e.g. Royal Ascot Royal Enclosure requires a hat with a solid base of
  10 cm or more; fascinators not permitted there).
- **Button/pillbox headpieces**, **halos and crowns**, **bridal headpieces**.

## Construction (designer's spec)
- Base: blocked sinamay, buckram covered in fabric, felt, leather or straw.
- Trims: feathers (coque, ostrich, goose biots), silk flowers, veiling (Russian/French net),
  crin, beads.
- Attachment: metal or plastic combs sewn under the base, millinery elastic (behind the ear
  under hair), satin-covered headbands, clips. Heavy pieces need two attachments.

## Placement and balance
- Worn usually on the right side, angled forward, above the eyebrow line.
- Visual weight: tall feathers balance a small base; keep the centre of mass over the
  attachment point.

## Gotchas
- Headbands on light pieces tip back; use elastic.
- Feathers from non-farmed birds may be restricted; source from licensed suppliers.
- Check event dress codes before marketing as appropriate.

## Verify
- [ ] Wear test 2-3 hours with movement.
- [ ] Attachment rated for the weight.
- [ ] Dress-code compatibility stated.
