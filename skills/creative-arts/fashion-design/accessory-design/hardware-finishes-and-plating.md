# Hardware finishes and plating

**Goal:** hardware that looks right next to the leather, stays that way in use, and meets
chemical and allergy rules.

## Base metals
- **Zinc alloy (zamak):** die-cast, cheap, complex shapes; must be plated.
- **Brass:** premium, machined or cast; can be plated or left natural (patinates).
- **Stainless steel:** durable, nickel-containing; good for rings and chains.
- **Aluminium:** light; anodised colours.

## Finishes
- **Electroplating:** nickel, nickel-free (e.g. white bronze), gold tones (light gold, rose,
  antique), gunmetal, ruthenium, black nickel; often finished with lacquer.
- **PVD** (physical vapour deposition): hard, wear-resistant coloured coatings; more durable
  than standard plating.
- **Antique/brushed/polished** textures.
- **Enamel/epoxy** coloured fills.

## Specify
- Base metal, finish name with a physical master sample (hardware colour standard), thickness
  (e.g. microns of plating) where possible, nickel-free requirement, lacquer.
- EU REACH Annex XVII nickel release limit 0.5 ug/cm2/week for prolonged skin contact (EN 1811).
- Lead and cadmium limits (REACH, CPSIA for children's products).

## Tests
- Salt spray (ISO 9227/ASTM B117): e.g. 24-48 h without corrosion.
- Abrasion/rub, sweat (artificial perspiration) tests, colour fastness of coating.

## Gotchas
- Gold tones vary between suppliers; approve one master and keep it.
- Cheap plating wears at contact points (clasp edges) within months.
- Mixing finishes from different factories in one bag shows mismatch.

## Verify
- [ ] Master finish sample approved.
- [ ] Nickel and lead tests passed.
- [ ] Salt spray result recorded.
