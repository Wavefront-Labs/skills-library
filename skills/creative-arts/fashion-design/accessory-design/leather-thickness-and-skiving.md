# Leather thickness and skiving

**Goal:** right thickness per component, thinned where it folds or overlaps, so goods are
strong but not bulky.

## Units
- Metric mm or **ounces** (US): 1 oz = 1/64 in = about 0.4 mm. Leather is sold in ranges
  (e.g. 3-4 oz = 1.2-1.6 mm).

## Typical thickness by use
| Part | Thickness |
|---|---|
| Bag body (soft) | 1.0-1.4 mm |
| Bag body (structured) | 1.4-2.0 mm, or thinner with backing |
| Straps/handles | 2.5-4 mm or folded and stitched |
| Belts | 3.5-4.5 mm (veg-tan) |
| Wallet pieces / card slots | 0.6-1.0 mm |
| Linings | 0.5-0.8 mm |

## Splitting and skiving
- **Splitting:** machine reduces the whole piece to even thickness (band-knife splitter).
- **Skiving:** thinning edges or areas only, with a bell skiver machine or skiving knife.
  - Edge skive for turned edges (fold-over): skive 10-15 mm wide down to about half thickness.
  - Skive overlaps (seam lap) so the joint is not double thickness.
  - **Feather skive** at a taper for invisible joins.

## Tolerances
- Hides vary in thickness: specify a range (e.g. 1.2-1.4 mm) and measure with a thickness
  gauge (ISO 2589) at several points.

## Gotchas
- Over-skiving creates weak points that tear at folds.
- Thick leather on small SLGs makes them bulky; plan layers by counting thicknesses at the
  thickest stack.
- Splitting veg-tan too thin removes firmness.

## Verify
- [ ] Thickness specified per part in mm.
- [ ] Skive widths/depths on the pattern.
- [ ] Thickest stack measured on prototype.
