# Linings, counters and toe puffs

**Goal:** the hidden components that keep a shoe's shape and support the foot.

## Components
- **Toe puff (toe box stiffener)**: between upper and lining at the toe; keeps toe shape.
  Materials: thermoplastic sheets (heat-activated), solvent-activated, or leather in
  traditional shoes. Safety footwear uses steel or composite toe caps (EN ISO 20345).
- **Heel counter**: stiffener around the heel; holds the heel and gives rearfoot stability.
  Thermoplastic, fibreboard, or leather (traditional). Height and stiffness specified.
- **Lining**: leather (breathable, premium; calf, goat, pig), textile mesh (sneakers),
  shearling (winter). Lining colour and leather type are design choices.
- **Shank**: rigid strip (steel, fibreglass, wood, TPU) under the arch between insole and
  outsole; supports heels and prevents the sole folding at the waist.
- **Insole board**: cellulose board or leather (welted shoes have leather insoles with a
  holdfast/rib); strobel sock in sneakers.
- **Sock lining / footbed**: visible insole; cushioning foam, branding.

## Design implications
- Soft unstructured shoes omit or minimise counters and puffs (slippers, ballet flats).
- Heels above ~4-5 cm need a shank.
- Toe shape relies on the puff; a sharp chisel toe needs a firm puff.

## Gotchas
- Too-stiff counters blister; too soft collapse.
- Missing shank in heels = sole folding and instability.
- Leather lining vs textile changes sizing slightly (thickness).

## Verify
- [ ] Each component specified with material and size.
- [ ] Shank present for heeled styles.
- [ ] Wear test checks heel hold and toe shape retention.
