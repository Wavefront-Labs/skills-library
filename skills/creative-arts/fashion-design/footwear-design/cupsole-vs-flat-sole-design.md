# Cupsole vs flat sole design

**Goal:** pick the sole architecture that fits the shoe's look, comfort and construction.

## Cupsole
- A moulded rubber (or TPU/rubber) sole with **raised sidewalls** forming a cup the upper
  sits in. Upper is cemented and often **stitched through the sidewall** (classic tennis
  and court shoes).
- Durable, stable, supportive; heavier and stiffer.
- Sidewall height (e.g. 15-30 mm) and texture are key design features (stitching visible
  on the side, logos, foxing tape look).

## Flat/unit soles
- **Flat sole**: a slab of rubber, leather or EVA cemented under the lasted upper; minimal
  sidewall. Used for minimal sneakers, vulcanised looks (with foxing), loafers.
- **Midsole + outsole units**: foam midsole (EVA, PU, TPU foams) with rubber outsole pods;
  performance shoes.

## Choosing
| Want | Choose |
|---|---|
| Retro court/tennis style, durability | Stitched cupsole |
| Lightweight cushioning | Midsole/outsole unit |
| Minimal low profile | Flat sole |
| Repairable, dress | Leather sole, welted |

## Design specs
Sole height at heel and forefoot (heel-to-toe drop), sidewall height, tread pattern,
material and hardness (Shore A), colour, logo emboss positions; mould per size range.

## Gotchas
- Cupsoles require a mould per size (costly tooling); share across styles.
- Too-high sidewalls restrict flex at the forefoot; add flex grooves.
- Cemented-only cupsoles on heavy-use shoes can separate; stitch where possible.

## Verify
- [ ] Sole architecture chosen with use case.
- [ ] Heights, drop, material hardness specified.
- [ ] Flex test and bond strength test done on the sample.
