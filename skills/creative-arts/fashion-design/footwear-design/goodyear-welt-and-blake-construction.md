# Goodyear welt and Blake construction

**Goal:** know what each stitched construction gives in look, comfort, cost and repair.

## Goodyear welt
- Insole has a **rib** (gemming) on the underside; the upper and lining are stitched to the rib
  along with a **welt** strip; the outsole is then stitched to the welt (lockstitch).
- Cavity filled with cork; shank in waist.
- **Pros:** resoleable many times without touching the upper stitch; water resistant; robust.
- **Cons:** stiffer at first; heavier; visible welt edge; costlier; needs a welt-friendly last.

## Blake
- Upper lasted over insole; outsole stitched **straight through** the insole, upper and sole
  from inside (Blake machine).
- **Pros:** very flexible and light, slim close-cut sole edge (Italian look), cheaper.
- **Cons:** stitch inside the shoe can let water in; resoling needs a Blake machine; less
  structured.

## Blake Rapid
- Blake stitch to a midsole, then a second (rapid) stitch to the outsole. More robust and
  water-resistant than Blake, more flexible than Goodyear; resoleable.

## Spec
- Construction method, welt type (storm welt, 360 or 270-degree), welt colour, stitch density
  (e.g. 6-9 stitches per inch on the outsole), sole edge finish, sole leather or rubber.

## Gotchas
- "Goodyear welt" sometimes faked with a glued decorative welt; check for the rib/insole stitch.
- 360-degree welts need a last with the heel shape to carry them.
