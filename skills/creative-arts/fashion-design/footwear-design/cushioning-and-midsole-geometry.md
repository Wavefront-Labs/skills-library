# Cushioning and midsole geometry

**Goal:** midsoles that deliver the brief's cushioning and stability through foam choice and shape.

## Foams
| Foam | Character |
|---|---|
| **EVA** (compression-moulded or injected) | Light, cheap, packs out over time |
| **Supercritical EVA / blends** | Lighter, bouncier |
| **PEBA** (e.g. Pebax-based) | Very light, high energy return; race shoes; less durable |
| **TPU (beaded, e.g. expanded TPU)** | Durable, bouncy, heavier |
| **PU** | Durable, heavy; work/walking |

- Hardness spec: **Asker C** for foams (e.g. 45-60 C); softer = more cushioning, less stability.

## Geometry
- **Stack height:** heel and forefoot (mm). **Drop (offset)** = heel minus forefoot.
- **Rocker:** curved toe and heel; toe spring and rocker apex position (often ~65-75% of length)
  ease roll-off, especially with stiff plates.
- **Plates:** carbon fibre or nylon/TPU, full length or partial; stiffen forefoot and add propulsion.
- **Stability:** wider base (flare), raised sidewalls, medial posts (firmer foam zone),
  guide rails.
- **Sculpting/decoupling:** grooves, heel bevel to soften heel strike.

## Development
- CAD on the last bottom; proto with 3D-printed or CNC foam; mechanical tests (impact
  attenuation, e.g. ASTM F1976; energy return %).

## Gotchas
- High stacks without wide bases are unstable.
- Soft foams compress quickly in heat or after mileage; test durability.
- Rocker apex too far forward feels unnatural for walking.

## Verify
- [ ] Stack, drop and durometer specified.
- [ ] Mechanical test results vs benchmark.
- [ ] Wear-test feedback on stability.
