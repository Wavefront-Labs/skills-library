# Strobel and slip-lasted construction

**Goal:** understand the dominant athletic and casual shoe construction so designs suit it.

## How it works
1. The upper is closed into a 3D shape and its lower edge is **stitched to a flexible
   fabric or EVA sheet** (the strobel sock) with a strobel (zigzag overedge) machine.
2. The resulting "slipper" is **slip-lasted**: forced over the last from the top.
3. Sole units (midsole/outsole or cupsole) are cemented on after priming and heat activation.
4. A removable sockliner sits on top of the strobel inside.

## Advantages
- Flexible and light (no rigid insole board).
- Fast and automatable; suits knitted and engineered uppers.
- Cushioning midsoles bond directly under the foot.

## Contrast with board-lasted
Board-lasted shoes have the upper pulled over a stiff insole board and cemented underneath
(more stability, less flexibility). **Combination lasting** = board at the heel, strobel in
the forefoot.

## Design implications
- Uppers can be lighter and less structured; heel counters still recommended for stability.
- The strobel seam sits under the foot; sockliner thickness matters for comfort.
- Cupsoles (see the cupsole skill) or midsoles must wrap enough to hide the strobel seam.

## Gotchas
- Strobel shoes are hard to resole.
- Too-thin strobel material on heavy users stretches and causes instability.
- Knit uppers need fused skins at stress points for lockdown.

## Verify
- [ ] Construction specified (strobel, board, combination).
- [ ] Strobel material and sockliner thickness in the spec.
- [ ] Wear test checks stability and seam comfort.
