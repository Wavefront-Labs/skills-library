# Synthetic and bio-based alternatives

**Goal:** choose leather alternatives that perform, and describe them honestly.

## Options
| Material | Make-up | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **PU-coated fabric** | PU layer on textile backing | Most common "vegan leather"; can peel/crack over time |
| **PVC** | PVC on fabric | Cheap, durable, plasticiser concerns; avoid where brand restricts PVC |
| **Microfibre (PU microfibre)** | Non-woven microfibre impregnated with PU | Closest to leather in strength and feel; high-performance footwear |
| **Plant-based** (apple, cactus, grape, pineapple fibre) | Plant waste blended with PU or bio-polyols | Often still PU-bound; check bio-content % |
| **Mycelium-based** | Fungal mycelium sheets | Emerging; supply and durability variable |
| **Recycled PU / PET backing** | | Improves impact; check claims |

## Specify
- Backing fabric (knit, woven, non-woven), coating thickness, total thickness, weight, finish.
- Bio-based content (% by weight, ASTM D6866 or EN 16785 test), certification of claims.
- Performance: flex resistance (Bally flex, SATRA TM55, e.g. 100,000 cycles dry), hydrolysis
  resistance for PU (jungle test/ageing), tear and seam strength, colour transfer.

## Labelling
- EU footwear labelling (Directive 94/11/EC) uses pictograms: leather, coated leather,
  textile, other materials; synthetics are "other materials". Avoid "leather" in names for
  non-leather (restricted in several countries).

## Gotchas
- Cheap PU hydrolyses (crumbles) within a few years.
- "Vegan" says nothing about sustainability; many are fossil-based.
- Synthetic uppers do not stretch like leather; size and last accordingly.

## Verify
- [ ] Hydrolysis and flex tests passed.
- [ ] Bio-content claim tested.
- [ ] Label pictogram correct.
