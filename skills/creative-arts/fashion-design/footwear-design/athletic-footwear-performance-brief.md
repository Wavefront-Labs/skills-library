# Athletic footwear performance brief

**Goal:** a brief that tells design, development and testing what the shoe must do, in
measurable terms.

## Sections
1. **Sport and use:** road running (daily trainer, tempo, race), trail, court, field;
   surfaces; distances.
2. **Athlete profile:** level, weight range, foot strike (heel, mid, forefoot), pronation
   tendencies, sizes.
3. **Biomechanics needs:**
   - Cushioning/energy return (impact attenuation, rebound).
   - Stability (medial support, wide base, guide rails, heel counter).
   - Traction (lug pattern, compound) for surface.
   - Flexibility and forefoot rocker.
   - Lateral containment for court/field (side-to-side cuts).
4. **Targets:** weight (e.g. men's 9 US under 250 g), stack heights heel/forefoot (mm),
   offset (drop, e.g. 4-10 mm), price.
5. **Competitive set:** benchmark shoes with measured weight, stack, drop.
6. **Rules:** e.g. World Athletics road shoe rule: max 40 mm sole thickness; one rigid plate.
7. **Test plan:** lab (impact, flex, traction coefficient, durability) and wear test panels.

## Gotchas
- Briefs without numbers lead to opinion-driven development.
- Over-specifying technology (plates, foams) before function.
- Not checking competition rules for race shoes.

## Verify
- [ ] Measurable targets for weight, stack, drop, price.
- [ ] Benchmark data collected.
- [ ] Test plan with wear-test panel defined.
