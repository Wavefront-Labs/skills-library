# Cemented construction

**Goal:** understand the world's most common shoe construction so designs suit it and quality
can be checked.

## Process
1. Upper is lasted over the last and insole; lasting margin glued (cement lasting) to the insole
   underside, toe and heel by machine.
2. Bottom area **roughed** (abraded) to the sole outline so adhesive bonds.
3. **Primer** (for rubber: halogenation; for PU/EVA: specific primers) applied to soles.
4. Adhesive (PU or neoprene/polychloroprene) applied to both surfaces, heat-activated.
5. Sole pressed on (hydraulic press), held for set time; cooled; last pulled.

## Suits
- Fashion shoes, sneakers with cupsoles/unit soles, women's heels, sandals. Light, flexible,
  thin profile, any sole material, cheaper and faster.

## Limits
- Not resoleable in a practical sense (can be, but rarely economic).
- Bond failure (sole separation) is the main failure; quality depends on roughing and adhesive.

## Quality checks
- **Sole bond strength** (SATRA TM411 or EN ISO 17708 peel test): target values per
  category (e.g. > 3-4 N/mm for many fashion shoes).
- Visual: no glue lines on the upper, sole edges tight, roughing hidden under the sole edge.

## Design notes
- Avoid roughing lines showing: sole wall must cover the roughed area.
- Light-coloured uppers show adhesive stains; specify careful cleaning.

## Gotchas
- Wrong primer for the sole compound = separation in the field.
- Humid factories need adhesive reactivation controls.

## Verify
- [ ] Bond test passed.
- [ ] Sole edge clean and tight.
- [ ] Adhesive and primer specified for materials.
