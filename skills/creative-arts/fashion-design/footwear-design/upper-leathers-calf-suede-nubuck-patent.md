# Upper leathers (calf, suede, nubuck, patent)

**Goal:** match the leather to the shoe's look, price, wear and care.

## Main types
| Leather | What it is | Look | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full-grain calf (box calf) | Top grain intact, from young cattle | Fine, smooth, polishes well | Premium dress shoes |
| Corrected grain | Grain sanded and coated | Uniform, glossy, plasticky | Cheaper; doesn't age well, cracks |
| Suede | Flesh side napped (from split) | Soft nap | Water spots; protector spray |
| Nubuck | Grain side lightly buffed | Fine velvety nap | More durable than suede |
| Patent | Coated with high-gloss finish | Mirror shine | Creases crack; formal |
| Cordovan | Horse shell, dense membrane | Rolling creases, deep shine | Expensive, limited supply |
| Pull-up / oiled | Waxed/oiled leathers | Lightens where flexed | Workwear and boots |
| Exotic (crocodile, ostrich) | CITES-regulated | Distinctive grain | Permits required |

## Thickness
Upper leathers usually about 1.0-1.6 mm for shoes, thicker for boots/workwear; specify
a range in mm.

## Tannage
Chrome-tanned (soft, consistent, most uppers), vegetable-tanned (firmer; often for
soles/insoles), combinations. Check REACH/CPSIA restrictions on chromium VI.

## Choosing
- Formal and polishable: full-grain calf.
- Casual softness: suede, nubuck.
- Price-led: corrected grain or synthetic.

## Gotchas
- Grain quality varies within a hide; specify clicking (cutting) from the best areas for vamps.
- Suede colours vary between lots more than smooth leathers; buy whole lots.
- Patent and corrected grain don't stretch to fit like aniline calf.

## Verify
- [ ] Leather type, thickness and finish specified.
- [ ] Tannery certification (e.g. Leather Working Group) recorded where relevant.
- [ ] Swatch flex-tested for cracking.
