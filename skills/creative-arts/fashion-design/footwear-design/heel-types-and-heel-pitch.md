# Heel types and heel pitch

**Goal:** heels that match the last, carry the wearer safely, and suit the style.

## Types
| Heel | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Stiletto** | Thin, usually 70-120 mm; needs a steel spike/core inside plastic |
| **Kitten** | 30-50 mm, slim, curved |
| **Block** | Wide, stable; any height |
| **Cone** | Wide at top, tapering |
| **Cuban** | Low/medium, straight front, slight back taper; men's boots |
| **Stacked** | Leather layers, visible lifts |
| **Wedge** | Continuous to the toe; often cork, EVA, wood |
| **Platform** | Raises forefoot; lowers effective pitch |

## Heel height and pitch
- **Heel height** is measured at the back of the heel from ground to seat.
- The heel must match the **last's heel height**; a 70 mm heel on an 80 mm last tilts the
  foot and wears unevenly.
- **Pitch/effective height** = heel height minus platform height. Comfortable walking pitch
  is usually under ~90 mm.
- Heel breast (front face) angle and seat curve must fit the last bottom.

## Materials
- ABS or nylon plastic heels covered in leather; wooden heels (beech); stacked leather;
  metal heels; TPU top-pieces (replaceable tips).
- Stilettos: steel core inside; test with a fatigue/impact test (SATRA TM21 heel fatigue,
  TM34 heel attachment).

## Gotchas
- Heel set too far back makes the shoe tip backward; too far forward breaks the waist.
- Slim heels above ~100 mm need a steel shank in the waist.
- Heel attachment (nails/screws through the insole) must be checked for strength.

## Verify
- [ ] Heel height matches last.
- [ ] Fatigue and attachment tested.
- [ ] Top-piece material specified.
