# Pump, sandal and mule design

**Goal:** women's shoe styles that look elegant and actually stay on the foot.

## Pumps (courts)
- No fastening; the shoe stays on through **topline tension**, **throat depth** and heel cup fit.
- Throat (vamp line) depth: deeper vamp holds the foot better; a low-cut vamp shows toe
  cleavage but slips more.
- Topline: tight at the heel and sides; side gaping is the common failure on high heels.
- D'Orsay: open sides; needs a stronger heel counter and topline grip.

## Sandals
- Straps must hold the foot at three zones: toes/forefoot, instep/ankle, heel.
- Ankle strap stops heel slip; T-bar and toe post (thong) hold forefoot.
- Strap widths 5-15 mm typical; edges turned or painted; adjustable buckle at ankle.
- Footbed: sandals show the footbed; finish and shape it (moulded, covered).
- Open-toe: the last's toe area is designed for toes to sit on the edge without overhang.

## Mules
- Backless; holding is all in the vamp. Vamp must cover the instep high enough; heel height
  usually limited (up to ~70-80 mm) for walkability.
- Add a slightly raised heel seat and textured sock lining to stop the foot sliding.

## Heel height and fit
- Above ~90-100 mm, toe spring, platform and last pitch must change; use platform to reduce
  effective pitch.

## Gotchas
- Thin straps on high heels cut into the foot; widen or pad.
- Pump toplines need padding or binding to stop heel rubbing.
- Sandals with buckles that sit on the ankle bone hurt.

## Verify
- [ ] Wear test walking on stairs.
- [ ] No heel slip or side gape.
- [ ] Strap positions clear of bones.
