# Sole mould development and costs

**Goal:** budget and run sole tooling development so new soles arrive on time and within cost.

## Open vs proprietary
- **Open soles:** sole makers' catalogue soles; no tooling cost, available to anyone; quick.
- **Proprietary soles:** your design; you pay for moulds; unique; long development.

## Development stages
1. Sketch and side/bottom views on the last bottom.
2. **CAD** (Rhino, Fusion 360, Romans CAD, or the sole maker's CAD) built on the scanned last.
3. **3D-printed or CNC proto** sole for look and fit (fast, cheap).
4. **Sample mould** (often in aluminium or resin) in the base size.
5. Approve, then **production moulds** for each size or size group.

## Costs (very approximate; vary by region and process)
- Rubber compression moulds: per size mould in aluminium, a few hundred to a few thousand USD.
- Injection (PU/TPU/EVA) steel moulds cost more but last longer.
- Full size run: multiply by sizes (e.g. 8-12 moulds; less with paired sizes) and by left/right.
- Amortise: divide total tooling by the planned pairs over the sole's life.

## Lead time
- CAD to first mould sample: 4-8 weeks; production tooling 6-10 weeks.

## Gotchas
- Changes after cutting the steel are expensive; validate with protos and fit trials.
- Size grouping saves money but misfits extremes.
- Branding on soles in tooling cannot be changed cheaply.

- [ ] Tooling cost and amortisation in the cost sheet.
