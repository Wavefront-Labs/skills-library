# Single jersey, rib and purl structures

**Goal:** choose the base structure for a knit garment knowing its stretch, curl,
thickness and machine requirements.

## Single jersey
- All stitches knit on one needle bed: smooth V's on the face, loops on the back.
- **Curls** at edges (sides curl to the back, top/bottom to the front) - needs finished edges
  (ribs, hems).
- Light, economical, moderate widthways stretch; prone to spirality (twist) in some yarns.

## Rib
- Alternating knit and purl wales, knitted on **two needle beds** (V-bed flat machines) or
  dial-and-cylinder circular.
- **1x1 rib**: very elastic widthways, doesn't curl; used for cuffs, welts, fitted bodies.
- **2x2 rib**: more pronounced vertical lines, even more stretch; trims and bodies.
- **Fisherman/English rib (tuck rib)**: thick, lofty, uses tuck stitches; heavy.
- Rib narrows the fabric considerably vs jersey at the same needle count; plan width.

## Purl (links-links)
- Alternating rows of knit and purl stitches: same both sides, very lengthways stretch,
  thick, doesn't curl. Requires links-links machines or transfers on modern V-beds; slower.
- Basis for garter-stitch look and moss/seed combinations.

## Double-bed fabrics (interlock, milano) - see their own skill.

## Choosing
| Need | Structure |
|---|---|
| Light, drapey body | Single jersey |
| Fitted, stretchy | Rib |
| Textured, reversible | Purl, moss |
| Hems that don't curl | Rib or double-bed starts |

## Gotchas
- Using single jersey without edge finishes = curling garments.
- Ribs relax and narrow after washing; swatch and wash.
- Spirality in single jersey twists side seams; balance yarn twist or use compact yarns.

## Verify
- [ ] Structure chosen with stretch and curl in mind.
- [ ] Washed swatch measured for width/length change.
- [ ] Machine capability (beds, links-links) confirmed with the factory.
