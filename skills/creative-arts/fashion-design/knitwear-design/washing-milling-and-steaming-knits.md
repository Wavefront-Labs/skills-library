# Washing, milling and steaming knits

**Goal:** knitwear that reaches its final hand, size and surface after finishing, consistently.

## Why finishing matters
- Off-machine knits are stiff, with uneven stitches and oils/wax. Finishing relaxes the loops,
  removes wax, sets size and creates hand.

## Processes
- **Wash:** removes spinning oils and wax; softeners added for hand.
- **Milling / fulling:** controlled agitation in warm water with detergent felts wool and
  cashmere slightly, filling the stitch (cashmere "bloom"). Light, medium, heavy milling;
  heavy milling shrinks and densifies.
- **Tumble dry** (where allowed) adds loft.
- **Steaming and blocking:** garment pulled to size on a frame/board and steamed to set
  dimensions.

## Sizing
- Spec measurements **after finishing** (finished dimensions). The factory knits to a
  pre-finish size based on swatch shrinkage: e.g. knit 5% longer to mill down.
- Approve swatches finished with the same recipe before sizing the pattern.

## Spec on the tech pack
- Finishing recipe name/number from the factory, milling level, softener, temperature,
  drying method, and target hand (reference swatch).
- Care label consistent with the finish (superwash wool can be machine washed; milled
  cashmere usually hand wash or delicate).

## Gotchas
- Superwash yarns barely mill; non-superwash wool can felt badly in a home wash.
- Over-steaming flattens texture (cables, tuck).
- Different colours of the same yarn can shrink differently; check per colour.

## Verify
- [ ] Finished measurements within tolerance.
- [ ] Hand matches reference swatch.
- [ ] Finishing recipe on the tech pack.
