# Knit button bands and welts

**Goal:** edges that hold the garment's shape, lie flat, and button without gaping.

## Welts (hems and cuffs)
- **1x1 rib, 2x2 rib**: standard; widthways stretch keeps hems close.
- **Tubular / circular welt**: knitted as a tube on two beds; neat, roll-free, often used as
  a first course start on flat machines.
- **Mock rib, milano or double welt**: flat, non-clinging hems.
- Height: 5-8 cm hem welt on classic sweaters, cuffs 5-7 cm.

## Button bands
- Knit vertically (separate strip, linked or sewn on) or picked up and knitted horizontally
  along the front edge.
- Band width 2.5-3.5 cm for standard cardigans; full needle rib or 1x1 rib, often doubled
  (folded) for stability.
- Band length: slightly shorter than the front edge (~2-5%) so it does not ripple; stretch
  the band gently when attaching.
- Use grosgrain ribbon behind bands on fine-gauge or loose structures to prevent stretch.

## Buttonholes
- Knit-in (transfer) buttonholes or cut-and-sewn buttonholes (in fully fashioned production
  usually machine-sewn after knitting).
- Size: button diameter + 1-2 mm (knit stretches). First button 1-1.5 cm below neckline
  edge, last above the welt; space evenly.

## Gotchas
- Band too long: front edges wave; too short: fronts pull up.
- Horizontal buttonholes in rib bands stretch; vertical buttonholes pull less.
- Heavy buttons on light knits sag; add a backing button.
