# WHOLEGARMENT and seamless knitting

**Goal:** design garments knitted in one piece on the machine (no linking or sewing), using the
technology where it adds value.

## The technology
- **WHOLEGARMENT** (Shima Seiki, MACH2, SWG series) and **Knit and Wear** (Stoll ADF): flat
  machines with four needle beds or slide needles, knitting body and sleeves as tubes and joining
  at the underarm, completing the garment on the machine.
- Gauges commonly 5-18 gg; effective gauge is often half for tubular structures.

## Design rules
- Forms: body and sleeves as tubes; shaping by widening/narrowing, short rows for 3D (shoulders,
  heels, darts), seamless collars and pockets possible.
- No seams = no side-seam stabilisation; heavy garments can grow; tighter structures help.
- Intarsia and some jacquards are limited; check with the programmer.
- Rib and structure changes at cuffs and hems built in.

## Process
1. Brief the factory/programmer with sketch, measurements, yarn and gauge.
2. Programmer builds in SDS-ONE APEX (Shima) or M1plus (Stoll); first samples often need 2-3 rounds.
3. Measure and adjust stitch length and shaping; programming changes are the main cost.

## When to use it
- Good: premium basics, fit-critical pieces, zero-waste aims (no cutting), on-demand production.
- Less good: large intarsia, very fine gauges with complex detail, small budgets for sampling.

## Gotchas
- Fewer factories with WHOLEGARMENT capacity; lead times and MOQs vary.
- Repairs or alterations are harder without seams.

## Verify
- [ ] Factory confirmed capable of the gauge and structure.
- [ ] Sampling rounds budgeted.
- [ ] Measurements checked after washing/steaming.
