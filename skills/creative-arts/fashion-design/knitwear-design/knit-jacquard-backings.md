# Knit jacquard backings

**Goal:** colour patterns knitted in, with a backing chosen for weight, stretch and
production feasibility.

## How it works
Each row knits the colour needed on the face; other colours either **float** behind or are
knitted into a **backing** on the back bed.

## Backings
| Backing | Back | Weight/stretch | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Single-bed float (Fair Isle-like) | Floats | Light, stretchy | Float length limit; snags |
| Birdseye | Speckled colours alternate | Heavier, firmer | Most common double-bed jacquard; colours may show through on face |
| Ladder/striped back | Colours knit in stripes | Medium | Less show-through |
| Tubular (double jacquard) | Reverse image | Thick, two-layer | Reversible look, heavy |
| Blister | Puffed areas | Textured | 3D effects |

## Design rules
- **Colours per row**: 2-3 is economical; 4+ increases weight and cost and slows knitting.
- **Float length** (single-bed): keep under about 5-7 stitches at fine gauges, or float
  floats will snag; insert tie-ins.
- Design at stitch resolution: stitches are wider than tall (roughly 1.3-1.5:1 depending
  on gauge), so square artwork elongates; correct proportions in the chart.
- Small details smaller than 2-3 stitches disappear.

## Workflow
Artwork -> stitch chart at gauge -> colour index per stitch -> programmer assigns backing
(SDS-ONE APEX, M1plus) -> swatch.

## Gotchas
- Birdseye backing roughly doubles fabric weight vs single jersey.
- Colour show-through on light grounds; test.
- Too many colours per row reduce stretch and comfort.

## Verify
- [ ] Backing chosen and swatched.
- [ ] Colours per row and float lengths within limits.
- [ ] Chart corrected for stitch aspect ratio.
