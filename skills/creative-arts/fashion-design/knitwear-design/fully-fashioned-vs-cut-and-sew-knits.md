# Fully fashioned vs cut-and-sew knits

**Goal:** pick the knitwear construction that suits quality, price, design detail and waste.

## Methods
| Method | How | Character |
|---|---|---|
| **Fully fashioned** | Panels knitted to shape (increases/decreases), then linked | Fashioning marks visible at raglans/armholes; little waste; premium |
| **Cut-and-sew** | Knit fabric panels or yardage, cut with a pattern, overlocked | Fast, flexible shapes; 20-40% yarn waste; cheaper; edges overlocked |
| **Integral / partial fashioning** | Shaped panels with some cutting | Middle ground |
| **WHOLEGARMENT / seamless** | Garment knitted complete | No seams, highest tech (see wholegarment skill) |

## Decision guide
- Premium wool/cashmere, visible linking, comfort seams: **fully fashioned**.
- Fine-gauge jersey-like knits, complex woven-style shapes, low price: **cut-and-sew**.
- Expensive yarn: avoid cut-and-sew waste.
- Very chunky gauge: fully fashioned or hand-knit; cut-and-sew seams bulky.
- Prints or garment shapes requiring pattern pieces: cut-and-sew.

## Quality markers
- Fully fashioned: fashion marks, linked seams (one stitch, loop-to-loop), rib collars linked on.
- Cut-and-sew: overlocked seams, cut edges visible inside, collars sewn on.

## Costing
- Fully fashioned costs more machine time per piece but saves yarn; cut-and-sew is cheaper labour
  and quicker.

## Gotchas
- Cut-and-sew of loose-gauge knits can unravel at cut edges; stabilise and overlock immediately.
- Claiming "fully fashioned" when only partly fashioned misleads customers.

## Verify
- [ ] Construction stated on the tech pack.
- [ ] Yarn consumption reflects method.
- [ ] Seams/linking specified.
