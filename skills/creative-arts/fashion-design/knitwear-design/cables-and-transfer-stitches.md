# Cables and transfer stitches

**Goal:** cable and transfer patterns that knit cleanly, sit at the right width, and are
feasible on the chosen machine.

## Cables
- A cable crosses groups of stitches over each other (e.g. 2x2 cable = 2 stitches crossed
  over 2), right-leaning or left-leaning.
- Set on a **purl ground** (reverse stitches either side) so the cable pops.
- Crossing frequency sets the twist: crossing every 4-8 rows for a 2x2/3x3 cable.
- **Width take-up**: cables pull in widthways (often 15-30% narrower than jersey at the same
  stitch count). Swatch the panel and compensate stitches.
- Aran sweaters combine cables, honeycomb, diamonds, moss stitch panels.

## Transfer stitches
- Moving loops from one needle to another: used for cables on machines, for **pointelle**
  (eyelet holes from transfers leaving empty needles), for fully-fashioned shaping marks.
- Lace patterns are transfer-based.

## Machine limits
- On V-bed flat machines (Shima Seiki, Stoll), cables are made by racking and transferring;
  large crossings (e.g. 4x4 and more) stress yarn and may need slower speed or finer yarns.
- Check with the programmer: max crossing width at the given gauge and yarn.

## Charting
- Draw on a grid: one square = one stitch, one row; standard symbols for knit, purl, cable
  left/right, transfers, eyelets.
- Number rows and repeats; show the panel repeat boundaries.

## Gotchas
- Cables in hairy yarns lose definition.
- Forgetting width take-up makes cable sweaters too narrow.
- Very large cables add weight and bulk; check hand.

## Verify
- [ ] Cable panel swatched and width measured.
- [ ] Chart with symbols and repeats.
- [ ] Machine feasibility confirmed at the gauge.
