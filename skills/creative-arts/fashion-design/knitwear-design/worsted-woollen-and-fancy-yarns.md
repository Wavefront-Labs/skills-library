# Yarn fibre and spin: worsted, woollen and fancy yarns

**Goal:** pick the yarn whose construction gives the surface, hand and durability the knit
design needs.

## Spinning systems
| Yarn | How | Look and feel | Knit behaviour |
|---|---|---|---|
| Worsted-spun | Long combed fibres, aligned, smooth | Smooth, crisp, clear stitch definition | Great for fine gauges, cables, ribs; durable, less pilling |
| Woollen-spun | Shorter carded fibres, random | Lofty, hairy, warm, light | Soft blurry stitches; Shetland/lambswool character; pills more |
| Semi-worsted | Between | Balanced | Common in mid-market |
| Combed cotton | Short fibres removed | Smooth, lustrous | Clean stitch definition |
| Mercerised cotton | Caustic treatment | Shiny, strong | Crisp summer knits |

## Fancy yarns
Boucle (loops), slub (thick-thin), chenille (pile), tape/ribbon, brushed/mohair halo,
marl (two colours twisted), mouline, metallic lurex, gimp. They add texture but:
- hide stitch structure (complex stitches are wasted),
- may need larger needles and slower knitting,
- chenille "worms" (pile loops out) if knitted too loosely.

## Choosing
- Clear structure (cables, jacquard) -> worsted/smooth yarns.
- Soft, cosy, rustic -> woollen-spun, brushed.
- Texture as the design -> fancy yarns on simple stitches (jersey, garter).

## Gotchas
- Hairy yarns obscure colourwork edges.
- Low-twist soft yarns pill and abrade; check pilling (ISO 12945).
- Fancy yarns have larger minimums and colour limits at spinners.

## Verify
- [ ] Yarn spin matched to the stitch structure.
- [ ] Swatch confirms definition and hand.
- [ ] Pilling test on soft or fancy yarns.
