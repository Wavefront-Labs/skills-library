# Fair Isle and stranded colourwork

**Goal:** colourwork that reads as authentic Fair Isle or Nordic stranded knitting and knits
well on machine or by hand.

## Rules of the technique
- Traditional Fair Isle: **two colours per row** (one background, one pattern); colours change
  row by row to create shading.
- Motif bands: large bands (OXO, star, tree), smaller **peerie** bands (1-7 rows) between them,
  often symmetric vertically.
- Floats run behind; keep under **5 stitches** (hand) or the machine's limit (often 5-7 needles
  at 7-12 gg) or catch them.

## Colour sequencing
- Shade background and pattern colours light to dark toward the band centre and back again;
  contrast between pattern and background is what makes it readable.
- Squint test or greyscale the chart: if values are equal, the motif disappears.

## Machine production
- Single bed with floats = classic look but snaggable floats; or double-bed jacquard with
  birdseye or striped backing (see jacquard backings skill), heavier and stable.
- Supply a chart per colour per needle, colour codes matched to yarn shade cards.

## Hand-knit patterns
- Chart with symbols, worked in the round to keep the right side facing; steeks for cardigans.

## Gotchas
- More than two colours per row is jacquard, not Fair Isle; it changes weight and hand.
- Long floats at the underarm catch fingers.
- Traditional Shetland patterns carry heritage; credit sources and avoid claiming origin.

## Verify
- [ ] Max two colours per row.
- [ ] Float lengths checked.
- [ ] Greyscale value test passed.
