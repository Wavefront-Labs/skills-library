# Plying and number of ends per gauge

**Goal:** feed the right total yarn to the needle for the gauge and hand, by plying or by
running several ends together.

## Plied vs ends
- **Plied yarn:** singles twisted together by the spinner (2/28 Nm). Balanced, even, stronger.
- **Multiple ends:** several cones fed together into one feeder without twisting (e.g. 3 ends
  of 2/28 Nm). Cheaper flexibility; slightly less even.

## Calculating
- Resultant Nm of N ends of the same yarn = yarn Nm / N. Example: 3 ends of 2/28 (=14 Nm each)
  = 14 / 3 = about 4.7 Nm, suitable for 5-7 gg.
- Mixed yarns: add in tex (tex = 1000 / Nm), then convert back. Example: 2/28 wool (71.4 tex)
  + 1/30 silk (33.3 tex) = 104.7 tex = Nm 9.6.

## Uses
- **Colour mixing (mouliné/marl):** two ends of different colours fed together give a marl.
- **Hand:** more fine ends = softer, more fluid than one thick yarn at the same count.
- **Plating:** two ends in one feeder positioned so one shows on the face (e.g. elastane or
  a contrast colour plated).

## Tension and machines
- Each end comes from its own cone through tensioners; uneven tension gives loops and streaks.
- Machine feeder capacity limits ends; check with the factory.
- Waxing is usually applied to wool/cotton cones to reduce friction (see coned yarn skill).

## Gotchas
- Unplied ends can split on needles at fine gauges.
- Forgetting the plying in the count (writing 28 Nm for 2/28) doubles the yarn thickness error.

## Verify
- [ ] Ends and count stated per feeder in the spec.
- [ ] Swatch knitted at intended gauge and stitch length.
