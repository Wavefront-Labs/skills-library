# Milano, interlock and full needle rib

**Goal:** choose stable double-bed structures for tailored or structured knitwear and trims.

## Structures
| Structure | How | Character |
|---|---|---|
| **Full needle rib** | all needles both beds knit | Thick, dense, elastic; edges and heavy cardigans |
| **1x1 rib** | alternate needles on both beds | Classic stretchy rib |
| **Interlock** | two interlocked 1x1 ribs | Smooth both faces, stable, does not curl or ladder easily |
| **Full milano** | 1 course rib + 1 course single jersey on each bed (3-course repeat) | Firm, low stretch, flat both sides; structured jackets, dresses |
| **Half milano** | 1 course rib + 1 course jersey on one bed (2-course repeat) | Firm, slightly asymmetric; jackets, skirts |
| **Double jersey jacquards** | colour pattern + backing | Heavier, structured |

## Design use
- Milano and interlock give **woven-like stability**: tailoring-type knit jackets, structured dresses.
- They use much more yarn than single jersey (often 1.5-2x per area) and add weight.
- Tighter structures pill less and hold shape; good for fine gauge (12-16 gg) structured pieces.

## Specifying
- Name the structure explicitly; for milano state full or half and which face is right side.
- Give gauge and stitch density (wales and courses per cm) from an approved swatch.

## Gotchas
- Full needle rib at fine gauges can be very heavy and stiff.
- Milano curls less but still stretches in width; check stability with a weight test.
- Transfer and shaping on double-bed structures limit design detail.
