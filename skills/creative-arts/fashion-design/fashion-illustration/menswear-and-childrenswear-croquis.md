# Menswear and childrenswear croquis

## Menswear
- Stylisation is lower than womenswear: about **8.5-9 heads**, legs less elongated.
- Shoulders wide (~2 heads) and **wider than the hip** (~1.5 heads); waist barely tapers.
- Neck thicker, set wider; trapezius slope visible from neck to shoulder.
- Poses: weight evenly distributed or a relaxed contrapposto with a smaller hip tilt;
  hands in pockets, arms out from the body so sleeves and side seams read.
- Draw angular, straighter contours; muscle hints at deltoid, chest and calf, not curves.

## Childrenswear (heads drop as age drops)
| Age | Approx. heads | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Baby/toddler 1-2 | 4 | big head, no waist, short limbs, rounded belly |
| 3-5 | 5 | belly still forward, chubby legs |
| 6-8 | 6 | limbs lengthen, neck visible |
| 9-12 | 6.5-7 | slimmer, shoulders begin to widen (boys) / waist hints (girls) |
| Teen 13-16 | 7-8 | close to adult, stylise lightly |

Children have no hip-shoulder difference until puberty; use playful, active poses
(jumping, sitting, walking with toes in) and simple faces with eyes lower on the head.

## Gotchas
- A womenswear croquis with a beard does not read as menswear; rebuild the shoulder-hip ratio.
- Kidswear buyers want to see the garment at the real size range; avoid adult elongation.
- Keep one template per age band; garments drawn on the wrong band mislead on length.

## Verify
- [ ] Menswear shoulders wider than hips, waist taper minimal.
- [ ] Child figure head count matches the age band of the range.
- [ ] Pose shows the garment's key features.
