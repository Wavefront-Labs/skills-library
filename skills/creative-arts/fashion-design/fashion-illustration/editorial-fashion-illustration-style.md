# Editorial fashion illustration style

**Difference from design illustration:** design drawings explain a garment; editorial
illustration communicates attitude for a magazine, campaign, window or social channel.
Exaggeration, cropping and omission are allowed.

## Building a style
1. Study the canon (Antonio Lopez, René Gruau, Mats Gustafson and contemporary illustrators);
   note what each simplifies or exaggerates.
2. Make consistent decisions: line (continuous, broken, none), colour (flat, washes, limited
   palette), proportion (elongation, head size), texture (paper grain, brush).
3. Draw from life and catwalk photos repeatedly within those constraints until they become
   habits. A style is a set of repeatable choices.

## Composition
- Crop tightly or leave large negative space; magazine layouts need room for type.
- One focal point (face, sleeve, bag); simplify everything else.
- Unusual angles and partial figures read as editorial; full-length front poses read as design.

## Commission practice
- Confirm format, bleed, type area, deadline, usage rights and fee before sketching.
- Send 2-3 rough compositions for approval, then finals at 300 dpi at print size.
- Keep a portfolio of 12-20 consistent pieces.

## Gotchas
- Over-rendering editorial work kills its energy.
- Imitating a living illustrator closely harms your commissions; synthesise influences.

## Verify
- [ ] Style choices stated and consistent across a set.
- [ ] Composition leaves room for type when commissioned for print.
- [ ] Rights and specs agreed in writing.
