# Building and reusing a croquis template

**Goal:** a small library of figure templates you trace under layout paper (or lock as a
bottom layer digitally), so a 40-look range is drawn on one body and proportions compare fairly.

## Build the master
1. Pick a working height (e.g. 25-28 cm on A4/letter; 2000-2400 px tall digitally).
2. Divide into 9 equal head units (womenswear) and mark landmarks: chin 1, bust 2,
   waist ~3, hip/crotch 4, mid-thigh 5, knee 6, mid-calf 7, ankle 8, foot to 9.
3. Draw the front pose first: shoulder width ~1.5 heads, waist ~1 head, hip ~1.5 heads.
4. Add a **centre front line**, bust line, waist line, hip line, princess lines and
   armhole curve. These guides are what make the template useful; garments hang off them.
5. Ink cleanly in one weight; no face detail (it distracts when tracing).

## The set
Front static, front walking, 3/4 turn, back, and one seated or action pose. Same head
unit and scale on all of them. Name files `croquis_W_front_9h.png` etc.

## Use
- Under layout/marker paper on a lightbox, or as a locked bottom layer at 30-40% opacity.
- Duplicate the template per look; never draw on the master.
- For flats use a separate flat template (real 7.5-8 head proportion), not the croquis.

## Gotchas
- Templates traced from photos drift to ~7.5 heads; re-measure against the grid.
- Mixing templates of different heights on one lineup makes looks read as different sizes.
- Over-elongated legs (10+ heads) misrepresent hem lengths to pattern cutters; note
  real lengths in text when it matters.

## Verify
- [ ] All templates share head unit and landmark heights.
- [ ] CF, bust, waist and hip guides present on each.
- [ ] Master files locked or read-only.
