# Rendering knits, tweeds and fur

## General approach
1. Lay the base colour flat (marker or a flat layer).
2. Shadows with a darker value along folds and the far side of the body.
3. Texture last, **only where it reads**: strongest in mid-tones, faded in highlights and
   lost in deep shadow. Never cover the whole garment evenly.

## Knits
- Rib: parallel lines following the body's curve; close spacing for fine gauge.
- Cables: draw the twist as interlocking S-shapes with a shadow on one side of each cross.
- Chunky knits: bumps on the silhouette edge; the outline is never a smooth line.
- Jersey: no texture, soft clinging folds.

## Tweed
- Base colour + two or three fleck colours (marker dots, small strokes or a scatter brush).
- Hint the twill or herringbone with fine diagonal strokes in the mid-tone.
- Edges slightly broken to suggest a hairy surface.

## Fur and shearling
- Draw clumps, not hairs: tapered V-shaped strokes in the direction of the pelt.
- Silhouette edge broken; overlap clumps darker underneath, lighter on top.
- Long-hair fur: longer flowing strokes; short pile (mink, velvet): smooth with sheen.

## Digital
Use a texture brush or a clipped fabric scan at reduced opacity, warped to the garment;
paint shadows on a Multiply layer above it.

## Gotchas
- Uniform texture flattens the form. Fade it.
- Dark knits: draw texture with a lighter coloured pencil or white gel pen.

## Verify
- [ ] Texture scale matches the yarn or cloth.
- [ ] Silhouette edge suits the surface (broken for fur and chunky knit).
- [ ] Texture fades in highlights and shadow.
