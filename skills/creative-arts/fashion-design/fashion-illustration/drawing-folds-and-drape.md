# Folds, drape and tension lines

**Goal:** draw folds that explain how the garment fits and what it's made of, using few,
correct lines.

## Where folds come from
- Fabric hangs from **support points** (shoulders, bust, hips, bent elbows/knees) and
  pulls toward them. Folds radiate from support points or compress at bends.

## Fold types (standard drapery vocabulary)
| Fold | Where | Look |
|---|---|---|
| Pipe | Fabric hanging from one line (gathered skirt, curtain) | Tubes falling vertically |
| Zigzag | Compressed tube (bent elbow, trouser hem stacking) | Alternating diamond shapes |
| Spiral | Fabric wrapped around a limb (pushed-up sleeve) | Spiralling around the cylinder |
| Half-lock | Sharp bend (inside elbow, back of knee) | Fold that changes direction abruptly |
| Diaper/drape | Hanging between two points (cowl) | U-shaped swags |
| Drop/inert | Fabric resting on a surface | Soft spreading folds |
| Tension | Pulled across the body | Diagonal lines pointing to the pull |

## Fabric weight and line
- Light (chiffon): many small, soft folds; fine lines.
- Medium (cotton, wool): fewer, defined folds.
- Heavy/stiff (coating, taffeta): few large folds, crisp angles; edges hold shape.
- Knits cling and stretch: smooth over curves, fine tension lines.

## Practice
Drape fabric on a form or chair; draw only the 5 most important folds. Then the same
fabric type in 3 weights.

## Gotchas
- Too many folds look like crumpled fabric; choose few.
- Folds that point nowhere suggest bad fit (unless intended).
- Fold lines should taper (thick at base, thin at end).

## Verify
- [ ] Folds originate from support points or bends.
- [ ] Number and sharpness match the fabric weight.
- [ ] A viewer can guess the fabric from the drawing.
