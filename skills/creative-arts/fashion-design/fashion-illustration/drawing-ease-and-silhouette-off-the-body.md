# Drawing ease and silhouette off the body

## Contact points
Every garment **hangs from** some points and **leaves** the body at others. Mark them first:
- Shoulders, bust, hip points, waist (if belted) are typical hang points.
- Between them fabric either clings (fitted), skims (semi-fitted), or bridges (loose).
A fitted dress follows the croquis contour closely; a shift bridges from bust to hip in a
straight line; a trapeze swings out from the shoulder.

## Ease visible on the drawing
- Fitted: contour just outside the body line.
- Relaxed: visible air at the waist and under the arm.
- Oversized: shoulder seam drops down the arm, sleeve noticeably wider than the arm,
  hem stands clear of the hip.
Keep the body under the garment drawn lightly so the gap is believable.

## Fabric weight
- Light and fluid (chiffon, crepe): falls straight down from the hang point, clings
  to the forward leg in a walking pose, fine folds.
- Medium (cotton, wool suiting): holds shape, few large folds at elbows and knees.
- Stiff (taffeta, neoprene, felted wool): holds volume away from the body; folds are
  large and angular, the hem stays a wide ellipse.

## Gotchas
- Drawing every garment skin-tight hides the actual fit and misleads the sample room.
- Volume that ignores gravity: hems rise only where the leg pushes them.
- Hem ellipses must match eye level (below eye level the hem curves down at the sides).

## Verify
- [ ] Hang points and contact points consistent with the garment type.
- [ ] Ease visible and consistent between front and back views.
- [ ] Fold character matches the fabric.
