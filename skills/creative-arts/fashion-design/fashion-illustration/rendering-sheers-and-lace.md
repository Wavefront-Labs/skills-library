# Rendering sheers, chiffon and lace

**Goal:** make fabric look transparent and light, with lace that reads as lace, in marker,
gouache or digital.

## Sheers (chiffon, georgette, organza, tulle)
1. Render the body/garment underneath first (skin, lining, under-layer).
2. Apply a light, transparent wash of the sheer colour over it (marker: a light tone in one
   pass; digital: layer at 20-50% opacity, Multiply).
3. **Where fabric overlaps or folds, it gets darker** (two or three layers): add tone at
   gathers, folds, seams, hems.
4. Edges and hems: crisp line for organza (stiff), soft broken line for chiffon.
5. Leave highlights on organza (crisp sheen); chiffon is matte.

## Lace
1. Render the underlying layer (skin or lining).
2. Lightly indicate the lace's **main motifs** (flowers, scallops) in a fine line, mostly
   near edges and in focal areas.
3. Suggest the net ground with a light tone or a fine cross-hatch; don't draw it all.
4. Scalloped edges: draw the edge scallops clearly; they sell the read.
5. Digital: use a lace texture brush or a clipped scanned lace image with Multiply.

## Gotchas
- Opaque colour over skin kills transparency.
- Drawing every lace motif makes it heavy; edit to focus points.
- Black sheers: skin shows through as warm grey; don't render it pure black.

## Verify
- [ ] Underneath layer visible through the sheer.
- [ ] Overlaps darker than single layers.
- [ ] Lace suggested with motifs and edge, not over-drawn.
