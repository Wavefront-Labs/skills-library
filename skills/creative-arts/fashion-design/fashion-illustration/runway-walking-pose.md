# Runway walking pose

**Goal:** a walking figure that conveys movement and shows how garments swing, without
distorting their design.

## Mechanics of the catwalk walk
- Models walk with feet crossing toward a single line, giving pronounced hip swing.
- At the drawn moment: the **weight-bearing (front) leg** is straight under the balance line;
  the **back leg** is lifted from the knee, the foot often shown with heel up and
  foreshortened (shorter, wider shape) because it is angled away.
- Hip on the weight side is higher; shoulders counter-tilt mildly.
- Arms swing opposite to legs (the arm on the side of the forward leg swings back),
  relaxed.

## Drawing steps
1. Balance line; place the standing foot on it.
2. Hip line tilted up on the standing side; shoulders mildly opposite.
3. Standing leg straight; free leg: thigh slightly forward/back, knee bent, lower leg
   shortened, foot small and angled.
4. Garments: skirts swing away from the moving leg; trouser legs show a fold at the knee
   of the bent leg; coats open slightly at the hem.

## Using templates
Trace a walking croquis from runway photos (front-facing, full length) to set
proportions; keep a library of 3-4 walks for line-ups.

## Gotchas
- Drawing both feet flat makes the figure look static or skating.
- An over-foreshortened leg looks amputated; keep the knee visible.
- Strong swing hides side seams and pockets; use for presentation, not tech flats.

## Verify
- [ ] Standing foot on the balance line; free leg foreshortened.
- [ ] Garment drape responds to the stride.
- [ ] Opposite arm-leg swing is correct.
