# Skin tones and diverse figures

**Goal:** illustrate people of all skin tones and body types accurately and attractively,
reflecting real customers.

## Skin tone rendering
- Each skin tone has **value** (light to deep) and **undertone** (warm/yellow-red,
  cool/pink-blue, neutral, olive).
- Build each tone with 3 values: base, shadow, deep shadow, plus highlight.
- **Light skin**: shadows with warm pinks/peach, not grey.
- **Deep skin**: rich browns with red, orange or blue-violet undertones; shadows deeper and
  slightly cooler; highlights with a cool sheen (blue-violet or warm gold) rather than
  white, which looks chalky.
- Markers: build swatch charts per tone (Copic E, R, YR and BV families).
- Digital: base colour + Multiply shadow + Overlay highlight; sample from real photos
  rather than inventing.

## Diverse croquis
- Make templates beyond the default thin figure: different heights, plus sizes, petite,
  curvy, men's, non-binary presentations, older figures, disabled bodies (wheelchair
  users, prosthetics).
- Draw garments on them honestly: proportion and drape change with body shape; they
  also test whether designs work across your size range.
- Base plus-size proportions on real references, not a scaled-up thin figure.

## Gotchas
- Using one mid-brown for all deep skin flattens it.
- Pure grey shadows on any skin look dead.
- Tokenism: diversity should be in the line-up and templates by default.

## Verify
- [ ] Swatch chart covers light to deep tones with undertones.
- [ ] Line-up includes varied bodies and skin tones.
- [ ] Shadows/highlights coloured, not grey/white.
