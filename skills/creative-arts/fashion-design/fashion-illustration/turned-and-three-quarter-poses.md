# Turned and three-quarter poses

**Why:** side seams, pocket depth, back interest and silhouette volume only read on a turned figure.

## Construct
1. Start from the balance line (pit of neck to ground) as usual.
2. Draw the **centre front line** off-centre, shifted toward the far side of the torso
   (about two-thirds of the way across at a 3/4 turn). It follows the body's surface:
   over the bust, into the waist, over the belly.
3. The **far side** of the body is compressed: far shoulder, far bust and far hip are
   narrower and partly overlapped by the near contour.
4. Treat the body as stacked cylinders: shoulder, waist and hip guides become ellipses
   that follow the turn.
5. Near arm and leg overlap the torso slightly; far limbs narrow in width, not length.

## Dressing the turned figure
- Garment CF, button stand and zips follow the curved CF line.
- Necklines and hems are ellipses; the far half is shallower.
- The side seam becomes visible near the near edge; pockets on the far side shrink.
- Back views: show CB seam, zip, back yoke, vents; head often turned in profile.

## Gotchas
- A front-view torso with turned feet reads as a twisted figure.
- Keeping CF dead centre on a turned body is the classic error; buttons look sewn on the side.
- Symmetric necklines on a turned figure flatten the form.

## Verify
- [ ] CF line curved and shifted toward the far side.
- [ ] Horizontal guides drawn as consistent ellipses.
- [ ] Garment details follow CF; far side compressed.
