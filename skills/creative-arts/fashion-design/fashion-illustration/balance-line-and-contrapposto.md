# Balance line, weight-bearing leg and contrapposto

**Goal:** poses that stand convincingly and show the garment with natural movement.

## The balance line
- A vertical plumb line from the **pit of the neck** (front) straight down to the floor.
- In a standing pose with weight on one leg, the **ankle of the weight-bearing leg sits on
  or very near this line**. If it doesn't, the figure looks like it's falling.
- With weight on both legs, the line falls between the feet.

## Contrapposto (shoulder-hip opposition)
- The hip over the weight-bearing leg **rises**; the shoulders tilt the **opposite** way
  (that shoulder drops).
- Draw the shoulder line and hip line first as two angled lines converging on the
  non-weight side.
- The torso centre front line curves from neck to crotch; the waist compresses on the
  high-hip side, stretches on the other.
- The weight-bearing leg angles from the high hip to the balance line; the free leg is
  relaxed, can step out, bend or cross.

## Steps
1. Balance line.
2. Shoulder and hip angles (start with 10-15 degrees; more is dramatic).
3. Torso shapes following the angles.
4. Weight-bearing leg to the balance line, then free leg.
5. Arms last; keep them away from key garment areas (pockets, waist details).

## Gotchas
- Too much tilt distorts garment proportions; for design sketches keep poses modest.
- Hiding garment details behind arms or crossed legs defeats the purpose.
- Flip the drawing to catch lean errors.

## Verify
- [ ] Weight-bearing ankle on the balance line.
- [ ] Shoulder and hip lines tilt opposite each other.
- [ ] Key garment details visible.
