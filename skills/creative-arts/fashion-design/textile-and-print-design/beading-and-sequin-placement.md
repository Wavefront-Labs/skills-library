# Beading and sequin placement

**Goal:** embellishment that looks intended, sits correctly on the body, sews into seams and
is costed before sampling.

## Materials
- Seed beads (sizes 15/0 tiny to 6/0 larger), bugle beads (tubes), crystals (sew-on, hotfix),
  pearls, sequins (flat, cup; 3-10 mm common), paillettes (large sequins with an off-centre hole).
- Methods: hand needle, **tambour (Lunéville) hook** from the back on a frame (fast for sequins
  and bead strings), multi-head machine sequin attachments for production.

## Design and placement
1. Work on the graded pattern pieces at 1:1 or on the base-size marker.
2. Draw the design on each piece; mark density zones (solid, gradient, scattered).
3. Keep embellishment out of seam allowances plus ~5 mm, so the garment can be sewn; fill the
   gap by hand after assembly if the motif must cross the seam.
4. Engineer motifs to flow across seams; avoid beads in areas of high friction (underarm, inner
   thigh, seat).

## Weight and fabric
- Dense beading can add 300-1000 g+ to a dress; stabilise with organza or tulle backing and
  check the garment hangs.
- Light chiffons need a fine net backing.

## Costing
- Quote per piece from the embroidery house: count by hours or bead counts; sampling is slow
  (often 2-4 weeks).

## Gotchas
- Sequins catch on themselves when the fabric folds; orient the overlap downward.
- Beads under seams break needles and press marks.
- Dry-clean only; label accordingly; many sequins melt at iron temperature.

## Verify
- [ ] Seam clearance marked on artwork.
- [ ] Weight checked on the form.
- [ ] Cost and lead time confirmed before range sign-off.
