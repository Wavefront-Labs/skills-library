# Placement and engineered print design

**Goal:** prints designed for a position on the garment (placement) or to the shape of the
pattern pieces (engineered), not as a continuous repeat.

## Placement prints
- A single motif or graphic placed on a defined area: chest, back, hem, sleeve.
- Spec: artwork size (cm), distance from a fixed point (e.g. "8 cm below HPS, centred on CF"),
  print method (screen, DTG, DTF, heat transfer), ink colours.
- Usually printed on cut panels (cut-and-print) or on finished garments.

## Engineered prints
1. Get the final graded pattern pieces (DXF or PDF at 1:1) for the base size.
2. Lay out the pieces in the design software (Illustrator, Photoshop, or a CAD such as
   Kaledo/NedGraphics) as a marker.
3. Draw the print onto the pieces so motifs flow across seams: match at CF, side seams,
   sleeve to body. Leave seam allowance areas with continuous colour (bleed).
4. Grade: decide whether the print scales with size or stays fixed and centres; each size
   needs its own file if engineered to edges.
5. Print the whole marker digitally (print-then-cut), or cut pieces and print.

## Production notes
- Print-and-cut needs accurate fabric shrinkage allowance; pre-test and scale the file.
- Digital printing suits engineered work (no screens); screens for very high volume.
- Mark notches and grain in the artwork as non-printing guides for the cutters.

## Gotchas
- Pieces rotated or mirrored in the marker produce motifs upside down.
- Missing bleed shows white at seams after sewing.
- Fabric shrinks after printing and steaming; engineered matches drift.

## Verify
- [ ] Prototype sewn and seams checked for match.
- [ ] One file per size where required.
- [ ] Position and size spec in the tech pack.
