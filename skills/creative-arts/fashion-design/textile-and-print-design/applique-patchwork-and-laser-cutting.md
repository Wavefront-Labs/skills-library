# Appliqué, patchwork and laser cutting

**Goal:** surface designs built from shapes of fabric: applied, pieced or cut away.

## Appliqué
- Motif cut from one fabric and attached to another. Edge options: satin stitch (machine zigzag,
  dense), blanket stitch, raw edge (frays on purpose), turned edge (hand, couture).
- Fuse with double-sided web first to stop shifting.
- Reverse appliqué: layers stacked, top layer cut away to reveal below (e.g. Alabama Chanin style in jersey).
- Production: embroidery machines can place and tack appliqué fabric then satin-stitch; give the
  embroidery house a vector with placement and tack lines.

## Patchwork
- Pieces sewn together into a new fabric before cutting the garment. Match fabric weights and
  shrinkage; pre-wash all pieces.
- Design the patch layout on the garment pattern so seams fall in useful places.

## Laser cutting
- Laser vaporises and seals edges on synthetics (polyester, nylon, neoprene, leather) so they do
  not fray. Natural fibres (cotton) char and still fray; test.
- Files: vector (DXF/AI), closed paths, minimum feature size ~1-2 mm, bridges to hold islands.
- Settings: power, speed and frequency per material; brown edge discolouration on light fabrics.

## Gotchas
- Applying a stiff appliqué to a stretchy base kills stretch and puckers.
- PVC and some coated fabrics release chlorine gas when lasered; never laser PVC.
- Leather laser edges smell; air out samples.

## Verify
- [ ] Test sample washed or cleaned per care.
- [ ] Vector files closed and scaled 1:1.
- [ ] Material is laser-safe.
