# Print categories and motif families

**Goal:** speak the language studios, agents and buyers use, so briefs, trend boards and
print libraries are organised the same way.

## The standard categories
| Category | Includes | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Floral** | botanical, ditsy, tropical, painterly, chintz, toile de Jouy | Biggest category in womenswear; scale and density drive mood |
| **Geometric** | stripes, checks, dots, op-art, tiles, chevrons | Often yarn-dyed in wovens; printed versions for knits |
| **Conversational (novelty)** | recognisable objects: fruit, animals, cars, food, logos | Strong in kidswear and resort; can date fast |
| **Abstract** | brushstrokes, textures, marbling, gradients | Easy to recolour; good for engineered placements |
| **Animal / skin** | leopard, zebra, snake, tiger | Perennial; vary scale and colour to refresh |
| **Ethnographic / heritage** | paisley, ikat, batik, suzani, block-print styles | Credit and research the origin; avoid sacred motifs and appropriation |
| **Camouflage, tie-dye, washes** | effect prints | Often placed with "texture" in libraries |

## Using categories
- Trend boards: forecast agencies group by category, then by mood (e.g. "faded florals").
- Range planning: balance categories across the collection (a common split is 1-2 hero
  prints plus supporting smaller-scale coordinates and a geometric).
- Studio buying (Premiere Vision, Indigo, Printsource): designs are filed by category and sold
  with buyout rights; record the studio, design number and rights.

## Gotchas
- Conversationals and licensed motifs carry IP risk; check trademarks.
- Heritage motifs copied from a community's craft without credit damages brands.
- A category mix with all large-scale prints reads chaotic; mix scales.

## Verify
- [ ] Every print in the range tagged with category, scale and colourway.
- [ ] Rights and origin recorded for bought or researched designs.
