# Synthetics (polyester, nylon, elastane)

**Goal:** choose synthetic fibres for their performance and know the trade-offs.

## Fibres
| Fibre | Strengths | Weaknesses | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Polyester (PET) | Strong, crease resistant, quick drying, heat-settable (permanent pleats), cheap | Low absorbency (clammy), holds odour, pills (strong fibres keep pills attached), static | Linings, sportswear, blends, pleated garments |
| Nylon (polyamide 6, 6.6) | Very strong, abrasion resistant, elastic, light | Yellows in light, low absorbency | Hosiery, swim, outerwear shells, activewear |
| Elastane (spandex, Lycra brand) | Stretch up to ~500-600% and recovery | Degraded by chlorine and heat; never used alone | 2-40% in stretch fabrics |
| Acrylic | Wool-like, light, cheap | Pills badly, static, low durability | Budget knits |
| Polypropylene | Very light, wicks | Low melting point | Base layers |

## Design notes
- **Heat-setting**: pleats and crinkle in polyester are permanent; natural fibres aren't.
- **Elastane %**: 2-5% for comfort stretch in wovens and jersey; 15-25% for swim and
  shapewear. Elastane complicates recycling and dyeing.
- **Blends**: polyester-cotton (easy care), wool-polyamide (durable suiting), viscose-
  elastane (stretch drape).
- **Recycled**: rPET (mostly from bottles), recycled nylon (e.g. ECONYL from waste).
  Same performance; certify via GRS.
- **Microfibre shedding**: synthetics shed in washing; tightly constructed yarns and finishes
  reduce it.

## Gotchas
- Ironing too hot glazes or melts synthetics; care labels must reflect it.
- Polyester dyes need disperse dyes at high temperature; a poly-cotton blend dyes in two
  steps (cross-dyeing effects possible).

## Verify
- [ ] Fibre choice tied to required performance.
- [ ] Elastane percentage specified for the stretch needed.
- [ ] Recycled content certified if claimed.
