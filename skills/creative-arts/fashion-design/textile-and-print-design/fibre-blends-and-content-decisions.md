# Fibre blends and content decisions

**Goal:** pick fibre content and ratio for performance, hand, cost and end-of-life, and
label it correctly.

## What blending does
| Blend | Typical ratio | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Cotton/polyester | 65/35, 50/50, 60/40 | Strength, less shrink and wrinkle, lower cost; less breathable |
| Cotton/elastane | 97/3, 98/2 | Stretch denim, shirting; 2-3% gives comfort stretch |
| Wool/nylon | 80/20 (knit, socks), 90/10 | Abrasion resistance |
| Wool/polyester | 55/45 (suiting) | Crease recovery, cost |
| Viscose/elastane | 95/5 | Jersey drape plus recovery |
| Linen/cotton | 55/45 | Softer, cheaper linen look |
| Polyamide/elastane | 80/20 (swim, active) | Stretch, power |

## Decide by
- **Performance target:** abrasion, pilling, shrinkage, stretch, breathability.
- **Dyeing:** blends need two dye classes (e.g. reactive plus disperse for polycotton) or show
  heathered/cross-dye effects; this costs more and can give two-tone seams.
- **Hand and look:** small additions (5-15%) change hand noticeably (cashmere at 10% is mostly marketing).
- **Recycling:** mono-material (100% one fibre) is far easier to recycle; elastane hinders
  mechanical and chemical recycling.
- **Care:** the most sensitive fibre sets the care label.

## Labelling
- Content by weight in descending order, generic fibre names (FTC Textile Act in US, EU
  Regulation 1007/2011). Tolerances apply (EU 3% of total weight for declared fibres).

## Gotchas
- Fibres under 5% may be listed as "other fibre" in US labelling unless they have a function
  (elastane is named).
- Blends pill more when a strong synthetic holds the pills on the surface.

## Verify
- [ ] Ratio justified by a performance need.
- [ ] Dye route confirmed with the mill.
- [ ] Label wording checked against market rules.
