# Resist dyeing: shibori, tie-dye and batik

**Goal:** use resist techniques deliberately, knowing what each produces and how to keep
results consistent enough for a collection.

## Techniques
| Technique | Resist | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **Itajime** (clamp shibori) | fold into a stack, clamp between shaped boards | Repeating geometric blocks |
| **Arashi** | wrap on a pole, bind with thread, compress | Diagonal rain-like lines |
| **Kumo / kanoko** | bind small points | Spider-web or dot clusters |
| **Nui** | stitch, draw tight | Fine controlled lines and shapes |
| **Tie-dye** | twist, fold, rubber bands | Spirals, bullseyes, crumple |
| **Batik** | hot wax (canting/tjanting or tjap stamp) | Painterly lines, crackle where wax breaks |

## Dyes by fibre
- Cellulose (cotton, linen, viscose): fibre-reactive (e.g. Procion MX cold water, soda ash fix),
  or indigo vat.
- Protein (silk, wool): acid dyes with heat; or indigo.
- Batik needs cold-process dyes (wax melts in hot dye baths).

## Designing for a collection
- Sample on the final fabric, record fold size, binding spacing, dye concentration, time.
- Accept variation as part of the product but set limits for QC (e.g. a reference swatch range).
- Industrial alternatives: printed resist effects (digital) for scale; but label honestly.
- Garment-dye resist (dye after sewing) gives unique pieces; thread must be dyeable or contrast.

## Gotchas
- Wax residue left in batik stains; boil out or iron between paper fully.
- Indigo crocks; rinse and test rub fastness.
- Elastane and polyester threads resist dye and show.

- [ ] Recipe recorded per sample.
- [ ] Wash and rub fastness tested.
