# Fibre, yarn, piece and garment dyeing

**Goal:** pick the dyeing stage that gives the look, lead time and flexibility the
collection needs.

## Stages
| Stage | Look | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fibre/stock dyed | Heathers, melanges (mixed fibres) | Deep, even, colourfast | Early commitment, high minimums |
| Yarn dyed | Stripes, checks, jacquards, chambray | Woven-in patterns, rich colour | Long lead time, colour decided early |
| Piece dyed | Solid fabric dyed after weaving | Most common; flexible colour late | Solids only; shade variations between lots |
| Garment dyed | Finished garments dyed | Very late colour decisions, soft worn look, small runs | Shrinkage, seams pucker, uneven dye at seams, thread and trims must survive |

## Garment dyeing specifics
- Make garments in PFD (prepared for dyeing) fabric with **pre-tested shrinkage**; patterns
  oversized to compensate (shrinkage often 3-8% or more).
- **Thread**: use 100% cotton thread to take the dye (poly thread stays white - sometimes a
  design feature), or poly thread dyed to colour.
- Trims: buttons and zips must withstand the dye bath (no metal that corrodes; dyeable
  nylon or matching colour).
- Labels: dye-resistant or attached after dyeing.
- Expect variation; communicate "each piece unique".

## Choosing
- Want late colour decisions or small runs -> garment dye.
- Want stripes/checks -> yarn dye.
- Want large volumes of solids at good price -> piece dye.

## Gotchas
- Mixed-fibre garments (cotton body, poly trim) dye unevenly.
- Garment-dyed colour approvals need dyed garment samples, not lab dips alone.

## Verify
- [ ] Dye stage chosen with lead time and minimums confirmed.
- [ ] For garment dye: shrinkage-tested patterns, dyeable thread/trims.
- [ ] Shade approval on the actual dyed product.
