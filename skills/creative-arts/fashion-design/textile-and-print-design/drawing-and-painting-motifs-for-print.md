# Drawing and painting motifs for print

**Goal:** original motifs with a hand quality, captured digitally at a resolution and
cleanliness that survives repeat and printing.

## Generate
- Start from research: observational drawing of plants, objects, places (draw the same
  subject 10 ways: line, silhouette, wash, stippling, collage, monoprint).
- Media: gouache (flat opaque colour, separates cleanly), ink and brush (line work),
  watercolour (soft, harder to separate), oil pastel/crayon (texture), lino or rubber
  stamp (graphic texture).
- Paint at **1:1 or larger** than the final print size; reductions sharpen, enlargements blur.

## Capture
- Scan at **300-600 dpi** at intended print size (higher if you'll enlarge). Flatbed beats
  phone photos (even light, no distortion).
- Save masters as 16-bit TIFF or PSD.

## Clean
- Levels/Curves to clean the background; remove dust (Spot Healing).
- Separate each motif to its own layer (Select > Color Range, or Magic Wand + refine edge).
- For limited-colour screen prints, plan motif colours as flat areas from the start
  (gouache in a limited palette makes separation easy).

## Build a motif library
Name and store motifs by type (`floral_poppy_01.psd`), with the scale painted, so they can
be recombined into repeats and colourways.

## Gotchas
- Painting too small: details pixelate when scaled up.
- Watercolour gradients need digital printing; they can't be screen separated cheaply.
- Over-cleaning loses the hand quality you painted for.

## Verify
- [ ] Scans at 300+ dpi at final print size.
- [ ] Each motif on its own layer with clean edges.
- [ ] Colours planned for the intended printing method.
