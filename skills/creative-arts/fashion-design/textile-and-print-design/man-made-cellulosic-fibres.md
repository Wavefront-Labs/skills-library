# Man-made cellulosics (viscose, lyocell, modal)

**Goal:** use regenerated cellulose fibres for their drape and hand, knowing their
weaknesses and sourcing issues.

## Overview
| Fibre | Process | Properties | Design notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Viscose (rayon) | Wood pulp dissolved with carbon disulphide, extruded | Fluid drape, silky hand, absorbent, dyes brightly | **Weak when wet**; shrinks and distorts if washed hot; creases |
| Modal | Modified viscose, higher wet strength | Very soft, good with cotton in jersey | Better wash stability than viscose |
| Lyocell (Tencel brand) | Pulp dissolved in NMMO solvent, closed loop (solvent recovery ~99%) | Strong wet and dry, soft, drapey | Can **fibrillate** (peach-skin surface) - used deliberately or controlled with finishing |
| Cupro | Cotton linter, cuprammonium process | Silk-like, breathable | Classic lining fabric |
| Acetate / triacetate | Cellulose acetate | Lustrous, drapey | Low strength, heat-sensitive (melts with iron too hot), linings and eveningwear |

## Sourcing
- Pulp origin matters: ancient and endangered forests. Use producers ranked well by
  Canopy's Hot Button report; ask for FSC or PEFC certified feedstock.
- Branded fibres (Lenzing Tencel, Ecovero, Birla) give traceability.

## Design implications
- Great for fluid dresses, shirts, linings, jersey.
- Pre-shrink and test washing; viscose often needs "hand wash cold / dry flat" or dry clean.
- Bias cuts in viscose crepe drape beautifully but drop a lot.

## Gotchas
- Marketing "bamboo" is usually viscose from bamboo; must be labelled viscose (FTC, EU).
- Viscose seams can slip in loose weaves; check seam slippage tests.

## Verify
- [ ] Fibre and producer known, with feedstock certification.
- [ ] Wash and shrinkage tested.
- [ ] Labelling uses legal generic fibre name.
