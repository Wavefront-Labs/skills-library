# Embroidery design direction (hand and machine)

**Goal:** embroidery artwork and specs that a hand atelier or machine embroiderer can
produce at the right quality and cost.

## Hand vs machine
- **Hand embroidery** (ateliers in India, France - e.g. Lesage tradition): any stitch,
  threads, beads, raised work; slow and costly; lead time weeks.
- **Machine embroidery**: multi-head machines (Tajima, Barudan, ZSK) from digitised files;
  repeatable and scalable.

## Machine stitch types
- **Satin stitch**: smooth columns for lettering and borders; max width about 7-10 mm before
  floats snag (switch to fill).
- **Fill (tatami)**: large areas, rows of running stitches.
- **Running stitch**: outlines, fine details.
- Specialities: chenille, cording, appliqué, 3D puff (foam under satin), sequin devices.

## Digitising
- Artwork (vector preferred) is converted by a **digitiser** into a stitch file (DST for
  most commercial machines; PES, EXP for others). Digitising is paid once per design.
- Specify: size in mm, thread colours (Madeira/Isacord codes), placement from a landmark
  (e.g. "8 cm below HPS, 6 cm from CF").

## Cost and limits
- Price mainly by **stitch count** (thousands of stitches) plus thread changes.
- Minimum detail: text under about 5 mm tall becomes unreadable.
- Dense embroidery on light fabrics puckers; specify **stabiliser/backing** (tear-away,
  cut-away for knits, wash-away).

## Gotchas
- Embroidering after construction vs on cut panels: panels are easier and cheaper.
- Metallic threads break and slow machines; costs rise.
- Large dense fills make fabric stiff; check hand on sample.

## Verify
- [ ] Artwork, size, placement, thread codes specified.
- [ ] Stitch count and cost quoted.
- [ ] Sew-out sample approved on the real fabric with backing.
