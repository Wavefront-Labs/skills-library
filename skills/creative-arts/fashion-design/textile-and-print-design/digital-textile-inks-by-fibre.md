# Digital textile inks by fibre

**Goal:** match ink chemistry to fibre so colour, hand and fastness meet the brief.

## Ink to fibre
| Ink | Fibres | Process | Character |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Reactive** | cotton, linen, viscose, modal, lyocell, silk | pretreat, print, steam, wash | Bright, soft hand, good wash fastness |
| **Acid** | silk, wool, nylon (polyamide) | pretreat, print, steam, wash | Very bright on silk/nylon; swimwear and silk scarves |
| **Disperse / sublimation** | polyester (and high-poly blends) | print on transfer paper then heat press ~190-210 C, or direct disperse with fixation | Vivid, no hand; dull on cotton; white shows in cracks on stretched knits |
| **Pigment** | almost any, blends | print, heat cure (no steaming/washing) | Simple, cheaper; slightly stiffer hand, lower rub fastness on darks |

## Choosing
- Blends: pick by majority fibre, or use pigment. Polycotton with sublimation gives a faded
  look because the cotton does not take the dye.
- Swimwear (nylon/elastane): acid; or polyester/elastane with sublimation.
- Small runs and sampling: pigment is the least equipment-heavy.

## Process notes
- Pretreatment (thickener, alkali for reactive) is fabric-specific; PFD (prepared for digital)
  fabrics come pre-treated.
- Washing off after steaming removes unfixed dye; skipping it causes bleeding.
- Rub (crocking) fastness on dark pigment prints needs testing (AATCC 8 / ISO 105-X12).

## Gotchas
- Sublimated stretch knits show "grin-through" white when stretched; print stretched or use dyed ground.
- Reactive on silk can dull compared to acid; test both.
- Pigment on dark grounds needs white underbase.

## Verify
- [ ] Ink class matches fibre content.
- [ ] Strike-off washed and rub-tested.
- [ ] Process (steam/heat) confirmed with the printer.
