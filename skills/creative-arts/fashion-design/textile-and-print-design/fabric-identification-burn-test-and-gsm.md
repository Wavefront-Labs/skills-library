# Identifying fabrics: burn test, thread count, GSM

**Goal:** quickly identify unknown fabrics (deadstock, vintage, unlabelled swatches) well
enough to design with them, and know when lab testing is required.

## Burn test (do it safely: tweezers, over a metal tray, ventilated, water nearby)
Take a few yarns from warp and weft separately (blends may differ by direction).
| Fibre family | Near flame | Smell | Residue |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cellulose (cotton, linen, viscose) | Burns fast, afterglow | Burning paper | Soft grey ash |
| Protein (wool, silk) | Curls away, self-extinguishes | Burning hair | Crushable black bead |
| Polyester | Melts, shrinks away, black smoke | Sweet chemical | Hard black bead |
| Nylon | Melts, drips | Celery-like | Hard grey/tan bead |
| Acrylic | Burns, melts, flares | Acrid | Hard irregular bead |
| Acetate | Melts, burns | Vinegar | Hard black bead |
Blends give mixed results; burn tests can't give percentages.

## Weight (GSM)
- Use a **GSM circle cutter** (100 cm2 disc) and a scale accurate to 0.01 g:
  g/m2 = disc weight (g) x 100.
- Or cut 10 x 10 cm exactly. Test conditioned fabric (ISO 3801 / ASTM D3776).

## Thread count / ends and picks
Count warp ends and weft picks per cm (or inch) with a pick glass; e.g. 40 ends x 30 picks
per cm. Higher counts with finer yarns = smoother, denser fabric.

## When to use a lab
Any fibre content claim on a label must be accurate: quantitative analysis (ISO 1833 series,
AATCC 20A) by an accredited lab.

## Gotchas
- Finishes (flame retardant, resin) change burn behaviour.
- Fabric weight from a tiny swatch is imprecise; cut a proper disc.

## Verify
- [ ] Warp and weft burned separately.
- [ ] GSM measured with a calibrated cutter and scale.
- [ ] Lab test ordered before labelling content.
