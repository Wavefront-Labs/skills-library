# Building colourways from a palette

**Goal:** 2-4 versions of a print in different colour combinations, each balanced and
sellable, all drawn from the season palette.

## Principles
- **Keep the value structure**: the relative lightness of ground and each motif colour.
  A print designed with a dark ground and light motifs should keep that contrast in each
  colourway, or deliberately invert it.
- Each colourway needs a role: a neutral/core version (commercial), a seasonal colour
  version, an accent/statement version.
- The ground colour dominates perception; choose it first.

## Workflow
1. Separate artwork per colour (one layer or spot colour per screen).
2. Make a colourway grid: rows = colourways, columns = screens (ground, motif 1, motif 2...).
   Fill with palette codes.
3. Recolour: Photoshop (Hue/Saturation or fill each separation layer), Illustrator
   (Edit > Edit Colors > **Recolor Artwork** with palette swatch group).
4. Print at actual size on similar paper or digital-print a sample on fabric.
5. Review side by side under D65.

## Documentation
Colourway sheet: print name, colourway name/number, screens with Pantone TCX codes,
ground fabric, approved strike-off reference.

## Gotchas
- Recolouring with equal-value colours flattens the print (motifs disappear).
- Too many colourways multiply strike-off costs and minimums.
- Colours on white paper look different on coloured or textured grounds.

## Verify
- [ ] Each colourway preserves the intended value contrast.
- [ ] All colours come from the palette with codes.
- [ ] Colourway sheet documents screens and codes.
