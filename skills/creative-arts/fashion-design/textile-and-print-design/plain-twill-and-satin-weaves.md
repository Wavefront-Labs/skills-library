# Plain, twill and satin weaves

**Goal:** read and specify woven structures, because the weave determines hand, drape,
durability and appearance as much as the fibre.

## Plain weave
- Each weft goes over one, under one warp. Maximum interlacing = firm, flat, durable,
  creases more.
- Variants: **rib** (thicker yarns one way: poplin, faille, grosgrain), **basket** (pairs of
  yarns: oxford, hopsack).
- Fabrics: poplin, voile, lawn, chiffon (plain weave of high-twist yarns), organza,
  taffeta, canvas, muslin, chambray.

## Twill weave
- Weft floats over two or more warps, stepping one each row, creating **diagonal lines**
  (wales). Notation like 2/1 or 2/2 (over/under).
- Drapes better and hides soil better than plain; very durable.
- Right-hand twill (lines rise to the right, most denim) vs left-hand (softer denim).
- Variants: herringbone (reversing twill), houndstooth (twill + colour), gabardine (steep
  twill), serge, drill, chino, denim.

## Satin weave
- Long floats (over 4 or more) with interlacings spread apart: smooth, lustrous face.
- **Satin** = warp-faced (warp floats), **sateen** = weft-faced (usually cotton).
- Drapes and shines; floats snag and abrade more.
- Fabrics: satin, duchess satin, charmeuse, cotton sateen.

## Reading a fabric
Use a pick glass (linen tester): count interlacings, look for diagonals, check face vs back.

## Gotchas
- "Satin" is a weave, not a fibre (polyester satin, silk satin).
- Twill direction affects how a garment drapes and twists (leg twist in jeans).
- Loose satins slip at seams; check seam slippage.

- [ ] Weave identified with a pick glass.
- [ ] Spec names weave and fibre separately.
- [ ] Durability needs matched to weave choice.
