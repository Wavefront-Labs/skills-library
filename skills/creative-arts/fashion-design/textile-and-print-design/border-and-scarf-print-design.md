# Border and scarf print design

**Goal:** prints composed to edges: a border that runs along the fabric or hem, and square
or oblong scarves composed as a whole.

## Border prints (yardage)
- The border runs parallel to one selvedge; the repeat is horizontal along the length.
- Typical layouts: one-sided border (skirts, dresses cut with hem on the selvedge) or
  double border (both selvedges).
- Design the border depth to the garment: e.g. 30-40 cm for a midi skirt hem.
- Ground above the border is usually a small coordinate or plain to save cost.
- Garments must be cut cross-grain (hem on selvedge); check the fabric drapes acceptably that way.

## Scarves
- Common square sizes: 90 x 90 cm (classic silk carré), 70 x 70, 55 x 55 (bandana/neckerchief),
  140 x 140 (shawl); oblong 180 x 70.
- Composition: border, corner motifs, centre medallion or field; design to be read both when
  laid flat and when folded/knotted.
- Corners: draw the border on one side, then mitre or design a dedicated corner motif.
- Hem: allow 0.5-1 cm for a hand-rolled hem; keep key detail inside that margin.

## Colour and method
- Silk carré screen printing can use 10-30+ screens; digital printing removes the limit but
  colour on silk twill must be tested.
- Print the "wrong side" view if the fabric shows through (light twill, chiffon).

## Gotchas
- Border prints waste fabric on garments not cut to the border.
- Corners that do not meet when the repeat is tiled show a jog.
- Text on scarves reads backwards on half the knot; avoid directional text.

## Verify
- [ ] Full-size proof printed and folded/tied.
- [ ] Hem margin clear of key detail.
- [ ] Repeat and border depth fit the garment pattern.
