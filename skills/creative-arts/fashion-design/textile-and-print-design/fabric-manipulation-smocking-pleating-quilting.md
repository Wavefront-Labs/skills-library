# Fabric manipulation: smocking, pleating, quilting

**Goal:** turn flat cloth into textured or shaped surfaces, knowing how much fabric each
technique consumes.

## Smocking and shirring
- Hand smocking (English/honeycomb): fabric gathered on pleating threads then embroidered;
  consumes ~3:1 fabric to finished width.
- Machine shirring with elastic thread in the bobbin: ~1.5-2:1.
- Lightweight cottons, lawn, voile and viscose smock best.

## Permanent pleating
- Polyester and high-poly blends take permanent heat-set pleats; natural fibres pleat but
  relax with washing.
- Pleating houses use paper moulds: knife, box, accordion, sunray (fan), crystal (micro).
- Supply fabric hemmed or with selvedge placement; sunray skirts are pleated from a circle or
  half-circle piece; ask the pleater for fabric consumption (often 3:1).
- Issey Miyake-style garment pleating: sew oversized garment, then pleat whole (pattern 2.5-3x).

## Quilting
- Layers: face fabric, wadding/batting (polyester, wool, down), backing. Loft and weight set warmth.
- Patterns: channel, diamond, onion, freeform; stitch spacing 3-10 cm; down needs closer
  stitching or baffles to stop migration.
- Quilting shrinks the panels 3-10%; quilt oversized blocks then cut pieces.

## Pintucks
- Narrow stitched folds (1-3 mm); each tuck uses 2x its width.

## Gotchas
- Not calculating consumption makes costing wrong by 2-3x.
- Pleats on polyester can't be pressed out; mistakes are permanent.
- Quilted garments get much stiffer; check movement.

## Verify
- [ ] Sample of manipulated fabric approved.
- [ ] Consumption ratio in the cost sheet.
- [ ] Shrinkage after quilting/pleating built into the pattern.
