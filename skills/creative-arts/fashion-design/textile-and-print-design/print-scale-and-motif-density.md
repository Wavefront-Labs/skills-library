# Motif scale and density for garments

**Goal:** a print that reads as intended on a body, at the right size for the garment
category and pattern pieces.

## Scale bands (rough guide)
| Scale | Motif size | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| Ditsy/micro | under 2 cm | Shirts, blouses, childrenswear; reads as texture from afar |
| Small | 2-5 cm | Dresses, shirts |
| Medium | 5-15 cm | Dresses, skirts, scarves |
| Large | 15-40 cm | Statement dresses, outerwear, home |
| Oversize/placement | 40 cm+ | Engineered/placement prints |

## Density
- **Ground-to-motif ratio**: open (lots of ground, airy, 30% coverage), medium, packed
  (little ground, 80%+, busy). Packed prints hide seams better.
- Direction: one-way (motifs all upright) needs a one-way cutting layout (more fabric);
  two-way or tossed allows efficient markers.

## Test
1. Print the repeat at **actual size** on paper; wrap around a dress form or hold up to
   a person at 2-3 m.
2. Overlay on the flat pattern pieces at scale: large motifs get cut through at seams,
   darts and small pieces (collars, cuffs).
3. Test on a croquis, but remember croquis are scaled; use the paper test as truth.
4. Consider the wearer's size: a large print on a petite size can swallow the figure.

## Gotchas
- Judging scale on screen at random zoom.
- Large prints on garments with many seams look chopped.
- One-way prints add 10-20% fabric consumption.

## Verify
- [ ] Repeat tested at actual size on a body or form.
- [ ] Pattern pieces overlaid to check motif cuts.
- [ ] Direction (one-way/two-way) agreed with production for costing.
