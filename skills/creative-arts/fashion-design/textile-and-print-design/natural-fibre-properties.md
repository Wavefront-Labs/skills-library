# Cotton, linen, wool and silk for design

**Goal:** choose natural fibres knowing how they'll look, feel, wear and be cared for.

## Comparison
| Property | Cotton | Linen | Wool | Silk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hand | Soft, matte | Crisp, cool, slubby | Warm, springy | Smooth, lustrous |
| Moisture regain (approx.) | ~8% | ~12% | ~13-16% (absorbs much more without feeling wet) | ~11% |
| Wrinkling | Wrinkles | Wrinkles heavily | Resists, recovers | Moderate |
| Warmth | Cool | Very cool | Warm, insulates when damp | Light warmth |
| Strength wet | Stronger wet | Stronger wet | Weaker wet | Weaker wet |
| Shrinkage | Relaxation shrinkage | Some | Felting shrinkage (agitation + heat) | Low-moderate |
| Dye | Reactive/vat; good | Harder to dye evenly | Acid dyes; rich | Acid dyes; brilliant |
| Care issues | Shrink, fade | Creases | Felting, moths | Water spots, light damage |

## Quality indicators
- **Cotton**: staple length (long-staple: Pima, Egyptian Giza, Sea Island = finer, smoother
  yarns); combed vs carded; ply.
- **Linen**: line (long) vs tow (short) fibres; long = smoother.
- **Wool**: micron (fibre diameter): superfine merino under ~18.5 micron (soft, next-to-skin);
  over ~25 micron prickles; Super 100s-150s labels in suiting relate to fineness.
- **Silk**: momme weight (mm) for fabric weight (e.g. 16-22 mm charmeuse); mulberry vs wild/tussah.

## Design implications
- Linen and cotton crease: relaxed silhouettes or crease-resist finishes.
- Wool for tailoring (mouldable with steam), knits, outerwear.
- Silk for drape and lustre; check sweat and water marks for daywear.

## Gotchas
- Superwash wool (chlorine-Hercosett or plasma treated) can be machine washed; untreated felts.
- 100% linen trousers bag at knees; blends with cotton or viscose change hand and care.
