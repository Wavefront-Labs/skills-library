# Pilling and abrasion testing

**Goal:** know a fabric will survive wear before committing to bulk, using standard tests and
sensible targets.

## Pilling
- **ISO 12945-2 / ASTM D4970** Martindale pilling: fabric rubbed in a figure-8 under light load;
  assessed at set rub counts (e.g. 125, 500, 1000, 2000) against photographic standards.
- **ISO 12945-1** ICI pilling box: specimens on tubes tumbled in a cork-lined box (common for knits).
- Grades 1 (severe) to 5 (none). Common targets: 3-4 for knitwear, 3.5-4 for wovens; brands set
  their own.

## Abrasion
- **ISO 12947** Martindale abrasion: rubs to end point (thread breakage). Apparel targets often
  10,000-20,000 rubs; upholstery 20,000-50,000+.
- **ASTM D4157** Wyzenbeek (oscillatory cylinder), used in US upholstery: double rubs.

## Causes and fixes
- Pilling grows with short staple fibres, low twist, brushed surfaces, loose knits, and strong
  synthetic in blends (pills stay attached).
- Fixes: longer staple (combed/compact cotton, superfine merino with higher twist), tighter
  gauge, singeing, enzyme bio-polish, anti-pill finish.

## Gotchas
- Pilling grade depends on rub count; compare grades at the same count.
- Garment-washed products need testing after wash, not on greige.
- Lab results can vary; use accredited labs (SGS, Intertek, BV) and the same lab across seasons.

## Verify
- [ ] Test method and rub count stated in the fabric spec.
- [ ] Results meet brand targets.
- [ ] Re-test bulk fabric.
