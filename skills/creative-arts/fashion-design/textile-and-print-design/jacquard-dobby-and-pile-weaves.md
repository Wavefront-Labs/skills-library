# Jacquard, dobby and pile weaves

**Goal:** know which loom makes which structure, so woven designs are briefed in a form the
mill can produce.

## Dobby
- Shafts (typically up to 24-32) lift groups of warp ends; small geometric repeats: birdseye,
  piqué, small diamonds, stripes with texture.
- Repeat limited by shaft count across the warp. Good for shirting texture.

## Jacquard
- Each warp end controlled individually (hooks, now electronic); large figurative designs:
  damask, brocade, tapestry, matelassé, cloqué.
- Design in a jacquard CAD (e.g. EAT DesignScope, NedGraphics, ArahWeave): artwork mapped to
  weave structures per colour area.
- Colours come from warp and weft yarns; a two-weft jacquard on a single warp gives limited
  colours; more wefts = heavier cloth.
- **Float length**: long floats snag; mills limit floats (often 3-5 mm in apparel).

## Pile weaves
- Extra yarns form loops or cut tufts above the ground: **velvet** (warp pile, cut),
  **velveteen** and **corduroy** (weft pile, cut in wales), **terry** (uncut loops).
- Corduroy wale count: pinwale 16-21 wales/inch, standard ~11, jumbo 3-6.
- Pile has a direction ("nap"): cut all pieces in one direction; specify "with nap" on the
  marker (costs fabric).

## Briefing the mill
- Supply artwork at scale, colour references, target weight (gsm), width, content; ask for
  the mill's repeat width and colour limits.

## Gotchas
- Jacquard sampling has setup costs and long lead times; book early.
- Pile marks permanently if pressed flat; use a needle board.
- Reversed jacquards show the floats of the back face.

## Verify
- [ ] Structure matches loom type available.
- [ ] Float lengths within mill limits.
- [ ] Nap direction marked in the spec.
