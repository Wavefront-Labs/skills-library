# Groove, timekeeping and rudiments

**Goal:** be the drummer bands want: steady time, a groove that feels good, balanced
sound, and fills that never lose the "one".

## 1. Grip and rebound
- **Matched grip:** fulcrum between thumb and the first joint of the index (or middle)
  finger, about a third of the way up from the butt end. Other fingers wrap loosely.
- **Let the stick rebound.** Throw the stick at the head and let it come back up;
  don't bury it. Rebound is what makes speed and endurance possible.
- Strokes: full (starts high, returns high), down (starts high, stops low), tap
  (low to low), up (low, returns high). Accents are full/down strokes; ghost notes taps.

## 2. Rudiments that matter most
From the PAS 40 International Rudiments, start with:
- **Single stroke roll** (RLRL)
- **Double stroke roll** (RRLL) — second stroke from rebound/fingers, not a second wrist
  push
- **Single paradiddle** (RLRR LRLL) — basis of many grooves and fills
- **Flam** (grace note + primary stroke)
- **Drag/ruff**
Practise with a metronome at a tempo where every stroke is even, 2–5 min each; raise
5 bpm when clean. Use a practice pad and *Stick Control* patterns daily.

## 3. Building a groove
- Basic rock beat in 4/4: hi-hat eighths, kick on 1 and 3, snare on 2 and 4.
- **Balance:** the snare backbeat is the loudest; hi-hat sits underneath; kick matches
  the bass player. Beginners usually play the hi-hat too loud.
- **Ghost notes:** very quiet snare taps between backbeats add feel (funk, R&B). They
  should be barely audible — 1–2 cm stick height.
- **Consistency over complexity:** a simple groove played identically for four minutes
  is harder and more valuable than a busy one.
- Lock the kick with the bass guitar; listen to the bassist more than to yourself.

## 4. Timekeeping drills
- Play to a **click** at slow tempos (60–70 bpm) — slow time exposes rushing.
- **Click on 2 and 4 only**, then only on beat 1 of every bar, then every second bar:
  you supply more of the time internally.
- **Record yourself** and check against the grid in a DAW; look for a drift pattern
  (most drummers rush fills and drag after them).
- Count out loud while playing new patterns.

## 5. Fills
- A fill's job is to **set up the next section** and land on beat 1 with a crash +
  kick.
- Start short (last two beats of a bar) using singles or paradiddles around the toms.
- Keep the fill at the same time-feel; don't speed up. Practise "3 bars groove + 1 bar
  fill" cycles.

## 6. Hand percussion note
Cajón, congas and shakers follow the same principle: rebound, relaxed hands, simple
consistent parts that lock to the bass and kick.

## Gotchas
- **Hearing protection** is mandatory — acoustic kits commonly exceed safe levels in a
  practice room; use musician's earplugs (flat-response) at every session.
- Throne too low or too far makes the kick foot heavy and slow: thighs roughly level
  or sloping slightly down.
- Uncertain: grip fulcrum point varies by teacher (index vs. middle finger).

## Verify
- [ ] Rudiments even at a logged metronome tempo.
- [ ] Recorded groove shows no steady drift ahead of or behind the grid, especially
      after fills (check in a DAW; small human variation is fine).
- [ ] Snare backbeat clearly louder than hi-hat in a recording.
- [ ] Fills land on beat 1 with crash and kick.
- [ ] Ear protection worn for every session.
