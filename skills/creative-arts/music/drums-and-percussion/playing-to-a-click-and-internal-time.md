# Playing to a click and internal time

**Goal:** steady time with a click in studio and live, and time that stays steady without one.

## 1. Click setup
- A clear, short sound that cuts through (cowbell, woodblock, rim click) at a level you can hear but not so loud it fatigues; in-ear monitors or isolating headphones.
- Choose subdivisions for the tempo: slow songs need 8ths or 16ths; fast songs quarter notes or half notes.
- Accent beat 1 for long songs to keep the bar position.

## 2. Playing with it
- "**Bury the click**": when you're dead on, the kit masks the click. If you hear it clearly, you're early or late.
- Placement is a choice: on top (driving), centre, or slightly behind (laid back) - but consistent.
- Lock fills to the click: fills are where time rushes most.

## 3. Internal time drills
1. **Gap click**: click plays 2 bars, mutes 2 bars (then 4/4, 4/8); you keep time through the gap and check when it returns. Apps and many metronomes support this.
2. Click on 2 and 4 only, then only on beat 1, then once every 2 bars.
3. Slow tempos (50-60 bpm) to expose rushing.
4. Record and check against the grid in a DAW.

## 4. Live with tracks
- Click and cues from the playback rig (see stable-live-set skill) to in-ears; count-ins before songs; you often start the tracks or watch for the MD's cue.
- Make sure the click is in your mix only, never in FOH.

## Gotchas
- Pushing in choruses/fills and dragging in quiet parts is natural; practise those transitions specifically.
- Fighting the click (chasing it): relax, listen, adjust gradually.

## Verify
- DAW recording: hits within a few milliseconds of the grid where intended; tempo stable through gap-click sections.
