# Orchestrating for woodwinds

**Goal:** woodwind lines that speak in the right register, blend, and are playable.

## 1. Registers that matter
- **Flute (C, sounds as written):** low octave (C4-C5) soft and breathy, easily covered;
  middle clear; top (above about C6) bright and penetrating. Piccolo sounds an octave
  higher than written.
- **Oboe (C):** reedy and focused; best roughly from F4 to about D6. Lowest notes (Bb3-
  C4) are hard to play softly.
- **Clarinet (Bb, sounds a major 2nd lower; A clarinet a minor 3rd lower):**
  chalumeau (low, dark), throat tones (G4-Bb4 written, weaker), clarion (bright),
  altissimo. Huge dynamic range; can start from nothing.
- **Bassoon (C, bass/tenor clef):** reedy bass to plaintive top; staccato is
  characteristic; low notes are loud-ish.

## 2. Voicing chords in the section
- **Juxtaposition:** pairs side by side (fl-fl, ob-ob, cl-cl) for even blocks.
- **Interlocking:** fl1, ob1, fl2, ob2 alternating - blends colours.
- **Enclosure:** one colour inside another (oboes inside flutes).
- Keep the bassoon or bass clarinet on the bass with open spacing at the bottom.

## 3. Doubling
- **Unison** flute + oboe or flute + clarinet: richer, more present melody.
- **Octave** doubling (flute above clarinet, piccolo above flute) projects the line.
- Woodwinds doubling strings add definition; a flute an octave above violins brightens.
- In pit and big-band contexts, **woodwind doubling** means one player switching
  instruments (sax/flute/clarinet). Leave changeover time (several bars) and write the
  instrument change in the part.

## Gotchas
- Low flute under a full texture disappears; move it up or thin the texture.
- Clarinet throat tones crossing the break (around Bb4 to B4 written) at speed is awkward
  in some keys.
- Long phrases without rests have no breath; give breath marks.

## Verify
- [ ] Each instrument sits in a register that projects at the marked dynamic.
- [ ] Transposed parts are correct (Bb/A clarinets, piccolo, cor anglais in F).
- [ ] Doublers have time to switch instruments.
