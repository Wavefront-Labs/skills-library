# Four-way close and block chords

**Goal:** a section voicing in which every melody note is supported by a full chord.

## 1. Four-way close
- Four voices within an octave, melody on top, each note voiced as a four-note chord
  (root, 3rd, 5th, 7th or 6th) directly below the melody.
- Example: melody E over C6: E-C-A-G (top down). Over Cmaj7 with melody B: B-G-E-C.
- Use **6th chords** for major tonic when the melody is the root (C over C6: C-A-G-E)
  to avoid a minor 2nd between root and 7th.

## 2. Tension substitution
Replace chord tones with available tensions: 9 for root, 13 for 5th, 11 on minor
chords. Keep at least the 3rd and 7th (guide tones) unless the melody has them.

## 3. Non-chord melody notes
Passing and approach notes in the melody get their own chord (see approach-tone
reharmonisation): diminished 7th, chromatic or parallel approach, or the dominant of the
target.

## 4. Block chords (piano, Shearing style)
Four-way close in the right hand plus the melody doubled an octave lower in the left
hand (five voices).

## 5. Spreads
- **Drop 2:** drop the second voice from the top by an octave - more open, good for
  saxes and guitar.
- **Drop 2 and 4**, **drop 3** for wider ranges.
- Lead below about F4 in close position becomes muddy; switch to spread voicings.

## Gotchas
- Melody on a chord tone that the voicing doubles (e.g. root in both top and inner
  voice) weakens the sound.
- Minor 9th between inner voices and melody is harsh except on dominant b9.
- Check ranges for each section part after voicing.

## Verify
- [ ] Every melody note has a four-note voicing beneath it.
- [ ] No unintended minor 9ths against the lead.
- [ ] Voicing type changes with the lead's register.
