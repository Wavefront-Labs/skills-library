# Reharmonising passing and approach tones

**Goal:** passing and approach notes in a voiced melody get their own chords, so the
inner voices move instead of sitting under a dissonance.

## 1. Find the approach notes
Mark melody notes that are not chord tones or tensions and that resolve by step to a
chord tone (usually short notes on weak beats).

## 2. Techniques (most to least common)
- **Diatonic approach:** voice the approach note with the diatonic chord built on it
  (scale-wise parallel motion).
- **Diminished approach:** a diminished 7th chord with the approach note on top; works
  for most approach notes into a dominant or tonic.
- **Chromatic (parallel) approach:** the target voicing moved a half step above or
  below; all voices slide by semitone. Strong in fast passages.
- **Dominant approach:** voice the approach note as part of V7 of the target chord.
- **Tritone substitute** approach: bII7 into the target chord.

## 3. Choosing
- Fast passing notes: chromatic parallel is smoothest.
- Longer approach notes on beats: dominant or diminished for colour.
- Keep the bass or rhythm section on the original harmony; reharmonisation lives in the
  horns.

## 4. Check voice leading
Read each inner part alone: it should be singable, without leaps of more than a 4th on
quick notes.

## Gotchas
- Reharmonising every note sounds busy; leave chord tones on beats alone.
- Approach chords clash if the rhythm section plays a conflicting chord at the same
  moment; tell the pianist or write it in the chart.

## Verify
- [ ] Every non-chord note in a voiced passage has an approach chord.
- [ ] Inner parts are smooth when played alone.
- [ ] Rhythm section chart agrees with the horn harmony.
