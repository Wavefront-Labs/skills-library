# Piano reductions and rehearsal scores

**Goal:** a two-stave piano part that conveys the essential music, playable at tempo by a
good amateur or rehearsal pianist.

## 1. Priorities (keep in this order)
1. **Bass line** (the true lowest sounding line).
2. **Melody / main line** (and important entries singers need to hear).
3. **Harmony** as a compact voicing.
4. Rhythmic identity of the accompaniment (a representative figure, not every note).
5. Cues: important counter-melodies in small notes, marked with instrument names.

## 2. Techniques
- **Reduce doublings**: octave doublings across the orchestra become one line (add the octave only at climaxes).
- **Compress register**: keep each hand within a 9th-10th reach; move inner voices to the hand that has room.
- **Sustained strings**: play the chord on the beat and hold with pedal or repeat at harmony changes; tremolo strings -> repeated chords or a written tremolo only if practical.
- **Fast figuration** (string semiquavers, harp arpeggios): simplify to the first note of each group or a smaller pattern.
- **Percussion**: omit or indicate (e.g. "timp. roll" text, small-note rhythm).
- Keep the harmonic rhythm accurate even if voicing changes.

## 3. Choral rehearsal score specifics
- For a cappella works, a reduction of the voice parts (S+A in treble, T+B in bass) for note-bashing; mark "for rehearsal only".
- For accompanied choral works, the vocal score shows voices above the piano reduction.

## 4. Readability
- Bar numbers on every system, rehearsal letters matching the full score.
- Instrument cues in text ("Hn", "Fl") at entries; tempo and dynamics copied from the score.
- Avoid ledger-line forests: use 8va and clef changes; keep page turns at rests.

## Gotchas
- Including every note makes it unplayable; playability beats completeness.
- Crossing hands constantly to catch inner lines: re-distribute instead.

## Verify
- Play it (or have a pianist sight-read it) at rehearsal tempo; mark any bar that can't be played and simplify.
- Play along with a recording of the full piece: harmony and bass should match.
