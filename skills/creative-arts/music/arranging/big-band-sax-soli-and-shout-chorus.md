# Big band arranging: sax soli and shout chorus

**Goal:** a chart with clear form, idiomatic section writing and a climax that lands.

## 1. The band
5 saxes (2 alto, 2 tenor, baritone), 4 trumpets, 4 trombones (4th often bass), rhythm
(piano, guitar, bass, drums). Lead alto, lead trumpet and lead trombone carry each
section.

## 2. Typical chart form
Intro - head (melody, often saxes or unison) - solos with backgrounds - **sax soli** -
**shout chorus** - head out - ending/coda.

## 3. Sax soli
- A written, improvised-sounding line for the whole sax section in harmony (often four-
  way close with the bari doubling the lead an octave lower, or drop 2).
- Write it like a great solo: 8th-note lines with chromatic approach notes, rests for
  breath, and articulation (accents, ghosted notes, falls).
- Keep it in the alto's strong register (written about D4-C6); avoid the alto's lowest
  notes when voiced in close position.

## 4. Shout chorus
- The climax: full ensemble, usually the last chorus before the head out.
- Lead trumpet high (but within the player's endurance; concert top around C6-E6
  depending on band), brass in tight voicings, saxes doubling or answering.
- Rhythmic unison hits and **kicks** for the drummer; write the kicks in the drum part.

## 5. Backgrounds behind solos
Sparse pads or riffs, entering after the soloist's first chorus; stay out of the
soloist's register.

## Gotchas
- Soli lines without breaths cannot be played as written.
- Brass endurance: a hard shout chorus after a long chart needs rest before it.
- Drum part needs the ensemble figures cued, not just "time".

## Verify
- [ ] Form map shows head, solos, soli, shout and ending.
- [ ] Soli is breathable and articulated.
- [ ] Drum part shows the shout chorus kicks.
