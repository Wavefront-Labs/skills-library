# A cappella arranging

**Goal:** turn a band recording into a voices-only arrangement that grooves and stays in tune.

## 1. Map the track to roles
| Original | A cappella role |
|---|---|
| Lead vocal | Solo voice |
| Bass | **Vocal bass** (dedicated singer, low register) |
| Drums | **Vocal percussion (VP)** - usually improvised from a groove description, not notated note-for-note |
| Guitars/keys/pads | 2-4 **background (BG) voices** making chords and rhythmic figures |
| Hooks/riffs | assign to BGs as a featured line |

Sharon and Bell's process: listen, transcribe the essentials (form, bass line, key riffs), then write the bass first, then BGs, then solo, then VP notes.

## 2. Bass
- Simplify to the essential groove; use syllables with a soft attack and sustained vowel: "doom", "dm", "bm", "dn"; avoid hard consonants every note.
- Range E2-C4 typical; one octave lower than written in treble-8 or written in bass clef.

## 3. Background voices: syllables matter
- Choose syllables by **instrument imitated** and **articulation**:
  - sustained pads: "oo", "ah", "mm"
  - rhythmic guitar: "jin", "jen", "dah-n", "bop"
  - plucked/staccato: "dit", "doo", "bah"
  - bright brass stabs: "bwah", "dat"
- Vowels shape blend: all BGs on the same vowel. Avoid closing to the consonant early.
- Keep BG voicings close (3rds, open 5ths with the bass) and voice-led; let the bass carry the root.

## 4. Pitch and stamina
- Without instruments the group drifts flat: avoid long unison held notes in weak keys; choose keys that put bass and soprano in strong ranges; build in moments where everyone lands on a strong unison/octave to reset.
- Give singers breathing spots; rotate the rhythmic load among BG parts.

## Gotchas
- Over-writing: too many busy parts = mush. Strip to bass, VP, 2-3 BGs, solo.
- Notating VP in full detail; give a groove description and key hits instead.

## Verify
- Sing through with a recorder: does it groove without the original? Does the final chord match the starting pitch?
- Check each part for range and breath points.
