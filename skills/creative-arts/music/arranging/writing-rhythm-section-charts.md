# Writing rhythm section charts

**Goal:** charts that tell drums, bass, guitar and keys exactly what's fixed and leave room for
what should be played freely.

## 1. Three levels of specificity
| Notation | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Slash notation** (one slash per beat) + chord symbols | comp freely in the style |
| **Rhythmic notation** (slash noteheads with stems) | play these rhythms (kicks/hits), voicings free |
| Fully written notes | play exactly (riffs, bass lines, unison lines) |

## 2. Writing hits and kicks
- Ensemble hits: rhythmic slashes with accents in all rhythm parts; in the drum part, write the hit rhythm above the staff with "fill" or "time" between.
- Anticipations: tie from the and-of-4 into the next bar (show the push).
- Long holds: diamond/whole-note slashes with fermata; "let ring".

## 3. Part specifics
- **Drums**: "time" with style ("medium swing", "funk 16ths"), bar count per section, hits written above the staff, fills indicated ("fill" over the last bar). Note figures for kick/snare only where specific.
- **Bass**: written line for riffs; chord symbols with "walk" or "2-feel" for jazz; key rhythmic figures.
- **Guitar/keys**: chord symbols over slashes, voicings or tops where important ("top note G"), style notes (clean, strummed 8ths).

## 4. Road map and layout
- Rehearsal letters, bar numbers, repeats/D.S./coda kept simple; 4 bars per line; first and second endings clear.
- One page per part if possible; tempo, style and feel at the top.

## Gotchas
- Writing every comping rhythm: players lose the feel and ignore the chart.
- Inconsistent hits between parts: drums and horns land differently.
- Chord symbols ambiguous (C-7 vs Cm7 vs Cmi7): pick one convention for the whole book.

## Verify
- Read-through: the band plays the hits together first time, and the form is followed without stops.
