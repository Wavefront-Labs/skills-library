# Horn and string section writing

**Goal:** add a horn or string section to a song or piece so that the parts are
idiomatic, sound full, and players can read them at first sight in a session.

## 1. Know ranges and transpositions (practical, not extreme)
| Instrument | Written transposition | Comfortable sounding range (approx.) |
|---|---|---|
| Trumpet in B♭ | sounds a major 2nd lower | E3 – C6 (lead to D6 for strong pros) |
| Alto sax (E♭) | sounds a major 6th lower | D♭3 – A♭5 |
| Tenor sax (B♭) | sounds a major 9th lower | A♭2 – E♭5 |
| Trombone | concert, bass clef | E2 – F4 (B♭4 strong players) |
| Violin | concert | G3 – E6 for section work |
| Viola | concert, alto clef | C3 – A5 |
| Cello | concert, bass/tenor clef | C2 – A4 |
| Double bass | sounds an octave lower | E1 – G3 |
Write **in concert pitch in the score** and let the notation software (Sibelius,
Dorico, MuseScore) transpose parts — then proofread the transposed parts.

## 2. Horn voicings
- **Unison/octaves** (e.g. trumpet + tenor sax an octave apart): the punchiest pop
  horn sound. Use for riffs.
- **Close voicing** (all notes within an octave): tight, bright. Lead trumpet on top.
- **Drop-2:** take the second voice from the top of a close voicing down an octave —
  opens the sound, keeps it playable. Standard for 4-horn writing.
- Avoid seconds in the bottom of a voicing below roughly C3; low clusters go muddy.
- **Breathe:** horn players need rests. Write phrases of 2–4 bars with gaps.
- Mark **articulations** explicitly: staccato, accent, marcato (^), fall, doit, shake.
  Unmarked horn parts get played inconsistently.

## 3. String writing
- **Section size changes everything:** a quartet (2-1-1) needs complete harmony in four
  lines; a 16-piece section can use **divisi** (marking "div.") to split players.
- **Pads:** sustained chords, cellos on root, violas on 3rd/7th, violins spread above.
  Specify **bowing** (slurs = one bow), dynamics with hairpins.
- **Movement:** give the pad internal motion (a moving viola line) so it breathes.
- Colours: *pizz.* (plucked), *arco* (back to bow), *sul tasto* (soft, airy), *sul
  ponticello* (glassy), *con sord.* (muted), tremolo. Always write *arco* after *pizz.*
- Keep fast passages in comfortable keys (sharps — D, A, G, E — ring on open strings).

## 4. Writing playable parts
- Cue notes after long rests; rehearsal marks every 8 bars; bar numbers on each system.
- One part per page-turn safe spot; avoid turns mid-phrase.
- Chord symbols on horn parts only if you want improvisation.
- Specify **swing or straight**, tempo, and style in words at the top.

## Gotchas
- Forgetting to transpose (or double-transposing) is the classic error — check one
  bar of each part against concert pitch.
- Horns in their top register are loud; balance them with dynamics, not mixing.
- Uncertain: ranges vary with player level; ask the contractor what level is booked.

## Verify
- [ ] Every part checked: correct transposition and clef.
- [ ] No notes outside the practical ranges above for the booked level.
- [ ] Horn phrases include breathing rests; articulations marked.
- [ ] String parts specify bowing, pizz./arco and divisi clearly.
- [ ] Parts have rehearsal marks, cues and safe page turns.
