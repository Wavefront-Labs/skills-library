# Orchestrating for brass

**Goal:** brass that is powerful when needed, warm when quiet, and does not wear the
players out.

## 1. Instruments and transpositions
- **Horn in F:** sounds a perfect 5th lower than written; treble clef (old bass-clef
  notation varies). Practical range roughly written F#3-C6. Warm, blends with
  woodwinds and strings. Four horns: 1 and 3 high, 2 and 4 low.
- **Trumpet in Bb:** sounds a major 2nd lower; C trumpet at pitch in orchestras.
  Practical top about written C6.
- **Trombone (C, bass/tenor clef):** tenor and bass trombone; slide positions make
  some legato and fast passages awkward (e.g. between 1st and 7th position).
- **Tuba (C, bass clef):** bass of the section; doubles cellos/basses or bass trombone.

## 2. Voicing
- Close position in the middle register is strong; spread wider at the bottom
  (follow the harmonic series: wide intervals low, close high).
- **Chorale voicing:** trumpets on top, horns inner, trombones and tuba below.
- **Interlocking horns with trombones** gives a smooth, darker block.
- One horn is roughly half a trumpet or trombone in loudness at forte; double horns to
  balance a chord (two horns per note against single trumpets).

## 3. Balance and dynamics
- Brass at f covers strings easily; mark brass a dynamic lower than strings at tuttis.
- Muted brass changes colour, not just volume (see mutes).

## 4. Endurance
- Give rests: long high passages tire lips. Alternate players (horn 1 and 3 share high
  material).
- Avoid exposed high entries after long rests without a lead-in.

## Gotchas
- Writing horn parts in concert pitch or in the wrong transposition is common in
  notation software; check the instrument setting.
- Trombone glissandi only work within one slide position set.
- Low trombone and tuba close-voiced chords are muddy.

## Verify
- [ ] All parts sit in practical ranges and correct transpositions.
- [ ] Chords are spaced wider at the bottom.
- [ ] Each player has rests in long cues.
