# Pitching songs for sync licensing

**Goal:** music supervisors can say yes quickly because the track fits the brief and
clearance is easy.

## 1. Two licences per use
- **Sync licence** for the composition (from the publisher/writers).
- **Master-use licence** for the recording (from the label or owner).
Being **one-stop** (you control both, or all co-owners have pre-authorised) makes a
track much more likely to be chosen under deadline.

## 2. Prepare the catalogue
- Full mix, **instrumental**, clean version (no profanity), short edits (30/60 s) and
  stems if possible.
- Embedded metadata: title, writers and splits, publisher, PRO, contact email and
  phone, one-stop status, BPM, key, mood keywords.
- A lyric sheet; lyrics that are universal (not naming specific places or brands)
  are easier to place.

## 3. Pitching
- Respond to **briefs** exactly: mood, tempo, era, vocal/instrumental and deadline.
  Send two or three strong fits, not the whole catalogue.
- Send streaming links or a private playlist, never unsolicited attachments.
- Routes: music supervisors directly, sync agents, publishers, and production music
  libraries (exclusive or non-exclusive; read retitling and term clauses).

## 4. Deal points
Fees vary by media (ad vs TV vs indie film), term, territory and prominence (background
vs featured). The sync and master fees are often matched ("MFN"). Performance royalties
come later via the cue sheet.

## Gotchas
- An uncleared sample or uncontactable co-writer kills the placement.
- Exclusive library deals may prevent pitching the same track elsewhere.
- Key changes or long intros are hard to edit to picture; provide edit points.

## Verify
- [ ] All co-owners have agreed to quoted fees or one-stop authority.
- [ ] Instrumental, clean and edit versions exist with complete metadata.
- [ ] Each pitch matches the brief's mood, tempo and deadline.
