# Writing a contrasting bridge

**Goal:** a section that refreshes the ear after two verse-chorus cycles and makes the final
chorus hit harder.

## 1. Placement and length
- Usually after the second chorus; 4-8 bars (sometimes 16). Then final chorus (often doubled or lifted).
- Optional in modern pop; replace with a breakdown, post-chorus variation or a key change if the song already has enough contrast.

## 2. Contrast toolkit (change at least two)
| Dimension | Ideas |
|---|---|
| Harmony | start on a chord not yet used (IV, vi, bVI, ii); avoid the tonic early; end on V or a suspended chord |
| Melody | different range (lower or higher than the chorus), different contour, longer or shorter phrases |
| Rhythm | half-time feel, stop-time, sparser or busier lyric rhythm |
| Arrangement | strip down (vocal + one instrument) or build (drum fill into last chorus) |
| Lyric | new perspective: time shift (then/now), realisation, confession, question, address another person |

## 3. Lyric job
- Not new information for its own sake: it should change how the final chorus title lands ("the twist").
- Keep it short and concentrated; the bridge is often the emotional peak or the turning point.

## 4. Lead back into the chorus
- End on the dominant (V) or a suspended chord, a drum fill, a pause or a riser; the final chorus then arrives as a release.

## Gotchas
- Bridge that sounds like a third verse: same chords and melody shape.
- Too long: momentum dies before the last chorus.
- Lyric that introduces a new topic unrelated to the title.

## Verify
- Play chorus -> bridge -> chorus: the bridge should feel different within its first bar and make the chorus feel new.
- Read the bridge lyric then the chorus: does the chorus mean more?
