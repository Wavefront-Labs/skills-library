# Producing a song demo for pitching

**Goal:** a demo that makes the song, not the production, the star, and that suits the
artist or listener it is pitched to.

## 1. Pick the demo level
- **Work tape:** voice plus guitar or piano. Fine for co-writers, a publisher who
  already knows you, or to judge the song.
- **Full demo:** band or programmed production close to radio style. Expected for
  pitches to major artists and for sync.
Spend on the vocalist first, then the arrangement.

## 2. Arrangement
- Get to the first chorus quickly (under about 45-60 s in pop/country).
- Short intro (bars, not a minute), one solo at most, clear ending (not a fade if
  possible).
- Match current style of the target artist, but avoid trendy elements that date fast.

## 3. Vocalist
- Choose a singer of the same gender and range as the target artist. The lyric must be
  clear; diction beats style.
- Record the vocal in the target artist's key range, not yours.

## 4. Delivery files
- Mixed master WAV and high-quality MP3, tagged with title, writers and contact.
- **Lyric sheet** (PDF) with writers, publishers/PRO.
- For sync, also an **instrumental** and, if possible, stems.
- File name: `SongTitle_Writer1_Writer2_v3.mp3`.

## Gotchas
- A great production with a weak lyric still gets passed; fix the song first.
- Unsplit, unregistered songs cause problems when a cut happens; agree splits first.
- Over-long demos (4:30 plus) get skipped.

## Verify
- [ ] Vocal matches target artist's gender and range.
- [ ] First chorus arrives quickly; total length is radio-sized.
- [ ] Master, MP3, lyric sheet and (for sync) instrumental are labelled with metadata.
