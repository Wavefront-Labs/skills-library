# Hook types: melodic, lyric, rhythmic and instrumental

**Goal:** more than one memorable element, so the song is recognisable within seconds.

## 1. The four types
- **Melodic hook:** a distinctive melodic cell, usually in the chorus on the title.
  Signature leap, repeated note pattern or a turn the ear can predict on repeat.
- **Lyric hook:** the title or a phrase with a twist, internal rhyme or repetition.
- **Rhythmic hook:** a syncopated vocal rhythm or chant that you could clap.
- **Instrumental hook:** a riff, synth line, guitar figure or vocal chop that plays
  without words (intro riffs, post-chorus drops).

## 2. Stack and place them
- Chorus: melodic + lyric hook together on the title.
- Intro and post-chorus: instrumental hook, so the song identifies itself in the first
  bars.
- Verse: a smaller rhythmic or lyric hook keeps attention between choruses.
- Repeat hooks exactly at first; vary only after they are established.

## 3. Building a strong hook
- Keep it short: 2-4 bars.
- Repetition inside the hook (A-A-B shape) helps memory.
- Contrast with its surroundings: if verses are busy, the hook is sparse.
- Instrumental hooks often answer the vocal hook, filling the gap after it.

## Gotchas
- Too many hooks compete; one primary hook per section.
- An instrumental riff in the same register as the vocal masks it; move it an octave.
- A title that never appears in the hook leaves listeners unable to name the song.

## Verify
- [ ] The song has at least a melodic/lyric hook and an instrumental hook.
- [ ] The first 10 seconds contain a recognisable hook.
- [ ] Someone can hum or say the hook after one listen.
