# Editing and rewriting a lyric

**Goal:** turn a first draft into a focused, singable lyric where every line works.

## 1. Big-picture pass
- State the song's idea in one sentence. Does the **title** express it, and does every section serve it?
- Does each verse add **new information** (time moves, stakes rise, perspective changes)? Verse 2 that restates verse 1 is the most common fault.
- Does the chorus mean more each time it comes back?

## 2. Consistency pass
- **Point of view** and **tense** consistent unless the shift is deliberate (and signalled).
- Who is "you"? Clear across sections.
- Images consistent (no mixed metaphors).

## 3. Line pass
- Replace clichés ("tears like rain", "heart of stone") with specific images (see object-writing skill), unless the cliché is twisted deliberately.
- Cut filler words added for syllable count ("so", "just", "really", "oh yeah") - fix the melody or rephrase.
- Stronger verbs; fewer adjectives.
- Rhyme: avoid forced rhymes that bend meaning or word order; consider slant rhyme (see rhyme skill).

## 4. Singability pass
- Sing it: stressed syllables on strong beats (prosody); open vowels on high, long notes; avoid tongue-twisters at speed.
- Breath points where phrases end.

## 5. Process
- Put the draft away for a day; read it aloud; get feedback from a co-writer or listener who doesn't know your intent.
- Keep old versions; sometimes the first line was right.

## Gotchas
- Rewriting only the words you dislike, not the structure that causes the problem.
- Over-polishing until it loses the conversational voice.

## Verify
- Every line can answer "why is this here?"
- Read the lyric aloud without music: it sounds natural and clear.
