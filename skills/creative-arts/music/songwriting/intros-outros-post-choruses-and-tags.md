# Intros, outros, post-choruses and tags

**Goal:** frame the song so it hooks immediately and ends memorably.

## 1. Intros
- Streaming-era listeners skip early: keep intros **short** (0-10 seconds is common in current pop; a cold open on the hook or first vocal line is common).
- Types: instrumental hook (a riff that returns later), chorus preview (first line of the chorus over sparse music), groove-only (2-4 bars to establish feel), vocal ad-lib.
- Make the intro material reusable (it should return as the post-chorus or outro).

## 2. Post-choruses
- A section right after the chorus that **extends the hook**: chant, vocal chop, wordless melody ("oh-oh"), or the instrumental riff. Common in modern pop and EDM-pop.
- Lower lyric density than the chorus; repetition is the point.

## 3. Tags
- Repeat the last line (title) of the final chorus 1-3 times, sometimes with a melodic or harmonic change (deceptive cadence then resolve) to signal the ending.

## 4. Outros
- Options: cold ending (hit and stop), final chord ring-out, return of the intro riff, a breakdown with a new line (twist), or a fade (rarer now; works for groove songs).
- Plan the outro in the arrangement, not as an afterthought; DJs and radio edits may need clean endings.

## 5. Timing check
- Time to first chorus: many hits reach the chorus by 0:45-1:00; know the genre norm (compare references).

## Gotchas
- Long instrumental intros that only make sense live.
- Post-chorus that's a second chorus: it competes with the real hook.
- Outro that introduces new material with no connection.

## Verify
- Play the first 15 seconds to someone: do they know what the song is about or hum the hook?
- The ending feels intentional (no fade masking an unfinished arrangement).
