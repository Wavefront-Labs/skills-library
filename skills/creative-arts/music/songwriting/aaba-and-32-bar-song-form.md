# AABA and 32-bar song form

**Goal:** a song where the title-bearing A section repeats and a bridge (B) gives contrast,
as in standards and many pop and country songs.

## 1. The shape
| Section | Bars (classic) | Job |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | 8 | establish melody, groove and situation; **title in the first or last line** |
| A2 | 8 | same melody, new lyric developing the story; title again |
| B (bridge/middle eight) | 8 | contrast: new key area or chords, different melodic range and rhythm, new angle on the idea |
| A3 | 8 | return of A melody; lyric resolves or twists the title's meaning |
- Often followed by an instrumental A or a repeat of B-A3. Old standards may have an introductory "verse" before the 32-bar chorus.

## 2. Writing the A
- One strong melodic idea; the **title** sits where the melody resolves (usually the last line, landing on the tonic).
- Each A-lyric moves the story forward; the title line gains new meaning each time.

## 3. Writing the B
- Harmony: move away from the tonic (to IV, the relative minor, or a ii-V chain to a new key), ending on V to set up A3.
- Melody: different contour and rhythm (if A is high and long-noted, B is lower and busier, or vice versa).
- Lyric: shift perspective (time, place, emotion), often a question or realisation.

## 4. Modern AABA
- Beatles ("Yesterday", "Something"), Billy Joel, country ballads. In pop, often A-A-B-A with the A acting as a "verse/refrain" (title at the end of each verse) instead of a separate chorus.

## Gotchas
- A2 lyric that just repeats A1's information: no development.
- A bridge that is only a different chord progression but the same melodic rhythm: not enough contrast.
- Burying the title mid-line; place it on the resolution.

## Verify
- Title appears at the same place in each A and lands on a strong beat/tonic.
- Sing B then A3: the return should feel like coming home.
