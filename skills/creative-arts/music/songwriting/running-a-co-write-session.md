# Running a co-write session

**Goal:** finish a strong song (or a solid draft) in one session with collaborators, and leave
with clear ownership.

## 1. Prepare
- Bring 3-5 **titles/concepts** and a few melodic or lyric fragments; producers bring 2-3 track starts in different tempos/moods.
- Know your co-writers' recent work and the artist (if writing for one): their range, voice, themes.

## 2. Roles
- Common modern pop split: **track/producer** (beat and chords), **topliner(s)** (melody and lyric), sometimes the artist. Nashville-style: two or three writers with guitar/piano, title-first.
- Roles are flexible; say what you're good at early.

## 3. Session flow
1. 10-20 minutes of conversation: find a true story or emotion; pick a **title or concept** everyone likes.
2. Choose the track or groove; sing melodic ideas freely over it ("mumble" toplines) and record everything (voice memo/DAW).
3. Build the chorus first (title placement, hook), then verses, then pre-chorus/bridge.
4. Keep momentum: if stuck on a line, use a placeholder and move on; fix in the second pass.
5. Before finishing, record a **work tape/demo vocal** of the whole song.

## 4. Etiquette
- "No bad ideas" in the generating phase; say "what if..." rather than "no".
- Don't bring a nearly finished song to a co-write unless all agree it's a finishing session (and splits reflect that).
- Phones away; breaks every 90 minutes.

## 5. Before leaving
- **Agree splits** (default equal among writers in the room is common in pop/Nashville) and fill a **split sheet** (see split-sheet skill). Note who keeps the session files and who delivers the demo.

## Gotchas
- Not recording: the best melody of the day disappears.
- Avoiding the splits conversation until the song is released.

## Verify
- Leave with: full demo vocal recording, lyric sheet, split sheet signed, next steps (who produces the demo, who pitches).
