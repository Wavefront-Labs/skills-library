# Split sheets and songwriter splits

**Goal:** a written, signed agreement on who owns what share of a song's composition, made on
the day it was written. Not legal advice.

## 1. What the split sheet records
- Song title (and working titles), date, session location.
- Each writer: legal name, writer/stage name, **% share** of the composition (totals 100%), **PRO** (ASCAP, BMI, SESAC, PRS, SOCAN, APRA...), **IPI/CAE number**, contact.
- Each writer's **publisher** (name, PRO, IPI) and publisher share if known; "self-published" if none.
- Samples/interpolations included (and that their owners' shares will reduce everyone's pro rata).
- Signatures of all writers.

## 2. Deciding splits
- Common default in pop and Nashville: **equal shares among everyone in the room who contributed to writing**, regardless of who wrote which line. Some teams split by contribution (lyric vs music); agree the method before or at the start.
- Producer contributions: in pop/hip-hop, producers who create the track's musical elements are usually **writers** too; separate from producer **points** (royalties on the master).
- Arranging, mixing or playing an instrument part usually isn't songwriting unless agreed.

## 3. Master vs composition
- Split sheet = **composition** (publishing). The **master recording** ownership (label/artist) and producer royalties are separate agreements.

## 4. After the session
- Each writer (or their publisher) registers the work with their PRO using identical splits, titles and co-writer details; mismatches delay payments.
- Keep the signed sheet with the session files (see session-organisation skill).

## Gotchas
- Waiting until release to discuss splits: disputes and blocked registrations.
- Splits adding up to more than 100% across registrations.
- Forgetting sample owners' shares.

## Verify
- Shares total exactly 100%; every writer signed; PRO registrations match the sheet.
