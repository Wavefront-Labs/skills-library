# Developing a motif

**Goal:** turn a 2-5 note idea into phrases and sections that are varied but obviously related.

## 1. Define the motif
Write down its **pitch intervals**, **rhythm**, **contour** and **harmonic implication**.
Example: Beethoven 5: three repeated quavers + falling major 3rd, short-short-short-long.
Every technique below changes one or two of these while keeping the rest.

## 2. Techniques
| Technique | What changes | Example from motif G-G-G-Eb |
|---|---|---|
| Repetition | nothing | |
| **Sequence** | pitch level (real = exact intervals; tonal = within the key) | F-F-F-D |
| Inversion | interval direction | G-G-G-B |
| Retrograde | order | Eb-G-G-G |
| Augmentation / diminution | durations x2 / /2 | minims instead of quavers |
| Interval expansion/contraction | interval size | G-G-G-C |
| **Fragmentation** | use only part | G-G-G... repeated, or just the falling 3rd |
| Rhythmic displacement | position in the bar | starting on beat 2 |
| Reharmonisation | chords under it | same notes over Eb, then Cm, then Ab |
| Orchestration change | colour, register | strings to horns, low to high |
| Liquidation | strip features until only rhythm remains | leads into the cadence |

## 3. Building a passage
1. Present motif (2 bars). 2. Sequence it up a step (tension). 3. Fragment the tail and repeat faster (drive). 4. Cadence. This is the **sentence** shape (see phrases skill).
- In development sections: sequence through keys, combine motif with its inversion contrapuntally, and fragment toward the retransition.

## 4. In media and songwriting
- Film: the motif is the leitmotif; vary orchestration and harmony for emotional states (see leitmotif skill).
- Songs: rhythmic motif of the hook repeated with different pitches in verse lines.

## Gotchas
- Changing everything at once breaks recognisability: keep rhythm when changing pitch, and vice versa.
- Too many sequence steps (3+ identical) sound mechanical; break the pattern on the third.

## Verify
- Label every bar of the passage with the motif version it uses; unlabelled bars may be filler.
- Ask a listener whether the passage sounds like "one idea": that's the test.
