# Modes and modal writing

**Goal:** write music that clearly sounds in a mode, not like major or minor with odd notes.

## 1. Modes by brightness (each compared with major or minor)
| Mode | Formula vs major / minor | Characteristic note | Example vamp |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lydian | major with **#4** | #4 | I - II (C - D) |
| Ionian | major | - | |
| Mixolydian | major with **b7** | b7 | I - bVII (C - B-flat) |
| Dorian | minor with **natural 6** | 6 | i - IV (Dm - G) |
| Aeolian | natural minor | b6 | i - bVI - bVII |
| Phrygian | minor with **b2** | b2 | i - bII (Em - F) |
| Locrian | minor with b2 and **b5** | b5 | rarely tonal; unstable tonic |

To find a mode on any note: take the formula above from that tonic. (Parent-scale method: D Dorian = C major from D; but thinking in formulas is faster for composing.)

## 2. Making the mode audible
1. **Establish the tonic** with a pedal or drone, a repeated bass note or a vamp of 2 chords.
2. **Feature the characteristic note** in the melody and in the chords: Dorian's major IV chord, Mixolydian's bVII, Lydian's II.
3. **Avoid the dominant-tonic pull** of major/minor: no leading-note V7-I in Mixolydian or Dorian (it turns them into major or harmonic minor).
4. Modal cadences: bVII-I (Mixolydian), IV-i or v-i (Dorian), bII-i (Phrygian).
5. Use quartal voicings and open fifths to avoid triadic functional pull (jazz modal: So What chords).

## 3. Uses
- Dorian: funk, jazz (So What), folk (Scarborough Fair).
- Mixolydian: rock and Celtic (Norwegian Wood, Sweet Child o' Mine verse feel).
- Lydian: film "wonder" (John Williams, Simpsons theme).
- Phrygian: flamenco (with major 3rd: Phrygian dominant), metal.

## Gotchas
- A Dorian melody over a ii-V-I is just major; the tonic must be the modal one.
- Overusing the characteristic note on weak beats only hides it; put it on strong beats or as a held note.

## Verify
- Play the passage over a drone of the tonic: it should sound resolved on the tonic and coloured by the characteristic note.
- Ask a listener to sing the home note; it should be the modal tonic.
