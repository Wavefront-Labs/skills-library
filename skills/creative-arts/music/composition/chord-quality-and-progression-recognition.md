# Chord quality and progression recognition

**Goal:** hear a progression and write its Roman numerals (or chord symbols) and bass line.

## 1. Chord quality by ear
| Quality | Sound cue |
|---|---|
| Major | bright, stable |
| Minor | dark, stable |
| Diminished | tense, small, "squeezed" |
| Augmented | unresolved, dreamy, expanding |
| Dominant 7 | major + bluesy tension that wants to resolve |
| Major 7 | soft, lush, slightly dissonant top (the M7 against the root) |
| Minor 7 | mellow, minor but relaxed |
| Half-diminished (m7b5) | dark, unsettled, minor-ish |
| Diminished 7 | very tense, symmetrical, "silent movie villain" |

Drill: random root, one quality family at a time (triads, then sevenths), 10 min/day, until 90%.

## 2. Progression dictation routine
1. **Find the key and tonic** (sing do).
2. **Bass line first:** sing the bass notes in solfege. Bass do usually = I; sol = V; fa = IV or ii6; re = ii, V4/3 or vii dim 6; ti in the bass = V6 or V6/5 (first inversion).
3. **Then quality/function** for each bass note: is it the root (root position) or 3rd (first inversion)?
4. Use cadence templates: **V-I** (perfect/authentic), **IV-I** (plagal), **x-V** (imperfect/half), **V-vi** (interrupted/deceptive).
5. Write Roman numerals with inversion figures (I6, V6/5) or chord symbols with slash bass (G/B).

## 3. Common progressions to know by sound
I-IV-V-I; I-vi-IV-V (50s); vi-IV-I-V (axis); ii-V-I (jazz); I-V-vi-IV (pop); i-bVII-bVI-V (Andalusian); 12-bar blues.

## Gotchas
- Inversions fool you into hearing a different chord: always confirm the bass separately.
- I6/4 before V at cadences (cadential 6/4) sounds like I; it functions as V.
- Pop recordings: the bass may anticipate or sit on a pedal; label as slash chords.

## Verify
- Play your answer on a keyboard alongside the recording.
- Exam-style test: 4-chord progressions, 3 hearings, aim 90%.
