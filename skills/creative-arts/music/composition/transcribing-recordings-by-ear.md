# Transcribing recordings by ear

**Goal:** produce an accurate lead sheet, chart or full transcription from a recording.

## 1. Tools
- **Slow-down without pitch change:** Transcribe! (Seventh String), Amazing Slow Downer, Anytune, Moises, or any DAW (Ableton warp at 50-70%, Logic Varispeed in "speed only" mode).
- **Loop** short sections (1-2 bars); set markers at sections.
- **EQ / filter** to isolate: low-pass for bass lines, band-pass 1-4 kHz for guitar comping, phase/centre-cancel for hearing side-panned parts. AI stem separation (Moises, Logic Stem Splitter, RX Music Rebalance) helps but can smear notes; confirm by ear.
- Keep an instrument at hand to check pitches.

## 2. Top-down workflow
1. **Form map:** timestamps of intro/verse/chorus etc., bar counts, time signature, tempo (tap tempo).
2. **Key:** find the tonic by singing the resting note; confirm with the final chord.
3. **Bass line** (lowest, clearest functional info) for each section.
4. **Chords** over the bass: quality by ear (see chord-recognition skill), extensions last.
5. **Melody/solo** phrase by phrase: sing it back at full speed first, then slow down only for fast passages.
6. **Rhythm and feel details:** hits, pushes, swing ratio, articulation.

## 3. Writing it up
- Lead sheet: melody + chord symbols + form; chart: slashes, hits, road map (see chart-writing skills).
- Full solo: include articulations, bends, ghost notes; note fingering/position for guitar (tab).

## Gotchas
- Tuning: older records are often not at A=440; tune your reference instrument to the record (Transcribe! shows the deviation) or you'll mis-hear sharps and flats.
- Octave errors on bass and on heavily processed sounds; check by playing along.
- Over-slowing distorts timing perception; check rhythm at full speed.

## Verify
- Play your transcription along with the recording at full speed: every bar lines up.
- Have another musician read it down with the track.
