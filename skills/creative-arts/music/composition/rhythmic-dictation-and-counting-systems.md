# Rhythmic dictation and counting systems

**Goal:** hear a rhythm and write it accurately; read any rhythm by counting aloud.

## 1. Pick one counting system and use it always
| System | Simple-time quavers / semiquavers | Compound beat | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Numbers (US) | 1 + / 1 e + a | 1 la li (or 1 + a) | Bar position is explicit; good for bands |
| **Takadimi** | ta di / ta ka di mi | ta ki da | Beat-functional: same syllable = same position in any metre |
| Kodaly | ta, ti-ti, tika-tika | ti-ti-ti | For children; no bar position |

Takadimi (Hoffman, Pelto and White) is the most common in US university aural classes because syllables depend on position in the beat, not on note value.

## 2. Dictation routine (exam or classroom)
1. **First hearing - don't write notes.** Find the metre: conduct along; is the beat divided in 2 or 3? How many beats per bar?
2. Draw bar lines and beat ticks for the whole example (e.g. 4 bars x 4 beats).
3. **Second hearing:** fix the beat-onsets only (where are the long notes, where are the rests).
4. **Further hearings:** fill one beat at a time using syllables: whisper the syllables while listening, then translate syllables to notation.
5. Check each bar sums to the time signature.

## 3. Building speed
- Learn the **beat-pattern vocabulary**: in simple time there are only ~16 common one-beat patterns (crotchet; two quavers; four semiquavers; quaver-two semis; two semis-quaver; dotted quaver-semi; semi-dotted quaver; triplet...). Drill flash cards until each is recognised in under a second.
- Practise on musictheory.net rhythm exercises or with a partner clapping 2-bar phrases.

## Common errors and fixes
- **Dotted vs even**: dotted quaver-semiquaver "limps"; say "ta--mi" vs "ta-di".
- **Ties across the beat** written as long notes that hide beat 3: rewrite with ties.
- **Upbeat (anacrusis) missed**: if the first strong accent isn't the first note, you have an anacrusis; the last bar is shortened by the same amount.
- Losing count during long notes: keep conducting physically.

## Verify
- Clap your written answer back while the example plays: any beat where you diverge is wrong.
- Self-test with randomly generated rhythms (musictheory.net) and track accuracy over a week.
