# Simple and compound time, and beaming to show the beat

**Goal:** given a time signature, know what the beat is and how it divides, then notate
rhythms so a player sees the beat instantly.

## 1. Classify the time signature
| Top number | Type | Beat unit | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2, 3, 4 | **Simple** (beat divides in 2) | the note named by the bottom number | 3/4 = 3 crotchet beats |
| 6, 9, 12 | **Compound** (beat divides in 3) | a **dotted** note = 3 of the bottom unit | 6/8 = 2 dotted-crotchet beats |
| 5, 7, 11... | **Irregular** (see irregular-metre skill) | mixed 2+3 groups | 7/8 = 2+2+3 |

- Duple/triple/quadruple = beats per bar: 2/4 and 6/8 are both duple; 3/4 and 9/8 triple; 4/4 and 12/8 quadruple.
- Quick test for compound: top number divisible by 3 **and** greater than 3. 3/8 is simple triple (three quaver beats) unless at a fast tempo where it is felt in one.

## 2. Beaming rules (show the beat)
1. **Beam within a beat, not across beats.** In 6/8 beam quavers in threes (3+3), never 2+2+2 (that reads as 3/4).
2. In 4/4 you may beam four quavers across beats 1-2 or 3-4 (half-bar), **never across the middle** of the bar (beats 2-3).
3. In 3/4, six quavers may be beamed as one group *only* when the whole bar is quavers; mixed rhythms beam per beat.
4. Semiquavers beam per beat; in compound time a beat of six semiquavers is one beam group under the dotted crotchet.
5. **Rests follow the beat too**: in 6/8 a silent first beat is a dotted crotchet rest; in 4/4 a silent beats 2-3 is two crotchet rests, not a minim rest (the minim rest may only fill beats 1-2 or 3-4). A whole-bar rest is a semibreve rest in **any** metre.
6. Don't tie where a single dotted value shows the beat clearly, but do tie across the middle of a 4/4 bar rather than hiding beat 3 (a crotchet-minim-crotchet rhythm is fine; a dotted minim starting on beat 2 is not).

## 3. Converting between simple and compound
- Simple to compound (e.g. 2/4 to 6/8): each crotchet beat becomes a dotted crotchet; duplet quavers become triplets' natural form (two quavers in 2/4 become crotchet-quaver or three quavers in 6/8, depending on the swing intended).
- A **triplet** in simple time = the natural division in compound; a **duplet** in compound = the natural division of simple time.

## Gotchas
- 6/8 vs 3/4 is the classic exam trap: same six quavers, different grouping (3+3 vs 2+2+2) and different accent.
- 12/8 shuffles and blues are compound quadruple: write them in 12/8 (or 4/4 with a "swing" or triplet-feel marking), not 4/4 with triplets everywhere.
- Never beam over a rest at a beat boundary unless the publisher's style explicitly uses it (Gould permits beams over rests in some contexts; exam boards prefer not).

## Verify
- Clap the rhythm counting the beat aloud ("1-and-a 2-and-a" in compound): every beam group starts on a counted beat.
- Check each bar adds up exactly (6/8 = six quavers).
- Read it back as a player: can you see each beat start without counting? If not, rebeam.
