# Realising ornaments

**Goal:** turn ornament signs into the right notes and rhythm for the style, on paper
(theory exams) or in performance.

## 1. The signs and their realisations
| Sign | Name | Realisation (on note D in C major) |
|---|---|---|
| tr or tr~~ | Trill | Alternation with upper note. **Baroque/early Classical: start on the upper note** (E-D-E-D...), on the beat. **From c.1800: start on the main note.** End with a turn (termination) when written or at cadences. |
| Short zigzag | Upper mordent (Pralltriller) | D-E-D quickly, on the beat |
| Zigzag with vertical line | Lower mordent (Mordent) | D-C#/C-D (lower note in key, or as marked) |
| ~ (on the note) | Turn | E-D-C-D (upper, main, lower, main) |
| ~ (between notes) | Turn after the note | D held, then E-D-C-D leading to next note |
| Small note, no slash | Appoggiatura | Takes **half** the main note's value (two-thirds if dotted), on the beat, stressed |
| Small note with slash | Acciaccatura | As short as possible, just before or on the beat |

- Accidentals above or below a sign apply to the upper or lower auxiliary.

## 2. Writing out (theory exam style)
1. Identify the auxiliary note (diatonic unless an accidental is shown).
2. Fill the main note's value exactly: e.g. trill on a crotchet at moderate tempo = four semiquavers or a demisemiquaver group ending with a turn.
3. Keep the bar total correct and beam by the beat.

## 3. Performance practice
- Baroque cadential trills start on the upper note, slowly then accelerating, with the dissonance (upper note) emphasised.
- Classical appoggiaturas on strong beats are long and expressive (Mozart).
- Romantic and later: main-note trills, and grace notes often before the beat (Chopin's are variable; check the edition).
- Keep the pulse: an ornament never delays the next beat.

## Gotchas
- Mordent vs inverted mordent naming differs between UK, US and German usage; go by the sign (line through = lower).
- A turn on a dotted note is often realised to land on the dot.
- Over-ornamenting repeats in Baroque music is idiomatic; adding trills in Classical slow movements usually isn't.

## Verify
- Count your written realisation against the bar.
- Compare with an Urtext edition's ornament table (Henle and Barenreiter print them in the preface).
