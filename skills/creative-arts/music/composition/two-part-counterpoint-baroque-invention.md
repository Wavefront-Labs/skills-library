# Two-part counterpoint: writing an invention

**Goal:** a short (about 20-25 bar) keyboard piece in two independent voices built from
one motive, modelled on Bach's C major Invention BWV 772.

## 1. The motive
- 1-2 beats of running 16ths plus a contrasting rhythmic tail (Bach 772: a scalar figure
  plus leaps of a 4th/5th).
- It must outline the tonic chord, invert well (upside down), and fit against itself a
  bar later and against a slower countersubject.

## 2. Plan the form (typical)
| Section | Bars | Keys |
|---|---|---|
| Exposition: motive in RH, then LH imitates at the octave | 1-6 | I to V (or i to III/v) |
| Episode/development: sequences, inversion, motive fragments | 7-14 | V, vi, ii (related keys) |
| Return / recapitulation | 15-22 | back to I, final cadence (often over a tonic pedal) |
Cadences mark each arrival (authentic cadence in V, in vi, then I).

## 3. Writing technique
- **Implied harmony:** decide a chord per beat or half-bar first, then make both voices
  outline it. Strong beats = chord tones; weak 16ths may be passing/neighbour tones.
- **Rhythmic complementarity:** when one voice runs in 16ths, the other moves in 8ths or
  holds; the texture should always have 16th motion somewhere.
- **Sequences:** repeat a one-bar pattern down by step (descending 5ths sequence:
  vi-ii-V-I) 2-3 times, then break it. Never more than three statements.
- **Invertible counterpoint at the octave:** if voices swap, 5ths become 4ths -
  avoid 5ths on strong beats in material you will invert.
- **Modulation:** pivot chord or the new key's leading tone in one voice; confirm with a
  cadence. Related keys only (differing by at most one accidental).

## 4. Voice-leading checks
- No parallel 5ths/8ves between strong beats or between consecutive beats.
- Strong-beat dissonances only as prepared suspensions or accented passing tones.
- Keep voices within a 2-octave spread, no crossing for more than a beat.
- Bass line outlines functional progressions (roots by 5th and step).

## Gotchas
- Two voices running 16ths together for bars: muddy, loses independence.
- Leading tone doubled at a cadence (both voices on ti) - one must resolve to do.
- Modulating by just adding a sharp without a cadence: the key never establishes.

## Verify
- [ ] Motive stated in both voices, plus inversion or fragment in the middle section.
- [ ] Cadences in at least two related keys before returning home.
- [ ] Harmony clear when the two lines are played alone.
- [ ] No parallel perfect intervals; dissonances treated.
