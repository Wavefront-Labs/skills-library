# Fourth species and suspensions

**Goal:** use the suspension - the most expressive dissonance in tonal and modal music -
correctly, first in strict fourth species and then in chorale/tonal writing.

## The three stages
1. **Preparation** (weak beat): the note is consonant with the other voice.
2. **Suspension** (strong beat): the same pitch is tied or repeated while the other voice
   moves, creating a dissonance (or occasionally a consonance).
3. **Resolution** (next weak beat): the suspended voice moves **down by step** to a
   consonance. Upward resolution is a "retardation" and not part of strict style.

## Legal suspension types (two voices)
- Counterpoint above the CF: **7-6** and **4-3** (both resolve to imperfect consonances).
  **9-8** is allowed sparingly. Avoid 2-1 (unison).
- Counterpoint below: **2-3** (the lower voice is suspended, resolves down to a 3rd),
  and 4-5 is avoided (resolves to a perfect 5th, sounds weak). 9-10 is a 2-3 compound.
- Chains: 7-6 7-6 7-6 above, or 2-3 2-3 below, are idiomatic and fix parallel motion
  (a chain of 7-6 is parallel 6ths "delayed").
- 5-6 consonant syncopation and 6-5 are fine; consonant ties need no step resolution.

## Writing fourth species
- Start with a half rest, then tie half notes across every barline.
- Where a suspension cannot work (approach to a perfect interval, or the CF leaps),
  **break species**: drop the tie for one bar and use second-species motion.
- Cadence: counterpoint above, 7-6 into the octave (ti held over: 7-6-8); below, 2-3 into
  the octave/unison.
- Check parallels by looking at the resolution notes: 7-6 followed by 7-6 is fine;
  suspended 9-8 then 9-8 still yields parallel octaves.

## In tonal harmony (four voices)
- The resolution note must not be doubled in another voice at the moment of dissonance,
  except the bass in 9-8.
- The 4-3 over V is the stock cadential suspension; 7-6 is common in descending
  sequences; bass 2-3 suspensions decorate first-inversion chords.
- Ornamented resolutions (anticipation, échappée, a lower neighbour before resolving) are
  fine if the resolution still arrives on the expected beat.

## Gotchas
- A suspension prepared by a dissonance is not a suspension.
- Resolving the 4-3 while another voice already sounds the 3rd creates an ugly
  octave-ish clash; keep the 3rd out until the resolution.
- Over-use: in free writing 1-2 suspensions per phrase keep them expressive.

## Verify
- [ ] Each suspension has consonant preparation, strong-beat dissonance, step-down resolution.
- [ ] Only 7-6, 4-3, 9-8 above; 2-3 below.
- [ ] No hidden parallels at the resolution points.
- [ ] Species break used where a tie would create an illegal approach.
