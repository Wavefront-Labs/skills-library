# Pentatonic, blues, whole-tone and octatonic scales

**Goal:** use the common non-diatonic scales deliberately, knowing what harmony and mood each
brings.

## 1. Spellings (on C)
| Scale | Notes | Steps | Implied harmony / colour |
|---|---|---|---|
| Major pentatonic | C D E G A | M2 M2 m3 M2 m3 | No semitones; can't clash; folk, pop, East Asian |
| Minor pentatonic | C Eb F G Bb | m3 M2 M2 m3 M2 | Rock/blues lead; relative of Eb major pentatonic |
| Blues (minor) | C Eb F **Gb** G Bb | adds b5 "blue note" | Over dominant-7th blues changes |
| Major blues | C D **Eb** E G A | adds b3 | Country, gospel licks |
| Whole-tone | C D E F# G# A# | all M2 (6 notes) | Augmented triads, 7#5 / 9#5 chords; dreamy, ambiguous (Debussy) |
| Octatonic (half-whole) | C Db Eb E F# G A Bb | S T alternating | Dominant 7b9, #9, 13 chords; Stravinsky, jazz |
| Octatonic (whole-half) | C D Eb F F# G# A B | T S alternating | Diminished 7th chords |

## 2. Symmetry facts that save time
- Only **2** whole-tone scales exist (on C and on C#); only **3** octatonic scales. Transposing by the symmetry interval gives the same notes, so there's no tonic pull.
- Pentatonic over any chord of its parent major key's I, IV, V, ii, vi works because it lacks the 4th and 7th avoid-notes.

## 3. Composing with them
- Establish a tonic (bass pedal) when using symmetric scales; they have none of their own.
- **Pivot** between diatonic and whole-tone through an augmented triad; between diatonic and octatonic through a diminished 7th or a dominant 7b9 chord.
- Blues scale: bend the b3 toward the 3 and the b5 to 5; over a major blues, mix major and minor 3rds.
- Film: whole-tone for dreams/flashbacks; octatonic for tension and menace.

## Gotchas
- Whole-tone used for too long loses all direction; use it as a passage or a colour, not a key.
- The minor pentatonic over a major-key song's V chord makes a b7 clash on purpose (blues); over a major 7th chord it sounds wrong.

## Verify
- Spell by steps against the table and check symmetry (whole-tone = 6 equal steps).
- Play the scale over its implied chord: no unintended semitone clashes.
