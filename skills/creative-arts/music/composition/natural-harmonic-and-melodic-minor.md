# Natural, harmonic and melodic minor

**Goal:** spell all three minor forms in any key and choose the right one for the context.

## 1. The three forms (A minor shown)
| Form | Notes | Steps (T = tone, S = semitone) |
|---|---|---|
| Natural (Aeolian) | A B C D E F G A | T S T T S T T |
| Harmonic | A B C D E F **G#** A | T S T T S **T+S** S |
| Melodic (classical) | up: A B C D E **F# G#** A; down: as natural | up T S T T T T S |
| Jazz melodic minor | F# G# both ways | used as a chord-scale source |

- Raised notes are written as **accidentals**, never in the key signature.
- Spell by letter name first (one of each letter), then add accidentals; harmonic minor's 7th is often a double sharp in sharp keys (G-sharp minor: F-double-sharp).

## 2. Why each exists
- **Harmonic:** raising the 7th gives a leading note a semitone below the tonic, so the dominant chord (E-G#-B) is major and V-i cadences are strong. The by-product is the **augmented 2nd** (F-G#).
- **Melodic:** raises 6 and 7 ascending to remove the augmented 2nd in melodic lines; descends natural because the leading tone is not needed going down.
- **Natural:** modal, folk, rock and film colour; v (minor dominant) and bVII chords.

## 3. Practical choices
- Harmony in classical minor keys: use harmonic minor chords (V and vii dim7 with the raised 7th); iv and VI from the natural form.
- Melody: raise 6 and 7 when the line goes up to the tonic through them; use natural 6-7 coming down.
- Pop/rock: mostly natural minor (i-bVI-bIII-bVII); add the raised 7th only on V for a "classical" pull.
- Jazz: melodic minor modes generate altered and lydian-dominant scales (see chord-scale skill).

## Gotchas
- Augmented 2nd in vocal SATB writing is a voice-leading fault in exam style; resolve by using melodic minor or a different chord.
- Writing G-flat instead of F-sharp in A minor melodic: wrong letter, wrong spelling.

## Verify
- Check interval patterns against the table; one of each letter name.
- Play V-i with and without the raised 7th and hear the difference in pull.
