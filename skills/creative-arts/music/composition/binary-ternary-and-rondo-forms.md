# Binary, ternary and rondo forms

**Goal:** label the form of a piece and write in these forms with the right key plan.

## 1. Binary (AB, each half repeated)
- A: tonic to **V** (major key) or **III/v** (minor key), ending with a cadence in the new key.
- B: starts in the new key, moves through related keys, returns to the tonic with a perfect cadence.
- **Balanced binary**: the end of A returns, transposed to the tonic, at the end of B.
- **Rounded binary**: the **opening** of A returns mid-B in the tonic (A | B A'). Bridge to sonata form; standard for minuets.
- Baroque dances (Bach suites) are binary.

## 2. Ternary (ABA)
- A is **closed** (ends with a perfect cadence in the tonic); B contrasts (key: dominant, relative, parallel or subdominant; texture, mood); A returns (literally, da capo, or varied/shortened).
- Da capo aria; minuet-trio-minuet (each part itself rounded binary = "compound ternary"); many Romantic character pieces.
- Distinguish from rounded binary: in ternary, A is tonally complete on its own; in rounded binary, A modulates and is not.

## 3. Rondo
- Refrain A returns in the tonic between contrasting episodes: **ABACA** (5-part) or **ABACABA** (7-part).
- Episodes in contrasting keys; transitions and retransitions lead back to A; often a coda.
- **Sonata-rondo**: ABACABA where the third episode C is developmental and the second B returns in the tonic (Classical finales).

## 4. Composing method
1. Decide proportions (e.g. 16 + 16 + 16 bars ternary; 8+8 binary halves for a dance).
2. Plan the key scheme first, then cadences, then themes.
3. Make the contrast in B audible in at least two dimensions (key + texture, or tempo + mode).

## Gotchas
- Calling any piece with a middle section "ternary": check whether A is closed.
- In rondo, a refrain that returns identically each time grows dull; vary orchestration or ornament.

## Verify
- Draw a form chart: section letters, bar numbers, keys, cadences. Every boundary should have a cadence or a clear texture change.
