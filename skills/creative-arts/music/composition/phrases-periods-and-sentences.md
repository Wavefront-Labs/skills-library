# Phrases, periods and sentences

**Goal:** write 8-bar melodies (and longer themes) that sound complete and balanced.

## 1. Phrase basics
- A **phrase** ends with a **cadence**: weak (half cadence on V, imperfect) or strong (perfect authentic on I).
- Standard length is 4 bars; 8-bar themes are made of two phrases.

## 2. The period (antecedent-consequent)
| Bars | Unit | Ends with |
|---|---|---|
| 1-4 | Antecedent: basic idea (2 bars) + contrasting idea (2) | **Half cadence** (or imperfect authentic) |
| 5-8 | Consequent: basic idea returns (2) + contrasting idea varied (2) | **Perfect authentic cadence** |
- Question-and-answer. **Parallel period**: consequent starts like the antecedent; **contrasting period**: it doesn't.
- Example: "Twinkle Twinkle"-style melodies, many minuets and folk tunes.

## 3. The sentence (presentation-continuation)
| Bars | Unit |
|---|---|
| 1-2 | Basic idea (motif) |
| 3-4 | Basic idea repeated (exactly, sequenced, or over V instead of I) |
| 5-8 | Continuation: fragmentation (shorter units), faster harmonic rhythm, drive to a cadence |
- Beethoven Op. 2 No. 1 opening is the textbook case. Great for themes that build energy.

## 4. Writing method
1. Write the **cadence notes first** (bar 4: 2nd degree or leading note over V; bar 8: tonic over I).
2. Write a 2-bar basic idea with one distinctive rhythm.
3. Fill toward the cadence, keeping range within about an octave + 2 (voice: 10th) and climax on one high point, usually in the second half.
4. Harmony: harmonic rhythm 1-2 chords per bar, accelerating into cadences.

## 5. Extensions
- Phrase expansion (cadential delay, repeating the pre-cadence bar), elision (cadence bar is also the next phrase's first bar), 6-bar or 10-bar phrases for asymmetry.

## Gotchas
- Ending the antecedent on the tonic removes the "question"; the consequent then sounds redundant.
- Two identical 4-bar phrases is repetition, not a period: change the ending.

## Verify
- Sing it: bar 4 should feel open, bar 8 closed.
- Mark the cadences and the basic idea in the score; each unit must be audible as such.
