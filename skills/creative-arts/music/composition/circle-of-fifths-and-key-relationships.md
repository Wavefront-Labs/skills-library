# Circle of fifths and key relationships

**Goal:** name any key signature in a second and know which keys are near or far from the
home key.

## 1. Key signatures without memorising a table
- **Order of sharps:** F C G D A E B ("Father Charles Goes Down And Ends Battle"). **Flats:** the reverse, B E A D G C F.
- Sharp keys: the major key is a **semitone above the last sharp** (4 sharps, last is D#, key E major).
- Flat keys: the major key is the **second-last flat** (3 flats B-flat E-flat A-flat: E-flat major). F major (1 flat) is the one to memorise.
- Clockwise round the circle: each step adds a sharp (C G D A E B F#/Gb C#); anticlockwise adds a flat (C F Bb Eb Ab Db Gb Cb).
- Enharmonic overlap at 5-7 accidentals: B = C-flat, F-sharp = G-flat, C-sharp = D-flat. Pick the one with fewer accidentals unless the context (a modulation from a sharp key) demands otherwise.

## 2. Relationships
| Relationship | How to find it | Example from C major |
|---|---|---|
| Relative minor | same signature, a minor 3rd down (6th degree) | A minor |
| Parallel (tonic) minor | same tonic, 3 more flats | C minor |
| Dominant | one step clockwise | G major |
| Subdominant | one step anticlockwise | F major |
| **Closely related keys** | the five keys whose signatures differ by 0 or 1 accidental | Dm, Em, F, G, Am (= the keys of ii, iii, IV, V, vi) |

## 3. Using it
- **Modulation planning:** the closer on the circle, the smoother (fewer pivot problems). A move of 3+ steps sounds like a jump (use a direct modulation or a pivot from modal mixture).
- **Transposing a song:** count circle steps for the new key signature, then move every chord by the same interval.
- **Chord functions:** chords a fifth apart move strongly (ii-V-I goes anticlockwise round the circle).

## Gotchas
- Minor keys take their signature from the relative major, but harmonic/melodic minor raised notes are written as accidentals, never in the signature.
- Modes share signatures with their parent major (D Dorian = no sharps); don't label the key by the signature alone. Check the tonic note and final chord.

## Verify
- Quiz: random signature to major and minor key in under 2 seconds each.
- For a piece, list its home key's closely related keys and check that its modulations land in them (Classical style) or deliberately outside them.
