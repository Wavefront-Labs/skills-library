# Secondary dominants and tonicisation

**Goal:** add chromatic pull toward any diatonic chord by preceding it with its own dominant.

## 1. Spelling
- Choose the target chord (any major or minor diatonic chord: ii, iii, IV, V, vi; not vii dim).
- Build a **major triad or dominant 7th a perfect 5th above** the target's root: V/V in C = D-F#-A(-C); V7/vi = E-G#-B-D; V7/IV = C-E-G-Bb.
- Secondary leading-note chords: diminished triad or dim7 a semitone below the target root: vii dim7/V in C = F#-A-C-Eb.
- Label: V7/V, vii dim7/ii, etc. In chord symbols they are simply D7, E7, F#dim7.

## 2. Resolution rules (classical voice leading)
1. The chord's **3rd** (the temporary leading note, e.g. F# in V/V) rises a semitone to the target's root.
2. The **7th** falls by step.
3. Avoid doubling the temporary leading note.
4. Cross-relations (F in one voice, F# in another next chord) are avoided in exam style: keep the chromatic inflection in the same voice (F to F#).

## 3. Common uses
- **V/V before V** at a cadence (ii-V/V-V-I).
- **V7/IV** = I with a b7 added; gives a bluesy push to IV.
- **V7/vi** before vi: deceptive-sounding colour (the "Hey Jude" / Beatles move is related).
- **Extended dominants**: chains of dominants round the circle (E7-A7-D7-G7-C): ragtime, jazz, "Sweet Georgia Brown".
- **Related ii**: precede a secondary dominant with its ii (Am7-D7-G = ii/V-V/V-V) for jazz flow.

## 4. Tonicisation vs modulation
- Tonicisation: brief, one or two chords, home key never in doubt.
- Modulation: new key confirmed by a cadence in it. If you get a cadence (V-I in the new key) and stay, analyse as a modulation.

## Gotchas
- A secondary dominant that does not resolve to its target is a "deceptive" resolution and needs care (V7/vi to IV works; others can sound random).
- In minor keys V/III is just the diatonic VII (no accidental) - not a chromatic chord.

## Verify
- Every chromatic note in the secondary dominant resolves as its role demands (up for leading note, down for 7th).
- Play the passage: the target chord should sound briefly like a tonic.
