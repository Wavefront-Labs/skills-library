# Harmonising a melody (chorale style)

**Goal:** a correct, musical four-part harmonisation of a given soprano melody.

## 1. Analyse the melody
- Key, phrase ends (fermatas in chorales), any modulations (accidentals that imply a new leading note).
- Mark **cadences** at each phrase end: perfect (V-I) at the end; imperfect (to V) or perfect in related keys mid-way; interrupted (V-vi) for variety.

## 2. Bass line first
- Write the bass in **contrary motion** to the soprano where possible; mix steps and leaps; root position at cadences; first inversions for smooth stepwise bass motion.
- Check outer voices for parallel 5ths/8ves now; it's easiest to fix here.

## 3. Choose chords (one per beat in chorale style)
- For each melody note, list chords containing it (as root, 3rd or 5th), choose by function: tonic (I, vi), pre-dominant (ii, IV), dominant (V, vii dim).
- Harmonic rhythm: generally one chord per beat, change across the barline; avoid the same chord from weak to strong beat.
- Standard progressions: I-IV-V-I, I-ii6-V-I, I-vi-ii-V-I, cadential 6/4 (I6/4-V-I) at final cadences.

## 4. Inner voices (alto, tenor)
- Keep common tones; move by step; complete chords (root and 3rd at least; omit the 5th if needed).
- Spacing: no more than an octave between S-A and A-T. Ranges per voice (see SATB arranging skill).
- Doubling: root preferred; never double the leading note; in first inversion, double the soprano or bass note freely but avoid leading-note doubling.

## 5. Check list
- Parallel/consecutive 5ths and 8ves (also by contrary motion), hidden 5ths/8ves to outer voices, augmented 2nds (minor keys), leading-note resolution, 7ths resolving down.

## Gotchas
- Choosing chords left-to-right without planning cadences: dead ends at phrase endings.
- Root-position bass on every chord: a stiff, leaping bass line.

## Verify
- Play it: the bass is a good tune on its own; the cadences sound clear.
- Run the error checklist voice pair by voice pair (6 pairs).
