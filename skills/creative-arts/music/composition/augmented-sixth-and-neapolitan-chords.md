# Augmented sixth and Neapolitan chords

**Goal:** use the two classic chromatic pre-dominants and resolve them correctly to V.

## 1. Neapolitan (bII6)
- Major triad on the **lowered 2nd degree**, usually in **first inversion**: in C minor, Db-F-Ab with F in the bass.
- Function: pre-dominant (replaces iv or ii dim6). Progression: **N6 - (I6/4) - V - i**.
- Voice leading: **double the bass (4th degree)**. The b2 (Db) falls to the leading note B (a diminished 3rd) or to C over a cadential 6/4 then B.
- Common in minor; in major it is a borrowed colour.

## 2. Augmented sixths (in C minor; resolve to G)
All contain **Ab (b6) in the bass and F# (#4) above**: the augmented 6th expands outward to the octave G.
| Type | Notes | Added note |
|---|---|---|
| Italian (It+6) | Ab, C, F# | tonic doubled |
| French (Fr+6) | Ab, C, D, F# | 2nd degree (whole-tone sound) |
| German (Ger+6) | Ab, C, Eb, F# | b3 (sounds like Ab7) |

- Resolution: Ab down to G, F# up to G, others to chord tones of V.
- **German to V** creates parallel 5ths (Ab-Eb to G-D). Fix: resolve to **I6/4 first** (Eb holds), then V; or accept "Mozart fifths" between inner voices only where the style allows.
- In major keys, the same chords use b6 and b3 (borrowed) with #4.

## 3. Enharmonic use
- Ger+6 is enharmonic with a dominant 7th (Ab-C-Eb-Gb = Ab7). Resolving Ab7 as a dominant leads to Db: a route to distant keys.
- Fr+6 is a symmetrical sound used by Romantic and film composers for mystery.

## Gotchas
- Spelling: write F#, not Gb, or it's a dominant 7th, not an augmented 6th.
- Putting the augmented 6th interval between upper voices instead of bass and an upper voice weakens the effect (allowed but less idiomatic).

## Verify
- Check: b6 in bass, #4 in an upper voice, both move by semitone to the dominant octave.
- No parallel 5ths/octaves on the Ger+6 resolution unless deliberate.
