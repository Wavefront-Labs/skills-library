# Tuplets, syncopation and polyrhythm

**Goal:** notate irregular divisions and off-beat accents so they are read correctly first
time, and perform cross-rhythms accurately.

## 1. Tuplets
- A tuplet squeezes n notes into the time of m of the same value: triplet = 3 in the time of 2; duplet = 2 in the time of 3 (compound time); quintuplet = 5 in the time of 4.
- Notate with a number over the beam; use a **bracket** when the notes are not beamed together (crotchets, mixed rests). Write the ratio "5:4" or "3:2 crotchet" when the value is ambiguous.
- The tuplet's note value is the next value **down** from the space it fills unless more than double: a quintuplet over one crotchet uses semiquavers; seven in the time of a crotchet also uses semiquavers.
- Nested tuplets: write the outer ratio on the outer bracket; avoid unless the music truly needs it.

## 2. Syncopation that reads
- Syncopation = accent or onset off the beat, held through the beat. Show the beat: **tie across the beat** rather than writing a long note that starts off-beat and hides the next beat (in 4/4, quaver-crotchet-quaver across beat 2 is acceptable as a common pattern; anything longer, tie).
- Pop "anticipations" (a chord landing a quaver before the bar) are written as a tied quaver into the bar in charts; mark accent ">" where the push matters.
- In charts, show hits above the staff with rhythmic slash notation (see rhythm-chart skill).

## 3. Cross-rhythms
- **3:2** — find the common pulse (6): the 2-group plays on pulses 1 and 4; the 3-group on 1, 3 and 5. Say "not-dif-fi-cult": both hands on "not" (1), 3-group on "dif" (3), 2-group on "fi" (4), 3-group on "cult" (5). The usual mnemonic for 4:3 is "pass the gol-den but-ter".
- **4:3** — common pulse 12: 4-group on 1,4,7,10; 3-group on 1,5,9.
- Practise each line alone against a click of the common pulse, then combine, then remove the pulse.
- **Hemiola** — two bars of 3/4 felt as three bars of 2/4 (or one bar of 3/2); standard at Baroque cadences. Notate with ties/accents rather than changing the signature, unless the passage is long.

## Gotchas
- Writing a triplet when you meant swung quavers: in jazz charts use "swing" marking, not triplets.
- Tuplets that cross the middle of a 4/4 bar are hard to read; split or re-bar.
- Over-syncopating every bar removes the reference beat; anchor with downbeats in another part.

## Verify
- Play the passage in a notation program at slow tempo: playback should match the intended feel.
- Tap cross-rhythms against a metronome set to the common pulse until both hands hit the shared downbeat cleanly at full tempo.
