# Running a spotting session and cue list

**Goal:** leave the room with a cue list everyone has agreed: where each cue starts,
where it ends, and what it has to do dramatically.

## 1. Before the session
- Get a locked (or near-locked) cut with **burnt-in timecode** (BITC) and the session
  frame rate (23.976, 24, 25, 29.97 DF/NDF). Confirm the rate in writing; a wrong rate
  drifts every hit point.
- Watch the whole film once without stopping, noting only emotional reactions.
- Ask for the temp score and the director's notes on what they like and dislike about it.

## 2. In the room (director, editor, composer, music editor)
For each candidate cue, agree:
1. **In point** - the frame or action where music enters (often under a cut, a look,
   a door, or a line of dialogue ending, not on a random frame).
2. **Out point** - hard out, fade out, or segue into the next cue.
3. **Function** - tension, comedy, character, geography, pace, emotional release.
4. **Source vs score** - is the music in the world (radio, band) or underscore?
5. **Must-hit points** - cuts or actions that need a musical event.
Ask "what should the audience feel here?" rather than "what instruments?".

## 3. Cue numbering
Standard form is reel/act + sequence + type: `1M01`, `1M02`, `2M01` (reel 1, music 1).
Source cues are often `1S01`. Keep numbers stable; a cue added later becomes `1M02A`
so the paperwork never renumbers.

## 4. The cue list / spotting notes
| Cue | Title | In (TC) | Out (TC) | Length | Notes |
|-----|-------|---------|----------|--------|-------|
| 1M01 | Main Title | 01:00:12:04 | 01:01:40:10 | 1:28 | Hold back, reveal theme at logo |
Distribute within 24 hours; the music editor usually owns the master version.

## Gotchas
- Spotting from an unlocked cut means re-spotting; agree a re-spot pass in the schedule.
- Temp love: if the director is attached to temp, ask what quality they love (pulse,
  colour, tempo) rather than copying it.
- Do not score every scene; silence makes the cues that remain land harder.

## Verify
- [ ] Every cue has in/out timecode, length, function and must-hit notes.
- [ ] Frame rate and timecode start (often 01:00:00:00) are confirmed.
- [ ] Director, editor and composer have signed off on the list.
