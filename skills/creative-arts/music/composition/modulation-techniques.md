# Modulation techniques

**Goal:** move to a new key in a way that sounds intentional, then confirm it.

## 1. Pivot-chord (common-chord) modulation
1. List the diatonic chords of the old and new key; chords in both lists are **pivots**. C major to G major: C, Em, Am, G are shared.
2. Approach the pivot as a chord of the old key, leave it as a chord of the new key (Am = vi in C = ii in G), then go to the new key's **V-I** (D7-G).
3. Analyse with a bracket: vi = ii.
- Best for closely related keys (one accidental apart).

## 2. Direct (phrase) modulation
- End a phrase in the old key, start the next phrase in the new key with no pivot. Common between sections (verse in A minor, chorus in C major).
- Works for distant keys if the phrase boundary is clear.

## 3. Common-tone modulation
- Hold one note (melody or inner voice) while the harmony around it moves to a chord in a distant key: C major chord, hold E, move to E major or C# minor (E is common).
- Romantic and film staple for "lift" or surprise.

## 4. Enharmonic modulation
- Reinterpret a **diminished 7th** (any of its 4 notes can be the leading note: four possible keys) or a **German augmented 6th** (respelled as a dominant 7th) to land in a distant key.

## 5. Pop key changes
- **Truck-driver** lift: final chorus up a semitone or whole tone, often prepared by the new key's V (one bar) or simply direct. Prepare with V7 of the new key for a smoother lift.
- Up a minor 3rd via its dominant for a bigger lift; check the singer's range first.

## 6. Confirming the new key
A modulation is real when the new key gets a **cadence (V-I)**, ideally with its leading note in the melody. Without that, it's a tonicisation.

## Gotchas
- Pivot on a chord that only exists in one key: the listener hears a jolt, not a pivot.
- Modulating up for the last chorus without checking range ruins the vocal.
- In exams, missing accidental for the new leading note is the most common error.

## Verify
- Mark where the old key's leading note (or signature note) is cancelled and the new one introduced.
- Play from the modulation to the end: does the final cadence sound like home in the new key?
