# Tritone substitution and the ii-V-I

**Goal:** recognise, voice and reharmonise with ii-V-I and its common substitutions.

## 1. The ii-V-I
- Major: Dm7 - G7 - Cmaj7. Minor: Dm7b5 - G7(b9) - Cm(maj7)/Cm6.
- **Guide tones** (3rds and 7ths) move by step or stay. Line 1: F (3rd of Dm7) -> F (7th of G7) -> E (3rd of Cmaj7). Line 2: C (7th of Dm7) -> B (3rd of G7) -> B (7th of Cmaj7). Voice these two lines and the progression plays itself.
- Recognise it everywhere in standards: most bridges are chains of ii-Vs to new keys.

## 2. Tritone substitution (subV7)
- Any dominant 7th can be replaced by the dominant 7th a **tritone away**: G7 -> Db7.
- Why it works: G7 and Db7 share the **tritone** B-F (Cb-F); the 3rd and 7th swap roles.
- Result: chromatic bass descending to the root: Dm7 - Db7 - Cmaj7.
- Extend: ii of the subV (Abm7 - Db7 - Cmaj7) for a related ii-V a tritone away.
- Melody check: the melody note must fit the sub (over Db7: G = #11 fine; B = b7 fine; A = #9-ish, avoid on strong beats unless altered).

## 3. Other common substitutions
- **Diatonic substitutes**: iii for I, vi for I (Em7 for Cmaj7).
- **Backdoor**: Fm7 - Bb7 - Cmaj7 (iv-bVII7-I).
- **Secondary ii-Vs** in front of any target (related ii; see secondary-dominants skill).
- **Diminished passing chords**: Cmaj7 - C#dim7 - Dm7.
- **Coltrane changes** (major-third cycles) for advanced reharmonisation.

## 4. Reharmonising a standard (method)
1. Write the melody and original changes. 2. Mark structural cadences (keep their targets).
3. Substitute only where the melody note is a chord tone or good tension of the new chord.
4. Check bass line: chromatic or stepwise motion usually means it works.

## Gotchas
- Subbing every V7 gets monotonous; use subs where the bass motion helps.
- Comping a subV under a soloist who plays the original V7 lines: tell the band, or keep subs to arranged sections.

## Verify
- Guide-tone line check: 3rds and 7ths move by half/whole step or hold.
- Every melody note over a sub chord is a chord tone or an available tension.
