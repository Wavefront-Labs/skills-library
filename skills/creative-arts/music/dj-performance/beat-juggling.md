# Beat juggling

**Goal:** rearrange a break live, using two copies of the same record (or two decks of the
same file), in time with the beat.

## 1. Setup
- Two identical records ("doubles") or DVS with the same track on both decks; sharp crossfader curve.
- Mark the first kick of the break on both copies (stickers on the label at the cue point, or hot cues).
- Pitch both decks the same (or Sync off and equal pitch fader).

## 2. Back-cueing
- Hold the record and spin/drag it back to the marker by feel and sticker position while the other deck plays; this is the core skill.
- Practise until you can bring one deck back to its marker in under one bar.

## 3. Basic loop juggle
1. Deck A plays beat 1-2 of the break; crossfader on A.
2. Just before A reaches the end of the phrase, release deck B from its marker on the downbeat and cut the crossfader to B.
3. While B plays, back-cue A to the marker.
4. Repeat, alternating: an infinite loop of a 1- or 2-bar phrase.
- Then vary: play 1 beat from A + 1 from B for stutters; use B's offset to create new patterns.

## 4. Spinbacks and strobing
- **Spinback**: spin the record backwards with a flick to rewind with an audible effect, landing on the marker.
- **Strobe**: release and stop the decks alternately in rapid short bursts (on beats) for a stuttering effect.

## 5. With DVS or controllers
- Hot cues set to the drum hits replace back-cueing (finger drumming juggle). Many purists still back-cue with timecode vinyl, using hot cues as a safety net.

## 6. Building a routine
- Choose a break with clear kick/snare; plan a 2-4 bar sequence; mark it on a sheet; rehearse with a metronome until automatic; layer scratches over the juggle.

## Gotchas
- Pitch drift between decks causes flams; set pitch faders identically and check.
- Releasing late (after the beat) - release slightly ahead to account for platter start-up (motor torque high).

## Verify
- Record a 16-bar loop juggle: the loop sounds continuous and in time with no gaps or doubled hits.
