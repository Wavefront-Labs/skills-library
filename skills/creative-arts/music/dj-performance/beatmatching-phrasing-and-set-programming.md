# Beatmatching, phrasing and set programming

**Goal:** blend records so the dancefloor never notices the joins, and shape the night
so energy rises and falls on purpose.

## 1. Beatmatching by ear
Even with sync buttons, learn this — sync fails on live-played, old or badly
analysed tracks.
1. Track A plays to the room. Cue track B in headphones on its first downbeat.
2. Release B on A's downbeat (or nudge the platter/jog into time).
3. Listen: if B's kick runs *ahead* (flamming before A's), slow B with the **pitch
   fader**; if behind, speed it up. Use the jog wheel/platter edge for small
   corrections, the pitch fader for the actual tempo.
4. When aligned for 16+ bars without nudging, the tempos match.
- Practise with simple four-to-the-floor tracks at the same genre and similar BPM.
- **Split-cue or one-ear** headphone monitoring: one ear on the headphones, the other
  on the booth monitor.

## 2. Phrasing
Most dance music is built in **phrases of 8, 16 or 32 bars**. Start the incoming
track's intro so that its first big change (drop, vocal, bassline) lands where the
outgoing track's phrase ends. A beatmatched but off-phrase mix still sounds wrong.
- Count bars or use the waveform/phrase markers in rekordbox, Serato or Traktor.
- Mix outros into intros — they're designed for it.

## 3. Transitions
- **EQ blend / bass swap:** bring B in with its low EQ cut; at a phrase point, swap —
  cut A's bass, restore B's. Two basslines at once cause mud and level spikes.
- **Cut/drop mix:** instant switch on the "one" of a phrase; good for genre or tempo
  changes.
- **Echo out / filter:** loop or echo the end of A while B starts — for tracks with no
  mixable outro.
- Keep transitions **as long as the music allows**, not as long as possible.

## 4. Harmonic mixing
- Tracks in compatible keys blend cleanly. The **Camelot wheel** maps keys to codes
  (e.g. 8A = A minor, 8B = C major); adjacent numbers or the same number A/B are
  compatible.
- Software key detection is often good but not perfect — trust your ears.
- With key lock ("master tempo") on, large pitch changes still introduce artifacts.

## 5. Gain staging and sound
- Set each channel's **gain/trim** so the channel meters sit about the same; use the
  channel faders for mixing, not for loudness.
- Keep the master out of the red; a clipping club system sounds harsh and can trigger
  limiters or damage speakers.
- Booth monitor at a sensible level — if it's louder than the floor you'll mix
  badly and damage hearing. Wear earplugs.

## 6. Set programming and crowd reading
- **Warm-up DJs build, not peak:** play below the headliner's energy and tempo.
- Plan an **energy curve**: rises, a breather, a higher peak. Don't play every big
  track in the first hour.
- Prepare crates by energy and mood rather than a fixed running order; read the room
  and adapt.
- Watch the floor: feet and the back of the room tell more than the front row.

## Gotchas
- Bad analysis/beatgrid in software = sync drifts; check grids before the gig.
- Bring your music on **two USB sticks** (and correct format — check the venue
  CDJ model's supported file systems).
- Uncertain: phrasing conventions vary by genre (e.g. hip-hop and open-format mix
  differently from house/techno).

## Verify
- [ ] Hold a manual beatmatch 32 bars without nudging.
- [ ] Mixes land on phrase changes.
- [ ] No moment with two full basslines (recorded mix check).
- [ ] Master meter stays out of the red all set.
- [ ] Backup USB and an energy plan prepared before arrival.
