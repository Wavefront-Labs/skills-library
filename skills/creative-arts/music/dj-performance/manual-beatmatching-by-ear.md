# Manual beatmatching by ear

**Goal:** two tracks locked in tempo and phase using only your ears and hands.

## 1. Cue the incoming track
- In the headphones, find the **first kick of a phrase** (downbeat) on the incoming track; set a cue point there (CDJ: Cue button while paused; vinyl: hold the record at that kick).
- Pitch fader range: +/-6% or +/-8% for most mixing (finer resolution); +/-16% for big tempo gaps.

## 2. Start on the beat
- Release the incoming track exactly on a downbeat of the playing track (a phrase start is ideal).

## 3. Diagnose fast or slow
- Listen to both kicks (headphones in one ear, booth/main in the other, or **split cue** on the mixer: cue in one side, master in the other).
- **Galloping** kicks (double hits) mean they're out of phase; if the incoming kick drifts **ahead**, the incoming track is **faster**: lower its pitch. If it drifts **behind**, raise pitch.
- Correct phase with a **nudge**: touch the jog side / platter edge to slow it, or push the label / spin the jog forward to speed up briefly.

## 4. Fine-tune and ride
- After a phase correction, adjust the pitch fader in tiny steps so it stops drifting. Repeat: nudge for phase, fader for tempo.
- During the mix, keep checking; vinyl and older recordings drift, so ride the pitch (small fader moves) throughout.
- BPM displays help as a starting point, but trust your ears; displayed BPMs may differ by fractions.

## 5. Practice drills
- Two copies of the same track: match them by ear from different start points.
- Then two tracks of the same genre with small BPM differences (1-3 bpm).
- Practise without looking at the screen waveforms.

## Gotchas
- Correcting the pitch fader when the problem is phase (or vice versa): fix phase with the jog, tempo with the fader.
- Using Master Tempo/key lock on extreme pitch changes: artefacts.
- Monitoring too loud: ear fatigue and lost timing judgement.

## Verify
- Hold the beatmatch for 32 bars without touching the controls once it's set.
- Recorded mix: no flamming kicks in transitions.
