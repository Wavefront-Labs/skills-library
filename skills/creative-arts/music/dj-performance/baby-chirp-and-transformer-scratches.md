# Baby, chirp and transformer scratches

**Goal:** clean, rhythmic foundation scratches that every other scratch is built from.

## 1. Setup
- **Battle mixer** (DJM-S series, Rane Seventy, etc.) or controller with a sharp crossfader: set the crossfader curve to **sharp/cut** (the sound fully on within a millimetre) and cut-in gap minimal.
- Skip-proof needles/cartridges (Ortofon Concorde Scratch, Shure M44-7 style) with correct counterweight; slipmat on the platter.
- A scratch sample: a short vocal "ahh" or "fresh" at the start of the record/timecode cue. Mark the start point (sticker or hot cue).
- Platter can keep spinning under the slipmat while you hold the record.

## 2. The scratches
| Scratch | Record hand | Fader hand |
|---|---|---|
| **Baby** | push forward and pull back over the sample | fader open (no cuts) |
| **Forward** | push forward (sound), pull back | **cut** the backward motion (close on the way back) |
| **Backward (reverse)** | push forward silently, pull back with sound | cut the forward motion |
| **Tear** | split one stroke into two motions (forward in one move, back in two) | open |
| **Chirp** | quick forward push, then pull back | fader **open as each stroke starts and snapped closed as it ends** (close during the push, reopen for the pull, close again): two short "chirps" per cycle with the tails cut |
| **Transformer** | steady slow push/pull (or let the record play) | tap the fader open-closed rhythmically (e.g. 16ths) |


## 3. Practice routine
- Metronome or a simple 90 bpm drum loop.
- Each scratch in quarter notes, then 8ths, then triplets, 2 minutes each.
- Record yourself; listen for timing against the beat and clean cuts (no "bleed" of the backward sound when it should be cut).

## Gotchas
- Pressing down on the record too hard: drag, skipping. Light touch at the edge of the sample label area.
- Loose or worn crossfader causes bleed; check the cut-in setting.
- Mixer upfader/crossfader reverse (hamster) settings: know which way your fader is set.

## Verify
- Forward scratches: hear only forward strokes on the recording.
- Transformer: even, rhythmic gaps locked to the beat.
