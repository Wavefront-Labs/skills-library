# Flare and crab scratches

**Goal:** fast, clean crossfader patterns that cut the sound more times per stroke than
transforms or chirps.

## 1. Flares (invented by DJ Flare)
- Start with the fader **open**; a "click" is a quick close-open (bounce) of the fader off the cut-in point.
- **One-click flare**: record forward stroke with one click in the middle (sound-click-sound), back stroke with one click: each stroke heard as two sounds.
- **Two-click flare**: two clicks per stroke (three sounds per stroke).
- Technique: thumb rests on the closed side of the fader cap/mixer edge as a spring; the index finger flicks the fader against the thumb and it bounces open.
- **Orbit**: the same flare pattern on forward and back strokes continuously.

## 2. Twiddle
- Thumb and index (or middle) fingers alternate tapping the fader from opposite sides; fader starts open and closes briefly with each finger tap.

## 3. Crab
- Fader starts **closed**. Thumb rests on the fader's open side; the fingers (index, middle, ring, little, in sequence, e.g. from little to index) tap the fader into the thumb, each tap opening the sound briefly. The thumb acts as a spring pushing it back closed.
- 2-finger, 3-finger and 4-finger crabs produce 2-4 sounds per sweep; very fast.
- Fader tension: lighter feels quicker for crabs; set the mixer's fader feel/tension accordingly.

## 4. Drills
1. Fader only, no record: clicks to a metronome (8ths, 16ths), then triplets.
2. Add a slow baby scratch with one click per stroke.
3. Increase clicks and record-hand speed in small steps; record and review.
4. Combine: flare into crab into transformer phrases over a beat.

## Gotchas
- Tension in the wrist and forearm limits speed; the motion is from fingers.
- Fader cut-in too wide: clicks become mushy or silent.
- Practising speed without timing: scratches must sit on the beat.

## Verify
- Recording at slow speed: the count of sounds per stroke equals the intended pattern.
- Crabs: each finger produces an audible, distinct cut.
