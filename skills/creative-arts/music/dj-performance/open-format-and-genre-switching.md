# Open format and genre switching

**Goal:** keep a mixed crowd dancing across genres and tempos with smooth or exciting
switches.

## 1. Library for open format
- **DJ edits / intro versions** (clean 8-16 bar intros/outros), clean and dirty versions, acapellas and instrumentals (record pools like DJcity, BPM Supreme).
- Crates by genre **and** by tempo band (70-80/140-160 hip-hop and trap, 90-105, 115-130 house/pop, 170+ D&B).

## 2. Moving between tempos
- **Half/double-time links**: 70 bpm hip-hop mixes into 140 bpm material; 87 into 174 D&B.
- **Gradual tempo drift**: nudge the tempo a few bpm per track toward the next genre.
- **Hard switch**: echo out, spinback or quick cut on a strong downbeat (see transitions skill); the bigger the tempo jump, the more decisive the switch should be.
- **Acapella bridge**: play an acapella over the next genre's beat, then bring in the full track.

## 3. Reading mixed crowds
- Watch who is dancing and who isn't; test a genre with one recognisable track before committing.
- Respond in 1-3 track blocks per genre; return to proven floor-fillers when energy drops.
- Weddings/events: agree must-plays and do-not-plays in advance; announce key moments with the MC.

## 4. Technique details
- Keep transitions short (8-16 bars or cuts) in open format; long blends of mismatched genres clash.
- Key matters for blends and acapellas; for cuts, less so.
- Use clean versions where required; check explicit content before events.

## Gotchas
- Too many genre switches in a row confuses the floor; build blocks.
- Tempo-stretching a track far from its natural BPM (more than about 6-8%) sounds wrong.

## Verify
- Recorded set: tempo/genre changes land cleanly on beat; the floor stays full through switches (or recovers within one track).
