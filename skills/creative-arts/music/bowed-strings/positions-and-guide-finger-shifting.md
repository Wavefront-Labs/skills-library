# Positions and guide-finger shifting

**Goal:** know the positions and shift accurately with controlled or invisible slides.

## 1. Position geography
- Violin/viola: 1st position = first finger a whole/half step above the nut; 3rd
  position = first finger where 3rd finger was in 1st; up to 7th and beyond.
- Cello: 1st to 4th position in the neck, then thumb position.
- Learn pitches by finger and string in each position, and the hand frame (the shape
  within a position).

## 2. Shifting types
- **Guide-finger (old-finger) shift:** slide on the finger that played the last note to
  its new position, then place the new finger. Classical default; slide is inaudible
  if light and fast.
- **New-finger shift:** slide on the finger that will play the next note (expressive,
  more audible - Romantic style).
- **Same-finger shift:** finger shifts with itself, audible portamento for expression.

## 3. Technique
- Lighten finger pressure during the shift.
- Move the whole arm and thumb together.
- Slow the bow or lighten it during the slide to hide it.
- Practise with the slide audible first to learn the distance, then hide it.

## 4. Exercises
- Shifting exercises (Sevcik Op. 8, Yost, Cossmann for cello).
- Scales in one string (one-finger scales).

## Gotchas
- Thumb left behind in the old position.
- Pressing hard during shifts creates audible slides.
- Not knowing the target pitch in advance.

## Verify
- [ ] Shifts land in tune on recordings.
- [ ] Slides audible only when intended.
- [ ] Hand frame stable in each position.
