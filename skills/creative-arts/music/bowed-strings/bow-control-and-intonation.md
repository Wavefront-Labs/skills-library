# Bow control and intonation

**Goal:** produce a consistent, resonant tone with the bow and play in tune, the two
things that separate a string player who sounds "good" from one who sounds
"scratchy" — on violin, viola, cello and double bass alike.

## 1. Bow hold
- Thumb bent, its tip touching the stick near the frog; fingers draped over the stick,
  little finger curved on top (violin/viola) or relaxed alongside (cello).
- Double bass: French bow (similar to cello) or German bow (held like a saw handle) —
  choose with your teacher and stick with it.
- The hold should be **flexible**: fingers flex at the frog and straighten at the tip.
  A rigid hold is the most common cause of a choked sound.

## 2. Tone = speed × weight × contact point
The three variables must match each other:
- **Contact (sounding) point:** where the bow meets the string between bridge and
  fingerboard. Closer to the bridge = brighter, needs more weight and less speed.
  Over the fingerboard = soft, airy, needs less weight and more speed.
- **Bow speed:** fast = more air, can sound thin if weight is too low.
- **Bow weight** (arm weight into the string, not finger pressure).
- **Crunch = too much weight for the speed; whistle/skate = too little weight or too
  close to the fingerboard.**
- Exercise (Fischer-style): sustained open strings at five contact points, 8 slow
  beats each, adjusting weight to keep the tone constant.
- **Straight bow:** keep the bow parallel to the bridge. Practise in a mirror; the
  arm needs to extend forward at the tip.

## 3. Core strokes
- **Détaché:** separate, smooth, on-string strokes — the default.
- **Legato:** several notes slurred in one bow; plan bow distribution so you don't run
  out.
- **Martelé:** "hammered" — weight set before the stroke, quick release, stop.
- **Spiccato:** off-the-string bouncing; around the balance point of the bow.
- Mark **bowings** (⊓ down-bow, V up-bow) in your part; in a section, everyone bows
  together, following the principal.

## 4. Intonation
- Tune open strings to A = 440 Hz (or the ensemble's reference) using fifths.
- **Drones:** play scales against a sustained tonic drone (app or keyboard); listen for
  beats (wobbling) and adjust until they disappear.
- **Ringing tones:** on violin, notes like G, D, A, E stopped on adjacent strings make
  open strings vibrate sympathetically when in tune — a built-in check.
- **Tuning by function:** leading tones slightly high, minor thirds a touch lower in
  expressive/melodic playing; in ensembles match the chords. (Just vs. equal
  temperament: be aware, not dogmatic.)
- Practise slowly with **no vibrato** first; vibrato hides wrong pitches.

## 5. Shifting
- Shift on the **old finger** with a light "guide" touch along the string, release
  pressure during the shift, arrive, then place the new finger.
- Slow shifts audibly first; then make them inaudible at tempo.

## Gotchas
- Too much rosin = gritty sound; too little = slipping. Re-rosin every few sessions,
  not every time.
- Bridge tilting forward over time can collapse it — check it's upright when tuning.
- Uncertain: bass and cello bow holds vary between schools; follow a teacher.

## Verify
- [ ] Sustained open strings at each contact point with even tone.
- [ ] Bow stays parallel to the bridge in a mirror check.
- [ ] Scale against a drone without audible beating.
- [ ] Shifts silent at performance tempo.
- [ ] Bowings marked and matched with your section.
