# Developing string vibrato

**Goal:** a relaxed, controllable vibrato with variable speed and width.

## 1. Types
- **Arm vibrato** (from the elbow): used on cello and bass, and by many violinists in
  higher positions. Wider, warmer.
- **Wrist (hand) vibrato:** violin and viola; motion from the wrist with the forearm
  stable.
- **Finger vibrato:** narrow, fast; for subtle colour.
Most players combine them.

## 2. Mechanics
- The finger rolls back from the pitch and returns (vibrato goes below the pitch, not
  above, on the violin family).
- Thumb relaxed; the hand moves as a unit.

## 3. Exercises
- Rhythmic oscillations with a metronome: 2, 3, 4 per beat at 60 bpm, then faster.
- Without the bow first, then with long bows.
- Each finger separately; the 4th finger needs extra work.

## 4. Musical use
- Speed and width vary with dynamics and character: wider and slower for warm, narrower
  and faster for intensity.
- Continuous vibrato through note changes in Romantic style; less in early music.

## Gotchas
- Tension in the thumb stops vibrato.
- Vibrato above the pitch sounds sharp.
- Constant same vibrato in all music.

## Verify
- [ ] Rhythmic vibrato with each finger at several speeds.
- [ ] Pitch centre stays in tune.
- [ ] Vibrato varies with phrasing.
