# Jazz pizzicato and fiddle styles

**Goal:** idiomatic jazz bass pizzicato and fiddle bowing styles.

## 1. Jazz pizzicato (upright bass)
- Pluck with the side of the index (or index and middle) using arm weight, not just the
  finger.
- Pluck near the end of the fingerboard for warmth and sustain.
- Let notes ring for walking lines; the left hand holds the note fully.
- Amplify with a pickup or mic; set the amp low.

## 2. Fiddle styles (violin)
- **Shuffle bowing:** "Nashville shuffle" - long-short-short patterns with accents on
  the off-beats.
- **Double stops:** drone strings with the melody.
- **Slides and ornaments:** grace notes, slides into notes, cuts and rolls (Irish).
- **Chop:** percussive scratch near the frog for rhythm (bluegrass, contemporary
  string bands).

## 3. Learning
By ear: learn tunes from recordings, slowed down. Each style (Irish, Scottish,
bluegrass, Cajun) has different ornament and bowing conventions.

## Gotchas
- Classical-style pizzicato on upright sounds thin in jazz.
- Fiddle tunes learned from sheet music without ornaments sound wooden.
- Chop too heavy masks rhythm.

## Verify
- [ ] Pizzicato notes sustain and are even.
- [ ] Shuffle bowing groove with a backing track.
- [ ] Ornaments match the style.
