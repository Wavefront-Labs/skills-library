# String crossings and bariolage

**Goal:** even tone through crossings, without bumps or accents.

## 1. Arm levels
Each string has a bow level (arm height). Moving between strings is a change of arm
level: whole arm for slow crossings, more forearm and wrist for fast ones.

## 2. Anticipation
- Start moving toward the next string just before the change, so the bow arrives at
  the new level as the note starts.
- Keep the bow close to both strings in fast alternation.

## 3. Bariolage
Rapid alternation between a stopped note and an open string (or two strings), often with
the same pitch on two strings. Use a small, circular arm/wrist motion; keep the contact
point constant.

## 4. Exercises
- Open-string crossings in slurs of 2, 4, 8.
- Détaché crossings, then slurred, then mixed.
- Arpeggio crossings across three or four strings.
- Simon Fischer's string-crossing exercises in Basics.

## Gotchas
- Lifting the bow or jerking the elbow creates accents.
- Crossing too early clips the previous note.
- Contact point drifts during crossings.

## Verify
- [ ] Even tone on recordings of crossings.
- [ ] No accents unless marked.
- [ ] Bariolage at tempo with steady rhythm.
