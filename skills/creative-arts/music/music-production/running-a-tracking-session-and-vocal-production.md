# Running a tracking session and vocal production

**Goal:** leave a session with performances that are better than the artist expected,
organised so the mix engineer can start immediately.

## 1. Pre-production (before anyone sets up a mic)
- **Lock the song:** structure, key, tempo. Record a guide demo to a click; try the key
  with the singer at full volume — keys chosen at the sofa are often too low on stage.
- **Arrangement pass:** what does each section add? Remove parts that compete with the
  vocal (the most common arrangement problem is too many mid-range parts).
- **References:** pick 2–3 released tracks for sound and feel; agree them with the
  artist so "warmer" and "bigger" have a shared meaning.
- **Tempo map** in the DAW if the song changes tempo; build the click/cue to suit
  (count-ins, cowbell vs. click, accents on 1).
- Prepare a **session template**: tracks named, inputs routed, cue mixes set.

## 2. Running the tracking day
- Order: **rhythm section first** (drums, bass, guide guitar/keys, guide vocal), then
  overdubs. Some genres track live together — decide by what the music needs.
- **Gain staging:** record with healthy level and headroom — peaks around −10 to −6 dBFS
  at 24-bit is a common working target; there's no benefit to recording "hot".
- **Cue (headphone) mixes:** performers play to what they hear. Ask each one what they
  want more and less of; a bad cue mix ruins takes.
- **Keep energy up:** minimise technical downtime in front of the artist; set up the
  next mic during playback.

## 3. Directing performers
- Give **one note at a time**, specific and positive ("lean on the word 'down', less
  breath on the long note") rather than "do it better".
- Use feel language for emotion, technical language for fixes.
- Know when to stop: vocals and lead parts often peak within the first few takes,
  then decline with fatigue. Take a break rather than push.
- Keep the guide/"vibe" takes — first takes often have something the polished ones
  lose.

## 4. Vocal production
- Record **3–5 full takes** to playlists/take lanes, then targeted drop-ins for problem
  lines.
- **Comp** line by line (or word by word): choose for emotion first, pitch and timing
  second; tuning can fix small pitch, not lack of feeling.
- Record **doubles, harmonies and ad-libs** after the lead comp so they match its
  phrasing.
- Mark breaths and plosives to clean later, but leave breaths that carry emotion.
- Keep a **lyric sheet** marked with which take each line came from.

## 5. Session hygiene
- Name tracks before recording (*KickIn*, *LeadVox*) so files are named correctly.
- Write session notes: mic, preamp, settings, tempo, key, sample rate.
- Back up at the end of every day to two places.
- Export **consolidated stems** from bar 1 at session sample rate for the mixer.

## Gotchas
- Sample rate or buffer changes mid-session cause sync problems; fix them at setup.
- Latency in headphones makes performers drag — monitor via low-latency/direct paths.
- Uncertain: level targets and take counts are common practice, not rules.

## Verify
- [ ] Tempo, key and arrangement agreed and documented before the session.
- [ ] Every track named before recording; notes and backups done daily.
- [ ] Each performer approved their cue mix.
- [ ] Vocal comp sheet completed; doubles match the comp.
- [ ] Stems export from bar 1 and line up in a fresh session.
