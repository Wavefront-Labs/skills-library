# Recording acoustic guitar, piano and strings

**Goal:** natural-sounding acoustic recordings that fit a mix.

## 1. Acoustic guitar
- **Mono**: small-diaphragm condenser (KM184, SM81) **15-30 cm** from the guitar, pointed where the **neck meets the body (12th-14th fret)**. Warmer: angle toward the soundhole; brighter: toward the neck.
- Never point straight at the soundhole closely: boomy.
- **Stereo**: ORTF/XY at the 12th fret, or one mic at the 12th fret + one behind the bridge (lower bout) for body; or one at the fret + one over the player's shoulder (player's perspective) - check phase.
- Strumming in pop: thin it with a high-pass around 100-150 Hz in the mix; fingerpicking solo: keep the body.
- Fresh strings (a day old) and a quiet room; ask the player to stop tapping feet and breathing loudly near the mic.

## 2. Grand piano
- Lid on full stick. **Pop/rock (close)**: two mics inside, ~20-30 cm above the strings, one over the low-mid strings near the hammers, one over the high strings; or spaced pair along the hammer line. Pan low left/high right (player's perspective) or audience perspective, consistently.
- **Classical (distant)**: a pair (spaced omnis or ORTF) 1.5-3 m from the curve of the case, at lid height or above.
- Mics near the hammers = attack and clarity; further toward the tail = warmth.

## 3. Upright piano
- Open the top lid (and remove the front panel above the keys if possible); pair of mics above the top, 20-30 cm, spaced over low and high; or behind the soundboard for warmth (untreated rooms add mud).

## 4. Solo strings (violin, cello)
- Violin: mic **above** the instrument (0.5-1 m), pointing at the f-holes from above - the sound radiates upward; close mics sound scratchy.
- Cello: 0.5-1 m in front, aimed between the bridge and the end of the fingerboard.
- Ribbon or small/large condenser; room adds bloom.

## Gotchas
- Phase between multiple mics on one instrument: check mono and polarity.
- Piano pedal and bench noise; keep the mics away from the pedal mechanism or EQ out the thump.
- Too close on any acoustic instrument loses the instrument's whole radiation and exaggerates parts of it.

## Verify
- Compare with the instrument in the room: the recording should sound like the instrument, not a part of it.
- Mono check on stereo setups.
