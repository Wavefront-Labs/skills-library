# Reference-track analysis for arrangement

**Goal:** learn the arrangement decisions behind successful records in your genre and apply
them deliberately.

## 1. Choose references
- 2-3 tracks in the same genre, tempo range and era as your target release; at least one by the audience's current favourites.
- Buy/download high-quality files (not low-bitrate streams).

## 2. Map it in the DAW
1. Import into a new track, set the project tempo to the reference (warp/tempo-detect), align bar 1.
2. Add **locators/markers** at every section change; note bar counts (intro 8, verse 16, pre 8, chorus 16...).
3. Make an **instrumentation grid**: rows = instruments/elements, columns = sections; tick what plays where.
4. Note **energy changes**: drops, half-time, filter moments, stops, key changes.
5. Note **ear candy**: one-off FX, vocal chops, ad-libs, reverse sounds - where and how often.
6. Time stamps of the first chorus and hook (pop: first chorus often before 0:50-1:00).

## 3. Apply it
- Use the grid as a template for your song's sections: not copying parts, but matching density and timing conventions.
- Check your song's first-chorus time and intro length against the references.
- Where your arrangement differs, make it a conscious choice.

## 4. A/B during production
- Keep the reference on a track routed to the monitors directly (bypassing your master processing), level-matched by LUFS.
- Flip between them at the same section to compare density and impact.

## Gotchas
- Comparing at different loudness: louder always sounds better; match levels first.
- Copying a reference too closely (melody, distinctive parts) risks plagiarism; analyse structure, not content.

## Verify
- Your arrangement grid and the reference grid side by side: section counts and density curves similar or deliberately different.
- Blind A/B with a friend: does your track hold up section by section?
