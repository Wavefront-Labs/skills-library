# Recording vocals

**Goal:** a clean, consistent, intimate lead vocal with the room under control.

## 1. Room first
- Untreated rooms add boxy reflections that you can't remove later. Record in the **deadest space**. The most important surface is the wall **behind the singer**, because its reflections arrive at the front (most sensitive side) of a cardioid mic: hang duvets or acoustic panels there.
- Reflection filters behind the mic help less than absorbing the wall behind the singer.

## 2. Mic choice
- **Large-diaphragm condenser** (U87, TLM 103, AT4050) for most pop/ballad vocals: detail and air.
- **Dynamic** (SM7B, RE20, SM58) for loud rock singers, untreated rooms, or a darker, closer sound.
- Try 2-3 mics on the singer and pick by ear; the right mic for this voice matters more than price.

## 3. Placement
- Mouth **15-25 cm** from the capsule; **pop filter** 5-10 cm in front of the mic.
- Capsule at mouth or nose height, angled slightly down toward the mouth (reduces plosives and sibilance aimed at the capsule).
- Closer = more bass (**proximity effect**) and intimacy; further = more room and natural tone.
- Mark the singer's position (tape on the floor); consistency helps comping.

## 4. Gain and chain
- Preamp gain so loudest phrases peak around **-10 to -6 dBFS** (24-bit); quiet verses can sit at -20; don't chase loud levels.
- High-pass at 60-80 Hz (mic or preamp) to cut rumble and handling noise.
- Optional gentle compression on the way in (2:1-3:1, 2-4 dB gain reduction) only if you're confident; otherwise record clean and compress later.

## 5. The performance
- **Headphone mix**: the singer's voice loud enough with a little reverb; click and key instruments clear. Closed-back headphones to avoid bleed.
- One ear off for some singers to hear themselves acoustically.
- Record multiple full takes, then sections; keep every take (see comping skill).
- Water at room temperature; breaks every 30-45 minutes.

## Gotchas
- Plosives ("p", "b") thumping: move the mic slightly off-axis or above the mouth.
- Sibilance: angle the mic or choose a darker mic; de-ess later.
- Headphone bleed into the mic on quiet songs; turn down the click or use a click with less high end.

## Verify
- Solo the vocal with no effects: no room boxiness, plosives or clipping.
- Compare level and tone across takes: consistent distance means consistent tone.
