# Miking a drum kit

**Goal:** a balanced, phase-coherent drum recording that mixes easily.

## 1. Start with the kit and room
- Tune the drums and fix rattles before any mic goes up (see drum-tuning skill). Muffling: a pillow or towel in the kick, gel pads on ringy toms.
- Listen in the room: find where the kit sounds best; overheads should capture that.

## 2. Overheads (the core of the sound)
| Technique | Setup | Character |
|---|---|---|
| **Spaced pair** | two condensers ~60-90 cm above cymbals, equidistant from the snare | wide, but check phase on snare/kick |
| **XY / ORTF** | coincident or near-coincident pair over the kit | mono-compatible, narrower |
| **Glyn Johns** | mic 1 ~1 m above the snare pointing down; mic 2 at floor-tom height beside the floor tom pointing across toward the snare/hi-hat; **both mics equidistant from the snare centre** (measure with a cable); pan e.g. 3 o'clock/9 o'clock, not hard | natural, punchy with kick + snare added |
| **Recorderman** | one mic above the drummer's head pointing at the snare, one over the snare; both equidistant from the snare **and** the kick beater (measure with a string) | phase-coherent snare and kick |

## 3. Close mics
- **Kick**: dynamic (D112, Beta 52, RE20) inside the port, 5-15 cm from the beater for attack; outside the reso head for boom; optional sub-kick.
- **Snare top**: SM57 at the rim, 3-5 cm above the head, angled at the centre, rejecting the hi-hat (aim the null at it). Snare bottom: flip polarity.
- **Toms**: MD421 / e604 / clip-ons, 3-5 cm over the rim angled to the centre.
- **Hi-hat**: small-diaphragm condenser above, pointing away from the snare.
- **Room**: pair 2-4 m out, or a single mono room mic; compress heavily for vibe.

## 4. Phase and polarity
- Solo overheads + snare: flip snare polarity; keep the setting with more low-mid body.
- Snare bottom always polarity-inverted against snare top.
- Kick in + out: align in time (nudge or use an alignment plugin) or flip polarity, whichever is fuller.
- Measure overhead distances; small differences cause comb filtering on the snare.

## 5. Gain staging
- Peaks around -10 to -6 dBFS at 24-bit; drummers hit harder on takes than in soundcheck - leave headroom.
- Pads on condensers near loud sources.

## Gotchas
- Overheads too close to cymbals: harsh, cymbal-heavy, no kit picture.
- Snare mic hit by the drummer: angle it in and down, below the rim height of sticks.
- Ignoring phase until mixing: fix it at the mic.

## Verify
- Overheads alone should sound like a decent kit (balanced snare, kick present).
- Mono check of the full kit: no hollow, phasey snare.
