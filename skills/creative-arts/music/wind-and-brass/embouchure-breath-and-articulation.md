# Embouchure, breath and articulation

**Goal:** a centred, consistent sound with clean attacks across the range, and the
endurance to last a rehearsal — on brass (trumpet, horn, trombone, tuba) and woodwinds
(flute, clarinet, saxophone, oboe, bassoon).

## 1. Embouchure basics
- **Brass:** lips buzz inside a cup mouthpiece. Mouthpiece roughly centred
  (left-right); vertical placement varies by player and instrument. Corners firm,
  centre relaxed enough to vibrate. Don't puff cheeks.
- **Single reed (clarinet, sax):** lower lip slightly over the bottom teeth, top teeth
  resting on the mouthpiece, corners in "drawstring" fashion. Clarinet takes more
  mouthpiece and a firmer, flatter chin than sax.
- **Double reed (oboe, bassoon):** both lips cushion the reed over the teeth.
- **Flute:** lower lip covers roughly a quarter to a third of the embouchure hole; the
  air stream splits on the far edge. Small aperture, relaxed lips.
- Check your embouchure in a **mirror** every session; consistency is the aim.

## 2. Breath
- Inhale fully and relaxed through the corners of the mouth (or nose for sustained
  passages), filling low first. Big breath, not tense breath.
- Sound depends on **steady air speed**; think of blowing *through* the instrument.
- **Long tones:** sustain each note 8–16 beats at a fixed dynamic, then with a
  crescendo/decrescendo; listen for pitch and tone staying centred. The foundation
  exercise on every wind instrument.

## 3. Flexibility
- **Brass lip slurs:** slur between harmonics on one valve combination (e.g. trumpet
  open: C4–G4–C5) without tonguing, using air and aperture, not pressure.
- **Mouthpiece buzzing** (brass): sirens and simple tunes on the mouthpiece alone.
- **Woodwind overtones** (sax, flute): play harmonics on a low fingering to strengthen
  air support and voicing.

## 4. Articulation
- Tonguing starts the note; the air is already moving. "Tah" or "too" for normal
  attacks, "dah"/"doo" for softer legato tonguing.
- Brass/flute: tongue behind the top teeth. Reeds: tongue tip touches the reed tip.
- **Staccato** is short notes with space, not hard notes.
- **Double tonguing** ("tu-ku") and triple ("tu-tu-ku") for fast passages — brass and
  flute mainly; practise the "ku" alone first.
- Avoid the **huff attack** (starting with air pushed by the throat — "huh").

## 5. Tuning and section playing
- Tune to the ensemble's reference (often B♭ concert for bands, A for orchestras) after
  warming up; instruments go sharp as they warm.
- In a section: match the **lead player's** pitch, vibrato, articulation length and
  note endings. Listen down: play under the lead's volume.
- Note ends together — section cutoffs are as audible as entries.

## 6. Endurance (especially brass)
Rest as much as you play in practice. Excess mouthpiece pressure is the main killer
of endurance and range; use air and aperture, and stop when the lips stop responding.

## Gotchas
- Reeds vary hugely — rotate 3–4 broken-in reeds; a bad reed sounds like bad technique.
- Upper-register "pinching" usually means not enough air speed, not more lip.
- Uncertain: embouchure placements are individual; follow a teacher for your instrument.

## Verify
- [ ] Mirror check: consistent embouchure, no puffed cheeks.
- [ ] Long tones steady in pitch on a tuner for 8+ beats.
- [ ] Clean attacks without huffs at p and f.
- [ ] Lip slurs/overtones across at least three partials.
- [ ] In section, note lengths and endings matched to the lead.
