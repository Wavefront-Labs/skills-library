# Scale and arpeggio fingering systems

**Goal:** know every scale and arpeggio fingering by system rather than memorising 24
separate patterns.

## 1. The C-major group
C, G, D, A, E major (and their minors) use RH `1 2 3 1 2 3 4 5` and LH
`5 4 3 2 1 3 2 1` (one octave). Thumb crosses after 3, then after 4.

## 2. Black-key rule
For scales starting on black keys, **the thumbs go on white keys** and fingers 2-3-4
group around the black keys.
- B major: RH as C, LH `4 3 2 1 4 3 2 1`.
- F major: RH `1 2 3 4 1 2 3 4`, LH standard.
- Flat major scales (F, Bb, Eb, Ab, Db, Gb): the **RH 4th finger always goes on Bb**.
  Bb major RH `2 1 2 3 1 2 3 4`; Eb major RH `3 1 2 3 4 1 2 3`.
- LH in Bb, Eb, Ab, Db major: `3 2 1 4 3 2 1 3` (3 on the tonic, 4 on the 4th degree).
- Remember just where finger 4 falls in each hand; the rest follows from thumbs on
  white keys.

## 3. Arpeggios
- Root position major/minor triad arpeggios with only white keys: RH `1 2 3 1 2 3 5`,
  LH `5 4 2 1 4 2 1`.
- Black-key arpeggios: thumbs on white keys; e.g. Db major RH `2 1 2 4`.
- Diminished 7th and dominant 7th arpeggios use four-note groups: RH `1 2 3 4`.

## 4. Practice
- Hands separately, slowly, focusing on smooth thumb crossing (thumb moves early, under
  the hand, no elbow jerk).
- Rhythms (long-short, short-long) and accents on groups.
- Metronome ladder up to target tempo, then hands together.

## Gotchas
- Thumb on black keys causes uneven playing; keep to the rule.
- Elbows flapping on crossings: keep the forearm moving laterally instead.
- Practising only up, not down.

## Verify
- [ ] Each scale is played with the correct fingering up and down two to four octaves.
- [ ] Even rhythm at target tempo on recordings.
- [ ] Thumb crossings are inaudible.
