# Transposing at sight

**Goal:** play a song a tone up or down on the spot for a singer.

## 1. Interval method
- Think in the new key and read intervals and chord functions rather than notes.
- Write the new key signature in mind; play the first chord in the new key; continue by
  shapes.

## 2. Clef substitution
- Up a 2nd: read treble as alto clef (one octave up). Down a 2nd: read treble as
  tenor clef (one octave up). Up a 3rd: read treble as bass clef (two octaves up).
  Then apply the new key signature. Practise each substitution separately.

## 3. Harmonic method
- Analyse in Roman numerals; play the same functions in the new key.
- Best for accompaniment patterns and chordal textures.

## 4. Accidentals
- An accidental raising a note in the original raises the corresponding note in the new
  key (a sharp may become a natural).

## 5. Practice
- Hymns and simple songs, transposing up and down a tone and a semitone daily.

## Gotchas
- Semitone transposition (e.g. C to Db) changes all accidentals; practise it.
- Losing the key in modulations; track the harmony.
- Wrong octave in the bass.

## Verify
- [ ] A hymn can be transposed by a tone at sight.
- [ ] Accidentals are correct in the new key.
- [ ] Singer's range fits after transposition.
