# Piano technique and efficient practice

**Goal:** play with a relaxed, controllable tone and learn new pieces in fewer hours,
without building tension habits that limit speed or cause pain.

## 1. Posture and hand
- **Bench height:** forearms roughly level with the keys, elbows slightly in front of
  the torso. Sit on the front half of the bench, feet flat.
- **Distance:** you can reach both ends of the keyboard by leaning, without shifting.
- **Hand shape:** a natural curve — let the arm hang relaxed and look at the hand, then
  place it on the keys like that. Fingertips (not flat pads) for clarity; the thumb
  plays on its side corner.
- **Arm weight:** tone comes from the arm's weight transferred through a firm-but-not-
  stiff fingertip, not from pushing with the fingers alone. After each note, release —
  the key should come back up under a relaxed finger.

## 2. Fingering
- Write fingerings in the score while learning (1 = thumb, 5 = little finger) and keep
  them **consistent** every time you play.
- Prefer fingerings that **minimise hand shifts** in fast passages, and that put the
  thumb on white keys where possible.
- Scale fingerings: C, G, D, A, E major right hand 1-2-3-1-2-3-4-5 (one octave); learn
  the standard fingerings from a scale book rather than inventing them.
- Arpeggios: thumb passes under smoothly; lateral wrist movement, not a twisted thumb.

## 3. Pedalling
- **Syncopated (legato) pedal:** press the key, *then* change the pedal — release
  the pedal as the new chord sounds and depress it again immediately after. Pedalling
  at the same time as the note blurs or gaps the sound.
- Change pedal at every harmony change; listen, not count.
- Half-pedalling and the soft pedal (una corda) vary in effect by instrument — test on
  each piano.

## 4. Practice method
1. **Chunk:** work on 2–4 bars at a time, ending each chunk on the first note of the
   next.
2. **Hands separate** until each hand is secure, then together very slowly.
3. **Slow practice:** a tempo at which you make zero mistakes. Mistakes repeated are
   learned.
4. **Metronome ladder:** when clean three times in a row, raise 4–8 bpm. Drop back if
   errors return.
5. **Rhythmic variants** (dotted rhythms, stop-and-go groupings) for fast runs.
6. **Practise transitions** between sections separately — that's where pieces break
   down.
7. Short daily sessions (20–45 min focused) beat long weekly ones. Stop before fatigue.

## 5. Sight-reading and accompaniment
- Sight-read something easy daily; keep going and never stop to fix mistakes.
- In accompaniment, keep the beat at all costs: drop notes before you drop time.
- Learn to read chord symbols and voice them in the left hand (root–fifth–tenth or
  shell voicings).

## Gotchas
- Pain, numbness or tingling in wrists/forearms = stop and reassess technique; persistent
  symptoms need a doctor or a physio familiar with musicians.
- Digital pianos: use **weighted, full-size keys** for technique work; synth-action
  keyboards build the wrong muscle control.
- Uncertain: hand-position advice differs across schools (e.g. Taubman approach);
  consistency and freedom from pain are the shared goal.

## Verify
- [ ] Bench height and distance set before each session.
- [ ] Fingering written in for all hard passages and used consistently.
- [ ] Pedalled passages are legato without blurring harmonies (recorded check).
- [ ] Passage reaches target tempo via the metronome ladder without errors.
- [ ] No pain or tension in wrists or shoulders after 30 minutes.
