# Voicing a melody and touch control

**Goal:** a singing melody clearly above an even, soft accompaniment, even when both
are in the same hand.

## 1. How piano voicing works
Volume comes from key speed. Voicing means different key speeds at the same moment:
the melody finger presses faster, the others slower.

## 2. Technique
- Firm melody fingertip, the hand slightly tilted toward the melody finger (often the
  5th finger in the right hand for top-voice melodies).
- Accompaniment fingers stay close to the keys, pressing just to the key's
  "escapement" point.
- Arm weight goes through the melody finger.

## 3. Exercises
- Play a chord with the top note loud and others barely sounding; then each note in turn
  loud.
- Play the melody alone with its phrasing, then add accompaniment as soft as possible.
- "Ghost" the accompaniment (press silently) while playing the melody.

## 4. Touch control
- Practise five dynamics on one note (pp to ff), consistently.
- Listen for decay: shape phrases by the melody's decay, not just the attack.
- Legato through finger overlap and pedal.

## Gotchas
- Hitting harder rather than faster key speed creates harsh tone.
- Accompaniment repeated notes too loud cover the melody.
- Pedal too deep blurs voicing.

## Verify
- [ ] Recording: melody clearly above accompaniment in every bar.
- [ ] Each chord note can be brought out on request.
- [ ] Accompaniment is even and soft.
