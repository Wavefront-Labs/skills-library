# Daily sight-reading practice

**Goal:** read new music at the first attempt, steadily and musically.

## 1. Material
- **Easy**: 1-2 grades below your repertoire level. Volume beats difficulty: many short new pieces daily.
- Sources: graded sight-reading books (Paul Harris, ABRSM specimen tests), hymn books, easy duets, Sight Reading Factory (generated exercises at set levels).
- Each piece read **once** (re-reading becomes practice, not sight-reading).

## 2. The 30-second scan (before playing)
1. Key and time signature; tempo and character.
2. Patterns: scales, arpeggios, repeated rhythms, sequences, hand position changes.
3. Danger spots: accidentals, clef changes, leaps, rhythmic traps.
4. Silently tap the rhythm of tricky bars; choose a tempo you can sustain.

## 3. While playing
- **Never stop or correct**: keep the pulse; drop notes rather than time (left-hand bass and melody first).
- Eyes ahead: look at least a beat or a bar ahead; don't look down at the keys (develop keyboard geography by touch).
- Read intervals and shapes, not individual notes (see pattern reading).
- Keep dynamics and phrasing: musicality counts in exams and accompanying.

## 4. Routine (10-15 minutes daily)
- 3-6 new short pieces; one duet or accompaniment with a recording or partner (forces continuity).
- Track: level and accuracy; move up a level when you can play most pieces at 90% accuracy without stopping.

## Gotchas
- Choosing material that's too hard: stopping becomes a habit.
- Practising only one hand position or key; vary keys and styles.

## Verify
- Monthly test: unseen pieces at the target level, recorded; count stops (target zero) and errors.
