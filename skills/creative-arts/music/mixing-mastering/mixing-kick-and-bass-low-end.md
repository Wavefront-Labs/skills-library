# Mixing kick and bass (the low end)

**Goal:** a powerful, clear low end where kick and bass are both audible and translate to
every system.

## 1. Decide roles
- Who owns the **sub (40-60 Hz)** and who owns the **punch (60-120 Hz)**? Common: kick sub-thump + bass upper body (rock), or bass owns sub + kick provides click/punch (EDM, hip-hop with 808s).
- Choose sounds and arrangement parts that fit those roles before EQ.

## 2. Complementary EQ
- Boost the kick where you cut the bass and vice versa (e.g. kick +3 dB at 60 Hz, bass -2 dB at 60 Hz; bass +2 dB at 100 Hz, kick dip at 100 Hz). Keep cuts moderate.
- High-pass everything else (guitars, keys, vocals) so only kick and bass live below ~80-100 Hz.
- Low-pass very low rumble (< 25-30 Hz) on the bass/master if it's eating headroom.

## 3. Dynamics and ducking
- Compress the bass for consistent level (4:1, 3-6 dB GR); kick lightly.
- **Sidechain** the bass to the kick (or a dynamic EQ on the bass's low band keyed by the kick): 2-4 dB duck, fast attack, release to the groove. Makes room for the kick's transient.

## 4. Translation
- Add harmonics to the bass (saturation, amp sim, or a parallel distorted copy high-passed above ~200 Hz) so the bass line is audible on small speakers.
- Kick click (2-5 kHz) keeps the kick audible on phones.
- Keep **below ~120 Hz mono** (stereo bass sounds widen phasey on club systems and lose power in mono).

## 5. Judging low end in an imperfect room
- Spectrum analyser with a reference track overlaid (compare low-end shape, not exact curves).
- Headphones and a car check; a sub-only listen (low-pass monitoring).
- Level meters: kick and bass peaks shouldn't dominate the master's headroom.

## Gotchas
- Both kick and bass boosted in the same band: mud and lost headroom.
- Bass DI and amp out of phase; align and check polarity.
- Over-sidechaining: audible pumping where it's not the style.

## Verify
- Analyser: no single huge peak in the low end; kick and bass fundamentals distinct.
- Small speakers: bass line follows; car: low end not boomy compared to references.
