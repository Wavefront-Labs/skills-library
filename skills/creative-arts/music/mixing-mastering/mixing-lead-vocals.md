# Mixing lead vocals

**Goal:** a vocal that is upfront, intelligible and emotional, sitting in the track not on top
of it.

## 1. Prep
- Comp, tune and time first (see comping and pitch-correction skills). Clip-gain level the phrases. Remove clicks and noise.

## 2. Typical insert chain (order is a starting point)
1. **High-pass** 70-120 Hz (by ear: until it thins, then back off).
2. **Corrective EQ**: cut resonances or boxiness (narrow cuts around 200-500 Hz, harsh spots 2-5 kHz) found by sweeping.
3. **Compressor 1** (fast, peak control): e.g. 1176-style, ratio 4:1, fast attack, medium release, 3-5 dB GR on peaks.
4. **Compressor 2** (smooth levelling): LA-2A-style opto, 2-4 dB GR. **Serial compression** sounds more natural than one compressor doing 10 dB.
5. **De-esser** 5-9 kHz (female voices higher), reducing only sibilants by 3-6 dB.
6. **Tone EQ**: gentle presence boost 2-5 kHz and/or air shelf 10-12 kHz; low-mid warmth 150-250 Hz if thin.
7. **Saturation** (tape/tube) lightly for density and audibility on small speakers.

## 3. Space (send effects)
- **Short reverb** (plate, 1-1.8 s, pre-delay 20-80 ms so the dry vocal stays clear), high-passed ~300 Hz and low-passed ~8 kHz on the return.
- **Tempo delay** (1/4 or 1/8 dotted) quietly underneath for depth; slapback (80-140 ms) for rock/rockabilly.
- Duck the delay/reverb return with the dry vocal (sidechain compression) to keep words clear.

## 4. Placement
- Balance with the faders at the song's loudest section first, then automate (see vocal-riding skill).
- Carve space in competing instruments (dip 2-4 kHz on guitars/synths slightly) rather than over-boosting the vocal.

## Gotchas
- Boosting presence then de-essing harder and harder: harsh and lispy. Fix the cause (mic, EQ choice).
- Too much reverb on the dry insert sounds distant; use sends.
- Mixing the vocal solo; always judge in context.

## Verify
- Low-volume test: every word clear. Small speaker test: vocal level consistent with full-range monitors.
- A/B against a reference vocal in the same genre for level relative to the snare and overall tone.
