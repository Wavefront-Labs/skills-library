# Left-hand independence and expressive gesture

**Goal:** the right hand keeps time; the left hand and body show what time cannot -
dynamics, cues, sustain, character.

## 1. Stop mirroring
Mirroring (the left hand copying the beat pattern) adds no information. Rest the left
hand at the waist or chest when it has nothing to say.

## 2. Independence drills
- Right hand beats 4/4 while the left: slowly rises (crescendo) over 8 bars, then falls.
- Right hand beats 3/4; left hand cues on beat 2 of every other bar.
- Right hand beats; left hand holds a sustained, palm-up "keep going" gesture.
Practise each drill in front of a mirror or on video until the right hand pattern stays
unchanged.

## 3. Left-hand vocabulary
- **Crescendo:** palm up, rising. **Diminuendo:** palm down, lowering, or toward the
  body.
- **Sustain:** hand held, slight forward pressure.
- **Hush/subito piano:** palm out toward the section.
- **Cue:** look first, then a small preparatory gesture a beat before the entrance.
- **Release/cutoff** of one section while others continue.

## 4. Body and face
- Eye contact a beat before every cue.
- Gesture size = dynamic; gesture shape = articulation (smooth curved legato, sharp
  stopped staccato).
- Facial expression conveys character more than arm size.

## Gotchas
- Busy left hands become wallpaper; ensembles stop reading them. Use it less, mean more.
- Showing dynamics only with the right hand's size makes the pattern unclear.
- Cueing with a pointing finger is less clear than an open palm and eye contact.

## Verify
- [ ] On video, the left hand is still for most of the time and moves only with purpose.
- [ ] The right-hand pattern stays consistent during left-hand gestures.
- [ ] Every important entry gets eye contact and a cue.
