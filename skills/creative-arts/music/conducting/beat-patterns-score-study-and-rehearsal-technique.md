# Beat patterns, score study and rehearsal technique

**Goal:** give an ensemble a clear, readable tempo and character with your gesture,
arrive at rehearsal knowing the score better than anyone, and use rehearsal time to
fix what matters.

## 1. Beat patterns and the ictus
- **Ictus:** the precise point where each beat "happens" — a small bounce or click at
  the bottom of the gesture. Players follow the ictus, not the arm. Make it consistent
  in height (roughly at mid-chest, in a "box" in front of you).
- Standard patterns: **2** (down–up), **3** (down–right–up), **4** (down–left–right–up),
  **6** (subdivided 2 or full six with beats grouped), **1** (fast 3/4 or 6/8 in one).
- **Downbeat is always the clearest and lowest** — it tells everyone where bar 1 is.
- **Size = dynamic:** larger beats for loud, smaller for soft. **Shape = articulation:**
  smooth curves for legato, stopped/flicked for staccato.
- Fast compound metres: beat in 2 (6/8) or 1 (3/8, fast 3/4) — choose by what players
  need, not by notation.

## 2. Starting, stopping, holding
- **Preparatory beat:** one beat in the new tempo and character before the first
  sound. Breathe with it (singers and winds breathe on it). Most beginner false starts
  are unclear preps.
- Entrances on beats other than 1: prepare from the previous beat.
- **Cutoff:** a small loop ending in a clear stop, usually with the left hand; the
  release point is the ictus.
- **Fermata:** stop the pattern, sustain with the hand, then either cutoff or move on
  directly (decide and mark it in your score).

## 3. Left hand and cueing
- Left hand is for what the right can't do: cues, dynamics, releases, phrasing. Don't
  mirror the right hand constantly — it devalues the left.
- **Cues:** make eye contact a beat *before* an entrance, then gesture. Cue exposed
  entries after long rests; cue everything in early rehearsals.

## 4. Score study (before first rehearsal)
1. Overview: form, keys, tempo changes, instrumentation.
2. Mark phrase structure (bar lengths), climaxes, transitions.
3. Harmonic outline at important cadences.
4. Mark in pencil: tempo changes, metre changes, cues, breaths, balance problems you
   expect.
5. Sing every line; play the reduction at the piano if possible.
6. Listen to recordings *after* you have your own view.

## 5. Rehearsal technique
- **Plan by minutes:** list sections and goals ("bars 40–64: tuning winds, balance
  strings") with time allocations. Hard material when energy is high.
- **Stop with a reason.** Diagnose in one sentence, give bar number first ("Bar 48,
  from beat 3 — trumpets, softer under the flute"), then rerun.
- Talk less: aim for more playing time than speaking time.
- **Fix, then put back in context:** isolate a passage, then run from earlier.
- End by playing something through so the group leaves on music.

## Gotchas
- Watching the score instead of the players: memorise transitions so your eyes are up.
- Subdividing too much in slow music makes it feel heavy; subdivide only where needed.
- Uncertain: pattern shapes differ slightly between schools; consistency matters more
  than which one.

## Verify
- [ ] Ictus clear and consistent when filmed from the players' position.
- [ ] Every start in the piece has a rehearsed preparatory beat.
- [ ] Score marked for form, cues, tempo changes and fermata decisions.
- [ ] Rehearsal plan with timings written before each rehearsal.
- [ ] Stops always begin with a bar number and a specific instruction.
