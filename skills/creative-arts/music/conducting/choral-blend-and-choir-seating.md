# Choral blend and choir seating

**Goal:** singers placed so voices support each other and the sound blends.

## 1. Formations
- **Sectional** (SATB blocks) - easiest for learning notes.
- **Mixed / scrambled** (quartets) - better blend and independence; for well-prepared
  choirs.
- **Columns** (sections in columns, men in the middle) - good balance in some halls.
Choose by repertoire and hall.

## 2. Voice placement (Weston Noble method)
- Hear singers in pairs or trios singing the same phrase.
- Move singers until pairs sound like one voice; place bright voices next to darker
  voices, strong voices behind or next to weaker ones.
- Tall singers at the back; keep sight lines to the conductor.

## 3. Other blend tools
- Unified vowels (diction), matched vibrato (less in blended passages), even dynamic
  across sections.
- Have singers listen to the person next to them.

## Gotchas
- One strong voice at the front dominates.
- Changing formations too close to the performance confuses singers.
- Too much space between singers reduces blend.

## Verify
- [ ] Seating chart shows each singer placed for blend.
- [ ] Recording shows no individual voice sticking out.
- [ ] Formation chosen for the repertoire and hall.
