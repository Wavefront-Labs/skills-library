# Choral diction and vowel unification

**Goal:** a choir that sounds like one voice: same vowels, same consonant timing.

## 1. Vowels
- Choose a target vowel for each syllable (write the IPA in the score: [a], [e], [i],
  [o], [u]).
- Model it and have sections match each other.
- Use a slightly taller/rounder vowel in high registers for blend.

## 2. Diphthongs
English diphthongs are sung on the first vowel, with the second vowel added at the very
end: "night" = [na] held, [i] at release. Mark it.

## 3. Consonants
- **Initial consonants** before the beat, so the vowel lands on the beat.
- **Final consonants** placed together on a specified beat (e.g. "t" on beat 3); mark it.
- Voiced consonants (m, n, l) can be sung on pitch for legato.

## 4. Languages
- Latin: Italianate pronunciation (or German Latin for some repertoire); agree in advance.
- German: closed and open vowels, final "ch" and "r".
- French: nasal vowels, rules for liaison.

## 5. Rehearsing
Speak the text in rhythm, then on one pitch, then with the music.

## Gotchas
- Mixed vowels ("ah" sung in four ways) destroy tuning.
- Final "s" and "t" from every singer at a different time.
- Over-pronounced consonants in quiet passages.

## Verify
- [ ] Every sustained vowel has a chosen IPA symbol.
- [ ] Final consonants have a written placement.
- [ ] Recording shows unified vowels in sustained chords.
