# Fixing ensemble balance and intonation

**Goal:** chords that ring in tune and textures where the right line is heard.

## 1. Balance
- Use the **balance pyramid**: lower voices slightly louder, upper voices lighter. Bass
  supports the chord; tops sit on it.
- Ask the section with the melody to play alone, then add others until the melody just
  remains clear.
- Have players listen *down* to the bass and *across* to the melody.
- Change dynamics per section rather than asking everyone to be softer.

## 2. Intonation
- Tune from the **bass up**: roots first, then fifths, then thirds, then sevenths.
- Just-tuned intervals in sustained chords: major 3rd about 14 cents lower than equal
  temperament, minor 3rd about 16 cents higher, perfect 5th about 2 cents higher.
- Hold the problem chord; have each part enter one by one.
- Use a drone (tuner on the tonic) during slow passages.

## 3. Isolate
- Slow the passage, hold the chord, then put it back in tempo.
- Unisons first: tune an octave or unison line before the harmonies.

## 4. Habit
Five minutes of chorale tuning at the start of rehearsal builds listening.

## Gotchas
- Tuning only to an electronic tuner at A=440 ignores the chord context.
- Asking "play in tune" without saying who and in which direction is not useful.
- Loud playing is where intonation and tone collapse; check balance at forte.

## Verify
- [ ] Problem chords are tuned from the root up and ring (no beats).
- [ ] The melody is clear in recorded playback.
- [ ] A short tuning chorale is part of each rehearsal.
