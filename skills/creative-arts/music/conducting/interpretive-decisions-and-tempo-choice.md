# Interpretive decisions and tempo choice

**Goal:** a tempo and shape for every movement that you can justify from the score, not
from a favourite recording.

## 1. Evidence for tempo
- Composer's **metronome marks** (and whether they are authentic or editorial).
- Tempo words in their historical meaning (Allegro in Mozart is not modern "fast").
- The fastest notes: pick a tempo at which the quickest figures are clear in the hall.
- Harmonic rhythm: slow harmonic rhythm tolerates a faster tempo.
- Text (choral): the words must be intelligible.

## 2. Decide and test
- Sing or play the main themes at your candidate tempo with a metronome.
- Check that all sections work at that tempo (the slow inner section of a fast
  movement, the climax).
- Write your tempo in the score with the reason.

## 3. Rubato and transitions
- Mark where you take time (cadences, phrase ends) and where you return.
- Plan transitions between tempi: how many bars, and what the orchestra will follow.

## 4. Phrase shape and balance
Mark phrase peaks, the most important climax of the movement, and voices to bring out.

## 5. Performance practice
Consider vibrato, articulation, repeat observance and orchestra size relevant to the
period; decide and be consistent.

## Gotchas
- Copying a recording's tempo without checking the score.
- Choosing a tempo in your head that does not work in a resonant hall.
- Tempo changes the score does not ask for.

## Verify
- [ ] Every movement has a written tempo choice with a reason.
- [ ] The fastest passages are clear at that tempo.
- [ ] Transitions are planned bar by bar.
