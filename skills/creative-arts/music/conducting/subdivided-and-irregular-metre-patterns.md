# Subdivided and irregular-metre patterns

**Goal:** the ensemble knows exactly where each beat and each grouping falls.

## 1. When to subdivide
- Slow tempi (below about 50 bpm to the beat) - ensembles drift without subdivision.
- Ritardandi, especially into a fermata or final chord.
- Tricky entrances on the "and".
Subdivide only as long as needed; return to the main beat as soon as the tempo allows.

## 2. Subdivided patterns
- Each main beat keeps its direction (down, left, right, up for 4/4) with a small
  rebound for the subdivision in the same direction.
- Main beats are larger, subdivisions small, so the bar structure stays visible.
- 6/8 slow = subdivided 2: down-down-down (small), right-right-up.

## 3. Irregular metres (fast tempi, beat = group)
- Conduct the **groups** with unequal beats: 5/8 as 2+3 or 3+2 (two beats, one longer),
  7/8 as 2+2+3, 3+2+2 or 2+3+2 (three beats).
- Mark the grouping in the score from the music's accents and beaming, not the time
  signature alone.
- The long beat is given more space (a longer travel), not a different size.

## 4. Slow irregular metres
Conduct every eighth or use a modified pattern (5 as a 5-beat pattern).

## 5. Practice
Practise with a metronome on the eighth note and recordings; speak the groupings
("ONE-two, ONE-two-three") while conducting.

## Gotchas
- Changing groupings within a passage (2+3 then 3+2) without warning; mark both the
  part and score and say it in rehearsal.
- Too-large subdivisions obscure the downbeat.
- Do not subdivide during a passage that should flow; it makes it heavy.

## Verify
- [ ] Every irregular bar has its grouping written in the score.
- [ ] Subdivisions are visibly smaller than main beats.
- [ ] A recording of yourself shows a clear ictus on every group.
