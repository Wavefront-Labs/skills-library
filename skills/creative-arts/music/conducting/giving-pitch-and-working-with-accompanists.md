# Giving pitch and working with accompanists

**Goal:** the choir starts in tune and on time, and the accompanist is a partner.

## 1. Giving pitch a cappella
- Play the chord, then each section's starting note (bass up), on piano or pitch pipe.
- Sing the starting pitches on "oo" softly so the audience barely hears.
- Count in silently, then breathe with the choir on the preparatory beat.
- In concert, give the key chord once and the first notes discreetly.

## 2. Pitch drift
- Check pitch at the end of a cappella pieces; if the choir drifts flat, tune vowels and
  breath in rehearsal.
- Mark check points in the score where the piano can give a pitch if needed.

## 3. Working with the accompanist
- Share your tempos, breath points and cuts before rehearsal.
- Ask the accompanist to play parts during note-learning, then the accompaniment.
- Agree a gesture for starting introductions (eye contact plus prep beat).
- Respect their input on balance; they hear the piano differently.

## Gotchas
- Giving pitch loudly in a concert.
- Pianist following the score while the choir follows the conductor.
- Not giving the pianist breaks.

## Verify
- [ ] Choir starts in tune with discreet pitch-giving.
- [ ] Accompanist has tempos, cuts and breath points marked.
- [ ] Start gestures are agreed.
