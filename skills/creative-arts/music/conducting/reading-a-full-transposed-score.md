# Reading a full transposed score

**Goal:** hear the whole texture from the page and play a reduction at the keyboard.

## 1. Score order (top to bottom)
Woodwinds (piccolo, flutes, oboes, cor anglais, clarinets, bass clarinet, bassoons,
contrabassoon) - brass (horns, trumpets, trombones, tuba) - timpani and percussion -
harp, keyboards - soloists/choir - strings (violin I, violin II, viola, cello, bass).
Horns appear above trumpets in orchestral scores even though trumpets sound higher.

## 2. Transpositions to know
| Instrument | Sounds |
|---|---|
| Clarinet in Bb / trumpet in Bb | major 2nd lower |
| Clarinet in A | minor 3rd lower |
| Horn in F / cor anglais | perfect 5th lower |
| Piccolo, glockenspiel (2 oct), xylophone | higher than written |
| Double bass, contrabassoon, guitar | octave lower |
Older scores have horns and trumpets in D, E, Eb etc.; read the key in the instrument
label.

## 3. Clefs
Viola in alto clef; cello, bassoon, trombone in tenor clef in high passages. Use the
clef-substitution trick: reading a Bb part as tenor clef (with key adjusted) gives concert
pitch an octave apart.

## 4. Method
1. Find the bass line and the melody first; then harmony instruments.
2. Mark doublings and instrument entries.
3. At the keyboard, play bass + melody, then add inner harmony from the strings.
   Simplify; do not try to play everything.

## 5. Practice
Start with string quartet scores, then Haydn/Mozart symphonies, then later Romantic.
Daily 10 minutes at the keyboard.

## Gotchas
- Concert-pitch scores (common in film and modern music) exist; check the note on the
  first page.
- Divisi and "a 2" markings change how many players play a line.

## Verify
- [ ] You can name the sounding pitch of any transposing part at sight.
- [ ] You can play bass and melody of a Haydn movement at the keyboard.
- [ ] Score is marked with entries and doublings.
