# Travis picking and alternating bass

**Goal:** a thumb that keeps a steady bass while the fingers play independent syncopated
melody or chords.

## 1. The thumb pattern first
- Thumb alternates between two bass strings on every beat: for C, string 5 (root) then 4, then 6 (fretted G with 3rd finger moving) then 4: **5-4-6-4** ("alternating bass"). For G: 6-4-5-4; for D: 4-3-4-3 or 4-3-5-3.
- Light **palm mute** on the bass strings for the classic thumpy sound (Chet Atkins, Merle Travis).
- Practise thumb-only with a metronome until it is automatic (talk or count while doing it).

## 2. Adding the fingers
1. **Pinch**: thumb and finger together on beat 1 (thumb on 5, middle on 2).
2. Add **off-beat** finger notes: index on string 3 on the "and" of beats 2 and 4. Basic pattern (8ths): T, T+m, T, i... practise slowly.
3. Classic pattern for C (8ths): beat 1 pinch (5 + 1st string), "and" index 2nd string, beat 2 thumb 4, "and" middle 1st string, beat 3 thumb 6, "and" index 2nd, beat 4 thumb 4, "and" middle 1st.
4. Then move melody notes freely on the off-beats while the thumb never stops.

## 3. Thumb pick or bare thumb
- Thumbpick gives volume and attack (essential on steel-string at gigs); bare thumb is softer and more flexible. Choose early; switching later costs time.

## 4. Repertoire steps
- "Freight Train" (Elizabeth Cotten), "Dust in the Wind" (arpeggio-based), "Windy and Warm", Merle Travis' "Cannonball Rag" (advanced).

## Gotchas
- Thumb speeding up or stopping when fingers play syncopations: return to the thumb pattern alone, then add one finger note at a time.
- Bass too loud: the melody must sit on top; dig in more with fingers, mute the bass.
- Forgetting chord-shape bass changes (the 3rd finger walking between 5th and 6th strings for C).

## Verify
- Metronome at 60-100 bpm: record 8 bars; the bass should be perfectly even and the melody clearly louder.
- Play while singing or talking: the thumb continues unaffected.
