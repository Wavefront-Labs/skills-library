# Guitar setup: relief, action and intonation

**Goal:** a guitar that plays easily without buzz and plays in tune up the neck.

## Order matters
Fresh strings -> tune to pitch (in the tuning you'll use) -> **relief** -> **action** -> nut check -> **intonation** -> pickup height. Each step changes the ones after it.

## 1. Neck relief (truss rod)
- Capo at fret 1, hold the low E down at the fret where the neck joins the body (~16-17). Measure the gap between the string bottom and the top of the **7th-8th fret**.
- Typical targets: **0.010 in (0.25 mm)** electric (Fender spec 0.010 in), 0.012 in bass, slightly more for heavy pickers and acoustics.
- Too much gap = too much bow: **tighten** the rod (clockwise from the end you're looking at). Too little/back-bow (buzz in low frets) = loosen.
- Turn **1/8 to 1/4 turn** at a time; let the neck settle, retune, re-measure. If the rod is very stiff or at its limit, stop and see a tech.

## 2. Action (string height)
- Measure at the **12th fret**, string bottom to fret top.
- Typical electric: **low E 2.0 mm (5/64 in), high E 1.6 mm (4/64 in)**; acoustic 2.4 / 1.8 mm; bass 2.4 / 2.0 mm.
- Adjust saddles (Strat/Tele: individual saddle screws, follow fretboard radius) or bridge posts (Tune-o-matic). Acoustics: sand the saddle bottom (remove material = lower).

## 3. Nut check
- Fret each string at the 3rd fret: the gap over the 1st fret should be tiny (about a sheet of paper, ~0.1 mm). High nut = sharp first-position chords and hard playing; fix by a tech (nut files).

## 4. Intonation
- With a good tuner: tune the open string, then compare the **12th-fret fretted note** (not the harmonic) to the open/12th harmonic.
- **Fretted note sharp -> move the saddle back** (away from the neck, lengthens the string). **Flat -> move forward.**
- Retune after each adjustment. Aim within +/- 2-3 cents.

## 5. Pickup height
- Measure fret top to pole piece with the last fret held: Fender spec around 2.4 mm bass side / 1.6 mm treble side for single coils (neck), slightly closer at the bridge. Too close = weak sustain and wolf tones (string pull on Strats).

## Gotchas
- Adjusting action before relief: you'll chase buzz forever.
- Changing string gauge or tuning changes everything: redo the setup.
- Humidity/season changes affect relief; recheck quarterly.

## Verify
- Play every fret on every string at moderate attack: no buzz that's audible through the amp.
- Tuner check: open, 5th, 12th fret on each string within a few cents.
