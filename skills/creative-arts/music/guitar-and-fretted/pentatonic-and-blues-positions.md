# Pentatonic and blues positions

**Goal:** solo anywhere on the neck in any key with the pentatonic and blues scales, with
phrasing that sounds like music.

## 1. The five positions (A minor pentatonic: A C D E G)
- **Position 1** ("box 1"): root on the 6th string, 5th fret; frets 5-8. Pattern per string (low to high): 5-8, 5-7, 5-7, 5-7, 5-8, 5-8.
- Positions 2-5 each start on the next scale note on the 6th string (C at 8, D at 10, E at 12, G at 15) and link up the neck; position 5 overlaps position 1 an octave up (17 / 5).
- **Same shapes = relative major pentatonic** (A minor = C major pentatonic); the root you target changes.
- Learn each position with the **roots marked**; play them ascending/descending and in 3-note sequences.

## 2. Adding the blue note and extras
- **Blues scale**: add the b5 (E-flat in A) - in position 1, 6th fret on the 5th string and 8th fret on the 3rd string.
- Major-minor mix: over a dominant blues, mix the major 3rd (C#) into minor pentatonic licks (bend the b3 a quarter tone, or slide b3 to 3).
- The 2nd and 6th (B, F#) give the Dorian/Mixolydian colour.

## 3. Connecting positions
- Slide on one string between positions (e.g. 7-9 on the G string to move from box 1 to box 2).
- Play the same lick in two positions to learn the map.
- Use "horizontal" playing along 2 strings for vocal-like lines.

## 4. Phrasing over a 12-bar blues
- Call and response: 2-bar phrase, 2 bars of space.
- Target chord tones on chord changes: the 3rd of IV (D7's F#) in bar 5, root or 3rd of V in bar 9.
- Repeat a motif and vary it; use bends, vibrato and dynamics (see bends skill) more than speed.
- Learn 10 classic licks (BB King, Albert King, Clapton, SRV) and move them through positions.

## Gotchas
- Running up and down boxes = scale practice, not a solo. Leave space.
- Ending phrases on the b3 over a major chord sounds unresolved; resolve to the root, 5th or 3rd.
- Getting stuck in box 1; force yourself to solo a chorus in each position.

## Verify
- Play all five positions with a metronome at 80 bpm in triplets without stopping.
- Record a 3-chorus solo over a backing track; check you used 3+ positions and phrase endings land on chord tones.
