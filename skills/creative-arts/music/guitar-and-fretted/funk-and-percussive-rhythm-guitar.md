# Funk and percussive rhythm guitar

**Goal:** tight, percussive sixteenth-note rhythm parts that lock with the drums.

## 1. Constant motion
- The strumming hand moves down-up on every sixteenth note, always (down on 1, e, &, a
  alternating). Strike the strings only on the notes you want; miss them on the rest.
- This keeps time even in sparse parts.

## 2. Muting
- **Left-hand mute:** release fretting pressure (fingers still touching) to get the
  "chick" scratch sound.
- Alternate pressed (chord) and released (scratch) strums for the classic funk sound.
- Palm mute for some single-note lines.

## 3. Voicings
- Small three- or four-note shapes on the top strings: 9th chords (E9 on 7th fret),
  7#9, m7, 13.
- Single-note lines and double-stops on the top strings.

## 4. Groove
- Practise with a drum loop or metronome on sixteenths.
- Accents on the off-beats ("e" and "a").
- Play less: leave space for the bass and keys.

## 5. Tone
Bright clean tone: single-coil pickups (neck/middle or bridge/middle), light
compression, sometimes an auto-wah.

## Gotchas
- Stopping the strumming hand breaks the groove.
- Loose muting lets open strings ring.
- Big chords mask the other instruments.

## Verify
- [ ] Constant sixteenth motion with a metronome.
- [ ] Clean alternation of chord and scratch sounds.
- [ ] Recording locks with the drum loop.
