# Walking bass lines

**Goal:** a steady quarter-note line that outlines the changes and swings.

## 1. Basic rules
- Root on beat 1 of each new chord (most of the time).
- Chord tones (3rd, 5th, 7th) on beats 1 and 3.
- Beat 4: an **approach note** into the next root: a half step above or below
  (chromatic), a scale step, or the dominant (5th of the target).

## 2. Building a bar
For one chord per bar: root - chord tone - chord tone or scale - approach.
- C7 to F7: C - E - G - Gb (chromatic above F) or C - D - E - E (approach below).

## 3. Two-chord bars
Root on 1, root of the next chord on 3; use beats 2 and 4 to connect.

## 4. Two-feel vs four-feel
- **Two-feel:** half notes on 1 and 3, with occasional pickups; usually the head.
- **Four-feel:** quarter-note walking, for solos.

## 5. Practice tunes
12-bar blues in F and Bb, then rhythm changes, then standards (Autumn Leaves, All the
Things You Are). Learn the changes by ear.

## 6. Feel
Lay the notes on the front or middle of the beat with the ride cymbal. Even quarter
notes with slight emphasis on 2 and 4.

## Gotchas
- Scale runs without chord tones on strong beats.
- Random rhythm or skipping notes; keep quarter notes.
- Not hearing the root movement.

## Verify
- [ ] Each chord's root on its first beat.
- [ ] Approach notes lead into each new chord.
- [ ] Line swings with a recording.
