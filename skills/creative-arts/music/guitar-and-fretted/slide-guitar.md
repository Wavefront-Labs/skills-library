# Slide guitar

**Goal:** clean, in-tune slide lines without unwanted string noise.

## 1. Gear
- **Glass** slides: smooth, warm (Duane Allman used a glass bottle). **Brass/steel**: louder, brighter, more sustain. Ceramic in between.
- Finger: **ring** (leaves index and middle free to fret chords behind) or **little finger** (frees 3 fingers). Slide must cover all six strings; not so tight it can't tilt.
- Higher action and heavier strings (11s-13s) help avoid fret clatter.

## 2. Intonation
- Pitch is set **directly over the fret wire**, not behind it as in fretting.
- Slide parallel to the frets for chords; tilt for single notes on adjacent strings.
- Approach target notes from slightly below and settle; vibrato by shaking the slide lightly along the string (small, even).
- Very light pressure: the slide just touches the strings, never presses to the fretboard.

## 3. Damping (the essential skill)
- **Behind the slide**: let a free fretting-hand finger (usually index) rest lightly on the strings behind the slide to kill overtones and ghost notes.
- **Picking hand**: fingerpicking with the thumb and fingers resting on strings not being played (palm and fingers block). Many slide players play fingerstyle for this reason.
- Lift the slide between notes when you want separation; otherwise it glides.

## 4. Tuning approach
- **Open G or open D** (see alternate-tunings skill): full major chords under a straight slide; Delta blues and rock.
- **Standard tuning**: single-note lines and dyads (Derek Trucks uses open E; Warren Haynes and David Gilmour often standard). Harder for chords.
- Lap steel: horizontal guitar played with a bar; open tunings like C6.

## Gotchas
- Pressing too hard: buzzing and fret clatter, notes go sharp.
- Ringing open strings and sympathetic noise; damp constantly.
- Sitting behind the fret wire: everything is flat.

## Verify
- Tuner on: play a target note with slide; settle within +/-10 cents.
- Record a phrase and listen for noise in the gaps; there should be silence between phrases.
