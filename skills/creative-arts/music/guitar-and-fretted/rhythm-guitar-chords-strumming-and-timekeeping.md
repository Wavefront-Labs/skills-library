# Rhythm guitar: chords, strumming and timekeeping

**Goal:** play chord accompaniment that is clean, in time and dynamically controlled —
the job of a guitarist for most of any song. Same principles apply to ukulele and
(minus chords) to bass.

## 1. Clean chords
- **Fretting:** press just behind the fret (not on top of it, not mid-fret), on the
  fingertips, with the thumb behind the neck roughly opposite the middle finger.
- **Check every string:** strum the chord one string at a time; fix buzzes (too far
  from fret, not enough pressure) and muted strings (finger leaning onto the next
  string — arch the finger more).
- Learn in this order: E, A, D, Em, Am, G, C, Dm → power chords → F and B♭ barre shapes
  → movable E- and A-shape barre chords (the **CAGED** system maps these around the
  neck).
- **Barre chords:** roll the index finger slightly onto its bony side; use arm weight
  (pull back gently) rather than thumb-squeezing.

## 2. Fast chord changes
- **One-minute changes:** pick two chords, count how many clean switches in 60 s,
  log the number daily. A widely used drill that gives measurable progress.
- **Anchor/pivot fingers:** find a finger that stays put between chords (e.g. ring
  finger on G to D on the 3rd fret, B string, in some voicings).
- Move all fingers **as a shape**, landing together; don't place them one by one.
- Keep strumming through a messy change — rhythm beats perfect chords.

## 3. The strumming hand
- **Constant motion:** the hand moves down–up in steady eighths (or sixteenths)
  whether or not it hits the strings. Downstrokes on the beat, upstrokes off the beat.
  Patterns are created by *missing* strings, not by stopping the arm.
- Classic folk/pop pattern: D – D U – U D U (in eighths: 1, 2&, &4&).
- Strum from the wrist/forearm, loose grip on the pick (medium ~0.6–0.75 mm is a common
  starting gauge).
- **Dynamics:** fewer strings and lighter strokes in verses, full strums in choruses.

## 4. Muting
- **Fretting-hand mute:** relax finger pressure to stop strings — creates the "chk"
  percussive sound.
- **Palm muting:** heel of the picking hand lightly on the strings near the bridge;
  essential for rock/punk eighths.
- Unused strings (e.g. low E on a D chord) — mute with thumb or fingertip, or avoid with
  the pick.

## 5. Timekeeping
- Practise with a **metronome on 2 and 4** (set it to half the tempo and hear clicks
  as the backbeat) — trains a groove feel.
- Record yourself against a drum loop; compare your strums with the kick and snare.
- With a band: lock to the **hi-hat** for subdivision and the **kick/bass** for the
  downbeat.

## Gotchas
- Tune every time you pick up the guitar (clip-on tuner); retune after capo placement.
- High action or old strings make barre chords nearly impossible — a setup by a tech
  can fix a "hard" guitar.
- Uncertain: pick-gauge and string-gauge choices are taste; numbers above are starting
  points.

## Verify
- [ ] Each chord rings clean string by string.
- [ ] One-minute change count rising week on week (target: 30+ for common pairs).
- [ ] Strumming arm never stops during a pattern.
- [ ] Palm-muted eighths even at 120 bpm.
- [ ] Recording against a drum loop shows strums aligned with the beat.
