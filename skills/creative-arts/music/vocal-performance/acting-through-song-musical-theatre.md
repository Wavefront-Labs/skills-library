# Acting through song (musical theatre)

**Goal:** perform a song as a scene where the character wants something and changes.

## 1. Text work (away from the music)
1. Write the lyric out as prose. **Speak it as a monologue** with real intention until it sounds like thought, not verse.
2. **Given circumstances**: who am I, where, when, what just happened (the scene before), who am I talking to?
3. **Objective**: what do I want from the listener by the end? Phrase it as an active verb ("to make him stay", not "to be sad").
4. **Obstacle**: what stops me?
5. **Beats**: mark each change of tactic or new thought (often at verse/bridge boundaries, sometimes mid-line). Give each beat an action verb (to plead, to tease, to warn).
6. **Subtext**: what I really mean under each line.

## 2. Music as acting
- The composer has already made choices: key changes, rests, tempo changes, the high note. Ask **why** each happens; justify it with a new thought.
- Intros and instrumental breaks are **thinking time**: the character decides to speak.
- Rests = listening/reacting. The final note: does the character get what they wanted?

## 3. Focus and staging
- Audition/solo: place the scene partner just above the panel's heads (not at the panel); use 2-3 focus points for different beats.
- Movement only with intention (a new beat may produce a move); stillness reads as strong.
- Hands: let them respond to thought; avoid repeated gestures.

## 4. Vocal choices from acting
- Style choices (belt, mix, speech-like delivery) should serve the beat: speak-sing on conversational lines, full belt only at the emotional peak.

## Gotchas
- "Indicating" emotion (playing sad) instead of pursuing an objective.
- Singing the song identically every time; the objective should be fresh each time.
- Overacting the intro; save the first move for the first line or the first change.

## Verify
- Film yourself: mute the audio. Can you see the beat changes in your face and body?
- Ask a viewer what the character wanted and whether they got it.
