# Vocal hydration and singing when ill

**Goal:** a well-lubricated voice, and sound decisions about singing with a cold.
This is general health information from NIDCD, not medical advice; see a doctor.

## 1. Hydration and environment
- Drink plenty of water, especially when exercising. If you drink caffeinated drinks or
  alcohol, balance them with plenty of water.
- Use a humidifier at home, especially in winter or dry climates; NIDCD recommends
  thirty percent humidity.
- Take "vocal naps": rest the voice through the day.
- Avoid or limit medicines that dry the vocal folds, including some common cold and
  allergy medicines. If you have voice problems, ask a doctor which are safest.

## 2. Reflux
- Inflammation from gastroesophageal reflux (acid reflux, heartburn, GERD) is a listed
  cause of voice problems. Spicy food can move stomach acid into the throat or
  oesophagus. If heartburn persists, talk to a doctor about diet changes or medicines.
- Repeated throat clearing is a listed warning sign of a voice problem.

## 3. Singing when ill or tired
- Avoid speaking or singing when the voice is hoarse or tired.
- Rest the voice when sick: illness puts extra stress on it.
- Avoid the extremes of the vocal range such as screaming or whispering; talking too
  loudly and too softly both stress the voice.
- Avoid mouthwash or gargles containing alcohol or irritating chemicals.

## 4. When to get help
NIDCD's warning signs: a voice that becomes hoarse or raspy, loss of the ability to hit
some high notes, a suddenly deeper voice, a raw, achy or strained throat, talking that
becomes an effort, repeated throat clearing. If you think you have a voice problem,
see a doctor. An otolaryngologist (ENT) diagnoses voice disorders and may refer you to
a speech-language pathologist, who can teach healthier voice use.
