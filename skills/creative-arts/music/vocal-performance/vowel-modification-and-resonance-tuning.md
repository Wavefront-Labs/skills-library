# Vowel modification and resonance tuning

**Goal:** an even tone across the range, with high notes that do not get shouty or
thin, by adjusting vowels rather than pushing.

## 1. Why vowels must change
Each vowel has resonances (formants). F1 is roughly 300 Hz for [i]/[u] to about 800 Hz
for [a]. When the sung pitch (or its 2nd harmonic) rises past F1, the voice "turns
over" - the sound changes colour around the passaggio. Modification lets you manage
that change instead of fighting it.

## 2. Classical approach (aggiustamento)
- As pitch rises through the passaggio, move vowels slightly toward neighbours:
  [a] toward [ɔ], [e] toward [ɛ] or [œ], [i] toward [ɪ]/[y].
- Keep the jaw dropped more on high notes and the lips slightly rounded.
- The aim is "cover": a rounder, darker sound that keeps the ring (the singer's
  formant, around 2.5-3.5 kHz).

## 3. Contemporary/belt approach
- Belt uses the opposite: keep or open the vowel (toward [æ] or [a]) and keep F1 above
  the second harmonic, for a speech-like bright sound.
- Mix uses a middle path: narrow vowels ([ʌ], [ɪ]) to avoid both pushing and flipping.

## 4. Exercises
- Five-note scales on [i]-[e]-[a], letting the vowel adjust as you rise.
- Sirens on [u] or a straw, then open gradually into the vowel.
- Sing a phrase on a single vowel, then add the text while keeping the same space.

## Gotchas
- Over-modification makes text unintelligible; modify only enough to keep the tone even.
- Pushing more air instead of modifying causes strain and pitch sharpness.
- Mirror check: jaw jutting or tongue retraction are signs of compensation.

## Verify
- [ ] A scale through the passaggio has even tone with no audible break.
- [ ] Text remains intelligible on high notes.
- [ ] No throat tension or loudness increase as pitch rises.
