# Contemporary vocal styling

**Goal:** make a song sound idiomatic for its genre with deliberate, repeatable effects that
don't hurt the voice.

## 1. Styling tools
| Tool | What it is | Typical genre use | Safe practice |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Onsets**: breathy (h), simultaneous, glottal | how the note starts | breathy = intimacy (pop ballad); glottal = emphasis (rock, pop "uh-oh") | glottal onsets light, not hard clicks |
| Breathy tone | air through partly closed folds | soft pop, jazz | short phrases; heavy breathiness dries the folds |
| **Vocal fry** | creaky low register | phrase starts/ends in pop, R&B | very low effort; never push volume in fry |
| **Cry / sob** | tilted thyroid, emotional break | country, soul, ballads | keep larynx comfortable |
| **Twang** | narrowed epilarynx, bright | country, belt support, musical theatre | used for brightness instead of shouting |
| Slides/scoops | approaching from below | R&B, country | land on pitch, don't stay flat |
| Riffs and runs | melismas | R&B, gospel | see vocal-agility skill |
| Phrase-end releases | breath, fall-off, vibrato onset | all | vary them; don't end every phrase the same |
| Distortion/grit | false-fold or aryepiglottic rattle | rock, metal | learn from a qualified teacher; stop at any pain |

## 2. Applying it to a song
1. Listen to 3 reference singers in the genre and note where they use each device (verse vs chorus, line starts vs ends).
2. Mark your lyric sheet: onsets, breathy vs full, where vibrato starts (late in pop), where riffs go.
3. Contrast is the point: plainer verses, more effects in later choruses.
4. Record and review: effects should be audible but not distracting from the lyric.

## Gotchas
- Imitating a recording's processed sound (compression, Auto-Tune) with physical effort; that grit or "crispness" may be production.
- Using the same onset and release on every phrase sounds mannered.
- Pain, hoarseness after singing, or loss of high notes = stop and review technique with a teacher.

## Verify
- Recorded A/B: plain vs styled version of the same chorus; the styled version should sound more genre-authentic without pitch loss.
- Voice feels the same (or better) after a full run-through.
