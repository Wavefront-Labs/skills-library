# Delivering a studio vocal take

**Goal:** takes that are consistent and emotionally right, so the producer can comp a
great vocal quickly.

## 1. Headphone mix
- Ask for enough of your own voice (with a bit of reverb) to hear pitch; not so much
  you under-sing.
- One ear off if you pitch better hearing the room.
- Ask for the key instruments up (bass, piano/guitar for pitch; drums for time).

## 2. Mic technique
- Stay at a constant distance (often 15-20 cm from a condenser, behind a pop filter).
- Do not move on loud notes unless asked; the engineer manages compression.
- Avoid touching the stand or headphone cable during takes.

## 3. Performing
- Sing the whole song through several times for the producer to comp.
- Then fix sections ("drop-ins") as directed.
- Keep phrasing consistent between takes so lines join.
- Imagine an audience; studios feel dead and you need to supply energy.
- Hydrate between takes; rest the voice when it starts to tire.

## 4. Working with the producer
- Ask what they want (more breath, less vibrato, more attitude).
- Doubles and harmonies: match the lead's phrasing and consonants exactly.

## Gotchas
- Hearing yourself too loud in headphones causes flat singing.
- Changing mic distance between takes makes comping obvious.
- Over-singing after many takes: stop and come back fresh.

## Verify
- [ ] At least three full-song takes before drop-ins.
- [ ] Consistent mic distance and phrasing across takes.
- [ ] Doubles match the lead.
