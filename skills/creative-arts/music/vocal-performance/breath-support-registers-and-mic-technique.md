# Breath support, registers and mic technique

**Goal:** sing with a steady, free tone across your range, warm up efficiently,
recognise when to stop, and sound good through a live microphone.

## 1. Breath support
- **Inhale low and wide:** expansion around the lower ribs, belly and back, shoulders
  still. Raising the shoulders on the breath creates neck tension.
- **Support = controlled release:** keep the ribs expanded as you sing (the classical
  *appoggio* idea) so air flows steadily rather than in a rush.
- Exercise: hiss on "sss" for a steady 20–30 s at even volume; then sing a 5-note scale
  on one breath with the same evenness.
- Don't push air harder to get louder on high notes — more pressure usually means more
  strain, not more sound.

## 2. Registers
- **Chest voice:** speech-like, strong, lower range.
- **Head voice:** lighter, higher; the classical upper register.
- **Falsetto:** breathy, airy upper register (often in male voices).
- **Mix:** a blend that carries chest-like quality higher.
- **Passaggio:** the transition zone where the voice may "flip" or crack. Approximate
  ranges: around E4–G4 for many male voices, E4–F5 area for many female voices — but it
  varies; find yours with slow sirens.
- Smooth the break with **sirens** (slide low to high on "ng" or "oo") rather than
  forcing chest voice upward.

## 3. Ten-minute warm-up
1. Body: shoulder rolls, jaw release, gentle neck stretches (1 min).
2. Breath: hissing and pulsing "sh-sh-sh" from the belly (1 min).
3. **SOVT** (semi-occluded vocal tract) exercises: lip trills, humming, singing through
   a straw — they reduce strain while warming the folds (3 min).
4. Sirens through the whole range on "ng" (2 min).
5. Scales on vowels ("mee-meh-mah-moh-moo"), 5-note then octave (2 min).
6. A verse of your opening song at half volume (1 min).

## 4. Belting safely
Belt is a loud, speech-like high sound. It works with a bright, open,
"yell-like" vowel ("eh", "ae"), firm support and **twang** (a bright, narrowed sound),
not by shouting harder. Learn it with a teacher; build gradually.

## 5. Vocal health
- Hydrate (water hours before, not just during); steam inhalation helps dry rooms.
- **Red flags:** hoarseness lasting more than about two weeks, pain while singing,
  sudden loss of range, or voice "breaking" mid-note that persists — stop and see a
  laryngologist/ENT.
- Rest after heavy use; avoid whispering (it can strain) and shouting over noise.

## 6. Microphone technique (live, dynamic mic such as an SM58)
- Sing **on-axis**, 2–5 cm from the grille for normal levels.
- **Proximity effect:** closer = more bass. Use it for intimacy on soft passages.
- **Pull back** 10–15 cm on loud high notes to avoid distortion; push in on quiet lines.
- Don't cup the grille — it causes feedback and a honky tone.
- Ask for your voice in your monitor; pitch problems are often monitor problems.

## Gotchas
- "Pushing through" a cold or laryngitis to finish a gig is a common route to injury.
- Nearly every passaggio problem gets worse with volume; fix it quietly first.
- Uncertain: register terminology varies between methods (Estill, classical, CVT).

## Verify
- [ ] 20+ s steady hiss with shoulders relaxed.
- [ ] Siren across the full range without a hard break (or with a controlled one).
- [ ] Warm-up done before every rehearsal/show.
- [ ] No pain or persisting hoarseness after singing.
- [ ] Recorded live: consistent level through loud and soft passages.
