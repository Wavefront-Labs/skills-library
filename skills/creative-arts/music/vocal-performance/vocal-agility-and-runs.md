# Vocal agility and runs

**Goal:** fast melismatic passages (gospel/R&B runs, pop riffs, Baroque coloratura)
that are accurate and free of tension.

## 1. Lightness first
Agility needs a light, flexible voice. Sing runs with less weight than sustained
singing; heavy chest voice slows pitch changes.

## 2. Slow practice method
1. Transcribe the run note by note (use slowed playback, e.g. at 50% speed).
2. Sing each note on a vowel slowly and in time with a metronome.
3. Group notes (3s or 4s) and accent the first of each group.
4. Increase tempo in small steps; back off if accuracy slips.
5. Sing it at full speed with the lyric.

## 3. Patterns to drill
- Pentatonic and blues scale fragments up and down (the backbone of most R&B runs).
- Turns around a target note.
- Classical: scales on "ah" in triplets and sixteenths (Vaccai, Concone exercises).

## 4. Articulation
- Classical coloratura: clean separation without aspirating ("h") each note.
- Pop: slight glottal or vowel shifts are stylistic but keep pitch clear.

## Gotchas
- Speed before accuracy builds sloppy habits.
- Over-riffing distracts from the lyric; use runs at phrase ends.
- Jaw or tongue bouncing indicates tension.

## Verify
- [ ] The run, recorded, matches the transcription at full speed.
- [ ] No aspirated "h" between notes (classical) unless stylistic.
- [ ] Jaw and tongue are relaxed in a mirror.
