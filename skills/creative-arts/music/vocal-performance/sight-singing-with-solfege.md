# Sight-singing with solfege

**Goal:** sing an unseen melody in tune and in time at first sight.

## 1. Thirty-second preparation
1. Key and mode (signature + last note); metre and tempo.
2. Scan for: leaps, accidentals, rhythmic traps, the highest/lowest notes (is the range comfortable? choose octave).
3. Establish the key aloud or silently: sing **do-mi-sol-do'-sol-mi-do**, then the starting note's syllable.
4. Set a slow, steady pulse (conduct or tap).

## 2. Reading
- Read by **scale degree** (solfege syllables), not by note name; your ear knows how "sol to do" sounds.
- Steps are easy; for leaps, find the chord they belong to (do-mi-sol = tonic arpeggio; sol-ti-re = dominant) and sing through the chord tones silently.
- Accidentals: raised notes lean up (fi to sol, si to la), lowered lean down (te to la, le to sol).
- Keep going: a wrong note is better than a stop. Rhythm accuracy matters as much as pitch.

## 3. Daily routine (15 min)
- 2 min: scales and arpeggios in solfege in the day's key.
- 3 min: interval drills from do (do-sol, do-fa, do-la, do-ti-do).
- 10 min: 4-6 new short melodies (Ottman, Sight Reading Factory, hymn-book alto lines), each once only, slow tempo, conducting.

## Gotchas
- Drifting flat over a long melody: re-check do at phrase ends (a quick check on a piano in practice, never in an exam).
- Singing note names in fixed-do systems hides function; if your choir uses fixed do, still think in scale degrees.
- Going too fast; choose a tempo where you can see two beats ahead.

## Verify
- Record yourself, then compare to the melody played on piano: count pitch and rhythm errors.
- Progress test monthly: grade level of material at which you get 90% correct.
