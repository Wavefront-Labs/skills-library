# Applying conservation ethics and decision-making

**Goal:** make defensible treatment decisions, documented and agreed with stakeholders.

## Sources
- AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (United States).
- E.C.C.O. Professional Guidelines (Europe), in three parts covering the profession, a code of ethics and education.
- Icon professional standards (United Kingdom).
- BS EN 16853: conservation process, decision making, planning and implementation.

## Core principles in the codes
- Respect for the object's integrity, history and significance.
- Minimal intervention, and only what is needed.
- Use of compatible, stable, tested materials.
- Retreatability: modern thinking prefers this to absolute "reversibility", since few treatments can be fully undone; choose materials and methods that do not prevent later treatment.
- Documentation of everything done.
- Competence: work within your skill and refer when needed.
- Respect for owners and communities of origin.

## Decision steps
1. Establish the object's significance and the owner's aims.
2. Examine, diagnose and understand causes.
3. Define options, including no treatment and preventive measures.
4. Weigh risk and benefit against the principles; consult stakeholders.
5. Record the decision, its reasons and who agreed.
6. Carry out, document and review.

## Verify
- [ ] Options and reasons written.
- [ ] Stakeholders consulted.
- [ ] Decision recorded in the object file.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
