# Microchemical spot tests

**Goal:** answer simple material questions with tiny samples when instruments are not available or not needed.

## Typical tests
- **Chloride:** silver nitrate solution on an acidified extract gives a white precipitate of silver chloride.
- **Carbonate:** a drop of dilute acid produces fine bubbles (effervescence).
- **Iron(II):** bathophenanthroline paper turns pink-red (see `iron-gall-ink-corrosion-management`).
- **Starch:** dilute iodine solution turns blue-black.
Many others exist in the literature; each has interferences.

## Method
1. Take a particle of the sample under the stereomicroscope (see `stereomicroscopy-and-paint-cross-sections`).
2. Put it on a clean glass slide or ceramic spot plate; add a drop of reagent with a micro-pipette or fine capillary.
3. Observe under magnification; run a blank (reagent alone) and, if possible, a known control.
4. Record the result with photographs.
5. Collect and dispose of waste in labelled containers.

## Limits
- Spot tests can give false positives and cannot identify mixtures.
- Sampling must be agreed; do not sample without consent.

## Safety
Reagents (silver nitrate, acids, iodine, others) are hazardous: use gloves, eye protection, and follow chemical-hygiene and waste regulations and safety data sheets.

## Verify
- [ ] Blank and control run.
- [ ] Result photographed and recorded.
- [ ] Waste disposed of safely.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
