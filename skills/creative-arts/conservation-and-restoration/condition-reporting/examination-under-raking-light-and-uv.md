# Examination under raking light and UV for condition reports

**Goal:** produce a condition record that another person can use to tell, on a later
date, whether anything has changed — by examining under several lights in a fixed order
and describing what you see with standard terms, located precisely.

> **Status: draft.** Practice is standard in museums and conservation studios; the
> terminology below is common English usage, but institutions have their own forms and
> glossaries — use theirs where they exist.

## Setup
- Clean gloves (nitrile for most objects; clean cotton only where grip isn't an issue —
  see `creative-arts.conservation-and-restoration.art-handling-and-mounting.safe-handling-and-carrying-artworks`).
- A padded, clear table; the work flat or on an easel at a stable angle.
- Lights: daylight-balanced lamp, a lamp or torch you can hold at a **low angle**, a
  longwave **UV-A torch (~365 nm, filtered)**, a loupe or head magnifier (×3–×10), and a
  camera with a scale bar and colour target.
- The report form, or a printed photo of the work to mark up.

## Examination order
1. **Overall, normal light.** Front, back, edges, frame or mount. Record medium, support,
   dimensions, inscriptions/labels on the back, how it's hung or housed.
2. **Raking light.** Hold the lamp almost parallel to the surface (~5–15°), from each of
   the four sides in turn. It throws shadows from any relief: **cupping** and **tenting**
   paint, **flaking**, cockling of paper, canvas deformations from stretcher bars, dents,
   lifting veneer. Photograph from the side that shows it best and record that direction.
3. **Transmitted light** (paintings on canvas, works on paper where safe): a lamp behind
   shows thin areas, holes, tears, old patches and losses as bright spots.
4. **UV-A fluorescence, room dark.** Aged natural varnish glows greenish-milky; **later
   retouching and overpaint usually appear as dark patches** above it; shellac glows
   orange; some fluorescent adhesives, optical brighteners in modern papers/textiles glow
   bright blue-white. Photograph with the UV source fixed and a UV-blocking filter on the
   lens if you have one.
5. **Magnified details** of anything active: flaking edges, mould, insect frass, rust.

## Describe with a fixed vocabulary
Use one term per phenomenon and keep it consistent across reports:
- **Crack / craquelure** (network of age cracks — usually stable) vs **flaking** (paint
  lifting and at risk) vs **loss** (gone).
- **Abrasion** (worn surface), **scratch**, **dent**, **tear**, **puncture**, **hole**.
- **Cockling** (paper waves), **cupping** (paint islands curling at edges), **tenting** (paint
  ridges lifting), **blanching/bloom** (whitish haze).
- **Accretion**, **grime**, **foxing** (reddish-brown spots on paper), **mould**, **staining**,
  **tide line**.
- State: **stable** or **active/unstable**. This is the line that matters for loans and handling.

Locate each item: clock positions or a grid ("upper left quadrant, 12 cm from top, 4 cm
from left"), and draw it on the photo. Measure significant damage.

## Gotchas
- **UV is evidence, not proof.** Dark patches can be overpaint, but also pigments that
  absorb UV (some earths, some lakes) or thick varnish variation. Write "dark under UV,
  consistent with retouching", not "retouched".
- **UV safety:** don't look into the beam, wear UV-blocking glasses for prolonged work,
  keep exposure short — UV is also damaging to the object.
- Don't photograph raking light from only one side; damage parallel to the light is invisible.
- Record what you *couldn't* examine (glazed, framed, in a case) — absence in the report
  is otherwise read as absence of damage.
- Date, name, lighting, and camera settings on every report.

## Verify
- [ ] Examined under normal, raking (four directions), transmitted (if appropriate) and UV light.
- [ ] Each condition item uses standard terms, is located, measured and marked on a photo.
- [ ] Stable vs active damage clearly stated.
- [ ] UV findings worded as observations, not conclusions.
- [ ] Limitations, date, examiner and conditions recorded.
