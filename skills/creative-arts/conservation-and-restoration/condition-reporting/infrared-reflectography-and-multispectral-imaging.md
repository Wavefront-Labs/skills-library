# Infrared reflectography and multispectral imaging

**Goal:** see beneath paint layers and distinguish materials by their response to different wavelengths.

## Infrared reflectography (IRR)
- Many paints become transparent in the infrared, so carbon-based underdrawing can show through.
- Near infrared (roughly 700 to 1000 nm) works with a camera modified to remove its infrared-blocking filter, fitted with a long-pass filter. Shortwave infrared (about 900 to 1700 nm) uses an InGaAs camera and penetrates more pigments.
- Light with stable broad-spectrum sources (halogen or suitable LEDs) at a distance that does not heat the surface.
- Large works are captured in tiles and stitched with consistent exposure.

## Multispectral imaging (MSI)
- Take a series of images through narrow band filters or with narrow-band lighting, from ultraviolet to near infrared.
- Compare responses to separate pigments and retouching; false-colour infrared combines infrared and visible channels.
- Include a reflectance standard in each image to calibrate.

## Procedure
1. Fix camera and object; set up even lighting; include a target.
2. Capture each band with the same geometry; check focus changes for each filter.
3. Calibrate, stack and review; annotate findings.
4. Interpret with an expert, because IR response depends on pigments and layers.

## Verify
- [ ] Reflectance standard in every frame.
- [ ] Tiles stitched without exposure steps.
- [ ] Findings tied to examination notes.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
