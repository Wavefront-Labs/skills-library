# FTIR spectroscopy for conservation

**Goal:** identify organic and some inorganic materials from their infrared absorption spectrum.

## What it identifies
Binding media, natural and synthetic resins, adhesives, consolidants, plastics, fibres and some minerals and corrosion products. It gives molecular information, which XRF does not.

## Procedure
1. Take a very small sample (micrograms to a fraction of a milligram) with consent and records, or use a microscope accessory for micro-samples; some portable instruments can measure surfaces without sampling.
2. Prepare: attenuated total reflectance (ATR) with a diamond crystal is common; press the sample against the crystal with a controlled force; clean the crystal after.
3. Measure the spectrum over the usual mid-infrared range, collect a background first, and record settings.
4. Compare with reference libraries such as the IRUG spectral database and published spectra; interpret peaks, not just the best match.
5. Record the identification with the confidence and any ambiguities.

## Limits
- Mixtures and ageing complicate spectra.
- Sample contamination by handling or adhesives is common.
- Interpretation needs training.

## Verify
- [ ] Background and settings recorded.
- [ ] Identification supported by key bands.
- [ ] Sample location and remaining sample recorded.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
