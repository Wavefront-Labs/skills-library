# Artist interviews for contemporary art

**Goal:** record the artist's own knowledge about materials, intent and care while it is available.

## Preparation
1. Research the artist's work, materials and any existing documentation.
2. Prepare topics for the specific work (see below), and send them in advance if suitable.
3. Agree consent, recording method, access and rights, and who will hold the record.

## Topics
- Concept and meaning; what must stay the same.
- Materials, suppliers and techniques; substitutions that are acceptable.
- Fabrication and installation; who did it; instructions and diagrams.
- Ageing: what change is acceptable (fading, yellowing, oxidation, wear) and what is not.
- Handling, display, lighting, environment and loan conditions.
- Repair and replacement: what the artist wants if parts fail.
- Future: who may decide after the artist's lifetime.

## Practices
- Follow guidance from INCCA and similar networks for interviews.
- Record audio or video and keep a transcript; ask for review and clarification.
- Store with the object and in the collections system (see `recording-conservation-data-in-a-collections-management-system`).
- Distinguish statements of fact from preferences.

## Verify
- [ ] Consent and rights recorded.
- [ ] Key questions on intent, change and replacement covered.
- [ ] Record stored and linked to the object.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
