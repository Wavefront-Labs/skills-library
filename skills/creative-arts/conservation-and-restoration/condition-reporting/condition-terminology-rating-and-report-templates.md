# Condition terminology, rating and report templates

**Goal:** reports that different people can write and read the same way.

## Standard and sources
BS EN 16095 (Conservation of cultural property: condition recording for movable cultural heritage) sets principles for recording condition. The Spectrum standard (Collections Trust) covers condition checking procedures. Neither replaces a house style; both support one.

## Template fields
- Object identity: accession number, title or description, maker and date, materials and technique.
- Administrative: date, examiner, reason (loan, acquisition, exhibition, survey), location, lighting and equipment used.
- Dimensions and weight, including frame or mount.
- Description of condition by area: support, surface, edges, back, frame, mount.
- Previous repairs and alterations.
- Photographs and diagrams, referenced by file name.
- Overall condition rating and handling or display recommendations.
- Signature and date.

## Terminology
1. Adopt a short glossary (for example: abrasion, accretion, craquelure, crack, cupping, flaking, loss, scratch, stain, tear, tenting) and define each; use the same terms in all reports.
2. Describe damage by location, size, and appearance, not by guessed cause.
3. Distinguish "observed" from "interpreted".

## Rating scale
- Use a small number of levels (for example good, fair, poor, unstable) with written criteria for each, including whether the object may travel or be displayed.
- Apply the same criteria in every report; calibrate staff with sample objects.

## Verify
- [ ] Glossary and rating criteria written and shared.
- [ ] Template tested on three varied objects.
- [ ] Reports comparable between examiners.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
