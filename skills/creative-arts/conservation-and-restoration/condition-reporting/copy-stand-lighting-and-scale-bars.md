# Copy stand lighting and scale bars

**Goal:** even, glare-free images of flat objects with trustworthy scale.

## Setup
1. Mount the camera on a copy stand with the sensor parallel to the artwork; check with a level and by looking for equal dimensions of a square target.
2. Place two lights, one on each side, at roughly 45 degrees to the surface, at equal distances; check evenness with a grey card and by measuring the light across the field.
3. Remove glare by moving lights, using polarising filters on lights and lens (crossed), or shooting framed works at an angle that avoids reflection.
4. Use a lens at its sharpest aperture; avoid wide-angle distortion.

## Scale and labels
1. Place a scale bar beside the object in the same plane, with clear millimetre and centimetre marks; for thick objects, place at the same height as the surface of interest.
2. Add a label with accession number and orientation (top arrow).
3. Keep the same scale in the set of images.

## Cautions
- Heat and UV from lights can damage; use cool lamps and limit exposure.
- Do not place labels or scales on the object.

## Verify
- [ ] Even lighting on a grey card test.
- [ ] Scale bar in plane of the surface.
- [ ] Orientation and ID in each image.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
