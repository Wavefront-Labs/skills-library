# Pesticide-contaminated objects

**Goal:** protect health and manage contaminated collections respectfully and legally.

## Background
In the 19th and 20th centuries, museums often treated natural-history and ethnographic objects with arsenic, mercury compounds, DDT, naphthalene, paradichlorobenzene and other pesticides. Residues may remain, as dust, crystals, odour or surface deposits. Records are often incomplete.

## Assess
1. Review the object and collection records for treatment history; assume risk for organic objects acquired before the mid-20th century if no information exists.
2. Screen surfaces with portable XRF for elements such as arsenic and mercury; use wipe sampling or laboratory analysis for organic pesticides.
3. Seek advice from an industrial hygienist or health and safety officer; follow NPS Museum Handbook guidance and local law.

## Controls
- Wear nitrile gloves, a lab coat and, as advised, a respirator; avoid skin contact and dust.
- Work with extraction or in a fume cupboard; clean surfaces with a HEPA vacuum and damp wiping.
- Isolate and label contaminated objects; use separate storage and handling rules.
- Do not wash objects casually; contaminated wash water is hazardous waste.

## Ethical and legal points
- Items of Indigenous origin may be subject to law (for example NAGPRA in the United States); consult affected communities before any testing or treatment.
- Decontamination is difficult and damages objects; preference is containment.

## Verify
- [ ] Risk assessment recorded.
- [ ] PPE and cleaning protocols in place.
- [ ] Community consultation recorded where applicable.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
