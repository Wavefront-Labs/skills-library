# Furniture finishes and gilded surfaces

**Goal:** preserve original coatings and gilding, with minimal intervention.

## Finishes
1. Identify the finish if possible: shellac, oil or resin varnish, wax or lacquer; tests are done by conservators under controlled conditions.
2. Dry clean first with soft brushes and low suction.
3. Remove old wax and soil with mild solvent (for example a low-aromatic hydrocarbon) on swabs after testing in an unnoticeable area; avoid solvents that dissolve shellac or varnish.
4. Revive dull finishes by waxing with microcrystalline wax rather than stripping; avoid silicone polishes.
5. Repairs and consolidation of failing finishes belong to a conservator.

## Gilded surfaces
- Water gilding (gold leaf burnished over bole and gesso) is water-sensitive; oil gilding is more robust.
- Clean gently with dry brushes; avoid water and solvents until tested.
- Flaking gesso and gilding can be consolidated with a compatible adhesive (animal glue or an acrylic) applied under magnification.
- Losses can be filled and toned, not replaced with new gilding without agreement.

## Cautions
- Never use household polishes, which leave residues that darken.
- Keep frames away from heat and humidity change.

## Verify
- [ ] Original finish and gilding retained.
- [ ] Tests recorded.
- [ ] Wax or consolidant types recorded.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
