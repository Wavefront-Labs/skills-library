# Ceramic reassembly with reversible adhesives

**Goal:** put a broken pot, figure or tile back together with joins that are aligned,
strong enough for handling and display, and **reversible in solvent** — the default for
museum and archaeological ceramics.

> **Status: draft.** Method follows common conservation practice (Koob's B-72 method).
> Adhesive concentrations are typical; low-fired or friable bodies, and heavy objects
> needing epoxy, need conservator judgement.

## 1. Assess and clean
- **Body type:** porous earthenware/low-fired archaeological sherds behave very
  differently from vitrified porcelain. Porous edges soak up adhesive (weak join, stain);
  they usually need a **barrier/consolidating coat** of dilute B-72 (~5% in acetone) on
  the break edges first, allowed to dry.
- **Old repairs:** animal glue, shellac (fluoresces orange under UV), epoxies, rivets,
  plaster. Most are softened with warm water, solvents or poultices; remove them before
  rebonding. Never force a joint apart.
- Clean break edges (soft brush, water/ethanol swabs, scalpel for old adhesive). Dirt in
  a join is the commonest cause of a "step".
- **Salts** in archaeological ceramics need desalination first or the join and surface
  will fail later.

## 2. Dry-fit and plan the order
1. Lay out all sherds; tape joins together temporarily with low-tack tape on glazed
   areas (test it first on unglazed bodies — tape can lift surfaces).
2. Work out the bonding sequence. The rule: **build up from a stable base and never
   create a gap that only narrows.** If the final sherd must slide in between two others
   that are already fixed, it will not fit — "locked-out sherd".
3. Number pieces with a removable marker on tape, photograph the dry-fit.

## 3. Bonding with Paraloid B-72
- **Adhesive:** B-72 (ethyl methacrylate copolymer) at roughly **40–50% w/v in acetone**
  (some add a little ethanol to slow drying), filled into a squeeze tube. Pre-made tubes
  exist; homemade ones are made by suspending beads in a mesh bag in acetone for a day or two.
- Apply a thin bead to one edge, press the pair together, wiggle slightly to squeeze out
  excess and seat the join, and check alignment with a fingertip across the join — you
  should feel no step.
- **Clamp:** strips of tape across the join under light tension, and the object held in a
  **sandbox** (tray of sand or glass beads) so gravity holds the join closed.
- B-72 joins sets in minutes and reaches strength over a day; joins can be re-softened
  with acetone vapour or a swab and adjusted. Clean squeeze-out with an acetone swab once
  rubbery.
- Bond in sub-assemblies, let them cure, then join the sub-assemblies.

**Heavy or load-bearing pieces** (large stoneware, handles bearing weight, outdoor
objects): B-72 can creep; an epoxy may be needed, ideally over a B-72 barrier coat so it
can still be removed. That's a conservator's decision.

## 4. Losses
Fill gaps with plaster or a proprietary fill (over a barrier coat on porous bodies), shape
slightly below surface, and tone to read as a neutral or matching infill depending on the
display brief (museum vs. private "invisible" restoration).

## Safety
- Acetone is highly flammable and the vapour builds up quickly over a sandbox: work with
  **local extraction**, no flames, small capped quantities.
- Nitrile gloves are adequate for brief acetone contact but degrade with soaking; change them.
- Epoxies are skin sensitisers: always gloves; mix in ventilation.
- Archaeological sherds can carry soil fungi and historic consolidants — gloves and dust control.

## Gotchas
- Too-thin adhesive starves the join; too-thick stops the edges seating — feel for steps.
- Joins pulled out of line by tape tension: tape on both sides, opposing pulls.
- Temperature: B-72 joins can soften in hot display cases or cars (glass transition ~40 °C).

## Verify
- [ ] Old adhesives removed; edges clean; porous edges barrier-coated.
- [ ] Dry-fit done and bonding order planned with no locked-out sherds.
- [ ] Joins step-free to the fingertip; squeeze-out removed.
- [ ] Extraction used for acetone; epoxy (if any) over a barrier layer.
- [ ] Adhesive, fills and order recorded for the next conservator.
