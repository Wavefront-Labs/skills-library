# Degrading plastics and rubber

**Goal:** slow the decay of unstable materials and protect other collections.

## Cellulose nitrate (celluloid)
- Early plastic; degrades to give acidic gases (nitrogen oxides), discoloration, cracking and shrinkage; sometimes a camphor smell.
- Cellulose nitrate is flammable; large quantities of nitrate film have special storage rules. Follow your local fire and safety regulations.
- Isolate; do not seal tightly. Use ventilated storage and a cool environment.

## Cellulose acetate
- Loses acetic acid (vinegar smell), shrinks and becomes brittle (see `detecting-and-managing-vinegar-syndrome` for film).
- Store apart, ventilated, cool and dry.

## Natural rubber and other elastomers
- Oxidise, become sticky or hard and crack; surface bloom of sulphur and waxes may appear; very light and ozone-sensitive.
- Store dark, cool and with minimal oxygen exposure; do not stack. Avoid contact with other plastics.

## General steps
1. Identify the material (see `identifying-and-cleaning-plastics`).
2. Photograph and document condition.
3. Isolate from the rest of the collection and from each other.
4. Monitor with periodic checks; consider A-D strips for acid release.
5. Consider copying or documenting the object, since decay is irreversible.

## Verify
- [ ] Hazard and fire rules checked for nitrate.
- [ ] Items isolated and ventilated.
- [ ] Condition monitored.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
