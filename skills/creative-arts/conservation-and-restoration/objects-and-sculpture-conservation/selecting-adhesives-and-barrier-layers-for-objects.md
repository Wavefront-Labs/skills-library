# Selecting adhesives and barrier layers for objects

**Goal:** a join that is strong enough, durable enough and as retreatable as the object allows.

## Candidates
- **Paraloid B-72** (ethyl methacrylate / methyl acrylate copolymer) in acetone or other solvents: widely used; stable; reversible by solvents; moderate strength; research including work by Podany and colleagues compares its strength and reversibility.
- **Epoxy resins** (for example HXTAL NYL-1 or Araldite 2020): strong and clear; irreversible in practice; may yellow; used where strength is needed, like heavy ceramic or glass.
- **Hide glue and animal glues:** reversible with water, used for wood and some organic materials.
- **PVA emulsions:** flexible, used for wood and some composite objects, may creep.
- **Cellulose nitrate cements:** historic; yellow and embrittle; not recommended for new work.

## Choosing
1. List the load, expected handling and display conditions.
2. Choose the weakest adhesive that holds, to protect the object if it fails.
3. Consider working time, gap filling, colour, refractive index and solvent sensitivity of the object.
4. Test on mock-ups where possible.

## Barrier layers
Before bonding with epoxy, coat the clean break surfaces with a thin film of dilute B-72 and let it dry. The barrier limits adhesive penetration into porous material and lets solvent reach the interface later, so the join can be separated.

## Verify
- [ ] Adhesive justified in the report.
- [ ] Barrier film dry before bonding.
- [ ] Batch and mixing ratio recorded.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
