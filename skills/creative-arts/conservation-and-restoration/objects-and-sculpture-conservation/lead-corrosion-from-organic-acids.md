# Lead corrosion from organic acids

**Goal:** stop lead objects (and lead in composite objects) corroding from their storage or display materials.

## Cause
Lead corrodes rapidly in the presence of acetic or formic acid vapours, which are emitted by some woods (oak and some others), wood products, adhesives and paints. The white crust that forms contains lead carbonate and lead acetate or formate compounds.

## Recognition and assessment
1. Look for white or grey powdery corrosion, growth on the surface and a crust that can expand and crack.
2. Check the storage and display materials for sources of acid (cabinets, boards, packing).
3. Measure acid vapour with passive indicators or sampling where possible (see `pollutant-monitoring-with-ad-strips-and-sorbents`).

## Treatment (conservator only)
- Remove the corrosion mechanically or with chelating solutions (for example citrate or EDTA-based) under controlled conditions, or by electrochemical methods; techniques differ and risk damaging the surface, so follow published guidance.
- Rinse and dry; protect with a coating or wax if agreed.

## Prevention
- Remove the acid source; use Oddy-tested materials (see `oddy-test-for-display-materials`).
- Ventilate storage and cases; use sorbents (see `pollutant-monitoring-with-ad-strips-and-sorbents`).
- Do not seal lead objects into cases with untested wood or adhesive.

## Safety
Lead and lead compounds are toxic. Do not eat, drink or smoke near; use gloves, an appropriate respirator and extraction; wash hands; handle waste as hazardous.

## Verify
- [ ] Acid source identified and removed.
- [ ] Corrosion removed without surface loss.
- [ ] Lead safety measures followed.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
