# Removing biological growth and graffiti from monuments

**Goal:** clean without eroding surfaces or leaving harmful residues.

## Biological growth
1. Identify the growth (algae, moss, lichen) and the stone or metal beneath; some lichens are protective, some harmful, and removal can harm the surface.
2. Reduce moisture sources first (drainage, splashback, trees).
3. Remove loose growth with water and soft brushes.
4. If a biocide is needed, use a product legal in your jurisdiction, with a test patch; quaternary ammonium based products are widely used in monument care. Follow label and environmental rules.
5. Leave it to act and rinse as directed; growth often dies and weathers off over weeks.

## Graffiti
1. Photograph and report to the owner or authorities if vandalism.
2. Identify the paint or marker; test solvent gels or poultices on a small area; porous stone absorbs paint deeply.
3. Use the weakest effective method; avoid high-pressure water, abrasive blasting and strong paint strippers.
4. Consider a sacrificial protective coating (to be renewed) on frequently attacked surfaces.

## Cautions
- Biocides and solvents are hazards: PPE, waste handling and permits.
- Never use acid on carbonate stone.

## Verify
- [ ] Test patch accepted.
- [ ] No loss of stone or patina.
- [ ] Regulatory and waste requirements followed.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
