# Monument condition survey

**Goal:** a repeatable record that supports decisions and funding.

## Content of a survey
1. **Identification:** title, maker, date, location, owner, legal protections, history and previous treatments.
2. **Description:** materials, dimensions, construction and foundation.
3. **Condition:** cracks, losses, corrosion, biological growth, soiling, graffiti, earlier repairs, and structural stability (leaning, loose parts).
4. **Causes:** water, salts, pollution, vandalism, roots, vibration, past interventions.
5. **Risk:** public safety, collapse and theft risk.
6. **Photographs:** overall, details, and the same angles each time; scale and date.
7. **Recommendations:** immediate action, maintenance, treatment, monitoring interval, and priority.

## Using survey frameworks
Programmes such as Save Outdoor Sculpture! in the United States and Historic England guidance provide forms and criteria.

## Procedure
- Inspect from the ground and from safe access; use binoculars and scaffolds as needed; never climb unsafe structures.
- Record in a standard form or database; store images with the record.
- Repeat on a schedule and compare.

## Verify
- [ ] All sections complete.
- [ ] Photos consistent and dated.
- [ ] Priorities agreed with the owner.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
