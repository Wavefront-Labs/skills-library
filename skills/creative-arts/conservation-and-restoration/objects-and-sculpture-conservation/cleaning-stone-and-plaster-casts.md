# Cleaning stone and plaster casts

**Goal:** reduce harmful or disfiguring soiling without eroding surfaces.

## Stone
1. Identify the stone and its condition (porosity, salts, cracks, past treatments) before choosing a method.
2. Start with dry brushing and vacuuming of loose dirt.
3. Water: a fine mist or gentle wetting with soft brushes, kept to the minimum, can lift soiling on sound stone; avoid on salt-laden or very porous stone.
4. Poultices and gels (clay or cellulose with chelating or buffered solutions) can lift deeper soiling; test and follow published formulas.
5. Laser cleaning is a specialist method, effective on some stones and crusts.
6. Avoid abrasive blasting, strong acid or alkali cleaners and high-pressure water, which remove material or leave salts (guidance from Historic England and similar bodies).

## Plaster casts
- Dry clean with a soft brush and low-suction vacuum through a screen; plaster is porous and dissolves slightly, so do not wash.
- Many casts have a shellac or paint coating; test before any solvent.
- Consolidation or coating is a conservator's decision.

## Cautions
- Cleaning can reveal weaknesses; stop if material comes away.
- Wet methods near salts bring salts to the surface.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
