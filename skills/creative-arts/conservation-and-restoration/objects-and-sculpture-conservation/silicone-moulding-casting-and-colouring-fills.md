# Silicone moulding, casting and colouring fills

**Goal:** reproduce missing parts by casting from a mould of the original, and colour them appropriately.

## Moulding
1. Document the area; clean it. Decide if moulding is safe; silicone can leave oily residues in porous material and can pull friable surfaces.
2. Apply a barrier (dilute B-72 or another tested isolating layer) to the surface to be moulded, and a release agent where needed.
3. Use a silicone rubber designed for moulding (condensation or addition cure); check the data sheet for shrinkage and cure time; avoid contact with materials that inhibit cure (sulphur, some latex).
4. Build a support shell (mother mould) of plaster or rigid resin for large moulds.

## Casting
1. Cast fills in a stable material: epoxy with fillers, plaster, or other, depending on the object.
2. Degas if needed; fill the mould in a slow pour to avoid bubbles.
3. Cure, demould and trim.
4. Fix with a reversible or compatible adhesive (see `selecting-adhesives-and-barrier-layers-for-objects`).

## Colouring fills
- Colour the fill with paints in a reversible medium or with pigments in resin, tested for stability; texture to match.
- Slightly lighter or distinguishable fills are usual; the choice is ethical and agreed with the owner.
- Airbrushing and fine tools help blend.

## Verify
- [ ] No silicone residue on the original.
- [ ] Fill fits and is identifiable on close inspection.
- [ ] Materials recorded.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
