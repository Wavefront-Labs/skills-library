# Consolidating insect-damaged wood

**Goal:** restore strength to soft, tunnelled wood while keeping the original material.

## Check activity first
- Fresh bore dust (frass) beneath flight holes, live larvae, or new holes show active infestation; treat the infestation first (see `freezing-and-anoxic-pest-treatment`).
- Do not consolidate an active infestation; it will continue.

## Choose the consolidant
- Paraloid B-72 dissolved in acetone, ethanol or toluene mixtures in low-to-moderate concentration (commonly in the range of about 5 to 20 percent by weight, adjusted to wood density) is widely used; other resins (and wax-resin) appear in the literature.
- Test absorption and appearance on a hidden area. Gloss and darkening are risks.

## Method
1. Brush off loose frass; support fragile areas.
2. Apply the consolidant slowly with a brush, syringe or by dipping/injection, letting the wood absorb; repeat.
3. For large objects, vacuum impregnation may be used by conservators.
4. Wipe surface residues with solvent to avoid gloss.
5. Allow full solvent evaporation.

## Cautions
- Solvents are flammable and harmful; use extraction.
- Consolidant changes wood properties and is not truly reversible.
- Some finishes dissolve in the consolidant solvent; test.

## Verify
- [ ] Infestation inactive and monitored.
- [ ] Wood strengthened without glossy halo.
- [ ] Resin, solvent and concentration recorded.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
