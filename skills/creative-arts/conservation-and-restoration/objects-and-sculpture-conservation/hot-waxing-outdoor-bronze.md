# Hot waxing outdoor bronze

**Goal:** protect patina and slow corrosion through a maintained wax layer.

## Principle
Wax is a sacrificial barrier: it repels water and pollutants, is easy to renew, and does not hide the surface much. Hot application works wax into the patina, giving better coverage.

## Method
1. Survey and photograph; note active corrosion, runs, and losses (see `monument-condition-survey`).
2. Clean: wash with water and mild detergent, rinse, and dry fully; remove loose corrosion only as advised.
3. Warm the metal gently with a heat source until a brushed-on wax melts and flows; do not overheat; stay clear of fire hazards.
4. Brush on a thin, even layer of microcrystalline wax or a conservation wax blend; work in sections.
5. While still warm, brush out excess; buff when cool.
6. Record wax type, date and weather; schedule yearly or periodic reapplication.

## Cautions
- Hot metal and flames: burn and fire risks; follow site safety and permits.
- Do not wax over active corrosion or water-damp metal.
- Avoid applying in rain or high humidity.

## Verify
- [ ] Surface dry and clean before wax.
- [ ] Even sheen with no drips.
- [ ] Maintenance schedule and records kept.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
