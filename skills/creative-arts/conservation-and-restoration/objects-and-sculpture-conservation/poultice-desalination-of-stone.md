# Poultice desalination of stone

**Goal:** reduce soluble salts that damage stone when they crystallise.

## Principle
Water carries salts to the surface; a poultice of absorbent material placed on wetted stone dissolves salts and, as it dries, draws them into itself by evaporation. The poultice is then removed with the salts.

## Method
1. Identify the salts (conductivity, chemical spot tests, ion analysis) and the stone's condition.
2. Pre-wet the stone with deionised water, with care for weak or friable stone.
3. Put a thin tissue barrier on the surface to prevent poultice residue.
4. Apply a thick layer (a few centimetres) of poultice made of cellulose fibre mixed with deionised water; sometimes with sand or clay added.
5. Cover only if the plan requires slow drying; usually leave to dry fully so salts move into the poultice.
6. Remove the poultice, sample it (extract in water and measure conductivity), and repeat until salt extraction is small.

## Cautions
- Water can mobilise salts deeper; only treat if drying proceeds out toward the poultice.
- Check for staining and for loose or friable surfaces.
- Dispose of poultice as per local rules.

## Verify
- [ ] Salt types and levels measured.
- [ ] Extracts reducing each cycle.
- [ ] Stone surface intact.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
