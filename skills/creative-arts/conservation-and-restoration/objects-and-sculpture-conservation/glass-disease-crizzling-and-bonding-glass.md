# Glass disease, crizzling and bonding glass

**Goal:** protect unstable glass and join breaks with a clear, durable adhesive.

## Glass disease
- Some glass (often with high alkali and low stabiliser content, for example certain 17th-19th century and some other glasses) develops a fine network of cracks (crizzling) and may form moisture droplets that run (weeping) when humidity is high.
- Check regularly under magnification and raking light; photograph.

## Management
1. Keep relative humidity stable and moderate, commonly cited around 40 to 45 percent for unstable glass; avoid cycling and condensation. Confirm the target with a glass conservator and the object.
2. Avoid washing; moisture accelerates deterioration; dry clean gently.
3. Consider enclosing in a microclimate with buffered silica gel.
4. Coatings and consolidation are specialist and often controversial; only a conservator decides.

## Bonding broken glass
1. Dry-fit the pieces; record the order; clean edges with solvent.
2. Choose a clear adhesive: epoxy such as HXTAL NYL-1 or similar are widely used for optical clarity; Paraloid B-72 can be used where reversibility is preferred and strength is less critical.
3. Apply in a thin film, align, and support with tape or clamps; avoid stress.
4. Cure in a dust-free place; remove excess carefully.

## Cautions
- Epoxy cannot be removed without risk; weigh this against use.
- Adhesives can yellow; test.

## Verify
- [ ] RH recorded and stable.
- [ ] Joins aligned, clear and free from bubbles.
- [ ] Adhesive and batch recorded.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
