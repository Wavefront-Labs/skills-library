# Pinning and doweling broken stone

**Goal:** reconnect fragments so they carry load without splitting the stone.

## Materials
- **Pins:** austenitic stainless steel (grade 316), titanium, or glass-fibre-reinforced rod. Avoid ordinary iron or steel, which rusts and expands, splitting the stone.
- **Adhesive:** epoxy for structural strength in sound stone, lime-based grouts in some cases, chosen by the stone and the conservator. Test compatibility.

## Method
1. Clean the break and check the stone strength; remove loose or weak material.
2. Dry-fit; mark where pins go, in sound stone, away from edges and carved detail.
3. Drill holes perpendicular to the break, the same diameter and depth in both parts, with a slight clearance; use masking to protect surface.
4. Cut pins to length (determined by load and stone) and check the fit.
5. Apply adhesive in holes and on the faces; insert pins; clamp or support until cured.
6. Clean off excess adhesive before it sets.

## Cautions
- Poorly placed pins create stress and new breaks.
- Epoxy is difficult to reverse; document the position and type of pins.
- Outdoor stone: the join must handle thermal movement and water.

## Verify
- [ ] Alignment true; no gaps.
- [ ] Pin material is corrosion resistant.
- [ ] Positions drawn and recorded.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention.
