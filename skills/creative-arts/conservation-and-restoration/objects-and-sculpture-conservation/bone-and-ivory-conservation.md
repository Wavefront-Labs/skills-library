# Bone and ivory conservation

**Goal:** prevent cracking and surface loss and stay within the law.

## Behaviour
Bone and ivory are anisotropic organic materials; they expand and shrink with humidity changes, split along growth lines and cannot withstand rapid changes. Water and strong solvents may stain or damage.

## Care
1. Dry clean with soft brushes; mild cleaning with dilute aqueous or ethanol swabs only after testing.
2. Keep RH stable and moderate; avoid heat, drafts and display near lights.
3. Consolidate flaking or powdery surfaces with dilute Paraloid B-72 or similar, after testing.
4. Reduce adhesive residues and old repairs with the mildest effective solvent.
5. Avoid bleaching and polishing.
6. Support with soft mounts; do not clamp.

## Legal check
Trade and movement of ivory (elephant and others) are regulated internationally (CITES) and by national laws, which have been tightened in many places. Check requirements before moving, lending or selling any ivory, and document provenance.

## Verify
- [ ] RH stable and logged.
- [ ] No new cracking after treatment.
- [ ] Legal status checked.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
