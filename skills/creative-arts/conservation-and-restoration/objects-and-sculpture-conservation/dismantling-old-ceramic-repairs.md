# Dismantling old ceramic repairs

**Goal:** take apart earlier repairs, which may be failing, discoloured or misaligned, with minimum risk.

## Identify the adhesive
- Animal glue and starch pastes soften in warm water; shellac in ethanol; cellulose nitrate cements in acetone; PVA in acetone or alcohol mixtures; epoxy resists most solvents and may need heat or specialist solvent systems.
- Test with small swabs in an inconspicuous joint, and note the response.
- Find hidden features: rivets, plaster fills, overpaint and dowels; X-radiography can show metal.

## Method
1. Photograph and record all joins and fills.
2. Brace the object; protect it on a sand bag or foam.
3. Apply the chosen solvent by swab, poultice or in a solvent chamber until the join gives; do not force.
4. Ease sections apart gently; support heavy parts.
5. Clean residues from edges with solvent and swabs, avoiding contact with the glaze.
6. Dry and store parts with labels in order.

## Cautions
- Porous ceramics soak up adhesive and stain; limit solvent contact.
- Epoxy removal solvents (often strongly irritating) require full fume extraction and expert handling.
- Do not dismantle where the old repair is keeping an unstable object together unless a safe plan exists.

## Verify
- [ ] All parts accounted for and labelled.
- [ ] Edges clean and intact.
- [ ] Old adhesive types recorded.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
