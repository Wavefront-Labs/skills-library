# Lacquers and waxes for metal objects

**Goal:** apply a protective coating that suits the metal, the environment and the use.

## Options
- **Acrylic lacquers:** Incralac is an acrylic (Paraloid B-44-based) lacquer with benzotriazole for copper alloys, usually in an aromatic solvent such as xylene or toluene; check the current supplier's composition. Provides longer protection when intact but is hard to reapply and remove, and solvents are hazardous.
- **Microcrystalline wax:** products such as Renaissance Wax, applied thinly and buffed; easily maintained, removable, and often chosen for handled or displayed objects; less durable outdoors.
- **Other:** Paraloid B-72 films; polyurethane or others for specific uses, with limited use in conservation.

## Procedure
1. Clean the surface completely and dry.
2. Test appearance on a hidden area; consider gloss.
3. **Lacquer:** brush or spray in thin coats in a fume cupboard; control thickness; let it cure.
4. **Wax:** warm the metal slightly if appropriate, apply a thin layer, allow it to haze, then buff.
5. Record coating type, thickness and date; schedule inspection and renewal (wax typically needs renewal).

## Cautions
- Do not coat active corrosion without treating it first.
- Do not use a wax that contains silicone or abrasives.
- Solvent and fire safety for lacquers.

## Verify
- [ ] No pinholes, runs or streaks in the coating.
- [ ] Appearance accepted.
- [ ] Renewal schedule set.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
