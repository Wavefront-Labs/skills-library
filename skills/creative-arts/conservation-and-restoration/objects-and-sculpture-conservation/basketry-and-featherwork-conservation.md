# Basketry and featherwork conservation

**Goal:** protect fragile plant fibre and feather objects from loss and distortion.

## Basketry
- Plant fibres become brittle when dry; handle with full support.
- Dry clean with a soft brush and low-suction vacuum through a screen.
- Reshape with local humidification (vapour, not liquid water) and padded support forms; dry slowly.
- Repair splits with compatible fibre or tissue and a reversible adhesive.
- Store on padded supports, not stacked.

## Featherwork
- Feathers are fragile, light-sensitive, very attractive to pests, and carry dyes and pigments that fade.
- Dry clean with a soft brush and vacuum through a screen at very low suction, or use a gentle air flow.
- Avoid wetting; avoid pressure; support the object with soft forms.
- Control light strictly and check for pests regularly (see `pest-monitoring-with-blunder-traps-and-identification`).
- Check the legal status; some feathers are regulated.

## Cautions
- Pesticide residue may be present on older items (see `pesticide-contaminated-objects`).
- Do not use adhesives on feathers without conservator input.

## Verify
- [ ] Support fits the object without pressure.
- [ ] Light, pest and RH control recorded.
- [ ] Cleaning method gentle and recorded.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
