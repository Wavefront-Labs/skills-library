# Cleaning and stabilising leather objects

**Goal:** limit loss and distortion in leather artefacts.

## Identify
- Distinguish vegetable-tanned, alum-tawed, oil-tanned (chamois), rawhide, and skin or parchment; different tanning gives different responses to water and humidity.
- Check for red rot (see `consolidating-red-rot-in-leather-bindings`), previous dressings, pests and mould.

## Method
1. Dry-clean with a soft brush and low-suction vacuum through a screen.
2. Do not wash leather; water can cause shrinkage, hardening and loss of strength.
3. Support the shape with padded forms or tissue; humidify gently only under expert direction (see `reshaping-three-dimensional-textiles`).
4. Consolidate crumbling surfaces by conservator methods.
5. Avoid routine dressing with oils or waxes; they can darken, bloom and attract dirt, although they may be chosen in specific cases.

## Storage
- Stable moderate RH, away from heat and light; do not seal in plastic that sweats.
- Support with padded mounts; keep boxes acid-free.

## Verify
- [ ] Type and condition recorded.
- [ ] No wet cleaning attempted.
- [ ] Environment stable.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
