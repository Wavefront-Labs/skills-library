# Lining weak paper

**Goal:** provide an overall support for a sheet too weak to handle, with the least intervention that works.

## Decide first
- Lining is a major, hard-to-reverse treatment; consider alternatives: a sleeve, a mount, tear mending (see `tear-mending-with-wheat-starch-and-japanese-tissue`), local reinforcement or reformatting.
- Check the media for water sensitivity first.

## Materials
- Thin, strong Japanese tissue (kozo) selected to match the paper's weight and tone.
- Wheat starch paste or methyl cellulose, freshly made, diluted to a thin consistency.
- Release layers (polyester web, blotters) and a flat drying board.

## Procedure
1. Humidify or wet the sheet as suits the media; lay face-down on a polyester web on the working surface.
2. Brush paste onto the lining tissue on a separate surface (or apply a thin layer to the reverse of the sheet).
3. Lower the tissue onto the sheet, working from the centre outward to expel air; avoid wrinkles.
4. Blot, cover with a release web and blotters, and dry under very light weights, or by tension drying (see `tension-drying-paper-on-a-karibari`).
5. Trim the tissue edges flush or leave a margin, depending on the object.

## Failure modes
- Cockling or shrinkage if the lining is too heavy or paste too strong.
- Trapped air; media bleed if the sheet was not tested.

## Verify
- [ ] Sheet lies flat and supports its own handling.
- [ ] Lining tissue does not show through the image.
- [ ] Materials and paste strength recorded.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
