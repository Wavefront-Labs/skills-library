# Reattaching detached book boards

**Goal:** restore function to a book whose board or boards have come loose while keeping original material.

## Examine
- Why did it fail: broken cords or slips, torn hinges, weak leather joints, or a split in the text-block to board attachment?
- Is the sewing sound, are the endbands intact, is the spine covering strong?
- Document the binding structure and current damage with photographs.

## Typical methods
1. **Tissue-and-cloth hinge repair:** if the cords are intact but leather has split at the joint, lift the leather on both sides of the split, insert a strip of toned Japanese tissue and lined cloth reaching under the leather on the board and spine, and re-adhere the leather over it.
2. **Cord reattachment:** if the original cords or slips are present, re-lace them through the board using reversible adhesive, or re-lace into new slips added to the cords.
3. **Tipped-on or new hinge:** where the text block has pulled away, add a flexible hinge of cloth or tissue to the first and last leaves and to the board edge.
Adhesives are usually wheat starch paste, methylcellulose or a stable PVA mixed with paste to give flexibility; choose by the leather and board and test.

## Notes
- Keep the old leather; do not replace the spine unless unavoidable (see `rebacking-and-spine-lining-of-books`).
- Dry under light pressure with release layers; do not squeeze out adhesive onto leather.
- Treat red rot leather with care (see `consolidating-red-rot-in-leather-bindings`).

## Verify
- [ ] Board opens and closes without strain.
- [ ] Original material retained and repairs distinguishable.
- [ ] Adhesives and materials recorded.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention.
