# Humidifying paper in a chamber or Gore-Tex sandwich

**Goal:** make creased or distorted paper pliable enough to flatten, without wetting media.

## Humidification chamber
1. Use a sealed box or tent with a humidity source (ultrasonic humidifier or a tray of water); place the sheet on a mesh or tissue support so air reaches both sides.
2. Raise RH gradually and check every few minutes; paper takes up moisture through air, so stop when pliable.
3. Remove, place between blotters and boards under light weights to dry (see `flattening-paper-between-blotters`).

## Gore-Tex sandwich
Gore-Tex is a breathable membrane that passes water vapour but not liquid.
1. Build a stack: a damp blotter, a Gore-Tex layer, the sheet on a thin support, a second Gore-Tex layer, and a dry blotter on top (arrangements vary; follow a published method such as the BPG Wiki and test on scrap).
2. The vapour from the damp blotter rises through the membrane and relaxes the paper at a more even rate than the free chamber.
3. Weigh lightly and check frequently.

## Cautions
- Never leave humidified paper unattended or sealed for long; mould can start within a day or two at high RH.
- Water-sensitive media can move even by vapour; test and avoid if so.
- Do not overshoot; very damp paper stretches.

## Verify
- [ ] Sheet pliable but never visibly wet.
- [ ] Dried flat under controlled pressure.
- [ ] RH and times recorded.
