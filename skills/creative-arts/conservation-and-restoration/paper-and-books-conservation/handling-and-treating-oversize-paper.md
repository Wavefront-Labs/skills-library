# Handling and treating oversize paper

**Goal:** prevent damage to large sheets during handling, treatment and storage.

## Handling
- Plan the route and the surfaces before moving; clear tables and doorways.
- Use a rigid board or tray under the sheet; carry on a board rather than by the edges; use multiple people for large sheets.
- Roll only when needed and on a large-diameter tube, face-out or face-in as the paper's condition allows; avoid tight rolls that crack brittle paper.
- Do not fold; creases in aged paper break fibres.

## Unrolling and flattening
1. Humidify gradually in a chamber large enough to take the sheet (see `humidifying-paper-in-a-chamber-or-gore-tex-sandwich`); do not unroll dry brittle paper.
2. Unroll slowly while the paper is pliable; support from beneath.
3. Press between blotters and boards (see `flattening-paper-between-blotters`); weight as light as practical.
4. Where needed, repair tears and line with tissue or linen only after assessing media (see `lining-weak-paper`).

## Storage
- Store flat in map drawers, each sheet in a folder or between interleaving sheets; do not overfill drawers.
- If rolled storage is unavoidable, use large tubes with acid-free tissue; label the outside.
- For frequently used plans, consider encapsulating stable sheets (see `polyester-encapsulation-of-paper`).

## Verify
- [ ] Route, hands and supports planned.
- [ ] Sheet flat or loosely rolled on a large tube.
- [ ] Media checked before humidification.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
