# Removing inactive mould from paper

**Goal:** remove dormant mould spores and mycelium without spreading them or harming the paper, and without risk to the worker.

## Establish status
- Inactive (dormant) mould looks dry, powdery or fuzzy and wipes off as dust; active mould is damp, slimy, or smeary. If you cannot tell, treat as active.
- Mould is dormant when moisture has been absent; the cause (damp, high RH) must be fixed or growth restarts. If the item is wet, dry or freeze it first (see `freezing-and-drying-wet-books-and-paper`).
- Identification of species is rarely needed; the health and conservation approach is the same. Seek advice from a specialist for large outbreaks (see `mould-outbreak-response`).

## Health and safety first
- Mould spores are respiratory and allergenic hazards. Work in a ventilated space or fume/biosafety-type cabinet with local exhaust; wear gloves, a lab coat or coverall, eye protection, and a fitted filtering respirator rated for particles (for example FFP3 / P100 class) after a fit test.
- Tell anyone with asthma, allergies or a weakened immune system not to work with it.
- Isolate infested items from the clean collection.

## Procedure
1. Place the item in the work area on a clean surface with a disposable cover.
2. Brush gently with a soft brush toward the extraction, or use a HEPA-filtered vacuum with low suction and a screen or soft nozzle.
3. Do not use water or detergents on the paper to clean mould; moisture revives growth and can set stains.
4. Wipe nothing from fragile or friable media; stop if paper is weak.
5. Clean tools and surfaces with ethanol or a suitable cleaner afterward. Bag disposable materials and dispose of them as advised by your safety officer.
6. Record before and after photographs; note staining that remains (it may need later stain reduction, see `reducing-foxing-and-tidelines`).

## Verify
- [ ] Moisture source corrected; storage RH under control.
- [ ] No active growth after cleaning and on re-inspection.
- [ ] Personal protective equipment and waste handled per local rules.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention.
