# Rebacking and spine lining

**Goal:** repair a spine with new covering or lining so the book works, while keeping the original covering visible and in place when possible.

## When to reback
- The spine covering is lost or too weak to hold, and the boards are otherwise sound.
- Rebacking is a significant intervention: consider alternatives such as consolidation, a protective box, or a hinge repair first.

## Method
1. Remove the damaged spine material only as far as needed; pare the edges of the old leather and board if it must lift.
2. Choose new leather close to the original in type and colour (often a vegetable-tanned goatskin or calf), pared thin at the edges.
3. Prepare the spine lining: for a tight back, a light lining of tissue or fabric stuck to the spine; for a hollow back, a tube of paper between spine and covering (follow the original structure).
4. Apply adhesive (paste or paste mixed with methyl cellulose) to the new leather; lay it on the spine, work to the joints, and shape it.
5. Lift the old leather edge and tuck the new beneath it, or lay the old spine back over the new (laying on) if it survives.
6. Tie up with cord and dry; remove, adjust, and tone as agreed.

## Notes
- Record the spine structure before disturbing it.
- Test leather for red rot first (see `consolidating-red-rot-in-leather-bindings`).
- Spine linings affect how a book opens: more rigid lining makes it open stiffly.

## Verify
- [ ] Spine shape and opening match the original function.
- [ ] Old covering retained where possible and its position recorded.
- [ ] Materials recorded.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention.
