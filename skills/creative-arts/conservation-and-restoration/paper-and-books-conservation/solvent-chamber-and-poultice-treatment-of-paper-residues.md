# Solvent chamber and poultice treatment

**Goal:** soften adhesive residues without immersing the paper.

## Solvent chamber
1. Choose a solvent matched to the residue after small tests (for example acetone or ethanol; some conservators use others, always with safety checks).
2. Put the item on a perforated support over a tray with solvent-moistened blotters inside a covered container, in a fume cupboard.
3. Vapour softens the residue over minutes; check frequently and remove before the paper or media is affected.
4. Lift residue with a spatula or swab.

## Poultice
1. Mix a cellulose powder or gel with the chosen solvent to a paste; a barrier of thin tissue between paste and paper limits transfer.
2. Place on the residue for a measured time, then lift with the tissue, repeating as needed.
3. Clear any residue with clean solvent on a swab and blot.

## Cautions
- Solvents can mobilise inks; test first.
- Poultices can leave tide lines; keep moisture under control.
- Vapour and liquid solvents are fire and health hazards; use extraction.

## Verify
- [ ] Media stable under vapour and contact.
- [ ] Residue removed with no stain halo.
- [ ] Solvent, time and technique recorded.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
