# Toning repair tissues and papers

**Goal:** make mends and fills visually quiet without disguising them as original.

## Principles
- Tone the repair material only, never the original paper.
- Choose lightfast colourants (tested or rated), and prefer the weakest binder that stays put.
- A slightly lighter, cooler tone than the surround usually looks better than a darker one; adjust by thin layers.

## Methods
1. **Pre-toning tissue:** brush or float dilute watercolour, acrylic or dye onto Japanese tissue and dry; cut pieces from the toned sheet. Allows precise matching before use.
2. **Post-toning repair:** after drying, add colour with a fine brush, watercolour pencil or soft pastel; build slowly and let each layer dry.
3. Test the toning media for stability in the planned wash or humidity conditions if the sheet might be treated again.
4. Check colour under daylight and under display lighting; see `checking-retouching-for-metamerism` for the same concern.

## Cautions
- Avoid fugitive dyes; avoid media that bleed when later humidified.
- Do not tone beyond the repair edge.
- Pastel dust rubs off; fix by the conservator's method or avoid.

## Verify
- [ ] Toning stays within the repair.
- [ ] Colour unchanged when checked in different light.
- [ ] Media recorded.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
