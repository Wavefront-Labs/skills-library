# Polyester encapsulation

**Goal:** provide a clear, supportive and dust-proof enclosure for stable sheets that are handled often.

## When to use
- Maps, newspaper cuttings, brittle but stable single sheets, and ephemera that must be viewed without touching.
- Not for: friable or flaking media (pastel, charcoal, loose paint), items with static-sensitive surfaces, damp or mouldy paper, photographs, or objects needing ventilation. Plastic film can hold static and trap moisture.

## Materials
- Uncoated polyester film of a recognised conservation type (for example Mylar D or Melinex 516), clear, in a thickness chosen for stiffness.
- Seal by ultrasonic welding or with a narrow double-sided tape; the adhesive must not touch the object. A buffer sheet may be added behind paper if suitable.

## Procedure
1. Cut two polyester sheets larger than the item by a margin of several centimetres on all sides.
2. Lay the lower sheet, place the item with at least 5 to 10 mm clear of the seal, add the buffer sheet if used, then the top sheet.
3. Seal around three sides, leave a corner or section open to let air out, then seal the last side; do not trap damp air.
4. Round the corners; label.
5. Keep encapsulations flat; store in a box.

## Cautions
- Encapsulation is reversible only by cutting open; the item needs room to be removed.
- Static can attract dust to the outside; wipe with anti-static cleaner.

## Verify
- [ ] Item is dry, stable media.
- [ ] Seals clear of the item.
- [ ] Item labelled and boxed flat.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
