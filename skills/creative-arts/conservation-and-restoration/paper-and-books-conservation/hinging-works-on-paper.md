# Hinging works on paper

**Goal:** hold the sheet in the mat so it can expand and contract and be released without damage.

## Principles
- Hinges must be weaker than the paper: if stress occurs, the hinge tears first.
- Use Japanese tissue (kozo) and wheat starch paste; avoid pressure-sensitive tape and other adhesives.
- Attach at the upper edge only, on the back, so the sheet hangs freely and can move with humidity.

## Hinge types
- **Pendant (folded) hinge:** a tissue strip folded in half; one half is pasted to the back of the sheet and the other to the backing board.
- **T-hinge:** a strip pasted to the sheet edge and a second strip laid across it to attach to the board.
Large or heavy sheets need more hinges, still along the top edge.

## Procedure
1. Tear (not cut) tissue strips so the edges are feathered; water-tearing along a brushed line of water gives a fine feathered edge.
2. Brush paste onto the strip on a release sheet, making the paste thin and almost dry in feel.
3. Place on the back of the sheet, blot, cover with release layers and light weight; let dry.
4. Attach the free end to the mount board the same way.
5. Check the sheet hangs flat and can lift slightly from the mat without strain.

## Verify
- [ ] Hinges weaker than the paper.
- [ ] Only top edge attached; sheet free to move.
- [ ] Paste fully dry before closing the package.

## Professional note
