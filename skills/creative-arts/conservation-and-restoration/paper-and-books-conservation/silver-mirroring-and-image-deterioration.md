# Silver mirroring and image deterioration

**Goal:** identify and slow deterioration of silver-based photographic images.

## Recognising mirroring
- A bluish, metallic sheen on dark areas of a silver gelatin print or film, seen at a raking angle, caused by silver migrating to the surface.
- Related forms: redox blemishes (small coloured spots), yellowing and fading of the image, and silver sulfide-type tarnish.

## Causes in general terms
Oxidising pollutants, high or fluctuating humidity, poor enclosures, and contact with reactive materials trigger oxidation of the silver; the ions then migrate and redeposit.

## Management
1. Document with photographs under raking light.
2. Move to stable, moderate conditions: RH controlled and not high; temperature low and stable where possible.
3. Re-house in enclosures that pass the Photographic Activity Test (see `housing-photographs-in-pat-tested-enclosures`).
4. Reduce pollutants (see `pollutant-monitoring-with-ad-strips-and-sorbents`).
5. Do not rub or clean the surface; the mirror layer cannot be polished away safely. Treat only through a photographic conservator.

## Verify
- [ ] Deterioration recorded and dated.
- [ ] Conditions and enclosure improved.
- [ ] No cleaning attempts on the image surface.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
