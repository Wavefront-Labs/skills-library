# Tension drying on a karibari

**Goal:** dry a paper sheet or lined work under even tension so cockles and creases relax.

## About the karibari
A karibari is a traditional Japanese drying panel: a wooden lattice covered with layers of paper, which breathes and lets the paper dry gradually. Also used with boards or other breathable panels in Western practice.

## Procedure
1. Check the sheet and media tolerate damp treatment (see `spot-testing-media-solubility-before-washing`).
2. Lay the damp sheet face-down (or face-up with a protective tissue as appropriate) on the panel.
3. Brush wheat starch paste thinly on narrow strips of tissue or directly on the margins; paste down around the edges, keeping the tension even and avoiding pulling.
4. Let it dry slowly in a stable environment, away from sun and draughts. Do not try to speed dry by heat.
5. When fully dry, release by cutting the sheet inside the pasted margin with a knife along a straightedge.

## Failure modes
- Rapid drying causing the sheet to tear against its pasted edge.
- Uneven tension creating ripples.
- Releasing before fully dry.

## Verify
- [ ] Sheet taut during drying and flat after release.
- [ ] No tears at pasted margins.
- [ ] Paste and method recorded.

## Professional note
