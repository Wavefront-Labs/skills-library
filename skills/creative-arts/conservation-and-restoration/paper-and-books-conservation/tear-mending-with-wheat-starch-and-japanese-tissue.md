# Tear mending with wheat starch paste and Japanese tissue

**Goal:** stabilise a torn sheet (print, drawing, document, book leaf) with a repair
that is strong enough to handle, nearly invisible, and **removable with water decades
later** without damaging the original. This is the core repair of paper conservation.

> **Status: draft.** Method follows standard library/archive conservation practice;
> paste ratios and tissue weights are common starting points and vary by studio. Do not
> treat valuable or media-sensitive items (water-soluble inks, pastels, coated papers)
> without testing and supervision.

## Materials
- **Wheat starch paste** — purified wheat starch (e.g. Zin Shofu type) cooked in
  deionised water. A common start is about **1 part starch : 4–6 parts water** by volume,
  cooked while stirring until translucent and thick (stovetop or microwave in short
  bursts, ~10–20 min total), cooled, then **strained** through a fine sieve and diluted
  with water to the working consistency — for tears, usually thin like single cream.
  Keeps a few days refrigerated; discard if it smells sour or goes watery.
- **Japanese tissue** (kozo for strength, gampi or thin kozo for translucency), tone
  matched to the sheet or pre-toned with acrylics/watercolour on a test.
- Non-woven polyester (Reemay/Hollytex) or bonded polyester web (Bondina), blotters,
  glass or acrylic weights, a bone folder, a fine brush, a water pen or fine brush for tearing.

Avoid: pressure-sensitive tape of any kind, PVA on originals, rubber cement. Old tape
is the most common damage you'll be repairing.

## Method
1. **Examine and test.** Check media solubility with a barely damp swab on an
   inconspicuous spot; if inks run, use a drier method (thicker paste, remoistenable
   tissue, or methyl cellulose) or stop. Note grain direction of the sheet.
2. **Align the tear.** Most tears are *bevelled* — one edge overlaps the other. Find
   which side each bevel sits on and lay them back in their original overlap; paste a
   bevelled tear along its overlap first if it is wide.
3. **Prepare tissue strips** with the tissue's grain running **along** the tear. Draw
   the line with a water pen (or wet brush along a ruler), then pull the strip apart so
   the long fibres stay as a **feathered edge**. Cut edges show as hard lines and
   pull up; feathered edges blend.
   Width: about 3–6 mm, just wide enough to bridge the tear.
4. **Paste the tissue**, not the original: lay the strip on a non-woven, brush a thin
   even coat, lift it with tweezers, and lay it over the tear on the **verso**.
5. **Set and dry under light pressure:** non-woven over the repair, then blotter, then
   a small weight. Change blotters after a few minutes so moisture doesn't cockle the
   sheet. Leave until fully dry (15–60 min depending on humidity).
6. **Inspect.** Feathered fibres lie flat; the tear is aligned; no paste sheen or tide
   lines. If the recto needs support (long tear across image), consider a very fine
   gampi strip there too — usually avoid it.

## Gotchas
- **Too much moisture** → tide lines, cockling and halo stains. Thin paste goes on
  thinly, not wetly. When in doubt, more blotter changes.
- **Paste too thick** → stiff, shiny repair that cracks along its edge and creates a new
  hinge point where the sheet will tear next.
- **Tissue grain across the tear** gives a weaker repair that curls.
- Mends stronger than the paper move the stress elsewhere: the repair should be the
  weaker element.
- Removing old tape is a separate treatment (often heat or solvents under extraction);
  don't try to mend over it.

## Safety
Starch paste and tissue are benign. If tape removal or adhesive softening with solvents
is needed first, do it under local fume extraction with appropriate gloves, per the
solvent's SDS.

## Verify
- [ ] Media tested for water sensitivity before any moisture.
- [ ] Tear edges realigned on their original bevel.
- [ ] Tissue water-torn with grain parallel to the tear; feathered edges visible.
- [ ] Dried under non-woven + blotter + weight; no cockling or tide lines.
- [ ] Repair is reversible with water and documented (location, tissue, paste).
