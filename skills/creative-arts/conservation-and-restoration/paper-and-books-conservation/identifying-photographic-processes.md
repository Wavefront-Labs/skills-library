# Identifying photographic processes

**Goal:** name the process because each has its own risks and housing.

## Tools
- Magnification (10x loupe or stereomicroscope), raking and transmitted light, and, where available, UV.
- The Image Permanence Institute's Graphics Atlas and similar guides.

## Clues
- **Format and support:** cased image (daguerreotype, ambrotype), metal plate (tintype), glass plate negative, film base, paper print mounted on card.
- **Image colour and tone:** brown or purplish and matte (salted paper), warm and glossy with surface cracks (albumen), blue (cyanotype), neutral black/silver (gelatin silver), colour dyes (chromogenic).
- **Surface:** presence of a binder layer, fibre visibility, sheen, or craquelure.
- **Fading and deterioration:** yellowed highlights, silver mirroring, dye fading.

## Procedure
1. Look at the front and back; note any stamps or inscriptions.
2. Examine the surface at an angle and under magnification.
3. Work down a decision table: format, then binder type, then image material.
4. Confirm with a second guide; record the identification and the confidence.

## Cautions
- Never touch daguerreotype surfaces; handle cased images with care to avoid breaking glass.
- Nitrate film base is flammable; take advice before handling or moving it.

## Verify
- [ ] Process identified with the reasoning recorded.
- [ ] Hazards (nitrate, fragile glass) noted.
- [ ] Housing chosen to the process.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
