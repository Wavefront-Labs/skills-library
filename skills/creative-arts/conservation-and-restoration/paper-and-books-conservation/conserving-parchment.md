# Conserving parchment

**Goal:** protect parchment from its main risks, moisture change, heat and mishandling, and decide on careful treatment where required.

## Material behaviour
- Parchment and vellum are animal skin: collagen. They respond strongly to humidity: moisture causes expansion and cockling, drying causes shrinkage and stiffening.
- Immersion in water, or too much moisture and heat, can gelatinise the collagen, giving a stiff, glossy, irreversibly changed surface and loss of strength.
- Iron gall ink and pigments on parchment are often in poor condition; test media with the greatest caution.

## Preventive care
- Keep RH stable in the range advised for the collection (see `pas-198-and-bs-en-16893-environment-targets`) and avoid cycling.
- Avoid heat sources and strong light; avoid tight framing that restrains movement.
- Use spacers and flexible mounting rather than gluing down.

## Treatment (only by a conservator)
- Controlled humidification with local vapour and controlled drying under tension or restraint, often on a stretcher or board.
- Flattening in stages with careful monitoring; some distortion is part of the material.
- Mending with compatible materials (parchment or suitable tissue) and adhesives.

## Cautions
- Never wet parchment casually, never press it dry, and never use heat-based flattening tools.
- Illuminated manuscripts and bound parchment (bindings, pastedowns) bring further risks.

## Verify
- [ ] Display and storage RH stable and recorded.
- [ ] No water or heat applied outside a conservator-led plan.
- [ ] Media tested before any humidification.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
