# Detecting and managing vinegar syndrome

**Goal:** find acetate base film in decay and protect neighbours, then slow or bypass the decay.

## What it is
Cellulose acetate film base (often called safety film) slowly loses acetyl groups, releasing acetic acid with a vinegar smell. The reaction is autocatalytic: the acid speeds further decay. Signs: smell, shrinkage, buckling, channelling, and crystalline deposits.

## Detection
1. Smell the box, with care and not close.
2. Use A-D strips (Image Permanence Institute): a strip changes colour from blue through green to yellow as acid concentration rises; the IPI gives a scale from 0 to 3 and notes a critical point at about 1.5 where decay often accelerates. Place a strip in a sealed container with the film and read after the stated time.
3. Examine for shrinkage by comparing with the original frame size.

## Management
1. Isolate affected film from the rest of the collection, in ventilated containers where possible; acid vapour harms neighbours.
2. Use cold storage with controlled RH to slow decay (IPI guidance relates storage conditions to life expectancy); see `cold-storage-for-collections`.
3. Reformat priority material (copy to stable media) because decay cannot be reversed.
4. Do not seal decaying film in airtight bags unless guided by current advice; sealing traps acid.

## Cautions
- Cellulose nitrate film is a separate hazard (flammable); do not confuse them, and consult local regulations for storage.

## Verify
- [ ] A-D strip readings and dates recorded.
- [ ] Affected film isolated and plan for copying made.
- [ ] Storage environment documented.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
