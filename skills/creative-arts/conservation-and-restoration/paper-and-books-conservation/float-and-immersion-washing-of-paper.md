# Float and immersion washing

**Goal:** remove water-soluble degradation products and discolouration from paper that tolerates water.

## Prerequisites
- All media spot-tested stable (see `spot-testing-media-solubility-before-washing`).
- Paper strong enough to be wet and handled on a support.
- Sizing, coatings and adhesives considered; wet treatment can remove gelatine size and weaken some papers.

## Water
Water quality affects results. Tap water contains dissolved minerals that can deposit on the paper; fully deionised water slightly acidifies by absorbing carbon dioxide (typically near pH 5.5 to 6). For adjusted water, add a very small amount of calcium hydroxide or calcium carbonate / bicarbonate to deionised water to bring pH into the neutral to mildly alkaline range suitable for the paper (often pH 7 to 8.5; set by the conservator for the object). Measure with a calibrated meter (see `measuring-paper-ph`).

## Float washing
1. Support the sheet on a polyester web (Hollytex, Reemay) or on a screen.
2. Lower the sheet face-up, slowly and at a slight angle, onto the surface of the water so it wets from beneath and air escapes.
3. Leave until the sheet is relaxed; replace water as it discolours.

## Immersion washing
1. As above but sink the supported sheet fully in a shallow tray, with plenty of water.
2. Lift on the support, drain, and bathe again in fresh water until bath colour and conductivity stop rising (conductivity meter).

## Drying
Blot with blotters, then dry between felts, Hollytex and blotters under very light weights, or by tension drying. See `humidifying-paper-in-a-chamber-or-gore-tex-sandwich`, `flattening-paper-between-blotters`.

## Failure modes
- Tide lines from uneven drying.
- Cockling from uncontrolled drying.
- Bleeding media you did not test.

## Verify
- [ ] Wash water pH and conductivity recorded.
- [ ] Media unmoved.
- [ ] Sheet dried flat without tide lines.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
