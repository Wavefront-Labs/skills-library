# Spot testing media solubility

**Goal:** know which areas of a sheet will move or bleed before they are wetted.

## Principle
No wet treatment starts until every medium on the sheet has been tested. Media that look stable often move: water-soluble dyes (red and violet inks, felt-tip pens, rubber stamps), watercolour and gouache, iron gall ink in poor condition, some printing inks, photocopy and inkjet prints. Inkjet prints are often highly water-sensitive.

## Method
1. Work under a stereomicroscope or strong magnification and good light.
2. Place a small piece of white blotting paper under the test spot, and one over it if needed, on a smooth support (e.g. Mylar or polyester sheet).
3. Apply a very small drop of the test liquid with a fine brush or a micro-pipette to a hidden or least important portion of each medium.
4. Observe immediately, after about a minute, and look at the blotter for colour. Use a bare cotton swab only with suitable care so as not to rub.
5. Test with deionised water first, then (if the later treatment will use them) with the actual wash water, ethanol or other treatment solutions.
6. Mark the test location on a diagram; remove all liquid with blotter and dry under light weights.

## Interpreting
- Any colour on the blotter, halo, or haze means that medium is sensitive: do not immerse or wash without local protection (consolidation or a non-aqueous route).
- A negative test on one spot does not guarantee all areas are stable; test every distinct colour and each different ink.

## Verify
- [ ] Every medium tested and mapped.
- [ ] Results recorded; treatment plan changed for sensitive media.
- [ ] Test marks invisible under magnification.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
