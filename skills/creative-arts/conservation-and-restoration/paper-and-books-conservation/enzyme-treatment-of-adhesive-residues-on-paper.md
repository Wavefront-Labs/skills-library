# Enzyme treatment of adhesive residues

**Goal:** break down old adhesive or grease residues that water alone cannot remove, in a way that is selective and controlled.

## Choosing the enzyme
- **Amylase** digests starch (old wheat or rice paste, starch sizing).
- **Protease** (for example trypsin-type enzymes) digests protein (animal glue, gelatine, casein).
- **Lipase** acts on fats and oils (some grease stains).
Match the enzyme to the residue; identification of the adhesive, by a spot test or from the object history, comes first.

## Method
1. Spot-test media for water sensitivity, since enzyme solutions are aqueous (see `spot-testing-media-solubility-before-washing`).
2. Prepare the enzyme in the buffered solution and temperature recommended in the supplier's data sheet; enzymes work in a defined pH and temperature range, with warm conditions (roughly body temperature) common.
3. Apply locally under a barrier layer with a poultice (a cellulose powder or a gel) so contact is limited to the residue.
4. Leave for the time stated or found by trial (commonly tens of minutes), then remove the poultice and rinse with water on a blotter.
5. Deactivate and remove the enzyme by thorough rinsing with warm water; some workflows also use ethanol, depending on the enzyme and paper.
6. Dry under light weights. Verify residue removal with raking light.

## Cautions
- Enzyme powders and aerosols are respiratory sensitisers: weigh in a ventilated enclosure, wear gloves and a respirator rated for fine dust.
- Leftover active enzyme can keep digesting; rinse thoroughly.
- Test enzyme activity before use because stored enzyme loses activity.

## Verify
- [ ] Residue type identified or tested.
- [ ] Enzyme fully rinsed and dried.
- [ ] Solution, pH, temperature and time recorded.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
