# Aqueous deacidification of paper

**Goal:** neutralise acids in the sheet and leave an alkaline buffer, without harming media or paper.

## Decision
Deacidify only if testing shows the paper is acidic and the object is worth the intervention; not every item benefits, and the process can change colour, remove size or damage sensitive media. See `measuring-paper-ph` and `spot-testing-media-solubility-before-washing`.

## Solutions
- **Calcium hydroxide (limewater):** a saturated solution has a pH of about 12.4; it is usually used diluted or followed by rinsing.
- **Calcium bicarbonate:** made by bubbling carbon dioxide through calcium carbonate or hydroxide suspended in water; milder, pH near 8 to 9.
- **Magnesium bicarbonate:** made by passing carbon dioxide into magnesium carbonate or hydroxide in water, typically pH around 9. Often favoured for papers with some sensitivity to calcium.
Prepare the solution fresh, filter and check pH. Concentration and contact time come from the literature and the conservator.

## Procedure
1. Wash first where indicated (see `float-and-immersion-washing-of-paper`).
2. Support the sheet on a web and immerse in the alkaline bath for the time chosen (commonly a short period of tens of minutes).
3. Lift, drain, blot and dry under light weights or with another controlled method.
4. Re-measure pH and note appearance.

## Cautions
- High pH can darken lignin-containing paper and change some dyes and iron gall ink; test.
- Alkaline reserve is measured by titration; a target should be set per the standard (ISO 9706 asks for an alkaline reserve for new permanent paper).
- Avoid on sensitive media, bleach-sensitive grounds, photographs and some printing.

## Verify
- [ ] Media tested before immersion.
- [ ] pH and appearance recorded before and after.
- [ ] Solution recipe, pH and contact time recorded.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
