# Housing photographs in PAT-tested enclosures

**Goal:** protect photographs from chemical and physical damage in storage.

## Standards and testing
- The Photographic Activity Test (PAT) checks whether enclosure materials stain or damage photographs; ISO 18916 describes it. ISO 18902 specifies storage enclosures for processed photographs.
- Ask suppliers for PAT results for each product, and recheck when products change.

## Choosing enclosures
- **Polyester (uncoated) sleeves** are transparent, inert and allow viewing, but static is an issue with fragile surfaces (flaking emulsions, pastel-like media).
- **Paper envelopes** suit items needing protection from light and a lower static risk; paper may be buffered or unbuffered. Buffered paper is common for black-and-white prints on paper, but some processes (cyanotype, certain albumen and colour materials) are sensitive to alkaline buffering, so check the process and the current guidance.
- Avoid PVC and plasticised plastics, glassine, acidic or low-grade paper, pressure-sensitive tapes and rubber bands.
- Support enclosures in boxes rather than on edge unsupported; fill voids to prevent slumping.

## Albums
Magnetic or sticky albums are damaging; disassemble only under a conservator's advice. Interleave and box.

## Verify
- [ ] Supplier PAT results held.
- [ ] Enclosure type matches process.
- [ ] Items boxed and supported.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
