# Consolidating red rot in leather bindings

**Goal:** slow powdering and surface loss, and protect the binding with storage and support, as red rot cannot be cured.

## Recognising red rot
Vegetable-tanned leather (common on 19th-century bindings) breaks down to a reddish, powdery, fibre-poor material that rubs off on hands and shelves. The deterioration is associated with acid pollutants and sulphur compounds in the leather, and is not reversible.

## Options
1. **Do not dress.** Oily or waxy leather dressings may darken and soften leather and do not repair red rot; many conservators now avoid or limit them. Traditional mixes such as the British Museum Leather Dressing (lanolin, beeswax and a solvent) were used in the past and are now questioned.
2. **Consolidate the surface** with a low-concentration solution of hydroxypropylcellulose (Klucel G) in a solvent such as ethanol or isopropanol. Dilute solutions in the low single-digit percent range are described in the literature; test on a hidden area, apply by brush or light spray, and let the solvent evaporate. The consolidated leather will be stiffer and may darken.
3. **Support:** place the book in a protective box and avoid handling; wrap or tie.
4. **Reformat** if the text matters more than the object.

## Cautions
- Red rot powder is irritant; use gloves and a mask with local extraction when working.
- Consolidant strength and application method need testing; over-application leaves a glossy, hard surface.
- Document the extent and treatment; decide on dressing only after consideration.

## Verify
- [ ] Surface rub-off reduced when tested lightly with a dry cloth on a hidden area.
- [ ] Appearance change acceptable to owner.
- [ ] Treatment and consolidant concentration recorded.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention.
