# Measuring paper pH

**Goal:** get a defensible acidity reading before deciding on deacidification.

## Methods
- **Cold water extraction:** a small sample is soaked in deionised water and the extract is measured; the standard methods are TAPPI T 509 and ISO 6588. Most reliable but requires a sample, so it is destructive.
- **Flat surface electrode:** a drop of deionised water placed on the paper, with a flat membrane electrode pressed on; read when stable. Non-destructive, leaves slight dampness. Calibrate with buffers before each session (for example pH 4, 7 and 10 as the range requires).
- **Indicator pens (chlorophenol red type):** mark the paper; the colour is read against a chart. Quick but leaves a mark and gives only a rough value; avoid on valuable surfaces.

## Procedure (surface electrode)
1. Calibrate and rinse the electrode; keep deionised water fresh.
2. Place a drop (a few microlitres up to one drop) on a hidden area; slide the electrode over it.
3. Wait for the reading to settle (this may take a minute or more), blot, and record.
4. Test several areas; text, margins and any previously damaged portion may differ.
5. Record the method, area and value.

## Interpretation
- pH is logarithmic; one unit is a tenfold difference in acidity.
- Values below about 6 indicate acid paper; the permanent paper standard ISO 9706 asks for pH 7.5 to 10 for cold extract with an alkaline reserve.
- Different methods give different values; compare like with like.
- A pH reading says nothing about alkaline reserve or strength; use fold-endurance or similar only through a conservator or lab.

## Verify
- [ ] Meter calibrated with fresh buffers.
- [ ] Method and location recorded with the reading.
- [ ] Results used with the conservator's decision on treatment.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention.
