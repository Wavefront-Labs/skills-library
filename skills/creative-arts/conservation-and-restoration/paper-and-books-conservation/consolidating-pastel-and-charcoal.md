# Consolidating pastel and charcoal

**Goal:** weigh consolidation against its risks, and stabilise loose media by housing where possible.

## The problem
Pastel and charcoal sit on the paper surface with little or no binder. Vibration, handling or contact can dislodge them, but consolidation changes appearance: fixatives darken colours, reduce the matte look, and cannot be fully removed.

## Decision
1. Examine under magnification and raking light for loose media and rubbed areas (see `testing-and-protecting-friable-media`).
2. Prefer preventive measures: deep mats or spacers, no contact with glazing, no static-prone plastics, vibration-free transport, and handling with support.
3. Consolidate only where loose media is at active risk and housing cannot fix it, and only with a conservator experienced in pastels.

## Reported approaches (for conservator use)
- Very dilute solutions of cellulose ethers (methylcellulose, hydroxypropylcellulose) applied in micro-doses by brush or fine aerosol, in a controlled test area first.
- Some conservators apply consolidant from the reverse of the paper, using minimal moisture, to avoid changing the surface.
- Traditional spray fixatives are largely avoided because they darken and discolour.

## Verify
- [ ] Loose media photographed and mapped.
- [ ] Test area of consolidant examined for darkening.
- [ ] Housing reviewed so contact risk is reduced.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
