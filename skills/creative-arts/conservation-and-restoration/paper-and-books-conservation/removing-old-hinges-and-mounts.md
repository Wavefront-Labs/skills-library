# Removing old hinges and mounts

**Goal:** free the artwork from damaging old mounts without tearing or staining the sheet.

## Assess
- Note what holds the sheet: paste, animal glue, pressure-sensitive tape, rubber cement, or gummed paper.
- Check media sensitivity to moisture and solvent before any wetting (see `spot-testing-media-solubility-before-washing`).

## Methods
1. **Mechanical:** with a thin spatula, lift a corner of the hinge; slip the blade between hinge and sheet only where the bond is weak.
2. **Moisture:** use a damp swab or a local poultice of methyl cellulose; the adhesive softens in time; lift with a spatula and blot. Applied sparingly to avoid tidelines.
3. **Solvent:** for tape or synthetic adhesives, see `removing-pressure-sensitive-tape-from-paper`.
4. **Remove mount backing** slowly; do not tear it off in one go. Photograph what remains.
5. Reduce any paper remnants only after the hinge is off.

## Record
Keep a record of the old mount, its materials and any inscriptions on the backing; those can be historic evidence.

## Verify
- [ ] Sheet intact with no thinning.
- [ ] Old mount and notes documented.
- [ ] New hinging plan agreed (see `hinging-works-on-paper`).

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
