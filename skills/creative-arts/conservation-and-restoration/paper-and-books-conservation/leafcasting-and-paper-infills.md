# Leafcasting and paper infills

**Goal:** compensate losses so the sheet is structurally sound and visually continuous, using matched paper or pulp.

## Choose the method
- **Leafcasting:** a machine pumps a dilute slurry of paper pulp into a loss while the sheet is on a suction bed; the fibres fill the shape of the loss and bond to the edges. Good for many small and irregular losses or for fragile sheets.
- **Hand-cut infill:** a piece of paper is cut to the loss shape with a bevelled or feathered edge and adhered with paste. Better for single large losses and where an exact paper match is needed.

## Leafcasting steps
1. Spot-test media and consider water exposure (see `spot-testing-media-solubility-before-washing`).
2. Prepare pulp from a fibre matching the sheet (cotton, linen, kozo, etc.), refined and tinted to match before casting.
3. Humidify the sheet and place it on the machine with a web; apply controlled suction.
4. Pour the slurry; the pulp builds up to the thickness of the sheet; check frequently.
5. Dry under blotters and light weight; trim if needed.

## Hand-cut infill steps
1. Make a template of the loss; select paper close in thickness and fibre.
2. Cut or tear to shape with the edge feathered so it overlaps slightly with the original (often under the edge).
3. Apply wheat starch paste to the edge only; position, blot, dry under light weights.
4. Tone afterward (see `toning-repair-tissues-and-papers`).

## Verify
- [ ] Infill matches thickness and texture.
- [ ] Bond holds without strain; no ridge at the joint.
- [ ] Method and materials recorded.
