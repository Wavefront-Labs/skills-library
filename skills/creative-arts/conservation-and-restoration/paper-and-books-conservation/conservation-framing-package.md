# Conservation framing package

**Goal:** a frame that physically protects, buffers humidity, filters light and can be reopened.

## Components
1. **Hinged artwork:** see `hinging-works-on-paper`.
2. **Window mat and backing board** of museum board (see `cutting-window-mats`); a second backing of rigid board or corrugated polypropylene as a stiffener.
3. **Glazing:** glass or acrylic; UV-filtering versions exist, but UV filtering does not remove visible-light damage. Acrylic holds static, which can lift pastel or charcoal, so use glass or spacers for those.
4. **Spacers:** a strip or deep mat so the artwork cannot touch the glazing; essential for pastel and cockled works.
5. **Seal:** a sealed package of paper tape or backing barrier with a moisture buffer helps against RH swings; follow the owner's and conservator's preference.
6. **Dust cover and hangers:** a backing paper; secure fixings and hanging hardware rated for the load.

## Procedure
1. Clean glazing and frame rebate.
2. Stack: glazing, spacer, mat and artwork, backing, rigid board.
3. Fix with points or clips; seal the edges; attach the dust cover; add labels with date and materials.

## Cautions
- Avoid wood frame backs in direct contact with paper (acid and volatile emissions); use a barrier.
- Test seal materials for off-gassing where Oddy testing is possible (see `oddy-test-for-display-materials`).

## Verify
- [ ] Artwork cannot touch glazing.
- [ ] Package can be opened without damaging the work.
- [ ] Framing materials and date labelled on the back.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
