# Non-aqueous and mass deacidification

**Goal:** neutralise acid paper without water, singly or at scale, and know where it is not appropriate.

## Non-aqueous (single item)
- Solutions of alkaline compounds in organic solvent are sprayed or brushed on, for example magnesium methoxide or methyl magnesium carbonate (Wei T'o products are well known examples).
- Use only in a fume cupboard or booth with extraction; the solvents are flammable and harmful, and the compounds react with moisture.
- Advantages: no wetting, suitable for items that cannot be washed.
- Cautions: can alter media or cause a white deposit on coated paper; test first; the reagent must reach the sheet evenly.

## Mass deacidification (whole volumes)
- Commercial processes treat many books at once (examples include the Bookkeeper process, which uses magnesium oxide particles in a carrier fluid, and the Papersave process, an alkoxide-based system). Details differ between processes and have changed over time; check the current supplier.
- Evaluate candidates on penetration into the book, alkaline reserve achieved, effect on bindings, inks and coated paper, and cost per volume.

## Decision points
1. Is the collection a priority for long-term access and the paper acidic (for example mid-19th to mid-20th century wood-pulp papers)?
2. Are the volumes free of items that mass treatment can damage: photographs, coated paper, ephemeral inks, fragile bindings?
3. Does the budget justify treatment against alternatives (reformatting, controlled storage, cold storage)?
4. Run a pilot, test sample books, then audit results.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
