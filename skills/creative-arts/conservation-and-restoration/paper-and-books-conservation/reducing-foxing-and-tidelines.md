# Reducing foxing and tidelines

**Goal:** lessen disfiguring spots and water-stain edges only as far as is safe and justified.

## Assessing
- **Foxing:** reddish-brown spots. Causes discussed in the literature include fungal activity and iron or other metal contamination, often both. Examine under magnification and UV; some foxing fluoresces, which can help assess it, but cause cannot always be confirmed.
- **Tidelines:** brown lines at the edge of a former water stain, where dissolved colour and degradation products concentrated.
- Decide with the owner whether to treat: stains are part of the object's history, and treatment risks the paper.

## Options (least to most invasive)
1. Leave and house properly; control humidity so foxing does not worsen.
2. Dry clean surface dirt that adds to the appearance (see `dry-cleaning-paper-with-sponges-and-erasers`).
3. **Tidelines:** washing the whole sheet in water or adjusted water (see `float-and-immersion-washing-of-paper`) dissolves and redistributes the stain, often removing the line; local treatment risks creating a new line unless the whole stained area is washed evenly.
4. **Foxing:** local washing on a blotter or suction table; a chelating agent such as EDTA solution for metal-related spots, applied locally and rinsed; used with caution.
5. Bleaching (see `light-and-chemical-bleaching-of-paper`) is a last resort.

## Procedure notes
- Spot-test all media first (see `spot-testing-media-solubility-before-washing`).
- Work from the outside of a stain inward when treating locally; blot constantly.
- Dry flat under light weights to avoid new tidelines.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
