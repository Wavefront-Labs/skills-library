# Testing and protecting friable media

**Goal:** decide whether the media can withstand any contact, and keep unstable media from rubbing off.

## What counts as friable
Pastel, charcoal, chalk, soft graphite, loose powdered pigment, some gouache, flaking or lifting paint and ink.

## Examination
1. Look under magnification and raking light for loose particles, shed debris on the support or mount, and "dusty" rubbed areas.
2. Test by lightly touching a hidden portion with a very soft brush or air movement only if absolutely needed; usually visual evidence is enough to decide. Any sign of movement means no dry sponge or eraser cleaning.

## Protection
- Handle by the sheet edges with support board; never touch the media.
- Keep the work flat; avoid vibration and moving it vertically if loose.
- Store in a window mat or a lidded box with a recessed support so nothing touches the surface; avoid static-prone plastic films on pastel (they can lift particles).
- Frame only with space between media and glazing (a spacer or deep mat); use an anti-static glass or acrylic alternative where recommended by a conservator.
- Avoid long-distance transport on a vertical orientation; see `handling-works-on-paper`.

## Consolidation
Fixing media (spray consolidants) alters appearance and is usually irreversible; only a paper conservator should decide it and carry it out.

## Verify
- [ ] Media assessed as stable or friable and recorded.
- [ ] Handling and housing instructions attached to the object file.
- [ ] No cleaning attempted beyond gentle brush where media is loose.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
