# Resewing text blocks

**Goal:** recreate sound sewing while keeping as much original structure as possible.

## Decide
- If original sewing is intact, leave it; repair loose sections locally by sewing in or tipping.
- If sewing is broken or the thread failed, resewing may be necessary; consider long-term use, whether evidence of the original sewing will be lost, and whether archival record of the sewing pattern is needed.

## Procedure
1. Collate the book; check all leaves and sections; record page order.
2. Record the sewing: number of stations, supports (cords, tapes, slips), thread colour and type, endband attachment. Take photographs before dismantling.
3. Repair torn folds and weak leaves with Japanese tissue and paste; add guards where needed.
4. Prepare linen or other thread matching the original in thickness, waxed if appropriate; use the same supports where possible.
5. Resew on the sewing frame following the original pattern, keeping tension even, with kettle stitches at head and tail.
6. Check that the spine thickness matches the old, avoid swelling; press lightly and relink the covers.

## Cautions
- Resewing changes how the book flexes and may affect value; get owner agreement.
- Keep any original thread retrieved with the object file.

## Verify
- [ ] Sewing structure matches the original record.
- [ ] Sections in correct order, folds sound.
- [ ] Thread, supports and stations recorded.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention.
