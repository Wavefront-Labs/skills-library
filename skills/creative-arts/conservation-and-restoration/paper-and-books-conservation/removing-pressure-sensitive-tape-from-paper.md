# Removing pressure-sensitive tape from paper

**Goal:** release the tape backing and adhesive with minimal paper loss and media disturbance.

## Identify the tape
Types differ: cellophane tape (cellulose film with rubber or acrylic adhesive), polypropylene, masking tape, double-sided tape. Aged rubber-based adhesives often leave a stain and darkened carrier; acrylic adhesives may stay tacky or become harder. Identify by appearance and behaviour in a small test.

## Method
1. Test media for sensitivity to heat and solvents.
2. **Heat:** lay a release layer over the tape and warm gently with a tacking iron or heated spatula (use the lowest effective temperature); peel the backing back on itself at a low angle, supporting the paper with the other hand.
3. **Solvent (when heat is insufficient):** work under extraction; apply a small amount of acetone, ethanol or a similar solvent matched to the adhesive via a cotton swab or a poultice; blot rather than rub, because rubbing spreads dissolved adhesive.
4. Remove residue with solvent swabs on a blotter, or see `solvent-chamber-and-poultice-treatment-of-paper-residues`.
5. For residual stain, consider further stain treatment (see `reducing-foxing-and-tidelines`).

## Cautions
- Solvents move ink, toner and some prints; test carefully. Solvent vapour is flammable and harmful.
- Do not pull tape off cold; paper fibres lift.

## Verify
- [ ] Paper surface intact; no fibre loss.
- [ ] Media unchanged after treatment.
- [ ] Method and solvents recorded.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
