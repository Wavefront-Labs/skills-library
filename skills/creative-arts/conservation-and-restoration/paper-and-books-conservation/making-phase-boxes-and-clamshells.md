# Making phase boxes and clamshells

**Goal:** protect a fragile bound item with a custom, supportive enclosure.

## Choosing the type
- **Phase box:** a four-flap folder made of board that wraps the book and ties; quick and economical, used as a first-level protection for brittle volumes.
- **Clamshell (drop-spine) box:** a hinged-lid box in two trays, covered with cloth; more robust, for volumes handled or stored upright.

## Materials
- Lignin-free, acid-free board of suitable thickness; buffered unless the contents are alkali-sensitive.
- Bookcloth or buckram; spine cloth; closure (ties, cloth tapes).
- Adhesives: PVA of conservation grade or paste, kept off the item.

## Method (phase box outline)
1. Measure the book height, width and thickness; add clearance of roughly 3 mm (an eighth of an inch) all round.
2. Cut four flaps and a base from board with grain parallel to the spine.
3. Score and fold; join with adhesive tape or board-weight hinge; trim to size.
4. Tie closure. Label on the outside.

## Method (clamshell, outline)
1. Make a base tray and a lid tray with the spine piece connecting them.
2. Cover in cloth with turn-ins, using paste or PVA; press under weight.
3. Test fit; add a lining or a pad to support the book; label.

## Verify
- [ ] Book enters and leaves without scraping.
- [ ] No adhesive near the book; materials tested if risk.
- [ ] Label and shelf position recorded.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
