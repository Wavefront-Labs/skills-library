# Cutting window mats

**Goal:** make a mat that frames and protects without touching or shading the artwork.

## Board choice
- **Museum board:** cotton or purified wood-pulp board; look for lignin-free, acid-free and for most paper a calcium carbonate buffer. Unbuffered board is preferred for photographs and for materials sensitive to alkali (check the process; see `housing-photographs-in-pat-tested-enclosures`).
- **Thickness:** commonly 4-ply (about 1.4 mm) for the window, with a backing board of similar or greater thickness; thicker mats give more space.
- **Standards:** for photograph enclosures, the Photographic Activity Test (ISO 18916) and ISO 18902 apply; for other works, manufacturers state composition.

## Layout
1. Measure the artwork to the nearest millimetre.
2. Decide on the window opening so that it covers the edges of the sheet by a small margin (several millimetres) and does not clip any image or inscription.
3. Choose borders (commonly equal on three sides and slightly heavier at the bottom, but follow the design).
4. Mark the cutting lines lightly on the back of the board.

## Cutting
1. Use a sharp bevel mat cutter (with a new blade) and a straightedge; make a test cut on scrap.
2. Cut along each line with a smooth, single stroke; overcut slightly at the corners to avoid a hooked cut, or use a corner-correct method.
3. Remove the window; clean the edges of the bevel with a soft brush.
4. Remove all debris; never lay a bevel edge against the artwork.

## Verify
- [ ] Window opening neither crops nor loosely exposes the sheet.
- [ ] Clean bevels with no ragged corners.
- [ ] Board type recorded.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
