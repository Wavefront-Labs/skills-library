# Soft packing and travel frames

**Goal:** wrap or mount works so they are protected from abrasion, knocks and dirt in transit.

## Principles
BS EN 15946 sets out principles and methods for packing cultural heritage for transport; the PACCIN community of practice and published guides (for example Art in Transit) give further methods. Soft packing is for short moves and inside a crate; it does not protect against impact by itself.

## Soft packing a framed work
1. Condition-check and photograph (see `loan-condition-reports`).
2. Cover glazing with a protective layer: low-tack tape in a pattern applied carefully to hold shards if the glass breaks (some practices avoid tape on certain surfaces; follow the conservator's advice), or a sheet of polyester or acid-free card.
3. Wrap in clean tissue or polyethylene film, then a protective board; use corner protectors.
4. Add padding of polyethylene foam on corners and edges; wrap in a second layer.
5. Label with orientation, fragile and contents; do not write on the artwork's wrapping where it touches.

## Travel frames
A travel frame is a rigid outer frame or tray in which the painting or frame is held on padded supports, often with screws or brackets, so that it is secured without pressure on the paint surface. It is used inside a crate and can be moved separately.

## Cautions
- Avoid bubble film touching paint; avoid tissue that can stick to unvarnished surfaces.
- Do not stack framed works without padding.

## Verify
- [ ] Surface not touched by wrapping.
- [ ] Glazing protected and labelled.
- [ ] Orientation and handling labels clear.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
