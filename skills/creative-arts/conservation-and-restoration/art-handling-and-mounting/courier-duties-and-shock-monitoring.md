# Courier duties and shock monitoring

**Goal:** an informed representative accompanies the work and evidence is gathered on transport conditions.

## Courier duties
1. Before: agree the itinerary, contacts, insurance conditions, handling rules and the facility report (see `display-case-specification-and-facility-reports`).
2. Pack and load: supervise packing, check condition (see `loan-condition-reports`), watch loading and securing, confirm the vehicle's suitability and climate control.
3. In transit: stay with the vehicle where required, check at stops, keep communication open and avoid unscheduled opening.
4. At the venue: supervise unloading, acclimatisation (see `insulated-crates-and-acclimatisation`), unpacking and installation; check and sign condition reports.
5. Report: send a short report with any incidents and photographs.

## Courier kit
Torch, gloves, camera, tools for opening the crate, condition forms, contact list, tape and padding, and documents.

## Shock and vibration monitoring
- Place shock loggers (accelerometers that record time, size and direction of impacts) and tilt indicators in crates; also temperature and RH loggers.
- Set thresholds that match the object's tolerance (set by a conservator or engineer).
- Download and review data after transit; investigate events that exceed the threshold and inspect the object.
- BS EN 16648 covers transport methods for cultural heritage; refer to it for requirements.

## Verify
- [ ] Courier checks documented at each stage.
- [ ] Logger data reviewed and archived.
- [ ] Incidents reported at once.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
