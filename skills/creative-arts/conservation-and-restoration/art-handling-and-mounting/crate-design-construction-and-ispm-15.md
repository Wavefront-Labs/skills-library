# Crate design, construction and ISPM 15

**Goal:** a crate that protects the object and meets handling and legal needs.

## Design
1. Gather object weight, dimensions, fragility and route; set limits on shock and handling (see `courier-duties-and-shock-monitoring`).
2. Choose the structure: a timber frame with plywood panels is common; the lid should be secured with screws, with a seal if the crate is also climate-buffered.
3. Add runners or skids so a forklift or pallet jack can lift the crate without striking the case; mark lifting points and centre of gravity.
4. Provide internal supports and packing (see `foam-cavity-packing-and-cushion-design`).
5. Add handles, labels and a document pocket; put hazard or orientation marks on the outside.
6. Keep the crate as light as is safe.

## ISPM 15
- The International Standard for Phytosanitary Measures No. 15 (IPPC) regulates wood packaging material in international trade to limit spread of pests.
- Solid wood packaging (timber, pallets, runners) must be treated, commonly by heat treatment to a core temperature of 56 C for at least 30 minutes (marked HT) or by methyl bromide fumigation where still allowed, and carry the IPPC mark with a country code, producer code and treatment code.
- Wood packaging made wholly of processed wood material such as plywood, particle board or oriented strand board is generally exempt, but crates built from solid wood parts are not; check the rules of the destination countries and the current standard.
- Untreated or unmarked crates can be refused, treated or destroyed at borders.

## Verify
- [ ] Load, lifting points and orientation marked.
- [ ] ISPM 15 marks present on solid wood or exemption confirmed.
- [ ] Interior packing matches the object.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
