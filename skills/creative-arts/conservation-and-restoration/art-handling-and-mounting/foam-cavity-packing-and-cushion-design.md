# Foam cavity packing and cushion design

**Goal:** immobilise and cushion an object so shocks and vibration are absorbed.

## Materials
- Closed-cell polyethylene foams (Ethafoam and Plastazote types are common), polyester batting, acid-free tissue, and non-abrasive facing layers such as polyester or Tyvek.
- Avoid polyurethane foams that degrade, and foams that release acids; test where needed (see `oddy-test-for-display-materials`).

## Cavity packing
1. Draw the outline and plan supports at strong points of the object; identify weak, protruding or loose parts that must not carry weight.
2. Cut foam layers to form a cavity that follows the shape; keep the object snug, not squeezed.
3. Line the cavity with soft inert material, and have a lid layer that also contacts.
4. Test with a mock or the object, check each part is supported, and mark the orientation.
5. Place in a box or crate so foam is held in place.

## Cushion design
1. Estimate the weight and the contact area on each foam block.
2. Use the manufacturer's cushion curves, which plot peak deceleration against static stress for each foam thickness and drop height, to pick foam that works in its efficient range.
3. Too stiff a foam transmits shock; too soft bottoms out.
4. Provide enough foam thickness for the expected drop height set in your transport specification.

## Verify
- [ ] No weight on weak parts.
- [ ] Foam chosen from curves for load and drop.
- [ ] Orientation marked on the pack.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
