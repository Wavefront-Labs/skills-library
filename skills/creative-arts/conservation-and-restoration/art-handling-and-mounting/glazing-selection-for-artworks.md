# Glazing selection for artworks

**Goal:** the best glazing for each object and venue.

## Options
- **Glass:** clear, scratch resistant, inert; heavy and can break into shards; options include laminated glass (safer), UV-filtering and anti-reflective coatings.
- **Acrylic (Perspex or Plexiglas):** lighter and shatter-resistant, available with UV filtering and anti-reflective coatings; scratches and attracts static unless anti-static coated.
- **Laminated and safety glazing:** for public venues and large works.

## Choosing
1. Consider the object: pastel, charcoal, and flaking media should not touch glazing, and static from acrylic can lift particles, so use glass or anti-static acrylic with spacers.
2. Consider size and weight: large works usually need acrylic or laminated glass.
3. Consider light: UV filtering (see `uv-filtering-for-light-sources-and-glazing`), reflection control for the lighting.
4. Consider safety, transport and handling.
5. Consider cost, cleaning and availability.

## Practices
- Use spacers or deep mats so artworks cannot touch the glazing.
- Clean acrylic with products meant for it; avoid ammonia-based cleaners and rubbing dry.
- Label the glazing type on the back of the frame.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
