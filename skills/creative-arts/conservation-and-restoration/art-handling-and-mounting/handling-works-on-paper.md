# Handling works on paper

**Goal:** move unframed works without bending, tearing or smudging them.

## Preparation
- Clear a clean, flat surface bigger than the work; remove pens, food and drinks from the area.
- Wash and dry hands, or use gloves suited to the object (nitrile for slippery or smooth items; see `safe-handling-and-carrying-artworks`); sheets should not be handled with clammy hands.
- Plan the route and have a destination ready.

## Moving
1. Move works in a folder, mat or on a support board; lift by the support, not by the sheet.
2. For a loose sheet, use two hands and support it across its whole width; do not lift by one corner.
3. Carry flat. Do not roll unless it is the planned storage method.
4. Interleave stacks with clean tissue where friable media is involved, and use windows mats for display.
5. Lower slowly and check before releasing.

## Friable or fragile work
See `testing-and-protecting-friable-media`; move in a lidded box or in a tray, and avoid vibration.

## Verify
- [ ] Work supported at all times.
- [ ] Surfaces and hands clean.
- [ ] Route planned and clear.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
