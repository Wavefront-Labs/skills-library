# Rigging heavy objects with gantries and pallet jacks

**Goal:** move and hang heavy objects safely. Only qualified riggers and engineers should perform significant lifts.

## Regulations
In the UK, the Lifting Operations and Lifting Equipment Regulations (LOLER) require lifting equipment to be suitable, planned, supervised and thoroughly examined at set intervals (commonly every six months for equipment lifting people and accessories, and twelve months for other equipment). Other countries have equivalents.

## Equipment
- **Gantry or A-frame with chain hoist:** rated for the load with a margin; stable on the floor.
- **Slings and shackles:** use rated slings (webbing or round slings), protect the object from abrasion with padding; angles between sling legs raise the load on each leg.
- **Pallet jacks and skates:** for horizontal moves; check floor ratings; use ramps with safe slopes.
- **Suspension:** for hanging objects from ceilings, use rated hardware and independent safety lines, with a designed safety factor set by an engineer (see `centre-of-gravity-and-load-calculation`).

## Lift plan
1. Weigh or estimate the weight with margin; find the centre of gravity.
2. Define the route, clearances and floor strength.
3. Choose equipment with working load limits above the load.
4. Brief the team: roles, signals, stop rules.
5. Lift a few centimetres and check stability before proceeding.
6. Inspect equipment before and after.

## Verify
- [ ] Equipment rated and in date for examination.
- [ ] Lift plan written and briefed.
- [ ] Test lift passed before moving.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
