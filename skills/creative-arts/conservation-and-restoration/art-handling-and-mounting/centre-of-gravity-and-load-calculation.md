# Centre of gravity and load calculation

**Goal:** know where an object's weight acts and how loads distribute, so lifts and mounts are safe.

## Finding the centre of gravity
- **Support method:** rest the object on two supports with scales (or load cells) at each end. With total weight W and distance L between supports, the centre of gravity lies at distance x from support 1 equal to L times the reading at support 2 divided by W. Repeat in other orientations to find the point in each axis.
- **Suspension method:** hang it from two or more points and draw vertical lines; they intersect at the centre.
- For complex objects, use CAD or photogrammetry data with density estimates, and add margins.

## Stability
An object is stable if the vertical line through its centre of gravity falls inside its base; the lower the centre and the wider the base, the more stable it is. Account for tilt during lifting and transport.

## Loads
1. Weight = mass times gravity; use measured weights where possible.
2. For slings at an angle, the tension in each leg equals the load share divided by the cosine of the angle from the vertical; a wider angle raises tension, so keep angles within the rated limits.
3. Divide load among suspension points according to centre of gravity position, and allow for uneven sharing.
4. Apply a safety factor; hanging and lifting designs commonly use factors of four to one or more, set by the engineer or regulations.

## Verify
- [ ] Weight measured or estimated with margin.
- [ ] Centre of gravity marked on the object or crate.
- [ ] Calculations checked by a competent person.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
