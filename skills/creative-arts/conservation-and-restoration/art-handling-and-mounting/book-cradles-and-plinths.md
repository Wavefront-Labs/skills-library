# Book cradles and plinths

**Goal:** physical support for display.

## Book cradle
1. Find how far the binding opens without strain; the cradle's angle is set by this, and limited for tight or fragile bindings.
2. Make supports from board or foam shaped as wedges, with a central gap or lowered gutter, covered by polyester film or acid-free board and padded where needed.
3. Add side supports that hold the pages, using polyester strips that do not press on the text, and a spine support.
4. Test with a dummy book or a similar item first.
5. Record the opening angle for each book.

## Plinths and pedestals
1. Design height to suit the viewing and the object; many sculpture plinths place the object near eye level for the group of visitors; check access.
2. Make the base wider than the top and heavy enough for stability, or fix it to the floor; avoid easy tipping, particularly in public areas.
3. Use sealed or inert materials; finish with low-emission paints, and allow to air before use (see `oddy-test-for-display-materials`).
4. Add a top surface suitable for the object, and discrete fixings or a mount.
5. Consider access for cleaning and lighting.

## Verify
- [ ] Book opening angle within the limit.
- [ ] Plinth stable with a safety margin.
- [ ] Finishes cured and tested.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
