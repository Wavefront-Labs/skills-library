# Conserving tapestries and flags

**Goal:** identify the problems particular to large hung textiles and plan appropriate supports.

## Tapestries
- Woven with wool and silk wefts across a warp; hung for years, they suffer from weight, dust, light fading and dye differences, insect damage, slit separation and past restoration.
- Condition checks: slits, areas of weak or missing weft, dye migration, repairs, previous linings, and tension.
- Support strategies include stitching to a support fabric (see `stabilisation-stitching-onto-a-support-fabric`), slit stitching, and hook-and-loop or sleeve hanging (see `hook-and-loop-hanging-for-flat-textiles`).
- Display at a slight angle or flat, away from light, vibration and heat.

## Flags and banners
- Often made of silk or wool with painted or printed details, and may be weighted silk (see `recognising-weighted-silk`).
- Damage includes wind wear at the fly edge, fading, and tears; many have fugitive dyes.
- Support by stitching onto a supporting fabric or between nets, store flat or rolled on a wide tube, and do not display flying.

## Cautions
- Large textiles are heavy; plan handling with several people and rolling plans.
- Interventions must follow documentation and ethical review.

## Verify
- [ ] Condition survey recorded.
- [ ] Support suits structure and load.
- [ ] Display and storage environment controlled.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
