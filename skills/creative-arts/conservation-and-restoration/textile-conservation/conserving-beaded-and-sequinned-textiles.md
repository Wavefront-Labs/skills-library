# Conserving beaded and sequinned textiles

**Goal:** prevent bead and sequin loss and abrasion damage.

## Problems
- Threads holding beads weaken, break and allow whole rows to unravel.
- Beads and sequins weigh the fabric down and abrade each other and the base cloth.
- Some sequins were made of gelatin and are very fragile and water-sensitive; metal sequins tarnish; glass beads can show glass deterioration.

## Approach
1. Document bead patterns and condition before any treatment; collect loose beads with a record of where they came from.
2. Secure rows with couching or long stitches over thread lines; use compatible fine thread; avoid replacing original thread unless necessary.
3. Support the textile with a backing fabric if the base is weak (see `stabilisation-stitching-onto-a-support-fabric`).
4. Do not wet-clean; keep dry methods.
5. Store flat with tissue protecting the surfaces (not folded, not hung); do not stack.

## Cautions
- Do not glue beads without conservator approval; adhesives are hard to reverse.
- Do not move with beads uncovered; they catch on each other and on fabric.

## Verify
- [ ] Loose beads recorded and housed with the object.
- [ ] Support gives no strain.
- [ ] Storage flat and interleaved.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
