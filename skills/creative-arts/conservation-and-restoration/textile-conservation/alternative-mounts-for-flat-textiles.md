# Alternative mounts for flat textiles

**Goal:** pick a mount that fits the object, rather than defaulting to one system.

## Padded board and slant mount
- A rigid board padded with foam or wadding and covered in cotton, fixed at an angle (a slant, often 10 to 30 degrees) so the textile lies against it with weight carried by the board.
- Best for fragile textiles that should not hang: attach by stitching or by supporting at edges and corners with tape stitched on.

## Sleeve and rod
- A cotton sleeve stitched along the top edge takes a rod. The rod must be rigid and supported at the ends.
- Suitable for light, sound textiles; heavy ones sag at the stitching, so use only after assessing strength.
- Seal wood or use metal and test materials.

## Framing
- Mount the textile (stitched to a covered board) in a frame with a spacer so glazing does not touch; use UV-filtering glazing and low light.
- A vented or sealed frame follows the owner's and conservator's advice.

## Method (general)
1. Check strength, weight and condition and display period.
2. Choose a mount; make a mock-up with scrap.
3. Mount by stitching to the board using conservation-stitching; never glue.
4. Check for gravity, vibration and visitor access.

## Verify
- [ ] Textile weight carried safely.
- [ ] Mount allows removal without damage.
- [ ] Mount materials tested.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
