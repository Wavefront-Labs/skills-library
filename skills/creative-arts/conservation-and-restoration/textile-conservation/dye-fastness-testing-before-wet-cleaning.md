# Dye fastness testing before wet cleaning

**Goal:** decide which colours will bleed so wet cleaning is only done when safe.

## Why test
Dyes vary: some natural dyes and many early synthetic dyes bleed in water; reds, purples, browns and blacks are commonly fugitive. A single test on one area does not clear the whole object.

## Method
1. Select an inconspicuous spot for each distinct colour, on the reverse or an edge.
2. Moisten a cotton swab or a small piece of white filter paper with the planned liquid (first deionised water, then the cleaning solution at its working concentration and pH).
3. Place the damp swab on the textile over white blotting paper beneath, if the weave allows, and press lightly, holding for several minutes.
4. Look for colour on the swab, the blotter or the surrounding area; note any halo on drying.
5. Test also with alcohols or solvents if solvent cleaning is considered.
6. Repeat for every colour, including threads in embroidery and each stripe; record the results on a diagram.

## Decide
- Any transfer, however faint: no immersion or wet cleaning without a plan to lock the dye or to treat locally; dry methods may be the only safe choice.
- No transfer: wet cleaning may be considered, subject to the strength and condition of the fibres.

## Verify
- [ ] Every colour tested and mapped.
- [ ] Test liquids match the planned treatment.
- [ ] Results recorded in the file.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
