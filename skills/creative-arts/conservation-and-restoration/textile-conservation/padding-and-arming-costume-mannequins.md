# Padding and arming costume mannequins

**Goal:** support a garment on a form without strain, with the correct shape.

## Principles
- Support must carry the garment's weight on stable, broad areas, such as shoulders, rather than on seams or fragile fabric.
- Materials must be inert and non-staining: polyester wadding, polyethylene foam, cotton stockinette or jersey, acid-free tissue.

## Padding a form
1. Measure the garment; compare with the form and plan padding for chest, waist and hips.
2. Build up layers of polyester wadding or thin foam sheets, tacked with stitches, to the silhouette of the garment; keep the padding smooth.
3. Cover with a stockinette or jersey cover; sew in place.
4. Dress the garment with two people; do not pull on seams.

## Soft arms
1. Make a tube of cotton jersey filled with wadding or foam, shaped to the sleeve's size and bend.
2. Where an armature is needed, use covered wire; secure at the shoulder; keep the arm supported not to pull the sleeve.
3. For sleeve shape, use tissue stuffing inside the sleeve; leave the arm soft.

## Cautions
- Avoid using new fabrics that bleed or off-gas without testing (see `oddy-test-for-display-materials`).
- Never pin into the garment; use cotton tape.

## Verify
- [ ] Garment fits without tension at seams.
- [ ] Weight carried by padding, not stitches.
- [ ] Materials tested and recorded.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
