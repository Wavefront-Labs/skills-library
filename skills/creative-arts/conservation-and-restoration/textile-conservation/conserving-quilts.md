# Conserving quilts

**Goal:** protect a three-layer textile with mixed fibres and dyes.

## Assessment
- Identify the top, batting and backing materials and the stitching; note tears, failing seams and weak dyes, particularly in the top fabrics and any shattered silks.
- Check for soiling, stains, pest damage and previous repairs.

## Care
1. Vacuum through a screen at low suction (see `low-suction-vacuuming-of-textiles`).
2. Do not wet clean unless a conservator has tested dyes and fabrics; washing a quilt can cause dye bleeding and shrinkage.
3. Stabilise loose seams and tears by stitching onto a support patch or net.

## Display and storage
- Display flat or on a slanted board; avoid hanging for long, because the weight stretches the stitching. If hung, use a full-width system (see `hook-and-loop-hanging-for-flat-textiles`).
- Limit light, since dyes are often fugitive.
- Store flat, or rolled on a wide tube with tissue; refold along different lines every so often if folded.

## Verify
- [ ] Layers and weak areas mapped.
- [ ] Display limited in light and duration.
- [ ] Storage method recorded.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
