# Boxed and hanging garment storage

**Goal:** protect garments from creasing, strain, dust and light.

## Boxed storage
- Use acid-free, lignin-free boxes of ample size, kept flat.
- Line with acid-free tissue; lay the garment in as few folds as needed; pad each fold and sleeve with crumpled tissue.
- Place heavy or fragile items on their own. Label the outside, and add a list.
- Do not overfill; do not stack heavily.

## Hanging storage
- Only for garments with strong shoulders and weight-bearing fabrics; unsuitable for weak, weighted-silk or heavy beaded garments.
- Make padded hangers: a wooden hanger or a purpose-made form, padded with polyester wadding and covered with cotton, shaped to the garment's shoulder.
- Cover each garment with a cotton muslin cover or Tyvek sleeve (not plastic that sweats), long enough to prevent contact with the floor.
- Leave space between hangers to avoid crushing.

## Cautions
- Hang by internal tapes only if they are strong; do not use loops.
- Avoid wood boxes; test materials for off-gassing.

## Verify
- [ ] Garment supported without strain.
- [ ] Padding and covers clean, tested.
- [ ] Storage type recorded.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
