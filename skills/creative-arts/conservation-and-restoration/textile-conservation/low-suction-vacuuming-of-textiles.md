# Low-suction vacuuming of textiles

**Goal:** lift loose dust and soil safely before any other treatment.

## Equipment
- A vacuum with variable suction control (a museum or conservation vacuum), a soft brush attachment, and a small nozzle or tube.
- A rigid screen: a frame covered with fibreglass or nylon mesh, edges taped or bound so that they do not snag; or a piece of net held down gently.
- Clean support surface, padded.

## Method
1. Examine the textile; do not vacuum loose, fragile or friable areas (flaking paint, loose beads or sequins, shattered silk) without a conservator's advice.
2. Lay the screen over the surface; place the vacuum nozzle or brush on the screen, not on the textile.
3. Start with the lowest suction; increase only as needed.
4. Move slowly across the screen in parallel strokes; lift off at edges.
5. Where soil is stuck, use a soft brush lightly in the direction of the weave, with the nozzle held close.
6. Reverse and treat the back if safe.
7. Empty the filter regularly; clean the screen between objects.

## Cautions
- Direct suction can pull fibres, loops and loose threads; keep the screen between nozzle and object.
- Vacuum will not remove ingrained soil; it is also a preliminary step to wet cleaning.

## Verify
- [ ] No loose fibres or threads on the screen or in the filter other than dust.
- [ ] No distortion or pulled threads.
- [ ] Suction level recorded.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
