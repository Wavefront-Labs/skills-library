# Reshaping three-dimensional textiles

**Goal:** recover the original form of crushed hats, gloves, bags and similar items with gentle humidification and internal support.

## Preparation
- Study the structure: stiffening, wire, buckram, glued parts. Wetting can dissolve adhesives or loosen stiffeners.
- Test dyes and materials (see `dye-fastness-testing-before-wet-cleaning`).
- Make or find a padded form: polyethylene foam shaped to the interior and covered with cotton or tissue, or acid-free tissue stuffed loosely.

## Method
1. Humidify in a chamber or locally via Gore-Tex or fine vapour (see `relaxing-creases-and-flattening-textiles`), only until fibres become pliant.
2. Stuff the object gradually with tissue or insert the form, working from the inside; coax the creases out; never force.
3. Support the shape with tissue pads and polyester wadding at weak points; keep the form light.
4. Dry slowly in a stable environment; leave the support in place until fully dry.
5. For permanent display or storage, retain a custom support so the shape is kept.

## Cautions
- Leather, straw, felt and fur react differently to humidity; follow materials-specific advice (see `cleaning-and-stabilising-leather-objects`).
- Excess force breaks stiffened parts.

## Verify
- [ ] Shape recovered without cracks.
- [ ] Padded support documented and kept with object.
- [ ] Materials and times recorded.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
