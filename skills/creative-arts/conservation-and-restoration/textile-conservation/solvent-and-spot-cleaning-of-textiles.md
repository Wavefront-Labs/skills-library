# Solvent and spot cleaning of textiles

**Goal:** remove local stains or oily soil without wetting the whole textile.

## When it helps
- Oil and grease, wax, adhesive residues, localised tide lines, or textiles that cannot be immersed.
- Solvents and water can both cause dye movement and tide lines; test first.

## Method
1. Test each colour and the fibres for sensitivity to the proposed solvent or liquid (see `dye-fastness-testing-before-wet-cleaning`).
2. Work in a fume cupboard or with local extraction when using solvents; solvents are flammable and harmful.
3. Place the textile on a suction table or over blotters with an absorbent layer; apply the liquid in small amounts with a brush or swab, working from the edge of the stain inward.
4. Let suction or the blotters draw the dissolved stain through; replace blotters as they pick up soil.
5. For adhesive residues, a solvent poultice on a barrier layer may be used.
6. Rinse locally with deionised water if aqueous steps were used; avoid leaving a ring.
7. Dry flat; check for halos.

## Choosing liquids
- Low-aromatic hydrocarbons for oils and waxes; ethanol or acetone for some residues; aqueous detergent for water-soluble stains. Always the weakest effective liquid.

## Verify
- [ ] Test results recorded for each colour.
- [ ] No halo or ring after drying.
- [ ] Solvents, ventilation and quantities recorded.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
