# Wet cleaning textiles

**Goal:** reduce soil and acid degradation products from a textile that can bear water, with maximum support and control.

## Conditions
- All colours pass the dye test (see `dye-fastness-testing-before-wet-cleaning`).
- Fibres are strong enough to be handled wet, since wet fibres are weaker; weighted silk, brittle or heavily repaired textiles are usually not suitable.
- Surface decoration, metal threads and sizing are considered.
- The object has been vacuumed (see `low-suction-vacuuming-of-textiles`).

## Method
1. Prepare a tray larger than the textile; use deionised water at room temperature. Decide whether detergent is needed: a very low concentration of a mild non-ionic detergent is common (Synperonic A7 in UK practice, or Orvus WA Paste, a mild anionic, in US practice; check availability and safety data sheets).
2. Support the textile on a sheet of nylon net or a large sheet of polyester so it is lifted wet as one.
3. Lower into the bath; avoid agitation and wringing; press gently with a sponge.
4. After the soil has lifted, drain, and rinse in several baths of deionised water until the rinse water is clear and conductivity is near that of fresh water.
5. Lift on the support, blot with towels, and dry flat on clean blotters or towels, shaping gently (see `blocking-and-pinning-out-textiles`).

## Cautions
- Never lift an unsupported wet textile.
- Heavy soil may only partially lift; do not push further.
- Check for tide marks, shrinkage and distortion afterward.

## Verify
- [ ] Dye test passed for all colours.
- [ ] Rinse conductivity measured near deionised baseline.
- [ ] Dried flat without distortion; recorded.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
