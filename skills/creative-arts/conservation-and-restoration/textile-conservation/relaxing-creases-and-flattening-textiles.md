# Relaxing creases and flattening textiles

**Goal:** reduce creases and distortions with moisture applied as vapour, then hold the textile flat while it dries.

## Preparation
- Test dyes for sensitivity to moisture (see `dye-fastness-testing-before-wet-cleaning`).
- Do not humidify weak, shattered or metal-thread-sensitive areas without advice.

## Methods
1. **Humidity chamber or tent:** place the textile on a mesh in an enclosure with an ultrasonic humidifier (cool mist) or a damp pad; watch closely until fibres relax.
2. **Gore-Tex layer:** a damp blotter beneath a Gore-Tex sheet gives controlled vapour transfer to a small area.
3. **Local:** use a fine mist applied to a Gore-Tex or tissue barrier; do not spray directly.
4. When pliable, smooth with the hands or a soft tool, block to shape (see `blocking-and-pinning-out-textiles`) and place under glass plates or lightly weighted boards with a barrier layer (polyester web or tissue) to set flat while drying.
5. Leave until fully dry; leave under weight longer rather than less.

## Cautions
- Moisture risks tide lines, dye movement and mould; keep time short, and stay with the process.
- Heat (iron or steam) is not a conservation method for historic textiles.
- Glass is heavy; check weight on fragile textiles.

## Verify
- [ ] Creases relaxed without wet patches.
- [ ] Textile fully dry before release.
- [ ] Humidification time and method recorded.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
