# Adhesive supports and net overlays

**Goal:** choose the right method to support textiles too weak to stitch.

## Net overlays
- A fine net (nylon net or silk crepeline, often dyed to match) is laid over the textile and stitched at the edges and across it to hold fragments in place.
- Advantages: reversible by removing stitches; the textile is visible through the net.
- Limits: needs stitching points; net shows over textured or lustrous surfaces; some nets degrade or reflect light.

## Adhesive supports
- A support fabric coated with a heat-activated or solvent-activated adhesive (examples reported in the literature include BEVA 371 film and Lascaux acrylic dispersions) is attached to the textile with controlled heat or pressure.
- Used for very fragile textiles (shattered silk, painted silk) where stitching would cause more damage.
- Heat and pressure may alter the textile; adhesive removal is difficult; this is a specialist treatment with limited reversibility. Follow product data sheets and published case studies.

## Decision
1. Examine for fragility and strength under stitching.
2. Prefer stitching or net overlays, the most reversible options.
3. Consider adhesive supports only if the textile would be lost otherwise and with owner and expert agreement.
4. Test on scrap or a non-original sample first.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
