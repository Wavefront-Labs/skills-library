# Carving torso forms and building underpinnings

**Goal:** a made-to-measure support and silhouette for a garment whose shape depends on lost underclothes.

## Carved torso
1. Take measurements of the garment (bust, waist, hip, shoulder width, length); draw profiles on paper.
2. Cut layers of polyethylene foam (Ethafoam or Plastazote type) to the profile; glue or stitch layers together, with adhesives tested.
3. Carve with a serrated knife and rasp; smooth with sandpaper; dust off.
4. Cover with wadding and stockinette.
5. Mount on a stand with a fixing that carries the weight.

## Underpinnings
- Period garments need period shape: corsets, bum rolls, bustles, hoops, crinolines and petticoats.
- Reproduce the structure using cotton, polyester padding, wire covered in tape, or documented patterns; do not use the original underwear unless it is also an object for display and conserved.
- Build up gradually, checking the fit and silhouette against paintings and photographs.

## Cautions
- Do not strain the garment to fit; if it does not, rework the form.
- Check materials for off-gassing (see `oddy-test-for-display-materials`).

## Verify
- [ ] Silhouette matches evidence for the period.
- [ ] Garment hangs without distortion.
- [ ] Materials and construction documented.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
