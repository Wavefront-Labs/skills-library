# Rolled and folded textile storage

**Goal:** avoid sharp creases, pressure and distortion in large textiles.

## Rolled storage
- Suitable for large flat textiles without surface decoration (tapestries, carpets, flat panels); not for textiles with fragile decoration, heavy embroidery or pile that crushes.
- Use a tube of wide diameter (larger is better; many guides advise at least about 10 cm), cover with polyethylene foam and then a barrier or tissue.
- Roll with the right side outward if recommended for the object (check guidance for the type); roll with an interleaving tissue, keep tension slight, and cover the finished roll with clean cotton or Tyvek.
- Support the tube on brackets at both ends so it does not rest on the textile.

## Folded storage
- Use for textiles that cannot be rolled; fold with soft pads of crumpled acid-free tissue at each fold to avoid sharp creases.
- Use few folds, and change fold lines periodically.
- Store in an acid-free box on a flat shelf, never stacked heavily.

## Cautions
- Do not use cardboard tubes unless sealed and tested (acids).
- Inspect rolls and folds regularly.

## Verify
- [ ] No hard creases visible.
- [ ] Tube or box supported evenly.
- [ ] Storage method recorded with object.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
