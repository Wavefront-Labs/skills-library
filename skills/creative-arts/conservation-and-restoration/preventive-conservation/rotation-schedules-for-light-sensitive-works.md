# Rotation schedules for light-sensitive works

**Goal:** share limited light exposure across collections, balancing access and preservation.

## Method
1. Classify objects by light sensitivity (see `light-budgets-and-environmental-monitoring` and `microfade-testing`).
2. Set an annual or lifetime exposure budget in lux hours, using agreed light levels (commonly cited targets include about 50 lux for highly sensitive materials and about 200 lux for moderately sensitive ones; check your guidance).
3. Divide the budget by the lux level to calculate permitted display hours.
4. Build a schedule of display periods and rest periods in dark storage. Rest periods are not fixed numbers; they depend on how much of the budget was used and local policy.
5. Record exposure for each object: hours on display, measured lux, dates.
6. Review with curators and exhibitions staff, who need access and varied displays.

## Practices
- Use light dosimeters or loggers to verify real exposure.
- Include conditions like daylight and spotlights in the budget.
- Replace with facsimiles for permanent displays.

## Verify
- [ ] Budget and lux levels documented.
- [ ] Exposure log up to date.
- [ ] Rotation agreed with curators.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
