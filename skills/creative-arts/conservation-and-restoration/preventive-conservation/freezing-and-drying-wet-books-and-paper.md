# Freezing and drying wet books and paper

**Goal:** stabilise wet material before mould grows, then dry it with minimum distortion.

## First response
1. Ensure safety: no electric hazard, no structural risk, protective clothing. Floodwater and sewage need extra protection.
2. Stabilise the room: lower temperature and humidity, increase air flow; mould can grow within about 48 to 72 hours.
3. Document before moving.
4. Triage by priority and material type.

## Triage
- Coated (glossy) paper blocks stick together when drying; freeze quickly or separate with interleaving right away.
- Photographs: keep wet if drying is delayed, in clean cold water; or air-dry on clean surfaces; do not freeze prints without advice.
- Bound volumes and boxes: pack spine-down in crates, with sheets of freezer paper or polyethylene to prevent sticking.
- Items with soluble media (inks, water colour): handle gently.

## Freezing
1. Pack in milk crates or boxes lined with freezer paper; label.
2. Freeze quickly, in a commercial blast freezer if possible, to stop mould and damage.
3. Keep frozen until a drying plan is made.

## Drying
- **Vacuum freeze-drying:** frozen items are placed in a vacuum chamber where ice sublimes; this is a commercial or institutional service and is the method of choice for large amounts.
- **Air drying:** for small amounts, interleave with absorbent paper and change it; use fans, controlled humidity and space.
- After drying, clean and assess for mould (see `mould-outbreak-response`).

## Verify
- [ ] Mould avoided or contained.
- [ ] Material triaged and recorded.
- [ ] Drying method suited to quantity and item types.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
