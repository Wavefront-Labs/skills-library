# Significance assessment

**Goal:** a reasoned statement of why an item or collection matters, to guide decisions.

## Framework
Significance 2.0 (Russell and Winkworth, Collections Council of Australia) uses:
- **Primary criteria:** historic, artistic or aesthetic, scientific or research potential, and social or spiritual value.
- **Comparative criteria:** provenance, rarity or representativeness, condition or completeness, and interpretive capacity.

## Steps
1. Gather documentation: provenance, history, makers, use, research, associations.
2. Consult stakeholders, including communities of origin and users.
3. Apply each criterion with evidence; assign levels such as high, moderate, low or local, regional, national, international as your scheme defines.
4. Write a statement of significance, a short paragraph supported by evidence.
5. Use it in the survey and risk work (see `abc-method-risk-assessment`).

## Cautions
- Significance changes with time and view; schedule review.
- Avoid assessing alone; document who contributed.

## Verify
- [ ] Each criterion addressed with evidence.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
