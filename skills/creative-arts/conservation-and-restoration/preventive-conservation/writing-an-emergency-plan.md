# Writing an emergency plan

**Goal:** a plan that staff can follow under stress.

## Content
1. **Scope and risk:** hazards for the site (fire, flood, storm, earthquake, burst pipe, theft, power loss); use the ABC method or a simple risk list (see `abc-method-risk-assessment`).
2. **Roles:** the response team, leader, deputies, and how to contact them out of hours.
3. **Contact lists:** emergency services, utility firms, conservators, freezer and drying services, insurers, suppliers; keep copies off site.
4. **Salvage priorities:** a short list of the most significant or vulnerable items, their locations and how to remove them; floor plans marked with exits, shutoffs and extinguishers.
5. **Procedures:** first actions for each type of event; safety first, then access, assessment, stabilisation, salvage, recovery.
6. **Supplies:** a kit with torches, gloves, plastic sheeting, absorbent materials, labels, camera, tools, crates, boxes; stored where reachable.
7. **Recovery:** documentation, insurance steps, reporting and communication.

## Source guidance
The NPS Museum Handbook Part I (chapter on emergency planning), the Heritage Emergency National Task Force field guide, and CCI emergency resources offer templates.

## Practice
- Train staff and run drills.
- Review at least annually and after any incident.

## Verify
- [ ] Plan approved by management.
- [ ] Contact lists tested.
- [ ] Drill carried out and notes acted on.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
