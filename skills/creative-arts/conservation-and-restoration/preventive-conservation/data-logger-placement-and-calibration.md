# Data logger placement and calibration

**Goal:** readings that represent what objects experience, from sensors that are accurate.

## Placement
1. Put loggers near the objects, at similar height, inside display cases and storage furniture as well as in the room.
2. Avoid direct sun, lights, heaters, vents, doors, outside walls and the person walking by; these give misleading spikes.
3. Place enough loggers to represent each zone, and one outdoors if comparing with outside conditions.
4. Choose a recording interval (commonly 15 to 60 minutes) so that fluctuations show but data volume is manageable.
5. Label each logger, map its location, and record its serial number.

## Calibration
1. Check sensors on a schedule (often annually) and after any suspicious drift.
2. **Saturated salt method:** place the logger in a sealed container with a saturated solution of a salt that gives a known humidity; at about 25 C, magnesium chloride gives about 33 percent RH, sodium chloride about 75 percent, and lithium chloride about 11 percent. Leave to equilibrate (a day or more) at stable temperature, then compare the reading.
3. **Reference instrument:** compare with a recently calibrated hygrometer or psychrometer in the same place.
4. Correct or replace sensors that differ by more than their specification; note corrections in the data.

## Verify
- [ ] Map and serial numbers recorded.
- [ ] Calibration dates and results logged.
- [ ] Data downloaded and backed up.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
