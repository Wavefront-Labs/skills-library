# Pest monitoring with blunder traps and identification

**Goal:** know what pests are present, where, and in what numbers, before damage occurs.

## Framework
Integrated pest management (IPM) for collections: avoid, block, detect, respond, recover. BS EN 16790 describes IPM for cultural heritage. See also the Museum Pests site for identification help.

## Trap placement
1. Use unscented, flat sticky traps (blunder traps), without pheromone lures unless you want to monitor a specific species.
2. Place along walls, in corners, near doors, vents, pipes, storage furniture and display cases; map every trap with an ID.
3. Check at a regular interval (commonly every month, more often in summer or after an incident); date the sheet, and replace when dirty.

## Identification
1. Examine catches with a hand lens or stereomicroscope.
2. Compare with reference guides. Typical museum pests include carpet beetles (Anthrenus species), webbing clothes moths (Tineola bisselliella), silverfish, booklice, cockroaches, and wood-boring beetles such as furniture beetle (Anobium punctatum). Many insects caught are harmless visitors.
3. Count and graph catches per trap per period.
4. Photograph unclear specimens and send to a specialist.

## Response
- Define action thresholds in the plan (for example presence of any known pest species in a collection area).
- Inspect nearby objects and their packing, clean, remove the source and treat if needed (see `freezing-and-anoxic-pest-treatment`).

## Verify
- [ ] Traps mapped, dated and checked on schedule.
- [ ] Identifications recorded.
- [ ] Actions taken recorded.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
