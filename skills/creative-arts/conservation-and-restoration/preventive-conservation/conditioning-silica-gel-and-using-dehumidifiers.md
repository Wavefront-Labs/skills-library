# Conditioning silica gel and using dehumidifiers

**Goal:** stable humidity in cases and rooms by buffering and controlled equipment.

## Silica gel for cases
1. Use a silica gel made for conservation, buffered to hold a specific RH (products in the family of Art-Sorb or ProSorb exist, and others).
2. Condition to the target: in a sealed container alongside a hygrometer, expose the gel to the humidity you need until it reaches equilibrium, using a humidity chamber or salt solution; the supplier gives instructions.
3. Quantity: a published rule of thumb for a sealed case with low air exchange is on the order of 20 kg of gel per cubic metre of case volume; use the supplier's calculator and the case's measured air exchange.
4. Put the gel in sealed trays or sachets in the case (often under a false floor), not in contact with objects, and with an easy way to swap.
5. Check the RH with a logger; reset the gel when the case drifts, commonly every few months or yearly.

## Room dehumidifiers
- **Compressor** type works at warm temperatures; **desiccant** type works at lower temperatures.
- Place away from objects, with a drain or reservoir that is emptied; a humidistat and a logger to check output.

## Room humidifiers
- Ultrasonic or evaporative types; use deionised or demineralised water in ultrasonic units to avoid dust deposits; clean regularly to avoid microbial growth.
- Do not humidify when the building cannot tolerate it, because condensation in walls or on windows follows.

## Verify
- [ ] Case RH logged and within target.
- [ ] Gel quantity and conditioning recorded.
- [ ] Equipment maintained and checked.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
