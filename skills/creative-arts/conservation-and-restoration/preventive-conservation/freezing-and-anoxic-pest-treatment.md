# Freezing and anoxic pest treatment

**Goal:** kill all life stages of pests without chemical residues, and prevent reinfestation.

## Quarantine
1. Isolate incoming objects and borrowed items in a separate room or sealed bags.
2. Inspect for live insects, frass and damage; monitor with traps.
3. Hold for a period decided by policy (commonly some weeks) before moving into collections.
4. Treat if infested.

## Freezing
1. Check suitability: freezing suits many textiles, paper, natural history items and wood, but not composite or sensitive objects (for example panel paintings, lacquer, some glass, ivory, objects with unstable layers); consult a conservator.
2. Wrap in acid-free tissue and seal in polyethylene bags with the air pressed out, to avoid moisture condensing on the object.
3. Place in a freezer. Commonly cited protocols hold at about minus 20 C for at least one to two weeks (some use a lower temperature for a shorter period); check the protocol for your species and materials.
4. Return to room temperature slowly with the bag still sealed, until the contents are above the room dew point, then open.
5. Inspect and clean.

## Anoxic treatment
1. Seal the object in a barrier film bag or chamber (for example Escal or Marvelseal type film).
2. Reduce oxygen with nitrogen or argon flushing, or with oxygen scavengers (the Ageless type is used), to below about 0.3 percent oxygen; use an oxygen indicator.
3. Hold for several weeks at room temperature (commonly in the order of three to four weeks); check the protocol.
4. Open, inspect and clean.

## Cautions
- Treatments do not protect afterwards; fix the cause.
- Do not use inert gases in unventilated rooms (asphyxiation risk).

## Verify
- [ ] Suitability check recorded.
- [ ] Temperature or oxygen readings logged for the full period.
- [ ] Post-treatment inspection recorded.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
