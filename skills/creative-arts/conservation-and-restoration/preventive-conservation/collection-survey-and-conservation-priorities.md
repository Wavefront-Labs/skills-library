# Collection survey and conservation priorities

**Goal:** know the condition of the collection and decide what to do first.

## Survey design
1. State the aim (planning, funding, storage move, exhibition).
2. Choose scope: full survey of every object, or a statistically valid sample of each collection area.
3. Design a short form with fields for identification, location, materials, condition rating, hazards and recommended action (see `condition-terminology-rating-and-report-templates`).
4. Train surveyors and calibrate on shared examples.
5. Record data in a spreadsheet or database; photograph typical issues.

## Priority setting
- Combine condition, significance (see `significance-assessment`), risk (see `abc-method-risk-assessment`), and use or exhibition plans.
- Group needs into: urgent hazards; treatment candidates; storage and housing improvements; preventive actions; monitoring.
- Rank within each group and assign cost estimates.

## Output
A report with findings, priorities, costed recommendations and a schedule; share with managers. Programmes such as collection assessment schemes (the United States has a Collections Assessment for Preservation programme) use similar structure.

## Verify
- [ ] Surveyors calibrated.
- [ ] Priorities justified with criteria.
- [ ] Plan costed and scheduled.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
