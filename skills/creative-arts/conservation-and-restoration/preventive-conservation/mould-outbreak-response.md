# Mould outbreak response

**Goal:** stop growth, remove the cause and protect people and collections.

## Immediate steps
1. Protect staff: gloves, coverall, eye protection and a fitted particulate respirator; limit access; alert those with respiratory conditions.
2. Isolate affected items and the area; do not move large amounts through clean areas.
3. Photograph, record the extent and locations.
4. Find and stop the moisture source (leaks, condensation, flooding, humid air).

## Environment
- Reduce relative humidity (commonly aim for below about 60 percent and falling), improve air circulation and temperature control.
- Use dehumidifiers (see `conditioning-silica-gel-and-using-dehumidifiers`) and fans with care to avoid spreading spores; HEPA filtration helps.

## Treating materials
- If material is wet, freeze or dry quickly (see `freezing-and-drying-wet-books-and-paper`).
- Dry, inactive mould: remove by brushing or HEPA vacuum in a controlled space (see `removing-inactive-mould-from-paper`).
- Avoid bleach and fungicidal sprays; they harm collections and people and rarely solve the cause.
- Consult a conservator and, for large outbreaks, a specialist contractor and your health and safety officer.

## Afterward
- Clean the room and shelving; inspect everything nearby.
- Monitor RH and inspect regularly.
- Update the emergency plan (see `writing-an-emergency-plan`).

## Verify
- [ ] Moisture source fixed.
- [ ] RH under control and logged.
- [ ] Re-inspection shows no regrowth.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
