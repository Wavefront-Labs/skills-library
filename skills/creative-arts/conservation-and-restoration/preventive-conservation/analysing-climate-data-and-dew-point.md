# Analysing climate data and dew point

**Goal:** find out whether the environment threatens the collection, and when.

## Steps
1. Download data; check for gaps and sensor faults.
2. Plot temperature and relative humidity over time, with overlays for seasons and events (heating, visitors, weather).
3. Calculate average, minimum, maximum and the spread; look at daily and seasonal fluctuations.
4. Use tools such as the Image Permanence Institute's eClimateNotebook, which reports metrics including mechanical damage risk and mould risk, or do it in a spreadsheet.
5. Compare with targets (see `pas-198-and-bs-en-16893-environment-targets`).
6. Look for patterns: mid-day spikes, night rises, weekend changes, HVAC cycles.

## Dew point
- The dew point is the temperature at which air becomes saturated, and surfaces below it get condensation.
- Quick estimate when RH is above about 50 percent: dew point is about the air temperature minus (100 minus RH) divided by 5, in degrees Celsius. Use the Magnus formula for better accuracy.
- Compare dew point with surface and wall temperatures, and with temperature of cold-stored items when removing them (see `cold-storage-for-collections`).

## Mould risk
Prolonged high humidity (commonly cited as above about 65 to 70 percent) with poor air movement raises mould risk, and risk rises with temperature.

## Action
Choose fixes ranked by cost and effect: adjust set points, reduce infiltration, use buffer materials, add dehumidification (see `conditioning-silica-gel-and-using-dehumidifiers`).
