# Respirator selection

**Goal:** the right respirator for the hazard, fitted and used correctly.

## Start with controls
Respirators are the last line of defence. First use ventilation, extraction, enclosure and safer materials (fume extraction), then PPE as needed. Follow your regulator: for example the UK Health and Safety Executive requires face-fit testing for tight-fitting masks.

## Choose by hazard
- **Dust, mould spores, fine powders (including contaminated dust):** particulate filters: filtering facepieces FFP2 or FFP3 (EN 149), or N95 or P100 in the US; half-masks with P3 or P100 filters protect better and can be reused.
- **Solvent vapours:** a half or full mask with gas and vapour cartridges: type A (organic vapours, boiling point above about 65 C) under EN 14387, brown colour code; very low-boiling solvents such as acetone need type AX, or better extraction and a different approach; US organic vapour (OV) cartridges are typically black.
- **Mixed hazards:** combined filter (for example A2P3).
- Always check the safety data sheet and the filter manufacturer's table.

## Use
1. Medical clearance where required.
2. Fit test; facial hair prevents a seal; check the seal each time (user seal check).
3. Replace filters per manufacturer, when breathing resistance rises or you smell solvent.
4. Store clean, sealed.

## Cautions
- Respirators do not protect against all gases; for unknown hazards or oxygen-deficient spaces use specialist equipment.
- Training is required.

## Verify
- [ ] Hazard identified and filter matched.
- [ ] Fit test valid and recorded.
- [ ] Replacement schedule followed.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
