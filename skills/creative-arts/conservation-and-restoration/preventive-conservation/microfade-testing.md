# Microfade testing

**Goal:** replace guesses about light sensitivity with a measured fading rate for the specific object.

## Principle
A microfading tester focuses a high-intensity light beam, a spot a fraction of a millimetre across, on a small area of the object and measures colour change over minutes with a spectrometer. The rate of change estimates how quickly the colour will fade under normal gallery light.

## Procedure
1. Obtain consent, and choose test spots (representative colours, inconspicuous places) with a conservator.
2. Calibrate the instrument with Blue Wool standards (ISO 105 B01 to B08) on the same setup.
3. Position the probe at fixed distance, run the test for a set exposure, stop at the planned limit; keep exposure short to limit damage.
4. Record the colour change in CIELAB units and the equivalent light dose to reach a just noticeable change.
5. Classify sensitivity (for example, comparable to Blue Wool 1 to 2 as highly sensitive, and higher numbers as less sensitive) and repeat on key colours.

## Using results
- Calculate allowed annual exposure in lux hours and display period (see `light-budgets-and-environmental-monitoring`).
- Plan rotation schedules (see `rotation-schedules-for-light-sensitive-works`).
- Record in the object file.

## Cautions
- Tests are minimally invasive but not harmless; do not test irreplaceable colours without agreement.
- Different instruments and spot sizes produce different absolute values; compare with the same method.

## Verify
- [ ] Consent and test locations recorded.
- [ ] Standards run in the same session.
- [ ] Limits recorded and used in planning.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
