# PAS 198 and BS EN 16893 environment targets

**Goal:** choose and justify temperature and humidity targets from standards, instead of copying a single number.

## The documents
- **PAS 198:2012** (BSI): specification for managing environmental conditions for cultural collections. Risk-based: it asks you to understand what each material needs and the cost of control. It was followed by BS EN 16893:2018 (location, construction and modification of buildings or rooms for heritage collections) and BS 4971:2017 (conservation and care of archive and library collections).
- **BS EN 15757:2010:** specifying temperature and relative humidity to limit climate-induced mechanical damage in organic hygroscopic materials, based on an object's own historic climate (see below).
- **Bizot Green Protocol (2014 to 2015):** an international museum-loan agreement allowing a wider band than traditional strict values where objects tolerate it; commonly summarised as relative humidity 40 to 60 percent and temperature 16 to 25 C, with limits on daily fluctuation. Confirm against the current text.
- **ASHRAE Handbook (Museums, Galleries, Archives and Libraries chapter):** defines classes of control (AA, A, B, C, D) from precision control down to simple building protection, and the risk each carries for different collection types.

## Practical method
1. List materials in the collection and their sensitivity (hygroscopic organic, metals, photographic, composite).
2. Gather data on the current environment (see `data-logger-placement-and-calibration`).
3. Identify the target class or band using the documents above; acknowledge trade-offs with energy use and building limits.
4. Define limits for average, seasonal drift and short-term fluctuation.
5. Record the rationale and review periodically.

## Notes
- A single target such as 50 percent RH plus or minus 5 is a tradition, not a universal rule; stable conditions close to long-term experience are often more important than the number.
- Mould risk rises when relative humidity stays above about 65 to 70 percent.
- Check each document's current edition and whether it applies in your country.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
