# ABC risk assessment

**Goal:** a comparable, quantified ranking of risks to a collection.

## Method (CCI and ICCROM)
The ABC Method: a risk management approach to the preservation of cultural heritage (Michalski and Pedersoli, 2016) steps through:
1. **Establish context:** the collection, its value, the site and what the assessment is for.
2. **Identify risks:** across the ten agents of deterioration: physical forces, thieves and vandals, fire, water, pests, contaminants, light and radiation, incorrect temperature, incorrect relative humidity, and dissociation.
3. **Analyse and score:** for each risk, estimate
   - A: how often it happens or how soon;
   - B: loss in value for each affected item;
   - C: how much of the collection's value is affected.
   Scores are on defined scales and are summed to give a magnitude of risk.
4. **Evaluate:** rank risks; identify the biggest few.
5. **Treat:** options to avoid, block, detect, respond and recover; compare cost and effect.

## Practices
- Work with a team of staff who know the building and collection.
- Use the published scales exactly, and note uncertainty.
- Revisit after changes.

## Verify
- [ ] All ten agents considered.
- [ ] Scores and reasons written.
- [ ] Top risks have costed treatment options.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
