# Levelling and shaping fills

**Goal:** a fill at exactly the height of the surrounding paint, edges feathered into the
loss margins but not over the original.

## Methods
- **Wet levelling (water-soluble fills such as gesso, Modostuc):** a barely damp cotton
  swab or a damp chamois tip rolled over the fill dissolves the top; work in tiny circles.
  Fast and safe for the paint if the swab stays inside the loss.
- **Scalpel:** pare down hard spots with the blade almost flat, working towards the loss edge.
- **Abrasives:** micro-mesh or fine abrasive papers wrapped on a small stick, only for
  hard fills, protecting the surrounding paint with Melinex masks.
- **Solvent-soluble fills:** swab with a controlled solvent amount.

## Procedure
1. Let the fill dry fully; it may shrink.
2. Level until only the fill sits slightly proud.
3. Check under raking light from several angles and under magnification.
4. Final pass to flush; then texture.

## Gotchas
- Wet swabs creep onto original paint; on water-sensitive paint use solvent-based fills.
- Overworking produces a hollow (cupped) fill; add a skim coat and redo.
- Edges left proud make halos in raking light.

## Verify
- [ ] Fill level in raking light.
- [ ] No fill material on original paint (check in UV, fills often show differently).
