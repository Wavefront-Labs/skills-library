# Brush varnishing

**Goal:** a continuous, level varnish film applied by hand, with no brush marks and no disturbance of the layers beneath.

## Materials
- A clean, wide, soft brush (hake or similar flat natural-hair brush) kept for varnish only.
- Varnish at a concentration giving a thin, flowing film; a lidded jar so the solution does not change strength by evaporation.
- Painting laid flat, or horizontal under the working area, where the varnish can level.

## Procedure
1. Test on a sample board and on an inconspicuous area; confirm the paint is not sensitive to the solvent.
2. Load the brush lightly. Lay down a strip across the full width in one direction, slightly overlapping the previous strip while still wet.
3. Do not re-brush an area once the solvent has started to evaporate. Re-working a tacky film drags, leaves ridges and can lift paint.
4. Rotate the painting or cross-brush lightly once while the film is fully wet if your method calls for it, then stop.
5. Put on a second thin coat only when the first has set, rather than one thick coat.
6. Keep dust off the wet surface and keep the painting level until set.

## Failure modes
- Streaks and ridges: too viscous or too slow to work; thin the solution.
- Bubbles: overloaded brush or shaking the jar; allow bubbles to rise before use.
- Dragging a brush across thick impasto: tilt and tamp with a lightly loaded brush, or use spray.

## Verify
- [ ] No re-brushing of tacky areas.
- [ ] Even gloss in raking light.
- [ ] Brush and solvent type recorded.
