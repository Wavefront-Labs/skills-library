# Setting down flaking paint with a heated spatula

**Goal:** return each lifting flake to plane and bond it, without flattening impasto,
burning paint or squeezing adhesive onto the surface.

## Kit
- Temperature-controlled heated spatula (Willard or similar) with small and large tips.
- Silicone-coated release film (silicone Melinex/Mylar) or silicone paper.
- Consolidant as chosen (see `creative-arts.conservation-and-restoration.paintings-conservation.choosing-a-consolidant-for-paintings`),
  fine brush or micropipette, stereomicroscope or head loupe, blotter, small weights.

## Procedure
1. **Face or protect** very loose areas first if flakes could drop when touched.
2. **Introduce adhesive:** brush or pipette at the crack edge so it wicks under the
   flake; for raised tents, lift very slightly with a fine spatula to let it run in.
3. **Wait:** let the solvent or water partly evaporate until the flake softens and
   the adhesive is tacky (seconds to minutes).
4. **Set down:** release film over the area, then the spatula, starting cool (around
   40-50 C for aqueous glues, about 65 C to activate BEVA). Press from the centre of the
   flake to the crack edge so air and excess adhesive leave.
5. **Cool under weight:** a small sandbag or weight on blotter over the film while it sets.
6. Remove excess adhesive from the surface with a damp or solvent swab before it sets hard.

## Gotchas
- Too hot: impasto flattens, varnish blanches, paint blisters. Start low; raise in
  5-degree steps.
- A flake that will not go down is often too stiff or has shrunk; humidify it, or accept
  a slight lift rather than crushing it.
- Release film wrinkles print into soft paint; keep it smooth.
- Work in a grid and mark areas done; flakes look alike.

## Verify
- [ ] All lifting flakes treated; check in raking light from four directions.
- [ ] No adhesive on the surface (check in UV).
- [ ] Temperature and adhesive recorded.
