# Selecting a varnish resin

**Goal:** pick a varnish that saturates the paint, stays colourless, and can be removed later with solvents milder than those the paint can tolerate.

## Candidates and what to weigh
| Resin type | Examples | Main trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| Natural triterpenoid | dammar, mastic | Good saturation and traditional look; yellow, oxidise and become harder to remove with age. A hindered amine light stabiliser (HALS, e.g. Tinuvin 292) at a low percentage of resin weight slows yellowing in research by de la Rie and colleagues. |
| Low-molecular-weight (LMW) synthetic | Laropal A81 (urea-aldehyde), Regalrez 1094 (hydrogenated hydrocarbon), hydrogenated ketone resin (MS2A) | Designed for colour stability and easy removal; give high gloss and saturation. Dissolve in low-aromatic solvents. |
| Acrylic | Paraloid B-72 (ethyl methacrylate / methyl acrylate), B-67 (isobutyl methacrylate) | Very stable, lower gloss and saturation than LMW resins; higher molecular weight, so slower to dissolve. |

## Decision steps
1. Record what the surface was before cleaning (gloss, saturation, previous varnish type) and any known artist intent.
2. List the solvents the paint, ground and any retouching tolerate; the varnish must dissolve in something weaker than that.
3. Choose by the look the owner and conservator want (glossy / satin / matte; see the gloss-control skill) and the exposure (light, handling, display climate).
4. Apply to a test strip on a board or an inconspicuous edge before the painting; check saturation and gloss when fully dry.
5. Record resin, solvent, concentration, additives and manufacturer batch in the treatment report.

## Cautions
- Manufacturers change formulations and discontinue products; confirm the current technical data sheet. The manufacturer and exact grade of ketone resin MS2A is not confirmed here.
- Isolate fills and retouching with a varnish layer before final varnishing so later removal is selective (see `isolating-fills-before-retouching`).
- Natural-resin varnish over a LMW resin layer (or vice versa) complicates removal; do not mix systems casually.

## Verify
- [ ] Resin dissolves in a solvent safe for the paint beneath.
- [ ] Test panel looks right in raking light and daylight when dry.
- [ ] Resin, solvent, ratio and additives recorded.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
