# Flexible auxiliary supports for panels

**Goal:** support a weak or thinned panel against further warping and damage while
allowing it to move with RH, so it does not crack.

## When
- After cradle removal, for very thin panels, for panels with many splits, or where the
  panel's convex warp needs gentle control rather than flattening.

## Principles
- **Follow, do not force:** the support takes up the panel's current curvature.
- **Constant gentle force:** springs (or flexible battens) apply small, known forces at
  several points so the panel can swell and shrink.
- **No adhesive restraint across the grain.**
- **Reversible and adjustable:** attached by buttons, pads or blocks that can be removed.

## Typical construction
1. A secondary frame (seasoned wood, aluminium, or composite) shaped or adjustable to the
   panel's curvature, slightly smaller than the panel.
2. Spring units (compression springs in housings) at points spaced over the panel,
   pressing gently on pads glued to the reverse, or adjustable through slots.
3. Padded perimeter contacts where the frame holds the edges.
4. Calibrate the spring force with the panel at its equilibrium RH; record settings.
- Integrated "panel trays" in the frame with padded rebates and flexible fixings are a
  simpler option for many panels.

## Gotchas
- Too much force flattens the warp in a dry season and cracks the panel in a humid one.
- Glued pads must be removable without damage; use reversible adhesive on the wood.

## Verify
- [ ] Panel curvature and spring force recorded at a known RH.
- [ ] No new cracks after one seasonal cycle; check annually.
- [ ] Design drawings stored with the object file.
