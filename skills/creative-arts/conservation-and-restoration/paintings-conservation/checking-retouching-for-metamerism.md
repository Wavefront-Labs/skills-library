# Checking retouching for metamerism

**Goal:** make sure the retouching matches under the lighting the painting will actually meet.

## The problem
Two colours can look identical under one light source yet differ under another, because their spectral reflectance curves differ; this is metamerism. Retouching pigment mixtures often differ spectrally from the original pigments, so a match under the studio light may fail in the gallery.

## Procedure
1. Retouch under good daylight-balanced light, but do not trust a single source.
2. Check progressively under at least: north daylight or a daylight-balanced lamp, warm tungsten or halogen, and the LED type of the display location.
3. Look at the work at two or three distances and angles. Note any hue shift or change in value between retouch and original.
4. If mismatch appears, adjust the pigment choice; prefer pigments close to the original palette instead of mixing many hues.
5. Examine under UV (long-wave UV; see `examination-under-raking-light-and-uv`). Retouching commonly looks different from aged varnish under UV; this is useful for later detection, but strongly dark patches can reveal poor integration.
6. Record the lighting conditions used and the observations.

## Cautions
- UV is a hazard: protect eyes and skin; never examine under UV for longer than needed.
- Colour-matching instruments help but cannot replace visual checks in situ.

## Verify
- [ ] Retouching checked under at least three illuminants.
- [ ] Pigments chosen to minimise mismatch under display lighting.
- [ ] UV behaviour noted in the report.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
