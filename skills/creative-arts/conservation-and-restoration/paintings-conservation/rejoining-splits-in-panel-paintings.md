# Rejoining splits in panel paintings

**Goal:** close a split or open joint so the image surface is continuous, with a bond that
will fail before the wood does if the panel moves again.

## Before
- Understand why it split: restraint (cradle, battens, nailed frame), drying, impact.
  Remove the cause or the split will reopen.
- Stabilise RH; acclimatise the panel to the working room for days.
- Consolidate flaking paint along the split; face if needed.

## Align and join
1. **Align the paint surfaces, not the backs:** use a jig or clamping system that pushes
   the two edges level in plane (with padded cauls) while allowing them to close sideways.
2. Clean the split faces of old glue and dirt where reachable.
3. Glue with a reversible, slightly weaker-than-wood adhesive: warm animal glue is common;
   some practitioners use PVA emulsions. Apply thinly; close gently, do not force.
4. Where the split will not close fully (wood shrinkage), fit thin wedge-shaped (V-shaped)
   inserts of matched, seasoned wood, glued in, rather than forcing the panel.
5. Cure under alignment for the adhesive's full time.

## Avoid
- Butterfly keys and rigid battens across the grain: they restrain movement and cause
  new splits.
- Forcing closure against the panel's natural curvature.

## Verify
- [ ] Surfaces level in raking light across the join.
- [ ] Bond holds; no glue squeeze on paint.
- [ ] Cause addressed (restraint removed or reduced); RH plan in place.
