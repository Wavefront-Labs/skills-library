# Water-sensitive modern oil paint

**Goal:** identify sensitivity before it causes loss and keep treatment dry.

## Background
Researchers have documented 20th-century oil paintings whose paint swells or dissolves with water or aqueous cleaning solutions, a problem associated with water-soluble components forming in the paint. Causes and risk are still under study; consult recent literature (ICOM-CC Paintings and Modern Materials groups, Getty and university research) before treating.

## Steps
1. Suspect sensitivity in paintings of the later 19th and 20th centuries, especially with dull, matte, powdery or cracked passages, and where oil paint has not been varnished.
2. Test cautiously on the smallest hidden area with a dry cotton tip first, then moistening only if sensitivity cannot be excluded by other means; stop at any transfer of colour.
3. Prefer dry cleaning (soft brush, controlled suction through screening).
4. Avoid water, saliva and aqueous gels; do not assume non-polar solvents are safe; test.
5. Reduce exposure to humidity swings; control display and storage RH.
6. Record the sensitivity prominently in the condition report and on the object file so all later handlers know.

## Cautions
- A negative test does not guarantee the whole surface is safe; paints vary across a picture.
- Consult a paintings conservator before any wet treatment.

## Verify
- [ ] Sensitivity noted in the file and handling instructions.
- [ ] Only dry methods used on suspect areas.
- [ ] Display RH stable.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
