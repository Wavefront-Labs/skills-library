# Fill materials for paintings

**Goal:** level a loss with a material that takes texture, accepts retouching and can be
removed later without disturbing the original.

## Requirements
- **Weaker than the original** paint and ground, so stress fails the fill not the paint.
- **Reversible** in a solvent or with water the painting tolerates.
- **Stable:** no shrinking, cracking, yellowing.
- **Workable:** can be levelled, textured and isolated.

## Common choices
| Fill | Make-up | Removed by | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chalk-glue gesso | rabbit skin glue (about 7-10%) with chalk, sometimes a little titanium white | warm water | traditional; shrinks if overloaded with glue; good for canvas and panel |
| Modostuc | ready-made, water-based | water | easy to level and texture; widely used |
| BEVA 371 gesso | BEVA with fillers | hydrocarbon solvent, heat | for water-sensitive paintings; flexible |
| Acrylic dispersion fills | acrylic medium with fillers | solvent | flexible; harder to remove |
| Wax-resin fills | wax with fillers | heat, solvent | for wax-resin lined paintings |

- Avoid DIY spackles with unknown additives on works of value.

## Application
1. Clean the loss; consolidate edges.
2. Size the loss with dilute glue or the fill's medium if needed.
3. Apply fill slightly proud in thin layers, letting each dry.
4. Level and texture, see the levelling and texturing skills.

## Verify
- [ ] Fill confined to the loss; none on original paint.
- [ ] Removal tested on a sample.
- [ ] Fill type recorded in the treatment report.
