# Mimetic (illusionistic) retouching

**Goal:** make a loss visually disappear at normal viewing without altering original paint.

## Procedure
1. Fill, texture, level and isolate (see the earlier fill skills). Match the fill texture and gloss to its surroundings first; colour cannot hide a wrong surface.
2. Analyse the area: underlayers, glazes, translucency, local colour, surface sheen.
3. Build in thin layers: an opaque base to match tone, then translucent glazes for depth, adjusting hue and chroma.
4. Reproduce any craquelure or impasto only on the fill, using fine-tipped brushes or texture tools; do not extend into original paint.
5. Check frequently in daylight and display lighting and at oblique angles.
6. Examine the finished work under UV and magnification to confirm the retouching is confined to the loss.

## Ethical frame
AIC, ECCO and Icon codes require that additions be distinguishable from the original on close examination, reversible, and documented. A mimetic retouch is acceptable only where the owner agrees and the retouching is clearly recorded and detectable with technical means.

## Failure modes
- Overpainting onto original paint at the margin; mask with fine brushwork.
- Colour that matches in one light only (see `checking-retouching-for-metamerism`).
- Retouching too glossy or too matt for the surroundings.

## Verify
- [ ] Margins sit exactly on the loss.
- [ ] Surface texture and gloss match.
- [ ] Documented with before, during and after images including UV.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
