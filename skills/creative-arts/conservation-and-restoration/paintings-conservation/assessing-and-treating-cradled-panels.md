# Assessing and treating cradled panels

**Goal:** stop a cradle from cracking the panel it was meant to protect.

## What a cradle is
A lattice of fixed members glued along the grain on the reverse of a thinned panel, with
sliding members passing through them across the grain. Fitted widely in the 19th-20th
centuries. When the sliding members jam (dirt, glue, swelling), the panel cannot move
across the grain as RH changes.

## Assess
- **Washboarding:** a rippled surface where the panel bows between fixed members.
- **Splits and cracks** running alongside fixed members.
- **Jammed members:** try gentle movement with RH stable; note which move.
- Thickness of the panel (thinning before cradling often removed a lot of wood).
- Record with raking light, X-radiography if available, and RH history.

## Options, least invasive first
1. **Stabilise the environment:** a stable RH (and a microclimate frame) may be enough.
2. **Free the sliding members:** clean and wax channels, ease fits; reduces restraint.
3. **Modify:** remove sliding members only, leaving fixed members; or reduce the cradle.
4. **Remove the cradle:** sawn or chiselled off in sections under controlled RH, then the
   panel allowed to find its curve; a new flexible auxiliary support is usually needed.
   This is specialist work (Getty Panel Paintings Initiative trained practitioners).

## Gotchas
- A panel freed from a cradle will move, sometimes a lot; never remove a cradle without a
  plan for supporting the new curvature.
- Moving a cradled panel between climates is high risk; acclimatise slowly.

## Verify
- [ ] Assessment with images and RH data recorded.
- [ ] Option chosen with reasons and owner consent.
- [ ] Monitoring plan for movement after treatment.
