# Controlling gloss and saturation

**Goal:** match the surface appearance the owner, artist intent and period call for.

## What controls the look
- **Resin and refractive index:** higher-gloss, higher-saturation resins (LMW synthetics, natural resins) deepen dark passages; acrylics give a softer look. See `selecting-a-varnish-resin`.
- **Film smoothness:** gloss rises with a smoother, thicker film. Spray application and slow-levelling solvents give high gloss; fast solvents give a rougher, lower-gloss surface.
- **Matting agents:** fine particles that roughen the surface and scatter light. Common ones are microcrystalline wax (for example Cosmolloid 80H) and fumed silica. Dissolve or disperse them thoroughly and keep stirring during use.

## Procedure
1. Prepare a stock varnish and make small test boards with increasing matting agent (start low, for instance a few percent of resin weight, and vary).
2. View under the display lighting at the likely angles; compare against a darker area of the painting, where matting haze shows first.
3. Choose the lowest matting agent loading that gives the desired surface. Overloading gives a white, cloudy bloom over darks.
4. Apply a final thin satin layer over a glossy, stable saturating layer if desired, rather than a single heavily matted layer.

## Cautions
- Matting agents can settle between coats; mix again before each use.
- Do not use gloss to hide poor retouching; check retouching in raking light first.
- Document the varnish recipe in the report so the surface can be reproduced or removed.

## Verify
- [ ] Test board looks right on dark and light paint.
- [ ] No visible haze at oblique viewing angles.
- [ ] Recipe (resin, solvent, additives, percentages) recorded.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
