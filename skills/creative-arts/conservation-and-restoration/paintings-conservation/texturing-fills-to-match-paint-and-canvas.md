# Texturing fills to match paint and canvas

**Goal:** make the fill read as part of the surface under normal and angled light, so
retouching only has to match colour.

## Canvas weave
1. Take an impression of a sound area of weave (or a similar canvas) with a silicone
   rubber putty or a thin cast of silicone.
2. When the fill is just firm (leather-hard), press the impression in through a release
   film, align it to the weave direction, lift.
3. Re-wet and repeat lightly if the texture is too shallow.

## Brushwork and impasto
- Build up the fill in layers and carve with a scalpel or small chisel to continue the
  brushstrokes; or model wet fill with a brush or spatula.
- For impasto, work slightly higher than needed and carve down.

## Crack pattern
- Score fine cracks with a scalpel tip only where the surrounding craquelure clearly
  continues; never invent a pattern.

## Gotchas
- Over-texturing is as visible as a flat fill; compare in raking light from the
  same angle as the surrounding surface.
- Texture must sit at the level of the surrounding paint; check level first.
- Hard fills chip when carved; carve at leather-hard.

## Verify
- [ ] Fill invisible in raking light at the normal viewing distance.
- [ ] Weave direction aligned to the canvas.
- [ ] No texture or tool marks on the original.
