# Reducing cupping and tenting

**Goal:** bring raised paint back towards plane, enough to stop further loss, without
cracking stiff flakes or forcing them down.

## Read the deformation
- **Cupping:** paint islands curl up at their edges, concave in the middle. Common in old
  oil on canvas where canvas and glue size have shrunk under the paint.
- **Tenting:** two adjacent islands push up into a ridge over a crack: the support has
  shrunk and there is no longer room for the paint.
- Both mean the paint area is larger than the support area beneath it; pressing alone
  will crush or overlap edges.

## Approach
1. Consolidate first: an adhesive that also softens the flake helps (sturgeon glue with a
   little wheat starch paste is a classic cupping mix).
2. Relax the flake: local humidity (damp blotter and Gore-Tex or a humidity chamber over
   the area) and gentle warmth make stiff oil paint more pliable.
3. Set down progressively with a heated spatula and release film, centre first, then edges,
   repeating over several sessions if needed.
4. For extensive tenting caused by canvas shrinkage, consider structural options: relaxing
   the canvas on a low-pressure table with humidity, loose lining or lining. See
   `creative-arts.conservation-and-restoration.paintings-conservation.low-pressure-and-vacuum-hot-table-treatment`.

## Limits
- Very brittle, thick or ground-detached cups may not flatten without breaking. A stable,
  slightly cupped surface is better than crushed paint.
- Moisture risks canvas shrinkage in some old linens and blanching in paint; test.

## Verify
- [ ] Cause recorded (shrinkage, size failure, environment).
- [ ] Raking light images before and after; edges not overlapping.
- [ ] Bond checked; RH and heat used recorded.
