# Aqueous cleaning with controlled pH and conductivity

**Goal:** remove water-soluble grime from paint or varnish with a solution whose pH and
ionic strength are chosen for *this* surface, not plain tap or deionised water.

## Why control both
- **pH:** aged oil paint saponifies and swells at high pH; most practitioners keep oil
  paint in roughly pH 5.5-8.5 and work at the low end on delicate passages. Grime and
  fatty soils lift better at the high end; choose the lowest pH that works.
- **Conductivity (ionic strength):** deionised water (near 0 mS/cm) is strongly
  hypotonic to a paint film; it can draw ions and soluble components out and swell the
  surface. A solution close to the surface's own conductivity (isotonic) moves less.

## Measure the surface
1. Cast 2-3% agarose in deionised water, cut a small plug (about 5 mm).
2. Place on the test area 1-3 minutes, then put it on the sensor of a flat pH meter and
   a conductivity meter (Horiba LAQUAtwin-type pocket meters are standard).
3. Record pH and mS/cm for each colour area; values often differ between passages.

## Build the solution
- Choose a buffer with a pKa within about 1 unit of the target pH (Good's buffers and
  others commonly used: MES ~6.1, Bis-Tris ~6.5, HEPES ~7.5, TEA ~7.8, TRIS ~8.1).
- Make up in deionised water, adjust pH with dilute acetic acid or NaOH/KOH, then dilute
  or add salt to bring conductivity close to the measured surface value.
- Add chelators or surfactants only if water alone fails, see
  `creative-arts.conservation-and-restoration.paintings-conservation.chelators-and-surfactants-in-paint-cleaning`.
- The Modular Cleaning Program (Wolbers) organises these as stock solutions so tests can
  be mixed in series; follow the same logic even without the software.

## Test and apply
- Barely damp swab, roll, lift; read the swab. Test in each colour and in cracks.
- Watch for swelling, blanching and pigment on the swab; dark and red lakes, and
  water-sensitive modern oils, often fail first.
- Clear every aqueous treatment, see
  `creative-arts.conservation-and-restoration.paintings-conservation.clearing-residues-after-aqueous-cleaning`.

## Gotchas
- Pocket meters drift: calibrate pH at two points and conductivity with a standard
  before each session.
- Water reaching canvas through cracks can shrink some old linen canvases; keep swabs
  dry and treat cracked areas with gels instead.
- Labels: every bottle gets pH, conductivity, buffer, concentration and date.

## Verify
- [ ] Surface pH/conductivity measured and recorded per area.
- [ ] Solution pH and conductivity measured after mixing, not assumed.
- [ ] Test results recorded; no pigment pickup or swelling.
- [ ] Clearance step done and recorded.
