# Isolating fills before retouching

**Goal:** a barrier between original paint (and fill) and retouching, so retouching can
be removed by the conservator who follows you.

## Why
- Porous fills absorb retouching media: colour sinks, goes matte and becomes hard to remove.
- Retouching laid directly on cleaned original paint bonds to it; later removal risks the
  paint.

## How
1. **Isolate the fill:** a thin coat of dilute resin (Paraloid B-72 or similar, a few
   percent in a solvent safe for the paint), shellac in ethanol on gesso, or the main
   varnish, applied with a small brush to the fill only.
2. **Varnish the painting:** an isolating (first) varnish over the whole cleaned
   surface - commonly a low-molecular-weight resin brushed thin. Retouching goes on top.
3. Let dry fully before retouching.
4. After retouching, apply the final varnish layer (brushed or sprayed).

## Choose the isolation for reversibility
- The isolating layer must be removable in a solvent milder than what would harm the paint.
- Retouching media should be soluble in a solvent that does not dissolve the isolating
  layer too quickly, so you can correct retouching without stripping the varnish.

## Verify
- [ ] Isolating layer applied, recorded (resin, concentration, solvent).
- [ ] Retouching tested removable without disturbing the isolation.
