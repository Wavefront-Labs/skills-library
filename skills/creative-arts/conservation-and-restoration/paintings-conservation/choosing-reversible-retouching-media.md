# Choosing reversible retouching media

**Goal:** a medium that stays removable and stable for decades, applied only to loss, sitting on a protective layer.

## Options
- **Resin-bound conservation colours:** ground pigments in a low-molecular-weight resin (for example Gamblin Conservation Colors, which the manufacturer states use Laropal A81; confirm on the current technical data sheet). Soluble in low-polarity solvents; good depth.
- **Mineral-spirit acrylic colours:** Golden MSA colours use an acrylic resin (Paraloid B-67) in mineral spirits; check the current data sheet.
- **Paraloid B-72** (ethyl methacrylate / methyl acrylate) in acetone or ethanol/acetone mixes: very stable, used for pigment dispersions.
- **Aqueous media:** watercolour, gouache, or Aquazol (poly 2-ethyl-2-oxazoline) as binder; useful as an underlayer; sensitive to future water-based cleaning.

## How to decide
1. Find out what solvents the original paint and varnish tolerate (see `solvent-testing-and-varnish-removal`).
2. Pick a retouching medium that dissolves in a weaker solvent than those the original can bear.
3. Choose pigments for lightfastness; avoid fugitive colours. Prefer inorganic or established lightfast pigments from the manufacturer's rating.
4. Retouch on an isolating varnish layer over the fill (see `isolating-fills-before-retouching`).
5. Build colour with thin layers, aqueous underlayer first if wanted, then resin glazes for depth.
6. Finish with a protective varnish layer compatible with the retouching.

## Cautions
- Manufacturers reformulate; check technical data and safety data sheets each time.
- Do not use a medium that leaves a harder film than the surrounding paint.

## Verify
- [ ] Medium dissolves in a solvent weaker than the original tolerates.
- [ ] Pigment lightfastness checked.
- [ ] Medium and pigments listed in the treatment report.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
