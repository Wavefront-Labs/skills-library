# Strip lining weak tacking margins

**Goal:** give a painting new, strong tacking margins while leaving the rest of the
canvas unlined.

## When
- Tacking edges torn, brittle, cut back, or riddled with old tack holes; canvas otherwise sound.
- Before restretching onto a new or keyed stretcher.

## Materials
- Strips of polyester sailcloth or fine linen, about 5-8 cm wide plus the margin depth,
  cut on the straight grain; fray the inner edge slightly so it does not print a line.
- Heat-seal adhesive: BEVA 371 film or solution is standard; acrylic dispersions (Lascaux
  heat-seal types) are alternatives. Must be reversible without affecting the paint.

## Procedure
1. Remove the painting from its stretcher; flatten the turned-over edges with humidity
   and weights so they lie in plane.
2. Pre-coat strips with adhesive (BEVA film laminated on with a warm iron through
   release paper is quick).
3. Lay the strip so it overlaps the reverse of the painting by about 1-3 cm inside the
   foldover line; the rest extends out as the new margin.
4. Heat-set with a tacking iron or spatula through release film (BEVA about 65 C), working
   along the edge, then cool under weight.
5. Restretch using the new strips; the original margin stays where it was.

## Gotchas
- Too wide an overlap shows as a ridge or a line on the front, especially on thin paintings.
- Strips cut off-grain distort under tension.
- Keep heat away from the image area; wax-resin linings and heat-sensitive paint nearby
  will react.

## Verify
- [ ] No visible line on the front in raking light.
- [ ] Strips firmly bonded along the whole edge.
- [ ] Adhesive, fabric and overlap recorded in the treatment report.
