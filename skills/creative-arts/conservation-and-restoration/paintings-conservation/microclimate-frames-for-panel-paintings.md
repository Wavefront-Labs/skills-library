# Microclimate frames for panel paintings

**Goal:** keep a sensitive panel painting at a stable RH inside its frame when the room
cannot.

## Components
- **Front:** low-reflection laminated glass or acrylic glazing (e.g. anti-reflective
  acrylic), sealed into the rebate.
- **Spacer:** keeps glazing off the paint; seals the front.
- **Back:** a backboard (aluminium composite, polycarbonate) sealed with a barrier foil
  (Marvelseal type) and aluminium tape or gasket.
- **Buffer:** conditioned silica gel or Art Sorb sheet/beads behind the panel, in a cassette
  that can be reconditioned; small mass is enough in a well-sealed package.
- **Logger:** a small RH/T logger visible or retrievable inside the package.

## Target RH
- Usually the RH the panel has been stable at for years (often 45-55%), not an abstract
  standard. Condition the buffer to that RH before sealing.

## Build
1. Measure rebate depth; allow space for panel movement and padding (padded rebate,
   flexible turn-buttons, never screws through the panel).
2. Seal glazing into the frame; check seals.
3. Insert panel; add buffer and logger; seal the back.
4. Test the package: log for a few weeks; RH inside should vary by only a few percent
   while the room swings.

## Gotchas
- Sealed packages trap off-gassing; all materials inside must pass an Oddy test.
- Temperature changes shift RH inside quickly; avoid heat sources and spotlights on the frame.
- Condensation risk if a cold package is moved into a warm humid space; acclimatise sealed.

## Verify
- [ ] Logger data shows stable internal RH relative to room.
- [ ] Materials tested; buffer conditioned and labelled with date.
- [ ] Package details recorded for future opening.
