# Low-pressure and vacuum hot table treatment

**Goal:** apply even, gentle pressure (and if needed heat or humidity) across a whole
painting for consolidation or lining when local spatula work is not enough.

## Tables
- **Vacuum hot table:** heated platen, membrane over the painting, vacuum pulls the
  membrane down. Historic linings used high vacuum and heat and caused many textures to
  be flattened.
- **Low-pressure table (Mehra approach):** a perforated surface with fans giving low
  suction (tens of mbar or less), often used cold or with humidity, so the painting is
  held without crushing.

## Procedure outline
1. Examine and record impasto, texture and earlier linings; take raking light images.
2. Face weak areas if flakes could move.
3. Set up: release films, interleaves (silicone Melinex, Hollytex/Reemay), and a breathable
   membrane if humidity is used.
4. Start with the lowest pressure that holds the painting flat; raise only if needed.
5. Heat (for heat-seal adhesives such as BEVA 371 around 65 C): bring up slowly, hold only
   as long as needed, cool under pressure.
6. Humidity (for planar deformations or water-based adhesives): introduce via a
   humidity chamber or damp blotters beneath a membrane; monitor RH at the painting.

## Failure modes
- **Weave interference:** canvas weave prints into the paint under pressure plus heat.
- **Moating:** paint around impasto pressed down, leaving raised islands with a ring.
- **Flattened impasto and changed gloss.**
- **Overheating:** blistering, wax-resin migration, varnish blanching.
Mitigate with low pressure, soft interleaves, and protecting impasto with cut-out padding.

## Verify
- [ ] Before and after raking light images compared for texture loss.
- [ ] Temperature, pressure, time and humidity logged.
- [ ] Bond checked across the painting by gentle tapping and raking light.
