# Reducing planar distortions in canvas

**Goal:** return a deformed canvas towards plane so the image reads correctly and the
deformation does not lead to cracking.

## Types
- **Bulges and dents:** from impacts, leaning, or objects behind the canvas.
- **Corner draws:** diagonal folds from uneven tension at stretcher corners.
- **Stretcher-bar ridges and cracks:** the canvas pressed against the inner bar edge.
- **Tear-related distortions:** see the tear mending skill.

## Method
1. Check paint stability and consolidate first.
2. Relax the deformation locally: humidity through a Gore-Tex membrane and damp blotter,
   or a small humidity chamber over the reverse; monitor RH (target the minimum that
   relaxes the canvas; check after a few minutes).
3. Apply gentle warmth (spatula through release film) if needed.
4. Flatten with weights over blotter and Melinex, from the reverse, with the painting face
   down on a flat, padded and protected surface; let dry fully under weight.
5. Corner draws: re-tension locally with the stretcher keys or by adjusting tacks along the
   affected edges, then re-key evenly.
6. Stretcher-bar marks: add a bevel or padding strip to the inner bar edge so it no longer
   touches the canvas, then relax as above.

## Failure modes
- Water stain or tide line on the canvas reverse (keep moisture even and limited).
- Shrinkage of old linen when wetted.
- Flattened impasto from weights - pad around high texture.

## Verify
- [ ] Before/after raking light from four directions.
- [ ] RH, time and weights recorded.
- [ ] Deformation does not return after a week on the stretcher.
