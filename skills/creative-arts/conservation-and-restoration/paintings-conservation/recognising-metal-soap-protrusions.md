# Recognising metal soap protrusions and efflorescence

**Goal:** identify metal soap formation early and choose a sound management strategy.

## What they are
Fatty acids in oil binders can react with metal ions from pigments (lead, zinc and others) to form metal soaps. These can aggregate and grow into protrusions, form translucent patches or surface crusts, and appear as efflorescence. See Casadio et al., Metal Soaps in Art: Conservation and Research (Springer, 2019), for the research.

## Recognition
- Small raised pale or translucent bumps, often through dark paint, visible in raking light.
- Whitish bloom or crust on dark passages.
- Increased translucency of lead-white layers, or cracked, uneven surface.
- Confirmation needs sampling and analysis: cross-section, FTIR or similar (see `ftir-spectroscopy-for-conservation`), which a conservator or scientist does.

## Management
1. Document with macro photography and raking light; map locations.
2. Stabilise the environment: avoid raised temperature and high or fluctuating RH; the soap growth is a chemical process.
3. Mechanical removal of protrusions is irreversible and risky; only a paintings conservator should do so, and protrusions may recur.
4. Monitor on a schedule; compare images.

## Verify
- [ ] Locations mapped and photographed.
- [ ] Environmental conditions stable and recorded.
- [ ] Treatment decision recorded with conservator sign-off.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
