# Facing a painting

**Goal:** hold loose paint in place temporarily, then take the facing off without damage.

## When to face
- Before structural treatment (lining, tear mending, panel work) if paint is flaking.
- Before moving a painting with active flaking, or in an emergency salvage.
- Not as a long-term treatment: facings left on for years discolour and are harder to remove.

## Materials
- **Tissue:** thin Japanese tissue (kozo, 5-12 g/m2), lens tissue, or a polyester non-woven
  (Bondina) for stronger facings. Tear (do not cut) edges so they feather.
- **Adhesive (test first):**
  - sturgeon glue or wheat starch paste (aqueous; good grab; avoid on water-sensitive paint);
  - Aquazol, Klucel G, or Paraloid B-72 in solvent (non-aqueous);
  - BEVA 371 (heat-seal, reversible in hydrocarbon);
  - cyclododecane melted or in solution: sublimes away over days to weeks, no removal step,
    but slow and needs ventilation.
- The facing adhesive must be removable with a solvent that the paint and varnish tolerate.

## Procedure
1. Remove loose dust; check solubility of varnish and paint in the facing adhesive's solvent.
2. Lay tissue over the area; brush adhesive through it from the centre outwards so it
   conforms without trapping air.
3. Overlap pieces slightly; work around impasto rather than crushing it.
4. Let dry fully; label the facing (date, adhesive, solvent for removal) on the painting
   record and, if moving, on the packing.

## Removal
- Soften with the appropriate solvent or gentle warmth, lift at a low angle in the
  direction of the crack pattern, and check the tissue for flakes as it comes away.

## Verify
- [ ] Adhesive and removal solvent tested and recorded.
- [ ] Facing adheres everywhere over loose areas (check in raking light).
- [ ] Facing removed after treatment with no paint on the tissue.
