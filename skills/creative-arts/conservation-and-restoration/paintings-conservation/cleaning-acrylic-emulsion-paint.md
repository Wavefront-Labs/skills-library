# Cleaning acrylic emulsion paint

**Goal:** remove soiling from an acrylic emulsion surface without swelling, leaching or burnishing it.

## Why acrylic emulsion is different
- Films are soft at room temperature and attract and embed dirt.
- They contain surfactants and other water-soluble additives that can migrate to the surface; aqueous cleaning can swell the film and extract these. Research on this from conservators at Tate, the Getty and partner institutions (the Modern Paints project and related work) is the basis for current method; read it before treating.
- Solvents that dissolve or swell acrylics (aromatic hydrocarbons, ketones, esters, many alcohols) can damage the paint. Test carefully.

## Staged approach
1. Examine; test for sensitivity in an inconspicuous area with the proposed system.
2. Dry clean first: soft brush with vacuum extraction through screening.
3. If aqueous cleaning is needed, use controlled systems: adjusted pH and conductivity, low volume of water, delivered by gel or tightly controlled swab, and rinsed as needed. The Modular Cleaning Program (Wolbers/Stavroudis) is a recognised framework for choosing components. Added ions (higher conductivity) reduce swelling and extraction in published research; use the data from the sources, not guesswork.
4. Stop at the first sign of colour transfer, tackiness, gloss change or swelling.
5. Allow full drying; examine in raking light for residue or gloss change.

## Cautions
- Outcomes vary by manufacturer, age and pigment; no single recipe is safe.
- Consider consultation with the artist or estate if living.

## Verify
- [ ] Test area unaffected after drying.
- [ ] Gloss and texture unchanged.
- [ ] System, pH, conductivity and contact time recorded.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
