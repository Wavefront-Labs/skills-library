# Restretching and stretcher replacement

**Goal:** hold the canvas at even, moderate tension on a stretcher that does not damage it.

## Keep or replace the stretcher
- Keep the original if sound: it carries inscriptions, labels and history. Repair joints,
  add a bevel to inner edges if they press on the canvas.
- Replace if warped, broken, too small, or a strainer (fixed corners) that cannot be keyed.
- New stretchers: keyable mortise-and-tenon or expansion-bolt corners, bars bevelled on
  the canvas side, cross-bars for large sizes. Spring-tension systems maintain even
  tension through RH changes.
- Record and retain the original stretcher if replaced (with the owner's agreement).

## Stretch
1. Centre the painting; align the weave square to the bars.
2. Fix the middle of each side first, then work outwards, alternating opposite sides
   (top, bottom, left, right), a few tacks or staples at a time, about 5-8 cm apart.
3. Use the old tack holes when possible; stainless steel tacks or staples, or brass, to
   avoid rust.
4. Fold the corners neatly so they do not bulk; do not cut the canvas.
5. Key out gently and evenly; tension should be firm and drum-like but not tight.

## Gotchas
- Over-tension cracks paint and tears weak margins; strip line them first.
- Keys knocked in hard can shift the canvas; tie or secure keys so they cannot fall out
  in transit.
- Canvas tension rises in low RH for some fabrics and falls in others; check after a
  week in the display environment.

## Verify
- [ ] Weave square to the bars; corners neat.
- [ ] Even tension across the surface.
- [ ] Original stretcher and labels documented.
