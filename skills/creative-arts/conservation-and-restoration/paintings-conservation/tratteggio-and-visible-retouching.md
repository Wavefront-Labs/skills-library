# Tratteggio and visible retouching

**Goal:** integrate a loss visually without pretending to be original; the repair stays discernible on close inspection.

## Principle
Cesare Brandi's theory of restoration (Italian Central Institute for Restoration) argues that a loss may be re-integrated so the work reads as a whole, while the intervention remains recognisable and the original untouched. Tratteggio (also rigatino, or selezione cromatica when colours are chosen by selection) applies fine strokes or dots of separate colours that optically mix at normal viewing distance.

## Procedure
1. Fill and level the loss; isolate (see `levelling-and-shaping-fills`, `isolating-fills-before-retouching`).
2. Lay a flat base tone of the dominant colour of the area in a reversible medium.
3. Apply fine vertical hatching or dots in two to four colours mixed to match the surrounding tones; adjust warm/cool to bring the loss to the same value.
4. Keep stroke width consistent and the pattern within the loss only. Strokes must not overlap original paint.
5. Step back to normal viewing distance and check unity; correct by adding strokes of the needed colour.
6. Document the method, so later conservators know what is old repair.

## When it suits
- Large losses in early Italian paintings and where the owner wants honesty of intervention; a sensitive method for hard-to-reconstruct losses.
- The decision between tratteggio and mimetic retouching is an ethical and aesthetic one made with the owner and curator.

## Verify
- [ ] Retouching reads as one at viewing distance, discernible within about arm's length.
- [ ] No strokes over original paint.
- [ ] Method and pigments recorded.

## Professional note
General guidance only, not a treatment recommendation for a specific object. Work on heritage objects needs the judgement of a qualified conservator, working to the AIC Code of Ethics and Guidelines for Practice (or the ECCO / Icon equivalent) and the owner's consent; test, document and prefer the minimum intervention. Check current safety data sheets and local regulations for every material named.
