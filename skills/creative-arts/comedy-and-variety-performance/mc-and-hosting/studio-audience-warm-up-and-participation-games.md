# Studio audience warm-up and participation games

**Goal:** a studio audience that laughs and applauds on cue and stays energised through retakes.

## The role
- A warm-up performer prepares the audience before the recording, keeps them engaged in breaks, and explains what to do (applaud, laugh, stay quiet when told). The producer or floor manager defines what is needed.

## Flow (typical)
1. Welcome and house rules: phones, exits, toilets, what to expect.
2. Short comic crowd work and a few jokes to start laughter.
3. A participation game or two to break the ice.
4. Practice applause and reactions.
5. Introduce the host or talent.
6. Fill every break with short material or a game.

## Participation games
- Simple, quick, low-risk: audience-wide votes, shout-outs, quiz questions, small prizes.
- Choose volunteers who offer themselves; avoid embarrassing people.
- Keep a bank of games ready and test them.

## During a recording
- Keep energy high after retakes; thank them.
- Coordinate with the floor manager for timing.

## Safety and conduct
- Know evacuation routes and the venue's rules; see `evacuation-announcements`.
- Do not use personal or offensive material; follow production rules.

## Gotchas
- Wearing out the audience before the show starts.
- Games that drag.

## Verify
- [ ] The audience reacts on cue and energy holds through breaks.
