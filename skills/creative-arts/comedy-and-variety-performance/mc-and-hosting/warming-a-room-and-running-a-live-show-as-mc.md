# Warming a room and running a live show as MC

**Goal:** make the audience feel like a single room on the performers' side, get every
act on and off cleanly and on time, and keep the evening's energy level from start to finish.

## 1. Before doors
- Get the **running order** with each act's name (pronunciation!), slot length, intro
  preference and any no-go topics.
- Check the **light** system with each act: who signals, at what time remaining.
- Sound check the mic; know where the stand goes and how to reset it.
- Know the logistics you'll announce: interval length, bar, toilets, fire exits,
  raffle, next show.
- Walk the room; note who's in (birthday tables, big groups, front row).

## 2. Opening the show (often 5–10 min in comedy clubs)
1. **Energy first:** a big, confident walk-on, a warm greeting, applause practice ("let's
   hear it for…").
2. **Talk to the room:** light crowd work to make the audience feel seen and connect the
   tables to each other. Choose friendly, low-risk targets.
3. **House rules:** phones away, no talking/heckling, how to react. Keep it funny.
4. Some prepared material is fine, but the MC's job is the room, not their own set.
5. Bring the first act on when the room is laughing, not after it's settled.

## 3. Introducing acts
- **Hype, information, name — name last and loud.** "Our next act has been on [credible credit],
  and is one of my favourite comics on the circuit: please welcome… NAME!"
- Use the intro the act provided; don't do their jokes or reveal their premise.
- Start the applause, shake hands or swap at the mic stand cleanly, leave the stage.
- **Bringing them off:** lead the applause, repeat their name, step up only when
  they've left the mic.

## 4. Between acts and segues
- A short (often 1–3 min) spot to reset the room: comment on what happened, check in
  with earlier crowd-work people, then introduce the next act.
- Change the energy deliberately: lift it after a quiet act, settle it after a wild one.
- Don't eat other acts' time; your total stage time comes out of the evening.

## 5. When an act dies
- Come on with **more** energy, not less; acknowledge lightly if appropriate (never
  mock the act — they're your colleague), get a quick win with the room (applause,
  shared joke, crowd work), then introduce the next act with extra hype.
- If there's a rowdy table, deal with it between acts so the next act doesn't inherit it;
  involve venue staff if needed.

## 6. Time-keeping
- Track time with a visible clock or timer. Flash the light at the agreed point.
- If the show runs long, cut your own bits first.
- Announce the interval with a clear time and a reason to come back.

## 7. Closing
Thank the acts by name, thank the venue and staff, plug the next show, and get
people to the bar or the exits on a high. End crisply.

## Gotchas
- Forgetting or mispronouncing a name — write them large on your card.
- Doing your best material at the top and leaving the room expecting you, not the acts.
- Ignoring safety announcements (exits) in larger venues.
- Making the warm-up crowd work cruel; the room tenses for the whole night.

## Verify
- [ ] Running order with pronunciations, slot lengths and light times in hand.
- [ ] Room laughing and on side before the first act.
- [ ] Every act introduced with the name last and the applause led on and off.
- [ ] The show finished within its time; your own time was cut first if needed.
- [ ] Closing thanked acts/venue and plugged the next show.
