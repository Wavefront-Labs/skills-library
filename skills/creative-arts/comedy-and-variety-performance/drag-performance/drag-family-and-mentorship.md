# Drag family and mentorship

**Goal:** find and give support in a community where much craft is passed down person to person.

## What the tradition is
In many drag scenes, performers form "families" or "houses": a drag mother (or parent or mentor) teaches and supports newer performers, who may take the family name. Structures and terms differ between ballroom houses, pageant systems and local bar scenes, so ask rather than assume.

## Finding a mentor
- Go to shows, tip, and be a good audience member; get to know performers and hosts.
- Ask for advice on a specific topic rather than "teach me everything".
- Offer help: carry bags, run errands, help backstage.

## What mentors commonly teach
- Makeup, wigs, costume, number structure, stagecraft, booking, and scene etiquette.
- Where to buy and how to sew.

## Being a good drag child or sibling
- Be reliable, attend, and credit your teachers.
- Do not copy another performer's number or look without permission.
- Respect boundaries; a family is about mutual care.

## Gotchas
- Giving or accepting exploitative arrangements around money or booking.
- Treating the family as a shortcut to bookings.
