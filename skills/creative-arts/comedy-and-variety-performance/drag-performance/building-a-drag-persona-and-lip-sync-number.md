# Building a drag persona and a lip-sync number

**Goal:** create a drag character that reads clearly from the back of a bar, and a
lip-sync number that is precise, funny or moving, and safe to perform in heels on a
small, crowded stage.

## 1. Persona
- **Point of view first, look second.** Who is this character, what do they want from
  the audience, what's their sense of humour? Glamour, comedy, camp, horror, punk,
  "kings" playing masculinity — each needs a clear attitude.
- **Name:** memorable, sayable by a host over a bad mic, searchable online.
- **Signature elements:** a silhouette, colour, catchphrase or entrance the audience
  remembers.
- Persona can evolve; early gigs are where you find what works.

## 2. Preparing a lip-sync number
1. **Choose and edit the track.** Most bar slots want 3–4 minutes; many numbers are
   edits or mashups (song + spoken clips + sound effects). Cut long intros; build to a peak.
   Bring the file in the format the venue asks for, plus a backup on a phone/USB.
   *Music rights for performance are usually covered by the venue's licence; posting
   video of the number online is a separate question.*
2. **Learn the lyrics cold** — every word, breath and ad-lib. Mouth the consonants;
   audiences notice wrong words and late starts more than anything else.
3. **Map the beats:** mark the song structure (verse, chorus, bridge, final chorus,
   key change) and decide where each big moment goes: the reveal, the dip, the stunt,
   the audience moment.
4. **Act the song:** the face sells it. Decide the story of the number: are you
   heartbroken, triumphant, ridiculous?
5. **Rehearse in full costume and shoes** on a surface like the venue's.

## 3. Reveals and quick changes
- Tear-away panels (velcro/snaps), pull-strings, layered garments, wig-under-wig.
- Rehearse the reveal ~20 times; a stuck reveal kills the moment.
- Assign a stagehand or friend for off-stage changes; agree what they hand you and when.

## 4. Working the room
- Bar stages are tiny; numbers travel into the crowd. Walk the space before the show.
- **Tips:** accept graciously, keep the number going, hand cash to a helper or tuck it
  safely; thank the tipper with a moment of eye contact.
- Consent works both ways: don't drag unwilling patrons into the number; set
  boundaries with audience members who grab.

## 5. Hosting (brief)
Drag hosts run the evening: open with energy, introduce acts by name clearly,
keep the room tipping and the running order on time. See
creative-arts.comedy-and-variety-performance.mc-and-hosting.warming-a-room-and-running-a-live-show-as-mc
for the hosting craft.

## 6. Safety
- **Heels:** break them in; check heel tips and ankle straps; know the stage edge.
- **Stunts** (death drops, splits, cartwheels): learn from someone qualified and
  progress on mats; a death drop lands on the back of the shoulders and upper back
  with chin tucked, never the head or the tailbone. Do not do them on a wet floor or
  in a costume you haven't fallen in.
- **Tucking, binding and padding:** limit time, take breaks, stop for pain, numbness or
  breathing difficulty. *Specific safe durations vary by method and body; look for
  guidance from experienced performers and health sources.*
- Hydrate; wigs and padding cause overheating.
- Glue, lashes and makeup: patch-test new products.

## Gotchas
- Choosing a song you love but can't mouth (fast rap, unfamiliar language).
- Saving all the energy for the end; the first 20 seconds decide whether the crowd tips.
- Forgetting the track has a long fade; edit a clean ending.
- Wig not secured for a spin or dip.

## Verify
- [ ] You can describe the persona's point of view in one line.
- [ ] Track edited, timed, and backed up; lyrics word-perfect.
- [ ] Beat map with reveal and peak moments rehearsed in costume.
- [ ] Stunts practised on mats; shoes and wig secured.
- [ ] Binding/tucking breaks planned for long nights.
