# The non-hero stance

**Goal:** use comic failure as the engine of the act.

## The idea
In Steve Kaplan's The Hidden Tools of Comedy, a comic protagonist is a "non-hero": someone who wants something and fails, often because of a flaw, so that the audience can laugh from a position of safety. In stand-up it translates to a persona who is not the winner of the stories being told.

## Applying
1. In each story, put the persona in a position where they lose status, money, dignity or an argument.
2. Let the persona be wrong about something they are confident about; the audience sees it first.
3. The persona recovers through jokes, not through victory.
4. Keep the stakes small and human so the audience stays with you.

## Cautions
- A persona who only loses becomes sad; balance with moments of small, earned winning.
- Do not use the stance to make others the villains; the audience has to like you.

## Test
- Take one bit where you were right and rewrite it with you partially wrong or foolish. Compare the laugh on a recording.

## Gotchas
- Fishing for sympathy rather than laughs.

## Verify
- [ ] Each bit has a moment where the persona's plan fails.
