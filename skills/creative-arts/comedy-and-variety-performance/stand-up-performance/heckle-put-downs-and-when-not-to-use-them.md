# Heckle put-downs and when not to use them

**Goal:** a short, funny reply that ends the interruption and leaves the room with you.

## Principles
- A put-down is a joke at the heckler's expense; it works only if the room agrees they deserve it.
- Keep the reply short; end on the punch word.
- Aim at the interruption (the timing, the logic), not the person's body or identity.

## Building stock lines
1. Write 10-15 replies for common heckles (a shouted "tell a joke", shouting the punchline, a drunk comment).
2. Test them in rehearsal and at open spots.
3. Keep your best three that you can adapt to the moment.

## When not to use
- When the heckler is friendly or just enthusiastic.
- When the person is clearly vulnerable (very drunk, upset, a guest of the host).
- When the room is not with you; a put-down then makes you look mean.
- When a reply would escalate a hostile person.

## After
Return to material at once. Do not linger on the win.

## Gotchas
- Using a put-down you wrote for a different room; it may not fit the situation.
