# Status in stand-up

**Goal:** choose and shift your status deliberately.

## Concepts
- Status is your position relative to others in the room (and the subjects you talk about). The comic may play higher (confident, superior), lower (anxious, victim), or shift.
- Comedy often comes from a gap between claimed and actual status: the proud comic who is shown to be foolish, or the self-deprecating comic who reveals a surprising confidence.
- Kaplan's framing for comedy performers is the "non-hero": a protagonist who fails to be the winner. See `the-non-hero-stance`.

## Practising
1. Pick a bit and perform it three ways: high-status, low-status, then mixed (starts high, collapses).
2. Record all three. Which one earns the biggest laughs and fits the persona?
3. Mark the turning point where status flips; make the flip happen at the punch.

## Reading the room
- Early in a set a high-status stance without credit can alienate; a rapid, charming drop of status can win the room.
- Heavily heckled? Hold status calm rather than scrambling.

## Gotchas
- Persistent low status can feel needy; persistent high status feels arrogant. Vary within the set.

## Verify
- [ ] You can name the status of each bit and where it flips.
