# Mic distance, cord and plosives

**Goal:** a consistent, clean voice and no tripping.

## Distance
- Hold the mic roughly a fist's width from the mouth, aimed at the lips, and keep that distance. Typical dynamic stage mics lose volume and add boominess when distance changes; learn your venue's mic.
- For a bigger moment, speak louder rather than moving the mic away; for quiet lines, move closer slightly.
- Do not cup the mic head; it causes feedback.

## Plosives
- P and B bursts hit the mic as pops. Angle the mic slightly below or to the side of the mouth so the airstream passes it. A foam windscreen helps.
- Check the pops during sound check.

## Cord
1. Find where the cable is before you move; keep it on the side of the hand that holds it.
2. Move with the cord slack behind you, not wrapped round your wrist.
3. Collect excess in the off hand without looking at it.
4. If the cord catches, step over it deliberately; do not tug.
- Cordless mics avoid the issue but carry battery and signal risks; test them and know who can swap.

## Feedback
If the mic squeals, stop moving towards the monitors, lower the mic, and let the sound person adjust.

## Gotchas
- Moving the mic hand during act-outs; see `space-work-and-mime-in-stand-up`.

## Verify
- [ ] Sound check includes a loud line, a quiet line and a P-heavy phrase.
