# Stand-up delivery, crowd work and heckler handling

**Goal:** perform written material so it gets the laughs it deserves, handle the room
as it actually is tonight, and deal with interruptions without losing the audience.

## 1. Mic and stand (the first ten seconds)
- Walk on during the applause; take the mic out of the stand, move the stand **behind
  you** to one side so nothing is between you and the room (unless you use it as a prop).
- Hold the mic about a fist's width from the mouth, pointing at your chin, consistent
  distance. Pulling it away on big moments makes you disappear, not louder.
- Look at the room, take a breath, then start. Don't start talking while still adjusting.
- Mind the cable: step over it, never let it wrap.

## 2. Timing
- **Pause before the punchline** (a beat) and **after it** (let the laugh peak and begin
  to fall before you continue). Stepping on laughs is the most common beginner error.
- Ride a big laugh with a tag rather than moving on.
- Vary pace; a monotone rush flattens good jokes.
- Mark the stresses on difficult jokes in rehearsal: which word carries the punch.

## 3. Persona
- The audience needs to know **who's talking** — the comic's attitude, status and point of
  view — within the opener. A clear persona makes premises believable.
- Persona is an amplified version of you, not a costume; inconsistent personas confuse rooms.

## 4. Crowd work
- Ask **closed, easy questions** with a known comedic destination: "What do you do?"
  "Where are you from?" "How long have you two been together?"
- Have **prepared lines** for common answers (jobs, towns, couples) — much "improvised"
  crowd work is prepared.
- Listen to the answer; the funniest line is often just repeating it back with attitude.
- Punch toward the situation, not at vulnerabilities (bodies, disabilities, someone who's
  clearly uncomfortable). The room turns on a bully fast.
- Return to material with a bridge line. Crowd work is seasoning, not the meal, in a short set.

## 5. Hecklers: a graded ladder
1. **Ignore** a single mumbled comment if most of the room didn't hear.
2. **Acknowledge** lightly: repeat it so the room hears, give a gentle line, move on.
3. **Rebuke with a laugh:** a sharper line that wins the room to your side. The room
   must agree you're right.
4. **Involve the room / host:** "Do we want to hear from this table or the show?"
5. **Ask the venue/host to handle it.** Security is the club's job, not yours.
Rules: stay calm and in control (you have the mic); never get physical; never punch
down at a drunk who's clearly in distress. Have a few stock heckler lines ready, but
specific beats generic.

## 6. Reading the room and bombing
- Before going on: watch the previous acts. What's landing? How drunk, how big, how close?
- Adjust: tighter, faster material for loud rooms; slower, wordier stuff for attentive ones.
- **When it's dying:** acknowledge it honestly and lightly (it's often your biggest laugh),
  go to your strongest material, and shorten. Don't blame the audience.
- Afterwards, watch the recording before deciding what went wrong.

## 7. Time and the light
Hosts flash a light (or phone torch) when you have a minute (sometimes two) left. Finish with
your closer — don't start a new bit. Running over is the fastest way not to be rebooked.

## Gotchas
- Drinking to calm nerves before a set; timing goes first.
- Facing one side of the room only.
- Apologising in advance ("this is new, bear with me").
- Arguing with a heckler for minutes; win quickly or move on.

## Verify
- [ ] Stand moved, mic held at a consistent distance, opener delivered after a breath.
- [ ] You paused into and out of punchlines (check the recording).
- [ ] Crowd-work questions had prepared destinations and you returned to material.
- [ ] Any heckler handled at the lowest rung that worked.
- [ ] You came off on time after the light.
