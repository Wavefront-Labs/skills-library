# Second-language and international crowds

**Goal:** be understood without dumbing down the comedy.

## Language
- Slow slightly, enunciate and keep sentences short.
- Avoid idioms, local slang and wordplay that relies on pronunciation; see `wordplay-and-puns-in-stand-up`.
- Put the key word at the end of the sentence; it is easier to catch.
- Repeat essential setup words once.

## Content
- Choose universal topics: family, food, travel, work, relationships.
- Replace local references with ones the audience shares or explain them briefly.
- Use physical comedy and acting-out; see `character-voices-inside-a-stand-up-set`.

## Process
1. Ask the promoter about the audience's English level and cultural expectations.
2. Test the set on a local friend.
3. Avoid taboo subjects without local advice; norms around religion, politics and sex vary.

## During
- Watch for faces that look lost; slow down and add detail.
- Do not mock the audience's English.

## Gotchas
- Laughing at a joke yourself to signal it was funny.

## Verify
- [ ] A non-native friend can follow the whole set.
