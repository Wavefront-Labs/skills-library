# Movement and stillness on stage

**Goal:** make every movement mean something.

## Principles
- Stand still for the punch: stillness focuses attention on the line and the pause.
- Move on transitions between bits: a step to the side marks a new chunk.
- Face the whole room, shifting your attention rather than your feet.

## Fixing habits
1. Record your set on video from the front.
2. Count nervous movements: swaying, pacing, hand in pocket, mic juggling, shuffling.
3. Replace one habit at a time with a stable stance, feet roughly shoulder width, weight even.
4. Practise standing still through an entire bit.

## Using the space
- A small club stage lets you take one or two steps; a large stage may allow a deliberate walk for a story bit.
- Take care with edge-of-stage distance and lighting; check the stage in the sound check.
- With a stand, decide: stand-in-front, stand-as-prop, or stand-away; see `delivery-crowd-work-and-heckler-handling` for mic and stand basics.

## Gotchas
- Pacing as a nerve outlet: audiences read it as anxiety.
- Pointing at people; it can feel aggressive.

- [ ] On video, punches land from a still position and movements are on purpose.
