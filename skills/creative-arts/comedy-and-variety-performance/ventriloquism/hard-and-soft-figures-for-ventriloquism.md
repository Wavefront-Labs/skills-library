# Hard and soft figures

**Goal:** a figure that suits your act, your hands and your budget.

## Hard figures
- Traditional figures with a carved wood, fibreglass, or resin head and a control stick for the mouth, with optional lever controls for eyes, brows and more.
- Pros: durable, expressive, strong stage presence. Cons: weight, cost, and transport needs.
- They are made by specialist figure makers; Vent Haven Museum (https://www.venthaven.org) is a resource for the history and field.

## Soft puppets
- Cloth or foam figures operated with the hand in the head, with a movable mouth.
- Pros: lighter, cheaper, good for children's acts. Cons: fewer controls, and less rigid.

## Choosing
1. Decide your style: a classic dummy, a character puppet, or a children's friend.
2. Try figures in your hands; check the weight and the control.
3. Compare repair and maintenance options.
4. Budget for a case.

## Customising
- Hair, eyes, and costume personalise the figure.
- Avoid cutting or altering a collectible figure without advice.

## Gotchas
- A figure too heavy to hold for a full show.
- Buying a figure that cannot be repaired.

## Verify
- [ ] You can hold the figure comfortably for the length of your act.
