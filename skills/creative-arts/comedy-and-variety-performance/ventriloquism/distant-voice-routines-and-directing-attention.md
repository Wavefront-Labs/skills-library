# Distant-voice routines and directing attention

**Goal:** a short routine in which the audience believes a sound comes from elsewhere.

## Directing attention
- The audience believes the sound is where they look. Look at the supposed source, react, and gesture toward it.
- Use your own reactions: surprise, curiosity.
- Place the source object near where you are looking, such as a trunk, a cabinet or a door.
- Do not look at the audience while the voice speaks.

## Routine design
1. Setup: a prop is established (a box, a door).
2. The sound: a voice from inside. Use short phrases at first.
3. Dialogue: you respond to the voice, which has a clear character (a trapped person, a child, a ghost).
4. A joke or twist, and a final line.
5. Pacing: short exchanges so the illusion is not stretched.

## Timing
- Pause so the audience hears the voice; respond a half-second late as if hearing.

## Practice
- Film and watch whether attention is correctly directed.
- Test the sound in the venue.

## Gotchas
- Looking at the prop but speaking toward the audience.
- A routine too long for the strength of the illusion.

- [ ] Observers say they thought the voice was from the prop.
