# Parody sketches

**Goal:** a sketch that the audience enjoys even if they do not know the target well.

## Method
1. Choose a target whose style the audience recognises (a genre of programme, a type of ad, a famous format).
2. List its conventions: how it looks, sounds, who appears, the phrases it uses.
3. Find the assumption or flaw the conventions hide (it takes itself seriously, it is manipulative, it is absurdly formulaic).
4. Write a game from that flaw: apply the conventions to a situation where they do not fit, or push one convention to an extreme.
5. Keep the sketch about an idea, not just a reference; see `finding-and-heightening-the-game-of-the-sketch`.

## Craft
- Specific details of the form (camera moves, vocabulary) sell the parody.
- Keep one clear game; parody tends to turn into a list of references.
- Check age: a target that is stale or obscure will land only with a niche.

## Legal
Parody is generally treated differently from copying but laws vary by country; avoid using protected material beyond what the joke needs. See `parody-and-copyright`.

## Gotchas
- Parody that merely repeats the target without a point of view.

## Verify
- [ ] The sketch works for someone who has not seen the original.
