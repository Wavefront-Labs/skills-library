# Parody and copyright

**Goal:** make informed decisions about using a song's tune or lyrics; this is not legal advice.

## Key points
- Laws differ by country. In the United States, fair use is assessed case by case; in the UK, there is a fair dealing exception for parody, caricature and pastiche. Check the law where you perform and publish. I am describing general principles, not guaranteeing outcomes.
- Calling it parody does not guarantee protection.
- Factors that tend to matter: whether the new work comments on or mocks the original, how much was taken, whether the use competes with the original.
- Live performance in a licensed venue is separate from recording and publishing online.

## Practical steps
1. Decide whether your piece comments on the original or only borrows its tune.
2. Take only what is needed.
3. For recorded or published versions, consider asking the rights owner or seeking advice.
4. Check venue and platform policies, which may be stricter.
5. Keep a record of your reasoning.

## Original alternatives
- Write a new tune in a similar style; see `comedy-song-structure`.

## Gotchas
- Assuming a parody is safe because it is funny.
