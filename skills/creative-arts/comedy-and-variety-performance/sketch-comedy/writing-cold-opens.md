# Writing cold opens

**Goal:** a strong start that sets the show's tone.

## What it is
A scene or sketch at the very start of a show, before the opening titles or the main material. In sketch and topical shows it often sets up a theme or a timely joke.

## Craft
1. Get to the premise immediately; no setup beyond a sentence or two.
2. Use a strong, distinct game that is easy to follow even for latecomers.
3. Keep it short; it should be among the tightest sketches in the show.
4. Place the best joke early; viewers decide whether to stay.
5. Hand off to the show: many cold opens end on a button that leads into the title or the host.

## Topical cold opens
- Fast turn-around: pick a current event with a clear angle; see `shelf-life` considerations in `writing-topical-monologue-jokes`.
- Check legal and taste issues with the producers.

## Live shows
- Plan staging and set changes so the first sketch is simple to start and finish.

## Gotchas
- Opening with a slow premise; see `finding-and-heightening-the-game-of-the-sketch`.
- Repeating the same formula every week without refreshing.

## Verify
- [ ] The cold open is understood and funny within its first 20 seconds.
