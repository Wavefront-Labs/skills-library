# Comic slaps and knaps

**Goal:** a slap that reads as real and harms nobody.

## Principles
- Learn from a qualified stage-combat or physical-theatre instructor; slaps go wrong when taught by video alone.
- The audience sees the attacker's hand and the victim's reaction; the contact is staged so there is no real impact to the face.
- A **knap** is the sound added by the performers: commonly the victim claps or strikes a hand against their own body or the attacker's hand makes the noise on another surface. The sound is timed with the reaction.

## Process (outline)
1. Agree the choreography: who, which side, distance, and speed.
2. Sight-lines: the audience must see the hand's path.
3. The victim takes the cue from the attacker; the reaction (head turn, sound) sells it.
4. Practise slowly, then at speed, with a stage-combat teacher watching.

## Safety
- Agree words to stop.
- Do not perform with someone you do not trust.
- Do not slap to the real contact point on the face.

## Gotchas
- A slap performed too fast in a first rehearsal.
- A mis-timed sound that reveals the trick.

## Verify
- [ ] An instructor has checked the sequence and both performers feel safe.
