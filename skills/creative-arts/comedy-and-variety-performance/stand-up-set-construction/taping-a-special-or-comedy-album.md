# Taping a special or comedy album

**Goal:** capture the best version of a polished hour, once or across several nights.

## Readiness
- Do not tape until the hour is set; tape at the end of a tour or run of previews, when timings and wording are stable. See `previews-and-work-in-progress-shows`.
- Decide the end product: a video special (camera, lighting, editing) or an audio album.

## Setting
- A real audience in a suitable room. Capacity, acoustics (comedy needs dry rooms with tight laughter) and sightlines matter. Many tapings use two or more shows so the best performance can be selected.
- Fill the room: sparse seating kills laughter on tape. Papering (giving away tickets) is a common solution.

## Capture
- Audio: a feed from the board plus ambient audience mics so laughs sound natural.
- Video: multiple cameras, one wide, at least one on the comic; plan camera positions so they do not interrupt the audience.
- Plan editing: laughs can be trimmed in post, but you cannot create energy that was not there.

## Performance
- Treat the taping like a normal show, not a recital. Calm warm-up with a host helps.
- A short pickup session may be used to re-record fluffed lines; plan for it.

## Rights and release
- Clear audience release signage or ticket terms, venue agreement, music clearances and distributor contracts before the taping. Seek a lawyer or agent for terms; I am not confirming standard clauses.

## Gotchas
- Chopping the closing minutes for time constraints after the fact.

## Verify
- [ ] Release and clearance paperwork complete before recording.
