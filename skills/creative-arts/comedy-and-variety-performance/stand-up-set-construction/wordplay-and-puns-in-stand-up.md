# Wordplay and puns in stand-up

## Sources of wordplay
- **Homonyms / homophones:** one sound, two meanings (the connector is the word itself).
- **Idioms taken literally:** "break a leg", "cost an arm and a leg" -> act out the literal.
- **Hidden words and compound splits:** "therapist / the rapist" style splits — use carefully.
- **Malapropism in character:** a character misusing words reveals them.
- **Brand/jargon repurposing:** corporate phrases reinterpreted literally.

## Making a pun land
1. Bury the pun word at the very end (punch word last).
2. Make the setup sound like a normal sentence — the pun must be a surprise, not telegraphed.
3. Give the pun a second level: it should also be true or revealing about the speaker.
4. Keep them short; puns live in one-liner rhythm.

## Groan management
- A groan is a laugh at the joke's shamelessness; acknowledge it ("Yeah, I'm proud of that one")
  and you can get a second, bigger laugh.
- Several puns in a row build a persona (pun comics like Tim Vine) — commit fully or not at all.

## Gotchas
- Puns that only work written (spelling-dependent) die out loud.
- Accent and dialect: a homophone in your accent may not be one for the room.

## Verify
- [ ] Each pun survives being said aloud to someone who has not read it.
- [ ] Groans followed by a planned save line on tape.
