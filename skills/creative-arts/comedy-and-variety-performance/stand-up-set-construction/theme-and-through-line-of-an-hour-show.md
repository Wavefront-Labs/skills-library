# Theme and through-line of an hour show

**Goal:** an hour that feels like one show rather than six tens.

## Finding the through-line
- State it as a question or a stake the comic is trying to resolve ("Why can I not leave the house on time?"), not a topic ("Punctuality").
- The best through-lines are personal, specific and still open at the start of the show.
- Test: each bit either advances the question, complicates it, or deepens the persona's view of it. A bit that does none gets cut or becomes a standalone tangent.

## Method
1. Pull out your 60-90 best minutes of material and group by subject.
2. Look for what the groups share about you (a fear, a habit, a belief).
3. Write the through-line sentence; draft the final-minute resolution.
4. Re-order so the audience's understanding of it develops, not the order you wrote it in; see `hour-structure-and-emotional-turn`.
5. Add connective tissue: runner phrases, callbacks, a physical or audio motif; see `running-gags-and-runners`.

## Gotchas
- A through-line imposed on unrelated material: audiences feel the stretch. Cut the material, not the idea.
- Making the theme too serious early; earn it with laughs.

## Verify
- [ ] Someone who saw the show can state the through-line in a sentence.
- [ ] The opener and the closer both touch it.
