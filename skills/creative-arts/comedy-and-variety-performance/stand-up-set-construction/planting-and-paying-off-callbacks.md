# Planting and paying off callbacks

## Choosing the plant
- A specific, vivid image or phrase from a bit that laughed (not a throwaway).
- Ideally ambiguous, so a later context can reinterpret it.
- Some plants are written to get only a small laugh early, purely to set up the payoff.

## Spacing
- At least two bits between plant and payoff; the audience should have "forgotten" it.
- In a five-minute set: plant in minute 1, pay off in the closer.

## Wording the payoff
- Use the plant's exact key words — paraphrasing loses recognition.
- The payoff should be its own joke: new context plus the old phrase as the punch word.
- Do not explain the connection.

## Closing on a callback
- Gives the set a sense of shape and earns an applause break.
- Rehearse the closer twice: with the callback, and a standalone closer as backup if the
  plant died (if the plant got nothing, the callback will get nothing).

## Gotchas
- Too many callbacks feels self-referential; one or two per short set.
- In crowd-heavy shows, callbacks to audience moments often beat written ones (see crowd callbacks).

## Verify
- [ ] Payoff laugh larger than plant laugh in three consecutive recordings.
- [ ] Backup closer rehearsed and ready.
