# Joke prospecting from assumption lists

**Goal:** write a setup and then systematically find punchlines instead of waiting for inspiration.

## Steps
1. **Write a setup** — a true statement from a premise. E.g. "My doctor told me to cut down on red meat."
2. **List the assumptions** the audience makes about it. Aim for 10+:
   - The doctor is a medical doctor.
   - "Cut down" means eat less.
   - Red meat = beef/lamb.
   - I want to be healthy.
   - The doctor is giving advice, not instructions to butcher.
3. **Pick one as the target assumption.** Prefer assumptions that are strongly held and
   that, broken, say something about the speaker.
4. **Find the connector:** the word in the setup that supports the target assumption
   ("cut down", "red meat").
5. **Brainstorm reinterpretations** of that connector — 10 at least — without judging.
   - "cut down" literally: "So I've stopped using a knife."
   - "red meat" reinterpreted: "So now I only eat it when it's still pink."
6. **Write each as a punch**, punch word last, then pick the strongest.

## Tips
- Prospect for 20 minutes per setup; most hits come after the obvious first five.
- Keep the assumption list: the next tag often comes from a different assumption on the same setup.

## Gotchas
- Choosing a weak, obscure assumption gives a clever joke nobody laughs at.
- Reinterpretations that require explaining are not ready.

## Verify
- [ ] For a test setup: 10 assumptions, 3 targets tried, 15 reinterpretations, 2 punches tested live.
