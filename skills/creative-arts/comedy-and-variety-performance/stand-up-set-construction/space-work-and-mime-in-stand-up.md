# Space work and mime in stand-up

**Goal:** make an invisible object or place real enough to carry a joke.

## Rules
1. **Define it once**: size, height, weight and position, shown with the whole body, then never change it. Where your hand touched the door is where the door is.
2. **Show weight and resistance**: lift a heavy thing with your knees, push a stuck door with the shoulder.
3. **Few objects, big signals**: one clear mimed object outperforms a cluttered stage.
4. **Eyeline sells it**: look at the object's top, then its handle, then through it.
5. Let the audience supply detail. Name the object only if the mime is ambiguous.

## Using it in a bit
- Make the physical action the punch or the tag, not the setup. A silent beat of struggle can earn a laugh by itself.
- Keep the mic and the cord out of the space (see `cord-management-on-stage`): moving the mic hand mid-mime breaks the illusion.
- Rehearse on camera; what feels clear from inside often reads as vague.

## Gotchas
- Forgetting a mimed prop mid-bit and walking through it.
- Mime that needs a lot of room in a low, narrow club stage.

## Verify
- [ ] Muted video of the bit still shows the object.
- [ ] The object stays in the same place every time.
