# Tightening a joke: punch word last, fewest words

**Goal:** the same joke, fewer words, bigger laugh, laugh arriving exactly when you stop talking.

## 1. Find the punch word
The punch word is the single word that reveals the reinterpretation. Everything after it
is dead air that competes with the laugh.
- Wrong: "My dad was so cheap he'd reuse teabags, and he was proud of it."
- Right: "My dad was proud of a lot of things. Mainly reusing teabags."
Ask: at which exact word does the listener "get it"? Move that word to the end.

## 2. Reorder, do not just cut
- Move qualifiers ("actually", "yesterday", place names) into the setup.
- Turn trailing clauses into separate sentences in the setup.
- If a tag follows, it is fine: the tag gets its own punch word at its own end.

## 3. Cut words (economy pass)
Go word by word through the setup and remove anything that does not:
- force the target assumption,
- establish the speaker's attitude, or
- give a specific image that pays off in the punch.
Common fat: "So, um, like", "I was thinking the other day", repeated subjects,
adjectives that do not feed the joke. Specific nouns beat general ones ("a Toyota Corolla"
beats "a car") only when the specificity is part of the picture.

## 4. Test aloud
- Say both versions out loud with a timer. A setup should usually run under 15 seconds
  for a single joke.
- Record at a mic. Note the laugh start: if the laugh starts *before* your last word,
  your punch word is not last.

## Gotchas
- Cutting attitude makes a joke shorter and flatter. Keep the words that tell us how
  you feel.
- Rhythm matters: a three-beat setup sometimes needs a filler syllable to land on the beat.
- Do not tighten on paper only — what reads well can sound clipped.

## Verify
- [ ] Every punch in the set ends on its reveal word.
- [ ] Set time dropped after the pass while laugh count held or rose (recording compared).
