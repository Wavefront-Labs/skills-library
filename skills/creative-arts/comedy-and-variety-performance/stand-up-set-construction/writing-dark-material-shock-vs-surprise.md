# Dark material: shock versus surprise

## Shock vs surprise
- **Shock** comes from the subject alone (a taboo word or topic). It gets a gasp; gasps are not laughs.
- **Surprise** comes from structure: an unexpected reinterpretation. A dark joke works when the
  twist is clever enough that the room laughs at the construction.
Test: replace the taboo subject with a neutral one. If no joke remains, it is shock only.

## Earning the joke
- Build licence earlier in the set: establish persona and that you are in control.
- Put the darkest material after the room trusts you (middle to late), never as the opener.
- Personal connection (your own illness, your own family's tragedy) grants licence.
- Target the absurdity around the dark thing, not the victims.

## Framing
- Acknowledge the line: a knowing look or "I know" can convert a gasp into a laugh.
- Have the next joke ready immediately — momentum carries rooms over a dark line.

## Recovering after offence
1. Do not double down angrily or lecture the room.
2. Acknowledge with a self-aware line, then move to a reliable bit.
3. Afterwards, decide: was it the structure, the room, or the placement?

## Gotchas
- Dark material in a corporate or family room breaks the booking; check the brief.
- Filmed clips lose the context that earned the joke.

## Verify
- [ ] Neutral-subject test passed for each dark joke; gasp-to-laugh ratio tracked on tape.
