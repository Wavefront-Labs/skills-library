# Writing one-liners

**Goal:** jokes that set up and pay off in one or two sentences, under about 10 seconds.

## Structures
- **Misdirect:** the setup leads to an obvious picture; the last words switch it.
  "I haven't spoken to my wife in years. I didn't want to interrupt her." (Rodney Dangerfield style)
- **Paraprosdokian:** the end of the sentence reframes the beginning. "I want to die
  peacefully in my sleep like my grandfather, not screaming like his passengers." (often attributed to Bob Monkhouse)
- **Literal idiom:** an expression taken literally.
- **Definition / list twist:** three items, the third breaks the pattern.

## Procedure
1. Pick a premise. Write 20 short true statements about it.
2. For each, find the word that could be reinterpreted (connector) and write a twist.
3. Cut every non-essential word; punch word last.
4. Read aloud: if it needs a breath in the middle, it is too long.

## Delivery note
One-liner acts depend on rhythm; keep your delivery uniform so the twist is the only surprise.

## Gotchas
- A run of one-liners with no persona feels like a joke book; add attitude or a recurring character.
- Famous one-liners circulate online — check yours is not unconsciously remembered.

## Verify
- [ ] 20 one-liners under 10 seconds each; 5 surviving three open mics.
