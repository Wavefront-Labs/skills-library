# The mix: switching between narrator and character

**Goal:** a bit where you set up as yourself, become the characters, then step back out
to comment — and the audience always knows who is speaking.

## Structure of a mix
1. **Narrator setup** (to the audience): premise and context.
2. **Act-out** (in the scene): you play yourself-in-the-moment and one or more others.
3. **Narrator comment** (back to the audience): your attitude on what just happened — often the tag.
4. Repeat with a bigger act-out.

## Making switches readable
- **Eye line:** narrator looks at the audience; characters look at each other on fixed
  points either side (left = you, right = mum). Keep the points consistent all bit.
- **Body:** each character has one physical marker (posture, hand, head tilt).
- **Voice:** a pitch or rhythm change, not necessarily an accent.
- **Turn on the switch:** physically turn your head/shoulders when changing character.

## Writing the act-out
- Write the dialogue as a short script, then cut to the minimum lines.
- The funniest line usually belongs to the "other" character; your character reacts.
- The comment after the act-out should reveal what you really thought.

## Gotchas
- Staying in the act-out too long: the audience loses your point of view. Pop out every 20-30 seconds.
- Drifting eye lines make two characters merge.
- Mic stand in the way: take the mic off the stand for big act-outs.

## Verify
- [ ] A stranger watching a recording can say who spoke each line.
- [ ] Every act-out ends with a narrator comment that gets its own laugh.
