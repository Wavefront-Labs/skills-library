# After-dinner speaking set

## The format
- Usually after the meal, before or after awards, introduced by a toastmaster or MC.
- 20-40 minutes, lights often up, audience at round tables, some with backs to you.
- Many after-dinner speakers are not club comics: the style is anecdote-led, warmer, with
  a clear speaker persona (sports star, raconteur).

## Structure
1. Thank the hosts and reference the occasion (custom line).
2. Run of personal anecdotes, each ending on a punchline.
3. Topical or event-specific section.
4. Gentle crescendo, a sincere toast or message, final big laugh.

## Room craft
- Ask for tables to be turned or chairs angled; ask staff to stop clearing during the speech.
- Use a handheld mic or lectern mic; check radio mic battery.
- Speak more slowly: the room has drunk; round tables are distracting.
- Do not start until the coffee service is done.

## Timing
Agree length with the toastmaster; keep to it within a minute. Overruns delay awards.

## Gotchas
- Club pacing (laugh every 15 s) is not expected; but long dead stretches still lose the room.
- Drunk sports-dinner crowds may heckle; have a calm, good-humoured put-down ready.

## Verify
- [ ] Set timed to the agreed slot; custom opening approved by the organiser.
