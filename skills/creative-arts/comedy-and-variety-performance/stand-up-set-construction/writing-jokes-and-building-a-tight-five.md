# Writing jokes and building a tight five

**Goal:** turn observations into jokes that reliably get laughs, then order them into
a five-minute set (the standard showcase/audition length) that you can expand to 15,
30 and 60 minutes the same way.

## 1. The anatomy of a joke
- **Premise:** an opinion or observation with an attitude ("Self-checkout machines
  are the most passive-aggressive employees ever hired"). Attitude words — hard,
  weird, stupid, scary — give it energy.
- **Setup:** the minimum information needed so the audience forms an assumption.
- **Punchline:** reveals a second, surprising interpretation of the setup
  (misdirection). The funniest word goes **last**.
- **Tags/toppers:** extra punchlines on the same setup, no new setup required. A bit with
  three tags gets three laughs for the price of one setup.
- **Act-out:** perform the characters instead of describing them; shows rather than tells.

## 2. Writing process
1. Keep a notebook/notes app; capture premises, not jokes, daily.
2. For one premise, write 10 possible punchlines. Most will be bad; that is the point.
3. **Cut words** ruthlessly: every word between setup and punch is time without a laugh.
   Read it aloud and remove anything the audience doesn't need.
4. Test on stage (open mics). Paper doesn't laugh; the room is the only judge.
5. Record every set. Mark where laughs happened. Rewrite and retest.

## 3. Bits and chunks
Jokes on one topic form a **bit**; related bits form a **chunk**. Order inside a bit:
strongest setup first (earns trust), weaker middle, strongest tag last.

## 4. Building the five
- **Opener (first 30 s):** a strong, short, proven joke — ideally one that tells the
  audience who you are (your look, age, situation). Laughs early buy patience.
- **Middle:** your best bits in order of momentum; group by topic so segues are natural
  or use a short transition line.
- **Closer:** your strongest joke or a **callback** (a reference to an earlier bit that
  pays off twice). End on a laugh, say your name, leave.
- Write the set list as **trigger words** on a card/hand: "self-checkout / dentist /
  dad's phone / callback: robot".
- Time it out loud; most comics run long under laughter. Leave a 15–30 s buffer, because
  hosts flash a light for the last minute and running over gets you not rebooked.

## 5. Measuring
- **Laughs per minute (LPM):** count laughs on the recording. Many working comics
  aim for 4–6+ in a club set. *Benchmarks vary by style; storytellers run fewer, bigger laughs.*
- A joke that dies three times in different rooms is cut or rewritten, not defended.
- Keep a "graveyard" file; dead jokes sometimes revive as tags elsewhere.

## Gotchas
- Too much setup: if a setup is longer than ~3 sentences without a laugh, add a tag
  inside it or cut.
- Explaining the joke after the punchline (stepping on the laugh).
- Hacky premises (airline food, "men vs women") — if 100 comics have said it, find your own angle.
- Lifting material: never use another comic's jokes; it's career-ending.
- Saving the best for last when the room needs it now — the opener must be strong.

## Verify
- [ ] Each joke has the funniest word at the end.
- [ ] Every bit has at least one tag.
- [ ] Opener and closer are proven (worked in 3+ rooms).
- [ ] Set list fits on one card as trigger words.
- [ ] Recorded set runs under the time with laughter included; LPM counted.
