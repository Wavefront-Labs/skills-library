# Writing personal and confessional material

**Goal:** material only you could do — specific, revealing, funny — without it becoming therapy on stage.

## 1. Inventory
List what an audience notices about you in the first ten seconds (age, look, accent,
size) and the things they would never guess. Both are material; the first must usually
be addressed early or it distracts.

## 2. Distance test
Comedy = tragedy plus distance. For a painful episode ask:
- Can I tell it without my voice changing? If not, it is not ready.
- Am I the butt of the joke, or is someone who cannot answer back?
Material about recent grief/breakups often lands only after months of retelling.

## 3. Find the angle
- What did *you* do wrong, ridiculous, or petty in the story? That is usually the joke.
- What is the universal version? (Your divorce is specific; "dividing the friends" is universal.)
- Exaggerate your own flaw, not the other person's.

## 4. Protect real people
- Change names and identifying details; the audience does not need them.
- Consider whether a partner, child or parent would be harmed if they saw it filmed.
- Do not reveal others' medical or legal information.

## 5. Earn the serious beat
A sincere moment needs a laugh before and after it. Keep it short (one or two lines) and
return to the joke rhythm. Signal the turn with a change in pace, not an announcement.

## Gotchas
- Confession without jokes reads as asking the room to look after you; rooms go quiet.
- Self-deprecation as a persona can cap your status; mix it with pride or anger.

## Verify
- [ ] Each personal bit has a clear target (usually you) and a laugh every 15-20 seconds.
- [ ] You can perform the bit three nights running without emotional leakage.
