# Switching a joke shape

**Goal:** learn joke mechanics by reusing a proven structure on new subject matter — legitimately, not stealing.

## Method
1. Take a joke you admire. Strip its content and write the mechanism in brackets:
   "I'm so [trait] that [extreme consequence that is literal reading of trait]."
2. Identify why it works: which assumption it breaks, where the punch word is.
3. Swap the subject for one of your premises. Keep the mechanism, change every
   specific word and image.
4. Push until the new joke is about you and could not be traced to the original.
5. Repeat with five different switches of the same mechanism.

## Ethics line
- A switch that keeps the original's specific image, punch or premise is theft.
- The mechanism (exaggeration, misdirect, reversal) belongs to nobody; the specific picture does.
- Never perform a switch whose source a booker or comic would recognise.

## Uses
- Writers' rooms and topical monologues use switching to generate volume fast.
- Practice tool: switch 10 jokes a day for a month to internalise structures.

## Gotchas
- Switching "hack" structures ("my wife is so...") produces hack jokes; pick fresh models.
- Over-reliance gives a set of identical rhythms.

## Verify
- [ ] Five switches from one mechanism; none share a noun or verb with the source.
