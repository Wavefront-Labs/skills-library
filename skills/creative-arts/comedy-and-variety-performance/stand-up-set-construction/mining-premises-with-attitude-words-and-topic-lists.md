# Mining premises with attitude words and topic lists

**Goal:** a page of usable premises — opinions with feeling — that jokes can hang off.

## 1. Topic list
Write 30+ topics you can talk about with authority, under headings:
- Who you are (age, job, body, family role, background, where you live).
- What you are going through (dating, divorce, kids, debt, illness, moving).
- What you hate / love / fear.
Rule: specific beats general. "My mum's WhatsApp voice notes" beats "parents".

## 2. Add the attitude (Carter's four words)
For each topic finish one of these:
- "What's **weird** about ___ is ..."
- "What's **scary** about ___ is ..."
- "What's **hard** about ___ is ..."
- "What's **stupid** about ___ is ..."
Also usable: "I love ___ because", "I hate ___ because". The attitude word gives energy;
a premise without emotion produces observational lists with no laughs.

## 3. Turn it into a premise
A premise = topic + attitude + specific opinion. It is not yet funny; it is true.
- Topic: self-checkout. Attitude: stupid. Opinion: "It's stupid that I now do the cashier's
  job and the machine still accuses me of theft."
Test: could you argue it for two minutes? If not, it is too thin.

## 4. Rank and pick
Mark each premise for (a) how strongly you feel, (b) how many people share the
experience, (c) whether it says something about you. Write jokes on the top five first.

## Gotchas
- Generic attitude ("airline food is bad") — everyone has done it; push to your angle.
- Premises with no conflict produce chat, not jokes.
- Do not skip straight to punchlines; the premise is what you return to when a bit stalls.

## Verify
- [ ] 30 topics, 20 premises each containing an attitude word and an opinion.
- [ ] Five premises developed into bits and tried at a mic.
