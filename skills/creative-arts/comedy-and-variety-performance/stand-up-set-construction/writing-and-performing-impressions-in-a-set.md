# Impressions in a stand-up set

**Goal:** an impression that gets laughs from what the person says, not just recognition.

## Building the voice
1. Collect 20+ minutes of the subject's speech (interviews rather than scripted roles).
2. Isolate the **signature traits**: pitch, rhythm, a catchphrase, a breath pattern, a
   mouth shape, a gesture. Pick the two or three most identifiable.
3. Exaggerate those traits 20-30%. Accuracy everywhere else matters less.
4. Practise phrases you will actually use, recording and comparing side by side.
5. Find the physical anchor: jaw position, eyebrows, posture — it often fixes the voice.

## Writing for it
- Recognition gets one laugh. The joke is **the person in a situation they would never
  be in**, or saying the subtext out loud.
- Premise: "[Person] doing [mundane job]" or "[person] reacting to [current event]".
- Keep each impression short (20-60 seconds) unless it is the act.

## In the set
- Name the subject before or immediately in the first line — confusion kills it.
- Pick subjects the room knows; age and region of the crowd matter.
- Bridge back to your own voice with a comment.

## Gotchas
- Vocal strain: impressions with heavy rasp or extreme pitch can damage the voice; warm up.
- Impressions of private individuals or marginalised accents can read as punching down.

## Verify
- [ ] Recorded impression recognised by 4 of 5 listeners without being named.
- [ ] Bit gets laughs after the first recognition laugh.
