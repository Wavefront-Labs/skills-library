# Cruise ship and resort sets

**Goal:** a clean, broad set that plays to a mixed-age, mixed-nationality crowd who did not choose you.

## What is typical (confirm with the booking agent)
- Clean content is the norm; contracts usually spell out content rules and the ship may get complaint forms. Expect no political, religious or sexual material.
- Multiple sets across a week may need several different shows. Ask how many separate sets and how long each is.
- Slot lengths and early/late show splits vary by line and ship; do not assume.
- Contracts, travel, cabin, exclusivity and your fee come from the agent or entertainment company. Read the contract fully before signing.

## Writing the set
1. Broad observational topics: travel, food, families, ageing, technology, marriage.
2. Strong punchlines from universal experience, not from local slang. See `international-and-second-language-crowds`.
3. Slower rhythm and clearer enunciation than a club set; the audience includes non-native listeners.
4. Make a clean version of existing material; see `writing-a-clean-version-of-a-set`.
5. Avoid jokes about the ship, the line or the crew unless agreed.

## Onboard
- Know the show time, technical cues, and who runs sound and lights; a 10-minute tech check is common.
- Be present and polite: you are likely to meet the audience in the corridors all week.

## Gotchas
- Opening with a joke that needs cultural context.
- Treating the first show as a try-out for edgy material.

## Verify
- [ ] Set is clean to the contract's standard and timed with slack.
