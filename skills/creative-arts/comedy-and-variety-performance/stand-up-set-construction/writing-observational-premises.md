# Writing observational premises

**Goal:** turn things everyone has noticed but nobody says into premises with your point of view.

## Method
1. **Keep a noticing log** for a week: note moments of mild irritation, embarrassment or
   absurdity as they happen (phone notes). Write the exact detail: what was said, what
   the object looked like.
2. **Find the shared truth:** what does everyone do but pretend not to? ("Everyone
   pretends to read the terms and conditions.")
3. **Add your attitude and stake:** why does it bother *you*? Observational comedy
   without the observer is a list.
4. **Act it out:** observational material lands on the act-out — show the behaviour.
5. **Find the escalation:** the third or fourth example should be more absurd than the first.

## Freshness check
The 1990s "airline food / what's the deal with" style is now a parody of itself. Test:
- Search your premise phrase; if famous comics have a well-known bit on it, find a narrower angle.
- Prefer new technology, new social rules and your own specific environment.

## Recognition laugh vs surprise laugh
Observational material often gets the "that's so true" recognition laugh, which is
softer. Put a surprise punch on top: recognition in the setup, a twist about you in the punch.

## Gotchas
- Observing other people cruelly reads as mean unless you include yourself.
- Too local (your office) and the room does not share it — set it up.

## Verify
- [ ] Noticing log with 50+ entries; 10 turned into premises with an attitude.
- [ ] At least one bit gets both a recognition laugh and a surprise laugh on tape.
