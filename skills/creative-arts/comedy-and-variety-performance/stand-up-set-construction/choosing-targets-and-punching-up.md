# Choosing targets and punching up

## Find the butt
For every joke write: "Who is the joke on?" It is the person/group whose assumption,
status or behaviour the punch exposes. Sometimes it is the speaker.

## Power direction
- **Punching up:** target has more power than you or the audience (politicians, institutions,
  the rich, your own privilege). Audiences generally grant licence.
- **Punching down:** target has less power or is a marginalised group. Rooms often turn, and
  the joke tends to rest on a stereotype (a weak assumption).
- **Punching at yourself:** usually safest; status cost if overused.

## Satire that takes a position
1. State your actual opinion on the issue in one sentence.
2. Exaggerate the opposing logic to absurdity, or show the target's real behaviour in a new frame.
3. The audience should leave knowing what you think.

## Mixed rooms
- Make the joke on the behaviour, not the voters.
- Front-load your credibility (a joke at your own side) before attacking the other.

## Gotchas
- "It's just a joke" is not a defence the room accepts; the laugh is the test.
- Hecklers on political material: do not debate; get back to the joke.

## Verify
- [ ] Every political bit has a named target and an identifiable opinion.
