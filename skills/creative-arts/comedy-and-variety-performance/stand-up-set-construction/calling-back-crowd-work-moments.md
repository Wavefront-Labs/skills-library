# Calling back crowd-work moments

**Goal:** turn an improvised moment into a running thread that ties the set together.

## Capture
- Note in the moment (mentally or on a card) the person, a short phrase they said, and why the room laughed. Names and seats are the quickest handles ("Dave in the blue shirt").
- Not every exchange is callback material; pick the one that got a real laugh or created a character.

## Place the callback
1. Wait: a callback works after other material has intervened, not immediately.
2. Reintroduce the reference in the shortest words and let the room do the work.
3. Attach it to a new topic so it lands as a surprise, not a repeat.
4. Use it as the closer when it fits: the room feels the set was written around them. See `closing-on-a-callback` in `planting-and-paying-off-callbacks`.

## Care
- Keep the target good-natured; the person has to be comfortable with a second round.
- If they have left or you cannot recall the detail, drop it. A fumbled callback costs more than it earns.
- Callbacks to a heckler need the room's agreement that you won; otherwise leave it.

## Gotchas
- Over-referencing one person turns the set into a bit about them.
- Writing the callback into a recorded set: it dies when it is repeated verbatim in the next room.

## Verify
- [ ] Callback recalled correctly (name, detail) and landed after a gap.
