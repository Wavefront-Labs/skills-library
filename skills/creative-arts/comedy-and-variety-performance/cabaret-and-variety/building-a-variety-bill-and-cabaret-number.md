# Building a cabaret number and a variety bill

**Goal:** make a single cabaret/variety act that lands in an intimate room, and — as a
producer — order a bill of mixed acts so the evening builds rather than sags.

## 1. The cabaret number
Cabaret is direct address: the performer acknowledges the audience, often across a
small room with tables. A number (song, burlesque act, novelty turn) usually has:
1. **Entrance** that defines the character instantly (costume, music, a look).
2. **Hook** within the first 30 s — why should the room watch?
3. **Development** — the song's story, the act's escalating moments.
4. **Peak** — the reveal, high note, trick or emotional turn.
5. **Button** — a clean finish that cues applause; hold the final pose.
Typical length 3–6 minutes. *Producers vary; ask before you arrive.*

### Patter
Spoken links between songs in a cabaret set. Keep it specific and personal, and let
it lead into the next number's subject. Script and rehearse it; "improvised" patter
rambles.

## 2. Burlesque pacing (brief)
- A burlesque act is about **anticipation**: each removal is a mini-reveal with its own
  build, tease and payoff. Plan glove, stocking, gown, etc. against the music's beats.
- Tease with eye contact and the look to the audience before each removal.
- A **stage kitten**/assistant collects discarded costume between acts.
- Venue and local law set what can be shown; confirm the house rules in writing.

## 3. Novelty and speciality acts
Hula hoop, sword swallowing, fire, quick-change, physical theatre, musical saw...
Shape them like any act: one premise, escalate, peak, button. **Fire and dangerous
specialities** need venue permission, a risk assessment, a fire safety person with
appropriate extinguisher/fire blanket, and clear space from the audience. *Local rules
on fire performance vary a lot; check before booking.*

## 4. Building the bill (producer)
- **Opener:** high energy and broadly appealing — warms the room for the MC.
- **Alternate:** genre, energy and volume (song › comedy › speciality › burlesque).
  Never two similar acts back-to-back.
- **Tech-heavy acts** (rigging, set, mess) go before an interval or after a front-of-curtain
  act to cover the reset.
- **Before the interval:** a strong act so people come back.
- **Headliner/closer:** your strongest, most memorable act; then a quick finale and bows.
- Messy acts (glitter, water) follow with a sweep time or a floor act that doesn't need a
  clean stage.

### Timing an evening
List each act's length + 1–2 min for the MC intro/changeover. Two halves of 40–50 min
with a 15–20 min interval is a common shape. Add contingency; shows run long.

## 5. Paperwork
- **Tech sheet per act:** music file name and cue (start on light/start on pose),
  lighting mood, props in/out, who sets them, mic needs, intro text for the MC.
- **Running order** printed for the MC, sound, lights, stage manager, backstage wall.
- Fees, arrival time, and content rules agreed in writing.

## Gotchas
- Acts arriving without music in the right format — demand files in advance plus backup.
- Running orders that put the two slow ballads together.
- Ignoring changeover time: a 2-minute act with a 5-minute set-up hurts the evening.
- Glitter on the floor before a barefoot dancer.

## Verify
- [ ] Each number has an entrance, a hook, a peak and a button.
- [ ] Bill alternates genre and energy; strong acts before interval and at the close.
- [ ] Tech sheets collected for every act; running order distributed.
- [ ] Changeovers and messy acts planned; timing adds up with contingency.
- [ ] Dangerous speciality acts cleared with venue and covered by a risk assessment.
