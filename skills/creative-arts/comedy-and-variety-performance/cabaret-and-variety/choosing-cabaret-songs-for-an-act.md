# Choosing cabaret songs for an act

**Goal:** a set list that shows who you are and holds a room for 45-60 minutes or whatever your slot is.

## Principles
- **Voice and persona**: sing songs that sit well in your range and that you can act truthfully.
- **Theme**: an act often has a through-line (a story, an idea, a relationship). Songs earn their place by serving it.
- **Variety**: vary tempo, mood, key, and style across the set; avoid three ballads in a row.
- **Lyrics**: cabaret rooms are intimate; the audience hears every word. Choose lyrics you can inhabit and say clearly.
- **Familiarity**: a mix of well-known songs and less-known ones; avoid songs that are very overexposed unless you have a fresh angle.

## Process
1. List 30 songs you love and can sing.
2. Group by theme or mood.
3. Choose an opener and a closer; see the entrance-to-button shape in `building-a-variety-bill-and-cabaret-number`.
4. Fill the middle with contrasts.
5. Test in short slots and cut songs that do not work.

## Practicalities
- Check licensing and the venue's agreements for performance; recording and publishing need separate permission.
- Arrangements: your musical director may re-arrange; see `working-with-a-musical-director`.

## Gotchas
- A song that needs a range you only reach on good days.
- Choosing a song because it is popular rather than because it fits.

## Verify
- [ ] Each song has a reason in the act and the set varies in tempo and mood.
