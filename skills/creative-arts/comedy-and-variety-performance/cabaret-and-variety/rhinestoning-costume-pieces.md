# Rhinestoning

**Goal:** durable sparkle that reads under stage lights.

## Materials
- Flat-back rhinestones (glass or acrylic) in several sizes; glass is brighter but more costly and heavier.
- A strong adhesive suited to fabric and stones: many costumers use a jewellery or rhinestone glue (E6000-type products are common), applied in a ventilated space.
- Tweezers, a wax pickup pencil, and a protective surface.

## Process
1. Design the pattern: draw it or place stones on the garment without glue.
2. Lay out so the main lines are in view.
3. Apply a small dot of glue to the garment or the stone.
4. Place the stone with a pickup tool; press gently.
5. Let it cure for the time stated by the maker, without moving the item.

## Tips
- Start from the centre of a design and work outward.
- Use varied sizes for depth.
- Stones that will be on moving parts need a flexible adhesive.

## Care
- Spot-clean; avoid soaking and machine washing unless the adhesive allows it.
- Keep spares and glue for repairs.

## Gotchas
- Glue fumes; ventilate.
- Stones that fall off during a number; check before shows.

## Verify
- [ ] Stones stay on after a test movement and the costume sparkles from the back of the room.
