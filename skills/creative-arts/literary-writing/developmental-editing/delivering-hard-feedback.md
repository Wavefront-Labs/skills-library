# Delivering hard feedback

**Goal:** tell an author what is not working clearly enough to be useful and kindly enough to be heard.

## Before
1. **Be sure.** Re-read the evidence. If the main issue is taste, say it is taste.
2. **Know their goal** and their stage. Feedback on a first draft differs from feedback on a book going to submission.
3. **Decide the order.** Big issues first, with the root cause rather than the full list.

## Delivering
1. **Start with an honest, specific strength.** Not a formula; the strength must be real. Praise-then-hammer-then-praise can read as insincere if applied mechanically.
2. **Describe the reader experience.** "I lost track of the timeline in chapter 7" is about the book, not the author's talent.
3. **Give evidence**: a page, a scene, a pattern. Vague criticism cannot be acted on.
4. **Say why it matters** in terms of the author's goal.
5. **Offer a way forward**: options or a next experiment, not a verdict.
6. **Be plain about readiness.** If the book is not yet ready for its intended market, say so without euphemism and without predicting outcomes.
7. **Choose the medium.** For major structural news, a call or meeting alongside the written letter helps; send the letter after so they can reread.

## When they react
- Expect a delay. Offer time before discussing. Do not argue in the first reaction.
- Listen for the real concern behind the pushback (fear, money, time).
- Distinguish "I disagree" (valid, discuss) from "you are wrong" (revisit the evidence).
- Do not retreat from a well-founded point to restore comfort, and do not escalate.

## Verify
- [ ] Each criticism has evidence and a reason.
- [ ] The strength you named is true.
- [ ] A follow-up conversation is scheduled.
