# Running a critique group

**Goal:** a small, durable group whose members give each other honest, usable feedback.

## Forming
1. **Define the focus.** Genre, level and commitment: a novel group differs from a poetry group. Mixed-genre groups work if members are open readers.
2. **Aim for 4-8 active members.** Fewer risks collapse when someone is absent; more makes turns too infrequent.
3. **Match commitment.** Ask members to attend regularly and read before meetings. Hold a trial period, then confirm membership.
4. **Agree the logistics**: meeting frequency (every two to four weeks is common), online or in person, how pages are circulated.

## Ground rules
- **Submission limits** (pages or words per turn) and a deadline for circulating.
- **How to critique**: read the work as presented, note where you were pulled in and out, no rewriting.
- **Writer's questions** at the top of the submission to focus the group.
- **Confidentiality** of each other's drafts.
- **Pace**: equal time per writer; a timekeeper.

## Running meetings
1. Short check-in, then one writer at a time.
2. Strengths first, then problems tied to the reader's experience.
3. Writer's silence during discussion, questions at the end (or use the Critical Response structure, `creative-arts.literary-writing.developmental-editing.critical-response-process`).
4. Rotate facilitator and timekeeper.

## Long-term health
- Review how it is going once or twice a year.
- Handle a member who is consistently harsh, silent or absent directly and early.
- Admit that a group has a life cycle; ending or reshaping it is fine.

## Verify
- [ ] Written norms exist and every member has seen them.
- [ ] Circulation deadline and page limits are respected.
- [ ] A periodic review is scheduled.
