# Writing author queries

**Goal:** questions in the manuscript margin that the author can answer quickly and
that respect their authority over the text. CMOS (manuscript editing) treats queries as
a core editorial tool: the editor flags; the author decides.

## Query or change?
- **Change silently / with Track Changes:** clear errors covered by the style sheet
  (typos, house style).
- **Change and explain:** non-obvious corrections ("Changed to 'Tuesday' — ch. 3
  establishes the funeral was Monday").
- **Query without changing:** matters of meaning, fact, voice, or anything you're not sure
  the author intended.

## Format
- Comment bubbles in Word, prefixed with **AU:** (author query) — or as the publisher specifies.
- One issue per query, anchored to the exact words.
- Number or tag repeated issues ("AU: see query on p. 12") rather than repeating long text.

## Wording pattern: problem + reason + options + question
- Weak: "Awkward." / "Huh?" / "Fix this."
- Strong: "AU: 'She left the room' — she's still present in the next paragraph. Should
  she stay, or should the next paragraph move to the hallway?"
- Offer a suggested fix where useful: "Perhaps: 'She stepped back from the door'? OK?"
- Neutral, courteous tone; ask, don't command. Avoid sarcasm and exclamation marks.
- Make it answerable: yes/no or a choice where possible.

## Fact queries
- "AU: The Golden Gate Bridge opened in 1937; the scene is set in 1935. Change the year,
  or change the bridge?" Cite your source briefly.

## Volume
- Too many trivial queries bury important ones; resolve trivia via the style sheet.
- Put a summary of recurring issues in the cover letter instead of 50 identical queries.

## Gotchas
- Queries that rewrite the passage in your style.
- Ambiguous anchoring (comment attached to the wrong paragraph).
- Forgetting to remove resolved queries before final files.

## Verify
- [ ] Every query states problem, reason and a clear question.
- [ ] Tone neutral and courteous throughout.
- [ ] Recurring issues summarised, not repeated.
