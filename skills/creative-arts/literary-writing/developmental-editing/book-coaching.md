# Book coaching

**Goal:** support an author through drafting and revision with structure, accountability and craft guidance, while leaving the book theirs.

## Coach versus editor
- A **coach** works alongside the process: goals, habits, structure, feedback on pages as they come, motivation.
- An **editor** assesses a manuscript and delivers an editorial letter or edits (`creative-arts.literary-writing.developmental-editing.writing-an-editorial-letter`).
Some professionals offer both; be clear in each engagement which role is in play. A coach does not promise a publishing result.

## Structuring an engagement
1. **Discovery call**: goal, genre, stage, schedule, what has stopped them before.
2. **Plan**: a book map (premise, audience, outline), milestones, weekly output target realistic for their life.
3. **Cadence**: a regular session (weekly or fortnightly), a check-in channel, deadlines for pages.
4. **Feedback**: respond to submitted pages with craft notes; keep structural notes for logical checkpoints.
5. **Accountability**: agree what happens when targets are missed; the goal is learning, not shame.
6. **Wrap-up**: a final assessment, next steps (developmental edit, copyedit, publishing route).

## Contract and pricing
- Put scope, session length and number, response time, fees and cancellation in writing; see `creative-arts.literary-writing.developmental-editing.editing-contract-and-scope`.
- Price per session, per month or per package; check the going local rate rather than copying a number.
- Say you do not guarantee completion, publication or sales.
- Set a boundary on what you will not do (ghostwrite, write their pages).

## Ethics
Keep confidentiality, avoid giving legal, medical or financial advice, and refer out when the work needs a therapist or a lawyer.

## Verify
- [ ] The role (coach, editor, or both) is stated.
- [ ] Milestones and cadence agreed.
- [ ] No guarantees offered.
