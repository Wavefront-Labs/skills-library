# Writing a manuscript critique report

**Goal:** deliver a compact, structured assessment of a manuscript. It differs from a full editorial letter (`creative-arts.literary-writing.developmental-editing.writing-an-editorial-letter`): shorter, more standardised, often used for contests, first-look services or when the author wants a diagnosis without a long essay.

## Typical structure (2-6 pages)
1. **Header:** title, word count, genre, date, who it is for.
2. **Summary:** 3-6 sentences of what the manuscript is about, to show you read it and to let the author compare with their intent.
3. **Overall verdict:** one paragraph, honest on readiness (for example: strong premise, structure needs work before submission).
4. **Assessment by element**, each with a short comment and, if the service uses them, a rating on a stated scale: premise/concept, structure and pacing, characters, point of view and voice, dialogue, setting and world, prose, theme, market fit.
5. **Strengths:** specific, quoted or referenced.
6. **Priority issues:** top 3-5, ordered by impact, each with evidence (where it shows) and a direction to fix.
7. **Next steps:** what level of edit or revision comes next.

## Rules of thumb
- Write for the author's stated goal. A submission-ready check differs from a developmental workup.
- Every criticism gets an example and a direction, not a vague "pacing is off".
- Ratings must be defined (a legend), otherwise they read as grades.
- Separate taste from craft; mark taste as taste.
- Do not promise publication outcomes.

## Verify
- [ ] The summary matches what the author meant.
- [ ] Each priority issue has evidence and a direction.
- [ ] The report can be read in ten minutes and acted on.
