# Sensitivity reading

**Goal:** get an informed read on how a manuscript portrays a group, culture, condition or experience, so harmful stereotypes and inaccuracies are caught before publication. This is professional guidance on a practice that is debated; no reader speaks for a whole community.

## What it is
A sensitivity reader (also called an authenticity reader or cultural reader) reads a manuscript from the vantage of lived experience of a particular identity or subject, and comments on representation, language, stereotyping and factual or cultural errors. It supplements, and does not replace, an editor, research or the writer's own responsibility.

## Hiring
1. **Define the need**: which character, culture, condition or setting.
2. **Find readers** via publishers' lists, editors' networks, and professional directories; look at their experience, references and sample reports.
3. **Pay professionally.** Rates vary and are negotiated; ask for quotes rather than assuming a standard. Clarify scope (word count, turnaround, comment-only or a report).
4. **Agree confidentiality** and who owns the report.

## Briefing
Give the reader the full manuscript or relevant section, genre and audience, what the character does in the plot, and specific questions (is the dialect plausible? is this ritual handled correctly?). Allow them to flag things you did not ask about.

## Using the report
- Read without defending. Make a list of each comment and decide: accept, adapt, research further, decline. Record why.
- One reader's view is one view; for high-stakes portrayals, consider more than one.
- If a comment shows the premise itself is the problem, take that seriously; consider whether this story is yours to tell.
- Credit only with the reader's agreement; many readers prefer not to be named.
- The final decisions remain the author's and the publisher's.

## Verify
- [ ] Scope, fee, confidentiality and credit agreed in writing.
- [ ] Each comment has a recorded decision.
- [ ] The text changes reflect the decisions.
