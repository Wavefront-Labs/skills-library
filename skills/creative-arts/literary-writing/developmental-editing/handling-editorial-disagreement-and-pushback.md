# Handling disagreement and pushback

**Goal:** protect the book and the relationship when author and editor differ.

## Steps
1. **Pause and listen.** Understand exactly what they object to: the diagnosis (is there a problem?), the fix, or the tone.
2. **Separate problem from solution.** Authors often disagree with a suggested fix while a real problem remains. Say: "Let us set aside my suggestion; do you see the issue I am pointing at?" If they do, they can solve it their way.
3. **Re-check your evidence.** Return to the passage. Was your reading fair? Be willing to be wrong; if you were, say so plainly.
4. **Offer options.** A, B or C, each with trade-offs; the author chooses.
5. **Know what is negotiable.** Taste and approach are the author's. Factual errors, legal risks, internal contradictions and style-sheet consistency are less so. In a publisher's workflow, the house standard and contractual obligations also apply.
6. **Use stet and a note for copy-level disagreements.** An author may restore a change; record it on the style sheet so the decision stays consistent.
7. **Escalate when needed.** If the issue is risk (defamation, permissions, safety), explain consequences, recommend the author seek advice, and flag to the commissioning editor or client contact as your role requires.
8. **Document** the point and the decision.
9. **Step back if it is irreconcilable.** Finish the agreed scope professionally; do not rewrite the book to your taste.

## Words that help
"Here is what happened for me as a reader." "What were you hoping the reader would feel here?" "I may be misreading; what am I missing?"

## Verify
- [ ] The problem has been named separately from your fix.
- [ ] Non-negotiables are limited and explained.
- [ ] Decisions are recorded on the style sheet or in notes.
