# The diagnostic first read

**Goal:** capture the experience of an uncontaminated reader before you start analysing, so your diagnosis rests on what the book actually does to a reader.

## Setup
- Read in as few sittings as practical and on the format the author will publish in if you can (printed or an e-reader), not in track-changes with the margin open.
- Have a separate notes file open, not the manuscript.
- Clarify the brief with the author first: genre, audience, target publication route, what they most want checked.

## During the read
1. **Do not edit.** Resist fixing sentences. Note only page or chapter and a few words.
2. **Log reader responses at the moment they happen**: confusion, boredom, "I do not believe this", "who is this?", delight, a surprise that worked. Use short tags (`lost`, `slow`, `flat`, `wow`, `?`).
3. **Record where you stopped or skimmed** and why. Where a reader put the book down is a high-value data point.
4. **Track the questions the book raises** and when each is answered or dropped.
5. **Note the promise** the opening makes (genre, tone, protagonist problem) and whether the ending pays it off.

## Right after finishing
1. Write, within an hour and from memory, a **one-paragraph reader summary**: what happens, who matters, what it is about.
2. Write the **three biggest strengths** and **three biggest problems** as you felt them.
3. State a **hypothesis**: the underlying cause of the biggest problem (for instance, a passive protagonist, a late inciting incident, a premise that is too thin for the length).
4. Only then begin the analytic second pass (reverse outline, chapter audit, see `creative-arts.literary-writing.developmental-editing.writing-an-editorial-letter`).

## Failure modes
- Starting analysis early: you stop reading as a reader and miss the experience.
- Trusting the hypothesis without testing it on the second pass.

## Verify
- [ ] You have reader-response notes with page references.
- [ ] A reader summary written from memory.
- [ ] A hypothesis stated and flagged for testing.
