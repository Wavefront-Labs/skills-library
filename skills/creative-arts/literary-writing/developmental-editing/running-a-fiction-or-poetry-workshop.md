# Running a fiction or poetry workshop

**Goal:** give each writer useful readings of their work and a clear sense of what to try next, in a room that stays safe and on topic.

The traditional graduate model, associated with the Iowa Writers' Workshop (founded 1936) and widely copied, has the writer silent while the group discusses the piece. It has well-known critics and alternatives (see `creative-arts.literary-writing.developmental-editing.critical-response-process`).

## Before
1. **Set size and cadence.** 8-14 participants is common in classes; smaller for an adult group. Decide how many pieces per session and page or line limits so everyone can read in advance.
2. **Distribute work in advance** (a week is typical) and require written responses.
3. **Write norms** and circulate them: read generously, respond to the work not the person, no rewriting for them, confidentiality.

## Session structure (about 20-40 minutes per piece)
1. **Reader or writer reads a short passage aloud** (optional; useful for poetry).
2. **Facilitator frames**: remind the group of the norms and of the writer's own questions, if given.
3. **Discussion with the writer silent.** Start with what is working (specific), then where readers were confused or lost interest, then questions the work raises. For poetry: image, line break, sound, turn, ending.
4. **Writer asks questions** at the end, clarifying and not defending.
5. **Hand over written comments.** Writers need the group's notes to revise.

## Facilitator moves
- Redirect "I would have..." to "I experienced...".
- Stop a pile-on once a point has been made.
- Ask quiet members for an observation.
- Keep to time; end each piece on a next step.

## Failure modes
- A destructive "gotcha" culture; fix with norms and facilitation.
- Writers rewriting to please the group; remind them they decide.
- Critique of the author's life or identity rather than the text.

## Verify
- [ ] Everyone had pages in advance.
- [ ] Each writer leaves with strengths and two or three specific revision directions.
