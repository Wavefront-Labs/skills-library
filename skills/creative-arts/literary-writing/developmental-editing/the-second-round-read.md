# The second-round read

**Goal:** assess whether the revision solved the problems identified, find what the revision broke, and decide whether the book is ready for the next stage.

## Before reading
- Re-read your first letter, margin notes and the author's decisions log.
- Agree in writing what the second round covers (a re-read with a short memo, a fresh letter, or a further edit); see `creative-arts.literary-writing.developmental-editing.editing-contract-and-scope`.

## During the read
1. **Read as a reader first**, as in the first read (`creative-arts.literary-writing.developmental-editing.the-diagnostic-first-read`). Does the problem still show?
2. **Judge the problem, not the compliance.** The question is "does the book now work?" not "did they do what I suggested". An author's different solution may be better.
3. **Check the points from your letter** one by one: solved, partly solved, not solved, solved differently.
4. **Hunt revision damage**: continuity errors from moved scenes, dangling setups, repeated information, tonal seams where new material meets old, characters who change behaviour.
5. **Reverse-outline again** to see structure now.

## The follow-up
1. A short memo: what worked in the revision (specific), what remains (ranked), new issues, next step (more structural work, line edit, copyedit).
2. Do not reopen decisions settled in round one unless they now cause a clear problem.
3. If the book is ready for a lower-level pass, say so and advise the author about the next edit (`creative-arts.literary-writing.developmental-editing.choosing-the-right-level-of-edit`).

## Verify
- [ ] Each first-round point has a status.
- [ ] Revision-caused errors are listed.
- [ ] The memo gives a clear next step.
