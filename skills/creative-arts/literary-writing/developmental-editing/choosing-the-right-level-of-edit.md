# Choosing the right level of edit

**Goal:** match the work to what the manuscript can use now. Heavy line edits on a book with structural problems are wasted, and a proofread cannot fix a plot.

## The levels
- **Developmental (structural, substantive):** big picture. Premise, structure, pacing, character arcs, point of view, argument and organisation in nonfiction.
- **Line / stylistic:** sentence and paragraph craft, voice, rhythm, clarity. See `creative-arts.literary-writing.developmental-editing.line-editing-while-preserving-voice`.
- **Copyedit (mechanical):** grammar, usage, consistency, style sheet, fact flags. Many publishers use the Chicago Manual of Style for US trade books. See `creative-arts.literary-writing.developmental-editing.building-an-editorial-style-sheet`.
- **Proofread:** final check of formatted pages for errors the earlier passes missed or introduced.

Terminology varies by editor and country. The Editorial Freelancers Association's rates page lists the common categories, and editors use different words for overlapping work, so define the service in your contract. The classic framework "The Levels of Edit" (Van Buren and Buehler, Jet Propulsion Laboratory, 1980) grew out of technical editing and is useful for thinking about degrees of intervention.

## Decision method
1. **Read a sample** (the first 5,000-10,000 words and a later chapter) or use a sample edit (`creative-arts.literary-writing.developmental-editing.the-sample-edit`).
2. **Diagnose the dominant problem.** Structural problems: choose developmental first. If the draft is structurally sound but prose is loose: line edit. Clean prose with inconsistency and mechanical errors: copyedit. Edited, formatted, near-final: proofread.
3. **Respect the sequence.** Edit big to small; each lower level should come after the author finishes the one above it, because revisions create new errors.
4. **Consider the author's goal and budget.** Submission to agents, self-publication and a corporate document need different finish.
5. **Say what you will not do.** Put the level and its limits in writing; see `creative-arts.literary-writing.developmental-editing.editing-contract-and-scope`.
6. **Be honest about fit.** If the book needs a level you do not offer, say so and refer.

## Verify
- [ ] The recommended level is justified by two or three examples from the sample.
- [ ] The scope document names what is included and excluded.
- [ ] The author understands later passes follow their revisions.
