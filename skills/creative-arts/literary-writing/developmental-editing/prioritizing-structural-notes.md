# Prioritizing structural notes

**Goal:** give the author the few changes that will do the most, in the order that makes sense, rather than a flat list of forty problems.

## Method
1. **Dump everything first.** List every issue noticed, small and large.
2. **Cluster symptoms by cause.** Dull middle, flat stakes and a rushed ending may all trace to one root cause (a protagonist without a clear goal). Name the root; that is one note, not three.
3. **Rank by impact on the reader's experience**, from the structure outward: premise and promise; protagonist's want and stakes; structure and pacing; point of view; character consistency; scene-level craft; then prose. Higher levels generally change everything below them.
4. **Mark dependencies.** If fixing the opening alters chapters 3 to 9, say that these later issues should wait.
5. **Cap the list.** Aim for 3-5 major notes in a letter; put the remaining minor points in a short appendix or margin comments. A long list reads as hopelessness.
6. **Write each note the same way**: what I noticed (evidence), why it matters to the reader, options for fixing.
7. **Order the letter** by the order the author should work, not by the order you found them. Say so.
8. **Sanity check.** If the author fixed only the first note, would the book be better? If not, reorder.

## Failure modes
- A flat list that lets the author fix easy copy-level issues and avoid the structural one.
- Treating symptoms with separate fixes.

## Verify
- [ ] Notes grouped by cause.
- [ ] Order reflects dependency.
- [ ] No more than five major notes up front.
