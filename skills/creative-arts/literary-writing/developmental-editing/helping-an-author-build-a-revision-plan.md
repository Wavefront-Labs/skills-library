# Helping the author build a revision plan

**Goal:** turn a letter and comments into a plan the author can actually carry out, in an order that avoids rework.

## Steps
1. **Agree the target.** What would a successful draft look like (submission-ready, a specific word count, a specific problem solved)?
2. **Group notes into passes**, big to small. Typical order:
   - Pass 1: structural (reorder, cut, add, merge scenes or chapters).
   - Pass 2: character and arc continuity, point of view.
   - Pass 3: scene craft (goals, conflict, dialogue, setting).
   - Pass 4: line-level prose and voice.
   - Pass 5: copyedit and proofread (professional, if budget allows).
3. **Build a scene or chapter list** from a reverse outline. Next to each scene: keep, cut, rewrite, move, new. This turns advice into tasks.
4. **Estimate time honestly** for each pass; most authors underestimate. Work in a measurable unit (chapters per week).
5. **Set checkpoints.** After pass 1, re-outline and confirm the structure before moving on.
6. **Keep a decisions log** where they record what they changed, what they refused and why. Refusing a suggestion is allowed; unrecorded refusals cause trouble in the second round.
7. **Schedule feedback loops.** A beta reader or the editor re-reads after the structural pass, not at the very end.
8. **Protect the draft**: save versions per pass (see `creative-arts.literary-writing.scrivener.snapshots-compare-and-restore`).

## Roles
An editor suggests an approach; the plan belongs to the author. A book coach can run accountability (`creative-arts.literary-writing.developmental-editing.book-coaching`).

## Verify
- [ ] Plan lists passes in order with a scene-level task list.
- [ ] Timeline is realistic and has checkpoints.
- [ ] Decisions log exists.
