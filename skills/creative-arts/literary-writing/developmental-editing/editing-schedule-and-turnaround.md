# Scheduling and turnaround

**Goal:** a calendar that delivers every edit on time without burning out, and clients
who know exactly when to expect their work.

## Estimating turnaround
1. Hours needed = from your measured pace (see `setting-editing-rates-and-quoting`).
2. Realistic **editing hours per day**: deep editing is draining; many editors manage
   about 4-5 focused hours a day, not 8.
3. Developmental edits need **thinking time**: build in a gap between the first read and
   writing the letter (a day or more), and a reread of the letter before sending.
4. Turnaround = hours / daily editing hours + thinking gaps + buffer (e.g., +20%).
Typical developmental edits for a novel take several weeks; quote in calendar terms.

## Booking system
- A calendar of **slots** (start date + delivery date); a slot is confirmed by deposit.
- **Manuscript-due date** before the slot starts; late files move to the next free slot
  (state this in the contract — see `editing-contract-and-scope`).
- Avoid back-to-back long developmental edits; alternate with lighter work.
- Keep a waitlist for cancellations.

## Rush requests
- Only accept if capacity really exists; charge a rush fee (commonly 25-50% extra)
  because it displaces other work or adds evening hours.

## Communication
- Confirm start date when the file arrives; acknowledge receipt within a day.
- If a delay is likely, tell the client **as early as possible** with a new date — never
  on the due date.
- Send a short note at handover: what's included, how to read the edit, next steps.

## Gotchas
- Quoting turnaround as if you edit eight hours a day, every day.
- Overbooking to cover cancellations, then everyone runs late.
- No buffer for illness or a manuscript that's in worse shape than the sample.

## Verify
- [ ] Turnaround computed from pace, daily hours, thinking time and buffer.
- [ ] Every booking has a deposit, manuscript-due date and delivery date.
- [ ] Calendar has buffers between long jobs.
