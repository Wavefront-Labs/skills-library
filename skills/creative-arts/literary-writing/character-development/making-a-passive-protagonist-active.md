# Making a passive protagonist active

**Goal:** a protagonist who drives the plot through choices. "Passive protagonist" is
one of the most frequent developmental-edit notes: things happen *to* the character,
others make the decisions, and the climax is resolved by someone else or by luck.

## Audit
In the scene list, add three columns:
1. **Who makes the key decision** in this scene?
2. **What does the protagonist want** in this scene?
3. **What does the protagonist do** (not think or feel) to get it?
Scenes where the answer to (1) is someone else and (3) is "observes / is told / waits"
are passive.

## Fixes
- **Give a scene goal:** even a small, concrete want ("get the file before the meeting").
- **Turn reaction into decision:** instead of being summoned, she goes; instead of
  being told the secret, he steals the letter.
- **Make choices costly:** two options, both with a price. A choice with an obvious
  answer is not agency.
- **Let wrong choices happen:** agency includes mistakes that worsen things.
- **Take tools away from helpers:** mentors and allies who solve problems must be
  removed, delayed or wrong.
- **The climax must turn on the protagonist's action**, ideally using what they learned.
- Introspective literary protagonists can still be active: agency may be the choice to
  speak, to leave, to stay silent knowingly.

## Gotchas
- Confusing busyness with agency: running around reacting to events is still passive.
- Making the protagonist active only by making them reckless.
- Heroes swept along by prophecy or destiny with no choice — give them a choice about how.

## Verify
- [ ] The protagonist makes the key decision in most scenes.
- [ ] Each act turn is caused by a protagonist choice.
- [ ] The climax resolves through the protagonist's action.
