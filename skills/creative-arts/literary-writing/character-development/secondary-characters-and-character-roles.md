# Secondary characters and character roles

**Goal:** a supporting cast where every character has a job in the story *and*
seems to have a life beyond the protagonist.

## Functions (a character can hold more than one)
| Role | Function in the story |
|---|---|
| Confidant | Lets the protagonist voice what they feel; avoids internal monologue overload |
| Mentor | Supplies knowledge or a value; often removed so the hero must act alone |
| Ally / sidekick | Practical help; contrast in temperament |
| Love interest | Embodies what the protagonist needs; pressures the flaw |
| Foil | Highlights the protagonist by contrast: similar situation, opposite choice |
| Threshold guardian / obstacle | Tests commitment at a boundary |
| Comic relief | Modulates tone; works best when also useful |
| Catalyst | Triggers the inciting incident |

## Making them live
1. **Give each a want of their own**, independent of the protagonist ("the mentor
   wants to retire before the audit"). Supporting characters with only the hero's agenda read as props.
2. **One contradiction** each (the gentle bouncer, the cheerful mortician).
3. **Let them say no** to the protagonist at least once — friction proves independence.
4. **Minor characters** (one-scene): one specific detail or line, then gone. Don't give
   a full portrait to a waiter; readers will expect them to matter (see
   `creative-arts.literary-writing.plot-structure.chekhovs-gun-and-planting`).

## Procedure
- List the cast; beside each: function(s), want, contradiction, scenes appeared in.
- Characters with no function -> cut or merge (see `merging-characters`).
- Two characters with the same function -> merge or differentiate their functions.

## Gotchas
- The cheerleader best friend whose only job is to support.
- Stock types without a twist (the wise old mentor, the sassy friend).
- Supporting characters solving the protagonist's climax problem for them.

## Verify
- [ ] Every named character has a function and a want.
- [ ] No two characters duplicate the same function.
- [ ] Each supporting character pushes back at least once.
