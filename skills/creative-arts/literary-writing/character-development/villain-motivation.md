# Villain motivation

**Goal:** an antagonist whose actions follow from their own goals and logic, so the
conflict feels inevitable and the threat credible. "Every villain is the hero of their
own story" is the usual craft shorthand.

## Build the villain from inside
1. **Goal:** concrete and active — what they want by the end (control the company, get
   revenge, protect a secret, save their people by terrible means).
2. **Reason:** why it matters to them; ideally something the reader can understand, even
   share (love, fear, justice, survival).
3. **Method:** what they're willing to do that the protagonist isn't — the moral line.
4. **Plan and timeline:** what they're doing offstage while the protagonist acts. Write it
   out as a parallel outline.
5. **Competence:** they win early rounds; the protagonist must grow to beat them.
6. **Weakness:** a flaw or blind spot the climax can exploit, ideally tied to their motive.

## The mirror
Strong antagonists often want the same thing or share a wound with the protagonist but
chose differently (Killmonger and T'Challa in *Black Panther*). This sharpens theme.

## On the page
- **Show threat early** — an act that proves what they're capable of.
- **Escalate** their pressure at each act turn.
- Give them **a scene of humanity or doubt** if appropriate; it deepens rather than softens.
- Avoid the **monologue explanation** at the climax; reveal motive earlier through action,
  others' testimony or their POV.

## Gotchas
- Evil for its own sake — cartoonish unless the genre wants it.
- A villain who waits for the hero to arrive.
- A motive revealed only at the end, which can't recolour earlier scenes.

## Verify
- [ ] Villain's goal, reason, method and weakness written down.
- [ ] Offstage plan outlined in parallel to the main plot.
- [ ] Threat demonstrated in the first third; pressure escalates.
