# Character backstory and profile

**Goal:** know your characters well enough that they act consistently and surprise you
convincingly — while putting very little of that knowledge directly on the page
(Hemingway's iceberg).

## A useful profile (skip trivia like favourite colour)
1. **Formative events:** 2-3 moments that shaped beliefs (the wound; see
   `creative-arts.literary-writing.character-development.want-need-and-character-arc`).
2. **Present pressures:** job, money, health, family obligations, secrets — what is
   already squeezing them when the story starts.
3. **Want / fear / need** in one line each.
4. **Relationships:** whom they love, owe, resent, hide from.
5. **Body and habits:** how they move, dress, eat; one telling physical habit.
6. **Speech profile** (see `speech-patterns-and-idiolect`).
7. **Contradictions** (see `character-flaws-and-contradictions`).
8. **What they would never do** — and the circumstances in which they would.

## Character interview
Write a free-form Q&A in the character's own voice. Ask uncomfortable questions: "What
are you ashamed of?" "What do you lie about?" "What would you do with a million
pounds?" Stop when the voice comes alive; that voice matters more than the answers.

## Getting backstory on the page
- Reveal **in response to present pressure**, a sentence or two at a time.
- Through behaviour (the flinch at a raised hand) before explanation.
- Hold back the key formative event until it pays off as a revelation.
- Rule of thumb: if the reader doesn't need it to understand the current scene, leave it in the file.

## Gotchas
- Profile-filling as procrastination: cap it, then draft.
- Front-loading backstory in chapter one.
- Backstory that contradicts what the character does on the page — the page wins; update the profile.

## Verify
- [ ] Profile covers formative events, present pressures, want/fear/need.
- [ ] Interview done for each major character.
- [ ] Backstory on the page arrives in small pieces tied to present scenes.
