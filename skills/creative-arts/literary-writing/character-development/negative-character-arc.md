# Negative arc (fall, corruption, disillusionment)

**Goal:** a character who ends worse — morally, psychologically or in fortune — through
their own choices, in a way readers find inevitable and painful. Examples: Macbeth, Michael
Corleone in *The Godfather*, Walter White in *Breaking Bad*, Anakin Skywalker.

## Three common shapes (after K. M. Weiland)
- **Disillusionment:** believes a lie, learns the truth, but the truth is bleak; ends
  wiser and sadder (*The Great Gatsby*'s Nick, in part).
- **Fall:** believes a lie, rejects the truth when offered, clings to a worse lie, ends
  destroyed.
- **Corruption:** starts with the truth, is seduced by a lie, ends worse than they began.

## Building the fall
1. **Seed:** a flaw or desire present from page one (ambition, pride, love of control).
2. **Offer the truth:** characters or events present the better way — and the character
   refuses. Refusals make it tragic, not unlucky.
3. **Escalating steps:** each crossing of a line is a choice, slightly worse than the last.
   Track them in a list; no single leap from decent to monstrous.
4. **Point of no return:** an act that can't be undone (usually at or after the midpoint).
5. **Ending:** consequences land — death, isolation, a hollow victory.

## Keeping readers with them
- Sympathetic motive (protecting family), competence, and intimacy with their reasoning.
- A **foil** who chooses differently shows what was possible.

## Gotchas
- **External bad luck** instead of choices: that's misfortune, not an arc.
- **Unmotivated turns** — a decent person suddenly cruel with no step between.
- Moralising narration; let consequences speak.

## Verify
- [ ] Shape chosen (disillusionment, fall, corruption).
- [ ] Each step down is a decision; list shows gradual escalation.
- [ ] At least one clear chance to turn back is refused on the page.
