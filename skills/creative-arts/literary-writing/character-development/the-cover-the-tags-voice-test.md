# The cover-the-tags voice test

**Goal:** objectively check whether characters' voices are distinct, then fix the lines
that fail.

## Procedure
1. Pick a dialogue-heavy scene with 2-4 characters.
2. Copy it and **remove all dialogue tags, action beats and narration** — only the
   spoken lines remain, each on its own line, in order.
3. Shuffle a few lines (optional, harder test) or keep the order.
4. Give it to a reader who knows the characters (or do it yourself after a week away)
   and ask them to label who says each line.
5. Score: lines attributed correctly / total.
6. For each misattributed line, ask: *could anyone in the cast have said this?* If yes,
   rewrite it through that character's speech profile (see
   `speech-patterns-and-idiolect`): vocabulary, length, formality, avoidance.

## Table read variant
Have different people read each character aloud. Listen for lines where readers stumble
or where two characters sound the same; mark them.

## Interpreting results
- Below roughly two-thirds correct: voices are blurred; revisit profiles.
- Errors concentrated on one pair: those two characters probably share background and
  temperament — sharpen a contrast (one terse, one expansive; one deflects with jokes,
  one with silence).
- Purely functional lines ("Yes", "Where?") can't be distinct and don't need to be;
  focus on lines carrying attitude.

## Gotchas
- Fixing by adding catchphrases or dialect spelling instead of changing how the person thinks.
- Testing only with yourself immediately after writing — you know who said what.

## Verify
- [ ] Test run on at least two key scenes.
- [ ] Attribution score recorded before and after revision.
- [ ] Each rewritten line now could only come from its speaker.
