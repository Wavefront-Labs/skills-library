# Writing dialect and accent

**Goal:** convey how a character speaks — region, class, community, first language —
so the reader hears it, without making the text hard to read or turning the character
into a caricature.

## Prefer syntax and vocabulary over spelling
Order of preference:
1. **Word choice:** regional vocabulary ("wee", "y'all", "innit", "bubbler").
2. **Grammar and syntax:** characteristic constructions ("I'm after eating" in Irish
   English; "might could" in Southern US; double negatives; dropped copulas where real).
3. **Rhythm and idiom:** proverbs, typical comparisons, discourse markers.
4. **Spelling changes:** only sparingly and consistently, for the most distinctive
   features (one or two), if at all.

## Avoid eye dialect
**Eye dialect** is nonstandard spelling of words everyone pronounces the same way
("wuz" for *was*, "sez" for *says*). It signals nothing about sound — only that the
speaker is uneducated or comic. Historically it was used to demean marginalised
speakers; modern editors usually cut it.

## Procedure
1. Research the real variety: recordings, linguistics references, speakers' own writing.
2. Pick 2-4 consistent markers; note them in the style sheet.
3. Establish the dialect more heavily on first appearance, then lighten — readers keep hearing it.
4. Treat all characters equally: if the narrator and "standard" speakers aren't spelled
   phonetically, be cautious about spelling only some characters phonetically.
5. Consider code-switching: characters shift register by context; that can reveal a lot.
6. For communities not your own, use sensitivity / authenticity readers
   (see `creative-arts.literary-writing.developmental-editing` skills).

## Non-native speakers
- Show with a few authentic transfer features (word order, article use, idioms
  translated literally) rather than broken grammar throughout.
- Untranslated words: let context carry meaning; don't italicise by reflex (current
  practice in many publishers is to drop italics for non-English words).

## Verify
- [ ] Markers chosen from research, listed in the style sheet, used consistently.
- [ ] No eye dialect.
- [ ] Dialogue still reads at speed.
- [ ] A speaker of the variety (or sensitivity reader) has checked it, where possible.
