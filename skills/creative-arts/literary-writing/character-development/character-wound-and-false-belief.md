# The wound and the false belief

**Goal:** a specific past hurt (wound, or "ghost") that produced a specific mistaken belief
(the lie or misbelief), which in turn drives the character's present behaviour and the
inner conflict of the arc. The terms come from popular craft writing (K. M. Weiland, Lisa
Cron's *Story Genius*, Angela Ackerman and Becca Puglisi's *The Emotional Wound Thesaurus*).

## Build the chain
1. **Wound:** a concrete event, with a time and place ("Age 9, father left during her
   birthday party and never called"). Specific beats generic ("abandonment issues").
2. **Lesson drawn:** what the character concluded to protect themselves ("If I need
   people, they leave").
3. **False belief / lie:** state it as a rule ("Never depend on anyone").
4. **Present behaviour:** 3-5 habits the lie produces (refuses help, overworks, ends
   relationships first).
5. **Cost:** what the lie keeps from them now — this is what the plot will exploit.
6. **Truth needed:** the corrected belief the arc moves toward.

## On the page
- **Show behaviour first;** the reader sees the habits long before the cause.
- **Reveal the wound late and partially** — glimpses (an object, a flinch), then the
  full memory near the low point when it explains the choice they must make.
- **Plot pressure targets the lie:** design events that force the character to depend on
  someone.

## Gotchas
- **Trauma as decoration:** a dark past that never affects choices.
- **Opening with the wound** in a prologue or info-dump.
- **Choosing trauma for shock** — sensitivity to real experiences (abuse, war) matters;
  research and consider sensitivity readers.

## Verify
- [ ] Wound, lesson, lie, behaviours, cost and truth written as a chain.
- [ ] At least three scenes show behaviours before the wound is explained.
- [ ] Climax forces the character to act against the lie.
