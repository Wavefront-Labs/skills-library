# Flat (steadfast) character arc

**Goal:** a protagonist who doesn't fundamentally change but whose conviction is tested and
who changes the world or people around them. Common in series heroes (Sherlock Holmes,
James Bond), mentor-led stories, and stories like *Mary Poppins* or *Paddington 2*.
Terminology follows K. M. Weiland's arc framework; "flat" here is not Forster's flat character.

## Components
- **The truth the protagonist holds** ("people are basically good").
- **The world's lie:** the setting or other characters believe the opposite.
- **Supporting characters with positive arcs** — they are the ones who change.
- **The test:** pressure to abandon the truth increases until the climax.

## Structure
| Point | Beat |
|---|---|
| Opening | Protagonist's truth clear; world in conflict with it |
| First act turn | Protagonist commits to a fight that pits truth against lie |
| Midpoint | Truth seems to fail or costs a lot; doubts appear |
| Low point | Strongest temptation to give up the truth |
| Climax | Holds firm; the truth wins and changes others |
| Resolution | The world or key characters transformed |

## Making it dramatic
- **Doubt without conversion:** the protagonist wavers, but stands.
- **Cost:** holding the truth must cost (relationships, safety) or the arc is inert.
- **Show the change in others** with clear before/after scenes.

## Gotchas
- Mistaking a passive protagonist for a flat arc; a flat-arc character acts strongly.
- No supporting character changes — then there's no arc anywhere.
- Smugness: an unshakeable hero without doubt or loss reads as preachy.

## Verify
- [ ] Protagonist's truth and the world's lie stated.
- [ ] At least one supporting character has a visible positive arc.
- [ ] The climax tests the truth at real cost.
