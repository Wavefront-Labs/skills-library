# Positive change arc

**Goal:** a protagonist who starts with a mistaken belief and ends better, through change
the reader watches happen under pressure. Framework common in craft books (K. M. Weiland,
*Creating Character Arcs*, 2016; similar ideas in McKee and Truby).

## Components
- **The lie:** a false belief about self or world ("I'm only lovable if I'm useful").
- **The want:** the conscious goal the lie drives (be indispensable at work).
- **The need:** the truth that would fix them ("I'm worth loving as I am").
- **The ghost/wound:** the past event that made the lie reasonable.
- **The normal world:** shows the lie in action and its cost.

## Plot the arc against structure
| Point | Arc beat |
|---|---|
| Opening | Lie in action; character is getting by but paying for it |
| Inciting event | Life starts testing the lie |
| First act turn | Committed to the want, still under the lie |
| Midpoint | Glimpse of the truth; begins acting on it partially |
| Third act low point | Lie brings disaster; the character must choose |
| Climax | Acts on the truth at cost, defeating the antagonist (outer and inner) |
| Resolution | New normal shows the truth lived |

## Write it on the page
- **Choices, not epiphanies:** each stage is a decision showing which belief governs.
- **Relapse:** the character backslides before the climax; change should be hard.
- **Mirror characters** show what embracing or refusing the truth looks like.

## Gotchas
- **Epiphany by speech:** a mentor states the truth and the character simply adopts it.
- **Lie too abstract** ("fear") to dramatise; make it a specific sentence.
- Arc completes at the midpoint, leaving nothing for the climax to prove.

## Verify
- [ ] Lie, want, need and wound each written as one sentence.
- [ ] Every act turn tests the lie with a choice.
- [ ] The climax requires acting on the truth.
