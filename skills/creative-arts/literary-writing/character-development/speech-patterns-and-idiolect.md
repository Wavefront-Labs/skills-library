# Speech patterns and idiolect

**Goal:** readers can tell who is talking without tags. An **idiolect** is an
individual's personal way of speaking; in fiction it is built from choices on a few
dimensions and kept consistent.

## Build a speech profile (one card per major character)
| Dimension | Questions | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Vocabulary | Education, profession, region, era, slang? Latinate or Anglo-Saxon words? | A nurse says "obs", a lawyer says "notwithstanding" |
| Sentence length | Clipped fragments or long chains? | "Fine. Go." vs breathless run-ons |
| Formality/register | Contractions? Swearing? Titles? | "I do not believe so" vs "Nah" |
| Rhythm | Questions back? Lists? Trailing off? | Answers every question with a question |
| Topic gravity | What do they always steer toward? | Money, food, her son |
| Avoidance | What won't they say directly? How do they deflect? | Jokes when hurt; goes silent |
| Metaphor source | Where do their comparisons come from? | Farmer: weather and animals |

Derive each choice from **background** (class, region, job, age, first language) and
**psychology** (confidence, defensiveness, desire to impress).

## Procedure
1. Fill the profile for every speaking character with more than a few lines.
2. Write a short monologue for each on the same neutral prompt ("describe your
   kitchen"); they should sound unmistakably different.
3. Revise dialogue scene by scene against the profiles.
4. Apply the cover-the-tags test (see `the-cover-the-tags-voice-test`).

## Gotchas
- Differentiating only with dialect spelling or catchphrases — shallow and wearing
  (see `writing-dialect-and-accent` and `verbal-tics-and-signature-phrases`).
- All characters sharing the author's wit and vocabulary.
- Inconsistent idiolect without reason; under stress a character may change register —
  make that a deliberate signal.

## Verify
- [ ] A speech profile exists for each major character.
- [ ] Neutral-prompt monologues sound distinct.
- [ ] No two main characters share sentence length, formality and vocabulary all at once.
