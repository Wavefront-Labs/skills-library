# Character flaws and contradictions

**Goal:** characters who feel real because they're inconsistent in human ways and whose
flaws create story. E.M. Forster's test of a **round** character: capable of
surprising the reader in a convincing way.

## Flaws that matter
- A flaw must **cause plot trouble**: it leads to choices that make things worse.
  Clumsiness or being "too caring" are decorations, not flaws.
- Tie the flaw to the arc: the flaw often grows from a false belief (see
  `creative-arts.literary-writing.character-development.want-need-and-character-arc`).
- Aristotle's *hamartia* is an error of judgement, not necessarily a vice — a good
  person's mistake can be tragic.

## One root, two branches
Strongest characters have virtues and flaws from **the same root**:
| Root | Virtue | Flaw |
|---|---|---|
| Loyalty | Stands by friends | Covers up their crimes |
| Ambition | Drive, achievement | Ruthlessness |
| Caution | Prudence | Paralysis, missed chances |
| Honesty | Integrity | Cruelty |
This makes the flaw inseparable from who they are, and the arc costly.

## Contradictions
- Pair traits that seem opposed but coexist: a pacifist with a violent temper; a
  confident public speaker terrified of intimacy.
- **Reveal under pressure**: Burroway notes character is revealed through choices —
  put the contradiction in a scene where both sides want different actions.
- Explain little: let the reader hold the contradiction.

## Gotchas
- **Mary Sue / Gary Stu**: universally admired, instantly skilled, flaws that never cost anything.
- Flaws announced in narration but never acted on.
- Contradictions without any underlying logic read as inconsistency — know the
  root even if you don't state it.

## Verify
- [ ] Each major character has a flaw that causes at least one bad choice on the page.
- [ ] Main characters' virtue and flaw share a root.
- [ ] At least one scene dramatises a contradiction under pressure.
