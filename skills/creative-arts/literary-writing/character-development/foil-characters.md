# Foil characters

**Goal:** a character whose contrast with another (usually the protagonist) highlights
qualities, choices or themes. Classic examples: Laertes and Fortinbras as foils to Hamlet
(sons avenging fathers, acting decisively); Dr Watson to Holmes; Draco Malfoy to Harry.

## Design a foil
1. **Name the trait or choice** you want to illuminate (Hamlet's hesitation).
2. **Put the foil in similar circumstances** — same problem, same wound, same opportunity.
3. **Have them choose differently** (Laertes acts immediately). The similarity makes the
   difference meaningful.
4. **Decide the foil's relation:** ally, rival, friend, sibling or antagonist. A foil need
   not be an enemy.

## Stage the contrast
- **Paired scenes:** each faces the same test at different points.
- **Shared space:** put them side by side when the difference matters most.
- **Outcome contrast:** the foil's fate shows the cost or reward of the other path
  (a foil in a negative arc can show where the protagonist might end).

## Foil vs mirror vs antagonist
- **Foil:** contrast to illuminate.
- **Mirror:** shows what the protagonist might become (often a shadow).
- **Antagonist:** opposes goals. One character can be all three.

## Gotchas
- A foil so exaggerated (all good vs all bad) that the contrast becomes moralising.
- Foils with no story of their own — give them a goal.
- Stating the contrast in narration ("Unlike Ben, Tom never hesitated").

## Verify
- [ ] Trait or choice being illuminated is named.
- [ ] Foil faces a comparable situation and chooses differently.
- [ ] At least one paired scene stages the contrast without narration explaining it.
