# Non-human and societal antagonistic forces

**Goal:** a story where the main opposition is a force — nature, society, an institution,
technology, fate or the self — that still escalates and threatens like a strong antagonist.
These correspond to the classic conflict types: person vs nature, society, technology, self.

## Making a force behave like an antagonist
1. **Rules:** define how it works (the storm's path, the bureaucracy's procedures, the
   disease's stages). Consistent rules create credible threat.
2. **Escalation:** plan stages of increasing pressure — weather worsens, appeals fail,
   symptoms spread. Map them to act turns.
3. **Agency-like moves:** events that feel like counter-moves (the river rises after they
   cross; the rejection letter arrives just as they hope).
4. **No easy defeat:** the protagonist can't simply "kill" it; victory is survival,
   adaptation, escape or reform.

## Give it a human face
- **Society/institution:** represent it through specific people who enforce it — the
  officer, the clerk, the parent who echoes the town's view (*The Handmaid's Tale*; *One
  Flew Over the Cuckoo's Nest*'s Nurse Ratched as the institution's face).
- **Nature:** survival stories use companions whose choices create human conflict
  (*Into Thin Air*, *The Martian*).
- **Self:** addiction, fear, illness — externalise through choices and relapses; a
  human antagonist may embody the temptation.

## Theme
A force antagonist often carries theme (indifference of nature; injustice of systems).
Let events demonstrate it, not narration.

## Gotchas
- Static force: a blizzard that's the same on day five as day one.
- Abstract society with no individual faces.
- Victory by luck (rescue arrives) with no protagonist choice.

## Verify
- [ ] Force's rules and escalation stages listed.
- [ ] A human face represents a societal or institutional force.
- [ ] Climax is won (or lost) by protagonist choice.
