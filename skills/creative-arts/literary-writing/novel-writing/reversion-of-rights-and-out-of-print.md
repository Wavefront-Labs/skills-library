# Reversion of rights and out-of-print clauses

**Goal:** be able to get your book's rights back when the publisher stops actively
selling it — to reissue it, self-publish it, or license it elsewhere.

## Why old clauses fail
Traditional clauses define "out of print" as "not available for sale in any edition".
With ebooks and print-on-demand a book is technically always available, so the rights
never revert. Modern clauses need a **sales-based threshold**.

## What to negotiate at contract stage
- **Out-of-print definition by sales:** for example, fewer than a set number of copies
  (or a set royalty amount) across all formats in two consecutive royalty periods.
  Agents negotiate the number; the Authors Guild recommends sales-based tests.
- Formats that **do not** count as keeping the book in print: POD-only, ebook-only below
  threshold, licences to third parties with no sales.
- **Process:** author's written notice -> publisher has a limited time (often 6-9
  months) to reissue or put back above threshold -> otherwise rights revert automatically.
- Reversion of **unexploited sub-rights** separately (use-it-or-lose-it).
- Right to buy the cover art files / remaining stock at cost on reversion.

## Requesting reversion later
1. Check your royalty statements for the last two periods against the clause.
2. Send a dated written request citing the clause number.
3. Track the publisher's response deadline.
4. Get a **signed reversion letter** stating which rights revert and the effective date;
   keep it permanently (retailers and new publishers may ask for it).
5. Note what does not revert: the publisher's typesetting and cover design usually
   belong to them; you need new files to reissue.

## US statutory termination
Separately from the contract, US authors can terminate grants they signed on or after 1 January 1978
under 17 U.S.C. §203 (US law as read in October 2026; not for works made for hire; pre-1978 grants fall under §304 instead). The five-year window opens 35 years after signing, or, where the grant covers publication, at the earlier of 35 years after publication and 40 years after signing. Written notice is served 2 to 10 years before the effective date and recorded with the Copyright Office. Confirm against the statute and take legal advice before relying on it. The Authors
Alliance publishes guides on reversion and termination.

## Verify
- [ ] Contract defines out-of-print by sales, not availability.
- [ ] Notice-and-cure process and timeline stated.
- [ ] Reversion letter obtained and filed for any reverted title.
- [ ] Plan for new cover/interior files before reissuing.
