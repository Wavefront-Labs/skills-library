# Multiple and rotating viewpoint characters

**Goal:** several POV characters, each earning their place, rotated so readers always know
whose head they're in and why the story needs it.

## Decide whether you need it
Each POV character must:
- Have their **own goal and arc**, not just carry information.
- Be present at scenes nobody else can witness, or give a perspective that changes the
  meaning of events (dramatic irony between POVs).
Test: if you cut this POV, what would the reader lose? If "a few plot facts", deliver them
another way.

## Structure the rotation
- **Fixed rotation** (A, B, A, B) — common in romance (hero/heroine) and dual-timeline.
- **Weighted** — a main POV with occasional others (thrillers: villain chapters).
- **Free** — whoever is best placed for each scene (epic fantasy, *A Song of Ice and Fire*);
  needs a scene spreadsheet to keep balance.
- Keep **one POV per scene**; change POV at scene or chapter breaks.
- Track in a spreadsheet: scene, POV, word count, plotline. Check no POV vanishes for too
  long or dominates unintentionally.

## Orient readers
- Chapter headers with the POV name (common in genre fiction) or a strong first-line anchor:
  name the POV character in the first sentence and ground them in place.
- **Distinct voices:** vocabulary, sentence length, what they notice, inner preoccupation.
  Cover-the-name test: a page should be identifiable without the header.
- Choose consistent person/tense per POV (e.g., first person for one, third for another is
  possible but needs a reason).

## Gotchas
- **Too many POVs too early** — introduce them gradually; each new head costs the reader.
- **Retelling the same event** from two POVs without new information.
- **Lopsided appeal:** readers skip the weaker POV; give each a hook and stakes.
- Ending chapters on cliffhangers for every POV becomes mechanical.

## Verify
- [ ] Each POV has a goal, arc and unique access the story needs.
- [ ] One POV per scene; POV clear in the first sentence or header.
- [ ] Spreadsheet shows rotation balance and no long absences.
