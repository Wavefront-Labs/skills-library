# Advances, royalties and earning out

**Goal:** understand what a publishing offer is actually worth and what to negotiate.
Figures below are common US trade norms, not guarantees — confirm against the actual
contract and with your agent or the Authors Guild contract review.

## The advance
- An **advance against royalties**: money paid before sales, recouped from your royalties.
  Non-returnable unless you fail to deliver an acceptable manuscript.
- Paid in instalments: commonly on signing / delivery and acceptance / publication (and
  sometimes paperback publication). Check how "acceptance" is defined and whether a
  deadline applies to the publisher's decision.

## Royalties (typical starting points)
| Format | Common base |
|---|---|
| Hardcover | 10% of list price on first 5,000 copies, 12.5% next 5,000, 15% thereafter |
| Trade paperback | 7.5% of list (sometimes escalating) |
| Mass-market paperback | about 8-10% of list |
| Ebook | 25% of net receipts (the Authors Guild advocates higher) |
| Audio (publisher-produced) | often 25% of net |

- **List vs net:** "net receipts" is what the publisher receives after retailer discounts —
  a percentage of net can be about half the same percentage of list.
- **Deep-discount clauses** cut royalties when books sell at high discounts (often over
  50-55%); ask for limits.

## Earning out
- You earn out when cumulative royalties exceed the advance; after that, royalties are paid.
- Example: $20,000 advance; hardcover list $28 at 10% = $2.80/copy -> about 7,143
  hardcovers to earn out (ignoring other formats and sub-rights income credited to the account).
- **Joint accounting / cross-collateralisation:** in multi-book deals, advances on all
  books are recouped from earnings on all books. Ask for separate accounting.
- **Reserve against returns:** publishers withhold part of royalties because bookstores
  can return unsold copies. Ask for a cap (percentage) and a time limit (liquidated
  within a set number of periods).

## Statements
- Usually twice yearly, paid months after the period closes. Check audit rights
  (author's right to examine the publisher's books at the publisher's cost if errors exceed a threshold).

## Gotchas
- Comparing offers by advance alone; royalty bases, sub-rights splits and accounting matter.
- Not noticing that ebook royalties are on net while print is on list.
- Missing that most books never earn out — the advance may be all you receive.

## Verify
- [ ] Advance payout triggers and dates listed.
- [ ] Each format's royalty base (list or net) and escalators noted.
- [ ] Joint accounting, reserves and deep-discount terms checked.
- [ ] Break-even (earn-out) copies estimated.
