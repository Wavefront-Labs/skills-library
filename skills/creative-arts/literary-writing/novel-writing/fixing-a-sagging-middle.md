# Fixing a sagging middle

**Goal:** keep the long middle of a novel (roughly 25-75%) escalating. Middles sag
when the protagonist repeats the same kind of action against the same kind of obstacle
without the situation getting worse or changing shape.

## Diagnose
1. Reverse-outline the middle chapters: one line each, plus **what changed** by the end.
2. Mark any run of 3+ chapters where the answer is "nothing / more of the same".
3. Check the **midpoint** (around 50%): is there a reversal, revelation or
   commitment that turns the protagonist from reacting to acting, or raises the stakes
   from personal to larger? A missing midpoint is the most common cause.
4. Score the pressure (danger, deadline, emotional cost) per chapter on a 1-5 scale.
   A flat or falling line from 30-70% is the sag.

## Fixes (pick what the diagnosis points to)
- **Add or sharpen the midpoint:** a false victory or false defeat; a secret revealed;
  the stakes rise; the hunter becomes the hunted.
- **Change the type of obstacle**, not only its size: physical -> social -> moral.
- **Ticking clock:** introduce or tighten a deadline that already belongs to the story.
- **Collapse repetition:** three similar failed attempts -> one dramatised attempt plus
  a line of summary for the others (see `balancing-scene-and-summary`).
- **Promote a subplot:** let the B-story (often the relationship plot) carry the
  thematic argument through the middle and collide with the A-plot at the midpoint.
- **Cost every win:** each victory should open a new, worse problem ("yes, but").
- **Cut the waiting:** chapters where characters wait for information can usually go.

## Gotchas
- A random action set piece raises noise, not stakes: it must be caused by and cause
  other events.
- Moving the midpoint earlier often just moves the sag later; fix escalation after it too.
- Do not "fix" with more backstory; it slows things further.

## Verify
- [ ] Every middle chapter changes the situation (reverse-outline column filled).
- [ ] A clear midpoint shift exists near 50%.
- [ ] Pressure score rises (with brief dips for breathers) from 25% to the climax.
- [ ] Beta readers report no stretch in the middle where they put the book down.
