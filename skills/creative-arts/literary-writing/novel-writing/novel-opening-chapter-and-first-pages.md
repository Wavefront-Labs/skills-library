# The novel's opening chapter and first pages

**Goal:** first pages that make an agent, editor or reader keep going. Agents commonly
request the first 5-10 pages, or the first three chapters, with a query, so the opening
is judged before anything else.

## What the first pages must do
1. **A specific person** (name, body, a want) — not weather, not a crowd.
2. **Something already in motion** — tension, not a routine morning. It need not be the
   inciting incident; it must be a problem.
3. **Voice** — the narrative attitude that tells readers what kind of book this is.
4. **Orientation** — where and when, in passing, within the first page.
5. **A question** the reader wants answered by page 5.

## Procedure
1. Find where the story really starts: highlight the first moment in the draft where
   something changes. Many drafts start one to three chapters too early.
2. Cut or delay backstory: a sentence or two of the past on page one at most.
3. Rewrite the first line to carry voice and specificity: a concrete detail plus an
   attitude, and ideally a small disturbance ("The day my brother came home, the dog hid").
4. Make the last line of chapter one hook (see `chapter-endings-and-chapter-breaks`).
5. Cold-read test: give pages 1-5 to readers who know nothing; ask who it is about, what
   they want, what is wrong, and what they think happens next.

## Openings agents commonly reject
- Waking up / alarm clocks; describing oneself in a mirror.
- Dream openings that turn out not to be real.
- Weather or landscape with no character.
- Prologues of disembodied action unrelated to chapter one (if a prologue is
  essential, keep it short and make chapter one hook as well).
- Info-dumps on history or magic systems.
- Ungrounded dialogue: no idea who is speaking or where.

## Gotchas
- "Start with action" does not mean start with a fight; readers have no reason to care
  yet. Start with tension attached to a person.
- An opening that misleads about genre (cosy opening to a horror novel) loses the right readers.

## Verify
- [ ] Protagonist named and wanting something on page one.
- [ ] A problem or tension present by the end of page one.
- [ ] Under three sentences of backstory in the first five pages.
- [ ] Cold readers can state the central question after five pages.
