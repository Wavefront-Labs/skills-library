# Fixing head-hopping and POV slips

**Goal:** scenes that stay in one character's perception (in close third or first person),
so readers bond with that character and never feel jerked between minds.

## What counts as a slip
- **Head-hopping:** thoughts or feelings of two characters in the same scene without a
  deliberate omniscient narrator. "Tom thought she was lying. Ana wondered if he knew."
- **Knowledge slip:** the POV character "knows" what they can't — another's motive, events
  offstage, their own face ("her cheeks flushed pink" from inside her POV).
- **Perception slip:** describing what's behind the POV character, or what happens after
  they leave the room.
- **Voice slip:** vocabulary the POV character wouldn't use (a child thinking "ambivalent").

## Find them
1. Pick the scene's POV character; write their name at the top.
2. Highlight every verb of thought, feeling or perception (*thought, wondered, knew, felt,
   realised, saw, heard*) and every emotion attributed to anyone.
3. For each, ask: whose? Can the POV character perceive or infer it?
4. Check physical descriptions of the POV character: only what they could feel or see.

## Fix
- **Convert to observable behaviour:** "Ana wondered if he knew" -> Tom's POV: "Ana
  wouldn't meet his eyes."
- **Convert to inference:** "She must have known."
- **Feel from inside:** "her cheeks flushed pink" -> "heat climbed her face".
- **Move the switch** to a scene break (white space, `#`) if the other POV is needed.
- **Cut** information the POV character can't have; deliver it later in another way.

## Gotchas
- Fixing slips by adding "she thought he must be..." to every line — makes prose clunky;
  prefer showing behaviour.
- Mistaking legitimate omniscient narration (with its own voice) for head-hopping;
  check which mode the book intends.

## Verify
- [ ] Each scene labelled with one POV owner.
- [ ] No other character's thoughts stated as fact.
- [ ] POV character's appearance described only from inside.
- [ ] All POV changes happen at scene or chapter breaks.
