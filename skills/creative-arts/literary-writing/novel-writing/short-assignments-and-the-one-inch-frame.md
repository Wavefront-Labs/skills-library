# Short assignments and the one-inch picture frame

**Goal:** keep writing when the whole book feels impossible, by shrinking the day's task to
something finishable. From Anne Lamott's *Bird by Bird*: she keeps a one-inch picture frame
on her desk to remind her that today she only has to write what she can see through it; the
title comes from her father telling her brother, overwhelmed by a school report on birds,
to take it "bird by bird".

## Method
1. **Pick a one-inch subject** before you sit down: one scene, one character's entrance, one
   room, one memory, the first paragraph of the fight. Not "chapter 7".
2. **Write it down in one line** at the top of the page: "Today: Mara finds the letter in the
   coat pocket."
3. Write only that, fully — sensory detail, dialogue, what she feels.
4. **Stop** when it's done, even if time remains, or pick the next one-inch subject.
5. Log the assignment in your scene list. Over weeks the frames tile into a book.

## Choosing frames when stuck
- What does one character see from one window at one moment?
- The smallest event that must happen next for the plot to move.
- A memory the narrator would rather not have.
- A list: everything in the protagonist's pockets, kitchen drawer, search history.

## Why it works
Big goals feel like judgements on your worth; small ones feel like tasks. A finished small
piece gives momentum, and specificity (one room, one moment) produces better prose than
summary of a whole arc.

## Gotchas
- Frames that are still too big ("the wedding"). Shrink until you can see it.
- Frames that never connect: keep the scene list so the pieces add up to a plot.

## Verify
- [ ] Today's assignment written as one concrete sentence before starting.
- [ ] Assignment completed or explicitly carried over.
- [ ] Scene list updated.
