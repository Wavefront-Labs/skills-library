# Writing a novel synopsis

**Goal:** a summary that proves the book's story works — causally, emotionally and to
the end. Unlike the query, the synopsis **reveals the ending**.

## Format conventions
- Length: follow guidelines; if none, one page single-spaced (about 500-700 words) is the
  most widely accepted. Some ask for two pages, or 2-5 double-spaced.
- Present tense, third person, regardless of the novel's POV and tense.
- Common convention: CAPITALISE each character's name on first mention.
- Header: title, genre, word count, author name.

## Procedure
1. List the turning points from your outline: inciting incident, the protagonist's
   first commitment, midpoint, the dark moment / crisis, climax, resolution.
2. Write one short paragraph per turning point, connecting them with **because / so /
   but** (causality, not "and then").
3. Include the protagonist's internal arc: what they believe at the start, and how the
   climax forces a change.
4. Keep only 3-5 named characters; describe the rest by role ("her editor").
5. State the ending plainly in the last paragraph, including how the main
   relationships end.
6. Cut subplots that do not affect the main arc; one sentence for any that do.

## Gotchas
- Teasing ("Will she survive? Read to find out") — agents want the answer.
- Listing every scene; the synopsis is the spine, not the skeleton.
- Back-cover copy voice; a synopsis is clear and plain, with a little of the book's tone.
- Worldbuilding detail in SFF synopses: include only rules that drive the plot.

## Verify
- [ ] Within the requested length.
- [ ] All major turning points present and linked causally.
- [ ] Ending revealed; protagonist's change stated.
- [ ] No more than five named characters.
- [ ] Someone who has not read the book can retell the plot from it.
