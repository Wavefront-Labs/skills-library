# Knowing when the manuscript is done

**Goal:** stop revising at the right time — not before the book is ready, not years
after. Lamott (*Bird by Bird*, "How Do You Know When You're Done?") compares it to
putting an octopus to bed: you keep tucking in arms; at some point you stop.

## Signs you're close
- **Diminishing returns:** changes swap one acceptable option for another (comma in,
  comma out) rather than fixing problems.
- **Feedback converges:** recent readers' notes are small and disagree with each other,
  rather than repeating the same big issue.
- **You can state the book:** a one-sentence pitch and a one-page synopsis come easily.
- A full read in a few sittings without wanting to restructure.

## Signs you're not
- Two or more readers independently flag the same structural problem.
- You're avoiding a known issue ("the middle is slow, but...").
- You haven't done a full read since the last big structural change.
- The draft is far outside genre length (see `word-count-targets-by-genre`).

## Readiness checklist
- [ ] Structural revision done and validated by at least two readers.
- [ ] Self-editing checklist completed (see `self-editing-checklist`).
- [ ] Opening pages tested on cold readers.
- [ ] Query and synopsis written — if you can't write them, the book may not be clear yet.
- [ ] A rest period (a few weeks) followed by one final read.

## Perfectionism
- Set a decision rule before the final pass: e.g., "After this pass, I submit to my first
  batch of agents."
- Query in batches: early feedback (requests or not) tells you more than another solo pass
  (see `agent-research-and-agent-list`).
- Start the next book: distance makes the last one clearer and reduces the urge to
  tinker forever.

## Verify
- [ ] Checklist above complete.
- [ ] Latest feedback is small and scattered, not convergent.
- [ ] Decision rule set and followed.
