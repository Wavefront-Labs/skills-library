# Diagnosing and getting past writer's block

**Goal:** identify why you're stuck, because "block" is a symptom with several causes and
each has a different fix.

## Diagnose: ask in this order
| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Can write other things, not this scene | **Story problem** — the scene is wrong, unmotivated or unnecessary | Go back to the last scene that felt alive; the wrong turn is usually just after it. Ask what the character wants now. Try the scene from another POV, or skip it |
| Write a sentence, delete it, repeat | **Perfectionism / inner critic** | Lower the bar: freewrite badly for 10 minutes, `[TK]` brackets, drafting-only rule |
| Nothing comes for anything; tired, flat | **Depletion** (sleep, illness, stress, grief) | Rest is the fix; keep a tiny floor (5 minutes) or pause deliberately with a restart date |
| Dread after sharing work or a rejection | **Fear of judgement** | Stop showing drafts; write a "private draft" nobody will read |
| Don't know what happens | **Not enough material** | Research, notes, outlining, interviewing your character, reading; Lamott: "fill the well" |
| Avoiding the desk entirely | **Habit collapse** | Restart routine with a smaller floor; fixed time |

## Tactics that work across causes
- **Change the input:** longhand, a different room, dictation, a new document.
- **Write around it:** jump to a scene you're excited about; come back later.
- **Talk it out:** explain the plot problem aloud to someone (or to a voice memo). The
  sentence "the problem is..." often contains the answer.
- **List 20 things that could happen next** — the first 5 are clichés, the useful ones come later.

## Gotchas
- Pushing through a **plot problem** with willpower produces pages you'll cut.
- Treating **burnout or depression** as a craft issue — if flatness spreads to the rest of
  life, that is a health matter.

## Verify
- [ ] Cause named from the table before choosing a fix.
- [ ] For a story problem: the wrong turn located and a new option chosen.
- [ ] A restart date or a minimum floor set.
