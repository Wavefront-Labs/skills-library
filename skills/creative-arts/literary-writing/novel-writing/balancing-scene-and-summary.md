# Balancing scene and summary

**Goal:** spend page-time in proportion to importance. A *scene* plays an event in
real time (moment by moment, usually with dialogue); *summary* (narrative summary)
compresses time ("For three winters she worked the docks"). Burroway: scene is the
fundamental unit of fiction, summary is the connective tissue. A novel needs both.

## The importance test
For each event in the reverse outline (see `creative-arts.literary-writing.novel-writing.drafting-and-revising-a-novel`) ask:
1. **Does something change** (a decision, a revelation, a power shift)? -> scene.
2. **Is it routine, repeated or travel?** -> summary ("The drive took six hours").
3. **Does the reader need to watch the character do it to believe it?** -> scene.
4. **Is it backstory the reader needs only the result of?** -> one line of summary.

## Writing summary that moves
- Keep summary specific: one concrete detail per paragraph ("every morning the same
  burnt toast") stops summary from going grey.
- Use **iterative** summary ("Every Sunday he would...") for habits, then drop into a
  single scene ("But that Sunday...") — the classic summary-to-scene pivot.
- Summary is where narrative voice shows most; let it have opinions.

## Transitions
- Scene to summary: end the scene on its turn, then a white-space break or a
  time-cue ("By March...").
- Summary to scene: anchor time and place in the first sentence of the scene, then
  a sensory detail, then the first line of action or dialogue.

## Diagnosing proportion (Browne & King, "Proportion")
| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Beta readers skim | Routine events dramatised | Collapse to summary |
| "I didn't feel it" at a key moment | Key turn summarised | Expand to scene, slow down |
| Chapter reads like a synopsis | All summary | Pick the 1-2 turning moments, dramatise them |
| Writer's pet topic gets pages | Proportion signals false importance | Cut to match its weight in the plot |

Readers read page-count as importance: three pages on the hero's breakfast promise the
breakfast matters.

## Gotchas
- Dramatising arrivals and departures ("She parked. She walked in.") — start the scene late.
- Summarising the climax because it is hard to write: the climax must be scene.
- Long summary of backstory in chapter one — delay it.

## Verify
- [ ] Every turning point in the outline is played as a scene.
- [ ] No scene exists whose only content is routine or travel.
- [ ] Summary passages carry at least one concrete detail each.
- [ ] Page-count of each thread roughly matches its weight in the plot.
