# Freewriting and generative exercises

**Goal:** produce material without judgement, then harvest it. Peter Elbow (*Writing
Without Teachers*, 1973) popularised timed nonstop freewriting; Natalie Goldberg (*Writing
Down the Bones*, 1986) gave "rules of writing practice": keep your hand moving, don't cross
out, don't worry about spelling, lose control, go for the jugular.

## Core freewrite
1. Set a timer (10 minutes to start).
2. Write without stopping. If stuck, write "I don't know what to write" until something comes.
3. No deleting, no rereading until the timer ends.
4. Afterwards, underline anything alive: an image, a line of dialogue, a surprising thought.

## Variants
- **Focused freewrite:** start from a prompt tied to the book ("The night before she left,
  the kitchen...").
- **Looping (Elbow):** freewrite, pick the best sentence ("centre of gravity"), use it as the
  start of the next freewrite; repeat 3 times to drill into a topic.
- **Morning pages (Julia Cameron, *The Artist's Way*):** three longhand pages on waking, not
  for the book — clears noise.
- **"I remember" lists** (after Joe Brainard): each line starts "I remember..." — strong for
  memoir and backstory.
- **Character freewrite:** in the character's voice, answering "What I never told anyone is...".
- **Object or photo prompts:** describe it for 5 minutes, then write what happened just
  before the photo was taken.

## Harvesting
- Keep freewrites in one dated file or notebook; skim weekly.
- Copy keepers into a "salvage" document by project and tag them (scene idea, line, image).

## Gotchas
- Editing during the timer defeats the exercise.
- Expecting finished prose; the yield is usually a few lines per session.
- Using freewriting forever instead of drafting — it feeds the draft, doesn't replace it.

## Verify
- [ ] Timer used; no deletion during the run.
- [ ] Keepers underlined and copied to a salvage file.
- [ ] At least one item fed into the current draft or plan.
