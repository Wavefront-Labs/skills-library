# Building a daily writing routine

**Goal:** a habit that produces pages on ordinary days, not just inspired ones, and that
survives bad weeks.

## Design the routine
1. **Same time, same place** where possible. The cue does half the work; many writers use
   early morning before email, or a fixed lunch hour.
2. **Set a floor, not a ceiling.** A minimum you can hit on your worst day — 15 minutes, or
   250-300 words (roughly one manuscript page). Exceeding it is a bonus; missing it breaks
   the chain.
3. **Measure output in words or time, honestly.** Drafting: words. Revision: time or
   scenes, because revision often reduces the count.
4. **Log it.** Date, start/end time, words, one line on what happened. A spreadsheet or
   Scrivener's session target shows trends (best time of day, weekday vs weekend).
5. **Protect the slot:** phone in another room, notifications off, internet blocked if
   needed (Freedom, Cold Turkey, or simply a laptop without Wi-Fi).

## Arithmetic for planning
- An 80,000-word draft at 500 words/day, 5 days a week = 32 weeks.
- At 1,000 words/day every day = under 3 months.
- NaNoWriMo-style sprints (50,000 words in 30 days = 1,667/day) work for some, but plan the
  recovery month after.

## When the routine breaks
- Miss one day: resume the next with the floor, not double. Catch-up targets kill habits.
- Miss a week: restart with a smaller floor for a week.
- Track **streaks of showing up**, not word totals, during hard periods.

## Gotchas
- Waiting for a long uninterrupted block — most books are written in short daily ones.
- A routine copied from a famous writer who had no day job or children.
- Counting research, outlining or social media "writing" as drafting time.

## Verify
- [ ] A fixed slot and a minimum written down.
- [ ] Log kept for at least two weeks; average words/session known.
- [ ] Draft deadline computed from the real average, not the hope.
