# First-person narration

**Goal:** a first-person narrator whose voice carries the book, with a clear sense of when
and why they're telling it and strict limits on what they can know.

## Decide the narrating stance
- **Retrospective** (past tense, older self looking back — *Great Expectations*, *Jane
  Eyre*): allows reflection, foreshadowing ("I didn't know then..."), irony between the
  younger and older self. Decide how far back, and whether the narrator knows the ending.
- **Immediate** (often present tense — *The Hunger Games*): no hindsight, high suspense;
  the narrator can't reflect beyond the moment.
- **Peripheral narrator:** "I" observes someone else's story (Nick in *The Great Gatsby*).
  Decide why this witness tells it.
- Optional: an occasion or audience ("I'm writing this for my daughter") — sharpens voice.

## Knowledge limits
- The narrator knows only what they saw, heard, were told, or infer. Other characters'
  thoughts appear only as guesses ("She must have known").
- Scenes they weren't present at need a source: a letter, a confession, gossip — or be cut.
- Retrospective narrators may know more later; be consistent about what they reveal when.

## Voice
- Diction, rhythm and what they notice characterise them more than self-description.
- What they **won't** say or misjudge creates dramatic irony (see unreliable narration).
- Avoid "mirror" self-description (looking in a mirror to describe themselves).

## Line-level fixes
- **I-stacking:** many sentences opening "I" — vary syntax, start with the thing observed.
- **Filter verbs** ("I saw", "I felt", "I noticed") — render the perception directly:
  "The door was open", not "I saw that the door was open".
- Keep reported interior emotion specific, not labels.

## Gotchas
- The narrator describing their own face, or narrating scenes they couldn't witness.
- A retrospective narrator who never uses hindsight (wasted potential) or uses it to spoil
  every surprise.
- Voice so mannered it tires readers over 300 pages; test a chapter read aloud.

## Verify
- [ ] Stance (retrospective/immediate) and telling-time decided.
- [ ] Every scene has a way the narrator knows it.
- [ ] No more than an occasional filter verb or "I" opener per paragraph.
