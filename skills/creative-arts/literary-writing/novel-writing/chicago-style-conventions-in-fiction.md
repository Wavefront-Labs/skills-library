# Chicago style conventions in fiction

**Goal:** a manuscript that already follows the house style most US trade publishers
use — *The Chicago Manual of Style* (CMOS) with Merriam-Webster as the dictionary — so
copyediting focuses on substance. Section numbers change between editions; look up
specifics in the current edition (CMOS 18th ed., 2024).

## Numbers
- In fiction, **spell out** whole numbers zero through one hundred and round numbers
  (two hundred, three thousand) — CMOS's general (nontechnical) rule.
- Times: "four o'clock", "half past six"; numerals acceptable with a.m./p.m. ("4:30 p.m.").
- Years in numerals ("1987"); decades "the 1980s" or "the eighties".
- Dialogue: spell out almost everything, as spoken.

## Dashes and ellipses
- **Em dash** (—) for interruptions and breaks, closed up (no spaces) in Chicago style:
  "I was going to—" / "Don't."
- **En dash** (–) for ranges (pages 10–12); use "to" in running prose.
- **Ellipsis** (…) for trailing off; Chicago uses three spaced periods (". . .") in
  formal style, but many publishers accept the single ellipsis character — follow house style.
- Faltering speech: ellipsis; interrupted speech: em dash.

## Italics
- Titles of books, films, ships; emphasis (sparingly); non-English words that are not
  in Merriam-Webster (though many fiction publishers now avoid italicising them);
  internal direct thought if that convention is chosen (see
  `creative-arts.literary-writing.prose-style.rendering-direct-thought`).

## Capitalisation
- Kinship terms capitalised when used as names ("Mom said"), lowercase with a possessive
  ("my mom said").
- Titles before names capitalised ("General Lee"), lowercase alone ("the general").

## Possessives and punctuation
- Chicago: add 's to singular names ending in s ("James's"); plural possessive "the
  Joneses' house".
- Serial (Oxford) comma: yes.
- Commas in direct address: "Thanks, Mom."
- Dialogue punctuation inside quotation marks (US).

## Style sheet
Record your choices (numbers, spelling variants, italics rule, invented words) in a
style sheet for the copyeditor.

## Verify
- [ ] Numbers spelled out per Chicago's fiction norm.
- [ ] Em dashes closed up; ellipsis convention consistent.
- [ ] Kinship and title capitalisation consistent.
- [ ] Style sheet records all choices.
