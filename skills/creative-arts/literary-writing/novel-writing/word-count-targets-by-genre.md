# Word-count targets by genre

**Goal:** keep a debut manuscript within the length range agents and editors expect for
its category, because length affects printing cost, price point and perceived
readiness. Figures below are **commonly cited industry guidelines**, not rules; check
current agent guidance and comparable recent debuts.

## Commonly cited ranges (debut adult and children's fiction)
| Category / genre | Typical range (words) |
|---|---|
| Adult literary / upmarket | ~70,000-100,000 |
| Adult commercial / general | ~80,000-100,000 |
| Mystery / thriller / suspense | ~70,000-100,000 (cozies often shorter) |
| Romance (single-title) | ~70,000-100,000; category romance lines set their own lengths |
| Science fiction / fantasy | ~90,000-120,000 (epic fantasy can run higher; debuts much above ~120k-130k are a harder sell) |
| Horror | ~70,000-90,000 |
| Historical fiction | ~80,000-110,000 |
| Young adult | ~55,000-90,000 (fantasy at the upper end) |
| Middle grade | ~25,000-55,000 |
| Novella | ~20,000-40,000 |

## Why it matters
- Too long: higher print cost, harder to price; signals possible lack of editing.
- Too short: may read as underdeveloped or fall into a hard-to-sell gap.
- Established authors get more latitude than debuts.

## Counting and reporting
- Use the word processor count of the manuscript text (exclude front/back matter).
- In queries, **round** to the nearest thousand ("complete at 92,000 words").
- Traditional "page x 250 words" estimates are obsolete for submissions; use actual counts.

## If you're outside the range
- Over: see `cutting-a-manuscript-to-target-length`; consider whether it's two books.
- Under: check for thin subplots, summarised scenes that should be dramatised, or a
  category mismatch (it may be YA or a novella).

## Verify
- [ ] Manuscript category and genre identified honestly.
- [ ] Word count within the range or a deliberate, justifiable exception.
- [ ] Count rounded in the query.
