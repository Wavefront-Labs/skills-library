# Writing a query letter (fiction)

**Goal:** a 250-350 word email that makes an agent request pages. The query sells the
*story*, not the writing process or the author's hopes.

## Structure (one page, in this order or with the metadata first)
1. **Salutation:** "Dear Ms./Mr./Mx. Surname" — correct name, never "Dear Agent".
2. **Optional personal line (one sentence):** why this agent — a client book, a stated
   wish-list item, an interview. Skip if it would be generic.
3. **The pitch (150-200 words, present tense, third person):**
   - Who the protagonist is and what is specific about them.
   - The inciting situation.
   - What they want and what stands in the way.
   - The choice or stakes — what they will lose. End on the dilemma, not the ending.
4. **Metadata:** *TITLE* (caps is the convention), genre/category, complete word count
   rounded to the nearest thousand, and 1-2 comp titles (see
   `choosing-comp-titles`). Example: "TITLE is an adult literary mystery complete at
   92,000 words. It will appeal to readers of X and Y."
5. **Bio (2-4 sentences):** relevant publications, expertise or lived experience that
   bears on the book; if none, one plain sentence about yourself or omit.
6. **Close:** "Thank you for your time and consideration." Then name and contact.
   Paste requested pages below the signature as plain text, per the agent's guidelines.

## Writing the pitch
- Lead with character + situation, not a rhetorical question ("What if...?").
- Name at most 3 characters; more blurs.
- Concrete beats abstract: "her brother's debt to a loan shark" beats "dark secrets".
- Voice: let the book's tone show (a comic novel gets a funny query).
- Test: a stranger should be able to say what the book is about after one read.

## Gotchas
- Querying an unfinished novel (debut fiction must be complete and revised).
- Word counts far outside genre norms (see `word-count-targets-by-genre`) are an
  immediate pass for many agents.
- Attachments when guidelines say paste; multiple agents in To/CC.
- Self-praise ("a gripping page-turner"), mentions of film adaptation, or saying
  friends loved it.
- Revealing the ending belongs in the synopsis, not the query.

## Verify
- [ ] Under 350 words; one screen.
- [ ] Protagonist, want, obstacle and stakes all identifiable.
- [ ] Title, genre, word count and comps present.
- [ ] Follows this agent's posted guidelines (pages, synopsis, form vs email).
- [ ] Read aloud once; no typos in the agent's name.
