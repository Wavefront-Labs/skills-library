# Writing the fast, messy first draft

**Goal:** a complete draft, start to finish, as quickly as you can sustain, accepting that
most of it will be rewritten. Anne Lamott (*Bird by Bird*, "Shitty First Drafts") calls it the
**down draft** — you just get it down; the **up draft** fixes it up; a final **dental draft**
checks every tooth.

## Rules for the down draft
1. **Forward only.** No rereading yesterday's chapter except the last paragraph or two to
   pick up the voice. Revising backwards is the main reason first drafts never end.
2. **Bracket, don't stop.** Missing fact, name or scene: write `[TK: name of the ship]` or
   `[SCENE: they argue about money]` and keep going. "TK" (to come) is the publishing
   placeholder; it's searchable later because the letter pair rarely appears in English.
3. **Change course in a note, not in the text.** If you decide in chapter 14 that the sister
   is really a brother, write `[NOTE: from here Ana is Andrés; fix earlier]` and continue as
   if it were always so.
4. **Let it be long, flat and wrong.** Overwriting is fine; it's easier to cut than to invent.
5. **Name the critic.** Lamott's trick: picture the voices (parent, teacher, rival) as mice,
   put them in a jar, turn the volume down. Practically: a sticky note "Drafting. Not editing."

## Keep momentum
- Stop mid-scene, ideally mid-sentence, when you know what happens next (Hemingway's
  advice), so tomorrow starts in motion.
- Keep a running **scene list** (one line per scene) beside the draft so you can see the
  shape without rereading.
- A rough outline helps; a broken outline is information — note where the draft departs.

## When to stop drafting
When you reach "The End" — even if the last third is summary and brackets. A finished bad
draft can be revised; a polished first act cannot.

## Gotchas
- **Polishing chapter one for months** — the opening is the part most likely to be cut.
- **Sharing too early:** feedback on a half-draft restarts the critic. Wait until it's whole.
- **Treating the mess as the verdict.** The first draft measures nothing about the book.

## Verify
- [ ] No backward revision sessions since drafting began (only notes).
- [ ] All gaps marked with searchable placeholders (TK / NOTE).
- [ ] Draft reaches an ending, however rough.
