# Rights grants and subsidiary rights

**Goal:** know exactly which rights you are licensing to the publisher, for where and
for how long, and what you keep. The grant clause defines everything else in the contract.

## Primary rights
- The publisher's right to publish the work in book form: hardcover, paperback, ebook.
- **Territory:** World; World English; North America (US, Canada, Philippines often); UK
  and Commonwealth. Agents often sell North American rights and sell UK and translation
  rights separately for more total income.
- **Language:** English only vs all languages.
- **Term:** typically the full term of copyright, ended only by the reversion clause
  (see `reversion-of-rights-and-out-of-print`).

## Subsidiary rights (licensed onward by whoever controls them)
| Right | Who often controls | Typical split if publisher controls |
|---|---|---|
| Translation | Agent (sells via co-agents) or publisher | ~75/25 or 80/20 author/publisher |
| UK/Commonwealth | Agent if not World English | — |
| Audio | Publisher increasingly; agent may retain | 50/50 if licensed to third party |
| First serial (excerpts before publication) | Agent or publisher | ~90/10 author |
| Second serial (after publication) | Publisher | ~50/50 |
| Book club, large print, anthology | Publisher | ~50/50 |
| Film/TV/dramatic, merchandising | Agent / author retains | — |

Sub-rights income paid to the publisher is credited to your royalty account and helps
earn out the advance; income from rights you retain comes to you (minus agent
commission) immediately.

## Negotiating points
- Retain film/TV/dramatic and merchandising rights.
- Retain translation and audio if your agent has strong co-agents or audio contacts.
- If the publisher takes a right, ask for a **use-it-or-lose-it** provision: rights not
  exploited within a set period revert to you.
- Watch catch-all grants ("all rights now known or hereafter devised"); narrow them.

## Gotchas
- Granting World rights in all languages for a modest advance.
- Not checking whether "electronic rights" covers enhanced/interactive versions or adaptations.
- Forgetting that a retained right still needs someone to sell it.

## Verify
- [ ] Territory, language and formats listed in plain terms.
- [ ] Each sub-right marked: granted (with split) or retained.
- [ ] Film/TV and merchandising retained, or deliberately granted.
- [ ] Use-it-or-lose-it terms on granted but unexploited rights.
