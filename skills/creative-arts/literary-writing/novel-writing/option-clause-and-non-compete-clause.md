# Option and non-compete clauses

**Goal:** keep these clauses from tying up your next books or blocking you from writing.
Both are standard in trade contracts; both are negotiable. The Authors Guild model
contract and contract guide discuss recommended narrowing language.

## Option clause (the publisher's right to consider your next book)
Problems to look for and the usual fix:
| Broad version | Narrower version to ask for |
|---|---|
| "Next work" of any kind | Next **book-length work of fiction in the same genre/series** |
| Exclusive period starts after publication of the current book | Starts on **submission of a proposal or partial**, or on acceptance of the current manuscript |
| Long exclusive window (90+ days) | **30-60 days** |
| Requires a full manuscript | A proposal / synopsis + sample chapters |
| Matching right ("last look": you must let them match any other offer) | **Strike matching rights**; first look only |
| Option on "same terms" as current contract | Strike; terms to be negotiated in good faith |

## Non-compete clause
- Purpose: stop you publishing a book that would directly hurt sales of this one.
- Risk: broad versions forbid any book "on the same subject" or "likely to compete",
  which can block a whole career in one genre.
- Ask for:
  - a narrow definition: a book on the **same specific subject, for the same audience,
    that would directly compete**;
  - a time limit (for example until a set period after publication);
  - explicit carve-outs for short stories, articles, self-published work and other
    genres or pen names as relevant.

## Gotchas
- An option that never triggers because it waits on a publication date years away.
- Non-competes that cover pen names or all genres.
- Assuming "standard boilerplate" means non-negotiable — agents routinely amend these.

## Verify
- [ ] Option scope limited by form and genre.
- [ ] Option window short and triggered early; no matching rights.
- [ ] Non-compete defines competing work narrowly with a time limit.
- [ ] Planned future projects checked against both clauses.
