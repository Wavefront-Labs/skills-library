# Motivation-reaction units

**Goal:** make moment-to-moment prose read as clear cause and effect. Dwight Swain
(*Techniques of the Selling Writer*) broke scenes into **motivation-reaction units (MRUs)**:
an external stimulus, then the viewpoint character's response.

## The pattern
1. **Motivating stimulus** — something external and objectively observable, in its own
   paragraph: *The door slammed open. A man with a shotgun filled the frame.*
2. **Character reaction**, in its own paragraph, in this order (skip parts, but don't reorder):
   - **Feeling** (involuntary, immediate): *Fear jolted through her.*
   - **Reflex** (physical, instant): *She flinched back against the counter.*
   - **Rational action and speech** (deliberate): *She raised her hands. "The money's in the drawer."*
3. The reaction becomes the next motivating stimulus for another character, and so on.

## Why the order matters
- Real perception goes stimulus -> feeling -> reflex -> thought/action. Reversing it
  ("She screamed as the door burst open") makes readers process the effect before the
  cause and blurs suspense.
- Separating stimulus and reaction into different paragraphs lets readers feel the beat.

## Using MRUs in revision
- For a confusing action passage, label each sentence S (stimulus) or R (reaction) and
  F/Rx/A (feeling/reflex/action). Reorder anything out of sequence.
- Cut reactions that don't follow from a shown stimulus (unmotivated emotion).
- Add a missing stimulus when a character reacts to something the reader never saw.
- In fast scenes, compress to stimulus + action; in emotional scenes, linger on feeling.

## Gotchas
- Applied mechanically to every beat, MRUs produce stilted prose; use them mainly to
  diagnose clarity problems in action and emotional peaks.
- "As" and "-ing" constructions often hide reversed order (see the `prose-style`
  line-editing skills).

## Verify
- [ ] In key action passages, stimulus always precedes reaction.
- [ ] Reactions follow feeling -> reflex -> action/speech order.
- [ ] Every emotional reaction has a visible cause.
