# The hero's journey

**Goal:** use Joseph Campbell's monomyth (*The Hero with a Thousand Faces*, 1949) and
Christopher Vogler's screenwriting adaptation (*The Writer's Journey*) as a lens for
quest and transformation stories — to find missing beats, not to fill in a form.

## Vogler's 12 stages (in three acts)
**Departure**
1. **Ordinary World** — the hero's normal life and lack.
2. **Call to Adventure** — a problem or opportunity.
3. **Refusal of the Call** — fear or obligation.
4. **Meeting the Mentor** — advice, gift, training.
5. **Crossing the Threshold** — commitment into the special world (end of act one).
**Initiation**
6. **Tests, Allies, Enemies** — learning the special world's rules.
7. **Approach to the Inmost Cave** — preparation for the central ordeal.
8. **Ordeal** — death-and-rebirth crisis (often the midpoint).
9. **Reward (Seizing the Sword)** — gains something.
**Return**
10. **The Road Back** — consequences; recommitment.
11. **Resurrection** — the final, highest-stakes test; the transformed hero acts.
12. **Return with the Elixir** — back to the ordinary world, changed, with something to share.

## Archetypes (functions, not fixed characters)
Hero, Mentor, Threshold Guardian, Herald, Shapeshifter, Shadow, Ally, Trickster.
One character can wear several masks over a story.

## Using it
1. Map your outline against the stages; note missing or doubled ones.
2. Ask whether a missing stage is a weakness (no clear threshold = the protagonist never
   commits) or a deliberate choice.
3. Internalise: many stages work as psychological beats in literary fiction (the
   "special world" can be a new job or a grief).

## Limits and critiques
- Campbell's model generalises from selected myths and centres a particular (often male,
  individual) hero pattern; many traditions and stories don't follow it.
- Applied rigidly, it produces formulaic plots. Alternatives:
  `kishotenketsu-four-part-structure`, three-act, or Freytag models.

## Verify
- [ ] Outline mapped against the 12 stages.
- [ ] Each gap is either fixed or deliberately chosen.
- [ ] The resurrection/climax tests what the hero learned.
