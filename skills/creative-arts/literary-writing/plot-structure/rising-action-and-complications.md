# Rising action and complications

**Goal:** a sequence of obstacles that steadily increases pressure on the protagonist
from inciting incident to crisis. Burroway: conflict is the fundamental element of
fiction; complications are the events that intensify it.

## Progressive complication
Each complication should:
1. Arise from the protagonist's previous action or from the antagonist's response to it
   (causality, not coincidence).
2. Make the situation **worse or more complex**, never just different.
3. **Close an option** — the easy ways out disappear one by one, until only the hard
   choice at the crisis remains.

## Try-fail cycles
Plan the protagonist's attempts as a sequence of outcomes:
| Outcome | Effect |
|---|---|
| **No, and...** | fails and things get worse (strongest escalation) |
| **Yes, but...** | succeeds but at a cost or opening a new problem |
| **No** | plain failure (use sparingly; can stall) |
| **Yes** | plain success (end of sequence or false victory) |
A typical middle runs on "yes, but" and "no, and" outcomes.

## Escalating dimensions
Raise pressure on more than one axis:
- **Stakes** (what can be lost; see `creative-arts.literary-writing.plot-structure.outlining-with-scene-and-sequel` for scene-level stakes),
- **Time** (deadlines tighten),
- **Personal cost** (relationships, identity, values),
- **Obstacle type** (physical -> social -> internal/moral).

## Procedure
1. List every complication between inciting incident and crisis.
2. Beside each, write the option it closes and the cause that links it to the previous one.
3. Re-order so intensity rises (a stronger obstacle early and a weaker one later reads as decline).
4. Replace coincidental complications with ones caused by characters' choices.

## Gotchas
- **Episodic plotting:** obstacles that could be shuffled in any order — they aren't causally linked.
- **Repetition:** the same kind of obstacle at the same scale.
- Complications from outside forces only, never the protagonist's own flaw.

## Verify
- [ ] Each complication is caused by a prior event.
- [ ] Each closes an option; by the crisis only a hard choice remains.
- [ ] Intensity rises across the sequence.
