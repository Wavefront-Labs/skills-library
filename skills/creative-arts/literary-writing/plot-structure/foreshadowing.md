# Foreshadowing

**Goal:** prepare the reader for later events so that, when they come, they feel both
surprising and inevitable. Aristotle's ideal: events that happen "unexpectedly but
because of one another".

## Kinds
- **Direct:** a statement of what is to come — prophecy, a narrator's "I did not know
  then that...", a character's fear. Creates suspense (the reader waits for it).
- **Indirect:** details whose meaning appears later — an object, a skill, a habit, a
  weather pattern, a remark. Creates the "of course" feeling on payoff.
- **Symbolic / atmospheric:** imagery that mirrors what will happen (a broken mirror
  before a betrayal) — use lightly; it tips into cliché fast.
- **Dramatic irony:** the reader knows something the character doesn't (via another
  viewpoint or a prologue) — suspense instead of surprise (Hitchcock's bomb under the table).

## Procedure (it's mostly a revision job)
1. After drafting, list every major turn, reveal and climax resolution.
2. For each, ask: what would the reader need to have noticed for this to feel earned?
   (A skill the hero uses at the climax; a character trait behind a betrayal.)
3. Plant each element once early and once mid-book, each time **inside a scene doing
   other work**, so it doesn't stand out.
4. Calibrate visibility: the more surprising the payoff, the more subtly but more
   repeatedly it should be planted.
5. Test with a fresh reader: on the payoff, ask "did that feel fair or out of nowhere?"

## Gotchas
- **Overt foreshadowing** that tells the twist away ("Little did she know...").
- **Unpaid foreshadowing:** an ominous detail that never pays off reads as a dropped
  thread (see `chekhovs-gun-and-planting`).
- Foreshadowing only via a narrator's hindsight in every chapter becomes a tic.
- Introducing the solution's key element in the same scene as it is needed (deus ex machina).

## Verify
- [ ] Every major payoff has at least one earlier plant.
- [ ] No ominous hint is left unresolved without intent.
- [ ] Fresh readers call the ending surprising but fair.
