# The snowflake method

**Goal:** grow a novel outline from one sentence to a full scene list in expanding
steps, fixing structural problems while they are cheap. Developed by Randy Ingermanson.

## The steps (as published on his site; times are his rough guides)
1. **One-sentence summary** (about 15 words, no character names): "A rogue physicist
   races to stop the time machine she built from erasing her own childhood."
2. **One-paragraph summary** (five sentences): setup; first disaster; second disaster;
   third disaster; ending. The three disasters roughly mark the end of act one, the
   midpoint, and the end of act two.
3. **Character summaries:** for each major character — name, storyline in one
   sentence, motivation (abstract), goal (concrete), conflict, epiphany, and a
   one-paragraph storyline.
4. **Expand** each sentence of the paragraph from step 2 into a paragraph (a one-page synopsis).
5. **Character synopses:** one page per major character telling the story from their viewpoint.
6. **Expand** the one-page synopsis to about four pages (each paragraph -> a page).
7. **Character charts:** full details (history, appearance, change).
8. **Scene spreadsheet:** one row per scene — POV character, what happens, page/word
   estimate. Sort and rearrange.
9. (Optional) **Scene narrative:** a paragraph or more per scene about the planned content and conflict.
10. Write the first draft.

## Using it well
- Iterate: discoveries in step 5 or 8 often send you back to rewrite steps 1-2.
- Steps 1 and 4 double as the elevator pitch and the submission synopsis.
- Pair step 8 with scene-and-sequel planning (see
  `creative-arts.literary-writing.plot-structure.outlining-with-scene-and-sequel`).

## Gotchas
- Spending months perfecting charts and never drafting; set a deadline per step.
- Treating the three disasters as mandatory explosions — they are reversals that force
  the protagonist to change approach.
- Discovery writers may find steps 6-9 deadening; stop after step 4 or 5 and draft.

## Verify
- [ ] One-sentence summary under ~25 words.
- [ ] Paragraph has three escalating disasters and an ending.
- [ ] Every major character has goal, conflict and epiphany written.
- [ ] Scene spreadsheet covers the whole book with a POV per scene.
