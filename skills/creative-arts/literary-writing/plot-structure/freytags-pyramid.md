# Freytag's pyramid

**Goal:** apply Gustav Freytag's model (*Die Technik des Dramas*, 1863), drawn from
five-act tragedy, to outline or diagnose a story's dramatic shape.

## The parts
1. **Introduction / exposition:** the situation and characters before the conflict.
2. **Inciting moment** (Freytag's "exciting force"): the event that sets the conflict going.
3. **Rising action:** complications that increase tension.
4. **Climax:** the high point — in Freytag's tragedy, the turning point in the hero's fortunes,
   which in his scheme falls near the middle (act three).
5. **Falling action:** consequences unwinding toward the end, with a "moment of final
   suspense" (a last hope) in tragedy.
6. **Catastrophe / denouement:** the final outcome; in tragedy, the hero's downfall.

## Using it on a manuscript
1. Draw the pyramid; place each chapter or scene on it by tension level (1-10).
2. Find the climax: the point where the protagonist's fortune turns irreversibly.
3. Check the slope: rising action should rise (escalating stakes), not plateau.
4. Check the falling action is short in modern fiction: readers expect the peak late.

## Freytag vs modern practice
- In most contemporary novels and films the **climax sits near the end** (around 85-95%),
  with a short resolution; Freytag's mid-point climax is closer to what modern models call
  the midpoint reversal.
- Treat the pyramid as a vocabulary for tension, not a template. The three-act and beat-sheet
  models are more common in commercial fiction.
- Useful for literary fiction and short stories: exposition, complication, crisis, resolution
  map neatly onto many stories (Burroway's conflict-crisis-resolution).

## Gotchas
- Long exposition before the inciting moment — readers need the disturbance early.
- Confusing a loud event with the climax: the climax is where the outcome is decided.

## Verify
- [ ] Inciting moment, climax and denouement identified by scene.
- [ ] Tension plot shows a rising line to the climax.
- [ ] Climax position checked against genre expectations.
