# Designing a plot twist

**Goal:** a reveal that changes the meaning of what came before, and holds up on reread.
A good twist is a **recognition** (Aristotle's anagnorisis): a shift from ignorance to knowledge.

## Types
- **Identity:** someone is not who we thought (ally is the villain; narrator is dead).
- **Situation:** the world/frame is different (it was a test; it's a different time).
- **Motive:** the act we saw meant something else.
- **Reversal of fortune:** the victory was a defeat (and vice versa).

## Procedure
1. **State the hidden truth** in one sentence, and the **false assumption** the reader
   must hold until the reveal.
2. List how the reader will form that assumption: genre expectations, a character's
   claim, the viewpoint character's belief, framing (chapter titles, timelines).
3. **Seed double-meaning details**: lines and actions that read one way under the false
   assumption and another under the truth. Aim for several across the book.
4. **Never lie in narration** you claim is objective. A character may lie; an unreliable
   first-person narrator may misread; an objective narrator may omit and arrange, but
   not state falsehoods.
5. Place the reveal where it changes the protagonist's choice — ideally driving the
   climax, not after it.
6. After the reveal, give a short recontextualisation (a remembered line, a quick
   montage of clues) so the reader experiences the "click".

## Testing
- Ask cold readers when they guessed. Guessed in the first third = too obvious; nobody
  can explain it afterwards = unfair.
- Reread as if you know the truth: every seeded line must still make sense.

## Gotchas
- Twist for its own sake that undoes the emotional story ("it was all a dream").
- Twist relying on information withheld from the reader that the viewpoint character knew,
  with no narrative justification.
- Telegraphing with heavy-handed hints or a too-obviously suspicious character.

## Verify
- [ ] Hidden truth and false assumption written down.
- [ ] At least three double-meaning seeds, spread across the book.
- [ ] No false statement by an objective narrator.
- [ ] Reveal changes what the protagonist does next.
