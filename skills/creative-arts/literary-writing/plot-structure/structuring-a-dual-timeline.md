# Structuring a dual timeline

**Goal:** two stories in different times (often a present-day frame and a historical
story) that each hold attention and that illuminate each other, converging at the end.

## Design
1. **Each timeline needs its own complete arc**: protagonist, want, obstacles, climax.
   A timeline that exists only to deliver information to the other will be skimmed.
2. **Define the link:** a secret, an object, a place, a bloodline, a question. The
   present timeline usually *investigates* what the past timeline *lives*.
3. **Decide the convergence:** what the past reveals that changes the present
   protagonist's climactic choice. Both climaxes should land close together.
4. **Thematic echo:** pair parallel situations across timelines (two women facing the
   same choice 80 years apart) so the alternation creates meaning.

## Alternation
- Alternate chapters (A/B/A/B) or groups of chapters; keep the rhythm mostly regular so
  readers trust it.
- Switch **at a moment of tension** in the timeline you leave — readers then carry a
  question into the other thread.
- Watch the "favourite timeline" problem: if readers prefer one thread, strengthen the
  other's stakes rather than shrinking it.
- Reverse-outline both timelines side by side; check that tension peaks are staggered
  rather than both slack at once.

## Orientation
- Chapter headings with place and year, or character name and year.
- Distinct texture per timeline: tense (present for now, past for then), POV (first vs
  third), voice, or document forms (letters, diaries).

## Gotchas
- The present-day protagonist only reads letters/diaries and has no story of her own.
- Revealing the past timeline's ending in the present too early (kills its suspense).
- Coincidence-driven convergence (the antique shop happens to sell the exact locket).

## Verify
- [ ] Both timelines have independent arcs and climaxes.
- [ ] Each switch leaves a question open.
- [ ] Chapter headings or texture make the timeline obvious within a sentence.
- [ ] The past changes a present choice at the climax.
