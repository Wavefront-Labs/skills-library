# Index-card outlining

**Goal:** see the whole book at once and rearrange it physically. Anne Lamott (*Bird by
Bird*, "Index Cards") carries cards to capture ideas; screenwriters and novelists lay
out scene cards to structure a story. Digital equivalent: Scrivener's corkboard.

## Card format
One scene per card (3x5 in is standard):
- Top line: short scene name and POV character.
- Middle: what happens + **what changes** (the turn).
- Bottom corner: chapter number (pencil), word estimate, timeline date.
- Colour code by plot thread, POV character, or timeline (coloured cards, stickers or pens).

## Procedure
1. **Dump:** write a card for every scene you have or imagine, in any order. Ideas
   without a place go on cards too, in a separate pile.
2. **Lay out in rows** by act or quarter (four rows works well for novels: 0-25%,
   25-50%, 50-75%, 75-100%). Put the big beats down first — inciting incident, midpoint,
   crisis, climax — as anchors.
3. **Fill between the anchors** with the remaining cards in causal order.
4. **Read the colours**: long runs of one colour = a thread hogging space; long gaps =
   a thread disappearing (see `subplot-weaving`).
5. **Find holes:** where the "what changes" lines don't connect causally, write a blank
   card marked "?" for the missing scene.
6. **Cut:** cards whose turn duplicates another card's turn are candidates for merging.
7. Photograph the layout; number the cards; transfer to a scene list.

## For revision
Write one card per *existing* scene from the draft (reverse outlining), then rearrange.
Seeing a chapter physically move is often what unlocks a structural fix.

## Gotchas
- Cards with no "what changes" line are usually not scenes yet.
- Over-detailed cards resist rearranging; keep them short.
- Not recording the final order before the cards get knocked over.

## Verify
- [ ] Every card has a turn.
- [ ] Anchors placed at about 10-15%, 50%, 75% and the climax.
- [ ] Colour distribution checked for vanishing threads.
- [ ] Final order captured in a numbered list.
