# Choosing sample chapters

**Goal:** sample writing that proves you can deliver the book the proposal promises.
Editors buy nonfiction on proposal partly to judge voice and execution from the sample.

## Which chapters
- **Chapter one** is the default — it's what readers meet first and shows the book's
  opening strategy, voice and hook. Most agents expect it.
- A **second sample** (if requested or if the book is long) should show something the
  first doesn't:
  - for narrative nonfiction, a chapter deep in the story showing sustained scene work;
  - for a book mixing memoir and research, a chapter where the strands braid;
  - for prescriptive books, a chapter demonstrating the method in action.
- Avoid choosing only your easiest or most dramatic chapter if it's unrepresentative.

## Length
- Commonly 1-3 chapters, roughly 20-50 pages total; follow agent guidelines.
- Memoir by debut writers: agents may want much more, or the full manuscript.

## Preparation
1. Write the chapter outline first so samples sit correctly in the arc.
2. Revise samples to final, copy-edited quality (they are judged as finished writing).
3. If sending a non-consecutive chapter, add a one-paragraph **bridge note** at its start
   summarising what happened in between.
4. Make sure samples fulfil promises in the overview (if the overview promises humour,
   samples must be funny).

## Gotchas
- A sample chapter that's all background with no story.
- Samples contradicting the chapter outline.
- Rough drafts sent as samples "to show the idea".

## Verify
- [ ] Chapter one included (unless guidelines say otherwise).
- [ ] Second sample shows a different strength.
- [ ] Polished to final quality; consistent with outline and overview.
