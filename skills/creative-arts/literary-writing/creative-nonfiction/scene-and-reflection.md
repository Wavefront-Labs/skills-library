# Scene and reflection

**Goal:** true writing that both shows what happened and makes meaning of it. Gutkind
describes the "yellow test": highlight scenes in yellow and see how much of the piece
is dramatised; creative nonfiction is built of scenes, but the reflective voice is what
distinguishes it from reportage.

## The two modes
- **Scene:** a specific moment rendered with action, dialogue, sensory detail, in time.
- **Reflection:** the narrator thinking about what it meant — interpretation, context,
  memory's unreliability, the larger question. In memoir it is the voice of the
  **narrating self** (now) about the **experiencing self** (then).

## Diagnose
- Highlight scenes in one colour, reflection in another, information/research in a third.
- **Scene-only** drafts read like fiction without a point: why are we being told this?
- **Reflection-only** drafts read like a lecture or diary: the reader has no experience
  to reflect *with*.

## Placement patterns
1. **Scene -> reflection:** dramatise, then step back ("I didn't understand then that...").
2. **Reflection -> scene:** pose the question, then the scene as evidence.
3. **Braided within a scene:** short reflective sentences interleaved at key beats.
4. **Withheld reflection:** occasionally let a scene stand unglossed at a peak, and reflect later.

## Writing good reflection
- Earned: it follows from the specifics just shown.
- Honest about uncertainty: "I think", "I may be wrong" — doubt is a strength in memoir.
- Goes beyond the obvious moral ("I learned to appreciate my mother") to a surprising
  or complicated insight.
- Brief at first; longer passages after the reader is invested.

## Gotchas
- Reflection that repeats what the scene already made clear.
- Hindsight leaking into the scene itself (the experiencing self knowing what the
  narrator knows) when you want suspense.

- [ ] Highlight test shows both modes in every section.
- [ ] Each major scene is followed or framed by meaning-making somewhere.
- [ ] Reflection adds insight beyond the scene's obvious point.
