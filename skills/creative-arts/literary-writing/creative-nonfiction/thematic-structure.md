# Thematic structure

**Goal:** a nonfiction book or long essay organised by ideas, subjects or motifs rather
than time — common in memoir-in-essays, cultural criticism and hybrid books — that still
feels like it's going somewhere.

## Organising principles
| Principle | Example shape |
|---|---|
| Objects | Each chapter around one object (a coat, a recipe, a car) |
| Places | Houses lived in, cities, rooms |
| Questions | Each chapter asks a question about the central subject |
| Elements / categories | The four seasons; the senses; parts of the body |
| People | A chapter per relationship |
| Forms | A list, a letter, a glossary, an interview as chapters |
Choose a principle that **generates insight** about the subject, not just a container.

## Make each unit work
- Each chapter/section needs its own **arc**: a question, development, a turn or discovery.
- Each should be able to stand alone (many will be published separately as essays) yet
  gain meaning in sequence.

## Build a through-line
Thematic books risk feeling static. Add movement with:
1. **An underlying chronology** that advances lightly across chapters (early chapters
   lean to childhood, later to the present).
2. **A central question** that deepens and is answered (or reframed) at the end.
3. **An escalating emotional stake** — the hardest material saved for late in the book.
4. **Recurring motifs** that change meaning.

## Procedure
1. List candidate chapters with a one-line question for each.
2. Test the principle: does it yield 8-15 strong units without forcing?
3. Order for through-line, not by category order; read in sequence; cut redundancies
   (thematic books repeat backstory — set it once, refer later).

## Gotchas
- Repeated backstory explanations across chapters written as separate essays.
- A gimmicky principle that strains (a chapter forced to fit "the letter Q").

## Verify
- [ ] Organising principle generates insight and enough units.
- [ ] Each chapter has its own arc.
- [ ] A through-line (chronology, question or stakes) advances across the book.
- [ ] Backstory not re-explained in multiple chapters.
