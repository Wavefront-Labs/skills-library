# Writing about family and living people

**Goal:** tell your own story truthfully while limiting harm to people who did not choose to be in it, and limiting legal and relational risk. This is professional guidance; for anything touching defamation, privacy or contracts, take advice from a lawyer experienced in publishing.

For the legal frame (privacy torts, release forms, disguising identities) see `creative-arts.literary-writing.creative-nonfiction.privacy-and-consent-in-nonfiction`. This skill covers the craft and ethics decisions that come first.

## Decide before drafting
1. **Whose story is it?** Your experience of events is yours. Another person's private history (illness, abuse, an affair, a diagnosis) is theirs. Tell what you witnessed and felt; be careful with what you only learned second-hand.
2. **Draft honestly first, decide later.** Many writers draft without a censor, then make inclusion decisions in revision. Writing it does not commit you to publishing it.
3. **Apply a necessity test to each fact.** Does the story break without it? If the detail is only damaging, cut it.
4. **Sort the people.** Public figures, private adults, minors and the dead carry different risks. Minors and people who cannot respond deserve extra care.

## Options for each person
- Portray fully and keep the real name (only where accurate, fair and low risk).
- Change names and identifying details, and say so in an author's note. Changed names alone may not protect someone who is still identifiable to people who know them; this is a risk, not a guarantee.
- Composite or compress characters, and disclose it.
- Cut the person or the episode.

## Telling them
- Showing a draft or talking it through is a choice, not a duty. Some writers do it for family, some for no one. Consider the relationship, the risk of being blocked, and whether you could live with their objection.
- Do not promise approval or edits you will not honour.
- Publishers commonly ask authors to warrant that the book is not defamatory or invasive of privacy and to indemnify the publisher; many do a legal read of memoir. Read that clause in your contract and ask your agent or a lawyer what it means for you.

## Craft guardrails
- Write the other person with the same complexity you give yourself: motives, humour, reasons.
- Avoid diagnosing people ("narcissist") or alleging crimes you cannot document.
- Keep a record of sources for factual claims (letters, records, other witnesses).

## Verify
- [ ] Each living person has a decision: keep, disguise, composite or cut.
- [ ] Any disguise or compression is disclosed.
- [ ] Contract warranties are read and legal advice sought on any risky passage.
