# The situation and the story

**Goal:** stop a personal piece from being a list of events by finding the insight that makes the events matter.

Vivian Gornick's *The Situation and the Story: The Art of Personal Narrative* (2001) gives the working distinction. The **situation** is the context or plot: the events, the people, the setting. The **story** is the emotional experience the writer has brought to the situation: the insight, the thing the narrator has come to understand. Gornick's point is that the narrator who tells it has to be a persona shaped for the piece, not the whole person.

## Steps
1. **Write the situation in two sentences.** "My father sold the farm the year I left for college." If you cannot do this, you do not yet have a bounded piece.
2. **Write the story in one sentence, starting with "I understood that" or "What I could not see then was".** This is the insight. If the sentence is a platitude ("family is complicated"), the story is not found yet; push toward the specific, slightly uncomfortable thing only you could say.
3. **Choose the narrator's stance.** Decide who is telling: the person at the time, the person now, or both (see `creative-arts.literary-writing.creative-nonfiction.scene-and-reflection`). The story lives in the gap between them.
4. **Audit each scene against the pair.** Keep a scene if it carries the situation forward or sharpens the story. Cut scenes that are only interesting events.
5. **Place the insight late and earn it.** State it plainly once, near the end, after the scenes have made it unavoidable. Do not state it in the first paragraph and then illustrate it.

## Failure modes
- All situation, no story: a chronicle that stays at the surface.
- All story, no situation: abstraction and rumination with nothing for the reader to see.
- The narrator is smarter or kinder than the writer was; the reader senses the polish. Let the narrator be wrong in the past scenes.

## Verify
- [ ] Situation stated in two sentences, story in one specific sentence.
- [ ] Every scene serves one of the two.
- [ ] The insight appears in the writing once, not as a title or opening thesis.
