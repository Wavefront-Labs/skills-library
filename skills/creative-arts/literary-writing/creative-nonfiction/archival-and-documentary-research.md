# Archival and documentary research

**Goal:** ground a true story in documents — letters, records, newspapers, photographs
— that supply detail, correct memory and support fact-checking.

## Where to look
| Source | Examples |
|---|---|
| National archives | US National Archives (archives.gov), UK National Archives, others |
| Libraries' special collections | University and local history collections; search **finding aids** (descriptions of collections, box/folder level) |
| Newspapers | Library of Congress *Chronicling America* (US historic papers), subscription newspaper archives, local libraries' microfilm |
| Public records | Court records, property deeds, birth/marriage/death records, census records, military records |
| Government information | **FOIA** requests (US federal, via foia.gov) and state public-records laws; other countries have equivalent laws |
| Family sources | Letters, diaries, photos, home movies, documents in relatives' homes (memoir) |

## Working an archive
1. Read finding aids before visiting; list box and folder numbers you need; contact the
   archivist (they know the collection best).
2. Check rules: pencils only, photography allowed?, request limits, opening hours.
3. Photograph or scan with a slate/label for every item (collection, box, folder, item).
4. Note what is **absent** as well as present.

## Using documents in the narrative
- Details for scenes: weather reports in newspapers, menus, prices, letterhead, handwriting.
- Correcting memory: memoirists often find that documents contradict their recollection —
  decide how to handle it on the page (see
  `creative-arts.literary-writing.creative-nonfiction.memoir-scene-reconstruction-and-truth`).
- Quoting documents: permissions may apply for unpublished letters (see
  `permissions-and-fair-use-for-quotations`).

## Citation log
Spreadsheet: claim/detail | source | location (collection/box/folder/page or URL) |
date accessed | image file name | used on manuscript page. This makes fact-checking and
endnotes possible.

## Gotchas
- FOIA and records requests take months; start early.
- Online transcriptions and OCR contain errors — check images of originals.
- Losing track of where a photo of a document came from.

## Verify
- [ ] Finding aids checked and archivists contacted before visits.
- [ ] Every document image labelled with its location.
- [ ] Citation log links each detail to its source.
