# Writing a nonfiction book proposal

**Goal:** a business document that persuades an agent and then a publisher to buy a
nonfiction book on the basis of a plan plus sample. Most narrative nonfiction and
prescriptive nonfiction sell on proposal; **memoir by debut authors often needs a full
manuscript** as well, or a proposal with substantial sample (agents vary).

## Standard sections (order varies; agents may have templates)
1. **Overview (2-5 pages):** the hook, what the book is, why now, why it matters, the
   narrative arc or argument, the reader takeaway. Opens with the strongest possible
   paragraph — often a scene.
2. **About the author:** credentials, lived experience, access to the story, prior
   publications — framed as why *you* must write this.
3. **Platform:** concrete numbers and reach (see `building-an-author-platform`).
4. **Target audience / market:** specific reader groups, with evidence of size (organisations, communities, sales of related books).
5. **Comparable titles:** 4-8 recent books (last ~5 years), each with a line on how
   yours is similar and different (see
   `creative-arts.literary-writing.novel-writing.choosing-comp-titles`).
6. **Marketing and promotion plan:** what *you* will do — events, media contacts,
   newsletters, speaking, partnerships. Specific and realistic, no "I'll go on Oprah".
7. **Book specs:** estimated word count, delivery time (months from contract), any
   illustrations/permissions/research costs.
8. **Chapter outline:** a paragraph or more per chapter showing arc and content.
9. **Sample chapter(s):** usually the first chapter plus perhaps one more (see `choosing-sample-chapters`).

## Procedure
1. Draft the overview and chapter outline first — they force clarity about the book.
2. Write the sample chapters to final quality.
3. Assemble; total proposals commonly run 30-60+ pages including samples.
4. Query agents with a pitch letter (as for fiction) and send the proposal on request.

## Gotchas
- Overview that describes the subject, not the book (structure, arc, voice).
- Platform section that's vague ("active on social media").
- Comps that are classics or mega-bestsellers.
- Promising delivery timelines you can't meet.

## Verify
- [ ] All standard sections present with specific evidence.
- [ ] Overview opens with a hook and states why now and why you.
- [ ] Chapter outline shows an arc, not just topics.
- [ ] Samples polished to final quality.
