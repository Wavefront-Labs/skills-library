# Interviewing for nonfiction

**Goal:** interviews that produce story — scenes, specific details, dialogue and
emotion — not just facts, while being fair to the people you interview.

## Prepare
- Research the person and topic so you don't waste their time on basics.
- List what you need: facts to confirm, scenes to reconstruct, emotional turning points.
- Write questions, then be ready to abandon the list to follow the person.

## Ground rules (state at the start)
- Who you are, what the project is, where it may be published.
- **On the record** by default; agree terms for **off the record** (not usable) and
  **background** (usable without attribution) *before* the information is shared.
- **Recording:** ask permission and record the consent. US states differ: some require
  all-party consent for recording calls (the Reporters Committee for Freedom of the Press
  publishes a state-by-state guide); other countries have their own laws.

## Questions that produce story
- Open: "Walk me through that morning." "What happened next?"
- **Scene-gathering:** "Where were you standing? What could you hear? Who said what,
  as close as you remember? What were you wearing?"
- Feeling: "What went through your mind when...?" "What did you do with your hands?"
- Specific over general: "Tell me about one time when..."
- Last question: "What haven't I asked that I should have?"

## Technique
- Listen more than talk; use **silence** — people fill it with the most interesting material.
- Ask follow-ups on vague words ("It was hard" -> "Hard how?").
- Come back to difficult questions later in the interview, rephrased.
- Note body language and setting as well as words.

## After
- Transcribe soon (automatic transcription plus a careful check against audio).
- Quote verbatim within quotation marks; mark paraphrase as paraphrase.
- Verify facts with documents or other sources; memories differ.
- Follow-up interviews are normal; tell the person you may come back.

## Gotchas
- Leading questions that put words in the person's mouth.
- Treating reconstructed dialogue as verbatim without disclosure.
- Agreeing to give quote approval or manuscript review without thinking it through.

## Verify
- [ ] Ground rules and recording consent captured on tape or in writing.
- [ ] Scene-gathering questions asked for each key event.
- [ ] Transcript checked against audio; quotes verbatim.
