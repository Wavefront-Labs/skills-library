# Privacy and consent in nonfiction

**Goal:** tell true stories involving real people while reducing legal risk and
handling the ethics deliberately. This is general information, not legal advice; laws
differ by country (the UK and EU have stronger privacy rights than the US).

## US privacy torts relevant to writers
| Claim | What it is | Typical defence |
|---|---|---|
| **Public disclosure of private facts** | Publicising true private facts that are highly offensive and not of legitimate public concern (medical, sexual, financial details) | Newsworthiness/public concern; consent |
| **False light** | Portraying someone in a misleading way highly offensive to a reasonable person (not all states recognise it) | Accuracy, context |
| **Intrusion upon seclusion** | How information was gathered (secret recordings, reading private mail) | Lawful gathering |
| **Misappropriation of name/likeness** | Commercial use of identity | Editorial use is generally protected |
Defamation is separate (false statements of fact; see
`creative-arts.literary-writing.creative-nonfiction.memoir-scene-reconstruction-and-truth`
for the ethics and the publisher's legal process).

## Practical risk reduction
1. **List every identifiable person** and what you reveal about them; flag private facts
   (health, sexuality, criminal history, finances, children).
2. Ask of each fact: is it necessary to *my* story? Is it mine to tell (my experience vs theirs)?
3. **Disguise** non-public figures where identity doesn't matter: change names, physical
   details, location, occupation — and say so in an author's note. Disguise must be
   real; a "changed name" with every other detail intact identifies the person.
4. **Seek consent or show passages** where the stakes are high; get written releases for
   intimate portrayals when possible. Consent isn't always possible or ethical to seek (abusers).
5. Keep **documentation**: journals, letters, photos, interview notes, recordings (lawfully made).
6. Ask your publisher for a **legal read** (pre-publication vetting); check your contract's
   warranty and indemnity clause and whether you can be added to the publisher's insurance.

## Ethics beyond law
- Children, the dead's survivors, and people who can't respond deserve extra care.
- Write about others in the service of understanding, including your own part in events.

## Verify
- [ ] Identifiable-person list with sensitive facts flagged.
- [ ] Each private fact justified as necessary.
- [ ] Disguises substantive and disclosed in a note.
