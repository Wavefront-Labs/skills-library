# Verifying memory with research

**Goal:** make memoir claims that survive checking, and be open about what cannot be checked. Memory is reconstructive: vividness is not accuracy.

## Process
1. **Mark every checkable claim in the draft.** Highlight dates, ages, places, names, public events, quoted words, weather, prices, songs, school and job details.
2. **Build a timeline** from fixed points first: documents with dates (letters, photos with processing dates, emails, diaries, records you can legitimately access, tickets, receipts), then fit memories around them.
3. **Check against outside records.**
   - Public events: newspaper archives and library databases (for US material, the Library of Congress Chronicling America collection covers digitised historic newspapers up to a cutoff year; check its current range).
   - Places: old maps, directories, census and property records, archives (start at an archive's finding aids).
   - Culture: release dates for songs, films, products. A common slip is putting a song, gadget or phrase in a year before it existed.
   - Weather: historical weather data for a date if you describe it.
4. **Interview other witnesses**, separately, and ask open questions before you tell them your version. Note disagreements; they are material, not errors to hide.
5. **Resolve conflicts by weight of evidence**: contemporary documents beat later recollection. When two honest memories differ, say so in the text.
6. **Record what is reconstructed.** Dialogue you cannot recall verbatim is reconstructed; phrase accordingly and use an author's note describing your method.
7. **Keep a fact-check log**: claim, source, status (confirmed, unconfirmed, corrected). Publishers may ask for sources.

## Honest uncertainty
Phrases like "as I remember it" or "my mother disputes this" are legitimate tools when used where the uncertainty is real, not as a shield for invention.

## Verify
- [ ] Every date and public fact has a source or is flagged uncertain.
- [ ] Anachronisms checked (objects, songs, slang).
- [ ] Conflicting accounts acknowledged rather than silently chosen.
