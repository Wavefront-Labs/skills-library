# Building an author platform

**Goal:** demonstrable visibility and authority with the book's readers. Jane Friedman
defines platform as the ability to sell books because of who you are or who you can
reach. For prescriptive and narrative nonfiction it's often decisive in acquisition;
for memoir and fiction the writing matters most, but platform helps.

## Components
| Component | What counts | How to show it |
|---|---|---|
| **Email newsletter** | Subscribers who opted in; open rates | Count, open rate, growth |
| **Publications** | Bylines in outlets your readers read | List with links; notable pieces |
| **Speaking / teaching** | Talks, workshops, conferences, courses | Events per year, audience sizes |
| **Professional authority** | Credentials, position, research | Titles, institutions |
| **Media** | Podcast/radio/TV appearances, quotes | List of appearances |
| **Partnerships / networks** | Organisations that could promote or buy in bulk | Named partners, member numbers |
| **Social media** | Engaged audience on a relevant platform | Followers *and* engagement |
Publishers generally weight an engaged email list more than follower counts.

## Building it (start years before the proposal if possible)
1. Define the readers precisely (who has the problem or interest your book serves).
2. Publish regularly where they already read: essays, articles, guest posts.
3. Start a newsletter with a clear promise; link it from every bio; offer something
   valuable for subscribing.
4. Speak where they gather (associations, conferences, libraries, podcasts).
5. Build relationships with organisations and influencers in the field.
6. Track numbers quarterly.

## In the proposal
- Concrete numbers, dates and names; no adjectives ("10,400 newsletter subscribers,
  52% open rate; 30 talks a year to audiences of 50-400").
- Tie platform to the marketing plan: what you will do with it at launch.

## Gotchas
- Chasing vanity metrics on platforms the readers don't use.
- Buying followers or email lists (worthless and damaging).
- Platform building crowding out the writing itself.
