# Pitching essays to editors

**Goal:** get a commission or acceptance for an essay from a magazine, newspaper or
website.

## Pitch vs submission
- **Literary journals** usually want the **finished essay** submitted via their portal
  (see `creative-arts.literary-writing.poetry.poetry-submissions-to-journals` for the
  same submission practice).
- **Magazines, newspapers and websites** usually want a **pitch** first for reported
  or timely pieces; many personal-essay sections (e.g., newspaper columns) accept
  finished drafts — read each outlet's guidelines.

## Finding the right editor
- Read the section; note bylines and the editors' names (mastheads, editor social
  profiles, "pitch me" posts).
- Pitch one editor per outlet at a time.

## The pitch email (150-300 words)
1. **Subject line:** "PITCH: [working title or one-line hook]".
2. **Hook paragraph:** open with the essay's first lines or a vivid summary.
3. **What it is and why now:** angle, argument, the **news peg** or timely hook (anniversary,
   trend, current event) if relevant.
4. **Why this outlet's readers:** fit with their section.
5. **Why you:** expertise, experience, access; 2-3 relevant clips (links).
6. Estimated length and availability.
Keep it plain text, no attachments unless asked.

## After sending
- Follow up once after about 1-2 weeks for timely pieces (longer for evergreen), then
  move on and pitch elsewhere (say in the follow-up you'll be pitching elsewhere if timely).
- On acceptance: confirm word count, deadline, fee, rights (first serial / all rights /
  online only), kill fee, and editing process — in writing.

## Gotchas
- Pitching a topic instead of a story ("an essay about grief") — show the specific angle.
- Pitching something the outlet ran recently (search their archive).
- Signing all-rights contracts for pieces you want to include in a later book.

## Verify
- [ ] Outlet guidelines read; correct editor identified.
- [ ] Pitch has hook, angle, why now, why you, and clips.
- [ ] Rights and fee confirmed in writing before writing a commissioned piece.
