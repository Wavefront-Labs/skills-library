# Passive voice and strong verbs

**Goal:** sentences whose energy is in specific, active verbs — while keeping the
passive where it's the right tool.

## Identify true passive voice
Passive = form of *be* (or *get*) + past participle, with the actor demoted or omitted:
*The window was broken (by the boy).*
Not passive: *was walking* (progressive), *was tired* (adjective) — those may be weak but
aren't passive.

## When the passive is right
- The actor is unknown or unimportant: *He was born in 1950.*
- You want focus on the receiver: *The president was shot.*
- Deliberate evasion in a character's voice: *Mistakes were made.*
- To keep the topic in subject position across sentences for flow.

## Weak-verb patterns and fixes
| Pattern | Example | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Unnecessary passive | *The door was opened by Sam.* | *Sam opened the door.* |
| To-be + adjective | *She was angry at him.* | *She glared / slammed / show it* |
| Expletive opener | *There was a man standing at the gate.* | *A man stood at the gate.* |
| "It was ... that" | *It was the noise that woke her.* | *The noise woke her.* |
| Nominalisation | *She made a decision to leave.* | *She decided to leave.* |
| Generic verb + modifier | *He went quickly down the stairs.* | *He clattered down the stairs.* |
| Light verbs | *gave a shrug, took a look* | *shrugged, looked* |

## Procedure
1. Search for `was|were|been|being|is|are` + `\w+ed\b` to find likely passives.
2. Search `There (was|were|is|are)` and `It (was|is) .* that`.
3. Search nominalisation suffixes (*-tion, -ment, -ance*) in narration.
4. Revise each where active/specific is better; leave justified uses.

## Gotchas
- Swapping in flashy verbs that mismatch tone.
- Turning every "was" into action: "was" is fine for states and description.

## Verify
- [ ] Each remaining passive is justified.
- [ ] Expletive openers reduced.
- [ ] Generic verbs + adverbs replaced in key scenes.
