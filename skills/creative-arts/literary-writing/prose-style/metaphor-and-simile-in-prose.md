# Metaphor and simile in prose

**Goal:** comparisons that make readers see something freshly and that fit the voice.
Burroway (*Writing Fiction*, "Is and Is Not") on comparison: the figure must be apt
(true in the right respect), fresh, and consistent with the story's tone.

## Terms
- **Tenor:** the thing described (her silence). **Vehicle:** what it's compared to (a
  locked door). The **ground** is what they share.
- **Simile:** explicit ("like", "as"). **Metaphor:** implicit identity ("her silence was a locked door").
- **Implied metaphor** in verbs: "the news *curdled* the room" — often the most economical.

## Making them work
1. **Source the vehicle from the viewpoint character's world.** A mechanic compares
   with engines, a nurse with wounds, a child with toys. This characterises as it describes.
2. **Check aptness:** does the vehicle match the tenor in the respect that matters
   (shape, feeling, motion), and does it carry the right connotations? A "sunset" simile
   for a bruise brings beauty — is that intended?
3. **Check tone:** comic vehicles in tragic scenes undercut them (unless deliberate).
4. **Prefer concrete vehicles** for abstract tenors (grief like a stone in the shoe),
   not abstract for abstract (grief like despair).
5. **Ration:** one striking figure per passage lands; several compete. In fast action,
   cut most of them.

## Extending
- An extended metaphor runs across sentences or a whole scene; keep the logic
  consistent (see `cliche-and-mixed-metaphor`).
- Recurring vehicles across a book become motifs (see `symbol-and-motif`).

## Gotchas
- Strained figures that call attention to the writer ("her eyes were like twin
  submarine portholes of longing").
- Similes that add nothing ("as fast as lightning", "white as snow").
- Stacking ("like a ... , like a ..., like a ...").

## Verify
- [ ] Each figure is apt in the respect that matters.
- [ ] Vehicles come from the viewpoint character's world.
- [ ] No more than one prominent figure per paragraph in key scenes.
- [ ] No stock similes.
