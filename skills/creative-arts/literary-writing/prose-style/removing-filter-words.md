# Removing filter words

**Goal:** put the reader directly in the viewpoint character's experience. *Filter
words* report perception or thought ("she saw", "he felt") instead of presenting the
thing perceived, adding a layer between reader and event.

## Example
- Filtered: *She heard a car door slam outside. She felt a chill and wondered if it was him.*
- Unfiltered: *Outside, a car door slammed. A chill went through her. Was it him?*
In a close point of view, the reader already knows the perception is hers.

## Search list
Search the manuscript (whole word, case-insensitive) for:
`saw, see, seen, watched, looked, noticed, observed, heard, listened, felt, feel,
smelled, tasted, realised/realized, knew, thought, wondered, decided, seemed, appeared,
could see, could hear, began to, started to`

## Procedure
1. Run each search; for every hit ask: *is the act of perceiving the point?*
2. If not, cut the filter and present the perceived thing directly; often the verb
   then moves to the object ("a dog barked" rather than "she heard a dog bark").
3. "Wondered if X" -> a direct question in free indirect style ("Was it X?").
4. "Realised that X" -> just X, or dramatise the realisation.
5. "Began to / started to" -> the action itself, unless the interruption matters.

## When to keep a filter
- The perceiving itself matters: *She didn't see the car.* / *He watched her lie to the police.*
- Changing or doubting perception: *She thought she heard footsteps.*
- Deliberate distance (more distant third, or a cool narrative voice).
- Clarity in multi-character scenes, where "she saw" tells us *who* perceived.

## Gotchas
- Mechanical removal of every instance makes prose choppy; aim for intention, not zero.
- Removing "felt" but replacing it with clichéd physical reactions (stomach dropping)
  — choose specific sensations.

## Verify
- [ ] Every filter word in key scenes is there on purpose.
- [ ] "Wondered" and "realised" mostly converted to direct thought or action.
- [ ] Prose read aloud still flows after edits.
