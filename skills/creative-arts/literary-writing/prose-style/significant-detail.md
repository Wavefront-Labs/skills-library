# Significant detail

**Goal:** details that do double work — make the scene visible *and* carry meaning.
Burroway (*Writing Fiction*, "Seeing Is Believing") defines the significant detail as
one that is **concrete** (appeals to the senses), **definite/specific** (this one,
not a class), and that **suggests an abstraction or judgement** without stating it.

## Generic -> significant
| Generic | Significant |
|---|---|
| "a messy kitchen" | "three days of cereal bowls, the milk gone to glue" |
| "He was rich." | "He tipped the valet with a fifty and didn't wait for thanks." |
| "an old car" | "a '94 Corolla with one door primer-grey" |
| "She was sad." | "She ironed his shirts for the funeral, then ironed them again." |

The significant version lets the reader *infer* the abstraction (neglect, wealth and
arrogance, poverty, grief), which is more vivid and more persuasive than being told.

## How to find one
1. Name the abstraction you want the reader to infer (loneliness, status, fear).
2. List 10 possible concrete details that could imply it; the first three will be
   clichés — keep going.
3. Choose the most **unexpected but inevitable** one: surprising, yet obviously right.
4. Use **one** strong detail rather than five average ones; lists dilute.
5. Prefer details filtered through the viewpoint character's attention — what they
   notice characterises them too.

## Where to use
- Character introductions (one physical or behavioural detail beats an inventory).
- Setting (one sensory particular anchors a place; see the setting skills).
- Emotional peaks (an object or gesture instead of named emotion).

## Gotchas
- Brand-name and inventory detail that says only "I researched".
- Over-detailing minor items, which promises significance (see
  `creative-arts.literary-writing.plot-structure.chekhovs-gun-and-planting`).
- Details that imply the wrong thing (a "gleaming" detail in a scene of decay).

## Verify
- [ ] Key descriptions use specific nouns and verbs, not generic adjectives.
- [ ] Each important detail implies something you didn't state.
- [ ] No passage relies on a list where one detail would do.
