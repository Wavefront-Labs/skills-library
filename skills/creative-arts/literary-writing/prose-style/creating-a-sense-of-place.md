# Creating a sense of place

**Goal:** a setting the reader can inhabit and that matters to the story. Burroway
(*Writing Fiction*, "Long Ago and Far Away") treats setting as atmosphere, as a source of
conflict, and as a reflection of character — never as backdrop alone.

## Principles
1. **Filter through a character.** The same street is "cramped and filthy" to a newcomer
   and "home, finally" to someone returning. Attitude toward place characterises the
   viewer and lets description advance emotion.
2. **Make the place exert pressure.** Weather, terrain, distance, social codes, economy:
   the place should limit choices or create problems (the island ferry runs twice a week).
3. **Specific over generic.** Name the plants, the shop types, the local word for the
   thing, the sound of that particular bus. One telling particular beats a panorama.
4. **Place has time.** Season, time of day, historical moment; how the place is changing
   (gentrifying, flooding, emptying).
5. **Mood follows story.** Let the description shift when the character's situation
   shifts — the same kitchen safe in chapter one and threatening in chapter twenty.

## Procedure
1. Research: visit or use street-level imagery, local news, oral histories,
   residents' blogs, maps; collect 30+ specific details per key location in a file.
2. For each key location, write a paragraph from two different characters' viewpoints;
   keep the most revealing details.
3. Introduce a location with a quick anchor (2-3 details) and add details during action
   (see `weaving-description-into-action`).
4. Ask what in this place could only happen here — use it in the plot.

## Gotchas
- Travel-brochure description: pretty, neutral, nobody's point of view.
- Place-agnostic stories: if the story could be moved anywhere unchanged, the setting
  isn't doing work.
- Factual errors that locals will spot (street directions, opening hours, dialect).

## Verify
- [ ] Each key location described through a character's attitude.
- [ ] The place constrains or complicates at least one plot event.
- [ ] Details are specific to this place, not generic.
