# Read-aloud revision

**Goal:** catch rhythm, repetition and dialogue problems your eyes skip. Browne & King
(*Self-Editing for Fiction Writers*, "See How It Sounds") recommend reading aloud as a
core self-editing step, especially for dialogue.

## Methods
1. **Read aloud yourself**, at performance pace, with a pencil (or a printout).
   Slower than silent reading — budget about an hour per 8,000-10,000 words.
2. **Text-to-speech:** Word's *Read Aloud* (Review tab), macOS *Spoken Content*, or
   other TTS tools. A synthetic voice reads what is on the page, not what you meant —
   good for catching missing and doubled words.
3. **Someone else reads it to you** — where they stumble, readers will too.

## What to mark
| Mark | Problem | Typical fix |
|---|---|---|
| Stumble | Tangled syntax, ambiguous phrasing | Reorder, split |
| Breath runs out | Over-long sentence | Split or restructure |
| Sing-song | Repeated rhythm/sentence pattern | Vary length/structure |
| Echo | Same word or sound close together | Replace or cut (see `echo-words-and-repetition`) |
| Clunk | Unintended rhyme or alliteration | Change a word |
| Wooden dialogue | Nobody talks like that | Contract, cut, loosen |
| Boredom | Your voice goes flat | Cut or compress the passage |

## Procedure
- Don't stop to fix while reading — mark and keep going so you hear the flow.
- Revise marked passages, then re-read just those aloud.
- Do a full read-aloud at least once, before submission, after structural edits are done.

## Gotchas
- Reading in your head while moving your lips isn't reading aloud.
- Performing too dramatically can mask weak lines; read plainly.

## Verify
- [ ] Full manuscript read aloud (or TTS) at least once.
- [ ] Every mark addressed.
- [ ] All dialogue spoken aloud at least once.
