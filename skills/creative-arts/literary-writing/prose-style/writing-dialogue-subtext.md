# Subtext in dialogue

**Goal:** conversations where what characters mean differs from what they say, so the
reader works out the real conflict. Hemingway's "Hills Like White Elephants" is the
standard teaching example: a couple discuss drinks and the landscape while arguing
about an abortion that is never named.

## Set up before writing the scene
For each speaker, write privately:
1. **What they want** from this conversation.
2. **What they can't or won't say** (fear, shame, politeness, power).
3. **The surface topic** they'll talk about instead (dishes, plans, a TV show).

## Techniques
- **Displacement:** argue about a small thing that stands for the big one.
- **Evasion:** answer a different question; change the subject; joke.
- **Under-reaction / over-reaction:** "That's nice" to big news; rage at a spilled drink.
- **Silence and gesture:** a beat that contradicts the words (`"I'm happy for you." He
  rinsed the same cup again.`).
- **Repetition with change:** the same phrase means something new the third time.
- **Power moves:** interruption, refusal to answer, using a name or title.

## Revision moves
1. Highlight lines where a character states a feeling or motive outright ("I'm jealous
   because..."). Rewrite most as behaviour or displacement.
2. Read the scene with only the dialogue: does the real conflict still come through?
3. Ask a reader: what is this scene really about? If they can't say, add a clue; if the
   characters say it, remove it.

## When to be direct
At the climax of a relationship, a long-suppressed truth stated plainly can land hard —
precisely because the rest was subtext. Use it once.

## Gotchas
- **Too oblique:** readers must have enough context (prior scene, a clue) to decode it.
- **Therapy-speak** where characters analyse themselves.
- Narration explaining the subtext after the dialogue.

## Verify
- [ ] Each speaker's hidden want and surface topic written before drafting.
- [ ] Stated feelings mostly converted to behaviour or displacement.
- [ ] A cold reader can name the real conflict.
