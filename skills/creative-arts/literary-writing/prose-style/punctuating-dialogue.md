# Punctuating dialogue

**Goal:** dialogue punctuated so readers never stumble, following *The Chicago Manual of
Style* (US trade fiction standard; see its chapters on punctuation and on quotations and
dialogue) or the house style of your publisher.

## Tags vs beats
- **Tag (speech verb) — comma, lowercase tag:** `"I'm leaving," she said.`
- **Question/exclamation keeps its mark; tag still lowercase:** `"Now?" he asked.`
- **Tag first:** `She said, "I'm leaving."`
- **Beat (action, not speech) — full stop, new sentence:** `"I'm leaving." She picked up her keys.`
  Wrong: `"I'm leaving," she picked up her keys.` (You can't pick up a line of dialogue.)
- **Smiled/laughed/shrugged are beats,** not tags.

## Splitting a line
- Same sentence continues: `"If you go," he said, "don't come back."`
- New sentence after the tag: `"I'm going," she said. "Don't wait up."`
- Action interrupts mid-sentence (CMOS: em dashes outside the quotes):
  `"I don't"—she glanced at the door—"know."`

## Interruption and trailing off
- **Cut off by another speaker or event:** em dash inside the quote: `"Wait, I—"`
- **Trailing off or faltering:** ellipsis: `"I thought... never mind."` (CMOS spaced
  vs unspaced ellipsis styles vary by house; be consistent.)
- **Stammer:** `"I-I didn't."`

## Paragraphing
- **New speaker, new paragraph.** Their action beats go in their paragraph.
- **Multi-paragraph speech:** open quotes at the start of each paragraph; close only at the
  end of the last.

## Nesting and regional style
- **US (CMOS):** double quotes; single for quotes within quotes; commas and periods
  always inside the closing quote.
- **UK (many publishers):** single quotes, double within; commas/periods placed by sense
  (often still inside for dialogue). Follow the house style sheet.

## Thoughts and other speech
- Direct thought: often italics without quotes; or roman with a tag ("I'm late, she thought").
- Remembered or quoted speech inside narration: double quotes; texts/messages often italics
  or a distinct style — record the choice in a style sheet.

## Verify
- [ ] Every tag uses a comma; every beat is its own sentence.
- [ ] Interruptions use em dashes, trailing speech uses ellipses consistently.
- [ ] Multi-paragraph speeches have no closing quote until the end.
- [ ] One quote style (US or UK) throughout, recorded on the style sheet.
