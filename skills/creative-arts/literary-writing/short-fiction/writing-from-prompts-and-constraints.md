# Generating stories from prompts and constraints

**Goal:** use prompts and formal constraints to produce material you wouldn't invent
freely, then revise it into a story that stands on its own.

## Prompt recipes
- **Triad:** pick one from each list — a character with a want, a place, an object — and
  make the object matter. (Many workshops use cards or dice for this.)
- **First line from elsewhere:** borrow a line (from a newspaper, overheard), then cut it in revision.
- **Situation prompts:** "Someone returns something they stole years ago."
- **Photo prompts:** write what happened ten minutes before the photo.
- **Contest/journal themes:** treat the theme sideways; the obvious take is what everyone sends.

## Constraints (after the Oulipo, founded 1960 by Queneau and Le Lionnais)
- **Lipogram:** avoid a letter (Perec's *La Disparition* omits "e").
- **N+7:** replace each noun with the seventh noun after it in a dictionary — surreal raw material.
- **Exact word counts** (drabble, 50 words).
- **Abecedarian:** each sentence starts with the next letter of the alphabet.
- **Single sentence story**, **story in questions**, **story as a list**.
- **Restricted vocabulary or POV:** second person; only dialogue; no adjectives.

## From exercise to story
1. Write the constrained draft fast.
2. Identify what's alive (a character, an image, a voice).
3. Decide whether the constraint still serves the story; if not, drop it and revise freely.
4. Check the result has a change, not just a clever form.

## Gotchas
- Constraint as the point: readers don't care how hard it was.
- Sending prompt-pieces to the prompt's contest unrevised.

## Verify
- [ ] Prompt or constraint recorded with the draft.
- [ ] Revision pass made with the question "does the form still serve?".
- [ ] Story has a change and stands without knowing the constraint.
