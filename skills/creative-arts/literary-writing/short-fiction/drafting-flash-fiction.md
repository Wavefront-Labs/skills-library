# Drafting a flash fiction piece

**Goal:** a complete story — with change, not just mood — in a few hundred to about 1,000
words (some markets set 1,500; "sudden fiction" anthologies used up to ~1,750). Always
check the market's limit.

## Shape
- **One moment, one turn.** Flash usually dramatises a single situation and its shift.
- **Start in motion:** first sentence inside the situation.
- **Title as extra space:** context, irony or setting the text then doesn't need.
- **End on the turn or just after it** — a resonant image or line; no explanation.

## Drafting process
1. Freewrite the moment fast (10-20 minutes), ignoring length.
2. Find the turn: where does something change? Everything before it is setup to compress.
3. Cut to the bone: first paragraph often goes; summaries become implication.
4. Make every detail do double duty (characterise and set scene).
5. Read aloud; flash relies on rhythm. Fix any sentence that slackens.
6. Count words; trim to the market limit (titles usually don't count — check).

## Forms flash invites
- Single paragraph / single sentence pieces.
- Borrowed forms (a list, a recipe, an obituary) — the "hermit-crab" approach.
- Second-person and present tense are common but not required.

## Markets and reading
Read flash magazines (e.g., SmokeLong Quarterly, Flash Fiction Online, Wigleaf's annual
Top 50 list) to calibrate. Anthologies: *Sudden Fiction*, *Flash Fiction Forward*.

## Gotchas
- **Vignette without change** — beautiful, but no story.
- **Joke structure** (setup-punchline) that reads as a gag.
- Cramming a short story's plot into 1,000 words via summary.

## Verify
- [ ] One moment; a clear turn.
- [ ] Opening in motion; last line resonates without explaining.
- [ ] Under the target market's word limit.
