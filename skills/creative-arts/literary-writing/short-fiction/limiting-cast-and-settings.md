# Limiting cast and settings

**Goal:** a short story that concentrates attention on the few people and places the
central change needs. Every named character and new location costs the reader effort.

## Cast
- **One protagonist, one or two counterparts** usually carry the change. Others are
  functions (the waiter, the neighbour) — often unnamed or named once.
- **Count named characters.** In a 4,000-word story, more than four or five named people
  usually means some are doing the same job.
- **Merge:** two friends who give advice -> one. The sister who reveals the secret and the
  aunt who hosts the funeral -> one sister who does both.
- **Offstage characters** can be powerful (the absent father) without scenes.
- Naming: unnamed minor characters ("the driver") signal unimportance; naming signals they matter.

## Settings
- **Two to four locations** is typical; flash may have one.
- Choose locations that **pressure** the characters: a stuck lift, a hospital corridor, a
  car on a long drive. Confinement intensifies conflict.
- Reuse a setting at the end changed by what's happened (the same kitchen, now empty).

## Process
1. List every named character and location in the draft with word counts of their scenes.
2. For each, write its function in one line. Duplicate functions = merge candidates.
3. Collapse scenes in passing locations into summary or move the action to a main location.

## Gotchas
- Introducing a new character in the last third to solve the problem.
- Travel scenes between locations that exist only to move people.
- Over-trimming: a lone character with nobody to push against needs an internal or
  environmental antagonist.

## Verify
- [ ] Named characters and locations listed with functions; no duplicates.
- [ ] Each location adds pressure or meaning.
- [ ] No new important character after the midpoint.
