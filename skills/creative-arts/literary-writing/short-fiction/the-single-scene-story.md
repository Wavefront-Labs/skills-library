# The single-scene story

**Goal:** a complete story in one continuous scene — no time jumps, one place — like
Hemingway's "Hills Like White Elephants" (a couple at a station bar, roughly the time it
takes for a train to arrive). It echoes the classical unities of time and place.

## Build the scene
1. **Occasion with a clock:** the train is due, the doctor will call, the guests are arriving.
   A built-in deadline produces urgency.
2. **Two (rarely three) characters with opposed wants** about one issue — often unspoken.
3. **Surface topic vs real topic:** they talk about drinks and hills; the real subject is
   the operation. Subtext carries the story.
4. **Beats that escalate:** each exchange moves the balance of power; mark each beat's shift.
5. **A turn:** a line, gesture or decision that changes the relationship; end soon after.

## Getting the past in
No flashbacks. History comes through dialogue (accusations, shared jokes), objects in the
room, and what characters avoid saying.

## Staging
- Keep track of physical positions and objects; use them as beats (a glass, a bead curtain).
- Use the setting's changes (light fading, the train approaching) to mark time passing.

## Gotchas
- A scene that's only conversation with no physical world — anchor bodies and objects.
- Characters explaining their history to each other ("As you know").
- Ending on a stated realisation instead of a turn in behaviour.

## Verify
- [ ] One continuous time span and place.
- [ ] A clock or pressure in the situation.
- [ ] Each beat shifts the balance; a clear turn near the end.
