# Writing a first line

**Goal:** an opening sentence that establishes voice and raises a question strong enough
to carry the reader into the second sentence. In a short story there's no room for a
warm-up page.

## What a strong first line usually does (two or more of)
- **Voice:** a recognisable sensibility from word one. "Lolita, light of my life..."
- **Character in a situation:** someone specific, somewhere, under some pressure.
- **Disruption or oddity:** "As Gregor Samsa awoke one morning from uneasy dreams he found
  himself transformed in his bed into a gigantic insect." (Kafka)
- **A withheld piece:** the line implies something the reader wants explained.
- **Tone contract:** comic, eerie, lyrical — it promises the story's register.

## Drafting method
1. Write the story first; the real first line is often found in revision.
2. **Delete-test:** strike your first paragraph, then the second. If the story starts better
   later, it started late enough there.
3. Draft 10 alternatives: a line of dialogue, an action, a claim, an image, a statement of
   the situation, a line from the middle of the story moved up.
4. Read each with the second sentence; the pair must work together.
5. Check the first line against the ending: echo, inversion or answer strengthens unity.

## Avoid
- Weather and waking up (unless subverted), alarm clocks, mirror descriptions.
- A generic statement any story could start with ("Life is full of surprises").
- A shock line the story can't live up to, or one that's a cheat (it was a dream).
- Rhetorical questions to the reader.

## Gotchas
- Overloading: name, age, setting, backstory in one sentence. Pick one strong element.
- Dialogue openers without anchors — follow quickly with who and where.

## Verify
- [ ] First line has voice and at least one of situation, disruption or withheld question.
- [ ] Delete-test done; the story starts as late as it can.
- [ ] First and last lines read together intentionally.
