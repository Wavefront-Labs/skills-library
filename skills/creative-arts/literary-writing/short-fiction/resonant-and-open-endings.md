# Resonant and open endings

**Goal:** an ending that keeps working after the last line — the central change is
complete, but the meaning radiates rather than being stated.

## Closure vs openness
- **Emotional closure** is required: the character's shift (or failure to shift) has happened.
- **Plot closure** is optional: the reader may not learn whether she leaves him — if the
  story was about her realising she could.
- An **open ending** offers a limited set of possibilities the story has prepared, not a
  void. Readers should argue about which, not ask "did the author forget?".

## Tools for resonance
- **Final image or gesture:** a concrete thing charged by everything before it (Joyce's
  snow in "The Dead"; Joyce's "epiphany" is the classic frame).
- **Echo:** a word, object or line from the opening returns, changed.
- **Dialogue that means more than it says.**
- **Shift of distance:** pull out from the character to a wider view in the last lines.
- **Understatement:** the quietest line after the loudest moment.

## Revision moves
1. Cover the last paragraph. Is the story better ending one paragraph (or sentence) earlier?
   Often yes — the last paragraph explains.
2. Underline the final sentence's last word; strong endings tend to land on a concrete,
   stressed word, not "again" or "anymore".
3. Ask readers to say what the story is "about" — if they all say the same moral, the
   ending is over-told; if nobody can say anything, it's under-prepared.

## Gotchas
- **Moral summary** ("And she knew she would never be the same").
- **Abrupt stop** mistaken for open: if nothing has changed, it's an unfinished vignette.
- **Dream or death as an exit** from a story that doesn't earn it.

## Verify
- [ ] Central change identifiable in one sentence.
- [ ] Ending tested one paragraph earlier.
- [ ] Final beat is an image, gesture or line, not an explanation.
