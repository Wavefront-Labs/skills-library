# Contests and reading fees

**Goal:** enter contests that are worth the money and reputationally useful, and avoid
those that exploit writers.

## Vet a contest
- **Sponsor:** an established magazine, press, university or foundation with a track
  record. Look up past winners and whether their books/stories were actually published.
- **Judge:** named in advance is a good sign; check judges' conflict-of-interest policy.
  The Council of Literary Magazines and Presses (CLMP) publishes a contest code of ethics
  that many reputable sponsors follow.
- **Prize vs fee:** typical story contest fees run roughly $15-30, book contests $25-35;
  the prize should include publication and meaningful money.
- **Rights:** read what you grant. Avoid contests that take rights to all entries (not just
  winners) or claim copyright.
- **Warning signs:** fees with no named judge, "everyone's a finalist" anthologies you must
  buy, vanity "awards" with high fees. Check Writer Beware's blog.

## Fees in general submissions
- Many literary magazines charge a small reading fee ($2-5) via Submittable to offset
  costs; many also offer free reading periods or fee waivers — use them.
- Paying markets with no fee exist; Duotrope and Poets & Writers' databases filter by fee.

## Budget and strategy
- Set an annual contest budget; log each entry (fee, date, notification date, result).
- Enter work that's **ready**; contests are not feedback (some offer paid critique, separate).
- Most contests allow simultaneous submission; withdraw promptly if a piece is taken elsewhere.
- Free sources: Poets & Writers Grants & Awards database, Authors Publish, magazine newsletters.

## Gotchas
- Entering the same unrevised story in many contests after it's been widely rejected.
- Missing that a contest requires previously unpublished work (including blogs).

## Verify
- [ ] Sponsor, judge and past winners checked.
- [ ] Rights and eligibility rules read.
- [ ] Entry logged against the annual budget.
