# Microfiction and drabbles

**Goal:** a complete story at very short, often exact, word counts. Common forms:
- **Drabble:** exactly 100 words (title usually excluded). Associated with British SF fandom
  (the Birmingham University SF Society in the 1980s); widespread in fan fiction.
- **Dribble / minisaga:** 50 words (the *Daily Telegraph* minisaga competitions used 50).
- **55 Fiction:** 55 words (Steve Moss, *New Times* contest).
- **Six-word story:** often attributed to Hemingway ("For sale: baby shoes, never worn"),
  though the attribution is doubtful.
- **Microfiction** in magazines: often under 100 or under 300 words.

## Technique
- **Imply the plot; dramatise one beat.** The reader infers before and after.
- **The title is your budget extender:** setting, character or irony go there.
- **Specific nouns over adjectives.** "Her father's Timex" says more than "an old watch".
- **One turn, late.** The last line or last word turns the meaning.
- **Every word scanned:** cut articles, "that", "just", "very"; contract where natural.

## Hitting an exact count
1. Draft long (150-200 words).
2. Cut to near the target.
3. Adjust with swaps: "did not" / "didn't", hyphenated compounds (counting convention
   matters: check whether the market counts hyphenated words as one).
4. Count with a tool, not by eye; recount after every edit.

## Gotchas
- Exact-count forms reject entries at 99 or 101; follow the rules precisely.
- Puns and punchlines dominate weak micro; aim for resonance.
- Titles that are full sentences may be counted by some contests — read the rules.

## Verify
- [ ] Word count verified with a tool against the form's exact rule.
- [ ] Story has an implied before/after and a turn.
- [ ] Title adds information or irony.
