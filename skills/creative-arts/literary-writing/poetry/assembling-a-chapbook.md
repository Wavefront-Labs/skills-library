# Assembling a chapbook

**Goal:** a short, unified collection of poems that works as a whole — often a poet's
first publication. Chapbooks typically run around 15-30 pages of poems (presses and
contests specify their own ranges, commonly about 16-40 pages).

## Choose the thread
Chapbooks reward **tight unity** more than full-length books:
- One subject (a father's illness, a river, a job).
- One form or mode (a sequence of sonnets; persona poems in one voice).
- One narrative or time period.
Gather candidates; cut strong poems that don't fit the thread — they can go in a later book.

## Sequence
1. Print every poem; lay them out on a floor or table.
2. Pick the **opener** (a poem that introduces the voice and the thread, accessible and
   strong) and the **closer** (resonant, a sense of arrival or opening).
3. Arrange the middle for movement: emotional or narrative arc, variations of length and
   form, image echoes linking adjacent poems (last image of one, first of the next).
4. Avoid placing near-twins side by side.
5. Read the whole sequence aloud in one sitting.

## Placing it
- **Chapbook contests and open reading periods** (lists in Poets & Writers' databases).
  Many charge reading fees; check press reputation, past titles, production quality,
  and whether the prize includes copies and distribution.
- **Small and micro-presses** with chapbook series; read their books before submitting.
- **Self-production:** handmade (saddle-stitched, letterpress or digital printing) for
  readings; or print-on-demand. Many respected poets started this way.
- Check whether poems previously published in journals need acknowledgment (they usually do — an acknowledgments page).

## Manuscript format
Title page, table of contents, acknowledgments page, one poem per page (long poems
continue), page numbers, standard font; follow each contest's anonymity rules (no name
on manuscript if blind-judged).

## Verify
- [ ] Unifying thread statable in a sentence.
- [ ] Opener and closer chosen deliberately.
- [ ] Sequence read aloud end to end.
- [ ] Press/contest vetted; guidelines (length, anonymity) met.
