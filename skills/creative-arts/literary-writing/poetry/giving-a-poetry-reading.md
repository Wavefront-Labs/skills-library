# Giving a poetry reading

**Goal:** a reading that respects the time slot, lets each poem land, and leaves the
audience wanting the book. Applies to open mics, launches, festival slots and guest readings.

## Build the set
1. **Confirm the slot length** and whether it includes intro and Q&A. Plan to finish
   **a minute or two under**.
2. **Time every poem aloud.** A page of lyric verse is roughly 1-2 minutes.
3. **Choose for listening:** clear, varied poems; avoid three long elegies in a row.
4. **Order:** open with an accessible, confident poem; vary tone and length; end on
   a strong one. For a book launch, include poems representing the book's range.
5. **Total:** poems should be about 80% of the time; patter 20% at most.

## Prepare the reading copy
- Large-font printout or a marked book: slash marks for pauses, underlines for stress,
  notes on intro lines. Number the pages.
- Practise the whole set aloud twice, timed.

## Introductions (patter)
- One to three sentences per poem: occasion, context a listener needs, a dedication. Don't
  explain the poem's meaning or apologise.
- Name the poem's title clearly; repeat it after for longer poems, if helpful.

## Delivery
- **Slower than you think;** pause at line and stanza breaks briefly.
- **Microphone:** a hand's width from the mouth, steady distance; turn your head away for
  breaths, not from the mic while speaking.
- Look up at the ends of lines or stanzas; eye contact at key lines.
- Let silences follow endings before speaking again.

## Etiquette
- Stay within time; thank the host and other readers; mention books for sale once.
- At open mics, sign up early, read only your slot, listen to others.

## Gotchas
- Reading new poems you haven't practised aloud.
- Ten-minute preambles before the first poem.

## Verify
- [ ] Set timed under the slot, including patter.
- [ ] Reading copy marked and paginated.
- [ ] Rehearsed aloud twice with a timer.
