# Writing haiku and tanka

**Goal:** short Japanese-derived forms written in English with their essential
qualities — concreteness, juxtaposition, seasonal awareness — not just a syllable count.

## Haiku
- Japanese haiku count **on** (sound units), 5-7-5 = 17 on. English syllables are longer
  than on, so many English-language haiku poets write fewer than 17 syllables, often in
  three short lines of roughly short-long-short. The Haiku Society of America's
  definitions describe the form without requiring 5-7-5.
- **Kigo:** a season word or reference (cherry blossoms -> spring; cicadas -> summer).
- **Kireji / the cut:** a pause or break dividing the poem into two parts, so two images
  are **juxtaposed** and spark meaning between them. In English, a dash, colon, ellipsis or line break does the cutting.
  Basho: *an old pond— / a frog jumps in / the sound of water* (common translation).
- **Present moment and concreteness:** images, not explanation or abstraction; usually
  no metaphor stated, no title, minimal punctuation.
- **Senryu:** same shape, about human nature, often humorous, no kigo needed.

## Tanka
- Five lines, 5-7-5-7-7 on in Japanese; in English, often short-long-short-long-long without strict counts.
- Older than haiku; more room for emotion and personal statement.
- A **pivot / turn** around line 3: the first part presents an image, the second a
  personal response or shift.

## Procedure (haiku)
1. Carry a notebook; record moments of noticing with sensory detail.
2. Write the image as plainly as possible.
3. Look for a second image or element that, set beside it, creates resonance.
4. Cut every word that explains the connection.
5. Read aloud; check the cut lands.

## Gotchas
- 5-7-5 statements of opinion ("Autumn makes me sad") — counting syllables isn't haiku.
- Over-explaining the juxtaposition.
- Forced kigo when the moment has none (write senryu instead).

## Verify
- [ ] Two juxtaposed parts with a clear cut.
- [ ] Concrete, present-tense image; no stated moral.
- [ ] Season reference present (haiku) or turn present (tanka).
