# Writing a villanelle

**Goal:** a villanelle whose repetitions build pressure and change meaning. Famous
models: Dylan Thomas's "Do not go gentle into that good night", Elizabeth Bishop's "One
Art", Theodore Roethke's "The Waking".

## The form
- 19 lines: five tercets + a final quatrain.
- Two rhyme sounds only: `a` and `b`.
- Two refrains: **A1** (line 1) and **A2** (line 3), which rhyme with each other.
- Pattern:
  ```
  A1 b A2
  a b A1
  a b A2
  a b A1
  a b A2
  a b A1 A2
  ```
- Usually iambic pentameter in English, though not required.

## Procedure
1. **Write the refrains first.** They must bear repetition six/four times: strong,
   open to more than one reading, syntactically flexible. They should be able to answer
   or contradict each other at the end.
2. Choose rhyme sounds with many options: you need 7 `a` words (beyond the refrains, 5
   more) and 6 `b` words. List candidates before drafting.
3. Draft the middle tercets so each refrain arrives in a new context — as answer, as
   question, ironically, more desperately.
4. **Vary the refrains** slightly where meaning demands (Bishop alters "The art of
   losing isn't hard to master" across stanzas; the final "(*Write* it!)" breaks the frame).
5. The final quatrain joins A1 and A2: make the pairing land as climax or reversal.

## Gotchas
- Refrains that are complete, closed statements — they get dull.
- Middle stanzas that just fill space between refrains; each tercet must advance.
- Rhyme words chosen first and sense bent to fit them.

## Verify
- [ ] 19 lines, correct refrain placement, two rhyme sounds.
- [ ] Each refrain recurrence means something different.
- [ ] Final quatrain pairs the refrains with new force.
