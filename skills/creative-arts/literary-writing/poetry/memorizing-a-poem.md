# Memorizing and delivering a poem

**Goal:** recite a poem from memory reliably under pressure, with delivery that serves
meaning. Recitation programmes like Poetry Out Loud (US national recitation contest)
judge physical presence, voice and articulation, dramatic appropriateness, evidence of
understanding and accuracy.

## Memorise
1. **Understand it first:** paraphrase each sentence; look up every word and allusion;
   know the speaker and situation. Meaning is the strongest memory cue.
2. **Map the structure:** stanza topics, the turn, repeated words, rhyme scheme — write a
   one-line cue per stanza.
3. **Chunk:** learn line one; then one-two; then one-three (cumulative). Move to the next
   stanza only when the previous is solid, then link stanzas.
4. **Use imagery:** visualise each image in sequence (a memory-palace walk works well).
5. **First-letter prompts:** write only first letters of each word; recite from that.
6. **Spaced rehearsal:** short sessions over several days beat one long session. Recite
   before sleep and on waking.
7. **Test in conditions:** standing, aloud, to someone, with distractions.

## Deliver
- **Speak the sentences, not the lines:** follow punctuation; enjambed lines flow on.
- **Avoid sing-song** in metrical/rhymed poems — stress meaning words, not the beat.
- **Pacing:** slow down for key images; pause at stanza breaks and before the turn.
- **Stillness:** gesture only when it arises naturally; let the voice carry it.
- **Recovery:** if you blank, pause, breathe, go to the stanza cue; don't apologise.

## Gotchas
- Memorising by rote before understanding — leads to monotone and blanks.
- Paraphrase drift: small word substitutions; check accuracy against the text.

## Verify
- [ ] Poem recited error-free three times on separate days.
- [ ] Stanza cue list memorised.
- [ ] Delivery rehearsed for meaning, not metre.
