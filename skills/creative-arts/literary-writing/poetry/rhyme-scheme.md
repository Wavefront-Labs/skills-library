# Rhyme schemes

**Goal:** choose and execute a rhyme pattern that supports the poem rather than
dictating its words.

## Notation
Label line-end sounds with letters: the first rhyme sound is *a*, the next new sound
*b*, and so on. Unrhymed lines are often marked *x*. A Shakespearean sonnet:
`abab cdcd efef gg`.

## Common schemes
| Scheme | Pattern | Effect / use |
|---|---|---|
| Heroic couplet | `aa bb cc` (iambic pentameter) | Epigrammatic, witty, closed (Pope) |
| Ballad stanza | `xaxa` or `abab`, alternating 4/3 stresses | Narrative, song |
| Envelope (In Memoriam) | `abba` | Enclosing, meditative |
| Terza rima | `aba bcb cdc ...` | Forward chaining (Dante) |
| Rhyme royal | `ababbcc` | Narrative stateliness (Chaucer) |
| Ottava rima | `abababcc` | Comic or epic; couplet turn (Byron) |
| Spenserian stanza | `ababbcbcc` | Lush narrative |

## Choosing
- **Closure vs flow:** couplets close each thought; interlaced schemes (abab, terza
  rima) pull forward.
- **The final couplet** of a stanza delivers a turn or punchline — use it for that.
- Strict schemes suit wit, song, formality; slant rhyme and irregular schemes suit
  contemporary or conversational registers.

## Avoiding forced rhyme
- Inverted syntax to reach a rhyme ("to the shore went we") is the clearest tell.
- Filler words chosen only for rhyme.
- Fixes: rewrite the whole line so the rhyme word comes naturally; use slant rhyme;
  let the rhyme fall on a less prominent line; choose rhyme sounds with many options
  (-ay, -ee) for long schemes, sparse sounds (-orange) never.

## Procedure
1. Draft the content first, roughly, in lines.
2. Pick a scheme whose shape suits the content's movement.
3. Revise toward the scheme line by line, testing each rhyme word for sense.

## Verify
- [ ] Scheme notated and followed (or varied deliberately).
- [ ] No inverted syntax or filler for rhyme.
- [ ] Rhyme words carry meaning, not padding.
