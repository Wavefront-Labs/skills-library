# Extended metaphor and conceit

**Goal:** a metaphor sustained across a poem (or long passage) that discovers new
meaning as it develops. A **conceit** is an elaborate, often surprising extended
comparison: the **metaphysical conceit** (Donne, Herbert, Marvell) yokes unlike things
by ingenuity; the **Petrarchan conceit** exaggerates the beloved (eyes as suns).

## Model
Donne, "A Valediction: Forbidding Mourning": the lovers' souls as the two feet of a
drawing compass — one fixed, one roaming; the fixed foot "leans and hearkens" after the
other and makes its circle "just", ending where it began. Each property of the vehicle
maps to a property of the relationship.

## Procedure
1. **Choose a vehicle with many parts and actions** (a house, a ship, a garden, a
   machine, a game, a trade). List 10-15 of its parts, processes and vocabulary.
2. **Map** each to an aspect of the tenor; strike ones that don't fit. Keep the
   surprising-but-apt ones.
3. **Develop logically:** move through the vehicle in an order (from foundation to
   roof; from launch to wreck) so the poem has a path.
4. **Vary the angle:** literal moments, verbs drawn from the vehicle's vocabulary,
   implied metaphor in diction ("we *mortgaged* the summer").
5. **Turn:** near the end, let the metaphor reveal something — break down, reverse, or
   return to the literal.

## Gotchas
- Mixing a second vehicle in (see
  `creative-arts.literary-writing.prose-style.cliche-and-mixed-metaphor`).
- Mechanical one-to-one mapping that becomes a puzzle without feeling.
- Vehicle so strange that the reader can't hold the tenor in mind.

## Verify
- [ ] Vehicle mapped with a part list before drafting.
- [ ] Metaphor consistent and developed in an order.
- [ ] Poem ends with a turn the metaphor makes possible.
