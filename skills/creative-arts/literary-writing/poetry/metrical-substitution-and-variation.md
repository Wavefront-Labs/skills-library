# Metrical substitution and variation

**Goal:** use departures from a metre to create emphasis and naturalness. Paul Fussell
(*Poetic Meter and Poetic Form*) argues that expressive meaning lives in the tension
between the metrical pattern and the variations from it. Strict regularity drones.

## Common variations in iambic verse
| Variation | Pattern | Effect | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Trochaic inversion** | `/ x` in place of `x /` | Jolt, emphasis on first word | Very common in first foot and after a caesura; rare in the last foot |
| **Spondee** | `/ /` | Weight, slowness | Anywhere; marks key phrases |
| **Pyrrhic** | `x x` | Lightness, speed; often paired with a spondee (`x x / /`, the "double iamb" or ionic) | Mid-line |
| **Feminine ending** | extra unstressed syllable at line end | Softer, more conversational close | "To be or not to be, that is the ques-tion" |
| **Headless (acephalous) line** | first unstressed syllable dropped | Abrupt start | Line openings |
| **Elision** | two syllables slurred into one (*heav'n*, *th'*) | Keeps count | Older verse; modern poets let readers elide naturally |
| **Anapestic substitution** | `x x /` | Quickening | Loose iambics |

## Using variation
1. Establish the norm first: a few regular lines teach the reader the pattern.
2. Place variations on the words that matter: a trochee to hit a verb hard, a spondee on
   an image.
3. Mirror meaning: spondees for heaviness, pyrrhic-anapestic runs for speed.
4. Don't vary everything — if every line deviates, there's no pattern to deviate from.

## Practice
- Scan a Shakespeare sonnet and mark every substitution (see
  `creative-arts.literary-writing.poetry.line-breaks-and-metre` for basic scansion).
- Write ten regular iambic pentameter lines, then revise each with one meaningful substitution.

## Gotchas
- Trochee in the final foot of an iambic line usually breaks the metre.
- Mistaking normal speech stress for "variation" — scan natural stress, not sing-song.

## Verify
- [ ] Norm established before major variations.
- [ ] Variations fall on meaning-bearing words.
- [ ] Final feet remain iambic (or variation is deliberate).
