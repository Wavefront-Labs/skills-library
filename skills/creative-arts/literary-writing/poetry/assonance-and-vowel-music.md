# Assonance and vowel music

**Goal:** use the repetition and length of vowel sounds to bind a poem and control its pace.

## Terms
- **Assonance:** repetition of vowel sounds in nearby words, without matching consonants:
  *"the low moan of the old road"* (long *o*).
- **Euphony:** pleasant, smooth sound (long vowels, liquids, nasals).
- **Cacophony:** harsh, discordant sound (short vowels, clustered plosives) — a tool, not a fault.

## Vowel length and pace
- **Long vowels** (*moan, sleep, dawn, fire*) take longer to say; they slow a line and
  suit elegy, meditation, longing.
- **Short vowels** (*bit, cat, pick, up*) speed it; they suit quickness, comedy, tension.
- Tennyson's *"The moan of doves in immemorial elms, / And murmuring of innumerable bees"*
  slows with long vowels and nasals — sound enacting drowsy summer.

## Procedure
1. Read aloud slowly; underline the stressed vowel in each stressed syllable.
2. Look for runs of one vowel sound — do they fall on the words that matter?
3. Where a line should slow (a pause, a moment of weight), shift toward long vowels and
   open syllables; where it should hurry, toward short vowels.
4. Use **assonantal chains** across line breaks to bind lines without full rhyme
   (helpful in free verse).
5. Check accent: vowel sounds vary across dialects; write for your own ear but notice
   pairs that only work in one accent.

## Gotchas
- Accidental sing-song from too much matching.
- Reading visually: *love / move / rove* look alike but differ in sound.
- Sacrificing the precise word for a vowel effect — sense first.

## Verify
- [ ] Stressed vowels marked in key lines.
- [ ] Vowel length supports the pace of each passage.
- [ ] Assonance falls on important words.
