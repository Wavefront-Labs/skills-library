# Poetry slam rules and strategy

**Goal:** perform competitively in a slam, following the common rules and making smart
choices about poems and order. The slam began with Marc Kelly Smith at the Green Mill
in Chicago in the mid-1980s; rules below follow the long-standing conventions of national
US slams (Poetry Slam, Inc.). Local slams vary — always confirm with the slammaster.

## Standard rules
- **Original work:** poems must be written by the performer.
- **Time limit:** usually **3 minutes**, with a short grace period (commonly 10 seconds),
  after which points are deducted in increments (often 0.5 per 10 seconds over).
- **No props, costumes or musical accompaniment.** The body and voice only.
- **Judges:** typically five audience members chosen at random, scoring 0.0-10.0 (one
  decimal). The high and low scores are dropped; the middle three are summed.
- **Sacrificial poet:** a non-competing poet often performs first to calibrate the judges.
- **Rounds:** multiple rounds, sometimes with cuts; may need 2-4 different poems.

## Strategy
- **Know your times exactly:** rehearse with a stopwatch; aim for 2:40-2:55 to allow
  for applause pauses and nerves.
- **Order:** score creep is common (judges score later poets higher), so draw position
  matters; many slammers start with a strong accessible poem to get into later rounds, and
  save the strongest for the final round.
- **Accessibility:** clear images and emotional stakes score well; humor works in early
  rounds.
- **Memorise** (paper is allowed in most slams but costs connection).
- **Respect:** "the points are not the point; the point is the poetry" — the slam motto.

## Gotchas
- Going over time: penalties often decide close slams.
- Incidental props (a phone used as a prop, holding an object) can be ruled against.
- Performing someone else's poem or a cover.

## Verify
- [ ] Local rules confirmed with the host.
- [ ] Each poem timed in rehearsal under 3:00.
- [ ] Set list ordered for rounds; poems memorised.
