# Writing a ghazal

**Goal:** a ghazal that keeps the form's defining features rather than any poem in
couplets. The form comes from Arabic and flourished in Persian (Hafez, Rumi) and Urdu
(Ghalib); Agha Shahid Ali championed the strict form in English (*Ravishing
DisUnities: Real Ghazals in English*).

## The form
- Five or more **couplets (sher)**, traditionally up to about fifteen.
- Each couplet is **autonomous** — complete in itself, like a separate gem on a string;
  linked by mood and the refrain, not by narrative.
- **Radif:** a word or phrase repeated at the end of both lines of the first couplet
  and the second line of every couplet after.
- **Qafia:** a rhyme immediately **before** the radif in those same lines.
- **Matla:** the opening couplet, where both lines carry qafia + radif.
- **Maqta:** the final couplet, traditionally including the poet's name or pen name
  (**takhallus**), often addressing themselves.
- Lines roughly equal in length (traditional metre; in English, consistent syllable or stress count).

Example scheme (radif "in the rain", qafia -ong):
```
...long in the rain / ...song in the rain     (matla)
...........................
...wrong in the rain
...........................
...belong in the rain
```

## Procedure
1. Choose a radif that can recur in many contexts (a short phrase works better than a single noun).
2. List 10+ qafia words that rhyme and can sit right before the radif.
3. Draft couplets independently; each needs a turn between line 1 (setup) and line 2 (landing).
4. Traditional themes: longing, love (earthly and divine), loss, wine, the beloved —
   contemporary ghazals range widely, but the yearning tone is part of the tradition.
5. Write the maqta with your name or a self-address.

## Gotchas
- Calling any couplet poem a ghazal without radif and qafia (Shahid Ali's complaint about "free-verse ghazals").
- Couplets that depend on one another narratively.
- Radif that becomes a mechanical tag rather than gaining meaning.

## Verify
- [ ] Matla has radif and qafia in both lines.
- [ ] Every later couplet's second line ends qafia + radif.
- [ ] Each couplet stands alone.
- [ ] Maqta includes a signature or self-address.
