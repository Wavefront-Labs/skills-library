# Building concrete images

**Goal:** poems whose feeling travels through images the reader can see, hear, touch.
Addonizio & Laux (*The Poet's Companion*) begin their craft section with images; Oliver
(*A Poetry Handbook*, "Imagery") argues that images, not statements, make poems live.

## Imagist principles (Pound, "A Retrospect" / "A Few Don'ts")
1. "Direct treatment of the 'thing' whether subjective or objective."
2. "To use absolutely no word that does not contribute to the presentation."
3. Compose "in the sequence of the musical phrase, not in sequence of a metronome".
Pound: "Go in fear of abstractions." His "In a Station of the Metro" juxtaposes two
images in two lines with no stated link.

## Procedure
1. **Circle abstractions** in the draft: love, grief, beauty, soul, time, pain.
2. For each, ask: *what did it look like, sound like, smell like, in this specific case?*
   Replace with that particular ("grief" -> "his reading glasses still folded on the
   arm of the chair").
3. **Specify nouns:** "bird" -> "grackle"; "flower" -> "chicory".
4. **Activate verbs:** images move ("the field *unbuttons* its frost").
5. **Juxtapose:** place two images side by side and let the reader make the leap.
6. **Layer senses** (see `creative-arts.literary-writing.prose-style.sensory-detail-beyond-sight`).
7. **Trust the image:** delete lines that explain what the image means — often the
   last line of a draft.

## Gotchas
- Pretty images unrelated to the poem's emotional centre.
- Image inventories: a list of nice things with no arc or tension.
- Generic "poetic" images (moon, rose, tears) without a fresh particular.

## Verify
- [ ] Abstractions replaced or earned by preceding images.
- [ ] Nouns specific, verbs active.
- [ ] No line explains an image the reader already felt.
