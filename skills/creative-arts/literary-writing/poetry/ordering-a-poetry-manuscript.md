# Ordering a full-length poetry manuscript

**Goal:** a collection that reads as a book, not a pile of poems. Full-length
collections commonly run about 48-80 pages of poems; contests set their own limits.

## Gather and cut
1. List every finished poem with its subject, form, length and strongest image.
2. Mark the poems that are **necessary** — the core you'd build around.
3. Cut poems that are weaker versions of others, or that belong to a different book.
   A tighter book beats a padded one.

## Find the architecture
Options:
- **Sections** (often 3-4), each with a centre of gravity: theme, period, mode. Section
  titles or epigraphs can frame them.
- **Unsectioned** arc, when the book is a continuous movement.
- **Braided:** a recurring sequence interleaved through the book (numbered poems, a
  repeated title).

## Sequence
- **Opening poem:** an invitation that establishes voice and stakes — often not your
  most difficult poem.
- **Closing poem:** the last note readers carry away.
- **Adjacency:** link poems by image, word, or contrast; vary length and form to avoid
  monotony; separate near-duplicates.
- **Pacing:** place your strongest poems throughout, not only up front.
- **Title poem** placement: a meaningful position (opener, section centre, closer).
- Tools: printouts on the floor, index cards, or a spreadsheet with columns for theme,
  form, length, image.

## Front and back matter
Title page; table of contents; acknowledgments (journals where poems appeared); notes
(sources, epigraphs, forms); dedication optional. For blind contests, remove your name
and identifying acknowledgments as instructed.

## Test
- Read the manuscript start to finish in one sitting, aloud if possible.
- Have one or two trusted readers report where their attention dropped.
- Re-order and cut again.

## Verify
- [ ] Every poem earns its place; no padding.
- [ ] Architecture (sections/arc) can be described in a sentence each.
- [ ] Opening and closing poems chosen deliberately.
- [ ] Contest page limits and anonymity rules met.
