# Organic form in free verse

**Goal:** free verse whose shape grows from the poem's content and movement of
attention, not from habit. Denise Levertov's essay "Some Notes on Organic Form" (1965)
describes it as a method of apperception: the poet discovers the form inherent in an
experience; Coleridge earlier contrasted organic form with "mechanic" form imposed from
outside. Robert Creeley's line, quoted by Olson: "form is never more than an extension of content".

## Practice
1. **Start from the experience,** not the shape: what you perceived, in what order.
2. **Draft as prose or loosely,** keeping the order of perceptions.
3. **Lineate by units of attention:** a line break where the mind turns, pauses or
   adds; Levertov saw the line break as a kind of half-comma pause and a record of thought.
4. **Stanzas as movements:** a new stanza where the poem's thought or scene shifts.
5. **Read aloud;** adjust breaks so the voice performs the hesitation, speed or emphasis
   the experience had.
6. **Test each break:** does ending here create emphasis, ambiguity, or a meaningful pause?
   If not, rejoin.

## What organic form is not
- Not "anything goes": the form must be found, then held consistently within the poem.
- Not prose chopped at random. Every break should be defensible.

## Diagnostic contrasts
- Same poem relineated three ways (short, long, stanza'd): which matches the experience's
  pace? Keep notes of why.
- Look at Levertov, William Carlos Williams ("The Red Wheelbarrow"), Robert Creeley for
  breaks that enact perception.

## Gotchas
- Breaking on weak words (*the, of, and*) by default, unless intentional.
- Uniform stanzas imposed after the fact, fighting the content.
- Line breaks that only create visual "poemness".

## Verify
- [ ] Each break justified by attention, breath or emphasis.
- [ ] Stanza breaks align with shifts in thought or scene.
- [ ] Read aloud, the lineation performs the experience's pace.
