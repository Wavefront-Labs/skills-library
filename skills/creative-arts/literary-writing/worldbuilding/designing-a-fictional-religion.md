# Designing a religion

**Goal:** a believable faith that shapes characters and society, rather than a pantheon
list. Real religions are lived practices with history, politics and disagreement.

## Layers to define
1. **Cosmology and belief:** origins, gods or forces, afterlife, what is sacred and profane,
   the moral core (what makes a good person).
2. **Practice:** prayers, rituals (birth, coming of age, marriage, death), dietary rules,
   dress, holy days and calendar, pilgrimage.
3. **Institutions:** clergy, hierarchy, training, funding, relationship with rulers,
   courts or schools run by the faith.
4. **Texts and authority:** scriptures, oral tradition, prophets, who interprets.
5. **Variation:** sects, heresies, reform movements, folk practice vs official doctrine,
   lapsed and secular believers.
6. **History:** founding, schisms, wars, persecutions, conversions.
7. **Relation to the world's facts:** if gods demonstrably exist or magic is real, how does
   that change faith (certainty, bargaining) vs a world where it's uncertain?

## Bring it into the story
- **Everyday traces:** oaths, idioms, names, architecture, food, what's taboo.
- **Character-level belief:** each major character's relation — devout, doubtful, using it
  for power, rebelling. This is more interesting than the doctrine.
- **Conflict:** religion often drives politics, family pressure, guilt and justification.

## Avoid
- **Monolithic belief** where everyone believes the same way.
- **Thin real-world copies** (a renamed Catholic Church) without understanding; real
  faiths deserve research and care, especially minority or indigenous traditions.
- **Strawman religion** that exists only to be evil.

## Verify
- [ ] Belief, practice, institution, texts and variation each defined.
- [ ] At least three everyday traces appear on the page.
- [ ] Major characters each have a specific relation to the faith.
