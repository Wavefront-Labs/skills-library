# Building a world history timeline

**Goal:** a history deep enough that the present feels caused, recorded in a timeline
you can check for consistency — while only the parts that bear on the story reach the page.

## Work backwards from the story
1. List present-day facts the story depends on: who rules, who hates whom, why the ruins
   exist, why magic is outlawed.
2. For each, ask "why?" and write the **causing event** with an approximate date.
3. Ask "why?" again for the most important ones — two or three layers is usually enough.
4. Group into **eras** with names the inhabitants use (the Sundering, the Long Peace).

## Make it feel real
- **History is contested:** different peoples tell it differently; victors write records.
  Note in-world versions alongside "what really happened".
- **Slow forces** (climate change, migration, trade routes, disease, technology spread)
  matter as much as wars and kings.
- **Living memory:** what do grandparents remember? Events within ~80 years feel present;
  older events become legend.
- **Remnants:** every era leaves physical traces — place names, ruins, laws, songs, grudges.

## Calendar
- Decide year-counting (from a founding, a reign, a cataclysm) and months/seasons
  tied to the world's astronomy and agriculture.
- Keep conversions simple for readers; don't make them do arithmetic.

## The timeline file
- A spreadsheet or tool (Aeon Timeline, a Scrivener document, a wiki) with columns:
  date | event | cause | consequence | who knows about it | source of in-world account.
- Include the **story's own events** day by day at the end, with character ages.

## Gotchas
- Static history: a thousand years with the same technology and borders.
- Ages that don't work (a character remembering an event before they were born).
- Prologue history lectures — reveal through present consequences
  (see `creative-arts.literary-writing.worldbuilding.magic-system-design` on revealing through use).

## Verify
- [ ] Every story-critical present fact traces to a dated cause.
- [ ] At least one historical event has conflicting in-world accounts.
- [ ] Character ages checked against the timeline.
