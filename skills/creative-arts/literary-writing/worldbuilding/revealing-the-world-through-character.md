# Revealing the world through character

**Goal:** readers learn the world because characters live in it, want things from it and
react to it — not because the narrator stops to explain.

## Techniques
1. **Attitude over description:** describe the world as the POV feels about it. A local
   finds the capital's towers oppressive; a tourist finds them thrilling. The judgement
   carries information.
2. **Need-driven exposition:** reveal a rule at the moment the character must use, break or
   avoid it. The reader learns because stakes depend on it.
3. **Conflict-driven exposition:** characters argue about the world ("You can't take a
   name-oath to a Varr; they'll own you"). Disagreement makes information dramatic.
4. **Assumed familiarity:** insiders don't explain the ordinary; leave context clues.
5. **Mistakes and taboos:** a character's slip reveals a custom through consequences.

## Insider or outsider POV?
- **Outsider** (newcomer, apprentice, traveller): natural reason to learn; risk of
  "tour guide" feel. Classic in portal fantasy.
- **Insider:** authentic voice, immersive; risk of confusion. Use context and consistency.
- Mixed casts allow both.

## Fixing info-dumps
- Highlight paragraphs of world explanation. For each: can it move into dialogue with
  conflict, a moment of need, or a single clause? Can it be cut?
- Break necessary exposition into pieces across scenes.
- "As you know, Bob" — characters telling each other what both know — must go.

## Gotchas
- Narrator lectures in close POV (the character wouldn't think in encyclopedia prose).
- Outsider characters asking questions on the reader's behalf for pages.

## Verify
- [ ] World information appears through attitude, need or conflict.
- [ ] No "as you know" exchanges.
- [ ] POV choice (insider/outsider) deliberate and consistent.
