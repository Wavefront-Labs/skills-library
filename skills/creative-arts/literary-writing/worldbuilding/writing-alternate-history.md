# Alternate history

**Goal:** a story set in a world where history diverged at a specific point, with
consequences that are plausible and illuminating. Examples: Philip K. Dick's *The Man in
the High Castle* (Axis victory), Philip Roth's *The Plot Against America*, Michael
Chabon's *The Yiddish Policemen's Union*. The Sidewise Award recognises the genre.

## Steps
1. **Point of divergence (POD):** one event changed — an assassination succeeds, a battle
   goes the other way, an invention comes early. Be precise about date and mechanism.
2. **Research the real period** around the POD deeply: politics, economy, technology,
   key people and their tendencies.
3. **Extrapolate:** near-term consequences first (who takes power, what war follows), then
   long-term (borders, technology, culture). Ask what forces would persist anyway
   (geography, economics, demographics).
4. **Decide the butterfly policy:** do famous people still exist decades after the POD?
   Strict logic says births after the POD would differ; many writers allow "historical
   cameos" deliberately. Choose and be consistent.
5. **Choose the story's time:** right after the POD (history unfolding) or long after (a
   changed world as setting).
6. **Show the change through specifics:** currency, signage, songs, slang, maps, what's
   on the radio — and through characters whose lives were altered.

## Pitfalls to avoid
- **Great-man oversimplification:** one change rarely flips everything; show structural forces.
- **Wish-fulfilment** or apologia for real atrocities; handle sensitive histories with care.
- **Info-dumps** explaining the divergence; reveal it gradually.

## Verify
- [ ] POD defined with date and mechanism.
- [ ] Consequence timeline from POD to story time.
- [ ] Butterfly policy decided; real figures handled consistently.
- [ ] Changed world shown through concrete everyday details.
