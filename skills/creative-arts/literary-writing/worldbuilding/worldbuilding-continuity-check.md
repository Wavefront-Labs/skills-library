# Continuity checks

**Goal:** a manuscript (or series) where names, dates, geography, rules and facts agree
everywhere. Readers of speculative fiction notice inconsistencies quickly.

## Build the bible during revision
Create a document or spreadsheet (or Scrivener research folder; tools like World Anvil,
Campfire or a simple wiki) with tabs:
- **Names:** spelling, pronunciation, titles, nicknames, first appearance (chapter).
- **Places:** spelling, location, distance/travel time from others.
- **Timeline:** dated events, ages of characters, seasons.
- **Rules:** magic/tech, laws, customs, with chapter references for each on-page statement.
- **Objects:** who has what, when it changes hands.
- **Physical traits:** eye colour, scars, handedness.

## The check pass
1. **Extract:** read through and log every world fact with chapter/page.
2. **Search:** use find for each proper noun to catch spelling variants (Kaela/Kayla);
   search alternative spellings.
3. **Cross-check:** travel times vs. days elapsed; ages vs. dates; moon phases and seasons;
   who knows what when.
4. **Rules audit:** every magic or tech use against stated limits.
5. **Fix and record** the canonical version in the bible.

## For series
- Update the bible after each book's final edits (copyeditor's style sheet is a great base).
- Track open threads and promises to readers.

## Gotchas
- Building the bible only after drafting book three.
- Relying on memory for spellings and ages.
- Fixing one instance but not the others; search globally.

## Verify
- [ ] Bible has names, places, timeline, rules, objects, traits.
- [ ] Every proper noun searched for variants.
- [ ] Travel and timeline arithmetic checked.
