# Climate and ecology

**Goal:** environments that behave plausibly and that shape the people and plot living in them.

## Climate basics (Earth-like worlds)
- **Latitude bands:** warm and wet near the equator (rainforest), dry around 30°
  north/south (subtropical high pressure: Sahara, Australian deserts), temperate mid-latitudes,
  cold polar regions.
- **Prevailing winds** (trade winds, westerlies) carry moisture from oceans; mountains
  force rain out on the windward side, leaving rain shadows.
- **Ocean currents:** warm currents mild coasts (Western Europe), cold currents create
  coastal deserts (Atacama).
- **Seasons** from axial tilt; altitude drops temperature roughly 6.5 °C per 1000 m.
- Use the **Köppen classification** to name and check zones (tropical, arid, temperate,
  continental, polar).
- Non-Earth worlds (different tilt, tidally locked, two suns): decide the rule, then
  follow consequences consistently.

## Ecology
- Build **food webs**: producers -> herbivores -> predators. Large predators need large
  ranges and many prey; a dragon population implies a lot of food.
- **Carrying capacity:** cities need hinterlands; deserts support few people.
- Invented creatures: niche (what it eats, what eats it), adaptation to climate,
  relationship with humans (domesticated, pest, sacred).

## Climate shapes culture and story
- Agriculture and diet, building materials, clothing, calendar and festivals (harvest,
  monsoon), trade (what this region lacks and must import).
- Climate as antagonist: drought, winter, floods, storm seasons that set deadlines.
- Climate change in history (see `building-a-world-timeline`).

## Gotchas
- Single-biome planets (ice world, desert world) without explanation.
- Megafauna in impossible numbers.
- Same weather everywhere in the book regardless of season or place.

## Verify
- [ ] Each region's climate explained by latitude, wind, altitude or current.
- [ ] Major creatures have a plausible food supply.
- [ ] Cultures' food, building and calendar reflect their climate.
