# Designing a naming language

**Goal:** names for people and places that sound like they come from one culture,
without building a full language. Mark Rosenfelder's *Language Construction Kit*
(zompist.com) calls this a "naming language" — the minimum viable conlang for fiction.

## 1. Sound palette
- Choose **15-25 sounds**: a set of consonants and 3-6 vowels. Leave out some common
  English sounds to create distinctness (no *th*, no *w*; or lots of *k* and *r*).
- Decide **syllable shapes**: e.g., CV, CVC, V (C = consonant, V = vowel). Allowed
  clusters (*tr* yes, *ng* only at ends).
- Decide stress (first syllable, penultimate) — affects how names feel.
Different cultures in your world get **different palettes**, so readers hear borders.

## 2. Morphemes
Create 20-40 meaningful roots: *river, hill, ford, fort, white, old, new, king, son-of,
daughter-of, holy, north*. Build names by combination, like real toponyms
(English *Ox-ford*, *Kings-ton*; Welsh *Aber-* "river mouth", *Llan-* "church").
- Places: geography + descriptor (*Varn-thal* = "cold valley").
- People: given names from virtues/nature/ancestors; patronymics or clan names.

## 3. Rules and consistency
- Spelling conventions (one sound = one spelling; use *k* or *c*, not both).
- How names change over time and across borders (a foreign rendering of a city).
- Keep a **lexicon** file: morpheme, meaning, example names.

## Readability
- Pronounceable for English readers; avoid apostrophe soup (*K'th'rak*) unless
  apostrophes mark a real sound you've defined.
- Distinct first letters and lengths across major characters (see
  `creative-arts.literary-writing.character-development.character-naming`).
- Check for unfortunate real-world meanings.

## Verify
- [ ] Sound palette and syllable shapes written down per culture.
- [ ] Lexicon of morphemes with meanings.
- [ ] Names in each culture audibly share a palette distinct from neighbours.
- [ ] Main names pronounceable at a glance.
