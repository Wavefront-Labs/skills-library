# Designing alien species

**Goal:** aliens that feel genuinely other yet comprehensible enough for story — not
humans with forehead ridges. Models: Hal Clement's *Mission of Gravity* (biology from a
high-gravity planet), Le Guin's Gethenians in *The Left Hand of Darkness*, Ted Chiang's
heptapods in "Story of Your Life", Octavia Butler's Oankali.

## Build from the environment out
1. **Planet:** gravity, atmosphere, temperature, light (star type), day length, seasons,
   water or other solvent. These constrain body plans.
2. **Biology:** size and structure (heavy gravity → low, robust bodies), metabolism, life
   cycle, reproduction and sexes, lifespan.
3. **Senses:** which senses dominate (echolocation, infrared, electric fields, smell) —
   shapes art, language and metaphors.
4. **Cognition:** how they think about time, individuality, death; hive vs individual.
5. **Communication:** sound, light, scent, colour, gesture — and what's hard to translate.
6. **Social structure:** family, hierarchy, cooperation, conflict, from their evolutionary
   history (predator, prey, herd, solitary).
7. **Technology and culture:** what their bodies and senses make possible or impossible.

## Making difference matter
- **Misunderstanding as plot:** contact stories turn on what humans get wrong.
- **One radical difference** explored deeply beats many cosmetic ones.
- **Point of view:** an alien POV demands a different sensory vocabulary and logic.

## Gotchas
- **Planet of hats:** a whole species with one trait (warlike, logical).
- Aliens as thin metaphors for real human groups — can reinforce stereotypes.
- Human-like body plans and psychology without justification (fine for soft SF; choose deliberately).

## Verify
- [ ] Environment parameters written; biology derived from them.
- [ ] Senses, cognition and communication defined.
- [ ] At least one difference drives plot conflict.
