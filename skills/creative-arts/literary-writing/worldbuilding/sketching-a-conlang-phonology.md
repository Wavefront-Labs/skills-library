# Sketching a conlang phonology

**Goal:** a language sketch deeper than a naming language — enough to write
consistent phrases, songs or inscriptions — following Rosenfelder's *Language
Construction Kit* sequence (sounds, then words, then grammar).

## 1. Phoneme inventory
- List consonants and vowels in **IPA** (International Phonetic Alphabet chart), e.g.
  `/p t k b d g m n s ʃ l r j w/`, vowels `/a e i o u/`.
- Real languages range widely; 20-30 phonemes is typical and safe. Include a feature or two
  English lacks (/x/ as in Scottish *loch*, a vowel length contrast) for flavour.

## 2. Phonotactics
- Allowed syllable structure (e.g., `(C)(C)V(C)`), permitted clusters, which consonants
  can end words.
- Stress rule (fixed or variable).
- These rules make invented words consistent automatically.

## 3. Romanisation
- Decide how each phoneme is spelled for English readers (`/ʃ/` = *sh*; `/x/` = *kh*).
- Use diacritics sparingly — they complicate typesetting and ebook display; test them in your EPUB.
- Publish a short pronunciation guide (see `glossary-and-pronunciation-guide`).

## 4. Sound changes (for history and dialects)
Apply a few regular changes to derive an older or sister language: e.g., *p > f*
between vowels; final vowels drop. Names then show history (old city name vs modern
form). Tools: sound-change appliers (Rosenfelder's online SCA).

## 5. Grammar sketch (just enough)
- Word order (SOV, SVO, VSO...), how plurals and possession work, pronouns, a few verb
  forms. Build a 100-300 word lexicon of words the story needs.
- Translate the phrases you'll actually use and keep them consistent.

## Gotchas
- Building a full language instead of writing the book: scope to need.
- Relexing English (English grammar with substituted words) — fine for names, thin for full sentences.
- Untranslated passages that stall readers; keep conlang text short and meaningful in context.

## Verify
- [ ] Inventory and phonotactics written; new words follow them.
- [ ] Romanisation consistent and tested in ebook output.
- [ ] Every in-book phrase translated and logged.
