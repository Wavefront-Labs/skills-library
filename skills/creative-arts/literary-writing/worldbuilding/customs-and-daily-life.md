# Customs and daily life

**Goal:** the texture that makes an invented culture feel lived-in: how people eat, greet,
dress, marry, work and mourn. Readers believe worlds through small concrete habits more
than grand histories.

## A daily-life inventory
- **Time:** how the day is divided; working hours; rest days; calendar and festivals.
- **Food:** staples, what's eaten by rich and poor, mealtimes, table manners, drink.
- **Dwelling:** house layout, who sleeps where, light and heat sources.
- **Dress:** materials, what signals status, gender, age, marital state, mourning.
- **Family and kinship:** household size, inheritance, marriage customs, childrearing.
- **Etiquette:** greetings, forms of address, gift rules, hospitality obligations.
- **Taboos:** what's forbidden or shameful; punishments.
- **Speech:** idioms, oaths, curses, proverbs derived from the culture's environment and beliefs.
- **Hygiene, medicine, death rites.**

## Derive, don't decorate
Tie customs to environment, economy and belief: desert hospitality laws arise from survival;
a sea-faring culture's idioms reference tides. Derived customs feel inevitable.

## Show it in scenes
- **Through routine:** a character's morning shows the culture without explanation.
- **Through slips:** an outsider or child breaking a rule reveals it.
- **Through conflict:** customs constrain choices (a marriage rule, a guest right).
- **One or two vivid customs per scene;** don't parade them.

## Research
Look at ethnographies and social histories of real cultures for depth (and respect
sources when borrowing); note how real customs vary by class and region.

## Gotchas
- Everyone in a culture behaving identically.
- Earth-default habits (forks, handshakes, seven-day weeks) unexamined.
- Exotic customs as spectacle, unrelated to the story.

## Verify
- [ ] Inventory filled for the main culture(s).
- [ ] Each major custom traced to environment, economy or belief.
- [ ] Customs shown through routine, slips or conflict.
