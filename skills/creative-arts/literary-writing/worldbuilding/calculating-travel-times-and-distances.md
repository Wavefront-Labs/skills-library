# Calculating travel times and distances

**Goal:** journeys that take believable time, so news, armies and characters move at
consistent speeds — one of the most common continuity errors in fantasy.

## Rough daily rates (sustained over many days, flat good roads)
| Mode | Typical per day | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Walking (traveller) | ~30 km (~20 miles) | Fit adult, light load; less with packs/children |
| Army on foot | ~15-25 km | Baggage, foraging, camps |
| Ox cart / wagon | ~15-25 km | Slow, road-dependent |
| Horse (riding, one mount) | ~40-50 km (25-30 miles) | Horses need rest, fodder; short bursts faster |
| Courier with relays | 100+ km | Fresh horses at stations (Roman cursus publicus, Mongol yam) |
| Sailing ship | highly variable; ~100-200 km | Wind-dependent; 1 knot ≈ 1.852 km/h |
| River downstream | current + rowing | Upstream much slower |
Treat these as starting estimates, not facts about any specific historical case.

## Penalties and delays
- Mountains, forests, swamps, snow: halve (or worse) the daily rate.
- Rain and mud; river crossings without bridges; waiting for tides or winds.
- Rest days for animals; resupply in towns.
- Real historical comparison: Stanford's **ORBIS** model gives travel times and costs
  across the Roman world by season and mode — useful to sanity-check.

## Procedure
1. Measure route distance on your map (scale bar); routes are longer than straight lines (add ~20-30%).
2. Divide by the appropriate daily rate; add delays for terrain, weather, and plot.
3. Record every journey in the timeline (see `building-a-world-timeline`): depart date,
   arrive date, mode.
4. Check messages: news travels no faster than the fastest messenger (or magic you've defined).

## Gotchas
- Characters crossing a continent in a few days on one horse.
- Armies moving as fast as lone riders.
- Inconsistency: the same trip taking a week in chapter 3 and two days in chapter 20.

## Verify
- [ ] Every journey has distance, mode and duration recorded.
- [ ] Rates adjusted for terrain and season.
- [ ] News and messages obey the same speeds.
