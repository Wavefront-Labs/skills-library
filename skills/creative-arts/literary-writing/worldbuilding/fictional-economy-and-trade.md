# Economy and trade

**Goal:** an economy that explains how people eat, earn and get rich, and where money
creates conflict. Economic logic underlies politics, war and daily life.

## Build from the ground
1. **Resources:** what the land produces (grain, timber, ore, fish, a magic substance);
   what it lacks.
2. **Production:** who works and how — farms, guilds, slaves or serfs, factories, magic labour.
3. **Trade:** what's exported/imported, by which routes (rivers, coasts, caravan roads) and
   how fast. Chokepoints (passes, straits) concentrate wealth and conflict.
4. **Money:** coinage (metal, mint, debasement), barter, credit, banking, letters of exchange;
   price anchors (a loaf, a day's wage, a sword, a horse) for consistency.
5. **Distribution:** who holds wealth — landowners, merchants, temples, the state; how the
   poor survive.
6. **Shocks:** famine, plague, war, a new trade route, inflation.

## Consistency tools
- **Price list** with a few standard goods in local currency and conversions. Check every
  purchase on the page against it.
- **Wage ladder:** labourer, craftsman, soldier, clerk, noble income.
- **Distance and transport cost:** bulk goods by water, luxuries overland.

## Bring it into scenes
- Characters notice prices, debts, wages, shortages.
- Economic motives for antagonists (control a mine, a monopoly, a tax).
- Status markers: what wealth looks like (fabrics, spices, glass, meat).

## Gotchas
- Gold everywhere with no inflation; adventurers buying castles.
- Medieval settings without peasants.
- Magic or tech that would upend labour, ignored.

## Verify
- [ ] Resources, production, trade routes, money and distribution defined.
- [ ] Price list and wage ladder exist and are used.
- [ ] At least one economic pressure drives plot.
