# The what-if extrapolation

**Goal:** a speculative premise developed rigorously from a single change. Frederik Pohl
is credited with the remark that a good SF story should predict not the automobile but
the traffic jam — the point is consequences, not gadgets.

## Method
1. **State the change** as a "what if": "What if memories could be sold?" Keep it to
   one primary change.
2. **First-order effects:** direct results (people sell memories for money).
3. **Second-order effects:** how institutions respond (memory banks, laws, black markets,
   insurance, therapy changes).
4. **Third-order effects:** culture, values, language and identity (the poor sell their
   childhoods; nostalgia becomes a luxury; slang for memory-less people).
5. **Map across domains:** economy, law, family, religion, crime, art, war, class.
6. **Find the people most affected** — those who gain, those who lose, those caught between.
   Your protagonist is often in the conflict zone.
7. **Locate the story:** a specific person in a specific situation where the change bites.

## Questions that deepen
- Who controls it? Who's excluded?
- What new crimes and jobs exist?
- What do people still do the old way, and why?
- What would have to be true for this to happen? (origin plausibility)

## Gotchas
- Multiple unrelated changes: each multiplies consequences; readers lose the thread.
- A premise with no story — a world tour. Locate the human conflict.
- Ignoring obvious exploitation of the change.

## Verify
- [ ] One change stated.
- [ ] First-, second- and third-order effects listed across several domains.
- [ ] Protagonist placed where consequences create conflict.
