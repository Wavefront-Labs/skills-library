# Drawing a fantasy map

**Goal:** a map that is geographically plausible, useful for keeping the story
consistent, and (if printed in the book) readable at small size.

## Order of work
1. **Landmass and coastlines:** irregular, fractal coastlines; vary scale (peninsulas,
   bays, islands). Decide which way is north and the scale bar now.
2. **Mountains:** form in **chains**, typically along plate boundaries, often near
   coasts. Avoid isolated pimples and perfect circles.
3. **Climate:** prevailing winds bring rain to the windward side of mountains; the
   leeward side is dry (**rain shadow**) — deserts and steppes. Latitude sets broad
   bands (see `worldbuilding-ecology-and-climate`).
4. **Rivers:** start in high ground, flow **downhill to the sea or a lake**, and
   **merge as they go — they don't split** (except in deltas at the mouth). Rivers
   don't cross mountain ranges; they follow valleys. Lakes usually have one outflow.
5. **Biomes:** forests where it's wet, grasslands drier, deserts in rain shadows and
   subtropical high-pressure zones, tundra near poles and peaks.
6. **Settlements:** cities at river mouths, confluences, fords, natural harbours, mountain
   passes, oases; farms on floodplains. Roads follow valleys and coasts.
7. **Borders:** follow rivers, mountain ridges, and historical events — not straight lines
   unless imposed by a distant power.

## Tools
- Paper and pencil, then ink; or **Inkarnate**, **Wonderdraft**, **Azgaar's Fantasy Map
  Generator** (procedural, good for a base); vector tools for print.
- For print in a book: black-and-white line art, legible labels at trim size (test at
  5x8 or 6x9 inches), minimal tiny text; ask the publisher for specs.

## Using the map in writing
- Measure distances to set travel times (see `calculating-travel-times-and-distances`).
- Only label what the story uses; empty areas invite wonder and future books.

## Gotchas
- Rivers splitting into two seas, flowing uphill, or running across ranges.
- Deserts next to rainforests with no mountain or current to explain it.
- Map details contradicting the text (readers check).

## Verify
- [ ] Every river flows downhill, merges, and reaches a sea or lake.
- [ ] Dry regions are explained by rain shadow or latitude.
- [ ] Settlements sit where water and trade justify them.
- [ ] Labels legible at print size.
