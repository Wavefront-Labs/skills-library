# Glossaries and pronunciation guides

**Goal:** reader aids that help without being required reading — the book must still
work if nobody opens them.

## What to include
- **Glossary:** invented terms that recur, titles and ranks, place names that matter.
  Short definitions (one line), alphabetical, no spoilers.
- **Pronunciation guide:** names and terms readers are likely to mispronounce, plus a
  short note on general rules (e.g., "*c* is always hard; final *e* is sounded").
- **Dramatis personae:** for large casts, characters grouped by family/faction with a
  one-line identifier. Avoid spoilers (mark later-revealed facts carefully or omit).
- **Map(s)** where relevant (see `drawing-a-fantasy-map`).

## Respelling vs IPA
- For general readers, use **respelling** with stress in capitals: *Aeryn — AIR-in*;
  *Siobhan — shi-VAWN*.
- Add IPA only for audiences who expect it, or in an author's website appendix.

## Placement
- Print: back matter is conventional for glossaries; maps and dramatis personae at the front.
- Ebook: back matter with a link in the table of contents; consider linking first uses
  of terms to glossary entries sparingly (linking every term clutters). Front matter in
  ebooks eats into retailer samples — keep it lean.

## Audiobook
- Provide the narrator with a full pronunciation list (and audio files of you saying
  the names if possible) before recording. Inconsistencies in audio are very noticeable.

## Maintenance
- Generate the glossary from the style sheet/series bible so spellings match.
- Update for each book in a series; note first appearances by book.

## Verify
- [ ] Glossary entries are spoiler-free and one line each.
- [ ] Pronunciations given in respelling for hard names.
- [ ] Every glossary spelling matches the manuscript (search check).
- [ ] Narrator pronunciation list prepared if an audiobook is planned.
