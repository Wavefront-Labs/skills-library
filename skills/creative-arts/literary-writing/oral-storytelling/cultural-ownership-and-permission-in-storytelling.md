# Cultural ownership and permission in storytelling

**Goal:** tell traditional stories with respect for the communities that hold them. This is ethical guidance; where law is involved (copyright in a published retelling, community licences), seek advice.

## Principles
- A story may be owned in a community sense even when no copyright applies. Some stories are sacred, seasonal, restricted to certain tellers or occasions, or carry family or clan rights. Whether a particular story is restricted is for the tradition bearers to say; do not assume.
- "Folktale" and "public domain" are not the same as "free for anyone to tell in any form". Copyright and cultural protocols are separate questions.
- Local Contexts offers Traditional Knowledge (TK) Labels that communities can attach to materials to state protocols; where a collection carries them, follow them.

## Process
1. **Trace the source.** Where did you learn the story: a community teller, a published retelling, an archive? Who recorded it and under what terms?
2. **Find the community or its organisations** (cultural centre, language programme, elder, tribal or national storytelling body). Ask whether the story may be told by outsiders, in what setting, and with what introduction.
3. **Ask for permission directly and be ready for no.** Offer to credit them as they wish, share any fee if appropriate, and take guidance on pronunciation and context.
4. **Prefer stories from your own heritage or from sources that invite sharing.** The story world is large.
5. **If told yes, tell it as taught.** Keep the elements the community identifies as essential. Do not alter its meaning or mix in other traditions.
6. **Introduce it with attribution**: where it comes from, who gave it, and that you tell it with permission.
7. **Keep a record** of permissions and source.

## Red flags
- Telling sacred or ceremonial material for entertainment.
- Using an invented accent or costume to signal "authenticity".
- Claiming to be a keeper of a tradition you have not been trained in.

## Verify
- [ ] Source and rights holder identified.
- [ ] Permission obtained and recorded, or the story set aside.
- [ ] Introduction credits the source and community.
