# Planning a storytelling program

**Goal:** a set of stories (a 20-minute school slot, a 45-minute library program, a
festival concert) that works as a whole, like a musician's set list.

## Steps
1. **Brief:** audience (ages, size, familiarity with storytelling), venue (room,
   acoustics, mic), length, occasion or theme, any content restrictions.
2. **Choose a theme or thread** if helpful (tricksters, the sea, journeys, family) — it
   helps the host introduce you and ties the set together.
3. **Build the set list**:
   - **Opener:** short, engaging, establishes rapport — often participatory or funny.
   - **Middle:** vary **length** (a long tale between short ones), **mood** (funny,
     eerie, tender), **source** (folktale, personal, myth), and **energy** (participation vs quiet listening).
   - **Emotional peak:** your strongest or deepest story about two-thirds through.
   - **Closer:** satisfying, memorable; often a lighter or warm story after the deep one.
4. **Transitions:** a sentence or two linking stories ("That fox wasn't the only one who
   thought he was clever...") or a song; avoid long patter.
5. **Timing:** total stories at about 85-90% of the slot; list **spare** short stories
   and **cuttable** ones.
6. **Logistics:** arrive early, check sightlines and sound, water, a set-list card
   (story titles only).

## Reading the room during the set
Swap stories based on the audience's energy (see `reading-and-adjusting-to-the-room`).

## Gotchas
- All stories the same length and tone.
- Starting with the longest, heaviest piece.
- No buffer for late starts or questions.

## Verify
- [ ] Brief confirmed with the organiser.
- [ ] Set list varies length, mood and energy with a clear opener, peak and closer.
- [ ] Spares and cuts identified; timing under slot length.
