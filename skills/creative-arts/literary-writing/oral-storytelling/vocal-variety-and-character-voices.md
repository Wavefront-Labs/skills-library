# Vocal variety and character voices

**Goal:** a telling voice that holds attention and makes characters distinct without
turning into a cartoon. Doug Lipman (*Improving Your Storytelling*) stresses that
expressive tools should serve the images the teller is seeing and the listener's experience.

## The four controls
| Control | Use |
|---|---|
| **Pitch** | Rise for questions, excitement; drop for gravity, secrets; vary to avoid monotone |
| **Pace** | Slow for important moments and description; speed for chases and lists |
| **Volume** | Quiet draws listeners in (a whisper at the scariest moment); loud for impact — briefly |
| **Tone / timbre** | Warm, harsh, breathy, crisp: colours emotion |

## Character voices
- Give each major character **one or two markers**, not a full accent: a slightly
  higher pitch and quick pace for the mouse; a slow, low drawl for the giant.
- Keep them **consistent** each time the character speaks — listeners rely on them to
  follow dialogue without tags.
- Combine with a small **posture or head turn** per character (see
  `storytelling-gesture-and-stance`).
- Accents of real communities: be careful — caricature can offend and distract. Rhythm
  and word choice often suffice.

## Narrator voice
- Your natural, warm speaking voice is the home base; return to it between character lines.
- Narration should never sound recited — tell as if recounting to a friend.

## Practice
1. Record yourself telling; listen for monotone patches and rushed climaxes.
2. Mark a story map with pace/volume cues at key beats.
3. Warm up before performing: humming, lip trills, gentle sirens, articulation drills.
4. Hydrate; avoid shouting over noise — use a microphone for large rooms.

## Gotchas
- Speeding up when nervous (especially at the climax).
- Character voices so extreme they strain your voice or blur the words.
- Ending sentences with a habitual upward or downward inflection regardless of meaning.

## Verify
- [ ] Recording shows varied pitch, pace and volume.
- [ ] Each character recognisable by voice alone.
- [ ] Climax delivered slower, not faster.
