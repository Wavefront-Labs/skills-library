# Gesture, stance and space

**Goal:** a body that supports the story — calm enough not to distract, expressive enough
to help listeners see.

## Stance
- Feet hip-width apart, weight balanced, knees soft. **Planted** is the default: pacing
  and rocking pull attention from the story.
- Move deliberately, if at all: a step forward for intimacy or a key moment, then stay.

## Gestures
- Let gestures come from **seeing the image** (showing the height of the wall, the
  size of the fish), not from rehearsed choreography.
- Fewer, larger, completed gestures beat constant small fidgets.
- Hands rest at your sides or lightly together between gestures; avoid pockets and
  clutching notes.

## Placing in space (spatial mapping)
- Assign characters and places a **fixed location**: the king to your left, the
  daughter to your right; the castle up-left. Turn your head slightly toward each
  when they speak.
- Keep it consistent — listeners track the map unconsciously.
- Time can run left to right from the audience's view (their left = past).

## Eye contact
- Talk to individuals in different parts of the room for a phrase or thought each,
  not a scan. When "seeing" the story world, you may look into the middle distance —
  then return to the audience.
- In bright stage lights you may not see faces; still look at where they are.

## Microphones
- Handheld: keep it a consistent fist-width from your mouth, below the chin, and turn
  it with your head. Stand mics: adjust height before you start; don't wander off it.
- Lower your volume rather than backing away for whispers; the mic carries it.

## Gotchas
- Gesturing every word (semaphore); swaying; touching face or hair.
- Inconsistent character placement.
- Holding a phone or notes (slams usually forbid notes).
