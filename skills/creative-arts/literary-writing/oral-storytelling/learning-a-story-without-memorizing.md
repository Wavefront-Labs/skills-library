# Learning a story without memorizing

**Goal:** be able to tell a story fresh each time, reliably, without reciting a script that collapses when you lose a word.

## Method
1. **Read it several times for the shape**, not the wording. Then summarise it aloud in five sentences without looking.
2. **Break it into beats**: 8-15 moments, each a one-line event (see `creative-arts.literary-writing.oral-storytelling.shaping-a-story-for-telling-aloud`).
3. **Visualise each beat** as a film image: where are you, what is in the room, what do the characters see, hear, smell? Walk through them in order several times. Tellers often describe "telling what they see".
4. **Mark the fixed points.** Memorise exactly only what must be exact: the opening line, the closing line, a refrain, a rhyme, a riddle, a spell, the punchline. Everything else is in your words.
5. **Tell it aloud, out of order too.** Start from the middle; tell it to a wall, a pet, a recording, a friend. Each telling changes the wording and strengthens the structure.
6. **Record and listen once**, to catch filler and pacing. Do not rewrite it into a script from the recording.
7. **Practise recovery**: deliberately stop mid-story and pick up from the beat map. Knowing you can rejoin removes the fear of blanking.
8. **Rest and retell.** A story sleeps in memory between tellings; rehearse over days instead of one long session.

## If you must work from a text
Source text is fine to study (a published collection, a transcription) but do not read it in performance unless that is your intent. Respect licensing for retellings (see `creative-arts.literary-writing.oral-storytelling.adapting-a-folktale`).

## Failure modes
- Word-for-word memory: forgetting one phrase derails everything.
- Skipping the images: you can sequence events but have nothing to see.
- Rehearsing only silently.

## Verify
- [ ] You can tell the story in 5 minutes, 2 minutes and 30 seconds.
- [ ] You know the exact opening, ending and any refrain.
- [ ] You have recovered from a deliberate mid-story stop.
