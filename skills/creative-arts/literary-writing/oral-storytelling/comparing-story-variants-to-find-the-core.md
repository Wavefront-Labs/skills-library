# Comparing variants to find a story's core

**Goal:** discover what a story cannot lose, so your telling stays true to it while you adapt the rest.

## Steps
1. **Collect 3-6 variants** from different regions, periods or tellers, noting each source.
2. **Make a table**: columns for each version, rows for episodes in order (opening situation, task or problem, helper, obstacle, trick or magic, resolution, ending).
3. **Mark elements by frequency.** Present in nearly all versions: probable core. Present in one or two: local colour or one teller's addition.
4. **Check function, not surface.** Vladimir Propp's *Morphology of the Folktale* (Russian 1928; English translation 1958) analysed Russian wonder tales as a sequence of narrative functions (such as a lack, a departure, a donor test, a magical gift, a victory). Use that idea loosely: ask what each episode does in the plot, even when the characters change (a fairy, an old woman and a bird may all be the donor).
5. **Note variations in tone and meaning.** The same plot may be comic in one version and tragic in another; the ending often carries the meaning.
6. **Decide your telling.** Keep the core; borrow the best local images with credit; avoid blending incompatible endings.
7. **Record the decision** with the variants you used, so you can credit and revisit.

## Caveats
- Propp's scheme was built for a specific corpus; it does not fit every tale.
- Collectors edited oral tales for print; a printed version is a record, not the "original".

## Verify
- [ ] Table lists at least three sources.
- [ ] Core elements are those found across most versions.
- [ ] Your choices and sources are written down.
