# Finding variants with the ATU tale-type index

**Goal:** identify a tale's type number, then use it to find variants across cultures.

## What it is
The **Aarne-Thompson-Uther (ATU)** index classifies international folktales into numbered types. It grew from Antti Aarne's 1910 tale-type catalogue, revised by Stith Thompson (1928, 1961), then by Hans-Jorg Uther in *The Types of International Folktales* (2004, three volumes in the FF Communications series). Tales are grouped broadly (animal tales, tales of magic, religious tales, realistic tales, tales of the stupid ogre, anecdotes and jokes, formula tales). Commonly cited examples: ATU 510A for the Cinderella tale and ATU 333 for Little Red Riding Hood; verify numbers in the index rather than relying on memory.

Separately, Thompson's six-volume *Motif-Index of Folk-Literature* indexes smaller narrative motifs (a magic object, a forbidden room) that recur across types.

## Workflow
1. **Summarise the tale's plot skeleton** in 3-5 sentences, ignoring names and local detail.
2. **Search the index** by plot, using the printed 2004 volumes (libraries) or a reliable online or university folklore page that lists ATU types. The index is organised by number with a short plot summary per type.
3. **Confirm the type.** Compare your summary with the type's stated episodes; many tales combine types (record each).
4. **Follow the bibliography** under the type to national catalogues and published variants.
5. **Check motifs** for episodes that do not match a type.
6. **Record the type number in your notes** and in your program, and use it when asking librarians or folklorists for variants.

## Caveats
- ATU numbers are classifications made by scholars, not names used by tradition bearers.
- The index reflects the European and Western-collected material heavily; tales from other regions may be under-represented. Use regional catalogues too.
- Cross-check any number you quote.

## Verify
- [ ] Plot skeleton written without names.
- [ ] ATU number cross-checked in the index text.
- [ ] Two or more variants pulled from the citations.
