# Pacing and the pause

**Goal:** control time in a told story so listeners feel suspense, laugh on cue, and
absorb what matters.

## Principles
- **Expand the important, compress the connective.** Travel, setup and background
  move fast; the moment of decision or reveal slows down, with sensory detail.
- **The pause is the teller's most powerful tool:**
  - *before* a reveal or key line — builds anticipation;
  - *after* a punchline or striking image — lets it land (laughter needs room);
  - *after* a question to the audience — lets them think.
- Silence feels longer to the teller than to the audience. A 2-3 second pause is
  usually fine; nerves make tellers cut it to half a second.

## Comic timing
- Set up clearly, pause briefly, deliver the punch word **last** in the sentence, then
  stop talking until the laugh peaks and starts to fall.
- Don't step on laughs by continuing immediately; don't wait for laughs that don't come.

## Time limits
- Story slams like The Moth StorySLAM run about **five minutes**, with a warning signal
  and penalties for running long; festival sets vary.
- Rehearse with a timer; a story that runs 5 minutes in rehearsal often runs longer
  live (laughter, nerves, ad-libs) — aim for 4:30.
- Know what to **cut on the fly**: mark optional beats on your story map.

## Procedure
1. On your story map (see
   `creative-arts.literary-writing.oral-storytelling.shaping-a-story-for-telling-aloud`),
   mark beats as fast, normal, slow.
2. Mark pause points (`//`).
3. Rehearse aloud with a timer; record; check that slow beats really slow down.

## Gotchas
- Uniform pace: everything at the same speed reads as recitation.
- Rushing the ending because the time signal went — cut a middle beat instead.
- Filling pauses with "um", "so", "and".

## Verify
- [ ] Story map annotated with pace and pause marks.
- [ ] Rehearsal time 10% under any limit.
- [ ] Optional cuts identified.
