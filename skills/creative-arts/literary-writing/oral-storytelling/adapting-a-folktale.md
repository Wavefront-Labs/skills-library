# Adapting a folktale

**Goal:** make a traditional tale tellable for a specific audience and time slot without distorting or misattributing it.

## Steps
1. **Gather more than one version.** Read at least three variants from different sources and traditions (see `creative-arts.literary-writing.oral-storytelling.comparing-story-variants-to-find-the-core`). Note which elements recur.
2. **Know the audience and slot**: age, language level, setting, minutes. A tale for a 5-minute school slot differs from a 20-minute adult set.
3. **Keep the core.** Decide the elements that make the tale itself (a helper animal, three tasks, a trick, a transformation, the ending's justice) and protect them.
4. **Trim to what serves.** Merge repeated episodes if the rule of three is not essential; cut side characters; simplify names.
5. **Clarify.** Replace obscure cultural or period references the audience cannot decode, unless the strangeness is part of the pleasure; add a one-line orientation instead.
6. **Handle harsh content thoughtfully.** Violence, punishment and stereotypes in older collections need a conscious decision: soften, contextualise or choose another tale. Do not silently turn a character into a stereotype.
7. **Find your voice for it**: language, rhythm, repeated formulas. Retellings in your words are fine for tales in the public domain; a specific published retelling is the author's copyright, so work from the tradition or from sources you may use, and credit the version you learned from.
8. **Credit.** Say where the tale comes from ("a tale told in many parts of Europe", or the specific culture and source) rather than "my story". Source it to a community where it belongs to one (see `creative-arts.literary-writing.oral-storytelling.cultural-ownership-and-permission-in-storytelling`).

## Failure modes
- Over-sanitising until the tale has no stakes.
- Mixing traditions into an unidentified blend and presenting it as authentic.
- Using a copyrighted retelling's distinctive phrasing.

## Verify
- [ ] Core elements listed and kept.
- [ ] Source and tradition named in your introduction.
- [ ] Told aloud to a test listener and adjusted.
