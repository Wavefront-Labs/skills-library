# Reading and adjusting to the room

**Goal:** treat telling as a conversation — notice how listeners respond and adjust the
story as you go. Lipman (*Improving Your Storytelling*) frames telling as a relationship
between teller, story and listeners; the listener side is live feedback.

## Signs to read
| Engaged | Losing them |
|---|---|
| Stillness, leaning in | Fidgeting, side conversations |
| Faces tracking you | Looking at phones, the door, the clock |
| Laughs and reactions on cue | Silence where you expect laughs |
| Children calling out answers | Children wandering |

## Adjustments (because you learned beats, not words)
- **Trim:** drop optional beats or descriptive passages; get to the next turn.
- **Expand:** linger on what's landing — add an image, a repetition, a pause.
- **Shift energy:** add a participation moment, a change of volume, move a step closer,
  a direct question to the audience.
- **Swap:** replace the next story on the set list with a spare that suits the mood
  (see `planning-a-storytelling-program`).
- **Acknowledge** the elephant in the room (a crying baby, a fire alarm) lightly, then return.

## Hecklers and interruptions
- Children interrupting: often excitement — acknowledge ("You're right, it IS a
  wolf!") and continue.
- Adult heckler: brief, good-humoured response, then keep going; involve the host if it persists.

## Building the skill
- After each telling, note where attention dipped and rose; adjust your story map.
- Tell the same story to different audiences to learn its flexible parts.

## Gotchas
- Pushing through a planned script while the audience is gone.
- Over-adjusting to one restless person and losing the rest.
- Reading silence as boredom — deep listening is often silent.

## Verify
- [ ] Story map marks optional (cuttable) and expandable beats.
- [ ] Post-performance notes on attention dips recorded.
- [ ] Spare story ready for a mood swap.
