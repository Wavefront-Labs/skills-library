# Storytelling for children

**Goal:** stories that hold children's attention, invite participation and suit their
developmental stage.

## By age (rough guide)
| Age | Story types | Length per story | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2-4 | Cumulative tales, simple animal stories, rhymes | 2-5 min | Heavy repetition, actions, props; mix stories with songs |
| 5-7 | Folktales with clear good/bad, trickster tales, fairy tales | 5-10 min | Participation refrains; predictable patterns; gentle suspense |
| 8-11 | Longer folktales, myths, ghost stories (mild), funny tales | 10-15 min | Complex plots, wordplay, riddles; they enjoy being a little scared |
| 12+ | Myths, legends, personal stories, darker tales | 15+ min | Treat as adults; avoid "babyish" participation |

## Techniques
- **Repetition and pattern** (three brothers, three tasks) help children predict and join in.
- **Cumulative tales** ("The House That Jack Built") build memory and participation.
- **Participation** (see `audience-participation-and-call-and-response`).
- **Concrete, vivid images**, simple sentences, active verbs.
- **Sit at their level** for small groups; stand for larger ones.

## Running a session
- 20-30 minutes for young children: 2-4 short stories mixed with songs/stretches.
- Open with a ritual (a song, a bell, "Once upon a time...") and close with one.
- Arrange seating so all can see; set expectations gently.
- Have a short "stretch" story ready if attention dips.

## Scary and sensitive content
- Know the group (school policy, ages, recent events). Folktales contain violence;
  adapt as appropriate but avoid sanitising all tension away.
- Signal safety: a reassuring frame, humour, a satisfying resolution.
- With very young children, avoid real-world threats (lost parents) without resolution.

## Gotchas
- Talking down; children detect condescension.
- Stories too long for the age group.
- Ignoring the restless child instead of drawing them in (give them a role).

## Verify
- [ ] Story choice and length suit the age group.
- [ ] Session has opening and closing rituals.
- [ ] Backup short story ready.
