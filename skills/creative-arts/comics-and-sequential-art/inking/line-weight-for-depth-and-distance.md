# Line weight for depth and distance

**Goal:** ink alone pushes backgrounds back and brings subjects forward, without
waiting for colour.

## Scale of weights
| Plane | Line | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| Foreground | heaviest, often silhouetted | simplified shapes, strong contrast |
| Midground (subject) | medium-heavy holding lines, full detail | most rendering |
| Background | thin, even lines | simplified, fewer textures |
| Far distance | thinnest or no line (left to colour) | shapes only |

## Technique
- Choose tools by plane: brush or heavy marker for FG; brush/medium nib for figures;
  fine nib or technical pen (e.g. 0.1-0.3 mm) for BG.
- Reduce texture density with distance: fewer hatching lines, wider spacing.
- Keep background lines away from figure contours: leave a tiny gap (a "halo") where
  a background line meets a figure so the figure separates.

## Exceptions
- Silhouetted foregrounds (solid black) against a lit midground.
- Night scenes and noir can invert: heavy blacks in the background, figure in
  rim-light.
- Clear-line styles (ligne claire) deliberately use uniform weight; depth comes from
  colour and overlap instead.

## Gotchas
- Detailed, heavy-lined backgrounds swallow figures.
- Ruled backgrounds all at one technical-pen width look mechanical; vary at least two
  widths.

## Verify
- [ ] Squint test: subject stands out from background in line art alone.
- [ ] At least two line weights in backgrounds; lighter than figure lines.
