# Holding lines and outer contours

**Goal:** figures and objects that pop off backgrounds and give colourists clean,
closed shapes.

## The principle
- **Holding line** (outer contour): the line around the silhouette of a form, inked
  heavier than interior detail lines.
- **Interior lines** (folds, muscles, facial features) lighter, so they describe form
  without chopping the shape up.
- Result: the eye reads the silhouette first, then detail.

## Technique
1. Ink interior detail with a finer tool or lighter pressure.
2. Ink the silhouette with a heavier, confident stroke; brush for organic forms, pen or
   marker for mechanical.
3. Thicken holding lines where forms overlap (the near form gets the heavier line at
   the overlap) and on the shadow side.
4. Close the contour: gaps in holding lines make flatting harder (bucket fills leak).

## When to drop or break the holding line
- Light hitting an edge: break the line or thin it to nothing (lost edge) to show
  intense light.
- Atmospheric distance: background forms with thin or no holding lines recede.
- Clouds, smoke, water: often no holding line, or a broken one, to keep softness.
- Some styles (painted, ligne claire) use uniform line; know which style you're in.

## Colour holds
If the colourist will colour a line (e.g. glow edges), keep those lines closed and on
their own layer if digital.

## Verify
- [ ] Silhouettes read as heavier than interior lines.
- [ ] Contours closed where flats need them.
- [ ] Lost edges are deliberate (light, distance).
