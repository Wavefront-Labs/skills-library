# Cast and form shadows in ink

**Goal:** shadows that describe both form and space, with the correct shape.

## Two kinds
- Form shadow: the side of an object turned away from the light. Edge follows the
  terminator; soft on round forms, hard on angular ones.
- Cast shadow: the shadow an object throws onto another surface. Edge is
  generally sharper near the object and its shape describes the receiving surface
  (bends over steps, wraps around a leg).

## Projecting a cast shadow
1. Mark the light source (sun: direction; lamp: point) and the light's footprint
   on the ground plane directly below it.
2. From the footprint, draw lines through the base of the object; from the light,
   draw lines through the top. Where matching lines meet = shadow tip.
3. For sunlight, all shadows are parallel (or converge to the sun's vanishing point
   in perspective).

## Contact shadows
- The thin darkest black where an object meets a surface (feet on floor, cup on
  table). Always ink it; it grounds the figure.

## Inking
- Form shadows: solid black with a feathered or broken edge on organic forms.
- Cast shadows: solid black, sharper edge; occasionally hatched to differentiate
  from form shadow when they touch.
- Keep a white gap where a cast shadow crosses an object's own contour if they
  would merge confusingly.

## Verify
- [ ] Cast shadows fall away from the light, consistent across the panel.
- [ ] Every standing figure has a contact shadow.
