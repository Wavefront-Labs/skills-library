# Chiaroscuro and dramatic lighting in ink

**Goal:** high-contrast pages (noir, horror, Mignola/Miller/Toth tradition) where
shadow shapes do the modelling.

## Method
1. Pick one dominant light source per scene and mark it on the thumbnail (arrow).
   Low or side light gives the longest, most dramatic shadows; under-lighting reads
   as menace.
2. Map the terminator: on each form, find where it turns away from the light and
   fill everything past it solid black. Do not soften with hatching.
3. Merge shadows: let shadow areas of adjacent forms (face into hair into
   background) join into one large black shape. The merged shape is the design.
4. Keep only key information in light: eyes or one eye, hand, weapon.
5. Edge control: hard edges on hard surfaces, a few broken flicks or a drybrush
   edge on soft forms (cheek, cloth). No feathering bands.

## Tips
- Squint at the pencils, or set a 50 percent threshold on a greyscale study, to
  find the shapes.
- Rim light: a thin white line separating a black figure from a black background.
- Faces: keep the eye socket nearest light partly open; full black eye sockets
  lose the acting unless intended.

## Failure modes
- Two light sources in one panel with no story reason.
- Shadow shapes that follow the anatomy line by line (reads as outlining, not light).

## Verify
- [ ] Each panel readable as a pure two-value thumbnail.
- [ ] Light source consistent through the scene.
