# White-out and process white corrections

**Goal:** invisible corrections that scan clean and can be inked over.

## Materials
- **Bleed-proof white** paint (e.g. Dr. Ph. Martin's Bleed Proof White) or other opaque
  process/designer white gouache: covers India ink, can be inked over when dry.
- **Correction pens** (e.g. Pentel/Uni-ball correction pens) for small fixes: quick but
  can leave raised, glossy ridges that ink beads on.
- **White gel pens** (e.g. Uni-ball Signo white) for highlights and fine lines over
  blacks, not for big fixes.
- A dedicated brush (synthetic is fine) only for white.

## Application
1. Let the ink dry completely; white over wet ink turns grey.
2. Stir the white (it settles); thin with a drop of water to a creamy, not watery,
   consistency.
3. Apply in thin layers with the brush; two thin coats beat one thick one (thick white
   cracks and flakes).
4. Feather edges flat so there's no ridge.
5. Dry fully (minutes; use a hair dryer on low if needed) before re-inking.
6. Re-ink with a nib or brush; pens may drag on chalky white, so test.

## Creative uses
- Highlights on blacks (hair shine, stars, sparkle).
- Cutting into black shapes to sharpen edges.
- Spatter white with a toothbrush for snow/space dust.

## Scanning note
White and ink both scan fine at high contrast; grey white over smudged ink may show
in greyscale scans. Bitmap (1-bit) scans hide most minor issues (see
`creative-arts.comics-and-sequential-art.inking.scanning-inks-to-1-bit-line-art`).

## Verify
- [ ] Corrections opaque and flat; no grey halos.
- [ ] Re-inked lines crisp over the white.
- [ ] Corrections invisible in the scan.
