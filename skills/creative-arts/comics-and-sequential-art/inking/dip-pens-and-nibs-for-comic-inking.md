# Dip pens and nibs for comic inking

**Goal:** crisp, controlled lines with predictable thick-thin variation.

## Common nibs
| Nib | Character | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| Hunt 102 (crow quill) | very fine, flexible | detail, feathering, hatching |
| Hunt 107 / 108 | stiff (107) or very flexible (108) | outlines, varied line |
| Hunt 56 / school nibs | stiffer, rounder | lettering, even lines |
| G-pen (Zebra, Nikko, Tachikawa) | flexible, broad variation | manga figure lines |
| Maru (mapping) pen | very fine, small holder | manga detail, hair, backgrounds |
Crow quill nibs need a crow-quill holder; standard nibs fit a standard holder.

## Preparing new nibs
New nibs carry a protective oil/lacquer that repels ink. Clean before use: wipe with
rubbing alcohol, or briefly pass through a flame then rinse (flaming can affect temper;
alcohol or a potato poke are gentler). The ink should cling evenly when dipped.

## Technique
- Pull lines toward you; pushing into the paper catches and sprays.
- Press to spread the tines for thick, release for thin; start and end light for
  tapered strokes.
- Dip to just above the vent hole; tap or wipe excess on the bottle edge.
- Rotate the page instead of contorting your hand.

## Paper matters
Smooth (plate) Bristol lets nibs glide; vellum surface catches more but takes
drybrush better. Test each pad; some batches feather.

## Maintenance
Wipe the nib frequently with a damp cloth; dried ink changes the line. Replace when
lines go scratchy or tines split unevenly (often every few pages with heavy use).

## Gotchas
- Blots from overloaded nibs; keep a scrap sheet to test strokes.
- Smearing: wait for ink to dry (or work top-left to bottom-right if right-handed).

## Verify
- [ ] Test sheet strokes show clean thin-thick-thin variation.
- [ ] No catching or spatter on the page.
