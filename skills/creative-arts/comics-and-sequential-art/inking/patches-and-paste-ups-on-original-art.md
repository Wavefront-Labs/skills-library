# Patches and paste-ups on original art

**Goal:** replace a ruined face, panel or area cleanly, either on the board or
digitally.

## When to patch
- A large area went wrong (a face, a hand, a blotched black).
- Several layers of white have made the surface unusable.
- A redesign after inks (editor change).

## Physical patch
1. Draw the replacement on the **same paper** (same Bristol weight and surface), with
   extra margin. Use a lightbox to match position against the board.
2. Ink it; let it dry.
3. Cut it out with a sharp craft knife, ideally along existing ink lines or shadow
   edges, so the seam hides in black.
4. Adhere with an **archival/acid-free** adhesive (double-sided acid-free tape or
   archival glue stick). Traditional rubber cement works but yellows and fails over
   years; avoid on art you plan to sell.
5. Bevel or sand the patch edges lightly; touch up seams with ink/white.

## Scanning patched art
Patches cast shadows in a scanner. Scan as 1-bit line art or greyscale at high
contrast and clean seams in levels; press the board flat.

## Digital patch (often faster)
1. Scan the original page and the separately drawn fix at the same dpi.
2. Place the fix on a layer, align (Difference blend mode to match lines), mask edges.
3. Flatten to bitmap at the end. Note: the original board no longer matches print;
   tell buyers if original art is sold.

## Verify
- [ ] Seam invisible in the scan.
- [ ] Archival adhesive used on originals.
- [ ] Digital fixes documented if the original differs from print.
