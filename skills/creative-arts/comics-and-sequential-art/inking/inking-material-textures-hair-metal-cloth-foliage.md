# Inking material textures: hair, metal, cloth, foliage

**Goal:** surfaces identifiable by line alone, without overworking the page.

## Hair
- Ink clumps, not strands: shape the mass, then a few tapered strokes following
  flow from parting to tips.
- Spot black hair as a solid shape with white highlight bands following the head's
  curve (cut with white or leave paper).
- Taper every stroke toward the light side.

## Metal and chrome
- High contrast: hard-edged black reflection shapes next to pure white.
- Reflections follow the form (cylinders get vertical bands); a horizon line
  reflection reads as chrome.
- Few lines, no feathering.

## Cloth
- Folds radiate from tension points (shoulder, elbow, knee). Types: pipe, zigzag,
  spiral, half-lock, diaper, drop.
- Thin lines for silk; heavier blunt strokes for leather/denim; small broken
  marks at seams.

## Wood and stone
- Wood: grain lines following the plank, knots, end grain rings.
- Stone/brick: draw a few units fully, imply the rest with broken lines and chips
  at edges.

## Foliage
- Clusters as shapes with a light and shadow side; individual leaves only on the
  edge of the mass and in the foreground.

## Verify
- [ ] Each surface reads in a crop without context.
- [ ] Texture density lower in the background than foreground.
