# Tapered strokes and line quality

**Goal:** lines that look alive and intentional: tapered ends, smooth swells, no
hairy joins. McCloud's "Living in Line" (UC ch. 5) shows line character itself
carries emotion.

## Mechanics
- **Arm, not fingers**, for long strokes: move from the elbow/shoulder; fingers only for
  tiny details.
- **Ghost the stroke**: hover over the line once or twice before touching down.
- **Speed**: confident moderate speed gives smooth lines; too slow = wobble.
- **Taper**: start in the air, touch down lightly, press into the middle, lift off
  while moving (the flick). Both ends taper.
- **Direction**: pull strokes toward you or sideways in your comfortable direction;
  rotate the page to keep it.

## Joining lines
- Overlap tapered ends slightly in the same direction ("feathered joins") so the seam
  disappears. Butt-joining blunt ends shows a bump.
- For long contours, break at natural points (joints, overlaps).

## Line character
- Brush: organic, wide range of weight.
- Nib: crisp, sharper tapers.
- Technical pen: uniform, mechanical; tapering requires building up multiple strokes.
Choose tool per surface: skin and cloth (brush), metal (ruled pen), hair (nib/brush
flicks).

## Warm-up drills (5-10 minutes)
- Rows of parallel tapered strokes, both directions.
- Circles and ellipses in one stroke.
- S-curves swelling in the middle.
- Feathering bands tapering to points.

## Digital
Pressure curve and stabilisation affect taper heavily; tune them (see digital inking
brush skills).

## Verify
- [ ] Lines taper at both ends where intended; joins invisible at print size.
- [ ] No wobble in long contours.
