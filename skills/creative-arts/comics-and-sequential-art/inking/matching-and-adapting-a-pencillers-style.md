# Matching and adapting to a penciller's style

**Goal:** inks that suit the penciller and stay consistent with the book's look.

## Study first
- Look at the penciller's best-received inked work and self-inked pages. Note: brush
  or pen, thick-thin contrast, feathering amount, black spotting density, texture
  habits.
- Ask the penciller what they liked and disliked about previous inkers.

## Adapting
- Angular, graphic pencils: crisp pen and bold blacks; avoid soft brush feathering.
- Organic, flowing pencils: brush with tapered strokes; let line weight swell.
- Detailed pencils: finer tools, careful fidelity.
- Keep your own line quality; adapting means emphasis, not imitation of every quirk.

## Team consistency
- Multiple inkers on one issue: agree on border weight, black density, and
  rendering conventions for recurring textures (hair, costumes).
- Make a style sample page and get editor sign-off before the run.
- Inking over the same penciller long-term: keep a notes file of their marks and
  preferences.

## Verify
- [ ] Sample page approved by penciller or editor.
- [ ] Pages by different inkers in one book are hard to tell apart.
