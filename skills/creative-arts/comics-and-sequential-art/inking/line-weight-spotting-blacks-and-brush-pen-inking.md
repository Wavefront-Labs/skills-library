# Line weight, spotting blacks and brush/pen inking

**Goal:** turn pencils into reproduction-ready line art that reads at print size: clear
silhouettes, depth through line weight, and a balanced black/white composition.

## 1. Line weight: what it communicates
- **Thicker** lines: closer to the viewer, the outline of a figure (holding line), the side
  away from the light, heavy/weighted objects, the underside of forms.
- **Thinner** lines: distant objects, interior detail, the lit side, small textures.
- **Tapered** lines (thick to thin) give life and suggest turning form; uniform lines feel
  mechanical (which can be a deliberate style).
- Background vs foreground: drop the weight on background lines so figures pop.

## 2. Spotting blacks
"Spotting" means choosing where solid black areas go.
- Use blacks for **composition**: to lead the eye, separate planes, frame the focal point.
- Look at the page as a whole: squint or reduce to thumbnail size; the black shapes should
  form a pleasing, balanced pattern across the page, not random blotches.
- Common uses: hair, shadows under forms, night skies, cast shadows, silhouettes,
  costume elements.
- Pencillers often mark intended blacks with an "X"; the inker refines them.

## 3. Tools (traditional)
| Tool | Strength | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Sable/synthetic round brush (size 2–4) | Expressive swelling lines, fast blacks | Hard to control; keep tip clean |
| Brush pen | Brush feel, portable | Less ink load; quality varies |
| Dip pen nib (e.g. G-pen, crowquill) | Crisp, flexible line | Catches on paper if pushed upward |
| Technical/fineliner pens | Even line for panel borders, tech | No taper; use for mechanical objects |
| India ink (waterproof) | Opaque black | Shake; avoid letting it thicken |
| White gouache / correction ink | Fixes and highlights | |
Paper: smooth (plate) **bristol** for nib and brush; check it doesn't bleed.

## 4. Technique
- Ink in a consistent order: (1) panel borders (ruler, tech pen), (2) figures' main holding
  lines, (3) interior detail, (4) blacks, (5) textures/hatching, (6) backgrounds.
- **Pull, don't push**: draw lines toward you; rotate the page to get the angle.
- **Feathering:** quick tapered strokes transitioning from black into white; draw from the
  black area outward.
- **Hatching / crosshatching:** consistent spacing and direction that follows the form.
- Let ink dry fully before erasing pencils (a kneaded eraser is gentler).
- Work over a **light box** onto clean paper if you don't want to ink on the pencils.

## 5. Digital inking
- Canvas at least 600 dpi for line art (many work at 600–1200 dpi for print). *Your
  publisher's specs override this.*
- Use a pressure-sensitive brush with **stabilisation/correction** at a moderate setting
  (too high causes lag).
- Ink on a separate layer above the pencils (pencils in light blue/reduced opacity).
- Fill blacks with a fill tool on a separate layer, set to close small gaps.
- Flip the canvas horizontally regularly to catch distortion.

## 6. Reproduction checks
- Print or view at final size: thin lines may disappear and fine hatching may fill in.
- Make sure all lines are 100% black (no grey) if the book is printed as line art.

## Gotchas
- Tracing the pencils mechanically — interpret, don't copy.
- Uniform weight everywhere makes the art flat.
- Too much hatching muddies the page; blacks often do more.
- Smudging wet ink with your hand; work top-left to bottom-right if right-handed.

## Verify
- [ ] At thumbnail size, the black pattern guides the eye to each panel's focal point.
- [ ] Figures separate from backgrounds through line weight.
- [ ] Lines taper where forms turn; panel borders are clean and consistent.
- [ ] Lines survive printing at final size; blacks are solid.
- [ ] No pencil residue or stray ink on the final.
