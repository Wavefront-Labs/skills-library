# Stippling for comic inking

**Goal:** value and texture from dots that reproduce cleanly in print and screen.

## Where it fits
- Soft gradations on stone, skin texture, sand, rust, old surfaces, moonlit areas.
- Horror and underground styles (Wrightson, Crumb) use heavy stipple for mood.
- It is slow; reserve it for focal textures, not every surface.

## Technique
1. Plan value zones in pencil: light, mid, dark.
2. Dots perpendicular to the paper, lift cleanly; a dragged dot becomes a comma.
3. Build density from the dark side toward the light; leave the lightest area
   untouched.
4. Vary dot size only deliberately (larger toward shadow); random sizes look dirty.
5. Avoid lines of dots and clumps; rotate the board.

## Tools
- Technical pen 0.2-0.5 mm (Micron, Rapidograph) for even dots.
- Brush tip for organic irregular stipple.
- Digital: a stipple/scatter brush in CSP, Procreate or Photoshop with pressure
  controlling density; still hand-place the transitions.

## Reproduction
- Dots must be at least about 0.1 mm and separated; fine stipple fills in when
  reduced, especially on newsprint. Test-print at final size.
- When scanning, use 1-bit at 600-1200 ppi; greyscale scans with a threshold can
  clump dots.

## Verify
- [ ] Stipple survives a reduced test print.
- [ ] Gradations smooth, no accidental clumps or lines.
