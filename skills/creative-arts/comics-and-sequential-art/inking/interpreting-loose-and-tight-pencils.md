# Interpreting loose and tight pencils

**Goal:** inks that finish the penciller's intent, with the right amount of
inker contribution for the job.

## Know the job type
- Tight pencils: every line and black indicated (often with X marks for black).
  Job is fidelity: line quality and weight, not redesign.
- Loose pencils / layouts: figures and staging only. Job is embellishing or
  finishing: anatomy, textures, backgrounds, blacks are yours (credited
  "finishes" or "embellisher").
- Breakdowns: sometimes just stick figures and perspective; effectively you draw it.
- Ask the editor which one the book expects; credits and rates follow.

## Reading pencils
1. Read the whole page before inking; understand the light source and focal points.
2. Find the "right" line among sketchy multiples: usually the most confident,
   outermost one for contours.
3. X marks = solid black; vertical scribble = tone or texture; circled notes = ask.
4. Mark questions in non-photo blue on the margin, not on the art.

## Ethics of inking
- Do not fix "mistakes" in a tight penciller's style (stylised anatomy) without
  asking.
- Do fix tangents, perspective slips and uneven hands if the editor wants polish.

## Verify
- [ ] The penciller would recognise the page as theirs.
- [ ] Blacks agree with the penciller's light source.
