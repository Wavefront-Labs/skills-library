# Digital drybrush and texture brushes

**Goal:** organic texture in digital line art that holds up as black-and-white print.

## Brush sources
- Built-in: CSP Decoration/Brush groups, Photoshop's dry media brushes.
- Asset libraries: Clip Studio Assets, Photoshop brush packs bundled with Creative
  Cloud (e.g. the Kyle T. Webster sets).
- Make your own: scan real drybrush strokes, threshold, and register as a brush tip.

## Drybrush settings
- Brush tip with gaps (bristle texture), dual brush or texture applied per stroke.
- Size affected by pressure, low minimum size for flicked ends.
- **Anti-aliasing off** if output is bitmap line art; the grain must be pure black/white.
- Draw quickly; slow strokes fill texture in.

## Other textures
- Splatter (spray brushes) for grit, blood, snow; keep separate layers to control.
- Grain/paper on line art: apply sparingly; it can look like a dirty scan in print.
- Pattern fills (crosshatch, stipple) as brushes: vary scale and rotation to avoid
  obvious repetition.

## Print safety
- Check at 100% physical size and at bitmap conversion: very fine grain turns to noise
  or disappears.
- For greyscale effects use proper tones/screentones instead of soft brushes (see
  manga screentone and CSP tone skills).

## Gotchas
- One texture brush on everything: style looks stamped.
- Textures with grey pixels in a "pure B/W" book: printer may screen them unexpectedly.

## Verify
- [ ] Texture reads at print size and survives bitmap conversion.
- [ ] No obvious tiling or repeated stamp patterns.
