# Sable brush choice and care

**Goal:** a brush that holds a fine point and a full load of ink for months, not
weeks.

## Choosing
- **Kolinsky sable** round brushes are the traditional inking standard because they
  spring back to a point and hold a lot of ink. Widely used examples: Winsor & Newton
  Series 7, Raphael 8404, Da Vinci Maestro; quality varies batch to batch, so test.
- **Size**: #2 or #3 is the common workhorse for comics pages at 11x17 in; #0-#1 for
  fine detail; larger for spotting blacks (or use a cheap synthetic for fills).
- **Test the point** in the shop if allowed: wet it with clean water, flick it; it
  should snap to a single point with no stray hairs.
- Synthetic or blended brushes are cheaper and more ink-resistant but usually lose
  spring faster with heavy inking.

## Loading and using
- Dip about two-thirds of the hair length; never bury the ferrule in ink (ink dries in
  the heel and splays the brush).
- Wipe on the bottle neck or a scrap sheet to set the point.
- Keep a separate cheap brush for white-out and for filling blacks.

## Cleaning (India inks contain shellac/binders that harden)
1. Rinse in water frequently during a session; never let ink dry in the hairs.
2. At the end: rinse thoroughly, then wash gently with mild soap (brush soap or plain
   bar soap), working from heel to tip, until no colour comes out.
3. Rinse again, shape the point with your lips or fingers (soap-shaping: leave a little
   soap in to dry to a point), and dry horizontally or tip down.
4. Some inkers use ammonia-based or dedicated brush cleaners for dried shellac; use
   sparingly, they can dry hair.

## Storage
Never stand wet brushes on their hairs. Store dry in a case; protect sable from moths.

## Verify
- [ ] Brush returns to a single point after rinsing.
- [ ] No ink at the ferrule after cleaning.
- [ ] Separate brushes for white-out and fills.
