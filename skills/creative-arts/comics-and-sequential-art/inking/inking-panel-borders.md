# Inking panel borders

**Goal:** even, intentional borders that frame the art and match the book's style.

## Conventions
- Typical border weight: about 1-1.5 mm at original size (reduces to roughly
  0.6-1 mm in print). Keep it consistent across the book.
- Borders heavier than the heaviest figure line, so panels read as frames.
- Rounded corners, wavy (flashback) or broken borders are storytelling choices.

## Methods
1. Technical pen or marker with a raised ruler; ink corners slightly past and white
   out, or stop exactly and fill.
2. Border tape: draft-style black tape or masking tape for a clean edge, then fill.
3. Ink borders before the art if the art breaks the border (figure overlapping);
   paint the breaks back with white, or after the art and stop at the overlap.
4. Digital: many inkers ink only the art and let the letterer or CSP frame tools
   draw borders. Agree with the team which file owns borders.

## Gotchas
- Inconsistent gutter: borders must sit on the template's ruled panel lines.
- Bleed panels have no outer border; extend art to the bleed line.
- Balloons breaking borders: leave room and let the letterer handle the break.

## Verify
- [ ] Border weight identical on every page.
- [ ] Team agrees which file draws borders.
