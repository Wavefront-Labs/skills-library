# Line weight from the light source

**Goal:** line weight that implies lighting and volume before any colour or shading.

## Method
1. **Decide the light source** for the panel (and the scene): direction and height.
   Mark it in the margin (an arrow) if the penciller didn't.
2. **Lit side**: thin lines, sometimes broken where light is strongest.
3. **Shadow side**: thicker lines; as forms turn away from light, the line swells.
4. **Undersides**: heavier lines where a form rests or overhangs (under chin, under
   arms, under objects touching the ground) - suggests cast shadow and weight.
5. **Transitions**: taper smoothly from thin to thick as the contour moves around the
   form; a sudden jump looks like a mistake.

## Consistency
- Every object in the panel follows the same light.
- Keep the light consistent through a scene unless the source moves (lamp swings,
  a car passes). Sudden lighting flips between panels confuse readers.

## With blacks
Line weight is the first step; as the shadow side thickens enough, it becomes spotted
black. Plan which shadows become solid blacks
(see `creative-arts.comics-and-sequential-art.inking.line-weight-spotting-blacks-and-brush-pen-inking`).

## Gotchas
- Thick line everywhere "because it looks bold": lighting disappears.
- Contradicting the colourist's light: share the light source in notes.

## Verify
- [ ] Each panel's light source identifiable from line weight alone.
- [ ] Line weights consistent with it across all objects.
