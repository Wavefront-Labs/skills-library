# Working with manga assistants

Serialised mangaka routinely employ assistants (*ashisutanto*) to meet weekly or monthly deadlines.

## Division of labour
| Who | Typical task |
|---|---|
| Mangaka | name, pencils of characters, faces, key inks |
| Background assistant | perspective backgrounds, buildings, vehicles, props |
| Tone/effects assistant | screentone, speed and focus lines, beta |
| Finishing assistant | white correction, erasing, SFX inking, checks |

## Instructions on the page
- Mark tone numbers directly in blue/non-repro pencil (e.g. "61" for a Deleter tone number, or a
  layer colour code in digital), "beta" (black), "x" for solid fill, arrows for gradation direction.
- Background notes: reference number from the studio's photo library, time of day, perspective type.
- Keep a **studio sheet**: tone numbers per character (hair, skin, uniform), standard SFX font/style,
  line weights.

## Digital studio
- CSP share/team features or a shared drive with page files named by chapter-page (`ch12_p05.clip`).
- Lock character layers; assistants work on named layers (BG, Tone, FX) only.
- Use materials libraries for recurring backgrounds and 3D models so assistants reuse them.
- Track status per page (pencil, ink, BG, tone, finish) on a board.

## Quality control
- Mangaka reviews every page; the finishing assistant does a final pass.
- Consistency checks: character tones, recurring props, continuity of backgrounds.

## Gotchas
- Vague markup = rework. Every instruction answers "what, where, which number".
- Assistants' reference photos must be cleared for use (own photos or licensed).
- Labour and pay practices: agree hours, pay and credit in writing.
