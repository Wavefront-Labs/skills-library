# Manga white correction and finishing

*Shiage* (finishing) is the last stage before a page leaves the studio: erase, white-out, fix lines,
check every detail.

## Tools (analogue)
- Poster colour white or a dedicated manga white ink (e.g. Dr. Ph. Martin's Bleed Proof White, Deleter
  White) applied with a fine brush or dip pen.
- Correction pen for small fixes; correction tape/paper patches for big mistakes.
- Kneaded eraser then a hard eraser for pencil lines after ink is fully dry.

## Workflow
1. Wait until ink is fully dry (minutes for thin lines, longer for black fills).
2. Erase all pencil, working outward so you don't smear.
3. White-out: overshoot lines at intersections (*tobidashi*), stray specks, uneven line ends.
4. Add white highlights over blacks: hair shine, eye highlights, sparkle in black fills, white lines in
   speed-line bursts.
5. Re-ink lines damaged by white; white over white builds a lump that catches light in the scan.
6. Final check page by page at arm's length and at print size.

## Digital (CSP)
- Vector layers: use the vector eraser "up to intersection" for overshoot.
- Separate "white" layer above inks for highlights so they can be adjusted.
- Use transparency to erase on raster; avoid painting white on the line layer if the page will be
  converted to tone or trapped later.
- Run a "dust" check: fill transparent areas with a bright colour on a temporary layer below to spot gaps.

## Gotchas
- Thick white paint cracks and flakes in the post; thin coats, two passes.
- Highlights painted on the ink layer in digital cannot be toned under correctly.

## Verify
- [ ] No pencil remnants or blue line residue in the scan.
- [ ] Highlights read at print reduction.
