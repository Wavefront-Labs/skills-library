# Kakeami hatching

**Kakeami** ("woven net") is manga's fine cross-hatching: sets of short parallel strokes laid at angles
to build tonal value by hand, used for shadows, gritty seinen texture, old-film looks and areas where
screentone would look too clean.

## Building it
1. Set 1: short parallel strokes (about 3-8 mm), evenly spaced, tapered at both ends. Group them in
   blocks; the next block slightly overlaps.
2. Set 2: rotate the angle 30-45 degrees over set 1 for the next darker value.
3. Sets 3-4: additional angles for the darkest areas, approaching solid black.
4. Follow form: block direction curves around faces and folds, not always the same angle.
5. Fade edges by shortening strokes and widening spacing; don't end in a hard line.

## Rules
- Keep angle differences at least 30 degrees; near-parallel sets create a moire.
- Avoid 90-degree grid on skin; it looks like a net/mesh. Reserve it for hard surfaces.
- Pen: G-pen for confident tapering; maru pen for fine work. Rotate the paper rather than your wrist.

## Digital (CSP)
- Hatching brushes from Assets apply pre-built kakeami patterns; quick but less responsive to form.
- Hand-hatch on a vector layer with a tapering pen so you can thin lines later.
- Check at 100% and at print reduction (B4 printed small fills in hatching).

## Gotchas
- Strokes too close together fill in to grey mush or solid black when printed.
- Mixing kakeami and dot screentone in the same area can moire; separate them.

## Verify
- [ ] At final print size, each value step reads distinctly and the darkest value is not a muddy fill.
