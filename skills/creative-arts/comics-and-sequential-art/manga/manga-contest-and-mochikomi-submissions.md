# Manga contest and mochikomi submissions

## Routes
- **Mochikomi** (bringing in) - showing finished manuscripts to a magazine editor in person or, now
  usually, online (e.g. "online mochikomi" through Jump, Kodansha and Shogakukan portals). An editor
  gives direct feedback and may become your *tantou* (assigned editor).
- **Contests** (shou) - monthly and seasonal awards (e.g. Shonen Jump's Tezuka/Akatsuka awards, Jump
  Rookie, Kodansha's Afternoon Shiki Award, international categories such as Manga Plus contests).
  Prize winners often get a tantou and debut chances.

## Preparing the manuscript
- One-shot (*yomikiri*) of the length the magazine specifies, commonly 15-45 pages for weekly
  shonen contests; read each contest's rules.
- Complete: inked, toned, lettered (or text typed in balloons). Name-only submissions are allowed in
  some "name contests".
- B4 or A4 standard size, or digital at the specified resolution (often 600 dpi monochrome).
- Include profile sheet: name, age, contact, previous submissions, synopsis and title.

## What editors critique
- Character appeal and a clear protagonist goal in the first pages.
- Story structure in one chapter: setup, conflict, climax, satisfying end.
- Readability: panel flow right-to-left, balloon order, clear action.
- Drawing: faces, consistency, backgrounds.

## Etiquette and strategy
- Choose the magazine whose demographic and genre fit your work.
- Don't send the same work to two publishers' contests at once (most forbid simultaneous entries).
- Treat feedback as a revision brief; return with the next work.
