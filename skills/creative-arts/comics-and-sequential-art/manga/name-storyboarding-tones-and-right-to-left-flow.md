# Name storyboarding, tones and right-to-left flow

**Goal:** make pages that read like manga — right-to-left, cinematically paced, toned in
greyscale — starting with the *name* that editors review before any final art.

## 1. The name (ネーム, storyboard)
- A **name** is a rough of every page: panel borders, figures as quick sketches, all dialogue
  written in. It is the stage where story and pacing are judged.
- In Japanese professional practice, the editor reviews and revises the name before the
  artist starts drafts; many chapters go through several names. *Exact workflow varies by
  magazine and editor.*
- Draw the name at small size, fast, and **read it as a reader**: does each page turn pull you on?
- A one-shot (読み切り, yomikiri) is a common way to debut; chapter lengths in weekly
  magazines are often ~15–20 pages. *Check the magazine's guidelines.*

## 2. Right-to-left flow
- Pages, panels and balloons read **right to left, top to bottom**. The first panel is
  top-right; the first balloon in a panel is the rightmost/highest.
- Books bind on the right; the first page is on the left of a spread.
- Vertical text in balloons means tall, narrow balloons.
- When producing for Western release, pages are usually kept unflipped today; if a
  publisher asks for flipping, watch text in the art, handedness and asymmetrical designs.

## 3. Manga pacing
- **Decompression:** a moment can run over multiple panels or pages — a glance, a pause, a
  scenery panel. This gives manga its cinematic time.
- Big moments get large panels, splashes or double-page spreads.
- Panel shapes vary freely; diagonal and borderless panels for action or emotion.
- The **page turn** is the strongest beat; place reveals on the first panel after a turn.
- Keep the reader's eye flowing right-to-left through the gutter rhythm; avoid ambiguous
  T-junctions in panel layout.

## 4. Screentones
- Manga is traditionally black-and-white; **tones** (dot patterns) provide greys, textures
  and mood.
- Use a small number of tone values per page, chosen for clarity: e.g. a light tone for
  skin shadows, a mid tone for clothing, gradients for skies.
- **Moiré:** dot patterns interfering with each other or with the printing screen create
  ugly patterns. Avoid stacking tones of different line counts or angles; keep consistent
  LPI and angle; don't scale tone layers after applying.
- Digital: tones as layers (Clip Studio Paint's tone layer feature lets you set lines per
  inch and convert). Export final pages as 1-bit (monochrome) at high resolution
  (often 600 dpi or more) for print. *Publisher specs govern.*
- **Beta** (ベタ) = solid black fills; **white** = correction highlights.

## 5. Speed lines, focus lines, SFX
- **Speed lines** show motion direction; **focus lines** (radiating) emphasise a face or
  moment.
- **Kakimoji** (hand-drawn SFX) are part of the artwork, drawn with energy and integrated
  into composition.

## 6. Genre conventions (brief)
Magazine demographics shape expectations: shōnen (young male readership: action, growth,
rivals), shōjo (young female: emotion, relationships, flowery panel design), seinen and
josei (adult). Learn the conventions of your target magazine — layout density and tone
style differ.

## 7. Tools
Traditional: G-pen and maru-pen nibs, manga paper with printed guide lines, cut screentone
sheets. Digital: Clip Studio Paint is the industry standard (see
creative-arts.comics-and-sequential-art.clip-studio-paint.multi-page-comic-workflow-panels-tones-and-export).

## Gotchas
- Flipping layout direction halfway (left-to-right panel order sneaks in).
- Too many tones per page — greys turn to mud.
- Scaling tones → moiré in print.
- Skipping the name and finding pacing problems after inking.

## Verify
- [ ] A complete name exists and has been read through for page-turn pull.
- [ ] Every page reads right-to-left with no ambiguous panel junctions.
- [ ] Reveals sit after page turns; decompressed beats used deliberately.
- [ ] Tones consistent in LPI/angle, unscaled, few values per page; no moiré at print size.
- [ ] Output matches the publisher's mono/greyscale and dpi spec.
