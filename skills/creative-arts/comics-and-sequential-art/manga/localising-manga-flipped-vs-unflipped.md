# Localising manga: flipped vs unflipped

**Goal:** an English edition that respects the original art and reads smoothly.

## Reading direction
- **Unflipped** (right-to-left preserved): standard in English-language manga
  publishing for years; art stays as drawn. Include a "this book reads right to left"
  notice page at the Western front.
- **Flipped** (mirrored): older practice; reverses the art, causing errors (right-handed
  characters become left-handed, text in art reversed, clothing details flipped).
  Usually requires creator approval and fixes. Avoid unless contractually required.

## Text fitting
- Japanese dialogue is vertical in tall balloons; English horizontal text fits poorly.
  Use a narrower font, tighter leading, smaller size within legibility limits, and
  smart line breaks. Balloon redraws are normally not permitted.
- Keep reading order right-to-left between balloons.

## SFX
Options (publisher decides):
1. Leave Japanese SFX, add small English translation nearby.
2. Replace SFX with English lettering (requires retouching art).
3. Glossary at the back (less common now).

## Honorifics and culture
Decide on keeping honorifics (-san, -kun) or adapting; state the policy and keep it
consistent; translator notes for puns and cultural references.

## Verify
- [ ] Reading-direction notice present for unflipped books.
- [ ] Text fits balloons at legible size; order right-to-left.
- [ ] SFX and honorific policies consistent across the volume.
