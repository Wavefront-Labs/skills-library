# Tone scraping and highlights

**Goal:** soft highlights, glows and textures by removing tone, the classic manga
technique (kezuri).

## Traditional
- Apply the tone sheet, burnish lightly.
- Use a craft knife or dedicated tone scraper held at a low angle; scrape the dots'
  surface in short parallel strokes so dots shrink or vanish gradually.
- Directions: follow the form (around a sphere, along hair strands).
- Sand eraser or a fibreglass eraser gives softer textures.
- Never cut through the backing into the paper.

## Digital (Clip Studio Paint)
1. Tone layer (or a greyscale layer with Tone layer property).
2. Erase with a soft or textured eraser at low opacity/density: on a tone layer,
   reducing density makes dots smaller, mimicking scraping.
3. For crisp highlight shapes (hair shine), erase with a hard brush in shaped strokes
   (often a series of tapered strokes).
4. Alternatively paint with transparent colour on the tone layer using a "spray" or
   scrape-style brush (CSP has tone scraping brushes in asset libraries).

## Uses
- Hair shine bands; eye highlights; glossy surfaces; glows around light sources;
  sparkles in shojo backgrounds.

## Gotchas
- Scraping across dots unevenly leaves blotches; work in consistent directions.
- Digital: erasing a greyscale layer without tone property gives grey pixels that won't
  print as dots.

## Verify
- [ ] Gradations smooth at print size; directions follow form.
- [ ] Digital tone output remains dots (no stray grey).
