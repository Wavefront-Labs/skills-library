# Manga speed lines (ryusen)

**Ryusen** ("flow lines") are parallel lines that show motion. Manga often moves the *background*:
the character stays sharp while parallel lines fill the panel, so the reader rides along (McCloud's
"subjective motion").

## Types
- **Background ryusen** - the whole panel filled with parallel lines in the direction of travel;
  the figure drawn over with a white halo.
- **Object motion lines** - short lines trailing behind the moving object only.
- **Focus lines (shuchusen)** - radiate to a point (impact, surprise); a separate technique, often confused
  with speed lines.

## Technique (analogue)
1. Pencil a direction guide; all lines parallel to it (or converging slightly to a far vanishing point
   for depth).
2. Use a ruler raised off the paper (or with tape spacers) and a G-pen or maru pen; flick each line so it
   tapers (*irinuki*: tapered entry and exit).
3. Vary spacing: dense at the source of motion, sparser toward the open side.
4. Leave a white gap around the figure, or ink lines over and white them out.
5. Mixed lengths stop it looking mechanical.

## Digital (CSP)
- Special ruler > Parallel lines, set the angle; draw with a tapering pen (in/out taper on).
- Or Figure > Saturated line / Streamline tools: set line count, spacing, length jitter, and a reference
  position; they generate editable vector speed lines.
- Mask the figure area with a layer mask.

## Gotchas
- Lines that end abruptly look ruled, not fast; always taper.
- Speed lines in the wrong direction contradict the action of the previous panel.
- Too many speed-line panels in a row tire the eye; vary with focus lines or plain panels.

## Verify
- [ ] Direction matches the motion in neighbouring panels.
- [ ] Figure silhouette clear against the lines.
