# Chibi and super-deformed comedy

**Chibi / super-deformed (SD)** versions shrink a character to 2-3 heads tall with a huge head and
tiny body. In manga they mark a tonal shift into comedy, embarrassment or cuteness within an otherwise
normal-style scene.

## Proportions
- 2-head (very cute, baby-like) to 3-head (more expressive limbs).
- Head about as wide as the shoulders or wider; eyes larger and lower on the face; nose often omitted;
  hands are mittens or stubs; feet small.
- Keep the identity markers: hairstyle silhouette, accessory, colour scheme/tone, signature expression.

## When to switch
- Comedy reaction (character overreacts, falls over, sulks).
- Background characters arguing small in the corner of a serious panel.
- Omake (bonus) pages and chapter-end gags.
- A sudden switch is itself a joke beat: normal panel - SD panel - normal panel.

## Execution
1. Simplify linework and reduce tones; often flat white with a few speed lines or a sweat drop.
2. Use comedy symbols: big sweat drop, cross-popping vein mark, tears as streams, white blank eyes.
3. Background drops away or becomes a pattern (flowers, lines).
4. Return to normal proportion in the next panel so the story mood comes back.

## Gotchas
- Overusing SD within a serious arc undercuts tension.
- SD faces that lose identity markers become unreadable when several characters appear together.

## Verify
- [ ] Cover the dialogue: is each SD character still identifiable?
- [ ] The comedy beat begins and ends cleanly.
