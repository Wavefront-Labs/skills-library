# Scroll reveals in vertical comics

**Goal:** surprises that hit as the reader scrolls, the vertical-scroll equivalent of a
page turn.

## The screen is the frame
On a phone, readers see roughly one screen-height at a time (about 800 x ~1400 px
of an 800-px-wide comic, depending on the device). A reveal works when it enters the
screen after the set-up has left or as the reader scrolls down into it.

## Techniques
1. **Delay with space**: after the set-up panel, leave a gap (often about a screen
   height of empty or atmospheric space) so the reveal can't be seen yet.
2. **Tall panels unfolding**: a vertically long panel where the important part is at
   the bottom: the reader scrolls down a falling figure, a tower, a creature's length.
3. **Top-down reading order inside panels**: put the reaction at the top, cause at the
   bottom for a "look down" reveal.
4. **Black or coloured transition space** for mood changes (fade to black).
5. **Single-image punch**: a full-width impact panel after a run of small panels.

## Pitfalls
- Reveals placed right under the set-up: visible on the same screen.
- Too much empty space everywhere: reader fatigue, scrolling with nothing happening.
- Text balloons scattered in gaps without art can be missed; anchor them.

## Test
Preview on a real phone at actual width; scroll at reading speed.

## Verify
- [ ] Each reveal is out of view while the set-up is on screen.
- [ ] Spacing deliberate; no long empty stretches without purpose.
