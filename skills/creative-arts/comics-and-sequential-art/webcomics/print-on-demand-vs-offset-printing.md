# Print-on-demand vs offset

## Comparison
| | Print-on-demand (IngramSpark, Amazon KDP, Lulu) | Offset (domestic or overseas printers) |
|---|---|---|
| Minimum | 1 copy | typically 500-1000+ |
| Unit cost | high, fixed per page | drops sharply with quantity |
| Colour | digital toner/inkjet, good but less consistent; limited paper choices | best colour and paper control, spot colours, special finishes |
| Distribution | built in (Ingram catalogue, Amazon) | you store and ship, or use a distributor |
| Lead time | days | weeks (domestic) to 2-3 months (overseas plus freight) |
| Risk | no inventory | cash upfront; unsold stock |

## Break-even
- Get quotes for your exact spec (trim, page count, paper, binding, cover finish).
- Compute total cost = setup + unit cost x qty + freight + storage. Compare to POD unit cost x expected
  sales. For full-colour comics, offset usually wins somewhere in the few-hundred-copy range.
- Kickstarter projects usually use offset because pledges fund a known run.

## Quality checks
- Always order a physical proof (POD) or approve wet/digital proofs (offset).
- Offset: request paper samples; colour comics often use 80-100 lb (120-150 gsm) matte or gloss text.
- Saddle stitch is cheap for under ~48 pages; perfect binding needs about 40+ pages for a printable spine.

## Hybrid
- Offset run for conventions and backers; POD listing for ongoing online sales and libraries/stores.

## Gotchas
- POD page counts must be even and often within a min/max; spine width changes with page count.
- Overseas freight, customs and warehousing can double the apparent unit cost.
