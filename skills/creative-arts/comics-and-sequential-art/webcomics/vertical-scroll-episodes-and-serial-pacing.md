# Vertical-scroll episodes and serial pacing

**Goal:** design comics for a phone screen — scrolled, not paged — and structure each
episode so readers come back next week.

## 1. Two webcomic formats
- **Page-format** webcomics: traditional pages posted online. Fine on desktop; on phones,
  readers pinch-zoom. Consider larger text and fewer panels per page.
- **Vertical scroll** ("webtoon" style): one long, narrow canvas, panels stacked top to
  bottom. Designed for thumbs. This skill focuses on it.

## 2. Canvas set-up
- Platforms specify a **width** (commonly around 800 px for display; many creators work
  at 2× that, e.g. 1600 px, and downscale). *Check your platform's current specs — they
  change.*
- Work on a tall canvas in sections (e.g. several thousand px tall per file) and stitch.
- **Text size:** readable on a phone without zoom; test on an actual phone early.
- Colour for screen (sRGB); most vertical comics are full colour.

## 3. White space is time
- In a scroll, the **gap between panels** controls pacing: short gap = quick action; long
  gap = pause, suspense, scene change. Gaps of a full screen height or more are used for
  dramatic beats.
- **One panel per screen** is a useful baseline; bigger moments get taller panels.
- Reveals: put the setup at the bottom of a screen and the reveal after a scroll gap,
  so the reader's thumb produces the reveal (the scroll equivalent of a page turn).
- Panels can break the frame, bleed edge to edge, or float on a coloured background
  for mood transitions.

## 4. Reading flow
- Single column; **balloons read top to bottom**, roughly one or two per panel.
- Avoid side-by-side panels needing left-right reading; they're small and confusing on phones.
- Keep balloons near the panel they belong to — scrolling separates context fast.

## 5. Episode structure
- Typical episode: **hook** in the first screen or two (an image or line that grabs),
  a story beat that advances character/plot, and a **cliffhanger** or emotional button at the end.
- Episode length: many creators aim for roughly 40–60 panels or a few minutes of reading;
  platforms and genres differ. *Treat this as a starting range.*
- Each episode should feel like an event, not a slice that stops mid-scene for no reason.
- Plan season arcs; readers forgive slow starts less online — early episodes need momentum.
- **Episode thumbnail/cover image:** readable at icon size; it drives clicks.

## 6. Export
- Platforms often require slicing the long strip into images under a max height/file
  size; export in their order with numbered filenames.
- Check the compression; text and line art blur with low JPEG quality. *Use the
  platform's preferred format.*
- Upload a test episode privately and read it on a phone.

## 7. Schedule and sustainability
- Build a **buffer** of episodes before launch (many creators suggest 5–10+).
- Choose a schedule you can sustain for a year — consistency beats frequency.
- Plan hiatuses and announce them.
- Use templates (character colour sheets, background libraries, 3D models) to save time.

## Gotchas
- Drawing page-format layouts and slicing them vertically — the pacing breaks.
- Tiny text designed on a big monitor.
- Huge empty gaps used everywhere; space loses meaning.
- Starting without a buffer and burning out by episode 10.
- Ignoring how the thumbnail looks in a feed.

## Verify
- [ ] Canvas width and slicing match the current platform spec.
- [ ] Text readable on a phone at normal distance.
- [ ] Gaps vary deliberately; reveals happen after a scroll.
- [ ] Episode opens with a hook and ends on a cliffhanger or button.
- [ ] Buffer of finished episodes exists and the schedule is sustainable.
