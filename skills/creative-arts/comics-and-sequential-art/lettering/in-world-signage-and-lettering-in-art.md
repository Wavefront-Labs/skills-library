# In-world signage and lettering in art

**Goal:** signs, screens, newspapers and labels that look part of the drawing and
can be translated.

## Who letters it
- If the text is story-relevant (a sign that sets location, a text message), the
  letterer should set it on a separate layer so translators can replace it.
- Pure background texture text (shopfront clutter) can stay in the art.
- Agree in the script or with the editor.

## Making it belong
1. Fit to the surface: Illustrator Free Distort or Perspective Grid to match the
   art's vanishing points.
2. Choose a font that suits the object (neon script, street signage sans, newspaper
   serif); avoid the dialogue font.
3. Match line weight and rendering to the art (rough brush art: slightly rough text).
4. Let the colourist know the sign's colour or glow.

## Screens and phones
- Phone messages are a common modern device: consistent UI style across the
  series, legible point size (same as dialogue or slightly smaller).

## Eisner tradition
- Title lettering built into architecture or signs can serve as a splash title; plan
  with the artist.

## Verify
- [ ] Story-relevant text on a separate editable layer.
- [ ] Perspective matches the drawn surface.
