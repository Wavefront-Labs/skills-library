# Choosing a dialogue font

**Goal:** a dialogue face that feels hand-lettered for this book, reads effortlessly,
and has every glyph and style you'll need.

## Requirements checklist
- **Comic lettering design**: designed for balloons (all-caps forms, open counters).
  Major sources: Comicraft (comicbookfonts.com) and Blambot (blambot.com), plus other
  comic foundries.
- **Styles**: Regular + **Bold Italic** (emphasis) at minimum; Italic and Bold useful.
- **Crossbar I** alternate available (see the crossbar I skill).
- **Glyph coverage**: punctuation (em dash, ellipsis, inverted ?! for Spanish),
  accented letters for names/translations, and any languages you'll publish.
- **Lower case** if the book uses mixed case.
- **Licence** covering print, digital, and your publisher's use.

## Fit
- Match line character: a smooth, even font for clean art; a rougher, more irregular
  font for scratchy indie art; a wider, rounder font for cartoony humour.
- Avoid fonts strongly associated with a famous series unless intended.
- Test at final print size: set a dense balloon at 7-9 pt (print size) and print it.

## Pairing
- Captions: same family or a related face (often the same font, different colour box,
  or italic).
- Display/SFX: a contrasting display face or hand-drawn; never reuse the dialogue font
  for SFX.
- Special voices (robots, aliens): one alternate face per voice, used consistently.

## Gotchas
- Comic Sans: not a lettering font; reads amateur.
- Free fonts with incomplete glyph sets (no bold italic, no accents) fail mid-project.

## Verify
- [ ] Regular, bold italic, crossbar I, and needed accents present.
- [ ] Printed test at size is legible.
- [ ] Licence covers all planned uses.
