# Balloon connectors and butted balloons

**Goal:** show one speaker saying several things in a panel, with the right sense of
timing.

## Two methods
| Method | Look | Timing implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Connector** (bridge) | short thick band or narrow tube between two separate balloons | a pause/beat between statements |
| **Butted** (overlapping, merged) | balloons touching or merged into a double lobe | continuous speech, new thought without a pause |
Conventions vary by letterer; be consistent within a book.

## Rules
- Only the **first balloon** gets a tail to the speaker (the chain reads from it).
- Connected balloons flow in reading order: top-left to lower-right.
- Connector width moderate (thinner than the balloon, thick enough to look deliberate).
- **Across panel borders**: a connector crossing the gutter links one speaker's speech
  across panels (continuing thought); keep it short and clear.

## When not to link
- Different speakers: separate balloons with their own tails.
- Very long chains (4+): consider splitting across panels instead.

## Building in Illustrator
Draw each balloon, draw a rounded rectangle as the connector, Pathfinder > Unite all
into one shape so the stroke wraps continuously; or merge butted ellipses the same way.

## Verify
- [ ] Linked balloons read in order with one tail at the start.
- [ ] Connector vs butted usage consistent with the book's convention.
