# Lettering off-panel speakers

**Goal:** readers know who's talking even when they can't see them.

## Methods
1. **Tail to the border**: the tail ends at the panel edge, pointing toward where the
   speaker is (left side if they're to the left). The balloon stays inside.
2. **Balloon breaking the border**: the balloon overlaps the panel edge from the
   speaker's direction, tail pointing out of frame. Good when the speaker is just
   outside.
3. **Tail-less with context**: if the speaker is obvious from previous panels, some
   letterers drop the tail; risky, prefer a tail.
4. **Electronic voices** (phone, radio, TV) for off-panel devices: jagged/lightning tail
   to the device or border, often a squarer balloon or italic text.

## Clarity
- If the identity isn't established, the art or the previous panel must show the
  speaker; flag to the writer/editor otherwise.
- Keep the direction consistent with the scene geography (180 rule).

## Gotchas
- Pointing the tail to the wrong side: implies a different speaker.
- Using off-panel tails to fix an art problem where the speaker should be visible.

## Verify
- [ ] Every off-panel tail points toward a speaker located there.
- [ ] Electronic balloons used only for devices.
