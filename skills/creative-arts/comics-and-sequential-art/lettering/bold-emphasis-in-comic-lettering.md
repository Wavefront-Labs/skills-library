# Bold emphasis in comic lettering

**Goal:** emphasis that tells the reader how the line sounds, without turning the page
into a ransom note.

## Convention
- Comics emphasise words with **bold italic** of the dialogue font (not underline, not
  bold alone in most traditions).
- Emphasis marks **spoken stress**: the word an actor would hit. "I didn't say
  *she* took it" vs "I didn't say she *took* it."

## How much
- Often 1-2 emphasised words per balloon; many balloons need none.
- Silver Age books bolded a word per sentence for rhythm; modern style is sparser.
  Match the book's house style.

## Special cases
- **Shouts/burst balloons**: bigger and/or bolder text throughout; emphasis inside a
  shout can use extra size.
- **Captions**: narration usually lighter on emphasis; in italic caption fonts, emphasis
  may flip to roman or bold.
- **Names and titles**: don't bold just because they're important.

## From script to page
- Writers mark emphasis with bold, underline or asterisks; convert all to bold italic.
- If none marked, the letterer may add sparingly, or ask the editor; some editors want
  only scripted emphasis.

## Gotchas
- Emphasising every adjective: nothing stands out.
- Using faux bold/italic (app-generated): poor shapes; use the font's real bold italic.

## Verify
- [ ] Bold italic is the only emphasis style.
- [ ] Emphasis matches natural spoken stress and is sparse.
