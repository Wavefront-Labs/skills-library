# Balloon placement, reading order and SFX

**Goal:** place text so the reader takes in balloons in the right order without
thinking, never covers important art, and SFX adds sound without clutter.

## 1. Tools and fonts
- Most letterers work in a vector program (Adobe Illustrator, Affinity Designer) or
  Clip Studio Paint, on a layer above the art, never into the art file itself.
- Use a **comic lettering font** licensed for your use (foundries such as Blambot and
  Comicraft sell them; check licences for commercial/print/web). Not Comic Sans.
- Common conventions (Western, *house styles vary*):
  - All caps for dialogue; mixed case sometimes for stylistic books.
  - **"Crossbar I"** (I with serifs) only for the personal pronoun "I" and acronyms; the
    plain stroke I elsewhere.
  - **Bold italic** for emphasis words — a few per balloon, not every other word.
  - Double dash ("--") or em dash for interruptions; ellipsis for trailing off.
- Text size consistent through the book; check it at print size (often roughly 7–9 pt
  at printed size; *publisher specs govern*).

## 2. Reading order
- Western: left to right, top to bottom. The first balloon in a panel is top-left; the
  next lower and to the right.
- **Speaker placement matters:** if the second speaker is on the left, put their balloon
  lower or join the balloons in a way that forces the order.
- Follow the script's balloon numbers; if the art makes the order impossible, flag it.
- Balloons shouldn't cross panel borders unless they're deliberately linking panels.

## 3. Balloon shape and placement
- Text first, then balloon around it: shape the text block into a rough diamond/oval
  (short line, longer, longest, shorter), then fit an oval with even padding.
- **Don't cover faces, hands, key action, or anything the dialogue references.**
- Leave breathing room from panel borders.
- **Tails** point toward the speaker's mouth, not touching it; short, tapered, aimed at the
  head. Off-panel speaker: tail to the panel edge.
- **Linked balloons** (connectors) for the same speaker's consecutive thoughts in one panel.
- Variants: **whisper** (dashed outline), **shout/burst** (spiky outline, bigger bolder text),
  **radio/electronic** (jagged tail), **thought** (cloud with bubble trail, less common in modern
  comics, where captions do the work).

## 4. Captions
- Narration and location/time captions are boxes, usually in the top-left of the panel.
- Colour-code captions for different narrators; keep the code consistent across the book.

## 5. SFX
- SFX are drawn display lettering: shape, weight and texture match the sound (a heavy
  "THOOM" vs a tiny "tik").
- Integrate into the art: place it at the source, give it perspective or motion, and let
  it overlap art only where it doesn't hide storytelling.
- Use fewer SFX than you think; silence is also a sound.
- Ask the colourist/editor whether SFX is coloured by them or by you.

## 6. Production checks
- Spell-check against the final script; character names consistent.
- Check that no text falls outside the safe area or into the gutter/binding.
- Export text layers separately (or editable files) for translation and corrections.
- Final review at print size and on a phone screen for digital.

## Gotchas
- Text touching the balloon edge (cramped) or too much padding (floating).
- Tails crossing each other.
- Balloons placed after the art arrives covering a key face — involve the artist early
  with balloon space in the layout.
- Using the same font for SFX and dialogue.
- Bold emphasis on so many words that nothing stands out.

## Verify
- [ ] A fresh reader reads every balloon in script order on first try.
- [ ] No faces, hands or key action covered.
- [ ] Tails point to speakers' mouths without touching; no crossed tails.
- [ ] Crossbar I and bold emphasis used consistently; licensed font.
- [ ] Text in the safe area; spelling checked; editable layers delivered.
