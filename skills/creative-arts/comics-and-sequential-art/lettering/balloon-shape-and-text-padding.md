# Balloon shape and text padding

**Goal:** balloons that look hand-crafted: text shaped to an oval and an even margin
around it.

## 1. Shape the text first
- Centre-align text.
- Break lines so the block forms a rounded diamond/lozenge: short top line, longer
  middle lines, shorter bottom line. Example:
  ```
     WE CAN'T
   STAY HERE. THE
  TIDE'S TURNING AND
    THE BOAT WON'T
      WAIT.
  ```
- Avoid one-word orphan lines and breaking names or hyphenating.
- Leading tight but clear (often ~ the font's default or slightly less for caps fonts).

## 2. Draw the balloon
- An ellipse fitted around the text block, then adjusted.
- **Even padding**: roughly the height of a letter (cap height) to 1.5x on the sides
  and slightly less top/bottom is a common feel; consistency matters more than the
  number.
- Wider text blocks get flatter ovals; tall blocks rounder.
- Stroke weight consistent across the book (e.g. similar to the art's medium line).

## 3. Crops and borders
- Balloons at panel edges can be cropped by the border (flat side), common at panel
  tops. Keep the text fully inside with padding preserved.
- Don't let balloons touch other balloons unless intentionally butted.

## Tools
Illustrator: ellipse tool + Direct Selection to adjust; or balloon scripts/actions some
letterers use. CSP: Balloon tool with margin settings.

## Gotchas
- Rectangle-shaped text in an oval: corners crowd the line.
- Padding inconsistent page to page.

## Verify
- [ ] Every text block shaped to a rounded form.
- [ ] Padding visually even; no text near the stroke.
