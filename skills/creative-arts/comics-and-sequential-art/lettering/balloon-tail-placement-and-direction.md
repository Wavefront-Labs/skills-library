# Balloon tail placement and direction

**Goal:** zero doubt about who is speaking.

## Rules (common practice)
1. **Aim at the mouth** (or head), stopping short; a tail touching the face looks
   stabbed.
2. **Length**: long enough to point clearly; typically ending around halfway between
   balloon and speaker. Very long tails snaking across a panel suggest moving the
   balloon.
3. **Width**: tapered wedge starting fairly narrow at the balloon (a common guide is
   under ~1/4 of the balloon's width).
4. **Curve**: a gentle curve looks hand-drawn and lets you route around art; keep curve
   direction natural toward the speaker.
5. **Base position**: the tail leaves the balloon from the side nearest the speaker,
   usually the bottom.
6. **No crossed tails**: if tails would cross, swap balloon positions or re-order.

## Groups and crowds
- In a group, point precisely at the speaker's head; if ambiguous, place the balloon
  closer to them.
- Multiple speakers same line ("ALL: Yes!"): one balloon with multiple tails, or
  separate small balloons.

## Small panels
Short, direct tails; balloon partly outside the panel only if the style allows.

## Tool tips
Illustrator: draw tail as a separate triangle path and Unite (Pathfinder) with the
balloon; keep editable copies. CSP: Balloon tail tool with curve options.

## Verify
- [ ] Every tail points to the right speaker's mouth area, not touching.
- [ ] No crossed tails; tail lengths consistent across the book.
