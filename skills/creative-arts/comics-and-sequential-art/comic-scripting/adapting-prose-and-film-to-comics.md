# Adapting prose and film to comics

**Goal:** a comic that feels like the source but works as comics, within a fixed page
count.

## 1. Budget from the source
- List the source's scenes with a line each and a priority (A essential, B nice,
  C cut-able).
- Assign pages: A scenes get pages first. A 300-page novel into a 150-page graphic
  novel means most chapters get 4-8 pages; much must go.
- Screenplays map more directly (scenes, dialogue already), but run long: a rough
  starting estimate is ~1 comic page per 1-1.5 screenplay pages, then cut.

## 2. Compress
- Merge minor characters; combine scenes in the same location.
- Turn internal narration into images (a gesture, a symbol, a flashback panel) or a
  few first-person captions in the author's voice.
- Dialogue: keep signature lines verbatim; trim the rest to balloon length (see
  `creative-arts.comics-and-sequential-art.comic-scripting.balloon-word-counts-and-panel-text-budget`).

## 3. Find comics equivalents
- Prose time skips become scene-to-scene transitions.
- Film camera moves become panel sequences: a dolly-in becomes 3 panels moving closer.
- Montages become a grid of small panels or a single composite.

## 4. Rights and approvals
- Adaptation rights come from the rights holder; scope (formats, territories, term)
  must cover comics. Approvals may be required at outline, script and art stages;
  schedule for them.

## Gotchas
- Literal adaptations with caption boxes of prose read slowly; prefer images.
- Cutting a beloved scene without replacing its function confuses fans.

## Verify
- [ ] Scene list with priorities and page budget totals the commission.
- [ ] Each A scene's function survives.
- [ ] Narration converted to images where possible.
- [ ] Rights and approval stages confirmed.
