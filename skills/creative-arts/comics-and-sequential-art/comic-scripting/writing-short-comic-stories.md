# Writing short comic stories

**Goal:** a complete, satisfying story in a few pages; the format most new writers
are hired from (anthology slots, portfolio samples) and the hardest to fake.

## Constraints that make it work
- **One idea**: one situation, one turn. If you need two reveals, it's a longer story.
- **Cast of 1-3.** Every extra character needs introduction panels.
- **Start late, leave early**: open at the moment of pressure; end at the turn.
- **One or two locations.**

## Page budgets
| Length | Shape |
|---|---|
| 1 page | 4-6 panels: set-up, development, turn (like a strip with more room) |
| 3 pages | p1 situation, p2 complication, p3 turn/ending |
| 5 pages | p1 hook, p2-3 escalate, p4 crisis, p5 resolution (put the twist on p5 only if p4->5 is a page turn; check sides) |
| 8 pages | mini three-act: p1-2 set-up, p3-6 escalation with midpoint on p4-5, p7-8 climax and coda |

## Page sides
Anthology stories may start on a left or right page; ask. The turn should land after a
page turn, not on a facing page the reader sees early.

## Portfolio use
- Editors read samples to test structure, panel descriptions and dialogue economy.
- Send a 5-8 page short with a clean full script; if illustrated, all the better.
- No unfinished excerpts from a longer work unless requested.

## Gotchas
- Twist endings that rely on information hidden unfairly feel cheap; seed it on p1.
- Overwritten captions explaining theme; let the last image carry it.
- Exceeding the slot: anthologies have fixed page counts; 6 means 6.

## Verify
- [ ] Single idea stateable in one sentence.
- [ ] Exact page count; turn after a page turn.
- [ ] A reader new to the characters understands them by page 1.
