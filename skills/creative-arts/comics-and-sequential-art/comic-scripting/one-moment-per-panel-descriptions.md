# One moment per panel

**Goal:** every panel description describes a single drawable instant.

## The test
Could a photographer take this panel in one shot? If not, it's an impossible panel.

| Impossible | Fixed |
|---|---|
| "Mara walks to the door, opens it and sees Teo." | Two panels: Mara's hand on the handle / door open, Teo there. |
| "Teo remembers his father's death." | Show what we see: Teo staring at the photo; or a flashback panel. |
| "She realises he's lying." | Draw the expression and what triggers it: her eyes on his shaking hand. |
| "The fight lasts ten minutes." | Choose the moment: mid-swing, or the aftermath with a time caption. |

## Writing the description
1. **Lead with the shot** if it matters (wide/close/overhead), then the subject.
2. **Describe the frozen action**: "mid-leap", "about to", "just after".
3. **Include only what is visible.** Backstory goes in a note to the artist, marked as
   such.
4. **Name who is in the panel**; characters off-panel should be stated if they speak.
5. **Keep it short**: 1-4 sentences. Longer descriptions are usually 2 panels.

## Dialogue must fit the instant
A panel can hold talk that takes a few seconds; a long speech over a punch reads as
frozen time. Split speech across panels or choose a quieter moment.

## Choosing which moment (McCloud's choice of moment)
- Pick the moment that implies the most before and after: the wind-up or the impact,
  rarely the middle.
- Skip moments the reader can infer between panels (closure).

## Verify
- [ ] Every panel is one photographable instant.
- [ ] No thoughts, memories or sounds described as if visible.
- [ ] Dialogue duration plausible for the moment shown.
