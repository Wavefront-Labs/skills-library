# Balloon word counts and panel text budget

**Goal:** text that fits the art without covering it, and reads fast.

## Working limits (widely used rules of thumb, not standards)
| Unit | Comfortable | Warning |
|---|---|---|
| Words per balloon | up to ~25 | 35+ |
| Balloons per panel | 1-3 | 4+ |
| Words per panel | up to ~35-40 | 50+ |
| Words per page (standard US page) | ~150-210 | 250+ |
Large panels hold more; small inset panels hold almost nothing. A splash should carry
very little text.

## Counting
- In the script, count per panel. Word processors show selected-word counts; or keep a
  spreadsheet column per panel.
- A quick check: if a page's dialogue fills more than ~2/3 of a script page at 12 pt,
  it's probably heavy.

## Trimming without losing voice
1. Cut what the art shows ("Look, he's running away!").
2. Cut greetings, names in direct address, and "well/so/just" fillers.
3. Split a long speech across panels so the art can act it (a beat per panel).
4. Replace exposition with a prop or visual clue.
5. Turn a narration caption into an image.
Keep characteristic tics; cut neutral words first.

## Balloon size reality
Lettered at a typical 7-9 pt print size, 25 words is a balloon covering a good chunk
of a medium panel. Ask the letterer to test-letter one dense page if in doubt.

## Gotchas
- Text-dense pages in the first issue scare readers off.
- Translated editions often run longer (e.g. German, French); budget slack for them.

## Verify
- [ ] No balloon over ~35 words without a reason.
- [ ] Dense pages counted and trimmed; splash pages light.
- [ ] A test-lettered page fits without covering faces.
