# Scripting splash pages and spreads

**Goal:** big images used where they pay off, scripted so the artist knows what must
read first and where text and credits go.

## When a splash earns its page
- Scale or awe (first sight of a city, a monster, an army).
- A climax beat that should stop time.
- An opening establishing image with title and credits.
Not for: a conversation, a transition, or to fill a page count. Each splash costs
roughly 5 panels of story.

## Script it
```
PAGES 12-13 (DOUBLE-PAGE SPREAD)

Splash across both pages. The harbour from above, mid-explosion.
FOCAL POINT: Mara, tiny, on the pier at lower left, looking up.
Keep the centre gutter clear of Mara and the ship's name.
Room for: 1 caption top left, the title logo top right, credits lower right.

  1  CAPTION: The Calder Rose went up at 2:14 a.m.
  TITLE: ASH TIDE - PART THREE
  CREDITS
```
- Name the **focal point** and what reads first.
- Say what text goes on it (captions, title, credits) and roughly where.
- **Spread warnings**: nothing important in the centre ~1/2 inch (binding/gutter),
  especially for perfect-bound books.
- State whether **inset panels** sit on top of it.

## Spread placement
- A spread must start on an even (left) page to face its partner.
- Print floppies can have ads inside; ask whether the spread is guaranteed facing.
- Opening spreads at pages 2-3 are a common credits spread.

## Gotchas
- Too much text on a splash flattens its impact; keep to 1-2 captions.
- Spreads in digital single-page view split in half; artists may provide a spread
  version and two singles.

## Verify
- [ ] Each splash/spread has a stated reason, focal point and text plan.
- [ ] Spreads start on an even page; gutter-safe zone noted.
- [ ] Page budget still totals after counting splashes.
