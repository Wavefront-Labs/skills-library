# Interdependent and additive word/picture combinations

**Goal:** text and image that do different jobs, so the panel says more than either
could alone.

McCloud (Understanding Comics ch. 6, refined in Making Comics ch. 3) lists ways words
and pictures combine. The two most useful for working scripts:

## Interdependent
Words and picture **combine to convey an idea neither conveys alone**.
- Image: Mara hands Teo an envelope. Caption: "Forty thousand. The price of a ship."
- Neither the image (an envelope) nor the caption (a price) tells the story; together
  they reveal a bribe.
Use for: key plot information, irony, reveals.

## Additive
**One amplifies or elaborates the other.**
- Word-led additive: dialogue carries meaning, the image adds tone (a quiet "I'm fine"
  with the art adding clenched fists).
- Picture-led additive: the image carries action; a short caption adds time, place, or
  sound.

## Redundancy test
For each panel, cover the text: what is lost? Then cover the image: what is lost? If
nothing is lost in either case, one is redundant (duo-specific). Duo-specific panels
are fine for emphasis or very young readers, but in quantity they read slow and
patronising.

## Scripting practice
1. Write the panel description first, then ask what the words must add.
2. Prefer interdependent panels at turning points.
3. In dense dialogue scenes, give the art something to add (business, props,
   expressions) so the panel isn't only words.

## Verify
- [ ] Turning-point panels are interdependent: cover-test loses meaning both ways.
- [ ] Few duo-specific panels outside deliberate emphasis.
