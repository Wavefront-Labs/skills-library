# Picture-specific and word-specific panels

**Goal:** know which channel carries each panel, and avoid sequences where one channel
does all the work by accident.

## Definitions (McCloud, Understanding Comics ch. 6)
- **Picture-specific**: the picture tells everything; words add a soundtrack (SFX) or a
  tag ("Boom!", "Mara?").
- **Word-specific**: the words tell everything; the picture illustrates (a narrator box
  saying "The war ended in spring" over a generic field).
- **Duo-specific**: both say the same thing.

## When each works
| Type | Good for | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Picture-specific | action, wordless emotion, kinetic sequences | confusing if the action is complex |
| Word-specific | time jumps, summaries, literary narration, letters | illustration becomes wallpaper; reads like prose |
| Duo-specific | emphasis, children's books | redundancy, slow pace |

## Script checks
1. For each page, label panels P (picture), W (word), D (duo), I (interdependent).
2. A page of all W is prose with pictures: add business or acting so the art adds
   information, or cut text.
3. A page of all P in a complex plot: check a reader can follow cause and effect.
4. Action sequences: let them run picture-specific; add only SFX and short shouts.

## Gotchas
- Word-specific narration over generic images tempts artists to coast; give them a
  specific subject to draw.
- Converting a prose story to comics produces word-specific pages by default.

## Verify
- [ ] Each page has a deliberate mix; no accidental runs of W or D.
- [ ] Action pages picture-led.
