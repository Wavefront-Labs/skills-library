# Camera and shot notes in comic scripts

**Goal:** direct the camera only where the story depends on it, in vocabulary every
artist understands.

## Shared vocabulary
| Note | Means |
|---|---|
| ESTABLISHING / WIDE | location readable, figures small |
| FULL / LONG | whole figure head to toe |
| MEDIUM | waist up; most dialogue |
| CLOSE / CU | head and shoulders |
| EXTREME CLOSE / ECU | eyes, a hand, an object detail |
| OVER-THE-SHOULDER (OTS) | one character in foreground back, other facing |
| POV | what a character sees |
| HIGH ANGLE / BIRD'S-EYE | looking down; subject small, vulnerable |
| LOW ANGLE / WORM'S-EYE | looking up; subject imposing |
| INSET | small panel inside a larger one |
| WIDE PANEL / TIER-WIDE | panel spans the page width |

## When to specify
- **Information must be visible**: the clue on the desk, the gun in the drawer.
- **Emotional emphasis**: a close-up at a turning point.
- **Pacing**: "small panel" for a quick beat, "tier-wide" for a held moment.
- **Continuity**: who is on the left/right (180-degree rule) in a dialogue scene.

## When not to
- Every panel. Over-directing produces stiff pages and annoys good storytellers.
- Use "artist's choice" for transitional panels.

## Format
Put the shot first, in caps or as a short phrase:
```
PANEL 3
CLOSE on Teo's left hand, the burn scar visible as he grips the rail.
```

## Gotchas
- Film terms like "pan" or "zoom" don't exist in a still panel; describe the frame.
- Asking for an angle that hides the needed information (e.g. low angle on a desk clue).

## Verify
- [ ] Every camera note has a story reason.
- [ ] Required visual information is reachable in the requested shot.
