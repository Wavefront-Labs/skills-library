# Distinct character voices

**Goal:** readers can tell who is talking from the words alone, even in short balloons.

## Voice sheet (one per main character, in the series bible)
- **Vocabulary**: register (formal/slang), jargon from their job, words they never use.
- **Rhythm**: short clipped sentences vs long run-ons; interrupts others or waits.
- **Tics**: a recurring phrase, used sparingly (max once per scene).
- **What they avoid saying**: subtext drives better dialogue than stated feelings.
- 3-5 sample lines.

## Techniques that fit balloons
- Sentence length is the cheapest differentiator in a medium where text is short.
- Questions vs statements: one character always deflects with a question.
- Profanity level, contractions, and how they address others (names, nicknames, none).
- Letterer support: a distinct balloon style or font for non-human voices (agree in
  the bible, not per panel).

## Test
**Cover the names**: strip the speaker tags from a page of script; can a reader
assign each line? If not, revise the weaker voice.

## Dialect and accent
- Suggest with word choice and syntax rather than phonetic spelling; heavy
  apostrophes slow reading and can caricature.
- For other languages, use the translated-speech convention (brackets/angled) and
  state it once.

## Gotchas
- Every character sounds like the writer: witty, articulate. Give someone a plain voice.
- Tics overused become parody.

- [ ] Voice sheets exist for leads.
