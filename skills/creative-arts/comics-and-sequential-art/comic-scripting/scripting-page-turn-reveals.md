# Scripting page-turn reveals

**Goal:** surprises hit when the reader turns the page, not when their eye drifts to
the facing page early.

## Mechanics of a printed comic
- Readers see two pages at once: a left (even) and right (odd) page.
- A **page turn** happens after each odd (right-hand) page. What sits on the next even
  page is hidden until the turn.
- So: **reveals go on even pages** (preferably the first panel, top-left, or as a
  large panel/splash). The **last panel of the odd page** is the set-up: a question,
  a door opening, a character reacting to something off-panel.
- Exception: page 1 is a right-hand page in a standard floppy; the cover is the "turn"
  into it.

## Writing it
1. In the page breakdown, mark every page L or R.
2. Write the prompt on the R page's final panel: "Teo turns. His face goes white."
3. Put the answer on the next L page, panel 1 or a large panel: "Reveal: the burnt
   ship's figurehead on his desk."
4. Keep the R page's final panel free of the answer (no hint in dialogue).

## Facing-page leaks
- A big image on a right page is seen as soon as the reader turns to the left page.
  If it's a surprise, it belongs on a left page.
- Dialogue on the left page that names the surprise kills a right-page reveal.

## Digital and webtoon
- Guided-view and single-page digital readers turn every page; L/R matters less, but
  print collections will restore spreads. Write for print if a print edition exists.
- Vertical-scroll uses scroll space instead (see the webcomics product).

## Gotchas
- Inserted ads in floppies can change which pages face each other; confirm.
- Adding or cutting a page late flips every later side; re-check all reveals.

## Verify
- [ ] Every key reveal is on an even page and is not visible from the previous spread.
- [ ] The odd page before each reveal ends on a prompt.
- [ ] Sides re-checked after any page-count change.
