# Writing a plot breakdown for the artist (Marvel method)

**Goal:** a plot document that gives the artist the story, its page budget and its
emotional beats, while leaving panel-by-panel staging to them. Dialogue is written
later over the pencils.

## When to use it
- The artist is a strong storyteller who wants control of pacing and layout.
- Deadlines need the artist to start before a full script exists.
- Avoid with new or unknown collaborators: the plot method gives the artist many
  decisions, and a weak storyteller will misjudge space for dialogue.

## Structure of the document
```
ISSUE 4 - PLOT - 22 pages

PAGES 1-2 (spread): The harbour burns. We need to feel the scale: Mara tiny
against the fire. Must-have: the ship's name visible on the hull.

PAGE 3: Mara pulls Teo out of the water. He's alive but won't look at her.
Talk scene - leave room for about 6 short exchanges. End on Teo's hand: the
burn mark she recognises.

PAGE 4 (page-turn): Full-page flashback...
```
Rules:
1. **Assign every beat a page range** and total to the page count.
2. **State the purpose** of each page (what the reader must feel/learn), not the shots.
3. **Mark must-haves**: props, a specific expression, a clue that pays off later.
4. **Estimate dialogue load** ("about 6 exchanges", "silent page") so the artist
   leaves balloon room.
5. **Flag page turns** and the final-page hook.
6. Include key lines you already know; the artist can stage the acting to them.
7. Attach reference: character sheets, location photos, previous issue pages.

## Length
Typically 1/2 to 1 paragraph per page; a 22-page plot often runs 4-8 pages of text.
Too long and you are writing a full script without the format benefits.

## Gotchas
- Vague beats ("they fight") produce 6 pages where you budgeted 2. Give page ranges.
- Unspoken information that only dialogue can carry must still be noted, or the art
  won't show who is speaking to whom.
- Agree in advance who decides panel counts and when the writer sees layouts.

## Verify
- [ ] Every page accounted for and totals the commission.
- [ ] Each page has purpose, must-haves and expected dialogue load.
- [ ] Artist has confirmed questions before pencilling.
