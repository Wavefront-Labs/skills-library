# Single-issue (22-page) structure

**Goal:** an issue that hooks on page 1, turns in the middle, ends on a hook, and gives
the reader something complete for their money even inside a longer arc.

## A workable page map (a template, not a law)
| Pages | Job |
|---|---|
| 1 | Hook: action, mystery or a striking image. Page 1 is a right-hand page; the first spread is pages 2-3. |
| 2-3 | Orient: who, where, what's at stake. A spread here is common for a big establishing image. |
| 4-8 | Act one: the problem escalates; first choice made. |
| 9-12 | Midpoint turn around page 11: reveal, reversal or new information. |
| 13-18 | Act two complications; the plan fails; confrontation builds. |
| 19-21 | Climax of this issue's question. |
| 22 | Hook/cliffhanger or resonant final image; recap the stakes for next issue. |

## Rules
- **Answer one question per issue** even when the arc continues ("Will they get the
  ledger?" yes, but the ledger is fake). Readers who buy one issue need payoff.
- **Pages 1 and 22 are the most important pages**; write them first.
- **Page turns**: big reveals on even pages (seen after turning). Put the cliffhanger
  on the last right-hand page only if the last page is odd; otherwise use the final
  even page so the turn delivers it.
- **Recap efficiently**: a caption, a line of dialogue, or a recap page (Marvel-style
  credits/recap page) rather than three pages of exposition.
- **Scene count**: typically 4-7 scenes. More than ~9 fragments the read.

## Gotchas
- Spending pages 1-5 on setup with nothing happening; the browse-in-store reader
  decides on the first pages.
- Ending on a "to be continued" without a turn; the hook must change something.
- Ads may split spreads in print floppies; confirm ad placement before scripting a
  spread in the middle of the issue.

## Verify
- [ ] Page 1 hook and page 22 hook written and strong on their own.
- [ ] A reversal around the middle.
- [ ] The issue's own question is answered.
- [ ] Reveals land on page turns.
