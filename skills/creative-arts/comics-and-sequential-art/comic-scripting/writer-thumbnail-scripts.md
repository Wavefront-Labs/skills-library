# Writer thumbnail scripts

**Goal:** test the comic as a comic (space, rhythm, balloon load) before committing to
a text script, even if you can't draw.

## Setup
- Template sheet with 2-4 page rectangles at the book's proportion (US comic about
  1:1.54; manga B5 tankobon ~1:1.4; graphic novel per trim). Pair them as spreads:
  even page on the left, odd on the right.
- Pencil or a tablet at very small size: thumbnails should be 5-8 cm tall to stop
  you from rendering.

## Process
1. Draw panel borders first from your beat list; vary size by importance and time.
2. Stick figures/circles for characters; arrows for movement; X for the focal point.
3. Write the dialogue in rough ovals where it would sit. If it doesn't fit, the scene
   is overwritten, not the panel too small.
4. Look at each spread as a whole: does the eye path work, and is the reveal on the
   next page turn, not on the facing page?
5. Transcribe to a text script when the thumbnails read well, or send both to the
   artist marked "layout suggestions, not mandatory" if that's your agreement.

## Why it works
- You feel panel density physically; 9 panels with 4 balloons each visibly can't fit.
- You see facing-page spoilers (the reader's eye catches the right page while reading
  the left).
- You think in McCloud's choices: moment, frame, image, word, flow.

## Gotchas
- Don't send thumbnails that dictate camera to an artist who didn't ask for them.
- Keep them ugly. Beautiful thumbnails slow revisions.

## Verify
- [ ] Every page's balloons fit in their roughs.
- [ ] Reveals sit on page turns; spreads read as units.
- [ ] Text script panel counts match the thumbnails.
