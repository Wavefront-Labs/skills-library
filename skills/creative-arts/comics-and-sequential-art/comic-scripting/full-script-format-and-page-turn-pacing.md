# Full-script format and page-turn pacing

**Goal:** write a comic script that an artist can draw without guessing, paced so that
reveals land on page turns and pages don't drown in words.

## 1. Choose the method
- **Full script:** page by page, panel by panel, with descriptions and all dialogue. Most
  common in Western comics; gives the writer control of pacing.
- **Plot-first ("Marvel method"):** a page-by-page plot; the artist breaks it into panels,
  then the writer scripts dialogue over the art. Gives the artist room; needs trust and a
  second dialogue pass.
Agree the method with the artist before writing.

## 2. Page breakdown before script
List every page with a one-line summary before writing any panel. For a single issue
(commonly ~20–22 story pages in US periodicals; *varies by publisher*) this shows at once
whether the story fits. Mark:
- **Left (even) and right (odd) pages.** In a Western book, page 1 is a right-hand page;
  every even page is a left page that the reader sees *after* turning.
- **Page turns:** the reveal goes on the **left page after a turn** (even pages),
  so the reader can't see it before turning. Put the cliffhanger/question on the
  bottom of the odd (right) page before it.
- Spreads (two facing pages) only start on an even page.

## 3. Script format (one common layout)
```
PAGE SIX (left)

PANEL 1
Wide. The flooded train station at night. MAYA, 30s, wades through knee-deep water,
torch raised.
  1 CAPTION: Three hours after the dam broke.
  2 MAYA: Hello? Anyone?

PANEL 2
Close on the torch beam: a child's red boot floats past.
  3 SFX: plip
```
- Number the balloons/captions **continuously across the page** — letterers depend on it.
- **One moment per panel.** A panel can't show "she opens the door and then runs" — that's
  two panels.
- Describe what the artist must draw (who, where, action, key emotion, important props,
  camera if it matters). Leave composition freedom unless the story needs it.
- Introduce characters with a short visual description on first appearance.

## 4. Budgets that keep pages readable
- **Panels per page:** 4–6 is a common working average; 9 is dense; splash pages
  (one panel) are for big moments only.
- **Words per panel:** many editors aim for roughly 25–35 words max per panel and ~200
  per page at most; fewer is usually better. *These are rules of thumb, not standards.*
- **Balloons per panel:** two or three is comfortable; more crowds the art.
- **Per speaker:** a balloon is ~1–3 sentences. Long speeches break into linked balloons.

## 5. Pacing tools
- More panels = slower time; fewer, larger panels = impact.
- A silent panel (no words) creates a beat/pause.
- Cut between scenes on page ends, not mid-page, unless the cut is the effect.
- Vary panel sizes to signal importance.

## 6. Working with the artist
- Send the page breakdown first; adjust after their feedback.
- Provide reference for real places/objects.
- After pencils, revise dialogue to fit the art (cut words the image already shows).

## Gotchas
- Describing two actions in one panel.
- Putting the big reveal on a right-hand page where it's visible before the turn.
- Captions repeating what the art shows.
- Forgetting the letterer: dialogue order must match the reading path (left to right,
  top to bottom in Western comics).

## Verify
- [ ] Page breakdown fits the page count with turns marked.
- [ ] Every major reveal sits on an even (left) page after a turn.
- [ ] Each panel describes one moment; balloons numbered continuously.
- [ ] Word counts per panel/page within your budget.
- [ ] Artist has reviewed the breakdown before full script.
