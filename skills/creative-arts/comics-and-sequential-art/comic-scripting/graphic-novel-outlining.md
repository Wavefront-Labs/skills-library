# Graphic novel outlining

**Goal:** a long-form outline that fits the printed book and breaks into chunks the
artist can schedule.

## 1. Fix the container
- Total printed pages are usually a multiple of 8 or 16 (offset signatures) or an even
  number for print-on-demand; confirm with the publisher/printer.
- Subtract front/back matter: half-title, title, copyright, dedication, chapter
  openers, backmatter. A 160-page book may have ~148 story pages.
- Trim size affects density: a 6x9 in digest holds fewer panels than an 8.5x11 album.

## 2. Break into parts
1. Logline and ending first.
2. Three to five **parts/chapters** (chapters ease production and give readers rest
   points; chapter openers can be full-page images).
3. Within each, **sequences** (a sequence = one dramatic unit, a few scenes).
4. Budget pages per sequence; total must equal story pages.
   ```
   Ch1 (pp 1-34): Arrival 8 / The job 10 / First night 9 / Fire 7
   ```

## 3. Rhythm across 150+ pages
- Alternate dense dialogue sequences with open, silent or action sequences.
- Place full-page or spread images at structural moments (chapter climaxes), not at
  random.
- Mid-book reversal near the centre page.

## 4. Production planning
- Artists often draw out of order; outline lists which pages share locations/costumes
  so reference is gathered once.
- Schedule: pages per week x weeks; pencils/inks/colours/letters in overlapping waves.
- Lock chapter 1 script before the artist starts; outline the rest.

## Gotchas
- Writing the script before knowing the trim and page count; reformatting costs pages.
- Under-budgeting endings: last chapters tend to compress; budget them first.
- Forgetting blank versos: chapter openers on right-hand pages may force a blank left.

## Verify
- [ ] Story pages + front/back matter = a printer-valid total.
- [ ] Every sequence has a page budget; totals match.
- [ ] Chapter openers and spreads fall on the intended sides.
