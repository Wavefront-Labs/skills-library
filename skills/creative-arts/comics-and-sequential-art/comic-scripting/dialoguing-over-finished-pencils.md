# Dialoguing over finished pencils

**Goal:** dialogue and captions that fit the space, match the acting the artist drew,
and add information rather than describe the picture.

## Workflow
1. **Read the pages silently first**, as a reader would. Note where the art already
   tells the story; those panels may need no words.
2. **Mark free space** on low-res copies: circle the areas where balloons can go
   without covering faces, hands or key action. That is your word budget per panel.
3. **Read the acting.** Write to the expression and body language drawn, not the one
   you imagined in the plot. If a character is drawn smiling, the line must work with
   a smile, or flag the panel for a fix.
4. **Write the script against page/panel numbers** with balloon numbers, exactly like
   a full script, so the letterer can work from it.
5. **Add a placement guide**: a copy of each page with numbered balloon positions
   (rough ovals) and tail directions. This prevents misordered balloons.
6. **Check reading order**: the first speaker must be top-left of the panel. If the art
   puts the first speaker bottom-right, rewrite so the other character speaks first or
   ask for an art adjustment.

## What to avoid
- **Redundant text**: "I'll punch him!" over a punch. Use words for what the image
  can't show: motive, sound, time, subtext (McCloud's word/picture combinations).
- Exposition dumps that need more room than the panel has.
- Changing the plot in dialogue so that the art no longer makes sense.

## Gotchas
- Silent panels drawn with big open sky may tempt you to fill them. Don't, unless the
  story needs it.
- If the artist changed a beat, decide whether to adapt or request a redraw early;
  redraws after inks are expensive.

## Verify
- [ ] Every balloon has a marked position and fits its space at print size.
- [ ] Reading order works with speaker positions in each panel.
- [ ] No caption merely describes what the panel already shows.
