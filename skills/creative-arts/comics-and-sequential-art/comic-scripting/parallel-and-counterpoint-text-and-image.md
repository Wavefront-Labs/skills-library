# Parallel and counterpoint text and image

**Goal:** use the gap between what is said and what is shown as a storytelling engine.

## Parallel (McCloud)
Words and pictures follow **different courses without intersecting**.
- Captions narrate a childhood memory while the images show a present-day heist.
- The reader holds two lines and connects them at a chosen moment.

## Counterpoint (contradiction/irony)
Words claim one thing, pictures show another.
- "Everything's under control." over a burning building.
- A letter's polite text over images of the writer's violent day.

## How to script
1. Decide which track is "truth" (usually the picture).
2. Keep each track coherent on its own: captions should read as a continuous text if
   lifted out; images should sequence without the captions.
3. Mark the device in a note so the artist doesn't "illustrate" the captions:
   `NOTE: captions are her letter; art shows the present-day robbery. Don't depict the letter.`
4. Plan the **convergence point**: the panel where text and image meet (the letter's
   last line lands as the robbery goes wrong).
5. Visually separate the tracks if needed: caption colour/style for the parallel text.

## Gotchas
- Readers lose track if both tracks change topic often; keep each simple.
- Overuse makes the book feel cold or clever-clever; save it for sequences that need it.

## Verify
- [ ] Each track reads on its own.
- [ ] Artist note explains the device.
- [ ] A convergence point exists and lands on a strong panel.
