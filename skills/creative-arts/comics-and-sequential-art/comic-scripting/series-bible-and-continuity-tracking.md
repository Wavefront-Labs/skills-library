# Series bible and continuity tracking

**Goal:** one living document so the writer, artist, colourist, letterer and editor
make the same choices about who, where, when and what the rules are.

## Contents
1. **Logline and tone**: one paragraph, plus 3-5 comparable works.
2. **Characters**: name, age, appearance, voice notes (sample lines), relationships,
   secrets (marked as not-yet-revealed), status at the end of each arc. Link the
   artist's model sheet and the colourist's swatches.
3. **World rules**: technology/magic limits, geography, institutions. Rules that the
   plot depends on must be written before they are tested.
4. **Locations**: description, maps/floor plans, reference photos, recurring props.
5. **Timeline**: in-story dates per scene, ages, seasons (affects colour and costume).
6. **Continuity log**: per issue, a table of facts established.
   | Issue/page | Fact | Who knows |
   |---|---|---|
   | 2/14 | Teo's burn scar on left hand | Mara |
   | 3/7 | Ship is the *Calder Rose* | reader only |
7. **Style notes**: caption colour coding per narrator, SFX conventions, spellings.

## Practice
- Update the log the day an issue's script locks; artists check it before drawing.
- Keep it in a shared doc/wiki with a change history; date revisions.
- Mark spoilers so a new team member can read safely.
- Index by character name; searching beats reading.

## Gotchas
- Wrong hand/side details (scars, eye patch, parting) are the most common continuity
  errors in art; put them in bold.
- A bible that is never updated is worse than none: people trust stale facts.
- Don't overbuild the world before writing; add entries when the story uses them.

## Verify
- [ ] Every recurring character has visual ref, voice notes and current status.
- [ ] Continuity log updated through the latest locked script.
- [ ] Team members confirm they have the link.
