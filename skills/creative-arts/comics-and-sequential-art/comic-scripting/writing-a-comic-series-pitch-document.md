# Writing a comic series pitch document

**Goal:** a pitch that answers "what is it, why now, why you, why it sells" fast.

## Structure (3-6 pages total; follow any publisher guideline first)
1. **Title, format, length**: "ASH TIDE - 5-issue miniseries, 22 pp/issue, crime".
2. **Logline**: one or two sentences with protagonist, conflict and stakes.
3. **Hook paragraph**: tone and the unique angle.
4. **Comparables**: 2-3 titles ("Criminal meets On the Waterfront"). Use recent,
   successful comps in the same market.
5. **Characters**: 3-5, a short paragraph each with a sketch if available.
6. **Synopsis**: the first arc including the ending; editors want to see you can
   finish. One paragraph per issue for a mini; one page for an OGN.
7. **Series potential**: what later arcs look like (for ongoing pitches).
8. **Team and credits**: writer/artist/colourist/letterer, with links to work.
9. **Art**: character designs, cover mock-up, 5 sample pages (see the pitch packet
   skill `creative-arts.comics-and-sequential-art.comic-scripting.assembling-a-pitch-packet-with-sample-pages`).

## Craft notes
- Write the synopsis in present tense, plainly. No teasing ("will she survive?").
- Lead with what's different, not genre clichés.
- Proofread: editors filter on sloppiness.

## Submission etiquette
- Many publishers don't accept unsolicited pitches, or only during windows; check each
  publisher's submissions page and follow it exactly (format, file type, team-only).
- Never send the same pitch to the same editor twice after a no unless invited.
- Keep a log: publisher, date, editor, response.

## Gotchas
- Hiding the ending: reads as a writer who hasn't figured it out.
- Pitching a 12-issue epic as a debut; a 4-5 issue mini or OGN is easier to green-light.

## Verify
- [ ] Logline is two sentences or fewer.
- [ ] Synopsis includes the ending.
- [ ] Format, length and team stated on page 1.
