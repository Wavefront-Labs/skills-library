# Handling exposition in comics

**Goal:** the reader learns what they need, when they need it, without pages of
talking heads.

## Ladder (prefer the higher rung)
1. **Show it in the art**: a wanted poster, a newspaper headline, a scar, a map on the
   wall. One panel of a prop beats three balloons.
2. **Dramatise it**: two characters disagree about the fact; information leaks out of
   conflict ("You signed the deal. I watched you burn the copy.").
3. **Flashback panels**: a few panels, visually coded (colour, border style).
4. **Caption**: one short caption with time/place or a fact.
5. **Recap/infographic page**: a front-of-book recap or in-world document page.
6. **Dialogue lecture**: last resort; break it across panels and give the listener
   reactions/actions.

## Rules
- **Need-to-know timing**: deliver information the moment before it matters.
- **No "as you know"**: characters don't tell each other things both know.
- **Spread it**: one fact per scene beats five in the first scene.
- **Mystery is fine**: readers tolerate not knowing if the story is moving.

## Visual budgeting
A text-heavy explanation needs action or acting in the panels: walking and talking,
working on something, reacting. Static talking heads plus long balloons is the
classic failure (see
`creative-arts.comics-and-sequential-art.page-layout-and-pencilling.staging-talking-heads-scenes`).

## Verify
- [ ] Each expository fact is needed in the next few pages.
- [ ] At least half the exposition is carried by images or conflict.
- [ ] No balloon begins with "As you know".
