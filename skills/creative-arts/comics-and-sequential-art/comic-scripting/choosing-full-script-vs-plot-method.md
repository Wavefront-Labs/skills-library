# Choosing full script vs plot method

**Goal:** pick the method that fits the collaborator and deadline, and agree it in
writing before work starts.

## Decision table
| Factor | Full script | Plot-first (Marvel) | Hybrid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Writer control of pacing | high | low | medium |
| Artist control of staging | low-medium | high | medium |
| Start time for artist | after script | after short plot | after page-breakdown |
| Risk of dialogue not fitting | low | high | medium |
| Writer passes | 1 (+revisions) | 2 (plot, dialogue) | 1-2 |
| Best with | new/unknown artists, dense or twisty plots, translation | veteran storytellers, action-heavy books | established teams |

Hybrid forms:
- **Full script with loose panel descriptions** (the writer sets panels and dialogue,
  the artist chooses camera).
- **Page breakdown + dialogue**: per page, list beats and the dialogue, no panel split.
  The artist panels it.

## Questions to settle up front
1. Who decides panel count per page?
2. Does the writer see layouts/thumbnails before pencils? (Recommended for both.)
3. When is final dialogue locked, and does the letterer get it with or after art?
4. How are rates split if the writer does two passes? (Contract matter; some publishers
   pay plot and script separately.)

## Signals you chose wrong
- Plot method and the artist keeps adding pages: switch to page-by-page breakdowns.
- Full script and the art looks stiff and over-directed: loosen camera notes, let the
  artist propose layouts.

## Verify
- [ ] Method and decision points written in the collaboration agreement or email.
- [ ] A one-page sample done in the chosen method before the whole issue.
