# Daily editorial cartoon deadline workflow

Staff editorial cartoonists traditionally produce 4-6 cartoons a week, each conceived and finished in one
working day.

## Typical day
| Time | Task |
|---|---|
| Early morning | Read the news: front pages, wires, the paper's own news list; note 3-5 candidate topics. |
| Mid-morning | Rough 3-6 sketches across 2-3 topics. Thumbnails with caption ideas, 10-20 minutes each. |
| Late morning | Show the editor/editorial page editor. Agree one, fix wording, check it doesn't clash with the day's editorial (or deliberately complements it). |
| Afternoon | Pencils, inks (or digital equivalent), lettering, colour if the paper runs colour. 3-5 hours. |
| Deadline | File to the page designer at the agreed size and format, plus syndicate upload. |

## Tactics
- Keep an "evergreen" folder: 2-3 finished, non-time-sensitive cartoons for sick days and slow news.
- Have a fallback topic if breaking news kills the chosen one at 3pm. Re-sketch fast; simpler is fine.
- Draw at a fixed size template (e.g. horizontal ~2:1 for the op-ed page) so files drop straight in.
- Verify facts and quotes before inking; a corrections notice on a cartoon is humiliating.

## Filing
- High-resolution TIFF or JPEG at the paper's spec (often 300 ppi at print width, greyscale or CMYK),
  plus an RGB web version.
- Include caption, alt text and the date line; syndication services want a keyword list.

## Gotchas
- Refining an idea too long leaves no time to draw. Lock the idea by midday.
- Topic chosen before the news settles may be overtaken by events; check headlines again before filing.

## Verify
- [ ] Editor sign-off recorded; facts checked.
- [ ] Files delivered in print and web formats before the cut-off.
