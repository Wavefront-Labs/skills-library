# Drawing caricatures by exaggerating distinctive features

**Goal:** a drawing that reads as one specific person faster than a realistic portrait does.
Caricature is iconic abstraction (McCloud) aimed at a likeness: keep what identifies, drop the rest.

## Find the likeness first
1. Work from 3-5 references (front, three-quarter, a candid). One photo gives one expression, not the face.
2. Compare to an "average" face. Ask of each feature: bigger or smaller, higher or lower, closer or wider,
   longer or shorter than average. The answers are the caricature.
3. The likeness lives in *relationships*, not features: eye spacing vs nose length, forehead-to-chin
   proportion, jaw width vs cranium, distance nose-to-mouth. Write down the top three.
4. Note the head shape as one simple form (pear, egg, block, inverted triangle). Most caricatures
   start from that silhouette.

## Exaggerate
- Push the top relationships by a consistent factor; shrink what is ordinary so the strong parts read
  stronger ("if it's big make it bigger, if it's small make it smaller").
- Keep the internal logic: if the face is long, all vertical distances grow together.
- Typical facial "tells": eyebrow angle, smile shape and teeth display, eyelid weight, hairline, ears,
  glasses, signature hair. One or two props or a hairstyle can carry a public figure alone.
- Body: big-head/small-body convention for editorial use; use the person's real posture and gesture.

## Simplify
- Reduce lines to the ones that carry the likeness; delete wrinkles and detail that only add age.
- Use line weight to put emphasis on the defining features.

## Gotchas
- Exaggerating every feature equally produces a generic ugly face, not a likeness.
- Drawing from one press photo copies that photographer's expression and can raise copyright issues for
  close tracing; synthesise from several references.
- Cruelty is not likeness. Exaggerate what the person is known for, not body traits unrelated to the point
  (editors increasingly reject this).

## Verify
- [ ] Show it to someone without telling them who it is; they name the person within a few seconds.
- [ ] Shrink to thumbnail size: does the silhouette still read as the person?
- [ ] The top three relationships from your notes are clearly pushed.
