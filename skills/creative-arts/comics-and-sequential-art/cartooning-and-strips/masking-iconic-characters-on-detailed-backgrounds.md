# Masking: iconic characters on detailed backgrounds

**Goal:** reader identification with characters plus a vivid, tangible world.

## The effect (McCloud, Understanding Comics ch. 2)
McCloud describes "masking": simply drawn characters let readers "mask" themselves in
the character, while realistically rendered backgrounds and objects present a world to
be seen and sensed. He cites European ligne claire (e.g. Hergé) and much manga as
examples.

## Applying it
1. **Characters**: iconic, consistent, expressive designs (limited detail).
2. **Environments**: researched, detailed, perspective-correct; use photo reference
   or 3D.
3. **Objects of significance** drawn realistically to emphasise "otherness" or
   tangibility (a sword, a car, food).
4. **Antagonists or strangers** can be drawn more realistically than protagonists to
   create distance.
5. **Line consistency**: keep line weights compatible so characters and backgrounds sit
   in the same world (ligne claire uses uniform lines for both).

## Pitfalls
- Style clash: cartoon characters "pasted" over photo-traced backgrounds with different
  line quality.
- Backgrounds so busy they bury the characters; use value/colour separation.

## Verify
- [ ] Characters read clearly against backgrounds (value separation).
- [ ] Line quality unified between figure and environment.
