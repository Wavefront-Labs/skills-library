# Magazine cartoon submissions

Single-panel gag cartoon markets (The New Yorker, Private Eye, The Spectator, Reader's Digest, trade and
niche magazines) buy from freelancers who submit in batches.

## The batch
- Submit roughs, not finished art: about 10 cartoons per batch, weekly or on a regular cadence.
- Each rough on its own page/file, drawn clearly enough to judge, caption typed below.
- Name, contact and a unique ID number per cartoon (e.g. 2026-041) so you can track it.
- Check the magazine's current portal: The New Yorker takes online submissions with its own form and
  limits; many UK magazines take email batches.

## When one sells
- The editor buys the rough; you produce finished art to their size and style requirements.
- Rights: usually first publication rights plus archive/online use; check whether the magazine also
  licenses resale (The New Yorker's Cartoon Bank/CondeNast Store pays a share).
- Payment on acceptance or on publication varies; record it.

## Tracking
- Spreadsheet: ID, caption, date sent, market, result, payment. Rejected cartoons go to the next market
  (sequential, not simultaneous, unless the market allows).
- Acceptance rates are low (single-digit percentages for top markets is normal); volume matters.

## Craft notes
- Caption below the drawing in quotation marks for spoken lines; no speech balloons in classic style.
- Topical jokes have a short shelf life; evergreen jokes can circulate for years.

## Gotchas
- Submitting finished art for every gag wastes days.
- Sending the same cartoon to two markets simultaneously and both buying it damages relationships.
