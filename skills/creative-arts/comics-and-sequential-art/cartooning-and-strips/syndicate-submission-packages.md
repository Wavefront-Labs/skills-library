# Syndicate submission packages

Newspaper syndicates (Andrews McMeel Syndication/GoComics, King Features, Creators, Washington Post
Writers Group) still accept unsolicited strip submissions, though slots are rare. Check each syndicate's
current submissions page; requirements change and some accept only online portfolios.

## Classic package
1. **Cover letter** - one page: strip title, one-line premise, who it's for, your background, a link to
   where it's running online (an existing audience counts).
2. **Character sheet** - each main character drawn with a two-line description.
3. **Samples** - 24 to 36 daily strips (four to six weeks), plus a few Sundays if you can. Show the
   strip working over time: recurring cast, running gags, a short continuity.
4. Copies only, never originals. Name, email and title on every sheet.

## Format
- Daily strip: horizontal, standard proportion about 3.4:1 to 4:1 (print often ~ 4.5 x 1.25 in at
  reduction); draw larger (e.g. 13 x 4 in) at the same ratio.
- Sunday: modular layouts with a throwaway top tier so papers can drop panels.
- Digital: high-res PDF or images, lettering legible at newspaper size.

## What editors look for
- A distinct voice and premise that can generate thousands of strips.
- Consistent quality across the full sample, not five great strips and 25 filler.
- A 6-month buffer potential and a production schedule (see strip buffer skill).

## After submitting
- Expect weeks to months; most reply only to interest.
- Development deals may follow: revisions, trial period, then contract (term, split of syndication
  revenue, often 50/50, and rights to merchandise and collections - read carefully).

## Gotchas
- Sending one sample week signals you can't sustain it.
- Topical jokes date the package; keep most samples evergreen.

## Verify
- [ ] Lettering readable when printed at newspaper width.
- [ ] Every sheet identifies you; submission guidelines of that syndicate followed exactly.
