# Drawing plewds and sweat marks

Mort Walker's *Lexicon of Comicana* names the flying sweat droplets around a worried or straining
character **plewds**. They are emanata: symbols that show an inner state, not literal sweat.

## Shape
- Teardrop with the rounded end away from the head and the point toward the head (they fly off).
- Draw them as small closed outlines, often with a tiny highlight or left open; filled black reads as
  ink spatter instead.
- Size: roughly the width of the character's eye or smaller. Too big and they read as rain.

## Placement and count
- Arranged in an arc just off the head contour, usually on the upper half and to both sides.
- Count sets intensity: 1-2 = mild nerves; 3-5 = clear anxiety or exertion; a full halo = panic.
- Pair with other emanata: wavy "worry lines", squiggles, gulp text, or a single big drop on the temple.

## Variants
- Single large sweat drop on the side of the head (manga *ase*): embarrassment or exasperation, not effort.
- Running drips down the face: heat or physical labour, combine with heat squiggles (waftaroms/
  wavy lines) rising from the body.
- Droplets flying off with motion lines behind them: violent exertion, a fast head turn.

## Gotchas
- Plewds pointing the wrong way read as being drawn toward the head (incoming rain).
- Mixing too many emanata in one panel muddles the emotion; pick the strongest one or two.
- At strip reduction, tiny plewds fill in. Draw them at print scale or leave a gap from the contour.

- [ ] Read the panel with dialogue covered: is the emotion still clear?
- [ ] Check at 50% size that the drops stay open and separated.
