# Writing single-panel cartoon captions

**Goal:** a caption and image that need each other: neither is funny alone, together
they click.

## Principles
- **Interdependence**: the image sets up an incongruous situation; the caption reveals
  or twists it (McCloud's interdependent word/picture combination). If the caption
  explains the picture, it's redundant.
- **One speaker**: the caption is spoken by one character; the art must make the
  speaker obvious (open mouth, gesture, others listening).
- **Funny word last**.
- **Economy**: often under ~15-20 words; read it aloud and remove anything that
  doesn't change the joke.
- **Voice**: natural speech; the humour comes from the situation, not wordplay strain.

## Conventions
- Typeset or hand-lettered caption below the panel, in quotation marks for spoken
  lines (common magazine style), no quotes for narration.
- Captionless cartoons are fine if the image carries everything.

## Process
1. Draw (or describe) the situation.
2. Write 10+ caption variants.
3. Pick the shortest that lands; test on someone fresh.

## Verify
- [ ] Caption doesn't describe the picture.
- [ ] Speaker identifiable; punch word last.
- [ ] Shortest working version chosen.
