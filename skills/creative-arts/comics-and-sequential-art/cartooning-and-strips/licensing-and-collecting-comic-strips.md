# Licensing and collecting comic strips

## Collections
- Standard strip collection: about a year of dailies (300+ strips) plus Sundays, landscape or square
  trim; "treasury" editions combine two collections with colour Sundays.
- Curation: chronological for continuity strips; themed collections for gag strips.
- Layout: 2-3 dailies per page in a consistent grid; Sundays in colour on full pages.
- Rights: check who controls book rights. Syndicate contracts often include them (e.g. Andrews McMeel
  publishes many syndicated strips); self-published webcomics keep them and can crowdfund.
- Add value for buyers who read it free online: commentary, sketches, bonus strips.

## Character licensing
- A licensor (you or your agent/syndicate) grants a licensee rights to use characters on specific product
  categories, in specific territories, for a fixed term.
- Typical terms: royalty on wholesale price (commonly in the single to low double digits percent),
  an advance and a minimum guarantee, approval rights over designs and quality samples.
- Provide a **style guide**: approved character poses, colour specs (Pantone), logo usage, do's and
  don'ts, legal lines (copyright notice).
- Register trademarks for the strip name and characters in key categories before licensing.

## Gotchas
- Exclusive licences to one licensee across broad categories can block better deals; keep categories
  narrow.
- Without approval rights, off-model products damage the brand.
- Syndicate contracts may split merchandise income 50/50; know this before the deal.

## Verify
- [ ] Every agreement lists product categories, territory, term, royalty basis and approval process.
- [ ] Trademark filings cover the categories being licensed.
