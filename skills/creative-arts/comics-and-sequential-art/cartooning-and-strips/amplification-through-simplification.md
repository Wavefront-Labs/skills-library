# Amplification through simplification

**Goal:** a cartoon style where every line carries meaning, and readers project
themselves onto the characters.

## The idea (McCloud, Understanding Comics ch. 2)
- Cartooning strips an image down to essential meaning, **amplifying** that meaning.
- A simple face is universal: more people can see themselves in it; a detailed face is
  a specific other person.
- McCloud's "picture plane" triangle maps styles between realism, iconic abstraction
  and pure abstraction (lines as lines).

## Applying it
1. **List what must read**: emotion, identity, action. Keep only features serving those.
2. **Design from shapes**: a circle head, dot eyes, a distinctive hairline or
   silhouette shape, one costume colour.
3. **Exaggerate the expressive parts** (eyes, brows, mouth) and drop the rest
   (nostrils, cheekbones, knuckles).
4. **Consistency**: simple designs are easier to repeat identically every day; that
   consistency is part of strip craft (Brunetti emphasises drawing as a language you
   repeat).
5. **Test at thumbnail size**: identity and emotion still clear.

## Choosing the level
- Daily gag strips: highly iconic.
- Story-driven cartooning: moderately iconic characters with more realistic settings
  (see the masking effect skill).

## Verify
- [ ] Character readable and recognisable at thumbnail size.
- [ ] Every remaining detail has a purpose.
- [ ] Drawable consistently in under a few minutes.
