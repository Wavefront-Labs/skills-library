# Emanata and emotion marks

**Goal:** add instantly-readable emotional signals to simple cartoon figures.

## What emanata are
Mort Walker's The Lexicon of Comicana names the symbolic marks cartoonists draw around
characters. **Emanata** are lines and marks emanating from a character's head showing
state of mind: surprise, shock, embarrassment, dizziness.

## Common marks (Western cartoon conventions)
| Emotion/state | Mark |
|---|---|
| Surprise/shock | short radiating lines around the head |
| Anger | steam puffs from the head, jagged lines, red face (colour) |
| Pain | stars, spirals or small birds circling the head |
| Embarrassment | hatching lines on cheeks (blush) |
| Confusion | question marks, a squiggle above the head |
| Idea | light bulb |
| Sweat/nervousness | droplets flying off (Walker calls these plewds) |
| Dizziness | spirals in the eyes, swirl over the head |
Manga has its own set (the giant sweat drop, the cross-popping vein, nosebleeds);
don't mix systems without intent.

## Drawing them
- Draw with the same tool and line weight as the character; keep them simple.
- Place close to the head but not touching; short, quick strokes.
- One or two marks per panel; stacking several reads as noise.

## Verify
- [ ] Each emotion mark reads at small strip size.
- [ ] One convention system used consistently.
