# Fair comment and defamation risk for cartoonists

Not legal advice; this is the working checklist editors and cartoonists use before publication. Escalate
anything doubtful to the publication's lawyer.

## Why cartoons are usually protected
- **US:** *Hustler Magazine v. Falwell* (1988) - public figures cannot recover for emotional distress
  from a parody that no reasonable person would read as stating actual facts. Defamation of public
  figures also needs actual malice (*NYT v. Sullivan*, 1964).
- **England and Wales:** Defamation Act 2013 s.3 *honest opinion* (replaced common-law fair comment):
  the statement is opinion, indicates its basis, and an honest person could hold it on facts existing
  at the time. s.4 covers publication on a matter of public interest.
- Elsewhere (Australia, Canada, etc.) fair comment/honest opinion defences are similar but vary.

## Where cartoons get into trouble
- The image implies a **specific false fact**: showing a named person taking a bribe, committing a crime,
  having a disease. Exaggeration of a known stance is opinion; an invented event is a factual claim.
- Private individuals (not public figures) get much stronger protection.
- Misattributed quotes inside speech balloons presented as real.
- Using a photograph collage of a real person can add copyright and privacy issues.
- Some countries also have insult, blasphemy or lese-majeste laws; syndication spreads exposure.

## Pre-publication checklist
- [ ] Is the target a public figure acting in a public role?
- [ ] Would a reasonable reader see this as opinion/satire, not reportage?
- [ ] Is the factual basis true and publicly known (and could you cite it)?
- [ ] Any invented quote clearly hyperbolic or labelled?
- [ ] No private individuals, minors, or criminal allegations not already established in court?
- [ ] Editor/legal sign-off for anything touching a pending trial (contempt of court risk).

## Gotchas
- "It's just a joke" is not itself a defence; the defence is that it is recognisable opinion on true facts.
- Online republication can restart limitation periods in some jurisdictions.
