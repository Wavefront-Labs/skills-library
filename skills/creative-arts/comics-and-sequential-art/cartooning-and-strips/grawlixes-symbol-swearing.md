# Grawlixes (symbol swearing)

A **grawlix** is a string of typographical and pictorial symbols standing in for profanity, e.g.
`@#$%&!`. Mort Walker coined the term and named the parts:
- **jarns** - spirals; **quimps** - planet/Saturn symbols; **nittles** - asterisks/stars;
- **grawlixes** - the general term; skulls, lightning bolts, bombs and daggers are common too.

## Building one
1. Keep it 4-7 glyphs; a longer string reads as a longer, angrier rant.
2. Mix shapes so it doesn't read as a word: e.g. `#@!%` + a skull + a spiral.
3. Lettering: use a font with a grawlix/dingbat set (many Blambot and Comicraft fonts map symbols to
   keys) or draw the pictorial ones by hand so they match line weight.
4. Bold or larger size and a jagged balloon for shouting; small in a whisper balloon for muttering.
5. End with `!` to keep the sentence rhythm.

## When to use
- All-ages or newspaper strips where profanity is unprintable; also for comedy, where the symbols are
  funnier than the word.
- Partial grawlix: a real word with symbols replacing middle letters (`f#@!ing`) is a lettering choice,
  check your publisher's style guide.
- Some readers and translators expect consistency: decide once per series and add it to the style sheet.

## Gotchas
- Typing `@#$%` in a standard comic font may produce inconsistent weights; check every glyph exists.
- Don't put grawlixes in captions for narration; they are a speech convention.
- Ratings: some platforms still count a grawlix toward mature content when the context is explicit.

## Verify
- [ ] Glyphs share weight and baseline with the surrounding lettering.
- [ ] Balloon shape matches the volume implied.
