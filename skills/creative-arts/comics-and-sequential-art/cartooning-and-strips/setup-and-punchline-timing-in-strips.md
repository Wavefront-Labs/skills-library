# Set-up and punchline timing in strips

**Goal:** a joke that lands on the last panel, every day.

## Structure
1. **Set-up** (panel 1): establish the situation and expectation in as few words as
   possible.
2. **Development** (panel 2, optional 3): raise or twist the expectation.
3. **Beat** (optional): a silent or near-silent panel where a character pauses or
   reacts; creates timing like a comedian's pause.
4. **Punchline** (last panel): the reversal.

## Rules
- **Punchline word last**: the funny word or reveal is the final thing read (bottom
  right of the last panel). Rephrase to put it at the end.
- **Hide the punch**: readers' eyes can wander to the last panel early; keep it
  visually unremarkable until read, or put the punch in the dialogue rather than an
  eye-catching image.
- **One joke per strip**; secondary jokes dilute.
- **Economy**: cut every word that isn't needed for the set-up to work.

## Ending types
- **Verbal punchline**: the last line twists.
- **Visual reveal**: the last image recontextualises the set-up.
- **Reaction**: the last panel shows a character's reaction to the absurdity (good for
  character-driven humour).

## Testing
Read it aloud with pauses for gutters; if the joke works only when explained, rewrite.

## Verify
- [ ] Punchline word/image is the last thing read.
- [ ] Set-up under ~2 short balloons.
- [ ] One joke only.
