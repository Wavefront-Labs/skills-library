# Gag ideation and idea generation

**Goal:** a reliable supply of jokes, not waiting for inspiration.

## Habits professionals use
- **Scheduled writing time**: write gags at a fixed time, separate from drawing.
- **Idea file**: notebook/phone notes for observations, overheard lines, odd images.
- **Quantity first**: write 10-20 rough ideas to get 2-3 good ones. Kill your
  darlings at the culling stage, not during writing.

## Generators
1. **Character x situation matrix**: list your cast down one side, situations across
   (holidays, money, weather, technology, chores); fill cells with a joke.
2. **Reversal**: take a normal situation and flip roles (the dog walks the owner).
3. **Exaggeration**: push a real behaviour to absurdity.
4. **Literalism**: take an idiom literally.
5. **Wrong reaction**: a character reacts inappropriately calmly or wildly.
6. **Specificity**: real, specific details are funnier than generic ones.

## Culling
- Read ideas next day; mark the ones that still make you smile.
- Test on someone who doesn't know the strip.
- Keep rejects; some become good later with a new angle.

## Sustaining a strip
- Running characters with strong traits generate jokes from their conflicts.
- Seasonal/topical calendar planned weeks ahead (syndication deadlines run weeks
  ahead of publication).

## Verify
- [ ] Ideas produced in scheduled sessions; idea file maintained.
- [ ] Each strip chosen from several candidates.
