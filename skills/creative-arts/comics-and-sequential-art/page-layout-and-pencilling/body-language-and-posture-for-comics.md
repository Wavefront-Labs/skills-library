# Body language and posture

**Goal:** a reader can tell how a character feels from the body alone, even in a
long shot or silhouette.

## Eisner's expressive anatomy
Eisner argues the whole body acts: posture and gesture, not only the face, carry
emotion, and a "vocabulary" of recognisable poses lets readers understand instantly.

## Building blocks
- **Spine curve**: upright and arched back = confidence/pride; slumped forward =
  defeat; leaning forward = engagement/aggression; leaning back = withdrawal/ease.
- **Open vs closed**: arms open, chest exposed = open, welcoming; arms crossed,
  shoulders hunched, legs crossed = defensive, closed.
- **Weight**: weight on one leg (contrapposto) = relaxed; weight forward = intent;
  off balance = shock or fear.
- **Head**: chin up = defiance; chin down = shame or threat (with eyes up).
- **Hands**: clenched, open, hidden (pocket = concealment), touching face = anxiety.
- **Proxemics**: distance between characters shows relationship: close for intimacy or
  threat, far for estrangement. Use it across panels to show a relationship changing.

## Method
1. Draw the **line of action** (one curve) expressing the emotion first.
2. Add a stick/mannequin pose; check silhouette (fill black): does it read?
3. Then construct the figure and costume.

## Gotchas
- Stiff "action figure" poses: neutral body with an emotional face.
- Characters always facing the camera; profile and three-quarter views act better.

## Verify
- [ ] Silhouette test: emotion readable in black fill.
- [ ] Body and face express the same emotion (or a deliberate contradiction).
