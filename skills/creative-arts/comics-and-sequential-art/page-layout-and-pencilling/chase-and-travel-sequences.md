# Chase and travel sequences

**Goal:** readers feel the speed and always know who's ahead and by how much.

## Direction
- Pick a **direction of travel** and keep it. Western books: moving left-to-right
  reads as forward progress; the pursued and pursuer both travel the same way.
- A direction reversal means a turn or a change of plan; show it explicitly (a
  character turning a corner).

## Tension tools
- **Distance** between pursuer and pursued is the tension meter: show it shrinking or
  growing in wide shots.
- **Obstacles** as beats: a crowd, a fence, a closing door, traffic. Each obstacle is a
  mini set-piece with set-up, attempt, outcome.
- **Cross-cutting**: alternate pursuer and pursued panels; shorten panels as they close.

## Layout
- Wide, horizontal panels for momentum and distance.
- Narrow, quick panels for near-misses.
- Polyptych for continuous travel over one background (see
  `creative-arts.comics-and-sequential-art.page-layout-and-pencilling.polyptych-moving-figure-over-one-background`).
- Subjective motion lines for speed from the runner's view.

## Travel (non-chase)
Journeys over distance: a scene-to-scene sequence of changing landscapes, a map
panel, or a montage grid with a caption for time.

## Gotchas
- Pursuer suddenly running the other way due to an unplanned camera flip.
- Losing track of who's who in similar vehicles; vary colour/silhouette.

## Verify
- [ ] Direction of travel consistent; reversals shown.
- [ ] Distance readable at key moments.
- [ ] Each obstacle resolves.
