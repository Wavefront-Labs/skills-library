# Choice of frame: distance, angle and cropping

**Goal:** each panel's framing shows what the reader needs, from the viewpoint that
gives it meaning.

McCloud (Making Comics ch. 1) lists five choices: moment, frame, image, word, flow.
Choice of frame = where the reader stands.

## Distance
| Distance | Shows | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Extreme long / establishing | place, scale | scene openings, isolation |
| Long / full | whole bodies, action | fights, blocking |
| Medium | upper body, gestures | conversation |
| Close-up | face | emotion, turning points |
| Extreme close-up | detail | clue, intensity |

## Angle
- **Eye level**: neutral, most common.
- **High angle**: subject small/vulnerable; good for overview.
- **Low angle**: subject powerful/threatening.
- **Dutch (tilted)**: unease; sparing use.
- **POV**: puts the reader in a character's shoes.

## Cropping
- Crop to what matters; don't center everything.
- Cutting at joints (wrists, knees, neck) looks awkward; crop mid-limb instead.
- Leave room in the direction a character looks or moves (lead room).
- Leave space for balloons (top of panel in Western books).

## Through a scene
1. Open wide (orientation), move closer as tension rises, pull back for consequences.
2. Change distance or angle between consecutive panels so the eye registers a new
   panel; repeated identical framing is only for deliberate moment-to-moment effects.
3. Re-establish after a run of close-ups.

## Verify
- [ ] Each panel's key information is visible in its frame.
- [ ] Framing escalates with the emotional curve of the scene.
- [ ] No accidental repeats of identical framing.
