# Silhouette readability and shape language

**Goal:** each lead is identifiable from silhouette alone, and the dominant shapes
say who they are.

## Shape language
| Base shape | Reads as | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| Circle / soft curve | friendly, young, harmless | sidekick, comic relief |
| Square / rectangle | stable, strong, stubborn | tank, mentor, authority |
| Triangle (point up) | dynamic, aggressive | hero, villain; point down = unstable |

Mix one dominant shape with a secondary and a small accent (big-medium-small).

## Silhouette test
1. Thumbnail the cast at 1 inch tall.
2. Fill each figure solid black (digital: lock alpha and fill).
3. Line them up: can you name every character? Do they differ in height, mass,
   hair, cape, weapon or posture?
4. Fix the weak ones by changing one major shape (hair, coat flare, shoulder line),
   not by adding detail.

## In the page
- Pose characters so limbs separate from the body for key action panels.
- Distinct hair shapes matter most in crowded panels and at small print sizes.
- Silhouettes are reused in spotting blacks (silhouette panels); a strong design
  survives a pure black treatment.

## Verify
- [ ] Cast identifiable in a black-fill lineup.
- [ ] No two leads share the same height plus hair plus mass.
