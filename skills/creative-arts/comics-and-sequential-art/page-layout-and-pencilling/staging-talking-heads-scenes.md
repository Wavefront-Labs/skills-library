# Staging talking-heads scenes

**Goal:** a conversation page that's as engaging to look at as to read.

## Before drawing
1. Read the dialogue and find its **emotional beats** (where power shifts, a secret
   slips, a decision lands). Those panels get the closest framing or biggest size.
2. **Block** the scene: a top-down sketch of the room with character positions and any
   movement (someone stands, walks to the window).
3. Choose **business**: what are they doing while talking? Cooking, repairing, walking,
   packing. Business gives the art something to say and reveals character.

## Staging rules
- **180-degree line**: draw an imaginary line between the two speakers; keep the camera
  on one side so A stays left and B stays right. Cross only with a visible transition
  (a character moves, or a neutral shot on the line).
- **Speaker placement for balloons**: the first speaker in each panel on the left
  (Western), so balloons read in order without tangled tails.
- **Vary distance and angle**: establishing two-shot, then over-the-shoulder pairs,
  close-ups at beats, a pull-back for the end.
- **Acting**: expressions and gestures change across panels; no copy-pasted heads.
- **Re-establish** the space every page or so.

## Panel economy
Long conversation = more panels, not bigger balloons. Split long speeches across a
reaction panel so the listener acts.

## Gotchas
- Floating heads with no background for a whole page; keep enough setting to ground it.
- Speakers swapped sides between panels: readers lose who's talking.
- Identical framing repeated (the "photocopied head" page).

## Verify
- [ ] Blocking sketch exists; 180 line respected or crossed visibly.
- [ ] Each panel has a different distance, angle or action.
- [ ] Balloon order works with speaker positions.
