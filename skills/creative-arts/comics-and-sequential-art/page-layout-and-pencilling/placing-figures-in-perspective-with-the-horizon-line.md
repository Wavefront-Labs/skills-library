# Placing figures in perspective with the horizon line

**Goal:** figures that stand in the scene at the right size, not floating or giant.

## The horizon rule
The horizon is the camera's eye height. For figures on the same ground plane:
- If the camera is at standing eye level (~5.5 ft / 1.65 m), every standing adult's
  eyes are on the horizon, near or far.
- If the camera is lower (say waist height), the horizon cuts every standing figure at
  the waist, whatever their distance.
So: the horizon cuts all same-height figures at the **same body point**.

## Transferring a height
To place a second figure of the same height elsewhere:
1. Draw a reference figure.
2. Pick a point on the horizon (any VP). Draw lines from that VP through the reference
   figure's head and feet.
3. Slide along those lines to the new depth; draw horizontals across to the new
   location. That's the new figure's height.

## Variations
- Taller/shorter characters: adjust proportionally from the reference.
- Seated figures: transfer standing height, then fold.
- On stairs or a balcony: transfer to the base of the new level first, then apply.
- Children: shorter; their eyes fall below the horizon at adult eye level camera.

## Crowds
Sketch the ground plane grid, place feet positions, then use horizon cuts for heights.
Vary heights slightly (+/-10%) for naturalism.

## Verify
- [ ] Horizon cuts same-height figures at the same body point.
- [ ] Feet sit on the ground plane (no floaters).
