# Choice of image: rendering for clarity

**Goal:** draw each panel with the amount and kind of detail that makes its content
read instantly.

## Principles
- McCloud's choice of image: draw the panel's contents so they're recognisable and
  consistent: characters, objects, and environments the reader must identify.
- **Detail hierarchy**: the focal subject gets the most detail/contrast; secondary
  elements less; background least (or none) in action panels.
- **Consistency**: characters recognisable panel to panel (silhouette, hair, costume
  colour) even when simplified at a distance.

## Practical moves
1. Decide the panel's single job; render what serves it.
2. Drop backgrounds in fast action or emotional close-ups; replace with a tone,
   speed lines or colour.
3. Re-establish backgrounds after several empty panels so the reader keeps place.
4. Simplify distant figures to silhouettes or shapes; don't render buttons at 2 cm
   tall.
5. Check the **thumbnail read**: shrink the page to phone size; is the subject still
   clear?

## Gotchas
- Equal detail everywhere flattens the page: the eye has nowhere to go.
- Over-rendered crowds hide the hero.
- Style shifts mid-book (more detail on later pages as the artist improves) break
  consistency; adjust early pages or keep a style guide.

## Verify
- [ ] Each panel's focal subject has the highest detail/contrast.
- [ ] Page reads at thumbnail size.
- [ ] Characters recognisable in every panel.
