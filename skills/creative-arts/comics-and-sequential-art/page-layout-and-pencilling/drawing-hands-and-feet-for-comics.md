# Drawing hands and feet for comics

**Goal:** hands and feet that read clearly, support the acting, and do not eat
the page's time budget.

## Hands
1. Palm as a slightly wedged box, about the length of the face (chin to hairline).
2. Thumb from the side of the palm, mounted on its own pad; it opposes, it does
   not sit in line with the fingers.
3. Knuckles form an arc, not a straight line; the middle finger is longest.
4. Fingers in three segments shrinking roughly 1 : 0.75 : 0.6; draw them as a
   group (mitten) first, separate only the ones that act.
5. Gesture first: the hand's line of action from wrist to fingertip.

## Feet
- Wedge: high on the inside (arch), flat on the outside; the ankle bone is higher
  on the inside.
- In shoes, draw the foot then the shoe around it; the shoe's toe box lifts.
- Feet anchor figures: a floating foot reads as weightless. Put cast shadows under
  feet on the ground plane.

## Comics specifics
- Keep a hand library: 20 drawings or photos of your own hands in common acting
  poses (pointing, fist, open plea, holding cup, gun grip).
- Eisner: hands are the second face; feature them in close-ups for emotion.
- Hands are one-third larger in heroic styles; feet stay grounded and slightly big.
- Hiding hands in pockets or behind props every panel is visible avoidance.

## Verify
- [ ] Thumb attaches to the side of the palm.
- [ ] Hands agree with the expression in the same panel.
