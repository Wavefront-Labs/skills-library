# Subject-to-subject transitions

**Goal:** jump between subjects in the same scene and have the reader instantly know
how they relate.

## What it is
McCloud: transitions staying within a scene or idea but moving between subjects
(a gunshot panel, then a bird flying off; a speaker, then the listener's reaction).
The reader must do more closure than action-to-action.

## Common patterns
- **Cause -> effect**: the punch thrown / the vase shattering across the room.
- **Action -> reaction**: the reveal / a face reacting.
- **Speaker -> listener** in dialogue: alternate to show response.
- **Detail cut**: the conversation / a close-up of the shaking hand under the table.

## Making the link readable
1. **Eyelines**: the subject in panel 1 looks toward where panel 2's subject is.
2. **Consistent spatial layout**: keep characters on their established sides (180
   rule); a reaction shot from the wrong side reads as a different scene.
3. **Shared cue**: sound (SFX trailing into the next panel), light, a prop visible in
   both.
4. **Establish first**: show both subjects together once before cutting between them.

## Gotchas
- Cutting to a subject never established reads as a scene change.
- Long chains of cutaways in action lose geography; return to a wider shot every few
  panels.

## Verify
- [ ] Every subject cut has an eyeline, prop, or sound cue linking it.
- [ ] Geography re-established at least every 4-6 panels in complex scenes.
