# Borderless, bleed and inset panels

**Goal:** break the standard frame for effect while keeping reading order and print
safety.

## Borderless (open) panels
- No border; the image sits in the gutter space, often with a vignetted edge.
- Effect: openness, timelessness, memory, calm (McCloud notes bleeds/borderless panels
  make time feel unbounded).
- Keep the image shape readable: anchor it with a figure or ground plane so it
  doesn't float.

## Bleed panels
- Art runs off the page edge past the trim into the bleed (commonly 0.125 in / 3 mm).
- Draw to the **bleed line**, keep critical content inside the **safe/live area**
  (often 0.25-0.5 in inside trim; check printer).
- A bleed panel on one edge pulls the eye outward and enlarges the moment.

## Inset panels
- A small panel placed on top of a larger panel or splash.
- Uses: a detail, a reaction, a simultaneous moment, a close-up of the clue.
- Placement: where the reading order wants it (often after reading the big panel's
  focal point; bottom-right for a final beat). Never over a face or the key action.
- Give insets a clear border, often with a thin white outline (keyline) so they
  separate from the art below.

## Gotchas
- Too many borderless panels: page loses structure; reading order wanders.
- Bleed art with text near trim: captions get cut. Keep text in the safe area.

## Verify
- [ ] Borderless use is deliberate and anchored.
- [ ] Bleed art reaches the bleed line; critical content inside safe area.
- [ ] Insets read in correct order and cover nothing important.
