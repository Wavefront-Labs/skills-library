# Drawing impact panels

**Goal:** the reader feels the hit.

## Ingredients
1. **Contact point as focal point**: everything leads to it (lines, contrast, burst).
2. **Foreshortening**: the fist or weapon comes toward the camera, enlarged; the
   victim's head snaps away.
3. **Deformation and recoil**: the target's body bends, hair and clothes whip
   opposite to the force, spit/sweat/debris flies.
4. **Impact burst**: a starburst or radial lines centred on contact; or a flash of
   white/black shape behind.
5. **SFX**: placed near the contact, shaped to the force (coordinate with letterer).
6. **Panel**: larger than surrounding panels, possibly breaking the border or angled.

## Timing
- Precede the impact with anticipation (wind-up) in a smaller panel; the contrast
  sells the impact.
- Follow with a reaction/aftermath panel so the result is clear.
- Optional "freeze": a moment-to-moment close-up just before contact.

## Variations
- Silent impact: no SFX, white background, stillness. Shock rather than spectacle.
- Off-panel impact: show reactions of onlookers and the SFX only; leaves violence to
  imagination.

## Gotchas
- Impact drawn after contact (victim already falling) = loses punch.
- Burst lines cluttering the face or contact point.
- Every hit drawn as an impact panel: escalation dies.

## Verify
- [ ] Contact point is the clearest part of the panel.
- [ ] Anticipation and reaction panels bracket the impact.
