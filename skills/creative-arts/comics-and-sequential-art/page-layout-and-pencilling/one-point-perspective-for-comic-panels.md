# One-point perspective for comic panels

**Goal:** accurate frontal views of rooms, streets and corridors, with the vanishing
point working for the composition.

## When
The camera faces a flat surface head-on (a wall, a street end, a corridor). Lines
parallel to the picture plane stay horizontal/vertical; lines going into depth
converge on one vanishing point (VP) on the horizon.

## Construction
1. Draw the **horizon line** at the camera's eye level.
2. Place the **VP** on it, ideally at or near the focal subject.
3. Draw the front face of the room/box as a rectangle (true horizontals and verticals).
4. Connect its corners to the VP: these are the receding edges.
5. Cut the back wall with a smaller rectangle parallel to the front.
6. **Equal spacing in depth** (floor tiles, doors, windows): draw a diagonal from one
   corner through the midpoint of a vertical edge, or use a measuring point; each
   intersection marks the next equal unit as they shrink.
7. Figures: anyone standing on the same floor with eyes at camera height has their eyes
   on the horizon.

## Composition use
- Converging lines lead the eye to the VP; put the character there for emphasis.
- A centred VP feels symmetrical, formal, confrontational; off-centre feels more
  natural.

## Digital
CSP perspective ruler (1-point) or a grid layer; snap lines to it (see
`creative-arts.comics-and-sequential-art.clip-studio-paint.perspective-ruler-setup-and-snapping`
or the CSP user guide Rulers chapter).

## Gotchas
- VP off the horizon line: everything looks tilted.
- Objects not parallel to the walls (a turned chair) need their own VPs on the
  horizon (they're in two-point).

## Verify
- [ ] All receding lines hit the single VP.
- [ ] Standing adults' eyes cut by the horizon (eye-level camera).
