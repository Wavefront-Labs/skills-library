# Gesture and line of action

**Goal:** figures that read in silhouette at thumbnail size and carry the panel's
action.

## Method
1. Draw the line of action first: one C, S or straight curve through head to the
   weight-bearing foot. C = effort/compression, S = grace, straight = impact/thrust.
2. Lay the gesture in 10-30 s: head oval, spine along the line, shoulder and hip
   lines tilted opposite each other (contrapposto), limbs as single strokes.
3. Push it: exaggerate the curve 20-30 percent beyond reference; inking and
   construction will straighten it back.
4. Only then add construction (see mannequin skill).

## Rules that keep gestures readable
- Weight: plumb line from the pit of the neck must fall inside the base of support
  for a standing figure; outside it means falling or moving.
- Straights against curves: one side of a limb straight, the opposite curved.
- Avoid parallel limbs and twinning (both arms doing the same thing).
- In action panels the line of action should agree with the panel's reading
  direction (left to right for forward motion in Western books).

## Practice routine
- 50 thirty-second gestures from line-of-life.com or Croquis Cafe, daily warm-up.
- Then 5 gestures from memory per scripted panel before choosing one.

## Verify
- [ ] Filled-in silhouette still communicates the action.
- [ ] Line of action visible in the finished pencils, not lost in rendering.
