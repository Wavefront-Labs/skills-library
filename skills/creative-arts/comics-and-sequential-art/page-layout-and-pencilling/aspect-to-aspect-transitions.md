# Aspect-to-aspect transitions

**Goal:** suspend plot time and let the reader inhabit a place or mood.

## What it is
McCloud: panels showing different aspects of a place, idea or mood, bypassing time; a
"wandering eye" over a scene. He found it far more common in Japanese manga than in
mid-century Western comics, though Western use has grown.

## When to use
- Opening a location: rain on the window, a ticking clock, steam from a cup, an empty
  chair, before the character speaks.
- After a climax: aftermath details instead of a single wide shot.
- Seasonal or emotional atmosphere: cherry blossoms, crowds, a cat asleep.

## How to compose
1. Choose 3-6 details that share a sense (sound, temperature, emotion).
2. Vary scale: one wide, then close details.
3. Keep the colour and lighting consistent so they read as the same moment.
4. Little or no dialogue; a single caption or SFX at most.
5. Arrange panels in a calm grid or as small insets on a larger background panel.

## Pacing cost
Each aspect panel buys atmosphere at the cost of plot momentum. In a 22-page issue,
a 3-5 panel aspect sequence is plenty. In vertical-scroll webcomics, aspect panels with
generous spacing are cheap and effective.

## Gotchas
- Random details that don't share a mood read as filler.
- Using it mid-action deflates tension unless the stillness is the point.

## Verify
- [ ] Each aspect panel contributes to one shared mood.
- [ ] Sequence length proportionate to the story beat.
