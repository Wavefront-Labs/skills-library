# Focal point and leading lines

**Goal:** within a fraction of a second, the reader's eye lands on what the panel is
about.

## Tools for a focal point
1. **Contrast**: the strongest value contrast in the panel at the subject (dark
   against light or vice versa).
2. **Isolation**: empty space around the subject.
3. **Detail**: the most rendering at the subject.
4. **Leading lines**: perspective lines, limbs, gazes, architecture, speed lines
   converging on the subject.
5. **Placement**: rule-of-thirds intersections or deliberate centring for
   confrontation/symmetry.
6. **Faces and eyes** attract attention automatically; use that or keep extra faces
   small.

## Leading lines in practice
- Put the vanishing point at or near the subject; all receding lines point there.
- Character gazes form lines; multiple characters looking at the subject focus it.
- Focus lines (radial) for emphasis; standard manga technique.

## Competing elements
- Only one focal point per panel; a secondary point can lead to the next panel.
- Balloons are strong attractors: keep them near, not on, the focal point, and
  ordered to finish where the eye should exit.

## Gotchas
- Bright colour on a background object (colourist issue) stealing focus; flag it in
  colour notes.
- Busy textures equal everywhere = no focal point.

## Verify
- [ ] Squint test: the subject is the highest-contrast area.
- [ ] Leading lines converge on the subject.
- [ ] One clear focal point per panel.
