# Three-point perspective for dramatic shots

**Goal:** extreme up/down views with believable convergence: the superhero skyline
shot, the rooftop fall.

## When
The camera tilts up or down, so verticals also converge: to a third VP above (looking
up, worm's-eye) or below (looking down, bird's-eye).

## Construction
1. Horizon and two VPs as in two-point (often both far off-panel).
2. Third VP: directly above or below the centre of interest, far off the panel for a
   mild tilt, closer for a dramatic one.
3. The three VPs form a triangle; the **centre of vision** sits inside it. Keep the
   subject near that centre to minimise distortion.
4. Verticals all aim to the third VP; horizontals to the side VPs.
5. Figures: their vertical axis also points to the third VP; a figure seen from above
   foreshortens (big head, small feet).

## Choosing the strength
- Third VP very far = subtle, realistic.
- Third VP close = exaggerated, fish-eye-like drama; use for peak moments.

## Tools
CSP 3-point perspective ruler, or 3D models with camera angles; traditional with a
large board and string.

## Gotchas
- Mixing three-point backgrounds with eye-level figures (figures look pasted in).
- Placing the subject at the triangle edge where distortion is extreme.

## Verify
- [ ] Verticals converge to one third VP; figures obey it too.
- [ ] Subject near the centre of vision.
