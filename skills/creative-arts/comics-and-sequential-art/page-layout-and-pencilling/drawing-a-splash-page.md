# Drawing a splash page

**Goal:** a full-page image that stops the reader, reads instantly, and still works as
part of the sequence.

## Plan
1. Identify the **one thing** the splash is about (scale, reveal, impact, mood).
2. Thumbnail 3-6 compositions at small size; pick the clearest at phone size.
3. Reserve **text zones** before drawing: title/logo, credits, 1-2 captions. Usually
   top-left (first read) for captions; credits often bottom.
4. Decide bleed: full bleed (art to the trim, extending into bleed) or framed with a
   border.

## Compose
- **Dominant focal point** with strongest contrast; supporting elements lead to it.
- Value plan: a simple 3-value sketch (black, mid, white) to check the read.
- Big shapes first: a splash fails on unclear big shapes, not on missing detail.
- Exit path: the eye should finish near the bottom-right, toward the page turn
  (Western books).
- Keep important content inside the **safe area** (well inside trim); nothing critical
  in the binding side.

## Detail
A splash invites detail, but detail must stay subordinate to the focal point. Add
texture in mid-ground and background; keep the subject's silhouette clean.

## Gotchas
- Splash with no sense of scale: include a figure or familiar object.
- Busy splash with title dropped on top later covering key art.
- Splash on an odd page undermining a planned page-turn reveal (it should usually be
  on the even page after the set-up).

## Verify
- [ ] Reads at phone size; single focal point.
- [ ] Text zones clear; safe area respected.
- [ ] Placement on the correct page side for the reveal.
