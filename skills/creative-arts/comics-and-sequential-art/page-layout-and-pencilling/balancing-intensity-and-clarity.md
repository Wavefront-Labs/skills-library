# Balancing intensity and clarity

**Goal:** pages that excite without confusing. McCloud (Making Comics ch. 1) frames
this as the tension between clarity and intensity: intensity draws the eye; clarity
communicates.

## Intensity tools
Extreme angles, foreshortening, heavy blacks, diagonals, broken borders, large panels,
dramatic lighting, speed lines, figures bursting out of frames.

## Clarity tools
Eye-level shots, establishing panels, consistent screen direction, regular grids,
clean silhouettes, restrained backgrounds, clear reading order.

## Spending intensity
1. Rank the page's beats by importance. Spend intensity on the top 1-2, keep the rest
   clear and quieter.
2. Contrast makes intensity work: a dynamic panel pops only next to calm ones.
3. In fights, alternate: a clear establishing beat, then an intense impact, then a
   clear consequence.
4. Emotional climaxes may need stillness rather than dynamics: intensity isn't only
   action.

## Failure checks
- **Thumbnail test**: at phone size, can you still tell who does what?
- **Silhouette test**: fill the main figures in black; are poses readable?
- **Reader test**: can someone unfamiliar describe the sequence?

## Gotchas
- Every panel tilted and broken = visual noise; nothing stands out.
- Hyper-clear pages with no hierarchy feel flat; even quiet books need one moment
  of emphasis per page or spread.

## Verify
- [ ] Intensity concentrated on the page's key beats.
- [ ] Thumbnail and silhouette tests pass.
