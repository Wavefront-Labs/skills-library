# Thumbnailing and panel composition for clear storytelling

**Goal:** turn a script page into a layout a reader follows without effort, then pencil it
so the inker and letterer can finish it.

## 1. Thumbnail every page first
- Draw tiny (a few cm tall) page roughs in the correct **proportion** of the final page.
  Work fast, many versions; at this size you judge flow and balance, not drawing.
- Thumbnail the **whole sequence** (or at least each spread) together; facing pages are
  seen as one image.
- Mark balloons as rough ovals with the script's balloon numbers. If they don't fit,
  fix it now, not after inking.

## 2. The reading path
- Western comics read left to right, top to bottom (a "Z" path); manga reads right to
  left (see creative-arts.comics-and-sequential-art.manga.name-storyboarding-tones-and-right-to-left-flow).
- Panel order must be obvious. Avoid layouts where a reader can't tell whether to go
  across or down — a staggered gutter or a panel that spans tiers clarifies it.
- Within a panel, the first speaker is placed on the left (in Western reading) so balloon
  order matches characters' positions.
- Gutters (gaps between panels) are consistent unless you're signalling something.

## 3. Shot choice and camera
- **Establishing shot** when entering a new location or after a time jump.
- **Vary distance**: wide, medium, close-up, extreme close-up. Three identical mediums in a
  row reads as dead.
- **180-degree rule:** keep characters on the same side of the frame across a conversation
  so the reader doesn't lose who's where. Cross the line only on purpose with a neutral shot.
- **Camera angle** for meaning: low angle = power, high angle = vulnerability.
- Direct the eye: action and gaze lead toward the next panel (rightward in Western comics).

## 4. Composition inside the panel
- One clear focal point per panel (the script's "moment").
- Silhouette test: could the action read as a black shape? If not, re-pose.
- Leave **dead space for balloons** at the top, usually the top-left of the panel in
  Western comics. Don't put faces or key action where balloons must go.
- Overlapping planes (foreground, middle, background) give depth.

## 5. Page geometry for print
For any book, get the printer's/publisher's template. Know three boxes:
- **Trim:** where the page will be cut.
- **Bleed:** art extending past the trim (often ~3 mm / 0.125 in) so no white edge appears.
- **Safe/live area:** inside the trim; keep all text and critical art inside it.
A common US comic original is drawn at roughly 11 × 17 in board with a ~10 × 15 in live
area (scaled down for print). *Templates vary between publishers; always use theirs.*

## 6. Pencils
- Work from loose layouts (blue/red pencil or a light digital layer) to tighter pencils.
- Define what the inker needs: clear line choices, indicated blacks (mark with "X"),
  textures noted, perspective resolved.
- Use reference (photos, 3D models) for anatomy, vehicles and places; keep it consistent.
- Draw panel borders cleanly or leave them for the inker as agreed.

## Gotchas
- Drawing beautiful panels with no room for the 40-word balloon in the script.
- Repeating the same shot distance for a whole page.
- Losing track of a character's position across a conversation.
- Critical art past the trim line.
- Spending hours on panel 1 before the page layout works.

## Verify
- [ ] Every page was thumbnailed with balloons indicated before pencils.
- [ ] A fresh reader follows panel order without hesitation.
- [ ] Shot distances vary; characters hold screen positions in conversations.
- [ ] Text and key art sit inside the safe area; bleed art reaches past trim.
- [ ] Blacks indicated and perspective resolved for the inker.
