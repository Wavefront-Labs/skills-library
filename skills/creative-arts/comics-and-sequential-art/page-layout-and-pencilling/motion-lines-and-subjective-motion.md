# Motion lines and subjective motion

**Goal:** show movement clearly and with the right point of view.

## The options (McCloud ch. 4)
1. **Trailing motion lines** ("zip ribbons"): lines behind the moving object, tracing
   its path. The camera is still, the object moves. Classic Western approach.
2. **Subjective motion**: the background becomes parallel streaks while the subject
   stays sharp; the camera moves with the subject, so the reader feels they move too.
   Common in manga, now everywhere.
3. **Multiple images**: the object drawn several times along its path (stroboscopic),
   or fading ghost images.
4. **Photographic blur**: smeared shapes (digital motion blur); works in painted or
   coloured styles, weak in pure line art.

## Drawing trailing lines well
- Lines follow the path (arcs for swings, not straight lines for everything).
- Start them a little away from the object; taper toward the past; don't connect
  them to the figure's outline.
- Fewer, confident lines beat many scratchy ones; draw with a ruler/ellipse guide or
  vector tool for clean results.

## Subjective motion
- Draw streaks parallel to the direction of travel, converging toward a vanishing
  point if moving toward/away from the viewer.
- Keep the subject fully rendered and cleanly separated (white halo or contrast).
- In Clip Studio Paint, the Speed line / Stream line tools or a parallel line ruler
  produce consistent streaks (see the special ruler skill in the clip-studio-paint product).

## Gotchas
- Motion lines covering faces or key action.
- Mixing trailing and subjective motion in one panel confuses camera viewpoint.
- Overusing speed lines makes every panel feel equally frantic.

## Verify
- [ ] The direction of movement is unambiguous.
- [ ] The motion style matches the intended camera (still vs tracking).
