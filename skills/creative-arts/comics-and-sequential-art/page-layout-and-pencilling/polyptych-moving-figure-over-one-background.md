# Polyptych: moving figure over one background

**Goal:** a sequence where a single continuous image is divided into panels and a
figure appears in each at successive moments, so time flows across space.

## When it works
- Travel across a landscape (a rider crossing a plain).
- A fall or leap shown in stages down a tall building.
- A walk-and-talk where the conversation advances as characters move.
- A long action in one location (a pickpocket working through a crowd).

## Construction
1. **Draw the background as one image** across the whole tier or page area first
   (traditional: one sheet; digital: one layer under the frames).
2. Plan figure positions: evenly spaced for steady motion, bunching for slowing down,
   widening for acceleration.
3. **Place panel borders** so each panel contains one figure instance; borders sit on
   areas without crucial background detail.
4. Keep perspective and horizon continuous; a polyptych breaks if the background
   doesn't line up across gutters.
5. Digital: put the background on a layer outside the frame folder or copy it into
   each frame folder unchanged; CSP frame border folders mask each panel automatically.

## Variants
- Vertical polyptych for falls.
- Full-page polyptych with the figure appearing 4-6 times on a winding path.
- Webtoon: a tall continuous background with figure instances spaced by scroll.

## Gotchas
- Gutters must not cut through the figure.
- Reading order: the figure's path must follow the reading direction (left to right,
  top to bottom in Western books; right to left in manga).

## Verify
- [ ] Background aligns across every gutter.
- [ ] Figure path follows reading order; spacing matches intended speed.
