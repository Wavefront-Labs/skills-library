# Heroic and realistic figure proportions

**Goal:** every character keeps the same proportions on every page, and the canon
chosen matches the book's tone.

## Choose the canon
| Canon | Heads tall | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Realistic adult | 7 - 7.5 | crime, slice of life, European BD |
| Idealised | 8 | most mainstream adventure |
| Heroic | 8.5 - 9 | superhero leads (Marvel Way builds on 8.75) |
| Cartoon / youth | 3 - 6 | humour, all-ages, children |

Decide per character, not per book: a heroic lead next to a 7.5-head civilian reads
as larger than life without anyone being drawn "wrong".

## Landmarks at 8 heads (Loomis)
1 chin, 2 nipples, 3 navel, 4 crotch (midpoint), 5 mid-thigh, 6 below knee,
7 mid-shin, 8 soles. Shoulders about 2 head-widths for men (2.5-3 heroic), hips
about 1.5-2 for women. Elbows at the waist, wrists at the crotch, fingertips at
mid-thigh.

## Keep it consistent
- Draw a proportion strip for each lead (front view, horizontal head-height lines)
  and pin it beside the board or keep it on a reference layer.
- In foreshortened shots measure in heads along the limb's direction, not the page.
- Heroic exaggeration goes to chest, shoulders, legs below the knee; heads stay
  slightly smaller, hands and feet slightly larger.

## Failure modes
- Canon drift: leads shrink to 7 heads by page 20 when rushed. Check with the strip.
- Heroic legs on a short torso (crotch above midpoint) reads as a child.
- Women drawn at 9 heads beside men at 8: unintentional height difference.

## Verify
- [ ] Each lead has a proportion strip; spot-check 3 full figures per issue.
- [ ] Crotch sits at the vertical midpoint for adult figures.
