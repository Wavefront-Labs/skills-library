# Scene-to-scene transitions

**Goal:** the reader knows a scene has changed, and where/when they are now, without
confusion.

## Signals (use at least one, usually two)
1. **Break position**: start new scenes at the top of a page or a new tier. A scene
   change mid-tier is the hardest to read.
2. **Establishing shot** of the new location.
3. **Locator caption**: "Harbour Police HQ. Monday, 6:40 a.m."
4. **Colour shift**: new palette/time of day (coordinate with the colourist).
5. **Match cut**: a shape or action repeats across the cut (a cup set down / a gavel
   striking) to link scenes thematically.
6. **Dialogue bridge**: a line in scene A is answered by a line in scene B.
7. **Gutter change**: a wider gutter or a panel border style change (sparingly).

## Time jumps
- Big jumps need explicit captions or visual markers (aging, seasons).
- Flashbacks: consistent device (panel border style, desaturated colour, caption
  colour) used the same way every time.

## Gotchas
- Two scenes with similar locations and lighting blur; vary angle, colour, or caption.
- Locator captions on every scene of a single-location story are noise.
- Match cuts that are too clever confuse more than they connect.

## Verify
- [ ] Every scene change has at least two signals or a page break plus one.
- [ ] Flashback device consistent across the book.
