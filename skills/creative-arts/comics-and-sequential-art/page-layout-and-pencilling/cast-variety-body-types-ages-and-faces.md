# Cast variety: body types, ages and faces

**Goal:** a cast you can tell apart without colour or costume, which also looks like
a real population.

## Body types
- Vary height (plot each lead on a height chart), mass and where it sits (chest,
  belly, hips, legs).
- Use shape language per character, not one heroic template with different hair.
- Background extras: deliberately include short, heavy, thin, elderly, children,
  disabled people.

## Ages
- Children: larger head relative to body (4-6 heads), eyes lower on the face, short
  nose, rounder jaw.
- Elderly: features drop, ears and nose lengthen, neck forward, posture curves,
  joints prominent, less hair volume at the crown.

## Faces
- Change structure, not just accessories: brow height, eye spacing, nose bridge,
  jaw width, lip fullness, cheekbones.
- Draw relatives to share 1-2 traits deliberately.
- Avoid caricatured ethnic shorthand; use reference of real people and draw
  individuals.

## Process
1. Line up all leads' heads in profile and front: no two identical silhouettes.
2. Lineup test at thumbnail size, colour off.
3. Note each character's 2 signature facial traits on the model sheet.

## Verify
- [ ] Cast identifiable in greyscale headshot lineup.
- [ ] Crowd shots include at least three body types and age groups.
