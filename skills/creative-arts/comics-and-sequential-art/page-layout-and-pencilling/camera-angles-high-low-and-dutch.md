# Camera angles: high, low and Dutch

**Goal:** choose angles for meaning and draw them correctly in perspective.

## Angle = horizon line placement
The horizon line is the viewer's eye level.
| Angle | Horizon | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Eye level | through characters' eyes | neutral, conversational |
| High angle | above the subject (often near or above the panel top) | subject smaller, vulnerable; shows layout |
| Bird's-eye | far above, often off-panel; third VP below | overview, detachment, maps the scene |
| Low angle | below the subject's waist or near panel bottom | power, threat, heroism |
| Worm's-eye | at ground, third VP above | monumental, overwhelming |
| Dutch (tilted) | tilted horizon | unease, disorientation |

## Drawing them
1. Draw the horizon first; all figures standing on the same ground plane have their
   eye line cut by the horizon at the same body height if they're the same height
   (eye-level camera = horizon through every standing adult's head).
2. Extreme high/low angles need three-point perspective: the third vanishing point
   below (looking down) or above (looking up).
3. **Dutch angle**: draw the scene normally, then rotate the frame (not the figures
   alone). Digitally, draw on a rotated canvas or rotate the frame border folder.

## Choosing
- Use eye level as the default; angle shifts then register.
- Low angle for a character gaining power; high angle as they lose it.
- Dutch angle sparingly: madness, chaos, wrongness. Not every action panel.

## Gotchas
- Figures "floating" because their feet don't sit on the ground plane implied by the
  horizon.
- Low-angle faces: nostrils and chin underside show; draw them, or the angle reads false.

## Verify
- [ ] Horizon placed deliberately in every panel with an angle choice.
- [ ] Figures share the ground plane; three-point used for extremes.
