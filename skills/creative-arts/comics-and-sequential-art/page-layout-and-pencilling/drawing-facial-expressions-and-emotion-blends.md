# Facial expressions and emotion blends

**Goal:** faces that communicate exact emotions at a glance, including subtle mixes.

## Basic emotions (McCloud, Making Comics ch. 2, drawing on facial-expression research)
McCloud uses six primaries: anger, disgust, fear, joy, sadness, surprise.
| Emotion | Brows | Eyes | Mouth |
|---|---|---|---|
| Anger | down and together, V shape | narrowed, intense | tight lips or bared teeth, square |
| Disgust | lowered | narrowed | upper lip raised, nose wrinkled |
| Fear | raised and drawn together | wide, whites showing | stretched horizontally, open |
| Joy | relaxed or slightly raised | crinkled at outer corners (cheeks up) | corners up |
| Sadness | inner ends raised (slanted up to centre) | lids lowered, looking down | corners down, lower lip may push up |
| Surprise | high and curved | wide, round | dropped jaw, oval |

## Blends
Combine features from two primaries for nuanced states, e.g. joy + fear (nervous
smile), anger + joy (cruel glee), sadness + joy (bittersweet), surprise + joy (delight).
Keep each feature clearly one emotion's version so the mix reads.

## Intensity
Three levels per emotion (mild, moderate, extreme): mild changes brows/mouth slightly;
extreme involves the whole face and body.

## Practice tools
- **Expression sheet** per main character: 12-20 heads with varied emotions, kept with
  the model sheet so the character's face stays consistent.
- Mirror and photo reference of yourself.

## Gotchas
- Same neutral face with only mouth changes: the brows do most of the work.
- Eyes alone can't carry an emotion at small sizes; exaggerate brows and mouth.
- Joy without cheek lift reads as a fake smile (sometimes useful deliberately).

- [ ] Each emotion recognisable with the dialogue covered.
