# Foreground, midground and background depth

**Goal:** panels that feel three-dimensional and direct attention to the subject.

## Three planes
- **Foreground (FG)**: closest elements, often cropped by the panel edge (a shoulder,
  a railing, a silhouette). Frames the scene and puts the reader "in" it.
- **Midground (MG)**: usually the subject and the action.
- **Background (BG)**: setting, sky, distance.

## Depth cues to stack
1. **Overlap**: FG objects overlap MG overlap BG. The strongest cue.
2. **Scale**: same-size objects get smaller with distance.
3. **Line weight** (for inking): heavier in FG, lighter in BG.
4. **Value / detail**: high contrast and detail in FG/MG, softer and lighter in BG
   (atmospheric perspective). Or a dark silhouetted FG against lit MG.
5. **Placement**: lower in the frame = closer (with eye-level camera).

## Composition tips
- A FG framing element (doorway, over-shoulder) instantly sets the viewer's position.
- Silhouette the FG in solid black to push the subject back and frame it.
- Don't let FG elements tangent (touch) MG outlines: overlap clearly or separate.
- Leave BG simpler behind the subject's head for balloon space and readability.

## Gotchas
- Everything in one plane = flat, stage-like panels.
- Over-detailed FG competing with the subject.
- Tangents (edges just touching) flatten depth and create visual confusion.

## Verify
- [ ] At least two planes in most panels; subject in the clearest plane.
- [ ] No tangents between planes.
- [ ] Value/line weight supports depth order.
