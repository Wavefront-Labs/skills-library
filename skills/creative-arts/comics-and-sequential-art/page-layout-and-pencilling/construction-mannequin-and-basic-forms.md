# Construction mannequin and basic forms

**Goal:** a figure that can rotate to any camera angle with consistent volume.

## The mannequin
- Head: sphere with sides sliced off (Loomis ball), plus jaw plane; brow line and
  centre line wrap the form.
- Ribcage: egg or box tilted back; pelvis: box or bowl. Torso = two masses joined
  by a flexible spine; never one rigid tube.
- Limbs: tapered cylinders; joints as spheres (shoulder, elbow, wrist, hip, knee,
  ankle). Hands as mitten boxes, feet as wedges.
- Draw through: show the far side of each form lightly so overlaps are correct.

## Turning and foreshortening
- Cross-contour lines (centre line, belt line, chest line) show direction of turn.
- Foreshortened limbs: overlapping cylinders, near end larger; ellipses at joints
  get rounder as the limb points at the viewer.
- Use boxes for the torso when perspective is extreme; boxes take vanishing points.

## Workflow on the page
1. Gesture (line of action).
2. Mannequin over it in light blue or a low-opacity layer.
3. Anatomy and costume on top; erase or hide construction before inks.

## Failure modes
- Construction kills the gesture: keep the line of action layer visible.
- Flat ellipses at the waist when the camera is high or low.
- Arms attached to the ribcage instead of the shoulder girdle (no clavicle swing).

## Verify
- [ ] Every figure in a page has visible construction at layout stage.
- [ ] Ellipse degrees agree with the panel's eye level.
