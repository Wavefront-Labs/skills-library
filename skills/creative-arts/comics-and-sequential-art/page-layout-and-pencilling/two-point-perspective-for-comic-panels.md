# Two-point perspective for comic panels

**Goal:** convincing corner views of buildings, rooms and objects, consistent across a
sequence.

## When
The camera looks at a corner: two sets of horizontal edges recede to two vanishing
points (VPs) on the horizon; verticals stay vertical.

## Construction
1. Horizon at camera eye level.
2. **Place VPs far apart**, usually outside the panel (even off the page). VPs too close
   together cause the distorted, stretched corners amateurs produce. A rule of thumb:
   VPs at least about 2-3 panel widths apart for a normal lens feel.
3. Draw the nearest vertical edge (the corner).
4. Lines from the top and bottom of that edge to each VP define the two faces.
5. Add verticals to close the faces; windows/doors along the converging lines.
6. Equal spacing: use diagonals of rectangles or measuring points, as in one-point.

## Off-panel VPs in practice
- Traditional: tape the page to a larger board and mark VPs on the board; use a long
  ruler, or a perspective "vanishing point" pin with a string.
- Digital: CSP 2-point perspective ruler can place VPs off-canvas; snap strokes.

## Consistency across panels
- Draw a rough plan view of the location; use it to place cameras and keep the same
  architecture across shots.
- For repeated locations, build a simple 3D model (CSP 3D, SketchUp, Blender) and
  pose cameras; trace/adjust (see the 3D reference skills in clip-studio-paint).

## Gotchas
- Two-point objects whose VPs don't share the horizon line.
- Rooms where the wall corner angle is wildly wider than 90 degrees because VPs are
  too close.

## Verify
- [ ] VPs on the same horizon and far enough apart.
- [ ] Verticals truly vertical (unless intentionally three-point).
