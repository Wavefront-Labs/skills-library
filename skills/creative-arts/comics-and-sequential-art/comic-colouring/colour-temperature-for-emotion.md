# Colour temperature for emotion

**Goal:** a scene's warm/cool balance tells the reader how to feel before they read
a word.

## Associations (use, then subvert deliberately)
- Warm (reds, oranges, yellows): intimacy, anger, danger, energy, nostalgia.
- Cool (blues, greens, violets): calm, isolation, night, sadness, clinical spaces.
- Temperature is relative: a cool scene with one warm element puts focus there.

## Method
1. Before colouring, write a one-line mood per scene from the script.
2. Pick a dominant temperature and an accent temperature for each scene; make
   colour scripts (tiny thumbnails of each page in its key colours).
3. Shift temperature at turning points: an argument warms then cools after.
4. Light colour sets the temperature of everything (warm key light, cool shadows,
   or the reverse). Shadows take the complementary temperature of the light.

## Skin and continuity
- Characters' local colours shift with the light, but keep hue relationships so a
  blonde stays blonde under blue light.
- Use multiply/overlay layers for the scene tint over correct local flats rather
  than repainting flats.

## Verify
- [ ] Colour script shows a temperature arc across the issue.
- [ ] Each scene has one dominant temperature.
