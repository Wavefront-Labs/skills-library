# Dropping backgrounds to colour

**Goal:** a panel where the background is replaced or emptied into a flat colour
or gradient, isolating emotion or action.

## When
- Moment of emotion or realisation (the world falls away).
- Impact panel: hot red, yellow or white background.
- Panels the artist left without background (deadline or intent); the drop makes it
  look deliberate.
- Talking-heads pages: alternating drops keep focus on faces.

## Choosing the colour
- Emotion: red/orange for rage and impact, blue/violet for shock or sorrow, white
  for blinding moments.
- Contrast with the figure's dominant hue (complement) for separation.
- Gradient from top to bottom gives depth; radial gradient centred on the face
  works as a spotlight.
- Keep it from the scene's palette family unless the shift is the point.

## Execution
- Flat the background as one region (bucket on the background area of the flats).
- Optional texture: halftone dots, Ben-Day pattern, subtle noise.
- Hold any background line art in colour or drop it entirely (with artist approval).

## Verify
- [ ] Drop serves a story beat noted in the colour script.
- [ ] Figure separates from the drop at thumbnail size.
