# Colour to direct the eye

**Goal:** the most important thing in each panel is also the most visually
contrasty, and the eye moves through the page in reading order.

## Hierarchy tools (strongest first)
1. Value contrast: lightest light against darkest dark at the focal point.
2. Saturation: saturated focal element in a muted surround.
3. Hue contrast: complementary accent (a red scarf in a blue scene).
4. Detail/rendering: render the focus, flatten the background.

## Practice
- Knock back backgrounds: lower saturation and contrast, shift toward the scene's
  ambient colour. Often a single flat colour or gradient behind figures works.
- Keep balloon areas calm; busy colour behind lettering slows the read.
- Across the page: place accents along the reading path (top-left to bottom-right
  Z path in Western books) so colour hands the eye panel to panel.
- Separate overlapping figures with value, not outlines.

## Checks
- Greyscale check: desaturate the page; focal points must still pop.
- Squint or blur test: the first blob you see should be the story beat.

## Failure modes
- Everything saturated and rendered: no hierarchy, fatigue.
- Focal accents in the wrong panel corner pulling the eye backward.

## Verify
- [ ] Greyscale version keeps the same focal points.
- [ ] Backgrounds quieter than figures in every panel.
