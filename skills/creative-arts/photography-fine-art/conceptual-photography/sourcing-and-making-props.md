# Sourcing and making props

## Prop list
Make a spreadsheet: item, scene position, source (own/buy/borrow/rent/make), cost, status,
return date. Separate **hero props** (in focus, close) from **dressing** (background).

## Sourcing
- Charity shops, flea markets, online second-hand (eBay, Marketplace) - cheapest for period items.
- Prop houses/rental for furniture and specialist objects; book early, pay damage deposits.
- Friends' houses for lived-in objects; label and photograph items before taking them.

## Making and finishing
- Matte finishes photograph better than glossy (no hot specular highlights). Use dulling spray or matte
  varnish on shiny new plastics.
- Ageing: tea/coffee staining for paper, sanding edges, dry brushing paint, dust (fuller's earth).
- Scale props or foam for heavy items; hide fixings out of camera view.
- Food: undercook for colour, brush with oil for gloss, swap in fresh items under hot lights.

## Gotchas
- Logos and trademarks: remove or turn away to avoid clearance issues.
- Period accuracy: a modern plug socket ruins a 1950s interior; check background details.

## Verify
- [ ] Every item on the list has status and return plan; nothing unintended readable in frame.
