# Miniatures and forced perspective

## Miniatures that read as full-size
1. **Camera height to scale**: a 1:12 set needs the lens at ~14 cm for eye level (1.7 m / 12).
   Use a small lens (periscope/probe lens, short focal length) or a phone lens.
2. **Deep depth of field**: real scenes are sharp; miniatures shot shallow look tiny. Stop down
   (f/16-f/22) and **focus stack**.
3. **Light to scale**: one hard key (sun) far away for parallel shadows; small softboxes otherwise.
4. **Texture scale**: materials must look right at scale (fine sand, thin paper); avoid real
   wood grain and fabric weave that betray size.
5. Atmosphere: a little haze or a light grade adds distance.

## Forced perspective (on set)
- Place a larger object far from the camera and a smaller near it, aligned so they appear to touch.
- Requires deep DoF so both are sharp; lock camera on a tripod and mark positions.
- Shadows must agree - a shared light direction and no shadow giving away the gap.

## Gotchas
- Thomas Demand's paper models are deliberately slightly too clean - decide whether to reveal construction.
- Water and fire do not scale; shoot them separately and composite.

- [ ] Viewers read the intended scale; no detail betrays the trick.
