# Practical lights in frame

## Balance
A practical lamp in a long exposure blows out to white. Target: lamp shade glows bright but holds some
tone/texture, about 1-2 stops above the subject key.

1. Light the scene with your hidden lights first at the planned exposure.
2. Turn on the practical; meter the shade (spot) - dim the bulb (dimmer, lower wattage, ND gel or
   tracing paper inside the shade) until it sits where you want.
3. Bulbs: dimmable warm LED or tungsten; tungsten dims warmer (lower K) - nice for mood.
4. Screens (TV, phone): set screen brightness; at slow shutter speeds avoid refresh banding (1/60 or slower).

## Hiding the key
Put a concealed small light (LED panel, bare bulb) behind or beside the practical, aligned with it, to
light the subject in the same direction as the lamp would.

## Colour
Decide WB around the room's main source. Practicals at 2700 K with a 3200 K WB look warm - usually right.

## Gotchas
- Visible bulb hot spot through the shade: double-layer diffusion inside the shade.
- Mains flicker at fast shutter speeds: use a slower shutter or DC/battery LEDs.

## Verify
- [ ] Practical glows without clipping to a blank shape; subject lighting consistent with it.
