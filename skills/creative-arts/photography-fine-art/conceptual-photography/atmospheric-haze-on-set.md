# Atmospheric haze on set

## Machines
- **Hazer** (oil or water-based fluid): fine, even, long-hanging haze - best for photography.
- **Fog machine**: thick clouds, dissipates fast, uneven - for visible fog effects.
- **Low fog** (chilled): ground-hugging.
- Outdoors: haze blows away; use near-windless times, smoke in confined areas only with permits.

## Procedure
1. Close doors/vents; run the hazer to fill the space, then wait for it to even out (fans on low to mix).
2. **Backlight** or sidelight into the haze toward the camera - haze is invisible from the front.
   Hard sources (bare/fresnel, sun through windows) make visible beams.
3. Density: less is more; subtle haze lowers contrast in distance (aerial perspective). Measure by
   watching the background drop in contrast.
4. Keep haze consistent between frames of a series - top up at intervals.

## Safety
- Check smoke detectors/fire alarm with the venue (isolation may be needed).
- Use machine-approved fluid only; ventilate after; avoid for sitters with asthma.

## Gotchas
- Haze flattens blacks: re-meter and adjust contrast in post.
- Fog machines spit droplets close up; keep away from lenses and set dressing.

## Verify
- [ ] Beams visible where wanted; density even across frames.
