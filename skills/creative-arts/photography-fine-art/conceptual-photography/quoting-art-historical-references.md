# Quoting art-historical references

## Ways to quote
- **Compositional**: same geometry and figure placement (Wall after Hokusai, Manet).
- **Light**: Caravaggio/Vermeer window light, tenebrism.
- **Pose/gesture**: a recognisable figure pose in a contemporary setting.
- **Palette**: a painter's colour scheme.

## Procedure
1. Choose the source because its meaning connects to your idea; write what it adds.
2. Study it: grid over the painting to map figure positions, horizon, vanishing point, light angle.
3. Translate: lens choice to match perspective (paintings often have a high or wide viewpoint),
   light direction and size.
4. Decide fidelity: exact (homage), shifted (contemporary objects), or inverted (subversion).
5. Title or caption can signal the reference ("after ...").

## Rights
Paintings older than copyright term are public domain; recreating a living artist's or a recent
film's composition can raise copyright questions - get advice for commercial use.

## Verify
- [ ] Reference is recognisable to an informed viewer and the change you made is meaningful.
