# Eliciting expression and gesture

## Approaches
- **Scenario, not instruction**: "You've just read a letter that changes your plans" works better than
  "look sad".
- **Action-based**: give a physical task (look for keys, turn toward a sound) and photograph its
  middle or end.
- **Interrupt the camera face**: ask a question, have them close eyes and open on cue, look away and back.
- **Stillness for tableau**: Crewdson-style images want inward, absorbed expressions - ask sitters
  to think of something specific and to breathe out.

## Procedure
1. Spend time before shooting; explain the image and their role.
2. Start with easy frames to relax them; save the key frame for when they are warmed up.
3. Watch the eyes and mouth; tension shows in jaw and shoulders - ask for a shoulder drop or shake-out.
4. Shoot sequences; expression peaks are brief.
5. Keep crew quiet and few during intimate moments.

## Gotchas
- Asking for a smile gives a social smile (no eye involvement).
- Over-directing produces stiffness; give one note at a time.
