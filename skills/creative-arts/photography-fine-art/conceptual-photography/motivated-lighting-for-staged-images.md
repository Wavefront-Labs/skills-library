# Motivated lighting for staged images

A light is **motivated** when the viewer can attribute it to a source inside or just outside
the frame. It keeps a constructed image believable.

## Procedure
1. List the scene's plausible sources: window (day/moon), lamps, TV, street light, car headlights, fridge.
2. Choose the **key motivation** - the source that lights the main subject.
3. Place your real light so it comes from that direction, at a believable height and colour
   (window = daylight-balanced, large; lamp = warm, small, low).
4. Make the practical source look bright in frame (see practical lights) but light the subject with a
   hidden fixture aligned to it.
5. Fill should look like room bounce: soft, low, near the source colour.
6. Check shadows: the direction of every shadow must point away from a motivating source.

## Gotchas
- Unmotivated rim lights look like advertising.
- Several strong sources from different directions produce multiple shadows that betray the setup.

## Verify
- [ ] For each lit area you can point to its source in or just outside the frame.
