# Posing the body and hands

## Principles
- **Weight on the back foot** (contrapposto): hips and shoulders tilt naturally, body relaxes.
- **Angles**: body turned 30-45 deg from camera, face back toward it; fully square = confrontational
  (sometimes the point).
- **Separate limbs** from the torso: a small gap at the waist reveals shape.
- **Joints bend**: straight arms and legs look stiff; slight bends everywhere.
- **Chin forward and slightly down** to define the jaw (avoid double chin).
- **Hands**: give them a task (hold, touch, lean); show the side edge of the hand, not the back or
  flat palm; relaxed fingers with gaps.

## Directing
1. Demonstrate the pose yourself - mirroring is faster than verbal instruction.
2. Use "your left/right" consistently, or point.
3. Build from feet up: feet, weight, hips, shoulders, arms, head, eyes.
4. Shoot through small variations; talk continuously; give positive feedback.

## Gotchas
- Limbs toward the lens look enlarged (especially wide lenses).
- In staged tableaux, poses should look like action caught mid-moment; ask for the action, not a pose.

## Verify
- [ ] No tangents or amputated hands; posture reads natural or intentionally stylised.
