# Capturing brackets for merge

## How many frames
1. Spot-meter the brightest highlight that must hold detail and the darkest shadow. The difference in stops is the scene range.
2. A modern sensor holds ~11-13 stops at base ISO; if the range exceeds that, bracket.
3. Spacing 1-2 EV; enough frames that the darkest frame keeps highlights below clipping and the brightest puts shadows mid-histogram. Usually 3 frames at 2 EV; 5-7 for interiors with windows or sun in frame.

## Camera settings
- **Aperture priority or manual**, varying **shutter speed only** (never aperture: DOF changes; never ISO if avoidable).
- AEB (auto exposure bracketing) with bracket count and step; set the camera to burst or self-timer so one press fires the set.
- Base ISO, manual focus, fixed WB, RAW.
- Tripod with 2 s timer or remote; electronic first-curtain shutter to avoid shake.

## Handheld
- Fastest burst, shortest shutter speeds (bracket centred around 1/250 or faster); Lightroom's Auto Align handles small shifts.

## Bracketing for other merges
- Panorama: brackets at each position (see HDR panorama skill).
- Focus stacking: the same idea in focus distance, merged in Photoshop/Helicon, not Lightroom.

## Labelling
- Shoot a hand frame between sets. In Lightroom, Photo > Stacking > Auto-Stack by Capture Time (e.g. 2 s) groups brackets.

## Verify
- [ ] Darkest frame histogram has no highlight clipping; brightest frame shadows are well off the left edge.
