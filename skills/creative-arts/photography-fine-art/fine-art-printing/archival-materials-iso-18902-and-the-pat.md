# Archival materials: ISO 18902 and the PAT

## ISO 18902
Specifies requirements for albums, framing and storage materials (enclosures, boards, adhesives, plastics)
used with processed photographic materials (silver, chromogenic and inkjet prints).
Key points: chemical stability, no harmful plasticisers or sulfur, appropriate pH (buffered for some, unbuffered
for others), and passing the **Photographic Activity Test**.

## Photographic Activity Test (PAT, ISO 18916)
The Image Permanence Institute test: materials are incubated in contact with sensitive detectors (image
fading and staining). Materials that "pass the PAT" are safe in contact with photographs. Look for "PAT passed"
on boxes, sleeves, tissue, mat board, tape.

## Safe materials
- Plastics: uncoated polyester (Mylar D / Melinex 516), polypropylene, polyethylene.
- Paper/board: 100% cotton museum board, lignin-free, PAT-passed; unbuffered for alternative process or
  albumen/cyanotype; buffered is fine for most silver gelatin.
- Adhesives: wheat starch paste with Japanese tissue; PAT-passed tapes for non-contact use only.

## Avoid
PVC sleeves, rubber cement, pressure-sensitive tapes on the print, wood-pulp board, kraft paper, wooden
drawers (acid vapour), self-adhesive photo albums, oil-based paints in fresh frames (outgassing).

## Gotchas
- Not all "acid-free" products pass the PAT.
- Inkjet prints need time to outgas before sealing in polyester/frames (24 h or more).

## Verify
- [ ] Every enclosure/mount in contact with prints is PAT-passed or ISO 18902-compliant.
