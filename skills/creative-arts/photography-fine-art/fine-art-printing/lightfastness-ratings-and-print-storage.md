# Lightfastness ratings and print storage

## Ratings
- **Wilhelm Imaging Research (WIR)**: display permanence in "years until noticeable fading" under 450 lux,
  12 h/day, glass framed. Pigment prints on good papers often rate 100-200+ years; dye inks much less.
  Ratings for printer + ink + paper combinations - check yours.
- **Aardenburg Imaging** publishes measured fading (Megalux hours to conservation limit) for many papers -
  stricter, includes paper yellowing and OBA loss.
- Standards: ISO 18937 (light stability test methods), ISO 18941 (ozone), ISO 18946 (humidity).

## Display
- Light dose = lux x hours. Halving lux doubles life. Museums show photographs at about 50 lux (sensitive) to
  150-200 lux (stable modern prints).
- UV-filtering glazing; no direct sun; LED with no UV.
- Rotate works in long shows; keep reserves in the dark.

## Storage (ISO 18920 guidance)
- Cool and dry, stable: about 18-21 deg C or cooler; relative humidity about 30-50%, avoid fluctuations;
  colour (chromogenic) prints benefit from cold storage.
- Dark, in PAT-passed boxes/folders, interleaved with buffered or unbuffered tissue as appropriate.
- Flat storage (solander boxes) for large prints; avoid attics, basements, outside walls.
- Handle with nitrile/cotton gloves.

- [ ] Paper/ink combination has a published rating; display lux and storage RH/temp monitored.
