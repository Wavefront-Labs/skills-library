# Dry mounting, face mounting and glazing

## Mounting options
| Method | Notes | Reversible |
|---|---|---|
| Hinge in mat | traditional, archival | yes |
| **Dry mount** (heat-activated tissue, e.g. Drytac/Seal tissue, about 80-95 deg C in a press) | flat, permanent; standard for RC/inkjet display prints | mostly no (some removable tissues) |
| Cold mount (pressure-sensitive adhesive on foam board/dibond) | quick, large prints | no |
| **Dibond/aluminium composite** mount | rigid, frameless, large | no |
| **Face mount** (acrylic bonded to print face with optically clear silicone/adhesive - Diasec-type) | luminous, deep blacks, frameless modern look | no |

Use permanent methods for exhibition copies, not for unique vintage prints.

## Face mounting cautions
Done by specialist labs; adhesive must be compatible with the paper/ink (usually glossy/baryta or C-type);
let inkjet prints outgas 48 h+ first; acrylic scratches - deliver with protective film.

## Glazing choice
| Glazing | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| Float glass | cheap | reflections, green tint, breaks |
| UV-filtering glass (Conservation Clear) | blocks ~99% UV | reflections |
| **Anti-reflective UV glass** (Museum Glass) | nearly invisible | expensive, fingerprints |
| **UV acrylic** (Optium Museum Acrylic) | light, shatterproof, anti-reflective, anti-static | expensive, scratches |
Use acrylic for shipping and large works; glass is fine for small works installed locally.

## Gotchas
- Glazing must not touch the print (use mat or spacers) - condensation and ferrotyping/sticking.

## Verify
- [ ] Mount method suits print value; glazing UV-filtering with a gap from the print surface.
