# Pacing and narrative arc

## Structures
- **Narrative**: a journey or chronology; clear beginning, development, end.
- **Poetic/associative**: images linked by form or feeling; meaning accumulates (most fine-art books).
- **Sections or chapters**: divided by blanks or title pages, each with its own arc (e.g. four sections opening with a flag in Frank's *The Americans*).
- **Typology/grid**: equal weight, order by a variable (size, date, place).

## Openings and endings
- First image sets tone and terms: often quieter or establishing, or a strong emblem of the project.
- Last image should feel resolved or open the question you want to leave. Avoid ending on the "best" image just because it is strongest.
- Second-to-last often carries the emotional peak.

## Rhythm
- Alternate density (busy/quiet), scale (wide/close), light (bright/dark) and people/places. Three similar images in a row create emphasis; four become monotony.
- **Pauses**: a blank page, a white wall section, or a very quiet image resets attention.
- **Motifs**: let a recurring element (a colour, a shape, a person) return at intervals as a thread.

## Method
1. Put work prints in a line; move only one image at a time and reread from the start.
2. Read it fast (flipping) and slow; both should work.
3. Mark "beats": where the energy rises, where it rests. Draw a curve; adjust where it flatlines.
4. Show someone the sequence silently; watch where they slow and where they flip fast.

## Verify
- [ ] A cold viewer can recount the sequence's arc or mood in a sentence and no stretch feels repetitive.
