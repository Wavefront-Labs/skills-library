# Writing an exhibition proposal

## Research fit first
- Visit the venue (or review two years of its programme). Check submission policy: many commercial galleries do not accept unsolicited proposals; public galleries and artist-run spaces often have open submission windows.
- Note wall length, ceiling height, lighting, budget and audience.

## Package (follow their format exactly; otherwise this order)
1. **Cover letter** (one page): who you are, the project in two sentences, why this venue (specific), dates or season if flexible, what you are asking for.
2. **Project statement** (300 words).
3. **Images**: 10-20 JPEGs, 1920 px long edge, sRGB, named `01_Lastname_Title.jpg` in sequence order, or a PDF with one image per page.
4. **Image list**: number, title, year, medium ("archival pigment print"), framed size, edition.
5. **Installation plan**: number of works, sizes, framing, a sketch or floor plan for this specific space (shows you thought about it), any AV or special needs.
6. **Budget** (for public/non-profit venues): framing, printing, shipping, install, insurance, publicity; what you will fund and what you seek.
7. **CV and bio**.
8. Links: website, previous installation views.

## Craft
- Installation views of past shows are persuasive; include one if you have them.
- Show the work is ready or the timeline to completion.
- Keep the PDF under 10 MB.

## Follow-up
- One polite check-in after 6-8 weeks if no reply; record submissions in a tracker.

## Verify
- [ ] Package matches the venue's stated format, image list numbers match file names, and the plan fits their measured walls.
