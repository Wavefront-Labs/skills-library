# Condition reports, packing and shipping prints

## Condition report
For each work, at every handover (dispatch, arrival, install, de-install, return):
- Work ID, title, date, medium, dimensions (image, sheet, frame), edition.
- Frame: corners, finish scratches, glazing (cracks, scratches, haze), backing and hanging hardware.
- Print: surface (scratches, abrasions, fingerprints, silvering), planar distortion (cockling, creases), stains, foxing, fading, mould, adhesive failures, mount/mat condition.
- Overall photos plus close-ups of any damage, with a sketch or annotated photo locating it (grid of the print: top left, centre, etc.).
- Signed and dated by both parties with lighting conditions noted (raking light reveals surface damage).
- Use pencil or digital forms; never ink near prints.

## Packing framed works
1. Tape glass with low-tack film (or use acrylic glazing for shipping) to hold shards if it breaks.
2. Wrap in glassine or polyethylene sheeting (never bubble wrap directly against surfaces: it imprints).
3. Corner protectors, then a box with 5 cm foam on all sides, or a **travel frame/crate** for high-value works.
4. Label "Fragile, this way up, do not lay flat" and an image of the packed contents inside the lid.

## Unframed prints
- Interleave with acid-free tissue or glassine, in a sturdy archival box or between rigid boards; flat, never rolled unless large and the paper allows (image side out on a 10+ cm tube).

## Shipping and insurance
- Use fine-art shippers or couriers for valuable works; check insurance covers nail-to-nail (from your wall to theirs and back). Declare values per the price list.
- Customs: ATA Carnet for temporary exhibition exports; commercial invoice for sales.

## Verify
- [ ] Every work has signed outgoing and incoming condition reports with photographs; insurance certificate on file before dispatch.
