# Licensing images

A licence grants permission to use an image under defined terms; you keep copyright. Clear terms prevent disputes.

## Define the usage (every licence)
- **Licensee** and end client.
- **Media**: editorial (print magazine, online article), advertising, book cover, packaging, exhibition reproduction, social media.
- **Placement and size**: interior/cover, full page/quarter page, homepage hero.
- **Print run or impressions**; **territory** (country/worldwide); **duration** (one year, life of edition).
- **Exclusivity**: non-exclusive by default; exclusivity costs more and must be limited by category/time.
- **Credit line** required, e.g. `(c) Jane Doe`.
- Restrictions: no alteration, no AI training use, no sublicensing.

## Pricing factors
- Media type and reach, size/placement, duration, territory, exclusivity, and the image's uniqueness. Check industry calculators and ASMP/stock reference rates; fine-art images commonly license at premium rates.

## Documents
- Quote/estimate with usage description and price; licence document or invoice that states the granted rights and that rights are granted on full payment.
- PLUS (Picture Licensing Universal System) standardised terms and the PLUS Licence ID can be embedded in metadata.
- Keep signed licences tied to the image record.

## Infringements
1. Capture evidence (screenshots with URL and date, archive.org).
2. Check registration status (US: affects damages).
3. Send a retroactive licence invoice or a DMCA takedown (US platforms) depending on goal; escalate via a copyright attorney or the US Copyright Claims Board (small claims up to USD 30,000).

## Verify
- [ ] Each licence states media, placement, territory, duration, exclusivity and credit, and is filed with the image record.
