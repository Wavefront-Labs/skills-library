# Lighting photographs in exhibition

## Light levels (conservation)
- Silver gelatin black-and-white prints: up to **~100-200 lux**, moderate sensitivity.
- Chromogenic colour, inkjet pigment prints: **50-100 lux** (sensitive to fading; dye-based inkjet even more).
- Historic and alternative processes (salt prints, albumen, cyanotype, platinum heightened with dyes), and unknown or rare prints: **50 lux** maximum, often limited display time.
- Think in **lux-hours per year** (e.g. 50 lux x 8 h x 300 days = 120,000 lux-h): rotate works or use motion-activated lighting for long shows.
- No direct daylight; UV < 75 microwatts/lumen (LED sources are near zero UV).

## Quality
- High-CRI LED (CRI 95+, R9 > 80), **3000-4000 K** for galleries; match colour temperature across the room. Prints evaluated for 5000 K booths look warmer under 3000 K.
- Track spots with 24-36 degree beams for individual works; wall-washers for even light on a long series.
- Aim at about **30 degrees** from vertical to the wall at frame centre height to avoid glare reflections into the viewer's eye and frame shadows on the image.

## Glare and reflections
- Glazed frames: check from typical standing positions and from seated height; anti-reflective acrylic or glass removes most issues.
- Avoid placing lights behind the viewer that cast their shadow onto the work.

## Measure
1. Lux meter (or a calibrated phone app for rough checks) held at the face of the print, sensor facing the light.
2. Adjust with dimmers, spot distance or neutral density filters/snoots.
3. Record readings per work in the install log.

## Verify
- [ ] Every print measured within its lux target, uniform within about 20% across a print, and no glare from standing or seated positions.
