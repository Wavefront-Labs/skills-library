# Consignment agreements

On consignment the gallery holds works but **you own them until sold**. Many US states (e.g. New York Arts and Cultural Affairs Law Art. 12) make consigned works and proceeds trust property protected from the gallery's creditors; get it in writing regardless.

## Key terms
- **Works**: consignment receipt listing each work (title, edition number, size, framed/unframed, retail price, condition), signed by both.
- **Commission**: commonly 40-50% of the retail sale price; specify who bears framing and production costs and whether they are repaid before the split.
- **Pricing**: retail prices agreed in writing; maximum discount the gallery may give (e.g. 10%) and who absorbs it (often split).
- **Payment**: within 30 days of the gallery receiving payment; instalment sales: proportional payment and title retained until paid. Right to know the buyer's name for your records.
- **Insurance**: gallery insures works at the artist's net value while on premises and in transit (nail to nail).
- **Duration and territory**: term (e.g. 12 months, exclusive for a region or for primary market sales only).
- **Reproduction**: you keep copyright; gallery may reproduce for promotion with credit.
- **Return**: on termination, unsold works returned within 30 days at gallery's cost, in the condition received.
- **Accounting**: periodic inventory statements; right to inspect sale records.

## Practice
- Condition-report on delivery and on return.
- Keep your own inventory updated with each consignment location.
- A written letter agreement is far better than none; have a lawyer or Volunteer Lawyers for the Arts review the first one.

## Verify
- [ ] Every work at a gallery appears on a signed consignment receipt, and the agreement states commission, payment days, insurance and return terms.
