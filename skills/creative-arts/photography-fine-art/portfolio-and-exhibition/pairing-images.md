# Pairing images

A pair (facing pages, a diptych, adjacent frames on a wall) creates a third meaning from the relationship.

## Relationship types
- **Formal rhyme**: shape, line, gesture repeated (a curved road and a curved arm).
- **Colour or tone echo**: a red accent in both; matched palettes.
- **Contrast**: interior/exterior, crowded/empty, near/far, nature/artifice.
- **Narrative or cause**: before/after, question/answer, object and its context.
- **Scale shift**: landscape beside a detail of something in it.
- **Gaze/direction**: a figure facing toward the other image draws the eye across the gutter.

## Method
1. Lay out work prints in pairs; try every candidate against two or three others.
2. Look for pairs that create tension or a question, not just matching (pairs that are too similar cancel each other).
3. Check direction: left-page images that point outward lead the eye off the spread; flip pairs so motion and gaze lead across.
4. Check density: a heavy dark image beside a pale one unbalances unless intentional.
5. Note why each pair works in a sentence; if you cannot say, re-check it.

## In books
- Spread logic: verso (left) and recto (right). Recto is the stronger position; single images often go recto facing blank verso.
- Do not split an image across the gutter unless the binding lays flat.

## On walls
- Pairs hung close (5-10 cm) read as diptychs; with normal spacing (10-20 cm) they read as neighbours.

## Verify
- [ ] Each pair can be described by a stated relationship, and swapping either image weakens it.
