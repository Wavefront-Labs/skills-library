# Making a photobook dummy

A dummy (maquette) is a physical prototype used to test sequence, scale and materials, and to pitch publishers and dummy awards.

## Stages
1. **Rough dummy**: small work prints (4x6 in) taped into a blank notebook or folded sheets. Test sequence and pairs fast. Make several.
2. **Format decisions**: trim size (portrait, landscape, square), page count in multiples of 8 or 16 (printer signatures: 16 pages per signature is typical), image placement (full bleed, white border, one per spread).
3. **Layout dummy**: InDesign (or Affinity Publisher) layout printed on a desktop inkjet at actual size, trimmed and folded into signatures; check each spread physically.
4. **Finished dummy**: hand-bound (pamphlet stitch, Japanese stab, case binding) or print-on-demand (Blurb, Bob Books, Saal Digital) to show publishers a near-final object.

## Testing on the dummy
- Gutter loss: images crossing the spine lose 5-15 mm into the binding unless lay-flat or Swiss binding.
- Paper: uncoated vs coated, weight (150-170 gsm text common); show-through on thin paper with dark images.
- Typography: title page, text, captions placement (in the back is common for fine-art books).

## Using it
- Dummy awards (Kassel Dummy Award, Unseen Dummy Award, Mack First Book Award) and publisher meetings. Bring the dummy plus a one-page spec (size, pages, binding, images, estimated run).

## Verify
- [ ] Page count fits the signature plan, every spread has been checked physically at size, and a stranger can flip through it without explanation.
