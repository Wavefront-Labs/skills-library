# Editing with work prints on the wall

Screens show one image at a time; a wall shows relationships. Most photographers and editors do final edits physically.

## Prints
- Inexpensive work prints: 4x6 in (10x15 cm) for books and sequences of 40-100; 5x7 or A5 for exhibition edits of 15-30. Matte inkjet or lab prints; consistent density matters more than quality.
- Print the same relative scale as intended output; mixed sizes on a wall if the show will use mixed sizes.
- Write a small code on the back (`CST-024`) matching the file name.

## Wall
- Pin board, foam board or a magnetic wall; low-tack putty or artist tape (avoid pins through the image).
- Neutral grey or white surroundings; consistent, daylight-balanced lighting.
- For books, pin in pairs to simulate spreads; for shows, use a scaled wall elevation (e.g. 1:10) with tape marks for doors and corners.

## Process
1. Pin the generous edit loosely in clusters by theme or form.
2. Pull out obvious misfits; move outliers to a "maybe" column at the edge.
3. Arrange the line; step back 3-4 m and look at the whole, then walk along it.
4. **Live with it**: leave it up for days; notice which images you stop seeing.
5. Photograph every version of the wall with a date; it becomes the sequence record.
