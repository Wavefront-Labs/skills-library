# Writing a project statement

A project statement describes **one body of work**. An artist statement describes your practice as a whole.

## Structure (150-300 words)
1. **What**: the subject and form. "*Tideline* is a series of 30 large-format colour photographs of the shoreline near my home, made at low tide over four winters."
2. **How**: process or method if it matters to the meaning (camera, process, duration, collaboration).
3. **Why**: the idea, question or concern; what the viewer might consider. Specific beats grand.
4. **Context** (optional): one sentence on research, history or influences.

## Style
- First person, present tense, plain words. A non-specialist should understand every sentence.
- Concrete nouns and verbs; avoid inflated art language ("interrogates", "liminal", "problematises") and claims about what viewers will feel.
- Do not describe individual images in detail; do not explain away the work.
- Read it aloud; cut every sentence that would still be true of someone else's project.

## Variants
- **50 words**: for submission forms and wall labels.
- **100-150 words**: for open calls and press.
- **300 words**: for reviews, websites, grant applications.

## Process
1. Write freely, 500+ words. 2. Highlight the five most specific sentences. 3. Rebuild from those. 4. Ask someone outside art to read it and say back what the work is.

## Verify
- [ ] A reader who has not seen the photographs can say what the project is, how it was made and why, after one reading.
