# Sequencing, portfolio reviews and hanging a show

**Goal:** present a body of work so its order, pacing and physical display carry
meaning — in a review box, on a wall or in a book.

## 1. Sequencing
- Print all candidates **small** (e.g. 10×15 cm) and lay them on a table or pin to a
  wall. Sequence physically, not in software.
- **Opener:** a strong, inviting image that sets the tone, not necessarily the best.
  **Closer:** something that resolves or deliberately leaves open.
- **Pacing:** alternate density (busy/quiet, close/far, light/dark) so the viewer
  breathes. Avoid three near-identical frames in a row unless repetition is the point.
- **Pairs and links:** in books and on walls, adjacent images talk; link by form,
  colour, gesture or contrast. Ask "what does this pair say that neither says alone?"
- Remove anything that's there because it was hard to get.
- Leave the sequence for a few days, then re-check. Ask someone else to read it
  without your explanation.

## 2. Portfolio reviews
At festivals and review events, you typically get ~20 minutes with each reviewer.
- Bring **one project**, 15–25 prints in a box (or a tablet as backup), in sequence,
  same size, clean and unmounted or lightly mounted.
- **Research reviewers** in advance; book those whose programme fits (curators for
  shows, editors for publications, publishers for books).
- Have a **one-line description** and a 100–150 word statement; then stop talking and
  let them look.
- Ask specific questions ("does the sequence hold?", "what's missing?").
- Take notes; follow up within a week with a short thank-you and a link.

## 3. Hanging an exhibition
- **Centre line:** hang so the centre of each work sits at a consistent height —
  commonly around 57–60 in (≈145–152 cm) from the floor. Consistency matters more
  than the exact number.
- **Wall plan:** measure walls, draw elevations at scale, place frames on paper first.
  Even spacing (e.g. 5–10 cm for tight grids, much more for single works) and
  alignment on the centre line.
- **Hanging maths:** nail height = centre line + half frame height − distance from
  frame top to taut wire/hanger.
- Use **two hangers per frame** (or French cleats) for level, stable hanging; check
  with a level and laser line.
- **Lighting:** aim spots at ~30° from vertical to avoid glare in glazing; even
  illumination across works. Light-sensitive prints may need lux limits — check with
  the venue.
- **Wall text:** title wall, statement, labels (title, year, medium e.g. "archival
  pigment print", size, edition). Keep consistent placement (e.g. right of work at
  ~120–130 cm).

## 4. Into a photobook
- Make a **dummy** (cheap prints, folded and taped) before any design software.
- A spread is a pair; decide image-per-spread rhythm, blank pages and full bleeds.
- Test text placement and the cover last.

## Gotchas
- Framing lead times can be weeks — order early.
- Glazing reflections: anti-reflective acrylic or glass is expensive but often worth it
  for dark images.
- Uncertain: review formats and hanging conventions vary by institution.

## Verify
- [ ] Sequence tested on a fresh viewer without explanation.
- [ ] Review box: one project, consistent prints, statement ready.
- [ ] All works hung on the same centre line, level, evenly spaced.
- [ ] No glare from normal viewing positions.
- [ ] Labels complete and consistently placed.
