# Evaluating competitions and open calls

## Questions before entering
1. **Who judges?** Named jurors with relevant curatorial or editorial roles. Anonymous juries are a warning sign.
2. **What do winners get?** Exhibition at a real venue, publication, cash, portfolio review access, representation. Check past winners: where did the exposure lead?
3. **Fees vs value**: typical fees $25-$75 for a few images. Calculate fee per image and compare outcome value. High fees with vague prizes = a revenue scheme.
4. **Rights terms**: read them. Acceptable: non-exclusive licence to reproduce entries/winners for promotion of the competition, with credit, for a limited time. Unacceptable ("rights grab"): exclusive rights, unlimited commercial use, transfer of copyright, use of all entries (not just winners) in perpetuity.
5. **Exhibition costs**: who pays for printing, framing, shipping and return? Many open calls require artists to deliver framed work at their cost; budget it.
6. **Sales commission** if the show sells work (often 30-40%).
7. **AI and manipulation rules**: some forbid generative edits or composites; check before submitting.

## Red flags
- Every entrant "selected" for an online gallery or a paid book.
- Hanging fees for "selected" artists.
- Competition owned by a stock agency with broad licence terms.

## Track
- Spreadsheet: call, deadline, fee, jurors, rights terms OK (Y/N), images sent, result. Review yearly: which calls produced outcomes.

## Verify
- [ ] Terms read and rights acceptable, total cost (fee + production + shipping) budgeted, and jurors relevant to the work.
