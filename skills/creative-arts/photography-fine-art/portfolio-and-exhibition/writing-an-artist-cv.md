# Writing an artist CV

An artist CV lists professional activity. It is not a job CV: no skills or unrelated employment.

## Order (reverse chronological within each section)
1. Name, born year and place (optional), lives and works in, website, email.
2. **Education** (degree, institution, year).
3. **Solo Exhibitions** (year, title, venue, city, country).
4. **Group Exhibitions** (mark curated or juried shows).
5. **Publications / Books** (monographs you made), then **Bibliography** (articles, reviews about you).
6. **Awards, Grants and Residencies**.
7. **Collections** (public collections only; private collectors by name only with permission).
8. **Teaching / Talks / Lectures** (if relevant).
9. **Commissions**, **Professional Activity** (juror, reviewer).

## Format
- Entry format: `2025  Tideline, Fotogalleriet, Oslo, Norway (solo)`. Italicise titles of exhibitions and books.
- One to two pages for applications; a "selected" CV if longer. Label it "Selected Exhibitions" when trimmed.
- Upcoming shows listed with "(forthcoming)".
- PDF named `Lastname_Firstname_CV_2026.pdf`.

## Early career
- Include juried group shows, online publications of note, review participation, artist talks. Avoid padding (every Instagram feature).
- An empty section is better omitted than filled with weak entries.

## Honesty
- Never list a show that did not happen or exaggerate a group show as solo; curators check.

## Verify
- [ ] Every entry has year, title, venue and city; dates are reverse chronological and the file is current within three months.
