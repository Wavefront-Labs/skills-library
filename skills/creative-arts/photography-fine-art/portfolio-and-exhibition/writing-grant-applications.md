# Writing grant applications

## Find funders
- Public arts councils (Arts Council England DYCP / National Lottery Project Grants, Creative Scotland, Canada Council, state and local arts agencies in the US), private foundations, photography-specific awards (e.g. Magnum Foundation, Lucie Foundation, W. Eugene Smith Grant), residencies with stipends.
- Read eligibility first: residency/citizenship, career stage, individual vs organisation, previously funded projects.

## Read the criteria
- Funders score against published criteria (quality, public engagement, development, feasibility). Use their language in headings and answer each criterion explicitly.

## Application parts
1. **Project summary** (50-100 words): what, where, when, outcome.
2. **Project description**: the work and why now; method; your track record that shows you can do it.
3. **Outcomes and audience**: exhibition, book, workshops; numbers where possible; who benefits.
4. **Timeline**: phases with dates (research, production, editing, output, evaluation).
5. **Budget**: itemised expenses (fees at professional rates for yourself and collaborators, travel, materials, printing, access costs) and income (other funding, in-kind support). Must balance. Don't underpay yourself; many funders expect artist fee guidelines (e.g. a-n rates in the UK).
6. **Work samples**: 10-20 images in their exact specs; captions.
7. **Supporting letters** from partners (venues, publishers, communities).

## Craft
- Specific and measurable beats aspirational. Draft early; ask a previous recipient or the funder's officer (many offer advice calls).
- Keep every application: reuse sections.

## After
- If funded: report on time with images, outcomes and spend. If not: request feedback.

## Verify
- [ ] Every criterion addressed by heading, budget balances, samples match specs, and submission precedes the deadline by at least a day.
