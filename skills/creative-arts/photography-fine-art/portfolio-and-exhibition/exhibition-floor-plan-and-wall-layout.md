# Exhibition floor plan and wall layout

## Measure
- Each wall's length and height, doors, windows, sockets, vents, light tracks, fire equipment, radiators, columns. Note wall material (plasterboard, brick, concrete) for fixings.
- Draw a floor plan to scale (1:50) and an elevation per wall (1:20) in Illustrator, SketchUp, or on grid paper. Gallery planning apps or a spreadsheet can also work.

## Hanging height
- Common centre line: **145-150 cm (57-60 in)** from the floor to the centre of each work. The Smithsonian accessibility guidelines suggest keeping content visible to seated viewers; lower centres (140 cm) suit small works and wheelchair users.
- Hang everything on one centre line unless a grid or salon hang is the concept.

## Spacing
- Equal gaps measured between frame edges, typically 10-25 cm for medium works; larger works need more air. Keep at least 30-50 cm from corners and doors.
- Calculate: gap = (wall length - sum of frame widths) / (number of works + 1) for equal margins, or fix the gap and centre the group.

## Sightlines and flow
- The entrance view sets the tone: place the strongest image on the first visible wall.
- Follow the sequence in the expected walking direction (usually clockwise); place pairs facing across a room for dialogue.
- Leave quiet walls; avoid hanging on walls behind doors or above radiators.

## Hanging schedule
- Spreadsheet: work number, title, wall, position (distance from left corner to centre), centre height, fixing type, condition-checked by.
- Mark positions with low-tack tape on site before drilling.

## Verify
- [ ] Every work has a measured position on an elevation and a row in the hanging schedule; scaled plan matches the measured room.
