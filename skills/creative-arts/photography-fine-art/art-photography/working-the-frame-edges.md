# Working the frame edges

Szarkowski: the photograph is defined by what the frame leaves out. Edges are where most
compositional mistakes live.

## Edge scan (do every frame you care about)
1. Trace the four edges with your eye in the finder, corner to corner.
2. Look for: half-objects entering, bright patches, limbs cut at joints, horizons touching
   corners, poles or signs at the edge.
3. Fix by moving camera position or focal length first; cropping later loses resolution and
   often the relationships you wanted.

## Cutting figures
- Avoid cuts at joints (wrist, knee, ankle, neck); cut mid-limb or mid-thigh instead.
- Leave space in the direction of gaze/motion unless you want a trapped feeling.

## Aspect ratio
- Set the camera to the final ratio (1:1, 4:5, 3:2, 16:9) so the finder shows what will print.
- A series usually keeps one ratio; mixing reads as indecision.
- Full-frame-no-crop (filed black border) is a statement; know if you are making it.

## Gotchas
- Viewfinder coverage under 100% (older DSLRs) shows less than the sensor captures; edges surprise you.
- Lens corrections in post can crop edges slightly; leave a small margin.

## Verify
- [ ] All four edges scanned; nothing at the edge you would crop out later.
