# Colour harmony in the frame

## Schemes
- **Complementary** (opposite on the wheel: orange/blue, red/green, yellow/violet): maximum
  contrast; small area of one against large area of the other is strongest.
- **Analogous** (neighbours: yellow-orange-red): calm, unified.
- **Monochromatic** (one hue, varied lightness/saturation): quiet, tonal.
- **Accent**: an almost-neutral frame with one saturated spot draws the eye.

## Procedure (in the field)
1. Name the dominant hue in the scene before shooting.
2. Look for a second hue that harmonises or contrasts; move to include/exclude it.
3. Time of day: golden hour pushes everything analogous warm; blue hour sets warm lights
   against blue sky (complementary).
4. Remove distractors: a stray red sign in a blue-green frame owns the picture.

## Procedure (in post)
1. Use HSL to shift off-palette hues toward the palette and reduce their saturation.
2. Colour grading wheels: shadows one hue, highlights its complement or neighbour.
3. For a series, save the palette (eyedropper swatches) and check each image against it.

## Gotchas
- Albers: colour is relative; the same hue looks different by its neighbours; judge in context.
- Over-saturation flattens harmony; most fine-art palettes are restrained.

## Verify
- [ ] You can name the palette; no unintended saturated element competes.
