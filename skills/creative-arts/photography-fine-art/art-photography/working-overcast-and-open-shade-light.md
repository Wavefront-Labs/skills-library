# Working overcast and open-shade light

Overcast sky = one enormous soft source; open shade = light from the sky with the sun blocked.
Both are forgiving but can be flat and blue.

## Find the direction
Even in overcast, the sky is brighter in one direction. Hold up a hand or watch a face rotate:
the side toward the brightest sky gets the key. Put the subject at the **edge** of shade
(doorway, under a tree canopy edge) facing open sky - light comes from one side/front only.

## Add contrast
- **Negative fill**: black card or flag on the shadow side removes bounced light, deepening
  shadows (subtractive lighting).
- **Overhead flag**: blocks top light, removing dark eye sockets and adding catchlights from the horizon.
- **Background**: choose a darker background so the subject stands out.

## Colour
Shade is lit by blue sky: 7000-10000 K. Set WB to shade/cloudy or custom; leaves bouncing
green light add a cast - watch skin.

## Landscape use
Overcast is ideal for woods, waterfalls and intimate details: no hot spots, saturated foliage.
Exclude the white sky from the frame or it blows out.

## Verify
- [ ] Subject has a clear key side; shadows not muddy; colour neutral or intentionally cool.
