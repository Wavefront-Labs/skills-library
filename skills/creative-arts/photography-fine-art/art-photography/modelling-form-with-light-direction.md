# Modelling form with the direction of light

| Direction | Effect |
|---|---|
| Front (on-axis) | flattest, minimal shadow, shows colour and surface tone |
| 45 deg side/top | classic modelling, readable shape |
| 90 deg side | strong volume, half in shadow, maximum drama |
| Raking (grazing, near 90 deg to the surface) | maximum texture: stone, bark, fabric |
| Back / rim | outlines shape, separates from background, translucency |
| Top | sculptural for objects; eye sockets dark on people |
| Under | unnatural, theatrical |

## Procedure
1. Decide what the photograph must say about form: volume, texture, silhouette or surface.
2. Start with one light (or wait for the sun) at 45 deg; move it around the subject and watch
   the shadows - the "tonal gradient" across a curved surface is what reads as roundness.
3. Add fill only as needed to open shadows; fill reduces modelling.
4. For texture on a flat surface, rake the light; harden it for crisp micro-shadows.
5. For translucent subjects (leaves, glass) put light behind.

## Gotchas
- Two equal lights from opposite sides produce a flat face with a dark centre line.
- In landscape, low sun gives modelling; high midday sun flattens terrain (unless you want that).
