# Classic portrait lighting patterns

All patterns are defined by the **nose shadow** from a single key light.

| Pattern | Key position (relative to face) | Look |
|---|---|---|
| Butterfly / Paramount | in front, high (30-45 deg up), on camera axis | small shadow under nose; glamour; good for high cheekbones |
| Loop | 30-45 deg to side, slightly above eye level | small loop of nose shadow toward cheek, not touching lip shadow; universal |
| Rembrandt | 45-60 deg to side, higher | triangle of light on shadow cheek, below eye, no wider than the eye, no longer than the nose |
| Split | 90 deg to side | half the face lit; drama, mystery |
| Clamshell | butterfly key plus reflector/fill below | very open shadows; beauty |

## Procedure
1. Start with key at loop position, at roughly 1-1.5 m with a medium softbox.
2. Have the subject turn the face, not you move the light, to fine-tune the pattern.
3. Check the shadow cheek for the Rembrandt triangle; widen by moving the light toward camera.
4. Add fill (reflector or second light) only after the key is right.
5. Add hair/rim light and background light as needed.

## Gotchas
- Key too high = dark eye sockets and no catchlights.
- Glasses reflect the key: raise it or tilt glasses down slightly.

## Verify
- [ ] Nose shadow shape matches the chosen pattern; both eyes have catchlights.
