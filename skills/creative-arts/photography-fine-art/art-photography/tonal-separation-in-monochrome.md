# Tonal separation in monochrome

Red and green of equal luminance become the **same grey**. Black-and-white work depends on
seeing luminance, not hue.

## Training your eye
- Set the camera to monochrome preview (raw still keeps colour) to see tones live.
- Squint, or use a viewing filter (Wratten #90 amber "monovue") to judge tonal relationships.
- Identify key adjacent areas: face vs background, foliage vs flowers, sky vs cloud.

## Separation tools
1. **Light**: put the subject in light and the background in shade (most robust).
2. **Filter / mixer**: lighten one hue relative to another (see contrast filters).
3. **Local contrast**: dodge/burn or Clarity/Texture on edges.
4. **Development** (film): N+ expansion to spread close values.

## Digital procedure
1. In Lightroom B&W mixer, use the Targeted Adjustment Tool: drag on an area to lighten/darken its hue.
2. Push sliders gently; extremes (+/-60 and beyond) create noise, banding and halos on edges.
3. Check skin: Orange slider controls skin; keep natural.

## Gotchas
- Converting with "Desaturate" gives a flat result with no hue control.
- Large mixer pushes on JPEG files break up; use raw.

## Verify
- [ ] Key adjacent areas separated by at least about 1/2 to 1 stop of luminance.
