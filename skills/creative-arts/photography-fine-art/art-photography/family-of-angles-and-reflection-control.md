# Family of angles and reflection control

## Three reflections (Light Science and Magic ch.3-4)
- **Diffuse**: same brightness from every viewing angle; reveals the subject's tone/colour.
- **Direct (specular)**: mirror image of the light source; only visible from angles inside the
  family of angles.
- **Polarized direct**: direct reflection from non-metallic glossy surfaces, partly polarized
  (controllable with a polarizer).

## Family of angles
The set of light positions that will produce a direct reflection visible to the camera on a
given surface. For a flat surface it is the mirror-image cone of the lens's view of that surface.

## Procedure
1. Ask what each surface must show: tone (diffuse) or gloss/shape (direct).
2. Find the family of angles: from the camera, look at the surface - the area it mirrors is
   where a light would reflect. A small mirror or the subject itself shows it.
3. **To see texture and colour** (matt look on paper, painting, glossy print): put lights
   *outside* the family - e.g. two lights at 45 deg for copy work.
4. **To make glossy surfaces read as glossy/bright**: fill the family with a large, even source
   (big softbox or scrim) so the reflection covers the whole surface.
5. Use flags/black card to make the family dark when you need the surface black.

## Gotchas
- Curved surfaces have a much larger family of angles - you need larger sources.
- Longer lenses from farther away shrink the family; changes in camera position change it.

## Verify
- [ ] Each reflective surface shows the intended mix of diffuse and direct reflection.
