# Diffraction and the optimum aperture

Small apertures spread each point into an **Airy disk**. Diameter about 2.44 x wavelength x N
(at 550 nm: about 1.34 um x N). When the disk spans more than ~2 pixels, fine detail softens.

## Rough diffraction-limited apertures (visible at 100%)
| Sensor | Pixel pitch | Softening becomes visible from |
|---|---|---|
| 24 MP full frame | ~6.0 um | f/11 |
| 45-61 MP full frame | ~3.8-4.4 um | f/8 |
| 24 MP APS-C | ~3.9 um | f/8 |
| 20 MP Micro Four Thirds | ~3.3 um | f/5.6-f/8 |

These are onset points; f/16 on a 45 MP body still prints well at moderate size.

## Finding a lens's sweet spot
1. Tripod, flat detailed target (newspaper, brick wall), parallel to sensor.
2. Focus with live view magnified; shoot every full stop wide open to minimum.
3. Compare centre and corners at 100%. Usually best 2-3 stops down from wide open.
4. Record the best aperture per lens and focal length.

## Decision
- Need DoF beyond what the sweet spot gives? Compare (a) f/16 with diffraction against
  (b) focus stacking at f/8, or (c) tilt (tilt-shift or view camera).
- Raw sharpening can partly recover mild diffraction, not severe.

## Gotchas
- Diffraction depends on f-number, not focal length, but the visible effect depends on print size.
- Large format needs f/32-f/64 for DoF; the bigger negative tolerates it.

## Verify
- [ ] You know each lens's best aperture and the aperture where your sensor softens.
