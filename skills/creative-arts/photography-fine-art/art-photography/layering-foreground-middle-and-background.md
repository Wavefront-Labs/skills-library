# Layering foreground, middle ground and background

## Depth cues you can control
- **Overlap**: nearer objects partly hide farther ones.
- **Relative scale**: wide lenses close to foreground exaggerate it.
- **Atmospheric perspective**: haze lightens and cools distant layers.
- **Tonal separation**: alternate light/dark layers (dark foreground, light middle).
- **Focus**: selective focus separates planes; deep focus makes all layers equally present.

## Procedure
1. Identify three planes and an element in each.
2. Position so layers overlap but key shapes stay legible (no merging silhouettes).
3. Shoot from a lower or higher camera height to separate overlapping heads/objects.
4. Use backlight or side light so each layer has an edge.
5. In multi-subject layered frames (street work), wait for each layer to resolve; shoot sequences.

## Gotchas
- Too many layers = clutter; each must contribute.
- A dark foreground with no detail becomes a frame-within-frame vignette - intentional or not.

## Verify
- [ ] You can point to each layer and it separates by tone, scale or focus.
