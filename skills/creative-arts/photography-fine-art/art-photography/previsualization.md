# Previsualization

Adams: *visualization* is seeing the final print, with its tones, in the mind before exposure.
Exposure, development and printing then become means to that end rather than corrections.

## Procedure
1. Stand at the scene; decide what the image is about and which tones carry it.
2. Name the darkest area that must hold texture and the brightest that must hold detail.
3. Decide each area's print value: "this rock wall a deep Zone III, the snow a textured VII-VIII,
   the sky a dark V."
4. Measure the actual luminances (spot meter) and compare to your intended values.
5. Choose controls: exposure placement, filter, development (N+/-), and printing (grade, dodging).
6. Write it down (exposure record) so you can compare intention with result.

## Digital practice
Same thinking: decide the final tones, expose to protect what you need (ETTR or bracket),
then develop toward the visualized print, not toward a neutral default.

## Gotchas
- Visualization is not literal recording; Adams's "Moonrise" sky is far darker than reality.
- It takes practice: review prints against your notes to train the eye.

## Verify
- [ ] Final print matches the written intended values for key areas.
