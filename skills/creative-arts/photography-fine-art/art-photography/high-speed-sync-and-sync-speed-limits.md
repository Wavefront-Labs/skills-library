# High-speed sync and sync-speed limits

## Why sync speed exists
A focal-plane shutter above the **X-sync speed** (typically 1/160-1/250 s) never fully opens;
a slit travels across the sensor. A normal flash pulse lights only part of the frame -> black band.

## Options above sync speed
1. **HSS / FP mode**: flash pulses rapidly as a continuous light during the slit's travel.
   - Costs ~1.5-2 stops of power plus falloff with each faster stop.
   - Loses freezing ability (it is continuous light).
2. **ND filter** on the lens to stay at sync speed with a wide aperture - preserves full flash power.
3. **Leaf-shutter lens** (Hasselblad, Fuji X100, medium format) syncs at all speeds.
4. **Hypersync/long-duration tail sync** (some strobes) uses the flash tail; uneven gradient risk.

## Procedure (portrait at f/2 in bright sun)
1. Ambient: f/2, ISO 100 needs ~1/4000 s.
2. Option A: enable HSS on the trigger, shutter 1/4000, increase flash power; move light closer.
3. Option B: 3-stop ND, shutter at 1/250 (sync), normal flash at full efficiency.

## Gotchas
- Electronic shutter usually disables flash entirely or limits sync speed.
- Second-curtain sync is a different setting (motion trails behind subject).

## Verify
- [ ] No black band; enough flash power to reach the intended ratio.
