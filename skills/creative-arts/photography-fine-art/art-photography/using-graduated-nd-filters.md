# Using graduated ND filters

## Types
- **Hard edge**: abrupt transition; flat horizons (sea).
- **Soft edge**: gradual; uneven horizons, mountains.
- **Reverse grad**: darkest at the centre line, lighter toward top; sunrise/sunset with the sun at
  the horizon.
- Strength: 0.3 (1 stop), 0.6 (2), 0.9 (3), 1.2 (4).

## Procedure
1. Spot-meter the sky (excluding the sun) and the foreground; the difference is the grad strength.
   e.g. sky f/11 1/125, foreground f/11 1/30 = 2 stops -> 0.6 grad.
2. Expose for the foreground.
3. Slide the grad in the holder until the transition sits on the horizon; use DoF preview at the
   taking aperture to see the line (wide apertures make it softer).
4. Check the histogram and look for darkened mountain tops or trees crossing the horizon.

## When to bracket instead
Objects cross the horizon (trees, buildings), dynamic range > 4-5 stops, or the horizon is irregular.
Bracket and blend (luminosity masks) or merge HDR.

## Gotchas
- Resin filters scratch and some go magenta; glass/precision ones stay neutral.
- Lightroom's linear gradient does the same in post only if the sky is not clipped in the raw.

## Verify
- [ ] Sky and land both hold detail, no visible band, no dark tree tops.
