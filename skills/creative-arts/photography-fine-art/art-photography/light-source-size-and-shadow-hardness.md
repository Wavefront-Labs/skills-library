# Light source size and shadow hardness

**Rule:** the larger the light source *as seen from the subject*, the softer the light
(gradual shadow edge, wide transition). Apparent size depends on physical size **and distance**.

## Controls
| To soften | To harden |
|---|---|
| Bigger modifier (softbox, scrim, bounce wall) | Bare bulb, small reflector, Fresnel, sun |
| Move the source **closer** | Move it farther away |
| Diffuse (scrim between sun and subject) | Remove diffusion; use grids/snoots for control |
| Bounce off a large surface | Use a spot / optical snoot |

The sun is tiny in the sky (half a degree) = hard; overcast makes the whole sky the source = soft.

## Procedure
1. Decide shadow edge you want: crisp (graphic, texture, drama) or gradual (forgiving, rounded).
2. Look at the shadow of the nose or object edge; it shows hardness immediately.
3. Adjust distance first: halving distance doubles apparent size (and brightens 4x).
4. Feather (aim the edge of the modifier past the subject) for a softer gradient.

## Gotchas
- A huge softbox far away becomes a small source.
- Soft light reduces texture; for fabric or stone texture use harder, raking light.
- Softness and contrast are different: a soft source with no fill still gives deep shadows.

- [ ] Shadow edge character on the subject matches intention.
