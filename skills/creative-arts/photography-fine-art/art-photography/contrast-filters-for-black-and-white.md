# Contrast filters for black-and-white

A filter **lightens its own colour and darkens its complement** in the monochrome rendering.

| Filter (Wratten) | Filter factor (approx) | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Yellow #8 | 2x (1 stop) | slight sky darkening, "natural" for pan film |
| Orange #21 | 4x (2 stops) | stronger sky, lighter skin, haze cut |
| Red #25 | 8x (3 stops) | dramatic black skies, strong haze penetration, pale lips/skin |
| Deep red #29 | 16-20x | near-infrared drama |
| Green #11 | 4x | lighter foliage, darker red/skin; good for landscapes with greens |
| Blue #47 | 6x | emphasises haze and atmosphere, darkens reds |

## Procedure (film)
1. Decide which tones to separate (blue sky vs white cloud, red brick vs green grass).
2. Pick the filter that lightens the subject you want light.
3. Increase exposure by the filter factor; TTL meters can mis-read red filters - meter without
   filter and apply the factor.
4. Polarizer + red stacks for extreme skies.

## Digital equivalent
Shoot colour raw; in Lightroom B&W Mix or Photoshop Black & White adjustment, lower Blue/Aqua to
darken sky (red filter look) - and you keep all options. Do not shoot through a red filter on digital
(only one channel exposed, noisy).

## Gotchas
- Skies near the sun or on the horizon are not very blue; filters do little there.
- Overcast skies are white light; no filter darkens them.

## Verify
- [ ] Tones you wanted separated are at least about one zone apart in the print.
