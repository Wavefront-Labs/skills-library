# Choosing light modifiers

| Modifier | Quality | Spill | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shoot-through umbrella | soft, wide | lots | fast, cheap, fills room |
| Reflective umbrella | medium soft | moderate | groups, broad fill |
| Softbox (rect) | soft, directional | little to the sides | portrait key, still life; window-like catchlight |
| Octabox | soft, round | little | full-length portraits; round catchlight |
| Strip box | narrow, long | very little | rim/edge lights, glass and bottle highlights |
| Beauty dish | medium hard, crisp centre with gradient | some | beauty, contoured faces |
| Grid (on any of the above) | same quality, controlled spill | none | prevent light hitting background |
| Snoot / optical spot | hard, small circle | none | accent, background pools, gobo patterns |
| Bare reflector | hard | wide | sun simulation, background light |
| Scrim/diffusion frame | very soft, big | - | sun diffusion outdoors, large source in studio |

## Choosing
1. Decide quality (shadow hardness) -> size relative to distance.
2. Decide control: do you need light to stay off the background? -> grid or strip.
3. Decide catchlight shape.
4. Consider space and power: big modifiers lose 1-2 stops; double-diffusion more.

## Gotchas
- A modifier's softness depends on distance; a 1.2 m octa at 4 m is a small source.
- Silver interiors raise contrast and specularity vs white interiors.

- [ ] Modifier choice explains the shadow edge, spill and catchlight you see.
