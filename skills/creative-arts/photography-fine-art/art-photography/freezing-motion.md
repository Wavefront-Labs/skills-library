# Freezing motion

## Two ways to stop motion
1. **Short shutter speed** (ambient light).
2. **Short flash duration** in a dark-ish environment where flash is the only significant light.

## Shutter speed guide
| Subject | Speed |
|---|---|
| Walking person | 1/250 |
| Running, dancing | 1/500-1/1000 |
| Birds in flight, splashing water | 1/2000-1/4000 |
| Water droplets, hummingbird wings | flash (1/10000 or shorter) |
Motion across the frame needs faster speeds than motion toward the camera.

## Flash freezing
- The relevant number is **t.1** (time for 90% of the light), not t.5. Speedlights at low
  power reach 1/20000 s; studio strobes at full power can be 1/300 s - too slow.
- Lower power = shorter duration on most IGBT speedlights; on some studio packs the reverse
  (check spec sheet, or use "freeze" mode).
- Kill ambient: shutter at sync speed, aperture so ambient is 3+ stops under.

## Electronic shutter and rolling shutter
Full electronic shutter reads the sensor line by line; fast subjects skew (propellers,
golf clubs). Use mechanical shutter or a stacked/global-shutter sensor.

## Gotchas
- High speeds need light: raise ISO rather than lose the freeze.
- Hand shake is not the limit at 1/1000; subject motion is.

## Verify
- [ ] Fastest-moving part sharp at 100%; no skew from rolling shutter.
