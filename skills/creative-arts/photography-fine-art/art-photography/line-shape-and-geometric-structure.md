# Line, shape and geometric structure

## Elements
- **Horizontals**: rest, stability. **Verticals**: height, formality; keep them parallel
  (level camera or shift) unless convergence is the point.
- **Diagonals**: movement and depth; a frame corner-to-corner diagonal is the strongest.
- **Curves/S-curves**: guide the eye slowly through space.
- **Implied lines**: gaze direction, gesture, a sequence of similar objects.
- **Shapes**: triangles (three points of interest), circles, rectangles as frames within frames.

## Procedure
1. Find the dominant line first; decide where it enters and leaves the frame. Lines exiting
   exactly through a corner feel resolved; just missing a corner feels like an error.
2. Use small camera moves (10-50 cm, a step left, lower height) to make edges **touch,
   overlap or separate cleanly** - avoid tangents where two edges just kiss.
3. Choose focal length to control how lines converge: wide angles exaggerate diagonals near
   the lens; long lenses flatten into patterns.
4. Level the camera (in-viewfinder level) for architecture; tilt only on purpose.
5. Check in black-and-white preview - geometry reads more clearly without colour.

## Gotchas
- Tangents (a pole growing from a head, horizon through the neck) are the most common
  geometric fault; move, don't crop.
- Wide-angle keystoning reads as accident if slight; either correct fully or exaggerate.

## Verify
- [ ] Every major edge relates to another edge or the frame deliberately.
