# Catchlights and eye light

A catchlight is a direct reflection of a light source in the cornea. It gives eyes life and
reveals your lighting setup.

## Placement
- Classic position: **10-11 o'clock or 1-2 o'clock** in the iris, from a key raised 30-45 deg
  and to one side.
- Key too high or overhead (midday sun, high softbox) = no catchlight, dark sockets.
- One catchlight per eye is cleanest; a second smaller one from a lower reflector is acceptable;
  multiple strong ones look busy.

## Shape
Softbox = rectangle (window-like), octabox/beauty dish = round, ring light = ring around the
pupil, window = window shape with mullions. Choose consciously; the shape reads in big prints.

## Procedure
1. Look at the subject's eyes from the camera position as you place the key.
2. Ask the subject to lift their chin slightly or turn toward the light to bring catchlights in.
3. In open shade, a white reflector at waist height or a bright ground adds a lower catchlight.
4. Retouch: remove extra catchlights (light stands, second light) and keep one consistent pair.

## Gotchas
- Don't paint in fake catchlights inconsistent with the lighting.
- Very large catchlights shrink the visible iris and can look glassy.

## Verify
- [ ] Each eye has a clean catchlight in the same position.
