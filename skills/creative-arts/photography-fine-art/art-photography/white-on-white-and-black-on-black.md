# White-on-white and black-on-black

## White-on-white
1. **Expose for the subject's whites**, not the background: incident meter at the subject,
   then place whites about +1.5 to +2 stops over mid-grey so they are white but hold texture.
2. Background: light it a little *brighter* than the subject (0.3-0.7 stop) so it goes pure
   white while the subject's edges stay slightly greyer - that gives an outline.
3. Use side or top light to create a soft shadow edge on the subject; shadow is what separates.
4. Diffuse family of angles reflections so white glossy surfaces do not glare.

## Black-on-black
1. Expose so the subject's blacks sit at about -2 stops (dark but with texture).
2. Background darker still - more distance, flag it off, use velvet.
3. Separate with **edge/rim lights** and direct reflections from large sources positioned in the
   family of angles of the subject's glossy surfaces - black objects are visible via reflections.
4. Raking light shows texture of black fabric.

## Gotchas
- Auto exposure turns both into grey; use manual exposure.
- Lens flare from a bright white background lowers contrast - use a hood, don't overlight the background.
- Printing: blacks need detail that survives the paper's Dmax; check in soft proof.
