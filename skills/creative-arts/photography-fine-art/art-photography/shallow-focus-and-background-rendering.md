# Shallow focus and background rendering (bokeh)

## What makes the background blurrier
Blur disk size of the background grows with: wider aperture, longer focal length,
**closer subject distance**, and **farther background**. Moving the subject 3 m away from a
wall often does more than going from f/2.8 to f/1.8.

## Procedure for a subject-isolation frame
1. Put distance between subject and background first.
2. Choose a longer focal length (85-135 mm full frame for people) and fill the frame.
3. Open up; at f/1.4-f/2 at close range DoF is centimetres - focus on the near eye.
4. Look at the background **through the lens at taking aperture** (DoF preview or live view).
5. Choose backgrounds with soft highlights (leaves with sun, distant lights) for pleasing disks;
   avoid high-contrast lines (branches) that turn into nervous double-edged blur.

## Bokeh character
- Rounded aperture blades keep disks round when stopped down.
- Cat-eye disks toward the frame edge = optical vignetting; stop down 1/3-2/3 to round them.
- Onion-ring texture comes from aspherical elements; soap-bubble (sharp edged) disks from
  overcorrected spherical aberration (Trioplan style).
- Longitudinal chromatic aberration: magenta fringes in front of focus, green behind. Fix with
  Defringe in Lightroom or stop down.

## Gotchas
- Too shallow in a fine-art context can read as a lens effect rather than a subject; decide
  what must be readable.
- Autofocus at f/1.4 misses by millimetres: use eye-AF or magnified manual focus and shoot several.

## Verify
- [ ] Focus is on the intended plane at 100%; background shapes do not compete with the subject.
