# Lighting polished metal

Polished metal shows almost only **direct reflection**: it is a mirror of whatever surrounds it.
You do not light the metal - you light what it reflects.

## Flat metal (a plate, a blade)
1. Find the family of angles (the area the metal mirrors from the camera viewpoint).
2. To make it **bright**: fill that area with a large, evenly lit diffusion panel.
3. To make it **dark**: fill it with black card - metal goes black; light the edges or texture.
4. Using a gradient on the panel (feather the light) gives a graded reflection that reads as metal.

## Curved / round metal (a cup, cutlery)
- The family of angles is huge; surround the object with a **light tent** or a scrim on three
  sides, with the camera poking through a slit.
- Add black strips of card inside the tent to put dark "definition" lines along edges.

## Keep the camera out of the reflection
- Use a longer lens from farther away; drape black cloth around the camera; or shift/tilt the
  lens slightly off-axis (perspective control lens) so the camera is not in the mirror.

## Gotchas
- Fingerprints show at 100%: gloves and a final wipe with microfibre.
- Polarizers do nothing on metal reflections.
- Dulling spray exists but changes surface character; use only as last resort.

## Verify
- [ ] Metal reads as bright and shaped by highlights/dark lines; no camera or room reflections.
