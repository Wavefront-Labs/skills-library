# Researching a photographic project

## Procedure
1. **Working question**: one sentence, open not illustrative ("What does a town keep when its
   factory closes?" rather than "Pictures of the closed factory").
2. **Precedents**: list 10-20 photographers/artists who worked near the subject or method;
   look at their books (library, photobook archives). Note what you would do differently.
3. **Subject knowledge**: read history, talk to people, visit without camera. Specialists and
   archives often offer access that changes the work.
4. **Access**: who must agree (landowners, institutions, sitters)? Write to them early with a
   short project description and examples.
5. **Constraints**: format, time of day, distance, colour/B&W - decide provisional rules.
6. **Notebook**: keep reading notes, contacts, locations, shoot logs and edits in one place.
7. Re-read the question after the first shoots; let it change.

## Gotchas
- Over-researching delays shooting; set a date for the first shoot within weeks.
- Avoid copying a precedent's look; knowing it well is how you avoid that.
