# Near-far wide-angle composition

The "near-far" landscape puts a strong foreground very close to a wide lens (14-24 mm FF)
so it looms large, and links it to a distant subject.

## Procedure
1. Find the distant subject first (mountain, sky), then search for a foreground that echoes or
   leads to it (shape, colour, line).
2. Get low and close: often 30-60 cm from the foreground; tilt down slightly.
3. Arrange the foreground so it does not block the background and points into the frame.
4. Check corners: wide lenses stretch the edges; keep important objects out of corners.
5. Sharpness: hyperfocal at f/11 is often not enough at 30 cm; **focus stack** 2-5 frames or use tilt.

## Gotchas
- Too much empty middle ground breaks the link: move closer or choose a longer lens.
- Vertical lines converge when the camera tilts down; that is accepted in nature, ugly on buildings.
- Polarizer on ultra-wide = uneven sky.

## Verify
- [ ] Foreground, middle and background all sharp at 100%; clear visual route from near to far.
