# Exposure compensation for bright and dark scenes

In aperture priority, shutter priority, program and manual-with-Auto-ISO, the camera
picks exposure from the meter. **Exposure compensation (EV +/-)** biases that choice.

## Starting values (adjust from the histogram)
| Scene | Typical compensation |
|---|---|
| Snow, white sand, bright fog | +1 to +2 EV |
| Backlit subject, matrix metering | +1 to +1.7 EV |
| Mostly white subject on white | +1 to +1.5 |
| Dark interior, black cloth, low-key portrait | -1 to -2 EV |
| Small bright subject on large dark ground (stage) | -1 to -2 EV |
| Sunset with sun in frame | -0.7 to -1.5 to hold sky colour |

## Procedure
1. Take a test frame with 0 EV. Read the histogram, not the screen.
2. Ask: where *should* the main tone sit? Snow should be bright with texture, not grey.
3. Dial compensation in thirds; re-shoot; enable highlight blinkies.
4. In manual mode with Auto ISO, compensation changes ISO; in plain manual it only
   moves the meter needle (it does nothing to exposure).
5. Reset to 0 when the scene changes - the most common error is leaving +2 on.

## Gotchas
- Compensation is applied relative to the metering mode; +1 on spot is different from +1 on matrix.
- With flash, many bodies have a separate flash compensation; ambient EV may also affect flash output depending on custom settings.
- Bracketing ranges are centred on the compensated value.

## Verify
- [ ] Main subject tone looks right on the histogram (snow near the right edge, not middle).
- [ ] Compensation reset after the sequence.
