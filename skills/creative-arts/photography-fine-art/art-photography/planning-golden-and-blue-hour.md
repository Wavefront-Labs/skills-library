# Planning golden and blue hour

## Definitions (approximate, latitude dependent)
- **Golden hour**: sun from about +6 deg altitude down to -4 deg; warm, low, long shadows.
- **Blue hour**: sun between about -4 and -6 deg (in civil twilight); sky deep blue, city lights
  balanced with sky.
- Civil twilight ends at -6 deg, nautical -12, astronomical -18.
- Near the equator these periods last ~20 min; at high latitudes in summer, hours.

## Procedure
1. In PhotoPills (Planner) or TPE, drop the red pin on your camera position.
2. Read sunrise/sunset azimuth and the golden/blue hour times for the date.
3. Drop the subject pin; check whether the sun lines up behind/beside it and whether hills
   block the sun earlier than sunset (use elevation profile / geodetics).
4. Check the moon phase and rise time if wanted in frame.
5. Arrive 45 min before: compose in daylight, focus, set filters.
6. Shoot through the sequence; the best light often comes 10-20 min *after* sunset.

## Gotchas
- Sunset times assume a flat horizon; mountains steal 30+ minutes.
- Auto white balance neutralises golden light; set daylight WB (5200-5500 K) to keep it.
- For blue hour cityscapes, the balance point where lights and sky match is short; bracket.
