# Lighting glass: bright-field and dark-field

Glass is nearly invisible; you photograph its **edges**, which refract light from behind and
the sides. Two clean approaches:

## Bright-field (dark edges on a light background)
1. Light background (lit white wall or a softbox) behind the glass, sized just to fill the frame.
2. Leave the area *outside* the frame dark - edges then refract the darkness and draw dark lines.
3. Too large a background = edges disappear; shrink the lit area or bring in black cards at
   the sides just out of frame.

## Dark-field (light edges on a black background)
1. Large light source behind, with a smaller black card in its centre (or the glass in front of
   black velvet) so the frame sees only black.
2. Light from the surrounding bright ring refracts through the edges, which glow.
3. Adjust black card size: too small and light spills into the frame; too big and edges go dark.

## Additions
- A strip softbox to the side adds a long vertical highlight that says "gloss".
- Liquids: back-light coloured drinks; a white card behind the liquid only makes colour glow.
- Use glycerine for fake condensation.

## Gotchas
- Dust and fingerprints are fatal: clean glass with lint-free cloth and handle by the stem with gloves.
- Avoid reflections of the camera; black cloth around the lens.

## Verify
- [ ] Glass edges defined continuously; background clean; no stray highlights.
