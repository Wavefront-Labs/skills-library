# Broad and short lighting

Applies when the face is turned away from the camera (two-thirds or profile view), so one side
of the face is closer to the camera (the broad side).

- **Short lighting**: key lights the side turned **away** from the camera; the broad side is in
  shadow. Slims the face, adds depth and mood. Default for most portraits.
- **Broad lighting**: key lights the side **toward** the camera. Widens the face, lowers drama;
  useful for thin faces or to hide texture on the far side; glasses glare more.

## Procedure
1. Turn the subject's face about 30-45 deg from the camera.
2. For short: place the key on the side the nose points toward (opposite the camera side).
3. Check the far eye still gets a catchlight and the near cheek has some fill.
4. For broad: move the key around to the camera side of the face.

## Gotchas
- Short lighting with no fill can lose the near eye in shadow; add a reflector.
- With a frontal (face toward camera) pose there is no broad/short distinction.

## Verify
- [ ] Face shape looks as intended (slimmer or fuller) and both eyes are readable.
