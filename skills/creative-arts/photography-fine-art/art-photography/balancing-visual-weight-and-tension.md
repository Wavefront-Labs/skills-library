# Balancing visual weight and tension

## What carries visual weight
Brighter or higher-contrast areas, saturated warm colour, larger shapes, sharp areas over
blurred, human faces and eyes, text, isolated elements, and anything near the frame edge.

## Balance types
- **Symmetrical**: weight centred; calm, formal, frontal (Becher-style typologies, architecture).
- **Asymmetrical**: a large weak mass balanced by a small strong one placed farther from centre (lever principle).
- **Deliberate imbalance**: everything weighted to one edge; produces tension and expectancy.

## Procedure
1. Squint or blur the image (or view a tiny thumbnail): weights become tone masses.
2. Identify the two or three heaviest elements; locate them relative to the centre.
3. Decide: should the frame rest or pull? Move position (not just crop) to adjust.
4. Flip the image horizontally in editing: if it suddenly feels wrong, the balance
   depended on left-to-right reading; decide if that is intended.
5. Check edges: a bright patch at the edge drags the eye out; burn it or recompose.

## Gotchas
- Centring is not a mistake; centring a subject in a square frame is a strong formal choice.
- Rule-of-thirds is a starting habit, not balance; weight depends on tone and meaning too.

## Verify
- [ ] You can name the heaviest element and say why its position is intended.
- [ ] No unintentional bright edge pulls attention out of frame.
