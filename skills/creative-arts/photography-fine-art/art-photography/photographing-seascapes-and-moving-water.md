# Photographing seascapes and moving water

## Shutter choices
| Look | Time |
|---|---|
| Frozen spray | 1/500+ |
| Wave texture with direction (most expressive) | 0.2-0.8 s |
| Soft mist | 2-8 s |
| Flat glass | 30 s+ (10-stop ND) |
Receding waves at 0.3-1 s produce "drag lines" over rocks - fire as the wave starts to retreat.

## Procedure
1. Check tides (NOAA/UKHO or local tables) and swell forecast; mid to falling tide often exposes rocks safely.
2. Set up on stable rock; never turn your back on the sea; watch sets for 10 min first.
3. Remote release, burst during wave action; shoot dozens - every wave is different.
4. Use a 3-6 stop ND in daylight for 0.5 s; a polarizer reduces glare on wet rocks.
5. Wipe the front element between frames (spray); a lens hood helps.

## Gear care
Rinse tripod legs in fresh water after salt water; wipe camera with damp cloth; dry filters.

## Gotchas
- Sneaker waves: stay well above the wet line; keep an exit route.
- Long exposures on soft sand: legs sink - push them in firmly.

## Verify
- [ ] Wave lines read as intended direction; rocks sharp; no spray spots on the lens.
