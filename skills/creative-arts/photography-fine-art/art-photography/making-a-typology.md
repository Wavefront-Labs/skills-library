# Making a typology

A typology shows many examples of one class (water towers, houses, faces) under identical
conditions, so the viewer compares form.

## Capture rules
1. **Frontal, centred**, subject filling a consistent proportion of the frame.
2. **Even, shadowless light**: overcast sky (Bechers shot under overcast to avoid shadows),
   or even flash in studio.
3. Same camera height, slightly elevated for buildings (scaffold, ladder) to reduce convergence;
   use shift or a view camera to keep verticals parallel.
4. Same lens, same distance or same subject size; same format; black-and-white or neutral colour.
5. Record location, date, and dimensions in a log.

## Presentation
- Grid of 6, 9, 12 or 15 prints of identical size, aligned with equal gutters.
- Captions with place and date; no narrative sequence.

## Gotchas
- One off-angle or sunny frame breaks the grid - reshoot rather than include.
- Choose a class with genuine variation; otherwise the grid is repetition without interest.

## Verify
- [ ] In the grid, the only visible differences are in the subjects themselves.
