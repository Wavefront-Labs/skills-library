# Using a polarizing filter

Non-metallic glossy surfaces (water, wet leaves, glass, paint) reflect partly polarized light,
and skylight is polarized most at **90 deg from the sun**. A polarizer removes some of that light.

## Procedure
1. Use a **circular** polarizer (CPL) on autofocus/metering cameras.
2. Rotate while watching: glare on water/leaves drops, colour saturation rises.
3. For skies, point a finger at the sun with thumb up - the arc your thumb sweeps is max polarization.
4. Expect 1.3-2 stops light loss; re-meter.
5. Stop rotating at the effect you want - full removal of water reflections can make lakes look dead.

## Cross-polarization (studio)
Polarizing gel on the light + polarizer on the lens at 90 deg removes all direct reflection
from non-metallic surfaces: copying paintings, glossy prints, skin shine. Metals still reflect
(metal reflection is not polarized).

## Gotchas
- Wide lenses (< 24 mm FF) see a range of angles to the sun: uneven dark band in the sky.
- No effect on metal reflections or on light sources.
- Rainbows can disappear when rotated wrongly.
- Stacking with ND causes vignetting on wide lenses; use thin/slim mounts.

## Verify
- [ ] Glare reduced on intended surfaces; sky even across the frame.
