# Ground glass focusing and focus spread

## Viewing the image
- **Dark cloth** over head and camera back; block all light; a reflective outer side keeps it cool in sun.
- **Fresnel lens** (between ground glass and lens, or behind) brightens corners - can shift focus slightly
  if mounted on the wrong side; check calibration.
- Open the lens to its widest aperture for composing.
- **Loupe**: 4x-8x, focused on the ground glass texture (adjust diopter to see grain of the glass sharply).

## Focus spread method (choosing aperture)
1. Focus on the nearest point you need sharp; mark the rail position (tape/millimetre scale).
2. Focus on the farthest point; mark again.
3. Measure the distance between marks = **focus spread** (mm).
4. Set focus at the **midpoint** between marks.
5. Look up aperture from a focus spread table for your circle of confusion, e.g. (4x5, c about 0.1 mm):
   focus spread 2 mm -> f/11, 3 mm -> f/16, 4 mm -> f/22, 6 mm -> f/32, 8 mm -> f/45 (approx; use a
   published table or N = spread / (2 x c)).
6. Cameras like Linhof Technikardan and Arca-Swiss offer depth-of-field scales/rails.

## Gotchas
- Stopping down to judge DoF on the ground glass is unreliable (too dark); use the spread method.
- Check your ground glass is at the same plane as the film in holders (calibration).

## Verify
- [ ] Final aperture from focus spread; loupe check of near and far points.
