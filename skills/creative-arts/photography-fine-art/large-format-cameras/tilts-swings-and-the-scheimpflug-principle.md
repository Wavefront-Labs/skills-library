# Tilts, swings and the Scheimpflug principle

## Principles
- **Scheimpflug**: the film plane, the lens plane and the plane of sharp focus meet in a single line.
- **Hinge rule** (Merklinger): the plane of focus pivots around a hinge line at distance J below the lens,
  where J = f / sin(tilt). Small tilts (2-5 deg) move the hinge far down - ideal for landscapes.
- **Tilt** = rotation around a horizontal axis (ground planes); **swing** = rotation around a vertical axis
  (a wall receding to one side).

## Procedure (front tilt for a landscape)
1. Zero all movements; compose.
2. Focus on a far point (top of ground glass) with focus knob.
3. Focus on a near point (bottom of ground glass) **with the tilt** - tilt forward a little.
4. Refocus far with the focus knob; repeat near with tilt; converge in 2-4 iterations.
5. Stop down for depth around the tilted plane (tall objects like trees need DoF).

## Axis vs base tilts
- **Axis tilt**: pivot passes through the lens axis/middle of the standard; focus on the axis stays fixed
  while tilting - easier iteration (choose the axis point on the near or far object).
- **Base tilt** (most field cameras): pivot at the bottom; tilting shifts focus everywhere; needs more refocusing.
- Use **zero detents** to return to neutral exactly; check with a spirit level after packing/unpacking.

## Rear tilt/swing
Changes shape (perspective/keystoning) as well as focus - use for deliberate distortion (enlarge
foreground objects) or with product shots.

## Gotchas
- Tilt doesn't help with tall objects across the frame - they cross the plane of focus.
- Excess tilt makes the plane of focus pass beneath the near foreground objects.

## Verify
- [ ] Near and far points sharp at taking aperture with a loupe; movements recorded.
