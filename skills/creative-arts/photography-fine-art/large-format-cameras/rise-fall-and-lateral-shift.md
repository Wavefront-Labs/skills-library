# Rise, fall and lateral shift

## What they do
Rise/fall and shift move the lens (front standard) or film (rear standard) **parallel** to each other.
The film plane stays vertical, so verticals stay parallel; you are choosing a different part of the
lens's image circle.

## Rise (architecture)
1. Level the camera exactly: both standards vertical (spirit levels on rear standard), bed level.
2. The building's top is cut off: **raise the front standard** (or drop the rear) until it fits.
3. Don't tilt the camera upward - that is what causes converging verticals.
4. Check corners on the ground glass for darkening (vignetting = image circle limit) with the lens at
   taking aperture; use the cut corners of the ground glass to look through toward the lens - you must see
   the whole aperture from each corner.

## Fall
For subjects below eye level (tabletop, landscape foreground from a high viewpoint) - lower the front.

## Lateral shift
Move the front sideways to frame a subject to the side while keeping the film parallel to a facade
(e.g. avoid your reflection in a mirror or window while facing it squarely).

## Front vs rear
Front movements change framing without changing perspective; rear rise/shift changes the film position
similarly but also slightly moves the viewpoint relative to the subject - in practice front is preferred.

## Gotchas
- Combined rise + shift uses the image circle diagonally - runs out faster.
- Extreme rise on wide lenses: bellows binding; use a bag bellows.

## Verify
- [ ] Verticals parallel on ground glass; no vignetting at corners at taking aperture.
