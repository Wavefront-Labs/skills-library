# Selenium and gold toning

## Selenium
- Converts some silver to silver selenide: **protects** against oxidation and **deepens blacks**
  (increases Dmax), with colour shift (warm papers go purple-brown; neutral papers shift slightly).
- Dilution: Kodak Rapid Selenium Toner 1+9 to 1+20 (for archival protection), stronger 1+3 for colour shift.
  Often diluted in hypo clearing agent working solution.
- Process: fixed, washed (or after first wash) print in toner, 2-8 min with agitation. Compare against an
  untoned wet print under good light. Then hypo clear and full wash.
- Prints toned to completion protect best; even a light tone adds protection.

## Gold
- **Gold protective toner (GP-1)**: gold chloride + sodium thiocyanate; gives a slight blue-black on
  neutral papers and very good permanence; also used on salt and printing-out papers.
- Gold after sepia gives red/orange tones (gold over sepia).
- Gold toners are expensive and exhaust; use small volumes, one-shot.

## Safety
Selenium is toxic: gloves, ventilation, never drain (hazardous waste). Gold chloride is corrosive.

## Gotchas
- Selenium-toned prints may show staining if fixing was incomplete - two-bath fix first.
- Colour change shows more in warm-tone papers; test each paper.

## Verify
- [ ] Toned and untoned test prints compared; full wash and residual tests pass.
