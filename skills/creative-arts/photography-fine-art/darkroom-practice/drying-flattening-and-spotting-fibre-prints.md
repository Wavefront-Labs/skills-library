# Drying, flattening and spotting fibre prints

## Drying
1. Squeegee both sides lightly on clean glass or plexi.
2. **Fibreglass screens**: lay prints face down on screens to dry in still air. Curl is reduced face-down.
3. Alternatively, archival blotters (photo blotter books) - change blotters between batches.
4. Do not use heat drum dryers for archival work unless dedicated and clean.

## Flattening
- **Dry-mount press**: sandwich the dry print between two clean 4-ply museum boards or release paper,
  press at about 80-90 deg C for 30-60 s, then cool **under weight** (glass/books) for several minutes.
- Without press: slightly re-dampen the back, place between blotters under heavy weight overnight.

## Spotting (white specks from dust on negatives)
1. Dyes: Spotone (discontinued but available), Marshall's Spot-All, Peerless; match neutral, warm or cold tone.
2. Use a fine brush (00-000 sable). Load, then wipe almost dry on paper.
3. Build density with **tiny stippled dots**, not strokes; lighter than you think - dyes darken.
4. Work under good light with a magnifier; begin in the darkest areas surrounding the speck.
5. Black specks (scratches/pinholes on the negative printing black): etch with a scalpel (fibre) or bleach
   locally, then spot.

## Gotchas
- Glossy fibre shows dye sheen; spotting on matte surfaces is less visible.
- Spotting dye on RC sits on top; practise on a reject print.

## Verify
- [ ] Prints lie flat; spotted areas invisible at arm's length under exhibition light.
