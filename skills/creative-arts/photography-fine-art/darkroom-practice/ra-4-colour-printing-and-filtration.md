# RA-4 colour printing and filtration

## Processing
- Chemistry: colour developer + bleach-fix (blix); paper such as Kodak Endura or Fuji Crystal Archive.
- **Total darkness** (no safelight for practical purposes; very dim specific safelight at best).
- Drum (Jobo/Paterson drum) at 35 deg C: developer 45 s, blix 45 s, wash 90 s (per chemistry instructions).
  Room-temp tray processing with extended times is possible but less consistent.

## Filtration basics
Negative printing uses **yellow (Y) and magenta (M)** filtration; cyan usually stays at 0.
To correct a cast **add the same colour filter** as the cast (for negatives):
| Print looks too | Change |
|---|---|
| yellow | add Y |
| magenta | add M |
| cyan | subtract Y and M equally (or add C) |
| red | add Y and M |
| green | subtract M |
| blue | subtract Y |
Starting filtration often about 50-70 Y, 40-60 M (enlarger/paper dependent).

## Procedure
1. Make an exposure test strip at a starting filtration.
2. Then a **ring-around**: same exposure with +/-10 and +/-20 Y and M variations.
3. Judge dry prints under daylight-balanced viewing light; use viewing filters to estimate correction.
4. Change exposure after large filtration changes (more filter = more exposure).

## Gotchas
- Judge dry prints; wet ones look different.
- Mixed lighting in the negative cannot all be corrected.

## Verify
- [ ] Neutral greys neutral in the dry print under 5000 K viewing light.
