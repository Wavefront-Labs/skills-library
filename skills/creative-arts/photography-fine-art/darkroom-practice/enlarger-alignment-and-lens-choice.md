# Enlarger alignment and lens choice

## Why align
If the negative carrier, lens board and baseboard are not parallel, one side of the print goes soft
(and you stop down to hide it, losing sharpness to diffraction).

## Alignment procedure
1. Level the baseboard (spirit level), then the negative stage, then the lens board - all to the same reading.
   Basic spirit levels get you close.
2. Better: a **laser alignment tool** (Versalab Parallel) or mirror tool: place the mirror tool on the baseboard,
   a mirror/laser in the negative carrier; adjust stage/lens tilt until the reflection returns on itself.
3. Check: focus a grain-focused print at wide aperture at the centre and four corners; all should be sharp.
4. Lock all adjustment screws; recheck after moving the head.

## Enlarger lens focal length
| Format | Standard lens | Wide-angle option |
|---|---|---|
| 35 mm | 50 mm | 40 mm (bigger prints at same height) |
| 6x6 / 6x7 | 80 / 90-105 mm | - |
| 4x5 | 135-150 mm | 120 mm |
Use 6-element apochromatic designs (Rodagon, Componon-S, El-Nikkor N) for big prints. Best aperture: 2-3 stops down.

## Light source
- **Condenser**: more contrast and apparent sharpness; shows dust and scratches (Callier effect).
- **Diffusion / colour head**: softer, hides defects; filtration via dial-in yellow/magenta for VC paper.
- **Cold light** (fluorescent grid): very soft, short life consistency issues.
- **LED heads** (Heiland, Ilford Multigrade 500-style controllers): stable contrast control.

## Verify
- [ ] Wide-open corner sharpness matches centre; lens and source suit the negative format.
