# Silver recovery and chemical disposal

## Why
Used fixer contains dissolved silver (often 1-6 g/L at exhaustion), toxic to aquatic life; many water
authorities restrict silver discharge. Toners (selenium), dichromates, heavy-metal alternative process
chemistry are also hazardous.

## Methods
- **Collect** used fixer in labelled containers; take to a hazardous waste site or a photo-lab recovery service.
- **Steel wool (metallic replacement) cartridges**: fixer passes through steel wool; silver plates out as
  sludge; effluent still has iron and thiosulfate.
- **Electrolytic recovery units**: plate silver onto a cathode; used by labs; allows fixer reuse in some cases.
- Small amounts: let precipitate silver with steel wool in a bucket for some days, then dispose of sludge as hazardous.

## Other chemistry
- Developer (used) and stop bath: usually acceptable to drain in small home amounts with plenty of water where
  regulations permit - check local rules.
- Selenium toner, dichromate, potassium ferricyanide, silver nitrate solutions, cyanide (collodion): **never**
  drain; hazardous waste.
- Septic systems: don't pour fixer/developer at all.

## Gotchas
- Never mix acids with sulfide/cyanide-containing waste (toxic gas).
- Keep a log of volumes for studio/school compliance.

## Verify
- [ ] Every waste stream has a defined disposal route consistent with local regulations.
