# Zone System with roll film and digital

## Roll film
A roll holds many scenes but gets one development. Approaches:
1. **Dedicate rolls/backs**: interchangeable backs (Hasselblad, Mamiya) or separate bodies labelled N,
   N-1, N+1.
2. **Expose for shadows, develop for the most contrasty scene type of the roll** (usually slight N-),
   then use variable-contrast paper grades to adjust each frame.
3. **Cut the roll**: shoot a few blank frames between scenes and cut in the dark to develop sections differently
   (practical for 120 only with care).
4. Place shadows on Zone III-IV for safety; slight overexposure costs little in roll film.

## Digital
- The sensor has a fixed "development"; contrast control moves to raw processing and exposure.
- **Place highlights**, not shadows: spot-meter the brightest area needing texture and place it about
  2.5-3 stops above meter (sensor-dependent; test where clipping begins).
- Read shadows: if they fall below about 8-10 stops under the highlight, bracket and blend.
- Develop in Lightroom with Tone Curve/Shadows to place values (the print stage still matters).

## Gotchas
- Applying sheet-film N-2 thinking to roll film creates unprintable frames elsewhere on the roll.
