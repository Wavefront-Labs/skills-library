# Paper flashing and local bleaching

## Flashing (bring in difficult highlights)
Paper has a threshold before it records tone. A **flash** (uniform, non-image exposure) just below
threshold makes highlights respond sooner without visibly greying paper white.
1. Remove the negative (or use a clear area), lens stopped down; make a test strip of flash exposures.
2. Find the longest flash that leaves no visible tone after development (threshold).
3. Use about 70-90% of that time as the flash; then expose the image.
4. Local flashing: shape with a card or a small torch to flash only a sky or a bright window.

## Bleaching (brighten highlights)
**Potassium ferricyanide** (with fixer = Farmer's reducer) dissolves silver - lightening highlights,
adding sparkle to eyes, whites or edges.
1. Stock: potassium ferricyanide 10% solution; working: a few drops in a little water (pale yellow).
2. On a fixed, washed, wet print, squeegee the area; apply with a brush or cotton swab.
3. Rinse in water frequently; return the print to fixer to stop and clear, judging gradually.
4. Re-fix and wash fully (bleached prints are archival only if washed thoroughly).

## Safety
Ferricyanide is low toxicity but never mix with acids in heat (cyanide gas risk); gloves; dispose properly.

## Gotchas
- Over-flashing greys highlights; under-bleaching then over-bleaching creates yellow stains.
- Bleaching works faster on highlights than shadows (proportional); stop early.

## Verify
- [ ] Highlights have texture without grey veils; bleached areas blend without edges or stains.
