# Salted paper prints

Talbot's process: silver chloride formed in the paper fibres, printed out by UV.

## Salting
- 2% sodium chloride (or ammonium chloride) solution with 1-2% gelatin (or arrowroot starch for matte) and
  optionally sodium citrate for contrast/colour.
- Float or brush onto cotton paper; dry. Salted paper keeps indefinitely.

## Sensitizing
- 12% silver nitrate (some add citric acid); brush coat in subdued tungsten light; dry in the dark.
- Use within a day.

## Printing
1. Contact print in sunlight/UV in a split-back frame; **print-out**: inspect by opening half the back.
2. Print until noticeably darker than the final (shadows bronzed) - toning and fixing lighten.
3. Wash in water with a pinch of salt until milkiness (free silver) is gone.

## Toning and fixing
- Gold toner (gold chloride with borax or the thiocyanate formula) shifts red-brown to purple-brown and
  adds permanence; do it **before fixing**.
- Fix in **10% sodium thiosulfate** (with a little sodium carbonate) 2 baths x 3-5 min - do not use rapid fixer.
- Hypo clear, wash 30+ min.

## Gotchas
- Fixed salt prints lose a lot of density - overprint substantially.
- Measles/spots from contaminated trays/brushes; use dedicated glass/plastic.

## Verify
- [ ] Toned, fixed, washed print with expected colour and no yellow highlights.
