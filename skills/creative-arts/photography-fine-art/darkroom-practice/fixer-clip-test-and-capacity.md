# Fixer clip test and capacity

## Clip test
1. Put a drop of fixer on a film leader scrap (or immerse a strip) at working temperature with agitation.
2. Time how long until the film becomes fully transparent (milky emulsion clears) = **clearing time**.
3. Fix film for **2x clearing time** (Ilford guidance; some use 3x for T-grain films).
4. Fixer is exhausted when the clearing time is about **double** that of fresh fixer.

Typical fresh rapid fixer (ammonium thiosulfate, 1+4 for film): clearing about 30-60 s for traditional films;
T-grain (T-Max, Delta) take longer.

## Capacity (rough, check datasheet)
- Ilford Rapid Fixer 1+4: around 24 rolls 135-36 per litre for film.
- Paper fixer 1+4 (Ilford): around 40 8x10 RC per litre for archival use rather than the maximum.
- Keep separate fixer for film and paper; film depletes it faster.

## Paper fixing
Paper can't be clip-tested easily; use time limits from the datasheet and **count prints**. Fibre
paper: short fix (1 min at film strength) with two-bath method is preferred.

## Gotchas
- Dissolved silver load matters more than clearing time for archival paper processing.
- Over-fixing fibre prints lets thiosulfate into the base (hard to wash out) and can bleach highlights.

## Verify
- [ ] Clearing time logged per session; fixer replaced at 2x fresh clearing time or count limit.
