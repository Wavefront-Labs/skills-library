# Developing film and making a silver gelatin print

**Goal:** turn an exposed black-and-white roll into consistent negatives and a
finished, archival print with deliberate tone and contrast.

## 1. Developing roll film
**Kit:** daylight tank and reels, changing bag or dark room, thermometer, timer,
graduates, developer, stop bath (or water), fixer, wetting agent.
1. **Load the reel in total darkness** (practise with a dummy roll in daylight first).
2. Bring all chemicals to the **same temperature**, typically 20 °C. Temperature
   differences cause grain clumping or reticulation.
3. **Developer:** pour in, start timer, agitate per datasheet — a common regime is
   continuous inversion for the first 30 s, then 10 s (about 4 inversions) every
   minute. Tap the tank to dislodge air bubbles. *Example only:* HP5 Plus in D-76
   stock around 7.5 min at 20 °C — check the current Ilford/Kodak sheet; times vary by
   film, developer, dilution and how you want contrast.
4. **Stop:** 30–60 s acid stop or two water rinses.
5. **Fix:** per datasheet (rapid fixer often 3–5 min for film). Test fixer exhaustion
   with a film clip: clearing time × 2 = fix time.
6. **Wash:** e.g. the Ilford method (fill, invert 5 times, dump; then 10, then 20) or
   running water; hypo-clearing agent shortens washing.
7. Wetting agent, hang in a dust-free place, cut and sleeve when dry.
- **More development = more contrast.** Over-developed negatives have blocked
  highlights; under-developed are flat. Adjust time once you see consistent results.

## 2. Contact sheet and test strip
- Lay negatives emulsion-down on paper under glass; expose to make a contact sheet.
  Use it to choose frames, not to judge final tone.
- **Test strip:** put the negative in the enlarger, focus with a grain focuser,
  stop down 2 stops from wide open. Expose a strip in steps (e.g. 3 s increments or
  f-stop timing increments) across important areas.
- Choose exposure by the **highlights** (they should just hold detail); adjust
  contrast for the **shadows**.

## 3. Contrast with multigrade paper
- Variable-contrast (multigrade) papers change contrast with filtration: grade 0–1 soft,
  2–3 normal, 4–5 hard.
- Change contrast, re-test: higher grades may need different exposure (depends on the
  filter set).
- **Split-grade printing:** separate exposures at grade 00 (highlights) and grade 5
  (shadows) gives fine control.

## 4. Dodging and burning
- **Dodge** (hold back light) to lighten shadow areas; **burn** (add light through a
  hole in card) to darken skies and bright edges.
- Keep tools moving to avoid hard edges. Record each move on a **printing map**
  (sketch with seconds and grades) so the print is repeatable.

## 5. Processing the print
- Developer (RC ~1 min, fibre ~2–3 min), stop, fix (per datasheet; don't over-fix
  fibre), wash (RC short; fibre long, with hypo-clear), dry flat.
- Judge prints under light similar to display light; wet prints "dry down" slightly
  darker.

## Safety
Ventilate; wear gloves and eye protection when mixing; never eat in the darkroom.
Dispose of used fixer (contains silver) as local regulations require — not down the
drain in many areas.

## Gotchas
- Light leaks in the room: leave paper out 5 min under the safelight, develop it —
  any fog means the safelight or room isn't safe.
- Dust on negatives prints as white specks; clean with an anti-static brush.
- Uncertain: development times above are illustrative; datasheets are authoritative.

## Verify
- [ ] Negatives show shadow detail and printable highlights.
- [ ] Fix time based on a clearing test; wash method documented.
- [ ] Test strip taken; exposure chosen for highlights.
- [ ] Printing map with grades, times, dodges and burns recorded.
- [ ] Safelight fog test passed.
