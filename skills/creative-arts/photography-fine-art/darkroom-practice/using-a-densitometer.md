# Using a densitometer

## Concepts
- **Density** D = log10(1 / transmittance). D 0.3 = half the light, 1 stop.
- Transmission densitometers measure negatives; reflection densitometers measure prints.
- Black-and-white: use visual (V) channel; colour: status M for negatives, status A for prints.

## Procedure
1. Warm up; calibrate on the supplied reference (zero on air, check against a known patch).
2. Zero on an unexposed area of the developed film to read **net density** (above FB+F), or read
   absolute and subtract.
3. Measure test frames (Zone I, V, VIII) or a step tablet contact.
4. Negative **density range** (highlight minus shadow) maps to paper grade: diffusion enlarger about
   1.05-1.15 for grade 2 (varies by paper; ISO R value).

## Uses
- Film speed and development tests, comparing developers, checking enlarger light-source consistency,
  calibrating digital negatives, monitoring process control strips (C-41/RA-4).

## Without a dedicated unit
A spot meter on a light box, or a scanner with a calibrated step tablet, gives approximate densities.

## Gotchas
- Dust or fingerprints on the reading area add density.
- Reflection density of prints is limited by paper Dmax (about 2.0-2.3 glossy fibre, ~1.6-1.7 matte inkjet).

- [ ] Instrument reads the reference step tablet within its stated tolerance.
