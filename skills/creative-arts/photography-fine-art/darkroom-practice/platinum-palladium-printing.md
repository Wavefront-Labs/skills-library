# Platinum/palladium printing

## Chemistry (Bostick & Sullivan style kit)
- Solution 1: ferric oxalate (about 27%).
- Solution 2: ferric oxalate + potassium chlorate (contrast agent) - OR use **Na2** (sodium chloroplatinate)
  drops for contrast with palladium.
- Metal solutions: sodium chloropalladite (palladium, warmer) and/or potassium chloroplatinite (platinum, cooler).
- Developer: potassium oxalate (warm) or ammonium citrate (neutral); used warm for warmer tones.
- Clearing: 3 baths of EDTA (tetrasodium EDTA + sodium sulfite) or citric acid.

## Drop counts (8x10, as a starting point)
About 24 drops total: 12 metal + 12 ferric (split between solution 1 and 2 by desired contrast).
More solution 2 or Na2 drops = more contrast (for flatter negatives).

## Procedure
1. Coat 100% cotton paper (Arches Platine, Revere Platinum) with a glass rod (Puddle Pusher) or brush,
   humidity about 50-60%; dry 10-20 min in the dark then gentle warm air.
2. Contact print in UV (unit or sun); faint visible image.
3. Develop: pour developer over the print in one smooth motion; 1-2 min.
4. Clear in three EDTA baths 5 min each; wash 20-30 min; dry.

## Negatives
Needs a long density range (about 1.8-2.0): pyro-developed film or calibrated digital negatives.

## Safety
Metal salts and oxalates are toxic and sensitising; chlorate is an oxidiser. Gloves, ventilation.

## Verify
- [ ] Full tonal scale with clean highlights (no yellow iron stain after clearing).
