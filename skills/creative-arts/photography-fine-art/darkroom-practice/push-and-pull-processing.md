# Push and pull processing

## What it does
- **Push** = underexpose (rate film at higher EI) and overdevelop. Development raises mid-tones and
  highlights; **shadow detail is not recovered** - shadows are simply underexposed. Result: more contrast,
  more grain, thin shadows.
- **Pull** = overexpose (lower EI) and underdevelop: lower contrast, finer apparent grain, rich shadows.

## Times
Use the manufacturer's push/pull table (Ilford and Kodak datasheets list EIs with times per developer).
Rule of thumb when no table exists: about +30-50% time per stop push; -20-30% per stop pull.
E.g. HP5 Plus at EI 1600 needs substantially longer than box speed in DD-X/ID-11 - read the sheet.

## Procedure
1. Decide EI for the whole roll before shooting; mark the canister/film back.
2. Expose consistently at that EI (spot/centre meter on mid-tones; protect shadows if possible).
3. Develop with the extended/reduced time; keep agitation per datasheet (some reduce agitation for push to control highlights).
4. Print on a softer grade if pushed negatives are contrasty.

## Gotchas
- Push more than 2 stops in high-contrast light gives blocked highlights; in flat light it works better.
- Mixed-EI rolls can't be developed correctly; one EI per roll (or per back).
- Delta 3200 / T-Max P3200 are really ~EI 1000 films designed to push.

## Verify
- [ ] Marked EI and development recorded; negatives print with acceptable highlights.
