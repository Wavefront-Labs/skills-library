# Choosing film developer and dilution

| Developer | Character | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| D-76 / ID-11 stock | fine grain, full speed, smooth tones; benchmark | everything; datasheet reference |
| D-76 1:1 | slightly sharper, more grain, one-shot | general |
| XTOL (1:1) | fine grain with full or extra speed, sharp; ascorbate | modern T-grain and traditional films |
| HC-110 (dil. B 1:31 / 1:63) | liquid concentrate, long shelf life, clean, slightly compensating at high dilution | versatile, pushing |
| Rodinal 1:25 / 1:50 / 1:100 | very sharp, visible grain, compensating when dilute | slow films, stand development, large format |
| Ilfotec DD-X / T-Max dev | fine grain with speed | pushing T-grain/Delta films |
| Perceptol / Microdol-X | finest grain, loses ~1 stop speed | slow fine-grain look |

## Choosing
1. Start with the film manufacturer's datasheet combination.
2. **Dilute** = more acutance and compensation, longer times, can lose some shadow speed.
3. **Solvent** developers (stock D-76, Perceptol) soften grain but also edges.
4. One-shot dilutions are more consistent than replenished stock for low volume.
5. Change only one variable (developer, dilution) per test; keep records.

## Shelf life notes
D-76/XTOL powder mixed: weeks to months in full, tight bottles; XTOL can fail suddenly (test with a film leader).
HC-110 and Rodinal concentrate: years.

## Gotchas
- Minimum stock volume per film: dilute one-shot developers need enough active agent (e.g. XTOL 1:1 needs
  >= 100 ml stock per 135-36 roll per Kodak). Check the datasheet.
- Times under ~5 min are uneven; dilute further.

- [ ] Combination and time recorded; contact sheet shows the expected grain and shadow detail.
