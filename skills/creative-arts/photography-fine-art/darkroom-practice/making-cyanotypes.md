# Making cyanotypes

## Classic formula (Herschel)
- Solution A: 25 g ferric ammonium citrate (green type) in 100 ml water.
- Solution B: 10 g potassium ferricyanide in 100 ml water.
- Store separately in brown bottles; mix 1:1 just before coating. (New Cyanotype - Mike Ware - is a
  faster, single-solution alternative.)

## Procedure
1. Paper: 100% cotton, sized (Arches Platine, Hahnemuhle Platinum Rag, or watercolour paper). Avoid
   buffered papers - alkalinity degrades cyanotype.
2. Coat in subdued tungsten light with a brush or glass rod (hake brush); one even coat; dry in the dark.
3. Contact print with a negative (or photogram) in a contact frame; expose to UV: sun 5-20 min, UV unit
   10-20 min. Exposure is correct when shadows go bronze/grey-blue - **overexpose**, as washing loses density.
4. Wash in running water (a little citric acid or vinegar in the first bath helps clear highlights) until
   highlights are white, 5-15 min.
5. Optional: dilute hydrogen peroxide (few drops in water) instantly oxidises to the final deep blue.
6. Dry.

## Toning
Bleach slightly in very dilute washing soda (sodium carbonate), then tone in strong tea, coffee, or tannic
acid for brown/black tones.

## Gotchas
- Alkaline water or buffered paper turns the blue pale yellow; phosphates in detergents fade prints.
- Cyanotypes can fade in light and regain colour in the dark (reversible).

## Verify
- [ ] Deep blue with white highlights after wash; exposure time logged for that negative density.
