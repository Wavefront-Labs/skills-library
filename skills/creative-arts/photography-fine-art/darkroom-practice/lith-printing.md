# Lith printing

Lith developer (hydroquinone, low sulfite, very dilute) works **infectiously**: slow start, then shadows race
to black while highlights stay soft, warm and grainy. Result: hard gritty shadows, creamy coloured highlights.

## Procedure
1. Paper: lith-responsive papers (Fomatone, Foma variant/ Fomabrom, some Ilford/ADOX papers - test; many
   modern papers do not lith).
2. **Overexpose** 2-3 stops relative to a normal print (highlights set by exposure).
3. Develop in dilute lith developer (Moersch Easylith, Fotospeed LD20) at 1+15 to 1+30, 20-30 deg C.
4. Image appears slowly (several minutes), then shadows accelerate. **Snatch** the print into stop bath
   when shadows reach the desired depth - often a few seconds matter.
5. Fix and wash as usual; colours shift on drying and with toning (selenium, gold).

## Controls
- Exposure -> highlights and colour (more exposure = lighter, warmer, less contrast).
- Developer dilution/age -> colour and grain. "Old brown" developer (keep some used solution) increases
  infectious effect.
- Temperature -> speed.

## Gotchas
- Snatch point is judged under safelight - lit prints dry darker; keep a reference.
- Developer exhausts during a session; consistency is limited - make editions carefully.

## Verify
- [ ] Characteristic hard shadows and soft coloured highlights; snatch point recorded for repeats.
