# Sort-and-purge sessions — decluttering that actually leaves the house

**Goal:** a bounded area emptied, sorted and reduced in one session, with everything
leaving the home gone by the end of the day — not parked in the hallway for a month.

## 1. Before the session
- **Bound the area**: one drawer, one shelf, one closet. A first session of 2–3 hours
  is plenty; decision fatigue sets in fast, especially for clients who find letting go hard.
- **Agree the goal** with the client in their words ("I want to close the wardrobe door",
  "the spare room must fit a bed by June"). Every hard decision is tested against it.
- **Book the outflow first**: donation drop-off time, a charity collection, a skip/dumpster
  or junk removal, e-waste point. A session without a route out just moves clutter.
- Kit: heavy-duty bin bags (black for rubbish, clear for donation so they don't get
  thrown out by mistake), boxes, masking tape and marker, sticky notes, gloves, a
  surface to sort on (bed, table, tarp on the floor).

## 2. The sort categories
Label five areas or boxes before touching anything:
1. **Keep** — used in the last year, loved, or genuinely needed.
2. **Donate** — clean, working, complete.
3. **Sell** — only items worth the effort (rule of thumb: worth more than about an hour
   of the client's time to list and post). Set a deadline; unsold → donate.
4. **Discard / recycle** — broken, stained, expired, missing parts.
5. **Elsewhere** — belongs in another room or to another person. Don't walk it there
   mid-session (you'll get distracted); deliver the box at the end.

## 3. Method
1. **Empty the whole area** onto the sort surface. Seeing the full quantity is what
   persuades people ("I have eleven water bottles").
2. **Group like with like** before deciding — decisions are easier per category.
3. Pick up each item once and place it in a category. Use set questions when stuck:
   - When did I last use it? Would I buy it again today?
   - If it went, what's the worst that would happen, and could I replace it for under
     ~£20/$20 within 20 minutes? (the common "20/20" heuristic)
   - Do I keep it because I like it, or because of guilt / cost / who gave it?
   - Does it fit the goal we agreed?
4. **Maybe pile**: allowed, but capped (one box), dated, and sealed. If not opened in
   ~3–6 months, it goes — the client decides the window now, not later.
5. **Put back the keeps** into the cleaned space, most-used in the best spots
   (see `repair-maintenance.home-organizing-declutter-move-prep.home-organising-systems.zoning-containment-labelling`).
6. **Same-day exit**: bag, load and drop off donations; bins out; Elsewhere box delivered.

## 4. Sentimental items
- Leave them until last, or for a later session; momentum from easy categories helps.
- Keep the best representatives (one or two of the child's artworks, not all of them);
  photograph the rest if that helps.
- Offer a defined container ("memories box") — the box size is the limit.
- Never decide *for* the client. Your role is to ask questions and keep pace.

## 5. Disposal routes and what charities won't take
- Typically refused: stained/broken items, mattresses and upholstered furniture without
  fire-safety labels (UK), car seats and cribs (safety recalls), opened toiletries, old
  electronics. Check the local charity's list — it varies.
- E-waste, batteries, paint, chemicals, fluorescent tubes and medicines go to proper
  collection points, not the bin. Wipe phones/computers before they leave.
- Documents with personal data → shred, don't bin (see paperwork-organising).

## Gotchas
- Never "just bin it" for the client while they're not looking — it destroys trust.
- If the client is distressed, stuck on most items, or the home has blocked exits,
  unsafe floors or sanitation problems, this may be **hoarding disorder** — slow down,
  stay non-judgemental, and suggest specialist and mental-health support. Forced
  clean-outs tend to make it worse.
- Shopping for storage first defeats the purpose.

## Verify
- [ ] The area was fully emptied and every item had a decision.
- [ ] The maybe box is sealed, dated and capped at one.
- [ ] Donations, rubbish and e-waste physically left the home that day.
- [ ] Keeps fit the space with room to spare; the client agrees the goal was met.
