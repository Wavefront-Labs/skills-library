# Whole-house estate triage

**Goal:** an emptied property, with nothing valuable or legally important thrown away,
the family's wishes respected, and the executor holding a clear record of what went where.

## 1. Authority first
- Confirm who instructs you: usually the **executor/administrator** (or attorney for a
  living person who is downsizing). Get instructions in writing. Family members who
  aren't the executor may disagree — don't take direction from them without the
  executor's say-so.
- In probate, items may need valuing before sale or distribution, and selling before
  the executor has authority can cause legal trouble. Ask whether a probate valuation is
  needed before anything leaves. *(Rules differ by country/state — the executor's
  solicitor/attorney decides.)*

## 2. Secure and document (day 1)
- Change or secure the locks; check insurance — unoccupied homes often need notifying
  to the insurer within a set period (commonly 30–60 days) or cover lapses.
- Photograph/video every room as found, opening cupboards and drawers.
- Remove obvious portable valuables (jewellery, cash, small silver, keys, devices) to a
  secure place with a signed list.
- Note hazards: firearms (call police or a licensed dealer, never transport casually),
  medications (pharmacy take-back), chemicals, asbestos suspicion in older homes, mould.

## 3. The document and valuables search — before ANY disposal
Search systematically, room by room, every container, before a single bag goes out.
Look for:
- Will, codicils, funeral wishes; deeds, mortgage, tenancy; bank/building-society and
  investment statements; pensions and insurance policies; share certificates and premium
  bonds/savings bonds; tax records; vehicle documents; keys to safety-deposit boxes.
- **Hidden cash and valuables** turn up in books, coat pockets, freezers, mattresses,
  shoe boxes, under drawer linings and inside ornaments. Shake out books; check pockets;
  open every envelope. This is not paranoia; it's routine.
- Photos, letters and digital devices (phones, laptops, cameras) — family priority and
  potentially needed for accounts and passwords.
Put everything found into labelled document boxes and hand them to the executor with a list.

## 4. Triage categories
Tag items (coloured stickers work well) with:
1. **Family / bequest** — named in the will or claimed by beneficiaries (executor arbitrates).
2. **Appraise** — possible value: jewellery, art, antiques, collections (coins, stamps,
   watches, vinyl, militaria), designer items, quality furniture, tools. Get a specialist
   valuer or auction house to look before anyone decides it's "just old stuff".
3. **Sell** — estate sale, auction, dealer offer, or online listing. Mid-range furniture
   often sells for less than families expect; set expectations early.
4. **Donate** — charities, house-clearance charities, community groups.
5. **Recycle / dispose** — metal, e-waste, textiles, then general waste via licensed carriers.
6. **Shred** — personal papers not needed by the executor (identity-theft risk).

## 5. Sequence
Search → family walkthrough (with stickers, ideally one session per beneficiary) →
appraisal visit → sale removal → donation collection → recycling → final waste removal
→ clean. Record every outflow: date, item/category, destination, value/receipt.

## Working with grieving families
- Go at their pace for personal rooms; start in kitchens, garages and lofts.
- Offer a "memory box" per family member. Never mock or rush.
- Disputes between relatives: pause that item, label it "held", and refer to the executor.

## Gotchas
- Licensed waste carriers only — fly-tipping by a contractor can come back on the
  person who arranged it.
- Mattresses, fridges/freezers, TVs and paint have separate disposal rules and fees.
- Redirect the post before clearing: statements arriving later reveal forgotten accounts.
- Cancel utilities only after the final clean; keep power and water on for the work.

## Verify
- [ ] Written instruction from the executor/attorney is on file.
- [ ] Every room, container and book was searched before any disposal.
- [ ] Documents and valuables handed over with a signed list.
- [ ] Possible-value items were seen by a valuer before sale or donation.
- [ ] Outflow log and receipts supplied; property left empty and clean.
