# Zoning, containment and labelling — organising systems that survive real life

**Goal:** an organised closet, kitchen or garage that still works three months later,
because every item has one obvious home sized to how often it's used.

Rule of order: **declutter → zone → measure → contain → label → maintain.** Buying
containers before decluttering and measuring is the classic expensive mistake.

## 1. Zone by activity, not by item type alone
Walk the space with the household and list the *activities* that happen there
(kitchen: prep, cook, bake, coffee, lunches, cleaning). Each activity gets a zone placed
at its **point of use**:
- Kitchen: knives, boards and bowls between sink and hob; pots under/next to the hob;
  plates and glasses near the dishwasher; coffee kit together in one cupboard + counter spot.
- Closet: daily wear between shoulder and hip height; off-season and occasion wear high
  or at the ends; shoes visible near where you sit to put them on.
- Garage: zones on walls by category (garden, car, sport, tools, bulk/overflow), with the
  most-used nearest the door and the car's swing path kept clear.

**Prime real estate** = waist-to-eye height, front of shelf. Reserve it for weekly-use
items. Monthly → higher/lower. Yearly → top shelves, loft, under-bed.

## 2. Measure before you buy
Record for every shelf/drawer: width, depth, *clear* height (under hinges and lips),
and door-frame obstructions. Useful reference numbers (verify against your space):
- Double-hang closet: upper rod ~200–210 cm (80–84 in), lower ~100–105 cm (40–42 in).
  Single long-hang rod ~165–175 cm (66–69 in).
- Hanging depth: allow ~60 cm (24 in) from back wall for clothes on hangers.
- Folded-clothes shelves: 30–35 cm (12–14 in) deep; stacks no higher than ~25 cm or they
  topple. Deep shelves (>45 cm) bury things — use pull-out bins.
- Pantry shelves: 30–40 cm (12–16 in) deep is the usable max without risers/turntables.

Leave **20–25% empty space** in every zone so new items have somewhere to go.

## 3. Containment
- Container size is set by the *category quantity after decluttering*, not by what looks nice.
- Clear containers for pantry, fridge and anything you need to see stock level; opaque
  for visual calm (closets, living spaces) — but opaque must be labelled.
- Use drawer dividers rather than loose bins in drawers; measure internal width and
  depth, not the cabinet front.
- Deep cupboards: pull-out bins or turntables, one category per bin.
- Decanting dry goods only pays if the household will actually refill; otherwise keep
  packets in an open labelled bin.
- Test one container before buying twenty — lids, stacking and fit vary between brands.

## 4. Labelling
- Label the **category**, not the contents list ("Batteries", not "AA, AAA, 9V").
- Put labels where they're read: shelf edge at eye level, bin front (not lid only),
  both ends of garage totes.
- Use a label maker with laminated tape (Brother P-touch TZe-type) or printed labels;
  handwriting fades and peels in garages. Children or non-readers: add picture labels.
- Include a "Misc / to sort" bin per zone — honest overflow beats chaos.

## 5. Maintenance routine (hand this to the client)
- Daily 10-minute reset: everything back to its zone.
- Weekly: empty the misc bin.
- Seasonal: rotate wardrobes, re-check fridge and pantry dates, one-in-one-out review.
- A system the client can't maintain in 10 minutes a day is too complex — simplify it.

## Gotchas
- Over-containerising (bins inside bins) slows retrieval; one step to grab, one step to put away.
- Lids kill compliance for frequent-use items — go lidless.
- Garage: keep items off the floor (wall rails, shelving) but check wall fixings into
  studs/masonry; loaded shelving can weigh hundreds of kg. Anchor tall units against tipping.
- Don't design for the organiser's habits; design for the household's actual behaviour
  (if coats land on a chair, put hooks by that chair).

## Verify
- [ ] Every item has one named home; household members can say where things go.
- [ ] Weekly-use items sit in prime real estate.
- [ ] Containers were bought after measuring and after the declutter.
- [ ] Each zone has ~20% free space and a labelled overflow bin.
- [ ] A 10-minute reset restores the space; checked again after 2–4 weeks.
