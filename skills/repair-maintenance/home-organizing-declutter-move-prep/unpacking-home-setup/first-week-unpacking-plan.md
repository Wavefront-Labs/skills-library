# First-week unpacking plan — a working home in days, not months

**Goal:** sleep well on night one, cook by day two, and have no boxes left by the end
of week one. Boxes that survive a month tend to become furniture for a year.

## Day 0 — before the boxes come in (if you can get in early)
- Walk through and photograph the condition of the property (for deposits and disputes).
- Clean the kitchen cupboards, fridge and bathrooms while they're empty — it's ten
  times harder once full. Line drawers if you want to.
- Put room signs (matching your box colours/labels) on each door so movers place boxes
  correctly. Direct them; don't let everything get stacked in one room.
- Decide where big furniture goes **before** it comes in (measure alcoves and
  stair/door clearances). Moving a wardrobe twice is the most avoidable job.

## Night 1 — safety and sleep
1. **Safety:** test smoke and CO alarms; find the water stop-tap, the gas shut-off and
   the consumer unit/breaker panel; check all doors and windows lock.
   Arrange a lock change (or rekey) soon — previous owners' keys may be out there.
2. **Meter readings:** photograph gas, electric and water meters; send to suppliers.
3. **Beds:** assemble and make every bed before anyone is exhausted.
4. **Open First boxes:** bathroom (toilet roll, soap, towels, meds), chargers, kettle and mugs.
5. Plug in the router/modem if service is live.

## Day 2 — kitchen first
The kitchen is the room with the most boxes and the biggest quality-of-life payoff.
- Unpack box by box, but **place by zone** as you go: everyday plates and glasses near
  the dishwasher, pots by the hob, prep tools between sink and hob, food in one
  pantry area (see `repair-maintenance.home-organizing-declutter-move-prep.home-organising-systems.zoning-containment-labelling`).
- Don't aim for perfect; aim for "everything has a provisional home". Mark anything
  you're unsure of with masking tape and revisit on day 6–7.
- Run the dishwasher once empty to check plumbing for leaks before relying on it.

## Days 3–5 — bathrooms, bedrooms, living spaces
Order: bathrooms → bedrooms and wardrobes → living room → home office → kids'/play →
spare room and storage last. Within each room:
1. Furniture placed and assembled.
2. Daily-use items unpacked into their final spot.
3. Cables and electronics set up from the photos taken when packing.
4. Decor and art last — live in the room a few days before drilling.

## Days 6–7 — finish and flatten
- **Box breakdown as you go**: cut the tape, flatten, and stand flat boxes in one spot.
  Keep 10–20 good ones only if you'll move again soon or need storage; recycle or give
  the rest away (local marketplace listings move them in hours).
- Paper and bubble wrap: bag for reuse or recycle; bubble wrap is usually soft-plastics
  recycling, not kerbside.
- Garage, loft and storage boxes: unpack or **decide** — don't just stack. Anything you
  didn't need in the first week is a declutter candidate.
- Tick off box numbers on the inventory; chase any missing ones with the movers now.

## Admin to do in the first week
Change of address (bank, employer, doctor, vehicle registration, electoral roll,
subscriptions), mail redirection, register with local services, set up bin collection
days, update home insurance with the new address and any new valuables.

## Gotchas
- Unpacking into "temporary" spots that become permanent — use the masking-tape
  review on day 7.
- Hanging art before furniture is final leads to extra holes.
- Children and pets: set up their spaces early (familiar bedding and toys reduce stress);
  keep pets shut in a quiet room while movers come and go.
- Don't overload extension leads with every appliance in one room while you wait for
  furniture placement.

## Verify
- [ ] Alarms tested, shut-offs found, meters photographed on night one.
- [ ] Every bed made on night one; kitchen usable by day two.
- [ ] Box inventory fully ticked off; damage reported to movers within their deadline.
- [ ] No unopened boxes after day seven; flat boxes out of the house.

*Draft: sequence reflects common organiser practice; adapt order for households with infants or medical needs.*
