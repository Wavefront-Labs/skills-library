# Full service of a mechanical watch movement

**Goal:** return a mechanical watch to like-new running — clean, correctly
lubricated, regulated and water-resistant — on a typical three-hand automatic such
as an ETA 2824 / Sellita SW200 class calibre.

> Draft: always follow the calibre's technical communication (lubrication chart) where
> available — oil points and products differ by movement. Moebius references below are
> common choices, not universal.

## 0. Before opening
Record condition: photos of case, dial, hands; timegrapher reading (rate/amplitude
before service); note scratches already there. Test the crown and functions.

## 1. Case out
- Open the caseback (case wrench / ball for screw-backs, a case knife for snap-backs,
  vintage cases may be front-loading). Protect the case in a holder.
- Remove the **winding stem** (press the setting lever push-piece), lift the movement,
  refit stem temporarily if helpful.
- Remove hands (hand levers with a plastic sheet to protect the dial), dial, and fit
  the movement in a movement holder.

## 2. Let down and strip
- **Let down the mainspring** first: hold the crown, release the click with a
  pegwood/tweezers, and let the crown slip slowly. Never strip a wound movement —
  stored energy breaks pivots and fingers.
- Remove the automatic module and **balance cock with balance** early (protect the
  hairspring — place in a covered dish).
- Then pallet bridge/fork, train bridge, wheels, barrel bridge, barrel, keyless works
  (dial side). Use a parts tray with compartments; photograph each stage.
- Open the barrel; inspect/replace the **mainspring** (modern automatics: usually
  replace — use the correct size: height × thickness × length × barrel diameter).

## 3. Clean
- Remove oily/greasy residues with a pre-clean (pegwood points, brushes).
- **Cleaning machine** (L&R, Elma, etc.): cleaning solution → 2 rinses → warm-air dry;
  ultrasonic for plates and bridges. **Balance with hairspring, pallet fork and
  sometimes dial-side shock parts** handled to the machine's/maker's guidance
  (some watchmakers clean them separately by hand).
- Clean shock-setting jewels (Incabloc/Kif): remove springs, take out cap and hole
  jewels, clean, peg out holes with pegwood until clean.
- Optional: **epilame** treatment on escape wheel teeth and pallet stones to stop oil spreading.

## 4. Inspect
Under magnification: pivots (wear, bends), jewel holes (cracks, ovality), wheel teeth,
pallet stones (chips, secure), escape wheel teeth, hairspring (flat, centred, coils
not touching), automatic reversers/wheels (worn = low winding efficiency). Replace worn parts.

## 5. Reassemble and lubricate
General lubrication scheme (check the calibre chart):
| Point | Typical lubricant |
|---|---|
| Train jewels (third, fourth, centre) | Moebius **9010** (thin synthetic) |
| Escape wheel pivots, balance jewels (cap jewels) | 9010 (or 9415 per maker) |
| **Pallet stones** (impulse faces) | **9415** (special grease-like oil) — tiny amount, distribute by letting the watch run |
| Barrel arbor, keyless works, setting parts | **HP1300** or **9504** grease |
| Mainspring barrel wall (automatics) | **8217** or 8200 braking grease |
| Reversers | per maker (often dry, or 9010) |
Rules: oil cups ~ one-third to half full, no oil on wheels or pinion teeth (except
specified), oil cap jewels before fitting, never flood. Use oilers sized to the job.
Reassemble barrel → train → check freedom (gentle blow should spin the train) →
pallets → wind slightly → balance last. Then dial side, dial, hands (in correct
alignment at 12:00 and date change).

## 6. Time and test
- Timegrapher: amplitude ~270–310° dial up, beat error < 0.5 ms, regulate (see the
  timegrapher skill); positional test; 24 h run check on a winder or bench.
- Check date change at midnight, setting, hand clearances (hands not touching each
  other or crystal).

## 7. Case and seal
- Clean case and crystal; **replace gaskets** (caseback, crown/tube, crystal) with
  correct sizes, lightly greased with silicone grease.
- Case the movement, fit stem, close back.
- **Pressure test**: dry (vacuum/pressure) tester — watch case flex measured — to the
  rated depth or a practical test pressure; condensation test after (warm the watch,
  touch a cool damp cloth to the crystal: fogging inside = leak).

## Gotchas
- Oil migrates if surfaces aren't perfectly clean — clean again rather than over-oil.
- Fingerprints corrode steel parts; use finger cots and tweezers.
- Hands not seated parallel catch each other at certain times.
- A dropped hairspring or bent pivot ends the day — work over a tray, take time.

## Verify
- [ ] Before/after timegrapher figures recorded.
- [ ] Mainspring let down before strip; replaced/inspected.
- [ ] All parts inspected under magnification; worn parts replaced.
- [ ] Lubrication per calibre chart; no excess oil.
- [ ] Rate/amplitude/beat error healthy; 24 h run check passed.
- [ ] New gaskets; pressure and condensation test passed.
