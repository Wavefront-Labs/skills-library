# Turning a balance staff on the watchmaker's lathe

**Goal:** make a part you can't buy — classically a balance staff for a vintage
watch — to within a few hundredths of a millimetre, with polished pivots that run
true. The same techniques make stems, bushings and screws.

> Draft: a summary of traditional technique; skill comes from practice on scrap. Buy
> a ready-made staff when one exists — it's almost always better and cheaper. Exact
> lengths come from measuring the old staff and the jewels, not from this page.

## 1. Kit
- **8 mm WW-pattern lathe** (Boley, Lorch, Levin, Schaublin 70 etc.) with a set of
  collets (0.1 mm steps up to ~4 mm), a T-rest, a motor with foot speed control.
- **Gravers:** square HSS or carbide, 2–3 mm.
- Micrometer (0.001 mm reading if possible), staking set, Jacot tool or pivot
  polisher, loupes (×10 and ×20).
- Material: **blue-steel or silver-steel rod** (carbon tool steel) slightly larger than
  the largest diameter.

## 2. Sharpen the graver (most problems are here)
- Face the graver at ~45° to make a lozenge (diamond) face.
- Stone on fine India/Arkansas or diamond plate, then polish on a fine ceramic or
  with diamond paste on a flat — **the point must be mirror-polished and sharp enough
  to catch on a thumbnail**.
- Re-touch the point often; a dull graver tears steel and makes undersize parts.

## 3. Measure the original
From the old staff (even broken) and the watch:
- Overall length = distance between cap jewels (end-shake included — typically
  ~0.02–0.04 mm end-shake target).
- Pivot diameters: fit to the jewel holes — **the pivot should be ~0.01–0.02 mm
  smaller than the hole** (side-shake).
- Balance seat, hairspring collet seat, roller table seat diameters and heights.
Draw a dimensioned sketch.

## 4. Turn (hand turning, graver on the T-rest)
1. Hold the rod in a collet with minimal overhang. Speed: moderate (a few hundred to
   ~1,000+ rpm for small diameters); find what gives a clean cut.
2. Graver rests on the T-rest, point at **centre height**, slightly trailing; take
   small cuts — steel should come off as fine curls.
3. Rough the main diameters first, largest to smallest, working from the end:
   balance seat → hub → hairspring collet seat → roller seat → lower pivot area.
4. Measure constantly with the micrometer. Leave **~0.01 mm** on seats that will be
   polished.
5. Form the shoulders square and crisp; the balance riveting lip (hub undercut) is
   shaped to be spread later.
6. Turn the lower conical pivot area, leaving pivots oversize for now.
7. Part off, reverse into a collet (or a split chuck/wax chuck) to do the other end;
   check runout with a dial indicator or by eye at magnification.

## 5. Harden and temper
- Heat the staff (in a protective tube or coated in soap/boric acid paste) to
  **cherry red** (~780–800 °C for carbon steel) and quench in oil.
- Clean, polish a bright spot, then temper to **dark blue** (~300 °C) on a bluing pan
  — hard enough for pivots, tough enough not to snap.
- Many makers harden before final pivot turning; then finish pivots with a carbide graver
  or by grinding/burnishing.

## 6. Pivots
- Reduce pivots to size in the **Jacot tool** (burnisher on a lantern/bed) or with
  a pivot polisher on the lathe: form the conical shoulder, then burnish to a mirror.
- Pivot ends **domed/rounded**, not flat — they run on cap jewels.
- Check pivot fit in its jewel: free side-shake, no binding.

## 7. Fit
Rivet the balance onto the staff with a staking set (correct flat-faced and hollow
stakes), fit roller (friction) and hairspring collet, true and poise the balance,
check end-shake in the watch, and measure on a timegrapher.

## Bushings and stems
Same tools: bushings from brass rod, drilled in the lathe to fit a pivot with
side-shake, turned outside to press-fit a broached hole. Stems from steel rod — square
filed for the clutch, thread cut with a die, harden/temper.

## Gotchas
- Graver below centre height digs in; above rubs.
- Measuring a hot part after a heavy cut reads large.
- Forgetting to harden = pivots wear in weeks.
- Tempering past blue to grey/teal = too soft.

## Verify
- [ ] Dimensioned sketch from original staff and jewel measurements.
- [ ] All diameters within ~0.005–0.01 mm of target; shoulders square.
- [ ] Hardened and tempered blue; pivots burnished to mirror, ends domed.
- [ ] Balance runs true in both planes; poised.
- [ ] Correct end-shake and side-shake in the movement; timegrapher reading healthy.
