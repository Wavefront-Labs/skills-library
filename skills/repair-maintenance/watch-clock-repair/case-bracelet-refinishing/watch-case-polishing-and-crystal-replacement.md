# Watch case polishing and crystal replacement

**Goal:** remove wear from a steel case and bracelet so it looks factory-fresh
**without** rounding edges, softening lugs or removing more metal than needed — and
fit a crystal that seals.

> Draft: collector value of many vintage and luxury watches drops when polished;
> ask the owner and record their decision. Gold and platinum polish faster than steel —
> adjust. Precious-metal dust collection is required in many workshops.

## 1. Decide and document
- Photograph all surfaces. Identify each finish: **mirror polish**, **vertical/radial
  brushing**, circular graining, bead blast. Note the brush direction on every surface
  — follow the original exactly.
- Agree with the owner: light refresh vs full refinish; vintage pieces often get
  "clean only" or a light hand finish.
- Strip the watch: movement out, crystal, crown, bezel, pushers, gaskets out. Never
  polish a cased movement.

## 2. Masking
Kapton/polyimide or polishing tape over surfaces that must not change (brushed
areas while you mirror-polish, and vice versa), engravings, and the sharp transition
between finishes. Re-tape between each operation.

## 3. Flatten first (the step that keeps lines sharp)
- Deep scratches and dings on flat surfaces: **lap** them on a flat lapping disc/
  plate (or wet-and-dry paper on glass): 600 → 1000 → 1500 grit, keeping the surface
  dead flat and the edges crisp.
- Polishing a deep scratch with a soft wheel alone just rounds the surface around it.

## 4. Mirror polish
- Polishing motor with **hard felt** or stiff cotton buffs for flats, softer cotton for
  curves. Compounds in sequence (e.g. a medium cut such as a grey/brown bar, then a
  final white/blue/green finishing compound — names vary by maker).
- Light pressure, keep the part moving, **work across the edge, not into it**; let the
  wheel trail off edges so it doesn't snag (and throw the part — hold firmly, wear
  safety glasses).
- Separate buffs per compound; contaminated buffs leave scratches.
- Clean between compounds (ultrasonic or degreaser) so grit doesn't carry forward.

## 5. Re-brush
- Tape the polished areas. Recreate the satin finish with **Scotch-Brite-type pads**
  (grades from coarse to very fine), a fibre brush, or a satin-finish wheel. Single
  direction, straight strokes along a guide (a ruler/taped edge for straight lines).
- Match the original grain coarseness — compare against an un-worn area (e.g. under
  the bracelet end link).

## 6. Bracelets
- Refinish links assembled, folded to expose each surface in turn; brush along the
  bracelet length, polished centre links masked separately.
- **Stretch** (gaps between links from pin wear) can be reduced by replacing pins and
  tubes; it can't be polished out.
- Clasps: check springs and locking; replace worn parts.

## 7. Crystals
- **Acrylic (hesalite):** scratches polish out with a plastic polish (Polywatch-type)
  by hand. Replacement crystals are fitted with a **crystal lift/claw** that compresses
  the crystal while it seats — or tension-ring crystals pressed in.
- **Mineral/sapphire:** usually set in a gasket. Measure diameter (0.01 mm calipers)
  and profile (flat/domed, height), get the exact size. Fit gasket, press in with a
  **crystal press** and correctly sized dies (concave die for domed). Sapphire won't
  flex — wrong size cracks or doesn't seal.
- Glued crystals (some vintage): UV-curing glue, minimal amount, no squeeze-out.

## 8. Reassemble and seal
New gaskets (crystal, caseback, crown/tube), lightly silicone-greased. Pressure-test
and condensation-test.

## Gotchas
- Over-polished lugs and rounded case flanks can't be restored without laser welding.
- Polishing over the brushed/polished boundary blurs it — that line is the first
  thing a collector checks.
- Brushing in the wrong direction is obvious under any light.
- A loose crystal "fits" but leaks — every water-resistant crystal gets a pressure test.

## Verify
- [ ] Owner's decision and before photos recorded; finishes and directions mapped.
- [ ] Deep marks lapped flat; edges and chamfers still crisp.
- [ ] Mirror areas free of haze; brushed areas match original grain and direction.
- [ ] Boundaries between finishes sharp; no compound left in crevices.
- [ ] Crystal correct size, seated, gaskets new; pressure test passed.
