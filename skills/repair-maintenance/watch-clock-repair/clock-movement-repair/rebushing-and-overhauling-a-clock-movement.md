# Rebushing and overhauling a clock movement

**Goal:** take a tired spring-driven mantel or wall clock (time and strike, e.g. a
Hermle-type or older American/German movement) and make it run a full wind reliably
and strike correctly.

> Draft: methods are standard bench practice; bushing sizes and tools depend on the
> system you own (Bergeon, KWM). **Mainsprings store enough energy to break bones —
> always let down with a let-down tool and wear eye protection.**

## 1. Assess before you strip
- Clock stopped or stops before the end of the wind? Strike out of sequence?
- Remove hands, dial, and the movement from the case. With springs wound, look at
  every pivot hole while gently rocking each wheel: **pivot moves visibly sideways in
  its hole = wear.** Mark worn holes with a marker on the plate. Wear is almost always
  in the direction of the force from the mainspring.
- Check the pendulum suspension spring (kinked = replace) and crutch.

## 2. Let down the mainsprings
Use a **let-down key** that fits the arbor; wind slightly to free the click, lift the
click, and let the key turn slowly in your grip, a little at a time. Repeat for each
train. Never release clicks with a screwdriver alone.

## 3. Strip and clean
- Photograph both sides; note the strike train's lever positions.
- Separate plates; lay out wheels by train.
- Clean: ultrasonic clock-cleaning solution (ammoniated or ammonia-free), rinse,
  dry thoroughly (warm air). Peg out pivot holes with pegwood until clean.
- **Mainsprings:** remove with a **mainspring winder** (never by hand), clean, inspect
  for set/cracks, lubricate with clock mainspring grease/oil, refit. Replace weak or
  set springs with the correct width × thickness × length.

## 4. Rebush worn pivot holes
1. **Polish the pivot first** — a scored pivot will wear the new bush. Use a pivot
   file/burnisher or a lathe/Jacot tool; reduce scoring, keep the pivot cylindrical.
2. Measure the pivot; choose a bushing with the pivot hole ~**0.02–0.05 mm** larger
   (clock side-shake is more generous than watches) and a suitable outside diameter.
3. Centre the work: in a bushing machine (Bergeon/KWM), centre on the **original
   hole's centre** — worn holes are oval; use the centering tool so the bush goes where
   the hole used to be, not into the worn part. Filing the hole back toward its
   original centre before reaming is the traditional correction.
4. Ream the hole to the bushing's OD (minus press-fit), from the inside of the plate,
   with a cutting broach/reamer, keeping it perpendicular.
5. Press the bushing in from the inside; stake flush; chamfer and polish the oil sink.
6. Check: the wheel spins freely with end-shake and correct depthing (meshing) with
   its neighbours.

## 5. Reassemble and check the trains
- Assemble the time train, check free run (a light push spins it and it coasts down).
- Strike train: set the **warning** — when the warning pin is caught, there should be
  roughly **half to one revolution** of the warning wheel free run; the hammer should be
  at rest, not lifted, when the train stops.
- **Count-wheel** strike: lock lever drops into the count-wheel slots in sequence; sync
  the count to the hour hand.
  **Rack-and-snail**: rack tail lands on the correct snail step for each hour; gathering
  pallet picks up one tooth per blow.
- Oil pivot holes sparingly with clock oil (to fill the oil sink, not overflow),
  escapement pallet faces lightly. No oil on wheel teeth.

## 6. Escapement and beat
- Check pallets for wear grooves (a worn recoil escapement can be adjusted by moving
  the pallets or dressing faces).
- **In beat:** tick and tock evenly spaced. Adjust by bending the crutch slightly
  or with a self-setting beat/friction adjustment. Level the case first.
- Regulate with the pendulum bob nut (raise bob to speed up; one turn ≈ minutes per day,
  depends on pendulum).

## Gotchas
- Rebushing without polishing pivots — the new bush wears in months.
- Bush placed off the original centre ruins depthing and the clock stops.
- Overoiling collects dust and makes an abrasive paste.
- "Clock runs on the bench, stops in the case" = not in beat once levelled; check beat in place.

## Verify
- [ ] Mainsprings let down with proper tool; eye protection worn.
- [ ] All worn holes found and rebushed on original centres; pivots polished.
- [ ] Trains spin freely; strike warning and sequence correct.
- [ ] Clock in beat in its case; runs a full wind (e.g. 8 days) and strikes correctly.
- [ ] Rate regulated to within a minute or so a week.
