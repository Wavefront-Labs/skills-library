# Bed bug inspection and treatment protocol

**Goal:** confirm a bed bug infestation, eliminate every life stage including eggs,
and prove it's gone — bed bugs are the job where cutting corners guarantees a callback.

> Jurisdiction: United States (EPA guidance). Chemical choices and whether pesticides may be
> applied to mattresses depend on the product label and local rules.

## 1. Confirm (never treat on bites alone)
Bites are not diagnostic — many conditions look the same. Find evidence:
- **Live bugs**: adults flat, oval, mahogany; nymphs translucent to straw.
- **Faecal spots**: black ink-dot stains that smear when wiped with a damp cloth.
- Shed skins, eggs (pearly white, glued in cracks), blood spots on sheets.
Inspect in order: mattress seams/piping and tags, box spring underside (remove the
dust cover), bed frame joints and screw holes, headboard back, bedside furniture,
skirting boards and outlets near the bed, sofa seams, curtain folds.
Tools: torch, credit card or spatula to run seams, magnifier, specimen vial.
A trained canine team or interceptors under bed legs helps on low-level or
disputed cases.

## 2. Client preparation (makes or breaks the job)
Give a written prep sheet:
- **Launder** all bedding and clothing from the room: hot wash and/or **dryer on high
  heat** (heat kills; washing alone may not). Bag clean items in sealed new bags,
  keep them out of the room.
- De-clutter: no piles on floors; don't move items to other rooms unbagged (spreads them).
- Don't throw out the mattress unless it's torn — wrap it before carrying it out
  and mark it unusable.
- Don't use foggers (the spray does not reach cracks; EPA says use one only if bed bugs
  are on its label and never as the only method) or move to sleep on the sofa — bugs
  follow hosts.
- For heat jobs: remove heat-sensitive items (candles, aerosols, medication, plants,
  pets, vinyl records, some electronics).

## 3. Treat
**Option A — whole-room heat:**
- Verify the target temperature **inside the hardest-to-heat items** (mattress core,
  wall voids, pile of books), not just in the room air, and hold it for the time your
  heat-equipment maker's protocol requires. A commercial-heat study found 48.3 °C
  lethal to 99% of adults and 54.8 °C to 99% of eggs, and room air may need to reach
  about 55–65 °C to drive heat into items.
- Use wireless probes in insulated spots; move and flip items during the cycle;
  fans to circulate. Sprinkler heads and fire alarms must be managed per building rules.
- Heat has no residual — add a residual dust in voids/outlets afterwards.

**Option B — integrated chemical/non-chemical:**
- **Steam** (at least 130 °F / 54 °C at the surface, with a diffuser so forceful airflow
  does not scatter the bugs) along seams, tufts, furniture joints.
- **Encasements** on mattress and box spring (bite-proof, zip lock) — traps survivors.
- **Crack-and-crevice** labelled residual product in frames, skirting and furniture
  joints; the label governs product choice and where it may be applied.
- **Desiccant dust** registered as a pesticide for bed bugs, lightly in voids, outlets
  (power off), behind skirting. Never use pool or food-grade diatomaceous earth (EPA:
  it should not be used and can harm you if breathed in). Desiccants can take months
  to work.
- Expect **more than one visit**, because eggs survive many sprays.

**Small items:** dryer on high heat, portable heat chamber, or a freezer set to 0 °F
(−18 °C) with the item in a sealed bag for three days, checking with a thermometer.

## 4. Follow up
- Interceptor cups under each bed leg (check and clean weekly, reapply talcum); bed pulled
  off the wall; no bedding touching floor.
- Re-inspect at least every 7 days in case eggs remain, and keep checking for at least a
  year (EPA). Inspect adjacent units in flats.
- Close out only after no live bugs, no new faecal spots and empty interceptors over
  at least two consecutive checks.

## Gotchas
- "Treat the bed only" misses the bugs hiding away from it.
- Over-the-counter sprays scatter bugs and drive them into neighbours' units.
- A low-level infestation can produce no visible evidence; interceptors are your friend.
- Heat that stays below the adult-lethal figure (48.3 °C) in the mattress core is a failed treatment.

## Verify
- [ ] Physical evidence (bug, skin, egg or faecal spot) documented with photos.
- [ ] Prep sheet issued and checked on arrival.
- [ ] Heat: probe log shows the target temperature reached in the coldest spot and held
      for the protocol time; or chemical: all harbourage zones treated, encasements fitted.
- [ ] Follow-up visits scheduled to cover egg hatch.
- [ ] Two consecutive clean checks before closing.
