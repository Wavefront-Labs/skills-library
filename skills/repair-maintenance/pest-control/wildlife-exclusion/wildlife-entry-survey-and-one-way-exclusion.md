# Wildlife entry survey and one-way exclusion

**Goal:** get animals out of a building and keep them out with permanent repairs —
without trapping young inside, breaking wildlife law, or creating a worse problem
(a dead animal in a wall, a mother tearing a new hole).

> Wildlife law varies sharply by country and state: bats and other species are often
> protected, exclusion may be barred during breeding season, and relocating trapped
> animals is restricted in many places. Check before you touch anything.

## 1. Identify the animal
- **Sounds and timing:** squirrels — daytime scurrying, early morning and dusk;
  raccoons/possums — heavy thumps at night; bats — dusk emergence, scratching/chirping
  at dawn; birds — daytime, near vents.
- **Signs:** chewed edges (squirrels — clean gnaw marks), torn
  soffits/vents (raccoon — big damage), guano (bat droppings crumble to
  glittery dust when crushed; mouse droppings don't), nesting material in vents.
- A **dusk watch** from outside (sit 30 min before sunset to 30 min after) shows bat
  exit points better than any ladder search.

## 2. Survey the whole envelope
Ladder/binoculars/drone. Check, and photograph:
- Roof returns, soffit-fascia junctions, gable vents, ridge vent ends.
- Chimneys (no cap), plumbing vent stacks, dryer and bathroom vents.
- Gaps where dissimilar materials meet (siding/brick, flashing).
- Tree branches close to the roof (squirrel launch pads).
List **primary** (active, stained/rubbed) and **secondary** (potential) points.

## 3. Timing
- **Do not exclude during the birthing/dependent-young season** for the species
  (commonly spring to midsummer; check the dates for your region and species). Young left
  behind die in the structure or the mother causes major damage getting back in.
- If young are present: wait, or (for raccoons/squirrels) locate young, hand-remove
  into a warm box at the exit for the mother to relocate — where legal.

## 4. Exclude
1. **Seal all secondary points first** with permanent materials so the primary is
   the only exit.
2. On the primary, fit a **one-way device**:
   - Squirrels/raccoons: a one-way door or funnel of welded hardware cloth, fastened securely around the hole, pointing down/out.
   - Bats: tubes, netting draped 50 cm below the exit, or commercial cones —
     smooth inside so they can't crawl back. Licence/legal timing first.
   - Birds: remove nests (where not protected/active), then mesh.
3. Leave devices in place until no activity is seen (bats: several nights of no
   activity observed at dusk).
4. Confirm empty (dusk watch, a crumpled newspaper plug, or soft-block check), then
   remove the device and **permanently seal** the primary.

## 5. Proofing materials
- Galvanised hardware cloth of a mesh the animal cannot pass, screwed with washers or
  stapled + capped. Chimney caps with mesh. Steel vent guards.
- Sealant and metal flashing on roof returns. Trim back tree limbs.
- Birds: stainless spikes on ledges, post-and-wire, or netting sized to the species —
  netting must be taut and fully closed or birds get trapped.

## 6. Clean-up
Large accumulations of bat or bird droppings may need a professional hazardous-waste
company (CDC). Suppress dust (spray water), wear a NIOSH-approved respirator, eye
protection and gloves (histoplasmosis risk), remove contaminated insulation, sanitise
and replace it.

## Gotchas
- Sealing the primary before the animal is out = dead animal or new hole.
- Trapping and relocating often kills the animal and leaves young behind.
- Foam and plastic mesh are chewed through in a night.
- One missed gap at the roof line makes the whole job fail.

## Verify
- [ ] Species and legal status confirmed; timing outside dependent-young season.
- [ ] All entry points mapped and photographed; secondaries sealed.
- [ ] One-way device on primary; no activity for several days/nights.
- [ ] Primary permanently sealed; trees trimmed; clean-up done with PPE.
- [ ] Client given a re-check date and warranty terms.
