# Rodent inspection and control programme

**Goal:** get a building to zero rodent activity and keep it there — by identifying
the species, killing the current population with traps (and baits only where
justified), and closing the gaps that let them in.

> Rodenticide rules differ hard between jurisdictions (US EPA labels and state rules,
> UK stewardship, EU biocides). The label is the law; confirm local licensing before
> using any toxicant.

## 1. Inspect before you treat
Carry a strong torch, mirror, gloves, a P2/N95 mask (droppings and urine can carry
hantavirus/leptospirosis — never dry-sweep; wet with disinfectant first).

Read the signs:
| Sign | House mouse | Norway (brown) rat | Roof (black) rat |
|---|---|---|---|
| Droppings | small, pointed, scattered | larger, blunt, clustered | long, curved, pointed |
| Where | anywhere, inside cabinets/walls | ground level, burrows, drains | lofts, high runs, trees/cables |
| Behaviour to traps | curious, explores new things | neophobic (wary of new objects) | neophobic, climbs |

Also: rub marks (greasy smears along walls/beams), gnaw marks, burrows by
foundations, tracks in dust, fresh vs old droppings (fresh are soft and shiny).
A thin dusting of flour or talc on suspected runs overnight confirms traffic.
Check drains: brown rats often enter via broken sewer connections, not doors.

Map every sign on a floor plan. The map *is* the treatment plan.

## 2. Knock-down
**Traps first** — fast, no secondary poisoning, carcass recovered.
- Snap traps set **perpendicular to the wall, trigger toward the wall**, or in pairs
  back-to-back on the run. Rodents run along edges.
- Mice: many traps close together in active areas — many traps for a few days beats few for weeks.
- Rats: **pre-bait unset traps for a few days** (neophobia), then set. Use enclosures
  outdoors to protect non-targets.
- Baits on traps: a pea-sized dab of peanut butter or chocolate spread; nesting
  material (cotton) works for mice in winter.
- Avoid glue boards except where legal and checked often (restricted in some places; in
  England, rodent glue traps need a Natural England licence since 31 July 2024).

**Rodenticides** (only where traps are impractical and the label allows):
- Always in **tamper-resistant, secured bait stations**, labelled, mapped and numbered.
- Anticoagulants are slow-acting; dead rodents in voids cause odour — another reason
  to prefer traps indoors.
- Never broadcast loose bait; never place where children, pets or wildlife reach it.
- Remove bait once activity stops; EPA's review is concerned with risks to children,
  pets and non-target wildlife.

## 3. Proof (the step that actually ends the job)
- Mice pass gaps about the width of a pencil; rats about a thumb's width.
- Seal with materials they can't gnaw: steel/copper mesh stuffed tight then sealed,
  cement mortar, galvanised sheet, brush strips on door bottoms. Expanding foam
  alone is chewed through.
- Typical points: pipe and cable penetrations, gaps under doors, air bricks with
  broken grilles, weep vents, roof-wall junctions, broken drain pipes (camera survey;
  fit a rat valve/non-return flap where drains are the route).
- Remove harbourage: clutter against walls, vegetation touching the building,
  open food and bins.

## 4. Monitor to zero
Revisit within a few days, then weekly. Record per trap/station: take, captures, fresh
signs. Non-toxic monitoring blocks or tracking patches show remaining activity.
Two consecutive visits with no captures, no fresh droppings and no takes = control.

## Gotchas
- Treating without proofing gives you a subscription, not a fix.
- Rats avoid traps you set straight away; mice don't — don't use the same playbook.
- A smell in a wall after baiting is a dead rodent; you may need to open the void.
- Bait station on an open floor in a busy room is a liability and a poor placement anyway.

## Verify
- [ ] Species identified from droppings/behaviour before choosing method.
- [ ] Sign map made; traps placed on runs, perpendicular, close spacing for mice.
- [ ] Any bait is labelled-use only, in secured stations, logged on a map.
- [ ] All gaps a mouse or rat can pass sealed with gnaw-proof material.
- [ ] Two consecutive clean visits recorded before closing the job.
