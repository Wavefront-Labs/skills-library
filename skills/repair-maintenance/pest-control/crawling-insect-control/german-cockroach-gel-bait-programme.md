# German cockroach gel-bait programme

**Goal:** take a German cockroach infestation (kitchens, flats, restaurants) to zero
using baits placed where they live, and prove it with trap counts — rather than
spraying the visible ones and scattering the rest.

> Product names and active ingredients vary by market and label; some are restricted
> in food-handling areas. Follow the label.

## 1. Confirm and measure
- German cockroach: about 12 mm (0.5 in), light brown, **two dark stripes on the pronotum**.
  Lives indoors near warmth, water and food. Large, reddish-brown roaches coming from
  drains (American/Oriental) need a different, drain-focused approach.
- Place **sticky monitor traps** (glue boards in folded cards) in every room with
  food or water: behind fridge, under sink, beside dishwasher, in cupboard corners,
  near motors. Check daily for several days (many catches come in the first 24 hours).
- Count per trap and note **nymphs** (breeding population) vs adults only (possible
  introduction). The counts are your baseline.

## 2. Inspect harborage
Roaches hide in cracks they can touch top and bottom.
Torch and flushing aerosol (sparingly — it scatters them) or simply a mirror:
- Fridge/stove motor housings and the gap behind them.
- Hinges, cabinet corners, under drawers, behind kick plates.
- Electrical appliances: microwaves, coffee machines, toasters, routers.
- Door frames and wall-plumbing voids.
Look for faecal spotting (pepper-like specks), shed skins, egg cases (oothecae).

## 3. Bait like a sniper
- **Many small placements, close to harborage**: small dabs along cracks, hinges and
  corners where spotting is. Heavier at
  the hotspots from your trap map.
- Place **in** cracks and crevices, not on open exposed surfaces where it gets wiped
  or contaminated.
- **Avoid spraying residual insecticides on or near bait** — sprays give only short-term
  knockdown and can scatter roaches; follow the bait label.
- Where the label allows, add an **IGR** to help break the breeding cycle.
- Use insecticidal dust (e.g. boric acid, silica) lightly in voids and wall
  penetrations — a light film, not a pile (roaches walk around piles).

## 4. Fix sanitation and access
Client work list: degrease behind appliances, no dishes overnight, sealed food
containers, fix dripping taps, remove cardboard (harborage and egg transport),
seal pipe penetrations. Without it, you're feeding them a competing buffet.

## 5. Follow up and rotate
- Revisit after about a week (baits may take 7 days or longer to cut numbers): re-trap,
  replace consumed bait, count.
- Keep monitoring with traps until catches stop.
- If traps keep catching: look for competing food, move or add baits, or try a
  **different bait product**; re-clean, re-bait.
- Close out after two consecutive visits with zero catches.

## Ant note (same principles)
For trailing ants, find the trail, follow it to the nest/entry, and bait **on the
trail** with a slow-acting sugar or protein bait (test which they take). Don't spray
the trail — it works against baiting.

## Gotchas
- Total-release foggers ("bug bombs") often fail because they don't reach the crevices.
- In multi-unit buildings treat adjoining units too, or you're just pushing them next door.
- Old dried bait is not attractive; replace it, don't add to it.
- Bait inside a cupboard with lots of crumbs gets ignored.

## Verify
- [ ] Species confirmed; baseline trap counts by location recorded.
- [ ] Bait placed as small dots in cracks at the hotspots, no residual sprays nearby.
- [ ] IGR used where nymphs present.
- [ ] Sanitation list given to client and checked on follow-up.
- [ ] Counts fall each visit; rotation applied if bait uptake stalls; two zero visits to close.
