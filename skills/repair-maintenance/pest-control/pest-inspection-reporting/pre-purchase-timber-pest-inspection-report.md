# Pre-purchase timber pest (WDO) inspection report

**Goal:** inspect a property for wood-destroying organisms and conducive conditions,
and write a report that is accurate, defensible and tells the buyer (or owner)
exactly what was found, what wasn't checked, and what to do next.

> Report formats are often mandated — e.g. the NPMA-33 form in the US (used for
> HUD/VA-guaranteed transactions; a state-mandated form that excludes other forms
> takes precedence) and AS 4349.3 in Australia. This skill gives the method; use the required
> form and wording for your jurisdiction, and your insurer's terms.

## 1. Agree the scope in writing first
Before the visit: an inspection agreement stating the property, the pests covered
(subterranean and drywood termites, wood borers, wood decay fungi, carpenter ants/bees
as relevant), visual non-invasive inspection only, what is excluded, the report
deadline, and fee. Ask the agent/owner for access to subfloor, roof space and
outbuildings, and that pets are secured.

## 2. Kit
Torch (high-lumen + spare), sounding tool, moisture meter (pin and pinless), probe/
awl (permission needed for any probing that marks), ladder, coveralls, mask, camera,
thermal imager and/or termite radar (supplementary — never report on them alone),
mirror, a plan sketch sheet.

## 3. Systematic walk-through (same order every time)
1. **Site:** fences, retaining walls, stumps, trees, garden edges, timber in soil,
   woodpiles against the house. Outbuildings within the agreed scope.
2. **Exterior:** foundation/slab edges (edge exposed? covered by paths or garden beds?),
   weep holes, downpipes and drainage, decks and pergolas, window and door frames.
3. **Subfloor:** piers, bearers, joists, ant caps, ventilation, moisture, plumbing
   leaks, debris. Note clearance (too low to enter = limitation).
4. **Interior:** every room — skirting, architraves, floors, wet areas, built-in
   cupboards. Sound timber; look for mud, blistering paint, frass.
5. **Roof space:** trusses/rafters, top plates, around penetrations, signs of leaks.

At every finding: **photo + location on sketch + what it is + active or not**.

## 4. Classify findings
- **Live activity** (live insects/fungus) vs **evidence of past activity** (old
  workings, damage, treatment drill holes) vs **damage** (with visible extent).
- **Conducive conditions:** excess moisture (high meter readings in timber),
  inadequate ventilation, timber-soil contact, bridged slab edges, leaking showers.
- **Previous treatment:** drill holes, bait stations, durable notice in meter box.
- **Limitations and inaccessible areas** — be explicit: furnished rooms, stored goods,
  insulation covering timbers, floor coverings, wall linings, areas without safe
  access. Each limitation means risk; say so.

## 5. Write the report
Structure:
1. Property, date, time, weather (recent rain changes moisture readings), who was present.
2. Scope and type of inspection.
3. **Summary in plain language**: active timber pests found Y/N; damage found Y/N;
   conducive conditions Y/N; overall risk (low/moderate/high) with reasons.
4. Findings table: location, observation, photo ref, active/past, recommendation.
5. Limitations and inaccessible areas, and recommended further investigation
   (invasive inspection, plumber, structural engineer for damage extent).
6. Recommendations: treatment proposal (method, scope, estimated cost — separately
   if required by regulation), remediation of conducive conditions, re-inspection
   interval.

Write facts, not guesses: "mud tubes on north-east pier, broken open, live workers
present" — not "termites probably". Don't estimate structural adequacy of damaged
timber unless qualified; refer to a structural engineer.

## Gotchas
- "No visible evidence" is not "no termites" — the wording matters legally.
- A freshly painted or patched area in an older house is a reason to look harder.
- Don't move heavy furniture or lift fixed coverings without permission — record as limitation.
- Undisclosed conflicts (you also sell the treatment) must be declared where required.

## Verify
- [ ] Signed scope/agreement before inspection.
- [ ] Site, exterior, subfloor, interior, roof space all covered or limitations stated.
- [ ] Every finding has photo, location and active/past classification.
- [ ] Summary answers active pests / damage / conducive conditions in plain words.
- [ ] Recommendations include further investigation for limitations and a re-inspection interval.

## Checked

Date checked: 2026-10-25. Jurisdiction: United States (NPMA-33) and Australia (AS 4349.3); state or national rules and the required form govern.
NPMA-33 completion guidelines (rev 2019): the form is used for HUD/VA-guaranteed transactions and typically conventional sales, a state-mandated form that excludes other forms must be used instead, findings are based on visual inspection of readily accessible areas, and it covers termites, carpenter ants, carpenter bees and reinfesting wood-boring beetles. AS 4349.3-2010 (timber pest inspections) is cited in Australian timber pest reports; the standard's text is paid, so only its title and use were confirmed. Removed unconfirmed claims: 20 percent moisture reading threshold and the 12-month re-inspection interval.
