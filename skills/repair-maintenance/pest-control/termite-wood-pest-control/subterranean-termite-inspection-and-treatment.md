# Subterranean termite inspection and treatment

**Goal:** confirm whether termites (or another wood pest) are active, decide the right
treatment for the building, and apply it so the colony is cut off or eliminated —
not just the visible workers.

> Termiticide products, rates and licensing are jurisdiction-specific (US state
> structural pest licences, Australian AS 3660 series, etc.). The product label governs
> every rate, depth and spacing; none are given here.

## 1. Identify what you are dealing with
| Clue | Subterranean termite | Drywood termite | Flying ant | Wood-boring beetle |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Body | straight waist, straight antennae, pale | same | pinched waist, elbowed antennae | hard shell |
| Wings (swarmers) | 2 pairs equal length, shed easily | equal | front pair longer | — |
| Evidence | **mud tubes**, soil-packed galleries | **frass pellets**, kick-out holes | — | round/oval exit holes + fine powder |

Subterranean termites need soil contact or a constant moisture source; they build
pencil-width mud tubes over foundations to reach wood.

## 2. Inspect systematically
Kit: torch, sounding tool (screwdriver handle), moisture meter, probe, ladder, a
thermal camera or termite radar if you have one (aids, not proof).
- Walk the **perimeter** outside: foundation walls, slab edges, weep holes, where
  paths/garden beds/mulch are banked above the slab edge or damp-proof course.
- Subfloor/crawlspace: piers, bearers, joists, plumbing penetrations.
- Inside: skirting boards, door frames, bathroom and kitchen wet walls, window sills.
  Tap and listen for hollow sounds; look for bubbling paint and mud in joints.
- High moisture readings in timber are a red flag (leaks, poor ventilation).
- Break a small section of tube: live workers and moist mud = active; dry and empty
  = old (but the colony may have moved, not died).

**Do not disturb active workings before deciding on bait** — baits need foraging
termites to keep coming back.

## 3. Choose the treatment
- **Liquid soil barrier (non-repellent):** fipronil or imidacloprid class. Termites
  forage through it and carry it back (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked). The EPA label text could not be retrieved. Fast, whole-structure protection.
  - Trench and rod along the foundation, as the label directs, mixing treated soil
    back in.
  - Slabs: drill through along expansion joints/penetrations, inject, then **plug and
    patch** the holes. Volumes, depths and spacings come from the label.
  - Avoid wells, cisterns, drains and sumps; check label setback distances.
- **Bait system:** in-ground stations around the perimeter at the spacing the bait label
  gives, plus above-ground stations on active workings. Slow (weeks to months) but
  targets the colony with minimal chemical. Needs ongoing monitoring.
- **Spot/foam treatments:** inject into galleries for localised activity or
  drywood termites; fumigation for widespread drywood (see the fumigation skill).
- **Wood borers:** confirm activity (fresh pale frass, new holes), fix the moisture,
  then a borate surface treatment on bare wood; often activity has already ended.

## 4. Fix the conditions
Treatment fails if conditions stay: remove timber-soil contact, lower garden beds
below the slab edge or DPC, fix leaks and downpipes, ventilate subfloors, clear
cellulose debris under the house.

## Gotchas
- Repellent pyrethroid barriers with gaps let termites find the gap; non-repellents
  tolerate small gaps better.
- Spraying visible termites before baiting kills the couriers you need.
- Swarmers inside in spring = a colony inside or under the structure; outside on a
  stump may be unrelated.
- Old damage looks scary but may be inactive; report activity and damage separately.

## Verify
- [ ] Pest confirmed by specimen or definitive evidence, not just "damage".
- [ ] Perimeter, subfloor and wet areas all inspected and noted on a plan.
- [ ] Treatment method matches site (wells, slab type, client tolerance for chemicals).
- [ ] Label rate, volume used and drill locations recorded.
- [ ] Conducive conditions listed for the client; re-inspection booked.
