# Dent repair, filler and spot refinish

**Goal:** make a dented steel panel straight and invisible with the least filler and
the smallest refinish area. Structural damage (pillars, rails, high-strength steel, airbags
deployed) is collision-structural work that needs OEM repair procedures and measuring
equipment — out of scope here. Isocyanate (2K) paints require a supplied-air respirator
and a spray booth; don't spray 2K with a dust mask.

## 1. Decide the method
- **Paintless dent repair (PDR):** paint unbroken, dent shallow and soft-edged, access behind
  the panel or glue-pull possible. Fastest, no refinish. Creases and stretched metal are harder.
- **Pull/straighten + thin filler + refinish:** paint cracked, sharp creases, or no access.
- **Replace panel:** severe stretch, tears, or repair cost > replacement.
- Check panel material: magnet doesn't stick → aluminium (needs separate, dedicated tools
  to avoid galvanic contamination) or plastic.

## 2. Straighten the metal (the goal is ≤ ~ 3 mm low before filler)
1. Wash and degrease (wax and grease remover). Assess with a straight edge and
   by hand (flat palm, eyes closed — fingers feel highs/lows better than eyes).
2. Work from **outside in**: relieve the ridges (highs) around the dent with a body hammer on
   a dolly ("off-dolly" to lower highs, "on-dolly" to stretch/raise), then the low centre.
3. No back access: **stud welder** (weld pins/washers, pull with a slide hammer, grind off)
   or glue-pull tabs.
4. Stretched metal (oil-canning): shrink with a shrinking disc or heat-and-quench carefully.
5. Grind paint to bare metal ~ 25–50 mm beyond the damage with 80-grit (P80); taper the
   paint edge (featheredge) with P120–P180.

## 3. Filler
- Two-part polyester body filler; hardener ~ 2 % by weight (a ribbon of hardener across the
  diameter of a golf-ball-sized blob — follow the can). Mix by folding, not whipping (air
  pinholes).
- Apply **thin** (ideally ≤ 3–6 mm), pressing the first coat hard into the 80-grit scratches.
  Don't apply over primer or paint unless the product allows; never over rust.
- When "cheesy" (a few minutes), shape with a cheese grater/coarse file; when cured,
  **block sand** with P80 then P120/P180 on a long block, crosshatching.
- **Guide coat** (dry powder or a light mist of contrasting colour): low spots stay dark
  after sanding — fill and re-sand until it sands evenly.
- Glazing putty or fine finishing filler for pinholes.

## 4. Primer
- Bare metal: **epoxy primer** or etch primer (per the paint system) for adhesion/corrosion.
- **2K high-build (urethane) primer** over filler, 2–3 coats with flash time between.
  Cure, guide coat, block sand with P320–P400 (dry) or P600 (wet for basecoat).
- Sand the surrounding blend area with P800–P1000 or a grey scuff pad so new paint adheres.

## 5. Base and clear (blend)
1. Get the **paint code** (door jamb/VIN plate). Mix from the formula; check variants
   and spray a **test card (spray-out card)** to compare against the car in daylight.
2. Mask well back; tack-rag; anti-static wipe.
3. HVLP gun set per the paint TDS (e.g. 1.2–1.4 mm tip for base, ~ 2 bar at the cap
   is commonly cited for HVLP inlet — check the gun's spec).
4. **Basecoat:** apply to cover the primer, then extend each coat further out to **blend**
   into the adjacent original paint (fade the edge), letting each flash matte.
5. **Clearcoat:** 2 full wet coats over the whole panel (clear usually goes edge to edge
   on the panel; spot-blend clear only with a blending solvent technique where accepted).
6. Cure per the TDS (air dry or bake ~ 60 °C for 30 min typical).

## 6. Finish
Wet sand dust nibs or orange peel with P1500–P3000, then compound and polish with a
machine polisher (foam pads), finishing with a fine polish. Don't cut through clear on edges.

## Gotchas
- Thick filler cracks and shrinks — straighten better instead.
- Silicone contamination causes "fisheyes"; degrease repeatedly and avoid silicone
  sprays in the shop.
- Metallic/pearl colours need consistent gun distance and overlap or they mottle.
- Uncertain: mix ratios, pressures and cure temps differ per product — the TDS is the authority.

## Verify
- [ ] Damage checked as non-structural; method chosen (PDR/fill/replace) with reason.
- [ ] Metal within ~ 3 mm before filler; filler thin and over bare 80-grit metal.
- [ ] Guide coat shows a flat surface; primer blocked smooth.
- [ ] Spray-out card matches; base blended, clear even, cured per TDS.
- [ ] Final finish matches gloss and texture; correct PPE/booth used for 2K.
