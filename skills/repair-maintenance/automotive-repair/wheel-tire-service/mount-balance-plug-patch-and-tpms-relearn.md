# Mount, balance, plug-patch and TPMS relearn

**Goal:** fit and repair tyres so they hold air, run smooth, keep TPMS working and
stay on the car. The two serious risks are an exploding tyre during bead seating and a
wheel coming off from wrong torque.

## 1. Inspect before work
- Tread depth (legal minimum: 1.6 mm in the EU/UK; 2/32 in many US states — replacement
  recommended earlier, ~ 3 mm/4/32 in for wet grip).
- **DOT date code** (last four digits = week/year, e.g. "2319" = week 23 of 2019); many
  makers advise replacement at 6–10 years regardless of tread.
- Sidewall cuts, bulges (cord damage — not repairable), uneven wear (alignment), cracking.

## 2. Demount and mount (tyre changer)
1. Deflate fully (remove valve core). Note TPMS sensor position (valve stem) and
   **start the bead breaker ~ 90° away from the valve/sensor** so the shovel doesn't crush it.
2. Lubricate both beads and the rim with tyre lubricant (not grease/silicone spray).
3. Clamp the wheel; on alloy wheels use plastic protectors on the mount head.
4. Start the bead at the valve's position as the changer manual indicates so the sensor
   stays in the safe zone during the bead roll; for low-profile/run-flat tyres use a bead
   pressing tool/helper arm.
5. Mount the new tyre with direction/rotation arrows and outside markings correct; align
   any red/yellow match marks per the tyre/rim maker if road-force or match-mounting.
6. **Bead seating:** inflate with a clip-on chuck and an inline gauge while standing clear
   (not over the tyre). Never exceed the tyre's maximum seating pressure moulded on the
   sidewall (commonly 40 psi / 2.75 bar for passenger tyres). If beads won't seat at that
   pressure, deflate, re-lubricate and retry — don't add more pressure.
7. Fit a new valve (or service kit for TPMS sensors: nut, grommet, core) and inflate to the
   vehicle placard pressure (door jamb), not the sidewall maximum.

## 3. Balancing
- Clean the wheel's mounting face; centre on the balancer with the correct cone/flange
  (hub-centric) — mounting error is the No. 1 cause of "balanced but vibrates".
- Enter rim dimensions (auto arm), choose weight mode: **clip-on** for steel/compatible
  rims, **adhesive** ("ALU" modes, hidden behind spokes) for alloys.
- Dynamic balance to ≤ ~ 5 g (0.25 oz) residual per plane, typical; re-spin to confirm.
- Persistent vibration: **road-force** measurement and match-mount (rotate tyre on rim to
  match high spot of tyre to low spot of wheel).

## 4. Puncture repair (industry standard: off the rim, plug-patch)
- **Repairable only in the tread area** (not shoulder/sidewall), injury ≤ 1/4 in (6 mm)
  diameter for passenger tyres, and not overlapping previous repairs. No repair if the tyre
  was run flat (look inside for a rubbed band or rubber crumbs).
- Run-flat tyre repairs: follow the tyre maker's policy (some forbid them).
1. Demount; inspect inside.
2. Probe the injury angle; ream from inside out with a carbide cutter.
3. Buff the inner liner around the hole to a smooth texture (low-speed buffer), clean
   and apply vulcanising cement, let dry.
4. Pull a **combination plug-patch** (mushroom) through from inside, stitch the patch down
   from the centre outward, trim the stem flush on the tread.
5. Seal the buffed area with inner-liner sealant.
- String "plugs" from outside only and sealant-only fixes are temporary in industry
  guidance.

## 5. TPMS
- **Direct TPMS** (sensor in each wheel) after tyre rotation/new sensors usually needs a
  **relearn**: auto-relearn by driving, stationary relearn via the car's menu with a TPMS
  tool triggering each sensor in order, or OBD relearn (writing IDs to the module). Look up
  the procedure for the vehicle.
- Aftermarket/programmable sensors: clone the old ID or program for the vehicle.
- **Indirect TPMS** (ABS wheel-speed based): reset/calibrate via the menu after inflating
  all tyres to placard.

## 6. Refit and torque
- Clean hub and wheel faces; no lubricant on studs unless the maker specifies.
- Snug in a star pattern, lower the car, **torque with a calibrated torque wrench** to spec
  (often ~ 100–140 N·m passenger cars). Impact guns only with torque sticks, then check by hand.
- Advise a re-torque check after ~ 50–100 km (common on alloys).

## Gotchas
- Mixing tyre types/tread patterns across an axle affects handling and AWD systems
  (some AWD vehicles require all four tyres to match circumference closely).
- Uncertain: torque and pressure numbers are typical; vehicle and tyre maker data govern.

## Verify
- [ ] Tyre age, damage and tread checked; repair within the repairable area/size.
- [ ] TPMS sensor undamaged; new service kit; relearn completed, no warning light.
- [ ] Beads seated without exceeding the sidewall seating pressure; placard pressure set.
- [ ] Balanced on a correctly centred mount; residual within tolerance.
- [ ] Wheel nuts torqued with a wrench in a star pattern; re-torque advised.
