# Regluing chairs and repairing frame joints

**Goal:** make a wobbly chair or frame rigid again by fully remaking the joints, so the
repair lasts decades and can still be undone by the next restorer.

## 1. Diagnose before you dismantle
- Rock the piece on a flat floor and flex each joint by hand; mark every loose one with
  masking tape and a number. Usually several are loose even if one shows.
- Look for previous repairs: nails, screws, metal brackets, glue blobs, filler — all must
  come out. Squirting glue into a loose joint ("glue injection") almost never lasts: new
  glue will not bond to old dirty glue.
- Identify the glue: **hide glue** (pre-1950s, amber, brittle, softens with heat and
  moisture) or **PVA/modern** (rubbery, white or yellow). Hide glue can be reactivated;
  PVA must be scraped out mechanically.

## 2. Dismantle
- Take apart only the loose joints if the rest are sound. Use a **rubber or deadblow
  mallet** with a softwood block; strike close to the joint, never the middle of a rail.
- Stubborn hide glue: inject warm water + a little vinegar or alcohol through a 1.5 mm
  hole drilled in a hidden spot, wait 30 min, repeat; or heat with a heat gun on low
  (watch the finish). A reversed sash clamp spreads the frame gently.
- Look for hidden pins or wedges in tenons (pegged through the leg) before forcing —
  drill them out with a bit slightly smaller than the pin.
- Label each part and its mortise (e.g. "FL-1"); photos help.

## 3. Clean and refit
- Remove **all** old glue from tenons, dowels and mortises with a scraper, chisel, warm
  water (hide glue) or a sharp blade (PVA). Don't remove wood — that loosens the fit.
- Fit test: a tenon should slide in with firm hand pressure. Loose:
  - **Tenon:** glue on a veneer/shaving shim (long grain to long grain) to the cheeks,
    plane to fit. Never fill gaps with glue — only epoxy tolerates gaps, and it isn't
    reversible.
  - **Round tenon/dowel (spindle chairs):** wrap with a thin shaving or turn a new tenon
    on a lathe; a broken dowel is drilled out (centre punch, brad-point bit, step up
    sizes) and replaced with a fluted dowel the same diameter.
- **Dry-fit the whole assembly** with clamps before any glue. Rehearse the clamp order.

## 4. Glue choice
| Glue | Open time | Reversible | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hot hide glue (192 g bloom, 60–63 °C pot) | ~1 min | yes, heat/water | antiques, veneer |
| Liquid hide glue (Old Brown Glue, Titebond Liquid Hide) | 20–30 min | yes | complex chair glue-ups |
| PVA (Titebond Original/II) | 5–10 min | barely | modern pieces |
| Epoxy (slow cure) | 30+ min | no | gap-filling, last resort |
Hide glue sticks to old hide glue, so it is the default on antiques.

## 5. Broken legs and parts
- Clean break with long grain both sides: glue directly, clamp with cauls; add a hidden
  dowel across the break if it's short-grain.
- Short-grain break or missing piece: **scarf splice** — plane matching 1:8 slopes on
  old and new wood (same species, grain matched), glue, then shape and colour.
- Replace a rung/stretcher by turning or shaping a copy from the broken one's pattern.

## 6. Glue up and clamp
- Glue both surfaces thinly; work in sub-assemblies (e.g. back legs + rail, then front,
  then side rails). **Band/strap clamps** for chairs; sash clamps with pads for frames.
- Check square by measuring **diagonals** (equal within ~1 mm) and check the chair sits
  on all four feet on a flat surface before glue gels. Adjust clamp angle to correct.
- Wipe squeeze-out with a damp cloth (hide glue) or after it rubberises (PVA).
- Leave 12–24 h before load.

## Gotchas
- Nails or screws through joints split the wood further — remove, then plug holes.
- Corner blocks under seat rails carry much of the load; reglue and screw them back.
- A chair that rocks after glue-up: trim the long foot only after confirming joints are
  square; don't saw legs to cure a twisted frame.
- Heat and water can damage the finish; protect with tape and work locally.
- (Draft note: hide-glue temperatures and bloom strengths are typical, check the supplier.)

## Verify
- [ ] Every joint flexed by hand is rigid; no creak under seated load.
- [ ] Diagonals equal; chair stands on all four feet without rocking.
- [ ] No glue squeeze-out left on visible surfaces.
- [ ] No metal fixings through joints; old repairs removed.
- [ ] Glue used is reversible on antiques (hide glue) unless there was a reason not to.
