# French polishing and repairing shellac finishes

**Goal:** build a thin, deep, glass-clear shellac finish with a rubber, or bring a worn
original shellac surface back without stripping — shellac redissolves in alcohol, so
almost every fault is repairable in place.

## 1. Materials
- **Shellac:** flakes dissolved fresh in denatured alcohol / methylated spirits (the
  clearer "finishing" grade, not purple meths if you can avoid it). Grades: blonde/dewaxed
  (pale, clear), button/garnet (warm brown, traditional on mahogany and walnut).
  Mixed shellac goes off in about 6 months — it stays soft; test a drop on glass, it
  should dry hard in minutes.
- **Cuts:** 2 lb-cut ≈ 240 g flakes per litre of alcohol, for bodying; 1 lb-cut for
  washcoats and final work. Strain through a paint filter.
- **Rubber:** a wad of cotton wool or wool wadding (~a golf ball) inside a square of
  lint-free cotton/linen, twisted to a smooth pear shape with no creases on the face.
  Store in an airtight jar between sessions.
- Raw linseed oil (a drop as lubricant), fine pumice (FFFF) for grain filling, 0000
  steel wool or 400–600 grit paper.

## 2. Building a polish (new or stripped wood)
1. Sand to 180–220, dust off. Seal with 1 lb-cut by brush or rubber; let dry 1 h,
   denib with 320.
2. **Grain fill (open-grain woods):** sprinkle pumice, rub with a rubber charged with
   alcohol only, in small circles — pumice and wood dust are packed into the pores.
   Repeat until pores are level.
3. **Bodying up:** charge the rubber by opening it and adding 2 lb-cut to the wadding;
   press on paper — it should be damp, never dripping. Touch a fingertip of linseed oil
   to the face. Glide on and off the surface (never stop on it — it will stick and
   tear) in small circles, then figure-eights, then long straight strokes covering the
   whole panel. Light pressure at first, more as the rubber dries. Do 3–4 rubbers' worth
   per session, leave overnight, repeat for 3–6 sessions. Each session sinks slightly.
4. **Spiriting off / stiffing:** last session, cut the shellac with more alcohol in the
   rubber, finishing with almost-dry alcohol in long straight strokes with the grain.
   This removes the oil smears and leaves the gloss. Oil streaks that remain look like
   a smoky haze in raking light — more spiriting.
5. For a satin look, after a week cut back with 0000 steel wool and wax along the grain.

## 3. Reviving old shellac
- Clean first: a 1:1 mix of white spirit and water with a drop of washing-up liquid on
  a damp cloth (test first), or proprietary reviver. Dry immediately.
- **Dull, slightly crazed surface:** a rubber with 1 lb-cut thinned further, a light
  "skim" in straight strokes, often enough. Fine crazing can be reamalgamated with a
  rubber barely damp with alcohol — the surface melts and flows back. Work fast, test first.
- **White water/heat rings:** moisture trapped in the film. Try, in order: warm air from
  a hairdryer at a distance; a rubber barely damp with alcohol wiped quickly over; very
  fine abrasive (rottenstone) with a little oil. **Black** rings are water in the wood
  (oxalic acid bleach after removing finish locally) — a different, bigger job.
- **Scratches and chips:** drop-fill with 2 lb-cut on a fine brush in layers, let each
  harden, level with a sharp blade and 1000 grit, then pad over. Deep chips: shellac
  burn-in stick melted with a burn-in knife (keep it below smoking), levelled, padded.
- **Colour loss on edges:** tint the shellac with alcohol-soluble dye powder, or pigment
  in a separate "touch-up" rubber.

## Gotchas
- Shellac is ruined by alcohol spills and heat; warn the owner (coasters, no drinks parties).
- Too much oil in the rubber gives a gloss that turns cloudy; too wet a rubber drags and
  leaves ridges — a rubber that sticks means stop, let it dry 20 min.
- Don't polish in cold or damp rooms (<15 °C, high humidity): it blooms milky.
- Linseed-oiled rubbers and rags can self-heat: store in a sealed jar or spread flat outdoors.
- Alcohol vapour is flammable — no flames, ventilate.
- (Draft note: cut weights and session counts vary widely by tradition; treat as starting points.)

## Verify
- [ ] Shellac tested fresh (dries hard on glass).
- [ ] Surface shows even gloss with no oil smears or rubber marks in raking light.
- [ ] Pores filled to the level wanted; no grey pumice in the grain.
- [ ] Repairs (rings, chips) invisible from a normal viewing angle.
- [ ] Rubbers stored airtight; oily wadding disposed of safely.
