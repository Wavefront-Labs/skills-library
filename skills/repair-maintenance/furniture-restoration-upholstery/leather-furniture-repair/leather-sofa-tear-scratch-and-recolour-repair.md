# Leather sofa tear, scratch and recolour repair

**Goal:** make worn, torn or scratched leather upholstery look uniform and feel like
leather again, with a repair flexible enough to survive being sat on. For garments see
`repair-maintenance.laundry-textile-care.leather-suede-care.cleaning-conditioning-and-salt-stains`;
for shoes see `repair-maintenance.shoe-repair.upper-repair-refinishing.leather-upper-recolour-and-scuff-repair`.

## 1. Identify the leather (decides everything)
- **Water-drop test** in a hidden spot (under a cushion, back of skirt):
  - Darkens and soaks in within seconds → **aniline/nubuck** (unfinished). Colour is dye
    in the hide; pigment repairs will look painted. Repairs are limited to dye and wax.
  - Slowly soaks in → **semi-aniline** (light pigment). Thin pigment works.
  - Beads and sits → **pigmented/protected** leather — most sofas; fully repairable.
- **Bonded/bicast** (leather fibres or PU film on a fabric backing): peeling flakes, a
  fabric backing visible at tears. You can't really restore it; options are a full
  recoat with flexible filler (short life) or re-covering.

## 2. Clean and prep
1. Vacuum seams. Clean with a pH-neutral leather cleaner and a soft brush, working in
   small areas, wipe with a damp microfibre.
2. For recolouring, **degrease** with a leather prep solvent or isopropyl alcohol on a
   cloth or 1000-grit abrasive pad — body oils on headrests and arms stop colour sticking.
   Wipe until the cloth comes away clean; aim to remove the old topcoat sheen, not colour.
3. Lightly sand raised fibres and cracks with 600–800 grit; don't sand through to grain.

## 3. Tears and holes
1. Trim loose fibres; cut a **sub-patch** of thin canvas or leather 2 cm larger than the
   tear all round, rounded corners.
2. Insert under the tear with tweezers or a spatula, flat and wrinkle-free.
3. Apply flexible leather glue (or contact adhesive) to the patch and the underside of
   the tear edges; press edges together over the patch, butting, not overlapping.
4. Fill any gap with **flexible leather filler** in thin layers (≤0.5 mm), drying each
   with a hairdryer, sanding level with 600 grit between. Thick single layers crack.
5. Optional: press a grain pad/impression paper onto the last filler layer while tacky
   to copy the surrounding texture.

## 4. Cat scratches
- Shallow scuffs on pigmented leather: sand the raised fibres flat, apply **leather
  binder** (a thin water-based resin) 3–5 coats with a sponge, drying between, then
  sand smooth — this glues the fibres back down. Then filler for remaining pits.
- Aniline leather: trim/sand fibres, rub in matching dye and a leather balm or wax;
  expect improvement, not invisibility.

## 5. Recolouring
1. Match colour in daylight on the cleanest original area (often under a cushion);
   suppliers mix to a sample. Test on a hidden spot and let it dry — water-based
   colourants dry darker or lighter.
2. Apply with a sponge (dabbing, thin coats) or **airbrush/HVLP** at low pressure
   (~1.5–2 bar) in 3–6 light coats, drying each with a hairdryer. Feather edges for a
   local repair; panel-to-seam for a full recolour.
3. Seal with a **topcoat** matched to the original sheen (matt, satin, gloss) — 2 coats.
   Allow 24–48 h before use.

## 6. Conditioning
Pigmented leather barely absorbs conditioner — a light wipe-on is enough every 6–12
months. Aniline benefits more. Over-conditioning leaves sticky residue that attracts dirt.

## Gotchas
- Silicone or wax "sprays" from previous care stop everything sticking: degrease harder.
- Solvent-based products: ventilate, gloves; keep away from heat.
- Don't heat-gun filler; it blisters.
- Sun-faded top surfaces may need full-panel recolouring; spot repairs will show.

## Verify
- [ ] Leather type identified by water-drop test before choosing a product.
- [ ] Tear edges flush, no ridge of filler; flexes without cracking when pinched.
- [ ] Colour matches under daylight from normal sitting distance; sheen matches.
- [ ] No tackiness after 48 h; colour doesn't transfer to a white cloth.
