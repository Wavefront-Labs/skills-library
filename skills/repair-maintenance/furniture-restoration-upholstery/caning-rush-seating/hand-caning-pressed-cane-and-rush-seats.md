# Hand caning, pressed cane and rush seats

**Goal:** replace a broken woven seat with the right material and method for the frame,
tensioned so it is taut, even and lasts years.

## 1. Identify the seat type from the frame
- **Row of drilled holes** around the seat opening → hand (strand) caning.
- **Groove** routed around the opening (often with a wedge of reed in it) → pressed
  (sheet) cane with spline.
- **Rails with no holes, rounded corners or corner blocks** → rush, fibre rush, Danish
  cord (look for L-shaped nails on the underside of rails), or splint/Shaker tape.

## 2. Hand caning (seven-step)
- **Size cane by hole size and spacing:** measure hole diameter and centre-to-centre
  spacing. e.g. 5 mm holes at ~16 mm centres → "medium" (≈2.5–2.75 mm) cane; 6 mm holes
  at ~19 mm → "common". Binder cane one size wider. (Draft note: size names vary by
  supplier — check their chart.)
- Soak cane in warm water 10–20 min (don't oversoak; it goes fuzzy); keep it damp with a
  towel while working. **Shiny side up** always; never let it twist.
- Steps: 1) verticals front to back, 2) horizontals laid on top, 3) second verticals on
  top of everything (slightly offset), 4) horizontals **woven** over-under through steps
  1/3 — this is the first real weaving and sets tension, 5) diagonal one way, 6) diagonal
  the other way (weaving, forming the classic octagons), 7) binder cane laid over the
  holes and couched with a thin strand through each hole (or every other).
- Peg each hole temporarily with golf tees or cane pegs as you go; tie off ends
  underneath by looping under adjacent strands — never knot over a hole you'll need.
- Fit is loose while wet and tightens as it dries — tension steps 1–3 snug, not tight.

## 3. Pressed/sheet cane
1. Clean out the old spline: score along both edges with a utility knife, soften with
   hot water 1–2 h, chisel it out. Clean the groove fully.
2. Measure groove width and depth; buy spline that fits snugly (common 4–6 mm) and
   sheet cane with the same mesh as the original (e.g. 13 mm/½" fine open mesh). Cut the
   sheet 2.5 cm bigger than the groove all round.
3. Soak cane 30–60 min, spline 10–15 min.
4. Lay shiny side up, mesh aligned to the front rail. Drive cane into the groove with a
   hardwood wedge and mallet — front first, then back, then sides, keeping lines straight.
5. Trim the cane at the bottom inside edge of the groove with a sharp chisel.
6. Run wood glue (PVA, white or yellow) into the groove, tap the spline in, mitre corners
   or join at the back. Let dry 24 h — it shrinks drum-tight.

## 4. Rush, Danish cord and splint
- **Fibre/paper rush:** work around the four corners in a continuous figure-of-eight
  wrap, front corners first if the seat is wider at the front (fill the difference with
  short strands at the front corners until the remaining opening is square). Stuff
  triangular pockets with cardboard or rush offcuts every few rounds for a firm dome.
- **Danish cord:** 3-ply paper cord wrapped over the rails and hooked on the L-nails
  under the rail; weave in the original pattern (photograph before removing). Tension
  by hand — no soaking.
- **Splint/Shaker tape:** warp wraps over the front–back rails, weft woven in a twill or
  herringbone on top. Natural splint is soaked; cotton tape is not.

## Gotchas
- Don't hand-cane a grooved seat or vice versa — the frame dictates the method.
- Soaking too long makes cane soft and grey; drying too fast in sun or heat makes it
  crack — work in shade.
- Spline too big splits the rail; too small pops out.
- Old seats may hide broken rails — repair the frame before weaving.
- Use an awl rather than a knife near the cane; sharp blades nick strands under tension.

## Verify
- [ ] Correct method for the frame type; old material and pegs fully removed.
- [ ] Cane shiny side up throughout, no twists or splits.
- [ ] Weave lines parallel to the front rail; octagons even (hand caning).
- [ ] Seat taut when dry, doesn't sag under a pressing palm.
- [ ] Ends secured underneath; spline flush and tight.
