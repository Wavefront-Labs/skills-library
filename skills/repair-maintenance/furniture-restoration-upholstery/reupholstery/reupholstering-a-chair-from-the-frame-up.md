# Reupholstering a chair from the frame up

**Goal:** rebuild a seat that is firm, level, crisp at the edges and has a cover pulled
straight on grain with no wrinkles — and a frame that survives the next reupholstery.

## 1. Strip and record
- Photograph every stage of stripping: the old layers are your pattern for order, pleat
  positions and where the cover was pulled through.
- Work with a **ripping chisel** and mallet, striking **with the grain** along the rail,
  never into the frame. Remove every tack and staple; pliers for stragglers.
- Keep the old cover pieces — they become cutting templates (add 5 cm all round).
- Mask up (FFP2/N95): old stuffing is dusty, may hold mould, insect debris, and in
  some pre-1980s pieces asbestos-containing felt (rare; if unsure, stop and get it tested).
- Repair loose joints now (see `repair-maintenance.furniture-restoration-upholstery.frame-joint-repair.regluing-chairs-and-repairing-frame-joints`).
  Fill old tack-riddled rails with glued slivers or plane back a few mm.

## 2. Webbing
- **Traditional:** 50 mm jute webbing, folded end, 5 × 13 mm improved tacks in a W
  pattern, stretched with a **web stretcher** until it rings like a drum when tapped.
  Interweave front-to-back then side-to-side, ~2 cm gaps, then fold and tack a second
  row of 3 tacks over the first. Sprung seats: webbing on the **underside** of the rails.
- **Modern:** rubber (Pirelli-type) webbing at 5–10 % stretch for foam seats; elastic
  needs proper clips or staples, not tacks.

## 3. Springs (traditional sprung seat)
- Place double-cone coil springs on the webbing in a grid (typically 9–12 for an
  armchair seat), sew each to the webbing at 3 points with spring twine and a curved needle.
- **Lash** with laid cord: the classic **eight-way tie** (front-back, side-side, both
  diagonals), knotting on each spring, compressing springs about 2–3 cm, edge springs
  tilted outward for a domed seat.
- Cover with heavy hessian (12 oz/400 g), tacked and sewn to each spring top.

## 4. Stuffing and padding
- **Traditional:** first stuffing of horsehair or coir held by **bridle ties**; stitched
  edge rolls (blind and top stitching) to make a firm square front edge; scrim over;
  second stuffing of hair; calico cover pulled to shape; skin wadding on top.
- **Modern foam:** high-density foam (e.g. 30–35 kg/m³, higher for seats in heavy use),
  cut 1 cm oversize so it sits under tension. Spray adhesive to the base, then polyester
  wadding (Dacron) over to soften edges and stop the cover clinging.
- Many markets require fire-retardant foam and covers (e.g. UK Furniture and Furnishings
  Fire Safety Regulations, US TB117-2013): buy compliant materials and keep labels.

## 5. Cover: cutting and pulling
- Centre pattern and **grain** (warp runs front to back on seat, bottom to top on back).
  Mark centres on the frame and cover with chalk.
- **Temporary tack** (half-driven) at centre front, back, sides; then work outwards in
  pairs, pulling evenly — opposite sides alternately. Smooth tension, no ridges between
  tacks. Drive home once all looks right.
- Corners: cut to relieve around legs/arms at the line where the fabric meets the frame;
  front corners as a single **pleat** (or double) folded neatly to the back.
- Piping: 45° bias strips 4–5 cm wide over piping cord, sewn with a piping/zipper foot;
  joins on the bias.
- Finish with **gimp/braid** glued with hot glue or fabric adhesive and gimp pins, or
  back tacking strip; black cambric/bottoming cloth underneath.

## Gotchas
- Never staple and tack along the same line; staples are fine on modern frames but
  shatter old beech rails if too dense — space 1–2 cm.
- Velvet and pile: all pile in one direction (smoothing downward on backs, toward the
  front on seats).
- Over-tight webbing on a weak rail can pull the frame out of square.
- Wear eye protection when using a staple gun or pulling tacks.
- (Draft note: spring counts, foam densities and regulations vary; confirm locally.)

## Verify
- [ ] Webbing taut and drum-tight; springs upright and evenly lashed.
- [ ] Seat level side to side and edges firm and straight.
- [ ] Cover on grain, pattern centred, no wrinkles or puckers when sat on.
- [ ] Pleats symmetrical; piping straight; underside clean with bottoming cloth.
- [ ] Fire-safety labels/materials compliant where required.
