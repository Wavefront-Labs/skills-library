# Stripping and refinishing wood furniture

**Goal:** remove a failed finish and lay down a new one that is even in colour, smooth,
durable for the piece's use — without destroying veneer, patina or your lungs.

## 0. Decide whether to strip at all
Stripping removes patina (much of an antique's value). Try cleaning (mineral spirits / white spirit on a cloth) and reviving first; if the finish is
shellac, see french-polishing (`repair-maintenance.furniture-restoration-upholstery.french-polishing.french-polishing-and-shellac-repair`) — it can often be
repaired rather than stripped. Strip when the finish is crazed through, peeling, or
painted over.

## 1. Identify the old finish (solvent test, hidden spot)
- **Denatured alcohol** softens/goes tacky in ~1 min → **shellac**.
- **Lacquer thinner** softens quickly, alcohol doesn't → **nitrocellulose lacquer**.
- Neither touches it → **varnish / polyurethane / catalysed finish** (needs a stripper).
- Water beads and nothing lifts, surface dry-looking → probably **oil/wax**; clean with
  white spirit and 0000 steel wool.
- **Painted antiques and anything pre-1978 (US) / pre-1960s (UK):** assume lead.
  Test with a lead test swab (3M LeadCheck or similar). If positive: chemical strip only,
  no dry sanding or heat gun, wet-wipe waste, bag it, wash hands, keep children and
  pregnant people away.

## 2. Strip safely
- Strippers by type: **benzyl alcohol** gels (slow, 30 min–several hours, safer),
  **NMP** (now restricted in some markets — reproductive toxicant), **methylene
  chloride/DCM** (fast but banned for consumer sale in the US and EU; vapour metabolises to
  carbon monoxide and has killed people in bathrooms and basements).
- Work **outdoors or with cross-draught**, nitrile or butyl gloves (latex dissolves),
  splash goggles. A respirator with organic-vapour cartridges helps with most solvents
  but does **not** protect against DCM.
- Lay the stripper on thick in one direction, don't brush back and forth; cover with
  cling film to slow evaporation. Lift sludge with a plastic scraper along the grain, then
  coarse nylon pad/0000 steel wool in carvings with a sharpened dowel or toothbrush.
- Neutralise/clean as the label says (often white spirit or water wash). Let dry 24 h.
- Go gently on veneer: water-based or long-dwell strippers can soften hide-glue veneer.

## 3. Prep
- Sand by hand or random-orbit: 120 → 150 → 180 grit (stop at 180 for stain; 220 for
  clear finish on close-grain wood). Veneer is often 0.6 mm or less — never start below
  150 on veneer, never sand edges with a machine.
- **Raise the grain** before water-based stain/finish: wipe with damp cloth, dry, knock
  back with 220.
- Open-grain woods (oak, mahogany, walnut) for a glassy finish: grain filler, tinted
  slightly darker than the stain.
- Blotch-prone woods (pine, cherry, birch, maple): washcoat (1 lb-cut dewaxed shellac)
  or gel stain.

## 4. Colour matching
- Test on the underside or a sample board of the same species, sanded the same grit,
  **with the topcoat on** — the topcoat changes colour (oil-based varnish adds amber).
- Dyes (alcohol/water-based) colour evenly and transparently; pigment stains lodge in
  pores and look muddy if over-applied. Common recipe: dye for overall tone, then glaze or
  toner for final match. Build lighter than target — you can add, not remove.

## 5. Topcoat choice
| Finish | Durability | Method | Recoat |
|---|---|---|---|
| Danish/tung oil | low | wipe on, wipe off excess after 15 min | 12–24 h, 3+ coats |
| Wiping varnish (thinned poly) | medium–high | rag, thin coats | 6–12 h |
| Brushed oil polyurethane | high | natural bristle, thin coats, scuff 320 between | ~24 h |
| Nitrocellulose lacquer | medium | HVLP spray, 1.3–1.5 mm tip, 3–4 wet coats | 30–60 min |
| Water-based poly | medium–high | synthetic brush/spray | 2 h |
Spray lacquer only with explosion-proof extraction or outdoors, no pilot lights.

## Gotchas
- **Oily rags self-heat and ignite.** Linseed, tung, Danish oil and oil-varnish rags must
  be laid flat outdoors to dry, or sealed in a water-filled metal can. Never in a heap.
- Silicone from old furniture polish causes "fish-eye" craters: wash with naphtha, or add
  fish-eye remover/seal with shellac first.
- Blushing (milky lacquer) in humidity: retarder, or wait for drier air.

## Verify
- [ ] Old finish identified by solvent test before choosing a method; lead tested on paint.
- [ ] No stripper residue (surface not tacky; topcoat test patch cures normally).
- [ ] Colour matches the target sample under the room's lighting, with topcoat applied.
- [ ] Finish is even with no runs, fish-eyes, dust nibs or blush.
- [ ] Rags disposed of safely; waste bagged per local rules.
