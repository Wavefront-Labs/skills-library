# Leather upper recolour, scuff repair and boot zips

**Goal:** make worn or damaged uppers look right and work again — even colour, no
visible scuffs, a finish that matches the shoe's type, and closures that function.

> Draft: products vary (e.g. Saphir/Famaco/Angelus/Fiebing's dyes and fillers; water-
> vs solvent-based). Test every product on a hidden area first — leather tanning
> and existing finishes react unpredictably.

## 1. Identify the leather and finish
- **Smooth aniline/semi-aniline** (dress shoes): absorbs a water drop slowly; takes
  dye well.
- **Pigmented/corrected grain** (many mid-price shoes, boots): plasticky surface coat;
  dye won't penetrate — use **pigment colourant (paint)** after deglazing.
- **Suede/nubuck:** napped; needs suede dye/spray, never wax or cream.
- **Patent:** plastic coating; scuffs are in the coat — limited repair.
Test: a drop of water on an inconspicuous spot; dark absorbs = aniline-type.

## 2. Clean and strip
1. Remove laces; insert shoe trees.
2. Clean with leather cleaner or saddle soap and a damp cloth; dry.
3. **Deglazer / preparer** (acetone-based, or proprietary) on a cloth to remove wax,
   polish and factory finish where recolouring. Work in a ventilated area with nitrile
   gloves. Repeat until the cloth stays clean. Don't soak — it dries the leather.

## 3. Fill scuffs and scratches
- Light scuffs: polish and cream often enough.
- **Gouges where grain is lifted:** trim loose flaps with a sharp blade or glue them
  back down with a little contact cement; sand flush with 400–600 grit.
- **Missing grain:** apply leather filler in thin layers with a palette knife, dry
  between coats, sand smooth, 600 grit last. Feather the edges.

## 4. Recolour
- **Dye (aniline leathers):** apply with a wool dauber or brush in thin, even coats,
  circular for blending; 2–3 coats; let each dry. Going darker is easy; lighter is not
  possible with dye.
- **Pigment (corrected grain, colour changes, covering repairs):** thin coats with a
  sponge or **airbrush** (best for even coverage on large areas), dry with gentle warm
  air between coats. 3–5 thin coats beat 1 thick one (thick cracks).
- Mask soles and welts with low-tack tape; edges of the welt get edge dressing separately.
- Seal pigment with a matching finisher (matte/satin/gloss) if the system has one.

## 5. Polish and finish
- **Cream polish** (pigment + conditioner) to even colour, buff after 10 min.
- **Wax polish** for shine and protection: thin layers; for a mirror shine on toe
  and heel caps (hard areas only), add small amounts of wax with a drop of water,
  small circles, many layers.
- Never mirror-shine the vamp flex area — it cracks.
- Suede: brush with a suede brush; eraser for marks; suede dye/renovator spray at
  20–30 cm in light passes; brush when dry.

## 6. Patching and closures
- **Tears/holes:** back with thin leather or reinforcement tape cemented inside,
  then fill and colour the outside; or a visible stitched patch for work boots.
- **Boot zip replacement:** unpick the old zip stitching (seam ripper), measure length,
  match gauge (often #5) and colour, tape the new zip in place, stitch on a patcher
  machine through the original holes. Check the slider runs freely before finishing.
- **Elastic gussets** (Chelsea boots): unpick, cut new elastic gore to shape, cement
  and stitch through original holes.

## Gotchas
- Dye on a pigmented shoe sits on top and rubs off on trousers.
- Skipping deglazing = blotchy colour and peeling.
- Solvent dyes bleed into stitching; mask or touch up thread with a fine brush.
- Thick filler with one heavy coat shrinks and cracks.

## Verify
- [ ] Leather type tested; product tested on a hidden area.
- [ ] Surface deglazed until clean; scuffs filled and sanded flush.
- [ ] Colour even under daylight, both shoes matched; no rub-off on a white cloth.
- [ ] Finish suits the leather (no wax on suede, no shine on flex areas).
- [ ] Zips/elastics function smoothly; stitching in original holes.
