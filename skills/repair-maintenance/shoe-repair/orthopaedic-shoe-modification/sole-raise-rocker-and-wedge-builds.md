# Sole raises, rocker soles, flares and wedges

**Goal:** modify a client's existing shoes to a clinician's prescription so the
change is accurate, light, durable and doesn't create a new problem (instability,
tripping, a shoe that tilts).

> Draft: work to a written prescription from a podiatrist, orthotist or physician;
> don't decide clinical amounts yourself. Typical clinical rules quoted here (e.g.
> how much of a raise goes inside the shoe) vary between clinicians — confirm.

## 1. Before you cut
- Read the prescription: modification type, amount (mm), which shoe, location
  (e.g. rocker apex position), material if specified.
- **Is the shoe suitable?** Firm heel counter, sound upper, a sole you can bond to,
  laces/straps (slip-ons may slip off with a raised sole). Trainers with soft air/gel
  units are poor candidates.
- Measure and photograph the shoe before; note existing heel and forefoot heights.

## 2. Materials
- **EVA** (ethylene-vinyl acetate) sheet/blocks — light, easy to grind; choose density
  (e.g. medium-high for raises, softer for rockers' cushioning).
- **Cork** (or cork-rubber) — firm, traditional, heavier.
- **Crepe/rubber outsole** — wear surface on top of the build.
- Contact cement suited to EVA/rubber (polychloroprene or PU); prime per data sheet.

## 3. Raises (leg-length discrepancy)
1. Prescription gives total raise, e.g. 20 mm. Part may go **inside** as a heel lift
   (often up to ~6–10 mm fits inside the shoe), the rest outside.
2. **Heel-only raise** is fine for small amounts; above roughly 10–15 mm, raise the
   **whole sole** so the foot isn't forced into a steep plantarflexed position —
   with a lower amount at the forefoot, tapering (e.g. full at heel, about half to
   two-thirds at the ball), plus toe spring so they don't trip.
3. Remove outsole (or cut it off at the midsole). Rough surfaces.
4. Laminate EVA layers to height, cement, press. Grind to the shoe's outline on the
   finishing machine, matching the shoe's profile; keep the tread line natural.
5. New outsole on top; trim, finish edges.
6. Check: shoe sits flat; measure raise at heel and ball against prescription.

## 4. Rocker soles
Reduce forefoot motion and pressure (e.g. hallux rigidus, metatarsalgia, diabetic
foot). Prescription gives **type** (toe-only, mild/heavy, heel-to-toe) and **apex**
position — typically just **behind the metatarsal heads** for offloading the ball.
1. Build sole thickness to allow the curve (EVA layers, often 10–20 mm at the apex).
2. Mark the apex line across the sole; grind a smooth curve from the apex to the toe
   (toe spring) and, for heel-to-toe rockers, from the apex back to the heel.
3. The flat stable area under the apex must be wide enough to stand on comfortably.
4. Outsole, finish.
Apex too far forward = no offloading; too far back = the client rocks backwards and
feels unstable.

## 5. Flares and wedges
- **Wedge** (tilts the foot): a tapering layer, thicker **medially** (pronation
  control) or **laterally** (supination, lateral knee OA), e.g. 3–6 mm. Built under
  heel only, sole only, or full length as prescribed. Inside wedges (insoles) for small
  amounts are often preferred.
- **Flare** (stability, not tilt): extends the sole outward beyond the upper on one
  side (lateral flare against ankle-rolling), commonly 5–15 mm wider at the base, built
  with EVA and ground to a smooth angled wall.

## 6. Balancing the pair
- Weight: raises add mass — use lighter EVA on the raised shoe.
- Appearance: match outsole colour and edges on both shoes; some clients want the
  unmodified shoe given a matching outsole.
- Check both on a flat surface and with the client walking; look for tilt, catching toes.

## Gotchas
- Raising the heel only for a large LLD forces the foot into a high-heel position.
- Forgetting toe spring on a raised sole = trips.
- Sole layers that aren't fully bonded delaminate within weeks under the walk cycle.
- Never alter the prescribed amount on your own judgment; call the clinician.

## Verify
- [ ] Written prescription on file; shoe suitable for modification.
- [ ] Measured heights match prescription at heel and ball (± ~1 mm).
- [ ] Rocker apex placed per prescription; stable midstance area.
- [ ] Shoe sits flat with no unintended tilt; toe spring present.
- [ ] Client walks in them; prescriber informed of what was done.
