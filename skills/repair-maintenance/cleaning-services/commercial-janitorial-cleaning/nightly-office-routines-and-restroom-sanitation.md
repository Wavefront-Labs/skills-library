# Nightly office routines and restroom sanitation

**Goal:** clean a commercial building every night to the contracted standard, on time,
with restrooms that are sanitary and fully stocked — and evidence to prove it. The
**scope of work (SOW)** is the contract: what gets done, how often, to what standard.

## 1. Read the scope and build the schedule
- List every task with frequency: **nightly** (trash, restrooms, kitchen/breakrooms,
  entrances, touchpoints, spot vacuum), **weekly** (full vacuum, dust high-touch ledges,
  damp mop all hard floors), **monthly/periodic** (high dusting, vents, interior glass,
  machine scrubbing, carpet extraction).
- Convert to labour: production rates vary widely by building type and density; office
  cleaning is often quoted around **2,500–4,000 ft² per hour** (~230–370 m²/h) for
  routine nightly work. Measure your own rate on site and adjust.
- Agree access, alarm codes (kept securely), lock-up and lost-property procedure.

## 2. Organise the people: zone vs team
- **Zone cleaning:** one cleaner owns an area and does everything — clear accountability,
  good for small buildings.
- **Team (specialist) cleaning:** roles move through the building — *light-duty*
  (trash, dusting, touchpoints), *vacuum* (backpack vacuum), *restroom specialist*,
  *utility* (breakrooms, entrances, floors). Faster and more consistent in larger buildings.

## 3. Nightly office sequence
1. Trash and recycling: empty, replace liners when soiled, **keep streams separate** (a
   single contaminated bag can reject a recycling load). Check confidential-waste bins
   aren't touched unless in scope.
2. Dust and wipe: desks only where clear (don't move papers), phones and touchpoints with
   disinfectant if specified — door handles, light switches, lift buttons, handrails.
3. Breakrooms: counters, sinks, appliance fronts, tables, floor.
4. Vacuum traffic lanes nightly, edges weekly.
5. Entrances: glass doors, matting, floor spot-mop.

## 4. Restroom sanitation (the order matters)
1. **Wet floor signs** out, knock, gloves on, ventilate.
2. Flush toilets/urinals, apply bowl cleaner/disinfectant under rims — dwell.
3. Restock first (paper, soap, sanitary bags) so you see shortages while you work.
4. Clean **high to low, clean to dirty:** mirrors, dispensers, partitions and doors,
   sinks and taps, then fixture exteriors, then bowls/urinals with a brush, ending with
   the toilet base and the floor around it. **Red** cloths for toilets/urinals, separate
   colour for sinks/surfaces.
5. Disinfectant must stay wet for its **label contact time** (often 1–10 minutes) to
   claim disinfection.
6. Floor: sweep, then mop with a neutral cleaner or disinfectant toward the door; pour
   water into floor drains to keep traps full (odours).
7. Odour persists → check urinal screens, grout, floor drains, behind the toilet base;
   enzyme cleaners help on grout.

## 5. Chemicals and safety
- Safety Data Sheets (SDS) and, in the UK, COSHH assessments for every product; dilution
  via dispensing systems, not "a glug".
- Never mix chlorine bleach with acid cleaners or ammonia.
- Sharps in restroom bins: never compress bags by hand; use sharps containers.
- Lone working procedure at night; report building faults (leaks, lights, broken
  fixtures) via the facilities ticket system.

## 6. Quality control
Weekly **inspections** by a supervisor: score areas against the SOW (e.g. a 1–5 scale or
APPA appearance levels), photograph failures, rework by next shift, trend scores. Client
complaints logged with root cause. Rotate periodic tasks so they don't all slip.

## Gotchas
- Production rates on paper ≠ reality — first month, time the work.
- Under-stocked restrooms generate more complaints than imperfect dusting.
- Moving client papers or equipment invites trouble; agree what's off-limits.

## Verify
- [ ] Scope converted into a written nightly/weekly/periodic schedule with labour hours.
- [ ] Restrooms cleaned in sequence, contact times respected, fully restocked.
- [ ] Recycling streams kept separate.
- [ ] SDS available; dilution controlled.
- [ ] Inspection scores logged weekly and failures reworked.
