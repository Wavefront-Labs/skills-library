# Hot water extraction and spotting

**Goal:** remove soil and spots without shrinking, browning, bleeding dyes or leaving
sticky residue that resoils fast — and get it dry within hours. The IICRC S100
(carpet) and S300 (upholstery) standards are the industry references; this is a working
summary.

## 1. Inspect and identify before any chemical
- **Fibre:** read labels; for carpet, a burn test on a few fibres from a hidden spot
  helps: **wool** smells of burnt hair, ash crushes; **nylon** melts to a hard bead, celery
  smell; **polypropylene (olefin)** melts, waxy smell; **polyester** black smoke, sweet.
  Viscose/rayon ("art silk") is fragile when wet — specialist low-moisture only.
- **Upholstery cleaning code** on the label: **W** = water-based ok, **S** = solvent only,
  **W/S** = either, **X** = vacuum only.
- **Colourfastness:** dampen a hidden spot with your pre-spray, press a white towel —
  any colour transfer = dye bleed risk → change method.
- Pre-existing damage (wear, fading, burns, delamination, permanent stains) — point it out
  and note it before work.

## 2. Hot water extraction (carpet)
1. Move light furniture; protect corners with hose guards; put blocks/tabs under furniture
   legs left in place.
2. **Pre-vacuum thoroughly** — most soil in carpet is dry particulate; a vacuum removes it
   far better than water.
3. **Pre-spray** an appropriate pre-conditioner: alkaline (pH ~9–10) for synthetics;
   **wool-safe (pH below ~8–9 and wool-certified)** products for wool. Dwell ~10 min.
4. **Agitate** with a grooming brush or counter-rotating brush machine to work the product.
5. **Extract** with a rinse — an **acid rinse (pH ~4–6)** neutralises alkaline residue and
   reduces browning and resoiling. Overlap strokes, slow on the vacuum-only dry passes; one
   wet pass, then 2+ dry passes.
6. Water temperature: truck mounts run hot (often 90 °C+ at the unit, less at the wand);
   keep wool and fragile fibres cooler per maker guidance.
7. Groom the pile in one direction; place furniture back on blocks or foil tabs.

## 3. Low-moisture alternatives
**Encapsulation** (polymer that dries brittle and vacuums out) with a rotary or cylindrical
brush machine: fast dry (often <1 h), excellent for commercial maintenance between
extractions. **Bonnet** cleaning: surface appearance only.

## 4. Spotting (identify, then match)
Work **from the outside in**, blot don't rub, test first.
| Spot type | Approach |
|---|---|
| Water-soluble (soft drinks, mud) | Neutral spotter, extract |
| Protein (blood, milk, vomit, urine) | **Cool water** + enzyme/protein spotter; heat sets it |
| Oil/grease | Solvent (dry solvent spotter) on a towel, then detergent |
| Tannin (tea, coffee, wine) | Acid spotter/tannin remover |
| Dye stains (red drinks) | Reducing agent / heat-transfer method — test, can affect carpet dyes |
| Rust | Rust remover (often oxalic/hydrofluoric-free products) — gloves |
| Gum, wax | Freeze and chip; wax: iron through brown paper |

## 5. Drying
Target **under 6–8 hours**: extra dry passes, air movers, open windows or HVAC on, dehumidify
in humid weather. Slow drying causes **wicking** (stains reappear as moisture rises to the
tips), browning on cellulose-backed carpets, and odour.

## 6. Upholstery
Same order: vacuum with an upholstery tool, test, pre-spray lightly, agitate with a soft
brush, extract with an upholstery tool at low moisture, dry quickly. Unzip cushion covers
only if the maker allows; they can shrink or lose backing.

## Gotchas
- Overwetting is the cause of most complaints (shrinkage, browning, odour, delamination).
- High pH on wool → yellowing and damaged fibres.
- Residue = fast resoiling: always rinse properly.

## Verify
- [ ] Fibre and cleaning code identified; colourfastness tested; pre-existing damage noted.
- [ ] Pre-vacuumed; correct pH pre-spray with dwell and agitation.
- [ ] Acid rinse used; carpet damp, not wet, after final pass.
- [ ] Spots treated by type; any remaining permanent stains explained.
- [ ] Dry within target time; furniture protected on blocks.
