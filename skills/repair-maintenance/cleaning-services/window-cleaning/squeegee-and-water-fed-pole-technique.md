# Squeegee and water-fed pole technique

**Goal:** clear, streak-free glass, efficiently, without scratching it or falling off
anything. Two methods dominate: the **traditional squeegee** (any window you can reach
safely, interiors) and the **water-fed pole (WFP)** with pure water (exteriors up to
several storeys from the ground).

## 1. Squeegee kit and solution
- Strip washer/applicator (microfibre or cotton sleeve), squeegee channel and **sharp
  rubber** (soft rubber for warm weather, hard for cold), scrim/microfibre for detailing,
  bucket on a belt, scraper with fresh blade.
- Solution: water + a few drops of washing-up liquid or a window-cleaning concentrate.
  Too much soap = streaks and slip; too little = rubber drags and skips.
- Cut the rubber to overhang the channel by about 1/8 in (3 mm) at each end so it can
  flex into frames; replace it when the edge is nicked or rounded.

## 2. Technique
1. **Wet** the glass fully with the applicator; scrub corners and edges.
2. **Cut a dry edge:** at the top, tilt the squeegee and pull a 1-inch strip along the
   frame (or start in a top corner).
3. **Straight pull (beginners, large panes):** pull vertical strokes from the dry strip
   down, each overlapping the last by ~1–2 in, wiping the rubber with a cloth after each
   stroke.
4. **Fanning / S-method (pros):** keep the rubber on the glass in a continuous snake from
   top to bottom, turning the squeegee at each side so the leading edge always pushes
   water ahead. Faster, fewer lines — practise on large panes.
5. Finish: wipe the bottom edge and frame with a dry cloth (detailing). Check from an angle
   in daylight for streaks.

## 3. Scrapers and glass damage
- Use scrapers only wet, with fresh blades, pushing forward only — never drag backwards.
- **Tempered (toughened) glass** may carry "fabricated debris" fused to the surface from
  manufacture; scraping drags it and scratches. Test a corner; many tempered-glass makers
  and window cleaners advise no blades on tempered glass. Ask the client to sign a waiver
  if you scrape.

## 4. Water-fed pole (pure water)
- Pure water (**0 ppm TDS** — total dissolved solids) dries spot-free with no chemical;
  it pulls dirt into solution. Produce it with **RO (reverse osmosis)** then **DI resin**
  polishing (or DI only in soft-water areas).
- Check TDS with a meter at the brush every job; **replace DI resin when output rises above
  ~0–10 ppm** — spotting appears quickly above that.
- Technique: brush frames first (dirt runs from them), then scrub glass top to bottom,
  then **rinse** with the jets off the bristles — flowing water over the whole pane from
  top to bottom, frames last. Don't leave patches of dirty water. Low flow ~0.5–1 l/min
  is often enough.
- First clean of a very dirty or sealant-smeared window may need two visits.

## 5. Hard water stains
Mineral spots etched on glass: try a mild acid cleaner (glass-specific), a fine polish
compound with a glass-polishing pad, or 0000 steel wool wet with soapy water (test —
not on coated low-e glass). Etching beyond that is glass damage, not dirt.

## 6. Work at height
- **Prefer WFP from the ground** over ladders — it's the core of working-at-height risk
  reduction (UK Work at Height Regs; US OSHA and ANSI/IWCA I-14.1 for building maintenance).
- Ladders: short duration only, 1:4 angle, footed or tied, three points of contact, no
  overreaching. Never use a ladder against glass.
- Above WFP reach: MEWPs, building maintenance units or rope access by trained teams.

## Gotchas
- Cleaning in hot sun: solution flashes off and streaks; work the shady side.
- Self-cleaning (hydrophilic) coated glass — follow the maker; avoid abrasives and some
  chemicals.
- Soft brush bristles on WFP; hard ones scratch frames and leave marks on soft coatings.

## Verify
- [ ] Rubber sharp; solution right strength; no streaks viewed at an angle in daylight.
- [ ] No blade used on tempered or coated glass without testing/consent.
- [ ] WFP TDS read at the brush: 0–10 ppm before starting.
- [ ] Frames and sills wiped/rinsed; no drips left.
- [ ] Safe access method chosen and documented; ladders inspected.
