# Trauma scene containment and biohazard waste

**Goal:** return a contaminated space to a safe, sanitary state, protect workers from
bloodborne pathogens and other infections, dispose of waste lawfully — and treat the
family with dignity. This is regulated, high-risk work: in the US, OSHA's Bloodborne
Pathogens Standard (29 CFR 1910.1030), state medical waste rules and, in some states,
licensing; outside the US, check the local hazardous-waste and worker-safety rules. Under 1910.1030 the employer
writes an Exposure Control Plan, offers the hepatitis B vaccine series at no cost, and supplies PPE at no
cost; these are prerequisites.

## 1. Before entry
- **Release:** police/coroner must have released the scene. Confirm in writing.
- **Hazard assessment:** blood and body fluids, decomposition, sharps (needles, glass),
  drugs (fentanyl risk — never touch powders without the right protocol), firearms
  (police issue), structural damage, flies/maggots, mould, electrics.
- Talk with the family or client gently: what's to be kept, what's personal, insurance
  (many home policies cover trauma cleanup). Photograph before work for records.

## 2. PPE (minimum, upgrade by assessment)
Fluid-resistant coverall with hood, gloves, boot covers, eye and face protection, and a
respirator chosen by hazard assessment and fit-tested under the employer's respiratory program.
OSHA 1910.1030 requires the employer to supply appropriate PPE such as gloves, gowns, face
shields or masks and eye protection. Donning and doffing are done in a set order with a buddy check.

## 3. Containment: three zones
1. **Dirty zone (exclusion):** the affected area. Seal off with poly sheeting and tape;
   close or seal HVAC registers to stop spread; negative air (HEPA air scrubber) where
   aerosols are generated.
2. **Decontamination zone:** where PPE comes off, tools and waste containers are wiped
   and bagged.
3. **Clean zone (support):** staging, clean PPE, chemicals, rest area.
Movement is one way: clean → decon → dirty, and back only through decon.

## 4. Remediation sequence
1. **Gross removal:** absorb free liquids (solidifier), remove contaminated soft goods
   (mattresses, upholstered furniture, carpet and pad) — porous items saturated with
   blood are usually discarded, not cleaned.
2. **Find the full extent:** fluids travel — under floor coverings, into subfloor,
   baseboards, wall cavities. Lift and cut out until clean material is reached
   (then check).
3. **Clean** non-porous surfaces with detergent to remove soil — disinfectants don't
   work through organic matter.
4. **Disinfect** with an appropriate disinfectant (1910.1030: contaminated surfaces are
   decontaminated with an appropriate disinfectant; choose per label claims, e.g. EPA-registered in
   the US) effective against bloodborne pathogens, respecting the label **contact time**.
5. **Seal** cleaned but stained subfloor/structure with an odour-blocking primer where
   material is sound and cannot be removed.
6. **Odour:** remove the source first; then hydroxyl generators (safe with occupants) or
   ozone (unoccupied only, then ventilate — ozone harms people, pets, plants and rubber).

## 5. Verification
Visual inspection with strong light, **ATP swab testing** of cleaned surfaces (against your
company's threshold), luminol-type presumptive blood tests where
used, odour check after the space has been closed for a time. Document readings with
photos.

## 6. Waste
- Segregate: **regulated medical/clinical waste** (blood-saturated items) into
  labelled or colour-coded containers per 1910.1030 and local rules (labels are fluorescent
  orange or orange-red; red bags or containers may replace labels); **sharps** into closable,
  puncture-resistant, leakproof, labelled or colour-coded containers kept upright and not overfilled;
  ordinary waste separately.
- Use a licensed medical/clinical waste carrier; keep manifests/waste transfer notes.
- Never put biohazard waste in household bins or landfill skips.

## 7. Hoarding and gross filth (brief)
Plan structural safety, pests (rodents, insects), mould, and the client's mental health
— go at a pace agreed with them and any social workers; sort keepsakes with consent. Same
PPE and containment principles.

## Gotchas
- Visible clean is not decontaminated: fluid migrates under surfaces.
- Exposure incident (needle-stick, splash): wash, report immediately, medical evaluation
  per the exposure control plan.
- Workers' mental health: debriefs and support access are part of a sustainable team.
