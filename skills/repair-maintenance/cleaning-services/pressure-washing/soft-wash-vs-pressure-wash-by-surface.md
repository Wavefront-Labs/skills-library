# Soft wash vs pressure wash by surface

**Goal:** clean exteriors without etching concrete, furring wood, forcing water behind
siding or killing the client's garden. The core decision: **pressure** cleans by force
(hard surfaces), **soft wash** cleans by chemistry at low pressure (anything that can be
damaged, and organic growth).

## 1. Equipment basics
- Cleaning power ≈ **PSI × GPM**. For commercial-grade work, GPM (flow) does more of the
  rinsing work than very high PSI. Typical pro units: 3,000–4,000 psi at 4–8 gpm.
- **Nozzle colours (common):** red 0° (avoid — cuts and gouges), yellow 15°, green 25°,
  white 40°, black 65° (low pressure / chemical). Distance matters as much as the tip.
- **Surface cleaner** (rotating spray bar under a hood) for flat concrete: even, no
  "zebra stripes", much faster than a wand.
- Soft wash: 12 V pump or downstream injector delivering chemical at <~500 psi; the
  injector dilutes heavily, a dedicated pump lets you control mix.

## 2. By surface
| Surface | Method | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Concrete driveway, sidewalks | Pressure + surface cleaner, then rinse | Pre-treat oil with degreaser; post-treat with SH to kill organic staining. Hot water helps with grease. |
| Block paving / pavers | Lower pressure, surface cleaner | Joint sand washes out — re-sand (polymeric) after |
| Vinyl siding, render, painted surfaces | **Soft wash** | Pressure forces water behind panels and strips paint |
| Asphalt shingle roofs | **Soft wash only** | Black streaks = Gloeocapsa magma algae; pressure removes granules and voids warranties |
| Wood decks, fences | Low pressure (≤~500–1,200 psi, 25–40° tip, 12+ in away) or soft wash | Follow with a wood brightener (oxalic acid) |
| Brick | Low–moderate pressure, soft wash for organics | Old soft brick and mortar erode easily |
Always test in an inconspicuous spot.

## 3. Soft wash chemistry
- Active: **sodium hypochlorite (SH)** — pool chlorine ~10–12.5% — diluted with water
  plus a surfactant to help it cling.
- Typical working mixes (industry rules of thumb, adjust to growth and surface):
  **siding ~1–3% SH, roofs ~3–6% SH**. Example: 12.5% SH mixed 1:4 with water ≈ 2.5%.
- Apply bottom to top on walls (avoids streaking), dwell 5–15 min without letting it dry,
  rinse thoroughly top to bottom.
- Surfactant should be one designed for SH (many dish soaps break down chlorine).

## 4. Protect people, plants and water
- **Pre-wet plants** and lawns, keep them wet during work, rinse again after; cover delicate
  plants. Neutralisers (e.g. products that consume chlorine) can be used on runoff.
- **Runoff:** many jurisdictions prohibit wash water into storm drains (US Clean Water
  Act / local stormwater rules; UK Environment Agency guidance). Block drains, recover or
  divert to permeable ground or foul sewer per local rules — especially with oil or chemicals.
- PPE: chemical goggles, nitrile gloves, waterproof boots; respirator for SH misting.
  Pressure jets cause **injection injuries** — never point at skin; ER visit even for small ones.
- Close windows and doors, cover outlets and light fittings, move vehicles.

## 5. Deck technique
Keep the wand moving with the grain, constant distance, overlap passes. Stopping in one
spot leaves a lap mark; too close raises fibres ("furring") — sand lightly after drying
if needed. Let dry 24–48 h before sealing/staining.

## Gotchas
- 0° tips and turbo nozzles on wood, siding or soft stone = permanent damage.
- SH on metals (aluminium gutters, brass) corrodes/discolours — rinse well.
- Never mix SH with acids (oxalic, rust removers) in the same tank or session without rinsing — toxic gas.
- Oxidised (chalky) siding comes clean then looks streaky; tell the client first.

## Verify
- [ ] Surface identified and method chosen (pressure vs soft wash); tested first.
- [ ] Nozzle/distance appropriate; no lap marks or etching.
- [ ] SH mix recorded; plants pre-wet and rinsed; runoff controlled.
- [ ] Post-treatment applied where needed (SH on concrete, brightener on wood).
- [ ] Final rinse: no chemical residue on glass, metal or plants.
