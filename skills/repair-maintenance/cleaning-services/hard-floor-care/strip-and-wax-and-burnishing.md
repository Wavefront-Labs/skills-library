# Strip and wax and burnishing

**Goal:** resilient floors (VCT, sheet vinyl) with a durable, glossy, slip-resistant finish,
and a maintenance programme that delays the next strip as long as possible. "Wax" is
nearly always a **polymer floor finish** today. Always follow the floor and chemical
makers' instructions — LVT/LVP and many sheet vinyls with factory coatings must **not**
be stripped or finished like VCT.

## 1. Pads (common colour code — brands vary)
| Pad | Use |
|---|---|
| Black / brown | Aggressive stripping |
| Green / blue | Heavy scrub, scrub and recoat |
| Red | Light scrub, buffing (low speed) |
| White / beige / champagne | Polishing, high-speed burnishing |
Never use a stripping pad on a surface you want to keep (stone, factory-coated vinyl).

## 2. Full strip and refinish (VCT)
1. Clear the room, sweep/dust mop, put out wet floor signs and barriers; ventilate.
2. Mix stripper at the label dilution in a bucket (hotter water helps). Apply generously
   with a mop in manageable sections (~100–200 ft²); **don't let it dry** — dwell
   ~10 minutes, re-wet as needed.
3. Scrub with a rotary (swing) machine on a black/brown pad; edges and corners with a hand
   pad or doodlebug and a scraper.
4. Pick up slurry with a wet vacuum or auto-scrubber immediately.
5. **Rinse twice** with clean water (or a neutraliser) — stripper residue makes finish
   powder and peel. Test: finish is fully removed when a fingernail drag shows no film and
   the tile is uniformly dull; water sheets evenly.
6. Let dry fully.
7. **Apply finish** with a clean, dedicated finish mop or applicator: thin, even coats,
   figure-8 or straight lines with a 6-inch border at walls for the first coats (avoids
   build-up at edges). Typical: **4–6 coats** on new/stripped VCT; 30–45 minutes or until
   dry to touch between coats (humidity extends it).
8. Keep traffic off; cure overnight before burnishing (24 h+ preferred where possible).

## 3. Scrub and recoat (between strips)
Scrub with a green/blue pad and neutral cleaner or mild stripper to remove top worn layers
and soil, rinse, dry, apply 2–3 fresh coats. Done 2–4 times a year in many buildings,
extends strip intervals to years.

## 4. Daily/weekly maintenance and burnishing
- Daily: dust mop and auto-scrub or damp mop with a **neutral (pH ~7–8) cleaner** — high-pH
  cleaners soften finish.
- **Burnishing** with a high-speed (1,500–2,000+ rpm) burnisher and a white/champagne pad
  heats and hardens the finish to a "wet look"; frequency by traffic (nightly to weekly).
  Dust mop first: grit under a burnisher scratches.
- Entrance matting (at least 3–5 m of walk-off) stops most soil; budget for it.

## 5. Tile and grout
Alkaline cleaner for most ceramic/porcelain grout with a grout brush or an extraction
tool; acid cleaners only for mineral deposits and **never on stone or cementitious grout
for long** (acid dissolves cement grout). Seal grout after deep cleaning with a penetrating
sealer.

## 6. Natural stone (brief)
Marble and limestone: pH-neutral daily cleaning only. Etching/scratches need **honing**
with graded diamond pads (wet) and polishing — a specialist skill. Don't apply polymer
finish to stone that should be polished mechanically, unless that's the agreed programme.

## Gotchas
- Applying finish onto stripper residue = cloudy, powdery floor that peels.
- Too-thick coats stay soft and track.
- Slip resistance: ensure the finish meets the relevant slip standard (e.g. ASTM D2047
  static coefficient ≥0.5 in the US) and burnish only finishes designed for it.
- Never leave a wet floor unsigned or walk-through areas without a dry path.

## Verify
- [ ] Floor type confirmed suitable for stripping and finishing.
- [ ] Old finish fully removed; floor rinsed twice and neutral.
- [ ] Coats thin, even, dry between; edges not built up.
- [ ] Cured before burnishing; gloss even with no swirl marks.
- [ ] Maintenance schedule (daily, burnish, scrub-recoat, strip) written for the site.
