# Combine pre-harvest service and PTO driveline repair

**Goal:** get a combine through harvest without breakdowns or fires, and keep tractor
PTO drivelines safe and intact. In-field crop settings and loss checks are covered in
`agriculture-environment.crop-farming.combine-harvester.combine-settings-and-loss-checks`;
this skill is the mechanical side.

## 1. Safety rules for both jobs
- Engine off, key out in your pocket, separator and header disengaged, and wait for
  everything to stop — rotors, choppers and fans coast for a minute or more.
- Lower the header onto the ground or engage the **feeder-house safety stop/cylinder
  lock** before going under it. Never trust the hydraulics.
- Unloading auger and chopper: assume they can move; isolate before clearing blockages.
- PTO: never step over a turning shaft. Entanglement at 540 rpm takes a limb before you
  can react.

## 2. Combine pre-harvest service — work front to back
**Header**
- Sickle: replace cracked or dull sections, check guards for alignment (a straight edge
  along the guard tips), set hold-downs to about 0.5 mm clearance over the sections.
  Check wobble-box or knife-drive bearings for play.
- Reel: tines straight, bearings and drive chain; check reel height and fore-aft
  sensors move freely.
- Auger fingers and draper belts: tension, tracking, torn slats.

**Feeder house**
- Feeder chain: tension per the manual (often measured as slack at the bottom middle),
  worn or bent slats, sprocket wear. Check the stone trap.

**Threshing and separating**
- Rotor or cylinder: rasp bars and tines for wear and balance — replace in matched
  sets. Concave: bent wires and bars, check it closes evenly; recalibrate the concave
  zero position in the monitor after work.
- Inspect rotor drive gearbox oil and the variable-speed drive or belt.

**Cleaning shoe**
- Sieves and chaffer open and close freely through the full range; calibrate to the
  indicator. Fan bearings and balance.

**Drives and bearings**
- Every belt: cracks, glazing, tension, idler bearings. Every chain: stretch, lube.
- Spin bearings by hand, feel for roughness; use an IR thermometer during the first
  run-up and again after an hour — a bearing 20 °C hotter than its twin is failing.
- Grease every point on the lube chart; combines can have well over 50.

**Fire prevention**
- Blow down the engine bay, exhaust and turbo areas, and the area around bearings.
- Check for leaking fuel and hydraulic lines; check electrical cables for chafe.
- Two working extinguishers on board (one at the cab, one at ground level).
- Blow down daily during harvest and park away from buildings and stacked crop.

**Electronics**
- Yield monitor, moisture sensor and GNSS receiver: update software and calibrate
  before the first field. (Draft note: menu paths differ by brand and model year.)

## 3. PTO driveline repair
1. Check **guarding** first: master shield on the tractor, the plastic shaft guard
   turning freely and its restraint chains fixed. Replace cracked guards — never run
   without them.
2. **U-joints**: grab the yokes and feel for play; any knock means replace the cross
   and bearings. Grease at the interval on the shaft decal — commonly every 8 hours,
   longer for low-maintenance joints.
3. **Telescoping tubes**: grease the profile tubes, check minimum overlap at maximum
   extension (commonly at least a third of length) and that the shaft does not bottom
   out at the tightest turn or full lift.
4. **Phasing**: the yokes at both ends must be in line (same plane), or the driven
   machine sees speed pulsation and vibration.
5. **Protection devices**: replace shear bolts only with the specified grade and size —
   a harder bolt moves the failure to the gearbox. Slip clutches should be run in
   (loosened and slipped briefly) at the start of each season, as plates stick from
   rust. Fit overrunning clutches on high-inertia implements (mowers, rotary cutters).
6. Match 540 vs 1000 rpm and spline (6-spline 1-3/8 in vs 21-spline) to the implement.

## Gotchas
- Fitting a stronger shear bolt "because it keeps breaking" — find the jam instead.
- Combine fires usually start at a hot bearing or the exhaust with chaff build-up.
- Restarting a blocked machine with someone still inside the header area.

## Verify
- [ ] Header, feeder, rotor, shoe and drives inspected; worn parts replaced.
- [ ] Every grease point done and bearing temperatures checked after a run-up.
- [ ] Monitors and sensors calibrated; extinguishers charged.
- [ ] PTO guards complete, U-joints tight, tubes overlapped and phased.
- [ ] Correct shear bolts fitted and slip clutches freed.
