# Undercarriage wear measurement and track tensioning

**Goal:** measure undercarriage wear repeatably, turn readings into percent-worn and
remaining hours, and set track tension correctly so the undercarriage is not
wearing itself out.

## 1. Prepare the machine
- Clean the undercarriage. Packed mud hides wear and makes tension readings wrong.
- Park on firm level ground, travel forward (not reverse) a few metres so slack ends
  up on top, stop with the sprocket drive pulled tight on the bottom.
- Lower the blade/bucket, engine off, lockout. Nobody under or beside the track while
  anyone is in the cab.
- Record machine model and serial, hour meter, application (rock, sand, clay) and
  shoe width. Wear rates only mean something against hours and conditions.

## 2. Measure — the main components
Use the OEM wear tables (Cat, Komatsu, Deere and Berco publish them) for the exact
part number; they convert a dimension to percent worn.
1. **Track pitch** (internal pin/bushing wear): tape across 4 or 5 links on the upper
   tight section, pin centre to the same point 4 links on. Divide by the number of
   links and compare to new pitch. Stretched pitch means the chain rides high on the
   sprocket.
2. **Bushing outside diameter**: calipers at the sprocket contact point, or ultrasonic
   on wall thickness. Bushings wear on one side — note whether they've been turned.
3. **Link height**: depth gauge from rail top to shoe surface, or ultrasonic.
4. **Track rollers and carrier rollers**: tread diameter with calipers or a roller
   gauge; check flange wear and oil leaks at the seals. A leaking roller fails soon.
5. **Front idler**: tread height from the flange top.
6. **Sprocket**: tooth profile gauge; hooked or pointed teeth mean replace with the
   chain, not alone.
7. **Grouser height**: depth gauge on the shoe.
Ultrasonic gauges (such as Cat's Custom Track Service tools or generic UT gauges) must
be calibrated on the reference block and used with couplant on clean metal.

## 3. Interpret
- Plot percent-worn against hours for each component. The first part to reach 100% sets
  the rebuild date; plan to do pins/bushings, rollers and idlers together where it
  makes economic sense.
- Uneven wear (one side, inner vs outer flanges) points to misalignment, a bent track
  frame, or always turning the same way on a slope.
- At about 70–80% pin/bushing wear, consider a bushing turn on serviceable (non-sealed
  and lubricated) chains. (Draft note: turn and rebuild points are OEM- and
  chain-type-specific.)

## 4. Track tension (sag)
- Measure sag: lay a straight edge or string from carrier roller (or idler) to sprocket
  on the top run and measure the deepest point down to the grouser top. Dozers are often
  set somewhere around 25–50 mm and excavators looser, depending on conditions — use
  the operator manual figure for that model. (Draft note: ranges are indicative only.)
- **Tight track is the expensive mistake**: it multiplies pin, bushing, sprocket and
  idler wear and wastes power. Run it slightly looser in mud or snow that packs.
- **To tighten**: pump grease into the adjuster fitting with a grease gun.
- **To loosen**: open the relief valve **no more than one to one-and-a-half turns**,
  slowly, and let grease bleed out. Never stand in front of the valve, never remove it
  or the fitting entirely, and never point your face at it. The grease is at very high
  pressure from the recoil spring; a removed valve can be blown out like a projectile,
  and grease jet injection injuries are serious.
- If grease won't come out, travel the machine slightly — do not unscrew further.
- Never disassemble a recoil spring assembly without the OEM press and procedure; the
  spring is preloaded to many tonnes.

## Gotchas
- Measuring pitch on a slack section reads short.
- Wearing grousers down to save money on shoes increases link and roller wear.
- Mixed new and worn components (new chain on old sprocket) wear each other quickly.

## Verify
- [ ] Undercarriage clean, machine on level ground, locked out before measuring.
- [ ] All components measured and recorded against hours with percent-worn values.
- [ ] Sag within the manual's range for the working conditions.
- [ ] Grease valve closed, no grease weeping, no one stood in line with it.
- [ ] Next inspection date and projected rebuild hours recorded.
