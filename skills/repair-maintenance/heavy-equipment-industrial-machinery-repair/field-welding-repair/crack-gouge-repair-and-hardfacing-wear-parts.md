# Crack repair and hardfacing on heavy equipment

**Goal:** repair a cracked structure so it doesn't re-crack beside the weld, rebuild
and hardface wear parts, and do it without burning the machine, its hoses or its
electronics.

## 1. Protect the machine and yourself first
- Hot-work permit where required. Clear fuel, oil, grease build-up and dry vegetation
  from around the work; have an extinguisher and a fire watch for at least 30–60
  minutes after welding (smouldering in belly pans is common).
- **Hydraulic hoses**: spatter and heat can burn through a pressurised hose and spray
  oil into the arc — a fireball. Relieve pressure, cover hoses with welding blankets or
  remove them. Never weld on or next to a cylinder or tank still holding oil.
- **Batteries**: turn the battery disconnect off and disconnect batteries; keep arcs and
  grinding sparks away (hydrogen gas).
- **Electronics**: follow the OEM welding procedure — usually disconnect ECM
  connectors or use the specified isolation, and place the **ground clamp as close to
  the weld as possible on the same component**. Current through bearings, pins or
  ECMs pits and destroys them.
- Lower and block attachments. Ventilate; coatings and galvanised parts give toxic fume.

## 2. Find and prepare the crack
1. Clean to bare metal and find both crack ends with **dye penetrant** or
   **magnetic particle** testing. Cracks are always longer than they look.
2. Drill **stop holes** (about 10–12 mm) just beyond each end so the crack cannot run
   during gouging.
3. **Air carbon-arc gouge** (or grind) to sound metal in a U or V groove, gouging from
   the stop holes towards the middle. Typical: 8–10 mm carbons, 80–100 psi air.
   Grind off the carbon-rich gouged surface (1–2 mm) before welding.
4. Re-test with penetrant to confirm the crack is gone. Full-thickness cracks need a
   full-penetration groove, often with a back-gouge from the other side.

## 3. Preheat and weld
- Identify the material: frames are often structural steel, while buckets and wear
  plates are quenched-and-tempered abrasion-resistant steels (AR400, Hardox), and
  castings may be high carbon. Ask the OEM or check the parts book.
- **Preheat** with a rosebud and check with temperature crayons or an IR thermometer,
  75 mm each side of the joint. Common ranges: 100–150 °C for thick structural
  sections; 120–175 °C for AR plates; follow the steel maker's chart.
  Do not exceed the steel's maximum (often about 200–225 °C for Q&T steels) or it
  loses hardness.
- Use **low-hydrogen** consumables: E7018 stick (from a rod oven, 120 °C holding),
  or E71T-1/E81T gas-shielded flux core. Stringer beads, clean between passes, keep
  interpass temperature in the window.
- Grind the finished weld toes smooth and blend in the direction of stress; a sharp
  toe is where the next crack starts.
- Add a fish plate or gusset only where the OEM approves — badly placed doublers move
  the stress concentration rather than remove it.
- Slow cool under blankets in cold or windy field conditions.

## 4. Build-up and hardfacing
- Build worn parts back to near size first with a **buffer layer** of a tough,
  compatible build-up alloy; never stack hardfacing more than 2–3 layers thick, it
  cracks and spalls.
- **Hardfacing** (e.g. chromium carbide FCAW wire): cross-checking (fine transverse
  cracks) is normal for carbide overlays and relieves stress.
- Pattern by material: **stringer beads parallel to flow** for rock; **dot or waffle
  pattern** for fine materials and sand so fines pack between beads and protect the base.
- Bucket lips and cutting edges: replace bolt-on edges rather than hardfacing them;
  hardface heel shrouds, wear strips and side cutters where wear patterns show.

## Gotchas
- Welding over an unfound crack end: the repair cracks again within days.
- Ground clamp on the frame while welding the bucket: current passes through the pins.
- Wet or exposed E7018 rods pick up hydrogen and cause delayed cracking.

## Verify
- [ ] Hoses protected or removed, batteries disconnected, ECMs isolated per OEM.
- [ ] Crack ends found by NDT and fully removed before welding.
- [ ] Preheat and interpass temperatures recorded.
- [ ] Final weld toes blended; penetrant or MT test clean after 24–48 h.
