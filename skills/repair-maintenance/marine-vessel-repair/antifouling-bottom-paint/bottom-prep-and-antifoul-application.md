# Bottom prep and antifoul application

**Goal:** an antifouled bottom that stays clean for the season, bonds to what's
underneath, and isn't wasted by poor prep, wrong compatibility or thin film.

## 1. Choose the paint
| Type | How it works | Good for | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ablative / self-polishing** | wears away slowly, exposing fresh biocide | most cruising boats, boats that sit, trailered (some types) | Coats build up less; can be overcoated by most types |
| **Hard (contact leaching)** | hard film, biocide leaches out | racing (burnishable), fast powerboats | Builds up over years; loses effect out of water (many types) — check launch window |
| **Copper-free** (zinc pyrithione, Econea etc.) | — | **aluminium hulls, outdrives** (copper causes galvanic corrosion), areas with copper restrictions | Always required on aluminium and on/near drives |
- Check **local regulations**: some regions restrict copper levels or ban certain
  biocides (e.g. TBT is banned globally; parts of Washington State and others restrict
  copper on recreational boats).
- **Compatibility**: find out what's on the hull now (old records, the yard, a test
  patch). General rules — ablative over hard: usually OK after sanding; **hard over
  ablative/soft: not OK** without a tie-coat or removal; anything over Teflon/vinyl
  types: follow the maker's chart. When unknown: sand, apply the maker's **tie-coat/
  primer**, then the new system.

## 2. Prep
1. Pressure wash immediately on haul-out, before slime dries.
2. **Sanding**: antifoul dust is toxic. Use a **dust-extracted orbital sander** (many
   yards require it), P3/N100 respirator, disposable coveralls, gloves, goggles. Tarp to
   catch residue; follow yard waste rules. Wet sanding is an alternative.
   - Maintenance coat: scuff with 80–120 grit to remove loose paint and give key.
   - Bare gelcoat (new boat or stripped): degrease with solvent wash (several clean rags),
     then sand to dull with 80–120 grit, or use a primer system designed for gelcoat.
3. Fill dings with epoxy filler; fair.
4. Mask the **waterline** (fine-line tape), and protect transducers (most need a specific
   transducer antifoul or none — check the maker), anodes (**never paint anodes**) and props.

## 3. Barrier coat (when needed)
For osmosis prevention or after blister repairs: an **epoxy barrier coat** (e.g. 4–5
coats to ~250–350 µm dry, per the maker), applied within the maker's **overcoat window**
so each coat chemically bonds. Apply the first antifoul coat while the last barrier coat
is still in its "tacky" overcoat window, or use the specified tie-coat after full cure.
*Check the product datasheet for exact windows at your temperature.*

## 4. Application
- Stir thoroughly (biocide settles) — power stirrer; box paint between tins for colour
  consistency.
- **Air and hull temperature** within the maker's range (often ≥5–10 °C) and hull ≥3 °C
  above dew point (no condensation).
- Short-nap solvent-resistant roller (6–10 mm) + brush for edges.
- **Two coats minimum**, plus an **extra coat at the waterline, leading edges of keel,
  rudder and stem** (highest wear).
- **Film thickness**: use a wet-film gauge; under-applying is the main reason for early
  fouling. Calculate litres from the datasheet's spreading rate × hull area; if you
  have paint left over, the film is too thin.
- Respect minimum/maximum **launch windows** — some antifouls must be immersed within a
  set time, some need a minimum dry time.
- Paint under the **keel blocks/pads** when the yard moves supports (arrange it in advance).
- Pull tape while the final coat is still wet for a clean line.

## 5. Props and running gear
- Copper antifoul on bronze props/shafts works poorly and can promote corrosion;
  options: **foul-release coatings** (e.g. Propspeed-type silicone systems) applied to
  clean, sanded metal with their etch primer; or dedicated prop antifouls.
- Don't paint anodes, sacrificial collars or the cutless bearing water exit.

## Gotchas
- Old paint flaking in sheets = adhesion failure; more paint on top won't fix it. Strip.
- Thinning antifoul reduces film build; follow the datasheet.
- Hard antifouls left out of the water for months may need a light sand to reactivate.
- Never pressure wash ablatives aggressively mid-season — you're removing your paint.

## Verify
- [ ] Old coating identified; compatibility confirmed or tie-coat used.
- [ ] Dust-extracted sanding with PPE; residue contained.
- [ ] Two full coats + extra at waterline and leading edges; wet-film readings logged.
- [ ] Anodes and transducers left/coated correctly; copper-free used on aluminium/drives.
- [ ] Launch within the paint's window.
