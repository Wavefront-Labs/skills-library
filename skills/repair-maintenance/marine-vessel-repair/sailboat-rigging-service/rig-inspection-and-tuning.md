# Rig inspection and tuning

**Goal:** catch rigging failures before they drop the mast, and tune the rig so the
mast is straight side-to-side, has the right rake and bend, and stays put under load.

## 1. Inspection — every season, top to bottom
Go aloft (bosun's chair, two halyards, a competent person on the winch) or inspect
with the mast unstepped.
- **Swage fittings**: hairline **cracks** along the swage, rust weeping from the
  neck, or a "banana" bend. A magnifier and dye penetrant help. Lower swages on
  shrouds (where water sits) fail most.
- **Wire (1×19 stainless)**: broken strands ("meat hooks") — any one = replace that wire.
  Run a rag along it to catch them.
- **Rod rigging**: check cold-headed ends and bends at spreader tips; rod is serviced
  by re-heading at intervals.
- **Toggles, clevis pins, T-ball fittings**: wear, ovalised holes, correct pin size,
  split pins/rings present and taped.
- **Spreaders**: roots secure and uncracked; tips clamped to the wire, bisecting the
  shroud angle (otherwise the spreader can collapse).
- **Chainplates**: rust staining, cracks where they pass through the deck, leaking
  deck seals (wet core or rotting bulkhead knees around them). Pull and inspect
  suspect chainplates — crevice corrosion hides inside the deck.
- **Turnbuckles (rigging screws)**: threads clean, not bent; locked with pins/locknuts.
- **Mast step and partners**: corrosion at the foot (aluminium + steel/stainless contact),
  compression marks.
- Furler: spins freely; foil joints intact; halyard wrap protection.

**Replacement intervals** commonly quoted for stainless wire standing rigging: ~10 years
or ~ 20,000 nm of offshore sailing, sooner in hot climates; insurers increasingly ask for
age records. *These are industry rules of thumb, not standards.*

## 2. Static tuning (at the dock, calm, boat level)
1. **Slack everything**: shrouds and stays hand-tight. Mast at the right fore-aft
   position at the partners (wedges/chocks per the builder).
2. **Centre the mast athwartships**: hoist a tape measure on the main halyard; measure
   to a fixed point on each rail/chainplate. Adjust **cap shrouds** until equal (±a few mm).
3. **Set rake**: hang a weight on the main halyard; measure where it falls behind the
   mast at the boom. Rake per the sailmaker/class guide; adjust forestay/backstay length.
4. **Tension caps**: tighten both sides equal turns. With a **Loos gauge**, a common
   target for cruising boats is **~10–15% of the wire's breaking strength** at rest
   (some performance guides go to ~20%). Check the gauge model is matched to the wire diameter.
5. **Lowers**: tension so the mast stays in column — sight up the mail-track; it should be
   straight side to side. Forward lowers vs aft lowers set the **prebend** on fractional
   rigs (often 1–2× mast-section thickness of bend mid-height; per sailmaker).
6. **Intermediates/D2 shrouds** last; sight again for straightness.
7. Lock all turnbuckles, tape pins and spreader tips.

## 3. Sail tuning (under sail, ~12–15 kn true)
1. Beat on one tack. The **leeward cap shroud** should be just slack, not flopping. If it
   flops, tighten both caps equally (count turns, adjust leeward side then tack).
2. Sight up the mast: if the masthead falls to leeward, caps too loose; if the middle
   sags to leeward, lowers/intermediates too loose.
3. Tack and repeat. Same number of turns each side. Re-lock everything.

## Running rigging and winches (annual)
- Halyards and sheets: chafe at sheaves and clutches; end-for-end or replace.
- **Winch service**: strip, clean with solvent/paraffin, inspect pawls and springs,
  light coat of winch grease on gears and bearings, **light oil on pawls** (grease makes
  pawls stick → winch slips under load).

## Gotchas
- Mixing stainless and aluminium without isolation (Tef-Gel/Duralac) welds fittings with corrosion.
- Over-tensioned rigs can push the mast through the deck/keel step or pull up chainplates.
- "It's only one broken strand" — replace it; the rest are near failure too.

## Verify
- [ ] No cracked swages, broken strands or weeping chainplates; age recorded.
- [ ] Mast centred within a few mm; rake measured and logged.
- [ ] Cap tension measured with a Loos gauge; numbers logged both sides.
- [ ] Mast straight in column at rest and close-hauled; leeward shroud just slack.
- [ ] All turnbuckles locked, pins taped.
