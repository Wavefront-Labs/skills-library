# Winterising and recommissioning

**Goal:** nothing cracks from freezing, nothing corrodes or grows over winter, and the
boat launches in spring without leaks or a dead engine.

## 1. Haul-out and blocking
- Tell the yard where the **sling points** and through-hulls/transducers are (sling
  marks on the hull, or the builder's drawing). Slings on a transducer or prop shaft
  damage both.
- Pressure wash the bottom immediately (see antifouling-bottom-paint).
- **Blocking**: keel weight on blocks — the keel carries the load, stands only steady the
  boat. Stands opposed in pairs, chained together, on firm pads, not touching
  through-hulls or soft spots. Typical: 3 pairs for ~30–40 ft sailboats. Bow slightly up
  so cockpit and decks drain.
- Inspect while it's out: seacocks, anodes, cutless bearing (shaft play), rudder
  bearings, keel–hull joint ("Catalina smile" cracks), blisters, prop condition.

## 2. Engine (inboard diesel/petrol) — warm-up first
1. Run to operating temperature, then **change oil and filter** (acidic combustion
   by-products in old oil sit on bearings all winter). Change gearbox oil per schedule.
2. **Fuel**: fill the tank ~90–95% (less condensation, but leave expansion room); add
   stabiliser and biocide (diesel) or stabiliser (petrol); run to circulate.
3. **Raw-water circuit**: close the seacock, put the intake hose (or strainer lid
   connection) in a bucket of **propylene-glycol (non-toxic, pink) antifreeze**; run
   until it comes out of the exhaust. Close up; drain any low points the manual lists.
   Or drain the circuit entirely if the engine design allows.
4. **Closed coolant**: test freeze protection with a refractometer/hydrometer; replace
   if old (often every 2–5 years).
5. Petrol engines/outboards: **fog the cylinders** (fogging oil into the intake while
   running, or into plug holes), drain or stabilise carb fuel.
6. Remove the raw-water impeller (or loosen the pump cover) so it doesn't take a set.
7. Plug exhaust and air intake with rag (and a big tag on the key: "intakes plugged").

## 3. Freshwater and head
- Drain the water tanks and heater; **bypass the water heater** (otherwise it'll take
  litres of antifreeze). Pump propylene-glycol antifreeze through every tap, hot and
  cold, shower and deck wash until it runs pink.
- Head: pump antifreeze through the bowl and discharge; pump out the holding tank,
  rinse, add antifreeze. Fill the seawater intake loop.
- Bilge: dry it, add a little antifreeze to any low water traps.
- **Never use automotive ethylene-glycol antifreeze** in potable or overboard systems.

## 4. Electrical, interior, deck
- Batteries: fully charge; either remove to a frost-free place and maintain with a
  smart charger, or leave aboard on a monitored charger/solar. Discharged lead-acid
  batteries freeze and crack. Lithium: store at ~50–60% SoC as the maker specifies.
- Clean and dry the interior; prop cushions and lockers open; moisture absorbers or a
  dehumidifier on mains (with a safe, monitored supply).
- Remove sails, electronics and valuables. Grease winches and seacocks.
- **Cover/shrink-wrap**: supported frame for snow load, vents to prevent condensation
  and mould, zip door for access.

## 5. Spring recommissioning checklist
1. Remove intake/exhaust plugs. Refit the impeller with new gaskets.
2. Batteries in and charged; check terminals and charge voltage.
3. Seacocks: operate, grease, check hoses and double clamps.
4. Flush antifreeze out of the fresh-water system (run until clear, then a few minutes
   more); sanitise tanks; reconnect the water heater.
5. Engine: check oil, coolant, belts, hoses; new fuel filters if due; drain water from
   the separator.
6. Antifoul, anodes, prop, cutless bearing done before launch.
7. Safety gear: flares dates, fire extinguishers, lifejackets, VHF radio check.
8. **On launch, stay in the slings 10–20 min** and check every through-hull, the shaft
   seal/stuffing box and the bilge before the lift leaves. Start the engine, confirm
   cooling water at the exhaust.

## Gotchas
- Forgetting the deck wash pump, washdown and ice maker lines — they freeze too.
- Raw-water strainer bowls crack from trapped water — drain them.
- Shrink-wrap without vents grows mould inside the boat.
- A stuffing box that dripped last year may dry out and leak more after launch; adjust.

## Verify
- [ ] Keel carries the weight; stands paired, chained and padded.
- [ ] Oil changed warm; pink antifreeze out of exhaust, taps and head.
- [ ] Water heater bypassed; strainers and low points drained.
- [ ] Batteries charged and maintained; boat ventilated under cover.
- [ ] Spring: plugs removed, impeller back, everything dry after 15 min in the slings.

*Draft: blocking counts and antifreeze practice vary by yard and climate; follow the yard's rules and engine manual.*
