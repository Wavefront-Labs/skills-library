# Outboard water pump impeller replacement

**Goal:** restore full cooling flow on an outboard before a worn impeller cooks the
powerhead. Typical interval: every 1–3 seasons or ~100–300 h (check the maker's
schedule), sooner if run in sand/silt or ever run dry.

**Symptoms:** weak or no telltale ("pee") stream, overheat alarm, steam from the
exhaust relief, or a missing chunk of an old impeller.

## Kit
Correct **water pump kit** for the exact model and serial (impeller, key/drive pin,
gaskets, often wear plate and cup/liner), socket set, gearcase grease and marine
anti-seize, thread locker as specified, shop manual, flush muffs or a test tank, new
lower-unit drain/fill screw washers. On many motors you'll want gear oil handy too.

## Procedure (general — model specifics are in the service manual)
1. **Safety:** disconnect the battery/kill-switch lanyard; for small motors pull the
   plug caps. Put the motor in **neutral** (and note how the shift linkage looks).
2. **Mark and photograph** the trim tab position and shift linkage before removal.
3. **Disconnect the shift linkage** if required. Some motors (many Mercury/Yamaha)
   have a shift shaft coupler under the cowl or on the gearcase; others are simply
   separated as the gearcase drops — follow the manual.
4. Remove the gearcase bolts (often one hidden behind the trim tab) and **lower the
   gearcase straight down** — driveshaft and water tube come with it. Support it.
5. Unbolt the pump housing; slide it up the driveshaft. Note the impeller **key or pin**
   — don't drop it into the bilge or the bin.
6. **Inspect:** impeller vanes (set, cracked, missing pieces — find every missing piece;
   they lodge in cooling passages and the thermostat), the stainless **cup/liner**
   (grooves → replace), the **wear plate** (scored → replace), and the water tube grommet.
7. **Fit the new impeller:** lightly lubricate vanes with dish soap or the grease the
   maker specifies (not petroleum grease on some rubbers — check), install the key,
   and slide the impeller onto the driveshaft. Rotate the **driveshaft clockwise**
   (viewed from above — the normal direction for most outboards) while pushing the
   housing down so vanes fold in the correct trailing direction.
   *Never turn the driveshaft backwards after the impeller is in — it flips the vanes.*
8. Torque housing bolts per spec (often with thread locker); new gaskets/seals.
9. Grease driveshaft splines lightly (don't pack the end — a hydraulic lock can stop
   it seating), anti-seize gearcase bolts.
10. **Reinstall:** shift both powerhead and gearcase to neutral, guide the water tube
    into its grommet and align driveshaft splines by turning the flywheel **clockwise**
    slowly while lifting the gearcase. Don't force — a misaligned water tube or bent
    shift shaft is the classic mistake.
11. Torque bolts, refit trim tab to the marked position, reconnect linkage, check
    shift adjustment: neutral, forward and reverse engage cleanly (turn the prop by hand).

## Test
- On muffs/test tank with the water **on first** (never run dry even for seconds),
  start, idle: telltale stream steady within ~10–20 s.
- Check with a thermometer/IR gun at the thermostat housing after warm-up compared to
  the spec (often ~50–60 °C range on many outboards) and confirm no overheat alarm.
- Lower unit oil: worth changing at the same time; milky oil = water in gearcase
  (seal failure).

## Gotchas
- A weak telltale can be a **blocked telltale outlet** — clear it with a wire/pick
  before assuming the impeller.
- Running on muffs at high rpm out of gear can over-rev; keep it at idle/fast idle.
- Impellers take a set when stored compressed — an old impeller in a stored motor may
  fail after the first start.
- Some models need the gearcase removed in a specific trim position or the prop removed.

## Verify
- [ ] Correct kit for model/serial; all pieces of any broken impeller accounted for.
- [ ] Impeller installed with driveshaft turning the correct direction; never reversed.
- [ ] Shift works in all three positions; bolts torqued to spec.
- [ ] Steady telltale at idle; temperature in spec; no alarm.
