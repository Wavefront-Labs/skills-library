# Seacock and marine head service

**Goal:** every hole below the waterline can be closed by hand in seconds, and the
head pumps cleanly without leaking, back-siphoning or smelling.

## 1. Seacocks — the boat-sinking parts
**Each haul-out** (and ideally exercise monthly afloat):
1. **Operate every seacock** fully open/closed. A seized one is a failure — you can't
   shut it in an emergency. Free it ashore, never by hammering the handle afloat.
2. **Inspect the material**: genuine **bronze** or **DZR (dezincification-resistant)
   brass** or glass-reinforced composite (e.g. Marelon/TruDesign). Plain brass
   **dezincifies** in seawater — it turns pinkish/coppery and becomes brittle; fittings
   have snapped off when a hose was knocked. Scrape a spot: pink = replace.
3. **Through-hull + valve + tailpiece** should be compatible materials and threads
   (NPS vs NPT/BSP mismatches leak and are weak). A proper flanged seacock bolted
   through the hull is stronger than a ball valve screwed onto a through-hull alone.
4. Ball valves: grease per maker (many are greased via a zerk/remove handle);
   cone/plug seacocks (older): disassemble, clean, lap if needed, grease with
   waterproof grease.
5. **Hoses**: wire-reinforced where suction can collapse them; **double-clamp** below
   the waterline with all-316 stainless clamps (screw too — test with a magnet; strong
   attraction = cheaper steel). Clamps opposed by 180°. Replace hoses that are hard,
   cracked or soft/spongy.
6. Tie a **tapered softwood bung** to every through-hull for emergencies.

## 2. Manual marine head rebuild (Jabsco/Raritan-style)
**Signs:** water creeping into the bowl (inlet valve), contents flowing back
(joker valve), leaking at the pump shaft (shaft seal), stiff pumping (scale).
Kit: the head's **service kit** (joker valve, flap/inlet valves, O-rings, shaft seal,
piston O-ring), waterproof/silicone grease, gloves, eye protection, buckets.
1. Flush thoroughly with fresh water; close inlet and outlet seacocks. Expect a mess.
2. Remove the pump assembly (bolts to the base) and outlet elbow.
3. **Joker valve** (duckbill rubber in the outlet): replace — it's the most common
   failure; it should snap closed. Clear scale in the outlet elbow and first metre of
   discharge hose (scale builds there from urine + seawater).
4. Replace inlet flap valve, piston O-ring and shaft seal per the kit order; grease
   O-rings lightly with silicone grease.
5. Reassemble; test pump with seacocks open: dry-bowl and flush settings work, no drip
   at the shaft or base.
6. Monthly maintenance flush of **white vinegar** (let sit in the hose a few hours, then
   pump through) slows scale. Avoid oils, bleach-heavy cleaners and anything but marine
   toilet paper.

## 3. Back-siphon protection
If the head bowl rim is **near or below the waterline** at any heel angle, it needs
**vented loops** (anti-siphon loops) on both the inlet and discharge, high above the
waterline. Clean the vent valve regularly — a blocked vent is no protection. Open-
seacock heads have sunk boats at moorings.

## 4. Smell and holding tanks
- Rub a hot damp cloth on the hose, sniff the cloth: if it smells, the hose has
  **permeated** — replace with dedicated sanitation hose.
- Holding tank vents must be clear and generously sized (aerobic conditions = less
  smell); carbon vent filters help.
- **Y-valves** to overboard: follow local discharge law — many waters require the
  Y-valve secured to the tank (no-discharge zones).

## 5. Bilge pumps (quick check)
Lift each float switch by hand: pump runs. Clear strum box/debris. Check discharge
hose has a loop/check valve as specified and the overboard is above the waterline.
Have a manual pump too.

## Gotchas
- Never leave the boat with head seacocks open.
- Mismatched through-hull threads and "garden centre" plastic valves don't belong
  below the waterline.
- Shrinking hose onto a tailpiece with heat is fine; using sealant to make up for a
  poor fit isn't.

## Verify
- [ ] Every seacock operates by hand; material confirmed bronze/DZR/composite.
- [ ] Below-waterline hoses double-clamped with 316 stainless; bungs tied on.
- [ ] Head pumps without back-flow; joker valve replaced, no shaft leak.
- [ ] Vented loops present and clear where needed; float switches tested.
