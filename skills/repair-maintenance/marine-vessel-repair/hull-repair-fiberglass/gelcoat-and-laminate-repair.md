# Gelcoat and laminate repair on fibreglass (GRP) hulls

**Goal:** a repair that's as strong as the surrounding laminate, watertight, and
cosmetically invisible — from gelcoat chips to holed laminate.

## 1. Assess the damage
- **Gelcoat only** (chips, star cracks, scratches not through to glass): cosmetic.
- **Laminate damage** (exposed/broken fibres, delamination, a hole, soft spots):
  structural.
- **Tap test** with a small hammer or coin: a dull thud = delamination or wet core.
- **Moisture meter** (e.g. Sovereign/Tramex) relative readings vs a known-dry area
  shows wet laminate or core. Wet cored decks/hulls (balsa, foam) need core removal.
- Star cracks often come from flex or hardware loads underneath — fixing the gelcoat
  without addressing the cause means they return.

## 2. Resin choice
| | Polyester | Vinylester | Epoxy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Use | cosmetic, matching original lay-ups, gelcoat | blister barriers, osmosis repairs | structural repairs, bonding to old cured laminate |
| Secondary bond to cured GRP | weakest | good | best |
| Gelcoat over it? | yes | yes | **polyester gelcoat doesn't cure reliably over epoxy** — use paint or an epoxy-compatible filler/fairing |
| Cure | MEKP catalyst, typically 1–2% (temperature-dependent) | MEKP | fixed resin:hardener ratio by weight/volume — never "add more to speed up" |
Chopped strand mat (CSM) dissolves its binder in styrene (polyester/vinylester) — **don't
use powder/emulsion-bound CSM with epoxy**; use woven roving, biaxial or stitched cloth.

## 3. Structural laminate repair
1. **Remove damaged material** back to sound, dry laminate. Mark the outline.
2. **Grind a scarf** (bevel) around the hole: commonly **12:1** (12 mm of bevel per 1 mm
   of thickness) for hulls; a 6 mm laminate gets a ~72 mm bevel all round. Use a 36–80
   grit disc. Dust extraction and a P3/FFP3 respirator: GRP dust is nasty.
3. Back the hole from the inside if possible (a temporary backing plate with release
   film, or laminate from inside first).
4. **Clean**: vacuum, wipe with acetone (polyester) or per the epoxy maker; let it flash off.
5. **Cut plies**: smallest first (fills the deepest part) building up to the largest, or
   largest first then smaller — both are used; largest-first gives better secondary bond
   area. Match the original fibre orientation and add one extra ply.
6. Wet out with resin, roll out air with a consolidation roller. Peel ply over the final
   layer gives a bondable, blush-free surface.
7. Cure fully (epoxy: wash **amine blush** off with water and a Scotch-Brite before
   sanding/overcoating if peel ply wasn't used).

## 4. Fill, gelcoat and finish
1. Fair with thickened resin/fairing compound (epoxy + microballoons above the
   waterline cosmetic, + colloidal silica/low-density fillers as appropriate below).
2. **Gelcoat** (polyester repairs): tint gelcoat to match — old gelcoat has faded;
   test a sample on a hidden area and tint in daylight. Catalyse, apply slightly proud.
   Gelcoat won't cure in air: cover with **PVA release film/wax-additive (surfacing
   agent)** or plastic film.
3. Block-sand: 320 → 600 → 1000 → 1500 wet, then rubbing compound and polish with a
   machine buffer. Wax.

## 5. Small gelcoat chips
V-out the chip with a Dremel/countersink, wipe acetone, fill with catalysed gelcoat
paste (gelcoat + colloidal silica), cover with tape/film, sand flush and polish as above.

## 6. Blisters (osmosis) — outline
Small, hard blisters below the waterline: water has diffused through gelcoat into
the laminate. Options: leave and monitor (cosmetic), open/dry/fill/barrier-coat
individual blisters, or full peel, dry for weeks–months (track with a moisture meter),
fill and apply an epoxy **barrier coat** (see antifouling-bottom-paint). Get a survey on
anything extensive.

## Through-hull note
Below-waterline through-hulls through cored hulls: remove core around the hole and
fill solid with epoxy so water can't wick into the core. Bed with a marine sealant
suited to the fitting (polysulfide/polyurethane; don't use polyurethane on plastic fittings that may need removal).

## Gotchas
- Working temperature: most resins need ≥15 °C; cold slows cure, humidity causes blush.
- Over-catalysed polyester cracks and exotherms.
- Repairs bonded onto a contaminated, waxy surface delaminate — sand and clean properly.

## Verify
- [ ] Removed to dry, sound laminate (meter confirms dry before laminating).
- [ ] Scarf ratio met; plies match original thickness + one.
- [ ] Full cure; no soft spots or dull tap tone over the repair.
- [ ] Gelcoat colour matched in daylight; surface fair and polished.
