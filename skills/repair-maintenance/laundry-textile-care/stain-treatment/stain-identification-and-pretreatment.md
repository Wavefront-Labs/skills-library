# Stain identification and pre-treatment

**Goal:** remove a stain on the first attempt by matching the treatment to the stain's
chemistry — and avoid the moves (heat, rubbing, the wrong chemical) that make it permanent.

## 1. First aid (any stain)
1. **Act fast**; fresh stains are many times easier.
2. **Scrape/blot**, don't rub: lift solids with a blunt knife; blot liquids from the
   outside in with white cloth. Rubbing spreads and abrades fibres.
3. Work from the **back** of the fabric with a pad underneath, pushing the stain out the
   way it came in.
4. **Cold water** unless you know it's oil-based. Heat cooks protein and sets dye.
5. **Test** any chemical on a hidden seam first (colourfastness: dab, wait 5 min, blot
   with white cloth — any colour on the cloth = stop).
6. **Never tumble-dry** until the stain is fully gone. Air dry and inspect.

## 2. Classify the stain
| Class | Examples | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **Protein** | blood, egg, milk, sweat, vomit, grass (partly), baby food | Cold water rinse → enzyme (bio) pre-treater or detergent paste, soak 30 min–overnight in cold/lukewarm → wash. *No hot water, no chlorine bleach* |
| **Tannin** | tea, coffee, red wine, fruit juice, beer, cola | Flush with cool water → liquid detergent rub → oxygen bleach soak (sodium percarbonate) as hot as the care label allows. Avoid bar soap (soap can set tannins) |
| **Oil/grease** | cooking oil, butter, make-up, lipstick, collar grime, bike grease | Absorb (cornflour/talc for 15 min) → neat washing-up liquid (dish soap) or heavy-duty liquid detergent worked in → wash at the warmest safe temperature. Heavy grease: a solvent spotter first |
| **Dye** | ink, marker, curry/turmeric, dye transfer, grass (chlorophyll) | Ballpoint: isopropyl alcohol dabbed and blotted. Turmeric: detergent, then sunlight helps fade it. Dye transfer: rewash promptly with colour-run remover/oxygen bleach |
| **Combination** | chocolate, gravy, ketchup, lipstick, grass | Treat the **oily part first**, then protein/tannin |
| **Oxidisable/mineral** | rust, deodorant (aluminium salts), hard-water marks | Rust: an oxalic-acid or commercial rust remover (or lemon juice + salt + sun on whites). Deodorant build-up: soak in oxygen bleach or a vinegar soak, then wash |

## 3. Products and how to use them
- **Enzyme pre-treater / bio detergent paste**: needs time (15–60 min) and works best
  lukewarm (~30–40 °C). Not on wool or silk.
- **Oxygen bleach (sodium percarbonate)**: dissolve in warm water (it activates with
  warmth; most effective ≥40 °C), soak colourfast items 1–8 h. Safe on most colours;
  not on wool, silk or leather; not on metallic trims.
- **Chlorine bleach**: whites of cotton/linen only, diluted. Never on wool, silk,
  spandex/elastane, or protein stains (yellows them). Never mix with ammonia or acids
  (toxic gas).
- **Isopropyl alcohol (70%+)**: ink, marker, some dyes. Can strip some dyes and acetate.
- **Acetone/nail-polish remover**: nail varnish — but it **dissolves acetate and
  triacetate**; test first.
- **Hydrogen peroxide 3%**: dried blood on whites; can lighten colours.

## 4. Set-stain rescue
Stain survived a hot wash or a dryer? It's harder but often not hopeless:
1. Re-identify the class.
2. Long soak: overnight oxygen bleach (tannin/colour) or enzyme (protein).
3. For oil set by the dryer: dish soap or degreaser, then wash hot; repeat 2–3 times.
4. If two full cycles fail, consider a professional cleaner (dry-cleaner spotting) —
   tell them what the stain is and what you've used.

## Gotchas
- Salt on red wine is folklore with mixed results; quick dilution with cool water + blotting is more reliable.
- Hairspray for ink — modern formulas have little alcohol and add residue.
- Vinegar + baking soda fizzes but mostly neutralises itself.
- Silk, acetate, "dry clean only" structured garments: blot, then take to a professional.

## Verify
- [ ] Stain classified before treatment; product matched to the class.
- [ ] Colourfastness tested on a hidden area.
- [ ] No heat applied until the stain was gone; item air-dried and inspected under good light.
- [ ] No halo ring left (rinse the treated area thoroughly).
