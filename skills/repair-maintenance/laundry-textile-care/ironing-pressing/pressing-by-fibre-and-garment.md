# Pressing by fibre and garment

**Goal:** crisp, crease-free garments with no shine, scorch or melted spots.
**Ironing** = sliding the iron (shirts, cottons). **Pressing** = lift-and-place with
steam and pressure (wool, tailoring) — sliding stretches and shines wool.

## 1. Temperature by fibre (lowest first)
| Dots on label | Approx. soleplate | Fibres | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 dot | ~110 °C | acrylic, nylon, acetate, polyester, elastane blends | Low/no steam; press cloth; synthetics melt and glaze |
| 2 dots | ~150 °C | wool, silk, polyester blends | Wool: steam + press cloth, lift and place. Silk: dry, inside out, slightly damp |
| 3 dots | ~200 °C | cotton, linen | Iron damp; linen inside out for darks to avoid shine |
- **Blends: iron at the setting for the most delicate fibre.**
- Start with the lowest-temperature garments and work up — irons heat faster than they cool.
- Test on an inside seam or hem allowance first.

## 2. Kit
Steam iron (descaled; use distilled/demineralised water if your water is hard and the
maker allows), padded board, **press cloth** (cotton muslin or silk organza — organza
lets you see through), sleeve board, **tailor's ham** for curved areas, **clapper**
(hardwood block) for setting creases in wool, spray bottle, optional starch/sizing.

## 3. The shirt sequence (~4–5 min with practice)
Iron while slightly damp, cotton setting, steam on:
1. **Collar** — underside first, from the points inward (stops wrinkles running to the
   tips), then the top side. Fold and lightly press the fold line.
2. **Yoke** — drape one shoulder over the narrow end of the board, then the other.
3. **Cuffs** — inside then outside, opened flat. Iron around, not over, buttons.
4. **Sleeves** — lay flat along the seam line, smooth, iron from shoulder to cuff. For a
   crease-free sleeve use a sleeve board; for a classic crease, align the seam and
   press the fold.
5. **Front panels** — button side: point of the iron between buttons; then the back;
   then the other front.
6. Hang immediately on a shaped hanger and button the top two buttons; let it cool
   before wearing (heat-softened cotton re-wrinkles).

## 4. Trousers with a sharp crease
1. Turn inside out; press pockets and waistband over the board's tip; turn back.
2. Lay one leg flat with **inseam and outseam aligned** — the crease falls where the
   fabric folds naturally front and back. Fold the other leg out of the way.
3. Press cloth on (wool/dark fabrics). Steam, press down 5–10 s, lift, move — don't slide.
4. **Clapper**: immediately press the hardwood block on the steamed crease for ~10 s;
   it draws out moisture and heat and sets a sharp, shine-free crease.
5. Repeat for the other leg; hang by the hems or folded over a bar.

## 5. Wool tailoring and knits
- Jackets: use the ham for chest, shoulders and lapel roll — never press the lapel roll
  flat. Steam lapels and let them roll naturally.
- Knits: don't iron — **steam from 1–2 cm away**, pat into shape with your hand, dry flat.
- Velvet, corduroy, embossed or pile fabrics: steam from the back only, or press pile-down
  on a needle board/towel. Never press the face flat.

## 6. Garment steamers
Best for dresses, knits, silks and quick refreshes: hold the fabric taut from below,
work top to bottom with the head moving. They don't give crisp creases or collars.
Empty the tank after use; descale per the maker.

## Gotchas
- **Shine** on wool/dark fabrics = crushed fibres from heat + pressure without a cloth.
  Light shine may lift with steam held just above and brushing; heavy shine is permanent.
- **Scorch** (light brown) on cotton: sometimes reduced with hydrogen peroxide 3% on
  whites; burnt fibres don't recover.
- Never iron over stains — heat sets them. Never iron zips, prints or embellishments face-up.
- White flakes from the iron = scale; run the self-clean/descale cycle.
- Starch on dark fabrics leaves white marks; use it on whites and lights.

## Verify
- [ ] Temperature set to the most delicate fibre; tested on a hidden seam.
- [ ] No shine, glazing or scorch; press cloth used on wool, darks and synthetics.
- [ ] Shirt collar points flat with no tip wrinkles; trouser crease single and sharp.
- [ ] Garments hung to cool before wearing or bagging.
