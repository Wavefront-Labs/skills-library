# Seasonal storage and moth prevention

**Goal:** garments come out of storage clean, unyellowed, uncreased and free of holes.

## 1. Clean everything first
Moths and carpet beetle larvae feed on **keratin (wool, cashmere, silk, fur, feathers)**
and are strongly attracted to **sweat, food and skin residue** — they'll attack a
slightly worn cotton-wool blend before a clean pure wool. Invisible sugar stains
(wine, soft drinks) oxidise brown in storage. So:
- Wash or dry clean every item before storage, even if worn once.
- Remove dry-cleaner plastic bags (they trap moisture and off-gas) and wire hangers.
- Don't starch or use fabric softener on stored items — residues yellow and attract insects.

## 2. Containers and materials
- **Breathable** for hanging: cotton/calico garment bags, not plastic.
- **Boxes**: acid-free archival boxes, or lidded polypropylene (PP, recycling code 5)
  bins for everyday seasonal clothes — clean and dry, not airtight in damp places.
- **Tissue**: acid-free tissue between folds to soften creases. Use **unbuffered**
  acid-free tissue for silk, wool and other protein fibres (alkaline buffering can harm
  them); buffered is fine for cotton and linen.
- **Vacuum bags**: OK short-term for duvets and synthetics; avoid for wool, down and
  structured garments long-term — permanent creases, crushed fibres.
- Fold knits; hang only woven garments that hold their shape, on padded or shaped hangers.
- Avoid storing directly on wood shelves (acid migration) — line with tissue or cotton.

## 3. Where to store
- Cool, dark, **stable humidity ~40–55% RH**, temperature below ~20 °C if possible.
- Avoid lofts/attics and garages (heat swings, humidity, pests) and basements (damp).
  Under-bed and interior closets are better.
- Light (especially sunlight and fluorescent UV) fades and weakens fibres.
- Use a cheap hygrometer in the storage area; silica-gel packs help in small boxes.

## 4. Moth and carpet beetle prevention and detection
- **Pheromone traps** (for webbing clothes moth, *Tineola bisselliella*) in each wardrobe
  — they catch males and tell you if you have a problem; they don't solve one.
- Inspect 2–4 times a year: look for small holes, silky tubes or webbing, frass (sand-
  like droppings) and larvae in dark corners, under collars and in folds.
- Vacuum wardrobes, carpet edges and under furniture regularly; larvae live in fluff.
- **Cedar and lavender** are mild deterrents at best, and cedar loses potency — sand to
  refresh. Don't rely on them alone.
- **Mothballs/crystals** (naphthalene, paradichlorobenzene) are toxic, smell persistent
  and are restricted in some countries. Not recommended in homes.

## 5. Treating an infestation
1. Bag affected items, sort by fibre.
2. **Freeze**: seal item in a plastic bag (squeeze out air), freeze at **−18 °C or colder
   for at least 72 h** (some guidance says a week for bulky items), then thaw slowly in
   the bag to avoid condensation. Repeating once after a week catches survivors.
3. Or wash/dry clean at the hottest the fabric allows (larvae die at ~50 °C+ sustained).
4. Deep-clean the wardrobe: vacuum, wipe, empty and inspect drawers.
5. Persistent problems → professional pest control.

## 6. Wedding gown preservation (boxing)
1. Professional clean within weeks of the event, specifying hem soil and invisible
   sugar spills. Ask whether they wet- or dry-clean and what they do with beading.
2. Fold into an acid-free box with unbuffered tissue padding each fold (prevents hard
   creases), bodice supported with tissue. Some prefer a muslin-wrapped, padded
   hanger in a cotton bag instead — fine if the gown's weight isn't on the shoulders.
3. Don't buy "sealed" boxes you're told never to open. You *should* open it every year
   or two, inspect, and refold along different lines.
4. Store flat in a cool, dark, interior space; never in an attic or basement.
*Practitioners disagree on folding vs hanging for heavy gowns; both work with good support.*

## Verify
- [ ] Everything cleaned before storage; no plastic dry-cleaner bags or wire hangers.
- [ ] Breathable, acid-free (unbuffered for silk/wool) materials used.
- [ ] Storage spot is dark, stable and ~40–55% RH (hygrometer reading noted).
- [ ] Pheromone traps in place and checked; inspection on the calendar.
- [ ] Any infested item frozen ≥72 h at −18 °C or hot-washed before return.
