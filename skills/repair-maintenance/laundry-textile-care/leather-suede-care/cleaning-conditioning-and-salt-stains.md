# Leather and suede: cleaning, conditioning and salt stains

**Goal:** clean, supple leather and evenly napped suede, without the darkening, stiffening
or rings that come from too much water, the wrong product or over-conditioning.

## 1. Identify the leather first — the drop test
Put one drop of water on a hidden spot (inside facing, under a collar):
- **Beads on the surface** → **pigmented/finished leather** (most jackets, bags,
  furniture). Tolerates gentle cleaning and conditioning.
- **Darkens and soaks in within seconds** → **aniline or semi-aniline** (naked, dyed-
  through leather). Stains easily; minimal water; specialist products; test everything.
- **Napped surface** (suede = flesh side; **nubuck** = buffed grain side) → no creams
  or oils; dry methods first.

## 2. Smooth leather — clean and condition
1. Dust/brush off dirt; vacuum seams with a soft brush.
2. Clean with a **pH-balanced leather cleaner** (or a few drops of mild soap in water,
   barely damp cloth). Work in small sections, wipe off with a clean damp cloth, then
   dry cloth. Never soak.
3. Let dry naturally at room temperature — **no hairdryer, radiator or sun**; heat
   shrinks and cracks leather.
4. Condition **sparingly**: a small amount of leather conditioner rubbed in with a soft
   cloth in circles, wait ~20 min, buff off excess. Once or twice a year for garments is
   plenty. Over-conditioning makes leather sticky, dark and attracts dirt.
- Saddle soap is for heavy-duty/vegetable-tanned tack and boots — too harsh and alkaline
  for fine garment leather.
- Avoid household wipes, baby wipes, vinegar, olive oil and other kitchen oils (they go
  rancid and darken leather).

## 3. Suede and nubuck
1. Dry brush with a **suede brush** (brass/nylon bristle side for raising nap, crepe
   rubber for general cleaning) in one direction, then against to lift nap.
2. Marks and shiny spots: **suede eraser** (or a clean pencil eraser), then brush.
3. Oil/grease: cover immediately with cornflour or talc, leave several hours/overnight,
   brush off; repeat. Old grease spots need a specialist.
4. Water marks: lightly mist the **entire panel** evenly (edge to edge) with water so
   it dries uniformly, stuff/shape, dry at room temperature, then brush. Spot-wetting is
   what creates rings.
5. Protect new suede with a suede/nubuck protector spray (test first; spray at 20–30 cm).

## 4. Salt stains (boots, hems, cuffs)
Winter road salt leaves white tide marks and dries leather out.
1. Brush off loose salt.
2. Mix roughly **1 part white vinegar to 2 parts water**; dab with a cloth on the line,
   working outward into the clean area to avoid a hard edge. (Some care brands make
   dedicated salt-stain removers.)
3. Wipe with clean damp cloth; dry naturally, stuffed with paper.
4. Smooth leather: condition once dry. Suede: brush to restore nap.
Test vinegar on a hidden area first on aniline and dyed suede.

## 5. Recolouring and professional work
- Scuffs on **pigmented** leather can be touched up with matching leather colour/balm.
  Aniline leather recolouring is specialist work — it's easy to end up blotchy.
- Full garment cleaning of leather, suede and fur: specialist processors (solvent
  systems with fat-liquor replacement). Ordinary dry cleaning can strip oils and colour.
  Clean matching pieces (jacket + trousers) together so colour changes match.

## 6. Storage
- Broad padded hangers for jackets; never wire. Stuff shoulders and bags with acid-free
  tissue.
- Breathable cotton garment bags — **never plastic** (traps moisture → mould).
- Cool, dark, ~40–50% relative humidity; leave space between garments.
- Mould on leather: take outside, brush off, wipe with a cloth barely damp with a 1:1
  isopropyl alcohol/water mix (test first), dry, condition; fix the storage humidity.
- **Fur**: professional cold storage in summer is standard practice; at home, keep cool,
  dark, ventilated and away from cedar-strong odours and mothballs on the fur itself.

## Gotchas
- Wet leather drying fast near heat goes stiff and cracks — always slow dry.
- Waterproofing sprays with silicone can change the hand of fine leather.
- Faux leather (PU) peels with age regardless of care; don't use leather conditioner on it.

## Verify
- [ ] Drop test done; products matched to finish type and tested on a hidden area.
- [ ] Leather dried naturally; no darkening, stickiness or rings.
- [ ] Suede nap even after brushing; salt line gone with no new tide mark.
- [ ] Stored on padded hanger in a breathable bag at moderate humidity.
