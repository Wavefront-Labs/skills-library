# Wash programme design and linen quality control

**Goal:** consistently clean, hygienic, soft linen at the lowest cost per kg, with
rewash under target and no cross-contamination between soiled and clean linen.

## 1. The Sinner circle
Washing results come from four interacting factors: **Time, Temperature, Chemistry,
Mechanical action**. Cut one, raise another. Low-temperature programmes (40–60 °C) save
energy but need stronger chemistry or longer time; overloaded machines lose mechanical
action — no chemistry fixes that.
- Load washer-extractors to the manufacturer's rated **dry weight** for that
  classification (often 80–100% for cotton bed linen, ~60–70% for heavy soil or terry,
  less for polyester-cotton). Weigh loads; don't eyeball.

## 2. A typical programme structure (washer-extractor)
| Step | Purpose | Typical notes |
|---|---|---|
| Flush/pre-wash | remove loose soil; cool to avoid setting protein | cold–warm, short, high water level |
| Main wash (break + suds) | alkali + detergent + emulsifier | 60–75 °C for cotton; alkali raises pH ~10.5–11.5 |
| Bleach | oxygen (hydrogen peroxide or peracetic acid) or chlorine for whites | temperature-dependent; chlorine not with polyester blends' optical brighteners or above ~60 °C |
| Rinses (2–3) | dilute chemistry | high level, cold–warm |
| Sour / neutraliser | bring pH to ~5.5–6.5 to protect skin and fabric, remove iron/ alkalinity | final bath |
| Softener / starch (optional) | finish | not on towels if absorbency matters |
| Extract | remove water before drying/ironing | rpm/G-factor per machine; lower for poly-cotton to limit creasing |
*Values are indicative — the chemical supplier's programme sheet and machine
manufacturer's limits govern. Healthcare/infection-control linen has regulated
thermal disinfection requirements (e.g. UK HTM 01-04: 65 °C for 10 min or 71 °C for
3 min, plus mixing time) — check the applicable standard.*

Tunnel washers (CBW) run the same chemistry in modules with counterflow water; the
programme is set by zone temperatures, dwell time per module and dosing points.

## 3. Classification
Separate by **fibre** (cotton, poly-cotton, microfibre), **colour**, **soil** (light hotel
bed linen vs F&B napery vs kitchen cloths vs heavy soil/stained), and **hazard**
(infectious linen in soluble/alginate bags, loaded unopened). Food-soiled napery needs
higher emulsifier and often a separate programme; mops and microfibre must not share
loads with linen.

## 4. Verify chemistry, don't assume it
- Check **pH** at main wash and final rinse/sour with test strips or a meter each shift
  (or titration kits from the supplier for alkalinity and bleach strength).
- Audit dosing pumps: calibrate output monthly; check for empty drums, airlocked lines,
  kinked tubing.
- Record water hardness; hard water consumes detergent and greys linen.
- Swatch tests (soiled test cloths) quarterly to benchmark programme performance.

## 5. Linen flow and hygiene
- One-way flow: **soiled reception → sort → wash → dry/iron → fold → clean storage →
  dispatch**. Physical or procedural separation between dirty and clean sides; clean
  trolleys never go to the dirty side without disinfection.
- For healthcare/food sectors, a risk-based system such as **EN 14065 (RABC)** defines
  control points: wash temperature/time, chemistry dose, drying, storage.
- Handwashing station at the boundary; PPE on the soiled side.

## 6. Finishing and quality control
- Flatwork ironer: feed at ~50–60% residual moisture (from extraction) and set speed so
  linen exits dry; too wet = damp/creased, too dry = static, scorched and yellowed.
- Inspect every piece at feeding/folding: **stains → rewash**, **holes/tears/fraying →
  reject/rag**, greying → programme review.
- Track **rewash rate** (stained pieces rewashed / pieces processed) per programme and
  per customer; many plants target low single-digit percentages. Spikes point at a
  chemistry, dosing or overloading problem — or a customer misusing linen (make-up
  removal, shoe polishing).
- Track **linen loss and life** (washes to reject) for costing.

## Gotchas
- Chlorine + residual alkali + high temperature = fibre damage and tensile loss.
- Overdrying towels in tumble dryers costs energy and destroys softness.
- Rust spots often come from the machine (drum/fittings) or iron-rich water — sour helps.
- Lipstick and foundation are oil-based — need emulsifier and temperature, not more bleach.

## Verify
- [ ] Loads weighed and within rated capacity per classification.
- [ ] pH and chemistry checked each shift; dosing pumps calibrated and logged.
- [ ] Disinfection temperature/time met and recorded where required.
- [ ] Dirty and clean flows separated; rewash rate tracked against target.
