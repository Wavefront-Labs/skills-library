# Garment classification and spotting — the dry-cleaning plant workflow

**Goal:** every garment comes back clean, undamaged and pressed, with stains handled
on the spotting board rather than left to (or set by) the machine.

## 1. Intake inspection (at the counter, with the customer if possible)
- Check **care label** (circle symbols: **P** = perchloroethylene or hydrocarbon OK;
  **F** = hydrocarbon/petroleum only — no perc; **W** = professional wet cleaning;
  bar under = gentle process; crossed circle = no dry clean).
- Note and tag existing damage: holes, wear at cuffs/collars, missing buttons, weak
  seams, prior discolouration. Photograph high-value items.
- Empty pockets (pens and lipstick in a load ruin it); remove or protect fragile trims —
  sequins, beads, plastic buttons, glued embellishments can dissolve or detach in solvent.
- Ask what each stain is and whether the customer has treated it.

## 2. Classify into loads
Sort by: **process** (perc / hydrocarbon / wet clean), **colour** (light vs dark),
**weight and fragility** (heavy coats vs silks), **soil level**. Typical classes:
light silks and delicates (net bags, short cycle); light everyday; dark everyday;
heavy outerwear; items needing special handling (leather, fur, beaded — usually sent to
specialists). Leather and suede go to a specialist process — see leather-suede-care.

## 3. Pre-spotting (before the machine)
Solvent removes **oil-based soils** well but not **water-soluble** soils (sugar, salt,
sweat, many food and drink stains). Those must be pre-spotted or they can caramelise
and set during drying.
- **Dry side** agents (for oil, grease, paint, ink, cosmetics): volatile dry solvent
  (VDS) and paint-oil-grease remover (POG). Apply, tamp with a bone/brush, flush with
  VDS or the steam/air gun on the dry setting.
- **Wet side** agents (for tannins, protein, water-based): neutral synthetic detergent
  (NSD), tannin formula (mild acid), protein formula (enzyme or mild alkali). Apply,
  tamp, flush with steam, dry with air.
- Work order for an unknown stain: **dry side first**, then wet side — water on an oily
  stain locks it in.
- Use the vacuum and tamping on the spotting board; avoid excessive mechanical action
  on silks (chafing, light spots).
- Always test on a seam; acetates and some dyes are attacked by amyl acetate and
  acetone-type agents.

## 4. Machine cycle (typical closed-loop machine)
- Load to the machine's rated capacity — overloading reduces cleaning and increases
  creasing; delicates at ~50–60%.
- Wash in filtered/distilled solvent with appropriate detergent/sizing; extract; dry/
  reclaim with temperature set for the solvent (hydrocarbon machines dry cooler and
  longer than perc).
- Solvent maintenance: monitor distillation, filters and **KB (kauri-butanol) value**/
  solvent quality per the machine's manual; dirty solvent greys whites.
- Perc is a regulated chemical in many jurisdictions (some are phasing it out); follow
  local rules on handling, leak checks, waste and records.
*Machine parameters vary by manufacturer and solvent — the manual governs. This is an
outline, not settings.*

## 5. Post-spotting and finishing
- Inspect every garment under good light after cleaning; re-spot remaining marks
  (often water-soluble ones that survived).
- Finishing: steam-air form for jackets and coats, utility/legger press for trousers,
  hand iron for detail, puff irons for shoulders and sleeves. Pressing temperature by
  fibre (see `repair-maintenance.laundry-textile-care.ironing-pressing.pressing-by-fibre-and-garment`).
- Replace buttons, protect trims with covers, bag and tag.

## Gotchas
- Sugar stains invisible at intake turn brown after the dryer — always ask about drinks.
- Bonded and laminated fabrics, PU "leather" and glued interfacing can bubble or delaminate.
- Polystyrene beads and some plastic sequins dissolve in perc.
- "Dry clean only" sometimes means "the maker didn't test washing" — wet cleaning may be
  better for sweat-heavy items, but only with proper wet-clean chemistry and equipment.

## Verify
- [ ] Label, damage and stain notes recorded on the ticket at intake.
- [ ] Fragile trims removed or protected; process matches the label (P/F/W).
- [ ] Stains pre-spotted dry side then wet side, tested on a seam.
- [ ] Post-inspection passed under good light; no shine, double creases or residue.
