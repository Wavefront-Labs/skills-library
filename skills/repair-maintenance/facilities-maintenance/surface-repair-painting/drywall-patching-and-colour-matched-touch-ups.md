# Drywall patching and colour-matched touch-ups

**Goal:** a repaired wall that disappears under normal light — flat, no halo, same
colour and sheen as the surrounding paint — done with minimal dust and disruption in an
occupied building.

## 1. Before you start
- **Check what you are sanding.** Pre-1980s paint may contain lead; older textured
  coatings and some plaster finishes may contain asbestos. Check the asbestos/lead
  register or test before sanding. (Draft note: dates and rules vary by country —
  e.g. US pre-1978 lead rules, UK pre-2000 asbestos presumption.)
- Find the paint record: manufacturer, colour code and sheen should be in the O&M
  manual or CMMS. Without it, take a 25 mm chip to a paint store for spectrophotometer
  matching.
- Protect floors and furniture, and in occupied areas use a vacuum-attached sander or
  wet sponge sanding.

## 2. Sizing the repair
| Damage | Method |
|---|---|
| Nail pops, dents, < 10 mm holes | Lightweight filler/spackle, 1–2 coats |
| 10–100 mm holes | Self-adhesive mesh patch or "California" (paper-flange) patch |
| 100 mm + or crushed board | Cut back to a rectangle, add timber/metal backing battens, screw in a new piece of matching thickness (often 12.5 mm), tape joints |
| Hairline plaster cracks | Rake out to a V, fill flexible filler; recurring cracks get scrim/mesh tape |
| Corner bead damage | Cut out damaged length, fix new bead, mud |

## 3. Patch sequence (mesh or board patch)
1. Cut loose paper and crumbled gypsum back to sound board; seal torn brown paper
   with a shellac or oil-based primer, otherwise it bubbles under compound.
2. Fix the patch; screw heads just below the surface without breaking the paper.
3. **First coat:** setting-type compound (e.g. 20- or 45-minute) for strength and
   speed; 150 mm knife; fill and bed tape.
4. **Second and third coats:** lightweight topping compound, feathered out with a
   250–300 mm knife, each coat ~50 mm wider than the last. Let each dry fully
   (premixed: several hours to overnight depending on thickness and humidity).
5. Sand with 120–150 grit on a block or pole sander; don't sand into the face paper.
6. Inspect with a torch held flat to the wall (raking light). Any ridge or dip will
   show — skim again rather than painting over it.

## 4. Prime, then paint for an invisible touch-up
- **Always spot-prime** compound with a drywall primer/sealer. Unprimed filler absorbs
  paint differently and shows as a dull patch ("flashing").
- Use the same paint, same sheen, and ideally the same batch. Stir thoroughly.
- Match the **application method**: if the wall was rolled, roll the touch-up with
  the same nap (e.g. 10 mm); brush marks show on rolled walls.
- Feather the edges: work outward with a nearly dry roller so there is no hard edge.
- Sheen matters more than colour: flat/matt touches up well; eggshell, satin and
  semi-gloss usually flash. For anything above matt, repaint **corner to corner**
  (the whole wall plane) instead of spotting.
- Paint ages and yellows; a fresh can of the original colour on a 5-year-old wall
  may not match. Test a small area and view it dry, after at least an hour.

## 5. Stop it happening again
Where damage recurs (trolley routes, behind doors, bed heads), fit wall protection:
PVC corner guards, crash rails, kick plates, or wall-mounted door stops. Log recurring
locations in the CMMS so they show up in planning.

## Gotchas
- Thick single coats of premixed compound shrink and crack; build in thin coats.
- Painting over damp compound gives blisters and bleed-through.
- Water stains bleed through emulsion — seal with a stain-blocking primer, and find
  the leak first.
- Fire-rated walls: a hole through a fire-rated partition needs fire-rated board and
  compound, and penetrations need certified fire-stopping. Escalate if unsure.

## Verify
- [ ] Hazardous-material check done before any sanding.
- [ ] Raking-light check: no ridges, dips, or visible tape edges.
- [ ] Repair spot-primed before topcoat.
- [ ] Touch-up invisible at 2 m in normal light, from several angles, once dry.
- [ ] Paint details and location recorded on the work order.
