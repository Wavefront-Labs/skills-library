# Safe isolation and replacing sockets, switches and plugs

**Goal:** do the small electrical jobs a maintenance role is authorised for —
like-for-like accessories, plugs and cords, resets — without ever working live, and
recognise the faults that belong to an electrician.

## 0. Know your limits first
What a non-electrician may do is set by law and your employer. (Draft note: this varies
widely — in the UK, like-for-like accessory replacement by a competent person is
generally allowed but new circuits are notifiable; in many US states and in Australia/NZ,
almost all fixed-wiring work requires a licence. Check your authorisation in writing.)
Never: add circuits or outlets, work in distribution boards, work on three-phase, alter
earthing, or work on anything showing scorching, melting or damp.

## 1. Safe isolation — every time, no exceptions
1. Identify the circuit from the board schedule; confirm by plugging in a lamp or
   socket tester and seeing it go off.
2. Switch off the breaker and **lock it off** with a personal padlock and lock-out
   device; tag it with your name, date and reason. Keep the key on you. Removing a
   fuse? Keep it in your pocket.
3. **Prove the tester** on a known live source or a proving unit.
4. **Test dead** at the point of work between all conductors: L–N, L–E, N–E (and
   each line on multi-gang or two-way switching).
5. **Re-prove the tester** on the proving unit. Only now touch conductors.
Use a two-pole voltage indicator (to GS38-style standard: fused leads, shrouded probes).
Non-contact "volt sticks" are for a quick check only, never for proving dead.
Watch for: borrowed neutrals, two-way and intermediate switching fed from another
circuit, and sockets fed from two circuits (rings or multiple feeds).

## 2. Replacing a socket/outlet or switch like-for-like
1. Isolate as above. Photograph the existing connections before disconnecting.
2. Check conductor condition: strip back if insulation is brittle or copper nicked.
   Strip only enough to seat fully in the terminal with no bare
   copper showing.
3. Connect as found: line (brown/black/red), neutral (blue/white), earth/ground
   (green-yellow/green/bare — sleeved). Two cables in one terminal are normal on
   ring circuits; twist-free, both fully in.
4. Tighten terminals firmly (to the torque on the accessory if stated), tug-test each conductor.
5. Fold cables back neatly, fix the faceplate level without trapping conductors.
6. Restore power and test with a plug-in socket tester (polarity, earth present) and
   an RCD/GFCI test button where fitted.
Upgrades (USB sockets, dimmers, smart switches) need the box depth and a neutral; a
dimmer must be compatible with the lamp load.

## 3. Plugs and cords on portable equipment
- Rewireable plugs: brown to L (right, fused), blue to N (left), green-yellow to E
  (top), with the **cord grip on the outer sheath**. Fuse to the appliance rating
  (UK BS 1363 plugs: 3 A fuse up to about 700 W, 13 A from about 700 W to 3,000 W; check the
  appliance label, as some older equipment uses 5 A).
- Damaged cord: replace the whole cord or the appliance; don't tape or join a mains
  flex mid-length.
- Record repairs and arrange a portable appliance test where your site requires it.

## 4. Resets and fault reporting
- A tripped breaker or RCD: unplug everything on the circuit, reset once. If it
  re-trips immediately, reconnect loads one at a time to find the culprit. If it trips
  with nothing connected, stop — that is a wiring fault for an electrician.
- Never reset repeatedly, hold a breaker on, or upsize a fuse.
- Report: circuit ID, what tripped, loads connected, burning smell or heat, and any
  water ingress.
For panel thermography and periodic inspection see
repair-maintenance.building-services-equipment-maintenance.building-electrical-maintenance.switchgear-thermography-and-periodic-inspection.

## Gotchas
- A switched-off light switch does not mean the fitting is dead.
- Aluminium wiring, cloth-insulated or rubber cables: stop and escalate.
- Loose terminals arc and cause fires; re-torque if you find one warm.
- An isolated circuit can be re-energised by someone else: lock-off is the only
  protection.

## Verify
- [ ] Locked off, tester proved, dead-tested all pairs, tester re-proved.
- [ ] Connections as found, no bare copper, every conductor tug-tested.
- [ ] Socket tester shows correct polarity and earth; RCD trips on test.
- [ ] Work within your written authorisation; anything beyond referred.
- [ ] Lock and tag removed by you only, work order closed with details.
