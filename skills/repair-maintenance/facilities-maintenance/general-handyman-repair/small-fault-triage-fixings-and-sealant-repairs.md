# Small fault triage, fixings and sealant repairs

**Goal:** clear the daily list of small faults so each one stays fixed — decide fast
what is yours, what is urgent and what needs a specialist, then refasten, adjust and
reseal with the right materials rather than the nearest ones.

## 1. Triage every job before picking up a tool
Ask four questions in order:
1. **Is anyone at risk now?** Loose handrail, trip hazard, sharp edge, glass, water
   near electrics: make safe first (barrier tape, sign, isolate, remove from use),
   log it, then repair.
2. **Is it mine?** Gas, fixed wiring, fire-stopping, structural cracks, asbestos-
   suspect materials, lifts, and fire door certification belong to competent or
   licensed people. Make safe and escalate. Check the asbestos register before
   drilling older buildings.
3. **Is it a symptom?** Three loose toilet-roll holders on one wall can mean failed
   plasterboard; a door that suddenly binds can mean building movement or a dropped
   hinge. Fix the cause, or the job comes back.
4. **What do I need?** Batch jobs by area and kit so one trip clears five tickets.
Record the outcome on the work order with what you found and did.

## 2. Refastening fixtures — pick the anchor for the substrate
Find out what is behind the surface: knock, drill a 3 mm pilot, or use a stud/cable
detector. Always scan for cables and pipes; avoid the zones directly above, below and
beside sockets and switches, where cables commonly run. (Draft note: safe-zone rules
are region-specific, e.g. UK BS 7671 zones; treat any wall as live until scanned.)
- **Solid masonry/concrete:** nylon plug sized to the screw (e.g. 6 mm plug, 4–5 mm
  screw, ~5 mm deeper than plug length). Drill with hammer action, blow out the dust.
- **Plasterboard, light load (< 5 kg):** self-drill nylon or zinc anchors.
- **Plasterboard, medium load:** metal cavity anchors (setting-tool type) or spring/
  gravity toggles.
- **Grab rails, handrails, wall cabinets, TVs:** fix into studs, noggins or masonry,
  or use a rated heavy-duty toggle. Grab rails must be designed to take body weight
  — never on plasterboard plugs alone.
- **Stripped hole in timber:** glue in a hardwood dowel or matchsticks, let set, re-
  pilot, or move the fixing 10–15 mm. A larger screw is a stop-gap only.
- Use stainless or coated screws in wet areas.

## 3. Doors, drawers and cabinets
- **Concealed (Euro) cabinet hinges:** three adjustment screws — side (gap to
  neighbouring door), depth (in/out), height (on the mounting plate, slotted). Adjust
  a quarter-turn at a time, top hinge first. Hinge cup pulled out of chipboard: fill
  with hardwood plug or epoxy filler, re-drill 35 mm cup.
- **Sticking timber door:** tighten hinge screws first (replace one top-hinge screw
  with a 50–65 mm screw into the frame to pull the door back). Plane only after that,
  and reseal the bare edge.
- **Drawer runners:** replace bent ball-bearing runners as a matched pair of the same
  length and load rating; measure closed length and side clearance (typically 12.5 mm).
See repair-maintenance.facilities-maintenance.door-hardware-lock-maintenance for closers,
locks and fire doors.

## 4. Re-sealing with silicone
1. Cut out all old sealant; remove residue with a scraper and silicone remover. New
   silicone will not bond to old silicone.
2. Clean with isopropyl alcohol; surfaces must be fully dry. Treat mould first.
3. Mask both sides with tape, leaving a 4–8 mm gap. For baths, fill the bath with
   water before sealing so the joint is sealed at its widest.
4. Use sanitary (fungicidal) silicone for wet areas, acrylic decorator's caulk only
   for paintable dry joints. Cut the nozzle to the joint width at 45°.
5. Gun one continuous bead, tool with a wetted finger or profile tool, pull tape
   immediately. Keep water off for 24 h (check the cartridge).

## Gotchas
- Drilling without scanning is how pipes and cables get hit — scan every time.
- Overtightened screws in chipboard strip instantly; set clutch low on the driver.
- Silicone over damp or soap residue peels within weeks.
- Expanding foam and glue are not repairs for loose structural fixings.

## Verify
- [ ] Hazard made safe and logged before repair started.
- [ ] Refastened item takes a firm hand pull with no movement.
- [ ] Doors and drawers close without binding, gaps even (2–3 mm).
- [ ] Sealant bead continuous, smooth, no gaps at corners.
- [ ] Work order notes cause, fix and any escalation.
