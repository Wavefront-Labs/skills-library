# Stainless liner sizing and installation

**Goal:** a sealed, correctly sized flue path from appliance to terminal that drafts
well and keeps combustion products out of the house. Installing a liner for a solid
fuel appliance is notifiable building work in England and Wales (use a HETAS-registered
installer or building control); in the US, listed liners (UL 1777) installed to the
maker's instructions and local code. The liner maker's instructions decide details.

## 1. When to reline
- Flue fails a smoke test, has cracked or missing tiles, or no liner at all.
- New stove into an old, oversized masonry flue (poor draught, condensation, tar staining).
- Fuel change (e.g. to gas/condensing appliances needing a specific liner) — gas liners
  differ from solid fuel ones.

## 2. Size it
- **Rule:** liner internal diameter **not smaller than the appliance flue outlet** (and
  not more than about one size larger for good draught). Common stove outlets: 5 in
  (125 mm) and 6 in (150 mm).
- UK minimums under Approved Document J: e.g. 125 mm for some closed appliances up
  to 20 kW burning smokeless fuel; **150 mm** for wood-burning stoves except where the
  appliance maker allows smaller (often DEFRA-exempt stoves). Check the appliance
  manual and the rules.
- US: liner area generally sized to the appliance outlet; NFPA 211 provides ratios for
  masonry flues — follow the stove listing.
- **Length:** measure from the top of the stove collar/register plate to the top of the
  stack plus terminal allowance; add a margin. Survey the flue route (camera) for
  offsets that will grab the liner.

## 3. Choose the liner
- **Flexible stainless:** for flues with bends. Grades: **316/316L** for gas, oil and wood;
  **904L** (or double-skin 316/904) for multi-fuel/coal and heavy use — more resistant to
  acidic condensates. Double-skin (smooth bore inner) lasts longer and sweeps better
  than single-skin.
- **Rigid (twin-wall or single-wall rigid)**: straight flues; longer life, easier to sweep.
- **Cast-in-place / resurfacing** (pumped refractory mortar or ceramic coating): for
  repairing or forming a liner inside existing masonry flues, often where the flue is
  irregular or to restore a heritage chimney. Specialist kit and training.

## 4. Install (flexible liner, from the top)
1. Sweep the flue; remove the old pot or cut back as needed; check stack is sound.
2. Fit the **pulling cone** to the liner bottom; drop a rope down the flue from the top.
3. Wrap the liner with insulation if required (often recommended/required where the
   flue is external or oversized — check the maker's insulation spec), or plan
   vermiculite/perlite backfill with the bottom sealed.
4. Lower the liner from the top while a second person pulls gently from below — **never
   twist** or kink the liner; respect the direction arrow (flue gas flow).
5. Connect to the appliance with the correct liner-to-pipe adaptor; seal and secure.
   Fit and seal the **register plate** with an access door for sweeping where needed.
6. At the top: **top plate** sealed to the stack, clamp, rain cowl/cap suited to the fuel
   (bird guard; avoid fine mesh on solid fuel — it blocks with soot).
7. Data/notice plate fixed near the appliance or meter: flue type, size, installer, date.

## 5. Test and commission
- **Smoke test** the installed liner; draught test with the appliance hot; CO alarm fitted.
- Commissioning certificate and building control notification (UK) or permit/inspection (US).

## Gotchas
- Undersizing to "improve draught" breaks the rules and starves the appliance.
- Flex liners ovalise when pulled hard round tight offsets — survey first; rigid may not
  fit at all.
- Sharp tile edges can tear insulation wrap; use a protective sleeve on the way down.
- Liners have warranties tied to fuel type and annual sweeping — record the fuel.

## Verify
- [ ] Liner ID ≥ appliance outlet and complies with the regulation/maker minimum.
- [ ] Grade matches the fuel (316 vs 904L) and the direction arrow is correct.
- [ ] Liner continuous, unkinked; top and register plates sealed.
- [ ] Smoke and draught tests passed; CO alarm in place.
- [ ] Notice plate fixed and certification/notification completed.
