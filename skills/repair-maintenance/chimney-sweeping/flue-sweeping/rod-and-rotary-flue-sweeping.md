# Rod and rotary flue sweeping

**Goal:** a flue cleared of soot and creosote from appliance to terminal, without a speck
in the customer's room, and proof the flue draws afterwards. Frequency guidance: wood
burning — at least once a year (UK guidance often says up to quarterly when in use for
wood, twice yearly for bituminous coal); US NFPA 211 calls for annual inspection, cleaning
as needed (commonly when deposits reach ~1/8 in / 3 mm).

## 1. Set up (containment is the job)
1. Ask the customer: fuel, appliance, how long since last sweep, any smoke-back, chimney
   fire history. Confirm the appliance has been out and cold (ideally 12+ hours).
2. Lay dust sheets from the door to the fireplace; protect the hearth and nearby furniture.
3. **Open fire:** seal the opening with a sheet or a purpose-made fireplace cover with a
   rod port; vacuum (HEPA-filtered, industrial — a domestic vacuum will spread fine soot
   and can be damaged by hot embers) runs under the sheet throughout.
4. **Stove:** sweep through the stove (remove baffle/throat plate) or via a flue access
   door/register plate hatch, as access allows. Seal round the work area.

## 2. Choose brush and rods
- Brush diameter matched to the flue: same size or slightly larger for masonry flues;
  for **stainless liners use a poly (nylon) brush** of the liner's diameter — wire brushes
  scratch liners. Clay/round flues → round brush; square/rectangular masonry → square brush
  or a slightly oversized round.
- Rods: flexible (polypropylene or cane) for bends; stiffer for straight runs. Screw
  joints together **clockwise only** and keep turning clockwise while sweeping — turning
  anticlockwise unscrews rods in the flue (rod lost in the chimney is an expensive day).

## 3. Manual sweeping
1. Feed the brush up from below, adding rods, pushing with short strokes and rotating
   clockwise. Work the full height to the terminal; you know you're out when resistance
   eases and the brush shows at the pot (have someone outside look, or use a camera).
2. Sweep the smoke chamber, throat and smoke shelf with a hand brush/vacuum.
3. Withdraw slowly, still clockwise, letting soot fall into the sheet area.

## 4. Rotary (power) sweeping
- Drill-driven rods with whips/flails (polypropylene or wire-wound) — fast and effective,
  especially through bends and on lighter deposits.
- Use the drill's **forward rotation only**, moderate speed (often ~400–1000 rpm; check
  the system maker). Never in mortar-damaged or fragile clay flues: flails can dislodge
  loose mortar and liners.
- Good for liners with poly whips; keep whips matched to liner size.

## 5. Creosote stages
- **Stage 1 (dry, flaky soot):** brushes remove it.
- **Stage 2 (crunchy, tar-like flakes):** stiffer brushes, rotary flails.
- **Stage 3 (glazed, shiny, hard):** brushes won't touch it. Options: chemical creosote
  modifiers (powder or spray applied over repeated fires to turn glaze brittle), then
  rotary chain/aggressive heads by experienced operators only — and an inspection,
  because glaze means the system runs too cool (wet wood, oversized flue, air starved).
  Glazed creosote is the fuel of chimney fires.

## 6. Finish
1. Collect debris into bags; check the debris — lumps of mortar or liner pieces mean
   the flue is damaged (escalate to inspection).
2. Refit baffles, throat plates and gaskets correctly.
3. **Smoke test / draw test:** light a smoke pellet or match in the appliance/fireplace;
   smoke should be pulled up promptly. No draw → blockage remains (birds' nests are
   common) or a downdraught problem.
4. Clean up, then issue a **sweeping certificate**: date, appliance, flue, method,
   findings, recommendations. Insurers often ask for it.

## Gotchas
- Birds' nests: in the UK and EU, active nests are protected — check season and law.
- A clean sweep on a flue with no draw usually means a blockage you haven't cleared.
- Soot is a carcinogen: FFP3 mask, gloves, and wash.

## Verify
- [ ] Room and customer belongings sheeted; HEPA vacuum running throughout.
- [ ] Correct brush for flue type; rods turned clockwise only.
- [ ] Brush reached the terminal; debris checked for masonry/liner fragments.
- [ ] Appliance components refitted; smoke test shows good draw.
- [ ] Certificate issued with creosote stage and any defects noted.
