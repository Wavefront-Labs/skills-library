# Dryer duct cleaning and airflow testing

**Goal:** a clean, code-compliant exhaust path so the dryer dries faster and lint can't
ignite. Clothes dryers are a leading cause of home fires, mostly from lint build-up and
poor ducts. Certification exists (e.g. CSIA Certified Dryer Exhaust Technician in the US).

## 1. Ask and look first
- Symptoms: clothes take more than one cycle, dryer exterior hot, burning smell, lint
  around the door or behind the machine, "check vent" warnings on newer dryers.
- Dryer type: **vented** (this skill) vs **condenser/heat-pump** (no duct — clean the
  condenser/filters instead). Gas dryers also exhaust combustion products: a blocked
  vent means CO risk.

## 2. Measure airflow (before)
Pull the dryer out carefully (gas line and duct!), but leave it connected for the first test:
- Run the dryer on **air-fluff/no heat**, measure at the termination with a vane
  **anemometer** — record ft/min or m/s and photograph it. Or measure static pressure
  in the duct near the dryer with a manometer (maker's maximum is often ~0.6–0.8 in w.c.;
  check the dryer's install guide).
- A rough guide: healthy terminations often show well over ~1,000 ft/min (~5 m/s) on a
  standard 4 in duct; very low readings point at blockage or a crushed duct.
  These are field rules of thumb, not a standard — compare before vs after.

## 3. Inspect the duct
- **Material:** rigid or semi-rigid **metal**, smooth inside. Plastic or foil
  accordion ducts are not acceptable as concealed duct (and many codes ban them entirely);
  the only flexible piece allowed is a short, listed **transition duct** behind the dryer.
- **Diameter:** 4 in (100 mm) nominal.
- **Length:** US IRC M1502 limits the run to **35 ft equivalent**, deducting for fittings
  (commonly 5 ft per 90° elbow, 2.5 ft per 45°) unless the dryer maker allows longer.
- **Joints:** foil tape (not screws protruding into the duct — they catch lint), male end
  pointing in the airflow direction.
- **Termination:** outside (never into an attic, crawl space or chimney), with a
  backdraught damper; **no screen** on the termination (lint clogs it); a bird guard
  with large openings is acceptable where designed for dryers.

## 4. Clean
1. Disconnect at the dryer; cap the dryer outlet.
2. From the inside run a **rotary brush kit** (flexible rods, drill-driven, forward only,
   moderate speed) through the duct toward the outside, with the termination flap held
   open or removed and a bag or helper outside to catch lint.
3. Alternatively/also blow out with a compressed-air whip or a leaf blower from inside
   once loosened.
4. Clean the dryer's own internal lint path: lint filter housing (vacuum the cavity
   below the filter), blower area and the outlet — disconnect power/gas first, per the
   service manual. Lint inside the cabinet near the burner/element is a fire hazard.
5. Wash the lint filter if fabric softener has blinded it (water should pass through).

## 5. Fix what you can
Replace crushed or plastic/foil ducts with rigid metal and a short listed transition;
shorten the run or re-route to cut elbows; replace failed or screened termination caps.
Roof terminations need a dryer-specific roof cap and are often the worst clogged — use
safe roof access.

## 6. Measure airflow (after) and report
Re-test exactly as before. Record before/after values, duct length and fittings,
material, termination, any code issues and a **risk grade** (e.g. low / moderate / high —
high = foil or plastic in walls, over-length duct, attic termination, heavy lint).

## Gotchas
- Pulling the dryer out can kink a flexible gas connector — check for leaks after
  reconnecting (leak detector spray).
- Booster fans exist for long runs; they must be listed for dryers and interlocked.
- Lint at the termination looks clean outside while the elbow inside is packed.

## Verify
- [ ] Before/after airflow (or static pressure) measured and recorded.
- [ ] Whole duct and dryer lint path cleaned; termination flap moves freely.
- [ ] Duct material, diameter, equivalent length and termination checked against code.
- [ ] Gas connections leak-checked; dryer runs and dries in one cycle.
- [ ] Report with risk grade and recommended cleaning interval (often annual).
