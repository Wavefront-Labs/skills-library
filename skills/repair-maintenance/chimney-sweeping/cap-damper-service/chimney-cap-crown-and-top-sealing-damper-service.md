# Chimney cap, crown and top-sealing damper service

**Goal:** keep water, animals and downdraught out of the flue, and stop heat loss up
an unused chimney. Water is the main destroyer of masonry chimneys; most "chimney leaks"
come from the crown, the flashing or porous brick — water-test to find which rather than
assume.

## Roof safety first
Work at height is the biggest risk in this job. Use a roof ladder hooked over the ridge,
a harness and anchor or scaffold/edge protection as the job requires; don't work on wet,
icy or windy roofs; keep the ladder 1:4 angle and tied. Many tasks can be inspected by
drone or pole camera first.

## 1. Caps and bird guards
- **Size:** measure the flue tile/pot outside dimensions (single-flue caps clamp onto the
  tile) or the whole crown (multi-flue/outside-mount caps). Cap screen height should leave
  open area at least equal to (preferably greater than) the flue area.
- **Screen mesh:** US spark-arrestor caps commonly use ~5/8 in–3/4 in mesh — fine enough
  to stop birds and sparks, coarse enough not to soot up. Avoid fine mesh on wood/coal:
  it blocks with creosote and causes smoke-back.
- **Material:** stainless or copper; galvanised rusts in a few years.
- **Cowls (UK pots):** rain/bird guard, anti-downdraught (H-cowls, spinning cowls) for
  persistent downdraught — diagnose the cause (height, nearby trees/buildings) first.
  Gas appliances need gas-approved terminals only.
- Fix with the cap's clamp bolts or masonry anchors into the crown; never block the
  flue opening with silicone.

## 2. Top-sealing dampers
- Spring-loaded plate at the top of the flue, opened/closed by a stainless cable to a
  bracket in the firebox. Seals much better than old throat dampers (which warp).
- **Install:** fit the frame with high-temperature silicone to a clean, flat flue tile
  top (or the crown with an adaptor), run the cable down the flue, fix the handle bracket
  in the firebox; set cable length so the damper closes fully and the handle holds it open.
- **Warn the customer:** never light a fire with the damper closed; a red tag on the handle
  helps. Not for flues serving gas logs that need a fixed open damper by code.

## 3. Crown repair
- Hairline cracks: clean, prime, apply a flexible elastomeric crown sealant (brush-on)
  over the whole crown, feathered onto the flue tile and masonry.
- Wide cracks/crumbling: remove loose material; rebuild a crown with proper falls and a
  **drip edge overhang** (~2 in / 50 mm) using a crown mix (not ordinary mortar), and an
  **expansion gap** (bond break) between the crown and the flue tile, sealed with a flexible
  sealant — a rigid joint cracks as the tile heats and expands.

## 4. Flashing
Look for step flashing/counter-flashing let into mortar joints (not just stuck on with
sealant), lifted lead, rusted steel, failed sealant. Fix with new counter-flashing
chased ~1 in into the mortar joint and repointed. Roofer's work in many cases.

## 5. Water repellent for brick
Apply a **breathable** siloxane/silane water repellent to sound brick after repointing
and drying. Never use film-forming paints or sealers (trap moisture → spalling).
**Water test** suspect areas with a hose, one area at a time from the bottom up, with
someone inside watching.

## Gotchas
- A cap that's too low or with clogged mesh causes smoke-back; check after the first fire.
- Customers paint over damper cables; check the damper actually opens fully.
- Siloxane on wet or freshly pointed brick won't penetrate.

## Verify
- [ ] Safe access method used and documented.
- [ ] Cap open area ≥ flue area; mesh suitable for fuel; firmly fixed.
- [ ] Top-sealing damper closes airtight and holds open; customer briefed.
- [ ] Crown sealed with drip edge and flexible joint at the flue tile.
- [ ] Flashing let into joints; water test shows no leak.
