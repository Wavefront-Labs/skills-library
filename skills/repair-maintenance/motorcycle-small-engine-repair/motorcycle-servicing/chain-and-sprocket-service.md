# Chain and sprocket service

**Goal:** a correctly tensioned, lubricated chain that's aligned and replaced before
wear causes snatchy drive, accelerated sprocket wear or a thrown chain.

## 1. Clean and lube (every ~500–1,000 km, after rain or washing)
1. Bike on a **paddock stand** (or centre stand), engine off, in neutral.
2. Clean with **kerosene/paraffin** or a dedicated **O-ring-safe chain cleaner** and a
   three-sided chain brush. Avoid petrol, brake cleaner and aggressive solvents — they
   swell and destroy O/X-ring seals. Don't pressure-wash the chain.
3. Wipe dry.
4. Lube the **inside of the chain run** (rollers contact the sprocket there), on the
   lower run, rotating the wheel by hand — never with the engine running in gear. Let
   it soak/set (often 10–15 min or overnight per product) then wipe excess off.
   O/X-ring chains carry their lubricant inside the seals; external lube is mainly to
   keep seals and rollers from drying and corroding.

## 2. Check and set slack
- Measure on the **lower run midway** between sprockets, pushing up and down: total
  free play. Typical road bikes **~20–35 mm**, off-road bikes more — use the **figure on
  the swingarm sticker or manual**, measured in the position they specify (on stand,
  or on wheels with/without rider).
- Rotate the wheel and check at several points — chains wear unevenly; set slack at the
  **tightest spot**. A chain that's tight in one spot and slack elsewhere is due for replacement.
- **Too tight** is worse than slightly loose: as the suspension compresses, distance
  between sprockets increases and an over-tight chain loads the gearbox output shaft
  bearing and wheel bearings.
Adjust: loosen the axle nut, turn both adjusters **equal flats/turns**, check
alignment marks on both sides, tighten the axle nut to spec (new split pin if used),
recheck slack.

## 3. Alignment
Swingarm marks can be inaccurate. Better: a chain-alignment tool, or a straightedge/
string line along the rear tyre to the front, or measure axle-to-swingarm-pivot
distance both sides. A misaligned wheel wears the chain sides and sprocket teeth unevenly.

## 4. Wear check — when to replace
- **Stretch**: measure across a set number of pins on a tensioned chain (manual gives a
  service limit, e.g. "21 pins / 20 pitches" length). Or a chain-wear gauge.
- Quick test: pull the chain away from the back of the **rear sprocket** at 3 o'clock;
  if it lifts more than about half a tooth or you can see light under several rollers,
  it's worn.
- Kinked/stiff links that don't straighten after cleaning and lube, rusty rollers, missing
  O-rings → replace.
- **Sprockets**: hooked, pointed or "shark-fin" teeth, or teeth worn asymmetrically.
- **Replace chain and both sprockets as a set**. A new chain on worn sprockets wears out
  fast and can skip.

## 5. Replacing chain and sprockets
1. Loosen front sprocket nut **before** removing the old chain (rear brake on, in gear).
2. Cut the old chain with a **chain breaker** (grind the rivet heads of the master link
   first).
3. Fit sprockets: rear sprocket bolts with thread locker if specified, torque in a
   cross pattern. Front sprocket: new lock washer/nut per manual.
4. Count links: new chain to the correct link count for the sprocket sizes (compare
   with the old chain if sprockets unchanged).
5. **Rivet-type master link** (preferred for big road bikes): grease and O-rings in
   place, press the side plate to the spec width, then **flare/mushroom the rivets** with
   the tool to the spec diameter (measure with a vernier). **Clip-type links** are common
   on small bikes: clip's **closed end faces the direction of travel**.
6. Set slack and alignment; torque the axle nut; lube.
7. Recheck slack after ~100–200 km (new chains bed in).

## Gotchas
- Gearing changes (different sprocket sizes) change speedometer and ABS readings on
  some bikes.
- Using WD-40 alone as a lube — it's a cleaner/water-displacer, not a chain lube.
- Leaving lube on the tyre or brake disc — wipe and degrease if any gets there.

## Verify
- [ ] Slack within spec at the tightest point; adjusters equal; axle torqued.
- [ ] Wheel aligned by a method better than the swingarm marks.
- [ ] Chain clean, lubed, excess wiped; nothing on tyre or brake.
- [ ] Wear measured against the service limit; chain + sprockets replaced as a set.
- [ ] Master link rivets flared to spec (or clip faces direction of travel).
