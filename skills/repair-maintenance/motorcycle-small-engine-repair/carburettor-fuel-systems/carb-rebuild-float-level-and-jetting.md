# Carb rebuild, float level and jetting

**Goal:** a carburettor that starts easily, idles steadily and pulls cleanly through
the rev range. The usual enemy is stale fuel: ethanol blends leave **varnish** that
blocks the tiny pilot circuit after a few months of storage.

**Symptoms of a dirty carb:** runs only on choke, won't idle, hesitation off idle,
surging, fuel overflowing from the vent/overflow (stuck float needle).

## 1. Before you strip it
- Confirm **spark, compression and fresh fuel** first. Check fuel flows from the
  petcock/tap (vacuum petcocks need vacuum to open) and the tank vent isn't blocked.
- Drain the float bowl into a clear jar: water, rust or brown varnish confirms trouble.
- Photograph the carb, cable routing and hose routing. Note pilot screw setting by
  **counting turns in** to lightly seated (don't force — it damages the seat) before removal.

## 2. Strip and clean
1. Remove float bowl, float (tap out the pin), **float needle**, main jet, needle jet/
   emulsion tube, **pilot (slow) jet**, pilot screw (with its spring, washer and O-ring
   — tiny, easily lost), choke plunger. On CV carbs, remove the top cap, spring,
   diaphragm and slide; check the diaphragm for pinholes against a light.
2. Soak metal parts in carb cleaner or an **ultrasonic cleaner** (warm dedicated
   solution). Keep rubber, plastic and diaphragms out of solvent.
3. Clear jets with **compressed air and carb cleaner**, or a single strand of soft wire/
   jet-cleaning tool of the right size. Don't drill or ream — enlarging a jet changes
   calibration. Hold the pilot jet to the light: you must see a clean round hole.
4. Blow out every passage; spray cleaner into each orifice and watch it come out the
   connecting hole (pilot circuit: pilot jet ↔ idle ports near the throttle plate).
5. Fit a **rebuild kit**: float needle and seat, gaskets, O-rings. A worn float needle
   (ridge on the tip) causes flooding.

## 3. Float height/level
- Hold the carb **inverted and tilted** so the float tang just contacts the needle
  without compressing its spring-loaded pin. Measure gasket face to float bottom (or
  top, per manual) with a vernier/float gauge. Typical values: ~12–20 mm, model-specific.
- Bend the brass tang in tiny increments to adjust. Plastic floats with no tang are
  set by the seat or not adjustable.
- Or measure **fuel level** with a clear tube on the bowl drain (the manual gives a
  height relative to the bowl gasket) — most accurate.
- Float sinking/full of fuel (shake it, listen) = replace.

## 4. Base settings and tuning
- **Pilot screw**: back to the counted setting (or the manual's spec, often 1.5–2.5
  turns out). Warm the engine; adjust in ¼-turn steps for the highest, smoothest idle,
  then reset idle speed with the throttle stop screw.
- Mixture circuits by **throttle opening** (not rpm):
  - 0–¼ throttle: pilot jet + pilot screw.
  - ¼–¾: jet needle taper and **clip position** (raise needle = richer; on most needles
    moving the clip **down** raises the needle).
  - ¾–full: **main jet** (bigger number = richer, for most makers).
- **Plug reading**: after a sustained run in the range you're testing, kill engine
  without idling and read the plug — light tan/grey = OK; black sooty = rich; white/
  blistered = lean (dangerous for pistons).
- Multi-carb bikes: **synchronise** with vacuum gauges/manometer after rebuild.
- Changed intake/exhaust or altitude: jetting usually needs changes; stock settings
  assume stock parts and near sea level.

## 5. Fuel system and storage
- Replace cracked fuel lines and clamps; fit an inline filter on small engines.
- For storage: run the carb dry or drain the bowl, and use **fuel stabiliser** or
  ethanol-free fuel. This prevents most repeat rebuilds.
- EFI bikes: fuel pressure test, injector clean/flow test and diagnostic codes replace
  most of this — see the manual.

## Gotchas
- Swapping parts between carbs on multi-carb bikes; keep each carb's parts separate.
- Over-tightened float bowl screws strip soft castings; use a JIS screwdriver on Japanese carbs.
- Tiny pilot screw O-ring left in the body = no seal, erratic idle.
- Flooding with a new needle: debris on the seat, sticky float, or wrong float height.

## Verify
- [ ] Pilot jet hole visibly clear; every passage sprayed through.
- [ ] Float height to spec; no fuel leaks from overflow with petcock on.
- [ ] Starts on choke, idles steady warm, no hesitation through the throttle range.
- [ ] Plug colour light tan after runs at each throttle position tested.
