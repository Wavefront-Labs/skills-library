# Chainsaw bar and chain service

**Goal:** a saw that throws big chips, cuts straight without force and doesn't
overheat the bar — and a chain brake that works. A dull chain makes fine dust,
needs pushing and is the main cause of kickback-prone, tiring cutting.

Safety: engine off, **chain brake on**, plug cap off when working on the chain. Cut-
resistant gloves. Battery saws: battery out.

## 1. Identify the chain
Stamped on the drive links or the bar tail:
- **Pitch** (link spacing): ¼", .325", 3/8" low-profile (3/8" LP / .375 picco), 3/8", .404".
- **Gauge** (drive link thickness): .043", .050", .058", .063".
- **Drive link count** (e.g. 72 DL) for the bar length.
Chain, bar and **drive sprocket** must match pitch and gauge.

## 2. File size and angles (typical — follow the chain maker's chart)
| Pitch | Round file |
|---|---|
| ¼" / 3/8" LP | 4.0 mm (5/32") |
| .325" | 4.8 mm (3/16") (some .325 narrow/thin-kerf chains use 4.5 mm) |
| 3/8" | 5.5 mm (7/32") |
| .404" | 5.5–5.9 mm (7/32"–15/64") |
- **Top-plate filing angle ~25–35°** (most full-chisel/semi-chisel chains 30°), file held
  level or with the down-angle the chart specifies; file ~**1/5 of its diameter above the
  top plate** — a **file guide** sets this automatically.
- File from the inside of the cutter outward, **forward strokes only**, same number of
  strokes on every cutter. Do all cutters on one side, flip and do the other side.
- File every cutter back to the length of the shortest; uneven cutters make the saw
  pull to one side.

## 3. Depth gauges (rakers)
After every ~3–5 sharpenings, check with a **depth-gauge tool** laid across two cutters:
typical setting **~0.025" (0.65 mm)** for most saw chain in softwood; less for hard/frozen
wood. File the raker flat to the tool, then round the front edge. Too low = grabby
and kickback-prone; too high = chain won't bite.

## 4. Bar service
- Remove bar; clean the **groove** and **oil holes** with a groove cleaner or stick;
  clear the chain-adjuster and oil inlet hole.
- **Flip the bar** each sharpening/chain change for even wear.
- **Rails**: if one rail is higher (chain leans), or the rails have burrs/feathered
  edges, **dress** them flat with a flat file or bar dresser and square them.
- Grease the nose sprocket via its hole if the bar has one; check nose bearing for play.
- Bar is worn when the groove is too shallow (drive links bottom out) or rails are
  spread — replace.

## 5. Oiler and sprocket
- Run the saw at high revs pointed at light cardboard or a stump: an oil line should
  appear within ~10–20 s. No oil → clogged oil inlet/filter in the tank, worn oil pump
  drive (worm gear), or blocked bar hole. Adjustable oilers: increase for longer bars and hardwood.
- **Drive sprocket/rim**: worn grooves deeper than ~0.5 mm → replace. Rule of thumb:
  one sprocket every two chains; never a new chain on a badly worn sprocket.
- Check the **clutch drum** and needle bearing; grease the bearing.

## 6. Tensioning
Loosen bar nuts, lift the bar nose, adjust until the chain fits snug on the underside of
the bar but **pulls round freely by gloved hand**; you should be able to pull a drive
link partly out of the groove (~2–3 mm) and see it snap back. Tighten bar nuts while
still holding the nose up. **Re-tension after a few cuts** as a new chain warms and
stretches. Slacken a hot chain at the end of the day — it contracts as it cools.

## 7. Chain brake check
Engine running at idle, saw on the ground, flick the brake lever forward: chain must
stop instantly. A slow or failed stop → service the brake band and clutch area (dust,
worn band).

## Gotchas
- Hitting soil or a stone dulls a chain instantly — it'll need more than a touch-up.
- Wrong file size: too small undercuts ("hooks") the cutter, too large leaves a back-slope.
- Using motor oil instead of bar oil flings off; bar oil has tackifiers. Use bio bar oil
  where required.
- Chain running blue/smoking = no oil or too tight.

## Verify
- [ ] Pitch, gauge and DL count match bar and sprocket.
- [ ] Cutters equal length, correct angle and file height; rakers set with the gauge.
- [ ] Bar groove clean, rails square; oiler throws a visible line.
- [ ] Tension: snug but turns by hand; chain brake stops the chain instantly.
- [ ] Saw throws chips and cuts straight without pressure.
