# Bridge fitting and soundpost adjustment — violin family

**Goal:** a bridge whose feet meet the belly with no gaps, at a height and curve that
give correct string clearance and bowing angles, standing straight; and a soundpost
fitted snugly in the right place so the instrument speaks evenly.

## 1. Bridge position and checks (before cutting anything)
- Bridge feet centred between the inner **f-hole nicks** (the notches mark the stop/
  bridge line). Feet centred on the centre-line of the top.
- **Tailpiece side** of the bridge should be **perpendicular to the top**; the
  fingerboard side slopes. A bridge leaning toward the fingerboard (string tension
  pulls it forward during tuning) eventually **warps** — straighten it by pulling the
  top gently back with both hands while slackening tension slightly, every few tunings.

## 2. Fitting a new blank (e.g. Aubert, Despiau)
1. **Feet**: lay fine sandpaper (180–220) on the top over the bridge location (tape
  the paper; protect varnish around it), hold the blank at the correct lean and draw it
  back and forth so the feet take the arching. Finish with a sharp knife until the
  feet meet the top with **no light visible** front, back and sides. Thin the feet to
  ~1.0–1.3 mm (violin) at their edges.
2. **Height** (violin, typical): strings above the end of the fingerboard ~**3.0–3.5 mm
   (E)** and ~**5.0–5.5 mm (G)**. Viola ~3.5–4 / 5.5–6 mm. Cello ~5–5.5 (A) / 7–7.5 mm (C).
   *Ranges vary with player and steel vs synthetic strings.*
3. **Top curve**: draw the curve from a template; it must let each string be bowed
   without hitting its neighbours, slightly flatter on the E side.
4. **Thickness**: top edge ~1.2–1.4 mm (violin), tapering to ~4–4.5 mm at the feet; the
   fingerboard side is the one shaped, tailpiece side stays flat.
5. **String spacing** (violin): typically ~**33.5–34 mm** between E and G at the top;
   notches about one-third string diameter deep, smooth and graphite-lubricated; a
   parchment or rubber sleeve under the E.
6. Carve hearts and kidneys if desired (affects mass and tone); clean edges.

## 3. Soundpost
A spruce dowel standing between the top and back, slightly **behind the treble foot**
of the bridge (violin: roughly one post's diameter, often ~2–3 mm, behind the bridge
foot, and just inside the foot toward the centre). It transmits vibration and supports
the top under bridge load.
- **Fit**: ends cut to match the top and back arching at that spot, grain of the post
  **perpendicular** to the grain of the top. Length so it stands with light tension —
  not loose, not forced.
- **Setting**: slacken strings (keep the bridge in place if possible, or support the top),
  insert with an S-shaped **soundpost setter** through the treble f-hole, stand it, then
  check through the end-pin hole with a mirror/light that it's vertical from both views.
- **Adjustment** (small moves, ~0.5 mm at a time):
  - Toward the bridge = brighter, more focused, sometimes harsh; away = darker, freer, can
    get woolly.
  - Toward the treble/E side = more brilliance on E, less on G; toward centre = more G.
  - Tighter fit = brighter and more resistant; looser = softer.
*These are tendencies, not rules — every instrument responds differently; let the
player play between moves.*

## 4. Other quick checks
- **Fingerboard projection** and neck angle set bridge height possibilities — a very low
  projection forces a low bridge and weak tone (neck reset).
- **Open seams** (top/back to ribs): buzz and change tone. Reglue with **hot hide glue**
  only — it's reversible and repairs depend on that.
- **Pegs**: slipping → peg compound; sticking/cracking → refit or bush. Never glue a peg.

## Gotchas
- Never let the soundpost fall with strings at full tension — the top can crack.
- Don't remove all string tension at once: the bridge and soundpost can shift or fall.
- Synthetic glues and superglue on old instruments make future repairs destructive.

## Verify
- [ ] Bridge feet fit with no light gaps; tailpiece side perpendicular.
- [ ] String heights at the fingerboard end within the target range; spacing even.
- [ ] Each string bows cleanly without catching neighbours.
- [ ] Soundpost vertical in both views, snug, behind the treble foot; player approves tone.
