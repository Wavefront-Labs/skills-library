# Stuck slides, dents and valve service — brass instruments

**Goal:** free, sealed, smoothly moving slides and valves, bore dents removed without
stretching or thinning the metal, and joints soldered cleanly.

## 1. Stuck mouthpiece
**Never use pliers or a vice** — you'll crush the leadpipe or tear the braces.
Use a **mouthpiece puller** (Bobcat-style): the jaws sit between the mouthpiece shank
and the receiver, and the screws force them apart evenly. Tap lightly round the
receiver with a rawhide mallet if needed.

## 2. Stuck slides (tuning and valve slides)
Escalate gently:
1. Penetrating oil on the joint, wait (hours to overnight).
2. **Cloth/strap through the crook**: loop strong cloth or a leather strap through the
   slide crook and pull straight in line with the tubes while a helper holds the
   instrument (or hook it on a padded peg). Pull on both legs evenly — pulling one tube
   bends the slide.
3. **Gentle heat** with a heat gun or small flame on the outer tube only (expands it)
   — lacquer will be damaged by a flame, and soft-soldered joints nearby can let go.
   Wet rag on nearby joints.
4. Rawhide mallet taps against a wooden dowel on the crook, in line with the tubes.
5. Valve slides: **press the valve down** while pulling (otherwise you're fighting vacuum).
Clean and lubricate afterwards (slide grease for tuning slides, light slide oil or cream
for trombone hand slides).

## 3. Dent removal
- Small bore: **dent rods/mandrels** for tubes; **dent balls** (steel balls in graded
  sizes) — start a size or two smaller than the bore at the dent, push it with a rod or
  magnet into the dent, roll/tap the outside lightly, step up ball sizes until the bore
  is round. Lubricate the ball.
- Bells and bows: **burnishers** and bell mandrels; work from the edges of the dent toward
  the centre with many light passes. Hammering stretches and thins brass.
- **Magnetic dent-removal kits** let you work closed tubes without disassembly.
- Sharp creases and kinks can crack; anneal first (specialist).

## 4. Piston valves (trumpet, euphonium, tuba)
- Clean: remove valves, wipe, wash in lukewarm water with mild soap, clean casings
  with a cleaning rod and lint-free cloth.
- Oil: synthetic valve oil on the piston body; reinstall each valve **in its numbered
  casing**, guide aligned with the slot until it clicks.
- **Alignment**: the ports in the piston must line up with the casing tubes in the up
  and down positions. Worn felts/corks change this. Check with a **valve alignment
  tool/borescope** through a slide tube; adjust felt or cork thickness under the
  finger button and top cap.
- Leaky valves (worn pistons) = poor slotting and stuffy sound; replating/lapping is
  specialist work.

## 5. Rotary valves (horn, rotary trumpet, trombone triggers)
Oil: rotor oil through the slide tubes onto the rotor, bearing oil on the spindle and
bearing caps. **Restring** (string-action horns) with braided line in the factory
pattern; stop corks/bumpers set the alignment marks on the rotor and casing.

## 6. Chemical cleaning and soldering
- Full clean: disassemble, soak in warm water + brass-safe cleaner, snake through, then
  a mild acid descaler (e.g. citric-based) for limescale; neutralise and rinse. Don't use
  ammonia-based cleaners on lacquer/silver plate. Ultrasonic tanks clean slide crooks well.
- **Loose brace**: clean joint bright, flux, **soft solder** (low-melting tin-lead or lead-free
  tin-silver), torch heat applied to the larger part so solder flows by capillary action.
  Too much heat will release neighbouring joints and burn lacquer.
- Leadpipe and bell joints are often silver/hard soldered — specialist.

## Gotchas
- **Red rot** (pink spots eating through from inside, especially leadpipes) is
  dezincification; the tube needs replacing, not polishing.
- Over-greasing tuning slides pushes grease into the bore.
- Bent trombone hand-slide tubes cause friction long before you see the bend — check on
  a flat surface.

## Verify
- [ ] Slides pull and push smoothly with even tension; no leaks (pop test: pull slide
      with valve up — it should pop when air-tight).
- [ ] Bore at dented area round to the next ball size; no thinning or cracks.
- [ ] Valves aligned (borescope/tool) and move freely; felts/corks correct.
- [ ] Solder joints clean, fully flowed, no fumes or burnt lacquer around them.
