# Full setup and intonation — guitar and bass

**Goal:** low, buzz-free action, accurate intonation up the neck, and an instrument
that stays in tune. **Order matters**: each step affects the next.
relief → nut → action → intonation → pickups.

## Kit
Fresh strings, capo, feeler gauges (0.05–0.5 mm), 6" steel rule with 0.5 mm/64th
marks (or a string action gauge), truss rod wrenches (Allen/box, often 4 mm, 5 mm,
1/8", 3/16", 5/16"), small screwdrivers, a good chromatic or strobe tuner, nut files
(for nut work), radius gauges.

## 0. Prep
Restring at the tension you'll play (changing gauge changes everything). Tune to pitch.
Inspect frets for wear/high frets (see below) and check the neck for twist.

## 1. Neck relief (truss rod)
1. Capo at the **1st fret**, hold the low E (or the bass's E) down at the **last fret**
   (or where the neck meets the body).
2. Measure the gap between the bottom of the string and the top of the fret at the
   **7th–8th fret** with a feeler gauge.
3. Typical targets:
   - Electric: **~0.20–0.30 mm** (0.008–0.012")
   - Acoustic: **~0.25–0.38 mm** (0.010–0.015")
   - Bass: **~0.30–0.45 mm** (0.012–0.018")
4. Adjust: **clockwise (righty-tighty, looking at the nut) = less relief**;
   anticlockwise = more. Go **⅛ turn at a time**, retune, let it settle (minutes for
   small changes; leave overnight for old or big changes). Don't force a stiff rod —
   loosen slightly and lubricate; a stripped rod is a big repair.

## 2. Nut slot height
Press each string between the 2nd and 3rd frets; the gap over the **1st fret** should
be tiny — roughly the thickness of a sheet of paper (~0.05–0.10 mm under the
string). Higher nut slots make the first frets sharp and hard to play.
Cut slots with the correct-width **nut file**, angled down toward the headstock,
taking very small passes — too deep = open-string buzz and a new nut. (Shim a low slot
with bone dust + thin CA as a temporary fix.)

## 3. Action at the 12th fret
Measured from the top of the 12th fret to the bottom of the string:
| | Treble side (high E) | Bass side (low E) |
|---|---|---|
| Electric | ~1.5 mm (4/64") | ~2.0 mm (5/64") |
| Acoustic | ~1.8–2.0 mm | ~2.4–2.8 mm |
| Bass | ~2.0–2.4 mm (G) | ~2.4–2.8 mm (E) |
- Strat/individual saddles: raise/lower each saddle to follow the **fretboard radius**
  (use a radius gauge under the strings).
- Tune-o-matic: adjust the two thumbwheels.
- Acoustic: sand the bottom of the **saddle** (flat, square — use a block) to lower; shim
  or make a new saddle to raise. Lower by about twice the action change you want at
  the 12th (the saddle sits roughly twice as far from the 12th as the nut).
Players who dig in want higher; light players can go lower. Tune before measuring.

## 4. Intonation
With new strings in tune, compare the **12th-fret fretted note** with the open string
(or the 12th-fret harmonic) on a good tuner:
- Fretted **sharp** → move the saddle **back** (away from the neck; lengthen the string).
- Fretted **flat** → move the saddle **forward**.
Small moves, retune each time, play the fretted note with normal pressure. Check
again after any action change.

## 5. Pickup height
Fret at the last fret; measure from the pickup's pole piece to the bottom of the string.
Common starting point: humbuckers ~2.4 mm (3/32") bass side / ~1.6 mm (1/16") treble;
single coils lower and further back (~3.2 mm bass / ~2.4 mm treble) — strong magnets
pull the strings, causing warbling ("Stratitis") and poor sustain. Balance volume by ear.

## 6. Tremolo notes
Floating trems (Floyd Rose, vintage Strat): balance spring tension against string tension
**first**, before relief/action, and re-balance after any gauge or tuning change.

## Common problems
- **Buzz on one or two frets only** → high fret (rocker test with a short straight-edge
  over three frets) — fret level or tap/glue that fret.
- **Buzz everywhere low on the neck** → not enough relief or low nut slots.
- **Buzz at the top of the neck** → back-bow, fret sprout, or a "ski jump" at the body joint.

## Verify
- [ ] Relief, nut, 12th-fret action and pickup heights measured and noted.
- [ ] 12th-fret notes within ±2 cents of open/harmonic on all strings.
- [ ] Play every fret on every string clean at normal attack; no dead spots.
- [ ] Instrument re-checked after 24 h settling.
