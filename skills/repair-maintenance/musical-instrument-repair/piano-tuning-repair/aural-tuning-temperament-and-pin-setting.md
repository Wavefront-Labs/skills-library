# Aural tuning: temperament, octaves, unisons and pin setting

**Goal:** an equal-tempered tuning that sounds even in every key, octaves that are
clean for the piano's inharmonicity, pure unisons, and pins set so it all stays put.

## Kit
Tuning lever (hammer) with the right tip (usually #2 star/square for most modern pianos),
felt **temperament strip** and rubber wedge mutes, A440 tuning fork or electronic
reference, optionally an ETD app (e.g. TuneLab, Verituner, CyberTuner) for pitch raise
and checking.

## 0. Pitch check
Measure where the piano is. If it's more than ~10–15 cents flat of A440, do a **pitch
raise** (a fast rough pass, overpulling a little to anticipate the drop) before the fine
tuning — otherwise tension changes as you go will throw the early notes out.

## 1. Lever technique and pin setting (the most important skill)
- Hold the lever close to vertical (roughly 12 to 1 o'clock on uprights; follow
  the tuner's comfort on grands) so you're **twisting** the pin, not bending it.
- Approach pitch **from slightly sharp or flat** consistently (many tuners pull slightly
  above, then settle down) — then **set the pin**: a tiny counter-movement that leaves
  the pin's twist neutral, so it doesn't spring back.
- **Set the string**: firm test blows (fortissimo) after tuning each note. If it moves,
  it wasn't stable; redo. Stability beats speed.
- Very small lever movements; feel the pin "give" before it turns.

## 2. Temperament (one common sequence — F3–F4 octave)
Mute the outer strings of each trichord across the temperament octave with the strip, so
you tune only centre strings.
1. Tune **A4** to the fork (zero beats) or reference; tune **A3** a clean octave below.
2. Use **beat rates** of intervals. In equal temperament:
   - **Fourths** beat slightly **wide**, **fifths** slightly **narrow**, at about
     ~1 beat/s around the middle (fifths slower, ~0.5–0.8 bps; fourths ~0.8–1.2 bps).
   - **Major thirds** beat wide and fast — around 7–11 bps in this octave, increasing
     smoothly as you go up.
   - **Major sixths** beat wide and also increase smoothly.
3. Build out by fourths and fifths from A (e.g. A3–D4, D4–G3, G3–C4, C4–F3, F3–A#3...),
   checking every new note against **thirds** and **sixths** already tuned.
4. **Key check — contiguous major thirds**: play F3–A3, F#3–A#3, G3–B3... up the
   chromatic scale. Their beat rates must **increase smoothly** with no jumps. A jump
   pinpoints the wrong note. Also check **thirds inside fifths**: F3–A3–C4 sounds even.
*Many sequences exist (e.g. thirds-based "Bill Bremmer" or A-to-A methods); what matters
is that every check interval progresses smoothly.*

## 3. Octaves and stretch
Real piano strings are **inharmonic** — partials run sharp — so octaves tuned "pure"
for the piano are slightly wider than 2:1. Tune upward and downward by octaves from the
temperament, checking:
- **Octaves** and **double octaves** clean; **octave + fifth (12th)** clean.
- Treble: stretch so the top doesn't sound flat to the ear — compare with the 12th
  and double octave. Bass: listen for clean 6:3 / 4:2 octave types and a solid, non-
  sour sound.
Small spinets/consoles need more stretch than concert grands (higher inharmonicity).

## 4. Unisons
Pull the strip, one section at a time, and tune outer strings to the centre string
until there are **no beats** — a clean unison has no "waver" or roll in the decay.
Bring each string in with the same pin setting care; unisons drift first.

## 5. Common faults
- **False beats** (a string beating against itself): bad bridge pin seating, twisted
  string, loose bridge pin. Seat the string at the bridge with a brass punch gently; if
  it persists, tune around it and note it.
- **Jumping pins/loose pins**: tuning pins that won't hold (low torque) → oversize pins,
  CA glue treatment of the pinblock (specialist, contested), or pinblock replacement.
- **Broken string**: splice or replace with matching gauge (music wire gauge chart) —
  tune it several times over a week as it stretches.

## Gotchas
- Humidity changes move pitch more than anything: recommend stable 40–50% RH and tuning
  at least once or twice a year.
- Never tune a piano straight after it's been moved into a new room — let it settle a few weeks.
- ETD helps, but always confirm by ear with thirds and octave checks.

## Verify
- [ ] Contiguous thirds and sixths progress smoothly across the temperament.
- [ ] Octaves, double octaves and 12ths are clean through the compass.
- [ ] Unisons beatless; no notes move after firm test blows.
- [ ] Pitch at A440 (or the requested standard) recorded with humidity.
