# Pad seating and leak testing — woodwinds

**Goal:** every pad seals its tone hole with light spring pressure, linked keys close
together, and the instrument plays freely from top to bottom. Leaks are the cause of
most "the low notes won't speak" and "it gurgles/squeaks" complaints.

## 1. Find the leaks
- **Leak light**: a bright, thin LED strip (or bulb on a flexible wand) inside the body in
  a dim room. Close each key with **its normal spring pressure** (not finger squeeze) and
  look for light around the pad edge. Light = leak.
- **Feeler paper**: a strip of thin cigarette paper or Mylar (~0.02 mm) under the pad;
  close the key lightly and pull. Even drag all round = good seal. Free slide on one
  side = leak there. Test at 4 positions (front, back, both sides).
- **Suction/pressure test** (clarinet, oboe): close the open end, cover holes and keys,
  suck — the joint should hold vacuum for several seconds.
- **Magnaprobe / leak detectors** with sensors for flutes and saxes speed this up.
- Check from the top down; one leak high up affects everything below it.

## 2. Why pads leak
Torn or hardened pads, **pad not level** to the tone hole (shifted in the cup), **bent
key or cup**, tone hole rim nicked or not flat, loose key (worn pivot/hinge), or
**regulation** — a linked key holding a pad slightly open.

## 3. Replacing and seating pads
**Pad types**: saxophone — leather pads (with or without resonators); clarinet — skin
(fish/bladder), leather or synthetic (e.g. Valentino, Gore); flute — felt + skin
pads, shimmed with paper shims; oboe/bassoon — mix of cork and skin/leather.
1. Remove the key (note springs; needle springs slide out of cradles with a spring hook).
2. Warm the cup gently with a small flame or heat gun to soften the old adhesive; lift
   the pad out; clean the cup.
3. **Sizing**: the pad must fill the cup without tight squeezing (wrinkles) and be thick
   enough to sit with the cup about level when closed.
4. **Adhesive**:
   - **Shellac** (sticks): melted into the cup; stays re-meltable for floating and levelling.
   - **Hot glue** (sax): common in shops; also re-meltable.
   - **Flute**: pads are held by a screw/washer; level with **paper shims** under the pad,
     not glue.
5. Reinstall the key; warm the cup so the adhesive softens; close the key with light pressure
   — the pad "floats" to the tone hole. Hold closed while it cools (a clamp/wedge).
6. **Check with feeler paper**; adjust by re-warming and pressing the side that's light,
   or **bend the key/cup slightly** with key-bending pliers (skilled work; support the
   key so you don't bend the post or hinge tube).
7. Leather pads: once level, iron the seat lightly with a warmed **pad iron/slick** to
   form a crisp seat impression.

## 4. Regulation (linked keys)
Examples: sax G♯/low B-C♯-B♭ linkage, the **bis** and **front F** keys, the clarinet
**ring/bridge keys**, flute **foot joint** and right-hand keys.
- Adjust regulation **screws** (where fitted) or **cork/felt thickness** at the contact
  points so both pads close at the same moment. Feeler paper under each simultaneously.
- Replace corks with natural or synthetic cork / felt; thickness sets opening height and
  key noise.
- Key **height** (venting) affects intonation and tone of the lowest notes — follow the
  maker's specs or matched-to-player settings.

## 5. Tenon corks (clarinet, oboe, flute head joint cork)
Remove old cork, clean the tenon groove, glue new sheet cork (contact cement), scarf-
joint the ends, sand to a snug fit; joint should assemble with firm hand pressure using
cork grease. Too tight → cracks in wooden bodies.

## 6. Body care
Wooden clarinets/oboes: warm the instrument before playing in cold weather (cracking
risk), swab after every use, oil bores only per the maker. Cracks: pinning/banding is
specialist work.

## Gotchas
- **Over-tight springs** mask leaks in test but cause hard key action; under-tight springs
  leak under playing pressure.
- Pads seated with finger pressure only seal when squeezed — always test at spring pressure.
- Too much heat warps plastic keys, lifts lacquer and loosens soft-soldered posts.

## Verify
- [ ] Leak light dark around every pad at normal spring pressure.
- [ ] Feeler paper drags evenly at four points on each pad.
- [ ] Linked keys close simultaneously; no double-closing lag.
- [ ] Joints assemble firmly but easily; play-test full range, soft and loud.
