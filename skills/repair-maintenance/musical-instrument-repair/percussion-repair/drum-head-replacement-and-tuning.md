# Drum head replacement and tuning

**Goal:** heads evenly tensioned so each drum has a clear, consistent pitch, the snare is
crisp without choking, and the kit tunes quickly and stays in tune.

## 1. Remove and inspect
1. Loosen all tension rods a little at a time in a star pattern (avoid warping the hoop),
   then remove them. Lift off the hoop and head.
2. **Bearing edge**: run a fingertip round; look for chips, dents, flat spots, or old
   head glue/debris. Check flatness by setting the shell on a flat surface (glass/granite)
   and shining a light from inside: light gaps = low spots.
   Damaged edges cause pitch that won't settle and wrinkles that won't tune out; recutting
   is router-and-jig work (specialist).
3. Clean the shell and hoops; lubricate tension-rod threads with a light grease or
   lithium paste; check lugs for loose screws, rattles (replace damping springs/felts),
   and swivel nut wear.

## 2. Seating a new head
1. Centre the head on the shell; hoop on top; insert rods.
2. **Finger-tight** every rod until the washer just touches the hoop.
3. Tension in a **star (crosswise) pattern** — e.g. on 8 lugs: 1, 5, 3, 7, 2, 6, 4, 8 — in
   **equal half- or quarter-turns**.
4. **Pre-stretch**: bring it up well above playing pitch, press firmly in the centre with
   the palm (you may hear the glue crackle — normal), then detune back to finger-tight.
   This seats the collar and helps the head hold pitch.
5. Bring back up to pitch in small, equal increments.

## 3. Fine tuning — evenness at each lug
- Tap lightly **~2–3 cm (1") in front of each lug** with a stick or finger; adjust each lug
  so its pitch matches the others. Tune **up** to the note (if sharp, go below then back up).
- Tools: **DrumDial** (measures head deflection) or **Tune-Bot** (frequency per lug)
  speed this up and make tunings repeatable — log the numbers.

## 4. Batter vs resonant relationships (starting points)
- **Same pitch top and bottom**: longest sustain, pure note.
- **Resonant slightly higher** than batter: pitch bends **down** after the hit ("drop").
- **Resonant slightly lower**: pitch bends **up**.
- **Toms**: tune to musical intervals (e.g. 3rds or 4ths apart); start each tom at the
  lowest tension where the head has no wrinkles and a clear tone, then raise for the
  "sweet spot" where the shell resonates most.
- **Kick**: batter just past wrinkle-free for a deep thud; resonant slightly higher with
  a port hole (~10–12 cm) if miking; light muffling (pillow or felt strip) touching the
  batter lightly.
- **Snare**: batter as tight as the style wants (jazz high/crisp, rock mid); **snare-side
  (resonant) head** thin (~2–3 mil) and usually tuned **higher** than the batter for
  sensitivity.

## 5. Snare wires and strainer
1. Strainer **off**; centre the wires so both ends are the same distance from the hoop.
2. Engage the strainer; turn the tension knob up until the wires **just stop buzzing
   loosely**, then a touch more. Too tight chokes the drum and kills low-volume response.
3. Play soft ghost notes and rimshots: crisp both ways. Sympathetic buzz from other drums
   (toms) is normal — slight pitch tweaks to the toms reduce it.
4. Stretched/bent wires (some strands sagging) → replace the wire set.

## 6. Timpani (overview)
Pedal (balanced spring or ratchet) must hold any pitch without creeping. Head tuning:
**clear the head** — equal pitch at each lug at a mid pedal position. If the pedal creeps
up or down, adjust the **spring tension/balance knob** per the maker. Bowls and
heads are expensive — avoid moving timpani by the rims.

## Gotchas
- Tightening one lug a lot to fix a "dead spot" warps the head; go round evenly.
- Cheap or stretched heads won't hold pitch — replace batters when dented/pitted.
- Temperature and humidity change pitch; re-tune at the venue.
- Over-muffling with tape and gel hides a bad tuning; tune first, then muffle.

## Verify
- [ ] Bearing edges inspected; heads seated with pre-stretch.
- [ ] Pitch at every lug matches (by ear or DrumDial/Tune-Bot readings logged).
- [ ] Toms at intended intervals; kick and snare in the desired range.
- [ ] Snare crisp at ghost-note and full volume; no choking.
- [ ] Re-check after a day of playing; drift small and even.
