# Tissue tear repair and tipping in

**Goal:** near-invisible, reversible, flexible page repairs. The core materials are
Japanese tissue and wheat starch paste; the core rule is **never use pressure-sensitive
tape** (Sellotape, "magic" tape, even many "archival" tapes): adhesives migrate, stain
brown and become hard to remove.

## Materials
- **Japanese tissue (kozo):** thin (tengujo ~9–12 g/m²) for text areas, medium for
  heavier repairs. Long fibres make strong, feathered repairs.
- **Wheat starch paste** cooked thin (consistency of thin cream for mending), or
  **methyl cellulose** (weaker, wetter, good for blending with paste).
- Non-stick interleaving (Reemay/Hollytex polyester web, silicone paper), blotters,
  glass or acrylic weights, fine brush, water brush/pen, bone folder, tweezers.

## 1. Surface clean first
Dirt locked under a repair is permanent. Use a soft brush, then a vulcanised rubber
"smoke sponge" or crumbled vinyl eraser crumbs rubbed gently in circles, working from the
centre outward. Never over printed areas that smudge (pencil, pastel, foxing patches on
soft paper — test a corner).

## 2. Mending a tear
1. **Read the tear:** tears are usually bevelled; one edge overlaps the other. Realign
   the bevels exactly, the way they separated. Set the page on Reemay on a blotter.
2. **Make feathered strips:** draw a line on the tissue with a water brush (or a ruler
   and wet brush), let it soften, pull apart to leave long fibres along the edge. Strips
   ~5–10 mm wide; for a curved tear, follow the curve with short strips.
3. Apply thin paste to the tear edges with a fine brush (very sparingly — just enough to
   be tacky), lay the realigned edges together.
4. Lay the tissue on the back (verso) where possible, along the tear, fibres bridging it.
   Tamp gently through Reemay with the bone folder.
5. Reemay both sides, blotter both sides, light weight. Change blotters after ~15 minutes,
   then dry under weight for several hours or overnight.
6. Trim excess tissue at the page edge flush once dry.

## 3. Loose leaves: tipping in
For a single detached leaf with a sound inner margin:
1. Check its position from page numbers/signature marks.
2. Lay waste paper over the leaf leaving a **2–3 mm strip** along the inner edge exposed;
   brush paste (or PVA/paste mix for heavy stock) on that strip only.
3. Position into the gutter against its neighbour, aligned at head and fore-edge. Close
   with waxed paper either side and press briefly.
- Tipping-in restricts the opening a little; for several loose leaves or a damaged inner
  margin, use **guarding** instead.

## 4. Guarding
Paste a strip of Japanese tissue (or thin paper for heavier repairs) half onto the inner
edge of the leaf and half folded to wrap around the neighbouring leaf or around the fold
of the signature. Guards rebuild a broken fold so a signature can be resewn — see
repair-maintenance.book-repair-rebinding.resewing-text-blocks.taking-down-and-resewing-on-tapes.

## 5. Flattening creases and cockles
Lightly humidify (a damp blotter separated from the paper by Gore-Tex/Sympatex, or a
humidity chamber) until the sheet is relaxed, then dry between blotters under even weight.
Don't spray water on printed or inked areas: iron-gall ink, watercolour and some inks run.

## Gotchas
- Thick paste = shine and stiff, cockled repairs. Thin and sparse.
- Cut tissue edges show as hard lines; always tear/feather.
- Coated (glossy) papers resist paste and can stick to anything wet — test and interleave.
- Removing old tape: heat (low, a tacking iron) can lift the carrier; stains remaining
  need solvent work best left to a conservator.

## Verify
- [ ] Surface cleaned before mending.
- [ ] Tear edges realigned with bevels matched; repair on the verso.
- [ ] Repair flexes with the page, no shine or cockle once dry.
- [ ] Tipped-in leaf sits square; book still opens to the gutter.
- [ ] No pressure-sensitive tape used anywhere.
