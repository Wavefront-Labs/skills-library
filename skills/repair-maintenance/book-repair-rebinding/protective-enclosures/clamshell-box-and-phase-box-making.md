# Clamshell box and phase box making

**Goal:** a snug enclosure that protects a fragile book from light, dust, handling and
abrasion without squeezing it. A loose book in a box rattles and wears; a tight one
gets damaged every time it's taken out. Accurate measuring is 80% of the job.

## Choose the enclosure
- **Phase box** (folded archival board, two crossed wraps): fast, cheap, for damaged books
  awaiting repair or for collections. 30–60 minutes.
- **Clamshell / drop-spine box** (two nested trays on a cloth case): the standard for
  valued books; the book lies flat and lifts out without friction. A few hours.
- **Slipcase:** looks good but the book is pulled out past the spine edges, abrading them;
  avoid for fragile or loose-boarded books.

## Measure
Use a **book-measuring device** or two squares against a flat surface: height, width
(spine to fore-edge) and thickness at the thickest point. Measure each dimension at
several points and take the maximum. Write them down; double-check thickness — books
are rarely parallel.

## 1. Phase box (archival board, ~1–1.5 mm / or e-flute)
1. Cut a strip as wide as the book's **width** (+1 mm), long enough to wrap
   height-wise: H + T + H + T + flap.
2. Cut a second strip as wide as the book's **height** (+1 mm) wrapping width-wise:
   W + T + W + T + flap.
3. Score fold lines with a bone folder against a rule, allowing ~one board thickness
   extra at each fold; test-fold round the book.
4. Glue (PVA) the centre panels of the two strips crossed at 90°.
5. Closure: Velcro dots or a button and string, not rubber bands (they perish and stain).

## 2. Clamshell (drop-spine) box
Board: 2–2.5 mm archival binder's board, lignin-free, buffered for most books (unbuffered
for some photographs and proteins like silk/wool — check).
1. **Inner tray base:** H + 2 mm × W + 2 mm (1 mm clearance each side; slightly more for
   lifting fragile books).
2. **Inner walls:** height = T + 1–2 mm. Head, tail and fore-edge walls glued onto the edge
   of the base (they sit *on* the base edge). Spine side is left open on the inner tray.
3. **Outer tray base:** inner tray outer size + 2 × board thickness + ~1 mm; walls height
   = inner wall height + board thickness + ~1 mm, so the inner tray nests inside.
4. Glue walls with PVA, using a square and weights; cover each tray in cloth: one long
   strip running round the outside and turning over the top edge and inside.
5. **Case:** two boards (each = outer tray base + 3 mm all round) and a spine board
   (= outer tray wall height + board thickness × 2). Assemble on cloth with joint gaps
   (~2 × board thickness) like a book case; turn in.
6. Glue the outer tray onto the back board (fore-edge aligned), the inner tray onto the
   front board, open sides facing the spine of the case so the "drop spine" folds down.
7. Line the inside with paper. Press flat overnight. Add a spine label.

## Gotchas
- Tray walls glued to the base vs. around the base change every dimension — choose one
  convention and stick to it.
- Unbuffered vs buffered board matters for some materials; when in doubt for photos,
  use unbuffered.
- PVA moisture warps the case: weight it and line both sides to balance.
- Leave a finger notch or ribbon lift for fat books so they can be lifted out.

## Verify
- [ ] Book measured at several points; maximum values used.
- [ ] Book sits in the tray with ~1 mm clearance, no rattling, lifts out without friction.
- [ ] Box closes square; trays nest without rubbing.
- [ ] All board and paper archival (lignin-free); no rubber bands or metal fasteners.
- [ ] Box flat after a night in the press; label on the spine.
