# Taking down and resewing on tapes

**Goal:** turn a text block with broken sewing and loose sections into a sound, flexible
one, ready for a new or reused case. Sewing on linen tapes is the most forgiving
structure for repair work: strong, opens well, quick to learn.

## 1. Decide if resewing is right
- **Sewn book, thread broken, sections loose** → resew (this skill).
- **Adhesive ("perfect") binding with single leaves** → there are no folds to sew
  through; options are re-gluing with a fanned double-fan adhesive binding, or making
  guarded folios. Oversewing works but stops the book opening flat — avoid on valuable books.
- **Only one or two loose sections** → may be tipped or sewn back in place without a full
  take-down.

## 2. Collate and take down
1. **Collate first:** check every page and signature is present and in order; note
   signature marks (letters/numbers at the foot of the first page of each section) and
   plates. Write a pencil note of the sequence.
2. Remove the case (cut through the mull/hinges), then the old spine linings — soften old
   animal glue with a paste poultice for a few minutes and scrape with a dull knife;
   PVA may need patient mechanical removal.
3. Cut the old threads in the centre of each section and lift sections off one at a
   time, keeping order. Pull out old thread fragments.

## 3. Repair the folds
Broken or weak folds won't hold thread: guard them with Japanese tissue strips pasted
over the fold on the outside of the section (see
repair-maintenance.book-repair-rebinding.page-paper-mending.tissue-tear-repair-and-tipping-in).
Dry under weight. Fold any new single leaves into folios with a guard.

## 4. Mark sewing stations
1. Knock up the sections square at the spine and head, clamp between boards, spine up.
2. Mark: **kettle stations** ~1–1.5 cm from head and tail; then pairs of marks for each
   tape (tape width apart), typically 2–3 tapes for a small book, evenly spaced.
3. Square the marks across all sections with a try square; pierce each section from the
   inside fold with an awl in a punching cradle.

## 5. Sew
- Thread: unbleached linen, thickness matched to paper and number of sections (e.g.
  18/3 for typical octavo text, thinner for many thin sections to avoid spine swell).
  Wax it lightly.
- Tapes (linen, ~10–12 mm) taut in a sewing frame or taped to the bench.
1. First section: out at the head kettle station, in and out passing **behind** each tape,
   out at the tail kettle station.
2. Second section on top: in at the tail station, sew back to the head, tie a square knot
   with the tail end of the first section.
3. Each later section: sew along, and at each end make a **kettle stitch** — pass the
   needle between the two sections below, back up through the loop, pull snug.
4. Keep tension even: snug, not tight (tight thread tears paper, loose gives a floppy
   block). Tap the sections down with a bone folder as you go to control swell.
5. Finish with a double kettle stitch on the last section.

## 6. Endpapers, glue-up and lining
1. Make endpapers (folded folio of good paper, grain parallel to the spine), tipped or
   sewn on as the first and last sections.
2. Knock up square; clamp; brush thinned PVA or paste/PVA into the spine, working it
   between sections; wipe excess.
3. Optional: **round and back** with a hammer and backing boards for a traditional
   rounded spine.
4. Line: mull extending ~3 cm onto each side, then a kraft paper lining. Tapes are left
   long to be adhered under the endpapers or into split boards when casing in.

## Gotchas
- Wrong grain on endpapers makes the boards bow.
- Spine swell from thread: too many thick sections → a fat spine that won't case in.
- Don't skip collation — a misbound section after resewing means doing it all again.

## Verify
- [ ] Collation note written and checked after sewing.
- [ ] Every section caught at both kettle stations; no loose loops.
- [ ] Text block square at head; spine swell within the old case width (if reused).
- [ ] Book opens flat across the middle of each section without the sewing gaping.
- [ ] Spine lined with mull and paper; tapes intact and ready for casing in.
