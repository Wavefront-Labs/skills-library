# Rebacking and hinge repair

**Goal:** a book whose boards are firmly reattached and which opens freely, keeping as
much of the original cover as possible. This is general book repair for ordinary and
collectible books; rare or valuable items belong with a conservator.

## Materials
Wheat starch paste (reversible, slower) and PVA (strong, fast, not reversible — often
mixed 50/50 with paste for longer working time); bookcloth or toned Japanese tissue;
mull/super; kraft paper for hollows; bone folder; thin palette knife and scalpel;
waxed paper or Hollytex as barrier; boards and weights or a press. **Grain direction of
every paper and cloth must run parallel to the spine** — test by gently bending both ways;
it bends more easily along the grain.

## 1. Assess
- Inner hinge cracked but outer joint sound → inner hinge repair (section 2).
- Outer cloth split at the joint, board loose or off → reback (section 3).
- Spine cover missing → reback with new cloth, no original to relay.
- Text block itself broken (loose signatures) → see resewing, not just the case.

## 2. Inner hinge repair (board attached but loose)
1. Lift the pastedown along the hinge edge ~1 cm with a palette knife.
2. Cut a strip of Japanese tissue or thin cloth ~2.5 cm wide, grain parallel to the spine.
3. Paste half under the lifted pastedown onto the board, half onto the first leaf of the
   text block, working it into the gutter with a bone folder with the board open at 90°.
4. Paste the pastedown back down. Interleave waxed paper, close, press under light weight
   for a few hours.

## 3. Rebacking a cloth case
1. **Lift the cloth** on each board 2–3 cm back from the joint with a thin knife, working
   from the spine edge toward the fore-edge; don't cut it off. Lift the turn-ins at head
   and tail too.
2. Remove the old spine piece (the "backstrip") and set it aside. Peel off loose old
   lining; scrape away flaky glue with a damp (not wet) paste poultice if needed.
3. **Re-line the spine** with a fresh layer of mull extending ~2 cm onto each side and a
   paper lining on top, if the old lining failed.
4. **Hollow tube:** make a kraft-paper tube the width of the spine, glue one face to the
   spine. This lets the book open while the new cover moves freely.
5. **New spine cloth:** cut cloth (colour-matched or toned) wide enough to slide under the
   lifted cloth on both boards by ~2 cm, long enough to turn in at head and tail. Add a
   card spine stiffener the width of the spine on the inside.
6. Glue the cloth under the lifted board cloth, glue it to the outside of the hollow, turn
   in at head and tail. Rub down; form the joint groove with a bone folder.
7. Glue the lifted board cloth back down over the new cloth.
8. **Relay the original backstrip:** pare/thin its back if necessary, adhere with paste or
   a paste-PVA mix onto the new spine.
9. Press between boards with waxed paper, using knitting needles or brass-edged boards
   in the joints to keep the groove; leave overnight.

## 4. Corners
Bumped corners: work paste into the delaminated board layers, shape with a bone folder,
press. Missing cloth on corners: inlay toned tissue or cloth, matching texture as best
you can.

## Leather (brief)
Red-rot (powdery) leather won't hold glue: consolidate first (e.g. Klucel G in alcohol)
and reback with leather or toned Japanese tissue. Treat leather work as a step up in
difficulty.

## Gotchas
- Grain wrong on cloth or tissue = cockling and warped boards.
- Too much water in paste on cloth makes tide lines; use thick paste and work fast.
- PVA on the original spine is irreversible — choose paste where reversibility matters.

## Verify
- [ ] Grain direction checked on every material before cutting.
- [ ] Board opens to 180° without the new joint lifting.
- [ ] Book opens freely; spine hollow flexes, text spine doesn't crack.
- [ ] Original backstrip relaid flat, edges stuck down.
- [ ] Boards flat after pressing overnight; no cockling.
