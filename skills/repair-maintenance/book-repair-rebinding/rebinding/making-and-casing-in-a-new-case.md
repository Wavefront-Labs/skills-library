# Making and casing in a new case

**Goal:** a new hardcover case that fits the text block with even squares, a joint that
hinges cleanly and boards that stay flat. Start from a sound, lined text block (see
repair-maintenance.book-repair-rebinding.resewing-text-blocks.taking-down-and-resewing-on-tapes).

## Materials
Binder's board / greyboard (2–2.5 mm for most books), thin card for the spine
stiffener, bookcloth (paper-backed for PVA) or leather/paper for quarter bindings,
PVA, waxed paper, pressing boards with brass or wooden edges, a press or heavy weights,
cutting mat, steel rule, sharp knife, bone folder. **Grain of boards, cloth and spine
stiffener parallel to the spine.**

## 1. Measure (write the numbers down)
- **Height of text block** (H), **width** from spine to fore-edge (W), **spine
  thickness** (S, measure at the shoulder including endpapers).
- **Squares** (how far boards overhang the text): ~3 mm for small books, up to 4–5 mm
  for large ones.
- **Boards:** height = H + 2 × square; width = W − ~3 mm (joint allowance) + square at the
  fore-edge. Rule of thumb for a flat spine: board width ≈ W.
- **Spine stiffener:** height = board height; width = S + 2 × board thickness for a flat
  back (or measure round a rounded spine with a strip of paper).
- **Joint gap** between board and stiffener: ~2 × board thickness plus a touch (about
  6–7 mm for 2 mm board) — too narrow and the book won't open; too wide and it looks loose.

## 2. Make the case
1. Cut boards and stiffener, checking square at the corners.
2. Cut cloth: width = 2 × board width + stiffener width + 2 × joint gap + 2 × turn-in
   (~15–18 mm each side); height = board height + 2 × turn-in.
3. Draw a guide on the back of the cloth. Glue one board with PVA, place on the line;
   lay a spacer of the joint-gap width, place the stiffener, another spacer, second board.
4. Rub down on the right side through waxed paper.
5. **Corners:** cut off each cloth corner at 45° leaving about 1.5 × board thickness from
   the board corner. Glue and turn in head and tail first, tucking the corner cloth in
   with a bone folder ("library corner" or the "nick" for neat corners), then the fore-edges.
6. **Quarter binding:** cover the spine and a strip of the boards in cloth/leather first,
   then paper sides overlapping it by 2–3 mm.
7. Dry under light weight between waxed paper.

## 3. Titling
Before casing in. Options: hot-foil stamping (type in a pallet or a blocking press at the
foil maker's temperature), a printed label inset into a shallow recess, or tooling for
leather. Practise on scrap of the same cloth — cloths take foil very differently.

## 4. Casing in
1. Place the text block in the case to check fit and squares.
2. With the case on the bench and the block positioned on one board, slip waxed paper
   under the endpaper. Glue the **mull and tapes** to the board first (if not already
   on), then glue the whole **pastedown** (endpaper) with PVA (or PVA/paste mix for slower
   set), working from the centre to the edges, wiping into the joint.
3. Close the board onto it, open immediately to check position and wrinkles, rub down
   the pastedown into the joint, close with fresh waxed paper inside.
4. Turn over, repeat the other side.
5. **Press:** between boards with the brass/edged boards in the joints so the joint groove
   sets, ~12–24 hours. Change the waxed paper after ~15 minutes to drop moisture.

## Gotchas
- Cloth grain across the spine = warped boards and a tight spine.
- Too much glue on the pastedown → cockling and damp warped boards. Thin, even coat.
- Measure the actual block after sewing; swell is always more than you expect.
- Boards warp toward the side with the last adhesive applied; line the inside of the
  boards with paper (balance) when there's a long wait before casing in.

## Verify
- [ ] Squares even all round (within ~0.5 mm).
- [ ] Boards open to 180° without stress at the joint; spine moves freely.
- [ ] Pastedowns flat, no bubbles or wrinkles, joint groove crisp.
- [ ] Boards flat after 24 h out of the press.
- [ ] Title straight and centred on the spine.
