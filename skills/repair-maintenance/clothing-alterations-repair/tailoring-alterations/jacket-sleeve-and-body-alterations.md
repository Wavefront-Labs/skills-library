# Jacket sleeve and body alterations

**Goal:** change a tailored jacket's fit without wrecking its structure — and know
before you start which jobs are cheap, which are expensive, and which are impossible.

## 1. Triage: what can be altered
| Alteration | Difficulty | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Sleeve length from cuff (non-working buttons) | Easy | 1–4 cm usual limit (hem allowance) |
| Sleeve length, working (surgeon) cuffs | Hard | Buttonholes are cut — shorten from **shoulder** instead |
| Take in centre back seam | Easy–medium | Up to ~2.5 cm total; vents complicate it |
| Take in side/side-body seams | Medium | Through lining too |
| Let out | Depends on inlay | Check allowance before quoting |
| Shoulder width, collar | Hard | Expensive; often not worth it on fused RTW |
| Jacket length | Rarely advisable | Pocket placement and balance break |

Rule of thumb many tailors use: **if the shoulders don't fit, the jacket doesn't fit** —
advise against buying/altering instead of quoting a shoulder rebuild.

## 2. Fitting the jacket
- Client in the shirt they'll wear, standing naturally, arms relaxed.
- Sleeve: about 1–1.5 cm of shirt cuff showing; pin a fold at the cuff.
- Body: pin out excess at the centre back seam first, then the side seams; check
  buttoned and with arms forward (hugging motion). Pulling X-creases around the
  button = too tight.
- Collar gap (collar standing away from shirt collar): a sign of posture mismatch or
  shoulder issue — note it, it's a collar-and-shoulder job, not a quick fix.
- Mark with chalk; record measurements on the ticket.

## 3. Shortening sleeves from the cuff
1. Open the lining at the cuff (or through the sleeve lining seam, about 10 cm up).
2. Unpick the sleeve hem and buttons; remove the old hem interfacing/canvas strip.
3. Mark the new fold line; preserve the vent's mitre if there is one — re-mitre at the
   new length, keeping a 3.5–4 cm hem.
4. Re-insert a bias strip of hem interfacing, catch-stitch the hem up.
5. Shorten the lining by the **same amount**, leaving ~1 cm of ease fold so the lining
   doesn't pull the sleeve up.
6. Re-sew buttons with a thread shank; press with a clapper.
Gotcha: the old fold line and fade often show on worn jackets when lengthening — warn
the client.

## 4. Shortening from the shoulder (working cuffs)
Remove the sleeve at the armhole, lower the sleeve cap by the required amount (re-draw
the crown curve, keeping the same ease), re-set with the same pitch, restore the sleeve
head roll and shoulder pad. Mark the sleeve pitch (balance marks) **before** removal.
Budget hours, not minutes — this is advanced work.

## 5. Taking in the body
- Centre back: open the lining at the hem, unpick vents if needed, sew the new line
  tapering into the original at the collar and hem. Take the lining in less (leave ease).
- Side seams / side bodies: through shell and lining, tapering over 10–15 cm.
- Do not pass through pocket bags; if the seam crosses a pocket, redistribute the
  reduction elsewhere.
- Canvassed jackets: the canvas floats in front; body seams can be taken in, but don't
  stretch the chest — press with steam and a damp cloth, never with dry high heat.
  Fused jackets can bubble (delaminate) under heavy steam: test first, lower heat.

## 6. Trouser recutting (related work)
Seat and waist take in via the centre back seam (usually has generous inlay); recut
legs by tapering the inseam and outseam equally so the crease stays centred.
Re-press the crease from the hem up. Relining a jacket: copy the old lining pieces
as patterns, add a centre-back pleat for movement.

## Gotchas
- Always check fabric reserve before promising a let-out.
- Keep and re-use original buttons; mismatched button sets are obvious.
- Patterned cloth (stripe, check): the taking-in must not break the match at
  the centre back.
- Draft note: limits above are common workroom figures; they vary by maker.

## Verify
- [ ] Sleeves equal length, shirt cuff showing the agreed amount both sides.
- [ ] No pulling at buttons; back sits clean with no vertical drag lines.
- [ ] Lining has ease — sleeve and hem not puckered by short lining.
- [ ] Vents hang closed and straight; no bubbling on fused fronts.
- [ ] Original buttons/hardware restored; inside ticket records what changed.
