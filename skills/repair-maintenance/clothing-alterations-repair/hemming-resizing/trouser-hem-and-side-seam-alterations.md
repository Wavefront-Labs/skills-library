# Hems and side-seam resizing — the everyday alterations

**Goal:** shorten a garment or change its width so it looks factory-made: level hem,
no puckering, no pulled grain, and enough allowance left that it can be undone.

## 1. Fit first, cut never (yet)
1. Fit with the **shoes the client will wear** and the belt/underlayer they'll use.
   A trouser hem is set by the shoe, not by the floor.
2. Trousers: fold the excess **to the inside** and pin at the front crease. Common
   breaks: no break (hem just touching shoe top), slight break (front dimples once),
   full break. Back is typically 0.5–1 cm longer than front on a straight leg — a
   slanted hem; skip the slant on narrow/tapered legs.
3. Skirts/dresses: measure up from the **floor** with a hem marker or ruler at 8–12
   points while the wearer stands still; never measure down from the waist (hips and
   posture make that crooked).
4. Side seams: pin out (take in) or mark release (let out) **equally on both sides**,
   with the garment right side out on the body. Check sitting down.
5. Mark with tailor's chalk or a wax that irons out; avoid gel pens on pale fabric.

## 2. Hemming methods
**Standard turned hem (trousers, skirts):**
- Allowance: trousers 4 cm, straight skirts 4–5 cm, full/circle skirts 1–1.5 cm
  (curves don't fold up flat — use a narrow double-turned or rolled hem).
- Cut at finished length + allowance. Finish the raw edge (overlock/serge, zigzag, or
  Hong Kong binding on unlined wool).
- Press the fold first, then stitch: **blind hem** by machine (blind-hem foot; the
  zigzag "bite" should just catch 1–2 threads of the outer fabric) or by hand with a
  catch/blind stitch from left to right, picking one thread. Keep stitches loose —
  tight hand stitches show as dimples.

**Jeans original hem (euro hem):** keeps the factory worn hem.
1. Measure the amount to remove (e.g. 5 cm). Fold the leg **up by half** (2.5 cm) so
   the original hem sits that far above the fold; pin all round.
2. Stitch right next to the edge of the original hem band with a zipper foot,
   denim needle (100/16) and matching heavy thread.
3. Trim the excess fold to ~1 cm, overlock, press the original hem down, topstitch in
   the ditch at the side seams only (optional) to keep it flipped down.
Gotcha: on very tapered legs the inner circumference is smaller than the hem — it
won't sit flat beyond ~4 cm removal; do a conventional hem instead.

**Bulky seams:** hammer or press the flat-felled side seams of jeans before sewing
across them, and use a "hump jumper" or folded card under the foot to keep it level.

## 3. Taking in / letting out
- Unpick the waistband 5–8 cm either side of the seam if the change reaches the waist.
- Restitch the new line, **blending smoothly** back into the original seam over at
  least 10–15 cm — an abrupt angle makes a visible dent.
- Taking in: trim only once fitted; leave at least 1.5 cm allowance in wovens.
- Letting out: check the existing allowance (often 1–2.5 cm in the centre back seam
  of trousers, much less on side seams). Old stitch lines and fade marks may show —
  press with steam and a press cloth, test on the inside; denim fade lines will not
  disappear.
- Knits: use a stretch/narrow zigzag stitch or overlocker; a straight stitch will pop.
- Sleeves: shorten from the cuff on shirts (move the cuff and placket) — on jackets
  see tailoring-alterations; lined sleeves need the lining shortened by the same amount.

## 4. Press, don't iron
Press each seam flat as sewn, then open; use a clapper on wool for a crisp hem. Too
hot on synthetics leaves shine — press cloth, lower heat.

## Gotchas
- Fabric with a pattern (checks, stripes) must be matched at the side seam after
  taking in — pin at every stripe.
- Pre-shrink? You can't, but ask: an unwashed raw-denim hem may shrink ~3–5%.
- Never cut a client's garment before a second check of the pins on the body.
- (Draft note: allowance figures are typical workroom practice, not a standard.)

## Verify
- [ ] Hem is level when worn (check in a mirror or from the floor up), both legs equal.
- [ ] No stitches visible on the right side of a blind hem; no puckering at seams.
- [ ] New seam blends into the original without a dent or angle.
- [ ] Remaining allowance is enough to let out again later.
- [ ] Garment fits sitting and standing in the intended shoes.
