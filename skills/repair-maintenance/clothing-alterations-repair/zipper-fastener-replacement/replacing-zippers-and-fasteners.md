# Replacing zippers and fasteners

**Goal:** a closure that runs smoothly and holds under load, chosen so the repair is
the cheapest one that actually lasts.

## 1. Diagnose before you unpick
- **Teeth separate behind the slider:** worn slider (jaws spread). Often fixable by
  **replacing the slider** only — or, as a temporary fix, gently squeezing the slider
  jaws with pliers (cracks zinc sliders; last resort).
- **Missing/broken teeth, torn tape:** replace the zipper.
- **Slider came off one side (separating zip):** check the box/pin at the bottom;
  a damaged insertion pin means a new zipper.
- **Stiff zipper:** clean, then rub with graphite pencil, wax or zipper lubricant.

## 2. Identify the zipper
- **Type:** coil (nylon spiral), moulded plastic (Vislon-style), metal teeth; closed-end,
  separating (open-end), two-way.
- **Gauge:** measure closed teeth width in mm — that's roughly the size number
  (#3 ≈ 3 mm skirts/invisible, #5 jeans/jackets, #8–#10 heavy outerwear). Sliders are
  size- and type-specific; a #5 coil slider won't run on #5 metal. Brands mark size on
  the slider back.
- **Length:** measure the teeth, stop to stop. Coil zippers can be shortened easily;
  metal needs teeth removed with end-nippers and new stops crimped on.

## 3. Slider replacement
1. Remove the top stops (pry with pliers or cut the stitching at the top for coil).
2. Slide the old slider off; feed the new one on **both tapes evenly**, pull side up.
3. Run it down and up a few times; crimp new top stops ~3 mm above the last teeth.

## 4. Jeans fly zipper
1. Note topstitching path (photograph). Unpick the waistband 3 cm across the fly
   front, the bar tacks and the fly topstitching.
2. Remove the zipper; insert the new one (metal #5 typical, slightly longer than
   needed). Stitch the left tape to the fly facing first with a zipper foot, then
   the right tape to the fly shield.
3. Re-topstitch on the old holes with jeans thread (Tex 70ish) and a 100/16 needle,
   reinstate the bar tack.
4. Cut excess teeth above the waistband line after removing teeth there (end
   nippers), re-close the waistband.

## 5. Invisible (concealed) zipper
- Needs an **invisible zipper foot**. Unroll the coil with a cool iron before sewing.
- Sew each tape with the foot riding in the coil groove, to ~2 cm from the slider
  at the bottom; then close the seam below with a regular zipper foot, slightly
  outside the zipper stitching.
- Test that the pull hides when closed; if the coil shows, stitch closer.

## 6. Separating jacket zippers
- Match length exactly (the box and pin sit at the hem). Usually replacing the
  whole zipper: open the facing or lining, unpick from inside, baste the new one in
  first and check both sides align at the hem and collar before final stitching.
- Down jackets: sew through the existing stitch line to avoid new feather leaks.

## 7. Buttons, snaps and hooks
- **Sew-through buttons:** double thread, sew over a toothpick to create slack, then
  wind the thread round the stitches to form a shank (2–3 mm on coats).
- **Jeans tack buttons:** need a new tack button + hammer/press; reuse the original
  hole. Don't use a sew-on button there.
- **Prong snaps:** use the correct setting die; prongs that bend outward mean
  fabric too thick — add an interfacing layer or use a longer-prong snap.
- **Trouser hooks:** hook goes on the overlap, bar on the underlap; reinforce with
  a patch of interfacing behind.

## Gotchas
- Cheap generic sliders on brand zippers often run but skip — buy matched.
- Never shorten a separating zipper from the bottom.
- Metal zipper and a domestic machine: hand-walk over the teeth, or the needle breaks.
- Draft note: gauge ≈ mm rule works for most brands but isn't universal — check the slider.

## Verify
- [ ] Zipper runs full length smoothly 10 times without catching fabric.
- [ ] Teeth stay closed when the garment is flexed (sit-test for jeans).
- [ ] Topstitching follows the original line; bar tacks present.
- [ ] Invisible zipper shows no coil or tape from the outside.
- [ ] Buttons have a shank and don't pull the fabric when fastened.
