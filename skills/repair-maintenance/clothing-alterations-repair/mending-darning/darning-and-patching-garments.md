# Darning and patching garments

**Goal:** stop damage spreading and restore strength, choosing between a repair that
disappears and one that is deliberately visible.

## 1. Diagnose the damage
| Damage | Method |
|---|---|
| Split seam (fabric intact) | Restitch seam; ladder stitch from outside if inaccessible |
| Small hole in knit (moth, wear) | Woven darn or Swiss darning (duplicate stitch) |
| Thin/worn area in knit (heel, elbow) | Swiss darning over the weak stitches before it holes |
| Tear in woven fabric (L-shaped, straight) | Fusible underlay + machine darning, or hand darn |
| Worn-through denim (crotch, knee) | Underpatch + dense machine stitching |
| Hole you want to show off | Visible mend: sashiko, contrast darn, decorative patch |

First: **stabilise**. Catch any running ladders in knits with a safety pin or a thread
through the live loops; trim fluff, not fabric.

## 2. Woven darn (knits and wovens, by hand)
1. Put a darning mushroom/egg (or a light bulb, a jar) under the hole — fabric taut,
   not stretched.
2. Use yarn matching weight and fibre (wool on wool). A blunt darning needle on knits.
3. Outline: start ~1 cm outside the hole in sound fabric.
4. **Warp:** run parallel vertical stitches across the hole, picking up fabric on each
   side, rows close together, leaving small loops at turn-backs (allows shrink and stretch).
5. **Weft:** weave horizontally over-under through the warp threads, alternating
   each row, pushing rows together with the needle.
6. Weave tails into the back for 2–3 cm; don't knot.

## 3. Swiss darning (duplicate stitch)
Retrace the path of the knitted V stitches with new yarn, covering weak areas or
rebuilding a small hole row by row (anchor live loops at the top and bottom first).
Stitch tension must match the original or you get a tight puckered patch. Invisible
when the yarn colour and weight match well.

## 4. Woven tears and denim
- **L-tear/straight tear:** on the inside, iron on a lightweight fusible interfacing
  cut 1–2 cm bigger than the damage. Bring the torn edges together on top. Machine
  darn: short straight stitch (1.5 mm) back and forth across the tear, or a narrow
  zigzag, overlapping onto sound fabric.
- **Jeans crotch/thigh:** pin a denim patch (cut from a hem offcut or similar weight)
  behind the worn area, with 2 cm margin. From the right side, stitch dense rows in
  the direction of the denim twill with thread matched to the dominant colour (often
  two shades). Free-motion with feed dogs down, or forward/reverse in rows 2–3 mm
  apart. Catch the patch edges with a zigzag. Repair *before* it fully holes; a thin
  area is much easier than a hole.

## 5. Visible mending
- **Sashiko-style:** patch behind or on top, then running stitches in thick cotton
  thread (sashiko thread or perle 8) in a grid or traditional pattern. Stitches ~3–5 mm,
  even; leave slight slack on the back so the fabric still drapes.
- **Contrast darns** on knits: choose a deliberate colour; the technique is the woven
  darn above.
- Patches on top: turn under edges 6–8 mm or use felt/boiled wool that doesn't fray.

## 6. Seams
Machine: restitch 1–2 cm past both ends of the split, on the original line.
From the outside (lined garments, stuffed items): ladder (slip) stitch — alternate
small stitches along each folded edge, then pull to close invisibly.

## Gotchas
- Synthetic darning yarn on wool shrinks differently — mismatched fibres pucker
  after washing.
- Over-tight darns cut into the surrounding fabric and make a new hole at the edge.
- Moth damage means more moths: inspect the whole garment, freeze it (sealed bag,
  about a week) or wash per care label, and check the wardrobe.
- Invisible mending (re-weaving with threads harvested from a seam) is a specialist
  service; quote it separately. (Draft: freezing duration is common advice, not tested here.)

## Verify
- [ ] Repair extends into sound fabric on all sides.
- [ ] Garment drapes and stretches normally across the mend (knit still stretches).
- [ ] No knots or loose tails on the inside; ends woven in.
- [ ] After one wash, the area hasn't puckered or pulled away.
- [ ] Client agreed on visible vs invisible beforehand.
