# Restyling and converting existing garments

**Goal:** turn a dated, ill-fitting or damaged garment into something wearable that
looks designed, not "made from" — with seams, grain and finish to the standard of
new work. Plain resizing is in
`repair-maintenance.clothing-alterations-repair.hemming-resizing.trouser-hem-and-side-seam-alterations`;
tailored jackets in
`repair-maintenance.clothing-alterations-repair.tailoring-alterations.jacket-sleeve-and-body-alterations`.

## 1. Assess the donor garment
- **Fabric:** fibre (burn test or label), weight, stretch %, wear (thin spots at knees,
  seat, elbows, collars). Plan to cut *around* worn areas.
- **Usable area:** lay flat and measure the largest clear rectangles once seams, darts
  and pockets are removed. A men's XL shirt yields roughly 1.2–1.5 m² of usable cloth;
  a pair of adult jeans ~0.6–0.8 m² in leg panels.
- **Keepers:** factory hems, plackets, pockets, waistbands, buttonholes and selvedges
  are hard to reproduce — design around reusing them.
- **Wash and press first** — upcycled garments should shrink before cutting, not after.

## 2. Plan before cutting
1. Sketch the target; decide what stays (e.g. waistband, fly) and what is new.
2. Try the donor on the wearer inside out and pin the new shape directly, or trace a
   pattern from a well-fitting garment the wearer already owns.
3. Mark the **grainline** on each piece; a panel cut off grain twists after washing.
4. If combining garments, check fibre and care compatibility (don't join pre-washed
   cotton to unwashed linen or wool to a hot-wash fabric).

## 3. Common conversions
**Jeans to skirt:**
1. Unpick the inside leg seams and the crotch curve up to the fly front and to ~5 cm
   below the back yoke. Leave the fly intact.
2. Lay flat; the front and back crotch curves overlap — fold the front over flat and
   trim, then topstitch along the old line. Same at the back.
3. Fill the resulting triangular gaps front and back with inserts cut from the leg
   offcuts (match wash direction), sewn behind the opening with a double topstitch in
   jeans thread (Tex 70+, denim needle 100/16).
4. Mark the hem level from the floor, cut, then fray, double-turn hem or reuse the
   original hem band.

**Cropping and reshaping (tops, jackets, dresses):**
- Crop with 1.5–2 cm extra for a hem or a band; on jackets the lining is shortened
  ~1 cm more than the shell and slip-stitched with an ease pleat.
- Remove shoulder pads first, then re-check shoulder width; dated wide shoulders can be
  narrowed only by re-setting the sleeve (see the tailoring skill).
- Boxy to fitted: add bust/back darts or taper the side seams; tapering from an XL to
  a medium often needs the armhole raised too — check range of movement.

**Combining garments:** cut donor panels as rectangles, assemble into "new fabric"
(patchwork with 1 cm seams, overlocked, pressed one way), then cut the pattern from it.
Balance weights: heavy denim panels on a light shirt body drag.

## 4. Construction standards
- Seam allowances 1–1.5 cm; finish all raw edges (overlock, French seams on light
  fabrics, flat-felled on denim and workwear).
- Topstitch to match the donor (thread colour and stitch length 3–3.5 mm on denim).
- Press each seam as sewn.

## Gotchas
- Old stitch lines and fade marks show on denim, corduroy and velvet — place them in
  seams or make them a feature.
- Unpicking stretch denim can distort it; cut seams off instead when allowances aren't
  needed.
- Vintage garments may have deteriorated fibres (silk shatter, perished elastic):
  tug-test before committing time.
- Brand logos/labels on resale pieces: don't present reworked items as the original
  brand's product.
