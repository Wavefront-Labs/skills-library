# Tinplate and clockwork toy restoration

**Goal:** make a vintage toy displayable or working without destroying the thing that
makes it valuable: original lithography/paint and original parts. For valuable toys, a
cleaned original in "played-with" condition usually beats a repainted one — sometimes
by an order of magnitude.

## 1. Decide before you touch it
Ask, and write down the answers:
- **What is it worth as-is vs restored?** Check sold prices (auction results) for the
  same model in comparable conditions. Repainting a rare German tinplate toy (e.g.
  Märklin, Lehmann, Bing) commonly destroys most of its value.
- **Is the goal working, displayable, or conserved?**
- **Original vs replacement parts:** reproduction parts exist for popular trains and toys;
  mark or record them so the toy isn't later sold as all-original.
Default: **clean, stabilise, repair mechanism, minimal reversible retouch.**

## 2. Disassembly (tinplate)
- Tinplate toys are held by **tabs** folded through slots. Each tab tolerates very few
  bends before it fatigues and snaps. Straighten only as far as needed, using flat-nose
  pliers with tape over the jaws, or a thin flat blade; bend back the same way.
- Photograph tab positions and orientation. Some tabs are twisted rather than folded —
  untwist the same amount.
- If a tab breaks: a reversible fix is a small bracket glued (reversible adhesive, e.g. an
  acrylic resin like Paraloid B-72 for light duty) inside, not solder that heats the litho.

## 3. Cleaning
1. Dry first: soft brush and low-pressure air.
2. Test any wet method on a hidden area. Start with distilled water and a drop of
   neutral detergent on a barely damp cotton swab; roll the swab, don't scrub.
3. **Never** use abrasive polish, solvents like acetone/lacquer thinner, or wire brushes on
   lithographed surfaces — litho inks and the clear coat can lift instantly.
4. Rust on bare metal (undersides, axles): mechanical removal with a fibreglass pencil or
   brass brush on the rust only, then a thin coat of microcrystalline wax
   (e.g. Renaissance Wax) to stabilise. Rust *bloom* under litho is generally left alone.
5. Cast-iron toys: soft brushing, then wax. Avoid electrolysis/acid baths on painted
   originals — they strip paint too.

## 4. Clockwork motor
1. **Let the spring down** — hold the key, release the ratchet click with a fine screwdriver
   and let it unwind under control. Never unwind by disengaging gears.
2. Common faults: broken mainspring (often at the inner hook), stripped key square,
   worn ratchet, gummed oil.
3. Clean gears and plates in naphtha (motor removed from the toy body), dry, and oil
   pivots with a tiny amount of clock oil. Light grease on the spring.
4. Broken spring: replacement springs are sold by width, thickness and length; re-forming a
   hook on a hardened spring needs heat on the end only (annealing) — it is easy to
   ruin the rest of the spring, so buying a correctly sized replacement is safer.
5. Refit with the original tabs/screws; test run gently before full winds.

## 5. Retouching (if the owner wants it)
- Use reversible media: gouache or conservation-grade acrylics, applied with a fine
  brush only *inside* losses (inpainting), never over original paint.
- Match colour in daylight; build up in thin layers slightly lighter than target.
- Seal (if at all) with a reversible varnish; record it.
- Full repaints: disclose and record permanently ("restored"/"repainted" label inside
  is good practice).

## Gotchas
- Model train restorers: track voltage for old tinplate trains (e.g. some early electric
  sets) can be unusual; check before powering.
- Celluloid parts degrade, off-gas and corrode adjacent metal; isolate them.
- Uncertain: value effects are market-dependent and approximate; get a specialist
  appraisal for anything rare.

## Verify
- [ ] Originality/value decision recorded before any intervention.
- [ ] Every tab bent once at most; none cracked; positions photographed.
- [ ] Cleaning tested in a hidden spot; no litho loss or haze.
- [ ] Spring let down before motor removal; motor runs smoothly after service.
- [ ] Any retouch is reversible, confined to losses and documented.
