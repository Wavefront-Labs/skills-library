# Bonding, filling and retouching broken ceramics

**Goal:** reassemble a broken ceramic or glass object strongly and accurately, fill
missing areas, and make the repair either invisible (decorative/trade restoration) or
deliberately discernible (museum practice) — by choice, not by accident.

## 1. Decide the repair style
- **Museum/conservation style:** reversible adhesives, fills toned slightly lighter or
  plain, no overpaint onto original glaze. Honest; preserves value for serious pieces.
- **Invisible/trade restoration:** fills and overpaint (often airbrushed) extend onto the
  glaze around the break to hide it. Looks perfect, but must be disclosed at sale, and
  overpaint yellows over time.
- Japanese **kintsugi** (lacquer and gold) is a third, decorative option.

## 2. Assess and dismantle old repairs
- UV torch in a dark room: old overpaint and many adhesives fluoresce differently from glaze.
- Old adhesives: animal glue (warm water), shellac (ethanol), epoxy (softens with
  dichloromethane-based strippers or prolonged acetone poultice — ventilation, gloves).
- **Metal staples/rivets** (18th–19th c.) are historically interesting — think before
  removing; drilling holes for them were done by "china menders" and are part of the history.
- Never soak porous earthenware with stains in plain water first: it can draw dirt deeper.

## 3. Clean the break edges
- Edges must be spotless or joins won't close (the "step" problem).
- Remove old adhesive residues mechanically (scalpel, held low) and with solvent.
- Stained cracks in porous bodies: poultice with hydrogen peroxide (e.g. ~6–12 %
  solution with a thickener) under cling film, after pre-wetting with distilled water;
  rinse thoroughly. Do not use on lustre, gilding, or overglaze enamels; bleach
  (sodium hypochlorite) can leave salts that later spall the glaze — avoid it.

## 4. Bonding
- **Dry run first:** tape the pieces together with low-tack tape to plan the order —
  bonding one shard out of order can lock out the last piece.
- Adhesive choice:
  - **Paraloid B-72** (acrylic resin in acetone, ~50 % w/v, "conservator's glue"):
    reversible with acetone, good for earthenware; slight creep under load.
  - **Epoxy** (e.g. Araldite 2020 or HXTAL NYL-1 for glass — water-clear, low
    yellowing): strong, for porcelain and stressed joins; hard to reverse.
  - **UV-curing glass adhesive** for clear glass edge joins; cures in sunlight/UV lamp.
- Apply a thin film to one edge; press together; check alignment with a fingernail
  across the join — any step must be corrected before set.
- Support with tape, a sand tray, or plasticine during cure. Remove squeeze-out with a
  solvent-damp swab or once rubbery with a scalpel.

## 5. Filling losses
- Earthenware: fine surface filler (e.g. a gypsum/vinyl filler) or tinted B-72-bulked
  fills for reversibility.
- Porcelain: epoxy putty (e.g. Milliput superfine white) or epoxy resin bulked with
  fumed silica and pigments.
- Take a dental-wax or silicone mould from an intact matching area for missing rims,
  handles, petals; press or cast the fill.
- Sand flush with fine abrasive (wet 600–1200 grit, micromesh) protecting surrounding glaze
  with tape.

## 6. Retouching
- Museum: tone the fill to blend at arm's length but recognisable close up.
- Invisible: acrylic or specialist restoration glazes applied by brush/airbrush, feathered
  onto glaze; finish with a clear glaze medium matched to gloss.
- Match colour in daylight; build in thin layers.

## Gotchas
- Superglue (cyanoacrylate) is a poor long-term ceramic adhesive and yellows/embrittles;
  it's sometimes used only to tack-locate pieces before B-72 or epoxy.
- Repaired pieces are not dishwasher, oven or food-safe; tell the owner.
- Uncertain: solvent and peroxide strengths are typical conservator ranges; test first.

## Verify
- [ ] Repair style (museum/invisible) agreed with the owner and recorded.
- [ ] Old repairs identified (UV) and removed or deliberately retained.
- [ ] No steps at joins (fingernail test), adhesive appropriate and reversible where possible.
- [ ] Fills flush, shaped from a mould where needed, retouch confined as agreed.
- [ ] Owner told: not food/dishwasher safe; overpaint disclosed.
