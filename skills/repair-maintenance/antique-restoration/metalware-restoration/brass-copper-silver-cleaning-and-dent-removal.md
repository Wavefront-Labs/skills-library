# Brass, copper and silver: cleaning and dent removal

**Goal:** improve appearance and stability of antique metalware while keeping its
surface, crispness of detail and marks. Every polish removes metal; plated wares can be
polished through to base metal in one careless session.

## 1. Identify before cleaning
- **Solid silver:** hallmarks (e.g. lion passant for British sterling), heavy, no copper
  showing at worn edges.
- **Silver plate:** marks like EPNS, EP, "A1", maker's pseudo-marks; worn high points show
  yellow (brass/nickel silver) or pink (copper, often Sheffield plate — highly
  collectable, never re-plate without thought).
- **Brass vs bronze vs copper:** colour and a magnet (none are magnetic; magnetic = steel
  or iron core/plated). Lacquered brass looks evenly bright and resists tarnish — check
  for old lacquer first; polish won't cut through it and it needs removing or leaving.
- **Gilded/ormolu:** never polish; specialist only.

## 2. Gentlest-first cleaning ladder
1. Dust; wash in warm water with a few drops of neutral detergent, soft brush into
   recesses, rinse, **dry immediately** (warm air or lint-free cloth).
2. Remove old lacquer if failing (patchy tarnish): acetone on a swab, ventilated area.
3. **Silver:** a mild polish of precipitated calcium carbonate (whiting/precipitated chalk)
   slurried with water or ethanol on cotton wool, or a reputable long-term silver polish.
   Rub in straight lines, not circles. Avoid **dips** (acid thiourea): they work fast
   but strip patina, leave a dead white look and attack niello/stones; if ever used,
   swab briefly and rinse thoroughly.
   - Electrolytic method (aluminium foil + baking soda + hot water) removes sulfide
     tarnish without abrasion but can over-brighten recesses that should stay dark.
4. **Brass/copper:** mild commercial metal cream sparingly, or a paste of whiting.
   Avoid acid home remedies (vinegar/salt, ketchup) on valuable pieces: they etch and
   leave residues that re-tarnish fast (chlorides).
5. **Verdigris** (green corrosion) on copper alloys: remove loose deposits mechanically
   with wooden or bone tools. Powdery bright-green spots that keep returning may be
   **bronze disease** (chloride corrosion) — specialist conservation, not polish.
6. Leave dark tone in recesses for depth unless the owner insists; this is "patina".

## 3. Dent removal (sheet metal ware: jugs, kettles, trays)
- Thin silver and plate is easily stretched; copper and brass are more forgiving.
- Work from the inside out with smooth **wooden or nylon dollies/stakes** shaped to the
  curve, supporting the outside on a sandbag or leather pad.
- Tap many light blows working from the edge of the dent toward the centre; check against
  a template or the opposite side.
- Finish with a polished steel planishing hammer on a matching stake only if the surface
  needs it — every blow work-hardens; annealing (heat to dull red, quench) may be
  needed for copper, but **never heat soldered, plated or loaded (filled) pieces** —
  silver solder flows, plating blisters, and weighted candlesticks contain pitch/plaster.
- Seams and handles: soft-soldered repairs on silver devalue it; hard-solder (silver
  solder) work belongs to a silversmith.

## 4. Protection
- Display brass/copper: lacquer with a conservation lacquer (e.g. Incralac, an acrylic
  with benzotriazole) sprayed thin and even after a final acetone wipe with gloves on —
  fingerprints under lacquer etch permanently.
- Handled items: microcrystalline wax rather than lacquer.
- Store silver in anti-tarnish cloth/bags; keep away from rubber bands, wool and oak
  (sulfur and acids).

## 5. Re-plating decision
Re-plating destroys original plating and maker's surface; reasonable for worn
everyday EPNS cutlery, usually wrong for Sheffield plate or named makers. Tell the
owner the value trade-off first.

## Gotchas
- Polishing over hallmarks erases them — mask or avoid them.
- Brass cleaners containing ammonia can cause stress-corrosion cracking in old brass.
- Uncertain: product names are examples; formulations change — test on a hidden area.

## Verify
- [ ] Metal and plating identified; gilded/ormolu items excluded.
- [ ] Cleaning stopped at the gentlest effective step; hallmarks and detail intact.
- [ ] No base metal exposed on plated items; recesses keep appropriate tone.
- [ ] Dents raised without new stretch marks or heat on soldered/loaded pieces.
- [ ] Protective lacquer or wax applied with gloved hands; care advice given.
