# Rewiring antique lamps safely

**Goal:** make an old lamp or light fitting safe to use on mains power while keeping its
appearance. Most antique lamps arrive with perished rubber or brittle cotton-covered flex
and no earth — a shock and fire hazard. Local rules differ: in the UK and EU, follow
BS EN 60598 practice; in North America, UL 153 and polarised plugs. Fixed ceiling
fittings (chandeliers) connect to building wiring and may legally require an electrician
for installation even if you rewire the fitting itself. Note: mains electricity can kill.

## 1. Survey
- Photograph the lamp and the wire routing. Note any **gas or oil heritage**: gasoliers
  (gas chandeliers) have pipes that may still have been connected to gas; confirm caps and
  that no gas supply remains before drilling or heat.
- Identify the body: metal (brass, spelter, bronze) vs non-conductive (ceramic, glass,
  wood). **Metal bodies must be earthed** (Class I) unless built to double insulation
  (Class II) — almost never true of antiques.
- Check the lampholder: old brass holders often have perished internal insulation;
  replacing them is usually wiser than reusing.

## 2. Parts list (match to region)
- Flex: 3-core (live, neutral, earth) for metal lamps, typically 0.75 mm² (UK/EU) or
  18 AWG SPT-2/SVT (US) heat-resistant where it passes near the bulb.
  Fabric-braided cable is fine *if* it is a modern certified cable with a fabric outer
  braid over PVC/rubber insulation.
- Lampholder: E27/E14 Edison screw (EU), E26/E12 (US) or B22 bayonet (UK) — rated for
  the bulb wattage, with an **earth terminal** for metal lamps; shade ring threads match
  the original if you want to keep the shade carrier.
- Plug: fused UK plug (3 A fuse for a lamp), polarised US plug, or Schuko as appropriate.
- Cord grip/strain relief bush where flex enters the base; grommet or bush wherever cable
  passes a metal edge.
- Inline switch or switched lampholder as the original had.

## 3. Rewire
1. Unplug, disassemble from the top: shade carrier, lampholder, then unscrew the
   threaded rod/nut at the base. Pull a draw-string through with the old cable as you
   remove it — this makes threading the new cable through a column easy.
2. Inspect the central threaded tube (often 1/8 IPS or 10 mm) for sharp edges; deburr
   with a round file or fit a bushing.
3. Thread the new flex; tie an **underwriter's knot** (US) or use the holder's cord grip
   inside the lampholder so pull on the cable doesn't load terminals.
4. Strip ~8–10 mm per core; twist strands; no stray strands outside terminals. Do not
   tin flex ends with solder under screw terminals (solder creeps and loosens).
5. Connect **live** (brown/ black US hot) to the centre contact of an Edison screw holder,
   **neutral** (blue / white US) to the screw shell — this keeps the exposed thread at
   neutral. Earth (green/yellow) to the holder's earth terminal and/or a body earth tag
   (ring tag under a nut, star washer biting bare metal).
6. Fit the strain-relief grip at the base; rebuild the lamp.

## 4. Oil lamp conversions
- Use a converter burner that fits the original collar thread (common duplex sizes), so
  the conversion is **reversible** — never drill the font of a valuable lamp if avoidable.
- Run the cable through the font via the converter or a small side bushing at the base
  rather than through glass where possible.

## 5. Test before handing over
- **Earth continuity** from plug earth pin to every exposed metal part: low reading, e.g.
  under ~0.1 Ω + cable resistance (PAT guidance figure).
- **Insulation resistance** at 500 V DC between live+neutral and earth: commonly ≥ 1 MΩ
  to pass (PAT); typically far higher on new cable.
- Polarity check: live to centre contact.
- Bulb: LED or the lamp's rated maximum wattage; heat-damaged shades → use LED.

## Gotchas
- Original cloth flex may be asbestos-insulated in some old heaters/fittings — if in doubt,
  treat as hazardous.
- Spelter (zinc alloy) bodies crack easily when nuts are overtightened.
- Uncertain: test limits quoted are UK PAT-style guidance; your jurisdiction's limits apply.

## Verify
- [ ] Metal body earthed (or lamp confirmed non-conductive) and earth continuity tested.
- [ ] Live to centre contact; neutral on shell; insulation resistance passes.
- [ ] Strain relief at base and lampholder; no cable over sharp metal edges.
- [ ] Lampholder and bulb rating appropriate; plug/fuse correct for region.
- [ ] Gas/oil heritage checked; conversion reversible where possible.
