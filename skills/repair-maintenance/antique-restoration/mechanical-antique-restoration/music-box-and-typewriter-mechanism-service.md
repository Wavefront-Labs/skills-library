# Music box and typewriter mechanism service

**Goal:** get a stiff, silent or erratic antique mechanism working smoothly again while
keeping it original and reversible. Most "broken" antique mechanisms are just old,
hardened oil and dirt; genuine breakage is the minority. Work slowly and record everything.

## 0. Rules for every mechanism
- **Photograph every stage** of disassembly from two angles; bag and label parts by
  sub-assembly. You will not remember the order of 40 screws a month from now.
- **Springs store energy.** Never remove a mainspring barrel, governor or click while
  the spring is wound. Let it down first.
- Use correctly fitting, hollow-ground screwdrivers; a chewed antique screw slot is
  permanent damage.
- No WD-40 or general household oil. It gums. Use clock oil (e.g. Moebius 8030 or
  similar) on pivots and light grease on gear teeth only where the maker used it.

## 1. Cylinder music box
1. **Let the spring down:** hold the winding lever/key, release the click (pawl) with a
   pointed tool while letting the key turn slowly under control, a few clicks at a time.
   A **let-down key** (key with a large wooden handle) makes this safe. A runaway spring
   (governor failure) can strip the cylinder pins and comb teeth in seconds — if the
   box starts to race, stop it at the governor fan with a finger or soft wood.
2. Remove the comb only after marking its exact position (scribe a line on the bedplate
   at the comb base, or note shim positions). The comb-to-cylinder alignment sets which
   pins hit which teeth; it is the hardest thing to get back.
3. Clean the cylinder pins with a soft brush; never scrape. Bent pins can be straightened
   carefully with a slotted tool; many specialists leave this to professionals.
4. Clean the comb with a soft brush and naphtha; inspect for broken tips (tip
   replacement is specialist work) and missing/damaged **dampers** (thin wire/quill
   springs under the bass teeth that stop buzzing). Buzzing notes = damper work.
5. Clean and oil the governor (endless screw and fan): one tiny drop at each pivot. A
   dry or dirty governor causes speed hunting.
6. Reassemble, align the comb to the marks, and test with small spring tension first.

## 2. Manual typewriter (1920s–1960s desktop/portable)
1. Remove the platen, paper table, and body panels (photograph screw locations).
2. Degrease the segment (slotted arc where typebars pivot) with naphtha, working the
   keys to flush it — sticky typebars almost always mean gummy segment slots.
   Do not soak painted or decal surfaces; naphtha is generally safe on old shellac-free
   enamel but test in a hidden spot. Avoid acetone and alcohol on decals.
3. Clean the escapement and carriage rails; the carriage should glide freely when the
   escapement is held off. Oil sparingly on rails and escapement pivots only; **never oil
   the segment** (attracts dust and re-sticks).
4. Check the drawband (carriage pull cord); replace with braided fishing line or
   purpose-made cord, matching the mainspring tension to give a positive but not
   violent return.
5. Platen and feed rollers often harden; rubber re-covering is a specialist service.
   Grinding or slipping paper indicates this.

## 3. Gramophone spring motor
- Same let-down discipline; most have a barrel with a large, dangerous spring.
- Old grease in the barrel turns to tar and causes "thumping" (spring releasing in jerks).
  Opening a barrel and re-greasing (graphite-loaded grease is traditional) needs a
  mainspring winder; do not hand-unwind large springs.
- Adjust the governor friction pad so it holds 78 rpm with a strobe disc.

## Gotchas
- "Restoration" that replaces original parts can reduce collector value; keep removed
  parts with the object and record changes.
- Ultrasonic cleaning is fine for brass/steel parts, but not for painted, lacquered,
  pinned or glued components.
- Uncertain: this is a general practitioner synthesis; specific makes (Regina, Polyphon,
  Royal, Underwood, Victor) have quirks — consult a make-specific manual before deep work.

## Verify
- [ ] Spring(s) fully let down before any disassembly; photos of every stage exist.
- [ ] Music box comb returned to its marked position; no buzzing or skipped notes.
- [ ] Typebars return freely without oil in the segment; carriage advances on every key.
- [ ] Governor runs steady speed; only pivots lightly oiled with clock oil.
- [ ] Original parts retained and changes noted for the owner.
