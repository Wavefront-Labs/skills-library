# Tubeless setup and puncture repair

**Goal:** tyres that seat, hold air for days and self-seal small punctures — and a
reliable repair when they don't. Most tubeless failures are tape, valve or dried sealant.

## 1. Compatibility first
- Read the **ETRTO** numbers on tyre and rim (e.g. tyre 29-622, rim inner width 25 mm).
  622 = 700c/29er, 584 = 650b/27.5, 559 = 26 in. Bead seat diameters must match exactly.
- Both rim and tyre must be tubeless-ready (TLR/TCS/UST) for tubeless use.
- **Hookless rims:** only tyres approved for hookless, and a max pressure of **5 bar
  (72.5 psi)** under the ISO/ETRTO standard — often lower per the rim maker. Follow the
  lower of rim and tyre limits.

## 2. Tubeless conversion
1. Strip old tape; clean the rim bed with isopropyl alcohol — tape won't stick to oil.
2. **Tape:** width = rim inner width + 2–5 mm so it covers the bead-seat shoulders.
   Start ~10 cm before the valve hole, pull tight while pressing into the centre
   channel, overlap ~10 cm past the valve. Tension and smoothness matter more than layers.
3. Pierce the valve hole with a hot awl or sharp pick from the outside; fit the tubeless
   valve, tighten the lockring finger-tight plus a little.
4. Fit one bead fully, then most of the second, keeping beads **in the centre channel**
   for slack. Seat the last section with thumbs; levers can cut tape.
5. **Seat dry or with sealant:** remove the valve core, blast with a booster pump or
   compressor. Soapy water on the beads helps. Inflate to the tyre's max (not above the
   rim's) until both beads "pop" onto the shoulders; check the moulded line is even.
6. Deflate, add sealant through the valve with a syringe (or pour in before seating the
   last bead). Typical dose: **30–40 ml road, 60–90 ml trail MTB, 100+ ml for plus/DH**.
7. Refit core, inflate, spin and shake the wheel side to side so sealant coats the beads.
   Leave flat on each side for a few minutes.

## 3. Sealant maintenance
- Check every 2–3 months (sooner in heat/dry climates): dipstick through the valve or
  shake and listen. Top up to the original dose; peel out dried "boogers".
- Clogged valve: remove core, clear with a pick, or replace the core.

## 4. Punctures, tubeless
- Small hole: rotate the hole to the bottom, let sealant work, re-inflate.
- Bigger cut (2–6 mm): **tyre plug** (bacon strip) through the hole with the insertion
  tool, trim flush, re-inflate; it's a get-home fix on fast road tyres.
- Sidewall cuts and long slashes: inner patch on the inside of the tyre (vulcanising
  tubeless patch) after cleaning off sealant, or fit a tube with a boot.

## 5. Tube punctures
1. Find the cause before refitting: run fingers *carefully* inside the tyre, check rim tape.
2. Locate the hole (inflate, listen, or dunk in water). Two parallel slits = pinch flat
   (pressure too low).
3. Roughen, apply vulcanising solution, wait until it's touch-dry (several minutes — the
   key step people skip), press patch hard, leave a minute. Glueless patches are temporary.
4. Refit: slightly inflate the tube, valve straight, check no tube under the bead all
   round before full pressure.

## 6. Pressure
Start from a calculator (e.g. tyre maker's or SRAM/Silca-style) by rider + bike weight,
tyre width and surface; adjust 0.1–0.2 bar steps. Wider tyres and tubeless run lower.

## Gotchas
- Tubeless and latex-based sealants dry; a tyre "losing air" after months is often empty.
- Some sealants react badly to CO₂ inflation (the cold gas can clump latex sealant);
  if you must use CO₂, swap to air at the next chance. Check the sealant maker's notes.
- Presta valves: unscrew the tip nut before pumping; Schrader needs a different head.

## Verify
- [ ] ETRTO sizes match; hookless pressure limit respected.
- [ ] Beads evenly seated (moulding line parallel to rim both sides).
- [ ] Wheel holds pressure overnight within ~0.2 bar (road) / a few psi (MTB).
- [ ] Sealant dose recorded with the date.
- [ ] Puncture cause found and removed before re-inflating.
