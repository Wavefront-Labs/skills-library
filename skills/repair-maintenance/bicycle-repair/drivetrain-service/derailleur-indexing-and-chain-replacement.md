# Derailleur indexing and chain replacement

**Goal:** crisp, silent shifts in every gear and a chain replaced before it eats the
cassette. Work in this order — hanger, limits, B-tension, cable tension — because each
step assumes the one before it is right.

## 0. Before touching a screw
- Bike in a stand, rear wheel free to spin, chain clean enough to see wear.
- **Check the hanger first.** A bent derailleur hanger causes most "won't index" bikes.
  Look from behind: the jockey wheels should sit in the same vertical plane as the cog.
  A hanger alignment gauge (e.g. Park DAG-2/3) confirms it; replace rather than
  repeatedly bend aluminium hangers. Many modern frames use the SRAM UDH standard.
- Check the cable: frayed strands at the shifter, kinked housing or gritty ferrules make
  indexing drift. Replace inner and outer together if in doubt.

## 1. Measure the chain (do this every service)
- Chain checker at **0.5% (11/12-speed) or 0.75% (≤10-speed) → replace the chain.** At
  ≥1% the cassette (and often rings) are usually worn too and a new chain will skip.
- Ruler method: 24 links should measure 12 in (304.8 mm) pin-to-pin; 1/16 in (~1.6 mm)
  over ≈ 0.5% wear.
- Gotcha: cheap "drop-in" checkers over-read because they include roller wear; treat a
  borderline reading on them as "check with a ruler".

## 2. Fit a new chain
1. Match speed count and standard (Shimano 12-speed Hyperglide+, SRAM Flattop for
   AXS road/T-Type are not interchangeable with ordinary chains).
2. **Length** (no-chain-yet method): big ring + biggest cog, wrap the chain *without*
   going through the derailleur, overlap to the nearest full link, then add **2 links**
   (one inch). For full-suspension bikes, check at maximum chainstay length (shock
   deflated). SRAM Eagle/T-Type use their own chart based on chainstay length — follow it.
3. Join with the maker's quick-link/connecting pin, orientation arrows/lettering facing out
   where specified. Snap the link home by pedalling down with the rear brake held.
4. Never reuse a Shimano connecting pin; SRAM 12-speed links are single-use.

## 3. Rear derailleur: limits, B-tension, indexing
1. Shift to the smallest cog, then **release cable tension** (or unclip the cable).
2. **H limit screw:** upper jockey wheel directly under the smallest cog.
3. Re-attach the cable pulled snug, barrel adjuster wound in then out 1 turn.
4. **L limit:** shift to the largest cog by pushing the cage by hand; set the screw so the
   pulley lines up with the biggest cog and cannot go past it into the spokes.
5. **B-tension:** in the largest cog, set the upper pulley gap — typically ~5–6 mm on
   Shimano road/MTB; SRAM Eagle uses its plastic gauge (and a set sag position on
   full-sus). Too close = clatter; too far = slow shifts.
6. **Indexing:** shift up one click from the smallest cog. If it hesitates, add tension
   (barrel adjuster **anticlockwise**, viewed from behind) in quarter turns; if it
   over-shifts or rattles toward bigger cogs, reduce. Check all gears; fine-tune in the
   middle of the cassette.

## 4. Front derailleur (if fitted)
- Outer cage plate 1–3 mm above the big ring teeth, cage parallel to the rings.
- Low limit: chain in small ring/largest cog just clears the inner cage; high limit:
  big ring/smallest cog just clears the outer cage. Then cable tension with trim positions.

## 5. Electronic shifting (Di2, AXS)
- No cable: indexing is "micro-adjust" from the shifter/app (E-Tube, SRAM AXS app).
  Limits and B-tension are still mechanical. Charge the battery first — low charge
  disables the front mech before the rear on Di2.
- Firmware updates come through the app; update all components together.

## Gotchas
- Ghost shifting under load with good indexing → worn chain/cassette, sticky cable, or
  loose cassette lockring (40 N·m typical).
- A brand-new chain skipping only on the most-used cogs = worn cassette; replace it.
- Don't chase indexing with a bent hanger — it will never be right in every gear.

## Verify
- [ ] Hanger aligned; chain wear recorded and chain replaced at 0.5/0.75%.
- [ ] Chain length checked big/big (and at full suspension compression).
- [ ] Limits stop the chain at both ends; it cannot drop into spokes or off the small cog.
- [ ] Every gear shifts up and down in one click, silent in the middle cogs.
- [ ] Test ride under pedalling load, including out-of-saddle efforts.
