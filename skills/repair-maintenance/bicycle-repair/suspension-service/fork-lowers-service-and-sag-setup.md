# Fork lowers service and sag setup

**Goal:** a fork and shock that move freely, don't leak and are set for the rider.
The 50-hour lowers service is the most valuable thing a home or shop mechanic can do;
full damper rebuilds need model-specific parts and procedures.

## 1. Service intervals (typical — check the model's manual)
- Lowers service (seals, foam rings, bath oil): **every 50 hours** of riding (Fox
  recommends 125 h on some current models; RockShox commonly 50 h).
- Air-can service: 50 h on many RockShox; damper/spring full service ~100–200 h.
- Rear air shock: air-can service 50 h; damper service by a qualified shop.

## 2. Gather the exact data
Model, year and damper type decide oil volumes, weights and torques. Look up the
maker's service sheet: RockShox specifies its own bath oil (Maxima Plush on recent
models) and Fox uses Gold 20WT; volumes are typically in the 5–30 ml per leg range and
differ by leg and model. Don't guess — wrong volume causes hydraulic lock or poor
lubrication.

## 3. Lowers service (RockShox-style air fork, overview)
1. Clean the fork. Release all air from the air spring (remove valve cap, press core),
   note rebound and compression settings, remove the rebound knob.
2. Unthread the bottom bolts a few turns, tap each with a soft mallet to free the shafts,
   then remove. Catch the bath oil in a tray.
3. Slide the lowers off; remove remaining oil.
4. Pry out old **wiper seals** with a seal tool; remove foam rings. Clean lowers inside
   with a rag on a dowel and isopropyl alcohol; inspect bushings for play (rock the
   lowers on the stanchions before disassembly to check).
5. Soak new foam rings in suspension oil, fit; press new seals in square with the
   correct seal driver.
6. Grease seal lips with suspension grease, slide lowers on part way, inject the specified
   bath oil volume into each leg through the bottom (or before fitting, per model),
   push lowers fully home.
7. Refit bottom bolts and crush washers to torque (often ~6.8 N·m); refit rebound knob.
8. Re-inflate and cycle the fork; wipe excess oil.

## 4. Air-can service (brief)
Remove the top cap only after all air is out (both chambers). Clean, replace the main
piston O-rings and seal-head seals, re-grease with the specified grease, and equalise:
on most current forks the negative chamber equalises automatically at a dimple/port as
you compress past a point in the first few millimetres — check the travel is full.

## 5. Setup: sag
1. Set the shock pump to the starting pressure on the fork leg chart for rider weight
   **in riding kit**.
2. Rider in attack position, weight on pedals. Push the O-ring on the stanchion down,
   bounce gently, step off carefully.
3. Measure sag: **fork 15–20%** of travel (XC) to 20–25% (trail/enduro); **rear shock
   25–30%** (shock stroke, not wheel travel).
4. Adjust pressure in ~5 psi steps.

## 6. Rebound and compression
- **Rebound:** start mid-range. Too fast = bike bucks off drops/kicks; too slow = packs
  down on repeated hits and feels harsh. Push and release on flat ground — fork should
  return quickly with no top-out bounce.
- **Low-speed compression:** controls brake dive and pedal bob; add a few clicks if the
  bike wallows.
- **Volume spacers (tokens):** bottoming harshly at correct sag → add one; never use full
  travel → remove one.

## Gotchas
- Never open a damper with a pressurised internal floating piston (IFP) or bladder
  without the procedure — it can be nitrogen-charged on rear shocks.
- Over-tightened bottom bolts on hollow shafts strip; use the torque.
- Stanchion scratch that catches a fingernail = will leak; usually means new uppers.

## Verify
- [ ] Settings (rebound, compression, pressure) recorded before and after.
- [ ] Correct bath oil type and volume per the service sheet.
- [ ] No oil weep after 10 compressions and a ride; seals clean.
- [ ] Sag within target; rebound returns without top-out clunk.
- [ ] Full travel achievable on the biggest hits (O-ring near the seal).
