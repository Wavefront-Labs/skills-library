# Hydraulic disc brake bleed and pad replacement

**Goal:** a firm, consistent lever that bites at the same point every time, silent
rotors and no contamination. Diagnose first — most "bad brakes" are worn or
contaminated pads, not air.

## 1. Diagnose by symptom
| Symptom | Likely cause |
|---|---|
| Lever soft, firms up when pumped | Air in system → bleed |
| Lever comes to bar slowly over time | Leaking seal or contaminated piston → caliper/lever service |
| Bite point wanders (Shimano) | Air trapped in lever reservoir → lever bleed / "burp" |
| Squeal, poor power, glazed pads | Contaminated pads/rotor, or not bedded in |
| Rhythmic rub | Bent rotor or misaligned caliper |
| Lever firm but weak stopping | Worn pads (<0.5 mm material or 2.5–3 mm total incl. backing on many systems) |

## 2. Fluid: never mix them
- **Shimano, Magura, Tektro (most): mineral oil.** **SRAM, Hayes, Formula (most): DOT 4/5.1.**
  The correct fluid is printed on the lever or reservoir cap. Wrong fluid destroys seals.
- DOT fluid strips paint and is hygroscopic: wipe spills immediately with isopropyl
  alcohol, wear nitrile gloves, use fresh sealed fluid.

## 3. Pads
1. Remove wheel, pull pad retaining pin/clip, slide pads out.
2. Push pistons back square with a plastic tyre lever or piston press — never a
   screwdriver on ceramic pistons. If one piston sticks out, clean around it with
   isopropyl and work it gently; hold the other back while you do.
3. Choose compound: **resin/organic** = quieter, more bite, wears fast; **sintered/metal**
   = wet and long descents, louder. Check rotor compatibility (some rotors are resin-only).
4. Fit pads and spring, pin and retention clip. Pump the lever until pads contact.
5. **Bed in:** 10–20 moderate stops from ~20 km/h to walking pace, then several harder
   ones, without locking the wheel or stopping fully with the brake held hot.
- Contaminated pads (oil, lube spray): sanding rarely saves them; replace. Clean the
  rotor with isopropyl or disc-brake cleaner — never WD-40 or chain lube nearby.

## 4. Caliper alignment and rotors
- Loosen the two caliper bolts, squeeze and hold the lever, tighten bolts alternately
  (typical 6–8 N·m — check the maker's figure). Spin; adjust by eye against a light.
- True rotors with a rotor truing fork at the high spot; small bends only.
- Replace rotors below the minimum thickness stamped on them (often 1.5 mm for 1.8 mm
  Shimano/SRAM road and MTB rotors; check the stamp).

## 5. Bleeding — Shimano (mineral oil, gravity/"cup" method)
1. Remove pads, fit bleed block. Level the lever (reservoir horizontal) — rotate the
   lever on the bar if needed.
2. Remove the bleed screw at the lever, screw in the funnel, part-fill with oil.
3. Caliper: fit syringe with oil to the bleed nipple (8 mm/7 mm), open ¼ turn, push oil
   **up** until no bubbles reach the funnel. Close nipple.
4. Flick and squeeze the lever repeatedly: bubbles rise into the funnel. Tilt the bike
   lever-up and lever-down to free trapped air.
5. Plug funnel with its stopper, remove funnel, top off the port so it overflows, refit
   the bleed screw. Clean everything with isopropyl.

## 6. Bleeding — SRAM (DOT, two-syringe method)
1. Set lever reach and contact adjust (if fitted) per SRAM's bleed procedure; pads out,
   bleed block in.
2. Half-fill both syringes; de-gas them (plug tip, pull vacuum, tap, expel bubbles).
3. Caliper syringe on, lever syringe on (Bleeding Edge fitting on newer models).
4. Push from caliper to lever; then pull vacuum on the lever syringe and push back
   several times; clamp and remove caliper syringe; vacuum the lever, then push gently
   to pressurise. Remove, refit screw. Exact sequence varies by model — follow SRAM's
   current procedure for that brake.

## Gotchas
- Never squeeze a lever with the wheel and pads/block out: pistons pop out.
- Lever feel sold as "firm" can hide a pad gap too small → drag; check rotor clearance.
- Rim brakes: pads 1–2 mm from rim, toe-in ~1 mm at the leading edge, cable tension at
  the barrel adjuster; replace pads when wear grooves vanish, check rim wear indicators.

## Verify
- [ ] Correct fluid confirmed from the lever/cap marking.
- [ ] Lever firm, bite point same after 20 rapid pulls and after hanging bike vertically.
- [ ] No fluid on pads/rotor; rotor spins silent or near-silent.
- [ ] Pads bedded in; test stop from speed on a safe surface.
- [ ] Caliper and lever bolts torqued to the maker's figure.
