# Truing, tensioning and building wheels

**Goal:** a wheel that is straight (lateral), round (radial), centred (dish) and evenly,
adequately tensioned — tension is what makes it stay true. Small corrections, many passes.

## Tools
Truing stand (or the bike's own frame with a zip-tie pointer), correct-size spoke key
(the nipple size matters — 3.23 mm/0.130 in is common; 3.3, 3.45 and square/internal
nipples exist), dishing tool, spoke tensiometer (e.g. Park TM-1) with the conversion
table for the spoke type, spoke ruler, grease or spoke prep.

## 1. Which way to turn
The nipple is an ordinary right-hand nut on the spoke. Viewed from the tyre side
(spoke end pointing at you) it tightens **clockwise**; viewed from the hub side — where
your spoke key usually sits in a stand — that looks **anticlockwise**. The #1 beginner
error is turning the wrong way: mark one nipple and confirm with a quarter turn first.

## 2. Lateral truing
1. Spin, find the biggest wobble with the stand caliper just touching.
2. Rim pulls toward the side whose spokes are tighter. To move the rim **left**, tighten
   left-side spokes at that spot and/or loosen right-side ones — ideally both, ¼ turn
   each, so tension stays even.
3. Spread corrections over 2–4 spokes centred on the high point (half turn centre,
   quarter turn either side), not one spoke cranked hard.
4. Aim within ~0.5 mm for a rim-brake wheel; disc wheels tolerate a bit more.

## 3. Radial truing (hop)
- High spot: **tighten** the pair of spokes (one each side) at the high spot equally;
  low spot: loosen the pair. Equal amounts on both sides keep the lateral true.
- Fix radial before final lateral; the two interact. Aim under ~0.5 mm.

## 4. Dish
Flip the dishing tool between sides (or flip the wheel in a self-centring stand). If the
rim is off-centre toward the right, tighten all left-side spokes a quarter turn (or
loosen right). Rear and disc front wheels are asymmetric by design — drive/rotor side
spokes carry more tension.

## 5. Tension and balance
- Measure every spoke with the tensiometer. Target the rim maker's maximum; typical
  road/MTB drive-side targets are roughly **100–130 kgf** (≈1000–1300 N). Non-drive rear
  runs ~50–70% of drive side. Check the rim's published limit — carbon rims vary widely.
- Within a side, keep spokes within about ±10–20% of the average. A spoke much looser
  than its neighbours with a true rim hides a bent rim or hub flange issue.

## 6. Replacing a broken spoke
1. Remove tyre (and cassette/rotor if the spoke goes through that flange).
2. Measure the old spoke or look up length from a sibling on the same side; thread a new
   one following the same lacing pattern (head in/head out, over/under crossings).
3. Tension until it plucks with the same note as its same-side neighbours, then true.

## 7. Building from parts (three-cross, 32h)
1. Calculate spoke lengths from **ERD** (rim effective rim diameter — measure it, don't
   trust the catalogue blindly), hub flange diameters and centre-to-flange distances,
   using a spoke calculator. Round down to the nearest available millimetre.
2. Lace: first set of 8 heads-out on one flange to every 4th rim hole adjacent to the
   valve hole; second set heads-in on the same flange, crossing three; repeat the other
   flange. Valve hole sits between parallel spokes for pump access.
3. Grease threads (or spoke prep), run all nipples down to the same thread depth.
4. Bring tension up evenly in half-turn rounds, truing as you go.
5. **Stress-relieve:** squeeze parallel spoke pairs hard around the wheel; re-true.
   Repeat until it stops drifting. Check for spoke wind-up on bladed or thin spokes —
   hold the spoke with a bladed holder.

## Gotchas
- Truing a wheel with a cracked rim or flat spot just moves the defect. Inspect eyelets.
- Over-tightened nipples on aluminium can pull through the rim bed; stop at the limit.
- Rusted nipples round off — use a 4-sided wrench and penetrant; replace if rounding.

## Verify
- [ ] Lateral and radial runout within ~0.5 mm (stand caliper or dial gauge).
- [ ] Dish centred in the dishing tool, then in the frame/fork.
- [ ] Tension recorded per side; within limit and ±20% of the average.
- [ ] Stress-relieved and re-trued; no pinging on the first ride.
- [ ] Re-check after first 50–100 km on new builds.
