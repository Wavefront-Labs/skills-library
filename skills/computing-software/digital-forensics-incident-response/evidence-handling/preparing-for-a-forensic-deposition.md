# Preparing for a forensic deposition

A deposition (FRCP 30) is sworn, recorded testimony taken by the opposing side before trial. For a forensic examiner it is mainly a test of whether your methods and conclusions survive questioning, and whether you can be pushed into overstatement. It differs from trial testimony: there is no judge in the room and the transcript can be used later to impeach you.

## Preparation (days before)
1. Re-read your report, declarations, notes, and anything you produced or relied on. Know where every number came from.
2. List weak points honestly: gaps in the evidence, custody issues, tool errors, smear, assumptions about time zones, things you did not examine. Prepare plain explanations.
3. Re-validate key findings if time has passed (tools updated, new evidence).
4. Review the opposing expert's report for criticisms you will be asked about.
5. Meet counsel to understand the case themes and deposition logistics; do not let that meeting change your opinions.
6. Expect a Daubert/FRE 702 angle: questions on method acceptance, error rates, testing, peer review of your work.

## Answering technique
- Listen to the full question, pause, answer only that question, then stop.
- "I don't know" and "I don't recall" are acceptable when true; "I would need to see the document" is correct when asked about details.
- Ask for any document you are questioned about; read the relevant part before answering.
- Do not accept a paraphrase of your findings that changes their meaning; restate accurately.
- Correct yourself on the record as soon as you notice an error.
- Use plain language; explain jargon briefly.

## Common lines of attack
Tool reliability and versions; whether you validated tools; alternative explanations you did not exclude; whether you saw exculpatory data; your fees and who instructed you; past testimony and publications; inconsistencies between drafts and final.

## After
Review the transcript within the allowed period (FRCP 30(e) gives 30 days when requested) and submit errata for transcription errors and genuine corrections, with reasons.

## Verify
Before the deposition you can state, for each conclusion, the evidence item, the method, the limitations and the alternatives you excluded, without looking at notes.
