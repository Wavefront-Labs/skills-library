# Preparing for expert witness testimony

**Goal:** present digital forensic findings credibly, within your expertise, and survive
challenge to methods and qualifications.

## Legal frameworks
- **US Federal Rule of Evidence 702** (amended 2023): proponent must show it is more likely
  than not that the expert's knowledge helps, the testimony is based on sufficient facts,
  reliable principles and methods, reliably applied. **Daubert** factors: testability, peer
  review/publication, known error rate, standards, general acceptance. Some states use Frye
  (general acceptance).
- **England & Wales CPR Part 35** / Criminal Procedure Rules Part 19: overriding duty to the
  court, not the instructing party; statement of truth; disclose the range of opinion.

## Preparation
1. Re-read your report and notes; know every tool version, setting and hash.
2. Re-verify key findings; prepare to explain any error you have found since (disclose it).
3. Anticipate challenges: tool validation, chain of custody, timestamp interpretation,
   alternative explanations (malware did it, another user), attribution limits.
4. Prepare demonstratives: simple timeline graphics, annotated screenshots, analogies
   checked for accuracy.
5. CV and list of prior testimony; training and certifications (GCFA, GCFE, EnCE).

## On the stand
- Answer the question asked; short answers; "I don't know" and "that's outside my
  examination" are acceptable.
- Concede reasonable points; neutrality is credibility.
- Translate jargon; keep the same terms as the report.
- Never stretch beyond the evidence (e.g. who sat at the keyboard).

## Verify
Mock cross-examination by a colleague or counsel covering methods, tool reliability and
alternative explanations, with weak answers fixed before the hearing.
