# writing-quick-reference-cards

## Scope
One card = one task or a small set of related tasks a user does without you (join a
Teams Room meeting, reset a password, connect to VPN, scan to email). If it needs more than
a page, it is a KB article, not a card.

## Structure
1. Title as the task: "Connect to the VPN".
2. One line on when to use it.
3. Numbered steps, one action each, starting with a verb: "Select **Connect**".
4. Annotated screenshots next to the step they belong to (crop tight, arrow or box on the
   control).
5. "If it doesn't work" with one or two checks and the support contact.
6. Footer: version/date, owner, QR code to the full KB article.

## Language
- Use the exact UI labels in bold; match the user's OS language and version.
- Plain words, short sentences, no jargon ("sign in", not "authenticate").
- Tell users what they will see after key steps so they know they are on track.

## Accessibility
- Body text 11pt or larger, high contrast, no meaning by colour alone.
- Screenshots have alt text in digital versions; tagged PDF or HTML, not image-only PDFs.

## Test before publishing
Give the card to someone who has never done the task and watch silently. Every hesitation
is an edit.

## Distribution and upkeep
Publish on the intranet/KB and in the self-service portal; print laminated cards for
meeting rooms and shared devices. Review on every UI change and at least yearly; remove
old printed copies.

## Verify
A novice completes the task from the card alone, the card fits one page, and it is linked
from the related KB article and support portal.
