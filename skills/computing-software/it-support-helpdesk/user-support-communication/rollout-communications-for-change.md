# rollout-communications-for-change

## Every message answers
- **What** is changing (in user terms, not product names alone).
- **Why** it helps them or the organisation.
- **When** it affects them (their date, not the programme's).
- **What they need to do** (or "nothing"), and by when.
- **Where to get help**.

## Plan by audience
Executives and assistants, frontline staff, remote workers, shared-device users,
accessibility needs, and support staff themselves (they need detail first).

## Sequence (adjust to the change)
| When | Message |
|---|---|
| T-3/4 weeks | Announcement from a business sponsor: what and why |
| T-2 weeks | How it affects you, how to prepare, training links |
| T-1 week | Reminder with your date and actions; FAQ |
| T-1 day | Short reminder: "Tomorrow, when you sign in you will see..." |
| Go-live | What to expect, known issues, where help is (floor walkers, chat) |
| T+1 week | Tips, fixes for top issues, feedback survey |

## Channels
Email for the record, Teams/intranet posts for reach, manager briefings for credibility,
posters/screens on sites without desk-based staff, and change champions in each team.

## Writing it
Subject lines with the action and date ("Action by 3 Nov: install the new VPN"). Short,
scannable, one call to action. Screenshots of what users will see.

## Feedback
Pilot group first and fix the messages based on their questions. Track ticket themes
after each wave and update the FAQ and next messages.

## Gotchas
- Too many messages from IT get ignored; combine and keep the sponsor's voice for the big
  ones.
- Do not announce dates that have not been confirmed with the change board.

## Verify
Every audience has received the sequence, pilot feedback was incorporated, and
post-go-live ticket volume and survey results are reviewed against the plan.
