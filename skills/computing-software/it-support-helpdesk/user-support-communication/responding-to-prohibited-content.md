# responding-to-prohibited-content

Goal: when you stumble on something that may be illegal or a serious policy
breach, you protect the evidence, the organisation and yourself — and you
don't become the investigator.

## The A+ incident response steps
1. **Identify** — recognise it's potentially prohibited (illegal images,
   evidence of fraud, harassment, data theft, pirated software at scale).
2. **Stop.** Don't keep browsing, don't open more files, don't copy it, don't
   show colleagues, don't confront the user.
3. **Report through proper channels** — your manager and the designated
   contact (security, HR, legal) per policy. For suspected child abuse
   material: report to the designated contact immediately; laws generally
   prohibit copying or transmitting it, including "to show someone".
4. **Preserve** — leave the device as it is. If on, don't shut down or
   unplug unless instructed (volatile evidence); disconnect from the network
   only if instructed or if active harm is happening. Secure it physically.
5. **Document** — time, date, what you saw (briefly, factually), where, what
   you did, who you told. Keep notes outside the general ticket queue if the
   ticket is visible to the user or wide teams.
6. **Chain of custody** — if you hand over the device: log who, when, why,
   signatures; tamper-evident bag/seal where available. Every transfer
   recorded.

## What not to do
- Don't run cleanup tools, AV scans, reimage or "fix the ticket" first.
- Don't delete the content.
- Don't discuss with the user or others; it may tip off or defame.
- Don't continue remote sessions — end calmly with a neutral reason if
  needed, then report.

## After
Cooperate with the investigation, keep confidentiality, and only resume
work on the device when released in writing. Wellbeing support exists for
staff exposed to disturbing content — use it.

## Verify
You can name your organisation's designated contact for this situation now,
before you need it; the report and custody log exist for any case.
