# expectations-callbacks-and-complaint-handling

## Setting expectations
- Every interaction ends with: what happens next, who does it, and **when** the user
  will hear back. "I've passed this to the network team; they work these within one
  business day, and I'll update you by 3 pm tomorrow either way."
- Use the real SLA and queue state, not the hoped-for time. Under-promise slightly and
  deliver.
- If a date slips, tell the user before it passes, with the new date and reason.
- Avoid "should be quick", "shouldn't take long", "ASAP"; they create conflict later.

## Call-backs and follow-up
- Book call-backs as tasks or ticket reminders with a time; a promise kept only in your
  head fails on a busy day.
- Agree the method and window: "I'll call your mobile between 2 and 3."
- If you miss them: leave a voicemail with a ticket reference and next attempt time,
  send a written update, try once more; don't close for "no response" after one attempt
  (follow the documented attempt rule, typically 3 attempts over 3 business days).
- After a fix, follow up on intermittent issues a few days later before closing.
- Hand off follow-ups explicitly at shift end (who now owns the promise).

## Complaint handling
A complaint is feedback that something went wrong in the service, not an attack.
1. **Acknowledge** quickly (same day): thank them, show you understand the impact.
2. **Log** it as a complaint (record type or category), linked to the original tickets.
3. **Investigate** facts: ticket history, call recordings, timelines. Talk to the
   analysts involved.
4. **Respond** with what happened, what you've done for them (fix, expedite, apology),
   and what changes to prevent recurrence. Avoid blaming individuals or other teams to
   the customer.
5. **Close** with their agreement; escalate to a manager if they remain dissatisfied.
6. **Learn**: trend complaints monthly by cause (delay, communication, attitude, fix
   quality) and feed into coaching and process changes (ISO 10002 complaint handling
   principles: visibility, accessibility, responsiveness, objectivity).

## Verify
Tickets show a next-update time that was met, missed call-backs have documented
attempts, and complaints are closed with a response and a recorded cause.
