# e-waste-and-battery-disposal-rules

Goal: nothing from IT ends up in general waste, and the organisation can
show where it went (A+ 220-1202 4.5 environmental impact).

## Hierarchy
1. **Reuse** internally (spares, loaners) or donate/resell after
   sanitisation.
2. **Return** to manufacturer take-back schemes (many OEMs, toner makers).
3. **Recycle** via certified e-waste processors (R2v3 / e-Stewards).
4. Never landfill.

## Rules by item
| Item | Handling |
|---|---|
| Computers, monitors, peripherals | e-waste stream (WEEE in UK/EU — producers/distributors have take-back obligations; US rules vary by state) |
| **Lithium-ion batteries** (laptops, phones, UPS packs) | separate battery recycling; tape terminals; damaged/swollen = segregate in fire-safe container; dangerous-goods rules for transport |
| Lead-acid UPS batteries | battery recycler; heavy, acid hazard |
| Toner/ink cartridges | manufacturer return programmes; toner is an irritant — bag leaking cartridges |
| CRT monitors | hazardous (lead glass, high voltage) — specialist recycler |
| Mobile phones | e-waste after MDM release and wipe |
| Coin cells | battery recycling, tape (swallowing hazard) |
Check the **Safety Data Sheet** for disposal guidance on chemicals and
batteries.

## Records
Waste transfer notes / recycling certificates (by weight or item) linked to
asset disposal records; some jurisdictions require them (UK duty of care).

## Environmental power settings (same A+ objective)
Power plans and sleep timers on endpoints, Energy Star equipment, turning
off unused kit — part of the same environmental objective.

## Gotchas
- Data first: sanitise or remove storage before any device enters the
  e-waste stream.
- "Free collection" vendors without certifications may export to informal
  recycling — check certifications and downstream vendors.

## Verify
For a sample of disposed items you can show the recycling certificate and
that no batteries went into general waste bins.
