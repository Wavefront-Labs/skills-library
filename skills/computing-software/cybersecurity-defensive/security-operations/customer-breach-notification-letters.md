# Customer breach notification letters

Always drafted with legal counsel and privacy; content requirements vary by jurisdiction.

## Required elements (GDPR Art. 34 and common US state law content)
- Plain description of what happened and when (date range of the breach and of discovery).
- Categories of personal data involved (names, emails, SSNs, card data, health data).
- Likely consequences for the individual.
- Measures taken to address the breach and mitigate effects.
- What individuals can do (reset passwords, watch for phishing, credit freeze/monitoring).
- Contact point (DPO, dedicated helpline/email), and for US letters often regulator/credit bureau
  contact information and free credit monitoring offer details where applicable.

## Writing
- Plain language, short sentences, no jargon or minimisation ("sophisticated attack" clichés
  undermine trust). State what you know and that you will update if facts change.
- Specific to the recipient where possible (their data categories).
- Subject line clearly labelled ("Notice of data security incident") to avoid being dismissed as
  phishing; do not include links requesting credentials. Direct users to type your URL.
- Translations for the populations affected.

## Process
1. Identify affected individuals and data per person (data mapping from investigation).
2. Determine legal obligations per jurisdiction (timelines: GDPR "without undue delay" to
   individuals when high risk; US states vary, some 30-60 days).
3. Draft, legal review, executive approval; prepare call centre FAQ and staff scripts.
4. Send via mail/email per law; track delivery and bounces; substitute notice if contact info lacking.

## Gotchas
- Inconsistent messages across letter, website and press release; prepare all together.
- Phishers will imitate your notice; publish a verification page.

## Verify
Legal sign-off recorded, recipient list reconciled with affected data set, delivery logs retained.
