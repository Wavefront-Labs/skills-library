# Social media impersonation

## Common patterns
- Fake customer support accounts replying to customers' complaints and steering them to phishing or
  "refund" scams.
- Fake executive profiles (LinkedIn, X, WhatsApp) for investment scams or recruitment fraud.
- Fake job postings using the brand for advance-fee or data harvesting.
- Cloned brand pages running malicious ads (malvertising, crypto scams).

## Detection
- DRP/brand protection services, or monitoring searches for brand names, handles similar to official
  ones (character substitutions), executive names, logo reverse image search.
- Customer-facing teams report suspicious accounts via an internal channel.
- Verify your own accounts (platform verification badges) to make impersonators easier to distinguish.

## Evidence capture
Screenshots with URLs and timestamps, profile ID (numeric IDs persist after handle changes), archived
copies, examples of victim contact.

## Takedown
- Platform impersonation forms (X, Meta, LinkedIn, TikTok, YouTube) as the brand owner or authorised
  agent; trademark registration numbers speed brand cases.
- Escalate through platform partner/enterprise support channels where available.
- Fraud/ad abuse: report malicious ads via ad platform reporting.
- Track case IDs and time to takedown.

## Customer protection
- Publish official account list on your website; pinned warnings; support teams know how to answer.
- If customers were defrauded, coordinate with fraud and legal teams.

## Gotchas
- Impersonators recreate accounts quickly; continuous monitoring beats one-off sweeps.
- Parody/fan accounts may be lawful; focus on deception and fraud.

## Verify
Monthly: impersonations detected, takedown rate and median time, repeat offenders.
