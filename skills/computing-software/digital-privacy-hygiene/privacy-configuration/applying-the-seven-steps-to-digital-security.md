# SSD Seven Steps to digital security

**Goal:** turn the seven principles from EFF's Surveillance Self-Defense into decisions you can make today.

## The seven steps and what to do with each
| Step | Practical meaning |
|---|---|
| 1. Knowledge is power | Answer five questions before choosing tools: what to protect, who from, how likely, how bad if it fails, how much trouble you will accept. The answers give you a security plan (threat model) |
| 2. The weakest link | A chain is only as strong as its weakest link. Look at every part of your information and device use; an encrypted chat does not protect photos if unencrypted copies sit on a stolen laptop |
| 3. Simpler is safer and easier | Too many tools are hard to keep straight and hide the weak links. The safest solution is sometimes the least technical one, even pen and paper |
| 4. More expensive doesn't mean more secure | Do not assume the priciest option is best, especially if it drains resources needed elsewhere; low-cost habits such as shredding trash count |
| 5. It's okay to trust someone (but know who) | You already trust family, doctors and lawyers. For each tool or person, know who you are trusting and with what; a cloud service can access what you store there. The fewer people share a secret, the better |
| 6. There is no one perfect security plan | Make a plan that fits your risks; a plan that is too hard to follow day to day will not work |
| 7. What's secure today may not be secure tomorrow | Re-evaluate regularly and watch for major security news. Security is a continual process |

## Use it with someone else
1. Walk through the security-plan questions first. The plan page lists six: what to protect, who from, how bad the
   consequences are, how likely, how much trouble they will go to, and who their allies are.
2. Check the seven steps against their answers and pick a small number of changes to make first.
3. Write the plan down and mark a calendar date to review it (step 7).

## Gotchas
- Don't lead with tools. The plan decides the tools.
- Step 5 includes trusting you, the helper: be transparent and hand over control of accounts you set up.
