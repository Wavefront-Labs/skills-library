# Student privacy rights and school tech

**Goal:** limit what schools share and what school-issued tech observes, for children and adult students.

## FERPA (US) rights
- Parents of K-12 students (and students themselves from 18 or at college) can **inspect** education records,
  request **amendment** of inaccurate records, and must **consent** before most disclosures.
- **Directory information** (name, address, phone, photo, activities) can be shared without consent unless you
  **opt out** in writing; many schools require the opt-out annually. Vendors, yearbooks and data brokers use it.
- Separate opt-out: secondary schools give **military recruiters** names/addresses unless you opt out.
- Surveys about sensitive topics need parental notice/opt-out under the PPRA.

## School devices and accounts
- Assume school Chromebooks and Google/Microsoft school accounts are monitored (GoGuardian, Gaggle, Securly), often
  including browsing at home. Do personal things (health, identity, personal messages) on a personal device and
  account.
- Do not sign into personal accounts on school devices; personal phones on school Wi-Fi may be filtered/logged.

## Edtech and proctoring
- Ask the school which vendors get student data and under which contract; younger children are also covered by COPPA.
- Remote proctoring tools (Honorlock, Proctorio, Respondus) can have extensive access: use a separate user account or
  device, close other apps, uninstall after the exam.

## Breaches
- School platform breaches (e.g. the 2024 PowerSchool incident) expose names, birthdates, SSNs and medical notes.
- Freeze children's credit at Equifax, Experian and TransUnion (parents can request minor freezes) and keep it frozen
  until they need credit.

## Outside the US
UK/EU students and parents have GDPR access and objection rights; Australia, Canada have state/provincial rules.
