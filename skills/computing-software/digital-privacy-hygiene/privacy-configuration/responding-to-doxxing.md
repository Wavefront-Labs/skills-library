# Responding to doxxing

**Goal:** limit exposure and harm when personal information is gathered and published to target you. Doxxing, per EFF, is collecting information such as your address or email accounts, packaging it and releasing it to cause harm; it can lead to coordinated online and offline harassment and threats to safety.

## 1. Secure your accounts first
Per EFF, if an attacker can get into an important account (email, social media) they may reach the same private information. Use unique passwords (a password manager helps) and two-factor authentication. Removing your phone's advertising ID and blocking trackers reduces data available for purchase.

## 2. Find what is exposed
Search your name, nicknames, handles, address, phone and emails in a private window (so you are not logged in to Google). Keep a note of what you find and where. Optionally ask trusted friends or family to do the same. EFF notes it is common to be overwhelmed; prioritise high-risk items.

## 3. Remove results from Google
- "Results about you": enter your name and details (nickname, maiden name, several phones, addresses, emails), turn on notifications, review under the To review tab, then Request to remove. It handles phone number, home address and email address. Status shows under Removal requests (in progress, approved, denied, undone).
- From Search: search your name (plus city or address), open About this result (More), choose Remove result > It shows my personal info and I don't want it there > Contact info. Enter the name and contact details exactly as shown on the result; one type of contact info is enough.
- Google will not remove results on pages it treats as valuable to the public, such as government or educational sites and newspapers, and it won't remove info you can remove yourself (a social page or blog you own). Requests for someone else, or professional info posted to harm you, use the detailed removal request form; under-18s also use that form.
- Approved removals are for all queries, or only queries containing your name (query-based). The source page still exists: contact the site owner to remove it there.
- You can undo a request in Results about you, but that does not guarantee the URL reappears.

## 4. Reduce the source
- Data brokers and people-search sites supply addresses and phone numbers. You can request removal from each, often through multi-step email-confirmed processes that need repeating because data repopulates. Paid services such as EasyOptOuts and Optery claim to do this but need ongoing subscriptions and cannot promise full coverage.
- Some US states have address confidentiality programs ("Safe at Home") that give a proxy address, reducing exposure from property and voting records and data brokers.
- Audit each social media account's privacy settings and make accounts private if not used for public communication.

## Verify
Results about you shows removals as approved, and a fresh private-window search no longer shows the addresses or numbers.
