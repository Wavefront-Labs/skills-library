# Connected car privacy

**Goal:** modern cars collect location, driving behaviour and synced phone data, and some makers sold it to
insurers. Reduce collection and sharing, and check what has already gone out.

## Understand the exposure
- Mozilla's 2023 review rated cars the worst product category for privacy.
- The FTC acted against GM in 2025 for sharing drivers' precise location and driving behaviour (via a "Smart Driver"
  program) with consumer reporting agencies, which influenced insurance prices.

## Opt out and check reports
- In the car maker's app/portal: turn off driving-behaviour, "driver feedback", and data-sharing/marketing programs;
  decline insurer telematics unless you want usage-based pricing.
- Request your consumer disclosure reports from **LexisNexis Risk Solutions** and **Verisk** to see if driving data
  was shared; dispute or request deletion where state law allows.
- Use privacy-law rights (CCPA and other states) to request deletion from the manufacturer.

## Phones and cars
- Pairing over Bluetooth or USB can copy contacts, call logs and messages into the head unit. Deny contact/message
  sync prompts where possible; use CarPlay/Android Auto without granting extra data.
- **Rental and sold cars**: delete your phone from Bluetooth devices and the head unit's phonebook; clear navigation
  history and garage door codes; factory reset the infotainment before selling or returning a lease.

## Shared access and stalking
- Car apps (Tesla, Hyundai Bluelink, FordPass, MyChevrolet etc.) show live location and remote unlock. After a
  breakup, remove other drivers/app users, change the account password, and check for extra keys/phone keys.
- Dealers can sometimes disable the telematics modem; ask, but this may also disable emergency/remote services.

## Verify
- [ ] Data-sharing programs off in the car account; disclosure reports requested; no unknown users or keys on the car app.
