# Using prepaid and gift cards for privacy

**Goal:** pay some merchants without handing them your name, home address and main card number.

## Options compared
| Option | Identity needed | Privacy from merchant | Protection |
|---|---|---|---|
| Store gift card (Amazon, Apple, Steam) bought with cash | None | High for that store; purchases still tied to the account | Low; lost if account locked |
| Non-reloadable Visa/Mastercard gift card, cash | None at purchase | Medium; online use may need a ZIP/name registered | Low; fees, balance hard to use up |
| Reloadable prepaid card | ID verification (KYC) | Medium; issuer knows you | CFPB Prepaid Rule protections once registered |
| Virtual card numbers (bank or Privacy.com) | Bank-level KYC | Merchant sees a unique card, can pause/lock | Bank protections |

## Practical tips
- Registration: online merchants check the billing ZIP (AVS); many gift cards let you register a ZIP on the
  issuer site. Use details lawfully; do not supply someone else's identity.
- Avoid activation fees by buying cards in promotions; spend down odd balances by splitting payments at checkout.
- Keep the receipt and card number photo until the balance is used; a drained card is hard to dispute.
- Subscriptions: prepaid cards stop paying when empty, a feature against forgotten auto-renewals.

## Know the boundary with scams
Gift cards are legitimate for your own purchases. Anyone (a "government agency", "tech support", a boss by text)
asking you to buy gift cards and read out the numbers is running a scam.

## Gotchas
- Cards bought with a store loyalty account or card payment are linked to you anyway.
- Some merchants (hotels, car rental) refuse prepaid cards or place large holds.
- Large cash purchases of cards may be limited by store policy.

## Verify
- [ ] You know which merchants get a gift/virtual card instead of your main card, and their balances.
