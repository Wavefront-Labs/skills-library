# Data portability requests

**Goal:** get your data out of a service in a usable form, to move elsewhere or to see what they hold.

## Rights
- **GDPR Article 20**: the personal data you provided to a controller, in a "structured, commonly used and
  machine-readable format", with the right to transmit it to another controller without hindrance and, where
  technically feasible, to have it transmitted directly. It applies only where processing is based on consent or a
  contract and is carried out by automated means. It does not cover processing for a public-interest task or official
  authority, and must not adversely affect the rights and freedoms of others.
- **CCPA (California)**: the right to know lets you ask a business which categories and specific pieces of personal
  information it collected, the sources, purposes, and the third parties it shares with or sells to, for the 12 months
  before your request, free of charge. The Attorney General's FAQ does not describe a portable-format requirement.
- GDPR Article 15 (access) is a separate right. Make a separate request for it if you want more than the data you
  supplied.

## Use the service's own tool first
Many services offer a download-your-data tool in account or privacy settings. If the choice exists, pick a
machine-readable format over a reading format.

## Write a request when there's no tool
Use the privacy contact or form named in the privacy policy. For California requests, use a designated method of the
business (it must offer at least two, and its privacy policy gives instructions).
> Under Article 20 GDPR, please provide the personal data I supplied to you in a structured, commonly used,
> machine-readable format, including [account data, order history, uploads]. Account email: ...

Keep a copy and the date.

## Deadlines and follow-up
- GDPR Article 12: respond without undue delay and within one month; extendable by two further months where
  necessary, with notice and reasons within the first month. Free of charge, unless requests are manifestly unfounded
  or excessive (then a reasonable fee or refusal, which the controller must justify). If they refuse, they must tell
  you why within one month and of your right to complain to a supervisory authority and seek a judicial remedy.
- CCPA: 45 calendar days, extendable by another 45 (90 total) if the business notifies you.
- No reply: check you used a designated method, follow up in writing, then complain to the supervisory authority
  (GDPR) or, for California, report the business to the Attorney General's office.

## Gotchas
- Controllers may ask for extra information to confirm your identity where they have reasonable doubts (Article 12(6));
  CCPA verification information may only be used for verification. Give the minimum.
