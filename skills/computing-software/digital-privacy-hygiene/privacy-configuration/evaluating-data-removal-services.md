# Evaluating data removal services

**Goal:** pay someone to do the repetitive broker opt-outs, without paying for marketing
claims or handing your data to yet another company.

## What they do
Act as your **authorised agent** to submit opt-outs to a list of brokers, then rescan
periodically and resubmit when you reappear.

## Evaluate
| Question | Why |
|---|---|
| How many brokers, and which? Is the list published? | Numbers are inflated by tiny sites; check the big people-search ones are included |
| Do they show **evidence** (before/after screenshots, removal reports)? | Consumer Reports' 2024 study found manual removal and some services far more effective than others |
| Rescan frequency | Monthly or quarterly; brokers relist |
| What data do they require? | Name, addresses, DOB, phone are needed; avoid services wanting SSN or ID scans |
| Custom removal requests | Can you send them a URL they missed? |
| Their own privacy policy | Do they resell or use your data? |
| Price | Typically yearly subscription |

## Approach
1. Do a manual self-search first to know your baseline.
2. Pick a service whose list covers your worst listings.
3. After 2-3 months, recheck the top sites yourself; submit misses as custom requests.
4. Combine with free government tools where available (California DROP).

## Gotchas
- None remove data from Google itself; use Results about you separately.
- Cancelling the subscription means relisting over time.

## Verify
- [ ] Removal report reviewed; top people-search sites confirmed clear by your own search.
