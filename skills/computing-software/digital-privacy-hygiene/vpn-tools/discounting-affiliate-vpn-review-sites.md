# Discounting affiliate VPN review sites

**Goal:** pick a VPN on evidence, not on who pays the highest referral commission.

## Why rankings are unreliable
- VPN affiliate programmes pay a large share of first-year revenue (often 30-100%+), so "Top 10" pages rank by
  payout.
- Some review sites are owned by VPN companies: Kape Technologies (owner of ExpressVPN, Private Internet Access,
  CyberGhost) bought vpnMentor and WizCase in 2021. A site recommending its owner's brands is an advert.
- Coupon countdowns, "#1 Editor's Choice" badges and identical scores across many sites are marketing signals.

## Checks on a review
- Is there an affiliate disclosure? (US FTC rules require one; absence is a red flag.)
- Who owns the site? Look at the About and legal pages, company registry, and WHOIS history.
- Do they publish test methodology and raw data (speed, leak tests) or just prose?
- Does the review mention weaknesses and when it was last tested?

## Use independent criteria instead
- Privacy Guides' VPN criteria: independent audits, no-logs track record, accepts anonymous payment, open-source
  clients, modern protocols (WireGuard), RAM-only servers.
- Published audit reports from named firms and court cases or raids where no data was found.
- Jurisdiction and ownership you can verify.
- A short trial with your own leak tests.

## Gotchas
- Reddit threads and YouTube sponsorships are also affiliate channels; check for referral codes.
- "Free VPN" lists frequently recommend apps funded by data sales.
