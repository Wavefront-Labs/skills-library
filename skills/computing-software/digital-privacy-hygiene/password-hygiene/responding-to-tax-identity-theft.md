# Responding to tax identity theft

**Goal:** get your real return processed and stop it happening again.

## Signs
- E-file rejected: "a return with this SSN has already been filed".
- IRS letter asking you to verify identity (e.g. Letter 5071C, 4883C, 5747C) for a return you didn't file.
- IRS transcript shows income from an employer you don't work for (employment-related identity theft).
- Unexpected refund or notice of a balance due for a year you didn't file.

## Steps
1. **Verify the letter is real:** go to irs.gov yourself; for verification letters use the official Identity and
   Tax Return Verification Service (idverify.irs.gov) or the phone number listed on irs.gov, not numbers in
   unsolicited messages.
2. If you received a 5071C-type letter for a return you didn't file, tell the IRS through that service.
3. If your e-file was rejected, file on paper with **Form 14039** (Identity Theft Affidavit) attached, or submit
   14039 online.
4. Report at identitytheft.gov (generates a recovery plan, also covers other fraud).
5. Get an **IP PIN** for future years.
6. Freeze credit at all bureaus; check your Social Security earnings record.

## Expect
Resolution can take months; the IRS will contact you by mail. Keep copies of everything.

## Gotchas
- The IRS doesn't initiate contact by email, text or social media about refunds or verification.
- State tax agencies have separate identity theft processes; notify your state too.
