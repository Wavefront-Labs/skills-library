# Digital asset instructions in estate planning

**Goal:** your executor can find, access, close or keep your digital life without breaking laws or terms.

## Legal background
In most US states the Revised Uniform Fiduciary Access to Digital Assets Act (RUFADAA) sets the order: a platform's
own online tool (e.g. Google Inactive Account Manager, Apple Legacy Contact) beats your will, which beats the terms of
service. So set the online tools and mention digital assets in your will. Other countries differ; ask your lawyer.

## The inventory (letter of instruction)
Keep this separate from the will (wills become public in probate):
- Devices and how to unlock them.
- Password manager: which one, and how the executor gets access (emergency access, family organisation, sealed
  paper with the master password and Emergency Kit).
- Email and phone number (the recovery roots).
- Money: banks, brokerages, PayPal, crypto (see seed phrase planning).
- Subscriptions to cancel; domains and hosting to renew or transfer.
- Photos and documents to preserve; accounts to memorialise or delete (Facebook legacy contact, Instagram).
- Business accounts and who takes over.

## Platform tools to set now
Google Inactive Account Manager, Apple Legacy Contact, Facebook legacy contact, password manager emergency access.

## Wishes
State per category: delete, preserve and hand to X, or memorialise. Name a digital executor if your jurisdiction
allows.

## Maintain
Review yearly and when you change phone, email or password manager. Tell the executor where the letter is.

## Gotchas
- Sharing your password directly may breach some terms of service and is not legal authority; the tools and the
  will are.
