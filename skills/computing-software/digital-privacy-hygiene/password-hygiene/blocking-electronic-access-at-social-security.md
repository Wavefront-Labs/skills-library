# Blocking electronic access at Social Security

**Goal:** nobody can open an online account with your SSN to change your direct deposit or address.

## Two approaches
| Approach | When |
|---|---|
| **Create your my Social Security account** (ssa.gov/myaccount, Login.gov or ID.me) and secure it | You want online access yourself; an existing account blocks impostors from creating one |
| **Block electronic access** | You don't want any online/automated access to your record (e.g. victims of identity theft, elderly relatives) |

## Block electronic access
- Request via the SSA page (ssa.gov/myaccount/blockaccess.html) or by calling SSA.
- Effect: nobody (including you) can view or change your information online or through the automated phone service.
- To unblock later you must contact SSA and verify identity (phone or in person).

## Secure the account if you create one
- Login.gov/ID.me with a security key or authenticator app, backup codes stored.
- Turn on email notifications of changes.
- Check direct deposit and address details periodically.

## For older relatives
Combine with: a credit freeze, mail forwarding watch (USPS Informed Delivery), and representative payee rules if
applicable.

## Gotchas
- Blocking doesn't stop paper/in-office fraud; report suspected misuse to SSA OIG.
- Phone scammers impersonating SSA ("your SSN is suspended") are common; SSA doesn't suspend numbers.
