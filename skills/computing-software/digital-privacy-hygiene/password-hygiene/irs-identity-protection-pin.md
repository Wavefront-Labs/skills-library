# IRS Identity Protection PIN

**Goal:** a fraudulent return using your SSN gets rejected because it lacks your 6-digit IP PIN.

## Who
Anyone with an SSN or ITIN who can verify their identity. Parents and legal guardians can request one for a
dependent; dependents under 18 use an alternative enrolment option described on the IRS page.

## Get it
1. On irs.gov, use the IP PIN tool in your IRS online account (register and verify your identity if you have no
   account yet).
2. If you cannot verify online, the IRS page offers Form 15227 (an online application with an income limit on
   the last filed return and telephone access required; read the current limits on the page) or an in-person
   appointment at a Taxpayer Assistance Center (bring photo ID plus one more form of identification).

## Every year
- A new IP PIN is generated each year and is generally viewable in your online account from mid-January.
- Enter it in your tax software or give it to your preparer.

## Protect it
- The IRS will never ask for your IP PIN; a call, email or text asking for it is a scam. Give it only to your tax
  software or preparer.

## Gotchas
- Check the IRS page for opt-out rules before enrolling; the one-time enrolment option ends at the end of that
  calendar year.
