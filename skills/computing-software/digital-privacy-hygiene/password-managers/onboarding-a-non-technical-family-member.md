# Onboarding a non-technical family member

**Goal:** someone who currently uses one password everywhere ends the session with a
working manager they will keep using, and a way to recover if they forget.

## Before the session
- Family plan ready (Bitwarden Families, 1Password Families); you're the organiser.
- Make sure organiser/admin account recovery is enabled where the product supports it
  (1Password family organisers can recover members; Bitwarden needs an Account Recovery
  policy in an organisation), so a forgotten master password isn't fatal.

## The session (about 90 minutes)
1. **Master passphrase together**: 4-5 random words, written on a card for their home
   safe (see
   `computing-software.digital-privacy-hygiene.password-managers.memorizing-a-master-passphrase`).
2. **Install on their phone first**, enable autofill and Face/Touch unlock. Phone is
   where they'll succeed or quit.
3. **Five critical accounts only**: email, phone carrier, main bank, Apple/Google
   account, and the vault itself (2FA). Change each to a generated password, saving
   in the manager **before** submitting.
4. Leave everything else: add sites as they log in over the next weeks ("when you log
   in somewhere, tap Save").
5. **Shared collection** for household items (Wi-Fi, streaming) so they see value immediately.
6. Print the **emergency kit / recovery code** and file it with their important papers.

## Follow-up
- Day 3 phone call: did autofill work? Week 2: visit to fix friction.
- Monthly for 3 months: open the health report together and fix 5 reused passwords.

## Gotchas
- Don't migrate 300 browser passwords on day one; overwhelming them is how adoption fails.
- Explain "if the key icon doesn't offer your bank password, it's not the real bank site".

## Verify
- [ ] They can log into their email on their phone using autofill without help.
- [ ] Recovery path tested: you know how to restore their access.
