# Gift card payment scams

**Goal:** gift cards are the preferred untraceable payment for scammers. One rule covers most cases: legitimate
debts and businesses are never paid with gift cards read over the phone.

## How it shows up
- "Government agency", "utility about to cut power", "tech support" or "grandchild in jail" asks you to buy Apple, Google
  Play, Steam, Target or Amazon cards and read the numbers or send photos of the back.
- **Boss impersonation**: email or text from your "manager" asking you to buy gift cards for staff as a surprise and send
  the codes; uses the real name pulled from LinkedIn.
- Online seller or "romance" contact wants payment in gift cards.
- **Card draining**: cards on store racks are tampered (packaging opened, barcode replaced) so funds loaded at the till
  go to the criminal's card.

## Protect yourself
- If someone asks for payment by gift card, stop: it is a scam.
- Verify boss requests in person or by calling a known number; organisations do not buy staff gifts by text.
- Buy cards from behind the counter or check packaging for slits and stickers; keep the receipt and activation slip.
- Scam warnings at checkout exist because cashiers see this daily; if a cashier asks, tell the truth.

## If you paid
1. Contact the **card issuer** immediately (numbers listed on the FTC gift card scam page) with card number and receipt;
   unspent balances can sometimes be frozen.
2. Keep the cards and receipts.
3. Report at reportfraud.ftc.gov and to the store.
4. Expect follow-up "recovery" scams; ignore them.

## Verify
- [ ] Family and staff know the gift card rule and who to call before buying cards for anyone.
