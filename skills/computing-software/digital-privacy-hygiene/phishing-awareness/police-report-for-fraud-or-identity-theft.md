# Police report for fraud or identity theft

**Goal:** an official record that lets banks, credit bureaus and agencies reverse the damage.

## Order of actions
1. Stop the bleeding first: contact your bank or card issuer, freeze or lock accounts, change passwords and enable MFA, place fraud alerts or credit freezes.
2. File the national fraud report, then a police report if creditors or the law require it.

## Where (verify current process)
- US: IdentityTheft.gov (FTC) builds an Identity Theft Report and a recovery plan; a local police report is often needed in addition for disputing debts, and always for cases involving physical safety or known offenders. FBI IC3 (ic3.gov) for internet crime.
- UK: Action Fraud / the national reporting service, plus your bank and Cifas protective registration.
- Canada: Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre and local police. Australia: ReportCyber and IDCARE.
Other countries: national consumer-protection or cybercrime portal, then local police.

## What to bring
Government ID, proof of address, a dated timeline, account numbers (last digits), statements with fraudulent items, emails/texts/screenshots, transaction ids, and the national report number.

## At the station / on the call
- State it is identity theft or fraud and ask for a written report or reference number; ask for a copy.
- List all affected accounts; supplement the report later if more appear.
- Do not pay anyone claiming they can recover funds for a fee.

## After
Send the report to banks and creditors that dispute charges, to the credit bureaus, and keep a log of every call (date, name, reference). Review credit reports for months.

## Verify
You hold the report numbers, each creditor confirms the dispute in writing, and freezes appear on every bureau.
