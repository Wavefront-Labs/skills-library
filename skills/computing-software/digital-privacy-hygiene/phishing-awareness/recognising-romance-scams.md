# Recognising romance scams

**Goal:** help people (often older or recently bereaved) spot an online partner who
exists only to extract money, without shaming them.

## Patterns (FTC/FBI)
- Profile is attractive, professional; works abroad (oil rig, military deployment,
  doctor with an aid organisation, contractor).
- Moves off the dating app quickly to WhatsApp/Telegram.
- Professes love fast; always a reason they can't meet or video call (or video glitches).
- Money requests start small and grow: travel costs, customs fees, medical emergency,
  locked inheritance, crypto investment opportunity.
- Asks for gift cards, wire, crypto, or to receive and forward packages/money (making the
  victim a money mule).

## Verification steps
- **Reverse image search** the photos (Google Lens, TinEye, Bing); stolen photos often
  belong to influencers or soldiers.
- Ask for a **live video call** with a specific action (wave, say your name). Be aware
  real-time deepfakes exist; spontaneity and length help.
- Search their name + "scam"; look for inconsistencies in stories.

## Sextortion variant
Intimate images exchanged then threats to send them to contacts unless paid. Don't pay
(payment rarely ends it); stop contact, preserve evidence, report to the platform and FBI;
for minors, NCMEC's Take It Down helps remove images.

## Talking to a victim
Lead with care; they may be emotionally invested. Show the evidence (image search results)
rather than argue.

## Verify
- [ ] Any online relationship involving money requests has had photos reverse-searched.
