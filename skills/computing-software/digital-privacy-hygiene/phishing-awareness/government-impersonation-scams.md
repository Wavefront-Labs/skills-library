# Government impersonation scams

**Goal:** fear of arrest, deportation or losing benefits makes people pay quickly. Know what real agencies never do.

## Common scripts
- **Tax**: "IRS/HMRC: you owe back taxes, pay today or police will arrest you."
- **Social Security**: "Your SSN is suspended/linked to crime"; offer to "protect" money.
- **Police/court**: missed jury duty, warrant for your arrest, fine payable now.
- **Customs/immigration**: package with drugs in your name, visa problem.
- **Safe account**: "agency" or "Federal Reserve" tells you to move savings to a safe account, buy gold, or hand cash to a
  courier. Often chained after a fake tech-support or bank-fraud call.

## What real agencies do not do
- Demand immediate payment by **gift card, wire transfer, crypto, Bitcoin ATM, or cash courier**.
- Threaten arrest by phone, or tell you to stay on the line and not tell family or your bank.
- Ask you to move money to "protect" it.
- The IRS normally contacts you first by mail; SSA does not suspend numbers.

## Verify
- Hang up. Look up the agency's official number on its .gov site (not the caller ID or a search ad) and call it.
- Caller ID and badge numbers are spoofed trivially; official-looking letters and photos of IDs are faked too.
- Tell someone you trust; scammers rely on secrecy.

## Report and recover
- Report to the FTC (reportfraud.ftc.gov); IRS impersonation to TIGTA; SSA impersonation to SSA OIG.
- If you paid: contact the payment provider (bank recall, gift card company, crypto exchange) immediately; beware of
  follow-up "recovery" calls promising to get money back for a fee.

- [ ] Household members know: no agency takes gift cards, crypto or cash couriers, and every such call gets a hang-up and callback.
