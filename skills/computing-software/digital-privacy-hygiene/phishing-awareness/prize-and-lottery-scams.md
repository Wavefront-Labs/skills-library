# Prize and lottery scams

**Goal:** spot a fake win quickly, and help an older relative who believes they have won.

## The tell-tale signs
- You "won" a contest or foreign lottery you never entered.
- You must pay to collect: taxes, customs, processing or delivery fees, often by gift card, wire, crypto or cash
  by courier. Legitimate sweepstakes never charge winners.
- Pressure and secrecy: "don't tell anyone", deadline today.
- Calls claiming to be Publishers Clearing House, Mega Millions, or a government "grant" office; PCH notifies big
  winners in person and never asks for money.
- A cheque arrives "to cover the taxes" and you're asked to send part back: it will bounce later (fake cheque scam).

## Respond
- Do not pay, call back, or give bank details. Hang up.
- Check: did you enter? Contact the sweepstakes sponsor using details from its official site.
- Playing foreign lotteries by mail or phone is illegal for US residents; any "foreign lottery win" is a scam.

## If money was sent
1. Contact the payment provider immediately: bank/wire recall, gift card company with card numbers and receipts,
   crypto exchange.
2. Report: reportfraud.ftc.gov; mailed offers to the US Postal Inspection Service.
3. Expect follow-up "recovery" calls offering to get the money back for a fee; also scams.
4. Victims get put on "sucker lists"; change the phone number if calls continue and register on the Do Not Call list.

## Helping a relative
Stay calm and non-judgemental; repeated winners are often groomed over months. Ask their bank about trusted contacts
and transaction alerts.
