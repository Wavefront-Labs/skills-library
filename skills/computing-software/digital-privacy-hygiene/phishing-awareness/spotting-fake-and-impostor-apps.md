# Spotting fake and impostor apps

**Goal:** app store approval is not proof an app is real. Copycats ride on brands (banks, wallets, AI chatbots,
streaming) to steal logins, seed phrases or subscription money.

## Kinds of fakes
- **Impostor brand apps**: fake bank, wallet (e.g. "Ledger Live" copies), or "ChatGPT" apps with similar names and icons.
- **Fleeceware**: real but useless apps (wallpapers, scanners, horoscopes) with a free trial that becomes a large weekly
  subscription.
- **Phishing PWAs/WebAPKs**: a text or ad prompts "install our app" from a website; it lands on the home screen with your
  bank's icon but is a web page harvesting credentials.
- **Fake browser extensions**: copies of popular extensions or "PDF converters" that inject ads or steal sessions.

## How to tell
- Install from a link on the company's official website (reached by typing it), not from search or ads.
- Developer name matches the company exactly and has its other apps; install count and rating history match the brand's size.
- Banking and wallet apps are never distributed via SMS or social media links.
- An "app" that opens with a browser address bar or installed from a website prompt is a web shortcut.
- Wallet apps that ask for your seed phrase to "sync" or "verify" are theft, every time.

## Remove and recover
- Uninstall; on Android check Device admin and Accessibility first if it will not uninstall.
- Cancel subscriptions: iPhone Settings > [name] > **Subscriptions**; Android Play Store > Payments & subscriptions.
  Request refunds through reportaproblem.apple.com or Google Play refund requests.
- If credentials or a seed phrase were entered: change passwords, revoke sessions; move crypto from a seed-compromised wallet immediately.

## Verify
- [ ] Banking/wallet apps installed from links on official sites; no unknown subscriptions.
