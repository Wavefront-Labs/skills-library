# Phishing urgency and authority red flags

**Goal:** catch phishing by its emotional design, which works even when the email looks technically perfect.

## The levers
| Lever | Typical wording |
|---|---|
| Urgency | "within 24 hours", "final notice", "account will be closed today" |
| Authority | CEO, IT department, bank fraud team, police, tax office |
| Fear | "suspicious login", "legal action", "your computer is infected" |
| Reward | refunds, prizes, unclaimed packages, crypto gains |
| Secrecy | "don't tell anyone", "I'm in a meeting, just do this" |
| Unusual channel/payment | gift cards, crypto, wire, "new bank details" |

Any two together = treat as phishing until verified.

## Pause-and-verify routine
1. **Stop**: don't click, reply, or call numbers in the message.
2. **Check the ask**: is it asking for credentials, money, codes, remote access or opening a file?
3. **Verify out of band**: open the app or type the website yourself; call a number you already have.
4. **Ask someone**: a colleague, family member or IT.

## Technical cues (supporting, not primary)
Sender domain mismatch, reply-to different from sender, link text vs actual URL, generic greeting, attachments
you didn't expect. Polished, typo-free messages are now normal (AI writing), so don't rely on spelling errors.

## Gotchas
- Real organisations also send urgent messages; verification, not suspicion alone, resolves it.
- Your password manager not offering to autofill is a strong technical signal; trust it.
