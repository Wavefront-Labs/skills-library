# Sextortion email scams

**Goal:** recognise mass-mailed blackmail and respond without paying.

## The bulk scam
- Claims your device/webcam was hacked and you were recorded; demands bitcoin within 24-48 hours.
- "Proof": an old password from a data breach (in the subject), your email "sent from" your own address (spoofed),
  or a Street View photo of your house with your address (newer variant).
- No actual images or details beyond public/breached data.

## Response
1. Don't reply, don't pay.
2. Check the password at Have I Been Pwned; if you still use it anywhere, change it everywhere and enable MFA.
3. Report to IC3 (US) or your national cybercrime unit; mark as spam.
4. Optional: cover webcams and update devices, but the email is a bluff.

## When it's real (targeted sextortion)
Someone you chatted with (often posing as a peer on social media/games) has real images and threatens to share
them. Common against teenagers.
- Stop contact, don't pay (payment leads to more demands).
- Preserve evidence (usernames, messages), report the account on the platform.
- Report to the FBI (tips.fbi.gov/IC3) or police; for minors, NCMEC CyberTipline and **Take It Down** to block
  images on platforms.
- Tell a trusted adult; it's a crime against the victim, not the victim's fault.

## Gotchas
- Bulk emails reuse bitcoin addresses; searching the address often shows other reports.
