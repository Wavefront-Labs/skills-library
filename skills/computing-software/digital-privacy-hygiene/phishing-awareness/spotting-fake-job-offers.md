# Spotting fake job offers

**Goal:** job seekers are high-value targets: they share ID documents and bank details willingly. Separate real
offers from scams before handing anything over.

## Common patterns
- **Task scams**: paid to "rate apps/products" or "optimise orders"; balance grows in a dashboard but you must deposit
  money (often crypto) to unlock tasks or withdraw. Pure fraud.
- **Equipment cheque**: employer sends a cheque to buy a laptop from "their vendor"; cheque bounces after you pay.
- **Reshipping / package forwarding**: receive and re-ship parcels; goods are bought with stolen cards, you become a mule.
- **Payment processing**: move money through your account for a "client"; money laundering.
- **Upfront fees** for training, certification, background checks or equipment.

## Red flags
- Offer without a real interview, or interview only by text on Telegram, WhatsApp or Signal.
- Unsolicited message for a job you did not apply to, with high pay for little work.
- Recruiter uses Gmail/Outlook or a domain not matching the company's real site.
- Requests for SSN, passport scan, bank login or card details before a written offer from a verifiable company.

## Verify the employer
- Find the job on the company's own careers site, reached by searching the company, not from the message.
- Call the company's main number and ask HR whether the recruiter works there.
- Check the recruiter on LinkedIn: real employees have history and connections at the company.
- Real US employers collect I-9/W-4 details after the offer through HR systems, and never ask you to buy equipment.

## If you engaged
Stop contact, do not deposit cheques or forward goods, tell your bank, freeze credit if you sent ID/SSN, and report to the
FTC and IC3 and to the job site.

- [ ] Every offer traced to the company's official site and a human at its real phone number before sharing personal data.
