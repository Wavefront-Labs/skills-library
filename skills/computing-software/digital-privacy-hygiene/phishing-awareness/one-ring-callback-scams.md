# One-ring callback scams

**Goal:** don't run up premium charges by returning a missed call.

## How it works
The phone rings once from an unfamiliar number, often international or a Caribbean area code that looks domestic
(e.g. 809, 876, 473, 268, 284, 649, 664 in North America), then hangs up. Calling back connects to a premium-rate
line that bills per minute, sometimes with hold music to keep you on.

## Rules
- Don't call back numbers you don't recognise, especially international ones. Real callers leave voicemail or text.
- Look up the area/country code before returning any call.
- Turn on carrier spam blocking and the phone's unknown caller screening (iPhone Silence Unknown Callers / Call
  Screening; Pixel Call Screen).

## Block international premium
Ask your carrier to block international premium-rate or international calling if you never use it.

## If you called back
1. Check your bill for unexpected international/premium charges.
2. Call the carrier and dispute; carriers often reverse first-time charges.
3. Report to the FCC (consumer complaints) / your national regulator.

## Gotchas
- Variants send texts asking you to call a number; same rule applies.
- Some scams spoof local numbers (neighbour spoofing) instead; spam filters help there.
