# Avoiding recovery scams

**Goal:** victims' details are sold and reused. After a loss, offers to recover the money
are usually a second theft.

## Red flags
- Unsolicited contact (or ads targeted at victims) from "asset recovery", "crypto tracing",
  "blockchain forensic" firms, lawyers or "FBI/Interpol agents" who know your case.
- **Upfront fees**, "taxes" or "release fees" before funds are returned.
- Payment demanded in crypto, gift cards or wire.
- Guarantees of recovery; requests for remote access or your wallet seed.
- Fake government letterheads; real agencies don't charge to return money.

## What legitimate recovery looks like
- Your bank, card issuer, or payment provider via their official numbers.
- Law enforcement reports (IC3, local police) that you initiated; they don't charge.
- Court-appointed receivers or regulator-run compensation programmes announced publicly.
- Licensed attorneys you found and verified independently (bar association lookup), not
  ones who contacted you.

## Response
Don't engage; don't pay. Report the contact to FTC/IC3 as part of your existing case.
Warn family members, since victims are re-targeted repeatedly.

## Verify
- [ ] Household understands: no one legitimate charges a fee to return stolen money.
