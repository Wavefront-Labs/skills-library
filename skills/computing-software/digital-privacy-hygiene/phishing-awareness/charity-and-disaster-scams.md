# Charity and disaster scams

**Goal:** make sure donations reach real relief work.

## Red flags
- Appeals that appear hours after a disaster, often via text, social media, or cold calls.
- Names that sound like well-known charities (one word off).
- Pressure to give now, thanks for a donation you don't remember making.
- Requests for gift cards, wire transfers, crypto or cash.
- Vague claims about how the money is used; refusal to give a registered charity number or address.

## Verify before giving
- **US:** IRS Tax Exempt Organization Search (confirms status and deductibility); BBB Wise Giving Alliance
  (give.org), Charity Navigator, CharityWatch for ratings.
- **UK:** Charity Commission register check; **Canada:** CRA charities listing.
- Go to the charity's website yourself; don't use links or numbers from the message.
- Crowdfunding: donate to people you know personally, or to campaigns verified by the platform (GoFundMe has a
  disaster hub with verified fundraisers). Check who controls the payout.

## Paying
Credit card or the charity's official site; never cash or gift cards. Keep receipts for taxes and disputes.

## If you were scammed
Contact your card issuer to dispute; report to reportfraud.ftc.gov and your state attorney general (or Action Fraud
in the UK). Report fake fundraisers to the platform.
