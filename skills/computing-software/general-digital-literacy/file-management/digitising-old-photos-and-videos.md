# Digitise old photos and videos

## Prints
- Quick: **PhotoScan by Google Photos** (phone app) removes glare by combining shots. Good for sharing, not archiving.
- Better: a flatbed scanner at **600 dpi** for prints (1200 dpi for small prints you may enlarge), saved as TIFF or high-quality JPEG. Clean the glass; scan several at once and split with the scanner software or *auto-crop*.
- Bulk: services scan shoeboxes (ScanMyPhotos, Legacybox, local labs); ask about resolution, return of originals and file naming.

## Slides and negatives
- Need a film scanner or flatbed with a transparency lid; scan at **2400-4000 dpi**. A digital camera with a macro lens and light pad is fast and good.

## Video
- VHS/Hi8/MiniDV: a working player plus USB capture device (Elgato Video Capture, generic EasyCAP needs care) into a computer; MiniDV via FireWire captures digitally.
- Cine film (8mm/Super 8) needs professional frame-by-frame scanning.
- Save as **MP4 (H.264)** for viewing; keep a high-bitrate master if possible.
- Tapes degrade; do the oldest/most valued first and don't repeatedly play mouldy tapes (clean professionally).

## Organise
- Name files with dates: `1987-07_Grandma-80th_001.jpg`. Write captions (who, where) into metadata or a text file while relatives can identify people.
- Store copies in two places plus cloud (3-2-1); keep the originals.

## Verify
- [ ] Sample scans viewed at 100% show detail without blur, and all files exist in two backups.
