# Writing clear professional emails

**Goal:** the reader knows what you want and by when after the first two lines.

## Structure
1. **Subject line = the summary**, specific and searchable: "Approval needed: Q3 travel budget by
   Fri 17 Oct", not "Question" or "Hi". Prefixes like `Action:` / `FYI:` help.
2. **Greeting** matching the relationship ("Hi Sam," / "Dear Ms Patel,").
3. **First sentence = the point or request** (bottom line up front): "Could you approve the attached
   travel budget by Friday?"
4. **Context** in short paragraphs or bullets; one topic per email.
5. **Clear next step**: who does what by when. Questions numbered so replies can answer "1. yes".
6. **Sign-off and signature** with name, role and phone.

## Tone
- Polite and neutral; written text reads colder than speech. "Could you…" beats "You need to…".
- No sarcasm or jokes that rely on tone; avoid ALL CAPS and multiple exclamation marks.
- If it's emotional, complex or bad news, call or meet, then confirm by email.

## Before you send
- Re-read on the recipient's behalf: could it be misread? Is anything missing?
- Attachment mentioned? Attach it (Gmail and Outlook warn when "attached" appears with no file).
- Check the **To** field: autocomplete picks the wrong "Sam" surprisingly often.
- Add recipients last so a half-written email can't be sent by accident.

## Gotchas
- Replying to an old thread about a new topic buries it; start a new email with a new subject.
- Long emails get skimmed: if it's over ~5 short paragraphs, consider a document or a call.

## Verify
- [ ] A colleague reading only the subject and first line could tell what you need.
