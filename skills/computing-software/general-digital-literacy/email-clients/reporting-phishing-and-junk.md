# Report phishing and junk

**Goal:** the scam leaves your inbox, the provider learns from it, and your IT team or a national body hears about it.

## Gmail (computer)
- Phishing: open the message > next to Reply click **More** > **Report phishing**. Wrongly flagged: **Report not phishing**.
- Spam: select the email(s) > **Report spam** at the top. Google receives a copy of reported mail. For a sender you signed up to, use Unsubscribe instead.
- Rescue: More > Spam > tick the email > **Not spam**. Adding the sender to Google Contacts or creating a filter also helps; blocking a sender keeps their mail flagged even after you remove it from Spam.

## Outlook
- Outlook.com: select the message > above the reading pane **Report** > **Report phishing**. The sender is reported but not blocked; add them to blocked senders to block.
- Work or school accounts may have a Report button from your IT team; use it so security staff are alerted.
- Outlook mobile (Android, iOS): tap the three dots > **Report Junk** > Junk, Phishing or Block Sender.

## Forward to authorities
- UK: the National Cyber Security Centre says to forward suspicious emails to the address on its "Report a scam email" page (do not click links; no need to forward mail already in spam).
- US: report fraud at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.
- Impersonating a company: also tell that company through its own website, not links in the email.

## Don'ts
- Do not click links, reply, or open attachments in a suspected phishing message.
- If you already clicked or entered a password, change the password directly on the real site, turn on 2-Step Verification and tell IT or your bank.
