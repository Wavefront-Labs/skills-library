# Forward vs reply and forwarding etiquette

## What each does
- **Reply**: to the sender only. Attachments are **not** included.
- **Reply all**: sender plus everyone on To and Cc (Bcc recipients are never included). Attachments not included.
- **Forward**: to new people you choose; **attachments are included** and the whole thread below is quoted.

## Before forwarding
- Scroll the entire quoted thread: earlier messages may contain prices, personal data, internal comments or the sender's frank views. Delete what the new person shouldn't see.
- Ask yourself if the original sender would expect this to be passed on; ask them if unsure, especially for confidential or personal mail.
- Remove attachments that aren't needed (or the old "Re: Re: Fwd:" clutter).
- Add 1-3 lines at the top: why you're forwarding, what you need, by when. "FYI" alone wastes their time.

## Reply all discipline
- Use it when everyone needs the answer or a decision. Otherwise reply only to the sender.
- Moving people to Bcc ("moving Sam to Bcc") lets them drop off a thread politely.
- Outlook can block Reply all on a message via add-ins or *Respond > No Reply All* in some tenants.

## Forward as attachment
- Needed when reporting phishing or sending an exact copy with headers; see the forward-as-attachment skill.

## Gotchas
- Messages marked Confidential/encrypted, or sent with Do Not Forward (Microsoft Purview), can't be forwarded.
- Auto-forwarding rules to external addresses are often blocked by IT, and are a sign of account compromise if you didn't set one.
