# Citations and bibliography in Word and Google Docs

## Word
1. **References › Style**: built-in styles include APA, Chicago, GOST, IEEE, ISO 690 and MLA (check
   your institution's edition).
2. Cursor at the end of the sentence › **Insert Citation › Add New Source** › Type of Source (book,
   website…) › fill the fields (*Show All Bibliography Fields* for more) › OK. **Add New Placeholder**
   lets you cite now and fill the details later.
3. Reuse: Insert Citation lists sources already added. **Manage Sources** shows the *Master List*
   (sources from all your documents) and the *Current List* (this document); search or sort to find one.
4. Page numbers: click the citation › Citation Options › **Edit Citation**.
5. End of document: **References › Bibliography** › choose a format (e.g. Bibliography or Works Cited).
6. After citing a new source, click the bibliography › **Update Citations and Bibliography**.

## Google Docs
**Tools › Citations** › choose MLA (8th ed.), APA (7th ed.) or Chicago Author-Date (17th ed.) ›
**+ Add citation source** (a URL or ISBN can look up details) › hover a source › **Cite** to insert.
Replace any "#" placeholder with page numbers. At the bottom of the sidebar click **Insert bibliography**.
Generated elements such as the "Bibliography" heading appear in English whatever the document language.

## When to use a reference manager
Many sources, a thesis, or shared group work: a reference manager with a word-processor plug-in
handles style rules more reliably.

## Gotchas
- A works cited list holds sources you cited; a bibliography can include sources you consulted.
- Proofread generated entries against the current edition of the style guide.
- A web page still needs author or organisation, date, title and URL.
