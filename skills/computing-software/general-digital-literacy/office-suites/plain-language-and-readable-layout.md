# Plain language and readable layout

Plain language means content that is clear and easy to understand for its specific audience (US Plain Writing Act of 2010 principle, per digital.gov). The advice below is general good practice; test it on real readers.

## Writing
- Lead with the point: what the reader must do or know, then the detail.
- Keep sentences short, one idea each, in active voice.
- Use everyday words, address the reader as "you", and define acronyms on first use or avoid them.
- Write dates unambiguously, for example "4 March 2026".

## Layout
- Real heading styles every few paragraphs; bulleted lists for several items, numbered lists for steps.
- Left-aligned text and a readable size; avoid ALL CAPS, underlines that are not links, and meaning carried by colour alone.
- Keep tables simple.

## Measure
Word (Microsoft 365): **Home > Editor > Document stats**. Other Windows versions: **File > Options > Proofing**, tick **Check grammar with spelling** and **Show readability statistics**, then run Spelling & Grammar. Mac: Word > Preferences > Spelling & Grammar. Microsoft's targets: Flesch Reading Ease between 60 and 70 for most files, and a Flesch-Kincaid Grade Level around 7.0 to 8.0.

## Test
Ask someone outside the topic to read it and say what to do next. If they cannot, rewrite.
