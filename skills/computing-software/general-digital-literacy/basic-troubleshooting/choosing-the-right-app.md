# Choose the right app for a need

**Goal:** a tool that does the job, is safe, and won't trap your data or wallet.

## 1. Define the need
Write 3-5 must-haves (e.g. "edit PDFs, sign them, works on Windows and iPhone, under £5/month"). Nice-to-haves come second.

## 2. Check what you already have
Built-in tools cover many needs: Microsoft 365/Google Workspace (documents, forms, notes), Photos apps (basic editing), Preview/Edge (PDF signing), Clipchamp/iMovie (video), Notes/OneNote, password managers built into browsers. Your school, library or employer may licence software for free.

## 3. Shortlist 2-3 options
- Official store listings and reputable review sites; recent reviews from people with your needs.
- Open-source options (LibreOffice, GIMP, Audacity, VLC, Bitwarden) are free and often excellent.

## 4. Vet each
| Check | Look for |
|---|---|
| Publisher | Known company or active project; real website and support |
| Platforms | Works on all your devices; web version for shared computers |
| Cost model | One-off, subscription, freemium limits, auto-renewing trials; family/education discounts |
| Privacy | Privacy label (App Store), data safety section (Play); does it sell data or require broad permissions? |
| Data portability | Exports to standard formats (DOCX, PDF, CSV, JPG); avoid lock-in |
| Security | Updated recently; 2-step sign-in for cloud apps |
| Accessibility | Works with screen readers, keyboard, captions if you need them |

## 5. Trial
Use the free tier or trial on a real task; set a calendar reminder to cancel before it renews.

## Gotchas
- Search ads mimic real downloads; go to the publisher's site or official store.
- "Free" apps with heavy ads or data collection cost in other ways.

## Verify
- [ ] Chosen app meets every must-have in a real test; you know how to export and cancel.
