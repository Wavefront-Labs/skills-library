# Getting remote help safely

**Goal:** get hands-on help over the internet without handing your computer to a criminal.

## Windows Quick Assist (built in)
1. Both open **Quick Assist** (Start › type Quick Assist, or `Ctrl+Win+Q`).
2. The **helper** clicks *Help someone* and signs in with a Microsoft account; they get a 6-character
   **security code** valid for 10 minutes.
3. The **person being helped** enters the code under *Get help* and chooses *Allow* (view only or
   full control).
4. Either can end the session any time (Leave / close the window).

## Mac
Messages or FaceTime screen sharing (*Share My Screen* / *Ask to Share*), or Screen Sharing app with
an Apple Account. Work IT may use their own tools (Intune, TeamViewer, AnyDesk, Zoom remote control).

## Safety rules
- **You start the contact.** Legitimate companies (Microsoft, Apple, banks, broadband providers)
  don't phone or pop up to say your computer is infected and ask for remote access.
- Only allow people you know or IT staff you contacted through a known number.
- Watch the screen the whole time; don't leave them alone with your online banking open.
- Never let a remote helper see you log in to banking or ask you to "check a refund" there.
- After a suspicious session: disconnect from the internet, uninstall any remote tools they added,
  run a full scan, change passwords from another device, call your bank.

## Verify
- [ ] Quick Assist session ended (no app in the taskbar); no unknown remote-access apps installed.
