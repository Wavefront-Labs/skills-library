# Recycle e-waste and extend device life

**Goal:** get the most years from a device, then make sure it's reused or recycled, not binned.

## Extend life first
- **Battery replacement** often restores an old phone or laptop for a fraction of a new one's price.
- **Storage:** clean up, or upgrade an older laptop's hard drive to an SSD for a big speed boost; add RAM where possible.
- **Software:** a fresh reset can revive a slow device; ChromeOS Flex or Linux can extend a PC that no longer gets Windows updates.
- Cases and screen protectors prevent most fatal damage.
- Repair before replace: check repair guides (iFixit) and local repair cafes.

## When it's time to go
1. **Back up and wipe** (see wiping a device before selling): sign out of Find My/Google account, factory reset, remove SIM and memory cards.
2. Choose a route:
   - **Trade-in:** Apple Trade In, Samsung, carriers and retailers give credit; even broken devices may be accepted for recycling.
   - **Donate:** charities refurbish laptops and phones for schools and people without devices.
   - **Sell:** marketplace or refurbishers.
   - **Recycle:** retailer take-back (in the UK, sellers of electrical goods must offer take-back or fund local schemes; US: Best Buy and manufacturer mail-back), council recycling centres, certified recyclers (R2 or e-Stewards in the US).
3. Get a certificate of data destruction for business equipment.

## Batteries and hazardous items
- Lithium batteries cause bin and truck fires; never put them in household waste or recycling. Use battery drop-off points in shops. Tape over terminals.
- Swollen batteries: take to a recycling centre promptly; don't charge.
- Old CRT monitors, printers with toner, and fluorescent-backlit screens go to recycling centres.

## Verify
- [ ] Device wiped and removed from your accounts; receipt or confirmation from the recycler or trade-in programme kept.
