# Read statistics and charts critically

## Questions for any number
- **Source?** Who measured it, how, and when? No source = treat as opinion.
- **Compared with what?** "10,000 cases" means little without the population and last year's figure.
- **Relative vs absolute**: "doubles the risk" could be 1 in 10,000 to 2 in 10,000. Ask for the absolute numbers.
- **Average**: mean vs median (income averages are skewed by a few high earners).
- **Sample**: size and how chosen. Online polls of volunteers aren't representative. Margin of error matters for close results.
- **Correlation vs causation**: ice cream sales and drownings rise together (summer).
- **Survivorship and selection**: "every successful founder dropped out" ignores the dropouts who failed.

## Chart tricks
- **Truncated y-axis** making small changes look huge (bar charts should start at zero).
- Dual axes with chosen scales making two lines appear linked.
- **Cherry-picked time window**: start and end dates chosen to show a trend.
- Cumulative totals that can only go up; per-capita vs totals; 3D pies distorting slices.
- Map colours by area exaggerating large, sparsely populated regions.

## Checking
- Find the original dataset (ONS, Census Bureau, Eurostat, WHO, Our World in Data) and see if the claim matches.
- Fact-checkers (Full Fact, BBC Verify, FactCheck.org) regularly debunk viral stats.

## Verify
- [ ] Before sharing a statistic, you can name its source, base number and time period.
