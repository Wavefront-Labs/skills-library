# Deal with online harassment and cyberbullying

**Goal:** make it stop, stay safe, and keep evidence in case you need to escalate.

## Immediately
1. **Don't reply** or retaliate; harassers want a reaction.
2. **Document:** screenshots showing username, profile link, date/time and the message; save URLs. Full-page screenshots for threads.
3. **Block** the account; on Instagram/TikTok use **Restrict** to hide their comments without them knowing.
4. **Report** on the platform (⋯ › Report on any post, message or profile); choose harassment/bullying categories. Report each item, not just the account.

## Lock down
- Make accounts private; limit who can comment, tag, message or mention you.
- Filter words: Instagram *Hidden words*, X *Muted words*, YouTube blocked words.
- Remove location data, workplace and family details; check what's public (search your name).
- Change passwords and turn on 2-step verification if accounts may be compromised.
- **Doxxing** (your address or phone posted): report urgently, tell people you live with, consider police.

## Children and teens
- Stay calm, listen, praise them for telling you; don't remove their devices as punishment (they stop telling).
- Save evidence, report on the platform, inform the school (most have anti-bullying policies covering online behaviour).
- Support: Childline (UK 0800 1111), StopBullying.gov (US).

## When it's a crime
Threats of violence, stalking, sharing intimate images without consent, hate crime, or sustained harassment: report to police (UK 101 or 999 in danger; US local police/FBI IC3). Intimate images: **StopNCII.org** helps get them removed; under-18s: Report Remove (UK) / Take It Down (NCMEC).

## Look after yourself
Mute notifications, have someone else review reports, talk to friends or support services.
