# Close old accounts

**Goal:** fewer forgotten accounts holding your data and reused passwords waiting to be breached.

## Find them
- Search your email for "welcome", "verify your email", "your account", "receipt".
- Your password manager or browser's saved passwords list.
- "Sign in with Google/Apple" lists: your Google Account's linked apps page; Apple Settings › your name › Sign in with Apple. Also check Facebook and other social logins in their settings.
- **haveibeenpwned.com**: which breached sites had your email.

## Before deleting
- **Download your data** (photos, posts, order history) if you want it: look for *Download your information/Export*.
- Cancel subscriptions and remove saved cards first; deleting an app doesn't cancel billing.
- Move any credit or gift balance.
- Accounts used to sign in elsewhere (Google, Apple, Facebook, Microsoft): deleting them breaks those logins and email. Google says deleting an account loses its email, files, calendars, photos and Play purchases and you cannot reuse the Gmail username; download data first and add a new email address to banking or social accounts that used the Gmail address.
- Google and Microsoft can also close accounts that go unused: Google reserves the right to delete a personal account inactive for at least two years; Microsoft counts an account inactive after two years without sign-in. Signing in keeps it active.

## Delete
- Settings › Account / Privacy › **Delete account** (not just *Deactivate*, which keeps data).
- Hard to find? **JustDeleteMe** (justdeleteme.xyz) lists direct links and difficulty ratings.
- No option: email the company's privacy contact asking for erasure under data-protection law (UK/EU GDPR right to erasure; California CCPA right to delete). They must normally reply within one calendar month (UK; extendable by up to two further months for complex requests) or 45 calendar days (California, extendable by 45 days if they tell you).
- Many services hold a grace period so you can change your mind. Examples: Microsoft offers a 30- or 60-day reopen window you choose when closing, and Google says a recently deleted account may be recoverable for a limited time. Read the service's own page before relying on it.

## If you can't delete
Remove personal details, change the password to a random one, delete payment cards, and unlink connected accounts.
