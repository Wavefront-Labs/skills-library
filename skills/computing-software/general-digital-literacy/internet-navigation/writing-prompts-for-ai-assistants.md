# Writing effective prompts

**Goal:** useful output on the first or second try.

## A good prompt contains
1. **Goal:** what you want ("Draft a polite email declining a meeting").
2. **Context:** who it's for, background, what you've tried ("to a client I've worked with for years;
   I can offer next Thursday instead").
3. **Constraints:** length, tone, reading level, what to avoid ("under 120 words, warm, no jargon").
4. **Format:** bullets, table, steps, headings, a draft plus three subject lines.
5. **Examples** of what good looks like, or paste the text to work on.

Example: "Summarise the attached tenancy agreement for a first-time renter. List: rent, deposit,
notice period, who pays bills, anything unusual. Quote the clause number for each. Flag anything you're
unsure about."

## Iterate
- Ask for changes in follow-ups: "shorter", "more formal", "give 3 alternatives", "explain step 2".
- Ask it to ask you questions first: "Before answering, ask me what you need to know."
- Ask for reasoning or sources when facts matter, then check them (see verify skill).
- Start a new chat for a new topic; long threads drift.

## Don't
- Paste passwords, client data or confidential documents into consumer tools (see AI privacy skill).
- Rely on it for current events or exact figures without checking.

## Verify
- [ ] The output meets each constraint you set; facts are checked against sources.
