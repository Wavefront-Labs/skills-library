# Workstation ergonomics

**Goal:** work at a computer for hours without aches, numbness or sore eyes.

## Chair and posture
- Feet flat on the floor or a footrest; knees about level with hips.
- Lower back supported (lumbar support or cushion); sit back in the chair.
- Elbows at about 90°, close to the body; shoulders relaxed.

## Screen
- Top of the screen at or slightly below **eye level**; about an arm's length away.
- Directly in front of you, not to the side; two monitors: main one central, or both centred on the join if used equally.
- Avoid glare: screen at right angles to windows; adjust brightness to the room; dark mode if it helps.
- Text size comfortable without leaning in (zoom/display scaling).

## Keyboard and mouse
- Keyboard close, wrists straight and floating while typing (rests are for pauses).
- Mouse right next to the keyboard, same height; switch hands or try a vertical mouse if the wrist hurts.
- Learn keyboard shortcuts to reduce mouse use.

## Laptops
Laptop alone forces you to look down or reach up. For regular use: laptop **stand** (screen at eye level) + separate keyboard and mouse.

## Breaks and eyes
- **20-20-20:** every 20 minutes look at something 20 feet (6 m) away for 20 seconds; blink.
- Short break or change of activity every hour; stand or walk during calls.
- Employers in the UK must assess display screen equipment workstations and pay for an eye test for regular users who ask.

## Warning signs
Persistent pain, tingling or numbness in hands (possible repetitive strain injury or carpal tunnel), headaches or blurred vision: adjust the setup and see a GP/optician; ask your employer for a DSE assessment.

- [ ] Eyes level with the top of the screen, wrists straight, feet supported; break reminders set.
