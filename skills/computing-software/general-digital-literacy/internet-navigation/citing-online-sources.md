# Citing online sources

**Goal:** readers can find what you used, and you give credit (avoiding plagiarism).

## What to record when you find a source
Author (person or organisation), date published or updated, title, website or publisher, URL, and the date you accessed it for pages that change.

## Formats
Style rules (APA, Harvard, MLA and others) differ in punctuation, ordering and whether an access date is required. Use the style guide your school, employer or publisher requires and follow its web page example exactly.

## Tools
- **Word:** on the References tab, in the Citations group, pick the Style (for example APA or MLA). Click at the end of the cited text, click **Insert Citation**, then **Add new source**. In the Create Source dialog choose the type of source (for example a website), enter the details and click OK.
- **Google Docs:** click **Tools**, then **Citations**. In the sidebar choose MLA, APA or Chicago Author-Date, click **+ Add citation source**, choose the source type and how you accessed it, and fill in the details. The generated bibliography heading and some elements appear in English whatever your language.

## Gotchas
- Cite the original source, not the search engine, AI chatbot answer or the aggregator that quoted it.
- Paraphrasing still needs a citation; direct quotes need quotation marks plus page or paragraph.
- Save or archive important pages in case they change or disappear.
