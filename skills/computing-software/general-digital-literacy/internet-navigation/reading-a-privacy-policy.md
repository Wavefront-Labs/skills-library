# Read a privacy policy

**Goal:** decide in five minutes whether an app or site's data practices are acceptable to you.

## Shortcut: privacy labels
- **App Store:** app page › *App Privacy*: "Data Used to Track You", "Data Linked to You", "Data Not Linked to You".
- **Google Play:** app page › *Data safety*: data shared, data collected, encrypted in transit, can request deletion.
- These are self-declared by developers; the policy is the fuller version.

## What to search for (`Ctrl+F` in the policy)
| Search for | What you learn |
|---|---|
| "collect" | What data: contacts, location, photos, health, voice, browsing |
| "share", "third part", "partners" | Who else gets it: advertisers, data brokers, affiliates |
| "sell" | Whether data is sold (US policies must say under some state laws) |
| "retain" / "retention" | How long it's kept |
| "location" | Precise vs approximate, always vs when in use |
| "children" | Age limits and parental consent |
| "AI", "train", "machine learning" | Whether your content trains models; opt-out route |
| "delete", "rights", "opt out" | How to access, delete or limit use |
| "transfer" | Which countries your data goes to |

## Red flags
- Vague "we may share with partners for any purpose".
- Collection out of proportion to the service (a torch app wanting contacts).
- No way to delete your account or data.
- Policy changes "at any time without notice".

## After reading
- Use privacy settings and opt-outs immediately (ad personalisation, AI training, location).
- Deny unnecessary app permissions.
- Choose a more private alternative if the deal is bad.
