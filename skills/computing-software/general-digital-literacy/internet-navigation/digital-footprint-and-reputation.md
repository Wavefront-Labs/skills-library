# Digital footprint and reputation — find what's out there, then actually remove it

**Goal:** see what a stranger, employer or scammer can find about you with a basic
search, and use the legitimate removal channels that exist — rather than either ignoring
it or paying a "reputation management" service for things you can do yourself for free.

## 1. Auditing yourself
- Search your own full name in quotes, plus variants (maiden name, nicknames, old
  usernames), in a **private/incognito window** (so personalised results from your own
  search history don't hide what a stranger would actually see).
- Check **Google Images** for your name and for photos you know exist of you, to catch
  re-shared or tagged photos you didn't post yourself.
- Search your **phone number and home address** separately — these turn up on
  people-search/data-broker sites even when your name search looks clean.
- Check what your main social accounts show to someone **not logged in as your friend**
  — log out, or use a private window, and view your own profile as the public would see
  it (see social-media-privacy-and-safety for the settings that control this).

## 2. Old content you posted yourself
- Old forum posts, old blog entries, an old social account you forgot about: these are
  the easiest to deal with because you control them directly — delete the post, or
  deactivate/delete the old account from inside it.
- A genuinely abandoned account with content you can no longer log into: most platforms
  have an **account recovery** flow to regain access specifically so you can delete it
  (see email-account-security-and-recovery for the general recovery pattern) — try that
  before assuming it's permanently stuck.

## 3. Content someone else posted about you
- Ask the person directly first where that relationship exists — most unwanted tags,
  photos or posts from someone you know get removed faster by asking than by reporting.
- If that fails or isn't possible: most platforms have a **"report this content" or
  "request removal"** flow (see social-media-privacy-and-safety, section 4) — use the
  specific category that matches (harassment, impersonation, private information) rather
  than a generic report, which is usually slower to act on.

## 4. Removing yourself from search results and data brokers
- **Google's "Results about you"** tool (g.co/results-about-you, or search "remove my
  information from Google") lets you request removal of search results showing
  personal contact information (phone, address, email) and some other sensitive
  categories — this removes it from Google's *search results*, not from the original
  site hosting it; you still need to contact that site separately to remove the source.
- **People-search/data-broker sites** (Spokeo, BeenVerified, WhitePages and similar)
  compile public records into profiles; most have an **opt-out form**, usually found by
  searching "[site name] opt out." This is manual, per-site, and some sites re-list
  data after a period — treat it as a task to repeat periodically, not a one-time fix.
  Paid "data removal" services automate exactly this same manual opt-out process across
  many sites — worth the cost only if you value the time saved, not because they access
  anything you couldn't request yourself for free.
- Residents of the EU/UK and some US states have a legal **right to request removal**
  of certain data under GDPR/CCPA-style laws — look for a "Privacy requests" or
  "Do Not Sell My Info" link, typically required in those sites' footers for a
  jurisdiction's residents.

## 5. What can't realistically be removed
Court records, news coverage of a genuine newsworthy event, and anything legitimately
in the public record generally won't be removed by any request — "right to be
forgotten" style laws, where they apply, have specific tests (e.g. public interest)
that don't cover simply disliking that something true and newsworthy is findable. Don't
expect a removal request to succeed here; manage it through context instead (e.g. more
recent, positive content outranking an old story over time) rather than removal.

## Verify
- [ ] You've searched your own name, images, phone number and address in a private
      window, as a stranger would see it.
- [ ] Old content you control (forgotten accounts, old posts) has been reviewed and
      cleaned up or deleted.
- [ ] You've submitted at least one real opt-out (Google results-about-you or a
      data-broker opt-out form) rather than only noting it as a problem.
- [ ] You can tell the difference between "this will come down with a request" and
      "this is public record and won't."
