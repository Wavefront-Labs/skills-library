# Disclose AI use at school and work

## Find the rules first
- **School/university**: the assessment brief and course policy may allow AI for brainstorming but not writing, or ban it entirely. When unclear, ask the tutor in writing.
- **Work**: organisation AI policy (approved tools, banned data types, disclosure for client deliverables).
- **Publishers, journals, grant bodies**: most require a statement; AI can't be listed as an author.
- **Job applications**: some employers ask; dishonesty about AI-written answers can end an application.

## How to disclose
- Brief statement: "I used ChatGPT (GPT-4o, OpenAI) to suggest an outline and check grammar. All analysis and wording are my own."
- Citations when you quote or rely on AI output:
  - APA: OpenAI. (2025). *ChatGPT* (version) [Large language model]. https://chat.openai.com
  - MLA: describe the prompt, tool, version, date.
- Some institutions want the prompts and outputs in an appendix.

## Protect yourself
- Keep drafts and version history (Google Docs/Word version history shows your writing process) in case work is questioned. AI detectors are unreliable and produce false positives, especially for non-native English writers.
- Verify any facts or references from AI; invented citations are misconduct regardless of intent.
- Don't paste confidential data, student data or unpublished work into unapproved tools.
