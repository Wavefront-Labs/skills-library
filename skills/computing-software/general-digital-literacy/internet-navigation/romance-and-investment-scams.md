# Romance and investment scams

**Goal:** spot the pattern early, and protect yourself or a relative from losing savings. This page follows US FTC guidance and one UK regulator tool; use your own country's regulator elsewhere.

## Romance scam signs (FTC)
- You met on a dating site or app and they quickly want to move to email, calls or text.
- They say it is love fast but live far away, travel often, work on an oil rig, serve in the military or work for an international organisation, and always have a reason they cannot meet in person.
- After building trust they invent an urgent story: medical bills, a plane ticket, visa fees or legal trouble.
- They offer to help you start investing in cryptocurrency.
- Their profile photo appears under other names or does not match their details.

## Payment methods scammers ask for
Wire transfers, gift cards (they ask for the PIN codes), money transfer apps and cryptocurrency.

## Checks
- Run a reverse image search on their profile photo, and search their job plus "scammer".
- Talk to a friend or family member you trust before sending anything.
- For an investment firm in the UK, use the FCA Firm Checker, and the FCA Warning List for firms running scams; type the FCA address yourself rather than following links from emails or company sites. Call the FCA on 0800 111 6768 if contact details are missing or out of date.

## Rules
- Never send money or gifts to someone you haven't met in person.
- Never install apps or send codes at a contact's request.

## If it has happened
1. Stop communicating with the person.
2. If you paid by gift card, wire, card or cryptocurrency, contact the company or bank immediately, say you paid a scammer and ask for a refund.
3. Report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov and to the platform where you met them.
4. Keep evidence: chats, wallet addresses, transaction IDs.

- [ ] Contact and any investment firm checked with an official source before any money is sent, or none sent.
