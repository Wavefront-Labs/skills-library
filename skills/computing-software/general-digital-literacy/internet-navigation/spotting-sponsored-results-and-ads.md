# Spotting sponsored results and native ads

**Goal:** know when a result or article is paid placement.

## In search results
- Google (announced 13 October 2025) groups text ads under a "Sponsored results" label that stays visible as you scroll, and labels Shopping ads "Sponsored products". A "Hide sponsored results" control collapses the text ads. Other search engines use their own labels; read them.
- Scammers buy ads for brand names (login pages, support phone numbers, software downloads). Prefer the organic result or type the address you already know.
- Copycat sites charge for services that are free or cheaper on the official site. In the US, federal government websites often end in `.gov` or `.mil`; confirm your own country's official domain from a government source you already trust.

## On websites and social media
- Native ads and sponsored content carry labels such as "Sponsored", "Promoted", "Paid partnership" or "Advertorial"; link grids at the bottom of news articles are often ads.
- Affiliate review sites earn commission when you buy; look for a disclosure and compare several reviewers.
- Missing labels do not prove content is unpaid.

## Gotchas
- An ad's displayed URL can look official; check the address bar after you click.
- Phone numbers in sponsored results for customer service are a common scam route; get support numbers from the company's own site, app or card.
- Scammers push unusual payment methods and urgency; see the FTC's four signs of a scam (impersonation, prize or problem, pressure to act now, unusual payment).
