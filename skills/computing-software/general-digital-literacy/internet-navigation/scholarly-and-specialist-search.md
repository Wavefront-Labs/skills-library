# Scholarly and specialist search

**Goal:** evidence from peer-reviewed research and authoritative databases, not just top web results.

## Google Scholar (scholar.google.com)
- Left sidebar: **Since [year]** shows only recent papers sorted by relevance; **Sort by date** shows the newest first. The envelope icon emails you new results for a search.
- **Cited by** lists newer papers that referenced this one (often more specific); **Related articles** and **All versions** (alternative sources, sometimes a free copy) sit under each result.
- A **[PDF]** or **[HTML]** link to the right of a result is a readable version of the article.
- **Cite** under a result gives a formatted citation and export to BibTeX, EndNote, RefMan or RefWorks. Check it against your style guide.
- Library access links such as "Find it@" your institution appear for participating libraries; they are managed on the Scholar settings page.

## Specialist databases
- **PubMed** (health and medicine): filters include article types such as Clinical Trial and Review, and text availability filters for full text and free full text.
- **DOAJ** (directory of open access journals) and **CORE** (open access research papers) for open-access material.
- Legislation, statistics and patents: use your government's official legislation and statistics sites and a patent office database; confirm each is the official source.

## Getting full text legally
- Follow [PDF]/[HTML] and All versions links in Scholar.
- Use your public or university library's e-resources.
- Ask the author for a copy.

## Reading critically
- Preprints are not peer-reviewed yet; reviews and meta-analyses outweigh single studies.
- Check a journal's reputation (for example whether DOAJ lists it) to avoid predatory journals.
