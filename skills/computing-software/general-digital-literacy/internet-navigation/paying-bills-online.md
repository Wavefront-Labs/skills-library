# Pay bills online

## Methods
- **Direct debit (UK) / autopay (US)**: the company pulls the amount due. Protected by the **Direct Debit Guarantee** in the UK (immediate refund for errors). Set up via the provider's website or app.
- **Standing order / recurring transfer**: you push a fixed amount from your bank; you control it but must update amounts.
- **Card payment** on the provider's website or app; credit cards add purchase protections but some billers charge fees.
- **Bank bill pay** (US online banking) or **bank transfer** using the payee's account details.

## Safely
- Pay through the provider's **official app or website** typed in yourself, not links in texts or emails.
- New payee by bank transfer: UK **Confirmation of Payee** shows whether the name matches the account; heed a no-match warning.
- Bank details "changed" by email or text: call the company on a known number before paying (invoice redirection fraud).
- Keep confirmation numbers or screenshots of payments.

## Stay on top
- Calendar reminders a few days before due dates; enable bill alerts in the banking app.
- Check bills when they arrive even on autopay; errors and price rises pass silently.
- Keep a buffer in the account for direct debits to avoid fees.

## Gotchas
- Cancelling a direct debit at the bank doesn't cancel the contract; tell the company too.
- Utility "pay now or be disconnected" calls demanding immediate payment by gift card or crypto are scams.

## Verify
- [ ] Each regular bill has a payment method, a due-date reminder, and appears on your statement as expected.
