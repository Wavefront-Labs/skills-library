# Find free-to-use images and media

**Goal:** a picture for a presentation, poster or website that you're allowed to use, credited properly.

## Where to look
| Source | What the licence says |
|---|---|
| **Wikimedia Commons** | each file's description page links the full licence text; if several are offered you may use any. Credit the original author, not just the uploader |
| **Unsplash** | images can be downloaded and used free for commercial and non-commercial purposes with no permission needed (attribution appreciated); images cannot be sold without significant modification, and you may not compile them to replicate a competing service |

## Search engine filters
In Google Images on Android, tap **Filter**, then **Usage rights**, then a licence type; Creative Commons images are usually free to use but require credit. Google says to always confirm an image's licence information, because it relies on information from hosting sites that may be inaccurate. Open the source page and confirm the licence there.

## Read the licence
- **CC0:** a public domain dedication. **CC BY:** credit the creator. **SA:** share adaptations on the same terms. **NC:** noncommercial only. **ND:** no adaptations.
- Credit format: **T**itle, **A**uthor, **S**ource link, **L**icence link.
- Public domain files may not legally require attribution, but giving the original source is recommended.

## Gotchas
- Other restrictions may apply, including trademarks and the privacy and personality rights of people shown; commercial use of images of people may need the subject's agreement.
- Wikimedia gives no warranty that a file's licensing information is correct.
- "Found on Google" is not permission.

## Verify
- [ ] Every image has a recorded source and a licence that allows your use; credits included where required.
