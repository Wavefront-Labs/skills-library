# Request your data from a company

**Goal:** find out what a company knows about you (for a complaint, dispute or just curiosity), and get a copy.

## Your rights
- **UK GDPR:** right of access via a **subject access request (SAR)**; anyone can make one without a solicitor, and organisations usually have **one month** to respond (the ICO's SAR page says its guidance is under review after the Data (Use and Access) Act, so check it for changes). The EU GDPR has a similar right.
- **California (CCPA):** right to know; businesses must respond within **45 calendar days**, extendable by another 45 days if they tell you (California Attorney General).
- Elsewhere check your national privacy regulator.

## Self-service first
Many services have download or data-request tools in their account or privacy settings; look there before writing a formal request.

## Make the request
1. Find the company's privacy contact (privacy policy › "Data Protection Officer" or privacy email/form).
2. Write (ICO suggests a subject line saying "subject access request", the date, and details to identify you): who you are, account/customer number, what you want (all personal data, or specific: call recordings, CCTV for a date, emails mentioning you), preferred format (electronic).
3. Send by email or their form; keep a copy with the date.
4. They may ask for proof of identity; respond promptly.

## Templates
The ICO's SAR page says what to include in a request and has a complaint letter or email template.

## If they don't reply or refuse
- Chase in writing after the deadline.
- Complain to the regulator: **ICO** (UK), your EU data protection authority, or the California Attorney General.
- Some data can be withheld (other people's data, legal privilege); they should explain.
