# Copyright takedown requests

This page covers the US DMCA process (17 U.S.C. section 512) and YouTube's strike policy. Laws elsewhere differ; for a serious dispute consult a qualified lawyer.

## When your work is copied
1. Collect evidence: URLs of the copy, your original with date, screenshots.
2. Use the platform's own copyright report form where one exists.
3. For a standalone website, send a notification to the host's designated agent. Under section 512(c)(3) an effective notification is written and includes a physical or electronic signature, identification of the work and the infringing material, your contact information, a statement of good-faith belief that the use is not authorised, and a statement that the information is accurate and, under penalty of perjury, that you are authorised to act for the owner.
4. The US Copyright Office keeps a DMCA Designated Agent Directory to find a service provider's agent.

## Before filing
- Consider fair use first (see the fair use skill).
- Anyone who knowingly materially misrepresents that material is infringing, or that it was removed by mistake, can be liable (section 512(f)).

## If your post is taken down
- Under section 512(g) you can send a counter notification. The provider restores the material not less than 10 nor more than 14 business days after receiving it, unless the complainant tells the agent it has filed a court action.
- YouTube: a valid copyright removal request leads to a copyright strike. Strikes expire after 90 days if you complete Copyright School and your channel has fewer than 3 strikes; channels with 3 copyright strikes in 90 days are subject to termination. A Content ID claim typically does not result in a strike.
