# QR code scams

**Goal:** scan QR codes without landing on fake payment or login pages. This page follows an FTC consumer alert (December 2023).

## Where they appear
- Scammers have covered QR codes on parking meters with their own codes.
- Emails or texts you were not expecting, often urging immediate action.
- Messages claiming a package could not be delivered, to create urgency.

## Before you scan
1. If a QR code is in an unexpected place, inspect the URL before you open it.
2. Look for misspellings or a switched letter in a lookalike address.
3. Do not scan a QR code in an email or text you were not expecting. If the message seems legitimate, contact the company on a phone number or website you already know is real.

## If you entered details
Card details: contact your card issuer or bank and report the charge. A password: change it and anywhere it was reused. Report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.
