# Spotting AI-generated text, images and deepfakes

**Goal:** don't be fooled by, or pass on, synthetic content presented as real.

## Clues (none is proof on its own)
- **Images:** odd hands/teeth/ears, garbled text on signs, jewellery or glasses that merge, too-smooth
  skin, inconsistent shadows and reflections, backgrounds that melt. Newer models fix many of these.
- **Video:** lip-sync slightly off, unnatural blinking, flickering edges round the face, voice
  without breathing.
- **Voice clones:** a "relative" calling in distress from an unknown number, urgency, request for
  money or secrecy.
- **Text:** generic, confident, oddly balanced prose with no concrete sources; fabricated quotes,
  references or statistics; sites full of near-identical articles.

## Checks that work better than clues
1. **Source:** who posted it first? Is a reputable outlet reporting it? (lateral reading)
2. **Reverse image search** (Google Lens, TinEye, Bing Visual Search) to find the original or earlier
   versions.
3. **Content credentials:** look for the "CR" icon or check at contentcredentials.org/verify; some
   cameras and AI tools sign media (C2PA). Absence proves nothing.
4. Fact-checkers (Full Fact, Snopes, AFP Fact Check, Reuters Fact Check).
5. Voice scam: hang up and call the person back on their known number; agree a family code word.

## Gotchas
- AI-text detectors are unreliable and wrongly flag human writing; don't accuse people on their score.
- Real content can be dismissed as "AI" (the liar's dividend); verify both ways.

## Verify
- [ ] Before sharing a surprising image or clip, you've found its original source.
