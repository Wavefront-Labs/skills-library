# Reporting a scam and recovering

**Goal:** limit the damage, raise the chance of getting money back and help stop the scammer. This page covers US (FTC) and UK (NCSC) channels; follow your own country's official agency elsewhere.

## Within minutes
1. **Tell the payment provider first**, using the number on the card or the app:
   - Credit card: report it to the issuer immediately and ask for a refund.
   - Debit card: report it to your bank or credit union immediately and ask for a refund.
   - Wire transfer: tell the wire company a scammer tricked you and ask for a refund.
   - Gift card: contact the issuer, say a scammer tricked you into giving the number and PIN, and ask for a refund. Keep the card and receipt.
   - Cryptocurrency: contact the exchange or ATM operator, say it was fraudulent, and ask them to reverse it.
2. UK: your bank may also be reached on 159 (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked). The page that describes it could not be read.
3. **Change passwords** you gave away, and on any other account that reused them.
4. If you gave remote access, disconnect the computer and have it checked.

## Report
| Where | US | UK |
|---|---|---|
| Scams and fraud | ReportFraud.ftc.gov | Report Fraud, run by City of London Police, formerly Action Fraud; Police Scotland on 101 in Scotland (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked). The police sites could not be read. |
| Scam texts | forward to 7726 (SPAM) | forward to 7726 (free through most phone providers; contact your provider if it does not work) |
| Scam emails | report the email on the FTC site | forward to report@phishing.gov.uk |
| Identity theft | IdentityTheft.gov (if your Social Security number was used) | no confirmed route in this skill |
Also report on the platform where it happened (marketplace, social network, dating app).

## Keep evidence
Screenshots, phone numbers, profile links, transaction IDs, emails.

## After
- US: a credit freeze is free to place or lift, but you must contact each of Equifax, Experian and TransUnion separately.
- Tell trusted friends or family; scammers rely on shame.
- Be careful of follow-up "recovery" offers from strangers: anyone who contacts you after a scam offering to get money back is likely another scammer.

- [ ] You have a report reference and your payment provider's claim reference.
