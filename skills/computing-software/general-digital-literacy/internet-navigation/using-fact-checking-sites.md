# Use fact-checking sites

## Where to look
- **Google Fact Check Explorer** (toolbox.google.com/factcheck/explorer): a search page in Google's Fact Check Tools for finding published fact-checks.
- Established fact-checkers include **Full Fact** (UK), **FactCheck.org** and **Snopes** (US) and **BBC Verify**.
- Fact-checkers that follow the **IFCN Code of Principles** (Poynter) commit to non-partisanship and fairness, transparency of sources, funding and organisation, transparency of methodology, and an open and honest corrections policy.

## Method
1. Pull out the specific claim (who, what number, when). Search key words plus "fact check".
2. **Lateral reading:** open new tabs to see what others say about the source and claim instead of studying the original page.
3. Images and video: use a reverse image search such as Google Lens to find earlier versions and context; the InVID-WeVerify verification plugin helps with video.
4. Quotes: search the exact quote in quotation marks and check the original speech or transcript.
5. Statistics: find the original report from the statistics agency, not an infographic of it.

## Reading a fact-check
- Good ones show sources and links, explain the rating, and date the check. Read the explanation, not just the label.
- No fact-check yet does not mean true; a breaking claim may be days ahead of checkers.

## Before sharing
- If you can't verify it, don't share it, or share with clear "unverified" context.

## Verify
- [ ] You can name the original source of the claim and at least one independent check.
