# Copyright and AI-generated content

This page reflects US Copyright Office guidance. Other countries differ, and this is not legal advice; ask a qualified lawyer for commercial decisions.

## Ownership (United States)
- The Copyright Office's report "Copyright and Artificial Intelligence" is issued in parts. Part 2 (published 29 January 2025) addresses the copyrightability of outputs created using generative AI; Part 3 on training was released in pre-publication form on 9 May 2025.
- Part 2 concludes that prompts alone are not enough: protection does not extend to the mere provision of prompts.
- Outputs are protected only where a human author has determined sufficient expressive elements, for example where a human-authored work is perceptible in the output, or the human makes creative arrangements or modifications.
- Using AI to assist creation, or including AI material in a larger human work, does not bar copyrightability.
- The Office found the case has not been made for changes to existing law to give extra protection to AI outputs.

## Practical rules
- Check the terms of each AI tool for what rights it gives you in outputs, and your school, employer or publisher for AI-use rules and disclosure.
- Keep your prompts and edits as a record of human contribution.
- Don't put others' copyrighted or confidential material into AI tools without permission.
- AI output can resemble existing works; check it before publishing.
