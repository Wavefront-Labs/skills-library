# Choosing search keywords

**Goal:** find a good answer in the first page of results.

## Method
1. **Pick the key nouns** from your question. "Why does my laptop get really hot when I watch videos" becomes `laptop overheating video playback`.
2. **Be specific:** add the model, version, place or year, for example `<laptop model> overheating` or `bin collection dates <town>`.
3. **Use the words an answer would use:** experts write "reset", an error code, "eligibility criteria", not "it broke" or "can I get".
4. **Put exact error messages in quotes**, which searches for that exact phrase.
5. **Drop filler** (`how do I`, `please`), but keep words that change meaning (`not`, `without`, `vs`).

## Refine when results miss
- Too broad: add a word (`recipe` becomes `vegan lasagne recipe no oven`).
- Too few: remove a word or try synonyms (`car` / `vehicle`, `cheap` / `budget`).
- Wrong meaning: add context (`python snake` vs `python programming`) or leave a word out with `-` directly before it (`jaguar speed -car`).
- Read the result snippets and related searches for better vocabulary, then search again.
- Local results: add the town; official information: add the agency name.

## Gotchas
- Leading questions bias results: `is coffee bad for you` finds harm; `coffee health effects evidence review` is more neutral.
- Autocomplete reflects popular searches, not accuracy.
