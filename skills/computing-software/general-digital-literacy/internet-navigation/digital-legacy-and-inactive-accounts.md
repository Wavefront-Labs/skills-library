# Digital legacy and inactive accounts

**Goal:** the people you trust can get to photos, documents and money-related accounts, and close the rest, without breaking terms or guessing passwords.

## Set it up while you can
- **Google:** **Inactive Account Manager** in your Google Account: choose how long the account must be inactive, up to 10 trusted contacts (each verified by phone number) and which data they can download. Google judges activity from sign-ins, My Activity, Gmail use and Android check-ins. It does not override Google's separate two-year inactive-account policy.
- **Apple:** Settings › your name › Sign-In & Security › **Legacy Contact** (iOS 15.2 / macOS Monterey 12.1 or later, two-factor authentication on): add someone; they need the access key you generate plus a death certificate. They cannot access purchased media or iCloud Keychain data (passwords and passkeys).
- **Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, X:** each has its own help page for memorialising or removing an account after a death; look for it in that service's help centre.
- **Microsoft:** has a help page on accessing Outlook.com, OneDrive and other services when someone has died, and closes accounts left unused for two years. Banks and utilities have their own bereavement processes.

## A simple access plan
1. Password manager with an **emergency access** feature (check whether yours offers one) or a sealed note with the master password stored with your will.
2. List of important accounts (banks, pensions, email, cloud photos, domain names, crypto) without passwords in the will itself; wills become public in some countries.
3. Note where 2-step verification codes come from (phone passcode, authenticator, backup codes).
4. Wishes: which accounts to memorialise, which to delete, where photos go.

## For someone handling a death
- Don't log in as the person using their password; it may breach terms and complicate estates. Use each provider's request forms.
- UK: the **Tell Us Once** service reports a death to most government organisations in one go (GOV.UK); banks have their own bereavement teams.
- Cancel subscriptions to stop payments; download photos before closing accounts.
