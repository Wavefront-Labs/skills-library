# Group chat and notification management — turn the noise down, not off

**Goal:** stop a phone buzzing all day from group chats while still seeing the messages
that need a reply, and know the controls for muting, scheduling quiet hours, and leaving
a group cleanly.

## 1. Mute at the right level
Three different scopes exist in most apps — pick the narrowest one that solves the
problem:
- **Mute this conversation** (WhatsApp: open chat › ⋮ › Mute notifications, choose
  8h/1wk/always; Teams/Slack: channel name › Mute) stops just that one group, nothing
  else.
- **Mute notifications but keep the badge/unread count** vs **archive/hide entirely** —
  check which your app does; some "mute" options still show an unread dot you'll want if
  you intend to catch up later.
- **App-wide Do Not Disturb** (the phone's own Focus/DND mode, not the chat app's) is
  the blunt instrument for "nothing from anything" during sleep or a meeting — set it on
  a schedule rather than remembering to toggle it daily.

## 2. Notification triage without muting everything
- Most apps distinguish **@-mentions / direct replies** from general group chatter —
  check notification settings for an option like "notify only on mentions" (Slack:
  Preferences › Notifications › per-channel "Mentions only"; Teams: channel › ⋮ ›
  Notification settings; WhatsApp has no per-type filter, only full mute).
  This is the single best setting for a busy group: you still get pinged when someone
  actually needs you.
- Schedule a **daily/weekday quiet period** (iPhone Focus, Android Digital
  Wellbeing/Bedtime mode, or the chat app's own "Scheduled summary"/quiet hours where
  offered) instead of leaving every group fully muted indefinitely and forgetting to
  check it.
- Allow specific **people** through Focus/DND even while a Focus mode is active (iPhone
  Focus › Allowed Notifications/People; Android similar under Focus mode exceptions) so
  a spouse or child can still reach you when groups are silenced.

## 3. Group admin controls (if you run the group)
- Restrict **who can post** (WhatsApp group settings › "Who can send messages"; Teams
  channel moderation; Slack channel settings) for announcement-only groups — stops a
  120-person group dissolving into side chat.
- Set an **expectation in the group description/pinned message** about response times
  and purpose ("logistics only — DM for anything else") so people self-filter before
  posting.
- Remove inactive/wrong members promptly; a group that keeps adding people "just in
  case" is the main cause of notification overload for everyone in it.

## 4. Leaving a group without drama
- Most apps notify the group when you leave (WhatsApp shows "X left"); if that matters,
  mute instead of leaving, or message the organiser privately first.
- Archiving/hiding a chat (where offered) keeps you technically in it with zero
  notifications and zero visible list clutter — often the better option over leaving
  outright for a group you might need again (work project channels especially).
- For a group that's actively hostile or spam, **leave and report** rather than muting
  indefinitely — report options sit in the same menu as leaving on most platforms.

## Verify
- [ ] At least one noisy group is set to mentions-only or muted, not left fully noisy.
- [ ] A scheduled quiet period (Focus/DND) exists, with allowed-people exceptions for
      whoever must always reach you.
- [ ] You know the difference between "mute," "archive," and "leave" in your main chat
      app, and picked the right one for a group you tested this on.
- [ ] If you admin a group, posting permissions match its actual purpose.
