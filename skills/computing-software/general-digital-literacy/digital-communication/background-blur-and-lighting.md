# Background blur and lighting

**Goal:** you're clearly visible, well lit and the room behind you isn't a distraction.

## Background effects
- **Teams:** before a meeting, on the pre-join screen select **Effects and avatars**; in a meeting select **More actions > Video effects and settings > Video effects**. Choose Blur (Standard blur or Portrait blur) or a background, or **Add new** to upload a .JPG, .PNG or .BMP. Microsoft warns that blur or replacement may not hide sensitive information.
- **Zoom (desktop app):** profile picture > Settings > **Video & effects** > **Virtual backgrounds**; in a meeting click the arrow next to Video > **Video & effect settings**. The toggle **I have a green screen** is in the Virtual backgrounds settings.
- **Google Meet (computer):** open the Backgrounds and effects panel from your self view (before joining, or during the call by hovering over your self view or via More options). Availability depends on your Workspace edition, admin settings and a supported browser.
- **Windows Studio Effects:** on devices with a neural processing unit (NPU), click the Network, Sound or Battery icon by the clock and use the **Studio Effects** tile for background blur, eye contact and automatic framing. Which effects appear depends on NPU capability; lower-capacity NPUs may offer only Standard Blur.
- **Mac:** in macOS Sonoma or later choose **Portrait** (blur) or **Studio Light** from the **Video** menu in the menu bar during a video call (macOS Ventura: Control Centre > Video Effects). Portrait needs Apple silicon or Continuity Camera with iPhone XR or later; Studio Light needs a Mac laptop with Apple silicon on Sonoma or later, or Continuity Camera with iPhone 12 or later.

## Lighting
- Light source **in front** of you (window or lamp behind the screen), not behind you; a bright window behind turns you into a silhouette.
- Soft, even light: a lamp bounced off a wall, or a ring light at eye level, slightly above.
- Avoid overhead light alone (dark eye sockets).

## Framing
- Camera at **eye level** (stack books under the laptop); head and shoulders in frame, a little space above your head.
- Look at the camera when making a key point.
- A tidy, neutral real background beats a virtual one that flickers around your hair and hands.

## Gotchas
- Virtual backgrounds can cut off your hands when you gesture; switch to blur for demonstrations.
- Effects use extra processing; Google notes devices may slow down, so turn effects off if the call stutters.
- Blur does not fully hide everything behind you (screens, documents); check before sensitive calls.
