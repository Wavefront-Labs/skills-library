# Handle disagreements online

## Before replying
- **Pause**: draft, don't send. Wait 20 minutes or overnight for emotional messages. Remove the recipient or use a notes app to draft.
- Reread their message assuming good intent; text loses tone, and short replies often read colder than meant.
- Ask: what outcome do I want? Being right in public rarely gets it.

## Writing the reply
- Acknowledge their point ("I see why the deadline change is frustrating").
- Stick to facts and the issue, not the person; "I" statements ("I'm concerned that...") over "you always...".
- Propose a next step; keep it short.
- Don't add new people (Cc'ing a manager) as an escalation move without warning.

## Move channels
- After two back-and-forths, switch to a **call or video**: "Can we talk this through for 10 minutes?" Then summarise agreement in writing.
- Public social media argument: reply once politely or move to DMs; audiences rarely change their mind in threads.

## Disengage, block, report
- You don't have to reply to trolls or bad-faith arguments; mute, block or restrict.
- Harassment, threats or discrimination: screenshot with dates, report to the platform or HR (workplace bullying policies, ACAS in the UK), and see the online harassment skill.

## Verify
- [ ] Your reply is calm on reread tomorrow and proposes a next step or a conversation.
