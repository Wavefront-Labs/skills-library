# Social media privacy and safety — control the audience, not just the content

**Goal:** know exactly who can see each post before you make it, stop sharing details
that enable scams or stalking, and act fast if an account is taken over. Menus below are
named generically; wording shifts between app versions, but the settings exist on every
major platform.

## 1. Set the default audience first
- Every platform has an account-wide default (Instagram/TikTok: Private account toggle;
  Facebook: Settings › Privacy › who can see future posts; X/Twitter: Protect your posts;
  LinkedIn: more public by design — review Profile viewing options instead).
  **Set the default before posting anything**, then loosen it per-post only when you
  mean to.
- Review **old posts** with a privacy checkup tool where offered (Facebook's Privacy
  Checkup) rather than assuming a changed default applies retroactively — it usually
  doesn't for existing posts.
- Audit who can **tag you**, **comment**, and **message** you separately from who can see
  your profile — these are usually three different settings, not one.

## 2. What not to share, and why
- **Real-time location**: a tagged location at a restaurant you're currently at tells
  strangers your home is empty right now. Post travel photos after you're home, not
  during.
- **Full birth date, employer, school, kids' names/schools, routine ("gym every Tuesday
  6am")**: individually harmless, combined they're exactly what identity-theft
  verification questions and targeted scams use. A caller claiming to be your bank
  sounds far more convincing with your mother's maiden name or pet's name pulled from a
  public post.
- **Photos of documents**: boarding passes (barcode holds a booking reference), ID cards,
  new house keys, licence plates — all give away more than the obvious photo content.
- Check what a photo's **background** reveals (mail with an address, a screen showing
  private info) before posting, not after.

## 3. Securing the account itself
- Turn on **two-factor authentication** (an authenticator app or passkey over SMS where
  offered) on every social account — these are prime hijack targets because a hijacked
  account is used to scam your actual friends, who trust messages "from" you.
- Use a **unique password** per platform, via a password manager; reused passwords mean
  one breached site compromises every account using the same one.
- Review **active sessions/connected apps** periodically (Settings › Security, worded
  differently per platform) and revoke anything you don't recognise or no longer use.

## 4. Blocking and reporting
- **Block** stops a person seeing your content and contacting you; it does not always
  notify them, but on some platforms they can still see public content from a different
  account. **Report** flags content/behaviour to the platform's moderators — use it for
  harassment, impersonation or scams, not just mute-worthy annoyance (use mute/unfollow
  for that).
- Screenshot harassment or threats **before** blocking, in case you need evidence later
  — once blocked you typically lose the ability to view the original content.
- Impersonation (a fake account using your name/photos): report it specifically as
  impersonation, which most platforms fast-track, rather than a generic report.

## 5. If an account is hijacked
Act within minutes, not hours — a hijacked account is used immediately to message your
contacts.
1. If you still have access: change the password immediately, then check/revoke active
   sessions and connected apps, then re-enable/rotate 2FA.
2. If locked out: use the platform's **"my account was hacked"** recovery flow (every
   major platform has one, usually reached from the normal login page's "forgot
   password" link) rather than a generic support email, which is far slower.
3. Warn your contacts through another channel (text, a different platform) that the
   account was compromised, so they don't act on scam messages sent "from" you.

## Verify
- [ ] You can state your account's current default audience for new posts.
- [ ] Location, routine, document photos and family identifying details are not posted
      publicly, or not posted at all in real time.
- [ ] 2FA is on for every social account you use, with a unique password per platform.
- [ ] You know your platform's specific "account was hacked" recovery link before you
      need it.
