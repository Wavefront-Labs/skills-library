# Emoji and reactions at work

**Goal:** quick, friendly acknowledgements without seeming flippant or being misread.

## Where they help
- **Reactions replace "OK thanks" replies:** 👍 or ✅ on a message acknowledges it without notifying the whole channel.
- Agreed meanings in a team: ✅ done, 👀 looking at it, 🎉 congratulations. Write them down in the channel description so newcomers know.
- Softening tone in short chat messages ("Can you send it today? 🙂").

## Where to avoid
- Formal email, first contact with clients, complaints, bad news, HR or legal topics.
- Instead of words where clarity matters; "👍" to "Can you finish the report by Friday?" may mean "seen" rather than "yes".
- Many reactions on a serious announcement (redundancies, bereavement); a written reply is kinder.

## Misreadings
- 🙂 reads as passive-aggressive to some younger colleagues; 😂 and 💀 mean "very funny" to some, odd to others.
- 👍 can feel curt or dismissive to some people; 🙏 is "thanks" or "please" or "high five" depending on culture.
- Some gestures (👌, 👍) are offensive in certain countries.
- Screen readers read emoji names aloud ("face with tears of joy"); several in a row are tiresome. Put them at the end, not in place of words.

## Practical
- Teams: hover › react; Slack: hover › add reaction, or `+:emoji:` reply.
- Custom emoji in Slack/Teams should follow the workplace policy; nothing offensive or about colleagues.
- GIFs: keep them in social channels; can be disabled by admins.

## Verify
- [ ] Your reaction can't be mistaken for agreement to something you haven't agreed to; important answers are in words.
