# Choose a professional email address

## For job hunting and personal professional use
- Use your name: `firstname.lastname@`, `firstinitial.lastname@`, `firstname.middleinitial.lastname@`. Add a profession only if needed (`jane.smith.design@`).
- Avoid nicknames, birth years, jokes, numbers that look random, and hard-to-spell words.
- Free providers are fine (Gmail, Outlook.com); avoid old ISP addresses (lost when you switch broadband).
- Keep a separate address for job applications and professional networks so it isn't buried in shopping mail.

## For a business or freelancer
- Your own domain looks credible: `hello@yourbusiness.co.uk`, `jane@yourname.com`.
- Buy the domain from a registrar (Cloudflare, Namecheap, Squarespace Domains, Microsoft/Google partners) and use **Google Workspace**, **Microsoft 365 Business**, **Zoho Mail** or **Fastmail** for the mailbox.
- Follow the provider's setup wizard to add **MX**, **SPF**, **DKIM** and **DMARC** DNS records; without them mail lands in spam or can be spoofed.
- Use role aliases (`info@`, `support@`, `invoices@`) forwarded to people, so staff changes don't break contact details.

## Housekeeping
- Matching display name ("Jane Smith" or "Jane Smith | Smith Design").
- Professional signature: name, role, phone, website; no inspirational quotes or giant images.
- Renew the domain automatically; a lapsed domain lets someone else receive your mail.

## Verify
- [ ] Test emails from the new address reach Gmail and Outlook inboxes (not spam) and the name reads clearly when spoken.
