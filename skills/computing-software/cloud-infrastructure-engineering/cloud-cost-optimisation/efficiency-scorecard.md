# Cost efficiency scorecards

A scorecard is an internal benchmarking programme: the same KPIs, defined the same way, for each team or scope.

## Steps
1. **Select a curated set of KPIs** that reflect organisational priorities across cost, performance, efficiency and
   sustainability, and that mean something to each persona. Candidates the Framework names:
   - allocation and tagging compliance (the Reporting and Analytics page lists overall tagging compliance as a measure of success);
   - commitment utilisation, effective savings rate and the percentage of commitment discount waste (Rate Optimization page);
   - forecast accuracy and forecast drift (Forecasting page);
   - unit cost metrics for scopes that matter (Unit Economics page).
2. **Standardise definitions** so comparisons between teams are meaningful. The Framework lists clear KPI
   definition, transparent goals, accurate data collection and supportive leadership as conditions for effective
   internal benchmarking.
3. **Automate the data** into reporting that teams can use without the central team (Walk and Run behaviour).
4. **Optionally add incentives**: the Framework says gamification or incentive programmes can strengthen internal
   benchmarking, and recognition programmes feature at Run.
5. **Set a governance cadence** to review and retire KPIs as business priorities change.
6. **Link to action**: pair each result with improvement targets and accountability so benchmarking drives outcomes
   rather than staying observational.

## Gotchas
- Decide in advance how metrics outside a team's control are treated (central commitments, for example), and say so on the scorecard.
- Frequent redefinition breaks trends; keep a record of changes.

## Verify
- [ ] KPI definitions are documented and shared across teams.
- [ ] Each metric has an accountable owner and a route to remediation.
