# External / peer benchmarking

## Sources the Framework names
- The FinOps Foundation community, often via informal sharing with peer organisations.
- State of FinOps data, for insight into the practices and performance of other FinOps teams.
- Analysts, large system integrators and technology service providers, who sometimes share
  aggregated or summary information on technology usage and efficiency.

## Steps
1. **Start from defined KPIs.** Well-defined KPIs are the foundation of meaningful benchmarking; the
   FinOps KPI Catalog gives illustrative examples across scopes and technology categories.
2. **Protect sensitive data.** Perform external benchmarking carefully so pricing, usage and other
   commercially sensitive information is not exposed. Because many organisations will not share
   detail publicly, expect in-depth external benchmarking to be hard.
3. **Compare like with like.** The Framework talks about organisations using technology in similar
   ways; state the cohort and definition of every external number you use.
4. **Turn gaps into action.** Translate gaps into prioritised recommendations with improvement
   targets and accountability, so insight does not stay an observational data point.
5. **Keep it one input.** Balance benchmarking with other approaches to understanding technology
   value; perfect information is rarely available, so refine benchmark KPIs continuously and decide
   on the data you have.

## Maturity signals
- Crawl: awareness of external benchmarking, no formal process.
- Walk: selective sharing through trusted channels such as the FinOps community and peer groups.
- Run: trusted peer networks, contribution to industry programmes and working groups, and
  benchmarking insight in executive reporting.

## Verify
- [ ] Each external comparison states its source, cohort and KPI definition.
- [ ] Commercially sensitive data was not shared outside agreed channels.
