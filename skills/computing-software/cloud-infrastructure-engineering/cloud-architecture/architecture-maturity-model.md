# Architecture capability maturity assessment

## Steps
1. **Choose a model**: the US Department of Commerce ACMM (referenced in TOGAF) scores nine
   elements on levels 0 (none) to 5 (optimising): architecture process, development, business
   linkage, senior management involvement, operating unit participation, communication, IT
   security, governance, IT investment and acquisition strategy.
2. **Gather evidence**, not opinions: artefacts in the repository, board minutes, waiver logs,
   how many projects went through review, whether principles are cited in decisions.
3. **Interview** a sample: executives, architects, delivery leads, security.
4. **Score each element** with the evidence attached; report the profile, not one average.
5. **Set target levels** per element tied to business need (level 3 "defined" is a common
   realistic target; level 5 is rarely worth it).
6. **Improvement plan**: two or three initiatives per cycle, owner and date; reassess yearly.

## Gotchas
- Self-assessment inflates scores; require evidence for each level claimed.
- Maturity is a means; tie targets to outcomes such as review lead time.

- [ ] Element-level scores with evidence.
