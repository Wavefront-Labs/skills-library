# 14 CFR Part 33 orientation

**Goal:** understand what engine certification proves so the installation and airframe work builds on it. Practice guidance; the regulation, the engine's certification basis and the regulator's findings govern.

## Selected requirements (section numbers and titles checked against the eCFR table of contents; verify scope in the current text)
- Life-limited parts: 33.70.
- Safety analysis: 33.75 (hazard analysis of engine failure effects).
- Bird ingestion: 33.76; foreign object ingestion including ice: 33.77.
- Endurance test: 33.87.
- Blade containment and rotor unbalance tests: 33.94.
- Instructions for continued airworthiness: 33.4. Declared ratings, operating limitations and the engine installation manual are part of the type design data.

## Interface with airframe certification
- The engine type certificate gives ratings and limitations; the airframe applicant shows installation compliance with Part 25 powerplant provisions (Subpart E from 25.901) including fire protection, controls, fuel and oil systems, and engine-out safety.
- Installation manuals state interfaces (bleed, power extraction, inlet distortion limits, mounts and loads).

## EASA equivalent
CS-E, with its AMC, for engines certified under EASA; validation arrangements exist between authorities.

## Practice
1. Maintain a requirement matrix linking the engine's declared limits to the installation analysis (inlet distortion, bleed schedule, thrust reverser loads).
2. Track certification differences when the engine is certified under a different authority.

## Verify
- [ ] Every engine limit declared in the installation manual has a matching check in the airframe installation analysis.
