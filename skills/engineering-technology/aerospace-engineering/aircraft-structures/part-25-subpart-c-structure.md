# 14 CFR Part 25 Subpart C

**Goal:** know the structure of the requirements so loads and strength work is traceable to them. This is practice guidance; the regulation, the aircraft's certification basis and the regulator's findings govern.

## Layout (read the current text for exact wording)
- **General:** 25.301 loads, 25.303 factor of safety (1.5 on limit loads unless otherwise specified), 25.305 strength and deformation (limit loads without detrimental permanent deformation; ultimate loads withstood for at least 3 seconds), 25.307 proof of structure (analysis validated by test, or test).
- **Flight loads:** 25.331 onward (symmetric and unsymmetric manoeuvres, gust and turbulence 25.341, flaps, control surface loads).
- **Ground loads:** landing and ground handling in the 25.471 range.
- **Emergency landing:** 25.561 onward (25.561(b)(3) ultimate inertia forces on occupants in a minor crash landing: up 3.0 g, forward 9.0 g, sideward 3.0 g on the airframe and 4.0 g on seats and attachments, down 6.0 g, rearward 1.5 g).
- **Fatigue and damage tolerance:** 25.571. **Aeroelasticity:** 25.629.

## Certification basis
Fixed at application for the type certificate with the amendment level of Part 25 applicable on that date plus any special conditions and exemptions. The equivalent EASA text is CS-25 with its Acceptable Means of Compliance. Validation between authorities relies on bilateral arrangements; each authority may impose differences.

## Practice
1. List each applicable paragraph and its amendment in a compliance checklist.
2. Identify the means of compliance (analysis, test, similarity, inspection).
3. Record the loads cases and the margins of safety traceable to each paragraph.
4. Only the authority (or authorised designee or organisation) accepts findings; analysts supply evidence.

## Gotchas
- Amendment level matters: do not apply a later amendment than the basis unless chosen.
- Advisory circulars are acceptable means, not the only means.
