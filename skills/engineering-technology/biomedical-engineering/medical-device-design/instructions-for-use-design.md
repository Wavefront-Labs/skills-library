# Instructions for use and labelling usability

**Goal:** the IFU and labels let the intended user complete critical tasks safely, and are evaluated as part of the user interface.

## Design
- Base content on the use specification and the use-related risk analysis; each risk control by information for safety must appear where the user needs it.
- Order by the task sequence, one action per step, imperative verbs, units consistent with the device display.
- Warnings: state the hazard, consequence and avoidance; place at the point of use, not only in a front chapter. Use a consistent signal-word hierarchy.
- Pictograms and recognised symbols (for example ISO 15223-1:2021 symbols; Amd 1:2025 modifies the authorized-representative symbol, iso.org) reduce text but must be understood; do not invent symbols for critical meanings without testing.
- Language and reading level for lay users (home devices) differ from clinician instructions; test with representative users.
- Quick reference cards and on-device labels supplement, not replace, the IFU.

## Evaluate
- Formative studies on drafts; include the IFU in summative evaluation of critical tasks, as the user would receive it (unprompted use).
- Information for safety is the weakest risk control; do not rely on it alone for severe hazards.

## Verify
- [ ] Each risk control by IFU is traceable to a section and a use scenario.
- [ ] Representative users complete critical tasks using only the supplied information.
- [ ] Labelling content meets applicable regional requirements (confirm for EU, US and other markets).

> Practice guidance, not regulatory or clinical advice. Confirm current standard editions and rules for your jurisdiction, and defer to qualified regulatory, clinical and engineering judgement.
