# Writing a need statement

**Goal:** a one-sentence need that names the problem, the patient or user population and the outcome sought, with no solution embedded.

## Format
"A way to [address a problem] in [a population] in order to [achieve an outcome]."
Example: "A way to detect early surgical-site fluid collection in post-operative abdominal patients in order to reduce readmission." Compare with a bad statement: "A sensor to detect fluid under the skin", which hides the solution and closes options.

## Steps
1. **Gather the evidence** from observations (see clinical-observation-protocol): list what happens, to whom, how often, and consequences.
2. **Draft** the statement with a verb that does not imply a technology (detect, reduce, prevent, simplify).
3. **Strip solutions:** remove any device, material, mechanism or brand words.
4. **Quantify** the problem in a supporting paragraph: incidence, cost, time, error rate, with sources (clinical literature, registries, hospital data) and note uncertainty.
5. **Define the outcome** as something measurable (fewer readmissions, shorter time, fewer errors), not as a feature.
6. **Check the population:** who is affected and who will use the solution (patient, nurse, surgeon, technician); they may differ.
7. **Review** with at least two clinicians who did not see your notes; revise the wording they interpret differently.

## Gotchas
- Needs that are really wishes for a feature.
- Problems too broad ("improve outcomes in heart disease") or too narrow (only your preferred solution fits).
- Missing the current workaround, which defines the benchmark to beat.

## Verify
- [ ] The statement contains no solution.
- [ ] Each part (problem, population, outcome) is backed by data.
- [ ] A clinician not involved can restate the need correctly.
