# Equivalence demonstration

**Goal:** show that clinical data on another device can be used for yours because the devices are equivalent, with enough documented evidence.

## Concept (EU MDR)
- Equivalence requires similarity in three areas: **technical** (design, specifications, properties, deployment, principles of operation), **biological** (same materials in contact with the same tissues, release of substances), and **clinical** (same clinical condition, site, population, kind of user, and relevant critical performance). Differences must not result in a clinically significant difference in safety and performance.
- MDR also requires, for implantable and class III devices relying on equivalence, a contract giving the manufacturer ongoing access to the other device's technical documentation, unless the devices are from the same manufacturer; confirm current rules and the guidance.

## Steps
1. Identify candidate equivalent devices through the literature and market review.
2. Build a **comparison table** across technical, biological and clinical characteristics with data sources for each parameter.
3. Evaluate each difference: is it clinically significant? Provide bench, preclinical or clinical evidence to justify.
4. Show data access and sufficiency of the equivalent device's clinical data.
5. Conclude and record limits; if equivalence cannot be shown, plan own clinical investigation.
6. Keep updated with post-market data.

## Traps
- Claiming equivalence on a similar intended purpose alone.
- Using literature on a competitor device with no access to its technical data.
- In the US the 510(k) concept of substantial equivalence differs; do not mix terms.

## Verify
- [ ] Comparison table complete with sources.
- [ ] Differences analysed and justified.
- [ ] Data access arrangements documented where required.
