# Design and development plan

**Goal:** a controlled plan that shows how a device will be designed, verified and validated, who does what and when, kept current as the design evolves.

## Contents
- Scope, intended use and the regulatory strategy outline (markets, classification to be confirmed with regulatory staff).
- **Stages** and the **design reviews** at the end of each stage with attendees and independence (a reviewer not directly responsible for the stage).
- Activities per stage: user needs, design inputs, risk management, outputs, verification, validation, design transfer, with the **responsible functions**.
- Methods and tools: requirements tool, CAD, test method validation, software lifecycle plan (see software-development-plan).
- Interfaces between groups, suppliers and contract manufacturers; communication and decision making.
- Resources and schedule with milestones and dependencies; links to risk management plan, usability plan, clinical evaluation plan and verification and validation master plan (see vv-master-plan).
- Configuration and change control approach (see design-change-control).

## Practice
1. Write the plan at project start but expect updates; record each revision and the reason.
2. Keep it proportional: a simple accessory needs a short plan.
3. Quality requirements apply even if development is agile: sprint outputs map to plan stages.
4. Include regulatory expectations: 21 CFR 820.10 (fetched, eCFR) now requires a quality system that complies with ISO 13485, incorporated by reference, and 820.10(c) points to ISO 13485 clause 7.3 Design and Development for class II, class III and certain class I devices; confirm the transition date on FDA's QMSR page; EU MDR general requirements.

## Verify
- [ ] Plan approved before design activities start and updated at each review.
- [ ] Each plan activity has an owner, input and output document.
- [ ] Reviews actually held and recorded.

> Practice guidance, not regulatory or clinical advice. Confirm current standard editions and rules for your jurisdiction, and defer to qualified regulatory, clinical and engineering judgement.
