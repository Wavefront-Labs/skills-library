# Hydrogel crosslinking

**Goal:** a gel with defined stiffness, swelling and degradation, made without harmful residues.

## Routes
- **Ionic (physical):** alginate crosslinked by divalent calcium; mild, reversible; gel stability depends on ion exchange in vivo.
- **Covalent chemical:** carbodiimide, glutaraldehyde (toxic residues), genipin (natural crosslinker), Michael-type addition with thiols, click chemistries.
- **Photocrosslinking:** polyethylene glycol diacrylate or methacrylated gelatin and hyaluronic acid with a photoinitiator (for example LAP or Irgacure 2959) under UV or visible light; permits injectable or printed forms and fast gelation.
- **Enzymatic:** transglutaminase or horseradish peroxidase systems.
- **Thermal and self-assembly** gels (collagen, Matrigel-type materials).

## Control and characterisation
- **Crosslink density** sets modulus, mesh size and swelling: higher density gives stiffer, less swollen gels with smaller mesh; theoretical link via the Flory-Rehner model and rubber elasticity; measure with rheology (storage modulus plateau), compression, and equilibrium swelling ratio.
- **Mesh size** determines diffusion of nutrients and drugs.
- **Degradation:** hydrolytic or enzymatic; link with crosslink design.
- **Residuals:** unreacted monomers, initiators and crosslinkers can be cytotoxic; wash and test extracts (see chemical-characterization and biocompatibility-evaluation-iso-10993).
- **Cell viability** for cell-laden gels under UV and radical exposure.

## Verify
- [ ] Modulus and swelling measured across batches with acceptance range.
- [ ] Residual initiator and monomer levels below toxicological limits.
- [ ] Gelation time and conditions reproducible.
