# Calcium phosphate bone grafts

**Goal:** choose a bone void filler with the resorption rate and handling needed for the site.

## Materials
- **Hydroxyapatite (HA, Ca/P about 1.67):** stable, slow resorbing, osteoconductive; used as coating (see plasma-spray-coating) and as blocks and granules.
- **Beta-tricalcium phosphate (beta-TCP, Ca/P about 1.5):** resorbs faster than HA, replaced by bone over months to years.
- **Biphasic calcium phosphate (BCP):** HA plus beta-TCP mixtures set the resorption rate.
- **Others:** calcium sulphate (fast resorbing), bioactive glasses, and cements (calcium phosphate cements that set in situ).

## Design factors
- **Porosity and pore size:** interconnected macropores (commonly hundreds of micrometres) support bone ingrowth and vessels; microporosity affects dissolution and protein adsorption (see scaffold-porosity).
- **Crystallinity and phase purity:** sintering temperature sets phases; impurity phases (for example calcium oxide) raise dissolution and tissue reaction; specify and test by XRD.
- **Mechanics:** brittle, low tensile strength; for non-load-bearing or protected sites.
- **Form:** granules, blocks, putties, injectable pastes and composites with collagen or polymers.
- **Sterilisation:** gamma or EO; effects on dissolution tested.

## Verify
- [ ] Phase composition by XRD and Ca/P ratio measured.
- [ ] Porosity and particle size distribution verified.
- [ ] Preclinical resorption and bone formation data for the site.
