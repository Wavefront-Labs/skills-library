# Titanium surfaces for osseointegration

**Goal:** a surface that promotes fast, strong bone attachment without shedding particles.

## Surface approaches
- **Machined (turned):** smooth; slower bone response.
- **Grit blasting:** alumina or titania particles roughen the surface; residual grit particles must be controlled.
- **Acid etching:** adds micro-pits; **SLA-type** surfaces combine large-grit blasting with acid etch to give macro and micro roughness.
- **Plasma-sprayed titanium or hydroxyapatite (see plasma-spray-coating)** and **sintered or additive porous coatings** for ingrowth in orthopaedics.
- **Anodised surfaces** (see titanium-anodizing) and chemical treatments that alter oxide thickness and chemistry; **hydrophilic** or bioactive surface treatments aim to speed early healing.

## Characterisation
- **Roughness parameters** at different scales (Sa, Sdr) with optical profilometry or confocal; moderately rough surfaces (Sa of roughly 1 to 2 micrometres) are often cited as favourable for dental implants, confirm in the literature for your application.
- SEM for morphology, EDS or XPS for chemistry and residual blast media, contact angle for wettability, and cleanliness tests.

## Risks
- Coating delamination, particle release, grit contamination, and surface ageing during storage (hydrocarbon adsorption) that reduces wettability; packaging may preserve it.
- Peri-implantitis in dental implants is more severe on rough surfaces once exposed; the trade-off is clinical.

## Verify
- [ ] Surface roughness and chemistry meet specification across lots.
- [ ] Coating adhesion and particle shedding tested for coated surfaces.
- [ ] Biological response evidence available (preclinical or clinical).
