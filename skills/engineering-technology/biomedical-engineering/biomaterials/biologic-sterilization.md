# Sterilising biologic materials

**Goal:** reach the required sterility assurance while preserving the structure and function of the biologic.

## Challenges
- Collagen, proteins, growth factors and decellularised tissues are sensitive to heat, radiation and chemicals. Steam is usually unsuitable. Gamma or e-beam radiation can cause chain scission and crosslinking in collagen, changing strength and degradation; EO leaves residuals and can alter proteins; low-temperature plasma and peracetic acid based methods are used for tissues.
- Viral inactivation and prion risk for animal and human tissue must be controlled by source control, validated inactivation and testing, per the animal tissue standards and tissue regulations.

## Approach
1. Control bioburden at the source and manufacturing (cleanroom, antibiotic washes where allowed) to allow a lower sterilisation dose (see bioburden-testing and vdmax-dose-setting).
2. Select and validate the method with function tests after treatment: mechanical properties, thermal stability (shrinkage temperature), enzymatic degradation rate, biological activity, histology.
3. **Radiation at low temperature** or in an inert atmosphere reduces radical damage; reduced dose options with the standard's dose-setting method.
4. **Supercritical carbon dioxide with additives** is used as a mild method; validate microbial kill and material effects.
5. **Aseptic processing** where terminal sterilisation is not possible, with media fills and environmental controls.
6. Check residuals (EO, peracetic acid), endotoxin (see endotoxin-testing) and shelf life.

## Verify
- [ ] Sterilisation validated to the standard with sterility assurance level stated.
- [ ] Function of the biologic confirmed after sterilisation and ageing.
- [ ] Viral and prion safety documentation complete for tissue-derived materials.
