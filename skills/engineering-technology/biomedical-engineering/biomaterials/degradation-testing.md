# Degradation testing

**Goal:** characterise how a resorbable device loses mass, molecular weight and strength, to support claims about performance through healing.

## Method
1. **Media:** phosphate buffered saline at pH 7.4 as base case; for metals use media with chlorides and proteins as justified; for ceramics acidic and neutral media; control volume to sample ratio and refresh solution.
2. **Temperature:** 37 C for real-time. Elevated temperature acceleration is possible for polymers but only below the glass transition temperature region where mechanisms stay the same; justify and confirm with real-time data.
3. **Time points:** enough points to define the curve (early, middle, late), triplicate or more samples per point.
4. **Measurements:** mass, water uptake, molecular weight (GPC), thermal properties (DSC), pH of medium, mechanical properties (wet), degradation products by chromatography, morphology. Note that ISO 10993-13 is scoped to non-resorbable polymers, so for resorbable polymers design the product analysis from ASTM F1635 (and ISO 13781 for poly(lactide)) plus a toxicologist.
5. **Mechanics at temperature and hydrated state** because many polymers soften when wet.
6. Include sterilised test articles and the final packaged product after shelf ageing.
7. Interpret: strength loss often precedes mass loss for polyesters (bulk erosion), and local acid release can accelerate degradation (autocatalysis).

## Verify
- [ ] Protocol states medium, volume, temperature and sampling plan.
- [ ] Real-time data support any acceleration claim.
- [ ] Degradation products characterised for toxicological assessment.
