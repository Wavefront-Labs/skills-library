# Antimicrobial coatings

**Goal:** reduce device-associated infection with a coating whose benefit outweighs toxicity and resistance risks.

## Types
- **Silver** (ions or nanoparticles): broad spectrum; release rate sets duration; cytotoxicity and argyria concern at high exposure.
- **Antibiotic-loaded coatings and cements:** local high concentration; risk of promoting resistance and allergic reactions.
- **Chlorhexidine and silver sulfadiazine** combinations used in some central venous catheters.
- **Antiseptics, quaternary ammoniums, peptides and contact-killing surfaces; anti-adhesive coatings** that prevent attachment without killing.

## Evaluation
1. **Efficacy:** in vitro tests such as ISO 22196-type contact tests, zone of inhibition, biofilm assays under flow (relevant to the clinical environment), and challenge models (animal infection models) for clinical claims.
2. **Release kinetics** and duration; coating durability during insertion and use (shear, hydration).
3. **Safety:** cytotoxicity, sensitisation and systemic exposure from released agents; toxicological assessment.
4. **Resistance:** evidence the agent does not select for resistant strains, a major concern for antibiotics.
5. **Regulatory:** the coating may make the product a combination product or place it in a higher class depending on the drug or biocidal function and the jurisdiction; confirm early with regulators.
6. Clinical evidence of infection reduction is usually needed for claims; bench efficacy alone is insufficient.

## Verify
- [ ] Efficacy shown under conditions representing clinical use.
- [ ] Toxicology and release data support safety.
- [ ] Regulatory pathway for the coating agent confirmed.

> Practice guidance, not regulatory or clinical advice. Confirm current standard editions and rules for your jurisdiction, and defer to qualified regulatory, clinical and engineering judgement.
