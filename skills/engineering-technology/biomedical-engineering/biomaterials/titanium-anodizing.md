# Titanium anodising

**Goal:** thicken the oxide layer on titanium for colour coding, improved surface chemistry or wear behaviour, with controlled process.

## Process
- The part is made the anode in an electrolyte; applied voltage grows an oxide layer whose thickness rises with voltage. Thin oxides produce interference colours (bronze, blue, gold, purple, green) used to identify implant sizes or types; colour depends on thickness, not on dye.
- **Type II-style anodising** (low-voltage colour and thin film) is common for orthopaedic screw colour coding; **Type III-style hard anodising** gives thicker, harder oxides for wear and fretting; processes and standards are aerospace-derived (AMS 2488) and adapted by implant makers; confirm the specification used.
- Electrolytes (phosphate, sulphate, others) affect oxide structure and porosity; bath chemistry and temperature are controlled.

## Effects
- Oxide improves corrosion resistance and can reduce metal ion release; a thick oxide may lower fatigue strength slightly and must be tested; fretting performance can improve with a hard oxide.
- Surface roughness and cleanliness before anodising affect uniformity of colour.

## Control
1. Define voltage, current profile, time, bath composition and temperature; validate the process (IQ OQ PQ).
2. Colour, thickness and adhesion verification with a standard for acceptance (colour tolerance samples, thickness by interference or XPS depth).
3. Cleaning and passivation after rinsing; check residues.
4. Bridge with marking (laser marking before or after) per ASTM F86 considerations.

## Verify
- [ ] Colour and thickness within tolerance across lots.
- [ ] Fatigue tests performed on anodised parts when it could be affected.
- [ ] Residual chemistry and cleanliness verified.
