# Medical silicone elastomers

**Goal:** choose a silicone grade and process that meet mechanical, biological and manufacturing needs.

## Basics
- Silicone elastomers (polydimethylsiloxane based) are flexible across temperature, chemically stable and widely used for tubing, seals, lead insulation, catheters, and body-contact devices.
- **Cure systems:** platinum-catalysed addition cure gives cleaner products with low by-products and is common for medical grades; peroxide cure leaves peroxide breakdown products that may need post-curing.
- **Hardness:** Shore A roughly 10 to 80 across grades; tear strength and elongation are modest compared with polyurethanes, so edges and thin sections need design care.
- **Fillers:** silica reinforces; fillers and pigments must be approved for the contact class.

## Biocompatibility
- Suppliers offer grades by contact duration (short-term, long-term, implant), with biocompatibility data; the finished device still needs its own evaluation because processing adds contamination and changes extractables.
- Low-molecular-weight cyclic siloxanes (D4, D5, D6) and platinum residues are extractables to characterise (see chemical-characterization); post-cure and extraction reduce them.
- Silicone is not an inert surface for adhesion; it attracts particles and is hydrophobic; surface treatment or coatings alter this.

## Processing
- Liquid silicone rubber injection moulding, compression and transfer moulding, extrusion; cleanroom handling (see cleanroom-contamination-control) for implants; sterilisation by EO, gamma or steam are generally compatible (confirm grade).

## Verify
- [ ] Supplier grade and master file for the intended contact class.
- [ ] Extractables and leachables assessed on the finished device.
- [ ] Mechanical tear and fatigue at thin sections tested.
