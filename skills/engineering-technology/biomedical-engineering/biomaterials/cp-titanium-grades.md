# Commercially pure titanium grades

**Goal:** pick the CP titanium grade whose strength, formability and corrosion behaviour match the implant.

## Facts
- CP titanium (unalloyed) comes in grades 1 to 4 in the implant specification (ASTM F67). The grades differ mainly in oxygen, iron and other interstitial limits. Strength rises and ductility falls from grade 1 (softest, most formable) to grade 4 (strongest). Check the standard for the current composition and minimum mechanical property limits.
- Corrosion resistance is excellent in body fluids due to a stable passive oxide film (see passivation-astm-f86).
- Modulus about 100 to 110 GPa (lower than steel or cobalt chrome, higher than bone).

## Applications and choices
- Dental implants and abutments, craniomaxillofacial plates and meshes, pacemaker cases, and some spinal and orthopaedic components.
- Grade 4 is common for dental implants needing strength with biocompatibility; grade 1 or 2 where forming is needed (meshes, plates).
- Compared with Ti-6Al-4V (see astm-f136-eli-titanium), CP grades have lower strength and fatigue strength, so choose them when loads are low or where aluminium and vanadium are to be avoided.
- Machining and forming: work-hardening, galling; surface contamination (alpha case) from hot processes.
- Heat treatments and cold working modify strength; specify per the standard and supplier certificates.
