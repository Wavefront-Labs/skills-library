# Beta titanium alloys

**Goal:** use lower-modulus titanium alloys to reduce stress shielding while keeping adequate strength and fatigue life.

## Facts
- Beta-stabilising elements (molybdenum, niobium, zirconium, tantalum) stabilise the body-centred cubic beta phase and avoid aluminium and vanadium. Examples include Ti-15Mo, Ti-13Nb-13Zr and Ti-Nb-Zr-Ta compositions; some have implant standards (for example ASTM F2066 and F1713; confirm editions and which alloys are covered).
- Elastic modulus can be in the range of roughly 55 to 85 GPa depending on composition and heat treatment, lower than Ti-6Al-4V (about 110 GPa), so closer to bone (see stress-shielding-prediction); values depend strongly on processing, so verify.
- Strength and fatigue: strength can match or exceed CP titanium; fatigue strength and notch sensitivity must be checked on the final geometry.
- Phase stability: aging can precipitate omega or alpha phases, changing modulus and ductility; heat treatment must be controlled.
- Wear: lower surface hardness can mean poorer wear and fretting than Ti-6Al-4V, so articulating or fretting surfaces may need coatings or other measures.

## Use
- Spinal rods, fracture plates and stems where stiffness reduction is a design target.
- Manufacturing: forging, machining and AM variants, with traceability of heat treatment.

## Verify
- [ ] Modulus and tensile properties measured on production material.
- [ ] Fatigue and fretting tests on the intended design.
- [ ] Chemistry and phase control documented by specification.
