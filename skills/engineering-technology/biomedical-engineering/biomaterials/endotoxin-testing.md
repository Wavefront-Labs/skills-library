# Endotoxin and pyrogen testing for devices

**Goal:** show that the device does not release bacterial endotoxin or pyrogenic material at levels harmful to patients.

Regulated laboratory testing; use validated methods in an accredited laboratory and confirm current USP, Ph. Eur. and FDA text.

## Endotoxin (bacterial) test
- **LAL (Limulus amebocyte lysate) assays:** gel-clot (qualitative or semi-quantitative), kinetic turbidimetric, kinetic chromogenic. Recombinant factor C (rFC) assays are used as alternatives in some compendia; confirm acceptance in the current USP, Ph. Eur. and with the regulator.
- **Extraction:** rinse or soak the device in LAL reagent water (endotoxin-free), FDA recommends rinsing each of 10 test units with 40 mL of non-pyrogenic water, adjusted by patient-contacting surface area for unusually small or large devices (or other volume and surface contact justified by the device), then test the extract; extract the fluid-contacting surfaces for devices with fluid paths.
- **Limits (FDA guidance Pyrogen and Endotoxins Testing: Questions and Answers, March 2026 edition):** 0.5 EU/mL or 20 EU per device for devices contacting the cardiovascular and lymphatic systems; 0.06 EU/mL or 2.15 EU per device for devices contacting cerebrospinal fluid; intraocular contact may need a lower limit and other contacts are set case by case with the review division.

## Method validation
1. **Inhibition and enhancement test** (product interference): spike the device extract with a known endotoxin standard and check recovery in the range of 50-200% (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked). Dilute within the maximum valid dilution or neutralise interference.
2. **Sample qualification:** repeat on three lots in the validation.
3. **Routine testing:** per lot (batch release) or a monitored sampling plan, with control standard endotoxin and negative controls.

## Material-mediated pyrogenicity
Endotoxin is only one cause of pyrogenic response; materials can contain chemical pyrogens. ISO 10993-11 and USP 151 describe the **rabbit pyrogen test**; it is used when material-mediated pyrogenicity is a risk based on the biological evaluation and the contact (FDA says it will consider alternatives such as the monocyte activation test case by case).

## Controls to reduce endotoxin
Use endotoxin-controlled water and cleaning in production, depyrogenation by dry heat (for glass and metals) or strong cleaning, bioburden control (see bioburden-testing), and cleanroom practice.

## Verify
- [ ] Extraction volume and device area documented.
- [ ] Inhibition and enhancement passed.
- [ ] Results meet the per-device limit with documented rationale.
