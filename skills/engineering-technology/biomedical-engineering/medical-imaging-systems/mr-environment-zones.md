# MR environment zones

**Goal:** control who and what can enter the strong magnetic field, to prevent projectile accidents, burns and implanted-device injuries.

## Zones (ACR four-zone model)
- **Zone I:** public access areas, outside the MR suite.
- **Zone II:** interface between public and controlled areas, where patients are met and screened (reception, interview, changing).
- **Zone III:** restricted area where unscreened people and unrestricted ferromagnetic objects could reach the magnet's field; access is controlled by MR personnel; the console is usually here.
- **Zone IV:** the scanner room itself, always under direct supervision, with no ferromagnetic objects unless evaluated.

## Practice
1. Layout and physical barriers (locked doors, signage, access control); the magnet's stray field contours (a 5 gauss line is commonly used for pacemaker limits; other sources cite 9 gauss (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)) are mapped when planning zones; implant limits are referenced to the labelled MR conditions.
2. Screening: staff, patients and visitors by questionnaire and, where needed, imaging or metal detection before entering Zone IV; implant device MR conditions checked against labelled limits.
3. **Equipment labelling:** MR Safe, MR Conditional (with conditions) and MR Unsafe markings (see the labelling standard); device testing supports the labels.
4. **Quench and emergency:** quench pipe, oxygen monitor, emergency procedures; firefighters and non-MR resuscitation equipment kept outside.
5. Competence: designated MR safety officer and training.

## Verify
- [ ] Stray field map and zone plan documented.
- [ ] Screening forms and implant-labelling workflow audited.
- [ ] Emergency drills include non-MR responders.
