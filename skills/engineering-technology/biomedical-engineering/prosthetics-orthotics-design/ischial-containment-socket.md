# Ischial containment (IC) transfemoral socket

**Goal:** hold the femur in adduction with a narrow medial-lateral (ML) shape that contains the ischial tuberosity and ramus, so that the femur is stable in the socket and gluteus medius can work.

Prosthetic fitting is performed by a certified prosthetist under local practice rules. This describes engineering concepts, not a substitute for clinical training.

## Concepts
- Narrow ML width relative to the quadrilateral socket; the femur sits in adduction (about 5-10 degrees (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)) so the abductors keep a good lever arm, reducing lateral trunk lean and Trendelenburg gait.
- The ischial tuberosity and the pubic ramus are "contained" within the socket wall rather than sitting on a seat; the posterior-medial wall contour gives the lock.
- Load is shared over soft tissue and the ischium; pressure-tolerant tissue gets total contact; high-pressure areas include adductor longus tendon, ischium, pubic ramus, distal femur.

## Steps
1. Measure ML, AP dimension, circumferences, residual length, tuberosity-to-end length.
2. Capture shape (plaster cast or scan) with the femur held in adduction and the pelvis neutral; palpate landmarks.
3. Rectify: relief around bony prominences, compression of fleshy zones; narrow ML to the target; contour the posterior wall to contain the tuberosity.
4. Check socket (transparent): test fit standing and walking, check pressure indicators, adjust trimlines (keep Scarpa's triangle clear, adequate relief for the adductor longus tendon).
5. Laminate or thermoform the definitive socket after fit and alignment.

## Typical problems
- Pinching of the adductors if ML too narrow; distal oedema if poor total contact; ischial pain when containment is incorrect; socket too high on the lateral side.
