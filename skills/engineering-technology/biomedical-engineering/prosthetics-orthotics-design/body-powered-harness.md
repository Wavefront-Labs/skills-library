# Body-powered systems and harness

**Goal:** a reliable, low-cost, proprioceptive prosthesis where body motion pulls a cable to operate the terminal device.

## System
- A harness (commonly figure-of-nine for transradial, figure-of-eight or chest strap alternatives) transmits shoulder motion (scapular abduction, glenohumeral flexion) through a Bowden cable to the terminal device; the harness also assists suspension.
- Control uses limited excursion and force available from the shoulder girdle (a few centimetres of excursion and modest force; confirm from your clinical references), so the system must be efficient.

## Design
1. Place the harness ring and the cable attachments to provide maximum excursion with comfort; anchor the control attachment strap so the pull is not lost to slack.
2. Use a low-friction cable housing with minimal bends; check for fraying and wear.
3. Choose a terminal device that suits available force (voluntary opening spring or band strength).
4. Suspension: the harness helps hold the socket; pair with self-suspending designs where possible.
5. Check that axilla and neck pressure points are padded; use broad straps.

## Common problems
- Cable stretch or friction reducing range, harness slipping, skin irritation in the axilla, and fatigue of the sound side.

## Verify
- [ ] User operates the device with full opening at working positions.
- [ ] No skin breakdown at harness points.
- [ ] Maintenance routine for cables and housing provided.
