# Spinal orthoses

**Goal:** apply the right control to spinal segments with a comfortable, wearable brace. Prescription, fitting and monitoring belong to the clinical team.

## Types
- **Cervical orthoses:** soft collars to rigid collars and halo vests, ranging from comfort and reminder to limiting motion; halo is the most restrictive (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
- **Thoracolumbosacral orthoses (TLSO):** custom or prefabricated shells (bivalve) limit motion in the thoracic and lumbar spine after fracture or surgery.
- **Lumbosacral orthoses (LSO)** and corsets: abdominal compression and reminder.
- **Scoliosis braces:** rigid TLSOs (for example Boston-type) apply three-point pressure systems with relief areas, to limit curve progression in adolescent idiopathic scoliosis; evidence (BrAIST, Weinstein et al., N Engl J Med 2013;369:1512-1521: bracing success 72% versus 48% with observation, patients told to wear the brace at least 18 hours per day, and a significant positive association between hours worn and success) showed more hours of wear associated with greater success.

## Engineering notes
- Three-point bending principle: two opposing forces on one side and one on the other at the apex produce a corrective moment (textbook mechanics (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)).
- Materials: thermoplastic shells (polyethylene, polypropylene), foam liners; shape from scan or cast and CAD rectification.
- Skin: pressure under shell edges; breathing room and ventilation.
- Verification: in-brace imaging by the clinic; wear time compliance (temperature sensors) is useful data.

## Verify
- [ ] Fit and pressure relief assessed on the patient.
- [ ] Brace strength and strap integrity tested.
- [ ] Wear and outcome follow-up planned with clinicians.
