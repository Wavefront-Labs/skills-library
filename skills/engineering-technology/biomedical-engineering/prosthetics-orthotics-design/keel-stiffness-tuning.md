# Keel and heel stiffness tuning

**Goal:** match foot stiffness to the user's body mass and activity so push-off, stability and heel-strike feel natural and safe.

## Method
1. Choose the foot **category** from the manufacturer's chart for body mass (including carried loads) and K-level or activity level; manufacturers rate load limits and stiffness categories for each foot.
2. **Heel stiffness:** too stiff, the knee flexes quickly or the foot slaps late; too soft, the heel bottoms out and the user stumbles on a soft heel.
3. **Keel (forefoot) stiffness:** too soft, the toe collapses with little push-off and a short step on the other side; too stiff, the user vaults and the knee is pushed back (hyperextension on a transtibial) and the heel lifts late; clinical teaching on these symptoms varies by foot design (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
4. **Roll-over shape** (the effective rocker as seen in the shank frame) should resemble that of a normal ankle-foot. Adjust alignment (see bench-alignment-setup) together with stiffness; a stiffness change may require re-alignment.
5. Check shoe heel height with the foot and re-tune if shoes change.
6. Try categories above and below; evaluate in gait on walk and ramp trials.

## Verify
- [ ] Foot load rating exceeds user weight with margin.
- [ ] Observed gait shows smooth roll-over and no knee instability.
- [ ] Final category and alignment recorded.
