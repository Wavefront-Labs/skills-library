# Bone-anchored (osseointegrated) limb prosthesis systems

**Goal:** understand how direct skeletal attachment replaces a socket and what it means for design and safety.

Surgical, regulatory and clinical decisions belong to qualified teams; availability and approval status differ by jurisdiction and device.

## Principle
A titanium implant (fixture) is anchored in the residual bone, with a percutaneous abutment passing through the skin to connect the prosthesis. Load transfers directly to the skeleton, removing socket pressure and suspension issues.

## Design variants
Variant descriptions below are literature summaries (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
- **Threaded** fixtures (screw-in) historically in two surgical stages, one to osseointegrate and one to attach the abutment.
- **Press-fit** stems with porous or hydroxyapatite coating for ingrowth, sometimes in one stage.
- **Intramedullary** implants with a modular external segment.
- Safety link or breakaway component between abutment and limb to limit overload bending or torque on the bone; its release load is a design and risk-file parameter.

## Engineering concerns
- Stress distribution and bone remodelling around the stem; stress shielding; fatigue of the abutment under gait loads.
- The skin-implant interface is permanent and open: infection, stoma care and biofilm risk dominate complications.
- Loading protocol is staged (graded weight-bearing) to protect bone-implant integration.
- Interfaces: quick-release couplings, rotation adapters and safety link; materials and tolerances.

## Verify
- [ ] Fatigue and overload tests of fixture, abutment and safety component with documented loads.
- [ ] Risk file includes infection and mechanical fracture.
- [ ] Clinical and regulatory pathway confirmed for the jurisdiction.
