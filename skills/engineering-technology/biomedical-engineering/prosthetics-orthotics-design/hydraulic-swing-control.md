# Hydraulic and pneumatic swing control

**Goal:** adapt knee flexion and extension resistance in swing to walking speed, giving natural swing at different cadences.

Adjustment is performed by a trained prosthetist following manufacturer settings.

## Why fluid control
- A **constant-friction** knee gives the same swing resistance regardless of speed, so it is tuned for one cadence.
- **Fluid control (hydraulic or pneumatic)** produces resistance that rises with the speed of the shank, so the knee swings more slowly at slow walking and more quickly at fast walking, and extension stops the shank smoothly.

## Mechanics
- A piston moves in a cylinder and forces fluid (oil) or gas through an orifice or valve. For turbulent flow through an orifice, the pressure drop and so the resistance force varies roughly with the **square of the piston speed**; viscous (laminar) flow gives a linear relationship. Orifice area, valve shape and fluid viscosity set the curve.
- Separate valves for **flexion** and **extension** resistance; settings are independent.
- **Stance control** hydraulic knees use a one-way or a yielding resistance in stance flexion to allow controlled flexion with load (for stairs and ramps).
- **Pneumatic** units use air (compressible), lighter but with a less damped response; hydraulic units have better control.

## Settings (clinical sequence)
1. Set the extension stop and the alignment first.
2. Adjust flexion (swing resistance) so that heel rise is natural and the foot clears the ground.
3. Adjust extension resistance so that the shank does not hit the stop hard or lag at heel strike.
4. Check at several speeds.

## Typical problems
- Too little flexion resistance: excessive heel rise; too much: reduced heel rise and circumduction.
- Too little extension resistance: terminal impact; too much: knee not fully extended at heel strike (instability).
- Fluid leaks, air in the cylinder and worn seals change the curve.
- Fluid viscosity falls as the temperature rises, so the knee swings more freely after warm-up or in hot environments and stiffens in cold.

## Verify
- [ ] Gait observation at slow, medium and fast cadences.
- [ ] Maintenance schedule (seal and fluid checks) in the instructions for use.
- [ ] Bench test records resistance versus speed for the production unit.
