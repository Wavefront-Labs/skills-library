# Upper-limb orthoses and splinting

**Goal:** immobilise, support or position joints of the hand, wrist and arm for injury, post-surgical or neurological indications. Splinting is prescribed and supervised by therapists or clinicians.

## Types
- **Static splints:** hold position (resting hand splint, wrist cock-up in a functional wrist extension position).
- **Dynamic splints:** use springs or bands to apply controlled motion or correct contracture.
- **Serial and static progressive splints** to gain range.
- **Functional braces** for fracture support.

## Fabrication
1. Choose thermoplastic by rigidity and moulding behaviour (low-temperature thermoplastics mould by hand in warm water, high-temperature types give a stronger result); consider perforation for ventilation.
2. Mould over the patient's limb positioned as prescribed; avoid pressure over bony points and nerve areas (for example ulnar styloid, dorsal radial nerve).
3. Round all edges; add padding; place straps so pressure is wide.
4. Check circulation, sensation and motion of unsplinted joints at fitting and after the first wear.
5. Provide wear schedule, hygiene and signs to report.

## Verify
- [ ] Joint positions match the prescription.
- [ ] No pressure marks or colour change after trial.
- [ ] Patient demonstrates donning and understands the wear schedule.
