# Stoma care and infection management

**Goal:** protect the skin-implant interface of a percutaneous abutment. Clinical protocols are set by the implanting surgical team; this skill covers the engineering and hygiene understanding.

## Why it matters
- A permanent skin penetration is an infection route to bone. Infection rates are meaningful in published series, with most superficial infections treatable and some deep infections leading to implant removal (confirm rates from the current literature for the system used).

## Practice
1. Stoma design: surgery creates a thin, well-fixed skin lining with minimal soft tissue (a thin skin flap with no mobile tissue around the abutment) to reduce movement and gaps.
2. **Hygiene:** daily cleaning of the stoma and abutment as instructed by the clinic, dry the area, avoid contaminated water immersion unless protected, keep skin clear of debris and pressure.
3. **Surveillance:** redness, swelling, discharge, pain, fever, or loosening are triggers to contact the clinic promptly.
4. **Design features:** smooth abutment finish and coating (for example surface treatments that favour soft tissue adhesion), a protective cap, and components that keep forces low at the interface.
5. Record infection episodes, treatments and outcomes for post-market data.

- [ ] User instructions come from the surgical team protocol for the specific system.
