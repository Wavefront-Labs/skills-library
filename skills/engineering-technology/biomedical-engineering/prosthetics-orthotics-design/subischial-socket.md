# Subischial socket

**Goal:** a transfemoral socket that trims below the ischium for comfort and range of motion while still controlling the femur. The prosthetist decides suitability and fitting.

## Concept
- Compared with ischial containment (see ischial-containment-socket), the proximal trimline is lower; hip flexion and sitting are less restricted, with lighter weight.
- Stability depends on total contact, tissue compression and shape of the sockets' mediolateral dimension, plus suspension (suction or liner) since the ischial seat is not relied on.

## Design logic
1. Good candidates have enough residual limb length and soft tissue and reasonable hip strength; short or very mobile limbs may need more containment.
2. Shape the socket with firm lateral support to hold femur adduction; build in relief at bony areas and compression in muscular regions.
3. Use secure suspension (suction with a liner or valve, or a locking liner), because the socket cannot rely on bony containment against rotation.
4. Test socket (check socket) trials for comfort, adduction control, pistoning and gait before the definitive socket.

## Verify
- [ ] Gait shows no excessive lateral trunk lean or pistoning.
- [ ] Sitting and range of motion acceptable.
- [ ] Skin check after wear trial; fit reviewed with volume change.
