# KAFO and orthotic knee joints

**Goal:** give knee stability in stance, and allow knee flexion in swing where function allows. Prescription and fitting are done by clinicians; this explains the engineering choices.

## Joint types
- **Locked knee (drop lock, ring lock):** straight knee for stance and walking; a manual unlocking lever for sitting; the gait is stiff-legged.
- **Offset joint:** the knee axis lies behind the load line to give stability in stance, with free swing, suitable for mild weakness; limited when the quadriceps is very weak.
- **Bail lock:** a locking bail released by the user or by a pull.
- **Stance control orthosis joints:** automatically lock in stance and release in swing, giving a more natural gait; mechanisms use a cable, a pendulum or a sensor (mechanical or microprocessor). Reliability, weight and cost are issues.
- **Dynamic and spring-assist joints** for partial support.

## Design considerations
- Joint strength for the patient's mass and activity; mediolateral and rotational stiffness of uprights; fit of thigh and calf shells or cuffs; foot plate and ankle joint (AFO component; see afo-design).
- Alignment: axis placement matches anatomical axis; trial with adjustable joints.
- Energy cost and weight; donning and doffing.

## Verify
- [ ] Locking reliability tested through cycles.
- [ ] Alignment verified in standing and walking.
- [ ] User can lock and unlock independently and safely.
