# Alignment measurement tools

**Goal:** make alignment observations repeatable and record them objectively.

## Tools
- **Plumb line and spirit levels:** static reference lines against the body and components.
- **Laser line systems:** project a vertical load line on the patient from a base, to see how the line passes the joints.
- **Load line devices and pylon load cells:** measure forces and moments at the pylon (sagittal and frontal), so alignment can be tuned for target moment patterns, with real-time feedback (such as a smart pyramid or instrumented adapter).
- **Gait labs:** motion capture and force plates give joint angles, GRF and centre of pressure for research and complex cases.
- **Pressure insoles:** distribution of foot loading.

## Practice
1. Define the measurement target (for example knee moment profile at midstance) and acceptable range from literature or the clinic's practice.
2. Calibrate the tool; confirm sign conventions and where the sensor is relative to joints.
3. Measure at a controlled walking speed; repeat for repeatability.
4. Adjust one alignment variable at a time; record the change and the user's comments.
5. Note limits: tool readings do not replace observation, patient comfort and safety.
