# Limb volume management

**Goal:** keep socket fit acceptable through daily and long-term volume change. Clinical decisions belong to the prosthetist and treating team.

## Causes
- Post-amputation shrinkage and maturation over months; daily fluid shifts (activity, heat, sodium and medication, dialysis, heart failure); weight change; socket wear itself.
- A change of a few percent in limb volume can alter pressure distribution and comfort noticeably.

## Strategies
1. **Measure:** circumference at fixed levels, tape or scan volume at standard times of day; log with activity.
2. **Sock ply:** add or remove prosthetic socks of known thickness to take up loss; a sock-ply change record helps decide when the socket is too large.
3. **Liner choice:** thicker or different-durometer liners; gel liners with a shuttle lock.
4. **Adjustable sockets:** panels, dials or bladders that the user tightens or loosens through the day; design must keep alignment and avoid pistoning.
5. **Compression and shrinker garments** early after amputation to control oedema and shape the limb.
6. **Remake or revise** the socket when ply count is excessive or the user cannot keep fit comfortable.

## Verify
- [ ] Baseline volume and times recorded.
- [ ] Skin check for pressure marks after changes.
- [ ] Criteria for socket revision documented.
