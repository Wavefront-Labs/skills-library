# Flexible inner socket with frame

**Goal:** combine a flexible, comfortable inner shell with a stiff outer frame that carries load. A prosthetist selects and fits the design.

## Concept
- The inner socket is a flexible thermoplastic (for example low-density polyethylene or a copolymer) vacuum-formed over the positive model; it adapts to muscle contraction, reducing pressure peaks.
- The rigid frame (laminate, carbon or thermoplastic) has windows or struts that allow the flexible wall to expand in muscular areas and provides structure, suspension attachment and load transfer.

## Design points
1. Wall thickness of the inner socket (commonly a few millimetres) balances flexibility and durability; thin walls tear at trimlines.
2. Window shape and position: open over muscle bulk, supported at bony and load-bearing zones.
3. Edge finishing and trimlines avoid cracks (stress concentrations at corners).
4. Frame-to-inner connection allows the inner socket to be replaced when it wears.
5. Ventilation and heat: flexible walls with windows cool better but need cleaning.

## Failure modes
- Cracks at window corners and trimlines, loosening of the inner socket, discolouration and odour.

## Verify
- [ ] Check socket trial for pressure and gait; adjust windows.
- [ ] Mechanical test of the frame and inner socket per the applicable structural testing (confirm standard, for example ISO 10328 principles for lower limb structural tests).
- [ ] Replacement and cleaning instructions given.
