# Seal-in liner suspension

**Goal:** hold the prosthesis on the limb with an airtight seal between a silicone liner and socket, giving suction without pin locks or sleeves.

Fitting is a prosthetist task under local practice and manufacturer instructions; this explains the design concept.

## How it works
- A silicone or gel liner is rolled on the residual limb. One or more circumferential sealing rings, or a liner with a sealing membrane, contacts the inside of the socket wall.
- The limb-liner-socket assembly is airtight except for an expulsion valve (or valved port); air is pushed out as the limb enters and the negative pressure helps hold the limb during swing.
- Distal gap volume is reduced by a close socket-to-liner fit; the liner is normally slightly oversized in diameter for compression.

## Choosing and sizing
1. Measure circumference at the proximal, mid and distal levels; liner size is chosen to be slightly smaller than the limb at a stated measurement point per manufacturer chart, for light compression.
2. Choose thickness (for example thin for sensitive skin and dynamic use, thicker for cushioning bony residual limbs).
3. Check limb shape: conical shapes seal more easily than bulbous; very short limbs may need another suspension.

## Fitting points
- Liner is donned inside-out and rolled up, with no air bubbles or wrinkles.
- Socket fit is checked in standing and swing; pistoning measured with the limb in socket.
- Daily volume change needs patient guidance (socks, adjustable inserts).

## Failure signs
- Loss of suction or hissing from the valve; a pressure sore at the sealing ring; skin rash from sweating; sleeve or ring wear.

## Verify
- [ ] Pistoning within acceptable limits while walking and during swing.
- [ ] Skin check after first days; remove or revise if breakdown.
- [ ] Liner care and replacement interval explained.
