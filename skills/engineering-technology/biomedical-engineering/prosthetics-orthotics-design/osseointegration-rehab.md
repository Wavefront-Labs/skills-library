# Loading and rehabilitation protocol

**Goal:** explain the structure of rehab after a bone-anchored implant so equipment and test devices support safe progressive loading. Clinical protocols belong to the surgical and rehabilitation team.

## Concept
- Implantation may be in one or two stages (insert the intramedullary component, then later the percutaneous abutment). Protocols differ by system and surgeon.
- Rehabilitation raises load gradually, often starting with static loading using a training prosthesis and a load scale, progressing to partial weight bearing, walking aids and then full weight bearing and a definitive prosthesis, over weeks to months.
- Loading is staged to allow bone adaptation around the implant and avoid early failure (loosening or periprosthetic fracture).

## Engineering support
1. Training prosthesis with adjustable length and a load cell or bathroom scale feedback to measure axial load.
2. A safety component (a breakaway or shear device in some systems) limits torque and bending to protect the bone.
3. Interface between abutment and prosthesis (quick-connect) rated and tested per the structural standards.
4. Data on load history help clinicians adjust.

## Verify
- [ ] Clinical protocol provided by the system maker or surgeon is followed.
- [ ] Load measurement method calibrated.
- [ ] Safety component torque and bending limits verified and labelled.
