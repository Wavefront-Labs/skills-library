# Ankle-foot orthosis (AFO) design

**Goal:** control ankle and foot position and motion to match a patient's deficit (foot drop, spasticity, weakness), within comfortable, durable shape.

Orthotic prescription and fitting are done by certified clinicians; this explains engineering choices.

## Types
- **Posterior leaf spring (PLS):** trimlines behind the malleoli; flexibility gives dorsiflexion assist for mild foot drop.
- **Solid AFO:** fixed at the ankle (about neutral), limits plantar and dorsiflexion; used for stronger spasticity or instability.
- **Hinged (articulated) AFO:** ankle joint with plantarflexion stop and optional dorsiflexion assist or free motion; allows ankle motion in stance and stair use.
- **Ground reaction AFO (GRAFO):** anterior shell to resist knee flexion in crouch gait.
- **Carbon composite spring AFO:** lightweight, energy-storing posterior strut.

## Design variables
- **Trimline** position relative to the malleoli and the calf: posterior = more flexible, anterior of the malleoli = stiffer.
- **Thickness and material:** thermoplastics (polypropylene, co-polymer) 3-6 mm are common; thicker or corrugated stiffens; composites tune stiffness by layup.
- **Ankle angle:** set in neutral or slight dorsiflexion; may be tuned to knee alignment.
- **Footplate** length and shape: full-length with toe lever or to metatarsal heads; match footwear.
- **Interfaces:** padding at bony prominences; straps with enough width to share pressure.

## Fitting checks
- Heel remains seated; no pistoning; skin check after first wear; walking assessment (foot clearance in swing, stance stability).
- Stiffness measured on a bench (torque versus angle) when comparing designs.

## Verify
- [ ] Gait goal met (clearance, stance control) and no pressure marks after wear.
- [ ] Shoe fits with orthosis.
- [ ] Mechanical test shows stiffness in the intended range.
