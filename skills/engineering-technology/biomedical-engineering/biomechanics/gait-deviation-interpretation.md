# Interpreting gait deviations

**Goal:** turn kinematic, kinetic and EMG curves into defensible statements about what deviates, why, and what is uncertain. Clinical decisions belong to the treating team.

## Method
1. Check data quality first (spatiotemporal sanity, consistent events, kinematic marker artefact) before reading deviations.
2. Compare to age-matched reference data, and adjust for walking speed; many deviations are partly speed effects.
3. Read sagittal then frontal then transverse plane, by gait phase (initial contact, loading, midstance, terminal stance, swing).
4. Identify the primary deviation (arising from the impairment) versus compensations elsewhere (for example a trunk lean to unload a weak abductor, or vaulting for foot clearance).
5. Common patterns: **foot drop** (reduced swing clearance, steppage or circumduction), **crouch** (excess stance knee flexion), **stiff-knee** (reduced peak swing knee flexion), **equinus** (plantarflexed at contact), **recurvatum** (knee hyperextension), **Trendelenburg** (pelvic drop or trunk lean with weak abductors), **antalgic** (short stance on the painful side).
6. A summary index such as the Gait Deviation Index (GDI) scales to 100 for typical gait with about 10 points per standard deviation below; useful for tracking, not for diagnosis.
7. Report the repeatability and the minimum detectable change; one trial is not a finding.

## Verify
- [ ] Reference data population and speed-matching stated.
- [ ] Primary versus compensatory deviations reasoned and not over-claimed.
- [ ] Uncertainty (marker error, trial count) noted.
