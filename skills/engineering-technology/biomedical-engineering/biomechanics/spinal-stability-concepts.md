# Spinal stability concepts

**Goal:** use a consistent vocabulary and test logic when judging how implants affect segment stability.

## Concepts
- **Three subsystems (Panjabi):** the passive subsystem (vertebrae, discs, ligaments, facets), the active subsystem (muscles and tendons) and the neural control subsystem that coordinates them. Stability needs adequate function of all three.
- **Neutral zone (NZ):** the part of the range of motion near the neutral position where little resistance exists. Injury and degeneration tend to increase NZ more than total range of motion, so NZ is a sensitive indicator of instability.
- **Range of motion (ROM) and stiffness** are reported alongside NZ from a load-displacement curve.
- **Clinical instability** (White and Panjabi) is a clinical judgement about loss of the spine's ability to keep patterns of displacement under physiological loads without neurological deficit or deformity; it is not a purely mechanical number.

## Implant testing logic
1. Test intact, injured (destabilised) and instrumented states of the same specimen with pure moments in flexion-extension, lateral bending and axial rotation (see motion-segment-testing).
2. Compare ROM, NZ and stiffness; fixation should reduce motion toward the intact state without over-constraining adjacent levels.
3. Adjacent level motion and loads changes are a consideration; report them.
4. In vitro results are time-zero; healing, muscle action and fatigue behaviour are not included.

## Verify
- [ ] Loading protocol (moment, preload) stated.
- [ ] NZ reported, not only ROM.
- [ ] Limits of in vitro extrapolation to patients stated.
