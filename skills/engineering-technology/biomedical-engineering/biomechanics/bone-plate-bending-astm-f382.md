# Metallic bone plate bending (F382)

**Goal:** report comparable bending stiffness, structural stiffness and strength of bone plates.

## Method
1. Mount the plate with the load applied on the bending plane in four-point bending, with loading and support rollers at the spacing and dimensions the standard specifies (confirm in the text (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)). The fatigue annex establishes a uniform four-point bending test; ASTM states the results compare designs and do not predict in vivo performance.
2. Apply a load at a controlled rate and record load versus displacement; determine bending stiffness from the linear portion, bending structural stiffness (the stiffness including the plate geometry), and bending strength (load at an offset yield; definitions per the standard (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)).
3. Fatigue: cyclic four-point bending to a defined run-out at a stated stress ratio and frequency (values per the standard (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)), to obtain fatigue strength.
4. Test several plates; report mean and range; note failure location (usually through a screw hole).

## Practice
- The plate's weakest section is at the holes; orient the test so the hole section lies in the constant moment region.
- Compare to a predicate plate in the same fixture; stiffness differences in bending also drive stress shielding.
- Simulating the bone-plate system in the clinic (screw fixation, gap, bone) is a separate test.

## Verify
- [ ] Roller spacing and support type recorded per the standard.
- [ ] Orientation and hole position stated.
- [ ] Fatigue run-out and stress ratio stated.
