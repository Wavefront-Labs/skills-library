# TENS device design

**Goal:** a safe constant-current stimulator that delivers comfortable, effective pulses through skin electrodes.

## Parameters (typical ranges; every range is (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked))
- Pulse shape: balanced biphasic or asymmetric biphasic pulses to avoid net DC (see charge-balanced-waveforms).
- Frequency commonly from a few Hz up to about 100 to 150 Hz; pulse width of tens to hundreds of microseconds; amplitude adjustable to the user's threshold, in the tens of milliamps maximum at the load specified in the standard.
- Modes: continuous, burst, modulated.

## Electronics
1. **Constant-current output stage** (see constant-current-stimulator) with voltage compliance high enough for electrode and skin impedance variation (up to kilohms), generated by a boost converter from the battery.
2. **Current limiting, open-circuit and lead-off detection**, output monitoring and a single-fault safe state.
3. **Electrodes:** adequate area keeps current density low (burn risk with small or lifted electrodes); sticky electrodes with gel; skin irritation checks.
4. Software: ramped start, interlock to stop on electrode removal, timers and logging.

## Safety and regulatory
- Particular standard IEC 60601-2-10 sets output limits and test loads; home use adds IEC 60601-1-11 (see home-healthcare-environment); classification and exemptions differ by market (in the US OTC TENS is regulated under a device classification regulation; confirm current text and any prescription use conditions).
- Contraindications and precautions in the labelling (for example pacemakers, pregnancy, near the neck or eyes) must come from the clinical risk analysis and the standard.

## Verify
- [ ] Output waveform measured into specified loads and compared to limits.
- [ ] Single-fault tests (short circuit, open circuit, component failure).
- [ ] Usability testing with lay users.

> Practice guidance, not regulatory or clinical advice. Confirm current standard editions and rules for your jurisdiction, and defer to qualified regulatory, clinical and engineering judgement.
