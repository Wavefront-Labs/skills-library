Safety-critical, regulated work. This is professional practice guidance: confirm the current regulation, edition and market, and defer sign-off to qualified safety and homologation engineers.

# Rescue sheet and emergency response information

## 1. Purpose
Firefighters and rescue crews need quick, reliable information to cut and move a vehicle safely without hitting airbag inflators, high-strength members or HV cables.

## 2. Content of a rescue sheet
Plan and side view diagrams (usually one or two pages) show:
- Airbag modules and gas generators, belt pretensioners, seat belt locks and active head restraints.
- Reinforcements and ultra-high strength steel zones (hot-stamped pillars, door beams) that standard tools cannot cut; recommended cut points.
- HV battery, cables, inverter, disconnect points, orange cabling; fuel tank and lines; gas struts and pyrotechnic bonnet lifters.
- 12 V battery location and disconnect, fuse box, emergency shut-off or service disconnect.
- Door and glass information (laminated glass areas, fixing of the roof), roof removal strategy, dashboard lift points.
- Vehicle identification (type of propulsion).
The ISO 17840 series standardises the layout and symbols for rescue sheets and emergency response guides and for propulsion-energy identification and quick-response codes; confirm the current parts.

## 3. Delivery
Printable PDF for each model year and body style, on the manufacturer's responder website; a label or QR code at a standard location (such as the fuel flap and/or B-pillar, as the standard describes) so responders can retrieve the right sheet; consumer ratings such as Euro NCAP include rescue information assessment.

## 4. HV and propulsion information
Identify HV by labels and under-bonnet markings; instruct on isolating the HV system (service plug, emergency loop cut), and warn about residual voltage, battery fire hazards and the possibility of delayed thermal runaway; include guidance on extinguishing media (copy supplier-approved text; consult fire-service advisers).

## 5. Quality process
Keep it synchronised with design changes (new airbag, pillar change); validate with a fire service or rescue trainer review; include translations; have the documentation owner sign off each release.

## Verify
- [ ] Every restraint component, HV and fuel element shown with recommended cut zones.
- [ ] Vehicle propulsion identification and retrieval code in the standard positions.
- [ ] Sheet updated with each design change and reviewed by rescue experts.
