# Door beam and sill design

1. Objectives: limit intrusion distance (so the occupant is not struck), and **intrusion velocity** (so loads on the body stay low), while absorbing energy over the available crush space (commonly cited as 150-200 mm (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)).
2. Door beams: high-strength tube or hot-stamped beam, one or more in the door, with end brackets spreading load into the pillars; mid-span stiffness controls displacement; consider beams at pelvis and chest heights.
3. Sill: strong, reinforced rocker with internal bulkheads or extrusions, loaded in bending; ties to floor cross-members and seat cross-member.
4. B-pillar: high bending strength at belt line; tailored strength (hot-stamped with a softer lower zone) for controlled folding and to avoid sill pivot.
5. Load transfer: cross-members spread load to the opposite side; seat structure and tunnel are load-sharing members.
6. Door interior: energy absorbers (foam, plastic honeycomb) at pelvis and abdomen; arm rest design.
7. Check with the barrier and pole configurations; check opening after the crash.

**Gotchas:** stiff door increases intrusion velocity at the occupant if the sill collapses.

## Verify
- [ ] Intrusion and velocity at chest, abdomen and pelvis heights meet targets in CAE and test.
- [ ] Doors open after the test per protocol.
