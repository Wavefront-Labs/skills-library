# Powertrain intrusion control

1. Risk: a rigid engine or transmission impacts the dash and drives it rearward; the rigid mass limits the available crush stroke.
2. Package: shorten the stack of rigid components; allow the engine to move down or sideways (engine dive, subframe collapse or release) rather than into the cell; use a ramp or deflector on the dash lower cross-member.
3. Dash structure: strengthen the toe-pan and dash cross-member with ultra-high-strength steel; pedal break-away or collapse.
4. Measurements: footwell intrusion (toe pan and pedals) and A-pillar/IP displacement are rated in regulations and consumer tests; set targets in mm for each location.
5. Subframe: design mounting bolts and subframe shape to detach or fold predictably; avoid subframe striking the dash.
6. EV: electric motor, inverter and reducers have different mass distribution and often leave more crush space in front but may include a front HV cabling and cooling hardware; maintain HV cable routing safe from deformation.
7. Verify with CAE and barrier tests with sensors on the dash and pedals.

**Gotchas:** rigid components such as the steering rack and HVAC unit intrude later in the event.

## Verify
- [ ] Footwell and pedal intrusion measured in test within target.
- [ ] HV components and cables remain intact after the barrier tests.
