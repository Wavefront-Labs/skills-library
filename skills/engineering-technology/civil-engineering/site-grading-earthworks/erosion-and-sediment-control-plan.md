# Erosion and sediment control plan

Principle: prevent erosion first (cover, minimise disturbed area, divert clean water); trapping sediment is the less efficient last resort.

## Plan content
1. Site assessment: soils (dispersive or sodic clays need special treatment), slopes, drainage paths, receiving waters, sensitive areas, rainfall erosivity in the construction season.
2. Phasing: clear only what is built next; stabilise within the permit time after work stops (often 7 to 14 days). Keep vegetated buffers.
3. Controls by purpose: clean-water diversions; stabilised construction entrance; perimeter sediment fence or compost sock on contour with ends turned uphill; check dams in small ditches; inlet protection; slope cover (mulch, blankets, hydroseeding); temporary and permanent seeding.
4. Sediment basin: required above a disturbed-area threshold set by the permit (about 2 ha / 5 acres is common, check yours). Storage rules differ (a figure near 3,600 ft3 per acre appears in many US permits); state the rule applied. Use a skimmer outlet and baffles.
5. RUSLE A = R K LS C P ranks hot spots; it is not a regulatory prediction.
6. Inspect after significant rain and at permit frequency; clean out sediment before fences or traps are about half full; keep records and corrective actions per the permit (SWPPP or local equivalent).
7. Final stabilisation (vegetation or hard cover) before controls are removed.

## Verify
Controls on contour with no concentrated flow around the ends; discharge within permit limits; plan matches the staged earthworks drawings.
