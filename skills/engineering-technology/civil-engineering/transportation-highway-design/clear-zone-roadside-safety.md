# Roadside safety and clear zone

Goal: give an errant vehicle a forgiving recovery area, or shield hazards that cannot be removed.

## Method
1. Clear-zone distance from the Roadside Design Guide tables by design speed, traffic volume and foreslope or backslope (faster and steeper means wider). Adjust on horizontal curves. Many agencies apply their own tables: use the one that governs the project.
2. Treat hazards inside the zone in order: remove, redesign (make traversable), relocate, reduce severity (breakaway supports), shield, delineate.
3. Slopes: about 4H:1V or flatter is traversable; 3H:1V is recoverable but not safely traversable; steeper may warrant a barrier.
4. Barrier warrant: a barrier is itself an obstacle; install only if hitting it is less severe than the hazard it shields. Use hardware tested to the current criteria (MASH in the US).
5. Barrier details: length of need, flare rate, working width behind the barrier, height, post spacing, crashworthy terminal.
6. For borderline cases use the Roadside Safety Analysis Program (RSAP) cost-benefit.

## Gotchas
- Hardware acceptance changes over time; verify current FHWA/agency acceptance letters.
- Culvert headwalls and ditches are roadside hazards: use traversable end treatments.

## Verify
Plot the clear-zone limit on plan and cross-sections and list every object inside it with its disposition.
