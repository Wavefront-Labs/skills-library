# Subsurface drip installation and management

**Goal:** long-life buried driplines that deliver uniformly and are not sucked shut or invaded by roots.

## Layout
- Depth: commonly 15-45 cm (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked), set by crop, tillage practice and germination method (shallower where seeds are germinated with the dripline). Spacing set by crop row and soil wetting width.
- Install with a precision shank at consistent depth; use GNSS guidance to repeat rows for planting and tillage.

## Protection
1. **Vacuum relief valves** at high points and submain ends to prevent soil sucked into emitters at shutdown.
2. **Check valves** or anti-siphon design where needed.
3. **Root intrusion**: emitters with herbicide impregnation or physical barriers; occasional chemical treatment as allowed; maintain flow and pressure; do not under-irrigate.
4. **Rodents and insects**: keep weeds down; use buried tape with burial depth; monitor damage.

## Management
- Flushing: flush ends with high velocities at the start and frequently; flush lines to remove particles.
- Monitoring: install flow meters and pressure gauges on each zone and log; drop in flow indicates damage or clogging.
- Salinity: salts accumulate above the dripline; leach with rain or sprinkling before planting.

## Gotchas
- Compaction from machine traffic can crush lines.
- No visible inspection; rely on flow and pressure data.
- Tillage must avoid cutting lines; mark depth and location.

## Verify
- [ ] Zone flow and pressure within the tolerance set at commissioning (for example 5 percent (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)) of baseline each season.
