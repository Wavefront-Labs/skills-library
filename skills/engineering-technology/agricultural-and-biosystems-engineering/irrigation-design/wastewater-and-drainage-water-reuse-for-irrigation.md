# Wastewater and drainage water reuse

**Goal:** use marginal water safely and sustainably, protecting health, soil and crop.

## Regulation first
Reuse is regulated: treatment level, pathogen limits, crop restrictions, buffer distances and monitoring depend on the jurisdiction. Reference frameworks include the WHO Guidelines for the safe use of wastewater, excreta and greywater, Volume 2: Wastewater use in agriculture (2006), the US EPA Guidelines for Water Reuse (EPA/600/R-12/618, 2012, informational only; the US has no federal reuse regulation), and state or national rules (California Code of Regulations Title 22, Division 4, Chapter 3, water recycling criteria, is a well-known one; section 60304 sets treatment levels by crop and irrigation use). Confirm the regime that applies and obtain permits before design.

## Quality parameters
- Pathogens (indicator organisms and helminth eggs), salinity (EC), SAR, nutrients (N and P; N in effluent can be a credit or an excess), boron, chloride, heavy metals and trace organics, suspended solids and BOD.
- Drainage water: high salts, selenium and boron in some regions; reuse by blending, or on salt-tolerant crops in sequence.

## System design
- Drip systems clog from suspended solids and biofilm: fine filtration, chlorine or other treatment and frequent flushing (`emitter-clogging-diagnosis-and-treatment`).
- Marked pipe and signs (many US jurisdictions use purple pipe (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)), no cross connection with potable water, backflow protection.
- Contact limits with edible crops: drip or furrow irrigation reduces contact; avoid spray on crops eaten raw unless the standard permits.
- Salinity management with leaching, and nutrient accounting in the nutrient plan.
