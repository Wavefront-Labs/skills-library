# Drain pipe sizing, envelope and outlet

**Goal:** a pipe network that carries the design flow without surcharge, does not silt up, and has a secure outlet.

## Sizing
- Flow in a lateral is q x area drained (`drainage-coefficient-selection`), accumulated along the mains.
- Size by Manning's equation for full-pipe gravity flow; Manning n depends on the pipe (smooth-wall plastic is lower than corrugated; use the manufacturer or NRCS value). Add a safety margin because flow is not quite uniform.
- Grade: drains need enough slope for self-cleaning; use the design guide's minimum for the pipe size and soil, and note that the steeper grades also limit pipe size.
- Depth and cover: depth determined by drainage design, with minimum cover for traffic and a maximum for the pipe's crush rating.

## Envelope and filter
- Fine sands and silts can enter openings and silt pipes. Use a gravel envelope or a geotextile filter sized to the soil gradation per the drainage manual; clay soils usually do not need one.
- Heavy iron ochre sites need envelopes and maintenance plans.

## Outlet
- Free outlet above the ditch normal water level, with an animal guard and a rigid pipe section with erosion protection and an identification marker; avoid submerged outlets unless a controlled structure (water table management) is intended.
- Controlled drainage structures can hold water in off-season to reduce nitrate loss.

## Verify
- [ ] As-built grade checked by laser or camera inspection.
- [ ] Flow measured at the outlet after the first significant rains.
- Manning n values, minimum grades, envelope gradation and outlet details are not given here (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked); use the manufacturer and NRCS or ASABE drainage guidance. Drainage design is work for a qualified drainage engineer under local regulation.
