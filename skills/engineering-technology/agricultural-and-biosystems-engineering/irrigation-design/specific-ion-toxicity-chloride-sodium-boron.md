# Specific ion toxicity: chloride, sodium and boron

**Goal:** prevent crop injury from individual ions at concentrations too low to show as general salinity.

## Guidelines
FAO 29 (1985) provides rough thresholds for surface irrigation of sensitive crops. FAO 29 Rev. 1 (1985) Table 1 places boron below about 0.7 mg/L as no problem, between 0.7 and 3.0 as increasing, and above 3.0 as severe; for chloride below about 4 meq/L no problem, 4-10 increasing, above 10 severe; for sodium (surface irrigation) SAR below 3 no problem, 3-9 increasing, above 9 severe. For overhead sprinkling the table gives sodium and chloride below 3 meq/L as no problem and above 3 as increasing. Table values checked against the FAO 29 Rev. 1 text; tolerances are crop specific (tree crops, vines and berries are generally sensitive to Na and Cl, many cereals and cotton more tolerant).

## Sprinkler foliar uptake
Overhead sprinkling lets leaves absorb Na and Cl directly; sensitive crops can be injured at lower concentrations than by surface irrigation. Irrigate at night or low evaporation conditions, use lower frequency and larger droplets, or switch to drip or low-angle systems.

## Management
- Leaching fraction to move salts below the root zone (use the leaching requirement from ECw and crop tolerance).
- Change rootstock or crop to tolerant ones, particularly for boron and chloride.
- Blend with better water; avoid applying saline water in sensitive stages.
- Tissue analysis (leaf Na, Cl, B) confirms uptake.
