# Air release and pressure regulating valves

**Goal:** no trapped air, no vacuum collapse, and regulated pressures along the pipeline.

## Air valves
- Types: air release (small orifice, releases accumulated air under pressure), air and vacuum (large orifice, exhausts air on filling and admits air when draining), and combination (both).
- Locate at high points, long uphill sections ahead of slope changes, downstream of pumps and valves, and at long horizontal runs at intervals as in the design manual. AWWA Manual M51 (Air-Release, Air/Vacuum, and Combination Air Valves; second edition 2016, listed as reaffirmed Nov 2025 on store.awwa.org) is the standard reference for air valves in water systems; follow manufacturer sizing charts for the pipe size and flow.
- Install an isolating valve below each air valve. Protect from freezing.
- Large-orifice vacuum valves should be sized so the inflow of air prevents the pressure falling below a safe negative value.
- Choose non-slam designs or throttled-exhaust types where fast closing of the float would cause surge.

## Pressure regulation
- Pressure-reducing valves keep the downstream pressure at set value; set the pilot spring; install with upstream strainer and isolation. Provide a relief or a bypass where required.
- Pressure-sustaining valves hold upstream pressure at a set value (useful for filling lines).
- Check for cavitation when the pressure drop ratio is large (select a valve with cavitation limits or use two stages).
