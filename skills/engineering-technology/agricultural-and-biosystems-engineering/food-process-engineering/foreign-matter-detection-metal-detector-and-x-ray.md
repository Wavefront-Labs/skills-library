# Foreign matter detection: metal detector and X-ray

**Goal:** detect and reject contaminated product reliably and prove it routinely.

## Technologies
- **Metal detector**: balanced coil; detects ferrous, non-ferrous and stainless steel fragments; sensitivity depends on metal type (ferrous easiest, stainless hardest), product signal (wet, salty, conductive products create a product effect) and aperture size (smaller aperture is more sensitive).
- **X-ray**: detects dense contaminants (metal, glass, stone, bone) by density difference; can also check fill levels and missing components; not suited to low-density plastics or thin items.
- **Others**: magnets, sieves, vision systems, optical sorters.

## Installation
1. Position as late as possible after final processing; ideally after packaging for ingredients and finished goods (foil may defeat metal detectors, so X-ray is used).
2. Reject device (air blast, pusher, diverter) with a lockable bin and a sensor to confirm rejection.
3. Mount to avoid vibration and electrical interference; metal-free zone around the aperture.

## Verification
Pass certified test pieces (ferrous, non-ferrous, stainless; sizes per the plant's sensitivity targets) through the product stream at the start and end of production and at intervals (often hourly), recording the result; test pieces placed in the product path at the worst-case positions. Failures trigger holding product since the last passed check.

## Gotchas
- Setting sensitivity from air tests alone is wrong; set with product.
- Track rejects to find the contamination source.
