# Hygienic zoning and plant layout

**Goal:** prevent cross-contamination from raw materials, people, air and water to ready-to-eat product.

## Zoning
- Typical zones: raw/low-risk, processing (basic hygiene), high-care and high-risk (ready-to-eat after the kill step). Physical barriers separate them.
- Map flows: raw materials, packaging, waste, people, equipment and air. Aim for one-way flow from raw to finished; avoid crossings, and where they are unavoidable, separate them in time with cleaning between.

## Design features
1. **Personnel**: changing rooms with hand-wash stations, boot washes and a step-over bench at zone boundaries; separate colour-coded clothing by zone.
2. **Air**: higher pressure in high-care than in adjacent areas; filtered and conditioned air with cleanable ducting.
3. **Floors and drains**: sloped floors, drains sized for flows, no drain flow from a low-hygiene to a high-hygiene zone; trench drains with removable covers.
4. **Surfaces**: coved wall-floor junctions, smooth washable surfaces, sealed penetrations.
5. **Utilities**: compressed air, steam and water quality (potable); backflow protection.
6. **Equipment**: leave service access space and cleaning clearance around machines.

## Environmental monitoring
Listeria and Salmonella environmental monitoring programs verify zoning performance with swabs of Zone 1 (food contact) to Zone 4 (outside processing areas) sites, per the plant's hazard analysis and regulatory requirements.

- [ ] Smoke test or pressure readings confirm air flows from clean to less clean areas.
