# Seal integrity and leak testing

**Goal:** packages that stay closed and hermetic through filling, processing, distribution and shelf life.

## Flexible packs and trays
- Seal strength: peel or tensile test of a seal strip (ASTM F88 is the common method (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked); check the edition); record peak and failure mode (peel, tear, delamination). Set a minimum from the product and process (retort pouches need to survive the process pressure).
- Visual: wrinkles, channels, contamination in the seal, creases: inspect samples at set intervals.
- Leak tests: bubble emission test (submerge the pack in water in a vacuum chamber and look for bubbles; ASTM D3078, Determination of Leaks in Flexible Packaging by Bubble Emission), gross leak testing by internal pressurisation (ASTM F2096), and dye penetration of seals in porous medical packaging (ASTM F1929; confirm it suits your food pack before using it). Tracer-gas and vacuum decay systems are used online or at-line in some plants.
- Online: vision systems for seal defects, checkweighers, and inline leak detectors for MAP packs.

## Cans and jars
- Double seam inspection for metal cans: measure seam thickness, tightness (wrinkle), overlap, and body hook and cover hook lengths with a seam micrometer or by tear-down or cross-section; the can maker's specification gives the limits. Seamer set-up checks at start and after each adjustment.
- Vacuum or headspace and closure torque for glass jars; thermal process lines also check for can distortion.
- Post-process cooling water must be chlorinated or otherwise controlled, because small leaks can pull contamination into the can during cooling.

## Records and response
Sampling frequency, limits, and responses (hold, re-test and re-inspect) set in the HACCP plan; trend data to catch drifts.
