# Retort types, operation and deviation handling

**Goal:** deliver the scheduled process every batch and respond correctly when it fails.

## Types
- **Still steam retort** (saturated steam): venting removes air; a vent schedule is critical because trapped air creates cold spots. Used for metal cans and glass.
- **Water immersion / water spray / steam-air**: used for pouches, trays and glass; over-pressure with air protects containers from deformation or lid damage; circulation ensures temperature uniformity.
- **Agitating (rotary, shaka, reciprocating)**: reduces process times for viscous products; the process must be established for the agitation conditions.
- **Continuous (hydrostatic, rotary valve)** for high volume.

## Operation
1. Follow the scheduled process exactly: initial temperature, venting, come-up time, process temperature and time, cooling.
2. Instruments (21 CFR 113.40): mercury-in-glass reference thermometer or equivalent (accurate to 0.5 C), temperature recorder-controller, pressure gauge; calibrate as the regulation and plan require.
3. Retort temperature distribution survey (TD) shows cold spots in the vessel; repeat on changes.
4. Cooling water must be chlorinated or otherwise sanitised with a measurable residual (21 CFR 113.60(b)) to limit can seam contamination.

## Deviations
If the process is lost or not documented to be met (low temperature, short time, vent error), the lot must be isolated; the process authority or qualified person evaluates (reprocess, destroy or test) per the applicable regulation (for US low-acid foods, 21 CFR 113.89 sets process deviation handling). Records are kept and reviewed.

## Gotchas
- Operators of low-acid retorts need approved training (21 CFR 113.10: supervisors must have attended an FDA-approved school, e.g. Better Process Control School).
- Pressure vessel codes apply to retort fabrication and inspection.

- [ ] Chart records match the schedule; deviations logged with disposition.
