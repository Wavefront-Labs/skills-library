# Particle size analysis and sieving

**Goal:** a repeatable particle size distribution for specification, process control and energy calculation.

## Sieving
1. Take a representative sample using a riffle splitter or rotary sample divider.
2. Dry sample if needed; weigh a sample size suited to the sieve stack (not overloaded).
3. Stack sieves (square-mesh test sieves to a standard such as ISO 3310 or ASTM E11) in decreasing aperture; shake for a fixed time on a mechanical shaker (a typical time is 10 minutes, set from a convergence test), with agitation aids for sticky powders.
4. Weigh each fraction, check that total recovery is within about 1 percent.
5. Compute mass fraction retained, cumulative undersize, and the median size.
- ASABE standard S319 (fineness of feed materials by sieving) defines the geometric mean diameter by mass, dgw, and the geometric standard deviation, Sgw; use the current edition.

## Other methods
Laser diffraction for fine powders and suspensions (D10, D50, D90; wet or dry dispersion; assumes spherical particles); image analysis for shape; microscopy; sedimentation. Results from different methods are not interchangeable: report the method.

## Practice
- Do not use sieving below about 40 micrometres (blinding); for flours, air-jet sieving helps.
- Static and moisture cause clumping; anti-static aids or conditioned sample.
- Report shaker time, sample mass, sieve series and standard.

## Verify
- [ ] Repeat sieving gives similar D50 (replicate scatter below a few percent).
- [ ] Reference material (such as certified glass beads) checked periodically.
