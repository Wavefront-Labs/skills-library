# Drainability, dead legs and weld quality review

**Goal:** find and fix places where product or cleaning liquid can stagnate.

## Checklist
1. **Dead legs** (stagnant branches): keep as short as possible; common guidance is a length of no more than about 2 pipe diameters measured from the flowing stream (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) (check the specific 3-A or EHEDG requirement for the equipment). Use zero-dead-leg valves and tees where available.
2. **Drainability**: pipes and equipment slope to a drain (about 1-2 percent (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)), no horizontal pockets, bottoms sloped, outlets at the lowest point. Pipe supports must not trap water.
3. **Crevices and joints**: avoid threaded connections, overlapping plates, hollow sections and open bolt heads in product zones; seal fully welded hollow frames.
4. **Welds**: check for undercut, lack of fusion, porosity, pits and heat tint; use a borescope in tubing; radius corners.
5. **Instruments** (probe fittings, sight glasses, sampling valves) mounted in hygienic fittings flush with the pipe.
6. **Exterior**: slope tops of equipment, keep legs and feet open and cleanable, keep gap of about 100-150 mm above floor (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) where recommended.

## Inspection
Walk-through with a flashlight and mirror, borescope and visual inspection at shutdown; dye penetrant for weld defects; document findings with photographs and corrective actions.

## Gotchas
- Retrofits often add dead legs (extra ports and sensors).
- Gaskets worn or poorly seated create crevices.
