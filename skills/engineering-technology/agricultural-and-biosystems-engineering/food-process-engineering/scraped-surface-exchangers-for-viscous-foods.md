# Scraped-surface exchangers for viscous foods

**Goal:** heat or cool products that foul, crystallise or are too viscous for plate or tube units.

## How it works
A rotating shaft with spring-loaded or fixed blades scrapes the heat-transfer wall. The scraping renews the boundary layer, so the product-side film coefficient depends mainly on rotor speed and number of blades rather than on the product velocity. This keeps U workable for viscous materials and breaks up fouling or crystal layers.

## Where used
Ice cream and sorbet freezing, margarine and shortening crystallisation (the chilling and working of fat), viscous sauces, fruit preparations with particles, and cooked dough or paste where tube fouling is severe.

## Selection
- Area from the duty and U from the maker's tests on a similar product (use pilot tests for new products).
- Rotor speed: higher speed raises U but also viscous heat dissipation, shear damage and power draw.
- Gap and blade type set by particle size.
- Viscous dissipation can be a heat source; account for it in cooling duty. Heat of crystallisation is released during fat or ice crystallisation.
- Motor power from the product viscosity, rotor speed and the maker's data.

## Operation and hygiene
Seals and bearings are the main wear points; fit sanitary seals with flush or steam barrier where needed; CIP is possible if rotor clearances allow, as validated by the maker.
