# Hazard analysis and CCP determination

**Goal:** a documented, defensible set of controls for biological, chemical and physical hazards.

## Steps (Codex HACCP structure)
1. **Team** with product, engineering, microbiology and operations expertise; for US regulated plants a Preventive Controls Qualified Individual (PCQI) oversees the food-safety plan under FSMA Preventive Controls rules (21 CFR Part 117), and a HACCP-trained person for seafood, juice and meat regimes under their own rules.
2. **Describe the product** and intended use and consumers (vulnerable groups).
3. **Flow diagram**, verified on the floor.
4. **Hazard analysis**: for each step list biological, chemical (including allergens) and physical hazards; evaluate severity and likelihood; decide which are significant.
5. **Control measures**: choose process controls (kill step, cooling, pH), allergen controls, sanitation controls and supply-chain controls.
6. **CCPs**: use a decision tree; steps where control is essential and measurable (cooking, pasteurising, metal detection after the last step). Not every control is a CCP; many are prerequisite or preventive controls with their own monitoring.
7. **Critical limits** with scientific support (time, temperature, pH, aw, concentration).
8. Then monitoring, corrective action, verification and records (see monitoring skill).

## Gotchas
- Copying a generic plan without verifying the plant's actual process.
- Re-run the analysis on any process, product or supplier change.
- Legal requirements depend on product category and country.
