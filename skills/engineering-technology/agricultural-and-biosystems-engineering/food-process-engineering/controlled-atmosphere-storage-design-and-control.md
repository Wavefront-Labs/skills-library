# Controlled-atmosphere storage design and control

**Goal:** slow ripening and senescence by lowering O2 and raising CO2 at low temperature, without causing fermentation.

## Design
- **Gas-tight room**: sealed doors and panels, pressure relief and equalising bag; check with a pressure-decay test before each season (the acceptance criterion is defined by the maker or industry guideline).
- **Gas control**: O2 reduced by nitrogen generator, or by respiration; CO2 removed by scrubbers (activated carbon or lime) or added; ethylene scrubbers or oxidisers.
- **Refrigeration** and humidity per cold-room design (see the load skill); fans must be moisture-compatible.

## Set points
Atmosphere regimes are species and cultivar specific; examples for apples fall in the range of roughly 1-3 percent O2 and 1-5 percent CO2 at about 0-3 C, with ultra-low oxygen at about 1 percent (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked). Use current regimes from postharvest research and the variety's recommendations.

## Control and monitoring
- Analysers calibrated with certified gases; sample line scheduling across rooms.
- **Dynamic CA**: monitor respiratory quotient (RQ = CO2 produced / O2 consumed) or chlorophyll fluorescence to find the lowest safe O2 without fermentation (the RQ threshold for fermentation is cultivar-specific, commonly cited around 1.2-1.5 (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)).
- Logging and alarms for out-of-range gases and temperature.

## Safety
Low-oxygen rooms are confined-space hazards: entry only with oxygen monitoring, ventilation and a rescue plan, in accordance with local confined-space regulations.
