# Sanitiser selection and validation

**Goal:** a sanitation step that demonstrably reduces microbial load without leaving unacceptable residues or damaging equipment.

## Selection factors
- **Chemistry**: chlorine-based (hypochlorite), peracetic acid (PAA), quaternary ammonium compounds, hot water or steam, and alcohol-based sprays for non-food-contact surfaces. Check the product label or regulatory approval for permitted use, concentration, contact time and whether a rinse is required; label rules are legal limits.
- **Compatibility**: corrosion of stainless steel by chlorides, effect on gaskets, interaction with residual detergent.
- **Soil load**: sanitisers work on clean surfaces; they do not replace cleaning.
- **Resistance and rotation**: some organisms adapt to quats; rotation of sanitiser classes is practised in plants with persistent Listeria.

## Validation and verification
1. Concentration: test strips or titration per label; PAA and chlorine decline in use, so check at start and periodically.
2. Contact time and temperature are recorded.
3. Efficacy: swabs before and after cleaning and sanitising, plate counts for indicator organisms, ATP bioluminescence for organic residue (limits set by the plant by correlation with plate counts).
4. Trend results; investigate repeated positives in Zone 1 and 2 sites and take corrective action.
5. Document in the sanitation standard operating procedures (SSOPs), part of the food-safety plan.

## Gotchas
- Wet surfaces and standing water negate sanitising (dilution, harbourage).
- Fogging and aerosol use have worker-safety implications.
