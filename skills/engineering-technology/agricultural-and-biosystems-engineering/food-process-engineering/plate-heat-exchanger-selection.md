# Plate heat exchanger selection

**Goal:** a compact exchanger that meets the thermal duty, the pressure drop limit and hygiene requirements.

## Why plates
Thin corrugated plates give turbulent flow at low velocities, so U is high for low-viscosity products (milk, juice, beer) and approach temperatures are close. Regeneration (heat from the hot product pre-heating the cold feed) is the main energy saver in pasteurisers (`htst-pasteurizer-hold-tube-and-regeneration-design`).

## Choose
- Plate pattern: high-theta (steep) for high U and pressure drop, low-theta for lower pressure drop.
- Gaps and viscosity: use wide-gap plates for fibre, pulp or viscous products; very viscous products go to scraped-surface or tubular units (`scraped-surface-exchangers-for-viscous-foods`).
- Passes and arrangement to match the flow ratio and keep the velocity in range.
- Gasket materials (EPDM, nitrile, fluoroelastomer, as compatible with fat, temperature and chemicals); food-contact approvals under the applicable regulations.
- Frame: pressure rating, space for added plates, access for opening and inspection.

## Hygiene
Hygienic design standards (for example 3-A and EHEDG guidelines) apply. Dairy HTST regulators (such as the US Pasteurized Milk Ordinance) require specific pressure relationships between the raw and pasteurised sides; ask the regulator and the equipment maker.

## Verify
- [ ] Pressure drop calculated at maximum flow and at fouled condition.
- [ ] Gasket leak and cross-contamination check procedure written.
