# CCP monitoring and instrument calibration

**Goal:** monitoring that is accurate, timely and recorded so deviations are caught before product ships.

## Monitoring design
- **What**: the critical limit parameter (temperature, time, pH, concentration, detector response).
- **How**: instrument type with accuracy better than the margin in the critical limit; continuous recording for high-risk CCPs.
- **Frequency**: continuous where possible; otherwise often enough to detect failure and limit affected product.
- **Who**: trained operator with authority to stop the line.

## Calibration
1. Maintain a reference thermometer traceable to a national standard.
2. Check working probes at the ice point (0 C) and at a second point near the critical limit (e.g. in a stirred, controlled bath). Record error and tolerance (commonly +/- 0.5 to 1 C for cooking CCPs, plan-specific).
3. Calibrate pH meters with buffers bracketing the range; check chemical test kits.
4. Schedule: at start-up, after any impact, and periodically; define actions for out-of-tolerance (re-evaluate product processed since the last good check).

## Corrective action and records
Predefine actions for deviation: hold and assess product, correct the process, document cause. Records are reviewed and signed within the time your regime sets (US FSMA preventive controls: within 7 working days, 21 CFR 117.165(a)(4); seafood HACCP: within 1 week, 21 CFR 123.8; meat and poultry follow 9 CFR 417), with verification activities (record review, direct observation, periodic testing).

## Gotchas
- Un-synchronised clocks make time-temperature records meaningless.
- Data logger sensors left in poor positions mislead.
