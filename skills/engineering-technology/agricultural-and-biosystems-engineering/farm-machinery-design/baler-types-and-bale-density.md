# Baler types and bale density

**Goal:** the bale shape and density that the market, storage and handling system need, at a moisture that stores safely.

## Types
- Small square: a plunger compresses charges in a chamber; density set by rail tension; knotters tie twine. Labour intensive.
- Large square: dense, stackable bales; high plunger loads, so shear-bolt or slip-clutch protection matters (`slip-clutch-and-shear-bolt-protection`).
- Round, fixed chamber: belts or rollers form a fixed-size bale; firmer, more uniform core.
- Round, variable chamber: starts small and grows; looser core, tight outer layers. Density set by belt or arm tension (often hydraulic).
- Wrap: twine, net or film (baleage). Net is quick and sheds weather but adds plastic waste; follow local disposal rules.

## Density and moisture
- Density = dry matter per volume (kg DM/m3), from bale weight, moisture and measured volume.
- Extension services commonly cite roughly 15-20 percent moisture for baling dry hay (Kansas State: 15-18 percent; Guelph: 15-20 percent, alfalfa 20 percent or less for safe storage; Florida: 13-15 percent), drier for big dense bales. Treat as a starting value and check local guidance.
- Baleage is much wetter and must be wrapped and sealed promptly for fermentation.
- Wet dense hay can self-heat and, in extreme cases, ignite. Monitor stack temperature in the first weeks. Do not pull apart a hot stack without fire service advice.

## Setting up
1. Match pickup, feed and any cutter to the crop.
2. Raise density in steps and weigh sample bales.
3. Lopsided bales mean uneven windrow feed; steer to centre the windrow.
