# Hay moisture and drying rate

**Goal:** reach safe baling moisture quickly with minimal leaf loss and weather damage.

## Drivers
- Weather: solar radiation, humidity (vapour pressure deficit), temperature and wind. Rain on cut hay is the largest loss.
- Crop: leaf dries fast, stems slowly; thick stems and dense legume canopies are slow (see `mower-conditioner-cutting-and-conditioning`).
- Swath: wide, thin, fluffy swaths dry faster; heavy swaths stay wet at the bottom.
- Soil moisture and stubble cushion: wet soil slows drying; stubble lets air under the swath.

## Interventions
- Tedding spreads and turns the swath to speed early drying; each pass costs leaf, especially in legumes when dry, so ted early while leaves are pliable.
- Raking forms windrows for the baler; do it when leaves hold together (often with some humidity in early morning or evening).
- Avoid raking or tedding dry legumes where leaf loss matters.

## Measuring
- Hand hay meters are quick but need crop-specific calibration; check against oven-dry samples (forage labs such as Clemson's dry at 60 C, and Cargill's document calls 60 C the gold standard; confirm with your lab).
- Sample several spots and depths in the field and in formed bales.
