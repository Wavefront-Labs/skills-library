# Forage harvester chop length and kernel processing

**Goal:** silage with the right particle size for the animals and the silo, with processed kernels, at acceptable fuel and wear.

## Theoretical length of cut (TLC)
TLC = feedroll linear speed per cutterhead revolution divided by the number of knives (consistent units). Changing feedroll gearing or removing knives changes it. Real particle length differs from TLC, so always measure actual particles.

## Target length
- Corn silage targets depend on dry matter, storage and the herd; drier crop needs shorter chop to pack and exclude air. Nutritionists often set targets using particle-size separators (for example the Penn State shaker box). Take targets from the ration nutritionist.
- Grass and legume haylage: longer chop supports rumen function but packs less well.

## Kernel processing
- A processor (two rolls with a speed differential and a gap) cracks kernels. The gap is narrowed for dry, mature corn; set per manufacturer.
- Check a sample for whole or half kernels (extension kernel-processing score methods exist). Roll wear lowers the score, so recheck the gap and roll condition during the season.

## Knives and shearbar
Sharpen regularly and hold shearbar clearance to the maker's value; dull knives raise fuel use and shred forage. Stop and lock out before touching the cutterhead; it keeps turning after shutdown.

## Verify
- [ ] Particle-size screen matches the target.
- [ ] Kernel score is within target.
- [ ] Fuel use and throughput logged to catch dulling.
- Unconfirmed: current best target values; confirm with a nutritionist.
