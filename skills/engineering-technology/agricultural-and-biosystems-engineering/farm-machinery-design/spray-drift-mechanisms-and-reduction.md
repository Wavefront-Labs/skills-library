# Spray drift mechanisms and reduction

**Goal:** keep spray where it is intended.

## Mechanisms
- **Particle drift**: small droplets (below about 100-150 micrometres) stay airborne and are carried by wind.
- **Evaporation**: small drops shrink, making them more drift-prone in hot, dry conditions.
- **Vapour drift**: volatile active ingredients volatilise after deposition; controlled by product choice, not nozzles.
- **Inversion drift**: in temperature inversions, fine drops suspend and move laterally with little dilution.

## Controls
1. Use coarser droplet classes or air-induction nozzles as allowed by the label.
2. Lower boom height to the minimum giving full overlap (see boom-stability skill).
3. Reduce speed; high speed raises turbulence behind the boom.
4. Avoid spraying in high wind, and in conditions of temperature inversion; wind speed limits are set by label or regulators.
5. Use hooded or shielded booms and air-assist where appropriate.
6. Respect buffer zones and no-spray areas required by the label and local regulation.
7. ISO 22369 and national spray-drift risk classification schemes rank sprayer configurations by drift potential (check applicable scheme).

## Gotchas
- Drift reduction nozzles can reduce coverage; verify efficacy on the target.
- Wind direction and speed at boom height, not at the weather station, matter.
- Record keeping (conditions, nozzle, pressure) is often a legal requirement.

- [ ] Application records show weather, nozzle, pressure and speed.
