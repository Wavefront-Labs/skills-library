# Chain and belt drive design

**Goal:** a drive that carries design power at the required ratio with expected life, in dust and shock load.

## Design power
Design power = transmitted power x service factor. Agricultural drives see shock and overloads, so use a higher service factor than for steady industrial loads. Take the value from the belt or chain maker's catalogue.

## Chains
- Select roller chain from catalogue power ratings by pitch, small sprocket teeth and speed. Very small sprockets (use the chain maker's minimum tooth count from the catalogue; not confirmed here) wear faster and are noisy.
- Adjust slack to the maker's value; add a tensioner on long or variable drives.
- Lubricate as designed; in dust, sealed chains or light oil beat heavy grease that traps grit.
- Replace chain and sprockets together once elongation reaches the maker's limit (about 3 percent is the commonly quoted roller-chain limit, lower, 200/N percent, for sprockets over 66 teeth, and about 2 percent for some transmission chains per gauge manuals; use the maker's limit).

## Belts
- Select V-belts from maker tables by design power and speed. Variable-speed belts in combines need the right gearing and tension spring.
- Tension is critical: low gives slip and heat, high loads bearings. Use the catalogue deflection-force method.
- Align pulleys with a straightedge or laser.
