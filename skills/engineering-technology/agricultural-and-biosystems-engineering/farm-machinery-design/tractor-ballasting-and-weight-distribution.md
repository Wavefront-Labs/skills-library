# Tractor ballasting and weight distribution

**Goal:** enough rear (or total) weight for traction, enough front weight for steering and braking, without overloading tyres or axles.

## Procedure
1. Weigh the tractor with implement attached on axle scales (front and rear). Do not use nameplate weights.
2. Compare distribution with the manufacturer's guidance for the tractor type. Common extension rules of thumb for static front share: roughly 25-35 percent for two-wheel drive, 35-40 percent for front-wheel assist, close to 50 percent for four-wheel drive with large articulated machines. Treat these as starting points; follow the operator manual.
3. Dynamic weight transfer: drawbar pull shifts load to the rear axle; trailed implement pull is lower on the hitch than a mounted implement, so transfer differs. Mounted implements add rear load and reduce front load; add front weight to keep steering.
4. Choose ballast: front weights/suitcases, rear wheel weights, or liquid ballast in tyres where permitted. Liquid ballast mixtures and fill levels are specified by tyre makers; calcium chloride is corrosive to rims.
5. Re-check tyre load ratings at the new weights and set inflation per the manufacturer's load-inflation table.

## Gotchas
- Excess ballast increases compaction, fuel use and rolling resistance; remove it for light work and transport.
- Exceeding axle or tyre ratings is a safety issue; staying within limits takes priority over traction.
- Front-lift instability with heavy rear implements: check front axle load stays above the manufacturer's minimum (often around 20 percent of tractor weight).

## Verify
- [ ] Axle scale readings with implement in working and transport positions.
- [ ] Measured slip within range and steering control adequate on hills and at transport speed.
