# Operator noise and vibration exposure

**Goal:** know the operator's daily exposure and bring it to acceptable levels.

## Noise
- Measure at the operator's ear with a sound level meter or dosimeter over the real duty cycle; report dB(A) and an 8-hour equivalent.
- Limits vary by jurisdiction. In the EU, Directive 2003/10/EC sets lower and upper action values of 80 and 85 dB(A) (peaks 112 and 140 Pa) and an exposure limit of 87 dB(A) (200 Pa peak). US OSHA 29 CFR 1910.95 has a 90 dB(A) 8-hour level (Table G-16) and an 85 dB(A) TWA hearing-conservation action level; NIOSH recommends 85 dBA over 8 hours. Check whether general-industry rules cover your agricultural setting.
- Reduce: sealed cabs, good door seals, exhaust and gearbox design; hearing protection in open-station machines.

## Whole-body vibration
- Measure with a seat-pad triaxial accelerometer following ISO 2631-1 and compare with local action and limit values (EU Directive 2002/44/EC: whole-body A(8) action value 0.5 m/s2 and limit 1.15 m/s2, or VDV 9.1 and 21 m/s1.75 at the Member State's option; hand-arm 2.5 and 5 m/s2; other countries differ).
- Vertical vibration at the seat usually dominates; shocks matter on rough ground.
- Reduce: air or well-kept seat suspension tuned to operator weight, cab or axle suspension, tyre pressure, lower speed on rough ground, shorter exposure.

## Verify
- [ ] Results recorded with method and duty cycle.
- [ ] Seat suspension set to operator weight.
- Confirm limits with the local authority; they change.
