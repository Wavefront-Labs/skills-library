# Combine threshing cylinder and concave setup

**Goal:** threshing complete with minimal damage.

## Principles
Grain is separated from the head by impact and rubbing between rotating cylinder (rasp bar) or rotor and the concave. Speed and clearance control threshing intensity. Small-grain crops (wheat, barley) need higher speed and smaller clearance than maize or soybeans. Typical peripheral speeds range roughly 20-35 m/s; the operator manual gives the starting settings per crop.

## Adjustment order
1. Start with the manufacturer's crop settings table.
2. Check threshing: examine material at the rear of the combine for unthreshed heads (in tailings and straw) and damaged grain in the tank (cracks, broken kernels, hulled seed).
3. Unthreshed grain: increase speed or reduce clearance in small steps (one change at a time).
4. Grain damage or excessive straw breakage: reduce speed or open the concave.
5. Adjust for moisture: tough, damp crop needs more aggression; dry fragile crop (soybeans, pulses) needs less.
6. Check again after the machine settles, and when conditions change through the day.

## Gotchas
- Dry soybeans shatter and split at high speeds; dry-crop harvesting windows matter.
- Rotor machines have vane and clearance settings; follow the maker's order.
- Broken straw overloads the cleaning shoe, raising losses.

## Verify
- [ ] Samples from the grain tank show broken-grain share within the market's tolerance.
- [ ] Unthreshed loss measured with a catch pan under the separator is acceptable.
