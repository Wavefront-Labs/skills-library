# Moldboard and disk plow design and setup

**Goal:** a clean inverted furrow with the lowest draft for the soil.

## Parts and geometry
- Moldboard bottom: share (cuts), moldboard (lifts and inverts; general-purpose, stubble and sod types differ in curvature), landside (resists side force), frog, and sometimes a coulter or jointer. Bottom width commonly ranges around 35-45 cm; depth should not exceed about half of bottom width for good inversion (typical rule, check the manufacturer).
- Disk plow: concave disks with tilt and disc angles; suited to sticky, hard or abrasive soils and rough land.

## Setup
1. Tractor tyre or track width fixed to match furrow width so the front furrow wheel runs in the furrow.
2. Level the plow side to side with the lift linkage, and fore-aft with the top link, so all bottoms enter evenly.
3. **Line of pull**: set the hitch so the line from the tractor centre of pull through the plow's centre of resistance gives minimal side draft; adjust the hitch clevis or drawbar offset until the plow tracks straight without steering effort.
4. Set the first-furrow width with the front furrow adjustment and depth with gauge wheel or hitch.
5. Add coulters and jointers where residue is heavy, at correct clearances so they do not choke.

## Gotchas
- Shares and landsides wear; worn tools increase draft and leave unploughed strips.
- Ploughing wet clay causes smearing and compaction; wait for suitable moisture.
- Fuel use is highest in primary tillage; consider conservation systems where soil conservation rules apply.

## Verify
- [ ] Furrow width equal for each bottom, even depth, residue buried as intended.
- [ ] Draft or fuel per hectare is in line with the prediction.
