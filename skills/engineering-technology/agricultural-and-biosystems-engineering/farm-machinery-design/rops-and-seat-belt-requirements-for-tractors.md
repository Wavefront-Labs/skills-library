# Rollover protective structures and seat belts

**Goal:** every tractor in use has rollover protection and it is used correctly.

## Why
Tractor overturns are a leading cause of farm deaths. A ROPS (rollbar or cab frame) plus a fastened belt keeps the operator inside the protected zone; a ROPS without the belt can be worse than none because the operator can be thrown out and crushed.

## Types and standards
- Structures are tested to standards (OECD tractor codes; ISO, SAE and ASABE standards). Details and editions vary; confirm which applies to the tractor's year and market.
- Do not weld, drill or cut a ROPS without the manufacturer's approval. After an overturn a damaged structure normally needs replacement, unless the manufacturer says otherwise.
- Retrofit kits exist for many older tractors; some jurisdictions fund rebates and some regulations require ROPS on employee-operated tractors. In the US, 29 CFR 1928.51 requires the employer to provide ROPS (and seat belts) on agricultural tractors of more than 20 engine horsepower manufactured after 25 October 1976 that employees operate (checked in eCFR). Confirm with your authority.
- A foldable ROPS must be locked upright in use; fold only for low clearances, then do not wear the belt.

## Practice
- Belt on with ROPS up; belt off if the ROPS is folded or absent.
- Slopes, loaders, towing, hitching and ditch banks are the usual overturn situations; follow the manual for hitching, weight and speed.
- Loader tractors handling bales may also need falling-object protection (FOPS).
