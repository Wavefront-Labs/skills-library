# No-till planter and residue management setup

**Goal:** establish seed in residue with consistent depth and without hairpinning.

## Residue cover target
Conservation tillage is generally defined as at least 30 percent residue cover after planting; programs such as NRCS practice standards may set specific requirements, so check the local program. Measure with the line-transect: stretch a rope or tape with 100 equally spaced marks diagonally across rows and count marks over residue; repeat at several locations.

## Row unit setup for no-till
1. **Coulter** ahead of the opener cuts residue and loosens a narrow slot; set depth shallower than the seed opener.
2. **Row cleaners** (residue managers) move residue off the row without moving soil; set to just touch the soil.
3. **Opener and gauge wheels**: set depth with gauge wheel adjustment; use adequate down force (see row-unit downforce skill) to hold depth in hard soils.
4. **Seed firmers or tubes** reduce seed bounce.
5. **Closing wheels** press soil on the seed; adjust pressure by soil moisture to avoid sidewall compaction or open slots.

## Gotchas
- Planting into wet soil in heavy residue causes hairpinning (residue pushed into the slot).
- Row cleaners set too deep move soil and lose moisture benefits.
- Heavy residue distribution from the combine (chopper, spreader width) matters as much as planter setup.

## Verify
- [ ] Residue cover measured and recorded after planting.
- [ ] Dig test: seeds at target depth with firm soil contact in several rows.
