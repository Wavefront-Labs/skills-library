# Row unit downforce and opener design

**Goal:** consistent seeding depth across variable soil by keeping gauge wheels firmly on the ground without excess compaction.

## Concepts
- **Gauge wheel loading**: the part of unit weight and downforce carried by gauge wheels sets depth. Too little load: depth varies; too much: sidewall compaction and soil stress.
- **Passive springs**: set by hand; **pneumatic or hydraulic downforce**: adjustable from the cab; **active systems**: load sensors on gauge wheels adjust force in real time.
- Openers: double disc (most common), single disc, hoe; disc diameter and offset affect residue cutting and soil throw.

## Setup
1. Set the unit weight and spring so that gauge wheels just remain in contact at the working depth in the hardest soil; do not add more than necessary.
2. In variable fields, raise downforce in hard areas; reduce in soft areas; use sensors if available.
3. Check depth by digging seed rows; check seed-to-soil contact and closing.
4. Ride quality: parallel linkage geometry, wheel arm length and mounting hold depth across contour.

## Gotchas
- Wet soil plus high downforce causes sidewall compaction, restricting root growth.
- Speed raises demand on downforce; test at planned speed.
- Worn disc blades (reduced diameter) change seed placement; replace by the manufacturer's wear limits.

## Verify
- [ ] Depth within +/- 0.5 cm over 20 consecutive plants (or manufacturer tolerance).
- [ ] No visible sidewall smear in damp conditions.
