# Dairy barn cow comfort and heat stress control

**Goal:** limit the drop in intake and milk yield and the fertility loss in warm conditions.

## Temperature-humidity index
`THI = (1.8 T + 32) - (0.55 - 0.0055 RH) (1.8 T - 26)` (T in C, RH in percent). Published thresholds disagree: Armstrong's scheme (reviewed in PMC6722315) has no stress below 72, mild 73-79, moderate 80-89, severe above 90, while the same review cites onset above 68 (Xue et al.); the review's own printed formula is garbled, the one here is the NRC form (1.8T-26) and only the 68 and 72 values were confirmed. The 68-74 range quoted elsewhere is unconfirmed (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked). Thresholds vary with breed, yield and acclimatisation, so use the source your programme adopts.

## Cooling methods
1. **Shade** for cows outdoors and at feed bunks.
2. **Fans**: high airspeed at cow level (around 2-3 m/s (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)) above stalls and feed alley; use large-diameter, slowly rotating fans or tunnel ventilation; angle toward cows; spacing from the manufacturer or the handbook.
3. **Soakers (sprinklers) with fans** at the feed line: wet the cow then evaporate by fan; cycle times (for example several minutes off to evaporate) per extension guidelines; manage water use and drainage.
4. **Evaporative pads or fogging** in dry climates.
5. **Cross and tunnel ventilation** for large barns with controlled airflows at 1.5 m/s or more across cows (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
6. Holding pen cooling and parlour exit cooling.

## Design
Apply the sensible and moisture balances (see that skill) for maximum airflow, and check static pressure for fans; ensure the electrical supply and water supply.

## Gotchas
- Wet cows need adequate evaporation; wet but not dried reduces cooling.
- Minimum ventilation in winter should still be adequate for moisture.

## Verify
- [ ] Respiration rate counts and THI records show cows below stress thresholds under the cooling plan.
