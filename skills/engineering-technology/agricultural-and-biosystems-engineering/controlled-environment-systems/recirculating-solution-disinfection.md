# Recirculating solution disinfection

**Goal:** stop root pathogens (Pythium, Phytophthora, Fusarium, viruses) from circulating through a closed hydroponic system.

## Options
- Slow sand filtration: biological filter, low maintenance and no chemicals, large footprint and slow flow, but proven in many nurseries.
- UV: lamps in a stainless chamber; disinfection depends on dose (energy per area), so the solution must be clear (filter first) and the flow matched to the lamp rating. Fungal spores need far lower doses than viruses, which need high doses; use the supplier's validated dose for the target organisms and monitor lamp output (lamps lose output with age and fouling).
- Heat treatment: pasteurising the solution (commonly cited as around 95 C for 30 s followed by heat recovery) is reliable for most pathogens; energy and chelate stability must be managed.
- Ozone: strong oxidiser; destroys chelated iron and manganese (replace the loss) and creates hazards; ventilate and use destruct units; dose to a target ORP or residual.
- Hydrogen peroxide, chlorine or chlorine dioxide: dosing to a residual and a contact time; needs control of the dose to avoid root injury; some products are regulated pesticides for use on edible crops, so check the label and the local approvals.

## Design
1. Pre-filter (for example 50-100 micrometre screen or disc filter) to cut turbidity and protect the unit.
2. Treat the whole return flow or a side stream sized to the pathogens risk and the system turn-over; consider treating at the point of return to the supply tank.
3. Ensure a storage tank and aeration after ozone or chemical treatment.
4. Maintain: lamp hours, quartz sleeve cleaning, dosing equipment calibration.

## Verify
- [ ] Microbiological check: plate counts or pathogen assay before and after the unit.
- [ ] Lamp output or residual measured regularly.
- Unconfirmed: dose and temperature values; confirm with the unit supplier and a plant pathologist.
