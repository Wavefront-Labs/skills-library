# Tunnel ventilation and cooling cell design

**Goal:** high airflow along the house to remove heat and cool animals by air speed and evaporative cooling.

## Principle
Fans on one end wall exhaust air; inlets (often evaporative cooling cells or open curtain inlets) at the other end create a through-flow. The air speed along the length provides a convective cooling effect for animals, and air temperature rises along the house as it picks up heat.

## Design
1. **Airflow**: `Q = A_cross x v`, where v is the target air speed (about 2-3 m/s for large poultry, 1.5-3 m/s for swine and dairy) and A_cross the house cross-sectional area (width times average height).
2. **Fan capacity**: provide the airflow at the system static pressure (including cooling pads), typically 25-50 Pa.
3. **Cooling cells**: pad area from airflow and face velocity (see fan-and-pad skill); place in sidewalls near the inlet end or in end walls.
4. **Length**: temperature rise along the house limits length; long houses are often split into zones or use pre-cooling; check with a heat balance.
5. **Inlets**: tunnel inlets sized for velocity and sealed against leaks; sidewall inlets used for minimum ventilation closed in tunnel mode.
6. **Controls**: stage from minimum to transition to tunnel; avoid sudden transitions that chill animals; use variable speed fans and alarms and backup generator capacity.
7. **Animal welfare**: air speed over chicks and young animals must be limited.

## Gotchas
- A leaky house loses effective tunnel flow; seal side inlets and doors.
- Evaporative cooling raises humidity, which can reduce animals' ability to cool (check THI).
