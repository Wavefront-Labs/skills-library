# Greenhouse foundations and anchorage

**Goal:** anchorage that resists wind uplift and settlement.

## Types
- **Ground-driven pipe posts** for hoop houses and tunnels; depth and diameter set by soil and wind (maker's tables).
- **Concrete footings** (poured, cast-in-place or precast) for glass houses and gutter-connected ranges, often with a concrete curb or sill wall for the sidewalls.
- **Perimeter walls** (concrete, block) that insulate and prevent water and pests entry.
- **Screw piles** or helical anchors for sites with limited excavation.

## Design checks
1. Uplift: the weight of the footing plus soil resistance must exceed the design wind suction on the roof and sidewalls (with a safety factor from the code).
2. Bearing capacity of soil under the loads; settlement limits to keep the frame aligned.
3. Frost depth: footings below the local frost depth to avoid heave; insulated foundations where frost protection is needed.
4. Corrosion: galvanised or coated anchors in contact with soil and fertiliser.
5. Drainage: gutters and downpipes to drain away from foundations; soil improvement under floors.
6. Soil investigation (borehole or test pits) for large installations.

## Gotchas
- Post depth for hoop houses depends on soil strength; sandy soils need deeper or larger footings.
- Dig service lines locations before excavating.

## Verify
- [ ] Footing dimensions follow the engineered drawings and local code, and inspection records exist for rebar and concrete.
