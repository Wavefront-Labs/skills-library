# Poultry house ventilation stages and minimum rates

This is professional practice guidance; legal welfare and building requirements for your country apply.

## Stages
1. **Minimum ventilation** (cold weather): small timed fan operation (cycle on and off) to hold humidity, CO2 and ammonia within limits while conserving heat; air enters through ceiling or sidewall inlets at good velocity (see inlet skill). Minimum rates scale with bird age and weight; chicks need warmth first and low rates.
2. **Transition**: stage in more fans and inlets as temperature rises, still using inlet velocity to keep air jets from dropping onto birds.
3. **Tunnel ventilation**: for hot weather, fans at one end and inlets at the other pull air down the house at a design air speed (set by the handbook or integrator guide; no figure given here) to produce a wind-chill effect; evaporative cooling cells at the inlet end add cooling.

## Air quality limits
The EU broiler directive (2007/43/EC, Annex II, fetched from EUR-Lex) requires ammonia not above 20 ppm and CO2 not above 3 000 ppm at bird head level, and a 48-hour average relative humidity not above 70 percent when the outside temperature is below 10 C; when the outside shade temperature is above 30 C the inside temperature may be no more than 3 C above it (Annex II point 3, re-fetched from EUR-Lex). It also requires alarm and back-up systems for failure of mechanical ventilation. Other jurisdictions and assurance schemes differ; confirm current legal limits where you operate.

## Design checks
- Total fan capacity per bird or per kg live weight for the design hot day (use handbook factors and local climate); heat production of birds grows with live weight and age.
- Static pressure of the system (see fan selection skill); pad area for the evaporative cells.
- Controllers: temperature, humidity and pressure sensors with stage logic and alarms; back-up power and alarm systems because failure in hot weather can kill the flock quickly.

## Verify
- [ ] Measure air speed and temperature at bird level along the house in tunnel mode; ammonia readings in winter stay within limits.
