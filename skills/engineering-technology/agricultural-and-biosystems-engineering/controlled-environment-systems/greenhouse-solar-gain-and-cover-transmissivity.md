# Greenhouse solar gain and cover transmissivity

**Goal:** the cooling load and light level inside the structure.

## Solar input
- Outdoor global solar radiation can reach about 1000 W/m2 on a clear summer day at mid-latitudes. Inside a greenhouse, transmitted radiation is typically 60-80 percent of outdoor after cover, frame and screen losses.
- Cover transmissivity: clean horticultural glass 85-90 percent for total light, single polyethylene film in the 80-90 percent range when new, twin-wall polycarbonate about 75-85 percent. Dirt, condensation and ageing reduce it by several points.
- Diffuse covers scatter light, reducing shadows and giving more uniform canopy light.

## Energy partition
Absorbed solar energy at the crop and floor is partly used for transpiration (latent) and the rest heats the air (sensible); a covered crop with full canopy uses more of it for transpiration. A rough design value for the cooling load is solar input minus the portion removed by evaporation and conduction, often taken at 0.4-0.6 of outdoor radiation (Nelson, ASHRAE application guidance); take figures from the standard method.

## Shading and screens
White-wash, shade cloth (fixed or retractable), reflective external screens and light diffusing films reduce solar gain by selected percentages. Choose shade levels for the crop's light requirement (see the DLI skill).

## Verify
- [ ] A pyranometer inside and outside the greenhouse measures transmissivity over several hours of sun.
