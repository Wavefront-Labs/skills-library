# Glazing and film cover selection

**Goal:** the cover that balances light, heat retention, life and cost for the crop.

## Options
- **Horticultural glass** (low-iron, tempered 4 mm): highest light transmission and long life (decades), options for diffuse and anti-reflection coatings; heavy, needs a stiffer frame; high capital cost.
- **Polyethylene film**: low cost, single or double layer inflated; typical life 3-4 years for UV-stabilised film; additives for anti-drip, infrared (thermal) retention and diffusion.
- **Twin-wall polycarbonate or acrylic**: good insulation, impact resistance, life around 10-15 years; lower light transmission and a yellowing effect with age.
- **ETFE film**: long life (15-25 years claimed by makers) and high transmission including UV; used in high-value or large structures; check the supplier's warranty and data.
- **Rigid PVC and fibreglass** are less common.

## Selection factors
1. **Light**: high-light crops (tomato, cucumber, cut flowers) and winter production need high transmission; diffuse covers help dense canopies.
2. **Energy**: U-value and infrared properties set heating costs (see the envelope skill).
3. **Condensation**: anti-drip treatments protect crops.
4. **Hail and wind** resistance, local code.
5. **Life-cycle cost**: annualised cost including replacement, labour and downtime.
6. **UV transmission** for pollinators and some crop colour or disease effects.
