# Deep water culture and aeration

**Goal:** roots in a deep, well-aerated, cool nutrient pool that supports the crop without root disease.

## Layout
Floating rafts (foam boards with net pots) on a pond or bed 20-30 cm deep are widely used for leafy greens such as lettuce and basil. A large water volume buffers EC, pH and temperature.

## Dissolved oxygen
- Oxygen solubility falls as the water warms: roughly 9 mg/L at 20 C and 8 mg/L at 25 C for water in equilibrium with air.
- Practical target: keep DO well above 5-6 mg/L at the root zone, nearer saturation if possible.
- Aeration: air blowers feeding diffuser tubes or stones along the bed, venturi injectors on the recirculation line, falls and cascades at the return, and where economically justified, oxygen injection. Spread the aeration evenly; measure DO at several points with a calibrated meter.
- Water circulation (a gentle current) prevents dead zones and keeps temperature and nutrients uniform.

## Temperature and disease
- Keep the solution about 18-24 C; above about 25 C DO falls, and root rot organisms such as Pythium thrive. Use chillers or heat exchangers on warm sites; shade and insulate beds.
- Keep light out of the solution (algae); use clean water and sanitised rafts; stagger crops and clean between cycles.
- Consider UV or other solution disinfection (`recirculating-solution-disinfection`).

## Monitoring
EC, pH, temperature and DO logged continuously if possible; top-up with water and stock solution (`nutrient-solution-ec-and-ph-management`).

## Verify
- [ ] DO measured at three positions per bed at full crop biomass (when the demand is highest).
- [ ] Temperature log across a hot day.
