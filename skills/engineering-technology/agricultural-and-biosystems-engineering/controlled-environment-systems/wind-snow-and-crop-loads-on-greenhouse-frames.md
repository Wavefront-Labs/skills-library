# Wind, snow and crop loads on greenhouse frames

Structural design is regulated by building codes and must be carried out or checked by a licensed engineer where required; this skill summarises what the checks include.

## Loads
- **Wind**: velocity pressure from the design wind speed and exposure; pressure coefficients depend on roof shape and openings; open vents and torn film increase internal pressure. Wind often governs low, light structures.
- **Snow**: ground snow load adjusted for roof slope and thermal conditions; heated houses melt snow but gutters can collect large drifts and valley loads in ridge-and-furrow ranges.
- **Crop load**: hanging crops (tomato, cucumber) add a vertical load on the wires and trusses; designers use an allowance specified by the standard or grower data (on the order of tens of kg per square metre for heavy crops).
- **Equipment**: thermal screens, lamps, sprinklers, gas lines on trusses.
- **Dead load** of frame and glazing.

## Standards (check current editions)
European standard EN 13031-1 (greenhouses: design and construction, commercial production); in the US, ASABE EP460 (Commercial Greenhouse Design and Layout; editions 2001 and 2004 seen in listings) and other ASABE structural standards (confirm the number and edition at asabe.org) and the local building code (ASCE 7 loads); other countries use national codes.

## Practice
1. Define site wind and snow data and risk class (lifetime) of the structure.
2. Run load combinations for strength and deflection of purlins, rafters, columns and bracing.
3. Check connections, anchors, and film or glass fastenings under wind suction.
4. Stability bracing in both directions and wind-resistant ends.
