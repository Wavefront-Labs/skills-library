# Field testing fan performance

**Goal:** know how much air the installed fans actually deliver.

## Method
1. Choose a representative set of fans (new, middle-aged and oldest); record model, size, motor power and age.
2. Measure with an in-situ fan assessment device (for example a fan assessment numeration system with an anemometer grid that traverses the fan face) or a calibrated vane anemometer traverse; the measurements give airflow at the building's operating pressure.
3. Measure static pressure across the building with a manometer at the operating condition; measure electrical power with a power meter.
4. Compute airflow per watt (m3/h per W) and compare with the fan's published data at the same pressure.
5. Check mechanical condition: belt tension, pulley alignment, blade cleanliness, shutter opening, guards.

## Interpretation
- Airflow of installed fans is commonly 70-90 percent of the rated value in older buildings, depending on maintenance; large shortfalls point to dirty blades, worn belts, closed shutters or high pressure.
- Compare with the required ventilation rates for the building at its stocking density.

## Action
Clean blades and shutters, replace belts and motors, correct inlets, or add capacity.

## Gotchas
- Measuring at zero static pressure (open doors) overstates airflow.
- Wind affects fan airflow; test in calm conditions.

## Verify
- [ ] Re-test after cleaning and adjustment; airflow improvement recorded.
