# Photoperiod control and blackout systems

**Goal:** the day length the crop needs for flowering, vegetative growth or dormancy, controlled by curtains and lighting.

## Responses
- Short-day plants (many ornamentals such as chrysanthemum and poinsettia) flower when the night is longer than a critical length, so in long summer days growers extend the night with blackout curtains.
- Long-day plants (some bedding plants, many perennials) flower when nights are short; in winter, lights extend the day or interrupt the night.
- Day-neutral plants ignore photoperiod. Critical night lengths vary by species and variety; read the crop guide.

## Blackout systems
- Motorised black cloth or laminated blackout screens above or below the glazing; set schedules by clock or from daylight sensors. Must be light-tight: a small leak may delay flowering in the exposed areas.
- Heat: a curtain closed in the early morning or late afternoon sun traps heat. Use reflective-faced or white-faced blackout material and ventilation, or time the closure to limit heat build-up, with the climate computer controlling vents and the screen.
- Check also lights from neighbours, outdoor lighting and gaps at gables and edges.

## Night break and extension lighting
- Night break: low-intensity lighting for a short period in the middle of the night (cyclic lighting is often used to save energy); needs only low light levels of a few micromoles per square metre per second at plant height for many species, provided the light spectrum includes red wavelengths; use the crop recommendation.
- Day extension: higher intensity, sometimes using LED with the specified spectrum (`led-grow-light-selection-and-layout`).

## Cannabis and hemp
Photoperiod is used to control flowering in these crops; legal status and licensing differ by place; follow local rules.

- [ ] Curtain schedule and leaf temperatures logged on hot days.
