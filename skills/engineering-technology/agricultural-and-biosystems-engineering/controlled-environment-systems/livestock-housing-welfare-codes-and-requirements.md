# Livestock housing welfare codes and requirements

This is a guide to finding and applying requirements; it does not replace the law or the competent authority.

## Where requirements come from
- **Legislation**: in the EU, directives on the protection of pigs (2008/120/EC), laying hens (1999/74/EC), broilers (2007/43/EC), calves (2008/119/EC) and animals kept for farming purposes (98/58/EC) set minimum space, light, ventilation and equipment; the UK, Canada, Australia and New Zealand have codes of practice or regulations; the US has few federal on-farm rules but state laws and species-specific industry programmes and retailer audits apply.
- **Voluntary and certification schemes**: assurance programmes (national or retailer) often set stricter limits.
- **Scientific bodies**: guidance from the World Organisation for Animal Health (WOAH, formerly OIE) and farm animal welfare councils.

Worked example, broilers: Directive 2007/43/EC Annex II requires ammonia not above 20 ppm and CO2 not above 3 000 ppm at bird head level, a 48-hour average relative humidity not above 70 percent when outside temperature is below 10 C, and alarm and back-up systems for failure of mechanical ventilation (EUR-Lex text, fetched). Check the current consolidated version of each directive and the national implementing law.

## Typical design parameters regulated or specified
1. Stocking density (kg per square metre or animals per square metre).
2. Ventilation and air quality: ammonia, CO2, temperature and humidity ranges.
3. Lighting: minimum lux, photoperiod and dark period.
4. Noise: limits for continuous noise.
5. Floors, bedding, enrichment and resting area dimensions.
6. Alarm and back-up requirements for mechanically ventilated buildings.
7. Records and inspection.

## Engineering practice
1. Identify the species, jurisdiction and any assurance schemes.
2. Extract each numerical requirement into a design checklist (stocking, airflow, alarms).
3. Design with margin: the legal limit is a minimum at the worst condition.
4. Document compliance (calculations and drawings) and plan inspection access.

## Verify
- [ ] Each requirement is traced to a clause in the current legal text or code and checked by the farm or veterinary adviser.
