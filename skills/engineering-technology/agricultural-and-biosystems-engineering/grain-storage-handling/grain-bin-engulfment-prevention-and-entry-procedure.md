# Grain bin engulfment prevention and entry procedure

This is professional practice guidance, not legal advice. Grain bin entry is lethal: engulfment (flowing grain or bridged grain collapse) can bury a person in seconds. Follow the governing regulation (in the US, OSHA's grain handling standard 29 CFR 1910.272 and the confined-space rules; other countries have their own) and your employer's safety program; train and qualify workers before they enter.

## Principle
Avoid entry whenever possible: use tools from outside (poles, cameras, bin sweeps with remote control, vacuum systems).

## When entry cannot be avoided
1. **Written permit** with the date, bin, hazards, controls, entrants and attendant, signed by the authorising person.
2. **Lockout and tagout** all energy sources: augers, unloading equipment, conveyors and any equipment that can discharge grain; physically lock and verify zero energy. Make sure that no one can start the equipment.
3. **Atmosphere testing** of oxygen, flammable gas and toxic gases (carbon monoxide, fumigants like phosphine where applicable) before entry (1910.272(g)(1)(iii): combustible and toxic gases when expected, oxygen unless continuous ventilation is provided; action below 19.5% oxygen); ventilate.
4. **Observer (attendant)** outside, in constant contact, trained and able to call for help and initiate rescue but not enter without proper equipment.
5. **Body harness and lifeline**, positioned and sized to keep the person from sinking further than waist-deep (1910.272(g)(2), checked in eCFR).
6. **Never walk on grain that is moving or has crusted or bridged**; never "walk down" grain; do not enter on top of a bridge or a vertical wall of grain.
7. **Rescue plan**: written, practised, with rescue equipment (grain rescue tubes, a lifeline, proper retrieval hardware) and arrangements with trained local responders.
8. Do not use stored-grain fumigants while people are in the bin.
