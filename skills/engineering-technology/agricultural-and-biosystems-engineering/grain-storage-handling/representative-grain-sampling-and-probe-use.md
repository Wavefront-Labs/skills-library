# Representative sampling and probe use

**Goal:** a sample whose test results represent the lot within the stated tolerance.

## Principles
Grain is heterogeneous: moisture, dockage, damage and contaminants vary across a lot. Every test result depends first on the sample. Sample from moving grain where possible (a stream or a conveyor, taken across the whole stream at intervals), because it avoids bias from segregation; static sampling with probes is the fallback.

## Methods
- Stream sampling: automatic or manual cross-cut sampler taking increments at regular intervals across the whole flow.
- Probes for trucks and railcars: compartmented probes (take several depth sections), triers or pelican samplers for bag and stream sampling. The number and position of probe insertions follow the standard that applies; inspection services publish patterns for trucks and railcars (for example the US Federal Grain Inspection Service sampling procedures (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)), and ISO 24333 covers sampling of cereals and cereal products in general (title, edition and scope (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)).
- Bins: probe from the top gives the top layer only; sample the stream during loading or unloading for a representative result.

## Procedure
1. Choose the sampling plan and size from the contract or the standard; keep the increments uniform.
2. Combine increments into the composite sample, mix, and reduce with a sample divider (riffle or rotary), not by hand grabbing.
3. Seal and label (lot, date, source, sampler, location) and keep a retained portion.
4. Deliver without delay; moisture sample in an airtight container to avoid change.

## Mistakes
Sampling only the top; using a probe that selectively picks up fines; grabbing from one side; hand-quartering; slow delivery of moisture samples; contaminated sampling equipment.
