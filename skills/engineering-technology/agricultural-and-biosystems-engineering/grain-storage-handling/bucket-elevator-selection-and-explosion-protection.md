# Bucket elevator selection and explosion protection

**Goal:** vertical lift at the required rate with safe, reliable operation.

## Capacity
`Q = V_b x f x (n per metre) x v` with bucket volume V_b, fill factor f (about 0.6-0.75 depending on loading), bucket pitch (buckets per metre) and belt speed v; belt speeds of about 1.5-3.5 m/s for grain, selected by head pulley diameter and discharge type: **centrifugal discharge** (higher speed, loose materials) or **continuous/positive discharge** (slower, for fragile or more cohesive materials). CEMA publishes a bucket elevator standard; confirm its number, title and edition with CEMA (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).

## Components
Head pulley with lagging, boot with take-up and cleanout, belt or chain, casing with inspection doors, and drive with backstop.

## Safety systems for grain elevators
Elevator legs are common sites of dust explosions. Typical protection includes the items below. For inside bucket elevators at US grain elevators, 29 CFR 1910.272(q) requires: no jogging to free a choked leg; belts and lagging bought after 30 March 1988 to be conductive (surface resistance not above 300 megohms); access to the head pulley section and boot; bearings mounted outside the casing or condition-monitored (vibration, temperature or similar); and a motion detection device that shuts down the elevator when belt speed falls by no more than 20 percent of normal. 1910.272(m) requires scheduled inspection with certification records and prompt correction of overheated bearings and slipping or misaligned belts. Items:
1. Belt alignment and tracking monitors, belt speed (underspeed) switches, bearing temperature or vibration sensors on head and tail.
2. Backstops, plugged-chute detectors in the discharge, magnets or grates to remove tramp metal.
3. Explosion vents on the casing (venting per NFPA 68), and control of dust leakage; housekeeping.
4. Static-dissipating belts; antistatic and sparking-resistance in buckets (plastic buckets with correct materials).
5. Lockout before maintenance.
Regulatory requirements (for example OSHA 29 CFR 1910.272 for grain-handling facilities in the US, or national rules elsewhere) apply to installations; a qualified engineer must confirm what applies to the site.
