# Air-screen cleaner selection

**Goal:** remove weeds, chaff, broken kernels and foreign material to the required purity at the target throughput.

## How it works
Material falls through an aspirating air stream that lifts light material (chaff, dust), then moves over a series of oscillating screens: a scalping screen takes out large material, a grading screen passes the grain but retains oversize seeds, and a sizing or sifting screen removes small material. Airflow and screens are adjusted to the crop.

## Selection
- Capacity: per unit width of screen (maker rating for the crop); allow headroom of 20-30 percent so that the machine is not at its limit when feed varies.
- Screen opening: select by kernel dimensions (round perforated screens separate by width, slotted screens by thickness); typical combos are given in maker's charts and seed association manuals.
- Number of screens and fans: two-screen cleaners for simple jobs; four screen and multi-aspiration for seed.
- Secondary: indented cylinder (length), gravity table (density, for seed and legumes), colour sorter, magnet and destoner.

## Setting
1. Feed rate: even and steady.
2. Airflow: raise until the first loss of good grain appears in the light fraction, then back off.
3. Screen slope and stroke: for sticking material increase the slope; for sliding grain, reduce.
4. Check the output and the screenings (loss) against a purity analysis (`representative-grain-sampling-and-probe-use`).

## Safety and dust
Dust extraction and housekeeping are needed (`grain-dust-explosion-hazard-and-housekeeping`); guard drives; lockout before screen change.

- [ ] Purity before and after; loss of good grain in the screenings below the target.
