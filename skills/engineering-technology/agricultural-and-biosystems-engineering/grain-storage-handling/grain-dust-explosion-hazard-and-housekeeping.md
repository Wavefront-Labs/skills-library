# Grain dust explosion hazard and housekeeping

This is professional practice guidance, not a substitute for the standard or a qualified safety engineer.

## Hazard
Combustible dust explosions require fuel (dust), oxidiser (air), ignition source, dispersion (a cloud) and confinement (the "explosion pentagon"). Grain elevators, dryers, mills and dust collectors are typical sites. Secondary explosions occur when a primary event shakes accumulated dust into suspension; they usually cause the most damage.

## Governing documents (check the current editions and your jurisdiction)
- US OSHA grain handling facilities standard, 29 CFR 1910.272, which includes housekeeping, hot work, preventive maintenance and emergency action requirements.
- NFPA 61 (prevention of fires and dust explosions in agricultural and food processing facilities) and NFPA 652 (combustible dust fundamentals), including a documented dust hazard analysis.
- In the EU and elsewhere, ATEX directives and national rules.

## Housekeeping
1. Written housekeeping programme with priority areas (e.g. floors, ledges, equipment near ignition sources, enclosed areas); regular schedule.
2. Remove dust accumulations before they reach the limit set by the standard (1910.272(j): remove fugitive grain dust whenever it exceeds 1/8 inch (0.32 cm) at priority housekeeping areas, checked in eCFR).
3. Clean by vacuum with listed equipment or sweeping that does not create dust clouds; avoid compressed air blow-down unless the standard permits it with precautions.

## Ignition sources
Hot work permits, bearing temperature monitoring, belt alignment, magnets to prevent metal sparks, electrical equipment rated for dust areas, static bonding, smoking prohibition, and safe dryer operation.

- [ ] Housekeeping logs and inspections of priority areas up to date; dust hazard analysis current.
