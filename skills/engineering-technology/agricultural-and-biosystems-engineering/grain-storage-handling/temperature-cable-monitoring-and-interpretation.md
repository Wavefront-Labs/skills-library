# Temperature cable monitoring and interpretation

**Goal:** early warning of heating or insect or mould activity in stored grain.

## Layout
- Cables hang from the roof with sensors at regular vertical intervals (often about 1 m or 3 ft (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)). Number and spacing vary by maker and bin diameter, with the aim to have each cable represent a limited grain volume; place cables toward the centre, mid-radius and near the wall for large bins.
- Fit before loading grain; keep cables away from filling streams.

## Reading
1. **Trend, not absolute**: logging daily readings at the same time; compare each sensor with its neighbours and with the average of the bin.
2. A sustained rise of about 2-3 C (about 5 F) above expected (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked), especially in one zone, flags heating even if the absolute temperature is below limits; chase it with aeration, turn and inspect.
3. Seasonal pattern: cooling cycles reduce temperatures in a controlled way; warm layers at the top in spring sunshine and cold at the walls in winter appear as moisture migration.
4. Verify with a manual probe or inspection from the access hatch.

## Limitations
A cable senses only grain within a limited radius (perhaps 1 m or less around the sensor (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)), so hot spots can develop between cables; heat conduction through grain is slow, so a spoilage hot spot can be large before detection. Combine with CO2 monitoring and regular inspection.

## Gotchas
- Cable breakage during unloading and wrong sensor indexing.
- Moisture migration produces condensation near the top in cold weather.
