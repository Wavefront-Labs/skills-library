# Bin foundation, roof and anchorage loads

**Goal:** a bin that stays put, empty or full, and a roof that handles snow and air.

## Foundation
1. Total weight = grain weight plus structure; the pressure on the ring wall and the floor slab differs: much of the grain weight rests on the floor, the friction share on the wall (see the Janssen skill).
2. Soil bearing capacity needs a geotechnical investigation; weak soils and frost heave require a larger base or piles.
3. Differential settlement damages the walls; reinforce and keep the base level.

## Wind and overturning
Empty or partly filled bins have the highest vulnerability to wind: check overturning and uplift (wind pressure on the shell and roof) and provide anchor bolts or cast-in anchors per the design code for the site's design wind speed.

## Roof loads
Snow and rain loads from local building codes; additional load from the aeration system and equipment hung from the roof; roof peaks allow free flow of air.

## Roof vents for aeration
Provide enough vent area for the airflow of the fans so that the pressure drop at the exhaust is small; Purdue Extension recommends at least 1 square foot of roof vent area for every 1000 cfm of fan airflow (about 5 m/s through the vent, computed); check against the bin maker's data.

## Regulatory
Structural design and building permits are local; use the manufacturer's engineered drawings and a licensed engineer where required.

## Gotchas
- Unanchored empty bins have blown over in storms.
- Vent area reductions by snow and screens decrease airflow.

- [ ] Anchor bolts and base ring installed per drawings with inspection records.
