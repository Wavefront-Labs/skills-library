# Grain moisture measurement and meter checks

**Goal:** moisture numbers that you can trust for drying, storage and trade.

## Methods
- **Reference**: air-oven methods (specified temperature and time by grain type, in standards such as ASABE S352 (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) and official grain standards); this is the basis for calibrating meters.
- **Electronic meters**: capacitance (dielectric) or microwave meters read a sample or flowing grain. Each grain type has calibration curves; accuracy depends on temperature, kernel density and moisture range; very wet or very dry samples and cold grain are less accurate.
- Inline sensors in dryers and conveyors need compensation for temperature and flow.

## Good practice
1. Take a representative sample (see grain sampling skill), mix, and test at least twice.
2. Let cold samples warm in a sealed bag before testing, as the meter instructions require, to avoid condensation errors.
3. Compare the meter against the oven or an official reference sample monthly and at harvest start; log bias and apply the maker's offset if allowed.
4. Use the correct grain, calibration year and software version.

## Storage targets
Extension guidance for safe storage of good-quality grain with aeration (Purdue Extension AED-20, Midwest US): corn 15.5 percent if sold by spring, 14 percent for 6-12 months, 13 percent beyond a year; soybeans 14 percent if sold by spring, 12 percent up to a year, 11 percent beyond; wheat, oats and barley 14 percent up to 6 months, 13 percent beyond; 1 point lower for poor-quality grain. Other extension services use slightly different tables, so use your own region's. Warmer climates need lower values. See the EMC skill for the physics.

## Gotchas
- Moisture varies within a bin; surface samples mislead.
- Different meters read differently; settle trade disputes by the agreed reference.

## Verify
- [ ] Meter within the stated tolerance (maker or authority tolerance, often about 0.5 point (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)) of the oven on at least three samples.
