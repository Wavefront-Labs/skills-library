# Angle of repose and flowability testing

**Goal:** know how the material piles, flows and arches.

## Angle of repose
- **Static (poured)**: pour through a funnel onto a flat plate to form a cone; measure height and radius; angle = arctan(h/r). Typical cereals fall roughly 20-30 degrees, oilseeds a little lower, powders and flours higher (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked); measure your own material.
- **Dynamic (drained)**: angle left in a box or the bin after discharge.
Report the method; values differ by method and moisture.

## Related properties
- **Angle of internal friction** and **wall friction angle** (for the construction material: smooth steel, corrugated steel, concrete) from direct shear or wall-friction testers; used in pressure and hopper design.
- **Flowability** classification (free-flowing grains versus cohesive meals and powders) from shear-cell testing (Jenike type) or simple tests like cone and arching tests.

## Practical effects
1. Peak shape in bins: peak height from roof geometry and repose angle gives extra volume and load.
2. Hopper and chute angles must exceed friction limits with margin; fines and moisture raise wall friction.
3. Segregation: fines accumulate at the centre in a centre-filled bin and coarse grain rolls to the wall, which affects aeration and moisture.

## Gotchas
- Wet or mouldy grain bridges and may not flow even in steep hoppers.
- Dust and fines accumulation at the centre increase airflow resistance (use spreaders or cleaners).

## Verify
- [ ] Repeat the cone test three times; values agree within a few degrees.
