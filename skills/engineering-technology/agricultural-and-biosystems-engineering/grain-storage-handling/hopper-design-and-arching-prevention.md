# Hopper design and arching prevention

**Goal:** a hopper that always empties without intervention.

## Geometry
1. **Slope**: the hopper wall must be steeper than the material's friction limit with margin (for free-flowing grain, hopper angles from horizontal commonly around 45-60 degrees on grain-bin hoppers (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked); confirm the material's wall friction first; fines or higher moisture require steeper or smoother walls).
2. **Outlet size**: large enough to prevent arching; for cohesive materials, outlet dimension must exceed a critical arching dimension determined from shear-cell tests (Jenike). Free-flowing whole grain seldom arches, but fines, damp or mouldy grain, and meals do.
3. **Shape**: conical hoppers need smaller outlets than wedge or slotted hoppers for the same material; slotted outlets with a length at least three times the width help cohesive materials (Jenike rule of thumb (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)).
4. **Transitions**: smooth with no ledges; avoid inward projections at joints.

## Flow aids (secondary)
- Wall liners with lower friction (stainless, UHMW polyethylene) change the required angle.
- Air-assist cannons and pulsed air for fluidisable powders; vibrators (can compact the material and worsen arching if misapplied).
- Mechanical agitators and sweep augers in flat-bottom bins.

## Gotchas
- Fill method changes the stress in the hopper; fill gently from the centre.
- Corrosion reduces smoothness of wall surfaces.
