# Grain dryer energy efficiency and fuel use

**Goal:** lower fuel (and electricity) per unit of water removed without quality loss.

## Metrics
- **Specific energy**: energy per kg of water removed. The latent heat of evaporation of free water is about 2,450 kJ/kg; practical grain dryers use roughly 4,000 to 6,500 kJ/kg water, depending on type, recirculation and operation; compare with maker's data.
- Extension guides often quote fuel per bushel per point of moisture; typical values for LP gas drying of corn are of the order of 0.02-0.03 gal per bushel per point, depending on dryer; use your own measured value.
- Include fan electricity (maybe 5-10 percent of energy).

## Measure
1. Meter fuel (gas meter or tank gauge), grain throughput and moisture removed (inlet and outlet samples).
2. Compute fuel per bushel-point and per kg water; trend per season.

## Improve
1. Recirculate cooling-section exhaust air to the heater inlet (common in mixed-flow dryers).
2. Reduce excessive airflow that exits unsaturated; match airflow to heat capacity.
3. Insulate plenums, seal leaks, fix burner tuning (air-fuel ratio, CO and O2 checks).
4. Pre-dry in the field to limit points removed; fan-dry stage or dryeration finish to utilise ambient energy.
5. Heat recovery heat exchangers on exhaust where fines do not foul.
6. Avoid over-drying: control to target, not below.

## Gotchas
- Higher temperature increases efficiency but can degrade quality; stay within grain temperature limits.
- Efficiency falls as grain gets drier (bound water requires more energy).
