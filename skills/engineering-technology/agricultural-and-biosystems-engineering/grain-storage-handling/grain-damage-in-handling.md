# Grain damage in handling

**Goal:** minimise breakage and fines so the grain keeps its grade, stores well and mills or germinates properly.

## Why it matters
Broken kernels and fines raise the broken corn and foreign material (BCFM) count, lower grade and price, restrict airflow in bins (fines concentrate and block it), feed insects and mould, and reduce seed germination.

## Causes
- Impact at drop points (free fall onto a hard surface or onto previously damaged grain); impact velocity rises with fall height.
- Mechanical contact: high-speed augers, bucket elevators with high belt speed, hammers, back-feed.
- Grain condition: dry corn (below about 15 percent) and rapidly dried grain are brittle; stress cracks form during fast drying and cooling (`continuous-flow-grain-dryer-design-and-setup`); cold grain is more brittle, and varieties differ.

## Mitigation
- Reduce drop heights; use grain ladders, controlled-flow spouts or cushion boxes; keep spouts full.
- Use enclosed drag conveyors or low-speed belts for fragile grains (`drag-chain-conveyor-sizing`); slower auger speed with full flights.
- Avoid over-drying; cool the dried grain slowly (`dryeration-and-tempering-procedure`).
- Clean out fines at filling with a spreader or cleaner (`air-screen-cleaner-selection`).

## Measuring
Breakage susceptibility is measured by laboratory impact devices (Stein breakage tester and the Wisconsin breakage tester are the classic ones for corn); compare before and after a handling step. Mass of fines from a sieve test of the stream before and after each machine indicates which machine is the culprit.
