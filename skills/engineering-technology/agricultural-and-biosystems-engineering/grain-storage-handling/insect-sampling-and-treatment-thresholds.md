# Insect sampling and thresholds

**Goal:** detect infestation early enough to act with cooling, cleaning or approved treatment before grain is downgraded.

## Why sample
Insect populations multiply faster in warm grain (activity rises sharply above about 20 C and is low below about 15 C, and many species stop breeding well below 10 C (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)). Insect-damaged kernels and live insects can cause downgrading or rejection at purchase.

## Methods
- Grain samples taken by probe or from the stream, sieved over a standard sieve (use the one named in the applicable method) and live insects counted per kg or per sample.
- Probe traps (pitfall traps pushed into grain) caught over days; more sensitive than sampling; used in warm weather.
- Berlese funnel (heat and light drive insects through a screen) or a flotation test for hidden infestation inside kernels; CO2 monitoring complements them (`co2-monitoring-for-early-spoilage-detection`).
- Check the top surface, the grain at the bin walls, the sump and under the cap, and the equipment (augers, bins, boots) where residues harbour insects.

## Schedule
Sample about every two weeks during warm periods (higher frequency in warm, humid conditions), monthly in cool, and after any rise in grain temperature (typical practice, not from a standard (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)).

## Thresholds and actions
- Many buyers and grain standards apply a zero-tolerance or near-zero tolerance for live insects injurious to stored grain (for example 7 CFR 810.107 defines infested wheat, rye and triticale in a representative sample as two or more live weevils, or one live weevil plus one or more other live insects injurious to stored grain, or two or more such live insects; other grains have their own counts in the same section; the Canadian Grain Commission has its own definitions). Confirm the standard that applies to your sale.
- Responses: cool the grain by aeration to below insect development temperatures, clean and turn the grain, remove the infested portion, use an approved residual insecticide or fumigation (`fumigation-planning-and-regulatory-requirements`) only by licensed persons and following the label.
- Good sanitation (clean bins before filling) is the most cost-effective control.
