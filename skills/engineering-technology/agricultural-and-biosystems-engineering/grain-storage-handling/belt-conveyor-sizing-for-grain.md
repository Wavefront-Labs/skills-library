# Belt conveyor sizing for grain

**Goal:** a belt that carries the required flow at limited speed and incline with adequate power.

## Capacity
Volumetric flow `Q = A_s x v`, with A_s the material cross-section on the belt (from belt width, troughing angle, surcharge angle) and v belt speed. Free-flowing grain has a low surcharge angle (around 5-15 degrees (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)), so for a given belt width the cross-section is moderate; use maker tables for the width and trough angle (20-45 degree idlers (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)).

## Speed and incline
- Grain belts commonly run about 2-3.5 m/s (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked); faster speeds increase dust and wear and complicate loading.
- Maximum incline for grain on a smooth belt is limited by the material's angle of friction on the belt: roughly 15-17 degrees (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) (cleated or pattern belts allow more).

## Power and tension
Use the CEMA belt conveyor method or ISO 5048 (title, edition and scope (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)): power for moving the belt and material (idler friction and material flexure resistance), lift (`m g H`), skirt friction and acceleration at loading; add drive efficiency. Tension via friction at the drive pulley (wrap, lagging) must prevent slip.

## Details
Loading chute centred in the direction of belt travel, skirtboards, belt cleaners (scrapers), take-up (gravity or screw), covers for dust control, walkways and emergency stop cords. Static-dissipating belting where dust is a risk. Guard pinch points.

## Verify
- [ ] Throughput measured by volume or weigh scale is within tolerance; belt tracks straight at full load.
