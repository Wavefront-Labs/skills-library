# Hazardous area classification for grain facilities

Area classification and equipment selection are regulated engineering tasks; this note explains the concepts and does not replace the code or a qualified engineer.

## Frameworks
- **US National Electrical Code (NFPA 70)**: Class II locations for combustible dusts. Classification by Division 1 (dust present in ignitable quantities in normal operation) or Division 2 (abnormal conditions), or under the zone system for dusts. Group (e.g. Group G for grain dust) determines equipment ratings. Check the current NEC articles (such as Article 502 for Class II and Article 506 for zone classification; the NEC text is paywalled (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)).
- **IEC 60079 and ATEX**: zones 20 (continuous dust cloud), 21 (likely in normal operation) and 22 (unlikely, short duration) with equipment category and temperature class.
- NFPA 61 and OSHA 1910.272 reference electrical requirements for grain facilities.

## Practice
1. Map each area: interior of bucket elevator legs and dust collectors are the most hazardous (Zone 20/21 or Division 1); work floors with typical dust levels are zone 22 or Division 2 depending on housekeeping and source control.
2. Select equipment: dust-ignition-proof enclosures, motors, junction boxes, lights, sensors; rated surface temperature below the ignition temperature of dust layers and clouds (and with margin).
3. Wiring methods: sealed conduit, dust-tight fittings and glands; no open sparking.
4. Maintain integrity: gaskets, covers, glands and cleanliness; periodic inspection by competent persons.
5. Document the classification drawings and review when processes change.

- [ ] Classification drawings exist, equipment certificates match the zones or divisions, and inspections are recorded.
