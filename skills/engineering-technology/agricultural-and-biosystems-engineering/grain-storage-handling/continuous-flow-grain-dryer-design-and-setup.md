# Continuous-flow grain dryer design and setup

**Goal:** a dryer that removes the required points of moisture at throughput without damaging grain.

## Types
- **Cross-flow (column)**: air passes across a thin column (often a few tens of centimetres); simple, but grain nearest the plenum is over-dried; recirculation of exhaust air improves efficiency.
- **Mixed-flow (concurrent / counter-flow / crossflow combos)**: grain flows over alternate inlet and outlet ducts and is mixed; more uniform and efficient.
- **Tower dryers** with cooling section at the base.

## Settings (follow the maker; ranges are indicative)
- Drying air temperature: for feed grain corn commonly from about 80 to over 100 C depending on dryer type and moisture removed; for seed corn and other seed crops use much lower temperatures (seed corn air temperatures around 40-45 C); milling wheat and malting barley also need lower temperatures to protect quality.
- Grain exit temperature: after the cooling section should be within about 5-10 C of ambient.
- Throughput = metering speed; slower discharge removes more points of moisture.
- Number of points removed per pass is limited (often 5-10 points); remove more with multiple passes or lower throughput.

## Setup and control
1. Set the discharge rate for target moisture; check by moisture meter on discharged grain.
2. Check burner and fan systems; keep screens clean.
3. Use the maker's temperature limit alarms; high-limit controls for fire protection.
4. Control by moisture sensors for automatic discharge adjustment; recalibrate each season.

## Gotchas
- Fines in the grain plug screens and ignite; clean the plenums and screens regularly.
- Fire risks: follow the dryer's maintenance and lock-out procedures.

## Verify
- [ ] Moisture sample series at the discharge within about 0.5 point of target; stress cracks and test weight checked on trial.
