# Dryeration and tempering procedure

**Goal:** finish drying with the heat already in the grain, improving quality and capacity.

## Procedure
1. Dry in the dryer to about 1-3 percentage points above the final target moisture (extension sources differ) (the dryer is stopped before the cooling section; remaining heat is used).
2. Move hot grain (without cooling) directly to a dryeration bin and let it **temper for at least about 4 to 6 hours** without airflow (Iowa State, UMN): moisture inside kernels equilibrates and the heat drives it out.
3. Then cool with a low airflow (often about 0.5-1.0 cfm/bu in guidance) until grain is within about 5-10 F of ambient. The cooling front carries warm moist air out; moisture removed is typically 2-3 points (UMN Extension).
4. Check the final moisture and temperature; transfer to storage.

## Benefits
- Higher dryer capacity (no cooling section).
- Lower fuel per point.
- Reduces stress cracking and breakage since cooling is slow.

## Cautions
- Condensation on cold roofs or walls when warm moist air leaves the bin: ensure roof vents are open.
- Do not leave hot grain in tempering for too long (mould or insect growth in humid grain).
- Safety: hot grain bins can hold carbon monoxide or have elevated temperatures; follow bin entry rules.
