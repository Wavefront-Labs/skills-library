# Flat storage and bunker design

**Goal:** low-cost storage that does not fail structurally or spoil grain.

## Loads
- Lateral pressure on warehouse walls from piled grain depends on pile height and angle; walls should be designed for the full-depth case with the code's K and friction, and reinforced for end walls and doors. Piles against a wall that was not designed for them can collapse the wall.
- Floor slab loads; truck and loader traffic loads.

## Aeration
- Floor ducts or portable fans with ducts feed air through the pile; the layout must give a path length per unit airflow equal to those in bins (use airflow resistance data).
- Grain depth is limited by fan pressure and moisture; depth of 5-8 m is typical for dry grain in warehouses with aeration.

## Moisture and cover
- Keep drainage away from the pile and the floor sealed against ground moisture; roofs or tarpaulins protect piles; water ingress causes spoilage.
- Store only dry grain at or below the safe storage moisture for the planned period (see moisture skill); temporary outdoor piles (ground piles) are for short periods with tarp covers and frequent monitoring.

## Pest and fire control
Sanitation, fumigation capability (see fumigation planning skill) and clear access routes; no equipment-generated sparks near dust; fire protection systems per local code.

## Gotchas
- Poor distribution of fines in a flat pile gives uneven airflow.
- Condensation under covers in cold weather.
