# Lot traceability and blending

**Goal:** know where every load came from and went, and blend only in ways that are allowed and meet the specification.

## Traceability
- Assign a lot number at receiving: date, field or supplier, bin, quality results and handling steps. Keep the weigh-ticket numbers, moisture, grade, treatments and test results with the lot.
- One step back, one step forward: record the supplier and the customer for every lot; in food and feed regulation this is often a legal requirement (for example EU General Food Law Regulation 178/2002 Article 18 and the US FDA food traceability rules); confirm what applies.
- Retain samples for the shelf life or the contract period; label and store them securely.
- Identity-preserved crops (organic, non-GM, specialty) need segregation, clean-out procedures between lots and documented checks.
- Test your system with a mock recall: how fast can you identify all customers of a lot?

## Blending
- Weighted average: blend moisture and test weight are weighted averages by mass (test weight by volume when volumes are mixed, so use density arithmetic). Other factors (percent damaged) average by mass fraction.
- Plan blends to hit spec at the receiving point; check with a sample of the blend, not just calculation, because segregation arises during handling.
- Do not blend a lot that exceeds a legal limit for mycotoxins, pesticide residues or foreign matter with clean grain to dilute it below the limit where the regulation forbids; many jurisdictions prohibit this for food. Blending to improve quality grades is generally permitted when the product is correctly declared. Check the rule with the regulator.
