# Variable-rate economics and break-even

**Goal:** know whether a variable-rate system pays on your fields.

## Partial budget
- **Benefits**: yield or quality gain (units x price), input savings (less product in low-response zones), reduced lodging or environmental compliance.
- **Costs**: equipment (controllers, sensors, upgrades), software, soil or imagery data (sampling cost per hectare), consultant fees, extra labour and time.
- **Net return per hectare** = benefits - annualised costs.

## Break-even
- Annualised equipment cost = (purchase price - salvage) / years + interest.
- Break-even area = annual fixed cost / (net benefit per hectare before fixed costs).
- Spread across the number of crops and operations that use the same equipment (seeding, fertiliser, lime, spraying).

## Key drivers
1. **Within-field variability**: stable and large differences in yield potential or soil nutrients.
2. **Response**: variable rate pays if the optimal rate differs between zones and the response curve is non-linear; if yield is insensitive, savings come only from lower inputs.
3. **Input prices**: high fertiliser or seed prices increase gains from targeted use.
4. **Accuracy of the prescription**: poor zones dilute benefits.

## Testing
Use on-farm strip trials (alternate uniform and variable-rate strips across the field, replicated) and analyse yields with simple statistics (see on-farm trials skill); published studies show mixed results, so local evidence matters.

## Gotchas
- Benefits measured in one year may not repeat with weather.
- Count only net benefit, including data costs.
