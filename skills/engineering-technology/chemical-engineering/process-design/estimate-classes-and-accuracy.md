# Estimate classes and accuracy ranges

**Goal:** label every capital estimate with its class and expected accuracy so decisions match the information available.

## The classification (AACE International recommended practice for the process industries)
| Class | Project definition | Typical use | Typical accuracy range (indicative (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | about 0 to 2% | Screening, concept | low about -20 to -50%, high about +30 to +100% |
| 4 | about 1 to 15% | Feasibility, study | low about -15 to -30%, high about +20 to +50% |
| 3 | about 10 to 40% | Budget authorisation | low about -10 to -20%, high about +10 to +30% |
| 2 | about 30 to 75% | Control, bid check | low about -5 to -15%, high about +5 to +20% |
| 1 | about 65 to 100% | Check estimate, bid | low about -3 to -10%, high about +3 to +15% |
The class numbers, definition percentages and ranges above are from memory of AACE 18R-97 and could not be confirmed this session (the standard is sold by AACE; the one search found a secondary source giving Class 5 as -50% to +100%, which differs in split from the table). These ranges are indicative only; read the current AACE recommended practice for the industry and confirm the exact table before quoting it in a report.

## Using classes
1. State the class and range in the estimate cover page.
2. Do not quote a class 5 estimate to three significant figures.
3. Match the contingency to the class and the risk (see the contingency skill).
4. Plan the work to move from class 5 to 4 to 3: what definition (PFDs, equipment list, P&IDs, plot plan) closes the gap.

## Gotchas
- The percentage ranges are not statistical confidence intervals; they depend on technology novelty, contractor, location.
- Accuracy refers to total installed cost, not individual line items.

## Verify
- [ ] Estimate class stated with the deliverables list that supports it.
- [ ] Contingency and range aligned.
