# Materials and corrosion for exchangers

**Goal:** Select exchanger tube and shell materials for corrosion, temperature and cost: carbon steel, stainless, duplex, titanium and alloys.

## Steps
1. List the fluids with chlorides, acids, sulphur compounds, temperature and pH on both sides.
2. Start with carbon steel and add corrosion allowance; step up to stainless, duplex, titanium or nickel alloys by duty (for example, chloride stress corrosion cracking limits austenitic stainless steel in warm chloride water).
3. Check the cooling water side: tube velocity limits (minimum to avoid fouling and maximum to avoid erosion) depend on the material.
4. Consider galvanic couples and the combination of the tubesheet and the tube materials.
5. Confirm choices with a materials engineer and the corrosion data for the actual service.

## Gotchas
- Corrosion data are conditional on trace species and temperature.
- Cheaper materials may cost more over the life.

## Verify
- [ ] Material selection records the basis and the allowances.
- [ ] Velocity limits are checked.
