# Reach clp for process engineers

**Goal:** Explain what REACH and CLP mean to process engineers: registration, safety data sheets, classification and authorisation; confirm the current law.

## Steps
1. REACH (Regulation (EC) No 1907/2006, Registration, Evaluation, Authorisation and Restriction of Chemicals) covers registration, evaluation, authorisation and restriction of chemicals in the EU; CLP (Regulation (EC) No 1272/2008, on classification, labelling and packaging of substances and mixtures) sets classification, labelling and packaging, in line with the UN GHS.
2. Check that every substance in the process has registration status (manufacturer, importer or downstream user duties) and a current safety data sheet with exposure scenarios where required.
3. Check the candidate list of substances of very high concern, the authorisation list (Annex XIV) and the restriction entries (Annex XVII) for process chemicals, which change regularly.
4. Great Britain has its own UK REACH (created by the REACH etc. (Amendment etc.) (EU Exit) Regulations 2019, SI 2019/758) and GB CLP (HSE) after Brexit; other regions have different systems (for example TSCA in the US).
5. Confirm the current legal text, the lists and the duties with the regulator (ECHA in the EU) or a regulatory specialist. Professional practice guidance only; the governing regulators, standards and a qualified engineer decide.

## Gotchas
- Annex and list content changes frequently.
- A downstream user must check that its use is covered by the registrant's identified uses and exposure scenarios, or take the further steps REACH requires (article-level duties: confirm in the Regulation text) (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
