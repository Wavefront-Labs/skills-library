# Facility siting and spacing

**Goal:** Plan facility siting and spacing: separation distances, occupied buildings, blast and thermal effects, and use of recognised guidance.

## Steps
1. List the hazards and the credible scenarios (fire, explosion, toxic release) and the receivers (control rooms, offices, public boundary).
2. Use recognised guidance for occupied building siting (for example API RP 752 Management of Hazards Associated with Location of Process Plant Permanent Buildings, 4th edition, RP 753 for Portable Buildings, 2nd edition, and RP 756 for Tents, 2nd edition, all published January 2024 per the API announcement; confirm the current editions). OSHA 29 CFR 1910.119(e)(3)(v) requires the process hazard analysis to address facility siting and the spacing tables of your insurer or company.
3. Evaluate the blast and thermal loads with a consequence model and the building response criteria; relocate or harden the occupied buildings if necessary.
4. Consider the ignition sources, drainage of spills, and the wind direction.
5. Document the basis and revalidate on change; the study should be done by competent engineers. Professional practice guidance only; the governing regulators, standards and a qualified engineer decide.

## Gotchas
- Spacing tables are minimums; scenarios decide.
- Temporary buildings need the same review.

## Verify
- [ ] Occupied buildings assessed against the credible scenarios.
- [ ] Basis is documented and reviewed on change.
