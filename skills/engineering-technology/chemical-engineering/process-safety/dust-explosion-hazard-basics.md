# Dust explosion hazard basics

**Goal:** Recognise combustible dust explosion hazards: test parameters (Kst, Pmax, MIE, MEC), the dust explosion pentagon, and prevention and protection.

## Steps
1. Determine whether the dust is combustible with tests from a laboratory: Kst and Pmax (severity), minimum ignition energy (MIE), minimum ignition temperature, minimum explosible concentration.
2. The explosion pentagon needs fuel, oxidant, ignition source, dispersion and confinement; remove one to prevent.
3. Prevent by controlling ignition sources, inerting, housekeeping to limit dust layers, and bonding and grounding.
4. Protect by explosion venting (designed to the standards, for example NFPA 68, Standard on Explosion Protection by Deflagration Venting, current edition 2023 per the NFPA catalog; EN 14491:2012 Dust explosion venting protective systems (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)), suppression, isolation and containment.
5. Follow the regulations that apply (OSHA in the US; ATEX in the EU, where Annex I of Directive 1999/92/EC defines the dust zones 20, 21 and 22) and consult a specialist for design; confirm the current editions. Professional practice guidance only; the governing regulators, standards and a qualified engineer decide.

## Gotchas
- Test data are for the specific material, particle size and moisture.
- Secondary explosions from the dust layers are often the most damaging.
