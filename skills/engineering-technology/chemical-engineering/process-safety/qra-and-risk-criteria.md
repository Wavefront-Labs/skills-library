# Qra and risk criteria

**Goal:** Describe quantitative risk assessment (QRA): frequency and consequence, individual and societal risk, and the use of risk criteria set by the regulator.

## Steps
1. Define the scope and the release scenarios (hole sizes, failure frequencies from generic databases), and the ignition and outcome events (event trees).
2. Calculate consequences (dispersion, fire, explosion) and vulnerability (probit or thresholds) at each location.
3. Combine frequency and consequence into individual risk contours and societal risk (FN curves), and compare with the criteria of the authority; criteria differ between countries (for example the UK HSE ALARP framework and tolerability limits, Dutch and other national criteria; the HSE guidance page could not be retrieved, so confirm the current figures at hse.gov.uk (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)); confirm what applies.
4. Use ALARP or equivalent judgement to evaluate the measures with cost-benefit analysis.
5. Communicate the uncertainty and sensitivity; a QRA is done by competent specialists using validated software. Professional practice guidance only; the governing regulators, standards and a qualified engineer decide.

## Gotchas
- Generic frequency data have large uncertainty.
- Compare only with the criteria set by the relevant authority.

## Verify
- [ ] Scope, data sources and assumptions are documented.
