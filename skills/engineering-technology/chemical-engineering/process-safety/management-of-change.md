# Management of change

**Goal:** no process, equipment, procedure or organisational change is made without understanding and controlling its effect on safety. Regulations such as OSHA 29 CFR 1910.119(l) require written MOC procedures for changes to chemicals, technology, equipment and procedures (other than replacement in kind) and changes affecting a covered process; other jurisdictions have equivalents. Confirm your regulatory requirements.

## What is a change
Technology (feed, rates, temperature, pressure limits), equipment (new items, material change, relief device change, instrument range and set points), procedures, facilities (layout, buildings), organisation (staffing, critical roles), and software/control logic changes. A **replacement in kind** (same design specification) is not a change; define the term clearly in the site procedure.

## Steps
1. **Request**: describe the change, reason, scope, and affected documents; assign a number.
2. **Technical basis** (1910.119(l)(2) also requires the impact on safety and health, modifications to operating procedures, necessary time period and authorization requirements to be addressed before the change): design calculations for the new conditions (hydraulics, relief, materials, area classification, controls).
3. **Hazard review**: scale to the risk (checklist, what-if, or HAZOP for significant changes); consider interactions with other units, impacts on safeguards and the relief and flare system.
4. **Authorisation** by designated managers (operations, engineering, safety), with the right to reject.
5. **Update documents** before start-up: PSI, P&IDs, operating procedures, maintenance plans, emergency response, alarm and trip lists, SIS documents.
6. **Training**: inform and train affected employees and contractors before the change is placed in service.
7. **Pre-startup safety review** (see pre-startup-safety-review) to verify the change was built and documented as designed.
8. **Close-out**: verify documentation and as-built drawings, record the date.

## Temporary changes
Set an expiry date and conditions; review before extension; revert or make permanent via the full process. Temporary hoses, bypasses and jumpers on safety systems are a common incident cause.

## Gotchas
- Small changes with large consequences: a change of set point, a changed pump impeller, a different supplier's material.
- Back-door changes by maintenance or operations during emergencies; require retrospective review and close-out.
