# Atex and dsear overview

**Goal:** Understand ATEX (equipment Directive 2014/34/EU and workplace Directive 1999/92/EC) and the UK DSEAR for explosive atmospheres; confirm current law.

## Steps
1. The EU ATEX framework has two directives: 2014/34/EU (recast, 26 February 2014) for equipment and protective systems intended for use in potentially explosive atmospheres, and 1999/92/EC (16 December 1999, the 15th individual directive under 89/391/EEC) on minimum requirements for the safety and health of workers potentially at risk from explosive atmospheres.
2. Employers must assess the explosion risk, classify places into zones (gas: 0, 1, 2; dust: 20, 21, 22, defined in Annex I of 1999/92/EC), draw up and keep up to date an explosion protection document (hazards, risk evaluation, measures taken) and use suitable equipment with appropriate marking and category.
3. In Great Britain DSEAR (Dangerous Substances and Explosive Atmospheres Regulations 2002) implements the workplace requirements, and the Equipment and Protective Systems Intended for Use in Potentially Explosive Atmospheres Regulations 2016 (SI 2016/1107) apply to the supply of equipment.
4. In North America the NEC and the Canadian electrical code use the class and division or zone methods; confirm local rules.
5. Check the current legal text and the harmonised standards used with the competent authority. Professional practice guidance only; the governing regulators, standards and a qualified engineer decide.

## Gotchas
- The equipment marking must match the zone, gas group and temperature class.
- Regulations after Brexit differ between Great Britain and the EU.
