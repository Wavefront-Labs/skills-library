# Runaway relief design considerations

**Goal:** Understand relief design considerations for runaway reactions: two-phase flow, DIERS methods, vent sizing data needs and preferred layers.

## Steps
1. Establish the runaway scenarios (loss of cooling, mischarge, contamination, agitation failure) and the adiabatic data of the reacting mixture.
2. Determine the flow regime (gas only, vapour from the boiling system, or gassy) and expect two-phase flow, which needs much larger relief areas than vapour only.
3. Use the DIERS methods for vent sizing (for example the Leung method for simple cases and a dynamic simulation for complex ones) with the data from a vent sizing calorimeter; seek a specialist (this skill states no specific numbers).
4. Prefer preventive layers: inherently safer design, controlled feed, interlocks and independent trips, before relying on relief alone.
5. Plan for the safe discharge, collection and treatment of the relief effluent. Professional practice guidance only; the governing regulators, standards and a qualified engineer decide.

## Gotchas
- Vapour-only sizing of a two-phase flow gives an undersized device.
- Data for the real recipe are required.
