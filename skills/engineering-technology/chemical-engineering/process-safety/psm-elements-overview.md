# OSHA PSM elements overview

**Goal:** a map of the process safety management requirements so engineers know what documents and programmes support the design. This is a summary for orientation; the governing text is 29 CFR 1910.119 (United States federal OSHA). Confirm the current regulation, state plan variations, and applicability (threshold quantities in the appendix) with qualified safety professionals and counsel. Other jurisdictions use different regimes (for example Seveso III Directive 2012/18/EU and the UK COMAH Regulations 2015 for major accident hazards).

## Applicability
Processes involving listed highly hazardous chemicals at or above the threshold quantities in Appendix A, and processes with a flammable gas or a flammable liquid with flashpoint below 100 F (37.8 C) on site in one location in a quantity of 10,000 pounds (4535.9 kg) or more (with exemptions such as hydrocarbon fuel used solely for workplace consumption). Check the standard.

## The elements (paragraphs c to p)
1. **Employee participation**: written plan, access to PHAs and information.
2. **Process safety information (PSI)**: chemical hazards, technology (block flow, operating limits, consequence of deviation), equipment (materials, P&IDs, electrical classification, relief design basis, ventilation, design codes, safety systems).
3. **Process hazard analysis (PHA)**: appropriate method (what-if, checklist, HAZOP, FMEA, FTA), team, address hazards, previous incidents, controls, facility siting, human factors; resolve findings; **revalidate at least every five years**.
4. **Operating procedures**: steps for each phase, operating limits, safety and health considerations; annual certification of currency.
5. **Training**: initial and refresher at least every three years.
6. **Contractors**: selection, safety performance evaluation, information and training.
7. **Pre-startup safety review** (PSSR) for new and modified facilities.
8. **Mechanical integrity**: written procedures, training, inspection and testing, deficiency correction, quality assurance for equipment in scope.
9. **Hot work permit**.
10. **Management of change** (MOC).
11. **Incident investigation**: start within 48 hours, report, resolve and retain.
12. **Emergency planning and response** (referencing 1910.38 and 1910.120).
13. **Compliance audits** at least every three years, with a certified report and response to findings.
14. **Trade secrets** access for those developing PSI, PHAs etc.

## Mapping to CCPS Risk Based Process Safety (RBPS)
CCPS organises 20 elements under four pillars: commit to process safety, understand hazards and risk, manage risk, learn from experience (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked). PSM elements fit within these.

## Gotchas
- Documentation must be current (PSI updated with changes); a stale P&ID undermines everything.
- A new process engineer should locate where each element lives in the site's management system.
