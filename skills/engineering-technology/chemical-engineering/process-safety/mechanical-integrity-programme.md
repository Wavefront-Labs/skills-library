# Mechanical integrity programme

**Goal:** keep critical equipment fit for service through planned inspection, testing and maintenance. Regulations such as OSHA 29 CFR 1910.119(j) require written procedures, training, inspection and testing to recognised and generally accepted good engineering practices (RAGAGEP), correction of deficiencies and quality assurance; other jurisdictions have equivalents. Inspection of pressure equipment must be carried out by qualified inspectors. Confirm the requirements and current standard editions.

## Scope (typical)
Pressure vessels and storage tanks, piping systems (including components such as valves), relief and vent systems and devices, emergency shutdown systems, controls (monitoring devices and sensors, alarms and interlocks), and pumps (critical rotating equipment); this is the list in 29 CFR 1910.119(j)(1).

## Programme elements
1. **Equipment register** with identification, service, materials, design data and criticality.
2. **Inspection codes** commonly applied: API 510 (pressure vessels in service), API 570 (piping), API 653 (aboveground storage tanks), API 576 (relief device inspection), supported by API 580 and 581 for risk-based inspection (RBI) (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked). Use the editions your jurisdiction or company adopts.
3. **Inspection and test plans** (ITPs): method (visual, thickness measurement, radiography, ultrasonic, dye penetrant), locations (corrosion monitoring locations), frequency (from code, manufacturer, corrosion rate and remaining life; for example remaining life divided by two, subject to code maximums (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)), acceptance criteria.
4. **Safety system testing**: relief valves bench-tested at set intervals; interlocks and SIFs proof-tested per the SIS plan.
5. **Deficiency management**: out-of-tolerance findings assessed (fitness-for-service as in API 579 (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)), temporary measures, repair or replace before further operation or with safe operating limits; track to closure.
6. **Quality assurance**: materials, fabrication, spare parts and installation checks (positive material identification where needed).
7. **Training** of inspectors and maintenance personnel; documentation and records retained.

## Practice
- Use corrosion rate calculated from thickness history (short-term and long-term) to set intervals; update with inspection results.
- Integrate with MOC: a change of service or materials alters corrosion mechanisms and inspection plans.

## Gotchas
- Backlog of overdue inspections is a typical audit and incident precursor; trend overdue items.
- Injection points, dead legs and corrosion under insulation are known high-risk locations in many plants.

## Verify
- [ ] Equipment register with plan, last and next inspection dates.
- [ ] Overdue items reported with risk assessment and management sign-off.
