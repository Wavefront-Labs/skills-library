# Inherently safer design strategies

**Goal:** Apply inherently safer design strategies: minimise, substitute, moderate and simplify, before adding protective layers.

## Steps
The four strategies below follow the CCPS/Kletz usage (CCPS, Inherently Safer Chemical Processes: A Life Cycle Approach; confirm the wording there (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)).
1. Minimise: reduce the inventory of hazardous material in vessels, piping and storage.
2. Substitute: use a less hazardous material or process route where practical (for example a water-based solvent instead of a flammable one).
3. Moderate: use less severe conditions (lower temperature or pressure) or dilute the hazardous material.
4. Simplify: remove unnecessary complexity and the opportunity for error; design for tolerance of errors.
5. Record the options considered at each design stage (concept, detail) with the reason for the choice, and do not transfer the risk elsewhere; this is guidance, defer to a qualified process safety engineer.

## Gotchas
- Inherent safety options are cheapest in early design.
- Check that a change does not create a new hazard.

## Verify
- [ ] Options are recorded in the design review.
- [ ] Each alternative has a documented residual risk.
