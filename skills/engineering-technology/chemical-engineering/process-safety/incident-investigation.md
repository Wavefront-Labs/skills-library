# Incident investigation and learning

**Goal:** understand why an incident or near miss occurred and prevent recurrence. Regulations such as OSHA 29 CFR 1910.119(m) require investigation of incidents that resulted in or could reasonably have resulted in a catastrophic release, initiated within 48 hours, by a team including a person knowledgeable in the process, with a written report, resolution of findings and retention of reports (five years in the OSHA text, paragraph (m)(7), checked against the eCFR); other jurisdictions have comparable duties (for example reporting to the regulator). Confirm the requirements for your location and any duties to notify authorities.

## Process
1. **Immediate**: make the scene safe, care for people, notify as required, preserve evidence (do not restart equipment, photograph, collect samples, preserve control system data and logs, secure failed parts).
2. **Team**: appoint a team with the right expertise and independence (process, operations, maintenance, safety, instrumentation, a leader trained in investigation methods); for serious events consider external specialists.
3. **Collect facts**: interviews (separately, non-blaming), data (historian trends, alarm logs, shift logs, maintenance and inspection history, MOCs), procedures, drawings, weather, time line.
4. **Analyse**: build a timeline, then identify causal factors and root causes using a recognised technique (fault tree, causal factor charting, "5 whys", barrier analysis, TapRooT or similar). Look beyond the immediate cause to underlying management system causes (design, procedures, training, maintenance, change management, supervision).
5. **Recommendations**: specific corrective and preventive actions with owners and dates, prioritised by effectiveness (eliminate or substitute, engineering controls before administrative controls).
6. **Report**: facts, analysis, root causes, recommendations, distribution; review with the workforce.
7. **Track** actions to closure with verification of effectiveness; feed into HAZOP revalidation, MOC and training.
8. **Share** lessons across sites and with industry as appropriate.

## Near misses
Investigate near misses with a proportionate effort; they are cheaper lessons than losses and reveal weak barriers.

## Gotchas
- Stopping at "operator error" prevents learning; ask why the error was likely.
- Evidence lost within hours (control system data overwritten) is the biggest practical risk; set up automatic preservation.
- Blame culture suppresses reporting.

## Verify
- [ ] Investigation began within the required time and the report lists root causes and tracked actions.
- [ ] Action closure evidence and effectiveness checks recorded.
