# Seveso comah overview

**Goal:** Understand the Seveso III Directive (2012/18/EU) and its UK equivalent COMAH: thresholds, tiers, safety reports and duties; confirm the current law.

## Steps
1. Seveso III (Directive 2012/18/EU of 4 July 2012, repealing Directive 96/82/EC) applies in EU member states to establishments holding dangerous substances above threshold quantities; there are lower-tier and upper-tier levels.
2. Operators must notify, have a major accident prevention policy and, for the upper tier, a safety report, an internal emergency plan, and cooperate on external plans and land-use planning.
3. In Great Britain, the Control of Major Accident Hazards Regulations 2015 (SI 2015/483, COMAH) apply with a similar structure; the HSE and the environment agencies act together as the Competent Authority (HSE COMAH pages). Northern Ireland has its own Control of Major Accident Hazards Regulations (Northern Ireland) 2015 (SR 2015/325).
4. Check the inventory against the thresholds in the Directive annexes (the list is amended; confirm the current text), including the aggregation rule (Annex I Part 2: the directive applies when the sum q1/QU1 + q2/QU2 + ... is 1 or more, with separate sums for lower-tier and upper-tier thresholds).
5. Law changes and national implementation varies; confirm the current text for the jurisdiction with the competent authority or legal adviser. Professional practice guidance only; the governing regulators, standards and a qualified engineer decide.

## Gotchas
- Threshold values and substance categories are updated with CLP changes.
- Domino effect rules (Article 9) may apply to neighbouring sites.
