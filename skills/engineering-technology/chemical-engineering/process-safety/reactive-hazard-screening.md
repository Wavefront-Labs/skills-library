# Reactive hazard screening

**Goal:** Screen process reactive hazards with literature, chemical interaction checks and thermal screening (DSC, ARC) so that hazards are recognised and prevented.

## Steps
1. Collect the data on all materials and reactions: heats of reaction, decomposition onset, incompatibilities, and the known hazards from reliable references and safety data sheets.
2. Use an interaction matrix for mixtures and an evaluation of the potential for gas generation or runaway.
3. Do thermal screening (differential scanning calorimetry) to find the onset temperature and the decomposition energy, and adiabatic calorimetry (ARC or equivalent) for the self-heat rate and time to maximum rate.
4. Set safe operating limits with a margin below the onset (company practice; there are rules of thumb but confirm with a specialist).
5. Test in an accredited laboratory with trained staff; this is hazard recognition and prevention, not a design for making hazardous materials. Professional practice guidance only; the governing regulators, standards and a qualified engineer decide.

## Gotchas
- Screening in small samples can underestimate the adiabatic risk.
- Contamination and the scale change the onset.

## Verify
- [ ] Thermal data exist for all new materials and mixtures.
- [ ] Operating limits have a documented margin.
