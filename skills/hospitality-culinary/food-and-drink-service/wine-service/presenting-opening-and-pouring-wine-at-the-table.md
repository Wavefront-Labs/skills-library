# Presenting, opening and pouring wine at the table

**Goal:** get the bottle the guest ordered open, checked and poured at the right
temperature and pace, with no drips, no popped corks and no argument about faults.

## 1. Present
Stand to the right of the host (whoever ordered), label facing them, and name it:
producer, wine, **vintage**. The vintage is the thing that most often differs from
the list — say it out loud so a substitution is agreed now, not discovered later.

## 2. Open a still wine (waiter's friend)
1. Cut the foil **below the lower lip** of the bottle neck so wine never runs over
   foil on the pour. Pocket the foil, don't leave it on the table.
2. Wipe the top of the cork with a napkin.
3. Enter the worm just off-centre so the spiral runs down the middle; turn until
   one turn of the worm remains visible — going through can push cork crumbs in.
4. Lever with the first step of the two-step hinge, then the second; ease the last
   few mm out by hand, quietly.
5. Check the cork smell discreetly (a musty cork *suggests* TCA, it doesn't prove it)
   and place it beside the host.
Broken cork: re-enter at an angle against the glass; if it falls in, decant through
a fine strainer and say so.

## 3. Open sparkling
Bottle chilled (a warm bottle gushes). Remove foil, keep a thumb on the cage from
the moment you loosen it (usually six half-turns), point it away from guests and
yourself. **Turn the bottle, not the cork**, at ~45°, letting the cork sigh out.
Pour in two stages — a little foam, let it settle, then top up.

## 4. The taste and faults
Pour ~25–30 ml for the host. They're checking **condition, not preference**.
Faults worth replacing: cork taint (wet cardboard/musty basement, muted fruit),
oxidation (brown edge, sherry/bruised-apple on a wine that shouldn't have it),
heat damage (cooked/stewed fruit, pushed cork). "I don't like it" is not a fault;
many houses will still swap it — know your house's policy and who decides.
If unsure, taste it yourself (in a separate glass) or get the manager/sommelier.

## 5. Pour
- Ladies-first or clockwise from the host's right is common; the **host is served
  last**. Follow house protocol.
- Pour from the guest's right, label visible, don't touch glass to bottle; finish
  with a twist and catch the drip with a napkin.
- Fill **about a third** of a red glass, less than half a white; leave room to swirl.
  A 750 ml bottle gives roughly five 150 ml glasses — fewer if you over-pour, which
  makes the table run dry before the main.
- Watch levels and top up, but don't force-refill people who are slowing down.

## 6. Temperature and glassware (rules of thumb)
| Style | Serve around |
|---|---|
| Sparkling, light whites, rosé | 6–10 °C |
| Fuller whites, light reds (Pinot, Gamay) | 10–14 °C |
| Full reds | 15–18 °C |
Restaurant "room temperature" is usually too warm for reds: 10–15 minutes in an
ice bucket fixes it. A white left in ice all meal ends up too cold to taste.

## 7. Decanting
- **For sediment** (older reds, vintage port): stand the bottle upright for hours if
  possible, pour slowly in one motion over a light source, stop when sediment
  reaches the neck.
- **For air** (young tannic reds): splash-decant. Ask first — some guests want to
  watch the wine open in the glass.

## 8. By the glass
Date and time-label every opened bottle; inert-gas or argon preservation, or a
needle system (e.g. Coravin) for premium pours. Typical shelf life once opened is a
few days for still wine, sparkling less — taste before pouring, every time.

## Gotchas
- Foil cut above the lip = metallic drips on the tablecloth.
- Not saying the vintage leads to the most common wine dispute.
- Pouring the host first then everyone else empties the bottle unevenly.
- Never sniff-and-reject silently at the bar — the guest saw the bottle opened.

## Verify
- [ ] Vintage confirmed aloud at presentation.
- [ ] Foil cut below the lip, cork intact, no crumbs in the wine.
- [ ] Sparkling opened with thumb on cage and no pop.
- [ ] Host tasted first, served last; pours even and ~⅓ glass.
- [ ] Wine at the right temperature band; ice bucket used or removed as needed.

*Uncertain:* exact temperatures and BTG shelf life vary by source and wine; treat
as starting points, and follow local responsible-service and pour-size laws.
