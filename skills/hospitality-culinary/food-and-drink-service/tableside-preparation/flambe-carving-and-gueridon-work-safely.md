# Flambé, carving and guéridon work — safely

**Goal:** finish a dish at the table that tastes as good as the kitchen version,
looks deliberate, and never sets fire to a guest, a curtain or you.

## 1. Before anything: house and local rules
Many venues, insurers and some local fire codes restrict open flame indoors, require
a fire blanket/extinguisher within reach, or ban flambé near sprinklers or drapes.
Check yours. **This is the part to confirm locally — rules differ widely.**

## 2. Guéridon mise en place
Set the trolley before you approach — returning to the kitchen mid-show kills it.
- Réchaud (spirit lamp or butane burner) checked, fuel full, lighter.
- Pan (copper or steel suzette pan), two serving spoons + fork for one-hand
  "pince" handling, ladle, cutting board, carving knife and fork.
- **Spirits pre-measured into a small jug or ramekin** (e.g. 30–45 ml per portion).
- Mise in small dishes in order of use; a dish for waste; a clean plate per guest
  warming on the trolley edge or from the kitchen.
- Trolley positioned so the flame side faces away from the guests and any drapes,
  burner at least an arm's length from the table edge.

## 3. Flambé rules
1. **Never pour from the bottle into a hot pan.** Flame can travel back into the
   bottle and it can burst. Always from the pre-measured jug, pan pulled off the heat.
2. Warm the spirit briefly in the pan's edge or a ladle — cold spirit won't light;
   40% ABV or stronger lights reliably, liqueurs below ~30% often won't.
3. Tilt the pan edge to the burner flame or light with a long lighter, **away from
   yourself and the guests**, and shake gently until the flame dies out.
4. Never lean over the pan; long sleeves rolled, hair tied back.
5. If it won't go out, cover with the lid — have it on the trolley.

## 4. Crêpes Suzette (classic sequence, outline)
Butter and sugar caramelise in the pan → orange (and lemon) juice and zest to make
the sauce → fold pre-made crêpes into quarters and warm in the sauce → orange liqueur
and/or brandy from the jug, flame → plate three crêpes, nap with sauce.
The sauce should coat a spoon; too thin means it wasn't reduced before the crêpes went in.

## 5. Tableside Caesar (outline)
Wooden bowl: anchovy and garlic mashed to paste → egg yolk (or coddled egg) →
lemon, Worcestershire, mustard → oil whisked in slowly to emulsify → romaine tossed
→ parmesan and croutons. Ask about allergies (egg, fish, dairy, gluten) *before*
starting; raw/coddled egg is a food-safety risk for vulnerable guests — many houses
use pasteurised yolks.

## 6. Carving at the table
- Rest the joint in the kitchen first; carving a just-out roast floods the board.
- Steady with the fork through the side, never press the fork into the slice you're
  cutting. Long even strokes, **across the grain**, slices of even thickness.
- Plate on hot plates, sauce on the side or under, never over a crust you just
  showed off.

## 7. Showmanship that works
Narrate lightly ("this is the orange butter, now the Grand Marnier"), keep
movements economical, keep the trolley tidy as you go. Practise the full
routine in an empty room until you don't think about the sequence.

## Gotchas
- Pouring from the bottle — the single most dangerous habit.
- Butane cartridges left in a hot station or flame under a sprinkler head.
- Crêpe sauce reduced too far turns to bitter caramel in seconds.
- Food-safety still applies: temperatures, gloves/utensils, allergen cross-contact
  from shared pans.

## Verify
- [ ] Local/house flame rules checked; extinguisher or blanket within reach.
- [ ] Trolley fully set before approaching; nothing fetched mid-routine.
- [ ] Spirit measured into a jug; pan off heat to add it; lit away from guests.
- [ ] Allergies asked before starting.
- [ ] Carved slices even, across the grain, on hot plates.

*Uncertain:* quantities and recipe outlines are generic; follow the house recipe.
