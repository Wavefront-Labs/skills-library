# Taking and protecting an allergen order

**Goal:** a guest with an allergy or dietary requirement gets a dish that is
actually safe, through a chain where every hand-off is confirmed — not a server's
best guess.

## 1. Know the regulated list for your jurisdiction
- **UK / EU:** 14 allergens that must be declared (celery, cereals with gluten, crustaceans, eggs,
  fish, lupin, milk, molluscs, mustard, tree nuts, peanuts, sesame, soya,
  sulphites above 10 parts per million). Food businesses must be able to give allergen info for every dish.
- **US:** 9 major allergens (milk, eggs, fish, crustacean shellfish, tree nuts,
  peanuts, wheat, soy, sesame).
- **Australia/NZ, Canada and others** have their own lists.
Check current law where you work — lists and disclosure duties change.
Guests can be allergic to anything; the list is the legal minimum, not the limit.

## 2. Ask early, in the same words every time
At the greeting or before the order: "Does anyone at the table have any allergies
or dietary requirements I should know about?" Asking after the order means re-doing it.

## 3. Get the detail
- **Allergy, intolerance or preference?** All get respect; the kitchen needs to know
  which, because a true allergy needs cross-contact control, not just leaving off
  the garnish. Don't argue — if in doubt, treat it as an allergy.
- **How severe?** Trace amounts? Shared fryer OK? Airborne flour?
- **Religious/ethical** (halal, kosher, vegan, Jain): ask what it means *to them* —
  e.g. a vegetarian who eats fish, or whether shared cooking equipment is a problem.

## 4. Check, don't guess
Use the venue's **allergen matrix** for the current menu (it must match today's
recipes and suppliers). Hidden sources servers miss: sauces, stocks, dressings,
marinades, bread brushed with egg, frying oil shared with battered fish or prawns,
garnishes (nuts, sesame), desserts with nut flour, Worcestershire (fish), pesto (nuts,
dairy), soy sauce (wheat). If the matrix doesn't cover it — **ask the chef in person**.
"I think it's fine" is never an answer to a guest.

## 5. Ticket, chef, and the plate's journey
1. Flag the order in the POS with the allergen modifier **and** a clear note
   ("SEAT 3 — SEVERE PEANUT").
2. **Tell the chef verbally** as well; POS flags get lost in a rush.
3. The kitchen prepares with clean board, utensils, pan, gloves, fresh oil if needed.
4. At the pass, the allergen plate is **carried separately** (not on a tray with
   other plates), ideally by the person who took the order or the chef, with a
   marker (flag pick, coloured plate) per house practice.
5. At the table, confirm: "This is your peanut-free risotto."
6. Any change or substitution mid-meal goes back through the same chain.

## 6. If you can't do it safely, say so
Shared fryers, nut-based kitchens, flour in the air — if the kitchen can't guarantee
it, tell the guest plainly and offer what's genuinely safe. A guest can make an
informed choice; they can't recover from a false assurance.

## 7. If someone reacts
Signs: hives, swelling of lips/tongue/throat, wheezing, vomiting, collapse.
- Call emergency services immediately; say "anaphylaxis".
- Help them use their own adrenaline auto-injector if they have one; follow the
  device's instructions and first-aid training.
- Lie them flat with legs raised (or sitting if breathing is hard); don't stand them up.
- Keep the dish and ticket; record what was served and by whom.
This is first-aid territory — get trained; this note is not a substitute.

## Gotchas
- Server memory instead of the matrix — recipes and suppliers change.
- Allergy flag only in the POS, not spoken to the chef.
- Allergen plate on a crowded tray with sauces splashing.
- Removing a garnish from a finished plate is not making it allergen-free.
- Staff meals and specials not on the matrix.

## Verify
- [ ] Allergy question asked before ordering, same wording every time.
- [ ] Allergy vs intolerance vs preference, and severity, established.
- [ ] Dish checked against the current matrix or the chef in person.
- [ ] POS flag + verbal to the chef; plate carried separately and confirmed at table.
- [ ] Staff know where the first-aid kit is and how to call for help.
