# Running a table through the service sequence

**Goal:** take a table from sat to paid with no guesswork at the pass, no
"who had the fish?", and a pace the guests set rather than one the kitchen
or your section forces on them.

House standards vary (fine dining serves from the left and clears from the
right; many casual venues just say "never reach across a guest"). Learn your
house's version first; the sequence below is the common skeleton.

## 1. The sequence
1. **Greet within ~1–2 minutes of seating** even if you're slammed: "I'll be
   right with you" buys five minutes; being ignored for three costs the table.
2. **Water and drinks.** Offer water (still/sparkling/tap per house policy),
   take a drinks order, mention specials and anything that has 86'd.
3. **Take the food order** once menus close or heads lift — closed menus are the
   classic signal. Ask about allergies *before* the order, not after (see
   allergen-and-dietary-service).
4. **Fire starters**, drop cutlery for the courses ordered (mark the table:
   steak knife, fish knife, soup spoon) *before* the food lands.
5. **Two-bite check-back** — about two minutes after a course lands, once
   people have tasted. Ask a specific question ("How's the steak cooked?")
   rather than "Everything OK?", which only gets "fine".
6. **Clear when the table is finished**, not plate-by-plate while others still eat
   (casual US houses often clear as each guest finishes — know your house).
7. **Call away / fire mains** when the starter plates go, so the kitchen has the
   ~10–15 minutes it usually needs. Too early and mains crash into starters.
8. **Crumb and reset** before dessert; offer dessert and hot drinks.
9. **Bill on request** in fine dining; in casual, drop it with a "no rush". Process
   payment at the table where possible.
10. **Farewell** and a genuine invitation back.

## 2. Seat (position) numbers — the one habit that fixes everything
Pick a fixed **pivot point** for every table (e.g. the seat with its back to the
kitchen door is seat 1), then number clockwise. Write every order by seat number.
Any runner can then deliver without auctioning: food goes straight to the right person,
no "who had the…" across the table. Women-first or guest-of-honour-first service
orders are layered on top of seat numbers, not instead of them.

## 3. Carrying and clearing
- **Two-plate carry:** first plate between thumb and fingers, second underneath
  braced on the forearm/heel of the hand. **Three-plate** adds one on the forearm.
  Practise with empty plates; never carry more than you can set down silently.
- Hold plates by the rim, thumb flat on the rim, never on the food.
- Set the plate with the **protein toward the guest** (at "6 o'clock") unless the
  chef says otherwise.
- **Clearing:** scrape and stack away from the table, or at the side, never in
  front of guests. Cutlery collected under your thumb on the top plate.
- Hot plates: warn ("hot plate"), and never let a guest take it from you.

## 4. Reading the table
- Business meeting: minimal interruptions, fast check, bill on cue.
- Date: slower pacing, fewer check-ins.
- Families with small children: bread/kids' food fast, bill offered early.
- Cutlery placed together on the plate (positions vary by country) = finished.
- Watch for the scan of the room — that's someone needing you.

## Gotchas
- **Don't auctioneer** ("Who had the burger?"). That's what seat numbers prevent.
- **Mis-fired courses** come from forgetting to call away. Make it a reflex tied to
  clearing starters.
- Don't stack the next-course cutlery in your pocket — use a tray or side plate.
- Handling cash/cards then plates without washing hands is a food-safety fail.
- The check-back is useless if you're gone before they answer.

## Verify
- [ ] Every table greeted within the house standard time.
- [ ] Every ticket has seat numbers from the house pivot point.
- [ ] Cutlery for the next course was down before the food arrived.
- [ ] Mains called away when starters cleared; no courses crashed together.
- [ ] Check-back was a specific question, two bites in.
- [ ] Nothing was cleared while another guest was still eating (unless house style).

*Uncertain/varies:* serve/clear sides and greeting times are house- and
country-specific; numbers above are typical, not universal standards.
