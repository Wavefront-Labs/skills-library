# Building and running a wedding-day run-of-show

**Goal:** the couple experiences their day without ever handling a logistics
question, and every vendor is in the right place at the right minute because
they all work from the same timeline.

## 1. Build the run-of-show (finalise ~2 weeks out)
One document, minute-by-minute, with **who** (role + name + mobile) and **where**
for each line. Columns: time · item · location · owner · notes.
Start from the fixed points and work outwards:
- Ceremony start (often fixed by the celebrant/venue).
- Sunset / golden hour for photos (look it up for the date and location).
- Meal service times the kitchen can actually deliver.
- Venue curfew and noise cut-off; vendor load-out deadlines.
Typical block lengths (adjust per couple and culture):
| Block | Allow |
|---|---|
| Hair & makeup | 45–60 min per person with the lead, plus buffer |
| Couple + family photos after ceremony | 30–45 min (shot list helps) |
| Cocktail hour / canapés | 60–90 min (covers photos and room flip) |
| Speeches | ~5 min each; cap the number |
| Each course plated | 20–30 min |
**Add buffers**: 10–15 minutes before every fixed point. Things run late at the start
of the day and the buffer is how you absorb it.

## 2. Vendor coordination
- Confirm each vendor 1–2 weeks out: arrival time, load-in point, parking, power
  needs, meal break, contact on the day, final balance paid.
- Send everyone **the same final run-of-show** (version-numbered).
- Pre-agree with the photographer the shot list and family group names (and who
  will marshal family — appoint a loud relative).
- DJ/band and MC get the announcement script with pronunciations of names.

## 3. Ceremony-to-reception transition
- If the same room is flipped (ceremony → dinner), the guests must be out for the
  flip — cocktail hour is your flip time. Pre-stage tables, linen and centrepieces
  so the flip is fast; agree a realistic flip time with the venue team.
- Signage and ushers direct guests; bar and canapés open the moment guests arrive.
- Move ceremony flowers to the reception if planned (assign who).

## 4. On the day
- Arrive before the first vendor. Walk the venue against the floor plan and the
  wet-weather plan.
- Emergency kit: sewing kit, safety pins, stain remover, tape, scissors, lighter,
  first-aid basics, phone charger, painkillers, tissues, spare copies of vows and
  run-of-show.
- Hold the rings and readings until handover to the right person.
- Be the single point of contact for every vendor and the venue.
- Feed the couple — they often forget; plate their meal to them first.

## 5. Recovering a slipping timeline
Late by 20 minutes? Decide early, don't hope:
- **Absorb** in buffers and cocktail hour.
- **Compress**: shorter group photo list, fewer speeches between courses, combine
  cake cutting with dessert.
- **Never move hard stops** (curfew, ceremony slot if the celebrant has another,
  sunset) — protect them.
- Tell vendors affected by the new times immediately.
Tell the couple only what they need to decide; absorb the rest quietly.

## 6. Couple management under pressure
Calm voice, two options not ten, no vendor drama in front of them. If family
conflict surfaces, a named family point person handles it with you.

## Gotchas
- No buffer before the ceremony — hair and makeup always run long.
- Run-of-show versions differing between vendors.
- Forgetting sunset time for photos.
- Flip time underestimated for a same-room ceremony.
- No wet-weather plan for an outdoor ceremony until it rains.

## Verify
- [ ] One version-numbered run-of-show sent to every vendor and the venue.
- [ ] Buffers before each fixed point; curfew and sunset marked.
- [ ] Every vendor confirmed with arrival, load-in, contact and payment status.
- [ ] Flip plan and wet-weather plan agreed with the venue.
- [ ] Emergency kit packed; rings/vows custody clear.
- [ ] Any timeline slip handled by compressing, not moving hard stops.

*Uncertain:* block durations are common planning estimates; traditions and venues
vary widely.
