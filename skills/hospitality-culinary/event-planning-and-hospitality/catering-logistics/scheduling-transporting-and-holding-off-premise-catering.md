# Scheduling, transporting and holding off-premise catering

**Goal:** food cooked in one place arrives at another hours later, safe and
still good to eat, with every plate, power lead and ladle you need — because
there is no walk-in or dry store to run back to.

## 1. Quantity planning
- Start from **confirmed numbers** + a small over-production margin (often
  ~5%), not hopeful numbers.
- Plated: one portion per guest + a few spares per course.
- Buffet: guests take more; plan by weight per head (**varies by menu and audience; use
  your own history**).
- Canapés: set pieces per guest from your own event history.
- Convert portions → recipe yields → ingredient orders; include trim loss.

## 2. Back-timed production schedule
Work backwards from service time:
- Service 19:00 → plate-up from 18:45 → regenerate/finish on site from 17:30 →
  arrive and set up 16:00 → depart base 15:00 → load 14:30 → final cooking/chilling
  done 14:00 → day-before prep (stocks, sauces, cook-chill items, desserts).
- Assign every task to a person and a day. Cook-chill items need cooling time in
  the schedule.
- Build in transport delay and site access time (loading docks, security, lifts).

## 3. Pack and load lists
- A written **pack list** by category: food (by course, labelled container count),
  equipment (ovens, burners, chafers, fuel, gas bottles), smallwares, service ware,
  linen, cleaning, first aid, fire blanket, waste bags, hand-wash station.
- Tick off when loaded; a second person checks. Load in reverse order of need
  (last-needed goes in first).

## 4. Temperature control in transit
Common food-safety rules (check your local code):
- Keep hot food **at or above 63 °C** (UK: gov.uk says hot food kept below 63 °C is limited to 2 hours) / **57 °C (135 °F)** (US
  FDA Food Code) (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked). Chilled food must be kept at **8 °C or below** (a legal requirement in England, Wales and Northern Ireland, a recommendation in Scotland), with 5 °C or below recommended for fridges; chilled food may be out of the fridge for up to 4 hours (gov.uk).
- Hot: pre-heat insulated carriers (hot water or a heated pan inside) and fill them
  full — air space cools food. Cold: pre-chill carriers, ice packs, refrigerated van
  if journey is long.
- Probe and record temperatures at departure, arrival, during service.
- Many codes allow food out of temperature control for limited time windows under documented procedures (UK: 2 hours for hot food, 4 hours for chilled food) — know yours exactly before
  relying on it.

## 5. Field kitchen setup
- Site visit beforehand: kitchen space or marquee, power (amps per circuit;
  generator?), water (potable supply, hand-wash), drainage, waste, gas rules,
  ventilation, access route for trolleys.
- Lay out: receiving/holding → cooking/regen → plating line → pass → service;
  dirty return separated from clean food flow.
- Hand-wash station with soap and paper towels set up first.
- Fire safety: extinguishers, gas bottle positions per venue rules.

## 6. Service and breakdown
- Plate in waves; keep backups covered and in holding, not on the line.
- Buffets: replenish with fresh trays rather than topping up old ones.
- Leftovers: discard or chill rapidly per your HACCP plan — never take hot-held
  food back for re-serving.
- Pack-out against the same list; count loss/breakage.

## Gotchas
- Half-full hot boxes — the food drops below safe temperature.
- No hand-wash station on site.
- Power: two induction hobs plus a coffee machine on one domestic circuit trips it.
- Forgetting the ladle (or lighters for the chafers) — the pack list exists for that.
- Underestimating site access: a 400 m trolley push through a garden.

## Verify
- [ ] Quantities derived from confirmed numbers + margin and your past data.
- [ ] Back-timed schedule with names and transport/access time.
- [ ] Pack list checked by two people at load and pack-out.
- [ ] Temperatures probed and logged at departure, arrival and service.
- [ ] Site power, water, hand-wash, waste and fire safety confirmed beforehand.
- [ ] Leftovers handled per HACCP plan.

*Uncertain:* temperature thresholds and time rules differ by country; quantities are
rules of thumb — confirm with local food-safety law and your own records.
