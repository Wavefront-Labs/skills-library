# Rostering and briefing a casual event team

**Goal:** the right number of people, in the right place, knowing the plan —
even when half of them have never worked this venue before and you have ten
minutes to brief them.

## 1. Headcount from service style
Starting ratios (calibrate to your venue and standard):
| Service | Servers | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Plated dinner, standard | ~1 per 20 guests (2 tables of 10) | Fine dining: ~1 per 10 |
| Buffet | ~1 per 30–40 guests | Plus runners for replenishment |
| Cocktail / canapés | ~1 per 25–30 guests | More if food is heavy |
| Bar | ~1 bartender per 75–100 guests | Fewer with simple package |
Add: team leaders/captains (~1 per 5–8 staff), runners, glass collectors, cloakroom,
kitchen porters. **These are commonly cited rules of thumb, not standards.**

## 2. Rostering
- **Call times** staggered: setup crew early; service staff ~60–90 min before
  doors for briefing and final set; bar before doors.
- Rest breaks as required by employment law (often a meal break after a fixed
  number of hours) — schedule them between courses or during speeches.
- Release order planned: cut staff as service winds down to control labour cost,
  keeping enough for breakdown.
- Confirm casuals 24–48 hours out; keep a standby list for no-shows (expect some).
- Check required certifications: responsible-service-of-alcohol (RSA/TIPS or local
  equivalent), food handler, right-to-work, ID.

## 3. Station assignments
- A **station map** on one page: tables/sections by server, bar positions, buffet
  stations, door/cloak, runners' routes.
- Pair new casuals with experienced staff in adjacent sections.
- Put strong servers on the head table and VIP tables.
- Name one person responsible for each recurring task (water top-ups, bread, clearing
  the buffet, glass collection).

## 4. The pre-event briefing (10 minutes, standing)
Use the same order every time:
1. **The event:** who the client is, occasion, guest count, VIPs, dress code.
2. **Timeline:** doors, seating, each course, speeches (no service during them),
   end time.
3. **Menu:** each course and dietary meals — which tables/seats, how they're marked.
4. **Beverage:** package rules, what's included, last call, refusal policy for
   intoxicated guests.
5. **Stations:** read the map, confirm everyone knows their section.
6. **Signals:** how the captain cues a course (hand signal, radio, nod).
7. **Safety:** fire exits, assembly point, first aid, who to call.
8. Questions — then everyone repeats their station back to their team leader.

## 5. Floor supervision during the function
- Stand where you can see the whole room; walk it between cues.
- Watch for lagging sections and redeploy — move a runner or a server from a
  finished section.
- Cue courses together so tables are served in waves, head table first.
- Watch alcohol: identify intoxication early and act under the refusal policy.
- Keep breaks happening; tired casuals make mistakes.

## 6. After
Sign-out with times (for payroll), quick debrief with team leaders, note which
casuals to rebook or not.

## Gotchas
- Ratios for "standard" applied to a fine-dining client brief.
- No standby for no-shows on a big night.
- Briefing that skips dietary seat mapping.
- Breaks forgotten until someone walks off.
- Staff without RSA serving alcohol.

## Verify
- [ ] Headcount derived from service style and guest count, with leaders.
- [ ] Call times, breaks and release order on the roster.
- [ ] Certifications checked for alcohol and food handling.
- [ ] Station map printed; new staff paired with experienced ones.
- [ ] Briefing covered event, timeline, menu/dietary, beverage, stations, signals, safety.
- [ ] Sign-out times recorded.

*Uncertain:* ratios and break rules vary with venue, service standard and local
employment law.
