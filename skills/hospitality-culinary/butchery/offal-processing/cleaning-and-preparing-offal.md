# Cleaning and preparing offal — the fifth quarter, kitchen-ready

**Goal:** turn organ meats and extremities into clean, mild, well-textured raw material,
and use it before its short shelf life runs out.

## Handling rules that apply to all offal
- **Shelf life is short:** plan on 1–3 days chilled (0–3 °C) for liver, kidney,
  sweetbreads, blood; slightly longer for tongue, heart, tails. Freeze on day of
  receipt if not used. Smell on opening — any sour or ammonia note means discard.
- Offal is high in moisture and enzymes and spoils from the surface; keep it in
  shallow covered trays, not stacked deep.
- Some jurisdictions restrict certain offal (e.g. specified risk material such as
  bovine brain and spinal cord in BSE-control regimes). Buy only from approved sources
  and confirm what can legally be sold or served locally.

## By item
**Liver.** Peel the thin outer membrane (lift a corner, pull with a damp cloth). Cut
out the large veins and ducts. Slice 1–1.5 cm for searing. Soaking in milk for an hour or
two softens strong flavour (pig and ox liver especially); calf and lamb rarely need it.
Cook pink for lamb/calf, but chicken liver parfait must reach a safe core (commonly
~70 °C for 2 min equivalent — *Campylobacter* outbreaks are linked to pink chicken liver).

**Kidney.** Remove suet (save it for pastry), then the membrane. Halve lengthwise and
snip out the white core and tubes with scissors. Lamb kidneys stay whole or halved;
ox kidney is diced for pies. A soak in salted water or a quick blanch reduces the
urinary note in pig kidney.

**Heart.** Trim fat, valves, and stringy tendons inside the chambers; cut out the
blood vessels at the top. Lean and dense: sear fast and rare in thin slices, or braise long.

**Tongue.** Soak, then simmer 2–3 h (ox) or ~1 h (lamb) until tender; **peel the skin
while hot** — cold, it sticks. Trim the gristle and bone at the root.

**Sweetbreads** (thymus, pancreas). Soak in cold water several hours, changing it,
to draw out blood; blanch 2–3 min in acidulated water, shock, peel off membranes and
fat, then press between trays overnight for an even texture.

**Tripe.** Usually bought "dressed" (scalded, bleached or unbleached). Rinse thoroughly;
scrape off any fat; simmer 2–3 h before using in a dish. Honeycomb (reticulum) is most
prized.

**Trotters, tails, cheeks.** Singe or shave hairs off trotters; split trotters
lengthwise with a saw. Trim tail fat; cut at the joints by feel. Cheeks: remove the
outer silver membrane before braising.

**Marrow bones.** Ask for canoe-cut (lengthwise) or 5–8 cm cross-cut femurs; soak in
salted ice water 12–24 h, changing water, to whiten. Roast cut-side up ~15–20 min at 230 °C.

**Blood.** Collect only from an approved slaughter source; stir in an anticoagulant
(commonly salt plus a little vinegar, or sodium citrate, per supplier) and use within
a day or freeze. Strain before use.

## Gotchas
- Blanching is a texture/cleaning step, not a cook step — still cook to a safe end temperature.
- Membranes left on liver and kidney shrink on heat and curl the slice.
- Mixing offal with other trim in a sausage changes the shelf life of the whole batch to the offal's.

## Verify
- [ ] Received cold, from an approved source; smell checked on opening.
- [ ] Membranes, ducts, valves and gristle removed; colour clean.
- [ ] Labelled with receipt date and use-by; used or frozen within 1–3 days.
- [ ] Poultry liver dishes reach a validated safe core temperature.
