# Jointing and boning birds — clean pieces, intact skin, contained pathogens

**Goal:** break poultry and small game into kitchen-ready cuts with skin intact and
nothing left on the frame, quickly enough to keep the meat cold. For on-farm
killing and plucking see agriculture-environment.livestock-farming.on-farm-slaughter.small-flock-poultry-processing.

## Hygiene first (poultry is the high-risk species)
- Dedicated colour-coded board (commonly yellow for raw poultry in UK/AU codes),
  knife and tubs. Never cut poultry then red meat or ready-to-eat food on the same
  surface without wash–rinse–sanitise.
- Don't rinse whole birds under the tap: splash spreads *Campylobacter* and
  *Salmonella* across the sink area; cooking kills them, washing doesn't.
- Keep birds ≤ 5 °C; work two or three at a time from the fridge, not the case.

## Eight-piece joint (chicken)
1. Breast-side up. Pull a leg away, cut the skin between leg and breast, pop the
   thigh joint by bending the leg back, then cut through the joint, **scooping the
   "oyster"** (the nugget on the back) off with the thigh.
2. Find the knee by flexing; the joint sits just toward the drumstick from the fat
   line. Cut through the gap — if you hit bone you're off the joint.
3. Remove the wishbone first (scrape and lift it out) so breasts come off cleaner.
4. Cut along one side of the keel bone, keep the knife on the ribcage and peel the
   breast off with the wing attached; take the wing at the shoulder joint, or leave
   the first wing joint on and French it for an **airline/supreme**.
5. Ten-piece: cut each breast in half crosswise. Carcass → stock.

## Spatchcock
Bird breast-down, cut along both sides of the backbone with poultry shears,
remove it, nick the cartilage at the top of the keel, flip and press flat. Tuck
wing tips. Optional: two skewers crosswise through thigh and breast to hold flat.

## Glove (tunnel) boning for ballotine
Bone the bird without opening the skin: cut out the wishbone, work around the
shoulder joints from the neck cavity, then peel the meat back off the carcass like
removing a glove, keeping the blade on the bone, especially over the keel where
skin is thinnest. Leg and wing bones can stay in or be pulled through.
The other way is a back-cut bone-out (split along the spine, bone out flat), which is
easier and used for galantine/roulade; you then roll and tie or wrap in film.
**Gotcha:** a single tear over the breastbone lets stuffing leak; patch with a
sliver of skin or put the tear on the underside.

## Game
- **Feathered** (pheasant, partridge, grouse): pluck against the feather direction
  holding skin taut; or skin and take breasts/legs only when shot damage is heavy.
  Hang 2–7 days at ~4–8 °C by preference (uncertain; tradition varies widely and
  some hygiene codes restrict it). Feel for and remove shot and feather tracks: a
  bruised, dark track is cut out.
- **Rabbit/hare:** skin from the hind legs down; remove the silvery membrane
  over the saddle; joint into back legs, saddle (2–3 pieces), forelegs.
- **Venison:** seam-bone the haunch like beef (see the primal-breakdown skill);
  remove all fat and silverskin, which taste tallowy.

## Verify
- [ ] Frame scraped clean — little meat left on keel or back.
- [ ] Skin intact over each piece; ballotine skin has no breastbone tear.
- [ ] Pieces matched in size for even cooking (thighs vs breasts).
- [ ] Game is shot-free; checked by feel and with a light.
- [ ] Poultry station sanitised before anything else used it.
