# Seam butchery and yield tracking — break primals along the muscle, cost every kilo

**Goal:** take a primal (beef chuck, pork leg, lamb shoulder) down to clean, saleable
subprimals by separating muscles along their natural membranes rather than sawing
across them, and record a cutting test so you know what each finished kilo really cost.

## Set-up before the knife touches meat
- **Cold:** meat core ≤ 5 °C (≤ 7 °C is the common legal ceiling for red meat in
  many jurisdictions — check yours). Work in batches so nothing sits on the bench
  more than ~20–30 min. Cold fat cuts cleanly; warm fat smears and hides the seams.
- **Kit:** a 6" semi-flex boning knife for seaming, a stiff boner for bone work, a
  scimitar or breaking knife for long cuts, a steel, a hook, a mesh (chain-mail)
  glove on the non-knife hand, mesh apron for boning work. Separate tubs for
  primal cuts, trim (by lean %), fat, bone and waste.
- **Weigh the primal first** and write it down. No starting weight = no yield test.

## Seam-cutting technique
1. **Find the seam with your fingers before the knife.** Muscles are wrapped in
   fascia; push a thumb into the soft line between two muscles and they will part.
2. **Use the knife as little as possible:** short strokes with the tip, blade angled
   toward the muscle you are *not* keeping, letting the hook or your hand pull the
   muscle away under tension. Tension does the work; the knife only releases
   connective tissue.
3. **Follow bone with the blade flat against it** — "keep the knife on the bone" —
   so meat stays on the cut, not on the carcass. Scrape, don't saw.
4. **Lift whole muscles, then trim.** Seam out the full muscle (e.g. flat iron /
   oyster blade from the chuck, eye of round, pork leg cushion) before deciding
   what it becomes. Trimming as you go wastes more.
5. **Denude last:** remove silverskin with the blade angled slightly up, pulling the
   skin taut — see portion-cutting skills for denuding detail.

## Gotchas
- **Scoring a muscle while seaming** turns a premium steak into stew trim. Slow down
  at the transition points (where a muscle wraps another).
- **Fat seams carry gland tissue** (e.g. prescapular / popliteal lymph nodes in
  shoulder and leg). Remove them; they're unpleasant and a classic customer complaint.
- **Bone dust and saw cuts:** bandsaw only for cross-cuts through bone (chops,
  shanks). Clean the bandsaw guard and wheel; bone dust is a spoilage source.
- **Don't let weights "drift":** weigh each tub at the end, before anything goes to
  display or the kitchen.
- **Cutting conventions vary** — US (NAMP/IMPS numbers), UK, French, Australian
  (AUS-MEAT HAM numbers) name and split primals differently. Agree the spec with
  the customer/chef before cutting.

## The cutting (yield) test

| Primal as purchased | 18.40 | 100 | cost price | — |

- Usable yield % = sum of saleable weights ÷ start weight.
- **True cost per kg of a cut** = (purchase cost − value of by-products like trim,
  bone sold for stock) ÷ kg of that cut. Rough rule: cost per kg of a steak is
  often 1.5–2.5× the primal's price per kg — verify with your own test.
- Untracked loss over ~2–3% of start weight usually means a scale error, purge not
  counted or meat walking out the door.
- Repeat the test for every new supplier, grade or primal spec, and when staff change.

## Verify
- [ ] Start weight recorded; all outputs weighed; sum within ~2% of start.
- [ ] Each saleable muscle came off whole, unscored, with glands removed.
- [ ] Meat stayed ≤ 5 °C core; benches and tools sanitised between species.
- [ ] Trim tubs are labelled by lean % and dated.
- [ ] You can state the cost per kg of each subprimal from today's test.
