# Filleting round and flat fish — and getting shellfish ready safely

**Goal:** maximum clean fillet off the frame, no bones in the portion, and fish and
shellfish kept in the safe zone from delivery to pan.

## Check what you received
- **Fresh fish:** clear, convex eyes; bright red gills (brown = older); firm flesh that
  springs back; sea smell, no ammonia; slime clear not milky. Store on ice at
  0–2 °C, belly down, ice not touching cut flesh directly (melt-water bleaches it).
- **Live bivalves** (oysters, mussels, clams) must be closed or close when tapped;
  discard open, dead or cracked ones. Keep at ~2–5 °C under a damp cloth, never in
  fresh water or sealed airtight. Keep shellfish tags (many jurisdictions require
  90 days — confirm locally).
- Tuna, mackerel and other scombroids: break the cold chain and histamine forms;
  cooking won't remove it.

## Scale and gut
Scale tail-to-head under water or in a bag to contain scales (back of a knife or a
scaler). Snip fins. Gut through the vent to the throat, remove viscera, scrape out the
bloodline along the spine with a spoon — it turns bitter. Rinse cavity briefly, dry.

## Round fish — two fillets
1. Cut behind the gill plate and pectoral fin down to the backbone, angled toward the head.
2. Turn the knife flat along the backbone toward the tail and, in one long smooth
   stroke (or several along the top of the spine), lift the fillet, **letting the
   blade ride on the bones**. Feel the blade tick over the ribs.
3. Flip and repeat. For large fish (salmon) go along the back first, then the belly.
4. Trim belly fat and the thin belly flap edge; remove rib bones by sliding the
   blade under them.

## Flat fish — four fillets
Dark side up. Cut down the centre line (lateral line) to the bone, around the head
and along the fin frills. Work one fillet from the centre outward with the knife
flat on the bones, then the other; flip and repeat. Small flat fish (sole) can be
skinned whole first — Dover sole skin peels from the tail after a nick and good grip with salt.

## Pin-bone, skin, portion
- **Pin bones:** run a finger head-to-tail to raise them, pull with fish tweezers
  **toward the head** (with the angle of the bone) to avoid tearing flesh.
- **Skinning:** fillet skin-down, tail toward you, cut down to the skin near the tail,
  hold the tab and angle the blade slightly down, then wiggle the *skin* side to side
  while the knife holds still.
- Portion to weight like any protein (thicker cut at the tail end).

## Shucking an oyster
Cup down in a folded towel, hinge toward you. Insert the shucking knife into the
hinge, twist to pop; sweep the blade across the top shell to cut the adductor muscle,
remove top shell, then cut the muscle under the meat. Keep the liquor; check for grit.
**Never shuck toward your palm without the towel** — it's the most common injury.

## Parasites
Fish to be served raw or lightly cured (sashimi, ceviche, gravlax) generally needs a
freeze step (e.g. US FDA guidance of −20 °C for 7 days, or −35 °C for 15 h) unless your
code exempts the species/source — confirm locally. Candle fillets of cod family over a
light box to spot worms.

## Verify
- [ ] Frame shows little flesh left; fillets have clean, unragged edges.
- [ ] Pass a finger along each fillet: no pin bones remain.
- [ ] Fish held on ice at 0–2 °C until use; scombroids never temperature-abused.
- [ ] Dead or gaping bivalves discarded; shellfish tags kept.
- [ ] Raw-service fish has a documented freeze step or approved exemption.
