# Sausage making and dry curing — texture from cold, safety from salt, nitrite, pH and water

**Goal:** sausages that bind and don't smear, and dry-cured products that are safe
because every hurdle (salt, nitrite, acidity, low water activity) was measured — not
because "it smelled fine".

## Fresh sausage: cold is the whole game
- Meat and fat partially frozen (−2 to 0 °C) before grinding; grinder head, plates
  and bowl chilled. Fat that warms above ~4–5 °C smears, coats the lean and gives a
  crumbly, greasy sausage.
- Typical ratio 70–75% lean : 25–30% fat; salt **1.5–2%** of total meat weight.
- Grind once through a coarse plate, season, then **mix until a sticky "primary
  bind" forms** (strands of protein pull when you lift a handful) — about 1–2 min by
  paddle with a splash of ice water. Under-mixed = crumbly; over-mixed warm = rubbery.
- **Fry a test patty** before casing; adjust seasoning.
- Stuff without air pockets, twist links alternately, prick only visible air.

## Dry curing: dose by weight, always
Weigh the meat to the gram and calculate everything as a % of that weight
(**equilibrium curing**) — it can't over-salt and is reproducible.

| Ingredient | Whole muscle (coppa, lonza, bresaola) | Fermented salami |
|---|---|---|
| Salt | 2.5–3% | 2.5–3% |
| Curing salt | Cure #2 at 0.25% | Cure #2 at 0.25% |
| Sugar (dextrose) | 0–1% | 0.3–0.6% (food for culture) |
| Starter culture | — | per maker's dose (e.g. T-SPX, F-RM-52) |

Cure #1 / #2 here means the US-style 6.25% sodium-nitrite pink salts (#2 also carries
nitrate for long cures). Other countries sell different strengths. **Check the strength on your bag** and the
legal ingoing limits in your jurisdiction; nitrite is toxic in excess. In the US, 9 CFR 424.21 allows
1/4 oz sodium nitrite per 100 lb of chopped product and caps nitrite at 200 ppm in the finished product.

## Process for salami
1. Grind cold, mix in salt, cure, spices, dissolved culture; bind as above.
2. Stuff firmly into fibrous or natural casings; prick; weigh and **tag each piece with
   its green weight**.
3. **Ferment** at the culture's temperature (often 20–24 °C, ~90% RH) for 24–72 h
   until **pH ≤ 5.3** (measure with a calibrated meter on a sacrificial piece; strips
   are unreliable). This acid drop is the key early safety barrier.
4. **Dry** at ~12–15 °C, 70–80% RH, gentle air movement, until **30–35% weight loss**
   (whole muscles similar). Water activity below ~0.90 is the commonly cited
   shelf-stable target (uncertain; depends on code and pH).
5. Optional: spray *Penicillium nalgiovense* for a protective white mould.

## Gotchas
- **Case hardening:** humidity too low early → the outside seals, the core stays wet
  and spoils. Fix by raising RH; vacuum-pack for a few days to equalise.
- **Mould:** white powdery = fine; fuzzy green/blue can be wiped with vinegar on
  whole muscles; black, yellow-orange or hairy mould = discard.
- **Never skip nitrite in long dry cures** — *C. botulinum* thrives in the anaerobic core.
- Commercial production needs a written **HACCP plan** and often a regulator-approved
  variance for fermented/dried meats; a home method is not a business licence.

## Verify
- [ ] Every ingredient weighed against the meat weight; curing salt type confirmed.
- [ ] Fresh mix showed a sticky bind; test patty tasted before stuffing.
- [ ] Salami reached pH ≤ 5.3 within the fermentation window (logged).
- [ ] Each piece reached target weight loss; no soft core when sliced.
- [ ] Chamber temperature and RH logged daily.
