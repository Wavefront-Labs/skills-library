# Kosher and halal meat preparation — the butcher's role under religious supervision

**Goal:** understand where the butcher's work sits inside kosher and halal systems,
do the cutting steps correctly, and never break the chain of supervision that makes
the product acceptable to observant customers. **The religious authority, not the
butcher, decides what is permitted.** This skill describes common practice; rulings
differ between authorities and communities, so follow your certifier's written
standard. For the home-kitchen side see religion-philosophy.jewish-practice.kashrut-practice.setting-up-and-keeping-a-kosher-kitchen.

## Kosher: steps after shechita
Slaughter (*shechita*) is done by a trained, licensed *shochet*; the lungs and organs
are inspected (*bedikah*). "Glatt" means the lungs were found free of adhesions — a
stricter grade many customers expect. Then:

1. **Porging (*nikkur*)** — removal of forbidden fat (*chelev*), certain blood vessels
   and, in the hindquarter, the sciatic nerve (*gid hanasheh*) and its branches. Hindquarter
   porging is so laborious and specialised that most communities outside Israel simply
   **don't sell hindquarter cuts as kosher** (sirloin, rump, round go to the non-kosher
   trade). Forequarter porging is done by a trained *menaker* under supervision; a
   general butcher should not attempt it unsupervised.
2. **Kashering (removing blood)** — the meat must be soaked, salted and rinsed within
   about **72 hours** of slaughter, or be washed (*shriyah*) before 72 h to extend the window
   (practice varies; confirm with your authority). Common sequence:
   - **Soak** in cool water about 30 minutes, fully submerged.
   - Drain, then **salt** all surfaces with coarse (kosher) salt, on an inclined or
     perforated surface so blood drains away; leave about **1 hour**.
   - **Rinse** thoroughly, commonly three times, to remove salt and blood.
   Liver cannot be kashered by salting; it is broiled over an open flame.
3. **Supervision and sealing** — a *mashgiach* oversees the process; product is tagged
   or sealed (*plomba*) and labelled with the certifying agency's mark (*hechsher*).

**Gotchas:** ground meat must be made from already-kashered meat; once meat is frozen
before kashering, salting is no longer accepted by most authorities (broiling only);
equipment used on non-kosher meat cannot be shared without the certifier's kashering procedure.

## Halal: what the butcher controls
- Slaughter (*dhabiha/zabiha*) by a Muslim slaughterman, invoking God's name
  (*tasmiyah*), cutting the throat to allow full bleeding. Whether pre-slaughter
  stunning is acceptable differs between certifiers — know your certifier's position;
  it determines which suppliers you can use.
- After slaughter, cutting is ordinary butchery; what matters is **segregation**:
  dedicated or fully cleaned equipment, separate storage above non-halal product,
  no pork or pork derivatives (gelatine, casings, lard) anywhere in the product,
  and alcohol-free marinades where the certifier requires.
- **Traceability:** keep certificates for each delivery; labelled halal product must
  match a certified source and an unbroken chain. Mislabelling is fraud in many jurisdictions.

## Shared gotchas
- Never relabel product "kosher-style" or "halal" to sell it. Use only the terms your certification allows.
- Animal-welfare law on unstunned slaughter differs by country (some ban it,
  some grant religious exemptions). Know the law that applies to your supply.
