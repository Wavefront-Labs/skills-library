# Special assistance, minors and de-escalation

**Goal:** passengers who need help get it with dignity; unaccompanied children are never
unsupervised or handed to the wrong adult; and tension in the cabin is defused before it
becomes a safety issue. Written for aircraft cabins; the principles carry to rail and sea.
**Follow your operator's manual for codes, forms and restraint authority.**

## 1. Passengers with reduced mobility (PRM) and disabilities
- Read the **SSR codes** on the passenger list: `WCHR` (can do steps and walk to seat, needs
  help over distance), `WCHS` (can't do steps, can walk in cabin), `WCHC` (immobile, needs
  carrying/aisle chair), `BLND`, `DEAF`, `DPNA` (intellectual or developmental disability needing help), `MEDA` (medical case).
- **Ask, don't assume:** "How can I help you best?" Speak to the passenger, not their companion.
- Pre-boarding as policy allows. Brief them individually: exits, belt, call button, toilet
  access (onboard wheelchair availability), and how you'll help in an evacuation.
- Blind passengers: offer your arm, describe the route ("three rows on the left"), tactile
  brief of seat/belt/call button, describe the meal tray like a clock face.
- Deaf passengers: face them, speak clearly, written/visual briefings, make sure they don't
  miss PA announcements.
- Mobility aids: know stowage rules; battery types for powered wheelchairs. Exit-row seats
  aren't allowed for most PRMs (regulatory).
- Rights frameworks (e.g. EU Regulation 1107/2006, US Air Carrier Access Act) mean refusing
  assistance has legal weight — escalate doubts to the senior.

## 2. Unaccompanied minors (UMNR)
- Receive from ground staff with the **UM envelope/documents**: child's details, the
  person collecting at arrival (name, ID, contact).
- Seat near crew per policy, introduce yourself and the toilets, check in regularly,
  meals and comfort, don't let them leave the aircraft with anyone else.
- At arrival: handover only to ground staff, who release to the **named adult after ID
  check**; sign the documents. Note the child's condition and anything notable.
- Disruption (diversion, delay overnight): the airline keeps custody — never release to a
  friendly passenger.

## 3. De-escalation
Many manuals use **levels** (e.g. ICAO guidance: level 1 verbal disturbance, 2 physically
abusive, 3 life-threatening, 4 flight-deck breach). Respond by level:
1. **Early, calm, private:** go to the passenger, crouch to their eye level, use their name,
   listen, acknowledge ("I can see this has been frustrating"), offer what you can.
2. **Clear expectation:** state the specific behaviour that must stop and why (safety, other
   passengers, law). Avoid arguing, sarcasm, touching or public embarrassment.
3. **Options and consequence:** seat change, water/food, cut off alcohol (common trigger —
   stop serving early, inform colleagues), then a formal warning card where used.
4. **Team and captain:** inform senior and captain early; a witness should be present;
   log times and words.
5. **Restraint** only as the manual permits, on the captain's authority (the Tokyo
   Convention gives the commander authority to restrain), with trained technique and
   restraint kit, and continuous monitoring of the restrained person's breathing.
6. Captain decides on police at arrival or diversion.

## 4. Comfort and airsickness care
- Airsickness: seat over the wing or forward if possible, fresh air vent on, focus on the
  horizon/eyes closed, cool cloth, sips of water, ginger/crackers; bags ready. Clean
  spills with the body-fluid kit (gloves, absorbent, disinfectant).
- Anxious flyers: explain noises and turbulence plainly, check on them after take-off,
  breathing exercise (in 4, hold 4, out 6).
- Fainting: lay flat, legs raised, oxygen per manual, tell the senior.

## Gotchas
- Never lift a passenger by yourself; use aisle chair and ground staff.
- Don't pass a UM's documents to the child.
- Alcohol plus delays is the most common route to disruption — spot it early.

## Verify
- [ ] Every SSR-coded passenger briefed individually.
- [ ] UMs supervised throughout; handed over only via documented ID check.
- [ ] Disruptive behaviour handled at the lowest workable level and logged.
- [ ] Captain informed early of any escalation; restraint only per manual.
- [ ] Ill or anxious passengers checked on during the flight.
