# At-seat, dining-car and station-stop service

**Goal:** deliver hotel-style service in a moving, narrow, time-boxed space — planned
around the timetable, safe in the aisle, and coordinated with the guard/conductor at
every stop. **Operator procedures and the rule book for your railway govern safety
duties; who does what (host vs guard/train manager) differs by operator.**

## 1. Plan around the timetable
- Before departure: read the **diagram** (your shift), the train formation (which coach is
  which, where first class and the buffet/kitchen are), expected loadings and reservations
  (sleeper berths, dining-car sittings, assisted travellers).
- Mark the stops and **longer non-stop sections** on your sheet. Trolley rounds and
  dining sittings fit the non-stop sections; aisles are busy and doors unsafe around stops.
- Check stock against the load list; report shortfalls before departure; check chiller and
  oven/boiler working; food-safety temperatures logged where required.

## 2. At-seat trolley service
- Work one direction through the train, announcing yourself ("Trolley service coming
  through, hot drinks and snacks"); brake the trolley every time you stop.
- Pull **back from the vestibule/doors** during station approach and stops — passengers
  board and alight with luggage; don't block.
- Hot drinks: lids on, hand over not across; never pour over a passenger on curves.
- Card payment terminals depend on mobile signal — know the dead zones (tunnels, rural
  stretches) and have the offline mode/fallback procedure ready.
- First-class complimentary service: track who's been served by seat, like a cabin crew seat map.

## 3. Dining-car service
- **Sittings** timed to the route (e.g. first sitting after departure, second after the
  major stop) so passengers aren't mid-meal when they need to alight; ask each table their
  destination when seating them.
- Plates and glasses with lips or anti-slip mats; carry less than you would in a restaurant;
  keep one hand free on curves and at points; pour wine at the table only on straight track.
- Serve courses fast enough that the last course is done 10–15 min before a guest's stop.

## 4. Sleeper hosting
- Welcome at the berth: explain locks, lights, washbasin/shower, call button, wake-up time,
  breakfast order, where the emergency equipment and exits are.
- Take wake-up/breakfast orders and note arrival times — night trains can run early or late;
  update guests.
- Night rounds per operator policy; know which passengers have assistance needs for the
  emergency procedure.
- Secure the coach doors and keep corridors clear of luggage.

## 5. Tickets and courtesy
On-board ticket checks (if your role includes them): greet, check, explain rather than
accuse when a ticket is wrong, follow the penalty-fare or excess procedure exactly and
calmly. Escalate abusive situations to the train manager/BTP-equivalent rather than confront.

## 6. Station stops
- Know the stopping pattern; make announcements before arrival (station, side doors
  open, short platforms — which coaches won't fit).
- Booked **assistance:** the ramp and passenger handover at the right coach and station;
  confirm with the guard so the train isn't dispatched before the ramp is clear.
- Door safety: on many railways the guard/driver controls doors and dispatch — your job
  is to assist, look out, and never hold doors. Mind-the-gap warnings for elderly and
  luggage-heavy passengers.
- After departure from a busy stop, a quick walk-through to see new passengers and offer service.

## 7. Disruption and emergencies
Know the train's emergency equipment (fire extinguishers, emergency door releases, first-
aid kit, defibrillator if fitted), the passenger communication (alarm) procedure, and
the evacuation rule (usually *stay on the train unless told to leave*). During long
delays: water and information, regular updates every 10–15 min.

## Verify
- [ ] Diagram, formation and stopping pattern known; service plan fits non-stop sections.
- [ ] Trolley braked at every stop and clear of vestibules at stations.
- [ ] Dining guests finish before their stop.
- [ ] Sleeper guests briefed and wake-ups delivered.
- [ ] Assistance at stops coordinated with the guard; emergency equipment locations known.
