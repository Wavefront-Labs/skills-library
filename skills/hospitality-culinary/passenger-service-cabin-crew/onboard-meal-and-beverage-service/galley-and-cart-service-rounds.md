# Galley and cart service rounds

**Goal:** serve every passenger the right meal and drinks within the time the flight
allows, safely, with special meals and allergies handled first and the galley kept secure.
**Service flows, meal codes and equipment follow your airline's service manual; this is the common practice.**

## 1. Galley set-up on boarding
- Check the **catering load** against the load sheet: meal counts per cabin and per
  choice, special meals (SPMLs) by seat, bar stock, ice, hot water.
- Report shortages before doors close — catering can still deliver then, not after.
- Check ovens, water boilers, coffee makers, chillers working; circuit breakers in.
- Stow everything; latch every compartment; carts brakes on and locked in their stowage.

## 2. Plan the service against flight time
Work back from **top of descent** (cabin must be secure ~20–30 min before landing):
- Service start: once the seat-belt sign allows and the captain confirms, usually ~15–20 min after take-off.
- **Hot meals:** oven times by product (often 20–30 min for casseroles at medium/high —
  follow catering labels). Load ovens on the climb if your airline allows, so meals are
  ready when carts go out.
- Short sectors: combine meal + drink on one pass; long-haul: drinks round, meal round,
  tea/coffee round, clear-in, pre-landing service.

## 3. Special meals and allergies — first, always
- Hand out SPMLs **before** the cart reaches those rows. Match **seat number and name**,
  label code (e.g. VGML vegan, AVML Asian vegetarian, KSML kosher, MOML Muslim, GFML
  gluten-free, DBML diabetic, CHML child) and tell the passenger what it is.
- Kosher meals: seals intact; let the passenger break the seal.
- **Allergies:** airlines generally cannot guarantee an allergen-free cabin. Follow
  airline policy (e.g. not serving nuts in nearby rows, buffer-zone announcement), never
  promise "nut-free"; know where the epinephrine/medical kit is and how to report.

## 4. Cart service technique
- Two crew per cart, one each side, or one crew per half-cart on narrowbodies.
- Serve **window → middle → aisle**, front-to-back or back-to-front per airline; the
  cart's brake is set **every time it stops** (foot pedal).
- Never leave a cart unattended in the aisle; when you must step away, return it or
  have a colleague hold it.
- Hot drinks: pour in the cart, not over passengers; hand over with handle toward the
  passenger, warn "hot"; lids where policy requires.
- Keep a running count of choices to avoid running out of one option at row 10 — offer
  both options from the front and track tally; apologise early and offer alternatives.
- Collect trays/waste promptly; trash compacted per airline rules.

## 5. Premium cabins
- Personalised service: greet by name, take orders ahead (sometimes on the ground), and
  serve by course (amuse-bouche, starter, main, cheese/dessert) at the passenger's pace.
- Table laying, plating from the galley to a standard photo/specification, wine service
  (show the bottle, pour a taste where required).
- Dine-on-demand: track each passenger's course on a seat-map sheet.

## 6. Turbulence and galley safety
- When the captain signals turbulence: stop the service, **brake and secure carts** (into
  the galley if possible), hot liquids away, sit and strap in when instructed. Severe
  turbulence → sit in the nearest seat immediately.
- Galley: latches closed when not in use, no items on worktops during taxi/take-off/
  landing, oven doors latched, hot water off on descent.
- Burns are the main galley injury — use oven gloves, open ovens away from your face.

## 7. Food safety
Keep chilled items cold (chillers/dry ice), hot meals served promptly after heating,
hand-washing/gloves, no meals re-served from used trays.
