# Pre-flight checks, demos and evacuation commands

**Goal:** have the cabin ready for any emergency before the doors close, and react with
practised commands if it happens. **Your airline's Operations/Cabin Crew Manual (approved
by its regulator, e.g. EASA, FAA, CAA) and aircraft type training are the authority —
commands, equipment and timings differ by operator and type. This is the common pattern.**

## 1. Crew briefing
Before boarding: crew positions and door responsibilities, emergency procedures and
commands review, equipment and **silent-review** reminders, flight details (time,
weather, turbulence), special passengers (PRMs, unaccompanied minors, inmates/deportees,
medical cases), security notes and the purser/senior's expectations. Many airlines also
quiz crew on procedures; know your door and your alternates.

## 2. Pre-flight equipment checks (your assigned area)
Check presence, seal/serviceability and stowage per the equipment list:
- Fire extinguishers (halon/water) — pin and seal intact, gauge in range.
- **PBE/smoke hoods**, crash axe, flashlights (charging indicator), megaphones.
- Portable oxygen bottles — pressure in range, mask attached.
- First-aid kit, emergency medical kit seal, **AED** if carried.
- Life vests (crew and demo), infant vests, child restraints, extension belts.
- Lavatory smoke detectors, waste-bin fire flap closed.
- Your jump seat: harness, interphone, emergency light.
Report anything missing or defective to the senior/captain **before departure** — the
Minimum Equipment List decides whether the aircraft can go.

## 3. Boarding and cabin secure
- Exit-row passengers: assess willingness and ability, give the **exit-row briefing**.
- Bags under seats/in bins, aisles and exits clear, seats upright, tables stowed, belts
  fastened, infants on lap belts, window blinds per operator policy.
- Galley secured: carts locked, compartments latched.

## 4. Doors: armed and cross-checked
On the command (e.g. "Cabin crew, arm doors and cross-check"): arm your door's slide,
check the indicator, then **cross-check** the opposite door, and report to the senior.
On arrival, disarm and cross-check on the command before any door opens. An
inadvertently deployed slide is costly and can injure ground staff.

## 5. Safety demonstration
Given live or by video, synchronised with the PA: seat belt, exits (point to nearest
using both arms), floor-path lighting, oxygen masks (fit your own first), life vest
(inflate only on exiting), brace position, safety card. Watch passengers, not just your
own movements; follow up later with anyone who ignored it if seated at an exit.

## 6. Silent review (30-second review)
Strapped in for take-off and landing, mentally run: brace commands, my exit, how to open
it, if it's unusable what's my alternate, location of equipment, able-bodied helpers.
Critical phases (take-off, landing) → sterile cockpit; contact the flight deck only for safety.

## 7. Evacuation commands (generic pattern)
- **Brace** (unexpected impact): shout "Brace, brace! Heads down, stay down!" repeatedly
  until the aircraft stops.
- On **evacuation command** (from the captain, or crew initiation where your manual allows):
  check outside conditions (fire, water, obstruction) **before** opening the door; open,
  ensure slide inflated (pull the manual handle if not), then command loud and short:
  "Release seatbelts! Get out! Leave everything! Come this way! Jump! Slide!" 
- Exit blocked → "Exit blocked! Go that way!" redirecting with arms.
- Ditching: life vests on, inflate at the door, rafts per manual.
- Aim: evacuation capable within **90 s** (the certification standard) with half the exits.

## 8. First response basics
Fire: locate, alert (interphone), fight (extinguisher, PBE), and keep monitoring.
Medical: assess, first aid, oxygen, AED, PA for medical professionals, inform flight deck,
ground medical advice via the airline's provider. Record everything.

## Verify
- [ ] Briefing attended; your door, alternate and equipment locations known.
- [ ] All equipment in your area checked; defects reported before departure.
- [ ] Exit-row briefing given; cabin secure.
- [ ] Doors armed and cross-checked (and disarmed on arrival) on command.
- [ ] Silent review done for each take-off and landing; commands rehearsed per manual.
