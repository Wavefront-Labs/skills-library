# Cabin steward rounds and embarkation day

**Goal:** make each guest feel personally known within the first day, keep cabins
immaculate on a rhythm that fits the ship's day, and run the service so illness and
safety rules hold for a whole voyage. **Each cruise line's standards manual and the ship's
safety management system (under SOLAS) are the authority; this is the common practice.**

## 1. Embarkation day
The highest-pressure day: hundreds to thousands of guests arrive within a few hours.
- **Before guests:** cabins turned over from the previous voyage, inspected (bedding,
  bathroom, minibar, safe open, lifejackets/muster info present), cabin-ready times
  communicated to guest services. Turnover is tight: typically ships disembark and embark on
  the same morning.
- **Arrival:** greet in your section; introduce yourself by name, show how things work
  (thermostat, safe, power sockets, phone, TV daily schedule), ask about preferences (bed
  configuration, pillows, ice, allergies, celebrations), and note them on your section sheet.
- **Luggage delivery** to cabins through the afternoon — check tags and cabin numbers;
  unresolved bags go to the purser/guest services.
- **Muster/safety drill:** guests must complete the drill before or shortly after departure
  (SOLAS). Crew sweep cabins and public areas for anyone missing it; many lines now use an
  e-muster (video + check-in at the station). Know your own emergency duty station.

## 2. Cabin steward rounds
A steward typically handles one section of cabins, with two services a day:
- **Morning (full service):** when the guest is out (track "Do not disturb" / "make up
  room" signs). Strip and make beds, bathroom clean and restock, vacuum, dust, empty bins,
  check minibar, refresh towels. Work in a fixed order so nothing is missed.
- **Evening turndown:** tidy, turn down beds, fresh towels, deliver the next day's
  programme, adjust lights and curtains. Personal touches (towel animals, celebration
  notes) per line style.
- Keep your section sheet: guest names, preferences, special requests, celebrations,
  requests passed to other departments (maintenance, room service).
- Report maintenance faults immediately (leaks, AC, toilets — vacuum toilet systems clog
  easily from wipes/paper towels).

## 3. Illness prevention
Cruise ships are close communities; norovirus spreads fast.
- Hand hygiene constantly; follow the ship's **outbreak prevention plan** (e.g. US CDC
  Vessel Sanitation Program for ships calling in US ports).
- Report guests who say they're unwell to the medical centre per policy; isolated cabins get
  the enhanced cleaning protocol (PPE, specific disinfectant, separate cart).
- During outbreak levels: self-service stops, more frequent touch-point cleaning.

## 4. Dining rotations
- **Assigned dining** (fixed time and table): the same waiter team serves the same guests
  every night — learn names, drinks and allergies by night two.
- Allergies and special diets: record at the first meal, communicate to the galley, confirm
  each night.
- On "rotational dining" lines, guests and their waiters move through themed restaurants on a
  schedule — keep their preference notes travelling with them.
- Pace courses for show times; guests on second sitting/late shows plan around dinner.

## 5. Activity hosting
- Know the day's programme; host trivia, deck games, tours of the ship; welcome newcomers,
  bring solo travellers in.
- Weather and sea state cancel outdoor activities — have the indoor alternative.
- Shore days: know departure times and the all-aboard rule; remind guests at events.

## 6. Multi-day relationships
Use names; remember the anniversary; follow up on yesterday's problem before they ask.
Service recovery should be quick — the guest is with you for days. Handover notes go to
the next shift and, on back-to-back voyages, to the next week.

## Gotchas
- Never enter a cabin during "Do not disturb" except for safety per procedure.
- Guest valuables: never handle safes or belongings beyond what's needed; report found items.
- Gratuity questions go to guest services; don't discuss others' tips.

## Verify
- [ ] All section cabins inspected ready before boarding opens.
- [ ] Every guest greeted and preferences recorded on day one.
- [ ] Morning service and turndown done daily per standard; faults reported.
- [ ] Illness protocol followed; outbreak cleaning done where triggered.
- [ ] Dining allergies confirmed nightly; notes handed over between shifts.
