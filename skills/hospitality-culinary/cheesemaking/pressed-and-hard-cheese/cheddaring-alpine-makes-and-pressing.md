# Cheddaring, alpine makes and pressing — controlling moisture for long aging

**Goal:** a firm, close (or properly eyed) cheese at the right moisture and pH to age for months
to years, with a sound rind. Front-of-make practice (milk, culture, rennet, cutting) is in
culturing-renneting-and-cutting-the-curd.

## The core idea
Hard-cheese style is set by **how much whey you remove and when acid develops**: smaller cuts,
higher cook temperatures, longer stirring and pressing → drier, harder cheese. Acid that
develops too fast in a wet curd gives sour, crumbly, pasty cheese; too slow gives rubbery, bland
cheese and late gas defects.

## Cheddar
1. Mesophilic culture, rennet, cut to ~1–1.5 cm, cook slowly to ~38–39 °C over ~30–45 min,
   stir until curd is firm (squeeze test: it springs apart).
2. **Drain** at the recipe pH (typically ~6.1–6.3 in the whey).
3. **Cheddaring:** let the curd mat into slabs, cut into blocks, turn and stack every ~10–15 min,
   keeping them warm (~35–38 °C). The curd becomes fibrous, like cooked chicken breast, as acid
   and pressure expel whey. Continue until curd pH ~5.3–5.4 (recipe dependent).
4. **Mill** into finger-sized pieces, **dry salt** at ~2–2.5% of curd weight in 2–3 additions,
   stirring until salt dissolves (curd sweats).
5. Hoop, press (see below). Clothbound: bandage with lard and muslin; or vacuum-pack/wax for block cheddar.

## Alpine (Gruyère/Comté type)
Thermophilic culture (often plus some mesophilic); cut to **rice-grain size** with a harp; cook
slowly to ~50–54 °C and stir long (30–60 min) until curds are springy and squeak. Collect under the
whey in a cloth (no air between curds), press, then **brine or surface-salt** and wash the rind.
Eye-forming types (Emmental) add propionic bacteria and are warm-roomed for eye formation.

## Washed-curd (Gouda type)
After cutting, drain off ~1/3 of the whey and replace with warm water (~40 °C): this removes
lactose, limits acid, and gives the sweet, supple paste. Pre-press under whey, then mould and press.

## Pressing
- Lined or perforated moulds, followers on top. Start light, increase in steps, re-dress (flip and
  re-cloth) between stages to avoid creases and seal the rind evenly.
- Example schedule for a ~5 kg cheddar (uncertain; use the recipe): ~10 kg 15 min → flip → ~20 kg 1–2 h →
  flip → ~40 kg overnight. Commercial presses work in bar/psi.
- Keep the room warm enough (~20–24 °C) during pressing so curds knit; too cold → open texture.

## Salting and rind
- Brine (Gouda, alpine): saturated brine (~23% salt) with calcium chloride, pH ~5.0–5.2, ~10–14 °C;
  time ~3–4 h per kg (rough); flip and wet the top surface.
- Dry the surface before the cave. Rind choice: natural (brushed/turned), washed (see affinage),
  bandaged, waxed or coated.

## Gotchas
- **Late blowing** (splits, gas holes weeks later) in hard cheeses: clostridia from silage-fed milk.
  Use silage-free milk or bactofugation/approved nitrate/lysozyme (check legality).
- Mechanical openings (irregular holes) = curd too cold or too dry when moulded.
- Salt unevenly mixed = salty and sour patches.
