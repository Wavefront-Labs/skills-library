# Making bloomy, blue and washed-rind cheeses — farming moulds on purpose

**Goal:** grow the intended surface or internal flora evenly and fast enough that it wins over
unwanted moulds, and ripen the paste to the right texture without ammonia burn or slip-skin.

## Shared foundations
- These cheeses ripen from the **surface inward** (bloomy, washed) or from internal veins (blue):
  geometry matters — small, flat cheeses ripen evenly; large ones need longer and more care.
- Ripening flora are added to the milk with the culture (or sprayed later): **Geotrichum candidum**
  (de-acidifies the surface, wrinkled rind, prevents slip-skin), *Penicillium candidum/camemberti*
  (white bloom), *P. roqueforti* (blue), *Brevibacterium linens* and *Debaryomyces* yeasts (washed rinds).
- **Isolate them:** blue spores travel. Make and ripen blues in a separate room, or after other
  cheeses with full sanitation; keep soft cheese tools separate. Contamination of a bloomy room by
  blue is a classic, expensive mistake.

## Bloomy rind (Camembert/Brie type)
1. Mesophilic culture plus *P. candidum* and *Geotrichum*; long, gentle set (soft multiplier ~4–6).
2. Ladle large curds into hoops (traditionally without cutting much); turn after ~1–2 h, then
   several times. Drain at ~20–24 °C overnight; target next-day pH roughly ~4.6–4.8.
3. Salt (~1.5–2% surface dry salt or brief brine).
4. **Ripening:** ~11–13 °C, ~90–95% RH. White fuzz appears around day 5–8. Turn daily.
   Wrap in two-ply ripening paper at ~day 10–14 when fully covered; continue at 4–8 °C.
   Camembert is classically ready at ~4–6 weeks; ripens from the edge in.

## Blue
1. *P. roqueforti* in the milk; soft, open curd — salt the curds and hoop **without pressing** so
   air gaps remain for the mould.
2. Drain and turn; dry-salt the surface.
3. **Pierce (needle)** at ~1–3 weeks (once the rind has closed) with stainless rods right through,
   in a grid, to let oxygen in — blue grows along the channels. Re-pierce for big wheels.
4. Ripen ~8–12 °C, ~85–95% RH; then foil-wrap to slow growth.
- Too dry or too pressed a curd → little blue. Rind slipping, sticky, bitter → humidity too high/salt low.

## Washed rind (smear-ripened)
1. Make a semi-soft curd; mould, drain, salt.
2. **Wash** with a light brine (~3–5% salt), sometimes with beer, wine or spirit, every 1–3 days
   initially, using a dedicated cloth/brush. Inoculate the wash with *B. linens* or rub young cheeses with
   brine used on older ones ("old-young" smearing — carries flora but also any contamination).
3. Ripen ~12–15 °C, ~92–95% RH. The rind turns from ivory to orange-pink, sticky, pungent.
- Don't over-wet: puddles under cheeses become slimy and grow unwanted bacteria.

## Ripening-room control
- **Humidity** below target → cracked, dry rinds and poor mould growth; above → slip-skin, sticky rinds, Mucor.
- **Airflow:** gentle and even; ammonia builds up from ripening rinds and burns the surface
  (brown, sharp-smelling). Ventilate a little each day.
- **Unwanted moulds:** *Mucor* ("cat hair", grey fuzz) on bloomy rinds — prevent with fast
  *Geotrichum*, correct acidity and lower humidity in the first days; black or green spots on
  bloomy cheeses usually mean contamination.

## Verify
- [ ] Ripening cultures and doses logged per batch; blue work isolated.
- [ ] Room temperature, RH and airflow logged daily (hygrometer calibrated).
- [ ] Bloomy cheese fully white-coated by ~day 10–14; no Mucor.
- [ ] Blues pierced on schedule; veins well distributed on a trial cut.
- [ ] Washed rinds sticky-orange, even, without slimy or grey patches.
