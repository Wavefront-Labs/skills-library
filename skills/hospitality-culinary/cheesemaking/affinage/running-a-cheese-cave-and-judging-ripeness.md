# Running a cheese cave and judging ripeness — patience, routine and attention

**Goal:** bring each cheese to its peak by controlling its environment and rind, catching
defects early, and releasing it when it's ready — not when the shelf is full.

## 1. Zones
Different styles need different rooms (or at least shelves and microclimates). Typical
craft ranges (uncertain; tune to your cheeses):
| Style | Temp | RH |
|---|---|---|
| Natural-rind tommes, clothbound cheddar | 10–13 °C | 85–90% |
| Alpine / washed hard wheels | 12–15 °C | 90–95% |
| Bloomy rind (after initial bloom) | 8–12 °C | 90–95% |
| Blues | 8–12 °C | 85–95% |
| Washed-rind soft | 12–15 °C | 92–95% |
| Wax/vacuum-packed | 8–12 °C | lower is fine |
Calibrate hygrometers (salt test: sealed bag with damp salt → 75% RH). Log temperature and RH daily;
a data logger catches overnight swings you'd never see.

## 2. Routine care
- **Turning:** flip cheeses regularly (daily for young/soft, weekly or less for old hard wheels) so
  moisture distributes, the shape stays even and the base rind doesn't stick or rot.
- **Brushing / rubbing:** natural rinds and clothbound cheeses brushed to control mould growth and
  mites; mites show as brown dust — brush, vacuum shelves, and keep boards clean. Severe mite
  infestations are controlled by cleaning, freezing boards, or (by some) covering rinds.
- **Washing:** washed-rind and alpine wheels washed with brine (and cultures) on a schedule
  (see making-bloomy-blue-and-washed-rind-cheeses); more often early, less as the rind matures.
- **Boards:** spruce or other untreated wood boards help hold humidity and flora; scrub, dry and
  rotate them. Some regulators require validated cleaning or non-wood shelving — check yours.
- **Coating:** oil, lard and cloth, or wax for moisture control on harder cheeses.

## 3. Reading the rind (and fixing it)
| Defect | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Cracks | Too dry, or curd too dry at moulding | Raise RH; rub with brine/oil; seal cracks |
| Slip-skin (rind sliding off) | Too wet, too acid, poor Geotrichum | Lower RH, more airflow |
| Sticky, slimy hard rind | Too humid, poor airflow | Dry down, brush, increase air |
| Brown/sharp, ammonia smell | Ammonia build-up | Ventilate; paper wrap change |
| Unwanted black/green spots | Contaminant moulds | Brush/wipe with brine; review sanitation |
| Bulging, splits after weeks | Gas defect (yeasts, coliforms, late blowing) | Usually not fixable — trace to make |

## 4. Judging ripeness
- **Hard cheeses:** take a core with a **cheese iron** (trier) from a consistent place (often the
  side), smell and taste, examine texture and openings; plug the hole back with the rind end and seal.
  See cheese-evaluation-and-grading for scoring.
- **Soft cheeses:** press gently at the centre and edges; ripe bloomy cheese yields evenly with a
  thin chalky core at most; aroma mushroomy, not ammoniacal. Cut a sacrificial sample per batch.
- **Keep a cave log** per batch: make date, weights (moisture loss %), each treatment, tasting
  notes at each check. Ripeness windows shift with season and milk.
- **Release for sale** when it matches the style target and keeps a margin of good condition for
  the time it will spend in shops and fridges. Very ripe soft cheese should be sold locally and fast.

## 5. Food safety in the cave
Caves are wet and cool — the environment *Listeria* likes. Run an **environmental monitoring**
programme (swabs of drains, floors, shelves, brushes), clean drains, and keep a one-way flow from
make room to cave to packing.

## Verify
- [ ] Temperature and RH logged daily for each zone; hygrometers calibrated.
- [ ] Turning/brushing/washing schedule followed and initialled.
- [ ] Defects recorded with cause and action taken.
- [ ] Each batch has a cave log with weights and tasting notes before release.
- [ ] Environmental swabs on schedule and negative.
