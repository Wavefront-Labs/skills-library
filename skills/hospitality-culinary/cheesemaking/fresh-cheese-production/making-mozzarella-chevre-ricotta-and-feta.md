# Making mozzarella, chèvre, ricotta and feta — fresh cheese done properly

**Goal:** fast, high-moisture cheeses with the right texture, made under tight hygiene because
there's no aging step to buy back safety. General milk, culture and rennet practice is in
culturing-renneting-and-cutting-the-curd.

## Safety framing
Fresh cheese is wet, low in salt and near-neutral pH — ideal for *Listeria*. Use pasteurised milk
unless you're licensed and validated for raw; sanitise everything; keep finished cheese ≤ 4–5 °C;
give realistic use-by dates (days to a couple of weeks) and validate commercial shelf life.

## Mozzarella (pasta filata)
Stretching works only in a narrow acidity window — **curd pH ~5.1–5.3** (calcium partly removed
so the protein flows).
- Cultured method: thermophilic culture, rennet, cut ~2 cm, cook to ~38–41 °C, drain, then let the
  curd mat acidify (hours) while you test.
- **Stretch test:** drop a small piece into ~80 °C water for a minute, then pull. Shiny, stretches
  long without breaking = ready. Breaks short = acid not there yet; melts to soup/grainy = over-acid.
- Stretch: cut curd into strips, cover with water at ~75–85 °C, work with paddles until it's one
  smooth, glossy mass; fold and stretch (don't overwork — that toughens and loses fat).
  Wear heat-resistant gloves.
- Shape balls, firm in iced water, then into light brine or salted water.
- Quick citric acid method (acidify milk with citric acid before rennet) is faster and less flavourful.

## Chèvre / lactic-set cheeses
- Goat (or cow) milk at ~20–22 °C, mesophilic culture, **a very small rennet dose** (a few drops per
  4 L), left undisturbed **18–24 h** to a soft, yoghurt-like coagulum with whey on top (pH ~4.5–4.6).
- Ladle gently into cloth or moulds, drain 6–24 h at room temperature, salt ~1–1.5% by weight.
- Too warm or too much rennet → rubbery, grainy curd.

## Ricotta and paneer (acid–heat)
- **Whey ricotta:** fresh sweet whey (within a few hours of the make) heated to ~85–90 °C, acidified
  lightly (vinegar, citric) — fine flakes rise; skim gently. Adding ~10–20% milk improves yield.
- **Whole-milk ricotta:** heat milk to ~85–90 °C, add acid until curds and clear-ish whey separate,
  rest 10 min, ladle to drain. Less stirring = softer curd.
- **Paneer:** same, then press firmly under weight for 1–2 h. Doesn't melt; cuts clean.

## Feta and brined cheese
- Make: sheep/goat (or cow) milk, mesophilic culture (plus lipase for traditional bite, optional),
  rennet, cut ~2 cm, gentle stir, drain in moulds, flip, dry-salt.
- **Brine:** ~7–10% salt with calcium chloride (~0.02–0.05%) and vinegar to pH ~4.5–4.8 — without
  calcium and acid, the brine pulls calcium out and the cheese goes **soft and slimy**.
- Store submerged in brine, cold. Halloumi is similar but the curd is poached in whey (~85–90 °C)
  until it floats, then salted — which is why it grills without melting.

## Yoghurt cheeses
Labneh: strain salted yoghurt in cloth 12–48 h in the fridge. Quark/fromage blanc: lactic set like
chèvre, drained less.

## Gotchas
- Wrong milk: UHT/heavily homogenised milk gives poor mozzarella and weak curds.
- Mozzarella that won't stretch is almost always pH — measure, don't guess.
- Fresh cheese in a warm display case for a few hours shortens shelf life drastically.

## Verify
- [ ] Mozzarella curd stretched at measured pH 5.1–5.3; glossy, smooth finish.
- [ ] Chèvre coagulated to pH ~4.6 before ladling.
- [ ] Feta brine strength, calcium and pH measured and logged.
- [ ] Finished cheese held ≤ 5 °C with date and use-by label.
