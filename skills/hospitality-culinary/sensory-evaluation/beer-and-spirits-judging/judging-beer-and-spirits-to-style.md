# Judging beer and spirits to style

**Goal:** score an entry fairly against its declared style, identify what's wrong and
why, and write feedback the producer can act on. Competitions publish their own
guidelines — e.g. the **BJCP Style Guidelines** and scoresheet for beer (latest
edition in force, 2021 at time of writing [check]), and event-specific rules for
spirits. Follow the event's version.

## 1. Beer scoresheet (BJCP-style, 50 points)
Common allocation: **Aroma 12, Appearance 3, Flavour 20, Mouthfeel 5, Overall
Impression 10**. Score bands roughly: 45–50 outstanding, 38–44 excellent, 30–37 very
good, 21–29 good, 14–20 fair, under 14 problematic.
- **Judge to the style declared**, not to what you like. Read the style description
  before each flight.
- Pour into a clean, rinsed glass; note **aroma immediately** — volatile notes fade.
- Note appearance (colour, clarity, head retention), then taste: malt, hops,
  fermentation character, balance, finish; mouthfeel (body, carbonation, warmth,
  astringency).

## 2. Off-flavour taxonomy (beer)
| Off-flavour | Perceived as | Common cause |
|---|---|---|
| **Diacetyl** | Butter, butterscotch, slick | Incomplete fermentation/rest, infection |
| **DMS** | Cooked corn, creamed corn | Pilsner malt, weak/covered boil, slow chilling |
| **Acetaldehyde** | Green apple, fresh-cut pumpkin | Young beer, yeast pulled early |
| **Oxidation** | Papery, cardboard, sherry | Oxygen after fermentation, age, heat |
| **Lightstruck** | Skunky | Light exposure (clear/green glass) |
| **Phenolic** | Clove, plastic, medicinal | Wild yeast/infection, chlorine (chlorophenols) |
| **Solvent / hot alcohol** | Nail polish, burning | Hot fermentation |
| **Sour/acetic** (when not to style) | Vinegar, lactic | Bacterial infection |
| **Astringent** | Grainy, puckering | Over-sparging, high pH, husk |
Note that some "off-flavours" are **in style** (e.g. clove phenols in Weizen,
diacetyl at low levels in some British ales). Off-flavour training kits (spiked
samples) are the fastest way to learn thresholds.

## 3. Spirits nosing and tasting
- Use a tulip/copita glass; small pour (~15–25 ml).
- **Nose at a distance first**, then closer; avoid deep inhales of cask-strength
  spirits — alcohol anaesthetises the nose.
- **Reduce with water**: many panels assess at ~20–30% ABV (or a set addition) to
  open aromas and allow fair comparison. *[Panel conventions differ; follow the event.]*
- Assess nose, palate (sweetness, texture, spice, wood), finish length, balance.
- Spirits faults: sulphur/rubbery, soapy, cardboard/papery (oxidised), feinty
  (heavy, sweaty — poor cut), excessive raw new-make, over-oaked/tannic, off-cask
  notes (musty).

## 4. Flight management
- Order flights lighter → heavier, lower → higher ABV and hop/peat intensity.
- **Keep flights small** (around 8–12 beers per flight is common) and take breaks.
- Water and plain crackers between samples; spit spirits.
- Two judges per flight; compare scores — if more than ~7 points apart on beer, discuss
  and reconcile.
- Mini-best-of-show: re-taste the top entries side by side.

## 5. Writing feedback
For every flaw: **what** you perceived, **how strong**, **likely cause**, **how to fix**.
"Moderate buttery diacetyl in aroma and flavour; suggest a diacetyl rest and check
fermentation finished before packaging." Note the positives too. Avoid "Nice beer!"
with no explanation.

## Gotchas
- Scoring a great beer low because it's entered in the wrong category — note it
  but score to the declared style per rules.
- Palate fatigue late in big flights, especially hoppy/peated ones.
- Warm beer tastes more alcoholic and shows more flaws; judge at sensible temperature.

## Verify
- [ ] Style guideline read before the flight.
- [ ] Every scoresheet has descriptors, causes and fixes for faults.
- [ ] Scores within the reconciliation range of your co-judge.
- [ ] Spirits assessed at consistent dilution.
