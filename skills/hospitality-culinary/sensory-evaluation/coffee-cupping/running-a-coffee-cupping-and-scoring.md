# Running a coffee cupping and scoring

**Goal:** assess coffees side by side under identical conditions so differences come
from the coffee, not the brewing — for buying, QC of roasts, or menu selection.

*Note: the legacy SCA protocol uses a 100-point cupping form (80+ = specialty). Newer
protocols exist, such as the Coffee Value Assessment (CVA). Check which protocol your
buyers/QC system uses and follow its current document.*

## 1. Standard setup (legacy SCA protocol, widely used)
- **Sample roast**: 8–12 min roast duration, done 8–24 h before cupping. Same roast degree for all samples.
- **Ratio**: **8.25 g coffee per 150 ml water** (≈1:18), in identical cups.
- **Grind**: 70–75% of grounds pass a #20 sieve; ground just before use.
- **Water**: clean, odour-free, 200 °F (±2 °F) at pour.
- **Cups**: 5 cups per sample to check uniformity.
- Quiet, odour-free room; no perfume, no smoking or strong food beforehand.

## 2. Sequence
1. **Fragrance (dry)**: smell each sample's dry grounds.
2. **Pour** water to the brim, start a timer. Grounds form a crust.
3. **Break** at 4 min: push the crust back with a spoon, nose close,
   inhaling the aroma release. Note **aroma (wet)**.
4. **Skim** the foam and floating grounds with two spoons; rinse spoons between cups.
5. **Taste** once cooled (the protocol cites 170 °F as a common tasting temperature, 10–15 min after pouring): fill the spoon, **slurp hard** so coffee
   sprays across the palate and aerates. Spit (or swallow when only a few samples).
6. Taste again as it cools. Acidity,
   sweetness and defects show differently with temperature; **flavours you find only
   when cool are often the most honest**.
7. Score per attribute on the form: (legacy) fragrance/aroma, flavour, aftertaste,
   acidity, body, balance, uniformity, clean cup, sweetness, overall, minus defects.

## 3. Taints and faults
Note any cup that's off: musty/earthy, phenolic/medicinal, ferment/overripe,
potato, rubbery, stale paper. Deduct per your form's rules for taints and faults.
One defective cup out of five matters — that's 20% of the bag.

## 4. Green grading
Take a fixed sample (size per the grading handbook) and count **primary** and
**secondary** defects using the full-defect equivalents in the handbook. Also check
moisture and colour uniformity.

## 5. Calibration
- Cup a **reference coffee** each session to anchor scores.
- Cup blind (coded cups), randomised order.
- Compare scores among cuppers and discuss the gaps and what each person tasted.

## Gotchas
- Different roast degrees between samples overwhelm everything else.
- Tasting only hot — misses defects and sweetness.
- Contaminating cups by not rinsing spoons between samples.
- Scoring the coffee you expect (origin bias) — cup blind.

## Verify
- [ ] Ratio, grind time, water temperature identical for all samples.
- [ ] 5 cups per sample; any non-uniform cup noted.
- [ ] Each sample tasted at more than one temperature.
- [ ] Reference coffee scored within your team's calibration band.
