# Evaluating a fragrance on strip and skin

**Goal:** describe and judge a finished fragrance reliably — for retail advice, buying,
competitive benchmarking or development feedback. For smelling raw materials and
composing, see handcrafts.perfumery.perfumery-materials.smelling-and-evaluating-raw-materials
and handcrafts.perfumery.scent-composition.building-a-perfume-formula.

## 1. Conditions
- Odour-neutral, ventilated room; no scented products on you; avoid evaluating right
  after eating or coffee.
- Unscented, absorbent **smelling strips** (blotters, mouillettes). Label each strip
  with name/code **and the time** before dipping or spraying.
- Coffee beans as a "reset" are a popular myth with weak support; better to step into
  fresh air or smell **unscented skin** (your inner elbow/forearm) between samples.

## 2. Strip technique
1. Spray once onto the strip from ~10–15 cm, or dip ~1 cm into the liquid.
2. **Wait a few seconds** for the ethanol to flash off — the first sniff is mostly
   alcohol.
3. Hold the strip ~2–5 cm below the nose and wave gently; take **short sniffs**, then
   move away. Long inhales cause **olfactory fatigue** within seconds to minutes.
4. Smell again at set times: ~1 min, 15 min, 1 h, 3–4 h, next day. Write a note each
   time. Keep strips upright in a holder, not touching.

## 3. The drydown: top, heart, base
- **Top notes** (first ~5–15 min): volatile — citrus, light herbs, aldehydes, fresh
  fruit.
- **Heart/middle** (~15 min to a few hours): the character — florals, spices, green notes.
- **Base** (hours to a day+): woods, musks, amber, resins, vanilla, patchouli.
Modern perfumes are often **linear** (designed to smell similar throughout) rather than
following a strict pyramid; the published "note pyramid" is marketing, not a formula.
Judge what you smell over time.

## 4. Placing it in an olfactory family
Common classification systems (e.g. Michael Edwards' Fragrance Wheel, the Société
Française des Parfumeurs classification) use families such as:
- **Fresh**: citrus, green, aromatic, water/marine
- **Floral**: single floral, floral bouquet, soft floral (aldehydic/powdery)
- **Amber** (formerly "oriental"): vanilla, resins, spices, sweet balsams
- **Woody**: sandalwood, cedar, vetiver, mossy woods
- **Chypre** (citrus top, floral/rose heart, oakmoss/patchouli base) and **fougère**
  (lavender, coumarin, oakmoss — classic barbershop)
- **Gourmand**: edible notes (caramel, praline, vanilla)
State the main family and a modifier: "woody amber with a spicy top."

## 5. Performance and quality
- **Longevity**: hours until barely perceptible on strip and **on skin** (skin is
  shorter and differs by skin type, temperature, moisturiser).
- **Sillage/projection**: how far it radiates from skin in the first hour and later —
  ask someone else; you'll go nose-blind to your own.
- **Quality judgement**: coherence (does it smell like one idea?), smoothness of
  transitions, no harsh synthetic spikes or sour off-notes, balance between notes,
  originality versus the reference market.
- **Faults**: oxidised citrus (stale, turpentine-like), sour/vinegar notes, discolouration
  — usually from age, heat or light.

## 6. Comparing fragrances fairly
- Same dose on each strip, same time, coded blind if possible.
- Compare **at the same stage** of drydown.
- No more than ~3–4 fragrances in one session; rest between rounds.
- Skin-test only a couple at a time (wrists/inner arms), noting skin conditions.

## Safety
Patch-test new products on skin if sensitive; some materials are allergens (IFRA
standards and labelling regulations apply). Don't spray near eyes; ventilate when
evaluating many samples.

## Gotchas
- Judging from the first sniff (alcohol + top notes only).
- Smelling at different times and calling it a difference in fragrance.
- Spraying too much — overwhelms and fatigues.
- Trusting the advertised pyramid over your own nose.

## Verify
- [ ] Each strip labelled with name/code and time.
- [ ] Notes recorded at least at 1 min, ~30 min, ~2–4 h and next day.
- [ ] Family placed with a modifier; longevity and sillage recorded on skin.
- [ ] Comparisons blind, same dose, same stage.
