# Shaking, stirring and balancing cocktails

**Goal:** make classic and original drinks that are balanced, properly cold and
properly diluted — every time, not just the first time.

## 1. Measure
Use a **jigger** for every pour while learning, and in most bars always. Free-pour only
with tested counts. Standard units: 30 ml (1 oz), 15 ml (½ oz), 22.5 ml (¾ oz),
7.5 ml (¼ oz). Small errors in sugar and acid ruin balance more than errors in spirit.

## 2. Shake or stir?
- **Shake** drinks with citrus, juice, cream, egg or other cloudy ingredients. Shaking
  chills fast, dilutes, and aerates (texture, froth). Hard shake ~10–15 s with plenty
  of solid ice. Double-strain (Hawthorne + fine strainer) into a chilled glass.
- **Stir** spirit-forward clear drinks (Martini, Manhattan, Negroni, Old Fashioned).
  Stirring chills and dilutes with a silky, clear texture. Stir ~20–40 s with cold,
  dry ice; taste a drop on the back of your hand or a straw.
- **Dry shake** (no ice) before the ice shake builds egg-white foam; the **reverse dry
  shake** (ice shake, strain, then shake again without ice) gives a denser foam.
- **Build** (in glass) for highballs: ice, spirit, then mixer; a gentle lift-stir keeps
  bubbles.

## 3. Dilution is an ingredient
A shaken or stirred drink typically ends up roughly **20–30% water** by volume from melted
ice; that water is what makes it drinkable. *[Commonly quoted range; varies with ice and
technique.]* Wet, small or old ice over-dilutes; use large, cold, dry cubes. For
batching (see bar-station-operations), add the water you'd get from stirring.

## 4. Balance templates
- **Sour** (Daiquiri, Whisky Sour, Margarita-family): ~**60 ml spirit : 22.5–30 ml
  citrus : 15–22.5 ml simple syrup (1:1)**. Classic Daiquiri e.g. 60 rum / 25 lime /
  15–20 syrup. Adjust sugar to the fruit — limes vary.
- **Spirit-forward**: base spirit plus modifier (vermouth, liqueur), bitters, little or
  no sugar — e.g. Negroni 1:1:1, Manhattan ~2:1 whisky:sweet vermouth + 2 dashes
  bitters.
- **Highball**: ~1:3 spirit:mixer, tall glass, lots of ice.
Taste for the four levers: **sweet vs sour**, **strength**, **dilution/temperature**,
**aroma**. A sour that tastes harsh is often under-diluted rather than unbalanced.

## 5. Syrups and infusions
- **Simple syrup 1:1** (sugar:water by weight) — dissolves cold with stirring;
  refrigerate, shelf life ~2–4 weeks. **Rich 2:1** — sweeter, keeps longer; use about
  two-thirds the volume.
- **Flavoured syrups**: heat with spices, steep herbs off heat, or cold-process fruit
  (oleo-saccharum: citrus peels + sugar, sit a few hours, then dissolve with juice/water).
- **Quick infusions**: spirit + ingredient in a jar, taste daily (chillies: hours; tea:
  minutes; dried fruit: days); strain when right. Label with date and contents.
- **Citrus**: juice daily; lime and lemon lose brightness within a day.

## 6. Garnish for aroma
Express a citrus peel over the drink (skin side down), rub the rim if desired, then
garnish or discard. Garnish should signal the flavour, not decorate randomly. Keep
garnishes fresh and covered.

## Gotchas
- Shaking stirred drinks clouds them and over-aerates (fine if the guest asks).
- Pre-chilled glasses matter as much as technique.
- Raw egg white: food-safety rules apply (pasteurised whites or local guidance).
- Cream liqueurs curdle with citrus.

## Verify
- [ ] Every component jiggered; recipe written for consistency.
- [ ] Taste test before serving: no hot alcohol burn, no sharp acid spike, not cloying.
- [ ] Drink served cold, correct dilution, garnish fresh.
- [ ] Syrups and infusions labelled and in date.
