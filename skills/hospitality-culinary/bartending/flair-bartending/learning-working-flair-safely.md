# Learning working flair safely

**Goal:** add movement and showmanship to real drink-making without spilling, breaking
glass or slowing tickets. **Working flair** happens during service with the moves woven
into the pour; **exhibition flair** is choreographed, multi-object, usually with
practice bottles and minimal liquid, for shows and competitions.

## 1. Gear
- **Practice (flair) bottles**: plastic, weighted like a 700 ml/750 ml spirit bottle;
  start with these, never glass.
- **Shaker tins** (tin-on-tin) — sturdy, no glass halves.
- **Pour spouts** fitted tightly; a loose spout launches itself.
- **Mats** on the floor at practice, or practise over grass/carpet.
- Non-slip shoes. Clear space around you with nothing breakable.

## 2. Core move progression
Practise each move until it's ~90% clean before stacking it into a sequence. A
common order:
1. **Bottle grips and hand-to-hand passes** behind the back, under the leg.
2. **Single flip** (one rotation, catch by the neck) — low height first.
3. **Palm stall / back-of-hand stall** — balance the bottle on the hand.
4. **Tin moves**: tin rolls over the hand, tin flip-and-catch, tin into tin.
5. **Bottle-to-tin pours**: flip, catch, pour with a counted measure.
6. **Pass + pour combos**: two-object juggling only after singles are automatic.
Film yourself; drop rates tell you more than how it felt.

## 3. Working flair in service
- Use **real moves only where they don't cost time**: a flip on pick-up, a spin
  before the pour, a tin roll while capping. If a move adds more than a second or two
  per drink in a rush, drop it.
- **Liquid pours must stay accurate.** Use measured pour counts tested against a
  jigger, or pour into a jigger with style. Flair never excuses over-pouring
  (see bar-inventory-and-costing).
- **Read the room**: a packed Friday ticket rail is not the time; a quiet bar with an
  audience is.
- **Glass bottles in service**: only moves you land essentially every time, at low
  height, over the bar, away from guests. Never throw anything toward or over guests.
- Keep spouts and caps on; a full bottle mid-air can empty fast.

## 4. Exhibition routines
- Build a 2–4 minute routine to music: opening hook, build-up, peak trick, clean finish
  with the drink served.
- Rehearse in the exact space and lighting; ceilings and lights cause drops.
- Competitions publish rules (time, drinks required, penalties for drops, spills and
  broken glass) — read the current rules for each event. Judges typically score
  technique, difficulty, drink quality, presentation.
- Have a **recovery move** ready for drops: pick up smoothly, keep smiling.

## 5. Safety rules
- Never flair with glassware during service if you can't guarantee the catch.
- Broken glass in an ice well: **burn the ice** (melt it all away with hot water) and
  replace; never scoop around shards.
- Watch for wrist and shoulder strain; warm up, take breaks.
- Some venues ban flair entirely — follow house rules and insurance terms.

## Gotchas
- Practising with full bottles too early: expensive and dangerous.
- Moves that look good but break pour accuracy.
- Flair making the drink worse (warm, diluted, wrong) — the drink still comes first.
- Neglecting other guests while performing for one.

## Verify
- [ ] Each move landed ≥9/10 on camera before using it on the bar.
- [ ] Service pour counts checked against a jigger after flair moves.
- [ ] Ticket times unchanged versus non-flair service.
- [ ] No glass flairing over guests; ice well protocol known.
