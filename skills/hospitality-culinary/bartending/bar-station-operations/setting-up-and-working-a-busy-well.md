# Setting up and working a busy well

**Goal:** a station where every bottle and tool is where your hand expects it, so you
can make many drinks quickly, accurately and cleanly on the busiest night.

## 1. Station setup (every shift, the same way)
- **Speed rail**: house spirits in a fixed order every bartender knows (e.g. vodka,
  gin, rum, tequila, whisky, then triple sec/most-used liqueurs). Labels facing out,
  spouts all angled the same way.
- **Ice well**: full, fresh, scoop in its holder — never a glass as a scoop.
- **Tools** within one reach: jiggers, tins, mixing glass, bar spoon, Hawthorne and
  fine strainers, muddler, knife, board.
- **Garnish tray** covered, cut for the expected volume, with a backup in the fridge.
- **Juices and syrups** in labelled, dated squeeze bottles in a fixed order.
- **Glassware** stocked and chilled for the top-selling drinks.
- **Towels**: dry for glass and hands, damp for spills; bar mats down.
- **POS** working, float counted.

## 2. Prep and batching
- Squeeze citrus and make syrups before service; amounts based on last comparable
  night plus margin.
- **Batch** high-volume or spirit-forward specials. A batch of Negronis can be
  mixed in bulk and either stirred to order or **pre-diluted** and kept cold.
  Pre-dilution: add water equal to what stirring would add — often ~20–25% of drink
  volume for a stirred spirit-forward drink. *[Check by tasting against a freshly
  stirred one.]*
- **Don't batch citrus** for long holds; fresh juice fades within hours. Batch the
  non-citrus parts and add juice to order, or batch citrus only for the shift.
- Label every batch: contents, ratio, date/time, initials. Batched drinks with alcohol
  still follow local licensing rules.

## 3. Sequencing a round
For a ticket like "2 Mojitos, a Martini, 3 beers, a G&T":
1. **Glass everything first**, lined up in front of you.
2. Start the **longest** items: muddle the Mojitos, start the Martini stirring (or
   have a partner stir).
3. Pour **shaken drinks together** in one shake when they're the same; otherwise use
   two tins in sequence.
4. **Build fizzy/still drinks and beers last** so they're cold and bubbly when served.
5. Garnish everything at the end in one pass.
Rule of thumb: ice/shake/stir in parallel, carbonation last, garnish last.

## 4. Working with others
- **Bar-back** keeps ice, glass, garnish and bottles flowing; tell them *before* you
  run out ("two more limes, gin backup").
- Call out when moving behind someone ("Behind!") and with glass/knives ("Sharp!").
- Service bar (drinks for servers): work tickets in order; tell servers ETA honestly.

## 5. Keeping pace
- Take the next order while a drink is shaking.
- Acknowledge waiting guests even when you can't serve them yet — a nod, "with you
  next".
- Avoid steps across the bar; everything for a drink should be within one pivot.

## 6. Breakdown
Wipe and cap bottles, dump and clean the ice well (burn if glass broke), wash and air-dry
tools, store juices and garnishes labelled (most cut fruit is discarded at close), log
waste and 86'd items, restock for the next shift.

## Gotchas
- A bottle out of its rail position slows everyone else.
- Glass scooping ice — chips and contaminates the well.
- Over-prepping garnish that goes to waste.
- Batches that taste fine warm but flat when chilled — taste at serving temperature.
