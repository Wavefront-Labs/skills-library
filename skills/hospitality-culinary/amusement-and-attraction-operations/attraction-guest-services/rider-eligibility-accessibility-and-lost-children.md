# Rider eligibility, accessibility and lost children

**Goal:** keep guests safe on rides without humiliating anyone, make the park usable for
guests with disabilities, reunite lost children fast, and turn bad moments into recovered guests.
**Your park's policies and each ride's manufacturer restrictions are the authority; this is the method.**

## 1. Rider eligibility
Eligibility comes from the ride manufacturer's restrictions and the park's risk assessment:
height (minimum and sometimes maximum), body proportions that fit the restraint,
ability to hold a posture/brace, ability to evacuate, health warnings (pregnancy, heart,
back, recent surgery).
- **Measure consistently:** guest stands straight, shoes on, hats off, back to the stick;
  measure at the top of the head, not hair. If in doubt at the line, they don't ride — a
  re-measure by a lead is fine; "close enough" is not.
- Measure at the **queue entrance** so families don't wait 45 min to be turned away.
- **Rider switch / child swap:** one adult waits with the ineligible child while the other
  rides, then swaps without re-queuing. Explain it proactively.
- **Test seats** at the entrance let larger guests check restraint fit privately.
- Say "This ride's restraint is designed for…" — about the ride, never the person. Offer
  alternatives that suit them.

## 2. Accessibility accommodations
- Publish an **accessibility guide** per attraction: transfer requirements (can the guest
  move from wheelchair to ride seat, with or without a companion's help — staff usually
  may not lift guests), ride effects (drops, darkness, loud noise, strobes), service-animal
  rules, where wheelchair-accessible vehicles exist.
- **Return-time / access passes** (e.g. Disney's DAS, Merlin's Ride Access Pass and similar)
  let guests who can't wait in a physical queue get a return time equal to the current wait.
  Eligibility rules and documentation differ by operator — apply them consistently and
  don't ask for a diagnosis beyond what policy requires. *Uncertain: specific programmes
  change often; check current policy.*
- Accessible queue entrance, companion rules, quiet rooms, sensory guides, sign language
  times for shows.
- Evacuation plans must cover guests who cannot use stairs/catwalks.

## 3. Lost children
Speed matters. A typical procedure (follow yours):
1. **Report**: get the child's name, age, description (clothing, hair, height), last seen
   time and place, and a photo from the parent's phone.
2. **Alert**: radio the description park-wide immediately; staff at exits watch for the
   child (some parks run a lockdown-style protocol such as "Code Adam" — exits monitored
   until the child is found).
3. **Search**: nearby rides, toilets, shops, and the meeting points kids head for. A parent
   or guardian stays at the last-seen point or at guest services with a staff member.
4. **Found child**: a staff member stays with them (two staff, or in a visible public area —
   never alone and out of sight), takes them to the designated lost-children point, and
   **verifies the adult's identity** (child recognises them, details match, ID) before release.
5. If not found within your set time (often 10–15 min, sooner for very young children or
   concerning circumstances), call police.
6. Log every case.
Prevention: wristbands with a parent's mobile number; tell kids "find a staff member in uniform".

## 4. Lost property
Log items with time, place, finder; secure valuables (phones, wallets) separately; check
ID before release. Items found on rides are retrieved by staff after lockout or close.

## 5. Guest recovery
Follow a short model like **listen → apologise → act → follow up**:
- Let them finish; acknowledge feelings without arguing about facts.
- Fix what you can now: return-time pass after a breakdown, replacement food, ride photo.
- Know your empowerment limits and escalate beyond them.
- Note the recovery in the log so another team doesn't repeat it.

## Gotchas
- Shoes-on vs shoes-off and hairstyle tricks cause most height disputes — publish the rule.
- Don't announce a lost child's name on a PA if policy forbids; describe instead.
- Never let a guest "just slip under" an eligibility rule because they waited a long time.

## Verify
- [ ] Eligibility checked at queue entrance; same method every time; rider switch offered.
- [ ] Accessibility guide accurate; access-pass policy applied consistently.
- [ ] Lost-child steps practised; release only after identity verified.
- [ ] Lost property logged and secured.
- [ ] Guest recoveries logged and within empowerment limits.
