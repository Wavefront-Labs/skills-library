# Building show, parade and character schedules

**Goal:** a daily entertainment timetable that spreads guests around the park, fits each
venue's turnover, protects performers' health, and survives the weather.

## 1. Start from the park's day
- Map **attendance curve**: arrival peak, midday peak, evening/close. Entertainment pulls
  guests out of ride queues — schedule big shows to relieve peak queue times, not when
  the park is empty.
- Anchor the **fixed pieces** first: parade(s), nighttime spectacular (tied to sunset and
  close), headliner stage shows. Then fill with smaller sets and character meets.
- **Stagger** shows in neighbouring venues so their exits don't release crowds into the
  same path at once; leave ≥ 15–20 min between nearby show-endings and a parade step-off
  on the same route.

## 2. Stage show slots
For each venue:

`slot = audience load-in + show length + exit + reset`

Example: 20 min show, 10 min load-in, 5 min exit, 15 min reset → a show every **50–60 min**
at best. Performers' rules (below) may cap shows per day lower than the venue can.
- Publish shows at round times guests remember (e.g. :00, :30).
- Last show must end before park close minus exit time.

## 3. Parades
- Route walk-through per day; know the **step-off time**, route duration (often 15–30 min
  end-to-end, longer for a guest to see it pass), and units in order.
- Rope/guest-control teams in place 30–60 min before step-off; crossing points defined.
- Plan the "hold" points for unit spacing and a stop-and-perform point if used.
- Wind and rain thresholds for floats and inflatables are set with the technical team in advance.

## 4. Costume characters and performers
- **Set lengths:** costumed (full-head) characters often work **20–30 min sets** then a
  break of equal or longer length; shorter in heat. Face characters can work longer.
  *Uncertain: exact limits come from your park's health & safety assessment and union/
  collective agreements — follow them, these are typical values only.*
- **Heat stress:** reduce set length, move meets to shade/indoors, provide cooling vests
  and hydration; a handler or "attendant" accompanies every costumed character to
  manage guests, give cues, and end the set early if needed.
- **Rotation:** build performer schedules with set, break, meal, and travel time between
  locations (backstage routes take time). Respect maximum on-stage hours per day.
- Cover/understudy plan: who covers each role if someone is sick.
- Characters never appear in two places at once — public-facing schedules must avoid it.

## 5. The daily schedule document
Columns: time, venue/location, show/character, performers, tech/crew, guest-control staff,
notes (weather backup, accessibility seating). Publish a guest-facing version (app, map,
signage) and a backstage call sheet. Changes go through one owner to avoid two versions.

## 6. Weather calls
- Decide calls at set times (e.g. 60 and 30 min before) based on forecast and lightning
  detection. Options: proceed, modified version (no floats, cover show), move indoors, cancel.
- Communicate instantly to guest services, app, and front-of-house staff.

## Gotchas
- Parades block paths — check they don't cut guests off from exits, first-aid or rides with timed entries.
- Over-scheduling meets with no break buffer causes characters to cut lines off early and upset guests.
- Accessible viewing areas need guarding before guests fill them.

## Verify
- [ ] Shows staggered against attendance peaks and each other's exits.
- [ ] Each venue's slot length includes load-in, exit and reset.
- [ ] Performer sets and breaks within the park's health and agreement rules; handlers assigned.
- [ ] Parade route, guest control and step-off confirmed.
- [ ] Weather call times and alternatives agreed; one owner for schedule changes.
