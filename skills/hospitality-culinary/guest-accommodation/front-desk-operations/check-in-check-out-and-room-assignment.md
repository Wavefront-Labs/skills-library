# Check-in, check-out and room assignment

**Goal:** every arriving guest gets the right room quickly with the right payment
guarantee, every departing guest leaves with a correct bill, and the next shift
knows exactly what's pending.

## 1. Before arrivals (morning / early shift)
Pull the **arrivals list** in the PMS and pre-assign (block) rooms:
1. VIPs, loyalty members, special requests (high floor, quiet, accessible,
   connecting, cot) first.
2. Long stays and early arrivals next — they need rooms that are vacant or due out
   early.
3. Then everyone else, using rooms already **vacant clean / inspected** first.
Check against the **room-status report**: out-of-order rooms can't be sold;
rooms due out need housekeeping turnaround before they're ready. Tell housekeeping
which due-outs to prioritise for early arrivals.

## 2. Check-in (aim for a few minutes)
1. **Greet first**, look up, smile — even if on the phone, acknowledge with a nod.
2. Find the reservation; confirm name, dates, room type, rate, and number of guests.
3. **ID check** and registration details as required by local law (some countries
   require passport details for foreign guests to be recorded or reported).
4. **Payment guarantee:** take a card pre-authorisation for room + incidentals
   (amount per house policy), or a cash deposit. Explain it's a hold, not a charge,
   and that it releases after check-out (timing depends on the guest's bank — often
   several business days).
5. **Upsell once, specifically:** "For [amount] more per night I can put you in a
   corner room with the sea view." Offer, don't push; accept a no immediately.
6. Encode keys (one per adult or per house policy). Never say the room number aloud
   in a busy lobby — write it on the key wallet and point to it.
7. Explain: breakfast times/location, Wi-Fi, check-out time, lift direction.

## 3. Room not ready
Store the luggage (tagged, with a receipt), take a mobile number, offer the bar,
pool or a coffee, and text or call when the room is inspected. Give a realistic time.
Push housekeeping for that room specifically.

## 4. Check-out
1. Print or show the folio; walk through any charges the guest may not expect
   (minibar, room service, city tax).
2. Settle against the pre-auth card or another payment; split folios if company
   pays room and guest pays incidentals.
3. Disputed charge: if small and plausible, adjust per your authority; if not, refer
   to the duty manager. Note the reason in the PMS.
4. Collect keys (or deactivate), ask about the stay, and offer luggage storage or
   transport.
5. **Express check-out**: folio emailed and charged automatically — check the
   charges posted overnight before closing it.

## 5. Shift handover
Written log (PMS log or book) covering: pending arrivals and special requests,
rooms not ready, guest complaints in progress, payments outstanding, wake-up calls,
maintenance issues reported, and cash float count. Count the float together.

## Gotchas
- Assigning a "due out" room to an early arrival without checking it's checked out.
- Pre-auth too low — incidentals then go unpaid.
- Saying room numbers aloud: a guest-safety issue.
- Upselling every guest the same way — it starts to sound like a script.
- Not releasing unused blocked rooms before evening, so the house shows full
  when it isn't.

## Verify
- [ ] Arrivals pre-assigned with requests honoured, against real room status.
- [ ] Each check-in has ID, registration data and a valid payment guarantee.
- [ ] Upsell offered once, specifically, with no pressure.
- [ ] Room numbers not spoken aloud.
- [ ] Every folio checked line-by-line before settlement.
- [ ] Handover log written and float counted with the next shift.

*Uncertain:* legal ID/registration and pre-auth release times vary by country and
bank; follow local law and the property's standards.
