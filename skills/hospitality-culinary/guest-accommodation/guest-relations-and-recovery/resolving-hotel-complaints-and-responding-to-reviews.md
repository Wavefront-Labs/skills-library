# Resolving hotel complaints and responding to reviews

**Goal:** a guest whose stay went wrong checks out feeling the hotel cared and
fixed it — and the next guest reading the review sees a property that takes
problems seriously.

## 1. Own it — whoever hears it, owns it
The person the guest tells is responsible until the problem is handed over
*by name* ("I've told Sam, the duty manager; they'll call you within 15 minutes").
Never say "that's not my department".

## 2. The resolution flow
1. **Listen and write it down** — the details matter for the fix and the log.
2. **Acknowledge and apologise** for the inconvenience, without blaming housekeeping
   or engineering in front of the guest.
3. **Ask what would help**, or offer a choice: "I can send maintenance now, or move
   you to another room — which would you prefer?" Many guests want the fix, not money.
4. **Act with a time promise**, then beat it.
5. **Close the loop:** call or visit the room after the fix. Then again at check-out.

## 3. Common problems and first moves
| Problem | First move |
|---|---|
| Noise (neighbours, street, plant room) | Security/knock for neighbours; offer room move to a quiet side |
| Room not clean on arrival | Move to an inspected room or clean immediately while guest waits in lounge |
| AC/heating/hot water failure | Engineering now; room move if not fixed fast |
| Wrong room type / bed type | Move or upgrade; the reservation is the promise |
| Billing error | Correct immediately, reprint folio, apologise |
| Lost item / theft claim | Take details, check lost & found, escalate to security; don't speculate |

## 4. Compensation judgement
Proportion compensation to **impact on the stay**, not to the guest's volume:
- Minor, fixed quickly: apology + small gesture (amenity, drinks voucher, late check-out).
- Significant (bad night's sleep, room unusable for part of the stay): partial room
  rate adjustment for the affected night, upgrade, or loyalty points.
- Severe (unsafe, health, multiple failures): full night waived, manager-level
  decision, and a written follow-up.
Know the **empowerment limit** for your role. Log the cost and reason.
Recovery offers that cost little but show attention (a handwritten note, a
favourite drink sent up) often outperform discounts.

## 5. VIPs and loyalty members
- Read the guest profile before arrival: previous complaints, preferences, tier.
- A guest who complained last time should get pre-arrival attention ("We've put you
  on a high floor away from the lifts").
- Elite loyalty tiers may carry contractual benefits (late check-out, upgrades
  subject to availability) — check the brand's rules before refusing.

## 6. Logging
Every complaint goes on the guest profile and the incident/complaint log: what,
when, who handled it, what was given. Weekly review for repeat themes (same room,
same shift, same failing equipment).

## 7. Responding to online reviews
- Reply within a few days, signed by a named manager.
- **Thank, acknowledge the specific point, explain the fix** (briefly), invite them
  back or to contact you directly. Keep it short.
- Never argue facts, reveal booking details, or name staff negatively. Don't offer
  compensation publicly.
- Reply to good reviews too, briefly and specifically.
- Template phrases are OK as a skeleton but each reply must reference what the guest
  actually wrote.

## Gotchas
- A room move offered but the new room isn't inspected yet.
- Compensating loud guests more than quiet ones with worse problems.
- Fix done, nobody checks back — the guest assumes nothing happened.
- Review replies that sound defensive or copy-pasted.
- Not logging it, so the guest gets the same broken room next visit.

## Verify
- [ ] Complaint owned by name and handed over explicitly if needed.
- [ ] Guest offered a choice and given a time promise that was met.
- [ ] Compensation proportional and within empowerment limit, logged.
- [ ] Loop closed after the fix and at check-out.
- [ ] Profile updated; repeat themes reviewed weekly.
- [ ] Review reply specific, short, signed, non-defensive.

*Uncertain:* compensation tiers and loyalty obligations depend on brand standards.
