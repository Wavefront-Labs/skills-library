# Dialing in espresso — dose, ratio, time, taste

**Goal:** get a new coffee (or yesterday's coffee on today's weather) tasting good in
as few shots as possible, then keep it there through a shift.

## 1. Fix the recipe frame before touching the grinder
Pick three numbers and write them on the grinder:
- **Dose** — dry coffee in the basket. Match the basket's rating (an 18 g basket wants
  roughly 17.5–18.5 g). Weigh every shot while dialing in; scales to 0.1 g.
- **Ratio (brew ratio)** — beverage out : dose in, by weight. A common starting point
  is **1:2** (18 g in, 36 g out). Shorter (1:1–1:1.5, "ristretto") reads heavier and
  sweeter-sharper; longer (1:2.5–1:3, "lungo"/modern light-roast style) reads thinner
  and more transparent. Lighter roasts usually want longer ratios.
- **Target time** — roughly **25–32 s** from pump on is a typical window. Treat time as
  a *diagnostic*, not the goal: taste decides.

## 2. Move one variable at a time
With dose and ratio locked, **grind is your main dial**:
- Too fast (e.g. 36 g in 18 s) → grind finer.
- Too slow (36 g in 45 s, or drips) → grind coarser.
Make small steps, then **purge** the grinder before the test shot — on many grinders a
couple of grams of old grounds sit in the chute (retention) and the next shot is
half the old setting. Single-dose grinders retain less but still check.

## 3. Taste, then decide which way
| Taste | Likely cause | Move |
|---|---|---|
| Sour, salty, thin, finishes fast | Under-extracted | Finer grind, or longer ratio, or hotter water |
| Bitter, dry, ashy, hollow, astringent | Over-extracted (or channeling) | Coarser grind, or shorter ratio |
| Sour *and* bitter at once | Uneven extraction — channeling | Fix puck prep before changing grind |
| Flat, papery, muted | Stale coffee or too-old shot | Check roast date; drink within a minute |

Once the shot is in range, **ratio is a flavour dial** (longer = more clarity, less
body) and temperature is a fine-tune (±1 °C at a time, if your machine allows it).

## 4. Puck prep (why identical recipes still vary)
1. Dry, clean basket. Wet baskets channel.
2. Dose, then break clumps — a WDT tool (fine needles stirred through the bed) works.
3. Level the bed (tap or distributor), then **tamp level**, firm and consistent.
   Exact tamp force matters far less than levelness and repeatability.
4. Lock in and **pull immediately** — the puck heats on the group and pre-cooks.
Watch a bottomless portafilter: a centred, even stream is good; jets, blond spots and
spurts mean channeling.

## 5. Optional: measure
A coffee refractometer gives TDS; extraction yield ≈ TDS × beverage weight ÷ dose.
Many bars aim around 18–22% EY. Treat these as guide numbers — taste still wins, and
reading technique (filtering, temperature) changes results. *[Ranges are widely quoted
industry rules of thumb, not a standard; unconfirmed for every roast style.]*

## 6. Holding it through service
- Re-taste every hour or two; heat, humidity and the hopper emptying shift grind.
- As beans age past ~3–4 weeks from roast, expect to grind finer. Very fresh beans
  (under ~5–7 days) are gassy and erratic; rest them.
- One barista changes the grinder at a time, and says so.
- Log changes on the grinder sheet: date, bean, dose, yield, time, setting, note.

## Gotchas
- Changing dose *and* grind together tells you nothing.
- Volumetric buttons drift as grind changes; re-weigh after any grind move.
- Milk drinks hide sourness and highlight bitterness — dial on the straight shot
  but taste one milk drink too.
- Dirty group screens and shower spray cause channeling you can't grind away.

## Verify
- [ ] Dose, yield and time written down and weighed, not guessed.
- [ ] Each test changed exactly one variable, with a purge first.
- [ ] Final shot tastes neither sharply sour nor dry/bitter, and two consecutive shots
      land within ~2 s and ~1 g of each other.
- [ ] Bottomless pour shows no spurts or jets.
