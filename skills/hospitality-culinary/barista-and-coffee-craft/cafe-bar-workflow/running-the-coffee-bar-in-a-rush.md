# Running the coffee bar in a rush

**Goal:** keep every drink right and the queue moving during the peak, without the bar
falling apart behind you.

## 1. Before the rush (mise en place)
- Grinder dialled and tasted (see dialing-in-espresso); hoppers full; backup bags open-able.
- Milk fridge stocked by type, alt-milks labelled, spare jugs clean and cold.
- Cups stacked in reach by size, warm on the machine; lids, sleeves, sugar and napkins
  topped up away from the machine so customers don't crowd the barista.
- Clean, damp cloths assigned: one for the steam wand only, one for the bench, one dry for
  portafilters. Never cross-use the wand cloth.
- Knock box emptied.

## 2. Layout and station flow
Arrange things in drink order so hands move one direction: **order → cup → grinder →
group → milk → finish → handoff**. Cups and the ticket rail upstream; the handoff point
downstream, away from the order queue, so waiting customers don't block ordering.
Keep the grinder within one step of the group heads.

## 3. Sequencing (the core skill)
- Always have **something running**: shot pulling while milk steams while the next
  portafilter is being dosed.
- Read the **next 3–4 tickets** at once. Group drinks by milk: steam one larger jug for
  two lattes on the same milk rather than two small jugs.
- Start the **slowest element first**: shots go in, then milk, so both finish together.
- Black drinks (long black, espresso) and batch filter slot into gaps while milk spins.
- Never let shots sit waiting for milk for more than a few seconds — they flatten.
- **Batch-brewed filter** and **pre-prepared cold brew** carry load in a rush; check
  they're fresh (many bars discard batch filter after ~30–60 min held hot).

## 4. Two (or more) baristas
Fixed roles beat "everyone does everything":
- **Shots person:** grinds, doses, pulls, calls doses/times, manages the ticket order.
- **Milk person:** steams, pours, finishes, calls out.
Swap roles every so often. In three-person setups the third runs orders, filter,
cleaning, restock. Say out loud before you change the grinder.

## 5. Calling drinks
- Name the drink exactly as written plus the name or number: "Oat flat white for
  Sam." Repeat modifiers (decaf, extra shot, oat) — that's where errors hide.
- Use a marking system on cups or tickets (milk type, size, strength) and keep it
  identical across staff.
- When a drink is remade, the original customer gets the fixed one first.

## 6. Consistency across baristas
- Written **recipe cards** at the machine: dose, yield, time, milk volume, temperature,
  cup size per drink.
- Same cup, same mark lines for milk levels.
- Taste a drink from each barista during the shift; correct quietly and specifically.

## 7. Clean as you go
Purge and wipe the wand every jug; flush the group after every shot; knock out
immediately; wipe spills before they become sticky; rinse jugs between milks. A clean
bar is a fast bar — and shows the customer you care.

## 8. Rapport at the machine
Eye contact and a word while working ("two minutes on your cappuccino") buy patience.
If you're behind, say so honestly. Remember regulars' orders, but confirm — people
change.

## Gotchas
- Pre-grinding several doses "to save time" stales the coffee within minutes.
- Over-filling jugs to batch milk wrecks texture; two jugs are faster than one remake.
- Allergy orders: wipe, use the dedicated jug, and hand them over yourself.
- The rush ends; the mess doesn't — reset the bar before you relax.

## Verify
- [ ] Ticket times stay roughly steady through the peak (not growing with each order).
- [ ] No shots dying on the drip tray waiting for milk.
- [ ] Remakes/errors counted at the end of the rush and causes noted.
- [ ] Bar clean and restocked within 10 minutes after the rush ends.
