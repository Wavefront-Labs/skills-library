# Pour-over and immersion recipes

**Goal:** a repeatable filter recipe for any bean, and a quick way to adjust it when
the cup tastes wrong.

## 1. The constants
- **Ratio:** start at **1:16** (15 g coffee : 240 g water). Range ~1:15 (stronger)
  to 1:17 (lighter). Always weigh water, not volume.
- **Water temperature:** just off the boil (~92–96 °C) for light and medium roasts;
  cooler (~88–90 °C) for dark roasts, which extract easily and turn bitter.
- **Water itself:** this is the hidden variable. Very soft water extracts sharply
  and tastes thin-sour; hard, high-alkalinity water buffers acidity and makes coffee
  flat and chalky (and scales kettles). Filtered water of moderate hardness, low
  alkalinity, no chlorine taste, is the usual target. If a coffee tastes great at the
  roaster and dull at home, suspect water first. *[Specific mineral targets (e.g. SCA
  water standard) are published but vary by edition; check the current one.]*
- **Fresh grind**, just before brewing.

## 2. Pour-over (cone: V60-style; flat-bed: Kalita-style)
1. Rinse the paper filter with hot water (removes paper taste, warms the brewer);
   discard the rinse water.
2. Grind medium-fine (like coarse sand / table salt for a single cup cone).
3. **Bloom:** pour ~2–3× the coffee weight in water (30–45 g for 15 g), make sure all
   grounds are wet, wait 30–45 s. Fresh coffee puffs as CO₂ escapes.
4. Pour the rest in **2–4 pulses** in slow spirals, keeping the bed level and avoiding
   the paper walls. Finish pouring by ~1:45–2:00.
5. Give a gentle swirl after the last pour to flatten the bed.
6. **Drawdown** should finish around **2:45–3:30** total for a single cup. A flat bed
   after drawdown is good; a deep crater or grounds high on the walls = uneven pour.

## 3. Immersion
- **French press:** coarse grind, 1:15–1:16, steep 4 min, break the crust, skim the
  foam, wait another few minutes for fines to settle, then plunge only to the
  surface and pour gently. Decant fully — coffee left on grounds keeps extracting.
- **AeroPress:** very forgiving; e.g. 15 g, medium-fine, 220–250 g water, 1:30–2:00
  steep, gentle press ~30 s. Inverted or standard both fine.
- **Clever-style dripper:** immersion steep, then drains through paper — clean like a
  pour-over, simple like a press.
Immersion extracts more evenly; grind coarser than pour-over.

## 4. Adjusting by taste
| Cup tastes | Likely | Change |
|---|---|---|
| Sour, thin, short finish | Under-extracted | Finer grind / hotter / longer |
| Bitter, dry, harsh | Over-extracted | Coarser / cooler / shorter |
| Weak but balanced | Ratio too long | More coffee (1:15) |
| Heavy, muddy | Too strong or too many fines | Longer ratio, coarser, check grinder |
| Slow drawdown + bitter | Fines clogging filter | Coarser, gentler pour, less agitation |
Change **one thing per brew**, and note it.

## 5. Developing a recipe per bean
Brew at your default, then three cups: one finer, one coarser, one at a different
ratio. Taste side by side as they cool (acidity and defects show more at lower
temperature). Record bean, roast date, dose, water, grind setting, times, notes.
Light washed coffees often want finer/hotter; dense natural and dark roasts often want
coarser/cooler.

## Gotchas
- Grind settings aren't portable between grinders, or even two of the same model.
- Batch brewers and big Chemex-style batches need coarser grind than a single cup.
- Pouring onto the paper walls bypasses the bed and weakens the cup.
- Old paper filters / unrinsed paper add cardboard taste.

## Verify
- [ ] Coffee and water both weighed; ratio written down.
- [ ] Total time within the method's target window.
- [ ] Flat bed after drawdown (pour-over); no excessive sediment (immersion).
- [ ] Cup is sweet and clear with neither sharp sourness nor dry bitterness, warm and cool.
