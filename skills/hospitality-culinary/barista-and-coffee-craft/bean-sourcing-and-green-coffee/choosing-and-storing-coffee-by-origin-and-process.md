# Choosing and storing coffee by origin and process

**Goal:** buy coffees that will taste the way your menu promises, and keep them tasting
that way until they're used.

## 1. Processing drives flavour as much as origin
| Process | What happens | Typical cup |
|---|---|---|
| **Washed** | Fruit removed, beans fermented and washed before drying | Clean, bright, transparent acidity |
| **Natural (dry)** | Whole cherry dried on the bean | Fruity, jammy, heavier, sometimes winey/boozy |
| **Honey / pulped natural** | Skin removed, some mucilage left during drying | Between the two: sweet, rounder |
| **Wet-hulled** (common in Indonesia) | Hulled at high moisture | Earthy, herbal, heavy body, low acidity |
| **Anaerobic / co-ferments** | Controlled oxygen-free or additive fermentations | Intense, sometimes polarising; disclose added fruit/flavour if you know of it |
These are tendencies, not guarantees — producer skill dominates.

## 2. Origin as a starting expectation
Broad generalisations buyers use (many exceptions):
- **East Africa** (Ethiopia, Kenya): floral, citrus, berry; high, structured acidity.
- **Central America**: balanced, chocolate/caramel with fruit at higher altitude.
- **South America** (Brazil): nutty, chocolate, low acid, body — espresso-blend backbone;
  Colombia ranges widely.
- **Indonesia**: earthy, spicy, heavy (often wet-hulled).
Higher altitude generally means denser beans and more acidity. Varietal (e.g. Gesha,
SL28, Bourbon, Caturra) matters too; ask the importer.

## 3. Assessing green samples
1. Request **pre-shipment samples** (PSS) and cup them (see coffee-cupping skills).
2. Look at the green: uniform size and colour; count defects in a fixed weight
   (insect damage, black/sour beans, broken beans, foreign matter).
3. **Moisture content** ~10–12% is the commonly cited safe range; much higher risks
   mould, much lower ages fast. Water activity, if your importer reports it, is a
   better shelf-life indicator.
4. Ask the **crop year / harvest date** and when it landed. Green coffee fades over
   months — papery, woody, "past-crop" notes. Plan to roast most green within roughly
   a year of harvest. *[Shelf life depends heavily on packaging and storage.]*
5. Confirm the arrival sample matches the PSS before accepting.

## 4. Storing green
Cool, dry, dark, away from strong odours (green coffee absorbs smells). Hermetic
liners (GrainPro-style) or vacuum packing extend life. Keep off concrete floors; use
pallets. Rotate first-in, first-out.

## 5. Freshness of roasted coffee
- Print **roast date**, never just a best-before.
- Rest: filter ~2–10 days, espresso ~7–21 days off roast is a common window; very fresh
  coffee is gassy and uneven.
- Store in valve bags or airtight containers, away from light and heat. **Don't
  refrigerate** open bags (condensation, odours).
- **Freezing** whole beans in airtight, single-use portions works well for long holds;
  thaw sealed to avoid condensation, and don't refreeze.
- Grind only to order.

## 6. Seasonal menu rotation
Coffee is harvested seasonally per region, so the best fresh lots arrive at different
times through the year. Build the menu around arrivals: a seasonal single origin for
filter, and an espresso blend whose components you can swap as crops change while
keeping the target flavour (re-cup blends whenever a component changes).

## Gotchas
- A great sample roast doesn't guarantee your roaster/profile will show it.
- "Natural" coffees can mask defects behind fruit; cup carefully.
- Label allergens/additives honestly for flavoured or co-fermented lots.
- Price per kg green ignores weight lost in roasting (~15%) — cost on roasted weight.

## Verify
- [ ] Every coffee on the menu has origin, process, crop year and roast date recorded.
- [ ] Arrival samples cupped and matched to the approved PSS.
- [ ] Green moisture and storage conditions checked; FIFO in place.
- [ ] Roasted stock used within its freshness window; nothing open in the fridge.
