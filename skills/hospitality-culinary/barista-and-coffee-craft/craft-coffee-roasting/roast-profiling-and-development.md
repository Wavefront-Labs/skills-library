# Roast profiling and development

**Goal:** turn a green coffee into a roast that tastes how you intend, and do it again
next week on purpose rather than by luck.

## 1. Log everything
Run roasting software (Artisan is free and widely used; Cropster and others are
commercial) with bean-probe and exhaust/environment temperature. Every roast records:
green weight, moisture/density if known, charge temperature, gas/airflow changes,
turning point, yellowing, first crack start, drop time and temperature, roasted weight.
**Weight loss** (typically ~12–20% depending on roast degree) is a quick sanity check.

## 2. The phases (bean-probe readings vary by machine — use your own as relative markers)
1. **Charge** — beans drop into a preheated drum. Charge temperature depends on batch
   size and machine; a bigger batch needs more stored heat.
2. **Turning point** — probe temperature bottoms out and starts climbing (~1–1.5 min).
3. **Drying** — beans lose moisture and go from green to yellow; grassy smell becomes
   hay/bread.
4. **Maillard / browning** — yellow → tan → brown; bready, toasty, caramel aromas.
5. **First crack** — audible popping as steam and CO₂ rupture the cell structure.
   Mark the *first* clear, confident cracks, not the odd stray pop.
6. **Development** — first crack to drop. This is where acidity softens and sweetness
   and body build.
7. **Second crack** (if reached) — quieter, snapping, oily surfaces; dark-roast territory.

Typical total roast times for craft drum roasters run ~9–13 minutes. *[Wide range;
fluid-bed and very small sample roasters run faster.]*

## 3. Rate of rise (RoR)
RoR is the bean-probe temperature change per minute. The guiding shape most roasters
aim for is a **steadily declining RoR** from turning point to drop.
- **Flick** (RoR rises again just after first crack) and **crash** (RoR plunges toward
  zero) are associated with roasty/smoky and flat/baked flavours respectively.
- Moisture release at first crack makes RoR dip; anticipate by reducing gas *before*
  first crack rather than after.
Treat "smooth declining RoR" as a widely used heuristic, not a law — taste the result.

## 4. Development ratio
**Development time ratio (DTR)** = development time ÷ total roast time. Many light
filter roasts land roughly **15–20%**, espresso and medium roasts a bit higher.
Too short → grassy, sour, underdeveloped (grinds hard, pale centre). Too long at low
energy → baked: flat, papery, bready, no sweetness.

## 5. Common defects and their fixes
| Defect | Looks/tastes | Typical cause |
|---|---|---|
| Scorching | Burnt flat patches on bean face | Charge too hot / drum too slow |
| Tipping | Dark burnt bean ends | Too much heat early |
| Baked | Flat, cardboard, no sweetness | Stalled/crashing RoR, slow roast |
| Underdeveloped | Grassy, peanut, sour | Dropped too soon after first crack |
| Quakers | Pale beans that don't colour | Unripe cherries (green issue) — pick out |

## 6. Roast-to-brew feedback
1. **Rest** roasted coffee 24 h (filter) to ~5–7 days (espresso) before judging.
2. **Cup** every production roast against the reference/target roast (see
   coffee-cupping skills): same water, same grind, blind if possible.
3. Change one variable per roast (charge, a gas step, drop point) and record it.
4. Measure roast colour with a meter if available; it catches drift that eyes miss.

## Replicating a profile
Run a warm-up roast or a set preheat routine (first roast of the day runs differently
on a cold machine). Match charge temp and batch size, follow the reference curve with
the same gas/airflow steps at the same bean temperatures, and drop at the same
temperature *and* development time. Ambient temperature and green moisture shift the
curve; adjust gas, not the target.

## Safety
Chaff collectors and exhausts accumulate chaff and oils: clean on schedule, as chaff
fires are a real risk. Never leave a roaster unattended.

## Verify
- [ ] Every roast logged with phase markers and weight loss.
- [ ] RoR declines without large flick or crash.
- [ ] DTR and drop temperature within ±small tolerance of the reference.
- [ ] Rested coffee cupped against reference; difference noted and acted on.
