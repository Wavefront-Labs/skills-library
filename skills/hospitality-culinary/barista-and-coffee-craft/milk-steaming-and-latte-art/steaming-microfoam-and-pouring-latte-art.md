# Steaming microfoam and pouring latte art

**Goal:** silky, paint-like milk with no visible bubbles, at drinking temperature, in
the right volume for the drink — then a clean free-pour pattern.

## 1. Setup
- **Cold milk, cold jug.** Starting from fridge temperature (~3–5 °C) gives you time
  to texture. Fill the jug to just below the base of the spout (roughly a third full);
  more milk than that and there's no room to roll.
- **Purge the wand** before (water) and **wipe and purge** after (milk residue bakes on).
- Size the jug to the drink: ~350 ml jug for one small/medium drink, ~600 ml for two.

## 2. The two phases
1. **Stretching (adding air):** tip just below the surface, steam fully on. You should
   hear a gentle, regular *tss-tss* paper-tearing sound — not screaming (too deep)
   or big spluttering (too high). Lower the jug as the milk rises to keep the tip
   at the surface.
   - Flat white / latte: stretch briefly, until the volume grows ~10–20%.
   - Cappuccino: stretch longer, ~30–50% growth (style varies by bar).
   - Stop stretching early — by about body temperature (when the jug feels lukewarm).
     Air added late makes stiff, bubbly foam.
2. **Texturing (rolling):** sink the tip slightly and angle the jug so the milk spins
   in a whirlpool. The spin folds big bubbles into microfoam. Keep it spinning until
   temperature.

**Stop temperature:** about **60–65 °C** — the jug is too hot to hold comfortably for
more than a second or two. Above ~70 °C the milk tastes cooked and the foam breaks.
Use a thermometer until your hand is calibrated. "Extra hot" requests: go to ~70 °C
and warn that art suffers.

## 3. Finish before you pour
- Tap the jug once on the bench to pop surface bubbles, then **swirl** continuously
  so foam and milk stay integrated. Milk that sits separates in seconds.
- Shine like wet paint = good. Dull, thick, or visible bubbles = re-do or accept a
  plain pour.
- Pull the shot so it is ready *as* the milk finishes; crema dies quickly.

## 4. Free-pour basics
1. **Tilt the cup** ~30–45° toward you. Pour from **high** (~10 cm) in a thin stream
   into the centre to sink milk under the crema without marking it — fill about half.
2. **Drop the spout close** to the surface (almost touching) and **speed up the flow**.
   White foam now sits on top — this is where the pattern happens.
3. **Heart:** hold position until a white circle forms, let it grow, then lift and
   draw through the middle toward the far edge.
4. **Tulip:** push a small blob, stop, push the next into it (stacking), then pull
   through.
5. **Rosetta:** close and fast, wiggle the jug side to side while slowly backing up the
   cup; then lift and pull through.
Level the cup as it fills so the design spreads instead of sliding off.

## 5. Plant milks
- Use **barista editions** (they contain added fat/stabilisers/acidity regulators for
  foaming); standard cartons often won't hold foam.
- Oat steams well but foams *faster* — stretch less. Stop a bit cooler (~55–60 °C).
- Soy can **curdle** in very acidic or very hot coffee; pour slower and keep it cooler.
- Almond tends to thin, big-bubbled foam. *[Behaviour varies a lot by brand and
  formulation; test each new product.]*
- Keep separate jugs and cloths for allergen milks and label them.

## Gotchas
- Screaming wand = tip too deep, no air going in; big bubbles = too shallow.
- Steaming two drinks in one jug: split by pouring half into a second jug, swirling both.
- Re-steaming already-steamed milk gives poor foam and is a food-safety issue; steam
  fresh.
- Weak steam pressure (machine still recovering) stretches poorly — check the boiler
  gauge. See espresso-machines skills.

## Verify
- [ ] Milk is glossy, no visible bubbles, and pours like paint.
- [ ] Temperature lands in 60–65 °C (thermometer check at least weekly).
- [ ] Foam depth suits the drink (flat white thin, cappuccino noticeably deeper).
- [ ] Pattern is centred, symmetrical and has contrast against the crema.
- [ ] Wand purged and wiped after every jug.
