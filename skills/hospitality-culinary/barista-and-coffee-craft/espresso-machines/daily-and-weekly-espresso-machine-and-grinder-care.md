# Daily and weekly espresso machine and grinder care

**Goal:** consistent shots from equipment that doesn't fail mid-service, by doing the
boring maintenance on a schedule. Always follow the manufacturer's manual for your
model — the numbers below are typical, not universal.

## 1. Know your gauges
- **Steam boiler pressure** — commonly ~1.0–1.5 bar on commercial machines. Low = weak
  steaming; check after the machine recovers.
- **Brew pressure (pump gauge)** — typically set ~9 bar at the group during
  extraction. Machines with **pressure profiling** (paddles, programmable curves) let
  you ramp pre-infusion low and taper at the end; change one profile parameter at a
  time and taste, as with grind.
- **Brew temperature** — on PID/multi-boiler machines, typically ~92–96 °C at the group.
  Heat-exchanger machines need a **cooling flush** before a shot if idle. Calibrate with
  a group thermometer (e.g. a Scace-style device) if available.
If gauges read off-spec, call a technician — don't adjust pressure stats and safety
valves yourself.

## 2. Every shot / during service
- Flush the group a second or two before locking in (clears grounds, stabilises temp).
- Knock out, wipe the basket dry.
- Purge and wipe the steam wand after every jug.

## 3. End of day (daily)
1. **Backflush** each group with a blind (solid) basket:
   - Water-only backflush: several cycles of ~5–10 s on/off.
   - Then with **espresso machine detergent** in the blind basket (dose per the
     product), same cycles, then rinse cycles with water until no foam/odour.
   - Some manufacturers limit chemical backflushing (e.g. machines with certain
     group designs or rotary-pump settings) — check the manual. *[Frequency guidance
     varies by maker: daily chemical backflush is common practice but not universal.]*
2. **Soak portafilters and baskets** (not the plastic handles) in hot water with
   detergent; scrub, rinse.
3. **Wipe gaskets and screen** with a group brush.
4. **Steam wand**: soak the tip in a jug of hot water with milk-system cleaner if the
   holes look crusted; clear holes with a pin. Purge.
5. Drip tray out, washed, drain flushed with hot water.
6. Grinder: empty hopper (or leave per bar policy), wipe oils from hopper and chute,
   brush out the chute.

## 4. Weekly
- **Remove shower screens** and dispersion screws; soak and scrub. Screens clogged with
  oil cause channeling you'll mis-diagnose as grind.
- Inspect **group gaskets**: hard, cracked, or the portafilter swinging further past
  centre than it used to = replace (often every 6–12 months in a busy café).
- Grinder: run grinder-cleaning tablets/pellets through (food-safe products made for
  this), or open and brush burrs per the manual. Re-dial afterwards.

## 5. Water and scale
Scale is the main killer of machines. Use an appropriate **filter/softening system**
for your local water, change cartridges on the volume or time the maker specifies,
and test hardness. Descaling commercial boilers is usually a technician job — don't
put descaler through a machine not designed for it.

## 6. Grinder burrs
- Burrs dull with throughput; signs are slower dialling, more fines, muddier cups, a
  setting creeping finer over weeks.
- Typical replacement intervals are quoted in kilograms ground (often several hundred
  kg to well over 1,000 kg depending on burr size and material) — use the maker's figure.
- New burrs need a "seasoning" period; expect to re-dial heavily.
- Re-zero/calibrate after burr changes, never let burrs touch while running.

## Gotchas
- Chemical in a group that wasn't rinsed = soapy first shot. Always pull and dump one.
- Never backflush a portafilter that has a normal basket — it won't build pressure
  and sprays.
- Steam wands can scald; point away and purge into a cloth, not a hand.
- Turn machines off only per maker advice; many are designed to stay on overnight.

## Verify
- [ ] Gauges in the manual's range at open.
- [ ] Backflush water runs clear and odour-free; first shot tasted and normal.
- [ ] Screens, gaskets, wand tips visually clean; gaskets not hardened.
- [ ] Filter cartridge date/volume logged; burr kg tracked.
