# Picking, sorting and pressing grapes — the decisions you can't undo

**Goal:** bring in fruit at the ripeness your wine style needs, remove what doesn't belong,
and get juice or must into the tank without oxidation, bitterness or microbial head-starts.
For canopy work before harvest see agriculture-environment.horticulture.viticulture.canopy-management-and-harvest-timing.

## 1. The picking call
- **Sample properly:** 100–200 berries per block, from both sides of the row, top/middle/bottom
  of clusters, sun and shade. Picking whole "nice" clusters inflates sugar readings.
- Crush the sample, measure:
  - **Sugar** — °Brix (refractometer or hydrometer); potential alcohol ≈ Brix × 0.55–0.6 (varies with yeast and conditions).
  - **Titratable acidity** (TA, g/L as tartaric) and **pH** — pH matters more for stability;
    above ~3.6–3.7 for reds (3.4–3.5 for whites) wine is harder to protect.
- **Taste:** skins (tannin drying vs. ripe), seeds (green and bitter vs. brown and nutty), pulp
  (does it release from the seed). Sugar can race ahead of flavour in hot years; acid crashes fast in heat.
- Rough style windows (uncertain, varies by variety/region): sparkling base 17–20 °Brix;
  aromatic whites 20–23; full reds 23–26.
- Weather overrides charts: rain dilutes and rots; heat drops acid in days. Book the crew and the press.

## 2. Picking and transport
Pick cool (night or early morning), small bins (not crushed under their own weight), to the
winery fast. Warm, broken fruit starts oxidising and fermenting in the bin.

## 3. Sorting
Remove leaves, MOG (material other than grapes), rotten, raisined, unripe or bird-pecked
clusters. Sort clusters on a table before destemming; optionally sort berries after.
Grey rot (*Botrytis*) in red grapes gives laccase enzyme, which SO₂ doesn't stop — heavy rot
means faster processing and possibly shorter skin contact.

## 4. Destem and crush (or not)
- **Destem + crush:** standard for most reds; releases juice, starts extraction.
- **Destem, no crush:** whole berries → fruitier, partly carbonic character.
- **Whole cluster:** stems add structure, perfume, sometimes green notes; only if stems are
  lignified (brown) and the style wants it (classic in Pinot Noir, Syrah). Raises pH a little.
- **SO₂ at crush:** commonly 30–50 mg/L total for healthy fruit, more for damaged fruit; none
  for some natural-wine styles (higher risk). Add evenly as the must fills the tank.

## 5. Pressing
- **Whites/sparkling:** often whole-cluster press for low phenolics; pneumatic press program
  rising slowly in steps (e.g. 0.2 → 0.4 → 0.8 → 1.2 → up to ~2 bar) with rotations between.
  Champagne-style fractions: cuvée (first, finest) and taille (later, more phenolic).
- **Separate fractions:** free run / light press / hard press; taste and measure pH (it rises
  with pressure) and decide later where hard press goes.
- **Reds:** pressed after fermentation (see fermentation management); basket or pneumatic press, keep press wine separate.
- **Rosé:** short skin contact (a few hours) or direct press, colour by eye in a glass.

## 6. Juice settling (whites)
Chill to ~8–12 °C, settle 12–48 h (pectic enzyme helps), rack clear juice off the lees
("débourbage"). Aim for some turbidity (often ~50–250 NTU) — too clean can stick fermentations.

## Verify
- [ ] Sample method documented; Brix, TA, pH and tasting notes logged per block.
- [ ] Fruit arrived cool within hours of picking; MOG and rot sorted out.
- [ ] SO₂ (or deliberate no-SO₂) recorded; press program and fraction volumes logged.
- [ ] White juice settled and racked before inoculation.
