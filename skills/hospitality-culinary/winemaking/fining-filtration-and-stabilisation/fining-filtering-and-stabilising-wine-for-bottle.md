# Fining, filtering and stabilising wine for bottle — no haze, no crystals, no refermentation

**Goal:** a wine that stays clear and sound in the customer's fridge and cellar, with the
least stripping of flavour. Every step is chosen by a test, not by habit.

## 1. Fining (by bench trial only)
| Agent | Removes | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| Bentonite (clay) | Proteins (heat haze) | Whites, rosé |
| PVPP | Small phenolics, browning, bitterness | Whites, rosé |
| Egg white (albumen), gelatin | Harsh tannins | Reds |
| Isinglass, casein, pea/potato protein, chitosan | Clarity, phenolics, browning | Whites/reds |
| Copper sulfate (tiny) | H₂S | Reductive wines |
Run a trial series (see so2-acid-and-routine-wine-analysis for bench-trial method), wait 24–48 h,
taste blind against an untreated control; use the **lowest** dose that fixes the problem.
**Allergens:** egg, milk (casein) and fish (isinglass) derivatives trigger labelling rules in many
markets, and animal products rule out "vegan" claims — record what you used.

## 2. Protein (heat) stability — whites and rosé
Unstable proteins haze when the wine warms in transport.
- **Heat test:** filter samples (0.45 µm), heat to ~80 °C for 2 h (methods vary: some use 2–6 h),
  cool to room temperature, measure turbidity (NTU) against an unheated sample. A rise of more
  than ~2 NTU (common threshold) = unstable. Test each bentonite rate (e.g. 0–0.8 g/L in steps)
  and pick the lowest that passes.
- Bentonite lees take volume (a few % of the wine) — plan to rack or filter them out.

## 3. Tartrate (cold) stability
Potassium bitartrate crystals ("wine diamonds") form when wine gets cold.
- **Cold stabilisation:** chill to around −4 °C (rule of thumb: −(alcohol% ÷ 2) + 1 °C), hold ~1–3
  weeks, filter cold. Contact seeding with cream of tartar (~4 g/L, stirred, 2–4 °C) is faster.
- **Test:** conductivity test (chill with seeding; drop in conductivity under ~5% passes — lab thresholds vary)
  or a fridge/freezer test (sample at −4 °C for 3–6 days, look for crystals).
- **Additive route:** CMC (whites), potassium polyaspartate, mannoproteins, metatartaric acid
  (short-term only) inhibit crystal growth — less energy, check legality and labelling.
- Re-test after blending and after any acid adjustment — both upset stability.

## 4. Filtration
- **Depth filtration** (pads, lenticular cartridges): clarifying from coarse to fine grades;
  watch differential pressure, stop before pads blind and dump particles.
- **Crossflow:** one pass from rough to bright wine, less loss, high capital cost.
- **Membrane (final) filtration:** 0.45 µm (or 0.65 µm for some yeast-only targets) before bottling
  removes yeast and bacteria — essential for **wines with residual sugar or no MLF**.
  **Integrity test** (bubble point / diffusion) before and after each run; a membrane that fails
  after the run means the bottled wine is not assured sterile.
- Check **filterability** (a filterability index or Vmax test) before membrane work — unfined,
  colloid-rich wine blinds membranes fast.
- Many reds with no sugar and complete MLF are bottled **unfiltered** by choice; that's a style
  decision that relies on dryness, SO₂ and cleanliness.

## 5. Pre-bottling check list
Analyse the final tank: alcohol, RS (glucose + fructose), malic (MLF done?), free/total SO₂ and
molecular SO₂, pH, TA, VA, dissolved O₂ and CO₂, turbidity, heat and cold stability results,
and a microbiological plate or rapid test for sweet or unfiltered wines.

## Verify
- [ ] Each fining agent and dose chosen by a recorded bench trial; allergens logged.
- [ ] Heat test passes (NTU rise below threshold) for whites/rosé.
- [ ] Cold stability passed by conductivity or freezer test after the final blend.
- [ ] Membrane integrity test passed before and after filtration where used.
- [ ] Full pre-bottling panel recorded and signed off.
