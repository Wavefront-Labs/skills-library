# Running a wine bottling and tirage — the last chance to ruin a vintage

**Goal:** every bottle filled to height, closed tight, with minimal oxygen and zero microbial
contamination; and, for sparkling, a controlled second fermentation in the bottle.
Stability work comes first — see fining-filtering-and-stabilising-wine-for-bottle.

## 1. Before the day
- Pre-bottling analysis done and signed off; wine at bottling temperature (often ~15–20 °C — cold
  wine fills low and expands later; very warm wine loses SO₂ and aroma).
- Free SO₂ adjusted to target plus ~5–10 mg/L for bottling losses (uncertain; depends on the line).
- Dry goods checked: bottle neck dimensions match the closure, corks/capsules/labels/cartons
  counted with ~5% spare, label legal text (alcohol, allergens, sulphites, origin, lot) proofed.
- Mobile bottler booked? Agree who sanitises what, and the run order (driest, cleanest wines first).

## 2. Line hygiene
- Clean then sterilise every wine-contact surface from tank outlet to filler spouts and
  corker jaws: hot water ≥ ~80–85 °C at the outlet for ~20 min, or steam, or an approved
  chemical sanitiser with a potable rinse. Run membrane integrity test (if filtering in-line)
  before and after.
- Swab or plate-test filler spouts on sweet/unfiltered runs.

## 3. Oxygen control
- Purge receiving tank, lines and filler bowl with N₂/CO₂/argon; sparge empty bottles with
  inert gas before filling where the line allows; vacuum or inert-gas headspace before corking,
  or under-cap gas flush for screwcaps.
- Measure **dissolved O₂** in tank and in bottles from the start, middle and end of the run.
  A common target is DO pickup under ~0.5 mg/L and low total package oxygen (uncertain; set your own spec).
- Long stoppages: oxygen and temperature creep in the filler bowl — discard the first bottles after a restart.

## 4. Closures
| Closure | Oxygen ingress | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Natural cork | Variable bottle to bottle | TCA ("corked", musty) risk; buy screened corks |
| Technical/micro-agglomerated | Low, consistent | Many sold TCA-tested |
| Screwcap (Saranex liner) | Low | Fresh whites; tin liner = very low, may run reductive |
| Synthetic | Higher | Short-life wines |
For corks: vacuum corker or CO₂ flush, fill height leaves ~10–15 mm ullage below the cork at
20 °C; keep bottles **upright a few minutes to ~24 h** (pressure equalises) before laying down.

## 5. During the run
Check every ~15–30 min: fill height/volume (legal tolerance), closure insertion depth and
screwcap torque/thread roll, label placement, lot code legible. Pull and hold bottles that fail.
Record bottle counts and losses.

## 6. Traditional-method sparkling
1. Base wine: dry, clean, stable, moderate alcohol (~10.5–11.5%).
2. **Tirage:** add ~20–24 g/L sugar (≈ 5–6 atm of pressure; ~4 g/L ≈ 1 atm), selected yeast
   acclimatised to alcohol, riddling aid; fill heavy sparkling bottles and crown-cap.
3. Ferment ~12–15 °C, then age on lees (months to years) for autolytic character.
4. **Riddling (remuage):** rotate and tilt bottles over days/weeks (or gyropalette) until lees
   collect in the neck.
5. **Disgorging:** freeze the neck in brine/glycol, open, eject the ice plug; top with
   **dosage** (sugar-in-wine liqueur) to style — e.g. Brut usually < 12 g/L — and cork, wire.
- Pét-nat (ancestral) bottles mid-fermentation instead; risky — measure sugar precisely.

## Gotchas
- Never bottle wine with residual sugar or incomplete MLF without sterile filtration or a validated stabiliser.
- Over-pressurised sparkling bottles are hazardous: use rated bottles, safety glasses when disgorging.

## Verify
- [ ] Line sterilised and integrity tested; temperatures/times logged.
- [ ] DO measured start/middle/end; within spec.
- [ ] Fill height, closures and lot codes checked through the run; rejects counted.
- [ ] Sparkling: tirage sugar weighed; pressure checked on a test bottle with an aphrometer.
