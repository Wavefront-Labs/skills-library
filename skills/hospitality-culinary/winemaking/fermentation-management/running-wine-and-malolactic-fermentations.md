# Running wine and malolactic fermentations — steady to dry, then stable

**Goal:** take must to dryness (or the planned residual sugar) cleanly, extract what a red
needs, and finish malolactic conversion when the style calls for it — without H₂S,
volatile acidity or a stuck tank.

## 1. Yeast choice
- **Inoculated:** rehydrate dry yeast per maker (commonly ~10× weight of water at 35–40 °C with
  a rehydration nutrient, 15–20 min), then **temper** it by adding small amounts of must until it is
  within ~10 °C of the tank before pitching. Typical dose 20–30 g/hL. Predictable, strain-chosen traits.
- **Native/spontaneous:** relies on vineyard and cellar flora. More complexity possible; more
  risk of slow starts, VA and sticking. Use healthy fruit, moderate SO₂, keep it clean, and have
  a plan (a commercial strain on standby).

## 2. Nitrogen (YAN)
Measure yeast-assimilable nitrogen in the juice. Common targets ~150–250 mg N/L, more for high
sugar (uncertain; strain-dependent). Add complex nutrient and/or DAP **in steps** — e.g. at
inoculation and at ~1/3 sugar depletion. Avoid DAP late (after ~half dry): yeast can't use it
and it feeds spoilage organisms.

## 3. Temperature and the sugar curve
- **Whites/rosé:** ~12–18 °C for aromatics; cooler is slower and riskier to stick.
- **Reds:** peak ~25–30 °C for colour and tannin extraction; above ~32–35 °C yeast stress and die.
- Measure Brix/density and temperature **at least daily**; plot it. A steady fall of ~1–3 °Brix per
  day is normal. A flattening curve before dryness is the earliest warning of a stick.

## 4. Cap management (reds)
CO₂ pushes skins up into a cap that dries, heats and can grow acetic bacteria.
- **Punch-downs** (pigeage): gentle, small tanks, 2–3× a day.
- **Pump-overs** (remontage): spray juice over the cap, 1–2 tank volumes a day early, less late;
  open pump-overs add oxygen (good early, helps yeast).
- **Délestage** (rack and return): drain juice, let cap settle, return — big extraction.
- Ease off as the wine nears dryness to avoid harsh tannins. Press at dryness or after
  **extended maceration** (days–weeks) for softer, more integrated tannin; taste daily.

## 5. Trouble
- **H₂S (rotten egg)** during fermentation: usually nitrogen stress — add nutrient (before
  half dry), splash with an open pump-over. After fermentation, bench-trial a copper addition
  at the smallest effective dose (copper residue limits apply).
- **Stuck fermentation:** check temperature, measure YAN, test for VA; rack off gross lees, warm to
  ~20 °C, then build a restart culture of a robust strain (e.g. a bayanus-type) acclimatised
  stepwise to the stuck wine.
- **VA** (vinegar, nail polish) creeping up: keep caps wet, tanks topped, SO₂ ready once done.

## 6. Malolactic fermentation (MLF)
*Oenococcus oeni* converts sharp malic acid to softer lactic acid; standard for most reds and
buttery Chardonnay; often blocked in crisp aromatic whites.
- Conditions: pH > ~3.2, temperature ~18–22 °C, **low SO₂** (keep free SO₂ near zero, total
  low), alcohol < ~15%. Inoculate during late primary or after it.
- Track with **paper chromatography** or enzymatic malic test; done when malic < ~0.1–0.3 g/L.
- Then add SO₂ promptly to protect the wine (see cellar chemistry).
- Diacetyl (butter) comes from MLF; less if wine stays on lees with yeast after MLF.

## Verify
- [ ] YAN measured; nutrient additions logged with timing.
- [ ] Daily Brix and temperature plot with no unexplained flattening.
- [ ] Reds: cap never left dry; pressing decision noted with tasting notes.
- [ ] Dryness confirmed (glucose + fructose < ~2 g/L) and MLF complete by test, then SO₂ added.
