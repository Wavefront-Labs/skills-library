# Costing and packaging a group itinerary

**Goal:** turn a route idea into a trip that is enjoyable to travel, operationally
realistic, and priced so it makes money at the group size you can actually sell.
(For a personal trip plan, see business-finance.personal-travel-planning.itinerary-planning.day-by-day-itinerary-and-pacing.)

## 1. Route and pacing
- **Anchor the "hero" experiences first** (the thing in the brochure headline), then route
  around them to avoid backtracking.
- **Daily driving limits:** coach groups tire beyond ~4–5 h on the road per day; insert a
  comfort/photo stop every ~2 h. Drivers' legal hours (e.g. EU tachograph rules) cap the
  day too — check the operator's limits before you promise a sunrise start after a late
  dinner.
- **Two-night stays** at least every few days; one-nighters back-to-back exhaust guests
  and cost time in check-in/out and luggage handling.
- Sequence attractions by opening hours, closure days (many museums close Mondays),
  crowd peaks, and walking intensity — put the strenuous day early, not after a long transfer.
- Build in **free time** (a half-day per 4–5 days) — it lowers costs and raises satisfaction.
- Check seasonality: festivals, holidays, weather, and attraction closures for your dates.

## 2. Costing sheet
Split every cost into two buckets:
- **Fixed (per group):** coach and driver, guide/tour leader fees and expenses, driver
  accommodation and meals, parking/tolls/permits, group admission flat fees.
- **Variable (per person):** hotel room share, meals, entrance tickets, per-head activities,
  insurance, local taxes.

Per-person cost at group size *n*:

`cost_pp = variable_pp + (fixed_total / n)`

Build the sheet at several sizes (e.g. 15, 20, 25, 30, 35) — the per-person price drops
as fixed costs spread.

## 3. Price, markup and break-even
- **Markup vs margin:** a 25 % markup on cost is only a 20 % margin on price. State which.
  `price = cost / (1 − target_margin)`.
- **Minimum numbers:** pick the smallest group size at which the selling price still covers
  cost; publish it in terms and conditions with a cancellation deadline (e.g. 6–8 weeks
  before departure).
- **FOCs (free of charge places):** hotels and suppliers often give 1 free place per 20–25
  paying guests. Use it to cover the tour leader; don't price it in until the contract says so.
- **Single supplement:** room cost difference for single occupancy plus your markup —
  priced separately.
- **Agent commission:** if selling through agents, price must absorb their 10–15 % (varies).

## 4. Contingencies baked into price
- **Currency buffer:** cost in local currency at a cautious exchange rate (e.g. 3–5 % worse
  than today), or hedge.
- **Fuel/supplier surcharge** clause where the market allows it.
- Small contingency line (2–5 %) for price rises between costing and operation.
- Tickets that need pre-purchase, non-refundable deposits and their deadlines go into a
  **supplier payment calendar**.

## 5. Packaging the product
Write the itinerary day by day with: overnight location, meals included (B/L/D),
transfer times, activity level, what's optional (and its price). List inclusions and
exclusions explicitly. Check legal duties — e.g. under package travel rules (EU Package
Travel Directive, UK PTRs) the organiser carries liability and must offer insolvency
protection. *Uncertain: obligations vary by market; confirm before selling.*

## Gotchas
- Forgetting driver/guide meals and rooms is the classic costing hole.
- Hotel tax per person per night is often excluded from the rate.
- A route that "fits" on a map ignores border waits, ferries and city traffic — add buffers.

## Verify
- [ ] No day exceeds driving limits; hero sights placed with opening days checked.
- [ ] Every cost tagged fixed or variable; per-person cost computed at 4+ group sizes.
- [ ] Margin, minimum numbers and cancellation deadline stated.
- [ ] Currency and contingency buffers applied.
- [ ] Inclusions/exclusions and legal package obligations covered in the written product.
