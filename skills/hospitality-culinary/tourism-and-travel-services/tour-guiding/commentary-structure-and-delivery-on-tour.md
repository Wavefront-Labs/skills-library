# Commentary structure and delivery on tour

**Goal:** commentary that guests remember and repeat — built around a theme, delivered so
everyone can see and hear, timed to the route, and flexible enough to handle questions.

## 1. Build the tour around a theme
Facts don't stick; meaning does (the core idea of Freeman Tilden's heritage-
interpretation principles and Sam Ham's thematic interpretation).
- Write one **theme sentence** for the whole tour — a claim, not a topic. "The river made
  this city rich and then nearly destroyed it" beats "History of the river".
- Each stop is evidence for the theme. If a fact doesn't serve it, drop it or save it for questions.
- Collect **human stories**: a named person, a decision, a consequence. Verify them —
  repeated myths (the "rule of thumb" etc.) get guides embarrassed by informed guests.
  Flag legends as legends.

## 2. The stop structure (~3–7 min each)
1. **Position the group** (see below) and wait until they're settled.
2. **Hook:** a question, surprise or "look at the third window up…" — direct their eyes first.
3. **Story/explanation** linked to what they can see.
4. **Tie back** to the theme in one line.
5. **Bridge** to the next stop ("…which is why the merchants built what we'll see next").
Then *move* — long stops kill energy and pacing. Aim for more stops with less talk.

## 3. Positioning (walking tours)
- Stand so **the group faces away from the sun, traffic and distractions** and you face
  them; the sight is behind you or to the side, so point, don't turn your back.
- Gather them off the footpath — never block entrances or crossings.
- Speak to the back row. Count and look for stragglers before you begin.
- Large groups (>20) or noisy places: use a **whisper/radio headset system**; check
  batteries and channel before departure and collect receivers at the end.

## 4. Coach commentary
- Hold the mic close (2–5 cm) and slightly below the mouth; turn the volume down rather
  than shouting; mute when not speaking.
- **Talk in front of the sight:** "In about 30 seconds, on the left…" — at speed you have
  only seconds. Say left/right relative to the guests' seats.
- Alternate talk with silence and music; continuous commentary for hours exhausts people.
- Safety first: stay seated/belted where the law requires; never stand at the front while
  the coach moves if regulations forbid it.

## 5. Delivery
- Vary pace and pitch; pause before the punchline.
- Use numbers people can picture ("as tall as 12 double-decker buses") rather than raw stats.
- Adjust language and humour to the group: children, international guests with
  non-native English (slower, simpler words, avoid idioms), specialists.
- Invite participation: "Who can spot the date on the gable?"

## 6. Questions
- Repeat the question so the whole group hears it.
- If you don't know: say so, offer to find out, and follow up by the next stop or by email.
- Defer long or niche questions to the walking leg so the group isn't held.
- Contentious topics (politics, religion, recent conflict): present perspectives, keep it
  factual, don't push your own view.

## Gotchas
- Many cities require **licensed guides** for certain sites or for guiding at all —
  check local rules. *Uncertain: varies widely by country and site.*
- Practise the route at the tour's real time of day (crowds, closures, shade).
- Running out of time at the end means you overtalked early; time each stop in rehearsal.

## Verify
- [ ] Tour has a one-sentence theme; every stop links back to it.
- [ ] Each stop follows hook → story → tie-back → bridge, within ~7 min.
- [ ] Group positioned so all can see and hear; headsets checked if used.
- [ ] Coach cues given before the sight appears.
- [ ] Stories fact-checked; unknown answers followed up.
