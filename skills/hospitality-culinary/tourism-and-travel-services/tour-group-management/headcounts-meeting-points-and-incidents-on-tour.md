# Headcounts, meeting points and incidents on tour

**Goal:** never leave anyone behind, keep a mixed group moving together without anyone
feeling rushed or held back, and respond calmly and correctly when something goes wrong.

## 1. Headcount discipline
- Know your number — **write it on your hand or the run sheet** and update it if anyone
  joins or leaves.
- Count at **every** departure: coach, site exit, restaurant, boat. Count heads, not "it looks full".
- On the coach: count walking down the aisle, or use a seat-plan (same seats each day
  speeds this up); for larger groups, two leaders count independently.
- Short-cut method: ask everyone to check that their neighbour from the morning is present
  (**buddy system**). Useful backup, never a substitute for your own count.
- Say "We're all here, thank you" so the group learns the count matters.

## 2. Meeting-point briefings
Every time the group splits, brief **what / where / when / what if**:
- "We meet **here**, at the fountain by the blue door, at **14:30** — that's 45 minutes."
- Point at it, and ask them to photograph it.
- "If you get lost, come back here; I wait until 14:45, then I'll call you." 
- Hand out a card on day one with your mobile, the hotel name/address in local script,
  and the coach number. Collect guests' mobile numbers into a group chat if they agree.
- Be at the meeting point **5 min early**. Lateness rules (how long you'll wait) are set in
  the day-one briefing and applied consistently.

## 3. Pacing mixed abilities
- Ask on day one (privately) about mobility, health, dietary needs. Plan walking routes with
  benches and lifts; know where the coach can drop near the entrance.
- Walk at the pace of the slowest third, and put a **back marker** (co-leader or trusted
  guest) at the rear on walks.
- Offer optional shortcuts: "Those who'd like to rest, the café is here; I'll collect you at 11."
- Watch for heat, dehydration and fatigue; schedule water and seat breaks.

## 4. Conflict and difficult behaviour
- Address issues **early, privately and briefly**; don't argue in front of the group.
- Listen, acknowledge, state what you can do. Separate the person from the behaviour.
- Common flashpoints: seat hogging (rotate seats daily), chronic lateness (apply the rule
  you announced), complaints about rooms (solve with the hotel, then report back).
- Alcohol or harassment: keep others safe first; involve operator duty manager; document.

## 5. Incident response
**Lost guest:** check last seen time/place; call their mobile; send someone to the
meeting point; inform the hotel and the coach; after an agreed time (e.g. 60 min, or
immediately for a vulnerable guest or child), contact local police and your operator.
Never leave the rest of the group unsupervised to search.

**Illness/injury:** ensure scene safety, call local emergency number, give first aid within
your training. Collect the guest's insurance/assistance details (from the day-one
manifest); decide who accompanies them; keep the rest of the programme running if safe.

**Afterwards:** write an **incident report** the same day — time, place, what happened,
actions, witnesses, contacts made. Notify the operator per its procedure. Keep guest
medical details confidential.

## Gotchas
- Most "lost guest" incidents happen at toilets, shops and photo stops — recount after those.
- Don't promise refunds or compensation on the spot; escalate to the operator.
- Know the local emergency number (112 in the EU, 911 in North America, 999 in the UK, etc.).

## Verify
- [ ] Group number known; counted at every departure.
- [ ] Every split had a what/where/when/what-if briefing.
- [ ] Mobility and medical needs known and reflected in pacing.
- [ ] Conflicts handled privately; rules applied consistently.
- [ ] Every incident logged in writing the same day and escalated per procedure.
