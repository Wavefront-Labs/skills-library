# Tempering chocolate and making ganache bonbons

**Goal:** chocolate that sets glossy, snaps cleanly and releases from moulds, filled with
a smooth ganache that stays good for weeks.

## 1. Why temper
Cocoa butter can crystallise in six forms; only **form V (beta)** gives gloss, snap and
contraction (release from moulds). Tempering melts all crystals, then builds just form V.
Untempered chocolate sets soft, dull, streaky (fat bloom) and sticks in moulds.

## 2. Temperature curves (typical — use the maker's curve on the bag)
| Chocolate | Melt to | Cool to | Work at |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dark | 45–50 °C | 27–28 °C | 31–32 °C |
| Milk | 40–45 °C | 26–27 °C | 29–30 °C |
| White | 40 °C | 25–26 °C | 28–29 °C |
Brand curves differ by a degree or two; the bag's numbers win.

## 3. Methods
**Seeding (most reliable at small scale):**
1. Melt two-thirds of the chocolate to the melt temperature (microwave in short bursts,
   stirring; or bain-marie with no steam reaching it).
2. Add the remaining third as chopped tempered chocolate/callets, stir until it reaches
   working temperature. Remove unmelted seed.
**Tabling:** pour two-thirds of the melted chocolate onto marble, spread and gather with
a scraper and palette until it thickens (reaches cool temperature), return to the rest
and stir to working temperature.
**Cocoa butter powder (e.g. Mycryo):** melt, cool to ~34–35 °C, add ~1 % of the weight
as powder, stir and cool to working temperature.

## 4. Test the temper
Dip a knife tip or strip of acetate; leave at ~20 °C. **Tempered = sets within ~3–5 min,
glossy, even, snaps.** Streaky or soft after 5+ min = not tempered — reheat and repeat.
Keep the bowl in temper while working by short heat bursts; overcrystallised (thick)
chocolate gives thick shells and dull finish — warm slightly.

## 5. Ganache
- Basic ratios by weight (chocolate:cream): **dark 1:1** for soft fillings, ~2:1 for
  firmer bonbon/truffle centres; milk and white need more chocolate (~2.5:1 to 3:1).
- Pour hot (not boiling) cream over chopped chocolate, wait 1 min, **emulsify from the
  centre** with a spatula or a stick blender kept submerged (no air). Add butter at
  ~35 °C, liqueur after.
- **Split ganache** (greasy, grainy): add a little warm cream or milk and blend.
- **Shelf life** depends on water activity (aw): less water, more sugar/invert sugar
  (glucose, invert, sorbitol) lower aw. Typical artisan ganache ~2–4 weeks; only claim
  longer with aw testing. Exact aw targets are recipe-specific — not confirmed here.

## 6. Moulded bonbons
1. **Moulds clean and polished** with cotton wool (no fingerprints); at ~20–22 °C.
2. Optional colour: tempered coloured cocoa butter (~29–31 °C) brushed/sprayed, set.
3. **Shell:** fill with tempered chocolate, tap out bubbles, invert and drain, scrape
   flat; set.
4. **Fill** with ganache at **≤ ~28–29 °C** (warmer melts the shell), leaving ~2 mm
   below the rim. Crystallise ~12–24 h at ~16–18 °C.
5. **Cap** with tempered chocolate, scrape clean with a transfer sheet or acetate.
6. Set, then release by turning out and a firm tap. If they won't release: chocolate
   was untempered or the mould too warm.

## 7. Environment and storage
- Work room ~18–21 °C, low humidity (< ~55 %).
- Store finished chocolate **~12–18 °C**, dry, away from odours. Fridge storage risks
  **sugar bloom** (condensation dissolves sugar, leaving a grey dusty film).
- **Fat bloom** (greasy grey streaks) = poor temper or temperature swings.

## Gotchas
- Any water/steam seizes melted chocolate thick and grainy.
- Compound/coating chocolate (vegetable fat) doesn't need tempering and doesn't behave
  like couverture.
- Thick shells are an overcrystallisation symptom, not a feature.

## Verify
- [ ] Temper test set glossy within ~5 min at room temperature.
- [ ] Bonbons released cleanly with a mirror shine and no cracks.
- [ ] Ganache was smooth (not split) and at ≤ 29 °C when filled.
- [ ] Storage temperature logged; no bloom after a week.
