# Building a plated dessert with contrast

**Goal:** a dessert that tastes balanced (not just sweet), has something crisp, creamy,
cold and bright on every forkful, and can be sent in under two minutes during service.

## 1. Design around one idea
- **Pick one star** (a fruit in season, a chocolate, a nut) and no more than two
  supporting flavours. Four or five competing flavours read as muddled.
- **Contrast checklist** — aim for most of these on the plate:
  - **Temperature:** something cold (ice cream/sorbet/granita) against something warm
    or room temperature (a warm cake, a sauce).
  - **Texture:** crisp/crunchy (tuile, crumble, sablé, nougatine, praline) + creamy
    (crémeux, mousse, cream) + something juicy or soft (fresh or poached fruit, gel).
  - **Acid and bitterness:** citrus, yoghurt, crème fraîche, passion fruit, cocoa nibs,
    coffee — cut sweetness. A dessert without acid tastes heavy.
  - **Salt:** a pinch in caramels, crumbles, chocolate elements lifts flavour.
- **Sweetness overall lower** than a standalone pastry — guests have just eaten a meal.

## 2. Components and how far ahead
| Component | Make ahead | Holds |
|---|---|---|
| Crumbles, tuiles, sablés | same day / 1–2 days airtight with desiccant | humidity softens |
| Crémeux, curds, set creams | 1–2 days chilled | in piping bags |
| Mousses, entremets | 1 day, or frozen | |
| Gels (agar fluid gel, pectin) | 2–3 days chilled | blend agar gel smooth after setting |
| Ice cream/sorbet | churned daily or held at service temp | |
| Sauces (coulis, caramel, anglaise) | 1–3 days | warm caramel in bain-marie |
| Fresh fruit | cut to order or within the hour | oxidises |

**Agar fluid gel:** agar at roughly 0.5–1 % of liquid, boiled 1–2 min, set, then blended
to a smooth, pipeable gel. Concentrations vary by brand.

## 3. Build the plate (à la minute)
1. **Plates:** chilled for frozen/creamy desserts; room temperature or warm for warm
   desserts with ice cream (plate the warm and cold elements apart, so the ice cream
   doesn't melt in a puddle).
2. Anchor element first (piped crémeux, cake slice, tart) — it sets the composition.
3. Sauce and gels next, in dots or a swipe.
4. Fruit and crisp elements placed so they don't sit in liquid.
5. **Crumble/crumb as an anti-slip bed under the frozen element** — it keeps the quenelle
   from sliding and adds crunch.
6. **Frozen element last**, just before the runner takes it:
   - **Quenelle:** dip a spoon in hot water, shake dry, drag across firm ice cream toward
     you in one motion; the spoon's heat releases a smooth three-sided oval.
   - Ice cream held around −12 to −14 °C quenelles best; colder is too hard.
7. Final garnish (herbs, tuile shard, zest) and a rim wipe.

## 4. Service flow
- Pastry station set like a line: chilled drawer with piping bags, crisps in airtight
  tubs at hand, ice cream in a dipping cabinet at service temperature.
- Pick up desserts when mains are cleared, on expo's call; build in a row like the hot
  line (see hospitality-culinary.culinary-arts.plating-and-presentation).
- Keep a **spec photo** for each dessert.

## Gotchas
- Crisp elements go soft within minutes on wet components — add at the very end, or
  put a barrier (chocolate, cocoa butter spray) on them.
- Too many elements slow service; if a dessert takes over 2 minutes to plate, simplify.
- Gelatin-set elements melt under warm lights; agar holds to ~80 °C.
- Allergens accumulate fast in desserts (nuts, gluten, dairy, egg) — keep an allergen
  matrix per component, and have at least one dessert free of the main allergens.

## Verify
- [ ] Tasted on one spoon: sweet, acid, creamy, crisp and cold all present.
- [ ] Crisp element still crunchy when it reaches the table.
- [ ] Quenelle holds its shape for ≥ 2 min on the plate.
- [ ] Plating time from fire to pass under ~2 minutes.
- [ ] Allergen matrix covers every component.
