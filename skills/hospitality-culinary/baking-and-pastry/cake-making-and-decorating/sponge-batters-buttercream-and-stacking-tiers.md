# Sponge batters, buttercream and stacking tiers

**Goal:** level, moist layers that carve cleanly, a straight-sided sharp-edged finish,
and a tiered cake that arrives standing.

## 1. Batters by mixing method
- **Creaming** (butter cakes, Victoria sponge): cream room-temperature butter (~18–20 °C)
  and sugar 3–5 min until pale and fluffy; add eggs gradually (curdling = eggs too cold
  or added too fast; a spoon of flour rescues it); fold in flour last, briefly.
- **Reverse creaming:** mix butter into flour/sugar first, then liquids — finer, tighter,
  more level crumb that carves and stacks well; good for wedding tiers.
- **Foaming** (génoise, chiffon, Swiss roll): whisk eggs and sugar (warm over a bain-marie
  to ~40–45 °C for génoise) to the ribbon stage — trail holds 3–5 s on the surface.
  Fold flour in gently in 2–3 additions; fold melted butter into a little batter first.
- **All-in-one:** everything at once with a raising agent; fast, fine for traybakes.

## 2. Baking
- Tins greased and lined; fill ~½–⅔ full. Weigh batter between tins for equal layers.
- Around **160–180 °C** for most cakes (lower and longer for deep tiers; baking strips or
  a heating core help large tins bake level).
- Done: skewer comes out clean or with a few moist crumbs, sponge springs back, edges
  just pulling from the tin; internal ~96–99 °C for butter cakes is a useful check.
- Cool in tin 10 min then on a rack. Wrap and chill or freeze — chilled cake carves and
  fills more cleanly.

## 3. Level, torte, fill
1. Level with a serrated knife or cake leveller using the tin rim as a guide.
2. Torte (split) into even layers.
3. Brush with **simple syrup** (1:1 sugar:water, flavoured) for moistness — sparingly.
4. Pipe a **dam** of stiff buttercream around the edge before soft fillings (jam, curd)
   so they don't bulge out.
5. Stack on a board with a dab of buttercream to stick; check level with a spirit level.

## 4. Buttercreams
| Type | Method | Character |
|---|---|---|
| American | butter + icing sugar + splash of milk | sweet, crusts, easy |
| Swiss meringue (SMBC) | whites + sugar heated to ~70 °C, whipped to stiff cool meringue, butter added | silky, stable, less sweet |
| Italian meringue | sugar syrup at ~118–121 °C streamed into whipping whites, then butter | most stable in warmth |

- SMBC/IMBC "soupy" = too warm (chill 10 min, re-whip); "curdled" = too cold (warm the
  bowl edge, keep whipping).

## 5. Finishing smooth
1. **Crumb coat:** thin layer to trap crumbs; chill until firm (~20–30 min).
2. **Final coat:** generous layer, then a bench scraper held vertical against the cake on
   a turntable, rotating steadily. Fill gaps, scrape again.
3. **Sharp edge:** chill, then pull the top "lip" inward with an offset spatula.
4. **Fondant/sugarpaste:** over a firm buttercream or ganache coat. Roll ~4–5 mm, lift,
   smooth the top then the sides with smoothers, flaring the skirt out to avoid pleats.
   Ganache under fondant gives the sharpest edges and holds in warm rooms.

## 6. Tiering
- Each tier on its own **cake board** the same size as the cake.
- **Dowel** every tier that carries weight: insert a dowel, mark at the icing surface,
  remove, cut all dowels (typically 4–6 per tier, more for heavy tiers) to that length,
  reinsert. Dowels, not cake, carry the load.
- Stack chilled tiers; a central dowel through all tiers into the base board for
  transport.
- **Transport** tiers separately for tall cakes, assemble on site; car floor, flat,
  non-slip mat, air-conditioned.

## Gotchas
- Domed cakes: oven too hot or too much raising agent.
- Bulging sides a day later: filling without a dam, or cake settling — let a filled cake
  "settle" under light weight a few hours before the final coat.
- Fresh flowers: food-safe only, stems wrapped or in posy picks — many are toxic.

## Verify
- [ ] Layers weighed equal and level within ~2 mm.
- [ ] Sides vertical, top edge sharp, no crumbs in the final coat.
- [ ] Each carried tier has dowels cut to an identical length.
- [ ] Cake stands level on a spirit level before leaving.
