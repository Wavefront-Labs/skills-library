# Sourdough schedule, shaping and proof judgement

**Goal:** a loaf with good oven spring, an open even crumb and a bold crust — by
reading the dough, not the clock. Yeasted doughs follow the same logic, faster.

## 1. Starter (levain) health
- Feed at a fixed ratio, e.g. **1:5:5** (starter:flour:water by weight) for a ~12 h
  cycle at ~24 °C; 1:2:2 for ~4–6 h. Ratios and timings shift with temperature.
- **Use at peak:** doubled-plus, domed or just starting to fall, smells sour-sweet, a
  spoon of it floats (float test is a hint, not proof).
- Sluggish starter? Warmer spot, more frequent feeds, a little wholegrain rye.

## 2. Mixing and desired dough temperature (DDT)
- Target dough at roughly **24–26 °C** for most sourdough.
- **Water temp = (DDT × 3) − flour temp − room temp − friction factor** (friction 0 for
  hand mixing, ~5–15 °C for machines — measure your mixer). Use ×4 with preferment
  temperature included, if you count the levain.
- **Autolyse** (flour + water, 20–60 min) before adding levain and salt eases gluten
  development. Salt ~2 % of flour.
- For hydration, percentages and scaling, see
  hospitality-culinary.baking-and-pastry.baking-formulation.

## 3. Bulk fermentation (the step that decides the loaf)
- **Folds:** 3–4 sets of stretch-and-folds or coil folds, ~30 min apart in the first
  2 h, until the dough holds its shape and is smooth.
- **Judge by volume and feel, not time.** End bulk at roughly **50–75 % rise** (use a
  straight-sided, marked container), domed edges, bubbles on top and sides, jiggly,
  airy when you tilt the container. At 24 °C this is often 4–6 h; colder takes longer.
- Underfermented → tight, gummy crumb with big isolated holes. Overfermented → slack,
  sticky, spreads flat, sour.

## 4. Divide, pre-shape, shape
1. Turn out gently; divide by weight.
2. **Pre-shape** into a loose round; rest 20–30 min uncovered until it relaxes.
3. **Shape** with surface tension: for a boule, drag the dough towards you on an
   unfloured patch of bench so the skin tightens; for a bâtard, fold top third down,
   sides in, roll up and seal the seam.
4. Into a floured **banneton** (rice flour sticks least), seam side up.

## 5. Proof and the poke test
- **Cold retard:** 8–16 h at ~3–5 °C develops flavour and makes scoring easy.
- **Poke test** (lightly floured finger, ~1 cm):
  - Springs back immediately → underproofed; give more time.
  - Springs back slowly, leaving a small dent → ready.
  - Doesn't spring back → overproofed; bake now, expect less spring.
- Cold dough reads slower — calibrate the poke test for retarded dough over a few bakes.

## 6. Score, steam, bake
- Preheat oven (and Dutch oven or stone) **45–60 min**, ~250 °C.
- **Score** with a lame at ~30–45° to the surface, one confident stroke ~6–12 mm deep,
  for an "ear". A vertical cut opens symmetrically without an ear.
- **Steam** for the first ~15–20 min: lidded Dutch oven, or deck-oven steam injection,
  or a tray of boiling water/ice cubes in a home oven (careful of glass doors). Steam
  keeps the crust soft so the loaf can expand.
- Then vent/uncover, drop to ~230 °C, bake to a deep brown — often 20–25 min more.
- Done: hollow knock, **internal ~96–98 °C**.
- **Cool ≥ 1 h** before cutting; the crumb is still setting.

## Gotchas
- A flat loaf is usually over-proof or weak shaping, not "bad flour".
- Seam-side scoring or dull blades drag the dough.
- Wholegrain flours ferment faster and absorb more water.

## Verify
- [ ] Starter peaked and used within its window.
- [ ] Dough temperature recorded within ±1 °C of DDT.
- [ ] Bulk ended at a measured rise, not a time.
- [ ] Poke test logged before baking.
- [ ] Internal temp ≥ 96 °C; crumb cut after cooling shows even open structure.
