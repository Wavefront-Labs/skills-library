# Sugar stages, caramel and pulled sugar

**Goal:** hit the exact sugar stage the recipe needs, keep syrups from seizing into
crystals, and handle very hot sugar without getting burnt.

## Safety first
Cooked sugar at 150–170 °C sticks to skin and keeps burning. Keep a **bowl of iced water**
next to you to plunge hands into, wear long sleeves, don't taste from the pan, keep
children and pets away, and **never add cold liquid to caramel at arm's length over the
pan** — it erupts. Use a deep pan.

## 1. Sugar stages (sea level; drop ~1 °C per ~300 m altitude)
| Stage | Temperature | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| Thread | 106–112 °C | syrups, glazes |
| Soft ball | 112–116 °C | fudge, fondant, Italian meringue (~118–121) |
| Firm ball | 118–120 °C | caramels (soft) |
| Hard ball | 121–130 °C | nougat, marshmallow, gummies |
| Soft crack | 132–143 °C | toffee, butterscotch |
| Hard crack | 146–155 °C | brittles, lollipops, pulled/blown sugar |
| Caramel | 160–180 °C | light to dark caramel |
Cold-water test: drop syrup into iced water and feel the result, as a cross-check on
the thermometer. Calibrate the thermometer in boiling water (100 °C at sea level).

## 2. Stopping crystallisation
- **Clean pan, clean sugar.** Wash down the sides with a wet pastry brush while boiling,
  or cover with a lid for a minute so steam washes them.
- **Don't stir** once boiling (wet method) — swirl the pan.
- **Interfering agents:** glucose syrup (~10–20 % of sugar), invert sugar, or a few
  drops of acid (lemon, cream of tartar) block crystals forming.
- Fudge and fondant *want* fine crystals: cook, cool undisturbed to ~45–50 °C, then beat.

## 3. Caramel
- **Wet caramel:** sugar + some water, boil to colour. More forgiving, slower.
- **Dry caramel:** sugar in a dry pan, a layer at a time, melting each before adding
  more; stir gently once melted. Faster, deeper flavour, burns quickly.
- Stop the cook: off heat, add warm cream/butter **gradually**, standing back; or dip the
  pan base in cold water.
- Colour judges flavour: amber ~170 °C; past ~185 °C it's bitter. Lift it off the heat
  a shade early — residual heat keeps cooking.

## 4. Confections
- **Soft caramels:** sugar, glucose, cream, butter to ~118–122 °C; pour into a framed
  tray, cut when set. Each degree firms the chew.
- **Toffee/butter crunch:** to ~145–150 °C.
- **Nougat:** honey + sugar/glucose syrups (~120 °C and ~140–150 °C variants depending
  on recipe) streamed into whipped whites, then nuts; press between wafer paper.
- **Marshmallow:** syrup ~120 °C into gelatin (or whites), whip until thick and lukewarm.
- Humidity ruins hard candy: store airtight with desiccant.

## 5. Pulled and blown sugar
- Many professionals use **isomalt** (less sticky, less browning, more humidity-tolerant)
  or a sugar/glucose/water recipe with a touch of tartaric acid, cooked to ~155–165 °C.
- Pour onto a silicone mat; fold edges in as it cools until it can be handled at
  ~50–55 °C (wear nitrile or sugar gloves).
- **Satin:** pull and fold repeatedly under a sugar lamp until glossy and pearlescent —
  stop before it turns dull and grainy.
- Keep the working mass under the lamp; pull petals, ribbons, leaves from the edge.
- **Blown sugar:** a ball of satin sugar on a hand pump; inflate slowly, cooling with a
  fan to set the shape.
- Assemble pieces by heating contact points with a torch or alcohol burner.
- Display in low humidity; sugar showpieces soften and sag above ~50–60 % RH.

## Gotchas
- Grainy caramel: undissolved crystals on pan sides or a stirred syrup.
- Thermometer touching the pan base reads high.
- Burnt sugar can't be fixed; start again.

## Verify
- [ ] Thermometer calibrated; target stage reached within ±1 °C (with cold-water cross-check).
- [ ] Caramel smooth, no crystals, colour stopped at intended shade.
- [ ] Hard candies stay non-sticky in their storage container after 48 h.
- [ ] Pulled sugar shows a satin sheen; iced water bowl was in reach throughout.
