# Croissant lamination and fold schedules

**Goal:** hundreds of distinct, unbroken butter and dough layers that puff into an open
honeycomb crumb with a shattering crust.

## 1. The rule that matters most: matched plasticity
Butter and dough must bend the same way at the same temperature.
- **Butter** should be pliable but cold — roughly **12–16 °C** for lamination. Too cold:
  it shatters into shards (visible streaks, layers break). Too warm: it melts into the
  dough (layers merge, butter leaks in the oven).
- **Dough** chilled to a similar firmness (typically ~2–6 °C straight from the fridge,
  warming as you work).
- Use **high-fat "dry" butter** (82–84 % fat, often sold as lamination/pastry butter);
  standard butter has more water and less plasticity.
- Room should be cool (< ~22 °C). In warm kitchens, work fast and rest more.

## 2. The détrempe (dough)
- Typical croissant dough: strong flour, ~50–55 % water/milk, 10–12 % sugar, 2 % salt,
  3–4 % fresh yeast (or ~1–1.5 % instant), a little butter. Percentages vary by recipe.
- **Mix to moderate development only** — overmixed dough fights back during sheeting.
- Flatten to a rectangle, wrap, chill overnight or at least a few hours (retarding
  also builds flavour).

## 3. Butter block and lock-in
1. Pound/roll butter between parchment into a precise square or rectangle (e.g.
   ~25–30 % of dough weight for croissant).
2. **Lock-in:** dough rolled to twice the butter's area; enclose the butter (envelope
   or French/English methods), sealing edges with no air pockets.
3. Ends trimmed square — square corners keep layers even.

## 4. Fold schedules and layers
| Fold | How | Multiplies layers by |
|---|---|---|
| **Single / letter fold** | fold in thirds | 3 |
| **Double / book fold** | ends to centre, then fold in half | 4 |

Common croissant schedules: **3 single folds** (27 butter layers before shaping) or
**1 double + 1 single** (12). Puff pastry classically has **6 single folds** (729).
More folds is not better for croissants: layers get too thin and merge, and the crumb
becomes bready.
- **Rest/chill 20–30 min** between folds (wrapped, in the fridge or freezer briefly) to
  relax gluten and recool butter.
- Brush off excess flour before every fold.
- Turn the dough 90° each fold so you roll alternately along each axis.
- Mark the dough (finger dents) with the number of folds done.

## 5. Sheeting and shaping
- Sheet to ~3.5–4 mm (sheeter or rolling pin in stages, not all at once).
- Croissants: isosceles triangles (e.g. 9–10 cm base × 25 cm high), notch the base,
  roll without pressing — press and you weld layers.
- Cut with a sharp knife or wheel; a blunt cut seals the edges and they won't rise.

## 6. Proof and bake
- **Proof warm and humid, but below butter's melting point:** ~24–27 °C, ~75–80 %
  humidity, 1.5–3 h. Butter melting in the proofer = greasy, flat croissants.
- Ready when roughly doubled, wobbly when the tray is shaken, layers visible at the
  sides.
- Egg wash gently (don't glue the cut layers); a second wash just before baking for gloss.
- Bake ~190–200 °C (convection lower, e.g. 170–180 °C) ~15–20 min, to a deep brown.

## Gotchas
- Butter leaking during the bake: underproofed, proofer too warm, or dough torn.
- Dense, bready crumb: too many folds, butter too soft, or underproofed.
- Shrinking pieces after cutting: gluten not relaxed — rest longer before cutting.

## Verify
- [ ] Butter and dough temperatures checked before lock-in (butter ~12–16 °C).
- [ ] Cross-section of a cut edge shows clean, separate layers, no butter streaks.
- [ ] Fold count recorded on the dough.
- [ ] Proof judged by jiggle and visible layers, proofer below ~28 °C.
- [ ] Baked crumb shows an open honeycomb; no butter pool on the tray.
