# Ice-cream base balancing, churning and overrun

**Goal:** a smooth, scoopable frozen dessert with small ice crystals, the intended
sweetness and a consistent overrun — batch after batch.

## 1. What a base is made of (typical target ranges)
| Component | Ice cream / gelato | Sorbet |
|---|---|---|
| Fat | ~6–10 % gelato, 10–16 % ice cream | 0 % |
| Milk solids non-fat (MSNF) | ~8–11 % | — |
| Sugars | ~16–22 % | ~22–30 % |
| Total solids | ~36–42 % | ~28–34 % |
| Stabiliser | ~0.2–0.5 % | ~0.2–0.5 % |
These ranges are widely used starting points and vary by school (Italian gelato vs
American ice cream). A balancing spreadsheet is the normal professional tool.

- **Too little solids** → icy and hard. **Too much** sugar or fat → soft, greasy or
  sticky; too much MSNF → sandy (lactose crystals).
- **Sorbet:** a refractometer reading of roughly **25–30 °Brix** for most fruit sorbets
  is a quick check; adjust with syrup or water.

## 2. Sugar: sweetness vs softness
Each sugar has a **sweetening power (POD)** and an **anti-freezing power (PAC)**
relative to sucrose (= 100):
| Sugar | POD | PAC |
|---|---|---|
| Sucrose | 100 | 100 |
| Dextrose | ~70–75 | ~180–190 |
| Invert sugar | ~125–130 | ~190 |
| Glucose syrup DE 40 (solids) | ~45–50 | ~80–90 |
Swapping some sucrose for **dextrose softens without adding sweetness**; glucose adds
body. PAC targets depend on serving temperature (higher PAC for colder cabinets). Values
cited vary between sources — treat these as approximate.

## 3. Making the base
1. Weigh all ingredients (grams).
2. Mix stabiliser **with some of the sugar first** so it disperses without lumps.
3. **Pasteurise:** e.g. 85 °C for a few seconds or ~65 °C for 30 min (custard bases with
   yolk usually to ~82–85 °C); follow local dairy rules — some jurisdictions regulate
   this for retail.
4. Cool fast to ≤ 4 °C.
5. **Age 4–12 h** chilled: fat crystallises, proteins and stabiliser hydrate — smoother,
   better overrun.
6. Blend (stick blender) before churning.

## 4. Churning and overrun
- Batch freezer: run until the mix reaches roughly **−6 to −8 °C** and holds a soft
  ribbon. Faster freezing = smaller crystals.
- **Overrun** = air incorporated.
  **Overrun % = (weight of a cup of mix − weight of same cup of ice cream) ÷ weight of
  ice cream × 100.**
  Typical: gelato ~20–35 %, premium ice cream ~25–60 %, economy up to ~100 %.
- Don't over-churn; fat churns to butter and the texture turns greasy.

## 5. Hardening and serving
- Extract into pre-chilled containers; **harden quickly** in a blast freezer
  (~−30 to −40 °C) or the coldest freezer. Slow hardening grows crystals.
- Store ≤ −18 °C (lower is better).
- **Serve:** ice cream ~−12 to −14 °C; gelato often ~−11 to −13 °C. Temper storage tubs
  in the dipping cabinet before service.
- **Heat shock** (repeated warming/refreezing) causes iciness — keep cabinets steady,
  lids on.
- Pacojet: freeze base solid at −22 °C in the beaker, spin to order; balance for that
  temperature.

## 6. Inclusions and alcohol
- Alcohol lowers freezing point strongly; keep spirits low (a few %) or it won't set.
- Add inclusions (chopped chocolate, nuts, ripples) at the end of churning; coat nuts in
  fat/chocolate so they stay crisp.

## Gotchas
- Icy texture usually means low solids, slow freezing or heat shock — not too little fat.
- Fruit varies in sugar; measure Brix each batch for sorbet.
- Allergens: milk, egg, nuts, plus stabilisers listed on labels.

## Verify
- [ ] Base balanced on a spreadsheet within target ranges, recipe weights recorded.
- [ ] Pasteurisation and cool-down temperatures logged; base aged ≥ 4 h.
- [ ] Overrun measured and within ±5 % of target.
- [ ] Scoops cleanly at the cabinet's serving temperature; no ice crystals on the tongue.
