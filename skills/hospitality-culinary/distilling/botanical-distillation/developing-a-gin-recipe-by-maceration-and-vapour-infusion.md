# Developing a gin recipe by maceration and vapour infusion

**Goal:** a gin with a clear identity that tastes the same batch after batch —
juniper first, then a deliberate supporting cast — made reproducibly at scale.

**Legal:** gin definitions (e.g. EU/UK "distilled gin", "London dry gin";
US "distilled gin") set rules on juniper predominance, minimum ABV and what may be
added after distillation. Check the category you're labelling for.
Licensed operation required.

## 1. The botanical bill (structure)
A classic framework:
- **Juniper** — the backbone, typically the largest single share by weight
  (often 40–60%+ of the bill).
- **Coriander seed** — citrus/spice lift; usually second.
- **Angelica root** — earthy, dry, helps "fix" volatile aromatics.
- **Orris root** — floral, a fixative; small amounts.
- **Citrus peel** (dried lemon, orange, grapefruit) — top notes.
- **Spice/accents** — cassia, cardamom, liquorice, pepper, local botanicals.
Total loads are commonly quoted in the ~15–40 g of botanicals per litre of charge
range (at ~40–60% ABV charge) — **wide variation; establish yours by trial.**

## 2. Test with single-botanical distillates first
Distil each botanical alone in a small lab still (or macerate in neutral spirit and
redistil): the same spirit, same ABV, same g/L. Then blend the distillates at
small scale to find ratios fast. Rough small-scale blends translate better to the
production still than guessing ratios straight in the pot.

## 3. Maceration vs vapour infusion
- **Maceration (steep):** botanicals soaked in the charge (neutral spirit diluted to
  ~40–60% ABV) for a period from hours to ~24–48 hours, then distilled with them
  in the pot. Gives fuller, heavier, rooty character. Long steeps can extract bitterness.
- **Vapour infusion (basket/Carterhead):** botanicals sit in a basket in the vapour
  path; lighter, fresher, more delicate notes (good for citrus and florals).
- **Hybrid:** heavier roots/spices macerated, delicate citrus/florals in the basket.
- **One-shot** (recipe scaled to the batch, bottled after dilution) vs **multi-shot**
  (concentrated distillation, diluted with neutral spirit after) — multi-shot is
  efficient but some category definitions restrict it.

## 4. The run
- Use clean neutral spirit (NGS, ~96%) diluted for the charge; low-quality NGS
  shows through.
- Discard a small foreshots fraction; heads are usually short.
- Hearts carry the juniper and citrus early; as the run falls, heavier root and
  spice notes and then oily, stewed tails arrive. Cut tails earlier for a brighter gin.
- Record ABV and nose every fraction (see running-a-pot-still-and-making-heads-hearts-tails-cuts).

## 5. Proofing and cloudiness (louching)
Botanical oils (especially from citrus and juniper) are less soluble at lower ABV.
Diluting a heavily oiled hearts cut to bottling strength (~37.5–47%) can make it
**cloudy**. Fixes: cut tails earlier, reduce oil-heavy botanicals, dilute slowly
with soft/demineralised water, rest, chill-filter (see blending-and-proofing).
Test-dilute a sample before diluting the batch.

## 6. Scaling and consistency
- Weigh botanicals per litre of charge at a fixed charge ABV; keep lot numbers.
- Botanicals vary by harvest — **sensory-check each new lot** against the reference.
- Keep a reference sample of each batch.

## Gotchas
- Too many botanicals → muddy, nothing distinct.
- Over-long maceration of roots/spice → bitterness.
- Fresh citrus in the pot "cooks" — stewed marmalade notes.
- Cloudiness discovered only after bottling.
- Labelling "London dry" while adding flavouring after distillation.

## Verify
- [ ] Category rules for the label checked (juniper predominance, ABV, additions).
- [ ] Single-botanical distillates tested and blend ratios set at small scale.
- [ ] Bill weighed in g/L at a fixed charge ABV; lots recorded.
- [ ] Method chosen per botanical (macerate vs basket) and documented.
- [ ] Sample test-diluted to bottling strength — clear, or louche accepted deliberately.
- [ ] New botanical lots checked against reference before production.

*Uncertain:* g/L ranges and maceration times are indicative craft figures, not
standards; calibrate on your still.
