# Nosing new-make and detecting spirit faults

**Goal:** catch a fault at the still, cask or vatting stage — while it's still
fixable — and describe spirit in words the whole team uses the same way.

## 1. Conditions
- **Glass:** tulip-shaped nosing glass (copita, Glencairn-style) — the narrow rim
  concentrates aroma. Same glass every time.
- **Strength:** dilute to ~20–23% ABV with still, neutral water (taint-free) for
  most assessment; full-strength nosing numbs the nose quickly and masks faults.
  Some houses also nose at a standard reference strength (e.g. 20%) for every sample.
- **Volume:** a consistent small amount (e.g. 20–30 ml) so headspace is comparable.
- **Environment:** quiet, odour-free room, no perfume or coffee; same time of day
  (late morning is often recommended). Take breaks — fatigue sets in after a dozen
  samples.
- Cover glasses with a watch glass/lid between noses.
- Taste only per your company's rules; spit; keep water and plain crackers.

## 2. Method
1. **Look:** clarity, colour (for cask samples), any haze.
2. **Nose** — short gentle sniffs, mouth slightly open; first impression, then after
   swirling, then after a minute. Note intensity, then descriptors.
3. **Taste** (if permitted): texture, sweetness, bitterness, heat, finish length.
4. **Record** on a standard sheet using a shared **flavour wheel** vocabulary
   (e.g. fruity/estery, floral, cereal, feinty, sulphury, woody, peaty). Score
   intensity on a fixed scale, not adjectives only.
5. Compare against the **reference sample** side by side, blind where possible.

## 3. New-make assessment
New-make should taste like the house spirit: clean, with the intended character
(fruity, cereal, heavy/oily). Check each spirit run's hearts and compare with the
reference new-make. Drift usually means a cut-point, fermentation or still change.

## 4. Common faults and likely causes
| Fault | Smells like | Likely cause |
|---|---|---|
| **Sulphury** (meaty, rubber, cooked veg, struck match) | Rotten egg → cabbage | Yeast stress, too little copper contact, fast distillation; may fade in active casks |
| **Feinty / tails** | Wet cardboard, leather, sweaty, oily | Late tails cut, tails smearing into hearts |
| **Solvent / estery-harsh** | Nail-polish, glue | Early heads cut, heads smearing, high-ester infected wash |
| **Butyric** | Baby sick, rancid butter | Bacterial infection in fermentation |
| **Acetic** | Vinegar | Infected or old wash |
| **Burnt / scorched** | Burnt toast, rubber | Solids scorched on still; direct-fire too hot |
| **Musty / mouldy** | Damp cellar, wet cardboard | TCA-type taints (corks, wood, water), mouldy grain or warehouse |
| **Metallic** | Coins | Equipment contact, water |
| **Papery / stale** | Cardboard, flat | Oxidation in partly empty containers, filter pads |
Descriptions are for training; use reference standards (spiked samples) to
calibrate the panel.

## 5. Maturation checks
On cask samples, track colour, oak intensity, vanilla/spice, fruit, tannin dryness,
and whether new-make sulphur has disappeared. Flag casks that are leaky, musty or
over-oaked early (see choosing-casks-and-managing-spirit-maturation).

## 6. Running a small panel
- 3–6 trained assessors, independent scores (no discussion first).
- **Triangle test** (two same, one different) to check whether a change is
  detectable.
- Regularly retrain with spiked reference samples so everyone names a fault the
  same way.

## Gotchas
- Nosing at full strength — everything smells like ethanol.
- Different glasses or volumes across samples.
- One person deciding alone, unblinded, at the end of a long day.
- Mistaking young-spirit sulphur (often fades) for a permanent fault — note it and
  re-check.

## Verify
- [ ] Same glass, volume and ~20% dilution for every sample.
- [ ] Notes use the shared vocabulary and intensity scale.
- [ ] Each sample compared to the reference, blind where possible.
- [ ] Any fault named and traced to a probable cause with an action.
- [ ] Panel retrained with reference standards on a schedule.

*Uncertain:* fault causes are typical associations; confirm with lab analysis where
it matters.
