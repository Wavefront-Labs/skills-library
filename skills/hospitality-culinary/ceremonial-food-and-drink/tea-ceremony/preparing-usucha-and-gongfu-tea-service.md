# Preparing usucha and gongfu tea service

**Goal:** serve tea in a formal tradition with correct preparation and respectful,
recognisable form — while being honest that full ceremonial procedure (*temae*) is
learned from a teacher in a specific school.

**Uncertainty note:** Japanese tea procedure differs between schools (Urasenke,
Omotesenke, Mushakōjisenke and others) in hand order, wiping and bow. What follows is a
simplified common core, not any school's formal temae. Don't present it as such.

## Part A — Usucha (thin tea), Japanese style

### Utensils
*Chawan* (bowl), *chasen* (bamboo whisk), *chashaku* (bamboo scoop), *natsume* (tea
caddy), *fukusa* (silk cloth for ritual purification of utensils), *kama* or kettle,
*hishaku* (ladle), *kensui* (waste-water bowl), *chakin* (linen cloth). Sweets
(*wagashi*) served before the tea.

### Preparation (simplified)
1. **Fresh, sifted matcha** — sift through a fine sieve to remove lumps. Matcha stales
   quickly once opened; keep chilled, airtight, use within weeks.
2. **Warm the bowl** with hot water, soften the whisk tines in it ("*chasen tōshi*") and
   inspect for broken tines; discard water, dry the bowl with the chakin.
3. **Tea:** about **2 heaped chashaku scoops (~1.5–2 g)** for usucha.
4. **Water:** ~60–70 ml at roughly **70–80 °C**. Boiling water makes it bitter. (Koicha,
   thick tea, uses roughly 3–4 g per guest and much less water, kneaded rather than
   whisked — taught separately.)
5. **Whisk** with a fast M- or W-shaped motion from the wrist, whisk tines just touching
   the bottom, ~15–20 s, until a fine, even foam covers the surface. Urasenke prefers
   abundant foam; Omotesenke less — another school difference.
6. Lift the whisk from the centre, leaving a small mound of foam.
7. **Present** the bowl with its "front" (the most decorated side) facing the guest.

### Guest etiquette (widely shared)
- Eat the sweet before the tea arrives.
- Bow and say to the next guest "*o-saki ni*" ("excuse me for going before you").
- Take the bowl in the right hand, place on the left palm, **turn it clockwise in two small turns**
  so you don't drink from the front (how far, and the direction, vary by school — confirm locally).
- Drink in a few sips, finishing with an audible slurp of the last; wipe the rim with
  fingers, wipe fingers on *kaishi* paper; turn the bowl back so the front faces the host.
- Admire the bowl, low, with elbows on knees.

## Part B — Gongfu cha (Chinese tea service)

### Equipment
Gaiwan (lidded bowl) or small clay teapot (Yixing pots are often dedicated to one tea
type); **cha hai** (fairness pitcher); small cups; tea tray with drainage; strainer;
kettle with temperature control; tea pick/tongs.

### Method
1. **Warm everything** with boiling water: pot/gaiwan, pitcher, cups. Discard.
2. **Leaf:** roughly **5–8 g per 100 ml** of vessel — far more than Western brewing.
3. **Rinse/awaken** (common for oolong and pu-erh): cover with hot water, discard after
   a few seconds. Many skip it for green and delicate teas.
4. **Water temperatures (typical):** green ~75–85 °C; white ~85–90 °C; rolled oolong
   ~90–95 °C; dark oolong, pu-erh ~95–100 °C.
5. **Steep times:** start short — **~10–20 s** — and lengthen each infusion by ~5–10 s.
   Good oolong or pu-erh gives 6–10+ infusions.
6. Decant **fully** into the cha hai (no water left on the leaves), then pour into
   cups — everyone gets the same strength.
7. Serve cups with both hands; guests may tap two fingers on the table as thanks (a
   southern Chinese custom, not universal).

## Gotchas
- A clumped matcha bowl almost always means unsifted tea or too-cool water.
- Broken whisk tines scratch bowls; soak the chasen before use, dry upright on a holder.
- Over-steeping gongfu tea in the first round ruins the whole session.

## Verify
- [ ] Matcha sifted; water temperature measured before pouring.
- [ ] Usucha has an even, fine foam with no lumps when the bowl is tilted.
- [ ] You can name which school's form you followed or say it's a simplified core.
- [ ] Gongfu infusions logged with times; each cup from the pitcher matches in colour.
