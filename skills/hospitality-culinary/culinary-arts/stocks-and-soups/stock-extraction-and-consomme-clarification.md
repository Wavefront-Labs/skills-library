# Stock extraction and consommé clarification

**Goal:** clear, well-flavoured stock with body from gelatin, a sparkling consommé, and
soups that taste seasoned rather than salted — cooled safely.

## 1. Stock fundamentals
- **Start cold.** Bones in cold water, bring slowly to a bare simmer. Proteins coagulate
  gradually and rise as scum you can skim. A rolling boil emulsifies fat and protein
  into the liquid: cloudy, greasy stock.
- **Skim** often in the first 30–60 minutes; ladle from the edge.
- **Never stir** a stock once it's simmering; it clouds.
- Add mirepoix (onion:carrot:celery roughly 2:1:1 by weight; white mirepoix with leek
  and no carrot for white stocks) and a bouquet garni partway through for long stocks,
  so the veg doesn't turn to mush and bitterness.
- **Typical simmer times** (kitchen rules of thumb, not hard limits):
  - Fish fumet: 20–30 min (longer turns bitter/chalky from bones).
  - Chicken: 3–4 h.
  - Veal/beef: 6–8 h or more.
  - Vegetable: 45–60 min.
- **Brown stock:** roast bones well (deep brown, ~200–220 °C), add tomato paste and roast
  briefly (pinçage), deglaze the roasting tray into the pot. Burnt bits make bitter stock.
- Bone choice: knuckles, feet, wings and necks give gelatin. Good stock **sets to a
  jelly** when cold.
- **Remouillage:** second extraction from the same bones; weaker, good for braising liquid.
- Season stock lightly or not at all — it'll be reduced later.

## 2. Consommé clarification (classic raft)
1. Start with **cold, fat-free, well-flavoured stock**.
2. **Clearmeat:** minced lean meat (or fish for fish consommé) + egg whites + finely
   chopped mirepoix + tomato (acid helps) — mixed and chilled. A common ratio is roughly
   **1 egg white and ~100 g mince per litre** of stock; recipes vary widely.
3. Whisk clearmeat into the cold stock. Heat slowly, **stirring gently until ~50–60 °C**,
   then stop stirring for good.
4. The proteins coagulate and rise into a **raft**, trapping particles. Make a small
   hole (ladle-sized) to let it simmer through and to ladle from.
5. Barely simmer 45–90 min. Bubbling hard breaks the raft — start again.
6. Ladle out through the hole into a chinois lined with wet muslin/coffee filter.
   Don't press the raft.
7. Degrease completely (blot with paper or chill and lift fat). Season now.

Alternative: **gelatin (freeze-thaw) clarification** — set the stock with gelatin, freeze,
thaw slowly in a cloth over a container in the fridge; the clear liquid drips through.
Yields lighter body; useful when you don't want meaty clearmeat flavour.

## 3. Soup composition
- **Puréed soups:** sweat aromatics without colour, add main veg and stock, cook until
  fully tender, blend (lid vented, towel over the top — hot liquid blows lids off),
  pass through a sieve for restaurant smoothness. Adjust body with stock, not water.
- **Cream/velouté soups:** finish with cream or a liaison (yolk + cream); don't boil
  after the liaison or it curdles.
- **Broth soups:** cook components separately when they need different times; add to
  hot broth to order so noodles and greens don't bloat.
- Season in layers and finish with acid (lemon, vinegar) and fresh herbs.

## 4. Cooling — where kitchens get inspected
- Typical two-stage target (US FDA Food Code): **57 → 21 °C within 2 h, then to ≤ 5 °C
  within a further 4 h** (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked). The FDA Food Code is a PDF that could not be read as text, and only secondary state-agency summaries were found, which describe 135 °F to 70 °F in 2 h with a 6 h total to 41 °F.
  UK (gov.uk): food kept below 63 °C for up to 2 h must then be cooled as quickly as possible to 8 °C or lower, or discarded.
  Check your jurisdiction and HACCP plan.
- Methods: split into shallow containers, ice wand / ice bath with stirring, blast
  chiller. **Never** put a large stockpot of hot stock straight in the walk-in.
- Label with date, time and initials and keep to your HACCP plan's shelf life.

## Verify
- [ ] Chilled stock sets to a gel and has a thin, easily lifted fat layer.
- [ ] Stock is clear enough to see the bottom of a white ladle.
- [ ] Consommé shows no cloudiness when held against light; no fat droplets on top.
- [ ] Puréed soup coats a spoon evenly with no fibres after passing.
- [ ] Cooling log shows each stage met within the time limits.
