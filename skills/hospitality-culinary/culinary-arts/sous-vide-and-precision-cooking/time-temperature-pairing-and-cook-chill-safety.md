# Time-temperature pairing and cook-chill safety (sous vide)

**Goal:** exact, edge-to-edge doneness *and* a documented safety margin. Sous vide moves
food through the danger zone slowly and seals it airless — the conditions some
pathogens like — so the safety half is not optional.

## 1. The two clocks
1. **Time to core temperature** — depends mostly on **thickness** (roughly with the
   square of thickness): doubling thickness roughly quadruples heating time.
2. **Time held at temperature** — what pasteurises (kills enough pathogens) and, for
   tough cuts, what tenderises.

**Lethality is time × temperature.** UK gov.uk guidance gives these equivalent core-temperature
treatments: 70 °C for 2 min is standard advice, and 60 °C for 45 min, 65 °C for 10 min, 75 °C for 30 s or 80 °C for 6 s are alternatives.
US safe minimums are 165 °F / 74 °C for poultry and 145 °F / 63 °C with a 3 min rest for whole pork (foodsafety.gov).
For longer holds at lower temperatures use one published lethality table (for example the US FSIS tables) consistently and cite it in your HACCP plan.

## 2. Pairings
Take bath temperature and time for each item from your chosen published table, for the thickness you cook.
If a pairing does not reach a recognised pasteurisation treatment, treat it as not pasteurising: cook it short and serve it at once.

## 3. Bagging and sealing
- Chamber vacuum for liquids; external/edge sealers need dry contents or a pre-freeze.
- **Water displacement** works with zip bags for short cooks.
- Season lightly; raw garlic and some aromatics turn harsh; salt on long cooks can cure
  the texture. Fats like butter are fine.
- **Floating bags** cook unevenly: remove air, add a weight, rack the bags.
- Don't overload the bath: water must circulate, and the setpoint must recover after
  loading. Cover the bath (lid or balls) to stop evaporation on long cooks.

## 4. Cook-chill-regenerate
1. Cook to pasteurisation per your table.
2. **Chill in ice water** (≥ 50 % ice) to a cold core temperature quickly, within the time limit in your HACCP plan. Check with a probe through foam tape on the bag.
3. Label: item, cook date/time, bath temp, use-by. Shelf life depends on pasteurisation
   level and jurisdiction; set it in your HACCP plan with documented validation.
4. **Regenerate** in a bath at or slightly below the serving temperature until the core
   is reached (roughly the original heating time), then sear.

## 5. Regulation
Many authorities treat vacuum-packing and sous vide as a **specialised process** that
needs a written HACCP plan or a variance (e.g. the US FDA Food Code requires one for
reduced-oxygen packaging) (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked). The FDA Food Code PDF could not be read as text. Check before you sell sous-vide food.

## Gotchas
- Circulator accuracy: verify against a calibrated reference thermometer.
- Searing: dry thoroughly, very hot pan or torch, briefly so you don't overcook
  the band you just perfected.
- Frozen-in-bag items: allow extra heating time and probe.
- Bags rated for cooking temperature only — cheap bags can split or leach.

## Verify
- [ ] Every pairing on the menu is traceable to a published table entry.
- [ ] Core temperature checked with a probe on at least one bag per batch.
- [ ] Chill log shows the core reached your plan's temperature within its time limit.
- [ ] Labels carry cook date, temperature and use-by.
- [ ] Bath reference-checked this week; written HACCP plan covers reduced-oxygen packaging.
