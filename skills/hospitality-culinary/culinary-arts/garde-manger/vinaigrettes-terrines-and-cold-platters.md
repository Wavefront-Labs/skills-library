# Vinaigrettes, terrines and cold platters (garde manger)

**Goal:** cold food that tastes seasoned at fridge temperature, holds its structure on a
buffet, and stays safe for the whole service.

## 1. Dressings
- **Classic vinaigrette: ~3 parts oil to 1 part acid**, then adjust: sharper vinegars
  (sherry, red wine) may want 4:1; citrus can sit at 2:1. Dissolve salt in the acid
  **before** adding oil — salt doesn't dissolve in oil.
- **Emulsify** with mustard, honey, a little yolk or garlic paste. A blender gives a
  stable temporary emulsion; whisked will separate — shake to order.
- **Cold dulls flavour**: season dressings slightly more than tastes right warm; always
  taste on a leaf, not off a spoon.
- **Dress leaves at the last moment**, in a bowl, by hand, just enough to coat. Dry the
  leaves thoroughly first (spinner) — water stops the dressing clinging.
- Creamy dressings: base of mayonnaise, yoghurt or crème fraîche; label and date;
  these go off faster than vinaigrettes.

## 2. Terrines and pâtés (forcemeat)
1. **Keep everything cold** — meat, fat, bowl, grinder parts — ideally ≤ 4 °C. Warm
   fat smears and the terrine turns crumbly and greasy ("broken" forcemeat).
2. Typical straight forcemeat: lean meat to fat roughly **2:1 to 3:2**; salt ~1.5–2 %
   of total weight. Many recipes add a panade (bread + milk/egg) for tenderness.
3. **Test quenelle:** poach a spoonful, cool and taste before filling the mould —
   seasoning and texture can still be fixed.
4. Line the mould (cling film, caul fat, bacon or ham), pack with no air pockets,
   garnish layered in (pistachios, diced tongue, mushrooms) if using.
5. **Bake in a bain-marie** at ~130–150 °C to a core of roughly 65–70 °C for pork/game
   mixes (check your HACCP minimum, poultry higher).
6. **Press** overnight with a weighted board cut to fit the mould — this gives the clean
   slice.
7. Mousseline: puréed fish or poultry + egg white + cream, worked over ice; lighter, more
   delicate, lower poaching temperatures.
- **Curing salt** (nitrite, "pink salt") is used in some pâtés and terrines for colour
  and safety; it's toxic in excess. Weigh precisely to the recipe; never substitute
  it 1:1 for salt.

## 3. Canapés
- One or two bites, eaten standing, no cutlery, no drips.
- **Moisture barrier:** butter, cream cheese or a crisp base fried/toasted to keep wet
  toppings off bread or pastry. Assemble as late as possible.
- Plan **quantities:** a common catering rule is 4–6 pieces per guest per hour for a
  pre-dinner reception, 10–14 for a canapé-only event of 2–3 hours. Varies with crowd.
- Uniformity: use cutters, templates and a set number of pieces per tray.

## 4. Cold platters and buffets
- Build height and flow at the back, low items at the front; arrange in lines or
  fan slices overlapping in the direction of the guest's reach.
- **Garnish must be edible** and relate to the food.
- Smaller platters replaced often beat one huge platter going warm.
- **Cold holding:** UK chilled food must be at 8 °C or below (legal in England, Wales and Northern Ireland) and gov.uk advises a 5 °C fridge — chilled
  wells, ice beds with drainage, or chilled slates.
- **Time as a control:** if food is out of refrigeration, many codes allow a fixed time
  (UK gov.uk: chilled food can be out of the fridge for up to 4 hours; if it has been out for less than 4 hours it can go back in the fridge, and after 4 hours it must be thrown away). Confirm your
  local rules and mark the "out" time on a label.
- Allergen labelling for each dish on the buffet; separate serving utensils.

## Gotchas
- Salad leaves wilt from acid within minutes; acid-tolerant salads (slaws, grain
  salads) can be dressed ahead.
- Aspic/gelatin glazes melt under warm lights.
- Terrine sliced warm crumbles; chill fully and slice with a hot, dry knife.
