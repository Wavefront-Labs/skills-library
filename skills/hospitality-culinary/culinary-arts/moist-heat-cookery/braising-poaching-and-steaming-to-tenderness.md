# Braising, poaching and steaming to tenderness

**Goal:** tough, collagen-rich cuts turned tender and moist; delicate items poached or
steamed without toughening — chosen by liquid temperature, not guesswork.

## 1. Pick the method by the cut
- **Collagen-rich, hard-working cuts** (shin, cheek, shoulder, short rib, shank, thigh):
  braise or stew. Collagen converts to gelatin only with long, moist heat around
  **70–95 °C** over hours.
- **Tender, lean or delicate items** (fish, chicken breast, eggs, fruit): poach or steam
  at lower temperatures, briefly. Long cooking just dries them.

## 2. Braising (and stewing)
1. **Season and sear** the meat well for flavour (combination method). Remove.
2. Sweat aromatics/mirepoix in the same pan; add tomato paste if using and cook out.
3. **Deglaze** (wine, stock), scrape the fond.
4. Return meat. Liquid comes **⅓–½ up the meat** for a braise; fully covering for a stew.
5. Bring to a simmer, cover (lid or cartouche), and finish **in the oven at ~140–160 °C**
   for even all-round heat. Liquid should barely tremble; a hard boil makes stringy meat.
6. Typical times (rules of thumb): chicken thigh 45–60 min; lamb shank / beef cheek
   2.5–4 h; short rib 3–4 h. Done is **fork-tender**: a fork slides in and lifts out
   with no resistance.
7. **Cool in the liquid** where possible — meat reabsorbs moisture. Braises are often
   better the next day; chill, lift the fat, reheat.
8. Reduce and strain the liquid to sauce consistency; adjust seasoning after reducing.

**Curries and stews:** bloom ground spices in fat for 30–60 s before adding liquid;
add coconut milk/yoghurt late and keep below a boil to avoid splitting (yoghurt:
stir in off heat or temper first).

## 3. Poaching
| Type | Liquid temp | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| Shallow poach | ~70–80 °C, liquid half-way | fish fillets in wine/fumet (cuisson becomes the sauce) |
| Deep poach | ~70–80 °C, submerged | eggs, chicken, whole fish, fruit |
| Simmer | ~85–95 °C | tougher items, poaching sausages |

- **Court bouillon** for fish/shellfish: water, acid (wine/vinegar), aromatics, salt;
  simmer 20–30 min before use.
- **Poached eggs:** very fresh eggs; strain off the watery white through a sieve;
  water at a bare simmer, a splash of vinegar optional; 3–4 min; shock in iced water
  for service and reheat 30–60 s in hot water.
- Start large items (whole chicken, ham) in cold liquid; add small items to hot liquid.

## 4. Steaming
- Water at a full boil before loading; keep the lid on; arrange in a single layer with
  space for steam to circulate.
- Season before steaming — salt doesn't stick after.
- Watch condensation dripping onto bao or custards; wrap the lid in a cloth.
- Combi ovens do this at scale — see hospitality-culinary.culinary-arts.combi-ovens.

## 5. Pressure cooking
- Cuts braise times by roughly two-thirds (e.g. short ribs ~45–60 min at high pressure).
- **Never overfill:** max two-thirds full, half for beans, grains and foaming foods.
- Check the gasket and vent are clean before each use.
- **Natural release** for meat and foaming foods; quick release only per the
  manufacturer, steam vent pointed away. Never force the lid.
- Flavour is less reduced; reduce the liquid afterwards.

## Gotchas
- Lean cuts (tenderloin, breast) braised for hours turn dry and stringy — collagen, not
  time, is what makes braising work.
- Too much liquid = boiled, washed-out meat and thin sauce.
- Salting stews heavily early: they reduce. Season at the end.
- Thickening with flour? Dredge before searing, or finish with a slurry/beurre manié.

## Verify
- [ ] Braised meat is fork-tender and holds its shape (not shredding unless intended).
- [ ] Liquid stayed at a tremble, not a boil — checked with a thermometer at least once.
- [ ] Poached fish is just opaque and flakes; eggs have set whites and runny yolks.
- [ ] Pressure cooker gasket and vent checked; release method matched the food.
- [ ] Sauce reduced and seasoned after reduction.
