# Station setup, ticket timing and working the pass

**Goal:** every dish on a ticket arrives at the pass at the same moment, at standard,
while your station stays stocked and clean through the rush.

## 1. Mise en place before service
- **Prep list from pars:** each item has a par (what you need for a typical service,
  e.g. 30 portions of fish on Saturday). Count what's left, prep the difference, plus
  a buffer for the forecast covers.
- **Lay out the station the same way every shift**, in the order you use things: hot
  items closest to the stove, garnish and finishing salt nearest the plating area,
  backup containers in the lowboy directly under.
- Every container labelled (item, date, initials) and in **FIFO** order.
- Tools: spoons, tongs, fish slice, towels (dry for handling hot pans — wet towels
  conduct heat), sani bucket, side towel, probe, tasting spoons.
- **Run through one of each dish** (or walk it mentally) before doors open. Check sauces
  are seasoned and at temperature.
- Know your **86 list** — what's limited or out — before the first ticket.

## 2. Reading tickets
- Ticket shows table, covers, course, modifiers (no gluten, sauce on side, allergy).
  **Allergy modifiers are read aloud and confirmed.** A missed allergy is the most
  serious error on a line.
- **"All day"** = the total count of an item across all open tickets ("four salmon all
  day"). Keep a running all-day for your station.
- **Fire** = start cooking now. Many kitchens hold mains until expo fires the course.
- **Ordering vs fire:** the ticket may print at order time, but you cook to the fire call.

## 3. Timing a table
1. Identify the **longest-cooking item** on the ticket (e.g. 14 min for a thick steak
   at medium-well).
2. Work backwards: everything else starts so it finishes at the same moment, including
   rest and plating time.
3. Communicate with other stations: "Grill, 6 minutes on table 12" → others time to it.
4. Write or mark times; use a timer per pan in a rush.
5. Hold perfect items only briefly; past a few minutes most things degrade. A dish that
   dies waiting is re-fired.

## 4. The pass and call language
- **Expo/head chef calls**, the line replies **"Heard"** / **"Yes, chef"** — every call,
  every time. Silence means it wasn't heard.
- "**Behind**", "**Hot behind**", "**Sharp**", "**Corner**", "**Open door**" when moving.
- "**On the fly**" = needed immediately (a re-fire or a missed dish).
- "**86 [item]**" = out; tell expo the moment you're down to the last few, not after.
- "**Walking in**" / "**Service please**" = plates at the pass for runners.

## 5. Staying out of the weeds
- **Clean as you go:** wipe the board and station between tickets; empty the bin and
  restock between waves, not at the peak.
- **Restock early:** at ~25 % left, call it or grab backup during a lull.
- When behind: **say so early** ("I'm in the weeds, need 2 minutes on 14"). Expo can
  re-sequence; a surprise late dish can't be fixed.
- Prioritise by fire order, not by which ticket feels urgent.

## 6. Close down
Wrap, label and date everything; record wastage; write tomorrow's prep notes; deep
clean the station; return backups to the walk-in; log fridge temperatures.

## Gotchas
- Moving things on someone else's station breaks their muscle memory.
- Cold-holding mise in a lowboy that is opened constantly can drift above 5 °C — probe
  it mid-service.
- Modifiers on KDS screens are easy to miss when the font is small; read the whole line.
