# Searing, roasting, frying and judging doneness

**Goal:** deep, even browning outside and the target doneness inside, repeatably, at
service pace.

## 1. Searing and sautéing
- **Dry surface:** pat proteins dry; season salt just before searing (or 40+ minutes
  ahead so it reabsorbs — salting 5–30 min ahead draws out moisture that steams).
- **Hot pan, thin film of high-smoke-point fat** (refined rapeseed/canola, grapeseed,
  clarified butter). Fat should shimmer, just short of smoking.
- **Don't crowd.** Crowding drops pan temperature and the protein steams grey. Work in
  batches; leave space roughly a finger's width between pieces.
- **Leave it alone:** it releases from the pan when the crust has formed. Forcing it
  tears the crust.
- **Baste** thicker cuts in the last minutes with butter, garlic, thyme — tilt pan, spoon
  foaming butter over. Butter burns above ~150 °C; add it late.
- Sauté (small pieces, tossing): high heat, short time, pan large enough.

## 2. Roasting
- Large joints: sear first or roast hot to start, then lower. Or **reverse sear**: low
  oven (100–130 °C) to ~5–8 °C under target, then hard sear — very even interior.
- Roast on a rack or vegetable trivet so the base doesn't stew.
- Convection/fan ovens cook faster; many cooks drop the set temperature about 20 °C vs
  conventional — confirm with your oven.
- Roast vegetables: cut evenly, dry, oil lightly, single layer, 200–220 °C.

## 3. Grilling and broiling
- Clean, oiled grates; zones: hot zone for marking, cooler zone for finishing.
- **Crosshatch:** place at 10 o'clock, rotate to 2 o'clock, flip, repeat.
- Salamander/broiler: watch constantly; sugars burn in seconds.

## 4. Deep frying
- Typical oil temperatures: **160–170 °C** for blanching chips/thicker items,
  **175–190 °C** for finishing and battered items. Check equipment and recipe.
- Fry small batches; overloading drops temperature and gives greasy food.
- Dry the food; shake off excess batter/crumb. Lower away from you with the basket.
- **Never** let water near hot oil; never put a fat fire out with water — lid, fire
  blanket or class F/K extinguisher.
- Oil care: skim between batches, filter daily, discard when dark, smoky at normal
  temperature, foaming or smelling acrid. Many kitchens use test strips for polar
  compounds; limits vary by country.

## 5. Judging doneness
**Probe thermometer is the reference.** Insert into the thickest part, away from bone.
Common pull temperatures (core, before rest) — check your house standard and local
food-safety minima:

| Item | Pull at | After rest ≈ |
|---|---|---|
| Beef/lamb rare | 48–50 °C | 52 °C |
| Medium-rare | 52–54 °C | 55–57 °C |
| Medium | 57–60 °C | 60–63 °C |
| Pork (juicy, modern) | 60–62 °C | 63 °C |
| Chicken/poultry | 72–74 °C | 74 °C+ (or lower with long hold — see sous-vide skill) |
| Fish (most) | 50–55 °C | flakes, just opaque |

Minced/ground meat and poultry have stricter regulatory minima in most places
(US foodsafety.gov: 160 °F / 71 °C for ground meat and sausage, 165 °F / 74 °C for poultry, 145 °F / 63 °C with a 3 min rest for whole pork cuts and 145 °F for fish).
UK gov.uk standard advice is a core temperature of 70 °C for 2 minutes, with 75 °C for 30 s among the equivalent alternatives.
The pull temperatures in the table are cooks' targets, not regulatory minima; check them against your local food-safety rules.

- **Carryover cooking:** large roasts can rise 3–8 °C after leaving the heat; steaks
  1–3 °C. Pull early.
- **Rest:** roughly 5 min for steaks, 15–30 min for joints, loosely covered. Less juice
  floods the board.
- **Touch test** (press the meat vs the base of your thumb) is a useful at-speed check
  once calibrated against a probe, not a substitute for one with poultry or large joints.

## Gotchas
- Cold-from-fridge thick steaks cook unevenly; temper 20–30 minutes.
- Non-stick pans don't build fond and shouldn't go to very high heat.
- Calibrate the probe in iced water (should read 0 °C) weekly.

## Verify
- [ ] Crust is even and brown, not grey patches or black spots.
- [ ] Probe reading logged; final temperature after rest is the target.
- [ ] Fryer oil read with a thermometer before each batch; recovery time respected.
- [ ] Resting juice on the board is minimal.
