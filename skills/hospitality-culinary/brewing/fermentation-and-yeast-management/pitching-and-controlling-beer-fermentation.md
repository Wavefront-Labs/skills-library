# Pitching and controlling beer fermentation — healthy yeast, controlled heat

**Goal:** a clean, fully attenuated fermentation on a predictable timetable, with the
flavour coming from the yeast you chose, not from stress.

## 1. Pitch enough healthy yeast
Common targets (cells per mL per °Plato of wort):
- **Ales: ~0.75 million**; high-gravity ales up to 1.0.
- **Lagers: ~1.5 million**; up to 2.0 for strong lagers.
Example: 20 L of 12 °P ale → 0.75 × 10⁶ × 20 000 mL × 12 ≈ **180 billion cells** — about
two fresh liquid packs or one pack plus a starter. One 11 g dry sachet is usually ~100–200
billion viable cells (manufacturer figures vary).
- **Starter:** ~1.035–1.040 wort, stir plate, 18–24 h, room temperature; decant spent beer
  and pitch the slurry at high krausen or after crashing.
- **Viability** (commercial): methylene or methylene-violet blue stain and a haemocytometer;
  repitch only slurry >90% viable.

## 2. Oxygenate
Yeast needs oxygen to build sterols for growth. Target ~8–10 ppm dissolved O₂ for ales,
~10–12 ppm for lagers and high gravity. Pure O₂ through a 0.5–2 µm stone gets there in
~60 s at homebrew scale; shaking reaches only ~8 ppm. Oxygen **only at pitch** — after that it causes staling.
Dry yeast generally doesn't need wort oxygen (check maker's advice).

## 3. Control temperature (the biggest flavour lever)
- Pitch at or slightly below fermentation temperature. Fermentation is exothermic; the
  beer runs 2–5 °C above the room in the first 48 h, so control **beer** temperature (probe
  in a thermowell), not air.
- Ales commonly 17–20 °C; lagers 9–12 °C; kveik strains can run 30–40 °C cleanly.
- **Too warm early** → fusel alcohols (hot, solventy) and excess esters.
- **Raising temperature late** (+2–3 °C in the last third) helps attenuation and clean-up.

## 4. Diacetyl rest and readiness
Diacetyl (butter/butterscotch) forms early and is reabsorbed by active yeast.
- **Lager rest:** when ~2/3 to 3/4 attenuated, free-rise to ~15–18 °C for 2–3 days.
- **Forced diacetyl test:** sample into two sealed containers; heat one to ~60–70 °C for
  10–20 min, cool, smell both. Butter only in the heated one = precursor still present → wait.
- **Done** = gravity stable for 2–3 days, no diacetyl or green-apple acetaldehyde, then crash.

## 5. Harvest and repitch
Harvest from the cone of a conical after crashing (middle layer — not first trub, not last).
Store under beer at 1–4 °C, use within 1–2 weeks, and limit repitches to ~5–10 generations
before refreshing from a lab culture (uncertain; varies by strain and hygiene).

## 6. Mixed and sour fermentation
- **Kettle sour:** acidify wort with *Lactobacillus* (~35–45 °C, CO₂ purged, pH starting ~4.5)
  to pH ~3.2–3.5, then **boil to kill it** before normal fermentation. Fast, contained.
- **Mixed culture** (*Brettanomyces*, *Lacto*, *Pedio*) in barrels or tanks takes months.
  **Keep separate soft goods** (hoses, gaskets, valves) for sour programmes; Brett survives
  in scratches and is the classic way a clean brewery contaminates its flagship.

## Verify
- [ ] Pitch rate calculated and logged with yeast generation and viability.
- [ ] Beer temperature (probe) held within ±1 °C of the profile.
- [ ] Final gravity stable 2–3 days; forced diacetyl test clean.
- [ ] Sour cultures confined to dedicated equipment.
