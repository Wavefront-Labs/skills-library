# Balancing, cleaning and troubleshooting draught lines — a perfect pour, every pour

**Goal:** beer leaves the tap at about **2 fl oz per second (~60 mL/s; ~1 US gal/min)** with
the brewer's carbonation intact, the right head, and no off-flavours from the lines.

## 1. Two numbers that must agree
1. **Applied pressure** is set by the keg's carbonation and temperature — not by pour speed.
   Read it off a carbonation chart: e.g. ~2.5 vol at ~3 °C needs about 12 psi CO₂ at sea level
   (add ~1 psi per 2 000 ft / 600 m of altitude). Lower pressure → beer slowly goes flat;
   higher → it over-carbonates and foams.
2. **System resistance** must use up that pressure by the faucet:
   `applied pressure ≈ line length × restriction per ft + rise in ft × 0.5 psi + fittings`
   Typical restriction: 3/16" ID vinyl ≈ 2–3 psi/ft (sources vary; measure your own);
   1/4" vinyl ≈ 0.85; 3/8" barrier tubing ≈ 0.1–0.2. Taps and couplers add ~1 psi.
   Example: 12 psi, tap 1 ft above keg (0.5 psi), fittings 1 psi → ~10.5 psi of line to lose
   → ~4–5 ft of 3/16" vinyl at ~2.2–2.5 psi/ft.
**Never fix foam by dropping CO₂ pressure** — the keg will drift flat over days. Change the
restriction (line length, a flow-control faucet) instead.

## 2. Gas choice
- **Short direct-draw** (kegerator, walk-in behind the bar): pure CO₂ at chart pressure.
- **Long-draw** systems (lines >~15 m, beer pumped through a glycol-cooled trunk): need
  20–30 psi to push beer; pure CO₂ at that pressure over-carbonates, so use **blended gas**
  (CO₂/N₂) from a blender, mixed to keep each beer's carbonation.
- **Nitro stouts:** ~25% CO₂ / 75% N₂ at ~30–40 psi through a stout faucet with a restrictor plate.
- Use food-grade gas; check regulators and fittings with soapy water for leaks.

## 3. Temperature
Keep kegs at **~1–3 °C** constantly, and glycol-cool long-draw lines right to the tower.
A 3 °C rise in the line or a warm tower is the commonest cause of foam on the first pour.

## 4. Cleaning schedule
Common standard (e.g. BA Draught Beer Quality Manual):
- **Every 2 weeks:** recirculate caustic line cleaner at the maker's strength (typically ≥2%
  caustic for ~15 min with circulation, or static ~20 min), rinse with water until pH-neutral;
  disassemble and brush faucets and couplers.
- **Quarterly:** acid clean to remove beerstone; check seals, replace worn washers.
- **Lines:** replace vinyl roughly annually; barrier tubing lasts longer.
- Never let cleaner reach a keg — disconnect couplers and use the cleaning adaptor.

## 5. Diagnose from the glass
| Symptom | Likely cause |
|---|---|
| Foamy, first pour only | Warm tower/faucet, unchilled line section |
| Foamy all the time | Warm keg, too little restriction, kinks, dirty line, pressure too high |
| Flat, little head | Pressure too low, gas empty/leak, greasy glass |
| Bubbles stuck to glass | Dirty glass (not "beer clean") |
| Buttery, sour, vinegar | Dirty lines (diacetyl, *Pediococcus*, acetic bacteria) — clean now |
| Cloudy beer that should be clear | Keg disturbed, too warm, or dirty line |
| Sputtering | Empty keg or bad coupler seal |
Beer-clean glass test: wet glass, shake salt inside — salt sticks evenly if clean.

## Verify
- [ ] Pour rate ~2 oz/s with a 2–3 cm head at chart pressure.
- [ ] Keg temperature logged at 1–3 °C; tower cold.
- [ ] Cleaning log shows a clean every 14 days and last acid clean date.
- [ ] Tasting the first pour from each line reveals no buttery/sour notes.
