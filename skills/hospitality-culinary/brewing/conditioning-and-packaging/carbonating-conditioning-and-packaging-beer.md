# Carbonating, conditioning and packaging beer — clear, fizzy right, and not oxidised

**Goal:** get finished beer into keg, cask, bottle or can at the carbonation and clarity
the style needs, with as little oxygen as possible so it stays fresh.

## 1. Before packaging
- Fermentation **truly** finished: gravity stable 2–3 days, diacetyl test clean (see
  pitching-and-controlling-beer-fermentation). Packaging early is how bottles explode.
- **Cold-crash** to ~0–2 °C for 1–3 days to drop yeast and haze. Use CO₂ (not air) to fill
  headspace as the beer contracts, or the fermenter sucks in oxygen and airlock liquid.
- **Fining:** gelatin (homebrew) or isinglass/silica (commercial) added cold; give it 1–3 days.
  Isinglass and gelatin are animal products — note for vegan labelling.

## 2. Carbonation targets (volumes of CO₂)
British cask ales ~1.0–1.5; most ales 2.2–2.6; lagers 2.5–2.7; wheat and Belgian 2.8–4.0.
Too high for the bottle type is dangerous — standard thin bottles are typically rated to
~3 volumes; use heavy Belgian/champagne bottles above that.

## 3. Force carbonation (keg or brite tank)
Set regulator pressure from a carbonation chart: e.g. at ~3–4 °C, ~12 psi (0.8 bar) gives
roughly 2.5 volumes. Leave 5–10 days to equilibrate, or use a carbonation stone in a brite
tank to speed it up. Shaking a keg to rush it often overshoots — avoid for repeatable results.

## 4. Priming (bottle or cask conditioning)
Beer already holds residual CO₂ depending on its **warmest temperature after fermentation**
(~0.85 vol at 20 °C, ~1.0 at 15 °C). Add only the difference.
- Sucrose ≈ **3.8 g/L per volume** wanted; dextrose (corn sugar, monohydrate) ≈ 4.4 g/L.
- Example: target 2.4 vol, beer last at 20 °C → need ~1.55 vol → ~5.9 g/L sucrose.
- Dissolve in boiled water, mix gently into the bulk beer (not per-bottle), then fill.
- Condition at ~18–22 °C for ~2 weeks, then chill. Very long-aged or high-ABV beer may need
  fresh yeast added at bottling.

## 5. Cask (real ale)
Rack with low residual yeast plus primings and finings into a cask, condition a few days in the
brewery. In the cellar (11–13 °C): stillage, rest 24 h, **vent with a soft spile**, then
swap to a hard spile once the hiss settles; tap, check clarity before serving. Serve within ~3 days
of venting (oxygen replaces dispensed beer). Cask-breather devices change that — check local rules.

## 6. Kegs and cans: oxygen control
- Purge kegs by filling with sanitiser and pushing it out with CO₂ (zero air contact);
  "purge cycles" with gas alone leave more oxygen behind.
- Fill closed-transfer under CO₂ counter-pressure. Commercial target: total package oxygen
  (TPO) ideally <100 ppb, best practice <50 ppb (uncertain; depends on brewery spec).
- **Canning:** check seam dimensions with a seam inspection routine at start-up and regularly;
  under-cover the lid with foam or CO₂ before seaming. A poor seam leaks slowly and fails weeks later.

## Gotchas
- Bottling before final gravity → over-carbonated "gushers" or bottle bombs.
- Packaged hoppy beer loses aroma quickly at warm temperature: store cold, date-code.
- Carbonation reading by feel is unreliable; use a chart and measured temperature.

## Verify
- [ ] Gravity stable and diacetyl-free before packaging.
- [ ] Carbonation calculated from residual CO₂ + priming, or set by chart; logged.
- [ ] Sample bottle opened after 2 weeks: correct fizz, clear pour, no gushing.
- [ ] Kegs liquid-purged; cans pass seam check; DO/TPO measured where possible.
