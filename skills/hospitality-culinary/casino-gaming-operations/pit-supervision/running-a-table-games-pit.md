# Running a table-games pit

**Goal:** keep every table in your section open, honest, correctly staffed and priced
for demand, with paperwork that reconciles to the cent. Exact forms, signatures and
thresholds are set by your property's internal controls and regulator — follow them.

## 1. Opening and closing tables
- **Opener**: the table's chip inventory (by denomination) recorded on the opener slip
  and verified against the rack by you and the dealer; sealed openers are checked
  against the prior closer.
- **Closer**: count the rack at close with the dealer, lid and lock the rack, sign the
  closer; drop box removed by the drop team under procedure.
- **Win/loss** per table ≈ closer − opener + credits − fills + drop. You don't compute
  the official figure (count room/accounting does) but you should know it roughly to
  spot anomalies. *[Formula sign conventions vary by system; check your property's.]*

## 2. Fills and credits
- **Fill**: chips brought from the cage to a table running low. **Credit**: excess chips
  returned to the cage.
- Process: request entered (system or paper) → cage prepares → **security escorts** →
  dealer counts it out on the layout in view of camera → dealer, you and runner sign.
- Never fill to a table with the box open or a game in progress mid-hand.
- Watch fill frequency: a table needing unusual fills deserves a look.

## 3. Rating players
Every rated player's session: **average bet, time played, game, buy-in, cash-out**.
Ratings feed comps and host decisions (see player-hosting-and-comps). Rate honestly —
inflated ratings cost the house real money. Note cash buy-ins against reporting
thresholds (e.g. currency transaction reporting where applicable) per AML policy.

## 4. Table limits and demand
- Raise **minimums** when tables are full and players are waiting; lower them to fill
  a quiet pit. Announce changes and give seated players the house-standard notice
  (often they're grandfathered for a period or until they leave).
- Keep a spread of minimums open so lower-budget players aren't pushed out.
- Higher **maximums** need management approval and bankroll awareness.

## 5. Dealer rotation and breaks
- Standard rotations are often around **60–80 min on, ~20 min break** — whatever the
  property and labour rules require. Rotate dealers across games to keep skills fresh
  and break up familiarity with players.
- Assign stronger dealers to busy/high-limit games; pair new dealers with tables you
  can watch closely.
- Track who dealt which table when — surveillance will ask.

## 6. Dispute resolution
1. **Freeze the hand** — nobody touches the cards or chips in question.
2. Listen to the player, then the dealer, separately and calmly.
3. If the facts are unclear, **call surveillance** for a review and tell the player
   you're doing so and roughly how long it takes.
4. Decide per procedure; explain the decision briefly. Escalate to the shift manager
   if the player won't accept.
5. Document the dispute, the review outcome and any payout.
Make decisions consistently — a "goodwill" payout to one player is a precedent for all.

## 7. Watching the game
Stand where you can see several tables' layouts and dealers' hands. Watch payouts on
big bets, new players with big bets, sudden bet-spread changes, and dealer procedure.
See protecting-the-table-game for what to look for.

## Gotchas
- Signing paperwork you didn't actually watch.
- Moving a minimum up mid-shoe on seated players without notice → complaints.
- Letting breaks slip in a rush — tired dealers make costly errors.
- Comping on vibes instead of rating.

## Verify
- [ ] Every opener/closer, fill and credit signed with the count witnessed.
- [ ] Ratings entered for all rated players before they leave.
- [ ] Rotation sheet complete; no dealer over the break limit.
- [ ] Each dispute logged with surveillance reference if reviewed.
