# Protecting the table game

**Goal:** keep the game honest for both the house and the players by following
procedure so consistently that deviations — yours or anyone's — stand out. Your job is
to **notice and notify**, not to accuse or confront. Surveillance and security decide.

## 1. Procedure is the protection
Most game-protection failures are procedural slips that someone exploits:
- **Clean hands** every time you touch the rack or leave the table.
- **Chips sized and cut** where the camera sees them; payouts placed beside, never on, the bet.
- **Cards**: hole card never lifted high or exposed; burn cards per procedure; decks
  checked at open and on change; shuffle exactly as written.
- **Bets locked**: no bets touched after the first card / "no more bets".
- **Tray (rack) discipline**: chips by denomination in fixed tubes; high denominations
  in the most protected position; never leave the rack unattended or uncovered.
- **Fills and credits** only with paperwork and supervisor/security present.

## 2. Common cheating moves to recognise (awareness, not training)
| Move | What you notice |
|---|---|
| **Past-posting** | A bet gets added or swapped to a higher value after the result |
| **Pinching / pressing** | Chips removed from (or added to) a bet after a loss/win is known |
| **Capping** | Chips slipped onto a winning bet before payout |
| **Collusion** | Players signalling, chip passing, distraction timed to a move |
| **Card marking / switching** | Bends, crimps, smudges, cards in a player's hand that don't match |
| **Distraction** | A spill, a dropped chip, an argument right as a bet is being settled |
| **Dealer–player collusion** | Unusual payouts, a dealer peeking, chips going off the table |
Any sudden pattern change (big bet jumps, someone watching a dealer's hands closely,
a "team" that moves together) is worth a quiet report.

## 3. Advantage play is not cheating — but the house watches it
Legal skill-based approaches exploit information or rules the game gives away:
- **Card counting** (blackjack) — betting more when remaining cards favour the player.
- **Hole-carding** — reading a dealer's carelessly exposed hole card. The fix is
  *your* procedure.
- **Shuffle tracking**, **dealer tells**, sequencing.
Legal status and the house's responses (e.g. flat-betting a player, reshuffling,
barring) depend on jurisdiction and policy. *[Laws vary — some places limit barring.]*
Dealers don't act on suspicion alone; they inform the supervisor discreetly.

## 4. How to escalate
1. **Stay calm and keep dealing** unless procedure says otherwise.
2. Signal the supervisor using your house's discreet method; or ask for "a floor" with
   a neutral reason.
3. If a bet/payout is in dispute, **stop and freeze the bet** in position: "Floor,
   please" — surveillance can review footage.
4. Never accuse a guest. Never physically handle a guest. Security does that.
5. After: write the **incident report** — time, table, seat, what you saw, exact
   amounts, who was involved — facts only.

## 5. Your own conduct
Follow tipping/toke rules exactly, never carry personal items or chips to the table,
never discuss your game or surveillance with guests, and report approaches from anyone
asking you to "help them out". Insider schemes are prosecuted.

## Gotchas
- Speed pressure leads to shortcuts that look exactly like theft on camera.
- Friendly regulars are not exempt from procedure.
- Over-reacting can expose the casino to liability; observe and report.

## Verify
- [ ] Clean hands, sizing and bet-locking done on every hand in an observed shift.
- [ ] You can describe past-posting, pinching and capping and the counters for each.
- [ ] You know the discreet escalation signal and the incident-report form.
- [ ] No unescorted fills/credits; rack never left open.
