# Rated play and comp decisions

**Goal:** give comps that bring valued guests back without giving away more than their
play is worth, while looking after the player as a person. Your property's comp
matrix and approval limits override the example numbers here.

## 1. Theoretical win (theo) is the basis
Comps are usually based on what the house *expects* to win from the play, not what the
guest actually lost.

**Table games:**
`theo = average bet × hours played × decisions per hour × house edge`
Example: $100 average bet, 4 hours blackjack, ~60 hands/h, assumed 1% edge
(property-set figure, reflects average player skill) → 100 × 4 × 60 × 0.01 = **$240**.

**Slots:** `theo = coin-in × (1 − payback %)` (hold). $20,000 coin-in on a 92% game
→ **$1,600**.

Many properties use **ADT** (average daily theoretical) to rank players.
*[Edge and decision-rate assumptions per game are set by each property; use yours.]*

## 2. Reinvestment
The property sets a **reinvestment rate** — a share of theo returned as comps
(often quoted somewhere in the ~10–40% range depending on tier, market and comp
type). With $240 theo and 30% reinvestment, about **$72** of comps is in line: a
dinner, not a suite.
- **Room, food and beverage, show tickets** are costed at the property's internal cost
  or retail per policy; know which.
- **Free play/promo credits** count toward reinvestment.
- Stack all comps for a trip (airfare, room, F&B) against the trip's theo.

## 3. Evaluating a rating
Before comping, read the rating critically:
- Is the average bet realistic, or was one big hand averaged up?
- Is time accurate (did they actually play 4 hours or sit for 4 hours)?
- Game mix: a craps player on hard ways has a different edge from a pass-line/odds
  player.
- Multiple short trips vs one long one.
If the rating looks off, check with the pit before acting.

## 4. Loss rebates and discounts on loss
High-end players may negotiate a **discount on actual loss** (e.g. 10% back if they
lose over an agreed buy-in). These are agreed **before** play, in writing, approved by
management. Don't invent them at the table after a loss.

## 5. Building the relationship
- Learn preferences (game, food, drink, room type, birthdays) and record them in the
  CRM — hosts change; the record stays.
- Reach out before and after visits; invite to events matched to their value.
- Solve problems fast (room issues, restaurant bookings); that's most of the job.
- Say no gracefully: "I can do X tonight; Y would need more play on your next visit."

## 6. Responsible gambling is part of hosting
- Comps and attention must never pressure someone to play beyond their means.
- Signs to note: chasing losses, borrowing, distress, rising frequency, asking for
  credit they can't support, family complaints.
- Follow policy: a supportive, private conversation by trained staff, information on
  help services, and **self-exclusion** options. Self-excluded players get **no**
  marketing, comps or hosting.
- Never extend credit or markers outside the formal credit process.

## 7. Compliance
Comps are reportable and audited. Large cash activity triggers AML reporting; hosts
must never structure transactions or advise a guest to avoid reporting.

## Gotchas
- Comping on actual loss instead of theo → rewarding luck and losing money long-term.
- Promising suites or flights above your approval limit.
- Treating the loudest guest as the most valuable.
- Forgetting the excluded-players check before inviting a lapsed guest.
