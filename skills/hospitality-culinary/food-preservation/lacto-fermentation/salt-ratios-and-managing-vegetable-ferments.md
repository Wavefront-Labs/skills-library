# Salt ratios and managing vegetable ferments

**Goal:** a sour, crunchy, safe ferment every time — by salting by weight, keeping
everything under brine, and knowing which surface growth is harmless and which is not.

## 1. Salt by weight, not by volume
Salt crystals vary wildly in volume (fine table salt vs flaky kosher). Weigh it.
- **Dry-salt method** (shredded cabbage → sauerkraut): NCHFP uses about 3 Tbsp canning or pickling salt per 5 lb of cabbage
  (roughly 2-2.5 % of cabbage weight; weigh your own salt). Massage until it weeps its own brine. If the juice does not cover the
  cabbage, add brine of 1½ Tbsp salt per quart of boiled, cooled water.
- **Brine method** (whole or chunked veg): use the salt percentage of a tested recipe. NCHFP warns not to reduce the salt in fermented
  pickles or sauerkraut because it is vital to safety and texture.
Use canning or pickling salt; anti-caking additives can cloud the brine.

## 2. Keep it anaerobic
Lactic acid bacteria tolerate salt and no oxygen; moulds need air. The rule: **under the
brine is fine, above it is trouble.**
- Pack tightly and leave headspace for bubbling (for a crock NCHFP says the rim should sit 4-5 in above the cabbage).
- Hold veg down with a glass weight, a brine-filled zip bag, or a cabbage leaf + weight.
- Airlock lids or a water-sealed crock let CO₂ out without letting air in; a loosely
  lidded jar works but needs burping daily (pressure builds fast in days 2–5).
- If the brine level falls, top up with brine made as the recipe says.

## 3. Temperature and timeline
- NCHFP sauerkraut: keep it at 70-75 °F (21-24 °C) and expect about 3-4 weeks; at 60-65 °F (16-18 °C) about 5-6 weeks;
  below 60 °F (16 °C) it may not ferment, and above 75 °F (24 °C) it may turn soft.
- Taste from the earliest date with a clean fork. Move to the fridge when you like it —
  cold slows but doesn't stop it.
- *Optional check:* pH ≤ 4.6 with strips or a meter shows the ferment
  has acidified (acid foods are pH 4.6 or lower). Home strips are imprecise; treat as a confirmation, not a guarantee.

## 4. Reading the surface
| What you see | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| White, flat, wrinkled film | Kahm yeast — harmless but off-flavours | Skim, top up, chill sooner |
| Cloudy brine, white sediment | Normal bacterial activity | Nothing |
| Fuzzy, raised, coloured (blue, green, black, pink) | Mould | Discard if it's in the brine or veg; a tiny surface spot on a dry-salt kraut may be scraped generously — judgement call, when in doubt throw it out |
| Slimy, soft veg, rotten smell | Spoilage (too warm / too little salt) | Discard |

## 5. Gotchas
- Check jar-weighted kraut 2-3 times a week and remove any scum (NCHFP).
- Ferments are **not** shelf-stable like canned goods; store chilled unless processed by a tested recipe.

## Verify
- [ ] Salt weighed and recorded as a % of a stated base.
- [ ] All solids below the brine; weight and airlock or daily burping in place.
- [ ] Temperature held in the range your tested recipe gives (NCHFP kraut: 70-75 °F).
- [ ] Smell is clean and sour; any surface growth identified using the table above.
- [ ] Finished ferment is in the fridge, labelled with salt % and start date.
