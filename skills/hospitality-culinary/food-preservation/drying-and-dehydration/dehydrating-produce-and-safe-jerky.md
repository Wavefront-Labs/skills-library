# Dehydrating produce and making safe jerky

**Goal:** dry food far enough that microbes can't grow, keep its colour and flavour, and
store it so it stays dry — with the extra heat step that jerky needs to be safe.

## 1. Equipment and temperatures
- A dehydrator with a thermostat and fan is the most controllable. Check the real
  temperature with an oven thermometer — dials can be 5–10 °C off.
- Jerky: preheat the dehydrator or oven to 140 °F (60 °C) (NCHFP); USDA gives 130-140 °F for a dehydrator. Use the temperature in a tested produce-drying guide for fruit, vegetables and herbs.
- Ovens work at their lowest setting with the door propped open a few cm and a fan
  nearby; many won't go low enough for herbs.

## 2. Preparing produce
- Slice **evenly** (a mandoline helps). Uneven slices dry unevenly.
- **Fruit that browns** (apple, pear, banana, peach): dip in an ascorbic acid or diluted lemon juice solution as a tested guide specifies.
- **Skinned fruit** (grapes, blueberries, cherries): "check" the skins with a brief
  boiling-water dip so moisture can escape.
- **Most vegetables: blanch** (steam or boil briefly, then cool, per a tested guide) to stop enzymes that
  cause off-flavours and colour loss, and to cut drying time. Onions, garlic, peppers,
  mushrooms and herbs are usually dried raw.
- Arrange in a single layer with space for airflow; rotate trays if the unit heats unevenly.

## 3. Jerky — the safety step
Dehydrator temperatures alone may not kill *E. coli* O157:H7 or *Salmonella* before the
meat dries out (dry heat protects bacteria). Current US guidance:
1. Use lean meat (fat goes rancid), trimmed, sliced about ¼ in (6 mm) thick, across or with the grain;
   partially freezing makes slicing easier.
2. Marinate in the fridge (salt/soy, acid, spices). Optional curing salt #1 per its label.
3. **Heat to a safe internal temperature** — 160 °F (71 °C) for beef, 165 °F (74 °C) for poultry (USDA FSIS Meat and Poultry Hotline) —
   *before* drying (for example boil strips in the marinade for 5 min, per NCHFP), or *after* drying: for strips up to ¼ in thick,
   10 min in an oven preheated to 275 °F (135 °C) (NCHFP).
4. Dry at about 140 °F (60 °C) until done (NCHFP).

## 4. When is it done?
Test cooled pieces (warm food feels softer than it is):
- **Fruit:** leathery and pliable, no beads of moisture when squeezed or torn.
- **Vegetables:** brittle or crisp.
- **Herbs:** crumble readily; stems snap.
- **Jerky:** bends and cracks but doesn't snap; no moist spots inside.
NCHFP: jerky takes about 10-24 h without marinade heating, so start checking after 3 h if strips were boiled in the marinade.
**Case hardening** (hard outside, moist inside) comes from too-high heat early — lower the
temperature and slice thinner.

## 5. Conditioning and storage
- **Condition fruit:** pack loosely in a closed jar and shake daily for several days. Any
  condensation → back in the dehydrator. This evens out moisture between pieces.
- Store airtight in glass, vacuum bags or mylar with oxygen absorbers, cool and dark.
- NCHFP: dried jerky keeps about two weeks at room temperature in a sealed container; refrigerate or freeze it for longer.
- Rehydrate veg in boiling water 15–30 min, or straight into soups.

## Verify
- [ ] Real temperature checked with a thermometer, not the dial.
- [ ] Fruit pretreated, vegetables blanched where appropriate.
- [ ] Jerky heated to 160 °F / 71 °C (165 °F / 74 °C poultry) before or after drying.
- [ ] Doneness tested on cooled pieces; fruit conditioned with no condensation.
- [ ] Packed airtight, labelled with date.
