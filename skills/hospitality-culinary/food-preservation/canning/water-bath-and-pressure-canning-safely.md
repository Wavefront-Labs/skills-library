# Water-bath and pressure canning, safely

**Goal:** put food in jars that are shelf-stable and cannot grow botulinum toxin — by
matching the process to the food's acidity and following a *tested* time, not a guess.

## 1. The one decision that matters: acid or not
*Clostridium botulinum* spores survive boiling (100 °C) but cannot grow below **pH 4.6**.
- **High-acid (pH ≤ 4.6):** most fruit, jams and jellies, vinegar pickles, *acidified*
  tomatoes → **boiling-water bath** is enough, because the acid does the safety work.
- **Low-acid (pH > 4.6):** vegetables (beans, corn, carrots), meat, poultry, fish, stock,
  soups, plain beans → **pressure canner only**. NCHFP gives about 10.5 lb of pressure
  at sea level to reach 240 °F (about 116 °C); the exact gauge pressure depends on altitude,
  gauge type and the recipe's process schedule.
- **Tomatoes sit on the line.** Always add acid per jar: 1 Tbsp bottled lemon juice per
  pint / 2 Tbsp per quart, or ¼ / ½ tsp citric acid per pint / quart (NCHFP tomato
  acidification directions).

## 2. Use a tested recipe, unchanged
Processing times come from heat-penetration testing for that jar size and density.
Use current USDA / National Center for Home Food Preservation (NCHFP), Ball, or
equivalent national guidance. Safe edits: swap dry spices, reduce sugar in *some* jams
(only with a low-sugar pectin recipe). Unsafe edits: adding flour, thickeners, butter,
extra onions/peppers, using bigger jars, purée instead of chunks. Pumpkin purée, refried
beans and thickened sauces have **no** tested home process — freeze them instead.

## 3. Process (both methods)
1. Inspect jars for chips; use new flat lids (bands are reusable). Current lids need no
   pre-boiling — follow the maker's instruction.
2. Keep jars hot; fill hot food into hot jars (hot pack) or raw pack as the recipe says.
3. **Headspace:** use the amount the tested recipe states. Too little → food boils out and breaks the seal; too much → poor vacuum.
4. Debubble with a plastic tool, wipe rims with a clean damp cloth (vinegar for fatty food),
   apply lid, band **fingertip tight** (air must vent).
5. **Water bath:** jars covered by at least 1 in of briskly boiling water (2 in if the process is over 30 min); start the timer only at a full rolling
   boil. **Pressure:** exhaust (vent) steam for 10 min before closing the vent/adding the
   weight, start timing only at target pressure, keep it steady; if pressure drops below
   target, bring it back and **restart the time from zero**.
6. Let the pressure canner depressurise naturally — never quick-cool it (siphoning, broken
   jars, under-processing). Leave water-bath jars 5 min in the pot with the heat off and the lid removed.
7. Cool jars 12–24 h undisturbed; don't retighten bands.

## 4. Altitude
Water boils cooler higher up. Add time to water-bath processes and raise pressure for pressure
canning as the recipe's own altitude table says. NCHFP's peach recipe shows the pattern: a hot-pack pint
of 20 min at 0–1,000 ft becomes 25, 30 and 35 min at 1,001–3,000, 3,001–6,000 and above 6,000 ft, and
the weighted-gauge canner moves from 5 lb to 10 lb above 1,000 ft. Other foods differ; always use the
table printed with the recipe.

## 5. Gotchas
- **Dial gauges drift:** check them for accuracy every year before use; NCHFP says replace one that is more than 2 lb off.
- Small-capacity pressure saucepans are not recommended for canning by NCHFP.
- Oven canning, open-kettle (no processing) and dishwasher or microwave "processing" are not recommended.
- Do not seal jam or jelly with paraffin.

## 6. Storage and spoilage
Remove bands, wash, label with contents and date, store cool and dark (NCHFP: below 95 °F / 35 °C; 50–70 °F / 10–21 °C keeps quality best). **Discard without tasting** any jar with a bulging or unsealed lid, spurting liquid,
off smell, mould or cloudiness that wasn't there. Botulinum toxin may give *no* sign, which
is why process correctness matters more than inspection.

## Verify
- [ ] Each food classified high- or low-acid; low-acid went in a pressure canner.
- [ ] Recipe is a current tested one, followed without unsafe edits.
- [ ] Headspace per the recipe; venting (10 min) and timing started at the right moment; altitude applied.
- [ ] After 12-24 h: lid concave and doesn't flex when pressed.
- [ ] Unsealed jars refrigerated, or handled as the tested recipe or extension service says.
