# Vinegar brines and shelf-stable pickles

**Goal:** pickles that are safe because they are acidic enough, and good because they
stay crisp and well-spiced — whether eaten next week from the fridge or next year from the shelf.

## 1. Know what kind you're making
- **Quick / refrigerator pickles:** hot or cold brine poured over veg, kept refrigerated and
  eaten promptly. Not processed, so not shelf-stable.
- **Shelf-stable pickles:** brine of tested strength + water-bath processing (see
  hospitality-culinary.food-preservation.canning.water-bath-and-pressure-canning-safely).
  Here the ratio is **not** negotiable.
- (Fermented pickles are a different craft — see the lacto-fermentation skill.)

## 2. Acid rules
- Use vinegar labelled **5 % acidity** (white distilled, cider, white wine). Vinegar of unknown
  acidity must not be used (NCHFP).
- Do not change the vinegar, food or water proportions of a tested recipe (NCHFP: acidity is as important to safety as to taste and texture).
- Low-acid veg (peppers, onions, carrots, beets) rely entirely on the brine to bring the pH
  to 4.6 or lower. Packing *more* veg per jar than the recipe dilutes the acid — don't.

## 3. Brine for refrigerator pickles (not for canning)
- A common starting point: equal parts vinegar (5 %) and water, plus pickling salt (no anti-caking
  agents — they cloud brine) and optional sugar. Keep these jars in the fridge; do not can them.
- Bring to a simmer to dissolve and pour over packed veg. Whole spices stay clear; ground spices cloud and darken.

## 4. Crispness
- Start with fresh, firm produce free of spoilage.
- **Cut a 1/16 in (about 1.5 mm) slice from the blossom end** of cucumbers: blossoms may carry an enzyme that softens them (NCHFP).
- **Low-temperature pasteurisation** for cucumber pickles: hold the water at 180-185 °F (82-85 °C) for 30 min,
  starting jars in 120-140 °F water and adding hot water to 1 in above the jars; use a candy or jelly thermometer.
  Use it only when the recipe indicates; above 185 °F can soften pickles and below 180 °F risks spoilage (NCHFP).

## 5. Gotchas
- Use canning or pickling salt; flake salt is not recommended and anti-caking additives can cloud the brine (NCHFP).
- Pink, soft or slimy pickles or a bulging lid → discard.

## Verify
- [ ] Vinegar is labelled 5 %; proportions are exactly the tested recipe's (no dilution).
- [ ] Jar type is clear: fridge (labelled "keep refrigerated", dated) or processed.
- [ ] Shelf-stable jars processed for the tested time with altitude adjustment and sealed.
- [ ] Blossom ends trimmed and veg fresh, for crunch.
