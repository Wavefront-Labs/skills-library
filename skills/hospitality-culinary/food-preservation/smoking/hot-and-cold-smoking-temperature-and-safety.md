# Hot and cold smoking: temperatures and safety

**Goal:** smoke food so it tastes clean (not bitter) and is safe — by controlling
chamber temperature, core temperature and time, and pairing cold smoking with a proper cure.

## 1. Hot vs cold — they are different processes
| | Cold smoking | Hot smoking |
|---|---|---|
| Chamber | cool enough that the food is not cooked | hot enough to cook the food through |
| Cooks the food? | No | Yes |
| Safety comes from | prior **cure/salt** (and often later cooking) | reaching a safe **core temperature** |
| Examples | smoked salmon, bacon (before cooking), cheese, salt, kippers | hot-smoked trout/mackerel, ham, sausages, brisket |

Smoke itself is a weak preservative; it adds surface antimicrobials and slows rancidity,
but **cure, drying and temperature do the real work.**

## 2. Prep: cure and pellicle
1. Cure or brine first (see hospitality-culinary.food-preservation.curing.equilibrium-curing-and-nitrite-calculation).
   Cold-smoked meats intended to sit in the temperature danger zone for hours need nitrite (confirm the cure rules that apply to you).
2. Rinse, pat dry, and air-dry uncovered in the fridge (or a cool breeze) until
   the surface is tacky — the **pellicle**. Smoke sticks to a dry tacky skin; a wet
   surface gives patchy, sooty colour.

## 3. Clean smoke
- Aim for **thin, blue, almost invisible smoke**. Thick white billowing smoke is
  smouldering, oxygen-starved combustion: bitter, creosote-coated food.
- Give the fire enough air; keep vents open and adjust heat with fuel amount instead.
- Cold smoking: use a separate smoke generator (maze/tube pellet smoulder or an external
  firebox piped in) so the heat stays outside the chamber. Do it in cool weather or
  at night; add a tray of ice if the chamber creeps up.

## 4. Wood
Use seasoned, untreated wood only; never painted or treated timber.

## 5. Safe minimum core temperatures (US, verify with a probe)
US foodsafety.gov / USDA FSIS minimum internal temperatures for hot-smoked food:
- Fish: **145 °F (63 °C)**, or until the flesh is opaque and separates easily with a fork.
- Poultry (including ground poultry): **165 °F (74 °C)**.
- Ground meat and sausage: **160 °F (71 °C)**. Whole pork cuts: **145 °F (63 °C)** followed by a 3-minute rest.
Cold smoking does not cook the food, so those temperatures are not reached; its safety depends on the cure and on
refrigeration.

## 6. Safety gotchas
- **Listeria** in cold-smoked fish is a real risk for pregnant, elderly and immune-
  compromised people; keep it refrigerated and consume within its stated life.
- Don't let raw food sit warm between steps — if the session stalls, chill and resume.
- Use a calibrated probe thermometer (ice-water 0 °C check) and a separate chamber thermometer; lid dials can be inaccurate.
- Commercial production needs a HACCP plan and local regulator approval; confirm the rules for vacuum-packed smoked fish with your regulator before selling.
- Smoke in an open-air spot, away from the house; carbon monoxide is lethal indoors.

## Verify
- [ ] Hot or cold chosen deliberately; cold smoking only on cured product.
- [ ] Pellicle formed before smoke went on.
- [ ] Smoke ran thin and blue, from untreated hardwood.
- [ ] Chamber stayed in range (log it); core temp confirmed with a calibrated probe.
- [ ] Finished product chilled promptly and labelled with date.
