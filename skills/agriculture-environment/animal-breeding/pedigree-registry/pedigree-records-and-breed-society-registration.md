# Pedigree records and breed-society registration

**Goal:** every animal you sell or breed from has an identity, verified parents and
papers that match — so its value isn't lost and breeding values are right.
Society rules differ widely; this is the common shape. **Read your society's
current rulebook** for deadlines, fees and required tests.

## 1. Identify at birth, permanently
- Use the legally required ID first (e.g. official ear tags/EID for cattle, sheep and
  goats; microchips for dogs and horses in many countries), then the society's own
  scheme (tattoo, herd prefix + number).
- Record **at birth**: dam, date, sex, birth weight or ease score, colour/markings,
  twin status, and the **service sire** (from your mating records — see
  heat-detection-joining-and-pregnancy-checking).
- Multi-sire groups: you don't know the sire — DNA is the only honest answer.

## 2. Notify the society on time
- Most societies have a **birth notification** window (often within weeks to months)
  and a later, more expensive **full registration**. Late notifications can be refused
  or require DNA.
- Use the society's online portal or approved herd-management software that submits
  directly; enter IDs exactly as on tags — one transposed digit makes a different animal.
- ET and AI calves usually need extra data: the AI straw/sire code, ET certificate,
  recipient ID.

## 3. Parentage verification
- **SNP-based DNA parentage** (hair follicles, tissue-sampling ear tags, or blood) is now
  standard; it compares the offspring to both recorded parents. Sires used widely usually
  must already have a DNA profile on file.
- The same sample often also gives **genomic EBVs** and **carrier status** for breed
  genetic conditions — order them together to save a second sample.
- A parentage mismatch is common in real herds (mis-mothered twins, a neighbour's bull):
  correct it, don't hide it.

## 4. Performance recording
Join the breed's performance-recording scheme if there is one: weights at set ages,
scan data, calving ease, temperament, fertility. This feeds the EBVs everyone uses
(see selection-index-and-inbreeding-control). Weigh within the scheme's age windows —
out-of-window records are often discarded.

## 5. Ownership changes and sales
- Transfer of ownership is a separate step with its own form or portal action; a buyer
  without a completed transfer can't register progeny.
- Give the buyer: registration certificate or digital record, health status,
  test results, and breeding history for females.
- For dogs/cats, limited vs full registration (breeding/export rights) is set by the
  breeder — decide before sale and write it into the sale agreement.

## 6. Your own back-up record
Keep a herd/kennel book or software export **outside** the society system: one row per
animal (ID, name, sire, dam, birth date, DNA status, sale/death date and reason).
Societies change systems; you'll want your history.

## Gotchas
- Retagging a lost tag with a new number without updating the society breaks the link.
- Birth dates guessed after the fact skew age-adjusted weights.
- Showing or selling an animal as "pedigree" with incomplete registration can breach
  society rules and consumer law.
- Societies may differ on whether foreign registrations transfer; check before importing.

## Verify
- [ ] Every animal born this season has legal ID + society ID recorded within days.
- [ ] Birth notifications submitted within the society's window.
- [ ] DNA parentage confirmed for all animals to be sold or kept for breeding.
- [ ] Your own off-system record matches the society's for a random 5 animals.
