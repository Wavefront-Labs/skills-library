# Embryo-transfer programme management (farm side)

**Goal:** multiply your best females fast. The ET practitioner or vet does flushing,
OPU, grading and transfers; your job is picking donors, having enough recipients
ready on the right day, and keeping the animals in condition. Most programme failures
are on the farm side. Written for cattle; sheep/goat ET is similar in logic but surgical
or laparoscopic.

## 1. Choose the route
- **MOET (conventional superovulation + flush):** donor is given FSH over ~4 days,
  inseminated, and flushed ~7 days after heat. Typical yield is often quoted around
  5–7 transferable embryos per flush, with huge variation (0 to 20+).
- **OPU-IVF:** oocytes aspirated from the ovaries, fertilised in the lab, embryos ready
  ~7 days later. Can be repeated every ~2 weeks, works on pregnant donors and young
  heifers, and lets you use one expensive (e.g. sexed) straw across many oocytes.
- **Buy embryos** in: no donor needed — only recipients.

## 2. Pick donors
- Top of your index (see selection-index-and-inbreeding-control), structurally sound,
  normal cycles, **body condition ~2.5–3.5 on a 5-point scale and rising**.
- At least ~60 days calved, cycling, no uterine infection.
- Some cows simply respond poorly; after two bad flushes, stop spending on her (or try OPU).

## 3. Recipients — the most underestimated part
- Plan **~1.5–2 recipients per expected embryo** if transferring fresh, because not every
  synchronised recipient will have a suitable corpus luteum on the day.
- Ideal recipients: well-grown heifers or young cows with good calving history, moderate
  size (calving ease!), fully vaccinated (BVD, lepto, IBR per local vet programme),
  on a **stable, rising plane of nutrition** — no ration, group or housing changes from
  ~3 weeks before to ~2 months after transfer.
- **Synchronisation**: the vet sets the protocol (prostaglandin, CIDR/progesterone-based,
  GnRH). Recipients' heat must match the donor's within roughly **±24 hours** for fresh
  transfer. Frozen embryos allow fixed-time transfer ~7 days after recipient heat.
- Record each recipient's heat date and time precisely — the ET tech will ask.

## 4. Timeline sheet (example, fresh MOET)
| Day | Donor | Recipients |
|---|---|---|
| −10 to 0 | sync start, heat (day 0) | sync started to land ±1 day of donor |
| ~9–13 | FSH twice daily, prostaglandin | — |
| ~14–15 | standing heat, AI ×2–3 | heat observed and recorded |
| ~21–22 | flush (day 7 after heat) | transfer same day |
| +28–35 days | — | pregnancy scan, sexing scan later if wanted |
Exact drug days and doses come from your vet's protocol; this is the shape, not a prescription.

## 5. Results to track per flush/session
Embryos recovered, transferable (grade 1–2), transferred fresh vs frozen, recipient
pregnancy rate (fresh grade-1 embryos into good recipients commonly 50–70%; frozen a
little lower — verify with your provider's own figures), calves born, cost per pregnancy.

## Gotchas
- **Animal health and paperwork:** export or registry rules often require donor testing
  and approved collection teams — check before the flush, not after.
- Moving or mixing recipients after transfer causes embryo loss.
- Overfat donors and heifers in a feed dip respond badly.
- Breed society registration of ET calves usually needs parentage DNA and ET notification.
- Drug names and withdrawal periods are country-specific; follow label and vet advice.

## Verify
- [ ] Donors chosen on index and condition, with ≥60 days post-calving.
- [ ] Enough synchronised recipients with recorded heat times for the expected embryos.
- [ ] No management changes planned for recipients in the 2 months after transfer.
- [ ] A results sheet with cost per pregnancy filled in after every session.
