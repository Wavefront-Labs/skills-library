# Responsible companion-animal breeding — health tests to homes

**Goal:** a healthy litter from tested parents, a safe birth, well-socialised young
and good placements. Dog-centred; cat differences noted. Numbers are commonly cited
guidance — have a vet who knows breeding on call and follow their advice.

## 1. Before mating
- **Health-test both parents** for the breed's known problems: hip/elbow scoring
  (minimum ages set by the scheme, e.g. 12 months in the UK BVA scheme, 24 in OFA final
  hips), annual eye exams, DNA tests listed by your kennel club / breed club, cardiac
  or hearing tests where relevant. Cats: e.g. PKD DNA, HCM echo for at-risk breeds,
  blood type (to avoid neonatal isoerythrolysis).
- Check the planned litter's **COI** in the kennel-club mating tool; prefer lower than
  the breed average.
- Bitch: mature, fit, not on her first season, up-to-date vaccines and worming. Many
  clubs/regulators cap litters per bitch and age at mating — check local rules.

## 2. Timing the mating
- Ovulation varies widely (day 9–14 is "average", but day 5–25 happens). Use **serial
  blood progesterone tests** from ~day 5 of season. Ovulation is often around 4–10 ng/ml
  (5 is a common marker), and fertile eggs are ready ~2–3 days after ovulation — mate
  then, and again 48 h later. Vet labs give their own reference values.
- Cats are induced ovulators: several matings over 1–3 days during calling.

## 3. Pregnancy and preparation
- Gestation: dogs ~63 days from ovulation (58–68 from mating); cats ~63–67 days.
- Ultrasound from ~25–30 days; X-ray at ~55 days to count puppies — knowing the count
  tells you when whelping is truly finished.
- Whelping box in a warm, quiet room a week ahead: pig rails, washable bedding, a
  heat source with a cool side. Room ~24–26 °C for the first week.
- Kit: clean towels, scales (grams), notebook, thermometer, iodine for cords, thread,
  clean scissors, heat pad, puppy milk replacer and feeding bottles, vet's number.

## 4. Whelping
- Take rectal temperature twice daily from ~day 58; a **drop of ~1 °C below her
  normal** (often under ~37.2 °C) usually means whelping within ~12–24 h.
- Stage 1 (restless, nesting, panting) up to ~12–24 h. Stage 2: puppies, typically
  every 30–60 min.
- **Call the vet for dystocia** if: strong straining >20–30 min without a puppy; >2 h
  between puppies with more expected; green/black discharge before the first puppy;
  stage 1 >24 h without progress; a puppy visibly stuck; bitch collapsing.
- Clear membranes from the face if she doesn't, let her tear the cord or tie and cut
  ~2 cm from the belly, count placentas.

## 5. The first weeks
- **Weigh daily** at the same time. Puppies may lose a little on day 1 but should gain
  steadily and roughly double birth weight by ~7–10 days. A puppy not gaining is a vet
  call, not a wait-and-see.
- Neonates can't regulate temperature — chilling kills. Check warmth and that all nurse.
- Watch the dam for eclampsia (restlessness, trembling, stiffness in early lactation) —
  emergency.
- Worm per vet schedule (often from 2 weeks), first vaccinations per vet.

## 6. Socialisation and placement
- Weeks 3–12 are the socialisation window: daily novel surfaces, sounds, handling, gentle
  people of all types, car rides, short crate time. Kittens: weeks ~2–7 matter most.
- Puppies leave no earlier than **8 weeks** (legal minimum in many places); kittens ~12–13.
- Screen buyers, use a written contract with a take-back clause, hand over health
  test results, vaccination card, microchip transfer and registration papers.

## Gotchas
- Breeding before health results are back "because she's in season now".
- Assuming whelping is over without a puppy count.
- Licensing: many jurisdictions require a breeding licence above a threshold of litters.

## Verify
- [ ] Both parents' health results in hand before mating.
- [ ] Mating timed by progesterone, not calendar.
- [ ] Puppy count known; vet briefed before due date.
- [ ] Daily weights logged and rising for every puppy.
- [ ] Each buyer has a contract, health records and microchip transfer.
