# Natural mating management — heats, joining and pregnancy checks

**Goal:** a tight, predictable calving/lambing with few empties, using sires rather
than AI. Figures below are common extension rules of thumb for temperate cattle and
sheep; breeds, climate and system shift them — check with your vet or advisor.

## 1. Before joining: females
- **Body condition score (BCS)** at joining drives conception and twinning. Targets
  often given: beef cows BCS ~2.5–3 (1–5 scale), ewes ~3–3.5.
- **Flushing ewes**: a rising plane of nutrition (better grazing or ~250–500 g/day
  supplement) for ~3–4 weeks before and through the first cycle raises ovulation rate.
- Heifers: well-grown — commonly ~60–65% of mature weight at first joining.
- Vaccinations and parasite treatment done **before** joining, not during.

## 2. Before joining: sires (~6–8 weeks ahead)
Sperm takes ~60 days to produce, so illness or heat stress two months ago shows up now.
- **Breeding soundness exam** by a vet: physical (feet, legs, eyes, teeth), scrotal
  circumference, testicle tone, penis/prepuce, and semen evaluation for bulls.
- Rams: feel the testes — firm, even, springy; lumps on the epididymis are a cull sign.
- Condition: fit not fat; a sire loses a lot of weight over joining.

## 3. Sire ratios and joining length
- Mature bull: ~1 per 25–40 cows; yearling bull ~1 per 15–20.
- Mature ram: ~1 per 50–100 ewes in a paddock; ram lambs ~1 per 25–40.
- In big multi-sire mobs, keep spare capacity; one injured sire in a single-sire group
  means a whole cycle lost — watch daily for the first 3 weeks.
- **Joining length**: two or three cycles (cattle cycle ~21 days, sheep ~17) gives a
  compact birth period; a 9-week joining is a typical cattle target.

## 4. Seeing who's been served
- **Ram harness/crayon (raddle)**: change crayon colour every ~14–17 days (one cycle).
  Ewes marked in colour 1 then again in colour 2 = returned = possible ram problem.
  Many first-cycle marks and few repeats = sire working well. Go from light to dark colours.
- **Cattle**: tail paint or chalk, heat-mount detectors, or a chin-ball marker on the
  bull. Rubbed-off paint = mounted. Note dates.
- **Teaser rams** (vasectomised) put in ~14 days before the entire rams synchronise ewes
  into a tighter first cycle.
- More than ~15–20% returns in the second cycle → suspect the sire, disease, or nutrition.

## 5. Pregnancy diagnosis
- **Ultrasound scanning**: cattle by a vet/scanner from ~30–35 days, ageable to the
  week up to ~3–4 months; sheep transabdominal at ~45–100 days from ram in, which also
  counts lambs (singles/twins/triplets) — feed accordingly.
- Book the scan for the right window *before* joining starts.
- Empties: decide on culling rules in advance; repeated empties are usually
  heritable-low-fertility or management problems.

## 6. Records
Joining dates, sire per group, marks by colour and date, scan results, birth dates.
Calculate: % pregnant, % in first cycle (target often ~60%+ for cattle), birth spread.

## Gotchas
- Leaving sires in all year: no batch, no records, and heifers bred too young.
- Hot weather and fever in sires cause temporary infertility for ~2 months.
- Two bulls in a small mob fight; one dominant bull may do all work and get injured.
- Mineral deficiencies (e.g. selenium, iodine, copper) vary by region — get local advice.

## Verify
- [ ] Every sire passed a soundness check 6–8 weeks before joining.
- [ ] Female BCS recorded at joining and on target.
- [ ] Marking colour/paint changed each cycle and returns counted.
- [ ] Scan booked for the right window; results recorded per female.
