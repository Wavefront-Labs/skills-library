# Mare management — from cycle to foal at foot

**Goal:** get a mare pregnant early in the season with one embryo, and get a healthy foal
up and suckling. The stud vet does scanning and drugs; this is the stud hand's playbook.
Numbers are common guidance for light breeds; drafts and ponies vary.

## 1. Planning the season
- Mares are **long-day breeders**: they cycle in spring–summer. For early foals
  (Thoroughbreds aim for foals soon after 1 January, the universal birthday in many
  stud books), put barren and maiden mares under **lights: ~16 h of light per day**
  from about 1 December (northern hemisphere) — roughly 60–90 days to induce cycling.
- Body condition moderate and rising; overweight barren mares are harder to get in foal.
- Stallion choice: conformation, temperament, race/sport record, progeny record,
  fertility figures, and whether the breed allows AI (Thoroughbred registries require
  **live cover**; most sport-horse studbooks allow chilled or frozen AI).
- Get the swabs/blood tests the stud and stud book require (e.g. CEM swabs, EVA, EIA)
  before the mare travels.

## 2. Reading the cycle
- Oestrous cycle ~21 days; heat lasts ~5–7 days; ovulation typically 24–48 h before
  the end of heat.
- **Teasing**: present the mare to a teaser stallion over a safe board daily. In heat:
  leans in, lifts tail, squats, urinates, "winks". Out of heat: ears back, squeals, kicks.
- The vet **scans follicles**: a follicle of ~35–40 mm+ softening, with uterine oedema
  that then fades, is close to ovulation. An ovulation-inducing drug (e.g. hCG or a
  GnRH analogue, vet-prescribed) is often used to time it.

## 3. Covering / AI timing
- Live cover: within ~48 h before ovulation; re-check by scan and cover again if not ovulated.
- Chilled semen: ~12–24 h before ovulation; frozen: as close to ovulation as possible
  (often within ~6 h either side), so scans may be every 6 h.
- Cover safely: mare twitched/hobbled per stud practice, tail bandaged, washed, an
  experienced team of at least two; stallion handler in charge.
- Post-breeding uterine inflammation: older mares may need vet-guided lavage/oxytocin.

## 4. Pregnancy checks
- **14–16 days**: ultrasound for pregnancy and **twins**. Twins usually end badly in
  horses, so one is pinched out by the vet now — early is the only easy window.
- Re-check ~25–30 days (heartbeat) and ~40–45 days; early loss is common.
- Vaccinate per vet (e.g. EHV-1 abortion vaccine at months 5, 7, 9 in many protocols;
  tetanus/flu boost 4–6 weeks before foaling for colostrum antibodies).

## 5. Foaling
- Gestation ~340 days (range ~320–370). Signs: udder filling, **waxing** of teats, milk
  calcium rising (test strips; a sharp rise suggests foaling within ~24–48 h).
- Foaling alarm or camera, clean straw box. Stage 2 is **fast — ~20 minutes**. Call the
  vet at once if: no progress after ~10–15 min of hard straining; one foot or a nose only;
  soles facing up; **red velvety bag** first (red-bag, premature placental separation —
  cut it and help get the foal out immediately).
- **Rule of 1-2-3** (common guide): foal standing within ~1 h, sucking by ~2 h,
  placenta passed by ~3 h. Keep the placenta for the vet to check it's complete.
- Foal **IgG test** at ~12–24 h; low results need plasma or colostrum from the vet.
- Foal heat around day 7–10: breeding on it is possible but conception is lower.

## Gotchas
- Missing the 14–16-day twin check.
- Retained placenta >3 h is an emergency (laminitis, sepsis).
- Weaning and young-stock sales prep (handling, feet, conformation photos) start months
  before the sale, not the week of.

## Verify
- [ ] Mare's swabs/tests done before travelling to stud.
- [ ] Covering/AI matched to a scanned follicle and confirmed ovulation.
- [ ] Twin check at 14–16 days recorded.
- [ ] At foaling: 1-2-3 timings logged, placenta checked, foal IgG tested.
