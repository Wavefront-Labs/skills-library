# Cattle AI: semen handling

**Goal:** get semen from the tank to the cow alive. This assumes you have done a hands-on
AI course; nobody learns rectal palpation from text. Always follow the semen company's
thaw instructions where they differ from the figures below.

## 1. The tank
- Liquid nitrogen is around -320 °F. Check the level regularly with a stick; SDSU says refill
  at about 4 cm of nitrogen left. Keep the tank clean, dry and ventilated, not directly on concrete.
- Never raise a canister above the frost line (about -103 to -106 °C). Wisconsin: no more
  than 8 seconds in the neck; SDSU: if you cannot find the straw in 7-10 seconds, lower the
  canister back into the nitrogen for 10-15 seconds before trying again.
- Use tweezers or forceps, never fingers.

## 2. Thawing and loading
1. Prepare a water bath first and check it with a thermometer. SDSU: 95-98 °F (35-37 °C),
   at least 30 seconds. Wisconsin: 96 °F for a minimum of 45 seconds.
2. Thaw no more than four straws at once (SDSU), and only as many as you can inseminate
   within 15 minutes (SDSU) or 10 minutes (Wisconsin) of thawing.
3. Dry the straw completely, check the identification, cut the end opposite the cotton plug
   (Wisconsin: a quarter inch below the sealed end, at 90 degrees), and load the gun.
4. Warm the gun before loading (inside your shirt) and keep the loaded gun warm and out of
   sunlight (shirt or gun warmer). Heat, cold, sunlight, sweat, blood, water, urine and manure
   all harm the semen. Disinfectants on a straw can kill sperm: rinse cleaned equipment well.

## 3. Deposit
Deposit semen after the cervix and before the bifurcation of the uterine horns (SDSU).
Timing, restraint and rectovaginal technique come from a hands-on AI course and the semen
company or vet; this skill does not give them.

## Verify
- [ ] Tank level checked; canister never raised above the frost line.
- [ ] Water bath thermometer-checked; gun warmed; straw dried.
- [ ] Straw used within the time limit your source gives.
