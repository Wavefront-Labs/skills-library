# Shot placement and ethical shot judgement

**Goal:** every shot you take is one you are confident will kill quickly — and the shots
you pass on are part of the skill, not a failure of it.

> Draft. Legal calibres, minimum energies, seasons, licences and blood-trailing dog rules
> vary by country and state; check your regulations. This is general practice taught in
> hunter-education and deer-stalking courses.

## 1. Before the season: know your effective range
- **Zero** the rifle with the exact ammunition you'll hunt with (common: ~2–4 cm high at
  100 m for a ~200 m point-blank range, or a true 100 m zero with a known drop table).
  Re-check after travel or any knock.
- Your **effective range** = the distance at which you can keep every first shot inside a
  ~10–12 cm circle (a deer's heart-lung zone is roughly 20–25 cm) **from field positions**
  (sticks, kneeling, prone off a pack) — not from a bench. For many hunters that is
  150–200 m, not 400.
- Practise cold-bore first shots; that's the only shot that matters.

## 2. Wind and approach
- Hunt into the wind (or crosswind) — game smells you long before it sees you. Carry a
  wind checker (powder puff) and check often; thermals rise in the morning as the ground
  warms and sink in the evening.
- Stalk slowly, move when the animal's head is down or behind cover, use terrain.
- Stand hunting: place stands downwind of expected travel routes; plan the approach so
  your scent doesn't cross the trail.

## 3. The heart-lung zone by angle
Aim for the **vital zone (heart and lungs)** — large, lethal and forgiving.
- **Broadside:** aim just behind the shoulder, about **one-third of the way up the body**
  from the bottom of the chest (slightly lower on deer; follow the back of the foreleg up).
- **Quartering away:** aim so the bullet exits through the **opposite shoulder** — the
  entry point moves back toward the last ribs.
- **Quartering towards:** aim at the near shoulder's inside point so the bullet travels
  back through the chest; harder and more meat damage — many hunters pass.
- **Straight on (frontal):** centre of the chest below the throat; small target, only at
  close range and from a steady rest.
- **Avoid** head and neck shots as a habit (small targets, jaw wounds), and **never take
  rear-end or running shots** with a rifle unless you're trained and it's legal/accepted (e.g. driven boar hunts).

## 4. The go / no-go checklist (every shot)
- Positive identification of species, sex/age class if regulated — and that it's an animal.
- **Safe backstop:** solid ground behind the animal; never skyline shots on a ridge.
- Animal **stationary**, at a clear angle, no branches/grass in the bullet's path.
- Within your proven range, from a solid rest, with a stable sight picture.
- No other animal directly behind or overlapping.
If any is "no", don't shoot.

## 5. After the shot
- Stay on the scope; watch the reaction and **mark the exact spot** the animal stood and
  where it was last seen (landmarks, GPS).
- Typical reactions: heart-lung hit — a kick or bound and a fast run of 30–100 m, often
  crashing; shoulder/spine — drops immediately (be ready for a follow-up shot if it tries to
  rise); gut hit — hunched, slow walk away.
- **Waiting:** for a good heart-lung hit, ~10–20 minutes, then approach; for a suspected
  gut/liver hit, wait several hours (many guides say 4+ for gut, ~2 for liver) unless legal
  tracking dogs are available. Pushing a wounded animal makes it run far.
- Approach from behind/uphill; check the eye — an open, glazed eye that doesn't blink when
  touched with a stick indicates death. Be ready to finish humanely.
- Follow up every shot — see game-tracking skills for trailing.

## Gotchas
- Range estimation errors cause most misses past 200 m; use a rangefinder.
- Shooting from offhand at "a quick chance" is how animals get wounded.
- Shoulder-shot animals can still run; don't assume a miss if there's no blood at once.

## Verify
- [ ] Zero confirmed with hunting ammunition this season.
- [ ] Effective field range established by your own groups from field positions.
- [ ] Every shot passes ID, backstop, angle, rest and range checks.
- [ ] Hit site and last-seen point marked; correct waiting time applied.
- [ ] Every shot followed up until found or definitively ruled a clean miss.
