# Cage trapping and exclusion programme

**Goal:** reduce the damage a pest species causes to an acceptable level at the lowest
cost and animal-welfare impact — which usually means exclusion first and trapping as a
targeted, measured campaign, not an endless one.

> Draft. Legal status of species, permitted methods, relocation rules (often illegal or
> discouraged) and dispatch methods vary widely; many native species are protected.
> Rodenticides and poisons are outside this skill and heavily regulated.

## 1. Define the problem
- **Confirm the species** from sign (tracks, droppings, damage pattern, hair, camera trap)
  — the fix for rats differs from squirrels.
- Quantify the **damage** (crop loss, chewed wiring, poultry losses) and set a goal:
  "no birds lost from the run", "< 5% seedling loss".
- Check the **legal status** and whether a licence or permit is needed.

## 2. Exclude first
Removing animals without fixing access just makes room for the next ones.
- Buildings: seal entry points (rats ~1.5–2 cm gaps, mice ~6 mm) with steel mesh/hardware cloth,
  metal flashing, cement; chimney caps; trim branches ~2 m from roofs.
- **One-way exclusion doors** over the main entry once others are sealed — animals leave and can't
  return. Never seal in dependent young during the breeding season: check, wait, or use timing.
- Gardens and crops: rabbit fencing (~1–1.2 m high, 25–30 mm mesh, bottom 30 cm turned
  outward and buried or pegged), electric fencing for larger mammals, netting over fruit,
  secure poultry housing (buried mesh apron, closed at dusk).
- Remove attractants: spilled feed, open bins, windfall fruit, harbourage (log piles, long grass) near buildings.

## 3. Cage trapping
- Size the trap to the species (manufacturer specs); a too-large trap catches non-targets.
- **Placement:** on established runs, along walls/fences, at entry points; level and stable
  (a rocking trap spooks animals). Shade it and cover partially.
- **Pre-bait:** wire the door open and bait for several days so animals feed confidently;
  then set. Baits: rats — peanut butter, grain; squirrels — nuts, corn; raccoons/possums —
  fruit, sweet bait (avoid meat/fish to reduce cat captures).
- **Check at least once every 24 h** (often a legal requirement), ideally early morning;
  many jurisdictions require more frequent checks in heat.
- Release non-targets immediately; record them.
- **Dispatch** humanely and legally, or handle according to rules — relocation is often illegal,
  stresses animals and moves disease; don't assume it's the kind option.

## 4. Shooting programmes (where legal and safe)
- Control shooting (e.g. rabbits, foxes) by night with lamps/thermal under appropriate
  licences, with safe backstops and land-owner/neighbour notification. Coordinated with
  trapping and fencing, not instead of them.

## 5. Monitor and decide
- Record traps set, nights set, catches, non-targets → **catch per unit effort (CPUE)**,
  e.g. catches per 100 trap-nights.
- Falling CPUE with falling damage = working; move traps or stop.
- Flat CPUE with continuing damage = immigration exceeds removal → fix exclusion or change method.
- Use sign counts, camera traps or damage surveys before and after.

## Gotchas
- Trapping during the breeding season can orphan young in a roof void (smell, welfare, legal problems).
- Handling: wear gloves; wildlife carries diseases (leptospirosis, rabies in some regions, ringworm).
- Unchecked traps are cruelty; never set more traps than you can check.

## Verify
- [ ] Species confirmed and legal status/permits checked.
- [ ] Entry points sealed or fenced; attractants removed.
- [ ] Traps sized, pre-baited and checked within 24 h; log kept.
- [ ] Non-targets recorded and released; dispatch method legal and humane.
- [ ] CPUE and damage tracked; programme adjusted or stopped based on the data.
