# Trapline sets and pelt handling

**Goal:** catch the target furbearer selectively and humanely, and turn it into a clean,
properly dried pelt that grades well at a fur sale.

> Draft. Trapping is heavily regulated: licences, seasons, permitted trap types and sizes,
> trap tagging, check intervals (often 24 h), setbacks from roads/paths, and landowner
> permission. Some traps are banned in some countries (e.g. many foothold traps in the
> EU/UK). Use only devices meeting your jurisdiction's humane trapping standards
> (e.g. AIHTS-certified, US trap BMPs).

## 1. Plan the line
- Scout before the season for sign (tracks, trails, slides, scat, den sites) along
  travel funnels: field edges, creek crossings, culverts, fence gaps, dam spillways.
- Get written landowner permission; map every set (GPS waypoints) so no trap is ever left behind.
- Keep the line short enough to check every trap within the legal interval (daily is best
  practice regardless).

## 2. Match trap to species and set
- **Water sets for aquatic species** (muskrat, beaver, mink): body-gripping traps sized to the
  species at runs, den entrances and feed beds; or foothold traps on **drowning/submersion
  sets** where legal. Body-grippers kill quickly when correctly sized and placed.
- **Land canines (fox, coyote):** foothold traps with offset or padded jaws, laminated, on
  short chain with swivels and a center stake or appropriate drag. Classic set:
  **dirt-hole set** — a hole ~7–8 cm wide angled back under a backing (clump/rock), bait
  deep in the hole, lure above, trap bedded solid in front ~15–20 cm, covered with sifted
  soil, pan level.
- **Flat set:** a urine/scent post (stick, clump) with the trap bedded in front — no bait,
  good for wary animals.
- **Raccoon:** dog-proof (enclosed-trigger) traps near water edges and trails reduce
  non-target catches of pets.
- Selectivity: pan tension (~1–2 kg for canines to exclude small animals), no visible bait
  for raptors, avoid sets near houses/pet routes.

## 3. Trap preparation
New traps: clean off grease, then dye/wax so they're odourless and rust-free. Handle with
clean gloves; carry in a clean basket. Bedding: the trap must not rock — a wobbling trap gets
dug up.

## 4. Checking and dispatch
- Check early in the morning. Approach calmly; release non-targets (a catch pole and a
  release board/cover for the head help; know how to compress springs).
- Dispatch quickly and humanely by legal methods for the species (often a .22 to the
  brain for canines; aquatic sets kill by design). Avoid damaging the pelt.

## 5. Pelt handling
- **Cool** the carcass, keep it clean and dry; comb out burrs and mud.
- **Skinning style:** case-skinned (cut across from heel to heel past the vent, peel like a
  sock) for fox, coyote, raccoon (often), mink, muskrat, marten; open-skinned (cut up the belly)
  for beaver (stretched round).
- **Fleshing:** remove all fat and meat on a fleshing beam with a two-handled fleshing knife,
  working from tail toward head, without cutting or "burning" the hide. Grease left on causes
  taint and slipping fur.
- **Stretching:** on the correct-sized wire or wooden stretcher (fur-in or fur-out according
  to species and buyer convention — e.g. muskrat, mink fur-in; fox, coyote turned fur-out
  partway through drying). Centre the belly/back, don't overstretch.
- **Dry** in a cool, shaded, well-ventilated place (~10–15 °C), never by a heater or in
  sun; ~2–5 days. Remove from the stretcher when dry; store cold, hanging, not in plastic.

## Gotchas
- Lost traps (no GPS, poor marking) keep catching — a legal and ethical failure.
- Pelts taken before prime (early season, blue-skinned) sell for little; check primeness by the skin colour on the flesh side.
- Rotten or "slipped" pelts are worthless; skin and freeze quickly in warm spells.

## Verify
- [ ] Licence, season, trap types and landowner permission confirmed; traps tagged if required.
- [ ] Every set has a GPS waypoint; daily check log kept.
- [ ] Trap type/size and pan tension matched to target species.
- [ ] Non-targets released or reported per law; dispatch method legal and quick.
- [ ] Pelts fleshed clean, stretched to the correct board and dried cool.
