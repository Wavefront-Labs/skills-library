# Predator and deer calling setups

**Goal:** an animal that comes looking for a sound and arrives where you can see it first
and shoot it — which is 70% setup and wind, 30% the call.

> Draft. Electronic callers are banned for some species/places (e.g. many states prohibit
> e-callers for big game or waterfowl); night hunting rules vary. Sequences below are
> common starting points, not proven formulas.

## 1. Setup is everything
- **Wind:** callers circle downwind to smell the source. Set up so the downwind side is
  open ground you can see and shoot (e.g. a field edge), or place the caller/decoy upwind of
  you so the animal's downwind loop brings it past you. Crosswind setups are the common
  compromise.
- **Visibility:** sit with your back to a tree/bush, shadowed, with the sun behind you. You
  should see the approach lanes before the animal sees you.
- **Distance:** caller/decoy ~20–40 m out for predators so their attention is off you; for
  deer, within your bow or rifle range of where the deer will stop.
- Approach the stand quietly, from downwind, without skylining.

## 2. Predator calling (coyote, fox)
- **Sounds:** prey distress (rabbit, rodent, bird), coyote vocalisations (howls, pup
  distress, challenge barks).
- **Sequence (starting point):** wait 2–3 min after settling; call **30–60 s** at low volume
  (a predator may be close), pause 2–3 min; repeat increasing volume. Total stand ~15–20 min
  for coyotes (longer, ~30 min, for bobcats, which come slowly).
- A coyote stopping out of range: a lip squeak or bark ("woof") often stops it for a shot.
- Use a motion decoy (fluttering feather/fur) near the caller to hold their eyes.
- Move a good distance (~1 km+) between stands so you don't call the same animals twice.

## 3. Deer calling (by season)
- **Grunt call:** short, soft contact grunts (3–4 grunts) every ~15–20 min early season;
  tending grunts during the rut. Don't overcall.
- **Doe bleat / estrous bleat:** effective pre-rut to rut; a can call works well.
- **Rattling** (antlers or rattle bag): pre-rut to peak rut. Sequence: grind and tickle
  antlers for 30–60 s, pause 10–15 min, repeat, louder with brush-raking and hoof thumps;
  bucks often approach from downwind — watch there.
- If a deer sees you calling or locates the source with nothing there, it rarely returns —
  a decoy solves that.

## 4. Decoys
- **Deer decoy:** visible from a distance, positioned so the deer approaches broadside.
  A buck decoy faced **toward** you (bucks often circle to confront a rival buck's face);
  a doe decoy faced **away** (bucks approach from behind). Unscented, handled with gloves;
  remove/cover when carrying (safety: blaze orange in transit).
- **Waterfowl/turkey** decoys follow their own patterns (see waterfowl-decoy-spreads-and-wingshooting).

## 5. Reading responses
- Animals coming straight and fast = committed: stop calling, get ready.
- Hang-ups (stops out of range): try softer, more subtle calls, or go silent; don't
  hammer the call louder.
- Track your stands: date, wind, call, response, time to response — patterns emerge.

## Gotchas
- Calling with your scent blowing to the sound's location educates every animal around.
- Overcalling to a visible animal makes it suspicious.
- Safety: predator/turkey/deer calls can bring other hunters stalking the sound — wear
  orange where required, never call while hidden in a way another hunter could shoot at.

## Verify
- [ ] Wind direction known; downwind approach visible and shootable.
- [ ] Caller/decoy placed out from you; sun and cover favour you.
- [ ] Sequence and stand time planned per species and season.
- [ ] Response log kept (stand, wind, sequence, outcome).
- [ ] Legal check on electronic calls and hours done before the hunt.
