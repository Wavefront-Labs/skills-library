# Guided hunt client management and safety

**Goal:** a client who is safe, hunts legally, takes a shot they can make, and leaves
feeling well looked after — even on a hunt where nothing was shot.

> Draft. Guide licensing, outfitter registration, insurance, firearm import rules, tagging and
> trophy export permits (e.g. CITES for some species) are jurisdiction-specific. This covers
> the practice, not the paperwork.

## 1. Before arrival
- Confirm in writing: species, dates, what's included, the physical demand (miles per day,
  elevation, cold), success expectations (give honest historic rates), trophy rules, tipping
  norms, cancellation terms.
- Collect: licence/tag details, firearm and ammunition (calibre, bullet type and weight —
  check it meets legal and your standards), medical conditions, medications, allergies,
  dietary needs, emergency contact.
- Send a kit list (clothing layers, boots broken in, binoculars, rangefinder).

## 2. Day one — the shooting check
Never skip it, however experienced the client says they are.
- **Zero check** at camp range: 3 shots at 100 m from a rest. Travel knocks scopes off.
- **Field-position assessment:** shots off sticks/bipod/kneeling at realistic distances.
  Set the client's **maximum distance** privately from what you see, and tell them it.
- Note their habits: safety use, trigger control, flinch, how long they take to settle.

## 3. Daily safety briefing
- Muzzle control and when chambers are loaded/unloaded (e.g. chamber only on the guide's
  instruction, unload before vehicles/fences/scrambles).
- Where everyone is when stalking; who has the final call on shooting (**the guide**).
- Plan for the day: route, terrain hazards, weather, turnaround time.
- **Emergency plan:** first-aid kit (including trauma tourniquet), satellite messenger/radio,
  evacuation points, the nearest hospital and how long it takes, who in camp knows the route.

## 4. Controlling the stalk and shot
- Speak in short, quiet commands; agree hand signals.
- **Confirm the animal** together (species, sex, legal/agreed age or trophy class) — point
  out by landmarks ("left of the dead tree, the one facing right").
- Set the client up: sticks positioned, range called, angle described, safe backstop
  confirmed, **then** "take him when you're ready" — or "no shot".
- Have a clear stop word. If the client is rushed, shaking or unsure, pass. You can find
  another animal; you can't take back a wounding.
- After the shot: watch, mark, and run the follow-up yourself (see blood-trailing-and-sign-reading).
  Be ready to back up with a follow-up shot if your rules and the law allow it and the client
  agreed beforehand.

## 5. Camp operations
- Food hygiene, safe water, warm dry sleeping; clear routines (wake-up, meals, departure times).
- Check clients' feet, layers and hydration — tired, cold clients make poor shooters and
  poor decisions.
- Keep vehicles, ATVs and firearms storage locked and organised; no alcohol until rifles are put away.

## 6. Trophy and meat care
- Photograph respectfully (blood cleaned, tongue in, set naturally) before field dressing.
- Cape correctly for the planned mount; skin, salt or freeze, label with client name, tag
  number and species. Handle meat as in field-dressing skills.
- Brief the client on export/import paperwork and timelines.

## 7. Communication
- Daily debrief: what happened, what's next, what they did well.
- When the hunt goes badly (weather, no game), say so plainly and offer the options.

## Gotchas
- Clients overstate ability and understate fitness: the day-one check is your real information.
- Pressure to "get a trophy" leads to marginal shots. The guide owns the call.
- Mixing guides and clients across a ridge without radio contact — always know where everyone is.

## Verify
- [ ] Written pre-hunt confirmation, client details and medical info on file.
- [ ] Zero and field-position shooting checked on day one; max distance set.
- [ ] Daily safety briefing and emergency plan delivered and understood.
- [ ] Every shot confirmed on species/class, backstop and range before the go command.
- [ ] Trophy and meat labelled and cared for; client debriefed each day.
