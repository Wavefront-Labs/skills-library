# Field dressing, quartering and meat cooling

**Goal:** clean meat cooled to 35-40 °F (3-4 °C) as fast as possible — heat and contamination, not
the shot, are what spoil most wild venison.

> Inspection, tagging, carcass-transport and disease-surveillance rules (e.g. CWD
> zones restricting movement of heads/spines, trichinella testing for boar/bear) vary by
> jurisdiction. Some hunting-meat sales require trained-hunter examination of the carcass.

## 0. Kit
Sharp knife, gloves, plastic bags for organs, ice or snow, clean water, cloth or paper towels.

## 1. Before the first cut
- **Tag** the animal as required.
- Look at the carcass and organs for abnormalities. If in doubt, keep the organs for inspection or report.

## 2. Traditional field dressing (gralloch)
1. Pinch up the skin behind the sternum; make a shallow cut and, with two fingers inside
   lifting the belly wall, cut back to the pelvis without puncturing
   the stomach or intestines.
2. Cut the diaphragm free from the rib cage; cut the windpipe and gullet as far forward
   in the chest as possible.
3. Pull out the lungs, heart and entrails (Penn State).
4. Keep heart and liver in a plastic bag on ice or snow.
5. Wipe out blood with a clean cloth or paper towels, or rinse with clean water and dry and ventilate the cavity.

## 3. Cool it fast
- Bacteria grow fastest between 40 and 140 °F (5-60 °C). Cool the carcass quickly to
  35-40 °F (3-4 °C) to prevent bacterial growth (Penn State Extension).
- Freezing a carcass before processing may toughen the meat; ice or snow sealed in
  plastic bags can go in the cavity.
- **Aging:** if you age a carcass, hold it at 40 °F (4 °C) or below for two to three days
  at most. With the hide on, Penn State gives 32-38 °F (0-3 °C) for 2-3 days.

## 4. Hides
- Hides to keep: rub the inside liberally with fine salt and let it absorb for 24-48 hours before sending to a taxidermist.

## 5. Pack-out and transport
- Keep meat cool in transit. Know the rules for transporting parts across state/country lines (CWD).

## Gotchas
- Clean your knife frequently between cuts to avoid dragging bacteria into the meat.
- Cook venison to a safe final temperature: whole cuts 145 °F (63 °C), ground 160 °F (71 °C), stews and leftovers 165 °F (74 °C) (Penn State Extension).

## Verify
- [ ] Animal tagged; carcass and organs looked over for abnormalities.
- [ ] Gut cavity opened without puncturing intestines.
- [ ] Cooling started promptly.
- [ ] Carcass/quarters cooled to 35-40 °F (3-4 °C) and held at or below 40 °F.
- [ ] Transport rules checked.
