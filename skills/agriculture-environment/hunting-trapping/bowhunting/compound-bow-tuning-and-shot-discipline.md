# Compound bow tuning and shot discipline

**Goal:** broadhead-tipped arrows that hit with your field points out to your maximum
range, and the discipline to shoot only at relaxed, broadside-to-quartering-away animals
inside it.

> Draft. Minimum draw weights, broadhead rules, crossbow legality and tracking-dog rules
> differ by jurisdiction. Have a pro shop set draw length and check the bow if you're unsure;
> never dry-fire a bow.

## 1. Fit and arrow build
- **Draw length** correct for your body (armspan ÷ 2.5 is a starting estimate,
  then fine-tune with the shop). Too long is the most common error — it causes string slap
  and inconsistent anchors.
- **Draw weight** you can draw smoothly while seated, cold, without raising the bow skyward.
- **Arrow spine** matched to draw weight, arrow length and point weight (use the
  manufacturer's spine chart/calculator). Total arrow weight ~6–8 grains per pound of
  draw weight is a common hunting range; **FOC** (front-of-centre) ~10–15%+ helps
  broadhead flight and penetration.

## 2. Tuning sequence
1. **Paper tuning** (field points, ~1–2 m from paper): a clean bullet hole or slight
   nock-high tear is the goal. For left/right or high/low tears, the common rule of thumb
   is to **move the rest toward the tear** ("chase the tear") — sources word this
   differently and it is easy to invert, so move ~1 mm, re-shoot, and confirm the tear
   shrinks before going further (direction wording unverified; check your bow maker's
   tuning guide). If rest moves don't fix it, check cam timing/yoke tuning.
2. **Walk-back or French tuning** for centre-shot: shoot at a vertical line from 10–40 m;
   arrows drifting left/right with distance → nudge rest horizontally.
3. **Broadhead tuning:** shoot field points and fixed broadheads at the same aiming point
   at ~20–40 m. If broadheads group away from field points, move the **rest toward the
   broadheads** (and string/arrow adjustments as needed) — not the sight. Mechanicals usually
   fly like field points but still check them.
4. Then set sight pins (e.g. 20/30/40 m) with broadheads; check every pin before the season.
5. Spin-test every arrow with its broadhead; discard wobblers.

## 3. Practise how you'll hunt
- From a treestand height or seated in a blind, in hunting clothes, from awkward stances.
- Know your maximum: the distance at which you put every arrow inside ~10 cm. For many
  hunters 25–35 m; past that, a deer's reaction to the shot ("jumping the string") at
  arrow speeds becomes a real factor.
- Use a rangefinder with angle compensation from stands.

## 4. Shot discipline
- Bow kills by **haemorrhage** through cut tissue — a pass-through of both lungs is the target.
- **Broadside or slightly quartering away only**, animal relaxed (not alert, looking at you,
  or tensed). Wait for the near front leg to step forward to open the vitals.
- From a treestand, aim lower and consider the **exit**: the arrow must pass through both lungs
  on its downward path — steep shots close under the stand often hit only one lung.
- Never shoot quartering-towards or frontal with a bow; avoid the shoulder blade.
- Safety: full-body harness attached from leaving the ground to returning (lineman's
  belt + tree strap / safety rope); haul bow and arrows up with a rope, never climb with them.

## 5. After the shot
- Watch the arrow (lighted nocks help) and the animal; mark the spot.
- Examine the arrow: bright frothy blood (lung) — wait ~30–60 min; dark red blood (liver) —
  wait ~3–4 h; green/brown matter with gut smell — wait ~6–12 h (often overnight in cool
  weather). Pushing a wounded animal ends the trail.
- Follow the blood trail quietly (see game-tracking skills).

## Gotchas
- Sights moved to "fix" broadhead groups hide an untuned bow; tune the bow.
- Dull or nicked blades: re-sharpen/replace — practice heads ≠ hunting heads.
- Cold weather adds clothing bulk: string catches on sleeves; check with an arm guard.

## Verify
- [ ] Draw length and weight comfortable seated and cold.
- [ ] Paper tune clean; broadheads group with field points at 30+ m.
- [ ] Every hunting arrow spin-tested and numbered.
- [ ] Max effective range proven from hunting positions; rangefinder carried.
- [ ] Harness worn at height; only broadside/quartering-away relaxed shots taken; wait time by hit sign.
