# Waterfowl decoy spreads and wingshooting

**Goal:** birds finishing into a landing zone 20–35 m from the gun, and shots that kill
cleanly — with nothing taken beyond the range your load and skill can handle.

> Draft. Shot type rules (lead bans over wetlands are widespread), bag limits, shooting
> hours, species ID requirements and electronic-call bans vary by country/state; check yours.

## 1. Read the birds and the wind
- Waterfowl land **into the wind**. Set up with the wind at your back or across your
  shoulder so birds approach facing you, slowing, in your shooting window.
- Scout where birds are actually feeding or loafing the day before; "be where they want to be"
  beats any spread.

## 2. Decoy spreads
- **Leave a landing hole** ~10–20 m across, ~20–30 m from the hide — that is where you want
  birds to drop their feet.
- **J-hook:** a dense cluster upwind with a tail curving downwind past the hide; birds work
  up the tail into the hook. Good for divers and puddle ducks.
- **U / open-pocket:** two arms downwind with the hole between; good for puddle ducks and
  field geese.
- **Goose field spread:** family groups, feeders (heads down) and some sentries; landing area
  in front of layout blinds, downwind edge.
- Quantity: a few well-placed decoys on small water (6–12) can beat 100 badly placed; big open
  water and late-season birds may need more.
- Add motion (jerk string, wind-driven) — still water with static decoys looks dead.
- Keep spacing natural (~1–2 m between ducks); spook-tight bunches signal alarm.

## 3. Concealment
- Brush the hide with local vegetation; shadow is as important as cover.
- Faces and hands are the brightest thing — face masks/paint, gloves.
- Stay still until the shot is called; one person calls the shot ("take 'em").

## 4. Gun, choke and load
- **Fit:** when the gun is mounted with eyes closed, opening them should show the bead
  aligned down the rib. Poor fit = consistent misses.
- **Non-toxic shot** (steel, bismuth, tungsten) where lead is banned. Steel patterns
  tighter and loses energy faster: use ~2 sizes larger than lead equivalents and open the
  choke one step (e.g. improved cylinder/modified). Check the gun is proofed for steel.
- **Pattern your gun** at 30 and 40 m on paper.

## 5. Shooting technique
- Mount the gun to your cheek, not your cheek to the gun; eyes on the bird, not the bead.
- Three common lead methods:
  - **Swing-through:** start behind the bird, swing through it, fire as the muzzle passes ahead.
  - **Pull-away:** start on the bird, pull ahead and fire.
  - **Maintained lead:** muzzle held a set distance ahead, matching speed.
  Whichever you use, **keep the gun moving after the shot** (follow-through) — stopping the
  swing is the commonest miss.
- **Range discipline:** most duck shots should be within ~35 m; ~40 m is a practical
  upper limit for most hunters and steel loads. Use decoys as range markers (set one at 35 m).
- Shoot the birds in your zone (left/right/centre agreed in advance); never swing across a
  partner or through the hide line.

## 6. Retrieves
- Mark each down bird; send the dog or retrieve immediately. Cripples first — a crippled duck
  on open water dives and is lost quickly; a second shot on water is often needed.

## Gotchas
- Calling too much at birds already committed flares them; let them work.
- Sky-busting (shooting at birds too high) wounds and educates birds.
- Ice, cold water and boats: waders/drowning risk — wear a belt, PFD in boats, never hunt alone on big water.

## Verify
- [ ] Wind direction checked; hide downwind of the landing hole.
- [ ] Spread has a defined 20–30 m landing zone and motion.
- [ ] Gun fits; load and choke patterned at 30/40 m; non-toxic where required.
- [ ] Range markers set; shots taken inside ~35–40 m only.
- [ ] Every downed bird marked and retrieved; cripples dispatched quickly.
