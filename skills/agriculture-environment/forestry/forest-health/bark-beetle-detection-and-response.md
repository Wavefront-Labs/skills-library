# Bark beetle detection and response

**Goal:** catch attacked trees while the brood is still under the bark and remove or
neutralise them before the next generation flies, to protect neighbouring trees.

> Species, generation counts and flight timing are regional (European spruce bark beetle
> in Europe; mountain pine beetle, southern pine beetle and *Ips* engravers in North
> America). Use your national or state forest-protection service for flight dates and
> legal duties. Specifics below come from Colorado State Forest Service pages on mountain
> pine beetle.

## 1. Know the triggers
Outbreaks follow host stress and breeding material: fresh unremoved windthrow and
snow-break, drought, overstocked stands, and host-species logs or slash left in the forest
through the flight season. Plan surveys after storms and during dry summers.

## 2. Detect: what to look for
- **Boring dust**: fine dust in bark crevices and on the ground at the base of the tree.
- **Pitch tubes**: popcorn-shaped resin masses on the trunk (brown, pink or white).
- **Woodpecker damage**: stripped bark, with piles of bark at the base.
- **Exit holes** in the bark appear after adults have emerged.
- **Crown colour is late**: foliage turns yellowish to reddish across the whole crown,
  usually 8-10 months after a successful attack. By then the beetles have usually left, so
  spend effort on neighbouring trees.
- **When to look**: the Colorado service recommends inspecting pines in the fall, after the
  late-summer flight to new host trees.

## 3. Confirm under the bark
Remove a patch of bark. Tunnels (galleries) on the inner bark, and live eggs, larvae,
pupae or adults, confirm infestation; empty galleries with exit holes mean the beetles
have gone. Gallery pattern identifies the species (check your regional guide). Record GPS
position, tree count and brood stage for each spot.

## 4. Respond
1. Remove infested trees and slash so beetles cannot emerge and fly to new trees; the
   Colorado service's best practice is removal by the following spring.
2. Disposal options: take to a local transfer station, landfill or secure wood drop-off
   site; or solar treatment (stack cut logs off the ground in full sun under clear
   plastic). Do not move infested wood except to a secure site, as it can spread beetles.
3. Preventive insecticide sprays: only on high-value trees, registered pyrethroid
   products used to the label, by a licensed applicator where required.
4. Verbenone and other pheromone products: effectiveness is debated; newer concentrations
   work when beetle pressure is low. Order before the flight season.
5. Trap trees: infested trees are cut and destroyed, usually in fall or winter. Only use
   them if you can remove them on time.

## Gotchas
- Felling infested trees and leaving them in the forest makes things worse.
- Red-top trees are usually already empty.

## Verify
- [ ] Surveys timed to the regional flight season and after wind or drought events.
- [ ] Each spot confirmed under bark with brood stage recorded.
- [ ] Infested trees and slash removed or treated before beetles emerge.
- [ ] Follow-up survey around each spot.
