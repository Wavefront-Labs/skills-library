# Directional felling cuts

**Goal:** put a tree on the ground exactly where planned, with the hinge controlling
the fall and you already on your escape route when it goes.

> Draft. This is a reference, not a substitute for hands-on certified training (e.g.
> UK Lantra/NPTC, US Game of Logging, national equivalents). Many jurisdictions require
> a certificate to fell commercially. Numbers are common teaching values.

## 0. Prerequisites
- PPE: helmet with mesh visor and ear defenders, cut-resistant trousers/chaps, chainsaw
  boots, gloves. Saw with working chain brake, chain catcher, throttle lockout, sharp chain.
- Never fell alone in the woods without a check-in plan; first-aid kit with a trauma dressing.

## 1. Size up the tree (do it every time)
1. **Lean:** sight it from two directions 90° apart (plumb line / axe handle held loosely).
2. **Crown weight:** heavy side branches, snow load, ivy.
3. **Hazards overhead:** dead limbs ("widowmakers"), hung branches, power lines (if within
   2 tree lengths of a line — stop, get the utility involved).
4. **Defects:** rot (sound the stem with the axe back), splits, forks; these weaken the hinge.
5. **Felling direction:** within ~30° of natural lean is easy; against the lean needs wedges
   or a pulling aid and experience.
6. **Felling zone:** clear 2 tree lengths of people and animals.

## 2. Escape routes
Clear two routes at about **45° back from the line of fall** on either side, 4–5 m long.
Never retreat straight back — butts kick back off stumps.

## 3. The notch (face cut)
- Depth **about 1/5 to 1/4 of the diameter** (never past ~1/3).
- **Open-face notch** (~70°+ opening): hinge holds until the tree is nearly on the ground;
  best general choice. **Conventional (45°)** and **Humboldt** (lower cut angled down)
  close earlier; Humboldt keeps the waste on the stump for steep ground.
- The two cuts must **meet exactly** — no overcut (bypass) past the apex, no gap.
  A bypass cuts through the hinge fibres.
- Aim: the notch's hinge line is perpendicular to the intended direction. Use the gunning
  sight on the saw housing.

## 4. Hinge and back cut
- Hinge thickness ≈ **10% of the diameter**; hinge length ≈ **80%** of diameter. Keep it
  even — a thinner side lets the tree swing toward it.
- **Standard back cut:** level, from the back, ~2–5 cm (about 1/10 dia) above the notch
  floor, stopping at the hinge. Set a wedge as soon as the bar is deep enough.
- **Bore (plunge) cut** for forward-leaning or larger trees: bore in through the centre
  behind the hinge with the lower nose of the bar (attack with the bottom quadrant to avoid
  kickback), establish the hinge, cut back toward the rear leaving a **strap** (holding wood),
  get clear-path ready, then cut the strap from outside. Prevents barber-chairing.
- **Barber chair** (stem splits vertically and the butt swings up): a heavy forward-leaning
  tree cut with a slow conventional back cut. Use a bore cut with strap release.

## 5. Wedges
Plastic/aluminium wedges only (never steel near a chain). Drive them before the kerf
closes. Two wedges side-by-side lift more evenly.

## 6. When it falls
Shout a warning, engage chain brake, walk away on the escape route watching the crown.
Stop ~5 m out and watch for falling branches.

## Hung-up tree
Do not cut the tree it's lodged in, do not climb, do not fell another tree onto it.
Use a winch, felling lever/turning hook, or rotate the butt; mark the area and get help.

## Verify
- [ ] Lean, hazards and felling zone assessed; two 45° escape routes cleared.
- [ ] Notch depth 1/5–1/4 dia, cuts meet, aimed along the intended line.
- [ ] Hinge ~10% thick, even across; back cut above the notch floor.
- [ ] Tree landed within ~1 m of the target line; stump shows clean, intact hinge fibres.
- [ ] No cutting above shoulder height, no one in the 2-length zone.
