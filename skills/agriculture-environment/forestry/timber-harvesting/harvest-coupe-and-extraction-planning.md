# Harvest coupe and extraction planning

**Goal:** a written, mapped plan that lets crews fell and extract the coupe with the
least ground damage, cleanest log presentation and no surprises (protected features,
soft ground, overhead lines, public access).

> Draft. Legal steps (felling licences, notifications, environmental rules) are
> jurisdiction-specific — check yours. Figures are typical for ground-based
> cut-to-length and whole-tree systems.

## 1. Map the constraints first
On a 1:2,500–1:10,000 base map (or GIS layer) mark:
- Coupe boundary, stand types, estimated volume by product (see forest-inventory).
- **Watercourses and buffers:** often 5–20 m no-machine zones; mark crossing points.
- Slopes: ground-based machines generally to ~30–35% slope (wheeled) / ~40–50% (some
  tethered or tracked); steeper → cable yarding or winch-assist.
- Soft ground, peat, wet flushes; archaeology; nesting sites; veteran/retained trees.
- Overhead power lines (plan a safe-distance zone, typically ≥ 2 tree heights for
  felling), pipelines, public paths and roads needing closures or signage.

## 2. Choose the system
| Ground / product | Typical system |
|---|---|
| Firm, gentle, CTL logs | Harvester + forwarder |
| Firm, gentle, whole tree to roadside | Feller-buncher + grapple skidder, processing at landing |
| Small or sensitive sites | Motor-manual felling + small forwarder / tractor + trailer / horse |
| Steep | Cable yarder (skyline), tethered machines |
The extraction distance matters: forwarding becomes expensive beyond ~600–1,000 m.

## 3. Lay out racks, trails and landings
- **Racks (thinning/CTL):** usually 4–4.5 m wide, at ~18–20 m centres so the harvester
  boom reaches the middle between racks; on clearfell, rack spacing follows machine reach.
- **Skid trails:** follow contours where possible, avoid long straight downhill runs that
  channel water; keep grades under ~15–20% for loaded travel.
- **Landings:** on firm, level, well-drained ground next to a road that takes timber
  lorries; space for each product stacked separately, for the loader crane to swing,
  and for lorry turning. Keep away from watercourses.
- Plan **brash mats** on racks over soft ground — the harvester lays branches in front of
  itself; forwarder traffic stays on the mat.

## 4. Felling direction and sequence
- Fell toward the rack at an angle (herringbone) so stems can be processed or
  skidded without swinging through the residual stand.
- Start furthest from the landing and work back, or work so loaded machines go downhill.
- Mark retained trees and no-go zones in the field with paint/tape consistent with the map.

## 5. Operating rules to write down
- Rutting limit (e.g. stop or move when ruts exceed ~20–30 cm or water starts flowing in
  ruts); wet-weather shut-down rule.
- Product specs (lengths, top diameters, defect rules) for the harvester's bucking file.
- Stacking: one product per stack, butts flush on one face, stacks clear of road edges.
- Emergency plan: grid reference / what3words-style location, nearest A&E, access route.

## Gotchas
- A beautifully drawn rack plan on a dry-summer visit can be unworkable in a wet winter.
- Landings are always too small. Size for the peak, not the average.
- Stream crossings planned on the day become sediment problems; site and build them first.

## Verify
- [ ] Map shows boundary, buffers, crossing points, slopes, hazards, retained features, landings.
- [ ] Extraction system matches slope and ground; max extraction distance known.
- [ ] Rack/trail spacing matches machine reach; brash-mat sections flagged.
- [ ] Written stop rules for rutting and weather, product specs, emergency plan.
- [ ] Permits/notifications and any utility or road closures confirmed before start.
