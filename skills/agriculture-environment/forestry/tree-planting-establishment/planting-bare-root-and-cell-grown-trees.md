# Planting bare-root and cell-grown trees

**Goal:** get 90%+ of planted trees alive after the first growing season. Most failures
are decided before the tree is in the ground — dried roots, bad holes, weeds.

> Draft. Densities and dates are temperate-zone norms (UK/N. Europe/N. America); follow
> your grant scheme, regional species guide and nursery instructions where they differ.

## 1. Site preparation (months before)
- **Weed control:** the single biggest survival factor. Spot-spray or scrape a 1 m
  diameter weed-free circle per tree, or cultivate. Grass is the worst competitor for water.
- **Wet or compacted ground:** mound (inverted turf/mineral mounds) or ridge so roots sit
  above the water table. **Brash-heavy restock sites:** windrow or scarify to get planting spots.
- Mark out the spacing: 2 m × 2 m ≈ 2,500/ha for productive conifer; 2.5–3 m for many
  broadleaf/native schemes (~1,100–1,600/ha). Follow the grant spec.

## 2. Stock handling — the cold chain
Bare-root fine roots die after **minutes** of exposure to sun and wind.
- Order stock with a root collar diameter and height spec; reject bundles with
  dry, mouldy or blackened roots (healthy root cortex is pale/white-ish when scraped).
- Keep bags sealed (co-extruded white-on-black planting bags), shaded, cool (ideally
  1–5 °C if storing; never frozen unless the nursery cold-stored them that way).
- Plant bare-root while dormant (roughly Nov–Mar in the northern temperate zone);
  cell-grown stock tolerates a longer window but still needs moist soil.
- On site, carry trees in a planting bag; take out **one tree at a time**. Never carry
  a handful of bare-root stems in the open, never dip-and-leave in a bucket for hours.
- If stock arrives and can't be planted within a few days, heel it in: trench, lay
  bundles open, cover roots firmly with soil, water.

## 3. Planting technique (notch / slit with a spade)
1. Stand on the spot; clear surface litter.
2. **T- or L-notch:** push the spade in vertically, lever back; make a second cut at
   right angles and lever to open the notch. For cell plugs, a plug spade or dibber hole
   the size of the plug is enough.
3. Insert the tree so roots hang straight down, **not J-rooted** (bent up), with the
   root collar at or just below ground level (planting slightly deep is safer than high
   on dry sites; on mounds plant into the top, not the side).
4. Withdraw the spade, close the notch by heeling firmly beside — not on — the stem.
5. **Tug test:** a light pull on the stem should not move it.
Good planters do 800–1,500+ cell plugs a day on prepared ground; quality before speed.

## 4. Protection and aftercare
- Browsing deer/rabbits: shelters (spiral guards for rabbits, 1.2–1.8 m tubes for deer)
  or fencing. Tubes need a stake and checking; remove once trees are stable.
- Weed control around each tree for at least 3 growing seasons (herbicide, mulch mat
  or hand weeding).
- Where large pine weevil is present on conifer restock sites, use pre-treated or
  protected stock or delay replanting per local guidance.

## 5. Survival assessment
- At the end of the first season: count living trees on fixed-radius plots (e.g. 0.01 ha
  circular, 5.64 m radius) at ≥ 1 plot per hectare, at least 5 per site.
- Express as stems/ha versus target. **Beat up** (replant gaps) if stocking falls below
  the scheme minimum (often ~80–90% of planted density).
- Record causes: drought, browse, weevil, weeds, frost-heave, planting error (J-roots,
  loose trees — dig a few dead ones up to look).

## Gotchas
- A tree that looks fine in spring can be dead from root desiccation by June.
- Planting in frozen or waterlogged soil, or before a dry spell on light soils, kills stock.
- Heeling the tree on the stem breaks the bark at the collar.

## Verify
- [ ] Weed-free spot or cultivation in place before planting.
- [ ] Bare-root stock bagged, shaded and handled one at a time; roots never visibly dried.
- [ ] Spot-check 20 trees: roots straight, collar at ground level, tug test passes.
- [ ] Protection matched to the browsers actually present.
- [ ] First-season survival plot count done and beat-up decision recorded.
