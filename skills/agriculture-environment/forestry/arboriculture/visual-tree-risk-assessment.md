# Visual tree risk assessment

**Goal:** decide whether a tree poses an unacceptable risk to people or property, and
what proportionate work (if any) brings it down — without over-felling healthy trees.

> Draft. This summarises the logic shared by common methods (ISA TRAQ basic assessment,
> QTRA, VTA). Formal assessments for insurers, councils or court need a qualified assessor
> and the method's own forms. Advanced diagnostics (resistance drilling, sonic tomography)
> are out of scope here.

## 1. Targets first
A defective tree with nothing under it is low risk. Identify every target within
**1–1.5× tree height**: roads, footpaths, houses, play areas, parked cars, power lines.
Estimate **occupancy**: constant (house), frequent (busy road), occasional, rare
(woodland path). Note whether targets can be moved (relocate a bench) — cheaper than tree work.

## 2. Walk the tree: roots → stem → crown
Stand back first (from 2–3 sides, ~1 tree-height away), then close up.
**Roots / root plate**
- Soil heaving or cracking on the side away from the lean; recent lean change.
- Severed roots (trenching, kerbs), compaction, raised grade burying the root flare.
- Fungal fruiting bodies at the base (e.g. *Meripilus*, *Kretzschmaria*, *Armillaria*,
  *Ganoderma*) — many indicate root or butt decay; identify, don't guess.
**Stem**
- Cavities, cracks, seams, bulges/ribs (wound wood over internal cracks), bleeding.
- **Codominant stems with included bark** (a tight V with bark pinched in) — a classic split point.
- Mallet sounding: a hollow note suggests decay; extent needs diagnostic tools.
- Lean: is it old and corrected (crown grown upright) or new?
**Crown**
- Deadwood: size and position over targets (dead limbs > ~5–10 cm over a path matter).
- Hangers (broken limbs lodged in the crown), cracked limbs, weak unions,
  heavy end-weight, sudden dieback, sparse foliage.
- Previous topping: regrowth from topping cuts is weakly attached and grows heavy.

## 3. Rate the risk
Combine three things (TRAQ-style wording):
1. **Likelihood of failure** within the timeframe (e.g. 1 year): improbable / possible /
   probable / imminent.
2. **Likelihood of impact** given the failure and occupancy: very low / low / medium / high.
3. **Consequences**: negligible / minor / significant / severe.
Overall risk → low / moderate / high / extreme. Record which **part** (a limb, the stem,
the whole tree) drives the rating — the remedy usually targets only that part.

## 4. Mitigation, least invasive first
- Remove the specific deadwood or hanger; reduce an over-extended limb.
- Crown reduction (shorten to suitable growth points, typically ≤ 20–30% of crown)
  to cut sail area and lever arm — not topping.
- Cable/brace codominant stems (dynamic or static systems), with inspection schedule.
- Move or restrict the target (close a path, move a bench).
- Fell only when the risk can't be reduced acceptably otherwise. Consider retaining a
  monolith/standing stem for habitat where safe.
- Set a **re-inspection interval** (often 12–18 months, shorter for known defects, and
  after storms).

## Gotchas
- Bats and nesting birds are legally protected in many countries; check before work.
- Tree Preservation Orders/conservation areas (UK) or municipal permits may apply.
- Healthy-looking trees fail from root decay; dramatic-looking cavities are often sound
  (a hollow tube can be strong). Don't overreact to one symptom.

## Verify
- [ ] Targets and occupancy listed within 1–1.5× height.
- [ ] Root, stem and crown each inspected and defects recorded with location and photos.
- [ ] Risk rating states the failing part, timeframe, likelihood and consequence.
- [ ] Mitigation is the least invasive option that reduces risk to acceptable.
- [ ] Re-inspection date and legal checks (protected species, permits) recorded.
