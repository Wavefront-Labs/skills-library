# Coppice rotation management

**Goal:** a woodland split into coupes cut in sequence, so every year there is a crop
to cut and a mosaic of young-to-old growth that suits both produce and wildlife.

> Draft. Rotation lengths and practice reflect temperate European (especially UK)
> coppice. Felling permission may be needed (UK: felling licence above set volumes
> unless exempt) — check before cutting.

## 1. Plan the rotation
- **Rotation length** by species and product:
  - Hazel: 7–10 yrs (hurdles, thatching spars, bean poles, pea sticks).
  - Sweet chestnut: 12–20 yrs (fencing, posts, cleft paling).
  - Ash, alder, field maple, birch: 15–25 yrs (firewood, turnery, charcoal).
  - Willow (osier beds): 1–3 yrs (basketry, living willow).
- **Number of coupes = rotation length** (one coupe per year), area of each
  ≈ total coppice area / rotation. Coupes under ~0.25–0.5 ha are easily overwhelmed by
  deer browsing and shading from edges; aim larger where you can.
- Sequence coupes so neighbouring ones are of different ages (patchwork), and draw them
  on a map with the year of cut. That map is your woodland plan's core.

## 2. Cutting the stools
- **Season:** winter, after leaf fall and before buds burst (roughly Oct–Mar in the
  northern temperate zone) — sap is down, the stool regrows vigorously, and nesting
  birds are not disturbed.
- Cut every stem in the stool, close to the stool (~5–15 cm above the old cut),
  with a **clean, sloping cut facing outward** so water runs off. Avoid tearing bark
  — undercut heavy stems first.
- Tools: bow saw, billhook, small chainsaw for larger stems.
- Remove or stack brash; leave the stool open to light.
- Old, neglected ("overstood") coppice can be restored but some stools die — cut in
  the same way and **layer** (peg down a living stem to root) to fill gaps.

## 3. Protect the regrowth
Browsing of the first 2–3 years' shoots by deer (and rabbits) is the main cause of
failed coppice.
- **Dead hedge / brash fence** around the coupe using the cut brash, stakes woven,
  ≥ ~1.5–1.8 m high.
- Deer fencing (~1.8–2 m) for valuable or repeatedly browsed sites.
- Deer management (culling in coordination with neighbours) is often needed alongside.
- Check the protection monthly in the first growing season.

## 4. Standards
In coppice-with-standards, keep timber trees (often oak) over the coppice at roughly
**30–60 standards/ha** of mixed ages; more shades the coppice out. Fell and replace a
few each rotation; recruit new standards from good maidens or singled stems.

## 5. Rides, edges and glades
- Keep rides wide (at least as wide as the adjacent trees are tall for sun), with
  **scalloped edges** and graded structure (short vegetation → shrubs → trees).
- Cut ride edges on rotation in late summer/autumn; that's prime habitat for
  butterflies and other insects.

## 6. Produce
Sort as you cut: straight rods by length for hurdles/poles, larger stems for firewood
(season 12–24 months under cover to < 20% moisture), brash for dead hedges or chips.

## Gotchas
- Cutting in spring or summer weakens stools and disturbs nesting birds (legally
  protected in many places).
- Tiny coupes surrounded by tall trees regrow poorly from shade.
- Unprotected first-year regrowth can be eaten to the stool in one season.

## Verify
- [ ] Map with numbered coupes and cut years, number = rotation length.
- [ ] Cuts close to stool, sloping outward, no torn bark.
- [ ] Browse protection in place before bud burst; checked monthly.
- [ ] Standards density within ~30–60/ha; recruitment planned.
- [ ] Next year's coupe marked and felling permission checked.
