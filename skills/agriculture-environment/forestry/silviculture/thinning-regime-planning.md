# Thinning regime planning

**Goal:** decide when, how hard and from where to thin an even-aged stand so the
volume goes onto the best stems, the stand stays wind-firm, and the regime leads
cleanly into the chosen regeneration system at the end of rotation.

> Draft. Numbers below are typical temperate-conifer rules of thumb; regional yield
> models (e.g. local yield tables, FVS in the US, Forestry Commission yield models in
> the UK) and species guides override them. Verify locally before marking anything.

## 1. Know what you are steering toward
Write the objective in one line before marking a single tree: sawlog volume on a
40–60 year rotation, maximum biomass, continuous cover, wildlife structure. The
objective sets the thinning type:
- **Low thinning (from below):** remove suppressed and subdominant trees. Safe,
  mimics natural mortality, little gain in growth of the main crop.
- **Crown thinning (from above):** remove competitors of chosen crop trees in the
  dominant/codominant class. Puts growth on the best stems; the default for quality timber.
- **Selective / diameter-limit "high-grading":** removing the biggest, best trees. This
  is *not* a thinning regime — it degrades the stand. Avoid it.
- **Systematic (line/row) thinning:** removing every nth row for machine access,
  usually combined with selective thinning between racks in first thinning.

## 2. When to thin — the density trigger
Thin on density, not on the calendar:
1. Measure basal area (BA, m²/ha or ft²/acre) with a prism/relascope from 10+ points
   (see forest-inventory skills), plus trees per hectare and quadratic mean diameter.
2. Compare with a density guide: stand density index (SDI) or relative density (RD,
   % of maximum). Common working bands: **thin at ~55–60% of max SDI, thin back to
   ~35–40%**; below ~25–35% the site is under-used; above ~60% self-thinning
   (competition mortality) begins.
3. Proxy when no guide exists: thin when live crown ratio of the crop trees falls
   toward ~35–40%, or when crowns of dominants begin to close and lower branches die fast.

First thinning typically falls when the stand reaches merchantable size *and* before
crown ratio collapses — leaving it late is the commonest mistake.

## 3. How hard — intensity
- Express intensity as % of BA removed. 25–35% of BA per entry is usual; heavy entries
  (>35–40%) in a previously unthinned, tall, slender stand invite windthrow and snow break.
- Check **height:diameter (H/D) ratio** of the residual trees: H/D above ~80–100 (both in
  the same units) marks unstable, slender stems — thin lightly and more often there, or
  not at all on wind-exposed shallow soils (some UK guidance designates "no-thin" regimes
  on high wind-hazard sites).
- Re-entry cycle: usually every 5–10 years, when the stand regrows to the upper trigger.

## 4. Which trees — marking
1. Choose crop trees first: 150–300/ha for sawlog conifers (fewer, larger for
   hardwoods, often 50–100/ha), spaced fairly evenly, straight, vigorous, good crown.
2. Release each crop tree by removing 1–3 direct crown competitors.
3. Then remove dead, diseased, forked and damaged stems where it helps the stand.
4. Leave the rest — "neutral" trees maintain stability and shade out epicormic shoots.
5. Mark consistently (paint on the cut side and stump height, so check-cruising is easy).

## 5. Fit it to the regeneration system and rotation
- **Clearfell + replant:** thinning is purely about final crop quality and value.
- **Shelterwood:** final thinnings become a preparatory cut, then a seed cut (leave
  ~30–50% canopy), then removal cuts once regeneration is established.
- **Continuous cover / irregular:** single-tree or group selection, keeping a target
  diameter distribution and harvesting stems at a target diameter.
- **Rotation length:** compare mean annual increment (MAI) with current annual increment
  (CAI); volume rotation is where MAI peaks (CAI = MAI). Financial rotation is usually shorter.

## Gotchas
- First thinning in a stand that was never thinned and is now tall and slender is the
  highest windthrow risk you will create. Go light, or accept a no-thin regime.
- Extraction racks remove ~15–20% of BA on their own — count them in the intensity.
- Thinning in sap season raises bark damage on residual trees and, for some species
  (e.g. pine, spruce with Heterobasidion), root-rot infection — treat stumps (urea or
  Phlebiopsis) where that is the regional practice.

## Verify
- [ ] Pre- and post-thin BA, stems/ha and QMD recorded from a proper plot sample.
- [ ] Residual density falls in the target band of the local density guide.
- [ ] Removal ≤ planned % of BA, including racks; H/D of residuals checked on exposed sites.
- [ ] Marked trees are predominantly crop-tree competitors, not the best stems.
- [ ] The next entry date and the end-of-rotation regeneration method are written down.
