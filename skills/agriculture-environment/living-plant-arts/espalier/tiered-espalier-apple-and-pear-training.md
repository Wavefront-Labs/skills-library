# Tiered espalier apples and pears

**Goal:** a tree trained flat with a central stem and pairs of horizontal arms (tiers),
built one tier a year, and maintained by summer pruning so it fruits on short spurs and
holds its shape for decades.

## 1. Choose plant and site
- **Spur-bearing** cultivars only (most apples and pears); avoid **tip-bearers** (fruit on
  shoot tips — they'd be pruned off).
- **Rootstock** sets vigour: apples on semi-dwarfing **M26** or **MM106** are common for
  espaliers; pears on **Quince A**. Very dwarfing stocks may be too weak to fill the arms.
- A sunny wall or fence (or free-standing post-and-wire frame). Allow roughly
  **3–4.5 m** of wall width per tree for a three- or four-tier espalier on MM106.

## 2. Supports
- Horizontal **wires** (galvanised ~2.5–3 mm) on vine eyes held ~10–15 cm off the wall
  for air flow, at **~40–60 cm vertical spacing** — the first at ~40–45 cm above ground.
- Tension wires with straining bolts.
- Fix **bamboo canes** to the wires along the line each arm will take (and a vertical cane
  for the leader) — you tie shoots to canes, not directly to wire, for straight lines.

## 3. Year 1 (from a maiden whip — unbranched one-year tree)
1. Plant in winter (bare-root) at the centre of the frame.
2. Cut the whip back to a bud just above the first wire (~5 cm above it), choosing a spot
   with **three good buds** below the cut: one for the new leader (top) and one each side
   for the arms.
3. In spring/summer: train the top shoot **vertically** on its cane; train the two side
   shoots on canes at **~45°** (easier growth), and rub out other buds/shoots on the stem
   or cut them to 2–3 leaves.
4. **End of the first summer/autumn:** lower the two arms gently to **horizontal** and tie
   them to the wire. (If one arm is weaker, keep it more upright longer to boost it.)

## 4. Following years: one new tier each year
- Each winter, cut the leader back to just above the next wire, again choosing three buds,
  and repeat the process for the next tier.
- Meanwhile, the arms extend each year; tie the extension in along the wire. Once an arm
  reaches its allotted length, treat its tip like a side shoot (cut back in summer).
- When the top tier is formed, stop the leader by cutting it at the top tier (later, cut
  regrowth back in summer each year).
Bought "ready-trained" two- or three-tier espaliers skip a few years.

## 5. Summer pruning — the main maintenance (modified Lorette)
In late summer (often **mid–late summer** in the UK/northern temperate zone, when new
shoot bases turn woody):
- Cut **new side shoots growing from the main arms** (longer than ~20 cm) back to
  **three leaves** above the basal cluster.
- Cut **side shoots growing from existing spurs** back to **one leaf** above the basal cluster.
- Leave short shoots (< ~20 cm) — many will form fruit buds.
- Regrowth after summer pruning can be cut back to one bud in winter.

## 6. Winter (light)
- Thin crowded old spur systems on mature trees: remove weak and older spurs so the rest
  get light.
- Check ties — loosen any that are cutting in; replace rotten canes.
- Remove diseased (canker) wood.

## Gotchas
- Lowering arms to horizontal too early stalls them — vertical growth is vigorous,
  horizontal is fruitful.
- Winter pruning hard on espaliers produces lots of vigorous upright water shoots; the
  shape is held by **summer** pruning.
- Timings are for temperate northern-hemisphere conditions and **not confirmed for your
  climate**; spacing depends on cultivar and rootstock vigour.

## Verify
- [ ] Cultivar is spur-bearing; rootstock vigour matches the space.
- [ ] Wires level and tensioned; canes fixed for every arm.
- [ ] Each year's tier: two balanced arms tied horizontal by the first autumn.
- [ ] Summer pruning done to 3 leaves (from arms) / 1 leaf (from spurs).
- [ ] Ties checked and loosened each winter; tree fruiting on spurs along the arms.
