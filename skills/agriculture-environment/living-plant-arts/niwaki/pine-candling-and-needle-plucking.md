# Pine candling and needle plucking (niwaki)

**Goal:** turn a garden pine into a tree of distinct, layered **pads** (clouds) with light
and air between them, by controlling where it grows each spring and thinning it each autumn.

## 1. How pines grow (why the method works)
- Pines produce new growth once a year (single-flush species like Scots or black pine)
  from buds at the shoot tips — the spring shoots are **candles**.
- Pines **don't reliably regrow from bare wood without needles**: cut beyond the last
  needles and that branch usually dies back. Always cut to where needles or buds remain.
- Vigour concentrates at the top and outer tips; left alone, the inner and lower growth
  weakens and dies, and pads merge.

## 2. Spring: candle pinching (*midoritsumi*)
When candles have extended but before the needles open out from them (roughly mid- to
late spring in temperate climates):
1. On each shoot tip there is often a cluster of candles: one strong central candle and
   several smaller ones. **Remove the strongest central candle** and any extras, keeping
   **two** reasonably even candles pointing sideways (forming a Y, for a flat pad).
2. **Shorten the remaining candles** — by about **one-half to two-thirds** in strong
   areas (top, outer tips), less (or not at all) on weak, inner and lower shoots.
   Snap by hand to avoid browning needles that scissors cut.
3. This balances vigour across the tree and keeps pads compact and dense.
*Variation:* for **Japanese black pine** (*Pinus thunbergii*), many practitioners instead
cut the whole new candle off in early summer (**decandling**) on vigorous trees, leaving
a second flush of shorter shoots — a different technique, mainly used in bonsai and on
black pines specifically. Don't decandle single-flush species like Scots pine.

## 3. Autumn–winter: thinning and needle plucking (*momiage*)
Late autumn into winter (the pine is less active, sap less sticky):
1. **Thin shoots:** on each branch tip, reduce to **two** shoots (a fork), removing
   downward-pointing growth, upright shoots growing out of the pad, and crowded shoots.
   Make the pads flat-topped and slightly domed.
2. **Pluck needles:** pull old (previous years') needles off and thin this year's
   needles so roughly the **same number of needle pairs remain on each shoot** — often
   fewer on strong shoots (e.g. ~5–8 pairs) and more on weak ones (e.g. ~10–15 pairs), to
   rebalance strength. Pull needles **downward/with the grain** gently, or cut them,
   leaving the sheath; never strip the buds.
3. Remove all needles growing beneath the branch and on the branch's underside so pads
   read clearly from below and light reaches inside.
4. **Clean the interior:** take out dead twigs and debris. The whole tree should look
   airy — you should see sky through and between the pads.

## 4. Building structure (longer term)
- Choose a main framework of branches and **remove competing branches cleanly** with a
  saw (to the collar; see
  agriculture-environment.landscape-grounds-keeping.amenity-tree-shrub-care.formative-pruning-and-hedge-cutting).
- Lower branches can be **bent and tied down** with rope or guy wires to a peg or bamboo
  to create horizontal tiers (protect the bark with padding); release after a year or two
  when the branch holds.
- Leave a few "sacrifice" shoots where you want branches to thicken; remove them later.

## 5. Safety and tools
Use a stable tripod orchard ladder (*sanpo kyatatsu*-style) — standard ladders in trees
are dangerous. Sharp pruning shears and a pruning saw; gloves (resin is sticky, clean
tools with alcohol or a solvent).

## Gotchas
- Cutting all candles equally makes the strong areas stronger and the weak areas die.
- Over-plucking weakens the tree; on a struggling pine, do little and fix roots/soil first.
- Species, timing and pair numbers vary; figures above are **commonly quoted guide
  values, unconfirmed for your species or climate**.

## Verify
- [ ] Spring: at most two candles left per tip; strong areas shortened more than weak.
- [ ] Autumn: shoots reduced to forks; needle numbers balanced by vigour.
- [ ] No cuts made back to needle-less wood.
- [ ] Pads separated with visible gaps of light between them.
- [ ] Next spring, growth is more even across top and bottom of the tree.
