# Making and watering a kokedama

**Goal:** a small plant growing in a moss-wrapped soil ball that holds together for a
year or more — and a watering routine based on weight, which is what keeps it alive.

## 1. Choose a plant
Best: plants that like even moisture and tolerate a smallish root run — ferns (maidenhair,
Boston), pothos/epipremnum, peace lily, philodendron, fittonia, ivy, small begonias,
some orchids (use a bark-rich mix), and outdoors small maples or other bonsai-type
seedlings. **Avoid** succulents and cacti for a first one (the wet moss rots them unless
you change the mix and water much less). Start with a plant from a ~9–10 cm pot.

## 2. Materials
- **Soil:** a sticky mix that holds shape. Traditional: **keto-tsuchi** (Japanese peat clay)
  mixed with **akadama**. A common substitute: roughly **2 parts peat-free compost or
  coir/peat : 1 part akadama or bonsai soil : a little clay or bonsai keto** to bind. You
  need it to form a ball like soft modelling clay when wet. (Exact ratios vary; adjust
  until it holds.)
- **Sheet moss** (e.g. *Hypnum*) live, or preserved/dried sphagnum. Use moss from a
  supplier — **collecting wild moss is restricted or illegal in many places**.
- **String:** cotton or jute (rots in 1–2 years, plants roots may grip by then) or nylon
  fishing line (invisible and lasting).
- Bowl of water, scissors, gloves, a tray.

## 3. Make it
1. Soak the moss in water for a few minutes; squeeze it out.
2. Wet the soil mix and knead until it holds a ball when squeezed. Make a ball roughly
   **1.5–2 × the size of the plant's root ball**.
3. Take the plant out of its pot, gently remove **about a third of the old soil** (loosen
   the outside), keep roots moist.
4. Split the soil ball in half, sandwich the roots, press it back together around the
   root ball, and squeeze to firm it — add soil until it's a round, compact ball.
   Alternative: wrap the roots in a layer of damp sphagnum first, then the soil.
5. Lay the sheet moss, **green side out**, around the ball, covering it completely with
   overlaps; trim excess.
6. **Bind:** tie the string around the base of the stem, then wrap around the ball in
   many directions (like a ball of yarn), each wrap pulled snug into the moss but not
   cutting. 15–30 wraps is typical. Tie off underneath. For hanging, add a loop of string
   or wire.
7. Soak the finished ball for a few minutes and let it drain.

## 4. Watering by weight (the main skill)
1. **Weigh it** (kitchen scale) or note how heavy it feels just after a full soak and drain.
2. Check every day or two: when it feels **noticeably lighter** (often around half of the
   soaked weight for small balls, but learn your own), it needs water.
3. **Soak** the ball in a bowl of room-temperature water (rainwater or filtered if your tap
   water is hard) for **~10–20 minutes**, until bubbles stop.
4. **Drain** on a rack until it stops dripping before hanging or putting back on its dish.
Indoors in warm rooms this is often every 4–7 days; more often in summer and with
hanging balls in moving air; less in winter. A light mist on the moss in between
keeps it green but doesn't water the plant.
Feed with a quarter-to-half strength liquid fertiliser in the soak water every few weeks
in the growing season.

## 5. Light and placement
Bright, indirect light for most houseplant subjects; direct sun dries and bleaches moss.
Avoid radiators and air-conditioning vents. Sit on a dish or slate (they drip).

## 6. Trouble signs
- **Brown, crispy moss:** too dry or too much sun — soak, mist, move.
- **Mould, smell, yellowing lower leaves:** too wet / not draining; let it dry more
  between soaks; improve airflow.
- **Roots poking out everywhere:** plant has outgrown it — remake into a bigger ball or
  pot it up.

## Gotchas
- Kokedama dry out far faster than potted plants — the most common death is
  forgetting it for a week in summer.
- Moss often browns indoors over months even when the plant is fine; refresh with new
  moss when you rebind.
- Soil ratios and soak times are typical hobby guidance, **unconfirmed for your
  plant or conditions**.

## Verify
- [ ] Ball holds its shape when squeezed after soaking; no loose soil showing.
- [ ] Moss fully covers the ball and is bound firmly with string.
- [ ] "Full" weight known; watering triggered by weight, not by calendar.
- [ ] Ball drained before hanging; plant shows new growth after a month.
