# Wiring and repotting bonsai

**Goal:** position branches with wire so they set in place without scarring, and repot
on the tree's schedule so roots stay healthy in a small pot — the two routine
techniques that most of bonsai styling rests on.

## Part A — Wiring

### Choose the wire
- **Anodised aluminium:** soft, forgiving, reusable; most common for deciduous trees
  and beginners.
- **Annealed copper:** stronger per thickness, hardens as it's bent; traditional for
  conifers (pines, junipers).
- **Gauge:** the wire should be about **one-third the thickness of the branch** (aluminium;
  copper can be a bit thinner). Test: it should hold the branch where you bend it. Too
  thin won't hold; too thick damages bark.
Common sizes run from ~1 mm to 6 mm+; keep several gauges.

### Apply it
1. Work trunk-to-tip, thicker branches first. Water the tree the day before (hydrated
   branches bend better), not immediately before (wet bark slips).
2. **Anchor** each wire: wrap around the trunk or wire **two branches of similar size with
   one wire** — over the trunk between them — so each side anchors the other.
3. Coil at **~45°** to the branch, evenly spaced, firm against the bark but not biting.
   Steeper (closer) coils don't hold; flatter coils slip.
4. Wire in the direction you'll bend: to bend down, the wire should come *from below* at
   the bend (so it supports the outside of the curve). Don't cross wires over each other.
5. Bend slowly, supporting the outside of the bend with your thumbs to avoid cracking.
   Listen for cracking; small cracks in junipers usually heal, a split in a brittle
   branch may not. Protect big bends with **raffia** wrapping under the wire, or use
   guy wires anchored to the pot or trunk.
6. Don't wire immediately after repotting; avoid wiring weak or sick trees.

### Remove it on time
Check every couple of weeks in the growing season. **Remove the wire when it starts to
bite** into the bark — often 4–12 weeks on fast deciduous trees, many months on conifers.
**Cut it off** with wire cutters in short pieces rather than unwinding (unwinding damages
bark and buds). If the branch springs back, rewire in a new path.

## Part B — Repotting

### When
- **Timing:** typically **early spring as buds swell**, just before leaf-out — roots
  regrow quickly. Tropical species (e.g. ficus) in warm early summer.
- **How often:** young/fast trees every 1–2 years; mature deciduous every 2–4; older
  conifers every 3–5. Signs: water sits on the surface or runs straight through, roots
  circling and filling the pot, tree lifting.

### Steps
1. Prepare: new or cleaned pot with **drainage mesh** taped over holes and **tie-in wires**
   threaded through; fresh soil sieved to remove dust.
2. Cut the old tie-in wires; ease the tree out (run a knife round the pot edge).
3. Comb out roots with a root hook/rake from the outside in, working the surface and
   underside. Remove ~**a quarter to a third** of the root mass on vigorous deciduous
   trees; much less on pines and junipers (some keep part of the old soil for their
   mycorrhizae). Cut thick downward roots to favour fine radial roots.
4. Base layer of coarser soil, then a mound of mix; set the tree at the chosen angle and
   front; wire it firmly into the pot (an unstable tree won't regrow roots).
5. Work soil into all voids with a chopstick (gentle poking, no air pockets).
6. **Water thoroughly** until it runs clear from the drainage holes.

### Soil
A free-draining, mostly inorganic mix is standard, e.g. roughly **akadama : pumice : lava
1 : 1 : 1** for many species, more grit for pines/junipers, more akadama for deciduous;
particle size ~2–6 mm, sieved.

### Aftercare
Shade and shelter from wind and frost for ~2–4 weeks; no fertiliser for ~4–6 weeks
(damaged roots burn); water when the surface begins to dry, not on a fixed schedule.

## Gotchas
- Wire left on too long leaves spiral scars that take years to grow out.
- Repotting in autumn or mid-summer is risky for most temperate species.
- Species timings and ratios above are **general guidance, not checked per species**;
  look up your species or ask a local club — climate changes timing.

## Verify
- [ ] Wire about 1/3 branch thickness, at ~45°, anchored, no crossings.
- [ ] Branches hold position after bending; checked fortnightly for biting.
- [ ] Repot timed to bud swell; root reduction appropriate to species.
- [ ] Tree firmly tied in; water drains through freely.
- [ ] Shade and no-feed period followed; new growth appears within weeks.
