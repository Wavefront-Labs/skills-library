# Setting, pursing and brailing a purse seine

**Goal:** encircle a school of pelagic fish with a wall of net, close the bottom before
the school dives, and land the catch in good condition. Purse seining is fast,
coordinated work with heavy loads on wires and blocks; this is an overview of the
sequence for crew, not a substitute for on-board training. Vessel layouts (tuna seiners
with skiffs vs. small-pelagic seiners with side thrusters) differ.

## 1. The net
- **Corkline** (floats) on top, **leadline** (weights) on the bottom, net walls between —
  often hundreds of metres to over a kilometre long and tens to ~200+ m deep depending on
  fishery.
- **Purse rings** hang from the leadline on bridles; the **purse line** (wire) runs
  through them. Hauling the purse line closes the bottom like a drawstring bag.
- **Bunt**: strongest, finer-meshed section where fish are concentrated at the end.
- Stack the net correctly after every set so it pays out cleanly next time —
  corks, web and rings in separate rows.

## 2. Finding and judging a school
- **Sonar and echo sounder** show school size, depth and movement; birds, surface
  "boiling" and whale/dolphin activity are visual cues. See echo-sounder-and-sonar-interpretation.
- Judge: **depth** (a school deeper than the net will escape under it), **speed and
  direction** (lead the set ahead of where it's going), size (worth setting?) and species/size
  (mixed or juvenile schools — move on if non-target).
- Current and wind direction: plan so the net drifts away from the vessel and doesn't
  foul the propeller.

## 3. Setting
1. Approach from the downwind/downstream side, taking into account current, the school's
   heading and vessel turning circle.
2. Release the net end (tied to a skiff, or a buoy/"dan" with a light) and steam in a
   **circle around the school** at speed, net paying out over the stern.
3. Close the circle by coming back to the net end; pick it up and connect both ends.
4. Time from release to closing: minutes — the faster the better.

## 4. Pursing
1. Start **heaving the purse line** from both ends with the purse winch as soon as the circle
   is closed.
2. A **tom weight** may be dropped to help sink and close the purse rings quickly.
3. Keep the vessel clear of the net (side thrusters, skiff towing the vessel off); watch the
   corkline — fish trying to escape below show as the net being pulled down.
4. When rings are up ("rings up"), the bottom is closed.

## 5. Drying up and landing the catch
1. Haul the net through the **power block** (overhead hydraulic block), stacking it on deck,
   gradually reducing the volume of the bag ("drying up") until fish are concentrated in
   the bunt alongside the vessel.
2. **Brail** (a big dip net lifted by crane) or **pump** fish aboard into refrigerated
   seawater (RSW) tanks or the hold — see icing-chilling-and-hold-stowage.
3. Avoid over-crowding in the bunt for too long — fish damage and oxygen depletion lower
   quality.

## 6. Bycatch and slipping
- Release protected species (dolphins, turtles, sharks) **before** the bag dries up —
  e.g. backdown procedures and Medina panels in tuna fisheries; hand-release at the
  corkline. Follow regional fisheries body rules.
- **Slipping** (releasing an unwanted catch) is regulated in many fisheries: it must be
  done early while fish are still alive, and often reported.
- FAD (fish aggregating device) sets have higher bycatch — subject to closures and limits.

## Gotchas
- Setting on a deep or fast school → "water haul" (empty set).
- Net rolling up or the purse line fouling on rings — slow down and clear before continuing.
- Crew near the power block and running net: stay clear of the stack area and bights.
- Heavy catches can list the vessel — know your stability limits; don't overload.

## Verify
- [ ] Net stacked cleanly with purse line and rings free before every set.
- [ ] School depth checked shallower than effective net depth before setting.
- [ ] Bottom closed (rings up) before the school can sound.
- [ ] Bycatch released early and logged; catch stowed in RSW/ice promptly.
