# Crab and lobster pot strings and hauling cycles

**Goal:** a string of pots that fishes all season, lands legal-sized animals in good
condition, and doesn't kill whales or turtles or fish on as ghost gear when lost.
Minimum sizes, escape-gap sizes, pot limits, season dates, V-notching, marking and rope
rules **vary by fishery and change often** — check current regulations every season.

## 1. The pot
- **Parlour pots/creels** (two chambers: kitchen with bait and a parlour animals move
  into, making escape harder), side- or top-entrance pots, collapsible creels for small boats.
- **Escape gaps/vents** sized so undersized animals walk out (legally required in many
  fisheries) — check the gauge size.
- **Biodegradable panel or ghost-panel** (rot-away cord or clip) so a lost pot stops
  fishing after a period — required in many places.
- **Bait**: oily fish (mackerel, herring), fish frames or salted bait in a bait bag or on a
  spike; secure so it lasts the soak. Fresh bait fishes noticeably better.

## 2. Strings (trawls) of pots
- Pots on a **ground line** (weighted or sinking line) at even spacing (e.g. ~15–25 m),
  string length from a few pots to 50+ (local limits on string length and end lines).
- **End lines** to surface buoys with the required markings (vessel ID, colour codes).
  Use **sinking rope** for ground lines and minimise floating rope in the water column
  (entanglement risk).
- Weak links/breakaway buoy lines, or "ropeless"/on-demand buoy systems where whale
  entanglement rules apply (e.g. North Atlantic right whale areas).

## 3. Placing the gear
- Lobster: rocky ground, edges, boulder fields and reef margins. Brown crab: often sand and
  gravel next to rock, deeper water. Snow crab and dungeness: soft bottoms at specific depths.
- Shoot along depth contours, not across them; mark both ends in the plotter with the string ID.
- Spread strings to learn where catches are good — then move the weak ones regularly.
- Avoid other fishers' gear and closed or protected areas.

## 4. Hauling cycle
- **Soak**: commonly ~1–3 days for lobster/crab depending on season and bait, longer in
  bad weather (but catch quality and bait condition drop).
- Haul with the **pot hauler**: line over the sheave, pots come up in sequence, one crew
  member lands and opens each pot. **Never stand in coils of rope** as the string shoots away.
- Empty, sort and re-bait each pot quickly, stack in shooting order on deck.
- Shoot the string again (moved or same ground) — the shooting run is the most dangerous
  moment: rope runs fast; keep feet clear and a knife within reach.

## 5. Sorting and handling the catch
- **Gauge** every animal with the legal measure (lobster carapace length; crab carapace
  width). Undersized → return immediately to the water, gently, ideally near where caught.
- **Berried (egg-bearing) females**: return (required in most fisheries). In V-notch
  fisheries (e.g. parts of New England, UK), cut the notch in the designated tail flipper
  as required, and never land V-notched animals.
- **Band** lobster claws; keep in seawater tanks or covered wet, cool and shaded.
  Remove soft-shelled animals if markets reject them.
- Record catch per string per haul (CPUE) — your best guide for moving gear.

## Gotchas
- Lost pots without degradable panels = ghost fishing for years.
- Floating rope in end lines in whale areas → entanglement and closures for everyone.
- Overloading a small boat's deck with stacked pots in a swell — stability risk.
- Seasonal moults: soft crabs die in storage — check shell hardness.

## Verify
- [ ] Every pot has a correct-size escape gap and degradable panel.
- [ ] Buoys and end lines marked and rigged to current entanglement rules.
- [ ] String positions logged; CPUE recorded per string.
- [ ] Every animal gauged; berried/V-notched animals returned.
