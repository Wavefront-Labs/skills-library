# Longline rigging, baiting, setting and seabird mitigation

**Goal:** fish thousands of baited hooks efficiently, land quality fish, and keep
seabirds, turtles and sharks off the hooks. Covers **demersal** (bottom) longlines
(cod, haddock, halibut, toothfish) and **pelagic** longlines (tuna, swordfish) in outline.
Hook types, mitigation measures and gear limits are set by your fishery and regional
fisheries body — **use their current rules**; the measures below are the widely used set
promoted by ACAP and others.

## 1. Gear
- **Mainline**: the backbone line, km long. Demersal: on the seabed with anchors and
  buoy lines at each end. Pelagic: suspended from floats, often with branchlines down
  to a target depth.
- **Snoods/gangions/branchlines**: short lines from mainline to hook, spaced evenly
  (e.g. ~1–2 m for demersal autoline gear; tens of metres apart for pelagic).
- **Hooks**: **circle hooks** reduce deep hooking and turtle bycatch in many fisheries
  (often required); size matched to target.
- Systems: **tub gear/hand-baited** (small boats), **autoline** (automatic baiting
  machine, line stored on magazines), pelagic **line shooter** with a mainline reel.

## 2. Baiting
- Bait: squid, mackerel, herring, sardine, etc. Fresh-frozen bait, cut to a consistent size.
- Bait firmly so it survives the drop and doesn't spin off; hook point exposed.
- **Thaw bait** so it sinks — frozen bait floats near the surface longer and attracts birds.
- Autoline: check the baiting machine's hit rate — missed hooks are wasted effort.

## 3. Setting
1. Plan the set across the bottom contour (demersal) or across the thermal front/current (pelagic).
2. Deploy the end buoy and anchor (demersal) and pay out mainline at the planned speed.
3. **Line weighting** sinks hooks fast: integrated-weight line or added weights at fixed
   intervals (pelagic: weighted swivels near the hook, per rules).
4. Set the **bird-scaring (tori) line** before the first hook goes in (see §5).
5. Log start/end positions, times, number of hooks, bait type.

## 4. Soak and hauling
- **Soak time**: often a few hours to overnight; longer soaks increase sand-flea/sea-lice
  damage and shark depredation.
- Haul with the **line hauler**; one crew member at the rail gaffs fish (into the head, not
  the body, to protect fillets) as they arrive.
- Unhook, bleed and chill immediately (see icing-chilling-and-hold-stowage).
- Bycatch: cut the line as close to the hook as safely possible or use a **dehooker**;
  turtles — follow handling guidance (dehookers, line cutters, bring small turtles aboard
  to remove hooks per protocol).
- Hands near a hauler and running hooks: gloves, no loose clothing, a knife in reach.

## 5. Seabird mitigation (core measures)
Birds dive on baited hooks as they're set — albatrosses and petrels drown. Common best
practice (often required in combination):
- **Bird-scaring line (tori line)**: a line with streamers towed from a high pole at the
  stern, covering the area where hooks sink. Lengths ~100–150 m with streamers every
  ~5 m are typical specs; follow your fishery's spec.
- **Night setting** (between nautical dusk and dawn) — fewer birds forage.
- **Line weighting** so hooks sink beyond reach quickly.
- **Hook-shielding devices** (pelagic) that release the hook only at depth.
- **Offal management**: don't discharge offal during setting/hauling; it attracts birds.
Record and report all seabird interactions.

## Gotchas
- Setting in daylight without a tori line, or with a tangled one.
- Line weights not attached → hooks drift near the surface.
- Shark and marine mammal depredation (orcas, sperm whales taking fish off the line): move
  off, shorten soaks; some fisheries allow pots instead.
- Hooks in hands: a common injury; know how to treat or get help.

## Verify
- [ ] Hooks, snood spacing and weights match fishery rules.
- [ ] Tori line deployed before the first hook every set.
- [ ] Set log with times, positions, hooks and mitigation used.
- [ ] Bycatch handling kit (dehooker, line cutter) on deck and crew trained.
