# Otter trawl: setup, shooting, towing and hauling

**Goal:** get a bottom trawl to the seabed in shape — doors spread, groundgear in
contact, net open — tow it efficiently, and recover it without injury. This is an
overview for deckhands and new skippers; every vessel's gear and procedures differ,
and **deck operations under tension kill people** — follow your vessel's safety
procedures and skipper's instructions. Gear (mesh sizes, devices) is set by
fisheries regulations in your area.

## 1. The gear, front to back
**Warps** (steel wire from winch drums) → **trawl doors** (otter boards — spread the gear
sideways by hydrodynamic force) → **sweeps/bridles** (herd fish into the net mouth) →
**net**: headline with floats above, **footrope + groundgear** (rubber discs, rockhopper
or bobbins) below → body and extension → **codend** (where catch collects, with the
regulated mesh size and any selectivity devices).

## 2. Setup checks before shooting
- Codend: correct mesh size and type for the fishery, tied off securely (codend knot or
  quick-release), lifting strop attached.
- **Selectivity devices** fitted and undamaged: square-mesh panels (let small fish
  escape), sorting grids (e.g. Nordmøre grate in shrimp trawls), turtle excluder devices
  where required.
- Groundgear matched to bottom: light for sand/mud, rockhopper for hard ground.
- Doors: check shoes for wear, backstrop/chain settings (they set door angle of attack —
  the main spread adjustment).
- Warps marked at regular lengths (or winch counters working); both sides equal.
- Sensors (door spread, headline height, catch sensors) charged and mounted.

## 3. Shooting
1. Vessel steaming slowly ahead into or with the planned tow direction.
2. Codend over the stern first, then net, streamed out behind.
3. Doors connected, lowered into the water, checked to be "flying" (spreading) before
   paying out warp.
4. Pay out warp to the planned **scope**: rule of thumb ~**3:1 warp-to-depth** in deeper
   water (often 4–5:1 in shallow water) — adjust from sensor readings and gear behaviour.
5. Brake winches evenly; both warps same length (check marks), otherwise the gear tows
   crooked.

## 4. Towing
- **Speed** typically ~2.5–4 knots over ground for demersal trawls — follow your net
  maker's design speed.
- Watch: door spread and headline height (net geometry), warp tension evenness, engine load.
- Unequal warp tension or a door collapsing → the gear may be fouled or crossed.
- Tow duration: long enough for a catch, short enough for quality (often 2–5 h demersal);
  shorter tows reduce crushing and improve survival of discards.
- Avoid known obstacles (wrecks, cables), closed areas and protected habitats —
  **check your plotter's closed-area layers and VMS rules**.

## 5. Hauling
1. Slow down; heave warps evenly on both winches.
2. Doors to the gallows/hang-off point, secured (chains/ hooks) before disconnecting.
3. Net hauled on the net drum; codend lifted aboard with the split-strop/lifting gear.
4. **Stay out of the bight** of every rope and wire, and away from the codend while it's
   lifted. Never stand beneath a suspended load.
5. Release catch into the hopper/pound; codend retied, net checked for damage
   (see net-mending-and-rope-splicing).

## 6. Reducing bycatch
Use the devices required (and voluntary ones that work in your fishery), avoid known
juvenile areas and spawning closures, move on when catches show high bycatch, and log
discards accurately — many fisheries have landing obligations and observer coverage.

## Gotchas
- Crossed doors after a sharp turn — turn wide with gear down.
- Fasteners (snagging the seabed): reduce speed, heave carefully, never use full power
  on snagged gear — vessels have capsized from a stuck trawl pulling the stern down.
- Ice and wet decks + running wires = top injury causes. PFDs on deck.

## Verify
- [ ] Codend mesh and selectivity devices match current regulations.
- [ ] Warps equal and scope set for depth; sensors show doors spread and net open.
- [ ] Tow plotted clear of closed areas and obstructions.
- [ ] Deck crew briefed on hauling positions and bights before every haul.
