# Icing, chilling and hold stowage

**Goal:** fish that reaches the buyer firm, bright and safe — because quality is set in
the first hour after capture and price follows. The rule: **bleed → clean → chill to
~0 °C fast → keep it there**. Food-safety rules (HACCP-based in most markets) apply on
board.

## 1. On deck: minutes count
- Get fish **out of the sun and wind** immediately; deck temperature can raise fish
  temperature rapidly.
- **Sort** by species and size (grade) as the catch comes aboard; release legal-to-return
  or undersized fish alive and quickly; log what's discarded where landing obligations apply.
- Don't throw, stand on or stack fish deep — bruising and gaping lower grade.

## 2. Bleeding and gutting
- **Bleed** live fish: cut the gill arches or the throat/artery behind the gills, put in
  clean running seawater (or a bleeding tank) for **~10–20 min** depending on size and
  species. Bled fillets are whiter, keep longer and fetch more.
- **Ikejime** (spiking the brain, sometimes destroying the spinal cord with a wire) for
  premium markets (tuna, snapper, bass) — delays rigor and improves texture.
- **Gutting**: required for many demersal whitefish (cod, haddock) to slow spoilage from
  gut enzymes; remove guts and kidney (blood line along the backbone), wash well. Small
  pelagics (herring, mackerel, sardines) are usually left whole and chilled fast instead.

## 3. Chilling
- **Target**: fish core temperature close to **0 °C** (above freezing) as fast as possible
  — ideally within hours; many quality schemes demand core ≤ ~4 °C on landing (check your market).
- **Slurry ice** (ice + seawater, a few % salt) cools fastest because it surrounds the fish:
  great for first chilling.
- **RSW/CSW** (refrigerated or chilled seawater tanks) for bulk pelagics; keep tanks at about
  −1 to 0 °C and circulating.
- **Flake or crushed ice in boxes**: a layer of ice under, around and on top of each layer of
  fish. A common rule: **~1 kg ice : 1–2 kg fish** in temperate waters, more in the tropics;
  top up if ice melts away.
- **Scombroid species** (tuna, mackerel, mahi-mahi): **histamine** builds up fast if fish
  warm — chill immediately (within a couple of hours to ≤ ~4 °C, colder is better); histamine
  isn't destroyed by cooking or freezing.

## 4. Stowage
- Clean, food-grade boxes (or pounds with shelving) — don't stow fish too deep (the weight
  crushes the bottom layers): shelve every ~0.5 m in pounds.
- Drainage so meltwater doesn't soak fish; belly-down for gutted fish.
- Hold insulated and clean; wash and disinfect between trips.
- Label boxes: species, date/haul, area — traceability is required in many markets and
  feeds catch documentation.

## 5. Records
Log: haul time, chilling start, hold/RSW temperatures (at least daily, ideally continuous),
ice used, landing time. Use a probe thermometer to spot-check fish core temperature.

## Gotchas
- Fresh water (from rain or a hose on gutted fish for long periods) damages flesh quality — use
  clean seawater.
- Freezing fish accidentally in over-cold brine (below about −1.5 °C) spoils fresh-fish grades.
- Dirty boxes contaminate fresh catch with spoilage bacteria.
- Rough handling during rigor (stiffening) causes gaping fillets.

## Verify
- [ ] Every fish bled (where appropriate) and in slurry ice or RSW within minutes.
- [ ] Hold/RSW temperatures logged and near 0 °C throughout the trip.
- [ ] Ice ratio maintained; no fish exposed in boxes when checked.
- [ ] Boxes labelled for traceability; landing temperature checked with a probe.
