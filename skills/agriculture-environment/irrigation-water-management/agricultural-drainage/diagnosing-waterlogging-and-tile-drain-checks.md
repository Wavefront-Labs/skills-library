# Diagnosing waterlogging and checking tile drains

**Goal:** work out *why* a paddock is wet before spending on drains, then keep existing
tile and mole systems flowing.

## 1. Is it surface water or a high water table?
Walk the paddock after rain and again 2–3 days later.
- **Ponding on the surface that drains within a day or two** of rain stopping,
  with dry soil below 20–30 cm → a **surface** problem: poor shaping, hollows, a
  compacted or crusted layer. Fix with land forming, surface drains, cultivation of
  the pan — not tile.
- **Soil saturated at depth for days**, water seeping into holes → a **water table**
  (regional or perched over a clay layer) → subsurface drainage may pay.

## 2. Auger-hole / pit diagnosis
1. Auger or dig holes to ~1.2–1.5 m at the wet spot and at a "good" spot.
2. Note the depth where water seeps in, and check again after **24 h** — the level it
   settles at is the water table. Water rising to near the surface after rain then
   falling quickly suggests a perched table above a slowly-permeable layer.
3. Read the soil colour down the profile:
   - **Grey / blue-grey (gleyed)** horizons → saturated most of the time.
   - **Orange/red mottles in a grey matrix** → fluctuating water table; the top of
     mottling roughly marks the usual high-water level.
   - Uniform brown/red → well drained at that depth.
4. A shallow PVC pipe slotted at the bottom (simple piezometer/observation well) read
   weekly through the wet season gives you the real water-table trace.

Also test for **sodicity** if structure collapses when wet (dispersive clay — soil
crumb in distilled water goes cloudy within hours). Drains won't fix a sealed sodic
surface; gypsum and management are needed first.

## 3. Choosing a fix (outline)
| Situation | Usual option |
|---|---|
| Surface ponding, slow infiltration | land forming / grading, spoon or W drains, break compaction |
| Heavy clay, stable subsoil, slope | **mole drains** (~0.5–0.6 m deep, 2–3 m apart) (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) feeding collector pipes, pulled when subsoil is plastic-moist |
| Permeable subsoil, high water table | **tile / pipe drains**, depth and spacing designed from soil conductivity (Hooghoudt equation) |
| Salinity from rising table | subsurface drainage + an approved disposal route for saline drain water |

Drain spacing design is an engineering job; regional rules of thumb exist but vary
hugely with soil (from ~10 m in clays to 40+ m in permeable soils (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)). **Get a design**
rather than guessing for a new system.

## 4. Maintaining existing tile drains
- **Outlets:** keep them clear of silt, weeds and debris; fit **rodent guards**
  (flap or grate) and an erosion-protected apron. The outlet should be above normal
  ditch water level, or the whole system backs up.
- **Flow check after rain:** a drain that ran last year and doesn't now (while the
  field is wet) is blocked. Walk the line looking for **wet streaks, sinkholes or
  blowouts** over the pipe — that pins down the break.
- **Iron ochre** (orange slime at outlets) clogs pipes in iron-rich soils; jet-clean
  with a high-pressure drain jetter from the outlet upward, often every 1–3 years (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)
  where it's bad.
- **Root intrusion:** keep willows, poplars and similar thirsty trees well away
  (often quoted as 30 m) (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) from tile lines.
- **Map it:** record line positions (GPS during install, or from aerial photos of
  drying patterns) before you lose track. Unknown tile is regularly broken by new
  fencing, trenching and deep ripping.

## 5. Mole drains
Moles last roughly 3–10 years (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) depending on soil stability. Re-pull when the paddock
starts staying wet again after rain. Pull in late spring when the subsoil is moist and
plastic but the surface is dry enough to carry the tractor — too wet and the channel
smears shut, too dry and it shatters.

## Gotchas
- Breaking a tile line with a post-hole digger or ripper is common; check maps before
  digging.
- Controlled-drainage boxes (raising the outlet in the growing season) save water and
  nitrate but must be lowered before wet periods and field operations.
- Discharging drain water may need a permit (nutrients, salinity, wetland rules).
