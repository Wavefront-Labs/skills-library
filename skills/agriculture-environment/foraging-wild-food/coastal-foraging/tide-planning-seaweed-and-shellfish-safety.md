# Tide planning, seaweed and shellfish safety

**Goal:** come back with clean seaweed and safe shellfish — and come back at all.
The two serious risks are the tide (getting cut off) and contaminated shellfish
(biotoxins, sewage), both managed by checking official information before you go.

## 1. Tide planning
- Get the **tide table** for the nearest standard or secondary port (national
  hydrographic office or a reputable tide app). Note low water time and **height**.
- **Spring tides** (around new and full moon) give the lowest lows and expose the
  lower shore where kelps, dulse and many shellfish live. Neap lows barely expose it.
- Arrive **~2 hours before low water**, work down with the falling tide, and head back
  as it turns. The **rule of twelfths** describes the flow: over the 6 hours from low
  to high, the water rises roughly 1/12, 2/12, 3/12, 3/12, 2/12, 1/12 of the range per
  hour — the middle two hours rise fastest.
- Know your exits. Bays, sandbars and rocky points get cut off from behind first.
  Mudflats and quicksand can trap people; never walk out on soft mud you don't know.
- Weather: onshore swell makes low water higher and waves dangerous on rocks.
  Rockweed and green algae are extremely slippery — grippy boots.
- Tell someone your plan; carry a charged phone in a waterproof bag.

## 2. Seaweed
- Pick **attached, growing** seaweed from clean water; avoid washed-up material
  (unknown age and source) unless you're gathering it for the garden.
- **Cut, never pull**: leave the holdfast and the lower part of the frond so it
  regrows. Cut kelps above the growth meristem at the stipe/blade junction (e.g. take
  the outer half of sugar-kelp blades).
- Common edible groups (temperate examples): kelps (sugar kelp, oarweed), dulse,
  laver (nori), sea lettuce (*Ulva*), carrageen (Irish moss), pepper dulse,
  bladderwrack (more for broths/teas). Very few seaweeds are dangerous to eat in
  small amounts, but **acid kelp** (*Desmarestia*) contains sulfuric acid and must be
  avoided; and kelps concentrate iodine — keep brown seaweeds to modest amounts.
- Rinse in seawater on the shore, then fresh water at home; dry quickly (rack, low
  oven, dehydrator) until brittle, or freeze.
- Legal note: seaweed harvesting may need landowner (foreshore owner) permission and
  is restricted in marine protected areas; commercial harvesting is licensed in many
  places.

## 3. Shellfish — the safety checks before you collect
1. **Is the area open?** Check the official shellfish harvesting status for that beach
   on the day — food safety or fisheries agency closure maps/hotlines. Closures are
   issued for **biotoxins** from harmful algal blooms: paralytic (PSP), amnesic (ASP)
   and diarrhetic (DSP) shellfish poisoning toxins. **Cooking does not destroy these
   toxins**, and the water can look perfectly normal.
2. **Is the water classified/clean?** Avoid areas near sewage outfalls, harbours,
   marinas, river mouths after heavy rain (norovirus, E. coli, Vibrio). In the UK and
   EU, commercial beds are classified A/B/C; recreational gatherers should prefer
   class-A or monitored, open areas. Many authorities advise waiting several days
   after heavy rainfall.
3. **Rules:** minimum sizes, bag limits, seasons, gear restrictions and licences
   apply in many jurisdictions (e.g. a recreational shellfish licence). Check them.
The old "only eat shellfish in months with an R" saying reflects warm-water risk
(spoilage, blooms, spawning) but is not a safety test — official status is.

## 4. Gathering
- Mussels: take from rocks covered at each high tide (not from the high splash zone),
  pick medium-sized, leave beds thinned not stripped; twist off rather than tearing
  the bed.
- Cockles/clams: rake or dig by hand where allowed; look for siphon holes.
- Keep shellfish **alive and cool** (damp seaweed/cloth in a cool box, not sealed in
  water). Discard any with broken shells or that don't close when tapped before
  cooking; discard any that don't open after cooking.
- Purge sand from clams/cockles in clean seawater for some hours.
- Cook thoroughly (steam until open and then a few minutes more); vulnerable people
  (pregnant, immunocompromised, liver disease) should avoid raw shellfish.

## 5. If someone gets ill
Tingling or numbness of lips and mouth, dizziness or difficulty breathing after eating
shellfish suggests PSP — **call emergency services immediately**. Report illness to
the local health authority so the area can be checked.

## Verify
- [ ] Tide table checked; arrived before low water with exits planned.
- [ ] Official shellfish status checked the same day; no recent heavy rain or nearby outfalls.
- [ ] Size/bag limits and licence requirements met.
- [ ] Seaweed cut above the holdfast from attached plants.
- [ ] Shellfish kept alive and cool, cooked thoroughly, dead ones discarded.
