# Finding patches, judging ripeness and picking efficiently

**Goal:** big, clean harvests of wild fruit and nuts at peak ripeness, from patches
you know will produce again next year — without picking a toxic berry along the way.
Examples are mostly temperate Northern Hemisphere; the method transfers.

## 1. Scout and map
- **Find them in flower, harvest them in fruit.** Spring blossom shows where the
  trees are (elder's white plates, blackthorn's white flowers before leaves, wild
  plums and cherries). Mark them on a phone map (any app that saves pins) with notes.
- Productive spots: sunny edges beat deep shade — hedgerows, woodland margins,
  clearings, riverbanks, old orchards, parks.
- Keep a **phenology log**: date of first ripe fruit each year per patch. Ripening
  shifts with the season by a week or two; the log tells you when to check.
- Nut trees: look for last year's husks/shells under trees in winter or spring.
- Masting: oaks, beech and many nut trees crop heavily in some years and little in
  others; don't write off a tree after one poor year.

## 2. Ripeness cues (examples)
- **Blackberries:** fully black, plump, come away with a gentle tug; red/purple ones
  are sour. Best mid-season; late-season ones get mouldy and insect-ridden.
- **Bilberries/huckleberries:** dark blue-black, soft.
- **Elderberries:** heads hanging down, all berries dark purple-black; strip from stems
  with a fork. **Cook before eating** (raw berries, unripe berries and green parts
  cause nausea).
- **Sloes and rosehips:** traditionally after the first frost softens them; freezing
  at home imitates it. Rosehip seed hairs are irritating — strain through fine cloth.
- **Crab apples / wild plums:** colour change and fruit falling freely; taste.
- **Hazelnuts:** husks turning yellow-brown and nuts starting to drop; squirrels often
  beat you, so pick slightly early and let them finish in a dry place.
- **Sweet chestnuts:** spiny cases splitting open on the ground; take the shiny, plump
  nuts. Don't confuse with **horse chestnut / buckeye** (toxic): smooth or bluntly
  warty single case, one big round conker, not 1–3 flat-sided nuts with a pointed
  tassel tip inside very spiny burr.
- **Walnuts/black walnuts:** green husks softening and darkening; husks stain hands
  for days (gloves).

## 3. Picking efficiently
- Shallow containers so fruit isn't crushed (blackberries over ~10 cm deep crush
  themselves). A container on a cord round your neck frees both hands.
- **Berry rake/comb** for bilberries and huckleberries (be aware some areas restrict
  rakes as they can damage plants).
- A walking stick or hook to pull high bramble/elder branches down.
- Pick top-down and inside-out on a bush; work one side fully before moving.
- Nuts: shake branches over a tarp; collect daily during drop — fallen nuts mould or
  get taken.
- Pick on dry days; wet fruit spoils fast. Get soft fruit cool within hours.

## 4. Nut handling
- **Float test:** put nuts in water; floaters are usually empty, rotten or weevilled —
  discard.
- Dry in-shell nuts in a single layer, well-ventilated, before storage; check for mould.
- Acorns need tannin leaching before eating (see wild-food-processing skill).

## 5. Berries never to eat (learn regional lists)
Examples: yew (red aril — seeds and foliage highly toxic), deadly nightshade, woody
nightshade, holly, ivy, mistletoe, lords-and-ladies (*Arum*) red berry spikes,
pokeweed, baneberry, privet, spindle, and in North America also Virginia creeper.
Rule: never eat a berry you can't name to species. Children: teach "only with an adult
who knows".

## 6. Respect and law
- Picking fruit for personal use is tolerated or legal in many places (e.g. UK common
  law tradition for wild fruit), but rules vary; reserves and private land need
  permission. Commercial picking usually needs permits.
- Leave plenty for wildlife (birds rely on hedgerow fruit in autumn), don't break
  branches, and don't strip a bush.
- Avoid roadside fruit at exhaust height and sprayed hedges.

## Verify
- [ ] Trees and patches mapped from flowering time; ripening dates logged.
- [ ] Each fruit/nut identified to species; toxic lookalikes (e.g. horse chestnut) ruled out.
- [ ] Fruit picked dry, in shallow containers, chilled quickly.
- [ ] Nuts float-tested and dried before storage.
- [ ] Picking legal there and a fair share left for wildlife.
