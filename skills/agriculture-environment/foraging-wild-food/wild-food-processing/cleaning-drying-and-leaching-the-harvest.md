# Cleaning, drying and leaching the wild harvest

**Goal:** get foraged food from basket to a safe, storable state the same day it's
picked, keeping flavour and avoiding the classic spoilage and food-safety traps.
Identification happens *before* processing (see the mushroom and plant foraging
skills) — a second ID check while cleaning is cheap insurance.

## 1. Sort and clean on the day
- Spread the haul on a tray and **re-check every item's identity** — strays from a
  mixed patch get caught here. Discard anything damaged, slimy, insect-riddled or
  doubtful.
- Mushrooms: brush and trim; avoid soaking (they absorb water and dry poorly). Cut
  through larger specimens to check for maggots.
- Greens: wash in several changes of cold water; spin or pat dry. Nettles: gloves.
- Berries: wash just before use or freezing, not before fridge storage.
- Process within hours; foraged produce deteriorates faster than shop produce.

## 2. Drying (the workhorse method)
Principles: low heat, moving air, thin single layers, and dry to the point that
spoilage organisms can't grow. Typical dehydrator settings (adjust to your machine):
- **Herbs and greens** (nettle, mint, yarrow, wild thyme): ~35–40 °C, or air-dry small
  bundles upside-down in a dark, airy place. Done when leaves crumble and stems snap.
- **Mushrooms** (porcini, chanterelle-type, morels): slice ~5 mm; ~40–55 °C. Done when
  **cracker-dry** — slices snap rather than bend. Morels dry well whole. Chanterelles
  rehydrate tough; many people sauté-and-freeze them instead.
- **Fruit** (apple/crab-apple rings, plums halved, berries): ~55–60 °C; berries with
  skins may need blanching or pricking. Done when leathery with no moist pockets
  when torn. Fruit leather: purée spread ~5 mm thick until no longer tacky.
- **Seaweed**: see coastal-foraging skill — dry until brittle.
- Oven drying: lowest setting with the door ajar; watch carefully.

**Conditioning** (fruit especially): pack dried pieces loosely in a jar ~two-thirds
full for 7–10 days, shaking daily. Condensation on the glass = not dry enough; back
to the dryer. Then store airtight, cool and dark; label with species and date.

## 3. Leaching acorns (and other tannin-rich foods)
Acorns contain tannins that are bitter and can upset digestion; leaching removes them.
1. Float-test whole acorns; discard floaters and any with holes.
2. Dry in-shell for a few days, then crack and shell; remove the papery skin as much
   as possible.
3. Choose a method:
   - **Cold leaching:** grind coarsely, put in a jar or cloth bag in cold water; change
     water daily (or several times a day) until the water stays clear and a nibble isn't
     bitter — often several days to a week or more depending on species (red/black oak
     groups are more tannic than white oaks). Cold leaching keeps the starch able to
     bind, good for flour.
   - **Hot leaching:** boil chopped kernels, discard the brown water and replace with
     **already boiling** water (cold water between boils can set bitterness, per
     common forager guidance), repeat until not bitter. Faster; flour binds less.
4. Taste test: no bitterness or astringency left.
5. Dry the leached meal at low temperature, grind finer; store frozen (oils go rancid).

## 4. Freezing and other methods
- Freezing: blanch greens 1–2 min, cool in iced water, squeeze, freeze in portions.
  Berries: freeze on a tray, then bag. Mushrooms: sauté first, then freeze — raw
  frozen mushrooms go mushy.
- Infusing vinegars, making syrups, jams and jellies: follow tested recipes for sugar
  and acidity.

## 5. Food-safety traps
- **Garlic or wild garlic (ramsons) in oil at room temperature is a botulism risk**
  (low-acid, oxygen-free). Keep pesto/oil preparations refrigerated and use within
  a few days, or freeze. Same for herbs-in-oil.
- Low-acid foods (mushrooms, greens) must be **pressure-canned** if canned, never
  water-bath canned; use tested guidance (e.g. USDA/National Center for Home Food
  Preservation).
- Mould on dried goods: discard the whole jar.
- Pantry moths and weevils: freeze dried goods for a few days after drying, store in glass.

## Verify
- [ ] Every item re-identified while sorting; doubtful ones discarded.
- [ ] Dried goods pass the snap/leathery test and a jar conditioning check.
- [ ] Acorns leached until no bitterness; meal stored frozen.
- [ ] No garlic- or herb-in-oil kept at room temperature.
- [ ] Containers labelled with species and date.
