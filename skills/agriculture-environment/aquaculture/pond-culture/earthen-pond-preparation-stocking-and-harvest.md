# Earthen pond preparation, stocking and harvest

**Goal:** one complete crop cycle in an earthen pond for warm-water fish (tilapia,
carps, catfish) — from a dry pond to fish on the bank. Semi-intensive systems use
natural food (plankton) plus feed. Densities and rates are commonly quoted extension
figures; they depend on species, climate, aeration and markets — check local advice.

## 1. Pond preparation (between crops)
1. **Drain and dry** until the bottom cracks (~1–3 weeks). Drying oxidises organic sludge
   and kills many pathogens and predators.
2. Remove excess black sludge; repair dykes, inlet screens and the outlet/monk.
3. **Lime** if the soil pH is low (below ~6.5) or water alkalinity is low (below
   ~20 mg/L CaCO₃): agricultural lime is often applied at ~1–2 t/ha on the dry bottom,
   more on acid soils. **Quicklime** (CaO) at ~0.5–1 t/ha also disinfects wet patches — caustic;
   handle with PPE. Test soil pH first if you can.
4. Fill through a fine **inlet screen** (~0.5–1 mm mesh or a filter sock) to keep out wild
   fish and predators' eggs.

## 2. Fertilisation — grow the food
- Fertilise about **1–2 weeks before stocking** so the bloom is ready.
- Organic: e.g. chicken or cow manure, often ~500–1,000 kg/ha initially (dry weight
  varies), then smaller weekly doses.
- Inorganic: N and P fertilisers (e.g. urea + TSP/DAP), small weekly doses.
- **Target bloom**: green water with a **Secchi disk** reading of ~**30–40 cm**.
  - >60 cm → bloom too thin: fertilise more.
  - <20–25 cm → too dense: stop fertilising, consider partial water exchange — night
    oxygen crashes are likely (see dissolved-oxygen-ammonia-and-ph-control).

## 3. Stocking
- **Density** examples for semi-intensive ponds: tilapia ~1–3 fish/m² with feed;
  carp polyculture ~0.5–1 fish/m² across species (e.g. silver, grass and common carp
  together using different food niches).
- Buy healthy, uniform fingerlings from a reputable hatchery; check for wounds,
  abnormal swimming and parasites.
- **Acclimatise**: float transport bags in the pond ~15–30 min to equalise temperature
  (aim within ~2 °C), then gradually mix pond water into the bag before release. Stock
  in the cool of morning or evening.
- Record numbers and an average weight (weigh a sample of 50–100 fish).

## 4. Grow-out management
- **Feed** at a percentage of body weight that drops as fish grow (see
  ration-setting-and-fcr-management); feed at the same place and time daily.
- **Sample** monthly: net ~30–50 fish, weigh, return. Adjust feed to the new biomass.
- Watch at dawn: fish **gulping at the surface** = low oxygen; aerate or add fresh water.
- Control predators (birds, otters): netting, scare devices, pond-edge clearance.

## 5. Harvest
- **Partial harvest**: seine the pond to take market-size fish and leave smaller ones.
  Drag a seine net ~1.5× the pond width, with weights on the footrope tight to the bottom.
- **Total (drain) harvest**: lower the water over 1–2 days, seine the last fish into a
  catch basin at the outlet.
- Stop feeding **~24–48 h** before harvest for clean guts and better transport survival.
- Keep fish in clean, aerated water for sale; handle gently — scale damage reduces value.
- Record yield (kg/ha), survival (%) and feed used to calculate FCR.

## Gotchas
- Fertilising with too much manure in hot weather causes dawn oxygen crashes and fish kills.
- Wild fish entering through an unscreened inlet compete and breed (tilapia also over-breed:
  consider mono-sex/all-male stock).
- Permits may be needed for water abstraction, discharge and stocking non-native species.

## Verify
- [ ] Pond dried and limed (or soil pH measured and limed as needed).
- [ ] Secchi disk reading of 30–40 cm at stocking.
- [ ] Fingerlings acclimatised and counted; sample weights recorded monthly.
- [ ] Harvest record with kg/ha, survival and FCR.
