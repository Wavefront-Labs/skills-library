# Fish disease recognition and farm biosecurity

**Goal:** notice problems a day earlier than the fish die in numbers, collect useful
samples, and keep pathogens out in the first place. Diagnosis and prescription are a
**fish vet's** job; many diseases are **notifiable** and must be reported. Treatment
products and legality vary by country and species (food fish especially).

## 1. Daily observation — your best tool
At every feed, note:
- **Appetite** (first and most sensitive sign) — see ration-setting-and-fcr-management.
- **Behaviour**: flashing (rubbing on surfaces = skin parasites), gasping at surface,
  crowding at the inflow (low O₂ or gill problems), lethargy, swimming at the edge,
  spiralling.
- **External signs**: white spots, grey/white patches, frayed fins, ulcers, bulging eyes,
  swollen belly, darkening, excess mucus, pale or swollen gills.
- **Mortality**: count dead fish daily per unit. Plot it. A rise above baseline (e.g. 2–3×
  the normal daily rate) is your trigger to investigate.
- Always check **water quality** first — many "diseases" are low oxygen or ammonia
  (see dissolved-oxygen-ammonia-and-ph-control).

## 2. Quick farm diagnostics
Take a few **live, sick-looking (moribund)** fish — not long-dead ones, which decompose fast.
- **Skin scrape**: gently draw a coverslip tail-ward along the flank; place mucus on a slide
  with a drop of water; look at ×40–×100.
- **Gill clip**: small piece of gill filament on a slide.
- What you might see:
  - **Ich / white spot** (*Ichthyophthirius*): large round ciliates with a horseshoe
    nucleus; visible white dots on fish.
  - **Costia** (*Ichthyobodo*): tiny flickering flagellates.
  - **Trichodina**: saucer-shaped ciliates with a toothed ring.
  - **Gill/skin flukes** (*Gyrodactylus*, *Dactylogyrus*): worm-like, hooked attachment organ.
  - **Sea lice** (salmon) — counted to regulatory thresholds per fish.
  - Bacterial (e.g. columnaris "saddleback" lesions, Aeromonas ulcers) and fungal
    (cottony *Saprolegnia*) infections need culture for confirmation.
- Send fresh or chilled (not frozen, unless the lab asks) samples to a lab with history:
  species, size, water data, mortality chart, feed, recent handling.

## 3. Common responses (vet-guided)
- **Fix the environment** first: oxygen, ammonia, density, handling.
- **Salt baths** (sodium chloride) for many freshwater external parasites/stress — dose
  and time depend on species and must be tested on a few fish first.
- Licensed bath treatments (e.g. formalin, hydrogen peroxide, peracetic acid) by
  label/prescription; **aeration on**, observe throughout, never exceed time.
- Medicated feed (antibiotics) only on vet prescription with **withdrawal periods**.
- Ich: life stages off the fish are the target — repeat treatments over the parasite's
  temperature-dependent cycle.
- **Vaccination** is standard for several bacterial/viral diseases in salmon, trout, tilapia.

## 4. Biosecurity
- **Source** stock from certified disease-free suppliers; keep health certificates.
- **Quarantine** new stock separately (often ~2–4 weeks) before mixing.
- Dedicated equipment per unit (nets, buckets), **footbaths/hand washes** at entrances,
  disinfection (iodophor, virkon-type) of gear between units.
- Remove mortalities daily and dispose of them per rules (ensiling, rendering, incineration).
- Restrict visitors and vehicles; log visits.
- **Fallowing** (empty sites for a period) and single-year-class stocking break disease cycles.

## Gotchas
- Treating without a diagnosis (e.g. random antibiotic use) wastes money and builds resistance.
- Formalin in warm water lowers oxygen — oxygen crash during treatment.
- Recently dead fish give poor lab results.
- Reporting obligations for notifiable diseases apply even on small farms.
