# Net-pen husbandry and grading at sea

**Goal:** keep a cage site's fish growing, nets intact and escapes at zero. Written
for circular HDPE pens (salmon, sea bass/bream, lake tilapia); steel-platform and
submersible systems differ. Follow your site's SOPs, vessel safety rules and lifejacket
rules — working on pens is a drowning risk.

## 1. Daily routine
- **Weather and sea state** check before going out; know the site's operating limits.
- Walk/ride each pen: **floating collar** intact, net above water (jump net), **bird nets**
  tight, no holes visible, mooring buoys in place.
- **Mortality removal** ("morts"): lift-up/air-lift system or divers; count and record per pen
  daily. A rise of mortality above the pen's baseline is the earliest disease or
  oxygen warning — escalate fast (see fish-disease-recognition-and-biosecurity).
- **Feeding** observation by underwater camera (see ration-setting-and-fcr-management).
- **Water**: temperature, dissolved oxygen at depth, and algae/jellyfish watch.

## 2. Nets and moorings
- **Inspect nets** by camera/ROV or divers on a schedule (e.g. weekly) and after storms —
  look for holes, chafe at the bottom ring or sinker tube, and predator damage.
- **Fouling** (mussels, hydroids, algae) cuts water flow and oxygen and increases load on
  moorings. Control by in-situ **net washers** (high-pressure discs), regular net changes,
  or antifouling-coated nets per local permits.
- **Moorings**: inspect lines, shackles, chains and anchors on the plan's schedule; check
  grid tension after storms. Damaged moorings are the main cause of large escapes.

## 3. Crowding and grading
Why: fish grow at different rates; grading reduces aggression and lets you feed and
market each size class. **Split** pens when the biomass approaches the pen's density limit.
1. **Starve** fish for ~24–48 h (species/temperature dependent) to reduce oxygen use and
   stress.
2. **Crowd gently**: raise the net or use a crowding net/sweep to concentrate fish.
   Monitor oxygen in the crowd; add oxygen/aeration if it falls; keep crowding time short.
3. Pump or brail fish across a **bar grader** (spacing set to the size split) into two pens.
4. Count with a fish counter if available; estimate average weights by sampling.
5. Watch closely for the next few days — mortality spikes follow rough handling.
Avoid grading at high water temperatures or when fish are already stressed (lice treatments,
disease).

## 4. Density and biomass
Keep biomass per pen below the site's licensed or welfare density (salmon examples
often ~15–25 kg/m³ at maximum; species differ). Update biomass weekly from counts, sample
weights and morts.

## 5. Predators and escapes
- Predator nets around pens or tensioned main nets; bird nets over the top.
- Never use lethal predator control unless licensed — many marine mammals are protected.
- **Escape plan**: report escapes to the regulator within the required time, deploy
  recapture nets if permitted, investigate the cause.

## Gotchas
- A slack net deforms in current — volume collapses, fish crowded, oxygen falls.
- Divers and boats near feed pipes, mooring lines and propellers: lock out feed systems before dives.
- Night-time handling and grading increases accident risk; plan in daylight.
- Licence conditions (biomass limit, sea lice thresholds, discharge) are legal, not advisory.

## Verify
- [ ] Mortality logged per pen daily with trend.
- [ ] Net inspection log up to date, holes repaired same day.
- [ ] Grading done with oxygen monitored and post-grading mortality tracked.
- [ ] Biomass per pen below licensed limits; escape contingency plan posted.
