# Ration setting and FCR management

**Goal:** feed the fish what they'll eat and convert — no more. Feed is usually the
**largest single cost** in fish farming (often quoted at ~50–70% of production cost),
and uneaten feed fouls water. Numbers below are illustrative — use your feed
manufacturer's tables for your species, feed and temperature.

## 1. Know the biomass
- **Biomass = number of fish × average weight.**
- Count at stocking; subtract daily mortalities.
- **Sample** every 2–4 weeks: net a representative group (~50–100+ fish, from several
  spots), weigh in bulk (kg ÷ count = mean weight) or individually for size spread.
  Starve ~12–24 h first for accuracy (gut contents).
- Update the biomass in your feed sheet the same day.

## 2. Set the daily ration
- Feeding rate (% of body weight per day) **falls as fish grow** and **rises with
  temperature** up to the species optimum, then falls above it.
- Illustrative warm-water example (tilapia, ~28 °C): fry ~10–15% BW/day, 10–50 g fish
  ~4–6%, 100–300 g ~2–3%, >400 g ~1–1.5%. Salmonids in cold water feed much less.
- **Daily ration (kg) = biomass (kg) × rate (%) ÷ 100**, split into meals: more meals for
  small fish (e.g. 4–8/day), fewer for large (2–3/day).
- **Pellet size**: roughly 20–30% of the fish's mouth width; change sizes on the
  manufacturer's weight bands, mixing over a few days.
- **Cut or stop feed when**: dissolved O₂ is low (morning, warm days), water is outside
  the species' temperature range, during handling/grading/treatment, and before harvest.

## 3. Read the feeding response
- Feed slowly, watching. Stop when the **fish stop actively taking pellets** (floating
  feed is easy to see; for sinking feed use underwater cameras, or check pellets in the
  outflow or a lift-up).
- Score each meal: 3 = aggressive, 2 = normal, 1 = slow, 0 = no response. Falling scores
  for two or more meals = check water and fish health (see
  fish-disease-recognition-and-biosecurity).
- **Demand feeders** let fish self-feed — check they aren't being triggered by debris or
  dominated by a few fish.

## 4. Calculate performance
- **FCR = feed given (kg) ÷ weight gain (kg)** for the period. (Economic FCR includes
  mortality losses: total feed ÷ net harvest weight gain.)
- Typical ranges are often quoted roughly as ~1.0–1.3 for salmon, ~1.4–1.8 for tilapia
  and catfish on good pelleted feed; verify for your system.
- **SGR (%/day) = 100 × (ln final weight − ln initial weight) ÷ days**.
- Plot FCR and SGR per unit monthly. A rising FCR with stable SGR → overfeeding/wasted feed;
  falling SGR with stable FCR → underfeeding, poor water or health.

## 5. Feed storage
Cool, dry, off the floor, away from sunlight and vermin, first-in-first-out, use within
the manufacturer's shelf life (often ~3 months for many feeds). Rancid or mouldy feed causes
disease.

## Gotchas
- Feeding to the table without watching the fish → overfeeding in cool or stressed weeks.
- Out-of-date biomass estimates make every ration wrong.
- Dominant fish eat first: spread feed widely and grade by size (see
  net-pen-husbandry-and-grading-at-sea).
- Feeding at night or before dawn when oxygen is lowest.

## Verify
- [ ] Biomass updated at every sample and after significant mortality.
- [ ] Daily ration sheet with rate, meals and appetite score per unit.
- [ ] FCR and SGR calculated per unit per month.
- [ ] Feed store dated and rotated; nothing past shelf life.
