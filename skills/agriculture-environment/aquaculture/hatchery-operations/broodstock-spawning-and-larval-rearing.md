# Broodstock, spawning and larval rearing

**Goal:** a reliable batch of healthy juveniles from your own broodstock. Hatchery
practice is species-specific; this is the common workflow. Hormone products and doses
are regulated and species-specific — **use veterinary or authority-approved
protocols**; doses here are omitted on purpose.

## 1. Broodstock conditioning
- Keep broodstock at low density in excellent water with a high-quality broodstock diet
  (more protein, lipids, vitamins — astaxanthin and fatty acids matter for egg quality).
- **Environmental cues** bring fish into spawning condition: temperature and
  **photoperiod** programmes (e.g. salmonids: shortening day length; many marine fish:
  photo-thermal cycles that can shift spawning out of season).
- Check readiness: females with soft, swollen bellies; males releasing milt on gentle
  pressure. For many species, an **ovarian biopsy** (cannula) shows oocyte stage.
- Track individuals (PIT tags) and avoid inbreeding: use many pairs (~50+ effective
  parents is often recommended for a stable line).

## 2. Spawning
- **Natural** (tank spawning; e.g. many marine fish, tilapia) → collect floating eggs
  with an egg collector at the overflow.
- **Induced** (carp, catfish, many marine species): hormone injection (e.g. GnRH
  analogues, carp pituitary, hCG) per protocol; ovulation follows after a number of
  **degree-hours** that depends on temperature.
- **Stripping** (salmonids, carp, catfish): anaesthetise, dry the fish's belly, gently
  press eggs into a dry bowl; add milt from several males.

## 3. Fertilisation and incubation
1. **Dry method**: mix eggs + milt **without water** first, then add a little water to
   activate sperm (sperm lives under ~1 min once activated), stir gently ~1–2 min, rinse.
2. Adhesive eggs (carp, catfish) need **de-adhesion** (clay or tannin solution) before
   incubating in **McDonald/Zuger jars**; salmonid eggs go into trays; pelagic marine eggs
   into conical incubators with gentle upwelling.
3. Incubate in clean, oxygenated water in the dark for light-sensitive species.
4. Time development in **degree-days** (°C × days). Example: rainbow trout hatch at roughly
   ~300–350 degree-days. Remove dead (white) eggs daily; an approved disinfectant
   treatment against fungus may be used.
5. Record fertilisation rate (check eyed/cleavage stage on a sample) and hatch rate.

## 4. Larval rearing and live feed
- Larvae live on the **yolk sac** first; feed **just before** yolk absorption (larvae
  swim up/look for food).
- Small-mouthed marine larvae need **live feed**: **rotifers** (~100–300 µm) first, then
  **Artemia** nauplii (~450 µm), then enriched metanauplii, then weaning to micro-diets.
  Freshwater species (trout, carp) can often take dry starter feed or Artemia directly.
- **Live-feed culture**: rotifers on microalgae or commercial paste; **enrich** rotifers
  and Artemia with DHA/EPA products for a few hours before feeding.
  Artemia cysts hatch ~18–24 h at ~28 °C, 25–35 ppt salinity, strong aeration and light.
- **Green-water** technique (adding microalgae to larval tanks) improves feeding and
  survival for many marine larvae.
- **Weaning**: overlap live feed and micro-diet over days, reducing live feed gradually.

## 5. Hygiene and records
Disinfect eggs where allowed (e.g. iodophor for salmonid eggs), foot baths, separate
equipment per tank, clean tanks daily (siphon). Log: spawn date, parents, number of eggs,
fertilisation %, hatch %, survival at key stages, feeds, water quality.

## Gotchas
- Overripe eggs (stripped too late) look normal but fertilise poorly.
- Water contact before mixing milt ruins fertilisation.
- Cannibalism in some species (catfish, sea bass) — grade early and often.
- Swim-bladder inflation failures in marine larvae: surface skimmers to remove oil films.

## Verify
- [ ] Broodstock records with tag IDs, conditioning programme and maturity checks.
- [ ] Fertilisation and hatch rates measured on samples every batch.
- [ ] Live-feed cultures running before larvae need them.
- [ ] Survival logged at first feeding, weaning and juvenile stage.
