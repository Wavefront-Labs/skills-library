# Mowing, feeding and lawn renovation

**Goal:** a dense lawn that crowds out moss and weeds on its own, achieved mostly by
correct mowing height and a planned renovation, not by chemicals.

> Written for cool-season lawns (fescues, ryegrass, bents, Kentucky bluegrass). Figures are
> US extension guidance (inches, pounds per 1,000 sq ft); follow local advice and the
> product or seed label. Warm-season grasses follow a different calendar.

## 1. Mowing
- **One-third rule:** mow often enough that no more than one-third of the leaf is removed
  at one time. Scalping weakens grass and lets weeds in.
- **Heights (UC IPM, set the mower to):** tall fescue 1.5-3 in; Kentucky bluegrass and
  perennial ryegrass 1.5-2.5 in. Cutting at the high end of the range makes turf more
  tolerant of drought, heat, traffic, shade and pests. Penn State: most lawns should be
  mowed at 2 in or above.
- Frequency: regularly in spring when growth is active, less in summer when growth slows,
  more again in fall.
- Use a sharp blade.
- Leave clippings on the lawn where they decompose quickly; if a delayed mow leaves heavy
  piles, collect them and use them as mulch.

## 2. Feeding
- Turfgrass benefits from annual nitrogen. Penn State suggests slow-release nitrogen in
  mid to late spring and/or late summer to early fall.
- Apply at the rate on the manufacturer's label.

## 3. Moss and weeds
- Moss means a **cause** (shade, compaction, poor drainage, acid soil, mowing too low).
  Find and fix the cause or it returns.
- Pre-emergent herbicides go on before weed seeds germinate (early to mid spring per
  Penn State); they also affect new grass seed, so check the label before overseeding.
- For rosette weeds, dig them out with a weeding tool or use a selective herbicide
  strictly per its label.

## 4. Renovation
1. **Dethatch** where thatch is a problem (thatch is a slow-decaying layer between blades
   and soil that can block water). Use a dethatcher or verticutter; it leaves grooves for
   seed. Rake up the debris. NC State notes it is generally not necessary to remove thatch.
2. **Aerate** after dethatching. Cores are better than spiking (UC IPM). Hand tool: cores
   1/4-1/2 in wide, 4-6 in apart, about 3-4 in deep. Water a few days before to soften the
   soil. Leave the cores on the lawn; they break up and act as topdressing.
3. **Time it:** Penn State: aerate in cool weather (early to mid spring or late summer to
   early fall); late summer to early fall is usually the best time to establish a lawn from
   seed; overseeding thin turf can also be done in late winter or spring. NC State (tall
   fescue): overseed 15 August to 1 September; do not aerate in spring or summer; fall
   aeration suits heavy traffic or clay soil.
4. **Overseed** at the seed supplier's rate. Penn State seeding guide, pounds per 1,000 sq ft
   for a new lawn: Kentucky bluegrass 2-3 (100% stand) or 3-4 (blend); turf-type perennial
   ryegrass 4-5; turf-type tall fescue 6-8; fine fescues 4-5. For renovating with turf-type
   ryegrass it lists 2-5. NC State: turf-type tall fescue 6 lb, optionally plus 1-2 lb
   Kentucky bluegrass.
5. **Topdress** lightly with sand or compost to help seed-to-soil contact (NC State).
6. **Water:** keep the seedbed moist by light watering several times a day; as seedlings
   grow, water less often but more deeply.

## Gotchas
- Pre-emergent herbicide and seed conflict: read the label for the reseeding interval.
- Seed must touch soil; do not seed into thick debris.

## Verify
- [ ] Mower set within the species range; no cut removes more than a third.
- [ ] Nitrogen applied at label rate in the recommended seasons.
- [ ] Cause of moss identified.
- [ ] Renovation done in the right window, in order: dethatch, aerate, seed, topdress.
