# Garden site survey and layout

**Goal:** a scaled plan of your garden that puts each use and plant where conditions
suit it, built in stages you can afford — instead of buying plants first and finding
places for them later.

> Draft. Amateur-garden method; doesn't cover structural work (retaining walls over ~1 m,
> drainage to sewers, works near trees with legal protection) that may need professionals
> or permissions.

## 1. Survey (ideally across a few months)
- **Measure** the boundaries and fixed features (house, doors, windows, sheds, trees,
  manhole covers, taps, outside power). Use a long tape and offsets from the house wall;
  a satellite map view is fine for a first outline but check dimensions on the ground.
- **Sun:** note compass orientation. Photograph or sketch where shade falls at ~9:00,
  12:00, 15:00 and 18:00, ideally in spring and midsummer; winter shade is much longer.
  Mark zones: full sun (6+ h), part shade (3–6 h), shade (<3 h).
- **Soil:** in 2–3 spots, a squeeze test (clay ribbons; sand feels gritty and falls apart;
  loam holds briefly), a pH kit, and a **drainage test** — dig a 30 cm hole, fill with water,
  let it drain, refill: under ~1 h = free draining; more than a day = poor drainage.
- **Microclimate:** wind corridors between buildings, frost pockets at low points, rain
  shadows by walls, hot reflective walls.
- **Existing assets and problems:** good trees and shrubs, views to keep or hide, slope,
  damp spots, hard-standing.
- Your **hardiness zone** (USDA, RHS H-ratings, or local equivalent).

## 2. Draw a base plan
- Graph paper at **1:50 or 1:100** (1 cm = 0.5 m or 1 m), or a free 2D tool. Put the survey
  on it, then lay tracing paper or a new layer on top for ideas — keep the survey clean.

## 3. Decide uses and zone them
List what you want: seating, dining, kids' play, veg beds, compost, bins/storage, washing
line, shed, pond, lawn. Then place by conditions:
- Seating where the sun is when you'll use it (evening sun for after-work).
- Veg, fruit and herbs in the sunniest open spot, close to the kitchen and a tap.
- Compost, bins, storage near access but screened.
- Shade-tolerant planting where shade is unavoidable rather than lawn (grass sulks in shade).

## 4. Size things properly
- **Paths:** ≥ 0.9–1.2 m for a wheelbarrow/two people; 0.6 m minimum for stepping paths.
- **Beds:** reachable from one side ≤ ~0.6–0.7 m deep; from both sides ≤ ~1.2 m
  (raised veg beds); ornamental borders 1.5–3 m deep look generous.
- **Patio:** ≥ ~3 × 3 m for a table and four chairs with room to pull chairs out.
- Allow plants their **mature spread** — especially trees (crown and roots), kept well clear
  of house foundations and drains.

## 5. Right plant, right place
For each bed, write the conditions (sun, soil, moisture, wind) and choose only plants that
match. Check eventual height and spread, evergreen/deciduous, and season of interest.

## 6. Phase the work
1. Structural/hard landscaping and drainage (messy, affects everything else).
2. Trees, hedges and large shrubs (the slow bits).
3. Beds and perennials.
4. Fill with annuals while the permanent planting fills out.
Spread it over seasons and budget; autumn is a good planting season for most of it.

## Gotchas
- Shade maps done in summer underestimate winter shade badly.
- Trees planted 2 m from the house "because it looked small" are an expensive problem later.
- Mark underground services before digging (utility records; in many countries a call-before-you-dig service).

## Verify
- [ ] Scale base plan with measured boundaries, features and north arrow.
- [ ] Sun/shade, soil type, drainage and microclimate marked on it.
- [ ] Each use placed where its conditions are met; access paths ≥ 0.9 m.
- [ ] Each bed's plant list matches its recorded conditions and mature sizes.
- [ ] A phased schedule with seasons and rough costs exists.
