# Diagnosing and fixing houseplant problems

**Goal:** go from "my plant looks sad" to a specific cause and fix, checking water,
roots and light before pests.

## 1. Gather facts before acting
- When did it start? Any move, repot, season change or heating on?
- How and how often are you watering? Does the pot drain?
- Where is it, and how bright is that spot?

## 2. Check the roots
Inspect the root system whenever a plant wilts (UC IPM).
- Healthy roots are white (UC IPM); Illinois Extension adds that dark brown to black
  roots suggest overwatering and rusty orange to light brown suggest underwatering.
- Brown, soft or rotted roots are linked to overwatering, dryness, root disease, excess
  salts, compacted soil or poorly drained containers (UC IPM).
- A root-bound plant may simply need a larger container (Illinois).

## 3. Read the symptoms (UC IPM)
| Symptom | Causes listed |
|---|---|
| Older leaves yellow | Insufficient fertiliser (especially nitrogen), insufficient light, sap-sucking pests |
| Newer leaves yellow | Overwatering, soil pH imbalance, micronutrient deficiency |
| Whole plant yellow | Excess light, over- or underwatering, low fertiliser, high temperature, insects |
| Brown leaf tips | Overfertilising, salt build-up, too dry or too wet soil; also too much sun, low humidity, drafts |
| Leaf drop | Poor root health, compacted or pot-bound roots, sudden light, temperature or humidity change |
| Leggy growth with long stems | Inadequate light |
| Leaf spots after a move | Excess light (sunburn) |

## 4. Fix water, light and feeding
- Overwatered: withhold water until the soil is quite dry, then restart a new schedule
  gradually; after two weeks apply houseplant fertiliser (Illinois). If there is no
  improvement in two more weeks, repot in fresh soil.
- Leggy plants: move to a brighter spot or turn them occasionally (Illinois).
- Spots from too much sun: move the plant to a spot with less sun (Illinois).
- Low indoor humidity favours mites and can brown leaf tips (UC IPM).

## 5. Pests (UC IPM unless noted)
Isolate an infested plant immediately and wash your hands afterwards. Check new plants
weekly for several weeks, then monthly if no problems appear.
- **Fungus gnats:** adults are about 1/10 to 1/8 inch; larvae feed on roots. Use sterile
  potting soil, remove standing water and let soil dry between waterings; yellow sticky
  traps catch adults; repeated Bti applications control larvae.
- **Spider mites:** about 0.5 mm, under leaves, stippling and sometimes webbing; use a
  10X hand lens. Wash leaves with water, reduce dust; oil or soap as a last resort.
- **Mealybugs:** scrape or wash off; for small infestations dab with 70% (or weaker)
  isopropyl alcohol on a cotton swab, testing a small area one to two days beforehand.
- **Scale:** scrape off, wash off crawlers; oil or insecticidal soap works best on the
  small immature stage. Heavily infested plants are best discarded (Illinois).
- **Thrips:** under 3 mm, stippling and shiny black dots; wash off with water.

## Verify
- [ ] Roots inspected and either healthy or treated.
- [ ] Cause matched to the symptom table before any change.
- [ ] Pest identified by sign and the plant isolated.
- [ ] Reassess after the two-week intervals above.
