# Indoor seed starting and hardening off

**Goal:** short, sturdy, dark-green seedlings that go outside without a check,
not pale, leggy ones that flop or rot.

> The seed packet beats any general table. Follow the grow-light maker's recommended height.

## 1. Kit
- Clean trays or modules with drainage.
- A **sterile, well-drained soilless seed-starting mix**. Do not use bagged topsoil,
  vegetable-garden mixes or potting mixes (Clemson).
- Clear lid or plastic cover for early germination; a heat mat is optional if the room is
  warm (Cornell: heat mats speed germination, probably unnecessary if the room is
  68 to 72 F).
- Light: plan for artificial light, a windowsill alone is often too weak.

## 2. Sowing
- Fill, water, sow, label with variety and date every time.
- Germination: Cornell calls 68 to 72 F a tolerable temperature for germinating most seeds.
  Check the packet for crops that need warmer or cooler conditions.
- Keep the cover on until seeds have germinated (Clemson two-step method), or remove the
  bag when seedlings start to appear (one-step method).
- Tomatoes: start about 6 to 8 weeks before the last frost date (Cornell). Check the packet
  for other crops.

## 3. After germination
- Light: Cornell recommends 14 to 16 hours of direct light; Clemson recommends 16 to 18
  hours of bright light (natural plus artificial). Use a timer.
- Distance: keep lights 3 to 4 inches from plant tops (Cornell); Clemson says 2 to 4 inches
  above seedlings for fluorescent tubes. Raise the light as plants grow.
- From the two-true-leaf stage, a fan nearby gives a gentle sway; it dries the mix faster, so
  watch watering (Clemson).
- Overwatering shows as algae, fungi or slime on the surface (Cornell).

## 4. Damping-off and legginess
- **Damping-off** is caused by Pythium species, which infect and kill seedlings. Prevent it
  with a sterile, well-drained mix and clean containers (Clemson).
- **Leggy** seedlings come from too little light; fix light first.

## 5. Transplanting and feeding
- In the two-step method, move seedlings to larger containers when one or two sets of true
  leaves appear. Handle them by the seed leaves, not the stem (Clemson).
- Begin feeding when the first true leaves appear (Clemson; Cornell says after the first two).

## 6. Hardening off
- Clemson: start once plants are about 3 inches tall. Put them outdoors in the day, bring
  them in before sundown, and gradually increase direct sun for two to three weeks.
- Cornell: a few hours outside on day one, about six hours on day two, then all day, and
  overnight when temperatures allow.

## Gotchas
- Sowing too early gives overgrown plants waiting to go out.
- Unhardened seedlings stall or scorch.

## Verify
- [ ] Sterile soilless mix; labels in.
- [ ] Light on a timer at the recommended height.
- [ ] Stems short and thick; no damping-off spreading.
- [ ] Hardened off gradually before planting out.
