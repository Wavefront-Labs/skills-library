# Container media, watering and feeding

**Goal:** plants in pots that don't swing between drought and waterlogging, and don't
starve part-way through the season.

> Fertiliser products and rates come from the product label and override anything here.

## 1. Container choice
- **Size:** Penn State: a single tomato needs at least a 20-inch-wide pot; peppers and
  eggplants can thrive in a 14-inch pot; pots should be at least as tall as they are wide.
  Oregon State: tomatoes, peppers and eggplant need five-gallon containers; 10-inch pots
  suit herbs, parsley and green onions. NC State: most herbs fit in 4- to 6-inch pots and
  carrots want 10-12 inches of mix depth; its Tables 18-1 and 18-2 list minimum sizes
  by crop.
- **Drainage holes are necessary in all containers** so roots do not stand in water and
  rot (NC State). Oregon State: drill holes along the side about 1/2 inch from the bottom
  and raise the pot slightly on bricks or wooden slats.
- Plastic pots do not dry out as quickly as porous ones such as terracotta (Penn State).
- Dark-coloured containers absorb more heat than light ones, so avoid them in full sun
  (NC State). Unglazed terracotta absorbs water that can freeze and crack it, so do not
  leave it outdoors over winter (NC State).

## 2. Potting media
- Use a soilless potting mix or purchased potting soil, never straight garden soil or
  topsoil: it can be too heavy, retain too much moisture, have too little pore space for
  oxygen, and carry diseases, insects and weeds (NC State, Penn State, Oregon State).
  NC State gives a common blend of 50% peat and 50% perlite; Penn State says compost can
  make up to half of the mix.
- **Do not put a layer of gravel or rocks in the bottom** to improve drainage (NC State).
  Water collects in the mix just above the gravel and drains into it only once the mix
  has no air space left, so it does not prevent saturation.

## 3. Watering
- Finger test: stick a finger 2-3 inches into the mix; if it is dry, water (NC State).
  Oregon State: water when the top of the soil feels dry.
- Water until it runs out of the drainage holes and empty any saucer afterwards.
- Mature plants usually need water at least once a day (NC State); in hot summer months
  at least once a day (Penn State). Do not let the mix become soggy.

## 4. Feeding
- Slow-release fertiliser at planting, then soluble fertiliser every two to four weeks
  (Penn State).
- NC State: with pre-plant controlled-release fertiliser, more is needed after eight to
  10 weeks; liquid fertiliser once a week at half strength or every two weeks at full
  strength.
- Oregon State: a water-soluble all-purpose fertiliser every three to four days at half
  the recommended strength, or dry fertiliser about every three weeks.
- The extension sources differ, so follow the label of the product you use.

## 5. Repotting and overwintering
- Healthy plants in optimal conditions should be repotted annually using fresh potting
  mix, best in early spring before active growth (NC State).
- Bring tender plants indoors when night temperatures drop to 55 F and acclimate them
  over about a week. Insulate the root ball of outdoor containers with leaves, straw or
  closed-cell foam (NC State).

## Verify
- [ ] Every container drains freely; no gravel layer in the bottom.
- [ ] Soilless mix, not garden soil.
- [ ] Watering decided by finger check, not by the calendar.
- [ ] Feeding follows the product label.
