# Pruning roses, shrubs and climbers

**Goal:** prune so the plant flowers better and stays healthy, which mostly means
knowing **when** a plant makes its flower buds, then cutting in the right place.

> Timings are for one climate; shift for yours. For flowering shrubs other than roses,
> find out whether the plant flowers on old or new wood from a local extension guide
> before cutting.

## 1. Tools and cuts
- Sharp **bypass pruners** (preferred over anvil types, which crush stems), long-handled
  loppers and a pruning saw for large old stems.
- Dip shears periodically in a 70 percent alcohol solution (Clemson).
- Cut 1/4 inch above an outward-facing bud, angled to match the bud's angle.
- Seal cuts on canes thicker than a pencil with nail polish or wood glue (Clemson).

## 2. Always first
Remove dead, diseased and damaged wood, plus crossing stems, then step back before
cutting anything else.

## 3. Hybrid teas, floribundas, grandifloras
- Prune in early spring as buds swell ("when they begin to swell, go ahead and prune").
  Clemson gives mid-to-late February near the coast and mid-April in the mountains.
- Cut canes down to 15 to 18 inches above ground and keep three to five of the healthiest,
  most vigorous canes.
- Remove any cane thinner than a pencil.
- In fall, cut back by about one third of height to reduce winter cane breakage.

## 4. Old garden roses, once-blooming climbers and ramblers
- Old garden roses: remove no more than one third of each bush, and prune right after bloom.
- Once-blooming old-fashioned climbers and ramblers: prune after flowering, remove the
  oldest canes and keep five to seven strong canes.

## 5. Deadheading
Make a diagonal cut just above the next five- or seven-leaf branch down the stem, above a
strong bud.

## Gotchas
- Pruning stimulates growth.
- Check for active bird nests in hedges and shrubs before spring cutting; rules vary by place.

## Verify
- [ ] You know whether the plant flowers on old or new wood before cutting.
- [ ] Dead, diseased and crossing wood removed first.
- [ ] Cuts just above outward buds, angled, no stubs.
- [ ] Rose type matched to its timing (spring-prune bush roses, after-bloom for old garden and once-blooming types).
