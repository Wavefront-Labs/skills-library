# Planting a mixed border for succession

**Goal:** a border that looks planned from February to November — something always
coming, something peaking — with a manageable workload.

> Draft. Timings are for temperate (UK/N. Europe/northern US) gardens. Check each plant's
> hardiness zone, height and spread on the label or a reliable plant database.

## 1. Read the site
- **Aspect and light:** full sun (6+ h), part shade, full shade — choose plants for it.
- **Soil:** squeeze test (sticky clay / gritty sand), drainage (does a 30 cm hole of
  water empty within a day?), pH kit. Pick plants that suit rather than fighting it.
- **Depth:** a border under ~1.2 m deep can only hold 2 layers; 2–3 m deep allows proper
  layering front-to-back.

## 2. Build in layers
1. **Structure (≈ 20–30% of plants):** evergreen shrubs, clipped balls, grasses,
   small trees. They hold the picture in winter. Repeat them along the border.
2. **Main perennials (≈ 40–50%):** the seasonal show. Choose **3 peak windows**
   (e.g. late spring, high summer, autumn) and pick 2–3 plants for each.
3. **Fillers and ground cover (≈ 10–20%):** low, spreading plants that close gaps and
   suppress weeds (hardy geraniums, *Alchemilla*, *Nepeta*).
4. **Bulbs layered in:** tulips, alliums, narcissus, camassia between perennials —
   they flower before the perennials leaf up, then the foliage hides their dying leaves.
5. **Seasonal fill:** annuals or bedding in deliberate gaps for colour.

## 3. Arrangement rules that work
- Tall at the back (or middle of an island bed), but bring a few see-through tall plants
  (*Verbena bonariensis*, grasses) forward to avoid a staircase.
- Plant in **odd-number groups (3, 5, 7)** or drifts, and **repeat** key plants 2–3 times
  along the length: repetition is what makes a border read as designed.
- Limit the palette: 2–3 main colours plus foliage greens/silvers. Contrast form too
  (spires, domes, umbels, grasses).
- **Density** (per m²): large perennials 3–5, medium 5–7, small/ground cover 7–11;
  shrubs at their mature spread.

## 4. Planting
- Best in **autumn** (warm soil, winter rain) or spring; avoid frozen or waterlogged soil.
- Lay out pots on the bed first, adjust, then plant. Hole twice the pot width, same depth
  (shrubs/perennials at the same level they grew in the pot).
- Water well, then **mulch** 5–7 cm of compost/bark, keeping it off stems.
- **Bulbs** in autumn: depth ≈ 2–3× bulb height, pointed end up; tulips are best planted
  late (Nov in UK) to reduce tulip fire disease.

## 5. Maintenance calendar
- **Late winter:** cut back last year's stems (leave seedheads for birds over winter if you like), mulch.
- **Spring:** stake tall perennials *before* they need it (grow-through supports).
- **Late May (UK) – "Chelsea chop":** cut back by up to ~half some late-flowering
  perennials (e.g. *Sedum/Hylotelephium*, *Phlox*, *Helenium*) for shorter, sturdier,
  later flowers.
- **Summer:** deadhead repeat-flowerers; water new plantings in dry spells (first 1–2 years).
- **Autumn:** note gaps and clashes (photos monthly help); lift and **divide** congested
  clumps every 3–5 years.

## Gotchas
- Label spreads are mature sizes; young borders look sparse for 1–2 years — fill with annuals, don't overplant shrubs.
- Thugs (some *Lysimachia*, mint, *Houttuynia*) take over borders; check spread habits.
- Many bulbs need sun after flowering — don't bury them under dense shrubs.

## Verify
- [ ] Each month Mar–Oct has at least two plants in flower or strong foliage interest.
- [ ] Key plants repeated 2–3× along the border; groups in odd numbers.
- [ ] Plant densities per m² checked against the plan.
- [ ] Structure plants make the bed readable in winter.
- [ ] Maintenance calendar written; monthly photos taken for next year's adjustments.
