# Small-flock poultry processing

**Goal:** kill birds quickly and humanely, and produce clean, safe carcasses — chilled
fast enough that bacteria don't grow. Rules on home slaughter and on selling the meat
vary by country; **home-killed poultry often may not be sold** without a licensed
facility or a specific small-producer exemption. Check before you plan to sell.

## 1. Prepare
- **Feed withdrawal:** remove feed **~8–12 hours** before (water stays available).
  An empty crop and gut means less contamination when eviscerating.
- Set up: killing cones or a restraint method, sharp knife (and spare), scalder with an
  accurate thermometer, plucker or table, a clean eviscerating table, potable water,
  ice-water chilling tubs (ice ~1 kg per bird is a rough guide), bins for offal and
  feathers, gloves and aprons. Work in shade, away from flies.
- Practise the kill method on paper/with an experienced person first if it's new to you.

## 2. Humane killing
Calm handling: catch at night or in dim light, hold birds by both legs supported against
your body, never by one leg or the neck.
- **Killing cone + neck cut:** place bird head-down in the cone (calms most birds).
  With a sharp knife, cut deeply on one side of the neck just behind the jaw to sever the
  **carotid artery and jugular vein**; don't cut the windpipe or spinal cord. Allow
  ~1–2 minutes to bleed out fully; reflex flapping afterwards is normal and is not
  consciousness.
- Many welfare bodies recommend **stunning first** (e.g. a purpose-made electrical or
  percussive stunner) to render the bird unconscious immediately — preferred where you
  have the equipment.
- **Manual cervical dislocation** is used for small birds by trained people; done
  badly it causes suffering — get trained.
Whatever the method: the bird must be dead (no rhythmic breathing, no eye blink to a
touch of the cornea) before scalding.

## 3. Scald and pluck
- **Scald** whole bird in hot water with a little dish soap (helps water penetrate):
  - Typical **~60–65 °C (140–150 °F) for ~30–60 s**, dunking up and down.
  - Test: wing and tail feathers pull out easily → done. Under-scald = feathers stick;
    over-scald = skin tears and cooks.
  - Keep the temperature steady — add hot water as birds cool it.
- **Pluck** immediately: a drum plucker takes ~15–30 s; by hand, pull with the grain
  in handfuls. Singe hairs if wanted. Remove feet at the hock joint, and the head.

## 4. Evisceration (clean table, clean hands)
1. Remove the oil gland at the tail (cut out the small lump above the tail, scooping under it).
2. Slit the neck skin, free the **crop** and windpipe and oesophagus from the neck;
   loosen them toward the body.
3. Make a shallow cut across the abdomen below the keel to the vent — careful not to cut
   into intestines. Cut around the **vent** so it comes away with the intestines.
4. Reach in along the inside of the ribs, cup the organs and pull out in one mass.
   Keep the gut intact — **if contents spill on the carcass, trim or rinse the area
   immediately**.
5. Remove lungs (pressed against the ribs; a lung scraper helps), any remaining
   windpipe, and kidneys if preferred. Save heart, liver (remove the green **gall bladder**
   without bursting it) and gizzard (cut, peel lining) if wanted.
6. Inspect: abnormal livers (spots, enlarged), cloudy air sacs, tumours → discard and
   consider asking a vet about flock health.
7. Rinse inside and out with cold potable water.

## 5. Chill fast
Into **ice water immediately**; target carcass core **≤ 4 °C (40 °F) within ~4 hours**
(check with a probe thermometer in the thickest breast). Then drain, bag and refrigerate
or freeze. Many people rest carcasses 1–2 days in the fridge before cooking/freezing
for tenderness (rigor resolves).

## Gotchas
- A blunt knife causes a slow, poor kill — sharpen before you start and between birds.
- Over-scalding causes torn skin that looks bad and spoils faster.
- Children and visitors near the kill area should be prepared; clean all surfaces after.
- Times and temperatures are common extension guidance, **not confirmed for your
  setup**; local food-safety and animal-welfare law takes priority.

## Verify
- [ ] Legal position on home kill and sale checked.
- [ ] Every bird confirmed dead before scalding; bleeding complete.
- [ ] Feathers released cleanly at scald; skin intact.
- [ ] No gut spillage left on carcasses; offal inspected.
- [ ] Core temperature ≤ 4 °C within 4 hours, verified with a probe.
