# Horse feeding and condition routine

**Goal:** a ration built on forage, matched to the horse's weight and work, changed
slowly — the pattern that keeps the gut working and avoids colic, ulcers and laminitis.

## 1. Know the horse's weight
Scales are best. Otherwise use a **weight tape** around the girth, or the formula
(adult horses, commonly cited):
```
Weight (kg) = girth² (cm) × body length (cm) ÷ 11,900
```
Body length = point of shoulder to point of buttock. Example: girth 185 cm, length 160 cm
→ 185² × 160 ÷ 11,900 ≈ 460 kg. Tapes and formulas can be off by ±10% (more for ponies,
drafts, pregnant mares) — use them for **trend**, measuring the same way each time.

## 2. Score body condition (Henneke 1–9)
Feel and look at six areas: neck crest, withers, behind the shoulder, ribs, loin
(along the back), tailhead.
- **1–3:** thin — ribs, spine, hip bones visible.
- **4:** moderately thin — faint rib outline, ridge along back.
- **5:** moderate (ideal for most) — ribs felt but not seen, back level, fat around
  tailhead beginning to feel spongy.
- **6–7:** fleshy — fat felt over ribs, crease down the back possible.
- **8–9:** fat/obese — can't feel ribs, crease, fat pads on crest, behind shoulder, tailhead.
Score monthly; most leisure horses do best at 5 (4–6). Overweight and cresty-necked
animals are at high risk of **laminitis** (especially ponies, natives, metabolic types).

## 3. Build the ration — forage first
- **Total dry-matter intake:** about **1.5–2.5% of bodyweight per day**; at least
  **1.5% of bodyweight as forage** (hay/haylage/pasture DM). For a 500 kg horse: ≥ 7.5 kg
  DM forage (≈ 8.5 kg hay as fed, since hay is ~88–90% DM).
- **Weigh hay nets** — "a section" or "a net" varies hugely. A hanging luggage scale works.
- For a horse in light work or at maintenance with good-quality forage, often **forage +
  a vitamin/mineral balancer + salt** is enough.
- **Concentrates (hard feed):** add only for energy the forage can't supply (hard work,
  lactation, poor doers). Keep each meal to roughly **≤ 0.5 kg per 100 kg bodyweight**
  (some guidelines say ~2–2.5 kg for a 500 kg horse), spread across meals; big starchy
  meals overflow the small intestine and ferment in the hindgut (colic, laminitis risk).
- **Water:** 25–50+ L/day, more in heat and work; clean and always available. Check
  troughs/buckets daily; a horse drinking less (cold weather) has higher impaction colic risk.
- **Salt:** free access or added daily (sweating horses need more).

## 4. Changes and routine
- Change feeds (new hay batch, new hard feed, spring grass) **gradually over ~7–14 days**,
  replacing a fraction each day.
- Turnout onto lush spring or autumn grass: limit time (start with ~1 h and build up) or
  use a grazing muzzle for laminitis-prone animals. Grass sugars peak on sunny afternoons
  after cold nights.
- Feed at the same times; avoid long empty periods (stomach acid → gastric ulcers).
  Slow-feeder hay nets stretch out eating time.
- Feed after exercise only once the horse has cooled and stopped blowing; no big hard feed
  within ~1 h before hard work.

## 5. Daily checks that catch problems
Eating up? Droppings normal in number and consistency? Drinking? Any pawing, rolling,
looking at the flank, lying down unusually (colic signs) — call the vet early. Feel the
feet for heat and a bounding digital pulse (laminitis). Teeth checked by a vet or
equine dentist ~yearly; quidding (dropping chewed feed) and weight loss can be teeth.

## Gotchas
- Mouldy or dusty hay → respiratory disease; soak or steam dusty hay, never feed mouldy.
- Weight tapes and formulas aren't accurate for ponies/foals — use pony-specific versions.
- Ration figures are commonly cited NRC-based guidelines, **unconfirmed for any individual
  horse**; a nutritionist or vet should check rations for performance, breeding, or
  metabolic horses.

## Verify
- [ ] Weight (tape/formula) and BCS recorded monthly for each horse.
- [ ] Forage weighed and ≥ 1.5% of bodyweight DM daily.
- [ ] No single hard-feed meal over the limit; changes made over 1–2 weeks.
- [ ] Water and droppings checked daily; colic signs known by everyone on the yard.
