# Rotational grazing: pasture cover, residuals and stocking

**Goal:** give pasture enough rest to regrow, graze it at the right height, and match
animal demand to grass supply — with measurements, so decisions aren't guesses.

## 1. Measure pasture cover
**Pasture cover** = kg of dry matter per hectare (kg DM/ha) above ground level.
- **Rising plate meter:** take ~50+ readings walking a W across the paddock; convert the
  average with the meter's equation (calibrate seasonally — the default equation can be
  well off for your pasture).
- **Sward stick/ruler:** measure compressed height at ~20–30 points; rough conversions
  for dense temperate grass are often ~**150–250 kg DM/ha per cm** of height above ground
  — calibrate by cutting a quadrat (e.g. 0.25 m², cut to ground, dry and weigh).
- **Visual** estimates are fine with practice, checked against a meter.
Walk the whole farm **weekly** in the growing season; record cover per paddock.

## 2. Targets (temperate ryegrass dairy/beef systems — adapt to your pasture)
- **Pre-grazing cover:** often ~2,500–3,200 kg DM/ha (about 2.5–3 leaves per ryegrass
  tiller). Graze too early → you eat the plant's reserves; too late → stem, dead
  material, lower quality.
- **Post-grazing residual:** ~1,500–1,600 kg DM/ha (~4–5 cm height for ryegrass).
  Grazing lower than that slows regrowth; leaving much higher wastes feed and lets
  quality fall.
- **Leaf stage:** ryegrass grows 3 live leaves per tiller before the oldest dies; graze
  between 2 and 3 leaves. Leaf appearance rate sets the **rotation length** (fast in spring
  ~18–25 days; slow in winter ~60–100+ days).
Native or tall-grass/rangeland systems use different targets (e.g. "take half, leave
half" and longer rests) — **don't apply ryegrass numbers to them**.

## 3. Paddock size and grazing days
```
Feed on offer (kg DM) = (pre-grazing cover − target residual) × area (ha)
Daily demand (kg DM) = head × intake (kg DM/head/day)
Days in paddock = feed on offer ÷ daily demand
Area needed per day (ha) = daily demand ÷ (pre-cover − residual)
```
Example: 100 cows × 16 kg DM = 1,600 kg/day; pre 2,900, residual 1,600 → 1,300 kg/ha
available → **1.23 ha per day**. A 4 ha paddock lasts about 3 days (fence it into
strips or daily breaks to avoid regrazing regrowth, which typically starts after ~3–4 days).

## 4. Rotation length and the feed wedge
- **Rotation length (days) = total grazing area ÷ area grazed per day.** 60 ha ÷ 1.23 ≈
  49 days. If growth is fast (spring), shorten rotation — and take surplus paddocks out
  for silage/hay.
- **Feed wedge:** rank paddocks from highest to lowest cover and plot them against the
  target line (from pre-grazing target down to residual). Paddocks above the line =
  surplus (cut or graze sooner); a wedge falling below the line = deficit coming
  (slow the rotation, supplement, reduce stock).
- **Average farm cover** is the single best number to watch: dropping week after week
  means demand > growth.

## 5. Stocking rate check
Match long-term stocking to average growth: annual pasture grown (kg DM/ha) × utilisation
(often 60–80% in well-managed rotations, less in set-stocked or rangeland) ÷ annual
intake per head. Overstocking shows as falling average cover, bare ground and weeds;
understocking as rank, stemmy pasture.

## 6. Practicalities
- Water within reach in every paddock (or lane) — see
  agriculture-environment.irrigation-water-management.stock-water-systems.stock-water-reticulation-and-trough-checks.
- Back-fence on long strip grazing so regrowth isn't nipped off.
- In wet weather, on-off grazing (a few hours then off to a stand-off area) protects soil.

## Gotchas
- Rising plate calibrations change with season and pasture type.
- Moving stock "when it looks ready" without measuring usually drifts into overgrazing
  in dry spells.
- All targets above are commonly published for **perennial ryegrass/clover** pastures and
  are **not confirmed for your species or climate**.

## Verify
- [ ] Weekly farm walk recorded per paddock; average farm cover tracked.
- [ ] Pre-grazing and residual targets written down for your pasture type.
- [ ] Daily area allocation calculated from cover, not guessed.
- [ ] Residual measured after grazing and within target.
- [ ] Feed wedge reviewed weekly with a surplus/deficit decision.
