# Milking routine and mastitis control

**Goal:** milk every cow the same way, every time, so she lets milk down fully, teats
stay healthy and new infections are rare — and catch the infections you do get early.

## 1. The routine (per cow)
Consistency matters more than any single step: cows respond to a predictable routine.
1. **Calm entry.** Shouting, dogs or rushing release adrenaline, which blocks
   oxytocin and let-down. Wear clean gloves (nitrile) — bare hands carry bacteria cow to cow.
2. **Forestrip** 2–3 squirts from each quarter onto a dark strip cup or the floor (not
   your hand). Look for clots, flakes, watery or discoloured milk → clinical mastitis.
   Forestripping also stimulates let-down.
3. **Pre-dip** (common in housed herds; less used in some grazing systems): cover the
   teat barrel with an approved pre-milking disinfectant; leave **~30 seconds contact
   time**.
4. **Wipe dry** with a clean single-use paper towel or a laundered cloth per cow — one
   per cow, focus on the teat ends.
5. **Attach the cluster** about **60–90 seconds after first stimulation** (forestrip):
   that's when let-down peaks. Too early → cups climb and squeeze; too late → let-down
   wanes. Align the cluster so it hangs evenly; minimise air admission when attaching.
6. **Take-off:** let automatic cluster removers (ACRs) do it at the set flow threshold;
   avoid over-milking (teat-end damage) and don't strip with the machine on.
7. **Post-dip or spray immediately** after take-off — cover at least two-thirds of every
   teat. This is the single most effective step against contagious mastitis.
8. Keep cows standing ~30 min after milking (fresh feed works) while teat canals close.

## 2. Detecting infections
- **Clinical:** abnormal milk at forestripping, hot/swollen quarter, sick cow. Record cow,
  quarter, date, treatment. Treat per your vet's protocol; mark treated cows clearly
  (leg bands, spray) and follow **withholding periods** — milk from treated cows must not
  enter the vat.
- **Subclinical:** no visible signs but high cell counts.
  - **Individual cow SCC** from herd tests: a cow above ~200,000 cells/mL is likely
    infected.
  - **California Mastitis Test (CMT)/rapid mastitis test:** squirt milk from each quarter
    into the paddle, add equal reagent, swirl ~10 s: gel/thickening = high cells in that
    quarter.
  - **Bulk tank SCC:** a herd trend. Many processors penalise above set thresholds (e.g.
    250,000 or 400,000 cells/mL; EU legal limit 400,000).
- Culture milk samples from clinical cases and high-SCC cows (aseptic sampling) — knowing
  if it's *Staph. aureus*, *Strep. uberis* or *E. coli* tells you whether the problem is
  contagious (milking time) or environmental (bedding, laneways).

## 3. Machine and hygiene
- Machine tested by a qualified technician at least yearly (vacuum level, pulsation,
  reserve). Replace rubber **liners** at the maker's interval (commonly ~2,000–2,500
  milkings or 6 months) — worn liners slip and damage teats.
- Score **teat ends** a few times a year: rough rings at the orifice mean over-milking or
  machine issues.
- Clean the plant after every milking per the chemical supplier's wash programme (hot wash
  temperatures matter).

## 4. Dry-off and fresh cows
- Dry off abruptly at the planned date; use dry-cow therapy and/or an internal teat
  sealant as your vet recommends (selective dry-cow therapy based on SCC is now common).
- Clean, dry bedding in calving areas — many new infections start in the dry period.

## Gotchas
- One relief milker skipping steps can lift the herd's SCC within weeks.
- Teat dip diluted with dirty water or left in uncovered cups loses effect.
- Thresholds above are commonly quoted industry figures, **unconfirmed for your
  processor or country**; antibiotic use must follow your vet and local law.

## Verify
- [ ] Every milker follows the same written routine (forestrip → pre-dip → dry → attach
      at 60–90 s → post-dip).
- [ ] Clinical cases recorded by cow/quarter; treated cows marked; withholding honoured.
- [ ] Bulk SCC trend monitored weekly; high-SCC cows CMT-tested.
- [ ] Liner change and machine test dates logged.
