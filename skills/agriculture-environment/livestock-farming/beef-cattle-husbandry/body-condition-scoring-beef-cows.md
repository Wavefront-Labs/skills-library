# Body condition scoring beef cows

**Goal:** know which cows are too thin to calve well and get back in calf, early
enough to fix it — body condition at calving is the strongest management lever on
next year's pregnancy rate.

## 1. Which scale?
- **US and many others: 1–9** (1 emaciated, 9 obese). Used below.
- **UK, Australia, NZ: often 1–5** (or 0–5/1–8 in some guides). Roughly, US 5 ≈ UK/AU 2.5–3 (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
  Pick one scale, write it on your sheet, and don't mix them.

## 2. Where to look and feel
Score from behind and the side, then confirm by hand in the crush (chute). Key points:
- **Short ribs (transverse processes)** over the loin — the most reliable spot to feel.
- **Spine** along the back.
- **Hooks (hip bones) and pins (pin bones)** either side of the tailhead.
- **Tailhead** — fat pads either side.
- **Ribs** (the fore ribs over the barrel) and brisket fullness.

Press with fingers flat over the short ribs (the descriptions per score below are paraphrased from extension guides and not checked line by line (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)):
| BCS (1–9) | What you see/feel |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | Emaciated: all bones sharp and visible, no fat, muscle wasted. |
| 3 | Very thin: individual ribs and spine obvious, hooks and pins sharp, no fat over the tailhead. |
| 4 | Thin: fore ribs visible (usually 3–5 ribs show), spine still visible, short ribs easily felt with light pressure. |
| 5 | Moderate: ribs just covered (maybe last 1–2 visible), short ribs felt only with firm pressure, some fat beside the tailhead. |
| 6 | Good: ribs not visible, smooth back, tailhead fat pads soft and filling. |
| 7 | Very good/fleshy: spongy fat over ribs and tailhead, brisket full. |
| 8–9 | Fat to obese: bones hidden, patchy fat (rolls), tailhead buried. |

Score to the nearest whole or half point; calibrate by scoring the same 10 cows with a
second person and comparing.

## 3. Targets (commonly used guidelines, spring-calving cow-calf)
- **Mature cows at calving: BCS 5–6** (Montana State ideal 5-6; Alabama extension: 5 for mature cows, 6 for heifers). Below 5, the interval from calving to first heat
  lengthens and pregnancy rates drop noticeably (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
- **First-calf heifers at calving: BCS 6** — they are still growing and lactating.
- **At joining/start of breeding:** ideally at least 5 and stable or rising.
- Avoid > 7 at calving: higher calving difficulty in some herds and wasted feed.
Moving a cow **one BCS (1–9 scale)** takes roughly **34–41 kg (75–90 lb)** of liveweight
gain, beyond normal pregnancy gain (Arizona extension about 90 lb per score; a Nebraska EC281 example gives a 75 lb gap between BCS 5 and 6). That's slow in late pregnancy — so act at
**weaning/pregnancy testing**, months before calving, when cows are not lactating.

## 4. When to score
1. **At weaning / pregnancy testing:** sort into thin (< 5), target (5–6) and fat groups.
2. **~90 days before calving:** last practical chance to put condition on cheaply.
3. **At calving.**
4. **At start of joining.**
Record per cow (ear tag, date, BCS) — a cow repeatedly thin is a culling candidate.

## 5. Acting on scores
- **Sort and feed by group:** thin cows (and first-calvers) get the better pasture,
  supplement (energy/protein per a ration check — see
  agriculture-environment.livestock-farming.livestock-nutrition.feed-budget-and-ration-check)
  while fat cows can be held on lower-quality feed.
- **Early wean** calves (e.g. from ~3–4 months with good calf nutrition) when cows are
  thin in a dry season — stopping lactation is the fastest way to let a cow regain condition.
- Check for causes of unexpected thinness: worms, liver fluke, trace-element deficiency,
  teeth in old cows, lameness, Johne's disease.

## Gotchas
- Long winter coats hide thinness: always confirm by hand.
- Gut fill changes appearance but not BCS — feel bones, don't eyeball bellies.
- Breed differences: dairy-cross and *Bos indicus* cows look different at the same
  BCS; calibrate within your herd.
- Targets and kg-per-score are typical extension figures, **not confirmed for your
  breed or system**.

## Verify
- [ ] Scale named on the record sheet; two scorers agree within half a score on a sample.
- [ ] Every cow scored at weaning/preg-test and ~90 days before calving.
- [ ] Thin group identified with a feeding or weaning plan and a re-score date.
- [ ] At calving, ≥ 80–90% of mature cows at BCS 5 or above.
