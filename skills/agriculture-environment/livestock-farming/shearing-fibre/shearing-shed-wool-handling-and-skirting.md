# Shearing shed wool handling and skirting

**Goal:** each fleece goes from the board to the right bale with the least handling,
with stains, contamination and short pieces kept out of the main fleece lines — buyers
discount whole lots for a few contaminants.

*(This covers shed and wool handling. Shearing technique itself — the blows and
positions — needs hands-on training; it's not something to learn from text.)*

## 1. Before shearing day
- **Sheep must be dry.** Wet wool can heat, discolour and rot in the bale, and
  shearing wet sheep is a health risk to shearers. Shed sheep under cover overnight
  if rain is likely. Shearers may refuse wet sheep — follow the shed's wet-sheep vote/
  rules.
- Empty the gut: hold sheep off feed (but with water) for some hours before shearing
  (commonly ~8–12 h; follow welfare codes) — easier on sheep and shearers, cleaner board.
- Crutch heavily dagged sheep beforehand.
- Shed prep: sweep, remove twine, baling plastic, feed bags; no polypropylene baling
  twine anywhere near wool; check the press, scales, bale packs, stencils.

## 2. Board and floor flow
- Rouseabouts **pick up each fleece immediately** as the shearer finishes and keep the
  board swept between sheep: **bellies** (shorn first) to their own bin, **locks** and
  second cuts swept to the locks bin.
- Keep stained wool (urine-stained, dung, dags) separate at the board — don't let it into
  the fleece.

## 3. Throwing the fleece
1. Gather the fleece with the cut (shorn) side down, tips up: scoop it from the board
   with the hind/britch end in your hands and the neck folded in.
2. At the table, **throw** with a flicking motion so it spreads flat, cut side down,
   whole and unbroken across the skirting table (slatted so locks fall through).
Good throwing means less handling and quicker skirting.

## 4. Skirting
Remove from the edges of the fleece:
- **Heavy sweat points, stained and dag-affected edges, britch extremes** where they're
  much coarser, **burr- or seed-contaminated edges**, any **cotted** (matted) areas, and
  short second cuts.
- Skirt *only as much as needed* — overskirting throws good fleece into lower-value pieces;
  light skirting is the norm for most modern clips (follow your classer's standard).
- Pieces go to the pieces bin; stain and dags to their own lines; contaminated wool
  separate.
- Look for **dark or medullated fibres** (in white wool) and coloured spots — keep
  separate.

## 5. Classing and bins (brief)
The wool classer sorts skirted fleeces into lines by fibre diameter, length, strength,
colour and style; separate bins for each fleece line, pieces, bellies, locks, stain,
crutchings. Lines must be reasonably even — a mixed bale loses value. Classers are
usually registered/accredited (e.g. AWEX in Australia); follow their specification sheet.

## 6. Pressing and branding the bale
- Use approved **bale packs** (nylon packs; no jute in some markets); no foreign material
  (stones, twine, plastic, metal).
- Press to the target weight range for your market (e.g. Australian wool bales are
  commonly pressed to around 110–204 kg depending on type, with a minimum weight; check
  current specs).
- **Brand** each bale: farm brand, wool description/line code, bale number. Record bale
  number, line and weight in the **wool book / specification** sheet.
- Store bales dry, off the ground, away from vermin.

## Contamination checklist (what buyers hate)
Polypropylene (twine, bags), plastic, hair from dogs or goats, black/coloured fibre,
paint and branding fluid (only scourable branding products), stones, metal, vegetable
matter where avoidable.

## Gotchas
- One baling-twine fibre in a fleece can show up as a defect in finished white fabric.
- Shed safety: wet floors, moving shearers' handpieces, back strain on pressing — lift
  and press per the shed's procedure.
- Bale weights and code details vary by country and change over time — **not confirmed
  against current specs**; your broker or classer has the current rules.

## Verify
- [ ] Sheep dry and off feed appropriately; shed swept and plastic-free.
- [ ] Bellies, locks, stain kept separate from the board onward.
- [ ] Fleeces thrown flat, skirted to the classer's standard.
- [ ] Each bale within weight specs, branded and entered in the wool book.
- [ ] No polypropylene or foreign material in any bale.
