# Farrowing and the piglet's first week

**Goal:** most pre-weaning losses happen in the first 3–4 days (crushing, chilling,
starvation). Good farrowing supervision and week-one care is where you save them.

## 1. Before farrowing
- Wash, disinfect and **dry** the farrowing room; run it empty a few days if you can
  (all-in/all-out).
- Move sows in **~5–7 days before due date** (gestation ~114–116 days; "3 months, 3
  weeks, 3 days"). Wash sows on entry if your system does.
- Two climates in one pen: **sow** comfortable at **~18–20 °C** room temperature;
  **piglets** need **~30–35 °C at birth** in the creep (heat lamp or mat), falling to
  ~26–28 °C by weaning. Too hot a room = sow eats less and milks poorly.
- Check: drinkers flow (sows need ~2+ L/min), feeders clean, creep heat working, crate
  bars sound.

## 2. Watching the farrowing
Signs within ~12–24 h: nesting behaviour, restlessness, milk can be expressed from teats
(often within ~6–12 h of farrowing).
- Normal interval between piglets ~**15–20 minutes**; whole farrowing commonly 2–5 h.
- **Intervene (clean glove, lube) if** > ~30–45 minutes since the last piglet with the
  sow straining, or she stops straining but clearly isn't finished (still restless,
  afterbirth not passed, fewer piglets than expected). Ask your vet for a written
  protocol for oxytocin use — misuse can cause harm.
- At birth: clear the airway, dry the piglet (drying powder or towel), put it on a teat
  or under the heat.

## 3. Colostrum (first 24 h)
- Each piglet needs colostrum within its first hours; it has almost no energy reserves.
  A common target is **≥ 200–250 g colostrum per piglet in 24 h**.
- **Split suckling** for large litters: once the litter is born and the first-born have
  fed, shut the biggest/strongest piglets in the creep for **~1–2 hours** so the small
  ones get uncontested teats. Mark the ones you've fed.

## 4. Cross-fostering
- Equalise litters to the sow's **number of functional teats** (count them).
- Do it **within ~24–48 hours**, after piglets have had colostrum from their own mother.
- Move the **biggest** surplus piglets onto a foster sow, ideally one that farrowed at a
  similar time; keep fostering to the minimum — repeated moving spreads disease.

## 5. Day 1–7 routine
- **Iron:** sow's milk lacks iron; piglets in indoor systems become anaemic. Typical
  practice is **iron dextran ~200 mg by intramuscular injection at ~day 1–3** (neck
  muscle, clean needle) — follow your vet and product label.
- Other common processing (tail docking, teeth clipping, castration) is **regulated and
  restricted in many countries** — routine docking is banned in the EU in principle; do
  only what's lawful and justified, by trained staff, with pain relief where required.
- **Check creep temperature by behaviour:** piglets huddled/piled = cold; spread out away
  from the heat, panting = too hot; lying side by side under the heat edge = right.
- Twice-daily check: every piglet suckling, bellies full, no scours, no lameness. Sow:
  eating, drinking, temperature normal (~38.5–39.5 °C); a feverish sow off feed with
  hard udder may have MMA (mastitis-metritis-agalactia) — call the vet; the litter needs
  supplementary milk.
- Introduce creep feed from about ~7–10 days in small fresh amounts.

## 6. Records
Per litter: born alive, stillborn, mummified, fostered on/off, deaths (with cause:
crushed, starved, scours, splay leg, other), weaned. **Pre-weaning mortality** above
~12–15% is worth investigating; crushing and low-viability piglets are usually the biggest
categories.

## Gotchas
- Draughts at piglet level are as bad as low temperature — check at floor height.
- A sow lying down quickly crushes piglets; anti-crush rails and a well-heated creep
  that pulls piglets away from the sow cut losses.
- Figures (temperatures, colostrum, iron dose) are typical industry guidance,
  **unconfirmed for your genetics or system** — use your vet's and genetics supplier's protocol.

## Verify
- [ ] Room and creep temperatures logged at sow and piglet level.
- [ ] Farrowing supervised with intervention rules written down.
- [ ] Colostrum intake ensured (split suckling for large litters).
- [ ] Fostering done within 48 h to teat count; iron given and recorded.
- [ ] Litter records complete; causes of deaths recorded.
