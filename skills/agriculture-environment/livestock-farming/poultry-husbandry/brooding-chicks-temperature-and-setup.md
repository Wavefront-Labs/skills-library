# Brooding chicks: temperature and set-up

**Goal:** chicks find warmth, water and feed in their first hours, so first-week
mortality stays low (commonly targeted below ~1% in commercial broilers) and they grow
evenly. A chick can't regulate its own temperature for its first ~10–14 days.

## 1. Before the chicks arrive
1. Clean, disinfect and dry the house; fresh litter (wood shavings ~5–10 cm), levelled.
2. **Pre-heat 24–48 h ahead** (longer in cold weather) so **litter/floor** reaches
   ~28–32 °C — not just the air. A cold floor chills chicks even under warm air. Check
   with a probe or infrared thermometer.
3. Relative humidity ~60–70% in the first days (dry air dehydrates chicks).
4. Water: drinkers filled and at the right height (nipple lines at chick eye level for
   day-olds, so they peck upward); water ~20–25 °C, not cold from the main.
5. Feed: starter crumb on **chick paper** covering ~25–50% of the brooding area, plus
   feeder trays. Lighting bright (e.g. ~30–40 lux) and near-continuous for the first days
   to help chicks find feed and water.
6. Biosecurity: footbath or boot change at the door, visitors' log.

## 2. Temperature by age (typical guide, whole-house brooding, at chick height)
| Age | Approx. air temp |
|---|---|
| Day 0–3 | 30–33 °C |
| Week 1 (end) | ~29–30 °C |
| Week 2 | ~27 °C |
| Week 3 | ~24–25 °C |
| Week 4 | ~21–22 °C |
| After ~5 weeks | ~20 °C or ambient (feathered) |
With spot brooders (heat lamp, gas canopy), the area under the heat is hotter (~35 °C at
the edge for day-olds) and chicks choose their zone. Genetics guides for your strain take
priority over this table.

## 3. Read the chicks, not just the thermometer
- **Huddled under the heat, noisy, cheeping loudly** → too cold.
- **Panting, wings out, crowded at the edges/away from heat** → too hot.
- **Crowded on one side** → draught.
- **Evenly spread, active, some eating, some drinking, some sleeping, quiet chirping** → right.
Walk through slowly several times a day in the first week; listen as you enter.

## 4. Crop-fill check (are they eating?)
Gently feel the crops of ~30–100 chicks from different areas:
- **~8 h after placement:** target ~80% or more with full, soft crops.
- **~24 h:** target ~95% or more.
Hard crop = feed but no water (check drinkers); empty = not finding feed or too cold.

## 5. Ventilation
Even in the cold, give **minimum ventilation** to remove moisture, CO₂ and ammonia.
Ammonia you can smell (> ~10–20 ppm) harms chicks' eyes and lungs; aim to keep it
below ~10 ppm. Wet, caking litter around drinkers → fix leaks and increase ventilation.

## 6. Daily routine (first two weeks)
- Record mortality and culls by cause (starve-outs, leg problems, navel infection,
  dehydration); count every day.
- Check drinker height, raise with the birds; flush lines; clean or refill feeders.
- Gradually move from paper/trays to main feeders by ~day 5–7.
- Check for **pasty vent** (droppings stuck to the vent) — soak off gently; a sign of
  chilling or dehydration.
- Weigh a sample at day 7: broilers are commonly expected to be ~4–5× their placement
  weight; compare against the breed target.

## Small-scale/backyard version
A brooder box with a heat lamp (secured — heat lamps are a common cause of shed fires)
or a heat plate; start ~32–35 °C under the heat with a cooler area to move to; reduce by
~3 °C a week. Same principles: watch distribution, provide water first.

## Gotchas
- Long transport from the hatchery = dehydrated chicks: water immediately on arrival.
- Gas brooders produce CO — check ventilation and use a CO alarm.
- All targets above are typical breed-company figures, **unconfirmed for your strain**;
  follow your breed guide.

## Verify
- [ ] Floor temperature at target before chicks arrive.
- [ ] Chick distribution checked several times a day and heat adjusted.
- [ ] Crop fill ≥ 80% at 8 h and ≥ 95% at 24 h.
- [ ] Daily mortality recorded by cause; first-week total within target.
- [ ] Day-7 weight compared with breed target.
