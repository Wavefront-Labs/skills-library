# Planting container trees and shrubs

**Goal:** plants that are still alive and growing years after handover. Most landscape
plant losses trace back to the first hour: planted too deep, roots left circling, then
not watered.

## 1. Check the stock on delivery
- Reject or set aside: girdling roots at the surface, solid mats of circling roots,
  broken leaders, bark damage, dry root balls, pests.
- Keep plants shaded and watered until planting. Carry by the pot or root ball, not
  the trunk.

## 2. Find the root flare before digging
The root flare (root collar, where the trunk widens) must end up at or a little above
ground level (Arbor Day Foundation). Find it by scraping soil off the top of the root
ball; if the plant sits too low, pack soil under the root ball to raise it to slightly
above grade.

## 3. Dig the right hole
- Dig the hole 2 or 3 times as wide and as deep as the root ball (Arbor Day wording),
  after loosening a wider area first, and set the flare at grade as in step 2.

## 4. Prepare the root ball
- **Balled and burlapped:** set in the hole, remove all rope, twine and nails, use wire
  cutters to cut vertically up the side of the wire basket and peel it away. Vinyl or
  treated burlap must come off completely; regular burlap under the ball can stay.
- **Container stock:** slide out and inspect the roots before planting.

## 5. Backfill and water in
1. Set the plant straight with the flare at or slightly above grade.
2. Backfill and water in.

## 6. Mulch
- Spread mulch 2 to 4 inches deep over the backfilled area, keeping it 4 inches from
  the trunk.

## 7. Establishment watering
- In dry weather water generously every 7 to 10 days during the first year, slowly at
  the dripline (Arbor Day). Volumes, staking duration and local schedules: use your
  local extension service.

## Verify
- [ ] Root flare visible at or just above grade after settling.
- [ ] Wire, twine, nails and synthetic wrap removed.
- [ ] Mulch in place, clear of the trunk.
- [ ] Watering schedule assigned.
