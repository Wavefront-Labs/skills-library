# Core aeration and overseeding renovation

**Goal:** turn thin, compacted, thatchy turf back into a dense sward with one planned
renovation, timed so the new grass establishes before stress arrives.

## 1. Diagnose first
- **Thatch** is a slow-decaying layer between the grass blades and the soil that can block
  water reaching the roots. Where it is a problem, dethatch with a dethatcher or
  verticutter (UC IPM). NC State notes removal is generally not necessary, so measure and
  decide rather than dethatching by routine.
- **Compaction and thin turf:** find the cause (shade, wear, drought, disease, wrong
  grass, low fertility) and fix it in the same programme; renovation alone repeats.
- Check the seed label and local extension advice before choosing species and rate.

## 2. Timing
- Penn State: aerate in cool weather (early to mid spring, or late summer to early fall).
  Late summer to early fall is usually the best time to establish a lawn from seed;
  overseeding thin turf or bare patches can also be done in late winter, spring or early
  fall.
- NC State (tall fescue lawns): overseed thin areas 15 August to 1 September; do not
  aerate in spring or summer; fall aeration is for lawns with heavy traffic or clay soils.
- Soften the soil a few days before aerating by watering (UC IPM).

## 3. Sequence
1. **Mark irrigation heads, cables and services** before any machine goes on.
2. **Dethatch** where needed; a dethatcher leaves grooves for seed. Rake up the debris,
   then aerate (UC IPM). For large areas NC State suggests several passes with a power
   rake.
3. **Core aerate:** remove cores rather than just spiking (UC IPM). Hand-tool guidance:
   cores 1/4-1/2 in wide, 4-6 in apart, about 3-4 in deep. Roller-type machine tines are
   seldom closer than 6 in apart. Leave cores on the lawn to break up and return as
   topdressing (UC IPM), or break them up by power rake or by dragging chain-link fence
   (NC State).
4. **Overseed.** Penn State, pounds per 1,000 sq ft for a new lawn: Kentucky bluegrass 2-3
   (100% stand) or 3-4 (blend); turf-type perennial ryegrass 4-5; turf-type tall fescue
   6-8; fine fescues 4-5. For renovating turf with turf-type perennial ryegrass it lists
   2-5. NC State: turf-type tall fescue 6 lb, optionally plus 1-2 lb Kentucky bluegrass.
   NC State warm-season overseeding: 5-10 lb per 1,000 sq ft.
5. **Topdress** with a light layer of sand or compost for seed-to-soil contact (NC State).
6. **Water** the seedbed lightly several times a day; as seedlings grow, water less often
   but more deeply (NC State).

## 4. Aftercare
- Mow with a sharp blade, removing no more than one-third of the leaf per cut. NC State
  warm-season overseeding: begin mowing two to three weeks after emergence, at 1.5-3 in.
- Keep weeds cut short until desirable turf is established (NC State).
- Pre-emergent herbicides are applied before weed seeds germinate (Penn State, early to
  mid spring); read the label for the reseeding interval before seeding after one.

## Gotchas
- Seed must touch soil; seeding into thick debris fails.
- Rates here are extension figures; follow the seed supplier's label for your cultivar.

## Verify
- [ ] Cause of thin turf identified; thatch measured before deciding to dethatch.
- [ ] Operations done in the right window; services marked.
- [ ] Seed rate matches the label and extension guidance for the species.
- [ ] Seedbed kept moist until established.
