# Green speed: Stimpmeter measurement and topdressing

**Goal:** consistent, true green speed across all greens, achieved with the gentlest
combination of mowing, rolling and topdressing — not by scalping the turf.

## 1. Measuring speed with a Stimpmeter
1. Find a **flat area** of the green about 3 × 3 m. Check flatness: roll balls both
   ways; if the difference between directions is large (more than ~15%), find flatter ground.
2. Place the ball in the notch, hold the Stimpmeter steady with the tapered end on the
   turf, and slowly raise it until the ball releases on its own (~20°). Don't push.
3. Roll **three balls** in one direction. If they finish within ~20 cm of each other,
   mark the average stopping point. Measure from the release point (the tapered end)
   to the average.
4. Turn around and roll three balls back the other way from the same spot. Measure.
5. **Speed (feet) = average of the two directions.** For a slight slope, the USGA
   correction is `S = 2 × S↑ × S↓ / (S↑ + S↓)` (uphill and downhill readings).
6. Record date, time, green, weather, HOC, whether rolled/mowed. Measure at the same
   time of day — speed changes as greens dry out and grow during the day.

Rough interpretation (feet): 7–8 slow-medium for many member courses; 9–10 fast;
11+ tournament-fast. Pick a speed the turf and contours can carry: fast speeds on
severe slopes leave no pinnable areas.

## 2. Levers on speed (gentlest first)
1. **Rolling** (lightweight greens roller): roughly +0.5 to +1 ft for a day or so;
   rolling 3–4 times a week is common and lets you raise HOC.
2. **Dry the surface:** dew removal (whipping, mowing early), sensible irrigation —
   soft wet greens are slow.
3. **Grooming / light verticutting** to stand up and remove lateral growth.
4. **Lower height of cut** in small steps (e.g. 0.1–0.25 mm at a time). Many bentgrass
   greens sit around 2.8–3.5 mm in season, *Poa annua* similar or a little higher;
   **going too low is the commonest way to thin greens and invite disease**.
5. **Nitrogen:** lean, steady feeding keeps growth (and thus afternoon slowing) in check.
   Spoon-feed rather than big doses.
6. **Plant growth regulators** (e.g. trinexapac-ethyl) reduce clipping yield and smooth
   speed through the day — label rates and intervals only.

## 3. Sand topdressing programme
Purpose: dilute organic matter (thatch) in the top few cm, smooth the surface and firm it.
- **Light and frequent:** e.g. ~0.1–0.2 mm depth (about 10–20 L of sand per 100 m²)
  every 7–14 days in the growing season, plus heavier applications into core holes
  at aeration.
- Use sand that **matches the rootzone particle size** (ask for the lab grading, compare
  with your rootzone spec). A finer sand layered on a coarser rootzone creates a layer
  that holds water — exactly what you're trying to avoid.
- Apply dry sand from a calibrated spreader, then **drag or brush** in, then irrigate
  lightly. Mowers dull quickly on sand — sharpen or skip a mow if it hasn't been
  worked in.
- Target total yearly sand to roughly keep pace with organic matter production; USGA
  articles commonly cite on the order of 1–1.5 cm (0.4–0.6 in) per year for many greens.
- **Monitor organic matter** with lab tests (loss on ignition) of the top 0–2 and 2–4 cm
  each year; rising OM % means more sand or more coring.

## 4. Consistency across greens
Measure all 18 greens monthly (not just one). Differences of more than ~0.5 ft between
greens are noticeable to golfers; look for the cause (shade, drainage, older turf,
different HOC on a worn mower).

## Gotchas
- Calibrate the mower **bench setting**, not just the dial — reels and bedknives wear.
- A "stimp" on a damp morning before mowing reads very differently from the afternoon.
- Volume-per-area conversion for thin topdressing is easy to get wrong: depth (m) ×
  area (m²) = m³, so 0.1 mm over 100 m² is 0.01 m³ (10 L), and a 500 m² green at
  0.15 mm needs ~75 L. Figures in this skill are typical guidance, unconfirmed for your turf.

## Verify
- [ ] Stimpmeter readings taken correctly (3 balls each way, flat spot, same time).
- [ ] Log of speed vs HOC, rolling, weather kept for each green.
- [ ] HOC changes made in small steps with turf density watched.
- [ ] Topdressing sand grading matches rootzone; OM tested annually.
- [ ] Greens within ~0.5 ft of each other on the monthly survey.
