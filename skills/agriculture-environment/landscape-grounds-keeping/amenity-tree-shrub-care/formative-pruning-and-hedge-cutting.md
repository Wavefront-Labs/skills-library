# Formative pruning of young trees, and hedge cutting

**Goal:** small, cheap cuts on young trees now so they never need big, damaging cuts
later — and hedges that stay dense to the base.

## Part A — Formative pruning of young trees

### When
Most species: late winter/early spring dormant season, or summer for species that
bleed heavily (maples, birch, walnut). Avoid the period of leaf flush. Start 1–3 years
after planting once the tree is established (except removing broken/dead wood, any time).

### What to look for (stand back and walk round the tree first)
1. **Dominant leader:** one main stem for most amenity trees. Where two stems compete
   (codominant), **subordinate** one by shortening it (reduction cut back to a lateral)
   or remove it if small. Codominant stems with **included bark** in the fork are a
   classic later failure point.
2. **Dead, damaged, diseased, crossing/rubbing** branches → remove.
3. **Branch spacing:** aim for main scaffold branches spread around the trunk and spaced
   vertically (for large-growing trees, often ~45–60 cm apart). Remove or shorten
   branches clustered at one point.
4. **Branch size:** keep lateral branches less than ~half the trunk diameter at their
   point of attachment — reduce any that are getting too large.
5. **Crown raising (lifting):** done gradually over years for clearance (e.g. footpaths
   ~2.5 m, roads higher — check local standard). Keep at least **two-thirds of the height
   as live crown** on young trees.
**Remove no more than ~25% of the live foliage** in one season (less for older trees).

### Making the cut
- Cut just outside the **branch collar** (the swollen ring at the base of the branch),
  angled to follow the branch bark ridge. Don't cut flush to the trunk; don't leave stubs.
- For branches too heavy to hold: **three-cut method** — (1) undercut ~30 cm out, a
  third through; (2) top cut a little further out to drop the branch; (3) final cut at the
  collar.
- No wound paint (current guidance: doesn't help and can hinder).
- Sharp, clean tools; disinfect between trees where disease (e.g. fireblight) is present.

### Basic health check while you're there
Look for: ties or stakes cutting into bark (remove stakes after ~one season), strimmer
damage at the base, fungal fruiting bodies, cracks, dieback, soil heaving. Report
anything structural to an arborist; work at height or with chainsaws needs the right
training and certification.

## Part B — Hedge cutting

### Timing
- Formal evergreen and deciduous hedges: usually **1–2 cuts a year** — late spring/early
  summer and late summer. Fast growers (privet, Leyland cypress) may need 3.
- **Leyland cypress and most conifers (except yew) don't regrow from old brown wood** —
  never cut back into it.
- **Nesting birds:** in many countries it is an offence to damage an active nest (e.g. the
  UK Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981; US Migratory Bird Treaty Act). Check before
  cutting in the breeding season (roughly March–August in the northern hemisphere) and
  stop if you find an active nest.

### Technique
1. Clear litter and check for hidden wire, fences and nests.
2. Set out a **string line** for the top on long or formal hedges.
3. Cut sides first, **bottom upward**, with a **batter**: the base wider than the top
   (an A-shape, roughly 10–15 cm narrowing per 1 m height), so light reaches the base
   and it doesn't go bare at the bottom.
4. Keep the hedge trimmer blade parallel to the face, sweeping in arcs; on the top, keep
   the blade slightly tilted, working toward the side you've already cut.
5. Collect arisings (sheet laid at the base saves time).

### Safety
Hedge trimmers: eye, ear and hand protection; never cut above shoulder height off a ladder
— use a platform or long-reach trimmer. Check for overhead power lines.

## Gotchas
- "Lion-tailing" (stripping inner branches and leaving tufts at the ends) weakens trees.
- Topping a tree is never formative pruning — it causes weak regrowth and decay.
- Numbers (spacing, % removal) are guideline values from the cited standards as commonly
  summarised, **not confirmed for any species** — adapt.

## Verify
- [ ] One clear leader; no codominant forks left unaddressed.
- [ ] All cuts at the collar, no stubs, no flush cuts, ≤ 25% foliage removed.
- [ ] Stakes and ties checked/removed.
- [ ] Hedge has a batter, the top is level to the line, no cuts into old conifer wood.
- [ ] Nesting check done and recorded in season.
