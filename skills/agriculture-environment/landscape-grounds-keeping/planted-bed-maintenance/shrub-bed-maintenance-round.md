# Shrub and bed maintenance round

**Goal:** leave every bed on the round at the contract's presentation standard, in the
least time, with weeds controlled at the seedling stage rather than fought when mature.

## 1. Know the standard you're working to
Maintenance contracts typically specify outcomes (weed size, bare soil, mulch depth,
edge frequency, litter removal). Get the spec and visit frequency per site;
your round is built around it. If there is no spec, agree one with the client in writing.

## 2. Round sequence (per bed)
1. **Litter pick first** (bags, gloves, litter picker, sharps container — needles turn up
   in public beds; never pick by hand).
2. **Weed:** working from the back of the bed to the front.
   - **Hoe** annual weed seedlings in dry weather — slice just below the surface; they
     wither.
   - **Hand-fork** perennial weeds (dandelion, dock, couch/quackgrass, bindweed), getting
     the whole root where you can.
   - **Spot-treat** persistent perennials with herbicide only if the contract allows it,
     you're licensed/certified as required locally, wind is calm, and you follow the label
     (shield desired plants; no drift onto lawns or water).
3. **Prune / tidy:** deadhead spent flowers, remove dead or broken wood, cut back plants
   obstructing paths, signs and sight lines (road junction visibility is a safety item).
   Leave structural pruning to the scheduled pruning visits.
4. **Edge:** re-cut lawn edges with a half-moon edger or powered edger to a clean vertical
   face, trim the overhanging grass with shears or a strimmer on its side.
   Collect arisings.
5. **Mulch top-up** where thin (see below).
6. **Blow/sweep** paths and kerbs; leave no soil or clippings on hard surfaces.
7. Photo + note any defect for the client (damage, dead plants, irrigation faults).

## 3. Mulch
- Penn State: apply a 2 to 4 inch layer of organic mulch and no more (less on poorly
  drained soil; 1 to 2 inches for fine or double-shredded mulch); more than 4 inches may
  harm the root system. UNH: about 2 to 3 inches, spread out to the dripline.
- Weed the area **before** mulching (UNH).
- Keep mulch off the trunk: Penn State says 3 to 5 inches from young trees and 8 to 10
  inches from mature trees; UNH says at least 3 inches.
- Organic mulches need periodic reapplication; check depth before adding more, since old
  mulch may need removing first (Penn State).
- Coverage: area = volume / depth (1 m³ at 50 mm covers 20 m²).

## 4. Seasonal bedding changes (where used)
- Lift the old display, fork over, rake level, set out plants to the planting plan at
  the specified spacing, water in.
- Keep the plan and count; replace losses within the contract's response time.

## 5. Herbaceous and shrub calendar (outline)
- **Late winter:** cut back perennials and ornamental grasses left for winter structure,
  feed, mulch.
- **Spring:** hoe regularly; stake tall perennials early.
- **Summer:** deadhead, water new plantings, hoe.
- **Autumn:** cut back collapsed perennials (leave seedheads that are part of the design),
  lift tender plants, plant bulbs.

## Gotchas
- Strimming against shrub stems and tree bark is a leading cause of landscape plant death;
  mulch rings and care avoid it.
- Removing "weeds" that are actually self-sown desirables or new perennial shoots in spring
  — learn the planting before weeding hard.
- Herbicide rules differ widely by country and council (some ban glyphosate in public
  spaces) — **check local rules**; this skill doesn't confirm any product's legality.

## Verify
- [ ] Spec and visit frequency known for each site.
- [ ] No weeds above the spec size; no seed heads on weeds.
- [ ] Edges vertical and clean; arisings removed; hard surfaces swept.
- [ ] Mulch depth checked with a ruler at a few points (2 to 4 inches).
- [ ] Defects logged with photos for the client.
