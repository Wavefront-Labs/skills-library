# Football pitch line marking and match preparation

**Goal:** a square, correctly sized, safe pitch on match day, and a routine that
spreads wear so the goalmouths and centre survive the season.

## 1. Setting out a pitch from scratch (or re-squaring)
Tools: two 100 m tapes, a 30 m tape, pegs, string line, assistant.
1. Choose a size within the competition's rules. Law 1 allows touchlines 90–120 m and
   goal lines 45–90 m (international matches: 100–110 m × 64–75 m). Many leagues set a
   tighter standard; junior sizes vary by age group — check the governing body.
2. Peg the first **touchline** along the long axis with a tight string.
3. Square the corner with the **3-4-5 method** (scaled up): from corner peg A measure
   30 m along the touchline to B. From A swing 40 m roughly at right angles, and from B
   swing 50 m; where the two tapes meet is on the true perpendicular. (Any multiple of
   3-4-5 works; bigger is more accurate.)
4. Peg the other corners the same way, then **check diagonals** — both must be equal
   (e.g. 105 × 68 m → diagonal ≈ 125.1 m). Unequal diagonals = not square.
5. Mark centre line, centre circle (9.15 m radius), penalty areas (16.5 m from posts
   inside goal line, 16.5 m into the field), goal areas (5.5 m), penalty mark (11 m),
   penalty arc (9.15 m from the mark), corner arcs (1 m). Lines max 12 cm wide, all
   the same width, and lines belong to the areas they enclose.
Record peg positions with buried markers or GPS so you can re-find them.

## 2. Marking (routine)
- **Transfer wheel or spray marker** with turf marking paint, diluted per the label.
  Over-strong paint scorches grass and builds up.
- Mow before marking, mark before final brushing; run along the string line with
  the wheel centred on the old line.
- Use a robot marker with GNSS if available; still check one diagonal occasionally.
- Never use creosote, sump oil or herbicide to mark (old practices; damage turf and
  often banned).

## 3. Match-day preparation (typical sequence)
1. **Day before:** mow to match height (often ~25–30 mm for winter football on cool-season
   grass; follow the league's surface standard), mark lines, check goal posts are
   anchored and nets secure, check irrigation heads are flush.
2. **Morning:** check the surface: walk the pitch for stones, holes, glass, sprinkler
   heads; roll lightly only if the soil is dry enough (rolling wet soil compacts it).
3. Brush/drag to stand the grass up and remove dew (reduces disease and speeds play).
4. Pre-match inspection with the referee: lines, flags, goals, surface firmness.
5. If frost or waterlogging: follow the competition's inspection procedure — don't
   "fork it and hope". Playing on frozen or saturated turf causes long-term damage.

## 4. Surface performance (optional but useful)
Simple field tests: a **Clegg impact hammer** reading for hardness (track readings
across the season), a ball rebound test, and a quick infiltration ring test in wet
months. Write down where readings are extreme.

## 5. After the match: repair
- **Divot and stud repair** within 24 h: tread in or fork up lifted turf, replace divots,
  fill scars with divot mix (sand/soil + pre-germinated seed of the sward species).
- Concentrate on goalmouths and centre circle. Rotate the training area and **move the
  pitch a few metres sideways** between seasons where the site allows, to shift the worn
  goalmouths and touchline paths.
- Spot-aerate (solid tines) badly compacted areas when conditions allow.

## Gotchas
- Lines painted every few days on a slow-growing winter sward build up paint and kill
  the grass under them — dilute more and paint less often where allowed.
- Corner flags must be ≥ 1.5 m high with a non-pointed top (Law 1).
- Dimensions above are from the Laws of the Game as commonly published; **confirm the
  current edition and your league's rules** before marking.

## Verify
- [ ] Both diagonals equal within a few centimetres.
- [ ] All dimensions match the competition rules; line width consistent ≤ 12 cm.
- [ ] Surface walked for hazards on match morning; goals anchored.
- [ ] Divot repair done within 24 h; worn areas logged for renovation.
