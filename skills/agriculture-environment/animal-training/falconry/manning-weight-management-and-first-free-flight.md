# Falconry: manning, weight management and the first free flight

**Goal:** take a newly acquired hawk from nervous to flying free and returning to the
fist, safely. Written around a **Harris's hawk** (the usual beginner's bird in Europe);
falcons and accipiters need different handling. Falconry is **heavily regulated**: in
the US you need a federal-compliant state permit and an apprenticeship; in many other
countries birds must be captive-bred, ringed/registered, and quarry/land permission
apply. Learn under a mentor — this text is not a substitute.

## 1. Kit and housing
- **Mews** (aviary) with a shelf perch and a weathering area with a bow or block perch,
  protected from weather and predators.
- Leather **anklets/Aylmeri** with flying jesses, a **swivel**, a **leash**, a glove
  (gauntlet) on the non-dominant hand, **accurate scales** (to 1 g for small hawks,
  ~5 g for bigger), a **creance** (~30–50 m light line on a stick), a lure,
  and **telemetry** (transmitter + receiver) before any free flight.
- Food: whole day-old chicks, quail, rabbit — with roughage (fur/feather) for casting.

## 2. Manning (making the bird tame)
- Spend long periods with the hawk on the fist: indoors first, then garden, then
  busier places. Bating (jumping off the fist) is normal early; let it hang briefly,
  lift it back with a smooth swing — no jerking.
- Feed on the fist so the glove means food. The bird is "manned" when it eats
  calmly on the fist with you moving around and people nearby.

## 3. Weight — the core skill
A hawk responds to food when it's keen but not starving. That point is its **flying (or
response) weight**.
- Weigh **every day, same time, before feeding**, and log weight, food given (grams),
  weather and behaviour.
- Reduce weight slowly — **a few grams per day** for a Harris (more like 1 g for small
  falcons/merlins) — by feeding slightly less, until the bird steps to the fist for food.
- Typical response weight for a Harris is often ~10–15% below its fat (fully fed)
  weight, but **every bird differs**: find it from behaviour, not a table.
- Cold weather burns more energy; feed more on cold days. Dangerous lows cause
  "sour" behaviour, then collapse — a bird that sits fluffed up, eyes half-closed, needs
  food and warmth now and a vet.
- Learn **keel** condition: feel the breastbone; a sharp keel = too thin.

## 4. Calling off
1. Hawk on a low perch, you a leash-length away, tidbit on the glove; whistle as it
   steps or jumps over. Reward.
2. Increase to a few metres, then attach the **creance** and call over 5, 10, 20, 50 m.
   The bird should come **instantly**, not after looking around.
3. Introduce the **lure** (a meat-garnished dummy): swing and drop it, whistle; the hawk
   comes to it and is picked up onto the glove with a food exchange.
4. Repeat for several days at consistent flying weight.

## 5. First free flight
- Only when the bird has come instantly on the creance at full distance for ~2–3 days.
- Fit telemetry and **check the signal** before release. Calm day, open area, no roads,
  no livestock or other raptors.
- Short distances first; finish the session on a success and feed up.

## Gotchas
- Flying a bird too heavy = it ignores you and may be lost. Too light = deadly.
- Never fly without working telemetry.
- Imprinted birds can become screaming or aggressive — follow your mentor's rearing method.
- Hoods are standard for falcons, less common for Harris hawks.
- Quarry, season and land permissions are law — check them.

## Verify
- [ ] Weight log with food intake exists for every day since arrival.
- [ ] Bird steps to the fist instantly at a stable, known response weight.
- [ ] Creance recalls instant at full line length for multiple days.
- [ ] Telemetry fitted and range-checked before any free flight.
