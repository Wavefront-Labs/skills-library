# Organic weed control, fertility and certification

**Goal:** a certified organic arable system where the rotation does the work that
synthetic fertiliser and herbicide do in conventional farming — and where the records
prove it at inspection.

## 1. Certification basics
- Standards differ by market: e.g. **USDA National Organic Program** (US), **EU organic
  regulation 2018/848** (EU), plus national standards; your certifier implements them.
  Selling into a market requires certification to that market's standard (or an
  equivalence arrangement).
- **Conversion:** land must be free of prohibited substances for a set period before
  crops are sold as organic — **36 months** before harvest under the US NOP; under EU
  rules generally **2 years before sowing** for annual crops (perennials 3 years).
  Confirm with your certifier; reductions sometimes apply.
- You write an **Organic System Plan** (US term; equivalent management plans elsewhere)
  describing rotation, fertility, pest/weed/disease management, inputs, buffers and
  records. Changes to inputs or practices go to the certifier *before* you use them.
- **Inputs:** check every product (fertiliser, seed treatment, biological, adjuvant)
  against the certifier's approved list or input-review listing (e.g. OMRI in the US).
  "Natural" isn't the test; approval is.
- **Organic seed** required when commercially available in a suitable variety; keep
  evidence of searches if you use untreated conventional seed.
- **Buffers** against drift from neighbours; equipment cleaning records if shared;
  **split operations** (organic + conventional) need strict separation and records.
- Annual **inspection**: field maps, rotation history, input receipts, harvest and sales
  records that reconcile (yields × area ≈ sales + stock).

## 2. Fertility through the rotation
- **Fertility-building phase:** 1–3 years of legume-rich ley (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) (red/white clover,
  lucerne/alfalfa, vetch mixes) mown/grazed — the main N source in stockless and mixed
  systems. Cash crops then draw it down: the most N-hungry crop (e.g. wheat) goes first
  after the ley, less demanding crops later.
- Manure and compost: rules on sources (e.g. restrictions on manure from "factory
  farming" under EU rules) and timing (US NOP requires intervals between raw manure
  and harvest for food crops). Compost must meet process criteria to count as compost.
- P and K: rock phosphate, approved potassium sulfate, manures; lime by soil test as
  usual. Soil test every rotation and watch P/K draw-down in stockless systems.
- Green manures and covers (see cover-cropping skill).

## 3. Weeds without herbicides
Prevention and timing do most of it:
- **Rotation**: alternate autumn/spring sowing and include leys (mowing kills many
  annual weeds and depletes the seedbank). Perennial weeds (thistles, docks, couch)
  are the real threat: deal with them in the fallow/ley phases.
- **Stale (false) seedbed:** prepare the seedbed, let a flush emerge (7–14 days with (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)
  moisture), kill it with a very shallow pass (or flame), then sow without deep tillage.
  Repeat if time allows.
- **Delayed sowing** of autumn cereals so the first flushes are killed beforehand
  (trade-off: yield potential).
- **Blind harrowing / rotary hoeing** pre-emergence at the white-thread stage of weeds,
  shallower than the crop seed.
- **Tine weeding** post-emergence: crop must be anchored (cereals from ~3 leaf (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked);
  check crop tolerance). Set aggressiveness by tine angle and speed; check crop loss
  behind the machine (aim for low % crop buried/uprooted) and adjust.
- **Inter-row hoeing** with camera or RTK guidance in wider rows — much more effective
  than tine weeding on bigger weeds.
- Competitive varieties, higher seed rates, undersown clover.
Timing is everything: mechanical tools kill small weeds (cotyledon to 2 leaves (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked));
a week late and they don't work.

## 4. Pests and diseases
Resistant varieties, rotation breaks, clean seed, balanced fertility (excess N
invites aphids and mildew), and approved products only as a last resort, as allowed
in your plan.

## Gotchas
- Using a single unapproved product can cost a field its status and restart conversion.
- Contaminated purchased manure or straw (persistent herbicide residues such as
  aminopyralid) can damage sensitive crops.
- Yields drop in conversion while you still can't sell at organic premium — plan cash flow.

## Verify
- [ ] Conversion dates and certifier standard documented per field.
- [ ] Every input checked against the approved list before purchase.
- [ ] Rotation includes a fertility-building phase and a perennial-weed plan.
- [ ] Weeding operations timed to weed stage and logged.
- [ ] Harvest, storage and sales records reconcile for the inspection.
