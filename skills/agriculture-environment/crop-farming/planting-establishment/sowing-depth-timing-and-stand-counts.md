# Sowing depth, timing and stand counts

**Goal:** the target plant population, emerged evenly within a few days of each
other. Yield potential lost at establishment is rarely recovered later.

## 1. Timing
- Sow within the window that puts flowering in the low-risk period (see the variety
  skill). Inside that window, sow when **soil temperature and moisture** allow quick
  emergence.
- Soil temperature at seed depth (measured mid-morning with a probe thermometer):
  commonly cited minimums — maize ~10 °C and rising, soybeans ~10–13 °C, cotton (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)
  ~16–18 °C, sorghum ~15–18 °C; winter cereals and canola tolerate cool soils but
  emerge slowly in cold. Check local guidance.
- Moisture: seed must sit in moist soil and stay there. "Chasing moisture" deeper
  works only for crops with long coleoptiles/strong emergence (see below).
- Cold wet spells after sowing raise disease and slug damage; watch forecasts.

## 2. Seedbed
- Conventional tillage: firm below, fine but not powdery on top (aggregates mostly
  < 5 mm around the seed for small seeds). Overworked fine seedbeds crust after rain.
- No-till/direct drill: residue handling is the seedbed — even spread of chaff and
  straw at harvest, sharp openers, row cleaners where residue is heavy, no hair-pinning
  of straw into the seed slot.
- Control the green bridge and weeds before sowing (knockdown or tillage), timing it
  so dying weeds don't harbour pests.

## 3. Depth by crop (typical ranges; adjust for soil and moisture)
- Small seeds (canola, many pasture species): ~1–2 cm (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked). Too deep is the commonest failure.
- Cereals (wheat, barley): ~3–5 cm (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked). Semi-dwarf wheats with **short coleoptiles**
  fail when sown deep (> ~5–6 cm) (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) — check the variety's coleoptile length before
  moisture-seeking.
- Pulses: larger seed, commonly ~4–8 cm depending on species (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) and moisture.
- Maize: ~4–6 cm (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked), uniform depth matters for even emergence.
- Soybeans: ~2.5–4 cm (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
Herbicide placement: some pre-emergent herbicides need the seed **separated** from the
treated soil (knife-point + press wheel systems throw treated soil out of the row) —
follow the label.

## 4. Check placement behind the drill (every field, first few minutes)
1. Stop after ~50 m. Dig out a metre of row on several openers across the bar (including
   the wheel tracks and the ends).
2. Measure depth from the **seed to the original soil surface** (not to the top of
   ridged soil), and check spacing, seed-to-soil contact and fertiliser separation.
3. Adjust depth (opener/gauge wheel/press wheel settings), speed (fast sowing throws
   soil and makes depth erratic — many disc drills place best around 8–10 km/h (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked), tine
   drills often slower; check the manual), or press-wheel pressure.
4. Re-check after any change in soil type or moisture.

## 5. Stand counts
Count once emergence is complete (cereals ~2–3 weeks; check crop):
- **Row crops:** count plants in 1 m of row at ≥ 10 random spots per field.
  `plants/m² = plants per metre ÷ row spacing (m)`. Example: 25 plants per metre at
  0.25 m spacing = 100 plants/m².
- **Broadcast/narrow rows:** 0.25 m² or 0.5 m² quadrat, ≥ 10 throws in a W pattern.
- Note uniformity: gaps > ~30–50 cm (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) in maize rows, or late emergers (a plant a leaf
  behind its neighbours in maize behaves almost like a weed).
- Establishment % = plants/m² ÷ seeds sown/m² (seeds/m² = kg/ha × 100 ÷ TSW in g).
  Record it; it feeds the next seeding-rate calculation.

## 6. Replant decision
Compare **expected yield of the thin stand left as is** against **expected yield of a
replant at a later date minus replant cost**. Many crops compensate well (cereals
tiller, canola branches) — a stand at 50–60% of target (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) often yields close to normal
if even. Replant tables per crop (e.g. maize and soybean extension charts) exist; use
them. Diagnose *why* first (depth, crusting, pests, disease, herbicide) or the replant
fails the same way.

## Gotchas
- Wheel tracks and headlands often establish worse; count them separately.
- Slugs, mice, birds and wireworm can thin a stand overnight — scout the first week.
- Dry-sown seed sitting in dust germinates on the first small rain and may die if no
  follow-up rain arrives ("false break").

## Verify

- [ ] Depth and spacing dug and measured across the bar at the start of every field.
- [ ] Stand counted with ≥ 10 samples per field; establishment % recorded.
- [ ] Any replant decision based on yield comparison and a known cause.
