# Field scouting pattern and action thresholds

**Goal:** find problems early enough to act, identify them correctly, and decide
from numbers whether action pays. Scouting that only notices problems from the ute
window finds them a week too late.

## 1. Frequency
Weekly during establishment and the critical growth stages (stem elongation to
flowering/grain fill in cereals; flowering to pod fill in pulses and oilseeds), more
often when a pest is approaching threshold or weather favours disease. Before every
spray decision. After every stress event (frost, hail, waterlogging, heat).

## 2. Route and sample size
- Walk a **W or zigzag** through the field, starting at least ~20–30 m in from the (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)
  edge (edges aren't representative — but check them separately, pests often enter there).
- **At least 5 stops per field**, more for large or variable fields (common guidance
  ~1 stop per few hectares, minimum 5–10) (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked). Stratify by zone: different soil types,
  low spots, previous problem areas.
- At each stop, sample a fixed unit: e.g. 10 consecutive plants, 1 m of row, a
  quadrat, or 10 sweeps — **the same unit every time**, because thresholds are per unit.
- Record the growth stage (Zadoks for cereals, BBCH generally, V/R stages for soy and
  maize). Most thresholds and many label rates depend on it.

## 3. What to look at
- **Whole plant first**, then leaves (both sides), stems, heads/pods, roots (dig a few).
- **Pests:** counts per unit, and the **beneficials** (ladybirds, lacewings, hoverfly
  larvae, parasitised "mummy" aphids, spiders). High beneficials can hold a pest
  population below threshold.
- **Diseases:** incidence (% plants affected) and severity (% leaf area) on specific
  leaves (e.g. flag leaf, leaf 2). Note the pattern: patches vs uniform, field edges,
  low areas.
- **Weeds:** species, density per m², growth stage (herbicide efficacy depends on it),
  escapes in rows vs whole-field.
- **Nutrients / abiotic:** chlorosis pattern (older vs younger leaves — mobile nutrients
  like N and K show on older leaves first, immobile ones like S, Fe, Mn on young
  leaves), herbicide damage patterns (straight lines, overlaps, headlands), frost, waterlogging.

## 4. Tools
- Hand lens (10–20×) (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked), pocket knife, trowel/spade, sample bags, labels and a marker.
- **Sweep net:** standard 38 cm net (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked), 180° sweeps through the canopy; count insects per
  10 sweeps (common in pulses, lucerne, canola for some pests).
- **Beat sheet:** shake plants over a sheet (row crops like soybean, cotton); count per
  metre of row.
- Sticky and pheromone traps for flight timing (moths, midges).
- Phone app or paper form with GPS photo; send unknowns to a diagnostic lab rather
  than guessing.

## 5. Thresholds
- **Economic injury level (EIL):** pest density where damage cost equals control cost.
- **Economic (action) threshold (ET):** a density below the EIL at which you act so the
  population doesn't reach it. ET depends on crop value, control cost, crop stage and
  pest growth rate — so published thresholds shift with grain prices.
- Use local, current thresholds (extension publications). Examples of the *form*
  thresholds take: "aphids on X% of tillers at stage Y", "N per 10 sweeps", "N larvae
  per m of row", "% leaf area on flag leaf". Don't import numbers across regions
  without checking.
- If no threshold exists, decide from damage potential and record the reasoning.

## 6. Record and act
Per stop: GPS, date, growth stage, counts, photos. Summarise per field: problem,
level vs threshold, recommendation (no action / re-check in N days / treat), and the
product and re-entry/withholding periods if treating. Mark re-check dates; a
population under threshold today can be over it in five days in warm weather.

## Gotchas
- Morning vs afternoon counts differ for many insects (they move in heat).
- Disease symptoms can be herbicide or nutrient issues — check the pattern and history.
- Counting only "the worst plants" inflates numbers; sample at random within each stop.
- Misidentification is the costliest error: confirm before spraying.

## Verify
- [ ] W-pattern route with ≥ 5 stops away from edges (edges checked separately).
- [ ] Same sample unit used as the threshold you're comparing against.
- [ ] Growth stage recorded every visit.
- [ ] Beneficials counted alongside pests.
- [ ] Every recommendation tied to a threshold or written reasoning, with a re-check date.
