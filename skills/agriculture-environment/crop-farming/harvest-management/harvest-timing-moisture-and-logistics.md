# Harvest timing, moisture and logistics

**Goal:** get the crop off at the highest saleable value per hectare — balancing
moisture (drying cost or rejection), field losses (shattering, lodging, sprouting)
and weather risk — with machinery kept busy, not waiting on trucks.

## 1. Maturity and moisture
- **Physiological maturity** (maximum dry matter) comes before harvest-ripe: e.g.
  black layer in maize, loss of green colour in the peduncle/heads in cereals, seed
  colour change in canola. After that it's drying in the field.
- Test moisture with a **calibrated moisture meter** on a hand-threshed sample from
  several spots — not from one sunny edge. Recheck meter calibration against the
  receival site's meter or an oven test at the start of each season.
- Know your **receival limits**: they vary by market and crop. Commonly seen: wheat
  ~12.5% (Australia), 14.5–15% (UK/EU storage targets), maize priced to ~15–15.5% (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)
  with drying shrink beyond. Check your buyer's current standards.
- Moisture changes through the day: grain can gain several points overnight with dew
  and lose them by afternoon. Test before starting each day and as evening comes on.

## 2. Decide when to start
Harvesting early at higher moisture costs drying; waiting costs field losses and
quality. Early-harvest makes sense when:
- You have drying capacity (cost per point removed known — fuel and time).
- The crop is prone to shattering (canola, some pulses), sprouting (wheat in wet
  weather — falling number drops), or lodging.
- Area is large relative to capacity: if the harvest will take 4 weeks at full speed,
  you start early or the last fields suffer.
Field order: highest value and highest loss-risk first; weedy or lodged fields when
they're driest; seed crops before feed.

## 3. Windrowing (swathing) and desiccation
- **Windrowing** canola commonly at ~40–60% seed colour change (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) on the main stem
  (check local guides): even ripening, less shattering, earlier harvest. Windrows must
  be well formed and anchored against wind.
- **Desiccation/harvest aid** (e.g. diquat or glyphosate where registered) for uneven
  crops or green weeds: product, timing and **withholding/pre-harvest interval** per
  label; glyphosate is not appropriate on seed crops or malting barley in many markets.
  Some buyers prohibit pre-harvest glyphosate — check contracts.
- **Direct heading** with pod-shatter-tolerant varieties is increasingly an
  alternative to windrowing.

## 4. Measuring field losses
Check behind the header (combine skill covers adjusting the machine). For a quick
whole-field check: count grains on the ground in several 0.1 m² frames (after
subtracting pre-harvest natural shed counted in the standing crop).
```
loss (kg/ha) = seeds per m² × TSW (g) ÷ 100
```
Example: 40 wheat grains/m² with TSW 40 g = **16 kg/ha**. Typical acceptable total
header losses are often quoted around 1–2% of yield (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) — compute what yours is.

## 5. Logistics
- **Header capacity** (t/h actual, including turns and unloads) sets everything.
- **Chaser bin / grain cart** sized so the header unloads on the go and never stops
  full. Rule: cart cycle time (fill + travel to truck + unload + return) shorter than
  the header's fill time.
- **Trucks:** number = round-trip time to storage/receival (incl. queues) ÷ time to
  fill one truck at the header's rate. Receival queues at peak are the common bottleneck
  — on-farm storage or field bags buffer it.
- Communication: radios/phones, a field plan with entry points and dump spots.
- Records: field, date, load weight, moisture, quality — for marketing and next
  year's planning (see grain-storage-handling and precision-agriculture skills).

## 6. Weather and fire
- Forecasts drive daily plans: harvest the riskiest field ahead of rain.
- **Harvest fire risk** is serious in hot, dry, windy conditions: clean dust and chaff
  off the header daily (blow down), carry extinguishers and a water/firefighting unit
  in the paddock, follow any **harvest/grain-harvesting ban** declared on high-fire-
  danger days (legally binding in some regions).

## Gotchas
- One moisture test at midday on an exposed hilltop says little about the field.
- Delivering marginally over moisture can mean rejection, not a discount.
- Desiccated crop left too long shatters too.

## Verify
- [ ] Moisture meter checked against a reference this season.
- [ ] Field order set by value, loss risk and moisture.
- [ ] Withholding periods met for any pre-harvest product.
- [ ] Header never waits on carts or trucks for more than short periods (check a day's log).
- [ ] Losses measured, not guessed, in at least one field per crop.
- [ ] Fire kit in the paddock and daily blow-down done.
