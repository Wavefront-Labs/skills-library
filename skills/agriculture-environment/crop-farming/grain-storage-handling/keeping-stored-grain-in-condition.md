# Keeping stored grain in condition: sealing and fumigation

**Goal:** seal silos only when fumigating, make sure the seal is gas-tight when you do,
and avoid damage and grain degradation from sealing at other times. Source: GRDC
Groundcover (Australia), issue 151, March-April 2021. Jurisdiction: Australia; check
local rules and the product label elsewhere. (Aeration *design* is covered by
`engineering-technology.agricultural-and-biosystems-engineering.grain-storage-handling.grain-aeration-system-design`.)

## 1. What sealing is for
- The sole purpose of a sealable silo is to make fumigation of insects succeed. It does
  not keep weevils out, and there is no other time a silo should be sealed.
- Successful insect control depends on meeting Australian Standard AS 2628-2010 for
  gas-tight, sealable silos.

## 2. Verify gas-tightness
- A gas-tight silo meeting AS 2628-2010 passes a five-minute half-life pressure test when
  new. GRDC cites research that a three-minute half-life test is sufficient for existing
  silos.
- The test uses 250 pascals of pressure (about 0.036 psi), well below vehicle tyre
  pressure.
- When buying new, ask for the manufacturer's guarantee, cite AS 2628-2010 on the invoice
  and require a demonstrated pressure test on delivery or construction.
- Evidence cited: in a Queensland Department of Agriculture and Fisheries comparison of
  two near-identical silos, the one that passed the three-minute test held over 300 ppm
  phosphine throughout for the seven days needed to control all life stages (eggs,
  larvae, pupae, adults). The one that failed, though it looked well sealed, did not
  hold 300 ppm for seven days and never reached it in the middle or bottom. Survivors of
  such fumigations also add to fumigant resistance.

## 3. Do not leave silos sealed
Apart from the fumigation period set by the label, leave silos unsealed so air can
move. Two risks of prolonged sealing:
- **Structural damage:** temperature changes make internal air expand or, on rapid
  cooling, contract. During rapid change the air to exchange can exceed what the
  pressure relief valve passes, so pressure may reach damaging levels. The risk is worse
  in partly filled silos because there is more air. If a part-full silo must be sealed,
  watch the forecast for sudden temperature drops or a storm on a hot day.
- **Grain degradation:** sealing soon after filling traps warm harvest grain of varying
  moisture; warm head-space air can condense and drip onto the grain, causing mould.
  Leave the silo open to vent without letting rain in, for example with a sealable vent
  or a partly propped lid.
- Size the pressure relief valve and any head-space airflow piping adequately; GRDC says
  large flat-bottom silos (over 750 tonnes) typically need two or more valves.
- Ideally cool grain with aeration fans to even out moisture and temperature, which helps
  prevent mould and insects and extends seed viability.

## Verify
- [ ] Silo sealed only for the label's fumigation period.
- [ ] Pressure test passed before fumigating (5-minute half-life new, 3-minute existing).
- [ ] Pressure relief valve adequate; weather checked if part-full.
- [ ] Newly filled grain not sealed warm; aeration cooling used.
