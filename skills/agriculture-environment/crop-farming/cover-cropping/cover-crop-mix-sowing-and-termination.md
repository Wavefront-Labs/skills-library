# Cover crop mix, sowing and termination

**Goal:** a cover that does its job (hold soil, catch or fix nitrogen, build structure,
suppress weeds) without costing the next cash crop water, nitrogen or establishment.
Most cover-crop disappointments come from late sowing, wrong termination timing,
or herbicide carryover.

## 1. Start from the goal and the window
| Goal | Species that fit (examples) |
|---|---|
| Scavenge leftover N, stop leaching over winter | Cereal rye, oats, triticale, radish, mustard |
| Fix N for next crop | Legumes: vetches, crimson/berseem clover, field peas, faba beans |
| Erosion cover | Cereals (fast, fibrous roots), rye especially hardy |
| Break compaction / "bio-drill" | Tillage radish, other brassicas, deep-rooted species |
| Weed suppression | Dense cereal rye, buckwheat (summer), sorghum-sudan |
| Pollinators / diversity | Phacelia, buckwheat, clovers |
The **window** decides more than the goal: how many frost-free/growing days between
cash-crop harvest and next sowing? Legumes need warmth and time to fix meaningful N;
after a late harvest, cereal rye is often the only thing that establishes.

## 2. Mixes
- Grass + legume mixes balance quick cover and N fixation, and the C:N ratio.
- Keep mixes to species that suit the window and your termination method; every
  species must die by the planned method (some clovers and ryegrasses survive
  herbicides or crimping).
- **Rotation conflicts:** don't put brassica covers before canola/oilseed rape
  (clubroot, blackleg) or legume covers in a pulse-heavy rotation (shared root
  diseases); cereals before cereals can carry disease (take-all, Rhizoctonia) and pests.
- Seed rates: use local guides; mixes use a fraction of each component's sole rate.

## 3. Sow it like a crop
- Drilling beats broadcasting for establishment (seed-to-soil contact); broadcast
  into a standing crop (interseeding/aerial) only where moisture is reliable.
- Sow as soon as possible after harvest — every week of delay in autumn costs biomass.
- Inoculate legumes with the right rhizobia group if the field hasn't grown them recently.
- Check **herbicide plant-back intervals** from the cash crop's herbicides; residuals
  can wipe out a cover (especially small-seeded legumes and brassicas).

## 4. Termination
**Timing — the three trade-offs:**
- **Biomass and N:** legumes fix most N near flowering. Cereals' C:N rises as they
  mature; residue above roughly **C:N 25–30:1** (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) tends to tie up soil N temporarily
  (immobilisation) — early-terminated, leafy covers release N faster.
- **Water:** in dry regions, terminate early enough (commonly a few weeks before
  sowing) to leave soil moisture for the cash crop. In wet springs a late-terminated
  cover can dry out the seedbed usefully.
- **Green bridge:** terminate long enough before sowing (often 2–3 weeks) (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) that pests
  and diseases on the cover don't move straight to the crop (e.g. aphids/viruses,
  armyworm, slugs).

**Methods:**
- **Herbicide:** effective on most species; follow label rates for large, mature plants.
- **Roller-crimper:** works only at the right stage — cereal rye at anthesis (pollen
  shed) or later, vetch at full/late flower. Earlier, it springs back.
- **Mowing / tillage / grazing:** grazing gives income but watch compaction on wet soils.
- **Winter-kill:** frost-sensitive species (oats, radish, buckwheat in many climates)
  die by themselves in cold enough winters — check your typical minimums.
- **Planting green:** sowing the cash crop into the living cover then terminating —
  helps with weed suppression and soil moisture management, but raises slug/pest
  and N tie-up risk; start on a small area.

## 5. Next crop adjustments
- After cereal-heavy covers: often add some extra N early (starter) to offset tie-up.
- After legumes: account for an N credit, but measure (soil nitrate test) rather than
  assume.
- Check planter performance in heavy residue (row cleaners, closing wheels).

## Gotchas
- Cereal rye can be allelopathic to small-seeded crops sown immediately after.
- Volunteer covers (hairy vetch hard seed, ryegrass) become weeds; avoid letting covers set seed.
- Insurance/program rules on cover crop termination dates exist in some countries.
