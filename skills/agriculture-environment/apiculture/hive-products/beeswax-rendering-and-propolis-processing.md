# Beeswax rendering, propolis, pollen and comb honey

**Goal:** turn cappings and old comb into clean wax blocks, and harvest propolis,
pollen and comb honey in a saleable state. Hygiene and fire safety matter — beeswax is
flammable and boils over.

## 1. Beeswax
**Sources**: cappings (the best, pale wax), brood comb you're replacing (dark, more
residue, more slumgum), brace comb.
**Melting points**: beeswax melts at ~62–64 °C; it discolours above ~85 °C and is a fire
risk at much higher temperatures (flash point roughly 200 °C+). **Never heat directly
over a flame** — always water bath or steam.

Methods:
- **Solar wax extractor**: a glazed box tilted to the sun, comb on a metal tray with a
  mesh/filter; wax drips into a mould. Free and gentle; great for cappings; slow with old comb.
- **Steam extractor**: steam from a generator into a box of frames; wax runs out through a
  filter. Fast for old brood frames.
- **Water method** (small batches): wax + water in an old pot, heat gently until melted,
  pour through a fine mesh/old pillowcase into a bucket of hot water; cool slowly. Scrape
  the dirty underside off the cake ("slumgum" residue).
- **Clean**: re-melt over water and filter through fabric (lint-free) two or three times;
  cool slowly (insulated) to prevent cracks.
- Use only stainless, aluminium, or enamel; **iron, copper, brass and zinc discolour wax**.

Uses: foundation exchange, candles (match wick size to diameter), cosmetics, polish,
show blocks. Old brood comb wax can carry spores — don't make foundation from colonies with
foulbrood.

## 2. Propolis
- **Collect**: scrape from frames and crownboards with the hive tool, or put a **propolis
  trap** (a plastic mesh with narrow slots) under the crownboard in late summer — bees seal gaps.
- **Clean**: freeze the trap (~−18 °C), then flex or bang it — brittle propolis snaps off.
  Pick out bee parts and wax. Store sealed, dark, cool.
- **Tincture** (common practice): ~1 part propolis to ~3–5 parts food-grade ethanol
  (~70%), dark bottle, shake daily for ~2 weeks, filter. Selling it with health claims
  is regulated — check local rules; propolis can cause allergies.

## 3. Pollen
- **Pollen trap** at the entrance strips pollen loads from returning foragers. Use for a
  few days at a time — colonies need pollen for brood. Not on weak colonies.
- **Empty daily** (pollen moulds fast), clean of debris, freeze ~48 h to kill pests, then
  dry (to around ~5–8% moisture) or keep frozen.

## 4. Comb honey
- Strong colony, **strong flow**, thin unwired foundation or starter strips, in section
  frames/boxes (e.g. Ross rounds, cut-comb frames).
- Put supers on at the start of the flow; crowd the colony slightly so they fill fast and cap cleanly.
- Remove when fully capped; **freeze ~48 h** to kill wax moth eggs before packing.
- Cut comb: cut with a hot or very sharp knife, drain on a rack, pack in clear containers.
- Avoid oilseed rape and other fast-granulating sources.

## Gotchas
- Wax overheated → dark, smoky-smelling, unsaleable.
- Wax left in a warm room with uncovered combs → wax moth infestations.
- Royal jelly harvesting is specialised (cell grafting every 3 days); not covered here.
- Labelling and food-safety rules apply to all edible products; cosmetics have their own rules.

## Verify
- [ ] Wax melted only via water bath, steam or sun, never direct flame.
- [ ] Final wax block clean, pale/even, no dark layer underneath.
- [ ] Propolis frozen, cleaned and stored dark.
- [ ] Comb honey frozen before packing and labelled with batch and date.
