# Colony inspection and seasonal management

**Goal:** open a hive calmly every 7–10 days in the active season, know within a few
minutes whether the colony is queenright, healthy and has room, then act. Written
for temperate climates and Langstroth/National-type frame hives; timing shifts with
your local flowering calendar.

## 1. Before opening
- Warm (≥ ~15 °C), calm, ideally midday when foragers are out. Avoid thunder, rain, cold wind.
- Kit: veil/suit, gloves, **hive tool**, **lit smoker** (cool white smoke — fuel like
  hessian, pine needles, wood shavings), record card, spare frames/box, queen cage.
- Ask yourself the question you're opening for. Inspections without a reason disturb bees.

## 2. Opening and handling
1. A couple of puffs at the entrance, wait ~30 s; lift the crownboard a crack, puff, close, wait.
2. Remove an outer frame first (usually stores) and stand it outside the box, to make room.
3. Lift frames **vertically**, hold over the box, turn by rotating around the top bar —
   never tilt flat (fresh nectar and new comb can fall out).
4. Move slowly; crushed bees release alarm pheromone (banana smell) — smoke over it.
5. Replace frames in the same order and orientation. Done in ~10–15 min.

## 3. The five questions (write them on every card)
1. **Queenright?** Look for **eggs** (one per cell, standing upright, at the cell base).
   Eggs = queen present within the last ~3 days. You don't need to see the queen.
2. **Brood pattern**: solid capped worker brood with few gaps is good. Very patchy, sunken
   or perforated cappings → see varroa-monitoring-and-brood-disease-recognition.
3. **Space**: are the brood frames crowded, bees on every frame, stores in the brood
   area? → add space (super).
4. **Stores**: at least ~2 frames of honey/stores in the active season; heft the hive in autumn.
5. **Swarm signs**: queen cells along frame edges/faces → see swarm-prevention-and-artificial-swarm.
Also flag: multiple eggs per cell or eggs on cell walls = **laying workers**; bullet-shaped
drone brood in worker cells = **drone-laying queen** — both need intervention.

## 4. The year (temperate, Northern Hemisphere example)
- **Late winter**: heft for weight; if light, add **fondant** on top of the frames. Don't
  open the brood nest.
- **Spring build-up**: first full inspection on a warm day; clean floor; check queen;
  start varroa monitoring. Add a **super** (over a queen excluder, if you use one) when
  bees cover ~7–8 of 10 brood frames.
- **Swarm season** (often April–July): inspect every 7 days — queen cells are capped ~8
  days after the egg.
- **Main flow**: keep adding supers before the last one is full (add when ~2/3 drawn and filling).
- **Late summer**: harvest; then **varroa treatment** promptly so winter bees are raised healthy.
- **Autumn feeding**: heavy syrup (~2:1 sugar:water by weight) until the colony has
  enough winter stores — often quoted ~15–25 kg depending on climate and bee type.
  Fit mouse guards; reduce entrance against wasps/robbing.
- **Winter**: leave alone, check weight monthly, keep entrances clear, ventilation adequate.

## 5. Records
One card per hive: date, temperament, eggs/brood/queen seen, frames of brood, stores,
queen cells, actions, varroa count. Patterns over weeks matter more than one inspection.

## Gotchas
- Feeding syrup with supers on puts sugar in your honey.
- Robbing: never spill syrup or leave comb in the open; reduce entrances on weak colonies.
- Local rules may require apiary registration and notification of notifiable diseases.
- Bee stings can cause anaphylaxis — keep a plan and don't work alone if you've had reactions.

## Verify
- [ ] Each inspection answered the five questions on the record card.
- [ ] Eggs (or a reason for their absence) seen every visit in swarm season.
- [ ] Supers added before the colony ran out of space.
- [ ] Every colony goes into winter with adequate weight and post-treatment mite counts low.
