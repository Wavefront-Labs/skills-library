# Queen rearing: grafting, cell builders and mating nucs

**Goal:** produce well-fed, well-mated queens from your best colonies on a schedule.
Needs strong colonies, a nectar flow (or feeding), and plenty of drones in the area.
Grafting-free alternatives (Miller method, Jenter/Nicot cell-plug kits) follow the same
calendar.

## 1. The calendar (day 0 = egg laid)
| Day | Stage |
|---|---|
| 0–3 | egg |
| 3–4 | **graft young larvae** (≤ ~24 h old, as small as a "C" smaller than the cell base) |
| ~8 | cell capped |
| ~14 | **ripe cell — move to mating nucs** (the day before emergence at latest) |
| ~16 | virgin queen emerges |
| ~21–28 | mating flights |
| ~25–35 | laying; check for eggs |
Mistiming day 14–16 is the classic failure: the first virgin out kills the rest.

## 2. Choose breeding stock
Breed from colonies with your best record for temperament, health (low varroa growth,
hygienic behaviour), productivity and low swarminess. You also want drones from good
colonies — leave drone comb in them.

## 3. The cell builder
A strong, well-fed colony with lots of **nurse bees** and the feeling of being
queenless (at least in the area with the cells).
- **Queenless starter**: shake ~6+ frames of young bees into a box with pollen and
  honey, no brood, no queen; add the graft frame a couple of hours later; move cells to a
  finisher after ~24 h.
- **Queenright finisher / Cloake board**: queen below an excluder or a temporary slide
  board, cells in an upper box of young brood and stores.
- Feed light syrup (~1:1) and pollen if there's no flow.

## 4. Grafting
1. Prep a cell bar with plastic or wax cups; put them in the builder for a few hours
   to be "polished".
2. Take a frame of **young larvae** from the breeder — the larvae should be floating in
   royal jelly, tiny. Work in a warm (~25 °C), humid room or under a damp towel; bright
   light and magnification help.
3. Slide the grafting tool under the larva from the back of its curve, lift with its jelly,
   place it in the cup base and withdraw without rolling it over.
4. ~20–30 grafts per bar is typical. Mark the date.
5. Put the bar into the starter/builder fast; check acceptance after ~24 h (cups drawn
   out and filled with jelly). 50–80% acceptance is decent; below that, check larva age
   and builder strength.

## 5. Mating nucs
- Prepare nucs (mini-nucs such as Apidea, or 2–5 frame nucs) **queenless** a day before.
- On day ~14, carefully cut/move ripe cells (no jarring — pupae are fragile) into each nuc,
  one per nuc.
- Leave them alone ~3 weeks. Then look for **eggs in a regular pattern**.
- Mark (e.g. international colour code by year: white/yellow/red/green/blue for years
  ending 1-6/2-7/3-8/4-9/5-0) and optionally clip.

## 6. Introduction
- Make the receiving colony queenless; remove queen cells it raises.
- Introduce in a cage with a candy plug so bees release her over ~2–5 days; leave alone
  for at least a week before checking.
- Introducing into a nuc and then uniting by the newspaper method is the safest route.

## Gotchas
- Old larvae → poorly-fed "emergency" quality queens.
- Grafting in cold, dry air dries larvae in seconds.
- Not enough drones / bad weather in the mating window → poorly mated or lost queens.
- Laws in some places restrict queen movement and importing stock; check biosecurity rules.

## Verify
- [ ] Calendar written with dates for graft, check, cell transfer and egg check.
- [ ] Graft acceptance recorded at 24 h.
- [ ] Ripe cells moved by day 14.
- [ ] Mated queens laying a solid egg pattern before you introduce or sell them.
