# Honey harvest: clearing, extracting and moisture control

**Goal:** remove only ripe honey, get it out of the comb cleanly, and jar it at a moisture
level that won't ferment — in a hygienic way that meets food rules.

## 1. Is it ripe?
- Bees cap cells when honey is ripe. Rule of thumb: frames **≥ ~80–90% capped**.
- **Shake test** for uncapped areas: hold the frame horizontal over the hive and give a
  sharp shake; nectar that sprays out is too wet.
- **Refractometer** (calibrated with the supplied block or olive oil): aim for
  **≤ ~18% water**; above ~20% risks fermentation, and many legal standards (e.g. Codex,
  EU) cap moisture at 20% for most honeys (heather honey 23%). Measure several frames.
- Don't take so much that the bees are left short — leave the brood box stores.

## 2. Clearing bees from supers
- **Clearer board** with Porter escapes or a rhombus escape, placed under the supers
  ~24–48 h before harvest (bees move down, can't return). Check there's no brood in the
  supers and no gaps for robbers.
- **Fume board** with a bee-repellent (follow product instructions; warm weather) — fast.
- **Bee blower** or brushing/shaking frame by frame for small numbers.
- Take boxes away promptly, cover them, and don't leave honey exposed outdoors — robbing
  starts in minutes.

## 3. Extracting
Set up in a clean, bee-proof room, ideally warm (~25–30 °C) so honey flows.
1. **Uncap**: hot or cold uncapping knife, uncapping fork for dips, over an uncapping
   tray. Keep cappings — they hold good honey and good wax (see beeswax-rendering-and-propolis-processing).
2. **Load the extractor** evenly by frame weight to stop wobble.
   - **Tangential**: spin one side at low speed, flip, spin the other side fully, flip
     back to finish — prevents the comb breaking from honey weight on the inside face.
   - **Radial**: both sides at once; ramp speed gradually, ~10–15 min total depending on
     viscosity.
3. Return wet supers to the hives in the evening for the bees to clean (or store them
   wet and protect against wax moth).

## 4. Straining and settling
- Through a **coarse then fine strainer** (e.g. double sieve, ~600/200 microns) into a
  food-grade **settling tank** with a honey gate.
- Settle **24–48 h** at room temperature; air bubbles and wax particles rise; skim the
  surface before bottling.

## 5. Bottling and storage
- Clean, dry glass jars with new lids; fill from the bottom of the tank.
- Store cool and dry; honey is hygroscopic and absorbs moisture if left uncovered.
- **Crystallisation** is natural (glucose-rich honeys like oilseed rape set within days —
  extract them fast or they set in the comb). Re-liquefy gently at ≤ ~40–45 °C in a warming
  cabinet; overheating damages flavour and raises HMF.
- **Creamed / soft-set honey**: seed with ~10% fine-crystal honey, stir regularly at ~14 °C
  for a fine texture.

## 6. Food rules
Selling honey usually involves food-business registration, a clean extraction area,
traceability by batch and correct labelling (name, weight, origin, best-before, producer).
Rules vary — check your national food authority.

## Gotchas
- Harvesting during varroa treatments that require supers off — read labels.
- Extracting frames with brood in them (from a queen in the supers).
- High-moisture honey ferments: frothy, sour smell, bubbling under the lid.
- Plastic buckets and dirty utensils give off-flavours; use food-grade stainless or plastic.

## Verify
- [ ] Refractometer reading per batch logged, ≤18–20% water.
- [ ] Clearer boards on 1–2 days before; no robbing at harvest.
- [ ] Honey strained, settled, skimmed, jarred and labelled with a batch number.
- [ ] Wet supers returned or stored protected from wax moth.
