# Orchard pollination contracts and moving hives

**Goal:** deliver strong colonies to the right crop at the right time, get paid for
it, and bring the bees home alive. Pollination rates and strength standards vary a
lot by crop and region; numbers below are commonly cited examples — **put the agreed
figures in the contract**.

## 1. The contract — write it down
Include:
- Crop, location(s) with access route and placement points, **expected bloom dates**.
- Number of colonies and **minimum strength** (e.g. frames covered with bees; some
  growers' standard for almonds is ~8 frames, often a minimum ~6; check market norms).
- **Delivery** at a set bloom stage (commonly ~5–10% bloom, so the bees work the target
  crop rather than nearby weeds) and **removal** date or trigger (petal fall).
- **Price** per colony, payment timing, bonuses/penalties for strength.
- **Pesticides**: the grower gives advance notice (e.g. 48 h) of any spray, no
  bee-toxic insecticides during bloom, sprays at night/after foraging only if necessary;
  label "bee hazard" warnings respected. Who pays for losses.
- **Water** source for the bees, access rights, liability and insurance, and who
  inspects strength (third-party audit common in almonds).

## 2. Build colonies to standard
- Plan backwards from bloom: in late winter, feed **pollen substitute** patties and
  light syrup ~6–8 weeks before bloom to build brood.
- Varroa low going into the contract (see varroa-monitoring-and-brood-disease-recognition).
- **Frame count** on the day: lift the lid, count frames with bees covering most of the
  face (both sides), on a cool morning when bees are clustered. Record per colony.
- Equalise weak and strong colonies, or unite weak ones — don't send weak colonies.

## 3. Moving hives
- Move **at night** (all foragers home) or very early morning.
- Close entrances with screened blocks or use a **migratory screen**/net over loads;
  hives need **ventilation** — overheated, closed-in colonies die quickly ("melt").
- **Strap or staple** every hive (floor, boxes, lid together); palletised hives (4 per
  pallet) are moved with forklifts or a boom loader.
- Truck loads netted; travel without long stops in heat; carry water to spray on screens
  if bees get hot.
- Some jurisdictions require movement permits, health certificates, or inspections
  (e.g. interstate/border checks). Check before loading.

## 4. Placement in the crop
- **Density** examples (verify for your crop): almonds ~2 colonies/acre (~5/ha);
  apples ~1/acre (~2.5/ha); highbush blueberries ~2–4/acre; canola for seed much higher.
- Place in **groups** distributed through the block rather than all at one edge — bees
  forage mostly within a few hundred metres when bloom is dense. Example: groups every
  ~100–150 m.
- Sunny, wind-sheltered, entrances facing the morning sun, raised off flood-prone ground,
  accessible for trucks in wet weather.
- Water supply within reach.

## 5. During and after
- Check a sample of colonies weekly; record weather (bees fly above ~12–13 °C, little in rain/wind).
- **Pesticide incident**: dead bees piling at entrances, twitching bees — photograph,
  collect samples (frozen), notify the grower and your agriculture authority.
- Remove promptly at the agreed time — colonies stuck in an orchard after bloom risk later
  sprays and starve on monoculture.

## Gotchas
- Delivering before bloom (bees find competing forage) or after (missed window).
- Strength measured in the heat of the day looks weaker than it is.
- Concentrating hundreds of colonies from many apiaries (e.g. almonds) spreads disease and mites.

## Verify
- [ ] Signed contract with strength, density, timing, spray notice and price.
- [ ] Strength records per colony on the day of delivery.
- [ ] Load secured, screened and ventilated; permits in hand.
- [ ] Hives placed in distributed groups at the agreed density and removed on time.
