# Varroa monitoring and brood disease recognition

**Goal:** know your mite load in numbers, treat at the right time, and recognise brood
disease early, especially American foulbrood. Treatment products and legal status differ
by place: **only use products registered where you are, exactly per label**.

## 1. Count mites, don't guess
- **Alcohol wash:** take a half-cup scoop of bees, roughly 300, from a frame of older
  uncapped brood covered in nurse bees, with the queen removed from it first. Put them in
  a jar of 70% or stronger ethanol or isopropyl alcohol and shake vigorously for 60 seconds
  (Penn State; NC State says at least 30 seconds). Count the mites and divide by the bees
  to get the percentage.
- **Sugar shake:** same sample size (about 300 bees), NC State.
- **Sticky board:** left in for 24 hours; NC State gives 60 to 190 mites as the action
  level depending on colony size.
- **Drone brood:** action if 10% or more of drone pupae are infested (NC State).

## 2. Thresholds differ by source
- Penn State: 2% or less is acceptable; more than six mites in a 300-bee wash means act.
- NC State: act at 9 or more mites per 300 bees.
- Choose your regional authority's number and apply it consistently.

## 3. Treatment toolkit (IPM)
- **Drone comb removal:** drone-sized combs attract mites and are removed before drones
  emerge; effectiveness depends on the time of year (NC State).
- **Oxalic acid:** best applied when the colony is broodless, with going into winter an
  ideal time (NC State).
- **Formic acid:** NC State gives daily highs of 50 to 79 F, removal if over 82 F in
  the first week, not during a honey flow. Handle with precautions on the label.
- **Thymol products:** each has its own temperature range and schedule on its label
  (NC State lists Apilife VAR and Apiguard); do not use with honey supers where the label
  forbids.
- **Amitraz:** NC State notes mite resistance and does not recommend it at this time.
  Rotate treatments rather than relying on one product.

## 4. Brood disease recognition (NC State)
| Sign | Suspect |
|---|---|
| Sealed brood discoloured and sunken, often punctured cappings; remains rope out of the cell an inch or more on a twig | **American foulbrood (AFB)** |
| Dead brood usually young, unsealed larvae; remains rubbery and granular, not elastic | **European foulbrood (EFB)** |
| Hardened white or black "mummies" in cells, on the bottom board or outside the entrance | **Chalkbrood** |
| Dead larvae watery and granular with a thick skin forming a sac; head curled up like a canoe | **Sacbrood** |
Suspected AFB: verify it, then NC State's advice is to burn all frames and euthanise the
bees, scorch or fumigate the empty boxes, and tell your regional apiary inspector.
Check your own state or country for rules before acting.

## Gotchas
- Treating by the calendar without counting hides the real load.
- Never leave treatments in longer than the label allows.
