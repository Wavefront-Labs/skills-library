# Harvest stage and stem conditioning

**Goal:** flowers that last a week or more in the customer's vase. Most vase life is won
or lost in the first two hours after cutting.

> Draft. Harvest stages are commonly taught specialty-cut-flower guidance; varieties and
> markets (wholesale vs direct) shift the ideal stage. Check product labels for any
> commercial hydration/holding solution.

## 1. Clean buckets — non-negotiable
Bacteria block stem xylem. Scrub buckets with detergent, then sanitise (e.g. dilute
bleach, ~1 tsp per litre / or a commercial bucket cleaner), rinse. Fresh, cool, clean water
each harvest. Strip all foliage that would sit below the waterline.

## 2. When to cut
- **Time of day:** early morning (stems fully turgid, coolest) or evening. Avoid midday heat.
- **Stage** (a sample of common crops):
| Crop | Harvest stage |
|---|---|
| Zinnia | "Wiggle test": hold ~20 cm down the stem and shake — if the head stays upright (stem firm), cut; if it droops, wait. Fully open. |
| Dahlia | Fully open (won't open further after cutting); back petals fresh, not papery. |
| Sunflower (single-stem) | Petals just lifting off the disc. |
| Snapdragon | Lower 1/3 of florets open. |
| Sweet pea | 1–2 lower florets open, top in bud. |
| Peony | "Marshmallow stage": bud soft like a marshmallow, colour showing. |
| Tulip | Bud coloured but closed. |
| Lisianthus | 1–2 flowers open per stem. |
| Rudbeckia, cosmos | Cosmos: petals just opening; rudbeckia: fully open. |
| Grasses/foliage | Mature enough to hold up when cut (test-hydrate a few). |
Cut at the angle with sharp, clean snips; long stems (sorted by length later).

## 3. Into water immediately
Put stems in water **in the field** — carry buckets in a shaded cart. Don't lay cut stems
on the ground in the sun "while you finish the row".

## 4. Conditioning by stem type
- **Most soft-stemmed annuals:** recut under water or immediately before placing in a
  hydration solution or clean water; rest in a cool place ~2–4 h (or overnight) before
  arranging.
- **Woody stems** (lilac, shrubs, hydrangea): recut and split or slit the base ~2–3 cm; hydrangeas
  benefit from a dip in hot water (~80 °C) for ~30 s or full submersion if wilting.
- **Latex/sap stems** (euphorbia, poppies): **sear** the cut end with a flame or dip in
  boiling water ~7–10 s to stop sap loss; keep separate from other flowers for a few
  hours (daffodil/narcissus sap shortens other flowers' life).
- **Hollow stems** (delphinium, amaryllis): hold upside down and fill, or plug.
- Use hydration solution (low pH helps uptake) and later a holding solution/flower food
  with sugar for buds to open (follow the product label).

## 5. Cooling and ethylene
- Get flowers to a cooler at **~2–5 °C**, ~85–95% RH, as quickly as possible (tropicals
  like zinnia/dahlia prefer ~5–8 °C; some tropicals 10–13 °C).
- **Ethylene-sensitive** crops (sweet peas, snapdragons, delphinium, carnations, some lilies)
  drop or shatter: never store with ripening fruit, exhaust fumes or decaying material;
  growers use STS or 1-MCP anti-ethylene treatments (check legality/label).
- Keep the cold chain — warming and recooling causes condensation and botrytis.

## 6. Bunching and dispatch
Bunch to a count or weight, rubber-band at the stem, sleeve, label with harvest date and
"recut stem on arrival". Keep upright in clean water to the customer.

## Gotchas
- Dirty buckets cause more short vase life than any harvest mistake.
- Over-mature cut stage loses days of vase life; immature buds often fail to open (peonies excepted when cut right).
- Mixing narcissus with other flowers before they've rested in their own bucket.

## Verify
- [ ] Buckets washed and sanitised before every harvest.
- [ ] Crops cut at the stage in your table, early or late in the day.
- [ ] Stems in water within seconds of cutting; conditioned by stem type.
- [ ] Cooler at target temperature; ethylene sources kept away.
- [ ] Hold-test a few stems of each crop at room temperature: vase life recorded.
