# Apple tree formative and maintenance pruning

**Goal:** a tree with a strong framework, light reaching all fruiting wood, and a steady
supply of young fruiting wood, achieved with a few deliberate cuts a year, not a
winter-long hack.

> Applies to apples and pears (pome fruit) in temperate climates. Stone fruit has different
> timing and bud habits: use a local extension guide for it.

## 1. Two kinds of cut
- **Thinning cut:** removes a shoot or branch entirely back to its point of origin.
  Opens the tree without stimulating much regrowth.
- **Heading cut:** removes the terminal part of a shoot or limb. Use deliberately
  (forming branches), not as a general trim; favour thinning.

## 2. Choose a system
- **Central leader / spindle:** one upright leader with tiers of branches. Standard for
  dwarfing rootstocks and commercial orchards.
- **Open centre (vase):** leader removed, a few scaffolds radiating out.
- **Restricted forms** (cordon, espalier, step-over) on dwarfing stocks.

## 3. Formative pruning
**Newly planted whip:** make the first heading cut to a bud. Local guides differ on
height (Clemson: about 28 to 32 inches for standard trees, 30 to 35 for spur-type and
semi-dwarf; Oregon State: 24 to 30 inches for central leader). Follow your regional guide.
**Following years:** choose a leader and scaffold branches once buds have grown out
(Clemson: 4 to 5 inches), head the central leader back by about one third in year two,
and remove branches with angles under 45 degrees. The strongest crotch angle is 45 to
60 degrees; bend or tie limbs that are too upright while they are young and flexible,
and remove hardened narrow-angled ones. Training to a central leader or open centre
takes about four winters (Oregon State).

## 4. Winter maintenance pruning (dormant season)
In this order:
1. Dead, diseased, damaged wood, then crossing/rubbing branches.
2. Watersprouts (one-year shoots growing within the tree): thin them out.
3. **Renewal:** thin congested, aged spur systems so they are reinvigorated.
4. Keep the canopy open so light reaches fruiting wood.
- On mature apple and pear trees, about 20% of new growth can be removed (UC Master
  Gardeners); do not strip the tree.
**Spur vs tip bearers:** apples and pears mainly fruit on spurs of wood two or more years
old. Tip-bearing varieties fruit at the tips of long shoots, so prune them very lightly
and do not head every shoot.

## 5. Summer pruning
Remove unwanted growth in early summer or after harvest (Clemson: late August to early
September). It speeds framework development, opens the canopy and reduces regrowth in
vigorous trees. Too much opening can sunburn bark (UC). Focus on thinning, not heading.

## 6. Renovating an old, neglected tree
Spread corrective pruning over **2 to 3 seasons** (Clemson); Oregon State says "gradually
over several years". Do not fertilise that spring after severe pruning.

## Gotchas
- Pruning wounds are entry points for disease: prune in dry weather and keep tools clean.
- Pruning hard to "control size" on a vigorous rootstock stimulates regrowth: choose
  the rootstock for the size you want, or summer prune.

## Verify
- [ ] System chosen and visible: one leader, or a few scaffolds.
- [ ] Scaffold branches at 45 to 60 degrees, none under 45.
- [ ] Moderate amount removed this winter; thinning cuts outnumber heading cuts.
- [ ] Tip-bearing varieties not tipped all over.
- [ ] Next summer: few watersprouts, good light into the canopy.
