# Canopy management and harvest timing

**Goal:** an open, evenly lit fruit zone with balanced crop and leaf, then a harvest date
chosen from sampled numbers and tasting — not from the calendar.

> Draft. Written for VSP-trained wine grapes in temperate regions. Targets vary with
> variety, wine style, climate and winemaker; agree targets before the season.

## 1. Winter pruning sets the season
- **Spur pruning** (cordon): leave 2-bud spurs spaced ~10–20 cm.
- **Cane pruning** (Guyot): keep 1–2 canes of ~8–15 buds plus a renewal spur.
- Bud number is your first crop-level lever: start from ~15–20 buds per metre of row
  (adjust to vigour and past results).
- Weigh prunings from sample vines (kg/vine; "pruning weight") — it is the vigour measure
  for vine balance below.

## 2. Spring–summer canopy steps
1. **Shoot thinning** (shoots ~10–20 cm): remove doubles, shoots from the trunk/head,
   and crowded shoots; aim ~12–18 shoots per metre of row for VSP.
2. **Shoot positioning:** raise catch wires as shoots grow; tuck shoots vertically so they
   don't shade the fruit zone or flop into the row.
3. **Leaf removal** in the fruit zone: remove leaves (and laterals) around clusters on the
   morning/east side, ideally around fruit set to berry pea-size (berries adapt to sun
   early and are less prone to sunburn). On hot afternoon sides, keep some cover. Target
   ~1–2 leaf layers in the fruit zone and ~60–80% cluster exposure (varies by climate).
4. **Hedging/topping** when shoots exceed the top wire by ~30 cm; keep ~14–16 leaves per
   shoot (~1–1.5 m² leaf area per kg fruit is a commonly quoted adequacy figure).
5. **Crop thinning (green harvest)** at/around veraison if crop load is too high or ripening
   is uneven: drop wings, second-crop and late-colouring clusters.

## 3. Vine balance checks
- **Ravaz index** = yield (kg) ÷ pruning weight (kg) per vine. Around **5–10** is usually
  balanced; < 5 = vegetative (overgrown); > 10–12 = overcropped.
- Visual: shoots that stop growing near veraison, mid-canopy shade levels low, lateral
  growth modest.
- Dense canopies raise *Botrytis* and powdery mildew pressure — canopy work is also
  disease control.

## 4. Sampling toward harvest
From veraison (berries soften and colour), sample weekly, then every 2–3 days near target:
- **Berry sample:** ~100–200 berries per block, taken randomly from both sides of the row,
  from top/middle/bottom of clusters, from many vines (skip edge rows and odd vines).
- Crush, strain, measure:
  - **Sugar (°Brix)** with a refractometer (temperature-compensated). Many dry table wines
    ~22–25 °Brix; sparkling base much lower (~17–20).
  - **pH** with a calibrated meter — reds often ~3.4–3.7, whites ~3.1–3.4 typical targets.
  - **Titratable acidity** (g/L) by titration with NaOH to pH 8.2.
- Also taste: seeds brown and crunchy, skins breaking down, flavour past green/vegetal.
- Watch the weather: rain before harvest dilutes sugar and invites rot; heat spikes raise
  pH quickly.

## 5. Harvest call
Pick when numbers and taste meet the winemaker's targets, or earlier if disease or weather
forces it. Harvest in the cool morning; get fruit to the winery quickly.

## Gotchas
- Leaf removal on the hot afternoon side after veraison sunburns clusters.
- Small, biased samples (only nice-looking clusters) overstate ripeness by 1–2 °Brix.
- Brix can rise from dehydration (berry shrivel) without real ripening.

## Verify
- [ ] Bud and shoot counts per metre recorded against targets.
- [ ] Fruit zone open with ~1–2 leaf layers; clusters not sunburnt.
- [ ] Ravaz index computed from yield and pruning weights.
- [ ] Weekly random berry samples logged (Brix, pH, TA) with a calibrated meter.
- [ ] Harvest decision recorded with the numbers and tasting notes.
