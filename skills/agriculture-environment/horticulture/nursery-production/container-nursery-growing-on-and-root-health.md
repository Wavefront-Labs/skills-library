# Container nursery growing-on and root health

**Goal:** a batch of container plants that hit saleable size on schedule with a full,
healthy, non-circling root ball — what buyers actually check when they knock a plant out.

> Draft. Rates and EC bands are general guidance for woody ornamentals in bark/peat-free
> media; follow substrate supplier and CRF manufacturer specs and local guidance.

## 1. Potting on
- Pot liners/plugs when roots have **just** filled the cell (white root tips visible at
  the edge but not a dense mat). Too early → loose root ball; too late → circling roots
  that persist in the larger pot.
- Move up a sensible step (e.g. 9 cm → 2 L → 5–10 L); a huge jump leaves wet, unrooted media.
- Tease or score circling roots on pot-bound liners before potting.
- Plant at the same depth; firm lightly; water in thoroughly.
- **Air-pruning pots** or copper-coated pots reduce circling on trees and fast rooters.

## 2. Substrate and nutrition
- Bark-based or peat-reduced/peat-free mixes: aim for air-filled porosity ~15–25% and
  good water-holding; consistency batch to batch matters more than the "perfect" recipe.
- **Controlled-release fertiliser (CRF)** incorporated at potting: choose longevity to
  match the crop cycle (e.g. 5–6, 8–9, 12–14 months at a stated temperature — longevity
  shortens in warm conditions). Typical rates ~2–5 kg/m³ depending on product and crop.
- Peat-free media often need more nitrogen early (wood fibre ties some up) and more
  frequent, lighter irrigation.

## 3. Monitoring substrate EC and pH — PourThru
1. Irrigate normally; wait ~30–60 min.
2. Set the pot on a saucer and pour a fixed volume of distilled water on top (e.g. ~75 ml for
   a 1 L pot, ~150–250 ml for larger pots) to collect ~50 ml leachate.
3. Measure EC and pH of leachate. Common woody-nursery bands: EC ~0.5–1.5 mS/cm under
   CRF; pH ~5.5–6.5. Track trends on 5–10 marked pots per batch fortnightly.
Low EC + pale growth → CRF exhausted or leaching too much; high EC + root tip browning →
salt build-up, irrigate to leach.

## 4. Irrigation
- Group plants by water need and pot size. Overhead for small, dense pots; drip/spray
  stakes for 10 L+.
- Schedule by weight or a moisture sensor, not the clock: water when pots drop to a set
  weight; aim for ~10–20% leaching fraction.
- Capture and treat recycled water (disease risk — *Phytophthora*, *Pythium*).

## 5. Spacing and finishing
- Space as canopies touch ("pot-thick" → spaced) to keep plants bushy and air moving.
- Trim/pinch for branching (2–3 pinches on shrubby lines), stake and tie trees.
- Finish: weeds, liverwort and moss off the surface; clean pots; correct label; plants
  hardened off if they were under cover.

## 6. Root health and hygiene
- Knock out sample plants monthly: white, well-distributed roots to the base = good;
  brown, soft roots, especially at the bottom = waterlogging or root rot (*Phytophthora*).
- Stand pots on free-draining gravel or fabric, never in puddles.
- **Vine weevil:** notched leaf edges, C-shaped larvae in roots — apply entomopathogenic
  nematodes when media is ≥ ~5–12 °C (product-specific) in late summer/autumn, or approved controls.
- Disinfect benches and re-used pots; quarantine bought-in liners.

## Gotchas
- CRF longevity is temperature-driven: a hot summer releases it fast, and plants starve in autumn.
- Liverwort and moss signal over-wet surfaces; adjust irrigation before reaching for chemicals.
- Overwintering pots: roots are killed at temperatures the tops survive — group and protect.

## Verify
- [ ] Liners potted when roots just filled the cell; no circling roots carried over.
- [ ] CRF type/rate and potting date logged per batch.
- [ ] PourThru EC/pH tracked fortnightly within band.
- [ ] Irrigation by weight/sensor with measurable leaching.
- [ ] Sample root balls white and full; batch meets the sale spec (height, spread, shoots, pot clean).
