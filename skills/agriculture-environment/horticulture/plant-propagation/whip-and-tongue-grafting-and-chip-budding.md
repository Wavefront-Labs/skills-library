# Whip-and-tongue grafting and chip budding

**Goal:** a good take rate when joining a chosen variety (scion) onto a rootstock, with
two techniques that cover most fruit-tree work.

> Timings are for temperate northern-hemisphere fruit. Patented varieties and
> rootstocks may not legally be propagated without a licence.

## 1. Material
- **Rootstock and scion of the same diameter** so the cambium layers align; Iowa State
  suggests 1/4 to 1/2 inch.
- **Scionwood** (winter grafting): previous year's growth, collected fully dormant
  (Iowa State: February or early March; Missouri: February is ideal). Typically 1/4 to
  1/2 inch thick and 12 to 18 inches long. Seal in polyethylene bags to stop moisture
  loss and store at 32 F (0 C) until grafting. A home freezer (usually 0 F) can injure
  the buds.
  Choose rootstock and variety to suit the site; rootstock names are not covered here.
- **Budwood** (summer budding): non-flowering, well-ripened current-season shoots about
  the same diameter as the rootstock; strip the soft tip and leaves, leaving 3-4 mm
  leaf-stalk stubs (RHS).
- Tools: a sharp knife, grafting tape or polythene strips, grafting or pruning compound.

## 2. Whip-and-tongue (late winter, stock and scion dormant)
1. Make a smooth diagonal cut on the stock 1 to 2 inches long.
2. Prepare the scion the same way, using the middle portion of the scionwood.
3. Second cut: start about one-third of the way down from the pointed end, 1/2 to 1 inch
   long, slanted toward the base of the first cut, on both stock and scion.
4. Join the tongues so the cambium layers match, then wrap firmly with grafting tape.
5. Cut the scion 3 to 4 inches above the graft, leaving 2 or 3 buds.
6. Cover the union and the scion's cut end with grafting or pruning compound.

## 3. Chip budding (mid-summer to early autumn, RHS)
1. Clear shoots and leaves from the lowest 30 cm of the stock; make the cuts about
   15 cm from the ground.
2. Cut a chip from the budstick: first cut about 2 cm below the bud, blade about 5 mm
   deep at 30 degrees; second cut about 4 cm above the first, down through the wood to
   meet it.
3. Cut a matching notch in the rootstock, remove the sliver, seat the chip and align
   the cambium layers.
4. Bind firmly with grafting tape or polythene strips, leaving the bud and leaf stalk
   exposed.
5. Remove the tape once the bud starts to swell; swelling shows the bud and cambium
   have united.
6. The following spring cut back the stock just above the bud, tie the new shoot to a
   cane, and plant out the following winter.

## Gotchas
- Inaccurate cuts are the usual cause of failure; practise on spare shoots.
- Keep a record of take per variety and rootstock.

## Verify
- [ ] Scionwood dormant, labelled, sealed and stored cold (budwood used fresh).
- [ ] Cuts smooth and matched; cambium aligned.
- [ ] Union fully wrapped.
- [ ] Buds breaking (spring) or budded section swelling (summer).
