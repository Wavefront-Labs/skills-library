# Harvest maturity, cooling and storage

**Goal:** produce that reaches the buyer with the quality it had in the field. Shelf life
lost to slow cooling or wrong storage can't be recovered.

> Storage needs vary by crop and variety: use a crop-specific postharvest fact sheet for
> temperature and humidity. Food-safety rules
> (wash-water sanitiser levels, records) are jurisdiction-specific.

## 1. Harvest timing
- Pick high-transpiration produce early in the morning, when it is coolest and fully
  hydrated; start early to avoid temperatures above 70 to 75 F (Penn State).
- Use a maturity index for each crop, not just the calendar.

## 2. Remove field heat fast
A one-hour delay in cooling reduces produce shelf life by one day or more. A six-hour delay
in strawberry cooling can give 50 percent more water loss, and a four-hour delay in
asparagus cooling 50 percent tougher spears (Penn State).
| Method | Notes (Penn State) |
|---|---|
| **Room cooling** | Suits small and medium farms where storage and cooling share one space; slow, 24 hours to several days |
| **Forced-air cooling** | Pulls cold air through packages; usually 75 to 90 percent faster than room cooling; package vents about 5 percent of side area |
| **Hydrocooling** | Cold water removes heat about five times faster than air; unsuitable for berries, potatoes to be stored, sweet potatoes, bulb onions and garlic; use a labelled disinfectant, and chlorinate or otherwise sanitise recirculated water |
## 3. Store and avoid chilling injury
- Look up temperature and humidity for each crop; weight loss rises sharply as humidity
  drops (Penn State table: Brussels sprouts lose 1.6% per day at 95% RH and 6.4% at 80% RH at 32 F).
- Chilling injury examples (Penn State): beans darken below 41 F; tomatoes have poor flavour
  after 4 to 6 days below 50 F; cantaloupe is injured only if held below 36 F for extended periods.

## 4. Ethylene
Apples release high ethylene; broccoli (yellowing) and lettuce (russet spotting) are
sensitive. Damage usually takes more than 24 hours of exposure and is cumulative (Penn State).
Store producers apart from sensitive crops.

## Gotchas
- Wet produce into a cold room without airflow invites rot; dry or ventilate after hydrocooling.
- Mixed-load coolers compromise some crops; check each crop's range.
- Dirty wash water spreads pathogens across a batch; follow your food-safety rules.

## Verify
- [ ] Maturity index measured and recorded for each harvest lot.
- [ ] Time from harvest to cooled recorded.
- [ ] Each crop stored per a crop-specific fact sheet; humidity checked as well as temperature.
- [ ] Ethylene producers separated from sensitive crops.
