# Oyster mushroom substrate and fruiting

**Goal:** bags that colonise fully white in 2–3 weeks and give 2–3 flushes with good
biological efficiency — without green mould or bacterial blotch. Oyster (*Pleurotus*) is
the forgiving starting species; the same steps scale to others with tighter conditions.

> Draft. Temperatures and rates vary by strain (e.g. blue/grey vs pink vs king oyster);
> follow the spawn supplier's strain sheet. Sawdust "fruiting blocks" for shiitake/lion's
> mane need full sterilisation (pressure cooker/autoclave) and a clean flow hood — not covered.

## 1. Substrate
- **Wheat/barley straw** chopped to ~2–8 cm. (Alternatives: pasteurised hardwood pellets
  rehydrated, or coffee grounds mixed — the latter contaminates easily.)
- Target moisture after treatment: ~65–70% — a squeezed handful releases only a few drops.

## 2. Pasteurise (reduce competitors, don't sterilise)
Choose one:
- **Hot water:** submerge straw in water at **65–80 °C for 1–2 hours** (hold ~70 °C
  ideally), drain and cool to < ~27 °C.
- **Cold-water lime (high pH):** soak 12–24 h in water with hydrated lime (commonly
  ~2–5 g/L, aiming pH ~12+); drain. Low-energy; follow lime safety (eyes, skin).
Drain on a clean surface until at field capacity — waterlogged straw goes anaerobic and bacterial.

## 3. Spawning
- Use fresh **grain spawn** (fully white, sweet-smelling, no green/black spots or sour smell).
- **Spawn rate** ~5–10% of wet substrate weight (higher = faster colonisation, fewer contaminants).
- Clean hands/gloves, wipe surfaces with 70% alcohol. Mix spawn evenly through cooled straw;
  pack into clear or black polypropylene/poly bags (or buckets with holes), firmly but not
  crushed. Make small holes or Xs (~1 cm) every ~10–15 cm for gas exchange.

## 4. Spawn run (colonisation)
- Keep at ~20–24 °C (strain dependent), dark or dim. Bags self-heat — space them;
  a core temperature > ~30 °C invites contamination.
- 10–21 days to fully white. Small patches of other colours: green = *Trichoderma* or
  *Penicillium* (discard if spreading), orange/pink = possibly *Neurospora* (very
  contagious — remove bag immediately), yellowish wet slime/sour smell = bacteria (too wet).

## 5. Fruiting
Triggers: fresh air (lower CO2), light, humidity, and for some strains a temperature drop.
- **Humidity:** ~85–95% RH. Use a fruiting chamber (shotgun tent with perlite, or a
  humidifier-driven tent/room).
- **Fresh air exchange:** high — oysters are CO2-sensitive. Long stems and small caps
  ("trumpet" or coral-like) = too much CO2; increase airflow.
- **Light:** indirect light ~12 h/day (enough to read by).
- **Temperature:** ~13–24 °C depending on strain; mist the air/walls, not the pins directly
  once they form (wet caps → bacterial blotch).
- Cut larger openings in the bag where you want fruit.

## 6. Harvest and flushes
- Harvest when caps are flat to slightly upturned at the edge, **before** they flatten fully
  and drop spores (spore load is also an allergen indoors — ventilate).
- Twist/cut the whole cluster at the base. Rest the bag 7–14 days, keep humid; 2–3 flushes
  are typical, each smaller.
- **Biological efficiency (BE)** = fresh mushroom weight ÷ dry substrate weight × 100%.
  50–100%+ is achievable for oysters on straw.

## Gotchas
- Overly wet straw is the most common cause of bacterial failure.
- Fruiting rooms without airflow give long-stemmed, deformed mushrooms.
- Contaminated bags should leave the grow room sealed — opening them spreads spores.

## Verify
- [ ] Straw pasteurised at the recorded temperature/time or lime pH, drained to squeeze-test moisture.
- [ ] Spawn fresh and added at 5–10%; bags dated.
- [ ] Bags fully white in ≤ 3 weeks; contaminated bags removed.
- [ ] Fruiting conditions logged: RH, temperature, airflow, light.
- [ ] Yield per bag and BE recorded per flush.
