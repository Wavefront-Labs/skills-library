# Temperament testing and playgroup management

**Goal:** a room of dogs that play well, rest well and go home tired — not sore. The
job is mostly prevention: the right dogs together, attention on the floor, and
interrupting arousal before it becomes a scrap.

## 1. Temperament test (new dogs)
Prerequisites: vaccinations checked, spayed/neutered status recorded (many daycares
require neutering after a set age — policy, not welfare science), owner questionnaire
on dog history.
Staged, stop at the first fail:
1. **Handling** in a quiet room: touch collar, ears, paws, tail; lift a paw. Note
   flinching, freezing, mouthing.
2. **Through a barrier** with one calm, neutral "tester" dog: loose and curious is
   good; fixating stare, hard barking, hackles and stiffness are not.
3. **On-leash greet** with the tester (loose leads), 3–5 s, call apart.
4. **Off-leash with one tester**, then a small calm group of 3–4 for 15–30 min.
5. Assign a play category (e.g. small/gentle, mid-energy, rough-and-tumble) and note
   triggers. A dog can pass yet still be "not ready" — offer a trial half-day.
Daycare is not for every dog: dogs that don't enjoy other dogs, or are overwhelmed by
groups, should be advised toward walks or sitting instead. That's a welfare call, not a sales loss.

## 2. Composing groups
Group by **play style and size**, then energy, not breed. Keep:
- Small and large separate unless all are proven gentle together.
- Puppies with tolerant, appropriate adults, not with each other in a mob.
- Seniors and anxious dogs in a calm group.
Staff ratio: common industry guidance is roughly 1 staff to 10–15 dogs; local
licensing may set lower. Be wary of anything above that. Fewer for rough groups.
No toys or food in the group room unless every dog is proven non-guarding.

## 3. Reading play
**Healthy play** looks inefficient: loose, bouncy, curved bodies; play bows; open
"play face"; role reversal (chaser becomes chased, top dog goes on bottom);
self-handicapping (big dog lies down); natural pauses every few seconds.

**Warning signs:** one dog always chasing, the other always fleeing or hiding; pinning
that doesn't release; ganging up (several dogs on one); stiffening, hard stare, lip
lift, freeze; vocals rising in pitch and intensity; humping fixation; tail tucked
while still being pursued; play that never pauses.

**Consent test:** when unsure, gently separate the two. If the "victim" goes back to
the other dog, it was mutual; if it walks away or hides behind you, it wasn't — redirect
the pursuer.

## 4. Interrupt early
- Ladder: body block / walk between → call names and reward the check-in → leash
  and short time-out (1–2 min) in a calm area → move the dog to another group.
- Interrupt *every* 1–2 min in very high-arousal groups even if nothing is wrong —
  regular pauses keep arousal down.
- Count dogs and scan the room continuously; no phones on the floor.

## 5. Rest rotations
Dogs need far more rest than owners think. Many daycares now run structured nap
times (e.g. 1–2 h midday in individual crates/pens) and rotate dogs out of play.
Over-tired dogs are behind most afternoon incidents.

## 6. When a fight happens
- Stay calm and loud: a sharp clap or noise, water spray, or a board between dogs.
- Never grab near heads or collars of fighting dogs.
- Wheelbarrow: two staff each lift the hind legs of one dog and back away in an arc.
- Separate fully, check both dogs for punctures (fur hides them — feel for wet spots),
  and inform both owners the same day. Incident log with what preceded it.
- Review: was it the mix, arousal level, a resource, fatigue? Change the cause.

## 7. Health watch
Kennel cough spreads easily in groups. Send home coughing dogs; keep water bowls
cleaned daily; ventilate; keep the vaccination checks current.

## Verify
- [ ] Every dog has a test record and play category before joining a group.
- [ ] Groups split by size/style, ratio within your limit.
- [ ] Staff can name three signs of healthy and three of escalating play.
- [ ] Scheduled rest periods in the day.
- [ ] Fight protocol known; incidents logged with antecedents and both owners told.
