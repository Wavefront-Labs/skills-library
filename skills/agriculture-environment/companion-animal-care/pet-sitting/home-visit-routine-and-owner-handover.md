# Home visit routine and owner handover

**Goal:** the owner returns to a healthy pet, a secure house and a written record
of every visit — and anything that went wrong was escalated early, by the plan
you agreed before they left.

## 1. Meet-and-greet (before the first booking)
Visit while the owner is home and fill a **care sheet** together. Minimum fields:
- Each pet: name, photo, species/breed, age, microchip number, normal behaviour,
  hiding places, fears, escape tendencies, which doors/windows stay shut.
- Food: exact brand, amount per meal (in cups or grams — watch them measure it), times,
  treats allowed, what's off-limits. Where the spare supply is.
- **Medication:** drug name, dose, route, time, how the owner gives it (pill pocket,
  in food), what to do if a dose is refused or missed. Get the owner to demonstrate.
- Vet name, number, out-of-hours clinic, and **written authorisation for treatment
  with a spending limit** — many clinics won't act without it. Leave a copy with the
  vet if possible.
- Emergency contact who can make decisions if the owner is unreachable.
- House: alarm code, bins, plants to water, mail, lights, heating, wifi, Wi-Fi cameras
  (ask if there are any — and assume there might be).
Test the key in the lock and the alarm code while the owner is still there.

## 2. The visit routine (same order every time)
1. Enter, close the door behind you **before** greeting pets — door-dashing cats are
   the classic loss. Disarm alarm.
2. Headcount: **see every animal**. Cats that hide must be physically located, not
   assumed. A missing cat on visit one is far easier to find than on visit four.
3. Check the pet: eating? drinking? eyes, breathing, gait normal? Vomit or diarrhoea
   anywhere? Compare to the care sheet's "normal".
4. Feed exactly as written; refresh water; note what was left from last time
   (appetite trend is your early-warning system).
5. Medication by instruction only. Record time and dose given. Never substitute,
   double up for a missed dose, or give human medicines — call the vet.
6. Litter/toilet: scoop and **look** (no urine for 24 h in a male cat can be an
   emergency — urinary blockage — and needs a vet the same day).
7. Exercise/play per the sheet; dogs: toilet trip at minimum.
8. House round: windows, taps, heating, mail in, lights on rotation, curtains as agreed.
9. Recount pets, lock up, re-arm, **check the door is locked from outside**.

Typical visit cadence for cats is once or twice daily; dogs usually need several
visits or an overnight sitter. Diabetic pets on insulin need visits pinned to dosing
times — only accept if you're competent and the owner has trained you.

## 3. Owner updates
Send a short message each visit: photo, ate/drank/toileted, meds given, anything odd.
Keep the photo free of house numbers and keys if the owner shares it publicly.
Don't post clients' homes or addresses on your own social media.

## 4. Keys and security
- Code your key tags (client number, not name or address).
- Never leave keys under mats or in your car overnight on view.
- Don't announce on social media that a client is away.
- Handback: keys returned in person or to a lockbox the owner sets; written receipt
  if you hold keys long-term.

## 5. When it goes wrong
- **Sick or injured pet:** call the vet first, then the owner. Take the care sheet
  and the authorisation to the clinic.
- **Missing pet:** search the house thoroughly (cupboards, appliances, under beds),
  then the immediate area; tell the owner straight away; report the microchip number
  to the chip database and local vets/shelters. Put the cat's litter tray outside only
  if the owner agrees (practice varies).
- **House problem** (leak, break-in): safety first, then owner and police/plumber per
  the owner's instructions. Photograph everything.
- Keep the visit log; it is your record if anything is disputed. Insurance for pet
  sitters (public liability, care-custody-control) is strongly advised.

## Verify
- [ ] Care sheet complete, including meds demonstrated and vet authorisation signed.
- [ ] Every animal seen in person every visit.
- [ ] Food/water/litter intake recorded so trends are visible.
- [ ] Doors confirmed locked from outside; keys untagged by address.
- [ ] Owner got an update after each visit and knows the emergency plan.
