# Kennel and cattery intake, hygiene and illness watch

**Goal:** boarders go home the condition they arrived in. The two things that end a
kennel's reputation are an outbreak (kennel cough, parvo, cat flu) and an animal that
stopped eating unnoticed. Both are managed by routine.

## 1. Intake
- **Vaccination record checked at the door**, not taken on trust. Typical requirements
  (confirm with your licensing authority and vet): dogs — core vaccines
  (distemper/parvo/hepatitis), leptospirosis, often kennel cough (intranasal often
  required a set number of days before boarding); cats — feline enteritis and cat flu.
  No record, no admission — no exceptions for "regulars".
- Health check with the owner present: eyes, nose, coughing, skin, fleas, weight
  (weigh on arrival — your baseline for weight loss), stool. Refuse or isolate anything
  infectious-looking.
- Collect: diet and amounts, meds with written instructions, vet and emergency
  contacts, behaviour (with other dogs, handling, escape history), signed consent for
  vet treatment.
- Label everything brought in (bed, food, meds) with the animal's name and pen number.

## 2. Housing and the cleaning protocol
Order every day: **healthy young → healthy adult → elderly → isolation last**, with
separate apron/gloves/footbath for isolation. Never go back from isolation to clean pens.

Cleaning a pen (occupied: spot-clean daily, full clean between boarders):
1. Remove animal, bedding, bowls, toys.
2. **Remove organic matter first** (faeces, hair, food) — disinfectants are inactivated
   by dirt.
3. Wash with detergent, rinse.
4. Apply disinfectant at the label dilution and **leave the full contact time**,
   typically 5–10 minutes. Wiping it straight off is the commonest failure.
5. Rinse if the label says so; dry before the animal goes back (wet floors = slips,
   chilled cats).

Disinfectant notes: parvovirus and feline panleukopenia are non-enveloped viruses
and resist many quaternary ammonium products. Products commonly used against them
include accelerated hydrogen peroxide, potassium peroxymonosulfate and
diluted bleach (often quoted ~1:32 of household bleach, made fresh daily). Check the
product's label claims for the specific pathogen — don't take this list as the claim.
Never mix bleach with other chemicals.

## 3. Stress reduction
- Dogs: visual barriers between facing pens reduce barking cycles; quiet hours; same
  handler where possible; owner's unwashed item in the bed.
- Cats: cat-only building or block out of sight/sound of dogs; a hide box or shelf in
  every pen; don't face pens at each other; minimal handling for the first day.
  Pheromone diffusers are widely used; evidence for them is mixed.
- Keep routine identical day to day: feeding, cleaning, exercise times.

## 4. Exercise rotation
- Each dog out at least on the schedule your licence and the booking state (often
  2+ sessions/day). Keep a rota sheet; tick each session so no dog is missed.
- Only mix dogs from the same household or with written owner consent and an assessed
  temperament; most kennels exercise individually.
- Check gates double-shut (airlock) before opening a pen door.

## 5. Daily illness watch (record it)
Per animal, every day: eaten (all/part/none), drunk, urine, stool quality, vomit,
cough/sneeze, demeanour. Triggers to call the vet (adapt with your vet):
- Dog not eating 24 h, or a cat not eating ~24–48 h (cats risk hepatic lipidosis);
- Persistent vomiting/diarrhoea, blood in either;
- Honking cough + lethargy; eye/nose discharge in cats;
- Male cat straining without passing urine — same-day emergency;
- Bloat signs in deep-chested dogs (retching without producing, swollen belly) — emergency.
Move suspect cases to isolation, tell the owner, and log it.

## 6. Paperwork
Licensing rules (e.g. England's animal activity licensing) specify records, space,
staff ratios and fire plans. Keep: occupancy register, vaccine copies, daily sheets,
medication log, incidents. Check your local regime — requirements differ by country.

## Verify
- [ ] No boarder admitted without a checked vaccine record.
- [ ] Weight recorded on arrival and departure.
- [ ] Disinfectant used at label dilution with the full contact time, organic matter removed first.
- [ ] Isolation cleaned last with separate PPE.
- [ ] Daily eat/drink/toilet sheet filled for every animal; vet triggers known by all staff.
