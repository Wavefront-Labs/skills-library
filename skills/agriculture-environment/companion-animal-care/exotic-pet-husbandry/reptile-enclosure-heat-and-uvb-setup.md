# Reptile enclosure: heat gradient, UVB and humidity

**Goal:** an enclosure where the animal can choose its own temperature and UV exposure,
with every heat source thermostatically controlled. Most captive-reptile illness
(metabolic bone disease, respiratory infections, burns, poor appetite) traces back to
heat, UVB or humidity being wrong.

## 1. Start from the species, not the shop kit
For your species, look up (from a reputable care sheet, specialist vet or keeper
society — care sheets disagree, check two): basking surface temperature, cool-end
temperature, night drop, humidity range, and **UV (Ferguson) zone**. Examples
(commonly cited ranges — verify for your animal):
- Bearded dragon: basking surface ~40–45 °C, cool end mid-20s °C, Ferguson zone 3–4
  (UVI ~3–6 at basking spot), low humidity.
- Leopard gecko: warm-side surface ~30–35 °C, cool mid-20s; crepuscular, low-level UVB
  (zone 1) is now widely recommended though historically omitted.
- Corn snake / ball python: warm side ~30–32 °C (ball python basking a bit higher),
  cool mid-20s; ball pythons need higher humidity (~60–80%).
Enclosure size matters: a gradient needs length. Small tanks heat through evenly and
the animal can't cool down.

## 2. Heat
- Prefer **overhead heat** for basking species (halogen flood or deep heat projector by
  day; ceramic heat emitter or nothing at night if room temperature allows the night drop).
- **Every heat source on a thermostat** — dimming for lamps, on/off or pulse for
  ceramics and mats. Heat mats without a thermostat cause burns.
- Guard any lamp/ceramic the animal can reach (mesh cage); snakes coil around bulbs.
- Probe placement: put the thermostat probe where it reads what you want to control
  (e.g. at the basking zone), secured so the animal can't move it.

## 3. Measure, don't trust the dial
- **Infrared temp gun** for surface temperatures (basking rock, cool floor).
- Digital probe thermometers at warm and cool ends for air temperature.
- Hygrometer (digital; dial ones are often far off).
- Measure at the times that matter: basking spot 1–2 h after lights on, coolest point
  at night. Adjust lamp height or wattage before relying on the thermostat to throttle.

## 4. UVB
- Use a linear **T5 HO** tube (or a mercury vapour lamp for large enclosures) sized to
  ~2/3 of enclosure length, mounted on the basking side, with a reflector.
- Distance sets dose: follow the manufacturer's distance chart for the target UVI, and
  remember **mesh tops block roughly a third to half** of UVB.
- Ideally verify with a **UV index meter** (e.g. Solarmeter 6.5): target the species'
  zone UVI at the basking spot, falling to near zero in shaded retreats.
- **Glass and most plastics block UVB** — no lamps behind glass, no window sunlight
  counted as UVB.
- Replace tubes on the manufacturer's schedule (commonly ~12 months for T5) — output
  decays before the visible light does. A meter tells you exactly when.
- Photoperiod via timer: ~12 h typical; some keepers vary seasonally.

## 5. Humidity and substrate
- Raise humidity with larger water bowls, damp moss hides, misting, bioactive soil;
  lower it with ventilation and dry substrate.
- Every animal gets **a hide at each end** (warm and cool) plus a humid hide for
  shedding species.
- Avoid loose substrates linked with impaction for young or sick animals (debated for
  healthy adults); avoid cedar/pine shavings for reptiles.

## 6. Diet and supplements (brief)
Insectivores: gut-load feeders and dust with calcium; vitamin D3 and multivitamin
dosing depends on whether UVB is supplied — follow the vet/care-sheet schedule
rather than dusting everything with everything.

## 7. Handling and quarantine
Let new animals settle 1–2 weeks before handling; support the whole body, never by the
tail (many lizards drop tails). Quarantine new reptiles separately (often 60–90 days
for snakes, because of mites and serious viral diseases). Wash hands — reptiles
commonly carry Salmonella.

## Verify
- [ ] Basking and cool-end temperatures measured with a temp gun and probe, within the species range.
- [ ] Every heat source on a working thermostat; lamps guarded.
- [ ] UVB not behind glass; UVI at basking spot checked or set from the maker's chart (mesh accounted for).
- [ ] Hides at both ends; humidity within range on a digital hygrometer.
- [ ] Replacement date for the UVB lamp written on it.
