# Dog full groom — the order of work that avoids rework and injuries

**Goal:** take a dog from drop-off to finished trim in one predictable sequence,
deciding early whether the coat can be saved, and finishing without clipper burn,
nicks or a quicked nail.

## 1. Intake check (before any water)
- Hands over the whole body: lumps, scabs, hot spots, fleas, ear smell, sore joints.
  Note anything pre-existing on the card **and tell the owner at drop-off** — a
  lump found after the groom looks like groomer damage.
- Feel to the skin with a metal comb in the armpits, behind ears, groin, tail base and
  collar line. If the comb won't pass at the skin, it's matted.
- Agree the style and length in writing (blade number or comb length). "Short" means
  different things to different owners; show a photo or a comb.

## 2. Save it or shave it
Matted coat pelted to the skin cannot be humanely brushed out. Rule of thumb used in
salons: if de-matting would take longer than ~10–15 minutes of brushing per area or
the dog is objecting, shave under the mats with a short blade (#7F or #10) and rebook.
Get owner consent first — many salons use a signed matting release. Warn that
shaved, previously matted skin is often red or itchy for a few days.

**Double-coated breeds** (husky, malamute, collies, many shepherds, Pomeranian): do
not shave as a cosmetic choice; coat may grow back patchy or slowly (practitioner
consensus; the evidence base is mostly anecdotal). De-shed with an undercoat rake and
high-velocity dryer instead.

## 3. Bath
1. Brush out loose coat first if it's not matted — water tightens mats.
2. Lukewarm water, test on your wrist. Wet to the skin; double coats take minutes.
3. Shampoo twice on dirty dogs: first wash lifts dirt, second actually cleans. Dilute
   concentrates to the label ratio (commonly 1:8 to 1:32 — check the bottle).
4. Express anal glands only if trained and the salon offers it; many groomers leave
   it to the vet.
5. Rinse until the water runs clear and the coat squeaks — residue causes itching.
6. Conditioner on long coats, then towel off.

## 4. Dry
- High-velocity (HV) dryer for blowing water and undercoat out; keep the nozzle a
  hand's width away and moving; use a cone/low setting on the face and never blow
  into ears or eyes. Cotton in ears is an option for noise-sensitive dogs.
- Fluff/stand dryer and brush for curly coats (poodle, doodle) — dry straight for a
  plush finish. A curly coat dried in its curl cannot be scissored evenly.
- **Cage dryers:** never heated with brachycephalic (pug, bulldog), elderly or anxious
  dogs, and never unattended. Dogs have died in heated cage dryers.
- The coat must be bone dry to the skin before clipping; damp hair drags and gives
  an uneven line.

## 5. Clip and scissor
- **Blade numbers are backwards:** higher number = shorter. Approximate cut lengths
  (vary by brand): #4F ~9.5 mm, #5F ~6 mm, #7F ~3 mm, #10 ~1.5 mm, #15 ~1.2 mm,
  #30/#40 ~0.5–0.25 mm. "F" = finish/full-tooth, smoother on the body.
- Snap-on (attachment) combs go over a #30 or #40 blade for longer lengths.
- Clip **with** the growth for a longer, smoother finish; against it cuts shorter.
- Sanitary trim and pads with a #10 or #15 (pads: skim, don't dig between toes).
- **Check blade heat every few minutes** on the back of your wrist. Swap blades or
  spray coolant; a hot blade burns skin, especially on the belly and inner thigh.
- Keep the blade flat on the skin; tension loose skin (armpits, flank, ear leather)
  with your free hand. Nicks happen at skin folds and ear edges — scissor ear edges
  with your fingers guarding the leather.

## 6. Nails, ears, finish
- Nails: trim tiny slices; on white nails stop before the pink quick, on black nails
  stop when the cut face shows a grey/dark centre dot. Keep styptic powder at hand.
  File or grind sharp edges. Don't forget dewclaws.
- Ears: wipe visible ear with a dog ear cleaner on cotton; never probe the canal.
  Plucking ear hair is debated — many vets now advise against routine plucking.
  Report smell, discharge or redness to the owner (vet visit), don't treat.
- Finish: scissor feet round, tidy eyes with blunt-tip scissors pointing away from
  the eye, bandana/bow, final photo for the card.

## Verify
- [ ] Pre-existing lumps, wounds and parasites noted on the card and told to owner.
- [ ] Matting decision made before the bath, with owner consent for a shave-down.
- [ ] Coat dry to the skin before clipping; no heated cage drying of at-risk dogs.
- [ ] Blade temperature checked through the clip; no red patches on belly/thighs.
- [ ] No nail bleeding left unchecked; dewclaws done.
- [ ] Card updated with blade/comb numbers so the next groom matches.
