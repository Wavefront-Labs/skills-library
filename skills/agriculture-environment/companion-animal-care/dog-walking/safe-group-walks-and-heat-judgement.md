# Safe group walks and weather judgement

**Goal:** every dog comes back on time, uninjured, and still wearing what it left in.
Most walker disasters are escapes at the door or car, heat collapse, and fights in
badly mixed groups — all preventable with routine.

## 1. Before taking a dog on
- Meet-and-greet with the owner: recall reliability, reactivity to dogs/people/bikes,
  prey drive, medical issues, vet details, written consent for off-lead (or not).
- Check local rules: many councils cap dogs per walker (in parts of the UK commonly
  4–6) and require a licence or insurance for commercial walkers. Rules vary — check
  your area, don't assume.
- **Group size:** no more than you can physically hold on lead at once and see at
  all times off-lead. Four is a common practical ceiling; new walkers start with two.

## 2. Kit check at every pickup
- Collar or harness fitted: two fingers under the strap, not slipping over the head.
  Escape artists (sighthounds, nervous rescues) go in a martingale or escape-proof
  harness, ideally double-clipped to collar and harness.
- ID tag on; your own spare slip lead in your pocket.
- Water + bowl, poo bags, treats, phone charged, owners' emergency numbers and vet
  saved offline.
- **Door and car are the danger zones.** Lead on *before* the door opens; in the
  vehicle use crates or seatbelt tethers so a dog can't jump out when you open up.

## 3. Introducing and building a group
- Introduce new dogs one-to-one, on neutral ground, walking parallel before nose-to-
  nose. Keep leads loose (tight leads transmit tension) and let the greeting be 3–5
  seconds, then call apart.
- Add dogs to a group one at a time over several walks. Mix by size and play style,
  not just friendliness; a bulldozer player will bully a timid small dog.
- Watch for stiffening, hard stare, raised hackles, freezing over a stick or ball.
  Remove toys from groups where any dog guards.

## 4. Lead handling
- Hold leads in your non-dominant hand with the loops over the wrist plus a closed
  fist; keep your dominant hand free. Don't wrap leads around fingers — a lunging dog
  can break them.
- A walk belt or coupler helps for many dogs but you lose quick control of one dog;
  unclip reactive dogs from couplers.
- Retractable leads are a poor tool in groups (tangles, burns, no control); avoid.

## 5. Off-lead rules
Only with written owner consent, proven recall in that kind of place, away from roads
and livestock. Leash before gates, car parks and other dogs on-lead (an on-lead dog
may be on-lead because it's reactive). Around livestock keep all dogs on lead —
farmers may lawfully act against dogs worrying stock in many jurisdictions.

## 6. Heat and weather
- **Pavement test:** back of your hand on the tarmac for 7 seconds; too hot to hold,
  too hot for paws. Tarmac can be far hotter than air on a sunny day.
- Many walkers shorten or cancel walks around 25 °C+ and walk early/late; at high
  heat and humidity go to brief toilet trips only. Higher-risk dogs: brachycephalic
  breeds, overweight, elderly, thick-coated, dark-coated, heart/airway issues.
- **Heatstroke signs:** heavy panting that doesn't settle, thick drool, bright red or
  pale gums, wobbling, vomiting, collapse. Action: shade, cool (not iced) water poured
  over the body, airflow, and call a vet while cooling — then transport.
- Cold/wet: towel dry before return, coats for thin-coated breeds, check paws for
  ice balls and road salt; rinse salt off.
- Other hazards to know locally: blue-green algae in water bodies, grass seeds/awns,
  toxic plants, ticks, processionary caterpillars, fireworks season.

## 7. Incidents
- **Escape:** don't chase (it becomes a game); stop, crouch, call happily, show treats
  or run away from the dog. Call the owner early if it doesn't come back within minutes.
- **Fight:** don't put hands near heads. Separate by lifting the back legs of the
  aggressor (two people, one per dog) or putting a barrier between; then check both
  dogs and tell both owners, and get a vet check for any puncture.
- Log every incident and near-miss with time and place.

## Verify
- [ ] Every dog has a fitted collar/harness, ID tag and owner consent for off-lead status.
- [ ] Leads go on before doors/crates open, every time.
- [ ] Group size within local limits and what you can physically hold.
- [ ] Heat decision made before leaving (pavement test, temperature, dog risk factors).
- [ ] Owners get a walk report: time, duration, toilets, anything unusual.
