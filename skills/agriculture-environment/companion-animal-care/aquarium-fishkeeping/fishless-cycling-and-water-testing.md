# Fishless cycling and routine water testing (freshwater)

**Goal:** grow the filter bacteria that turn toxic ammonia into nitrate *before*
fish go in, then keep the water stable. "New tank syndrome" — fish dying in the
first weeks — is almost always an uncycled tank.

## The nitrogen cycle in one line
Fish waste and food → **ammonia** (toxic) → bacteria → **nitrite** (toxic) → other
bacteria → **nitrate** (much less toxic, removed by water changes and plants).
The bacteria live on filter media and surfaces, not in the water.

## Kit
- Liquid test kit for ammonia (NH3/NH4+), nitrite (NO2-), nitrate (NO3-), pH; KH/GH
  kit useful. Strips are less accurate, especially for ammonia.
- Water conditioner that neutralises **chlorine and chloramine** (many mains supplies
  use chloramine; plain "chlorine remover" and leaving water to stand don't remove it).
- Pure ammonia source: aquarium ammonium chloride solution, or household ammonia with
  **no surfactants or scent** (it must not foam when shaken).
- Heater set to the planned temperature; warm water (~25–30 °C) cycles faster.

## Fishless cycle, step by step
1. Set up tank, substrate, filter, heater; fill with conditioned water; run 24 h.
2. Dose ammonia to about **2 ppm** (follow the product's dosing table; with household
   ammonia, add a few drops at a time and test). Higher doses (4–5 ppm) can stall
   nitrite-oxidising bacteria — 2 ppm is the commonly recommended target.
3. Optional: add bottled nitrifying bacteria or a handful of mature media from a healthy
   tank to shorten the wait. Bottled products vary in reliability.
4. Test every 1–2 days. Phase 1: ammonia falls, nitrite appears. Phase 2: nitrite rises
   then falls, nitrate climbs.
5. Each time ammonia reads 0, re-dose to 2 ppm.
6. **Done when:** a 2 ppm dose goes to **0 ammonia and 0 nitrite within 24 h**, on
   several consecutive days. Typical time 3–6 weeks without seeding; days to 2 weeks
   with mature media.
7. Do a large water change (50–80%) to bring nitrate down, then stock within a day or
   two (keep feeding a little ammonia if delayed — the bacteria starve).

## Gotchas
- **Stalled cycle:** pH under ~6.5 slows nitrifiers dramatically. If KH is near zero,
  pH crashes; raise KH (e.g. a small dose of baking soda, tested) and keep dosing.
- Nitrite reading off the scale for weeks often means ammonia overdosing — change
  water and dose less.
- High-pH tap water (> ~8) makes more of the ammonia toxic free NH3; ammonia tests
  read total ammonia, so treat any non-zero as a problem with fish in.
- Nitrate test kits (API in particular) need the second bottle shaken hard for
  ~30 s; under-shaking gives false low readings.
- Don't rinse filter media under the tap (chlorine/chloramine kill bacteria); squeeze
  it in removed tank water.
- Cleaning everything at once or replacing all filter media "re-crashes" the cycle.

## Stocking after the cycle
- Add fish in small batches over weeks — the bacteria grow to match the waste load.
- Research adult size, school size, temperature, pH/hardness range and temperament for
  each species; "inch per gallon" is a poor rule (body mass grows with the cube).
- Quarantine new fish 2–4 weeks in a separate tank if you can; most disease comes in
  with new stock.
- Acclimatise: float the bag to match temperature (~15 min), then mix in tank water
  over 30+ min for sensitive species; net the fish in, don't pour shop water in.

## Routine
- Weekly: 20–30% water change with conditioned, temperature-matched water; gravel vac.
- Weekly or when in doubt: test ammonia, nitrite (both target **0**), nitrate (common
  targets: under ~20–40 ppm), pH stability.
- Feed what's eaten in 1–2 minutes, once or twice a day. Overfeeding is the commonest
  cause of ammonia spikes in established tanks.
- Any fish gasping at the surface or sudden deaths → test first, change water second,
  medicate last.

Marine and reef tanks cycle on the same principle but need salinity (refractometer,
typically SG ~1.024–1.026), live rock and a much stricter stability routine — treat
that as a separate skill.

## Verify
- [ ] Conditioner rated for chloramine used on all new water.
- [ ] 2 ppm ammonia cleared to 0 ammonia and 0 nitrite in 24 h before fish.
- [ ] Stocking staggered; species requirements checked against your water.
- [ ] Weekly water change and test log kept.
