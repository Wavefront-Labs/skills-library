# Range condition and total grazing pressure

**Goal:** keep the land in good condition while running a viable enterprise. The
rangeland rule is simple and hard: match the number of mouths (stock *and* wild and
feral herbivores) to the feed the country actually grows, and keep enough ground
cover to catch rain and hold soil.

## 1. Assess condition
At fixed monitoring sites (photo point + simple plot), score at the same time each
year (end of growing season is common):
- **Ground cover %** — everything protecting the soil (plants, litter, rock). Many
  regional programmes set targets around 40–70% depending on rainfall zone; check
  your local extension guidance.
- **Desirable perennial grasses** (in northern Australia "3P": perennial, palatable,
  productive) — density and vigour.
- **Soil surface**: crusting, scalding, rills, gullies, pedestalled plants.
- **Woody thickening / weeds**.
A simple class system many programmes use: **A** (good) → **B** (fair, some loss of
3P) → **C** (poor, mostly annuals/unpalatables, erosion starting) → **D** (degraded,
needs intervention). Movement from B to A takes good seasons and spelling; C to B is
slow; D rarely recovers by grazing management alone.

## 2. Forage budget
1. Estimate standing dry matter (kg DM/ha) at the end of the growing season — by
   photo standards, quadrat cuts, or satellite pasture products where available
   (e.g. regional pasture-growth models). Calibrate your eye with cuts.
2. Set **utilisation**: the share of growth that grazing may take. Conservative rates
   for rangelands are often ~15–30% of annual growth depending on land type; the
   rest is left for cover, trampling losses, termites and wildlife.
3. Usable forage = area × DM × utilisation.
4. Demand = animals × intake. A standard animal unit is roughly 8–10 kg DM/day for a
   mature 450 kg cow (adult equivalent / AE); a dry sheep equivalent (DSE) ~1 kg DM/day.
   Use your region's standard.
5. Days of feed = usable forage ÷ daily demand. Do it **before the dry season**, and set
   a destocking trigger date.

## 3. Total grazing pressure (TGP)
Domestic stock are only part of the demand. Kangaroos, feral goats, donkeys, camels,
deer and rabbits can equal or exceed stock in some seasons. Estimate them (aerial or
ground counts, dung counts, water-point cameras) and include them in the budget.
Controlling unmanaged grazers (cluster fencing, trap yards at water for goats,
licensed harvest) is often the cheapest condition gain.

## 4. Grazing distribution and water
- Cattle rarely graze far from water; heavy use concentrates in the **piosphere**
  around each point while distant country is under-used.
- Commonly cited working distance: grazing effort drops off beyond ~3–5 km from water
  for cattle (less for sheep). More, closer points — with fencing to control them —
  spread grazing evenly.
- **Turning off water points** to rest an area is one of the strongest levers in
  unfenced country (also starves feral grazers of water).
- Fence to land type where possible so stock can't camp on preferred country.

## 5. Spelling
- Rest paddocks during the **early growing season** (the first several weeks after the
  break) every few years so perennial grasses can set seed and rebuild reserves —
  "wet-season spelling" in northern systems.
- Rotations work when they give real rest at the right time; short rotations without
  growing-season rest add little.

## 6. Drought
Destock early against the forage budget and your trigger dates. Holding stock into
drought on dwindling cover is how A/B land turns to C/D. Keep core breeders; sell or
agist the rest.

## Gotchas
- Good cover after one wet year can hide a loss of perennials — check the species.
- Satellite cover products don't separate palatable from unpalatable plants.
- Fire, grazing and woody thickening interact; plan them together.

## Verify
- [ ] Monitoring sites and photo points re-scored each year at the same time.
- [ ] Forage budget done end of growing season including non-stock grazers.
- [ ] Ground cover at or above the local target going into the dry.
- [ ] Destocking trigger dates written down and acted on.
- [ ] A spelling plan exists for the coming growing season.
