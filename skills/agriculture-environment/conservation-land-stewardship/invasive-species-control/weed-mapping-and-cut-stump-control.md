# Weed mapping, prioritisation and cut-stump control

**Goal:** fewer weeds every year across the property, not a cleared patch that
reinfests. That comes from mapping, working in the right order, using the right
technique per species, and coming back before regrowth seeds.

## 1. Map first
- Walk the site in a grid or along tracks; record per infestation: species, GPS polygon
  or point, density class (e.g. scattered / <25% / 25–75% / >75%), life stage
  (seedling / flowering / seeding), and a photo. A phone GIS or field app is enough
  (see the conservation-gis skills in this field).
- Mark high-value assets: remnant vegetation, threatened species, watercourses.

## 2. Prioritise
Order of attack (asset-protection logic):
1. **New and small infestations of high-risk species** — eradication still possible;
   cheapest per hectare saved. Also any species on a legal "must eradicate" list.
2. **Outliers** — isolated plants spreading the front.
3. **Edges** of large infestations adjoining good bush — contain.
4. Core of dense infestations — last, and often only where replacement vegetation is
   planned.
Work **from the best bush toward the worst** (the Bradley method principle) so native
regeneration fills each gap you make. Don't clear more than you can follow up.

## 3. Methods (match to plant type)
Always read the herbicide **label or permit**: it sets which weeds, rates, the
method allowed, and whether it can be used near water. Wear the label PPE. Registered
products and rates differ by country — the examples below are illustrative only.
- **Hand pulling / grubbing:** seedlings and shallow-rooted plants; get the whole root
  or crown; bag seeding material.
- **Cut-and-paint (cut stump):** woody shrubs and small trees. Cut low and horizontal
  (no water-holding cups), **apply herbicide to the cambium ring within ~15 seconds**
  of cutting — the cut seals fast and uptake stops. Commonly glyphosate (often
  undiluted or 1:1 for this method per label) or picloram-based gels.
- **Frill / stem injection:** larger trees left standing to die (less disturbance,
  fewer resprouts for some species). Axe cuts around the trunk through bark into sapwood,
  angled down, filled immediately. Leave a gap between cuts on some labels.
- **Basal bark:** thin-barked stems < ~5–10 cm: oil-based triclopyr (± picloram) mix
  painted or sprayed around the full circumference from ground to ~30–40 cm. Works on
  stems with dry bark; good in winter.
- **Foliar spray:** dense low infestations; most off-target damage happens here — low
  pressure, coarse droplets, no wind (see crop-sprayer drift skill for nozzle logic).
- **Biocontrol** agents where released — check agency programmes before you spray the
  plants carrying them.

## 4. Timing and follow-up
- Treat **before seed set**. Flowering is your last call.
- Herbicide uptake is best when plants are actively growing and not drought-stressed.
- Follow-up visits: typically 3–6 months after primary control for resprouts and the
  first seedling flush, then at least annually. Seed banks of some species (gorse,
  broom, lantana-type shrubs, many acacias in invaded ranges) last years to decades.
- Record each visit against the map; density classes should drop.

## 5. Pest animals alongside
Weed and animal control interact: removing a weed thicket can expose harbour for
rabbits or foxes, and killing rabbits without weed follow-up can release weeds.
Pest-animal programmes (baiting, trapping, shooting, warren destruction) are tightly
regulated and often coordinated across neighbours — join the local programme rather
than acting alone; timing across properties matters more than effort on one.

## Gotchas
- Slashing a resprouter without herbicide often makes it bushier.
- Paint on dirty/dry cuts doesn't take up: cut, then paint immediately.
- Dense removal on slopes → erosion; stage it and leave dead stems standing.
- Disturbing soil brings up the seed bank — expect a seedling flush.
- Clean boots, vehicles and tools between sites to stop spreading seed and pathogens
  (e.g. Phytophthora).

## Verify
- [ ] Infestations mapped with species, density and stage.
- [ ] Work order follows the priority list and moves from good bush outward.
- [ ] Every herbicide use matches its label/permit (species, rate, method, water).
- [ ] Follow-up dates scheduled before the primary job ends.
- [ ] Map updated after each visit; density trending down.
