# Native seed collection, direct seeding and tubestock planting

**Goal:** a self-sustaining native plant community from the right genetics, at a cost
per established plant you can afford. Survival, not number planted, is the measure.

## 1. Seed collection
- **Permits and permission:** collecting on public land or from listed species usually
  needs a permit; always landholder permission. Check before you pick.
- **Provenance:** traditionally "local" (same vegetation type, often within tens of km).
  Many programmes now use **climate-adjusted** or composite provenance (mixing local
  with sources from hotter/drier parts of the range) to future-proof. Follow your
  programme's rule and record it.
- **Genetic diversity:** collect from **many plants** — common guidance is at least
  10–20 and ideally 50+ unrelated parents, spaced apart (well beyond pollen/clone
  distance) — and avoid isolated single trees (high selfing).
- **Take no more than ~10–20%** of available seed from a population (guidance varies);
  rare species get stricter limits.
- Timing: collect at maturity (capsules browning/hardening, pods rattling). Bag whole
  branchlets of eucalypt-type capsules and let them dry in paper bags; net shrubs that
  drop seed explosively.
- Record per lot: species, GPS, date, number of parents, collector, vegetation type.

## 2. Cleaning, storage and dormancy
- Dry, sieve, winnow; store cool and dry (paper or sealed with desiccant, in the fridge
  for many species). Viability drops fast in heat and humidity.
- Many natives are dormant. Common treatments (species-specific — check a seed guide):
  **hot water** soak for hard-seeded legumes (pour near-boiling water, soak overnight),
  **smoke water** for many fire-cued species, scarification, cold stratification.
- Do a quick germination test (e.g. 50 seeds on damp paper) to set sowing rates.

## 3. Direct seeding vs tubestock
| | Direct seeding | Tubestock |
|---|---|---|
| Cost per ha | Lower | Higher (plants + labour) |
| Best for | Large areas, shrubs/understorey, accessible flat ground | Small, steep or high-value sites, slow species, tight spacing |
| Risk | Weather-dependent, erratic establishment | Transplant shock, browsing, watering |

Direct seeding basics: kill weeds and the grass sward first (herbicide in the season
before, then a **scalper** or scalping blade to remove the weed-seed-rich top 5–10 cm);
sow into lines with a direct seeder at shallow depth (small seeds on or just below the
surface — depth is the commonest failure); bulk seed with sand or vermiculite to
spread tiny seeds evenly; sow just before the reliable rain season. Rates are often
quoted in grams per km of line and vary hugely by species — use your programme's mix.

## 4. Tubestock planting
1. Prep: weed control in a ~1 m circle (spot spray or mulch mat) before planting.
2. Soak tubes in a bucket until bubbles stop.
3. Plant with a **Hamilton planter, pottiputki** or spade: hole slightly deeper than the
   tube, plant so soil level is ~1–2 cm above the plug top, firm around (no air
   pockets), make a small water-holding dish on slopes.
4. Water in: ~2–5 L per plant at planting even if the ground is wet.
5. Guard if browsing is expected (rabbits, wallabies, deer, hares) — corflute, mesh or
   milk-carton guards held by stakes. Fenced exclusion is often cheaper for large sites.
6. Spacing and layout: clumps and irregular spacing look and function more naturally
   than rows; mix canopy, shrub and ground layers.

## 5. Establishment care
- Follow-up watering in the first dry season if possible; deep and infrequent beats
  little and often.
- Follow-up weed control at 3, 6 and 12 months.
- Remove or split guards once plants are well above them (often 1–2 years).
- **Survival counts:** fixed plots or the whole row at ~3 and 12 months. Typical
  acceptable survival for tubestock is often quoted ~70–80%+; much lower points to a
  fixable cause (weeds, browsing, drought timing, poor stock).

## Gotchas
- Pot-bound tubestock with circling roots establishes badly; reject or tease out.
- Planting in a dry spell "because the volunteers are booked" kills plants.
- Herbicide residues from pre-emergent products can stop direct-seeded natives.

## Verify
- [ ] Seed lots recorded with provenance, parents and permits.
- [ ] Dormancy treatment applied per species; germination test done.
- [ ] Weeds controlled before sowing/planting.
- [ ] Survival counted at 3 and 12 months against a target.
