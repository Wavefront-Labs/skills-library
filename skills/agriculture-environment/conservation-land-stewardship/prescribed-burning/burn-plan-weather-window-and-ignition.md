# Burn plan, weather window and ignition

**Goal:** fire that does what the plan says, stays inside its lines and goes out.
This is a summary of practice for people training under qualified burn bosses —
**it is not a substitute for accredited training, a permit, or a local fire agency's
requirements**, which govern who may light and when.

## 1. The burn plan (written, before burn day)
- **Objectives** in measurable terms: e.g. "reduce fine fuel to < 5 t/ha across 70% of
  the unit", "patchy cool burn leaving ≥ 30% unburnt for habitat", "stimulate
  grassland regeneration".
- **Map**: unit boundary, containment lines, assets (houses, fences, power poles, hollow
  trees to protect), water points, escape routes and safety zones, smoke-sensitive sites
  (roads, hospitals, vineyards).
- **Prescription**: the weather and fuel window that achieves the objective. Typical
  parameters (illustrative — prescriptions are site- and fuel-specific): temperature,
  relative humidity (RH) range, wind speed and direction band, fine fuel moisture,
  drought index, days since rain, fire danger index/rating ceiling.
- **Resources**: crew numbers, roles (burn boss, ignition, holding, lookout), water
  units, communications channel, first aid.
- **Contingency**: what triggers stop-lighting and suppression; who is called if it escapes.
- **Notifications and permits**: fire agency, neighbours, local authority; permits
  where required. Respect any total fire ban absolutely.

## 2. Containment
- Use natural breaks (creeks, roads, rock, green crops) where possible.
- Constructed lines: mineral-earth break, mown/slashed and wetted strips, or a
  **blackline** burnt in advance under milder conditions on the downwind edge.
- Check lines on the day: remove fuel bridges, overhanging dead branches, logs that
  cross the line. Hollow trees and stumps on edges are classic re-ignition sources.

## 3. On the day
1. Get a **spot weather forecast**; take readings on site (kestrel/belt weather kit):
   temperature, RH, wind speed and direction, at the start and regularly (e.g. hourly).
2. Briefing (all crew): objectives, map, prescription, roles, comms, escape routes,
   hazards, the go/no-go criteria.
3. **Test fire**: small, on the downwind edge, within containment. Watch flame height,
   rate of spread, spotting, smoke column. If behaviour exceeds the plan → don't proceed.
4. Burn boss makes the go/no-go and records it.

## 4. Ignition patterns
- **Backing fire:** lit along the downwind line, burns into the wind. Slow, low intensity,
  easiest to hold — the usual way to secure edges.
- **Flank fire:** lines lit parallel to the wind; moderate intensity.
- **Strip-head fire:** successive strips lit upwind of the black, each running a short
  distance with the wind into the previous strip. Narrow strips = lower intensity.
- **Spot/point ignition (grid):** spaced spots that join; good for patchy outcomes.
Light the downwind side first, secure it, then work upwind. Drip torch mix and
handling per agency SOP (commonly diesel/petrol blends) — never refuel near fire.

## 5. Mop-up and patrol
- Extinguish everything burning within a set distance of the line (often 10–30 m;
  plan states it) — logs, stumps, cow pats, hollow trees.
- Use a back-of-hand test on cooled ash near the edge; dig and stir hot spots.
- **Patrol** for days after; re-ignitions from smouldering logs under drier, windier
  days are a known escape pathway. Record when the burn is declared safe.

## 6. Cultural burning
Cultural burning by or with Traditional Owners/Indigenous practitioners is led by
those knowledge holders, under their protocols and on Country they speak for. It
typically uses small, cool, patchy fires timed by the condition of the country
(grass curing, dew, plant cues) rather than calendar dates, and walks with the fire.
Non-Indigenous crews support and learn, rather than direct. Agency permits still apply
in most jurisdictions.

## 7. After the burn
Assess against objectives: % area burnt, patchiness, scorch height, fuel remaining,
ecological responses at photo points. Feed it into the next prescription.

## Verify
- [ ] Written plan with measurable objectives, prescription, map, roles and contingency.
- [ ] Permits/notifications complete; no fire ban in force.
- [ ] On-site weather within prescription and logged throughout.
- [ ] Test fire behaviour matched the plan before main ignition.
- [ ] Mop-up distance met; patrol schedule kept until declared safe.
