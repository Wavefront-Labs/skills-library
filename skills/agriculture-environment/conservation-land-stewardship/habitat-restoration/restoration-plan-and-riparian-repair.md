# Restoration plan and riparian repair

**Goal:** a restoration that is still working in ten years. Most failed projects
planted trees without removing the thing that degraded the site — stock, altered
water, weeds, erosion — so the planting died or washed away.

## 1. Assess the site (before any plan)
Walk it in more than one season if you can, and record:
- **Drivers of degradation:** grazing pressure (stock, deer, rabbits, kangaroos),
  changed hydrology (drains, dams, incised channels), erosion, weeds, nutrient
  run-off, fire regime, recreation pressure.
- **What's left:** remnant natives, soil seed bank (signs of natural regeneration
  once grazing stops), habitat features (old trees, logs, rocks).
- Soil (texture, compaction, salinity/sodicity if relevant), aspect, slope.
- Constraints: tenure, access, permits (works in or near watercourses usually need a
  permit — check with your water/environment agency before moving earth), cultural
  heritage (consult Traditional Owners / heritage registers).

## 2. Set the target
- Choose a **reference ecosystem**: nearby intact remnants of the same vegetation
  type, regional vegetation mapping, historical records.
- Write measurable goals: e.g. "native ground cover ≥ 70% and 3+ native canopy species
  established across 2 ha by year 5; woody weeds < 5% cover".
- Pick the approach on the **recovery ladder** (SER standards): natural regeneration
  (remove pressures only) → assisted regeneration (plus weed control, small infill)
  → reconstruction (full replanting). Use the lightest approach that will work — it's
  cheaper and gives better local genetics.

## 3. Order of works
1. **Remove/limit the driver**: fencing, stock off, feral control, block drains, fix
   erosion heads. Without this, stop.
2. **Weed control** — primary knockdown in the season before planting
   (see invasive-species-control skills).
3. **Earthworks/hydrology** if needed, with permits.
4. **Revegetation** — seed or plant (see native-revegetation skills).
5. **Follow-up**: weeding, replacing failures, maintaining fences — budget for at least
   3 years of this. Follow-up is where projects are most often underfunded.

## 4. Riparian repair specifics
- **Fence stock out** of the riparian zone. Common widths: at least 10–20 m each side
  on small streams, wider on larger rivers or eroding banks; set back far enough that
  floods don't flatten the fence — on flood-prone streams use drop-down/floodgate
  sections or plain-wire designs that can be repaired cheaply.
- **Off-stream water** (trough, pumped or gravity) so stock don't need the creek;
  controlled hardened crossings where they must cross.
- Stabilise eroding banks with vegetation first; **bank battering + planting**, coir
  logs, or engineered log jams / large woody debris are for active erosion and
  normally need an engineer and permit.
- Plant in zones: sedges/rushes at the water line, shrubs on the bank, trees up top.
  Use local provenance stock.
- Leave fallen timber in the channel unless it's a flood risk to infrastructure — it's
  habitat and slows flows.

## 5. Monitoring over years
- Establish **permanent photo points** before works: marked post or GPS, compass bearing,
  same height, same season each year.
- Simple plots or transects: % native cover, weed cover, recruits, survival of plantings
  (count in fixed plots at 3, 12 and 24 months).
- Annual review: compare against goals and adjust — that's adaptive management, not
  failure.
- Recovery often takes 5–20+ years for structure; don't judge a woodland at year two.

## Gotchas
- Planting before fencing → browsed off in weeks.
- Herbicide near water needs an aquatic-registered product — check labels.
- Tree guards left on for years become litter and strangle stems; schedule removal.
- Natural regeneration can surprise you once grazing stops; leave a fenced control
  patch unplanted for a season before committing to full replanting if time allows.

## Verify
- [ ] Degradation drivers listed and the first works address them.
- [ ] Measurable goals and reference ecosystem written down.
- [ ] Permits/heritage checks done before earthworks or in-stream works.
- [ ] Photo points established before work starts.
- [ ] At least three years of follow-up budgeted.
