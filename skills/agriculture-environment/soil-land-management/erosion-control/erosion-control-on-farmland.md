# Erosion control on farmland

**Goal:** stop topsoil leaving the paddock — the cheapest fix is cover; earthworks
come second; gully repair is the expensive last resort.

## 1. Read the erosion you have
- **Sheet:** fine soil stripped evenly; signs are pedestalled pebbles, exposed roots,
  fence posts and trough bases standing proud, silt on the downhill fence.
- **Rill:** small channels (<30 cm deep) (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) that a tillage pass can erase.
- **Gully:** too deep to cross with machinery; has an active **headcut** that moves
  upslope in every flow.
- **Wind:** drifts against fences, scalded bare patches, sandblasted seedlings,
  dust plumes on dry windy days.
Walk the paddock **during or just after heavy rain** — you see the flow paths that
dry-day inspections miss.

## 2. Ground cover: the main control
Erosion rises steeply once cover drops below a threshold. Commonly used targets
(check your region's guides):
- **Water erosion:** keep **above 70% ground cover** (the NSW Government benchmark for reducing water erosion) (plants, litter, residue) on grazing
  land; on cropping land, ≥30% residue after sowing is the USDA-NRCS definition of conservation tillage,
  more on steep slopes.
- **Wind erosion:** keep **above 50% cover** (the NSW Government benchmark for reducing wind erosion), or roughly 1.5–2 t/ha of anchored (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)
  cereal stubble, on light soils through the windy season.
How to estimate: drop a 1 m² quadrat (or a 30 m tape, reading every 30 cm mark as
"covered/bare") at 20+ points; photo-standard charts help calibrate the eye.
Practices: retain stubble, sow cover crops into fallows, destock *before* cover
hits the threshold (set a trigger, e.g. destock at 1,000 kg DM/ha or 70% cover),
and use sacrifice/feed lots in drought instead of letting every paddock go bare.

## 3. Earthworks for water
- **Contour farming:** sow and work across the slope.
- **Contour banks** (graded banks) intercept runoff and walk it off the paddock at
  a gentle grade — commonly ~0.2–0.4% (about 1:500 to 1:250) (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) — into a stable
  outlet. Spacing tightens as slope steepens; a designer works it from the vertical
  interval and rainfall intensity. Survey with a laser or RTK level; don't eyeball.
- **Grassed waterways:** shallow, wide, permanently grassed channels for the
  collected water. Never cultivate or spray them out; lift implements when crossing.
- **Diversion banks** above a problem area keep outside water off it.
- Every bank needs a **stable outlet** — an unplanned outlet becomes the next gully.
- Get local design and permits: earthworks that change flows onto neighbours or
  into waterways are regulated in many places.

## 4. Gully repair (staged)
1. **Exclude stock** with fencing; grazing the gully sides keeps it active.
2. **Divert or reduce inflow** upstream (diversion bank, waterway, more cover in the
   catchment). A gully is a symptom of too much concentrated water.
3. **Stop the headcut:** shape it to a gentle slope and armour it — a rock chute
   (rock sized for the flow, on geotextile), or a drop structure for larger flows.
4. **Batter the sides** to roughly 1:3 or flatter (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) where you can, then seed and mulch
   immediately.
5. **Slow the flow** inside the gully: leaky weirs or rock/log check structures
   that trap sediment; space them so the crest of each is level with the toe of the
   one above.
6. Revegetate the bed and banks with grasses, sedges and deep-rooted shrubs suited
   to the site.
Big or deep gullies (>2 m, or with dispersive (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)/sodic subsoil that tunnels) need an
engineer or soil conservation officer — dispersive soils collapse around pipes and
rock if not treated (gypsum, compaction, filter fabric).

## 5. Windbreaks
- Orient **perpendicular to the damaging wind** (check a wind rose for the erosion
  season, not the annual average).
- A windbreak reduces wind speed for roughly **10–15× its height** (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) downwind (less
  but some effect upwind).
- Aim for **~40–60% porosity** (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) — a solid wall creates turbulence behind it.
- Use 2–5 rows mixing tall trees and lower shrubs; leave no gaps (gaps funnel and
  accelerate wind). Fence from stock until established.

## Gotchas
- Cultivation to "fix" rills smooths the surface but loosens soil for the next storm.
- Wheel tracks and stock pads running downhill become gullies; turn them across slope.
- Sodic subsoils exposed by erosion crust and resist revegetation — see
  `agriculture-environment.soil-land-management.land-reclamation.managing-saline-sodic-and-acid-soils`.

**Uncertainty:** cover thresholds and bank grades are typical extension figures;
local design standards (and rainfall erosivity) govern. Not field-verified by the
author.

## Verify
- [ ] Cover measured at 20+ points per paddock and above the threshold for the season.
- [ ] A destocking trigger is written down before it is needed.
- [ ] Every bank and waterway ends in a stable, armoured outlet.
- [ ] Gully headcut stabilised and fenced; inflow reduced upstream.
- [ ] After the next big rain: no new rills, waterways intact, gully head unchanged.
