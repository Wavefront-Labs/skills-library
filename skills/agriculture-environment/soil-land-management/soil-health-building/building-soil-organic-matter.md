# Building soil organic matter and structure

**Goal:** more organic matter, better aggregates and more life in the soil — and
the field tests to show it is working before the lab can.

## 1. Set realistic expectations
- Organic carbon rises **slowly**: a well-run programme might add in the order of
  0.05–0.1 percentage points of OC per year (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) in the topsoil, and often less in hot,
  dry climates. Structure and infiltration improve within 1–3 seasons; the lab
  number lags.
- OC has a ceiling set by climate and clay content. A sand in a dry climate will
  never hold what a clay in a wet one does — compare with similar soils nearby
  (a long-term pasture or fence line is a useful local "reference").
- Tillage, bare fallow, residue burning and overgrazing burn OC faster than most
  additions replace it. Stop the losses first.

## 2. The levers, in order of usual cost-effectiveness
1. **Keep living roots in the ground as many days as possible.** Roots and root
   exudates build stable OM more efficiently than surface residue. Fill fallows with
   cover crops; consider perennial phases (pasture leys) in rotations.
2. **Keep residue, cut tillage** (see
   `agriculture-environment.soil-land-management.conservation-tillage.transitioning-to-no-till`).
3. **Diversify.** Multispecies covers (e.g. a cereal + legume + brassica mix) give
   different root depths and feed a broader microbial community. A legume adds N;
   a cereal adds bulk and high-C residue.
4. **Manage grazing**, not just stocking rate: graze to leave residual (e.g. not
   below ~1,500 kg DM/ha or ~5 cm (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) on most temperate pastures — local guides vary),
   then rest long enough to recover.
5. **Import carbon** — compost, manure, biosolids (where permitted) — where it pays.

## 3. Field-scale compost
- **Recipe:** start around **25–30:1 C:N**, moisture ~50–60% (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) (squeeze test: a few
  drops, not a stream). Woody material and straw are high-C; manure and green
  material are high-N.
- **Windrows:** ~1.5–2 m high, 3–4 m wide (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked); turn when core temperature drops or
  every 1–2 weeks early on. Hold **131-170 °F (55-77 °C)** for at least 3 days in aerated static piles or in-vessel systems, or for 15 days with at least five turnings in windrows (US NOP, 7 CFR 205.203(c)(2); other schemes differ) to kill weed seeds and pathogens — the standard
  sanitisation target.
- **Mature when** temperature no longer climbs after turning, it smells earthy, and
  a cress/radish germination test in a jar of compost works normally.
- **Rates:** typically ~5–20 t/ha (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked); get it analysed and count its nutrients in the
  nutrient budget
  (`agriculture-environment.soil-land-management.nutrient-management.nutrient-budget-and-fertiliser-rates`).
  Immature, high-C compost can lock up N for a season.

## 4. Fix compaction properly
- **Find it:** push a cone penetrometer; resistance above about **2 MPa (~300 psi)** (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)
  at field capacity restricts most roots. Note the depth — plough pans often sit at
  15–30 cm. Dig a spade pit to confirm (flat, horizontal roots; platy structure).
- **Fix it:** rip or deep-loosen just **below** the pan when the soil at that depth
  is dry enough to shatter (wet soil smears and re-compacts). Follow immediately
  with a deep-rooted cover to hold the fissures open.
- **Don't recompact it:** controlled traffic, lower tyre pressures, no wet-soil
  traffic.

## 5. Monitor with field tests (same spot, same season each year)
- **Slake test:** drop an air-dry crumb into water. Collapsing within minutes =
  weak aggregates; holding together for 10 minutes = good.
- **Infiltration:** drive a 15 cm (6 in) ring ~7 cm in; pour 444 mL (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) (= 25 mm over
  that area) and time how long it takes to soak in. Repeat for a wet-soil reading.
- **Earthworms:** dig a 20 × 20 × 20 cm spadeful in moist, mild conditions; count.
  Roughly 10+ worms per spadeful (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) is commonly cited as healthy for temperate cropping.
- **Visual soil assessment** (VESS or similar scoring): photograph the same pit.
- **Lab:** OC (same method and depth), plus optional respiration or active-carbon tests.

## Gotchas
- Biochar and "biological" products vary hugely; trial on strips with an untreated
  check before buying at scale.
- Cover crops can use stored moisture the cash crop needs in dry areas; terminate
  early enough.
- Carbon-credit claims need stratified, depth-and-bulk-density-corrected sampling,
  not a single topsoil OC%.

**Uncertainty:** rates of OC gain, worm thresholds and residual targets are broad
generalisations; local conditions dominate. Not field-verified by the author.

## Verify
- [ ] Baseline recorded: OC%, slake, infiltration, worm count, photos, GPS points.
- [ ] Bare-soil days counted and a plan in place to reduce them.
- [ ] Compost hit 55 °C+ with turning and passed a germination check before use.
- [ ] Compaction depth measured before ripping; soil dry enough at that depth.
- [ ] Repeat tests scheduled for the same season next year.
