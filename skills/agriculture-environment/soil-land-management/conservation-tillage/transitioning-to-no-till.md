# Transitioning to no-till

**Goal:** stop disturbing the soil, keep residue on top, and still establish an even
crop — without trading erosion and fuel bills for weed and seeding problems.

## 1. Before the switch: fix what tillage was hiding
- **Compaction pans:** tillage often masks a pan. Check with a penetrometer and a
  spade; rip once *before* going no-till if roots are blocked (see
  `agriculture-environment.soil-land-management.soil-health-building.building-soil-organic-matter`).
- **Levelling:** smooth wheel ruts, gilgais and old furrows now — you won't get
  another chance without cultivating.
- **pH:** incorporate lime first if subsoil acidity needs it; no-till keeps surface
  lime in the top few cm (see
  `agriculture-environment.soil-land-management.land-reclamation.managing-saline-sodic-and-acid-soils`).
- **Weed seedbank:** a heavy problem (e.g. herbicide-resistant ryegrass) needs a plan
  before you lose cultivation as a control.

## 2. Start at harvest: residue management
Residue problems at sowing are created by the header.
- **Spread** straw and chaff across the **full cut width** — a 12 m front throwing
  residue 8 m wide leaves heavy rows that block seeders, cool the soil and harbour
  slugs. Check the spreader with a tarp or by eye in the stubble after the first run.
- **Cut height:** taller standing stubble (stripper front or high cut) flows through
  tine seeders better and traps snow/moisture; short, chopped residue is easier for
  discs. Pick for your seeder.
- **Weed seed:** harvest weed seed control — chaff lining, chaff decks, seed
  destructors or narrow-windrow burning — replaces some of the control tillage used
  to give.

## 3. Choose and set the seeder
| | Disc (single/double) | Tine with knife points |
|---|---|---|
| Disturbance | very low | low–moderate |
| Residue handling | cuts through, can **hairpin** straw into slot | can rake/block in heavy residue |
| Wet / sticky soils | struggles (sidewall smearing, open slots) | better |
| Pre-emergent herbicide safety | less soil throw — more crop-safety caution | throws treated soil out of the row |
| Speed / maintenance | fast, more parts | simple, cheaper |

Setting checks, every paddock:
- **Seed depth:** dig behind the seeder in several runs; seed in moist soil at
  target depth (cereals typically ~3–5 cm (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)), slot closed, seed in firm contact.
- **Hairpinning (discs):** straw tucked into the slot keeps seed out of soil — sow
  when residue is dry and brittle (afternoon), use row cleaners, or sharpen/replace
  worn discs.
- **Press wheels:** enough pressure to close and firm the slot, not so much you seal
  a clay surface.

## 4. Controlled traffic (CTF)
Keep all heavy wheels on permanent lanes so most of the paddock is never driven on.
- Match machine widths to a common module (e.g. 3 m track gauge; 9/12/18/36 m
  implements in multiples).
- Use RTK guidance (~2–3 cm repeatability) (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) so lanes stay put year to year.
- Typical outcome: wheeled area drops from well over half the paddock to ~15–20% (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
- Plan harvest logistics (chaser bins on lanes) — the header is usually the
  hardest machine to match.

## 5. The transition years
- **Yield dip:** many growers see equal or slightly lower yields for the first
  2–4 years, especially on cold, wet, poorly drained soils (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked), while structure and
  biology adjust. Dry-climate growers often gain from stored moisture early.
- **N:** residue ties up N early — add ~10–20 kg N/ha at sowing (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) on high-residue
  stubbles (rule of thumb; verify locally).
- **Slugs, mice, snails:** thrive under residue; bait and monitor (tiles/wet sacks
  overnight for slugs) before and after sowing.
- **Herbicide reliance rises:** rotate modes of action, use double-knock
  (glyphosate then paraquat-type knockdown) and keep crop competition high
  (narrow rows, higher seeding rates) to slow resistance.
- **Disease:** stubble-borne diseases (e.g. crown rot, yellow leaf spot in wheat)
  can rise — rotate crops, don't sow wheat into wheat stubble if you can avoid it.

## Gotchas
- The occasional "strategic" tillage (one pass to fix a weed or pan problem) is not
  failure — but repeated passes erase the structure gains.
- Uneven residue is the #1 cause of patchy no-till establishment. Fix the header.
- Strip-till is a good compromise on cold, wet soils for row crops (maize, sugar beet).

**Uncertainty:** numbers above (N top-up, yield-dip duration, wheeled-area figures)
are commonly reported ranges, not guarantees, and depend on climate and soil.
Not field-verified by the author.

## Verify
- [ ] Pan, levelling and pH issues corrected before the first no-till season.
- [ ] Residue spread to full cut width (checked in the paddock, not assumed).
- [ ] Seed depth and slot closure dug and checked in several runs per paddock.
- [ ] Slug/snail/mouse monitoring done before sowing.
- [ ] Herbicide plan uses multiple modes of action plus a harvest weed-seed tactic.
