# Patterning and heat-forming EVA foam armour

**Goal:** wearable armour plates that fit the performer, move with them, survive the
run and come on and off quickly. EVA is the default for stage and costume armour;
Worbla and Kydex follow the same patterning logic.

## 1. Pattern from the body
1. Wrap the area (or a dress form padded to the performer's measurements) in
   **cling film**, then **duct tape** over it in a smooth layer.
2. Draw the plate outlines and **seam lines** on the tape. Put seams where the body
   curves in two directions — a dome needs darts or seams; foam bends in one
   direction freely but stretches only a little.
3. Mark registration ticks across each seam, cut the tape shell off along a safe
   line, then cut along seam lines so each piece lies flat. If a piece won't lie flat,
   add another seam.
4. Trace onto card, true up curves, and label each piece (left/right, top).

## 2. Cut
- Foam: **high-density EVA**, 4–6 mm for plates, 2 mm for detail, 8–10 mm for thick
  or structural pieces.
- Trace onto the smooth side. Cut with a fresh snap-off blade, many light passes
  (not one forced pass), blade perpendicular for butt seams.
- **Bevel** where a seam must make a curve: cut at 45 degrees on the inside edges so
  the seam closes into a ridge.

## 3. Heat-form
- Heat with a **heat gun** on a medium setting, 10–15 cm away, keeping it moving,
  until the surface just changes sheen (EVA becomes pliable around 90–120 °C;
  unconfirmed — varies by foam). Both sides for thick foam.
- Form over the body form, a bowl or a padded mannequin; hold until cool (20–30 s).
- Heating also closes the pores before sealing.

## 4. Glue
- **Contact cement** (e.g. Barge, or a less toxic water-based contact cement): thin
  coat on both edges, wait until touch-dry (about 10–15 min), align the registration
  marks and press together from one end — no repositioning.
- Hot glue only for non-structural details.

## 5. Articulation and strapping
- **Overlapping lames** (bands) for elbows, shoulders and knees: each lame overlaps
  the next by 1–2 cm, connected by internal leather or elastic strips riveted or
  glued, so they slide over each other.
- **Strapping**: 25 mm nylon webbing with **side-release buckles** or Velcro, riveted
  or glued with a foam backing patch. Plan attachment to an under-garment (harness,
  vest, belt) rather than straps biting into skin.
- Check with the actor: can they sit, raise arms overhead, bend, breathe, and dress
  quickly?

## 6. Seal and finish
- Seal with a **flexible sealer** (Plasti Dip, Flexbond or several coats of latex);
  then acrylic metallic paints with a black wash for depth; dry-brush edges for wear.
- Line the inside with fabric or soft foam where it touches skin, for comfort and
  sweat.

## Other materials
- **Worbla** (thermoplastic): heat to ~90 °C, sticks to itself, often sandwiched over
  foam; needs priming (wood glue or gesso) before paint.
- **Leather**: wet-mould veg-tan, harden with heat or wax.
- **Metal**: heavier and louder; usually aluminium for stage.

## Gotchas
- **Seams split**: glue too thick or closed while wet.
- **Pieces don't match left/right**: forgot to flip the pattern.
- **Restricted movement**: plates too long over joints; shorten or add lames.
- **Overheating EVA**: blisters or burns. Keep the gun moving.
- **Too hot to wear**: add vent holes inside, keep plates off the torso where possible.

## Safety
Contact cement and Plasti Dip: ventilation and organic-vapour respirator. Heat guns
reach several hundred °C; don't heat foam on the performer.

## Verify
- [ ] Pattern lies flat and fits the performer's measurements.
- [ ] Seams tight and smooth; no glue gaps.
- [ ] Actor can perform full movement range in costume.
- [ ] Armour on/off within the required time.
- [ ] Sealed finish flexes without cracking.
