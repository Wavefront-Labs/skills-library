# Rigging costumes for quick changes

**Goal:** a costume that looks correctly fastened from the audience but comes off
and goes on in the time the script allows — often 30–60 seconds in the wings — and
keeps working through the whole run.

## 1. Get the facts
- Time available (from the stage manager's timings), where the change happens
  (booth, wings, under the stage), light level there, and how many dressers.
- The full **out** and **in** lists: every garment, accessory, wig and shoe.
- What the actor does immediately after: dancing, fighting, lying down. Closures
  must survive that.

## 2. Choose closures
| Closure | Speed | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Hook-and-loop (Velcro)** | fastest | Noisy when opened; can snag fabrics; 20–25 mm wide, sew-on not stick-on. |
| **Magnets** (sew-in, fabric-encased) | fast, quiet | Weaker; good for flaps and lapels, poor for stress points. |
| **Large sew-on snaps** | fast | Quiet-ish; need a firm pull; reinforce behind. |
| **Separating zip** | fast if the pull is reachable | Good for fitted bodices and trousers; add a large pull tab. |
| **Hooks and bars** | slow | Keep only at the waistband top for security. |
- **Rule**: closures out of sight, at the centre front or side where the dresser can
  reach, never at centre back if the actor has to do it alone.

## 3. Disguise
- **Button façade**: sew buttons on the **outside** as decoration, close the
  garment with Velcro or snaps underneath. Stitch the buttonholes closed.
- **False fronts** and dickies replace whole layers (a waistcoat front sewn into
  the jacket; a shirt front with collar and tie built in).
- **Pre-assembled layers**: jacket and waistcoat sewn together, or skirt and petticoat
  as one, so they go on in one move.
- **Shoes**: replace laces with elastic and add pull loops at the heel.

## 4. Construct for the run
- Back every closure with **interfacing or a twill-tape strip**; Velcro and snaps
  pull through single fabric layers within a few shows.
- Sew hook-and-loop with a straight stitch close to the edge all round, plus an X.
- Add **pull tabs** on zips and garment tags in contrasting colour so the dresser can
  grab in low light (often blue light in the wings).
- Keep openings large: necklines with a hidden extra opening, sleeves with snapped
  cuffs.

## 5. Plan and rehearse the change
1. Write a **change plot** with the dresser: who removes what in which order, and
   where each piece is preset (on a chair, opened, laid in stepping order).
2. Preset the incoming costume **open**, unfastened, trousers bunched over shoes.
3. Rehearse with the actor before tech, then in tech at speed. Time it.
4. Adjust closures based on what was slow.

## Gotchas
- **Velcro snags tights or lace**: cover the hook side when not mated, or use snaps.
- **Façade buttons pulled off**: anchored only through the face fabric; add a
  backing button.
- **Magnets interfere with mics or pacemakers**: check placement against radio mic
  packs and ask about medical devices.
- **Noise on stage**: Velcro opening is audible — none in a quiet scene onstage.
- **Change too slow in tech**: the plot, not the actor, is usually the issue.

## Verify
- [ ] Every visible closure reads as period-correct from the house.
- [ ] Change completed within time in a rehearsal at full speed, three times running.
- [ ] Each closure reinforced and stitched through a backing.
- [ ] Change plot written and preset marked for the dresser.
- [ ] Costume survives the actor's choreography without opening.
