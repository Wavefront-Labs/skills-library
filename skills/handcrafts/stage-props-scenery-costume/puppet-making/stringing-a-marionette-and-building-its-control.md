# Stringing a marionette and building its control

**Goal:** a string puppet that stands with feet on the floor, walks, turns its head
and gestures under one operator's hands, with strings that don't tangle and lengths
set to the performing bridge.

## 1. Before stringing
- **Joints** must swing freely without flopping sideways: knees hinge one way
  (open screw eyes, leather or fabric hinges), hips and shoulders loose.
- **Weight**: the figure must hang heavily enough that gravity, not the strings,
  does the work. Add lead in the feet and lower legs (and the pelvis) so legs fall
  straight. A 50–60 cm puppet commonly weighs 0.5–1 kg (unconfirmed — tune by feel).
- Hands light, so they rise easily.

## 2. The control (horizontal / airplane style)
Make from 12–15 mm hardwood dowel or flat strip:
- **Main bar** (fore-and-aft), about 25–30 cm.
- **Head bar** (crossbar) near the front, about 15 cm, fixed: carries the two head
  strings.
- **Shoulder bar** at the back, fixed, about 12–15 cm, carries the shoulder strings.
- **Detachable leg bar**, about 25 cm, hung on a hook at the front of the control;
  rocked to walk.
- A screw eye under the back for the **back (seat) string**, and a separate **hand
  bar** or a loop for the hand string.
Drill or screw-eye each string point; use small screw eyes so strings can be
adjusted.

## 3. Strings and attachment points
Use **braided black nylon or fishing line** (about 0.3–0.5 mm; braided resists
twisting) — black or dark to disappear against masking.
| String | Puppet point | Control point |
|---|---|---|
| Head (×2) | Just above and in front of each ear | Ends of the head bar |
| Shoulders (×2) | Top of each shoulder | Shoulder bar (takes the body weight) |
| Back | Small of the back / seat | Rear of main bar |
| Hands (×2) | Back of each wrist or palm | Hand bar or single string through the control |
| Knees (×2) | Just above each knee, front | Ends of the leg bar |

## 4. Set lengths
1. Decide the **performing height**: the distance from the operator's hand, standing
   on the bridge or floor, to the stage floor.
2. Hang the control at that height (from a hook or a stand). Tie the **shoulder
   strings** first so the feet just touch the floor and the puppet stands upright.
3. Tie the **head strings** so the head sits naturally with shoulders taking the
   weight — the head strings are just slack when the control is level.
4. Tie the **back string** slightly slack (tilt the control back to bow the puppet).
5. **Hand strings** long enough that hands hang at the sides with control level.
6. **Knee strings** with the leg bar on its hook: just slack.
Tie off with a temporary slip knot or wrap round a peg; finalise once it moves
well.

## 5. Test and tune
- **Walk**: rock the leg bar so knees lift alternately while moving the control
  forward. Feet should step down, not float.
- **Turn the head**: tilt the control side to side; the head should lead.
- **Bow**: tilt forward with back string; the puppet bends at the waist.
- If the feet float, add weight; if strings tangle, separate attachment points
  more or use a control with wider bars.

## Gotchas
- **Puppet hangs from its head**: shoulder strings too long; head strings take
  weight and the neck snaps forward.
- **Twisting strings**: monofilament with a memory; use braided line.
- **Floaty walk**: legs too light.
- **Tangles in storage**: hang the control on a hook, never pile the puppet.
- Rod, hand and shadow puppets need different control logic (rods from below, the
  hand inside), but the same principles of weight and balance apply.

## Verify
- [ ] Puppet stands on both feet with the control held level at performing height.
- [ ] Shoulder strings carry the weight; head strings just slack.
- [ ] Walk, bow, head turn and both arm lifts work without tangling.
- [ ] Strings invisible against the masking under show light.
- [ ] Control hangs in storage without tangles.
