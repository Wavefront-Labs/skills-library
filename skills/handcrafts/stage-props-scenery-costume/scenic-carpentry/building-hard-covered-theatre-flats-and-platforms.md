# Building hard-covered theatre flats and platforms

**Goal:** two of the most-used scenic units — a hard-covered flat and a stock 4 × 8
platform — built square, light, strong enough for the show's loads and able to be
struck and reused.

## 1. Hard-covered (Hollywood / studio) flat
**Materials**: 1 × 3 or 1 × 4 select pine (nominal; ~19 × 70 or 19 × 89 mm), lauan
plywood (3 mm / ⅛") or 6 mm MDF, wood glue, 18 ga staples or brads, 32–38 mm
screws.

**Frame (on edge)**
1. Cut **stiles** (verticals) full height minus nothing; **rails** (top and bottom)
   fit between the stiles. Toggles (intermediate rails) every 600 mm–1.2 m (2'–4')
   of height, also between stiles.
2. Set the timber **on edge** (the 19 mm face is what the skin sits on), so the
   flat has 70–90 mm of depth — stiffer than a traditional face-framed flat.
3. Glue and screw or staple at every joint. Check **square by diagonals**: the two
   measurements must match within 2 mm.

**Skin**
4. Glue the lauan face to the frame, staple every 100–150 mm around the edges and
   along toggles. Keep sheet joints over framing members.
5. Ease the edges with a block plane. Fill staple holes and seams, sand, then size
   or prime.

**Stand it up**: flats are braced with **jacks** (triangular braces) weighted
with stage weights or screwed to the deck, or joined to other flats at corners.

## 2. Stock 4 × 8 platform
**Frame**: 2 × 4 (38 × 89 mm) on edge or 1 × 6, perimeter plus joists at
**400–600 mm (16"–24") centres**. Glue and screw; check square by diagonals.
**Lid**: 18–19 mm (¾") plywood, glued and screwed at 150–200 mm centres. A
lid of 18 mm ply on 400 mm joist spacing is a common standard for performers
walking and dancing (unconfirmed for any specific load — have a qualified
person rate platforms carrying crowds, pianos or vehicles).

**Legs**
- 2 × 4 legs bolted inside the corners of the frame with **two carriage bolts each**
  (not only screws), plus centre legs on long sides for spans over ~1.2 m.
- **Cross-brace** every leg run with 1 × 3 or 1 × 4 diagonals in both directions once
  legs exceed about 300 mm, so the platform can't rack (sway sideways).
- Level with shims; screw to the deck or adjacent platforms.

**Wagons**: same frame with **locking castors** rated for the load, low-profile; add
a brake or stage-weight blocks where the unit stops.

## 3. Stairs
Treads 250–280 mm deep, risers 150–190 mm (6–7½"), all risers equal within 5 mm.
Strings from 2 × 12 or plywood; treads glued and screwed. Handrails where the drop
or the director's blocking requires them.

## 4. Tourable and reusable
- Use **bolts and hinges** (pin hinges, loose-pin back-flaps) at joints the crew must
  strike quickly; label every piece and joint with a numbering system.
- Keep to stock sizes (4 × 8, 4 × 4, 2 × 8) so units return to stock.

## Gotchas
- **Flats rack**: no diagonal check before skinning.
- **Platform squeaks**: lid not glued or screws too far apart.
- **Platform sways**: missing cross-bracing on tall legs.
- **Heavy flats**: oversized framing; 1 × 3 is usually enough below about 3.6 m tall.
- **Uneven risers**: trip hazard; measure every tread.

## Safety
Hearing and eye protection on saws, dust extraction for MDF. Any platform taller
than about 760 mm (30") generally needs guard rails on open edges (unconfirmed —
check your local code and the venue's rules). Mark edges with glow tape.

## Verify
- [ ] Diagonals within 2 mm on every flat and platform frame.
- [ ] Legs bolted, cross-braced, and the platform doesn't rack when pushed.
- [ ] Load expectations checked against a rating by a qualified person.
- [ ] Stair risers equal; edges glow-taped; rails where required.
- [ ] All units labelled and strike with hand tools in the planned time.
