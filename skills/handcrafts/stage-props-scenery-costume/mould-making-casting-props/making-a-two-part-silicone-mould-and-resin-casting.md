# Making a two-part silicone mould and resin casting

**Goal:** a durable mould that gives a run of identical prop copies (goblets, skulls,
weapons parts, food) in light polyurethane resin, with clean seams and no bubbles.

## 1. Prepare the master
- Seal porous masters (wood, plaster, clay) with shellac or a sealing primer.
- **Silicone type**: **tin-cure** (condensation) silicone tolerates almost any
  master; **platinum-cure** (addition) silicone is more durable and accurate but is
  **inhibited** — it won't cure — against sulphur clays, latex, some 3D prints and
  tin-cure residue. Test a dab on the master first.

## 2. Clay-up (part one)
1. Build a **mould box** of foamboard or acrylic, leaving **10–15 mm** of silicone
   thickness around the master on all sides.
2. Bed the master in **sulphur-free plasticine** up to its parting line — choose the
   line with no undercuts on either side.
3. Press **registration keys** (a pen cap, marbles) into the clay around the
   master, and add a clay **pour spout** at the highest point and thin **vent**
   rods at any high pockets where air will trap.

## 3. Pour part one
- Weigh the silicone by its **mix ratio by weight** (commonly 1A:1B or 10A:1B, per
  the data sheet). Mix slowly, scraping sides and bottom, for 2–3 minutes.
- Degas in a vacuum chamber if you have one; otherwise pour from height in a thin
  stream into one low corner and let it rise over the master.
- Cure per data sheet (typically 4–24 h at room temperature).

## 4. Part two
1. Flip, remove the clay (leave the master in the silicone).
2. Apply **mould release** to the silicone surface — silicone bonds to itself. Use a
   silicone-on-silicone release or a thin petroleum jelly film.
3. Rebuild the box, pour part two. Cure, then separate and remove the master.

## 5. Cast resin
- Use a **fast polyurethane casting resin** (pot life a few minutes, demould 10–30
  min). Mix 1:1 by volume or weight per the sheet, stir thoroughly but gently.
- Spray a release suited to resin (or none; silicone releases urethanes well,
  but release extends mould life).
- Close and **strap** the mould with rubber bands between boards, pour through the
  sprue, tilt to let air out of vents.
- **Hollow casts** (lighter, cheaper): pour in a little resin, close, and rotate by
  hand (**rotocasting**) until it gels; repeat with a second coat.
- Demould when cool; trim sprue and seam with a knife and sanding sponge.

## 6. Moisture and bubbles
Polyurethane resins react with **moisture**: damp moulds, humid air or a wet stir
stick give a foamed, bubbly cast. Keep containers closed, work below about 60%
relative humidity, and dry the mould between casts. A **pressure pot** (around 3–4
bar / 40–60 psi) shrinks remaining bubbles (unconfirmed — follow the pot's
rating).

## Gotchas
- **Silicone stays tacky**: cure inhibition or wrong ratio; never eyeball by
  volume if the sheet says weight.
- **Halves bond together**: no release on part one.
- **Flashing at the seam**: mould not strapped evenly.
- **Cast short in a pocket**: no vent at the high point.
- **Mould tears**: walls thinner than about 10 mm, or demoulding too early.

## Safety
Polyurethane resins contain isocyanates; mix with nitrile gloves, eye protection
and good ventilation; follow the supplier's SDS and use a respirator if specified.
Never cast resin against skin. Uncured resin is hazardous waste; cure it off before
disposal.

## Verify
- [ ] Platinum silicone tested against the master and clay before the pour.
- [ ] Mould halves separate cleanly and register by the keys.
- [ ] Mix ratio weighed per the data sheet.
- [ ] Casts are bubble-free, fully formed and within expected weight.
- [ ] Copies are interchangeable for the run, with seams cleaned off.
