# Slip, sgraffito and mishima inlay

**Goal:** crisp decoration that stays bonded through drying and firing. The variable
that decides success is **timing** — the moisture of the pot when slip goes on and
when you carve.

## Coloured slip
- Make slip from the **same clay body** (or a slip formulated to match its shrinkage),
  dried, crushed, slaked in water and sieved (60–80 mesh). Matching shrinkage is what
  keeps slip from flaking.
- Colour with commercial stains (often 5–15%) or oxides (iron, cobalt at around
  0.5–2%, copper); test-tile first — colours shift a lot with glaze over the top and
  with firing atmosphere.
- Consistency: thick cream for dipping and brushing; thicker for trailing.

## Applying slip
- Apply to **soft leather-hard** (firm enough to handle without denting, still cool
  and darker in colour). Too wet: the pot slumps under the added water. Too dry:
  slip doesn't bond and cracks or flakes off later.
- Brush 2–3 coats, letting each lose its shine before the next, or pour/dip for an even
  coat. Aim for opacity — a thin slip carves into streaky lines.
- Cover loosely with plastic so the slip and body equalise in moisture.

## Sgraffito (scratching through)
- Carve when the slip has lost its shine and is **leather-hard**: it should come off
  in clean curls, not smear (too wet) or chip and powder (too dry).
- Draw the design lightly with a soft pencil (burns out) or a blunt point.
- Tools: loop tools and ribbon tools for removing areas, a sharpened needle or
  dental pick for lines, a stylus for curves. Keep tools sharp.
- Brush crumbs away with a soft dry brush when they've dried — wiping wet crumbs
  smears colour into the background.
- Leave decorated work to dry slowly to stop slip curling at edges.

## Mishima (inlay)
The reverse of sgraffito: incise lines, fill with contrasting slip, scrape back.
1. At leather-hard, incise fine lines with a needle tool or V-tool.
2. Brush or squeeze thick coloured slip (or underglaze) into the lines, overfilling.
3. When the slip is leather-hard again, scrape the surface with a metal **rib** or
   blade until the lines show crisp and the surrounding clay is clean. Finish with a
   damp (not wet) sponge if needed.
4. Shallow, even lines give the cleanest result; deep wide lines crack as slip shrinks.

## Slip trailing
- Trail thick slip from a bulb or bottle with a nozzle; keep a constant pressure and
  distance. Practise on newspaper first.
- Feathering: trail parallel lines on a still-wet slip ground, then drag a fine point
  across them.

## Under the glaze
- A clear or transparent glaze over slip decoration usually intensifies colour.
  Opaque glazes hide it. Bisque first if your studio dips raw ware.

## Gotchas
- Flaking at bisque or glaze = shrinkage mismatch or slip applied too dry.
  Adjust with a slip recipe designed for application at leather-hard/bone-dry.
- Underglaze manufacturers' application stages differ; read the label.
- Moisture figures here (soft leather-hard etc.) are judged by feel; there's no
  replacement for a test pot.

## Verify
- [ ] Slip made from or matched to the body; colour test-tiled under the intended glaze.
- [ ] Slip applied at soft leather-hard to opacity.
- [ ] Sgraffito lines cut cleanly without smearing; crumbs brushed dry.
- [ ] Mishima lines scraped crisp with clean surrounding clay.
- [ ] No lifting at edges after bisque.
