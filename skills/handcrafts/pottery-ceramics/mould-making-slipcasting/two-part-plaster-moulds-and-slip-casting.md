# Two-part plaster moulds and slip casting

**Goal:** a plaster mould that releases cleanly and casts repeatable pieces with even
walls. Plaster absorbs water from the slip, building a clay layer on the mould wall;
you pour out the excess when the wall is thick enough.

## 1. The model
- Model in clay, plaster, wood, or 3D print (seal porous models). It must have **no
  undercuts** relative to the parting line, or the mould will lock.
- Add a **spare** (a funnel-shaped reservoir) at the opening; it becomes the pour
  hole and is trimmed off later.

## 2. Find the parting line
- The parting line runs along the widest silhouette so each half pulls straight off.
  Mark it with a pencil, looking down each pull direction for undercuts.
- Bed the model on its side in soft clay exactly up to that line. Smooth the clay
  bed flat and clean against the model.
- Press **natches** (registration keys) into the clay bed — a coin twisted or a natch
  tool — so the halves locate.

## 3. Pour the first half
- Build a **cottle** (wall) of boards, lino or plastic sheet around the bed, leaving
  ~25–40 mm of plaster thickness around the model. Seal seams with clay — leaks are
  sudden and messy.
- Mix **pottery plaster** by weight: typical studio ratios are around 100 parts plaster
  to 65–75 parts water (consistency); follow the bag. Lower water = harder, less
  absorbent mould.
- **Sift** plaster onto the water, let it **slake** 1–3 min untouched, then mix
  without whipping in air for ~2–3 min until it thickens like cream. Pour into a
  corner, not onto the model, and tap the table to release bubbles.
- Never pour plaster or plaster wash water down a sink — it sets in the drain. Let it
  settle in a bucket and bin the solids.

## 4. Second half
- Once set (warm, then cool), remove the clay bed but leave the model in the plaster.
- Apply **mould soap** (size) to the plaster face in 3–4 thin coats, sponging off
  excess each time, until water beads. Unsoaped faces bond permanently.
- Cottle and pour the second half. Separate after setting; clean the model out.

## 5. Dry the mould
- Dry thoroughly — days to a couple of weeks in warm, airy conditions — and keep below
  about 50°C (plaster weakens if overheated). A wet mould casts slowly and poorly.

## 6. Slip and casting
- Casting slip is **deflocculated** (sodium silicate, Darvan or similar) so it stays
  fluid with far less water. Buy ready-made to start, or follow a tested recipe; add
  deflocculant drop by drop — too much makes slip thicken again (over-deflocculation).
- Check **specific gravity** (weigh a known volume): often around 1.75–1.80 for
  earthenware/stoneware casting slips — figure varies, use the supplier's.
- Band the mould closed with straps or rubber bands, fill to the top of the spare, and
  top up as level drops. Typical wait 10–20 min; check thickness by scraping at the spare.
- Drain upside down at an angle until it stops dripping, then let the cast firm until
  it shrinks from the mould wall (it shows a gap at the spare).
- Demould, trim the spare with a knife, and **fettle** the seam line at leather-hard or
  dry with a knife and damp sponge. Sponge seams lightly; heavy sponging exposes grog.

## Gotchas
- Pinholes or "casting spots": dirty mould, air in slip (sieve and rest it), or
  over-wet mould.
- Uneven wall: slip not stirred (it settles) or mould unevenly dry.
- Silica in dry slip dust: wet-clean.
- Ratios above are typical figures from common studio practice; consult your plaster
  and slip supplier's data sheet.

## Verify
- [ ] Model checked for undercuts; natches cut; second face soaped.
- [ ] Plaster mixed by weight, slaked, poured bubble-free.
- [ ] Mould fully dry before first cast.
- [ ] Slip sieved, SG checked; cast wall even when sectioned.
