# Bisque and glaze firing an electric kiln

**Goal:** ware that comes out sound, glazes matured to the right cone, and a kiln
that lasts. Most studio ware gets two firings: **bisque** (makes it handleable and
absorbent for glazing) and **glaze** (melts the glaze).

## Cones, not just temperature
Pyrometric cones measure **heat-work** (temperature × time), which is what matures
clay and glaze. A controller's thermocouple reads temperature only. Put a **cone
pack** (e.g. one cone below, the target, one above, set in a pad of clay at the
manufacturer's angle) where you can see it or find it after firing. Bent target cone
with tip touching the base level ≈ correct. Check every few firings and whenever
results change.

## Bisque firing
- Ware must be **bone dry** (room temperature to the touch on your cheek; no cool
  damp feel). Damp ware can burst as water turns to steam.
- Loading: pieces may touch and nest (bowls inside bowls, rim to rim) as long as weight
  is supported. Keep 2–3 cm from elements.
- Typical schedule to cone 04–06 (manufacturer schedules vary; this is an example):
  - **Candle**: hold around 80–90 °C for several hours or overnight with the lid
    propped or vent on if work may be damp or thick.
  - Slow ramp (~50–100 °C/h) through to ~600 °C — chemically combined water leaves
    and quartz inversion happens near 573 °C.
  - Moderate ramp to peak; a short hold.
  - Venting during bisque removes carbon and sulphur burn-out fumes; carbon
    trapped in thick work can cause bloating and black cores.

## Glaze firing
- **Clean the foot**: wipe glaze off the bottom and lower ~5 mm of the foot; drips that
  touch a shelf weld the pot to it.
- Pieces must **not touch** each other — glaze fuses them together.
- Shelves coated with **kiln wash** (alumina/kaolin mix) on the top face only; never
  the underside (flakes fall onto glazed ware below).
- Use three posts per shelf (a stable tripod), stacked directly above the posts on the
  shelf below.
- Typical mid-fire schedule to cone 6 (example only): ~100 °C/h to ~600 °C, faster to
  about 100 °C below peak, slow final ~60 °C/h to peak, optional 10–15 min hold.
  Programmed slow cooling through 1000–750 °C develops mattes and crystals; fast
  cooling gives glossier surfaces.

## Cooling and unloading
- Don't open until well under about 100 °C (many wait until it's near room temperature
  at the elements). Opening hot causes **dunting** (cooling cracks) and crazing.
- Glaze shards and drips are glass-sharp; check shelves, grind off drips with a
  rubbing brick or grinder, re-wash bare spots.

## Kiln care and safety
- Ventilate: electric kilns emit fumes (and carbon monoxide in bisque). Use a downdraft
  vent or exhaust hood; never fire in living space without ventilation.
- Watch element condition and firing times — long firings that once were short mean
  elements are wearing; replace as a set.
- Don't leave a kiln unattended at start and end without an automatic shut-off and
  smoke alarm; the kiln exterior is hot enough to burn and ignite nearby materials.
  Keep the manufacturer's clearance from walls.

## Gotchas
- Schedules above are illustrative ranges, not your clay's; check clay and glaze
  manufacturer's recommended cone and follow your kiln manual.
- Thermocouple drift: if cones disagree with the controller, trust cones and apply a
  thermocouple offset.
- Low-fire glaze in a high-fire kiln load runs and destroys shelves.

## Verify
- [ ] Bisque ware bone dry; candled if any doubt.
- [ ] Glaze ware feet clean; no pieces touching; washed shelves top side only.
- [ ] Cone pack present and read after firing.
- [ ] Unloaded cool; drips ground off shelves; kiln vented throughout.
