# Western raku firing and post-fire reduction

**Goal:** pull glowing pots from a kiln at glaze maturity, smother them in combustibles,
and fix crackle, smoke and metallic effects — without breaking pots or burning people.
(This is the Western raku process; traditional Japanese raku for tea bowls differs in
clay, glazes and is usually not reduced in combustibles.)

## Clay and ware
- A clay body that tolerates **thermal shock**: heavily grogged, open stoneware or a
  dedicated raku body. Fine porcelain and dense bodies crack.
- Even wall thickness, no sharp internal corners; very wide flat forms crack more.
- Bisque fire first, as usual (to around cone 06–04).
- Raku ware stays porous and is **not food-safe** or watertight; decorative only.

## Glazes
- Low-temperature glazes that melt fast — typically around 900–1000 °C. Commercial raku
  glazes or recipes high in frit/borax.
- **Crackle**: a clear or white glaze that crazes on fast cooling; smoke enters the
  cracks and blackens them in reduction.
- **Copper matte / lustre**: copper-rich glazes that flash iridescent when reduced
  heavily and cooled quickly before re-oxidising.
- Unglazed areas turn black in reduction (carbon trapping) — part of the aesthetic.

## The firing
- Kiln: usually a lightweight fibre-lined kiln (often a lift-top or a drum) fired with
  a propane burner, outdoors.
- Pre-heat ware on top of the kiln to drive off moisture.
- Fire fast — often 30–60 min to temperature. Watch the glaze through the spy hole:
  it goes from bubbly and rough to **glossy and wet-looking** when mature. Draw
  rings or test tiles help. Many potters also use a pyrometer.
- Turn off the burner, open the kiln, and lift the pot with **raku tongs** gripping
  an unglazed area or where marks won't matter.

## Post-fire reduction
- Place the pot quickly in a metal bin (galvanised trash can) on a non-combustible
  surface, containing sawdust, shredded newspaper, straw or leaves.
- Let combustibles flame up for a few seconds (time affects crackle and copper colour),
  then **close the lid** to starve oxygen. Smoke and carbon now enter crackle and
  unglazed clay.
- Reduce 10–20 minutes (varies with effect wanted). Open to air briefly for some copper
  effects; quench in water to freeze copper colour (risky — increases cracking).
- Once cool enough to handle, scrub soot off with a scourer and water; a paste of
  cleanser lifts residues from glaze.

## Safety (non-negotiable)
- Outdoors, away from buildings and overhangs; check for fire bans.
- **Natural-fibre** clothing (synthetics melt onto skin), leather boots, long sleeves,
  heat-resistant gloves, face shield or safety glasses (IR-rated preferably). Tie hair back.
- Clear walking path from kiln to bin, rehearsed before the first pull.
- Fire extinguisher and water hose ready; never pour water on the propane burner area.
- Smoke from combustibles is heavy; stand upwind. Leak-check gas fittings with soapy water.

## Gotchas
- Pot cracks on removal: clay not raku-suited, ware damp, or uneven walls.
- No crackle: cooled too slowly before reduction, or glaze not crazing-prone.
- Copper turns plain green: re-oxidised; cut air faster or reduce harder.
- Temperatures above are common ranges, not universal; follow your glaze's guidance.

## Verify
- [ ] Raku-suitable clay, bisqued, preheated.
- [ ] Glaze judged mature (glossy) before pulling.
- [ ] Combustible bin and clear path prepared; PPE on; extinguisher ready.
- [ ] Reduction lidded; pot cleaned; marked as non-functional.
