# Knotting a macramé plant hanger

**Goal:** a symmetrical four-arm hanger that holds a pot level, built from the two
knots that carry almost all macramé — the **square knot** and the **half hitch** —
plus a **gathering (wrapping) knot**. Finished drop about 90–110 cm.

## 1. Materials
- 4–5 mm cotton macramé cord (single-strand or 3-ply). Single-strand gives a
  softer fringe; 3-ply is firmer and easier for beginners.
- **8 cords × 4 m** for a pot up to ~15 cm diameter, plus **2 × 1 m** for the
  wrapping knots. Rule of thumb: each working cord needs about **4× the finished
  length** of knotting it does, filler cords less (unconfirmed — knotting density
  changes this; cut extra on the first one).
- A 5–7 cm wooden or metal ring; a hook or clothes rail to hang the work from;
  tape measure; sharp scissors.

## 2. Top: ring and gathering knot
1. Thread all 8 cords through the ring and fold them in half: 16 ends, equal length.
2. Tie a **gathering knot** just below the ring: lay a 1 m cord along the bundle
   with a loop pointing down, wrap the long end tightly round the bundle and over
   the loop 8–10 times working downward, pass the end through the loop, then pull
   the top end until the loop disappears under the wraps. Trim both ends flush.
3. Hang the ring at eye height.

## 3. Arms (four groups of four)
Split the 16 ends into **4 groups of 4**. In each group the outer two are
**working cords**, the inner two are **filler cords**.
- **Square knot:** left cord over the fillers and under the right cord; right cord
  under the fillers and up through the left loop; pull. Then mirror it (right over,
  left under, through). The second half must mirror the first or you get a twist.
- **Half-knot spiral:** repeat only the first half; the sinnet twists into a spiral
  on its own. Let it turn — don't fight it.
For each arm: drop 10–15 cm from the gathering knot, tie **8–10 square knots** (or a
20–30 half-knot spiral), then leave a gap of 20–25 cm. Measure all four arms from the
ring so they stay equal.

## 4. Basket (alternating square knots)
1. Take 2 cords from one group and 2 from the neighbouring group; tie a square knot
   with them about **8–10 cm** below the arm sinnets.
2. Repeat round all four junctions.
3. Drop another 6–8 cm and alternate again, rejoining the original groups. Two or
   three rows make a net that cradles the pot.
4. Test-fit the pot now: the net should close under the base.

## 5. Bottom gather and fringe
1. Gather all 16 ends just below the pot base and tie a second **gathering knot**.
2. Trim the fringe to 15–25 cm. Unravel and comb ply cords if you want a brushed
   tassel; seal synthetic cord with a lighter instead.

## Faults
- **Sinnet twists when you wanted it flat:** halves of the square knot not
  alternating. Every knot starts from the side the last one ended.
- **Pot tilts:** arms unequal; measure from the ring, not from the previous knot.
- **Run out of cord on one arm:** working cords use far more than fillers; swap
  working and filler roles halfway, or start working cords longer.
- **Lumpy texture:** uneven tension — pull each half with the same force, keeping
  fillers taut.

## Safety
- Check the ring and ceiling hook for the full wet weight of pot, soil and water
  (a watered 15 cm pot can exceed 3 kg). Use a ceiling joist, not plasterboard alone.

## Verify
- [ ] 16 ends of equal length after folding through the ring.
- [ ] Gathering knots tight with no loop end showing.
- [ ] Square-knot sinnets lie flat; all four arms equal length from the ring.
- [ ] Alternating rows close beneath the pot; pot hangs level.
- [ ] Hook rated for the watered weight.
