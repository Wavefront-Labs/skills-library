# Square, diagonal and shear lashings

**Goal:** joints between poles that stay tight and rigid — a camp table, a
gateway, an A-frame or a pair of shear legs — using three lashings. The pattern is
always the same: **start hitch → wrapping turns → frapping turns → finish hitch**.

## 1. Materials
- Natural-fibre rope (sisal, manila) of **6–8 mm** grips poles well; slick
  polypropylene tends to slip. Synthetic is fine if you add more frapping.
- Rope length rule of thumb: about **1 m of rope per 25 mm (1 in) of combined pole
  diameter** for a square lashing — e.g. two 75 mm poles ≈ 6 m (a scouting
  pioneering rule; unconfirmed, cut a little long).
- Peeled, sound poles. Notch only if you must; it weakens the spar.

## 2. Square lashing (poles crossing at right angles)
1. **Start:** a **clove hitch** on the upright pole **below** the crossbar (on the
   side the load will push the crossbar toward). Twist the short end round the
   standing part.
2. **Wrapping turns:** take the rope up over the crossbar, behind the upright, down
   over the crossbar on the other side, and behind the upright again. Each turn
   goes **outside** the previous one on the crossbar and **inside** on the upright,
   so turns don't cross on top of each other. Make **3–4 full wraps**, each pulled tight.
3. **Frapping turns:** pass between the two poles and wind **2–3 turns round the
   wrapping turns** themselves, hauling hard on each. Frapping draws everything tight.
4. **Finish:** a clove hitch on the crossbar (or two half hitches round a pole).
   Tuck the tail.

## 3. Diagonal lashing (poles crossing but not touching, or bracing a frame)
1. **Start:** a **timber hitch** round **both** poles diagonally, pulled tight to
   draw the poles together.
2. **Wrapping:** 3–4 turns along the same diagonal, then 3–4 turns across the
   **other** diagonal.
3. **Frapping:** 2–3 turns between the poles round the wraps.
4. **Finish** with a clove hitch on either pole. Use this for the cross-braces of a
   trestle or anywhere the poles spring apart.

## 4. Shear (sheer) lashing for shear legs and A-frames
1. Lay two poles side by side. **Clove hitch** round one pole.
2. Take **8–10 loose wrapping turns** round both poles — loose enough that the poles
   can open into an A afterwards (a hand's width of slack overall is typical).
3. **Frap** 2 turns between the poles, not too tight, and finish with a clove hitch
   on the other pole.
4. Open the legs to the working angle. For shear legs, keep the angle modest —
   legs spread about **one-third of their height** at the base is a common guide
   (unconfirmed for your load; get a competent rigger for anything heavy).
5. For a **tripod**, lash three poles with a figure-eight weave between them and
   cross the outer legs over.

## 5. Tensioning and checking
- Lean your weight on the joint; it should not rotate or slip. Re-frap if it moves.
- Natural fibre loosens as it dries and stretches; re-tighten lashings after rain
  and the next morning.
- Guy shear legs and tall structures to ground anchors (pegs in a 1-2-3 holdfast).

## Faults
- **Joint twists:** clove hitch started on the wrong side, or too few frapping turns.
- **Wraps ride over each other:** not keeping outside/inside order on each pole.
- **Shear legs won't open:** wraps too tight; leave slack before frapping.
- **Rope too short at the finish:** start with the short end long enough for the hitch.

## Safety
- Pioneering structures are for light loads. People-carrying towers, bridges and
  swings need trained supervision, rated spars and inspection.
- Test every structure with a gradual load before use; stand clear of shear legs
  while raising them.

## Verify
- [ ] Correct lashing for the joint: square (crossing, touching), diagonal
      (springing apart), shear (opening A-frame/legs).
- [ ] 3–4 wrapping turns lie neatly side by side, not crossing.
- [ ] 2–3 frapping turns hauled tight between the poles.
- [ ] Started and finished with secure hitches; tails tucked.
- [ ] Joint holds under body-weight test; re-checked after drying out.
