# Choosing and tying knots for load and release

**Goal:** for any everyday job, choose a knot that holds under the real load, does
not shake loose when slack, and can be untied afterwards — and tie it correctly
first time. Twelve knots cover nearly everything; knowing *why* each one is chosen
matters more than knowing two hundred.

## 1. Ask four questions first
1. **What is it doing?** Joining two ropes (**bend**), tying to an object (**hitch**),
   making a fixed eye (**loop**), or holding things together (**binding**).
2. **Load direction:** along the rope, or sideways along a pole/rail?
3. **Must it release** after heavy load, or under load?
4. **Rope:** slippery synthetic (Dyneema, polyester braid), stiff, or very different
   diameters?

## 2. The working set
| Job | First choice | When to switch |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed loop, general | **Bowline** | Slippery rope or cyclic loading → add a stopper / use a **Yosemite finish**, or tie a **figure-eight loop** |
| Loop that must stay jammed-proof after heavy load | **Alpine butterfly** (also mid-line) | — |
| Join similar ropes | **Zeppelin bend** or **double fisherman's** (permanent) | — |
| Join unequal ropes / rope to a loop | **Sheet bend**; **double sheet bend** if the diameters differ a lot or rope is slick | — |
| Tie to a ring, rail or post, any angle | **Round turn and two half hitches** | — |
| Quick temporary hitch to a rail | **Clove hitch** | Unreliable on its own if the object rotates; add half hitches |
| Pull along a pole or another rope | **Rolling hitch** (turns toward the load) | — |
| Adjustable tensioning line (tent guy) | **Taut-line hitch** | — |
| Tensioning a load down | **Trucker's hitch** finished with two half hitches | — |
| Bind a sack neck or hold a whipping | **Constrictor knot** (very hard to untie; cut it) | — |
| Tie a parcel / reef a sail | **Reef (square) knot** — **never** as a bend joining two ropes under load | — |

## 3. Tying correctly
- **Leave a tail** of at least 10–12 rope diameters on loops and bends (more on
  slick synthetics).
- **Dress** the knot: lay every part where it belongs, no crossed strands that
  should be parallel (a figure-eight with a twisted pair looks right but isn't).
- **Set** it: pull each strand in turn, then load the standing part. An unset knot
  can capsize into a slip knot.
- **Slipped versions** (finish with a bight) give quick release on reef knots,
  hitches and bindings — not on life-safety loops.

## 4. Strength and selection rules of thumb
- Every knot weakens the rope, commonly quoted at **roughly 20–50% loss**; sharp
  turns weaken most. A round turn over a large post spreads load; a tight bend over
  a thin rail does not (published figures vary widely — treat as approximate).
- Splices (see splicing-ropework skills) are stronger than knots for permanent eyes.
- High-modulus ropes (Dyneema/Spectra) slip in many common knots; use knots
  approved for them or splice.

## 5. Faults
- **Bowline shakes loose** when unloaded and flogged: back it up or use a figure-eight.
- **Reef knot spills** when one end is jerked — it is a binding knot, not a bend.
- **Clove hitch rolls** off a rotating object.
- **Granny knot** (both crosses the same way) instead of reef: it slips or jams.
- **Jammed figure-eight**: expected after big loads; choose a bowline or butterfly
  if you need to untie it.

## Safety
- For climbing, arborist work, lifting people or rated rigging, follow that
  discipline's training and approved knots; this skill is general-purpose work.
- Stand clear of the line of pull when loading a knot for the first time.

## Verify
- [ ] Knot named for the job (bend / hitch / loop / binding) and load direction.
- [ ] Tail ≥ 10–12 diameters; knot dressed with no crossed parts.
- [ ] Set by hand before loading, then checked again after first load.
- [ ] Reef knot not used to join two load-bearing ropes.
- [ ] Release requirement met: test-untie after a typical load on a practice piece.
