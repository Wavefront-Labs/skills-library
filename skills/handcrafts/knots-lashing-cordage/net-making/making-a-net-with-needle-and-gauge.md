# Making a net with needle and gauge

**Goal:** an even diamond-mesh net of planned size — a bag net, garden net or
hammock body — tied by hand with every mesh the same size and every knot locked.
Mending damaged commercial netting is a separate skill; this is building from nothing.

## 1. Kit and sizing
- **Netting needle (shuttle):** plastic or wooden, narrower than the gauge so it
  passes through a mesh. Load by passing twine round the tongue and down the
  heel slot alternately until it is full but still slides freely.
- **Gauge (mesh stick):** a flat stick whose **width** sets half the mesh. A gauge
  of width *w* gives a **bar** (one side, knot to knot) of about *w* and a
  **stretched mesh** (knot to opposite knot, pulled closed) of about **2w**.
- **Twine:** 1–2 mm braided nylon or tarred twine for durable nets; cotton for
  decorative work.
- Twine use: allow roughly **1.5–2× the stretched-mesh length per mesh** plus knot
  allowance (unconfirmed — tie a 10 × 10 sample and weigh or measure what it used).

## 2. Starting row
1. Tie a foundation loop of cord to a fixed point (a hook or a chair back).
2. Tie the twine to it. Hold the gauge **under** the twine just below the
   foundation, take the needle down in front of the gauge, round behind it, and
   up through the foundation loop from the front.
3. Pull the twine tight so the gauge rises snugly against the foundation; pinch
   the crossing with your thumb.
4. Tie the **mesh knot (sheet bend)** — throw a loop of twine over to the left,
   pass the needle behind both legs of the mesh above, and out through the loop
   you threw. Pull down firmly so the knot locks *on* the gauge edge.
5. Repeat for the number of meshes wide you want. Slide the full gauge out
   occasionally; keep the last 2–3 loops on it.

## 3. Working back and forth
- At the end of a row, turn the work (or reverse direction) and net into the
  bottom loops of the previous row — one mesh into each loop.
- **Every knot is tied tight against the gauge.** Mesh size comes from the gauge,
  not from your eye.
- To join a new needleful, tie it on with a sheet bend **at a mesh knot**, never
  mid-bar.

## 4. Shaping
- **Increase (creasing):** tie **two** meshes into one loop of the previous row —
  the net widens by one mesh. Spread increases evenly, e.g. one at each end of every
  row for a fan shape.
- **Decrease:** take the needle through **two** loops of the previous row together
  and tie one knot over both.
- **Cylinder / bag net:** start on a ring of meshes, join the first and last mesh,
  and work round in a spiral; increase to widen the mouth, decrease to close the base.
- A square-mesh panel (meshes at 90° to the edge) is made by starting at a corner
  with 2 meshes, increasing at the end of every row to the width wanted, then
  decreasing to finish (corner-to-corner method).

## 5. Mounting
1. Thread a **head rope** (or frame cord) through the top row of meshes.
2. The **hanging ratio** controls how open the meshes sit: rope length ÷ stretched
   netting length. Around **0.5** gives roughly square-looking open diamonds; higher
   values pull meshes narrow (values from fishing-net practice — check for your use).
3. Seize the netting to the rope every 2–4 meshes with a clove hitch and half hitch.

## Faults
- **Knots slip into loose loops:** the throw went the wrong way or the knot was
  not pulled down hard on the gauge edge; it must look like a sheet bend, not a
  half hitch.
- **Uneven meshes:** gauge removed too soon or knots tied off the gauge.
- **Net narrows unintentionally:** a loop skipped; count meshes every row.
- **Needle won't pass through:** overfilled; load less.

## Safety
- Nets for climbing, cargo lifting or fall protection must be certified products.
- Store finished nets away from children and wildlife; birds and hedgehogs tangle in
  loose garden netting — keep it taut and anchored.

## Verify
- [ ] Gauge width chosen for the target stretched mesh (≈ 2× gauge width).
- [ ] Every knot is a locked sheet bend tied on the gauge.
- [ ] Mesh count checked each row; increases/decreases placed as planned.
- [ ] Joins made at knots, not mid-bar.
- [ ] Net mounted at a deliberate hanging ratio and seized at regular intervals.
