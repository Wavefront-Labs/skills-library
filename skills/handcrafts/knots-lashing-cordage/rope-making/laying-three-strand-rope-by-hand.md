# Laying three-strand rope by hand

**Goal:** a firm, balanced three-strand rope that lies straight without kinking or
unlaying when you let go — made from bought yarn or twine on a home ropewalk. The
same principle (twist one way, lay the other) underlies every laid rope and the
two-ply reverse-wrap cord made from plant fibre.

## 1. The principle
- **Yarns** are twisted one way, **strands** (several yarns) the opposite way, and
  the **rope** is laid the opposite way again. Each stage's twist locks the one
  inside it. In standard right-hand (Z-laid) rope the strands carry S (left-hand)
  twist and the yarns Z twist. In practice: crank all strands one way, and the rope
  lays up the **opposite** way of its own accord as the strands try to unwind.
- **Hardness of lay** comes from how much forming twist goes into the strands. Too
  little → soft, stretchy rope that opens; too much → kinks (hockles) and is weak.

## 2. Kit
- A **twister/rope machine**: three hooks geared to turn together at one end (a
  plywood plate with three cranked hooks driven by one handle works), and a single
  **swivel hook** at the far end on a sledge or weighted trolley.
- A **top** (laying top): a grooved wooden cone or plate with three grooves that sits
  between strands and keeps them apart as they lay up.
- Yarn: baler twine, cotton or sisal string. Length of walk: allow the finished rope
  to be roughly **20–30% shorter** than the warped strands (take-up varies with lay
  hardness; unconfirmed for your material — make a 1 m test first).

## 3. Warping
1. Fix the swivel end so it can slide toward the machine under light tension
   (weight the sledge or have a helper hold it).
2. Run yarn from each of the three machine hooks to the far swivel hook and back,
   so each strand has the same number of yarns (e.g. 3 yarns per strand for a
   ~10 mm rope from baler twine; adjust to taste).
3. Tension all strands equally — uneven strands make a rope where one strand spirals
   around the others ("corkscrew").

## 4. Forming the strands
1. Hold the far swivel **locked** (a pin or your hand) so it cannot turn.
2. Crank the machine so all three hooks turn clockwise. The strands shorten as they
   twist; let the sledge creep toward you, keeping steady tension.
3. Twist until a strand, slackened slightly, tries to kink back on itself in a tight
   loop. As a starting figure, strands shrink about 10–15% of their length during
   forming (unconfirmed; depends on yarn).

## 5. Laying up
1. Insert the **top** at the swivel end with one strand in each groove.
2. Release the swivel. The strands immediately begin to lay up behind the top.
3. Keep cranking in the **same** direction (this keeps the strands hard as they
   untwist into the lay) while a helper walks the top slowly toward the machine.
   Do not push the top — let the laying rope drive it.
4. Hold the top back slightly; this controls the angle of lay. Aim for a lay angle
   of about 40–45° to the rope axis for a general-purpose rope.
5. At the machine, stop, take the strands off the hooks together and whip
   immediately (or knot), then whip the swivel end.

## 6. Finishing
- Hang the rope under light load for a day to settle.
- Roll it under a board or your foot on the floor to even the lay.
- Singe stray fibres on natural rope; heat-seal ends on synthetics.

## Faults and fixes
- **Rope unlays when released:** strands under-twisted, or too much laying twist.
- **Rope kinks / hockles:** too much forming twist; shorten next time or crank less.
- **One strand prominent:** unequal yarn count or tension in that strand.
- **Loose and soft in the middle:** top walked too fast; slow down, hold it back.
- **Sledge jerks forward:** tension too low; add weight.

## Safety
- Twisted strands store energy; if a hook slips the strand whips back. Keep faces
  away from the line and wear eye protection with stiff fibres.
- Homemade rope has **no rated strength**. Never use it for climbing, lifting
  people or overhead loads.

## Verify
- [ ] Each strand has the same yarn count and was warped at even tension.
- [ ] Strands were formed with the swivel locked, and twisted until they just kink.
- [ ] Rope laid up behind a top with the crank turning the same way as for forming.
- [ ] Finished rope lies straight on the floor with no corkscrew or hockles.
- [ ] Both ends whipped; rope labelled "not for life-safety use".
