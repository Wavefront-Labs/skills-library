# Splicing an eye in double-braid rope

**Goal:** a neat, strong eye in core-and-cover (double-braid) polyester rope — the
usual sheet and halyard rope on small boats — that keeps most of the rope's strength
where a knot would lose a third or more. Three-strand eye splicing is covered in
seamanship skills; this is the braided-rope version, which is harder and less forgiving.

## 1. Kit
- **Tubular fid** sized to the rope diameter (fid sets are marked by diameter; the
  fid length — "one fid length" — and its "short section" are the measuring units
  of this method) and a **pusher**, or a wire fid for small line.
- Marker pen, tape, sharp knife, sail needle and waxed whipping twine.
- 10–12 mm rope is the easiest size to learn on.

## 2. Marking (one common sequence — check your rope maker's diagram)
1. Tape the end tightly. Measure **one fid length** from the end and mark **R**.
2. Form the eye to the size wanted from R and mark **X** on the standing part.
3. Tie a loose slip knot about **five fid lengths** past X to stop core and cover
   sliding apart beyond the work area.
4. At X, open the cover strands with the fid and pull the core out. Pull all the
   core out of the tail section and tape its end.
5. Slide the cover back toward the knot and smooth it; mark the core where it now
   exits the cover as **mark 1**.
6. Slide the cover back down. From mark 1 measure **one short fid section** toward
   the core end: **mark 2**. From mark 2 measure **one full fid length plus one short
   section**: **mark 3**. (Sequence and distances recalled from manufacturer
   instructions — unconfirmed; follow your rope's own splice sheet.)

## 3. Cover into core
1. Fit the fid on the taped cover tail. Insert it into the core at **mark 2** and
   exit at **mark 3**. Pull the cover through until R meets mark 2.
2. **Taper the cover tail:** from where it exits the core, cut out strands in
   pairs at intervals over about one fid length so it ends in a point; this keeps
   the splice from showing a lump.

## 4. Core into cover
1. Fit the fid on the core tail. Insert it into the cover at **R** and run it
   through the cover, past X, exiting a short way beyond — pull the core through
   until the **crossover** (where core enters cover and cover enters core) is snug.
2. Smooth out the crossover so there is no gap or bunching.

## 5. Bury and milk
1. Hold the crossover and **milk** the cover down from the eye toward the
   standing part — firmly, hand over hand. The tapered cover tail disappears inside
   the core, then the crossover disappears inside the cover.
2. Keep milking until the eye forms and the cover bunch is smoothed out all the way
   to the slip knot. Untie the knot and milk the bunching out of the standing part.
3. Trim the exposed core tail flush where it exits; it will draw back inside.

## 6. Lock and finish
- **Lock-stitch** the throat: sew through the splice several times at 90° offsets
  with waxed twine, about 5–10 times the diameter long, to stop the splice working
  loose when unloaded.
- Add a **seizing** at the throat if a thimble is fitted.
- Whip the other end of the rope with a **sailmaker's or palm-and-needle whipping**.

## Faults
- **Cover won't bury:** cover not tapered, or milked from the wrong end; start at
  the crossover.
- **Gap at the crossover:** core pulled too far; ease it back before burying.
- **Eye smaller than planned:** X marked too close to R; the eye shrinks a little
  on milking.
- **Core bunching inside cover:** slip knot left in too long or cover not smoothed
  back before marking mark 1.

## Safety
- A splice in an unknown or damaged rope is no stronger than the rope; retire
  UV-faded or glazed line.
- Life-safety and rated-lifting splices must be made to the manufacturer's procedure
  and inspected by a competent person.

## Verify
- [ ] Marks R, X, 1, 2, 3 placed before any cutting; fid size matched to rope.
- [ ] Cover tail tapered over about one fid length.
- [ ] Crossover snug with no gap; whole splice milked smooth, no lump.
- [ ] Throat lock-stitched; free end whipped.
- [ ] Test-loaded by hand (or with a tackle) — the eye does not slip or open.
