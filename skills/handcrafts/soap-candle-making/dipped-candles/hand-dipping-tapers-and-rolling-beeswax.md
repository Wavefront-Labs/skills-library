# Hand-dipping tapers and rolling beeswax

**Goal:** straight, evenly tapering candles built layer by layer, and quick rolled
beeswax candles. Dipping is rhythm and temperature control. Draft: practice
summarised; dip temperatures depend on your wax.

## 1. Setup
- A **tall dipping vat** (deeper than the candle length) in a water bath or on
  a thermostatic heater. Never heat wax directly over flame; wax is flammable above
  its flash point.
- Wax: **beeswax** (traditional, hard, long-burning; pure beeswax can need a slightly
  hotter vat), or a taper/dipping paraffin with stearin.
- Wick: **flat braid** or **square braid** sized for taper diameter (around 2 cm at
  the base for a standard taper). Cut a length for a **pair**: loop over a stick or
  ring so two tapers hang either side, weighted at the bottom (a nut or washer)
  for the first dips to keep them straight.

## 2. Temperature
- Dip temperature around 70-75 °C is often quoted for paraffin-type dipping waxes;
  beeswax often a little higher. Too hot: each dip melts off the previous layer, and
  candles don't grow. Too cold: lumpy, layered, flaky surface, bubbles.
- Watch the result: a smooth build of a fraction of a millimetre per dip means
  you're in range.

## 3. Rhythm
1. **Prime** the wick with a first dip, then let it cool and pull it straight.
2. Dip smoothly in and out in **one steady movement** (about 2-3 seconds in total).
   Pausing in the vat melts previous layers.
3. **Cool** between dips — hang them for a minute or two, or dip in cool water
   (then blot dry — water droplets cause pits; many beeswax dippers avoid water
   entirely).
4. Repeat. A taper takes **dozens of dips**.
5. Cut off the weight after the first few dips once the wicks hang straight.
6. Trim drips off the bottom regularly with a warm knife so the base doesn't get a
   blob.

## 4. Shape and finish
- Tapers form naturally because the bottom is in the wax longest. For a cleaner taper,
  dip a little less deep for the last few dips at the top end — or not; this varies
  by maker.
- For a glossy finish: last dip in hotter wax, then quick cool.
- Cut the base square with a warm knife, or melt it flat on a warm pan, while the
  candle is still slightly warm. Hang to cure for a day or two.
- Colour: overdip white tapers a few times in coloured wax.

## 5. Rolled beeswax candles
- Warm a beeswax sheet (honeycomb-embossed) slightly with a hairdryer until pliable.
- Lay a primed wick along one edge, fold the edge tightly over it, then roll evenly
  and tightly keeping the bottom straight. Cut the sheet diagonally first for a
  tapered spiral look.
- Press the end edge in to seal. Trim wick ~1 cm. Loose rolling burns fast and drips.

## Safety
Hot wax burns. Keep a lid nearby to smother a wax fire — never water.
Thermometer in the vat at all times.

## Verify
- [ ] Vat at the temperature where each dip adds a thin, smooth layer.
- [ ] Wicks primed and hanging straight; bases trimmed during dipping.
- [ ] Even taper with no lumps or bubbles; base cut square.
- [ ] Rolled candles tight and even; wick centred.
