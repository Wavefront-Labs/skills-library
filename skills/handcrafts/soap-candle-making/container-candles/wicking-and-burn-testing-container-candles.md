# Wicking and burn testing container candles

**Goal:** a jar candle that melts to the edges each burn, doesn't tunnel, soot or
overheat its jar, and throws scent. Wick choice is the craft; suppliers' wick charts
are only starting points. Draft: general practice; follow wax and wick supplier data.

## 1. Wax
- **Soy** container waxes: popular, prone to frosting and wet spots, slower scent
  "hot throw" development, need a cure.
- **Coconut** and coconut-soy blends: creamy, good throw, softer.
- **Paraffin** container waxes: strong throw, smooth surfaces, petroleum-based.
- **Beeswax:** burns hot and bright; needs larger wicks; little fragrance tolerance.

## 2. Wick sizing
- Wick to the **jar's inside diameter** and the wax/fragrance/dye combination.
  Supplier charts give a range per series (CD, ECO, HTP, LX, wooden).
- Test **three sizes** around the chart's suggestion in identical jars.
- Wide jars (beyond ~9-10 cm) often need two or three wicks.
- Wick tab glued centred; wick held upright with a centring bar while setting.

## 3. Fragrance and temperatures
- **Fragrance load:** often 6-10% of wax weight (wax maker states maximum;
  candle-safe fragrance oils only; check the IFRA candle category). More isn't
  always stronger; overloading causes sweating and poor burn.
- Add fragrance at the wax maker's recommended temperature (often around 80-85 °C
  for soy), stir gently for **2 minutes** to bind.
- Pour temperature per wax (soy often ~55-65 °C). Warm jars (heat gun or oven at low)
  reduce wet spots and pull-away.

## 4. Pour and cure
- Pour slowly, leave a little headspace. Let set at stable room temperature without
  draughts.
- **Sinkholes** around the wick: poke relief holes and top off with a small second
  pour, or heat-gun the top to smooth.
- Cure: soy often 1-2 weeks for best hot throw; paraffin a few days.

## 5. Burn test (essential)
- Burn each test on a level surface away from draughts. Record per hour:
  melt-pool diameter and depth, flame height, soot, mushrooming, jar temperature.
- **First burn** should reach a full melt pool to the edge in about one hour per
  2.5 cm (inch) of diameter — the commonly quoted rule.
- **Too small** wick: tunnelling, small flame, drowning. **Too large:** high flickering
  flame (over ~5 cm), soot, deep melt pool (beyond ~1-1.5 cm), hot jar,
  mushrooming carbon. Pick the size that just reaches the edge.
- Test the whole candle to the end, not just one burn — wicks behave differently
  as the jar empties and the flame drops into the jar.

## 6. Labels and safety
Include safety warnings (never leave unattended, trim wick to ~5 mm, burn on heat-resistant
surface, stop with ~1 cm wax left). Relevant standards and regulation exist (e.g.
ASTM F2417 for fire safety labelling in the US; CLP labelling of fragrance hazards in
the EU/UK) — check what applies where you sell.

## Verify
- [ ] Wick tested in at least three sizes per wax/fragrance/jar combination.
- [ ] Fragrance at or below the wax's maximum, added at stated temperature.
- [ ] Full melt pool on first burn; flame height and jar temperature within limits.
- [ ] Burned to the end without tunnelling or sooting.
- [ ] Labels with safety warnings and required hazard statements.
