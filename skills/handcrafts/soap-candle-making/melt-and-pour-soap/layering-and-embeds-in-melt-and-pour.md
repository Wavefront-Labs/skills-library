# Layering and embeds in melt-and-pour

**Goal:** neat, bubble-free moulded soaps with clean layers and suspended embeds,
from a pre-saponified base — no lye handling. Good for beginners, gifts and design
work. Draft: common practice; follow your base supplier's temperatures.

## 1. Choose the base
- **Clear** base for embeds and jewel-like colour; **white/opaque**, goat's milk,
  shea, etc. for creamy looks.
- **Low-sweat** bases resist glycerin dew in humid climates.
- Suspension bases are thicker and hold botanicals/exfoliants without sinking.

## 2. Melting
- Cut into small cubes; melt in short microwave bursts (stirring between) or a double
  boiler. Many bases melt around 50-60 °C.
- **Don't boil:** overheating evaporates water, makes the base rubbery, cloudy, and
  yellows it.
- Pour around 55-60 °C (base-dependent): too hot melts embeds and lower layers;
  too cool gives seams and lumps.

## 3. Colour and fragrance
- **Micas** and soap-safe pigments disperse well (pre-mix in a little glycerin or
  alcohol). Food colouring and many dyes **migrate/bleed** into neighbouring layers.
  Some micas "morph" (shift) in high-pH soap — check the supplier says MP-stable.
- **Fragrance load:** often around 1-3% of base weight (check the fragrance's IFRA
  certificate for rinse-off soap). Overloading causes sweating and soft soap.
- **Vanillin**-containing fragrances turn soap tan to dark brown over time; stabilisers
  slow it, or use a white base and accept the colour.

## 4. Layering
1. Pour the first layer; spritz the surface with **isopropyl alcohol (~90%+)** to
   pop bubbles.
2. Wait until a firm skin forms but the layer is still warm (press: it holds a dent,
   doesn't stick).
3. **Spritz alcohol again** right before the next pour — this is what makes layers
   bond. Unspritzed layers peel apart.
4. Pour the next layer slightly cooler than the first.

## 5. Embeds
- Pre-make embeds (shapes, cut cubes) in contrasting colour; let them harden fully.
- Spritz embeds with alcohol, place on a thin first layer, and pour around them at
  the lower end of the pour range so they don't melt.
- Float problems: embeds rise in fresh hot base — anchor with a thin set layer first.

## 6. Unmould and wrap
- Unmould once fully hard (an hour for small moulds; freezer 10-15 min helps release
  — not longer, it causes sweating).
- **Wrap promptly** in plastic film or shrink wrap: glycerin pulls moisture from air
  and "sweats" beads on the surface.

## Gotchas
- Cloudy clear soap: overheated base, or too much fragrance/additive.
- Layers separating: no alcohol spritz or too cold.
- Botanicals (dried petals) can brown in soap; test first.

## Verify
- [ ] Base melted without boiling; poured within the supplier's range.
- [ ] Colours MP-stable; fragrance within the rinse-off limit.
- [ ] Alcohol spritz between every layer; layers don't peel.
- [ ] Embeds sharp, not melted; bars wrapped once hard.
