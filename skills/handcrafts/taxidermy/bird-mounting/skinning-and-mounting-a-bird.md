# Skinning and mounting a bird

**Goal:** a bird mount whose feathers lie in their natural tracts, with a body of
the right size and a pose the real bird could hold. Birds are far more fragile
than mammals — the skin is thin as tissue and tears easily. Draft: general method;
details vary (wrapped vs. carved vs. commercial foam bodies).

## 0. Law first
Most wild bird species are protected, and possession of them, their feathers or
mounts can be illegal without permits even for found dead birds. Legally taken game
birds (ducks, pheasant, etc.) and domestic species are the normal working material.
Check the law where you are.

## 1. Before skinning
- Freeze in a bag with the head tucked and wings folded; plug vent, mouth and wounds
  with cotton to stop fluids staining feathers.
- Note the measurements and **photograph** the bird — overall length, body length
  and girth, and the colour of soft parts (bill, legs, eyes), which fade fast.

## 2. Skinning
- Part the feathers along the breast keel to find the bare skin tract and make one
  incision there.
- Work the skin off with fingers and a blunt tool; use corn starch or sawdust to
  soak up fluids so feathers don't mat. Cut through the legs at the knee and the
  wings at the elbow, leaving the lower bones in the skin.
- Invert the skin over the neck and head; on big-headed ducks you may need a
  separate cut on the back of the head. Remove eyes, brain, tongue.
- Clean meat from wing and leg bones; on large birds open the wing from beneath
  to clear the secondary area.

## 3. Clean and preserve
- **Degrease** — waterfowl especially are fatty. Scrape fat, wash in a
  degreasing detergent solution, rinse, and dry the feathers with a blow-dryer
  or tumble in sawdust/corncob grit. Grease left in turns feathers yellow and
  greasy within months.
- Preserve with a dry preservative (borax-based) or a bird tan; the skin is too
  thin for long soak tanning.

## 4. Body and wiring
- **Wrapped body:** wrap excelsior (wood wool) and thread into a copy of the real
  body. **Carved body:** carve from foam. Or buy a commercial foam body matched to
  the species. Size it to the measurements, not to the floppy skin.
- Insert a neck of wire wrapped to the real neck thickness, pushed into the skull.
- **Leg wires** run up the back of the leg bones, through the foot, and into the
  body; **wing wires** support open or flying wings. Wires should be stiff enough
  to hold, soft enough to bend into pose.

## 5. Grooming and drying
- Close the incision, then pose. Set the feathers back into their tracts with
  a probe or tweezers; birds look "wrong" mainly because feather groups are out
  of place, not because of the body.
- Hold the pose while it dries: pins, card strips, or a light wrap of thread or
  gauze over the body. Dry slowly for a week or more before removing.
- Restore the colour of bill and feet with paint, referring to your photos.

## Verify
- [ ] Species legally held; reference photos taken.
- [ ] No tears at head, wing or tail areas; feathers dry, fluffy, grease-free.
- [ ] Body matches recorded measurements; neck thickness natural.
- [ ] Feather tracts lie flat with no gaps or bare patches.
- [ ] Bill and feet colours matched to reference after drying.
