# Epoxy finishing and colour restoration

**Goal:** take a fully dried mount and make the bare-skin areas (eyes, nose,
lips, inner ears) look alive again. Drying shrinks and darkens every thin, hairless
area; finishing rebuilds and repaints them. Draft: general practice; product names
are examples.

## 1. Wait until fully dry
Finishing a mount that is still drying means your epoxy cracks or lifts as the skin
moves. Thin areas dry in days; thick necks and bases of ears can take weeks.
Pressing the skin at the eye corner and feeling no coolness or give is a crude test.

## 2. Rebuild with epoxy sculpt
- Use a two-part **epoxy putty / sculpt** (Apoxie Sculpt-type) mixed to a uniform
  colour, with water or a lubricant on tools for smoothing.
- **Eyes:** fill the gap between eyelid and glass eye, model the tear duct (the
  front corner) and the eyelid rim; follow your reference photos — eye shape is
  where character lives.
- **Nose:** rebuild the shrunken edges of the nostril and the nose pad texture.
- **Lips:** fill the lip line so there's no dark gap.
- Keep epoxy off hair; clean stray bits with a damp brush before it sets.

## 3. Restore colour
- Seal the epoxy (it can stay slightly tacky) and paint with an airbrush in thin
  layers — skin tones under the hair at eyes and nose, the dark of the nose, the
  pink-to-grey inner ear.
- Reference, again: species and individual vary. Avoid a uniform "painted" look by
  fading colours rather than hard-edging them.
- Gloss: a little gloss on the nose and on the eye (tear wetness) reads as alive; too
  much reads as plastic. Many finishers use a gloss medium only on the eye and
  wet-nose areas.

## 4. Groom
Brush or blow-dry the hair in its natural direction, clean glue residue with a
damp cloth, and lightly brush fur so seam lines disappear. For birds, re-check
feather tracts and use a very soft brush.

## 5. Habitat and base
- Bases range from a simple wood panel to full groundwork. Groundwork can be a
  lightweight foam core coated in a mache or ground mix, textured, painted and
  dressed with real rock, dried or artificial plants and leaf litter.
- Use habitat appropriate to species and season; a duck mount on a desert base
  looks odd to every hunter who sees it.
- Avoid real plant material that harbours insects unless freeze-treated or dried.

## 6. Hand-over care notes
Advise the owner: keep out of direct sun (fades colour), away from heat and humidity,
dust with a feather duster or low-power blow-dryer, and watch for pests (fine dust
under the mount, loose hair) which indicate beetles or moths.

## Verify
- [ ] Mount fully dry before epoxy work.
- [ ] Eyes, nose, lips rebuilt to reference; no dark gaps.
- [ ] Colour faded naturally, gloss used only where alive skin is wet.
- [ ] Hair clean, seams invisible after grooming.
- [ ] Base habitat matches species and the customer has care notes.
