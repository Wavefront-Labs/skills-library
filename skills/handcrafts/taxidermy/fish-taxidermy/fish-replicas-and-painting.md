# Fish replicas and painting

**Goal:** a fish that reads as the angler's actual catch: correct size and shape,
and colour painted back convincingly, because a fish's colour fades within minutes
of death regardless of method. Draft: general approaches summarised.

## 1. Replica or skin mount?
- **Replica** (fibreglass or resin cast from a mould): the fish can be released;
  replicas are durable and don't yellow or leak oil. Most commercial trophy work
  today is replicas, often cast from a stock mould library matched to the angler's
  measurements.
- **Skin mount:** the real skin over a form. Fatty species (salmon, some
  catfish) tend to bleed oil and yellow over time; thin-skinned species can
  shrink and wrinkle. Every visible colour is still paint.

## 2. Data from the angler
- **Length** (nose to fork or tip of tail — record which) and **girth** at the widest
  point. A soft tape round the fish, or a string marked and measured later.
- **Photos** in good light, side-on, both sides if possible, plus close-ups of the
  head and fins. Colour reference photos are the single thing the painter needs most.

## 3. Moulding and casting a replica (from your own fish)
- Clean the fish of slime (salt scrub or a slime-cutting soap) so detail registers.
- Pose and support the fish in clay to the parting line, build a two-part mould in
  silicone (detail layer) backed by a rigid mother mould, or gelcoat-and-fibreglass
  for a classic glass mould.
- Cast fins separately in many cases; they're thin and need reinforcement (thin
  resin with fibre, or wire). Cast the body hollow in resin or fibreglass layup.
- Assemble, fill seams, and fair them to match the scale pattern. A sloppy seam
  line along the back is the most common replica giveaway.

## 4. Painting
Airbrush work in thin layers (see
`handcrafts.model-kit-building.airbrushing.thinning-pressure-and-control`):
1. Primer/base (often white or silver), then **pearl** or iridescent layer for
   the belly sheen.
2. Build dorsal colour from dark down, fading to the flanks.
3. Spots, bars and lateral line through stencils (scale netting gives scale texture).
4. **Candy / transparent** colours over the pearls give the depth fresh fish have.
5. Gill plate reds, eye detail (or a glass eye set in), fin edges.
6. Clear coat: gloss for a wet look or satin; many use a durable automotive-grade
   clear, sprayed with full protection.

## Gotchas
- Painting from memory or a generic picture gives a "generic fish". Paint from the
  angler's photos.
- Skin mounts must be thoroughly degreased and the skin thinned or the colour
  ruins in a year or two.
- Solvent paints and catalysed clear coats need an organic-vapour respirator and a
  booth; isocyanate-containing clears need supplied air in most guidance.

## Verify
- [ ] Length (and which length) and girth recorded; reference photos on file.
- [ ] Mould/blank matches measurements within a few millimetres.
- [ ] Seams invisible; scale pattern continuous over joins.
- [ ] Colour checked side by side against the reference photo under daylight.
- [ ] Clear coat even, no runs, and protection used while spraying.
