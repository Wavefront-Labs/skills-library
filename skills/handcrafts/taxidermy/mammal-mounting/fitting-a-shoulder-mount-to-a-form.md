# Fitting a shoulder mount to a form

**Goal:** a cape that fits its form without stretch marks or slack, with
symmetrical eyes, ears that stand naturally and a face that dries crisp, not
shrunken. Written around a deer-type shoulder mount on a polyurethane form; the
same logic applies to most game-head mounts. Draft: general practice, not a
single supplier's method.

## 1. Measure before you order the form
- Take **eye-to-nose** (front corner of the eye to the tip of the nose) and
  **neck circumference** behind the ears, on the carcass or the skinned cape
  before it shrinks. Suppliers' catalogues index forms by these.
- Choose pose (upright, semi-sneak, turn) and wall-pedestal vs. wall mount
  before ordering; turn direction must match the wall it will hang on.

## 2. Test fit and alter
- Rehydrate the tanned cape and drape it on the form dry. Look for: nose too long
  or short, neck too thick (cape won't close), or too thin (wrinkles and slack).
- **Too big:** rasp or carve the form down. **Too small:** build up with clay or
  epoxy putty, or a layer of mache. Rasp off the form's smooth skin so adhesive
  bites.
- Set **eyes** now: glass or acrylic eyes in clay, checked from the front and from
  above for symmetry and for the right forward angle. Uneven eyes are the most
  common reason a mount "looks wrong".

## 3. Ears and face
- Fit **ear liners** (thin plastic or fibreglass inserts) into the turned ears,
  bedded in ear-butt clay or adhesive so the skin bonds to the liner without voids.
- Put clay or mache into the nose and lips where the form under-fills them;
  you'll sculpt these to shape once the cape is on.

## 4. Glue and sew
- Coat the form in **hide paste / mounting adhesive** (thinner on the face, enough
  to stay workable), pull the cape on, and work it into position from the face back.
- Tuck lip skin into the lip slot, set eyelids over the eyes with a modelling tool,
  and align the cape's natural hair patterns (the centre line, the brisket swirl).
- Sew the dorsal seam with a **baseball stitch** in waxed thread, pulling hair out
  of the seam as you go; it should vanish when brushed.

## 5. Set the face and dry
- Pin or **card** (thin card pinned over the area) eyelids, nostrils and lips so they
  can't pull back as they shrink. Use pins on ears to hold their curve.
- Dry slowly at room temperature, away from heaters and direct sun. Check daily for
  the first few days and push back anything drifting — once dry it's set.
- After drying: remove pins and cards, rebuild shrunken areas (eyes, nose) with
  epoxy, and paint (see
  `handcrafts.taxidermy.finishing-habitat.epoxy-finishing-and-colour-restoration`).

## Gotchas
- Stretching a small cape onto a big form shows as thin, parted hair. Size the form
  down rather than stretching.
- Air pockets under ears or face skin cause "drum" sounds and later lifting.
- Hanging direction: a right-turn mount on the wrong wall faces the wall.

## Verify
- [ ] Eye-to-nose and neck measurements match the form ordered.
- [ ] Eyes symmetrical from front and top views.
- [ ] Seam closes without strain; hair pattern aligned.
- [ ] Face carded and pinned; dried slowly with daily checks.
- [ ] No lifting or voids when tapped after drying.
