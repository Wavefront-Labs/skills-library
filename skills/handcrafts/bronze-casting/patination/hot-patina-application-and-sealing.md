# Hot patina application and sealing

**Goal:** an even, deliberate surface colour on bronze — browns, greens, blacks or
layered combinations — that is fixed and protected. Patina is controlled corrosion;
heat speeds and deepens it. Draft: widely used chemicals and approaches; dilutions
are typical starting points to test, not verified recipes.

## 1. Surface prep
- Uniform surface from fettling, typically a fine sandblast, so chemicals take evenly.
- **Degrease** (solvent or a detergent wash, rinse); wear nitrile gloves from now on.
  Fingerprints show as blotches.

## 2. Heat
- Heat with a propane torch (rosebud or wide tip) evenly, moving constantly.
  Typical working temperature: water spat on the bronze **sizzles** but doesn't
  instantly ball and skate away. Too hot and solutions flash off and flake;
  too cold and they pool and run.
- Heat **from behind or beside** the area being treated where possible; large pieces
  are worked section by section.

## 3. Common chemicals (dilute in water)
- **Liver of sulphur / potassium sulphide:** browns to blacks. Often applied first as
  a base, or alone for a dark statuary brown.
- **Ferric nitrate:** warm browns, reddish to tan; the backbone of many brown finishes.
- **Cupric nitrate:** greens and blue-greens (a verdigris-type green when applied hot
  in layers).
- **Bismuth nitrate, silver nitrate, titanium compounds:** whites, greys and others;
  specialist.
Apply with a brush (natural bristle, dabbing or stippling) or a spray bottle on a
fine mist. Build colour in thin layers, heating between them.

## 4. Layering and highlighting
- Typical workflow: dark base (sulphide), then mid colour (ferric), then accents
  (cupric) in recesses or on top.
- **Highlight** raised areas by rubbing back with fine abrasive pads (or after
  waxing, a cloth), which mimics natural wear and gives depth.
- Test on scrap from the same alloy, or an inconspicuous spot.

## 5. Sealing
- While the bronze is warm (not hot — wax should melt but not smoke), apply a
  **paste wax** (clear or tinted) with a brush, letting it soak in; buff when cool.
  Several thin coats.
- Outdoor pieces: many foundries use wax and annual re-waxing; some use a lacquer
  such as an acrylic (Incralac-type) under wax. Lacquer can peel later and is hard
  to touch up — I'm not certain which is more common in practice; it varies.

## Safety
- Nitrate and sulphide solutions are irritants and stain; sulphides smell of rotten
  egg (H2S) when heated — work outdoors or with strong extraction and a respirator
  rated for acid gases/vapours. Wear eye protection.
- Store chemicals labelled, away from heat; nitrates are oxidisers.

## Verify
- [ ] Clean, uniform surface, gloves on throughout.
- [ ] Temperature checked by water sizzle before applying.
- [ ] Colour built in thin layers and tested first.
- [ ] Highlights deliberate; wax applied warm and buffed.
- [ ] Owner told about re-waxing interval for outdoor work.
