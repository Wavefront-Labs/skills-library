# Cutting gates and chasing a casting

**Goal:** turn a rough casting, still bristling with gates and pocked with shell
debris, into metal that matches the sculptor's model so closely that welds, seams
and repairs disappear. Chasing is where foundries earn their reputation. Draft:
general art-foundry practice.

## 1. Knockout and clean
- Break the shell off with a hammer and chisel, then **sandblast** or pressure-blast
  the remaining refractory from crevices. Wear a blasting hood and respirator;
  shell dust contains silica.
- Remove the core material from hollow pieces where accessible.

## 2. Cut the gates
- Cut gates a little proud of the surface with a cut-off wheel or bandsaw, never
  flush — you can't put metal back. Grind down to the surface level with a
  **die grinder** and **carbide burrs**, then refine.
- Watch heat: long grinding builds heat that can discolour and ruin later patina
  uniformity; work in passes.

## 3. Welding: assembly and repair
- Larger sculptures are cast in sections and welded together. **TIG welding** with
  silicon bronze filler matched to the casting alloy is common, so the weld patinates
  like the parent metal. Mismatched filler shows as a differently coloured line after
  patination.
- Tack sections, check alignment against the model or photos, then weld fully;
  back-purge or design joints so welds are full-penetration where strength matters.
- **Porosity:** open pinholes with a burr to sound metal, then fill with weld.
  Larger voids may be plugged (drilled and fitted with a threaded or pressed bronze
  plug) then chased.
- **Cold shuts** (lines where two metal fronts met but didn't fuse) are weak: grind
  out and weld.

## 4. Chasing the surface
- Restore the texture of the original over every weld and ground area. Tools:
  burrs, files, rifflers, **chasing punches** (hammered steel tools with shaped ends,
  textured or smooth), rotary brushes.
- Copy the texture from the unworked areas: a sculptor's clay marks, a tool rhythm,
  a smooth skin. Reference photographs of the original model help.
- Check under raking light; welds are invisible straight-on and obvious in side light.

## 5. Final prep for patina
- Even the surface finish over the whole piece: a final light sandblast with fine
  media or a consistent abrasive pass so patina takes uniformly.
- Degrease and keep clean (no bare-handed fingerprints). Move on to
  `handcrafts.bronze-casting.patination.hot-patina-application-and-sealing`.

## Gotchas
- Flush-cutting gates nicks the surface.
- Grinding with a wheel used on steel embeds iron particles that rust-spot bronze.
  Keep bronze-only abrasives.
- Welding fumes from copper alloys: extraction and a respirator.

## Verify
- [ ] Gates cut proud, then ground and blended flush.
- [ ] Welds with matched filler; cold shuts and porosity opened to sound metal.
- [ ] Texture continuous under raking light; no visible weld lines.
- [ ] Surface uniform and clean for patina.
