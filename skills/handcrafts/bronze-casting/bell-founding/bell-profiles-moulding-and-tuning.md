# Bell profiles, moulding and tuning

**Goal:** understand and plan the bell founder's sequence — a bell that sounds the
intended note with its main partials in tune. This is a specialist trade, usually
learnt in a foundry; this skill is an orientation, not a complete method, and the
acoustics summary is simplified. Draft.

## 1. Bell metal
Traditional **bell bronze** is copper with a high tin content (often quoted as
roughly 20-23% tin). It's hard and brittle — it rings, but cracks if struck
wrongly or cooled badly. It's not the silicon bronze of art foundries.

## 2. The profile decides the sound
- A bell's **profile** (cross-section from crown to lip) and wall thickness control
  its pitch and partials. Founders keep proven profiles ("scales") for families
  of sizes; designs are scaled and adjusted.
- Bigger, heavier bells = lower pitch. At a fixed profile, doubling size roughly halves
  frequency (an octave down).
- The **strickle board** (or crook) is the template cut to the profile; it rotates
  around a central spindle to shape the mould surfaces.

## 3. Loam moulding (traditional)
1. Build the **core** (the inner shape) in brick, coated with loam (clay, sand, organic
   matter like straw or dung), swept to shape by the inner strickle. Dry with fire.
2. Build a **false bell** (thickness of the metal) over the core, swept by the outer
   strickle; inscriptions and decoration are applied as wax letters on the false bell.
3. Build the **cope** (outer mould) over the false bell, dry it, lift it off, remove
   the false bell, then lower the cope back over the core. The gap is now the bell.
4. Bury the mould in a pit and ram sand around it to resist the metal's pressure.

**Case moulding:** modern foundries often use reusable iron cases lined with
moulding sand, swept to profile; faster and more repeatable.

## 4. Casting and cooling
Pour the metal from crucible or furnace through the crown. Cool **slowly**, often
for days in the pit for large bells — fast cooling stresses brittle bell bronze.

## 5. Tuning
- A bell has many **partials**. In the Western tuned-bell tradition the main ones
  are the **hum**, **prime/fundamental**, **tierce** (a minor third — the
  characteristic bell sound), **quint** and **nominal**. The perceived **strike note**
  relates to the nominal.
- Bells are cast slightly sharp and metal is shaved from the inside on a vertical
  **tuning lathe** to bring partials into the desired relationship ("true-harmonic"
  or "five-point" tuning). Removing metal at different heights affects different
  partials. Measure with a strobe or spectrum analyser between cuts.
- You can only lower pitch by tuning; too flat and the bell is re-cast.

## 6. Handbells
Small tuned handbells are typically cast in sand and tuned on a lathe (inside and
out); the same principles, small scale.

## Verify
- [ ] Profile chosen from a proven scale for the target note and weight.
- [ ] Mould parts dried thoroughly; mould secured in rammed pit.
- [ ] Slow cooling plan in place for bell bronze.
- [ ] Partials measured and logged before and after each tuning cut.
