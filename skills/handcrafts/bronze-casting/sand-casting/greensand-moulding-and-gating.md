# Greensand moulding and gating

**Goal:** a sand mould that keeps its shape under molten metal, lets gas out, and
fills completely: the method for repeat parts, plaques and mechanical castings in
bronze, brass or aluminium. Draft: hobby/craft foundry practice summarised.

## 1. The pattern
- **Draft:** every vertical face tapers (a degree or two or more) so the pattern
  lifts out without tearing sand.
- **Shrinkage allowance:** metal shrinks as it cools. Bronze and aluminium shrink
  roughly 1-2% linearly — patternmakers used "shrink rules". Check a figure for your
  alloy.
- Decide the **parting line** (where cope and drag meet) so no part of the pattern
  is undercut relative to its half. Split patterns are made with dowelled halves.
- Smooth, sealed pattern surfaces (shellac, paint) release cleanly.

## 2. Sand
- **Greensand:** silica sand + clay (bentonite) + water. "Green" means damp, not
  colour. **Temper** it until a squeezed handful holds a clean fingerprint and
  breaks cleanly in two — not crumbly, not sticky. Too wet = steam explosions and
  gas holes.
- **Oil-bonded sand** (Petrobond-type) needs no water and gives finer finish; smokes
  on pouring.
- **Resin-bonded/no-bake** sand sets hard; good for cores and larger work.

## 3. Ramming up
1. Drag flask upside down on a mould board, pattern half in it. Dust with parting.
2. **Riddle** (sieve) fine facing sand over the pattern, then fill and ram in layers —
   firm round the pattern, not rock-hard (hard ramming blocks gas escape). Strike off.
3. Roll over, place cope flask and cope pattern half, dust parting, place sprue and
   riser pins, ram the cope.
4. Lift the cope, draw patterns (rap lightly first), repair any broken edges.

## 4. Gating
- **Sprue** (down channel) into a **runner** in the parting line, then **ingates**
  into the casting. Gate into thick sections; keep metal moving smoothly.
- A **riser** (feeder) on the heaviest section supplies metal as the casting shrinks
  — it must solidify after the part. Cut a small well at the sprue base to soak up
  the first, dirty, cold metal.
- **Vent** the cope with a thin wire over the cavity, not into it.

## 5. Cores
Hollow forms need a **core** — a separately made sand shape (often oil- or resin-bonded,
baked or set hard) sitting in **core prints** the pattern leaves. Vent the core;
gas from it must escape.

## 6. Close and pour
Blow out loose sand, close cope on drag (pins aligned), weight or clamp the cope
(molten metal lifts it), and pour fast and continuously. See
`handcrafts.bronze-casting.foundry-melt-pour.melting-and-pouring-bronze`.

## Defects and causes
- Gas holes / blows: wet sand, unvented cores, over-ramming.
- Misrun (incomplete): metal too cold, gates too thin, slow pour.
- Shrink cavity: no or undersized riser.
- Rough or burnt-in surface: coarse sand, no facing sand, metal too hot.
- Shifted halves: flask pins worn or misaligned.

## Verify
- [ ] Pattern has draft and shrink allowance.
- [ ] Sand passes the squeeze-break test.
- [ ] Riser on the heavy section; vents in the cope; cores vented.
- [ ] Cope clamped or weighted before pouring.
