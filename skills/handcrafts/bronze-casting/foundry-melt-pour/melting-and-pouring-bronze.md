# Melting and pouring bronze

**Goal:** clean bronze at the right temperature, into the mould in one continuous
pour, with nobody hurt. Molten metal is unforgiving: **water is the main enemy**
(trapped moisture flashes to steam and throws metal). Draft: general craft-foundry
practice; follow your furnace and crucible makers' guidance.

## 1. Alloy
- **Silicon bronze** (copper with a few % silicon, e.g. "Everdur"-type) is the common
  art-foundry alloy: fluid, weldable, patinates well.
- **Tin bronze** for bells and traditional work; different pour temperature and
  behaviour. Don't mix alloys or add unknown scrap (zinc-heavy brass fumes;
  leaded scrap contaminates).

## 2. Crucible and furnace
- Clay-graphite or silicon-carbide crucible sized to the charge with headroom.
  New crucibles are **tempered** (slowly heated per the maker) to drive moisture.
  Inspect every use for cracks and thinning; retire suspect crucibles.
- Use a crucible **plinth** block so the bottom heats; never set a hot crucible on
  concrete (spalls) or wet ground.
- Gas (propane) furnaces with a blower are common; induction furnaces in bigger studios.

## 3. Charging
- **Preheat everything** that will touch metal — ingots, scrap, skimmers, stirring
  rods — by placing them near the furnace mouth. A cold, damp ingot dropped into
  a melt can explode.
- Start with a modest charge, add more as it melts.

## 4. Temperature
- Measure with an immersion **pyrometer** (thermocouple). Silicon bronze is commonly
  poured somewhere around 1,100-1,200 °C depending on section thickness and shell
  temperature — thin-walled work wants hotter. Get the figure for your alloy from
  the supplier; I haven't verified a single number for every case.
- Overheating increases gas pick-up and oxidation; underheating gives misruns.

## 5. Cleaning the melt
- **Skim dross** with a preheated skimmer just before the pour.
- Some alloys and practices call for a **flux** or **degasser** added per the maker;
  silicon bronze is often poured with only skimming. Follow the alloy supplier.

## 6. The pour
- Crew roles agreed beforehand: **lead pourer**, **tail/second** on the shank,
  **skimmer** holding back dross at the lip. Walk the route with an empty crucible.
- Lift with tongs into a **pouring shank**, carry low and steady to the moulds
  (on dry sand beds), pour **fast and continuously**, keeping the cup full until
  metal shows at the vents. Don't pause mid-pour (cold shuts).
- Return excess to an ingot mould; never to a damp surface.

## PPE and site
- Natural-fibre clothing (synthetics melt into skin), **leather** jacket/apron,
  spats or foundry boots with trousers over them, leather gauntlets, a
  **face shield** over safety glasses (IR-rated helps).
- Dry sand floor, clear walkways, fire extinguisher (class D for metals is
  specialist; mostly you need clearance and sand), first aid for burns. No pets
  or onlookers in the pour area.

## Verify
- [ ] Alloy known; crucible inspected and tempered.
- [ ] All tools and charge preheated and dry.
- [ ] Pour temperature measured by pyrometer, not guessed by colour.
- [ ] Crew roles and route rehearsed; PPE full.
- [ ] Pour continuous until vents fill.
