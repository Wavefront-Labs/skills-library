# Die cutting, foiling and edge painting by hand

**Goal:** turn printed sheets into finished cards, invitations and stationery with
clean edges, accurate folds, crisp foil and no visible glue or bleeding paint.

## 1. Trimming
- Print with **bleed** (image extending ~3 mm past the trim line) and crop marks; trim to
  the marks, so slight variation never leaves a white sliver.
- **Guillotine**: square the stack against the back gauge and side lay, clamp, cut. Cut the
  long dimension first. Always keep both hands on the two-handed controls (powered) or
  the handle (manual) — never reach under the blade. Replace dull blades: they drag and
  produce "draw" (the top sheet cut shorter than the bottom).
- Small runs: a rotary or rail trimmer plus a steel rule and fresh scalpel.

## 2. Creasing and scoring
- **Crease** heavier stock (above ~170 gsm) before folding, or the fibres crack. Fold so
  the raised bead of the crease is on the **inside** of the fold.
- On a platen press: lock a **creasing rule** in the chase (lower than cutting rule), and
  glue a matching channel **counter** (matrix) on the platen. Without a counter, the crease
  is weak and can cut.
- Fold with the grain where you can; cross-grain folds crack even when creased.

## 3. Die cutting on a platen press
- **Steel-rule dies** (cutting rule bent into a plywood base, with rubber ejection strips)
  are made by die makers from your vector outline. Rule height is slightly over type high.
- Lock the die in the chase, set a hard **cutting plate** (steel or hard packing) on the
  platen, ink rollers **removed or locked off**.
- Build up impression gradually with packing until the die cuts cleanly through, leaving
  maybe a few tiny "nicks" (intentional ties) to hold the piece in the sheet. Too much
  impression damages the die and the press.
- **Register** the die to the print with gauge pins as for printing.

## 4. Hot-foil stamping
- Uses a heated die (magnesium for short runs, brass/copper for long runs or fine detail)
  pressing a foil film onto stock. Hand-operated foil presses (e.g. Kensol-type) or a
  converted platen with a heated base.
- Variables: **temperature, pressure, dwell** time. Start from the foil maker's range
  (often roughly 100–130 °C for many paper foils — check the datasheet) and adjust:
  - Foil not adhering / patchy: more heat or dwell.
  - Foil filling in fine lines, fuzzy edges: less heat or pressure.
- Uncoated, rough cotton papers take foil less evenly; test on the actual stock.
- Advance foil between hits so each strike uses fresh film.

## 5. Duplexing
Glue two (or three) sheets back to back for thick cards with contrasting cores. Use a thin,
even layer of PVA rolled on, or a sheet adhesive/dry-mount film, press flat under weight for
hours, **then** trim, so the edges align perfectly.

## 6. Edge painting
1. Trim the stack, then clamp it **tightly** and squarely between boards, edges flush.
2. Lightly sand the edge smooth.
3. Apply acrylic paint (or edge-gilding foil/metallic paint) with a sponge or small roller in
   thin coats, working from the centre out so paint isn't pushed between sheets.
4. Let it dry in the clamp. Fan the stack gently to separate any stuck sheets.

## Gotchas
- A stack that isn't clamped tight lets paint wick into the faces of the cards.
- Foil on top of printed ink may not adhere; test, or foil before printing.
- Dies cut fingers too: the press is just as dangerous with a die in it.
- Temperatures above are starting points; your foil and press decide.

## Verify
- [ ] No white slivers at edges; dimensions match spec to ±0.5 mm.
- [ ] Folds are crack-free with the bead inside.
- [ ] Die cut pieces pop out cleanly with no tearing; cut in register with print.
- [ ] Foil is solid, crisp, and doesn't flake with a fingernail.
- [ ] Painted edges are even with no bleed onto faces.
