# Platen-press makeready, inking and registration

**Goal:** an even, crisp impression across the whole forme, correct ink coverage, and
every sheet in the same place — consistently across a run and between colours.

## 1. Type-high and the forme
- Type height in the English-speaking world is **0.918 inch** (23.32 mm). Everything that
  prints must be exactly that; photopolymer plates sit on a **base** (e.g. a Boxcar
  aluminium base) whose height + plate = type high. Check with a type-high gauge.
- Lock the forme in the chase (see
  handcrafts.hand-printing.hand-typesetting.setting-justifying-and-locking-up-metal-type).

## 2. Rollers and ink
- **Roller height**: rollers should just kiss the type surface. Check with a "roller
  gauge" or a strip of type-high material: an inked roller should leave a stripe roughly
  2–3 mm wide. Too low = ink floods counters and shoulders; too high = patchy coverage.
  Adjust with roller trucks/bearers or tape on rails depending on the press.
- **Ink**: rubber-based inks stay open on the press for hours (good for beginners);
  oil-based dry faster on paper. Mix colours by weight and record the recipe.
- **Distribution**: put a little ink on the disc or ink plate, run the press (without
  printing) for many revolutions until the film is even and makes a fine "hiss" not a
  sticky "crackle". **Start with too little**: it's easy to add, slow to remove.
- Ink coverage check: printed solids look even, with no salty speckle (too little) or
  squash halo around letters (too much).

## 3. Packing and impression
- The **tympan** (oiled manila or mylar sheet) over **packing** (hard paper/pressboard sheets)
  sets impression strength on a platen press. Add or remove sheets to change pressure;
  never pack so heavily that the press strains.
- **Kiss impression** (traditional): the ink transfers with barely any bite. **Deep
  impression** (current fashion) on thick soft cotton paper: more packing — but it wears
  type and can crack the platen of light presses. Old metal and wood type is irreplaceable;
  use photopolymer for deep bite.
- **Platen parallelism**: if one side prints heavier, the platen is out of parallel.
  Adjust the platen bolts per the press manual, or locally "make ready".

## 4. Makeready
1. Pull a proof on scrap paper. Look at the back of the sheet for impression evenness.
2. Light areas: paste small pieces of thin tissue on a makeready sheet in the packing
   behind those areas (**underlay** under the forme or **overlay** in the packing).
   Heavy areas: cut away.
3. Repeat until the impression is even. This is slow and normal.

## 5. Registration
1. Print once on the tympan itself (no paper), then position a sheet over that image to
   mark where the paper's edges must sit.
2. Fix **gauge pins** into the tympan at the bottom edge (two pins) and side (one pin),
   positioned away from the printing area and away from the forme so they don't get crushed.
3. Feed each sheet against the pins the same way. Fine-tune by moving pins.
4. **Multi-colour**: print colour 1 for the whole run plus spares, then without moving
   the gauge pins change forme. Print a key colour on a mylar overlay on the tympan to
   locate colour 2.

## Gotchas
- Platen presses crush fingers: never reach into a closing press. Feed by rhythm, and
  let a misfed sheet go.
- Lead and solvent exposure: ventilation, gloves, no food.
- Grippers on a C&P must be set clear of the forme or they get crushed.
- Some details (roller stripe width, deep-impression limits) vary by press; follow its manual.

## Verify
- [ ] Forme and plates measure type high.
- [ ] Roller stripe on a type-high gauge is narrow and even across its length.
- [ ] Proof shows even ink and impression edge to edge (check the back of the sheet).
- [ ] Ten consecutive sheets register within your tolerance (overlay them to check).
- [ ] Pins and grippers clear of the forme; fingers clear of the platen.
