# Setting, justifying and locking up metal type

**Goal:** compose a forme of type where every line is set to the same measure and held
tight enough to print without a single sort pulling out, then distribute it back cleanly.

## 1. Measures and the case
- Anglo-American point system: 12 pt = 1 pica; 6 picas ≈ 1 inch. Type size is the body
  height; line length ("measure") is set in picas.
- **California job case**: capitals on the right third; lower case on the left and middle,
  arranged by frequency (big boxes for e, a, n, etc.); spaces near the centre. Learn the lay
  from a case chart — it's irregular by design.
- Never mix fonts in a case ("pied" type). A stray sort of another font ruins a job.

## 2. Setting in the composing stick
1. Set the stick's knee to the measure, tightened with a slug or lead of that length as a gauge.
2. Hold the stick in the left hand, thumb inside. Pick sorts with the right hand, **nick
   up** (the groove on the body faces you); that's how you know the letter's orientation
   without reading it.
3. Type reads **left to right, upside down** in the stick. b/d/p/q look alike mirrored —
   hence "mind your p's and q's". Check them.
4. Separate words with the standard word space — often a 3-to-em space (a third of an em).

## 3. Spaces and justification
Spaces (non-printing, below type height), from widest: **em quad** (square of the body
size), **en quad** (half), 3-to-em, 4-to-em, 5-to-em, and thin, plus copper/brass **hair spaces**.
1. Set the line until no further word fits.
2. **Justify**: add or swap spaces between words, evenly, until the line is snug in the
   stick — tight enough to stand when the stick is tilted, but not so tight it buckles.
3. Test: press lightly on the line with a finger; nothing should spring, and the line
   shouldn't drop if the stick is tilted forward.
4. Short lines (end of paragraph, centred lines) are filled with quads.
5. **Leading**: strips of type metal (2 pt leads, 6 pt slugs) between lines for line spacing.

## 4. Galley and proof
Lift the composed lines as a block (squeezing ends and sides with fingers) onto a galley,
tie the block round with page cord, and pull a galley proof. Correct by swapping sorts with
tweezers; re-justify any line you change.

## 5. Lockup in the chase
1. Place the forme on the imposing stone (a flat surface), the chase around it.
2. Surround the forme with **furniture** (wood or metal spacing blocks) and **reglets**
   (thin wooden strips), built so pressure goes straight through the forme from two sides.
3. Insert **quoins** (expanding locks) on the top and one side, between furniture —
   never directly against type.
4. Untie the page cord; **plane** the type with a planer block and mallet tapping gently so all
   faces sit flat and level.
5. Tighten quoins gradually and alternately with the key. Lift a corner of the chase slightly
   and tap the type: if nothing moves or drops, it's locked. Loose sorts mean a line wasn't justified.
6. Don't over-tighten — the forme can spring and the chase bow.

## 6. Distributing
After printing, clean type with a type wash, then distribute each sort back to its box,
reading the words. Accurate distribution is what keeps the case usable.

## Safety
Metal type is lead alloy: wash hands before eating, no food or drink in the composing room,
don't blow dust off cases; vacuum with a HEPA vacuum.

## Verify
- [ ] Every line is justified: the forme lifts on the galley without sorts falling.
- [ ] Proof checked for wrong-font, upside-down and b/d/p/q errors.
- [ ] Quoins sit against furniture, not type; the planed forme lifts as one unit.
- [ ] Type distributed back to the right case; hands washed.
