# Photo-emulsion screens, registration and curing

**Goal:** a sharp stencil that holds detail, prints that land in register, and ink that
survives washing (textiles) or doesn't smudge (paper).

## 1. Mesh and frame
- **Mesh count** (threads per inch, US): lower = thicker ink deposit and coarser detail.
  Rough guide: ~110 for plastisol on dark garments and underbases; ~156–160 general
  textile work; ~200–230 for fine detail, water-based inks and paper. (Metric meshes are
  per cm; convert.)
- Tension matters: a slack screen gives blurry, smeared prints. Retension or replace.

## 2. Coat
1. **Degrease** the mesh with screen degreaser and dry completely — oils cause pinholes.
2. In subdued yellow/safe light, fill a **scoop coater** with emulsion and coat the print
   side then the squeegee side in one smooth upward stroke each (1:1 or 2:1 coats for a
   thicker stencil for plastisol).
3. Dry **horizontally, print side down**, in the dark with a fan, until fully dry. Damp
   emulsion under-exposes and washes out.

## 3. Expose
- Film positive: **opaque black** on clear film (inkjet film with dense black, or laser).
  Grey or translucent artwork leaks light → fill-in loss.
- Place positive **right-reading on the print side**, press into tight contact (glass or
  vacuum), expose to UV.
- **Find the exposure time** with a step test / exposure calculator: expose strips for
  increasing times, wash out, and pick the shortest time where the emulsion is hard
  (not slimy on the squeegee side) yet detail washes out cleanly. Repeat when you change
  emulsion, mesh, coats or bulb. Under-exposure = stencil breaks down; over-exposure = fine
  detail won't open.

## 4. Wash out and prep
Spray both sides with cool water; then the image side gently until detail opens. Dry.
Inspect against light for pinholes; block them and any open edges with screen filler or tape.

## 5. Printing
- **Off-contact**: set the screen 2–3 mm above the substrate so mesh only touches under the
  squeegee and snaps back — sharp edges, no smear.
- **Squeegee**: a 70-durometer blade is a common all-rounder; sharper and harder for detail.
- **Flood stroke**: with the screen lifted, pull ink lightly across the image to fill the mesh.
- **Print stroke**: blade at about 75° (15° from vertical), firm even pressure, one steady pass
  (two for absorbent fabrics). Water-based inks dry in the mesh — keep the screen flooded
  between pulls and work quickly; plastisol won't dry at room temperature.

## 6. Registration
- **Paper**: tape registration guides (corner and side) on the bed or use a hinged table.
- **Garments**: a press with micro-registration, or pallet marks. Print colour 1 on a
  test sheet, then align screen 2 by eye through the mesh before locking. Plastisol
  multi-colour on dark garments: underbase white, **flash cure** until just tack-free, then top colours.

## 7. Curing
- **Plastisol** must reach its full cure temperature through the whole ink film —
  commonly about 160 °C (320 °F), check the ink's datasheet — on a heat press or tunnel/flash
  dryer. Under-cured plastisol cracks and washes off. Test: stretch test and a wash test.
- **Water-based** textile inks need heat for a period (datasheet: e.g. several minutes at
  ~160 °C) to drive off water and set the binder.
- Paper prints air-dry on a rack.

## Clean-up and reclaim
Remove ink immediately, then emulsion remover to strip the stencil; haze remover for ghosting.
Wear gloves and eye protection; use solvent-free products where possible.

## Verify
- [ ] Step test done for this emulsion/mesh; chosen time recorded.
- [ ] No pinholes; edges blocked.
- [ ] Prints are sharp with no smear or ghost from mesh contact.
- [ ] Colours register within tolerance.
- [ ] Textile prints pass a stretch and wash test after curing.
