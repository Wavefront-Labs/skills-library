# Gathering, blowing and transferring to the punty

**Goal:** a simple blown vessel (cup or bowl) made through the full sequence: gather,
shape, blow, transfer, open, anneal. This is a team process; learn it in a studio with
supervision — molten glass runs near 1100 °C.

## Equipment
- **Furnace**: holds molten glass, typically around 1100–1150 °C for soda-lime studio
  glass (varies by glass).
- **Glory hole**: reheating chamber; you will return to it constantly.
- **Bench** with rails; **marver** (flat steel/graphite table); wooden **blocks** (soaked
  in water) and **paddles**; **jacks** (tweezers-like shaping tool); shears; tweezers.
- **Annealer** (lehr): holds the finished piece near its annealing point and cools it slowly.

## 1. Gathering
- Preheat the blowpipe tip in the glory hole or a pipe warmer — glass won't stick to a
  cold pipe.
- Dip the tip just under the glass surface at a shallow angle, and **turn constantly**.
  Glass on a pipe obeys gravity; the turn keeps it centred. Lift out still turning,
  letting the thread break.

## 2. Marver and block
- Roll the gather on the marver to centre it and chill the skin slightly; shape a
  slight cone at the end.
- Or shape in a wet wooden block: steam forms a cushion. Keep turning.

## 3. First bubble
- Blow a short puff, then cap the pipe with your thumb — the hot air expands and inflates
  the bubble. Chilled skin forces the bubble to form evenly inside.
- Reheat and take a second gather over the first bubble for a larger piece.

## 4. Shaping at the bench
- Sit, roll the pipe along the rails with the left hand (right-handed gaffer), shape with
  jacks, paddle or wet newspaper pad held in the right.
- Cut a **jack line** — a groove at the neck near the pipe — which is where the piece will
  break off the pipe later.
- Reheat often. Working glass too cold causes cracks and stresses.

## 5. Punty transfer
- The assistant gathers a small amount of glass on the **punty** (solid rod), shapes it
  into a small disc, and brings it to the bottom of the piece, centred on the pipe's axis.
- When attached, the gaffer drips a little water on the jack line and taps the pipe; the
  piece breaks off cleanly at the line. It's now on the punty, open end out.
- Off-centre punty = a wobbly piece that is hard to open. Align carefully.

## 6. Open and finish
- Reheat the open end in the glory hole. Use jacks inside the opening and turn to open the
  lip; shears trim uneven rims (cut hot).
- Shape the final form, keeping the whole piece evenly warm.

## 7. Into the annealer
- Knock the piece off the punty with a sharp tap (usually by the assistant holding gloves
  or a fibre paddle) and place it in the annealer immediately.
- The annealer cycle (hold near the glass's annealing point, then slow cool over hours)
  removes stress. Unannealed glass can crack days later.
- Grind the punty scar later (see cold-working skills).

## Safety
- Didymium or IR-filtering glasses when looking into the furnace or glory hole.
- Natural-fibre clothing; no loose sleeves.
- Hot glass looks exactly like cold glass. Never touch glass on the bench or floor;
  communicate "hot" and "behind you" constantly.

## Gotchas
- Temperatures and schedules depend on the glass's maker data; the figures above are
  common studio ranges, not universal.
- Glass compatibility (expansion) matters when adding colour; use matched frit, bar and
  clear from compatible lines.

## Verify
- [ ] Pipe preheated; gather taken and kept centred by continuous turning.
- [ ] Jack line cut; punty aligned to the axis before transfer.
- [ ] Piece reheated whenever it stiffens; lip opened evenly.
- [ ] Annealed on a schedule suited to its thickness.
