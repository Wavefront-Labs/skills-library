# Grinding and polishing glass: flats and punty scars

**Goal:** a flat base or edge that's level, free of chips, and polished back to
transparency; a blown piece with its punty scar removed so it sits flat and safe.

## Principle: grit progression
Each grit removes the scratches of the one before. You **cannot skip** steps; leftover
deeper scratches show up as grey haze after polishing.
A typical sequence (with silicon carbide loose grit on a steel lap, or the equivalent in
diamond discs/pads):
1. **Coarse**: 60–80 grit — shape, flatten, remove the punty bump.
2. **Medium**: 120 then 220 grit — remove coarse scratches.
3. **Fine**: 400 then 600 grit — prepares for polish; surface looks evenly satin.
4. **Pre-polish** (optional): pumice or 1200-ish diamond.
5. **Polish**: **cerium oxide** slurry on felt, cork or a polishing pad, until clear.

Sequences vary by machine and abrasive; the doubling rule (each step roughly half the
grit size of the last) is a useful guide.

## Machines
- **Flat lap / flatbed grinder**: rotating horizontal plate fed with water and grit or
  diamond discs. For flat bottoms and faces.
- **Wet belt sander**: vertical water-fed belts for bevels, edges and shaping.
- **Lathe** with wheels: for faceting, cutting and engraving work in skilled hands.
- **Hand pads**: diamond hand pads with water for small fixes and edges.

## Removing a punty scar
1. Look at the base: the scar is a rough glass nub where the punty attached.
2. Coarse-grind on the flat lap (or belt for concave bases) holding the piece level and
   **moving it** across the lap — staying in one spot cuts a hollow.
3. For a concave base, grind the scar only, then use a small diamond burr or a cone wheel.
4. Go through the grits, rinsing the piece and your hands between each — one stray coarse
   grain ruins a fine step. Change the water too.
5. Polish with cerium to clear, or leave a satin finish if that's the design.

## Edges and bevels
- Arrises (sharp edges) chip: put a small 45° safety bevel on every ground edge with a fine
  belt or hand pad.
- For a polished bevel, carry the bevel through the same grit sequence.

## Checking progress
- Dry the surface and look under a strong raking light: an even satin = ready for next
  grit; bright individual scratches = go back.
- For flatness, rub on a sheet of fine wet-and-dry paper on float glass; high spots show.

## Safety
- Always wet. Dry grinding glass makes respirable silica dust and heats glass until it
  cracks.
- Eye protection (chips fly from edges); waterproof apron.
- GFCI/RCD protection on water-fed machines.
- Keep fingers off the grinding plate edge; hold large pieces with both hands.
- Dispose of grinding slurry by settling, not down drains (can block plumbing; check local
  rules).

## Gotchas
- Thermal cracking: pressing hard on a slow wheel heats a small zone. Light pressure,
  plenty of water.
- Annealed glass only — stressed (badly annealed) pieces often crack during grinding.
- Grit sizes above are common studio sequences, not a standard.

## Verify
- [ ] Grit sequence followed without skips; piece and hands rinsed between grits.
- [ ] Base flat (rocks on nothing) and punty scar fully removed.
- [ ] Edges bevelled; no visible scratches under raking light after polish.
- [ ] Worked wet throughout; slurry disposed of properly.
