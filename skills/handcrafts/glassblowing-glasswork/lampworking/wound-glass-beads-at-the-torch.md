# Wound glass beads at the torch

**Goal:** a round, evenly wound bead with clean holes and simple dot decoration that
survives because it was annealed.

## Glass and torch
- **Soft glass** (soda-lime) is the usual starting point for beads. It's sold by
  **COE** (coefficient of expansion), commonly COE 104 (e.g. Effetre/Moretti-type
  rods) or COE 96. **Never mix COEs** in one bead — they crack as they cool.
- **Borosilicate** (COE 33) needs a hotter oxy-fuel torch; it's tougher but has a
  smaller palette for beads.
- Torch: a single-fuel "hothead" style torch on MAPP/propane is a common starter for
  soft glass; surface-mix or premix **oxygen-propane** torches run hotter and cleaner.
  Follow the torch maker's setup and regulator guidance.

## Setup
- Fire-proof work surface, glass rods within reach, a graphite or steel **marver**,
  mandrels (stainless steel rods) dipped in **bead release** (clay slurry) and fully
  dried. Wet release spits and pops.
- **Didymium** (or equivalent sodium-flare-filtering) glasses — soft glass flares
  bright yellow, which hides what you're doing and is hard on eyes over time.
- Ventilation: a hood or extraction is standard; combustion products and any off-gassing
  from release need removing.
- **Annealing**: a small digitally controlled kiln, or at minimum a fibre blanket or
  vermiculite for slow cooling (less reliable).

## Flame zones
- The flame's hottest point is a little out from the tip of the inner cone. Work further
  out in the flame for gentle heat, closer in for melting.
- **Introduce rods slowly** from the outer flame: soft glass shocks and spits if plunged
  in cold. Warm the mandrel's release too until it glows faintly.

## Winding a bead
1. Melt the rod end into a molten gather (a "gob").
2. Touch it to the heated mandrel and **rotate the mandrel away from you** while feeding
   glass, winding a band. Keep the mandrel turning continuously, always, while glass is
   molten.
3. Pull the rod off with a flick through the flame to burn off the thread.
4. Heat the bead evenly in the flame, turning, and let surface tension pull it round.
   Bring it to a glowing, even state for a smooth bead.
5. Ends should "hug" the mandrel — a fully wrapped bead whose glass reaches the release
   on both sides, giving clean holes without sharp edges.

## Dots
- Pull **stringer** (thin rod) from a contrasting colour, or apply directly from a rod.
- Heat a spot on the bead, touch the rod to it, then pull away through the flame.
- Melt dots in flush or leave raised; stacked dots over a melted dot make "eyes".

## Annealing
- Put the hot bead straight into the kiln at the glass's anneal temperature (for many
  COE-104 soft glasses around 480–520 °C; check the manufacturer), hold (often ~30–60
  min for small beads), then cool slowly. Batch annealing (re-heating cold beads) is
  possible for small beads but garaging hot is preferred.

## Clean and finish
- Soak mandrels in water, twist beads off, ream out release with a bead reamer under
  water (wet — don't breathe release dust).

## Gotchas
- Cracks after cooling: unannealed, mixed COE, or uneven heating.
- Pitted holes: release too thin or too wet.
- Lopsided beads: rotation stopped or uneven heating; spin faster, heat broadly.
- The anneal figures are typical; manufacturer data sheets are authoritative.

## Verify
- [ ] All glass in a bead from one COE family.
- [ ] Release fully dry before use; didymium glasses on; ventilation running.
- [ ] Mandrel rotation never stopped while glass was molten.
- [ ] Bead annealed per the glass's schedule; holes reamed clean, wet.
