# Copper-foil panel construction

**Goal:** a flat panel or suncatcher whose pieces fit with even small gaps, foil sits
centred, and the solder seams are smooth, rounded beads. Leaded (came) work is a sibling
technique; this skill covers copper foil.

## 1. Pattern
- Draw full size; number every piece and mark grain/colour direction.
- Make two copies: one stays whole as the layout guide, one is cut up.
- Cut the pattern copy with **foil pattern shears**, which remove a thin strip (about the
  width of foil + solder gap) between pieces. Ordinary scissors make pieces too big.

## 2. Cut the glass
- Glue or trace each pattern piece onto the glass.
- Score with an oil-fed **glass cutter** in one continuous, even-pressure pass, edge to
  edge. Listen for a steady "zipping" sound; never go over a score twice (damages the
  wheel and chips the edge).
- Break immediately: **running pliers** for straight and gentle curves; **grozing/breaking
  pliers** for nibbling and inside curves. Deep inside curves need a series of relief scores.
- Gloves optional but eye protection always — chips fly.

## 3. Grind
- A **glass grinder** with water-fed bit brings each piece to the pattern line and
  roughens edges so foil sticks. Keep the sponge wet (dust, overheating).
- Check fit on the layout copy: aim for consistent ~1 mm gaps. Tight fits push pieces
  apart; wide gaps eat solder and look sloppy.
- Wash and dry pieces: grinder residue stops foil adhering.

## 4. Foil
- Choose **copper-foil tape** width so the foil overlaps each face equally — wider for
  thick glass. Black- or silver-backed foil suits clear glass where the back shows.
- Centre the glass edge on the tape, wrap all round, overlap a few mm at the end.
- Fold down both faces and **burnish** firmly with a fid or burnisher. Trim uneven foil
  with a craft knife.

## 5. Solder
- Assemble on the layout, pinned to a board with framing nails or a jig.
- Brush **flux** onto foil, only where you're about to solder.
- **Tack** solder at a few joints so nothing shifts.
- **Tin** every seam (thin flat coat) on both sides, then **bead**: run a raised, rounded
  bead by feeding solder steadily and moving the iron slowly. Iron around 370–400 °C for
  a typical 60/40 tin-lead solder (temperature-controlled irons recommended).
- Solder: 60/40 tin-lead is the traditional choice; **lead-free** solders exist for
  items handled often or by children. Lead safety: wash hands, don't eat/drink at the bench,
  ventilate flux fumes.
- Outer edge: zinc or lead came frame, or a reinforced, soldered-over edge.

## 6. Clean and patina
- Wash with flux remover or warm soapy water right after soldering — flux corrodes.
- Apply **patina** (copper or black) with a sponge while solder is clean; wash again.
  Polish with stained-glass wax.

## Gotchas
- Pieces wander during soldering = not pinned well; tack first.
- Holes or pits in beads: dirty foil or not enough flux.
- Cracked glass while soldering: iron held too long on one spot over thin glass.
- Iron temperatures above are typical; follow your solder maker's range.

## Verify
- [ ] Pattern cut with foil shears; pieces numbered.
- [ ] Scores single-pass; pieces ground to line with even ~1 mm gaps.
- [ ] Foil centred and burnished; flux applied just before soldering.
- [ ] Beads smooth and rounded both sides; flux washed off; patina even.
