# Sandblasting and resist stencils on glass

**Goal:** crisp frosted or carved designs in glass with clean edges and an even texture.
Sandblasting is the most accessible of the glass engraving methods; copper-wheel and
drill engraving and acid etching are covered only briefly below.

## Resist
The resist is a rubbery or vinyl mask; blasting removes glass where the resist is cut away.
- **Self-adhesive vinyl**: fine for light surface frosting; tears under deep carving.
- **Thicker blast resist** (rubber/"buttercut"-style resists): stands up to deep carving
  and stage blasting.
- **Photoresist** films: exposed with UV through a printed film for very fine detail (text,
  fine line art), developed in water.
- Clean glass thoroughly (alcohol) before applying, burnish down firmly — any lifted edge
  lets grit creep under.

## Cutting and weeding
- Transfer the design by drawing on the resist or with a vinyl cutter/plotter.
- Cut with a sharp craft knife held vertical — angled cuts give angled edges.
- **Weed** (peel away) the areas to be blasted. For stage blasting, weed in sequence:
  blast the deepest areas first, then weed the next section and blast again.

## Equipment and abrasive
- **Blast cabinet** with gloves, viewing window, lighting and a **dust collector** — never
  blast glass in open air.
- Abrasive: **aluminium oxide** or **silicon carbide** grit; around 150–220 mesh for surface
  frosting, coarser (80–120) for faster deep carving. Do not use silica sand (silicosis
  risk).
- Air pressure commonly around 20–40 psi for surface frosting on thin glass, higher for
  carving thicker glass — start low and test. Pressure and grit figures vary by equipment.
- Dry, clean compressed air — moisture clumps abrasive and spits.

## Blasting technique
- Hold the nozzle about 10–15 cm away, perpendicular for surface work.
- Move in even overlapping passes, like spray painting. Dwelling cuts uneven pits.
- **Surface etch**: one or two even passes until fully frosted.
- **Carving**: hold longer and closer for depth; angle the nozzle to shape walls. Shading is
  achieved by graduated blasting or "stage blasting" through sequential weeding.
- Check frequently: blow off with air, look against a dark background.

## Cleanup and finishing
- Remove resist (soak warm water if stubborn), wash with soap and water.
- Frosted surfaces mark with fingerprints and grease; some makers apply a commercial glass
  sealer for handling.
- Carved areas can be colour-filled with glass paints or left frosted.

## Other engraving methods (brief)
- **Copper-wheel engraving**: lathe-mounted copper wheels with oil and abrasive; highly
  skilled, fine modelled detail.
- **Drill/rotary engraving**: diamond burrs in a flexible shaft or rotary tool, worked wet.
- **Acid etching (hydrofluoric acid)**: extremely hazardous — even small skin exposure can
  be lethal; leave it to facilities with proper training and controls. Etching creams are
  a weaker alternative but still require care.

## Safety
- Fine abrasive and glass dust are respirable hazards; cabinet sealed and vented through a
  collector; respirator when emptying collector.
- Eye protection when handling blasted pieces (edges stay sharp).
- Annealed glass only — strained glass can break during blasting.

## Gotchas
- Fuzzy edges: resist not burnished, or nozzle angled.
- Blowthrough on thin glass: too much pressure or dwelling.

## Verify
- [ ] Glass clean; resist burnished with no lifted edges.
- [ ] Abrasive is aluminium oxide or silicon carbide (never silica sand).
- [ ] Even overlapping passes; texture uniform under a dark background check.
- [ ] Dust collected; resist removed; piece washed.
