# Full-fuse and slump firing schedules

**Goal:** a fused plate or panel that's flat, stress-free and the shape you planned,
then slumped into a mould. Fusing is two firings for a plate: **fuse** flat, then **slump**.

## Compatibility first
- Use glass tested compatible as a system: e.g. **Bullseye COE 90** or **System 96 /
  Spectrum** lines. COE number alone isn't a guarantee; manufacturer-tested
  "compatible" labelling is what counts. Incompatible glass cracks — sometimes weeks later.
- Test unknown glass with a stress test using a polariscope or two-sheet strip tests.

## Layering and the 6 mm rule
- Glass at full fuse tends toward about **6 mm** thickness (surface tension). Two 3 mm
  layers stay roughly their footprint; one 3 mm layer pulls in and gets ragged edges;
  thicker stacks spread. Design in 6 mm stacks for predictable edges.
- Cut and clean pieces (glass cleaner, no fingerprints — they burn in as marks).

## Shelf prep
- Coat the kiln shelf with **shelf primer** (several thin coats, alternating directions,
  dried or pre-fired as instructed), or use **thin-fire paper** / fibre paper.
- Never fire glass directly on a bare shelf; it sticks permanently.

## Fuse schedules (illustrative — check your glass maker's published schedules)
A typical schedule for a 6 mm two-layer piece of Bullseye-type glass looks like this:
| Segment | Rate | Target | Hold | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ~120–220 °C/h | ~680 °C | 30 min | Bubble squeeze: lets air escape from between layers before they seal |
| 2 | ~330 °C/h (AFAP in some guides) | **full fuse** ~800–815 °C | 10 min | Fuses layers smooth and round-edged |
| 3 | As fast as possible (don't open) | anneal ~480–516 °C | 60 min | **Anneal soak** — removes stress |
| 4 | ~80 °C/h | ~370 °C | 0 | Controlled cooling through the strain range |
| 5 | Off | room temp | — | |
- **Tack fuse** (pieces stuck but keep shape/texture): peak around 720–770 °C.
- **Contour fuse**: in between.
- Thicker work: slower rates and longer annealing — rates roughly halve as thickness
  doubles. Use the manufacturer's thick-slab charts.

## Slumping
- Place the fused piece centred on a **slumping mould** (ceramic or stainless steel)
  coated with primer; moulds need vent holes so trapped air escapes.
- Slump schedule: slow ramp (~150 °C/h) to around 620–680 °C (varies by mould depth and
  glass), hold 10–30 min, peek through a spy hole until it settles into the mould, then
  anneal as above.
- Too hot or too long a slump: glass stretches thin, marks from the mould, or drapes
  unevenly.

## Faults
- **Devitrification** (scummy dull surface): long time in roughly 700–760 °C range or dirty
  glass; clean thoroughly, move through that range briskly, or use devit spray.
- **Bubbles**: no bubble-squeeze segment, or trapped air in design.
- **Needle edges/thin spots**: piece less than ~6 mm.
- **Cracks**: incompatibility (cracks along colour boundaries, often later) or thermal
  shock (clean break during heating or cooling — went too fast or opened the kiln).

## Gotchas
- Schedule numbers above are typical ranges from manufacturer-published guidance; they
  vary by glass line and kiln. Use witness pieces and the maker's charts.
- Don't open the kiln to "check" during cooling — that's a thermal shock.

## Verify
- [ ] All glass from one compatible system; cleaned.
- [ ] Stack designed at ~6 mm; shelf primed or papered.
- [ ] Schedule includes bubble squeeze, anneal hold and controlled cool.
- [ ] Slump peeked and stopped at correct depth; annealed again.
