# Fitting a drawer to its opening (wooden runners)

**Goal:** a solid-wood drawer that slides on its own runners without racking or
rattling, closes flush with its front, and still runs in the humid season.

## 1. Check the opening first
- Measure the opening at **front and back, top and bottom**. A carcase that is
  out of square or has a twisted opening can't be cured by the drawer.
- Sides of the opening should be parallel or very slightly **wider at the back**
  (a fraction of a mm). Narrower at the back = a drawer that binds as it closes.
  Fix that by planing the runners/guides, not the drawer.
- Runners and kickers in the same plane; sight across them (winding sticks) for twist.

## 2. Make the parts deliberately oversize
- **Front:** plane to a tight fit in the opening — it should just enter, angled,
  at one end. This is the reference part.
- **Sides:** same height as the front or a hair less; length to the depth of the
  carcase less any stop allowance. Quartersawn, stable stock if you have it.
- **Back:** narrower in height, sitting on top of the bottom so the bottom can slide
  out under it.
- Usual joints: half-blind dovetails at the front, through dovetails at the back.
- **Bottom:** solid wood with grain running **side to side**, in grooves or drawer
  slips, screwed through a slot into the back, not glued — so it can move.

## 3. Glue up square and flat
Glue up on a flat surface; check diagonals. A drawer that rocks on a flat bench
will rock in the case. Let the glue cure fully before fitting.

## 4. Plane to fit
- Support the drawer on a board clamped in the vice (or a drawer board) so the side
  being planed is backed up.
- Plane the sides **from front to back**, toward the back so you don't spelch the
  dovetail ends; long, light shavings with a sharp, finely set plane.
- Try the drawer often. Look for **shiny/burnished patches** on the sides — plane
  only those.
- Target: slides in fully with **gentle push**, and pushing one front corner does
  not rack it. The traditional test is a "piston fit": pushing one drawer in on a
  well-fitted chest pushes air out of the others. (A demanding standard; not every
  carcase is tight enough to show it.)
- Height: plane the top edges until the drawer clears the kicker by a thin card's
  thickness.

## 5. Seasonal clearance
Fit for the season you are in:
- In a **dry** season, leave a little more side-to-side and top clearance, because it
  will swell.
- In a **humid** season you can fit snug.
Rough workshop guide: a couple of mm total top clearance for a deep (tall) flatsawn
drawer side fitted in winter — **check against your species and climate**.

## 6. Stops and lubrication
- Stops: small blocks glued to the runner at the back of the front (front stops) are
  more accurate than the drawer back hitting the carcase back, which moves.
- Set them so the front sits **flush or just a hair proud** of the carcase.
- Rub the runners and drawer sides with **paraffin wax or a candle**. Don't finish
  the inside surfaces with film finish; it gets tacky.

## Gotchas
- Fit the drawer **before** finishing the outside; finish adds thickness.
- Don't plane the drawer to fix a twisted carcase — fix the runners.
- A drawer bottom glued all round will split or push the sides out.

## Verify
- [ ] Opening parallel or slightly wider at the back, runners free of twist.
- [ ] Drawer glued square and doesn't rock on a flat surface.
- [ ] Slides fully without racking from a one-corner push.
- [ ] Clearance appropriate to the current season.
- [ ] Stops set; front flush; runners waxed.
