# Turning a bowl from a blank

**Goal:** a face-grain bowl, outside and inside shaped with a bowl gouge, a clean
surface from the tool, and no catches or flying blanks.

## Safety first (bowls are where turners get hurt)
- **Full face shield** (impact-rated), not just glasses. A blank coming off at
  speed is the real hazard.
- Stand **out of the firing line** (to the side of the blank's plane) when starting
  the lathe. Spin the blank by hand first to check clearance with the tool rest.
- **Start slow.** A common rule of thumb: diameter (in inches) × rpm ≈ 6,000–9,000
  for a balanced blank; i.e. a 300 mm (12") blank roughly 500–750 rpm, lower if
  out of balance or cracked. Increase only once it's round. Treat this as a
  starting point, not gospel.
- Inspect for cracks, bark inclusions and loose knots; cut them away or don't turn it.

## 1. Prepare and mount
- Cut the blank roughly round on the bandsaw. Mark the centre on what will be the
  **top** (rim) face.
- Mount on a **faceplate** with stout screws (several, well into sound wood) or
  between centres / on a screw chuck. The foot will be shaped first.

## 2. Shape the outside
- Tool rest close to the work, just below centre height so the gouge cuts at or
  slightly above centre.
- **Bowl gouge**, flute pointing roughly at 10–11 o'clock (for a cut moving left),
  handle low, **bevel rubbing** before you lift the handle to engage the edge. Cut
  from the **foot towards the rim** (from smaller to larger diameter) on the
  outside — that cuts with the grain fibres supported.
- Rough to shape, then refine with light cuts. Pleasing curves have no flat spots
  or bumps; run your hand over it with the lathe off.
- Cut a **tenon** (for jaws that grip outside) or **recess** (for jaws that expand
  inside) at the foot, sized to your chuck's jaws, with the dovetail angle matching
  the jaws. A poorly fitted tenon is the usual reason bowls come off the chuck.

## 3. Reverse and hollow
- Mount in the scroll chuck on the tenon, tighten firmly, retighten after a minute.
- Face off the rim. Hollow **from the rim towards the centre** (inside, that is
  the downhill direction). Start with the flute facing ~2 o'clock (toward the
  centre), bevel rubbing, and roll and push the tool as the arc progresses.
- Take the inside in steps: hollow the centre area first, then widen, keeping wall
  thickness even. Thick walls near the rim stop flex and chatter until the last pass.
- Check wall thickness with double-ended calipers.

## 4. Finish cuts and sanding
- A final light push cut, then **shear scraping** (scraper tilted ~45°) to clean up
  tear-out on the end-grain areas (two spots on opposite sides).
- Sand with the lathe at **low speed**, working through the grits; dust extraction
  and a mask — fine dust from some species is a sensitiser.
- Reverse onto jam chuck or vacuum/Cole jaws to finish the foot if needed.

## 5. Green wood
Wet wood turns easily but warps as it dries. Rough-turn to a wall of ~10% of the
diameter, seal end grain, dry slowly (weeks to months, paper bag or shavings),
then **re-turn** round when dry ("twice-turned"). Or turn thin in one go and
accept and enjoy the warp.

## Catches: why they happen
- The edge cuts **unsupported by the bevel** — the tool bites and jumps.
- The flute rolled too far open toward the wood (left wing exposed on the outside).
- Tool rest too far from the work.
Fix: bevel first, then edge; rest close; fresh, sharp edge.

## Verify
- [ ] Face shield on, out of firing line, blank checked, speed started low.
- [ ] Tenon or recess fits the chuck's jaw profile exactly.
- [ ] Outside cut foot-to-rim, inside cut rim-to-centre, bevel rubbing.
- [ ] Wall thickness even by calipers; end-grain tear-out cleaned up.
