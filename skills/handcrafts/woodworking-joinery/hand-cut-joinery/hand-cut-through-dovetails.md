# Hand-cut through dovetails (tails first)

**Goal:** two boards joined at a corner with interlocking tails and pins that go
together with hand pressure or light mallet taps, no gaps on the show faces, and
nothing broken during the fit.

## 1. Stock prep (where most bad dovetails really start)
- Both boards **flat, square-edged and the same thickness**, ends shot dead square
  to the faces and edges on a shooting board. An out-of-square end puts a gap on
  one corner no matter how well you saw.
- Mark the **face side and face edge** on each board; all gauging is from these.
- Set a marking gauge (a cutting/wheel gauge is easier on cross grain) to the
  **thickness of the mating board plus a hair** (~0.3 mm). The proud pins and tails
  get planed flush afterwards; flush-or-short ends can't be fixed.
- Gauge the baseline all round the end of both boards.

## 2. Lay out the tails
- Ratio: roughly **1:6 for softwood, 1:8 for hardwood** (the difference is mostly
  aesthetic; both hold). A dovetail marker or a sliding bevel does it.
- Start with **half-pins at each edge** (they stop the outer tail splitting off),
  then space the tails by eye or with dividers. Narrow pins/wide tails reads as
  hand-cut; equal spacing reads as router-made.
- Square the lines across the end grain, then mark the slopes on the face. Hatch
  the **waste** clearly — sawing on the wrong side of a line is the classic error.

## 3. Saw the tails
- Clamp the board **vertical and low** in the vice. Many makers tilt the board so
  each cut is plumb, then re-tilt for the other slope; it's easier than sawing at
  an angle.
- Saw on the waste side, **leaving the line**, down to — not past — the baseline on
  both faces. Watch both faces; a saw drifting off square across the thickness is
  what makes gaps on the inside face.
- Cut the half-pin shoulders with the board horizontal.

## 4. Remove the waste
- Coping or fret saw out most of the waste, staying ~1 mm above the baseline.
- Pare to the line: chisel in the baseline knife wall, half the thickness from each
  face, so you never blow out the far side. Undercut very slightly (a degree or two)
  in the middle, never at the faces.

## 5. Transfer to the pin board
- Stand the pin board in the vice, lay the tail board on it (a plane on its side
  makes a good support), align the face edges and the **inside of the tails exactly
  on the pin board's baseline**.
- Mark round the tails with a **sharp knife**, not a pencil — knife lines are the
  fit. Then square the lines down the face to the baseline with a pencil.
- Mark the waste again. Here the waste is between the knife lines' outer sides.

## 6. Saw and chop the pins
- Saw the pins **on the waste side, splitting nothing** — the knife line must stay
  visible on the pin. Undersized pins = gaps; oversized pins = a bit of paring.
  Err fat.
- Chop the waste from both faces with the chisel, as for the tails.

## 7. Test fit and fit
- Do **not** drive it fully home the first time. Tap it a third of the way, pull
  it apart, and look for **burnished (shiny) spots** — those are the tight places.
  Pare only those.
- When it goes together: glue sparingly on the pin cheeks, assemble, check square,
  and let the glue cure. Plane the proud ends flush working **in from the corner**
  so you don't spelch the far edge.

## Gotchas
- A dull saw wanders; a dovetail saw with fine rip teeth (15–20 ppi) sharpened for rip.
- Very soft woods crush; a tight fit in pine can split the tail board on assembly.
  Ease off sooner than you would in oak.
- Gaps can be shimmed with a glued sliver of the same wood, grain matching; better
  to practise on a scrap pair first than to shim a show joint. (Common workshop
  advice; how invisible a shim gets varies a lot by species.)

## Verify
- [ ] Ends shot square; gauge set a hair over mating thickness.
- [ ] Waste marked on both boards before every cut.
- [ ] Pins transferred with a knife from the actual tails, not measured.
- [ ] First fit dry and partial; shiny spots pared, nothing split.
- [ ] Assembled joint square, no gaps on show faces, proud ends planed flush.
