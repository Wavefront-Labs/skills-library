# Window-method marquetry and pressing

**Goal:** a picture or pattern of knife-cut veneers that fit tightly, laid flat on a
stable board, without gaps, lifts or warping.

## Materials
- Veneers (~0.6 mm typical; thicker "saw-cut" veneers need a fret saw instead of knife).
- A sharp scalpel or craft knife with spare blades, cutting mat, steel rule.
- **Gummed veneer tape** (water-activated paper tape) or low-tack tape.
- A **waster veneer** to make the window (cheap, plain veneer the size of the picture).
- Substrate: MDF or good plywood. **Balancing veneer** for the back.

## 1. Prepare the design and veneers
- Number each part on the drawing and mark **grain direction** — grain is your
  shading tool (sky horizontal, tree trunks vertical, water across).
- Flatten buckled veneer: dampen lightly (or use a commercial flattening solution),
  press between absorbent paper and boards, change paper until dry.

## 2. The window method
- Trace the design onto the waster.
- Cut out **one** part from the waster — creating a window.
- Slide the chosen veneer under the window, move it to find the best figure and
  grain, tape it in place behind.
- **Knife round the window edge** onto the veneer beneath, using the window as the
  template. Cut with several **light passes** — a heavy first cut wanders with the
  grain and splits brittle veneers.
- Remove, finish cutting the piece free, and insert it into the window from behind;
  tape it in. The piece now becomes part of the edge for its neighbours.
- Repeat for each part, generally working **background first, then foreground**,
  so foreground edges are cut last and stay crisp.
- Make cuts slightly angled (knife tilted a few degrees) so the piece wedges tight
  from the face; keep tilt consistent.

## 3. Sand shading
Dip edges of a piece into **hot sand** in a pan for a few seconds to scorch them
for shadow and roundness. Test on scrap; heat varies and it goes from tan to burnt
fast. Dampen the piece afterwards if it curls.

## 4. Laying the picture
- Picture taped on its **face side** (tape holds everything together; it's scraped off later).
- Glue: **PVA** works but creep can let joints open; **urea-formaldehyde** or
  powdered resin glues and **hot hide glue** are favoured for stability. Use per the
  manufacturer's open time and pressure guidance.
- Veneer **both faces** of the substrate at the same time, with a balancing veneer of
  similar thickness on the back, grain running the same way as the dominant face
  grain — otherwise the board cups.
- Apply glue to the substrate, not the veneer (veneer curls when wet). Even, thin coat.
- Press: a **veneer press** with flat cauls, or a **vacuum bag** (easier and even).
  Use release paper/plastic so squeeze-through doesn't glue the caul on. Press for
  the glue's full clamp time.

## 5. Finishing
- Dampen and peel/scrape off the gummed tape. Scrape with a card scraper, then
  sand lightly by hand, keeping the block flat — veneer is thin and different
  woods sand at different rates; sand-through happens fast.
- Fill small gaps with a filler made from the same veneer's dust and glue if needed.

## Gotchas
- Bubbles/lifted areas: tap with a fingernail (hollow sound). Lift with a warm
  iron (hide glue) or inject glue with a syringe and re-press.
- Burl veneers are brittle — back them with paper tape before cutting.
- Exact pressures and times depend on the glue; follow the product sheet.

## Verify
- [ ] Grain direction planned for every part.
- [ ] Each piece cut from the window, light passes, fits tight.
- [ ] Both faces veneered at once with a balancing veneer.
- [ ] Pressed evenly for the glue's full time; no hollow sounds.
- [ ] Tape removed, scraped/sanded without sanding through.
