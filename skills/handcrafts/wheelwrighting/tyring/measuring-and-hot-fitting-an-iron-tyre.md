# Measuring and hot-fitting an iron tyre

**Goal:** an iron (or mild steel) tyre slightly smaller than the wheel, expanded by
heat, dropped on, and shrunk by quenching so it compresses the whole wheel
tight. This was a shared job between wheelwright and smith, and it is a fast,
hot, team operation: plan it.

## 1. Measure with the traveller
- The **traveller** is a wheel on a handle, marked at a point. Roll it round the
  wheel's rim, counting turns, and mark where it ends; then roll it along the
  straight tyre bar (or round the made tyre) the same way.
- This avoids tape stretch and gives the true circumference.
- Measure the wheel **with felloe joints still open** as left for tyring (see
  `handcrafts.wheelwrighting.felloe-making.sawing-boring-and-jointing-felloes`).

## 2. Allow for shrink
- The tyre is made shorter than the wheel's circumference. The amount — the
  **"draw"** — depends on wheel size, tyre section and how much compression the
  wheel needs. Traditional allowances were learned per shop; one rough guide
  sometimes cited is on the order of 1/4 in to 1/2 in (~6–12 mm) for a cart wheel,
  but **treat that as unverified**: too much draw over-dishes or breaks spokes, too
  little leaves a loose wheel.
- Mark the shortened length on the bar with the traveller.

## 3. Make the hoop
- Bend the bar into a circle on a **tyre bender** (rollers), or by hand over a
  former for small work.
- **Forge-weld** the ends (scarf joint) or, in modern practice, electric-weld.
  Dress the weld flush. Re-check with the traveller.
- Punch or drill for tyre bolts/nails if the tradition uses them.

## 4. Heat
- Lay the wheel face down on the **tyring platform** (an iron plate with a hole for
  the nave) and clamp it down.
- Heat the tyre **evenly all round** in a ring fire (traditionally wood or shavings
  laid round it) until it has expanded enough to drop over the wheel — the
  expansion of iron is small (on the order of 1% at a dull-red heat), so even
  heating is essential. Don't take it to welding heat.

## 5. Fit and quench
- With **tyre dogs** (long levered tongs), several people lift the hot tyre and drop
  it over the felloes, then lever and hammer it down level.
- The wood will smoke and char; immediately **quench** with water from cans all round
  — this shrinks the iron and stops the felloes burning. The felloe joints close
  with a crack and the wheel tightens.
- Keep quenching evenly; uneven cooling pulls the wheel out of true.

## 6. After
- Check: joints closed, wheel true and dish acceptable, tyre seated level.
- Drive tyre bolts/nails if used. Bore and fit the axle box if not done earlier.

## Safety
Hot iron, a fire and several people lifting together. Leather gloves and aprons,
eye protection, a rehearsed lift with one person calling it, water ready **before**
the tyre comes out of the fire, and a clear floor.

## Verify
- [ ] Wheel measured with the traveller, joints open.
- [ ] Shrink allowance chosen deliberately and recorded.
- [ ] Hoop welded sound and re-measured.
- [ ] Heated evenly; fitted and quenched evenly all round.
- [ ] Felloe joints closed; wheel true; tyre seated.
