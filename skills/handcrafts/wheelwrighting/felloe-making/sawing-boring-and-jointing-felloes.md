# Sawing, boring and jointing felloes

**Goal:** a ring of curved rim segments that receive the spoke tongues and,
when the tyre is shrunk on, are compressed into one tight rim.

## 1. Timber and layout
- Traditional felloe timbers: **ash** (tough), **elm** (resists splitting), and
  beech for some work. Chosen with natural curve where possible, so grain follows
  the arc and short grain at the ends is minimised.
- Usually **one felloe per two spokes** (so a 12-spoke wheel has 6 felloes);
  other ratios exist.
- Mark out from a **pattern** made to the wheel's radius (inner and outer arcs) and
  the felloe's depth. Each felloe's length = the wheel's circumference ÷ number of
  felloes.

## 2. Saw and dress
- Saw the curves on a **bandsaw** or, traditionally, a narrow felloe saw (a frame
  saw), then dress the faces with an adze and plane.
- Faces flat and parallel; inner and outer curves true to pattern.

## 3. Boring for spoke tongues
- With the spokes already driven into the nave and their tongues cut (see
  `handcrafts.wheelwrighting.spoke-making.cleaving-shaping-and-tenoning-oak-spokes`),
  offer each felloe up to its two spokes and **mark the actual positions** of the
  tongues, rather than by measurement.
- Bore the holes on the line the spoke actually takes — because of dish, the
  spokes aren't radial in the plane of the felloe, so the bore isn't square to the
  felloe face. Hold the felloe in position and sight the angle, or use a boring
  jig/guide.
- Holes slightly smaller than the tongue makes for a tight drive.

## 4. Jointing and dowelling
- Ends of adjacent felloes are cut to meet on a line pointing at the wheel's
  centre, and drilled for a **dowel** (wood traditionally) to keep them aligned.
- **Leave a small gap** at each joint (commonly a few mm, depending on wheel size
  and practice). When the hot tyre shrinks, it pulls the felloes together, closing
  the joints and putting the whole wheel into compression. A wheel with joints
  already closed before tyring can't be tightened by the tyre.
- Exact gap is learned; too much and the tyre can't close it, too little and the
  wheel isn't held tight. Not given as a figure here because it depends on tyre
  shrink allowance (see `handcrafts.wheelwrighting.tyring.measuring-and-hot-fitting-an-iron-tyre`).

## 5. Fitting the felloes
Drive the felloes onto the tongues, working round the wheel, tapping evenly so
the spokes are not pulled out of line. Check the wheel is true and level before
tyring.

## Gotchas
- Short grain at a felloe's end: it'll break out at the dowel. Choose curved stock
  or keep felloes shorter.
- Boring square to the face when the spoke comes in at an angle: the felloe sits
  twisted and the wheel runs out of true.
- Bent (steam-bent one-piece) rims are a different tradition, mainly for lighter
  wheels.

## Verify
- [ ] Felloes sawn to pattern, grain following the curve.
- [ ] Tongue positions marked from the spokes in place.
- [ ] Holes bored on the spokes' true line.
- [ ] Joints radial, dowelled, with a deliberate gap.
- [ ] Felloes driven evenly; wheel checked true before tyring.
