# Action regulation and hammer voicing on a new build

**Goal:** turn an assembled but unregulated piano into an even, responsive instrument.
Regulation is a sequence — each step depends on the ones before — and voicing is done last.
Figures are typical starting points; follow the action manufacturer's specification.

## 1. Before regulating: stabilise the strings
- **Chipping up**: the first rough tunings on a newly strung piano. New strings stretch and
  the soundboard and bridges settle; expect several pitch raises over days to weeks before
  the tuning holds. Regulation is done after the tuning has largely stabilised, and touched up again.
- **String levelling** at the hammer strike point so all strings of a unison are struck
  together; **hammer spacing** to strike the strings squarely.

## 2. Bed the keyframe (grand)
The keyframe must sit firmly on the keybed with no rocking, or regulation drifts and keys
"knock". Tap along the frame for clicks; ease high spots on the frame's underside (or adjust
glide bolts) until it seats evenly.

## 3. Regulation order (grand action, condensed)
1. **Key level**: front edges of keys level (punchings under the balance rail), white keys at
   a set height, sharps level with each other.
2. **Key dip**: depth of travel, commonly about 10 mm for whites on many grands (check
   spec) — adjusted with front-rail punchings.
3. **Hammer line / blow distance**: rest distance from hammer to string, often ~45–48 mm on
   grands (spec varies), set via capstans or wippen support.
4. **Jack position**: jack sits under the knuckle correctly (adjust jack regulating button/screw).
5. **Let-off**: the point the jack trips and the hammer escapes — close to the string,
   often 1–3 mm (closer in the treble). Too far = weak control; too close = blocking.
6. **Drop**: how far the hammer drops after let-off on slow depression (repetition lever),
   often a little more than let-off.
7. **Repetition spring** strength: hammer rises slowly when key is held halfway, without
   bouncing or failing to return.
8. **Backcheck / checking**: hammer caught at a consistent height on the backcheck after a
   forte blow.
9. **Aftertouch**: small extra key travel after let-off; confirms dip and let-off are balanced.
10. **Dampers**: lift timing (begin lifting at about half key depression), even lift from the
    sustain pedal, full damping with no buzz.
11. **Pedals**: una corda shift, sostenuto, sustain lost motion.
Uprights follow an analogous sequence with different parts (bridle straps, damper spoons).

## 4. Voicing the hammers
Voicing changes hammer felt resilience to even out tone after regulation.
- New hammers are often too bright or uneven. Listen note by note at several dynamics.
- **Needling**: insert voicing needles into the shoulders of the hammer (not the striking
  point at first) to soften/darken tone; deep shoulder needling affects loud playing,
  shallow near the crown affects soft. Small steps, play-test constantly.
- **Hardening**: lacquer or hardening solution applied to the hammer shoulders to brighten
  dull notes — use sparingly; it's hard to reverse.
- **Filing/shaping** hammers to restore the crown profile on used hammers; on new hammers,
  only minor shaping.
- Aim for **evenness first** (no note jumping out), then overall tone colour.

## Gotchas
- Regulating before the tuning and keyframe settle wastes the work.
- Changing one parameter shifts the next — always follow the order.
- Over-needling can't be undone easily; many technicians start conservatively.
- Humidity swings change regulation; recommend climate control to the client.

## Verify
- [ ] Tuning stable across a week before final regulation.
- [ ] Keyframe seated with no knocking.
- [ ] Each regulation step measured and recorded across sample notes per section.
- [ ] Every note repeats reliably at a fast trill, pianissimo to fortissimo.
- [ ] Voicing even across the compass at soft, medium and loud dynamics.
