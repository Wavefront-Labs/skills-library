# Hairing a bow

**Goal:** a flat, even ribbon of hair at the correct length and tension, held by snugly
fitted wedges, that doesn't damage the stick or frog. Rehairing is the bowmaker's bread and
butter and the clearest test of hand skill. Don't learn on a valuable bow.

## 1. Materials and tools
- **Hair**: stallion tail hair, white (e.g. Mongolian, Siberian grades) for violin/viola/cello;
  black or salt-and-pepper sometimes for double bass. Buy in pre-sorted hanks.
- **Plugs/wedges**: maple or similar, cut to fit the tip mortise, the frog mortise, and the
  spreader wedge under the ferrule.
- Fine knives, small chisels, a hair comb, rosin, a spirit lamp, thread/silk, a bow-hair clamp
  or holder, hide glue or a dab of rosin as used by some makers.

## 2. Removing the old hair
1. Slacken the screw, unscrew and remove the frog.
2. Pry out the **spreader wedge**, slide the ferrule back, and remove the hair and frog plug.
3. Lift out the **tip plug** carefully — the tip (ivory/bone/metal/synthetic plate over
   a delicate head) is the most fragile part of the bow; never lever against the tip plate.
4. Clean mortises of old glue and fragments; check the head for cracks — a cracked head is
   a repair, not a rehair.

## 3. Preparing the hair
- Count/weigh a hank appropriate to the instrument (violin hanks are commonly cited around
  150–200 hairs or a few grams by weight; follow the bow's previous hair or your supplier).
- **Wash** in mild soapy water, rinse, comb straight while wet, and dry. All hairs should
  be oriented the same way (root ends together) — makers differ on which end goes to the tip.

## 4. Tip end
1. Tie the hank end tightly with thread, trim, and briefly flare/singe with the spirit lamp
   or dip in rosin so the knot locks.
2. Cut a **tip plug** that fits the mortise snugly, with the hair knot seated under it.
3. Seat the hair and plug; trim the plug flush. Too tight a plug can split the head.

## 5. Frog end and length
1. Put the frog on the stick with the screw adjusted so the **eyelet** sits a set distance
   from the front of the mortise (commonly a few mm forward, leaving room for later
   tightening and seasonal stretching).
2. Wet and comb the hair straight down the stick to the frog, checking it lies even and
   untwisted. Mark the length.
3. Tie at the mark, trim, flare, and set into the frog mortise with the **frog plug**.
4. Slide the **ferrule** over the hair, insert the **spreader wedge** so the hair spreads as
   a flat, even ribbon with no gaps or crossed hairs; reseat the slide.

## 6. Finishing
- Comb the ribbon again; let the hair dry (it shrinks as it dries). Check that, when the
  screw is fully slack, the hair is loose, and when tightened to playing tension the stick
  keeps a healthy camber without the eyelet running out of thread.
- **Rosin**: new hair needs generous rosin before it grips; powder rosin speeds the first application.

## Gotchas
- Twisted ribbon at the frog = uneven tension and one edge breaking hairs constantly.
- Hair too short = bow can't be slackened (stresses the stick/camber); too long = runs out of screw.
- Humidity changes hair length noticeably; tell clients it's normal.
- Counts and dimensions vary by tradition and instrument; treat numbers as orientation.

## Verify
- [ ] Head and frog checked for cracks before and after.
- [ ] Plugs fit snugly and flush; no wood split.
- [ ] Ribbon flat and even across its width, no crossing hairs.
- [ ] Bow fully slackens and reaches playing tension within the screw's range.
- [ ] Stick camber unchanged at playing tension.
