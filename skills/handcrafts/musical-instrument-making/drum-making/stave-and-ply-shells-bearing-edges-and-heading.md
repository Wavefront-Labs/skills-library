# Stave and ply shells, bearing edges and heading

**Goal:** a round, true shell with accurate bearing edges so the head seals and tunes
cleanly, and a properly fitted head — synthetic or natural skin.

## 1. Shell construction options
- **Stave**: vertical staves with bevelled edges glued into a polygon (like a barrel), then
  turned round on a lathe. Stave count and bevel angle: 360° ÷ (2 × number of staves) for each
  edge's bevel (e.g. 24 staves → 7.5° each edge). Strong, resonant, uses solid wood.
- **Segment (block)**: stacked rings of short segments, brick-bond offset, glued and turned.
  Good use of short stock; many glue lines.
- **Ply**: thin veneers glued in a mould under pressure, grain directions alternated.
  Commercial standard; needs a mould and press. Thickness and wood species shape tone.
- **Steam-bent / single-ply**: one solid board steamed, bent into a hoop round a form, and
  scarf-jointed; often with reinforcement hoops inside.
- Djembe and many hand drums are **carved** from a single log.
Check roundness: measure diameter at several angles; out-of-round shells won't seal heads.

## 2. Bearing edges
The bearing edge is the top lip where the head contacts — the single biggest factor in
how the drum tunes and sounds.
- Common profiles: **45° with a slight round-over** (bright, sensitive, more attack),
  **round-over** (warmer, vintage), double 45°. Cut on a router table with the shell
  riding on a flat jig, or with a router riding the shell.
- The **peak** must be continuous, level and in a single plane: check by setting the shell
  on a flat surface (glass/granite) with a light inside — gaps show light. Correct by
  sanding on a flat surface in a figure-8 motion.
- **Snare beds**: shallow dips in the snare-side edge where the snare wires cross, so they
  sit evenly. Typically shallow (around 1–2 mm deep), gradual, symmetrical — measure and sand.

## 3. Hardware
- Lay out lugs evenly with a paper strip template around the shell; drill square to the
  surface with a drill guide. Pair lugs with tension rods so the heads pull evenly.
- Seal the shell interior and exterior (oil, lacquer) before mounting hardware.

## 4. Heading
- **Synthetic (mylar) heads**: seat by tensioning evenly to firm, pressing the centre to
  stretch it, then detune and tune up again.
- **Natural skin (calfskin, goatskin, rawhide)**:
  1. Soak the skin until pliable (minutes for thin goatskin, longer for rawhide).
  2. Lay it over the shell with an even collar; fit flesh hoop or lacing (rope-tuned drums
     such as djembe use rings and a verticals/diamond-weave lacing system).
  3. Pull down evenly all round. Let it dry slowly at room temperature — it shrinks tight.
  4. Shave or trim excess hair/skin once set.
  Skins react to humidity: a drum tight in dry air can go slack in humid air, and a skin
  tensioned wet in a dry room can tear. Leave a little tuning range.

## 5. Tuning
- Finger-tight all rods; then tighten in a **star (opposite) pattern**, the same amount each
  (e.g. quarter turns). Tap an inch in front of each rod and match the pitch around the head.
- Snares: tension until crisp with no choke.

## Gotchas
- Bevel errors accumulate across staves — test-fit dry with a band clamp before gluing.
- Glue starvation on ply shells causes delamination and buzzes.
- Bearing edges must be done after the shell is finished/sealed only if the finish is
  removed from the very peak; finish buildup on the edge causes uneven seating.
- Numbers here are common practice, not standards.

## Verify
- [ ] Shell round within your tolerance at several diameters.
- [ ] Bearing edge shows no light against a flat surface.
- [ ] Snare beds symmetrical and even.
- [ ] Head pitch matches at every tension point.
- [ ] Skin heads dried evenly with no slack spots or tears.
