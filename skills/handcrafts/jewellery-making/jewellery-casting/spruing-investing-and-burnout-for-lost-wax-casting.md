# Spruing, investing and burnout for lost-wax casting

**Goal:** a complete, dense casting from a carved or printed wax model — no
missing prongs, no bubbles, no shrinkage holes — using gypsum-bonded investment
and a small kiln. The figures below are typical for silver with a common hobby
investment; **your investment and alloy data sheets override them**.

## 1. Sprue the model
- Attach the sprue to the **thickest section** of the piece; metal shrinks as it
  cools and must be fed from a reservoir, so the sprue should be **thicker than the
  section it feeds** (e.g. 3–4 mm wax rod for a ring shank of 2 mm).
- Keep it short (5–10 mm) and smooth-filleted where it joins; no sharp corners
  that break off investment.
- Mount on the sprue base so the top of the model sits **6–10 mm below** the
  flask rim and at least 6 mm from the walls.
- Weigh the tree (wax + sprues) **before** investing.

## 2. Metal weight
**Metal needed = wax weight × metal specific gravity**, plus a button (≈ 10–20 g
for small flasks). SGs: sterling ≈ 10.4, 14 ct yellow gold ≈ 13, 18 ct yellow ≈ 15.5
(approximate — check your alloy). Example: 1.8 g wax × 10.4 = 18.7 g + 15 g button ≈ 34 g.

## 3. Invest
1. Spray the tree with debubbliser/wetting agent; let it dry.
2. Weigh powder and measure water to the ratio on the bag — commonly about
   **38–40 ml water per 100 g powder** — with water at around 21–27 °C.
3. Add powder to water, mix 2–3 minutes until smooth.
4. **Vacuum** the bowl until it rises and breaks (about 1–1.5 min), pour gently
   down the flask wall (not onto the wax), then vacuum the flask again ~1 min.
5. Total working time is short — about **9–10 minutes** from water touching powder
   to set. Let the flask stand **1–2 hours** before burnout.

## 4. Burnout (typical stepped schedule)
Remove the sprue base and place the flask **sprue hole down** at first.
| Step | Temperature | Hold |
|---|---|---|
| Steam out wax | 150 °C (300 °F) | 1–2 h |
| Intermediate | 370 °C (700 °F) | 2 h |
| Full burnout | 730 °C (1350 °F) | 3 h |
| Casting temp (sterling, average work) | 480–540 °C (900–1000 °F) | ≥1 h to stabilise |
Thin, filigree work → hotter flask; heavy pieces → cooler (unconfirmed for your
mix — follow the investment maker's chart). Do not exceed ~730 °C: gypsum investment
breaks down and releases sulphur, which pits silver.

## 5. Cast
- Melt sterling with flux in a dedicated crucible to roughly **1000–1040 °C**
  (it melts near 890 °C; a little superheat helps fill).
- **Vacuum casting:** flask on the vacuum table, pump on, pour in one steady
  stream into the sprue hole. **Centrifugal:** balance the arm first, then release
  the instant the metal spins freely.
- Wait until the button loses its red colour (about **5–10 minutes** for silver),
  then quench in water — the investment breaks out violently. Pickle.

## Faults
- **Incomplete fill (rounded, missing tips):** flask or metal too cold, sprue too
  thin, or too few/too thin feeding sprues on thin sections.
- **Nodules / bubbles on the surface:** air trapped in investment; vacuum longer,
  use wetting agent.
- **Shrinkage porosity** (spongy holes near the sprue): sprue thinner than the
  section or too long; feed the heavy section directly.
- **Gas porosity / pitting:** overheated metal, dirty scrap, or burnout over
  temperature.
- **Fins:** investment cracked — heated too fast from cold or dried out before burnout.

## Safety
- Investment powder contains crystalline silica (quartz/cristobalite): mix under
  extraction or wear a P3/N100 respirator; clean up wet.
- Molten metal and wet tools explode: preheat crucibles and stir rods; work over
  dry sand; wear face shield, leather apron and closed shoes; no synthetic clothing.
- Kiln fumes from burnout: vent outdoors.

## Verify
- [ ] Sprue thicker than the feed section, short, filleted; model 6–10 mm below rim.
- [ ] Wax weighed; metal calculated with the right SG plus button.
- [ ] Investment mixed to the sheet's ratio, vacuumed twice, set ≥1 h.
- [ ] Burnout stepped; flask at casting temperature and stable.
- [ ] Casting complete with no fins, bubbles or porosity at the sprue.
