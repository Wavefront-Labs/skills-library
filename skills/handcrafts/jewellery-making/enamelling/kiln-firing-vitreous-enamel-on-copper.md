# Kiln-firing vitreous enamel on copper

**Goal:** a smooth, glossy, flawless enamel layer fused to a copper piece that
doesn't crack, craze or pop off — the base skill under every enamel technique, from
simple sifted pendants to cloisonné and champlevé.

## 1. Metal and enamel
- **Copper** sheet 0.8–1.0 mm (18–20 ga) for small pieces; fine silver and high-carat
  gold also enamel well. Sterling and brass are difficult (firescale, zinc).
- Lead-free **80-mesh** enamel powders; wash transparents before use (swirl in
  water, pour off the cloudy fines, repeat until clear) or they fire cloudy.
- Pieces should be **slightly domed** (doming block) rather than flat: domes
  resist warping and cracking.

## 2. Clean
- The metal must be **grease-free**: abrade with a glass brush or 400-grit wet,
  then pickle, rinse, and test — water should sheet across it without beading.
- Handle only by the edges from now on.

## 3. Counter-enamel first
- Enamel shrinks differently from metal on cooling; enamel on **one side only**
  pulls the piece and cracks or pops off. Apply a layer to the **back**
  (counter-enamel) of anything thinner than about 1 mm or larger than a coin.
- Spray the back with a holding agent (e.g. a gum-based product), sift an even
  layer about as thick as a postcard, dry, and fire with the front still bare —
  or fire both sides in the same firing on a trivet.

## 4. Applying the front
- **Sifting:** spray holding agent, sift from ~10 cm through an 80-mesh sifter in an
  even layer you can't see metal through but no thicker. Clean the edge with a
  fingertip.
- **Wet packing:** mix enamel with water (a drop of holding agent) to a wet-sand
  consistency and lay it with a fine spatula or quill, then tap the edge to settle
  and wick off water with tissue. Used for cloisonné cells and champlevé recesses.
- Let the piece **dry completely** on top of the kiln before firing; steam blows
  enamel off.

## 5. Firing
- Kiln at about **790–840 °C (1450–1550 °F)**. Typical firing for a small piece:
  **1.5–3 minutes** (unconfirmed for your kiln and enamel — run a test tile).
- Watch the stages through the door: **sugary** (grainy, dull) → **orange peel**
  (fused but rippled) → **glossy** (smooth). Remove at glossy. Under-fired enamel is
  rough; over-fired transparents burn out, opaques thin and show metal at edges.
- Lift out on a trivet with a firing fork, cool on a heat-proof surface. Enamel
  pings and crackles slightly as it cools.
- Build colour in **thin layers, one firing each**; thick single layers crack.

## 6. Cloisonné and champlevé (outline)
- **Cloisonné:** fire a clear flux base, bend fine-silver cloisonné wire
  (~0.25 × 1 mm) into cells, set it on the base and fire it in; wet-pack each cell
  and fire in several layers until the enamel is slightly proud of the wires.
- **Champlevé:** etch or engrave recesses into the metal and fill them.
- **Stoning:** grind the surface flat under running water with an alundum stone or
  diamond pad (220 → 400 → 600), then fire once more to restore gloss (flash fire).

## Faults
- **Cracks / chips:** no counter-enamel, enamel too thick, or flat thin metal.
- **Black specks / dark edges:** firescale from copper oxide; clean better, stone
  and fire again; brush off scale between firings.
- **Pits and bubbles:** grease, or wet enamel fired before dry.
- **Cloudy transparents:** fines not washed out, or under-fired.
- **Burnt-out reds/pinks:** over-fired or too many firings; fire them last.

## Safety
- Don't inhale enamel dust: sift over paper, recover powder, wear a dust mask when
  sifting, and never eat or drink at the bench.
- Kiln at 800 °C: long firing fork, heat-resistant gloves, eye protection
  (IR-filtering glasses are advisable for frequent kiln watching).
- Use lead-free enamels for wearable pieces.

## Verify
- [ ] Metal domed, cleaned until water sheets, handled by the edges.
- [ ] Back counter-enamelled.
- [ ] Each layer thin, dried completely, and fired to the glossy stage.
- [ ] No cracks, pits or firescale specks after cooling.
- [ ] Cloisonné/champlevé surfaces stoned flat and flash-fired to gloss.
