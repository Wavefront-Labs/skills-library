# Abrasive sequence and mirror polishing for jewellery

**Goal:** a flawless mirror surface — no scratches, ripples, or rounded edges — in
the least time. The rule underneath everything: **each step only removes the
scratches of the step before.** Polish cannot hide a deep scratch; it only makes it
shine.

## 1. Shape first, polish last
- Do all filing, soldering and setting-seat cutting **before** finishing. Polishing
  a piece then soldering it again throws firescale and pits back on.
- Remove firescale on sterling at the abrasive stage — it is a layer of copper
  oxide below the surface that polishing alone only reveals.

## 2. The abrasive ladder
Work in one direction per grit, then turn **90°** for the next grit so you can see
when the old scratches are gone.
| Stage | Tool | Removes |
|---|---|---|
| Shape | Cut 2 file, then cut 4 needle files | Saw marks, excess solder |
| Coarse | 220–320 grit paper on a sanding stick | File marks |
| Medium | 400 → 600 | 320 scratches |
| Fine | 800 → 1200 (wet-and-dry) | 600 scratches |
| Pre-polish | Rubber/silicone wheels coarse → fine, or radial bristle discs (e.g. 220 → 400 → 6 µm → 1 µm) on a flex shaft | Remaining fine scratches |
Rules of thumb:
- Flat surfaces stay flat: back the paper with a rigid stick or glass, not a fingertip.
- Don't skip grits — skipping from 320 to 1200 takes longer than doing every step.
- Inspect under a lamp with a 10× loupe after each grit. One deep scratch means go
  back a step **for that area**.

## 3. Compounds on a polishing motor
- Use separate mops per compound — **never** mix them.
1. **Tripoli** (brown, cutting compound) on a stitched muslin mop or felt wheel:
   removes the fine scratch pattern and leaves a bright satin.
2. Clean the piece completely (ultrasonic or warm soapy water and a soft brush) to
   remove all Tripoli grit.
3. **Rouge** (red for gold and silver; white rouge or similar for platinum and harder
   metals) on a soft, loose muslin mop: the final colour.
- Load the mop lightly; too much compound smears instead of cutting.
- Motor speed around **2800–3600 rpm** for a typical 100–150 mm mop (common bench
  figures — unconfirmed for your machine).

## 4. Technique at the wheel
- Hold the piece **below the centre line** of the mop, with the wheel turning
  **down and away** from you, so if it snags it goes into the hood, not your face.
- Present the piece so the wheel runs **off** edges, not into them; edges caught
  into the wheel are what get the piece snatched.
- Keep it moving and change direction often to avoid ripples ("orange peel") and
  dragged-out holes.
- Protect sharp detail and settings: rounded edges and washed-out engraving come
  from over-polishing. Use small wheels and a flex shaft for details instead.

## 5. Alternatives and finishing touches
- **Tumbling** in a rotary or magnetic tumbler with stainless-steel shot and burnishing
  compound (1–3 hours) gives a bright, work-hardened surface on chain and texture —
  but it does not remove scratches.
- **Hand burnishing** with a polished steel burnisher brightens edges and bezels.
- Final clean in an ultrasonic, then a soft cloth. Do not ultrasonic porous or
  fractured stones (opal, emerald, turquoise).

## Faults
- **Scratches reappear after polish:** previous grit skipped or not fully removed.
- **Wavy, rippled surface:** soft mop pressed too hard, or a stationary piece.
- **Black streaks:** too much compound; clean mop with a rake, polish lighter.
- **Pits / comet tails:** porosity in castings revealed — fill or accept; do not chase.

## Safety
- Polishing motors snatch chains and rings out of hands at speed: **never** polish
  chain on a wheel unless it's wrapped flat on a board; tie back hair, no gloves,
  loose sleeves or lanyards.
- Use extraction; compound dust and lint are respiratory irritants. Wear eye protection.

## Verify
- [ ] All shaping and soldering done; firescale removed at the abrasive stage.
- [ ] Each grit applied crosswise until the previous scratches were gone.
- [ ] Separate mops for Tripoli and rouge; cleaned between compounds.
- [ ] Edges crisp and detail intact; no ripples.
- [ ] Mirror surface shows no scratches under a 10× loupe.
