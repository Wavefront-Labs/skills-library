# Cutting and polishing a cabochon

**Goal:** a symmetrical, calibrated domed stone (e.g. an 18 × 13 mm oval) with a
clean girdle, a smooth even dome and a flawless polish, ready to set. Start with a
hard but forgiving material — jasper, agate, chalcedony (Mohs 6.5–7).

## 1. Choose and orient
- Work from a **slab** 4–6 mm thick. Wet it and study it: the pattern you want on
  top, cracks and vugs away from the dome.
- For chatoyant stones (tiger's eye), the fibres must run **parallel to the base**
  and across the long axis so the eye sits centred on the dome.
- Mark the outline with a **brass or aluminium scribe** (or fine silver pencil) round
  a calibrated template; draw a few millimetres oversize if the edge is ragged.

## 2. Trim
- Cut away waste on a **trim saw** with straight cuts tangent to the outline, then
  nibble close with more tangent cuts. Use the saw's coolant; keep fingers clear of
  the blade line and use a push stick on small pieces.

## 3. Dop the stone
- Warm the stone and a dop stick sized to the stone. Melt **dopping wax** onto the
  stick, press the stone **back-side** onto it and shape a wax collar that supports
  the stone but stays a millimetre below the girdle line. Let it cool fully.
- Dopping lets you hold and rotate the stone accurately. Some cutters skip it for
  big stones; it is harder to get an even dome by hand.

## 4. Grinding sequence (wet, on a cabbing machine)
Typical diamond-wheel sequence: **80 → 220 → 280 → 600 → 1200 → 3000**, then polish.
Water must cover the wheel at all times.
1. **80/220 — shape the girdle:** hold the stone vertical and rotate it against the
   wheel, grinding to the marked line. Keep the edge straight up and down.
2. **Bevel:** grind a **~15° bevel** round the base edge so it won't chip in the
   setting. Then grind a **~45° band** round the top edge — this starts the dome.
3. **Dome:** grind further facets above the 45° band and roll the stone across
   the wheel, front to back and side to side, to blend them into a smooth curve.
   Aim for a dome height roughly **one-third to one-half** of the width for a
   classic profile.
4. **280 → 600 → 1200:** each grit removes the scratches of the one before. Rock the
   stone continuously so no flats form. Dry and inspect under a lamp after every
   grit — any scratch still visible means **go back**.
5. **3000 (pre-polish):** the surface starts to show a satin sheen.

## 5. Polish
- On a leather or felt pad with **cerium oxide** (quartz family), or a resin/felt
  pad with **diamond paste** (14,000 → 50,000 mesh) — keep the pad damp, not wet.
- Light pressure, constant motion. A mirror finish with no "orange peel" or
  undercut pits is the target.
- Soft or porous stones (turquoise, malachite) need different handling and dust
  control (malachite dust is toxic — unconfirmed severity; treat as hazardous).

## 6. Unmount
- Chill the dopped stone in the freezer for 10–15 minutes; it pops off the wax.
  Clean remaining wax with alcohol.

## Faults
- **Flat spots on the dome:** stone held still; keep rocking.
- **Deep scratches show at polish:** skipped a grit or contaminated a wheel;
  return to 600.
- **Undercutting (pitting):** soft and hard zones in the stone wear unevenly; use
  harder wheels, lighter pressure and less time at each stage.
- **Chipped girdle:** no base bevel, or wheel too coarse at the edge.
- **Off-centre dome:** not rotating the stone evenly; check symmetry against the
  template from above.

## Safety
- **Silica dust causes silicosis.** Always grind wet; never dry-sand quartz-family
  stones. Wear eye protection; clean the machine wet, not with a brush.
- Keep long hair, sleeves and gloves away from spinning wheels and saws.
- Use a GFCI/RCD-protected outlet with water-fed equipment.

## Verify
- [ ] Pattern and any chatoyancy oriented before cutting; outline matches template.
- [ ] Girdle vertical, base bevelled, outline symmetrical.
- [ ] Dome even with no flats; each grit stage cleared all previous scratches.
- [ ] Mirror polish with no undercut pits under a bright lamp.
- [ ] Stone fits a calibrated setting of the same size.
