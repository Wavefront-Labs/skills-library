# Sharpening a graver and cutting clean lines

**Goal:** a graver that cuts a clean, bright line instead of skidding or digging,
and the hand control to start, hold and finish a line at even depth. Nearly every
engraving problem is a sharpening problem first, so sharpening comes before cutting.

## 1. Tools
- **Square graver** (HSS or carbide, about 2.4–2.8 mm / 3/32–7/64 in square) in a
  mushroom or palm handle, shortened so it projects about 20–25 mm past your
  fingers for push work.
- **Sharpening fixture** (a jig that holds the graver at fixed angles) and laps:
  a **600-grit diamond** lap for shaping, **1200–3000** for finishing, and a
  **ceramic lap with 0.5 µm diamond** or similar for the final polish.
- Hold-down: an **engraver's ball vise**, or a bench block with the plate taped down.
- Magnification: a 2.5–3.5× head loupe at least; a stereo microscope (5–10×) is
  standard for fine work.
- Practice plates: **copper or brass** 1–1.5 mm thick.

## 2. Sharpen
A graver has a **face** (the angled end) and a **heel** (a tiny second bevel
underneath that controls how it rides in the cut).
1. **Face:** set the fixture to about **45°** and grind on the 600 lap until the
   face is one flat plane, then refine on 1200 and polish to a mirror. (45° is a
   common general-purpose starting angle; harder metals often get a steeper
   face — unconfirmed for your alloy.)
2. **Heel:** turn the graver belly-down and set **about 15°** relative to the belly.
   Polish a heel only about **0.1–0.3 mm long** — just a bright line at the point
   — on the polishing lap. Too long a heel won't let you turn curves; none at all
   makes the tool dive.
3. **Test:** lower the point onto a thumbnail with no pressure. If it **catches**,
   it's sharp. If it slides, resharpen. Polish the heel again every few minutes of
   cutting; a chipped point must go back to the face.

## 3. Hold and body position
- Push graver: handle in the palm, index finger along the top, thumb on the side
  with its tip resting on the plate as a brake. The thumb of the other hand guides
  and stops the tool.
- Work at chest height; turn the **work** (ball vise), not your wrist, for curves.

## 4. Cutting a straight line
1. **Lay out** with a fine marker or a scribe on a thin coat of white engraving wax.
2. **Enter:** start steep (about **45°**), push in until the point is just buried,
   then **lower the handle** to ~15° so the heel rides and the tool cuts forward.
3. **Cut:** short, controlled pushes, even pressure. The chip should curl out ahead
   of the tool in a continuous ribbon. Depth comes from handle angle: raise it to go
   deeper, lower it to come up.
4. **Exit:** lower the handle steadily so the line tapers out, then flick the
   chip off forward. Don't lever the graver up — that leaves a burr.

## 5. Curves and practice
- For a curve, rotate the plate into the tool while you push; keep the graver
  direction constant. Tight curves need a shorter heel.
- Practise: rows of parallel straight lines 3 mm apart; then circles; then
  **C-scrolls and S-scrolls**; then shaded leaves. Each plate, an hour.
- **Bright cut:** tilt the graver 10–20° to one side so one wall of the cut is
  polished — this makes engraving sparkle.

## Faults
- **Tool skids out of the cut:** dull point, heel too long, or handle too low.
- **Tool digs in and stops:** no heel, or handle raised too high.
- **Chattered, stepped walls:** face or heel not polished.
- **Point snaps:** prying sideways, or a face angle too acute for the metal.
- **Wobbly lines:** turning the wrist instead of the work.

## Safety
- The graver point is a blade — slips go into the guiding hand. Keep that thumb
  **behind** the point, not ahead of it.
- Eye protection when sharpening on powered laps.

## Verify
- [ ] Face polished flat at the set angle; heel a short bright line at ~15°.
- [ ] Thumbnail test catches with no pressure.
- [ ] Entry, constant-depth cut and tapered exit with no burr.
- [ ] Chip curls cleanly and the cut walls shine under magnification.
- [ ] Curves cut by turning the work, with even line width.
