# Making fused loop-in-loop chain

**Goal:** a supple, even, ancient-style loop-in-loop chain built from fused fine
silver links, finished by drawing through a drawplate so every link looks alike.
Slow, but needs no solder and no special machinery.

## 1. Material and ring size
- **Fine silver (.999)** wire fuses to itself cleanly; sterling needs solder
  and is much harder to fuse. Common combination: **22 ga (≈0.64 mm) wire on a
  ~6 mm mandrel** (unconfirmed starting point; the link length after stretching
  is about 2.5–3× the ring diameter).
- A finished 45 cm single loop-in-loop chain uses very roughly 150–200 links;
  coil more than you need.

## 2. Coil and cut rings
1. Wind the wire tightly round a steel mandrel (a knitting needle or rod), turns
   touching.
2. Slide off and saw through the coil with a **jeweller's saw** (4/0 blade) along
   one side — or use a ring-cutting jig. Sawn ends meet flush; cutter-pinched ends
   leave a gap and will not fuse.
3. Close each ring so the ends meet **perfectly flush with no gap and no overlap**
   (push past and back, as for a solder joint).

## 3. Fuse each ring
1. Lay rings on a clean soldering block or charcoal, **joints facing out** so you
   can see them.
2. With a small, hot, **pointed** butane flame, heat the whole ring until it glows,
   then focus near the joint. The surface goes shiny and "flashes" — the joint has
   fused. Remove the flame **immediately**: a fraction of a second more and the ring
   balls up and melts through.
3. Check every ring by twisting gently with two pliers; an unfused joint opens.
4. Fused rings are annealed and soft. No pickle needed for fine silver.

## 4. Form links
1. Slip each ring over the tips of round-nose pliers (or two nails in a board) and
   open the pliers to stretch it into a long **oval**, fused joint on one side.
2. Pinch the middle of the oval slightly so it becomes a narrow-waisted loop.
3. Fold the oval in half into a **U** around a thin rod; the two loops at the top are
   the "ears".

## 5. Build the chain
**Single loop-in-loop:**
1. Start with one folded link, ears up. Thread a new straight (unfolded) oval through
   both ears, then fold it up so its own ears point up.
2. Thread the next oval through the **new** link's ears. Repeat.
3. Each link passes through only the one before.
**Double loop-in-loop:** each new link passes through the ears of the **previous
two** links (insert a tapered awl to open a path); denser and rounder.
Hold the growing chain on a wooden dowel or a scriber point in a vice so the ears
stay open. Open tight ears with an awl, not pliers.

## 6. Draw and finish
1. Anneal the finished length (fine silver anneals at a dull red glow).
2. Pull it through a **wooden drawplate** (hardwood with graded holes), a few holes at
   a time, coating with wax or soap. Drawing evens, lengthens and smooths the chain;
   stop before it becomes stiff.
3. Fit **end caps** soldered or glued to the ends and add a clasp.

## Faults
- **Ring balls up / melts through:** flame too long or too wide; small pointed flame
  and quick removal. Practise on scrap rings.
- **Ring won't fuse:** gap in the joint, dirt, or flame not hot enough.
- **Uneven chain:** links stretched to different lengths — make a stretching jig
  of two fixed pins.
- **Chain kinks:** ears folded in different directions; keep all links folded the same way.
- **Stiff after drawing:** work-hardened; anneal again.

## Safety
- Hot rings roll; use a ceramic or charcoal surface with a raised rim.
- Eye protection when fusing; ventilate when burning wax or soap off.

## Verify
- [ ] Rings sawn with flush joints; each one fused and twist-tested.
- [ ] All links stretched to the same length on a jig.
- [ ] Each new link threaded through the correct previous ears; folded consistently.
- [ ] Chain drawn through progressively smaller holes; supple, even diameter.
- [ ] End caps and clasp secure on a pull test.
