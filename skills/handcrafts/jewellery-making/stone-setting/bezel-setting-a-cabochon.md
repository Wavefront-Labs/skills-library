# Bezel setting a cabochon

**Goal:** a cabochon held firmly by a thin wall of metal that hugs its curve all
round, with no gaps, ripples or tool marks — the first setting every jeweller
learns and the most forgiving for soft or irregular stones.

## 1. Choose and size the bezel
- **Fine silver bezel strip** (0.3–0.4 mm thick) is soft and pushes over easily;
  sterling is harder and better left for thick, wide frames.
- **Height:** the bezel should rise just past the point where the stone's dome
  starts to curve inward — for a typical 4–6 mm high cab, about **2.5–4 mm**. Too
  low and the stone falls out; too high and it wrinkles and covers too much stone.
- Wrap the strip tightly round the stone's **girdle** (its widest point), mark
  where it overlaps, and cut there. Check the stone drops in **easily but without
  rattling** — a loose bezel cannot be pushed tight enough.

## 2. Solder the bezel
1. File ends square, close with no gap (see making-and-soldering-a-sized-ring-band).
2. Solder the seam with **hard solder**, pickle, and re-check the fit. If tight,
   stretch it slightly on a bezel mandrel; if loose, cut and resolder.
3. Sand the bottom edge flat on 400-grit paper on glass.
4. Solder the bezel to a **backplate** (0.5–0.8 mm sheet) with **medium solder**,
   pallions **outside** the bezel so the heat draws solder in. Heat the backplate,
   not the thin bezel; the bezel melts first if you aim at it.
5. Saw away the excess backplate and file flush. Drill or saw a window in the back
   if the stone is translucent and you want light through it.

## 3. Fit the stone
- If the stone sits **too deep**, add a shim (fine sheet or a bezel cup lift);
  if the stone is too tall, file the bezel down evenly.
- Use dental floss under the stone so you can lift it out during test fits.
- Hold the piece in a ring clamp, a thermoplastic or setter's cement on a stick, or
  a bench vice with leather jaws.

## 4. Push the bezel over
1. Use a **bezel pusher** (flat-faced steel or brass) held low at about **45°**,
   pushing at the base of the bezel wall first, then working up.
2. Sequence: push at **12 o'clock**, then **6**, then **3**, then **9**. This locks
   the stone centred.
3. Then 1:30 and 7:30, 4:30 and 10:30 — always opposite pairs.
4. Work round in smaller and smaller steps, pushing out the little "corners" of
   excess metal that gather between pushed points. Don't chase them all in one spot.
5. Finish with a **rocker** or **bezel roller** to smooth the wall, then a polished
   **burnisher** round the top edge to close it on the stone and brighten it.

## 5. Clean up
- Lightly file or sand only the outside wall — guard the stone with tape.
- Polish with a soft wheel or by hand. Avoid long buffing over porous stones
  (turquoise, opal) which absorb compound.

## Faults
- **Wrinkles/pleats** in the bezel: pushed one area too far before the opposite
  side, or bezel too tall. Push in opposing pairs and in small steps.
- **Stone rocks after setting:** bezel loose on the stone or a high spot on the
  backplate; you should have shimmed or sanded first.
- **Cracked stone:** too much force on a brittle or high-domed stone, or the tool
  slipped onto it. Opal and turquoise are soft — push gently and burnish more.
- **Gap at the base:** bezel soldered on a non-flat backplate.
- **Bezel melted while soldering:** heat aimed at the bezel; aim at the plate.

## Safety
- Never heat a set stone; do all soldering before setting.
- Pickle and solder safety as in the ring-band skill.

## Verify
- [ ] Bezel fits the girdle snugly with no rattle; height just past the dome's turn.
- [ ] Seam and base fully soldered; no gaps when held to light.
- [ ] Pushed in opposite pairs; no pleats or tool marks on the wall.
- [ ] Stone does not move when pressed with a fingernail.
- [ ] Top edge burnished bright and continuous all round.
