# Wire-wrapping a cabochon pendant

**Goal:** a flat-backed cabochon held securely in a frame of parallel square wires
bound together and bent over the stone as prongs, with a wrapped bail — no solder,
no glue. The classic "bundle wrap" teaches the habits used in every cold-connection
design: tension, clean bends and invisible ends.

## 1. Materials (for a ~25 × 18 mm cab)
- **Frame:** 3–5 lengths of **21 ga (≈0.7 mm) square** wire, half-hard, each about
  **30 cm** (roughly cab perimeter × 3–4; unconfirmed for very tall cabs — cut long).
- **Binding:** 1 m of **21–22 ga half-round**, half-hard, or 24–26 ga round dead-soft.
- Copper to practise; sterling, Argentium or gold-filled for finished work.
  (Gauges here are AWG; UK suppliers sometimes list SWG, which differs — check mm.)
- Tools: flat-nose and chain-nose pliers (smooth jaws), round-nose or bail-making
  pliers, **flush cutters**, nylon-jaw pliers for straightening, ruler, fine marker,
  needle file or cup bur for ends.

## 2. Prepare the bundle
1. Straighten each wire by pulling through nylon-jaw pliers once or twice (this
   also work-hardens it slightly). Don't over-pull dead-soft wire into springiness.
2. Lay the square wires side by side, perfectly flat, and tape the ends.
3. Find the **centre** and mark it, then mark the cab's **bottom width** either side
   of centre.

## 3. Bind
1. At the centre, wrap the half-round binding wire tightly **3–5 turns** round the
   bundle, flat face against the bundle. Repeat at each of your marks.
2. Each binding sits square to the bundle with turns touching; push them together
   with fingernails or flat-nose pliers.
3. Trim binding ends on the **back** of the bundle with the flat side of the flush
   cutter against the work, and squeeze the cut end down.

## 4. Form the frame
1. Bend the bundle round the cab at the bindings so it hugs the stone's girdle
   from the bottom up both sides. Bend at the **edges** of bindings, not in the middle.
2. Where the two sides meet at the top, bind them together with 4–6 turns of
   binding wire — this holds the frame closed.
3. The frame should fit the stone with **no gap and no forcing**: test with the cab
   in place.

## 5. Make the prongs (grip the stone)
1. Spread the bundle wires apart above and below the girdle: on the **front**, pull
   2–3 outer wires out at a few points (bottom centre and the two sides) with
   chain-nose pliers and fold them over the face of the stone as prongs.
2. On the **back**, fold one or two wires across to make a cradle so the stone
   cannot push out backwards.
3. Press each prong down so it touches the stone. Try to rock the stone: it must not move.

## 6. Bail and finish
1. Bring the remaining wires up behind the top binding. Use two or three to form
   the **bail**: wrap them round bail-making pliers (6–8 mm barrel) to the back and
   coil them neatly round the frame.
2. Turn remaining wire ends into small decorative spirals on the front, or coil
   them tightly and trim on the back.
3. File each cut end smooth or round it with a cup bur so nothing snags.
4. Optional: oxidise in **liver of sulphur**, then highlight raised areas with
   0000 steel wool or a polishing cloth.

## Faults
- **Stone loose:** frame too large, or prongs not pressed down. Tighten by
  squeezing a small kink into a frame wire on the back.
- **Bundle wires twist/splay:** binding too loose or wires not straightened; rebind.
- **Plier marks/scratches:** serrated jaws or gripping too hard — wrap jaws in tape.
- **Wire snaps at a bend:** work-hardened by repeated bending; plan bends and avoid
  bending back and forth.
- **Scratchy ends** catch on clothes: always file and tuck.

## Verify
- [ ] Wires straight and flat with bindings square and tight.
- [ ] Frame hugs the girdle with no gap.
- [ ] Prongs front and back; stone does not move when pushed.
- [ ] Bail large enough for the intended chain/cord and solidly coiled.
- [ ] Every wire end filed smooth and tucked or spiralled.
