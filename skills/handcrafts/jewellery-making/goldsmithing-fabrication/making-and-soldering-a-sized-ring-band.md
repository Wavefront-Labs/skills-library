# Making and soldering a sized ring band

**Goal:** a round, seamless-looking band in sterling silver (or gold) that comes out
at the size you planned, with a solder joint that never opens. This is the core
bench sequence — measure, saw, file, anneal, solder, pickle, true — that every other
fabricated piece reuses.

## 1. Calculate the blank
- Find the **inside diameter (ID)** for the size from a ring-size chart (UK letters,
  US numbers or ISO mm circumference). Measure the finger with a sizer, not string.
- **Blank length = (ID + stock thickness) × π.** Example: ID 18.0 mm, 1.5 mm
  thick strip → (18.0 + 1.5) × 3.1416 ≈ **61.3 mm**.
- For wide bands (over ~6 mm) go up a quarter to half size, as wide rings feel tighter.
- Allow nothing extra for the saw kerf if you file the ends square; the formula
  already lands close, and final stretching corrects small errors.

## 2. Cut and prepare
1. Scribe the length and saw on a **bench pin** with a **piercing saw** — 2/0 or
   1/0 blades for 1–1.5 mm silver; teeth pointing **down toward the handle**,
   tensioned to a high "ping". Saw upright, letting the blade cut on the down stroke.
2. File both ends dead square with a flat hand file. Check against a small square.
3. **Anneal** if the strip is springy: heat evenly with a soft torch flame to a dull
   red in a dim room (about 600–650 °C for sterling — unconfirmed, check supplier
   data), then quench or air-cool, and pickle.

## 3. Form and close the joint
1. Bend the strip round with half-round pliers or a mallet over a mandrel, ends
   meeting at the top.
2. Close the joint by pushing the ends **past** each other and back so they spring
   together under tension. Hold it to light: **no light should pass** through the
   seam. Solder fills a perfect joint; it does not fill a gap.
3. If the ends don't meet flush, run a saw blade through the closed joint once and
   re-close.

## 4. Solder
1. Clean the joint (pickle, then rinse) and paint **flux** over it.
2. Stand the ring on a soldering block, joint at the side or bottom.
3. Cut **hard solder** into pallions about 1 × 1 mm; place 1–2 across the seam on
   the inside.
4. Heat the **whole ring** first with a bushy flame, not the joint. Flux goes
   glassy; then focus slightly on the area around the joint. Solder flows
   **toward heat** — when the ring reaches temperature it flashes into the seam.
   Hard silver solder flows somewhere around **750–780 °C**; medium ~720 °C; easy
   ~700 °C (approximate — your supplier's sheet governs).
5. Stop the moment it flows. Quench, then **pickle** (sodium bisulfate solution
   warm, or citric acid) until white. Rinse.
6. Use hard solder for this first joint so later solders (medium, easy) for
   settings or wire won't reopen it.

## 5. True and size
1. Slide onto a **steel ring mandrel** and tap round with a rawhide or nylon mallet,
   turning the ring and flipping it end for end (mandrels are tapered).
2. Check size on a ring sizer stick. Up to about half a size can be stretched with
   a ring stretcher or by tapping it down the mandrel.
3. File off excess solder, then sand and finish (see jewellery-finishing skills).

## Faults
- **Solder balls up:** dirty metal, too little flux, or the joint is colder than
  the solder; heat the ring more broadly.
- **Joint opens later:** solder bridged a gap without penetrating; re-saw and redo.
- **Pits/porosity in the seam:** overheating sterling; work faster with a bigger flame.
- **Ring too small:** forgot the thickness in the formula, or ID measured wrong.
- **Firescale** (grey stain in sterling): from repeated heating; remove by
  abrading, or prevent with a firescale-preventing flux coat.

## Safety
- Pickle is acidic: add the powder to water, use a lidded crock, rinse with tongs
  of copper or plastic (steel tongs in pickle copper-plate your work).
- Ventilate when soldering; cadmium-free solders only.
- Tie hair back and keep the gas hose clear of the flame.

## Verify
- [ ] Blank length calculated as (ID + thickness) × π.
- [ ] Ends square; no light through the closed seam.
- [ ] Solder flowed fully through the joint, visible on both faces.
- [ ] Ring trued on a mandrel, checked on a sizer stick, and within ¼ size.
- [ ] Pickled clean and rinsed before finishing.
