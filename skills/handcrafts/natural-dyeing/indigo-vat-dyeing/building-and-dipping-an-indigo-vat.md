# Building and dipping an indigo vat

**Goal:** a working vat in which indigo is reduced to its soluble (yellow-green
"leuco") form, so fibre dipped in it turns blue when it meets air. Indigo needs no
mordant. It needs **alkali** (high pH) and a **reducing agent** (to remove oxygen).
Draft: the "1-2-3" fructose vat proportions widely taught; check them against
your source.

## 1. Vat types
- **Fructose (1-2-3) vat:** 1 part indigo powder, 2 parts lime (calcium hydroxide),
  3 parts fructose by weight. Easy, organic, reduces in an hour or so when warm.
- **Ferrous vat:** iron(II) sulphate as reducer with lime. Fast; iron can harsh-hand
  protein fibres. Traditional for cotton.
- **Fermentation vats** (woad, sukumo, bran/madder): bacteria do the reducing; slow
  to start (days-weeks), long-lived, need feeding and warmth. Deep knowledge in themselves.
- Chemical (thiourea dioxide/hydrosulphite) vats exist; fast but chemical.

## 2. Building a fructose vat
1. Make an indigo paste with a little hot water (wet it well — lumps don't reduce).
2. Fill a lidded vessel (bucket, stockpot) with hot water (~50 °C). Stir in fructose
   to dissolve, then indigo paste, then lime. Stir in a circle, gently.
3. Cover and wait. Ready signs: liquid under the surface is **clear yellow-green to
   amber**, and a coppery, bubbly **"flower"** (bronze film/foam) forms on top.
4. Skim the flower aside before dipping, and put it back after.

## 3. Dipping
- Wet the fibre thoroughly first (dry fibre carries air into the vat).
- Lower gently under the surface — **no splashing, no stirring air in** — for a few
  minutes. Squeeze gently underwater, lift slowly, squeeze out near the surface.
- Fibre comes out green-yellow and turns blue in air in minutes. Let it **fully
  oxidise** (10-20 min) before the next dip.
- **Depth comes from many short dips**, not one long one; many dips give a fast,
  deep blue. One long dip gives a weak blue that rubs off (crocking).

## 4. Reading and maintaining
- **Blue liquid / no flower:** under-reduced. Add fructose (or reducer), warm, wait.
- **pH too low** (strip below ~9-10 for most vats; check your method): add a little lime.
- **Sludge on bottom** is normal (lime, spent material). Don't disturb it when dipping.
- Keep warm (fructose vats work better at 25-40 °C). Store lidded.
- After use, top up: indigo when colour weakens, fructose and lime to keep reduction
  and pH.

## 5. Finishing
Rinse dyed fibre well, then an acid rinse (dilute vinegar) neutralises lime
alkalinity that otherwise harshens wool. Wash with neutral soap. Light crocking
on the first wash is normal; heavy crocking means the dye was built in too few,
too long dips.

## Safety
Lime is caustic; wear gloves and eye protection, especially for paste and topping up.
Keep vats covered and away from children and pets.

## Verify
- [ ] Vat shows yellow-green liquid and coppery flower before dipping.
- [ ] Fibre pre-wetted; dipped without splashing or air.
- [ ] Full oxidation between dips; depth from many dips.
- [ ] pH and reduction checked and corrected each session.
- [ ] Acid rinse done; rub test shows only slight crocking.
