# Scouring and mordanting fibre

**Goal:** clean fibre that bonds with natural dye, giving even, bright, more
lightfast colour. Most natural dyes wash out or dull on unmordanted fibre. The two
families of fibre need different treatment. Draft: the percentages are widely
published starting points; dyers vary them.

## 1. Weigh: everything is % WOF
Weigh fibre **dry** and calculate chemicals as **% weight of fibre (WOF)**:
100 g wool at 12% alum = 12 g alum. Write it on a tag with the fibre.

## 2. Scour
- **Protein (wool, silk, alpaca):** warm water with a pH-neutral detergent, held hot
  (not boiling) for wool for ~an hour; rinse at the same temperature. Avoid sudden
  temperature change and agitation: wool felts.
- **Cellulose (cotton, linen, hemp):** much harsher — simmer with soda ash
  (sodium carbonate) and detergent for an hour or more to strip waxes and
  sizing, rinse well. New "prepared for dye" (PFD) fabric still benefits.
- Patchy dye later almost always means poor scouring.

## 3. Mordant protein fibres with alum
- Common start: **potassium aluminium sulphate (alum) ~10-15% WOF**, some add
  **cream of tartar ~5-7% WOF** to brighten and keep wool soft.
- Dissolve in hot water, add to a pot with enough water for the fibre to move
  freely, add wet fibre, raise slowly to ~80-90 °C for wool (lower for silk,
  ~60-70 °C, which damages from heat), hold about an hour, cool in the bath.
- Rinse lightly or leave damp a day or two before dyeing; many dyers find colour
  takes better after resting.

## 4. Mordant cellulose: tannin then alum
Cellulose doesn't bind alum well alone. Classic three-step:
1. **Tannin** bath (gallnut, tara, sumac, or oak gall extract, ~5-10% WOF depending on
   strength) in hot water; soak several hours or overnight.
2. **Alum** bath (~10-15% WOF) or the more effective **aluminium acetate** /
   **aluminium triformate** (~5-8% WOF), used warm or room temperature.
3. Some dyers add a chalk or soda "fixing" bath between or after. Practices vary.
Dark tannins (oak gall) shift colours darker; tara is pale and neutral.

## 5. Iron as a mordant/modifier
Ferrous sulphate (iron) "saddens" (darkens, greys) colours; at higher levels it
weakens wool and silk. Usually used at low % (around 1-2% WOF) as an after-bath.
Keep a separate iron pot; iron contaminates other dye baths.

## 6. Mordants to avoid
Older recipes use **chrome (dichromate)**, **tin** and **copper** salts: these are
toxic or environmentally problematic. Most current craft practice avoids chrome entirely.

## Safety
Alum is relatively low-hazard but don't ingest; keep dye pots out of the kitchen.
Dust masks for powdered chemicals; gloves for iron and tannins.

## Verify
- [ ] Dry fibre weighed and % WOF written on a tag.
- [ ] Scoured until water runs clean and fibre wets out instantly.
- [ ] Protein fibre mordanted below boiling, no felting.
- [ ] Cellulose given tannin plus aluminium, not alum alone.
- [ ] Iron kept in a separate pot.
