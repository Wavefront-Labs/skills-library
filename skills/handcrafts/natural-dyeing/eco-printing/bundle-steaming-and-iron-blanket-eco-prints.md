# Bundle steaming and iron-blanket eco prints

**Goal:** a crisp leaf print on cloth or paper, in which the leaf's own dye and
tannins transfer in contact under heat and pressure. Results vary by season, tree
and water; expect to test. Draft: method as commonly taught.

## 1. Leaves that print
- Reliable printers: **eucalyptus** (oranges, reds, especially on wool and silk),
  **rose**, **maple** (some), **smoke bush**, **sumac**, **oak**, **geranium**,
  **onion skins** (as a dye scatter). Tannin-rich leaves print strongly with iron.
- Fresh leaves print sharp; dried leaves can be soaked in water to rehydrate.
- Test new leaves on a sample strip first; a leaf from the same species printed in
  spring and autumn can differ.

## 2. Prepare cloth or paper
- Scour cloth (see `handcrafts.natural-dyeing.mordanting-preparation.scouring-and-mordanting-fibre`).
- **Protein fibre (silk, wool):** alum mordant gives brighter prints. **Cellulose:**
  tannin then alum, as for dyeing.
- Some dip leaves (or the cloth) quickly in **iron water** (dilute ferrous sulphate)
  for dark, sharp outlines; too much iron gives muddy grey and can weaken silk.
- Paper: heavy cotton watercolour paper; often pre-soaked in alum water.

## 3. Lay out
- Lay damp cloth flat. Arrange leaves (vein side down generally prints more detail).
- **Blanket:** a second cloth dipped in iron water or a dye bath (e.g. logwood,
  onion), laid over the leaves, transfers a background colour everywhere a leaf
  isn't — leaves resist and print as outlines/silhouettes.
- Cover with a plastic barrier sheet if you don't want the print to transfer through
  the layers as you roll.

## 4. Roll and tie
- Roll the stack **tightly** around a dowel or copper/steel pipe (copper adds a
  modifier effect; steel adds iron), then bind firmly with string along the whole
  length. Contact pressure is everything.
- For paper: stack between ceramic tiles or boards and clamp.

## 5. Heat
- **Steam** (bundle on a rack above water, lid on) for ~1-2 hours, or **simmer** in a
  dye bath (onion skins, madder) for a coloured background.
- Let the bundle cool fully (some leave it overnight or for days) before opening.
  Patience gives deeper prints.

## 6. Cure and wash
- Unroll, remove leaves, hang to dry. **Cure** a week or more before washing to let
  colours set (common advice; extent of benefit varies).
- Wash gently in pH-neutral soap. Some fading of eucalyptus oranges is normal at the
  first wash.

## Gotchas
- Blurry prints: loose rolling or too-wet cloth.
- Grey mud everywhere: too much iron.
- Eco prints are less lightfast than good dye baths. Keep out of strong sun.

## Verify
- [ ] Leaves tested on a sample before the main piece.
- [ ] Cloth scoured and mordanted for its fibre type.
- [ ] Roll tight and bound end to end.
- [ ] Bundle cooled fully before opening; cured before washing.
