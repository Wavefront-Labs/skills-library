# Building and modifying a dye bath

**Goal:** a predictable colour on mordanted fibre, with a record so you can repeat it.
Each dyestuff has a temperature it likes, and a few modifiers shift whole colour
families. Draft: typical dyer practice; quantities are starting points.

## 1. Depth of shade = dyestuff % WOF
Use the dry fibre weight (WOF):
- **Madder root** (reds, oranges): ~50-100% WOF for strong reds.
- **Weld** (clear yellows, very lightfast): ~50-100% WOF dried plant.
- **Cochineal** (pinks to crimson, purples with iron): ~2-8% WOF — very strong.
- **Logwood** (purples, greys, blacks with iron): small amounts; lightfastness modest.
- **Onion skins, avocado pits/skins, walnut hulls:** kitchen or garden dyes; walnut
  contains its own tannin and dyes without mordant.
Lighter shades: lower %, or dye in an exhaust bath (the used bath's second round).

## 2. Extract
- Soak dried material overnight, then heat in water and strain (or bag it in muslin).
- **Temperature matters:** madder gives the clearest reds kept **below ~70-80 °C**;
  boiling dulls it brown. Weld also prefers not to be boiled hard. Cochineal tolerates
  a simmer. Many extracts are done twice and combined.
- Add calcium (a pinch of chalk) to madder in soft water — reds are brighter.
  That's a common recommendation I haven't verified across water types.

## 3. Dye
- Add wet, mordanted fibre to the bath with room to move. Raise temperature slowly,
  hold (often an hour), and ideally cool in the bath for depth.
- Stir gently; turn the fibre for evenness. Never shock wool with temperature changes.

## 4. Modify colour
- **Acid** (citric acid or vinegar): shifts some colours brighter/yellower or redder
  (cochineal toward scarlet/orange).
- **Alkali** (soda ash, a pinch): shifts cochineal toward purples-blues, madder
  toward bluer reds; harsh on wool in excess.
- **Iron** after-bath (ferrous sulphate, low %): saddens and darkens — weld to
  olive greens, madder to browns, cochineal to purples, logwood to greys/black.
- Dip a test sample in each modifier before committing a whole batch.

## 5. Rinse and test fastness
- Rinse in water at the dye temperature until clear, wash with pH-neutral soap, dry.
- **Lightfastness test:** cover half a sample, put it in a bright window for a few
  weeks, compare. Weld, madder, cochineal and indigo do well; many "kitchen dyes"
  (berries, onion on unmordanted fibre, turmeric) fade quickly.
- **Washfastness:** wash a sample with a white thread tied to it; check bleeding.

## 6. Record
Dye journal per bath: fibre and WOF, mordant, dyestuff % and source, temperature,
time, modifiers, water source, and a stapled sample. Repeatability lives here.

## Verify
- [ ] Dyestuff % WOF and mordant recorded.
- [ ] Temperature held within the dyestuff's preferred range.
- [ ] Modifiers tested on samples first.
- [ ] Fibre rinsed clear; light and wash tests done on a sample.
