# Forging strap hinges and pintles

**Goal:** a pair of strap hinges and pintles that carry a door or gate, swing
freely, sit flat on the wood and look like they belong together.

## 1. Plan the pair
- Strap length is commonly a good fraction of the door's width (long straps spread
  load on ledged and braced doors). Decide eye diameter (which equals the pintle pin
  diameter plus clearance) and terminal style before you cut stock.
- Mild steel flat bar (e.g. 6 × 30 mm for a garden door; heavier for a gate).
- Make both hinges side by side, step for step.

## 2. Decorative terminal
- Forge the end first: spade, bean, fleur, or a simple rounded taper. Spread the
  end on the anvil face with the cross-pein, then shape the outline with the hammer
  and hot-cut or file.
- Taper the strap toward the terminal slightly — a dead-parallel strap looks machined.

## 3. Rolling the eye
- At the other end, heat about the eye's circumference in length plus a bit.
- **Start the curl over the anvil edge**: hammer the very end over the edge to begin
  the bend (scarf the end slightly thinner first so it closes neatly).
- Roll the eye with the strap on the anvil face, eye-end up, tapping the curl back
  over itself; close it **around a mandrel or a bar of the pintle diameter** so the
  inside is round and the size is right.
- The eye should close snug on the mandrel, with the end tucked against the strap.
- Some hinges are made with the eye wrapped the other way (rolled under), depending
  on whether the strap sits on the inside or outside face; check what your
  door needs.

## 4. Pintles
- **Drive pintle:** a tapered spike driven into a timber post, with a vertical pin
  at the end. Forge a square-section taper, then upset and bend the pin end at 90°,
  drawing the pin round to diameter.
- **Plate pintle:** a plate with screw holes and a pin, for masonry or face fixing;
  the pin may be forge-welded, riveted or arc-welded on.
- Pins on both pintles **must be in line vertically** on the frame, or the door
  binds.

## 5. Holes
- Punch screw/nail holes hot (see
  `handcrafts.blacksmithing.forging-fundamentals.fire-management-drawing-out-and-upsetting`),
  or drill cold. **Countersink** for flat-head screws or bolts; for clench nails,
  punch square holes.
- Space holes so that a fastening sits near the eye (most load) and along the strap.

## 6. Hanging
- Fit pintles in the frame first, plumb and in line (a long straightedge or plumb
  line through both pins).
- Hang the door on the pintles, then fix the straps to the door. If the hinges were
  made slightly differently, this tolerates it better than fixing straps first.

## 7. Finish
Wire-brush; hot-wax or oil for indoors; for outdoors, galvanise or use a proper
primer and paint system. Leave the eye–pin bearing surface lightly greased.

## Gotchas
- An eye rolled oval binds on the pin.
- Pintle pins not in line: the door rises or drops as it swings and the hinges wear fast.

## Verify
- [ ] Both hinges forged in step and match.
- [ ] Eyes rolled round on a mandrel of the pin size.
- [ ] Pintle pins plumb and in line on the frame.
- [ ] Holes countersunk or square-punched to suit the fixings.
- [ ] Door swings freely, no binding or rise.
