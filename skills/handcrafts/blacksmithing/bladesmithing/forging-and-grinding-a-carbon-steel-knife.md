# Forging and grinding a carbon-steel knife

**Goal:** a working knife — straight, hardened and tempered correctly, with a
handle that stays on — from a bar of high-carbon steel.

## 1. Steel choice
Start with a simple, **forgiving** carbon steel: **1084**, 1075 or 80CrV2. They
harden in oil (fast quench oil) and tolerate imperfect heat control. 1095 and
alloy/stainless steels need tighter control. **Avoid unknown scrap** (old files,
springs) for a first knife: you don't know its heat treatment. Get the supplier's
datasheet for your steel.

## 2. Forging
Fundamentals in `handcrafts.blacksmithing.forging-fundamentals.fire-management-drawing-out-and-upsetting`.
- **Tip first:** forge the point by tapering the end off-centre so the curve of the
  spine forms; it's easier to do this before the bevels.
- **Bevels:** hammer the edge side at an angle, working from the tip toward the
  ricasso/handle, on both sides alternately. The blade **curves toward the spine** as
  the edge stretches — pre-curve the blank slightly the other way or correct by
  tapping on the spine edge.
- **Distal taper:** thin the blade gradually from handle to tip.
- Leave the edge **thick**, around 1–2 mm (about a coin's thickness); a thin forged edge
  overheats, decarburises and cracks.
- Don't forge carbon steel below a dull red (roughly where it stops glowing in shade):
  cracks start at cold forging.

## 3. Normalising
After forging, heat to **non-magnetic** (test with a magnet: the steel stops
attracting at about the critical temperature) and let it **cool in still air**.
Repeat two or three times at successively slightly lower temperatures. This refines
the grain coarsened by forging and relieves stress — skipping it is a common
cause of warping in the quench. (A common hobby practice; heat-treat specialists
specify exact temperatures per steel.)

## 4. Profile and grind
- Clean up the profile on a grinder or with files. Drill pin holes in the tang
  **now**, before hardening.
- Grind the bevels: scribe a **centreline** on the edge (the edge-scribe trick: lay the
  blade flat, run a drill bit of the blade's thickness along it), grind both sides
  evenly to it. A file guide sets a neat **plunge line**.
- Leave the edge about **0.5–1 mm thick** before hardening (a dime's thickness is the
  usual hobby guide) — too thin and it cracks or warps in the quench.

## 5. Harden and temper
Covered in `handcrafts.blacksmithing.heat-treatment.hardening-and-tempering-carbon-steel`:
heat to non-magnetic plus a little, quench edge-first in warmed oil, check hardness
with a file, and **temper immediately** (often around 200 °C / ~400 °F for 1084-type
steels, two cycles of an hour or two — check your steel's datasheet).

## 6. Finish grind and handle
- Finish the bevels **cool**: dip often in water; if the edge turns blue/straw from
  the grinder, you've drawn the temper there.
- Hand sand to the finish you want.
- Handle: full-tang scales (wood, micarta, G10) with pins and epoxy; or a hidden tang
  epoxied into a drilled handle block. Rough the tang and degrease before epoxy.

## 7. Sharpen
Set a bevel on stones (around 15–20° per side for a general knife), raise a burr,
remove it, strop. Test on paper.

## Verify
- [ ] Known steel with a datasheet.
- [ ] Forged edge left thick; no cold forging.
- [ ] Normalised before grinding; pin holes drilled before hardening.
- [ ] Hardened (file skates) and tempered straight away.
- [ ] Finish grind kept cool; handle bonded; edge sharp and burr-free.
