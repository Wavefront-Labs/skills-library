# Forging a pair of flat-jaw tongs

**Goal:** a matched pair of tongs that grips stock firmly at the jaw tips without
twisting, opens and closes smoothly, and has reins you can hold comfortably.
Tongs are the traditional first real tool a smith makes: every later job needs them.

## Stock
Mild steel (e.g. A36 or similar); for small tongs, around 16–20 mm (5/8–3/4 in)
square or round bar. Make both halves together — same heat, same steps — so they
match. Forging fundamentals:
`handcrafts.blacksmithing.forging-fundamentals.fire-management-drawing-out-and-upsetting`.

## 1. The jaw
- At the bar's end, mark a jaw length (roughly 50–60 mm for small tongs).
- Lay the bar across the anvil's **far edge** with the jaw section on the face and
  strike **half-face blows** (half the hammer face over the anvil edge) to step down the
  jaw to about half thickness. This step is the first offset.

## 2. The boss (pivot area)
- Rotate the bar **90° toward you** and set it on the anvil's near edge, behind the
  first step, and make a second set of half-face blows to form the boss: a flat area
  where the rivet goes, offset from the jaw.
- Rotate **back 90°** and make a third set of half-face blows to begin the reins. The
  boss is now a flat pad, with the jaw stepping one way and the rein stepping the
  other. Getting the rotation sequence wrong produces left/right mismatched halves —
  do both halves step by step together, and lay them side by side to check.

## 3. The reins
- Draw out the reins to a comfortable length (roughly 350–450 mm for general work),
  tapering. Many smiths **forge-weld or arc-weld** extension bar onto a short jaw
  piece to save drawing time.
- Round the reins' ends so they don't cut your hand.

## 4. Punch and rivet
- Mark the rivet position at the boss centre. **Punch** hot, from one side, flip, and
  drive through over the hardy hole; drift to size. Or drill cold.
- Fit a mild steel rivet with enough length to form a head. Heat the rivet (and
  ideally the boss) red, insert, and **set the rivet** with a rivet set or ball-pein
  hammer. Work the tongs open and closed while the joint is still hot so they don't
  seize.

## 5. Fit the jaws
- Heat the jaws, close them on a piece of the stock size they're for, and hammer the
  jaws onto it so they grip **parallel along their length**, contacting at the tips
  and the back.
- Reins should stay a finger-width apart when closed on the stock; adjust by bending
  the reins hot.
- Groove or V the jaw faces for round stock if needed.

## Gotchas
- Thin boss = the tongs flex and twist the work. Keep the boss thick.
- Jaws that touch only at the back let the work spin; only at the tip and it pivots out.
- Quenching the whole tongs hot in water when the rivet is tight can seize the joint;
  work it free while warm.

## Verify
- [ ] Both halves forged through each step together; mirror images.
- [ ] Boss flat and thick; rivet set firm but free to pivot.
- [ ] Jaws parallel on the target stock; reins a finger apart.
- [ ] Grip tested on a hot bar of that stock without slipping or twisting.
