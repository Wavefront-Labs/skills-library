# Inkle loom warping and pickup

**Goal:** a warp-faced band of even width with clean edges, plus simple pickup patterns.
The inkle loom is the most common band loom; rigid heddle band looms and backstrap setups
make similar bands (see backstrap weaving).

## How it works
- Warp threads alternate between **heddled** (pass through a fixed string heddle loop) and
  **open** (pass freely above). Pressing the open threads down makes one shed; lifting them
  up makes the other.
- The band is warp-faced: warp threads pack close and hide the weft, so the warp colours
  make the pattern.

## Make the heddles
- Tie string heddles from smooth, strong cotton around the heddle peg and a spacer
  (or the loom's heddle jig) so all loops are the **same size**. One heddle per heddled thread.

## Warp it
- Follow your draft (an inkle draft shows two rows: top = open threads, bottom = heddled
  threads, alternating). Count ends; typical bands use 30–80 ends of perle cotton or wool.
- Path: wrap the warp around the loom's peg circuit — open threads go **over** the top
  route, heddled threads through a heddle. Alternate each end: open, heddled, open...
- Change colour by tying the new colour to the old at a peg, not mid-path.
- Leave the tension peg/bar loose at first; tighten when all ends are on.

## Weave
1. Hand behind the heddles: push the open threads **down** (shed 1) or lift **up** (shed 2).
2. Pass the shuttle (a belt shuttle with a beveled edge doubles as beater).
3. Beat firmly with the shuttle edge, then change shed and beat again.
4. **Weft tension controls band width**: after passing, pull the weft so the edge warps
   just touch — consistently. Pull too hard → band narrows; too soft → weft shows and
   width wobbles. Width variations are the main beginner issue.
- Advance the warp around the loom by loosening tension, sliding the warp, re-tightening.

## Simple pickup
- Thread pattern threads (a contrasting colour) in a regular arrangement as the draft says.
- In each shed, use a pickup stick or fingers to **lift** chosen pattern threads from the
  lower layer, or **drop** pattern threads from the upper layer, before passing weft. The
  lifted threads float on top, forming motifs.
- Read pickup patterns from a chart pick by pick. Baltic-style pickup and pebble weave are
  standard families with many published charts.
- Floats over 3 picks get loose; well-designed charts avoid them.

## Finishing
- Weave until the knots approach the heddles. Cut off, secure ends with overhand knots,
  sewn hems, or a fringe.
- Steam press lightly (wool) or press (cotton).

## Gotchas
- Twisted or crossed warp at the heddles: an end skipped alternation during warping. Check
  the pattern at the first few cm and fix before going on.
- Uneven heddle lengths cause one shed to be bad.
- Width drifts most at the start of a session; measure.

## Verify
- [ ] Heddles all the same length.
- [ ] Open/heddled alternation matches the draft; first cm checked.
- [ ] Band width even (measure every 10–20 cm).
- [ ] Pickup charts followed without long floats; ends secured.
