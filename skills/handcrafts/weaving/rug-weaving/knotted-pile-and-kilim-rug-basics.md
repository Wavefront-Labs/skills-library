# Knotted pile and kilim rug basics

**Goal:** a rug that lies flat, has square edges and a dense, even surface. Rugs are
woven tighter and beaten harder than cloth; the warp must stand enormous tension.

## Loom and warp
- A sturdy upright or frame rug loom, or a heavy floor loom with a strong beater and brake.
  Light looms bow under rug tension and edges go wavy.
- Warp: tightly plied cotton, linen or wool rug warp; strong and low-stretch.
- Sett: commonly around 4–8 ends per inch for hand-knotted wool rugs with medium pile yarn,
  depending on knot density wanted (often stated as knots per square inch). Sample.
- Tension: as tight as the loom allows evenly — a warp that "sings" when plucked.

## Start
- Twine one or two rows, then weave a short **plain-weave/kilim band** — this protects the
  ends and becomes the kilim end finish.

## Knotted pile
Pile is formed by knots around pairs of warp ends, with several shots of ground weft
between each knot row to lock them.
- **Symmetric (Turkish/Ghiordes) knot**: yarn passes over both warps, wraps behind each,
  and both tails come up between them. Robust, slightly coarser pattern detail.
- **Asymmetric (Persian/Senneh) knot**: wraps one warp fully, passes under the other; tails
  emerge either side. Allows finer, more curvilinear designs. Can be open left or right.
- Cut pile yarn to length or knot from a continuous strand over a gauge rod and cut after.
- After each row of knots: 1–3 shots of ground weft (plain weave), beaten **hard** with a
  heavy beater comb. Then trim pile to an even height with shears (often trimmed again
  at the end).
- Follow a gridded chart: one square = one knot.
- **Rya** (Scandinavian): long, sparse pile with many weft shots between rows.

## Kilim (flat-weave)
- Weft-faced tapestry technique with **slits** where colour areas meet vertically — classic
  kilim stepped diagonals avoid long slits. See tapestry joins for dovetail/interlock options.
- **Soumak** (weft wrapping) and weft twining add texture and strength in flat-weave rugs.
- Rag rugs: fabric strips as weft on a strong cotton warp in plain weave or twill.

## Keeping it square
- Beat so the fell stays parallel to the bar; measure width at the fell regularly.
- Lay weft in generous arcs; rug weft pulled too tight is the main cause of waisting.
- Measure the length on both sides periodically; different measurements = the beat is uneven.

## Finishing
- Weave a closing kilim band to match the start.
- Secure warp ends: overhand knots in groups, a Damascus/braided edge, or a woven-back edge.
- Side cords: many rugs wrap the outer warps with yarn (overcasting) for durable edges.
- Wash (cool water, gentle agitation for wool), block flat to dry, final pile trim.

## Gotchas
- Wavy edges: uneven tension or weak loom.
- Rug won't lie flat: uneven beat or weft tension; blocking helps a little, not a lot.
- Figures above are typical teaching ranges; regional traditions use very different setts.

## Verify
- [ ] Warp at high, even tension on a rigid loom.
- [ ] Protective kilim band at start and end.
- [ ] Knot type chosen and consistent; ground weft beaten firmly every row.
- [ ] Width checked often; edges straight; ends finished securely.
