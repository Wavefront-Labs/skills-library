# Threaded-in patterns and twist management

**Goal:** a firm, warp-faced band with a clean pattern and neither the warp nor the
weaver tangled by accumulated twist.

## Tablets and how they work
- Usually square cards with four holes (A, B, C, D) at the corners. Each card carries
  four warp threads; turning the card a quarter turn changes which two threads are on
  top, opening a new shed and twisting the four threads into a cord.
- The band is a row of these cords, bound by weft.

## Reading a threading chart
- Columns = cards (card 1, card 2...); rows = holes A–D. Each cell gives the colour in that
  hole.
- Under each column: **S** or **Z** (sometimes shown as / or \), which tells you which side
  of the card the threads enter from. This decides the cord's twist direction and the
  pattern's angle.
- Convention for S/Z varies between authors — check the chart's legend and test on a few cards.

## Warping
- Cut warp lengths (band length + ~50 cm waste) or warp continuously around posts/pegs.
- Thread each card per the chart, stack cards in order, aligned so all **hole A** is in the
  same position (e.g. top front) before you start.
- Tension: one end tied to a fixed point (a post or loom), the other to your belt or a loom
  beam — backstrap-style or on an inkle/tablet loom.

## Weaving
1. Turn the whole pack a **quarter turn forward** (away from you) together.
2. Clear the shed — push the cards apart and beat (with the edge of a sword/beater or the
   shuttle) — cards can drag threads and close the shed.
3. Pass the weft, leaving a small loop at the edge, beat, pull the weft just snug.
4. Repeat. A typical simple sequence: 4 forward, 4 back.
- Tug the weft to the same tightness each pick: band width consistency is mostly weft tension.

## Twist management
- Turning in one direction builds twist **behind** the cards in the unwoven warp; after many
  turns it tightens and stops the shed opening.
- Fixes:
  1. **Reverse direction** regularly (4 forward, 4 back, or longer runs). Reversing creates
     a visible line in the pattern, part of many designs.
  2. **Flip** individual cards (turn them over on their vertical axis) — changes S to Z,
     which changes the pattern but also how twist builds.
  3. Untie the far end and let it unspin (if tied to a swivel or free end).
  4. Use a swivel/fishing swivel at the far end so twist releases automatically.
- Note where you flip or reverse in a weaving record so the pattern can be repeated.

## Patterns
- **Threaded-in patterns**: pattern comes from the colour placement and S/Z orientation;
  just turn all cards together.
- **Double-face** and **brocade** work: cards turned individually or in groups; more advanced.

## Edges
- Outermost cards often have all four holes the same colour, threaded opposite directions
  (one S, one Z) for neat, balanced edges.

## Gotchas
- Sloppy shed (threads straying) = cards not all aligned, or threads with uneven tension.
  Comb warp straight.
- Losing track of where you are: always stop at a known point in the sequence and note it.
- A dropped card stack ruins the order; tie a cord through hole sets when stopping.

## Verify
- [ ] Chart's S/Z convention confirmed; cards aligned at hole A to start.
- [ ] Shed cleared each turn; weft tension consistent; band width even.
- [ ] Twist managed by planned reversals or flips; notes kept.
- [ ] Edge cards threaded opposite for balanced edges.
