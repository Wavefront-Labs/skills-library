# Even beat, selvedges and wet finishing

**Goal:** cloth with consistent picks per inch, straight edges without loops or draw-in,
and a finish that turns loom-state cloth into fabric.

## Rhythm of weaving
1. Press the treadle (open the shed).
2. Throw the shuttle through; catch it at the other side.
3. **Angle or bubble the weft**: lay the weft in the shed at an angle of roughly 20–30°,
   or in gentle arcs ("bubbles"), so there's enough slack to bend over and under each end.
   A weft laid straight across pulls the edges in (draw-in).
4. Beat with the beater, change to the next treadle, and beat again on the closed or
   changed shed (many weavers beat as the shed changes — locks the pick in place).
5. Repeat. Rhythm produces consistency more than strength does.

## Consistent beat
- Measure **picks per inch/cm** with a ruler every few cm; for balanced plain weave
  aim to match the sett (e.g. 12 ends/inch → ~12 ppi).
- Weave at a consistent point: advance the warp often so the fell (weaving edge) stays in
  the same zone relative to the beater; weaving too close to the heddles changes the beat.
- Mark the cloth edge every ~25 cm with a safety pin and record ppi; it's easy to drift
  across a long project or after a break.

## Selvedges
- Let the shuttle **wrap the edge** end before you beat: tension the weft lightly against
  the selvedge with your catching hand, not so hard it pulls in.
- Loops at the edge: too much weft or slack. Draw-in: too little.
- **Floating selvedge**: an extra end each side threaded through the reed but not a heddle,
  used in twills so every pick catches the edge. Go over it entering, under it exiting
  (consistently).
- A **temple** (stretcher) holds width on wide or tight cloth; move it up every few cm.
- Measure width at the reed and at the fell regularly. Up to a few percent draw-in is normal.

## Broken warp ends
- Pin a replacement end (same yarn) into the woven cloth with a T-pin wound in a figure 8,
  thread it through the heddle and dent of the broken end, and weight it off the back of
  the loom (film canister or bottle on a string). Weave on; needle the old end back in later.
- Missing heddle: tie a **repair heddle** of strong cotton string onto the shaft.

## Finishing on/off the loom
- Hemstitch or twine the start and end on the loom if fringe will be left, or weave a hem
  allowance.
- Cut off, secure ends, then **mend**: weave in any loose ends, fix skips (floats where the
  shuttle went over the wrong ends) with a needle.

## Wet finishing
- Loom-state cloth is stiff and gappy; wet finishing lets threads relax, bloom and close up.
- Wash in warm water with mild soap. Agitation level depends on fibre:
  - **Wool**: gentle for a soft finish; more agitation for **fulling** (controlled felting
    — check often, it's irreversible).
  - **Cotton/linen**: can usually take machine washing; linen benefits from pressing while
    damp with a hot iron.
- Rinse at the same temperature (wool shocks into felting with sudden changes), spin or roll
  in a towel, dry flat or line, then press.
- Sample first: wash the sample the way the finished cloth will be cared for, and measure
  shrinkage.

## Gotchas
- Beating harder isn't the fix for a loose-looking cloth — check sett first.
- ppi drift after a break is very common; measure every session start.

## Verify
- [ ] Weft bubbled/angled each pick; ppi measured and consistent.
- [ ] Selvedges straight, draw-in within a few percent.
- [ ] Broken ends replaced and later darned in; skips mended.
- [ ] Cloth wet-finished appropriate to fibre after testing a sample.
