# Backstrap loom setup and tension

**Goal:** a working backstrap loom on which you can open both sheds cleanly and weave a
warp-faced band or cloth at even tension. Backstrap looms are used across many traditions
(Andean, Mesoamerican, Southeast Asian and others), each with distinct techniques and
meanings; this skill covers a generic setup. Learn specific traditions from their weavers
and credit them.

## Parts
- Two **loom bars** (end bars) holding the warp loops.
- A **shed rod** (or tube) holding one shed permanently.
- A **heddle rod** with **string heddles** lifting the other set of ends.
- A **sword** (flat beater) and a weft bobbin or stick shuttle.
- The **backstrap** (leather or woven strap round your hips) tied to the near bar.
- A fixed **anchor** (post, tree, door frame) tied to the far bar.

## Wind a continuous warp
- Use stakes driven into the ground or clamped to a table, spaced for warp length.
- Wind in a figure-eight around the two end stakes so a **cross** forms in the middle —
  each end crosses over to alternate sheds. The yarn is continuous, so there's no waste
  cutting at the ends.
- Count pairs; tie the cross with a cord on each side. Optional: lay a cord at each end
  stake to hold the loops before transferring to the loom bars.

## Transfer to the loom
1. Slide loom bars (or cords lashed to bars) into the end loops, replacing the stakes.
2. Insert the shed rod into one side of the cross — this holds the "shed rod shed".
3. Make string heddles around every end of the **other** shed: a continuous string looped
   around each end in turn and around the heddle rod, all loops the same length.
4. Remove the cross ties once both sheds are secured.

## Tension by body
- Tie the far bar to the anchor at roughly hip or chest height; sit or kneel and attach
  the backstrap. **Lean back** to tension, forward to slacken. The whole technique
  depends on this: you slacken slightly to open the heddle shed and tighten to beat.
- Keep the warp centred and at a comfortable height; poor posture tires the back quickly.

## Opening sheds
- **Shed rod shed**: slide the shed rod toward you and lean back a little; insert the sword
  on edge to widen the shed.
- **Heddle shed**: slacken tension slightly, lift the heddle rod and press down behind it on
  the warp (the stubborn part — the ends cling). Pass the sword and turn it on edge.
- Pass the weft, beat with the sword (several firm strokes). Alternate sheds.

## Warp-faced weaving and pickup
- Warp-faced bands: warp packed close so weft hides; pull weft to keep consistent width.
- Pickup: in complementary- or supplementary-warp structures, the weaver picks up
  particular warps with fingers or a pick before inserting the sword, following a pattern
  held in memory or charted. Many traditions pass these patterns orally.

## Finishing
- Continuous warps can be woven to have four finished selvedges in some traditions
  (closing the gap with a needle) — an advanced step. Otherwise cut and knot ends.

## Gotchas
- The heddle shed sticks: comb fuzzy ends, use smoother warp, or add a second heddle rod.
- Width creeping narrower: weft pulled too hard; measure frequently.
- Cultural note: motifs from specific communities may carry meaning or be owned in some sense;
  ask before reproducing them commercially.
- Details here are generic and paraphrased; tradition-specific practices vary widely.

## Verify
- [ ] Continuous warp wound with a secure cross.
- [ ] Shed rod and uniform string heddles both opening clean sheds.
- [ ] Tension controlled by posture; anchor solid.
- [ ] Band width consistent; posture comfortable for 20+ minutes.
