# Warping board to beam: back-to-front dressing

**Goal:** a warp wound onto the back beam under even tension, threaded and sleyed
correctly, so the cloth weaves without loose or tight ends. Back-to-front (beaming
before threading) is one standard method; front-to-back (sleying and threading first)
is also widely used — pick one and learn it well.

## 1. Calculate the warp
- **Sett** (ends per inch/cm) from the yarn — wrap test: wrap the yarn around a ruler
  for one inch without overlapping, count wraps; a common starting sett for plain weave is
  about half the wraps per inch (twill a little denser). Better still, weave a sample.
- **Width in reed** = finished width + draw-in and shrinkage (often ~10–15% combined, varies).
- **Ends** = width in reed × sett (+ doubled selvedge ends if used).
- **Warp length** = finished length + take-up and shrinkage (~10–15%) + **loom waste**
  (~60–90 cm for a floor loom: tie-on plus the length behind the heddles) + fringe/samples.

## 2. Wind the warp
- On a warping board or mill, wind the planned number of ends from the start peg to the
  end peg along a guide string measured to the warp length.
- Make the **cross** (figure-eight between two pegs near one end) on every pass — it keeps
  ends in order and is the most important thing on the warp.
- Tie the cross securely in four places with contrasting choke ties, plus ties every
  ~1 m along the warp and at both ends. Count in groups (tie a counting thread every 20 or 40 ends).
- Chain the warp off the board (crochet-chain) to stop tangles.

## 3. Spread in the raddle
- Insert **lease sticks** into the cross; tie them together.
- Spread the end loops across a **raddle** (coarse comb, e.g. groups per 1-inch dent)
  centred on the loom to the width in reed. Put the warp-end loop on the back apron rod.

## 4. Beam
- Wind onto the back beam with tension applied at the front by a helper (or with weighted
  bundles), shaking/combing the warp out as it goes.
- Use **warp sticks** or heavy paper between layers every turn or two so lower layers don't
  sink — sinking layers produce slack ends later.
- Stop with enough warp to reach the front beam plus tie-on.

## 5. Thread the heddles
- Move lease sticks behind the shafts; take ends in order from the cross.
- Thread each end through the heddle on the shaft specified by the **threading draft**
  (e.g. 1-2-3-4 for straight twill). Work from one side; check each group against the draft
  before tying it in a slip knot.

## 6. Sley the reed
- Pass ends through reed dents with a **reed hook** at the sley (e.g. 2 per dent in a 6-dent
  reed for 12 epi), centred.
- Check for missed dents and crossed ends: look along the reed in raking light.

## 7. Tie on
- Tie small bundles (around 2.5 cm) to the front apron rod with a half-bow (surgeon's or
  overhand plus half-bow), working from the centre outward alternately.
- Re-tension all bundles by feel/pat until even, then finish the knots.
- Weave a header with thick weft to spread the warp before weaving cloth.

## Gotchas
- Lost cross = ends out of order = crossed threads behind heddles. Never cut choke ties until
  lease sticks are in.
- Uneven beaming is the main cause of slack ends; weights on the back of the loom can rescue
  a few.
- Allowances quoted are typical ranges; sample and record your actual take-up.

## Verify
- [ ] Calculation written down: ends, width in reed, warp length with waste.
- [ ] Cross tied and transferred to lease sticks.
- [ ] Beamed with even tension and warp sticks; no sunken layers.
- [ ] Threading and sley checked against the draft; header weaves square with even tension.
