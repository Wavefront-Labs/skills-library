# Weft-faced shapes, joins and hatching

**Goal:** a flat, square tapestry with a fully covered warp, clean colour areas, joins
that don't gape and edges that don't pull in. Tapestry is discontinuous weft-faced
plain weave: each colour goes only as far as its shape.

## Warp and weft
- Warp: strong, smooth, low-stretch cotton or linen, under **firm tension**.
- Sett: open enough for the weft to pack down and hide the warp. Common pairings are
  around 6–8 ends per inch for medium wool weft (several strands of fine wool together);
  finer work goes denser. Sample — if warp shows, sett is too wide or weft too thin.
- Weft: wool (often several fine strands blended — this also lets you blend colours).

## Setup
- Weave a short plain-weave **header** and twined row to space the warp evenly.
- Put the **cartoon** (full-size drawing) behind the warp (high-warp) or under it
  (low-warp), and ink the main outlines onto the warp ends with a fine permanent marker
  so they're visible from the front as you weave.
- Wind each colour into a **butterfly** (finger skein) or a tapestry bobbin.

## Laying weft
- Lay weft in an arc or "hills" across the open shed, then pack down with fingers or a
  **tapestry fork/beater**. Too little weft → draw-in and warp showing; too much →
  bumps and wavy edges.
- Build **shapes** rather than full rows: weave a shape up a little, then the area next to
  it. Rising diagonals are built by stepping one warp in each pass or two.
- Gentle curves are steps; steep angles step less often. Very shallow angles produce long
  slits.

## Joins between colour areas
- **Slit**: colours turn on adjacent warps, leaving a vertical gap. Fine for short slits
  (a few passes); long slits are sewn closed afterwards or avoided.
- **Dovetail**: both colours turn around the **same** warp end, alternately in groups. Gives
  a toothed, slightly blurred line; no slit.
- **Interlock**: wefts loop around each other between warps. Strong, neat on one face,
  with a slight ridge on the back. Single interlock vs double interlock differ in where the
  ridge sits.
- Choose by the line you want: crisp vertical (interlock), soft (dovetail), quick and
  geometric (slits, sewn later).

## Shading
- **Hatching (hachure)**: two colours alternate in pointed fingers of varying length,
  creating a gradation without a blended yarn.
- **Pick-and-pick**: alternating colours every pick give vertical stripes; used for texture.
- Blend strands within one butterfly to shift colour gradually.

## Outlines and texture
- **Eccentric weft**: weft laid following a curved contour rather than horizontally; it
  outlines shapes but can distort the weave if overused.
- **Soumak** (weft wrapping) gives a raised line.

## Finishing
- Weave a footer to match the header. Cut off, knot warps in pairs or hem them back.
- Darn weft tails vertically into the back behind the same colour; sew slits closed.
- Light steam block (wool) if needed; tapestries hang best with a sleeve or Velcro strip
  sewn along the top.

## Gotchas
- Waisting (narrowing in the middle): insufficient weft each pass — check width every
  few cm with a ruler.
- Building one area too high before neighbours makes it hard to fill the gap; keep areas
  within a few cm of each other.

## Verify
- [ ] Warp fully covered; sett checked by a sample.
- [ ] Width measured regularly; no waisting.
- [ ] Each join chosen deliberately; long slits sewn.
- [ ] Tails darned into matching colour on the back; hanging method attached.
