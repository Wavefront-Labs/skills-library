# Threading and headspacing a bolt-action barrel

**Goal:** a barrel that screws hand-tight onto its action, shoulders squarely, and
closes on a GO headspace gauge but not on a NO-GO — with the chamber concentric to
the bore.

**Legal note:** in many jurisdictions making or modifying a barrel or chamber is
controlled work — licensing, registration or proof testing may apply. Know and follow
your local law; a newly fitted barrel should be proof-tested or at least test-fired
remotely before use where proof is not mandatory.

## 1. Measure the action
- Measure the receiver's thread major/minor diameters and pitch (many common actions
  use around 1.0625"–16 TPI tenons; **confirm on your action — do not assume**), the
  thread depth to the recoil lug face, and the bolt-face recess / counterbore depth.
- Note any cone-breech or bolt-nose recess the barrel must clear.
- True the action first if needed (lug abutments, receiver face) — that is a separate job.

## 2. Hold and dial in the barrel
- Hold the muzzle end in a 4-jaw chuck or headstock spider and the breech in a
  **steady rest** or outboard **spider**, so the breech end projects 50–75 mm.
- Dial in the **bore**, not the outside: a long indicator rod or a precision pin in
  the bore at the throat end, with a test indicator at the steady rest. Aim for
  < 0.005 mm (0.0002") runout at the breech, and check a few centimetres in too.
- Lathe speed for steel: low — 150–250 rpm turning, ~40–80 rpm for threading.

## 3. Cut the tenon
1. Face the breech end square.
2. Turn the tenon to the measured major diameter less ~0.05–0.08 mm for a snug-
   but-free fit, and to length = receiver thread depth (+ lug thickness if the lug
   sits on the barrel shoulder).
3. Undercut a **thread relief** at the shoulder.
4. Single-point the thread (see handcrafts.manual-machining.manual-turning.single-point-screw-cutting-on-the-lathe).
   Use the action itself as the gauge: thread in by hand with no shake and no bind.
5. Cut the barrel shoulder dead square; chamfer the thread start.
6. Cut any bolt-nose counterbore or extractor clearance per the action's dimensions.

## 4. Chamber
1. With the barrel still dialled in, rough the chamber by drilling undersize
   (optional, to reduce reamer load) — never past the shoulder area.
2. Use a **floating reamer holder** or a pusher rod in the tailstock; feed the
   finishing reamer by hand in **short pecks of 2–3 mm**, withdrawing to clear chips
   and flush with cutting oil each time. Low rpm (~50–80).
3. Measure progress with a depth micrometer from the barrel face to the reamer stop,
   or by test-fitting.

## 5. Headspace
1. Clean the chamber. Put the **GO gauge** in and screw the barrel into the action
   with the bolt (striker removed) in place.
2. Approach the final depth in 0.05 mm increments: bolt should close on GO with only
   the faintest feel.
3. Confirm the bolt will **not close on NO-GO**. A third, **FIELD** gauge marks the
   safe-service limit.
4. Torque the barrel to the action per the action maker, re-check GO/NO-GO after
   torquing — headspace changes as the shoulder crushes.
5. Polish the chamber lightly; crown the muzzle.

## Faults
- **Chamber runout / unsymmetrical brass:** bore not dialled at the throat.
- **Chatter rings in chamber:** reamer pushed too hard, not floating, or dry.
- **Bolt closes on NO-GO:** chamber too deep — set the shoulder back one thread
  pitch and recut (or scrap the barrel).
- **Tight thread:** fit by the action, not just the measurement.

## Safety
Barrel steels (4140, 416 stainless) produce sharp stringy swarf — chip hook, glasses.
Never fit a firing pin or use live ammunition during headspace checks.

## Verify
- [ ] Bore runout at the breech ≤ about 0.005 mm before cutting.
- [ ] Barrel threads on by hand, shoulders square with no gap under a feeler.
- [ ] Bolt closes on GO, will not close on NO-GO, after final torque.
- [ ] Chamber surface free of chatter; extraction clearance cut.
- [ ] Local legal requirements and proof/test-fire step completed.
