# Filing flat and square, and hand scraping

**Goal:** file a steel or brass part flat and square to within about 0.02–0.05 mm
by hand, and where a sliding or seating fit is needed, scrape it to an even
contact pattern against a surface plate.

## 1. Set-up
- **Vice height:** top of the jaws at your elbow height when standing with your
  forearm horizontal — the most important and most ignored factor.
- **Soft jaws** (aluminium or fibre) protect finished faces.
- Hold the work low in the vice — about 5–10 mm above the jaws — to stop chatter.

## 2. Files
- Length 250–300 mm for flat work. **Bastard** cut to remove metal, **second cut** to
  refine, **smooth** to finish. A **safe edge** (uncut) file works into a corner
  without cutting the adjacent face.
- New files on brass and bronze (they dull quickly on steel first and then skid on
  brass). Keep a separate brass set.
- Chalk the file to reduce **pinning** (metal clogging the teeth, which scores the
  work); clean with a file card frequently, picking stubborn pins with a soft brass
  or copper point.

## 3. Cross filing — removing metal flat
1. Stand with the left foot forward (right-handed), body at about 45° to the vice.
2. Right hand on the handle, left palm or fingertips on the file tip.
3. Push stroke only, full length of the file. **Lift slightly or relieve pressure on
   the return** — dragging back dulls teeth.
4. Flatness comes from **transferring pressure**: more on the front hand at the start
   of the stroke, even in the middle, more on the back hand at the end. Without this
   the work rounds over ("rocking").
5. Alternate direction about 30–45° each set of strokes so the scratch pattern shows
   where you have reached; the high spots shine.

## 4. Checking flat and square
- Use a hardened **straight edge** or the blade of an engineer's square against the
  light in several directions; light under the middle = hollow, at the ends = rounded.
- Check square from the reference face with an **engineer's try-square**: rest the
  stock on the reference, slide the blade down to the filed face, look for light.
- Mark the reference face and edge and work every other face from them.

## 5. Draw filing — finish
Hold the file across the work with both hands near the work, and push/pull it
along the length. Gives a fine, straight lay and removes cross-filing scratches.
Use a smooth or second-cut file with little pressure.

## 6. Hand scraping
When filing isn't flat enough (machine slides, surface plates, valve seats):
1. Spread a very thin film of **engineer's blue** (Prussian blue) on the surface plate.
2. Rub the work lightly on it in a figure-8; lift. High spots show **blue** (or shiny
   where the blue has been rubbed off — reading varies with which surface is blued).
3. With a flat scraper (carbide or HSS blade, edge honed with a slight radius),
   take short strokes of 6–15 mm at about 30° to the surface, removing only the spots.
4. Change direction 90° each round. Re-blue, re-spot, repeat.
5. Target: an even scatter of bearing spots — commonly quoted as 10–20 points per
   25 × 25 mm square for sliding ways (figure varies by trade standard).
6. **Flaking/frosting** (decorative crescent pattern) is for oil retention, done after
   the surface bears well.

## Faults
- Rounded faces: no pressure transfer; work too high in the vice.
- Deep scratches: pinned file — card it.
- Scrape spots don't spread: blue too thick (it reads everything as high).
- Surface plate itself not checked — it must be of a known grade.

## Safety
Always use a handled file — a bare tang can drive into the palm. Deburr sharp edges
as you go. Keep blue off hands and food areas.

## Verify
- [ ] Vice at elbow height; work low in soft jaws.
- [ ] Straight-edge shows no light in any direction on the filed face.
- [ ] Try-square shows no light between the faces.
- [ ] Draw-filed finish with an even straight lay.
- [ ] Scraped face shows evenly scattered bearing spots over the whole area.
