# Dressing the wheel and surface grinding to size

**Goal:** grind a part (hardened tool steel or soft steel) flat and parallel,
holding size to about ±0.005 mm (two tenths of a thou), with a clean finish and no
burn.

## 1. Choose and mount the wheel
- Typical toolroom wheel: 180–200 mm diameter, aluminium oxide, **46–60 grit**,
  soft-to-medium grade (H–J), vitrified bond — e.g. a "white" or "pink" friable
  oxide for hardened tool steel. Harder steels want **softer** wheels (they shed dull
  grains). CBN for high-alloy and HSS if available.
- **Ring test** before mounting: hang the wheel on a finger or rod and tap lightly
  with a wooden handle at 45° either side of vertical. A clear ring = sound; a dull
  thud = cracked, scrap it.
- Check the wheel's maximum rpm exceeds the spindle speed. Mount with the paper
  blotters, flanges clean, nut snug — not over-tight.
- Balance the wheel on its hub with a balancing arbor if the machine allows.
- Stand **aside** from the wheel's plane for the first minute of running.

## 2. Dress
1. Put a single-point **diamond dresser** on the chuck, angled 10–15° and trailing
   (pointing in the direction of wheel rotation, set slightly off the wheel's
   centreline so it cannot be dragged in — exact placement depends on rotation
   direction; follow the machine maker's guidance).
2. Bring the wheel down until it just touches the diamond.
3. Take 0.02–0.03 mm per pass; traverse across the face **slowly** for a fine finish,
   faster for a freer-cutting open wheel.
4. Finish with one or two 0.01 mm passes. Rotate the diamond in its holder every few
   dressings to keep it sharp.

## 3. Set up the work
- Stone the magnetic chuck lightly with a fine stone to remove burrs before every
  job; wipe it clean.
- Deburr the part. Place it across as many chuck poles as possible; surround small
  parts with **blocking** (steel blocks lower than the part) so they can't slide.
- Thin parts bow on the magnet and spring back when released: grind both sides
  alternately in small amounts, use shims under the high corners, or lower chuck power.

## 4. Grinding
1. Touch off with the wheel above the highest point (use a paper strip as a feeler:
   wheel just catches the paper ≈ 0.03–0.05 mm).
2. Table speed moderate; crossfeed per stroke about **1/4–1/3 of the wheel width**
   for roughing, less for finishing.
3. **Downfeed:** roughing 0.02–0.03 mm per crossfeed pass; finishing 0.005 mm or less,
   then one or two **spark-out** passes with no downfeed.
4. Flood coolant (water-soluble at the maker's ratio) — it reduces burn and stops
   thermal growth throwing size. Without coolant, take lighter cuts and let the part
   cool before measuring.
5. Measure on a surface plate with a micrometer or a height gauge and indicator,
   **at room temperature**. Turn over and grind the second side for parallel.

## Faults
- **Burn (blue/brown marks), heat checks:** wheel glazed or too hard, cut too heavy,
  no coolant. Dress, lighten cut.
- **Chatter marks:** wheel out of balance, loose spindle, or dull diamond.
- **Lines / "fish tails":** loose grains or swarf in the coolant.
- **Taper:** chuck not ground true — grind the chuck itself in place (an advanced job).
- **Oversize after cooling:** measured hot.

## Safety
Wheel guard always in place. Face shield or glasses. Grinding dust from hardened
steel and the wheel is fine and harmful — extraction or coolant. Never grind
aluminium or soft non-ferrous on the toolroom wheel (it loads and can burst under
heat). Demagnetise parts after grinding.

## Verify
- [ ] Wheel ring-tested, rated above spindle rpm, guard on.
- [ ] Freshly dressed; diamond sharp and trailing.
- [ ] Chuck stoned and clean; part blocked.
- [ ] Size within tolerance measured at room temperature; parallel checked at four corners.
- [ ] No burn colour, chatter or tracking marks.
