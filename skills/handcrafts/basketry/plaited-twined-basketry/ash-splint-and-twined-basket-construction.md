# Ash-splint and twined basket construction

**Goal:** a square-based splint basket that's square and sturdy, and a working
grasp of twining for round warps — the two main flat-element and twined methods.

## 1. Splint material
- **Black ash** (traditional in north-east North America) is pounded so the annual
  growth rings separate into long strips; the strips are split and scraped smooth.
  **White oak** splint is riven and shaved with a drawknife. Both need green or
  soaked wood and are a craft of their own (some ash populations are threatened by
  emerald ash borer — source responsibly).
- **Flat reed** (rattan core, commercial) is the usual substitute and learning
  material. Flat reed has a **right side** (smooth) and a rough side — bend it; the
  rough side splinters on the outside of the bend. Keep the smooth side out.
- Soak flat reed in warm water a few minutes until pliable; don't over-soak.

## 2. Square base (checkerweave)
1. Cut **stakes** (spokes): the base width + 2× the height + extra for tucking.
2. Lay half the stakes side by side, evenly spaced (smooth side down if inside of
   basket faces up). Mark the centre.
3. Weave the cross stakes over-under through them from the centre outward,
   alternating start positions.
4. **Square the base:** measure diagonals — equal diagonals = square. Adjust spacing.
5. Optional: twine round the base edge with a fine round reed to lock it.

## 3. Upsett and sides
1. Dampen the stakes and **bend them up** sharply at the base edge (upsetting);
   crease with a fingernail on the inside.
2. Weave the sides with **weavers** (narrower splint) in a **start-stop** method: each
   row is a separate weaver that overlaps itself for 4 stakes at the end — this keeps
   the basket square. Or continuous weaving with an odd number of stakes (add one by
   splitting a stake).
3. Push each row down tightly; keep stakes vertical and corners square.
4. Stagger the overlap position from row to row.

## 4. Rim
1. Cut alternating stakes level with the top row and bend the others over to the
   inside (or outside), tucking them down behind a weaver.
2. Place a **rim splint** on the inside and outside, filler (seagrass) between if
   wanted, and **lash** them with a narrow lasher through the space below the top
   row — twice round in opposite directions for an X-lash.

## 5. Twining
- Two (or more) wefts cross between each warp: **pair** = one twist per warp.
- **Z-twist and S-twist** look different; alternating rows makes a chevron effect
  ("arrow" pattern). Keep the twist direction deliberate.
- Twining packs tighter than plain weave and can hold round, stiff warps (round reed,
  willow, roots, spruce). Many traditions twine bags and hats over soft warps.
- **Plaiting** (palm, birch bark, pandanus): elements interlace at angles (diagonal
  plaiting) with no rigid stakes; corners form by folding elements.

## Gotchas
- **Rough side out** on bends = splintering; always check face.
- **Dry splint** cracks at the upsett — re-wet locally.
- **Basket twists** if stakes aren't vertical; correct row by row.
- Lots of weavers drying = shrinkage gaps; pack rows down again before the rim.

## Verify
- [ ] Base diagonals equal (square).
- [ ] Stakes upsett cleanly; sides vertical at each corner.
- [ ] Overlaps staggered; rows packed.
- [ ] Rim lashed tight; no stakes springing.
- [ ] Twining tension even with consistent twist direction.
