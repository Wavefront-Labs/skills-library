# Straw plaiting, corn dollies and rush mats

**Goal:** pliable material that doesn't crack, even plaits, and finished objects
that hold their shape after drying.

## 1. Straw
- **Varieties:** traditional long-stemmed, **hollow-stemmed** wheat (older varieties;
  many modern wheats are short and pithy — sources specialise in craft straw), also
  oat, rye and barley. Hollow stems are needed for corn dollies (the next straw slots
  into the end of the last).
- **Harvest/buy:** cut just before full ripeness for colour, dry. Strip the leaf sheath
  to the first node; cut above the top node (the "neck") for the working length.
- **Tempering:** soak in cold or lukewarm water ~20–30 minutes (thicker straw longer),
  then wrap in a damp towel for 15+ minutes. Work damp; re-temper as it dries.
  Over-soaked straw discolours.

## 2. Spiral plait (corn dolly core technique)
1. Tie 5 straws together just under the ears (heads).
2. Spread them like a five-pointed star around a central core (optional core of
   extra straws, which you can later remove to make it hollow).
3. Take a straw, bend it right across the next straw, then turn the work a quarter
   turn; repeat — each fold crosses the next straw anticlockwise. The plait builds
   up in a spiral.
4. **Widen** by placing each fold slightly outside the last; **narrow** by placing it
   inside. That's how "neck", "bell" and "lantern" shapes form.
5. **Joining:** when a straw runs short, cut it just after a fold and push the thin
   end of a new straw into its hollow stem.
6. Finish by tying off tightly and trimming.

## 3. Harvest knots and simple plaits
- Plain 3-straw plaits for braided hangings; harvest knots (traditionally given as
  tokens) from tied loops of plaited straw.
- Tie with fine thread, wrapped and knotted under a binding of straw or ribbon.

## 4. Rush
- **Bulrush** (*Schoenoplectus* spp., the craft "rush") harvested in summer, dried
  slowly (several weeks) in the shade so it stays green-gold. Store dry.
- **Prepare:** wet briefly (dip or a few minutes' soak), then wrap in damp cloth to
  **mellow** a few hours. Wipe the rush from butt to tip to squeeze out air and
  water — it becomes soft and supple.
- Rush is weak at the tip and strong at the butt; mix butts and tips for even
  thickness when plaiting.

## 5. Rush mats and baskets
- **Plaiting:** make long 3- or 9-strand plaits; then **coil** and **sew** the plait
  edge-to-edge with rush or strong thread to build round or oval mats and baskets.
- **Check-weave mats:** cross rushes over-under on a frame.
- Keep work slightly damp; finished pieces shrink as they dry — pack tight.

## 6. Straw marquetry
- Split straw lengthwise with a knife; open it flat; **iron** it flat on a cool-warm iron
  (scorching changes colour — a feature in some work).
- Glue flattened straw on paper backing, then cut shapes and lay on a base, varying
  grain direction for light play. Dyed straws for colour.

## Gotchas
- **Dry straw/rush cracks**; too wet discolours and moulds. Temper/mellow, don't drown.
- Pithy modern straw won't take joins — use proper craft straw.
- Store finished pieces dry; mould and pests love damp straw.

## Verify
- [ ] Straw hollow and tempered; bends without cracking.
- [ ] Spiral plait even; shape changes deliberate.
- [ ] Joins hidden; ends tied and trimmed.
- [ ] Rush mellowed and wiped; plait thickness even.
- [ ] Mat/basket flat or shaped as intended after drying.
