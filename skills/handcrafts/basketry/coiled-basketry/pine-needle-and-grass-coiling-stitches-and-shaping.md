# Pine-needle and grass coiling: stitches and shaping

**Goal:** a firm, even coiled basket — the bundle consistent, stitches evenly
spaced, walls rising where you want them and a finish you can't see.

## 1. How coiling works
A continuous bundle (the **core**: pine needles, grass, rush, raffia, cord or rope)
spirals out from the centre; each round is **stitched** to the previous one with a
binding material (raffia, waxed linen thread, sinew, yarn). Unlike stake-and-strand,
there's no warp — the stitching is the structure.

## 2. Materials
- **Pine needles:** long-needled pines (longleaf types are prized; length varies
  by species and region). Clean with warm soapy water, dry, and **soak in warm water
  ~20–30 minutes** before use to make them pliable. Snip off the papery sheaths at the
  ends (or leave on as a decorative detail at the start).
- **Grasses / rush:** gather mature, cut and dry; dampen before use.
- **Binding:** waxed linen thread (strong, colourful), raffia (traditional), or
  thin leather. Needle: blunt tapestry needle or a sharp needle for tight work.
- **Gauge:** a short tube (a section of straw, a drinking straw or a cut-off
  plastic tube) that the bundle passes through — keeps the core thickness even.

## 3. Start the centre
- Tie a small bundle of needles tightly with thread, wrap it, and bend into a tight
  circle; stitch it closed through the centre.
- Alternative: a drilled wood, gourd or shell disc as the centre with holes for the
  first round of stitches.
- The first two rounds are the fiddliest; keep them tight and small.

## 4. Coiling and stitches
1. Add needles continually into the gauge so the bundle stays the same thickness.
   Stagger additions so there are no thin spots.
2. Stitch through the top part of the previous coil, over the new bundle. Spacing:
   consistent (e.g. stitches about 1 cm apart); decide by the look you want.
3. Stitches:
   - **Plain / lazy stitch:** wrap the bundle several times, then take one stitch into
     the coil below.
   - **Split stitch:** pass the needle through the centre of the previous round's
     stitch, splitting it — creates radiating lines.
   - **Wheat stitch / V-stitch:** two stitches into the same place, forming a V —
     decorative and strong.
   - **Figure-eight:** around both coils, used in some traditions.
4. Add stitches as the circumference grows, keeping spacing even (split stitch:
   occasionally add an extra stitch between).

## 5. Shaping
- **Flat base:** lay each coil directly beside the previous one (outside it).
- **Walls:** place each new coil **on top of** the previous one, a little in or out,
  to rise straight, flare or curve in. Changing position gradually gives smooth curves.
- Look at the profile often; once a shape goes wrong it's hard to recover.

## 6. Finish
Gradually **taper** the bundle over the last 10–15 cm by trimming needles inside the
gauge, so the last coil thins to nothing; stitch over it with tight wrapping. Bury the
thread end in the coils.

## Fabric-rope variant
Cotton clothesline coiled and zigzagged on a sewing machine makes quick bowls and
baskets: zigzag wide enough to catch both coils; tilt the base against the machine to
raise walls.

## Gotchas
- **Uneven bundle** = wobbly coil and lumpy profile: always keep the gauge full.
- **Dry needles snap** — keep the working bundle damp, re-soak as needed.
- **Loose stitching** loosens more as needles dry and shrink; pull firmly.
- Wild-gathered needles: check permission and avoid diseased material.

## Verify
- [ ] Centre tight and small; no gap in the middle.
- [ ] Bundle consistent thickness all round.
- [ ] Stitches evenly spaced; pattern consistent.
- [ ] Profile matches the intended shape.
- [ ] Final coil tapers to nothing; thread ends invisible.
