# Round willow base, staking up, waling and border

**Goal:** a sturdy round stake-and-strand willow basket that sits flat, rises
evenly and has a border that doesn't spring open.

## 1. Prepare the willow
- Willow is sold dried in bundles by length (e.g. 3 ft, 4 ft, 5 ft) as **brown**
  (bark on), **buff** (boiled, bark stripped, tan) or **white** (stripped, pale).
- **Soak** in cold water: a common rule of thumb is **about a day per foot of
  length** for brown willow; buff and white soak much faster (an hour or two —
  verify with your supplier). Then **mellow**: wrap in a damp cloth or plastic for
  several hours or overnight so moisture equalises.
- Correctly mellowed rods bend round your finger without cracking. Over-soaked rods
  go slimy and dark; use within a day or two.
- Sort rods by thickness: thickest for base sticks and stakes, finer for weaving.

## 2. Base (slath)
1. Cut 6–8 base sticks from thick butts (e.g. for a 20 cm base, sticks a little
   longer than the diameter). Split half of them through the centre with a knife and
   **thread** the other half through, making a cross — the **slath**.
2. **Tie in** with two fine rods, tips first, going round the four arms of the cross
   in **pairing** (two weavers, crossing between each arm) for 2–3 rounds.
3. **Open out** the sticks to separate them evenly like spokes and continue pairing,
   joining in new rods tip-to-tip and butt-to-butt.
4. Keep the base **slightly domed** (crown up) — it sits better when finished. Press
   it flat periodically under your foot.
5. Stop at the planned diameter; trim stick ends flush.

## 3. Staking up
1. Cut stakes (one or two per base stick each side), sharpen the butt ends to a
   long slype.
2. Push one stake into the base beside each base stick, deep into the weave with a
   **bodkin** opening the way.
3. **Prick up**: crease each stake just outside the base with the knife point and bend
   it up; gather and tie the tops in a hoop to hold the shape.

## 4. Upsett and sides
- **Upsett:** a few rounds of **three- or four-rod wale** at the base of the stakes.
  This sets the basket's shape and lock — work it tightly and carefully; it decides
  whether the sides go up straight.
- **Sides:**
  - **Randing** (one rod in and out) or **French randing** (many rods worked in
    sequence — fast, even, good for beginners).
  - **Slewing** (two or more rods together) for speed on larger baskets.
- **Wale** a few rows before the border to firm the top.
- Keep stakes evenly spaced and the side angle consistent; check with the eye from
  above and the side every few rounds.
- **Rap down** (tap the weave down with a rapping iron) so rows pack tight.

## 5. Border
**Three-rod border** (one common border): each stake in turn is laid down behind and
in front of the next stakes in a set pattern so they lock. Kink each stake first just
above the weave so it bends cleanly. Trim ends on the inside at an angle resting on a
stake ("picking off") once the basket dries.

## 6. Handle (optional)
A bow handle of a thick rod pushed down beside stakes, wrapped with fine rods.

## Gotchas
- **Under-mellowed rods crack** at the kinks — especially at prick-up and border.
- **Dry-out mid-work:** keep the basket damp with a spray or cover between sessions.
- **Basket leaning:** uneven upsett or stakes unevenly spaced.
- Willow shrinks as it dries; weave firmly and rap down — loose work loosens further.
- Picking knife is sharp; cut away from you.

## Verify
- [ ] Rods mellowed — bend without cracking.
- [ ] Base slightly domed and sits flat when pressed.
- [ ] Upsett tight; sides rise at an even angle.
- [ ] Border locked; nothing springs out.
- [ ] Ends picked off cleanly after drying.
