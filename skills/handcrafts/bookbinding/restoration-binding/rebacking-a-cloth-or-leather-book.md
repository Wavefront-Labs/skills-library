# Rebacking a cloth or leather book

**Goal:** a book whose boards had come away or whose joints had split, now opening
safely on a new spine and joints, with the original spine laid back on so the book
looks like itself. Reversible, sympathetic materials only.

## 0. Assess first
- **Is the sewing sound?** If sections are loose or thread broken, it needs
  resewing, not a reback.
- **Is the paper sound?** Brittle paper (fold a corner twice; it snaps) may need
  conservation advice.
- Record the book's condition with photos before starting. For valuable or
  institutional items, consult a conservator — rebacking is an intervention.
- **Red rot** (powdery leather): consolidate with Klucel G in ethanol before
  handling (unconfirmed concentration — commonly a few percent; check a
  conservation supplier's guidance).

## 1. Remove the old spine
1. Slit along the joints with a thin knife or scalpel to free the spine covering.
2. Lift it off whole if possible; peel or pare off old linings on its back. Keep it,
   flat between blotters.
3. Clean the text block spine: apply a thin layer of wheat starch paste, wait for it
   to soften old glue and linings, scrape off with a bone folder or micro-spatula.
   Don't disturb the sewing.

## 2. Lift the board coverings
- With a thin lifting knife, lift the cloth or leather along the spine edge of each
  board, about **15–20 mm** in, and lift the pastedown on the inside the same
  distance. Work slowly; leather pares as you go.

## 3. Line and reinforce
1. Paste **Japanese tissue** (kozo, medium weight) to the spine, then a strong
   **linen or kozo hinge** strip that extends 2–3 cm onto each side (this becomes
   the new joint).
2. Add a paper lining matched to the spine shape.
3. Make a **hollow tube** (Oxford hollow) of kraft or bond paper the spine's height and
   width, glued to the spine. This lets the new covering flex.

## 4. New spine covering
- **Cloth books**: book cloth toned with acrylics to match the original's colour,
  backed with Japanese tissue if thin.
- **Leather books**: new goatskin or calf pared thin (joints ~0.4 mm), toned to match.
- Cut to spine height + turn-ins and width = spine + 2 × lift distance.
- Turn in head and tail around a card stiffener over the hollow. Slide the edges
  **under** the lifted board coverings; paste and rub down. Form the joints with a
  bone folder and tie up with cord between pressing boards; dry under light weight.

## 5. Lay down
1. Paste the lifted cloth/leather back onto the new covering on the boards.
2. Paste the old hinge strip under the lifted pastedown; reposition the pastedown.
3. **Lay the original spine on**: trim to fit, paste the back (wheat starch paste
   or a PVA/paste mix for cloth), and position. Tie up and dry.
4. Leather: dress lightly or not at all — modern conservation favours minimal
   dressing.

## Gotchas
- **Tearing the lifted cloth**: lift with a broad flat knife, not a point; dampen
  very slightly.
- **Book won't open after**: hollow omitted or hinge too stiff; joints too narrow.
- **Colour mismatch**: tone in daylight; test on scrap.
- **Spine lettering lost**: keep the original spine; lay it on even if damaged.
- Prefer reversible materials (paste, tissue) over PVA on valuable books.

## Verify
- [ ] Sewing checked sound before starting; condition photographed.
- [ ] Old spine kept and laid back on.
- [ ] Boards open to 180 degrees without strain at the joints.
- [ ] Lifted cloth/leather and pastedowns laid down flat, no bubbles.
- [ ] New material colour-matched in daylight and texture.
