# Sewing a Coptic binding

**Goal:** an exposed-spine book with separate boards, linked by chain stitches,
that opens completely flat — no glue on the spine. The same station layout and
link-stitch logic carries over to other unsupported link-stitch structures.

## 1. Materials
- 6–12 sections of 3–5 folded sheets each, grain parallel to the fold.
- Two boards (1.5–2.5 mm greyboard, covered in paper or cloth, or 3 mm wood)
  cut to page size plus about 2 mm all round. Covered boards should be pressed dry
  before punching.
- **Waxed linen thread** (e.g. 4-ply or 18/3 linen run through beeswax). Length:
  roughly section height × number of sections + one extra spine length; for a
  long book, join in new thread with a weaver's knot inside a fold rather than
  using a huge length.
- **Curved needle** (helps reach under the stitches), awl, punching cradle,
  pencil, ruler, bone folder.

## 2. Stations
- Make a **template strip** the height of the section. Mark an even number of
  stations: two near each end about 12–15 mm in from head and tail, and pairs
  spaced evenly between, e.g. 4 or 6 stations for a 20 cm book. Pairs are
  usually 1–2 cm apart; each pair carries its own chain.
- Pierce every section through the fold in the cradle using the template, keeping
  the template always the same way up (mark "head").
- On each board, pierce or drill holes at the same heights, **about 8–10 mm in from
  the spine edge** (closer and the board tears out; further and the cover hinges
  stiffly).

## 3. Sewing (one needle per station pair version)
This describes the common single-needle method, sewing two stations at a time
and working across the spine.
1. **First section to front board**: start inside section 1 at station 1, leave a
   10 cm tail. Go out through the station, around the outside of the board edge and
   in through the board hole from outside, then back up between board and section
   and into the same station. Pull snug.
2. Go along inside the fold to the next station, out, and repeat the board
   attachment. Continue to the last station.
3. **Add section 2**: at the last station, come out, pass the needle into the
   matching station of section 2 (lying on top). Inside section 2, go to the next
   station, come out, and **link back** under the thread between section 1 and the
   board, then back into the same station of section 2.
4. **Chain stitch**: from section 3 onwards, at each station come out, pass the
   curved needle **under the stitch connecting the two sections below** (from the
   spine side, outward), pull to form a loop link, then go back in at the same
   station. This builds the chain.
5. Kettle-link at the head and tail station each time you change section.
6. **Last section to back board**: do the board attachment as in step 1, then
   catch the chain of the previous section and go back in. Tie off inside the last
   section with a double half-hitch around the long stitch; trim to 5 mm.

## 4. Tension and spacing
- Pull each stitch **along the direction of sewing**, not away from the spine,
  or the holes tear.
- Hold sections square to the board each time; a cocked section shows up as a
  stepped spine.
- Constant moderate tension: the book should open flat, and the chain should be
  snug with no visible slack loops.

## Gotchas
- **Board tears out**: holes too close to the edge, or board too thin; use 2 mm+ or
  reinforce with a strip of Japanese paper.
- **Spine fans or swells**: thread too thick for thin sections; drop to finer
  thread or add sheets per section.
- **Chain twists**: always go under the link from the same side.
- **Pages stepping**: sections not knocked up square before sewing each one.
- Other structures: **longstitch** and **limp vellum** sew sections directly
  through a wrapper; the **pamphlet stitch** is a 3- or 5-hole figure-of-eight
  through a single section — same station discipline.

## Verify
- [ ] Every station hole lines up across all sections and both boards.
- [ ] Chains run straight across the spine without twists or loose loops.
- [ ] Book opens flat at every spread including the first and last.
- [ ] Boards hinge freely and don't pull at the holes.
- [ ] Knots tied off inside folds, trimmed and not visible from outside.
