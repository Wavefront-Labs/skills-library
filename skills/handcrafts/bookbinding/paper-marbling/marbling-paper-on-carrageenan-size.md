# Marbling paper on carrageenan size

**Goal:** sharp, repeatable marbled sheets for endpapers and covers — colours
that float, spread to the right size, comb cleanly and transfer without blotches or
lines.

## 1. Size (the bath)
- **Carrageenan** (from Irish moss) powder: about **2 tablespoons per 4 litres** of
  water, blended in thoroughly. Rest at least 12 hours, ideally overnight, so it
  hydrates and air bubbles rise. (Proportions vary by supplier — check the bag.)
- Alternative: **methyl cellulose** size, easier to keep but colours spread less
  crisply.
- Bath: a tray at least 3 cm larger than your sheet all round, size about 3–5 cm
  deep. Work at room temperature (around 18–24 °C); cold size is sluggish, warm size
  spoils faster. Carrageenan keeps a few days, refrigerated longer.

## 2. Alum the paper (mordant)
- Dissolve **aluminium sulfate (alum)** about 1–2 tablespoons per litre of warm
  water (unconfirmed — check the supplier's ratio). Sponge evenly on one side of
  each sheet, mark the alumed side with pencil on a corner, dry flat or use slightly
  damp.
- Alumed paper should be used within a day or two. Use unsized or lightly sized
  paper (text-weight, 80–120 gsm). Wear gloves; alum irritates.

## 3. Paints
- **Acrylic marbling paints** (thin, flow-type) or **watercolour/gouache** with
  **ox gall** as the spreading agent. Thin to the consistency of milk.
- **Test drop**: a good colour spreads to a 5–8 cm circle and keeps a crisp edge.
  Sinks or stays tiny = add a drop of ox gall (or water for acrylics). Spreads to a
  pale film = too much gall; add more paint.
- Colours dropped later need slightly **more** gall than earlier ones to push them
  aside.

## 4. Skim and throw
1. **Skim** the surface with a strip of newspaper the width of the tray immediately
   before each sheet; it removes dust and the surface skin.
2. Throw colours with a **whisk brush** (broom-straw) tapped against a rod or your
   finger, or drop from an eyedropper. First colour covers most of the surface,
   following colours fewer and smaller. This is the **stone** (Turkish) pattern.

## 5. Patterning
- **Gel-git (back and forth)**: draw a stylus or rake with teeth 2–5 cm apart
  across the bath one way, then back between the lines.
- **Nonpareil**: after gel-git, pull a fine comb (teeth 3–6 mm apart) slowly
  along the length.
- **Bouquet**: a nonpareil pulled through with a wavy comb motion.
- **Chevron**: rake back and forth at an angle.
Move slowly and steadily; jerks make broken lines.

## 6. Lay down and lift
1. Hold the sheet by opposite corners, alumed side down, curve it slightly, and lay
   it onto the surface in **one rolling motion** from the centre or one end. Don't
   stop or the line of hesitation prints.
2. Lift by one edge after a few seconds, drag over the edge of the bath to shed
   size, and rinse gently under running water or in a tray.
3. Dry flat on a line or rack; press flat between boards once dry.

## Gotchas
- **Bubbles / white spots**: air trapped laying the sheet; roll on rather than drop.
- **Colour washes off on rinse**: paper not alumed, or wrong side down.
- **Lines across the sheet**: hesitated when laying down.
- **Colours sink**: size too thin or too cold, paint too thick.
- **Muddy colours**: too many layers or unskimmed size.
- Paste papers (paste and paint combed by hand) suit endpapers too and need no bath.

## Verify
- [ ] Test drop spreads to the target circle with a crisp edge before the run.
- [ ] Size skimmed before every sheet.
- [ ] Pattern lines continuous with no breaks or hesitation lines.
- [ ] No white air spots on the finished sheet.
- [ ] Colour stays fast after rinsing and drying.
