# Gold tooling and titling a leather spine

**Goal:** a straight, evenly spaced, bright gold title and decoration on a leather
spine, struck once cleanly, with no smudged or "fat" letters and no bare patches.

## 1. Tools and materials
- **Handle letters** (individual brass letters on handles) or a **type holder**
  with loose brass type; **pallets** for lines across the spine, small tools for
  ornaments.
- A **finishing stove** (gas or electric) to heat tools.
- **Glaire**: traditional egg-white glaire (white beaten, left to settle, a little
  vinegar) or a commercial shellac-based glaire such as B.S. glaire.
- **23 to 23.75 carat gold leaf** in a book, a **gold cushion** and **gold knife**,
  a little Vaseline or coconut oil to make the leaf adhere for transfer.
- A **gold rubber** (crepe rubber) to clean surplus; cotton wool; paper template.

## 2. Plan and blind in
1. Set the title out on a strip of thin paper the height of the spine panel: centre
   each line, check letter spacing visually (round letters closer, straight
   letters wider apart).
2. Tape the strip to the spine. Heat the tools slightly (warm, not hot) and press
   through the paper to **blind in** a light impression. Remove the paper.
3. Go over the impressions directly on the leather with a slightly warmer tool to
   deepen and sharpen them. Lines now have to be right; gold will not hide a crooked
   letter.

## 3. Glaire and lay gold
1. Wash the area with a weak vinegar/water mix to clean the grain, let it dry.
2. Paint **glaire** into the impressions with a fine brush; two thin coats, each
   dry before the next. On porous calf, a first coat of paste-wash helps.
3. Cut the leaf on the cushion with the gold knife; a rectangle a little larger
   than the line.
4. Smear a trace of Vaseline on the back of your hand or over the spine panel,
   pick up the leaf with cotton wool or the warmed pad and lay it over the
   impressions. Lay a second leaf over the first for richer gold (two layers is
   common practice).

## 4. Strike
- **Temperature** matters most. Test on a damp sponge or wet pad: a tool that
  hisses sharply is too hot; a soft sizzle is about right. In numbers, many
  finishers work around 100–120 °C for goatskin (unconfirmed — use a test piece
  of the same leather; calf usually wants less heat).
- Hold the tool upright, find the blind impression by feel through the gold,
  rock very slightly (for curved spines and pallets, roll across), and press with
  steady pressure for 1–2 seconds. **One strike only** — a second strike doubles
  the image.
- Wipe tools clean on a leather pad between strikes.

## 5. Clean off and assess
- Wipe surplus gold with the **gold rubber**; it lifts unstruck gold cleanly. Wipe
  away grease with a little benzine or naphtha on cotton wool if needed.
- If a letter is patchy, re-glaire just that letter, re-lay gold and strike again
  in the same impression with the tool slightly cooler.

## Hot-foil variant
For editions or students: a **blocking press** or heated handle tool with
**hot-foil**: no glaire; set temperature and dwell per the foil supplier's sheet.
Faster and consistent, but flatter and less bright than leaf.

## Gotchas
- **Smudged / fat letters**: tool too hot or held too long.
- **Gold won't stick**: tool too cool, glaire too old or not dry, too little
  pressure.
- **Burnt leather**: too hot, darkens the impression.
- **Crooked title**: no paper template; spine not clamped level.
- **Glaire marks outside the lines**: show as dull patches; keep the brush tight.

## Verify
- [ ] Title centred, letters evenly spaced and on a straight baseline.
- [ ] Every impression bright and fully filled; no bare spots or double images.
- [ ] No gold or glaire residue outside the tooling after cleaning.
- [ ] Leather around the tooling not darkened or scorched.
- [ ] A test strike on scrap leather was done at the working temperature.
