# Sewing, rounding and casing in a hardcover

**Goal:** a sewn, rounded-and-backed text block in a cloth case that opens well,
holds its shape and has clean, square joints.

## 1. Paper and sections
- **Grain parallel to the spine** for every sheet: text paper, endpapers, boards,
  cloth and spine lining. Test by bending each way (softer bend = with the grain)
  or by dampening a strip edge. Cross-grain books warp and won't open flat.
- Fold sections (signatures) of 4–8 sheets; thinner sections round more evenly.
- **Endpaper sections**: a folded sheet tipped with a 3 mm line of PVA to a
  protective waste sheet, one at each end.
- Knock up the block square at head and spine, and press 30–60 min.

## 2. Mark up and sew on tapes
- Jog the block, clamp, and mark across the spine with a square: **kettle stations**
  about 12 mm (½ in) from head and tail, and two to four tapes of 12 mm cotton
  tape spread evenly (a book 25 cm tall: three tapes). Mark both edges of each tape.
- Pierce stations from inside the fold with an awl in a cradle.
- Sew all-along with unbleached linen thread (18/3 or 25/3 for typical text
  weights; thicker thread = more swell = easier round). Go round each tape and
  link sections at head and tail with a **kettle stitch**. Keep tension even and
  tap each section down with a bone folder.
- Leave 3–4 cm of tape free each side.

## 3. Glue up, round and back
1. Square the block in a press, spine projecting 3 mm. Brush thinned PVA, or a 50/50
   PVA/wheat-paste mix, into the spine; work it between sections. Wipe surplus.
2. While the glue is still flexible (15–30 min, leather-hard), **round**: lay the
   block flat, pull the top sections toward you with fingers and thumb while
   tapping the spine with a backing hammer; turn and repeat. Aim for a third of a
   circle, not a half.
3. **Back**: set backing boards (bevelled hardwood) in a lying press with the
   block projecting by the **board thickness plus a hair** (for 2.5 mm board, about
   3 mm). Hammer glancing blows from the centre outwards to form shoulders at
   roughly 45 degrees without flattening the round. Leave in the press to dry.

## 4. Line the spine
- Glue on **headbands** at head and tail (stuck-on, or sewn for better work).
- First lining: **mull/super** cut to spine length minus 6 mm, 3–4 cm wider than the
  spine on each side, pasted only to the spine.
- Second lining: **kraft paper** cut exactly to spine width and height, glued on.
  Rub down into the round. Dry.

## 5. Make the case
- **Boards**: greyboard/binder's board, grain head to tail. Height = block +
  2 × square (squares 3 mm typically). Width = block width from shoulder to fore-edge
  + one square − ~3 mm set back from the joint.
- **Spine stiffener**: thin card, width = spine across the round, height as boards.
- **Joint gap** between board and spine piece: about 2 × board thickness + cloth
  thickness, typically 7–9 mm for French-groove style casing (unconfirmed — test with
  a scrap case on your board and cloth).
- Lay out on glued cloth with 15–18 mm turn-ins. Cut corners at 45°, leaving 1.5
  board thicknesses at the tip; turn in head and tail, then fore-edges.
- Curve the spine stiffener over a rod to match the round.

## 6. Case in
1. Glue the tape ends and mull onto the waste sheet; slip waxed paper inside.
2. Lay the block in the case, check squares all round. Glue (PVA/paste mix) the
   endpaper, not the board, wiping out to the edges. Close the board straight down.
3. Repeat the other side, then **form the groove** with a bone folder and put the book
   in a press between **pressing boards with brass edges** (or knitting needles) set
   in the joints. Press overnight with waxed paper and blotters inside each cover.

## Faults and fixes
- **Boards warp outward**: cloth/paper shrank more than the paste-down; line the
  inside of the boards with paper before casing, keep grain correct, press longer.
- **Round collapses to flat or concave**: too little swell or rounded too late
  (glue set hard); use thicker thread or round sooner.
- **Book won't close cleanly**: joint gap too narrow for the board thickness.
- **Squares uneven**: block not square before sewing; check at every stage.

## Verify
- [ ] Every component has grain running head to tail.
- [ ] Sewing is tight, kettle stitches linked, block square.
- [ ] Round is about a third of a circle and shoulders match board thickness.
- [ ] Squares equal (about 3 mm) on all three edges of both boards.
- [ ] Book opens without the endpapers tearing at the joint, and lies closed flat
      with no board warp after 48 hours out of the press.
