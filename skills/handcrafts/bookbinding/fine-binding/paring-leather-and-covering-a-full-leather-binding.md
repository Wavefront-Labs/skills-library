# Paring leather and covering a full leather binding

**Goal:** a tight-back full leather binding with crisp raised bands, neat turn-ins
and well-formed headcaps, the leather thin enough at the joints to open freely and
thick enough on the boards to wear. Assumes the book is already sewn on raised
cords, rounded and backed, with boards laced on and the spine lined.

## 1. Choose and cut the skin
- Vegetable-tanned **goatskin (morocco)** is the standard; calf is smoother but
  scuffs and ages less well. Use binding-grade leather sold as archival/conservation
  quality.
- Lay the book open on the flesh side and mark the cover size plus **18–20 mm**
  turn-ins all round. Run the spine along the backbone of the skin (firmest, least
  stretchy part) with the grain/hair pattern head to tail.

## 2. Pare (skive)
Tools: a **French or English paring knife** stropped to a razor edge, a litho stone
or glass slab, and optionally a spokeshave or a **Scharf-Fix** style blade paring
machine for the overall thinning.
- **Overall thickness**: typically pare down to about 0.6–0.8 mm on the boards for
  a medium book (unconfirmed — set to the book's size; small books thinner).
- **Spine and joints**: thinner again, around 0.4–0.5 mm, so the hinge flexes.
- **Turn-ins**: feather the edge to almost nothing over the last 10–15 mm so the
  edge disappears under the pastedown.
- **Head and tail of spine**: pare thinnest where the leather turns over the
  headcap, otherwise it lumps.
- Pare with short, low-angle strokes, keeping the blade nearly flat; check thickness
  by pinching and by holding to a light. Strop every few minutes; a dull knife tears.
- Mark the board, joint and spine zones lightly in pencil on the flesh side before
  starting.

## 3. Prepare the book
- Sand the board edges and corners slightly round; leather tears over a sharp
  edge.
- For **false bands**, glue strips of leather or card across a hollow; for true
  raised bands the cords already stand proud.
- Fill any hollows on the boards so the surface is flat.

## 4. Covering
1. Dampen the grain side lightly with a sponge, then paste the flesh side with
   **wheat starch paste**, leave 5–10 min for it to relax, then paste again
   thinly. Leather should feel limp and cool, not wet.
2. Lay the book on its fore-edge on the leather; draw the leather up over the
   spine and onto both boards, smoothing from the spine outwards with the palm.
3. **Nip up the bands**: with **band nippers**, pinch the leather tight against
   each side of every band, then work down the panels between them with a bone
   folder. Tie up with cord between bands if they won't hold.
4. **Turn-ins**: open the board, turn in head and tail first. Over the spine,
   tuck the leather into the gap between spine and lining (or a hollow) and form a
   **headcap**: a small turned, rounded cap shaped with a bone folder and a
   thread tied in the joint to set the shape.
5. **Corners**: mitre or cut a library corner; pare the overlaps so they don't
   create a bump. Tuck with a folder.
6. Check the joints open freely before the paste sets: open each board at 90 degrees
   and smooth the leather into the joint.

## 5. Dry
- Stand the book with the boards slightly open, a waxed paper and dry board inside,
  no press weight on the spine. Let dry 24 hours before filling and pasting down.
- Fill the board inside to the level of the turn-ins with card before pasting down
  endpapers.

## Gotchas
- **Water stains / tide marks**: uneven damping of the grain; dampen evenly or
  not at all on pale calf.
- **Leather cracks at the joint later**: pared too thick there, or over-stretched
  when wet.
- **Lumpy turn-ins**: edges not feathered.
- **Bands lose definition**: not nipped while damp, or leather too thick.
- **Knife accidents**: always pare away from the free hand; keep the knife
  stropped — a sharp knife needs less force.

## Verify
- [ ] Leather graded: thinnest at joints, headcaps and turn-in edges.
- [ ] Bands crisp and square to the spine, panels smooth.
- [ ] Headcaps evenly formed, the same shape at head and tail.
- [ ] Both boards open fully without straining the joint after drying.
- [ ] No tide marks, scuffs or paste stains on the grain.
