# Styrene scratchbuilding and kitbashing

**Goal:** build parts or whole models that don't exist as kits — accurately, from
plans — and repurpose existing kits into new subjects. Draft: standard techniques.

## 1. Plans and scale
- Get drawings or photos with known dimensions. Scale every measurement:
  real size ÷ scale denominator. 1:35 → 3.5 m becomes 100 mm; 1:72 → 3.5 m ≈ 48.6 mm.
- Print the drawing to your model scale (scale the printout so a known dimension
  measures correctly). Stick it under clear film and build on it.

## 2. Material
- **Styrene sheet** (plasticard) in thicknesses (0.25-2 mm), **strip**, **rod**,
  **tube**, and structural shapes (I-beam, angle, channel) from Evergreen- and
  Plastruct-type suppliers. Same plastic as most kits, welds with liquid cement.
- Brass rod and tube for strength (pins, axles, antennas); joined with CA.

## 3. Cutting
- **Score and snap:** score along a steel rule with a sharp blade or scriber 2-3
  passes, then bend along the line; clean break. Thick sheet: score both sides.
- Strip: cut square with a **chopper** (guillotine jig) for identical repeated
  lengths. Punch-and-die sets make perfect discs (rivets, hatches).
- Mark with a fine pencil, not a pen (ink bleeds under paint).

## 4. Building boxes and shapes
- Box construction: build inside **bracing** (square strip in corners) so walls
  stay square and joints have area. Use a machinist's square and right-angle blocks.
- Cement edges with liquid cement; let it gas off before closing a box completely
  (trapped solvent warps sheet).
- **Curves:** heat-form thin sheet over a former with hot water or a heat gun (gently;
  styrene shrinks); laminate thin layers for compound shapes. Wrap thin sheet around
  tube for cylinders.
- **Stretched sprue:** heat sprue over a candle and pull for fine rod (aerials,
  wires, rigging).

## 5. Detail
- Rivets (punched discs, decal rivets, or embossed with a pounce wheel on thin sheet
  from behind), bolts (hex rod sliced), hinges, panel lines (scribed with a scriber
  and template).
- Keep detail in scale: a 2 mm-thick "metal sheet" at 1:35 is 70 mm in real life.

## 6. Kitbashing
- Combine parts from several kits to make a new subject or a variant. Keep a spares
  box of sprues sorted by type: wheels, hatches, engines, pipes, "greebles" (generic
  detail).
- Cut with a razor saw; fill and sand joins as in
  `handcrafts.model-kit-building.scale-model-assembly.cleanup-seams-and-assembly-sequence`.
- Unify mixed sources with a common primer and consistent panel detailing.

## 7. 3D printing
Resin 3D printing is now common for scratchbuilt parts; designed in CAD and
printed, then cleaned and cured. The skills above still apply for finishing.

## Gotchas
- Solvent-heavy joins on thin sheet distort it; apply sparingly.
- Boxes built without bracing go rhombus-shaped.
- Out-of-scale thickness is the commonest giveaway — use the thinnest sheet that's
  strong enough.

## Verify
- [ ] All dimensions converted to scale and checked on the drawing.
- [ ] Parts square (checked with a square) and braced.
- [ ] Detail thickness plausible at scale.
- [ ] Joins filled and primed; mixed parts read as one model.
