# Thinning, pressure and control

**Goal:** smooth, even coats and controlled fine lines, without spidering, tip
dry or a clogged airbrush. Airbrushing is three variables in balance: **paint
thickness, air pressure and distance**. Change one at a time. Draft: general
hobby practice; your paint maker's thinning guidance wins.

## 1. Equipment
- **Gravity-feed, dual-action** airbrush (press for air, pull back for paint) is
  the modelling standard; 0.2-0.3 mm nozzle for general work, 0.4-0.5 mm for primers
  and large areas. Single-action (button gives air + fixed paint) is simpler, less
  control.
- **Compressor with a tank and regulator** plus a **moisture trap**; tankless
  compressors pulse.
- A **spray booth** with extraction (or at least a well-ventilated area) and a
  respirator rated for the paints you use — lacquers are solvent-heavy.

## 2. Thinning
- Aim for **skimmed-milk** consistency for most coats (it flows off a stirring stick
  in a thin stream, not drips of cream).
- Use the paint's matching thinner: acrylic with its own thinner (water alone for
  some brands works poorly), lacquers with lacquer/leveling thinner. Typical
  starting ratios vary from 1:1 to 1:2 paint:thinner — test.
- **Flow improver / retarder** reduces tip dry for acrylics.

## 3. Pressure
Typical starting ranges (bar ≈ psi/14.5):
- **General base coats:** ~15-25 psi (1-1.7 bar).
- **Fine lines and mottling:** ~8-15 psi with thinner paint, close distance.
- **Primers / thick paints:** higher, ~25-30 psi.
Rule: thinner paint + lower pressure + closer = finer control. Thicker paint needs
more pressure.

## 4. Control
- Always: **air on first, paint off last** — start air, pull paint, move, release
  paint, then air off. Prevents spatter blobs at the start and end of strokes.
- Keep moving; start the stroke off the model and finish off it.
- Distance: ~10-15 cm for coverage; 1-3 cm for fine lines.
- **Spidering** (paint running in legs): too close, too much paint, or paint too thin.
- **Rough/sandy surface:** too far, pressure too high, or paint drying before landing.
- **Tip dry:** paint dries on the needle tip — wipe it with a damp brush or cotton
  bud; flow improver helps.
- Build colour in several **thin passes**.

## 5. Masking
Tamiya-type kabuki tape for hard edges (burnish edges down), Blu-Tack or putty
rolls for soft edges, liquid mask for complex shapes. Remove tape at a low angle
after the paint is touch-dry.

## 6. Cleaning
- Between colours: spray through thinner/cleaner until clear, backflush gently
  (finger over nozzle cap — only if your airbrush maker allows it).
- End of session: strip the needle carefully (pull from the back, never push a
  dirty needle forward through the nozzle), clean the cup and nozzle with cleaner and
  a soft brush. **Never bend the needle tip** — a bent tip ruins spray patterns; check
  under magnification.
- Lubricate the needle lightly (airbrush lube) occasionally.

## Verify
- [ ] Paint thinned to milk and tested on a card first.
- [ ] Pressure set and logged for the paint/job.
- [ ] Air-on-first, air-off-last discipline; no spatter.
- [ ] Even coats with no spidering or orange peel.
- [ ] Airbrush cleaned and needle undamaged.
