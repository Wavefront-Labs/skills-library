# Groundwork, water and composition for dioramas

**Goal:** a base that frames the models and tells a story at a glance, with
ground, vegetation and water that look right at scale. Draft: common methods;
resin products vary — follow their labels.

## 1. Story and composition
- One-sentence story first: "a recovery crew pulls a bogged truck out of a stream".
  Every element should support it; cut what doesn't.
- **Composition:** avoid placing the main subject parallel to the base edge — angle it.
  Use triangles (three points of interest), leave negative space, create a focal
  point where figures' gaze and lines lead.
- Plan the **viewing side** and sightlines; small vignettes may have one; larger
  dioramas need several.
- Base size: just big enough. A sprawling empty base weakens the story.

## 2. Base and landform
- A wooden plinth (sealed) with a raised frame edge looks finished.
- Build terrain in **XPS foam** carved with a knife and sanded, glued down.
- Cover with groundwork: **celluclay/papier-mâché**, air-dry clay, or ready-made
  **texture pastes**. Press in tracks, footprints and debris while wet. Sprinkle
  fine sifted soil, sand, small stones.

## 3. Paint the ground
Dark earth basecoat, then drybrush lighter earth tones; washes in ruts; pigments
for dust or mud. Ground rarely is one colour.

## 4. Vegetation in scale
- **Static grass** (fibres) applied with an electrostatic applicator stands upright;
  fibre length to scale (e.g. 2-4 mm for 1:35 short grass). Mix colours.
- **Tufts** (pre-made clumps) give quick variety. Vary sizes; cluster against
  rocks and edges — plants grow where mowers and feet don't reach.
- Leaves: punched paper or birch-seed leaves; scale trees from wire armature + foliage.
- Dried real plants (roots, moss) can work but check scale and seal against crumbling.

## 5. Water
- **Two-part epoxy resin** for still, clear water: pour in thin layers (each per the
  maker's maximum, often a few mm) to avoid heat and cracking. Seal the base and
  dam the edges first (tape or acetate) — resin finds every gap and leaks.
  Tint lightly; colour depth by painting the riverbed darker in the middle.
- **Acrylic water gels/effects:** for ripples, waves and splashes, stippled over set
  resin or used alone for shallow water. Dries clear from white.
- Puddles: a few drops of gloss varnish or resin in ruts.
- Place submerged items (wheels, legs) before pouring; it's permanent.

## 6. Integrate models and figures
- Set vehicles into the groundwork (they have weight — wheels sink a little).
- Figures interact with each other and the subject; mud on boots and lower
  trousers matching the ground.
- Unify everything with a final dusting of the same pigment colours.

## Gotchas
- Mixed scales (a 1:32 tree with 1:35 figures is fine; 1:72 grass with 1:35 is not).
- Resin poured too thick in one go: yellowing, heat, bubbles, cracks.
- No dam at the edges: resin runs off the side of the base.

## Verify
- [ ] Story in one sentence; nothing extraneous.
- [ ] Subject angled; focal point clear from the main viewing side.
- [ ] Ground and vegetation scaled and varied in colour.
- [ ] Water poured in layers with dams; clear and bubble-free.
- [ ] Models sit in the scene (weight, mud, interaction).
