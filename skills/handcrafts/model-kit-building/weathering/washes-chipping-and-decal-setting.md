# Washes, chipping and decal setting

**Goal:** a model that looks like a used real object at scale, with decals that
look painted on. The order of layers matters: each step protects the one before.
Draft: common modelling practice.

## 1. Sequence overview
Base paint → (chipping undercoat if using hairspray method) → **gloss coat** →
**decals** → gloss seal → **panel/pin wash** → **filters** → chipping and streaking →
**matte or satin coat** → pigments and mud → final small parts.

## 2. Gloss coat and decals
- Decals on matte paint **silver** (trapped air shows as a shiny film). Gloss coat
  first — acrylic gloss varnish airbrushed or a gloss lacquer.
- Soak decals briefly in warm water, slide onto a surface wetted with a **setting
  solution** (Micro Set-type), position, blot.
- **Softening solution** (Micro Sol / Mark Softer-type) makes the film conform to
  panel lines and rivets. The decal wrinkles while softening — don't touch it; it
  flattens as it dries. Repeat if needed; prick bubbles with a needle and re-apply.
- Seal decals with another gloss coat so washes don't stain the film edges.

## 3. Panel and pin washes
- Thin dark paint (enamel or oil paint thinned with odourless thinner, or ready-made
  panel-line accents) touched into panel lines and around details with a fine brush;
  capillary action carries it. Over a gloss coat it flows; on matte it stains.
- After a few minutes, wipe excess with a cotton bud lightly dampened in thinner,
  in the airflow direction (aircraft) or downward (vehicles, rain).
- **Enamel/oil over acrylic:** safe, because their thinners don't attack cured acrylic
  much (test first). Lacquer thinner will.

## 4. Filters
Very thin, transparent colour (oil or enamel) over whole panels to vary tone — e.g. a
yellow filter warms dark yellow armour, blue cools grey. Subtle, many thin layers.

## 5. Chipping
- **Sponge chipping:** dab dark brown/rust paint from a torn sponge, mostly blotted off,
  where wear happens (hatch edges, steps, leading edges). Less is more.
- **Hairspray technique:** rust/primer colour → hairspray or chipping fluid → top
  colour; then wet with water and work with a stiff brush or toothpick — the top coat
  flakes away realistically. Use acrylic for the top coat.
- Fine chips with a fine brush, dark-brown with a lighter edge highlight on the
  "torn paint" side.

## 6. Streaking and staining
Rain marks, rust streaks, grime: dots of oil paint, blended down with a damp
(thinner) brush in vertical strokes.

## 7. Matte coat and pigments
- A matte (or satin) varnish unifies the sheen and kills the gloss decal look.
- **Pigments** (weathering powders) for dust, mud, exhaust soot: dry-brushed on, or
  mixed with fixer/thinner for mud. Fix with pigment fixer. Pigments shift when
  varnished — apply after, or accept the change.

## Gotchas
- Silvering decals: no gloss coat or not enough setting solution.
- Wash attacked the paint: wrong thinner over unsealed paint.
- "Overweathering": real vehicles at scale distance look cleaner than you think.
  Check reference photos for the vehicle's era and use.

## Verify
- [ ] Gloss coat before decals; no silvering after softening.
- [ ] Washes over a gloss/sealed surface and cleaned up.
- [ ] Chipping placed only where wear happens.
- [ ] Final matte/satin coat even; pigments fixed.
- [ ] Compared to reference photos for plausibility.
