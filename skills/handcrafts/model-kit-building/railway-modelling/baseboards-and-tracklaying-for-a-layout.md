# Baseboards and tracklaying for a layout

**Goal:** a layout that runs trains without derailments or stalls. Scenery is fun,
but reliable track is what makes a layout used rather than abandoned. Draft: common
practice; product codes reflect UK/US conventions.

## 1. Scale and gauge
- **OO** (UK, 1:76 on 16.5 mm track), **HO** (1:87 on 16.5 mm, international), **N**
  (~1:148-1:160 on 9 mm), **O** (1:43-1:48). Smaller scale = more railway in the space,
  fiddlier handling.
- Decide the era and region early; it governs rolling stock, signals and scenery.

## 2. Baseboards
- Must be **flat, rigid and stable** with humidity. Common: plywood top (9-12 mm)
  on a softwood or ply frame (open-frame or L-girder), or lightweight ply box
  frames for portable/exhibition sections.
- Avoid chipboard/MDF tops in damp spaces (sag and swell). Seal timber.
- Height: higher than you think is comfortable for viewing (often 1 m+), reach no
  more than ~60-70 cm from an edge to any track (you will need to rerail stock).
- Join sectional boards with alignment dowels/pattern-makers' dowels so track lines
  up exactly at joints.

## 3. Track plan rules
- **Minimum radius:** use the largest you can. Sectional "first radius" curves look
  toy-like and limit long stock; check what your locomotives require.
- **Easements** (transition curves): flextrack bent so curvature increases gradually
  reduces lurching and derailments.
- **Gradients:** keep to ~1 in 40 (2.5%) or gentler; vertical curve transitions too.
  Steep grades stall long trains.
- Avoid an S-bend (reverse curve) without a straight between, especially with long
  stock.
- Turnouts: larger (medium/long radius) points run better than short ones.

## 4. Laying track
- Draw centrelines on the baseboard; lay a foam or cork underlay.
- Pin or glue track down without kinks at rail joiners; file rail ends square, debur.
- Joints on curves: solder rails before bending flextrack so joints don't kink.
- Check gauge and level with a track gauge and a small level. **Push a long coach
  through every section by hand** before powering.

## 5. Wiring
- Run a **bus** (heavy wire pair) under the board and solder **droppers** to each
  rail section — don't rely on rail joiners for power.
- **Frogs:** insulated frogs are simpler; **electrofrog/live frogs** run better at
  slow speed but need isolating and frog-polarity switching.
- **DCC vs DC:** DCC (digital) addresses locos individually with simpler wiring
  sections; DC needs cab control and isolating sections.
- Reversing loops need special wiring (auto-reverser on DCC).

## 6. Test-running and ballasting
- Run every loco and longest train forward and **backward** (propelling exposes
  faults) over every route before scenery.
- Ballast: spread loose, shape with a brush, mist with wetting agent (water + drop
  of washing up liquid), then fix with dilute PVA. Keep it out of point blades.
- Clean rails regularly (track rubber or cleaning fluid).

## Verify
- [ ] Baseboards flat (check with a straight edge) and stable.
- [ ] Radii and gradients within the stock's limits; easements on curves.
- [ ] Droppers to every rail section; frogs correctly wired.
- [ ] Every route test-run both directions before scenery and ballast.
