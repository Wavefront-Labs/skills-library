# Layering, glazing and faces on miniatures

**Goal:** a figure with clear form (light where light falls, shade in recesses),
smooth transitions and a readable face, built with acrylics and a brush. Draft:
the standard modern method summarised.

## 1. Prep and prime
- Clean mould lines, wash resin parts, assemble (or paint in sub-assemblies where
  areas are hard to reach).
- **Prime** with a spray or airbrushed primer. **Zenithal:** black or dark grey
  all over, then white or light grey from above (~45° and overhead). It pre-maps
  the light so your later layers follow it.

## 2. Paint consistency and tools
- **Thin your paints** — the most repeated advice for a reason. "Milk" consistency
  for basecoats (two thin coats > one thick); thinner for layers; much thinner for
  glazes.
- A **wet palette** (sponge + parchment paper in a box) keeps paint workable for hours.
- Brushes: a good-pointed size 0-1 synthetic or sable for most work; keep paint out
  of the ferrule; rinse often; clean with brush soap.

## 3. Basecoat → wash → layer
1. **Basecoat** each area in its mid tone.
2. **Wash / shade** (thin dark pigment-rich medium) into recesses — gives depth
   instantly. Don't flood flat areas; tidy pooling with a damp brush.
3. **Re-layer** the mid tone on raised areas, leaving wash in the recesses.
4. **Highlights**: progressively lighter mixes on progressively smaller raised areas,
   following the light source.
5. **Edge highlight**: thin, bright lines on sharp edges (armour, cloth folds).
   Use the side of the brush tip.

## 4. Glazing to blend
- A **glaze** is very thin, transparent colour (paint + lots of medium/water).
  Multiple glazes over a transition smooth stepped layers and tint (e.g. a red glaze
  over skin shadows for warmth).
- **Wet blending:** blend two colours while wet on the model — faster but harder.

## 5. Faces and eyes
- Basecoat skin, shade into eye sockets and under the nose/chin, highlight
  cheekbones, nose bridge, brow and chin.
- Eyes: paint the eye socket dark (dark brown), an off-white almond shape, then a
  dot of dark for the iris touching the top lid, centred both sides (so the figure
  looks forward). Tidy with skin colour. Many painters do eyes **first** after
  basecoat so mistakes are easy to cover.
- Lips: a slightly darker/redder skin mix, highlighted on the lower lip.

## 6. Metals: metallic vs. NMM
- **True metallics** (metallic paints) with shade washes and bright metallic highlights.
- **NMM** (non-metallic metal): painting the reflections with matte colour gradients —
  high contrast, sharp transitions. Advanced; study reference photos of chrome and gold.

## 7. Basing and varnish
- Texture paste or sand + PVA, paint, drybrush, tufts. Rim painted neat (black or
  earth).
- Protect gaming figures with a matte varnish (spray or brush); gloss for gems, eyes
  and wet effects.

## Gotchas
- Thick paint hides detail; you can't fix it except by stripping.
- No consistent light source = "flat" figures. Decide light direction first.
- Spray primer in high humidity frosts or goes grainy.

## Verify
- [ ] Primed (zenithal or plain) without obscuring detail.
- [ ] Paint thin; no brush strokes or lumps visible at arm's length.
- [ ] Light source consistent; recesses dark, high edges light.
- [ ] Eyes centred and looking the same way.
- [ ] Based and varnished.
