# Cleanup, seams and assembly sequence

**Goal:** a kit with no visible seams, gate marks or glue smears, built in an order
that lets you paint everything you'll see. The instructions show *what* goes
together; you decide *when*. Draft: widely shared modelling practice.

## 1. Read and plan
- Read the whole instruction sheet first. Mark: parts painted before assembly
  (cockpits, interiors, engines), parts to leave off until after painting
  (landing gear, antennas, clear parts), and colour callouts.
- Group into **sub-assemblies** you can paint separately and join later.
- Check for kit errata and aftermarket (photo-etch, resin) you'll integrate.

## 2. Removing parts
- Cut parts off the sprue with **flush sprue cutters**, leaving a little nub; never
  twist off. Then trim the nub with a fresh blade or a second flush cut, and sand.
- Clear parts: cut far from the part, then sand gently; stress lines are permanent.

## 3. Clean up
- Scrape **mould lines** with the back of a hobby knife blade, then sand.
- Fill **ejector-pin marks** on visible faces.
- Wash parts in warm soapy water if the plastic feels oily (mould release),
  especially resin parts.

## 4. Dry-fit
Always dry-fit before glue. Fix fit issues by sanding mating faces, thinning parts,
or adding shims (styrene strip). Fit problems hidden by glue become visible seams later.

## 5. Glue choice
- **Liquid polystyrene cement** (thin, capillary — Tamiya Extra Thin-type): hold
  parts together and touch the brush to the seam; it wicks in and **welds** the
  plastic. Squeeze gently so a little molten plastic ("weld bead") rises —
  sand flush once hard (a day) for a seamless join.
- **Thick tube cement:** gap-filling but stringy; easy to smear.
- **CA (superglue):** for resin, metal, photo-etch, and quick fills. Doesn't weld
  styrene; brittle.
- **White glue / PVA or canopy glue:** for clear parts (CA fogs clear plastic).

## 6. Seams
- After the weld cures, sand flush: coarse to fine sanding sticks (e.g. 400 → 1000 →
  2000+). Keep panel lines; rescribe any you lose.
- Gaps: fill with putty, CA + accelerator (sands like plastic), or a thin surfacer
  (Mr. Surfacer-type) brushed on and wiped with thinner-dampened cotton bud.
- **Prime and check:** primer reveals every flaw. Fix, re-prime.

## 7. Assembly order
Interior → paint → close fuselage/hull → seams → prime → main painting → decals →
weathering → final small parts. See
`handcrafts.model-kit-building.weathering.washes-chipping-and-decal-setting`.

## Gotchas
- Glue fingerprints in cement softened plastic: they're permanent. Hands off until set.
- Over-sanding erases rivets and panel lines.
- Forgetting nose weight (aircraft with tricycle gear) — "tail sitter". Read the
  instructions for "add weight here".

## Verify
- [ ] Sequence planned with paint-before-assembly parts marked.
- [ ] No gate nubs, mould lines or ejector marks on visible faces.
- [ ] Every joint dry-fitted before gluing.
- [ ] Primer shows no seams; panel lines intact or rescribed.
