# Wet-moulding a knife sheath

**Goal:** a sheath that holds the knife by friction alone (a positive "click" when the
handle seats), protects the edge with a welt so the blade never cuts the stitching, and
keeps its shape when dry. The technique — damp veg-tan pressed over a form and dried in
place — is the same for cases, holsters and masks.

## 1. Pattern
- Lay the knife on paper; trace it and add a **welt** strip along the edge side: a
  layer of the same leather 6–10 mm wide following the blade's edge outline, offset
  ~2–3 mm outside it. The welt is what the blade edge rides against.
- Add stitch allowance (~5 mm outside the welt) and enough material to wrap the
  thickness of the handle — measure handle thickness and add half of it to each side
  around the handle area.
- Belt loop: either a fold-over at the top or a separate stitched loop/dangler.
- Leather: **veg-tan only**, 3–3.5 mm (8–9 oz) for most belt knives.

## 2. Cut, glue welt, stitch
1. Cut out, glue the welt to one half, fold (or glue the second piece) and stitch the
   perimeter, keeping stitching **outside** the welt.
2. Dry-fit the knife: it should slide in with some drag before moulding.

## 3. Protect the knife
Wrap the blade and handle in **2–3 layers of cling film** (or put it in a thin freezer
bag). Wet leather left against carbon steel stains the leather black and rusts the blade
in minutes; tannins react with iron.

## 4. Wet and form
1. **Soak** the sewn sheath in room-temperature water for ~10–30 seconds, until bubbles
   stop rising, then let it sit 5–10 minutes so the surface is cool but not shiny — the
   same "cased" state as for tooling. (Warm water speeds penetration; hot water starts
   to shrink and harden the leather — that is cuir bouilli, not what you want yet.)
2. Insert the wrapped knife fully.
3. With a **bone folder** or modelling spoon, press the leather firmly into every
   contour of the handle: guard, finger choil, the step where blade meets handle, and the
   pommel swell. The retention comes from leather pressed **under the guard/choil step**.
4. Define the welt line and blade outline with the edge of the tool. Work from the centre
   outward; keep the stitch line flat.
5. Remove and reinsert the knife a few times while damp to check it will come out.

## 5. Dry and harden
- Remove the knife (do **not** leave it in to dry — risk of rust) or keep it wrapped and
  remove after an hour once the shape holds.
- Air dry 24 hours, or speed up in a low oven at about **50–60 °C (120–140 °F)** with the
  door ajar, checking every 15 minutes. Above roughly 70 °C leather can shrink and
  crack (unconfirmed exact threshold — it depends on the tannage; test scrap first).
- For extra stiffness (cuir-bouilli style), warm the formed sheath and rub in hot
  beeswax, or dip briefly in hotter water before forming; test on scrap, as results
  vary a lot.

## 6. Finish
Dye if wanted (before final sealing), then oil lightly, burnish edges and apply a top
coat or wax. Recheck retention once dry — leather shrinks slightly.

## Faults and fixes
- **No retention:** not pressed under the guard/choil step, or leather too dry while
  forming. Re-wet that area only and re-press.
- **Too tight:** knife won't seat — reinsert while damp and rock it gently.
- **Black stains:** steel touched wet leather — wrap better.
- **Cracked, shrunken leather:** dried too hot.
- **Blade cuts stitching:** welt missing or too narrow.

## Safety
Handle the knife with the edge away from the hand pressing the leather; a blade inside
damp leather can push through at the tip if forced.

## Verify
- [ ] Welt runs the full edge line; stitching sits outside it.
- [ ] Knife clicks in and holds when the sheath is held upside down and shaken gently.
- [ ] Knife can be drawn with one firm pull.
- [ ] No stain or rust on blade or leather.
- [ ] Sheath stiff, dry and sealed; the edge cannot reach the stitching.
