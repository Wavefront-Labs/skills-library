# Dyeing and antiquing veg-tan leather

**Goal:** even, streak-free colour on vegetable-tanned leather, optional antiqued
contrast in tooled recesses, and a finish that does not rub off on hands or clothes.
Chrome-tan arrives already dyed; this is for undyed veg-tan.

## 1. Choose a dye
| Type | Behaviour | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Spirit/alcohol** | penetrates fast, vivid | dries quickly so overlaps show; can dry the leather — oil after |
| **Oil-based** | penetrates, even, slower dry | forgiving for beginners; let cure ~24 h |
| **Water-based** | low odour, easy cleanup | can raise the grain slightly; usually less penetrating |
Always test on **scrap from the same hide** — veg-tan takes colour differently by area
and tannery.

## 2. Prepare
1. Finish carving/tooling and let the leather dry completely (dye on damp leather goes
   blotchy and lighter).
2. Clean with a **deglazer** or a light wipe of the recommended solvent only if the surface
   is waxy or has hand oils (unconfirmed for every brand — check the dye maker's advice;
   some don't recommend deglazing).
3. Optional: a very light coat of **neatsfoot oil** the day before evens absorption,
   though it also slightly reduces dye uptake.
4. Gloves on; cover the bench; ventilate for spirit dyes.

## 3. Apply evenly
- **Dauber/wool:** load lightly, wipe off excess, and apply in **small overlapping
  circles**, working quickly over the whole piece so no edge dries before you overlap it.
  Two or three thin coats beat one heavy one; dilute dye 1:1 with its thinner for the
  first coat if blotching is a risk.
- **Dip** small items for very even colour; **airbrush** for gradients and edges.
- Do the **flesh side and edges** too if visible, and colour edges before burnishing.
- Let dry fully between coats (spirit: ~15–30 min; oil: hours).
- **Buff** with a soft cloth to remove surface pigment — this is what later rubs off.

## 4. Antiquing (for tooled pieces)
Antique is a pigmented paste that sits in recesses and wipes off the high points.
1. Apply a **resist** first (a thin acrylic finish or a dedicated resist) over undyed
   or lightly dyed leather, and let it dry. Without it, antique stains the whole
   surface dark.
2. Work antique into all cuts and backgrounding with a cloth or brush.
3. Within a minute or two, wipe off the surface with a **damp** cloth or sponge, leaving
   colour in the recesses. Wipe in one direction; re-wet the cloth often.
4. For **block-dyed** effects, paint dye into the background before the resist.

## 5. Oil and top coat
- Once dry, a light coat of oil/conditioner restores suppleness (especially after spirit
  dye).
- Seal: a **water-based acrylic finish** for durable sheen (thin, even coats with a
  sponge or airbrush), or **wax/carnauba cream** for a softer look buffed to shine.
  Two thin coats; allow each to dry.
- Burnish edges after dyeing and before or after top coat depending on the finish
  maker's instructions.

## Faults and fixes
- **Blotchy/streaky:** dye on damp or oily leather, or heavy single coat. Dilute; more thin
  coats; dip if possible.
- **Colour rubs off (crocking):** excess surface pigment not buffed off, or no top coat.
  Buff hard, then seal.
- **Antique stuck everywhere:** no resist, or left too long before wiping.
- **Stiff, dry leather:** spirit dye without oiling afterwards.
- **Colour different from swatch:** different hide section; always test on scrap.

## Safety
Spirit dyes and many deglazers are flammable solvents: no flames, ventilate, gloves.
Dispose of oily rags flat to dry or in a sealed metal tin — oil-soaked rags can
self-heat and ignite.

## Verify
- [ ] Test swatch from the same hide matched before dyeing the piece.
- [ ] Colour even under raking light, no lap marks.
- [ ] White cloth rubbed firmly over the finished surface picks up little or no colour.
- [ ] Antiqued recesses dark, high points clean.
- [ ] Leather still flexible; edges coloured and finished.
