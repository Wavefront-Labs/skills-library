# Swivel-knife carving and stamping veg-tan leather

**Goal:** a carved design with clean, consistent-depth cuts and burnished, even-toned
bevels — the base workflow behind Sheridan and Western floral tooling. Only
**vegetable-tanned** tooling leather takes and holds impressions; chrome tan will not.

## 1. Leather and kit
- Tooling-grade veg-tan, **3–4 mm (8–10 oz)** for belts and panels; 2–2.5 mm (5–6 oz) is
  workable for small pieces. Firm, clean grain matters more than thickness.
- **Swivel knife** with a 1/4"-3/8" angled blade, stropped mirror-bright (strop with
  jeweller's rouge every few minutes of use).
- Stamps: **beveler** (smooth or checked), **backgrounder** (fine matting), **pear
  shader**, **camouflage**, **veiner**, **seeder**, **mule foot** to start.
- **Poly mallet or maul** (around 16–24 oz for most stamping), a **granite or marble
  slab** 25 mm+ thick on a solid bench, tracing film and a stylus or modelling spoon.

## 2. Case the leather
Casing = bringing the leather to the moisture where it carves and burnishes best.
1. Sponge clean water evenly onto the flesh and then the grain side (some carvers add a
   drop of dish soap or use a casing product). Do not puddle.
2. Wait while the surface returns to near its **original colour** — usually 10–30
   minutes depending on air and thickness. Bagging the piece in a plastic sack overnight
   in a fridge evens moisture well.
3. Test: press the cased leather against your cheek — **cool but not wet**. Too wet:
   cuts fuzz and impressions are mushy and dark. Too dry: tools skid and leave no
   burnish. Re-dampen lightly as you work, from the back if possible.
4. Back thin pieces with packing tape or stick-on shelf liner to stop stretching.

## 3. Transfer the pattern
Trace the design onto tracing film. Lay it on the cased leather and go over every line
with a stylus, pressing just enough to leave a clear dent. Lift a corner to check before
removing.

## 4. Cut with the swivel knife
- Index finger in the yoke, thumb and fingers on the barrel; blade tilted **slightly
  toward you** so the corner leads.
- Cut **about half the leather thickness** at the deepest, tapering out at the ends of
  flourishes (lift as you finish a curve so it "feathers" out).
- **Pull** the knife toward you and rotate the barrel with the fingers to steer; turn the
  work, not your wrist, for large curves.
- Cut primary outlines first; leave decorative cuts for the end.

## 5. Stamp in order
Hold each stamp vertical (or as designed), strike firmly and consistently, and **walk** it
— overlap each strike by about half the face so there are no steps.
1. **Bevel** along the outside of every cut line, smooth side toward the cut, pushing the
   background down so the design stands up.
2. **Background** the areas between elements with the matting tool to give contrast.
3. **Pear shade** petals and leaves for contour (lighter strikes toward the tip).
4. **Camouflage** at leaf/petal bases, **veiner** for leaf veins, **seeder** at flower
   centres, **mule foot** at stem ends.
5. **Decorative cuts** last with the swivel knife: short, fine, flicked curves following
   the form.

## Faults and fixes
- **Fuzzy cuts:** leather too wet or blade dull — let it dry slightly, strop.
- **Chatter marks / steps in bevels:** striking unevenly or moving the beveler too far
  between strikes.
- **Undercut flaps:** knife leaning sideways; keep the blade vertical sideways.
- **Pattern lost:** leather dried and dents vanished; transfer only what you can cut in
  one session.
- **Dark blotches:** water spots from uneven casing — case the whole piece evenly.

## Safety
Keep the off-hand outside the knife's path. Mallet work is loud on stone; hearing
protection for long sessions is reasonable.

## Verify
- [ ] Leather passes the cheek test while carving and stamping.
- [ ] Cuts are uniform in depth and taper cleanly at flourish ends.
- [ ] Bevels are smooth and burnished, with no stepping.
- [ ] Background is evenly matted with no stray tool marks on the design.
- [ ] After drying flat (weighted), the piece shows no stretching or curl.
