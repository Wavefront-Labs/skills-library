# Lasting an upper by hand

**Goal:** stretch a closed (sewn) upper tightly and evenly over the last so it takes the
last's exact shape, with no wrinkles, the centre line straight and the heel upright —
ready for hand-welting, Goodyear or cemented soling. Lasting is where a shoe's fit and
look are decided; most later faults trace back to it.

## 1. Before you start
- **Last** prepared: holes for tacks filled, feather edge marked. Remove any old tacks.
- **Insole:** vegetable-tanned shoulder ~3–4 mm, soaked, tacked to the last bottom
  (3–4 tacks) and left to dry so it takes the bottom shape; trim flush to the
  feather edge. For hand-welted work, cut the **holdfast** (rib) now.
- **Stiffener (heel counter)** and **toe puff** cut and skived to a feather edge — leather
  (dampened) or thermoplastic. Skived edges must vanish under the upper.
- **Upper** closed, linings trimmed. Case (dampen) the upper leather evenly so it
  stretches: damp through but not wet, as for tooling. Some leathers (patent, some
  chrome tannages) should be warmed rather than wet — check with the tannery.

## 2. Tools
Lasting pincers (pliers with a hammer face), lasting tacks (~13–16 mm for temporary
pulls), tack puller, sharp knife, rasp, lasting cement or paste, a bone folder.

## 3. Pull-over sequence
1. Insert the stiffener between upper and lining with paste; position the upper on the
   last, back seam on the heel centre line, height to the pattern.
2. **Toe first:** grip the toe-end lasting margin at the centre, pull firmly **forward and
   down**, and tack. Check the vamp centre seam/line is straight down the last.
3. **Heel:** pull the back down and tack at the heel centre. Check the top line height on
   both sides.
4. **Joints/balls:** pull each side at the widest point (inside and outside joint) and
   tack. The upper should now be tight with no slack across the instep.
5. Work **alternately side to side**, pulling in small steps toward toe and heel, tacking
   every 10–15 mm. Pull the upper and lining separately if the pattern allows — lining
   first, then the upper, so neither wrinkles.

## 4. Toe and heel shaping
- Insert the toe puff (pasted) once the vamp is pulled over.
- At the toe, gather the excess into **small, even pleats**, pulling each toward the
  centre, then tap them flat with the hammer face. Trim excess so no pleat is thicker
  than the rest of the margin.
- Same at the heel curve. Rub the upper down with the bone folder and leave it on the
  last to dry for **at least 24–48 h** (longer for thick leather) so it sets.

## 5. Secure the margin
- **Hand-welted:** the lasting margin is sewn to the holdfast with the welt; temporary
  tacks come out as you sew.
- **Cemented:** rough the margin and insole edge, cement both, press the margin down,
  then remove tacks.
Trim the lasting margin to a consistent width (~12–15 mm) and flatten.

## Faults and fixes
- **Wrinkles across the vamp:** under-pulled or unevenly pulled; re-pull while damp.
- **Twisted vamp line:** toe pull was off-centre — correct before tacking the sides.
- **Heel leaning:** back seam not on the last centre line; re-set at step 3.
- **Bumps at toe or heel:** stiffener/puff edges not skived thin enough.
- **Torn upper at tacks:** leather too wet or pulled in one heavy go; pull in stages.

## Safety
Keep tacks in a dish, not your mouth (traditional, but a swallowing hazard). Use cements
with ventilation; many contain solvents.

## Verify
- [ ] Vamp centre and back seam align with the last's centre lines.
- [ ] Top line heights match on both sides and both shoes.
- [ ] No wrinkles; upper tight to the last everywhere when pressed with a thumb.
- [ ] Toe pleats flat and even; stiffener and puff edges invisible from outside.
- [ ] Upper dried on the last for the full setting time before bottoming.
