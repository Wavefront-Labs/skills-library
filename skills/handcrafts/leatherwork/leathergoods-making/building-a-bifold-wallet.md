# Building a bifold wallet

**Goal:** a bifold that closes flat, holds 4–8 cards securely, and has crisp edges —
built in an order where every seam can be stitched and every edge finished. The same
logic (pattern → thin → stack → assemble inside-out) applies to card holders, passport
covers and organisers.

## 1. Pattern
- A standard bank card is **85.6 × 54 mm**. Card pockets need about **+3–4 mm width**
  (≈ 90 mm inner) and the pocket tops stepped **12–15 mm apart** so each card shows.
- Typical closed size: ~**115 × 90 mm**; outer panel ~**230 × 90 mm** flat.
- **Fold allowance:** the outer layer goes round the outside of the fold, so make the
  outer **2–4 mm longer** than the interior (more for thick leather) or the wallet will
  spring open. Mock it up in card stock or scrap leather first.
- Mark stitch lines (3 mm from edge) and the centre fold on the pattern. Leave ~5 mm
  of stitch-free gap at the spine so it can fold.

## 2. Materials and thickness budget
Wallet bulk is the enemy. Rough budget:
- **Outer:** 1.0–1.4 mm (veg-tan, calf or goat).
- **Interior/pockets:** 0.6–0.8 mm; card pockets often **0.5–0.7 mm**.
- At the stitched edge, the whole stack should ideally finish under ~**4 mm**. Count
  layers at the deepest edge: outer + interior + pockets. If it exceeds that, split or
  **skive** the edges of the pockets (feather the last 8–10 mm to ~0.3 mm) or fold
  pocket bottoms instead of stacking separate pieces.
- Thread 0.45–0.55 mm with 3–3.38 mm irons suits this scale. (Figures are typical maker
  practice, not a standard.)

## 3. Cut and prepare
1. Cut with a sharp knife against a steel rule; cut card-pocket tops last so they are
   perfectly straight.
2. **Finish the pocket top edges now** (bevel, sand, edge paint or burnish) — once
   stacked, you cannot reach them.
3. Skive where layers overlap and at the fold if stiff.

## 4. Assembly order (inside out)
1. Glue the lowest card pocket onto its backing with a **thin line of glue** 3–4 mm wide
   along the stitch edges only. Glue across the pocket opening and cards will not go in.
2. Stack each next pocket, checking a card slides in each one before the glue sets.
3. Stitch any **internal seam** that will be hidden later (e.g. the bottom of the pocket
   stack or a centre divider) now.
4. Glue the interior to the outer **while folded** around a card or a dowel of the
   wallet's final thickness — gluing flat and then folding causes wrinkles on the
   inside.
5. Trim edges flush with a fresh blade, mark, punch and saddle-stitch the perimeter
   (see handcrafts.leatherwork.hand-stitching.saddle-stitching-with-two-needles).

## 5. Edges
Sand flush 220→400→600, then either burnish (veg-tan) or apply **edge paint** in thin
coats (chrome or mixed tannages), sanding lightly between 3–4 coats, finishing with a hot
creaser or a polished final coat.

## Faults and fixes
- **Wallet springs open:** outer too short for the fold, or glued flat. Glue folded.
- **Too thick / won't fit a pocket:** layers not skived; plan the thickness budget first.
- **Cards fall out:** pockets too wide; leather stretches with use, so start snug.
- **Glue seeping into pockets:** too much glue or spread too wide; use a thin strip.
- **Stepped edges:** layers not trimmed flush before stitching.

## Verify
- [ ] Mock-up made and fold allowance tested before cutting good leather.
- [ ] Every pocket accepts a card snugly and the card stays in when shaken.
- [ ] Wallet closes and lies flat with ~6 cards in it.
- [ ] Edge stack is flush, sanded, and evenly finished all round.
- [ ] Stitch line even; no seam had to be "skipped" because it became unreachable.
