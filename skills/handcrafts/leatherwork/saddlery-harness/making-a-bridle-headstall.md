# Making a bridle headstall

**Goal:** a plain flat headstall (crownpiece with throatlatch, browband, two cheekpieces)
that fits the horse, has strong buckle turnbacks, and will not fail at the bit — tack
failure can injure horse and rider, so strength and inspection come first.

## 1. Measure
- **Poll to bit:** tape from the corner of the mouth on one side, over the poll, to
  the other corner, with a little slack. Typical cob/full sizes run roughly 100–115 cm
  (unconfirmed — measure the actual horse; sizes vary widely between makers).
- **Browband:** behind one ear, round the front of the forehead, behind the other ear,
  not tight — a browband that pinches the ears is a common fit fault. Full size is
  often ~38–41 cm (unconfirmed).
- Design cheekpieces so the buckle sits **at the middle hole** of 5–7 holes spaced
  ~2.5 cm, giving adjustment both ways.

## 2. Leather and cutting
- **Bridle leather** (curried veg-tan, waxed/stuffed) ~**3.5–4.5 mm**, or harness
  leather for driving. Cut strap from the **backbone/bend** area, parallel to the spine,
  where stretch is least.
- Widths: 5/8" (16 mm) for fine bridles, 3/4" (19 mm) common, 7/8"–1" for heavier.
- Cut with a **draw gauge/strap cutter** for consistent width. Even out thickness with a
  splitter so mating parts match.

## 3. Crownpiece
- The crownpiece splits into the **headpiece straps** (cheek buckle billets) and the
  **throatlatch**. Mark the split ~1/3 of the crownpiece length from each end;
  **punch a round hole** at the end of each split cut before cutting into it — a split
  that ends in a sharp slit will tear further under load.
- Skive the crown slightly where it sits on the poll if it is thick, for comfort.

## 4. Buckle turnbacks and keepers
1. Punch a slot (two holes joined) for the buckle tongue at the fold.
2. Skive the **turnback** end (the part folded back) to about half thickness over its last
   25–30 mm so the fold lies flat.
3. Fit the buckle, fold, and include a **keeper** (loop) between buckle and stitching,
   made from 1.5–2 mm leather, stitched or skived and glued closed with the join hidden
   under the strap.
4. Stitch the turnback with **at least 2–3 rows' worth of length** — a common rule is
   that the turned-back length is 3–4× the strap width. Bit ends use a **hook stud** or
   buckle; studs must sit in a reinforced, properly sized hole.

## 5. Stitching and finish
- Traditional fine bridle work runs **8–12 stitches per inch** (~3 mm pitch or finer);
  practical work 7–8 spi. Use waxed linen or polyester sized to the iron.
- Saddle stitch (see handcrafts.leatherwork.hand-stitching.saddle-stitching-with-two-needles),
  backstitching at each end.
- Crease a decorative line 3–4 mm from the edge with a heated **edge creaser** on damp
  leather, bevel and burnish edges, then condition or dress the leather.

## Safety and inspection
- Stress points: **bit ends, buckle turnbacks, crown splits**. Inspect these every use;
  replace cracked leather or worn stitching immediately.
- Use quality stainless or solid brass buckles; cheap cast buckles can snap.
- Never let a new rider use untested tack; before first fit, pull each turnback hard by
  hand against a fixed point (or hang a known weight from it, not yourself) and inspect
  stitching and buckle tongue for any give.

## Faults and fixes
- **Browband pinches ears:** too short; it should sit without pulling the crown forward.
- **Split tearing:** no stop hole at the end of the split.
- **Buckle twists/rolls:** turnback not skived or keeper too loose.
- **Stretch after use:** strap cut from belly area or across the spine.

## Verify
- [ ] Measured from the horse, with buckles landing on the middle holes.
- [ ] Every split ends in a punched hole.
- [ ] Turnbacks skived, stitched, with keepers fitted and tight.
- [ ] Stitching backstitched; no exposed cut ends at bit attachments.
- [ ] Fitted test: two fingers fit under the throatlatch; the browband sits clear of the ears.
