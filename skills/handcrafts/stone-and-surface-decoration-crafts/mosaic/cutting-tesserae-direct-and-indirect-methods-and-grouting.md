# Cutting tesserae, direct and indirect methods, and grouting

**Goal:** a mosaic whose lines flow (good andamento), with tesserae cut to shape, bonded
to the right substrate with the right adhesive, and grouted (or not) appropriately — that
will last where it's installed.

## 1. Materials and cutting
- **Vitreous glass** (20 mm squares, flat face + ridged back): cheap, uniform, cut with
  **wheeled glass nippers** (two carbide wheels). Place the wheels a little onto the edge,
  aligned with the desired cut line, and squeeze.
- **Ceramic/porcelain tile**: **tile nippers** (side-biting carbide jaws) nibble shapes; porcelain is tough.
- **Smalti** (Italian enamelled glass, irregular faces) and **marble/stone**: cut on a
  **hardie** (chisel set in a log) with a **martelina** (double-ended hammer): rest the
  piece on the hardie edge and strike it with a light, sharp blow along the intended line.
  Gives the uneven riven faces that make smalti sparkle.
- **Eye protection always**; cut inside a clear bag or with a shield. Glass shards and
  dust: sweep and vacuum, don't blow.

## 2. Andamento — the flow
The direction in which rows of tesserae run carries the image.
- **Outline** key forms first with a row (or two) of tesserae following their contour (opus vermiculatum).
- Fill the background in rows that echo the outline (opus musivum) or in a regular grid (opus tessellatum).
- Keep **interstices** (gaps) consistent — typically 1–3 mm; even gaps look more skilled
  than perfectly cut pieces.
- Avoid "crossroads" (four corners meeting) and long continuous straight joints in organic areas.

## 3. Methods
- **Direct**: stick tesserae face-up directly on the final base. You see the work as you go;
  best for 3D objects, textured smalti work and indoor panels.
- **Indirect (reverse)**: glue tesserae **face-down** on brown kraft paper with water-soluble
  glue (wheat paste / gum arabic) over a **mirrored** drawing. Then press the sheet into a
  bed of adhesive on site, wet and peel the paper off after setting. Gives a flat, even
  surface — right for floors, worktops and walls. You can't see the face while working.
- **Mesh (double-direct)**: tesserae glued face-up onto fibreglass mesh over the drawing
  (cling film between), then the mesh sections are bedded in thinset on site.

## 4. Substrate and adhesive
- Indoor panels: MDF or plywood sealed with dilute PVA, tesserae fixed with PVA — only
  where it stays dry.
- Wet areas and outdoors: **cement board** (or a masonry wall/rendered concrete) with a
  **polymer-modified cementitious tile adhesive (thinset)**. Never PVA or wood outdoors.
- Frost: outdoor work needs frost-proof tesserae (vitreous glass, porcelain, most smalti and
  dense stone) and full adhesive coverage under every piece so water can't collect and freeze.

## 5. Grouting
- Grout or not: smalti and textured stone work is often left ungrouted (or set in tinted
  mortar) for its reflections; glass and tile functional pieces are grouted.
- **Sanded** grout for gaps over ~3 mm, unsanded for fine gaps. Colour matters hugely — mid-grey
  is safest; white grout fragments a design, black outlines everything. Test on a sample board.
1. Wait for adhesive to cure (per datasheet, often 24 h).
2. Mix grout to a toothpaste consistency, work it in diagonally with a rubber float.
3. Wipe off excess with a barely damp sponge after initial set; buff the haze with dry cloth.
4. Seal grout (and porous stone) for wet areas.

## Verify
- [ ] Outlines read clearly; background rows follow andamento.
- [ ] Gaps consistent; no sharp edges standing proud (run a palm over it carefully).
- [ ] Substrate and adhesive suit the location (indoor dry / wet / outdoor frost).
- [ ] Grout fully packed with no pinholes; haze removed.
