# Stone, shell and wood inlay, and pietra dura

**Goal:** inlaid pieces that sit flush with no visible gap, bonded permanently — whether
shell markers in a fretboard, a wood motif in a tabletop, or a stone picture in marble.

## 1. The core principle: cut the piece first, then the hole to fit it
For recessed inlay (one material sunk into another): **make the inlay piece first**, then
use the piece itself to **scribe** its outline onto the ground, and cut the recess to
that scribed line. A recess cut from the drawing will never match a hand-cut piece.

## 2. Cutting inlay pieces
- **Shell** (mother-of-pearl, abalone, often sold as blanks ~1.5 mm thick): glue the
  pattern on, cut with a **jeweller's piercing saw** on a bench pin/V-board, blade teeth
  pointing **down** toward the handle, cutting on the downstroke. 2/0–4/0 blades are
  common for shell. File and sand edges square.
- Shell dust is harmful (respiratory irritant/sensitiser): wet-cut where possible,
  extraction, and a respirator.
- **Wood**: veneer or solid stock sawn or knifed to shape; for stringing and banding, use
  commercial or self-made strips.
- **Stone**: see pietra dura below.

## 3. Cutting the recess (wood ground)
1. Position the piece; hold it down firmly (a dab of CA glue or double-sided tape) and
   **scribe** round it with a sharp knife or scriber. Remove the piece; enhance the line
   with chalk or pencil rubbed in.
2. Rout the waste with a small router (Dremel-type base or trim router, 1–2 mm bit) to a
   depth just **less** than the inlay thickness, staying inside the line.
3. Pare to the line with fine chisels/knives, leaving the line itself; test-fit often.
- **Router inlay kits** (a guide bushing + removable collar) cut a matching recess and
  piece from a template — fast for repeat shapes in wood.

## 4. Gluing and levelling
- **Shell / stone in wood**: epoxy, often tinted black or with matching dust to hide tiny
  gaps. **CA glue** for small pieces. **Hide glue / PVA** for wood-in-wood.
- Press the piece in; clamp with a caul if needed. Clean squeeze-out before it cures.
- After cure, **level** with a scraper or sanding block until flush. Shell sands fast — sand
  across the ground carefully so you don't dish soft wood around hard shell.
- Finish as the ground requires (lacquer, oil, French polish).

## 5. Pietra dura (commesso)
Florentine technique of fitting shaped hardstones edge to edge to form an image, usually
in a marble ground (Indian **parchin kari**, as on the Taj Mahal, sets them into recesses in marble).
1. Choose stones for natural colour and pattern — veins become petals, shading, sky.
2. Slice to ~3–5 mm and glue a paper pattern piece on each.
3. Cut traditionally with a **bow saw strung with wire** fed with abrasive slurry (emery or
   carborundum); today often diamond wire or lapidary saws.
4. Shape the edges to an exact fit with abrasive files, with a slight **under-bevel** so the
   faces meet tightly while the backs leave room for adhesive.
5. Assemble face-down; bond with mastic (traditionally rosin/beeswax) or epoxy, back with slate
   or stone, then grind and polish the face flat.

## Gotchas
- Inlay thinner than its recess is a ruined job; recess slightly shallow and level down.
- Gaps grow with hesitant knife work: one firm scribe beats many shallow ones.
- Abalone is brittle and curved; flat-ground blanks help.
- Blade sizes and thicknesses are common practice, not specification.

## Verify
- [ ] Every piece was cut before its recess, and the recess scribed from it.
- [ ] Test-fit dry: tight fit with no light showing at the edge.
- [ ] After levelling, a fingertip can't feel the joint.
- [ ] Dust from shell/stone was controlled (wet, extracted, respirator).
