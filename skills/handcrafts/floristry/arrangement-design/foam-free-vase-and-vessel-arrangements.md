# Foam-free vase and vessel arrangements

**Goal:** a balanced arrangement in a vase, compote or bowl, held by reusable mechanics
instead of floral foam (a microplastic-shedding single-use product many florists and
events now avoid), with stems in water so it lasts.

## 1. Choose the mechanics for the vessel
- **Tape grid** (clear waterproof florist tape) across the mouth of a vase, squares
  2–3 cm. Fast, invisible on clear glass if you wrap a tape "rim" round the edge to anchor
  the ends. Best for vases where stems go straight down.
- **Chicken-wire pillow**: a piece of 2.5–5 cm galvanised or coated chicken wire scrunched
  into a loose ball that fills the vessel, standing a little proud of the rim. Anchor with
  waterproof tape or paddle wire across the top and down the sides (or a bit of string
  through drainage holes). Gives multiple layers of support so stems can angle out. The
  workhorse for compotes and low bowls.
- **Pin frog / kenzan**: heavy brass pins in a lead or steel base, fixed with florist
  putty to a *dry* vessel. Push soft stems straight on; slit woody stems first. Lets
  stems lean steeply — ideal for low, airy designs.
- Combine: a kenzan under a chicken-wire cap is a strong rig for heavy branches.
Fill with water + flower food **after** mechanics are in. Never use bare uncoated wire in
a precious vessel — it scratches and can rust-stain.

## 2. Proportion
- Classic rule: the arrangement is **1.5 to 2 times the vessel height** (or width, for a
  low bowl). Less looks stubby; more looks top-heavy and tips.
- Asymmetry reads modern and natural; aim for roughly a 2:1 visual weight split from one
  side to the other rather than a perfect dome.

## 3. Build order
1. **Structure / foliage first**: establish the outline — height, width and the main
   lines — with branches or sturdy foliage. Cover mechanics as you go.
2. **Focal flowers**: place the biggest, most important heads low and near the centre of
   gravity, recessed at different depths. Group in odd numbers.
3. **Secondary flowers** to bridge focal to edge, following the lines.
4. **Fillers and texture** to soften gaps — but don't fill every gap; negative space gives depth.
5. **Movement** last: trailing stems, grasses, airy pieces breaking the outline.
Turn the vessel frequently, or work on a turntable. Check from the viewer's eye level.

## 4. Colour
- Pick a palette before buying: monochrome (tints and shades of one hue), analogous
  (neighbours on the wheel), or complementary with one dominant colour.
- Use "bridge" colours (blush, peach, dusty mauve) to link strong hues.
- Dark flowers recede; light flowers advance — use them to create depth.
- Green is a colour: the foliage choice shifts the whole palette (blue eucalyptus vs bright pittosporum).

## Gotchas
- Chicken wire that doesn't stand above the rim gives no support for low, outward stems.
- Stems pushed through wire must reach the water — check level daily; wide bowls evaporate fast.
- Pin frogs fail in wet vessels: putty won't stick to wet surfaces.
- Heavy branches on one side tip a compote; counterweight or add a kenzan.
- "1.5–2×" is a guideline, not law; designs for a long table or a reception desk follow sightlines first.

## Verify
- [ ] No mechanics visible from the normal viewing angle.
- [ ] Every stem end sits in water; water level checked after building.
- [ ] Height is about 1.5–2× the vessel, or deliberately otherwise.
- [ ] Focal flowers sit low and at varied depths, not in a single plane.
- [ ] The arrangement doesn't wobble or tip when the table is nudged.
