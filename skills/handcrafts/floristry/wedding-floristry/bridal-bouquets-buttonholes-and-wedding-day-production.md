# Bridal bouquets, buttonholes and wedding-day production

**Goal:** deliver every piece on the order, fresh and correct, on a fixed date you can't
move — profitably. Wedding work is mostly production planning; the design is the easy part.

## 1. Consultation and quote
- Get: date, venues and access times, colour palette, dress style and silhouette,
  number of each item, budget, any allergies (lilies, scented flowers), and Pinterest images.
- Translate images to **recipes**: per item, list stems by variety and count. Price from
  stem cost × your markup + labour + sundries + delivery/setup. Reuse a standard
  recipe library (e.g. "bridesmaid, medium").
- Quote seasonal alternatives in writing: "peony, or garden rose if peony is out of season".
  Flower substitutions should be in the contract with a like-for-like clause.
- Take a deposit and set a final-numbers date (often 2–4 weeks out).

## 2. Ordering
- Total all recipes into one stem order by variety, plus **10–15% spare** for breakage
  and bad heads. Order to arrive with enough time to open blooms: roses and peonies often
  need 2–3 days to open; buy closed, open in warm water if needed.
- Confirm the order with the wholesaler in writing a week before.

## 3. Bridal and bridesmaid bouquets
- Most are spiral hand-ties (see handcrafts.floristry.hand-tied-bouquets.spiral-hand-tied-bouquet)
  built on the day before; cascades and shaped bouquets may use wired elements.
- Size to the person and dress: a petite bride can be lost behind a huge bouquet.
- **Handle**: trim stems, bind tightly with stem tape, then wrap in ribbon (spiral from the
  bottom up, tuck and secure with pearl-headed pins pushed **upward** into the binding, not
  through the stems where they'd stick out). Leave cut ends exposed so they can sit in water.
- Store overnight in a cool place in shallow water; dry the handle before handing over.

## 4. Buttonholes and corsages (wired work)
1. Choose robust heads: spray rose, small rose, ranunculus, wax flower, thistle, plus a
   foliage backing.
2. **Wire** each element: cut stems short (~2 cm), push a fine wire (e.g. 0.46–0.56 mm,
   depending on weight) through the base of the head or calyx, or "hook" it down through
   the centre, then bend both legs down to form a new stem.
3. **Tape** with stretched stem tape (stretch it to activate the tack), from the head down,
   covering the wire. Moisture-holding cotton under the tape is optional for long days.
4. Assemble the elements, bind with tape, trim to length, and finish with a pin or magnet.
   Magnets avoid pinholes in suits but are unsuitable near pacemakers — ask.
5. Mist lightly and store in a sealed box in the cooler; they're made the day before.

## 5. Day-of timeline
- Write a run sheet: build times, loading order (last on, first off), delivery addresses
  and times, contact numbers, a checklist per venue.
- Pack bouquets in buckets wedged so they can't tip; buttonholes in labelled boxes.
- Deliver personal flowers 1–2 hours before photos, not at the last minute.
- Bring an emergency kit: spare stems, tape, wire, pins, ribbon, scissors, water spray.

## Gotchas
- Lilies' pollen ruins dresses: remove anthers on every lily.
- Some flowers wilt out of water (hydrangea, sweet pea) — avoid them in buttonholes or
  bouquets for a hot outdoor day.
- Wire gauges above are approximate conventions; use the lightest wire that holds the head.
- Count twice: the most common failure is a missing buttonhole for a late-added usher.

## Verify
- [ ] Every item on the contract is ticked off against the run sheet before loading.
- [ ] Bouquet handles are dry, pins point inward/upward, nothing sharp exposed.
- [ ] Each buttonhole is labelled with recipient or role.
- [ ] Lily anthers removed; scented/allergen flowers match the brief.
- [ ] Spare stems and emergency kit are in the vehicle.
