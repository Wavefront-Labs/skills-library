# Spiral hand-tied bouquet

**Goal:** a bouquet where every stem runs the same direction, held at one tight binding
point, so it can be dropped straight into a vase, stands up on its own when presented,
and every stem reaches water.

## 1. Plan the recipe first
Price and design together. Write a recipe before touching stock, for example a mid-size
bouquet:
- 3–5 **focal** stems (roses, peonies, lisianthus)
- 5–7 **secondary** (spray roses, alstroemeria, stocks)
- 3–5 **fillers/textures** (wax flower, limonium, seeded eucalyptus)
- 5–8 **foliage** stems (eucalyptus, pittosporum, ruscus)
Odd numbers of focal stems read more natural. Lay the stems out on the bench by type,
**fully stripped below the binding point** — any leaf left there gets twisted into the
tie, rots, and takes up space. Strip about two-thirds of the stem length.

## 2. The spiral
1. Hold the first stem upright in your non-dominant hand, loosely, at the binding point —
   roughly one-third down from the heads, or just under where the widest heads will sit.
   The higher the binding point, the tighter and more domed the bouquet; lower = looser and wider.
2. Add each new stem **at the same angle, crossing in front of the last** (heads to the
   left, stem ends to the right for a right-hander — or the mirror). Every stem goes on
   the same way.
3. After every 2–3 stems, **turn the whole bunch a quarter turn** in the same direction,
   using your free hand to roll it. The spiral comes from consistent angle + consistent turn.
4. Distribute: mix focal, secondary and foliage evenly round the spiral; place foliage
   lower to collar the edge. Recess some flowers for depth rather than making a flat dome.
5. Never pull a stem out from below — it drags the spiral. Withdraw from the top.
6. Grip lightly. A crushing grip at the binding point bruises stems and stops you
   sliding stems in.

## 3. Tie and trim
- Tie at the binding point with raffia, string, or paper-covered bind wire: wrap 3–4
  times, pull firm, knot. Do not tie twice at different heights — it breaks the spiral's
  hinge and stems will snap.
- Trim the stems at an angle so the bunch **stands on its own** on the bench. Stem length
  below the tie is typically about the same as the bouquet height above it; shorter for a
  compact posy.
- A stand-up check: if it falls over, stems are uneven or the spiral is lopsided.

## 4. Wrap for gifting
- **Aqua pack:** place the stems in a cellophane bag or cut cello square, add water to
  just above the cut ends, gather and tie at the binding point. Keeps it hydrated for
  transport. Tell the customer to cut the tie and pop the bubble over the sink.
- Outer wrap: two or three sheets of kraft or tissue folded into a cone, layered at
  offsets; tie over the same binding point with ribbon.
- Add a care card: recut stems, fresh water + food every 2–3 days, cool spot, away from fruit.

## Gotchas
- Mixed stem thicknesses: put thick stems (sunflowers, hydrangea) in early and in the
  centre or the spiral gets crowded off one side.
- Weak-necked flowers (gerbera, ranunculus) snap at the tie if placed late under tension;
  support them between stiffer stems.
- Large bouquets get heavy: consider tying an interim string and resting on the bench.
  A grid of tape across a hand-tie vase is another option for very large designs.
- Stem counts above are a starting point; local market sizes and pricing vary widely.

## Verify
- [ ] Every stem runs in the same direction when viewed from below the tie.
- [ ] The bouquet stands upright on the bench unaided.
- [ ] No foliage at or below the binding point.
- [ ] One tie, at one point, firm enough that stems don't slide.
- [ ] Water reaches every stem end in the aqua pack; care card included.
