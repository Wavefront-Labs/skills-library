# Mossed wreaths and based tributes

**Goal:** a firm, even, long-lasting base (a wreath ring or a tribute shape) with clean
edges and densely covered design — made to a fixed funeral time, with tact.

## 1. Mossing a wire frame (the foam-free base)
- Frames: double-wire rings (25–40 cm common) or shaped frames. Use **sphagnum or
  flat/carpet moss**, soaked and squeezed to damp, not dripping.
1. Tie reel wire (0.56–0.71 mm on a reel, or garden twine for a plastic-free version) to
   the frame.
2. Take a fist-sized handful of moss, tease out debris, and press it onto the frame.
3. Wrap the reel wire tightly around moss and frame every 2–3 cm, binding firmly. Add the
   next handful overlapping the first. Keep the depth even — the commonest fault is a
   lumpy ring that won't take flowers evenly.
4. Join neatly, tie off, and tuck the wire end back into the moss. Trim stray strands
   with scissors so it's a tight, even sausage.
5. Optional: wrap the back with florist's plastic or a bin-liner strip pinned in place to
   stop moisture marking a coffin or door. (Many now skip plastic; mossing a bit drier helps.)

## 2. Edging
- **Leaf edging**: overlapping leaves (laurel, salal, galax, ivy), each pinned with a
  mossing pin (hairpin of wire), shiny side out, all overlapping the same direction round
  the ring. Then flowers go into the centre band.
- **Ribbon edging**: pleated ribbon pinned round the outer and inner edge for a traditional
  based tribute look.

## 3. Covering and design
- **Massed/based** work: short-cut heads (chrysanthemum blooms, carnations, roses) placed
  close together so no moss shows — the traditional style for letter tributes ("MUM",
  "DAD") and cushions. Count stems per letter before quoting; letters eat flowers.
- **Loose/natural** wreaths: clusters of foliage and flowers wired or pushed in, all
  angled the same direction round the ring.
- Flowers without a water source need a wired stem pushed into damp moss, or short
  stems pushed directly in; choose long-lasting varieties (chrysanthemum, carnation,
  spray rose, eryngium, hypericum, seeded eucalyptus).
- Flowers in moss last a couple of days in cool weather; mist before delivery.

## 4. Seasonal door wreaths
- Same mossed frame, with conifer clusters (Nordmann fir, blue pine, cedar) wired in bunches
  and laid overlapping round the ring, then dried oranges, cones, cinnamon, ribbon.
- Conifers chosen for needle retention last weeks outdoors in cold weather; indoors near a
  radiator they dry fast.
- Hang with a wire loop wrapped round the frame at the top.

## 5. Funeral orders: the practicalities
- Confirm: funeral director, date, delivery time and location, the deceased's name for the
  card, the sender's message, and any family wishes (e.g. "family flowers only").
- Deliver to the **funeral director**, usually the day before or the morning of; ask their cut-off.
- Card wording is the customer's; check spelling of names twice and read it back.
- Be calm and unhurried with grieving customers. Offer a few clear options and a price range.

## Gotchas
- Wet moss on a polished coffin marks it — back the tribute or moss drier.
- Pins pushed at an angle pop out in transport; push straight and fully.
- Heavy letter tributes need a rigid frame; check the funeral director can carry it.
- Wire gauges here are typical UK trade sizes; regional suppliers vary.

## Verify
- [ ] Moss is evenly thick all round; no thin spots or lumps.
- [ ] No moss or wire visible on the front once finished.
- [ ] Back is protected or dry enough not to mark.
- [ ] Card wording and name spelling checked against the order.
- [ ] Delivery time and place confirmed with the funeral director.
