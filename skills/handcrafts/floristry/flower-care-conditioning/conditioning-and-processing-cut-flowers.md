# Conditioning and processing cut flowers

**Goal:** get every stem from the box to the cooler fully hydrated and clean within an
hour of arrival, so it lasts its full vase life in the customer's hands. Most
"bad flowers" are bacteria, air in the stem or ethylene, not the grower.

## 1. Before the flowers arrive
- **Scrub buckets** with detergent then a dilute bleach or a commercial bucket cleaner,
  and rinse. Biofilm you can feel on the inside of a bucket is the bacteria that will
  block the stems. Clean buckets are the single biggest vase-life gain.
- Fill to the right depth: most stems in 10–15 cm of water; soft-stemmed bulb flowers
  (tulip, hyacinth, anemone) **shallow** — a few cm — because the stems rot when deep.
- Add **flower food** at the label dose. Under-dosing is worse than none: weak sugar
  solution feeds bacteria without enough biocide/acidifier to control them.
- Knives and secateurs cleaned; a dirty blade inoculates every stem it cuts.

## 2. Processing each stem (unbox immediately)
1. Inspect: check for botrytis (grey mould), bruised petals, wet sleeves. Photograph
   problems for the wholesaler claim before you process.
2. **Strip all foliage** that would sit below the waterline. Leaves in water rot fast.
   Rose thorns: strip gently with a stripper or gloved hand — gouging the bark opens wounds.
3. **Cut 2–5 cm off** at a sharp angle with a knife or clean bypass secateurs (not
   anvil, which crushes). Recut because the end has dried and sealed with air/debris in transit.
4. Straight into the prepared bucket — seconds, not minutes, for woody/air-prone
   stems like roses and hydrangea.
5. Leave at room temperature 1–2 hours to drink, then into the **cooler at about
   2–5 °C** with high humidity. Tropicals (anthurium, orchids, heliconia, ginger) are
   chill-sensitive: keep them out of the cooler, roughly 12–15 °C.

## 3. Problem stems and their fixes
- **Narcissus/daffodils** exude a sap that shortens the life of other flowers. Condition
  them alone for several hours (do not recut afterwards — that restarts the sap), then
  they can be combined. Or use a bulb-flower food made for mixing.
- **Tulips** keep growing and bend toward light after cutting. Condition wrapped tightly
  in paper, upright, in shallow cold water to straighten them; a pin through the stem
  under the head is folklore with mixed evidence.
- **Hydrangea** drinks through its heads as well as its stem. If it wilts, recut and
  fully submerge the head in cool water for 20–60 minutes. Some florists dip stem ends in
  alum; it's widely used but the benefit is debated.
- **Rose "bent neck"**: an air lock below the head. Recut under water, wrap the stem and head
  in paper to support it upright, and stand in deep warm water (~40 °C) for an hour.
- **Woody/sappy stems** (euphorbia, poppies): sear the cut end briefly in boiling water or
  a flame to stop sap loss; protect the heads from steam. Euphorbia sap irritates skin — gloves.
- **Lilies**: remove anthers as they open (pinch out with tissue) — pollen stains fabric
  and skin permanently; never rub a pollen stain, lift it with tape.

## 4. Ethylene and the cooler
Ethylene gas ages sensitive flowers fast (carnations, alstroemeria, snapdragons, many
orchids). Keep flowers away from **ripening fruit**, exhaust fumes and decaying plant
material; clear spent stock out of the cooler daily. Many growers pre-treat sensitive
crops with an ethylene inhibitor (e.g. 1-MCP or STS) — ask the supplier, you can't see it.

## 5. Rotation and records
Label buckets with arrival date and use **first in, first out**. Change water and recut
every 2–3 days for stock held on. Track which suppliers and varieties last; that data
decides purchasing better than any rule here.

## Gotchas
- Warm, humid stock straight into a cold cooler can sweat inside sleeves and invite
  botrytis — remove sleeves or let it breathe.
- Hammering woody stems is outdated: it shreds the tissue and feeds bacteria. Cut cleanly
  and, if needed, slit the base.
- Temperatures above are typical trade guidance; check species-specific needs from your
  wholesaler (uncertain for some novelty crops).

## Verify
- [ ] Buckets are visibly clean and dosed with flower food at label strength.
- [ ] No leaves below the waterline in any bucket.
- [ ] Every stem has a fresh cut made within seconds of going into water.
- [ ] Tropicals are out of the cooler; narcissus conditioned separately.
- [ ] No fruit or rotting stock in the cooler; buckets are date-labelled.
