# Coils, shapes and composing quilled designs

**Goal:** consistent coils and shapes that look identical across a design, assembled
neatly onto a base or into on-edge lettering, with no visible glue.

## 1. Materials
- **Strips**: 3 mm (1/8") is standard; 1.5 mm for fine work; 5–10 mm for fringed flowers
  and bold lettering. Typical weight around 120 gsm. Pre-cut strips are far more even than
  hand-cut.
- **Slotted tool**: the strip end slides into a slot; easy, but leaves a small crimp at the
  centre. **Needle tool** (or a fine needle/cocktail stick): no crimp, smaller centre, harder to learn.
- **Circle-sizer board / quilling board**: holes of set diameters so loose coils are uniform.
- **Glue**: a fine-tip bottle of PVA/tacky glue; apply with a needle or toothpick. Less is more.
- Tweezers (fine, straight or angled), a cork board and pins for assembly.

## 2. Basic coils
1. **Tight coil**: roll the strip all the way down without letting go; glue the end. Used
   for dots, centres, bases for 3D shapes.
2. **Loose coil**: roll fully, then release it into a circle-sizer hole (or let it relax
   freely), let it unwind evenly, then glue only the tail end to the coil.
   Same strip length + same hole = identical coils; that consistency is the craft.
3. Tear (don't cut) strip ends before gluing for a less visible join.

## 3. Shapes from a loose coil (pinch after gluing the tail)
- **Teardrop**: pinch one point. **Marquise/eye**: pinch two opposite points.
- **Square/diamond**: pinch two, rotate 90°, pinch two more. **Triangle**: three pinches.
- **Crescent**, **heart**, **bunny ear**: pinch and press against a curved form.
- Centre position: before pinching, move the coil's centre toward one side with a needle
  to make offset ("off-centre") shapes, often used for petals.

## 4. Scrolls and other elements
- **Open scroll**: roll only one end, leave the tail loose. **S-scroll**: roll both ends in
  opposite directions. **C-scroll / V-scroll / heart scroll**: fold the strip, roll both
  ends in or out.
- **Fringed flowers**: cut fine fringes along a wide strip (or use a fringer), roll it tight,
  glue, then fluff the fringe outward. Glue a narrower contrasting strip first for a centre.
- **Husking**: wrapping a strip around pins set in a pattern on a board, for precise
  geometric loops.

## 5. Composing a design
1. Draw or print the design on the base card; lay a sheet of baking paper over to work on
   if building elements first.
2. Make all repeated elements in batches with the same strip length and board size.
3. Dot glue on the **bottom edge** of each element only, then set it on the base. Hold
   for a few seconds.
4. **On-edge lettering/outlines**: stand a strip on its edge along a pencil line, gluing
   the edge, bending around curves with fingers and creasing at corners; then fill the
   letter with coils.
5. Work outlines first, then fill from the edges inward.
6. Mount in a shadow-box frame so glass doesn't crush the work.

## Gotchas
- Too much glue leaves shiny smears that show on coloured paper.
- Humidity relaxes loose coils; finish and glue promptly, and store finished work dry.
- Inconsistent strip lengths are why "identical" petals look different — measure or use a jig.
- Strip weights and widths above are common; brands vary.

## Verify
- [ ] Repeated shapes are the same size (check by stacking).
- [ ] No visible glue shine or strings.
- [ ] Every element is fixed to the base — a gentle tilt dislodges nothing.
- [ ] On-edge outlines stand vertically and follow the drawn line.
