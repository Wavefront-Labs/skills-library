# Reading diagrams, crease patterns and wet folding

**Goal:** fold models accurately from published diagrams, progress to collapsing from a
crease pattern (CP), and shape finished models with wet folding.

## 1. Diagram language (Yoshizawa–Randlett system)
- **Valley fold**: dashed line; fold toward you (paper forms a V). Arrow with a plain head.
- **Mountain fold**: dot-dash line; fold away from you. Arrow with a half head.
- **Fold and unfold**: arrow that returns — makes a crease reference.
- **Turn over**: looped arrow. **Rotate**: circular arrows with angle.
- **Push/sink/inside reverse**: hollow, "push here" arrow.
- **Repeat**: arrow with tick marks (one tick per repeat) or "repeat behind".
- The *next* diagram shows the result — always compare your model to it before moving on.
Diagrams usually show the coloured side shaded; check whether you start coloured-side up.

## 2. Core folds and bases
- **Inside reverse fold** (neck/head of a crane): pre-crease, then push the point inside
  between its layers. **Outside reverse**: wrap it around the outside.
- **Squash fold**: lift a flap, open its layers and flatten symmetrically.
- **Petal fold**: from a preliminary base, fold edges to the centre, then lift the bottom
  point up, collapsing along those creases (heart of the bird base).
- **Crimp** and **pleat**: paired reverse/valley folds to change angle.
- **Sink fold** (open or closed): push a point inside itself along a pre-creased line —
  the classic stumbling block; pre-crease sharply and open the model to do it.
- **Bases**: preliminary (square) base, waterbomb base, kite, fish, bird and frog bases.
  Most traditional models start from one; learn them until automatic.

## 3. Precision habits
- Align corners and edges **exactly** before creasing; then crease from the centre
  outward with a fingernail or bone folder. Errors compound: 1 mm early can be 5 mm later.
- Pre-crease reference lines when a diagram says "fold and unfold" — they're not optional.
- If stuck, look three steps ahead; the target shape often explains the move.

## 4. Crease patterns
- A CP is the unfolded paper's crease map: usually mountain = solid/red, valley = dashed/blue
  (conventions vary per designer — check the legend).
- Method: fold the grid first (e.g. a 16 or 32 grid for **box-pleated** designs, or the
  22.5° references for many bird-base-style designs), then precrease every line with
  the right parity, then **collapse** gradually, working from the centre out and
  bringing flaps together, rather than trying to collapse all at once.
- Thin, strong paper (tissue-foil, thin kami, double-tissue) makes complex CPs possible.

## 5. Paper choice
- **Kami**: cheap, crisp, practice paper. Fine for most intermediate models.
- **Foil-backed**: holds shape, but creases scar and won't unfold cleanly.
- **Washi / mulberry**: strong, soft, good for wet folding and organic models.
- **Tissue-foil / double tissue** (tissue laminated to foil or to itself): for very complex models.
- Size: bigger paper for complex models — a 15 cm square is too small for many super-complex designs.

## 6. Wet folding
1. Use thick, sized, unbleached paper (e.g. watercolour-like papers like Elephant Hide,
   or paper you've sized with **methyl cellulose**).
2. Dampen lightly and evenly with a cloth or sponge — damp, not wet; paper should feel cool.
3. Fold and shape with soft curves; clip or hold until dry. Too wet = paper tears or collapses.
4. Re-dampen locally to adjust. As it dries, the size sets the shape permanently.
Pioneered by Akira Yoshizawa; essential for sculptural animal and mask work.

## Verify
- [ ] Each step's result matches the next diagram before continuing.
- [ ] Reference creases are sharp and on the marked lines.
- [ ] CP collapses without tearing at the centre (precreases were complete).
- [ ] Wet-folded model is fully dry and holds its curves when handled.
