# Strip-planking and glassing a cedar canoe

**Goal:** a light, stiff canoe hull made of thin cedar strips glued edge to edge
over moulds, sheathed inside and out with fibreglass cloth in epoxy so it's
transparent, strong and watertight.

## 1. Strongback and moulds
- A straight, rigid **strongback** (box beam), checked level and free of twist with
  a string line or laser.
- **Station moulds** cut from plans (from offsets — see
  `handcrafts.boatbuilding.lofting.lofting-lines-from-a-table-of-offsets` if you
  have to loft them), set up on the strongback at stated spacing, centred and plumb.
- **Tape the mould edges** with packing tape so glue doesn't stick strips to them.

## 2. Strips
- Western red cedar, typically around 1/4 in (6 mm) thick by 3/4 in (19 mm)
  wide, ripped from clear boards. Bead-and-cove edges (rounded on one edge, hollow
  on the other) make the strips nest as they twist round the bilge.
- Scarf or butt-join strips to length; stagger joints.

## 3. Planking
- Start at the **sheer** (first strip along the sheer line) and work toward the keel
  line, both sides at once to keep the hull balanced. Cove edge facing up (so glue
  sits in the cove) is common.
- Glue edges with PVA (wood glue) or thickened epoxy. Hold with staples into the
  moulds, or for a staple-free hull, clamps and tape.
- At the stems, strips run past and are trimmed to the stem line. On the bottom, the
  two sides meet on a **keel line**; fit strips there with a careful bevel so the
  bottom closes.

## 4. Fairing
- Remove staples, scrape off glue, then fair the hull with a block plane, then a
  **longboard** (flexible sanding board) diagonally, and finally a random-orbit
  sander. You're removing ridges between strips, not shaping the hull; a typical
  final strip thickness is only slightly less than the original.
- Wipe with a damp cloth to raise the grain and preview the look under epoxy;
  sand again lightly.

## 5. Glass the outside
- Drape **fibreglass cloth** (often ~6 oz/yd², ~200 g/m²) over the hull dry and trim.
- **Wet out** with mixed epoxy: pour and spread with a squeegee, working the cloth
  clear (no white patches) without floating it. Remove excess so the cloth doesn't
  lift.
- **Fill coats:** once the wet-out is tacky (within the epoxy's recoat window), add
  one or two thin fill coats to bury the weave. Follow the epoxy maker's times;
  temperature matters.
- After cure: **amine blush** (a waxy film on many epoxies) must be washed off with
  water and a scrub pad before sanding or varnishing — or it will gum the paper
  and spoil adhesion.

## 6. Inside
Remove from moulds, scrape glue, fair and sand the inside (harder — curved
scrapers), and glass the inside the same way. Then fit gunwales, decks, thwarts,
seats (see `handcrafts.boatbuilding.boat-joinery-fitout.fitting-thwarts-coamings-and-scribed-joinery`).

## 7. UV protection
Epoxy breaks down in sunlight. Finish with several coats of a UV-protective
varnish (marine spar or two-part polyurethane).

## Epoxy safety
Epoxy is a common **sensitiser**: repeated skin contact can cause an allergy that
lasts for life. Nitrile gloves, sleeves, eye protection; don't sand uncured epoxy;
dust mask/respirator when sanding cured epoxy and glass; ventilate.

## Verify
- [ ] Strongback straight and level; moulds plumb, centred and taped.
- [ ] Strips planked from the sheer, both sides; joints staggered.
- [ ] Hull faired smooth without thinning strips excessively.
- [ ] Cloth fully wet out, weave filled, no dry or floated areas.
- [ ] Blush washed off before sanding; UV varnish over the epoxy.
