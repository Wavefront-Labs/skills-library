# Stitching panels and epoxy fillet seams

**Goal:** a plywood hull (dinghy, kayak, pram) where flat panels are stitched into
shape, then joined permanently by epoxy fillets and glass tape — the method most
plans and kit boats use.

## 1. Panels
- **Marine plywood** (e.g. okoume to BS 1088 or similar) — exterior ply has voids
  that become weak spots and rot traps.
- Panels longer than a sheet are joined by **scarf** (an 8:1 bevel glued with
  epoxy is a common ratio), a **butt block**, or kit **puzzle joints**. Join on a
  flat surface over plastic, weight it, let cure fully.
- Mark the stitch-hole positions per plans (often every 100–150 mm) and drill
  small holes a few mm in from the edge.

## 2. Stitching
- Stitch with **copper wire** twists or **cable ties**. Start at the keel/bottom
  seam, then sides, then bulkheads/frames and transom.
- Twist loosely at first. Once all panels are in, **true the hull**: check the
  centreline, measure diagonals and across the sheer at stations, sight for
  twist, and tighten stitches so panels meet edge to edge (or with a small even
  gap). The shape you lock in now is the boat.

## 3. Tack-weld
- Mix epoxy thickened to **peanut-butter** consistency with **wood flour** (and/or
  colloidal silica for strength and less sag). Apply small blobs ("tack welds")
  between stitches on the inside seams.
- After cure, **remove the stitches** — cut ties/untwist wire (warming copper wire
  with a soldering iron loosens it from the epoxy). Some builders leave wires in and
  grind them flush; cleaner to remove.

## 4. Fillets
- Wet the seam area first with unthickened epoxy so the fillet bonds into the wood.
- Pipe or spread thickened epoxy along the inside seam, then shape a **radiused
  fillet** with a rounded stick or plastic spreader (a common radius is ~20 mm on
  small boats; follow plans). Clean the edges with a scraper before it cures.
- A fillet distributes load over the panel; a sharp internal corner concentrates it.

## 5. Glass tape
- While the fillet is still green (tacky but firm — in the recoat window), lay
  **fibreglass tape** (woven or biaxial) over it and wet it out with epoxy. Bonding
  wet-on-tacky avoids sanding between steps.
- Outside: round over the outer seam edges with a plane/sander to a radius (glass
  won't follow a sharp corner), then tape or sheath the outside.

## 6. Finishing
- Wash **amine blush** off cured epoxy with water before sanding.
- Fill weave and low spots, fair, prime and paint or varnish with UV protection.

## Epoxy safety
Gloves (nitrile), eye protection, long sleeves; never sand uncured epoxy; respirator
for sanding dust; mix small batches — a large mass in a cup can exotherm (heat up
fast, smoke, and cure in the cup).

## Verify
- [ ] Marine plywood; scarf/joints fully cured before stitching.
- [ ] Hull trued: diagonals equal, no twist, before any epoxy.
- [ ] Tack welds cured; stitches removed.
- [ ] Fillets smooth and radiused, edges cleaned; tape laid wet-on-tacky.
- [ ] Outside edges radiused before glassing; blush washed off.
