# Lofting lines from a table of offsets

**Goal:** a full-size, **fair** drawing of the hull from which you take moulds,
patterns and bevels. The designer's offsets are measured off a small drawing and
contain errors; lofting finds and fixes them before timber is cut.

## 1. Read the offsets
- The table lists, for each **station** (cross-section), the **heights** (from a
  baseline) of the sheer, rabbet and keel, and the **half-breadths** (from the
  centreline) at each waterline and the sheer. Buttocks and diagonals may be included.
- Traditional format: **feet-inches-eighths**, e.g. `2-5-3` = 2 ft 5 3/8 in; a `+`
  means a sixteenth more. Misreading this format is a classic error.
- Check whether offsets are **to the outside of planking** or the inside (moulded
  lines) — usually stated on the plan. It decides the deductions in step 6.

## 2. Prepare the loft floor
- A flat, painted (light colour) floor of plywood sheets, or for small boats a
  large sheet of board or drafting film. Long enough for the full length plus a bit.
- Snap or draw a **baseline** and a **centreline** (for small craft the
  half-breadth plan can overlay the profile, sharing the baseline as centreline).
- Mark **station lines** square to the baseline at the designer's spacing. Check
  square with a 3-4-5 triangle or a large trammel — not a small set square.
- Draw **waterlines** parallel to the base in profile, and **buttock lines** parallel
  to the centreline in half-breadth.

## 3. Plot and fair: profile and half-breadth
- Mark offsets at each station: sheer height, keel/rabbet in profile; sheer and
  waterline half-breadths in half-breadth view.
- Bend a long, even **batten** through the points, held with weights ("ducks") or
  nails beside it. A fair curve has **no flat spots, bumps or kinks** — sight along
  it low from the end. Allow the batten to miss a point that's clearly wrong; the
  fair line wins over the number.

## 4. Body plan and cross-checking
- Draw the **body plan** (all station sections superimposed about one centreline,
  forward sections on one side, aft on the other).
- Transfer heights and half-breadths from the faired profile/half-breadth (with
  a tick strip, not the original table) and draw each section as a fair curve.
- Cross-check: a point on a waterline in the body plan must be the same distance
  from the centreline as that waterline at that station in half-breadth; the same
  for buttocks in profile. Correct any mismatch in **all views** and re-fair.
  Iterate until every view agrees — that is the lofting.
- **Diagonals** (lines across the body plan at an angle) catch errors at the turn
  of the bilge that waterlines and buttocks miss.

## 5. Ends and details
Loft the stem, transom (expanded to its true shape since it's raked), keel,
rabbet and any deadwood. Note bevels where planks meet them.

## 6. Plank thickness deduction
If lines are to the outside of planking, moulds must be **smaller** by the plank
thickness, measured **perpendicular to the hull surface** at each point, not
horizontally — near the turn of the bilge and at the sheer, the difference matters.
Draw the deduction line inside each section with a compass or a thickness gauge.

## 7. Take off
Lay plywood or paper over sections and transfer with nails pressed through, or
pin-pricks, to make **station moulds**. Mark waterlines and the centreline on every
mould; they set it up level on the strongback.

## Verify
- [ ] Offsets format and "to outside of planking" or not understood.
- [ ] Grid stations square, checked with a large triangle or trammel.
- [ ] All lines faired by batten; no kinks sighting along them.
- [ ] Body plan matches half-breadth and profile at every crossing.
- [ ] Plank deduction made perpendicular to the surface; moulds marked with reference lines.
