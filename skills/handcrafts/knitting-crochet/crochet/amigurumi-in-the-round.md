# Amigurumi in the round

**Goal:** firm, gap-free crocheted toys with smooth shaping, built from spheres and tubes,
assembled neatly and safely.

## Terms first: US vs UK
- US **single crochet (sc)** = UK **double crochet (dc)**. Patterns state which terminology
  they use; check before starting. This skill uses US terms.

## Materials
- Yarn: cotton or acrylic worsted/DK; cotton gives crisp stitch definition.
- Hook: **smaller than the yarn label suggests** (e.g. 2.5–3.5 mm for DK) so the fabric is
  dense and stuffing won't show.
- Stitch marker, polyester fibrefill, tapestry needle, safety eyes (optional).

## Magic ring
1. Wrap yarn around two fingers to form a loop, tail toward you.
2. Insert hook into the loop, pull up a loop, chain 1 (doesn't count as a stitch).
3. Work the stated number of sc (commonly 6) into the ring over both the loop and tail.
4. Pull the tail to close the centre tight. No hole.

## Spiral rounds
- Amigurumi is usually worked in **continuous spirals** — no slip-stitch joins — so there's no
  visible seam. Mark the **first stitch of each round** with a marker and move it up every round.
- Count stitches at the end of each round; the pattern gives a total in brackets, e.g. (18).

## Sphere maths (6-stitch start)
- Round 1: 6 sc in ring (6).
- Round 2: inc in each (12).
- Round 3: (sc, inc) × 6 (18).
- Round 4: (2 sc, inc) × 6 (24) … each round adds 6, with one more plain sc between increases.
- Work even (no increases) for several rounds for the equator.
- Decrease mirrors this: (2 sc, dec) × 6 (18), (sc, dec) × 6 (12), dec × 6 (6).
- **Stagger increases** (e.g. start each round's repeat at a different point) to avoid a
  hexagon with visible corners.

## Invisible decrease
- Insert hook into the **front loop only** of the next stitch, then front loop only of the
  following stitch, yarn over, pull through both loops, yarn over, pull through two. Lies
  flatter than a standard sc2tog.

## Stuffing
- Stuff firmly in small pieces as you close up; stuff before the opening gets too small.
- Shape with fingers as you go; overstuffing stretches stitches and shows stuffing.
- Close the last round: thread the tail through the front loops of remaining stitches and pull
  tight, then weave in.

## Assembly
- Pin pieces on before sewing to check proportions.
- Whipstitch (or mattress-stitch) open-ended parts (arms, ears) through both loops around
  the edge, matching stitch for stitch onto the body.
- Hide yarn tails by passing the needle through the body and trimming flush.

## Safety
- For toys for children under about 3, **embroider** facial features instead of using safety
  eyes or buttons — small parts are choking hazards. Selling toys commercially typically
  requires compliance with toy safety regulations in your market (e.g. CE/UKCA marking or
  CPSIA testing) — check local rules.
- Secure all parts firmly; give a firm tug test.

## Gotchas
- Gaps showing stuffing: hook too big or tension loose. Also try the **yarn under** technique
  (yarn over from the other direction) for tighter X-shaped stitches.
- Lost count in spirals: always move the marker.
- Lean/twist in tubes is normal in spiral crochet; it's accentuated in right- vs left-handed work.

- [ ] Magic ring closed tight; marker moved each round; counts match.
- [ ] Dense fabric with no visible stuffing; invisible decreases used.
- [ ] Parts pinned then sewn securely; safety features suited to the recipient's age.
