# Filet crochet charts and thread work

**Goal:** a square-meshed filet piece whose picture reads clearly, with straight edges,
blocked flat (or stiffened) in fine thread. US terms used; UK equivalents: US dc = UK tr.

## Materials
- **Crochet thread** (mercerised cotton), sized by number: higher number = finer. Size 10 is
  a common starting thread; 20–40 for finer lace.
- **Steel hooks** to suit the thread, often around 1.25–1.5 mm for size 10 (check the thread's
  recommendation and your tension).
- Good light and a magnifier for finer work.

## How filet works
- The fabric is a grid of **mesh units**. Each unit is either:
  - **Space (open)**: dc, ch 2 (the chain-2 crosses an empty square).
  - **Block (filled)**: dc, 2 dc (the two chain stitches replaced by dc worked into the space
    or stitches below).
- Each mesh unit shares its edge dc with the next. A row of n mesh units = **3n + 1** stitches
  (the +1 closes the last unit).

## Reading the chart
- Grid: filled square = block, empty square = space.
- Read row 1 from right to left (right-handed), row 2 left to right, alternating (because the
  work is turned each row). Mark completed rows.

## Foundation chain
- Chain 3 × (number of squares across) + 1 for the base, plus turning chain.
- Common: for a chart W squares wide, chain 3W + 3 (+ 2 more if row 1 begins with a space,
  depending on the pattern's convention). Different patterns count this differently — follow the
  pattern's stated chain, or work a small swatch row to confirm.
- Many crocheters prefer a **foundation double crochet** row instead, which is more elastic
  and avoids miscounting a long chain.

## Working rows
- Start each row with a turning chain:
  - If the first square is a space: ch 5 (counts as dc + ch 2), skip next 2, dc in next dc.
  - If the first square is a block: ch 3 (counts as dc), dc in next 3 stitches.
- Work dc into the dc of the row below for unit edges; over a space below, block = 2 dc into
  the ch-2 space.
- Count units at the end of each row against the chart.

## Shaping edges
- **Increase** a block at the start: chain 5 extra, dc in 4th ch from hook and next ch, dc in
  next dc. At the end: use extended/treble techniques or the pattern's method.
- **Decrease**: slip stitch across the units to remove at the start; leave units unworked at the
  end.

## Lacets and bars (decorative)
- **Lacet**: dc, ch 3, sc into the centre of the unit, ch 3 — spans two units, gives a V.
- **Bar**: dc, ch 5 across two units — often over a lacet.

## Irish crochet and doilies (brief)
- Irish crochet builds motifs (roses, leaves) worked over padding cord and joined with a
  mesh background. Doily patterns are worked in rounds from the centre; tension must be
  consistent or they ruffle (too many stitches) or cup (too few).

## Blocking and starching
- Wash gently, pin to shape on a padded board (rust-proof pins, at each mesh row or point),
  let dry.
- For stiffness (ornaments, doilies that stand): fabric stiffener, laundry starch spray or a
  boiled sugar-water starch. Sugar starch attracts pests and may yellow; commercial stiffeners
  are more stable. Apply wet, shape, pin, dry fully.

## Gotchas
- Blocks look uneven: dc heights inconsistent, often from pulling up loops differently on
  turning chains.
- Picture looks squashed: filet squares aren't truly square with every thread and hook —
  check a swatch; some designers adjust charts for this.
- Hook/thread sizes vary by brand.
