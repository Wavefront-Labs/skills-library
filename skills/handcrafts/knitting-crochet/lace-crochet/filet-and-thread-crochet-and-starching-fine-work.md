# Filet and thread crochet, and starching fine work

**Goal:** fine crochet lace — doilies, edgings, filet panels — with even tension,
correct counts and a crisp, flat finish.

## 1. Thread and hooks
- **Mercerised cotton thread** sized by number: higher = finer. **Size 10** is the
  common starting point; 20–30 for fine doilies; 80+ for very fine work.
- **Steel hooks** use their own numbering (in US sizing, higher number = smaller).
  Rough guide: size 10 thread with a ~1.25–1.5 mm hook; size 20 with ~1.0 mm. Always
  check the pattern and your tension.
- Good light and a dark lap cloth under white thread save eye strain.
- Hold thread with more wraps around the fingers than yarn — it's slippery and fine.

## 2. Filet crochet
Filet is a grid of **blocks** (solid) and **spaces** (open) that makes pictures and
lettering.
- **Space** = dc, ch 2, skip 2, dc (US terms). **Block** = dc in each of the 3 stitches
  (or 2 dc into a space + the next dc).
- The chart is a grid: filled squares = blocks, empty = spaces. Each square shares
  its edge dc with the next.
- Starting chain = (squares × 3) + 1, then turning chain (typically ch 3, counting as
  the first dc; ch 5 for a row starting with a space). Patterns differ — follow theirs.
- Read rows alternately right-to-left and left-to-right; mark the current row on the
  chart.
- Squares should come out **square**: if your rows are too tall, use a smaller hook or
  shorter dc; too short, a looser dc.

## 3. Doilies and edgings
- **Doilies** work in rounds from the centre; stitch counts (and chain-space counts) per
  round must be exact or the doily cups or ruffles.
  - **Cupping** (bowl shape) = too few stitches per round or tension too tight.
  - **Ruffling** = too many stitches or tension too loose.
- **Pineapple** and other motifs: count chain spaces at each round end.
- **Edgings** on fabric: work a foundation row of sc into a hem rolled over or into
  holes punched by a hemstitch/blanket-stitched edge; space stitches evenly; ease
  around corners with extra stitches.
- **Irish crochet**: individual motifs (roses, leaves) worked over padding cord, then
  joined with an irregular mesh background — plan layout on a fabric template.

## 4. Wash, starch, block
1. Wash gently in warm water with mild soap; rinse well (soap residue yellows).
2. Starch to suit: **spray starch** for light body; **dip starch** (diluted liquid starch
   or a commercial fabric stiffener) for firm doilies; **sugar starch** gives very stiff,
   3D work but can attract insects — use for display pieces only (traditional but
   uncertain long-term archival behaviour).
3. Pin on a padded board covered with plastic wrap: centre first, then opposing points
   (north/south, east/west) to keep it symmetrical; rustproof pins every point and
   picot.
4. Draw a circle and radial lines on paper under the plastic for round doilies.
5. Dry completely before unpinning.

## Gotchas
- **Thread tension** changes when you're tired; finish rounds, not mid-round.
- Joining new thread: overlap a few stitches with both ends, or use a weaver's knot;
  never knot and trim visibly.
- Hand oils discolour white thread — wash hands; store work in a clean bag.

## Verify
- [ ] Thread and hook matched; gauge tested on a small square.
- [ ] Filet squares come out square; chart rows tracked.
- [ ] Doily lies flat — no cupping or ruffling.
- [ ] Starched piece pinned symmetrical and dried completely.
