# Lace charts, lifelines and blocking to shape

**Goal:** lace that shows its pattern clearly — every yarnover open, edges straight
or pointed as designed — and a way back when you inevitably miscount.

## 1. How lace works
Each **yarnover (yo)** adds a stitch and a hole; each **decrease** removes one. In most
patterns they pair up so the count stays constant. If your count drifts, a yo or a
decrease went missing. Common pairs:
- **k2tog** leans right; **ssk** (or skp) leans left; **sk2p / s2kpo** central double
  decrease (centred version: slip 2 together knitwise, k1, pass both over).
- **Nupps** (Estonian): (k1, yo) several times into one stitch, then purl them all
  together on the next row. Work them loosely or they're impossible to purl together.

## 2. Reading lace charts
- Each square = one action in the row, not one stitch on the needle: a k2tog
  occupies one square though it consumes two stitches.
- **Grey "no stitch" squares** keep the grid aligned where counts change — skip them.
- Right-side rows read right to left; wrong-side rows left to right (flat knitting).
  Many lace charts show only RS rows: WS rows are "purl all", check the key.
- Outline repeats on the chart in colour; place **stitch markers between repeats**
  on the needle. When a marker has to move because a decrease straddles it, note
  where in the pattern that happens.

## 3. Lifelines
A lifeline is a smooth contrasting thread (dental floss, cotton) run through all
live stitches on a **plain row**, not threaded through markers.
- Add one every 10–20 rows, or at the end of each chart repeat.
- When a mistake can't be fixed by dropping down, pull the needles out, rip back to
  the lifeline, and pick up the stitches it holds.
- Interchangeable circular needles often have a hole in the connector to thread a
  lifeline as you knit.

## 4. Fixing without ripping
- Count stitches **between markers** every RS row; errors stay local to one repeat.
- Missing yo: on the next row, lift the bar between stitches onto the left needle and
  work it.
- Wrong decrease lean: drop the column down to that row and re-work with a crochet hook
  — only on simple patterns; with yarnovers involved, use the lifeline.

## 5. Casting off and edgings
- Lace must stretch hugely when blocked. Bind off **very loosely**: a needle 2–3 sizes
  up or a stretchy bind-off; same for cast-on.
- Knitted-on borders (worked sideways, joining to live edge stitches) give the
  traditional Shetland-style finish.

## 6. Block hard
1. Soak 20+ minutes in lukewarm water with wool wash.
2. Roll in a towel; don't wring.
3. Stretch to size on mats: **blocking wires** through straight edges, pins at each
   point of scalloped edges. Lace often blocks out 20–30% larger than its unblocked
   size (shawl patterns give finished measurements — block to those).
4. Leave pinned until bone dry. Unpin; it relaxes slightly.
Mohair and fuzzy yarns hide the pattern; block gently. Acrylic lace won't hold a
block well (heat-set lightly with steam only if the yarn label allows).

## Gotchas
- Laceweight on slippery needles drops stitches: use pointy, grippy needles.
- **Lost yarnovers** at the start of a row: wrap the yarn over properly before the
  first stitch.
- Knitting lace when tired = miscounts. Stop at the end of a repeat.

## Verify
- [ ] Stitch count correct at each marker every RS row.
- [ ] Lifelines in at regular intervals.
- [ ] Cast-on and bind-off as stretchy as the lace.
- [ ] Blocked to the pattern's finished measurements; all points pinned and even.
- [ ] Holes open and the motif readable from a distance.
