# Reading lace charts and blocking lace

**Goal:** lace knit accurately from a chart, with mistakes caught within a repeat, then
blocked so the pattern opens out fully.

## How lace works
- A **yarnover (yo)** makes a hole and adds a stitch; a **decrease** removes one. In most
  lace each yo is paired with a decrease so the count stays constant.
- Decrease direction shapes the lines: **k2tog** leans right, **ssk** (or skp) leans left,
  **sk2p / s2kp** (centred double decrease) removes two.

## Reading the chart
- Each square is a stitch action. Read right-side rows right to left, wrong-side rows left
  to right (for flat knitting) — unless the chart shows only right-side rows (common for lace
  with plain purl return rows). Read the legend: symbols vary between designers.
- **Grey / black "no stitch" squares** are placeholders where stitches don't exist yet or any
  more; skip them entirely.
- **Repeat boxes** (outlined in red or bold): work the boxed section as many times as stated.

## Staying on track
- Put a **stitch marker between every repeat**. If a repeat doesn't end exactly at a marker,
  the mistake is in that repeat — you only have to check a few stitches.
- **Count** stitches after each patterned row in long or complex sections.
- **Read your knitting**: yarnovers stack in diagonal lines; if a hole appears in the wrong
  place, the stitches before it are off.
- Use a magnetic board or highlighter tape to mark the current chart row.
- **Lifelines** every 10–20 rows (see hand-knitting skill) — lace is hard to rip back without one.

## Common errors and fixes
- **Missed yarnover**: in the next row, lift the strand between stitches onto the left needle
  and work it as the yo.
- **Extra stitch at a repeat end**: likely a forgotten decrease; tink back to the error.
- **Yarnover slipped off the needle**: on the following row it looks like a dropped strand;
  lift it back on.
- **Nupps** (Estonian bobble clusters): work loosely — they need room to purl together on the
  next row; a crochet hook helps.

## Yarn and needles
- Lace-weight yarns, often on needles larger than the yarn's "standard" size, to open the fabric.
- Sharp-tipped needles make decreases and nupps easier.
- Wool and wool blends block well and hold the opened shape; cotton and acrylic block less
  permanently (acrylic can be "killed" with steam, but irreversibly).

## Blocking lace
1. Soak in lukewarm water with a little wool wash for 20–30 minutes until fully wet.
2. Squeeze out water; roll in a towel and press. Don't wring.
3. Lay on blocking mats (foam tiles) or a bed with a towel.
4. Thread **blocking wires** through straight edges (through every edge stitch or the
   points of a border). Pin the wires out, stretching to the dimensions in the pattern.
5. Pin points and scallops of borders individually with rust-proof **T-pins**, working
   symmetrically: centre, opposite sides, then fill in.
6. Leave until **completely dry** (hours to a day). Unpin — lace "relaxes" slightly; blocked
   firmly enough, it'll hold the open shape.
- Re-block after washing.

## Gotchas
- Different charts show symbols for different conventions; always read the legend.
- Blocking doesn't fix mis-knit motifs; it reveals them.
- Measurements for blocking come from the pattern; overstretching distorts.

## Verify
- [ ] Legend read; no-stitch squares understood; markers between repeats.
- [ ] Stitch count checked each patterned row; lifelines in place.
- [ ] Wet blocked with wires and T-pins to pattern dimensions.
- [ ] Dried completely before unpinning; pattern opened and even.
