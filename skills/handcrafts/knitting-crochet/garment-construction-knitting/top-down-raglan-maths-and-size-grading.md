# Top-down raglan maths and size grading

**Goal:** a sweater that fits, built from your own numbers — and a written pattern
others can follow in their size.

## 1. Inputs
- **Gauge** from a washed swatch in the round: stitches (S) and rows (R) per cm.
- **Measurements:** full bust/chest, upper arm, wrist, back-neck width, yoke depth
  (shoulder-top to underarm), body and sleeve length.
- **Ease** (finished minus body): close fit 0–5 cm, classic 5–10 cm, oversized
  15 cm+. Negative ease for stretchy fitted pieces.
Draw a **schematic** with every finished measurement before calculating.

## 2. Raglan numbers (worked example, illustrative)
Gauge 2.2 st/cm, 3.0 rows/cm; finished chest 100 cm; upper arm 32 cm; neck
circumference 48 cm; yoke depth 22 cm.
1. **Body stitches** at chest: 100 × 2.2 = **220** (front 110 + back 110).
2. **Sleeve stitches** at upper arm: 32 × 2.2 ≈ **70**.
3. **Underarm cast-on:** usually 4–8% of the chest each side; say **8** stitches per
   underarm, shared by body and sleeve. So the yoke must deliver: front and back
   (220 − 2×8) ÷ 2 = **102** each; sleeves 70 − 8 = **62** each.
4. **Neck cast-on:** 48 × 2.2 ≈ 106 stitches: front 34, back 34, sleeves 17 each, plus
   **4 raglan "seam" stitches** (one per line) = 106.
5. **Increases needed:** front 34 → 102 = 68 (one each side per increase round =
   **34 rounds**); sleeves 17 → 62 = 45 (~**23 rounds**). Raglan increases add
   1 st each side of each line = +8 per round.
6. **Rows available:** 22 × 3.0 = 66 rounds. Front needs 34 increase rounds, sleeves
   only ~23: the sleeves finish early → stop sleeve increases and keep body increasing,
   or add **body-only increase rounds**, or increase every round for part of the yoke.
   That mismatch is the core raglan problem; a wider neck or deeper yoke resolves it.
7. **Back neck:** add 3–4 cm of **short rows** across the back to raise it; otherwise the
   front neck rides up.

Numbers above are a teaching example — rerun them with your own gauge.

## 3. Sleeves and body
- Separate: put sleeve stitches on hold, cast on underarm stitches, join the body.
- **Sleeve taper** from upper arm to wrist: (upper − wrist stitches) ÷ 2 = decrease
  rounds; spread over the sleeve length in rows, leaving 5 cm straight before cuff.
  Example: 70 → 46 = 12 decrease rounds over ~120 rounds → every 10th round.
- Body: add waist shaping with darts at the side "seams" if wanted.

## 4. Grading across sizes
- Base on a published size chart (standard body measurements); don't grade by
  multiplying.
- Make a spreadsheet: one column per size, every derived number a formula of gauge,
  measurement and ease. Check that each size has an achievable increase rate.
- Round to stitch multiples the pattern stitch needs (rib multiples, colour repeats).

## 5. Writing it up
- Give finished measurements and ease, gauge, yarn quantity per size, and a schematic.
- Numbers for all sizes in parentheses: `Cast on 96 (104, 112) sts.`
- Get a **test knit** and a **tech edit**; errors in maths are the most common complaint.

## Gotchas
- Raglan depth too shallow = tight underarms; too deep = baggy armpits. Measure a
  well-fitting sweater.
- Row gauge errors break yoke depth; check rows on the swatch too.
- Heavy yarns grow in length when worn; account for drop.

## Verify
- [ ] Schematic drawn; ease chosen deliberately.
- [ ] Yoke stitch counts reconcile with body + sleeve + underarm numbers.
- [ ] Increase rate achievable within yoke depth for every size.
- [ ] Try on at the split (put stitches on waste yarn) before committing.
