# Top-down raglan sweater maths

**Goal:** a seamless top-down raglan that fits, designed from your own gauge and measurements,
with the maths written so it could become a pattern. All numbers below are a worked example,
not standards — use your measurements.

## Inputs
- **Gauge** after blocking, e.g. 20 sts and 28 rows per 10 cm (2 sts/cm, 2.8 rows/cm).
- **Measurements**: chest circumference, upper arm, neck circumference (or the neck opening
  you want), wrist, yoke depth (shoulder top to underarm), body length, sleeve length.
- **Ease**: finished minus body. Example: 10 cm positive ease for a classic fit. Negative
  ease for fitted garments; 15–25 cm for oversized.

## Example targets
- Chest 90 cm + 10 cm ease = 100 cm → 200 sts body.
- Upper arm 30 cm + 5 cm ease = 35 cm → 70 sts per sleeve.
- Neck opening 50 cm → 100 sts cast on.
- Yoke depth target: ~21 cm → ~59 rows.

## Cast-on split
Divide the neck stitches among front, back and two sleeves, plus four raglan "seam" stitches.
A common split: back and front ~1/3 each of the non-raglan stitches, sleeves ~1/6 each.
- 100 − 4 raglan sts = 96. Back 34, front 34, sleeves 14 each.
- Shape the neck front higher-back-lower-front with short rows or by casting on fewer front
  stitches and adding them back — otherwise the neck sits too high at the front.

## Raglan increases
- Standard: 8 increases per increase round (one each side of each of the 4 raglan stitches),
  typically every other round.
- Stitches needed at the underarm, **before underarm cast-on**:
  - Body front/back each needs 100 sts (half of 200). Plan, for example, 6 sts cast on under
    each arm (3 to front, 3 to back) → front/back need 97 sts from the yoke.
  - Front: from 34 to 97 = 63 increases ≈ needs 32 increase rounds (2 per round on front).
  - Sleeves: from 14 to 70 − 6 underarm = 64 → 50 increases… so sleeves and body need
    **different rates**.
- Solving it: increase body and sleeves at different frequencies — e.g. sleeves every other
  round while the body also gets some every-round increases, or add body-only increases
  (front and back only) in some rounds. Lay this out in a spreadsheet: rounds vs stitches
  per section.
- Check: number of rounds available (from yoke depth × row gauge) ≥ the number your increase
  plan needs. If the plan needs more rounds than depth allows, the yoke will be too deep —
  switch to more every-round increases.

## Dividing
- At yoke depth, put sleeve stitches on waste yarn, cast on the underarm stitches, and join
  front and back to work the body in the round.
- Pick up underarm stitches (plus one or two extra at the corners to close gaps) when
  knitting the sleeves.

## Body and sleeves
- Body: knit to length, optionally waist shaping (decreases at side "seams" spaced over rows).
- Sleeves: decrease from upper arm to wrist evenly: (upper arm sts − wrist sts) ÷ 2 =
  number of decrease rounds, spaced evenly over the sleeve rows.

## Grading sizes
- Repeat the calculation for each size range from standard body measurement tables (several
  craft associations publish these). Keep the structure identical so the written pattern is
  one template with a number per size, in brackets.
- Check each size's yoke depth vs increase plan; larger sizes often need body-only increases
  or extra underarm cast-on.

## Write the pattern
- Schematic with finished measurements for each size; gauge; materials; abbreviations.
- Have it test-knit and tech-edited before publishing.

## Gotchas
- Raglan lines that are deep but wide in the body: common fit issue in larger sizes — increase
  the body faster than the sleeves.
- Row gauge matters in a yoke; swatch accurately.

## Verify
- [ ] Gauge, measurements and ease written down.
- [ ] Spreadsheet shows rounds vs stitches for each section reaching underarm targets.
- [ ] Yoke depth achievable with the increase plan.
- [ ] Try-on before dividing (put stitches on a long cable) confirms fit.
