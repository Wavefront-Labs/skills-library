# Domestic knitting machine setup and swatching

**Goal:** a domestic flatbed machine knitting reliable stockinette at a measured gauge,
with the ability to recover dropped stitches. Models differ (Brother/KnitKing, Silver Reed/
Studio, Passap double-bed, etc.); the general workflow is common, details from your manual.

## Gauge of the machine
- **Standard gauge** (~4.5 mm needle pitch): fine yarns (lace to fingering/sport).
- **Mid-gauge** (~6.5–7 mm): sport to DK.
- **Bulky/chunky** (~9 mm): worsted and thicker.
- Using yarn too thick for the machine jams the carriage and bends needles.

## Setup
1. Clamp the machine firmly to a sturdy table.
2. Set up the **yarn mast** with tension springs; thread yarn through the mast and the
   carriage feeder as the manual shows. Wax or use coned yarn for smooth feed (oiled coned
   yarn is washed after knitting to bloom).
3. Check the **sponge bar** (a foam strip pressing on needles) on many single-bed machines:
   a flattened sponge bar causes dropped stitches and is a common cause of problems on
   older machines — replace if it doesn't spring back.
4. Clean and lightly oil the needle bed and carriage per manual (machine oil, not excess).

## Casting on (open edge with waste yarn)
1. Bring the working needles to **working position (B)** with the needle pusher.
2. Knit 10–20 rows of **waste yarn** (contrasting colour), hanging the **cast-on comb** and
   weights after the first rows.
3. Knit one row of **ravel cord** (slippery nylon), then change to main yarn. Later, pulling
   the ravel cord removes the waste yarn and leaves live stitches for hemming or seaming.
- Alternatives: **e-wrap** cast-on (wrapping each needle by hand) for a closed edge.

## Tension dial
- The carriage's **tension dial** (stitch size) sets loop length. Higher numbers = looser.
- Start mid-range for the yarn; adjust in steps.

## Swatching
1. Cast on 40–60 stitches with waste yarn.
2. Knit a few rows main yarn, then mark the **centre 40 stitches** with contrast yarn tied
   at needles (or use the standard 'mark needles' convention), and knit e.g. 60 rows
   with the row counter, marking the start and end rows.
3. Take it off on waste yarn; **wash and dry the swatch** as the finished piece will be.
4. Measure the 40 marked stitches and 60 rows: stitches per 10 cm = 40 ÷ (measured cm) × 10.
   Rows likewise.
5. Try two or three tension settings in one swatch (separated by a row of eyelets or a
   coloured row) to choose the fabric you like.

## Knitting and patterning
- Move the carriage in smooth, full-width passes; jerky or partial passes drop stitches.
- **Punchcard** machines read a card for Fair Isle, slip, tuck or lace patterns;
  **electronic** machines read patterns from a built-in controller or a computer connection
  (some via third-party interfaces). The ribber adds a second bed for ribbing.

## Dropped stitches
- A dropped stitch ladders down the knitted fabric. Stop knitting.
- Use the **latch tool**: insert from the front into the dropped loop, catch each ladder
  bar in turn, latching up to the current row, then hang the stitch back on its needle.
- If several stitches dropped across a row, rip back by hand to a safe row (or to a row of
  waste yarn) and re-hang stitches using the transfer tool.

## Gotchas
- Yarn snapping or loops pulling up: mast tension too tight or yarn catching on cone.
- Carriage jams: yarn too thick for the tension/gauge, or needles not straight in their channels.
- Specific gauges/tension numbers vary by maker; follow your manual.

## Verify
- [ ] Machine gauge suits the yarn; sponge bar in good condition.
- [ ] Waste yarn and ravel cord cast-on used for open edges.
- [ ] Swatch washed and measured; tension number recorded with gauge.
- [ ] Dropped stitches latched up successfully.
