# Tunisian simple stitch and curl control

**Goal:** even Tunisian fabric with neat edges and minimal curl. Tunisian crochet holds a
row of loops on a long hook (like knitting) and works each row in two passes without turning.

## Hooks
- **Afghan/Tunisian hooks**: long straight hooks with a stopper, or **cabled hooks** (a short
  hook on a flexible cable) for wide pieces. Double-ended hooks are used for in-the-round or
  two-colour work.
- Go up **at least 1–2 hook sizes** from what the yarn label recommends for regular crochet —
  Tunisian fabric is dense, and a larger hook reduces curl and stiffness.

## Foundation
1. Chain the number of stitches wanted.
2. **Forward pass (FwP)**: skip first chain from hook; insert into the back bump of each
   chain, yarn over, pull up a loop, leaving it on the hook. You now have one loop per chain.
3. **Return pass (RetP)**: yarn over, pull through 1 loop; then (yarn over, pull through
   2 loops) repeatedly until one loop remains. That last loop is the first stitch of the next row.

## Tunisian simple stitch (Tss)
- Forward pass: skip the first vertical bar (the loop on the hook is it). Insert the hook
  **under the front vertical bar** of each stitch, from right to left, yarn over, pull up a loop.
- **Last stitch (edge)**: insert under both the front and back loops of the final edge stitch
  (or under the two strands at the edge) for a firm selvedge. This is where most edges go
  wrong.
- Return pass as above.
- Looks like a woven/grid fabric; very dense.

## Other stitches
- **Tunisian knit stitch (Tks)**: insert **between** the front and back vertical bars, through
  the fabric, under the horizontal strands — looks like knit stockinette. Curls strongly.
- **Tunisian purl stitch (Tps)**: yarn in front, insert under front bar, yarn over, pull up — a
  purl bump. Alternating Tks/Tps or Tss/Tps reduces curl.
- **Tunisian full stitch**: worked in spaces between stitches; drapier.

## Controlling curl
Tunisian fabric curls toward the front because all the loops form on one face. Options:
- **Bigger hook** (most effective).
- **Stitch choice**: include purl stitches (Tps), or use Tunisian full stitch, honeycomb (Tss/Tps
  alternating) or other combos that balance the faces.
- **Start with a few rows of a non-curling stitch** or add a crocheted border afterwards (sc or
  reverse sc), which can pull edges flat.
- **Blocking**: wet or steam block (respect fibre — acrylic is permanently changed by steam).
  Helps but won't fully cure a strongly curling stitch.
- **Return pass variations**: some designers use a looser return pass or (yo, pull through 3)
  occasionally — follow pattern advice.

## Binding off
- Slip stitch across: insert as for the stitch pattern, yarn over, pull through the stitch
  and the loop on the hook. Keep it loose — a tight bind-off pulls the top edge in.

## Colourwork
- Change colours at the start of a return pass: one colour on the forward pass, another on the
  return creates two-tone texture. Intarsia and entrelac are also common in Tunisian.

## Gotchas
- Edge narrowing: missed the last stitch of a forward pass. Count loops every row.
- Loose first stitch: tension the first return pass firmly.
- Curl remedies vary in effectiveness with yarn; swatch with your actual yarn and block it.

## Verify
- [ ] Hook 1–2+ sizes larger than standard crochet for the yarn.
- [ ] Loop count correct each forward pass; edge stitch worked under two strands.
- [ ] Curl controlled by hook, stitch choice or border; swatch blocked.
- [ ] Bind-off not tighter than the fabric.
