# Gauge swatching, cast-ons and finishing a knitted piece

**Goal:** a knitted piece that comes out the size the pattern promised, with edges
that stretch, invisible seams and ends that never pop out.

## 1. Swatch honestly
Gauge is stitches and rows per 10 cm. A 5% error on a 100 cm chest is 5 cm.
1. Knit at least **15×15 cm** (bigger than 10 cm so you measure away from edges) in
   the **pattern stitch** and with the **needles and method you'll use** (in the round
   if the garment is in the round — many knitters' purl rows differ from knit rows).
2. **Wash and block it the way you'll treat the garment**, dry fully. Many yarns
   (superwash, cotton, alpaca) grow when wet.
3. Measure across the middle 10 cm without stretching. Count half stitches.
4. Too many stitches per 10 cm → go up a needle size; too few → go down. Re-swatch.
Row gauge matters for raglans, yokes and anything worked to row counts; if you can
only match one, match stitch gauge and recompute lengths.

## 2. Cast-ons
| Cast-on | Use |
|---|---|
| **Long-tail** | General-purpose, firm and moderately elastic. Tail ≈ 3–4× finished width (or wrap the needle 10 times, measure, scale). |
| **Cable / knitted** | Firm edge; casting on mid-work (buttonholes). Not stretchy. |
| **German twisted / Old Norwegian** | Stretchier long-tail; good for sock cuffs and ribbing. |
| **Tubular** | Invisible, very elastic edge for 1×1 rib. |
| **Provisional** (crochet chain, waste yarn) | Live stitches to pick up later — grafting, top-down hems. |
If a cast-on is tight, cast on over two needles held together or a size larger, not
by "trying to be loose".

## 3. Bind-offs
- Standard: knit two, pass first over. Bind off **in pattern** (knit the knits, purl the
  purls) on ribbing.
- Too tight? Use a needle one or two sizes up, or **Jeny's surprisingly stretchy** /
  sewn bind-off for cuffs and necklines.
- Last stitch on a round: pull the tail through the first stitch for a **jogless** join.

## 4. Seams and grafting
- **Mattress stitch** (vertical seams): right side facing, pick up the running bars
  one stitch in from each edge, alternating sides, tighten every few cm. The seam
  disappears.
- **Kitchener stitch** (grafting) joins two sets of live stitches invisibly — sock
  toes, shoulders. Mnemonic for front/back needle: *knit off, purl on / purl off,
  knit on*. Keep tension matched to the knitting.
- Pick up stitches for bands about **3 stitches for every 4 rows** on a vertical
  edge (stockinette rows are shorter than stitches are wide) — check against the pattern.

## 5. Ends and blocking
- Weave ends **diagonally or duplicate-stitch along a row on the wrong side**, at least
  5 cm, change direction once, then trim. Weave in after blocking for slippery yarns.
- **Wet block:** soak, press water out in a towel, lay out to schematic measurements,
  pin if needed, dry flat. **Steam block** acrylic lightly (heat kills acrylic's spring).

## Gotchas
- Swatches knit flat for an in-the-round sweater lie about gauge.
- Gauge drifts when you're tense or relaxed — re-measure mid-project.
- Different needle materials (metal vs wood) can change gauge.
- Cables pull in: swatch the cable panel separately.

## Verify
- [ ] Washed swatch matches pattern gauge (stitches; rows if relevant).
- [ ] Cast-on and bind-off stretch as far as the fabric next to them.
- [ ] Seams invisible from the right side; grafts tension-matched.
- [ ] Ends secure after a firm tug; piece blocked to schematic.
