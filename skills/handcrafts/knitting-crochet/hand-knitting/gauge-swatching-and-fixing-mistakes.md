# Gauge swatching and fixing mistakes

**Goal:** a garment that comes out the size the pattern says, and the confidence to fix
any knitting mistake without starting over.

## Why gauge matters
Pattern sizing is built on **gauge** (stitches and rows per 10 cm/4 in). A difference of
half a stitch per inch on a 40-inch sweater is several inches of size. Accessories (scarves,
shawls) tolerate gauge differences; garments don't.

## Knit a swatch that tells the truth
1. Use the yarn and needles from the pattern, in the **pattern's stitch** (usually stockinette).
2. Cast on enough for at least 15 cm (6 in) of width; knit 15 cm tall. Add a few garter
   stitches at the edges to stop curling.
3. If the pattern is knit in the round, swatch in the round (or flat with yarn carried
   loosely across the back to mimic it) — many knitters purl at a different tension than
   they knit.
4. **Block** the swatch exactly as you'll block the garment (wash, lay flat, dry).
5. Measure in the centre over 10 cm, away from edges, without stretching. Count partial
   stitches.

## Adjusting
- **Too many stitches per 10 cm** (fabric too small): go up a needle size.
- **Too few** (fabric too large): go down a needle size.
- If row gauge is off but stitch gauge right, you can usually adjust length by knitting more
  or fewer rows, except in yoke/raglan shaping where row gauge matters — check the pattern.
- Re-swatch after changing needles; don't assume.

## Fixing mistakes
### Dropped stitch
- A stitch slid off and is laddering down. Catch it first with a locking stitch marker.
- Use a **crochet hook**: from the knit side, insert the hook into the loose stitch from front
  to back, catch the horizontal "ladder rung" above it, and pull it through. Repeat up the
  ladder, one rung per row, then put the stitch back on the needle.
- For purl-side, turn the work and fix from the knit side, or work the purl equivalent.

### Intentional laddering down
- To fix a mistake a few rows down in one column (e.g. a mis-crossed cable or a wrong purl),
  drop that stitch deliberately down to the error and rebuild it up with the hook.

### Twisted stitches
- Correct **stitch mount**: the right "leg" of the stitch sits in front of the needle.
  A stitch sitting backwards becomes twisted when knit. Reseat it by slipping it off
  and back on the right way before working.

### Tinking (un-knitting)
- "Knit" backwards: insert the left needle into the stitch below the one on the right needle,
  slip the right needle out, pull the yarn free. Stitch by stitch; safe and controlled.

### Ripping back (frogging)
- For bigger errors many rows down: run a **lifeline** first — thread a smooth cotton or
  dental-floss line through every stitch of a row below the mistake with a tapestry needle,
  avoiding markers. Then take the needles out and rip down to the lifeline; pick the live
  stitches back up from it.
- Habit: put a lifeline in every so often in complex lace or cables.

## Gotchas
- Small swatches lie — the edge stitches distort the measure.
- Swatch gauge can differ from garment gauge as weight of the knitting increases; measure the
  garment after the first several inches and compare.
- Very slippery yarns ladder fast; keep locking markers handy.

## Verify
- [ ] Swatch ≥15 cm square, in the pattern's stitch and method, blocked as the garment will be.
- [ ] Stitch and row gauge measured in the centre and match within tolerance.
- [ ] You can catch and rebuild a dropped stitch with a hook.
- [ ] Lifelines placed before risky sections.
