# Working relief on pitch with repoussé and chasing punches

**Goal:** raise a clean relief panel in copper, silver or brass sheet — outline crisp,
forms rounded and even, no tears — by alternating repoussé (from the back) and
chasing (from the front) on a pitch bed.

## 1. Materials and set-up
- **Sheet:** copper or fine/sterling silver 0.6–1.0 mm for relief up to ~10 mm deep;
  thinner sheet tears sooner, thicker needs more annealing.
- **Pitch:** a chaser's pitch (pine resin/rosin base with filler such as plaster or
  brick dust, plus a little oil or tallow to soften). Commercial grades are sold as
  soft/medium/hard; medium suits most copper work. Recipes vary widely — use a
  bought pitch first and adjust by adding oil (softer) or resin (harder).
- **Bowl:** a cast-iron pitch bowl on a leather or rope ring so it tilts and turns.
- **Hammer:** chasing hammer with a broad flat face (~25 mm) and a springy handle;
  you strike the punch, not the work.
- **Punches:** liners/tracers (thin blunt chisel edge), modelling punches (rounded
  ovals and squares of several sizes), planishing punches (flat, polished), and a
  matting punch for texture.

## 2. Setting on pitch
1. Anneal the sheet and pickle it clean. Turn the corners down slightly so they lock
   into the pitch.
2. Warm the pitch surface with a soft torch flame until it is tacky and just flows;
   do not boil or ignite it (it smokes and turns brittle).
3. Lightly oil or warm the back of the sheet, press it in, and push pitch up over the
   edges. Let it cool to room temperature — 20–30 minutes — before working, or the
   metal sinks rather than moves.

## 3. Lining the design (front)
- Transfer the drawing with carbon paper or scribe it.
- Hold the liner tilted slightly back, its heel trailing, and tap steadily (2–4 taps
  per mm of travel) so it walks along the line. Keep the depth even; you want a
  crisp groove, not a cut.

## 4. Repoussé (from the back)
1. Warm the pitch, lift the sheet (heat it gently and pry with pliers), and burn off or
   dissolve the pitch residue with a solvent such as mineral spirits or by heat and
   brushing (ventilated).
2. Anneal, pickle, and reset it face down.
3. Using large rounded punches, drive out the areas to stand proud, starting in the
   middle of each form and spiralling outward. Work the depth up in stages: go only
   a few millimetres per round.

## 5. Chasing (front) — refine
1. Remove, anneal, reset face up. Fill the hollows behind raised forms with pitch
   first (pour or press warm pitch in) so they are supported.
2. Push the background down around the forms with small modelling punches, sharpening
   the outline and the "foot" of each form.
3. Repeat back-and-front rounds, annealing each time the metal feels springy, until
   the depth and modelling are right. Finish with planishing punches and texture with
   matting punches.

## 6. Making punches
- Use 6–8 mm square or round **tool steel** (O1, W1) or reclaimed drift steel,
  100–125 mm long.
- File or grind the working face, round all edges a little (sharp edges cut the
  sheet), and polish to a mirror for planishers.
- Harden (heat to non-magnetic, quench per the steel) and temper the working end to
  a dark straw; draw the striking end softer (blue) so it does not chip. See
  handcrafts.blacksmithing.heat-treatment.hardening-and-tempering-carbon-steel.

## Faults
- **Tears:** metal over-worked without annealing, or a punch with a sharp edge.
- **Pillowing/lumpy background:** pitch too soft or warm; let it cool.
- **Doubled, stuttering lines:** liner held too upright or struck too hard.
- **Flat relief:** forms not supported with pitch before chasing from the front.

## Safety
Hot pitch sticks and burns — keep a bowl of cold water handy. Pitch fumes are
irritant: ventilate and never let it flame. Goggles when pickling.

## Verify
- [ ] Pitch firm at room temperature before each work session.
- [ ] Sheet annealed and cleaned at every reset.
- [ ] Outline continuous and even in depth; no cuts through.
- [ ] Relief depth reached in stages with no tears or thinning holes.
- [ ] Punch faces polished, edges radiused, tempered tough.
