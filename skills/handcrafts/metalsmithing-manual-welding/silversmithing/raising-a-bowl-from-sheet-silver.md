# Raising a bowl from sheet silver

**Goal:** turn a flat disc of sterling (or copper for practice) into a round bowl of
even wall thickness, free of cracks and folds, with a planished surface ready for
finishing.

## 1. Blank and material
- **Diameter:** a common starting rule is blank diameter ≈ finished height + the
  widest finished diameter (raising compresses rather than stretches, so the
  blank is not much larger than the "unrolled" profile). Make a card or copper
  test first for anything costly.
- **Gauge:** 1.0–1.2 mm (about 18 ga) for a small bowl of 100–150 mm; 1.5 mm for
  larger forms that will be raised hard. Practise in copper of the same gauge — it
  moves similarly and costs far less.
- Find and mark the centre with a centre punch; scribe concentric guide circles
  10–15 mm apart with dividers. These are your **course lines**.

## 2. Annealing — the rhythm of the whole job
- Sterling work-hardens after one full course. Anneal at a dull cherry red
  (roughly 600–650 °C; judge in dim light) with a large soft torch flame, keeping
  it moving. Hold briefly, then quench once the red has gone (sterling can be
  quenched; air-cooling is also fine).
- Pickle (warm sodium bisulphate or citric-acid solution) to clean. Never put
  iron tongs in the pickle — it copper-plates the work.
- **Firescale:** sterling grows subsurface copper oxide with each anneal. Coat with
  a borax/alcohol or proprietary flux barrier before heating to limit it.

## 3. Sinking the base
- Over a hollow in a wood stump or a sandbag, strike with a domed **blocking/
  sinking hammer**, working in overlapping blows in rings from the outer guide
  line inwards, turning the disc steadily. Sink to perhaps a third of the final
  depth. Keep the flat base you want unstretched — do not hammer inside it.

## 4. Raising in courses
1. Set the sunk form on a **raising stake** (T-stake or a long cross-peen stake)
   with the metal touching the stake about 5–10 mm below where you strike.
2. Hold the work tilted so the hammer face lands on metal standing *off* the
   stake: the blow pushes metal down onto the stake and compresses it.
3. Starting at the base line, strike with the **raising hammer** in a ring,
   rotating the bowl about 10–15 mm per blow, overlapping blows by roughly half.
4. Move up one course line and repeat to the rim. Each complete pass = one course.
5. Anneal after every course. Typical small bowls take 5–10 courses.
- **Crimping** (optional): flute the outer zone into radial corrugations on a
  grooved block before a course; then hammer the flutes down. Speeds raising but
  is easier to fold.

## 5. Controlling the shape
- Check the profile against a card template after each course.
- **Rim:** tap the edge with a mallet or the hammer face (caulking) on the bench
  every course or two; this thickens it and stops cracks starting there.
- Thickness is gained, not lost, in good raising. Measure with calipers or a
  double-ended gauge — thinning at mid-wall means blows are stretching rather than
  compressing (metal too flat on the stake).

## 6. Planishing
- Put the form over a polished **mushroom or planishing stake** whose curve
  matches the inside closely.
- Use a polished flat or slightly domed **planishing hammer** with light, even
  blows, the work in full contact with the stake under each blow, in tight rings.
  Planish from base to rim; a second, lighter pass refines the facets.
- Keep stake and hammer faces mirror-polished; every mark transfers.

## Gotchas and faults
- **Folds/pleats:** metal raised too fast or blows too heavy on one side. Hammer
  the fold flat early; once lapped it becomes a crack.
- **Cracks at the rim:** skipped annealing or no caulking.
- **Lopsided form:** uneven rotation; mark a start point and count blows per ring.
- **Lumpy surface after planishing:** stake curve does not match; air gaps under
  the blow.

## Safety
Pickle splashes: goggles. Torch work on a fireproof surface, with ventilation.
Hearing protection for long hammering sessions on steel stakes.

## Verify
- [ ] Blank size tested in copper or card; course lines scribed.
- [ ] Annealed and pickled after every course; no iron in the pickle.
- [ ] Profile matches template; base flat; rim caulked and crack-free.
- [ ] Wall thickness even within roughly ±0.1 mm of the start gauge or thicker.
- [ ] Planished surface even, no stake marks or folds.
