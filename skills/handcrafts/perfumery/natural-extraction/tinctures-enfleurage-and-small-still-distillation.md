# Tinctures, enfleurage and small-still distillation

**Goal:** capture scent from plant material yourself at a scale of grams to litres,
choosing the method that suits the material. Yields are small; the point is unique
materials, not cost savings. Draft: general craft practice.

## 1. Choose the method by material
| Material | Method |
|---|---|
| Dried resins, pods, woods, roots (vanilla, benzoin, orris, tonka) | Tincture in alcohol |
| Delicate flowers that keep producing scent after picking (jasmine, tuberose) | Enfleurage |
| Robust aromatic herbs, leaves, woods, seeds (lavender, rosemary, cedar shavings) | Distillation |
| Flowers for body oils | Warm or cold oil infusion |

## 2. Tinctures
- Grind or chop the material. Cover with high-proof ethanol (perfumer's alcohol or
  food-grade, ~95%) in a sealed jar; a common start is around 1 part material to
  5-10 parts alcohol by weight.
- Shake daily, steep for weeks (resins dissolve faster; woods and roots slower).
- **Recharge:** strain and add fresh material to the same alcohol to concentrate.
- Filter (coffee filter or lab filter paper), rest, then use as a perfume ingredient
  diluted further. Label strength as "tincture of X, n charges".

## 3. Enfleurage (cold)
- Spread a thin layer of odourless, solid fat (traditionally purified lard/tallow;
  modern craft often uses refined coconut, shea or palm-derived blends — results vary)
  on glass plates in frames.
- Lay fresh blossoms on the fat; seal frames together. Replace flowers every day
  (or as they fade) for weeks until the **pomade** is saturated.
- Wash the pomade in alcohol (stir, chill, separate) to get an **extrait**; evaporate
  the alcohol gently for an "absolute from pomade". Labour-intensive; tiny yields.

## 4. Distillation
- **Hydrodistillation:** material in water in the pot. **Steam distillation:** steam
  passes through material in a basket above the water — less cooking, better for
  delicate material.
- Copper alembics are traditional (copper reacts with sulphur compounds); stainless
  stills work too.
- Collect distillate in a **separator** (Florentine flask) — oil floats (most) or sinks
  (some, like clove). The water fraction is the **hydrosol**, itself a product.
- Yields are often well under 1% by weight; a home still on a few kilos of lavender
  may give a few millilitres of oil. Don't expect oil from low-yield flowers (rose
  gives mostly hydrosol at small scale).

## 5. Oil infusions
Steep material in a stable carrier oil (jojoba, fractionated coconut) warm (double
boiler, low heat for hours) or cold (sunny window for weeks), strain and recharge.
Good for body oils; too weak for most alcohol perfumes.

## Safety
- Ethanol vapour is flammable: no open flames when evaporating or filtering.
- Stills under pressure are dangerous — use designs meant for essential oils with
  a free vent; never seal a heated pot.
- Distilling alcohol (spirits) is licensed or illegal in many countries; essential
  oil distillation from water is usually fine — check locally.
- Homemade extracts have no IFRA certificate: you can't know their allergen content.
  Use conservatively in skin products.
