# Building and balancing an accord

**Goal:** a small blend (often 2-8 materials) that smells like a single new thing —
a rose that isn't any one rose material, an amber that isn't labdanum alone — which
you then use as a building block in many formulas. Draft: classic skeletons summarised
from common teaching; exact ratios are yours to find by trial.

## 1. What an accord is
When the parts disappear into a whole, you have an accord. If you can still pick
out each material, you have a mixture. Perfumers keep libraries of accords as
semi-finished bases.

## 2. Classic skeletons to start from
- **Rose:** phenylethyl alcohol (fresh, petal), citronellol and geraniol (rosy,
  geranium), a touch of something green or spicy (e.g. a tiny amount of eugenol or
  rose oxide), with a fixing base. Natural rose oils/absolutes add richness.
- **Amber (the perfumery "amber," not fossil resin):** labdanum + vanilla/vanillin +
  a balsam (benzoin, Peru or tolu balsam). Warm, resinous, sweet.
- **Fougère:** lavender + coumarin (hay, tonka) + oakmoss or a moss substitute, often
  with bergamot and geranium. Barbershop backbone.
- **Chypre:** bergamot top, a floral heart (often rose/jasmine), and oakmoss with
  labdanum and patchouli in the base. Note: natural oakmoss is restricted by IFRA;
  low-atranol grades or substitutes are used.

## 3. Ratio trials
- Make a **two-material** grid first: A:B at 9:1, 7:3, 5:5, 3:7, 1:9 (by weight, on
  diluted materials). Smell each on blotter after 10-15 minutes. Find the point
  where they fuse.
- Add a third material to the best pair in small steps (1, 2, 5, 10% of total), and
  so on. One variable at a time.
- Use dilutions (10% or 1%) for strong materials — some are active at trace levels.

## 4. Judging
- Smell blind if you can (have someone code the blotters).
- Judge over time: an accord that fuses at the top may split apart in the drydown.
- Test it in context: drop a few percent of the accord into a simple base or a
  draft formula; an accord that's lovely alone can vanish or dominate in a blend.

## 5. The accord library
For each accord record: name and version, formula in parts by weight, dilutions,
date, a short odour description, and where you've used it. Store a small labelled
bottle; accords often improve after a week of rest.

## Gotchas
- Too many materials early hide what's working. Start minimal.
- Natural materials vary batch to batch; note supplier and lot.
- An accord built with materials that exceed IFRA limits once diluted into a final
  product is a trap — check limits at the accord stage
  (`handcrafts.perfumery.perfumery-materials.smelling-and-evaluating-raw-materials`).

## Verify
- [ ] Two-material ratio grid done and best point recorded.
- [ ] Additions made one at a time, in small steps.
- [ ] Accord smells unified at top, heart and drydown.
- [ ] Tested inside a formula, not only alone.
- [ ] Logged in the accord library with lot numbers.
