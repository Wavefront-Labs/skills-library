# Building a perfume formula

**Goal:** go from an idea ("a dry, green fig") to a finished concentrate formula you
can reproduce exactly. Composition is iterative: you will make many numbered
trials. Draft: standard craft-perfumery method; percentages are rough orientation.

## 1. Brief and family
- Write a one-line brief: mood, use, gender-neutral or not, budget, natural-only or not.
- Place it in a **family** (citrus, floral, chypre, fougère, amber/"oriental",
  woody, gourmand, aromatic, aquatic) — it tells you the structural accords you'll need.

## 2. Structure: top, heart, base
- **Top notes:** volatile, first 5-30 minutes (citrus, light herbals, aldehydes).
- **Heart:** the character, roughly an hour to several hours (florals, spices, fruits).
- **Base:** fixatives and the drydown, many hours (woods, musks, resins, vanilla,
  ambery materials).
- A loose starting balance many teach is around 20-30% top, 30-50% heart, 25-45%
  base by weight in the concentrate — but modern perfumes vary hugely. Treat as a
  start, not a rule.

## 3. Write the formula in parts
- Work in **parts per thousand** (or grams to 1000) by weight, not drops — drop size
  varies with viscosity and dropper, so drop formulas aren't reproducible.
- Use pre-made **dilutions** (often 10% or 1% in perfumer's alcohol or DPG) for
  strong materials so small amounts are weighable. Record dilution in the formula.
- Start small: 5-10 materials. Build from accords
  (`handcrafts.perfumery.accord-building.building-and-balancing-an-accord`) rather
  than single materials.

## 4. Trials and mods
- Number every trial: `FIG-01`, `FIG-02` … Each trial changes **one thing** (or one
  planned group), noted with the reason ("+20 cedar for dryness").
- Blend a small batch (e.g. 2-5 g concentrate), let it rest at least a day or two
  before judging; many accords change significantly as they settle.

## 5. Evaluation
- **Blotters** (smelling strips) labelled with trial and time. Smell at dip, 15 min,
  1 h, 4 h, next day. Compare trials side by side, not from memory.
- **Skin test** the promising ones (diluted), noting how skin chemistry shifts it;
  respect IFRA limits and allergens (see
  `handcrafts.perfumery.perfumery-materials.smelling-and-evaluating-raw-materials`).
- Rest your nose: short sessions, smell your own skin or wool between; coffee beans
  are traditional but debated.
- Judge **sillage** (projection), **longevity** and whether the transitions are smooth.

## 6. Finish
Final concentrate to dilution: very roughly, extrait 15-30%, EDP 10-20%, EDT 5-15%
in alcohol — see `handcrafts.perfumery.compounding-maturation.compounding-macerating-and-bottling`.

## Gotchas
- Overdosing a powerful material (e.g. some musks, ambery woods, indoles) ruins
  everything — dilute first, add in tiny steps.
- Nose fatigue makes you overdose what you smell most; take breaks.
- Unrecorded changes make a great trial impossible to repeat.

## Verify
- [ ] Brief and family written.
- [ ] Formula in parts by weight with dilutions recorded.
- [ ] Every trial numbered with its change and reason.
- [ ] Blotter and skin evaluated over at least 24 hours.
- [ ] Final formula reproducible and within IFRA limits.
