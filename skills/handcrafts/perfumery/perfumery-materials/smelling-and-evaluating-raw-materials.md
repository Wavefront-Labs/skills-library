# Smelling and evaluating raw materials

**Goal:** know each material on your shelf well enough to describe it, predict how
long it lasts, and use it within safe limits. Perfumers train like this for years;
this is the method, not a shortcut. Draft.

## 1. Make standard dilutions
- Smell materials diluted, not neat: neat materials overload the nose and many are
  unpleasant or misleading at full strength.
- Standard: **10%** in perfumer's alcohol (or DPG/IPM for non-alcoholic work) for most;
  **1%** or **0.1%** for very strong materials. Label: material, supplier, lot,
  dilution, solvent, date.
- Weigh on a 0.01 g (or better, 0.001 g) scale; use pipettes per material to avoid
  cross-contamination.

## 2. Smelling technique
- Dip a **blotter** ~1 cm, label it, wave once, smell at a little distance, then
  close. Short sniffs, not long inhalations.
- Smell at intervals: immediately, 10 min, 1 h, 4 h, 24 h, and later for base
  materials. This maps **volatility** (how fast it evaporates) and **tenacity**.
- Smell in a neutral room without other fragrance. Limit sessions; nose fatigue
  sets in within minutes on strong materials.

## 3. Describe it
Write a profile per material in a notebook or spreadsheet:
- **Family words:** citrus, green, floral, fruity, spicy, woody, ambery, musky,
  animalic, balsamic, earthy, marine, aldehydic.
- **Specific associations:** "green banana peel", "pencil shavings", "wet stone".
- Change over time (top vs. drydown), strength (weak/medium/strong), and what it
  blends with.
- Compare it to its neighbours (several cedarwoods, several musks); comparisons train
  discrimination fastest.

## 4. Naturals vs. aroma chemicals
- **Naturals** (essential oils, absolutes, CO2 extracts, resinoids): complex, vary
  by origin and lot, often contain regulated allergens naturally.
- **Aroma chemicals:** single molecules (e.g. linalool, vanillin, Iso E Super-type
  woody materials, musks). Consistent, often more stable. Neither is "safer" by
  nature.

## 5. Safety limits
- **IFRA Standards** set maximum use levels of restricted materials by **product
  category** (fine fragrance vs. leave-on body products vs. candles differ). Check the
  current amendment; limits change.
- Ask suppliers for the **SDS** and an **IFRA certificate** for each material; the
  certificate gives max levels by category.
- In the EU/UK, certain **allergens** (e.g. linalool, limonene, geraniol, citral,
  and others; the list expanded under recent rules) must be labelled above set
  thresholds in cosmetics. Check current regulation for your market — I'm not
  certain of the latest thresholds.

## Gotchas
- Oxidised citrus oils (old, stored warm, with air) become more sensitising and smell
  flat. Store cold, dark, full bottles, and date them.
- Some materials (e.g. certain musks) are anosmic for some people; if you can't smell
  something others can, that's normal.

## Verify
- [ ] Every material has a labelled standard dilution.
- [ ] Profile written with time-based notes.
- [ ] SDS and IFRA certificate on file for each material.
- [ ] Max level for your product category known before use.
