# Managing a vegetable tan-liquor progression

**Goal:** tannin fully through the hide, evenly, without a hard outer case, giving
firm, mouldable leather (saddle, belt, sole, tooling leather). The core idea: start
weak, finish strong, and give it time. Draft: principles well established; the
numbers are orientation from craft sources, not verified for every tannin.

## 1. Tannin sources
- **Bark:** oak, hemlock, chestnut, spruce (traditional; ground bark leached in water).
- **Extracts:** quebracho, mimosa/wattle, chestnut, tara, sumac — sold as powders,
  consistent and convenient.
- Different tannins give different colours and feel: mimosa pinker and softer,
  quebracho reddish and firm, chestnut and oak warm brown and firm; tara pale.

## 2. Why weak to strong
Strong tannin on a fresh pelt fixes rapidly on the surface and "**case-hardens**" —
the outside tans and blocks the inside, leaving raw, rot-prone core. A **drawn grain**
(wrinkled, shrunk surface) is the other symptom. Start weak so tannin can travel.

## 3. The progression
- Traditional tanyards ran a series of pits: the newest hides went into the weakest,
  most-used liquor ("**rockers**", where hides are moved/rocked), then through
  progressively stronger "**handlers**" and finally "**layers**" (hides laid flat
  with ground bark between them) for weeks to months.
- Craft equivalent with one or a few vats: start at a low strength and **add
  concentrated liquor** every few days to raise strength gradually, stirring and
  moving the hide daily for even contact.
- Sole leather in old yards took many months; thinner leathers at craft scale can
  take a few weeks. Thick hides can take months.

## 4. Measuring strength
- A **barkometer** (a hydrometer scaled for tan liquor; degrees Bk, where each
  degree is roughly 0.001 specific gravity above water) tracks strength. Note that it
  measures all dissolved solids, not only tannin.
- Log readings and additions. Watch pH: vegetable tanning is commonly done in a
  mildly acidic range; very low pH speeds surface fixation.

## 5. Checking penetration
- Cut a small sliver from an edge (belly or shank, where it won't matter). A fully
  tanned cut shows **even colour right through**; a pale or white line in the middle
  means more time.
- Done hides often get a short soak in a strong liquor to "finish".

## 6. After the tan
Rinse, then **sam** (partially dry), oil/fatliquor and set out — see
`handcrafts.tanning.currying-finishing.fatliquoring-staking-and-setting-out`.

## Gotchas
- Mould on liquors is common and mostly harmless; rot smells on the hide are not.
- Iron contact (nails, steel vats) turns vegetable tannin black. Use plastic, wood
  or stainless.
- Insufficient deliming before tanning gives dark, brittle results.

## Verify
- [ ] Liquor started weak; strength raised in logged steps.
- [ ] Barkometer and pH readings recorded.
- [ ] Hide moved daily for even tanning; no iron contact.
- [ ] Cut section shows colour fully through, no raw core.
