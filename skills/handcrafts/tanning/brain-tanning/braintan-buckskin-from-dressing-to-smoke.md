# Braintan buckskin from dressing to smoke

**Goal:** soft, breathable, washable buckskin with no chemicals beyond what an animal
and a fire provide. Technically braintan is an oil-and-physical process (a "dressing"),
not tanning by tannin; the smoke gives it lasting washability. Labour-heavy: plan a
full day of softening per hide. Draft: summarised from the wet-scrape tradition.

## 1. Prepare: wet-scrape
- Soak and **buck** the hide in wood-ash lye or lime until hair and grain loosen (see
  `handcrafts.tanning.hide-preparation.fleshing-liming-and-dehairing-a-hide`).
- On a beam, scrape off hair **and the grain layer** — the thin, tight outer layer.
  Grain left on will not soften and shows as hard, shiny patches.
- Flip, scrape off the membrane. Rinse the buck out thoroughly (several soaks, a
  little vinegar is used by some) — residual alkali stiffens the hide.
- Wring out water: twist the hide on a pole or around a post with a stick.

## 2. Dress
- Traditional dressing: brains (about one animal's brain is often said to dress its
  hide) blended into warm water to a milky emulsion. **Egg yolks** or a mild soap and
  oil emulsion are common substitutes.
- Work the dressing into the damp, wrung hide, let it soak (often an hour or more),
  wring, and re-dress. Repeat until the dressing penetrates evenly: a wrung hide
  should look uniformly opaque, with no translucent patches.
- Hygiene: handle brains with gloves; avoid using brains from animals in areas with
  chronic wasting disease or similar concerns — egg is the safer default.

## 3. Soften (the hard part)
- The hide must be **kept moving while it dries**. Fibres that dry while still are
  glued together; moving fibres stay separate and soft.
- Methods: stretch on a frame with laces and push with a blunt stick; pull and
  stretch by hand; work over a cable or rope ("cabling"). Work evenly across the
  whole hide, especially edges and neck.
- Keep going until it is **completely** dry. If a patch dries hard, re-wet and redo
  that area. Stopping when it's merely "almost dry" gives stiff leather.

## 4. Smoke
- Sew the hide into a bag or tube, open end over a smoke source (a small pit of
  smouldering punk/rotten wood, not flame), with a skirt to direct smoke in.
- Smoke each side until it reaches the colour you want (golden to deep brown);
  often 20-60 minutes a side. Watch for heat: flame will scorch.
- Smoke fixes the dressing so the leather softens again after wetting; unsmoked
  braintan dries stiff after rain.

## Gotchas
- The most common failure is stopping softening too soon.
- Leftover grain = hard spots; scrape it all at the start.
- Too-hot smoke shrinks and scorches.

## Verify
- [ ] Hair, grain and membrane fully removed; buck rinsed out.
- [ ] Dressing penetration even with no translucent spots.
- [ ] Softened until fully dry; whole hide supple.
- [ ] Smoked both sides; a test wetting dries soft again.
