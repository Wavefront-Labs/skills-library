# Fleshing, liming and dehairing a hide

**Goal:** a clean, hairless, swollen and then delimed pelt ready to take tannin
evenly. This is the "beamhouse" stage; most tanning faults (patchy colour, bald
or hard spots, rot) start here. Draft: small-scale practice; lime concentrations
and times are orientation only.

## 1. Curing and storage
- Raw ("green") hides rot fast. Either **salt** immediately — fine salt rubbed into the
  flesh side at roughly the hide's weight or a good thick layer, folded flesh-to-flesh,
  drained on a slope — or **freeze**.
- Salted hides keep for weeks to months in cool storage; dry-salted hides become stiff
  "flint" and need long soaking.

## 2. Soak
Rehydrate in clean, cool water, changing it, until the hide is fully supple (hours
for fresh, a day or more for salted/dried). Add nothing else at this stage unless
your method specifies a wetting agent.

## 3. Flesh
- Drape over a **fleshing beam** (a smooth, rounded log or board), flesh side up,
  and push off fat, membrane and meat with a two-handled **fleshing knife**, working
  downward away from you.
- Remove the membrane fully: it blocks tannin. Don't cut into the hide fibres.

## 4. Lime or buck (loosen hair and open fibres)
- **Liming:** submerge in a solution of hydrated lime (calcium hydroxide) in water,
  stirred daily, for several days to a couple of weeks depending on temperature and
  hide thickness. Industrial liming adds sulphides to burn hair; craft tanners usually
  use lime alone and accept the longer time.
- **Wood-ash lye bucking** (traditional for braintan): a strong hardwood ash solution;
  works similarly.
- Test: hair should push off easily with a thumb. Hides swell ("plump") and turn
  translucent and rubbery.
- Wear gloves and eye protection — lime and lye are caustic.

## 5. Dehair and scud
- Back on the beam, grain (hair) side up, push hair off with the dull edge of the
  knife or a dehairing tool.
- **Scud:** push again firmly to drive out dirt, hair roots, pigment and grease from
  the grain — what remains shows as dark spots in finished leather.
- For buckskin, many also remove the grain layer itself ("graining"); see
  `handcrafts.tanning.brain-tanning.braintan-buckskin-from-dressing-to-smoke`.

## 6. Delime
Lime left in the hide interferes with vegetable tannins (can cause dark, brittle
leather). Rinse repeatedly; many use a mild acid (vinegar or a deliming agent)
and check with pH strips until neutral-ish (targets vary by tannage — check your
method). Commercial tanneries also **bate** with enzymes to soften; optional at
craft scale.

## Disposal
Spent lime and sulphide liquors are caustic and polluting — neutralise and dispose
according to local rules, not in a drain or watercourse.

## Verify
- [ ] Hide cured promptly; no smell of rot or slipping hair before liming.
- [ ] Membrane fully removed on the flesh side.
- [ ] Hair and roots pushed off completely; grain scudded clean.
- [ ] Delimed to the pH your tanning method expects.
