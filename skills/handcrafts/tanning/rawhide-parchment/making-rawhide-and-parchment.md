# Making rawhide and parchment

**Goal:** stiff, strong, untanned skin — **rawhide** for lacing, lashing, drumheads
and dog chews, or **parchment/vellum** for writing, bookbinding and drums. Neither is
tanned: they're cleaned skins dried under tension. They revert when soaked, which is
the point (rawhide is laced wet and shrinks tight). Draft.

## 1. Common start
- Flesh, lime and dehair as in
  `handcrafts.tanning.hide-preparation.fleshing-liming-and-dehairing-a-hide` (wood ash
  or lime). Rinse well; for parchment many skip heavy deliming because lime helps
  produce the white surface, but residual lime makes it more brittle — practices vary.
- For rawhide with hair on (some drums), skip liming: flesh and dry.

## 2. Rawhide
- Lace the clean wet hide into a frame or peg it flat, flesh side up, and let it dry
  under tension in shade with air movement.
- Cut lacing (**babiche**) from the wet or rewetted hide in a continuous spiral from
  a round piece, with a knife and a guide.
- Use: soak until pliable, lash on wet, let it dry and shrink — joints bind very tight.
- Rawhide reabsorbs water: protect outdoor work with oil, varnish or a wax.

## 3. Parchment / vellum
- "**Vellum**" traditionally means calfskin; "parchment" covers sheep and goat, though
  usage varies.
- Lace the wet, limed skin to a wooden **herse** (frame) with pegs that can be
  turned to tighten, cord tied around small pebbles folded into the skin edges.
- While it dries, scrape both sides with a **lunellum** (crescent knife), tightening
  the pegs as it dries and shrinks. Thin to even thickness; thin spots tear, thick ones
  stay stiff.
- Once dry, finish with **pumice** and pounce (fine powdered pumice/chalk or
  cuttlefish) to raise a nap that takes ink. Cut off the frame.

## 4. Drumheads
Goat, calf and deer rawhide are common. Thickness determines tone: thinner = brighter,
more sensitive; thicker = deeper, tougher. Mount wet over the shell, tension evenly
around, and let it dry tight. Drumheads drift with humidity.

## Gotchas
- **Humidity:** parchment cockles (waves) as moisture changes. Bound books need clasps
  or boards; framed parchment needs room to move.
- Holes and thin spots in parchment usually come from scraping too hard when wet.
- Rawhide dog chews and food-contact products: hygiene and local regulation apply.

## Verify
- [ ] Skin fully cleaned of flesh, hair and membrane.
- [ ] Dried under even tension; pegs tightened as it shrank.
- [ ] Parchment thickness even when held to the light, no holes.
- [ ] Surface takes ink cleanly after pouncing (test a pen stroke).
- [ ] Rawhide lacing cut continuous and even in width.
