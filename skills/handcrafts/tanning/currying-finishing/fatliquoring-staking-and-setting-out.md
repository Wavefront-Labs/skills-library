# Fatliquoring, staking and setting out

**Goal:** turn a freshly tanned, damp hide into usable leather — flat, the right
softness for its job, lubricated so fibres don't crack, and with a finished surface.
The currier's craft historically; at small scale the tanner does it all. Draft.

## 1. Sam
After tanning, rinse and let the hide drain/dry to **sammed** condition: damp,
cool to the touch, not dripping. Most currying steps want leather at this stage.

## 2. Set out
- Lay the hide grain up on a smooth table and push it flat with a **slicker**
  (sleeker): a blunt-edged steel, brass or glass blade. Work from the centre out,
  removing wrinkles and excess water, and stretching the hide to its full area.
- For firm leather (belts, tooling), setting out and drying flat gives the finished
  shape.

## 3. Fatliquor or stuff
- **Fatliquoring:** sulphated (emulsifiable) oils in warm water, worked into damp
  leather (in a drum industrially; by hand, in a tub or brushed on). The emulsion
  carries oil deep into the fibres, then breaks and fixes. A common craft approach
  uses sulphated neatsfoot or similar commercial fatliquor at the maker's rate
  (often given as a % of the leather's weight).
- **Stuffing:** heavy greases (tallow, cod oil, dubbin) worked into the flesh side of
  firm or harness leather, sometimes warm ("hot stuffing"). Gives water resistance and
  a heavier feel.
- Too little oil: leather cracks when flexed, dries hard. Too much: greasy, weak,
  won't glue or take dye/finish, spews (white or dark bloom) later.

## 4. Dry and stake
- Dry slowly away from heat — on a frame (toggled) for flat soft leather, or hung.
- **Staking:** draw the nearly-dry leather back and forth over a blunt blade (a
  staking beam or a staking post) to break fibres apart and soften. Like braintan
  softening, works best as it dries. Repeat once dry if it stiffened.
- Firm leathers aren't staked.

## 5. Surface finishing
- **Boarding / graining:** folding grain to grain and rolling with a cork board to
  raise or crimp the grain pattern.
- **Glazing/burnishing:** high-pressure rubbing (glass or agate glazing jack) for a
  shine on firm leather.
- Neutral finishes: a light wax or a commercial top coat. Dye is usually applied by
  the leatherworker later, but tanners may dye in the drum.

## Gotchas
- Oiling bone-dry leather only coats the surface; oil damp (sammed) leather.
- Uneven drying = uneven softness. Turn and check hides daily.
- Linseed and similar drying oils harden in leather over time — avoid unless intended.

## Verify
- [ ] Set out flat with no creases before drying.
- [ ] Fatliquor/stuffing amount logged; no greasy surface.
- [ ] Flex test: grain doesn't crack when folded.
- [ ] Softness matches intended use (staked or firm).
- [ ] Surface takes a test spot of dye or finish evenly.
