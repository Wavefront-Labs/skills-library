# Complete denture: setup, processing and finishing

**Goal:** a complete denture that matches the clinician's prescription and records, with
the occlusion and fit preserved through processing, finished smooth with no sharp or
porous surfaces. Work only on a valid prescription from a dentist or clinical dental
technician; regulation of dental labs and custom devices varies by country (e.g. MDR
custom-made device rules in the EU/UK) — follow yours.

## 1. Models and special trays
1. Pour primary impressions in dental stone; trim models.
2. Draw the outline (sulcus depth minus a couple of mm, avoiding frenums) and make
   **special (custom) trays** in light-cure or self-cure tray material with spacer as
   prescribed. The clinician takes border-moulded master impressions in them.
3. Box and pour **master models** in a harder stone; trim and record landmarks
   (incisive papilla, retromolar pads, hamular notches, post-dam area on the upper).

## 2. Record blocks (wax rims)
Make rigid baseplates (shellac or light-cure) with **wax occlusal rims** at average
dimensions (lab and clinic agree a standard; e.g. upper anterior rim height from the
sulcus). The clinician adjusts them to record lip support, occlusal plane, vertical
dimension and centric relation, plus centre line, high smile line and canine lines.

## 3. Mounting
Mount the models on an **articulator** using the face-bow record (if taken) for the upper
and the jaw registration for the lower. Check the registration has not distorted (rims
seated fully on the models, no rocking).

## 4. Tooth setup
1. Select teeth from the clinician's mould and shade prescription.
2. **Anteriors** to the recorded centre line, lip support and smile line; incisal edges
   follow the occlusal plane. Small irregularities look natural; check symmetry.
3. **Posteriors** over the ridge crest (to keep the denture stable), following the occlusal
   plane and the curves of Spee and Wilson.
4. Occlusion: commonly **bilateral balanced occlusion** for complete dentures (contacts on
   both sides in excursions) to stabilise them; other schemes (lingualised, monoplane) are
   used per prescription.
5. Wax up gum contours (festooning) for the try-in; carve neatly so the clinician and patient
   can judge appearance.

## 5. Try-in
The clinician tries in the wax setup in the mouth; adjustments and a new registration may
come back. Do not process until the try-in is approved.

## 6. Processing
1. Cut the **post-dam** on the upper model as prescribed.
2. **Flask**: invest the model and setup in a denture flask with stone/plaster in layers,
   coating with separating medium. Boil/heat out the wax.
3. Paint alginate separator (cold-mould seal) on stone surfaces.
4. Pack heat-cure **PMMA** dough (powder:liquid ratio per manufacturer) — trial-pack,
   then final closure, or use an injection system.
5. **Cure** using the manufacturer's cycle (e.g. a long low-temperature water-bath cycle
   then boil, or a short cycle). Too fast causes porosity from monomer boiling.
6. Bench-cool slowly before deflasking — rapid cooling causes stresses and warping.

## 7. Remount and finish
1. **Remount** on the articulator (using the remount index) and selectively grind to
   correct processing errors in occlusion.
2. Trim flash, refine borders without shortening them, keep the fitting surface untouched
   except for removing nodules.
3. Pumice then polish (brush, then high-shine compound) on the polished surfaces only.
4. Disinfect per protocol; store wet in a sealed bag.

## Gotchas
- Thermal shock and rough deflasking fracture models and warp bases.
- Polishing the fitting surface ruins fit.
- Posterior teeth set buccal to the ridge make the denture tip.

## Verify
- [ ] Setup matches recorded centre line, occlusal plane and vertical dimension.
- [ ] Try-in approved before processing.
- [ ] No porosity, no bubbles on the fitting surface; borders intact.
- [ ] Remounted occlusion even with balanced contacts as prescribed.
- [ ] Polished, disinfected, labelled with patient/prescription reference.
