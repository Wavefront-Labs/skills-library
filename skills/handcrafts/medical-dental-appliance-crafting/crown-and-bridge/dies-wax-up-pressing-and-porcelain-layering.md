# Dies, wax-up, pressing and porcelain layering

**Goal:** a crown or bridge that seats completely on the die with a closed margin, has
correct proximal contacts and occlusion, and matches the prescribed shade. Marginal fit is
the non-negotiable: a beautiful crown with an open margin fails clinically.
Work to a dentist's prescription and your lab's quality system.

## 1. Models and dies
1. Pour the impression in **Type IV die stone** (vacuum mixed, correct water:powder ratio).
2. Pin (e.g. Pindex) and base the model, then **section** the die with a thin saw from below,
   without touching the margin.
3. **Trim (ditch) the die** below the finish line under magnification, so the margin is a clear,
   intact ledge. Mark it with a fine red pencil. This is the most important step in the job.
4. Apply **die hardener** to the margin area, then **die spacer** to within ~0.5–1 mm short of
   the margin, in the number of coats your system specifies (space for cement).

## 2. Wax-up
- **Full-contour wax-up** (for monolithic pressed ceramics or cast metal): build the full tooth
  anatomy in wax, check occlusion on the articulator, contacts with neighbours, emergence
  profile, then refine the margin with margin wax and seal it under magnification.
- **Cut-back** (for layered restorations): full contour first, then reduce the incisal/facial
  areas uniformly to leave room for layering porcelain.
- Minimum thicknesses depend on the material (manufacturer's guidance for each ceramic) —
  too thin fractures, too thick looks opaque.

## 3. Investing, burnout and pressing (pressed lithium disilicate example)
1. Sprue the wax-up per the system (sprue size, angle, position at the thickest part).
2. Invest in the system's phosphate-bonded investment, at the specified liquid concentration
   (which controls expansion and therefore fit).
3. **Burnout** in a furnace on the specified cycle to eliminate the wax.
4. **Press** the ceramic ingot in a press furnace with the right program for the ingot size.
5. **Divest** carefully — glass bead blast at low pressure, then remove the reaction layer
   with the specified acid/procedure. Aggressive blasting chips margins.
6. Cut off sprues with a fine diamond disc, cooling well.
- Zirconia and many crowns now come from **CAD/CAM** milling; the die-trimming, fitting and
  finishing principles still apply.

## 4. Fitting
- Seat on the die under magnification; use fit checker or a marker on the intaglio to find
  binding spots; adjust only the marked spot, minimally.
- Check **proximal contacts** on the solid (unsectioned) model with shim stock — they should
  hold but let shim pull through with resistance. Check occlusion on the articulator.

## 5. Layering and characterisation
- For layered work: apply dentine and enamel porcelains to rebuild the cut-back, with
  effects (translucents, mamelons, opalescents) per the shade map, slightly over-building
  for **firing shrinkage**; fire per manufacturer.
- Shape and contour after firing; a correction bake as needed.
- **Stain and glaze**: surface stains for characterisation; glaze firing for a natural surface.
  Monolithic crowns are often just stained and glazed.

## Gotchas
- A rounded or chipped die margin cannot be reproduced; re-pour if damaged.
- Over-firing reduces translucency and can deform margins; under-firing leaves the
  ceramic weak and cloudy.
- Spacer on the margin opens it.
- Material-specific numbers (thickness, firing, investment ratios) come from the IFU; not stated here on purpose.

## Verify
- [ ] Margin is visible and intact on the die before wax-up.
- [ ] Restoration seats fully with margin closed all round under magnification.
- [ ] Contacts hold shim stock with resistance; occlusion marks as prescribed.
- [ ] Shade checked against the shade guide under daylight-corrected light.
- [ ] Lab documentation (materials, lot numbers, declaration) completed.
