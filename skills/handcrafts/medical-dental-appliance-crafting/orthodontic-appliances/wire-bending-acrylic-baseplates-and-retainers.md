# Wire bending, acrylic baseplates and retainers

**Goal:** removable orthodontic appliances (Hawley-type retainers, active plates with
springs or screws) whose wirework fits the teeth exactly, is passive or active only as
prescribed, and whose baseplate is strong, smooth and comfortable. Work to the
orthodontist's prescription design.

## 1. Model and design
- Pour models in hard stone; check for bubbles on teeth — a bubble becomes a clasp misfit.
- Draw the design on the model in pencil: clasp positions, bow, springs, screw position,
  baseplate outline (clear of gingival margins where springs act; extended for retention).
- Lightly trim the stone at clasp contact points (cervical undercuts) only as your lab
  protocol allows — usually ~0.5 mm, to engage the undercut.

## 2. Wire and pliers
- **Stainless steel spring-hard wire** in standard diameters (commonly 0.6–0.7 mm for clasps,
  0.7 mm for labial bows, 0.5 mm for finger springs — per prescription).
- Adams pliers, bird-beak (light wire) pliers, three-prong pliers, wire cutters.
- Bend with the pliers, guiding the wire with your fingers; **don't nick the wire** with
  plier jaws, and avoid re-bending the same spot — both create fracture points.

## 3. Key wirework
- **Adams clasp** (molars): two arrowheads that engage mesial and distal undercuts, joined
  by a bridge standing slightly away from the buccal surface; tags crossing the occlusal
  embrasures into the baseplate. Arrowheads should sit at about 45° to the tooth surface.
- **Labial bow**: runs across the labial of the incisors at about the junction of incisal and
  middle thirds, with U-loops over the canines for adjustment, passing through the
  canine/premolar embrasure into the baseplate.
- **Springs** (finger, Z, T): coil and arm positioned to move a tooth in the prescribed
  direction; boxed in wax so acrylic leaves them free to flex.
- Every wire tag in the baseplate area must stand slightly off the model so acrylic flows
  under it and locks it in.

## 4. Screws and expanders
Position expansion screws per design (e.g. midline for transverse expansion), with the
guide parallel to the occlusal plane and the arrow showing the opening direction. Wax over
the screw opening so acrylic doesn't lock it.

## 5. Acrylic baseplate (sprinkle-on / "salt and pepper")
1. Soak the model in water, then apply **separating medium (alginate seal)**.
2. Fix wires with sticky wax on the buccal side.
3. Alternate sprinkling self-cure polymer powder and wetting with monomer to build an even
   thickness (~2 mm), avoiding pooling.
4. Cure in a **pressure pot** with warm water (e.g. around 40–45 °C at ~2 bar for 10–20 min —
   check the acrylic's instructions) for a dense, bubble-free base.
5. Remove, trim to the outline with acrylic burs, smooth, then pumice and polish. Keep sharp
   wire ends clear of acrylic edges and soft tissue.

## 6. Vacuum-formed retainers
Trim the model, block out undercuts as prescribed, heat a thermoplastic sheet (thickness per
prescription, e.g. ~0.75–1 mm) in a thermoformer until it sags the specified amount, form,
cool, cut out with a disc or scissors, and smooth edges.

## Gotchas
- Arrowheads that sit too high don't engage; too deep they impinge on gingivae.
- Re-bent, nicked wire breaks in the mouth.
- Acrylic over spring coils immobilises them.
- Gauges and cure settings vary by protocol and material; numbers are typical.

## Verify
- [ ] Every clasp engages its undercut and seats passively on the model.
- [ ] Labial bow touches the incisors lightly, as prescribed (active or passive).
- [ ] Springs free to move; screw turns freely.
- [ ] Baseplate smooth, bubble-free and polished; no sharp edges.
- [ ] Appliance seats and is retained firmly on the model.
