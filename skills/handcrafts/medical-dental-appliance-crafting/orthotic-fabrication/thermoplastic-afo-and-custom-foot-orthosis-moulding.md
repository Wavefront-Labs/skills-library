# Thermoplastic AFO and custom foot orthosis moulding

**Goal:** an orthosis that controls the joint as the orthotist prescribed (rigid vs
flexible), fits the patient's model without pressure points, and has smooth, safe edges.
The orthotist (or podiatrist for foot orthoses) assesses, casts and fits; the technician
fabricates to their specification.

## 1. Model and rectification
- Fill the cast or print the scan to make a **positive model**; preserve marked landmarks
  (malleoli, navicular, metatarsal heads, fibular head, 5th met base).
- Rectify: **build up** over bony prominences for relief (malleoli, navicular, 5th met base)
  and smooth/reduce where the prescription calls for intimate contact or control. Keep
  measurements to the prescription.
- For foot orthoses, add any arch fill, heel expansion or forefoot extensions specified.

## 2. AFO drape/vacuum forming
1. Choose material per prescription: **polypropylene** (stiffer) or **co-polymer** (more
   flexible, tougher) in a thickness (commonly 3–5 mm) appropriate to the patient's size
   and the control needed.
2. Pull a thin stockinette over the model (helps vacuum and air escape); mount it on the
   vacuum pipe, toes down or as your setup requires.
3. Heat the plastic sheet in an oven until **uniformly clear/translucent** (polypropylene
   goes from white to clear) — typically around 180–200 °C for some time per mm; use the
   supplier's guidance. Under-heated plastic thins unevenly; over-heated bubbles and sags.
4. Lift by the edges with gloves, **drape** over the model in one motion, seal the seam at the
   back (pinch and cut), apply vacuum, and smooth it into contours with a wet cloth.
5. Let cool fully on the model under vacuum before removal (cooling too early distorts it).

## 3. Trim lines set the function
- **Rigid/solid AFO**: trim lines anterior to the malleoli, full-height posterior shell —
  resists dorsi- and plantarflexion.
- **Posterior leaf spring**: trim lines well posterior to the malleoli; narrow calf section
  — allows dorsiflexion, assists toe clearance.
- Add **corrugations** (formed ridges) at the ankle for stiffness if prescribed.
- Mark trim lines on the formed shell, cut with a cast saw or band saw, then grind and
  **smooth and round every edge** (finishing burrs, then heat-glaze edges carefully).

## 4. Strapping and padding
Rivet or bond straps (hook-and-loop with D-rings), padded where they cross the anterior shin.
Line with closed-cell foams (e.g. Plastazote) as prescribed.

## 5. Custom foot orthoses
1. Vacuum-form or press the shell (polypropylene, carbon composite, or EVA) over the
   rectified foot model.
2. **Posting**: add rearfoot posts (EVA or hard material wedges under the heel) and forefoot
   posts in the prescribed degrees (varus/valgus), ground flat so the orthosis sits stable.
3. Add top covers (EVA, leather, microfibre), extensions and pads (metatarsal domes) to the
   orthotist's/podiatrist's design.
4. Grind the sides to fit the patient's shoes.

## Gotchas
- Plastic thickness varies over curves; thin spots crack in use — check with callipers.
- Sharp trim edges cause skin injury, especially in insensate (e.g. diabetic) feet.
- Changing trim lines changes function; never trim beyond the marked lines without consultation.
- Temperatures and thicknesses are typical figures; follow the material datasheet.

## Verify
- [ ] Model rectifications match the prescription.
- [ ] Shell even in thickness; no thin spots or bubbles.
- [ ] Trim lines as marked; edges smooth and rounded.
- [ ] Posts flat on a level surface with prescribed angles.
- [ ] Orthosis seats on the model with no gaps over control areas.
