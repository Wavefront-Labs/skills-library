# Lens layout, edging and glazing spectacles

**Goal:** finished spectacles whose lenses deliver the prescription — power, axis, prism
and centration — at the wearer's eyes, fitted securely and safely in the frame. Prescriptions
come from an optometrist/ophthalmologist; dispensing and verification rules vary by country.

## 1. Inputs
- **Prescription**: sphere, cylinder, axis, add, prism (amount and base direction) per eye.
- **Dispensing measurements**: monocular **PDs** (distance and near), **fitting heights**
  (for progressives/bifocals), frame, lens type and material, coatings.
- **Frame**: check it's the correct model and undamaged; adjust to standard alignment first.

## 2. Verify uncut lenses
Use a **focimeter (lensmeter)**: confirm sphere, cylinder, axis and add match the order
**before** cutting. Cut lenses can't be returned. Mark the optical centre and the axis with
the focimeter's marker. Progressive lenses: re-ink the hidden engravings (with a
backlight/marker kit) to find fitting cross, prism reference point and add.

## 3. Layout (centration)
1. **Trace** the frame shape (frame tracer), giving the box dimensions (A, B), DBL (bridge)
   and shape.
2. Calculate **decentration** per eye: frame PD (A + DBL) ÷ 2 minus the monocular PD gives
   horizontal decentration in; vertically, place the optical centre (or fitting cross) at the
   specified height.
3. Prescribed **prism** can be ground in or obtained by decentration (Prentice's rule:
   prism dioptres = decentration in cm × lens power in dioptres) — usually only for small
   amounts and low powers; check practice policy.
4. Axis: align the cylinder axis on the layout blocker's protractor to the prescribed angle
   for that eye.

## 4. Blocking and edging
- Apply the **block** (adhesive pad + holder) at the layout point using the blocker; modern
  edgers use a centring/blocking device that combines layout and blocking.
- Select edge type: **bevel** (full-rim frames), **groove** (nylon supra/semi-rimless),
  **flat** with drilling (rimless).
- Select the material setting (CR-39, polycarbonate, Trivex, high-index, glass) — wrong
  settings chip or melt lenses.
- Edge, then **safety bevel** sharp edges; polish where visible (rimless, supra).

## 5. Glazing
- **Metal frames**: loosen the rim screw, insert, tighten; check the lens doesn't rotate.
- **Plastic (acetate) frames**: warm the frame in a frame heater or salt pan to expand the
  rim, push the lens in from the front. Don't overheat — acetate discolours or deforms.
- **Supra**: lens groove + nylon cord. **Rimless**: drill to the frame's hole pattern, mount
  with bushes, don't over-tighten (polycarbonate stress-cracks).
- Clean, then check for **strain** in a polariscope (glass and some plastics) if available.

## 6. Final check against tolerances
Re-check each lens on the focimeter in the frame: power, axis, prism at the wearer's PD
position, fitting heights for multifocals. Compare against your country's standard (e.g.
ISO 21987 / ANSI Z80.1 tolerances for power, axis, prism imbalance). Re-adjust frame alignment.

## Gotchas
- Axis errors matter more as cylinder increases; low-cylinder lenses tolerate more.
- Using binocular PD instead of monocular for asymmetric faces decentres both lenses wrong.
- Hot acetate stretches; lenses later pop out.
- Tolerances and prism policy are standard- and jurisdiction-specific; check the current standard.

## Verify
- [ ] Uncut lenses verified against the prescription before edging.
- [ ] Optical centres/fitting crosses at the measured PDs and heights.
- [ ] Axis within tolerance for each eye.
- [ ] Lenses secure with no rotation; edges smooth; no stress cracks at drill holes.
- [ ] Final focimeter check within tolerance; frame aligned.
