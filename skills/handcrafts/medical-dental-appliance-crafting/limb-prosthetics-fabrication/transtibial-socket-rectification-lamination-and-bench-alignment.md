# Transtibial socket: rectification, lamination and bench alignment

**Goal:** a below-knee prosthesis whose socket loads pressure-tolerant tissue and relieves
sensitive areas, made strong enough for the user's activity, and assembled in a sound
starting alignment for the prosthetist's dynamic alignment. The clinical prosthetist owns
assessment, casting, fitting and alignment decisions; the technician executes to their
prescription and modifications.

## 1. Model
- Cast (plaster bandage) or 3D scan of the residual limb, often over the liner the user
  will wear. Fill with plaster to make a **positive model**, or work digitally in CAD.
- Mark landmarks the prosthetist transferred: patellar tendon, tibial crest, fibular head,
  distal tibia, hamstring tendons.

## 2. Rectification (shaping the model)
- **PTB (patellar tendon bearing)** design: remove plaster at **pressure-tolerant** areas
  (patellar tendon bar, medial tibial flare, lateral shaft, popliteal area for counter-pressure)
  so the socket loads them; **add** plaster over **pressure-sensitive** areas (tibial crest,
  fibular head, distal tibia, hamstring tendons) for relief.
- **TSB (total surface bearing)** with gel liners: more uniform reduction (a set volume
  reduction, e.g. a few percent circumference, as prescribed) to load the whole surface.
- Smooth the model; check measurements against the original (circumferences at set levels,
  medio-lateral and A-P dimensions) and the prosthetist's sheet.

## 3. Check (test) socket
- Thermoform clear **PETG** or similar over the model (heated sheet draped/vacuum-formed).
- The prosthetist fits it on the user: looks for blanching, redness and gaps through the
  clear wall, and marks changes. Modify the model and repeat until fit is approved.

## 4. Definitive socket lamination
1. Seal the model, add any distal pad or soft insert as prescribed; pull on a **PVA bag**.
2. Lay up the reinforcement: layers of nylon/Perlon stockinette, fibreglass and/or
   **carbon-fibre** braid or cloth per the strength schedule (heavier users and higher activity
   need more; place carbon at the distal end and trim line regions where stresses concentrate).
3. Install the distal attachment (lamination plate / pyramid adaptor anchor) aligned roughly to
   the planned axis.
4. Pull an outer PVA bag, apply **vacuum**, and pour catalysed acrylic (or epoxy) laminating
   resin, working it through the layup to the distal end, then up; expel air and excess.
5. Cure fully; cut to the trim lines, sand edges smooth and rounded, and finish.
- Ventilation and PPE for resins and dust (respirator, gloves, eye protection).

## 5. Bench alignment
Starting alignment before the user walks on it:
- Socket set in slight **flexion** (often around 5° as a starting point) and slight
  adduction as per the prosthetist.
- Plumb line from the socket's reference point to the foot per the foot manufacturer's
  alignment recommendations (typically falling slightly anterior to the foot's reference).
- Prosthesis height to the prescribed length; foot toe-out as prescribed.
- Use an alignment jig/laser; tighten pyramid adaptor screws to the specified torque with
  thread locker as instructed.
The prosthetist then performs **dynamic alignment** while the user walks.

## Gotchas
- Over-reducing the model for "tight fit" causes skin breakdown — follow the prosthetist's numbers.
- Resin-starved areas are weak; resin-rich areas are heavy and brittle.
- Component torque and alignment figures come from manufacturer instructions; never guess.

## Verify
- [ ] Rectified model matches the prescribed measurements and modifications.
- [ ] Check-socket fit approved by the prosthetist.
- [ ] Laminate fully wetted, no voids or dry spots; edges smooth.
- [ ] Bench alignment recorded; screws torqued to spec with thread locker.
- [ ] Documentation (components, lot numbers, layup) recorded for traceability.
