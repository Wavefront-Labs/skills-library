# Silicone facial prosthesis: sculpting, moulding and colouring

**Goal:** an ear, nose or orbital prosthesis that matches the patient's anatomy and skin,
with thin, blending margins and secure retention. Done within a clinical team
(maxillofacial surgeon/prosthodontist, anaplastologist or maxillofacial prosthetist); the
patient's wellbeing and consent lead every decision.

## 1. Impression and model
- Clinician or prosthetist takes an impression of the defect site (and of the contralateral
  side for an ear, to mirror) with alginate or silicone impression material, supported
  so it doesn't distort tissue. Protect open cavities and airways.
- Pour in stone to make a **working model**. Increasingly, 3D scanning and printing provide
  the model and a mirrored starting form for the sculpt.

## 2. Sculpting in wax
1. Sculpt the form in sculpting wax (or modify a mirrored 3D print) on the model.
2. Try it on the patient: check symmetry, projection, angle and fit, viewed from all
   angles and at conversation distance, with the patient's glasses if worn.
3. Refine **margins** to be very thin and to lie in natural creases and shadows where possible.
4. Add surface texture (pores, wrinkles) by stippling and with textured tools/skin impressions.

## 3. Mould
- Make a **multi-part mould** (often three-part for ears) in dental stone or epoxy, with keys,
  so the silicone can be packed and the prosthesis removed without tearing thin edges.
- Retention components (magnets, clip housings for implant-retained bars) are positioned in
  the mould before packing.

## 4. Silicone and intrinsic colouring
1. Use medical-grade **platinum-cure silicone** formulated for facial prostheses. Mix
   parts A and B to the stated ratio.
2. **Colour-match** by adding pigments (intrinsic colour) to small batches and comparing
   against the patient's skin in natural daylight, with the patient present when possible.
   Build layers: a base skin tone plus regional variations (redness at the helix, veins,
   freckles), often with **flocking** (fibres) for depth.
3. Record the recipes by weight for remakes.
4. Pack the mould in layers (thin intrinsic colour areas first), close, and cure per the
   manufacturer (temperature and time).

## 5. Finishing
- Demould carefully; trim flash; keep margins thin.
- **Extrinsic colouring**: apply silicone-based paints or pigment in a silicone medium to the
  surface for final match and blending at the margins.
- Retention: medical adhesives (patient trained to apply and remove), anatomical undercuts,
  spectacle attachment, or **implant-retained** magnets/bar-and-clip (planned by the clinical team).

## 6. Delivery and aftercare
- Fit, check margins, colour in different lighting, and comfort.
- Teach application, removal, cleaning (mild soap), adhesive removal, and storage.
- Silicone prostheses degrade (colour fades, edges tear) and typically need replacement in
  roughly 1–2 years (varies with use, sun and care).

## Gotchas
- Colour matched under shop lighting can look wrong outdoors; check in daylight.
- Thick margins make the edge visible; thin margins tear sooner — a compromise.
- Silicone cure is inhibited by sulphur, tin and some latex — keep moulds and tools clean
  of contaminants (no latex gloves for platinum silicones).
- Materials and cure details must come from the specific product's IFU.

## Verify
- [ ] Symmetry and position approved by the patient and clinician at wax try-in.
- [ ] Silicone fully cured (no tackiness) and free of bubbles at the margins.
- [ ] Colour match accepted in natural daylight.
- [ ] Retention secure through normal movement; patient can apply and remove it.
- [ ] Colour recipes and materials recorded for remakes.
