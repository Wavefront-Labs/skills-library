# Blocking felt and straw hats and wiring brims

**Goal:** a blocked hat that holds its shape, fits the head and finishes neatly
inside — the core skill behind most structured millinery.

## 1. Measure and choose blocks
- **Head measurement:** tape around the head where the hat will sit (usually across
  the forehead, just above the ears, around the occipital bump). Hat blocks are sold by
  that circumference.
- **Crown block** shapes the crown; **brim block** the brim. Blocks are usually wood;
  cover them in cling film or foil first to protect them from stiffener and moisture.
- Felt **hoods** (cones for crowns, capelines with built-in brim for brimmed hats):
  wool felt is cheaper and stiffer; fur felt is finer, steams more easily, holds a
  sharper edge. **Sinamay** (abacá) and **parasisal** for straw work.

## 2. Blocking felt
1. Steam the hood thoroughly from inside (a steamer or kettle — careful, steam burns).
   Felt goes soft and stretchy when hot and damp.
2. Pull it down over the crown block, working evenly round; hold it with a
   **blocking cord/elastic** tied at the base, then pull out the remaining ease all round.
3. Work wrinkles out by steaming and stretching; push felt into the block shape.
   Push pins into the block below the cord line if needed.
4. Leave to dry fully on the block (overnight, often longer).
5. Apply **stiffener** (water- or spirit-based millinery stiffener) inside if more body
   is wanted; dry again.
6. Brim: steam and stretch over a brim block (or flat), pin, dry, stiffen.

## 3. Blocking straw (sinamay)
1. Cut 2–3 layers on the **bias** (sinamay stretches on the bias); dampen or steam,
   apply stiffener (or use pre-stiffened).
2. Stretch over the block, layers at angles, pin outside the finished line, dry.
3. Fine straws (parasisal): steam gently; they break if forced cold.

## 4. Cutting and wiring the brim
1. Mark the brim width with a gauge from the head-fitting line; cut cleanly.
2. **Millinery wire** (paper-covered or cotton-covered; heavier gauge for wider brims)
   stitched to the brim edge with a **wire stitch** (a blanket-type stitch over the wire),
   or machine zigzagged on sinamay.
3. Join wire ends with a **wire joiner** (a small metal sleeve, crimped with pliers)
   overlapped a few cm; place the join at the back.
4. Cover the edge: bias binding of the fabric, bound petersham, or a rolled sinamay edge.

## 5. Inside finish
- **Petersham headband** (curved/stretched with steam so it follows the head shape)
  stitched inside the headline; the join at the back left of centre.
- Optional sweatband for felt hats; a sizing ribbon to tighten fit.
- Mark the front; tilt and styling are part of design.

## 6. Trimming
Trims attach usually at the side or the back of the head; hand-stitch through the
hat with tiny stitches, not glue (glue shows and fails in heat). Fascinators fix with
a comb, headband or elastic.

## Gotchas
- **Shrinkage on drying:** felt shrinks slightly; size up blocks if in doubt.
- **Water marks** on felt from drips: steam evenly; dry with a pressing cloth.
- Over-stiffened hats crack; build in layers.
- Wire not joined smoothly = a bump in the brim edge.

## Verify
- [ ] Block matches head measurement; hood steamed evenly with no wrinkles.
- [ ] Fully dry before removing from the block.
- [ ] Brim cut evenly; wire stitched close with the joiner hidden at the back.
- [ ] Headband shaped and sewn; hat sits level and secure on the head.
