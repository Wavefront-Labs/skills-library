# Boning channels, busks and fitting a corset mockup

**Goal:** a corset that shapes the body comfortably, doesn't twist, wrinkle or pinch,
and survives the enormous tension of lacing.

## 1. Materials
- **Strength layer:** **coutil** (tightly woven herringbone or twill cotton, made for
  corsets) or a heavy cotton twill/canvas. Fashion fabric is laid over; it carries no
  stress. Stretchy or loosely woven fabric will fail.
- **Boning:**
  - **Spiral steel**: flexes in two planes; for curved seams and over the hips/bust.
  - **Flat steel**: rigid one way; at the back either side of the grommets, and beside
    the busk.
  - Synthetic whalebone / heavy polyester boning: lighter, for stays and costume.
  - **Rigilene** (woven plastic): only for very light support — it kinks under real
    tension; not suitable for waist reduction.
- **Busk**: front closure of two steels with loops and pins.
- **Grommets** (two-part, with washers) — not single-part eyelets, which pull out.
- **Waist tape**: non-stretch tape (e.g. 2.5 cm cotton or petersham) at the waistline,
  taking the strain off the seams.

## 2. Mockup (essential)
1. Make a toile in coutil or heavy twill with temporary boning (cable ties are a
   common cheap stand-in) and a busk or front opening, grommets or lacing strips at the back.
2. Lace onto the body with a **lacing gap** of around 5 cm at the back (lets it be
   adjusted and wear in).
3. Check:
   - Lacing gap **parallel** top to bottom. A V at the top = too tight at the top
     (or too loose at the bottom), etc.
   - No gaping at the top edge, no digging at hips or under the arms.
   - Waist reduction comfortable (a first corset rarely aims beyond 5–10 cm).
   - Seated: the lower edge shouldn't jab the thighs.
4. Pin out or let out at seams; transfer to the pattern; re-mockup if large changes.

## 3. Construction (one common method)
1. Cut panels on grain from strength fabric (and fashion fabric, flatlined).
2. Seam panels; press seams open or to one side.
3. **Boning channels:** sew twill tape or seam allowances into channels; channel width
   = bone width + ~2–3 mm. Stitch close but not so tight bones bind.
4. **Busk:** loop side: mark loop positions, stitch the front seam leaving gaps for the
   loops, insert, stitch close beside the busk to hold it. Pin side: punch small holes
   with an awl (not cutting threads) for the pins, push through, stitch beside.
5. **Waist tape** stitched along the waistline inside, between the busk and back.
6. **Grommets** at the back, set beside a flat steel on each side, spaced ~2–2.5 cm
   (sometimes closer at the waist). Use an awl to part threads rather than punching
   a hole where possible.
7. Measure each channel; cut bones ~6 mm shorter than the channel; tip steels.
8. **Bind** the top and bottom edges with bias binding of coutil or the fashion fabric.

## 4. Lacing
Two laces meeting at the waist with "bunny ear" loops for tightening from the
centre outward. Season a new corset: wear lightly laced for a few short sessions
before lacing it down.

## Gotchas
- **Wrinkles across panels** = fabric not strong enough or panels off grain.
- **Bones poking out** at the ends — not tipped or channels unbound; add bone caps or
  extra binding.
- **Twisting**: uneven panels left to right, or seam allowances not matched.
- Health: tight lacing compresses organs and restricts breathing; don't sleep or
  exercise in one; stop if numbness or pain.

## Verify
- [ ] Mockup fitted laced, lacing gap parallel and ~5 cm.
- [ ] Strength layer coutil (or equivalent), panels on grain.
- [ ] Spiral and flat steels placed correctly; bones tipped and shorter than channels.
- [ ] Busk, waist tape and grommets secure under full tension.
- [ ] Wearer reports no pinching when sitting, standing and breathing deeply.
