# Accurate piecing, basting, quilting and binding

**Goal:** blocks that come out exactly their size, points that meet, and a finished
quilt that lies flat with straight edges.

## 1. Cutting
- **Rotary cutter, self-healing mat, acrylic ruler.** Square the fabric edge first;
  cut strips across the width, then subcut.
- Use the ruler's lines, not the mat's (mat lines are often inaccurate).
- Blade guard closed every time you put it down — rotary cutters cause serious cuts.
- Starch fabric before cutting to stop bias edges (triangles) stretching.
- Most quilt patterns are in inches; work in the pattern's units throughout.

## 2. The seam that makes or breaks it
- Standard: **¼ inch** (6–7 mm). Because the fold of pressed seams takes a sliver,
  many quilters sew a **scant ¼"** — a thread's width less.
- **Test:** sew three 1½" × 3½" strips together, press. The centre strip must measure
  exactly 1". Adjust needle position or a seam guide until it does.
- **Chain piece** (feed pairs one after another without cutting thread) for speed.

## 3. Pressing
- **Press, don't iron** (lift and lower; don't push — pushing stretches).
- Press seams **to the dark side**, or **open** for complex blocks with many points.
- **Nest seams:** pressed in opposite directions on joining rows, they butt together
  and line up intersections. Pin at the intersections.

## 4. Common units
- **Half-square triangles (HSTs):** two squares right sides together, draw diagonal,
  sew ¼" each side, cut, press. Make them a little oversize and **trim down** to size.
- **Flying geese:** four-at-a-time method or stitch-and-flip; trim to size.
- **Appliqué:** needle-turn (hand), fusible with blanket or zigzag edge (machine).
- Measure each block; they should be the unfinished size (finished + ½").

## 5. Sandwich and basting
1. Backing and batting at least 10 cm larger than the top on all sides.
2. Tape the backing taut (not stretched) wrong side up on a floor or table.
3. Smooth batting, then the top.
4. Baste: curved **safety pins** every hand-width; or **spray baste** (ventilate);
   or thread-baste a grid for hand quilting.
- **Batting:** cotton (flat, shrinks slightly — crinkly vintage look after washing),
  polyester (loft, light), wool (warm, resilient), blends. Check the maximum quilting
  distance on the packet (e.g. "quilt up to 20 cm apart").

## 6. Quilting
- **Walking foot:** straight lines, stitch-in-the-ditch, gentle curves; feeds all
  layers evenly. Start from the centre and work outward.
- **Free-motion:** darning/free-motion foot, feed dogs lowered, you move the quilt.
  Speed of hands and machine must match for even stitch length. Practise on sandwiches.
- **Hand quilting:** short *betweens* needle, rocking stitch, a hoop or frame.
- Long-arm quilting for large quilts (hire or service).

## 7. Binding
1. Cut strips (commonly 2¼"–2½"), join on the diagonal, press in half lengthwise.
2. Trim quilt edges square.
3. Sew binding raw edges to the quilt front, ¼" seam, starting mid-side.
4. **Mitred corners:** stop ¼" from the corner, fold binding up 45° then down along
   the next side.
5. Join ends, fold to the back, hand-stitch (slip stitch) or machine-stitch in the ditch.
Add a **label** (maker, date, place).

## Gotchas
- Blocks the wrong size: seam allowance or pressing error — test seam first.
- **Wavy borders:** measure through the **centre** of the quilt for border length and
  ease the edges to fit, not the other way.
- Puckers on the back: basting too sparse, or quilting from the edges inward.

## Verify
- [ ] Strip test passes (centre strip exactly 1").
- [ ] Blocks measure their unfinished size; points meet.
- [ ] Borders cut from centre measurements; quilt square.
- [ ] No puckers on back; quilting within batting's max spacing.
- [ ] Binding full and even, corners mitred.
