# Drafting a bodice block and fitting a toile

**Goal:** a fitted **block** (sloper) that matches one body exactly, proven in cloth,
from which any number of styles can be developed without re-fitting from scratch.

## 1. Measure properly
Over close-fitting underwear, with an elastic tied at the natural waist:
bust, high bust, underbust, waist, hip (and depth below waist), nape-to-waist,
front-neck-to-waist, shoulder length, across back, across front, bust point
separation and depth (shoulder/neck point to apex), neck circumference, armhole
depth. Take each twice; record standing naturally — people tend to inhale and pull in.

## 2. Draft the block
Use a published drafting system (Aldrich and Armstrong are the standard references;
each has its own step sequence) — follow **one** system exactly; mixing steps from
different books gives mismatched curves.
Common structure:
1. Construct a rectangle: half-bust + ease wide, nape-to-waist long.
2. Mark armhole depth, across-back/front lines, neck curves and shoulder slopes.
3. Draw the armscye with a French curve through constructed points.
4. Place the **bust dart** (from shoulder or side) to the apex, and waist darts.
5. True all seams: shoulder seams same length, side seams same length (minus dart
   intake), neck and armhole curves smooth across the shoulder join.
**Ease:** a block typically includes minimal wearing ease (a few cm at bust) — check
your system; styles add design ease on top.

## 3. Toile
1. Trace the block, add seam allowances (e.g. 1.5 cm; 2.5 cm on side seams for
   letting out), mark **grainlines** and **balance notches**.
2. Cut in calico/muslin of similar weight to the intended fabric. Mark the centre
   front, bust line, waist line and grain with pen on the cloth.
3. Machine-baste with a long stitch; press seams open. Zip or pin closed at centre
   back/front.

## 4. Fit (work top-down)
Order: neck → shoulders → bust → armhole → waist → length.
- **Drag lines point to the problem.** Pulls toward the bust = not enough room there
  (full bust adjustment); horizontal folds at the back waist = sway back (take a wedge).
- Check the grain lines: horizontal lines should be level; centre lines vertical.
- Pin out excess; slash and insert strips where tight. Mark changes directly on the toile.
- Sit, reach, raise arms: the block must allow movement.
- Often 2–3 toiles are needed.

## 5. Back to paper
Unpick the toile, press, lay on the pattern and transfer every change. **Re-true**
seams. The revised paper is now the master block; keep it on card.

## 6. Dart manipulation
- **Pivot method:** pin at the apex, rotate the pattern to close one dart and open
  another — a shoulder dart can become a side, French, or neckline dart, or a
  gathered or princess seam.
- **Slash and spread** for fullness (gathers, flares).
- Dart points stop 2–3 cm short of the apex for a natural shape.

## Gotchas
- Measuring over bulky clothes inflates everything.
- Forgetting to true seams after changes → seams that don't match when sewn.
- Stretch fabrics need a separate block with negative ease; don't use a woven block.

## Verify
- [ ] Measurements taken twice; block drafted from one system.
- [ ] Seams trued; notches match.
- [ ] Toile fits with level grain lines and no drag lines while moving.
- [ ] Changes transferred and the master block re-trued and labelled.
