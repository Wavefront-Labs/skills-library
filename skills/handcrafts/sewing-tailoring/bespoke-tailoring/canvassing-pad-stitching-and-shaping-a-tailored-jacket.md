# Canvassing, pad stitching and shaping a tailored jacket

**Goal:** a jacket front that holds a three-dimensional shape — lapel rolls softly,
chest fills out, no collapse or bubbling — through decades of wear. Methods vary
between tailoring traditions (London, Neapolitan, etc.); this is a generalised
full-canvas approach.

## 1. Construction choices
- **Fused:** adhesive interfacing on the fronts. Fast, flat, can bubble with age
  and cleaning.
- **Half-canvas:** floating canvas in the chest and lapel; fused below.
- **Full canvas:** floating **hair canvas** the full front, attached only by hand
  stitching. Most shaping; the bespoke standard.

## 2. Prepare cloth and canvas
- **Shrink** both cloth and canvas first (steam or damp-press) — they will shrink when
  pressed later otherwise.
- **Canvas layers:** body canvas (wool/hair blend) + **chest piece** of horsehair
  canvas + **domette** (soft flannel) or felt over it to stop hair poking through.
  Cut the chest piece on the canvas, with a dart cut and closed to give a cup at the
  chest.
- Darts in canvas are **lapped and zigzagged**, never sewn as cloth darts (too bulky).

## 3. Iron work (shaping the cloth)
Wool shrinks and stretches under steam and pressure:
- **Shrink** the fullness at the front shoulder and chest hollows, **stretch** the
  front edge at the waist and the back shoulder blade area, with a damp cloth,
  iron and a **tailor's ham**. Set with a **clapper** (press heat and steam out with the
  wooden block) so the shape holds.
- This is the hardest part to learn from text; each cloth reacts differently — test
  on scraps of the same cloth.

## 4. Attach canvas and pad stitch
1. Baste the canvas to the front wrong side along the **roll line** and through the
   body in long lines, smoothing over a ham so canvas follows the shaped cloth.
2. **Bridle tape:** a linen tape sewn along the roll line, slightly **tight** (around
   0.5–1 cm shorter over its length, depending on chest shape) so the lapel hugs the
   chest. Hand-felled both edges.
3. **Pad stitching** the lapel: small diagonal (chevron) stitches through canvas,
   catching only a thread of cloth, in rows parallel to the roll line, while
   **rolling the lapel over your fingers**. This builds the permanent roll. Stitches
   get smaller toward the edge for firmness.
4. Trim canvas out of the seam allowances; tape the front edges (linen tape) so they
   don't stretch.

## 5. Collar
- **Under-collar** of melton or cloth, with collar canvas, pad-stitched along the
  stand and fall with the stand curving; shape with iron work (stretch the fall's
  outer edge so it lies on the shoulders).
- Top collar hand-felled at the gorge; the collar and lapel roll lines must meet as
  one continuous line.

## 6. Fittings
1. **Baste fitting:** jacket held with basting, no lapel finish, one sleeve; check
   balance (front/back length), shoulder pitch, chest, waist suppression.
2. **Forward fitting:** more finished, sleeves pitched; confirm collar and sleeve
   pitch.
3. Final fitting. Each fitting can mean ripping and rebuilding parts.

## Gotchas
- Canvas pulled tight = lapel can't roll; too loose = sag. Smooth over the ham, not flat.
- Pad stitches that show through on the lapel's right side: you caught too much cloth.
- Forgetting to shrink cloth/canvas → puckers after the first press.
- Pressing with a too-hot iron on wool shines it; always use a press cloth.

## Verify
- [ ] Cloth and canvas pre-shrunk; chest piece darted and covered.
- [ ] Bridle taped, slightly tight; lapel rolls on its own.
- [ ] Pad stitching invisible on the outside.
- [ ] Collar and lapel roll lines continuous.
- [ ] Front hangs balanced at fittings; no drag lines.
