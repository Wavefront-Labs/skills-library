# Surface stitches, transfer, hooping and goldwork basics

**Goal:** embroidery with even stitches, smooth fills and no puckering — made to be
used and washed, not just framed.

## 1. Materials
- **Ground fabric:** firm, even-weave linen or cotton for surface embroidery;
  Aida/evenweave for counted work (cross-stitch, blackwork). Prewash if the finished
  piece will be washed.
- **Thread:** **stranded cotton** (six strands, separated and recombined — use 1–3
  strands for most work; strip strands one at a time and put them back together so
  they lie flat), perlé cotton, **crewel wool** for crewelwork, silk for fine shading,
  metal threads for goldwork.
- **Needles:** *embroidery/crewel* (sharp, long eye); *tapestry* (blunt) for counted
  work; *chenille* (sharp, large eye) for wool and plunging metal threads.

## 2. Transfer the design
- **Light box/window** tracing with a water-soluble or heat-erasable pen (test that it
  actually removes from your fabric — some reappear in cold).
- **Water-soluble stabiliser** printed with the design, stitched through, dissolved.
- **Prick and pounce** for traditional work: prick the outline in paper, rub pounce
  powder through, paint the dots into a line.
- Heat transfer pencils for dark-to-light (permanent; lines must be covered).

## 3. Tension: hoops and frames
- **Hoop:** bind the inner ring with cotton tape so it grips. Fabric **drum-tight**.
  Remove the hoop when you stop for the day (hoop marks).
- **Slate frame / scroll frame** for large and goldwork pieces: fabric laced
  under even tension; frees both hands (one above, one below the fabric — "stab
  stitching").
- Puckering after release = fabric not tight enough while stitching, or threads pulled
  too hard.

## 4. Core stitches
| Stitch | Use |
|---|---|
| **Backstitch / split stitch** | Fine outlines; split stitch also as a padding edge under satin. |
| **Stem stitch** | Curved lines, stems. |
| **Chain stitch** | Bold lines; fills. |
| **Satin stitch** | Smooth solid shapes; outline with split stitch first for a crisp edge; keep each shape under ~1 cm wide or it snags. |
| **Long-and-short** (needle painting) | Shading; first row long and short alternating, subsequent rows split into previous. |
| **French knot** | Dots; wrap once or twice — more wraps make a messy knot; use more strands for a bigger one. |
| **Couching** | Laying a thread down and tying it with small stitches; base of goldwork. |
- **Start/finish:** waste knot (knot on the front, away from the work, cut later) or
  small holding stitches inside the shape. No knots on the back of washables if you can
  help it — they show through and come undone.

## 5. Goldwork basics
- **Jap gold / passing thread:** couched in pairs with fine waxed silk, stitches every
  3–4 mm, staggered like brickwork. **Plunge** the ends through to the back with a
  large needle or lasso, and secure.
- **Purls** (hollow coiled wire): cut into short lengths on a velvet board and sewn
  on like beads.
- **Padding** with felt or string under gold gives relief.
- Handle metal threads minimally; skin oils tarnish them. Real gold threads are
  costly; imitation and gilt are common for practice.

## 6. Finish
- Wash (if fabric and threads allow) to remove marks; roll in a towel.
- **Press face down** on a thick towel so stitches aren't flattened.
- Lace over board for framing; or construct into the item.

## Gotchas
- Too many strands = lumpy; too few = fabric shows through fills.
- Carrying threads across the back behind light fabric shows through — end and restart.
- Dark threads may bleed: test colourfastness before washing.

## Verify
- [ ] Design transferred with a mark you've tested removes.
- [ ] Fabric drum-tight; no puckering after release.
- [ ] Satin edges crisp; shading blended; knots tidy.
- [ ] Back neat with secured ends; no long carried threads.
- [ ] Pressed face down; marks gone.
