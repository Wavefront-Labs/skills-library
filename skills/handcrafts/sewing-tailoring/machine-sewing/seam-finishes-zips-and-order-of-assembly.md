# Seam finishes, zips and order of assembly

**Goal:** garments that look professionally made inside and out, built in an order that
means you never have to sew into a corner you can't reach.

## 1. Machine setup
- **Needle** to fabric: universal 70–80 for medium wovens; **microtex/sharp** for
  silks and tightly woven fabrics; **ballpoint/jersey** for knits; **stretch** for
  lycra; **denim/jeans** 90–100 for heavy fabric. New needle every project (or ~8 hours).
- **Stitch length:** 2.5 mm standard; 3–4 mm topstitching and heavy fabric; 4–5 mm
  basting; 1.5 mm reinforcing corners.
- **Tension:** test on two layers of the actual fabric. Top thread loops on the
  underside = top tension too loose (or misthreaded — rethread with presser foot UP so
  the thread enters the tension discs).
- Knits: a narrow zigzag or stretch stitch, or an overlocker, so seams don't crack.

## 2. Seam finishes
| Finish | Best for |
|---|---|
| **Overlocked** | Fast, most fabrics; standard ready-to-wear finish. |
| **Zigzag** | Overlocker substitute; sew within the allowance and trim. |
| **Pinked** | Stable wovens that barely fray. |
| **French seam** | Sheers and lightweights; encloses the raw edge: sew WRONG sides together at ~6 mm, trim to 3 mm, press, fold RIGHT sides together, sew at ~6–10 mm. |
| **Flat-felled** | Shirts, jeans; strong, double-stitched, reversible. |
| **Hong Kong / bound** | Unlined jackets, thick wools; edges bound in bias strip. |
Choose before cutting — French and flat-felled seams need the right allowance.

## 3. Prep stitches
- **Staystitching:** single row inside the seam allowance on curved and bias edges
  (necklines, armholes) right after cutting, to stop stretching.
- **Understitching:** stitch facing and seam allowances together near the seam so the
  facing rolls to the inside invisibly.
- **Press every seam** before crossing it with another — flat, then open (or to one side).

## 4. Zips
**Invisible/concealed zip** (skirts, dresses):
1. Insert **before** sewing the seam below it.
2. Press the zip coils flat (low heat) so the teeth open out.
3. With an invisible-zip foot, stitch each tape with the coil in the foot's groove,
   right sides together, from top down to the slider.
4. Close the zip; sew the remaining seam from the bottom of the zip downward with a
   regular zipper foot, starting a few mm to the side of the last stitch.
**Lapped zip:** one side covers the teeth — a standard for trousers fly-fronts and
vintage dresses. **Centred:** baste the seam closed, press open, stitch the zip
under it from the right side, remove basting.
Interface the zip seam allowances on lightweight or stretchy fabric to stop rippling.

## 5. Order of assembly (typical dress or blouse)
1. Staystitch; mark darts and notches.
2. Darts, tucks, pleats.
3. Shoulder seams (back to front).
4. Neckline facing or collar (neck is easier while sides are open).
5. Sleeves set **flat** (before side seams) for shirts; **in the round** for fitted
   set-in sleeves (sew sides first).
6. Side seams (and sleeve seams in one if set flat).
7. Zip, waist seam if present.
8. Hems last — after hanging bias-cut garments 24 hours to drop.
Principle: **work flat for as long as possible**; finish each seam right after sewing it.

## Gotchas
- **Skipped stitches** on knits: wrong needle type.
- **Puckered seams:** stitch too long/short, tension high, or pulling the fabric.
- Not clipping curves: inward curves need clips, outward need notches.
- **Grain** errors on cutting can't be fixed by sewing.

## Verify
- [ ] Needle and stitch tested on scraps; tension balanced.
- [ ] Every raw edge finished before the next step covers it.
- [ ] Each seam pressed before crossing.
- [ ] Zip lies flat, invisible zip's coil hidden, seam below continuous.
- [ ] Hems level when worn.
