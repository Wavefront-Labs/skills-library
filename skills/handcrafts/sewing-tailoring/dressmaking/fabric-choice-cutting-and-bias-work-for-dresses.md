# Fabric choice, cutting and bias work for dresses

**Goal:** dresses that hang the way the design intends — which is decided mostly
before the first stitch, at fabric choice and the cutting table.

## 1. Match fabric to design
Read the pattern's suggested fabrics and judge **drape**, **body** and **stretch**:
- **Structured** silhouettes (A-line, fitted bodices, pleats): cotton poplin, linen,
  wool crepe, brocade, taffeta.
- **Fluid** silhouettes (bias skirts, cowls, gathers): rayon/viscose challis, silk
  crepe de chine, charmeuse, georgette, rayon crepe.
- Hold the fabric up and let it fall over your hand; compare with how the design
  should move. A stiff fabric in a draped design looks boxy; a fluid one in a
  structured design collapses.
- Knits need patterns designed for stretch (check the stretch percentage required).

## 2. Prepare
- **Pre-wash/preshrink** the way you'll launder the dress: cottons and rayon can shrink
  5–10%. Dry-clean-only silks and wools: steam shrink.
- **Straighten the grain:** tear or pull a thread across the width to find the true
  cross-grain; if off-grain, pull diagonally to square it (only works on fabrics
  without a permanent finish).
- Press out the centre fold crease — it often doesn't wash out; avoid placing it on
  a visible spot.

## 3. Layout
- **Grainline arrows** parallel to the selvedge — measure at both ends.
- **Nap / one-way layout** for velvet, corduroy, satin, one-way prints: all pieces
  point the same way (costs more fabric).
- **Stripes and plaids:** match at side seams, centre fronts and sleeve heads; cut
  single layer; mark the stripe on the pattern's notches. Buy 15–25% extra.
- Large prints: place motifs deliberately (avoid bullseyes on the bust).

## 4. Cutting and slippery fabrics
- Cut **single layer** for slippery fabrics; lay on tissue paper or a cutting mat,
  or sandwich between tissue; pin through all. A **rotary cutter and weights**
  disturb the fabric less than scissors.
- Spray starch or gelatin stabiliser on silks before cutting (washes out — test first).
- Mark with tailor's tacks or chalk; no wax on silk.
- Use fine pins (silk pins) and a fresh microtex needle.

## 5. Bias cut
- Pieces cut at 45° to the grain drape and cling — the classic 1930s gown.
- **Hang** bias pieces (or the assembled garment) 24–48 hours before hemming; the
  hem drops unevenly as gravity stretches the bias. Level the hem on a dress form or
  the wearer.
- **Seams:** stretch slightly as you sew or use a tiny zigzag so seams don't crack
  when the fabric stretches. Stay-tape necklines.
- Expect bias-cut sizing to differ — the fabric grows narrower and longer when hung.

## 6. Lining, underlining, facings
- **Lining** (separate layer, hangs free): slides on and off, hides seams.
- **Underlining** (cut identically and basted to each piece): adds body to thin
  fabrics, makes sheer fabric opaque, gives hand-stitched hems something to catch.
- **Facings** finish edges; interface them with a weight lighter than the fabric.
- Always test interfacing on a scrap: fusibles can bubble or change the hand.

## Gotchas
- Not buying enough for matching or one-way layout.
- Off-grain cutting = twisting seams after washing; can't be fixed later.
- Pressing silk with too-hot iron: water spots and shine. Use a silk organza press
  cloth.

## Verify
- [ ] Fabric drape matches the design's movement.
- [ ] Preshrunk, grain straightened, grainlines measured at both ends.
- [ ] Prints/nap/stripes laid out deliberately and matched at seams.
- [ ] Bias pieces hung before hemming; hem levelled on the body/form.
