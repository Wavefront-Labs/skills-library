# Hand-stitch repertoire, hems and handworked buttonholes

**Goal:** know which hand stitch to reach for, and work it so it's invisible where it
should be and strong where it must be.

## 1. Kit and thread handling
- **Needles:** *sharps* (general), *betweens* (short, for fine hems and quilting),
  milliners/straws (long, for basting). Smaller number = larger needle in most
  ranges (except some specialty types).
- **Thimble** on the middle finger of the sewing hand; push with its side.
- Thread: polyester all-purpose for general; silk thread for basting couture fabrics
  (it doesn't mark when pressed) and for hand hems; buttonhole twist for buttonholes.
- **Length:** forearm length — longer tangles and wears out.
- **Beeswax** then press with a warm iron (wax into the thread) to stop knots and
  tangles; useful for buttons and buttonholes.

## 2. The stitches and when
| Stitch | Use |
|---|---|
| **Basting / tacking** | Temporary holding; long running stitches. |
| **Tailor tacks** | Transferring pattern marks through two layers. |
| **Backstitch** | Strong hand seam; repairs. |
| **Prick stitch** | Tiny surface backstitch for hand-set zips and topstitch look. |
| **Slip stitch** | Joining a folded edge to fabric invisibly (linings, facings). |
| **Ladder stitch** | Closing a seam from the right side invisibly (stuffed items, turned openings). |
| **Blind hem** | Hem caught under the folded edge; nothing shows on the right side. |
| **Catch / herringbone** | Flexible hem on knits and heavier wool; holds raw-edged hems. |
| **Felling** | Securing linings; strong, small slanted stitches. |
| **Whipstitch / overcast** | Joining edges; preventing fraying by hand. |
| **Blanket stitch** | Edge finishing; thread bars, belt loops (over a thread bar). |

## 3. Invisible hems
1. Press the hem up; finish the raw edge (overlocked, pinked or turned).
2. Fold the hem edge back 5 mm toward you.
3. Take a tiny bite of **one or two threads** of the garment, then a small stitch in the
   hem allowance 5–10 mm along. Keep thread **slightly loose** — tight hand hems
   pucker and "dimple" the right side.
4. Every 10 cm, take a small knot or backstitch so a snag doesn't release the whole hem.

## 4. Buttons
- Mark positions through the buttonholes after they're made.
- **Thread shank** on coats: place a pin or matchstick on top of the button, stitch over
  it, remove, wrap the thread round the stitches under the button to form a shank as
  long as the buttonhole layer is thick.
- Four-hole buttons: sew in two parallel bars or a cross; 4–6 passes per pair of holes,
  doubled waxed thread.

## 5. Handworked keyhole buttonhole
1. Mark length (button diameter + thickness, plus ~3 mm). Machine-stitch a small
   rectangle around the line to stabilise; cut the slit; punch or cut the keyhole eye
   at the end the button sits.
2. Overcast the cut edges lightly to stop fraying.
3. Lay a **gimp** (heavy cord) along the edge to pad it.
4. Work **buttonhole stitch** (needle into the slit, out beyond the gimp, thread looped
   under the needle point so a purl knot sits on the cut edge) close together along
   one side, fan round the keyhole, along the other side.
5. Finish the square end with a **bar tack** (a few long stitches across, blanket-stitched
   over).
Practise on scraps: even purls take dozens of buttonholes to master.

## Gotchas
- Knots on the right side — start with a hidden knot inside the hem or a backstitch.
- Polyester thread on silk can cut fibres over time; match strength to fabric.
- Pressing basting thread (polyester) can leave marks; use silk basting thread.

## Verify
- [ ] Stitch chosen for purpose (temporary/invisible/strong/flexible).
- [ ] Hem invisible from the right side and not puckered.
- [ ] Buttons sit with an appropriate shank and don't wobble.
- [ ] Buttonhole edges fully covered with even purls; ends bar-tacked.
