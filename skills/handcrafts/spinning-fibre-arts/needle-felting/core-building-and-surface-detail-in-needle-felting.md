# Core building and surface detail in needle felting

**Goal:** a firm, well-shaped needle-felted piece that holds detail and doesn't
sag or fluff — built economically, with fingers intact.

## How it works
Felting needles have small barbs near the tip. Each stab drags surface fibres into
the interior and tangles them. **No water involved** — shaping is purely from where
and how deep you stab.

## 1. Tools
- **Needles by gauge:** lower number = thicker = faster, coarser. **36** for roughing
  in the core, **38** general shaping, **40–42** for fine detail and finishing.
  Shapes: triangle (standard), star (four barbed edges, faster), twisted/spiral
  (smooth finish), **reverse** (barbs point up; pull fibres out to make fur/fuzz).
- **Work surface:** dense foam pad, brush mat or wool mat. Foam degrades; replace
  when crumbly (crumbs work into the piece).
- **Finger guards** (leather or silicone). Needles are sharp and barbed; a stab to the
  finger is the common injury. Keep a tetanus booster current.

## 2. Build the core
1. Use **cheap, coarse core wool** (or batting) for the interior; save expensive
   coloured merino for the surface. Corriedale-type wool felts fast and firm.
2. Roll the wool **tightly** into the rough shape, like a sleeping bag, then stab to
   hold the roll.
3. Stab **straight in and straight out** at the same angle. Bending a needle while
   it's in the wool snaps it.
4. Turn the piece constantly and stab all over; shape by stabbing more where you
   want it to shrink or dent.
5. Firmness: squeeze it. It should barely compress. Soft cores sag and pill; most
   beginner pieces are under-felted.

## 3. Join parts
- Leave **fluffy ends** on legs, ears, limbs; don't felt the joining end.
- Spread the fluffy fibres over the body and stab them in at angles. Add a wisp of
  wool across the seam to hide it.
- **Armatures** (pipe cleaners, chenille stems, wire) for thin legs or posable limbs:
  wrap tightly with wool before felting. Wire pieces aren't for small children.

## 4. Surface and detail
- Cover the core with a thin layer of coloured wool, stabbing shallowly with a 38–40.
- Details (eyes, nose, markings) with the 40–42: twist a tiny amount of wool between
  fingers into a thread, lay it on, stab in.
- **Eyes:** glass or safety eyes set into a pre-stabbed socket with a dab of glue;
  or felted wool eyes.
- Finish by going over the whole surface with a fine/twisted needle at shallow depth
  to smooth fuzz.

## 5. 2D needle felting (wool painting)
- Use a felt or wool fabric base on a foam pad. Lay thin wisps of colour and stab
  shallowly; layer colours like painting.
- Work light to dark or background to foreground; blend colours by pulling wisps together.
- Lift the work off the pad often, or it felts **into** the foam.

## Gotchas
- **Broken needles:** usually from levering sideways. Keep the stroke vertical.
- **Lumpy surface:** too much wool at once; add thin layers.
- **Too slow:** you're using a fine needle on the core. Start coarse.
- **Fuzzy halo:** finish more with a fine or twisted needle; trim with small scissors.

## Verify
- [ ] Core firm when squeezed; shape stable.
- [ ] Joins invisible and strong — pull gently and nothing moves.
- [ ] Surface smooth with intended detail.
- [ ] No broken needle tips left in the piece (run a magnet over it).
