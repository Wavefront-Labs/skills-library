# Sculpting figures with felting needles

**Goal:** firm, well-proportioned needle-felted figures that hold their shape, with
clean surface colour — without breaking needles or stabbing yourself.

## How it works
Barbed needles push fibres down into each other with each stab, tangling them. More
stabs = firmer and smaller. No water involved.

## Tools
- **Felting needles** come in gauges (higher number = finer) and cross-sections:
  - ~**36 gauge** (coarse): fast shaping of cores.
  - ~**38 gauge** (medium): general work.
  - ~**40–42 gauge** (fine): surface finishing, fine details, smoothing.
  - **Triangle** (3 edges of barbs), **star** (4, felts faster), **twisted/spiral** (fast,
    smooth finish), **reverse** (barbs pull fibres out — fuzzy texture, fur).
- A **foam pad**, wool felting mat or brush mat to work on.
- Optional multi-needle holder for flat areas.
- **Finger guards** (leather or silicone) — strongly recommended.

## Wool
- **Core wool** (coarse, cheap batting, e.g. corriedale or mixed-breed carded wool) for the
  body — it felts quickly and firmly.
- Finer coloured wool (merino or similar) for the surface layer.
- Carded batts felt faster than combed top, which is slippery and best for surface wisps.

## Building a core
1. Roll core wool tightly into the rough shape (tighter roll = less stabbing).
2. Stab all over, rotating constantly, straight in and straight out. Keep the needle angle
   the same coming out as going in — **bending or prying** is how needles snap.
3. Firm the core to roughly 2/3 of the final firmness; it should spring back slightly when
   squeezed.
4. Add wool to bulk areas; shape by stabbing more where you want indentations (necks, waists).

## Limbs, ears and attaching
- Felt limbs separately, leaving the attachment end fluffy (unfelted).
- Spread the fluffy end over the body and stab at angles through the join until it's
  invisible and solid. Add a wisp of wool over the seam.
- **Armatures**: pipe cleaners or wire wrapped in wool for poseable limbs or thin parts
  (tails, legs). Fold back wire ends so they can't poke out — not for small children's toys.
- Ears: felt flat on the mat (lift and turn often so it doesn't felt into the mat), leave
  the base fluffy to attach.

## Surface and detail
- Lay a thin wisp of coloured wool over the core, stab with a fine needle shallowly to fix it.
  Several thin layers cover better than one thick one.
- Details (eyes, nose, stripes): tiny rolled bits of wool, shallow stabs with a 40+ gauge.
  Glass/safety eyes are set in with glue — again not for small children.
- Finish by stabbing the whole surface lightly with a fine or twisted needle to smooth fuzz.

## 2D needle felting
- Felt wool onto a felt or wool fabric background; works like painting with fibre. Use a foam
  pad underneath and lift often.

## Safety
- Needles are very sharp and barbed; stabs go deep. Watch the needle, not your phone.
- Store needles in a tube or pad; keep away from children and pets.
- Broken tips can lodge in the work — find and remove them before giving the piece away.

## Gotchas
- Lumpy surface: surface wisps too thick, or coarse needle used for finishing.
- Floppy figure: under-felted core; keep stabbing.
- Gauge-to-use mappings vary slightly by needle maker.

## Verify
- [ ] Core firm with spring; shape built gradually.
- [ ] Limbs attached through fluffy ends with no visible seam.
- [ ] Surface colour in thin layers; finished with a fine needle.
- [ ] No needle tips lost in the piece; armature ends safely enclosed.
