# Park-and-draft and supported-spindle spinning

**Goal:** even singles from a spindle — the cheapest, most portable way into spinning,
and the best way to learn what twist and drafting actually do.

## 1. Choose the spindle
- **Weight sets the yarn:** heavier spindles (60–100 g+) suit thick yarn; light ones
  (15–30 g) suit fine yarn. Fine yarn on a heavy spindle snaps; thick yarn on a light
  spindle won't get enough spin. Around 30–40 g is the usual beginner compromise.
- **Top whorl:** hooked shaft, spins fast; flicked by rolling the shaft on your thigh.
- **Bottom whorl:** spins slower and longer; flicked with fingers.
- **Turkish:** cross-arm whorl; you wind a centre-pull ball directly as you spin.
- **Supported** (tahkli, Russian, etc.): rests tip in a bowl; for very fine, short
  fibres (cotton, cashmere) that can't bear a hanging spindle's weight.

## 2. Leader and fibre
1. Tie ~45 cm of plied yarn to the shaft, wrap it a few times, take it over the whorl
   and secure with a **half hitch** at the top (bottom whorl) or through the hook (top whorl).
2. Use commercial **combed top** or a well-carded roving for a first try; split it
   lengthwise into thin strips and **pre-draft** (gently pull to loosen).
3. Fluff the end of the leader and overlap it with the fibre.

## 3. Park-and-draft (learn the parts separately)
1. **Spin:** flick the spindle clockwise (Z twist), let twist build up in the leader.
2. **Park:** catch the spindle between your knees or under your arm so it stops.
3. **Draft:** pinch just above the twist with one hand; with the other, pull a short
   length of fibre out of the supply. Slide the pinch up and let the twist run into
   the drafted fibre.
4. Repeat until you have an arm's length of yarn. Then un-hitch and wind on.
Once the hands are confident, stop parking — draft while the spindle spins and
drops.

## 4. Winding a cop
- Wind the yarn onto the shaft in a cone (**cop**): wider near the whorl, tapering.
  Wind firmly — a loose cop slides and tangles.
- Re-tie the half hitch each time.
- Keep the cop balanced; a lopsided cop wobbles.

## 5. Supported spinning
- Rest the tip in a small bowl (lap or table). Spin with a finger flick, draft
  long-draw style from a rolag or punis with the twist running into the drafting zone.
- Fine fibres need **lots of twist** — supported spindles spin fast for that reason.
- Cotton especially: very short staple; prep as punis and draft with light hands.

## Gotchas
- **Drop and unwind** (spindle reverses): you let it spin too long. Catch it while it's
  still turning the right way.
- **Yarn breaks above the spindle:** too thin for the spindle's weight; draft thicker
  or use a lighter spindle.
- **Fibre won't draft:** hands too close together — keep them apart by more than the
  staple length, or the fibre is held at both ends.
- **Wobble:** shaft bent, hook off-centre or cop lopsided.
- Spin over carpet or grass while learning: hard floors chip whorls.

## Verify
- [ ] Spindle weight suits the target yarn.
- [ ] Leader holds; half hitch secure; no slipping.
- [ ] Park-and-draft produces a full arm's length of continuous yarn.
- [ ] Cop is firm and conical.
- [ ] Relaxed singles doubled back twist into a moderate 2-ply (twist adequate).
