# Drop spindle: park and draft

**Goal:** spin a consistent singles yarn on a suspended (drop) spindle by separating the
twisting and drafting steps, then move to continuous spinning once your hands learn it.

## Choosing a spindle
- **Weight**: heavier spindles (roughly 60–100 g) suit thicker yarns and beginners; light
  spindles (under ~30 g) spin fine yarn. Heavy spindles pull fine yarn apart.
- **Top-whorl**: whorl near the hook at top; spins fast, flick on your thigh.
- **Bottom-whorl**: whorl at the bottom; slower, steadier, often longer spin.
- **Turkish spindle**: arms that slide out leaving a ready-wound centre-pull ball.
- Spindle balance matters more than look: it should spin without wobble.

## Fibre
- Start with medium wool combed top or roving (e.g. Corriedale, Romney, Shetland) — easy
  to draft, grippy. Pre-draft it: split lengthwise into thin strips and gently loosen.

## Attach the leader
- Tie a ~45 cm length of yarn to the shaft above the whorl (bottom-whorl) or below (top),
  spiral it up to the hook/notch with a **half-hitch** at the tip.
- Fan out the end of the leader, overlap with fibre, and spin to catch fibre onto it.

## Park and draft
1. Spin the spindle **clockwise** (Z-twist by convention) and let it build twist in the
   leader and a short length of fibre.
2. **Park** the spindle — clamp it between your knees or under your arm — so it can't
   unspin.
3. Pinch the yarn with the front hand where twist ends; with the back hand, draft a length
   of fibre out (a few cm) from the supply. Fibres should slide, not break.
4. Slide the front-hand pinch back up toward the supply, letting twist run into the newly
   drafted section.
5. Repeat: spin, park, draft, release twist, until the yarn is about arm's length, or the
   spindle nearly touches the floor.

## Winding on
- Undo the half-hitch, wind the singles onto the shaft in a cone or "cop" shape (above the
  whorl for bottom-whorl; under it for top-whorl), leaving enough to re-hitch.
- Keep the cop tight and shaped; a lumpy cop wobbles.

## Joining
- When the fibre supply runs low, fan out its end and overlap with a new fanned end, draft
  through the overlap with twist running in. Joins should be invisible after plying.

## Moving on
- Once park-and-draft feels automatic, keep the spindle hanging and spinning while you
  draft — **spinning continuously**. Re-spin before it slows or it reverses ("backspin")
  and undoes twist.
- **Supported spindles** (tahkli, Russian, Tibetan-style): rest in a bowl, spun by flicking
  the shaft; fine short fibres (cotton, cashmere) spin well; tahkli is classic for cotton.
- **Plying on a spindle**: wind singles into a two-strand ball, or use an Andean plying
  bracelet to ply from one end of a single.

## Common problems
- **Yarn breaks and spindle drops**: not enough twist before drafting, or drafted too thin.
  Add twist, draft less.
- **Overtwisted kinky yarn**: too much spin for the length drafted; draft longer per spin.
- **Spindle reverses**: you let it run down; park sooner.
- **Fibre won't draft**: hands too close together (closer than the staple length) — move them
  apart.

## Gotchas
- Weight suggestions are approximate; spindle balance and personal style vary.
- Some spinners begin S-twist; consistent direction is what matters, then ply opposite.

## Verify
- [ ] Spindle chosen to suit the intended yarn thickness.
- [ ] Twist enters only after drafting (park-and-draft) until confident.
- [ ] Cop tight and shaped; joins secure.
- [ ] A self-plied sample shows consistent twist along the length.
