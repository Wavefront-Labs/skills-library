# Drafting, ratios and tension on a spinning wheel

**Goal:** singles of a planned thickness and twist, consistent from the first bobbin
to the last, rather than "whatever came out".

## 1. Know the wheel's three settings
- **Drive ratio** (which whorl groove): how many times the flyer turns per treadle.
  Higher ratio (e.g. 12:1+) = more twist per treadle = fine yarn. Lower (6–8:1) =
  thicker yarn. Find yours by marking the wheel and flyer and counting turns per
  wheel revolution.
- **Take-up tension** (brake band): how hard the bobbin pulls yarn in.
  - **Scotch tension** (flyer-led): a brake on the bobbin; adjust the tension knob.
  - **Irish tension** (bobbin-led): strong pull; suits thick yarn.
  - **Double drive**: one band drives both; tension comes from band tightness and
    the whorl/bobbin size difference.
- **Treadling speed:** keep it steady; change the ratio, not your foot, for more twist.

## 2. Set up
1. Tie a **leader** (a metre of plied commercial yarn) to the bobbin, through the
   orifice.
2. Set tension low: the leader should be pulled in gently, but you can hold it still
   without it snapping out of your hand.
3. Treadle clockwise for Z-twist singles (the usual convention; ply S).

## 3. Drafts
**Short forward draw (worsted-style)** — for combed top:
- Front hand pinches at the twist point; back hand holds the fibre supply.
- Front hand pulls a short length of fibre forward from the supply, then slides
  back, letting twist run into the drafted fibre. Twist **never** enters the supply.
- Smooth, dense, strong yarn.

**Long draw (woollen-style)** — for carded rolags:
- Let a little twist into the supply, then the back hand draws back a long arm's
  length in one smooth movement; the twist evens out in the thin spots first.
- Feed onto the bobbin quickly. Light, airy, warm yarn; needs a good rolag and a
  lower ratio at first. Many beginners learn a "short draw" then progress.

## 4. Consistency: the control card
- Wind a sample of your target singles around a card and keep it in view; compare
  every few minutes.
- **Wraps per inch (WPI):** wrap yarn around a ruler without packing; count wraps in
  2.5 cm. Roughly: 9–11 worsted weight, 12–14 DK, 14–16 sport, 18+ fingering
  (plied numbers vary by source — treat as approximate).
- **Twist check:** let a length of singles double back on itself. It should twist
  into a soft 2-ply with a moderate angle. Kinks tightly = overtwisted; drifts apart
  = underspun.

## Troubleshooting
| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Yarn won't wind on | Take-up too low, or yarn snagged on hooks | Raise tension a touch; move to next hook |
| Yarn yanked from hand | Take-up too high | Back tension off |
| Yarn drifts apart | Not enough twist | Slower hands or higher ratio |
| Kinky, hard yarn | Too much twist | Draft faster/longer or lower ratio |
| Wheel reverses | Treadling uneven / footman sticking | Smooth rhythm; start with a hand nudge |
| Lumps | Prep uneven or fibre caught | Better prep; pre-draft |

## Gotchas
- **Fill bobbins evenly** — move the yarn along hooks every few minutes or the bobbin
  loads in a lump and take-up changes.
- **Tension creeps** as the bobbin fills (fuller bobbin pulls harder in Scotch
  tension): ease off as you go.
- Oil the wheel at the maker's points; a dry wheel is a heavy one.
- Spin a **sample, ply it and wash it** before a big project; finished yarn differs
  from singles.

## Verify
- [ ] Drive ratio known; leader attached; tension adjusted with the leader alone.
- [ ] Draft chosen to match prep (top→short forward, rolags→long draw).
- [ ] Singles match the control card in WPI and twist along the bobbin.
- [ ] Sample plied and washed before committing the whole fleece.
