# Drafting, ratios and consistent singles

**Goal:** singles of a planned thickness and twist that stay consistent across a whole
project — the foundation of every handspun yarn.

## The wheel's systems
- **Flyer and bobbin**: the flyer inserts twist; the bobbin winds the yarn on because it
  turns at a different speed.
- **Tension systems**:
  - **Scotch tension (flyer-led)**: drive band turns the flyer; a brake band on the bobbin
    controls take-up. Easy to adjust; most common modern system.
  - **Irish tension (bobbin-led)**: drive band turns the bobbin; brake on the flyer. Strong
    take-up — good for bulky yarns.
  - **Double drive**: a single long band loops twice to drive both flyer and bobbin with
    different whorl sizes; take-up adjusted by overall band tension. Smooth once tuned.
- **Ratio**: how many times the flyer turns for one treadle. Larger ratio = more twist per
  treadle = suits fine yarns. Lower ratio for bulky yarns.

## Setting up
- Oil the wheel per the maker's guidance (flyer shaft, bearings).
- Attach a leader (a length of yarn tied to the bobbin shaft, through the orifice).
- Adjust take-up: with treadling steady, the leader should draw into the orifice firmly but
  allow you to hold it still without strain. Too much pull = yarn yanked on before twist
  builds; too little = overtwist and kinks.
- Treadle slowly and evenly; practise starting in the correct direction (usually clockwise
  for Z-twist singles).

## Drafting methods
- **Short forward draw (worsted/inchworm)**: front hand pinches at the orifice side, back
  hand holds fibre. Front hand pulls a short length forward from the fibre supply, then slides
  back to let twist in, smoothing as it goes. Twist never enters the drafting zone.
  Smooth, dense yarn. Best with combed top.
- **Short backward draw**: back hand draws fibre back while front hand controls twist —
  semi-woollen.
- **Long draw (woollen)**: twist is allowed into the drafting zone; the back hand draws back
  a full arm's length while twist holds the thin places and drafts the thick spots. Light,
  airy, lofty yarn. Best with rolags; feels magical when it works, frustrating at first.
- Feed the yarn on; repeat.

## Measuring consistency
- Make a **control card**: wind a short sample of the target singles (and a plied sample)
  around a card. Compare every few minutes by holding new yarn beside it.
- **Wraps per inch (WPI)**: wrap the yarn snugly around a ruler without packing; count wraps
  in an inch. Indicates grist (thickness class).
- **Twist check**: let a length of singles double back on itself; it twists into a 2-ply.
  The angle and twists per inch of that self-plied sample should stay similar.
- Treadles per draft: count treadles per drafting length (e.g. 4 treadles per 10 cm draft) —
  same count = consistent twist.

## Common faults
- **Drifting thinner over time**: hands speed up; count treadles.
- **Breaks**: underspun (too little twist), or drafted too thin; slow drafting or treadle more.
- **Kinky overtwist**: take-up too weak or drafting too slow; increase take-up or draft faster.
- **Slubs**: uneven prep — fix the fibre, not the hands.

## Finishing a bobbin
- Fill evenly: move the yarn along the flyer hooks (or use a sliding hook/WooLee-style
  level winder) so it doesn't build up in one place.
- Rest bobbins for a few hours before plying (twist sets a bit, easier to handle) — optional.

## Gotchas
- A slipping drive band is often misread as tension trouble.
- Ratio and WPI guidance are general; your yarn plan should come from a sample.

## Verify
- [ ] Take-up adjusted; leader draws in firmly without tugging fibre from your hands.
- [ ] Drafting method matches the prep and intended yarn.
- [ ] Control card made; WPI and twist checked regularly.
- [ ] Bobbins filled evenly.
