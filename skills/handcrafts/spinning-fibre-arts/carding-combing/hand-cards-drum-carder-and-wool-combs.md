# Hand cards, drum carder and wool combs

**Goal:** turn clean locks into a preparation that drafts evenly — airy rolags for
woollen yarn, aligned top for worsted — and blend colours or fibres on the way.

## The core distinction
- **Carding** (cards, drum carder) leaves fibres somewhat jumbled with air between
  them → spun woollen-style → light, warm, elastic yarn. Keeps short fibres.
- **Combing** (combs, flick carder) aligns fibres parallel and removes short bits
  (**noils**) → spun worsted-style → smooth, strong, lustrous yarn.
Pick the tool from the fibre's staple and the yarn you want (see
`handcrafts.spinning-fibre-arts.fleece-preparation.skirting-scouring-and-judging-a-raw-fleece`).

## Hand cards
1. Match the carding cloth to the fibre: fine cloth (around 100+ teeth per inch) for
   fine wools, coarser (about 70 tpi) for medium and longer wools. Wrong cloth tears fine fibre.
2. Load **thinly**: catch tips of teased locks on the left card's teeth, cut ends near
   the handle, until the card face is lightly, evenly covered. Overloading is the #1
   beginner error.
3. Card: the right card brushes **lightly** across the left — the teeth barely mesh;
   you are not scrubbing. 3–5 strokes, transfer the fibre to the other card, repeat
   until the web looks even.
4. Roll off a **rolag** with the back of a card from heel to toe, or roll the web along
   a dowel. Rolags spun from the end = classic long-draw woollen.

## Drum carder
- Feed **small tufts**, tips first, onto the licker-in (small drum); let the big drum
  take them. Feeding too much clogs the licker-in and makes neps.
- Fine fibre: feed slowly and consider a finer cloth (72 tpi general, 120+ for fine
  and blends). Check the maker's guidance.
- Pull the batt off with the doffer through the gap in the cloth, split it into
  strips and **card it again** — two or three passes for an even batt or a blend.
- Strip into roving with a **diz** (a button with a hole) or roll small **punis** for
  short fibres like cotton or angora.
- Clean the drum with a flick brush between colours.

## Wool combs (and mini-combs)
1. Lash locks onto a fixed comb, cut ends in, about a third of the way up the tines.
2. Swing the free comb **across the tips** in arcs, starting at the tips and working
   deeper. Never jam into the middle — that breaks fibre.
3. Transfer back and forth (usually 3–4 passes) until aligned.
4. **Diz off**: pull a continuous sliver of top through a diz, sliding fibre off in
   pulls of a few cm. Leave the short, tangled waste (noils) on the comb — expect
   20–40% waste; that is the price of worsted prep.
- Combs are sharp; work away from your body and park them guarded.
- A **flick carder** (a small card) tapped against lock tips and butts is a quick way
  to open individual locks for worsted spinning.

## Blending
- On a drum carder, layer colours for a heathered batt, or keep stripes for
  colour-change yarns. More passes = more uniform.
- Blend fibres of similar staple length; mixing short and long fibres makes the short
  ones fall out or pill.

## Gotchas
- **Neps** (little knots) come from overloading, tender wool, second cuts, or carding
  too hard. Pick them out or start with better prep.
- **Static** with fine wool in dry air: lightly mist with water (some use a dilute
  hair-conditioner spray — uncertain whether it affects later dyeing).
- Never card or comb damp wool — it rusts cloth and tears fibre.

## Verify
- [ ] Prep type matches yarn intent (carded→woollen, combed→worsted).
- [ ] Rolags/batts are even with no visible clumps or neps.
- [ ] Combed top is parallel, shiny and short-fibre free; noils set aside.
- [ ] A test draft pulls out smoothly without clumping.
