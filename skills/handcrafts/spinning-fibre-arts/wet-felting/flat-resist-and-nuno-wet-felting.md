# Flat, resist and nuno wet felting

**Goal:** felt that is **fully fulled** — dense, even, and doesn't pill or pull apart
— in the size you planned, from flat pieces to seamless vessels and silk-backed scarves.

## Why wool felts
Wool fibres have scales. Warm water + soap (alkaline, slippery) + agitation make
fibres migrate and lock; shock and pressure then shrink them tighter. **Felting**
(fibres entangle) and **fulling** (the felt shrinks and densifies) are two stages; most
failed felt was felted but never fulled.

## 1. Fibre and layout
- Fine **merino tops** (around 18–21 micron) felt fastest; coarser wools felt slower
  and rougher but are sturdier for slippers and rugs. Superwash wool **won't felt**.
- Lay thin wisps in **overlapping rows like roof tiles**, each layer at **90°** to
  the last. 2 layers for a scarf, 4+ for bags, 6–8 for slippers.
- Even thickness matters more than total thickness: thin spots become holes.
- **Shrinkage:** typically 30–50% depending on wool and how hard you full. Make a
  sample square (e.g. 20×20 cm laid out), full it, measure, and size the project by
  that ratio. Don't trust a generic percentage.

## 2. Wet out and felt
1. Cover with netting (curtain net). Sprinkle **warm** soapy water (olive-oil soap or
   a little dish soap) — enough to wet through, not flood.
2. Press flat with your palms to push out air.
3. Rub gently through the net in small circles, ~5 minutes per area, light pressure.
   Turn over and repeat.
4. **Pinch test:** pinch surface fibres. If they lift away, keep rubbing. When the
   fibres hold and only the whole skin lifts, it's **pre-felt / felted**.

## 3. Full
- Roll in bubble wrap or a bamboo mat around a pool noodle: 50–100 rolls, unroll,
  turn 90°, repeat from each direction to shrink evenly.
- Then throw the piece onto the table, knead, rub on a washboard. Hot water
  dip / cold rinse speeds shrinkage.
- Shape while fulling: stretch where too small, work edges to firm them.
- Stop when it has shrunk to size and the surface is firm.

## 4. Resist (seamless 3D)
- Cut a **resist** from floor underlay foam or thick plastic, enlarged by your
  shrinkage ratio.
- Lay wool on side A, wet, fold overhanging edges over the resist, flip, lay side B
  and fold its edges over to A. Alternate layers for even seams.
- Felt as flat work, then cut an opening and **pull the resist out** once pre-felt;
  full by hand from the inside to shape (hats over a hat block, slippers on the foot
  or a last).

## 5. Nuno felting
- Lay a thin, even wool layer on (or under) open-weave silk (chiffon, gauze, silk
  around 5 mm momme). Wool fibres migrate **through** the fabric.
- Rub gently and longer than you think — check the back: wool fibres should be
  visible through the silk before rolling.
- Shrinkage of the cloth makes the characteristic ruched texture.
- Tightly woven fabric won't let fibres through; test first.

## Gotchas
- **Too much water** floats the layout; too hot/rough too early = layers felt
  separately and delaminate.
- **Holes** = thin layout. Patch with wisps while still at pre-felt stage.
- Rinse soap out well (a dash of vinegar in the last rinse), then dry flat.

## Verify
- [ ] Sample square measured; project sized from it.
- [ ] Layout even, layers crossed.
- [ ] Pinch test passed before fulling.
- [ ] Finished felt shrunk to plan, firm, no loose surface fibres, edges solid.
- [ ] Nuno: wool visibly through the silk, fabric doesn't peel off.
