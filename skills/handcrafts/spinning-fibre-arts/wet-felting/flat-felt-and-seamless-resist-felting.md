# Flat felt and seamless resist felting

**Goal:** strong, even felt with no thin spots, shrunk to a planned size — first as a
flat sheet, then around a resist for seamless 3D forms (bags, vessels, slippers).

## How felting works
Wool fibres have scales; with **moisture, heat, alkaline soap and agitation** they migrate
and lock together, then shrink as they tighten. Fine wools (Merino ~18–23 micron) felt
easily and softly; coarser wools felt firmer and slower. Superwash wool **won't felt**.

## Layout for flat felt
- Work on bubble wrap (bubbles up) or a textured mat, with a towel beneath to catch water.
- Pull **thin wisps** of tops (fingers well apart, about staple length) and lay them
  overlapping like roof tiles in one direction.
- Lay the next layer **at right angles**. Two to four layers for a thin scarf-weight felt;
  more for thick slipper soles. Even thickness matters more than total amount: hold it up
  to the light (before wetting) — thin spots show.
- Optional decoration on top: yarns, silk fibres, prefelt shapes.

## Wetting
- Cover with netting (nylon curtain net) or a thin plastic sheet.
- Sprinkle **warm soapy water** (olive oil soap or a felting soap; dissolved) over it, press
  gently with flat hands to push water through and air out. Wet through but not swimming.

## Prefelt stage
- Rub lightly in small circles through the net, gradually increasing pressure, working
  over the whole area for 10–15+ minutes. Lift the net periodically so fibre doesn't felt to it.
- **Pinch test**: pinch some surface fibres and lift. If the top layer lifts away separately,
  keep rubbing. When fibres lift the whole piece as one, it's **prefelt** — holding together
  but not shrunk yet.
- Flip and rub the reverse.

## Fulling (hardening and shrinking)
- Roll the felt in the bubble wrap around a pool noodle or dowel; roll back and forth
  ~50–100 times, unroll, turn 90°, and repeat from all four directions and both sides —
  so shrinkage is even.
- Then throw it onto the table, knead, or rub on a washboard to full hard. Hotter water
  speeds it up.
- Wool typically shrinks around 30–50% in each direction depending on fibre and fulling —
  make a sample to measure your **shrinkage rate**.
- Rinse in alternately hot and cold water (shock helps the final fulling), then rinse out the
  soap; a splash of vinegar in the last rinse neutralises alkali. Shape and dry flat.

## Seamless forms over a resist
1. Cut a **resist** (template) from foam floor underlay or thick plastic, scaled up by the
   shrinkage rate (e.g. 40% shrinkage → resist about 1.67× finished size).
2. Lay out layers on one side of the resist, letting fibre extend a couple of cm past the edge.
   Wet, flip (with the net), fold the overhang neatly over the resist edges onto the dry side.
3. Lay out the second side, overlapping onto the folded edges; wet, flip, fold. Repeat for
   the planned number of layers — always alternate fibre direction.
4. Rub until prefelt, paying special attention to **edges** (they're thin and seams hide there).
5. Cut an opening, remove the resist, then full the form from inside and out, shaping over a
   last, bowl or your hand as it shrinks.

## Gotchas
- Holes and thin spots: uneven layout or edges not folded enough.
- Ridges at resist edges: rubbed too hard on edges early, or edges folded thick.
- Felt that won't firm up: not enough agitation, cold water, or superwash fibre.
- Shrinkage figures are ranges; measure from a sample of the same wool.

## Verify
- [ ] Layers thin, even and crossed; checked against light before wetting.
- [ ] Pinch test passed before fulling.
- [ ] Fulled from all directions; size matches the shrinkage plan.
- [ ] Rinsed, soap removed, shaped and dried.
