# Skirting and scouring raw fleece

**Goal:** clean, open, sound fibre ready for carding or combing — with the grease out and
no felting. Fleece prep largely determines how easy the spinning is and how the yarn looks.

## Judge the fleece
- **Staple length**: pull a lock; measure unstretched. Short (under ~5 cm) suits carding
  for woollen spinning; longer staples suit combing for worsted.
- **Crimp**: the waves in the lock. Fine wools have many small crimps per inch; coarser wools
  fewer, larger waves. Crimp relates roughly to fineness and loft.
- **Soundness**: hold a lock at both ends and flick it near your ear. A sound lock "pings"; a
  **tender** lock snaps at a weak point (break), often from illness or stress in the
  sheep's year. Tender fleece spins poorly — use for felting instead.
- **VM** (vegetable matter: hay, burrs, seeds): a little is normal; a lot means hours of picking.
- **Second cuts** (short bits from re-shearing) cause neps (lumps) in yarn; shake them out.

## Skirting
- Lay the fleece cut side down on a slatted table or mesh screen, outside if possible.
- Remove the dirty edges: belly wool, britch (back legs) and anything with dung tags,
  heavy VM or very short, matted bits. Shake to drop second cuts.
- Optionally sort by grade: shoulder (often finest/most consistent) vs back and britch.

## Scouring (washing out grease)
Raw wool carries **lanolin** (grease) plus **suint** (sweat salts) and dirt.
- Felting needs **heat + moisture + agitation** (and sudden temperature change). Scouring
  uses heat and moisture, so avoid agitation and shocks.
- Work in mesh laundry bags or lingerie bags in a basin, bucket or sink.
1. Optional cold **soak** (overnight, plain water) to dissolve suint and loosen dirt.
2. Fill with **hot** water — hot enough to melt lanolin, around 60–70 °C (very hot tap or
   kettle-topped). Add a wool scour or a strong dish detergent (generous amount for
   greasy fine wools).
3. Lower the fibre in gently, press it under, and **leave it**: 15–20 minutes. Don't stir.
4. Lift out (don't let it cool in the water or grease redeposits). Drain, gently press.
5. Repeat with fresh hot soapy water (fine/greasy fleeces often need 2–3 washes), then
   rinse in water at the **same temperature** until clear.
6. Spin out in a washing machine **spin cycle only** (no agitation) or press in towels.
- Fine wools (e.g. Merino) are greasiest and felt fastest — smaller batches, more soap.

## Drying
- Spread thin on a screen or towel out of direct hot sun; turn occasionally. Dry fully before
  storage — damp wool goes mouldy.

## Opening the fibre
- **Teasing/picking**: pull locks apart by hand (or a picker) to open and drop remaining VM.
- **Flick carding**: open the tips and butts of locks individually with a flick card for
  lock spinning or combing.

## Storage
- Store in breathable cotton bags or sealed bins after freezing (a few days) if moth risk.
  Clothes moth larvae love wool; cedar and lavender deter only mildly.
- Label with breed, date, weight and sheep/supplier.

## Gotchas
- Felted lumps: agitation or a temperature shock. Felt is irreversible; use it for felting.
- Sticky "tacky" wool after drying = grease left in; rewash hotter.
- Washing temperatures are common practice ranges; wool scour makers' instructions govern.

## Verify
- [ ] Soundness tested by flick; tender fleece set aside.
- [ ] Skirted of dung tags, heavy VM and second cuts.
- [ ] Scoured hot without agitation; rinsed at matching temperature.
- [ ] Fully dry, not tacky; stored moth-safe and labelled.
