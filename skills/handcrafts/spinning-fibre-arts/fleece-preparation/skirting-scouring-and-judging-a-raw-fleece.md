# Skirting, scouring and judging a raw fleece

**Goal:** clean, sound, unfelted wool sorted by quality, ready for carding or
combing — and an honest call on whether the fleece was worth buying at all.

## 1. Judge before you pay (or before you wash)
Unroll the fleece cut-side down on a slatted table or clean sheet.
- **Staple length:** pull a lock and measure. Roughly 5–10 cm suits most hand prep;
  under ~4 cm is hard to comb (card it); over ~15 cm (longwools) wants combs or
  flicking, not cards.
- **Soundness (break test):** hold a lock at both ends and flick it sharply near your
  ear. A ping and no snap = sound. A crack or the lock parting at one point = a
  **tender** fleece (stress or illness weakens a band of the fibre). Tender wool
  pills and breaks; use it only for felting.
- **Crimp:** fine, close crimp (merino-type) spins fine, elastic, lofty yarn; open
  wave (longwools) spins lustrous, drapey yarn.
- **Vegetable matter (VM):** a little hay is normal; burrs and seed heads through the
  whole fleece mean hours of picking. Walk away from heavy VM unless the fleece is special.
- **Cotting:** matted, felted areas on the sheep's back. Light cotting can be teased
  open; solid cotts are waste.

## 2. Skirt
Remove the belly wool, leg and britch (rear) wool, dung tags, heavily stained or
cotted edges and anything with burrs. Expect to lose 10–30% by weight. Pull out
**second cuts** (short snippets where the shearer went over twice) — they make neps
(little lumps) in the yarn. Sort the best (usually shoulder and side) from the rest
if you want consistent yarn.

## 3. Scour without felting
Felting needs **heat + moisture + agitation + temperature shock**. Washing uses the
first two, so the rule is: **no agitation, no sudden temperature changes.**
1. Optional cold soak overnight in plain water to lift suint (sheep sweat) and dirt.
2. Fill a tub with water **hot enough to melt lanolin** — around 60–70 °C, hotter for
   fine greasy wools like merino. Lanolin re-solidifies on the fibre if the water cools.
3. Add a wool scour or plain washing-up liquid (enough to feel slippery). Lay the wool
   in in mesh bags or loose; push it under gently; **do not stir**.
4. Soak 15–20 minutes, lift out, let drain (don't wring). Refill with water at the
   **same temperature** and repeat until the water stays fairly clear (fine wools
   may need 3–4 washes, longwools 1–2).
5. Rinse once or twice at the same temperature, no detergent.
6. Spin out in a washing machine **spin cycle only** (no water flowing in), or press in
   towels.
7. Dry flat on a rack in shade, turning once. Store only when bone dry.
Uncertainty: "wash in the washing machine on wool cycle" advice exists; it felts
fleece often enough that this skill doesn't recommend it.

## 4. Choose the prep
- **Short, crimpy, fine** → hand cards or drum carder → woollen spinning.
- **Long, lustrous** → combs or flick carder → worsted spinning.
- See `handcrafts.spinning-fibre-arts.carding-combing.hand-cards-drum-carder-and-wool-combs`.
- Some spinners spin longwool locks "in the grease" (unwashed) and wash the yarn — it
  works but leaves sticky hands and dirt in the yarn.

## Gotchas
- **Water too cool** = waxy, sticky wool that gums up cards. Check with a thermometer.
- **Pouring hot water directly onto the wool** is agitation — add wool to water.
- **Damp storage** = mould and moth. Moths love unwashed and dirty wool; freeze
  new fleeces in sealed bags for a few days if moths are a worry.
- **Dye later** — scour first; grease resists dye.

## Verify
- [ ] Staple measured and break-tested before committing hours to it.
- [ ] Belly, britch, tags and second cuts removed.
- [ ] Wool lifts out of the final rinse clean, with no waxy feel when dry.
- [ ] Locks still separate cleanly — no felting.
- [ ] Prep (cards vs combs) chosen from staple and crimp, not habit.
