# Two-ply, Navajo ply and setting twist

**Goal:** a balanced, finished yarn — plied in the opposite direction to the singles,
skeined, washed and measured — ready to knit or weave.

## Why ply
- Plying adds strength, evens out thin and thick spots, and balances twist. A singles has
  energy (it wants to twist back); plying opposite cancels it out.

## Two-ply
1. Put two full bobbins on a **lazy kate** (tensioned kate if available) behind you or
   at your side, so singles feed smoothly without snarls.
2. Tie both onto the leader. Treadle **opposite** to the spinning direction (singles spun
   Z/clockwise → ply S/anticlockwise).
3. Hold the singles apart with fingers of the back hand (a finger between them), front hand
   guiding the forming plied yarn. Let twist run up to the back hand's fingers, then let the
   plied yarn feed on.
4. Keep tension equal on both singles; uneven tension makes one ply wrap around the other
   (corkscrew).
5. **Balance check**: pull a length off and let it hang in a loop. Balanced yarn hangs
   straight or twists a turn or less; if it twists the plying direction, it's overplied;
   if it twists the singles direction, underplied. Adjust treadling per length.
- Leftover single on one bobbin? Wind it into a ball and ply from both ends, or chain ply it.

## Chain ply (Navajo ply)
A three-ply from one singles, keeping colour sequences intact (great for gradient-dyed fibre).
1. Tie the singles to the leader and make a large loop (like a crochet chain).
2. Reach through the loop and pull the singles through to make a new loop, about arm's
   length. Let twist run into the three strands of the loop.
3. Repeat, keeping the loop sizes consistent. Joins (loop transitions) are visible as small
   bumps if snatched; smooth them by easing through.
- Treadle slowly at first; this is a hand-coordination skill.

## Cabled and art yarns (brief)
- **Cabled**: overply two 2-ply yarns in the singles direction for a round, durable yarn.
- **Art yarns** (coils, beehives, core spinning): deliberately unbalanced or textured; plan
  for their behaviour in the finished item.

## Skeining
- Wind off the bobbin onto a **niddy-noddy** (or skein winder). Tie the skein loosely in
  four places with figure-8 ties so it can't tangle in water.
- **Yardage**: count wraps × wrap length (e.g. a 1.5 m-around niddy-noddy × wraps). Record
  weight and length; calculate metres per 100 g to compare with commercial yarn weights.
- WPI on the finished yarn also helps classify (lace, fingering, DK, worsted, bulky...).

## Setting the twist (finishing)
- Soak the skein in warm water with a little wool wash, 20+ minutes until fully wet.
- Squeeze out (don't wring); then finish according to the yarn:
  - **Gentle**: hang to dry, unweighted — preserves loft.
  - **Thwacking**: whack the skein against a surface to bloom woollen yarns.
  - **Light fulling**: alternate hot and cold water with agitation for a sturdier, slightly
    felted yarn (wool only; carefully).
  - **Weighting** while drying hides overtwist — it temporarily straightens yarn but the energy
    returns when knitted, so better to fix plying than weight heavily.
- Dry fully; twist into a hank for storage, label with fibre, weight, length and WPI.

## Gotchas
- Plying the same direction as spinning untwists the singles and they fall apart.
- Snarls from the lazy kate are usually too little brake tension on the kate.
- Balance is judged after washing: re-check the hang test once dry.

## Verify
- [ ] Plied opposite to singles; tension even on all plies.
- [ ] Hang test shows near-balance before and after washing.
- [ ] Skein tied in four places; yardage and weight recorded.
- [ ] Twist set with a finish suited to the yarn; labelled.
