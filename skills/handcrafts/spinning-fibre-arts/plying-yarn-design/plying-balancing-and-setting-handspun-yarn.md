# Plying, balancing and setting handspun yarn

**Goal:** finished skeins that hang straight, behave in knitting or weaving, and
match the yarn you designed — not a kinked loop of singles.

## 1. Pick a structure
| Structure | How | Character |
|---|---|---|
| **2-ply** | two singles twisted together | lighter, textured, good for lace |
| **3-ply** | three singles | rounder, smoother, excellent stitch definition |
| **Chain-ply (Navajo)** | one singles looped into a crochet chain and plied as 3 | keeps colour sequences intact; 3-ply look from one bobbin |
| **Cabled** | two 2-plies plied together again, opposite direction | very round, hard-wearing |
| **Art yarns** | coils, beehives, supercoils, core-spun | texture over consistency |

## 2. Plying (wheel)
1. Put full bobbins on a **lazy kate** — with a little brake (a tension band or
   rubber band) so they don't overrun and backlash.
2. Spin the **opposite direction** to the singles: Z singles → treadle
   anticlockwise for S plying.
3. Lower the drive ratio or treadle slower; plying needs less twist per length than
   spinning, but the take-up must be firm for thicker plied yarn.
4. Keep the singles separated by fingers of the back hand; let the twist run up to
   your fingers evenly, then let the plied yarn wind on.
5. **Leftovers:** wind the remainder of the fuller bobbin into a **plying ball** or
   onto an "Andean plying" bracelet and ply from both ends.

Chain plying: make a large loop (~arm's length), pull the singles through it like a
crochet chain, letting ply twist run into three strands at a time. Keep loops long
or the yarn is lumpy where they join.

## 3. Check balance before you set
Pull a metre off the bobbin and let it hang in a U.
- Hangs open and still = **balanced**.
- Twists in the plying direction = **overplied** (too much ply twist).
- Twists in the singles direction = **underplied**.
Adjust treadling for the rest. Balance also depends on the singles' twist; matching a
sample plied by hand from the singles (fold back on itself, let it twist) gives a
target to copy.

## 4. Skein and set
1. Wind onto a **niddy-noddy** or skein winder and count the wraps.
   **Yardage = wraps × length of one full wrap** (measure yours with a string; many
   niddy-noddies give 1.5 or 2 yd per wrap).
2. Tie figure-eight ties in 3–4 places.
3. Soak in warm water with a little wool wash for 20 minutes.
4. Finish to suit:
   - **Gentle:** press out water, hang to dry unweighted.
   - **Thwack:** whack the wet skein against a surface to bloom woollen yarn.
   - **Full:** alternate hot and cold baths with agitation to lightly felt (sturdy
     outer-wear yarns). Watch it — it can't be undone.
   - **Weighting** while drying hides imbalance but doesn't cure it; the yarn kinks
     again when knitted. Use lightly, if at all, on balanced yarn only.

## 5. Record it
WPI, yardage, weight, fibre, ratios used. Write it on a tag. That record is how you
spin the same yarn twice.

## Gotchas
- Singles rested on the bobbin for days are easier to ply (twist sets temporarily),
  but **ply within a few weeks**; very old singles are hard to manage.
- Plying from bobbins of unequal fullness = leftovers; weigh fibre equally beforehand.
- Mixed fibre blends full differently; test a mini-skein.

## Verify
- [ ] Ply direction opposite to singles.
- [ ] Hanging U-test shows balance (or the imbalance intended for an art yarn).
- [ ] Skein washed and dried; hangs straight.
- [ ] Yardage, WPI and weight recorded on a tag.
