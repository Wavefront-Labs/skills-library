# Running an informal group meeting

**Goal:** a get-together of a peer group — club committee, volunteer crew, book club planning
session, residents' association, band meeting — that starts on time, gives everyone a voice,
decides what it needs to, and ends with clear next steps, while still feeling friendly rather than
corporate.

For workplace decision meetings, see
`business-finance.general-management.meeting-facilitation.decision-meetings-agendas-and-action-tracking`;
for community workshops run by staff, see
`human-services.community-services-and-development.group-facilitation.facilitating-inclusive-community-meetings-and-workshops`.

## 1. Before: is a meeting needed?
- If it's just updates, send a message instead. Meet when you need discussion, decisions or the social glue.
- Know the **purpose in one line**: "Plan the summer fair and agree the budget."
- **Agenda people tolerate:** 3–5 items, each with a time and a verb — *decide*, *discuss*,
  *update* — and who leads it. Send it 2–3 days ahead.
  ```
  Summer fair planning — Thu 7:30–8:45, community hall / Zoom link
  7:30  Welcome + quick round (10)
  7:40  Date and venue — DECIDE (Ana) (15)
  7:55  Stalls and volunteers — DISCUSS (Ben) (20)
  8:15  Budget — DECIDE (Chris) (15)
  8:30  Actions + next meeting (10)
  8:40  Close / tea
  ```
- Put the hardest decision **second or third**, after a warm-up but before energy fades.

## 2. Opening
- **Start on time** even if people are missing; it teaches punctuality.
- **Welcome round:** name + one quick thing ("one thing you're looking forward to this month").
  Keep it under a minute each. It gets every voice in the room early, which makes later
  contribution easier.
- **Restate purpose and finish time.** Ask if anything urgent needs adding.
- **Roles:** a note-taker and, for longer meetings, a timekeeper. Rotate them.

## 3. During: balance airtime
- **Rounds** for important questions: everyone speaks once before anyone speaks twice.
- **Invite quiet people by name** — gently, with an easy pass: "Mia, anything to add? Fine to pass."
- **Redirect long talkers:** "Thanks — let's hear a couple of other views." See
  `relationships-social.group-leadership-and-teamwork.managing-group-dynamics.handling-dominators-cliques-and-free-riders`.
- **Parking lot:** off-topic but useful points go on a list for later or another meeting.
- **Summarise** before moving on: "So we're agreed on 14 June at the hall. Next: stalls."
- **Decisions** use the rule you agreed (see
  `relationships-social.group-leadership-and-teamwork.group-decision-making.getting-a-group-to-a-decision-it-will-keep`).
- Watch the clock; if an item overruns, ask the group: "Ten more minutes on this, or park it?"

## 4. Closing with next steps
- **Read back actions:** each one with **who** and **by when**.
- **Confirm the next meeting date** now — it's much harder to fix by message later.
- Quick close: "One word on how tonight went?" or just thanks.
- **End on time.** Socialising afterwards is a bonus, not overtime.

## 5. After
Send short notes within 24–48 hours: decisions, actions (who/what/when), parking lot, next date.
A bullet list is plenty. Pin it in the group chat or shared drive.

## Hybrid and online
- Give remote people the first word in rounds, they're easiest to forget.
- Use one shared screen for the agenda and notes.
- Online rounds go faster with a set order ("I'll go alphabetically").

## Gotchas
- **Waiting for latecomers** punishes those on time.
- **No notes** = the same discussion next month.
- **Overstuffed agendas** — cut to what *must* happen this meeting.
- **All-business** kills the social reason people joined. Leave time for chat before or after.
- **Accessibility:** venues with steps, late evenings and no online option exclude people. Ask what works.

## Verify
- [ ] Agenda went out in advance with times, verbs and leads.
- [ ] Meeting started and ended on time.
- [ ] Everyone spoke at least once (welcome round).
- [ ] Every decision and action has an owner and date, sent in notes within 48 hours.
- [ ] Next meeting date was fixed before people left.
