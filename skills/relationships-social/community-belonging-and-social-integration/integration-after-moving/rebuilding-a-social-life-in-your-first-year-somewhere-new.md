# Rebuilding a social life in your first year somewhere new

**Goal:** within a year of moving, have a handful of people you see regularly, a couple
you could call in a crisis, and somewhere you're known by name. It rarely happens by
accident for adults; it needs a plan.

## Month 1: map the entry points
List, for your new area:
- **Recurring groups:** sports clubs, parkrun or running clubs, choirs, community
  gardens, makerspaces, faith communities, board-game nights, book clubs, language
  exchanges.
- **Structured newcomer routes:** newcomer or expat associations, "new in town" meetups,
  library events, adult-education classes, settlement services for migrants
  (see human-services.community-services-and-development.settlement-services.settlement-orientation-interpreters-and-cultural-bridging).
- **Volunteering:** food banks, conservation groups, events — shared work makes talking easy.
- **Built-in networks:** colleagues, your children's school and clubs, neighbours, a
  dog park, your gym class.
- **Warm leads:** ask friends back home "Do you know anyone in ___?" Even a
  friend-of-a-friend coffee is a start.

## Months 1–3: commit and repeat
- Pick **two or three recurring activities** and go every week. Repetition with the same
  people is what turns strangers into friends (see
  relationships-social.community-belonging-and-social-integration.joining-communities).
- **Say yes** to nearly every invitation in this period, even mildly appealing ones.
- **Be a regular** somewhere: the same café, barber, market stall.
- **Follow up within a week** of meeting someone you liked: "Great chatting at choir —
  want to get a coffee before next week's rehearsal?" Most budding connections die from
  nobody making the second move.

## Months 3–6: host and deepen
- **Host something small:** drinks, a potluck, a walk. Inviting several half-known
  people at once lowers the pressure and makes you a hub.
- **Move a few acquaintances to one-to-one time.**
- **Introduce people to each other** — connectors become central.

## Months 6–12: the dip and the roots
- **Expect a low point.** Many people report the novelty wearing off and homesickness or
  loneliness peaking months in (the "culture shock" curve is a popular model, though
  evidence for a fixed U-shape is mixed). It's a phase, not a verdict.
- **Keep old friendships alive** with scheduled calls; they're your support while new
  ones grow, but don't let them replace local effort.
- **Take on a role** in one group (organiser, volunteer lead).
- **Review:** which activities produced connections? Drop the ones that didn't and try one new.

## For families and partners
- The partner who moved for the other's job often has fewer built-in contacts; prioritise
  their entry points.
- Children: school, after-school clubs and parents at the school gate are an entry
  point for adults too.

## Gotchas
- **Waiting to be invited.** As the newcomer, you'll usually need to initiate more at first.
- **Only socialising with people from your home country/region** — comforting and valid,
  but mixing in local groups speeds integration.
- **Work-only social life** disappears when you change jobs.
- **Online-only connection** rarely substitutes for face-to-face here.
- Persistent isolation with low mood may be worth raising with a GP; loneliness is
  common, and help exists.

## Verify
- [ ] You have a written list of at least 10 local entry points.
- [ ] You attend at least two recurring activities weekly.
- [ ] You followed up one-to-one with at least three people you met.
- [ ] You've hosted at least one small gathering.
- [ ] At 12 months, you can name people you'd call in a crisis locally.
