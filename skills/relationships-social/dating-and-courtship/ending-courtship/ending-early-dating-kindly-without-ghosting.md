# Ending early dating kindly — the clear no, not the ghost

**Goal:** let someone know you're not continuing, in a way that is clear enough to
end the uncertainty and kind enough that it doesn't hurt more than it has to.

Ghosting (just stopping replying) feels easier for the person doing it, but it leaves
the other person guessing, rechecking messages and sometimes blaming themselves. A
two-sentence message is almost always kinder.

## 1. Match the ending to how far it went
- **Matched/chatted, never met:** you can simply stop or unmatch; a short "I don't
  think we're a fit, good luck!" is a nice touch but not required by most norms.
- **One to three dates:** a message is appropriate and expected.
- **Several weeks of regular dating, or any intimacy:** a phone call or a short
  in-person conversation is more respectful, unless you feel unsafe (then a message
  is fine).

## 2. The message: thank, state, wish well
> "Hi Sam, thanks for the last couple of dates — I've enjoyed getting to know you.
> I don't feel the romantic connection I'm looking for, so I don't want to keep
> going. I wish you all the best."

Principles:
- **Be unambiguous.** "I'm really busy at the moment" or "let's see" invites them to
  keep trying. Say you're not continuing.
- **Keep the reason general and about you** ("I'm not feeling a romantic connection"),
  not a critique of them. Listing faults rarely helps and often hurts.
- **Don't offer friendship unless you mean it** and would actually follow through.
- **Send it soon.** Within a few days of deciding, not weeks of slow-fade replies.
- **Don't reopen.** No "maybe in the future" if you don't mean it.

## 3. If they push back
- A respectful reply ("Thanks for telling me, all the best") — reply briefly or not at all.
- Asking why: you can give one honest, gentle sentence, or say "I don't have a
  better explanation than that it just didn't click for me." You don't owe a debate.
- Persistent messages, anger, guilt-tripping: you've done your part. It's fine to
  stop responding and to block.
- **Safety first:** if someone becomes threatening, keep the messages, block, report
  to the app, and contact police if you feel unsafe. In that situation, ghosting is
  the right call.

## 4. Being the one turned down
- Reply short and gracious, or not at all: "Thanks for letting me know. Take care."
- Don't argue, ask for a list of reasons, or try to change their mind.
- Don't vent about them online or to mutual friends in a way that will get back.
- It's normal to feel hurt or embarrassed. One person's no is information about fit,
  not a verdict on your worth. Talk to a friend, and take a short break from apps if
  you need to.
- If you've been ghosted, it's fine to send one closing message ("Sounds like you're
  not interested — no worries, all the best") and then let it go.

## Gotchas
- **The slow fade** (replying less and less) is ghosting in slow motion; it prolongs
  the uncertainty.
- **Mutual friends or colleagues:** say it privately first, before they hear it
  elsewhere, and agree to be civil when you meet.
- **"It's not you, it's me"** is fine if it's true; padding it with excessive
  apologies can feel performative. Short and warm wins.

## Verify
- [ ] The person could not reasonably read your message as "maybe".
- [ ] The channel fits how far things went (message / call / in person).
- [ ] You didn't list their faults or make an offer you don't mean.
- [ ] You know when to stop replying and when to block for safety.
- [ ] When turned down yourself, you replied once, graciously, and stopped.
