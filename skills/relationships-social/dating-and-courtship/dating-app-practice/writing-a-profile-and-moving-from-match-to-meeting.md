# Dating-app practice — from profile to first meeting

**Goal:** attract matches who actually suit you, start conversations that go
somewhere, and convert a promising match into a safe real-world date before the
chat goes stale.

App features change often (Hinge prompts, Bumble's who-messages-first rules, paid
"boosts"); the principles below are app-agnostic. Check the current app's help pages
for specifics (unverified across versions).

## 1. Photos: do the heavy lifting
Aim for 4–6 photos, recent (last ~12 months), that together answer "what is this
person like and what would a date with them be like?"
- **First photo:** just you, face clearly visible, good natural light, no sunglasses.
- **One full-length photo**, so nobody is surprised in person.
- **One or two doing something** you actually do (hiking, cooking, playing music).
- **At most one group photo**, and you should be obvious in it.
- Skip: heavy filters, photos with an ex cropped out, all-selfie sets, a screen of
  car/fish/gym mirror shots with no face.
**Gotcha:** outdated or flattering-angle-only photos win matches and lose first dates.
The goal is a match who's pleased when you walk in.

## 2. Bio and prompts: specific beats impressive
- Replace adjectives with details. "I love travel" → "Still thinking about a bowl of
  noodles I had in Osaka in 2023." Specifics give people something to reply to.
- Include one or two **hooks** — an opinion, a question, a small challenge ("tell me
  your most controversial pizza topping").
- State what you're looking for honestly (casual, long-term, unsure) and any
  dealbreakers that really are dealbreakers (kids, smoking). It filters in both directions.
- Keep it short. Negativity ("no drama, no players") reads as baggage.

## 3. Openers that get answered
- **Reference something in their profile**: "Your photo at that climbing wall — are you
  a bouldering or ropes person?" A question that is easy and fun to answer.
- Avoid "hey", copy-paste openers, and anything about their body.
- Mirror their message length and pace roughly; one long paragraph to a two-word
  reply is a signal to slow down.

## 4. Moving from match to meeting
Long text chats tend to build a fantasy version of a person and then fizzle. A common
rule of thumb: suggest meeting **after a few good exchanges, typically within a week**.
> "I'm enjoying this — want to continue over a coffee? I'm free Wednesday evening or
> Sunday morning."
- Optionally do a **short video call first** — it confirms they are who their photos
  show and saves a wasted trip.
- Move off the app's messaging only when you're comfortable; your phone number reveals
  more than you think (some apps let you call in-app).

## 5. Safety
- First meeting in a **public place**, your own transport there and back.
- Tell a friend where you're going and who with; share live location if you like.
- **Romance-scam red flags:** refuses video, quickly professes love, moves you to
  another messaging app fast, a sudden crisis needing money or gift cards, investment
  or crypto "opportunities". Never send money to someone you haven't met. Report and
  block.
- Reverse-image-search photos if something feels off.

## 6. Pacing your own use
- Swiping is cheap; conversations are not. Have a few chats at a time rather than
  dozens, and actually follow through.
- Being ignored or unmatched is normal and rarely personal. Take breaks if it
  becomes discouraging.
- Ending a chat: a short "I don't think we're a match, but best of luck" is kinder
  than vanishing (see relationships-social.dating-and-courtship.ending-courtship.ending-early-dating-kindly-without-ghosting).

## Verify
- [ ] Profile has a clear solo face photo, a full-length photo, and a recent activity shot.
- [ ] Every prompt answer contains a specific detail someone could reply to.
- [ ] Your openers reference their profile and end in an easy question.
- [ ] You suggest a concrete meeting within roughly a week of good chat.
- [ ] You know the scam red flags and have a safety plan for the first meeting.
