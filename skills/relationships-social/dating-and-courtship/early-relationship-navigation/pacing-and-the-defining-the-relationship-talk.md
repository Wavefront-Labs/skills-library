# Pacing and the "defining the relationship" talk

**Goal:** get through the uncertain stretch between first dates and a committed
relationship with expectations stated, not guessed — so neither person is
blindsided by what the other thought was going on.

## 1. Pacing: let the relationship earn each step
Early relationships move along several tracks at once: how often you see each other,
how much you text, emotional disclosure, physical intimacy, meeting friends, meeting
family, future talk. Healthy pacing means **the tracks move together, at a speed both
people are comfortable with**.
- Each person can set their own pace on any track; the slower person's pace on a given
  track is the one that applies (see relationships-social.sexuality-and-intimate-communication.consent-negotiation.asking-checking-in-and-respecting-a-no
  for physical intimacy).
- **Red flag: love-bombing.** Intense declarations, constant contact, gifts and talk of
  a shared future within days or weeks — especially combined with impatience about
  your boundaries or friends — is a recognised warning pattern of controlling
  relationships, not simply romance.
- **Also notice the opposite:** months of regular dates while one person deflects any
  sign of commitment. That's information too.

## 2. When to have the talk
There's no fixed timeline; common triggers are:
- You'd be upset to learn they were dating other people.
- You're deciding whether to delete apps or turn down other dates.
- You're about to introduce them to family, travel together, or become sexually
  exclusive — or before you stop using protection (see
  relationships-social.sexuality-and-intimate-communication.sexual-health-conversations.raising-sti-testing-and-contraception).
- The uncertainty itself is making you anxious.
Many people do this somewhere between a few weeks and a few months in. **Don't assume
exclusivity just because time has passed** — people's defaults differ widely.

## 3. Having it
- **In person, privately, at a calm time**, not in the middle of a fight or by
  text late at night.
- **Lead with your own feelings and wants**, not a demand:
  > "I really like where this is going. I'm not seeing anyone else and I'd like us to
  > be exclusive. How are you feeling about it?"
- **Then actually listen.** Ask what they want, too. Clarify terms, because words mean
  different things: does "exclusive" mean not dating others, not sleeping with others,
  or calling each other partners? Is it okay to keep apps installed?
- **Accept a not-yet without pressuring.** "I'm not ready to decide" is a valid answer.
  You can ask, "Is that something you see wanting with me?" and choose what to do with
  the answer — but an ultimatum issued to force a yes rarely produces the relationship
  you wanted.

## 4. After the answer
- **Same page:** agree what changes (apps off, how you introduce each other).
- **Different pages:** decide whether you can genuinely accept their pace. If you're
  waiting and hurting, ending things kindly is a legitimate choice (see
  relationships-social.dating-and-courtship.ending-courtship.ending-early-dating-kindly-without-ghosting).
- **Revisit later** if it was "not yet": agree roughly when ("can we talk again in a
  month or so?") instead of hovering indefinitely.

## Gotchas
- **"Situationships" usually persist because nobody asks.** Asking is not needy; it's
  the only way to know.
- **Don't negotiate through jealousy tests** (posting with someone else, going quiet)
  — they create the very insecurity you're trying to resolve.
- **Non-monogamous arrangements** are a legitimate choice too, but they require even
  more explicit agreement, not less.
- **Cultural and religious contexts** may have their own milestones (see
  relationships-social.dating-and-courtship.courtship-customs.navigating-family-and-cultural-courtship-traditions).

## Verify
- [ ] You can say what each of you understands the relationship to be, in plain words.
- [ ] "Exclusive" (or whatever term you used) is defined to both people's satisfaction.
- [ ] Nobody was pressured into an answer.
- [ ] If the answer was "not yet", there's a rough time to revisit.
- [ ] You checked the pace for love-bombing or chronic deflection.
