# Explaining hard topics at a child's level

**Goal:** give a child a true answer they can actually hold — short, concrete, matched to
their age — and leave the door open so they come back with the next question.

## 1. Find out what they are really asking
Before answering, ask back: *"What made you think of that?"* or *"What do you think it
means?"* A four-year-old asking "where do babies come from?" may want "from a special
place inside the mum's tummy called the womb", not a biology lesson. Their answer shows
what they already know, what they misheard, and what is worrying them.

## 2. Answer short, true, and concrete
- **Short:** one or two sentences, then stop and watch. Let them pull more if they want it.
- **True:** never say something you'll have to un-say later. You can leave details out;
  don't put falsehoods in.
- **Concrete words:** young children take language literally. "We lost Grandpa" or "he
  went to sleep" can produce fear of getting lost or of bedtime. Say "Grandpa died. His
  body stopped working and it can't start again." Use correct names for body parts.

## 3. Rough guide by stage (individual children vary a lot)
| Age (approx.) | What they can handle | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 2–4 | Simple facts, the here-and-now, what changes for *them* | "Daddy and I are going to live in two houses. You'll sleep at both." |
| 5–8 | Cause and effect, finality of death, rules; magical thinking ("did I cause it?") | "The divorce is a grown-up decision. Nothing you did caused it and nothing you do can change it." |
| 9–12 | More detail, fairness, other people's views, what it means for the future | Explain the illness by name and what treatment involves. |
| 13+ | Near-adult information, nuance, their opinion | Share more of the reasoning; ask what they think. Teen conversations are their own craft — see relationships-social.parenting-communication-and-discipline.parenting-teens. |
The ages are indicative, drawn from general child-development guidance; use the child's
questions, not their birthday, to calibrate.

## 4. Always cover the three hidden questions
Children under stress quietly wonder: **Is it my fault? Will it happen to me / you? Who
will look after me?** Answer these even if not asked: "It's not your fault", "Most people
live until they're very old", "You'll still go to school and I'll still pick you up."

## 5. Name feelings, including your own
"I'm sad too. It's okay to cry." Showing measured emotion teaches that feelings are
survivable; falling apart in front of them, or hiding everything, both teach something
worse. If you need to cry hard, step away briefly and say you'll be back.

## 6. Close the loop and reopen it
End with "You can ask me about this any time." Expect the same question again days later
— repetition is how children process, not a sign you explained badly. Bring it up
yourself after a week if they've gone quiet.

## Gotchas
- **Over-answering** an anxious parent's whole worry onto a child who asked a small question.
- **"I'll tell you when you're older"** teaches them to ask someone else (often a less
  reliable someone).
- **Timing:** not right before bed, school drop-off or a party. Side-by-side moments (car,
  walking, drawing) feel safer than face-to-face interrogation.
- **Siblings of different ages** may need separate conversations.
- If you don't know: "I don't know — let's find out together" is a complete answer.
- For bereavement specifically, see
  human-services.bereavement-support-and-death-doula-work.supporting-bereaved-children.age-honest-death-conversations-and-grief-activities-for-children.
- Persistent sleep problems, regression, or distress lasting weeks after a big event is a
  reason to talk to a GP or child-health professional — this skill is everyday family
  communication, not treatment.

## Verify
- [ ] You asked what they meant before answering.
- [ ] Your answer contained no euphemism a literal-minded child could misread.
- [ ] You addressed fault, safety and "who looks after me" where relevant.
- [ ] Nothing you said will need retracting later.
- [ ] You invited further questions and followed up within about a week.
