# Host and guest conduct — the reciprocal duties of hospitality

**Goal:** make guests feel genuinely welcome when you host, and be the guest people
want back. The underlying rule on both sides: **reduce the other person's effort and
uncertainty.** For gift selection in depth, see the gift-exchange-and-reciprocity-practice
field.

## Part A — Hosting

### Before they arrive
- Give the practical facts up front: time, address, parking, dress level, whether
  it's a full meal, and an end time for overnight stays ("Friday to Sunday lunch").
- **Ask about dietary needs and allergies** when you invite, not on the day.
- For overnight guests: clean sheets, a clear surface for their bag, towels set out,
  the Wi-Fi password written down, a glass of water and a spare phone charger by the bed.

### When they arrive
- Greet at the door, take coats, offer a drink within the first minute or two.
- **Introduce newcomers** to at least one person with a talking hook.
- Show the essentials: bathroom, where to put things, any house rules (shoes off,
  pets, "the back door sticks").

### During
- Circulate; don't let one guest stand alone for long.
- Have a non-alcoholic option that isn't just water.
- Don't make guests watch you cook for an hour — prep ahead.
- Accept offers of help when it lets a guest feel useful (carrying plates, opening wine);
  decline deep cleaning.

### Ending gracefully
Signal the end kindly: stop refilling drinks, start tidying lightly, offer coffee
("one last coffee before you go?"), or say directly "this has been lovely — I've got
an early start." Walk guests to the door.

## Part B — Being a guest

### Arriving
- **Dinner at someone's home:** arrive on time to about 10–15 minutes late; never
  early (the host is still getting ready). Larger parties are looser. Norms differ
  by culture — in some places 30+ minutes late is normal, in others punctuality is strict.
- **Bring something:** a small host gift — wine, flowers *in a vase or already
  arranged* (unarranged flowers give the host a task mid-hosting), chocolates, a local
  treat. Don't expect your wine to be served that night.
- Ask before bringing an extra person, child or dog. Always.

### During
- Offer help once or twice; accept a "no".
- Follow house rules without comment (shoes, smoking outside, quiet hours).
- Mingle; don't spend the evening on your phone.
- Overnight: strip the bed or ask how they'd like it left, keep your space tidy,
  replace anything you use up, contribute — buy a meal, groceries, or take them out.

### Leaving
- **Read the signs**: the host yawning, tidying, the coffee offer, other guests
  leaving. Short visits leave people wanting more; the old proverb about guests and
  fish starting to smell after three days is a fair guide for stays.
- Say goodbye to the host personally, not by vanishing (at a big party slipping out
  unannounced is sometimes tolerated, but thank the host by message the same night).
- **Thank them** the next day by message or note — see correspondence-etiquette.

## Gotchas
- Hosts asking "anything you don't eat?" at the table: too late.
- Guests arriving early "to help" without being asked.
- Bringing a dish that needs oven space or prep without checking.
- Overnight guests assuming the host is their tour guide all day.
- Reciprocity: if you've been hosted repeatedly, invite back in some form.

## Verify
- [ ] Host: guests knew time, place, dress, food scale and end time in advance; dietary needs collected early.
- [ ] Host: every guest was greeted, given a drink and introduced to someone.
- [ ] Guest: arrived in the polite window with a gift that creates no work.
- [ ] Guest: left before the host had to hint hard, said goodbye in person, thanked within a day.
