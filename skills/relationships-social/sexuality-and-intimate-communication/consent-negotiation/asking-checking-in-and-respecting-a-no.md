# Asking, checking in and respecting a no

**Goal:** make sure any intimate or sexual activity is something both (all) people
genuinely want, every time — by asking clearly, checking in as things change, and
responding well to hesitation or a no. Relationship-education reference; not legal advice.

## 1. What valid consent involves
A widely taught summary (Planned Parenthood's "FRIES"):
- **Freely given** — no pressure, guilt, threats, or fear of the consequences of saying no.
- **Reversible** — anyone can change their mind at any point, including partway through.
- **Informed** — no deception about things that matter (for example, about
  contraception or condom use, or sexual-health status).
- **Enthusiastic** — look for a genuine yes, not reluctant going-along.
- **Specific** — yes to one thing is not yes to everything, and yes last time is not
  yes this time.

Also essential: **capacity.** Someone who is asleep, unconscious, or significantly
impaired by alcohol or drugs cannot consent. If you're unsure whether someone is too
drunk, treat it as a no. And there are **legal age-of-consent** and
position-of-trust laws that vary by jurisdiction.

Being in a relationship or married does **not** mean ongoing consent; consent is
needed each time.

## 2. How to ask (it can be natural, not clinical)
- **Ask open questions:** "Would you like to...?", "How does this feel?", "Do you
  want to keep going?", "Is there anything you'd like?"
- **Talk outside the moment too.** It's often easier to discuss likes, limits and
  expectations over coffee than mid-moment (see
  relationships-social.sexuality-and-intimate-communication.discussing-boundaries.naming-and-renegotiating-limits).
- **Make no easy to say:** "We can totally just watch the film instead" signals the
  no is safe.
- **Before something new**, ask specifically rather than assuming.

## 3. Checking in as you go
- Check in when things change (moving to a new kind of activity), when you notice a
  shift in their responses, or simply periodically: "Still good?"
- **Watch, as well as listen.** Signs to pause and ask: going quiet or still, stiff
  body, turning away, pulling back, not participating, crying, a hesitant "um, I
  guess".
- **Freezing is common.** Many people, under stress or discomfort, freeze rather than
  saying no. Lack of a no is not a yes.
- **Pause with care:** "Hey — you went quiet. Do you want to stop or slow down?" Then
  accept whatever they say without sulking.

## 4. Receiving a no, a not-now or a maybe
- **Stop and respond warmly:** "Of course. Thanks for telling me." A partner who feels
  safe saying no is more likely to say a real yes another time.
- **Don't ask "why?" in the moment** in a way that requires justification, and don't
  ask again repeatedly (that becomes pressure).
- **Treat "maybe" or "I'm not sure" as a no for now.**
- **Avoid emotional pressure:** sulking, "if you loved me you would", guilt, or
  bargaining all undermine consent.
- **Reassure** the relationship isn't at risk because they said no.

## 5. Giving and withdrawing your own consent
- You can say "I'd like to stop", "not that", "slow down", or "I've changed my mind"
  at any time, without explanation.
- Useful phrases: "I'm not into that, but I'd like to..." (offers an alternative);
  "Can we pause?"
- Some couples use agreed words or signals, especially for non-verbal moments.

## Gotchas
- **Silence or not resisting ≠ consent.**
- **Consent can't be obtained through deception** — about condoms, contraception or
  sexual-health status (see
  relationships-social.sexuality-and-intimate-communication.sexual-health-conversations.raising-sti-testing-and-contraception).
- **Power differences** (age, boss/employee, financial dependence) make "freely
  given" harder; take extra care or don't proceed.
- **If you've experienced sexual violence**, specialist services (e.g. RAINN in the US,
  Rape Crisis in the UK, 1800RESPECT in Australia) offer confidential support.

## Verify
- [ ] You can name the five FRIES elements and the capacity rule.
- [ ] You have phrases ready to ask, check in and offer an easy no.
- [ ] You know the non-verbal signs that mean pause and ask.
- [ ] Your response to a no is warm, immediate and pressure-free.
- [ ] You understand that consent is needed every time, even in a long relationship.
