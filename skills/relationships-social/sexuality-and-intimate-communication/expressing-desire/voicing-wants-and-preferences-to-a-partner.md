# Voicing wants and preferences to a partner

**Goal:** be able to say what you'd like — in affection, intimacy and sex — clearly
enough that your partner can respond, and kindly enough that it brings you closer
rather than making either of you feel criticised. Non-graphic relationship-education
reference.

Many people find this harder than any other conversation, often because of upbringing,
religious or cultural messages, or past experiences. Awkwardness is normal and
usually fades with practice.

## 1. Choose the moment
- **Outside the bedroom first.** Raising a preference during intimacy can feel like
  critique; a relaxed moment elsewhere — a walk, a car ride, a cosy evening — is easier.
  Side-by-side settings (walking, driving) reduce intensity for many people.
- **Not right after a disappointing moment,** when feelings are raw.
- **Ask permission to raise it:** "Can I share something I've been thinking about,
  about us being close?"

## 2. Phrasing that works
- **Positive and forward-looking:** "I'd love it if we..." / "I really like it when
  you... — could we do that more?" rather than "You never..." or "I don't like how you...".
- **Own it with "I":** "I've been wanting more cuddling in the mornings."
- **Pair a want with appreciation:** "I love how affectionate you are. Something I'd
  enjoy is..."
- **Make it a question, not an instruction:** "How would you feel about trying...?"
- **Be specific enough to act on**, without needing graphic detail; a preference
  stated vaguely ("more romance") leaves your partner guessing.

## 3. Tools when words are hard
- **"Yes / no / maybe" lists:** each partner privately marks a list of activities or
  kinds of affection as yes, no or maybe, then you compare and discuss overlaps.
  Various sex-education organisations publish versions; choose one that matches your
  comfort level. Never treat a "yes" on a list as consent in the moment — consent is
  still asked for each time (see
  relationships-social.sexuality-and-intimate-communication.consent-negotiation.asking-checking-in-and-respecting-a-no).
- **Write it down** — a note or message can be easier than saying it face to face.
- **Use a book or article as a prompt:** "I read something interesting, can I show you?"

## 4. Initiating
Unclear initiation leads to missed signals and hurt feelings.
- **Agree how each of you likes to be approached** — words, a particular touch, a
  text earlier in the day.
- **Initiate clearly and without pressure:** "I'd love to be close tonight — are you
  in the mood?" leaves room for a no.
- **Share initiating** if it has become one person's job; feeling always the one who
  asks can feel lonely.

## 5. Giving and receiving feedback
- **In the moment:** keep it short and positive ("yes, that", "I like that") or
  redirect gently ("a bit slower" / "can we try...").
- **Afterwards:** what you enjoyed most (see
  relationships-social.sexuality-and-intimate-communication.intimacy-building-communication.debriefs-and-building-trust-through-intimate-talk).
- **When receiving feedback:** thank them. Feedback is a gift that helps you know your
  partner, not a judgement of your worth.

## 6. Handling a no or a not-yet
- If your partner isn't interested in something, accept it gracefully: "Thanks for
  being honest." Ask, without pressure, whether there's a version they'd enjoy.
- Don't keep re-raising it to wear them down.
- Rejection of a request isn't rejection of *you*. If you feel hurt, say so later, calmly.

## Gotchas
- **Comparisons with exes** or with porn are hurtful and rarely helpful.
- **Shame runs deep for some people.** If embarrassment, anxiety or pain is getting
  in the way, a sex therapist or doctor can help — this is a normal reason to seek support.
- **Desire changes** with stress, health, medication, age and life stage; revisit preferences over time.
- If your partner wants something very different from you, see
  relationships-social.sexuality-and-intimate-communication.navigating-mismatched-desire.working-through-a-desire-gap-without-blame.

## Verify
- [ ] You raised a preference outside the moment, framed positively and as a question.
- [ ] You both know how the other prefers to be invited to intimacy.
- [ ] Feedback is welcomed, short in the moment and appreciative afterwards.
- [ ] A no from either partner is accepted without pressure.
