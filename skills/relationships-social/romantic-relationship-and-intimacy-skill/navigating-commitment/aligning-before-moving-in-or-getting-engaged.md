# Aligning before moving in or getting engaged

**Goal:** take big relationship steps as a deliberate joint decision with your eyes
open, instead of drifting into them because a lease ran out — and surface the
dealbreaker-level differences while they're still cheap to address.

Relationship researchers describe **"sliding vs. deciding"**: many couples slide into
living together for convenience without ever discussing what it means, which
research associates with lower later commitment and satisfaction than deciding on
purpose (correlational; the cause isn't fully established).

## 1. First, say what the step means to each of you
Before logistics, ask: *why now, and what does this mean?*
- Is moving in a step toward marriage, a trial, a money decision, or "it just made sense"?
- Is engagement about a wedding, a legal union, a religious commitment, children, security?
Mismatched meanings ("I thought this meant we were heading to marriage" / "I thought
we were saving on rent") are a classic source of later resentment.

## 2. The big-topics checklist
Work through these over several conversations (not one marathon). You don't need
identical answers — you need to *know* the differences and believe you can live
with them.
- **Children:** whether, how many, roughly when; fertility, adoption, step-parenting.
- **Money:** income, debts and credit (full disclosure), spending vs. saving styles,
  joint vs. separate accounts or a hybrid ("yours, mine, ours"), how shared costs
  are split (equally or proportional to income).
- **Home and household:** who does what chore, standards of tidiness, guests, pets,
  noise and alone time. Write a first division of labour down and review it after a
  month.
- **Where to live:** city vs. country, near which family, willingness to move for
  a career.
- **Work and ambition:** whose career takes priority when, caring for children or
  ageing parents, retirement hopes.
- **Faith, values and traditions:** religious practice, holidays, how children would
  be raised.
- **Family and in-laws:** how much involvement, money obligations to parents,
  holidays split.
- **Health and habits:** alcohol, smoking, mental and physical health history,
  sleep schedules.
- **Intimacy and fidelity:** expectations around monogamy and sexual connection
  (see relationships-social.sexuality-and-intimate-communication.discussing-boundaries.naming-and-renegotiating-limits).
- **Conflict style:** how each of you handles disagreements, and what you'll do if you
  get stuck (counselling?).

## 3. Running these talks well
- **One or two topics per sitting**, at a calm time, with a cup of tea, not at
  11pm.
- **Curiosity first:** "What did money look like in your family growing up?" often
  explains more than "Why do you spend so much?"
- **Separate "must" from "prefer".** Children usually can't be compromised on;
  where to spend Christmas usually can.
- **Write down agreements** for practical things (bills, chores, notice periods).

## 4. Practical and legal steps (jurisdiction-specific — check locally)
- **Moving in:** whose name is on the lease or mortgage, what happens to deposits and
  shared purchases if you split. Some places give cohabiting couples rights after a
  period (e.g. "de facto" relationships in Australia); others give few. A written
  cohabitation agreement can help.
- **Engagement/marriage:** wills, beneficiaries, name changes, and whether a
  prenuptial agreement makes sense — enforceability varies widely by country and
  state. Get local legal advice for significant assets.
- **Premarital education or counselling** (offered by many faith communities and
  counsellors) is associated in some studies with modestly better early-marriage
  outcomes.

## 5. Proposals
- Discuss marriage *before* a proposal; the question itself should not be a surprise
  in substance, only in timing.
- Check whether family or cultural steps come first (see
  relationships-social.dating-and-courtship.courtship-customs.navigating-family-and-cultural-courtship-traditions).

## Gotchas
- **"We'll figure it out later"** about children or faith is a common regret.
- **Moving in to fix a struggling relationship** usually intensifies the problems.
- **Financial secrecy** (hidden debt, gambling) discovered later is very damaging; disclose early.
- Pressure — "move in or we're done" — undermines the deliberate choice you're aiming for.

## Verify
- [ ] Each of you can say, in one sentence, what this step means to the other.
- [ ] You've discussed every topic in section 2 at least once.
- [ ] You know each other's debts and income, and have agreed how bills are split.
- [ ] Lease, ownership and (where relevant) legal protections are understood.
- [ ] Any must-have differences are named and you've decided, together, how to handle them.
