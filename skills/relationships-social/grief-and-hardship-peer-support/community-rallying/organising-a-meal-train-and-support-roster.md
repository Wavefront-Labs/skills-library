# Organising a meal train and support roster

**Goal:** turn a crowd of well-meaning friends, neighbours, colleagues or a faith community
into steady, non-duplicated help for one person or family in hardship — without the family
having to manage the helpers.

## 1. Get permission and a point of contact
- **Ask first.** "A lot of people want to help — would it be OK if I organised meals and lifts so
  you don't have to field it?" Some people don't want a public fuss.
- **Agree one family liaison** (the person, or someone very close) who tells *you* what's needed.
  Everyone else goes through you, not them.
- **Agree what may be shared** — the news itself, health details, address. Default to the minimum.

## 2. Build the needs list with the liaison
Capture, in one place:
- **Meals:** which days, how many people, dietary needs/allergies, dislikes, drop-off window, where
  to leave it (cool box on the porch?), whether to knock.
- **Other tasks:** school runs, dog walks, lifts to treatment, laundry, lawn, shopping.
- **What's *not* wanted:** visitors, phone calls, flowers, more lasagne.
- **Start and review date.** Commit to e.g. three weeks, then reassess.

## 3. Pick the tool
- **Dedicated meal-train sites** (MealTrain, TakeThemAMeal; Lotsa Helping Hands for broader care
  calendars; CaringBridge for health updates) — calendar of slots, volunteers sign up, notes on
  diet and drop-off. Good for big or mixed groups. *Uncertainty:* free vs paid features and
  privacy options vary and change; check current terms before putting an address on them.
- **A shared spreadsheet** (Google Sheets) — date, task, volunteer, contact, notes. Fine for
  people comfortable with it; lock the header row and instructions.
- **A messaging group** (WhatsApp, Signal) — good for quick swaps, bad as the record. Pin the
  roster link; keep chat minimal. **Never add the family to the helpers' group.**

## 4. Run it
- Seed the first week yourself with reliable people so it starts strong.
- Send helpers a short brief: drop-off time and place, disposable containers labelled with dish
  and date, no expectation of chat or thanks, no sharing of news.
- **Fill the gaps, prevent the gluts:** meals 3–4 nights a week is often plenty; cooking gives
  some people a sense of normality. Ask the liaison.
- Send the liaison one weekly summary, not a stream of notifications.
- Have a couple of reserves for dropouts.

## 5. Updates without crowding
- One channel for updates (a site journal or a weekly message from you), written or approved by
  the family. Stops twenty people texting "any news?".
- Steer people towards **reply-optional** messages to the family and away from visits unless invited.

## 6. Taper and hand over
- At the review date, ask the liaison what to keep, change or stop. Needs shift — meals often drop,
  lifts and childcare rise.
- Taper gradually rather than stopping dead, and hand the long tail to a few close friends (see
  `relationships-social.grief-and-hardship-peer-support.sustained-support.staying-in-touch-through-the-long-tail-of-grief`).
- Thank volunteers yourself so the family doesn't feel obliged to.

## Gotchas
- **Coordinator burnout** — share the role with a deputy from the start.
- **Food safety:** label allergens, keep chilled dishes cold, say if a dish contains nuts or alcohol.
- **Privacy and safety:** don't post a home address and "away at hospital all day" publicly.
- **Over-helping:** a crowd can feel like an invasion; the family's wishes beat helpers' wish to help.
- **Unequal reach:** online sign-ups miss older or less-connected neighbours who'd like to help —
  offer them a phone number.

## Verify
- [ ] The family agreed, named a liaison and said what can be shared.
- [ ] A written needs list exists with diets, drop-off rules and an end/review date.
- [ ] One roster is the source of truth; the family isn't in the helpers' chat.
- [ ] No day has duplicate meals and gaps are covered by reserves.
- [ ] A taper and long-term handover is planned.
