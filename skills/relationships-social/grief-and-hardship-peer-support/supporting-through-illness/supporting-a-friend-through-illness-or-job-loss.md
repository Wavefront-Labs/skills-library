# Supporting a friend through illness or job loss

**Goal:** stay a good friend to someone dealing with serious illness, redundancy, divorce or
another big setback short of bereavement — helping their morale while treating them as the same
competent adult they were before.

The core difference from grief support: the person is *in* the situation, often still making
decisions about it. **Autonomy and dignity** come first.

## 1. First response to the news
- Acknowledge: "That's really hard news. I'm sorry." (See
  `relationships-social.grief-and-hardship-peer-support.what-to-say.condolence-words-and-platitudes-to-avoid`.)
- Ask what they want from you: "Do you want to talk about it, or would you rather I just
  distract you?" Answers change day to day — ask again.
- Ask what they're telling others and keep their news **confidential**. Diagnoses and
  redundancies are theirs to share.

## 2. Hospital and home visits
- **Ask before visiting**, and check visiting rules, infection precautions and whether they're up to it.
- **Short visits** — often 20–40 minutes; illness and treatment are exhausting. Offer to leave.
- **Don't come if you're unwell** — a cold can be dangerous for someone on chemotherapy or after surgery.
- **Useful things to bring:** lip balm, a phone charger with a long cable, magazines, a puzzle book,
  snacks they're allowed, coins or card for parking or TV. Check before bringing food or flowers —
  some wards ban flowers.
- **Talk about normal things** — gossip, football, the series you both watch. Many patients are
  sick of being only a patient.
- **Practical cover:** lifts to appointments, sitting in on a consultation as a note-taker (if
  invited), looking after pets — see
  `relationships-social.grief-and-hardship-peer-support.practical-crisis-help.specific-offers-and-practical-help-in-a-crisis`.

## 3. Morale without toxic positivity
- **Don't insist on positivity.** "Stay positive, attitude is everything!" implies that if things
  go badly, it's their fault. *Note:* evidence that positive attitude improves cancer survival is
  weak; don't claim it does.
- **Make room for the bad days:** "That sounds exhausting. Do you want to rant?"
- **Celebrate real wins** they mention — a good scan, a job interview — at their level of excitement.
- **Keep inviting them**, and make it easy to decline or leave early. Being dropped from plans
  hurts more than being invited to things they can't do.

## 4. Dignity and autonomy
- **No unsolicited advice:** no miracle diets, alternative cures, "have you tried…" job tips.
  Ask "Would ideas be useful or not right now?"
- **Don't take over decisions** about treatment, money or jobs. Offer information only when asked.
- **Keep the relationship two-way.** Ask their opinion, tell them about your life (in moderation),
  let them help you sometimes. Being only a recipient of care erodes self-respect.
- **Body changes** (hair loss, weight, scars): follow their lead on whether to mention them.

## 5. Job loss specifically
- Shame is common; avoid "What did you do?" or "Everything happens for a reason."
- Useful help: a CV read-over (if asked), introductions to your network, mock interviews,
  buying the coffee without fuss, and normal social time that isn't about job-hunting.
- Ask before passing on job ads — a stream of them can feel like pressure.

## Gotchas
- **Ring theory:** comfort goes in toward the person at the centre, complaints go out to people
  further from it. Don't tell them how hard their illness is for *you*.
- **Avoiding them** because you don't know what to say is the most common failure. Awkward
  presence beats absence.
- **Long illnesses** outlast the initial rush of help; pace yourself and diary regular contact.
- **Serious mood changes** — hopelessness, talk of not wanting to live — warrant gently
  encouraging professional support and, if suicide is mentioned, urgent help.

## Verify
- [ ] You asked what kind of support they want, and asked again later.
- [ ] Their news stayed confidential.
- [ ] Visits were checked in advance, short, and not while you were ill.
- [ ] You gave no unsolicited cures, diets or "stay positive" pressure.
- [ ] They're still invited to ordinary plans and the friendship is still two-way.
