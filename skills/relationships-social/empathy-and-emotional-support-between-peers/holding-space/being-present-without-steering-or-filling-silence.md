# Being present without steering or filling silence

**Goal:** be a steady, non-judging presence while someone goes through something
painful, so they don't have to manage *your* reactions on top of their own. Holding space
is mostly about what you **don't** do: advise, reassure, redirect, fill the gaps.

## 1. Settle yourself first
Your calm is the container. Before or as you sit down:
- One or two slow breaths with a longer out-breath.
- Feet on the floor, shoulders down, hands still.
- Tell yourself: "My job is not to fix this. My job is to be here."
If you're anxious, they'll feel it and start reassuring you.

## 2. Full, quiet attention
- **Phone face down and silenced** — or in another room.
- **Body:** turned towards them, relaxed, at their level. Side-by-side (on a sofa, on a
  walk) can feel less intense than face-to-face.
- **Eyes:** soft attention, not a stare. Let them look away.
- **Touch:** only if you know they welcome it; ask if unsure ("Would a hug help?").

## 3. Let silence work
Silence feels long to the listener and short to the person thinking. When they stop:
- **Count slowly to five or ten** before speaking. They're often gathering the next thing.
- If you need to signal you're still with them: a nod, "mm", "I'm here", "take your time".
- Never rush to fill a pause with a story, a question, or a solution.

## 4. Follow, don't lead
- Let them choose the topic and the pace — even if they jump around or repeat themselves.
- Use **minimal encouragers** and **open prompts**: "Say more?" "What's that like?"
  "And then?"
- Reflect occasionally in a few words: "That's a lot to carry."
- If they want to talk about something light for a while, follow that too. People
  dose their own pain.

## 5. Make room for tears and anger
- Crying: stay put, don't hand tissues immediately (it can signal "stop"), keep them
  within reach. "It's okay" or silence.
- Anger, even at you or at someone you like: let it be said. Don't defend or correct
  in the moment unless it becomes unsafe.

## 6. Closing
- Let them signal the end, or gently check: "Do you want to keep talking or take a break?"
- Ask what would help next: "Is there anything you need tonight?"
- Don't summarise their problems back to them or end with advice.
- Follow up later — see
  relationships-social.empathy-and-emotional-support-between-peers.checking-in.

## What holding space is not
- Not passivity: you're intensely attentive.
- Not unlimited: you can say "I've got about an hour" at the start.
- Not therapy: you don't interpret, diagnose or work on their issues.

## Gotchas
- **"Everything will be okay"** is reassurance for you, not them; you don't know that.
- **Your own story** ("When my dad died…") pulls focus. Save it unless they ask.
- **Steering towards silver linings** or gratitude.
- **Your own limits:** if what you're hearing overwhelms you, it's okay to say so and get
  support afterwards for yourself.
- **Safety overrides non-directiveness.** If they talk about harming themselves or
  others, this becomes a different conversation — see
  relationships-social.empathy-and-emotional-support-between-peers.receiving-disclosure.
- Professional framing of the same skill:

## Verify
- [ ] Your phone was away and your body settled before they started.
- [ ] You let at least one long silence pass without filling it.
- [ ] You gave no advice or reassurance they didn't ask for.
- [ ] They set the topic and the pace throughout.
- [ ] You asked what would help next and arranged a follow-up.
