# Difficult-conversation entry — the first minute of a hard talk

**Goal:** raise a sensitive subject — a hurt, a worry, a request, a piece of
unwelcome news — so the other person can hear it rather than defend against it. The
first minute sets the course; couples researcher John Gottman's work is often cited
as showing that conversations which start harshly tend to end badly (the "harsh
startup" finding — treat the exact predictive figures quoted online as unconfirmed).
For working through the disagreement itself, see the
conflict-resolution-and-personal-negotiation field.

## 1. Before: get clear on one thing
Write or say to yourself in one sentence:
- **What specifically happened** (a behaviour, not a character trait).
- **What it meant to you** or how you felt.
- **What you'd like** — understanding, a change, a decision, or just to be heard.
If you have five grievances, pick one. Bundling turns a conversation into an indictment.

## 2. Timing and place
- **Private.** Never in front of others, never in a group chat.
- **Not when either of you is HALT** — hungry, angry, lonely, tired — or rushing
  out the door, driving in heavy traffic, or a few drinks in.
- **Not in the heat of the moment** if you can help it; a few hours or a day to cool
  down usually helps. But don't wait months — the longer you wait, the bigger it gets.
- **Medium:** in person or by voice call for anything emotional. Text is for
  "Can we talk later?", not for the talk.
- **Side-by-side** (walking, a drive) can feel less confrontational than face-to-face
  across a table.

## 3. The opening — four moves
1. **Ask for the conversation:** "Have you got twenty minutes tonight? There's
   something I'd like to talk about." This avoids ambush and lets them prepare.
2. **Name the awkwardness and your intent:** "This is a bit awkward for me, and I'm
   bringing it up because I care about us / want this to work."
3. **One I-statement about one specific thing.** A common template (sometimes called
   the XYZ statement): *"When you X, in situation Y, I feel Z."*
   - "When plans get cancelled on the day, like on Saturday, I feel like I'm not a priority."
   - Not: "You always flake. You're so unreliable." (generalisation + character attack)
4. **Stop and invite their view:** "I'd really like to hear how it looks from your side."
   Then **listen** (see active-listening). The opener is short; the listening is long.

## 4. Phrases that soften without diluting
- "I might be reading this wrong, but…"
- "I've noticed… and I wanted to check in rather than guess."
- "I'm not trying to blame you — I want us to figure it out."
- "Can I tell you something that's been on my mind?"

## 5. Phrases that raise defences
- **"We need to talk."** (dread, no information) — say the topic instead.
- **"You always / you never."**
- **"No offence, but…"** / **"Don't take this the wrong way…"**
- **Sandwiching** real criticism between fake praise — people learn to distrust the praise.
- **Opening with the verdict or the solution** before they've heard the problem.

## Worked example
Raising a friend's repeated lateness:
"Hey, have you got a minute? This is a bit awkward, but I'd rather say it than let
it bug me. When I've been waiting 30–40 minutes at our dinners — like last week —
I end up feeling a bit taken for granted. I know you've got a lot on. How's it been
from your end?"

## Gotchas
- Rehearsing a speech; you'll deliver a monologue. Rehearse just the first two sentences.
- Expecting resolution in one conversation; sometimes the goal is just to put it on the table.
- If they get defensive, reflect it ("Sounds like this feels unfair") rather than escalate.
- If there's any risk to your safety, prioritise that over the conversation; seek support.

## Verify
- [ ] You could state the specific behaviour, its impact and your ask in one sentence each.
- [ ] You chose a private time when neither of you was rushed, hungry, drunk or furious.
- [ ] Your opening asked permission, named the awkwardness, used one specific I-statement.
- [ ] You stopped and invited their view within the first minute.
