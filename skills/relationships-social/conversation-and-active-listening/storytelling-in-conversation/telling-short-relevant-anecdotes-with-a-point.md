# Storytelling in conversation — short, relevant anecdotes

**Goal:** tell a personal story in conversation that people enjoy and that moves the
conversation forward, rather than a meandering account that loses the room. This is
conversational storytelling, not a speech — for toasts, see
media-communication.public-speaking-and-rhetoric.occasional-speaking.toasts-acceptances-and-mc-work.

## 1. Pick the right story for the moment
Ask yourself before you start:
- **Is it relevant** to what was just said, or am I hijacking? A good anecdote adds to
  the thread; a bad one swaps it for yours (see active-listening on shift responses).
- **Is it the right size** for the setting? A 30-second story at a work coffee, a
  2–3 minute one at a dinner table, a longer one only when invited ("tell me the whole thing!").
- **Is it okay for this audience?** Check for people it might embarrass, private
  details of others, or topics that would hurt someone present.

## 2. Shape: hook, setup, turn, landing
1. **Hook (one sentence):** why they should listen. "The worst flight I've ever been on
   was to a wedding." / "I nearly got arrested in a supermarket once."
2. **Setup (as little as possible):** who, where, what you wanted. Two or three
   sentences. Cut every detail the ending doesn't need — the exact date, the
   name of the minor character, what you had for breakfast.
3. **Turn:** the thing that went wrong, surprised you, or changed. Slow down here;
   this is where detail earns its place. Use direct speech: "and he says, 'Sir, that's
   not your trolley.'"
4. **Landing:** the ending, the punchline, or what you took from it. Then **stop**.
   Don't re-explain the joke or retell the best bit.

## 3. Delivery
- **Present tense** for the turn can make it vivid ("so I'm standing there…").
- **Pause before the punchline** or reveal.
- **Watch the listeners** (see reading-social-cues). If attention drifts, skip ahead
  to the turn: "long story short —"
- **Self-deprecation** usually works better than stories where you're the hero.
- **Let others interject** — questions mid-story are a good sign; answer briefly and continue.

## 4. Hand back
End with a bridge that returns the floor:
- "…anyway, has anyone else had a travel disaster like that?"
- "So — never again. What's the worst flight you've had?"
This invites other people's stories and prevents the evening becoming your show.

## 5. Build a small repertoire
Most people have 5–10 reliable stories. Note them, practise trimming each to its
essentials, and know a short and a long version. Retelling makes them tighter —
but notice who's already heard it ("Have I told you about…?").

## Gotchas
- **"I'm not sure where this is going"** — if you can't name the ending before you
  start, don't start.
- Correcting yourself on trivial details ("it was Tuesday — no, Wednesday") derails flow.
- One-upping: answering someone's story with a bigger version of the same.
- Telling someone else's private story without permission.
- Stories as humblebrags (the point is your success) land worse than you expect.

## Verify
- [ ] The story connected to the thread just before it.
- [ ] You opened with a one-line hook and kept the setup to a few sentences.
- [ ] It ran under about two minutes unless people asked for more.
- [ ] You stopped after the landing and handed the floor back with a question.
