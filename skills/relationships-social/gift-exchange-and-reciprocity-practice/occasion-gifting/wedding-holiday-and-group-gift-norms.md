# Wedding, holiday and group gift norms

**Goal:** get through a year of birthdays, weddings, holidays and office collections
without last-minute panic, overspending or social missteps.

Norms below reflect broadly UK/US/Australian practice unless stated; amounts in
particular vary a lot by region, community and income (treat figures as unverified
examples, not rules).

## 1. Build a gifting calendar and budget
- **List the recurring occasions** in January: birthdays, anniversaries, the main
  holidays you celebrate, known weddings or births.
- **Set an annual gift budget** and split it across people and occasions. December
  and wedding season are where budgets usually blow out.
- **Set reminders 2–3 weeks ahead** for anything that needs ordering or posting.
- **Agree limits with family** where exchanges have grown unaffordable: "kids only",
  a cap, or a family secret Santa.

## 2. Weddings
- **Use the registry if there is one** — that's what the couple has asked for. Buy
  early for more choice.
- **Cash or honeymoon funds** are increasingly common and widely accepted. In many
  cultures cash is the *expected* gift (e.g. red envelopes, hongbao, at Chinese
  weddings, often in amounts with auspicious numbers and avoiding the number four;
  money dances or envelopes in many other traditions). Ask a family member if unsure.
- **How much?** Commonly scaled by closeness and means, not by "covering your plate"
  (a widespread but contested rule of thumb).
- **Timing:** many people send from the registry before the wedding; a gift up to a
  few months after is generally acceptable in Western etiquette.
- **If you can't attend,** a smaller gift or a card is still a kind gesture for close
  friends and family.
- **Engagement, shower and wedding** gifts can add up; it's acceptable to give a
  modest gift at the earlier events.

## 3. Babies, housewarmings and life events
- **Baby:** registry items, practical larger-size clothes (babies outgrow newborn
  sizes fast), or meals and help for new parents — often the most valued gift.
- **Housewarming:** something consumable (plant, good food, a bottle if they drink),
  or a small practical item. Avoid decor unless you know their taste.
- **Graduations, retirements, new jobs:** a card with a personal note is the core;
  cash is common for graduates.
- **Bereavement:** flowers, food or a donation to a named charity, plus a card.

## 4. Holidays
- **Know what's celebrated and how:** Christmas, Hanukkah, Eid, Diwali, Lunar New Year
  and others have different gifting customs; some involve money for children, sweets
  or food boxes for neighbours, others little gifting at all. Don't assume.
- **Inclusive greetings** in mixed workplaces ("happy holidays", or naming the holiday
  someone actually celebrates).
- **Teachers, carers, delivery people:** small tokens or a card are common in some
  countries; check school and employer rules, which may cap or ban gifts.

## 5. Office exchanges (secret Santa / white elephant)
- **Stick to the stated price limit** precisely; going over makes others look stingy.
- **Keep it universally safe:** food (check dietary needs), plants, mugs, desk items.
  Avoid jokes about appearance, alcohol if unsure, anything sexual or political.
- **Opting out** should be easy and stigma-free; if you organise, say so.
- **Gifts to a boss:** usually from the team as a group, not individually.

## 6. Organising a group gift
1. **One organiser**, a clear purpose and deadline.
2. **Suggest a contribution range** but make any amount — or none — fine, and keep
   contributions private.
3. **Use a simple collection method** (cash envelope, a payment app, or a collection
   site) and keep a list of who gave, for the card, not the amounts.
4. **Choose the gift** from the recipient's known wishes; buy promptly.
5. **Everyone signs the card**, including those who couldn't contribute.
6. **Report back** what was bought and return any surplus or donate it as agreed.

## Gotchas
- **Last-minute panic buys** cost more and land worse; the calendar fixes this.
- **Registry snubbing** ("I wanted to choose something personal") often gives the
  couple something they can't use.
- **Unequal sibling or grandchild spending** is noticed; keep it consistent.
- For choosing the item itself, see
  relationships-social.gift-exchange-and-reciprocity-practice.gift-selection.choosing-a-gift-that-fits-person-and-relationship.

## Verify
- [ ] Recurring occasions are in your calendar with reminders and a budget.
- [ ] Wedding gifts follow the couple's registry or the community's cash norms.
- [ ] Office gifts are within limit and universally safe.
- [ ] Any group gift you run has a deadline, private contributions and a report-back.
