# Asking for and repaying favours

**Goal:** be someone people are happy to help and glad to ask — by asking for favours
in a way that respects the other person, and keeping a loose, generous balance
without turning friendship into bookkeeping.

## 1. Asking well
- **Be specific:** what, when, how long. "Could you water my plants Tuesday to Friday?
  It's about five minutes a day" beats "can you help me out next week?"
- **Ask early.** Short notice turns a favour into an imposition.
- **Make it easy to say no:** "Totally fine if not — I've got a backup." Then *have*
  a backup.
- **Ask the right person:** someone with the skill or time, and someone you have (or
  are happy to build) a two-way relationship with.
- **Ask directly**, not through hints or guilt; hints force people to either volunteer
  awkwardly or ignore you.
- **Don't stack asks.** One favour at a time; let a request breathe before the next.

## 2. Making the favour easy to do
- Provide everything needed: keys, instructions, addresses, the tools, a written note.
- Offer to cover costs (fuel, parking, materials) up front.
- Show up on time and ready (for help moving: boxes packed, food and drinks there).
- Respect the limits they set (they can stay two hours, not all day).

## 3. Thanking and repaying
- **Thank promptly and specifically**, ideally in person or by message the same day.
- **Repay in kind or in a currency they value**: help with their move, a lift to the
  airport, a home-cooked meal, a bottle, a small gift, babysitting.
- **Size the repayment roughly to the favour.** A big favour (a full weekend helping
  you move) deserves more than a text; a small one (lending a ladder) is covered by
  thanks and lending back when asked.
- **Timing:** you don't have to repay immediately — offer: "Let me know whenever I can
  return the favour, I mean it" — and then **act when the opportunity arises**,
  without waiting to be asked.
- **Pay it forward too.** In neighbourhoods and communities, help given to others also
  maintains the shared pool.

## 4. Lending and borrowing
- **Borrower's rules:** return items promptly, clean, and in the condition received (or
  better — fill the tank, sharpen the blade). If you break it, say so and replace or pay.
- **Lender's rules:** only lend what you can afford to lose or have damaged; agree when
  you need it back.
- **Money between friends:** many people follow "only lend what you could treat as a
  gift". Put larger loans in writing, with a repayment schedule.

## 5. Being asked — and saying no
- You don't owe a yes. A clean no protects the friendship better than a resentful yes:
  "I'd love to help but I can't this weekend — could I do an hour on Sunday evening
  instead?" or simply "I'm sorry, I can't."
- Offer an alternative only if you mean it.
- **Watch for chronic one-way askers.** If someone only appears when they need something
  and never reciprocates, it's fine to say no more often.

## 6. Balance without scorekeeping
- **Keep a rough sense, not a ledger.** Over months, you should be broadly giving and
  receiving. If you notice you've asked a lot recently, look for ways to give back.
- **Asking can strengthen ties.** People often feel more warmly toward those they've
  helped (sometimes called the "Benjamin Franklin effect"; the research is mixed, but
  being willing to ask can signal trust).
- **Never itemise aloud** ("after everything I've done for you"); see
  relationships-social.gift-exchange-and-reciprocity-practice.reciprocity-norms.keeping-gift-exchange-balanced-over-time.

## Gotchas
- **Professional favours** (asking a friend who's a lawyer, accountant, designer or
  tradesperson for free work) cost them income. Offer to pay, or keep it to a quick
  question.
- **Favours with a hidden catch** ("I'll help you if...") damage trust; be upfront.
- **Neighbour relationships** last years; small favours early (taking in parcels,
  watching the house) build goodwill for bigger ones later.
- **Cultural norms differ**: in some communities favours are constant and uncounted;
  in others, independence is prized and asking can feel uncomfortable.

## Verify
- [ ] Your last ask was specific, early, and came with an easy out.
- [ ] You made the favour easy (supplies, costs covered, on time).
- [ ] You thanked them the same day and know how you'll return it.
- [ ] Borrowed items went back promptly and in good condition.
- [ ] You've said no without guilt when you couldn't help.
