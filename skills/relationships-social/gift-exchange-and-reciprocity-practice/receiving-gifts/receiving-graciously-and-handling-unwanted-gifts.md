# Receiving graciously and handling unwanted gifts

**Goal:** make the giver feel their effort was appreciated — whatever you think of the
gift — and deal with gifts you can't use without hurting anyone.

The receiver's job is to honour the **gesture**: the time, thought and money someone
spent on you. That's almost always genuinely worth thanking, even when the object
isn't to your taste.

## 1. In the moment
- **Open it while they're present** if that's the custom (it is in most Western
  settings). In some cultures — e.g. traditionally in parts of East Asia — gifts are
  set aside and opened later; follow the host culture, or ask (custom varies; unverified
  for every community).
- **Look at it properly** and at the giver, not just at the next parcel.
- **Thank them for something true and specific:**
  - "You remembered I've been wanting to try this!"
  - "What a lovely colour."
  - "This is so thoughtful — you went to a lot of effort."
- **Avoid false enthusiasm you can't sustain** ("I'll wear it every day!") — it sets you
  up for awkwardness later. Warm and honest-about-the-gesture beats gushing.
- **Don't comment on price** — not "you shouldn't have spent so much" repeatedly, and
  never "how much was it?"
- **Don't apologise for giving a smaller gift** in return; just say thank you.

## 2. The thank-you afterwards
- **When it's expected:** gifts received by post, wedding and baby-shower gifts, gifts
  from older relatives, and anything significant. Many families also expect
  children to send them.
- **Timing:** within a week or two for most gifts; for weddings, Western etiquette
  commonly allows up to about three months.
- **Format:** a handwritten card is the most valued; a call or message is fine for
  close, informal relationships.
- **Simple structure:**
  1. Thank them for the specific gift (or the amount, for money, more discreetly:
     "your generous gift").
  2. Say how you'll use or enjoy it ("It's already on the kitchen windowsill").
  3. A personal line about them or the occasion.
  4. Warm sign-off.

## 3. When you don't like it
- **Still thank them** for the thought; that's sincere.
- **Use it at least once around them** if it's practical and you can (e.g. wear the
  scarf when they visit), if that's comfortable for you.
- **If asked directly** whether you like it, find the true positive: "It's not what I'd
  normally pick, but I love that you thought of me."
- **Don't critique gifts later** to others who might pass it on.

## 4. Returns and exchanges
- **With a gift receipt:** it's generally fine to exchange quietly. If the giver might
  notice (e.g. clothing they'll expect to see), a light "It was a bit small so I swapped
  it for the next size up" is usually received well.
- **Duplicates** from a registry or wish list: exchange freely; the giver's intent
  was to give you that thing, and you have it.
- **Don't ask the giver for the receipt** unless you're close and they've offered.

## 5. Regifting — discreetly
- Acceptable to many people if done carefully, frowned upon by others.
- Rules: **never regift within the same social circle**, remove any tags or notes,
  only regift unused items in original condition, and keep a note of who gave what.
- Donating to charity is an alternative that avoids the risk altogether.

## 6. Gifts you should decline or handle carefully
- **Work rules:** many employers (especially public sector, healthcare, procurement)
  cap or ban gifts from clients or suppliers; check the policy and declare if required.
- **Overly lavish or intimate gifts** that imply an obligation you don't want — in
  dating, from a colleague, from someone you're not close to — can be politely
  declined: "This is incredibly generous, but I can't accept it. I really appreciate
  the thought."
- **Gifts used to control or pressure** (e.g. "after all I've given you") are a
  warning sign in a relationship.

## Gotchas
- **Over-reciprocating immediately** (running out to buy a matching gift) can turn a
  gift into a transaction; see
  relationships-social.gift-exchange-and-reciprocity-practice.reciprocity-norms.keeping-gift-exchange-balanced-over-time.
- **Forgetting who gave what** at a big event: have someone keep a list while gifts
  are opened.
- **Children** need coaching beforehand ("say thank you, even if it's not what you
  hoped for").

## Verify
- [ ] Your in-the-moment thanks named something true and specific.
- [ ] Thank-you notes went out within the expected window for each gift.
- [ ] Any exchange or regift was discreet and outside the giver's circle.
- [ ] You checked workplace gift rules before accepting from clients or suppliers.
