# Choosing a gift that fits the person and the relationship

**Goal:** give something the recipient will actually use or enjoy, at a level that
feels right for your relationship — so the gift says "I know you" rather than "I had
to buy something".

## 1. Collect clues all year
The best gift ideas come from listening, not from browsing in December.
- **Keep a running note** per person (phone notes app): things they mention wanting,
  hobbies, things they've broken or run out of, favourite authors, foods, colours, sizes.
- **Listen for "I've always wanted to..."** and "mine's falling apart".
- **Notice what they spend their own time and money on** — a good gift often upgrades
  something they already love.

## 2. Match the gift to the relationship stage
A gift carries a message about how close you are. Too big or too intimate can be
as awkward as too little.

| Relationship | Typical approach |
|---|---|
| New romantic partner (weeks) | Small, thoughtful, low-cost — a book they mentioned, a treat |
| Established partner | Personal, can be larger; something you've noticed they want |
| Close friend | Personal and fun; often an experience together |
| Colleague / secret Santa | Within the stated limit; universal, non-personal (food, plants, stationery) |
| Boss | Usually none individually; contribute to a group gift if one exists |
| Host / teacher / service provider | Small token; check workplace or school rules on gifts |

**Gotcha:** clothing, perfume and jewellery can feel very personal (and sizes are
risky); keep them for close relationships.

## 3. Calibrate the budget
- **Spend what you can afford.** The thought is visible in the choice, not the price.
- **Match roughly what the relationship expects** — group exchanges often have a set
  limit; follow it. Overspending can create an awkward debt (see
  relationships-social.gift-exchange-and-reciprocity-practice.reciprocity-norms.keeping-gift-exchange-balanced-over-time).
- If you're on a tight budget, a homemade gift, an offer of time, or a handwritten
  letter can mean a lot.

## 4. Choose the kind of gift
- **What they asked for.** Research suggests recipients tend to appreciate requested
  gifts more than givers expect, while givers often overrate the value of "surprise"
  (one study series; treat as a tendency). If they have a wish list or registry, use it.
- **Practical upgrade:** a better version of something they use every day.
- **Experience:** a meal, show, class or day trip — especially good for people with
  "everything", and doing it together adds a shared memory.
- **Consumables:** good food, drink (only if they drink), candles, flowers — no
  storage burden.
- **Personal / sentimental:** photos, a framed memory, something tied to an
  in-joke — best for close relationships.
- **Gift cards / money:** practical and welcome in many cultures; in others it can
  seem impersonal. A card for a *specific* place they love feels more thoughtful.

## 5. Avoid the classic misfires
- **Gifts that are really for you** (your hobby, your taste in music).
- **"Improvement" gifts** — diet books, gym memberships, self-help — unless explicitly requested.
- **Things that create work or clutter:** pets, large decor, items that need display
  when the giver visits.
- **Ignoring known restrictions:** dietary, alcohol, religious, allergy, or minimalist preferences.
- **Culturally loaded items:** e.g. clocks in Chinese tradition, knives in some
  cultures, certain flower types or numbers — check if you're unsure (examples
  unverified for every community).

## 6. Presentation
- Wrap it (even simply) and add a handwritten card — the note is often what's kept.
- **Include a gift receipt** for anything with sizes or that might be duplicated.
- Give on time; a late gift is fine, but say so warmly rather than apologising at length.

## Verify
- [ ] The idea came from something you heard or noticed about them.
- [ ] Size, intimacy and price fit the relationship stage and any group limit.
- [ ] It's for their taste, not yours, and isn't an "improvement" hint.
- [ ] Known restrictions and cultural meanings were checked.
- [ ] There's a card, and a gift receipt where useful.
