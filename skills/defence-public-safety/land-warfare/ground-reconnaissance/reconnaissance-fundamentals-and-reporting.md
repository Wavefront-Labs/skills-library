# Reconnaissance fundamentals and reporting

**Goal:** understand how ground reconnaissance answers a commander's questions about
terrain and enemy, and how to report what is seen so it is usable. Unclassified,
doctrine-level summary as taught at cavalry/reconnaissance schools and staff colleges.

## 1. Recon serves questions
Reconnaissance is driven by the commander's **information requirements** (US **CCIR /
PIR**): specific questions — "Is the bridge at X intact and what can it carry?" —
not "go and look around". The intelligence staff's **collection plan** assigns each
question to a sensor (ground scouts, drones, signals, etc.). Ground recon is chosen when
a human must judge terrain, see under cover or confirm what remote sensors suggest.

## 2. The fundamentals (US FM 3-98 list, paraphrased)
1. **Ensure continuous reconnaissance** — before, during and after an operation.
2. **Don't keep reconnaissance assets in reserve.**
3. **Orient on the reconnaissance objective** (the question).
4. **Report information rapidly and accurately** — late information is often worthless.
5. **Retain freedom of manoeuvre** — don't get decisively engaged.
6. **Gain and maintain contact** once made.
7. **Develop the situation rapidly** — decide quickly what a contact means.
Other armies phrase these differently; the ideas are near-universal.

## 3. Forms of reconnaissance
- **Route** — one line of movement: trafficability, bridges, choke points, bypasses.
- **Zone** — everything within boundaries; slowest, most thorough.
- **Area** — a specific location (a village, a crossing site).
- **Reconnaissance in force** — a deliberate fight to make the enemy reveal itself.
- **Special reconnaissance** belongs to special operations, a separate field.

**Stealth vs. fighting for information:** doctrine debates light, stealthy scouts versus
armoured recon able to fight. The usual answer is mixed: stealth by default, combat power
when the enemy screens. *(Drones have shifted this balance since ~2014; doctrine is
still adjusting.)*

## 4. Observation posts (conceptual)
An **observation post (OP)** is the basic recon building block: a concealed position
with a clear field of view over a named area, a reporting link, and a plan for relief,
resupply and withdrawal. Siting principles mirror fieldcraft — avoid obvious features
and skylines, use concealment, minimise movement
(see defence-public-safety.land-warfare.fieldcraft.camouflage-concealment-and-land-navigation).

## 5. Security operations
Recon units also protect the main body: **screen** (observe and warn), **guard**
(also fight to gain time) and **cover** (a self-contained force fighting well forward).
Each step up costs more combat power and buys more time.

## 6. Reporting
Use the format your force mandates; the common teaching mnemonic is **SALUTE**:
**S**ize, **A**ctivity, **L**ocation, **U**nit/uniform, **T**ime, **E**quipment.
Rules that matter more than the format:
- Report **what you saw**, separate from **what you think it means**.
- Give **time observed**, not time sent.
- "Nothing seen" in a named area is information — report negatives when asked.
- Precise locations and counts; avoid "a lot" or "near the wood".
- Route reports note load limits, widths, gradients and obstacles in a standard
  overlay, so engineers and planners can use them directly.

## Gotchas
- Recon held in reserve "for later" is the most common doctrinal error cited in
  training-centre observations.
- Recon that fights unnecessarily stops answering questions.
- Mnemonics vary (SALUTE, SALT, SIZE etc.); state which doctrine you follow.
