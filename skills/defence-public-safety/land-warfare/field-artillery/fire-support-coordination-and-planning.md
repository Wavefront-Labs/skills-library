# Fire-support coordination and planning

**Goal:** understand how indirect fire is planned and coordinated with manoeuvre forces —
who asks for fire, who approves it, and how the force avoids hitting its own troops.
This is an unclassified, conceptual summary of staff-college doctrine; it deliberately
contains no gunnery procedure, ballistics or targeting data.

## 1. What artillery is for
Doctrine describes fire support by the **effect** sought, not the amount fired:
- **Suppression** — prevents the enemy observing or firing for the duration of fire;
  the most common requirement in support of movement
  (see defence-public-safety.land-warfare.infantry-tactics.fire-and-movement-and-small-unit-tactical-principles).
- **Neutralisation** — renders a target temporarily ineffective.
- **Destruction** — permanent; expensive in time and ammunition, so used sparingly.
- Also **obscuration** (smoke) and **illumination**.
Bailey's history argues that from 1917 the decisive change was *indirect, predicted*
fire integrated into a plan, not bigger guns.

## 2. Organisation (typical, nation-dependent)
- Guns are grouped into **batteries**, batteries into **battalions/regiments**.
- **Observers** (forward observers, joint fires observers) sit with manoeuvre units and
  request fire; **fire-support officers/cells** at each headquarters plan and
  coordinate; the firing units deliver.
- Artillery is assigned a **tactical mission** (e.g. direct support to one formation vs
  general support to the whole force) that defines whose calls it answers first.

## 3. The planning cycle
Fire-support planning runs inside the headquarters' main planning process
(see defence-public-safety.military-planning-and-command.operational-planning.military-decision-making-process-and-orders-production):
1. **Commander's guidance for fires** — what fires must achieve, in which phase,
   with what priority.
2. **Decide – Detect – Deliver – Assess (D3A)** is the common targeting methodology:
   decide what matters and when, allocate sensors to find it, deliver the effect,
   assess whether it worked and re-engage or move on.
3. **Fire-support plan / annex** to the order: priorities of fire, allocation of
   assets, coordination measures, and the **observation plan** — fires are only as
   good as their observers.
4. **Rehearsal** — fire-support rehearsals are among the most valuable, because timing
   with manoeuvre is where plans fail.

## 4. Coordination measures (the safety net)
**Fire-support coordination measures (FSCMs)** are lines and areas on the shared map:
- **Permissive:** **Coordinated Fire Line (CFL)** — beyond it, fires need no further
  coordination; **Fire Support Coordination Line (FSCL)** — beyond it, attacks are
  coordinated rather than cleared, mainly to deconflict air and land fires;
  **Free-Fire Area**.
- **Restrictive:** **No-Fire Area (NFA)** — e.g. friendly patrols, hospitals, protected
  sites; **Restrictive Fire Area (RFA)**; **Restrictive Fire Line** between converging
  friendly forces.
Measures must move with the battle; a stale CFL is a fratricide risk.

**Clearance of fires:** before engagement short of permissive measures, the owner of the
ground confirms no friendly forces, civilians or protected objects are at risk — the
same point where legal (law of armed conflict) review of proportionality and distinction
applies.

## 5. Counter-battery (conceptual)
Locating and engaging enemy artillery (by radar, sound ranging historically, and now
drones) to protect the force. Historically decisive in WWI and again prominent since
2022 *(current practice is evolving; not detailed here).*

## Gotchas
- Terms differ: UK "fire support coordination centre"/"joint fires cell" ≈ US
  "fires cell". Always state the doctrine source.
- The FSCL is widely misunderstood as "a line beyond which anything goes" — it is a
  coordination line, and fires beyond it are still coordinated.
- Fire planning that ignores observation, communications and ammunition supply looks
  fine on paper and fails in execution.
