# Structured cold-case review

**Goal:** reinvestigate an unsolved case in a way that finds what the original enquiry
missed, uses science that didn't exist then, and doesn't repeat its blind spots. The
reviewer's posture is not to criticise the original team but to look with fresh eyes and
better tools.

## 1. Triage: which cases to work
Cold-case units have far more cases than capacity. Rank by **solvability factors**:
- Exhibits retained and in a condition suitable for modern testing (the top factor).
- A named or nameable suspect pool.
- Witnesses still alive and traceable; relationships that may have changed (former partners
  and associates often speak once loyalties shift).
- Evidence types newly testable (touch DNA, Y-STR, degraded DNA, genealogy, digital recovery).
Record the triage scoring so prioritisation decisions are defensible.

## 2. Audit the whole file before forming a view
- Locate everything: case papers, actions, messages, statements, unused material, exhibit
  registers, lab reports, photographs, media coverage. Older paper systems (e.g. card indexes,
  early HOLMES) may be split across stores.
- Rebuild the **timeline** and the original **lines of enquiry**; list which were never
  completed and why.
- Note hypotheses the original team favoured — and evidence they discounted.
- Check the **exhibit chain**: what exists, where, how stored, whether continuity is intact.
  Missing continuity limits what new results can prove in court.

## 3. Forensic re-examination
- Hold a forensic strategy meeting with the lab: which items, in what order, which techniques,
  how to conserve material for defence re-testing.
- Typical gains: DNA from previously "untestable" items, Y-STR on mixed samples, reanalysis
  of fingerprints against expanded databases, fibres or ballistics comparisons through national systems.
- Search new profiles in the national database (CODIS in the US, NDNAD in the UK) — offenders
  convicted since may now be on it.
- **Familial searching** and **investigative genetic genealogy (IGG)** are tightly governed:
  US DOJ interim policy (2019) limits IGG to violent crimes and unidentified remains after
  conventional database searches fail, using services whose users have opted in; several
  states have their own statutes. UK familial searching requires specific authorisation.
  A genealogy lead is intelligence, not evidence — confirm with a direct reference sample lawfully obtained.

## 4. Witnesses and suspects
- Re-interview with modern methods (see peace-model-investigative-interviewing); witnesses
  may now say what they withheld out of fear or loyalty.
- Trace and eliminate: work through the original suspect pool against new evidence,
  documenting eliminations.
- Media and anniversary appeals can produce new witnesses; plan hold-back information.

## 5. Victim's family
- Assign a family liaison officer. Families have often waited decades; manage expectations,
  explain the review's limits and don't promise outcomes.
- Tell them before media announcements and before any arrest becomes public.

## 6. Legal points
- Charging decisions must consider delay-related prejudice and whether evidence lost over time
  disadvantages the defence.
- In England & Wales, re-trying a person previously acquitted requires Court of Appeal
  approval under the Criminal Justice Act 2003 Part 10 "new and compelling evidence" provisions.

## Gotchas
- Retesting consumes the last of a sample — always consult on preservation first.
- Treating the original theory as the starting point rather than one hypothesis.
- Exhibits destroyed under routine retention schedules; flag cold-case retention holds.
