# Securing, cordoning and logging a crime scene

**Goal:** keep a scene as close as possible to its state at the time of the offence until
examiners arrive, and be able to prove who entered, when and why. Locard's principle —
every contact leaves a trace — cuts both ways: you leave traces too.

## 1. First officer at scene: the "5 Ps" order
1. **Preserve life** — first aid and medical access override evidence. Note what medics
   moved or cut (clothing, furniture) and their names.
2. **Preserve the scene** — stop further entry.
3. **Protect evidence** — from weather, traffic, animals, people.
4. **Prevent contamination** — limit your own movement; don't use the toilet, phone, taps or
   bins; don't smoke, eat or touch.
5. **Pursue offenders** — where a suspect is nearby, but coordinate so this doesn't trample the scene.
Make notes of first observations immediately: doors and windows (open/closed, locked),
lights, smells, temperature, people present, anything moved.

## 2. Cordons
- **Inner cordon** around where the offence and key evidence are; only examiners and
  essential staff in full PPE.
- **Outer cordon** further out to control the public, media and non-essential police;
  a rendezvous point (RVP) for arriving resources sits outside it.
- Start **big** — shrinking a cordon is easy, enlarging it after people have walked
  through is impossible. Include likely entry/exit routes and where items may have been discarded.
- Use physical tape and posted officers; tape alone isn't a barrier.

## 3. Common approach path (CAP)
A single route in and out, chosen to avoid probable offender routes, marked (stepping plates,
tape). Everyone uses it. Record who set it and when; examiners may move it after examining it.

## 4. The scene log
Started by the first scene guard and kept continuously. For every person in or out of the
inner cordon: name, role/agency, time in, time out, reason for entry, PPE worn. Also record:
times of cordon changes, weather changes, items brought in or removed, refusals of entry.
The log is an exhibit — write legibly, never leave gaps, and hand it over formally with a
signature when relieved.

## 5. Contamination control
- PPE at the inner cordon: suits, double gloves, overshoes, masks (DNA). Change gloves between items.
- **Cross-contamination between linked scenes** (victim, suspect, vehicle, suspect's home) is
  a classic defence argument. Different officers and examiners should handle victim and
  suspect scenes; record who went where.
- Suspects should not be transported in the same vehicle or held in the same room as victims.

## 6. Handover and forensic strategy
- Brief the crime scene manager/CSI: what happened, what's been touched, who's been in.
- The investigator and scene manager agree a **forensic strategy**: what the examination
  should establish (hypotheses), priorities, order (e.g. photography and fragile evidence
  first), and submissions.
- Photograph/video overall, mid-range, and close-up before anything is moved.
- Multi-scene incidents get a scene number each and a coordinated log.

## Gotchas
- Senior officers "just having a look" without logging — log them anyway, and challenge politely.
- Securing the room but not the garden, car or route the offender used.
- Relieving the scene guard without a logged handover.
- Releasing the scene before the examiner and investigator both agree.

## Verify
- [ ] Inner and outer cordons drawn generously and physically controlled.
- [ ] CAP marked and used by all.
- [ ] Scene log has no gaps; every entry has a reason.
- [ ] Linked scenes handled by different personnel and recorded.

*Uncertainty:* terminology is UK-based; US agencies use similar concepts (perimeters, scene
security logs) with local naming.
