# Lines of enquiry, decision logs and the case file

**Goal:** investigate methodically — keep alternative explanations open, record why each
decision was made, and assemble a file a prosecutor can charge on and a defence can't pick
apart. Based on UK investigative doctrine with US equivalents noted.

## 1. The investigative mindset
- **ABC: Assume nothing, Believe nobody, Challenge everything.** Including your own first theory.
- Keep at least two **competing hypotheses** alive (e.g. "assault by partner" vs "fall
  while intoxicated" vs "third party") and look for evidence that would *disconfirm* each.
- Confirmation bias and tunnel vision are the main causes of miscarriages of justice in case
  reviews. Name them in your log when you choose to close a line.

## 2. The golden hour(s)
Evidence is most available and most perishable right after the event:
- Preserve scenes (see crime-scene-management) and life first.
- Identify and separate witnesses; get first accounts.
- Capture volatile material: CCTV (overwrite cycles can be days), phone/ANPR data
  requests, social media posts, call recordings.
- Trace and arrest suspects where grounds exist; secure their clothing and devices.

## 3. Lines of enquiry
Typical fast-track and main lines:
- **Scene(s):** forensic opportunities, house-to-house.
- **Victim:** victimology — routine, relationships, recent changes, risk factors.
- **Witnesses:** identification, interview plan, vulnerable/intimidated witness needs.
- **Suspects:** TIE (Trace, Interview/Implicate, Eliminate) criteria for the pool of possible suspects.
- **Passive data:** CCTV, communications data, financial records, ANPR, digital devices.
- **Intelligence:** local systems, informant reporting (sanitised), other forces.
- **Media/public appeal:** consider timing and what to withhold (hold-back information that
  only the offender would know).
For each line: objective, owner, priority, deadline, outcome. On larger enquiries this sits in
an incident-management system (UK HOLMES2) as actions.

## 4. The decision log (policy file)
Record significant decisions **at the time**: what was decided, by whom, what was known,
the options considered and why this one. Include decisions *not* to do something (e.g. not
to submit an item for DNA) and the resource or proportionality reasoning. Reviews and courts
are far more forgiving of a reasoned decision that turned out wrong than of an unexplained one.

## 5. Timeline and evidential matrix
- Build a master timeline from every source (phone, CCTV, statements) with source references.
  Conflicts between sources are where the investigation is.
- For each element of the offence (points to prove), list the evidence for it, its source
  and its weakness. Gaps become actions.

## 6. The prosecution file
- Summary of the case in plain language, points to prove with evidence references.
- Statements, exhibits list with continuity, interview records/transcripts.
- **Disclosure:** record and retain all relevant material, including what undermines the
  prosecution or helps the defence (UK: CPIA 1996, unused-material schedules; US: *Brady*,
  *Giglio*). Disclosure failures collapse trials.
- Anticipated defences and how the evidence answers them.

## Gotchas
- Writing the log after the outcome is known (hindsight drafting) — it reads that way.
- Letting a suspect's arrest end other lines of enquiry.
- Treating "no further action" decisions as not worth recording.

## Verify
- [ ] At least two hypotheses recorded with evidence for and against.
- [ ] Every line of enquiry has an outcome, including "closed – reason".
- [ ] Points-to-prove matrix complete with no unexplained gaps.
- [ ] Unused material reviewed for disclosure.

*Uncertainty:* structure follows UK doctrine; US agencies use similar but locally defined
case-management systems.
