# IED incident organisation, the ICP and operator qualification

**Goal:** understand how a suspected improvised explosive device incident is organised so
that first responders, incident commanders and support staff do the right things around the
specialist operator. Render-safe procedures (RSPs) are restricted, taught only to certified
bomb technicians/IEDD operators, and are deliberately not covered here.

## 1. First-responder actions (before EOD arrives)
- **Don't touch, move, cover or open** a suspect item. Don't use radios or mobile phones
  within the immediate vicinity (local policy sets a distance, commonly tens of metres) —
  confirm your agency's rule.
- **Confirm, Clear, Cordon, Control** (UK "4 Cs", often extended with "Check" for secondary
  devices): confirm it's suspicious from a distance, clear people out, set a cordon, control
  access.
- Evacuation distances: use your agency's stand-off guidance (e.g. the public DHS/NCTC
  Bomb Threat Stand-Off Chart scales distance by device size, from about 70 ft/20 m for a
  pipe bomb mandatory evacuation to far larger for vehicles). When unknown, go bigger and use
  hard cover; glass is the main blast injury hazard to people inside buildings.
- **Secondary devices:** assume one may target responders or evacuees. Search the cordon,
  the ICP and assembly areas before using them; vary assembly points from any published plan.
- Gather information for the operator: who found it, when, what it looks like, what has
  happened to it, any warnings or calls received, building plans and utilities.

## 2. The incident control point (ICP)
- Located outside the cordon, out of line of sight of the device, behind hard cover, upwind,
  with access for vehicles and an exit route. Searched before occupation.
- Houses the incident commander, the EOD team and liaison from police, fire, EMS and the
  site owner. Single point of contact and logging.
- The EOD operator advises; the incident commander retains overall command of the incident
  (cordons, evacuation, traffic, public messaging). Clarify that division at handover.

## 3. Operator principles (the "why" behind EOD decisions)
- **Remote first:** use robots and remote tools before any manual approach.
- **One-person risk:** only one operator is exposed on a manual approach; everyone else stays
  at the ICP.
- **Minimum time at the device.** Every approach is planned with a defined purpose.
- **Wait times ("soak time")** after any remote action before approaching, per doctrine.
- Preserve evidence where it doesn't add risk — forensic recovery helps identify bomb-makers
  and prevent the next device (see post-blast-investigation).
These principles shape timelines: commanders should expect long incidents and plan relief,
traffic and public communications accordingly.

## 4. Command and communications
- Log all decisions and timings at the ICP.
- Press and public messages coordinated through the incident commander; avoid describing the
  device or tactics.
- Stand-down only when the operator declares the area safe and the scene has been handed over
  for investigation or returned.

## 5. Qualification paths
- **US:** civilian bomb technicians qualify through the FBI Hazardous Devices School (Redstone
  Arsenal) and maintain certification under National Bomb Squad Commanders Advisory Board
  standards (recertification cycles and continuing training hours).
- **UK:** police explosives officers and military Ammunition Technicians/ATOs complete defence
  EOD and IEDD courses; military IEDD qualification is graded by competence level.
- Other countries run equivalent national schools; NATO's EOD Centre of Excellence harmonises standards.

## Gotchas
- Evacuating people to an unchecked assembly point.
- Responders gathering in line of sight of the device.
- Commanders pressing the operator for speed — time pressure kills technicians.

## Verify
- [ ] Cordon set from agency stand-off guidance, with hard cover used.
- [ ] ICP and assembly points searched before use.
- [ ] Information pack ready for the operator on arrival.
- [ ] Command split between incident commander and EOD operator agreed and logged.
