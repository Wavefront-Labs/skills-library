# Land release, survey and clearance under IMAS

**Goal:** return land to communities safely and efficiently, using evidence to **cancel**
land that isn't contaminated, **reduce** areas through survey, and **clear** only where mines
are confirmed. Full clearance of every suspected hectare is neither necessary nor possible;
the International Mine Action Standards (IMAS) define how to release land with confidence.
Hands-on demining drills and mine-specific procedures are taught on accredited courses and
are outside this summary.

## 1. Land release: cancel, reduce, clear
- **Suspected hazardous area (SHA):** an area with indirect evidence of contamination.
- **Confirmed hazardous area (CHA):** direct evidence (a mine found, an accident, a reliable
  eyewitness).
- **Cancel** through non-technical survey, **reduce** through technical survey, **clear** what
  remains. Each decision is documented and justified so the national authority can accept it.

## 2. Non-technical survey (NTS)
- Collect and analyse information **without** entering the hazard: community interviews
  (farmers, former combatants, local authorities, accident survivors), records, maps, satellite
  imagery, land-use observations (fields farmed for years without accident are evidence).
- Use multiple, independent informants; record their reliability and what they saw first-hand.
- Polygon the evidence-based hazard boundaries; remove previous over-estimates. Poor early
  surveys often recorded huge SHAs that NTS can cancel.
- Community liaison and gender/diversity-sensitive engagement improves information — women,
  children and herders know different land.

## 3. Technical survey (TS)
- Physical intervention to confirm or refute contamination and define boundaries: targeted
  detector lanes, mechanical ground preparation or dog teams in sample areas.
- Results drive area reduction or confirm a CHA for clearance.

## 4. Clearance
- **Manual teams** (detector and excavation drills), **mechanical assets** (flails, tillers,
  ground-preparation machines — usually verified by manual or dog follow-up) and
  **mine detection dogs**; the combination is set by terrain, vegetation, soil and threat.
- **Clearance requirement** sets the depth and the area; national standards adapt IMAS to
  local mine types and conditions.
- Every item found is recorded and destroyed per procedure (usually in situ or at a demolition site).

## 5. Worksite safety (IMAS 10.20)
- Marked worksites with control point, safe areas, and lanes; minimum separation distances
  between deminers set by the threat.
- Personal protective equipment (frontal protection and visor) to the standard required.
- **Medical support:** trained medic and casualty-evacuation plan on every worksite, with
  the casualty-to-hospital time known and rehearsed.
- Daily briefings, fatigue management (strict work/rest cycles in heat), and accident/incident
  reporting and investigation.

## 6. Quality management and records
- **QA** (accreditation of organisations and procedures, monitoring) and **QC** (sampling of
  released land by an independent body) before handover.
- Data goes into the national information management system (commonly IMSMA-based).
- **Handover** to the community and authority with a certificate stating what was done to what
  standard; post-clearance monitoring of residual contamination with a clear liability procedure.

## 7. Context
States party to the Anti-Personnel Mine Ban Convention (Ottawa, 1997) have clearance
deadlines (Article 5) and report progress; cluster munitions fall under the 2008 Convention.

## Gotchas
- Skipping NTS and clearing unnecessary land, delaying release elsewhere.
- Single-informant evidence used to cancel land.
- Medevac plans that have never been rehearsed.
- Releasing land without QC sampling or handover documentation.

## Verify
- [ ] Every polygon has an evidence basis recorded.
- [ ] Cancel/reduce/clear decisions justified per national standard.
- [ ] Worksite medevac rehearsed; times known.
- [ ] QC passed and handover signed.
