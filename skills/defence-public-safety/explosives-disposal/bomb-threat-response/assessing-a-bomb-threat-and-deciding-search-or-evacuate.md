# Assessing a bomb threat and deciding: search or evacuate

**Goal:** respond to a bomb threat proportionately. Nearly all threats are hoaxes, but
automatic evacuation every time causes disruption, invites repeat hoaxes, and can move
people *towards* a real device. Good practice is a pre-written plan, a structured assessment
and a decision by a named, trained person, in consultation with police.

## 1. Taking the threat
- Keep a **bomb threat checklist** at every phone that receives external calls (CISA and
  NPSA publish free ones). On a call: stay calm, keep the caller talking, don't hang up
  afterwards (on some systems leaving the line open helps tracing), and fill in the checklist:
  exact words, when it will go off, where it is, what it looks like, why, who they are,
  caller's voice, accent, background noise, number displayed.
- Written/email/social media threats: preserve the message, don't forward widely, don't
  delete; screenshot with headers/metadata.
- **Call the police** immediately (emergency number) and give them the checklist details.
- Notify the designated decision-maker for the site.

## 2. Assessing credibility
Considerations (no single one is conclusive):
- **Specificity:** a named location, device description, timing and motive suggest higher risk
  than "there's a bomb in the building".
- **Context:** current threat level, events at the site, recent incidents, known disputes,
  a wave of similar hoax emails to many institutions at once (common, and usually a hoax campaign).
- **Corroboration:** any suspicious item found, unusual activity, CCTV.
Classify per your plan (e.g. low/medium/high) and record the reasoning.

## 3. The decision options
| Option | When |
|---|---|
| **Continue normal operations** (with heightened vigilance) | Low credibility, nothing found |
| **Search** (staff search with occupants in place, or reduced occupancy) | Credible enough to look, no specific location |
| **Shelter in place / invacuation** (move people to protected interior areas) | Threat outside or location unknown, outside routes risky |
| **Evacuate** | Specific credible threat in the building, or suspicious item found |
| **Lockdown** | Threat combined with active hostile behaviour |
Evacuation routes and **assembly points must be checked** for suspicious items first, and
assembly points should be well away (stand-off guidance: hundreds of metres for large devices)
and not in car parks. Have alternatives; don't always use the same published point.

## 4. Running a staff search
- People who know the area search it — they spot what's out of place.
- Use a pre-planned **search plan** dividing the site into sectors with search cards; start with
  public areas, evacuation routes and assembly points, then outside, then the rest.
- Search systematically: floor to waist, waist to head, head to ceiling.
- Radios/phones: follow your plan's rule (commonly, don't transmit within close distance of a
  suspicious item).
- On finding something: **don't touch it**. Apply the HOT test — is it **H**idden deliberately,
  **O**bviously suspicious (wires, batteries, smell, liquids), **T**ypical for the location or not?
  If suspicious: clear the area, cordon, inform police, and evacuate per plan.
- Report each sector as clear to the control point.

## 5. After
Record every decision and its time; debrief; review the plan. Repeat hoaxes are crimes —
preserve evidence for the police investigation.

## Gotchas
- No checklist at reception, so the caller's exact words are lost.
- Evacuating through areas that haven't been searched.
- Decision made by whoever is nearest rather than a trained, named person.
- Wave hoaxes treated as unique each time instead of coordinated with police.

## Verify
- [ ] Checklists at every external phone; staff trained.
- [ ] Named decision-maker and deputies listed in the plan.
- [ ] Search plan with sectors and cards exists and is rehearsed.
- [ ] Assembly points have alternatives and are searched before use.

*Uncertainty:* distances and radio rules vary by authority; use your national guidance.
