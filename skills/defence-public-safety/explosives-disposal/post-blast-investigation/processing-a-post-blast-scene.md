# Processing a post-blast scene

**Goal:** after an explosion (criminal, accidental or undetermined), recover the evidence that
shows what exploded, how it was initiated, and who was responsible — while keeping the
investigators safe in an unstable scene. This is forensic scene practice; it overlaps with
crime-scene management (see
defence-public-safety.criminal-investigation.crime-scene-management.securing-cordoning-and-logging-a-crime-scene).

## 1. Safety first, always
- Life saving and casualty removal come first; record what rescuers moved.
- **Secondary devices:** the scene must be swept by bomb technicians before investigators work.
- **Structural safety:** engineers assess damaged buildings; don't enter until cleared.
- Other hazards: gas leaks, fire, electrical, sharp debris, biological material, asbestos and
  dust. PPE suited to the hazard; respiratory protection where dust is present.
- Undetermined cause: treat as criminal until shown otherwise — accidental gas or dust
  explosions are investigated with the same rigour (NFPA 921 covers both).

## 2. Perimeter
- Find the **furthest piece of debris** and set the outer perimeter at roughly **1.5 times**
  that distance (a common rule of thumb in NIJ guidance). Fragments travel surprisingly far,
  including onto roofs and into neighbouring buildings.
- Inner cordon around the seat and dense debris; scene log, common approach path and
  contamination control as in any major scene.

## 3. Locate the seat and plan the search
- The **seat** (crater or point of greatest damage) and the direction of damage (bending,
  pushing of materials, scorch, fragment strikes) indicate where the explosion originated.
- Agree a **forensic strategy** with the scene manager, bomb technician and lab: hypotheses
  (device type, vehicle-borne, accidental source), search priorities, sampling plan.
- Divide the scene into zones/grids or use spiral search patterns from the seat outward;
  assign teams; photograph and map each zone before search, and each item in place before recovery.
- Use total stations or 3D laser scanning to map item positions — they support reconstruction
  and later court presentation.

## 4. Evidence recovery
- **Device components:** fragments of containers, wiring, power sources, switches, timers,
  tape, adhesives, and anything foreign to the scene. Recover everything that might be part
  of a device; the lab decides relevance.
- **Residue sampling:** swab surfaces near the seat and on fragments, take soil/debris from
  the crater; collect **control samples** from unaffected areas and **blank** swabs/containers
  to show the sampling kit was clean. Store in approved airtight containers; separate from any
  known explosive materials (including the bomb squad's own).
- **Victims:** post-mortem and medical teams recover fragments from bodies and injured people;
  coordinate with disaster victim identification (DVI) in mass-fatality incidents.
- Sift crater material through screens; vacuum or sweep fine debris where planned.
- CCTV, witness accounts (sound, flash, smoke colour, smell) and phone video are evidence too.

## 5. Reconstruction and reporting
- Lab analysis identifies explosive residues and component origins; national databases (e.g.
  the US TEDAC for device exploitation) link devices across incidents.
- Reconstruction combines the seat analysis, debris distribution and recovered components into
  a conclusion about the device and initiation, stated with its level of confidence.
- The report records methods, sampling with controls, chain of custody, and alternatives
  considered (accident vs deliberate) — the same discipline as any expert report.

## Gotchas
- Perimeter too small, so key components are trampled or swept by street cleaners.
- No controls or blanks taken, making residue findings challengeable.
- Bomb squad equipment contaminating samples.
- Rushing to reopen roads and losing the outer debris field.

## Verify
- [ ] Secondary-device sweep and structural clearance recorded before entry.
- [ ] Perimeter based on furthest debris; scene log running.
- [ ] Zones searched, photographed and mapped; positions recorded.
- [ ] Residue samples taken with controls and blanks; continuity complete.

*Uncertainty:* the 1.5× perimeter rule is a common guide, not a fixed standard; follow
your agency's procedures.
