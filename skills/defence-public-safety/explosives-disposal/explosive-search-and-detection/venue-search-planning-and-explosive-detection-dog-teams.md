# Venue search planning and explosive-detection dog teams

**Goal:** before an event or a protected visit, confirm a venue holds no explosive device and
then keep it that way until the event ends. A search is only as good as its plan, its
coverage record and the security that follows it. Specialist search teams are led by trained
search advisers (UK: PolSA/PolSA-trained officers; elsewhere bomb squad or protective services).

## 1. Planning
- **Define the task:** area, start/finish times, what "clear" will mean, threat context.
- **Recce** with venue staff: floor plans, plant rooms, ceilings/voids, roof, service areas,
  vehicle parks, deliveries, contractor activity, keys and access.
- **Sector the venue** into areas a pair can search in a defined time; number them on a plan.
  Prepare a **search card** per sector listing rooms, voids and features.
- **Priority order:** typically areas where the protected person or crowd will be (stage,
  VIP areas, seating), entry and exit routes, then everything else; external areas and
  vehicle parks included.
- Resources: search teams, detection dogs, technical equipment (mirrors, endoscopes, ladders,
  torches), venue staff with keys, communications plan, EOD on call.

## 2. Conducting the search
- Searchers work in **pairs**; each room or space is searched by height bands (floor to waist,
  waist to head, head to ceiling, then ceiling voids) and systematically (e.g. clockwise from the door).
- Venue staff accompany to unlock spaces and identify what is normal; unfamiliar items are
  what you're looking for.
- Each sector is marked when complete (sticker, seal or tape per plan) and reported to the
  search control point, which updates the master plan.
- Anything suspicious: don't touch, clear the immediate area, report to the search adviser who
  escalates to EOD. The rest of the search usually continues elsewhere under adviser direction.

## 3. Explosive-detection dogs (EDD)
- Dogs search faster in large or cluttered spaces, vehicles and seating rows. Use them as part
  of the plan, not as a replacement for physical search of voids and fixtures they can't reach.
- Work dogs in short periods with rests; heat, noise and food smells degrade performance.
- The handler directs the pattern; the search adviser assigns dogs to sectors where they add
  most value.
- "Vapour wake" trained dogs screen moving people in crowds; they need specific training and
  certification and are deployed per agency rules.
- Dog teams need current certification and documented training with blind exercises (see
  defence-public-safety.customs-border-and-wildlife-enforcement.detection-dog-handling.detection-dog-training-aids-records-and-screening-deployments).

## 4. Declaring the area sterile — and keeping it so
- When all sectors are reported clear, the adviser declares the area searched and hands it to
  security. Record the time.
- **Maintaining integrity** is the hard part: control every access point from that moment;
  screen people, bags, deliveries and vehicles entering; escort contractors; patrol cleared
  areas. A search followed by uncontrolled access is wasted.
- Late changes (extra deliveries, stage changes) require re-search of affected sectors.

## 5. Records
Search plan, cards completed and signed, timings, any finds and their resolution, dog teams
used, and the handover/integrity log. These are evidence if an incident occurs.

## Gotchas
- Searching the public areas thoroughly and forgetting plant rooms, roofs and ceiling voids.
- Contractors still working inside after the area is declared clear.
- Dogs worked to exhaustion on a long venue.
- No master plan, so two teams search one sector and nobody searches another.

## Verify
- [ ] Every sector on the master plan marked complete with time and searcher names.
- [ ] Voids, plant and external areas included.
- [ ] Access control in place at the moment of handover.
- [ ] Re-search triggers defined and used.

*Uncertainty:* terminology follows UK practice; team sizes and techniques vary by agency.
