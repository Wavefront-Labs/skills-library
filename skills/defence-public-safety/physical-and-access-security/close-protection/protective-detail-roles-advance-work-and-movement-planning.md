# Protective detail roles, advance work and movement planning

**Goal:** keep a principal (the person protected) safe, healthy and on schedule
through planning, not confrontation. Most of close protection is risk assessment,
advance work and logistics; a detail that is improvising is already behind. Reference
for protection professionals and corporate security managers; licensing (e.g. SIA
close protection licence in the UK), firearms rules and legal powers vary by
jurisdiction and are not covered here.

## 1. Start with a threat and risk assessment
- **Principal profile:** public visibility, controversies, known fixated persons,
  medical conditions, family, routines and preferences.
- **Threats:** realistic ones first — medical emergencies, road accidents, crime and
  harassment, unwanted media and protesters, fixated individuals — before rare
  high-impact scenarios.
- **Protective intelligence:** monitor communications and open sources for
  concerning approaches or threats; coordinate with law enforcement where criminal.
- Scale the detail to the assessed risk; over-protection draws attention and irritates
  principals.

## 2. Detail roles (generic)
- **Team leader / detail leader:** plans, briefs, liaises with the principal's staff.
- **Personal protection officer (PPO):** closest to the principal.
- **Advance agent(s):** ahead of the principal at venues and on routes.
- **Driver(s)** trained in protective/defensive driving; **residential security team**
  where applicable; **operations/intelligence** support for larger details.
Small details combine roles; everyone needs medical skills.

## 3. Advance work
For each venue:
- Arrival and departure points (primary and alternate), parking, the walking route
  inside, holding rooms, toilets, and where the principal will stand or sit.
- Contacts: venue security, management, local police where relevant.
- **Nearest suitable hospitals** (trauma capability, route time), and safe havens
  (police stations, secure hotels) along routes.
- Crowd, media and protest expectations.
- Communications coverage (mobile and radio dead spots).
Produce a short advance report that the team leader approves and the team is briefed on.

## 4. Movement planning
- Routes: primary and alternate, with timings; **avoid predictability** in routes
  and times for regular movements.
- Vehicle plan: who rides where, vehicle checks, fuel, medical kit, spare
  communications.
- **Emergency action plans:** medical emergency, vehicle breakdown, crowd surge,
  hostile approach — each with a clear "who does what" and where to go (car, safe
  room, hospital). The default response to danger is to **cover and evacuate** the
  principal, not to engage.

## 5. Client care and conduct
- Discretion: confidentiality agreements, no social media, no discussion of the
  principal's affairs.
- Professional appearance matched to the environment; low profile is often more
  effective than visible muscle.
- Manage the principal's staff relationships; the itinerary changes constantly.

## Gotchas
- Medical incidents are statistically far more likely than attacks — keep medical
  training current and an AED/trauma kit available.
- Plans briefed but not rehearsed fail under stress.
- Over-reliance on one route or hotel because it is convenient.

## Verify
- [ ] Written threat and risk assessment, reviewed when circumstances change.
- [ ] Advance report for every venue, including hospital and alternates.
- [ ] Emergency action plans briefed and walked through.
- [ ] Team roles assigned; communications tested.
