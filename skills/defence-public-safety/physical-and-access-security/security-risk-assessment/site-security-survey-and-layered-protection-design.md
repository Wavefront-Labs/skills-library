# Site security survey and layered protection design

**Goal:** find out what actually needs protecting at a site, from what, and which
affordable measures reduce the risk most — then present it in a way the owner can
fund and implement. Reference for security consultants and managers; methodology
follows common practice (ASIS guidance, ISO 31000 risk principles, CPTED).

## 1. Scope and assets
- Agree scope with the client: site(s), what's out of scope, and who the decision-maker is.
- Identify **assets**: people first, then information, property, operations and
  reputation. For each, the consequence of loss or harm (safety, financial,
  operational, legal).

## 2. Threats
- Gather data: crime statistics for the area, the site's incident history, sector
  threat reporting, insider-risk factors, police and neighbour input.
- Describe **realistic threat scenarios** in plain words: "opportunist theft from the
  unattended loading bay", "disgruntled former employee re-entering with a retained
  card", "protest at the main entrance". Avoid generic lists.

## 3. Survey the site (vulnerabilities)
Walk it at different times, including night and shift change. Work outside in:
- **Perimeter:** boundaries, gates, lighting, vegetation, signage.
- **Approach and grounds:** parking, sight lines, hiding places, vehicle access.
- **Building shell:** doors, windows, roof access, service entries.
- **Interior:** reception, access control zones, sensitive rooms, key control.
- **Systems:** CCTV coverage and image quality, alarms, monitoring and response times.
- **People and procedures:** visitor management, tailgating, guard instructions,
  training, how leavers are handled.
Photograph and log findings with location; don't rely on memory.

## 4. Assess risk
- For each threat scenario, rate **likelihood** and **impact** (a 5×5 matrix is
  common) considering existing controls; document the reasoning, not just the score.
- Identify the few scenarios that drive most of the risk.

## 5. Design: layers
Use **deter, detect, delay, respond** as a test for each scenario:
- The **delay** provided by physical barriers must exceed the time from **detection**
  to an effective **response** arriving — otherwise detection is just a record.
- Layers (defence in depth): perimeter → grounds → building → room → asset.
- **CPTED** principles: natural surveillance (sight lines, lighting), natural access
  control (one obvious route in), territorial reinforcement (clearly owned spaces),
  and maintenance (well-kept sites signal guardianship).
- Procedural and people measures often outperform hardware on cost.
- Safety and fire egress requirements are non-negotiable constraints.

## 6. Report
- Executive summary: top risks and top recommendations, in plain language.
- Recommendations **prioritised** (immediate / short / medium term) with indicative
  cost and the risk each one reduces.
- Keep the report itself protected — it is a map of the site's weaknesses.

## Gotchas
- Recommending technology before understanding the response capability.
- Surveying only in daytime office hours.
- Copying a previous client's report template's recommendations.

## Verify
- [ ] Assets and consequences listed with the client.
- [ ] Threat scenarios are specific and evidence-based.
- [ ] Each recommendation traces to a scenario and states the risk reduction.
- [ ] Delay vs detection-to-response time checked for top scenarios.
- [ ] Report classified and distributed only to need-to-know readers.
