# Strategic bombing theory, targeting doctrine and interdiction

**Goal:** analyse and discuss air-to-ground attack at the level of a staff college:
the theories behind it, the process doctrine prescribes for choosing what to strike,
the legal limits, and the historical evidence about what works. This is unclassified
conceptual material. It deliberately contains no weaponeering, target data or current
procedures — those are classified and specific to real forces.

## 1. The theories, in order
| Thinker / school | Core claim | What history said |
|---|---|---|
| Douhet (1921) | Bombing cities breaks civilian will; the bomber always gets through | Morale proved resilient (Blitz, Germany, Japan); bombers needed escorts |
| Trenchard / Mitchell | Independent air forces; attack the enemy's vital centres | Drove service independence; mixed operational results |
| ACTS "industrial web" (1930s) | Precision attack on key industrial nodes collapses an economy | Precision was poor in WWII; but oil and transport attacks in 1944–45 were decisive per USSBS |
| Warden "five rings" (1980s–90s) | Treat the enemy as a system: leadership, essentials, infrastructure, population, fielded forces; strike inward | Shaped 1991 planning; critics say it underrates adaptive enemies |
| Effects-based operations | Plan for effects, not target lists | Useful idea; formal EBO was later dropped from some joint doctrine as over-engineered |
| Pape (coercion) | Denial (defeating military strategy) coerces better than punishment | Influential; contested by other scholars |

## 2. Strategic attack vs interdiction vs CAS
- **Strategic attack:** aimed at the adversary's capacity or will to wage war as a whole.
- **Air interdiction:** divert, disrupt, delay or destroy enemy forces *before* they can be
  used against friendly forces; needs no detailed integration with friendly ground fire.
- **Close air support:** attack near friendly forces, requiring detailed integration
  (see `defence-public-safety.air-and-space-warfare.close-air-support.close-air-support-doctrine-and-air-ground-integration`).
- Interdiction works best combined with ground pressure that forces the enemy to move and
  consume supplies (Italy 1944's Operation Strangle showed its limits without it).

## 3. The joint targeting cycle (public doctrine)
US JP 3-60 describes six phases, NATO AJP-3.9 is similar:
1. End-state and commander's objectives. 2. Target development and prioritisation.
3. Capabilities analysis. 4. Commander's decision and force assignment.
5. Mission planning and force execution. 6. **Assessment.**
Key ideas: targets are justified by the **objective** they serve; **deliberate** targeting
works to a planned cycle (the air tasking order), **dynamic** targeting handles fleeting
targets; assessment (**battle damage assessment**, then munitions effectiveness, then
whether objectives are met) feeds back in.

## 4. Mission packaging, conceptually
A **composite air operation** combines strikers with supporting roles — escort fighters,
suppression of air defences, electronic warfare, tankers, airborne command and control,
reconnaissance. Historical lesson: unescorted bombers suffered prohibitive losses
(Schweinfurt–Regensburg, 1943) until long-range escort arrived. Packaging trades mass and
mutual support against complexity and coordination risk.

## 5. Law of armed conflict (always in scope)
- **Distinction:** only military objectives may be attacked.
- **Proportionality:** expected incidental civilian harm must not be excessive relative to
  the concrete and direct military advantage anticipated.
- **Precautions:** take feasible steps to verify targets and minimise civilian harm.
- WWII area bombing predates Additional Protocol I; judging it requires historical context,
  but modern doctrine treats these rules as binding and legal review as part of the cycle.

## Gotchas
- Equating bomb tonnage or sorties with effect — measure against objectives.
- Assuming precision guarantees effect: wrong target selection is precise failure.
- Ignoring enemy adaptation (dispersal, repair, substitution) — WWII German production rose
  until 1944 despite bombing.
- Citing bombing statistics without the source; USSBS figures were themselves contested.
- Uncertainty note: the historical judgements above summarise mainstream scholarship;
  several (Pape, EBO) remain actively debated.
