# Marksmanship fundamentals and coaching a qualification shoot

**Goal:** get a shooter producing small, consistent groups, centred on the aim point, and
able to pass their service's qualification course of fire. All live and dry practice runs
under the range's safety orders and the safe-handling drill in
`defence-public-safety.weapons-handling.small-arms.safe-handling-and-make-safe-drills-for-service-weapons`.

## 1. The fundamentals (wording varies; content does not)
1. **Stable position** — bone support over muscle; body behind the weapon; the weapon
   naturally points at the target (**natural point of aim**). Test: close eyes, breathe,
   open — if the sights have drifted off, move the body, not the arms.
2. **Breathing** — fire in the natural pause at the bottom of the breath; don't hold
   for long or vision and steadiness degrade.
3. **Sight alignment and sight picture** — focus on the **front sight / reticle**, not the
   target. Alignment errors cause angular error; a blurred target is fine.
4. **Trigger control** — a smooth, straight-back press, so the moment of release is a mild
   surprise. Jerking or "snatching" is the most common fault.
5. **Follow-through** — keep the position, sight picture and trigger to the rear through
   the shot, then reset. Call where the sights were when it fired.

## 2. Group first, zero second
- A shooter who cannot group cannot be zeroed: sight adjustments on a scattered pattern
  chase noise. Get a consistent group (e.g. a 5-round group meeting your service's size
  standard at the zeroing distance), **then** move the mean point of impact to the aim
  point with sight clicks per the handbook.
- Zero to the shooter, the weapon, the sight and ideally the ammunition lot. Re-confirm
  after any sight change, knock or change of firer.

## 3. Reading the group (coach's diagnosis)
| Pattern | Common cause |
|---|---|
| Tight group off-centre | Sights/zero, or consistent sight-picture error — not a firing fault |
| Vertical stringing | Breathing, inconsistent cheek weld/head position |
| Low and to the firm-hand side (pistol) | Anticipating recoil — pushing/jerking the trigger |
| Large, random group | Unstable position, poor trigger control, fatigue |
| One flyer | Called or uncalled? Uncalled flyers suggest a flinch |
These are tendencies, not laws — watch the shooter, not just the paper.

## 4. Coaching drills
- **Dry-fire** with a weapon **proved empty** and no live ammunition in the room, aimed at a
  safe backstop, to groove trigger control.
- **Ball-and-dummy:** the coach loads magazines mixing live and inert drill rounds unseen;
  a dip of the muzzle on a dummy exposes flinch to shooter and coach. Use only
  authorised inert rounds and keep strict accountability for both types.
- **Watch the shooter** from the side: head lifting, eyes closing, shoulder rolling into
  recoil. Give **one correction at a time**, then let them shoot.
- Fatigue matters: short, frequent sessions beat one long one.

## 5. Before qualification
- Walk the shooter through the **exact course of fire** — distances, positions, time limits,
  magazine changes, scoring.
- Rehearse stoppage drills and position changes dry until they are automatic.
- Confirm the zero shortly before the qualification shoot.
- Debrief failures with the fault, not the score: "trigger snatch under time" is coachable.

## Uncertainty
Group-size standards, zeroing distances, click values and courses of fire are service- and
weapon-specific; take them from the current handbook. Group diagnosis is heuristic.

## Verify
- [ ] The shooter can explain and demonstrate all five fundamentals.
- [ ] Consistent groups exist **before** any zero adjustment.
- [ ] You can diagnose a group and give one targeted correction.
- [ ] Dry-fire and ball-and-dummy use proved-empty weapons and accountable inert rounds.
- [ ] The shooter has rehearsed the exact qualification course of fire.
