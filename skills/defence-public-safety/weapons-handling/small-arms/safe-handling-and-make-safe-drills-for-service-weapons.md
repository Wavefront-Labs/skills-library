# Safe handling and make-safe drills for service weapons

**Goal:** never have an unintended discharge, and never hand over, store or clean a weapon
you have not personally proved empty. This is the generic discipline that sits under every
model-specific drill; **your weapon's issued handbook and your unit's standing orders always
override this page** (exact sequences differ between rifles, pistols and shotguns).

## 1. The four rules (every service teaches a version)
1. **Treat every weapon as loaded** until you have proved it otherwise yourself, now.
2. **Never point the muzzle at anything you are not willing to destroy.** Muzzle awareness
   applies while drawing, holstering, slinging, climbing into vehicles and in clearing bays.
3. **Finger straight and off the trigger** (outside the trigger guard, along the frame) until
   the sights are on the target and you have decided to fire.
4. **Be sure of your target and what is beyond it.** Rounds over-penetrate and ricochet.

The rules overlap on purpose: an accident needs at least two of them broken at once.

## 2. Generic unload / make-safe sequence
Done pointing in a safe direction (a clearing bay, sand trap or designated direction):
1. Safety catch / selector to **safe** where fitted; finger off the trigger.
2. **Remove the ammunition source first** — magazine out (or tube emptied on a shotgun).
   Doing step 3 before this just loads the next round.
3. **Operate the action** to eject any round in the chamber and lock it open if the design allows.
4. **Inspect:** look into the chamber and magazine well (**visual**), and where your drill
   says so, check with a finger (**physical**) — light, dirt and fatigue fool the eye.
5. Have it **checked by a second person** where orders require (armoury handback, range end-ex).
6. Close the action and, if your drill includes it, fire off the action / ease springs
   *pointing into the clearing bay*; return the selector to safe.
7. Pick up and account for the ejected round and the magazine.

**Gotcha:** the classic negligent discharge is "dropped the magazine *after* cycling the
action" or "checked the chamber, then reinserted a loaded magazine out of habit". Say the
steps out loud under training conditions until the order is automatic.

## 3. Clearing bays and handovers
- Use the clearing bay/barrel **every** time you enter a building, vehicle park or armoury
  where orders require it, even if "I didn't load it today".
- **Hand over a weapon open and empty**, action locked back where possible; the receiver
  proves it themselves before accepting. Never accept "it's clear" verbally.
- Never leave a weapon unattended or out of your control; signed-for means you are
  accountable (see `defence-public-safety.weapons-handling.armoury-and-weapons-maintenance.armoury-accountability-and-weapons-inspection`).

## 4. Holstering and carriage
- Holster slowly, watching the holster, with the thumb behind the slide/hammer area and the
  finger outside the guard; clothing and kit toggles entering the guard cause discharges.
- Use the retention level your force issues; check the holster is the right one for the
  weapon and any fitted light.
- Slung rifles: muzzle down or up per local order, never sweeping people in a queue or vehicle.

## 5. After a negligent/unintentional discharge
1. Stop. Muzzle in a safe direction. Make the weapon safe and prove it.
2. **Check for casualties** and give first aid; call for help.
3. Do not clean or alter the weapon — it may be examined.
4. Report immediately through the chain of command; expect an investigation. Most forces
   treat honest immediate reporting very differently from concealment.

## Uncertainty
The visual + physical check, "fire off the action" step and selector positions vary by
weapon and nation; this is a generic synthesis, not one army's text. Confirm against your
issued handbook.

## Verify
- [ ] You can recite the four rules and give an example of each being broken.
- [ ] You remove the ammunition source **before** operating the action, every time.
- [ ] You can prove a weapon empty visually and physically, then have it checked.
- [ ] You never accept or hand over a weapon without both parties proving it.
- [ ] You know your unit's clearing-bay locations and post-ND reporting chain.
