# Armoury accountability and weapons inspection

**Goal:** know where every weapon is at every moment, and know that every weapon issued
is serviceable. The two jobs are linked: an unaccounted weapon is a security incident, and
an unserviceable one is a safety incident. **Every action below is limited to the
maintenance level you are authorised for** — operators clean and function-check; only
qualified armourers repair, using authorised parts and the technical manual. No one
modifies a service weapon outside an approved modification instruction.

## 1. Accountability
- **Register by serial number**, not by type and quantity. Each weapon, major component
  with its own serial (e.g. some upper/lower receivers, optics) and accessory class is listed.
- **Issue and receipt:** the individual signs for the specific serials; the armourer proves
  every weapon empty on return (see `defence-public-safety.weapons-handling.small-arms.safe-handling-and-make-safe-drills-for-service-weapons`)
  and checks serials against the issue record before signing it back in.
- **Counts:** a physical count of every weapon at the frequency your regulations require
  (often daily on opening/closing, plus periodic full serial checks by someone independent
  of the armoury). Count against the register, not against yesterday's count.
- **Two-person control** for opening the store and for key/combination access, where
  required; keys are signed for and never leave the controlled location.
- **Discrepancies are reported immediately** up the chain and, for loss or theft, to the
  police/military police. Do not "find it tomorrow" before reporting.
- Physical security — locks, racks, alarms, separation of weapons from ammunition — is set
  by regulation; check the store against it at every handover.

## 2. Operator field maintenance
1. Make safe and prove empty.
2. **Field strip only to operator level** shown in the handbook; nothing else comes apart.
3. Clean the bore, chamber and working parts of carbon and fouling; clean optics with lens
   materials only.
4. **Lubricate** per the handbook — the right lubricant, the right places, not drowned.
   Climate matters: less in sand and dust, more protection in wet and salt.
5. Reassemble and carry out the **function check** in the handbook (safety, selector,
   trigger and reset, action cycling, magazine seating).
6. Report anything that fails a function check, is cracked, bent or missing — don't fix it.

**Gotcha:** the most common field faults are a **dirty or dry weapon**, damaged
magazines and loose sight mounts. Inspect magazines (feed lips, springs, followers) and
witness-mark optic mounts so movement is visible.

## 3. Armourer inspection and gauging
- Scheduled inspections follow the **technical manual's checklist**: visual condition,
  function, and **gauges** such as headspace, bore and muzzle-erosion gauges, plus spring
  and trigger checks where specified.
- A weapon that fails gauge or function limits is **tagged unserviceable**, removed from
  issue and either repaired by an authorised level or sent back for condemnation —
  never "issued to someone careful".
- Record inspection results and parts changed against the serial; history reveals
  weapons that repeatedly fail.
- Use calibrated gauges and record their calibration dates.

## 4. Tagging convention (adapt to yours)
| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Serviceable | Inspected, within limits, may be issued |
| Unserviceable — repairable | Withdrawn; awaiting authorised repair |
| Unserviceable — beyond repair | Awaiting condemnation and disposal under control |
| Quarantined / evidence | Involved in an incident; do not touch or clean |

## Uncertainty
Count frequencies, two-person rules and maintenance levels are set by each service's
regulations; gauge types and limits are weapon-specific and come only from the TM.

## Verify
- [ ] Every weapon is tracked by serial number, and the last count matched the register.
- [ ] Every return was proved empty and serial-checked before sign-in.
- [ ] Keys and access are controlled and logged.
- [ ] Operators stopped at operator-level strip and passed the function check.
- [ ] Failed items are tagged and out of the issue pool; gauges are in calibration.
