# Less-lethal use-of-force policy and deployment safety

**Goal:** use a less-lethal option only when it is lawful, necessary and proportionate,
deploy it in the way that minimises injury, and look after the subject afterwards.
"Less-lethal" is not "non-lethal": every one of these tools has caused deaths, usually
through misuse, vulnerable subjects or poor aftercare. **Your force's policy, the
manufacturer's current warnings and your certification override this page.**

## 1. The legal frame (common to most jurisdictions)
Before and during any use, you must be able to articulate:
- **Legality** — a power or lawful purpose exists (self-defence, prevention of crime, arrest).
- **Necessity** — lesser options (presence, communication, tactical repositioning, time)
  were not workable or had failed.
- **Proportionality** — the harm risked is not excessive for the threat faced.
- **Accountability** — you will record and justify it.
Most forces wrap this in a decision model (UK: National Decision Model; many US agencies:
a force-options framework rather than a rigid ladder). Re-assess continuously; a lawful
first use does not justify continued use once the threat reduces.

## 2. Conducted-energy devices (CED / Taser-type)
- **Only certified users** carry them; re-certify on the schedule your force sets.
- **Spark-test/function-check** at the start of duty per policy; check cartridges are in date.
- **Preferred target areas** are the back or the lower front torso/legs; **avoid the head,
  neck, chest/heart area and groin** (manufacturer warnings).
- **Minimise cycles.** Each cycle needs fresh justification; prolonged or repeated
  exposure is linked to serious harm. Use the incapacitation window to control and restrain.
- **Consider fall risk** — height, water, roads, stairs; a fall can be more dangerous than
  the device. Never use near flammables (some irritant sprays are flammable).
- **Higher-risk groups:** pregnant women, children, the elderly or frail, people of very
  low body mass, and anyone showing signs of **acute behavioural disturbance** (heat,
  agitation, superhuman strength). The last is a medical emergency first.

## 3. Irritant sprays (PAVA, OC, CS)
- Spray from the distance on the canister label (too close risks eye injury from the jet).
- Expect cross-contamination of colleagues and bystanders; announce before use where safe.
- **Aftercare:** move to fresh air, reassure, keep upright, flush eyes with copious cool
  water, remove contaminated clothing, monitor breathing; no creams or rubbing.

## 4. Impact munitions and batons
- Launched impact rounds are issued to specialists; aim zones are the large muscle masses
  of the lower body/limbs per policy — **head, neck, spine and chest strikes are potentially lethal**.
- Respect the published minimum and maximum engagement ranges for the munition.
- Batons: target large muscle groups; avoid head, neck, spine, joints and kidneys.

## 5. After every use
1. Restrain safely; **never leave a subject prone** longer than needed (positional asphyxia).
2. First aid and medical assessment; CED probes in sensitive areas are removed by medical staff.
3. Preserve evidence: cartridges, AFIDs, device logs, body-worn video.
4. Complete the use-of-force report the same shift; download the device data.

## Uncertainty
Target zones, spray distances and cycle guidance change with manufacturers' updates and
national guidance; check the current versions — do not rely on this summary.
