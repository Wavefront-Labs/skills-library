# Permissible-purpose skip tracing and location verification

**Goal:** locate a person for a legitimate legal or financial purpose — collecting a
debt, serving process, enforcing a judgment, finding a missing heir or witness —
using lawful sources, confirming you have the *right* person, and refusing work that
looks like stalking or harassment. Reference mainly reflecting US practice; the UK,
EU and elsewhere apply data-protection law (e.g. GDPR) with different rules. Not legal advice.

## 1. Purpose comes first
- Record the **client, the legal purpose and the authority** for every file before
  searching. Commercial data products in the US are gated by law:
  - **FCRA** — consumer reports only for a permissible purpose.
  - **GLBA** — "credit header" data (name, address, SSN from credit files) only under
    listed exceptions (e.g. fraud prevention, legal compliance).
  - **DPPA** — motor vehicle records only for permitted uses.
  Vendors require you to certify the purpose on each search; that certification is
  audited and false certification carries liability.
- **Debt collection** contact with third parties to find a debtor is restricted
  (FDCPA "location information" rules and Regulation F in the US).
- **Red flags — decline or escalate:** the client is an ex-partner or family member,
  vague purpose, urgency to find a person who has moved away, requests for daily
  movements, or a subject in an **address confidentiality programme** or with a
  protective order. Locating a domestic-abuse survivor for an abuser has led to deaths;
  responsible firms screen for this.

## 2. Sources by category
- **Client data:** application forms, past addresses, references, employer, phone,
  bank details — often the best leads and already lawfully held.
- **Licensed commercial databases** (identity-resolution products aggregating
  credit headers, utility, property and phone data) — via permissible-purpose accounts.
- **Public records:** property and tax records, court filings, business registrations,
  professional licences, voter records where lawfully accessible, obituaries and probate.
- **Open sources:** search engines, social media visible to the public, news.
- **Contact development:** speaking with relatives, neighbours or former employers —
  honest identification required where law requires it, no pretexting for protected
  records (pretexting to get bank or phone records is a crime in the US).

## 3. Confirming identity and address
- Resolve to the right person with **multiple identifiers**: full name and variants,
  date of birth, past addresses, associates. Common names produce false matches.
- Treat aggregated data as **leads**, not facts — records are often stale or merged.
- Verify the current address with at least two independent, recent indicators (e.g.
  a recent property or utility record plus a confirming contact or site check),
  and date the verification.
- Document every source, date and finding in the file (audit trail).

## 4. Reporting
Report only what the purpose needs (address for service, not lifestyle detail),
grade the confidence, and note conflicts. Retain and destroy data per law and contract.

## Gotchas
- Same-name relatives (Jr./Sr.) at the same address.
- Old data reported as current because a vendor "last seen" date is ambiguous.
- Contacting a debtor's employer or family in ways that reveal the debt.

## Verify
- [ ] Permissible purpose recorded and certified for each search.
- [ ] Misuse red flags considered; any concerns escalated.
- [ ] Identity confirmed on at least two identifiers beyond name.
- [ ] Address verified by two independent recent sources and dated.
- [ ] Report limited to what the purpose requires.
