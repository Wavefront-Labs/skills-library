# Lawful case surveillance: planning, logs and admissible video

**Goal:** observe and document a subject's activities for a legitimate case —
typically an insurance or compensation claim, litigation or workplace investigation —
in a way that is lawful, proportionate and produces evidence a court will accept.
Reference for licensed investigators; this covers governance and documentation, not
covert tradecraft. Not legal advice; laws differ significantly by jurisdiction.

## 1. Before deploying: is this lawful and justified?
- **Legitimate purpose and instruction** in writing (e.g. insurer disputing the
  extent of a claimed injury). Record why surveillance is necessary and why less
  intrusive methods (records, interviews) won't do.
- **Legal limits:** observe only from places you are lawfully allowed to be;
  respect **reasonable expectations of privacy** (no filming into homes, bathrooms,
  medical settings); no audio recording of conversations you're not party to; no GPS
  trackers without lawful authority; and conduct that becomes a course of following
  can amount to **harassment or stalking** under criminal law. UK: data-protection
  law applies to private investigators (RIPA governs public authorities, not private
  firms) — document your lawful basis and a proportionality assessment.
- **Licensing:** confirm you and any subcontractors are licensed where required.

## 2. Planning
- **Pre-surveillance research:** confirm the subject's identity (a recent photo),
  address, vehicles, work pattern and the specific activities relevant to the claim.
- **Site assessment** of the area (lawfully, from public places) for safe, legal
  observation points and parking rules.
- **Objectives:** what activity would be relevant (e.g. lifting, sport, working a
  second job), so you don't collect irrelevant personal life.
- **Notification:** many investigators inform local police of their presence and
  vehicle to avoid alarming neighbours — follow your firm's policy.
- **Safety:** check-in schedule, no confrontation, plan for the subject approaching you.

## 3. Observation logs
Keep **contemporaneous** notes, time-stamped to the minute: time, location, what the
subject did, conditions (weather, light), and when observation began and ended — plus
gaps ("lost sight 10:42–10:55"). Write facts, not conclusions ("carried two shopping
bags, approx. 5 kg each, from car to house, no visible limp"). Never edit after the fact;
add dated amendments.

## 4. Admissible video
- Camera **date/time set accurately** and checked against a reliable source; note any offset.
- Record **continuously** across relevant activity where possible; explain breaks in the
  log. Selective editing invites the accusation of misleading clips.
- Preserve **original files** unaltered; hash where your process supports it; produce
  working copies for the report; maintain **chain of custody**.
- Identify the subject clearly (an establishing shot linking person to place).
- Narration or captions only in separate files, not burned over the original.

## 5. Termination criteria
Stop and report when: the subject appears aware of you (continuing risks a
harassment complaint and your safety), conditions make lawful observation impossible,
the objective is met, or the authorised hours run out. Never escalate to stay on a subject.

## 6. Reporting
Chronological report keyed to log entries and video file names/timestamps, objective
language, total hours, and a statement of methods and limitations. Activities
inconsistent *and* consistent with the claim are both reported — omitting favourable
observations undermines credibility.

## Gotchas
- Clock drift and time-zone/DST errors that break the timeline.
- Filming third parties (children, neighbours) unnecessarily.
- Over-interpreting short clips of activity.

## Verify
- [ ] Written instruction with purpose; necessity and proportionality recorded.
- [ ] Observation points lawful; no audio or trackers without authority.
- [ ] Log entries contemporaneous, factual and gap-annotated.
- [ ] Original video preserved with clock check and chain of custody.
- [ ] Report includes both consistent and inconsistent observations.
