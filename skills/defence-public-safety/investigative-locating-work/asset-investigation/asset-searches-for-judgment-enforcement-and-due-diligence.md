# Asset searches for judgment enforcement and due diligence

**Goal:** find out what a person or company owns, and in what name, so that a creditor
can decide whether to sue and how to collect, or an investor can decide whether a
counterparty is what it claims. Reference for investigators and litigation support;
the methods below use public records and legal process. Not legal advice; rules
differ by jurisdiction (US-centric with UK notes).

## 1. Define the question
- **Pre-litigation:** is the defendant collectable? (No point winning against
  someone with nothing reachable.)
- **Post-judgment:** where are reachable assets, to levy, garnish or lien?
- **Due diligence:** do the subject's stated assets, companies and history check out?
The question sets the depth and budget.

## 2. Public-record sources by asset type
- **Real property:** county recorder/assessor records (deeds, mortgages, tax rolls);
  UK HM Land Registry title registers. Check spouses, trusts and LLCs as owners.
- **Business interests:** Secretary of State filings, annual reports (officers,
  registered agents); UK Companies House (officers, persons with significant control,
  accounts, charges); aggregators such as OpenCorporates for multi-jurisdiction leads.
- **Liens and secured debt:** **UCC filings** reveal lenders and collateral (which
  hints at equipment, receivables, accounts); tax liens; judgment liens.
- **Litigation:** court dockets (federal PACER, state courts) — other creditors,
  divorces (financial disclosures), bankruptcies (schedules of assets under oath).
- **Vehicles, vessels, aircraft:** where lawful — FAA aircraft registry and vessel
  registries are public; state motor vehicle data is DPPA-restricted in the US.
- **Intellectual property:** patent and trademark assignments.
- **Open sources:** news, professional profiles, company websites, social media
  (lifestyle indicators: property, boats, businesses).

## 3. Bank accounts and financial assets — use legal process
Bank and brokerage account information is **not** lawfully available by pretexting
(obtaining it by impersonation is a federal crime under GLBA in the US, and illegal
in the UK). Lawful routes:
- **Post-judgment discovery:** interrogatories, document requests, depositions or
  debtor's examinations (UK: order to obtain information).
- **Subpoenas** to banks identified through leads (cheques the debtor paid, UCC filings,
  payments received by the client).
- Bankruptcy schedules and examinations.

## 4. Tracing ownership and transfers
- Map **corporate structures**: officers, shared addresses, registered agents, nominee
  directors, related entities formed around the time of the dispute.
- Look for **transfers to insiders** after the claim arose (property deeded to a
  spouse for nominal consideration) — potentially voidable as fraudulent/voidable
  transfers; counsel decides.
- Offshore structures need jurisdiction-specific counsel and, often, local investigators.

## 5. Reporting
State each asset, its owner of record, value indicator, encumbrances (mortgages,
liens), source and date retrieved; separate verified facts from inferences; attach
record copies. Note which assets are likely **exempt** (e.g. homestead protections)
for counsel's analysis.

## Gotchas
- Assessed value ≠ market value ≠ equity after mortgages.
- Common names and recycled company names create false matches.
- Records lag reality — a sale may not yet be recorded.
