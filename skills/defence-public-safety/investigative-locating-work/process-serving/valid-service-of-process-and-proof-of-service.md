# Valid service of process and proof of service

**Goal:** deliver court documents to the right person in a way the court will accept,
and prove it with an affidavit that survives challenge. Defective service can void a
default judgment months later, so the details matter. Rules vary by court and
jurisdiction — check the specific rules for the court issuing the papers (e.g. FRCP
Rule 4 and state rules in the US, CPR Part 6 in England & Wales). Not legal advice.

## 1. Know which methods are allowed
- **Personal service:** handing the documents to the person named. If they refuse to
  take them, most jurisdictions allow leaving them in the person's presence after
  telling them what they are — check local rules.
- **Substituted (abode) service:** leaving documents at the person's dwelling with a
  person of suitable age and discretion who resides there (US FRCP 4(e)(2)(B) model);
  some states require a follow-up mailing.
- **Corporations:** serve the registered agent or an authorised officer.
- **Mail, email or other alternative service:** only where the rules permit or a court
  orders it (often after showing diligent attempts failed). **Publication** is a last resort.
- **International:** the **Hague Service Convention** (where applicable) sets routes —
  personal delivery by a private server may be prohibited in some member states.
- **Restrictions:** some jurisdictions bar service on Sundays or certain holidays,
  or in certain places (e.g. some courthouses); minors and incapacitated persons have
  special rules.

## 2. Before you go
- Check the papers: correct court, case number, all documents and copies, any
  deadline for service (e.g. 90 days after filing under FRCP 4(m)).
- **Identify the person:** description, photo if the client has one, date of birth,
  vehicles; verify the address (see the skip-tracing skill
  `defence-public-safety.investigative-locating-work.skip-tracing.permissible-purpose-skip-tracing-and-location-verification`).
- Know the licensing or registration rules for servers in that county/state.

## 3. Serving
- Confirm identity: ask the person to confirm their name; note any refusal and
  physical description (height, build, age, hair, distinguishing features).
- Say you are serving court papers; hand over; leave.
- Stay lawful: no trespass after being told to leave, no force, no impersonation of
  officials, no deceptive pretext that your jurisdiction prohibits. Be polite and
  disengage from confrontation — safety first.
- **Evasive parties:** vary days and times (early morning, evening, weekend where
  permitted), try workplace (where lawful and discreet), and record every attempt.
  Persistent evasion supports a motion for alternative service.

## 4. Proof of service
The affidavit/proof of service (under penalty of perjury) states:
- Server's name and qualifications/licence number.
- Exact documents served.
- Date, time and address of service.
- Method, and to whom (name or description; relationship for substituted service).
- Physical description of the person served.
- For diligence affidavits: each attempt with date, time and result.
Many servers keep **GPS-stamped logs and photos** of attempts; some states require
electronic records. **"Sewer service"** (falsely claiming service) is fraud and has led
to prosecutions and mass vacated judgments.

## Gotchas
- Serving the right name at the wrong address (same name, different person).
- Leaving papers with a visitor or a minor who doesn't live there.
- Missing the service deadline while chasing an evasive party — seek alternative service early.

## Verify
- [ ] Method permitted by the issuing court's rules for this party.
- [ ] Identity confirmed and description recorded.
- [ ] Every attempt logged with date, time, location and result.
- [ ] Affidavit complete, accurate and signed; filed within the deadline.
