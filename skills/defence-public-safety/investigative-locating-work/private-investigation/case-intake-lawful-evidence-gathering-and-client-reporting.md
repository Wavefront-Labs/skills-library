# Case intake, lawful evidence gathering and client reporting

**Goal:** take on cases you can lawfully and ethically do, gather evidence that will
survive scrutiny in court or a tribunal, and report it so the client (often a lawyer
or insurer) can act. Reference for licensed investigators; licensing is required in
most US states and many countries (the UK has no statutory licensing at time of writing
but data-protection and other laws apply) — check yours. Not legal advice.

## 1. Intake and screening
- **Who is the client, and why do they want this?** Record the purpose. Common lawful
  work: litigation support, insurance claims, due diligence, workplace misconduct,
  locating witnesses, infidelity or custody matters (handled carefully).
- **Screen for misuse:** requests to locate an ex-partner, to monitor someone's
  movements without a legal purpose, or to get phone/bank/medical records are red
  flags. Decline or refer.
- **Conflict check** against existing and past clients and subjects.
- **Engagement letter:** scope, deliverables, budget/retainer, lawful-methods clause,
  confidentiality, who owns the work product (attorney-directed work may be privileged
  as work product — the lawyer should engage you directly when that matters).

## 2. Planning
Define the questions to answer and the evidence that would answer them; choose
methods (records research, interviews, surveillance, open-source review) and estimate
hours. Agree check-in points with the client.

## 3. Lawful limits (US-centric — confirm locally)
- **Pretexting** to obtain financial (GLBA) or telephone records (Telephone Records and
  Privacy Protection Act) is a crime; impersonating police or officials is illegal everywhere.
- **Recording conversations:** federal law and most states are one-party consent;
  some states (e.g. California) require all-party consent. Never record a conversation
  you are not party to.
- **Trespass:** no entry onto private property without permission; observing from
  public places is usually lawful.
- **GPS trackers:** many states restrict or ban placing a tracker on a vehicle you do
  not own or have consent for — common ground for civil and criminal liability.
- **Data protection:** in the UK/EU, investigators processing personal data need a
  lawful basis and must handle data securely and for limited periods.
- **Social media:** view only what is publicly visible; no fake friend requests to
  represented parties (lawyers' ethics rules apply to their agents).

## 4. Evidence handling
- Contemporaneous notes with dates and times; original files preserved, working
  copies used; hashes for digital files where feasible.
- **Chain of custody** for physical items and media.
- Interview statements signed where possible, in the witness's words.

## 5. The report
- Structure: purpose and scope, summary of findings, methods, detailed chronological
  findings with sources, exhibits list, and limitations.
- **Facts vs opinion** clearly separated; no speculation presented as fact.
- Write for a reader who will be cross-examined on it — or for yourself on the stand.
- Deliver securely; retain per licence rules and contract.

## Gotchas
- Scope creep into unlawful methods to satisfy an impatient client.
- Losing privilege because the client (not counsel) engaged you.
- Reports padded with irrelevant personal detail — a data-protection and credibility risk.

## Verify
- [ ] Purpose documented; misuse screening and conflict check done.
- [ ] Engagement letter signed with lawful-methods clause.
- [ ] Every method checked against local law (recording, tracking, trespass, pretexting).
- [ ] Evidence preserved with notes and chain of custody.
- [ ] Report separates fact from opinion and states limitations.
