# Lost-person behaviour and hasty search tactics

**Goal:** find a missing person in wild terrain faster by searching where people of
that type are statistically likely to be, containing the area early, and treating
clues as seriously as the subject. Reference for SAR team members and coordinators;
your team's training, the search manager and local protocols direct the actual search.

## 1. Fix the start point
- **PLS** (point last seen) or **LKP** (last known position, e.g. a car at a trailhead
  or a phone ping) becomes the **IPP** (initial planning point).
- Protect it: the IPP area holds scent for dogs and tracks for trackers. Keep vehicles
  and searchers off it until those resources have had a chance.

## 2. Lost-person behaviour (LPB)
Robert Koester's *Lost Person Behavior* (based on the International Search & Rescue
Incident Database, ISRID) gives, per subject category (hiker, hunter, child by age band,
person with dementia, despondent person, autistic subject, etc.):
- **Distance from IPP** rings (25%, 50%, 75%, 95% of subjects found within).
- **Elevation change**, typical find locations (structure, road, drainage, brush),
  and behaviours, which differ by category.
- **Survivability** trends by time and weather.
Use it to prioritise, not to exclude: the subject may be the exception. Tables are split by terrain and climate (temperate mountain and flat, dry, urban) — use the right table and confirm figures against your copy of the book.

## 3. The first hours: reflex tasks
- **Hasty teams:** small, fast, self-sufficient teams check high-probability
  routes — trails, drainages, the route the subject planned — and likely spots
  (shelters, viewpoints, junctions where people take wrong turns).
- **Containment:** trail blocks, road patrols, camp-ins at junctions, and **track traps**
  (smoothed soil or sand at chokepoints, checked periodically) stop the search area growing.
- **Attraction:** sirens, whistles, lights at night, and **sound sweeps**
  (synchronised calling then silent listening) for responsive subjects. Not useful for
  subjects who don't respond (young children afraid of strangers, some people with dementia).
- **Investigation** runs in parallel: interviews with family, phone data, trip plans,
  social media, past behaviour. Many searches are solved by investigation, not boots.

## 4. Clue awareness
A search looks for **clues** as much as the subject: footprints (get the sole pattern
from family or a matching shoe), wrappers, clothing, broken vegetation, scent. Every
team member should know the subject's description, clothing and footwear. When you find a clue:
stop, mark, photograph with scale, record coordinates, report, don't trample.

## 5. Later: grid or area searches
Systematic coverage of segments with line spacing chosen for the **probability of
detection** wanted (see the search-management skill
`defence-public-safety.search-and-rescue.search-management.probability-based-search-planning-and-segmentation`).
Tighter spacing = higher POD per pass but slower and more searchers.

## Gotchas
- Searching too far from the IPP too early, while the subject is near the trailhead.
- Ignoring "that's probably nothing" clues.
- Searcher safety: weather, terrain, fatigue and night operations; never search alone.

## Verify
- [ ] IPP defined and protected; scent-article collected for dogs.
- [ ] Subject category chosen and LPB distances plotted.
- [ ] Hasty and containment tasks assigned in the first operational period.
- [ ] All teams briefed on subject description and footwear.
- [ ] Clues logged with location, time and photo.
