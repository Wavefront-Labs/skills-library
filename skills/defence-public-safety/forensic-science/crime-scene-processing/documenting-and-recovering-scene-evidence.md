# Documenting and recovering scene evidence

**Goal:** leave the scene with every relevant item recorded *in place*, recovered
without contamination, and traceable from the floor to the courtroom. A scene can only
be examined once in its original state, so the order of work matters more than speed.

## 1. Take control before you examine
- **Scene log from minute one:** every person in or out, time, reason. The log is an
  exhibit in its own right; gaps are what defence counsel attacks.
- **Cordons:** an inner cordon around the area of evidential interest and an outer
  cordon for staging/command. Set them **wider than you think** — shrinking a cordon is
  easy, expanding one after people have walked through is not.
- **Common approach path (CAP):** one route in and out, chosen where offenders were
  *unlikely* to have walked (not the obvious doorway path). Mark it with stepping
  plates; document the CAP itself.
- **Preserve fragile evidence first:** footwear marks in rain, blood in sun, items about
  to be moved by medics. Cover or record them before the general survey.
- **PPE** (suit, double gloves changed between items, mask, overshoes) protects both
  you and the DNA picture — Locard's exchange principle cuts both ways.

## 2. Record before you touch
Work **general → specific**:
1. **Walk-through** on the CAP, hands in pockets, noting transient conditions: lights
   on/off, doors and windows, temperatures, smells, clocks, mobile phones ringing.
2. **Photography / video:** overall (establishing the location), mid-range (relating an
   item to its surroundings), close-up **without scale, then with scale** (L-scale,
   perpendicular to the item, filling the frame). Shoot before markers go down, then
   with numbered markers. Keep a photo log (frame, item, direction, time).
3. **Sketch / plan:** rough sketch on scene with measurements (two fixed reference
   points per item, or baseline/triangulation); finished plan later. Laser scanning or
   total stations where available — still note the measurement method.
4. **Notes:** contemporaneous, dated, signed. Record what you *didn't* find where you'd
   expect something (no forced entry, no weapon).

## 3. Search systematically
Pick the pattern to the space and evidence size:
| Pattern | Use |
|---|---|
| Line / strip | Large open outdoor area, several searchers shoulder to shoulder |
| Grid (double strip) | Same, crossed at 90° for higher assurance |
| Zone / quadrant | Rooms, vehicles, small defined areas — assign one per examiner |
| Spiral (inward/outward) | Small open area with a single searcher; less used now |
Search at the height evidence lands (ceilings for spatter, under furniture for casings).
Record the pattern used and its limits — "searched" without a method is not defensible.

## 4. Recover and package
- **Sequence:** least-destructive first, most transient first — e.g. trace/fibres before
  fingerprint powder, swabs before items are lifted, fingerprint before DNA swabbing only
  if your lab's sequencing policy says so (confirm locally: policies differ).
- **Packaging basics:** paper for anything biological or damp (plastic traps moisture
  and rots DNA); rigid tubes/boxes for sharps and firearms (made safe by a trained
  officer first); nylon/foil bags for fire debris (volatiles escape through plastic and
  paper); one item per package.
- **Labels:** unique exhibit number, description, location recovered, recoverer, date/
  time, and a **continuity (chain of custody) label** signed at every hand-over.
  Seal with tamper-evident tape, signature across the seal.
- **Controls:** collect substrate and elimination samples (a swab of the unstained
  surface, elimination prints/DNA from residents and attending staff).

## Gotchas
- First responders contaminate unknowingly: get their footwear and elimination samples
  early and note where they walked.
- Never move or reposition an item "for a better photo" — if it was moved, say so.
- Body at the scene: the pathologist or coroner/ME controls it; coordinate before any
  recovery from the body.
- Multiple linked scenes (victim, suspect, vehicle) need **different examiners** or
  documented decontamination between them to rule out cross-transfer.
- Exhibit numbering conventions (initials + sequence, e.g. "ABC/1") vary by force —
  follow yours.

## Verify
- [ ] Scene log has no time gaps; CAP is documented.
- [ ] Every recovered item has overall, mid and close-up (with/without scale) photos and
      a measured position.
- [ ] Search pattern and area covered are written down.
- [ ] Each package: correct material, sealed, signed seal, continuity label started.
- [ ] Elimination and control samples taken and logged.
