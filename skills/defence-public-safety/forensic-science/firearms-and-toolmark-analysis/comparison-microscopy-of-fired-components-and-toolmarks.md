# Comparison microscopy of fired components and toolmarks

**Goal:** decide, with a documented method and honest limits, whether a fired bullet,
cartridge case or toolmark was produced by a particular firearm or tool — or could not
have been. This is a laboratory discipline; firearm safety and test-firing are done
under the lab's own range procedures by trained staff.

## 1. Intake and safety
- Every submitted firearm is **checked and made safe by a qualified examiner** before
  anything else, and its condition recorded as received (photographs, serial number,
  loaded state, safety function, any obstructions). Recording before making safe matters:
  "as received" condition can be evidence.
- Sequence with other disciplines: swab for DNA and examine for fingermarks **before**
  handling for firearms work, unless safety forbids. Agree the sequence with the case
  manager.
- Fired components: mark on a non-examination surface (e.g. bullet base, case mouth) or
  only on the container — never on striation-bearing areas.

## 2. Class characteristics first (fast exclusions)
- **Bullets:** calibre, number of lands and grooves, direction of twist, land/groove widths.
- **Cartridge cases:** calibre, firing-pin shape (round, elliptical, rectangular),
  breech-face mark type (parallel, arched, granular), ejector/extractor positions.
- A difference in class characteristics → **elimination** without microscopy of fine detail.
- Use reference collections and databases of general rifling characteristics to suggest
  possible makes/models for an unknown weapon (investigative lead only).

## 3. References and comparison
1. **Test-fires** from the submitted firearm into a water tank or other recovery medium
   using ammunition matching the evidence where possible. Compare test-fire to test-fire
   first — this shows how reproducible that weapon's marks are.
2. On the **comparison microscope**, examine evidence vs test side by side with matched
   magnification and oblique lighting adjusted to the same angle on both stages.
3. Look for agreement in **individual characteristics** (random microscopic imperfections
   from manufacture and wear) beyond what class or **subclass** characteristics
   (shared by a batch of tools made in sequence) could explain. Subclass carry-over is a
   known trap — especially on breech faces and broached barrels.
4. Photograph or 3D-scan the agreeing areas; note the areas that do *not* agree.

## 4. Conclusions (AFTE range)
- **Identification** — sufficient agreement of individual characteristics.
- **Inconclusive** — some agreement but insufficient; or no agreement of individual
  detail with matching class; or unexplained disagreement. Give the reason.
- **Elimination** — significant disagreement of class (or individual, per lab policy).
- **Unsuitable** — no microscopic marks of value.
Wording standards have moved: US DOJ uniform language and many courts now bar "to the
exclusion of all other firearms" and "100% certainty". Report error-rate evidence from
black-box studies when asked; note PCAST's critique of the subjective method.

## 5. Toolmarks
- Impressed (e.g. a screwdriver pressed into a door frame) vs striated (dragged). Cast the
  mark (silicone casting material) if the substrate can't be submitted; photograph first.
- Make test marks in lead or soft metal at several angles — angle and pressure change
  striated marks a lot. Don't test on the evidence surface itself.

## 6. NIBIN / IBIS correlation
Enter cartridge cases from scenes and test-fires so unlinked shootings can be connected.
A correlation candidate is a **lead**; it must be confirmed by microscopic comparison
before it's reported as an identification. Timely entry is the operational value.

## Gotchas
- Ammunition differences (bullet jacket, primer hardness) change mark quality — note them.
- Barrel wear or later firing between offence and seizure can reduce agreement.
- Bias control: blind verification of identifications, minimal case context, and record
  the verifier's independent conclusion.

## Verify
- [ ] Firearm condition recorded as received and made safe by qualified staff.
- [ ] Trace/DNA/fingermark sequencing agreed before handling.
- [ ] Class characteristics compared before individual detail; subclass risk considered.
- [ ] Conclusion uses lab-approved wording; inconclusive reasons stated.
- [ ] NIBIN leads confirmed microscopically; identifications blind-verified.
