# ACE-V friction-ridge examination

**Goal:** turn a mark on a surface into a defensible conclusion — identification,
exclusion or inconclusive — using a documented method that another examiner can repeat
and that survives scrutiny for error and bias.

## 1. Development: pick the sequence by surface
Non-destructive first, then the techniques that would ruin later ones. A typical order
(confirm against your lab's manual — e.g. the Home Office Fingermark Visualisation
Manual — which is the authority here):

| Surface | Typical sequence |
|---|---|
| Non-porous (glass, painted metal, plastics) | Visual/light source → cyanoacrylate (superglue) fuming → fluorescent dye stain → powder; VMD for some plastics |
| Porous (paper, card, raw wood) | Visual/light source → DFO or 1,2-indanedione (fluorescent) → ninhydrin → physical developer (wet/after previous ones) |
| Adhesive side of tape | Specialist powder suspensions/ gentian violet-type stains |
| Blood-contaminated | Protein stains (e.g. acid black/acid yellow) after photography |

- **Photograph at every stage** with a scale — development can fade or over-develop.
- Powder dusting at the scene is quick but can destroy marks better developed in the lab
  by fuming; bring the item in where practical.
- Wet porous items: let dry before amino-acid reagents; physical developer is used on
  wetted paper.

## 2. ACE-V
**Analysis (of the latent alone, before seeing any exemplar):**
- Assess quality and quantity: Level 1 (pattern, ridge flow), Level 2 (minutiae — ridge
  endings, bifurcations, dots, and their relationships), Level 3 (pores, edge shapes) if
  clarity allows.
- Note distortion: pressure, slippage, substrate, development artefacts, overlapping marks.
- Decide **suitability**: of value for comparison / for exclusion only / of no value.
  Document the features you are relying on *now* — features "found" only after seeing
  the exemplar are the classic route to error.

**Comparison:** side by side with known prints (ten-print card, palm prints, AFIS
candidate). Start from a target group in the latent and look for it in the exemplar,
not the other way round. Account for every difference: is it explainable distortion or
a true discrepancy?

**Evaluation:** reach one of the agreed conclusions:
- **Identification / source identification** — sufficient agreement, no unexplained
  discrepancy. Many jurisdictions now prohibit "100% certain", "zero error rate" or
  "to the exclusion of all others" language; follow current OSAC/national guidance.
- **Exclusion** — a discrepancy that distortion cannot explain (or a different pattern
  type in a clearly comparable area).
- **Inconclusive** — say *why* (insufficient latent quality, incomplete exemplar such as
  missing palm or tip areas → request better exemplars).

**Verification:** a second qualified examiner repeats the examination. **Blind**
verification (verifier doesn't know the first conclusion) is best practice for
identifications and mandatory in some labs for exclusions and complex marks. Record
disagreements and how they were resolved through the lab's conflict procedure.

## 3. Bias and documentation
- Use **sequential unmasking / linear ACE-V**: analysis is documented before exemplars are
  viewed; case context the examiner doesn't need (confessions, other evidence) is
  withheld by a case manager.
- Annotate charts of corresponding features; keep bench notes sufficient for review.
- The 2004 Madrid bombing misidentification (an AFIS hit verified by several examiners)
  is the textbook case of confirmation bias and of verification that wasn't independent.

## 4. Testimony
Explain the method, the conclusion scale, and the known limitations — black-box studies
(e.g. FBI/Noblis 2011) show a low but non-zero false-positive rate. Don't overstate.

## Gotchas
- AFIS candidate lists rank similarity, they don't identify — every candidate needs full ACE-V.
- A mark of "no value" today may be of value with a better image; preserve originals and
  capture at high resolution (1000 ppi is a common minimum for latents — verify policy).
- Simultaneous impressions (several fingers laid down together) can be evaluated as a group
  only under specific rules — check your lab's standard.

## Verify
- [ ] Development sequence recorded and photographed at each step.
- [ ] Latent analysis (features, suitability) documented before exemplar viewed.
- [ ] Conclusion uses the lab's approved scale and wording; inconclusive reasons stated.
- [ ] Verification done by a second examiner (blind where policy requires).
- [ ] Report and testimony avoid absolute-certainty claims.
