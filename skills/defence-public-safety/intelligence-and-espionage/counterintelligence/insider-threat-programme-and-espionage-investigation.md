# Insider-threat programmes and espionage investigation

**Goal:** understand how organisations and services detect and counter hostile
intelligence activity — principally the trusted insider — with programmes that work
and that treat staff fairly. For physical/technical counter-espionage measures (TSCM,
meeting security) see
`defence-public-safety.physical-and-access-security.counter-surveillance.tscm-sweeps-and-espionage-risk-countermeasures-for-organisations`.

## 1. The shape of the field
- **Defensive CI:** vetting, security education, need-to-know, reporting of approaches,
  protective security. Most CI work, by far.
- **Investigative CI:** inquiries into suspected espionage, usually jointly with law
  enforcement because the end state may be prosecution.
- **Offensive CI:** penetrating or manipulating the hostile service (doubled agents,
  deception). Run only by state services; noted here for completeness.
- Lesson of the Angleton era (CIA, 1950s–70s): an unbounded mole hunt paralysed a service
  and harmed innocent officers. Suspicion needs evidence and an end point.

## 2. What the case history teaches
- Ames (CIA, arrested 1994) and Hanssen (FBI, 2001) each spied for years; unexplained
  wealth, access beyond role and prior security concerns were visible but not joined up.
- Herbig's PERSEREC data: money is the most common motive historically, with divided
  loyalty, ideology and disgruntlement increasingly prominent; most spies volunteered
  rather than being recruited cold.
- Gotcha: "the insider" is usually not a cinematic spy. Most insider incidents are data
  theft for a competitor or new employer, sabotage after a grievance, or negligence.

## 3. Building an insider-threat programme
1. **Governance** — senior owner; HR, security, legal, IT and line management at the table;
   written policy on what is monitored and why.
2. **Know your critical assets** — which information, systems and people matter most.
3. **Personnel security** — vetting proportionate to access; **continuous evaluation**
   rather than one-off clearance; aftercare when life circumstances change.
4. **Technical controls** — least privilege, logging, data-loss prevention, user activity
   monitoring on high-risk systems, disclosed to staff.
5. **Reporting culture** — easy, safe channels for staff to report concerns or approaches,
   plus defensive briefings before foreign travel or conferences.
6. **Hub** — a small multi-disciplinary team that correlates signals from HR, security and
   IT, and decides when to escalate.

## 4. Indicators and the critical-path model
The critical-path model (Shaw and Sellers) describes a pathway: personal predispositions
→ stressors (debt, divorce, demotion) → concerning behaviours (rule-breaking, unexplained
affluence, unusual access attempts, unreported foreign contacts) → **organisational
response**. The decisive variable is often the last: poor management responses to early
concerns accelerate people down the path. No single indicator means anything; look for
clusters and change. Most people with stressors are not threats — the right first
response is usually support, not suspicion.

## 5. Investigation discipline
- Open a case on articulable facts, define its scope and review points.
- Involve legal counsel early; preserve evidence to criminal standard in case of prosecution.
- Keep the circle small; leaks warn the subject and destroy careers if the suspicion is wrong.
- Respect employment law, privacy law and union agreements; covert monitoring of staff
  generally needs specific justification and authorisation.
- Close cases explicitly, including exonerations, and correct records.

## 6. Uncertainty
Indicator lists in the open literature are drawn from caught cases (survivorship bias);
their predictive value for unknown cases is unproven. Use them as prompts, not as scoring.

## Verify
- [ ] Critical assets and their access populations are identified.
- [ ] HR, security, IT and legal share a governed process for concerns.
- [ ] Monitoring is disclosed in policy and proportionate to risk.
- [ ] Staff know how to report an approach and get pre-travel briefings.
- [ ] Every investigation has a factual basis, scope, legal review and a closure record.
