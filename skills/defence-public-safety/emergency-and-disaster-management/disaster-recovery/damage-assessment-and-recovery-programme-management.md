# Damage assessment and recovery programme management

**Goal:** move a community from response to a planned, funded, fair recovery: know what
was damaged, unlock outside assistance with solid documentation, and rebuild in a way
that lowers the next disaster's impact. Funding programme names below are US
(Stafford Act, FEMA PA/IA); other countries have equivalents — check your national scheme.

## 1. Start recovery during response
- Appoint a **recovery coordinator/manager** in the first days, separate from response
  command, so recovery decisions (debris contracts, temporary housing sites, rebuilding
  rules) aren't made by default under response pressure.
- Begin documentation immediately: photographs with GPS/time, force-account labour and
  equipment hours, contracts, donated resources. Undocumented costs are usually
  unrecoverable.

## 2. Damage assessment in phases
1. **Rapid/initial assessment (hours–days):** windshield surveys, aerial imagery, 911 and
   utility data, social media — to set priorities. Categories (destroyed / major / minor /
   affected) by consistent criteria.
2. **Building safety evaluation:** trained evaluators (e.g. ATC-20 for earthquake,
   ATC-45 for wind/flood) post **placards**: green *inspected* (lawful occupancy),
   yellow *restricted use*, red *unsafe*. Placards are legal notices — record them
   in a database so occupants, insurers and permits all see the same status.
3. **Preliminary/joint damage assessment:** local, state and federal teams verify
   damage to public infrastructure (public assistance) and homes (individual assistance)
   to support a declaration request. Have cost estimates, insurance coverage and
   population data ready. The statewide per-capita impact indicator (published yearly in the Federal Register; $1.89 for FY25 in FEMA PDA reports) is one factor weighed with others, not the only test.

## 3. Recovery organisation and plan
- Organise by **recovery support functions** (FEMA NDRF): six functions that include housing, infrastructure systems and health and social services; FEMA's current pages name a Community Assistance function, so confirm the current list. Each has a coordinating agency and partners.
- Build a **recovery plan** with the community, not for it: vision, priorities, projects
  with costs and funding sources, timeline, metrics. Engage displaced residents,
  businesses, faith groups, and under-served populations — they recover slowest.
- Phases: short-term (days–weeks: debris clearance, sheltering, utility restoration),
  intermediate (weeks–months: interim housing, reopening schools/business), long-term
  (months–years: rebuilding, economic revitalisation, mitigation).

## 4. Key workstreams
- **Debris management:** pre-arranged contracts, temporary debris sites with
  environmental controls, segregation (vegetative, construction, hazardous, white goods,
  e-waste), load tickets and monitoring for reimbursement. Debris often is the largest
  early cost.
- **Housing:** shelter → interim (repairs, rental assistance, temporary units) → permanent.
  Avoid temporary sites in hazard zones and far from schools and work.
- **Rebuilding standards:** enforce current codes; consider elevation, buyouts or
  relocation in repetitive-loss areas. Have a mitigation plan ready so rebuilding can include it.
- **Fraud and fairness:** check **duplication of benefits** between insurance, grants and
  loans; publish eligibility rules; offer case management for complex households.

## 5. Track progress
Publish recovery dashboards: households in interim vs permanent housing, businesses
reopened, infrastructure restored, funds obligated and spent. Recovery that is invisible
loses political support long before it's finished.

## Gotchas
- A governor's request for a major disaster declaration is due within 30 days of the incident (44 CFR 206.36; extendable only on a written request made before the 30 days end); late or thin PDA data loses funding.
- Rebuilding exactly as before ("back to normal") recreates the same vulnerability.
- Contractors must be procured under the funding programme's rules or costs get disallowed.
- Mental-health and social impacts peak months later — plan services past the first year.

## Verify
- [ ] Recovery lead appointed early; cost documentation began during response.
- [ ] Damage categories and placards recorded consistently in one database.
- [ ] Declaration package includes damage, costs, insurance and impact data on time.
- [ ] Recovery plan has community input, funded projects and metrics.
- [ ] Debris load tickets, procurement records and benefit-duplication checks in place.
