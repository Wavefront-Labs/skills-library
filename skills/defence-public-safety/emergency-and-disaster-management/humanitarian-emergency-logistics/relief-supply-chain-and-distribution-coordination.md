# Relief supply chain and distribution coordination

**Goal:** get the right relief items to the people who need them, in time, in the right
quantities, without waste, theft or harm — and know where every item went. Logistics mistakes are expensive.

## 1. From needs to a pipeline
1. **Needs assessment** (joint/multi-sector where possible): affected population numbers
   and profile (households, children, older people, disabilities), what markets still work,
   access constraints, what other agencies are delivering.
2. **Quantify** with standards, not guesses. Sphere minimums are the usual reference,
   e.g. **2,100 kcal/person/day** as the planning figure for general rations (adjusted to context), **15 L water/person/day** (minimum average, context-dependent, not a ceiling), at least **3.5 m² living space per person** excluding cooking space. Convert to
   kits or tonnage per household.
3. **Pipeline plan:** for each item — total need, in stock, in the pipeline (ordered,
   in transit), gap, and **lead time**. A pipeline break (when stock runs out before
   resupply) is the failure to plan for; review it weekly.
4. **Market check:** if local markets function, **cash or vouchers** may be faster,
   cheaper and give people choice. Logistics then supports cash delivery and market
   monitoring rather than moving goods.

## 2. Sourcing
- **Pre-positioned stocks** (agency warehouses, humanitarian response depots) for the
  first days; **local/regional procurement** next (faster, supports the economy, fits
  local preferences) following the organisation's procurement rules; international
  procurement for specialised items.
- **In-kind donations:** accept only what was requested and meets specifications.
  Unsolicited donations (used clothing, expired medicines, mixed goods) clog ports and
  warehouses — publish a "what we need / don't send" list early.

## 3. Transport and warehousing
- Do (or read) a **logistics capacity assessment**: ports, airports, roads and bridges,
  fuel, customs procedures, warehouse availability, transporters.
- The **Logistics Cluster** (led by WFP in UN-coordinated responses) offers common
  services — shared storage, transport, information products like access maps. Use them
  rather than every agency competing for trucks.
- **Warehouse discipline:** goods received against a waybill and counted; stock cards
  or a warehouse system per item and location; **FEFO** (first expiry, first out) for
  food and medicines, FIFO otherwise; regular physical counts reconciled to records;
  pallets off the floor, pest control, temperature control for medicines.
- Every movement has paper or digital documentation (waybill, goods received note,
  dispatch note). The paperwork is what donors audit.

## 4. Last-mile distribution
- **Registration:** list of eligible households (from assessments, local authorities,
  community committees), with tokens or cards. Verify against duplicates; protect
  personal data.
- **Distribution site:** safe, accessible, shaded, with separate queues for vulnerable
  people, crowd control by design (barriers, a one-way flow), and enough staff and
  community volunteers. Announce date, place, entitlement and complaints channel in
  local languages ahead of time.
- At the point: check entitlement, hand over, record (signature/thumbprint/scan),
  manage complaints on site.
- **Post-distribution monitoring:** did people get it, is it used, was it sold, any
  protection problems (exploitation, diversion)? Feed results back into kit design.

## 5. Coordination
Share pipeline and distribution plans through the clusters (food security, shelter/NFI,
WASH, health) and maintain a 3W so areas aren't served twice while others are missed.

## Gotchas
- Kits designed far away (wrong cooking pots, culturally unsuitable items) go unused or
  get sold — consult the affected population.
- Distributing heavy items far from where people live shifts the transport burden to them.
- Fuel and customs clearance are frequent hidden bottlenecks.
- Women and people with disabilities are disadvantaged by long queues and unsafe sites —
  design for them.

## Verify
- [ ] Quantities derive from population data × stated standard.
- [ ] Pipeline shows need, stock, in-transit, gap and lead time, reviewed weekly.
- [ ] Warehouses have stock cards reconciled by physical count.
- [ ] Distributions have registration, entitlement announcement, records and a complaints route.
- [ ] Post-distribution monitoring done and acted on.
