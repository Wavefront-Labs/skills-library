# Mutual aid and multi-agency field coordination

**Goal:** turn many organisations arriving at a disaster — neighbouring fire services,
state/national assets, NGOs, volunteers, sometimes international teams — into one
coordinated response, with no gaps, little duplication, and everyone accounted for.

## 1. Ask for the right thing
- Request by **capability and type**, not by agency name: "two Type 1 swift-water rescue
  teams", "one Type 2 shelter management team". Resource typing (e.g. the NIMS Resource
  Typing Library) tells the sender exactly what kit, people and self-sufficiency to bring.
- Each request states: mission, quantity, type, reporting location and time, expected
  duration, self-sufficiency (food, fuel, lodging), and the point of contact. Vague requests get the wrong resources.
- Use pre-agreed mechanisms (intra-state agreements, EMAC between US states (a congressionally ratified compact, Public Law 104-321, covering liability, licence reciprocity, workers' compensation and reimbursement in governor-declared emergencies), national
  mutual-aid protocols, international requests via the national government) — they
  settle liability, reimbursement and licensing *before* deployment. Confirm the
  reimbursement rules in writing; disputes months later are common.

## 2. Receive and stage
- Designate **staging areas** away from the hazard with space, access, fuel and toilets.
- **Check-in** every arriving resource: team leader, personnel roster, equipment, comms
  capability, hours worked so far, special limitations. Issue the comms plan and a
  safety briefing.
- International urban search-and-rescue and medical teams arrive through a
  **reception/departure centre** and register with the OSOCC (INSARAG model) — local
  liaison must expect and support this.

## 3. Task clearly
A **task/mission assignment** (ICS 204 equivalent) includes:
- Who (team, leader), what (objective and expected output), where (map reference,
  boundaries), when (start, review time, operational period), reporting chain and
  channel, hazards and safety measures, and what to do on completion.
- Assign to geographic **sectors/divisions** with clear boundaries; overlap is how two
  teams search the same street and miss the next.
- Keep assignments within the team's type and certification.

## 4. Liaison and coordination
- Each agency sends an **agency representative** with authority to commit its resources to
  the command post or EOC; the **Liaison Officer** is their single point of contact.
- Daily **coordination meeting** at a fixed time: situation, priorities, gaps, who will
  fill them. Keep it short; detailed planning in sector meetings.
- Maintain a **3W matrix (who, what, where)** — agency, activity, location, dates,
  contact. In humanitarian settings this is standard cluster practice; in domestic
  disasters it prevents three food distributions on one block and none on another.
- Integrate NGOs and faith groups through their coordinating body rather than one by one.
- **Spontaneous volunteers:** route through a volunteer reception centre — register,
  brief, assign, sign out — rather than turning them away or letting them self-deploy.

## 5. Interoperability
- Radio: a single communications plan with shared tactical channels and plain language.
  Where radios can't talk, use gateways or exchange radios/liaisons.
- Common maps and grid references; agreed place names (local names confuse visiting teams).

## 6. Demobilise deliberately
Release in reverse priority with a debrief, equipment return, a fatigue/welfare check, and
travel plan. Thank and document — it affects whether they come next time.

## Gotchas
- Self-deploying resources overwhelm logistics and safety tracking — enforce check-in.
- Liaisons without authority slow everything; ask agencies for decision-makers.
- Credentials and professional licences may not cross borders without the mutual-aid
  mechanism — check before tasking medical staff.
- Hours-worked limits for visiting teams are easy to lose track of — log them.

## Verify
- [ ] Requests stated as typed capabilities with mission, location, duration, POC.
- [ ] Every arriving resource checked in, briefed and on the comms plan.
- [ ] Each team has a written assignment with boundaries and reporting times.
- [ ] 3W matrix current; daily coordination meeting held.
- [ ] Demob includes debrief and reimbursement documentation.
