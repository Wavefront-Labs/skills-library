# Household kit, family plan and shelter-or-evacuate

**Goal:** your household can cope for several days (many guides say at least 3) without power, water or shops, can leave home quickly with essentials, and everyone knows what to
do and how to reconnect if you're apart. Adjust for your local risks.

## 1. Know your local risks (15 minutes)
Check your local council/emergency management website for the hazards that matter where
you live (flood, wildfire, storm, earthquake, heat, winter storm, industrial site
nearby). Sign up for local alerts and make sure phones have emergency alerts enabled.
Find out your **evacuation zone** if you live in a flood/coastal/wildfire area.

## 2. Home supply kit (stay-at-home)
- **Water:** **1 US gallon (about 4 litres) per person per day** for several days, for drinking and sanitation (Ready.gov) — more for
  hot climates, nursing mothers, pets. Store sealed and replace expired items.
- **Food:** non-perishable things you already eat (tins, peanut butter, cereal, UHT
  milk) + a **manual tin opener**. Rotate into normal meals.
- **Light and power:** torches/flashlights and spare batteries (avoid candles — fire
  risk), battery or wind-up radio, power bank, car charger.
- **Health:** first-aid kit, your prescription medicines (keep them organised and protected) where possible,
  glasses, hearing-aid batteries, hygiene items, sanitiser, rubbish bags.
- **Tools:** whistle, dust masks, duct tape, local paper map, the means to turn off
  utilities (know where your water stop tap, gas meter valve and fuse box/breaker are).
- **Specific needs:** baby supplies, pet food and carrier, mobility aids, medical
  device backup power.
- Replace expired items as needed and re-think your needs every year (Ready.gov).

## 3. Grab bag (one per person, light enough to carry)
Water and snacks for a day, medicines and a list of them, phone charger, torch,
copies of **important documents** (ID, insurance, prescriptions, contacts) in a waterproof
bag or securely stored digitally, some cash in small notes, a change of clothes, rain
layer, keys, a contact card for children.

## 4. Family emergency plan (write it down)
- **Contacts:** an **out-of-area contact** everyone calls or messages (local networks may
  be jammed; texts often get through when calls don't).
- **Meeting places:** one just outside home (fire), one in the neighbourhood, and one
  outside the area if you can't get home.
- **School/work/care arrangements:** who collects children; know the school's plan.
- **Roles:** who takes the grab bags, the pets, checks on an elderly neighbour.
- Practise: a fire drill and a leave-quickly drill once a year.

## 5. Shelter in place or evacuate?
**Follow official instructions first.** If told to evacuate, go — early. Without
instructions, general rules:
| Situation | Usual action |
|---|---|
| Wildfire approaching, flood rising, ordered evacuation | **Evacuate** by known route; don't wait for the last minute |
| Chemical release outside, official shelter-in-place instruction | **Shelter in place**: get inside, close windows/doors, turn off ventilation, tune to local news |
| Tornado | Shelter in the lowest interior room without windows |
| Earthquake | Drop, cover, hold on; evacuate afterwards only if the building is damaged |
| Floodwater on the road | Don't drive or walk through it — 30 cm (12 in) of rushing water can carry away most cars and 15 cm (6 in) of fast-moving water can knock an adult over |
Leave a note of where you went if you evacuate and authorities advise it.

## Gotchas
- Generators indoors or in garages kill by carbon monoxide — outside only, at least 20 feet (6 m) from doors, windows and vents (CDC), never in a garage.
- Kits are useless if nobody knows where they are; tell everyone.
- Phones die fast in outages — keep them charged when a warning is issued.
- Insurance details are exactly what you need after a disaster; photograph your
  possessions now.
