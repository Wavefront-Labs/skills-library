# Recognising concealment indicators during inspection

**Goal:** at the examination stage — a parcel on a postal line, a bag on the bench, a vehicle at
the booth, a container opened at the shed — recognise signs that something is hidden, confirm it
with the right tool rather than destructive guesswork, keep officers safe, and preserve the case.
This is the inspector's anomaly-recognition craft; it does not catalogue hiding places. Which
shipments get selected is a separate discipline:
`defence-public-safety.customs-border-and-wildlife-enforcement.smuggling-interdiction.risk-targeting-cargo-and-controlled-deliveries`.

## 1. The core principle: everything should be consistent
Concealment always costs the smuggler something — weight, space, workmanship, or a story that
has to hold together. Inspection looks for the place where **the object, the paperwork and the
person disagree**. Learn what normal looks like for the goods, vehicle models and traveller
profiles you see every day; anomaly spotting depends on that baseline.

## 2. Anomaly classes
**Documentary / declaration**
- Description, value, weight or HS code that doesn't match the goods or each other.
- New or unknown shipper/consignee, residential addresses for commercial quantities, freight paid
  in cash, a routing that makes no commercial sense, cheap goods shipped expensively.
- Repeated small parcels to the same address under different names.

**Physical — weight, density, dimension**
- Item heavier or lighter than its declared content should be; uneven weight distribution.
- **Internal and external dimensions that don't agree** (a void that isn't accounted for).
- Density readings or X-ray images showing an organic mass where only metal/plastic should be,
  or a uniform block inside an item that should be varied.

**Workmanship and tampering**
- Fresh paint, new adhesive, sealant, filler or welds on old items; mismatched screws or tool marks;
  re-glued packaging; factory seals that look reapplied.
- Unusual odours, or strong masking odours (coffee, detergent, fuel).

**Behavioural (travellers and drivers)**
- Story inconsistent with ticket, luggage or documents; unfamiliarity with own bag's contents or
  vehicle; excessive attention to a particular item while it is examined. Judge behaviour against
  context, never appearance — and remember nervousness alone is common in innocent people.

One anomaly justifies a closer look, not a conclusion. Several together justify escalation.

## 3. Confirming without destroying
Escalate in order of intrusiveness, and follow your agency's legal authority at each step:
1. **Non-intrusive inspection:** X-ray/CT for parcels and bags, large-scale scanners for vehicles and
   containers; compare against reference images of the declared goods.
2. **Detector dogs** and trace detection (ion mobility, Raman/FTIR handheld analysers) for screening.
3. **Measurement tools:** density meter, fibrescope/borescope into existing openings, tape measure
   for dimension comparison.
4. **Physical/intrusive examination** only with authority, documented reasoning, photographs before
   and after, and a witness — damage claims follow wrongful intrusive exams.

## 4. Officer safety
- **Synthetic opioids (fentanyl and analogues):** treat unknown powders as hazardous — nitrile gloves,
  open suspect powder packages only under approved procedure, avoid creating dust, wash hands
  with soap and water (not alcohol gel). Have **naloxone** available and staff trained. *(Uncertain:
  real-world risk from brief skin contact is debated and lower than early reports suggested; follow
  your agency's current PPE policy regardless.)*
- Sharps, chemicals, firearms, and live or venomous wildlife in parcels and baggage — open slowly, away
  from your face, and stop if something is unexpected.
- Don't taste, sniff deeply or field-test outside the approved kit.

## 5. Seizure and case handling
- Photograph in situ before moving anything; record exactly where and how it was found.
- **Chain of custody** from the bench: sealed, signed evidence bags, logged transfers.
- Before seizing, consider whether the case is a candidate for a **controlled delivery**; stop and
  consult the investigations/intelligence unit rather than alerting the recipient.
- Feed results back — positive *and* negative — to targeting so profiles improve.

## Gotchas
- Inspectors who only look where finds were made last month miss displacement to new methods.
- A clean X-ray isn't proof of nothing; dense goods mask contents. Combine tools.
- Over-zealous intrusive exams without documented grounds damage trust and cases.

## Verify
- [ ] Declaration, object and person were checked for consistency, not just the object.
- [ ] Anomalies noted by class; escalation was proportionate and recorded.
- [ ] Least-intrusive confirming tool used first; photos before and after any intrusive exam.
- [ ] PPE and naloxone protocol followed for unknown powders.
- [ ] Chain of custody unbroken; controlled-delivery option considered; results fed back.
