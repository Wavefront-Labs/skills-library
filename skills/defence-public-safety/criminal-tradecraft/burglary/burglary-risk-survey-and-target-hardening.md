# Burglary risk survey and target hardening

**Goal:** as a security consultant or crime-prevention officer, assess a dwelling or small
commercial premises for burglary risk and recommend proportionate controls the client will
actually use. The frame is **deter → detect → delay → respond**: each layer buys time for the
next, and no single lock or camera does all four.

## 1. Gather context first (desk work, 30 min)
- **Crime pattern:** police open data / crime maps for the street and neighbourhood, the client's
  own history. A property burgled once is at sharply elevated risk for weeks after, and so are its
  neighbours (repeat and near-repeat victimisation) — act fast after an incident.
- **What's attractive:** cash, jewellery, laptops, power tools, keys for high-value cars, stock.
- **Occupancy pattern:** empty all day? seasonal? night-time-only business?
- **Insurer requirements:** many policies specify lock standards or a graded alarm; non-compliance can
  void a claim. Read the policy before recommending.

## 2. Walk the perimeter like an inspector
Go round outside, then inside, in daylight and ideally after dark. Note:
- **Natural surveillance:** can neighbours/passers-by see doors and windows? Tall hedges and side
  passages that screen the rear are a classic weak point (CPTED).
- **Access control to the rear:** side gates locked and full-height; bins, ladders, garden furniture
  that give climbing aid are stored or secured.
- **Signs of occupancy and invitation:** post piling up, boxes from new electronics at the kerb,
  keys visible from the letterbox or hallway, car keys near the front door.
- **Doors:** rear and side doors are usually weaker than the front. Check frame condition, hinge side,
  glazing near the lock, letterbox position, and the cylinder type.
- **Windows:** ground-floor and those reachable from flat roofs; old or unlocked fanlights;
  patio doors without anti-lift protection.
- **Outbuildings:** sheds and garages hold tools that help an intruder and are themselves targets.

## 3. Recommend by layer, specify to a standard
Name the **standard**, not a brand — it gives the client something checkable.
| Layer | Typical controls |
|---|---|
| Deter | Lighting (dusk-to-dawn or PIR on approach routes), visible alarm box, tidy frontage, signage |
| Delay — doors | Tested door sets (UK **PAS 24** / Secured by Design; commercial **LPS 1175**); mortice deadlocks to **BS 3621** (UK) or **ANSI/BHMA Grade 1–2** (US); euro cylinders resistant to snapping (**TS 007 3-star** or **SS312 Diamond**); hinge bolts, London bar/door chain |
| Delay — windows | Key-operable locks, laminated glass at vulnerable panes, anti-lift on sliders |
| Detect | Graded intruder alarm (EN 50131 / PD 6662 grade 2 domestic is typical; higher for commercial), monitored or app-notified; see `defence-public-safety.physical-and-access-security.intrusion-detection-systems.intruder-alarm-zoning-sensor-selection-and-false-alarm-management` |
| Respond | Monitoring with police response where eligible, keyholders listed, CCTV that captures usable faces at entry points |
| Reduce reward | Safe anchored and rated to the insurer's cash/valuables limit, property marking, asset register, stock out of window view |

*(Uncertain: grade names and certification schemes vary by country; check the local equivalent
before quoting a standard.)*

## 4. Key and procedural control
- Know how many keys exist and who holds them; re-key or change cylinders on moving in, after
  lost keys, or after staff leave.
- Keyless car fobs in a signal-blocking pouch or away from the front door.
- Closing-up checklist for businesses: all doors and windows, alarm set, cash removed, lights pattern.
- Social media: don't post holiday dates in real time.

## 5. After a burglary (victim and client support)
1. Don't enter if an intruder may still be inside; call police. Avoid touching entry points — forensics.
2. Get a crime reference number; photograph damage; list stolen items with serials.
3. **Secure the point of entry the same day** (emergency boarding/locksmith) and upgrade it, not
   just repair it — repeat risk is highest now.
4. Cancel cards, change locks if keys were taken, warn neighbours (near-repeat risk).
5. Signpost victim support; burglary often causes lasting anxiety, especially for older people.

## Gotchas
- A strong lock on a weak frame or a glazed door adds little — survey the whole door set.
- Over-hardening fire escapes breaks fire-safety law; locks must allow escape without a key.
- Cameras that only show tops of heads are evidentially useless; check mounting height.
- Recommendations the client finds inconvenient won't be used (unset alarms, propped doors).

## Verify
- [ ] Local crime pattern and the client's history were reviewed before the site visit.
- [ ] Every external door and accessible window is listed with its weakness.
- [ ] Each recommendation names a layer and a checkable standard or spec.
- [ ] Insurer requirements and fire-escape constraints were checked.
- [ ] Key holdings are known; re-keying advised where control is lost.
- [ ] Post-incident steps include same-day securing and neighbour warning.
