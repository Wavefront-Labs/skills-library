# Frontline detection of counterfeit notes, documents and goods

**Goal:** give cash handlers, door and age-check staff, compliance teams and brand-protection
investigators a reliable check routine for the three common forms of counterfeiting, and a
lawful, safe way to handle what they find. Production methods are out of scope; detection relies
on knowing the genuine article well, not on knowing how fakes are made.

## 1. The core principle: compare to genuine, check several features
Counterfeits are usually caught because **one feature is wrong**, not because all are. Train on
genuine items until they feel normal, and always check **at least two or three independent
features** — no single test is enough.

## 2. Banknotes — feel, look, tilt (then tools)
Use your issuing central bank's current guide; features differ by series and change with new issues.
- **Feel:** genuine paper notes have a crisp, slightly rough feel and raised (intaglio) print on key
  areas; polymer notes have a distinctive slippery feel and should spring back when scrunched.
- **Look:** hold to the light for watermark/window and see-through registration; check fine print
  is sharp, not blurred or dotted.
- **Tilt:** holograms, colour-shifting ink, moving images should change cleanly.
- **UV lamp:** genuine paper is generally dull under UV with specific fluorescent features; ordinary
  paper often glows uniformly. *(Uncertain: UV behaviour varies by series — check the issuer guide.)*
- **Gotcha: counterfeit detection pens** only test for starch in ordinary paper; they say nothing about
  polymer notes and can be fooled. Don't rely on them alone.
- **Gotcha:** fakes are passed at **busy moments**, in poor light, or mixed into a bundle, often for
  low-value purchases paid with a high-value note to get genuine change.

## 3. Identity and travel documents
- Check against a **reference** (PRADO for European documents, the ID-checking guide your regulator
  endorses, or a document-reader database) — know what the genuine version looks like.
- Physical: laminate lifting or bubbles, uneven edges, photo that sits *on* rather than *in* the
  document, wrong fonts or spacing, missing tactile or optically variable features.
- Consistency: the **MRZ** (machine-readable zone) must match the printed data; check digits can be
  validated by a reader; dates and ages make sense; the face matches the holder.
- **Imposters** (a genuine document used by someone else) are more common than forgeries for age and
  ID checks — compare the face carefully, ask the holder to state the date of birth or postcode.
- Use a document scanner/IDV service where risk justifies it; it complements, not replaces, a person.
For altered handwriting/signatures see
`defence-public-safety.forensic-science.questioned-documents.handwriting-comparison-and-alteration-examination`.

## 4. Counterfeit goods (brand protection, retail, marketplaces)
- **Price and channel:** heavy discounts, unofficial sellers, marketplace listings with stock photos,
  no warranty, shipping from an unexpected origin.
- **Product and packaging:** spelling errors, wrong fonts/colours versus a genuine reference, poor
  stitching or finish, missing or reused serials/holograms, safety marks (CE/UKCA/UL) that look wrong.
- **Safety-critical categories** — electrical chargers, cosmetics, medicines, car parts, toys — carry
  real harm; escalate quickly. Electronic components: see
  `engineering-technology.technical-authentication-testing.counterfeit-detection.counterfeit-electronic-component-inspection`.
- **Investigator controls:** documented test purchases (photograph, preserve packaging, keep receipts),
  marketplace takedown programmes, and **customs recordation** of your trademarks so border officers can
  detain suspect shipments (e.g. EU Application for Action, US CBP recordation).

## 5. Handling what you find
- **Stay calm and safe.** Don't accuse or confront; the passer may be an innocent victim or may react badly.
- **Banknotes:** follow your jurisdiction's rule — in many countries you should **retain** a suspected
  counterfeit (do not hand it back) and give it to police or the central bank; record who presented it,
  time, CCTV ref. Handle by the edges and put it in an envelope. Never knowingly pass it on — doing so is
  an offence. (US: contact local police or the Secret Service; UK: police, which forward to the Bank of
  England.)
- **Documents:** refuse the transaction/entry per policy; retain the document only if law and policy allow;
  report to police or the issuing authority.
- **Goods:** preserve samples, chain of custody notes, and refer to the rights holder and trading
  standards/consumer authority or police IP-crime unit.

## Verify
- [ ] Staff check at least two independent features and know the current note series.
- [ ] Nobody relies on a detection pen alone; polymer notes are checked differently.
- [ ] ID checks compare face, MRZ and a genuine reference.
- [ ] Test purchases are documented with chain of custody; trademarks are recorded with customs.
- [ ] The retain/report procedure and the right agency for your country are written down.
