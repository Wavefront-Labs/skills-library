# Mission command and the headquarters battle rhythm

**Goal:** command a force so subordinates act quickly and coherently when the plan meets
reality — and run a headquarters whose routine feeds the commander's decisions instead of
consuming everyone's time. This is doctrine-level command philosophy and staff
organisation as taught in NATO and US/UK staff education.

## 1. Mission command (the philosophy)
Derived from 19th-century Prussian *Auftragstaktik*; now the stated approach of most
NATO armies. US ADP 6-0 lists seven principles:
1. **Competence** — trained, capable leaders at every level.
2. **Mutual trust** — built in training, not declared in orders.
3. **Shared understanding** — of the situation, purpose and problem.
4. **Commander's intent** — purpose, key tasks, end state; clear enough that a subordinate
   can act when the plan no longer fits.
5. **Mission orders** — say *what* and *why*, minimise *how*.
6. **Disciplined initiative** — acting within intent when orders don't cover the situation.
7. **Risk acceptance** — commanders accept prudent risk to gain tempo.
Mission command is **not** "no control": the commander still sets boundaries, priorities
and coordinating measures. Tight control is right for some tasks (e.g. airspace and fires
coordination, deconflicting adjacent units).

## 2. Writing intent that works
- **Purpose** ("in order to…"), **key tasks** (what must be done regardless of plan),
  **end state** (conditions for own force, adversary, terrain/environment, civilians).
- Test: if communications fail, could a subordinate two levels down act correctly from
  this paragraph alone? If not, rewrite.

## 3. Decision support
- **CCIRs** (commander's critical information requirements) define what the commander
  must know, now: **PIRs** (about the adversary/environment) and **FFIRs** (about own
  forces). Each linked to a **decision** — otherwise it's just curiosity. Brief CCIR
  triggers straight to the commander at any hour.
- **Decision points** from wargaming with conditions and the decision to take. The
  decision support matrix/template tells the current-operations staff when to wake the
  commander.
- **Running estimates** by each staff function keep the picture current.

## 4. Decision cycle and tempo
Boyd's **OODA** (observe–orient–decide–act) is the shorthand: the side that cycles
faster and orients better forces the other to react. In practice, tempo comes from
delegated authority and simple, understood plans more than from faster software.

## 5. Battle rhythm (the HQ's routine)
- A deliberate daily/weekly cycle of boards, working groups and briefings that produces
  inputs for the commander's decisions. Typical layers:
  **Current operations** (now to ~24–72 h), **future operations** (next days, branch
  plans, FRAGOs), **plans** (the next phase or sequel).
- Typical events: shift change brief; **commander's update brief (CUB)**; targeting /
  effects working group and board; sustainment working group; assessment working group;
  plans update. Each has a **charter**: purpose, chair, inputs, outputs, attendees, and
  which decision forum it feeds.
- Rules: map every meeting's output to a decision or product; sequence inputs before
  outputs (working group → board → commander); protect the commander's thinking time and
  battlefield circulation; review the rhythm weekly and cut meetings that don't feed decisions.

## 6. Assessment
Plan → prepare → execute → **assess** continuously: are we accomplishing tasks (measures
of performance) and creating the intended conditions (measures of effectiveness)? Adjust
the plan, not just the report.

## Gotchas
- Headquarters bloat: battle rhythm expanding until staff only brief each other.
- Intent paragraphs that restate the mission without purpose.
- Over-reliance on the digital common operating picture leading to micromanagement from
  above ("the long screwdriver").
- CCIRs that never change across phases are probably not driving decisions.

## Verify
- [ ] Intent states purpose, key tasks and end state; passes the comms-failure test.
- [ ] Each CCIR is tied to a decision and a decision point.
- [ ] Battle-rhythm events have charters and feed a decision or product.
- [ ] Current ops / future ops / plans horizons are owned by named cells.
- [ ] Assessment changes plans when conditions aren't moving.
