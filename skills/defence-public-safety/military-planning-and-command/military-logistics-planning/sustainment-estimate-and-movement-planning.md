# Sustainment estimate and movement planning

**Goal:** prove — before the plan is approved — that the force can be delivered, fed,
fuelled, repaired and medically supported for the operation's duration, and show the
commander where sustainment limits the plan. Historians' maxim that logistics sets the
limits of the possible is the reason this estimate starts in mission analysis, not after.

## 1. The logistics estimate
Run it in parallel with the operational planning steps (see
`defence-public-safety.military-planning-and-command.operational-planning.military-decision-making-process-and-orders-production`):
1. **Requirements:** force size and composition × duration × **planning factors**
   (consumption rates per person or per platform per day, by activity level). Use your
   service's approved planning factors — they vary by climate, terrain and intensity and
   are the biggest source of error if guessed.
2. **Capabilities:** what the force carries (days of supply on hand), what sustainment
   units can deliver per day, host-nation support, contracted support, allied support.
3. **Shortfalls and risks:** compare; identify the **limiting factor** (often fuel, water
   in hot climates, or transport capacity rather than stocks).
4. **Conclusions:** supportability of each COA, constraints the commander must accept,
   and recommendations (pauses, alternate routes, pre-positioning, extra lift).

## 2. Supply by class (NATO/US convention)
Class I subsistence/water · II clothing and general equipment · III fuels and lubricants ·
IV construction/barrier materials · V ammunition · VI personal items · VII major end items ·
VIII medical · IX repair parts · X civil-affairs/ non-military programmes.
For each critical class set a **stockage objective** (e.g. days of supply at each level)
and a **reorder/push** method. Track daily in a logistics status report.

## 3. Lines of communication and reach
- Map the **lines of communication** (sea/air ports of debarkation, road and rail routes,
  pipelines) with capacities and vulnerabilities.
- Calculate **distribution capacity**: vehicles × payload × round-trips per day (allowing
  for loading/unloading, driver hours, maintenance and route conditions). Turnaround time
  usually limits more than vehicle count.
- Relate to **operational reach** and the risk of **culmination**: where the force
  outruns its supply, plan logistics pauses or forward bases.

## 4. Deployment and movement
- Sequence forces so enablers (port opening, movement control, sustainment units) arrive
  early — a common failure is deploying combat units faster than the reception capability.
- **RSOM/RSOI** (reception, staging, onward movement, and integration) moves forces from
  ports to their assembly areas; plan throughput per day at each node.
- Movement tables/control: who moves when on which route, with timings and halts, to
  avoid congestion and conflict between units.

## 5. Maintenance and medical support
- **Maintenance:** expected failure and battle-damage rates, repair and recovery
  capacity, spares (Class IX), and the policy of "fix forward" vs evacuate.
- **Medical:** estimated casualty rates from the plan (commonly from approved
  methodologies/models), treatment capacity by **role** (Role 1 first aid and primary care
  → Role 4 definitive care at home), evacuation times (e.g. NATO's 10-1-2 timeline
  guideline), blood supply, and patient movement capacity.

## 6. Products
- Sustainment paragraph (paragraph 4 of the order) and logistics annex.
- **Logistics synchronisation matrix** aligned to the operation's phases and decision points.
- Branch plans for disrupted routes, surge consumption or loss of a key node.

## Gotchas
- "Tooth-to-tail" pressure cuts enablers first; show the commander the consequence.
- Water and fuel in hot or austere environments dominate weight and transport.
- Contracted and host-nation support may not be available in all threat conditions —
  state the assumption.
- Forgetting the return leg and redeployment.

## Verify
- [ ] Requirements calculated from approved planning factors, not guesses.
- [ ] Limiting factor identified and briefed for each COA.
- [ ] Stockage objectives set for critical supply classes.
- [ ] Distribution and reception throughput calculated per day at each node.
- [ ] Maintenance and medical (role, evacuation timelines) covered in the annex.
