# COA wargaming and analytical wargame design

**Goal:** use structured adversarial play to find a plan's weaknesses before reality does,
and to design wargames that answer a sponsor's question. Wargames are tools for
**insight and decision support**, not prediction — a recurring theme in every
professional handbook.

## Part A — COA wargaming inside the planning process
Step 4 of the MDMP/JPP (see
`defence-public-safety.military-planning-and-command.operational-planning.military-decision-making-process-and-orders-production`).

**Prepare**
- Gather tools: the COA statements and sketches, the adversary's **most likely** and
  **most dangerous** COAs, maps/models, time-phased friendly and adversary capabilities,
  evaluation criteria, and a recording sheet or synchronisation matrix.
- Assign roles: **facilitator** (often XO/COS), **red cell** (an intelligence officer who
  thinks and argues as the adversary — not a strawman), **recorder**, and functional
  representatives (fires, sustainment, protection, information, etc.).
- List assumptions and the **critical events** or decision points to wargame.
- Choose a method: **belt** (phases across the whole front in time bands), **avenue-in-depth**
  (one axis at a time), **box** (one critical area/event in detail). Box method suits time-short planning.

**Execute** — for each critical event:
1. **Action** — friendly force acts.
2. **Reaction** — red cell responds realistically.
3. **Counteraction** — friendly adjusts.
Then assess results and **record**: timings, resources used, decision points, required
CCIRs, branch plans needed, risks, sustainment and casualty implications, and changes to
the COA. Keep it moving; record disagreements rather than resolving every one in the room.

**Outputs:** refined COAs, synchronisation matrix, decision support template/matrix,
updated CCIRs and risk, and evaluation data for COA comparison.

**Red-team discipline:** the facilitator protects red's freedom to win; a wargame where
blue always succeeds is a rehearsal, not analysis.

## Part B — designing an analytical or educational wargame
1. **Sponsor and question:** who needs what decision or insight? Write 1–3 objectives as
   questions ("What are the key logistics risks in phase 2?"), agreed in writing.
2. **Game type** to fit the question and time:
   | Type | Good for |
   |---|---|
   | Seminar / discussion | Policy, concepts, broad issues |
   | Matrix game | Fast, flexible multi-actor problems; players argue actions, adjudicator rules |
   | Manual board / map | Operational and logistical trade-offs with rules |
   | Computer-assisted | Large-force calculations; risk of hiding assumptions |
   | Kriegsspiel-style (free adjudication) | Expert players, judgement-based outcomes |
3. **Players and cells:** blue, red, sometimes green (other actors), and a **white
   cell** (control and adjudication). Choose red players with real expertise in the
   adversary.
4. **Model and adjudication:** rules, probability tables or expert judgement; document
   the logic so results are traceable. State data sources and their classification.
5. **Data collection plan:** what to capture (decisions and their reasons, not just
   outcomes), who captures it, how (scribes, forms, recordings).
6. **Playtest** with a small group before the event.
7. **Run it:** brief players on purpose and rules, keep to time, run a **hotwash** at
   the end while memories are fresh.
8. **Report:** answer the sponsor's questions, separate *findings* from *insights* and
   *recommendations*, and state limitations (abstractions, player knowledge, single run).

## Gotchas
- Answering a question nobody asked because the game was designed before the objectives.
- "Wargame as validation": manipulating red or adjudication so a preferred plan wins.
- Over-complex rules eat time and hide the analysis.
- Results from one play-through treated as statistics.
- Exercise scenarios (for training) and analytical wargames have different goals —
  don't let a training script answer an analytical question.

## Verify
- [ ] Objectives/questions agreed with the sponsor (or commander) before design.
- [ ] Red cell staffed with someone empowered to play the adversary well.
- [ ] Action–reaction–counteraction recorded per critical event with decision points.
- [ ] Data collection plan exists and captures reasoning.
- [ ] Report separates findings, insights and recommendations, with limitations.
