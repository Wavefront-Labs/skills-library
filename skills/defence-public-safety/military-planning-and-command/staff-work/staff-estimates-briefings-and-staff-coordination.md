# Staff estimates, briefings and staff coordination

**Goal:** be the staff officer a commander relies on: you know your function's picture
now, you bring answers rather than problems, your briefs take the time they need and no
more, and nothing leaves your desk uncoordinated.

## 1. The staff system
- The NATO **continental staff system** numbers functions; the prefix shows the level
  (**J** joint, **G** army division and above / general officer level, **S** brigade and
  below, **N** naval, **A** air):
  1 personnel · 2 intelligence · 3 operations · 4 logistics · 5 plans · 6 communications
  and information systems · 7 training/force development (varies) · 8 finance/resources ·
  9 civil-military cooperation. Some nations use differing numbers above 6 — check yours.
- The **chief of staff / executive officer** directs and synchronises the staff; the staff
  supports the commander and subordinate units — it has no command authority of its own.
- Special and personal staff (legal, medical, chaplain, public affairs, etc.) advise
  directly within their specialism.

## 2. The running estimate
Each section keeps a continuous assessment of its area, at minimum:
- Facts and assumptions, with dates; status of friendly capabilities in your function;
  significant changes; conclusions and **recommendations** to the commander.
- Update to the battle rhythm, and immediately when something affects a CCIR.
- The estimate is your briefing source — if your brief and your estimate differ, one is wrong.

## 3. Briefings (FM 6-0 identifies four types)
| Type | Purpose | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| **Information** | Inform, no decision | BLUF, facts, implications |
| **Decision** | Get a decision | Problem, facts/assumptions, options, analysis, recommendation, "request decision" |
| **Mission** | Pass instructions (e.g. orders brief) | Five-paragraph order sequence |
| **Staff** | Coordinate/exchange among staff | Section-by-section updates |
Rules:
- **Bottom line up front:** say the conclusion or the decision needed in the first sentence.
- Know your audience's time; rehearse; one slide per idea; source every number.
- Anticipate questions; have backup slides, not more main slides.
- End a decision brief by stating the decision required in words that can be answered yes/no or by choosing an option.
- Afterwards: record the decision and guidance, and disseminate it the same day.

## 4. Completed staff work
The classic principle: present a solution so complete that the commander only has to
approve or disapprove. In practice:
- Work out all the details, coordinate with every affected section, and present a single
  recommended action with the supporting product (draft order, signed letter, plan) ready.
- Don't ask the boss what to do; ask yourself "If I were the boss, would I sign this?"
- Partial ideas and long explanations are for drafts with your supervisor, not the commander.

## 5. Staff coordination
- **Taskers/staff actions** have an action officer, suspense (deadline), and list of
  sections to coordinate with. Log them.
- Coordinate laterally and with subordinate units **before** briefing: a section that
  first hears your plan in front of the commander will object then.
- Record **concurrence** or **non-concurrence** with reasons; unresolved non-concurrence
  goes to the chief of staff with both views represented fairly.
- Keep a **staff journal/log**: what you received, decided, sent, when.
- Write in the service's style: short paragraphs, active voice, BLUF, the "so what" up front.

## Gotchas
- Stovepiping: perfect sectional work that nobody else knows about.
- Slide-deck culture replacing analysis — a clear one-page paper often beats 30 slides.
- Briefing assumptions as facts; dating information omitted.
- Missing suspenses because the tasker log lives in one person's inbox.

## Verify
- [ ] Your running estimate has dated facts, assumptions and recommendations.
- [ ] Every brief opens with BLUF; decision briefs end with a clear decision request.
- [ ] Products are coordinated with affected sections, with concurrences recorded.
- [ ] Decisions and guidance recorded and disseminated the same day.
- [ ] Staff actions logged with action officer and suspense.
