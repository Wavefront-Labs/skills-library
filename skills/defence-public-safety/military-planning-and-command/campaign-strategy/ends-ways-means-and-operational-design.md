# Ends, ways, means and operational design

**Goal:** design a campaign — a series of related operations — whose military actions
add up to the political purpose they serve, and recognise when they no longer do. This is
the staff-college body of theory used to analyse campaigns historically and to frame
plans; it is conceptual, not tactical.

## 1. Start from the political end
- Strategy is the link between **policy objectives** and the **use of force** (and other
  instruments: diplomatic, information, military, economic — "DIME").
- Clausewitz's core point: war serves political purposes; its character is shaped by
  them and by friction, chance and passion (the "trinity" of people, army, government).
  A campaign that wins battles but not the political object has failed.
- Write down the **desired end state** and the **conditions** that define it, in terms
  the political leadership has agreed. Ambiguous ends are the most common root failure
  in campaign case studies.

## 2. Ends–ways–means–risk (Lykke)
- **Ends:** objectives. **Ways:** concepts for achieving them. **Means:** resources.
  **Risk** is the gap between them.
- Test a strategy by asking: are the ends **suitable** (they achieve the purpose), the
  ways **feasible** with these means, and the whole **acceptable** in cost and risk?
  If not, change the ends, find new ways, add means — or accept and state the risk.
- Re-run the test as conditions change; strategy is continuous, not a document.

## 3. Operational design (the campaign-level toolkit)
- **Understand the environment and the problem** before solutions: actors, relationships,
  tensions. Doctrines call this design or framing.
- **Centre of gravity (COG):** the source of power or strength that gives an actor
  freedom of action or will. **Strange's model** breaks it down:
  critical capabilities (what the COG does) → critical requirements (what it needs) →
  **critical vulnerabilities** (requirements that are deficient and exploitable). Analyse
  your own side's COG with equal rigour — protecting it is part of the design.
- **Decisive points:** conditions, events or places that give a marked advantage when
  achieved; arranged along **lines of operation** (physical, often conventional) or
  **lines of effort** (logical, e.g. security, governance, economic development).
- **Operational reach and culmination:** how far and long a force can operate before
  it must pause; the culminating point is where it can no longer continue effectively.
  Plan pauses and sustainment so culmination doesn't happen by surprise.
- **Phasing** (e.g. shape, deter, seize the initiative, dominate, stabilise, enable civil
  authority in older US joint doctrine) and **transitions** between phases.
- **Conflict termination:** how operations end and hand over — the war-to-peace
  transition is planned at the start.

## 4. Theory as a lens (short reading list by idea)
| Idea | Associated thinker |
|---|---|
| War as continuation of policy; friction; culminating point | Clausewitz |
| Deception, knowing enemy and self, winning without fighting | Sun Tzu |
| Lines of operation, interior lines | Jomini |
| Indirect approach | Liddell Hart |
| Decision cycles (OODA) and tempo | Boyd |
| Sea power; maritime strategy | Mahan, Corbett |
| Strategy as a broader practice | Freedman, Gray, Strachan |
Use these to ask better questions, not to borrow slogans.

## 5. Assessing a campaign
Set **measures of effectiveness** (are we creating the conditions?) separately from
**measures of performance** (are we doing the tasks?). Plenty of activity with no change
in conditions signals a strategy problem, not an effort problem.

## Gotchas
- COG analysis that lists everything as a COG is useless; pick one per level and
  justify it.
- Mirror-imaging the adversary's goals and reasoning.
- Treating means as fixed when politics might change them — or ends as fixed when
  they've drifted.
- "Lines of effort" without owners and metrics are decoration.

## Verify
- [ ] End state and conditions are written and agreed with the political level.
- [ ] Ends-ways-means checked for suitability, feasibility, acceptability; risk stated.
- [ ] COG analysis (friendly and adversary) via capabilities → requirements → vulnerabilities.
- [ ] Decisive points arranged on lines of operation/effort with phases and transitions.
- [ ] Assessment uses measures of effectiveness, not activity counts.
