# Integrating influence activities into campaign planning

**Goal:** understand, as a planner or staff reviewer, how psychological operations
(PSYOP; renamed **MISO**, military information support operations, in US doctrine in
2010, with the US Army restoring "PSYOP" for its units in 2017 while joint doctrine kept
MISO — check the current term in your nation's doctrine) fit into a
military plan, how they are governed, and how their effect is assessed. This skill is
deliberately at the level of published doctrine and staff integration — the planning
framework, approvals and legal limits — not message-crafting technique.

## 1. What it is (and isn't), doctrinally
- Planned activities to convey selected, approved, **truthful** information to
  **foreign** audiences to influence attitudes and behaviour in support of objectives.
  NATO and US doctrine both stress that PSYOP target foreign audiences.
- Distinct from **public affairs**, which informs domestic and international publics and
  the media and must stay credible and separate. Doctrine requires deconfliction, not
  merger, of these functions.
- One of several information-related capabilities coordinated in an information cell/
  working group (alongside public affairs, civil-military cooperation, key leader
  engagement, electronic warfare, operations security, military deception).

## 2. Place in the planning process
- PSYOP planners join **mission analysis**: they contribute to the understanding of the
  information environment and to the staff's running estimates.
- They derive **psychological objectives (POs)** from the commander's objectives, and
  **supporting objectives** that describe a desired, observable behaviour change of a
  defined audience. A PO that can't be observed can't be assessed.
- Products appear in the order as an annex (in US/NATO formats, typically in the
  information annex) with tasks, coordination and approval chains.

## 3. Target-audience analysis as research
Doctrine treats audience analysis as a structured research step: identify which
groups' behaviour bears on the objective, their conditions, vulnerabilities to
information, susceptibility, and accessibility through available media — drawing on
intelligence, open-source research and cultural advisers. For a reviewer the key
questions are: is the analysis evidence-based and current, are assumptions stated, and
are sources recorded? (Analytic method detail is beyond this skill's scope.)

## 4. Approvals and legal limits (the part most planners must know)
- **Approval authority:** programmes and products are approved through a defined chain,
  often set at high level by policy; planners must know who can approve what, and the
  timeline that imposes.
- **Legal review** by the staff judge advocate/legal adviser: compliance with the law of
  armed conflict (e.g. no perfidy, protection of civilians, prohibition on encouraging
  violations), domestic law and policy (in the US, restrictions on targeting US persons),
  and host-nation agreements.
- **Attribution** policy (attributed, delayed attribution, non-attributed) is a policy
  decision with oversight requirements — not a planner's choice.
- **Ethics and credibility:** doctrine notes that falsehood discovered damages the
  credibility of all communication, including public affairs. Truthfulness is both an
  ethical and a practical constraint.

## 5. Coordination and deconfliction
Coordinate through the information working group and the targeting process:
de-conflict with public affairs messaging, civil-military projects, key leader
engagements and allied/partner activities so the force doesn't contradict itself.
Coordinate with intelligence on collection to support assessment.

## 6. Assessment
- Set **measures of effectiveness** tied to the observable behaviours in the supporting
  objectives and baseline them before activity starts.
- Distinguish **measures of performance** (products delivered, reach) from effect;
  attribution of behaviour change to an activity is genuinely hard — state confidence.
- Feed assessment into the battle rhythm's assessment working group.

## Gotchas
- Treating influence as an add-on after the plan is made, rather than part of design.
- Objectives framed as messages ("tell them X") instead of behaviours.
- Blurring public affairs and PSYOP — damages credibility and may break law or policy.
- Assuming reach equals effect.

## Verify
- [ ] Psychological objectives derive from commander's objectives and name observable behaviours.
- [ ] Audience analysis is documented with sources and assumptions.
- [ ] Approval chain and legal review identified and scheduled in the timeline.
- [ ] Deconfliction with public affairs and other activities recorded.
- [ ] MOEs baselined and tied to the objectives.
