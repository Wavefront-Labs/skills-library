# Military decision-making process and orders production

**Goal:** a staff turns a higher headquarters' order into a feasible, understood plan and
issues it with enough time left for subordinates to plan too. This describes the
published doctrinal process (US Army MDMP, which mirrors the joint planning process,
NATO's COPD and the UK Combat Estimate) as a staff-work method — it is the same
problem-solving structure taught at staff colleges.

## 0. Time first
- **One-third / two-thirds rule:** the headquarters uses at most a third of the available
  time; subordinates get two-thirds. Build a reverse planning timeline at the start.
- **Parallel planning:** issue **warning orders (WARNOs)** early so subordinates start
  before the full order exists.

## 1. Receipt of mission
Gather the higher order, maps/products and current estimates; alert the staff; issue
WARNO 1 (type of operation, general location, initial timeline, movements to start).

## 2. Mission analysis (the most important step)
- Analyse higher commander's intent and concept two levels up.
- Identify **specified tasks** (stated), **implied tasks** (necessary but unstated) and
  **essential tasks** (those that define success) → draft **mission statement**:
  who, what (task), when, where, why (purpose).
- Constraints (must/must not), facts and assumptions (assumptions must be valid and
  necessary — track them; each needs a way to be confirmed).
- Running **staff estimates** from each function; intelligence preparation of the
  operating environment; available resources vs requirements; risk assessment.
- Initial **commander's critical information requirements (CCIRs)**.
- Brief the commander → approved mission statement, **commander's intent** (purpose, key
  tasks, end state) and **planning guidance**. WARNO 2.

## 3. Course-of-action (COA) development
Each COA must be **feasible, acceptable, suitable, distinguishable and complete** (the
screening criteria). Develop 2–3 genuinely different options; for each: broad concept,
array of forces and tasks, decisive/shaping/sustaining efforts, a sketch and statement.
Distinct COAs, not one favourite plus two straw men.

## 4. COA analysis (wargaming)
Step each COA through action–reaction–counteraction against the most likely and most
dangerous adversary COAs, recording results in a synchronisation matrix: decision
points, branches, resource issues, risks. See
`defence-public-safety.military-planning-and-command.wargaming.course-of-action-wargaming-and-analytical-wargame-design`.

## 5. COA comparison
Compare against **evaluation criteria** set in advance (e.g. simplicity, flexibility,
risk, time, sustainability), often in a weighted decision matrix. The matrix informs —
it doesn't decide; record the reasoning.

## 6. COA approval
Brief the commander with the recommendation; the commander selects or modifies, refines
intent and CCIRs. WARNO 3.

## 7. Orders production, dissemination and transition
- **Five-paragraph OPORD** (NATO/US): 1 *Situation*, 2 *Mission*, 3 *Execution*
  (intent, concept, tasks to subordinate units, coordinating instructions),
  4 *Sustainment* (UK "Service Support"), 5 *Command and Signal*. Annexes carry detail.
- Conduct a **confirmation brief** (subordinates restate their task and purpose) and
  **rehearsals**; transition to the current-operations staff.
- Changes after issue go out as **fragmentary orders (FRAGOs)** that state only what changed.

## Abbreviated planning
With little time the commander gives more directive guidance, fewer COAs (sometimes one),
and the staff shortens steps — but mission analysis is never skipped.

## Gotchas
- Assumptions turning into "facts" without validation is a common planning failure.
- Overlong orders: subordinates must find their task and purpose on one page.
- Planning that consumes the subordinates' time negates the plan's quality.
- Branch plans for likely deviations are part of the product, not an extra.

## Verify
- [ ] Timeline built with the one-third/two-thirds rule; WARNOs issued.
- [ ] Mission statement has who/what/when/where/why; essential tasks identified.
- [ ] Assumptions listed with validation method; CCIRs drafted.
- [ ] COAs pass FAS-DC screening and were wargamed against two adversary COAs.
- [ ] OPORD follows five paragraphs; confirmation brief done.
