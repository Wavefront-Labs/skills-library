# Line patrol, sign cutting and encounter procedure

**Goal:** detect and respond to illegal crossings between official ports of entry, locate
people (many of whom are in medical danger), and process every encounter lawfully —
including people seeking protection. Written mainly from US Border Patrol practice; EU
(Frontex and national border guards) and other services operate under different law.

## 1. Detection layers
- **Line watch:** fixed or mobile observation of likely crossing areas.
- **Technology:** unattended ground sensors, surveillance towers and cameras, aerial
  assets. A sensor hit is a lead, not an identification — animals and weather trip them.
- **Sign cutting:** systematically checking ground for evidence of passage ("sign"):
  footprints, disturbed vegetation, dropped items. Agents drag dirt roads ("drag roads")
  with tyres so fresh prints stand out, then cut those roads at intervals.

## 2. Sign cutting and aging (the craft)
- **Cut for sign** across the probable line of travel rather than following a single print;
  you find the trail faster and avoid destroying it.
- Look low, with the light — low-angle sun or a flashlight held near the ground at night
  makes prints readable.
- **Aging sign:** compare with a reference print you make yourself in the same soil; look at
  edge crispness, debris blown or fallen into the print, insect/animal tracks over it, dew
  or frost, overnight wind. Practise aging in your area; soil and weather change everything.
- Count groups by distinct sole patterns and stride; note direction and whether people are
  carrying loads.
- Communicate the trail (location, direction, estimated age and number) so units can
  leapfrog ahead rather than everyone following from behind.
The same tracking skills are used in search and rescue; cross-train with SAR teams.

## 3. Legal limits away from the line (US)
- Roving patrol stops need **reasonable suspicion** (*US v. Brignoni-Ponce*, 1975 — apparent
  ethnicity alone is never enough).
- Fixed **checkpoints** may stop vehicles briefly for questioning without suspicion
  (*US v. Martinez-Fuerte*), but searches need consent or probable cause.
- Statutory authority extends within a "reasonable distance" (regulation: 100 air miles)
  of the border, but constitutional limits still apply.

## 4. Encounter procedure
- Safety first: numbers, terrain, possible smuggling guides; call for backup with location.
- **Medical assessment immediately.** Heat illness, dehydration, injuries from falls and
  walls, and exposure are common; hundreds of migrant deaths are recorded in the US
  southwest each year. Carry water and medical kits; request EMS/air evacuation early.
- Identify vulnerable people: unaccompanied children, pregnant women, injured, trafficking
  victims (signs: someone else controls documents/speech, debt, fear).
- **Protection claims:** anyone expressing fear of return must be referred into the
  asylum/credible-fear process; the duty of non-refoulement is international law.
  Record the expression of fear accurately.
- Property: inventory and receipt belongings; return them at release/removal.
- Report: detection method, sign/time line, apprehension location, health status and care,
  statements, any use of force.

## Gotchas
- Following tracks without radioing them — the group is lost when your shift ends.
- Treating a distress call from the desert as an enforcement call; rescue comes first.
- Ignoring a stated fear of return because it wasn't phrased as "asylum".

## Verify
- [ ] Trail reports include location, direction, age estimate and group size.
- [ ] Every encounter has a recorded medical assessment.
- [ ] Protection claims recorded and referred.
- [ ] Stops away from the border have articulated reasonable suspicion.

*Uncertainty:* US case law and practice summarised; sign-aging is highly local and best learned
in the field from experienced trackers.
