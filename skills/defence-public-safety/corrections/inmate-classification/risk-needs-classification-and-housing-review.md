# Risk/needs classification and housing review

**Goal:** place each person in the **least restrictive** custody level and housing that safely
manages their risk — of escape, of harm to others, of being harmed — using a structured,
documented process that is reviewed as circumstances change.

Classification decisions affect liberty, safety and access to programmes, and are reviewable
(administrative appeal, judicial review, civil-rights litigation). They must follow the agency's
validated instrument and policy, and the reasons must be written down.

## 1. Two separate questions
- **Security level / categorisation** — how secure must the *facility* be? (External risk:
  escape likelihood and the harm if they did escape.) England & Wales: adult male categories
  A–D; US: custody/security levels such as minimum, medium, close, maximum.
- **Internal placement** — where in the facility, and with whom? (Internal risk: violence,
  victimisation, gang or co-defendant conflicts, self-harm, medical and mental-health needs.)
Mixing the two up is a common error: a low escape risk can still be a high risk to a cellmate.

## 2. Initial classification (at reception/intake)
1. **Gather the facts:** current offence and sentence or remand status, prior custody behaviour,
   escapes/attempts, detainers or warrants, violence history, intelligence, age, medical and
   mental-health screening, known enemies and co-defendants.
2. **Score the objective instrument** your agency uses, exactly as written. Objective
   classification instruments are designed and validated on local populations; skipping items
   or "rounding up" breaks validity.
3. **Vulnerability and sexual-safety screening** — e.g. US PREA screening for risk of
   victimisation and abusiveness within 72 hours of arrival; England & Wales Cell Sharing Risk
   Assessment (CSRA) before a person shares a cell. Use the results in the housing decision.
4. **Overrides:** a discretionary override (up or down) is allowed but must cite a specific,
   recorded reason and usually supervisor approval. High override rates (agencies often target
   roughly 5–15%, but check local guidance) suggest the instrument or practice needs review.
5. **Record the decision and tell the person** the outcome and how to challenge it.

## 3. Needs and case planning
Risk-Needs-Responsivity (RNR) principles: match intensity of intervention to risk, target
criminogenic needs (e.g. substance misuse, antisocial attitudes, employment), and deliver in a way
the person can respond to. Needs tools (e.g. OASys in England & Wales; LSI-R or COMPAS in some US
systems) feed sentence/case plans. Know which tool your agency uses and its known limitations,
including published debate about fairness across groups.

## 4. Housing placement
- Check separation/non-association (keep-separate) lists before every placement and move.
- Apply legal separations: juveniles from adults, sex separation, remand/sentenced where
  required. Transgender placement follows a specific case-by-case policy and review panel in
  most systems.
- Protective custody and segregation/restrictive housing are restrictive placements with their
  own authorisation, review frequency and health-check requirements; international standards
  (Mandela Rules 43–45) prohibit prolonged solitary confinement (over 15 consecutive days).

## 5. Reclassification and review
Review at set intervals (policy-defined, often every 6–12 months) and on trigger events: new
charges or sentence, serious disciplinary findings, positive progress, intelligence, health
change, or approaching release. Progression to lower security as risk reduces is a purpose of
the system, not a favour.

## Gotchas
- Classifying by offence label alone over-classifies many people and misses institutional risk.
- An undocumented override is indistinguishable from bias on review.
- Placing someone in a shared cell before the cell-sharing/vulnerability screen is complete is a
  recurring finding in in-cell homicide inquiries.
- Separation lists go stale; update them when intelligence changes.

## Verify
- [ ] Instrument scored completely and as written; score and level recorded.
- [ ] Any override has a specific written reason and approval.
- [ ] Vulnerability/cell-sharing screen done before shared housing.
- [ ] Separation list checked for this placement.
- [ ] Next review date set; person told the decision and appeal route.

*Uncertainty:* instrument names, review intervals and override targets vary by agency and
change over time; the 5–15% override figure is a commonly cited rule of thumb, not a standard.
Confirm against your agency's classification manual.
