# Cell, person and mail search procedure

**Goal:** find and remove items that threaten safety (weapons, drugs, phones, escape aids)
through searches that are **authorised, proportionate, respectful and documented** — so finds
stand up in an adjudication or prosecution and the search itself is never the complaint.

Searches are an intrusion governed by law and policy. Every search type below needs a lawful
basis and must be conducted exactly as your agency's search policy sets out; this file is a
reference to that process, not an authority to go beyond it.

## 1. Authority and proportionality
- Know *which* search you are authorised to do and *why*: routine (scheduled/random), targeted
  (intelligence-led, usually needs a supervisor's authority and a recorded reason), or
  post-incident.
- Use the **least intrusive search** that meets the need. Escalation to a more intrusive level
  normally needs a specific justification and authorisation recorded at the time.
- Strip/full searches are tightly regulated (US: *Bell v. Wolfish*, *Florence v. Board of
  Chosen Freeholders* and state law; England & Wales: Prison Rules and the national searching
  policy). Intimate (body-cavity) searches are generally performed **only by medical
  practitioners** under specific legal authority — never by custody staff.
- Searches must not be used as punishment or harassment; repeated searches of one person
  without a recorded reason invite a complaint and a finding.

## 2. Searching people
- **Same-gender searcher** for anything beyond a basic metal-detector or scanner pass, per
  policy (US PREA standards restrict cross-gender pat and strip searches; UK policy sets
  rules including for transgender people). Have a witness officer where policy requires.
- Explain what will happen before you start; give clear instructions; keep dignity measures
  (e.g. never fully unclothed at once where the policy uses a two-stage search).
- Use available technology first where policy allows (walk-through or hand-held metal
  detectors, body scanners, detection dogs) — it is less intrusive and often more effective.
- Stop and seek a supervisor if the person refuses; refusal is handled under the use-of-force
  and disciplinary policies, not improvised.

## 3. Searching cells and areas
- Two officers where possible: one searches, one observes and records. The occupant is usually
  removed (and searched) first; follow local policy on whether they may observe.
- Work systematically — a fixed order (e.g. door → walls clockwise → fittings → furniture →
  bedding → property) so nothing is skipped and the record can say what was searched.
- **Leave the cell as you found it.** Damage to property, family photos thrown on the floor, or
  legal papers read without authority are the commonest search complaints and destroy trust.
- Check fabric of the cell (bars, windows, vents, fittings) for tampering as part of the same search.

## 4. Mail, property and visits
- Screen incoming mail and property per policy (scanners, drug-detection equipment,
  photocopying policies where in force). Drug-impregnated paper is a recognised route; follow
  your agency's current screening process rather than ad-hoc methods.
- **Legal and other privileged correspondence** (lawyers, courts, some oversight bodies) may
  normally be opened only in the person's presence to check for illicit enclosures, and **not
  read**, unless specific legal authority exists (England & Wales: Prison Rule 39). Misreading
  privilege rules can void prosecutions and breach the law.
- Visits: follow the visits-search and observation policy; record intelligence, don't act alone.

## 5. Finds: handle as evidence
1. Photograph in place if policy allows; note exact location.
2. Handle minimally with gloves (possible fingerprints/DNA; possible sharps; unknown substances
   can be dangerous — do not smell or taste, and follow your drugs-exposure safety procedure).
3. Bag, seal, label (who, what, where, when); start the **continuity/chain-of-custody record**.
4. Store in the designated secure location; submit intelligence and incident reports.
5. Inform the person what was taken and how it will be dealt with (adjudication, police referral,
   return, destruction).

## Gotchas
- The search record must say what *was* searched, not just what was found. "Nothing found" in an
  unsearched area is a false record.
- A find without continuity paperwork is useless at a hearing.
- Unknown powders: treat as hazardous (synthetic opioids and cannabinoids); PPE first.
- Staff are a contraband route too — follow staff-search rules yourself without exception.

## Verify
- [ ] Search type, reason and authorising officer recorded before or at the search.
- [ ] Least intrusive method used; any escalation justified and authorised.
- [ ] Gender and dignity rules followed; witness present where required.
- [ ] Privileged mail opened only as the law permits and not read.
- [ ] Every find bagged, labelled and logged with unbroken continuity.

*Uncertainty:* the legal references are US federal and England & Wales headline authorities
only; state, provincial and agency search rules differ significantly. Confirm search levels,
gender rules and mail-screening methods in your current local policy.
