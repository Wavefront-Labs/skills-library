# Escort and court transport procedure

**Goal:** move a person in custody from A to B — court, hospital, another facility — with the
right person, the right papers, a restraint level that matches assessed risk, their health and
dignity protected, and an unbroken record of who had custody at every moment.

Escorts are high-risk: more escapes happen outside the wall than inside it, and people have died
in transport from heat, untreated illness and positional asphyxia. Restraint and search on
escort are governed by policy and law; follow your agency's escort policy exactly.

## 1. Before the move: risk assessment
- Complete or check the **escort risk assessment** for each person: escape risk (history,
  intelligence, outside associates, length of sentence remaining, recent bad news), violence
  risk, self-harm risk, medical needs, vulnerability, separation requirements.
- The assessment sets the **restraint level, staffing ratio and vehicle**. Higher-risk escorts
  may require authorisation by a senior manager and police notification.
- Restraint level must be **justified by the risk** — routine maximum restraint for everyone,
  restraint during childbirth or labour, or restraint during medical treatment without a
  specific, reviewed justification has been found unlawful in several jurisdictions (e.g.
  England & Wales High Court rulings on hospital escorts; US federal and many state laws
  restrict restraint of pregnant people). Review the level whenever circumstances change.
- Check for **separation/non-association** with others on the same vehicle; keep juveniles,
  women and men, and vulnerable people separated as the law requires.

## 2. Handover: identity, papers, property
- **Positive identification** on collection and on handover: photo, name, number, date of birth,
  and where used biometrics. Wrong-person releases and transfers happen; this is where they're caught.
- **Paperwork travels with the person**: warrant/committal or production order, the escort
  record (in England & Wales the Person Escort Record, PER), risk flags, medical information
  needed in transit, and medication with administration instructions.
- Record the exact time custody passes to you and from you, with signatures — this is legal
  transfer of responsibility.
- Search the person and their property to policy before they board.

## 3. The vehicle and the route
- Search the vehicle before loading and after unloading (contraband and items left behind).
- Check communications, fuel, first-aid kit, cell doors, ventilation and climate control.
  **Heat in secure vehicles is a known killer** — monitor temperature and never leave people
  locked in a stationary vehicle without welfare checks.
- Vary routes and times where policy requires; avoid predictable stops. Don't disclose
  movement details on open channels or to the person in custody in advance.
- Make welfare checks at the intervals policy sets: water, toilet breaks at secure locations,
  medication on time, observation for self-harm risk.

## 4. At court or hospital
- Court cells and docks have their own rules on restraint in court; the court can direct removal
  of restraints and decisions should be recorded.
- Hospital escorts (including long "bedwatches"): positioning of officers, restraint review with
  clinicians, dignity during examinations, and the duty to respect medical confidentiality as far
  as security allows.

## 5. Incidents en route
- Medical emergency: stop safely, call ambulance, give first aid; healthcare overrides schedule.
- Escape attempt or hostile interception: follow the escort contingency plan — call police,
  protect life; do not pursue in a way that abandons others in your custody.
- Collision: secure the people in custody first, then the scene; notify control.
- Record every incident and all timings.

## Gotchas
- Mixing up two people with similar names at a busy court is the classic error — always
  two-point identity check.
- Restraint "because that's what we always do" will not survive review.
- Medication left at the facility can mean a missed dose for a person with epilepsy or diabetes.
- People get bad news at court (sentence, refused bail) — raise self-harm risk on the way back.

## Verify
- [ ] Current escort risk assessment completed; restraint level justified and recorded.
- [ ] Identity checked at every handover and each transfer of custody signed and timed.
- [ ] Warrant/order and escort record with the person; medication with them.
- [ ] Vehicle searched before and after; temperature and welfare checks logged.
- [ ] Any incident reported with full timings.

*Uncertainty:* reflects published UK (PER, court-escort contracts) and general US jail
transport practice; restraint rules for pregnancy, medical treatment and court are jurisdiction-
specific and have changed through recent litigation — check current local policy.
