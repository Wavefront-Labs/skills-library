# Traffic stops and impaired-driving investigation

**Goal:** stop a vehicle lawfully and safely, and if impairment is suspected, build a case
in which each step (stop, detention, arrest, test) rests on the observations from the
step before. Follows the US NHTSA model; other jurisdictions differ in tests and powers.

## 1. The stop
- **Basis:** reasonable suspicion of a traffic or criminal violation (*Whren v. US*:
  any objective violation suffices in US federal law; some states are stricter). UK officers
  have a general power to stop vehicles (Road Traffic Act 1988 s.163).
- Before lighting up: call in plate, location and direction; choose a stop location with a
  wide shoulder, lighting and no blind curve. Don't stop on a bridge or just over a hill crest.
- Position the patrol car offset toward traffic, behind the stopped vehicle, to create a
  safety lane. Use takedown/spot lights at night.
- Approach: decide driver-side vs passenger-side per conditions — passenger-side approach
  keeps you out of passing traffic, a leading cause of officer deaths at stops.
- Explain the reason for the stop early. Keep the stop no longer than its mission requires
  (*Rodriguez v. US*: extending a stop for an unrelated dog sniff needs its own suspicion).

## 2. DUI detection — the three phases
**Phase 1, vehicle in motion.** Note driving cues (weaving, drifting, wide turns, slow
response to signals, braking erratically, driving without lights at night) and the stop itself
(slow to stop, strikes kerb). Record them; they are the first evidence.

**Phase 2, personal contact.** Odour of alcohol or cannabis, bloodshot eyes, slurred speech,
fumbling documents, inconsistent answers, divided-attention test opportunities (asking for
two items at once). Then the exit: how they get out of the vehicle.

**Phase 3, pre-arrest screening.** The three Standardized Field Sobriety Tests:
- **Horizontal Gaze Nystagmus (HGN):** six clues (lack of smooth pursuit, distinct nystagmus
  at maximum deviation, onset before 45°, each eye).
- **Walk-and-Turn:** eight clues (balance during instructions, starts too soon, stops
  walking, misses heel-to-toe, steps off line, uses arms, improper turn, wrong step count).
- **One-Leg Stand:** four clues (sways, uses arms, hops, puts foot down), 30-second count.
Then a preliminary breath test where lawful.

## 3. SFST validity gotchas
- The tests are validated **only when administered exactly as standardised**. Deviating from
  the instructions, the stance, or the surface is the main line of defence cross-examination.
- Check for medical exclusions: leg or back injuries, inner-ear conditions, age and weight
  limits per the manual; footwear (offer to let the subject remove high heels).
- Surface must be reasonably dry, hard, level and non-slippery; note conditions.
- Record clues as observed, not as "failed".
- Cannabis and other drugs don't map onto the alcohol validations; refer to a Drug
  Recognition Expert (DRE) for the 12-step evaluation where available.

## 4. After arrest
- Implied-consent advisement read verbatim from the agency form.
- Observation/deprivation period before an evidential breath test (commonly 15–20 minutes,
  no eating, drinking, smoking or regurgitation) — check your instrument's protocol.
- Blood draws for drugs or refusals: warrant is generally required (*Missouri v. McNeely*,
  *Birchfield v. North Dakota* for blood vs breath).
- Arrange for the vehicle (tow/inventory per policy) and passengers.

## 5. Crash scenes (brief)
Protect the scene (vehicle placement, cones/flares), triage and EMS, then preserve evidence
(final rest positions, debris fields, tyre marks) before vehicles move; call reconstruction
for serious injury or fatal crashes.

## Verify
- [ ] Report lists cues from all three phases in order.
- [ ] SFST instructions given verbatim, exclusions checked, surface noted.
- [ ] Deprivation period timed and logged before the evidential test.
- [ ] Stop duration justified by its purpose.

*Uncertainty:* clue counts follow the NHTSA manual as commonly taught; editions change, and
non-US jurisdictions use different screening (e.g. UK roadside breath and drug-wipe procedures).
