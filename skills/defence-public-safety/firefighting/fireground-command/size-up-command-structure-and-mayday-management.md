# Size-up, command structure and Mayday management

**Goal:** turn a chaotic arrival into an organised incident within minutes, keep
every firefighter accounted for, and be ready when something goes wrong. Reference
for company and chief officers; follow your service's command procedures (US
NIMS/ICS, UK national operational guidance/JESIP, or local equivalents).

## 1. Initial radio report (first-arriving unit)
A consistent initial report sets everyone's mental picture:
1. Unit identity and arrival on scene, address confirmation.
2. Building: size, storeys, occupancy, construction if known.
3. Conditions: nothing showing / smoke showing / fire showing, and where.
4. Initial action: e.g. "offensive, going in with a line on side A".
5. Command: "Engine 1 establishing [name] Command" (or passing command if the
   officer is committed inside — a choice that must be explicit).
6. Instructions for incoming units (water supply, staging).
Follow with a **360° walk-round** where practical and a follow-up report including
basement access, rear conditions and any life-hazard information.

## 2. Strategy and risk-benefit
- **Offensive** (interior), **defensive** (exterior, protect exposures), or a declared
  **transition** between them. Never have crews interior and master streams flowing
  into the same building at the same time.
- Re-evaluate at fixed intervals set by your SOP.

## 3. Structure
- **Divisions** are geographic (Division 2 = second floor). **Groups** are functional (Ventilation Group, Search Group).
- Keep **span of control** at 3–7 resources per supervisor; 5 is the usual target.
- Hand-over of command is face-to-face where possible, with a briefing of the plan,
  assignments and hazards, then announced on the radio.

## 4. Accountability and RIT
- A **personnel accountability system** (tags, boards or electronic) tracks who is in
  which division or group.
- **PAR** (personnel accountability report) roll calls at benchmarks: on change of
  strategy, on Mayday, at time intervals, after a collapse or explosion.
- In the US, OSHA 29 CFR 1910.134(g)(3)–(4) requires at least two people outside the IDLH atmosphere when a team of at least two is inside for interior structural firefighting ("two-in, two-out"). Services commonly add a dedicated **Rapid Intervention Team (RIT/RIC)** — staged, equipped and with a plan; check your own SOP and state rules.

## 5. Mayday
Firefighters call Mayday for being lost, trapped, out of/low on air, injured or
caught in collapse. Report the Mayday content your service trains (commonly location, unit, name, assignment, air and resources needed).
Commander's actions:
1. Acknowledge, get the LUNAR, and **clear the channel** (emergency traffic; move other
   units to another channel if your SOP does).
2. Deploy RIT; request additional alarms and EMS; keep fire attack going — fire
   control often protects the trapped member more than anything else.
3. Assign a dedicated officer to the Mayday; conduct a PAR.
4. Keep calm, concise radio. Document times.

## Gotchas
- Officers who join the attack lose the picture; command from outside where possible.
- "Freelancing" (unassigned work) defeats accountability — address it immediately.
- Radio overload kills Maydays; enforce discipline before you need it.

## Verify
- [ ] Initial report covered building, conditions, action, command and instructions.
- [ ] Strategy declared; no offensive/defensive mix.
- [ ] Every crew assigned to a division or group and tracked.
- [ ] RIT in place before interior IDLH operations.
- [ ] You can recite your service's Mayday procedure from memory.
