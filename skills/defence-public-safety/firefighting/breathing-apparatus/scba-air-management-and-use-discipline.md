# SCBA air management and use discipline

**Goal:** never run out of air in a hazardous atmosphere. Most SCBA-related
casualties trace back to air management and procedure, not equipment failure. Follow
your manufacturer's instructions and service SOPs; this is a general reference.

## 1. Standards and terms
- US: **NFPA 1981** (design/performance) and **NFPA 1852** (selection, care and maintenance) (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) — being consolidated into newer NFPA standards; check current numbering. Europe/UK: **EN 137** (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked). Respiratory programme rules: OSHA 29 CFR 1910.134 in the US.
- **IDLH:** immediately dangerous to life or health — every interior fire atmosphere
  is treated as IDLH.
- **PASS/ADSU:** motion-sensing alarm that sounds if the wearer stops moving; often
  integrated into the set.

## 2. Daily and pre-use check (typical sequence)
1. Cylinder full (the gauge and the regulator/HUD agree within tolerance), within
   hydrostatic test date, no damage.
2. Harness and straps intact; facepiece clean, lens uncracked, valves seating.
3. Open cylinder; check for leaks; close and watch for pressure drop over the
   manufacturer's specified time.
4. Bleed down slowly and confirm the **low-air alarm** activates at the set point
   (set point per your manufacturer and the NFPA standard in force).
5. PASS/ADSU activates manually and on motion timeout.
Record the check.

## 3. Donning and seal
Facial hair that comes between the sealing surface and the face is not permitted (OSHA 1910.134(g)(1)). Do a user seal check every time you don the facepiece. OSHA requires fit testing before first use and at least annually (1910.134(f)(2)).

## 4. Air management in use
- Know your **own consumption**: run timed work drills in full gear so you know how
  many minutes a cylinder lasts *you* under heavy work, not the rated duration.
- **Turnaround point:** many services teach exiting with the low-air alarm never
  sounding inside the hazard — i.e. turning around with enough air to get out plus a
  reserve. The **rule of air management** (NFPA's guidance (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)) treats the low-air alarm as an emergency, not a signal to start leaving.
- **Check air at benchmarks** (on entry, each floor or task, every few minutes) and
  report it; the crew's turnaround is set by the member with the least air.
- **Entry control** (UK-style BA boards or tag systems) records entry time, pressure
  and calculated time of whistle; an entry control officer tracks every wearer and
  triggers emergency procedures if a team is overdue.

## 5. Emergencies
- Low or no air: declare a Mayday early. Know your set's emergency/bypass valve, the **RIC universal air connection (RIC UAC)** on NFPA sets (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked), and any manufacturer
  buddy-breathing feature — and train on them, as conditions inside are dark and cramped.
- Entanglement: stay calm, conserve air, swim or reverse out per training.

## 6. After use
Clean and disinfect the facepiece; decontaminate the harness (fire residue carries
carcinogens); refill or replace the cylinder; complete the post-use check and log any
defects. Remove faulty sets from service with a tag.

## Gotchas
- Air consumption rises sharply under stress and heavy work.
- Partly used cylinders "topped up" informally break tracking: follow SOP.
- Cold weather affects regulator performance and gauge readings.

- [ ] Turnaround and reporting rules briefed before entry.
- [ ] Entry control recorded every wearer in and out.
- [ ] Set cleaned, refilled and checked after use.
