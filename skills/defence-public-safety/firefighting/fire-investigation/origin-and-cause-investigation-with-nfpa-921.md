# Origin-and-cause investigation with NFPA 921

**Goal:** determine where a fire started and why, in a way that holds up in court,
insurance litigation or a fatality review. **NFPA 921** (Guide for Fire and Explosion
Investigations) is the methodological reference cited by courts in many countries;
**NFPA 1033** sets investigator qualifications. Check the current editions (NFPA 921 has a 2024 edition) (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).

## 1. The method is the scientific method (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)

1. **Recognise the need** (the question to answer).
2. **Define the problem** (scope, legal authority, who else has an interest).
3. **Collect data** — scene examination, witness interviews, documents, records.
4. **Analyse the data** — patterns, timelines, electrical evidence.
5. **Develop hypotheses** (inductive reasoning) about origin, then cause.
6. **Test hypotheses** (deductive reasoning) against *all* the data, including
   whether alternative hypotheses can be eliminated.
7. **Select the final hypothesis** — or declare it undetermined.
Origin comes first: you cannot credibly determine cause without a defined origin.

## 2. Scene work
- **Safety first:** structural stability, utilities isolated, atmospheric hazards,
  PPE (fire debris contains carcinogens and sharp hazards).
- **Legal authority:** emergency entry ends when the fire is out and the scene released;
  in the US, *Michigan v. Tyler* (1978) allows a reasonable, continuous investigation after the fire, but later separate entries need consent or a warrant; under *Michigan v. Clifford* (1984, plurality) an administrative warrant suffices if the primary object is cause and origin, and a criminal warrant if the primary object is evidence of a crime.
- **Document before disturbing:** photograph exterior to interior, least to most
  damaged; sketch with measurements; record the position of doors and windows (they
  change ventilation and patterns).
- **Interested parties:** notify those who may be affected (insurer, manufacturer,
  property owner) before destructive examination, to avoid **spoliation** claims.

## 3. Reading the evidence
- **Fire patterns:** lines of demarcation, V-patterns, char depth, calcination of
  plasterboard, clean burn, protected areas. Use them to plot heat and flame vectors back
  toward an area of origin.
- **Ventilation effects:** post-flashover burning and ventilation can create heavy
  damage far from the origin. Full-room involvement is the classic way investigators
  misplace the origin.
- **Arc mapping:** locating electrical arc sites on energised conductors can define
  the area of origin, because a circuit arcs where fire first attacks it while it is
  still energised.
- **Debris layering:** excavate in layers, as an archaeologist would; reconstruct
  furniture positions.
- **Samples** for ignitable-liquid analysis: sealed clean metal cans or approved bags,
  comparison samples, labelled, with chain of custody. Canine alerts must be confirmed by
  laboratory analysis.

## 4. Cause classification
NFPA 921 classifies cause as **accidental, natural, incendiary or undetermined** (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
"Incendiary" requires positive evidence of deliberate ignition, not just the absence
of an accidental source. Eliminating all known accidental causes and calling the fire
incendiary without a competent ignition source — **negative corpus** — is explicitly rejected as a method by NFPA 921 (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).

## Gotchas
- **Expectation and confirmation bias:** being told "the tenant was behind on rent"
  before examining the scene. Keep contextual information out of the origin analysis.
- Old folklore indicators (crazed glass, "alligatoring", spalling as proof of
  accelerant) are unreliable; NFPA 921 discusses why.
- An undetermined cause is a legitimate, honest result.

## Verify
- [ ] Authority for scene entry documented; interested parties notified.
- [ ] Scene photographed and sketched before excavation.
- [ ] Origin supported by multiple independent data (patterns, arcs, witnesses).
- [ ] Every credible alternative hypothesis tested and its elimination documented.
- [ ] Classification follows NFPA 921 definitions; no negative corpus.
