# Initial hazmat identification, zoning and decontamination

**Goal:** at a hazardous-materials incident, keep yourself and the public from becoming
casualties while you identify the product, then hand a well-organised scene to the
hazmat team. This covers awareness/operations-level practice; offensive work
(plugging, patching, product transfer) is for technician-level teams only.

## 1. Approach and isolate
- Approach **uphill and upwind** (and upstream for liquids); stage at a distance
  until you know what you have.
- **Isolate and deny entry.** If the product is unknown, use Guide 111 of the Emergency Response Guidebook (ERG2024; it also applies when a placard shows but the material is unidentified, or hazardous materials are suspected with no placard visible) and isolate as that guide says; for identified materials use the orange guide's isolation distance.
- Do not walk into vapour clouds, puddles or spilled powders to "have a look". Use
  binoculars.

## 2. Identify
Information sources, in rough order of speed:
- **Placards and UN/NA numbers** (four-digit numbers on vehicles and tanks) → ERG
  yellow (by number) or blue (by name) pages → orange guide page for initial actions.
- **NFPA 704 diamond** on fixed facilities (health, flammability, instability, special;
  0–4 scale).
- **Shipping papers** (in the cab, with the engineer/conductor for rail, on the bridge
  for vessels), **Safety Data Sheets** (SDS) at facilities, GHS pictograms on containers.
- **Container shape** (pressure vessels, cryogenic tanks) gives clues before you can
  read anything.
- Monitors (multi-gas, PID, radiation) only by trained users who know their limits.
- **Green pages** of the ERG give initial isolation and protective action distances for toxic-inhalation hazard materials.
Treat any confusion between sources as unknown until resolved.

## 3. Zones
- **Hot (exclusion) zone:** contamination present; entry only in correct PPE with a
  backup team.
- **Warm (contamination reduction) zone:** where decontamination happens.
- **Cold (support) zone:** command, staging, medical. Upwind.
Mark them physically (tape, cones) and tell everyone, including police and EMS.

## 4. PPE levels (US EPA scheme; other countries use similar tiers)
- **Level A:** fully encapsulating vapour-tight suit + SCBA — highest skin and
  respiratory protection.
- **Level B:** SCBA with splash protection — highest respiratory, lower skin.
- **Level C:** air-purifying respirator — only when the product and concentration
  are known.
- **Level D:** work uniform. Structural turnout gear gives limited chemical protection;
  ERG guides say when it is or is not adequate.

## 5. Decontamination
- **Gross decon** for victims: remove clothing (CHEMM: this alone may eliminate as much as 80–90% of all contamination), then a high-volume, low-pressure water rinse.
  Privacy and warmth matter; people refuse decon when they feel exposed.
- Responders: technical decon line in the warm zone, set up *before* entry teams go in.
- Contain runoff where practical, but life safety comes first.
NFPA 470 (consolidating former 472/473) sets competency expectations; in the US,
OSHA 29 CFR 1910.120 (HAZWOPER) governs training levels.

## Gotchas
- Secondary devices and deliberate releases: if it looks intentional, treat the scene
  as a crime scene and think about secondary hazards.
- Water-reactive materials — the ERG says when not to use water.
- Symptoms in responders (odour, eye irritation) mean you are too close: back off.

## Verify
- [ ] Approach was up-wind/up-hill and isolation distance set from the ERG.
- [ ] Product identified from at least two sources, or treated as unknown.
- [ ] Zones marked and communicated; decon ready before any entry.
- [ ] PPE level matched the identified hazard and the task.
