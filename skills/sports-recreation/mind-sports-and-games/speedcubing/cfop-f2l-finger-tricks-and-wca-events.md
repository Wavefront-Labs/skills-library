# Speedcubing — CFOP, F2L, finger tricks and WCA events

**Goal:** go from solving the cube to solving it fast — sub-60, sub-30,
sub-20 seconds — by learning CFOP in stages and practising the right
things, then compete at WCA events.

## 1. Notation
Faces: **R L U D F B**; a letter = 90° clockwise turn looking at that
face; **'** = anticlockwise; **2** = 180°. Lowercase (r) or "w" (Rw) =
two layers; **x y z** = whole-cube rotations; **M** = middle slice.
Every algorithm you'll learn is written this way.

## 2. The path through CFOP (Fridrich)
1. **Cross** — four bottom edges, solved on the bottom (not top and
   flip). Plan the whole cross during the 15-second **inspection**; aim
   for ≤ 8 moves. Practise solving cross on bottom from the start;
   learning it late is painful.
2. **F2L (first two layers)** — pair each bottom corner with its middle-
   layer edge and insert together (4 pairs). Learn **intuitively** first
   (understand how pairs join), then the efficient cases. This is where
   most time is lost and gained.
3. **OLL (orient last layer)** — make the top face one colour. Start with
   **2-look OLL** (~10 algorithms: edges, then corners), later full OLL
   (57 cases).
4. **PLL (permute last layer)** — put last-layer pieces in place. Start
   with **2-look PLL** (~6 algs), then full PLL (21 cases).
Rough milestones (individual variation is large): beginner method ~1-2
minutes; CFOP with 2-look last layer ~25-40 s; full PLL + good F2L
sub-20.
Alternatives: **Roux** (blocks + M-slice moves, fewer moves) and **ZZ**.
Pick one and stick with it.

## 3. Finger tricks and turning
- Hold the cube with thumbs on the front, fingers on the back; **don't
  regrip** constantly.
- U moves with index fingers flicking (U with right index, U' with left);
  R/R' with wrist; F moves with an index or thumb push.
- Learn algorithms **with finger tricks from the start** — retraining
  later is slower than learning right.
- Turn smoothly, not frantically: fewer lock-ups beats raw speed.

## 4. Look-ahead
The biggest step after algorithms: while solving one F2L pair, track the
next pair's pieces with your eyes. Drill by solving **slowly with no
pauses** — slow-turning practice reduces pauses more than fast
turning. TPS (turns per second) rises naturally as pauses disappear.

## 5. Equipment
- A modern **magnetic speedcube** (most major brands) is a large upgrade
  over an original non-speed cube.
- Tension/magnet settings: adjust so it doesn't pop or lock; light
  silicone-based lubricant as recommended by the manufacturer.

## 6. Practice routine
- Timer: csTimer (free, web) generates WCA-style scrambles and computes
  averages.
- Track **ao5** and **ao12** (average of 5/12, dropping best and worst).
- Split practice: full solves + targeted drills (cross-only, F2L-only,
  algorithm sets you're learning).
- Record and watch your solves to find pauses.

## 7. WCA competitions
- Events include 3x3, 2x2, 4x4-7x7, OH (one-handed), blindfolded, FMC
  (fewest moves), Pyraminx, Megaminx, Skewb, Square-1, Clock.
- Main 3x3 rounds are **average of 5** (best and worst dropped, mean of
  middle three).
- **15-second inspection**; starting after 15 s gives +2 penalty, after
  17 s DNF. Stackmat timer: both hands on the pads to start/stop.
- A solve that's one move off from solved gets +2 (and further
  deviations DNF) — check the current WCA Regulations for exact rules.
- First competition: register on the WCA website; cutoffs and time
  limits apply per round.

## Verify
- [ ] You solve cross on the bottom, planned in inspection.
- [ ] You know 2-look OLL and PLL with finger tricks.
- [ ] Your practice log has ao5/ao12 and drill time.
- [ ] You know the inspection and penalty rules before your first comp.
