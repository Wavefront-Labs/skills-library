# Scrabble and word games — word study, rack management and board strategy

**Goal:** score more per turn and win more games of Scrabble (and similar
games like Words With Friends) by learning the right words, keeping good
racks and reading the board.

## 1. Which dictionary?
- **Collins Scrabble Words (CSW)** is used in most of the world and in
  international events; **NWL** (NASPA Word List, formerly TWL) in North
  American club play. Words With Friends uses its own list.
- Learn for the list your club/events use; check the current edition.

## 2. Word study, in order of value
1. **All valid two-letter words** (about 100 in NWL, ~127 in CSW — check
   the list). They make parallel plays possible; the single biggest
   improvement for new players.
2. **Three-letter words**, especially with J, Q, X, Z (e.g. QI, ZA, XU,
   JO are valid in the major lists).
3. **Q-without-U** words (QI, QAT, QAID, QOPH, TRANQ…).
4. **High-probability bingo stems:** 6-letter combinations like
   SATIRE, RETAIN (+1 letter → many 7-letter words) and SATINE.
   A **bingo** (all 7 tiles) earns a 50-point bonus.
5. **Hooks:** letters that extend a word at the front or back (e.g. S
   hooks on most nouns; "E" hooks on many words).
Tools: Zyzzyva (word study/flashcards) and Quackle (analysis — simulates
plays) are widely used free programs. Study with spaced repetition and
anagram drills.

## 3. Rack management (leave)
- Your **leave** (tiles kept after a play) matters as much as the score.
  Good leaves: balanced, with letters like E, R, S, T, N, A, I, and a
  blank or S. Bad leaves: duplicates (II, UU), Q without U, too many
  vowels or consonants.
- Aim for roughly **3-4 consonants and 3-4 vowels** after drawing.
- Sometimes score less to keep a strong leave; exchange when the rack is
  hopeless and the bag has plenty of tiles (exchanges are typically only
  allowed with at least 7 tiles in the bag).
- Don't burn an S or blank for a few extra points; save them for bingos
  or big plays (roughly an S is worth ~8 points of extra score, a blank
  ~25-30 in expert valuations — rough figures).

## 4. Scoring on the board
- **Premium squares:** triple-word, double-word, triple- and double-letter;
  put high-value tiles on letter premiums in **both directions** (a tile
  on a double/triple-letter square counted in two words).
- **Parallel plays** that form several two-letter words score well.
- **Hooks** let you land bingos and reach triple-word lanes.

## 5. Board strategy and defence
- Avoid opening triple-word squares for your opponent unless you're
  likely to use them first or you're behind and need volatility.
- Ahead: close the board (block bingo lanes). Behind: open it.
- **Tile tracking:** cross off played tiles on a tracking sheet (allowed
  in clubs/tournaments) — it tells you what's left, crucial near the end.

## 6. Endgame
- When the bag is empty you know your opponent's rack exactly (from
  tracking). Plan the **sequence** of plays: go out in two moves while
  blocking their best play, and avoid getting stuck with a high tile.
- Going out first gives you the value of the opponent's remaining tiles
  (×2 in many rule sets: you gain their tile sum and they lose it).

## 7. Challenges
Under "double challenge" (common in NWL play) a failed challenge costs the
challenger their turn; under Collins tournament rules a failed challenge
often costs 5 points instead. Know your event's rule before bluffing a
phoney.

## Gotchas
- Playing for score alone and keeping terrible leaves.
- Opening a triple-triple lane when behind on board position.
- Untracked endgame tiles.

## Verify
- [ ] You know all two-letter words in your lexicon.
- [ ] Your leaves after most plays are balanced.
- [ ] You tracked tiles and knew the opponent's final rack.
- [ ] You know your event's dictionary and challenge rule.
