# Bridge — bidding, declarer play and defence

**Goal:** reach sensible contracts with your partner, make them as
declarer by planning before playing to trick one, and defend with a
partnership signalling language. Examples assume a natural 5-card-major
system (Standard American Yellow Card / 2/1 style); Acol and other
systems differ in details — agree your system and **fill in a convention
card**.

## 1. Hand evaluation
- **High-card points (HCP):** A=4, K=3, Q=2, J=1 (40 in the deck).
- Add length/shape points sensibly (e.g. +1 for each card beyond four in
  a long suit) — methods vary.
- Rough game targets: about **25-26 combined points** for 3NT or four of
  a major, ~29 for five of a minor, ~33 for a small slam, ~37 for a grand
  slam.

## 2. A bidding skeleton (5-card majors, 15-17 1NT)
- Open **1NT** with 15-17 HCP and a balanced hand.
- Otherwise open **1♥/1♠** with 5+ cards in the suit and ~12-21 HCP;
  else open your longer minor.
- **2♣** opening for very strong hands (~22+).
- Responding: 6+ points to respond; new suit at the 1-level is forcing;
  raise partner's major with 3+ support.
- **Conventions worth learning first:** **Stayman** (2♣ over 1NT asks for
  a 4-card major), **Jacoby transfers** (2♦/2♥ over 1NT show hearts/
  spades), **Blackwood** or RKC (4NT asks for aces/key cards before a
  slam), takeout doubles.
- At duplicate, **alert** partner's artificial bids per the regulations;
  opponents are entitled to know your agreements.

## 3. Declarer play: plan before trick one
When dummy appears, stop and plan:
- **No-trump:** count **sure winners**; if short, find where extra tricks
  come from (long suits, finesses); usually **hold up** the ace of the
  suit led to cut the opponents' communication.
- **Suit contracts:** count **losers** from declarer's view; decide how to
  eliminate them — **ruffing** losers in the short hand, discarding on
  a long suit, finessing.
- **Draw trumps** early unless you need trumps for ruffing first.
- **Finesse:** lead *toward* the honour you hope wins (e.g. lead from
  the hand opposite the AQ; if the king is on your left, the queen wins).
  A simple finesse succeeds about 50% of the time.
- **Entries:** keep ways to reach the hand with winners; unblock high
  cards from the short hand first.

## 4. Defence
- **Opening leads (common agreements):** top of a sequence (K from KQJ),
  fourth-best from a long suit against NT, partner's bid suit, avoid
  leading away from unsupported kings/aces against suit contracts.
- **Signals:** **attitude** (high = encourage, low = discourage, or
  reverse in "upside-down" methods), **count** (high-low = even number),
  **suit preference**. Agree which with partner.
- **Second hand low, third hand high.**
- Count declarer's points and distribution from the bidding and play —
  by mid-hand you can often place every key card.

## 5. Duplicate scoring
- **Matchpoints (pairs):** every overtrick counts; prefer the higher-
  scoring game (NT over a major if equal tricks), take safe overtricks.
- **IMPs (teams):** make your contract first; safety plays matter;
  bid vulnerable games more aggressively.
- Play online (BBO, Funbridge) to get many deals reviewed against the field.

## Gotchas
- Playing to trick one before planning — the most common declarer error.
- Undiscussed bids at the table (is it forcing?) — write agreements down.
- Forgetting to alert.

## Verify
- [ ] You and partner have a filled-in convention card.
- [ ] As declarer you counted winners (NT) or losers (suit) before trick one.
- [ ] You can name your partnership's lead and signalling agreements.
- [ ] You adjust tactics to matchpoints vs IMPs.
