# Competitive diving — hurdle, takeoff, rotation and rip entry

**Goal:** build dives on a sound takeoff and a clean, vertical, splash-free
("rip") entry, and understand the dive numbering and scoring that decide a list.
Learn new dives only with a coach, in water of certified depth, with dryland
(trampoline/dry board with spotting belt) progressions first.

## 1. Boards and platforms
- **Springboard:** 1 m and 3 m. The **fulcrum** (a wheel under the board) moves
  the pivot: back = more spring but slower; forward = stiffer, quicker. Set it
  the same every time and note your setting.
- **Platform:** 5, 7.5 and 10 m (competition is mainly 10 m). No spring — height
  comes from your jump; armstand dives are platform-only.

## 2. Forward approach and hurdle (springboard)
1. Walk 3+ steps to the end of the board, controlled and the same every time.
2. **Hurdle:** drive one knee up, arms swing up, land on both feet at the end
   of the board with the body vertical.
3. **Ride the board:** press down with the board, arms circling, then extend up
   as it recoils. Takeoff mostly *up*, with lean only as needed for the dive.
Fault: leaning forward in the hurdle → dive travels out and short of rotation.

## 3. Back takeoff
Stand backwards on the end, heels over the edge, arms swing; press the board
with the legs and lift through the arms. Keep the head neutral at takeoff;
throwing it back early costs height and lines.

## 4. Dive groups and numbers
| Group | Number | Faces | Rotates |
|---|---|---|---|
| Forward | 1xx | water | forward |
| Back | 2xx | board | backward |
| Reverse | 3xx | water | toward the board |
| Inward | 4xx | board | toward the board |
| Twisting | 5xxx | any of the above + twist | |
| Armstand (platform) | 6xx | | |
Example: **105B** = forward group, 2½ somersaults (the last digit counts half
somersaults: 5 = 2½), position B (pike). Positions: **A** straight, **B** pike,
**C** tuck, **D** free (twisting).

## 5. Rotation and come-out
- Tuck is fastest rotation, pike slower, straight slowest — that's why tariffs differ.
- **Spot** the water (or a fixed point) to know where you are.
- **Come-out:** open (kick out of the tuck/pike) at the right moment to stop
  rotation and line up for the entry. Too early = short, too late = over.
- Reverse and inward dives bring the body back toward the board — the classic
  fear dive. Distance from the board must be safe; judges also mark it.

## 6. Rip entry
- Arms straight overhead, hands flat, one hand grabbing the other
  (**flat-hand grab**), palms hitting the water first to punch a hole.
- Body **vertical** and tight, toes pointed; after entry, keep going and "save"
  underwater (pike or roll) to pull the legs through without splash.
- Short or long entries throw water — the commonest score-killer.

## 7. Scoring (overview)
Judges (typically 5 or 7) score 0–10 in half points on approach, takeoff, flight
and entry. The highest and lowest scores are dropped (more in larger panels);
the remaining sum × **degree of difficulty (DD)** = dive score. A safe dive done
beautifully can beat a harder dive with a poor entry. Synchro adds execution and
synchronisation judges. *DD tables and panel rules come from World Aquatics;
check the current edition.*

## Verify
- [ ] Fulcrum setting recorded and repeated each session.
- [ ] Hurdle lands vertical at the board end; dive goes up, not out.
- [ ] You can decode a dive number (e.g. 205C, 5132D).
- [ ] Rip entries: vertical, flat-hand grab, small splash on simple dives.
- [ ] New dives learned on dryland/trampoline with a spotter first.
