# Stand-up paddleboarding — stance, stroke and touring safety

**Goal:** paddle a SUP efficiently and safely — and avoid the classic SUP
incident: being blown offshore on an inflatable, without a leash, unable to
paddle back.

## 1. Board and paddle
- **Board types:** all-round (wide, stable, ~10–11 ft), touring (longer, pointed
  nose, tracks straight), race (long, narrow), surf (short, manoeuvrable).
  Beginners: wide all-round with plenty of volume for their weight.
- **Inflatables (iSUP):** pump to the maker's pressure (commonly ~15 PSI or as
  stated). Under-inflated boards flex and wobble. Check the valve is closed.
- **Paddle length:** for all-round use, roughly your height + 15–25 cm (shorter
  for surf, longer for touring/race) — adjustable paddles make this easy.
- **Blade angle:** the blade bends **forward** (away from you, toward the nose).
  Holding it the other way round is the most common beginner mistake.
- **Leash:** coiled leash for flatwater/touring, straight leash for surf.
  On rivers use a **quick-release waist leash** only — an ankle leash can snag
  and pin you underwater in current.
- PFD/buoyancy aid; required as a vessel in some jurisdictions.

## 2. Getting up
1. Kneel in the middle of the board (at the carry handle), paddle across the board.
2. One foot then the other to standing, feet **parallel**, hip-width, either side
   of the handle. Knees soft, look at the horizon, not your feet.
3. Start in flat water, knee-deep or deeper (falling onto the fin in shallow
   water hurts).

## 3. The forward stroke
- **Reach:** top hand on the T-grip, bottom hand about shoulder-width down.
  Hinge at the hips and reach forward.
- **Catch:** bury the whole blade vertically before pulling.
- **Power:** pull the board past the paddle using the core and hips, not the arms;
  the shaft stays near vertical (top hand over the water).
- **Exit** at the feet, not behind them — strokes behind the feet steer more than they propel.
- Switch sides every few strokes, or keep one side and "J" slightly to track straight.

## 4. Turning
- **Sweep stroke:** wide arc from nose to tail on one side, board turns the other way.
- **Reverse sweep:** from tail to nose, for a quick pivot.
- **Cross-bow:** reach across the nose without switching hands.
- **Pivot / step-back turn:** step back into a surf stance near the tail, nose
  lifts, sweep — very fast turn; used in SUP surf and buoy turns in racing.

## 5. Falling and remounting
Fall to the side, away from the board, flat (not onto the board or the shallow
bottom). Remount: from the side at the handle, grab the far rail, kick and slide
on to your stomach, then kneel, then stand.

## 6. Wind and touring safety
- **Offshore wind** (blowing from land out to sea) is the big danger: it's calm
  near the shore and strong further out. Check a wind forecast; avoid offshore
  wind days, or stay very close in.
- Paddle **upwind first** on the outbound leg so you return downwind.
- In strong wind, kneel or lie down and paddle like a kayak/prone — smaller sail area.
- Tell someone your plan; phone in a waterproof case; whistle; know water
  temperature (cold-water shock after a fall).
- Respect boat traffic and swim areas; give way to larger, less manoeuvrable craft.

## 7. Racing and SUP surf (pointers)
Racing: buoy turns via step-back pivot, drafting behind another board's tail.
SUP surf: smaller board, surf stance, the same lineup etiquette as surfing (see
sports-recreation.water-sports-and-aquatics.surfing.wave-selection-pop-up-and-lineup-etiquette)
— a paddle gives you an advantage; don't hog waves.

## Verify
- [ ] iSUP inflated to spec; paddle sized; blade bends toward the nose.
- [ ] Correct leash type for the water (quick-release waist leash on rivers).
- [ ] Strokes exit at the feet; you can track straight 100 m.
- [ ] You can remount from deep water.
- [ ] Wind direction checked; outbound leg goes upwind.

*Draft: inflation and paddle-length figures are typical; follow the board maker.*
