# Rowing — stroke sequence, rigging and erg pacing

**Goal:** move the boat (or flywheel) with a connected, repeatable stroke,
set the boat up to fit the rower, and pace erg tests and races instead of
dying at 1,000 m. Sculling = two oars each; sweep = one oar each.

## 1. The stroke sequence
1. **Catch:** compressed at the front, shins near vertical, arms long, body
   leaning forward from the hips. Blade drops in squared, quickly, *before*
   the legs push.
2. **Drive:** **legs → body → arms**. Legs push first while the back holds
   its angle; then the body swings back; then the arms draw in. The handle
   moves level.
3. **Finish:** handle to the lower ribs (sweep) or just below the chest
   (sculling), blade tapped down and out cleanly, feathered.
4. **Recovery:** reverse order: **arms → body → legs**. Hands away, body over,
   then slide forward slowly.
- **Ratio:** recovery slower than the drive (roughly 2:1 at low rates). Rushing
  the slide stops the boat (rower's mass moving toward the stern checks it).
- Common faults: "shooting the slide" (legs push but handle doesn't move —
  back not connected), opening the back early, arms bending at the catch.
- **Crab:** blade caught in the water at the finish, the handle hits the rower;
  square/feather cleanly and keep handle heights level.

## 2. Crew timing
- Follow **stroke** seat; blades in together and out together. Watch the
  shoulders/back in front, not your own blade.
- Coxswain/steers calls: rate changes, pushes, "weigh enough" (stop).
- In sweep, pressure balance between sides keeps the boat running straight.

## 3. Rigging basics
- **Span** (sculling, pin to pin) or **spread** (sweep, pin to centreline)
  together with **oar length** and **inboard** sets the **gearing** — heavier
  gearing feels harder per stroke. Crews set this per boat class and athlete.
- **Footstretcher** position: at the finish the handle should clear the body;
  at the catch the shins near vertical. Move it rather than changing technique.
- **Height** of the gate above the seat: hands clear the knees on the recovery.
- **Pitch** of the blade (a few degrees) is usually factory/rigger-set.
Record settings; change one thing at a time.

## 4. The erg (rowing machine)
- Same sequence: legs-body-arms, arms-body-legs.
- **Damper vs drag factor:** the damper lever sets airflow; the **drag factor**
  (shown in the monitor's menu on Concept2-type ergs) is what actually matters
  for consistency. Many rowers train around 110–140; heavier damper is not
  "harder training", it just changes the gearing.
- **Split** = time per 500 m. **Rate** = strokes per minute (spm). Steady state
  often at 18–22 spm; race pace much higher.
- Steady-state ("UT2") pieces should allow conversation; most volume goes here.

## 5. Pacing a 2k test or race
- Typical plan: a fast start (first ~250 m a little quicker than average),
  settle to target split by 500 m, hold through the third 500 (the hard part),
  build in the last 500 and sprint the last 250.
- Pick the **target average split** from recent pieces; the most common error is
  a too-fast first 500 leading to a collapse.
- Warm up properly (~15–20 min with a few bursts). Hydrate; sit down after —
  lightheadedness is common.

## 6. On the water: safety
Know the local circulation pattern (which side of the river to row), launch and
land into the stream, check weather and river levels; capsize drill before
single sculling; never row alone in cold, high water.

## Verify
- [ ] Filmed: legs-body-arms on drive, arms-body-legs on recovery.
- [ ] Ratio slow on the slide at low rate.
- [ ] Footstretcher set so finish and catch positions are correct.
- [ ] Erg drag factor checked and kept consistent.
- [ ] 2k plan written with target splits per 500 m.
