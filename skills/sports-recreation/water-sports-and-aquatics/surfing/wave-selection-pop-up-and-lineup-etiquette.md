# Surfing — wave selection, pop-up and lineup etiquette

**Goal:** catch and ride green (unbroken) waves safely, and fit into a
lineup without dropping in on anyone. The biggest beginner errors are the
wrong board and the wrong beach on the wrong day.

## 1. Board and kit
- **Learn on volume:** a soft-top 8–9 ft board floats, paddles fast and is
  forgiving. Moving to a shortboard too early is the most common reason
  people plateau. Volume (litres) relative to body weight is the key number;
  beginners want a lot more than experienced surfers.
- **Leash** on the back ankle; check the cord and rail saver. Wax the deck
  (base coat + temperature-specific top coat) or use a traction pad.
- Wetsuit thickness to the water temperature (e.g. 3/2 mm temperate,
  5/4 mm with boots/hood in cold water).

## 2. Reading the forecast
- **Swell height and period:** longer period (12 s+) = more powerful,
  better-organised waves; short period (<8 s) = weak, choppy wind swell.
  Height at a long period breaks much bigger than the number suggests.
- **Swell direction** vs how the beach faces: determines which spots work.
- **Wind:** **offshore** (land to sea) grooms waves clean; onshore makes them
  crumbly; light or no wind in the early morning is often best.
- **Tide:** many breaks only work at certain tides; reefs can get dangerously
  shallow at low.
- **Break type:** beach breaks (sand, shifting peaks — best to learn), point
  breaks (long, peeling waves along a headland), reef breaks (shallow, powerful —
  not for beginners).

## 3. Before paddling out
Watch for 10 minutes: where the waves break, where surfers paddle out, sets
(bigger waves arrive in groups). **Rip currents:** darker, flatter channels with
no breaking waves and foam/debris moving seaward. Caught in one: don't fight
it head-on; paddle parallel to the beach out of it, or float and signal.
Surf patrolled beaches between the flags where lifeguards show.

## 4. Paddling and getting out
- Lie centred (nose just above the water), long strokes, chest slightly up.
- Getting through broken waves: **duck dive** (shortboards — push the nose
  under, knee on the tail) or **turtle roll** (longboards — flip over holding
  the rails). Never throw your board — someone may be behind you.

## 5. Catching and the pop-up
1. Position: sit where the waves start breaking (the **peak**); turn toward
   the beach early as a wave approaches.
2. Paddle hard and keep paddling 2–3 strokes after you feel the wave take you.
3. **Pop-up:** hands under the chest, push up, bring the back foot then the front
   foot under you in one movement; land low, knees bent, feet across the
   stringer, front foot about midway. Practise on land 50 times.
4. Look where you want to go — down the line, not at your feet.

## 6. First manoeuvres
- **Angling/trimming:** ride along the open face (the **shoulder**), not straight.
- **Bottom turn:** at the bottom of the wave, compress, lean on the rail and
  drive back up the face — the turn that sets up everything else.
- **Cutback:** turn back toward the breaking part (the pocket) when you get too
  far out on the shoulder. Then top turns/snaps.

## 7. Lineup etiquette
- **Priority:** the surfer closest to the peak (breaking part) has the wave.
- **Never drop in** — don't take off in front of someone already riding.
- **Don't snake** (paddle around someone to get inside position).
- Paddle out wide of the riding zone; if someone is riding toward you, go
  toward the white water behind them, not into their path.
- Wait your turn; locals and rotation matter; beginners stay off crowded,
  advanced peaks.
- Control your board — the leash is not a licence to bail it.

## Verify
- [ ] Board has enough volume to paddle easily and catch waves.
- [ ] Forecast checked: period, direction, wind and tide suit the spot.
- [ ] You can identify a rip before entering.
- [ ] Pop-up is one motion, landing in a balanced stance.
- [ ] You can state the priority rule and have never dropped in.
