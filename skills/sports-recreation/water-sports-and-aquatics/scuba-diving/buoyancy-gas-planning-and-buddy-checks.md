# Scuba diving — buoyancy, gas planning and buddy checks

**Goal:** dive within no-decompression limits with enough gas to get you and
your buddy to the surface from anywhere in the dive, while hovering motionless
and not touching the reef. Assumes entry-level certification (PADI/SSI/BSAC or
similar); this does not replace training.

## 1. Buddy pre-dive check
Agencies use mnemonics (e.g. PADI's **BWRAF** — "Begin With Review And Friend"):
- **B**CD: inflates/deflates; dump valves work.
- **W**eights: right amount, secured, quick release known.
- **R**eleases: straps, buckles, cylinder band tight.
- **A**ir: valve fully open; breathe from both second stages while watching the
  gauge (needle shouldn't drop); full pressure; check buddy's alternate air source.
- **F**inal OK: mask, fins, computer on, plan agreed.
Agree: max depth/time, turn pressure, hand signals, lost-buddy procedure
(search ~1 minute, then surface and reunite).

## 2. Weighting and buoyancy
- **Weight check** at the surface: full gear, BCD empty, normal breath, you
  float at eye level and sink slowly on exhale. Re-check at the end of the dive
  with ~50 bar/500 psi (an aluminium cylinder becomes buoyant as it empties).
- Overweighting is the most common fault — it forces more air in the BCD,
  bad trim and harder buoyancy control.
- **Neutral buoyancy:** add small bursts to the BCD as you descend (wetsuit
  compresses), vent as you ascend. Fine control is with the **lungs**.
- **Trim:** horizontal in the water; weights placed to avoid feet-down.
  **Frog kick** keeps silt down near the bottom.

## 3. Gas planning
- **SAC/RMV:** your surface air consumption. From a logged dive:
  litres used ÷ minutes ÷ absolute pressure at average depth (bar = depth/10 + 1).
- **Consumption at depth** = SAC × ambient pressure.
- **Rule of thirds** (common for overhead/drift; conservative): one third out,
  one third back, one third reserve.
- **Rock bottom / minimum gas:** gas needed for *two* stressed divers (use
  ~2–3× normal SAC) to ascend from the deepest point including a safety stop.
  Turn the dive at or before that pressure plus margin.
- Typical recreational guidance: start the ascent with ~50–70 bar left.

## 4. No-decompression limits and ascents
- Each depth has an **NDL** — max bottom time without mandatory decompression
  stops. Your dive computer tracks it in real time; tables (e.g. recreational
  dive planner) for planning (see sports-recreation.water-sports-and-aquatics.dive-computers).
- **Ascent rate:** slow — commonly **≤ 9–10 m/min** (some agencies say 18 m/min
  max; slower is safer).
- **Safety stop:** 3 minutes at 5 m (15 ft) on every dive.
- **Avoid:** reverse profiles, yo-yo diving, deep dives late in a multi-dive
  day, flying within ~12–24 h after diving (follow DAN/agency guidance).

## 5. Nitrox (EANx)
Higher oxygen = longer NDLs but an oxygen-limited **maximum operating depth**:
MOD (m) = 10 × (ppO2 limit ÷ FO2 − 1). At ppO2 1.4 with EAN32 → about 33.8 m.
Analyse your own cylinder, label it, set the computer to the mix. Requires a
nitrox course.

## 6. Problems and drills
- **Equalise early and often** on descent (pinch-and-blow gently, before you feel
  pain). Never force; ascend a little and retry.
- **Out-of-air:** signal, take buddy's alternate (octopus) or donated primary,
  controlled ascent together. Practise every season.
- **Uncontrolled ascent:** vent BCD, flare body, exhale.
- **Decompression sickness** symptoms (joint pain, numbness, fatigue, skin
  rash, confusion) after a dive → 100 % oxygen, call emergency services / DAN
  hotline (see health-medicine.occupational-and-environmental-medicine.undersea-and-hyperbaric-medicine.decompression-illness-and-dive-fitness).

## Verify
- [ ] Buddy check done every dive; lost-buddy plan agreed.
- [ ] Weight check passes at the end of the dive.
- [ ] You can hover motionless for 1 minute.
- [ ] SAC calculated from your logs; turn pressure planned from rock bottom.
- [ ] Ascent ≤ 9–10 m/min with a 3-min safety stop; nitrox MOD known.
