# Swimming — stroke technique, turns and interval sets

**Goal:** swim faster for the same effort by fixing drag first (body position),
then propulsion (catch), then train with structured interval sets instead of
"just doing lengths".

## 1. Body position and streamline — drag first
- **Head neutral:** look at the bottom slightly ahead, not forward. Lifting the
  head sinks the hips and legs.
- **Long, horizontal line:** press the chest gently down, engage the core; legs
  near the surface. A kick that starts from the hip, not the knee.
- **Streamline** off every wall: arms overhead, one hand on the other, biceps
  squeezing the ears, tight core, pointed toes. It is free speed.

## 2. Freestyle (front crawl)
- **Entry:** hand enters in front of the shoulder (not crossing the centre line),
  extends forward.
- **Catch:** bend the elbow and tip the fingertips down so the forearm faces
  backwards early (**early vertical forearm**, EVF) — then push water back,
  not down.
- **Rotation:** roll the body ~30–45° to each side with the stroke; breathe
  with the rotation, one goggle in the water. **Bilateral breathing** (every 3)
  balances the stroke; many distance swimmers breathe every 2 in races.
- **Exhale** continuously underwater so you only need to inhale on the breath.
- Drills: catch-up, single-arm, fingertip drag, 6-kick switch.

## 3. The other strokes — the one fault that matters most
| Stroke | Common fault | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Backstroke | sitting (hips down), head lifted | ears in the water, push hips up; rotate shoulders |
| Breaststroke | kicking and pulling together | "pull, breathe, kick, glide" — timing |
| Butterfly | arms only, no undulation | kick twice per stroke, press chest, breathe low and early |
Breaststroke and fly require two-hand simultaneous touches at the wall; the
stroke rules (World Aquatics) define legal kicks.

## 4. Turns and starts
- **Flip (tumble) turn (free/back):** keep speed into the wall, last stroke
  ends with the hands at the hips, tuck chin to chest, somersault, feet plant
  high on the wall, push off on the back or side, rotate to the front during
  the streamline. Exhale through the nose while rotating.
- **Open turn (breast/fly):** two-hand touch, elbow back, knees tucked, one
  arm over, push off on the side.
- **Underwater dolphin kick:** in competition free/back/fly you must surface
  by **15 m** after a start or turn. Breaststroke allows one pull-out cycle.
- **Starts:** track start (one foot forward), enter through "one hole".
  Shallow pools: never dive without training and adequate depth.

## 5. Interval training
- Use the **pace clock**. A set like `10 × 100 free @ 1:45` means swim 100 m,
  leave every 1:45 whatever the swim time; the remainder is rest.
- **CSS (critical swim speed)** test: time a 400 and a 200 (after a warm-up).
  CSS pace per 100 m ≈ (T400 − T200) ÷ 2. Threshold sets are swum at about
  CSS pace with short rests (e.g. 10–20 s).
- A session: warm-up (300–600 easy + drills) → main set → kick/pull → cool-down.
- Track total weekly volume; increase gradually; shoulder pain often signals
  crossing over at entry or too much paddles work.

## 6. Open water
- **Sighting:** lift the eyes just above the water ("crocodile eyes") every
  6–10 strokes, then turn to breathe.
- **Drafting** behind another swimmer's feet or at their hip saves energy.
- Wetsuit buoyancy changes body position; practise in it before races.
- Safety: tow float, bright cap, swim with others, know water temperature and
  currents; cold-water shock is a real risk.

## Verify
- [ ] Filmed side-on: head neutral, hips near the surface.
- [ ] You exhale underwater and can breathe both sides.
- [ ] Flip turns without stopping; you surface before 15 m.
- [ ] CSS tested and used to set the main-set pace.
- [ ] Open water: sighting every 6–10 strokes with minimal stroke loss.
