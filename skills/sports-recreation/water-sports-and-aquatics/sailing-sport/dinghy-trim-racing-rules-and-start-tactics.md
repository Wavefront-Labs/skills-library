# Sailing (sport) — dinghy trim, racing rules and start tactics

**Goal:** sail a dinghy or small keelboat fast and in control, race without
fouling people, and win starts and shifts. For passage-making sail trim and
reefing see transportation-logistics.sailing-boating-transport.sailing-vessels.sail-trim-and-reefing-on-passage.

## 1. Points of sail
- **No-go zone:** about 40–45° either side of the wind — you can't sail there.
- **Close-hauled** (beat) → **close reach** → **beam reach** (wind across) →
  **broad reach** → **run** (wind behind).
- **Tack:** turn the bow through the wind. **Gybe:** turn the stern through the
  wind — the boom crosses fast; in a dinghy, centre the mainsheet, call it,
  duck, and steer gently out of it to avoid a death roll or capsize.

## 2. Trim by the telltales
- Sails in until the luff stops flapping, no further. **Telltales** (wool strips
  near the luff): both streaming = right. Windward one lifting → sheet in or bear
  away. Leeward one stalling/lifting → ease or head up.
- Main leech telltales should stream most of the time upwind.
- **Controls:** **kicker/vang** controls the boom rising (twist); **cunningham**
  and **outhaul** flatten the sail as the wind rises; flatter sails for more wind
  and flat water, fuller for light wind and chop.
- **Centreboard:** fully down upwind, part up on reaches, mostly up downwind.

## 3. Heel and balance (dinghy)
- Sail upwind flat (dinghies) — heel causes weather helm, slows and slips. Hike
  out, ease the main in gusts ("ease, hike, steer").
- Fore-aft: weight forward in light air, back when planing.
- **Capsize recovery:** stay with the boat, uncleat sheets, stand on the
  centreboard, pull on it to right the boat; the crew is scooped in. Turtle
  (fully inverted) → pull the board out first. Practise in a warm, supervised session.

## 4. The core Racing Rules of Sailing (RRS, World Sailing)
- **Port/starboard (rule 10):** on opposite tacks, **port tack keeps clear of
  starboard** (starboard = wind over the boat's starboard/right side).
- **Windward/leeward (rule 11):** same tack and overlapped — **windward keeps
  clear**.
- **Clear astern (rule 12):** boat behind keeps clear.
- **Tacking (rule 13):** while tacking, keep clear of others.
- **Mark-room (rule 18):** at a mark, overlapped boats inside get room when the
  first reaches the **zone** (typically three hull lengths).
- **Avoid contact (rule 14)** even with right of way.
- **Penalty:** usually a **two-turns penalty** (one tack + one gybe per turn)
  when you foul; one turn for touching a mark. Check sailing instructions.
- *Rules revised every four years — read the current RRS edition.*

## 5. The start
- **Sequence** (common): warning signal at **5 min**, preparatory at **4**,
  one-minute at **1**, start at **0** (flags plus sound; the flags are
  authoritative, not the sound).
- **Line bias:** head to wind in the middle of the line, or sail along it; the
  end that's further **upwind** is favoured. Starboard end often favoured for
  right-of-way too.
- Hold position with sails flapping, keep a gap to leeward to accelerate into,
  hit the line at full speed at the gun. Over early → return and restart (rule 30
  flags such as I, Z, U, black flag change the penalty).
- Know the **first shift** and the favoured side of the course before the start.

## 6. Upwind tactics
- **Lifts and headers:** a **header** (wind shifts toward the bow, forcing you to
  bear away) is a cue to tack in oscillating breeze; a **lift** you keep.
- Stay between the fleet and the next mark; don't overstand the **layline**.
- Pressure (darker ripples on the water) is often worth more than a small shift.
