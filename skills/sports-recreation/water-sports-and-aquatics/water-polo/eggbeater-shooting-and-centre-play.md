# Water polo — eggbeater, shooting and centre play

**Goal:** stay high in the water without touching the bottom, handle the ball
with one hand, shoot past a keeper and understand the fouls and the centre
battle that decide most possessions. Teams are 6 field players + goalkeeper.

## 1. Eggbeater — the base skill
- Sit upright as if on a chair, knees wide, legs alternating circular kicks
  (one clockwise, one anticlockwise) so one leg is always pushing down.
- Drills: hands out of the water for 30 s, then elbows out, then holding a
  weight; "eggbeater walks" sideways. Height out of the water on the shot and
  block comes from this.
- **Heads-up freestyle:** swim with the head up to see the ball and
  players; dribble the ball between the arms on a counterattack.

## 2. Ball handling and passing
- Pick up the ball by scooping under it or pressing it down and lifting —
  one hand only (field players may not use two hands; only the keeper may, inside
  their area).
- **Dry pass:** to a team-mate's hand, out of the water — fastest, preferred.
  **Wet pass:** into the water in front of a team-mate, used to drive.
- Elbow high, rotate the torso, follow through; use the eggbeater to rise.

## 3. Shots
| Shot | When |
|---|---|
| Power shot | from outside, keeper set — corners |
| Skip shot | bounce on the water in front of the keeper — hard to judge |
| Lob | over a keeper who has come out or is low |
| Backhand / sweep | centre-forward with back to goal |
| Push / tip shot | on the drive close in |
**Fake (pump)** to make the keeper commit, then shoot. Aim low corners against
tall keepers, high corners against keepers who sit deep.

## 4. Fouls — the rhythm of the game
- **Ordinary foul:** free throw to the other team, taken without delay — e.g.
  impeding a player, or pushing off an opponent. Where the line sits between
  ordinary and exclusion foul is a matter of the referee's interpretation.
- **Exclusion (major) foul:** player sent out for about **20 s** (or until a goal
  or change of possession) → **6-on-5** power play (man-up).
- **Penalty:** foul preventing a probable goal inside the 5 m area → shot from
  the 5 m line.
- Three personal major fouls = out for the game.
- Shot clock (typically **30 s**, reset rules for second chances) — check your
  competition's current rules; World Aquatics has revised these several times.

## 5. Centre-forward vs centre-back (the "hole")
- **Centre-forward (hole set):** posts up at 2–3 m in front of goal, back to the
  defender, battles for position, takes the entry pass and either shoots
  (sweep/backhand) or draws an exclusion. Big legs, strong hands.
- **Centre-back (hole D):** fronts or plays behind the centre-forward; denies
  the entry pass; avoids the exclusion foul (which is what the attack wants).
- Perimeter players feed the centre, drive, and shoot from outside.

## 6. Systems
- **Press (man-to-man)** defence: tight on every attacker; risky against drivers.
- **Zone / M-drop:** perimeter defenders drop back to crowd the centre,
  inviting outside shots the keeper can see.
- **6-on-5 sets:** commonly a 4-2 (four players near the 2 m line, two out at
  about 5 m) or a 3-3 — move the ball fast; five defenders can't cover every angle.
- **Counterattack:** the moment possession turns, sprint heads-up; a
  numerical advantage (2-on-1) is the cleanest goal.

## 7. Goalkeeping (pointers)
Eggbeater high, hands up and wide, square to the shooter, read the shooter's
arm and hips, stay big on fakes.
