# Artistic swimming — sculling, figures and routine construction

**Goal:** develop the support skills (sculling, eggbeater) that every movement
relies on, perform positions and figures with height and control, and
understand how routines are put together and judged. Events: solo, duet
(including mixed), team, plus acrobatic routines.

## 1. Sculling — the engine
Small figure-of-eight hand movements with flat, angled palms: sweeping out
(palms out and slightly down) and in (palms in), forearms doing the work.
| Scull | Position | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Flat / standard | on back, hands by hips | holding a back layout |
| Support scull | upside-down, forearms horizontal by the head/chest | holding verticals, ballet legs |
| Torpedo | hands overhead | travelling head-first |
| Reverse torpedo | hands overhead | travelling feet-first |
| Canoe | front layout, hands under hips | front positions |
Drill: hold each scull for 30–60 s without leg movement, then with one leg
lifted. Weak sculling = sinking positions = lost marks.

## 2. Eggbeater and boosts
Eggbeater (alternating circular leg kicks) supports arm movements above water
(as in water polo, more vertical and higher). **Boosts:** explosive
eggbeater/dolphin action to lift the torso high out of the water — judged on height.

## 3. Positions and figures
- **Basic positions:** back layout, front layout, ballet leg (one leg vertical
  from back layout), vertical (fully inverted, legs out of the water),
  crane, fishtail, knight, split, tuck, pike.
- **Figures** are defined transitions between positions (e.g. **ballet leg**,
  **barracuda** — a fast thrust from back pike to vertical — **flamingo**). Age-group
  competitions test figures individually.
- Quality markers: **height** (how much of the legs is out of the water),
  **stability** (no wobble or travelling), **control and extension** (pointed
  feet, straight knees, exact angles).

## 4. Lifts and acrobatics (team)
Several swimmers form a base underwater (eggbeater/scull) and lift or throw a
"flyer". Types: platform (flyer stands on a base), stack, and throws/jumps.
These need careful build-up out of the water and with a coach — collisions and
falls on to a base are the main injury source.

## 5. Routine construction
- Choreograph to music with **counts**; swimmers hear the music underwater via
  speakers. Every movement is set to the count, which is how sync works.
- **Elements** in a routine: hybrids (leg sequences with the body inverted),
  acrobatic movements, transitions, and in technical routines some
  required elements.
- **Scoring (overview of the current approach):** teams **declare the difficulty**
  of each element in advance; judges score **execution** of each element and
  **artistic impression**, while a technical controller checks the declared
  elements were performed — undeclared or failed ones are penalised or reduce
  the score. World Aquatics changed the scoring system substantially from 2023;
  *read the current judging handbook for exact components and base marks.*
- Keep within time limits (including deck work allowance).

## 6. Practicalities
Nose clip (and spare tucked in the costume), goggles for training only, hair
set with gelatine for competition, stage make-up waterproof. Breath-hold work
is significant — train it progressively and with supervision; never
hyperventilate before underwater sections (shallow-water blackout risk, see
sports-recreation.water-sports-and-aquatics.freediving).

## Verify
- [ ] You can hold flat and support sculls 60 s each.
- [ ] Vertical held still for 5+ s with height to the knees or higher.
- [ ] Each figure in your test performed with control at the listed angles.
- [ ] Routine counted to the music and synced on video.
- [ ] Difficulty declaration and elements checked against the current rules.
