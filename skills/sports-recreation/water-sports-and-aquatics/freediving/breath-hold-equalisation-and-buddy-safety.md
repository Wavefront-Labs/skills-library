# Freediving — breath-hold, equalisation and buddy safety

**Goal:** dive deeper and longer on one breath by relaxing, equalising
efficiently and diving with a trained buddy — because **blackout happens
without warning** and drowning follows when no one is watching. Learn with a
certified course (AIDA, Molchanovs, PADI Freediver, SSI, CMAS).

## 1. The rule above all
**Never freedive alone. One up, one down.** Your buddy watches you from the
surface for the entire dive, meets you in the last ~10 m of the ascent (the
blackout zone), and watches you for at least **30 seconds** after you surface.
In the pool: never practise breath-holds alone, including in the bath.
Spearfishers are over-represented in fatalities because they dive solo.

## 2. The breathe-up
- Slow, relaxed breathing for ~2 minutes: inhale ~3–5 s, exhale ~6–10 s,
  belly first. Lower heart rate, quiet mind.
- **No hyperventilation.** Fast, deep breathing lowers CO2 — the urge to
  breathe is delayed but oxygen doesn't rise meaningfully, so you can run out
  of oxygen before you feel you must breathe → **shallow-water blackout**.
- Final breath: full inhale (belly, chest, shoulders) without strain.

## 3. Recovery breathing
On surfacing: grip a float or the edge, **exhale passively a little, then
inhale sharply** and hold briefly — "hook breathing" (a few cycles) re-pressurises
and pushes oxygen in. Signal OK to your buddy (hand sign + verbal "I'm OK").
**Samba / LMC** (loss of motor control — shaking, confused) can follow; the buddy
supports the airway and talks the diver through it.

## 4. Equalisation
- **Valsalva** (pinch nose, push with chest/diaphragm) works shallow but is
  inefficient and tiring at depth.
- **Frenzel:** close the glottis, use the tongue as a piston to push air from the
  mouth into the ears. Learn on land: pinch your nose, say "kuh" or "tuh" with
  the throat closed — cheeks puff.
- **Mouthfill** for deeper dives (advanced, taught in courses).
- Equalise **early and often**, every metre or so near the surface. Never force:
  if it won't go, stop, ascend a bit, retry. Masks need equalising too (exhale
  into the mask through the nose).

## 5. Diving technique
- **Duck dive:** bend at the hips, lift the legs vertical so their weight
  pushes you down, then fin. Head neutral.
- **Freefall:** below the depth where you become negatively buoyant (with
  correct weighting, roughly 10–15 m for many divers) stop finning and sink —
  saves oxygen.
- **Weighting:** neutral at about 10 m (one-third of target depth is another
  rule used), so you're positive near the surface for a safer ascent.
- Turn calmly at the bottom (or the lanyard target), streamline ascent, no
  rush, look up only near the surface.

## 6. Training
- **CO2 tables** (fixed hold, shrinking rest) build tolerance to the urge.
  **O2 tables** (increasing hold, fixed rest) build hypoxia tolerance — only
  ever dry (on land, lying down) or supervised in the pool.
- Dynamic apnea (horizontal distance) in the pool with a buddy walking alongside.
- Depth in **open water** on a line with a lanyard and a buoy; buddy on the
  surface at the line.

## 7. Red flags — end the session
Ear or sinus pain, blood in mask/mouth (squeeze), any LMC, cold, fatigue,
unusual anxiety. Don't fly or freedive straight after scuba (residual nitrogen).

## Verify
- [ ] Buddy present and trained; one-up-one-down observed; 30-s surface watch.
- [ ] Breathe-up is slow — no hyperventilation.
- [ ] Recovery breathing used on every surfacing, with OK signal.
- [ ] Frenzel works on land and in shallow water.
- [ ] Weighted for neutral around 10 m; depth increased gradually.
