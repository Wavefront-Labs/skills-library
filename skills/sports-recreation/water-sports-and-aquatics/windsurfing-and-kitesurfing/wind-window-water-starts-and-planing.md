# Windsurfing and kitesurfing — wind window, water starts and planing

**Goal:** learn wind-powered board sports without becoming a rescue statistic:
understand the wind, control the power, get up on the board and get back to
where you started. **Kitesurfing must be learned with a certified school**
(IKO or national equivalent) — a kite can drag or lift a person in seconds.

## 1. Wind first
- **Direction relative to the shore:** **cross-shore** or cross-onshore is safest
  (you drift back to the beach). **Offshore** blows you out to sea — beginners don't go.
  Direct onshore makes launching and getting out hard.
- **Strength and gusts:** check a forecast and live station; gusty, shifting
  wind (e.g. behind buildings or hills) is harder and riskier than strong steady wind.
- Pick kite/sail size for wind strength and body weight; when in doubt, smaller.
- Check hazards downwind: rocks, piers, swimmers, power lines (kites + lines conduct).

## 2. Kitesurfing: wind window and control
- The **wind window** is the area downwind of you where the kite can fly.
  The **edge** of the window (left/right, and overhead at **zenith**) is the
  **neutral zone** — little pull. The middle downwind (**power zone**) pulls hardest.
- Steer the bar like a bicycle handle: pull left = kite goes left.
- **Depower:** push the bar away to reduce power. Sheet in = more power.
- **Safety systems (know them before flying):** the **quick release** on the
  chicken loop (pull/push to release, depending on brand), the **safety leash**
  that keeps the kite attached to one line so it flags out, and how to reset.
- **Self-rescue** in deep water (pack down the lines, use the kite as a sail) is
  part of every proper course.

## 3. Kitesurfing: getting up
Body drag (no board) downwind and upwind → **water start**: board on the feet,
kite at ~12 o'clock, dive the kite smoothly through the power zone, stand up as it
pulls, then park it at ~10/2 o'clock and ride. Edge the board's heel side to go
across the wind; too much kite movement → bounced downwind.

## 4. Windsurfing: getting up
1. **Uphaul:** on a big, stable board, stand across the board, pull the sail up
   with the uphaul rope, mast hand then sail hand on the boom.
2. **Steering:** tilt the rig back toward the tail to head up into the wind,
   forward toward the nose to bear away.
3. **Beach start:** in waist-deep water, rig on the windward side, step on with
   the back foot and let the sail lift you on.
4. **Water start** (with smaller boards/stronger wind): lift the rig clear of the
   water, let the wind fill it and pull you onto the board.

## 5. Harness and planing (both sports)
- **Harness:** hook in so your body weight, not your arms, holds the pull. Arms
  get pumped otherwise.
- **Planing:** the board rises onto the surface and accelerates. Windsurf:
  bear off slightly, sheet in, move feet back into the **footstraps** once
  planing. Kite: drive the kite once for speed, then edge.
- **Gusts:** depower (kite bar out; windsurf sheet out or rake back) — don't
  hold on harder.

## 6. Staying upwind
Drifting downwind is the beginner's main problem. Edge harder against the
board (kite) or point higher (windsurf), keep the kite lower in the window and
moving less. Plan turns (tacks/gybes) so you come back upwind of your start.

## 7. Right of way and conduct
- On starboard tack (wind on your right side) you generally have right of way
  (as in sailing). Overtaking rider keeps clear.
- Kitesurfing: upwind rider keeps the kite high, downwind rider keeps it low
  when passing. Never launch near people; launch with a helper and signals.
- Wing foiling uses a hand-held wing and hydrofoil board — foils are sharp;
  helmet and impact vest recommended.

## Verify
- [ ] Wind direction is cross- or cross-onshore and within your range.
- [ ] Kite: you can activate the quick release and self-rescue (school-taught).
- [ ] You can name the power zone and neutral zone in the wind window.
- [ ] You can start (water start or uphaul) and return to your starting point.
- [ ] You can state the starboard right-of-way rule.
