# Kayaking and canoeing — strokes, rolling and reading whitewater

**Goal:** paddle efficiently in a straight line, turn on demand, recover from a
capsize, and read moving water well enough to avoid its real killers
(strainers, hydraulics, cold). Flatwater first, then moving water with a club or
guide.

## 1. Kit
- **PFD/buoyancy aid** always worn and zipped. Helmet on whitewater. Spray deck
  (skirt) on closed-deck kayaks, with the **grab loop outside**.
- **Paddle length:** kayak — wider boats and recreational paddling need longer
  (roughly 220–240 cm), low-angle touring longer, whitewater shorter (~190–200).
  Canoe — standing, the grip at about chin/nose height, or sized seated.
- Dress for the **water** temperature, not the air: cold-water shock and swim
  failure kill paddlers who capsize in cold water.
- Throw bag, whistle, knife on the PFD for moving water.

## 2. Strokes
- **Forward stroke:** power from **torso rotation**, not the arms. Plant the blade
  fully near the feet, rotate the torso to pull it back to the hip, exit. Arms
  relatively straight; push with the top hand at eye height for a high angle.
- **Sweep stroke** (turning): wide arc from bow to stern on one side.
- **Draw stroke:** move the boat sideways; reach out and pull water toward the hull.
- **Reverse and stop strokes** on both sides.
- **Canoe J-stroke:** solo or stern paddler finishes the forward stroke by
  turning the blade (thumb down) and pushing out — corrects the turn away from
  the paddling side. Tandem: bow powers, stern steers.

## 3. Bracing and capsize recovery
- **Low brace:** blade flat on the water, knuckles down, slap and hip-snap to
  recover balance. **High brace** (knuckles up) for bigger tips — keep elbows
  low and in to protect shoulders.
- **Wet exit:** lean forward, pull the spray-deck grab loop, push out with the
  hands on the cockpit rim, somersault out. Practise before anything else.
- **Roll:** hip snap (knee lifts the boat while the head stays down) is the
  key; C-to-C or sweep roll technique learned in a pool with a coach.
- **Assisted rescues:** bow rescue (capsized paddler grabs a rescuer's bow and
  hip-snaps up), T-rescue to empty and re-enter a sea kayak, paddle-float self-rescue.

## 4. River grades
International scale: **Class/Grade I** (easy moving water) → **II** (simple rapids,
clear channels) → **III** (irregular waves, manoeuvring needed) → **IV** (difficult,
scout first) → **V** (very difficult, serious consequences) → **VI** (rarely run).
Grades change with water level — a II at low water may be a IV in flood.

## 5. Reading moving water
- **Downstream V (tongue):** smooth V pointing downstream = the main channel.
  Upstream V points at a rock.
- **Eddy:** calmer water behind an obstacle flowing upstream — your rest spots.
  The **eddy line** between current and eddy is where boats flip.
- **Eddy turns and peel-outs:** enter/exit at an angle with speed, **lean
  downstream** (into the turn, showing your hull to the current) — leaning
  upstream flips you.
- **Ferry glide:** cross the river by angling the bow upstream into the current
  and paddling — angle and speed set how fast you cross.

## 6. Hazards that kill
- **Strainers:** fallen trees and debris that let water through but not people.
  Avoid at all costs; if swept toward one, swim aggressively over it head-first.
- **Hydraulics / stoppers:** recirculating water below drops and especially **weirs
  (low-head dams)** — uniform, man-made ones are near-impossible to escape.
- **Sieves/undercut rocks.** **Foot entrapment:** never stand up in fast water
  deeper than the knees; swim on your back, feet downstream at the surface.
- Scout from the bank when in doubt; portage when still in doubt.

## Verify
- [ ] PFD worn; dressed for water temperature; spray-deck loop out.
- [ ] Forward stroke uses rotation; you can paddle straight without ruddering.
- [ ] Wet exit done; you can perform/receive an assisted rescue.
- [ ] On moving water you can eddy out and ferry with a downstream lean.
- [ ] You can spot a strainer and a weir and plan around them.

*Draft: paddle-length ranges are rough; get fitted by a shop or coach.*
