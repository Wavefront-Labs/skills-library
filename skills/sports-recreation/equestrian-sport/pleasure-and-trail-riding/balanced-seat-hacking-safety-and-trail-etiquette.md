# Pleasure and trail riding: balanced seat, hacking safety and etiquette

**Goal:** enjoy riding out in control — a secure seat, a horse that listens, and the
habits that prevent most hacking accidents. Lessons with a qualified instructor are the
foundation; this is the practice guide.

## 1. Balanced seat
- **Alignment:** ear–shoulder–hip–heel in a vertical line; sit on both seat bones
  evenly; heels lower than toes, stirrups under the balls of the feet.
- **Hands:** a straight line from elbow through the hand to the horse's mouth;
  thumbs on top; soft, following hands.
- **Rising trot:** rise and sit with the rhythm; on circles and turns, sit when the
  horse's outside foreleg is back (the "diagonal") — "rise and fall with the leg on the wall".
- **Look where you're going**, not down at the horse.

## 2. Aids
Leg to go forward; seat and hand to slow; inside rein to flex, outside rein to control
speed and bend; outside leg to hold the quarters. Ask lightly, then firmer, then release
the moment the horse responds — **the release is the reward**.

## 3. Before you leave the yard
- **Tack check:** girth tight (re-check after a few minutes' walking), bridle buckles
  secure, stirrup leathers equal and safety stirrups or bars down so a foot can come free.
- **Rider kit:** hat to a current safety standard fastened, boots with a heel, gloves,
  **hi-vis** for horse and rider, body protector if you prefer, charged phone *on you*
  (not on the saddle), and tell someone your route and return time.
- **Horse check:** sound, shoes on, no sore spots; ride with a calm companion horse if
  the horse is young or nervous.

## 4. Spooks and emergencies
- **Spook:** sit deep, heels down, look where you want to go, keep a contact and leg
  on; let the horse look at the object from a distance, then ride past calmly.
  Don't punish fear.
- **Bolting/losing control:** **one-rein stop** — shorten one rein and bring the horse's
  nose around toward your knee in a gradually smaller circle, sitting back, the other
  rein loose. Practise at walk in an arena so it's reflexive. (Don't do it abruptly at
  speed on slippery ground — the horse can fall; disengage gradually.)
- **Fall:** stay still, check yourself before moving; let the horse go rather than
  being dragged. Carry an emergency contact card.

## 5. Road riding (rules vary — check your local road code)
Ride on the correct side of the road in single file on narrow roads; thank drivers;
use clear hand signals; avoid roads in poor light; ride in hi-vis even in daylight.
In the UK, the Highway Code and the BHS road-safety guidance are the references.

## 6. Trail etiquette
- Use only routes where horses are permitted (bridleways, byways and equivalent).
- **Pass walkers and cyclists at a walk**, giving them room; call a greeting so the
  horse knows the "strange shape" is a person.
- Leave gates as you found them; don't canter past other horses or near livestock.
- Avoid churning soft paths in wet weather; pick up droppings on paved areas if required.

## 7. Building confidence
Start with short, familiar routes with a steady companion; extend gradually; ride
within your comfort zone ("stretch, don't strain"); lessons in an arena build the
skills that make hacking feel safe.

## Common faults
Gripping with the knees, looking down, heavy hands, loose girth, no hi-vis, cantering
in groups without agreeing first.
