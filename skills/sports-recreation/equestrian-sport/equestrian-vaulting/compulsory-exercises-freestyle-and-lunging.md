# Equestrian vaulting: compulsories, freestyle and lunging

**Goal:** understand how vaulting is trained and judged — gymnastics and dance on a horse
cantering on a circle, controlled by a lunger — and the progression that keeps
vaulters and horses safe. Learn only at a club with a qualified trainer and a schooled
vaulting horse.

## 1. Setup
- The horse canters (lower levels may walk) on a **circle of at least ~15 m diameter**
  (FEI), on the **left rein** by convention, controlled by the **lunger** standing in
  the centre with lunge line and whip.
- The horse wears a **vaulting surcingle** (roller with two handles and foot loops),
  a thick back pad, and side reins.
- Vaulters wear close-fitting clothing and soft-soled gymnastics shoes.

## 2. The barrel first
Every exercise is learned on the **vaulting barrel** (a static or mechanical
horse-shaped barrel), then at walk on the horse, then at canter. This is non-negotiable —
it protects the horse's back from mistakes and the vaulter from falls.

## 3. Compulsory exercises (FEI-style list; varies by level and year)
- **Mount** — run alongside, swing the right leg up while the hands pull on the
  handles, landing in the seat.
- **Basic seat** — sitting upright, arms out to the sides at about eye height, legs long.
- **Flag** — kneeling on one knee, the other leg extended back high, opposite arm forward.
- **Mill** — four leg passes around the horse's neck and croup to complete a full circle
  in the seat, 4 canter strides per phase.
- **Scissors** (forward and backward) — swing up into a handstand-like position and cross
  the legs to turn around.
- **Stand** — standing on the horse's back, arms out, knees soft.
- **Flank** — swing up to a side handstand position, landing sitting backward/sideways.
- **Dismount** — vault off to the inside, landing on the feet facing forward.
Each is judged on form, mechanics, balance and harmony with the horse; held exercises
need a minimum number of strides (e.g. four). Check the current FEI vaulting guidelines
for the exact list at your level.

## 4. Freestyle
A choreographed routine to music within a time limit: individual, **pas-de-deux** (two
vaulters) or **squad/team** (several vaulters, up to three on the horse at once).
Judged on technique (difficulty, execution) and artistic merit (composition,
choreography, interpretation of music). Build it around safe, well-practised moves;
difficulty without balance costs more than it earns.

## 5. The lunger and horse score
- The lunger keeps the horse in a steady rhythm and correct circle, calm and forward.
- The **horse score** (gaits, rhythm, frame, behaviour, lungeing) is a significant part
  of the total in competition.
- Horse welfare: vaulting horses need strong backs, steady temperaments, regular
  fitness work outside vaulting, and limited vaulting time per day. Rotate vaulters;
  give walk breaks.

## 6. Safety
- Fall training (rolling) on mats.
- A spotter/trainer present at all times; only one new skill at a time.
- Arena surface deep and soft; no vaulting in slippery conditions.
- Vaulters never land heavily on the horse's back — "land light" is a constant cue.

## Common faults
Collapsing posture in basic seat, landing heavily, rushing mill leg passes, choreography
beyond the vaulter's balance, lunger letting the circle drift.

## Verify
- [ ] Each compulsory performed cleanly on the barrel before the horse.
- [ ] Held exercises maintained for the required strides at walk, then canter.
- [ ] Freestyle timed to the music and within the limit.
- [ ] Horse's weekly vaulting load and fitness work logged.
