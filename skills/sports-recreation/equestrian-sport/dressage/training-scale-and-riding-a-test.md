# Dressage: the training scale and riding a test

**Goal:** build the horse's way of going in the classical order and convert that into
marks — accurate figures, prepared transitions and a clear picture for the judge.
Work with an instructor; a horse's welfare and soundness come before any movement.

## 1. The training scale (in order, each built on the previous)
1. **Rhythm** — regular, even beats in each gait (walk 4, trot 2, canter 3) at a
   consistent tempo.
2. **Suppleness (looseness)** — the horse swings through the back, relaxed, mouth
   soft, stretching willingly forward-down when allowed.
3. **Contact** — a steady, elastic connection from the rider's hand to the bit, the
   horse seeking it, not leaning or avoiding (behind the vertical).
4. **Impulsion** — controlled thrust from the hindquarters through a swinging back.
5. **Straightness** — hind feet follow the front feet on lines and circles; both sides
   equally supple.
6. **Collection** — more weight on the hindquarters, shorter, more elevated strides —
   only after the above.
When something goes wrong, go back down the scale. Most problems are rhythm or
suppleness problems in disguise.

## 2. Half-halts and transitions
- **Half-halt:** a brief coordinated aid — seat and leg ask the hind leg forward while
  the outside hand momentarily closes — to rebalance and get attention. It's a
  "conversation", not a pull.
- **Transitions** (between and within gaits) are the main gymnastic tool: frequent,
  well-prepared transitions engage the hindquarters. Prepare with a half-halt a few
  strides before the marker.

## 3. Lateral work (progression)
- **Leg-yield** (lower levels): horse moves forward and sideways, slight flexion away
  from the direction of travel; body straight.
- **Shoulder-in:** forehand brought in so the horse moves on three tracks, bent around
  the inside leg — builds straightness and engagement.
- **Travers / renvers, half-pass** at higher levels: bent in the direction of travel.
Check rhythm stays the same in every lateral movement — losing it costs marks.

## 4. Riding the test
- Learn the arena: standard **20 × 40 m** (lower levels) and **20 × 60 m** with
  letters (A K V E S H C M R B P F; centre line D L X I G). Memorise the order.
- **Accuracy:** transitions **at the letter** — when the rider's body/shoulder is level
  with it. Circles are round, the right size, touching the correct points; diagonals
  hit the letters; centre lines straight.
- **Corners:** ride them as quarter-circles appropriate to the level, preparing for the next movement.
- Ride with a caller (if permitted) until memorised; know the "error of course"
  penalty — rules differ by federation.
- Each movement scored 0–10; some have **coefficients** (×2). **Collective marks**
  (gaits, impulsion, submission, rider position/effectiveness of aids — the collective
  set changed recently in some federations) are added. Final score is a percentage.

## 5. Warm-up
Stretch and swing first (long rein walk, rising trot on circles), then contact and
transitions, then a brief rehearsal of key movements. Avoid over-drilling — arrive
with a fresh, relaxed horse.

## 6. Welfare and rules
Tack and equipment rules (bits, nosebands, spurs, whips) are strict — check your
federation's rulebook (FEI, British Dressage, USDF/USEF, etc.). Over-tight nosebands and
forced head positions (hyperflexion) are under increasing scrutiny and may be penalised.

## Common faults
Riding front to back (hand holding the head), losing rhythm in lateral work,
transitions late or early, corners cut, rider tipping forward, test ridden from memory with no preparation.
