# Polo: swing technique, ride-offs and the line of the ball

**Goal:** hit the ball reliably at speed, know who has right of way (the rule that keeps
polo safe), and play your position. Learn at a polo school — on a **wooden horse** first,
then on schooled ponies.

## 1. Seat and grip
- Polo is ridden in a **two-point seat** — standing in the stirrups, knees gripping,
  weight in the heels, the horse controlled one-handed (left hand) neck-reining.
- Mallet in the right hand (polo is played **right-handed only** under the rules of most
  associations), thumb wrapped around the grip, the loop around the wrist.

## 2. The four basic shots
- **Off-side forehand:** ball on the right of the pony. Rotate the shoulders back,
  mallet raised high and vertical, swing through like a pendulum from the shoulder,
  strike the ball just ahead of or level with the stirrup, follow through toward the target.
- **Off-side backhand:** sends the ball backward on the right; swing from in front.
- **Near-side forehand / backhand:** ball on the left — mallet crosses over the pony's
  neck; turn the shoulders fully.
- Key cues: eyes on the ball, swing from the shoulder not the wrist, lean out from the
  hips (not collapsing over the pony), keep the pony's line steady.
Also: under-the-neck and tail shots (more advanced).
Practise on a wooden horse in a cage — far more repetitions than on a live horse.

## 3. Line of the ball and right of way
The **line of the ball** is the path the ball is travelling (or would travel). The player
following the line most closely, with the ball on their right (off side), has the
**right of way**. Others must not cross that line in front of them in a way that risks
a collision — "crossing" is the most important foul. Summarised principle; full rules
are detailed (check HPA/USPA/FIP rulebooks).

## 4. Ride-offs and hooking
- **Ride-off:** two riders move alongside each other, shoulder-to-shoulder (rider's
  shoulder against rider's shoulder, ponies' shoulders level), pushing the opponent off
  the line. Angle of approach must be shallow (dangerous angles are fouls).
- **Hook:** using your mallet to block an opponent's swing, only when you're on the same
  side as the ball and the swing; never above shoulder height, never across the pony.
- **Dangerous riding** — zig-zagging in front, sharp angles, bumping at speed — is penalised.

## 5. Positions
- **No. 1:** forward attacker, marks the opponents' No. 4 (back), scores goals.
- **No. 2:** aggressive attacker/worker, ties up the opposing No. 3.
- **No. 3:** playmaker, usually the strongest player; long hitter, transitions defence
  to attack.
- **No. 4 (Back):** defender, protects the goal, backhands to turn play.
Players have **handicaps** (e.g. −2 to +10 goals outdoors; check your association's scale);
team handicap sums determine levels and handicap-based starting goals.

## 6. Ponies and chukkas
- A match is typically 4–6 **chukkas** of ~7 minutes (7½ in some rules); ponies tire,
  so players change ponies between chukkas (a pony typically plays at most two chukkas
  per match with rest between — check welfare rules).
- Ponies need conditioning, schooling to neck-reining and stopping, and protective
  bandages/boots and tail tied up.
- Arena polo uses a smaller enclosed arena and an inflatable ball; different rules.

## Safety
Helmet to polo standard (often with faceguard), knee guards, boots, gloves; eye
protection recommended. Never cross the line; always call your intentions.

## Common faults
Swinging with the wrist, looking up before contact, sitting down in the saddle, chasing
the ball across the line, ride-offs at dangerous angles.

## Verify
- [ ] Four basic shots executed on the wooden horse with consistent contact.
- [ ] Can explain the line of the ball and right of way to a coach's satisfaction.
- [ ] Ride-off practised at walk/trot with a partner.
- [ ] Kit meets the club's safety requirements; pony string plan respects rest rules.
