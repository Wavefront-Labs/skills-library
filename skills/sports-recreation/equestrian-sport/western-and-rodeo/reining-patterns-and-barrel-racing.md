# Western: reining patterns and barrel racing

**Goal:** understand what reining judges reward and how to ride its manoeuvres, and run
a barrel pattern efficiently. Cutting and roping are covered only in passing.

## 1. Neck-reining and seat basics
Western horses are ridden on a **loose rein**, one hand (in most classes), steered by
the rein against the neck plus seat and leg. Sit deep, legs long, weight in the heels;
cues should be quiet — heavy hands on a curb bit are severe.

## 2. Reining manoeuvres
- **Circles:** large fast and small slow circles; judges want correct lead, clear
  speed changes, round shape, horse guided willingly.
- **Lead changes:** flying changes at the centre of the arena, exactly on the line,
  simultaneous front and back.
- **Spins (turnarounds):** 360° turns on a stationary inside hind pivot foot, front
  legs crossing; counted precisely (e.g. "four spins right"). Over- or under-spinning
  is penalised.
- **Rundown and sliding stop:** run straight along the long side, building speed;
  stop cue (seat drops, "whoa", light rein) — the horse plants the hind feet and slides
  with the front legs walking. Requires sliding plates (shoes) and suitable ground.
- **Rollback:** after the stop, a 180° turn over the hocks and immediate lope off.
- **Back up:** straight and willing.

## 3. Scoring (NRHA-style)
Each run starts at **70**; each manoeuvre gets a score from −1½ to +1½ in half-point
increments; **penalties** are deducted (e.g. ½ point for a slight over/under-spin or
starting a circle at a trot; 1 point for being out of lead; 5 points for
spurring in front of the cinch or using the free hand to instil fear; a **0 score**
for wrong pattern, equipment violations, abuse). Check the current NRHA handbook — the
penalty list is long and specific.
Memorise the pattern number posted (NRHA has a set of standard patterns).

## 4. Barrel racing
**Cloverleaf pattern:** three barrels in a triangle. Start through the timer line, go
around the first barrel (right or left — both directions legal), cross to the second
(turned the opposite way), then the third (same direction as the second), and run home
through the timer. Fastest time wins.
- **Penalty:** usually 5 seconds per knocked-over barrel; going off-pattern = no time.
- **Technique:**
  - Approach the barrel on a "pocket" — a line that starts a few feet wide of the barrel,
    so the horse can turn around it close without hitting it.
  - Rate (slow) before the turn with seat and a half-halt; sit deep; inside hand guides;
    outside leg holds the hip; eyes on the next barrel.
  - Push out of the turn hard toward the next pocket.
- **Training:** pattern work at walk and trot most days, full-speed runs sparingly to
  preserve the horse's mind and legs. Horses that "anticipate" turns get drilled
  slowly or on alternate patterns.

## 5. Other events (pointers)
- **Cutting:** separating a cow from the herd and letting the horse hold it; the rider
  puts the hand down and lets the horse work. Judged on a 60–80 scale (NCHA).
- **Roping (tie-down, team roping):** timed; requires roping skill and a rate-trained
  horse; specific rodeo association rules (PRCA etc.).

## 6. Welfare and kit
Saddle fit and pad, cinch tight before fast work, protective boots (splint/bell),
sliding plates only for reining with suitable ground. Rodeo welfare rules vary —
follow the association's.

## Common faults
Pulling on a curb for stops, over-spinning (losing count), circles drifting off-centre,
riding barrels too close (knocks), running barrels at full speed every session.
