# Track cycling: fixed-gear handling, starts and event tactics

**Goal:** get through track accreditation, ride the banking confidently, and understand
the tactics of the main events. Velodromes run mandatory induction sessions — do them
before anything here.

## 1. Fixed gear handling
- **No freewheel and no brakes.** The pedals turn whenever the wheel turns. Stop
  pedalling = you get thrown. Slow down by resisting the pedals gradually.
- Getting on: start holding the rail or with a holder; clip in, push off, pedal
  immediately.
- Getting off: slow by resisting, ride high-to-low near the exit, grab the rail
  when nearly stopped (follow the track's procedure).
- Keep pedalling through the bankings; never stop pedalling or freewheel by habit.
- **Speed on the banking:** the steeper the banking, the more speed you need to stay up
  (around 25 km/h or more is commonly quoted for typical 250 m indoor tracks — your
  coach will specify). Too slow on the banking and the tyres slide down.

## 2. Track markings and etiquette
- **Côte d'Azur** (the flat apron at the bottom) — not for riding in races.
- **Black line** (measurement line, ~20 cm above the inside edge) — distance measured here.
- **Red sprinter's line** — the riders in the sprinter's lane (between black and red)
  may not be passed on the inside (rules vary by event).
- **Blue stayers' line** — above it is for overtaking/recovery.
- **Look over your shoulder before moving up or down** — a rider coming from behind has
  the right of way; overtake on the right (outside), never undercut.
- Ride up the banking to slow, not down.

## 3. Standing starts
Holder (or a start gate) holds the bike; crank at ~2 o'clock for the dominant foot.
Explode out of the saddle, keeping the bike straight with strong arm pull, stay out of
the saddle for the first several revolutions, then sit as cadence builds. Practise
under a coach — crooked starts are common.

## 4. Event tactics (summary)
- **Match sprint:** two or three riders over ~3 laps (250 m tracks); tactical cat-and-mouse
  early — slow riding, track stands, positioning high on the banking. The leader
  watches the rival; the follower wants the jump from behind and the draft. Jump from
  high on the banking to use gravity.
- **Keirin:** riders paced by a derny/e-bike that leaves with ~3 laps to go; position
  behind the derny matters.
- **Pursuit (individual/team):** start on opposite sides; even pacing like a TT; in the team
  pursuit, riders rotate turns at the front by swinging up the banking and dropping
  back on the last wheel.
- **Points race / scratch / elimination / omnium:** bunch races — positioning, reading
  the field, gaining laps.
- **Madison:** pairs; the active rider hand-slings the incoming partner forward.
  Approach from behind, take the partner's hand and sling firmly forward-and-left; the
  relieved rider then rides up the banking to rest above the stayers' line. Practise
  slings at low speed with coaching — collisions are common in madisons.

## 5. Gearing
Gears are expressed as chainring/sprocket (e.g. 50×15) or as **gear inches**.
Endurance riders typically run smaller gears with higher cadence than sprinters.
Beginners and accreditation sessions use modest gears; youth categories have gear
restrictions — check the governing body's rules.

## 6. Equipment
Track bike with tight chain tension, lock-ring on the rear cog, no brakes; tyres
inflated per track/manufacturer guidance; clipless pedals; helmet compulsory. Check
wheel nuts and chainring bolts before every session.

## Common faults
Stopping pedalling (thrown), not looking before changing line, riding too slowly on the
banking, undercutting on the inside, gear too big for accreditation.

## Verify
- [ ] Track induction/accreditation completed.
- [ ] Can mount, ride and dismount without stopping pedalling abruptly.
- [ ] Shoulder check performed before every line change.
- [ ] Bike checked: chain tension, wheel nuts, lockring.
- [ ] Event rules and gear restrictions read for your category.
