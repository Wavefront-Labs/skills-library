# Mountain biking: cornering, drops and line choice

**Goal:** ride technical trails in control — body position that lets the bike move,
cornering that holds grip, drops you can land, and lines that keep momentum.
Progress on graded trails one step at a time; skills courses accelerate this.

## 1. Attack (ready) position
Out of the saddle (dropper post down), pedals level, knees and elbows bent and out,
hips back and low over the bottom bracket, chest low, eyes up looking well ahead,
one or two fingers covering the brakes. The bike moves under you; your body stays calm.

## 2. Braking
- **Brake before features and corners, not in them.** Braking in a corner or on a
  root/rock reduces grip and blows suspension.
- Front brake gives most stopping power; modulate, don't grab. Rear alone skids.
- On steep descents, weight back and low, but keep weight on the front tyre for steering.

## 3. Cornering
- **Look through the corner** to the exit — the bike goes where you look.
- **Flat corners:** brake before, enter wide, apex late, exit wide. Lean the bike more
  than your body (press the bike down with the inside hand), outside foot down
  (heavy pedal), weight through the tyres.
- **Berms:** keep pedals level, lean with the berm's angle, push into it to generate
  speed; don't brake on the berm.
- Late apex keeps you on line for the next feature.

## 4. Drops
- **Roll-down** (slow, steep but rideable): weight back, let the front wheel roll down,
  arms extend, then absorb.
- **Drop off** (gap to landing): approach with enough speed (too slow → front wheel
  drops first and you go over the bars). At the lip, push the bike forward slightly
  (a gentle "manual" motion lifting the front) so both wheels land together or rear
  slightly first. Absorb with knees and elbows.
- Start with small drops (~30 cm) onto flat or downslope landings; landing to flat is
  harsher.
- Never attempt drops or jumps you haven't inspected on foot first.

## 5. Line choice
- Scan 2–3 features ahead; the smooth line isn't always fastest — the line that keeps
  momentum and sets up the next corner is.
- On rock gardens, pick the line, commit, and stay off the brakes; hesitation stops
  the wheel against obstacles.
- Roots: cross perpendicular where possible; wet roots are ice — don't brake or lean on them.
- Technical climbing: stay seated for rear traction, lean forward to keep the front down,
  smooth power (spin, don't mash), choose a gear before the climb.

## 6. Setup checks
- **Tyre pressure:** lower than road — often around 18–28 psi depending on rider
  weight, tyre width, tubeless or not, and terrain. Too high = poor grip; too low =
  rim strikes and burps. Adjust 1–2 psi at a time.
- **Suspension sag:** set per the manufacturer (often ~20–30% depending on fork/shock
  and discipline); rebound slow enough to avoid bucking, fast enough not to pack down.
- Dropper post working; brakes bled with good pads; chain lubed.

## 7. Trail etiquette and safety
Ride open trails only; yield as signed (commonly bikes yield to walkers and horses);
don't skid (erosion); ride wet-sensitive trails only in dry conditions. Helmet always
(full face for downhill/bike parks), gloves, knee pads. Carry a repair kit, water and a phone.

## Common faults
Sitting down on descents, staring at the obstacle, braking mid-corner, locked arms,
too much tyre pressure, rolling into drops too slowly.
