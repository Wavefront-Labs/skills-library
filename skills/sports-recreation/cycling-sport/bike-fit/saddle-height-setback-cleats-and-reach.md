# Bike fit: saddle height, setback, cleats and reach

**Goal:** a comfortable, efficient position that you can hold for hours, set up in a
logical order with small measured changes. A professional fit is worth it for
persistent pain or aero optimisation; this is the self-fit baseline.

## 0. Before you start
- Bike on a level trainer, front wheel levelled, in your usual shoes and shorts.
- Record **every current measurement** first (saddle height centre-of-BB to top of
  saddle along the seat tube, setback, saddle tilt, bar reach/drop, cleat positions).
  Photograph them. You must be able to go back.
- Change **one thing at a time**, in steps of ~2–5 mm, then ride a few times before
  judging.

## 1. Cleats (contact point first)
- **Fore-aft:** ball of the foot (1st metatarsal head) roughly over or slightly ahead of
  the pedal spindle; many fitters now set cleats slightly further back for comfort and
  stability. Numb toes/hot foot often improve by moving the cleat back.
- **Rotation:** match your natural foot angle — watch how your feet sit when you sit on
  a table and let your legs hang, or ride with float and see where the heel settles.
  Forcing feet straight causes knee pain.
- **Lateral (stance width):** knees tracking roughly vertically over the feet when
  viewed from the front; adjust cleat sideways or use pedal spacers.

## 2. Saddle height
Methods (use one, verify with another):
- **Knee angle at bottom of the stroke:** about **25–35° knee flexion** (measured with a
  goniometer or video at the 6 o'clock position, from straight). 
- **Heel method:** heel on pedal at the bottom, leg just straight; then riding on the
  ball of the foot gives roughly the right bend. Quick sanity check.
- **Inseam × 0.883** (LeMond formula) BB-to-saddle-top — a starting point only.
Signs: **hips rocking** side to side or toes pointing down at the bottom → too high
(back-of-knee pain). **Front-of-knee pain**, feeling cramped → too low.

## 3. Setback (saddle fore-aft)
- Classic start: **KOPS** (knee over pedal spindle) — with cranks horizontal, a plumb
  line from just below the kneecap falls near the pedal axle. It's a starting point,
  not a law.
- Better test: you should be able to hold your torso briefly with hands lightly off
  the bars at moderate power without sliding forward.
- Moving the saddle back effectively raises it slightly — re-check height.
- **Saddle tilt:** level or nose very slightly down. Too nose-down → sliding forward and
  hand pressure; nose-up → soft-tissue pressure.

## 4. Reach and drop
- Set **after** saddle position — never move the saddle to fix reach.
- In the hoods, elbows slightly bent, shoulders relaxed, back flat-ish; your view of
  the front hub is often obscured by the bars (rough check only).
- Change reach with stem length (10 mm steps) and bar choice; drop with spacers/stem
  angle.
- Numb hands, sore neck and shoulders → too long or too low. Cramped breathing and
  knees hitting the torso → too short/low.
- Handlebar width ≈ shoulder width (acromion to acromion) as a starting point;
  narrower is a modern aero trend.

## 5. Aero position (TT/triathlon)
Lower and longer, pad stack/reach set so the upper arm is ~vertical; saddle often more
forward (rules limit setback for UCI events). Sustainable > extreme: test power and
comfort for the event duration.

## 6. Common problem → likely cause
| Problem | Check |
|---|---|
| Front knee pain | Saddle low/forward, cleats too far forward |
| Back knee pain | Saddle high |
| Lateral knee pain | Cleat rotation/stance width |
| Numb toes | Cleat too far forward, tight shoes |
| Saddle sores/numbness | Saddle tilt, wrong saddle width, too high |
| Neck/hands | Reach too long, drop too big |

## Verify
- [ ] Original measurements recorded before changes.
- [ ] Knee angle at bottom of stroke measured (25–35°) on video.
- [ ] No hip rocking at steady moderate power.
- [ ] Can ride an hour in the hoods without numb hands or neck pain.
- [ ] Changes logged with dates and how they felt over several rides.
