# Road racing: bunch riding, breakaways and time-trial pacing

**Goal:** stay safe and efficient in the bunch, spend energy only where it buys
position, and pace solo efforts so you don't blow up.

## 1. Bunch skills
- **Hold your line:** ride predictably; no sudden swerves or braking. Look ahead
  through riders, not at the wheel in front.
- **Gap to the wheel:** 30–60 cm and slightly offset so a touched wheel doesn't send
  you down. **Your front wheel must never overlap theirs** — overlapping is how crashes start.
- **Feather brakes** gently rather than braking hard; soft-pedal to lose speed.
- Elbows relaxed: bumping shoulders happens; stay loose and steady.
- **Point out hazards** (holes, gravel, parked cars) with hand signals or calls.
- **Drafting** saves a large share of effort (commonly cited as up to ~30% at race
  speeds behind one rider, more deeper in the bunch). Stay sheltered in the front third
  — safer than the back (accordion effect) and in position for moves.

## 2. Crosswinds and echelons
In a crosswind the shelter is diagonally behind, downwind. Riders form an **echelon**
stretched across the road. The road width limits how many fit — those left in the
"gutter" get no shelter and are dropped. When a crosswind section approaches, move up
early. Forming one: rotate in a tight two-line paceline across the road.

## 3. Breakaways — when they work
More likely to stick when:
- The bunch is disorganised (no team chasing) or teams with interests are represented
  in the break.
- The break has several strong riders willing to share work; **riders with the same
  goal** (not all sprinters waiting for each other).
- Terrain favours it: hills, technical sections, crosswinds, late in a hard race.
Attack when others are tired or after a regroup lull, from a few wheels back with
speed, not from the front where everyone sees it.

## 4. Through-and-off (rotating paceline)
Two lines: one moving up, one dropping back. Take a short pull (a few seconds) at the
front, ease to the side, drift back. Smooth, equal turns are faster than heroic long
pulls. Don't surge when you hit the front — keep the speed.

## 5. Sprint finishes
Know the final kilometres (recon, course map, wind direction). Be in the first ~10–15
wheels approaching the last km. Lead-outs deliver a sprinter at ~150–250 m to go.
Hold your line in the sprint — deviating is a common relegation offence.

## 6. Criteriums
Short laps, many corners: cornering speed and position decide everything. Take the
corner's racing line, outside-inside-outside, pedal up (inside pedal) and weight on
the outside pedal, look through the exit. Stay near the front to avoid the
accordion sprint out of every corner.

## 7. Time-trial pacing
- Use **power**, not speed or heart rate (HR lags; speed varies with wind and gradient).
- Target an even power around your sustainable power for the duration (for a ~40–60 min
  effort, roughly FTP; shorter TTs above it — see cycling-training-and-performance).
- **Start below target** for the first minutes — adrenaline makes overcooking easy —
  and build. A slightly **negative split** (second half faster) is the classic aim.
- Push slightly harder uphill and into headwinds (time gains are larger there),
  ease slightly on descents and tailwinds.
- Aero position matters more than a few watts at higher speeds — practise holding it.

## 8. Rules and licences
Race under your federation's licence and rules (UCI internationally; national bodies
locally); categories by ability; mandatory helmet; check gearing restrictions for
juniors.

## Common faults
Overlapping wheels; chasing every move; pulling too long; braking in corners; starting
TTs too hard; sitting at the back.
