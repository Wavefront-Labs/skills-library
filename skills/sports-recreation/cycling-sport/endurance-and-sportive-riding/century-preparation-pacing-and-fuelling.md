# Endurance riding: century preparation, pacing and fuelling

**Goal:** finish a 100-mile/160 km sportive, a 200 km audax or a bikepacking day feeling
in control — the three keys are training volume, conservative pacing and constant
eating.

## 1. Build-up (typical 8–12 weeks from a fit base)
- One **long ride** per week, growing by ~10–20 minutes or 10–15 km a week, reaching
  ~75–80% of event distance two to three weeks out.
- Two or three shorter rides midweek including one with sustained efforts (sweet-spot
  intervals) — see cycling-training-and-performance.
- Ride the long ones on similar terrain and with your event kit and food.
- Taper: reduce volume ~40–50% in the final week, keep a little intensity.

## 2. Pacing
- Most riders start too fast in a group surge. Set a ceiling:
  - **Feel:** conversational pace for the first hours.
  - **Heart rate:** mostly in an easy-to-moderate zone (roughly zone 2) with brief climbs above.
  - **Power:** a common target for a 5–7 hour ride is around 60–75% of FTP average,
    with climbs capped near FTP. (Treat ranges as starting points.)
- **Climbs:** spin a low gear, sit, don't chase other riders up.
- Ride with a group at your pace and share turns, but drop off a group that's too fast.

## 3. Fuelling
- **Carbohydrate:** aim for roughly **60–90 g per hour** for long rides (higher
  intakes require gut training). Start eating within the first 30–45 minutes and
  every 20–30 minutes thereafter — don't wait for hunger.
- Mix sources: drink mix, gels, bars, rice cakes, bananas, real food at stops.
- **Fluid:** often ~500–750 ml per hour, more in heat; add **sodium/electrolytes**
  on long or hot days. Avoid drinking far beyond thirst (hyponatraemia).
- **The bonk** (glycogen depletion) hits as sudden weakness, irritability, cold sweat —
  prevention is eating; cure is fast carbs and easing off.
- Test every product in training, never new food on event day.

## 4. Route and controls
- Download the official **GPX** to your head unit; check turn-by-turn and battery life
  (power bank for longer events).
- Audax/randonneuring: **brevet card** stamped at controls; minimum and maximum
  times (e.g. 200 km typically has a 13.5 h limit under ACP-style rules — check the
  organiser). Unsupported — you carry everything.
- Note aid stations, shops and water points; plan to arrive at controls with buffer.

## 5. Comfort
- **Bike fit** (see bike-fit skill) matters more over distance.
- Good bib shorts, **chamois cream**, clean kit each day; change position often; stand
  for a few pedal strokes every 10–15 minutes.
- Hands: padded gloves, vary hand positions, check bar tape.
- Sunscreen, layers for descents and night; lights for audax (often required).

## 6. Kit list
Spare tubes ×2, tyre levers, mini pump/CO2, tubeless plugs, multi-tool with chain
breaker, quick link, phone, cash/card, ID, emergency contact, rain jacket, food.
Bikepacking: bags packed with heavy items low and central; test-ride loaded.

## Common faults
Going out with the fast group, skipping early feeding, untested nutrition, new
shorts or saddle on event day, no lights or spares.

## Verify
- [ ] Longest training ride reached ~75–80% of event distance.
- [ ] Pacing ceiling defined (feel/HR/power) and written on the stem.
- [ ] Fuel plan: grams of carbs per hour and timer reminders; products tested.
- [ ] GPX loaded, battery plan, controls/cut-offs known.
- [ ] Repair kit and lights checked the night before.
