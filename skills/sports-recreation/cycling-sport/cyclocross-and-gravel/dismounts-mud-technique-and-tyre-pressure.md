# Cyclocross and gravel: dismounts, mud technique and tyre pressure

**Goal:** lose as little time as possible getting off and on the bike, keep traction on
mud, sand and off-cambers, choose pressures that grip without pinch-flatting, and pace
long gravel days.

## 1. Dismount (right-side, the usual convention)
1. Approaching the barrier, unclip the **right** foot and swing the leg over the saddle
   behind you while still rolling; momentarily stand on the left pedal with the right
   leg alongside the left.
2. Right hand moves to the top tube (or the down tube if shouldering).
3. Unclip the left as the right foot touches down — step and run in stride.
4. Lift the bike over the barriers (by the top tube and bars), clear the planks, set it
   down smoothly.
Brake before, not during, the dismount. Practise at walking pace on grass.

## 2. Remount
- Hop onto the saddle with the **inside of the right thigh** landing on the saddle
  (sliding on, not jumping high), pushing the bike forward. Find pedals without looking.
- Practise until it's reflexive; the "stutter step" before remounting costs time.
- Some riders bunny-hop low barriers instead — only if confident (UCI barriers are
  typically ~40 cm max).

## 3. Shouldering and run-ups
For long or steep run-ups and stairs: reach the right arm under the down tube, lift the
bike onto the right shoulder (top tube on shoulder), hand around the head tube or on the
bars to stabilise. Run with short quick steps. Toe spikes in shoes help in mud.

## 4. Mud, sand and off-camber
- **Mud:** keep a steady cadence in a slightly higher gear for traction, stay seated for
  rear grip, look for the line with grip (often the fresh, untracked edge), avoid
  sudden steering or braking. Run when riding is slower — no shame.
- **Sand:** weight back, enter with speed, steady pedalling, keep the front light and
  straight; don't steer sharply.
- **Off-camber:** weight on the outside (downhill) pedal, look ahead, keep the bike
  upright-ish, pick a line high on the camber so a slide still ends on the course.
- **Pit bike:** in muddy races, swap to a clean bike every lap or half lap; practise
  exchanges in the pit.

## 5. Tyre pressure
Lower pressure = more grip and comfort, but risk of rim strikes and burping.
Rough starting points (verify for your setup; rider weight, tyre width, rim width and
tubeless/tubular all change this):
- CX, 33 mm tyres, tubeless: often in the high teens to mid-20s psi; tubulars run lower.
- Gravel, 38–45 mm tubeless: often ~25–40 psi.
Adjust 1 psi at a time, test on the course; use a digital gauge (floor-pump gauges vary).
UCI-sanctioned cyclocross caps tyre width at 33 mm.

## 6. Gravel pacing and logistics
- Long events (100–300+ km): pace by **power or heart rate**, not by the group early on;
  fast starts blow up long days. Typical target for very long events is well below FTP
  (e.g. ~60–75% for many hours — see endurance-and-sportive-riding).
- Fuel and drink from the start (carbs per hour, electrolytes); know aid-station distances.
- Repair kit: tubeless plugs, spare tube, CO2/mini pump, quick link, multi-tool.
- Surface awareness: descend loose gravel with weight back, light hands, no sudden braking.

## Common faults
Braking during the dismount, jumping onto the saddle, wrong side for the course's
barriers, too-high tyre pressure, starting gravel events at race-pace.
