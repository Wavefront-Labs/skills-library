# Race basketing and electronic timing procedure

**Goal:** get the right birds entered and registered for a race, time their arrival
correctly with an electronic timing system (ETS), and understand how the result
(velocity) is computed so you can spot errors.

> Draft: general procedure. ETS brands (Benzing, Tauris, Unikon, Bricon and others)
> differ in menus and club-system handling; your club/federation's rules decide
> basketing times, clock tolerances and how disputes are handled.

## Before race night
- Check **health and condition**: only birds that are fit, moulting appropriately,
  and vaccinated per your federation's rules. A doubtful bird stays home.
- Confirm each bird's **chip ring** is paired to its leg ring in your loft system
  (done once, when the chip is fitted).
- Check the **loft antenna/pads** at the trap: every bird should register when walked
  through. Test with a couple of birds.
- Know the **loft coordinates** registered with the club — velocity depends on them.

## Basketing (marking) at the club
1. Bring the loft module/clock to the club; connect it to the club system.
2. Each bird is scanned: the chip and leg ring are read and recorded against the race.
   Watch the screen — mismatches must be resolved there.
3. Birds go into the **race transporter baskets** for the release point.
4. The club system **synchronises the clock** (it should be set from a reference
   time; drift is checked again on return).
5. Keep the printout/receipt of entries.

## Race day
- After **liberation** (release time published by the convoy/race controller), the
  ETS is armed — make sure it's powered and the antenna connected.
- Keep the loft calm and the trap entrance clear so birds walk straight in — a bird
  sitting on the roof doesn't clock.
- Birds register automatically when crossing the antenna; check the display shows
  arrivals.

## After the race
1. Take the clock back to the club at the set time; the system reads arrivals and
   checks clock drift.
2. **Velocity** = distance from release point to your loft ÷ (arrival time − release
   time). Expressed in yards/minute (UK/US) or metres/minute (Europe). Example:
   400 km = 400,000 m flown in 6 h 40 m (400 min) → 1,000 m/min.
3. Results are ranked by velocity across lofts — so loft-specific distances matter.

## Gotchas
- A dead battery or loose antenna = no times. Charge and check before liberation.
- **Trapping**: train birds to come straight in (feed after arrival, calm loft).
- Clock and system **time zones/DST** — the club system usually handles it, but a
  manually set clock is a classic source of error.
- Weather: hold-overs and late releases change everything — follow the race
  controller's announcements.
- Re-check distance coordinates when you move loft — they're on the federation record.

## Verify
- [ ] Every entered bird scanned with matching chip/leg ring at basketing.
- [ ] Clock synchronised at basketing and drift checked at return.
- [ ] Antenna tested and ETS powered before liberation.
- [ ] Your own velocity calculation matches the published result.
