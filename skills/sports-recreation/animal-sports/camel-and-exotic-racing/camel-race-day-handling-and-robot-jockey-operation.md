# Camel race-day handling and robot-jockey operation

**Goal:** bring a racing camel to the start calm, sound and correctly equipped, and —
where robot jockeys are used — operate the jockey from the chase vehicle legally and
humanely.

> Draft, low confidence on specifics: camel racing practice differs a lot by region.
> In Gulf states (UAE, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, Oman) child jockeys are banned and
> lightweight **robot jockeys** are standard; in Australia and some other places
> adults ride (often in novelty or festival races like camel cups). Equipment rules,
> permitted robot features (e.g. any "whip" or electric devices) and anti-doping
> rules are set by the national racing federation — **read the current rules**; do not
> rely on this file for them.

## Before race day
- **Fitness and soundness**: daily handling, feet and pads checked, no lameness.
  Race camels need gradual conditioning; sudden increases cause injury.
- **Veterinary and registration**: identification (often microchip), race
  registration, any required vet inspection and drug-testing compliance.
- **Equipment** fitted and checked days before: saddle/pad that doesn't rub, halter and
  nose line (where used), robot jockey mounting.

## Robot jockey basics
- A small battery-powered unit strapped to the saddle, controlled by radio from a
  handset in the **chase vehicle** that drives alongside the track.
- Typical functions (vary by model and are regulated): a moving arm with a light
  whip or crop, a speaker for the trainer's voice, sometimes reins. Some units
  report speed/heart rate.
- Pre-race checks: battery charged, firmly strapped (it must not slip or swing),
  radio paired to *your* handset and on the right channel, arm movement tested at rest.
- Operating: use the voice channel first — camels know their trainer's voice;
  use the whip arm sparingly and only within permitted limits. Over-use is both a
  welfare issue and, increasingly, a rules issue.

## Race-day handling
1. **Arrive early**; let the camel settle. Water and shade per the federation's
   timing rules (feeding windows may be restricted pre-race).
2. **Warm-up**: walk then canter; check gait for any unsoundness.
3. **At the start**: handlers hold the camel steady until the release (gates or line
   start depending on venue). Camels can kick and bite — keep clear of hindquarters
   and stay at the shoulder.
4. **Chase vehicle**: the driver focuses on the road; a separate operator runs the
   handset. Track-side roads are busy — vehicles drive fast in a pack.
5. **After the race**: catch up, cool down, water, check legs and feet.

## Gotchas
- Battery or radio failure mid-race leaves the camel running riderless — it'll
  usually run on with the field; the team must be ready to catch it at the finish.
- Mixing up channels between handsets of different camels happens — label everything.
- Heat: race scheduling is usually early or in cooler months; heat stress in camels
  is real despite their reputation.
- **Exotic racing** (ostriches, other species at festivals) varies wildly; welfare
  criticism is common — follow local rules and veterinary advice.

## Verify
- [ ] Camel registered, identified and vet-cleared under current federation rules.
- [ ] Robot jockey charged, secured, paired and tested before heading to the start.
- [ ] Warm-up showed a sound gait.
- [ ] Post-race cool-down and leg/foot check done and logged.
