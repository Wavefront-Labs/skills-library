# Team driving commands and race run/rest strategy

**Goal:** drive a dog team that responds to voice on the trail, put each dog in the
position that suits it, and — for distance races — schedule running and resting so the
team finishes strong and healthy.

> Draft: widely used mushing terms and practice. Race rules (mandatory rests, vet
> checks, required gear) are specific to each race — read the current rules. Animal
> welfare comes first: any doubt about a dog's soundness means it doesn't run.

## Commands (standard, but each musher's dogs learn *their* cues)
- **Hike / let's go** — start or speed up.
- **Gee** — turn right. **Haw** — turn left.
- **On by** — pass a distraction, other team or trail junction without turning.
- **Whoa** — stop (backed by the brake; dogs stop because the sled stops).
- **Easy** — slow down.
- **Come gee / come haw** — 180° turn back the way you came.

Teach directional commands to leaders on walks and at junctions they'd naturally take,
pairing the word with the turn; reinforce consistently.

## Positions on the gangline
- **Lead dogs**: responsive, confident, keep the line tight even when stopped.
- **Swing dogs**: right behind leaders; help swing the team round corners.
- **Team dogs**: the engine in the middle.
- **Wheel dogs**: closest to the sled; strong and calm about the sled moving behind them.

Rotate where possible and note who runs well beside whom.

## Sled handling
- Set the **snow hook** and/or tie off before stopping anywhere you'll step off; a team
  will leave without you.
- Brake to keep the gangline tight on downhills — a slack line tangles and hurts dogs.
- Use the **drag mat** to control speed without stopping.
- **Pedal** (push with one foot) and run uphills to help the team.

## Distance race strategy (run/rest)
- Many mushers aim for **roughly equal run and rest** periods in long races — a
  rule of thumb, shaped by race-mandated rests, conditions, and team fitness.
- Plan rests at checkpoints and on-trail camps; at each stop: **straw down, feed,
  booties off, check feet and wrists, massage, snack, then rest yourself**.
- Feed calories and **water** (often as soaked meat/kibble soups) — dehydration is a
  frequent problem.
- **Booties** on rough or icy snow to protect feet; check for splits between toes.
- Leave a tired or sore dog at a checkpoint with the vets (dropping dogs is normal,
  not failure).

## Gotchas
- Head-on passes: give clear **on by**, slow down, and make room.
- Moose and wildlife on trail can be dangerous to the team; know local guidance.
- Overspeeding a young team early burns them out; run the plan, not the adrenaline.
- Sprint racing is a different sport: short, fast heats, tighter gangs, no rest
  scheduling — training emphasis changes accordingly.

## Verify
- [ ] Leaders respond reliably to gee, haw, whoa and on by at junctions.
- [ ] Every stop begins with the hook set or the team tied off.
- [ ] Written run/rest plan matched to mandatory rests and checkpoints.
- [ ] Each rest includes foot, wrist and hydration checks, logged per dog.
- [ ] No dog runs with an unresolved injury or vet concern.
