# Outrun, lift, fetch, drive and pen — a trial run

**Goal:** handle a working dog through the standard trial phases with calm, well-timed
commands so the sheep move in straight lines at a steady pace and lose as few points
as possible.

> Draft: the course pattern follows the common ISDS/USBCHA-style format; point
> allocations, course shapes and time limits vary by trial and class. Read the
> trial's schedule and course sheet.

## Commands (voice and whistle — each handler has their own whistles)
- **Come bye** — dog circles clockwise round the stock.
- **Away (to me)** — anticlockwise.
- **Lie down / stand** — stop. **Walk up** — move straight toward the stock.
- **Steady / take time** — slow down. **That'll do** — come off the stock.
Whistles carry far further than voice; each whistle should be a distinct, consistent
pattern the dog has learned. Sound different-enough whistles for opposite flanks.

## Phases and what judges look for
1. **Outrun** — the dog leaves the handler at the post and runs out wide, pear-shaped,
   to finish behind the sheep without disturbing them. *Deductions:* tight or crossing
   over (dog crosses the line between handler and sheep), stopping, needing redirects.
2. **Lift** — the first contact: the dog should approach steadily so the sheep move off
   calmly in a straight line. *Deductions:* sheep bolting, dog rushing or gripping.
3. **Fetch** — bring the sheep in a straight line through the fetch gates to the
   handler, then round behind the post. *Deductions:* wobbling off line, missed gates,
   speed changes.
4. **Drive** — push the sheep away through the drive gates, across the field
   (cross-drive) and back, in straight lines with tidy turns at the gates.
5. **Shed** — split off a specified number of sheep within a ring, with the dog coming
   through the gap on command and holding the separated group.
6. **Pen** — handler holds the gate rope; dog and handler guide sheep into the pen
   calmly. Handler can't let go of the rope until done (rules vary).
(Some classes end with a **single** — separating one marked sheep.)

## Handling tips
- **Read the sheep first** — watch earlier runs: which way do they pull (toward
  exhaust pens, gaps, other sheep)? Plan to counter that draw.
- **Fewer commands, better timed.** Over-commanding breaks the dog's own stock sense.
- Use small flanks to steer lines; big flanks change the sheep's direction too much.
- On the drive, stand still at the post; you can't follow along on most courses.
- Keep sheep moving at a walk — pace changes cost points.

## Gotchas
- **Grip** (biting) can disqualify, depending on circumstances and judge.
- A dog that's too close pushes sheep fast; "get back" or a wider flank fixes pressure.
- Time limits end runs mid-phase; budget time for shed and pen.

## Verify
- [ ] Outrun finished behind the stock without crossing over.
- [ ] Sheep lifted calmly and fetched through the gates in a straight line.
- [ ] Drive gates made with tidy turns, steady pace.
- [ ] Shed and pen completed within time.
- [ ] Score sheet reviewed against your memory of each phase to plan training.
