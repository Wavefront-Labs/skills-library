# Agility course walk and handling plan

**Goal:** use the pre-run course walk to decide, obstacle by obstacle, where you'll
be and what your body is saying to the dog — so the dog's path follows the numbers
and you don't improvise mid-run.

> Draft: general handling vocabulary shared by most systems; names and preferred moves
> differ between handling schools, and faults/time rules differ between organisations
> (AKC, UKC, Kennel Club, FCI, USDAA…). Check your rulebook.

## Before the walk
- Get the **course map**: numbers, standard course time (SCT), class. Note
  the obvious **traps** — an off-course obstacle in line with the dog's natural path,
  a tunnel mouth beside a jump, weave entry angle.
- Know your dog's habits: does it read rear crosses? drive tunnels? miss weave entries?

## Walking it (the method)
1. **Walk the dog's path first**, at dog speed in your head, from 1 to the end. Feel
   where the line naturally takes the dog.
2. **Mark the decision points** — places where the dog must turn or choose between
   obstacles. These are where handling matters; straight lines mostly run themselves.
3. **Choose the move at each decision point**:
   - **Front cross** — you cross in front of the dog to change your side; strong,
     clear turn cue, but you must get there first.
   - **Rear cross** — cross behind the dog as it goes forward; good when you can't
     get ahead, needs a dog that reads it.
   - **Blind cross** — you turn your back briefly while crossing ahead; fast, but
     many venues/handlers avoid it near trap obstacles.
   - **Serpentine / threadle** — pull the dog between obstacles toward you.
   - **Wrap / tight turn** — collect the dog round a wing.
4. **Walk your own path** at running pace, saying your verbal cues out loud and
   moving your shoulders, arms and feet as you will on the run. Repeat 2–3 times.
5. **Set the start**: lead-out distance you can trust your dog's stay to hold.

## Running it
- **Shoulders and feet steer**; the dog follows your motion more than your words.
- Cue the next obstacle **before** the dog commits to the current one (one obstacle
  ahead in your head).
- **Contacts**: decide criteria in training (running vs stopped) and don't change it
  in the ring — inconsistency kills contacts.
- **Weaves**: get the entry, then move on; trust the dog to finish.

## Gotchas
- **Late cues** cause refusals and run-outs, and over-rotation causes the dog to
  turn wide or spin.
- **Over-handling** a straight line slows the dog.
- **Tunnel suck**: dogs love tunnels; position to block the wrong mouth early.
- The handler's eye-line pulls the dog: looking at an off-course jump can send the dog
  to it.

## Verify
- [ ] Each decision point has a named move and a verbal cue.
- [ ] Traps identified and a blocking position planned for each.
- [ ] Handler path walked at speed at least twice.
- [ ] Result reviewed (ideally on video): faults, refusals, off-courses traced back
      to the moment of a late or wrong cue.
