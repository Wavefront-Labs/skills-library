# Aresti sequence flying and energy management

**Goal:** turn an Aresti diagram into a rehearsed, flown sequence that stays inside
the box, keeps enough energy for every figure, and scores well with judges.

> Draft: competition aerobatics as run by IAC/CIVA-style rules. Category rules, box
> sizes, minimum altitudes and the aircraft's limits come from the current rulebook
> and the POH — always check those. Aerobatics requires dual instruction in a suitable
> aircraft; this is a reference, not training.

## Reading the Aresti
- Each figure is drawn from the start (small dot) to the end (short perpendicular bar).
  Solid lines = upright flight, dashed = inverted.
- Rolls are marked on lines: arrow-style marks for aileron rolls, with fractions
  (½, ¾) and multi-point notation (e.g. 4/8 = four-point roll over a full rotation).
  Snap and spin elements have their own symbols.
- Each figure has a **K-factor** (difficulty); score = judge grade × K.
- Translate the drawing into a **flight card**: figure, entry speed, entry direction
  (into or out of the wind, toward or away from the judges), and expected exit.

## Energy plan
- Note each figure's **entry speed** for your aircraft (from POH and practice):
  looping figures need more; hammerheads and spins need less.
- Mark where the sequence **gains** energy (downlines) and **spends** it (uplines,
  loops). If a high-energy figure follows a low-energy exit, you need a dive or a
  pause in the box — or a sequence-legal choice of entry.
- Track **altitude**: start near the top of the box; know your floor (category-
  dependent) and which figures lose height.

## Flying it
1. Fly it **on the ground first**: walk the sequence with a model ("dirt dancing"),
   saying entry speeds and wind correction aloud.
2. **Lines**: draw clean horizontal, vertical and 45° lines; judges grade line
   angles harshly. Pause briefly to show the line before and after rolls.
3. **Wind correction**: crosswind shoves you out the back of the box — add a little
   out-of-box drift correction on downwind legs, and use "box-in/out" figures
   (half-rolls on lines, etc.) where the rules allow.
4. **Look out** between figures; a break in the sequence for safety is always allowed.

## Common downgrades
- Lines off-axis (a 45° that's really 35°).
- Loops not round — speed lost at the top makes them egg-shaped; manage pull
  force through the figure.
- Roll rate inconsistent, points not equal, or wings not level on stop.
- Hammerhead pivot too early or with pendulum.
- Out of box penalties and altitude infringements.

## Gotchas
- **G-tolerance** varies day to day (sleep, hydration, rest). Grunt/strain techniques
  help; warm up with lighter figures.
- Inverted fuel/oil systems have time limits; know yours.
- Spins: confirm entry and recovery technique for *your* aircraft type — they differ.

## Verify
- [ ] Every figure has a written entry speed and direction on the flight card.
- [ ] Energy and altitude budget shows no figure entered short of speed or below floor.
- [ ] Sequence rehearsed on the ground with wind corrections.
- [ ] Flown version reviewed on video/ground critic notes for lines, roundness, box.
