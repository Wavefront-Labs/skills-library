# Cold inflation, launch and wind-layer steering

**Goal:** get the envelope from bag to flying safely as a pilot-plus-crew team, then
"steer" a balloon the only way it can be steered — by choosing altitudes where the
wind blows the direction you want.

> Draft: general practice for sport ballooning. Exact procedures follow the balloon's
> flight manual, the pilot-in-command's briefing and national rules (licence, airspace).
> Wind limits for inflation vary; many pilots won't inflate above ~8–10 kt surface wind.

## Site and briefing
- Launch site with a clear downwind path (no power lines, trees, buildings for a
  good distance). Check the forecast winds aloft and release a **pibal** (small helium
  or self-made balloon) to see what each layer is doing.
- Brief crew: who holds the **crown line**, who handles the throat, how to signal
  stop. **Gloves on**, no loose clothing near the burner; no one stands downwind of
  the basket during hot inflation.

## Cold inflation (fan)
1. Lay the basket on its side, burners pointing into the envelope throat, envelope
   stretched downwind. Attach and check flying wires/carabiners and the parachute
   vent's rip line.
2. Two crew hold the throat open; the inflator fan fills the envelope with cold air.
3. The **crown line** crew walks out to the top and holds tension so the envelope
   doesn't roll or rise early.
4. Pilot walks inside (when appropriate) to check the parachute vent is seated and
   fabric undamaged.

## Hot inflation and launch
1. Pilot burns in short controlled blasts into the throat (crew keep hands clear of
   the flame path). The envelope rises; the crown-line crew lets it come up steadily,
   resisting — not stopping — it.
2. Basket comes upright; crew put weight on the basket ("hands on") while the pilot
   heats to near equilibrium.
3. Final checks: fuel, instruments, passengers briefed (landing position: knees bent,
   hold handles, face the direction of travel).
4. **Hands off** on the pilot's command; one more burn to a positive, steady climb.

## Steering by layers
- Winds often turn with height (in the northern hemisphere, typically veering
  clockwise as you climb — a general tendency, not a rule).
- Climb or descend through layers, watching GPS track; level off in the one going your
  way. Burn rhythm: small, frequent burns hold height better than big ones.
- Balloons respond slowly — anticipate. Heat added now shows up as climb many
  seconds later.

## Gotchas
- **Thermal activity**: mid-morning sun brings unpredictable lift and gusts — most
  flights are dawn or late evening.
- **Under-heated launch** in wind → the basket drags or hits obstacles. Get to
  near-equilibrium first.
- **Power lines** are the leading serious hazard; plan landings well clear.
- Crew should never be lifted: if the basket rises unexpectedly, **let go**.

## Chase and retrieve
Chase crew track by radio/phone and GPS; respect landowners, close gates, ask permission.

## Verify
- [ ] Pibal or winds-aloft read and launch direction checked against obstacles.
- [ ] Crown line, throat and fan roles briefed; gloves on.
- [ ] Envelope and vent checked during cold inflation.
- [ ] Launch made only at near-equilibrium with crew hands-off on command.
- [ ] Track change achieved by altitude, and landing site clear of wires.
