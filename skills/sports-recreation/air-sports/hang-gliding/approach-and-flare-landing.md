# Approach and flare landing — hang glider

**Goal:** arrive on final into wind, at a safe airspeed, at the right height — then
transition upright and push out a full, well-timed flare so the glider stalls just as
your feet arrive.

> Draft: a summary of commonly taught technique. Your instructor's specifics, the
> glider's speed range and the landing field's rules take precedence. Exact flare
> timing is glider- and conditions-dependent and only learned by practice.

## 1. Plan before you're low
- Know the wind direction at the LZ (streamer, windsock); check again from altitude.
- Choose the approach: **aircraft approach** (downwind → base → final, like the
  canopy and power-aircraft patterns) is predictable for traffic; a **figure-8**
  over the downwind side loses height in a controlled box before final. Use whatever
  the site specifies.
- Set a **decision point** for turning final high enough to have a long, straight final.

## 2. Airspeed is life on approach
- Pull the bar in (nose down) to fly **faster than trim** for the whole approach —
  extra speed gives control authority and penetrates the **wind gradient**, where the
  wind drops near the ground and the glider loses airspeed.
- Never turn low and slow. Make final turns with speed on and wings level by
  ~ a few hundred feet (site-dependent).

## 3. Transition and final
1. On final, **transition upright**: move hands one at a time from the basetube to the
   downtubes at about shoulder height, body vertical, legs down. Keep the speed on
   while doing it — beginners let the bar out and slow down here.
2. Fly straight down the field a metre or two off the ground, nearly level, letting
   the glider bleed speed off gradually ("round out").
3. **Flare**: when speed has bled to just above stall (the glider feels light; ground
   speed close to walking pace in light wind), push out **hard, fast and fully** — up
   and out, arms extended. A half-flare is the classic error.
4. Step or run out, keeping the wings level.

## Gotchas
- **Early flare**: glider balloons up then drops you — the nose falls and hits
  ("whack"). **Late flare**: you land fast and run or slide. If in doubt, a strong
  late flare is usually better than a weak early one.
- **Wings not level** at flare → one wingtip hits, glider pivots. Correct roll before
  flaring.
- **Downwind or crosswind landings** need extra speed and a more aggressive flare —
  or choose a different field.
- **Wheels** on the basetube are cheap insurance for whacks; many schools require them.
- Thermals off the LZ can lift you on final; turbulence near trees and hedges pushes
  the glider around — keep speed and avoid low-level manoeuvring.

## Verify
- [ ] LZ wind direction confirmed before entering the pattern.
- [ ] Approach flown with speed above trim; final reached wings level, well above
      the ground.
- [ ] Upright transition completed early on final without losing airspeed.
- [ ] One full, decisive flare at the right moment; landed on feet, wings level.
- [ ] Each landing debriefed: early, late or on time? Ideally with video.
