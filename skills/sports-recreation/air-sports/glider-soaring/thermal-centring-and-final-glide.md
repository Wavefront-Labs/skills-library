# Thermal centring and final glide — climb well, get home

**Goal:** turn rising air into height efficiently, then leave the last thermal with
enough height to reach the airfield safely — not just on paper.

> Draft: general soaring technique (BGA/SSA-style training). Numbers are illustrative;
> use your glider's flight manual for speeds and your instructor's/club's rules for
> minimum arrival heights. Instrument behaviour varies by vario/flight computer.

## Centring a thermal
1. **Recognise entry**: a surge (glider accelerates, nose pitches), one wing lifting,
   the vario rising. Don't turn on the first blip — wait 1–2 s for the vario to
   confirm, then **turn towards the wing that lifted**.
2. **Bank 30–45°** at the manual's recommended thermalling speed (often ~1.1–1.2 ×
   stall in the turn). Too shallow and you circle outside the core.
3. **Shift the circle toward the strong side.** Two common methods:
   - *Tighten-on-weak / straighten-on-strong*: when lift increases, flatten the bank
     for a few seconds (moving the circle toward it), then re-bank.
   - *Reference-point*: note the heading where lift is best; one turn later, shift
     a few seconds toward that heading.
4. Use an **averager** (climb rate per full turn) to judge whether it's worth staying.
5. **Look out.** Thermals collect gliders — join in the same direction as those
   already circling, and keep sight of them. FLARM helps but doesn't replace eyes.

## Leaving: speed to fly
Set **MacCready** to the climb rate you realistically expect in the next thermal.
Between thermals, fly the speed the flight computer/ring demands: faster in sink,
slower in lift. Strong day = higher MC = faster cruise; scratchy day = low MC.

## Final glide
1. Required height ≈ distance ÷ effective glide ratio, adjusted for wind, plus your
   **safety margin** (arrival height over the field, often ~1,000 ft / 300 m for
   less experienced pilots — club rules vary).
2. Calculate with **MC 0 or low** for safety margin, or the computer's "final glide
   required" figure. Headwind hurts more than tailwind helps.
3. **Monitor it continuously.** Every few km compare actual height with required; a
   steady loss means sink, wind or a dirty wing (bugs, rain) is costing performance.
4. **Have a field.** Until the airfield is assured, keep a landable field within glide.

## Gotchas
- The vario lags; the seat-of-the-pants surge is earlier. Learn to feel it.
- Flight computers assume the polar is accurate; bugs and rain can degrade glide
  noticeably — add margin (some pilots set a "bugs" percentage).
- **Get-home-itis**: committing to a marginal final glide instead of picking a field
  early is a classic accident chain. Field selection by ~1,500 ft AGL (or your club's
  figure), committed circuit by ~1,000 ft.
- Low thermalling near terrain or other gliders reduces margins fast.

- [ ] Final glide figure includes wind and an explicit safety margin.
