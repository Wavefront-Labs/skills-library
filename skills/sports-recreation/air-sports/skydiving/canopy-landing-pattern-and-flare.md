# Canopy landing pattern and flare — land where you meant to, upright

**Goal:** after opening, fly a predictable pattern to a clear landing area and finish
with a level, fully-flared final so you touch down slowly on your feet — or in a
correct parachute landing fall (PLF) when that isn't possible.

> Draft: general practice taught in USPA/BPA-style canopy courses. Exact altitudes,
> pattern direction and rules are **set by your drop zone (DZ)** and your instructor's
> briefing — those always override this file. Not a substitute for supervised training.

## 1. Before you board
- **Read the wind**: windsock, flags, other canopies landing. Note direction and
  strength, and whether it is gusty or shifting.
- **Learn the DZ pattern**: left- or right-hand, the landing target, the hazard list
  (power lines, water, trees, the runway) and the "alternate" areas.
- **Pick your altitudes** (typical student numbers; your DZ may differ): holding area
  upwind of the target until ~1,000 ft AGL, downwind leg starting ~1,000 ft, base
  ~600 ft, final no lower than ~300 ft. Check your altimeter, not your gut.

## 2. Under canopy, top to bottom
1. **Check and clear** — canopy square and flying, controllable; look around for traffic.
2. **Fly to the holding area** upwind of the target and stay there, doing gentle
   control checks (a practice flare at altitude tells you where the stall point is).
3. **Downwind leg** (flying with the wind, offset to one side of the target): ground
   speed is high, so the target slides past quickly. Judge your height relative to the
   target, not the clock.
4. **Base leg** — a 90° turn crosswind. Here you adjust: if you're high, extend; if low,
   cut the corner early. This is the leg that corrects mistakes.
5. **Final** — turn into wind. Get established, **wings level, hands up (full flight)**
   for the canopy to build speed. Small corrections only with flat turns (light toggle
   plus slight harness input) — no big turns now.
6. **Flare** — begin at roughly 10–15 ft (often described as about twice your height,
   or when the ground starts to "rush"): a smooth, progressive pull to full flare by
   touchdown, hands finishing at or below the hips and held there. Feet and knees
   together, ready to run or PLF.

## Gotchas
- **The low turn kills.** A sharp turn near the ground dives the canopy and accelerates
  you into it. Below your decision altitude, land *ahead* in whatever direction you're
  facing — a downwind landing with a PLF beats a turn at 50 ft.
- **Hook turns** (deliberate high-speed turns to final) are a separate, advanced
  canopy-piloting discipline with coaching and a dedicated landing area. Don't copy
  them.
- **Flare too high** and the canopy stalls back and drops you; **too low or too
  fast** and you slam in. Practice flares at altitude every jump.
- **Wing loading** (exit weight ÷ canopy area) changes everything. A smaller canopy is
  faster and less forgiving — follow your DZ's downsizing progression.
- **Turbulence** downwind of hangars and tree lines; avoid flying low over them.
- Follow the canopy below you; **traffic lands in the pattern direction**, not yours.

## Parachute landing fall (PLF)
Feet and knees together, knees slightly bent, chin tucked, elbows in. Contact the balls
of the feet, then roll along the calf, thigh, buttock and the back of the shoulder.
Default to it on any doubtful landing — rough ground, downwind, a missed flare.

## Verify
- [ ] Wind direction and strength known before boarding; pattern direction confirmed.
- [ ] Holding area, downwind, base and final flown at planned altimeter heights.
- [ ] Practice flare done at altitude this jump.
- [ ] No turn made below the decision altitude; final flown wings level.
- [ ] Landed within the target area on feet or with a correct PLF — debrief with
      an instructor using video if available.
