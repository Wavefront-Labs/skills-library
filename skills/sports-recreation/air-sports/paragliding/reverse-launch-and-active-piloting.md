# Reverse launch and active piloting — get it up cleanly, keep it up

**Goal:** launch reliably when there's enough wind to make a forward launch messy,
and fly in active air by feeling the wing through the brakes so it rarely collapses —
and when it does, it recovers fast.

> Draft: standard school technique, paraphrased. Wind limits, launch etiquette and
> wing-class choice come from your instructor and site rules. Collapse handling is
> best learned over water on an SIV (simulation d'incident en vol) course.

## Pre-flight (every flight)
Lines clear and untwisted, no knots; risers the right way round; leg straps and chest
strap closed (an open leg strap is a classic fatal error); reserve handle and pin
checked; helmet done up; wind and airspace checked; launch clear.

## Reverse launch — the method
1. Lay the wing out in a gentle arc, leading edge up. Clip in, then **turn to face the
   wing** — the risers cross; know which way you turned so you turn back the same way.
2. Hold the A-risers in one hand, the brakes (and optionally C/rear risers) in the
   other, per your school's grip. Check the wind is straight up the slope.
3. **Inflate**: a step back and a smooth pull on the As; let the wing rise. Use the
   brakes/Cs to stop it overshooting as it arrives overhead.
4. **Control at the top**: centre yourself under the wing — move your feet *under*
   the side that drops, rather than hauling the brake. Look up and check: fully open,
   no knots, no cravats (tip stuck in lines).
5. **Turn** (the correct direction to uncross the risers), lean forward into the
   harness, keep light brake pressure, and run off. Keep running until well clear of
   the ground.

Abort criteria: anything not right at step 4 — kill the wing (pull rear risers or
brakes hard and run toward it) and lay it out again.

## Active piloting in the air
- **Pitch**: if the wing surges ahead of you, add brake to slow it; as it drops behind,
  release brake to let it fly. Timing is the skill — you damp the oscillation.
- **Pressure**: hold a light, constant contact on both brakes. A sudden **loss of
  pressure** on one side is the warning of an impending collapse: apply brake on that
  side quickly and briefly to restore internal pressure, then release.
- **Roll**: weight-shift toward the side that's loaded to keep the wing balanced over you.
- In turbulence, **keep flying the wing** — hands up and passive is how small
  deflations become big ones.

## If it collapses (asymmetric)
1. **Stop the turn first**: weight-shift away and apply opposing brake carefully —
   enough to keep straight, not so much the flying side stalls.
2. Once straight, pump the collapsed side's brake with a long, smooth stroke if it
   doesn't re-open by itself.
3. If you're spinning, low, or it won't recover — **throw the reserve** without delay.

## Gotchas
- Pulling brakes to stop a wing that's falling *sideways* often stalls it. Move your
  feet instead.
- Higher-aspect, higher-class wings (EN-C/D) collapse more dynamically; a school
  graduate belongs on EN-A/low EN-B.
- Thermic midday air is rough; learn active piloting in mellow evening conditions first.

## Verify
- [ ] Pre-flight includes leg straps and reserve pin every time.
- [ ] You can ground-handle (kite) the wing reverse for minutes without it falling.
- [ ] On launch you look up and confirm a clean wing before turning.
- [ ] In flight, you sense pressure changes and correct pitch before surges become
      collapses.
- [ ] You can describe your stop-the-turn-first collapse sequence and reserve trigger.
