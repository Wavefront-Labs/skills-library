# Escape rooms — puzzle triage, team roles and room design

**Goal:** get out in time by organising the team, searching properly and
using hints wisely — and, if you're building one, design a room whose
puzzles flow fairly.

## 1. Before the clock starts
- Listen to the briefing: what's off-limits (ceiling tiles, sockets,
  anything with a "do not touch" sticker), how hints work, whether there
  are red herrings.
- Ideal team size is often 3-5 even when 8 are allowed; more players
  means more parallel puzzles but more chaos.
- Agree to say everything you find **out loud**.

## 2. Search properly
- Search systematically: one person per wall/area, then swap — fresh
  eyes find what others miss.
- Check undersides, inside drawers' tops, behind pictures, under rugs,
  pockets of clothing, books.
- **Pile found items in one central spot**, used items in another
  (or hand them back to the game master if the room allows). Mixing used
  and unused items is the classic time sink.
- Don't force anything: if it needs force it's usually not the solution
  (and you may break it).

## 3. Puzzle triage
- **Locks tell you the answer format:** a 4-digit lock needs 4 digits; a
  directional lock needs a sequence of arrows; a letter lock a word of
  that length. Match clues to locks by format.
- Write every number/symbol you find on a shared sheet or whiteboard.
- Rooms are **linear** (one path) or **non-linear** (parallel tracks).
  In non-linear rooms split into pairs per track; in linear rooms
  everyone swarms the current puzzle with different ideas.
- **Rotate** when stuck for ~3 minutes on one puzzle — a new person sees
  the obvious.
- When nearly every item is used, the remaining items belong to the
  remaining lock.

## 4. Hints — use them
- Rooms are tuned so most teams need some hints. Take one after ~5
  minutes of no progress anywhere; pride is the main cause of failing.
- Ask specific questions ("Is the clock relevant to the safe?").
- Track time: by halfway you should be roughly halfway through the
  puzzles; if not, hint earlier.

## 5. Team roles (informal)
- **Searcher(s)**, **solver(s)**, a **scribe/tracker** of codes and used
  items, and a **timekeeper** who calls hint timings.
- Avoid one person holding all the clues in their hands.

## 6. Designing a room (or a home escape game)
- Start from the **story and the final reveal**, then design backward.
- Map the **puzzle flow** as a diagram: each puzzle's inputs (clues) and
  output (what it unlocks). Check no puzzle needs outside knowledge or
  a leap only you would make.
- Mix types: search, observation, logic, physical, teamwork.
- Use each clue once (and say so in the briefing) to reduce confusion.
- **Playtest** with people who don't know you; watch silently; note every
  stall and every wrong answer that seemed reasonable — fix those.
- Write a **reset checklist** (every lock code, item location) and a hint
  script with tiered hints per puzzle.
- Safety: an always-openable exit, no real electrical hazards, nothing
  that requires climbing.

## Gotchas
- Unused and used items mixed together.
- Keeping clues silent "until sure".
- Designers' puzzles that make sense only to the designer — playtest.

## Verify
- [ ] Items were piled centrally, with used items separated.
- [ ] Every found code was written on the shared sheet.
- [ ] Hints were taken after no progress anywhere for ~5 minutes.
- [ ] (Design) Puzzle flow diagram, reset checklist and outside playtest done.
