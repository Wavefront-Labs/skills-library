# Miniature wargaming — army lists, tactics and painting for play

**Goal:** field an army that wins on objectives, play the board
intelligently and get models painted to a decent standard without it
taking years. Examples reference Games Workshop systems (Warhammer 40,000,
Age of Sigmar) and historical games like Bolt Action; exact rules and
points change with every edition and balance update — always use the
current official documents.

## 1. Army list building
- **Read the mission pack first.** Most modern competitive games are won
  on **objectives/victory points**, not by killing everything. Your list
  needs units that can *hold* and *take* objectives as well as damage.
- Build around **roles**: objective holders, screeners (cheap units to
  block enemy deep strike/movement), anti-tank/anti-big damage,
  anti-infantry/horde damage, and fast units for secondary objectives.
- **Redundancy:** two or three units for each critical role — one gets
  killed.
- Avoid "one of everything" lists: focus multiplies buff and synergy value.
- Check the **points limit and composition rules** (e.g. caps on duplicate
  units, characters, allied factions) — illegal lists are the most common
  tournament problem. Official list-building apps help but can lag
  updates; double-check against the latest balance PDF.

## 2. Tabletop tactics
- **Deployment:** deploy out of line of sight of the enemy's long-range
  threats if you go second; screen against deep strike by covering the
  reserve-arrival zones (rules commonly forbid arriving within a set
  distance of enemies — check your edition).
- **Turn sequence discipline:** every turn ask: what scores this turn?
  what does the opponent need to score? Move with that in mind before
  thinking about shooting.
- **Target priority:** kill what threatens your scoring or what scores
  for them; finish damaged units rather than spreading damage.
- **Terrain:** use blocking terrain to hide; trade units on your terms by
  offering a bait unit forward where the enemy can only reach it.
- **Trade efficiently:** losing a cheap unit to take out an expensive one
  is good.
- Measure before committing; agree terrain rules (what blocks line of
  sight) before the game starts.
- Keep a mini-score sheet each turn.

## 3. Painting for the table
"Battle ready" in a reasonable time:
1. **Prime** (spray primer in dry, mild conditions, ~20-30 cm away, short
   passes). Humid or cold spraying gives a rough "frosted" finish.
2. **Basecoat** big areas with thinned paint (two thin coats beat one
   thick one).
3. **Shade/wash** (or use contrast/speed paints over a light primer to do
   base + shade in one).
4. **Drybrush or edge highlight** raised details.
5. **Basing:** texture paste or sand, drybrush, tufts — a good base makes
   an average model look finished.
6. **Varnish** (matt) for handling protection.
Batch-paint a squad colour by colour. Many events require painted armies
— check before entering.

## 4. Terrain
Foam board, cork tiles and found objects make cheap ruins and hills;
prime with acrylic or a foam-safe primer — solvent sprays can melt
polystyrene foam.

## 5. Etiquette
Show your list and key rules before the game; ask "are you happy with
that position?" before resolving close measurements; clean dice in the
open.

## Gotchas
- Building only for damage and losing on points.
- Old edition rules/points in an army app.
- Spraying solvent primer on foam terrain.

## Verify
- [ ] List is legal under the current points and composition rules.
- [ ] Every critical role has at least two units.
- [ ] Each turn you knew what you and your opponent could score.
- [ ] Models are primed, based and varnished to the event's painting requirement.
