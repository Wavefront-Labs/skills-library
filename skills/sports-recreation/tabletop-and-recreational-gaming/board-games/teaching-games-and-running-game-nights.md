# Board games — teaching games and running game nights

**Goal:** get the right game to the table, teach it in ten minutes so
everyone can play their first game meaningfully, and keep the evening
fun — plus the cross-genre strategy that helps you win more.

## 1. Choosing the game
- **Player count:** the box range is the *possible* range; the
  "best at" count is often narrower. BoardGameGeek (BGG) lists community
  votes for best/recommended player counts — check it.
- **Weight (complexity):** BGG's 1-5 weight scale is a good guide; for
  mixed groups stay around 1.5-2.5. A new player's first heavy game is
  where game nights lose people.
- **Length:** box times assume experienced players; add about 50% for a
  first play, more if anyone is prone to slow turns.
- Have a short **filler** (10-20 min) for while people arrive and for the
  end of the night.
- Co-operative games are a good bridge for competitive-shy players, but
  watch for the "alpha player" who runs everyone's turns.

## 2. Teaching order (the one that works)
1. **Theme and goal in one sentence:** "We're rival merchants; most
   victory points after 8 rounds wins."
2. **How you win / how the game ends.** Players need the goal before the
   details make sense.
3. **Turn structure:** what you do on a turn, in order.
4. **The main actions**, with the components in hand.
5. **Scoring** — show where points come from.
6. **Critical edge rules only** ("you can't take the same action twice").
   Leave rare exceptions until they come up.
7. **One strategic hint** per player ("food matters early in this one").
Then start; answer questions as they come. Aim for 10-15 minutes.
Learn the rules *before* the night, set up the board before people
arrive, and play a solo practice turn if it's new to you.

## 3. Strategy that transfers between games
- **Early game: invest in your engine; late game: convert to points.**
  In engine-builders and deck-builders, count how many turns remain and
  stop investing when the payoff won't come in time.
- **Efficiency:** compare actions by points (or resources) per action.
- **Worker placement:** take the spot that's most valuable *to the
  opponents* when it's close; watch what others need.
- **Deck-building:** thin weak starting cards; don't buy everything.
- **Read the scoreboard and others' tableaus** — mid-game is when to
  block the leader.
- After the game, ask the winner what they did — fastest way to improve.

## 4. Keeping the night fun
- **Analysis paralysis:** suggest planning during others' turns; use a
  sand timer only if the group agrees.
- **Kingmaking** (a losing player deciding who wins) is part of some
  games; agree as a group whether to play for second place or not.
- Rules disputes: check the rulebook index, then the publisher's FAQ or
  BGG rules forum; if unresolved, make a table ruling, apply it
  consistently for the rest of the game and look it up later.
- Rotate who picks the game.
- Let newcomers keep their hand of cards private but invite questions —
  playing their turn for them kills the fun.

## Gotchas
- Teaching every exception up front: players remember nothing.
- Playing the heaviest game you own with first-timers.
- Not counting the final rounds: you keep building an engine that never fires.

## Verify
- [ ] The game's best player count and weight suited the group.
- [ ] The teach started with goal and end condition and took under ~15 minutes.
- [ ] Every player scored points from more than one source in their first game.
- [ ] Any rules dispute was settled once and applied consistently.
