# Trading card games — deck building, metagame and limited play

**Goal:** build decks that do the same strong thing most games, choose a
deck that is well-positioned in the current field, and draft well in
limited formats. Examples use Magic: The Gathering (MTG) and the Pokémon
TCG; the principles carry to Yu-Gi-Oh!, Lorcana and others. Card-specific
legality changes constantly — always check the current format banned list.

## 1. Know the format
- **Constructed** (bring your own deck): MTG Standard/Pioneer/Modern etc.
  use 60-card minimum decks, up to 4 copies of a card (basic lands
  exempt), and a 15-card sideboard. Pokémon: exactly 60 cards, max 4
  copies of a card by name (basic Energy exempt), no sideboard in
  standard play.
- **Limited** (build from packs opened at the event): MTG booster draft
  and sealed use 40-card minimum decks.
- Formats rotate; check legal sets before buying.

## 2. Consistency: curve and counts
- **Mana curve (MTG):** count cards by cost. A typical aggressive deck
  has many 1-2 drops and few above 4; control decks play more expensive
  cards and more lands. Rough starting points many players use: about 24
  lands in 60-card decks (fewer for very low curves), about 17 in 40-card
  limited decks — then adjust by curve and card draw.
- **Pokémon:** consistency comes from Supporters/Items that search and
  draw; a deck with too few search cards bricks. Count basic Pokémon so
  you rarely mulligan.
- **Hypergeometric odds:** the chance of drawing at least one copy of a
  card by a given turn. With 4 copies in 60 cards, the chance to see at
  least one in an opening 7 is roughly 40%; with 8 functionally similar
  cards, roughly 65%. Online hypergeometric calculators make this exact —
  use them to decide counts, not gut feel.
- **4-of your key cards;** trim situational cards to 1-2 or sideboard.

## 3. Reading the metagame
- The **meta** = what decks the field will play. Sources: recent large
  tournament results, deck-share data sites, your local store's regulars.
- Pick a deck that is good against the top 2-3 decks, or the top deck
  itself if it's dominant and you know it well. Familiarity with a deck
  is often worth more than a few percent of matchup edge.
- Expect your local event to lag the online meta.

## 4. Sideboarding (MTG and other best-of-3 formats)
- Board against what the opponent's deck *does* (graveyard, artifacts,
  flyers, combo pieces), not against single cards you saw.
- Cut cards that are bad in the matchup 1-for-1; keep the curve and land
  count intact.
- On the draw vs on the play matters: some players cut expensive cards on
  the play against aggro.

## 5. Booster draft (MTG limited)
- Early picks: **power and removal** over colour commitment.
- Watch what's **flowing**: strong cards in a colour arriving late (picks
  6-9) signal that colour is open upstream.
- Settle into 2 colours by mid-pack 2; aim for ~15-17 creatures in many
  formats and a good curve.
- Cheap removal and evasive creatures win most limited games.

## 6. Playing
- **Mulligan** hands that can't function (no lands, all lands, no basic in
  Pokémon is a forced mulligan by rule).
- Think in **card advantage** (trading 1 card for 2) and **tempo**
  (using mana efficiently to pressure).
- Play-test: 20+ games before an event, logging wins/losses by matchup.

## Gotchas
- Building with too many "fun 1-ofs": inconsistent.
- Net-decking a list without learning its sideboard plan.
- Ignoring rotation/ban announcements before buying cards.

## Verify
- [ ] The deck is legal in the current format (count, copy limits, banned list).
- [ ] Key-card odds were checked with a hypergeometric calculator.
- [ ] You can name the top three meta decks and your plan against each.
- [ ] You have a written sideboard plan per matchup (best-of-3 formats).
