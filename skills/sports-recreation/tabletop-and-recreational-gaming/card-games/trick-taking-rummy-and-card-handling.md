# Card games — trick-taking, rummy and card handling

**Goal:** play traditional card games well at the kitchen table: the
handful of principles behind most trick-taking, rummy and shedding games,
and clean shuffling and dealing. Bridge as a competitive mind sport is in
`sports-recreation.mind-sports-and-games.bridge`; poker in
`sports-recreation.mind-sports-and-games.poker`.

## 1. Trick-taking games (whist, hearts, spades, euchre)
Common rule core: lead any card; others **must follow suit** if able;
the highest card of the led suit wins unless a **trump** is played.
- **Count the suits.** Track how many cards of each suit have gone
  (13 per suit in a 52-card deck). When a suit has run out for an opponent
  (they discarded on it), they can trump it next time.
- **Count high cards:** note when the ace and king of each suit have
  appeared; your queen may now be the winner.
- **Lead from strength** to set up a long suit; lead *through* a strong
  hand (so that opponent must play before your partner, who can then
  finesse or play low).
- **Second hand low, third hand high** — a classic rule of thumb for
  partnership games: the second player usually ducks; the partner in
  third seat plays high to win or force out a big card.
- **Void yourself** in a short suit early when trumps are good, so you
  can ruff (trump) later.
- **Hearts:** avoid taking hearts and the queen of spades; pass dangerous
  high spades if you lack low ones; watch for someone "shooting the moon".
- **Spades/euchre:** bid what your hand realistically takes; overtricks
  ("bags") in spades accumulate penalties.

## 2. Rummy family (rummy, gin, canasta)
Goal: form **melds** — sets (same rank) and runs (consecutive same suit).
- **Watch the discard pile:** what opponents pick up tells you what they
  collect; don't discard into it.
- Discard **high deadwood early** (unmatched high cards cost most if
  someone goes out).
- Keep **flexible cards** — a 7 of hearts that fits both a run and a set
  beats an isolated king.
- **Gin rummy:** knock early when your deadwood is low and the stock
  is still deep; late in the hand, opponents are close to gin, so play for
  safety. Count deadwood exactly before you knock.
- Canasta and other partnership rummies: coordinate freezing the discard
  pile; rules vary heavily by region — agree on them first.

## 3. Shedding games (crazy eights, Uno-style)
- Keep wild/special cards for the end or to stop a player about to go
  out.
- Change suit to one you hold many of and others appear short in.
- Track how many cards each player holds; attack the closest to winning.

## 4. Handling the cards
- **Riffle shuffle** about seven times for a well-mixed 52-card deck
  (a widely cited mathematical result for riffle shuffles); overhand
  shuffles alone mix poorly.
- Deal clockwise one card at a time (unless the game specifies batches),
  starting left of the dealer; the player to the dealer's right cuts.
- Keep cards held so no faces show; sort by suit and alternate colours.
- A misdeal (card exposed, wrong count) → redeal; agree this upfront.

## 5. House rules
- State house rules **before** the first deal (e.g. stacking draw cards,
  scoring variants). Most arguments at family tables are unannounced
  house rules.
- Write the scoring down; use a pad, not memory.

## Gotchas
- Forgetting to follow suit (a revoke) — penalised in many games.
- Discarding the card the next player obviously wants.
- Assuming your family's rules are the official ones.

## Verify
- [ ] You can say how many trumps are still out at any point in a trick-taking hand.
- [ ] In rummy you discarded high deadwood early and kept flexible cards.
- [ ] The deck was riffle-shuffled several times and cut.
- [ ] House rules were announced before play.
