# Mobility and flexibility — stretching methods, CARs and hip/shoulder routines

**Goal:** gain usable range of motion for your sport or lifts (a deep squat,
overhead position, splits) and keep it, with methods matched to the goal and
time of day. *Flexibility* = passive range; *mobility* = range you can actively
control.

## 1. Methods and where they fit
| Method | What | Best for | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| Static stretching | hold a lengthened position 20–60 s+ | long-term passive range | after training, separate sessions |
| Dynamic stretching | controlled swings and movements through range | warm-ups | before training |
| PNF (contract–relax) | stretch, contract the stretched muscle ~5–10 s, relax, go deeper | fast range gains | after training |
| Loaded stretching | resistance in the stretched position (e.g. deep RDLs, Jefferson curls with light load, deficit push-ups) | strength at end range | as training |
| CARs (controlled articular rotations) | slow, maximal-range, tension-held circles of a joint | joint health, daily check | daily, warm-ups |
| End-range isometrics (e.g. PAILs/RAILs-style) | contract into and out of a stretch at end range | active control | dedicated sessions |
- Long static holds right before maximal strength/power work can slightly
  reduce performance; short holds (<30–60 s per muscle) within a dynamic warm-up
  have little effect.
- **Total time** per week in a stretch matters more than the exact method for
  passive range — e.g. several minutes per muscle group per week, most days.

## 2. Warm-up template (RAMP)
**R**aise (light cardio 3–5 min) → **A**ctivate (glute bridges, band pull-aparts)
→ **M**obilise (dynamic stretches, CARs for the joints in use) → **P**otentiate
(build-up sets or sprints specific to the session). 10–15 minutes.

## 3. Hip routine (squat, running, martial arts)
- **Hip CARs**: standing, big slow circles of the knee, 3 each direction.
- **90/90 transitions**: sit with both knees at 90°, rotate side to side; add
  lean-forward holds.
- **Couch stretch** (hip flexors/quads): back knee against a wall, front foot
  forward, squeeze the glute, 60–90 s/side.
- **Hamstrings**: active straight-leg raises, then loaded **RDL** for range.
- **Deep squat hold**: 2–5 min accumulated a day, holding a support if needed.
- **Adductors**: Cossack squats, frog stretch.

## 4. Ankle dorsiflexion (squats, landing)
- **Knee-to-wall test:** foot facing a wall, knee drives forward to touch it
  with the heel down; measure toe-to-wall distance. Under ~10 cm is often
  considered restricted (rough guide).
- Drills: weighted knee-over-toe rocks, calf stretching (straight and bent knee),
  heel-elevated squats as a temporary aid.

## 5. Shoulder and thoracic routine (overhead lifts, swimming, climbing)
- **Shoulder CARs**, **wall slides**, **band dislocates** (wide grip, narrow over time).
- **Thoracic rotation**: open books on the floor; thoracic extension over a foam roller.
- **Lat stretch**: hands on a bench, sit back (overhead range is often limited by lats).
- Test: lying on your back, arms overhead — can they reach the floor with the
  lower back flat?

## 6. Splits and advanced range
Front/middle splits: 3–5 sessions/week, combining PNF and loaded work; most
adults need many months; don't force pain in the groin or behind the knee.

## 7. Foam rolling and myths
Foam rolling gives short-term range and comfort but doesn't "break up" tissue.
"Tightness" is often protective; strength through range helps more than passive
stretching alone. Sharp, nervy or joint pain = stop and get assessed.

## Verify
- [ ] Method chosen to match the goal (warm-up vs long-term range vs control).
- [ ] Baseline tests recorded (knee-to-wall, overhead reach, sit-and-reach or squat depth).
- [ ] Weekly time per target area adds up to several minutes on most days.
- [ ] End-range strength (loaded stretching) included.
- [ ] Retest every 4–6 weeks.
