# Bodybuilding — hypertrophy training, contest prep and posing

**Goal:** build muscle in proportion across the off-season, diet down to
stage condition without losing that muscle, and present it well on stage —
posing wins and loses close placings. Applies to natural/tested and untested
federations (IFBB/NPC, WNBF, national bodies); divisions differ.

## 1. Hypertrophy training
- **Effort:** most working sets within ~0–3 reps of failure; the last reps drive growth.
- **Volume:** roughly 10–20 hard sets per muscle per week, split over 2+ sessions
  (see sports-recreation.strength-and-fitness-training.strength-programming.progressive-overload-rpe-and-programme-design).
- **Range of motion:** full ROM with emphasis on the **stretched position**
  (e.g. deep RDLs, incline curls, overhead extensions); lengthened partials as
  an intensity tool.
- **Exercise choice:** stable machines and cables let you push close to failure
  safely; keep favourites for a block and progress load/reps.
- **Proportion:** assess photos (front, side, back) every 4–8 weeks; prioritise
  lagging parts (more sets, first in the session).

## 2. Off-season (gaining)
- A modest **calorie surplus** (often ~5–10 % above maintenance, weight gain
  ≈ 0.25–0.5 % bodyweight/month for intermediates — less fat gain than a "dirty bulk").
- **Protein** ~1.6–2.2 g/kg/day across the year.
- Stay within a body-fat range that keeps the next prep reasonable.

## 3. Contest prep
- **Length:** typically **16–24+ weeks** for natural competitors, depending
  on starting condition. Start earlier than you think.
- **Rate of loss:** ~**0.5–1 % bodyweight per week**; slower late in prep to
  preserve muscle.
- Keep training heavy (intensity maintained), reduce volume only if recovery fails.
- Increase steps/cardio gradually rather than slashing food.
- Diet breaks or refeeds can help adherence; evidence on metabolic benefits is mixed.
- Track: weekly average weight, photos in the same light and poses, gym
  performance. Expect low energy, libido and mood late in prep — tell the
  people around you.

## 4. Peak week — do less, not more
Manipulating carbs, water and sodium in the final week is common, but drastic
**water cuts and diuretics are dangerous** (cramps, arrhythmias, fainting) and
banned in tested federations. Practise any carb-load beforehand in a "mock peak".
Most competitors look best when the final week changes little.

## 5. Posing
- **Mandatory poses** (men's bodybuilding, typical list): front double biceps,
  front lat spread, side chest, back double biceps, back lat spread, side triceps,
  abdominals and thighs, (most muscular in some federations). **Classic physique**
  adds a favourite classic pose (vacuum common); **men's physique**, **bikini**,
  **figure**, **wellness** use **quarter turns** (front, side, back, side) and
  a walk — check your federation's division rules.
- **Practice** daily for 8–12 weeks before the show: holding poses for 30–60 s
  builds the conditioning to hold them tight on stage. Film in stage-like light.
- **Transitions** count: smooth, controlled, smiling; stay "on" even when not
  being compared.
- **Individual routine** (free posing) to music in some divisions — 60–90 s,
  choreographed to show strengths.

## 6. Show day
Tanning (multiple coats, competition tan from approved providers), suit/trunks
to federation rules, pump-up with bands before going on, small snacks and
sips of water as practised.

## Verify
- [ ] Hard sets per muscle tracked; lagging parts get priority.
- [ ] Off-season surplus is modest; weight gain rate tracked.
- [ ] Prep start date gives enough weeks at ≤1 %/week loss.
- [ ] No diuretics or severe water cuts in the peak plan.
- [ ] Poses and quarter turns rehearsed daily for 8+ weeks, filmed.
