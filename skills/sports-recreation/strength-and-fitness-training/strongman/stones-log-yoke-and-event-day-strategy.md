# Strongman — stones, log, yoke and event-day strategy

**Goal:** handle the odd, heavy implements of strongman safely and
efficiently, and score points on the day — strongman is won on events you
don't lose badly, not only on the ones you win. A base of squat, deadlift and
pressing strength comes first (see sports-recreation.strength-and-fitness-training.powerlifting.squat-bench-deadlift-technique-and-attempt-selection).

## 1. Atlas stones
- **Tacky** (pine-resin compound) on forearms and hands — check whether the
  competition allows it; clean stones afterwards.
- **Setup:** stone between the feet, feet wide, round the back over the stone
  (stones are lifted with a rounded back — the one place it's normal), hands
  under and around it.
- **Lap:** deadlift the stone into the lap (onto the thighs, squat position),
  regrip higher up and around.
- **Load:** extend the hips explosively into the platform/barrel and
  "triple-extend" the stone up and over, or to the shoulder for stone-to-shoulder.
- Gotcha: biceps tears happen when the arms curl a stone with straight-ish
  elbows under load — keep the arms wrapped, hips doing the work. Build with
  light stones for reps.

## 2. Log clean and press
- **Clean:** pull the log to the belt/stomach (a "roll"), then roll it up the
  torso to the shoulders, elbows driving under. Some lifters clean in one motion
  at light weights.
- **Press:** dip and drive (push press) or a split/push jerk; the log's neutral
  handles shift the bar path forward — lean back slightly and drive the head through.
- Belts restrict the clean — many lifters wear it lower or loosen for log.

## 3. Axle and circus dumbbell
- **Axle** (thick bar, ~5 cm) — **continental** clean: pull to the belt, rest,
  then roll up to the front rack; no hook grip possible, grip limits the deadlift.
- **Circus dumbbell:** one-hand clean (often both hands to the shoulder), then
  press — exaggerated lean away.

## 4. Carries and moving events
- **Yoke:** get under it like a squat, brace hard, take quick, short steps,
  keep the hips under you; the yoke swings if steps are long. Pick-and-drop
  practice for turns if allowed.
- **Farmer's walk:** deadlift the handles, short fast steps, shoulders packed,
  eyes ahead; use straps in training sparingly — grip *is* the event.
- **Frame carry**, **sandbag carry**, **truck/vehicle pulls** (harness, low
  angle, hand-over-hand rope pulls), **tyre flips** (push into the tyre with the
  chest and hips; never curl it with straight arms).
- **Medleys** (several implements in a row): practise the transitions — they're
  where time is lost.

## 5. Training without a full gym
- Event day once a week: 2–3 events, practise competition weights or
  close to them; the rest of the week is barbell strength + conditioning.
- Substitutes: trap-bar for farmer's, heavy sandbags for stones, safety-squat-bar
  walkouts for yoke, axle or thick-grip handles on a barbell.
- Event weeks are hard on the body — reduce barbell volume around them.

## 6. Event-day strategy
- **Read the event list and scoring early**: max weight vs max reps vs time;
  points per placing (often descending by placing), tie-breaks.
- **Max reps in 60 s:** pace — don't sprint the first reps and fail early; know the
  standard (e.g. lockout criteria, down command) and get the judge's attention.
- **Max weight events:** choose a safe opener; skip attempts strategically to
  save energy.
- **Moving events:** plan any drops/pick-ups; tacky and chalk as allowed.
- Between events: eat, hydrate, keep warm. Watch other competitors' scores — sometimes
  one more rep is all the placing needs.
- Weight classes and novice/masters divisions exist in most federations.

## Verify
- [ ] Stones: lap and load technique practised with light stones; arms not curling.
- [ ] Log: clean method chosen and repeatable.
- [ ] Carries: short, quick steps; braced, no swinging.
- [ ] Each event's standard and scoring read before the show.
- [ ] Pacing plan written for each max-rep event.
