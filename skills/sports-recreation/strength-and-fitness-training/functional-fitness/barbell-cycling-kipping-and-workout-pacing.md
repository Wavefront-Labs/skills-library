# Functional fitness — barbell cycling, kipping and workout pacing

**Goal:** do mixed-modal workouts (weightlifting + gymnastics + monostructural
cardio) fast and safely: scale correctly, move efficiently, and pace so you
don't blow up halfway. Applies to CrossFit-style classes, the Open, HYROX-type
races and garage-gym training.

## 1. Workout formats
- **For time:** complete the work as fast as possible (often with a time cap).
- **AMRAP:** as many rounds and reps as possible in a fixed time.
- **EMOM:** a set of work every minute on the minute; the rest is what's left.
- **Chipper:** a long list of movements done once through.
- **Intervals:** work/rest set by the coach.
Benchmark workouts (e.g. "Fran": 21-15-9 thrusters and pull-ups) let you
measure progress.

## 2. Scaling — the most important skill
- Scale **load, reps, movement** or **range** so the workout keeps its intended
  **stimulus** (e.g. Fran is meant to be a short, very intense effort — if it takes
  you 15 minutes at Rx weight, scale).
- Common scales: lighter bar; ring rows or banded/jumping pull-ups instead of
  kipping; step-ups for box jumps; single-unders (×2 or ×3) for double-unders;
  pike or box HSPU for handstand push-ups.
- **Rx** only when you can hold good positions for every rep while fatigued.

## 3. Barbell cycling
- **Touch-and-go:** keep tension, let the bar descend under control and rebound
  lightly off the floor — fast for deadlifts and light cleans. Never bounce heavy.
- **Power clean cycling:** drop under minimally, keep the bar close, hips
  through; at moderate weights, quick singles (drop, reset fast) can be faster
  overall than grinding touch-and-go reps — choose by load.
- **Thrusters:** front squat into a push press in one motion; drive with the
  legs, elbows up, breathe at the top lockout.
- **Wall balls:** squat to depth, drive the ball with the legs to the target,
  catch and absorb into the next squat.

## 4. Gymnastics progressions
- **Kipping pull-ups:** learn the **kip swing** (hollow ↔ arch) on the bar first.
  Prerequisite: several strict pull-ups and shoulder stability; kipping without
  strict strength strains the shoulders. Then butterfly pull-ups.
- **Toes-to-bar:** kip swing + hip flexion, close the hip then extend.
- **Handstand push-ups:** strict first (or pike/box), then kipping from a tripod
  head position with a padded surface.
- **Muscle-ups (bar, then ring):** strong strict pull-ups and dips + the
  transition drill; false grip for strict ring muscle-ups.
- **Double-unders:** wrists (not arms) turn the rope, small bounce, rope sized
  to your height; single-single-double drills.

## 5. Pacing and break strategy
- Pick a **sustainable** pace for the first minutes — if you feel great at 2 min
  in a 20-min AMRAP, that's right.
- **Break early, rest short:** e.g. 21 pull-ups as 8-7-6 with 5-second breaks beats
  15 unbroken then singles. Plan the breaks before the clock starts.
- Use the cardio (row/bike/run) to recover breathing slightly — but keep moving.
- Transitions are free time: walk to the next station with purpose, chalk once.
- Count reps aloud or use a tally.
- Grip (pull-ups, toes-to-bar) and shoulders fatigue first — split those sets more.

## 6. Safety
- **Exertional rhabdomyolysis** is a real risk, especially for newcomers doing
  high-rep eccentric work (pull-ups, GHD sit-ups, jumping lunges). Symptoms:
  severe muscle pain and swelling, weakness, **dark (cola-coloured) urine** →
  urgent medical care. Introduce volume gradually.
- Technique over time — fatigue is when injuries happen.

## Verify
- [ ] Workout scaled to keep the intended stimulus and time domain.
- [ ] Strict pull-up and shoulder base before kipping.
- [ ] Break plan written for the high-rep movements.
- [ ] Barbell: TNG vs singles chosen by load.
- [ ] Newcomers on reduced volume; rhabdo symptoms known.
