# Personal training — client assessment, programme individualisation and cueing

**Goal:** take a new client from first contact to steady progress: screen them
safely, find out what they actually want, build a plan that fits their body
and life, coach movement clearly, and keep them coming back. Programme design
mechanics are in sports-recreation.strength-and-fitness-training.strength-programming.progressive-overload-rpe-and-programme-design.

## 1. Intake and screening
- **PAR-Q+** (Physical Activity Readiness Questionnaire) or your certifying
  body's equivalent, signed. Any "yes" answers → follow its follow-up questions
  and, where indicated, **medical clearance** before vigorous training.
- History: injuries, surgeries, medications (e.g. beta-blockers blunt heart
  rate — RPE beats HR zones for them), pregnancy/postpartum, current activity.
- **Scope of practice:** trainers don't diagnose injuries, prescribe diets for
  medical conditions or treat pain. Refer to a doctor, physiotherapist or
  dietitian, and work alongside them.
- Consent, emergency contact, and where the AED and first-aid kit are.

## 2. Goals that mean something
- Ask "why" until you get to the real goal ("play with my kids without back
  pain", "first pull-up", "lose fat for health"), then turn it into a
  measurable target and timeline (SMART goals).
- Agree process goals too (3 sessions/week, 8,000 steps, protein at each meal) —
  clients control those; outcomes follow.

## 3. Assessment
- **Movement:** bodyweight squat, hinge, lunge, push-up, overhead reach,
  single-leg balance. Note what you see (e.g. heels lifting, knee valgus, low back
  rounding), not labels.
- **Baselines:** e.g. push-ups to form failure, 3–5RM goblet squat or trap-bar,
  plank, a walk/run or bike test, resting HR, girths/photos/weight if relevant and
  wanted (some clients prefer no scale).
- Keep session one achievable — the client should leave feeling capable.

## 4. Individualising the programme
- Start from patterns (squat, hinge, push, pull, carry, core) and pick the
  **variation** that suits the client today — e.g. box squat or goblet squat
  before barbell back squat; incline push-up before floor push-up.
- **Regress** when form breaks or pain appears; **progress** when they own it.
- Fit the life: 2 sessions + 1 homework session beats a 5-day plan they won't do.
- Manage load around stress, sleep, menstrual cycle symptoms, shift work.
- Re-assess every 6–8 weeks and show the client their progress.

## 5. Coaching cues
- **One cue at a time.** Pick the fault that matters most.
- **External cues** (focus on the environment/outcome — "push the floor away",
  "drive the bar to the ceiling") generally beat **internal** ones ("contract your
  quads") for performance; use internal cues sparingly for awareness.
- **Show → do → feedback:** demonstrate, let them try, then praise one thing
  and correct one thing ("sandwich" feedback, specific not generic).
- Use tactile cues only with consent and explanation.
- Reduce feedback over time so clients learn to self-correct.

## 6. Session structure (60 min)
Check-in (sleep, soreness, pain, mood) → warm-up (10) → main strength (25–30)
→ accessories/conditioning (15) → cool-down and a homework/habit target (5).
Log everything.

## 7. Adherence and retention
- Early wins and visible progress matter more than perfect programming.
- **Motivational-interviewing** style: open questions, reflect back, let the
  client say why change matters to them.
- Plan for obstacles (travel, holidays) with short home sessions.
- Reliability, punctuality and clear cancellation policy keep a business running.

## 8. Professional standards
Hold a recognised certification (national registers or bodies such as NASM,
ACE, NSCA, REPs-equivalents), first aid/CPR, insurance; keep up CPD.

## Verify
- [ ] PAR-Q+ (or equivalent) signed; any red flags cleared.
- [ ] Goal written in measurable form, plus process goals.
- [ ] Baseline assessment recorded; re-test date set.
- [ ] Each exercise has a regression and progression ready.
- [ ] Cues are external and one at a time; feedback is specific.
