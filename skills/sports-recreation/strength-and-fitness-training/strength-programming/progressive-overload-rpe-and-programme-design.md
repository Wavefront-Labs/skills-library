# Strength programming — progressive overload, RPE and programme design

**Goal:** write a resistance-training plan that keeps you getting stronger
for months, using a few clear variables, instead of random workouts. For
how blocks of training fit across a season see
sports-recreation.strength-and-fitness-training.periodisation.mesocycles-deloads-and-peaking.

## 1. Start from the goal
| Goal | Typical reps | Load | Rest |
|---|---|---|---|
| Max strength | 1–6 | heavy (~80–95 % 1RM) | 3–5 min |
| Hypertrophy (muscle size) | ~6–15 (up to 30 near failure works) | moderate | 1.5–3 min |
| Muscular endurance | 15+ | light | short |
| Power | 1–5 fast reps | light–moderate, max speed | full |
In practice, most plans blend these: heavy main lifts + moderate-rep accessories.

## 2. Exercise selection
- **Main lifts (compound):** a squat, a hinge (deadlift/RDL), a horizontal press
  (bench), a vertical press (overhead), a horizontal pull (row), a vertical pull
  (pull-up/pulldown). Plus a carry or core work.
- **Accessories** fix weak points or add volume (lunges, dips, curls, face pulls,
  hamstring curls).
- Pick variations you can load safely and progress. Keep the main lifts stable
  for at least a training block so progress is measurable.

## 3. Volume, intensity, frequency
- **Volume:** count **hard sets** per muscle per week (sets taken within ~0–4
  reps of failure). Many lifters grow on roughly **10–20** hard sets per muscle
  per week; beginners need less. Start low and add.
- **Intensity:** % of 1RM or effort (RPE).
- **Frequency:** training each muscle/lift **2–3×/week** usually beats once a
  week at equal volume.

## 4. RPE and RIR
- **RIR** = reps in reserve. **RPE 10** = no more reps possible; **RPE 8** ≈ 2 RIR.
- Prescribe e.g. `3 × 5 @ RPE 8` — the load auto-adjusts to the day.
- Beginners misjudge RIR (usually underestimate how many reps remain) — film
  sets or occasionally take an accessory set to failure to calibrate.
- **Percentages** need a recent 1RM or an **estimated 1RM** (e.g. Epley:
  e1RM ≈ weight × (1 + reps/30)); less accurate above ~10 reps.

## 5. Progression schemes
- **Linear progression (novices):** add a small fixed load each session
  (e.g. 2.5 kg on squats, 1–2.5 kg on presses) while sets of 5 are completed.
  Stall twice → reduce ~10 % and build back, or move to weekly progression.
- **Double progression:** a rep range, e.g. 3 × 8–12. When all sets hit 12,
  raise the load and start again at 8. Great for accessories.
- **Weekly/wave progression (intermediates):** add load week to week across a
  3–6-week block (e.g. 5/3/1-style waves), then deload.
- **Track everything:** a training log (paper or app) with load, reps, RPE.
  If numbers don't rise over 4–6 weeks, change one variable.

## 6. Splits
- **Full body 3×/week** (ideal for beginners).
- **Upper/lower 4×/week.**
- **Push/pull/legs** 3–6×/week.
Choose by how many days you will *reliably* train.

## 7. Example novice week (full body, 3 days)
- A: Squat 3×5, Bench 3×5, Row 3×8–12, Plank.
- B: Deadlift 1×5 (or RDL 3×8), Overhead press 3×5, Pull-ups 3×AMRAP-2, Lunges 2×10.
Alternate A/B; linear progression on the 3×5 lifts.

## Gotchas
- Too much volume too soon → aching joints, stalled lifts.
- Program hopping every two weeks prevents measurable progress.
- Technique breaking down at the top end = load too high for now.
- Sleep, protein and calories limit progress more than the "perfect" programme.

## Verify
- [ ] Goal and rep ranges match.
- [ ] Each main movement pattern covered at least twice a week.
- [ ] Weekly hard sets per muscle counted.
- [ ] Each lift has a defined progression rule and stall rule.
- [ ] Log shows load or reps rising over 4–6 weeks.
