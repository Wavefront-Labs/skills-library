# Periodisation — mesocycles, deloads and peaking

**Goal:** organise training over weeks and months so fitness accumulates,
fatigue is cleared before it becomes injury or burnout, and the athlete is at
their best on the days that matter. Applies to strength sports and to the
gym side of field, court and endurance sports. Session-level programme design
is in sports-recreation.strength-and-fitness-training.strength-programming.progressive-overload-rpe-and-programme-design.

## 1. Time scales
- **Macrocycle:** the season or year (or a 4-year Olympic cycle).
- **Mesocycle (block):** usually **3–6 weeks** focused on one emphasis.
- **Microcycle:** usually one week — the repeatable pattern of sessions.
Start planning **backwards** from the competition date(s).

## 2. Models
| Model | Idea | Fits |
|---|---|---|
| Linear (traditional) | volume high → intensity rises over the macrocycle | novices, single-peak sports |
| Daily/weekly undulating (DUP) | vary reps/intensity within the week (heavy/medium/light days) | intermediate strength, hypertrophy |
| Block (accumulation → transmutation → realisation) | concentrated blocks: high volume general work → specific/intensity → peak | advanced athletes, multiple peaks |
| Conjugate | train several qualities concurrently with rotating max-effort exercises | powerlifting gyms (Westside-style) |
No model is magic; consistency and fatigue management matter more than the label.

## 3. Deloads
- **Purpose:** drop accumulated fatigue while keeping fitness.
- **How:** commonly cut **volume ~30–50 %** (fewer sets) while keeping intensity
  moderate-to-high, for about **one week**.
- **When:** planned every 4–8 weeks (often the last week of a block), or
  reactive when: performance falls for 2+ sessions, joints ache, sleep and
  motivation drop, RPE climbs at the same loads.
- Beginners on linear progression rarely need planned deloads; intermediates
  and older athletes usually do.

## 4. Peaking and tapering
- Strength sports: in the last **1–3 weeks**, reduce volume substantially while
  keeping or increasing intensity — heavy singles at RPE 7–9, then very light
  work in the final days. Openers practised in the last heavy session.
- Endurance/team sports: **taper** of about 1–3 weeks with volume reduced
  ~40–60 %, intensity maintained, frequency mostly maintained.
- Don't test anything new in the taper — no new lifts, shoes, or nutrition.

## 5. In-season (team/field sports)
- Goal is **maintenance**: 1–2 short, intense strength sessions per week,
  scheduled away from match day (e.g. not within ~48 h before a game).
- Reduce volume, keep intensity; avoid soreness-inducing novelty
  (e.g. new eccentric-heavy work) mid-season.
- Off-season and pre-season carry the hypertrophy and big-strength blocks.

## 6. Monitoring load and fatigue
- **Session RPE (sRPE):** session RPE (0–10) × duration (min) = training load.
  Track weekly totals and watch for sudden spikes.
- **Acute:chronic workload ratio** (last week vs 4-week average) is widely
  used; spikes well above the recent norm have been associated with injury risk,
  though the metric's validity is debated — use it as a prompt to talk, not a rule.
- Wellness questionnaires (sleep, soreness, mood), jump tests (CMJ), bar-speed
  tracking and HRV as readiness signals.
- **Functional overreaching** (short dip then rebound) is OK; persistent
  performance decline with mood/sleep disturbance suggests non-functional
  overreaching/overtraining → reduce load, investigate (including health and
  nutrition — see health-medicine.nutrition-and-dietetics.sports-nutrition.periodised-fuelling-and-hydration).

## 7. A worked outline (powerlifter, meet in 16 weeks)
Weeks 1–5 hypertrophy/accumulation (sets of 6–10, higher volume) → week 6 deload →
weeks 7–11 strength (3–5 reps, 80–88 %) → week 12 deload → weeks 13–15
peaking (singles/doubles, volume down) → week 16 meet week (openers early,
then rest).

## Verify
- [ ] Plan written backwards from competition date(s).
- [ ] Each block has a single emphasis and an end-of-block deload or test.
- [ ] Deload rule (planned and reactive) is written down.
- [ ] Taper/peak plan cuts volume, keeps intensity.
- [ ] Some load monitoring (sRPE or similar) in place.
