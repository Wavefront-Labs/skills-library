# Endurance and conditioning — heart-rate zones, zone 2 and interval design

**Goal:** build a bigger aerobic engine and the ability to repeat hard efforts,
with a plan that uses the right intensity on the right day. Works on any
modality: running, cycling, rowing, swimming, ski-erg, bike.

## 1. Setting intensity zones
- **Max HR:** formulas (220 − age, or 208 − 0.7 × age) can be off by 10–20 bpm
  for an individual. Better: the highest value seen in a hard field test (e.g.
  after a 3–5 min all-out finish, or race finishes) — only if healthy and cleared.
- **Heart-rate reserve (Karvonen):** target = resting HR + % × (max − resting).
- **Threshold-based (preferred for trained people):** estimate **lactate
  threshold HR** from a 30-min solo time trial — average HR of the last 20 min ≈ LTHR.
  Zones as % of LTHR.
- A common five-zone model:
| Zone | Feel | Approx. |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | very easy, recovery | <~68 % HRmax |
| 2 | easy, conversational ("zone 2") | ~68–80 % HRmax |
| 3 | tempo, "comfortably hard" | ~80–87 % |
| 4 | threshold, hard | ~87–93 % |
| 5 | VO2max and above | >~93 % |
Exact percentages vary between systems — be consistent with one.
- **Talk test:** zone 2 = you can speak in full sentences; threshold = a few
  words; zone 5 = no talking. **RPE** works too and doesn't lag like HR.

## 2. Zone 2 / aerobic base
- Long, easy sessions build mitochondria, capillaries and fat oxidation.
  Most endurance athletes do the majority of their volume here.
- **Cardiac drift:** HR rises over a long steady session at the same pace —
  slow down to stay in the zone or accept it in hot conditions.
- Optical wrist HR can be unreliable in intervals and cold; a **chest strap**
  is more accurate.

## 3. Weekly structure — polarised (80/20)
- Roughly **80 % of sessions easy** (Z1–2), **20 % hard** (Z4–5); minimal "grey
  zone" (Z3) for those who keep doing every session moderately hard.
- For time-crunched general fitness (3 sessions/week): 2 easy + 1 interval, or
  1 easy long + 2 shorter sessions with one hard.

## 4. Designing intervals by goal
| Goal | Example | Work:rest |
|---|---|---|
| VO2max | 4–6 × 3–5 min at Z5 | ~1:1 |
| Threshold | 3 × 10 min or 2 × 20 min at Z4 | ~3–4:1 |
| Tempo | 20–40 min continuous Z3 | — |
| Anaerobic/repeat sprint (team sports) | 8–12 × 15–30 s hard | 1:3–1:6 |
| Short intervals (30/30) | 2–3 sets of 10 × 30 s on/30 s off | 1:1 |
- Progress by adding reps, then duration, before increasing intensity.
- Quality matters: if the last rep collapses, the session was too hard.
- Warm up 10–15 min including some short pick-ups; cool down.

## 5. Conditioning tests
- **Cooper 12-min run** (distance), **1.5-mile/2.4 km run**, **beep test**
  (multistage fitness test), **Yo-Yo intermittent recovery** (team sports),
  2 km row, 20-min FTP test on a bike.
- Retest every 6–8 weeks under the same conditions.

## 6. Mixing with strength training
- High volumes of endurance work can blunt strength/hypertrophy gains (the
  **interference effect**), mostly with lots of running and close scheduling.
- Separate hard lifting and hard intervals by ~6+ hours or on different days;
  cycling/rowing interfere less than running for leg strength.
- Easy zone 2 work has little interference and helps recovery.

## Safety
New exercisers, people with cardiac history or symptoms (chest pain,
breathlessness out of proportion, fainting) should get medical clearance before
maximal tests.

## Verify
- [ ] Zones set from a test (not only an age formula) and written down.
- [ ] ~80 % of weekly sessions are genuinely easy (talk test passes).
- [ ] Each interval session has a clear goal and work:rest ratio.
- [ ] A conditioning test is scheduled every 6–8 weeks.
- [ ] Hard lifting and hard intervals are separated.
