# Adventure and obstacle racing — obstacles, grip and strategy

**Goal:** finish obstacle course races (OCR — Spartan, Tough Mudder-style)
with fewer failed obstacles, and compete in adventure races (multi-day or
one-day multisport with navigation) as an efficient team.

## Part A — Obstacle course racing

### 1. Know the format
- Read the event rules: **penalty** for a failed obstacle (e.g. a burpee
  penalty or penalty loop) vs **mandatory obstacle completion** in elite
  waves (fail = lose your band / DQ). Rules vary by brand and wave.

### 2. Technique on common obstacles
- **Monkey bars / rings / rigs:** keep arms slightly bent, swing from the
  hips, reach on the forward swing, skip bars only if you're strong.
  Chalk isn't allowed in all races; dry your hands on your shirt/legs.
- **Walls (6–8 ft):** run in, plant one foot on the wall (or the kick
  board) at hip height, grab the top with both hands, pull and swing a leg
  over. Taller walls: teammate assists are often allowed in open waves.
- **Rope climb:** use your feet — **J-hook** (rope around one leg, stand on
  it with the other foot) or **S-wrap**. Climbing by arms alone burns out grip.
- **Carries** (bucket, sandbag): hug close to the body; short steps;
  rest by shifting position, not putting it down.
- **Spear throw / balance beams:** practise at home; these are where
  penalties are cheapest to avoid.

### 3. Grip endurance
- Dead hangs: build to 60–90 s+; vary grip (towel, thick bar, rings).
- Farmer carries, pull-ups, and hang-and-swing on monkey bars weekly.
- Train grip **while tired** — race obstacles come after running.

### 4. Running
OCR is mostly running: trail runs with hills, plus intervals. Practise
obstacles at the end of runs.

## Part B — Adventure racing

### 1. Team and navigation
- Teams (often 2–4, sometimes mixed gender required) stay within a set
  distance of each other. The team is only as fast as its slowest member —
  **tow lines** and load sharing help.
- Navigation with map and compass to **checkpoints (CPs)**; GPS usually
  banned except sealed for safety. Assign a lead navigator and a checker.
- Before each leg: plan the route, identify **attack points** (obvious
  feature near the CP) and catching features beyond it.

### 2. Transitions
- Transitions (TA) between disciplines (trek, MTB, paddle) are free time to
  lose. Pack bags per discipline, labelled; rehearse changeovers.
- Eat and drink at every TA; set a timer.

### 3. Pacing and sleep (multi-day)
- Start slower than you think; long races are lost to early burn-out.
- Sleep strategy: short planned sleeps (e.g. 20–90 min) at TAs, often in the
  pre-dawn low.
- Foot care: tape hot spots early, change socks at TAs.

### 4. Mandatory kit
Always carry the race's mandatory kit (first aid, emergency blanket,
headtorch, whistle, waterproof). Kit checks can lead to time penalties.

## Gotchas
- Arm-only rope climbs; wet hands on rigs.
- Ignoring the penalty rules and losing the elite band at obstacle 3.
- Navigator working alone and nobody double-checking.
- **Rule details and obstacle specs differ per brand, event and year.**

## Verify
- [ ] Penalty/obstacle rules read for your wave.
- [ ] Rope climb with J-hook or S-wrap, minimal arm use.
- [ ] Dead hang ≥60 s after a hard run interval.
- [ ] (AR) Route plans with attack points for each CP; TA bags packed and rehearsed.
