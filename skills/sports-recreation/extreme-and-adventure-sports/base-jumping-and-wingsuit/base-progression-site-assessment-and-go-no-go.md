# BASE and wingsuit — progression, site assessment and go/no-go

**Goal:** understand the safe pathway into BASE (Buildings, Antennas,
Spans, Earth) and wingsuit BASE, and the decision-making that separates
long careers from short ones. This is **not** a substitute for a first
jump course with a qualified mentor; BASE has a high fatality rate and
very small error margins. Content here is general and **draft — verify
every figure with an established FJC provider.**

## 1. Prerequisites
- **Skydiving first.** Common FJC entry requirements are on the order of
  **150–200+ skydives**, recent currency, with many deliberate canopy
  accuracy landings and some "hop-and-pop" / low-delay exposure. Some
  providers ask for more.
- **Canopy skill** is the real prerequisite: landing precisely in a small
  area, flying a heading under stress, correcting line twists fast.
- **Wingsuit BASE** typically requires an established BASE log (often
  ~200 BASE jumps) **and** substantial skydiving wingsuit experience
  (often 200+ wingsuit skydives). Proximity flying comes much later.

## 2. First jump course (FJC)
- Typically several days with a recognised instructor: gear (BASE
  container, large docile canopy, larger pilot chute, slider configurations),
  packing your own rig under supervision, then jumps from a forgiving
  object (often a high bridge over water or a big cliff with a clean landing).
- Pack-check by the mentor for early jumps. Never borrow unfamiliar gear.

## 3. The dominant risks
- **Off-heading openings** toward the object: the reason BASE is unforgiving.
  Body position at pilot chute throw, symmetrical harness loading and
  packing quality matter. Know your object-strike avoidance response
  (steer away immediately, rear risers).
- **Low delay / altitude margin:** short objects give seconds; any
  malfunction must be handled on main canopy alone (no reserve on most BASE rigs).
- **Landing areas:** small, sloped, obstructed or windy LZs injure more
  jumpers than exits.
- **Wingsuit:** terrain clearance misjudgement; poor flight plan; flying
  too close too soon.

## 4. Site and object assessment
Before any new site:
- **Height:** measured (laser rangefinder), not guessed. Determine delay
  from height and rig configuration.
- **Exit point:** clear of overhangs (rock drop test — count the time a
  rock takes to fall, and whether it hits anything).
- **Obstacle-free flight path** for the canopy, with an alternate.
- **Landing area:** size, slope, obstacles, wind behaviour; a bail-out option.
- **Access, legality and land manager rules.** Many sites prohibit BASE;
  jumping there can bring arrest and damage access for the community.
- **Rescue:** mobile coverage, distance to help, someone on the ground who knows the plan.
- Local knowledge from experienced jumpers at that site beats online beta.

## 5. Weather and go/no-go
- **Wind:** at exit and at LZ; rotors behind ridges; valley winds change
  through the day. Many jumpers set personal maximums and stick to them.
- **Visibility and light:** fading light hides landing hazards.
- **Human factors:** fatigue, peer pressure, summit fever, "we hiked four
  hours". Walking down is always an option and the best jumpers do it often.
- Write a **personal minimums** card (wind, delay, experience at object
  type) and don't revise it at the exit.

## 6. Wingsuit progression
- Large, simple suits first; long flights over open terrain with huge
  clearance; learn your glide ratio and flare with GPS data.
- Proximity lines only after mapping them with GPS/terrain data and
  building from big-clearance versions.

## Gotchas
- Rushing the skydiving log to qualify faster.
- Assuming a bridge "over water" is safe — water landings drown people.
- Copying someone else's delay on a different rig/object.

## Verify
- [ ] Skydive count, canopy accuracy and currency meet a reputable FJC's standard.
- [ ] Every object height measured and delay planned with margin.
- [ ] Off-heading response rehearsed.
- [ ] Written personal minimums carried and respected.
- [ ] Legal status of the site checked before going.
