# Inline and roller skating — stopping, striding and first tricks

**Goal:** skate confidently with three reliable ways to stop before going
fast, and know which discipline (recreational, slalom, aggressive, derby)
to move into. "Inline" = wheels in one line; "quads" = two pairs, like
traditional roller skates.

## 1. Gear
- Snug skates with good ankle support; heel shouldn't lift.
- **Wheels:** softer (~78–82A) for rough outdoor surfaces, harder
  (~85A+ inline, often higher for rink/aggressive). Bigger inline wheels
  (90–110 mm) roll faster, smaller (≤80 mm) are more agile.
- **Protection:** helmet, **wrist guards** (most common injury is the wrist),
  knee and elbow pads.
- Start on smooth, flat ground: a tennis court, empty car park or rink.

## 2. Ready stance
- Feet hip-width, **knees bent**, weight over the balls of the feet, hands
  forward. Sitting back is the cause of most falls.
- Practise "V-walk": heels together, toes out, small steps forward.

## 3. Falling
- Fall **forward** onto pads: knees first, then elbows, then wrists sliding.
  Get low before you fall.
- Practise kneeling falls at standstill, then slow speed.
- Getting up: kneel on one knee, put the other skate flat between your
  hands, push up.

## 4. Striding
- Push **out to the side and slightly back** with the whole wheel line (not
  the toe), return the foot under the body, glide on the other.
- Fully transfer weight to the gliding skate; small pushes, long glides.

## 5. Stopping — learn three
1. **Heel brake (inline):** scissor the braked skate forward, lift its toe,
   press down the heel pad while keeping knees bent. Most reliable for beginners.
2. **Plough / snowplough stop:** feet wide, toes in, push heels out at
   low speed. Works on inline and quads.
3. **T-stop:** drag the back skate behind, perpendicular, lightly on the
   inside edge. Wears wheels; use moderate pressure.
- Quads: **toe stop** drag or plough; derby skaters learn plough,
  T-stop and later hockey stops/turnaround toe-stops.
- Rule: don't go faster than the speed you can stop from in your planned space.

## 6. Turning
- **A-frame turn:** feet wide, lean and press the outside skate.
- **Crossovers:** step the outside skate over the inside one around a
  curve; builds speed in turns.
- Learn edges: skating on one foot, inside and outside edges.

## 7. Next steps by discipline
- **Freestyle slalom:** cones in a line (often 80 cm spacing for basic
  forwards moves); start with forward cross, snake, two-foot slalom.
- **Aggressive inline:** grind-plate skates, skatepark riding, grinds on ledges/rails.
- **Roller derby:** quad-based contact team sport; leagues run "fresh meat"
  intake programmes teaching falls, stops and minimum skills before contact.
- **Fitness/distance:** larger wheels, long strides, road safety.

## 8. Outdoors
- Rough surfaces, gravel and cracks stop wheels dead — scan ahead.
- Downhills only once heel-brake/plough is solid.
- Skate on paths where allowed; local laws vary for roads.

## Maintenance
Rotate wheels so they wear evenly; keep bearings dry; replace worn heel brakes.

## Gotchas
- Sitting back, straight legs.
- Learning on a slope.
- No wrist guards.
- **Wheel hardness/size and cone spacing are common ranges, not standards.**

## Verify
- [ ] Can stop with at least two methods from jogging speed.
- [ ] Controlled forward fall onto pads.
- [ ] Glides on one foot for 3+ seconds each side.
- [ ] Crossovers on a gentle curve both directions.
