# Parkour and freerunning — vaults, precisions and landings

**Goal:** move efficiently over obstacles while keeping joints healthy for
years. Parkour emphasises efficient movement; freerunning adds expressive
and acrobatic moves. Both rest on the same foundations: landing, rolling
and controlled progression.

## 1. Conditioning first
- Squats, lunges, step-ups and **calf raises** (landings load ankles/calves).
- Push-ups, pull-ups, dips — needed for climb-ups and cat leaps.
- **Quadrupedal movement (QM):** crawl forwards, backwards and sideways on
  hands and feet, knees off the ground; builds shoulders and coordination.
- Train jumps on low obstacles at high volume before height.

## 2. Landing
- **Precision landing:** land on the **balls of the feet**, heels
  not touching hard, knees bent and tracking over toes, absorb quietly.
  Quiet landing = good absorption.
- From drops: absorb through ankles, knees and hips; **never land
  straight-legged**. Above roughly hip-to-shoulder height (depends on you),
  roll instead of sticking it.
- Practise **sticking** precisions: land and hold still without stepping
  or wobbling.

## 3. The parkour roll
- Diagonal across the back: from one shoulder to the opposite hip.
- Crouch, place hands to one side, tuck the head, roll over the shoulder
  (not the top of the head or spine), come up onto feet moving forward.
- Practise on grass/mats, then concrete at low speed. Both sides.
- Purpose: turn vertical drop energy into forward motion.

## 4. Basic vaults
- **Safety vault (step vault):** hand and the same-side foot on the
  obstacle, the other leg passes between. Most controlled; learn first.
- **Speed vault:** one hand on the obstacle, body sideways, legs pass
  over together. Fast for waist-high rails/walls.
- **Lazy vault:** angled approach, hands sequentially, legs swing over.
- **Kong (monkey) vault:** dive forward, both hands on the obstacle,
  tuck knees through between arms. Learn over a low box with a dive-push;
  a common fault is not pushing hard enough, so feet catch.
- Learn vaults on low, solid obstacles and **both sides**.

## 5. Jumps and climbs
- **Cat leap (arm jump):** jump to a wall and hang with hands on top, feet
  on the wall to absorb. Then **climb-up** (pull, press over).
- **Wall run:** step hard into the wall, push up, grab the top.
- **Tic-tac:** kick off a wall to redirect.

## 6. Progression and safety
- **Break the jump:** before a scary jump, do the same distance at ground
  level 10 times, then a slightly higher version, then commit.
- Check every surface: **grip, stability, loose stones, wet**. Test-push walls and rails.
- Train with others; know where first-aid is.
- Gradual volume: shins and Achilles complain when landing volume jumps
  quickly. Rest days matter.
- Don't attempt flips without coaching; use gym/trampoline facilities to learn them.

## 7. Training in public
- Respect property and people; leave if asked; don't damage surfaces.
- Avoid rooftops and drops with uncertain consequences. "Don't attempt it if
  the failure mode is a hospital trip" is a common community rule.
- Many cities have parkour parks and coaches/classes (ADAPT is one
  coaching qualification lineage).

## Gotchas
- Loud, stiff landings; heel strikes.
- Skipping the roll and taking drops on the knees.
- Only training one side.
- Wet concrete and painted surfaces are slippery.
- **Height thresholds for rolling are individual guidance, not hard numbers.**

## Verify
- [ ] Silent stuck precision landings over a known distance.
- [ ] Roll smooth on concrete both sides.
- [ ] Safety, speed and kong vault over a waist-high obstacle.
- [ ] Every new jump broken down at low height first.
