# Orienteering leg technique: attack points and control flow

**Goal:** get round a competitive orienteering course with no big mistakes. Speed comes
from avoiding 3-minute errors far more than from running faster. Basic map-and-compass
skills (contours, setting the map, bearings, pacing) are in
transportation-logistics.navigation-wayfinding.map-compass-land-navigation.terrain-association-and-bearings;
this skill is about applying them under race pressure.

## 1. Know the map and the clues
- Forest maps are usually **1:10,000 or 1:15,000** (ISOM standard); sprint maps
  **1:4,000 or 1:3,000** (ISSprOM). Check the scale on the map each race — legs look
  the same length on paper at different scales.
- Colours: **white = runnable forest**, yellow = open, light → dark green = slower →
  impassable vegetation, blue = water, brown = landforms, black = rock and man-made.
  On sprint maps, **olive green = out of bounds** and crossing it disqualifies you.
- **Control descriptions** (IOF pictorial symbols) tell you exactly *where* on the
  feature the flag is: e.g. boulder, north side; re-entrant, foot. Read the clue before
  you start each leg, not when you arrive.

## 2. Plan every leg with the same four questions
1. **Where exactly is the control** (feature + clue)?
2. **What's my attack point?** A big, unmistakable feature close to the control
   (path bend, lake corner, hill top) — typically within ~100–150 m.
3. **What's my route to the attack point?** Use **handrails** (paths, streams,
   fences, ridges) and compare routes: straight through green vs. longer round a path.
   Rule of thumb on flat ground: a path detour of up to roughly 30–50% extra distance
   is often faster than rough forest (uncertain: depends heavily on terrain and runner).
4. **What's my catching feature?** Something beyond the control that tells you you've overshot.

## 3. Rough and fine — the traffic lights
Split each leg into zones and change speed:
- **Green (rough):** following a handrail or heading for a big feature. Run fast, check
  the map occasionally.
- **Amber:** approaching the attack point. Slow slightly, tick off features.
- **Red (fine):** attack point → control. Slow down, take a compass bearing, pace-count,
  read every contour. Most errors happen when runners stay in green too long.

## 4. Thumbing and compass
- **Fold the map** small and keep your **thumb on your current position**, moving it
  as you pass features. Look down, the thumb shows where you are in a second.
- Keep the map **oriented to north** at all times (a thumb compass makes this a
  continuous habit).
- **Aiming off:** when the control is on a linear feature (stream, fence), aim
  deliberately to one side so you know which way to turn when you hit it.

## 5. Control flow (punching)
On the way into the control, **already look at the next leg** — at least the exit
direction. Punch (SportIdent/emit card in the unit, or electronic contactless: wait for
the beep/flash), check the **control code** on the flag matches your description sheet,
then leave immediately in the planned direction. Standing at the flag reading the map
is time lost on every control.

## 6. When you're lost
Stop. Don't run on hoping. **Orient the map**, then find something unmistakable that you
can see, or go back to the last certain point. Watch for **parallel errors**: a similar
re-entrant or path just beside the right one. Also, never follow other runners — they
may be on a different course.

## Gotchas
- Wrong control code = mispunch = disqualification. Check every time.
- Missing a control in the sequence also disqualifies; punch them all in order.
- Sprint races: out-of-bounds areas and uncrossable walls cost more than slow running.

## Verify
- [ ] For each leg you can name attack point, route and catching feature.
- [ ] You change speed between rough and fine zones instead of one pace throughout.
- [ ] Thumb on position and map oriented throughout.
- [ ] Every control code checked; no out-of-bounds crossings.
- [ ] Afterwards, compare split times with others: find your biggest time losses, not your slowest running.
