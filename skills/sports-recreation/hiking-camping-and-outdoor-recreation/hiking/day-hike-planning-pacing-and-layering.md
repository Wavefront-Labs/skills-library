# Day-hike planning, pacing and layering

**Goal:** pick a route you can finish in daylight, walk it at a pace the whole group can
hold, and stay dry and warm enough that a weather change is an inconvenience rather than
an emergency.

## 1. Estimate the time honestly
Estimate moving time from both distance and total ascent (ascent drives the time), add
time for breaks, and plan on the slowest member's pace. Calibrate on your own logs:
compare your estimate with actual time on two or three hikes and keep a personal
multiplier. Trail apps often show distance prominently and ascent in small print.

## 2. Set a turnaround time before you start
NPS: be aware of the time you set out and turn around so you do not get stuck outside in
the dark; set a turnaround time if the hike runs long. Avoid hiking after dark. Decide the
time at the trailhead, when nobody is tired or summit-fevered, and be ready to turn back
in hazardous conditions.

## 3. Pace and keeping together
- Let the slowest hiker set the pace and stay together on marked trails (NPS).
- Do not rely on your phone: there may be no coverage. If you use it, keep the battery
  charged and use airplane mode when you do not need it (NPS).
- Higher elevations take more effort because of thinner air; acclimate to a new altitude
  (NPS).

## 4. Layering system
Dress in layers to manage sweat in cold weather and avoid cotton, which retains water
(NPS). Insulation for the pack means a jacket, hat, gloves, layers and rain gear (NPS).
| Layer | Job |
|---|---|
| Base | Move sweat off skin; not cotton |
| Mid | Insulate |
| Shell | Block wind and rain |

## 5. Footwear and water
Wear suitable hiking shoes or boots with good ankle support (NPS); sturdy, broken-in
boots (USFS). Carry at least 1 liter of water
per 2 hours of hiking, plus water treatment supplies (NPS, Great Smoky Mountains).
Drink plenty of water even on cool, wet days (USFS).

## 6. Before you go: trip plan and the ten essentials
- **Trip plan:** write an itinerary (route, your contact details, when you expect to
  arrive and return, who is with you) and give it to a trusted person who is not hiking;
  they should contact the park if you do not return in a reasonable time (NPS).
- **Forecast:** check the weather before you go (NPS). Temperatures can change
  dramatically with elevation; USFS says for every 1,000 feet gained the temperature
  often drops three to five degrees (Fahrenheit page).
- **Ten essentials (NPS Great Smoky Mountains list):** navigation (map, compass, GPS);
  sun protection; insulation; illumination (flashlight or headlamp; your phone does not
  count); first-aid supplies (check they are not expired); fire (matches, lighter, fire
  starter); repair kit and tools; food that needs no cooking; hydration; emergency
  shelter (tent, tarp, bivy sack or space blanket).

## Gotchas
- A phone is not a substitute for the essentials.

## Verify
- [ ] You have a written estimate (distance, ascent, breaks) and a turnaround time.
- [ ] Someone knows your route and when to raise the alarm.
- [ ] No cotton next to skin; insulation and rain gear in the pack.
- [ ] Ten essentials packed, headlamp included even for a "short" hike.
