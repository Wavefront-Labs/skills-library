# Finding and hiding geocaches

**Goal:** turn more searches into finds, log them properly, and place caches that pass
review and that people enjoy. Details below follow the largest listing site
(Geocaching.com); other sites (e.g. Opencaching) have their own rules — check theirs.

## 1. Before you go
- Read the full listing: cache **size**, **difficulty/terrain (D/T)** ratings
  (1–5 each), attributes (e.g. "not available at night", "special tool required"),
  and the **recent logs**. Three or more recent **DNFs** (Did Not Find) in a row
  often mean the cache is missing.
- Leave the encrypted **hint** for when you're stuck, but download it for offline use.
- Mystery/puzzle caches: the posted coordinates are usually fake; you must solve the
  puzzle for the real ones. Many listings offer a checker to confirm your answer.

## 2. Ground zero: the GPS isn't exact
- A phone or handheld GPS is typically accurate to **about 3–10 m** in the open, worse
  under tree canopy, in canyons and between buildings — and the hider's device had
  error too. So expect the cache within a circle of roughly 10–15 m, not at the arrow.
- When the distance reading drops to ~10 m, **put the device away** and think like a
  hider: what's the one odd thing here? A stump with sticks piled on, a lamp-post
  skirt, a fence post cap, a rock that doesn't match, a tree fork at eye height.
- If the arrow wanders, walk away 20–30 m and come back from a different direction;
  wait for the reading to settle.
- Search methodically: spiral outwards from the most likely spot. Check at ground,
  waist and eye height. Gloves and a torch help.

## 3. Muggles and manners
"Muggles" are non-cachers. Searching in public looks suspicious; if people are watching,
walk away and come back, or pretend to be on your phone. **Never** search on private
land without permission, near schools, rail lines or critical infrastructure, and
don't damage anything — no digging, no prying bark or loose masonry. Re-hide the
cache **exactly as found**, with the same camouflage, so the next person has the same
puzzle.

## 4. Logging
- **Sign the physical logbook** (bring a pen; logs are often tiny rolled strips —
  a fine pencil also works when wet).
- Log online: "Found it", or "Didn't find it" — honest DNFs help the owner and others.
- Report problems with a "Needs maintenance" log (wet log, cracked container), not
  only in prose.
- Swaps: if you take an item, leave something of equal or greater value. No food,
  scented items, knives, or anything dangerous.

## 5. Trackables
Travel Bugs and geocoins have a tracking code and move cache to cache. Read the
trackable's **goal**, move it on within a couple of weeks, and log the "retrieve" and
"drop" online so its journey is recorded. Never keep one; never post the tracking
code publicly (it's the "proof" code for logging).

## 6. Hiding a cache that passes review
- **Proximity rule:** a new physical cache must be at least **0.1 mile (~161 m)** from
  other physical cache stages (a long-standing guideline on Geocaching.com; check the
  current guidelines before submitting).
- Get **permission** from the landowner or land manager; many parks require a permit.
- Use a **waterproof container** (lock-n-lock boxes, ammo cans, preforms with good
  seals); film canisters leak. Label it as a geocache with the listing name.
- Take coordinates by **averaging** — several readings on different visits, or the
  device's averaging function.
- Rate D/T honestly, write a useful hint, and commit to maintenance: you must visit
  and fix it when problems are logged.
- **Never bury** a cache, place it in a protected habitat, or anywhere it could be
  mistaken for a suspicious package.

## Gotchas
- Cached (offline) listings go stale — refresh before a trip.
- Night-caching and private land are where most bad encounters happen.

## Verify
- [ ] Listing, recent logs and attributes checked; hint available offline.
- [ ] At ground zero you searched a ~15 m radius methodically, not just the arrow.
- [ ] Cache re-hidden exactly as found; physical log signed; online log posted (find or DNF).
- [ ] Any trackable moved on and logged.
- [ ] For hides: permission obtained, proximity checked, coordinates averaged, waterproof container.
