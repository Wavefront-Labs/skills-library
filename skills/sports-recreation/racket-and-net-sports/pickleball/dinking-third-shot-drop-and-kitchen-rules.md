# Pickleball — dinking, third-shot drop and kitchen rules

**Goal:** get your team to the kitchen line, win the soft game there, and
avoid the kitchen faults that hand away points. The rules here follow
USA Pickleball's rulebook, which is updated every year — check the current
edition for detail.

## 1. Key rules
- **Serve:** underhand, contact below the waist, paddle head below the
  wrist, diagonal into the opponent's service court, landing beyond the
  kitchen line. The **drop serve** (ball dropped and hit after the bounce)
  is also legal and relaxes the motion rules.
- **Two-bounce rule:** the return of serve must bounce, and the serving
  team's next shot (the third shot) must also bounce. After that, volleys
  are allowed.
- **Kitchen (non-volley zone, NVZ):** the 7 ft (2.13 m) zone on each side
  of the net. You may not volley while touching the zone or its line —
  including your **momentum** carrying you into it after a volley, even
  after the ball is dead. You may enter it any time to play a bounced ball.
- **Doubles scoring (traditional):** only the serving team scores; score
  called as *serving team score – receiving team score – server number
  (1 or 2)*. Games to 11, win by 2. Rally scoring is used in some events.

## 2. Ready position and the kitchen line
- Both partners at the kitchen line is the strongest position. Stand just
  behind the line, knees bent, paddle up in front of the chest, backhand-ready.
- The **transition zone** (mid-court) is where points are lost — get through
  it or reset in it.

## 3. Return of serve
Hit a **deep, high, slow** return to the baseline; the extra time lets
the returner get to the kitchen line. Then move up immediately.

## 4. Third shot: drop or drive
- The serving team is pinned back by the two-bounce rule. The third shot
  decides whether they can come forward.
- **Third-shot drop:** a soft, arcing shot that lands in the opponents'
  kitchen so they must hit up. Lift from the shoulder, open face, slow
  paddle, contact out in front; aim for the apex of the arc on your side of
  the net. Then move in a few steps and split step.
- **Third-shot drive:** hard and low, best to the weaker opponent or when
  the return is short; often followed by a drop on the fifth shot.
- A good drop that lands just over the net (not deep in the kitchen) is the
  target.

## 5. Dinking
- Soft shots from kitchen line to kitchen, landing in the opponent's
  kitchen so the ball has to be hit upward.
- Short paddle motion from the shoulder, little wrist, paddle face slightly
  open. Bend the knees to get low rather than bending at the waist.
- **Cross-court dinks** are safer (more court, lower net in the middle).
- Be patient: wait for a ball that is high enough to **speed up** (attack)
  and aim the speed-up at the opponent's paddle-side shoulder or hip.

## 6. Resets
When you're attacked in the transition zone, absorb pace with a soft,
open paddle and drop the ball back into the kitchen to neutralise the
point. Resets are the skill that separates intermediate from advanced.

## 7. Doubles extras
- **Stacking:** partners arrange to keep a forehand in the middle or keep a
  player on a preferred side, legal as long as the correct server serves
  from the correct side.
- **Erne:** jumping around the kitchen post (outside the court) to volley —
  legal if you don't touch the kitchen.

## Gotchas
- Volleying with a foot on the kitchen line.
- Rushing forward after a bad third shot — stop, split step and reset.
- Forgetting the two-bounce rule on the serving team's first volley.

## Verify
- [ ] 10-dink cross-court rally without a pop-up.
- [ ] 6 of 10 third-shot drops land in or just short of the kitchen.
- [ ] Both partners at the kitchen line by the fourth or fifth shot in drills.
- [ ] Every player can call the score correctly in doubles.
