# Padel — wall play, bandeja, lobs and net positioning

**Goal:** use the walls instead of fighting them, win the net, and keep it
with controlled overheads rather than smashing everything. Padel is always
doubles in competition, played in an enclosed court with glass walls.

## 1. Rules that matter
- **Serve underhand:** bounce the ball behind the service line, strike it at
  or below **waist height**, diagonally into the opponent's service box. It
  may hit the side glass after the bounce but not the mesh/fence (a fault).
  Two attempts.
- **Ball must bounce once on your side** before or after hitting walls —
  i.e. it must cross the net and bounce before touching any of the
  opponent's walls. You may play it off your own walls after the bounce, and
  you may hit it against your own walls to return it.
- **Scoring:** like tennis; many events use a **golden point** at deuce
  (one deciding point; current pro tours have changed this rule — check).
- Leaving the court to play a ball that bounced out is allowed where the
  court has exits ("out-of-court play") — advanced.

## 2. Wall play — let the ball come
- Biggest beginner error: rushing the ball before the wall. Let it come
  off the **back glass**, turn side-on, move back toward the wall, then step
  forward and play it as it comes off.
- **Back-wall rebound:** ball bounces, hits the back glass, comes toward
  you; keep the racket low and play it gently upward (often a lob).
- **Side then back (or back then side):** read the angle — balls travel off
  glass like a pool ball off a cushion. Practise each combination slowly.
- Don't hit hard off the back glass from deep — you're defending.

## 3. The lob (globo)
The lob is padel's most important shot: it pushes the net team back and
lets you take the net.
- High enough to clear the net player's reach, deep enough to bounce
  near the back wall.
- After a good lob, both players **advance to the net together**.

## 4. Overheads
- **Bandeja:** a controlled, sliced overhead (sideways "tray" motion) from
  mid-court to keep the ball low and deep so you **keep** the net. Contact
  slightly to the side and in front; cut under the ball's side.
- **Víbora:** more aggressive sliced overhead with sidespin that skids off
  the side glass.
- **Smash** (flat or "por tres"/"por cuatro" to send the ball out over the
  walls) — only on a short, high lob. A smash that bounces off the back
  wall and comes back is a free ball for the opponents.
- Rule of thumb: if you're behind the service line, bandeja; if close to the
  net with a short lob, smash.

## 5. Net play and positioning
- **Net position:** about 2–3 m from the net, side by side, moving together
  ("tied with a rope").
- Volleys are short and blocked, aimed low at the feet or into the
  middle between opponents.
- **Chiquita:** soft, low ball to the net players' feet from the back, used
  to win the net.
- **The middle:** usually taken by the player with the forehand in the
  middle (the left-side player for two right-handers, who also often hits
  more overheads).

## 6. Equipment
Solid perforated rackets (no strings), round heads for control, diamond
heads for power. Low-pressure padel balls. Use proper padel shoes (herringbone
or omni soles for artificial turf).

## Gotchas
- Hitting the ball before the back wall when you had time.
- Smashing every lob and giving it back.
- One player at the net and the other at the back — a big gap in the middle.

## Verify
- [ ] 8 of 10 back-glass rebounds returned as controlled lobs.
- [ ] Serve legal: bounce behind the line, contact at waist height or below.
- [ ] 10 bandejas that land deep without bouncing back off the back glass to the opponents.
- [ ] Pair moves forward together after every good lob.
