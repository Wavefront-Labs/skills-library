# Table tennis — spin reading, serve-receive and loop technique

**Goal:** read the spin coming at you, return serve without popping it up,
and attack with a topspin loop. At every level beyond casual, spin decides
more points than speed.

## 1. Grip and ready position
- **Shakehand:** index finger along the bottom of the backhand rubber,
  thumb resting near the forehand rubber, other three fingers wrapped
  loosely around the handle. A neutral grip (blade angled evenly) suits both
  wings.
- **Penhold** (Chinese or Japanese) is a legitimate alternative; this skill
  assumes shakehand.
- **Ready:** feet wider than shoulders, slightly behind the table's
  backhand corner for a right-hander, knees bent, racket in front at table
  height.

## 2. Reading spin
- **Topspin** jumps forward and down off your bat if you don't close the
  face — returns go long. **Close** the bat (tilt it forward).
- **Backspin** (underspin) makes returns drop into the net — **open** the
  bat and lift.
- **Sidespin** sends returns sideways off your bat — angle against it.
- **Cues:** watch the opponent's bat path at contact — low-to-high brush =
  topspin, high-to-low = backspin, side brush = sidespin. A fast arm with
  little forward ball speed often means heavy spin; a slow-looking ball can
  be "no spin" (a deliberate deception).
- The ball's logo spinning, the bounce shape, and the sound (brush vs
  thud) help too.

## 3. Legal serve (ITTF/WTT rules — check current wording)
- Ball rests on the open, flat palm, then is thrown up **at least 16 cm**
  near-vertically, with no spin imparted by the hand.
- Struck as it falls, behind the end line and above table height.
- The ball must be visible to the receiver throughout — no hiding it with
  the arm or body.
- In doubles, serves go diagonally right half to right half.
- **Pendulum serve** (forehand side, arm swinging across the body) is the
  common first spin serve; vary short backspin and short no-spin with the
  same motion.

## 4. Receiving
- **Push:** for short backspin — open bat, brush under the back of the ball
  and keep it short and low. Aim deep or very short; a mid-length push is
  attacked.
- **Flick:** for short balls you can attack — wrist snap over the top.
  Backhand flick ("banana flick" with sidespin) is the modern standard.
- **Block:** for fast topspin — short stroke, closed bat, use the incoming
  speed.
- **Third-ball attack:** the server's plan — serve short backspin, receive
  a long push, then loop it.

## 5. The forehand loop
- Turn the waist and drop the bat behind and below the ball, weight on the
  right leg (right-hander).
- Swing forward and upward, rotating the waist and transferring weight to
  the left leg; brush the ball with a thin contact.
- Against backspin: more upward, slower ball, contact at the top of the
  bounce or just after. Against topspin: more forward, closed bat, contact
  earlier.
- Finish around forehead height; relax and recover.

## 6. Equipment basics
- Start with a **pre-assembled** or all-round blade with medium-speed
  inverted rubbers (smooth surface, ~1.8–2.0 mm sponge).
- Rubbers must be ITTF-authorised and one side red, the other black (newer
  approved colours exist on one side; check your competition's rules).
- **Pimples-out / long pips** produce odd spin returns — learn to play
  against them rather than starting with them.
- Clean inverted rubbers regularly; grip fades as they get dusty.

## Gotchas
- Pushing serves back high and long.
- Hitting the loop flat (no brush) and overshooting.
- Hiding the serve — opponents and umpires will call it.

## Verify
- [ ] 8 of 10 short backspin serves pushed back low and short or deep.
- [ ] 10 consecutive forehand loops against backspin feed.
- [ ] Serve passes a partner's check for toss height and visibility.
- [ ] Can call the spin of a coach's serve before it bounces, 7 of 10.
