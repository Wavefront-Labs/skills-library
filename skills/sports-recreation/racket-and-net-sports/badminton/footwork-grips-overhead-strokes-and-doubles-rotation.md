# Badminton — footwork, grips, overhead strokes and doubles rotation

**Goal:** reach every corner from a balanced base, generate power from a
relaxed grip, and move with a doubles partner without gaps or collisions.

## 1. Grips
- **Forehand grip:** "shake hands" with the handle, the racket face
  perpendicular to the floor; the V of thumb and finger on the thin top
  bevel. **Relaxed** until the instant of contact — squeezing late is where
  power comes from.
- **Backhand (thumb) grip:** rotate slightly and press the thumb flat on
  the wide back bevel for backhand clears, drives and net shots.
- **Panhandle** (face flat to the net) is used briefly for net kills and
  flat drives; as a default grip it blocks good technique.

## 2. Footwork
- **Base:** in singles, roughly mid-court, a little behind centre.
- **Split step** as the opponent hits.
- **Chassé** (side-shuffle) and crossover steps to the rear court; a long
  **lunge** onto the racket-side leg to the front, heel landing first, knee
  over the foot (not beyond it).
- Recover to base after every shot — most lost points are a step late.
- "**Shadow**" footwork drills without a shuttle build the patterns.

## 3. Overhead strokes
All three start from the same preparation — that is the deception:
- Turn side-on, racket up behind the head, non-racket arm pointing up at
  the shuttle.
- **Clear:** contact high and slightly in front, full swing, sending the
  shuttle high and deep to the back line. Defensive clears go higher.
- **Drop:** same preparation, slow the racket at contact (or slice the
  face) so the shuttle falls just over the net.
- **Smash:** contact further in front, steeper downward angle, forearm
  rotation (pronation) and wrist snap. A jump smash adds angle at a fitness
  cost.
- Wrist-only power comes from forearm rotation plus finger squeeze, not a
  floppy wrist.

## 4. Net play
- Racket up and in front at the net. **Tumbling net shot:** gentle slice
  under the shuttle so it spins and tumbles over.
- **Net kill:** a short tap down on any shuttle above net height.
- Take the shuttle as high (early) as possible.

## 5. Serving (BWF Laws)
- The whole shuttle must be below **1.15 m** from the floor at the moment of
  contact (the fixed-height rule used in BWF events; some leagues still use
  the older below-the-waist rule — check).
- Both feet stationary and in contact with the floor until the serve.
- Racket must move forward continuously from the start of the serve.
- **Low serve** (doubles staple) skims to the front service line; **high
  serve** (singles) to the back.

## 6. Singles tactics
Move the opponent to all four corners; clear and drop to stretch them, then
smash the short or weak reply. Pace your smashes — a single big smash from
mid-court beats three from the back line.

## 7. Doubles rotation
- **Attack = front and back:** one player at the net, one smashing from
  the rear. Lift = you're defending.
- **Defence = side by side:** after lifting, both players take half the
  court each, mid-court, racket low and in front.
- **Rotation:** when the back player is pushed to defend, the front player
  drops back to side-by-side; when the side-by-side pair gets a short ball,
  the player who plays it short follows to the net and the other takes the back.
- Talk: "yours"/"mine" and agree who takes the middle (usually the player
  with the forehand).

## Gotchas
- Grip strangled all the time — slow and causes forearm pain.
- Watching your own shot instead of recovering.
- Telegraphing the drop with a different preparation.

## Verify
- [ ] 10 consecutive deep clears landing in the back 1 m.
- [ ] Clear, drop and smash look identical until contact on video.
- [ ] Low serve clears the net and lands inside the front service line 8 of 10.
- [ ] Doubles pair switches attack/defence formation on every lift in drills.
