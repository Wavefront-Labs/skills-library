# Tennis — groundstrokes, serve and match tactics

**Goal:** rally consistently with topspin, serve a reliable first and
second ball, and win points by percentages rather than winners.

## 1. Grips (for a right-hander; base knuckle on the racket bevels)
- **Continental** ("hammer" grip): serve, volleys, slice, overheads.
- **Eastern forehand:** flat-ish forehand, easy to learn.
- **Semi-western forehand:** the modern default — more topspin, good for
  high balls; harder on low balls.
- **Two-handed backhand:** bottom hand continental, top hand eastern
  forehand (common setup). **One-handed backhand:** eastern backhand grip.
Grip choice changes the contact point: the more western the grip, the
further in front and higher the contact.

## 2. Footwork and preparation
- **Split step:** a small hop landing as the opponent strikes the ball, so
  you can push off in any direction.
- **Unit turn:** turn shoulders and hips together as soon as you read the
  side; the racket goes back because the body turned, not because the arm
  pulled it.
- Recover toward the middle of your opponent's possible angles after each
  shot (not always the centre mark).

## 3. Groundstrokes with topspin
- Racket head drops below the ball, then swings low to high, brushing up
  the back of the ball.
- Contact out in front of the front hip, body rotating through.
- Finish over the opposite shoulder (forehand "windshield-wiper" finish).
- **Margin:** aim 1–1.5 m over the net and 1–2 m inside the lines;
  topspin brings it down.
- **Slice backhand:** high-to-low, open face, continental grip — for
  defence, approach shots and changing pace.

## 4. The serve
- **Continental grip** (an eastern forehand grip "frying-pan" serve limits
  you later; change early).
- **Stance:** front foot about 45° to the baseline, toes behind it.
- **Toss** with a straight arm to just above full reach, slightly in front
  and to the right (right-hander) — consistency of toss is most of a
  consistent serve.
- **Trophy position:** tossing arm up, racket arm bent, knees flexed.
- **Swing:** racket drops behind the back, then accelerates up to the ball
  with forearm pronation; land inside the court on the front foot.
- **Second serve:** more spin (slice or kick), not less speed and push.
  Double faults come from slowing the arm.
- Foot fault: touching the baseline or court before contact.

## 5. Volleys
Continental grip, short punch with little backswing, racket head above the
wrist, step toward the ball. Split step as the opponent strikes.

## 6. Singles tactics
- **High-percentage tennis:** cross-court rallies (lower net, longer
  court), change direction down the line only on a ball you can attack.
- Most points are lost by errors at recreational level — make the
  opponent play one more ball.
- **Return of serve:** shorten the swing, block deep through the middle.
- **Patterns:** serve wide then hit to the open court; serve to the backhand
  on big points.

## 7. Doubles basics
- One-up-one-back or both up (better) — move as a pair, like a rope ties you.
- The net player poaches cross-court returns.
- Middle balls: the player with the forehand or the one who hit the last
  ball cross-court usually takes it — agree a rule.

## Gotchas
- Arming the ball with no shoulder turn.
- Tossing inconsistently and chasing the toss.
- Painful outer elbow (tennis elbow): check grip size and string tension,
  don't play through it.
