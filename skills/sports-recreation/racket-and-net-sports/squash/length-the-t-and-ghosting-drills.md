# Squash — length, the T and ghosting drills

**Goal:** push the opponent into the back corners with tight length, own
the T, and move efficiently enough to keep doing it. Squash is won from
the back of the court first.

## 1. Swing basics
- **Grip:** a continental-type ("shake hands, V on the top edge") grip with
  the face slightly open; it rarely changes between forehand and backhand.
- **Racket prep early:** racket up (cocked wrist, head above the hand) as
  you move, so the swing is short and compact when you arrive.
- **Swing:** from high back, through the ball beside or slightly in front of
  the front foot, open face for slight underspin, finish across the body
  but not wildly (a wide swing is a safety hazard and a let/stroke issue).
- **Ball:** the **double yellow dot** ball is for advanced play — it needs
  warming up and bounces very little. Beginners use single yellow, red or
  blue (bouncier) balls.

## 2. Length — the core shot
- **Straight drive** that bounces near the back of the service box and dies
  in the back corner, tight to the side wall.
- Aim at a target height on the front wall (well above the service line
  for most players) and let it come down deep. A ball that hits the back
  wall hard before bouncing twice "sits up" in the middle.
- **Crosscourt** wide enough to beat the opponent's volley — hit it to touch
  the side wall about level with the back of the service box.
- Rule of thumb: at recreational level, more length and fewer "winners".

## 3. The T and movement
- After every shot, **return to the T** (just behind the junction of the
  short line and half-court line) — or the spot that covers the opponent's
  likely replies.
- **Split step** as the opponent hits.
- Move with a few efficient steps: the last step is usually a lunge with
  the racket-side foot to the front corners and the body side-on in the
  back corners.

## 4. Variety
- **Volley:** intercept on the volley whenever you can — it takes time away.
- **Drop:** soft shot to the front corners, best when the opponent is behind
  you.
- **Boast:** angle the ball off the side wall first so it travels to the
  front opposite corner. Good attacking option when the opponent is deep;
  a poor boast hands them the front of the court.
- **Lob:** high crosscourt over the opponent to recover from the front.

## 5. Ghosting (movement without a ball)
- Stand on the T; a partner or app calls a corner (or follow a set
  pattern). Move, shadow the swing, return to the T.
- Typical session: 6–10 sets of 30–60 s on, equal rest. Focus on split step,
  racket prep and recovery, not just speed.
- Ghosting also builds the specific fitness squash demands.

## 6. Interference: let and stroke
- After your shot, you must give your opponent **direct access** to the
  ball, a fair view, and **freedom to swing**.
- If they're impeded and could have reached the ball: **let** (replay).
- If you prevented a winning shot, or failed to clear and they'd have
  reached it: **stroke** (point to them).
- If they couldn't have got it anyway: **no let**.
- **Stop** rather than hit when the opponent is in your swing or line. Safety
  first — players are hit by balls and rackets.

## 7. Safety
- **Eye protection** (sports-rated) is mandatory for juniors and doubles in
  most federations, and strongly recommended for all.
- Warm up properly; squash is high-intensity with sudden stops.
- Non-marking shoes, water, and stopping when dizzy — cardiac events are a
  known risk for unfit players starting hard.

## Gotchas
- Watching your shot from the back instead of returning to the T.
- Hitting to the middle of the court (the opponent's favourite ball).
- Playing through instead of asking for a let when there's danger.

- [ ] Ghosting set: back on the T before the next call every rep.
- [ ] You can explain let vs stroke vs no let to a partner.
