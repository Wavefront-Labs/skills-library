# Barre — isometric holds, pulses and alignment cueing

**Goal:** lead a barre class where participants reach deep muscular
fatigue ("the shake") through holds and small movements, while staying
aligned — especially knees and pelvis — so the burn comes from muscles,
not joints.

## 1. Class structure (typical 50–60 min)
1. **Warm-up** (5–8 min): mobility, light cardio, plank or push-ups.
2. **Arms** (5–8 min): light weights (0.5–2 kg), high reps, small ranges.
3. **Thighs** at the barre (10–12 min): plié and relevé positions, chair.
4. **Seat/glutes** (8–10 min): kneeling or standing leg lifts and pulses.
5. **Core** (8–10 min): on the mat, often with a small ball or band.
6. **Stretch** after each block and at the end.
Branded methods vary in order and terminology; **check the programme you're licensed for.**

## 2. The working method
- **Isometric hold:** find the working position (e.g. heels lifted in a
  wide plié) and hold 20–60 s.
- **Pulses:** 2–3 cm ("an inch") up and down in the hold position.
- **Full range:** larger movements through the joint, to contrast.
- **Combination:** e.g. 16 full-range, 16 pulses, 16 holds with arms, then
  stretch. Moving between hold → pulse → full keeps time under tension high.
- Shaking muscles are expected; **sharp joint pain is a stop sign.**

## 3. Positions
- **First position:** heels together, toes slightly turned out — only as
  much turnout as the **hips** allow (not twisted from knees/feet).
- **Second position (wide plié):** feet wider than hips, toes out, knees
  bending in line with toes.
- **Relevé:** lifted onto balls of the feet; weight over the big and
  second toe, not rolling to the outer edge.
- **Parallel / chair:** feet hip-width, knees bent, hips back.

## 4. Alignment cueing
- **Knees track over toes** in every plié; if knees fall inward, reduce
  turnout or depth.
- **Neutral pelvis:** "hip bones and pubic bone in a vertical plane";
  avoid the exaggerated **tuck** (posterior tilt) old barre styles used
  constantly — many methods now teach neutral with optional small tucks.
- **Ribs over hips**, shoulders down, crown of the head long.
- **Light hands** on the barre — it's for balance, not for hanging off.
- One cue at a time, then praise the correction.
- Use tactile cues only with **consent**, and verbal/visual first.

## 5. Modifications
- **Knees:** shallower plié, less turnout, heels down instead of relevé.
- **Pregnancy:** no prolonged supine core after the first trimester (per
  common guidance), avoid deep twists, keep balance support near.
  **Refer to qualified pre/post-natal guidance.**
- **Wrists:** forearm planks, fists instead of flat hands.
- **Beginners:** shorter holds, more breaks, both hands on the barre.
- Offer progressions: longer holds, lifting heels higher, adding a ball
  squeezed between the thighs.

## 6. Music and atmosphere
- Steady, moderately fast beats (often ~120–130 BPM) to pace pulses on
  the beat or half-beat. Music is motivating but timing is less strict than
  aerobics. Count reps aloud during holds — people last longer with a
  finish line ("8 more… 4… hold…").

## Equipment
Barre (or chair back), mat, light weights, small soft ball, loop band.
Check portable barres are stable before class.

## Gotchas
- Forced turnout from the feet → knee strain.
- Constant hard tucking → lower-back and hip-flexor complaints.
- Endless pulses without stretching between blocks.
- **Timings and BPM are typical, not standardised.**

## Verify
- [ ] Each block uses hold, pulse and full-range work, then a stretch.
- [ ] Knees track over toes in all pliés.
- [ ] Neutral pelvis taught with tuck optional.
- [ ] Modifications for knees, wrists and pregnancy offered.
