# HIIT and bootcamp — circuit design, work-rest and outdoor logistics

**Goal:** design sessions that are genuinely high-intensity for each
person, balanced across the body, easy to follow in a busy room or park,
and safe for mixed abilities.

## 1. Choose the work-rest structure
| Format | Typical structure | Use for |
|---|---|---|
| Tabata-style | 20 s on / 10 s off × 8 (4 min) | Short, very hard finishers (simple moves only) |
| Classic HIIT | 30–40 s on / 20–30 s off | Main circuits, mixed groups |
| 1:1 | 45 s on / 45 s off | Hard efforts with real recovery |
| EMOM | Every minute on the minute, set reps, rest the remainder | Strength-endurance, self-pacing |
| AMRAP | As many rounds/reps as possible in X min | Partners/teams, scalable |
- True high intensity needs **enough rest**; ratios with little rest
  (e.g. 40/10 for 30 minutes) become moderate cardio as quality drops.
- Simpler moves for shorter rest; technical moves (Olympic lifts, box
  jumps) only with longer rest and good coaching. **Ratios are common
  practice, not prescriptions.**

## 2. Balance the circuit by movement pattern
For a 6–10 station circuit, include:
- **Squat** (goblet squat, jump squat), **hinge** (kettlebell swing, RDL),
  **lunge** (reverse lunge, step-ups), **push** (push-up, press),
  **pull** (bands, TRX rows), **core** (plank, dead bug), plus
  **cardio** (skipping, shuttle runs, rower/bike).
- **Alternate** body regions so one area recovers while the next works
  (lower → upper → cardio → core).
- Avoid back-to-back grip- or shoulder-heavy stations.
- Each station shows **three levels** (e.g. card with images):
  regression / base / progression.

## 3. Running stations with a group
- Number stations, lay them out in a loop with clear walking direction.
- **Demo all stations** before starting (quick 2-rep demo each), or demo
  in waves for long circuits.
- Use an **interval timer** with audible beeps (app or gym timer);
  cue "**rotate**" during rest and point to the next station.
- Pairs at stations: one works, one counts or does an active hold;
  partners swap — useful when equipment is limited.
- Position yourself to see everyone; correct form at the riskiest
  stations (swings, jumps) first.
- Scale timing: newcomers can take extra rest; nobody should feel they must
  match others.

## 4. Session shape (45 min)
1. Warm-up 8–10 min (dynamic mobility, movement prep for the circuit).
2. Circuit(s) 25–30 min (e.g. 2–3 rounds).
3. Finisher 3–5 min (optional, simple).
4. Cool-down 5 min.

## 5. Outdoor bootcamp logistics
- **Permission:** many councils/parks require a permit or commercial-use
  licence for paid fitness groups; check before advertising.
- **Risk assessment:** surface (holes, wet grass, glass), traffic, other
  park users, shade, toilets, water, lighting for early/late sessions.
- **Weather plan:** heat (shift times, shade, extra water breaks), rain
  (slippery surfaces → no sprinting or jumping on wet grass), lightning
  (cancel).
- Kit: first aid kit, phone, participant emergency contacts, equipment
  that's easy to carry (bands, kettlebells, cones, mats).
- Insurance covering outdoor group sessions.

## 6. Screening and safety
- Health screening (e.g. PAR-Q) for new members; low-impact options always
  available. Watch for signs of over-exertion (pallor, dizziness, nausea).
- Avoid max-effort jumps and sprints for newcomers in week 1.

## Gotchas
- "Intensity" created by complexity rather than effort.
- All-lower-body circuits that cripple people for days.
- No permit for a paid park class.

## Verify
- [ ] Work-rest ratio chosen for the goal and move complexity.
- [ ] Circuit covers squat, hinge, lunge, push, pull, core, cardio.
- [ ] Every station has regression and progression.
- [ ] Outdoor: permit, risk assessment and weather plan in place.
