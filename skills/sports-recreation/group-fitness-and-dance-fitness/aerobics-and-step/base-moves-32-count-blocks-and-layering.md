# Aerobics and step — base moves, 32-count blocks and layering

**Goal:** teach choreographed aerobics or step so that a class ends up
doing a full 32-count combination (or several) together, built one piece
at a time without losing anyone.

## 1. Base moves
- **Low-impact aerobics:** march, step-touch, V-step, grapevine, knee lift,
  hamstring curl, mambo, box step (jazz square), heel dig.
- **High-impact:** jog, jumping jack, skip, hop variations (offer
  low-impact versions always).
- **Step:** basic step (up-up-down-down), V-step, turn step, knee lift
  (repeater), over the top, straddle down, A-step, L-step, corner-to-corner.
- Each base move fills a known count (e.g. basic step = 4 counts; a knee
  lift on step = 4 counts) — plan with counts, not by feel.

## 2. Music and BPM
- Pre-mixed **32-count** fitness music.
- **Step:** commonly ~118–128 BPM; faster increases trip/ankle risk and
  makes full foot placement impossible. Aerobics floor: ~130–140 BPM.
  **Typical guideline ranges; check your programme.**
- Start combinations on the top of the phrase (count 1 of the 32).

## 3. Step height and safety
- Beginners: platform only or one riser (~15 cm total); most participants
  ~15–20 cm; experienced up to ~25 cm. Knee angle at the top of the step
  shouldn't be much tighter than ~90° under load.
- Place the **whole foot** on the platform; heel not hanging off.
- Step down **close** to the step (about a shoe length away), toe-ball-heel.
- Never step down facing away from the platform (backwards off it).
- Keep arms optional for beginners until footwork is solid.

## 4. Building a combination
Two common methods, often combined:
- **Add-on (linear build):** teach block A (8 counts) → repeat → teach
  block B → link A+B → teach C → link A+B+C, etc.
- **Layering:** teach a simple base pattern for the whole 32 counts, then
  change one element at a time ("now turn the basic", "add a knee on the
  second set", "now arms") until it becomes the final choreography.
- **Holding pattern:** a simple move (march, basic step) you return to
  while people catch up or before teaching the next layer.

## 5. Lead-leg changes
- Most combinations should be **symmetrical**: performed leading with the
  right then left. Build in a **tap-change** (a tap or single-count move
  that switches the lead leg) at the end of the 32 counts.
- Count the combination with the lead-leg change to check it really
  alternates sides. Uneven combos fatigue one side and confuse the class.

## 6. Cueing
- Cue **4 counts ahead**: "basic, basic, V-step in 4…3…2…".
- Use **directional cues** (point), **numerical cues** (fingers for
  repeats), and **footwork cues** ("right foot up").
- Name moves consistently; introduce move names on the first teach.

## 7. Planning a class
- Warm-up (rehearse base moves) → 2–4 combinations built and linked →
  final run-through of all blocks → conditioning (optional) → cool-down.
- Write cards: each block with counts, lead leg, layers in order.
- Test the whole build on your own to music before class.

## Gotchas
- Combinations that don't alternate lead leg.
- Tempo too fast for step.
- Teaching all 32 counts at once.
- Skipping low-impact options.

## Verify
- [ ] Every block written with counts adding to 32.
- [ ] Lead leg alternates via a tap-change; checked to music.
- [ ] Step BPM within your programme's range; heights offered by level.
- [ ] Class completes the full combination together by the end.
