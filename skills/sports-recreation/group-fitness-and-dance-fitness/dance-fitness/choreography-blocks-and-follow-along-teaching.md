# Dance fitness — choreography blocks and follow-along teaching

**Goal:** deliver a Zumba-style class where people sweat, smile and can
follow without stopping to learn. Dance fitness is mostly taught by
**follow-along** with minimal verbal instruction, so choreography must be
predictable and cues mostly visual.

## 1. Licensing and brands
- **Zumba** is a trademarked programme: teaching under the name requires
  Zumba instructor training and (for ongoing access to music/choreo)
  membership (ZIN). Other branded formats have their own licences.
- Unbranded "dance fitness" is fine, but music still needs appropriate
  public performance licensing via the venue or fitness-music providers.
  **Licensing details vary by country; check yours.**

## 2. Build each song from its structure
- Map the song: intro, verse, pre-chorus, chorus, bridge, outro. Listen and
  count in 8s; write e.g. "Intro 2×8, Verse 4×8, Chorus 4×8…".
- Assign **one move pattern per section** and **repeat it every time the
  section returns** — repetition is what makes follow-along work.
- Typical: verse = a simple base step with arm variation; chorus = the
  signature, highest-energy move; bridge = something distinct or a breather.
- Keep each block to 1–2 distinct steps; the class should "get it" by the
  second chorus.

## 3. Base steps and rhythms
- Teach a library of base steps per rhythm: **merengue** (march, side
  step), **salsa** (forward/back basic, side basic), **cumbia** (back step
  with a drag), **reggaeton** (bounce, step-tap with body wave),
  plus world/pop styles (Bollywood, afrobeats, hip-hop grooves).
- **Rhythm breakdown:** first time through, show the footwork without arms;
  add arms on repeat (**layering**). Some instructors break down
  complicated moves for one 8-count at half tempo during the song, then
  return to full.

## 4. Follow-along cueing
- **Visual signals** before each change (in the last 4 counts):
  - Point direction (left/right/forward).
  - Hold up fingers for "repeat X times" or circle a finger for "same again".
  - Hand on chest = "new move", or sweep hand for "turn".
  - "Watch me" (two fingers to eyes) for a new block.
- Mirror the class (move to their right with your left).
- Turn your back to the class for complex directional moves so they
  copy your orientation; face them for cardio moves to read energy.
- Verbal cues: short hype ("Woo!", "Last one!"), not instruction.

## 5. Playlist and intensity curve
- 45–60 min class ≈ 12–15 songs of ~3–4 min.
- **Shape:** warm-up song(s) at moderate tempo → build to peaks → recovery
  song → peaks → a **toning** song (squats, lunges to the beat) →
  cool-down with stretching.
- Alternate rhythms/styles to vary muscle use and keep interest.
- Rotate in **1–2 new songs** at a time; keep favourites so regulars feel
  confident and newcomers see people who know it.
- Note BPM: very fast tracks need half-time steps.

## 6. Safety and inclusion
- Low-impact options for jumps and fast turns; keep knees soft on
  twisting moves (pivot on the ball of the foot, avoid planted-foot twists).
- Space: everyone needs room to step side to side; check floor surface for
  grip (turns on sticky floors stress knees).
- Smile, celebrate attempts; "no wrong moves, just keep moving" lowers
  anxiety for newcomers.

## 7. Practising
- Learn each song well enough to do it without music; film yourself.
- Practise the cueing hand signals, not just the dance.

## Gotchas
- Too many new moves per song.
- Talking over the music to explain.
- A playlist with no recovery dip.

## Verify
- [ ] Every song mapped in 8-counts with one block per section.
- [ ] Repeat sections use identical choreography.
- [ ] Visual cues given in the 4 counts before each change.
- [ ] Playlist follows a warm-up → peaks → cool-down curve.
- [ ] Licensing/branding position checked.
