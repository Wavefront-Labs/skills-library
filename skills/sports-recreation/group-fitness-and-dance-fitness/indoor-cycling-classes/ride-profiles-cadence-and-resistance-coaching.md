# Indoor cycling classes — ride profiles, cadence and resistance

**Goal:** lead a 45-minute ride where everyone works at their own
intensity on a well-fitted bike, with a ride plan mapped to music and
safe cadence/resistance combinations.

## 1. Bike setup (do this with every newcomer)
- **Saddle height:** with the pedal at the bottom, knee slightly bent
  (~25–35° of flexion); a quick check is hip-level saddle when standing
  beside the bike.
- **Fore/aft:** at 3 o'clock pedal position, the front of the knee roughly
  above the pedal axle.
- **Handlebars:** level with saddle or higher for beginners and anyone with
  back issues.
- Feet secure (cleats clipped or cages tight); water bottle and towel ready.
- **Emergency brake**: show where it is (usually push down on the
  resistance knob/lever) — fixed-gear flywheels keep spinning.

## 2. Cadence and resistance
- **Cadence** (RPM) ranges commonly taught:
  - Seated flat / sprint: ~80–110 RPM.
  - Climbs (seated or standing): ~60–80 RPM.
  - **Never below ~50–60 RPM** with very heavy resistance (knee stress),
    nor above ~110–120 RPM (bouncing, uncontrolled). Ranges vary by
    programme; **check your certification's numbers.**
- Always enough resistance to feel the road — spinning fast with no
  resistance is uncontrolled and hard on knees.
- Use an **RPE scale** (1–10) or **heart-rate zones**/power (% of FTP) if
  bikes show watts; cue all three so riders with/without data can follow.

## 3. Ride profile (example 45 min)
1. **Warm-up** 5–7 min: flat road, light resistance, 85–95 RPM, RPE 3–4.
2. **Block 1 — rolling hills** (~10 min): alternating seated climbs and
   flats.
3. **Block 2 — intervals** (~12 min): e.g. 30 s hard (RPE 8–9) /
   30–60 s recovery × 6–8; or a Tabata-style 20/10 for advanced groups.
4. **Block 3 — big climb** (~8–10 min): adding resistance every minute;
   seated → standing → seated.
5. **Finish sprint** (1–2 efforts).
6. **Cool-down + stretch** (5 min).
Plan peaks and recoveries; not every song should be maximal.

## 4. Music mapping
- Match cadence to the beat: pedal strokes at song BPM (e.g. 90 BPM =
  90 RPM, one foot per beat) or half-time for climbs (130 BPM track ≈
  65 RPM climb).
- Start efforts on the phrase or the drop; cue 4–8 counts ahead.
- Choose music that fits each profile segment's tempo.

## 5. Coaching cues
- Position: "hands light, shoulders down, elbows soft".
- Standing climbs: hips over the saddle, weight on the pedals not the bars.
- **Jumps** (alternating seated and standing) need enough resistance; skip
  for beginners.
- Avoid gimmicks off the bike's design: push-ups on handlebars, isolated
  hip "tap backs" at speed, or weights while pedalling fast — these add
  injury risk with little benefit.
- Give options: "if you're new, stay seated; if you've been here a while,
  add a quarter turn".

## 6. Safety
- Room ventilation and fans; hydrated riders; watch for dizziness/pallor.
- Gradual intensity for new riders (first 2–3 classes at moderate effort).
- Riders should slow the flywheel with resistance/brake before dismounting.
- Clean and check bikes (loose saddles, wobbling pedals).

## Gotchas
- Cueing high cadence with zero resistance.
- Saddle too low (knee pain) or too high (hip rocking).
- Every song a sprint — no structure, no recovery.
- **Cadence/RPE values are common teaching norms, not universal standards.**

## Verify
- [ ] Every bike checked for saddle height and fore/aft before class.
- [ ] Ride plan written with time, cadence, resistance/RPE per segment.
- [ ] Music BPM matched to cadence targets.
- [ ] No cues below ~60 or above ~110 RPM at high load.
