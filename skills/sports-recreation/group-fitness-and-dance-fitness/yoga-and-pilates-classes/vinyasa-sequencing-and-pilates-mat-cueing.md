# Yoga and Pilates classes — sequencing and mat cueing

**Goal:** teach fitness-oriented yoga and mat Pilates classes that are
logically sequenced, clearly cued and accessible at multiple levels. This
skill treats yoga as a movement class; it does not cover the broader
philosophical or spiritual practice.

## Part A — Vinyasa-style yoga

### 1. Sequence toward a peak
Pick a **peak pose** (e.g. a balance, hip opener or arm balance) and work
backwards: which muscles must be warm, which joints open, which component
actions practised?
Typical 60-min arc:
1. **Centering** (2–5 min): breath awareness, seated or supine.
2. **Warm-up** (5–10 min): cat-cow, gentle twists, low lunges.
3. **Sun salutations** (10 min): build heat; A then B variations.
4. **Standing sequence** (15–20 min): warriors, triangle, side angle,
   balances, building to the peak.
5. **Peak** and counter-poses.
6. **Floor** (10 min): hip openers, backbends, forward folds, twists.
7. **Savasana** (5 min+): final relaxation — don't cut it.
- Balance each side; mirror sequences left and right.
- Counter-pose after intense actions (backbend → gentle forward fold or twist).

### 2. Breath-linked cueing
- Movement on the breath: "**inhale** lift the arms, **exhale** fold".
  Cue the breath first, then the action.
- Cue from the ground up: feet, legs, pelvis, spine, arms, head.
- Give **one or two** alignment points per pose (e.g. warrior II: front knee
  over ankle, pointing toward the second toe).
- Hold times: 3–5 breaths standard in flow classes.

### 3. Levels and props
- Offer options at every stage: knees down in chaturanga (or skip to
  upward/cobra), hands on blocks in lunges and triangle, straps for
  hamstrings, blankets under knees/hips.
- Present props as tools, not as "easier versions".
- Hands-on adjustments only with explicit consent (consent cards/chips are common).

## Part B — Mat Pilates

### 1. Principles to teach
Commonly cited: breath, centring/**powerhouse** (deep abdominals,
pelvic floor, glutes, back extensors), control, precision, flow.
- **Neutral spine vs imprint:** neutral keeps natural curves; imprint
  gently presses the lower back toward the mat — beginners often imprint
  in exercises with legs lifted to protect the low back.
- Breath: usually exhale on effort, ribs knitting down.

### 2. Core mat repertoire (classical names)
Hundred, roll-up, single leg circles, rolling like a ball, single leg
stretch, double leg stretch, spine stretch forward, saw, swan, side kick
series, teaser, swimming, plank variations. Contemporary programmes add
pre-Pilates foundation work (pelvic tilts, bridging, chest lifts).
- Teach **foundations first**; earn the advanced variations (teaser, roll-over).
- Typical reps: 5–10 slow, controlled; quality over quantity.

### 3. Cueing Pilates
- Precise, anatomical-but-plain language: "lengthen the back of the neck",
  "knit ribs toward hips", "reach legs long from the hip".
- Use imagery sparingly; check it lands with the group.
- Watch for neck strain in curl-ups (offer head support, lower the head).

## Contraindications and modifications (both)
- **Osteoporosis:** avoid loaded spinal flexion (roll-ups, deep forward
  folds with rounding) and end-range twisting.
- **Pregnancy:** avoid deep twists across the belly, prone work later on,
  and prolonged supine positions after the first trimester (common guidance).
- **Hypertension:** avoid prolonged inversions and breath holding.
- Refer to specialised training for therapeutic classes. **This is general
  guidance, not medical advice.**

## Qualifications
Yoga: commonly a 200-hour teacher training (e.g. Yoga Alliance RYT-200);
Pilates: recognised mat certification. Check gym and insurance requirements.

## Gotchas
- Random pose order with no peak or counter-poses.
- Rushing or skipping savasana.
- Advanced Pilates exercises taught before imprint/neutral is understood.

## Verify
- [ ] Sequence plan names the peak and the prep poses.
- [ ] Every pose cued breath first, then action and one alignment point.
- [ ] Props/regressions offered throughout.
