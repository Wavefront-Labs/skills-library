# Group class instruction — cueing, phrasing and scaling

**Goal:** run a class where everyone, from first-timer to regular, moves
safely, knows what's coming before it happens, and leaves wanting to come
back. Applies to any format: dance, step, HIIT, cycling, barre, aqua.

## 1. Before class
- Arrive early: test sound, mic and equipment; set out regressions kit
  (lighter weights, blocks).
- **Screen** new participants: ask about injuries, pregnancy, recent
  illness, medical conditions; many facilities use a **PAR-Q** form.
  Offer options, never diagnose. Refer anyone with red flags (chest pain,
  dizziness) to a medical professional before exercise.
- Know emergency procedures, AED location and first aid.
- Introduce yourself to newcomers, and position them where they can see you.

## 2. Class arc
1. **Warm-up** (5–10 min): rehearse movements of the main block at lower
   intensity; raise heart rate gradually.
2. **Main block(s):** peaks and recoveries; build intensity in waves.
3. **Cool-down** (5 min): lower intensity, then stretching/mobility.
Write the plan with times; know where you can cut if running over.

## 3. Cueing
Cue **before** the change — typically **4 counts ahead** in music-driven
formats ("four more… and two… switch to…"). Layers:
- **What:** name of the move ("squat").
- **How:** one key form point ("knees track over toes").
- **Where/when:** direction and count ("to the right, in 4").
- **Why/motivation:** occasionally, not constantly.
Use **visual cues** too: hand signals for number of reps, direction
(point), "watch me" for new moves, thumbs to check in.
- **Mirror** facing the class: if you say "right", move your left.
- One cue at a time; avoid talking constantly — quiet moments let people
  hear the music and their bodies.

## 4. Music and phrasing
- Most fitness music is in **32-count phrases** (4 × 8 counts); pre-mixed
  fitness tracks keep the phrase consistent. Start new moves at the
  top of a phrase (count 1 of 32) for a professional feel.
- Find "the one": the strong beat that starts a phrase, often a cymbal or
  change in the melody.
- Tempos (rough, varies by format/certification): warm-up ~120–130 BPM,
  floor/aerobic ~130–140 BPM, step ~118–128 BPM, strength ~125–132 BPM.
  **Approximate industry norms; check your programme's guidelines.**
- Music licensing: public performance in classes typically needs a
  licence (via the venue or licensed fitness-music providers).

## 5. Scaling for mixed abilities
For every main exercise prepare **three levels**: regression, base,
progression. E.g. push-up: incline (hands on bench) → knees → full →
decline. Jump squat → squat → low-impact step-out.
- Teach the base, show options, then let people choose; demonstrate the
  regression sometimes so it isn't stigmatised.
- Offer **low-impact** alternatives for all jumps.
- Watch faces and form; correct the group first, then individuals quietly.

## 6. Voice and presence
- Use a headset mic; speak from the diaphragm, don't shout over music.
  Keep music volume moderate (many guidelines suggest staying around
  ~85–90 dB or below for participants; **check local standards**).
- Hydrate; voice strain is the most common instructor injury after joints.
- Energy follows you: smile, make eye contact, use names.

## 7. Qualifications
Most gyms require a recognised group exercise certification (e.g. ACE,
AFAA, or registers such as REPs/CIMSPA-recognised qualifications in some
countries), CPR/first aid and insurance.

## Gotchas
- Cueing on the change instead of before it.
- Teaching to the front-row regulars only.
- Exercises with no low-impact option.

## Verify
- [ ] New participants screened and positioned.
- [ ] Every main exercise has regression and progression planned.
- [ ] Transitions cued 4 counts ahead, on the phrase.
- [ ] Class finished on time with a cool-down.
