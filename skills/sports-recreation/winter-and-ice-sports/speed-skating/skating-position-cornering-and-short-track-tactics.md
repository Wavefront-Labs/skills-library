# Speed skating — position, cornering and short-track tactics

**Goal:** skate faster with less effort by holding a low, relaxed position,
pushing sideways (not backwards), carrying speed through corners, and — in
short track — racing tactically. Long track is a 400 m oval in pairs; short
track is a 111.12 m oval in packs.

## 1. Equipment
- **Long track:** **clap skates** (klapskates) hinge at the front, so the blade
  stays on the ice longer at the end of the push. Long, flat blades.
- **Short track:** blades mounted offset to the left and **bent** (curved to the
  left) to follow tight corners; fixed (non-clap) boots, high rocker.
- **Sharpening** on a jig with stones, plus setting rocker and bend — a learned
  skill; a burr or wrong bend makes you slip in corners.
- **Short-track safety (ISU and most clubs require):** helmet, cut-resistant suit,
  gloves, neck guard, shin and knee guards, glasses. Blades are razor sharp — padded
  barriers exist for a reason.

## 2. Position
- Knees bent deeply (roughly 90–110° at the knee is often cited), hips back,
  upper body near horizontal and relaxed, back rounded slightly, head neutral.
- Weight over the middle/heel of the blade, not the toes.
- Arms: swinging for starts and sprints, one or both on the back for
  endurance.
- Holding position is a strength-endurance job — dryland "low walks", slide
  boards and wall sits build it.

## 3. The straight-line push
- Push **sideways**, not backwards: the blade runs forward on an edge while the
  leg extends to the side. Full extension, then recover the leg under the hip.
- Set the new blade down under the body, flat, before pushing. Common fault:
  standing up during recovery (bobbing) — wastes energy and air resistance.

## 4. Cornering
- **Crossovers** (right over left, counter-clockwise direction): left skate on
  outside edge, right skate on inside edge, both pushing sideways out of the curve.
- Lean in from the ankles, keep hips low. Short track: left hand often touches
  the ice for balance.
- Enter wide, hit the apex, exit wide — and accelerate out of the corner, not
  into it.

## 5. Long-track racing
- Distances 500 m to 10,000 m plus mass start and team pursuit.
- **Lane changes:** skaters swap inner/outer lanes on the crossing straight
  each lap; the skater coming from the outer lane has priority.
- **Pacing:** opener is fast, then target even lap times; in 1500 m+ most
  athletes lose 0.5–1 s per lap at the end. Splits on a board or coach call.

## 6. Short-track tactics
- **Passing:** most passes are on the **inside** coming out of a corner, or a
  wide pass on the outside with more speed. The overtaker is responsible for
  avoiding contact — impeding draws penalties/disqualification.
- **Position:** leading lets you dictate pace but costs drag; 2nd/3rd conserves
  energy for a late move. Watch the lap counter.
- **Relay exchanges:** the incoming skater pushes the outgoing skater from
  behind (on the hips) to hand over; the outgoing skater must be accelerating.
  Practise timing — bad exchanges crash the pack.

## Verify
- [ ] Filmed side-on: knee angle low and upper body still on the straights.
- [ ] Push direction is to the side, leg fully extended.
- [ ] Corners: speed out is ≥ speed in.
- [ ] Short track: full safety kit worn; you can execute a relay push at speed.
- [ ] Lap split chart for your race distance with a target pace.
