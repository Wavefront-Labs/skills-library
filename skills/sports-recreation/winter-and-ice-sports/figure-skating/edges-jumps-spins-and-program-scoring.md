# Figure skating — edges, jumps, spins and programme scoring

**Goal:** build the skills ladder in the right order (edges first), recognise
each jump, and understand how a programme earns points under the ISU Judging
System (IJS) so training time goes where scores come from.

## 1. Blades and boots
- Figure blades have a **rocker** (curved profile) and **toe picks** at the front.
  Skating too far forward catches the pick; most skating is on the middle-back.
- Each blade has an **inside and outside edge** separated by a hollow; sharpening
  sets the hollow depth — a skate technician matters. Blunt edges slide out on
  jumps. Guards on when walking, soakers off and blades dried after skating to
  prevent rust.
- Boots stiff enough for your jump level; heel locked in.

## 2. Edges and turns — the foundation
- **Forward and backward crossovers** for speed and curves.
- **Edges:** long, controlled forward/back inside and outside edges, free leg
  extended, knee bent. Weak edges are the root of most jump problems.
- **Turns:** three-turns (change direction on one foot, edge changes), mohawks
  (change direction foot to foot), brackets, rockers, counters (advanced).

## 3. The six jumps (all land backward outside edge, typically right foot for
counter-clockwise rotators)
| Jump | Take-off | Picked? | Points (rel.) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Toe loop | back outside edge | toe pick | lowest |
| Salchow | back inside edge | edge | low |
| Loop | back outside edge | edge | middle |
| Flip | back inside edge | toe pick | higher |
| Lutz | back **outside** edge (counter-rotated) | toe pick | higher |
| Axel | **forward** outside edge, extra half rotation | edge | highest |
- **Flip vs Lutz** differ only by take-off edge; a wrong or unclear edge earns an
  **edge call** ("e" or "!") and loses value.
- **Under-rotation:** landing short of full rotation (“q” quarter, "<" under, "<<"
  downgraded) cuts the base value.
- Progression: waltz jump → single salchow/toe → loop → flip → lutz → axel →
  doubles. Off-ice jump drills (rotation in place, harness work) speed this up.

## 4. Spins
Upright/scratch, sit, camel (horizontal free leg), layback; combinations change
position and/or foot. Centre the spin on the ball of the foot behind the pick;
travelling spins mean an off-centre entry. Levels (1–4) come from features such
as difficult variations, change of edge, and 8+ revolutions in a position.

## 5. How IJS scoring works (overview)
- **Technical Element Score (TES):** each element (jump, spin, step sequence,
  lifts in pairs) has a **base value** from the ISU Scale of Values, adjusted by
  **Grade of Execution (GOE)** from −5 to +5 by judges. Jumps in the second half
  of a free skate carry a bonus at many levels.
- **Program Component Score (PCS):** judged artistic/skating quality
  (currently composition, presentation and skating skills), multiplied by a
  factor.
- **Deductions:** falls, time violations, costume issues.
- **Well-balanced programme rules** limit how many jumps and repeats you can do.
- Practical consequence: a clean double with + GOE can outscore a fallen triple;
  pick content you land reliably.

*Base values, PCS component names and well-balanced rules change by ISU
communication each season — check the current one.*

## 6. Pairs and ice dance (pointers)
Pairs add lifts, throw jumps and death spirals; ice dance has no jumps, stresses
patterns, twizzles and timing. Both need dedicated coaching.

## Verify
- [ ] You can hold every edge (FO, FI, BO, BI) for a full circle each foot.
- [ ] You can name a jump from its take-off (edge vs pick, forward vs back).
- [ ] Flip and Lutz take-off edges filmed and checked for edge calls.
- [ ] Spins stay in one spot for 6+ revolutions.
- [ ] Planned programme checked against the season's Scale of Values and rules.
