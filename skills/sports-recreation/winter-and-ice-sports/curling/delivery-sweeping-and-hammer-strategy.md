# Curling — delivery, sweeping and hammer strategy

**Goal:** throw a stone on the line and weight the skip calls, sweep it
usefully, and understand why teams blank ends or put up guards. Four players
per team (lead, second, vice/third, skip), eight stones each, usually 8 or 10 ends.

## 1. Gear and ice
- **Slider** sole under the sliding foot, **gripper** on the other. Club shoes
  with slip-on sliders are fine to start. Clean shoes — grit on the ice ruins it.
- **Broom/brush** (synthetic pad) for sweeping and, for many, as a stabiliser
  during delivery; a **stabiliser** (a padded support) is common for beginners.
- The ice is **pebbled** (sprayed droplets), which is why stones curl. Never
  touch it with bare hands or kneel on it longer than needed.

## 2. The delivery
1. Foot in the **hack**, stone in front, broom/stabiliser on the other side,
   eyes on the skip's broom (the target).
2. Lift, draw back, then **push out of the hack** with the sliding foot moving
   under the body and the trailing leg extended behind.
3. Slide on a straight line at the broom — the stone, your sliding foot and the
   target in one line. The arm stays relaxed; weight comes mostly from the leg
   drive, not the arm.
4. **Release** before the near hog line with a gentle handle **rotation**
   (about 2–4 full turns down the sheet) — **in-turn** (clockwise for a
   right-hander, curls right) or **out-turn** (anticlockwise, curls left). A stone
   curls in the direction it rotates. The skip calls it.
5. Hog-line rule: the stone must be released before the hog line (many venues use
   sensor handles).

## 3. Weight and sweeping
- **Weight** = speed. Draw weight stops in the house; guard weight stops in
  front of it; takeout weight hits and removes. Many teams talk in "zones" or
  split times (hog-to-hog seconds).
- **Sweeping** briefly warms/cleans the ice: the stone travels **further** and
  **curls less**. It cannot make a stone go shorter or curl more.
- So: the thrower and sweepers judge weight; the skip judges line. Calls:
  "Hard!" / "Yes!" (sweep), "Off!" / "Whoa!" (stop). Sweep across the stone's
  path just in front of it, with pressure and speed.
- Past the tee line, only one sweeper from each team may sweep their own or
  the opposition's stone (rules vary slightly for mixed doubles and by level).

## 4. Positions
- **Lead:** throws stones 1–2, mostly guards and draws; sweeps the rest.
- **Second:** stones 3–4, often takeouts.
- **Vice (third):** stones 5–6, holds the broom when the skip throws, agrees
  the score.
- **Skip:** stones 7–8, calls the game.

## 5. Shot types
Draw, guard (centre or corner), takeout, **hit and roll** (remove and roll
behind cover), **peel** (remove a guard and the shooter), **freeze** (draw to
rest against another stone), **tick** (nudge a guard sideways — legal under the
modified FGZ).

## 6. Hammer and strategy
- **Hammer** = last stone of the end; the team that scores gives it up. With
  the hammer you want **two or more** points; scoring one is often treated as
  a modest outcome. If only one is available, you can **blank** the end (clear the
  house) to keep the hammer.
- **Without** the hammer you want to **steal** (score anyway) or force the
  opposition to take one.
- **Five-rock rule (free guard zone):** the first five stones of an end (in
  most current rules) cannot remove opponent stones in the free guard zone
  (between hog line and tee line, outside the house); it protects guards and
  creates offence. Know whether your league uses the four- or five-rock version.
- Generally: leading late → keep the house clean (takeouts); trailing → put up
  guards and get stones in play.

## Verify
- [ ] Your slide finishes balanced, on the broom line.
- [ ] You can throw both in-turn and out-turn with 2–4 rotations.
- [ ] You know: sweeping = further + straighter, never shorter.
- [ ] You can explain why a team with the hammer might blank an end.
- [ ] You know your league's free guard zone variant.
