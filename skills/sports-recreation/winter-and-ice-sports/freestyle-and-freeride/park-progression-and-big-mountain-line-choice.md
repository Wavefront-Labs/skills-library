# Freestyle and freeride skiing — park progression and line choice

**Goal:** build tricks in a safe order in the park, and pick big-mountain lines
you can actually ski — with an honest read of consequences. Assumes solid
parallel skiing (see sports-recreation.winter-and-ice-sports.alpine-skiing.parallel-turns-carving-and-mogul-technique).

## 1. Setup
- **Park:** twin-tip skis (turned-up tails for riding switch), mounted closer to
  centre; shorter for spinning, softer flex for butters and rails. Detuning the
  edges under the foot reduces rail catches.
- **Freeride:** wider (≈100–115 mm+ waist) for floating powder, longer, often
  rockered tips; bindings with a higher DIN range set properly by a shop.
- Helmet always; back protector common in the park. Big-mountain:
  avalanche transceiver, probe, shovel, and a partner who can use them
  — and the training (see backcountry-and-ski-touring).

## 2. Park trick progression
1. **Ride switch** comfortably on groomed runs (looking over a shoulder).
2. **Straight airs** off small jumps: speed check, pop off the lip, stay
   centred, land on the downslope. Add a **grab** (e.g. safety: hand to outside
   of the ski under the foot) — grabs stabilise you.
3. **180s:** take off forward, land switch. Set up the rotation with shoulders
   *before* the lip; spin from the legs and hips, not by throwing the arms late.
4. **360s:** same, more wind-up; spot the landing early. Then 540, 720 as speed
   and air time allow.
5. **Boxes → rails:** start on wide, low boxes. Pop on, skis across the feature
   (or straight for a 50-50 style), weight centred, flat bases, look at the end.
6. **Off-axis spins and flips** belong on an airbag, trampoline or water ramp
   first; do not learn inverts on snow without coaching.
Etiquette: look uphill before dropping in, call your drop, never sit on landings.

## 3. How judged runs are scored (overview)
Slopestyle, big air, halfpipe and freeride competitions use an overall
impression score. Common criteria: **difficulty** of tricks, **execution**
(clean landings, style, grab hold), **amplitude**, **variety** (spin directions,
rail and jump mix) and **progression**. Freeride World Tour judges line,
fluidity, control, air and style, technique. *Exact criteria wording differs
per tour/federation; read the event's judging criteria.*

## 4. Big-mountain line choice
1. **Scout from the bottom and from a viewpoint**: photograph the face, mark
   entry, exit and key features (rocks, airs, chokes).
2. **Exposure:** identify **no-fall zones** — above cliffs, rock bands, or long
   slides into trouble. If you would not ski it perfectly on a bad day, don't.
3. **Snow:** wind slab, sastrugi, refrozen crust vs. powder — the same line skis
   differently every hour. Check avalanche forecast danger rating and aspect.
4. **Sluff management:** on steep powder your own loose snow (sluff) chases you.
   Ski across, let it pass, then continue; avoid stopping under your sluff path.
5. **Cornices:** never walk to the edge; they break further back than expected.
6. **Airs/drops:** know the landing — steep, deep and clear of rocks. Flat
   landings break legs. Spot a marker visible from the top.
7. **Exit plan:** where you'll regroup, and what happens if you fall.

## Common mistakes
- Spinning off the lip with no speed check → overshooting into flats.
- Rails with edges engaged → "catching" and falling across the rail.
- Freeride lines chosen from top only → can't recognise the entry.

## Verify
- [ ] Switch skiing is easy on blue runs before you spin.
- [ ] Each new trick learned on the smallest feature first.
- [ ] For a freeride line: entry, features, no-fall zones and exit identified from below.
- [ ] Avalanche forecast checked; rescue kit carried and partner briefed off-piste.
