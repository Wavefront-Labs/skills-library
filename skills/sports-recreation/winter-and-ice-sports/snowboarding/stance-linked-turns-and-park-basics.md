# Snowboarding — stance, linked turns and park basics

**Goal:** get from sliding sideways to linked, controlled turns, then into the
park with a clear progression that avoids the classic beginner injuries
(wrists, tailbone, head).

## 1. Setup
- **Regular or goofy:** which foot leads. Tests: the foot you step forward with
  when gently pushed from behind, or the foot you slide with on a slick floor.
  If unsure, regular (left forward) is more common — try both on day one.
- **Stance width:** about shoulder width or a little wider; many boards mark a
  reference stance.
- **Binding angles:** a common all-mountain start is a mild **duck** stance, e.g.
  front +15°, back −6° to −9°. Freeriders often run both angles forward (e.g.
  +18/+6). Carvers go more forward. Re-check screws after the first runs.
- **Boots** snug enough that the heel does not lift on toeside. Wrist guards and
  a helmet for the first season; impact shorts help with tailbone falls.

## 2. Day-one progression
1. **Skating:** front foot strapped, push with the back foot (for lift lines).
2. **Heelside side-slip:** facing downhill, sitting slightly, control speed by
   tilting toes up and down. Then **toeside** (facing uphill, on knees if you fall).
3. **Falling leaf:** while side-slipping, shift weight to the front foot to drift
   one way, back foot to drift the other. Builds steering without turning.
4. **First turns:** from heelside, look and point the leading hand where you want
   to go, flatten the board through the fall line, roll onto toeside. Then back.
5. **Linked turns:** continuous S-turns; speed controlled by finishing each turn
   across the hill.

## 3. The edge catch (the big crash)
A board is a long edge. **Catching the downhill edge** slams you down instantly —
backwards on heelside (head/tailbone), forward on toeside (wrists/face).
Prevention: always keep a little tilt onto the **uphill** edge when moving across
the hill; flatten only briefly through the fall line. Falling: tuck chin, fall on
forearms/backside, don't put straight arms out.

## 4. Carving and riding
- Weight centred or slightly forward at the start of the turn; knees bent,
  shoulders roughly aligned with the board. Counter-rotating the shoulders against
  the board causes skids.
- **Carve:** edge the board early and let the sidecut turn it; a clean pencil
  line on groomed snow.
- **Switch:** riding with the other foot forward — learn it early; needed in the park.

## 5. Park basics
- **Park Smart / "smart style" principles** (signage varies by country): look
  before you drop, call your drop, start small, scout every feature first at slow
  speed, don't stop on landings, respect the features' size ratings (S/M/L).
- **Ollie:** load the tail (lean back slightly, bend back knee), spring off the
  tail, suck both knees up. Nollie = from the nose.
- **Jumps:** speed check at the top, ride straight and flat (no edge) up the
  takeoff, stay centred in the air, land on the downslope (the landing), not the
  "knuckle" or flat. Too little speed (landing on the knuckle) is the common
  beginner mistake.
- **Boxes and rails:** start with a wide, ground-level box. **50-50** (board
  pointing along the feature) first, then **boardslide** (board across). Flat
  base on the feature — any edge catch on a rail throws you off hard.
- **Halfpipe** and **boardercross** need coaching; not covered here.

## 6. Chairlifts and drags
Front foot strapped, back foot free. Drag (T-bar/button) lifts are hard on a
board: weight on the back foot, don't sit down.

## Verify
- [ ] You know your lead foot and angles; screws checked.
- [ ] Falling leaf done on both edges without catching the downhill edge.
- [ ] Ten linked turns on a green/beginner run with steady speed.
- [ ] Each park feature scouted once before you hit it; start with S features.
- [ ] Wrist guards and helmet worn while learning.
