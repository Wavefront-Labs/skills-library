# Alpine skiing — parallel turns, carving and moguls

**Goal:** progress from controlled wedge turns to round parallel turns, clean
carves and confident bumps/steeps, by fixing balance first and edges second.
Applies to recreational skiers and anyone coaching them informally.

## 1. Equipment that decides whether technique is possible
- **Boots matter more than skis.** A boot that lets the heel lift or the foot
  slide forward kills edge control. Shell-fit check: foot slid forward to touch
  the toe, roughly one to two fingers' gap behind the heel. A bootfitter can
  heat-mould liners and punch shells. Flex: softer for beginners, stiffer as
  speed rises.
- **Binding release value (DIN)** is set from weight, height, boot sole length,
  age and skier type (I/II/III) on the manufacturer's chart — have a shop set
  and torque-test it. Do not crank it up yourself to stop pre-release; that is
  how knees get hurt.
- Ski length: beginners around chin height, carvers nose-to-head; sharp, tuned
  edges matter on hardpack.

## 2. The progression
1. **Wedge (snowplough) turns:** tips together, tails apart, pressure the outside
   ski to turn. Goal: speed control by turn shape, not by a wider wedge.
2. **Wedge christie / stem:** start in a wedge, match the inside ski to the
   outside ski after the fall line. Gradually match earlier.
3. **Parallel:** both skis tip onto new edges together. Drill: traverse, lift the
   inside ski tail slightly, roll the knees/ankles into the hill.
4. **Carving:** roll skis onto edge and let the sidecut make the turn — tracks
   are two clean lines, no smear. Build angles from the ankles and knees
   (angulation) and keep the upper body facing slightly down the hill rather
   than leaning the whole body in (inclination only).
5. **Short turns:** upper body quiet and facing down the fall line, legs turn
   under it. Use a **pole plant** (a light touch, timed at the end of each turn,
   down the hill) to trigger the next one.

## 3. Balance cues that fix most problems
- **Fore-aft:** shins touching the boot tongues all the time; hands forward
  where you can see them. "Back seat" (weight on the tails) is the single most
  common fault — skis run away, turns skid.
- **Outside ski:** most pressure goes to the outside ski in each turn.
- **Hip over feet:** stand tall in the boot, not folded at the waist.

## 4. Moguls and steeps
- **Moguls:** look 2–3 bumps ahead, turn on the top or the shoulder of each
  bump, absorb by bending knees as you rise and extend into the trough. Keep
  skis close; pole plant on the top of the next bump. Start on soft, small
  bumps — icy moguls punish everything.
- **Steeps:** commit to the fall line with the upper body; short, round turns
  finished across the hill control speed. Hop turns for very steep or narrow
  terrain. Hesitation that pushes weight uphill is what causes slides.

## 5. Gates (intro)
Slalom is short, quick turns around hinged poles; giant slalom (GS) is longer,
faster arcs. Begin turns *above* the gate so the apex is at or before it — late
turns pile up. Racing needs back protection and, in slalom, shin guards and
pole guards. *Exact course-setting distances vary by FIS level; not covered here.*

## 6. Safety and etiquette
- **Skier responsibility code (FIS rules / national codes):** the person below
  you has priority; overtake with room; stop only where you are visible from above;
  look uphill before entering a run; respect signs and closures.
- Helmet; know your limits by run grading (colours differ between Europe and
  North America — a European red is roughly a North American blue/black,
  but grading is not standardised).
- Cold, flat light and fatigue cause most injuries — the "last run" of the day
  is a cliché for a reason.

## Common faults → fix
| Fault | Likely cause | Drill |
|---|---|---|
| Skis run away | back seat | hands forward, shin pressure, hop turns on gentle slope |
| Skidding, no carve | rotating upper body | "railroad tracks" on green runs |
| Inside ski wanders | weight on inside ski | lift inside tail through the turn |
| Stuck in wedge | fear of speed | more turn shape, flatter terrain |

## Verify
- [ ] Boots fitted and DIN set by a shop from the chart, not guessed.
- [ ] You can link parallel turns on a blue/intermediate run with speed controlled by turn shape.
- [ ] Carved turns leave two thin tracks on groomed snow.
- [ ] In moguls you can see three bumps ahead and keep a rhythm for 10+ turns.
- [ ] You can state the priority rule (downhill skier has right of way).
