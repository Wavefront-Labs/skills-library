# Terrain technique and team scoring tactics

**Goal:** run efficiently on grass, mud and hills, and use the team scoring rules so
every runner's place counts — including pushing rivals back.

> Draft: scoring here follows the common US high-school/NCAA model (5 scorers, up
> to 7 runners). Other systems differ (e.g. UK leagues and championships score 4, 6
> or more runners; some count positions only of complete teams). Always check the
> meet's rules.

## Team scoring (common model)
- Each team's first **five** finishers score their **finishing place** among
  scoring teams; lowest total wins. Runners 6 and 7 don't score but **displace** —
  they take places that push other teams' scorers down.
- Runners from incomplete teams (fewer than five finishers) are usually removed from
  the scoring order.
- **Tiebreaker** is commonly the sixth runner's place (rules vary).
- **Spread** (gap between 1st and 5th scorer) matters: a team with a tight pack and no
  stars can beat a team with one star and a weak fifth.

## Pack running
- Agree target positions/pace with teammates; the 3–5 runners run together, sharing
  pacing and encouragement, then break up in the last kilometre.
- Coaches often split at a landmark to tell each runner how many to pass.
- **Every place matters**: passing one runner in the final 200 m is worth one
  point to your team and one against another — the difference in tight meets.

## Course and start
- **Walk the course**: hills, turns, mud, narrow sections, the last 400 m. Find the
  **first narrowing** — position before it matters.
- Wide starts with a box per team funnel quickly; the first 300–600 m is often run
  harder than race pace to avoid getting stuck. Don't overdo it: you have to recover.

## Terrain technique
- **Uphill**: shorten stride, increase cadence, lean slightly from the ankles, drive
  arms. Keep effort steady rather than pace. Attack over the crest — many runners ease
  off at the top.
- **Downhill**: relax, lean with the slope, quick light steps, arms out for balance.
  Fast downhilling is free time if you're confident.
- **Mud**: shorter strides, feet under hips, look for firmer lines at the edges
  (if inside the course boundary).
- **Turns**: take the tangent line; accelerate out.

## Spikes
- Choose spike length by ground: longer (e.g. 9–15 mm) for deep mud and soft grass,
  shorter for firm ground; some courses ban spikes on paved sections or require flats.
  Check meet rules on maximum length.
- Tie laces double and tight — losing a shoe in mud is common.

## Gotchas
- Going out too fast to get position, then going backwards for the last 3 km.
- Fixating on watch pace — terrain makes pace meaningless; race effort and place.
- Elbow wars at the start; protect space but avoid contact penalties.
