# Long jump approach, check marks and take-off

**Goal:** hit the board at near-maximum controllable speed with a consistent last few
strides, then take off upward and forward without stalling — the run-up is most of
the jump.

> Draft: standard coaching practice. Step counts and distances are examples; your
> approach is measured on *you*. Triple jump uses the same approach principles; its
> phase rhythm is noted briefly at the end.

## Build the approach
1. **Count strides**: young/novice jumpers 10–14 strides, experienced 16–22 (men's
   elites often ~20+). Even numbers start with the take-off foot back.
2. **Measure on the track**: run your approach from a start line away from the pit
   (backwards approach is fine: run toward a start mark and note where foot N lands),
   repeat until consistent, then measure the distance with a tape (in feet or metres,
   or in foot-lengths).
3. **Transfer to the runway**: place your **start mark** that distance from the board.
4. **Check mark**: a coach watches a mark ~4–6 strides from the board (or at stride 4
   from the start — the "coach mark") to see whether the jumper hits it consistently.
   Tune the start mark, not mid-run steps.

## Run-up rhythm
- Same start every time (walk-in, rocking start, or standing — pick one).
- **Accelerate progressively**: drive phase, then tall upright running by halfway,
  reaching near-top usable speed in the last 4–6 strides.
- Don't **stutter or reach** for the board; if the step is off, pass on it in training,
  and in competition adjust the start mark for the next attempt.

## Penultimate step and take-off
- **Penultimate stride** is slightly longer, lowering the hips a little (center-of-mass
  drop); the **last stride is shorter** and quicker.
- **Plant**: take-off foot lands flat-ish and active (pawing back), slightly ahead of
  the hips. Don't heel-jam or sit back.
- **Drive**: free knee drives up to horizontal, opposite arm up, eyes forward and up,
  chest tall. Take-off angle around 18–25° — not a high jump.
- **Flight**: sail (simple), hang, or hitch-kick (running in the air) — all are mostly
  about controlling forward rotation for the landing.
- **Landing**: extend legs forward, heels in the sand first, then collapse forward or
  sideways past the mark — falling back loses distance (measured to the nearest mark).

## Rules to remember
- Foul if any part of the take-off foot touches the ground beyond the take-off line
  (the plasticine/indicator board), or running through.
- Measured from the take-off line, not where you took off — every cm behind it is lost.
- Wind over +2.0 m/s makes a mark ineligible for records (still counts in the comp).

## Triple jump in one line
Hop (same foot), step (other foot), jump: roughly even-ish phases (a common target is
~35%/30%/35%), flat hop to keep speed, active landing on each contact.

## Gotchas
- Lengthening the approach without the speed to use it → slow, uncontrolled board.
- Headwind/tailwind change step length: be ready to move your start mark 15–30 cm.
- Looking at the board causes chopping; trust the mark, look ahead.

## Verify
- [ ] Approach distance and stride count written down; start routine fixed.
- [ ] Coach mark hit within a few centimetres on most runs.
- [ ] Take-off within ~10–20 cm behind the line in training jumps.
- [ ] Video shows a longer penultimate and a tall, active take-off.
