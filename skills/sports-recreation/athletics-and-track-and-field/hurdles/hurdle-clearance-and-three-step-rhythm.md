# Hurdle clearance and three-step rhythm

**Goal:** clear each hurdle with minimal time in the air and keep an even three-step
rhythm between barriers — hurdling is sprinting with obstacles, not jumping.

> Draft: standard coaching technique. Heights/spacings below are the senior standards
> as I know them; youth and masters use lower hurdles and shorter spacings — check
> your federation's tables.

## Standard set-ups (senior, 10 hurdles)
| Event | Height | Start → H1 | Between | Last → finish |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 110mH men | 1.067 m | 13.72 m | 9.14 m | 14.02 m |
| 100mH women | 0.838 m | 13.00 m | 8.50 m | 10.50 m |
| 400mH men | 0.914 m | 45 m | 35 m | 40 m |
| 400mH women | 0.762 m | 45 m | 35 m | 40 m |

## Approach to the first hurdle
Most senior sprint hurdlers take **8 strides** to H1 (some 7); this sets the take-off
foot. Put the lead-leg foot in the **rear** block of an 8-step approach (count back
from take-off). Rise to hurdling posture earlier than a flat sprinter.

## Clearance
1. **Take-off**: attack from roughly 2 m before the barrier (men's 110mH; shorter for
   100mH) — too close and you jump up; too far and you float.
2. **Lead leg**: drive the **knee** up first, then extend the lower leg; heel leads,
   foot just clearing the rail. Don't kick a straight leg up from the hip.
3. **Lean**: chest dips forward over the lead leg; opposite arm reaches forward,
   balancing.
4. **Trail leg**: comes through flat and to the side — knee high, foot turned out
   (toe up), then pulled through to the front quickly so the first step after landing
   is a full sprint stride.
5. **Landing**: on the ball of the lead foot, under the hips, ~1–1.2 m past the
   barrier; snap straight into the next step.

## The three-step rhythm
Between sprint hurdles: land — 1 — 2 — 3 — take off. The three strides are not equal:
short landing step, longer middle, slightly shorter third to set up take-off. If you
can't fit three, the hurdles are too far apart for your current speed — move them
closer in training (shortened spacing is a standard drill), not four-step.

## 400mH stride planning
Stride count between hurdles is the event's core decision: typical men 13–15, women
15–17. Odd counts keep the same lead leg; even counts alternate legs — so 400m
hurdlers train **both lead legs**. Many change down (e.g. 13 → 15) as fatigue sets in;
plan *where*, rather than chopping steps in a panic.

## Gotchas
- **Floating** (high clearance) wastes time; aim for the rail just below the hip line.
- Hitting hurdles is not illegal unless deliberate or knocked with the hand;
  trailing a leg/foot outside the hurdle (beside it, below the height) is a DQ.
- Arm crossing the body throws rotation; keep the lead arm forward and the other
  driving back.
- Learning on full-height barriers too early builds fear and jumping. Use low or
  soft barriers and short spacings first.

## Verify
- [ ] Consistent 8-step (or 7) approach hitting the same take-off point.
- [ ] Video: knee-first lead leg, flat trail leg, minimal flight above the rail.
- [ ] Three-step rhythm maintained across all ten hurdles (or planned pattern in 400mH).
- [ ] Touchdown times (H1 to H2, etc.) recorded and steady.
