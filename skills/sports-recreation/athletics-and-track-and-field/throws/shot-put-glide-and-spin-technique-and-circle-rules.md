# Shot put: glide and spin technique, and circle rules

**Goal:** apply force to the shot over the longest effective path, release it at a
good angle, and stay in the circle so the throw counts.

> Draft: standard coaching technique. Implement weights and rules are senior
> standards as I know them; age groups and masters use lighter implements — check
> your federation. Discus, hammer and javelin share the rule ideas (circle/runway,
> sector, exit) but not the technique.

## Implements and circle (senior)
- Shot: **7.26 kg** men, **4 kg** women. Circle 2.135 m diameter with a curved
  **stop-board** (toe-board) at the front; landing sector ~34.92°.
- Release cue: shot sits at the base of the fingers, against the neck under the jaw,
  elbow up and out. **Push, never throw** — a shot that leaves from behind the shoulder
  line is a foul (and an injury risk).

## The power position (learn first)
Standing throw: feet roughly shoulder-width-plus apart in a heel-toe line, back to the
sector, right foot (for a right-hander) mid-circle and turned, weight on the bent
right leg, left arm long across the body. Then: **legs → hips → chest → arm** in
sequence — drive the right hip round, block with the left side, punch the shot up and
out at ~35–40° (lower than the textbook 45° for real athletes).

## Glide (O'Brien)
1. Start at the back facing away from the sector, weight on the right leg.
2. Drop into a crouch, left leg kicks low toward the stop-board, right leg drives and
   **pulls under** the body — fast, low, not a hop.
3. Land in the power position with the right foot under the hips, shoulders still
   turned away (**separation** between hips and shoulders).
4. Throw from the power position; left side **blocks**.

## Rotational (spin)
1. Start facing away; wind up, then pivot on the left foot while the right leg sweeps
   wide.
2. Run across the circle on the balls of the feet, landing the right foot in the
   middle and the left foot quickly at the board — like a discus turn, but tighter.
3. Deliver from the power position with stronger rotational speed. Higher potential,
   harder to control; many youth throwers start with glide.

## Rules that cost throws
- Begin from a **stationary** position inside the circle.
- After the shot lands, **exit from the rear half** of the circle, with first contact
  with the ground outside on the back half — exiting forward is a foul.
- Touching the **top of the stop-board** or the ground outside the circle during the
  throw is a foul (touching the inside of the board is allowed).
- Shot must land inside the sector lines.
- Measure from the inside edge of the circle to the nearest mark made.

## Gotchas
- **Reverse** (swapping feet after release) prevents fouling; but don't reverse
  before you finish the push.
- Opening the left side early (shoulders turning too soon) leaks power.
- Throwing on a wet circle: shorten the glide/turn; use proper throwing shoes.

## Verify
- [ ] Power-position throws reach a clear distance before full technique is attempted.
- [ ] Video: hip-shoulder separation at landing, block with the left side.
- [ ] Release angle and height measured or estimated (aim ~35–40°).
- [ ] No fouls in practice: rear exit and stationary start every time.
