# Legal race walking technique and the judging rules

**Goal:** walk fast while staying visibly legal, because a walker is only as fast as
the judges allow.

> Draft: rule details follow World Athletics practice as I understand it; event
> distances and penalty-zone times have changed several times recently (e.g. the
> 35 km and the marathon-distance walk). Check the current rulebook and the meet's
> technical information.

## The two rules
1. **Contact**: the walker must have contact with the ground as judged by the human eye
   — **no visible loss of contact** (no flight phase visible to a judge).
2. **Straightened knee**: the advancing leg must be **straightened (not bent at the
   knee) from the moment of first contact until the vertical upright position**.

Note "judged by the human eye": video slow-motion often shows brief flight in elite
walkers; the rule is what judges can see at normal speed.

## How judging works
- Several judges around the course. A judge can show a **yellow paddle** (caution:
  symbol "~" for contact or "<" for bent knee) — advisory only.
- A judge who believes the rule is broken submits a **red card** to the chief judge.
- Red cards from **different** judges accumulate; at a set threshold (commonly
  3 or 4 depending on event and rules) the athlete is penalised: many events now use a
  **penalty zone** (wait for a set time, distance-dependent) before disqualification
  on a further card. Some lower-level meets disqualify directly.
- Red-card count is shown on a board on the course — watch it.

## Technique for legal speed
- **Heel strike with a straight leg**: land on the heel, toes up, knee locked; hold it
  straight as the body passes over.
- **Hip rotation**: hips move forward and around, lengthening the stride without
  over-reaching. Over-striding in front encourages a bent knee and lift.
- **Cadence over stride**: elite walkers sustain very high cadences (often reported
  ~190–210 steps/min); quicker, lower steps keep contact.
- **Arms**: bent ~90°, swing forward and back (not across), hands no higher than
  chest — high arms lift the body.
- **Posture**: tall, slightly forward from the ankles, relaxed shoulders.
- Push off from the toes of the rear foot without pushing *up*.

## Drills
- Straight-leg walking slowly in front of a mirror or video, checking the knee.
- Hip-rotation walking (exaggerated hips, short steps).
- Fast cadence efforts with a metronome.
- Film at race pace from the side and play at normal speed — what a judge sees.

## Gotchas
- **Late race fatigue** is when knees bend and contact goes — technique must survive
  the last 5 km.
- Surging to pass increases lift; accelerate by cadence.
- Feeding stations and turns: walking form must stay legal while drinking.
- A bent-knee habit in training (from easy walking) carries into races — walk legally
  in all training sessions.
