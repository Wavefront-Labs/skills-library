# Race pacing and tactics: 800m to 10,000m

**Goal:** run close to your best possible time — or beat the people in front of you —
by spending energy evenly, staying in a good position, and saving the hard surge for
when it pays.

> Draft: established coaching principles; split targets are examples to compute your
> own, not prescriptions.

## Lap-split arithmetic (400 m track)
- Lap time = goal time ÷ (distance / 400). 5000 m in 18:00 = 1080 s ÷ 12.5 laps =
  **86.4 s per lap**; 1500 m in 4:30 = 270 s ÷ 3.75 laps = **72 s/lap** (the first
  300 m is a partial lap → ~54 s).
- Write splits on a wristband or know the key ones (1 km, 1 mile, halfway).
- Watch for the **start line differences**: the 1500 m starts 300 m from the finish
  line; the 5000 m and 3000 m both start 200 m out (partial
  first laps). Check the track markings.

## Pacing principles by event
- **800 m**: largely anaerobic; elite races are often slightly faster first lap
  (~1–3 s), because the second lap slows regardless. Going out far too fast is the
  common amateur error.
- **1500 m / mile**: even pace is efficient; championship races are often slow early
  then very fast — be ready for a sit-and-kick race.
- **5000 m / 10,000 m**: even or slightly negative split is most efficient. Each second
  too fast per lap early costs more later.

## Position and tactics
- **Lane one** is the shortest path; running wide on bends adds distance (running
  in lane 2 adds roughly 7–8 m per lap — on bends only).
- Sit **on the shoulder** of the leader or just behind and slightly out, so you can
  respond without being **boxed in** (trapped on the rail with runners ahead and
  beside).
- In 800 m, runners start in lanes and break to the inside at the **break line** after
  the first bend; don't cut in early (DQ risk) or sprint the first 100 m chasing position.
- **Front-running** works if you're stronger on pace than kick; **sit and kick**
  if your finishing speed is better.
- Move up gradually — a big surge to pass costs a lot; pass on straights if you can.

## The kick
- Long kick (from 300–400 m out) suits strength runners; short kick (last 150–200 m)
  suits speed. Know which you are from training and racing data.
- Commit fully; looking back costs time.

## Steeplechase specifics
3000 m steeplechase: 28 barrier jumps and 7 water jumps (barriers 0.914 m men,
0.762 m women). Step on the water barrier; land in the shallow end with a quick
second step out. Leave room in front of you at barriers — they don't fall over.

## Gotchas
- Adrenaline makes the first lap feel easy: check the first split and correct.
- Getting spiked or tripped in packs — protect space with your elbows (legally) and
  keep stride consistent.
- Laps can be miscounted in 10,000 m — trust the lap counter board.
