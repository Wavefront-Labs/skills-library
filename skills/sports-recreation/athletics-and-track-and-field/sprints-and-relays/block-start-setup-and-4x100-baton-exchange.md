# Block start setup and 4x100m baton exchange

**Goal:** get out of the blocks fast and legal, and pass the baton at full speed
inside the changeover zone — the two places where sprint races are commonly won or lost.

> Draft: standard coaching practice. Rule details (zone lengths, false-start rules)
> follow current World Athletics rules as I understand them — check the edition your
> competition uses. Block settings are starting points; individuals differ.

## Block setup
1. **Front pedal**: about 1.5–2 foot-lengths behind the start line (heel-to-toe
   measures). **Rear pedal**: roughly another 1–1.5 foot-lengths behind the front.
   Many athletes use "medium" spacing; tall athletes often longer.
2. **Front leg** = usually the stronger/preferred push leg; try both on video.
3. Pedal angles: front flatter (~45°), rear steeper (~60–80°) — adjust for comfort.
4. **On your marks**: hands just behind the line, shoulder-width, bridge of thumb and
   fingers; shoulders over or slightly in front of hands; rear knee down.
5. **Set**: hips rise to slightly above shoulders; front knee ~90°, rear knee
   ~120–135°; weight forward onto hands; **hold still**.

## Start and drive
- React to the gun by **pushing both pedals** hard; rear leg drives through fast,
  arms punch big and opposite.
- **Low heel recovery**, shin angles forward, steps short-to-long: rise gradually over
  the first ~20–30 m — popping up early wastes the push.
- Eyes down/ahead a few metres early, rising naturally with the torso.
- **False starts**: under current World Athletics rules, one false start
  disqualifies the athlete (age-group/local rules may differ). Reaction under 0.100 s
  measured by start sensors counts as a false start.

## 4x100m baton exchange
- **Zone**: the changeover/takeover zone is **30 m** (World Athletics rule change
  from 2018: the old 10 m acceleration zone merged into a 30 m takeover zone). The
  baton — not the runners — must be passed within it.
- **Go mark**: the outgoing runner places a check mark (tape) back down the track,
  measured in foot-lengths (often ~20–30 to start; tune in practice). When the
  incoming runner hits the mark, the outgoing runner sprints flat-out, **not looking
  back**.
- **Call**: incoming runner shouts "hand!" (or "stick!") when ~2 m behind; outgoing
  runner extends the arm back without slowing.
- **Upsweep** (hand opens downward, baton pushed up into the V) or **downsweep**
  (palm up, baton pressed down into it). Downsweep is common at elite level and
  leaves less baton for the next pass; upsweep is often taught as more secure. Choose one
  for the team and drill it.
- **Handedness**: runner 1 (bend) carries right hand → runner 2 receives left; runner
  3 (bend) right; runner 4 left. Each runs on the appropriate side of the lane.

## Gotchas
- Outgoing runner leaving too early = pass outside the zone (DQ); too late =
  incoming runner overtakes and slows. Tune the go mark in practice at race speed.
- Looking back slows the outgoing runner — trust the mark and the call.
- Pass completed only when the baton is in the receiver's hand alone.

## Verify
- [ ] Block settings written down and repeatable.
- [ ] 10/20/30 m split times improving with consistent setup.
- [ ] Go marks tested at full speed; passes completed in the zone on video.
- [ ] Every runner knows hand, lane side and call word.
