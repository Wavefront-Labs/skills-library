# Decathlon/heptathlon scoring strategy and two-day management

**Goal:** maximise total points, not individual event results — which means banking
safe marks, avoiding zeros, and having energy left for the last event.

> Draft: event orders and the scoring formula are as used by World Athletics; exact
> coefficients come from the official combined-events scoring tables (use a published
> calculator; don't reconstruct them by hand). Indoor pentathlon/heptathlon orders differ.

## Event order (outdoor)
- **Decathlon** — Day 1: 100 m, long jump, shot put, high jump, 400 m.
  Day 2: 110 m hurdles, discus, pole vault, javelin, 1500 m.
- **Heptathlon** — Day 1: 100 m hurdles, high jump, shot put, 200 m.
  Day 2: long jump, javelin, 800 m.

## How scoring works
- Each event converts a mark to points with a formula of the form
  **points = A × (B − T)^C** for running (T = time) and **A × (M − B)^C** for jumps and
  throws (M = distance/height), with event-specific constants.
- Consequence: the tables are slightly **progressive** — improvements at higher levels
  earn a bit more per unit. And a **no mark** (three fouls, no height) scores **zero** —
  catastrophic compared with a modest mark.
- Use a scoring calculator to build a target sheet: your PBs → points, and what a
  realistic "good day" mark per event yields.

## Strategy
1. **Points-per-effort**: find events where a small improvement buys many points
   (often technical events like pole vault and hurdles for developing athletes) and
   prioritise them in training.
2. **Bank a mark**: in long jump and throws, first attempt is a safe, non-foul
   throw/jump (e.g. take off well behind the board); attack on attempts 2–3.
3. **Opening heights** (HJ, PV): start low enough that clearing is near-certain. A
   no-height in pole vault ends most competitive decathlons. Passing heights saves
   energy but raises the risk; decide in advance.
4. **Track the running total** against the target sheet after each event; adjust
   risk (e.g. if ahead of target, safer pole vault opening).
5. **1500 m/800 m** last: know the time you need for a target total or place; pace it.

## Two-day management
- **Warm-ups**: a full warm-up for the first event, then shorter re-warm-ups; don't do
  a sprint-intensity warm-up before every event.
- **Between events**: get off your feet, shade or warmth, layers on. Keep spikes,
  tapes, poles and implements organised in one bag per event.
- **Fuel**: small frequent carbohydrate snacks and fluids; plan meals for night one.
- **Sleep and recovery** night one: food, hydration, light mobility, early bed.
- **Mental reset**: a bad event is just points lost; the next event starts at zero.

## Gotchas
- Fouling all three long jumps/throws through over-aggression.
- Starting the high jump or pole vault too high to impress.
- Hurdles are first on day 2 — legs are stiff; extra warm-up time.
- Time schedules slip: re-check call-room times.
