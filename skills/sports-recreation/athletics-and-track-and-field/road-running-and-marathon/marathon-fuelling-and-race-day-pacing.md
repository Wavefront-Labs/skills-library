# Marathon fuelling and race-day pacing

**Goal:** arrive at 30–35 km with enough glycogen and fluid left to hold pace, by
loading carbohydrate beforehand, taking it on steadily during the race, and not
spending it early.

> Draft: reflects common sports-nutrition guidance (e.g. ~30–90 g carbohydrate per
> hour during long endurance events, more when the gut is trained). Individual needs
> vary; people with medical conditions (diabetes, GI disease) should get professional
> advice. Not medical advice.

## The 2–3 days before (carb-loading)
- Raise carbohydrate intake substantially — commonly cited targets are around
  **8–12 g per kg body weight per day** for 36–48 h — while running little (taper).
- Choose familiar, lower-fibre foods (rice, pasta, bread, potatoes, juice) to reduce
  gut trouble. Expect a 1–2 kg weight gain (stored glycogen holds water) — that's normal.
- **Race breakfast** 2–4 h before: familiar carbs (~1–4 g/kg), some fluid. Nothing new.

## During the race
- **Carbohydrate**: aim for **~30–60 g/h** if untrained gut; well-trained athletes
  often target 60–90 g/h using glucose+fructose products. One typical gel ≈ 20–30 g.
  Start early (by 30–45 min), then every 20–30 min — don't wait until you feel empty.
- **Fluid**: drink to thirst, guided by your **sweat rate** (weigh yourself naked
  before and after a 1-hour training run; weight lost in kg ≈ litres sweated, plus
  what you drank). Don't drink so much that you gain weight — over-drinking risks
  **hyponatraemia** (low blood sodium), which can be dangerous.
- **Sodium**: helpful for heavy/salty sweaters and long hot races (sports drink,
  electrolyte tabs).
- Take gels **with water**, not with sports drink (too concentrated).

## Pacing plan
- Pick a goal pace from recent training/races (e.g. a half-marathon result through a
  calculator) and be honest about conditions — heat and hills cost time.
- **Even or slightly negative splits**: run the first half at goal pace or a few
  seconds per km slower. Banking time early usually costs more later.
- Use a **pace band** or watch, but trust kilometre markers over GPS — GPS reads long
  in cities and tunnels and the course is measured on the shortest line.
- The first 5 km should feel too easy. If it doesn't, slow down.

## Gotchas
- **Nothing new on race day** — shoes, gels, breakfast, socks. Practise fuelling on
  long runs (gut training).
- The gels at the aid stations may differ from your brand; carry your own or train
  with theirs.
- Hitting the wall (~30 km+) is mostly glycogen depletion and overpacing — both fixable.
- Hot day: slow the plan (many runners add ~1–3% per few degrees above cool
  conditions — rough guide), pour water over yourself, increase sodium.
- Course-specific: elevation profile, where the aid stations are, wind exposure.

## Verify
- [ ] Carb-load plan written with grams per day and familiar foods.
- [ ] Gel schedule (minutes or km) set and practised in long runs.
- [ ] Sweat rate estimated; drink plan avoids weight gain.
- [ ] Goal pace realistic for conditions; first 5 km at or slightly slower than it.
- [ ] Post-race: splits by 5 km reviewed — positive split > 2–3% means rethink pacing or fuelling.
