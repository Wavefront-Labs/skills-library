# Esports coaching — VOD review and scrim structure

**Goal:** turn hours of practice into measurable improvement. Most amateur
teams scrim a lot and learn little because nothing is reviewed or
targeted. Applies to any team esport (CS, Valorant, LoL, Overwatch, etc.).

## 1. The weekly cycle
1. **Plan:** pick 1–2 team focuses for the week (e.g. "retakes on B",
   "objective setup 60 s before spawn").
2. **Scrim blocks** practise those focuses.
3. **Review** the blocks against the focus.
4. **Adjust** the next week's focus from what review found.
Write focuses down and share them before the first scrim.

## 2. Structuring scrims
- Ask partner teams for the format you need (full maps/games, or specific
  scenarios like "15 rounds of B retakes"). Many teams agree to scenario
  scrims if asked.
- Before the block: state the focus and the one thing each player is
  watching for.
- During: the coach takes **timestamped notes** (round number/game time +
  one line). Don't coach mid-round over voice unless your rules and the
  partner allow it.
- After each map/game: **5-minute debrief** while it's fresh: what went to
  plan, the biggest mistake, one adjustment for the next map.
- Record everything (demos, replays, POV recordings).

## 3. Running a VOD review that works
- **Keep it short:** 30–45 minutes, 5–8 clips. Long reviews stop being absorbed.
- **Choose clips tied to the focus,** not every mistake. Prepare them in advance.
- For each clip: pause *before* the decision, ask "what's happening, what
  are our options?" Let players answer before you do. Then play it out.
- Show **good examples too** — what to repeat is as useful as what to stop.
- End with **2–3 concrete takeaways** written down ("Entry waits for flash
  pop before swinging").
- Individual mistakes go to 1:1 reviews, not public team sessions, unless
  they're team-relevant.

## 4. Using stats
- Stats show *where* to look, not *why*. A low first-blood success rate
  sends you to the VODs of those rounds.
- Prefer rate stats over totals (per round, per 10 min) and compare by
  role — a support's KDA is not comparable to a carry's.
- Small samples mislead: a few maps of data says little. Track across
  weeks.
- Useful team metrics: round win % by side/site/scenario, objective
  control %, post-plant/after-man-advantage conversion, pistol/eco round
  results, opening duel win %.

## 5. Opponent preparation
- Watch 3–5 recent VODs of the opponent on the maps/picks you expect.
- Build a one-page sheet: their default setups, favourite strats, key
  player tendencies, what they do when losing.
- Prepare **1–2 counter-ideas**, not ten; practise them in scrims that week.
- Gotcha: over-preparing for one opponent's habits while neglecting your own
  fundamentals.

## 6. People
- Give feedback specific and behavioural ("you peeked before trade was
  ready"), not personal.
- Watch for tilt and fatigue: performance drops after long scrim days;
  2–3 focused blocks beat six tired ones.
- Agree a comms standard and a "reset" phrase for tilted moments.

## Gotchas
- Reviewing every round = reviewing nothing.
- Coach talks for the entire review; players stay passive.
- Changing strategies weekly before any are learned.
- **Tournament rules on coach comms during matches vary widely; check
  each event's ruleset.**

## Verify
- [ ] Weekly focus written and shared before scrims.
- [ ] Every scrim block has timestamped notes and a debrief.
- [ ] Reviews ≤45 min with written takeaways.
- [ ] Next week's focus derived from last review.
- [ ] Opponent sheet exists before each official match.
