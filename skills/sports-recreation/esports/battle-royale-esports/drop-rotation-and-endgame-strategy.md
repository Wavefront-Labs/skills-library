# Battle royale esports — drops, rotations and endgame

**Goal:** finish high in a lobby of 60–100 players consistently, because in
competitive BR **placement points usually outweigh kills**. Applies to
Fortnite, Apex Legends, PUBG and Warzone-style games; scoring tables differ
per event.

## 1. Read the scoring before the strategy
- Competitive formats award points per placement plus points per kill/
  elimination; some cap kill points or multiply them by placement.
- Calculate: is a top-5 with 2 kills worth more than 8th with 8 kills in
  *this* ruleset? Teams lose tournaments by playing pub-style aggression
  under placement-heavy scoring.
- Multi-game series: one 20th place costs more than one extra win gains in
  many formats. Consistency first.

## 2. Drop spot ("landing")
- In organised play, teams usually **claim an uncontested drop** and keep
  it all series; contested drops are a coin flip that ends games early.
- A good drop has: enough loot for the whole team, a vehicle or mobility
  item if the game has them, and a central-ish location or reliable
  rotation route.
- Practise the exact **drop path** (glide/jump timing) until your team
  lands first at your POI every time.
- Plan a **loot route** per player so no two people loot the same building.

## 3. Loot economy
- Set a loot timer (e.g. leave by the first zone close). Over-looting is the
  #1 reason teams get caught rotating late.
- Priorities: armour/shields, healing, a primary weapon for each range,
  then utility (grenades, build materials in Fortnite).
- Share resources: the player with the most heals carries spares.

## 4. Rotations
- **Early rotation:** move toward the next zone before it is revealed if you
  are far from centre. Claim a strong building/ridge inside.
- **Edge-of-zone (edge play):** stay on the edge of the circle, so threats
  can only come from one side. Lower risk of third parties, but you may be
  forced through enemies when the zone shifts.
- **Deep zone:** hold the centre with a strong position; higher risk early,
  easier later rotations.
- Use terrain: ridgelines, cover lines, buildings. Avoid open fields when
  rotating late; if you must, use smoke/vehicles and move together.

## 5. Fights and third parties
- Every loud fight attracts other squads. Before taking a fight ask:
  can we win it in under ~20 seconds and reset (heal, rearmour) safely?
- After a fight, **reset immediately** — heal, reload, reposition — before looting.
- Third-party opportunistically: wait for two teams to trade, then hit the
  winner while they're weak.

## 6. Endgame (final 3–4 zones)
- Hold **high ground** or hard cover with sightlines into where the zone will close.
- Count teams remaining; know which team is where (comms: "Team on the
  ridge north, two players").
- Final circles in many titles move toward the remaining teams; prepare
  for the zone to pull you out of position and pre-plan the next cover.
- Utility (grenades, abilities) is worth more in the final 2 circles than
  anywhere else — save some.

## 7. Practice
- **Scrims** with structured lobbies; review deaths, not kills.
- Track average placement and points per game, not K/D.

## Gotchas
- Pub habits (hot drops, chasing kills) under placement scoring.
- Rotating as individuals; arrive as a team.
- Map and zone behaviour change by season. **Details above are general
  patterns, not verified against the current season of each title.**

- [ ] Team has a fixed drop and loot route with a leave time.
- [ ] Rotation decision (edge vs deep) is called before each zone close.
- [ ] Average placement tracked over at least 10 games.
