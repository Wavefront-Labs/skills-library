# RTS and card esports — build orders, macro and ladder climbing

**Goal:** climb ladder by executing a sound plan cleanly. In RTS that means
a reliable build order and strong macro; in digital card games it means
picking a proven deck and piloting it with fewer mistakes than opponents.

## Part A — RTS (StarCraft II, Age of Empires II/IV-style)

### 1. One build, learned to muscle memory
- Pick **one standard build per matchup** from a current guide or pro
  replay. Write it as supply/population timings ("14 pool, 16 hatch, 18 gas").
- Practise vs the AI until you hit every timing within ~5 seconds without
  looking at notes. Only then add variations.

### 2. Macro beats micro at most levels
- **Never stop producing workers** until your target count (SC2: often
  ~60–75 workers; AoE: depends on age/strategy).
- **Don't get supply/pop blocked** — build supply ahead of need. A replay
  showing frequent "supply blocked" is the first thing to fix.
- **Spend your money.** Unspent resources (high bank) mean missing
  production. Benchmark your average unspent resources in replays.
- Hotkeys: production buildings on control groups; cycle-cast/inject or
  queue production without moving the camera far. Hotkey layout should be
  fixed and practised.
- APM is a symptom, not a goal; efficient actions matter more than spam.

### 3. Scouting
- Scout at a fixed timing to answer one question: is the opponent doing
  something abnormal (early aggression, fast expansion, tech rush)?
- Have a prepared response for each common deviation.

### 4. Replay review (every loss)
Check: when did worker production stop? supply blocks? bank at 5/8/10 min?
what did the scout show and did you react?

## Part B — Digital card games (Hearthstone, MTG Arena, Marvel Snap)

### 1. Deck choice
- Use a **proven list** from stats sites at your rank; tier and win-rate
  data beats home-brew while learning the format. Adjust 1–3 tech cards
  for the meta you actually face.

### 2. Piloting
- **Mulligan with a plan:** know which cards you keep vs aggro and vs
  control. Keep curve plays; ship expensive cards against fast decks.
- Each turn: plan the next two turns before playing; count opponent's
  possible damage/answers ("what beats me if I do this?").
- **Tempo vs value:** board presence now vs card advantage later; know
  which role your deck plays in each matchup (who's the beatdown).
- Sequence correctly: draw/scry effects before deciding plays; play cards
  that reveal information first.

### 3. Tracking
Use a deck tracker (where the game's terms allow it) to log win rate per
matchup; 50+ games is the minimum for any conclusion — card-game variance is high.

## Part C — Ladder routine (both genres)
- Sessions of a fixed length; **stop after 2–3 losses in a row** (tilt).
- One focus per session ("no supply blocks", "count lethal every turn").
- Review one loss per session.
- Ladder MMR is noisy over small samples; judge progress weekly.

## Gotchas
- Switching builds/decks every loss.
- Copying pro aggression without their macro behind it.
- Balance patches change builds and card lists — **worker targets and
  sample sizes above are rough community norms, not verified figures.**
- Some trackers/overlays are banned in some games or tournaments; check the rules.

## Verify
- [ ] One written build per matchup hit within 5 s of timings vs AI.
- [ ] Replays show no long supply blocks and low banked resources.
- [ ] Card deck mulligan rules written for aggro and control matchups.
- [ ] Win rate tracked per matchup over 50+ games.
