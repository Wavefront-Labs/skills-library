# Casting and shoutcasting — play-by-play, colour and observer craft

**Goal:** make the game understandable and exciting for viewers who can't
see what the players see. A cast succeeds when a newcomer can follow it and
an expert learns something.

## 1. The two roles
- **Play-by-play (PBP):** narrates *what is happening now*. Short, fast,
  present tense, names and locations: "Kai swings long, one down, two
  more pushing B!" Drives energy and owns the big moments.
- **Colour / analyst:** explains *why* and *what next* in the gaps:
  economy, strategy, player tendencies, draft logic. Speaks mostly in
  downtime (buy phase, deaths, between fights).
- Solo casters do both; switch modes deliberately.

## 2. Co-casting handoffs
- Agree a **handoff signal**: PBP ends a sentence with a question or the
  co-caster's name ("…what do you make of that, Sam?"), or a hand tap off-camera.
- **Never talk over a fight** — the PBP caster owns live action. Colour
  jumps in when action pauses.
- Leave space: a two-second silence at a big moment can be stronger than
  shouting.
- Don't both explain the same thing; colour adds a *new* layer.

## 3. Prep (do this every match)
Build a one-page **prep sheet**:
- Team names, player handles with **pronunciation** (ask the teams/admins),
  roles, notable stats or storylines (rematch, debut, elimination stakes).
- Format and stakes: best-of, bracket position, prize/seeding consequences.
- 3–5 talking points for downtime, so you never fill silence with "yeah".
- Map/patch notes relevant to the game.
Gotcha: mispronouncing a player's name in a big moment is the error
audiences remember most.

## 4. Delivery
- **Name + action + location** for PBP. Avoid vague "he goes there".
- Vary pace and pitch: build with the action, release at the peak.
- Avoid filler habits ("you know", "for sure", repeated catchphrases).
  Record yourself and count them.
- Speak to viewers, not to your co-caster.
- Stay neutral between teams unless you're an explicitly partisan stream.
- Keep a water bottle; voice fatigue sets in fast during long days.

## 5. Working with the observer
- The **observer** (in-game camera operator) decides what viewers see. Agree
  in advance: who to follow on key rounds, replay requests via a back channel.
- Cast **what is on screen**. If a kill happens off-camera, acknowledge it
  ("we lost a player on A — let's see the replay") rather than describing
  what viewers can't see.
- Ask for **replays** of big plays in the next downtime; colour explains them.
- If you observe your own cast (small streams), prioritise: the fight >
  the player with the most impact > minimap/overview during downtime.

## 6. Tech basics for self-produced casts
- Dedicated mic (dynamic is forgiving in noisy rooms), headphones so game
  audio doesn't bleed. Mix game audio well under voices.
- Test levels before going live; co-casters at matched loudness.
- Delay settings: tournaments often require a stream delay to stop
  ghosting; confirm with the admin.
- Rights: check the publisher's licence/policy for broadcasting the game
  and that you have permission to cast that event.

## 7. Getting better
Review your VODs at 1.25×; note dead air, talking over fights, errors.
Cast amateur leagues and replays to build reps.

## Gotchas
- Explaining during a fight; PBP missing a kill.
- Colour repeating PBP.
- Inside-jokes the audience doesn't get.
- **Broadcast delay and licensing rules differ per event/publisher; check
  before streaming.**
