# Fighting game esports — frame data, neutral and matchup study

**Goal:** stop losing to the same situations by knowing what is safe, what
is punishable and what the opponent's options are. Terms follow 2D/3D
traditional fighters (Street Fighter, Tekken, Guilty Gear); platform
fighters (Smash) share the ideas with different vocabulary.

## 1. Frame data in five numbers
Games run at **60 frames per second**; one frame ≈ 16.7 ms.
- **Startup** — frames before the move can hit (a 4f jab hits on frame 4;
  conventions differ by game whether the active frame is counted).
- **Active** — frames the hitbox is out.
- **Recovery** — frames before you can act again.
- **On block** — advantage after the opponent blocks. **−4** means you
  recover 4 frames later than them; a 4f move then punishes you.
- **On hit** — advantage after hitting; big plus frames allow a combo or
  frame trap.
Rule of thumb: if a move is more negative on block than your fastest
attack's startup, **punish it** — learn your best punish for −4/−5, −6/−9
and −10 or worse. Sources: in-game frame data menus (SF6, Tekken 8 have
them) and community wikis; trust the game menu over an outdated wiki.

## 2. Training mode routine (30 min)
1. **Execution (10 min):** your bread-and-butter combo from a light, a
   medium and an anti-air confirm. Target 10 in a row, both sides of screen.
   Turn on input display to see dropped/mistimed inputs.
2. **Punishes (10 min):** record the dummy doing the 3–4 unsafe moves you
   get hit by most; set it to random playback after block; practise
   punishing on reaction.
3. **Defence (10 min):** record a mixup (low/overhead, throw/strike) or
   blockstring with a gap; practise blocking, teching or interrupting.

## 3. Neutral and footsies
- **Neutral** is the phase where neither player has advantage; spacing wins it.
- Know your **pokes** (long, safe normals) and their max ranges.
- **Whiff punish**: stand just outside the opponent's poke, let it miss,
  hit their extended hurtbox during recovery.
- **Anti-air** every jump you can. Not anti-airing is the most common reason
  intermediate players lose — jumps get rewarded with huge combos.
- Gotcha: pressing buttons at a range where nothing reaches only gives the
  opponent whiff punish practice.

## 4. Offence after a knockdown (okizeme)
- Learn one **meaty** (attack timed to hit on the first wakeup frame) and the
  basic strike/throw mixup.
- Respect **reversals** (invincible wakeup moves); bait them occasionally by
  blocking instead of attacking.

## 5. Matchup study
For each character you lose to, write a one-page note:
- Their 3 most-used unsafe moves and your punish.
- Their best pokes and range; your answer (whiff punish, low profile).
- How to block their main mixup.
- Their gameplan (zoner, grappler, rushdown) and your counter-plan.
Then lab it and play long sets (FT5/FT10) against that character.

## 6. Competing
- Tournaments are usually **double elimination**, often best-of-3 then best-of-5
  in top 8. Know the ruleset (stage/character select, counterpicks, controller rules).
- Bring your own controller; check console/PC version and input lag settings.
- Between games: one adjustment, not five.

## Gotchas
- Learning long showcase combos before learning to block.
- Copying pro frame-trap pressure without knowing the gaps.
- Frame data changes with balance patches. **Numbers above are illustrative
  conventions; check your game's current data.**

## Verify
- [ ] You can name your fastest normal's startup and your punish for −4..−10.
- [ ] Main combo lands 8/10 on both sides in training mode.
- [ ] You anti-air the majority of jumps in a replay.
- [ ] Matchup notes exist for your two worst matchups.
