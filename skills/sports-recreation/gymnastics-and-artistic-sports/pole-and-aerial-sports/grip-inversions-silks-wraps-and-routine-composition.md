# Pole and aerial sports — grip, inversions, silks wraps and routine composition

**Goal:** build the strength and grip to climb and invert with control,
learn wraps and drops progressively, and compose a judged routine. Pole
sport (IPSF and other federations publish codes of points), aerial silks
and aerial hoop/lyra share a strength-first progression. Train with a
qualified instructor, on a properly installed apparatus, over a crash mat.

## 1. Equipment and setup
- **Pole:** usually 45 mm (competition) or 40/50 mm; **static** or
  **spin** mode. Learn basics on static first; spin mode adds momentum and
  dizziness.
- **Silks/tissu and hoop:** rigged from a load-rated point by someone
  qualified, with a swivel and rated hardware. Never rig from an unknown
  beam.
- **Crash mat** under the apparatus every time you're upside down.

## 2. Grip and contact
- Pole relies on **skin contact**: shorts and exposed arms/legs, no lotion
  on training days.
- Clean the pole with alcohol before and during sessions; grip aids help
  sweaty hands but are restricted in some competitions.
- **Contact points:** hands, inner elbow, armpit, inner knee, thigh, ankle.
  Bruises ("pole kisses") are common at first.
- Silks: grip with the whole hand and engage the shoulders (pull the
  shoulder blades down, never hang passively in the joint).

## 3. Conditioning first
- **Active hang:** hanging with shoulders engaged (shoulder blades down).
- Pull-ups or assisted pull-ups, hanging knee raises → leg raises,
  hollow-body holds, grip endurance.
- Before inverts, many schools expect solid hanging knee/leg raises and a
  controlled climb.

## 4. Pole progression
- **Spins** (on static or spin pole): fireman spin, chair spin.
- **Climb:** hands high, knee hook or "sit" with thighs gripping, step up.
- **Pole sit:** hold the pole between the thighs with no hands — the
  foundation for many tricks.
- **Basic invert:** from standing, hands stacked, lift the legs up (tuck
  first, then straight), hook the outside leg, then the inside, to an
  inverted crucifix hold. Use the core, not a jump, and have a spotter.
- **Advanced:** shoulder mount, Ayesha, handsprings — after the basics
  are strong and controlled.
- Train **both sides**.

## 5. Silks progression
- **Foot lock:** wrap the silk around the foot and stand on the tail to
  rest in the air. The foundation for climbing and resting.
- **Climbs:** Russian climb (one foot) or foot-lock climb.
- **Inversions:** straddle up from standing (tuck first), with control.
- **Hip key:** a wrap around the hip that locks you inverted — used as a
  base for many poses.
- **Drops** (unrolling falls): learn low, with an instructor checking the
  wrap before every drop. Wrong wraps cause falls to the floor. Never
  attempt drops from videos alone.

## 6. Routine composition and scoring
- Routines are set to music; many codes require elements from groups such
  as strength holds, flexibility, spins and transitions/flow.
- Typical scoring: Technical (difficulty values of elements), Execution
  (form, control, lines), and Artistic (choreography, musicality,
  use of space). Check the current code for your federation.
- Plan **rest points** in the routine (a pole sit, a foot lock) — grip
  fatigue is the main cause of falls late in routines.
- Floorwork and transitions count, too.

## Gotchas
- Inverting by kicking up rather than controlled lifting.
- Training through grip fatigue — that's when falls happen.
- Unchecked silks wraps before drops; DIY rigging.

## Verify
- [ ] Active hang 30 s and five controlled hanging knee raises.
- [ ] Pole sit and basic invert controlled on both sides, with mat.
- [ ] Silks foot lock on both feet without help.
- [ ] Routine includes planned rest points and fits the code requirements.
