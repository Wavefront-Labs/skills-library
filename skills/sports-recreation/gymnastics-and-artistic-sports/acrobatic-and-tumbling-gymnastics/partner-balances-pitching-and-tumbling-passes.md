# Acrobatic and tumbling gymnastics — partner balances, pitching and tumbling passes

**Goal:** build safe partner balances and throws on a sound base, and link
tumbling elements into fast, straight passes. Acro gymnastics (FIG) uses
pairs, trios and groups of four, with **balance**, **dynamic** and
**combined** routines to music. Power tumbling (FIG trampoline discipline)
uses eight-element passes on a sprung track. All inverted and thrown
skills require a qualified coach and spotting.

## 1. Roles
- **Base:** strong, stable support, usually the larger partner. Locks
  out joints (straight arms, stacked shoulders over hands or hips over
  knees) so bones carry the weight, not muscles.
- **Top:** lighter, very tight and controlled body, strong handstand.
- **Middle** (in trios/groups): supports the top and is supported by the
  base.
- Everyone needs core strength, a good handstand and clear communication.

## 2. Balance principles
- **Stack:** the combined centre of gravity must sit over the base of
  support. Joints aligned vertically.
- **Counterbalance:** partners lean away from each other with straight
  arms, balancing each other's weight.
- **Tension:** the top must stay rigid; a soft top is impossible to
  balance.
- **Entries and exits:** every balance has a planned way up and down —
  rehearse the exit as much as the hold.
- Beginner balances: the base lying on their back with straight arms and
  legs supporting the top (e.g. a "front bird" on the base's feet), then
  standing balances like a thigh stand, then hand-to-hand later.
- **Holds:** competition balances must be held for a set time (typically 3 s
  — check the current FIG code).

## 3. Pitching and catching (dynamic)
- **Pitch:** the base(s) throw the top into the air using legs, not arms —
  deep squat, then drive up with straight arms as the top jumps.
- The **top** jumps at the same moment and stays tight in the air through
  the somersault or twist.
- **Catch:** bases catch with bent arms and legs absorbing the landing,
  ideally at the top's hips/waist or into a cradle.
- Progress from simple straight-up pitches onto a crash mat, with spotters
  on each side, then add rotations.
- **Timing calls** ("ready, one, two, up") keep everyone synchronised.

## 4. Power tumbling passes
- A typical beginner pass: **run → hurdle → round-off → flic-flac (back
  handspring) → back somersault (tuck)**.
- **Round-off:** turn the run into backwards rebound, landing with feet
  together and snapping the legs down fast.
- **Flic-flac:** sit back like into a chair, swing the arms up and over,
  push through the shoulders, snap the legs down.
- **Whip-back:** a fast, low back somersault without hands — keeps
  momentum high between elements.
- Each element must **rebound** into the next with no stops; the pass
  should travel in a straight line.
- Train on the tumble track or rod floor before a sprung floor; use spotters
  and pits for new saltos.

## 5. Scoring (broadly)
Difficulty (element values), Execution (form and landings), and in acro,
Artistry (choreography and partnership). Check current FIG codes for both
disciplines.

## 6. Safety
- Matting, spotters and progressions for every new skill.
- Communication protocol: agreed "bail" word to abort.
- Bases should learn to drop safely or catch into a cradle; tops learn to
  fall to their feet or roll out.
- AcroYoga (recreational) shares partner-balance principles but is
  non-competitive — the same safety rules apply.

## Gotchas
- Bases using bent arms (muscle) instead of stacked straight arms (bone).
- Tops relaxing during a hold.
- Rushing pitches before the top can complete the skill on a trampoline.

## Verify
- [ ] Top holds a front bird on a stacked base for 5 s with planned exit.
- [ ] Pitch to straight jump, caught cleanly 10 of 10 with spotters.
- [ ] Round-off–flic-flac rebounds without a stop.
- [ ] Group uses agreed calls and a bail word.
