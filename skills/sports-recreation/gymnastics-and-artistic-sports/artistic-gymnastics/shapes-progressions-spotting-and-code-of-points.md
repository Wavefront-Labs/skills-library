# Artistic gymnastics — shapes, progressions, spotting and the code of points

**Goal:** build skills on solid basic shapes in a safe progression, and
understand how routines are scored. Women's artistic (WAG): vault, uneven
bars, beam, floor. Men's (MAG): floor, pommel horse, rings, vault,
parallel bars, high bar. Learn under a qualified coach in a gym with proper
matting — the skills below aren't for back gardens.

## 1. The shapes everything is built from
- **Hollow:** lying on the back, lower back pressed to the floor, arms by
  the ears, legs and shoulders lifted. The base for handstands, kips,
  vaults and twisting.
- **Arch:** the opposite, controlled — chest and legs lifted face down.
- **Tuck, pike, straddle:** used in salto and jump shapes.
- **Tight body:** squeezed glutes, legs together, toes pointed. A
  "banana" handstand or a loose body is a deduction and an injury risk.
Hollow holds, arch holds and "hollow rocks" (30 s+) are daily drills.

## 2. Handstand — the master skill
- Hands shoulder-width, fingers spread, shoulders fully open (arms by ears,
  pushing tall through the shoulders), hollow body, legs together.
- Progressions: wall handstand (chest to wall, then back to wall), kick-up
  with a spotter, freestanding holds, handstand walking.
- Wrists take a lot of load — warm them up and build gradually; persistent
  wrist pain in young gymnasts needs medical assessment (growth-plate
  injuries are a known concern).

## 3. Typical progressions by apparatus
- **Floor:** forward roll → cartwheel → round-off → back walkover/back
  handspring (on a wedge or with a spot first) → saltos with a spot on a
  tumble track or into a pit.
- **Bars:** front support → cast (lift the hips away from the bar) →
  back hip circle → glide kip → cast to handstand.
- **Beam:** start on a line on the floor, then a low beam: walks, relevés,
  turns, jumps, cartwheel — only then raise the beam.
- **Vault:** run and hurdle onto the springboard → straight jumps onto mats
  → handspring onto a raised mat stack → over the table.
- **Rule:** a new skill is learned on soft surfaces/lowered apparatus with
  a spotter, then progressively on regular equipment. Move on only when the
  previous step is consistent and clean.

## 4. Spotting
- A coach physically supports the gymnast through the movement — e.g. one
  hand on the lower back, the other under the thighs for a back
  handspring.
- Spotters must be trained for the specific skill; poor spotting can cause
  injury. Communication ("ready… go") and agreed bail-out plans.
- Pits, foam blocks and tumble tracks reduce impact while learning.

## 5. Conditioning and flexibility
- Core (hollow/arch), upper body (pull-ups, dips, presses), leg power
  (jumps), and active flexibility (splits, bridges/shoulder flexibility).
- Load management for young athletes: rest days, monitoring growth spurts,
  and reporting pain.

## 6. The code of points (FIG)
- **D-score (difficulty):** value of the counted elements (graded A, B, C…
  upward), connection bonuses and composition requirements.
- **E-score (execution):** starts at 10.0, with deductions for form breaks
  (bent knees/arms, legs apart), landings (steps, hops, falls — a fall is a
  1.0 deduction), and artistry on women's beam and floor.
- **Final score = D + E** minus neutral deductions (out of bounds, time).
- The code changes every Olympic cycle — check the current FIG code. Many
  national grassroots programmes (levels/grades) use their own simpler
  scoring.

## Gotchas
- Learning skills before the shapes are solid.
- Skipping progressions under pressure to "get the skill" for a meet.
- Ignoring wrist, back or heel pain.

## Verify
- [ ] 30 s hollow hold and a 30 s wall handstand with straight body.
- [ ] Each new skill demonstrated on its previous progression first.
- [ ] Coach-approved spot plan for every inverted skill.
- [ ] You can explain how D and E combine and name three common deductions.
