# Trampolining — shaped jumps, somersault progressions and routines

**Goal:** bounce in control in the centre of the bed, stop on demand, and
build to somersaults safely through progressions. Competitive trampolining
uses ten-skill routines judged on difficulty, execution, time of flight and
horizontal displacement. Learn inverted skills only at a club with a
qualified coach, spotters and safety decks — back-garden trampolines lack
both.

## 1. Basic bounce and control
- Arms swing up (forward and up) as you leave the bed, circling down and
  around as you land, legs straight in the air, feet hip-width on landing.
- Land on the **cross** in the centre; drifting means you're leaning
  forwards or backwards on take-off.
- **Kill bounce (check):** bend the knees as the bed pushes back up to
  absorb the bounce and stop dead. It's the first safety skill: any time
  you lose control, kill the bounce.
- Height comes later; control first.

## 2. Shaped jumps
- **Tuck jump:** knees to chest, hands to shins, back to straight before
  landing.
- **Pike jump:** legs straight together, reach toward the toes.
- **Straddle (pike-open) jump:** legs wide and straight, hands to the toes.
- Shapes must be clear and **opened out** before landing.

## 3. Drops
- **Seat drop:** land seated, legs straight, hands beside the hips, back
  upright; push to stand.
- **Front drop:** land flat on the front (forearms and hands, chest, thighs
  together) — not knees first.
- **Back drop:** land on the upper back with the chin tucked and legs up
  toward vertical; return to feet.
- Combinations like seat–half twist–seat build twisting awareness.

## 4. Twisting
- Half twist and full twist in a straight jump: twist from the shoulders
  and hips while staying **straight and tight**; arms pull in to speed up
  the twist.
- Don't start the twist before leaving the bed.

## 5. Somersault progressions (with coach and spotting)
- **Front somersault:** front drop → three-quarter front (to back drop)
  → tucked front somersault to feet. Often taught with a hand spot or a
  push-in safety mat.
- **Back somersault:** back drop → back pullover → tucked back
  somersault with a spotter and/or overhead spotting rig.
- **Barani:** front somersault with a half twist — a key skill for higher
  levels.
- Keep the head neutral (not thrown back) and spot the bed on landing.
- Never attempt a somersault you can't complete with a kill bounce after.
- **One person on the trampoline** at a time; multiple jumpers cause
  collisions and are the most common cause of injuries on home trampolines.

## 6. Routines and scoring
- A competition routine is **ten contact skills** in a row with no extra
  bounces between them.
- Common score components (FIG):
  - **Difficulty (D):** degree of difficulty by somersault and twist
    content.
  - **Execution (E):** form deductions (bent legs, open shapes, poor
    landings).
  - **Time of flight (ToF):** total time in the air — higher bounce,
    higher score.
  - **Horizontal displacement (HD):** penalty for moving away from the
    centre.
- Check the current FIG code and your national grade system for exact
  values.

## 7. Safety at clubs and home
- Safety decks or mats at the ends; spotters along the sides (at least one
  each side for beginners).
- Take off jewellery; trampoline socks or bare feet.
- Home trampolines: one jumper, enclosure nets, no somersaults without
  instruction.

## Gotchas
- Travelling across the bed (leaning on take-off).
- Opening shapes too late and landing piked.
- Learning somersaults from videos without spotting.

## Verify
- [ ] 10 consecutive straight jumps landing on the cross; kill bounce on call.
- [ ] Tuck, pike and straddle jumps open before landing.
- [ ] Seat–front–back drop sequence controlled.
- [ ] Somersaults attempted only with coach sign-off and spotting.
