# Dancesport — frame, connection, Latin action and competition floorcraft

**Goal:** dance as one couple with a stable frame and clear lead, move
correctly for each style, and navigate a crowded competition floor so
judges can see you. Two main styles (international): **Standard/Ballroom**
(waltz, tango, Viennese waltz, slow foxtrot, quickstep) and **Latin**
(cha-cha, samba, rumba, paso doble, jive). WDSF and WDC run separate
competition systems; American Smooth and Rhythm are a parallel style.

## 1. Standard: the hold and frame
- Leader's right hand on the follower's left shoulder blade; follower's
  left hand rests on the leader's right upper arm; joined hands (leader's
  left, follower's right) at about the follower's eye level.
- **Body contact** at the right side of the torso, slightly offset so each
  partner's right foot steps between the other's feet.
- **Frame:** elbows held up and slightly forward, shoulders down, a
  stable "box" from shoulder to elbow. The frame doesn't move
  independently — the body moves it.
- **Follower** extends the upper body up and left (outward), not leaning on
  the leader.
- **Posture:** tall, weight forward over the balls of the feet.

## 2. Standard movement
- **Rise and fall** (waltz, foxtrot, quickstep): lower through the knees at
  the end of beat 1, rise through the feet and legs over beats 2–3,
  lower at the end of 3. Tango has **no rise and fall** — flat, staccato
  movement with a compact hold.
- **Swing and sway:** the pendulum-like movement and body inclination that
  make turns flow.
- **Heel leads** on forward walks in most dances, toe leads on backward.

## 3. Latin technique
- **Straight legs:** the supporting leg straightens as weight arrives
  (a key difference from social dancing).
- **Hip action ("Cuban motion"):** comes from bending and straightening
  the knees and transferring weight fully over each foot, not from
  swinging the hips deliberately.
- **Weight on the ball of the foot** for most steps, with pressure into the
  floor.
- **Arm styling** follows the body action, not decoration bolted on.
- Dance-specific character: rumba slow and sustained, cha-cha sharp, samba
  bounce action, jive kicks and flicks, paso doble Spanish-styled march.

## 4. Lead and follow
- Leads come from the body through the frame, not by pushing with the
  arms. A lead must be given **before** the step it signals.
- The follower responds to the body, keeps their own balance, and
  maintains the frame.
- Practise basics with eyes closed (follower) to train connection.

## 5. Floorcraft
- **Line of dance:** travelling dances move **anticlockwise** around the
  floor.
- Plan corners and have **alternative figures** ready when blocked
  (a check, a pivot, or a spin turn variation).
- Avoid collisions; the leader is responsible for navigation.
- Use open space where judges can see you; don't get stuck behind
  couples.

## 6. How competitions run
- **Heats** with many couples dancing at once; **rounds** (first round,
  quarter-final, semi-final, final) recall couples.
- Adjudicators mark couples to recall in early rounds, then place couples
  in the final; results are computed by the **skating system**.
- Judges assess posture and hold, timing and basic rhythm, line, movement,
  partnering, floorcraft and character.
- Dress codes and syllabus figure restrictions apply in many novice and
  age-group events — check the event's rules.

## Gotchas
- Pulling with the arms instead of leading with the body.
- Bent supporting knee in Latin.
- Dancing the routine regardless of traffic.

## Verify
- [ ] Hold a stable frame through one minute of waltz basics.
- [ ] Latin basic movements with straight legs on full weight transfer.
- [ ] Follower can follow a natural turn with eyes closed.
- [ ] Couple has an alternative figure ready for each corner.
