# Cheerleading — stunt progressions, tosses and routine counts

**Goal:** build stunts from the ground up so every flyer is caught every
time, and put stunts, tumbling, jumps and dance into a routine counted in
eights. Competitive all-star and school cheer are governed by **level-based
safety rules** (e.g. USASF/IASF in all-star, school-specific bodies
elsewhere) that restrict which skills each level may perform — always
check your current rulebook.

## 1. Stunt group roles
- **Flyer:** the person lifted. Tight body, locked legs, hips square,
  eyes up, arms in a high V or set motion. Holds their own weight up
  through the core.
- **Main base:** holds one foot and drives the lift; usually the stronger
  side.
- **Side base:** holds the other foot; balances and supports.
- **Back spot:** behind, holding the flyer's ankles or waist, watching the
  flyer's head and shoulders at all times; responsible for catching the
  head and neck in a fall. Calls the counts.
- **Front spot** (optional): adds support and catches from the front.

## 2. Stunt progression
1. **Thigh stand / shoulder sit** — learning tension and communication.
2. **Prep (elevator):** flyer's feet at bases' shoulder height.
3. **Extension:** bases lock arms straight overhead.
4. **Liberty:** flyer on one leg, the other knee bent with the foot by the
   knee (a "liberty" shape).
5. Body positions (heel stretch, arabesque, scorpion), then transitions and
   release moves at higher levels.
Move up only when the previous stunt is solid every rep.

## 3. Load and dismount
- **Load-in:** flyer steps in on the bases' hands, presses down through
  the bases' shoulders, bases dip with the legs (not the back) and drive up
  together on the count.
- **Cradle dismount:** bases pop the flyer up from the stunt, flyer stays
  straight then piked into the "cradle" (bases' arms under back and
  thighs, back spot under the arms). Flyer keeps arms on the bases'
  shoulders; nobody looks away.
- **Falls:** the rule is "**no one hits the floor**" — bases and spot
  catch above the waist; flyers never bail to the floor.

## 4. Basket toss
- Two bases interlock wrists to make a "basket" for the flyer's feet,
  a third base and back spot lift at the waist.
- Dip and drive with the legs as the flyer jumps, flyer stays straight and
  tight on the way up, performs the skill (toe-touch, kick, twist only when
  allowed by level), and returns to a cradle catch.
- Baskets are restricted to higher levels and require spotting on mats.

## 5. Pyramids
Linked stunts where flyers bear weight on or connect to each other.
Strict braced-and-height rules by level. Build one stunt at a time, then
connect.

## 6. Jumps, motions and tumbling
- **Toe touch:** whip arms up, kick the legs into a straddle at hip height
  or higher, chest up, land feet together.
- **Motions:** sharp, locked arm positions (high V, low V, T, punch) —
  sharp placement scores.
- **Tumbling:** standing and running tumbling (round-off, back handspring,
  saltos) — same progressions as gymnastics, restricted by level.

## 7. Routines on eight-counts
- A routine is choreographed in **eight-counts** to music (often about
  2:30 for all-star).
- Typical structure: opening, standing tumbling, stunts, running tumbling,
  pyramid, basket tosses, jumps, dance.
- Every athlete has a count sheet: what they do on every count.
- Score sheets reward difficulty and execution in each category plus
  overall impression; deductions for falls and safety infractions.

## Gotchas
- Back spot not watching the flyer's head.
- Bases lifting with the back instead of the legs.
- Performing skills above the team's level.

## Verify
- [ ] Every group cradles from prep 10 of 10 with no drops.
- [ ] Extensions held 3 counts locked, then cradled.
- [ ] Each athlete can recite their counts for any section.
- [ ] Coaches have checked every skill against the current level rules.
