# Rhythmic gymnastics — apparatus handling, body difficulties and composition

**Goal:** keep the apparatus constantly moving under control while
performing body difficulties, and build a routine that scores on both
difficulty and artistry. Individual routines are typically about 1:15–1:30;
group routines (five gymnasts) are longer. Check the current FIG Code of
Points — details change every cycle.

## 1. The apparatus
FIG senior apparatus: **hoop, ball, clubs, ribbon** (rope remains in many
junior and national programmes).
- **Hoop:** rolls on the body and floor, rotations around the hand or
  body part, throws and catches, passing through it. Keep it in one plane —
  a wobbling hoop looks and scores poorly.
- **Ball:** held in the open hand, **never gripped** with the fingers (a
  "grip" is a fault). Rolls over arms and back, bounces, throws caught with
  one hand softly, body following the ball.
- **Clubs:** small circles, mills (interlocking circles), asymmetric
  movements, rhythmic tapping, throws of one or both clubs.
- **Ribbon:** the pattern must be continuous — spirals (tight circles),
  snakes (waves), and figure-eights. The ribbon (6 m for seniors) must not
  stop, knot or wrap around the body unintentionally.
- **Throws and catches:** high throws with rotations of the body underneath
  them are the riskiest and most valuable. A drop is costly, especially if
  the apparatus leaves the floor area.

## 2. Body difficulties (BD)
Three groups, each needing a minimum of repetitions per routine under
the code:
- **Jumps/leaps:** split leap (180°+), ring leap, stag leap — fixed shape
  in the air, height and amplitude.
- **Balances:** on relevé (toes) or flat foot, held with a defined shape,
  e.g. a front or side split balance, ring balance, penché.
- **Rotations (pivots):** turns on relevé holding a shape, e.g. passé
  pivot, attitude pivot; value rises with each additional 360°.
Each body difficulty must be performed **with** an apparatus element
(handling at the same time), not with the apparatus static.

## 3. Dynamic elements with rotation (DER) and apparatus difficulty
- A high throw, two or more body rotations underneath (rolls, chainés,
  walkovers), then catch. Extra criteria (catch without hands, out of
  visual field) add value.
- **Mastery elements** combine unusual handling with body movement.
- **Dance steps:** a required sequence of rhythmic, musical steps with
  apparatus handling.

## 4. Flexibility and conditioning
- Daily progressive flexibility: oversplits, back flexibility (bridges,
  backbends), shoulder mobility. Warm muscles first.
- Strength for control: ankles and calves for relevé, core, hip flexors
  and back for leap height and balance holds.
- Extreme flexibility training in children carries injury risk (lower back,
  hips) — follow a qualified coach and avoid forced passive stretching.

## 5. Composition and artistry
- Music sets the character; movement should match its tempo, accents and
  phrases, not just run alongside it.
- Use the whole floor area (13 × 13 m) with varied levels, directions and
  travelling.
- Balance high-value difficulties with safety: put risky throws where a
  recovery is possible.
- **Scoring (broadly):** Difficulty (body and apparatus) + Execution
  (technical and artistic deductions from 10). Check the current code for
  the exact split and maximums.

## Gotchas
- Static apparatus during body difficulties (not credited).
- Gripping the ball or letting the ribbon stop.
- Composition that loads all the difficulty into the last 20 s when tired.

## Verify
- [ ] Ribbon spirals and snakes continuous for 30 s without knots.
- [ ] Ball caught from a high throw with one open hand, 7 of 10.
- [ ] Passé pivot 720° on relevé with apparatus handling.
- [ ] Routine timed to the music and counted phrase by phrase.
