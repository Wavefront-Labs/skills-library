# Aid leading with aiders, and hauling the bag

**Goal:** move up a pitch on gear rather than holds, efficiently and without
shock-loading marginal placements, then haul the team's supplies to the anchor.

> Draft: an overview of standard big-wall systems (e.g. as described in big-wall
> guidebooks and courses). Gear setups differ widely between climbers; practise on
> short pitches near the ground before committing to a wall.

## The aid kit
- Two **aiders** (ladders) — or one pair per side — each clipped to an
  **adjustable daisy** on the harness, plus a **fifi hook** or similar for resting.
- A rack sized for the pitch (cams, nuts, offsets, hooks, maybe cam hooks), many
  free carabiners, and slings for extensions.
- **Rope**: lead line and a separate **haul line** trailing from the harness.

## Leading an aid pitch (rhythm)
1. Place a piece as high as you can reach.
2. **Bounce-test**: clip a spare aider (or a daisy) to it and give a strong body-weight
   bounce while still clipped to the lower piece — if it fails, you're on the lower
   piece. Skip bounce-testing only on placements that would be destroyed by it
   (and then step gently).
3. Clip the aider to the new piece, step up the rungs; clip the daisy short to the
   new piece for balance.
4. **Top-step**: climb to the highest rung you can (top steps) so the next
   placement is as high as possible — this is where speed comes from.
5. **Clip the rope** to pieces you leave as protection (not every aid piece, but enough
   to protect the next fall). Note the grade: A1/C1 easy solid placements up to A4/A5
   strings of body-weight-only gear.
6. Move the second aider to the new piece and repeat.

Cleaning is done by the follower on **ascenders (jumars)**, removing gear.

## Hauling the bag (1:1 basic)
1. Build a solid **anchor** at the top of the pitch.
2. Fix the lead rope for the follower first (they can start jugging).
3. Attach a **progress-capture pulley** (e.g. Protraction-type) to a strong point; run
   the haul line through it so the bag side can't slip back.
4. Attach an **ascender** to the free side of the haul line, clipped to your harness;
   **step down / sit back** using your body weight to lift the bag; the pulley holds it
   as you reset.
5. For heavy bags, use a **2:1** (extra pulley on the bag side) or the "space haul"
   (body weight + legs pushing off the wall).
6. **Dock** the bag at the anchor with a docking cord (releasable) once it arrives.

## Lower-out
At the bottom anchor, the follower **lowers the bag out** on a separate line
(releasable hitch) instead of letting it swing into the wall — especially on traverses.

## Gotchas
- **Shock-loading** a marginal piece by stepping too fast or falling from a top step.
- Rope management: lead line, haul line and lower-out line tangle — flake them
  carefully.
- Dropped gear is a hazard to parties below — tether essentials.
- Water and food: roughly 3–4 L of water per person per day is a common wall planning
  figure (rough guide; heat increases it).

## Verify
- [ ] Each aid placement bounce-tested (or deliberately exempted).
- [ ] Protection clipped to the rope at reasonable intervals.
- [ ] Haul system has a progress capture and the bag can't drop if you let go.
- [ ] Bag docked with a releasable cord; lower-out set before release.
