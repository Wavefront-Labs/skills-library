# Ice tool placement, crampon technique and screw placement

**Goal:** move efficiently on steep ice with secure tool and crampon placements,
protect it with well-placed screws, and recognise when the ice itself is the hazard.

> Draft: standard ice-climbing instruction. Ice is variable and the consequences of
> falling on ice are severe (crampons catch, tools fly). The traditional rule — **the
> leader must not fall** — still applies. Learn on top-rope with an instructor.

## Tools
- **Swing** from the elbow and shoulder with a relaxed grip, flicking the wrist at the
  end — like throwing a dart or hammering a nail. Aim for a slight concavity or pocket.
- A good stick: the pick sinks in with a solid "thunk" and doesn't move when weighted.
  Test with a short downward tug, not a hang-and-hope.
- **Hooking**: use existing holes or features — less effort, less shattering.
- Remove by reversing the angle (lift the handle up, wiggle along the pick) — don't
  lever sideways (it breaks picks).
- Keep tools offset; avoid placing one tool directly above the other on the same
  line (dinner-plates fall on the lower one).

## Crampons
- **Front-pointing** on steep ice: kick from the knee, heel **low** (slightly below
  the toes), so secondary points engage. Two solid kicks beat four nervous ones.
- **Flat-footing (French technique)** on lower-angle ice: all bottom points in contact,
  ankles flexed, feet splayed (duck walk / sideways).
- Stand on your feet: legs straight, hips in, arms straight — the **triangle** stance
  (two feet level, one tool high and centred) is the base position.
- Monopoint vs dual-point: monopoints suit pockets and mixed; dual points are stabler
  in fat ice.

## Ice screws
- Find solid, bubble-free, dense ice; clear rotten surface ice first.
- Place at **waist to chest height**, roughly perpendicular to the ice surface.
  Advice on a small tilt has changed with drop testing over the years — follow the
  screw manufacturer's current instructions (unconfirmed here). Finish with the
  hanger aligned toward the direction of load (usually down).
- Turn in to the hanger; it should bite within the first turns and seat with even resistance.
- Avoid placing near cracks, chandeliers or the edge of a pillar.
- **V-thread (Abalakov)**: two intersecting screw holes threaded with cord for anchors
  and rappels. Test it and back it up while others descend when possible.

## Reading conditions
- **Cold** brittle ice shatters ("dinner-plates"); **warm/sunny** ice softens and
  detaches — free-standing pillars are most dangerous during temperature swings.
- Listen for hollow sounds; avoid climbing under others (falling ice).
- Many ice routes are **avalanche terrain** — check the avalanche forecast and slopes above.

## Gotchas
- Crampon snag on the other leg or trousers: wear gaiters/fitted pants, walk with
  feet slightly apart.
- Overgripping causes "screaming barfies" (painful rewarming) and pump: shake out,
  relax grip.
- Leashless tools: don't drop them — use tethers if required.
- Mixed climbing (rock + ice) torques tools on rock edges — learn on top-rope.

## Verify
- [ ] Each tool placement tested before weighting.
- [ ] Heels low on front-points; stance triangle used at rests.
- [ ] Screws in solid ice at waist–chest height, hanger down, fully turned.
- [ ] Conditions checked: temperature trend, sun, avalanche forecast, other parties above.
