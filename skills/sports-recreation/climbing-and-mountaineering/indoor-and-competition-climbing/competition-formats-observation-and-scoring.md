# Competition formats, observation and scoring

**Goal:** get the most from your climbing on competition day by understanding the
format's scoring and using observation time to read routes before you touch them.

> Draft, medium confidence on scoring details: IFSC and national federations have
> changed boulder scoring recently (from tops/zones/attempts counting to a points
> system — e.g. points for a top and for a zone minus a small deduction per failed
> attempt). Local gym comps often use their own "redpoint" or points-per-problem
> formats. **Read the technical regulations for your event.**

## Formats at a glance
- **Bouldering**: several problems, a fixed time per problem (rotation) with rests,
  or open-format sessions at local comps. Score = tops and **zones** (an intermediate
  hold) with fewer attempts better.
- **Lead**: one onsight attempt on a tall route; score = **highest hold controlled**
  (a "+" for using it with a move toward the next). Time limit applies; must clip
  draws in order.
- **Speed**: standardised 15 m wall with an identical route worldwide; two climbers
  race head-to-head. A false start (reaction under the threshold) eliminates.
- **Combined formats** (e.g. boulder & lead) sum the separate scores.

## Isolation and observation
- At higher-level comps climbers wait in **isolation** to prevent seeing others climb
  (onsight conditions). No phones/route info.
- **Observation period** (group observation before lead rounds, or the start of each
  boulder rotation): typically a few minutes, no touching beyond allowed start holds.
- Use it systematically:
  1. Find start, zone, and top/finish holds; where the clips are (lead).
  2. Read the **sequence**: hand order, foot holds, body positions, clipping positions.
  3. Spot **trick moves**: coordination jumps, hidden footholds, holds that look good
     and aren't (setters love those), volumes to smear on.
  4. Identify **rests** (lead) and the likely **crux**.
  5. Mime it with your hands. Talk it through with others if group observation allows.

## Attempt strategy (boulder)
- First attempt: a careful flash attempt — reading done, commit.
- If you fail: what failed — body position, foot choice, power, timing? Change *one*
  thing next go.
- **Secure the zone** when time is short: the zone often decides placings.
- Count your time: rest enough between attempts to recover, but leave time for the last
  try.

## Lead strategy
- Climb efficiently low down; save power for the top.
- Clip from good positions; skipping a clip is not allowed (each draw must be clipped
  in order), and a clip from a bad position wastes energy.
- Every hold matters: if falling is likely, reach for the next hold to earn the "+".

## Gotchas
- Isolation boredom and cold: bring layers, food, a warm-up plan with time stamps.
- Tape and chalk rules, and stepping on bolt holes / walls without holds (often
  "out of bounds" areas) — read the briefing.
- In local comps with scorecards: get a witness signature for each send.

## Verify
- [ ] Format, scoring and time limits known before warm-up.
- [ ] Observation routine covers start, crux, rests, clips and finish.
- [ ] Attempt log notes one change per attempt.
- [ ] Results checked against your scorecard/judge records promptly (disputes have deadlines).
