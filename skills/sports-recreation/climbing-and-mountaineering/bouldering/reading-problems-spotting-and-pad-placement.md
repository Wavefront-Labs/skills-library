# Reading problems, spotting and pad placement

**Goal:** figure out the problem before wasting attempts, and fall onto pads (or be
guided onto them) every time — bouldering has no rope, so the ground is the safety system.

> Draft: general practice. Highball bouldering (tall problems) carries serious injury
> risk; build up gradually with multiple pads and experienced spotters.

## Read before you pull on
1. **Find the start and the finish**: marked start holds (indoors), sit start (SS) or
   stand, and the finish — matched on the last hold or a **topout** onto the boulder.
2. **Trace the sequence** with your hands from the ground: which hand on which hold,
   where feet go, where the body must turn.
3. **Look for body positions**, not only holds: flagging, drop-knees, heel hooks,
   toe hooks. Ask which way each hold pulls best (a side-pull wants your body away
   from it).
4. **Identify the crux** and any move you might fall from awkwardly (a dyno, a
   high-step on a slab, the topout).
5. Watch others or film your attempt; small foot changes often unlock moves.

## Technique highlights
- **Heel hook**: heel on a hold above/beside you, pull with the hamstring to hold the
  body in and take weight off the arms.
- **Toe hook**: top of the foot pulls under/behind a hold — great for stopping swings.
- **Dyno / deadpoint**: generate momentum from legs; catch the hold at the peak (the
  "dead point") with a committed grab.
- **Topouts**: get your hips over the lip, mantle with straight arms; don't lie flat
  belly-down early. Outdoors, check the top and descent before you start.

## Pad placement (outdoors)
- Pads go where you'll **land**, not under the start: think through each move's fall
  direction, including swings off a dyno and a fall from the topout.
- Cover gaps between pads, rocks, roots and edges; no pad edges or seams under feet.
- **Highball / multi-pad**: stagger extra pads under the hardest moves.
- Re-position pads as the climber moves up — that's part of spotting.

## Spotting (the right way)
- Stance: feet staggered, knees bent, arms up, **thumbs tucked** against fingers (to
  avoid breaking them).
- Goal is to **protect the head and neck and direct the climber onto the pad** — not
  to catch them. Aim hands at the hips/torso.
- Watch the climber's hips, stay in position, stay focused (no phones).
- Communicate: "spotting!" or "ready?" before the climber starts.

## Gotchas
- Landing with straight locked legs hurts knees/ankles: land bent, roll back.
- Indoor mats are large but **other people under the wall** are a hazard: check
  before starting and don't walk under climbers.
- Skin: file calluses, take rest days; tearing a flapper ends sessions.
- Outdoors: brush off chalk and tick marks, don't climb wet sandstone (it breaks).

## Verify
- [ ] Sequence and crux identified from the ground before first attempt.
- [ ] Pads placed for each move's fall line, including the topout.
- [ ] Spotter knows their role and stance; climber agreed "ready".
- [ ] Problem attempts logged with what changed between attempts.
