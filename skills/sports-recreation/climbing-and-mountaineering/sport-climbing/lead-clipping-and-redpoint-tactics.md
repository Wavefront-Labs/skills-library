# Lead clipping and redpoint tactics

**Goal:** clip every bolt cleanly and safely, and turn a route you can't yet climb
in one go into a redpoint (a clean lead after practice).

> Draft: widely taught technique. Learn leading and lead belaying under supervision.
> Fixed-gear quality and local ethics vary by crag; check the guidebook and local
> advice.

## Clipping the quickdraw
1. Find a **clipping stance**: straight arm on a good hold, hips close to the wall,
   draw roughly at chest-to-face height. Clipping from a bad hold or above your head is
   where falls with slack happen.
2. Grab rope from the tie-in knot, bring it up to the draw.
3. Clip with the gate facing **away from the direction you'll climb**, and with the rope
   running **from the wall, out through the carabiner, to you** (not the reverse).
   - **Back-clip** = rope running from you, through the krab, toward the wall. In a fall
     the rope can unclip itself across the gate. Re-clip correctly.
4. **Z-clip** = pulling rope from *below* the last clip and clipping it into the next
   draw — the rope zig-zags and you can't move up. Unclip the upper draw to fix.
5. Two common methods: **pinch** (thumb pushes the rope in, gate facing away) and
   **middle-finger** (gate facing you, rope pulled with middle finger). Practise both
   hands.

## Before you leave the ground
- Partner check: harness buckles, knot (figure-8 follow-through, tail long enough),
  belay device loaded correctly, rope end knotted (stopper knot) or long enough for
  the lower.
- **Stick-clip** the first bolt if the start is hard or the ground is bad.
- Know whether the anchor is a lower-off (clip and lower) or needs threading/rappel.

## Falling well
- Don't grab draws; look down, push slightly off the wall, keep feet in front.
- Never let the rope run **behind your leg** — it can flip you upside down.
- Practise falls progressively with an experienced belayer giving a **soft catch**.
  Early lead fears are best fixed by planned falls, not avoidance.

## Redpoint tactics
1. **Work the route**: on the first goes, hang on bolts to figure out each section
   ("beta"): hand sequences, foot placements, clipping positions.
2. **Write the beta** or rehearse it visually; note rests (jugs, knee-bars, no-hand
   rests) and where you'll chalk.
3. **Link sections**: bolt-to-bolt, then longer links, then from the ground to the crux,
   and from the crux to the top (overlapping links).
4. **Pre-clip strategy**: decide which clips are made from which hold — especially
   around the crux.
5. **Rest between burns** long enough to be fresh (often 20–40 min for hard attempts).
6. Climb faster through pumpy sections; rest fully at rests ("shake out").

## Gotchas
- **First few bolts** are where ground-falls happen — slack management and the belayer's
  position matter most there.
- Rope drag from wandering lines → use longer draws on traversing sections.
- Rusty/spinning bolts or worn lower-offs: report to local route equippers.
- Grades are local opinions; height and style change them.

## Verify
- [ ] No back-clips or z-clips on review (partner or video).
- [ ] Clipping stance used for every clip on your project.
- [ ] Beta written down, including clip positions and rests.
- [ ] Link progress logged per session; fall practice done before committing to runouts.
