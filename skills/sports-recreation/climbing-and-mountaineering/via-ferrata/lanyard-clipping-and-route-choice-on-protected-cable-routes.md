# Lanyard clipping and route choice on via ferrata

**Goal:** stay attached to the cable at all times with equipment that can survive a
fall, and pick routes within your ability and the day's weather.

> Draft: general practice. Equipment standard (EN 958 for via ferrata sets in
> Europe) and grading systems vary by country. Falls on via ferrata can still be
> serious — the energy absorber limits force, it doesn't prevent hitting ledges.

## Equipment
- **Climbing harness**, **helmet** (rockfall is common), gloves (cable wear), approach
  shoes or boots.
- **Via ferrata set** to the current standard: two arms with self-locking
  carabiners, an **energy absorber** (tear webbing). Not a home-made sling — a fall
  onto a static sling generates forces that can kill.
- **Weight range**: sets are certified for a user weight range (commonly around
  40–120 kg); lighter children may need a set rated for them or an additional
  rope belay from an adult. Check the label.
- Rest lanyard (short, separate) for hanging on steep sections — optional.
- Recalled sets exist: check for manufacturer recalls; retire after a fall or after
  the absorber has started deploying.

## Clipping technique
1. Clip **both** carabiners to the cable, gates facing the same way, ideally above the
   waist.
2. At an anchor (where the cable is fixed to the rock): unclip **one** carabiner, clip
   it beyond the anchor; then move the second. **Never have both unclipped.**
3. Keep the carabiners above you on steep sections so a fall is shorter.
4. Move on rungs and rock with your legs; use the cable for hands when needed.
5. **One person per cable section** between anchors on steep ground — a fall onto the
   person below can pull both off.

## Grades and route choice
- Common scales: **A–E/F** (Hüsler/Schall-type, Alps) or 1–6 (Italian/others); many
  guidebooks give both a difficulty and a commitment/length rating.
- Beginners: A/B grades, short routes with escape options.
- Check: **total time** (approach + route + descent), exposure, escape points,
  maintenance status (recent closures).

## Weather
- **Thunderstorms**: a steel cable on a ridge is a lightning conductor. Start early,
  check the forecast, turn back early when cumulus build. Don't touch the cable in a
  storm; move off the route if possible.
- Wet rock and rungs are slippery; cold metal numbs hands.

## Gotchas
- **Queueing** on popular routes — plan for slow traffic.
- **Rockfall** from parties above; wear a helmet, don't stand directly below others.
- Fatigue in arms from over-gripping the cable — use legs.
- A "small slip" fall can deploy the absorber; inspect after any fall.

## Verify
- [ ] Set is certified, within weight range, not recalled, absorber intact.
- [ ] Both carabiners clipped at all times except when passing an anchor, one at a time.
- [ ] One person per anchor-to-anchor section on steep ground.
- [ ] Route grade, time and escape options checked against your party's ability.
- [ ] Forecast checked; turnaround time set for thunderstorm risk.
