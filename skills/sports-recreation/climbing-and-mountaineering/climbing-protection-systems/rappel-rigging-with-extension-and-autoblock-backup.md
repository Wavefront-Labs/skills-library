# Rappel rigging with extension and autoblock backup

**Goal:** descend on a rope safely: an anchor you trust, ends that can't slip through,
a device threaded correctly, and a backup that holds you if you let go.

> Draft: widely taught systems. Rappelling is a leading cause of climbing accidents —
> mostly from rope-end, rigging and anchor errors, not equipment failure. Learn and
> practise near the ground under supervision. Device-specific instructions take
> precedence (assisted-braking devices in particular).

## 1. Check the anchor
- Two or more solid points (bolts, trees, slung blocks), connected to a **metal**
  rappel ring/chain/maillon where possible. Rope running directly over webbing can
  melt it if pulled.
- Inspect fixed tat: faded, stiff, chewed or single-strand slings are replaced or backed up.

## 2. Thread and join the rope
- Single-rope rappel: thread to the middle mark.
- Two ropes: join with a **flat overhand** (offset overhand, "EDK") with tails at
  least ~30 cm and dressed tight. Avoid the flat figure-8 (it can roll at lower loads).
- Know which rope to **pull** — the side with the joining knot, so the knot moves away
  from the ring rather than jamming in it — and say it out loud.

## 3. Stopper knots
- Tie a **stopper knot** in each rope end (e.g. a double overhand/barrel knot), unless
  there's a specific reason not to (e.g. high snagging risk in wind, confirmed short
  rappel to ground — still the minority case). Rappelling off rope ends is a classic
  fatal accident.

## 4. Extended rappel with friction-hitch backup
1. Girth-hitch a sling or a purpose-made extension to the harness belay points; tie
   an overhand to make an extension ~ forearm length. Clip a locker at the end.
2. Thread the **device** on the rope(s) and clip it to the extension (locker locked).
3. Tie an **autoblock** (or prusik/Machard) on the brake strands below the device,
   clipped to the harness **belay loop** with a locker.
4. The autoblock must not reach the device when loaded (it would stop gripping) —
   the extension creates that space.
5. **Test before unclipping from the anchor**: weight the system while still clipped
   to the anchor; check the device, both strands through it, lockers locked, the
   backup grips.

## Device choice
- **Tube/ATC**: light, handles two ropes, smooth; needs the backup.
- **Guide plate**: as a tube for rappel, plus auto-block mode for belaying seconds.
- **Assisted-braking devices** (e.g. Grigri, Megajul-type): single-rope rappels;
  follow the manufacturer's rappel instructions — handle use differs.

## Gotchas
- Different diameters/stretch in two ropes cause creep: the knot can slide; keep an
  eye on it and pull the correct side.
- Loose clothing and hair caught in devices.
- **Rope stuck** on pull: check knot position, flip the rope away from cracks; never
  climb up a single stuck strand without protection.
- Darkness and fatigue at the end of the day are when errors happen — partner check.

## Verify
- [ ] Anchor inspected; rope runs on metal.
- [ ] Rope ends knotted or explicitly confirmed on the ground.
- [ ] Device, extension, backup and lockers checked by partner.
- [ ] Weighted test done while still clipped to the anchor.
- [ ] Pull side identified and the rope pulls freely before the last person descends
      (test-pull when possible).
