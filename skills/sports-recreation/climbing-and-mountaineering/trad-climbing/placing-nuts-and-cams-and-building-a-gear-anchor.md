# Placing nuts and cams, and building a gear anchor

**Goal:** protection that will actually hold a fall in the direction it will be
loaded, and an anchor that survives any single piece failing.

> Draft: standard trad-climbing practice. Learn placements and anchors with a qualified
> instructor or mentor, ideally with practice falls on test placements (aiders,
> bounce-testing) before relying on them. Rock quality decides more than any rule here.

## Nuts (passive)
- Seat the nut in a **constriction** that narrows in the direction of pull (usually
  down, sometimes outward on traverses).
- Maximum **surface contact** on both sides; wiggling at the top should not
  dislodge it. Give it a firm **tug** down to set (not so hard you can't remove it).
- Check the rock: no flakes, hollow blocks or crumbly edges; tap and listen.
- **Lifting out**: rope movement can lift a nut up and out — extend it with a
  quickdraw or sling.

## Cams (active)
- For **parallel-sided** cracks and flares where nuts don't fit.
- Choose the size where the lobes are about **50–90% retracted** (mid-range) — the
  manufacturers' colour-coded range marks help.
  - **Tipped out** (lobes nearly open) = little holding power.
  - **Over-cammed** (fully retracted) = may be impossible to remove.
- Stem **pointing in the direction of the expected load** (down and out toward the
  next piece/belayer).
- Avoid flaring cracks, crystal-filled pockets, and edges where lobes sit on corners.
- **Walking**: rope movement wiggles cams deeper or into a wider spot. Extend placements.

## Extending and rope drag
- Any piece off the line of the rope gets a longer sling/alpine draw.
- Zig-zag lines create drag and can **zipper** pieces out in a fall: the bottom piece
  pulled outward first, then the next. First piece(s) after the belay should be
  **multi-directional** (opposed nuts or a cam) to stop this.

## Building a gear anchor — ERNEST / SERENE
Principles (both acronyms say the same thing):
- **E**qualised (load shared in the expected direction), **R**edundant (no single
  point of failure, including the master point), **N**on-**E**xtending (if one piece
  fails, the others aren't shock-loaded), **S**olid/**S**trong, **T**imely (efficient).
1. Place **at least three** good, independent pieces (two bomber ones on solid rock
   may suffice at some anchors — a judgement call).
2. Join them with a **cordelette** or sling: gather the strands toward the expected
   load direction and tie an overhand or figure-8 to form a **master point**.
3. Alternatively, build with the rope: clove hitches to each piece and to yourself.
4. Belay from the master point or your belay loop as the system requires; anticipate
   upward pulls (lead falls past the belay) with an upward-direction piece.

## Gotchas
- A placement that looks good in the wrong direction is no placement.
- Bad rock beats good technique — expanding flakes are invisible from above.
- Cleaning: second carries a **nut key**; stuck cams usually came from over-camming.
- Gear choice and count for the route: read the guidebook's gear notes.

## Verify
- [ ] Every placement checked for direction of load and rock quality.
- [ ] Cams within mid-range retraction; nuts tug-tested.
- [ ] Pieces off the rope line extended; first piece multi-directional.
- [ ] Anchor has ≥2–3 independent good pieces with a redundant master point.
- [ ] A mentor reviewed your placements on the ground (or on a top-rope practice).
