# Fantasy leagues — draft, waivers, trades and commissioning

**Goal:** enjoy a season-long fantasy league and finish competitive: draft
for value, work the waiver wire every week, make trades that help both
sides, and (if you run it) keep the league fair and alive. For paid daily
fantasy (DFS) contests see
`sports-recreation.gambling-and-wagering.fantasy-sports-betting.dfs-lineup-construction-and-contest-selection`.

## 1. Read the settings before anything else
Strategy is set by the **scoring and roster rules**, not by generic
rankings. Check:
- **Scoring:** e.g. fantasy American football standard vs **PPR** (point per
  reception) vs half-PPR — PPR lifts pass-catching running backs and slot
  receivers. Superflex/2QB leagues make quarterbacks premium.
- **Roster slots and bench size**, flex positions, whether there is an
  injured-reserve slot.
- **Waiver type:** rolling priority, reverse standings, or **FAAB**
  (free-agent acquisition budget, blind bidding).
- **Trade review:** commissioner veto, league vote, or none; trade deadline.
- **Playoff weeks** and how many teams qualify.
- **Keeper/dynasty** rules if players carry over.
Most apps (ESPN, Yahoo, Sleeper, the official game for leagues like FPL) let
you import custom rankings — re-rank for your scoring first.

## 2. Drafting: value over replacement
- **Value-based drafting (VBD):** a player's worth = projected points minus
  what a freely available replacement at the same position would score.
  The positions with a steep drop-off after the top tier go first.
- **Tiers beat rank lists.** Group players into tiers; when a tier is about
  to run out, take the last one; when several remain, take the best player
  from a thinner position.
- **Snake draft:** know your pick slot and plan for the turn (two picks back
  to back at the ends).
- **Auction draft:** budget by position before the draft; avoid spending on
  the first names nominated when everyone's budget is full; leave $1s for
  the end.
- Wait on positions with little scoring spread (often kicker/defence in
  US football — draft them last or stream them).
- Bye weeks: don't stack starters with the same bye, but don't pass on
  value to avoid it either.

## 3. In-season management
- **Set lineups every week**, checking injury news close to kickoff — the
  most common way to lose is an inactive starter.
- **Waivers:** target role changes (a starter injured, a new opportunity)
  more than one big game. In FAAB, bid aggressively early in the season for
  breakouts and keep some budget for injuries in the run-in.
- **Streaming:** rotate weekly at positions with matchups that matter more
  than the player (defences, sometimes quarterbacks or pitchers).
- Play the long game in the playoff weeks: check schedules for those weeks
  from about mid-season.

## 4. Trading
- Trade from depth to need. A fair trade helps both rosters — lopsided
  offers burn goodwill and get ignored.
- Sell high on a player whose output outruns his underlying usage (snaps,
  targets, minutes, expected goals); buy low on the reverse.
- Explain the offer in a message; it accepts far more often than a silent
  proposal.

## 5. Commissioning a league
- Publish the **constitution** before the draft: scoring, waivers, trade
  review rules, deadline, tiebreakers, payout or prizes, what happens to
  inactive teams.
- **Trade veto policy:** veto only for collusion, never for "bad" trades;
  state this up front.
- Keep teams engaged: weekly recap, a trash-talk channel, a punishment or
  trophy for last place.
- Handle abandoned teams by setting lineups or replacing the manager —
  inactive teams distort the playoff race.
- If money is involved, check local law: in some jurisdictions paid
  season-long leagues are regulated or prohibited (I have not verified the
  rule for any particular place; check yours).

## Gotchas
- Pre-season rankings from one source are consensus noise; adjust for your
  scoring.
- Drafting a kicker/defence early is almost always a waste.
- Reacting to one big week on waivers — check the usage, not the points.
- Changing league rules mid-season without a vote.

## Verify
- [ ] You know the scoring, roster, waiver and trade-review rules.
- [ ] Your draft board is tiered and re-ranked for this scoring.
- [ ] Lineups are set with late injury news checked every week.
- [ ] Each trade offer has a written reason that helps both sides.
- [ ] As commissioner, the rules (including the veto policy) were published before the draft.
