# Reading tactics and fan analytics

**Goal:** get more out of watching sport by seeing *why* things happen —
the shape, the plan, the matchup — and by using stats to check your eye,
not replace it.

## 1. Watch the right thing
TV cameras follow the ball; the game is decided off it.
- **Look away from the ball for a few seconds at a time.** Where is the
  defensive line? Who is free? Which player keeps drifting into the same
  space?
- **Watch one player for five minutes.** Their positioning without the
  ball shows the plan (a winger tucking inside, a full-back overlapping,
  a basketball big setting screens).
- **Restarts and transitions** reveal coaching: set pieces, out-of-bounds
  plays, what happens in the five seconds after possession changes.
- Wide-angle replays, "tactical cam" or all-22 footage (where offered)
  show the whole shape.

## 2. A frame for any team sport
Ask four questions about each team:
1. **In possession:** how do they build — short, direct, through one key
   player? Where do they try to create overloads?
2. **Out of possession:** do they press high or sit in a block? Where do
   they force play (wide, inside)?
3. **Transitions:** counter fast or regroup?
4. **Set pieces / special situations.**
Then: **what changed** at half-time, after a substitution or a red card?
Coaches' adjustments are the most visible piece of tactics.

Formation numbers (4-3-3, 3-5-2) describe a starting picture; teams shift
shape in and out of possession — describe what you see, not the team sheet.

## 3. Fan analytics — the useful ones
- **Football (soccer):** xG (expected goals) — the probability that a shot
  of that type/location is scored. A team regularly out-creating opponents
  on xG while losing is often unlucky; a striker scoring far above xG for a
  short stretch usually cools off.
- **Basketball:** per-possession stats (offensive/defensive rating), true
  shooting % (counts free throws and threes), usage rate.
- **Baseball:** OPS/wOBA for hitters, FIP for pitchers (strips out fielding
  luck), WAR as a rough all-in value.
- **American football:** EPA (expected points added) per play, success rate.
- **Cricket:** strike rate and average together; for bowlers, economy in
  context of phase (powerplay vs death overs).
Exact formulas differ by provider — two sites' xG for the same match can
differ noticeably. Compare like with like.

## 4. Statistical traps
- **Small samples:** five games is noise. Rates stabilise at very different
  sample sizes by stat — shooting percentages take long, usage/minutes
  stabilise quickly.
- **Regression to the mean:** extreme early-season numbers usually drift
  back toward normal.
- **Context:** opponent strength, home/away, game state (a team leading
  sits back, inflating opponents' possession).
- **Counting vs rate stats:** totals reward playing time; use per-90/per-game
  or per-possession for comparison.
- **Survivorship:** "every champion did X" ignores teams who did X and failed.

## 5. Punditry and discussion
- Separate **process** (good chances created) from **outcome** (won/lost).
- Distrust single narratives ("lacked desire") that ignore the shape.
- Re-watch a key passage: first impressions are often wrong about who made
  the mistake.

## Verify
- [ ] You can describe each team's in-possession and out-of-possession plan after a match.
- [ ] You noticed at least one in-game adjustment and its effect.
- [ ] Any stat you quote is a rate, has a decent sample and a named source.
- [ ] You separated "played well" from "won" in your verdict.
