# Fan community organising — supporters' clubs, watch parties and tifo

**Goal:** build a fan group that lasts beyond one enthusiastic season —
with clear structure, legal watch parties, safe displays and a community
people want to join.

## 1. Starting a supporters' club
- **Check the club's official scheme.** Many clubs/leagues recognise
  branches (official supporters' clubs) and give perks: ticket
  allocations, merchandise, player visits. Recognition usually needs a
  minimum membership and a named committee.
- Minimum structure: a short written **constitution** (purpose, membership,
  fees, how officers are elected, how money is handled), three officers
  (chair, secretary, treasurer), and a separate bank account — never a
  personal one.
- Fees: keep them low; publish a yearly account of what money was spent on.
- Communication: one announcement channel (email list or broadcast channel)
  plus one chat space. Don't scatter across five apps.
- An AGM once a year keeps it democratic and passes on the work before the
  founders burn out.

## 2. Watch parties
- **Broadcast rights:** showing a match to the public is often a separate
  commercial licence from a home subscription. A pub or venue with a
  commercial subscription is the simplest legal route; screening a home
  subscription in a hired hall can breach the rights holder's terms (rules
  differ by country and competition — confirm with the venue/broadcaster).
- Partner with one bar: guaranteed space and the sound on for your games,
  in return for reliable numbers. Agree it in writing.
- Arrive early to test the screen and audio; have a backup stream plan for
  the venue (their responsibility, but check).
- Make it welcoming: newcomers' table, kit amnesty, kids-friendly fixtures.

## 3. Tifo and card displays
- **Talk to the club's safety officer / supporter liaison weeks ahead.**
  Most grounds require approval of design, materials and set-up times;
  unapproved large displays can be confiscated.
- Materials must usually be **fire-retardant** (certified flags/fabric);
  pyrotechnics are banned at most venues and criminal in some countries.
- **Card mosaics:** design on a seat map (grid of row × seat); print a
  numbered card per seat with a one-line instruction; tape cards to seats
  before gates open; count empty seats in the section — gaps ruin the
  image.
- **Large banners/flags:** plan the roll-out direction, who holds the
  edges, and how it's passed overhead and retrieved without blocking
  stairways.
- Funding: small donations from members, published total spent.
- Photograph from the opposite stand to show what worked.

## 4. Online fan communities
- Write short rules (no abuse of players or other fans, no ticket touting,
  spoilers policy) and enforce them consistently with more than one
  moderator.
- Rival-fan abuse and pile-ons on players damage the club and the group;
  remove them fast.
- Pin a FAQ: tickets, travel, where to watch.
- Avoid scraped/illegal stream links — platforms remove communities for it.

## Gotchas
- One person doing everything = the club dies when they burn out.
- Treasurer and chair being the same person invites trouble; split them.
- Display material that isn't certified fire-retardant is the usual reason
  approval is refused.

## Verify
- [ ] Constitution, three officers and a club bank account exist.
- [ ] Watch-party venue holds the right commercial broadcast licence.
- [ ] Any tifo was approved by the venue, with certified materials and a seat map.
- [ ] Online rules are published and more than one moderator enforces them.
