# MMA: striking-to-takedown chains and cage wrestling

**Goal:** connect striking, wrestling and grappling so each threatens the others —
especially against the cage, where most MMA scrambles happen.

## 1. Rules context
Most regulated pro MMA uses the **Unified Rules of MMA** (as adopted by athletic
commissions; check your jurisdiction and promotion). Key items: 10-point must system,
scoring prioritising effective striking/grappling, then aggression, then cage control;
fouls include strikes to the back of the head and spine, groin strikes, eye pokes,
grabbing the fence, kicks/knees to the head of a **grounded** opponent (definition of
"grounded" changed in recent revisions). Amateur MMA often restricts elbows and
some submissions.

## 2. Stance for MMA
Wider and more upright than wrestling, slightly lower and more square than boxing —
a compromise that lets you sprawl and check kicks. 4–6 oz gloves change defence: the
high-guard shell works less well; head movement and distance matter more.

## 3. Striking-to-takedown chains
The takedown is hidden when the opponent reacts to strikes:
- **Jab–cross → level change → double leg:** the cross brings their hands up; shoot as
  they cover.
- **Overhand → body lock:** close distance behind the punch, duck under, lock hands.
- **Catching a kick → single leg / dump.**
- **Counter-wrestling:** shoot as they throw a committed punch (their weight is forward).
And the reverse: threatening takedowns (feints, level changes) opens the striking.
Drill chains on pads that hold a sprawl at the end.

## 4. Cage wrestling (offence)
- Pin the opponent's back to the fence with head under their chin or in the chest,
  **underhooks** (double unders best), hips close.
- Score: knees to the thighs and body, short punches, stomps to the feet where legal.
- Takedowns off the cage: double leg pulled away from the fence, single leg, body-lock
  trip, outside trip.
- Control ends round-winning momentum for judges — but stalling invites a stand-up.

## 5. Cage wrestling (defence) — getting back up
- **Wall walk:** back to the cage on the ground, post hands and get shoulders to the
  fence, feet underneath, then press back up the cage.
- Fight for **underhooks** and hand control; get your head off the cage.
- **Turn out:** underhook, pummel, pivot so *they're* on the fence.
- Never grab the fence (foul, point deduction).

## 6. Ground and pound
- From top (guard/half/mount): **posture up** before striking, control a wrist or the
  head; short strikes and elbows (where legal) in combination with passing.
- From bottom: control posture, wrist control, stand-up or sweep; MMA guard is about
  getting up more than in BJJ, since judges reward top position.

## 7. Fight camp outline (typical 8–10 weeks)
- Technical: striking, wrestling, BJJ sessions daily or alternating.
- Sparring: scheduled, controlled; hard rounds limited and supervised.
- Strength and conditioning periodised toward peak at fight week.
- **Weight cut:** make most of the loss through gradual dieting; any acute
  dehydration cut needs professional supervision — severe cuts are dangerous and some
  promotions/commissions now restrict them.

## Safety
Headgear and 7–16 oz sparring gloves for most sparring; mouthguard; groin guard.
Track head impacts and observe medical suspensions.

## Common faults
Shooting from too far without a setup; head down in exchanges; giving back while
scrambling up; grabbing the fence; burning out in cage clinches; striking flat-footed
so you can't sprawl.

## Verify
- [ ] At least three strike-to-takedown chains drilled on both sides.
- [ ] Wall-walk and underhook turn-out succeed against a resisting partner.
- [ ] Current rules (Unified or promotion-specific) read, especially grounded-opponent and fence fouls.
- [ ] Camp plan has scheduled, limited hard sparring and a supervised weight plan.
