# HEMA: longsword guards, treatise interpretation and sparring

**Goal:** learn the core of the German (Liechtenauer) longsword tradition, interpret
sources critically rather than copying videos, and spar safely. Italian (Fiore),
rapier and sabre traditions use different vocabulary but the same method applies.

## 1. The four guards (Liechtenauer tradition)
- **Vom Tag** ("from the roof"): sword held above the head or at the shoulder, point up/back.
- **Ochs** ("ox"): hilt at head height beside the head, point aimed at the opponent's face.
- **Pflug** ("plough"): hilt at the hip, point at the opponent's face/chest.
- **Alber** ("fool"): point lowered to the ground in front.
Other common positions: Langort (long point), Nebenhut, Schrankhut. Guards are
moments in motion, not static poses.

## 2. Core cuts
- **Oberhau** (descending cut) and **Unterhau** (rising cut), from either side.
- **Zornhau** ("wrath cut"): a diagonal descending cut from the shoulder, the
  foundation of much of the system — it ends in a bind and offers a thrust.
- The "master cuts" (Meisterhäue: Zornhau, Krumphau, Zwerchhau, Schielhau, Scheitelhau)
  each counter specific guards or attacks.
- Cut with the **true edge**, extending the arms, stepping with the cut (the
  step and blade arrive together), hips and shoulders behind it.

## 3. The bind
When blades meet, judge pressure: **strong (hard)** or **weak (soft)** — *Fühlen*
(feeling). Against hard pressure, go around (Winden or disengage); against soft, push
through to thrust. **Indes** ("meanwhile") — acting in the same moment as the opponent
— is the core tactical idea.

## 4. Interpreting a treatise
- Read the **primary source** (a facsimile and transcription) alongside at least one
  **translation** — Wiktenauer collects many freely. Note which manuscript (Ringeck,
  Lew, Döbringer, Meyer, Fiore's Getty vs Pisani-Dossi) — they differ.
- Separate what the text says from what an interpreter adds. Many details (exact foot
  angles, timing) are interpretive.
- Pressure-test: does the technique work in free sparring against a resisting partner?
  If not, revisit the reading.
- Credit interpreters when teaching their reconstructions.

## 5. Kit and safety
- **Feders** (flexible steel training swords) or club-approved synthetic swords;
  blunt tips with rolled or rubber-capped points; check flex and damage.
- **Mask:** fencing mask rated (commonly 1600N) plus a back-of-head protector/overlay.
- **Jacket:** padded HEMA jacket — many tournaments specify **350N** or 800N puncture
  resistance; check the event's rules.
- **Gloves:** padded HEMA gauntlets (hand injuries — broken fingers — are among the most
  common). Also throat protector, elbow and knee guards, rigid groin guard, chest
  protector.
- Inspect blades for burrs (file them smooth) and stiff spots.

## 6. Tournament scoring (typical, varies by event)
- Points by target (often head and thrusts > body > hands/limbs).
- **Afterblow** rule in many events: an opponent's counter-cut within a tempo after
  being hit reduces or cancels the scorer's point — rewards defence, not "hit and get hit".
- **Doubles** (both hit simultaneously) often penalise both fencers, with a limit
  causing elimination.
- Control rules: excessive force is penalised.
Always read the specific ruleset — HEMA has no single governing standard.

## 7. Practice structure
Solo cutting drills (with a partner checking edge alignment) → paired drills from
the treatise → controlled pressure drills → free sparring at agreed intensity.

## Common faults
Swinging with the arms only; flat (wrong-edge) cuts; cutting without stepping;
static guards; going for doubles; sparring without gauntlets.

## Verify
- [ ] Four guards and the Zornhau demonstrated with step and true-edge alignment.
- [ ] A technique interpreted from source + translation, with notes on what's interpretive.
- [ ] Kit meets the event or club's puncture and padding requirements.
- [ ] Tournament ruleset (targets, afterblow, doubles) read before entering.
