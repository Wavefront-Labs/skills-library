# Kendo: men, kote, do strikes and yuko-datotsu

**Goal:** strike with the unity of spirit, sword and body that referees score as a point,
and behave correctly in the dojo. Related arts (iaido, jodo) share etiquette and
some footwork but are kata-based; this skill focuses on kendo.

## 1. Equipment
- **Shinai** (four bamboo slats, tsuba guard, tsuka-gawa handle cover, tsuru string on
  the back edge = the "blunt" side). Inspect before every session: splinters and cracked
  slats can pierce the mask grille — the classic serious injury. Replace or repair.
- **Bogu** armour: men (helmet), kote (gauntlets), do (chest), tare (hip protector).
- **Bokuto** (wooden sword) for kata and basics.
Beginners train without armour for weeks to months before bogu.

## 2. Kamae and footwork
- **Chudan-no-kamae:** left hand at the end of the tsuka, about a fist in front of the
  navel; right hand near the tsuba, light grip; shinai tip pointing at the opponent's
  throat. Right foot forward, left heel slightly raised, feet about a fist's width apart.
- **Okuri-ashi** (sliding step): the right foot moves first forward, the left follows
  immediately; heels never touch down fully; no crossing.
- **Suriashi:** feet slide across the floor.
- **Fumikomi:** the stamping step with the right foot at the moment of striking —
  driven by the left leg, not jumping.

## 3. The strikes (datotsu-bui — target areas)
- **Men:** top of the head (also sayu-men — left/right upper head).
- **Kote:** right wrist (left wrist only in certain kamae).
- **Do:** right side of the torso (gyaku-do: left).
- **Tsuki:** thrust to the throat flap — usually restricted for juniors and lower grades.
Strike with the **monouchi** (the front quarter of the shinai, string side up), wrists
snapping (tenouchi — wringing grip at impact), arms extended, body following through
past the opponent.

## 4. Ki-ken-tai-ichi
Spirit (kiai — shouting the target name, e.g. "Men!"), sword (correct strike) and body
(fumikomi) must occur **simultaneously**. A strike without the shout and stamp in
unison looks like a tap and won't score.

## 5. Yuko-datotsu — what scores (FIK rules, summarised)
A valid point requires:
- Full spirit and appropriate posture.
- Striking a target area with the **datotsu-bu** (the monouchi) of the shinai, with
  the **jinbu** (correct edge, string on the opposite side).
- **Hasuji** (correct angle of the blade).
- **Zanshin** — continuous alertness and readiness after the strike (passing through,
  turning to face, kamae re-established).
Three shinpan (referees) flag points; two flags needed. Shiai is first to two points
(sanbon shobu) within the time limit (often 5 min, varies by event).

## 6. Etiquette (reigi)
Bow entering and leaving the dojo; bow to the shomen and to teachers; seiza for
mokuso (meditation) at the start and end; tie and remove men facing away or as your
dojo prescribes; never step over a shinai. Etiquette is part of grading.

## 7. Grading
Kyu grades then dan grades through national federations affiliated with the FIK.
Gradings include kirikaeshi, waza demonstration, jigeiko and Nihon Kendo Kata;
higher dan grades add a written exam. Minimum intervals between dan grades apply.

## Common faults
Hitting with the right hand only (left hand should be the power), jumping instead of
fumikomi, striking with the tip or the side of the shinai, no kiai, stopping after
the strike (no zanshin).
