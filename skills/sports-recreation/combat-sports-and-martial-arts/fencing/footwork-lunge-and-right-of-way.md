# Fencing: footwork, the lunge and right-of-way

**Goal:** move efficiently on the piste, deliver a lunge that lands, defend with
parry-riposte, and understand who gets the point when both lights go on.

## 1. The three weapons
| | Target | Touch by | Right-of-way |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Foil** | Torso (lamé) | Point only | Yes |
| **Épée** | Whole body | Point only | No — first hit (within ~40–50 ms window, both score as a double) |
| **Sabre** | Above the waist incl. arms and head | Point or edge (cut) | Yes |
Electric scoring: body cord connects to the weapon; a scoring box lights for valid
touches (coloured) and off-target (white, foil only).

## 2. En garde and footwork
- Feet roughly shoulder width plus, at right angles (front foot pointing at the
  opponent, back foot perpendicular), heels in line.
- Knees bent over the toes, weight centred, torso upright, back arm raised (foil/épée)
  or on the hip (sabre).
- **Advance:** front foot lifts toes first and lands heel first, back foot follows the
  same distance. **Retreat:** back foot first, then front.
- Keep steps small and equal; never cross feet; never let the stance narrow.
Drill: coach-led footwork with constant changes of direction and speed.

## 3. The lunge
1. **Arm first:** extend the weapon arm toward the target (point threatening) —
   starting with the arm establishes the attack (and in foil/sabre, priority).
2. Front foot kicks forward low, landing heel-first; back leg drives and straightens
   fully, back foot flat on the floor.
3. Front knee over the ankle (not beyond — knee injury risk), torso upright, back arm
   drops parallel to the back leg for balance.
4. **Recover** to en garde by pushing back off the front heel and bending the back knee.
**Flèche** (running attack, foil/épée; banned in sabre in favour of the *flunge*/run-in rules) for
longer distances.

## 4. Parry and riposte
- **Parry:** deflect the blade with your forte (strong part, near the guard) against
  their foible (weak part). Learn the main parries for your weapon (e.g. 4, 6 in foil/épée;
  3, 4, 5 in sabre).
- **Riposte:** immediate counter-attack after a parry — it has priority in foil/sabre.
- Keep parries small; big sweeping parries open the line and slow the riposte.

## 5. Distance (measure)
The core skill: be just out of the opponent's lunge range while staying close enough
to attack. Use footwork to "steal" distance (small advances) and draw their attack
into short distance, then riposte or counter-time.

## 6. Right-of-way (foil and sabre)
When both fencers hit, the referee awards the point to the fencer with **priority**:
- An **attack** — arm extending and point/edge continuously threatening the target —
  has priority over a counter-attack.
- A **parry** takes priority from the attacker; the riposte then has priority.
- If the attack misses or stops, priority can pass to the opponent's remise/counter.
- An attack into the opponent's established **point in line** (arm already straight,
  point threatening) loses priority unless the blade is first deflected.
- **Simultaneous** actions with no clear priority: no point (foil) or referee decides
  (sabre "simultaneous" rule).
Referees' interpretations shift with convention; watch elite bouts with commentary to learn them.

## 7. Safety kit
FIE-rated or club-standard mask, jacket, plastron (underarm protector), glove, chest
protector (required for women, recommended for all), breeches, and knee-high socks.
Check blades for kinks and replace broken blades immediately — a broken blade is the
main serious-injury risk.

## Common faults
Lunging before extending the arm; front knee past the toes; bouncing upper body;
parrying with the foible; too-wide parries; losing distance by advancing on the same rhythm.
