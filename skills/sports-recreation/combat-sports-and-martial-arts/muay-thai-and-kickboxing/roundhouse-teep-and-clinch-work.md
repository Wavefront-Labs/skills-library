# Muay Thai and kickboxing: roundhouse, teep and clinch work

**Goal:** throw a powerful Thai-style roundhouse and a teep, defend kicks by checking,
and control the clinch with knees. Train under a coach; pad holders matter as much as kickers.

## 1. Rulesets
- **Muay Thai:** punches, kicks, knees, elbows, clinching and sweeps/dumps from the clinch.
  Judges weigh effective and damaging techniques, balance and ring control; kicks and
  knees often score highly. 5 × 3-min rounds in traditional pro bouts.
- **K-1 style kickboxing:** punches, kicks, knees; elbows banned; clinching limited to a
  brief hold for a single knee.
- **Full contact / "American" kickboxing:** kicks above the waist only in some
  organisations.
Verify the event's rules — they change between promotions.

## 2. The Thai roundhouse
- Step out slightly at an angle with the lead foot (for a rear kick), **rise onto the
  ball of the supporting foot and pivot** so the heel faces the target.
- Swing the leg **from the hip**, leg mostly straight, rotating the hips fully through;
  the same-side arm swings down and back for counter-rotation while the other hand guards
  the face.
- Strike with the **lower shin**, not the foot (the foot breaks; the shin is conditioned).
- If it misses, **follow through** and spin 360° back to stance rather than stopping —
  some coaches prefer returning the leg; both are taught.
- Targets: thigh (low kick), body, head.

## 3. Teep (push kick)
Lift the knee high, push the ball of the foot (or the whole sole) through the
opponent's hips or belly, hips thrust forward, lean back slightly. Uses: stop forward
pressure, maintain distance, disrupt rhythm, score. Lead-leg teep is the jab of Muay Thai.

## 4. Checking kicks
Lift the lead (or targeted) leg, knee turned out toward the kick, shin angled so the
kick hits shin or knee rather than the thigh. Hands stay up. Opponents' shins can break
on a well-timed check. Alternatives: step back out of range, catch the kick (and sweep
or counter).

## 5. Clinch
- **Plum (double collar tie):** both hands cupped on the back of the head (hands on top
  of each other, not interlaced fingers), elbows squeezing together on the collarbones,
  pulling the head down.
- **Knees:** straight knees (drive hips forward) and diagonal knees to body; keep balance
  by pulling the head down.
- **Fighting for position:** swim the arms inside ("inside position wins"), frame on the
  neck/biceps, turn the opponent and off-balance them for sweeps/dumps (in rulesets that allow).
- Elbows from the clinch break where legal.

## 6. Pad drills
- **Thai pads** (kick pads on the forearms): holder meets the kick with a slight forward
  pressure; calls combinations ("jab, cross, switch kick").
- Classic combos: jab–cross–lead hook–rear kick; jab–rear teep; lead teep–rear kick;
  cross–switch kick; catch–counter drills.
- Belly pad for knees and teeps.

## 7. Shin conditioning
Repetitive bag kicking at increasing volumes over months; avoid the myths (hitting
sticks/bottles hard — causes damage). Shin guards in sparring always.

## Safety
Sparring is light and technical most of the time; hard sparring is limited. Use shin guards,
16 oz gloves (or per gym rules), mouthguard, groin guard. Stop for concussion symptoms.

## Common faults
Kicking with the foot; not pivoting; dropping the hands when kicking; leaning back on
the roundhouse so it lacks hip; flat-footed teep; interlocking fingers in the plum.
