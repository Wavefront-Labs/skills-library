# Taekwondo: kicking mechanics and electronic-scoring sparring

**Goal:** fast, balanced kicks that register on the electronic scoring system, and a
sparring approach built around the Olympic (World Taekwondo) rules. ITF taekwondo uses
different rules and scoring — check which you compete under.

## 1. Kicking mechanics
The general sequence: **chamber → pivot → extend → re-chamber → land in stance.**
- **Roundhouse (dollyo chagi):** lift the knee pointing at the target, **pivot the
  supporting foot** so the heel turns toward the target (~180° for full power; less
  for fast scoring kicks), hips turn over, snap the lower leg; strike with the instep
  (ball of foot in some traditional schools). Re-chamber before landing.
- **Back kick (dwit chagi):** turn the back, look over the shoulder, kick straight back
  with the heel along a line close to the supporting leg — not swinging wide. Strong
  counter against an advancing opponent.
- **Cut/push kick (pushing front kick):** blocks and disrupts an attack, controls distance.
- **Axe kick (naeryo chagi)** and **spinning hook (dwi huryeo chagi)** for head shots.
- Supporting knee slightly bent; guard hands stay up; the body leans back as needed to
  counterbalance high kicks without falling away.

## 2. How electronic scoring works (WT, verify current rules)
- Athletes wear a **PSS (Protector and Scoring System)** trunk protector and electronic
  headgear, plus **sensor socks**; a kick registers when the sensor sock contacts the
  hogu or headgear with sufficient force (the threshold varies by weight category and
  gender).
- Typical point values (have changed over cycles): punch to trunk 1; kick to trunk 2;
  turning kick to trunk 4; kick to head 3; turning kick to head 5; opponent penalty
  (gam-jeom) gives 1. Punches and some head points are judged by corner judges
  rather than sensors.
- Matches recently use a best-of-three-rounds format. Rules changed in recent cycles —
  read the current WT competition rules.
Implication: **contact location and sock contact matter more than power**; fast,
accurate kicks with the sensor area of the foot score.

## 3. Sparring tactics
- **Stance:** closed (same lead leg as the opponent) vs open (opposite) changes which
  kicks are available and safe — learn the typical attacks from each.
- **Front leg** is the primary weapon in modern WT sparring: fast cut kicks, front-leg
  roundhouses and hook kicks to the head score without committing.
- **Counter-attack:** let them commit, then back kick or step-back roundhouse.
- **Penalties to avoid** (gam-jeom): stepping out of the area, falling, grabbing/holding,
  avoiding or delaying the match, kicking below the waist, hitting the head with the
  hand. Penalties also gift points.
- Constant bouncing in stance keeps you ready, but be economical with energy.

## 4. Flexibility
Head kicks need hip flexibility and active (not just passive) range:
- Dynamic leg swings in warm-up.
- Static stretching (front split, middle split work) after training, held 30–60 s.
- Strength in the end range: slow controlled kicks held at height.
Never force splits cold.

## 5. Poomsae (brief)
Patterns (Taegeuk 1–8 for coloured belts, black-belt poomsae after). Graded on accuracy
(stances, techniques, direction) and presentation (power, speed, rhythm, expression).
Also an independent competition discipline.

## Safety
Full protective kit in sparring (headgear, trunk protector, groin guard, forearm and shin
guards, mouthguard). Check kicks on pads before partner work. Head kicks cause
concussions — follow return-to-play protocol after any head impact symptoms.

## Common faults
No pivot on the supporting foot (knee strain, weak kick); dropping hands when kicking;
falling after high kicks; telegraphing with a shoulder lean; kicking with toes pointed up (misses sensors).
