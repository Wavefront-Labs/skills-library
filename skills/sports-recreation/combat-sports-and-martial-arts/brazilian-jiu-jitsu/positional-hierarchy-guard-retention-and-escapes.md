# BJJ: positional hierarchy, guard retention and escapes

**Goal:** understand which positions are good and bad, survive and escape the bad ones,
keep your guard, and roll safely. "Position before submission."

## 1. Positional hierarchy (top player's view, best → worst)
1. **Back control** (hooks in, seatbelt grip) — strongest attacking position.
2. **Mount** (sitting on the torso).
3. **Knee on belly**.
4. **Side control** (chest-to-chest across).
5. **Inside opponent's guard** (between their legs) — neutral-to-bad for the top player.
For the bottom player it's the reverse: guard is your home; flat on your back under
side control or mount is where you lose.

**IBJJF points** (verify current rules): takedown 2, sweep 2, knee on belly 2, guard
pass 3, mount 4, back control 4. Positions must be held ~3 seconds. Advantages and
penalties break ties; submission ends it.

## 2. Frames and the hip escape (shrimp)
The bottom player's two main tools:
- **Frames:** forearms and hands as rigid struts against neck, hips or shoulder to keep
  space — don't push with straight arms (armbar risk), use bone structure.
- **Hip escape:** on your side, feet on the mat, push off a foot and drive the hips
  away from the opponent to create space; then insert a knee/leg to recover guard.
Solo drill: shrimp the length of the mat both sides every class.

## 3. Escaping mount
- **Bridge and roll (upa):** trap one of their arms (grip the wrist/elbow to your chest)
  and the same-side foot (your foot outside theirs), bridge hard up and over the trapped
  shoulder, roll into their guard.
- **Elbow-knee escape:** frame, shrimp to one side, bring your knee inside and recover half
  guard then full guard.
Combine them: a bridge makes them post, which opens the elbow-knee escape.

## 4. Escaping side control
Frame on the neck and hip, create space, shrimp out and re-guard; or turn to your knees
(wrestle-up). Don't push with straight arms, and don't give your back by turning away
when they're ready for it.

## 5. Closed guard and retention
- **Closed guard:** legs locked around their waist; break posture by pulling the head
  and collar down. Attacks: armbar, triangle, kimura, hip-bump sweep, scissor sweep.
- **Guard retention** in open guard: keep feet and knees between you and them
  (feet on hips/biceps), stay on your side or hip, not flat; when they move to pass,
  move your hips to face them and re-insert a knee or foot.
- Passers attack by controlling legs and pinning hips — the bottom player wins by
  moving the hips first.

## 6. Gi vs no-gi
Gi allows collar and sleeve grips (slower, more grip-based guards); no-gi relies on
wrist control, underhooks and body locks, faster scrambles; heel hooks and other
leg locks have rank and ruleset restrictions in IBJJF — check before competing.

## 7. Rolling safely
- **Tap early and clearly** (hand on partner or mat several times, or say "tap").
  Pride causes injuries — especially in heel hooks and neck cranks where damage comes
  before pain.
- Apply submissions slowly and release on the tap.
- Beginners roll with higher belts or at "flow" intensity first; avoid spazzing.
- Hygiene: clean gi every session, short nails, no training with skin infections.

## Common faults
Lying flat on the back; straight-arm pushing; chasing submissions from bad positions;
holding breath; forgetting to shrimp.
