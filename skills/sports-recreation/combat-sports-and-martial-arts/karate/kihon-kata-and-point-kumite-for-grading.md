# Karate: kihon, kata and point kumite for grading

**Goal:** perform basics, kata and kumite at the standard a grading panel expects, and
know how point competition is scored. Syllabuses differ by style (Shotokan, Goju-ryu,
Wado-ryu, Shito-ryu, Kyokushin) and by association — your own syllabus sheet rules.

## 1. Kihon (basics)
- **Stances:** zenkutsu-dachi (front stance; ~60–70% weight front, back leg straight,
  hips square for a straight punch), kokutsu-dachi (back stance, ~70% weight rear),
  kiba-dachi (horse stance), neko-ashi-dachi (cat stance). Names and proportions vary by style.
- **Hikite:** the non-punching hand pulls back sharply to the hip — it adds power and
  graders watch for it.
- **Kime:** focus — a split-second full-body tension at impact, then relax. Punches that
  float with no kime look weak.
- **Core techniques:** oi-zuki (lunge punch), gyaku-zuki (reverse punch), age-uke
  (rising block), soto-uke/uchi-uke, gedan-barai (down block), mae-geri (front kick),
  mawashi-geri (roundhouse), yoko-geri (side kick).
- Practise moving up and down the floor in stance, same height throughout (no bobbing).

## 2. Kata
- Learn the **embusen** (floor pattern) first, then techniques, then rhythm and
  power.
- Panels look for: correct stances and techniques, **timing** (fast/slow contrasts),
  **kime**, balance, eye direction (look before you turn), breathing, kiai at the
  prescribed points, and starting and finishing on the same spot.
- Understand **bunkai** (applications) — senior gradings often ask for them.
- Rehearse at full intensity regularly; slow practice hides balance errors.

## 3. Kumite for gradings
Lower grades: prearranged sparring (gohon/sanbon/kihon ippon kumite) — attacker
announces target, defender blocks and counters with control. Higher grades: jiyu
kumite (free sparring). Control and distance are graded, not damage.

## 4. Point kumite (WKF rules — check current edition)
- **Ippon (3 points):** jodan (head-level) kicks; techniques on a thrown/fallen opponent.
- **Waza-ari (2 points):** chudan (body) kicks.
- **Yuko (1 point):** punches (tsuki) and strikes (uchi) to scoring areas.
Scoring needs good form, sporting attitude, vigorous application, awareness
(zanshin), good timing and correct distance. Excessive contact, especially to the face,
is penalised. Matches end on time or an 8-point gap.
Tactics: fast gyaku-zuki and kizami-zuki counters, foot-sweep follow-ups, controlling
the centre of the area, staying out of range until committing.

## 5. Grading day
- Clean, correctly tied **gi** and belt; no jewellery; short nails.
- Bow on entering and leaving; answer with "Osu" or "Hai" as your dojo does.
- Know the Japanese counting (ichi to ju) and the names of your kata and techniques.
- Typical intervals between kyu grades are a few months of regular training;
  dan grades need years. Associations set minimum training times.

## 6. Conditioning
Flexibility (hip openers for kicks), leg strength for low stances, and anaerobic
fitness for kumite rounds. Warm up properly — cold kicking strains hamstrings and groin.

## Common faults
Hikite left floating, stances shrinking as you tire, rushing kata, telegraphing
punches by pulling back, turning without looking, uncontrolled contact in kumite.
