# Judo: breakfalls, kuzushi and grip fighting

**Goal:** fall safely, understand why throws work (balance-breaking), take and fight for
grips, and know how a contest is scored. Practise only on proper mats with a qualified coach.

## 1. Ukemi — breakfalls first
Everyone learns to fall before learning to throw.
- **Ushiro ukemi (back):** tuck the chin hard to the chest, round the back, roll down
  the spine, slap both arms on the mat at ~45° from the body, palms down, as the
  upper back lands. Head never touches.
- **Yoko ukemi (side):** leg sweeps across, land on the side with the same-side arm
  slapping; chin tucked.
- **Mae mawari ukemi (forward roll):** roll diagonally across the shoulder to the
  opposite hip, finishing in a side breakfall or standing.
- Never post a straight arm to catch yourself — that's how collarbones and elbows break.
Progress: from sitting → squatting → standing → being thrown by a partner.

## 2. The throw sequence
- **Kuzushi:** break the opponent's balance — move their weight onto their toes, heels
  or one foot (eight directions of balance-breaking).
- **Tsukuri:** fit-in — position your body correctly for the throw while they're off
  balance.
- **Kake:** execution.
Without kuzushi, strength-only throws fail and injure. Uchikomi (repetition fit-ins)
drills kuzushi and tsukuri without throwing.

## 3. Standard grip (kumikata)
Right-handed (ai-yotsu/kenka-yotsu describe same-side vs opposite-side stances):
- **Left hand: sleeve** — grip the outside of the opponent's right sleeve near the elbow.
- **Right hand: lapel** — grip their left lapel around collarbone/chest height.
Pull the sleeve out and up ("look at your watch") while the lapel hand lifts and
drives — that's the core kuzushi engine.

## 4. Grip fighting
Competition judo is won or lost on grips:
- Get your dominant grip first; **break** grips you don't want (two-on-one strip,
  rotating the wrist out, pushing the gripping hand down while stepping away).
- Don't stay in a bad grip — strip and regrip.
- Rules penalise negative gripping: holding without attacking, avoiding grips, one-side
  gripping too long, breaking grips with two hands repeatedly, cross-grips without an
  immediate attack. **Check current IJF rules** — gripping rules change every cycle.

## 5. First throws
- **O-soto-gari** (major outer reap): kuzushi back over their heel, step alongside,
  reap the near leg with your leg straight back. Partner must know ushiro ukemi.
- **O-goshi** (major hip throw): kuzushi forward, turn in, hip below and across theirs,
  lift and rotate.
- **Seoi-nage** (shoulder throw): forward kuzushi, turn in low, load onto the back.
Drill each on both sides eventually; competitors develop a tokui-waza (favourite throw).

## 6. Scoring (IJF, summarised — verify current edition)
- **Ippon:** throw largely on the back with force and speed and control; or
  pin (osaekomi) for the required time (20 s in recent rules); or submission
  (shime-waza chokes, kansetsu-waza armlocks for seniors). Ends the contest.
- **Waza-ari:** partial throw or shorter hold (10 s recently). Two waza-ari = ippon in
  current rules.
- **Shido:** minor penalties; accumulated shidos can lose the contest.
- Golden score if tied at time.

## 7. Newaza basics
Transition from throws to the ground: pins (kesa-gatame, yoko-shiho-gatame), turnovers,
escapes. Juniors have restrictions on chokes and armlocks — follow age rules.

## Safety
Beginners don't do randori (free practice) until breakfalls are automatic. No
neck-cranking or diving head-first into throws (prohibited and dangerous).
Trim nails, clean judogi, no jewellery.

## Verify
- [ ] Back, side and forward ukemi performed with chin tucked and slap timing correct.
- [ ] Throws demonstrated with visible kuzushi before entry.
- [ ] Standard sleeve-lapel grip taken and a basic grip break performed.
- [ ] Current IJF scoring and gripping rules read for your event.
