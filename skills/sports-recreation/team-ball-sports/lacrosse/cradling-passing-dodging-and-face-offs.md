# Lacrosse — cradling, passing, dodging and face-offs

**Goal:** keep the ball in your stick under pressure, beat a defender, and
win possession battles. Men's field, women's field, box (indoor) and the
Olympic **Sixes** format differ in contact, equipment and restarts — the
stick skills transfer.

## 1. Grip and cradle
- **Top hand** near the head of the stick (not choked right up), **bottom
  hand** at the butt end. Hands are switched for the other side.
- **Cradle:** a rhythmic curl of the top wrist that keeps the ball seated
  in the pocket by centripetal force. Keep the stick near your ear or
  vertical ("in the box" by your head and shoulders) when pressured.
- **Protect** with the body: carry the stick on the side away from the
  defender, using the off-arm and shoulder legally (in men's field; women's
  rules restrict body contact).

## 2. Passing and catching
- **Pass:** top hand pushes forward like throwing, bottom hand pulls toward
  the armpit; step toward the target with the opposite foot; follow
  through to the target ("point the head").
- **Catch:** give with the ball — let the stick head travel back with it
  ("soft hands"). Present a target at ear height away from the defender.
- **Wall-ball:** 15–30 minutes, both hands, a few times a week, is the
  standard way to build stick skills. Vary distance, quick sticks
  (catch-and-release), and one-handed.

## 3. Dodges
Beat the defender with a change of direction while protecting the stick:
- **Split dodge:** drive one way, then switch hands across the body to go
  the other way. Fast but exposes the stick briefly.
- **Roll dodge:** plant into the defender, pivot on the inside foot and
  spin away, keeping the stick tight to the body.
- **Face dodge:** fake a pass or shot, bring the stick across the face to
  the other side, then go.
- **Rule of thumb:** dodge with **one hand down the alley** toward goal; a
  sideways dodge just moves the defender.

## 4. Ground balls
"Win the ground ball, win the game." Get low, bottom hand down by the
ground, scoop **through** the ball rather than at it, and bring the stick
to your face immediately. Box out the opponent before the ball arrives.

## 5. Face-offs and draws
- **Men's field/box face-off:** two players crouch with sticks back-to-back
  over the ball. Techniques include the **clamp** (trapping the ball under
  the stick head) and the **rake**. Wings sprint in when the whistle goes.
  Highly specialised — face-off specialists ("FOGOs") practise it alone.
- **Women's draw:** the ball is placed between two sticks held at hip height
  and thrown into the air on the whistle. Players at the centre circle line
  try to catch it.
- **Sixes** uses a different restart (no face-off after goals in current
  rules) — check World Lacrosse's current Sixes rules.

## 6. Team concepts
- **Ride:** after a turnover in the offensive end, attackers pressure the
  defensive clear to win the ball back.
- **Clear:** defenders and goalie move the ball past midfield; offside
  rules require set numbers of players on each side of the field.
- **Man-up (extra-man):** after a penalty, use a 3-3 or 1-3-2 set and
  move the ball quickly; an open shooter usually appears within a few
  passes.
- **Defensive slides:** the "hot" defender slides to the dodger, the next
  defender rotates to cover the open attacker.
- A **shot clock** applies in many competitions; know your league's
  length.

## Gotchas
- Cradling with the arms instead of the wrist (loses the ball under checks).
- Only practising your strong hand.
- Running sideways instead of attacking the goal on a dodge.

## Verify
- [ ] 50 consecutive wall-ball catches each hand.
- [ ] Ground-ball scoop to cradle in one motion, 8 of 10.
- [ ] Can execute a split and roll dodge at speed.
- [ ] Team can run a clear without an offside violation.
