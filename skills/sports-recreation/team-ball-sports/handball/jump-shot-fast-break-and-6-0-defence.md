# Handball — jump shot, fast break and 6-0 defence

**Goal:** score from the backcourt and on the break, defend the 6 m area as a
line, and avoid the step and time violations that give the ball away.
Seven a side (six court players plus goalkeeper); only the goalkeeper may be
inside the 6 m goal area.

## 1. Rules that shape everything
- **Three steps:** you may take up to three steps holding the ball, then
  must shoot, pass or dribble. You can dribble, catch, and take three more
  steps — but not dribble again after catching (double dribble).
- **Three seconds:** you may hold the ball still for at most three seconds.
- **Goal area:** court players may not land in it with the ball; jumping
  from outside and releasing before landing inside is legal.
- **Passive play:** the referees raise a warning arm when a team stops
  trying to attack; after the warning, a set number of passes (currently
  four under IHF rules — check your competition) before a shot is required.
- **7 m throw:** awarded when a clear chance is illegally destroyed.

## 2. The jump shot
- Approach with the last step on the **opposite** foot to the throwing arm
  (a right-hander takes off from the left foot), driving the knee of the
  throwing side upward.
- Ball held high and back, elbow at or above shoulder height.
- Release at the top of the jump: hips and trunk rotate, then the arm
  whips through; wrist snap directs the ball.
- **Aim:** the corners and low near the keeper's feet. Watch where the
  keeper leans and shoot away from it.
- From the **wing**, jump toward the goal area to widen the shooting angle;
  "fall" shots inside the airspace are legal.

## 3. Fast break (counterattack)
- **First wave:** wingers sprint the instant possession changes; the
  goalkeeper throws a long pass directly to them.
- **Second wave:** backcourt players fill in at speed, outnumbering a
  retreating defence before it sets up.
- **Third wave/fast restart:** after conceding, the centre throw-off can be
  taken quickly from the centre area (rules since 2016 allow this), so a
  team that celebrates too long concedes again.
- **Empty goal / 7th court player:** a team may replace the keeper with a
  court player for an extra attacker. A long-range shot into the empty net
  punishes a turnover.

## 4. Defence: 6-0 and alternatives
- **6-0:** all six defenders on or near the 6 m line, moving together as a
  wall. Step out to meet the shooter with arms up, then drop back into the
  line. Help sideways on breakthroughs. Strong against pivots and wing
  play; weak against good long-range shooters.
- **5-1:** one defender pushed out in front to disrupt the playmaker.
- **3-2-1:** aggressive, deep layers; good against teams that can't pass
  well, risky against runners behind it.
- **Contact rules:** you can block with the torso from the front, and use
  bent arms to control the attacker; pushing, holding and hitting the arm
  are fouls. Progressive punishment: warning, 2-minute suspension, red card.

## 5. Pivot (line player)
Plays among the defenders at the 6 m line, setting screens for backcourt
shooters and getting free for a quick pass and a falling shot.

## 6. Goalkeeping basics
Stand a step off the line, square to the ball, hands up and wide. Move to
narrow the angle as the shooter changes position; stay big and react to
the ball rather than guessing early. Any part of the body can save inside
the goal area.

## Gotchas
- Travelling with a fourth step after a catch.
- Shooting into the keeper's hands from the middle of the goal.
- Slow transition after losing the ball — handball punishes it within
  seconds.

## Verify
- [ ] Jump shot from 9 m hits a target corner 5 of 10.
- [ ] Wingers launch within one second of a turnover in drills.
- [ ] 6-0 line moves as one unit for 30 s of ball movement.
- [ ] Players can explain the passive-play warning and the pass count.
