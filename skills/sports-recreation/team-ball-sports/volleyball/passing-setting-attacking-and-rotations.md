# Volleyball — passing, setting, attacking and rotations

**Goal:** turn the opponent's serve into a hittable set and a kill, and line
up legally while doing it. Indoor: six a side, three contacts; beach: two a
side, three contacts, stricter hand-setting.

## 1. Forearm pass (bump) and serve receive
- **Ready position:** feet wider than shoulders, knees bent, weight forward
  on the balls of the feet, arms out front.
- **Platform:** one hand cupped in the other, thumbs together and pointing
  down, elbows straight. The flat surface is the forearms, not the wrists or
  hands.
- **Move first, then pass:** get your body behind and slightly inside the
  ball, stop, then present the platform. Swinging the arms adds error;
  angle the platform toward the target and let the legs lift.
- **Target:** the setter's spot, roughly right-centre at the net, about a
  metre off it. A pass "on the net" is as bad as a shank.
- Receive formations usually use 3 passers (often the libero plus two
  outside hitters).

## 2. Overhead pass (setting)
- Hands form a window above the forehead, thumbs and index fingers making a
  triangle, fingers spread.
- Contact on the finger pads, absorbing slightly then extending arms and
  legs together. Square up to the target before the ball arrives.
- **Faults:** a **double contact** (ball comes off the hands at different
  times) or a **lift/catch** (ball rests in the hands). Indoor allows a
  little more leeway on doubles on the first contact; beach officials are
  strict about hand sets — many beach players bump-set instead.

## 3. Attack approach (spike)
For a right-hander, a common **four-step approach**: right, **left**,
**right-left** (the last two quick, as a "step-close"). Shorter players and
beginners use three steps (left, right-left).
- Arms swing back on the step-close, then up together to load the jump.
- Jump **behind** the ball so you hit it in front of the hitting shoulder.
- High elbow, open hand, wrist snap for topspin.
- Time it: start the approach when the set leaves the setter's hands and is
  at its peak height for a high outside ball.

## 4. Blocking
- Start at the net, hands up at shoulder height.
- Step (slide or crossover) to line up with the hitter's hitting shoulder,
  jump slightly after the hitter, and **press** hands over the net into the
  opponent's space. Thumbs up, fingers spread, hands angled back toward court.
- Touching the net during play is a fault.

## 5. Serving
- **Underhand** for beginners; **overhand float** (no spin, wobbles like a
  knuckleball) is the most common reliable serve; **jump serves** add power
  at a cost in errors. A missed serve is a free point — serve in first.

## 6. Rotation, overlap and the libero (indoor)
- Six positions numbered 1 (right back, server) to 6 (middle back),
  counter-clockwise. Rotate clockwise each time your team wins the serve
  back.
- **Overlap rule:** at the moment of serve, each front-row player must be
  closer to the net than their corresponding back-row player, and players
  must be in correct left-right order within each row. After the serve,
  anyone can move anywhere (but back-row players can't attack above the net
  from in front of the 3 m line).
- **Libero:** back-row specialist in a contrasting shirt; can't attack above
  the net, and in most rule sets can't overhead-set from in front of the
  3 m line to a hitter attacking above the net.
- **5-1** uses one setter; **6-2** uses two setters, each setting from the
  back row.

## Gotchas
- Swinging arms on the pass instead of moving the feet.
- Starting the approach too early and running under the ball.
- Rotational faults when subs come on — confirm positions before every serve.

## Verify
- [ ] 7 of 10 served balls passed to the setter's zone.
- [ ] Sets land in a 1 m target with no double-contact calls.
- [ ] Hitter's approach timed to contact in front of the shoulder.
- [ ] Team can line up legally in all six rotations without prompting.
