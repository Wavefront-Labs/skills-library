# American football — stance, routes, blocking and coverage reads

**Goal:** know your position's base technique and enough scheme to read what
the defence (or offence) is doing before the snap. Fundamentals here apply
to tackle and, minus contact, to flag football.

## 1. Stances
- **Two-point** (receivers, linebackers, backs): feet staggered, weight on
  the balls of the feet, knees bent, ready to explode.
- **Three-point** (linemen): one hand down, back flat, weight balanced so
  you can go forward, back or sideways without tipping off the play. Too
  much weight on the hand = obvious run tell.

## 2. Route running
- Every route has a **release** (beating press off the line), a **stem**
  (the vertical push that sells deep), a **break** and a finish.
- **Route tree** (common numbering, varies by team): 0 hitch/flat-ish,
  1 flat, 2 slant, 3 comeback, 4 curl, 5 out, 6 dig/in, 7 corner, 8 post,
  9 go. Learn your team's version, not someone else's.
- **Selling the break:** run the stem at the same speed and body lean as a
  go route; sink the hips in 2–3 controlled steps before the cut. Rounding
  the break gives the DB a free break on the ball.
- **Depth discipline:** a 12-yard dig run at 9 yards breaks the QB's timing.
- Catch with hands, not the chest; eyes the ball all the way into the tuck.

## 3. Blocking
- **Leverage wins:** low man wins. Pad level below the defender's.
- **Run blocking:** first step short and quick ("power step") toward the
  target, hands inside the defender's chest plate, drive with the legs and
  keep the feet moving.
- **Pass protection:** kick-slide back to set depth, hands inside, stay
  between the rusher and the QB; don't lunge.
- **Holding** is grabbing outside the frame or pulling a defender down —
  hands inside the shoulders keeps you legal.

## 4. Tackling more safely
Youth and school programmes in the US widely teach a **heads-out,
shoulder-led** "rugby-style" tackle: close space, chop the feet, head to
the side of the ball carrier, shoulder contact, wrap and roll. Leading
with the crown of the helmet ("spearing"/targeting) is illegal at most
levels and dangerous. Follow your league's contact-practice limits and
concussion protocol.

## 5. Reading coverage shells
Look at the **safeties** pre-snap, then confirm after the snap:
- **Cover 1:** man coverage, one deep safety in the middle.
- **Cover 2:** two deep safeties splitting the field; corners squat on short
  routes. Weak spots: the seam between the safeties, the hole between corner
  and safety on the sideline.
- **Cover 3:** one deep middle safety, corners bail to deep thirds; four
  underneath zones. Weak spots: flats and curl areas.
- **Cover 4 (quarters):** four deep defenders; stout against verticals,
  softer underneath.
Disguise is constant at higher levels — a two-high shell can rotate to one
high at the snap. Treat pre-snap reads as a hypothesis.

## 6. Down and distance
- 1st and 10: balanced; aim for 4+ yards.
- 2nd and short: shot play window — the defence has to respect the run.
- 3rd and long: passing down; screens, crossers and pressure-beaters.
- 4th down: decide by field position and distance; analytics has pushed
  coaches to go for it more often than tradition suggested.

## Gotchas
- Linemen standing up out of the stance (lose leverage immediately).
- Receivers slowing down to "set up" the break — a visible tell.
- QBs locking onto one receiver instead of reading the defender the
  play is built to read.
