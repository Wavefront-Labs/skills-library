# Cricket — batting basics, bowling actions and field settings

**Goal:** survive and score as a batter, bowl a legal, repeatable length,
and understand why the captain puts fielders where they are in each format.

## 1. Batting
- **Guard:** take middle or middle-and-leg from the umpire so you know
  where your off stump is.
- **Stance:** side-on, feet shoulder-width either side of the crease, weight
  balanced, eyes level, bat tapped behind the back foot.
- **Grip:** both hands together near the top of the handle, the "V"s of
  thumb and forefinger roughly in line between the splice and the outside
  edge. Top hand controls; bottom hand is relaxed.
- **Backlift** toward first or second slip, not straight back.
- **Front-foot defence** to a full ball: step toward the pitch of the ball,
  head over it, bat angled down beside the front pad, soft hands so it drops
  dead.
- **Back-foot defence** to a short-of-length ball: step back and across,
  get tall, play under the eyes.
- **Rule of thumb:** play straight (bat vertical) to straight balls; cross-bat
  shots (pull, cut, sweep) only to short or wide balls.
- **Leave** well outside off stump early in an innings — knowing where off
  stump is matters more than any shot.

## 2. Bowling
- **Legal action:** the bowling arm must not straighten by more than 15°
  from the point it reaches horizontal until release (the ICC regulation;
  check your level's rules). Overstepping the popping crease with the front
  foot is a **no-ball**.
- **Run-up:** consistent and measured — mark it in strides and footprints.
- **Pace bowling:** side-on or front-on action, but don't mix (hips and
  shoulders misaligned — "mixed action" — is a known back-injury risk for
  young fast bowlers; follow your board's workload guidelines).
  - **Seam:** upright seam, ball hits the seam and deviates.
  - **Swing:** seam angled toward slips (outswing for a right-hander) or fine
    leg (inswing); a shiny side and a full length help.
- **Spin:** off-spin turns from off to leg for a right-hander (fingers),
  leg-spin from leg to off (wrist). Flight and dip beat batters as much as
  turn.
- **Line and length:** the "good length" sits roughly where the batter is
  unsure whether to go forward or back; on most pitches, a line around off
  stump. Track where your balls pitch with cones in practice.

## 3. Fielding and field settings
- **Attack vs defence:** slips, gully and short legs take catches; deep
  fielders save boundaries.
- **New ball / Test cricket:** more slips, a gully, fewer boundary riders.
- **Spin to a right-hander (off-spin):** slip, short leg or leg slip, plus
  midwicket and long-on for the slog.
- **White-ball formats:** fielding restrictions apply in powerplays
  (limited fielders outside the 30-yard circle); the exact numbers differ
  for T20 and ODI and change occasionally — check the playing conditions.
- Bowl **to your field**: an off-side field with a leg-side ball wastes
  both.

## 4. LBW in one paragraph
Out leg before wicket if the ball would have hit the stumps, didn't pitch
outside leg stump, and hit the pad in line with the stumps — or outside the
line of off stump only if the batter offered no shot. (MCC Law 36 has the
full detail.)

## Gotchas
- Batting with a closed face and playing across the line early.
- Bowlers bowling too many overs too young — follow age workload limits.
- Fielders drifting out of position between balls; captains should check
  every over.
