# Baseball and softball — hitting, throwing and fielding fundamentals

**Goal:** make hard contact, throw accurately without hurting your arm, and
make the routine play every time. Baseball and fastpitch softball share
most mechanics; the softball pitch (underhand windmill) and shorter bases
are the big differences.

## 1. Hitting
- **Stance:** feet a little wider than shoulders, knees soft, weight on the
  balls of the feet, hands near the back shoulder, head still with both eyes
  on the pitcher.
- **Grip:** knuckles that knock on a door roughly lined up; grip in the
  fingers, not the palm.
- **Load:** shift weight slightly onto the back leg and move the hands back
  as the pitcher delivers.
- **Stride:** short and soft, toward the pitcher, landing on a closed front
  foot. Over-striding drops the head.
- **Swing:** hips start the rotation, hands stay inside the ball, barrel
  enters the zone early and stays on the pitch's plane. Finish high.
- **Contact point:** out in front of the plate for inside pitches, deeper
  for outside pitches (go the other way with them).
- **Approach:** have a plan per count — hunt a pitch in a zone when ahead,
  protect the plate with two strikes.

## 2. Throwing and arm care
- **Grip** across the four seams for a straight, carrying throw.
- Turn the glove-side shoulder to the target, step toward it, arm comes up
  into a "high-L" or similar position, then rotate hips and shoulders and
  finish with the throwing hand outside the glove-side knee.
- **Arm care:** a progressive throwing warm-up every day, no throwing
  through pain, rest days. For youth baseball, follow pitch-count and rest
  rules (e.g. MLB/USA Baseball **Pitch Smart** guidelines, or your league's
  rules) and avoid pitching year-round. Pitchers shouldn't also catch in the
  same game (common league rule).
- **Fastpitch windmill:** the underhand motion is gentler on the shoulder
  but overuse injuries still occur — workload limits still matter.

## 3. Fielding
**Ground balls:**
- Ready position as the pitch is delivered; first step toward the ball.
- Approach slightly around it to get in line, field it out in front with
  the glove open to the ball, bare hand on top (alligator), wide base,
  butt down.
- Funnel to the belly, crow-hop or shuffle, throw.
**Fly balls:** turn and run to the spot with the glove down, then catch
above the head on the throwing-hand side. Call it loudly ("I got it!");
the centre fielder has priority in the outfield, outfielders over infielders.

## 4. Cutoffs and relays
On a ball to the outfield, an infielder lines up between the fielder and
the target base; the outfielder throws to the cutoff's chest at a height
they can catch. The catcher (or another player) shouts "cut" or "let it go".
Throwing to the right base matters more than arm strength.

## 5. Base-running rules worth knowing
- **Force out:** a runner forced to advance can be put out by touching the
  base; otherwise they must be tagged.
- **Tag up:** on a caught fly ball, runners must retouch their base after
  the catch before advancing.
- **Infield fly** (fewer than 2 outs, runners on 1st and 2nd or bases
  loaded): the batter is out on an infield pop-up, so defenders can't drop
  it for a double play. Running on contact with two outs.
- Rules vary by league and age (leading off, stealing, dropped third strike) — read your league's book.

## Gotchas
- Pulling the head on the swing ("peeking") and over-striding.
- Throwing from the arm only with no step toward the target.
- Fielding grounders back on the heels.
