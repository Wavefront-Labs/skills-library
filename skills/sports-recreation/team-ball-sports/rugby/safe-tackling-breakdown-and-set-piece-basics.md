# Rugby — safe tackling, the breakdown and set-piece basics

**Goal:** tackle and be tackled without injury, win or slow the ball at the
breakdown, and know your job at scrum and lineout. Union and league share
the tackle; the breakdown and set pieces differ sharply (noted below).

## 1. Tackling safely
Tackle safety is the first thing to coach and the last to stop drilling.
- **Approach:** get square and close the space quickly, then shorten your
  steps ("chop") as you arrive so you stay balanced.
- **Height:** aim low — the hips to the thighs. Many unions now set a
  maximum legal tackle height at the waist for community rugby (e.g. the
  RFU since 2023); check your union's current law trial. High tackles that
  hit the head are sanctioned and are the main concussion route.
- **Head placement:** head **to the side** of the ball carrier, cheek to
  their hip or thigh ("cheek to cheek"), eyes open and up, chin off the
  chest. Never put your head in front of the carrier's legs — that is how
  necks and heads get hit by knees.
- **Contact:** drive the shoulder in, wrap both arms tightly, keep driving
  the legs, roll to land on top.
- **Being tackled:** carry the ball in two hands into contact, lower your
  height, take contact on the shoulder, and place the ball back toward your
  team on the ground.
- **Concussion:** "if in doubt, sit them out." Any player with suspected
  concussion leaves the field and follows a graduated return-to-play
  protocol (World Rugby and national unions publish these).

## 2. The breakdown (union)
- **Tackler** must release the carrier and roll away; the carrier may
  place or pass the ball immediately.
- **Jackal:** the first defender arriving, on their feet, may try to lift
  the ball before a ruck forms. Must stay supporting their own weight.
- **Ruck** forms when at least one player from each side is on their feet
  in contact over the ball. Now no hands — win it by driving over.
- **Clear-out:** support players arrive low, through the gate (from your
  own side, behind the hindmost foot), and drive defenders beyond the ball.
- **Offside line** for others is the hindmost foot of the ruck.
- **League instead:** no contest — the tackled player gets up and plays
  the ball with a foot; the defence retreats 10 m; six tackles per set.

## 3. Scrum (union)
Eight per side. The referee's sequence is **"Crouch, bind, set"**.
Front row: flat backs, hips below shoulders, feet back, bind on the
opponent's jersey not the arm. Collapsing or standing up is penalised and
dangerous. Uncontested scrums are used if trained front rowers aren't
available. League scrums are uncontested restarts.

## 4. Lineout (union)
- Ball thrown straight down the middle between two lines a metre apart.
- **Lifters** grip the jumper at thigh/shorts and lower them safely to the
  ground; never let go mid-air.
- Use simple codes for the target jumper (front, middle, back).

## 5. Passing and phase shape
- **Spin pass:** hands out toward the target, ball across the body, follow
  through to the receiver's chest.
- **Receiving:** hands up early with fingers spread, running onto the ball.
- **Phase play:** go forward, support the carrier, recycle quickly, attack
  the slow defence. A "pod" shape of forwards with backs outside is common.
- **Kicking:** box kicks from the base, grubbers through rushing lines,
  touch-finders to relieve pressure.

## Gotchas
- Leading with the head or dropping the head into the tackle.
- Arriving at the ruck from the side (penalty) or off your feet.
- Forward passes: the pass must not travel forward relative to the
  passer's hands (referees judge by hands, not by where the ball lands).
