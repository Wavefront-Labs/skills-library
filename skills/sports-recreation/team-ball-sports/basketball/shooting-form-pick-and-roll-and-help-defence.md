# Basketball — shooting form, pick-and-roll and help defence

**Goal:** a shot that holds up under fatigue, an offence built on the most
common action in the sport, and a defence that helps without giving up
open threes.

## 1. Shooting form (build it close, then move out)
A common teaching cue is **BEEF** — Balance, Eyes, Elbow, Follow-through:
- **Balance:** feet roughly shoulder-width, shooting-side foot slightly
  ahead, toes toward the rim (many shooters turn the feet a little — fine
  if consistent).
- **Eyes:** pick a target (front or back of the rim, or "just over the
  front") and keep it there through the release.
- **Elbow:** under the ball, wrist cocked back, ball on the finger pads not
  the palm. The guide hand sits on the side and does nothing.
- **Follow-through:** snap the wrist, hold the finish ("hand in the
  cookie jar") until the ball lands.
- Power comes from the **legs**; a shot that falls short late in games is
  usually tired legs, not the arm.
- **Progression:** form shots one-handed 1 m from the rim, then free-throw
  range, then add a catch, then a dribble, then movement. Only extend range
  when the miss pattern is short/long rather than left/right.

## 2. Ball-handling minimums
Eyes up; dribble with the fingertips; protect the ball with the off arm
and body; low dribble in traffic, high on open floor. Both hands — a
defender will force you to your weak side.

## 3. The pick-and-roll (ball screen)
The handler uses a teammate's screen to get a step on their defender.
- **Screener:** set a stationary, wide, legal screen (moving screens are
  fouls). Angle it so the handler goes toward the space you want.
- **Handler:** set up the defender by moving away first, then come **off
  the screen shoulder-to-shoulder** so no defender can squeeze through.
- **Reads** depend on how the defence plays it:
  - **Drop** (screener's defender sits back near the paint): pull-up
    jumper or a floater in the gap.
  - **Hedge / show** (screener's defender steps out high): pass to the
    rolling screener.
  - **Switch:** attack the mismatch — big on small inside, small on big off
    the dribble.
  - **Blitz / trap:** quick pass to the screener short-rolling into the
    middle, who plays 4-on-3.
- The other three players space to the corners and wing so help defenders
  have to leave a shooter.

## 4. Man-to-man help defence
- **Ball-you-man:** see the ball and your player at all times — stand in the
  triangle between them.
- **Help side:** when your player is two or more passes away, sink toward the
  paint to stop drives; one pass away, "deny" the passing lane.
- **Rotate:** if a teammate gets beaten, the nearest helper stops the ball
  and everyone rotates down one; the last open player is the furthest from
  the ball.
- **Closeout:** sprint the first two-thirds, then chop the feet with high
  hands; don't fly past — a shooter driving past you is worse than a
  contested shot.

## 5. Zone basics
A **2-3 zone** guards areas, not players: two up top, three across the
baseline. It protects the paint and tempts outside shots. Beat it with
ball movement, a player in the high post (free-throw line), skip passes
and offensive rebounding.

## Gotchas
- Fixing shooting form during games — change it in practice only.
- Screener slips too early or sets a moving screen.
- Over-helping off a corner shooter (the most efficient spot for threes in
  most leagues; FIBA and NBA line distances differ).
- Ball-watching on defence and losing your man on a back-cut.
