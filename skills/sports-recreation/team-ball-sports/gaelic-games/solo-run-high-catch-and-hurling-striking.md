# Gaelic games — solo run, high catch and hurling striking

**Goal:** carry, pass and score legally in Gaelic football, and strike and
control the sliotar in hurling/camogie. Both use H-shaped goals: over the
bar = **1 point**, under the bar = **goal (3 points)**. Fifteen a side.
GAA rules are revised frequently — recent football rule changes (2025
experimental rules, including a 2-point arc in football) mean you should
check the current Official Guide before relying on detail.

## 1. Gaelic football: carrying
- You may take up to **four steps** holding the ball, then must play it.
- **Solo (toe-tap):** drop the ball onto the foot and kick it back to your
  hands while running. Repeat as often as you like.
- **Bounce:** allowed, but **not twice in a row** — you must solo between
  bounces.
- **Hand pass:** strike the ball off the palm of one hand with the fist or
  open hand of the other. Throwing the ball is a foul. A **fist pass**
  is also legal and useful for scores (a fisted point).
- **Pick-up:** the ball can't be picked straight off the ground with the
  hands in senior adult football — lift it with the foot (toe-lift) into the
  hands. (Ladies' football and some underage grades differ.)

## 2. The high catch and mark
- **High catch:** jump off one foot, drive the knee up for protection and
  height, catch with hands in a "W" behind the ball, fingers spread, and
  pull it into the chest.
- **Mark:** a clean catch from a kick-out past the 45 m line (and in some
  recent rules from other kicks) earns a free kick. The rules on which kicks
  qualify have changed several times — check current rules.

## 3. Kicking points
- **Drop punt** is the standard kick: ball dropped vertically from hands to
  boot, struck with the laces, follow-through toward the target.
- Approach on a slight curve, plant the standing foot beside the ball's
  drop line, keep the head over it.
- **Free kicks:** off the ground or from the hands. Pick a target behind
  the posts and keep a routine.
- The "**hook**" or "**outside of the boot**" kick curls the ball from tight
  angles.

## 4. Hurling and camogie: the hurley and sliotar
- **Grip:** dominant hand on top near the handle end, other hand below
  about a third of the way down. Many players hold "left over right" or
  vice versa; keep the bas (blade) facing the ball.
- **Helmets with face guards are mandatory** at all levels.
- **Roll lift:** press the bas on the moving ball and roll it up into the
  hand.
- **Jab lift:** for a moving ball, push the bas under the ball and pop it
  up — the ball can't be picked up by hand from the ground.
- **Carrying:** the ball can be held for up to four steps, but may be
  caught in the hand only twice in a possession; otherwise **solo** it on
  the bas while running.
- **Striking from the hand:** toss the ball up, step in, swing through
  with wrists rolling; for distance, a full swing from the shoulder.
- **Ground strike:** keep the hands apart, hit along the ground first
  time — fast but low control.
- **Catching:** catch overhead with the hand protected behind the bas
  if possible.

## 5. Tactics and scoring
Points are the currency; percentage shooting matters. In football, short
kick-outs to retain possession versus long contested kick-outs is a key
tactical choice. In hurling, puck-outs follow the same logic.

## Gotchas
- Double bounce in football (common beginner foul).
- Lifting the ball off the ground with the hand in hurling.
- Playing hurling or camogie without a helmet in training — never.

## Verify
- [ ] Solo run 30 m at speed without losing the ball.
- [ ] Football: 6 of 10 points from 25 m, central.
- [ ] Hurling: clean jab lift and roll lift 8 of 10.
- [ ] Players can explain the steps, bounce and pick-up rules for their code.
