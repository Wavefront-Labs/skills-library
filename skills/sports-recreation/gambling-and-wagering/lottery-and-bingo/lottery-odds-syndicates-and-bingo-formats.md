# Lottery and bingo — odds, syndicates and formats

**Goal:** enjoy low-stakes social games knowing the real odds, run a
syndicate that won't end friendships, and know how bingo formats work.
Lotteries return the smallest share of stakes of common gambling forms;
treat a ticket as the price of a daydream, not an investment.

## 1. Lottery odds from first principles
Pick-k-from-n lotteries: the number of possible tickets is
`C(n, k) = n! / (k! (n−k)!)`.
- 6 from 49: C(49,6) = **13,983,816** → 1 in ~14 million.
- 6 from 59 (UK Lotto since 2015): C(59,6) = **45,057,474**.
- Two-drum games multiply: Powerball = C(69,5) × 26 = **292,201,338**.
- EuroMillions = C(50,5) × C(12,2) = **139,838,160**.
Rule changes alter these — always check the operator's current odds table.
Each draw is independent: past numbers, "hot" or "due" numbers don't matter.

## 2. What you get back
- Many national lotteries return roughly **~50%** of sales as prizes (the
  rest funds good causes/government and operating costs); scratch cards
  often return more (~60–70%+). Published per game by operators. **These are
  typical ranges, not a specific game's figure.**
- Jackpot "rollovers" raise the jackpot but more tickets are sold, increasing
  the chance of **sharing**.
- **Choosing unpopular numbers** doesn't change your odds of winning but
  reduces how often you share: avoid birthdays (1–31), patterns, and
  sequences like 1-2-3-4-5-6 (thousands of people play it).
- Lump sum vs annuity (US): advertised jackpots are often the annuity
  total; the cash option is much smaller, then taxed.

## 3. Running a syndicate (pool)
- **Written agreement** signed by all: members, stake per draw, which games
  and numbers, how prizes split (equal shares per stake), what happens if
  someone misses a payment, who holds tickets and claims prizes.
- Keep a **payment log** and photograph/share each ticket before the draw.
- Organiser buys tickets from a recorded source (online account or
  photographed receipts). Many operators provide official syndicate tools.
- Decide upfront: small wins reinvested or paid out? Leaving members?
- Syndicates improve chances per draw by buying more lines but don't change
  expected value per pound/dollar.

## 4. Claiming
- **Sign the back of paper tickets** where the operator advises it; check
  tickets — unclaimed prizes are common. Claim deadlines (often 180 days–1 year)
  apply.
- Large wins: get legal/financial advice before going public, where
  anonymity rules allow.

## 5. Bingo formats
- **90-ball (UK/Europe/Australia):** tickets with 3 rows × 9 columns,
  15 numbers each. Prizes for **one line**, **two lines** and **full house**.
  Often sold in strips of 6 covering all 90 numbers.
- **75-ball (US/Canada):** 5×5 card under B-I-N-G-O, free centre square;
  games have **patterns** (line, four corners, blackout/coverall, letter shapes).
- Callers use traditional calls in the UK ("two little ducks, 22").
- Online bingo adds auto-daub; land halls need attention and dabbers.
- Call "Bingo/House!" immediately on completion; a claim after the next
  number is often void.

## 6. Raffles
- Fundraising raffles are regulated (permits, ticket limits, prize rules) in
  many places; check local rules before running one.
- Draw publicly, record ticket stubs, publish winners.

## Responsible play
Set a monthly limit; avoid "subscriptions" you've forgotten; seek
help if spending escalates.

## Gotchas
- Believing in due numbers; buying more after losses.
- Syndicate run on trust with no record.
- **Odds above valid for the stated matrices; operators change rules.**
