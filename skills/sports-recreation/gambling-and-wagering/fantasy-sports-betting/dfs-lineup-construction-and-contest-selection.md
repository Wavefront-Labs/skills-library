# Daily fantasy sports — lineups and contest selection

**Goal:** build lineups suited to the contest type and avoid contests where
the rake and opposition make winning unrealistic. DFS (DraftKings,
FanDuel-style) is legally treated as a skill game in some jurisdictions
and gambling in others — **check local legality and age limits**.

## 1. How the economy works
- You pick players under a **salary cap**; scoring follows the site's
  rules (they differ between sites — read them; e.g. full vs half PPR in NFL).
- The site takes a **rake** (often ~10–15% of entry fees). To profit you
  must beat the field by more than the rake.
- A small group of high-volume professionals with optimisers wins a large
  share of prize money in big contests. Recreational players benefit from
  **beginner-only** and smaller-field contests.

## 2. Projections and value
- Start with **projections** (median points) and add a sense of range:
  **floor** (bad outcome) and **ceiling** (great outcome).
- **Value = projected points per $1,000 salary.** E.g. 3x value means
  3 points per $1k — a common rough baseline target in NFL DFS for cash
  lineups. **Treat specific multipliers as sport- and site-dependent.**
- Key inputs: role/usage (minutes, snaps, targets), opponent defence, pace,
  Vegas totals/spreads (high implied team totals = more fantasy points),
  injuries and late news.

## 3. Cash games vs GPPs
- **Cash games** (50/50s, double-ups, head-to-heads): roughly half the field
  gets paid. Build for a **high floor**: high-usage players, safe value,
  popular chalk is fine.
- **GPPs (tournaments):** top-heavy payouts. Build for **ceiling** and
  **uniqueness**: you need what others don't have if it hits.

## 4. Stacking and correlation (GPP)
- **Stacks:** pair players whose scores rise together, e.g. NFL quarterback +
  1–2 of his receivers; MLB 4–5 consecutive hitters in a lineup; a
  **bring-back** (one opposing player) captures shoot-out games.
- Avoid negative correlation (e.g. your RB vs the defence you rostered).

## 5. Ownership and leverage
- **Projected ownership** estimates how many entries roster a player.
- **Leverage:** if a chalk player at 40% ownership fails, every lineup
  without him gains; in GPPs, fading some chalk or pairing it with low-owned
  pieces differentiates you.
- Don't be contrarian for its own sake — a 1%-owned player is usually low-
  owned for a reason.

## 6. Contest selection
- Prefer: smaller fields, beginner contests, single-entry or
  limited-entry (3-max) contests, lower rake.
- Avoid: high-stakes head-to-heads against unknown opponents, massive
  150-max GPPs where pros enter full portfolios.
- Track ROI by contest type; cut the types you lose in.

## 7. Process on slate day
1. Read news and confirmed lineups/injuries.
2. Update projections; identify value plays from price errors.
3. Build cash lineup, then GPP lineups.
4. Use **late swap** to replace inactive players before their game locks.

## 8. Pick'em / props-style apps
Player-prop "pick'em" apps price combinations with built-in margin; the
math is closer to parlay betting — see odds-implied-probability-and-value-betting.

## Responsible play
Fixed weekly budget, typically a small slice per slate; DFS is high-variance.
Use site deposit limits and self-exclusion if needed.

## Gotchas
- Setting lineups early and missing scratches.
- Using a GPP lineup in cash games and vice versa.
- Ignoring site-specific scoring differences.
