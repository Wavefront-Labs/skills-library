# Vinyl and media: identifying pressings and grading records

**Goal:** know exactly which edition you're holding (that's where the value is),
grade it honestly, and keep it playing well.

## 1. Identify the pressing
The same album can exist in dozens of pressings worth from pennies to thousands. Use:
- **Runout / dead wax** (smooth area by the label): hand-etched or stamped **matrix
  numbers**, stamper codes, mastering engineer initials, plant marks. Read both sides
  under a raking light — tilt the record.
- **Label variations:** colour, logo, rim text, address, catalogue-number format,
  "Made in…" country.
- **Sleeve details:** printer credits, laminate (flipback) vs card, barcode presence
  (barcodes generally mean late 1970s onward), price codes, inserts and posters.
- Enter matrix strings in **Discogs** search (put them in quotes) and compare with each
  release's "Barcode and Other Identifiers" section. Discogs user notes often name the
  true first press points.
Reissues frequently copy the original label art — the runout usually gives them away.

## 2. Grading (Goldmine / Record Collector conventions)
Grade **disc and sleeve separately**: e.g. "VG+/VG".
- **M (Mint):** sealed or perfect, unplayed. Rarely used for used stock.
- **NM (Near Mint):** looks unplayed, no marks, plays perfectly.
- **VG+:** light signs of handling, faint scuffs that don't affect play.
- **VG:** visible light scratches, some surface noise in quiet passages, still enjoyable.
- **G / G+:** heavy wear, constant noise but plays through without skipping.
- **F/P:** skips, cracks, warps — filler only.
Play-grade anything valuable: visual grade can mislead both ways (shiny records can
be groove-worn; scuffy ones can play clean). Note **warps** (flat on the turntable?),
**dish**, off-centre holes, seam splits, ring wear, writing and stickers on the sleeve.
Price falls steeply below VG+ for most titles.

## 3. Crate digging
- Flip fast; pull anything with an interesting label, era, or genre you know.
- Check every pulled record out of its sleeve under good light — the wrong disc in a
  sleeve is common.
- Smell the sleeve: mildew means damp storage, check for mould on the vinyl.
- Use the Discogs app's barcode/catalogue lookup on your phone for a quick value
  range ("median sold" is better than "lowest listed").

## 4. Cleaning
- Dry: carbon-fibre brush before each play.
- Wet: a record-cleaning solution (distilled water plus a little surfactant;
  isopropyl in modest concentration is debated — avoid high concentrations on
  shellac 78s entirely), microfibre or vacuum machine, or an ultrasonic cleaner.
  Never wet the label. Dry fully before re-sleeving.
- Replace paper inners with **anti-static poly-lined inners**; keep original printed
  inners separately — they're part of value.

## 5. Storage
- **Upright**, snug but not crammed, never stacked flat (warps, ring wear).
- Away from heat sources and sunlight; stable cool room.
- Outer polythene sleeves protect jackets; open-top so the record isn't sealed against
  the shrink.
- CDs: replace cracked jewel cases, keep discs out of sun (disc rot is mostly
  manufacturing defects but heat accelerates it). Tapes and VHS: cool, dry, away
  from magnets and speakers.

## Gotchas
- "First pressing" claims on marketplaces are often wrong — verify by matrix yourself.
- Sealed copies can be resealed reissues; check for reseal signs (new shrink, no seam).
- Grading inflation online: buy from sellers who play-grade and accept returns.

## Verify
- [ ] Runout matrix strings recorded for each valuable record and matched to a Discogs release.
- [ ] Disc and sleeve graded separately, play-tested if valuable.
- [ ] Records stored upright in anti-static inners and outer sleeves.
- [ ] Valuations use sold history, not lowest asking price.
