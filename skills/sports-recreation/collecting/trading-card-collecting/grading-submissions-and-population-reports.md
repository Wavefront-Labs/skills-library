# Trading cards: grading submissions and population reports

**Goal:** submit only cards whose likely grade makes the fee worthwhile, ship them
safely, and value cards using population data and real sold prices.

## 1. Pre-grade before you pay
Graders (PSA, BGS/Beckett, CGC, SGC) score four things. Check each under a bright
light with a loupe, card in a penny sleeve:
- **Centering:** measure border widths front and back. A PSA 10 typically needs
  around 55/45–60/40 front (back tolerance looser); check each grader's current
  published standards — they differ and change.
- **Corners:** any whitening or softness under magnification usually rules out a 10.
- **Edges:** chipping (very visible on dark-bordered cards), rough cuts.
- **Surface:** print lines, scratches, dimples, fingerprints, staining. Tilt the card
  against a light to catch scratches on holo/chrome surfaces.
One noticeable flaw typically caps a card at 8–9. Be harsh — most collectors over-grade
their own cards.

## 2. Is it worth grading?
Compare: (sold price at the likely grade) − (sold price raw) vs (grading fee + shipping
both ways + insurance + time). Fees scale with declared value and turnaround tier;
check the grader's current schedule. Many modern base cards are worth *less* graded
at PSA 8 than the fee. Grade when:
- The card is a key rookie, low-numbered parallel, or vintage star, **and**
- You're confident of a high grade, or it's vintage where even mid grades carry a premium.

## 3. Population reports
Each grader publishes how many copies of each card they've graded at each grade
("pop report"). Use it to:
- Gauge scarcity: a PSA 10 with pop 15 behaves differently from one with pop 15,000.
- Spot **pop growth** — a rising pop dilutes prices; modern cards can see pops
  explode after a player breaks out.
- Compute gem rate (10s ÷ total graded) to estimate your odds.
Caveat: pops include resubmissions ("crack-outs") so they overstate unique copies.

## 4. Packing a submission
- Card in a **penny sleeve** → **semi-rigid card saver** (PSA prefers Card Saver I)
  — not screw-downs or toploaders unless the grader allows them.
- Stack savers, sandwich between cardboard, band loosely (rubber band on the
  cardboard, not the cards), bubble wrap, sturdy box. Tracked and insured.
- Fill the online form carefully: year, set, card number, player, variation.
  Wrong variety = delays or wrong label.
- Photograph front and back of every card before sending.

## 5. Pricing from comps
Asking prices are meaningless. Use **sold** listings: eBay sold filter, aggregators
that show best-offer accepted prices (e.g. 130point), auction house archives, and
price-tracking sites. Compare the same card, same grader, same grade, recent window.
PSA, BGS and SGC 10s sell at different prices for the same card. Watch for shill
bidding on thin markets.

## 6. Market timing
Sports cards move with player performance, injuries, playoff runs and hall-of-fame
announcements; Pokémon/TCG with set releases, reprints and anniversaries. Selling into
hype and buying in the off-season is the classic pattern — not a guarantee.

## Gotchas
- Trimmed and recoloured vintage cards exist; graders reject as "altered" — buy
  high-value raw vintage cautiously.
- Grading backlogs can run months; tier choice affects both cost and wait.
- Fake slabs and fake labels: verify the cert number on the grader's site.

## Verify
- [ ] Every submitted card pre-graded on all four criteria with notes.
- [ ] Expected value at likely grade exceeds total cost with margin.
- [ ] Pop report and gem rate checked for each submission.
- [ ] Packing in sleeves + semi-rigids, photographed, insured.
- [ ] Valuations cite at least three recent sold comps.
