# Cataloguing, storage and insurance for a collection

**Goal:** know exactly what you own, keep it from deteriorating, be able to prove it
after a loss, and leave it in a state someone else could sell or inherit.

## 1. Catalogue first, buy second
A collection without a catalogue is a pile. Use a spreadsheet (or a hobby app that
exports to CSV — avoid apps that lock your data in). One row per item, columns:
- **Your ID** — permanent, never reused (e.g. `C-0001`). Write it on the holder or
  sleeve, never on the object itself.
- **What it is** in the hobby's standard terms, plus the standard catalogue number
  (Scott/Stanley Gibbons for stamps, KM/Krause for coins, Discogs release ID for records).
- **Condition / grade** as you judged it, and the date you judged it.
- **Acquired:** date, source, price paid (including fees and postage), receipt reference.
- **Provenance** notes and certificate numbers (grading slab, authentication cert).
- **Location** — which box, album page or shelf.
- **Current estimated value** and the date and basis of that estimate.
Photograph every item front and back with the ID visible; name image files by ID.
Keep the spreadsheet and photos in two places, one off-site/cloud.

## 2. Storage and conservation basics
The enemies are light, humidity swings, heat, pests, acidic materials and handling.
- **Light:** UV and daylight fade inks, paper and dyes irreversibly. Display rotates;
  storage is dark. Use UV-filtering glazing for anything framed.
- **Humidity:** aim for roughly 40–55% relative humidity and stable temperature
  (~18–22 °C). Avoid lofts, garages, cellars and exterior walls. A cheap digital
  hygrometer in each storage area is the minimum. Above ~65% RH mould and foxing
  start; very dry conditions crack wood and lacquer.
- **Materials:** archival, acid-free, lignin-free boxes and folders; inert plastic
  sleeves (polyester/Mylar, polypropylene, polyethylene). **Avoid PVC** — soft "vinyl"
  flips and pages off-gas and damage coins, stamps and photos (green slime on coins is
  the classic sign).
- **Handling:** clean dry hands or nitrile gloves; hold by edges; never use tape,
  rubber bands, paper clips or ballpoint near items.
- **Pests:** silverfish, carpet beetles, mice. Inspect boxes twice a year.

## 3. Insurance
Standard home contents policies usually cap single items of "valuables" or
collections at a low limit — often only a few thousand in local currency — and may
exclude accidental damage or items in transit (check your policy wording; limits
vary widely by insurer and country). Options:
1. **Schedule** high-value items on your home policy (each listed with value).
2. A **specialist collectibles policy** — usually cheaper per unit of value, covers
   breakage and shows, sometimes agreed-value.
Know the difference between *agreed value* (pays the listed figure) and *market/
actual cash value* (pays what it's judged worth at loss). Re-value every 2–3 years
or after a market move; keep dated valuation evidence (auction results, dealer
appraisals). The catalogue and photos are your claim file.

## 4. Display
Rotate items so none is in light permanently; use museum putty or proper mounts,
not adhesives; keep displays away from radiators, kitchens and sunny windows.

## 5. Disposal and succession
Write a one-page note for whoever deals with the collection after you: where the
catalogue lives, the five most valuable items, which dealers or auction houses suit
it, and roughly what it's worth. Heirs routinely sell collections for a fraction of
value because nobody knew what was in the boxes. When you sell, sell by item for
key pieces and in lots for common material; auction premiums and fees typically take
a substantial cut — confirm the seller's commission before consigning.

## Gotchas
- PVC holders and old "magnetic" photo albums actively damage items.
- Cleaning coins, polishing silver or "restoring" paper usually *destroys* value.
- An uninsured collection in a home policy's general contents is often under-covered.

## Verify
- [ ] Every item has a permanent ID, a photo and a location in the catalogue.
- [ ] Catalogue and photos backed up off-site.
- [ ] Storage rooms have a hygrometer reading roughly 40–55% RH.
- [ ] No PVC, tape or acidic paper touching items.
- [ ] Policy wording checked for single-item limit; valuables scheduled or specialist cover in place.
- [ ] A succession note exists.
