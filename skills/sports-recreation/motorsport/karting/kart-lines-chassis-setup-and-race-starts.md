# Karting — kart lines, chassis setup and race starts

**Goal:** drive a kart the way it wants to be driven, make a few simple
setup changes with purpose, and survive and gain at the start. Karting is
the entry point to circuit racing, from rental "arrive and drive" to owner
classes (e.g. Rotax, IAME X30, KZ shifter karts — classes vary by country).

## 1. How karts differ
- **No differential:** the rear axle is solid, so both rear wheels turn at
  the same speed. To corner, the kart's chassis flexes and the **inside
  rear wheel lifts** slightly off the ground. Setup and steering inputs
  exist mostly to manage that.
- **No suspension** and very low weight — the chassis is the spring.
- **Direct steering:** small, smooth inputs. Sawing at the wheel scrubs
  speed.
- **Lines:** generally a bit straighter and earlier to the throttle than
  cars; many karting corners reward a **V-line** (brake straight, turn
  sharply at the slowest point, then straighten and accelerate) because a
  kart loses momentum when it scrubs. Lighter, low-power karts reward
  rolling more speed — practise both and use lap times to choose.

## 2. Driving technique
- **Braking** is usually only on the rear axle (owner karts; many shifter
  karts also have front brakes). Brake in a straight line; locking the
  rears spins the kart.
- **Throttle:** in direct-drive karts, keep momentum — lifting is costly
  because the engine has no gears to help recover.
- **Kerbs:** riding high kerbs can unload the wheels and lift the kart;
  use flat kerbs, avoid sausage kerbs.
- **Eyes up:** look through the corner to the exit.
- **Body:** sit still; leaning shifts weight and can help a kart that won't
  turn, but it's a crutch.

## 3. Basic chassis setup (owner karts)
Change **one thing at a time** and log lap times.
- **Tyre pressures:** the first tuning lever. Higher pressure = more grip
  sooner but can overheat; lower = slower to warm. Check hot pressures
  after a session; manufacturers publish starting ranges.
- **Rear track width:** wider rear = less rear grip (the kart turns/slides
  more); narrower = more rear grip.
- **Front track width** and **caster/camber** change how much the inside
  rear lifts — follow the chassis maker's guide.
- **Seat position:** affects weight distribution dramatically; set it by the
  manufacturer's measurements.
- **Gearing (sprockets):** bigger rear sprocket = more acceleration, lower
  top speed. Gear so you just reach top RPM at the end of the longest
  straight.

## 4. Wet weather
- **Rain tyres** and a wet setup (raise the kart, widen the front, stiffen
  as recommended).
- Drive **off the dry line**, which is rubbered and slippery when wet; look
  for grip on the outside of corners.
- Be very smooth on throttle and brakes.

## 5. Race starts and racecraft
- Most kart races use **rolling starts**: hold formation and pace, accelerate
  at the green/start light. Jumping the start draws penalties.
- **Drafting/slipstream:** a kart close behind gains speed on straights;
  pairs can "push" each other away from the pack.
- **Turn one:** pick a safe line, expect contact from behind, protect the
  inside.
- **Front bumper/nose cone penalties** exist in CIK-FIA and many clubs for
  pushing — don't use the bumper to pass.

## 6. Safety kit
Full-face helmet to the required standard (often Snell K or FIA), karting
suit, gloves, boots, neck brace for juniors, and a **rib protector** — kart
seats bruise ribs badly. Tie long hair back (it can get caught in the axle).

## Gotchas
- Over-steering and scrubbing speed.
- Changing three setup items at once.
- Locking the rears into the first corner.
