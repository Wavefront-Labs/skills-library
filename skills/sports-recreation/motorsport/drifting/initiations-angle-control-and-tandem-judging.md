# Drifting — initiations, angle control and tandem judging

**Goal:** start a slide on purpose, hold it at a chosen angle along a
chosen line, link corners, and understand how judges score. Only on a
closed venue (skid pad, drift day, circuit) — never on public roads.

## 1. The car
- **Rear-wheel drive** with a **limited-slip differential** (LSD), or a
  welded diff for budget builds (welded diffs make the car awkward at
  low-speed road manoeuvres and wear tyres).
- Enough power to spin the rears on the tyres you run; beginners start
  with low-grip or used rear tyres and modest power.
- Hydraulic handbrake and steering-angle kits are later upgrades; a stock
  car with an LSD is enough to learn.
- Basic safety: helmet, seats and belts that hold you, plus whatever the
  event requires (cage, harness at higher grip levels).

## 2. Skid-pad progression
1. **Donuts:** turn the wheel to full lock, use throttle to break the rear
   loose and hold a circle with throttle. Learn to modulate the circle
   size with throttle (more = wider radius as the car slides more, less =
   grip returns).
2. **Countersteer reflex:** let the steering wheel spin back through your
   hands as the rear slides (caster self-steer), catching it rather than
   forcing it.
3. **Figure-eight:** two donuts linked — teaches transitions from one
   direction to the other.
4. Then a single corner, then linked corners.

## 3. Initiations (ways to start the slide)
- **Power-over:** enough throttle mid-corner to break rear grip — needs
  power.
- **Clutch kick:** with the throttle held, quickly depress and release the
  clutch, sending a spike of torque to the rear wheels. The most common
  initiation; hard on drivetrains.
- **Handbrake (e-brake):** pull briefly to lock the rears, clutch in,
  then clutch out with throttle to keep the slide. Good for tight entries
  and corrections.
- **Feint (Scandinavian flick):** steer briefly away from the corner, then
  into it; the weight swing breaks the rear loose. Used for high-speed
  entries and transitions.
- **Braking drift:** trail-brake hard into the corner to lighten the rear.

## 4. Angle and line control
- **Throttle controls angle and radius:** more throttle = more angle and
  the car moves out; less = less angle and the car tightens and grips.
- **Steering** follows the slide (countersteer); you are steering the
  front wheels to point where the car is going.
- **Eyes far ahead** at the next clipping point.
- Hold a steady angle with small throttle inputs; big stabs cause spins
  or straightening.

## 5. Transitions
Lift slightly and let the car's weight swing to the other side, then
throttle again as the steering winds through to the opposite lock. Well-
timed transitions flow; late ones end in a straighten or a spin.

## 6. Judging (competition)
Exact criteria vary by series (Formula Drift, Drift Masters, local
grassroots), but common elements are:
- **Line:** hitting **clipping points** (touch close to a cone or wall
  with the front or rear bumper) and **clipping zones** set by judges.
- **Angle:** high, consistent angle throughout.
- **Style/commitment:** speed, aggression, smoothness of transitions.
- **Tandem:** the **lead** car must run the ideal line at speed; the
  **chase** car is scored on **proximity** while mirroring the leader's
  line and angle. Contact rules and passes are series-specific.

## Gotchas
- Too much power and grip on the first day — learn on low-grip tyres.
- Fighting the wheel instead of letting it self-correct through your hands.
- Ignoring maintenance: drifting kills clutches, tyres, axles and cooling.

## Verify
- [ ] Hold a donut in both directions, varying circle size with throttle.
- [ ] Clean figure-eight transitions five times in a row.
- [ ] Initiate with clutch kick and handbrake on cue.
- [ ] Can explain lead vs chase scoring for your series.
