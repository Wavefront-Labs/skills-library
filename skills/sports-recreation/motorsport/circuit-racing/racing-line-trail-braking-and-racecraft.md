# Circuit racing — racing line, trail braking and racecraft

**Goal:** go faster by being smooth, then race wheel to wheel without
contact. Start with instructed track days (HPDE in the US) before club
racing; a race licence normally needs a medical, a course and a test from
your national sporting authority (FIA member club).

## 1. The racing line
- **Outside – inside – outside:** turn in from the outside edge, clip the
  **apex** on the inside, let the car run out to the outside edge at
  **track-out**. This maximises corner radius and so speed.
- **Late apex:** turn in a bit later and apex later than the geometric
  middle. It sacrifices a little entry speed for a straighter, earlier
  throttle exit — and exit speed carries all the way down the next
  straight. Default for most corners leading onto straights.
- **Corner priority:** the corner before the longest straight matters
  most. In a sequence (esses), compromise the first corner to nail the last.
- Find **reference points** for braking, turn-in, apex and track-out
  (marker boards, kerbs, patches) and use them every lap.

## 2. Braking
- **Threshold braking:** hard initial application to near the limit of
  grip (or ABS activation), in a straight line. Most novices brake too
  early and too gently, then coast.
- **Trail braking:** gradually release the brake as you turn in, so weight
  stays on the front tyres for grip and helps the car rotate. The brake
  comes off progressively as steering goes on — never abruptly.
- **Weight transfer:** braking loads the front (more front grip, less
  rear), accelerating loads the rear. Abrupt inputs upset the balance.
- **Heel-and-toe** (manual gearbox): right foot on the brake, blip the
  throttle with the side of the same foot while downshifting so the revs
  match and the rear doesn't lock.

## 3. Understeer vs oversteer
- **Understeer:** the front slides wide. Cause: too much entry speed or
  throttle too early. Fix: ease the throttle slightly, reduce steering
  (more lock doesn't help).
- **Oversteer:** the rear steps out. Fix: look where you want to go,
  countersteer quickly and smoothly, be gentle with the pedals (lifting
  abruptly in a rear-drive car can make it worse).
- **Slow in, fast out** is still the right instinct for learners.

## 4. Racecraft
- **Overtaking:** the classic pass is a late-braking move on the inside
  into a corner. You need your front wheels alongside the other car's
  (rules vary — commonly at least the front axle at the driver's door) by
  turn-in to be entitled to racing room.
- **Defending:** one change of direction to defend on a straight is the
  usual rule; moving under braking or weaving is penalised.
- **Leave a car's width** to a car alongside at the edge of the track.
- **Exit-speed pass:** take a tighter, slower entry behind a car to get a
  better exit and pass on the straight.
- **Starts:** know the start procedure for your series (rolling or standing),
  and expect chaos into turn one — survive it.

## 5. Flags (common FIA meanings; check local regs)
- **Yellow:** danger — slow, no overtaking. **Double yellow:** be prepared
  to stop.
- **Green:** clear. **Red:** session stopped, slow down and return to pit.
- **Blue:** let a faster (lapping) car through.
- **Black:** come into the pits. **Black/orange (meatball):** mechanical
  problem. **Chequered:** finish.
- **Safety car / full course yellow:** follow the series' speed and
  overtaking rules.

## 6. Learning tools
Data logging (lap timers with GPS) and video show where time is lost —
usually braking points and minimum corner speeds. Compare laps, change one
thing at a time. Tyre pressures change as tyres heat; check hot pressures
after sessions.

## Gotchas
- Charging deeper on the brakes before mastering the line.
- Driving every corner with an early apex.
- Missing flags while focused on a rival.
