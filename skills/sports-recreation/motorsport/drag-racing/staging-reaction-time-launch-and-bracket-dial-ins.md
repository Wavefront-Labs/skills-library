# Drag racing — staging, reaction time, launch and bracket dial-ins

**Goal:** stage consistently, react to the tree without a red light, launch
cleanly, and (in bracket racing) predict your own time precisely. Start at
a sanctioned strip's **test-and-tune** or street night — never on public
roads.

## 1. Before you run
- **Tech inspection:** helmet, seat belts, battery tie-down, no leaks,
  and car-specific requirements that tighten as cars get quicker (e.g. roll
  bar or cage below a certain elapsed time — check the NHRA/IHRA rulebook or
  your strip's rules for the thresholds).
- **Burnout:** heats and cleans the drive tyres. Drive around the **water
  box** (not through it) on street tyres in most cases; slicks do a burnout
  through the water. A short, controlled spin; don't overheat street tyres.

## 2. Staging
- Two pairs of light beams at the start line. **Pre-stage** lights when
  your front tyres break the first beam; **stage** lights when you roll a
  few more inches into the second.
- Creep in slowly — idle forward, brakes covered. Consistent staging means
  consistent times.
- **Deep staging** (rolling further so the pre-stage light goes out)
  shortens the distance to the finish but slows your reaction slightly.
  Not allowed in some classes.
- **Courtesy staging:** both racers pre-stage before either fully stages
  (customary, and required in some classes).

## 3. The Christmas tree and reaction time
- **Sportsman tree:** three ambers counting down at 0.5 s intervals, then
  green. Racers leave on the **last amber**, anticipating the green.
- **Pro tree:** all three ambers flash together, green 0.4 s later.
- **Reaction time (RT):** time from green to your car leaving the stage
  beam. A **perfect light** is .000 (sportsman RT is measured against the
  green, so a good one is around .000–.030). A **negative RT is a red
  light** — you lose unless the other driver also red-lights or crosses a
  boundary line.
- Practise on a practice tree app or box; your car's roll-out (how far it
  moves before the beam clears) affects the timing.

## 4. The launch
- **Manual:** bring revs to a launch RPM, release the clutch quickly but
  progressively to avoid bogging or excessive spin.
- **Automatic:** brake-torque (foot on brake, build revs) only to the point
  the car doesn't creep through the beam.
- **Launch control** where fitted.
- Measure success by the **60-foot time** on the slip: it predicts the rest
  of the run. Too much wheel spin or a bog both hurt it.
- Keep it straight; lift if it gets out of shape.

## 5. Reading the timeslip
Includes RT, 60 ft, 330 ft, 1/8 mile ET and speed, 1000 ft, 1/4 mile
**elapsed time (ET)** and **trap speed**. ET measures performance from leaving
the stage beam; the win is decided by who crosses the finish first (RT + ET
in heads-up racing).

## 6. Bracket racing
- You declare a **dial-in** — your predicted ET. The slower car gets a
  head start equal to the difference in dial-ins (handicap start).
- **Breakout:** running quicker than your dial-in loses (unless both break
  out — then the smaller breakout usually wins).
- Winning combines a good RT with running close to the dial without
  breaking out; drivers often "take stripe" by **lifting or braking** at the
  finish to avoid breaking out, which takes practice.
- Track conditions matter: air density (density altitude), track
  temperature and wind change ETs. Log every run.

## Gotchas
- Inconsistent staging depth from run to run.
- Leaving on the green with a sportsman tree (you'll be late).
- Chasing ET with big burnouts and inconsistent launches in bracket racing.

## Verify
- [ ] 5 runs with RT within a .050 window, no red lights.
- [ ] 60-foot times within about 0.05 s of each other.
- [ ] Log of dial-ins vs ETs with weather noted.
- [ ] Car passes tech without exceptions.
