# Rally — pace notes, recce and loose-surface control

**Goal:** drive a stage you can't see at speed by trusting a precise set
of notes, and keep the car pointed where you want on gravel, snow and
tarmac. Rally is a two-person job; the co-driver is half the result.

## 1. Pace notes
Pace notes describe each corner, its severity and what's between corners.
**Systems vary** (numbers 1–6, or descriptive words; the scale may run
either way). A common modern numeric system:
- **6** = fast, barely a corner; **1** = very slow, hairpin-like. Some
  crews use "hairpin" separately.
- Direction and grade first: "**left 4**", "**right 2**".
- Modifiers: "**tightens**", "**opens**", "**long**", "**don't cut**",
  "**cut**", "**over crest**", "**caution**" or "!" for danger.
- Distances between corners in metres: "**100**", or "**into**" / "**and**"
  when the next corner follows immediately.
- Example call: "*Left 4 tightens 3, 50, right 5 over crest, don't cut,
  150.*"
Use one system consistently in every event; writing style and abbreviations
must be unambiguous to both crew members.

## 2. Recce (reconnaissance)
- Drive the stage at limited speed (strictly enforced; speed limits and
  number of passes are set by regulations) to write notes.
- **First pass:** driver calls corners, co-driver writes. **Second pass:**
  co-driver reads them back and the driver corrects.
- Note hazards: rocks on the inside, ruts, ditches, jumps, surface changes.
- Notes written on dry gravel change once 50 cars have run — cars ahead
  "sweep" loose gravel off the line, and lines get cut in. Adjust with
  "gravel notes" crews where allowed.

## 3. Co-driving on stage
- Read **ahead** of the car — typically one or two calls in advance,
  timed to the driver's rhythm. Too early and they forget; too late and
  they're past it.
- Steady voice, same rhythm every time; louder and sharper for cautions.
- Keep your place in the notes with a finger or highlighted lines.
- Off stage: run **time controls**, timecards and the **roadbook** (tulip
  diagrams for road sections). Arriving early at a time control is
  penalised as much as late in most rules.

## 4. Loose-surface car control
- **Weight transfer is the steering wheel on gravel.** Braking loads the
  front for turn-in; lifting off rotates the car.
- **Left-foot braking:** on front- and four-wheel-drive cars, brake with
  the left foot while keeping the throttle on, to settle or rotate the car
  without losing drive. Learn on a closed area at low speed first.
- **Scandinavian flick (pendulum turn):** turn briefly away from the
  corner, then sharply into it, using the weight swing to get the rear
  sliding so the car is pointed in early. Overused on slow corners it
  wastes time; modern pros use it selectively.
- **Handbrake:** for hairpins in two-wheel-drive cars; in 4WD cars you
  normally dip the clutch (or the car has a hydraulic handbrake disconnecting
  the centre diff).
- **Look where you want to go** and commit — hesitating on gravel kills
  momentum and grip.
- **Tarmac** rally is closer to circuit driving but with notes and no run-off.

## 5. Safety
- The **OK board** (green "OK" sign) is shown to following cars after any
  stop or crash with no injury. The **SOS** (red cross) means medical help
  is needed — the next crews must stop.
- Fire extinguishers, approved seats and harnesses, FHR (head and neck
  restraint) — follow the national authority's technical regs.
- **Spectators:** stay in designated zones; never stand on the outside of
  a corner.

## Gotchas
- Driving beyond the notes ("I saw it was fine") — the next corner may
  not be.
- Co-driver losing their place after a "caution" and freezing.
- Recce speeding — teams get excluded.

## Verify
- [ ] Both crew members can read back each other's notes on a test road.
- [ ] Co-driver times calls 1–2 corners ahead, tested on video.
- [ ] Driver can left-foot brake smoothly at low speed on gravel.
- [ ] OK/SOS boards are in the car and both know the procedure.
