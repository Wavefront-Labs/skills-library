# Road motorcycle racing — body position, trail braking and track-day lines

**Goal:** ride a circuit smoothly with less lean angle at the same speed,
brake hard and progressively, and stay on the bike. Start with track days
in the novice group with instruction; club racing needs a licence from your
national motorcycle federation (usually an approved course).

## 1. Countersteering
Above walking pace, a bike turns by pushing the **inside** bar forward
(push right bar → lean and turn right). Firm, deliberate pushes give quick
turn-in; holding the push maintains lean. Every quick direction change on
track is a countersteer.

## 2. Body position (hanging off)
- **Purpose:** moving your mass to the inside lets the bike lean less at
  the same speed — more tyre left, more margin.
- **Before the corner** (while still upright and braking): shift your
  hips across the seat so one buttock cheek is off the inside, outside
  knee locked into the tank.
- **Inside foot:** ball of the foot on the peg, so your foot doesn't drag.
- **Upper body:** head and chest low and to the inside, looking through
  the corner. Elbows bent, **light on the bars** — hold yourself with the
  legs and core, not the arms. Arm tension makes the bike weave.
- **Knee down** is a lean-angle gauge, not a goal.

## 3. Braking
- The front brake does most of the work (two fingers on most modern
  sport bikes). Squeeze progressively: take up the slack, then build
  pressure as the front loads up. Grabbing it causes a front lock or stoppie.
- **Rear brake:** light, for stability; mostly used in slow corners and in
  the wet.
- **Trail braking:** release the front brake gradually as you lean in, so
  the front tyre stays loaded and the bike holds a tighter line. Abrupt
  release while leaned can make the bike run wide; staying hard on the
  brake deep into the lean causes **lowsides** (front washes out).
- **Engine braking:** downshift with rev-matching or a slipper clutch; many
  modern bikes have electronic engine-brake control and quickshifters.

## 4. Lines
- Late apex for most corners leading onto straights, as in cars — exit
  speed matters.
- Bikes use more of the width of the track and favour getting the bike
  upright early for drive.
- **One change at a time:** work on braking marker *or* turn-in point per
  session.
- Look ahead: eyes at the apex while braking, at the exit while at the
  apex.

## 5. Throttle and the highside
- Open the throttle smoothly from the apex, picking the bike up as you
  accelerate.
- A **highside** happens when the rear tyre loses grip and suddenly regains
  it, flicking the rider over the top. Avoid it by opening the throttle
  gradually and by not snapping it shut when the rear slides. Traction
  control helps but isn't a guarantee.
- **Lowsides** (sliding out) are more common and usually less injurious.

## 6. Tyres
- Cold tyres are the biggest crash cause in early laps. Use **tyre warmers**
  where allowed, and take a gentle out-lap.
- **Pressures:** set by the tyre manufacturer's track recommendation,
  typically much lower than road pressures — check hot pressures after a
  session and adjust. Values vary by tyre and conditions; don't copy a
  friend's.
- Watch for **cold tear** (tyre surface tearing because it's too cold or
  pressure too high) and **overheating** (greasy, blistering surface).

## 7. Gear
One-piece leathers (or two-piece zipped together), back protector,
race boots, gauntlet gloves, a certified full-face helmet (ECE 22.06 or
FIM-homologated for racing); airbag suits are increasingly required in
racing.

## Gotchas
- Hanging off late, mid-corner (unsettles the bike).
- Death grip on the bars.
- Pushing hard on lap one on cold tyres.
