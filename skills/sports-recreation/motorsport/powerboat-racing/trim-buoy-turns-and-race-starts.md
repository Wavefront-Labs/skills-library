# Powerboat racing — trim, buoy turns and race starts

**Goal:** keep a racing boat flying at the right attitude, get round the
turn marks without losing the boat or the lead, and start legally. This is
a lower-confidence draft: classes, hulls and rules vary widely (UIM
international, national bodies, jet-ski/PWC series). Treat numbers and
procedures as illustrative and confirm with your class rules and a coach.

## 1. Hull types (what you're steering)
- **Monohull (deep-V)** — common in offshore and many entry classes; rides
  rough water well.
- **Tunnel hull / catamaran** — circuit classes (e.g. formula classes):
  two sponsons with a tunnel creating aerodynamic lift. Very fast and
  sensitive to trim and wind.
- **Hydroplane** — rides on minimal contact points at high speed.
- **Personal watercraft (jet ski)** — stand-up or sit-down, jet-driven,
  steered by thrust: **no throttle, no steering**.

## 2. Trim
- **Outboard trim** changes the engine angle relative to the transom:
  - **Trim in (down):** bow down — helps getting on the plane and in turns,
    better in chop; too much ploughs and causes **bow steer**.
  - **Trim out (up):** bow up — less wetted surface and more speed on flat
    water; too much causes **porpoising** (rhythmic bow bounce) or, in
    tunnel boats, a **blow-over** (the boat flips backwards).
- **Trim tabs** (on many offshore boats) adjust running attitude and
  side-to-side list.
- Trim **progressively** and one input at a time; trim back in before
  turns and when wind or chop increases.
- **Chine walk:** a deep-V boat rolling side to side at speed — reduce
  trim or speed.
- **Hooking:** the hull suddenly digs in and grabs during a turn — often
  from too much trim-in or sharp steering at speed.

## 3. Buoy turns (turn marks)
- **Set up wide** and early, like a late-apex corner: brake the boat by
  backing off and trimming in, turn tight at the mark, then accelerate
  out.
- Most circuit courses are run **anti-clockwise** (turn marks on the left)
  — check the course briefing.
- **Hitting a buoy** is usually penalised; missing a mark can mean
  disqualification.
- Exit speed matters more than the closest pass; a tight entry that
  stalls the boat on the plane costs more.
- Wakes from other boats at the turn can launch you — choose a line that
  avoids crossing them at a bad angle.

## 4. Rough water
Stand or brace (depending on class), keep the bow slightly down, and use
throttle to "fly" over wave tops without landing on the transom. Back off
before big wave sets; most offshore crashes come from launching off a wave
and landing badly.

## 5. Starts
Common procedures (class-dependent):
- **Clock start:** boats approach a start line timed to a countdown clock
  and must not cross early.
- **Rolling start** behind a pace boat.
- **Pontoon/dock start** (some circuit classes): boats start from a fixed
  pontoon with engines running.
- Jumping the start = penalty laps or time.

## 6. Safety
- **Kill cord (lanyard)** attached to the driver at all times — it stops the
  engine if they are thrown out. Many accidents are driverless boats
  circling.
- Approved helmet, impact-rated life jacket, and in many classes a
  **safety cell/canopy** with harness and air supply.
- Learn underwater egress (capsize) training if your class has an enclosed
  cockpit — many bodies require it.
- Rescue boats on course; follow flag/light signals.

## Gotchas
- Trimming out for speed going into a turn.
- Not attaching the kill cord "just for a short test".
- Racing beyond your licence or experience grade.
