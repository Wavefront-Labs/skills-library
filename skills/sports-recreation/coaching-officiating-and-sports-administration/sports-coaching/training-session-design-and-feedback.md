# Training session design and feedback

**Goal:** run sessions where athletes get lots of relevant repetitions at the right
difficulty, and leave measurably better at one or two things — instead of a
collection of drills and long speeches.

> Draft: synthesis of common coach-education content (games-based and constraints-led
> approaches, practice-variability research, RAMP warm-up). Sport-specific content,
> safeguarding rules and qualification requirements come from your national governing
> body.

## 1. Start from one objective
Write a single sentence: "By the end, players will ___ (observable behaviour) under
___ (conditions)". E.g. "attackers scan before receiving in a 4v2 rondo". Two
objectives max. Every block in the session serves it.

## 2. Structure (example 60–90 min)
1. **Warm-up (10–15 min)** — RAMP: *Raise* (heart rate), *Activate* and *Mobilise*
   (muscles and joints used in the session), *Potentiate* (sharp, sport-specific
   intensity). Ideally uses the ball/implement and links to the objective.
2. **Skill block** — the objective in a simplified or **constrained** game (fewer
   players, smaller area, scoring rules that reward the target behaviour).
3. **Game block** — closer to the real sport, still with a constraint nudging the
   objective.
4. **Cool-down & review (5 min)** — two questions, one takeaway.

## 3. Practice design principles
- **Maximise reps**: lines and queues kill sessions. Small groups, multiple stations.
- **Variable/random practice** transfers better to games than repeating the same
  drill in a fixed order — even if performance *in* the session looks worse.
- **Challenge point**: aim for roughly a success rate that's neither trivial nor
  hopeless (many coaches target ~60–80%). Adjust with **STEP**: *Space, Task,
  Equipment, People* — e.g. bigger space = easier for attackers.
- Plan **progressions and regressions** before the session.

## 4. Feedback that works
- **Fewer, shorter, specific**: one coaching point at a time ("hips square before
  you pass"), not five.
- **Questions** first for older/experienced athletes ("Where was the space?") —
  self-discovery sticks better.
- **Demonstrations** for movement skills: show, then let them try within seconds.
- Mix **positive** and corrective; praise specific behaviours ("good scan before the
  ball arrived"), not generic "good job".
- **Fade** the frequency as learners improve; let them self-correct.
- Freeze-play sparingly; coach "on the fly" or in natural breaks.

## Gotchas
- Over-talking: if athletes are standing for more than ~2 minutes at a time, cut.
- Drills that don't resemble the sport's decisions (unopposed patterns forever).
- Ignoring **load**: training hard every day without planned recovery raises injury risk.
- Youth: development > winning; follow LTAD principles and safeguarding (two-adult
  rule, DBS/background checks where required).

## Verify
- [ ] Session plan has one written objective and each block links to it.
- [ ] Estimated activity time ≥ ~70% of session time.
- [ ] Progressions/regressions listed for each block.
- [ ] Feedback count kept short; at least one questioning episode.
- [ ] Post-session note: did the target behaviour appear more often by the end?
