# Positioning, game management and handling dissent

**Goal:** see incidents clearly, apply the laws consistently, and keep control of
players' behaviour so the game is decided by the players, not by arguments.

> Draft: principles common to many sports' referee education (football's Laws of the
> Game, rugby, basketball and others). Specific sanctions (cards, sin bins, technical
> fouls) and reporting duties come from your sport's law book and competition rules.

## Positioning
- **Angle beats distance**: being 15 m away with a clear side view of a challenge is
  better than being 5 m away behind it.
- **Keep play between you and your assistant/partner** (e.g. football's diagonal
  system) so incidents are seen from two sides.
- **Anticipate**: move to where the ball will go next, not where it is. Read the
  passer's body shape.
- Set-pieces and restarts: take a position that sees the likely contact zones (e.g.
  penalty-area holding at corners).
- Avoid being in passing lanes; keep moving, don't stand still in the centre.

## Game management
- **Advantage**: let play continue if stopping would penalise the non-offending team;
  signal clearly; come back to misconduct at the next stoppage.
- **Preventive officiating**: talk early — "keep your hands down", "watch it number
  6" — before fouls happen; visible presence near flashpoints.
- **Consistency**: same decision for the same act, for both teams, whole match.
  Players accept a strict referee who's consistent more than a lenient one who isn't.
- **Selling decisions**: clear signals, confident whistle tone (sharp for routine,
  long/loud for serious), stillness when giving a decision.

## Handling dissent — a step-ladder
1. **Ignore** low-level, non-personal frustration (a groan, a gesture to themselves).
2. **Body language/look** — eye contact, stand tall.
3. **Quiet word**: "That's enough, I've made my call."
4. **Public warning**: stop and speak clearly so others see.
5. **Formal sanction** per the laws (caution/card/technical foul).
6. **Send off/dismissal** for offensive, insulting or abusive language or physical
   contact with an official — no ladder for those.
Address the **captain** when several players protest; keep crowding away.

## Afterwards
- Write the **match report** promptly: exact words used (for abuse), time, player
  numbers, who witnessed. Factual, not emotional.
- Report assaults or threats immediately to the competition and, where appropriate,
  police.
- Self-review: one thing to repeat, one to improve (video if available).

## Gotchas
- **Running toward an incident while blowing** can look like panic; arrive calm.
- Changing a decision because of protests destroys credibility — change only on new
  information (e.g. an assistant's signal).
- **Personal safety** — leave with another official or club officer if tensions are high.
- Over-talking with players invites negotiation.

## Verify
- [ ] Positioning keeps clear angles on key contests; play kept between you and partner.
- [ ] Advantage applied and returned to for misconduct where needed.
- [ ] Dissent handled via the ladder; sanctions match the law book.
- [ ] Match report filed on time with factual detail.
