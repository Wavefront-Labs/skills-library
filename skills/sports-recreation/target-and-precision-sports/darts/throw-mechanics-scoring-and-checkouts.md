# Darts — throw mechanics, scoring and checkouts

**Goal:** throw a repeatable dart, score heavily in 501, and finish legs
reliably on doubles. Steel-tip standards below; soft-tip (electronic)
boards use different distances and often allow single-out.

## 1. Board setup (steel-tip standard)
- **Bullseye centre 1.73 m (5 ft 8 in)** from the floor.
- **Throw line (oche) 2.37 m (7 ft 9¼ in)** horizontally from the board
  face. Diagonal from bull centre to oche ≈ 2.93 m — a handy check.
- Rotate a bristle board's number ring/segments occasionally to spread
  wear; good even lighting with no shadows on the board.

## 2. Stance and grip
- **Stance:** most right-handers stand with the right foot forward at the
  oche, side-on or at an angle; weight mostly on the front foot; body
  still during the throw. Don't lean so far that you lose balance.
- **Grip:** firm enough to control, loose enough to release cleanly —
  tension in the fingers shows up as wobble. Common grips use 3 or 4
  fingers; the dart should point slightly up at release.
- Find a grip point on the barrel (centre of gravity is a common start)
  and use it every time.

## 3. The throw
1. **Aim:** dart, eye and target aligned (sight along the dart).
2. **Take-back:** bring the dart back toward the face/eye in a straight
   line, elbow staying roughly in place.
3. **Accelerate and release:** forearm moves forward, elbow rises slightly
   at the end; release at a consistent point.
4. **Follow-through:** hand finishes pointing at the target.
Only the forearm and hand move much; the shoulder and body stay quiet.
Same rhythm for every dart.

## 4. Scoring strategy (501)
- Most players aim at **treble 20 (T20)**; switch to **T19** when the 20
  bed is blocked by darts (shadowing) or to set up a better leave.
- Misses around T20 land on 1 and 5; around T19 on 7 and 3 — the 19 area
  "misses" score better on average, which some players prefer.
- **Leave even numbers** for doubles; a common preferred finishing double
  is **D16 (32)**, because a single 16 leaves D8, then D4, D2, D1.
- **Three-dart average** = points scored ÷ darts × 3; track it.

## 5. Checkouts (double-out)
You must finish exactly on a double (or bull = D25 = 50). Learn standard
routes:
- **170** = T20, T20, Bull (the highest checkout).
- **Bogey numbers** (no three-dart checkout): 169, 168, 166, 165, 163,
  162, 159.
- Common: **100** = T20, D20; **81** = T19, D12; **60** = 20, D20;
  **40** = D20; **36** = D18; **32** = D16; **121** = T20, 11, Bull (one
  route among several).
- **Setup shots:** when you can't finish, leave a good double (32, 40, 36)
  rather than an odd number.
- With one dart left and an odd number, hit a single to leave a double.
A printed checkout chart is a useful learning aid; memorise 2-80 first.

## 6. Practice routines
- **Around the clock** (1-20 doubles in order).
- **Bob's 27** (start with 27 points, aim three darts at each double,
  add double values hit, subtract the double value if all miss).
- 100 darts at T20 tracking hits.
- Practise checkouts from random numbers 40-100.

## 7. Matches
Formats: legs (first to 0 from 501), sets (best of 5 legs etc.); players
alternate throwing first. In cricket darts (a different game), you close
15-20 and bull — strategy is about closing numbers and scoring on open
ones.

## Gotchas
- Changing grip/stance after a bad leg.
- Going for a 3-dart 'big fish' with bad odds instead of a setup.
- Leaving odd numbers for your last dart.

## Verify
- [ ] Board at 1.73 m bull height, oche at 2.37 m.
- [ ] The same stance and grip point every dart.
- [ ] You know checkouts from 2-100 without looking.
- [ ] Your practice log tracks three-dart average and double hit rate.
