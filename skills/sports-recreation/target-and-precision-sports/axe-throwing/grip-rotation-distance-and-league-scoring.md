# Axe throwing — grip, rotation, distance and league scoring

**Goal:** stick the axe every throw and hit the bull, then the killshots
— by controlling rotation through your distance from the target rather
than by changing your throw. Venue and league rules differ (WATL and
IATO are the two main leagues); measurements below are typical, not
authoritative — check the venue's posted rules.

## 1. Safety
- **One thrower per lane**, and only when the lane is clear. Retrieve
  axes only when both throwers in adjacent lanes have thrown (venues use
  "throw together, retrieve together").
- Closed-toe shoes; no throwing after excessive drinking (most venues
  enforce limits).
- **Carry axes blade down**, by your side; hand them over handle first.
- Never throw at a bouncing or wobbling axe target; if an axe bounces
  back, let it fall — don't try to catch it.
- Keep the axe **sharp** — dull axes bounce. Venues sharpen; if you own
  one, a file and a puck stone maintain the edge.

## 2. The standard throw
**Two-handed (beginners):**
1. Stand with feet about shoulder-width, square to the target, at the
   throw line.
2. Grip the handle near the end: dominant hand first, other hand over it,
   thumbs side by side (not crossed). Blade straight, vertical.
3. Bring the axe straight back over your head (it shouldn't tilt), then
   forward in one smooth motion.
4. **Release** when the axe reaches roughly eye level / arms pointing at the
   target; **follow through** pointing down toward the target.
**One-handed:** same principle with one hand; feet staggered with the
opposite foot forward (or same-side, by preference). Keep the wrist
locked — a flicking wrist adds unwanted spin.

## 3. Rotation control = distance
A standard hatchet makes about **one rotation** from the usual line (~12
feet / ~3.6 m from the target in many venues). How the axe lands tells
you what to change:
- **Handle hits first / axe lands handle-down (under-rotated)** → **step
  forward** a little.
- **Axe lands with the head tipped down / top of the blade hits
  (over-rotated)** → **step back** a little.
- Adjust in small steps (half a foot); **keep the throw identical**.
- Most venues allow standing anywhere behind the minimum line — find
  your personal spot and mark it mentally.

## 4. Aiming
- Look at the exact spot you want, not the axe.
- Vertical misses: release point (early release → high; late → low).
- Horizontal misses: the axe isn't travelling straight back and forward
  (dropping the shoulder, twisting).
- Hold the axe blade aligned with the target's vertical grain lines.

## 5. Scoring (typical; differs by league)
- **WATL-style target:** rings score 1, 2, 3, 4 from the outside in with a
  **6-point bullseye**. **IATO-style targets** use fewer rings (1, 3, 5).
- **Killshots** — two small blue dots near the top — are worth more (8 in
  WATL as I understand it; IATO's value differs) and usually must be
  **called** before throwing.
- The axe must stick; the score is the highest ring the blade touches
  (some leagues require the blade to break the line).
- Match formats: 10 throws per game, best of three games; ties go to
  "big axe" or sudden-death killshots in WATL.
I have not verified the current point values for each league; they
change between seasons.

## 6. Knife throwing (brief)
- **No-spin** technique: the knife flies point-first; grip like a pencil
  or with an index finger along the spine; push straight from the
  shoulder.
- **Spin** technique: like axes — adjust distance per half-spin.
- Knives need a soft, end-grain or poplar target and a clear backstop.

## Gotchas
- Throwing harder to fix rotation (it makes it worse) — move your feet.
- Wrist flick in one-handed throws.
- Retrieving while the next lane is throwing.

## Verify
- [ ] You threw and retrieved only when the lane rules allowed.
- [ ] Rotation corrected by distance, with an identical throw.
- [ ] Killshots called before throwing.
- [ ] You know the scoring rules of your venue/league.
