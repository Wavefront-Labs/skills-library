# Clay target shooting — gun mount, lead and target reading

**Goal:** break more clays by mounting the gun the same way every time,
reading each target's line and speed, and applying a lead method
deliberately. Shotgun licensing is regulated by country; beginners should
start with a coach at a registered ground.

## 1. Safety at the stand
- Guns carried **open and empty** (break-action broken over the arm, semi-
  autos with action open and a flag) everywhere except the stand.
- Load only in the stand, muzzle pointed downrange; never turn around
  with a closed gun.
- After shooting, open the gun before turning; check both barrels are
  clear — an obstruction (e.g. a wad or a smaller-gauge cartridge stuck in
  the barrel) can burst it.
- Eye protection and hearing protection always.

## 2. Eye dominance and gun fit
- Check **eye dominance**: make a small triangle with your hands, look at
  a distant object with both eyes, close each eye in turn — the eye that
  still sees the object is dominant.
- **Cross-dominant** shooters (e.g. right-handed with left-eye
  dominance): shoot from the dominant-eye shoulder, close/dim the dominant
  eye at the shot, or use a small blinker on the glasses.
- **Gun fit:** with a proper mount, the eye should sit directly over the
  rib; a coach checks with a pattern plate. Stock length and comb height
  can be adjusted.

## 3. Gun mount
- Start with the stock just below the armpit (or pre-mounted for some
  disciplines), muzzles near the target's line.
- **Bring the gun to the face**, not the face down to the gun; stock into
  the shoulder pocket at the same time.
- Lead hand points at the target; muzzles move with the target as the
  gun comes up.
- Practise mounting at home (with an empty, safety-checked gun or snap
  caps) on the line where a wall meets the ceiling.

## 4. Reading the target
For each new stand, watch a pair before shooting:
- **Line and speed**: crossing, quartering, incoming, going away,
  dropping, rising, rabbit (rolling).
- **Pick-up point**: where your eyes first see the target clearly.
- **Hold point**: where the muzzles wait — usually part-way back toward
  the trap from the break point, on the target's line.
- **Break point**: where you plan to shoot it (often the target's most
  readable part of the flight).

## 5. Lead methods
- **Maintained (sustained) lead:** muzzles in front of the target from the
  start, keeping a gap as you swing and fire.
- **Pull-away:** start on the target, match its speed, then pull ahead
  and fire.
- **Swing-through:** start behind, swing through the target and fire as
  the muzzles pass in front.
The **perceived** lead differs by method — the faster the gun moves
relative to the target, the less lead you'll see. Pick one method per
target and commit. Always **follow through** after the shot; stopping the
gun is the most common miss behind.

## 6. Disciplines
- **Trap (DTL, Olympic trap):** going-away targets from a trap ahead;
  gun usually pre-mounted; muzzles held above the house.
- **Skeet:** crossing targets from high and low houses, fixed stations;
  in some forms the gun starts low (ISSF).
- **Sporting clays / English sporting / FITASC:** varied stands imitating
  game; a wide mix of targets.

## 7. Chokes and cartridges
Tighter chokes for longer targets (trap, long sporting crossers), more
open for close skeet targets. Use cartridges within the gun's proof
marks and the ground's shot-size/load limits (often a maximum of
28 g and shot size around 7-9 on many grounds — check).

## Gotchas
- Lifting the head off the stock to look at the target (shot goes high).
- Stopping the swing at the shot.
- Hold point too close to the trap: gun races and overtakes the target.

## Verify
- [ ] You know your dominant eye and the gun fits.
- [ ] Each stand is read (line, pick-up, hold, break point) before shooting.
- [ ] You named a lead method per target and followed through.
- [ ] Gun open, barrels checked clear, every time you leave the stand.
