# Lawn bowls and pétanque — delivery, weight, bias and end strategy

**Goal:** get your bowls/boules closer to the jack than the opponent's
more often by controlling line and weight, and by reading the head to
pick the right shot. Covers flat-green lawn bowls (crown green and
short-mat differ in details), pétanque and similar boules games
(bocce rules vary more).

## Part A — Lawn bowls

### 1. Bias and line
- Bowls are **biased**: shaped so they curve as they slow. The **small
  ring/disc** (the bias side) is on the **inside** of the curve.
- **Forehand** (right-hander: bowl curls right-to-left) and **backhand**
  (curls left-to-right) — choose the hand that avoids obstacles in the head.
- Aim at a point **wide of the jack** (the "aiming point" or shoulder of
  the arc), not at the jack. The width depends on green speed and bowl
  model.
- Check bias direction before every delivery: wrong-bias bowls are a
  classic beginner miss (and embarrassing).

### 2. Delivery
- Step forward on the foot opposite your bowling hand, low body,
  pendulum arm swing along the intended line, release close to the
  ground (no bouncing — damages the green).
- Keep one foot on or over the mat at release (check your rules).
- Same routine every bowl; **weight** (pace) comes from the length of
  backswing, not from forcing the arm.

### 3. Weight
- **Draw shot:** bowl comes to rest at the jack — the core shot.
- **Yard-on / follow-through weight:** a bit more pace to push a bowl or
  move the jack.
- **Trail:** move the jack back to your bowls behind it.
- **Drive/fire:** fast, straight shot to break up the head (little bias
  effect).
- Short vs long: a bowl just short can be promoted by later bowls, while
  bowls slightly **behind** the jack ("position bowls") cover the jack
  being knocked back. A good head has some of each; which is better
  depends on the head (coaches disagree on a general rule).

### 4. Team roles and the head
- **Lead:** places the jack and draws close (best drawing shots).
- **Second/third:** build or rescue the head; third measures and advises.
- **Skip:** directs play and plays the last, often tactical, bowls.
- Skip's signals: show the target point and the weight wanted.
- **Count the shots**: when you're "holding shot", consider guarding it
  (a bowl in front blocking a drive) or covering behind; when down,
  pick between drawing in and driving.

## Part B — Pétanque

### 1. Basics
- Thrower stands in a circle (feet inside, on the ground until the boule
  lands); the **cochonnet** (jack) is thrown 6-10 m in standard rules.
- Boules are thrown underhand, palm down — this gives backspin.
- The team **farther** from the jack throws next until it's closer or
  out of boules.

### 2. Pointing (placing)
- **Roulette** (rolling), **demi-portée** (lands about halfway, then rolls)
  and **portée/plombée** (lofted high, lands near the jack and stops) — pick
  by terrain: rough ground → higher lob.
- Read the ground: spot your landing point and look for stones and slopes.

### 3. Shooting (tirer)
- Knock the opponent's boule away; a **carreau** is when your boule
  replaces theirs on the spot.
- Shoot when the opponent has a boule close and you can't out-point it.

### 4. End strategy
- Count boules left for both teams — the team with more boules in hand
  controls the end.
- Don't point too early with your best boule; sometimes leaving the
  opponent to play first is stronger.

## Gotchas
- Wrong bias (bowls) and throwing from outside the circle (pétanque).
- Aiming at the jack instead of the shoulder of the arc.
- Shooting when you have more boules in hand than the opponent (often
  unnecessary).

## Verify
- [ ] Bias checked before every delivery.
- [ ] You can name your aiming point and intended weight before bowling.
- [ ] In each end you knew who was holding shot and how many boules/bowls remained.
- [ ] Pétanque: feet stayed in the circle and you chose point vs shoot deliberately.
