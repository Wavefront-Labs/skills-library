# Tenpin bowling — approach, release, lane reading and spares

**Goal:** raise your average by repeating the same shot, hitting your
target on the lane (not the pins), reading how the oil changes, and
converting spares — spare shooting is where most amateur pins are lost.

## 1. Equipment
- **Ball weight:** heaviest you can control comfortably (often ~10% of
  body weight up to 16 lb max); too heavy hurts accuracy.
- **Grip:** house balls use a **conventional** grip (fingers to the
  second knuckle). A drilled ball with **fingertip** grip (first knuckle)
  enables more revs; get a pro shop to fit it.
- **Reactive resin** balls hook more on oil; a **plastic (polyester) ball**
  goes straight — ideal for spares.
- Shoes: bowling shoes only (a slide sole on the sliding foot).

## 2. The approach (four-step, right-handed)
1. **Stance:** pick a board on the approach dots (start position);
   ball held at waist/chest, elbow in.
2. **Step 1 (right foot):** push the ball out and slightly down.
3. **Step 2:** ball swings down past the leg.
4. **Step 3:** ball at the top of the backswing (roughly shoulder height
   at most).
5. **Step 4 (left foot slide):** ball swings down, release just past the
   sliding foot; knee bent, shoulders square to the target line.
Timing — ball and feet arriving together — matters more than speed. A
five-step approach adds a short first step with the left foot.

## 3. Release
- **Straight-ish (beginner):** thumb at ~12 o'clock, hand behind the ball.
- **Hook release:** thumb exits first (~10 o'clock for right-handers),
  fingers lift and rotate through — "handshake" finish.
- Keep the swing loose and pendulum-like; muscling the ball pulls it off
  line.
- **Follow-through:** arm finishes up toward the target.

## 4. Targeting
- **Aim at the arrows** (about 15 feet down the lane), not the pins —
  far closer and easier to hit.
- Boards are numbered from the right gutter for right-handers (1-39); the
  second arrow is board 10, the centre arrow board 20.
- **Strike line (right-hander):** the ball should enter the **pocket**
  between the 1 and 3 pins.
- Adjust **feet and target together**: the common rule — to move the ball's
  pocket hit, move your feet and target the same direction ("**2 and 1**"
  move: e.g. feet 2 boards, target 1 board — for a ball hitting high/
  crossing over, move left with feet more than target).

## 5. Lane oil and transition
- Lanes are oiled down the middle, drier on the outside (typical **house
  shot**); the ball skids in oil and hooks in dry areas.
- As games go on, oil is pushed down the lane and **breaks down** where
  balls travel — the ball starts hooking earlier. Respond by moving
  **feet and target left** (right-handers) a board or two at a time.
- Sport/challenge patterns are flatter and much harder; check the
  pattern sheet if posted.

## 6. Spare system
- Use the **plastic ball** and a straight shot for single-pin spares —
  removes oil/hook from the equation.
- **3-6-9 (and variations) system:** from your strike position, move a set
  number of boards per pin to the left or right. Many bowlers instead
  shoot **cross-lane** for corner pins: **10-pin** from the left side of
  the approach, **7-pin** from the right side.
- Practise the 10-pin (right-handers' hardest): it's the most common leave.

## 7. Scoring and leagues
- **Strike** = 10 + next two balls; **spare** = 10 + next ball; open frame
  = pins knocked down. Max 300.
- League **handicap** = a percentage (commonly 80-100%) of the difference
  between your average and a basis score (e.g. 200-230). Check your
  league rules.
- USBC (US) and national bodies certify averages for tournaments.

## Gotchas
- Looking at the pins instead of the arrows.
- Using a reactive ball for 10-pins and hooking into the gutter.
- Moving only feet (or only target) when transition comes.
