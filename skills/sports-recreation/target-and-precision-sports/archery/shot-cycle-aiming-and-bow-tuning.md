# Archery — shot cycle, aiming and bow tuning

**Goal:** shoot the same shot every time, aim with the right system for
your bow, and set the bow up so good shots group together. Covers
Olympic-style recurve, compound, barebow and traditional bows; get
initial coaching at a club — form faults learnt alone are hard to undo.

## 1. Safety first
- Only nock an arrow on the shooting line, pointing downrange; never
  draw with anyone forward of the line.
- **Never dry-fire** (release without an arrow) — it can break the bow
  and injure you.
- Check arrows for cracks (flex carbon shafts and listen) before each
  session; use arrows of the correct spine and length for your draw.
- Follow whistle commands at ranges (commonly: two whistles to the line,
  one to shoot, three to collect, several for emergency stop).

## 2. The shot cycle (recurve example)
1. **Stance:** feet shoulder-width, square or slightly open to the target,
   weight even.
2. **Nock and set:** hook the string in the first joints of three fingers
   (split-finger: one above, two below the arrow for Olympic recurve);
   bow hand relaxed, pressure through the thumb pad/web, no gripping.
3. **Pre-draw / raise:** bow arm up, shoulder down.
4. **Draw** using back muscles, elbow in line with the arrow.
5. **Anchor:** the same point every time — under the chin with string
   touching chin and nose for Olympic recurve; side of the face/corner of
   mouth for many barebow/traditional archers.
6. **Transfer to back tension / expansion** — keep pulling through the
   clicker (recurve) or release aid trigger (compound) rather than
   holding still.
7. **Release** by relaxing the fingers (not opening them) while the back
   keeps working; the hand moves back along the neck.
8. **Follow-through:** hold the position until the arrow hits.
Consistency of anchor and release matters more than any single form
detail.

## 3. Aiming systems
- **Sight (recurve, compound):** set elevation by distance; move the sight
  **toward the group** (group low → sight down; group left → sight left).
- **Compound:** peep sight + scope, release aid, often a level bubble —
  check the bubble each shot.
- **Barebow:** aim with the arrow point; **string walking** (moving the
  finger position down the string by distance) or **face walking**.
- **Instinctive / gap shooting** for traditional bows.
- Adjust sights only from **groups** (3-6 arrows), never from one arrow.

## 4. Basic tuning
- **Brace height** within the manufacturer's range; adjust by twisting
  the string; settle on the quietest/most forgiving.
- **Nocking point:** usually slightly above square (roughly 3-6 mm for
  recurve, varies); use a bow square.
- **Centre shot:** the arrow point sits just outside the string when
  viewed from behind (recurve, right-handed).
- **Arrow spine:** too stiff or too weak shafts plane away from the group;
  use the manufacturer's spine chart for draw weight and arrow length.
- **Bare-shaft tuning:** shoot fletched and unfletched arrows at ~18-20 m;
  where bare shafts land relative to fletched shows spine and nock height
  issues. **Paper tuning** is common for compounds.
- **Tiller** and limb bolt adjustments: small changes, one at a time,
  written down.

## 5. Rounds and competition
World Archery outdoor recurve: 70 m on a 122 cm face (compound 50 m on
an 80 cm face); indoor 18 m on 40 cm faces; field and 3D rounds use
marked/unmarked distances. Scores are 10 to 1 per arrow (X counts for
ties). Check national federation rules for local rounds.

## Gotchas
- Gripping the bow (torque → left/right misses).
- Plucking the string (opening fingers sideways).
- Changing sight, form and equipment at the same time.
