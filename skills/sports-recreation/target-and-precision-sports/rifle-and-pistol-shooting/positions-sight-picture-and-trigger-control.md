# Rifle and pistol shooting — positions, sight picture and trigger control

**Goal:** put shots in a small group consistently at a club range — air
rifle/pistol at 10 m, smallbore (.22) at 25-50 m, or fullbore — using
stable positions and a clean trigger release. Firearm ownership and use
are tightly regulated and vary by country; join a licensed club and
follow its range rules and your local law.

## 1. Safety (non-negotiable)
Universal rules:
1. Treat every gun as loaded.
2. Never point the muzzle at anything you aren't willing to destroy.
3. Finger off the trigger until sights are on target.
4. Know your target and what's beyond it.
Range practice: follow range-officer commands; actions open, magazines
out and chamber flags inserted when not on the firing line; never handle
guns while anyone is forward of the line. Wear **eye and hearing
protection** (hearing for all powder firearms).

## 2. Position: bone support and natural point of aim
- Stability should come from **bone structure**, not muscle tension.
- **Prone (rifle):** body angled ~10-20° from the line of fire; supporting
  elbow under the rifle; sling taking the weight.
- **Standing rifle:** hips slightly forward, supporting arm resting on the
  hip/torso, rifle balanced on the hand.
- **Pistol (one-handed ISSF):** body side-on (~45° or more), arm straight,
  wrist locked, free hand relaxed in a pocket or on the belt.
- **Natural point of aim (NPA):** settle in position, close your eyes,
  breathe, open them. Where the sights rest is your NPA. If it's off the
  target, **move your body/feet**, not your arms, until it rests on the
  target with eyes open.

## 3. Sight picture and alignment
- **Iron/aperture sights:** focus your eye on the **front sight**, not the
  target; the target is slightly blurred. Centre the front aperture/post
  in the rear aperture (eye centres it naturally).
- **Pistol open sights:** front post level with the rear notch tops, equal
  light either side; hold at 6 o'clock or centre per your sights' zero.
- **Scopes:** consistent head position (cheek weld) to avoid parallax.
- Accept the **arc of movement** — you'll never be perfectly still. Break
  the shot while the wobble is centred, don't snatch at a moment.

## 4. Trigger control and breathing
- Press the trigger **straight back**, smoothly, increasing pressure until
  it breaks — the shot should slightly surprise you.
- Use the pad of the finger; avoid touching the stock/frame with the
  trigger finger.
- **Breathing:** exhale naturally and hold in the natural respiratory
  pause; shoot within a few seconds; if it's not right, breathe and
  restart.
- **Follow-through:** keep the sights on the target and trigger held back
  for a moment after the shot; **call the shot** (where you saw the sights
  when it broke).
- Dry-fire practice (where safe and the gun allows it) builds trigger
  control cheaply.

## 5. Zeroing and diagnosis
- Shoot **groups** (5-10 shots) and adjust sights to move the **mean point
  of impact** onto the centre. Sight clicks: move the rear sight in the
  direction you want the group to move (check marking on your sight).
- Group size = your precision; group position = zero. Fix size before
  chasing zero.
- Pistol patterns (right-handed shooters) like low-left often indicate
  anticipation/jerking the trigger; plot shots in a diary.

## 6. Match disciplines
ISSF events include 10 m air rifle and air pistol, 50 m rifle three
positions, 25 m pistol; national bodies run smallbore, fullbore and
benchrest leagues. Read the specific match rules: shot times, sighting
shots, equipment restrictions.

## Gotchas
- Muscling the gun onto the target instead of moving NPA.
- Focusing on the target instead of the front sight.
- Adjusting sights after single shots.

- [ ] Each shot called and logged.
