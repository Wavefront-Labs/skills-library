# Cue sports — cueing, position play and safety

**Goal:** pot more balls by delivering the cue straight, control where
the cue ball goes next, and win the tactical exchanges. Examples cover
snooker and pool (8-ball/9-ball); English billiards and carom share the
cueing fundamentals.

## 1. Stance and bridge
- **Stance (right-hander):** line up the shot standing behind it; step in
  with the right foot on the line of the cue, left foot forward and to the
  left, body side-on, chin low over the cue (snooker players go lower than
  most pool players).
- **Bridge:** a stable base. Snooker: flat hand, thumb cocked forming a V
  for the cue. Pool: often a closed (looped finger) bridge for power, open
  for precision. Bridge length ~20-30 cm from the cue ball.
- **Grip:** loose, at a point where the forearm is vertical at address
  (cue ball contact). A tight grip twists the cue.

## 2. Cue action (straight cueing)
- Only the **forearm** swings, pivoting at the elbow; shoulder and head
  still.
- **Pre-shot routine:** a few feathering strokes to check line → brief
  pause at the back → smooth acceleration → follow through straight
  (and stay down until the ball drops).
- Drill: a straight shot along the spots (snooker: cue ball on the brown
  spot, shoot over the blue/pink/black spots and back) — the ball should
  return straight to the cue tip.
- Eyes: last look at the **object ball** at the moment of delivery
  (most players).

## 3. Aiming
- **Contact point:** the point on the object ball opposite the pocket
  line. **Ghost ball:** imagine where the cue ball centre must be at
  impact (one ball-width behind the contact point along the pocket line)
  and aim the cue at that.
- Practice cut angles from thin to full; aiming is learned by repetition,
  not calculation.

## 4. Cue ball control
- **Stun:** strike slightly below centre so the cue ball arrives with no
  spin; on a full shot, it stops dead. On a cut, it leaves along the
  tangent line (~90° to the object ball's path).
- **Screw / draw:** strike below centre with a good follow-through → the
  cue ball comes back.
- **Top / follow:** above centre → cue ball runs on after contact.
- **Side (English):** off-centre left/right — changes the angle off
  cushions; also causes **squirt/deflection** (the cue ball leaves
  slightly off the aimed line) and **throw** on the object ball. Use
  sparingly until your plain-ball game is solid.
- Pace control matters more than spin: most positional errors are pace
  errors.

## 5. Position play
- Plan **two or three shots ahead**: pot this ball so the cue ball lands
  on the correct side of the next ball for the following one.
- Play for an **angle**, not a dead-straight shot, so you can move the cue
  ball naturally.
- Prefer the simplest route with the least spin and fewest cushions.
- **Snooker break building:** keep the cue ball near the black (or the
  pink/blue) so you're always on a high-value colour after each red;
  open loose reds early in the break, not when you're out of position.
- **9-ball:** route from ball to ball in order; avoid crossing the table
  through clusters.

## 6. Safety
- When the pot isn't high-percentage, play safe: leave the cue ball
  tight on a cushion or far from the object ball, or hidden behind a
  blocker (a **snooker**).
- Snooker: on a safety, roll the cue ball onto the baulk cushion while
  returning the red to a cushion.
- Pool: in 9-ball "push out" rules and ball-in-hand fouls make safety
  very powerful — know the house or tournament rules.
- Choose a shot by expected value: chance of pot × reward vs what you
  leave if you miss.

## Gotchas
- Lifting the head before the cue ball strikes.
- Over-hitting: pace is position.
- Using side when plain ball would do.

- [ ] You can stun, screw and follow on demand on a straight shot.
- [ ] Before each pot, you named where the cue ball will stop.
- [ ] You chose pot vs safety deliberately, considering what you leave.
