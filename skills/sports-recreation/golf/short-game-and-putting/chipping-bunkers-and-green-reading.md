# Short game and putting — chips, bunkers and green reading

**Goal:** get up and down more often and stop three-putting. Roughly half
of strokes in an average round are played within ~100 yards, so this is
where most golfers save the most shots.

## 1. Chipping (low, mostly rolling)
- **Setup:** narrow stance, ball back of centre, weight ~60–70% lead side,
  hands slightly ahead of the ball, shaft leaning toward the target.
- **Stroke:** a putting-style motion with little wrist hinge; keep the
  lead wrist flat through impact; body turns gently.
- **Landing spot:** pick a spot ~1 m onto the green and let the club's
  loft decide the roll. Rough carry:roll ratios (flat green, average speed):
  - 7-iron: ~1:4, 9-iron: ~1:2, pitching wedge: ~1:1–1:2, sand wedge: ~1:1.
  **Rules of thumb; calibrate on your course.**
- Gotcha: "scooping" to help the ball up — causes fats and thins. The loft
  lifts the ball.

## 2. Pitching (higher, more carry)
- Wider stance than chipping, ball centre, more wrist hinge; turn through
  with the body. Length of backswing controls distance (e.g. "clock" system
  — 7:30, 9:00, 10:30 backswings with the same tempo).
- Use the **bounce** (the angled sole) by keeping the shaft more vertical
  at impact so the club glides rather than digs.
- Build a distance chart for each wedge at each backswing length.

## 3. Greenside bunkers (the splash shot)
- Open the clubface **before** gripping (expose the bounce), open the stance
  slightly, ball forward of centre, dig feet in for stability.
- **Hit the sand, not the ball:** enter about 2–5 cm (1–2 in) behind the
  ball and keep accelerating; the sand carries the ball out.
- Full-ish swing; the sand absorbs most energy. Finish the swing.
- **Rules:** in a bunker you may not ground the club in the sand before
  the stroke or test the sand (Rules of Golf, Rule 12).
- Fried egg / plugged lie: square or slightly closed face, steeper swing, expect more roll.
- Long bunker shots (20–30 m): less open face or a gap/pitching wedge.

## 4. Putting stroke
- Eyes over or just inside the ball line; ball slightly forward of centre.
- Rock the shoulders; minimal wrist action. Backstroke and through-stroke
  roughly equal length, smooth tempo.
- Strike centre of the putter face — off-centre hits lose distance.

## 5. Speed control first
- Most three-putts come from **bad speed**, not bad line.
- Aim for the ball to finish **~30–45 cm (12–18 in) past** the hole on
  makeable putts; on long putts aim to stop in a 1 m circle.
- Drill: ladder drill — putts to 5, 10, 15, 20 m, each must finish beyond
  the previous one and short of the next.

## 6. Reading greens
- Find the **fall line** (straight uphill/downhill line to the hole): putts
  along it don't break.
- Read from below the hole if possible; look at overall slope as you
  approach the green.
- Feel slope through your feet (the basis of methods like AimPoint).
- Faster greens and downhill putts break more; uphill putts break less.
- Grain (on Bermuda grass) makes putts faster down-grain; shiny = down-grain.
- Pick the **apex** or an aim spot and putt straight at it with the right speed.

## Gotchas
- Decelerating on bunker shots and short putts.
- Lob wedge for everything when a bump-and-run is safer.
- **Ratios and distances above are approximations, not measured data.**
