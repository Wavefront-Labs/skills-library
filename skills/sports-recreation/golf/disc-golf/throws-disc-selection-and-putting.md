# Disc golf — throws, disc selection and putting

**Goal:** throw controlled, repeatable lines with a small bag of the right
discs, and make more putts inside 10 m. Disc golf scoring is like golf:
fewest throws from tee to basket.

## 1. Flight numbers (Innova-style 4-number system)
`Speed / Glide / Turn / Fade`, e.g. **7 / 5 / −1 / 2**.
- **Speed (1–14):** how fast it must be thrown to fly as designed. Higher
  isn't "farther" unless you have the arm speed.
- **Glide (1–7):** how well it holds the air.
- **Turn (+1 to −5):** tendency to drift right (for right-hand backhand,
  RHBH) during the fast part of flight. More negative = more turn
  (**understable**).
- **Fade (0–5):** hook left at the end as it slows. Higher = more fade
  (**overstable**).
Brands rate slightly differently; plastic type and wear change flight
(beat-in discs become more understable). **Directions are for RHBH;
reverse for forehand or left-handed backhand.**

## 2. A beginner bag (5–6 discs)
- **Putter** (speed 2–3) for putting and short approaches.
- **Midrange** (speed 4–5), straight/neutral — the most useful disc for
  learning form.
- **Fairway driver** (speed 6–8), slightly understable.
- An **overstable** approach/utility disc for wind and forehands.
- Skip distance drivers (speed 11+) until you can throw a fairway driver
  ~90+ m with control. **Distances are rough guidance.**
- Light weights (~160–170 g) can help newer players.

## 3. Backhand form
- **Grip:** power grip (four fingers under the rim) for drives; fan grip
  for control and approaches.
- **Reach-back** straight back along the line, disc flat at chest height.
- **Pull** the disc across the chest close to the body, leading with the
  elbow; the **snap** comes from unfolding the arm late.
- **Plant** the front foot (right foot RHBH) pointing roughly 90° from the
  target to brace and pivot.
- **X-step run-up** adds momentum once standstill form is solid; a fast
  run-up with poor form adds only errors.
- Common faults: rounding (disc swings wide, starts right), throwing
  nose-up (stalls and fades early), gripping too tight.

## 4. Forehand (sidearm, flick)
- Two fingers (middle and index) under the rim, thumb on top.
- Short arm motion with a wrist flick; keep the disc's outside edge slightly
  down to avoid it flipping over.
- Use overstable discs; forehand torque flips understable discs.

## 5. Lines
- **Hyzer:** disc released with the outside edge tilted down — curves
  left (RHBH), reliable and wind-safe.
- **Anhyzer:** edge tilted up — curves right.
- **Flat:** follows the disc's natural stability.
- **Hyzer-flip:** release an understable disc on hyzer; it flips flat and
  flies straight a long way.
- Headwind makes discs act more understable; tailwind more overstable.

## 6. Putting
- **Circle 1 (C1):** within 10 m of the basket. Aim for one chain link,
  not the basket.
- **Push putt:** little spin, disc pushed straight on a flat plane;
  good in wind, shorter range.
- **Spin putt:** more wrist snap and spin; longer range, more come-backers.
- Practise 3–4 m putts until near-automatic, then 5 m, 7 m, 9 m.
- **Rules:** inside C1 you must not step past your lie marker before
  demonstrating balance after the putt (no "falling putts" — PDGA rule
  802.04 on "falling putt", check current rule numbering).

## 7. Basic rules and etiquette
- Lie is where the disc lands; mark it or throw from behind it with a
  supporting point within 30 cm behind the marker.
- **OB** (out of bounds) usually costs 1 stroke; **mandatories** (arrows
  you must pass a certain side of) have defined penalties.
- Furthest from basket throws first; shout "Fore!" if a disc heads toward
  people. Watch for walkers on shared parks.

## Gotchas
- Distance drivers too early.
- Running up before the arm motion works.
- Putting at the whole basket.

## Verify
- [ ] Can read a disc's flight numbers and predict its RHBH path.
- [ ] Midrange thrown straight standstill 50+ m with hyzer and flat releases.
- [ ] 8/10 from 4 m with a consistent putting style.
- [ ] Knows OB, mando and C1 balance rules.
