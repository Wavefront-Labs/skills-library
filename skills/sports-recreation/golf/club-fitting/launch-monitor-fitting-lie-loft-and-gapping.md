# Club fitting — launch monitor, lie, loft and gapping

**Goal:** match clubs to a golfer's delivery so good swings get rewarded:
optimal launch and spin with the driver, irons that sit correctly at
impact, and even distance gaps through the bag. **Draft: target windows
below are commonly quoted fitting ranges, not manufacturer specs.**

## 1. Before hitting
- Interview: goals, typical miss, ball flight, injuries, current clubs and
  specs, budget. Measure height and **wrist-to-floor** (a starting point for
  length and lie), hand size (grip size).
- Warm the player up fully; fit with the ball they play (or a premium ball).
- Get a **baseline** with their current club.

## 2. Driver fitting with a launch monitor
Key numbers: club speed, ball speed, **smash factor** (ball ÷ club speed;
~1.45–1.50 is good with driver), launch angle, spin rate, attack angle,
face-to-path, carry and dispersion.
- Typical targets (vary with speed and attack angle):
  - Higher speed (~105+ mph club): launch ~11–14°, spin ~2,000–2,600 rpm.
  - Moderate speed (~85–95 mph): launch ~13–16°, spin ~2,300–2,800 rpm.
  - Positive attack angle allows higher launch with lower spin.
- Process: head first (forgiveness, CG, loft), then shaft, then loft/hosel
  setting fine-tuning. Change one variable at a time; hit 5–10 good shots
  per configuration; compare **averages and dispersion**, not best shots.
- Too much spin → ballooning, short carry: try lower loft (with launch
  check), lower-spin head, or more forward CG. Too little spin → knuckly,
  falls out of the sky.

## 3. Shafts
- **Flex** follows speed and transition (a quick, aggressive transition
  can need stiffer than speed alone suggests).
- **Weight** affects tempo and consistency: heavier for strong, fast
  tempos; lighter for slower swings to gain speed.
- **Profile/kick point** influences launch and spin modestly.
- Evaluate by dispersion and strike consistency as much as by distance.

## 4. Irons: lie angle
- **Lie** = angle between shaft and sole. Wrong lie points the face left
  or right at impact.
  - Toe up at impact (too upright) → face points **left**.
  - Toe down (too flat) → face points **right**.
- **Dynamic test:** lie board and tape on the sole; hit 5+ shots; mark
  should be centred or slightly toe of centre. Check with one mid-iron,
  then confirm across the set.
- Most forged irons bend ~2° easily; many cast irons can be bent with care.
  Check manufacturer limits. Lie changes also change effective loft slightly.

## 5. Lofts and gapping
- Measure every club's **loft** on a loft/lie machine — retail sets can be
  off by a degree or more.
- Hit each club on the monitor and record average carry. Aim for **even
  gaps** (often ~10–15 yards / ~9–14 m in the irons; tighter for wedges).
- Common issues:
  - Big gap between pitching wedge (modern lofts often ~43–46°) and sand
    wedge (~54–56°): add a gap wedge (~50°).
  - Long irons that carry the same distance: replace with hybrids/fairway woods.
- Wedges: loft spacing ~4–6°, choose **bounce** by turf conditions and
  swing (steep diggers and soft sand → more bounce).

## 6. Other specs
- **Length:** from wrist-to-floor and the test strike pattern.
- **Grip size:** too small can encourage overactive hands; measure hand.
- **Swing weight** shifts with length/grip changes; restore if needed.
- Putter: length, lie, loft and alignment features also fittable.

## 7. Deliverable
Write a spec sheet: head, shaft (model/flex/weight), length, lie, loft,
grip size, swing weight per club, plus baseline vs fitted numbers.

## Gotchas
- Fitting on best shots, not averages.
- Fitting a fatigued player late in a session.
- Ignoring the ball model.

## Verify
- [ ] Baseline and fitted launch numbers recorded and compared on averages.
- [ ] Lie board marks centred across the irons.
- [ ] Measured lofts and carries show even gapping.
- [ ] Spec sheet produced.
