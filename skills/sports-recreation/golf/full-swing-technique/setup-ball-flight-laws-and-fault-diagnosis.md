# Golf full swing — setup, ball flight laws and fault diagnosis

**Goal:** understand why the ball flies where it does and fix the cause, not
the symptom. Most swing faults start in setup; most curve comes from the
relationship between face and path at impact. Right-handed golfer assumed;
mirror for left-handers.

## 1. Setup
- **Grip:** lead hand so you see 2–3 knuckles looking down; the "V"
  between thumb and forefinger points roughly between chin and trail
  shoulder. Trail hand on the side, not under. Overlap, interlock or
  ten-finger are all fine. Grip pressure moderate.
- **Posture:** hinge at the hips (not the waist), back straight, knees
  slightly flexed, arms hanging naturally under the shoulders.
- **Alignment:** feet, hips and shoulders parallel to the target line (a
  rail-road track: feet on the inner rail, ball on the outer). Use an
  alignment stick in practice.
- **Ball position:** driver opposite the lead heel; mid-irons roughly
  centre to slightly forward; short irons/wedges centre.
- **Driver vs irons:** driver ball teed with about half the ball above the
  crown, spine tilted slightly away from target to hit **up** (positive
  attack angle). Irons hit **down** — low point of the swing *after* the
  ball, divot starting at or ahead of the ball.

## 2. Ball flight laws (modern understanding)
- **Starting direction** is mostly set by the **clubface angle** at impact
  (roughly 75–85% face for irons; more with driver).
- **Curve** comes from the **face relative to the path** (face-to-path):
  - Face open to path → ball curves right (fade/slice).
  - Face closed to path → curves left (draw/hook).
- Examples:
  - **Slice:** starts left or straight, curves hard right. Usually
    out-to-in path ("over the top") with a face open to that path.
  - **Push-slice:** starts right, curves more right — face open, path
    in-to-out but face even more open.
  - **Hook:** face closed to path, often in-to-out path.
  - **Pull:** starts left, straight — face and path both left.
- Old "path sets start direction" teaching is outdated; launch monitors
  confirmed face dominance.

## 3. Strike faults
- **Fat (ground first):** low point behind the ball — weight hanging back,
  early release ("casting"). Drill: line on the ground, take divots on the
  target side of it.
- **Thin (bottom of ball):** low point too early or standing up; same drill.
- **Toe/heel strikes:** check with **impact tape** or foot spray on the
  face. Heel and **shanks** (hosel contact) often from moving toward the
  ball during the swing.
- Strike location affects curve too (gear effect on woods: toe hits draw,
  heel hits fade).

## 4. Practising
- Warm up with wedges, then mid-irons, then woods.
- One swing thought per session.
- Hit fewer balls with purpose: pick a target and routine every shot.
- Film down-the-line and face-on at hand height to check setup and path.
- Launch monitor data (club path, face angle, attack angle, low point)
  makes diagnosis objective if available.

## 5. When to get lessons
If a fix makes things worse within a few sessions, or pain occurs (lower
back, lead elbow, wrist), see a qualified coach (PGA professional) —
self-diagnosis often chases the wrong cause.

## Gotchas
- Fighting a slice by aiming further left (makes the path more out-to-in).
- Ball too far forward with irons → thins and pulls.
- **Face-dominance percentages are approximate and vary with club and strike.**

## Verify
- [ ] Setup checked on video against alignment stick.
- [ ] You can explain any shot's shape in face/path terms.
- [ ] Iron divots start at or ahead of the ball.
- [ ] Impact tape shows centred strikes most of the time.
