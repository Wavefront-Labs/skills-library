# Mini golf — bank shots, pace and competition putting

**Goal:** score lower at mini golf by treating every hole as a geometry and
pace problem. Works for leisure "crazy golf"/adventure courses and as an
intro to competitive minigolf.

## 1. Stroke basics
- Same as a short golf putt: eyes over the ball, shoulders rock, wrists
  quiet, smooth tempo.
- Hit the ball on the putter's **sweet spot** (often marked); toe/heel
  hits wobble and lose pace.
- **Pace beats line on most holes:** many holes have a narrow window of
  speed that makes the ball drop or clear an obstacle without
  bouncing back. Too fast lips out or rebounds; too slow fails ramps.

## 2. Reading the lane
Before putting:
- Walk the lane: slope (look at it from low down), worn tracks where
  others' balls run, seams, dirt, wet patches.
- Where do **missed** balls go? Find the lane's "funnel" — many holes guide
  balls toward a common spot.
- Note the **start area**: you can usually place the ball anywhere within
  the tee mat; pick the spot that gives the best angle.

## 3. Bank shots
- **Angle in ≈ angle out** off a firm rail (angle of incidence = angle of
  reflection), with some loss on softer bumpers and with spin/pace.
- **Mirror trick:** imagine the hole reflected behind the rail; aim at the
  reflected image. For two-rail shots, mirror twice.
- Hit banks with **moderate pace** — fast balls skid and rebound at
  shallower angles than expected; very soft balls die on the rail.
- Use corners: hitting a corner's two rails can redirect the ball predictably.

## 4. Obstacles
- **Ramps/hills:** need firm, committed pace; practise the minimum speed
  that clears. Downhill after the ramp means the hole is reached at
  speed — aim to "die" the ball at the cup if the hole lies after a slope.
- **Windmills/moving blades:** time the swing by counting the rotation;
  hit as the gap begins to open, so the ball arrives in the middle of the gap.
- **Loops:** require more speed than seems natural; under-hit balls roll back.
- **Tubes and tunnels:** look where they exit; often the exit sets up a gimme.

## 5. Leisure scoring
- Usual cap: **6 strokes** max per hole (local rules vary). Balls off the
  course: replace near where it left, usually +1 stroke.
- Let faster groups through; don't hold up the queue practising.

## 6. Competitive minigolf (in brief)
- Governed internationally by the **World Minigolf Sport Federation
  (WMF)**; standardised course systems include **Eternit** (smaller
  asbestos-cement lanes), **Beton** (concrete, longer lanes), **felt** and
  **Minigolf Open Standard** courses, among others.
- Players carry **bags of many balls** of different hardness, bounce and
  weight, choosing per hole and per temperature (warm balls bounce more).
  Ball choice is a large part of the competitive game.
- Aim is usually a hole-in-one on every lane; a round of 18 in the low 20s
  can be competitive at top level.
- **Details on systems and ball science from community sources; verify with
  national federations.**

## Gotchas
- Blasting every putt; pace errors cause most 3-putts.
- Ignoring the tee-mat placement freedom.
- Reading banks without accounting for rail softness.

- [ ] Used the mirror method for at least one bank shot and got the predicted rebound.
- [ ] Ramp holes cleared on first attempt more often than not.
- [ ] Kept within the stroke cap and pace of play.
