# Course management — targets, dispersion and scoring formats

**Goal:** lower scores without changing your swing, by choosing targets and
clubs that fit the shots you actually hit. Most amateurs lose shots by
aiming at flags, under-clubbing and taking on hazards they rarely clear.

## 1. Know your real numbers
- **Carry distances** per club (not total distance, not your best ever).
  Measure on a launch monitor or with a rangefinder on the course; use the
  **average** carry.
- **Dispersion:** how wide your shots spread left-right and long-short.
  A mid-handicapper's 7-iron might spread 20–30 m wide; driver much wider.
  **Typical estimate, not a measured standard — measure your own.**
- Most amateurs come up **short** of the green far more than long: club up.

## 2. Plan the hole backwards
1. Where do you want to putt from (usually below the hole)?
2. Which side of the green is the **safe miss** (no bunker, no water, room
   to chip)?
3. What approach distance do you like (a full wedge beats an awkward half shot)?
4. Which tee shot leaves that, with the least trouble in your dispersion
   ellipse?
- Fit your dispersion pattern in the widest safe area, not your
  "perfect shot" on the fairway centre.

## 3. Tee strategy
- Driver is right when the landing area is wide or the trouble is short of
  your carry; otherwise a 3-wood/hybrid/iron into the fat part.
- Aim away from the worst trouble: with OB left, tee up on the left side of
  the tee box and aim right-centre.
- A penalty (water/OB) costs a stroke or more; a shot in light rough
  usually costs a fraction of one.

## 4. Approach shots
- Aim at the **centre of the green** unless the flag is in an easy position
  and you have a short club. Flags tucked behind bunkers are for scratch
  players.
- **Plays-like distance:** adjust for elevation (roughly 1 m of club per
  1 m of height change is a rough rule), wind, temperature and lie.
- From trouble: take the **sure way back** to the fairway. Avoid "hero"
  shots through small gaps — expected cost of failure is large.

## 5. Par 5s and laying up
- Going for it only pays if the miss is safe. Otherwise lay up to your
  favourite full-wedge distance.

## 6. Scoring formats change strategy
- **Stroke play:** every stroke counts; avoid big numbers above all. A
  double bogey avoided beats a birdie chased.
- **Match play:** play the opponent. If they're in trouble, play safe; if
  they're close, you may need to attack. Concessions and hole-by-hole
  scoring make one blow-up hole cost only that hole.
- **Stableford:** points per hole vs par, adjusted by handicap strokes;
  once you can't score a point, **pick up** and save time. Encourages
  aggression with little downside on a bad hole.
- Know your **course handicap** and which holes you receive strokes on
  (stroke index on the scorecard).

## 7. Track it
Record per round: fairways, greens in regulation, penalties, up-and-downs,
putts. Strokes-gained apps show where shots are actually lost —
often penalties and approach shots, rather than putting.

## Gotchas
- Using best-ever distances.
- Firing at every flag.
- Ignoring the stroke index in net competitions.
