# Caddying — yardages, reads and player management

**Goal:** save your player shots and stress: right numbers, sensible
advice when asked, clean rules compliance and a calm presence. Applies to
club caddies (looping for members/guests) and tournament caddies.

## 1. Preparation
- Walk or study the course: landing areas, hazards, carries over trouble,
  false fronts, green slopes.
- **Yardage book:** many courses sell them; otherwise make notes per hole
  — distances from sprinkler heads/markers to front, centre and back,
  carries to bunkers and water.
- Pin positions: check the day's **pin sheet** (often paces from the front
  and from a side edge).
- Learn your player's **carry distances** per club and typical miss
  (ask at the range).

## 2. Giving numbers
- Give the **raw yardage** to front, pin and back, then the **plays-like**
  number with the adjustment reasons (uphill, wind, lie, temperature).
  E.g. "158 to the flag, plays 165 — into the wind, slightly uphill."
- Rangefinders and GPS devices are allowed for distance in most
  competitions (if a Local Rule permits); devices measuring slope or
  wind are usually not allowed in competition — check the event's rules.

## 3. Club and strategy advice
- Offer an opinion when asked or when it clearly matters ("Long is dead
  here"), then **back the player's decision fully**. Doubt at address costs
  more than a slightly wrong club.
- Know the player's tendencies: if they always come up short, nudge up a club.

## 4. Green reading
- Give your read from behind the ball or below the hole; agree the line
  together. Mention the speed (uphill/downhill, grain).
- **Rules:** a caddie must **not stand behind the player on the line of
  play** while the stroke is being made, and (since the 2019 rewrite) a caddie may not
  stand behind the player to help with alignment once they've begun taking
  their stance (Rule 10.2b(4); a player who backs away and restarts the stance avoids the penalty). Pointing out the line before the stroke is
  allowed, but you must not touch the putting green to indicate it.
  **(Check current Rules of Golf; amendments happen every few years.)**
- Only the player and their caddie/partner may give advice to the player
  (Rule 10.2a); don't advise opponents.

## 5. Bag and course duties
- Clean the ball (on the green after marking) and clubs; keep towel wet/dry.
- **Rake bunkers**, replace or fill divots, repair pitch marks.
- Tend or remove the **flagstick** as the player prefers (leaving it in is
  allowed since 2019). Lay it down off the line.
- Stand still and quiet during shots; keep your shadow off the line.
- Position the bag ahead toward the next tee to keep pace.
- Count the clubs before the round (max 14) — the player is penalised for
  extra clubs.
- Carry rain gear, umbrella, snacks and water; scorecard handled as asked.

## 6. Player management
- Read their mood: some want chat, some silence. Mirror and stay positive.
- After a bad shot, move on: next target, next number.
- Never blame the player or show frustration.
- Keep track of score in competitions and remind them of format specifics
  (e.g. handicap strokes in Stableford).

## 7. Professional basics
Punctual, tidy, phone silent. Tip customs vary by club and country; the
caddie master can tell you standard rates.

## Gotchas
- Giving the flag number without front/back when the green is deep.
- Breaching the alignment rule on putts (two-stroke penalty in stroke play).
- Carrying a 15th club.
- **Rules references based on the 2023 Rules of Golf; verify current edition.**

- [ ] Every approach given as raw + plays-like with reasons.
