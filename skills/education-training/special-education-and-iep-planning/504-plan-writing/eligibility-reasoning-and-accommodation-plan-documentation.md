# Eligibility reasoning and accommodation-plan documentation for Section 504

**Goal:** decide eligibility on the right legal standard, select accommodations that
remove the barrier the disability actually creates, and record it all so teachers
implement it and an Office for Civil Rights (OCR) reviewer would find it sound.

*US federal law; state and district procedures add detail. Not legal advice — check
your district's 504 procedures and OCR's current guidance.*

## 1. 504 vs IEP — know the difference
- **IDEA/IEP:** one of 13 disability categories **and** needs specially designed
  instruction. Provides special education + services.
- **Section 504:** any physical or mental impairment that **substantially limits one or
  more major life activities** (learning, reading, concentrating, thinking, breathing,
  eating, major bodily functions…). Provides accommodations and related aids/services
  so the student has equal access. Many students with ADHD, diabetes, severe allergies,
  anxiety, or a temporary but substantial injury fit here.
- A student found ineligible for an IEP should be **considered** for 504, not dropped.

## 2. Eligibility reasoning (write it down step by step)
1. **Impairment?** Documented by evaluation data from varied sources — grades, teacher
   observations, medical information, behaviour records, parent input. A medical
   diagnosis helps but is neither required nor sufficient.
2. **Which major life activity?** Name it.
3. **Substantially limits?** Compare to most students in the general population. Since the
   ADAAA (2008), "substantially limits" is **not a demanding standard**; construe broadly.
4. **Ignore mitigating measures** (medication, insulin pump, learned compensatory
   strategies) when judging limitation — *except ordinary eyeglasses/contacts*.
5. **Episodic or in remission** conditions (epilepsy, asthma, depression) count if
   substantially limiting when active.
6. **Good grades don't rule it out.** A bright student with diabetes or ADHD can still be
   substantially limited in a major life activity.
Decisions are made by a **group of knowledgeable people** (about the student, the
evaluation data and placement options) — not one person.

## 3. Choosing accommodations
For each limitation ask: *what barrier does this create in this setting, and what removes
it without lowering the standard?*

| Limitation | Example accommodations |
|---|---|
| Concentrating (ADHD) | preferential seating, chunked assignments, check-ins, extended time *if* data show slowed processing |
| Eating / major bodily functions (diabetes) | access to snacks, water, nurse, blood-glucose checks in class, exam retake if hypo/hyperglycaemic |
| Breathing / food allergy | allergen-safe areas, staff trained on epinephrine, field-trip planning |
| Anxiety | quiet testing location, advance notice of changes, a pass to a designated adult |

- Keep it **individual**; avoid a menu of 15 generic items nobody tracks.
- Accommodations change *access*, not *content* — modifications that lower standards are
  normally an IDEA matter (see
  `education-training.special-education-and-iep-planning.accommodation-planning.choosing-accommodations-vs-modifications-for-inclusion`).
- Note which accommodations also apply to **state tests and college-entrance exams**
  (those need separate approval from the test owner).

## 4. Documentation that holds up
Record: meeting date and attendees; data sources reviewed; the eligibility reasoning
for steps 1–5; each accommodation with **who is responsible** and **in which settings**
(classes, tests, field trips, extracurriculars, transport); how teachers are notified;
review date; parent notice of **procedural safeguards** and right to an impartial hearing.
Pair medical needs with an individual health plan from the nurse.

## Gotchas
- **"Has a diagnosis, so gets a 504"** or **"doing fine academically, so no 504"** —
  both skip the actual analysis.
- **Plan on paper, not in the classroom.** The most common OCR complaint pattern is
  non-implementation; confirm each teacher has acknowledged the plan.
- Forgetting non-academic settings (lunch, PE, trips, clubs).
- No periodic review — re-evaluate before significant changes of placement and at the
  district's review interval (often yearly; federal law requires "periodic").
- Retaliation against families who request evaluation is prohibited.

## Verify
- [ ] Eligibility notes name the impairment, the major life activity and why the
      limitation is substantial, without counting mitigating measures.
- [ ] Each accommodation ties to a named limitation and lists responsible staff and settings.
- [ ] Teachers have received and acknowledged the plan.
- [ ] Parents received procedural safeguards; a review date is set.
