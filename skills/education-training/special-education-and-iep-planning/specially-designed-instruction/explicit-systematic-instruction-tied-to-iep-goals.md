# Explicit, systematic instruction tied to IEP goals

**Goal:** instruction that is *different* from what the general classroom already offers
— adapted in content, method or delivery to the student's disability (34 CFR
§300.39) — and that visibly moves the student toward their IEP goal.

## 1. From goal to teachable steps (task analysis)
1. Take the annual goal (e.g. "solve two-step word problems with 80% accuracy").
2. List every sub-skill and step an expert does without thinking: read the problem,
   identify the question, find relevant numbers, choose operations, write the equation,
   compute, check against the question.
3. Pre-test each step; **teach from the first failed step**.
4. For functional/life skills (hand-washing, making a purchase), write the chain as
   10–15 observable steps and teach by **forward or backward chaining** (backward:
   you do all but the last step, the student finishes and gets the natural reward).

## 2. The explicit lesson
| Phase | What happens |
|---|---|
| Review | quick retrieval of prerequisite and previously taught steps |
| State the goal | "Today you'll learn to find the question in a word problem." |
| **I do** (model) | think aloud, one clear strategy, consistent language, several examples |
| **We do** (guided) | student does steps with you; prompts and immediate feedback |
| **You do** (independent) | student works alone; you watch for errors |
| Check | 3–5 items scored → data point for the goal |

Keep it **systematic**: a planned sequence of skills, small steps, examples ordered from
clear to ambiguous, and **non-examples** to sharpen the concept.

## 3. High response rates
- Aim for many **opportunities to respond** — several per minute in drill segments:
  choral response, response cards, whiteboards, pointing.
- **Affirm correct responses briefly**; **correct errors immediately**: model the
  correct answer → have the student do it → retest later in the lesson.
- Aim for high accuracy during guided practice (practitioners often cite ~80–90%+
  before moving to independent). *(Draft: ballpark figure, not a fixed rule.)*

## 4. Prompting and fading
Choose a **prompt hierarchy** and fade deliberately:
- **Most-to-least** (full physical → partial → model → gestural → verbal → independent)
  for new skills where errors are costly — closer to errorless learning.
- **Least-to-most** for skills partly known: wait → verbal → gesture → model → physical.
- **Time delay**: give the prompt immediately at first, then wait 3–5 s before prompting
  so the student can respond independently.
Record the prompt level on each trial; mastery means *independent* performance.

## 5. Structured literacy example
For a student with a reading disability, SDI is often a structured-literacy programme
(Orton-Gillingham-based or similar) delivered with fidelity: explicit phonology,
sound-symbol, syllable, morphology, syntax and semantics, cumulative review, multisensory
practice. See
`education-training.tutoring-and-subject-instruction.literacy-and-reading-tutoring.phonics-decoding-and-fluency-intervention`.
Use the published programme's lesson plan; don't improvise its sequence.

## 6. Fidelity and data
- **Fidelity checklist** for the programme/lesson (components delivered, minutes,
  group size). Low fidelity explains flat data more often than the student does.
- Graph goal data weekly and apply decision rules (see
  `education-training.special-education-and-iep-planning.progress-monitoring.cbm-data-collection-and-decision-rules`).
- **Minutes delivered vs IEP minutes** — missed sessions can be a compliance issue.

## Gotchas
- **Re-teaching the gen-ed lesson slower** is not SDI. Something about method or content
  must change.
- **Homework help as "resource room"** time — common, and usually not goal-aligned.
- **Prompt dependence** — never fading the adult support.
- **Skipping the model** because the student "should know this".
- Group size creep: an intervention validated at 1:3 often fails at 1:8.

## Verify
- [ ] The goal is task-analysed and teaching starts at the first failed step.
- [ ] Lessons include model, guided and independent phases with error correction.
- [ ] Students make frequent active responses in every lesson.
- [ ] Prompt levels are recorded and faded toward independence.
- [ ] Fidelity, minutes delivered and goal data are logged weekly.
