# CBM data collection and decision rules for IEP progress monitoring

**Goal:** know within weeks — not at the annual review — whether instruction is working,
and change it when it isn't.

## 1. Choose a measure that fits the goal
Curriculum-based measures are short, standardised, sensitive to growth and have
equivalent alternate forms:
| Skill | Typical CBM |
|---|---|
| Early reading | letter-sound fluency, nonsense-word fluency |
| Reading (gr 1–6ish) | oral reading fluency (1 min, words correct per minute) |
| Reading comprehension (older) | maze (choose correct word every ~7th word, 2.5–3 min) |
| Math | computation or concepts-and-applications probes (digits/problems correct) |
| Writing | correct word sequences in a 3-min write |
Use a published system (DIBELS 8, Acadience, aimsweb Plus, FastBridge, easyCBM) — they
provide alternate forms and norms; check the **NCII tools chart** for evidence ratings.
For behaviour or functional goals, design your own: operational definition + frequency,
duration or percentage-of-opportunities recording sheet.

## 2. Baseline and goal
- **Baseline:** median of 3 probes given in one week (reduces day-to-day noise).
- **Goal:** baseline + (weeks to goal × target weekly rate of improvement). Use the
  publisher's growth norms; *ambitious* ROI for students receiving intensive
  intervention. Or use an end-of-year benchmark for the student's instructional level.
- Measure at the student's **instructional level** if grade-level probes are at floor,
  but plan to move toward grade level.

## 3. Collect consistently
- **Weekly** (at least every other week) for intensive goals; same conditions, same
  directions, same administrator where possible.
- Score immediately; plot on a graph with **weeks on x** and score on y; draw the
  **aimline** from baseline to goal.
- One person owns the data; schedule it like instruction.

## 4. Decision rules
After enough points (often 6–8+; *NCII and publishers differ on the minimum*):
- **Four-point rule:** 4 consecutive points **below** the aimline → change instruction.
  4 consecutive **above** → raise the goal. Mixed → keep going.
- **Trend-line rule:** fit a trend line (ordinary least squares or Tukey method) through
  the most recent 6–8 points. Trend flatter than aimline → change instruction; steeper →
  raise the goal.
- Draw a **vertical phase-change line** on the graph whenever instruction changes and
  label it, so later data can be read against the change.
- "Change instruction" means a real adjustment: more time, smaller group, different
  programme, more explicit modelling, more practice — and check fidelity first (see
  `education-training.special-education-and-iep-planning.specially-designed-instruction.explicit-systematic-instruction-tied-to-iep-goals`).

## 5. Report progress
- Report on the schedule written in the IEP (usually each grading period).
- For each goal: current score vs baseline vs goal, the graph, **on track / not on
  track**, and what you're doing about it. Plain language for families: "Sam read 58
  words correctly in a minute, up from 42 in September; to reach 75 by June he needs
  about 1.5 more words each week — he's on track."
- Avoid "progressing" without numbers.

## Gotchas
- **Using unit tests or grades** as progress monitoring — they change difficulty and
  content each time, so a trend means nothing.
- **Too few data points** before deciding, or the reverse: 20 flat points and no change.
- Probes practised in class → inflated scores (don't teach the probes).
- Changing two things at once; you won't know what worked.
- Graph scale tricks: keep the y-axis consistent across reports.

## Verify
- [ ] Each goal has a measure with alternate forms (or a defined behaviour recording).
- [ ] Baseline is the median of 3 probes; the aimline is drawn on the graph.
- [ ] Data are collected at least every two weeks under standard conditions.
- [ ] A named decision rule was applied and instruction changes are marked on the graph.
- [ ] Parent reports give numbers, the graph and an on-track judgement.
