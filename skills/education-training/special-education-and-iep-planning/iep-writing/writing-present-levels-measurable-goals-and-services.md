# Writing present levels, measurable goals and services in an IEP

**Goal:** an IEP in which every need found in the evaluation leads to a present-levels
statement, a measurable goal, and a service that will plausibly achieve it — "reasonably
calculated to enable a child to make progress appropriate in light of the child's
circumstances" (*Endrew F.*, 2017).

*Scope: US IDEA (34 CFR §300.320). Other countries (e.g. UK EHC plans, Australian
individual learning plans) differ in law and format. State forms and rules vary — use
your state/district form and check state regulations; this page is not legal advice.*

## 1. Build the need list from the evaluation
Read the evaluation report(s) and pull every area of need (academic, communication,
behaviour, motor, adaptive, social-emotional), with the **specific scores and
observations**. Add teacher data, work samples, progress-monitoring data and parent input.
Each need must be addressed somewhere in the IEP — by a goal, a service, an accommodation,
or a stated reason it isn't.

## 2. Present levels (PLAAFP)
For each area write:
1. **Current performance in measurable terms** — "Reads 42 words correct per minute on
   grade-3 passages (grade-level benchmark ~ 90 for this season)", not "reads below grade
   level".
2. **Strengths** and what works for the student.
3. **How the disability affects involvement and progress in the general curriculum.**
4. Parent concerns.
The present-level number becomes the goal's **baseline**. No baseline → the goal can't
be measured.

## 3. Measurable annual goals
Formula: **Given [condition], [student] will [observable behaviour] [criterion] [measured
how], by [date].**

> Given an unpractised grade-3 passage, Sam will read aloud at 75 words correct per minute
> with ≥95% accuracy, measured by weekly 1-minute CBM probes, by the annual review date.

- Observable verbs (read, write, solve, initiate), never "understand", "improve", "be aware".
- **Ambitious but reachable** — use growth-rate norms where they exist (see
  `education-training.special-education-and-iep-planning.progress-monitoring.cbm-data-collection-and-decision-rules`).
- Short-term objectives/benchmarks are **required only for students taking alternate
  assessments** under federal law; some states require them for all.
- State **how and when progress will be reported** to parents (e.g. each grading period).
- "Stranger test": could a new teacher measure it tomorrow without asking you?

## 4. Services, supports and minutes
List each with **who / what / how much / where / start–end date**:
- **Special education** (specially designed instruction): "Reading SDI, 30 min × 5/week,
  special-education setting."
- **Related services** (speech-language, OT, counselling, transport…).
- **Supplementary aids and services**, program modifications, supports for school staff.
- **Accommodations** for instruction and for state/district assessments (and whether
  the student takes the alternate assessment, with justification).
Services must be tied to goals; a goal with no service delivering it is a red flag.

## 5. Placement / LRE
Start from the general-education classroom with supports and move to more restrictive
settings only as needed. **Explain the extent, if any, to which the student will not
participate with non-disabled peers**, and why supports couldn't make the general setting
work. Placement is decided by the team after the services, not before.

## 6. Other required pieces (check your form)
Transition plan from age 16 at the latest (earlier in some states; see
`education-training.special-education-and-iep-planning.transition-planning.writing-measurable-postsecondary-goals-and-transition-services`),
special factors (behaviour, limited English, blind/deaf, communication, assistive tech),
extended school year consideration, and **prior written notice** for proposals/refusals.

## Gotchas
- **Copy-paste goals** from last year with new dates and no new baseline.
- **Services by availability** ("we only have a group on Tuesdays") instead of need.
- Vague present levels ("struggles with math") make every downstream piece unmeasurable.
- Writing the IEP fully before the meeting and presenting it as final ("predetermination")
  can deny parents meaningful participation — bring a **draft**, labelled as such.
- Timelines (annual review, triennial re-evaluation, days to implement) are legal
  deadlines; track them.

## Verify
- [ ] Every evaluated need maps to a goal, service or accommodation (or a stated reason).
- [ ] Each goal has a baseline, condition, observable behaviour, criterion, measure and date.
- [ ] Services state minutes, frequency, setting, provider and dates, and link to goals.
- [ ] LRE explanation justifies any time away from non-disabled peers.
- [ ] Draft was shared with parents before the meeting, marked as a draft.
