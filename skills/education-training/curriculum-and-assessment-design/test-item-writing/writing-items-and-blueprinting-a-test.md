# Writing items and blueprinting a test

**Goal:** a test whose questions sample what was taught in the right proportions, and whose
items measure the target knowledge — not reading speed, test-wiseness or luck.

## 1. Blueprint first (table of specifications)
Rows = content areas or objectives; columns = cognitive level; cells = number of items or points.
| Content | Recall | Apply | Analyse | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cell structure | 3 | 4 | 1 | 8 (20%) |
| Transport | 2 | 6 | 4 | 12 (30%) |
| … | | | | |
Set the weights from teaching time and importance, and agree them before writing items. The
blueprint stops you writing 30 easy recall questions because they're quick to write.
Decide formats per cell: multiple choice for breadth, constructed response for explanation and
multi-step reasoning.

## 2. Writing multiple-choice items
**Stem**
- Put the **whole question in the stem**; a student should be able to answer it with options covered
  ("cover-the-options" test).
- One clear problem per item; include only necessary information (for application items, a short
  scenario is the necessary information).
- Phrase positively. If you must use NOT/EXCEPT, bold or capitalise it — and use sparingly.
**Options**
- **3 options is often enough**; research suggests three plausible options perform about as well as
  four or five with weak fillers (Rodriguez, 2005). Use 4 if all are plausible.
- **Distractors = real errors**: the answer you get from a common misconception or slip.
- Options similar in length, grammar and detail; the key shouldn't be the longest or most qualified.
- Avoid "all of the above"; use "none of the above" rarely and never as a filler.
- Order numbers logically (ascending) and other options randomly or alphabetically; check the key
  position isn't predictable across the test.
- One defensibly best answer — have a colleague confirm.

## 3. Writing constructed-response items
- State the task, the expected scope ("in 3–5 sentences", "show your working") and the marks.
- Write the **mark scheme at the same time**: acceptable answers, alternatives, partial credit
  points, and common wrong answers that earn nothing.
- Avoid optional questions ("answer 3 of 5") if scores must be comparable.
- Try the item yourself and time it.

## 4. Common item flaws (check every item)
- Grammatical cues (stem ends in "an", only one option starts with a vowel).
- Repeating a word from the stem only in the key.
- Absolute words ("always", "never") in distractors that test-wise students eliminate.
- Overlapping options (e.g. "less than 10" and "less than 20").
- Window dressing — long, irrelevant stories that test reading load.
- One item giving away the answer to another.

## 5. Fairness and accessibility review
- **Bias/sensitivity:** contexts that assume culture-specific knowledge (sports rules, holidays,
  idioms) unless that knowledge is the construct; stereotyped roles; upsetting topics.
- **Language load:** plain vocabulary and short sentences when language isn't what's being tested —
  especially for multilingual learners.
- **Accessibility:** readable font and layout, alt text for diagrams in digital tests, no meaning
  carried by colour alone; check accommodations (extra time, readers) are compatible.

## 6. Assemble and pilot
Order roughly easy → hard within sections, check timing against the blueprint, produce a key and
mark scheme, and have a colleague take the test blind. After first use, run item analysis (see
education-training.curriculum-and-assessment-design.item-analysis-and-psychometrics.classical-item-analysis-for-teacher-made-tests).

## Gotchas
- Writing items from the textbook's wording tests recognition of phrasing, not understanding.
- Recycled items circulate among students; rotate or bank them.
- *Uncertainty:* guidance on the ideal number of options and on "none of the above" differs across
  sources; follow your exam body's style guide where one exists.

## Verify
- [ ] A blueprint with weights by content and cognitive level was agreed before writing.
- [ ] Each MCQ passes the cover-the-options test and has error-based distractors.
- [ ] Constructed-response items have a mark scheme written alongside them.
- [ ] Every item was checked against the flaw list and a bias/language review.
- [ ] A colleague took the test blind and agreed with the key.
