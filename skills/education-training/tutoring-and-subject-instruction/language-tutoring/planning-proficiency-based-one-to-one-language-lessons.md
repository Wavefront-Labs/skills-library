# Planning proficiency-based one-to-one language lessons

**Goal:** move a learner measurably up a proficiency level by planning each lesson
around what they need to *do* in the language, not around the next grammar chapter.

## 1. Place the learner (first lesson)
- **Needs analysis:** why they're learning (travel, work meetings, exam, family),
  deadlines, time available per week, past study.
- **Placement:** short conversation that escalates in difficulty (self-introduction →
  describe a past event → give and defend an opinion → hypotheticals). Note where
  fluency or accuracy collapses. Add a short reading/listening task.
- Map to the **CEFR** (A1–C2; the 2020 Companion Volume adds Pre-A1 and "plus" levels).
  Use its **can-do descriptors** — e.g. B1: "can deal with most situations likely to arise
  while travelling"; B2: "can interact with a degree of fluency and spontaneity".
  US contexts often use ACTFL levels instead; *exact CEFR↔ACTFL alignment is approximate*.
- Set a goal as 3–5 target can-do statements for the next ~10 lessons.

## 2. Rough guided-hours expectations
Moving one CEFR band commonly takes on the order of **100–200 guided hours** at lower
levels and more at higher ones, varying hugely with language distance (an English speaker
learning Japanese vs Spanish) and self-study. *Treat these as ballpark figures from
publisher/exam-board guidance, not guarantees*; say so to the learner.

## 3. Lesson shape (60 minutes)
| Min | Stage | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 | Warm-up chat, recycle last lesson's words | retrieval |
| 5–15 | **Input**: short text/audio at or just above level | comprehensible input, noticing |
| 15–35 | **Task**: a real communicative goal (plan a trip, complain about a bill, pitch an idea) | fluency, meaning first |
| 35–50 | **Focus on form**: the 1–2 language gaps the task exposed | accuracy |
| 50–58 | Re-do or vary the task | use the new form |
| 58–60 | Homework + words to the learner's SRS deck | spacing |

This is task-based (TBLT) ordering: grammar arises from the need. For beginners or
exam-driven learners a more structured **present–practise–produce (PPP)** sequence is
fine; most tutors blend both.

## 4. Error correction — selective and planned
- During **fluency tasks**, don't interrupt; note errors and do delayed feedback.
- During **accuracy work**, correct immediately. Prefer **prompts** that make the learner
  self-repair — elicitation ("Yesterday I…?"), metalinguistic clue ("past tense?"),
  clarification request — over **recasts** (just saying it correctly), which learners often
  don't notice (Lyster & Ranta).
- Correct **patterns that impede meaning or recur**, not every slip.
- Keep a running **error log** per learner; recycle into future focus-on-form.

## 5. Vocabulary and pronunciation
- High-frequency words first; learners need roughly the 2,000–3,000 most frequent word
  families for everyday conversation (Nation; *estimates vary by study*).
- Teach chunks ("Would you mind if…"), not isolated words.
- Pronunciation: prioritise features that block intelligibility (stress, key vowel
  contrasts) over accent; use **minimal pairs** (ship/sheep) and record-and-compare.

## Gotchas
- **Tutor talking time.** Aim for the learner speaking well over half the lesson; record
  one lesson and check.
- **Endless free conversation** feels pleasant but plateaus — add a task and a focus.
- **Teaching grammar the learner can't yet use** (natural acquisition orders exist, e.g.
  third-person -s in English is acquired late regardless of teaching).
- **No measurement.** Re-run can-do checks every ~10 lessons, or use a practice exam
  (DELE, DELF, IELTS, JLPT etc.) for exam learners.
- Online platforms (Preply, italki) take commissions and have their own rules; not
  covered here.

## Verify
- [ ] Learner has a CEFR (or ACTFL) placement and 3–5 written can-do targets.
- [ ] Each lesson includes input, a meaningful task and targeted focus on form.
- [ ] Correction during fluency work is delayed; accuracy work uses self-repair prompts.
- [ ] An error log and vocabulary deck exist and are recycled.
- [ ] Progress is rechecked against can-do statements on a fixed cadence.
