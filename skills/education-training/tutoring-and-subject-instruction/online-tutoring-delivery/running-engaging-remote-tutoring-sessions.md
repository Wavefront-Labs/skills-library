# Running engaging remote tutoring sessions

**Goal:** an online session where the student does as much thinking and writing as in
person, the tech never eats more than a minute, and both of you are protected.

## 1. Kit (tutor side)
- **Wired internet** if possible; otherwise sit near the router. Close sync/backup apps.
- **Headset or decent USB mic** — audio quality matters more than video. Echo usually
  means someone isn't on headphones.
- **Pen input** for maths/science: a graphics tablet (e.g. small Wacom/XP-Pen), an iPad
  with stylus joined as a second device, or a **document camera** over paper. Writing with
  a mouse is too slow to model working.
- Light in front of you, camera at eye level, neutral background.

## 2. The shared workspace
- Use a **collaborative whiteboard** both can write on (Bitpaper, Miro, Excalidraw,
  Zoom Whiteboard, or the platform's own) rather than only screen-sharing your notes —
  the student must hold the pen.
- Pre-load each board with the session's problems or a PDF page so you don't waste time
  typing. Keep one board per student so past work is reviewable.
- Subject tools: **Desmos** (graphing), **GeoGebra**, **PhET** simulations, Google Docs
  with suggestion mode for writing tutoring.
- Have a fallback: if the whiteboard fails, student writes on paper and holds it to the
  camera or sends a phone photo.

## 3. Session structure for remote
| Stage | Remote-specific move |
|---|---|
| Before | Send the link + board link 24h ahead; test call on the first session |
| 0–3 min | Tech check: "Can you hear me, see the board, write on it?" |
| Body | Change activity every 8–12 min; student writes, you annotate in another colour |
| Last 3 min | Student summarises; you export/save the board and share it |

## 4. Engagement at a distance
You lose body language, so check explicitly and often:
- **Ask for output every few minutes** — "Write the next step on the board", "Type your
  answer in the chat before you say it" (everyone answers, not just whoever's fastest in
  small groups).
- Use **names** and direct questions; avoid "Any questions?" (silence is the default).
- **Watch the board cursor** — an idle cursor is a disengaged student.
- **Cameras:** ask for camera on for one-to-one if policy and student comfort allow; don't
  make it a battle. Some platforms' attention features are unreliable — trust output.
- Thumbs-up/down reactions as quick confidence checks; follow a thumbs-down immediately.
- Shorter sessions (45 min) often work better than 60 for younger students online.
  *(Draft: common practitioner advice, not a tested threshold.)*

## 5. Connection problems
- Lag on video → turn off video first, keep audio + board.
- Audio fails → move to phone audio (dial-in) while keeping the board.
- Agree up front: if disconnected, both rejoin the same link; after 5 minutes of failure,
  reschedule (state the policy in writing).

## 6. Safeguarding and professionalism (minors)
- Follow your organisation's or local child-protection rules. Typical expectations:
  parental consent for online sessions, a parent/guardian nearby or the door open, the
  child in a shared space rather than a bedroom where possible, **no private messaging on
  personal social accounts**, sessions on the organisation's accounts.
- **Recording:** only with explicit consent and a clear storage/retention policy; laws on
  recording minors and data protection (e.g. GDPR, COPPA, FERPA where applicable)
  vary — *check your jurisdiction*.
- Disable "allow participants to share screen/annotate" in group sessions until needed,
  and use a waiting room or passcode.
- Never ask a student to install remote-control software on a family device.

## Gotchas
- **Tutor monologue over a shared PDF** — the most common failure of online tutoring.
- **Too many tools.** One platform + one board; more apps = more login failures.
- Time zones and daylight-saving changes cause missed sessions: state times with zone.
- Browser permissions (mic/camera) are the top first-session issue; test before.

## Verify
- [ ] A test call was done; audio is clean with headset.
- [ ] The student wrote on the shared board in every session segment.
- [ ] Engagement was checked by student output at least every ~5 minutes.
- [ ] Disconnection and rescheduling policy is written and agreed.
- [ ] Safeguarding, consent and any recording comply with your organisation's policy.
