# Phonics, decoding and fluency intervention in reading tutoring

**Goal:** find which part of reading is breaking down, teach it explicitly and in sequence,
and measure that the student reads more accurately and fluently each few weeks.

*For adult beginners see
`languages-linguistics.literacy-and-reading.adult-literacy.tutoring-adult-beginner-readers-one-to-one`.*

## 1. Place the student (simple view of reading)
Reading comprehension ≈ **decoding × language comprehension**. Quickly test both:
- **Decoding:** a nonsense-word list (e.g. *vop, slint, crade*) — real words can be
  memorised, nonsense words can't. Note which patterns fail (short vowels, blends,
  digraphs, vowel teams, silent-e, multisyllable).
- **Phonemic awareness:** "Say *stop* without the /t/." Weak oral sound manipulation
  needs work before or alongside letters.
- **Language comprehension:** read a passage *to* them and ask questions. Good listening
  comprehension + poor reading = decoding problem. Poor both = vocabulary/language too.
- **Fluency baseline:** 1 minute on an unpractised grade-level passage; count words
  correct per minute (WCPM) and accuracy %. Compare to published norms
  (Hasbrouck & Tindal) — *check the current table for the grade and season*.

## 2. Teach decoding explicitly
- **Follow a scope and sequence** (any published structured-literacy one): single
  consonants and short vowels → CVC → digraphs (sh, ch, th) → blends → silent-e →
  vowel teams → r-controlled → syllable types → morphology (prefixes, suffixes, roots).
- **Lesson pattern (~20 min of a 45–60 min session):**
  1. Phonemic warm-up (oral blending/segmenting, no print).
  2. Review cards of known sound–spellings (fast, mixed).
  3. Introduce one new pattern: say it, show it, model blending ("/sh/ /i/ /p/ → ship").
  4. Word reading with the pattern, then mixed with earlier patterns.
  5. **Spelling/dictation** of the same words — encoding cements decoding.
  6. Read a **decodable text** that uses only taught patterns.
- **Irregular "heart words"** (said, was, of): mark the regular parts, have them learn by
  heart only the odd part. Don't teach them as whole-word pictures.
- **Correct errors on the spot, at the sound level:** point to the missed grapheme, "What
  sound?", then reblend. Don't say "sound it out" generically, and **don't prompt guessing
  from the picture or first letter** — that rehearses the wrong habit.

## 3. Build fluency
- **Repeated reading:** same short passage 3–4 times across a session; chart WCPM each time.
- **Echo/paired reading:** you model a sentence with expression, they repeat.
- Use text the student reads at ~95%+ accuracy; below ~90% is frustration level and
  becomes a decoding lesson, not fluency practice.
- Fluency is the bridge to comprehension — an accurate but laborious reader has no
  working memory left for meaning.

## 4. Add comprehension
Once decoding is fairly automatic: pre-teach key vocabulary, ask the student to predict,
stop to summarise each paragraph in one sentence, and ask inference questions ("How do you
know she was angry?"). Read richer texts aloud *to* them to keep vocabulary growing past
their decoding level.

## Gotchas
- **Treating all poor readers as phonics cases.** Some decode fine and fail on vocabulary
  or background knowledge — the placement step exists to catch this.
- **Levelled (predictable) books for beginners** encourage guessing; use decodables until
  the pattern set is broad.
- **Skipping dictation.** Spelling practice is half of the intervention.
- **Not measuring.** Re-test WCPM on a fresh passage every 2–4 weeks; flat data after
  ~6–8 weeks of good-fidelity tutoring should prompt a referral for evaluation
  (dyslexia, language disorder, hearing/vision). *Draft: these intervals are common
  practice, not a fixed standard.*
- Don't diagnose dyslexia yourself; describe what you observed and refer.

## Verify
- [ ] You know whether the gap is decoding, language comprehension or both.
- [ ] Lessons follow a written scope and sequence and include dictation.
- [ ] Error correction is at the grapheme level, never "guess from the picture".
- [ ] Practice texts are decodable or ≥95% accurate for this student.
- [ ] WCPM and accuracy are charted over time and the trend is up.
