# Diagnostic baseline and score-improvement plan for test-prep tutoring

**Goal:** turn a target score and a test date into a weekly plan that spends time where
points are actually lost, and show the student evidence it is working.

## 1. Get the facts first
- **Which test, which version, which date**, and the score target (from the student's
  target schools/programmes, not a vague "as high as possible").
- **Know the current format.** Formats change: the SAT went fully digital and
  section-adaptive in 2024 (two modules per section; module 2 difficulty depends on
  module 1; scored 400–1600) and is taken in the **Bluebook** app. The ACT announced a
  shorter test with Science optional from 2025. *Re-check the official site before each
  new student — do not rely on old books.*
- Use **official** practice tests for diagnostics; third-party tests often mis-calibrate
  difficulty and timing.

## 2. Diagnostic baseline
- Full-length, **timed, realistic conditions** (same device type, breaks as on test day).
- Record: score per section, time remaining per section, and the student's confidence
  on each question if you can (circle "guessed").
- **Classify every miss** in an error log:

| Cause | Fix |
|---|---|
| Content gap (doesn't know rule/concept) | teach + targeted drill |
| Misread / careless | process habits (underline the ask, re-read answer vs question) |
| Strategy (got lost, wrong approach) | model the method, practise approach selection |
| Timing (ran out, rushed last third) | pacing plan |
| Guessed right | count as a miss for planning |

## 3. Prioritise by yield
Rank gaps by **frequency on the test × number of misses × ease of fixing**. A few
recurring content areas (e.g. on SAT Math: linear equations and functions; on
Reading & Writing: standard English conventions like punctuation) usually give more
points than rare hard topics. On adaptive tests, accuracy on the first module matters
because it gates access to the harder, higher-scoring second module. *Exact scoring
algorithms aren't published.*

## 4. The plan (example: 10 weeks, 2 sessions + homework/week)
- Weeks 1–6: 2–3 priority content areas per week; each session = teach → drill
  question sets of mixed difficulty → review misses into the error log.
- **Every 2 weeks:** a full timed practice test. Review *every* miss and every guess.
- Weeks 7–9: mixed timed sections, pacing drills, second-tier gaps.
- Week 10: one final full test ~1 week out, then light review; protect sleep the last
  nights. No new topics in the last few days.
- Review the error log at the start of every session — re-test the items that were
  missed (retrieval practice is the main engine of retention).

## 5. Strategy and pacing
- Compute **seconds per question** per section and set checkpoints ("question 10 by
  minute 12").
- **Two-pass approach** where the format allows: do sure items, flag hard ones, return.
- **Never leave blanks** when there's no penalty for wrong answers (true for the current
  SAT and ACT — *check for your test*; some older/other exams penalise guessing).
- Process of elimination; on the digital SAT, use the built-in strikethrough and
  graphing calculator (Desmos) — practise with them.

## Gotchas
- **More practice tests ≠ improvement.** Untaken lessons from the last test are wasted.
- **Ignoring careless errors** — they're often the cheapest points.
- **Chasing the hardest problems** while easy-medium accuracy is under ~90%.
- **Overpromising points.** Gains vary; don't guarantee a number. *(Draft: typical gains
  are not stated here because published figures vary and are often vendor-sourced.)*
- **Test anxiety:** normalise it, rehearse test-day logistics, and refer on if severe.
- Accommodations (extended time) must be applied for through the test maker, well in
  advance.

## Verify
- [ ] Format and dates were checked on the official site this cycle.
- [ ] Diagnostic was full-length, timed and official.
- [ ] Every miss and guess is classified in an error log.
- [ ] The weekly plan targets the highest-yield gaps first.
- [ ] Full timed tests every ~2 weeks show the trend; pacing checkpoints are met.
