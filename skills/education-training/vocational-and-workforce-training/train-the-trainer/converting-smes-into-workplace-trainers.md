# Converting SMEs into workplace trainers

**Goal:** an expert who can *do* the job becomes someone who can *teach* it — designs a
focused session, facilitates rather than lectures, and is checked against a standard
before training others alone.

## 1. Know the main problem: expert blind spot
Experts compress steps they no longer notice and overestimate what novices can absorb.
Typical symptoms: skipping "obvious" steps, jargon, too much content, telling war stories
instead of practice, and "just watch me". The programme exists to fix this.

## 2. Programme shape (example: 2–3 days + follow-up)
| Block | Content |
|---|---|
| Unpacking expertise | task analysis of one job they'll teach; list steps, key points, common novice errors |
| Session design | objectives, structure, activities, checks |
| Facilitation | questioning, managing groups, feedback, demonstrations |
| Microteaching ×2 | 10–15 min teach-back, filmed, peer + coach feedback, re-teach |
| Certification | observed live session against a rubric |
*(Length is illustrative; complex or safety-critical roles need more.)*

## 3. Unpacking expertise
- Have the SME **do the task while a novice or colleague writes every step**; the SME is
  usually surprised by the count.
- Ask "What do new people get wrong?" and "How do you know it's right?" — these give the
  key points and checks.
- For procedural skills, use the TWI job-breakdown format (see
  `education-training.vocational-and-workforce-training.on-the-job-training.job-breakdowns-coached-practice-and-sign-off`).

## 4. Session design (one page)
- **Objectives** in performance terms: *audience, behaviour, condition, degree* (ABCD) —
  "Given a fault log, new technicians will identify the failed component within 10
  minutes."
- **Cut content** to what the objective needs; move the rest to a reference sheet.
- Structure: hook/why it matters → short input (≤10–15 min chunks) → demonstration →
  practice → check → summary. Plan **at least half the time for learner activity**.
- Include a **check** that proves the objective was met.

## 5. Facilitation skills to practise
- **Questioning:** open questions, then wait 5+ seconds; redirect answers to the group.
- **Adult learners** want relevance and to draw on their experience — ask before telling.
- **Giving feedback:** specific, behavioural, timely; one improvement at a time.
- **Group management:** dominant talkers, silent participants, sceptics ("What would make
  this work in your area?").
- **Demonstration technique** (see
  `education-training.vocational-and-workforce-training.technical-skills-instruction.demonstration-supervised-practice-and-error-correction-in-the-shop`).

## 6. Microteaching and feedback
- Each SME teaches a 10–15 min segment to peers acting as novices; record it.
- Feedback in order: self-review first ("What would you change?"), then peers, then coach,
  using the same rubric as certification.
- **Re-teach** the same segment with changes — the second run is where learning shows.

## 7. Trainer certification and follow-up
- Observe a real session with a rubric: objectives stated, content focused, learner
  practice time, checks of understanding, accurate content, safety modelled, feedback
  quality.
- Co-train with an experienced trainer first, then solo with observation, then periodic
  peer observation (e.g. annually).
- Look at **learner outcomes** too (sign-off pass rates, errors after training).

## Gotchas
- Selecting trainers purely on technical excellence; some top performers dislike teaching.
- No protected time to prepare — sessions revert to slide-reading.
- Slide decks inherited from the previous trainer with no objectives.
- Feedback that's only praise ("Great job!") changes nothing.
- Certification as attendance rather than observed performance.

## Verify
- [ ] Each SME produced a task breakdown with common novice errors.
- [ ] Each has a one-page session plan with ABCD objectives and a check.
- [ ] At least half of planned time is learner activity.
- [ ] Each did two microteaching rounds with rubric-based feedback.
- [ ] Certification is by observed live session; follow-up observation is scheduled.
