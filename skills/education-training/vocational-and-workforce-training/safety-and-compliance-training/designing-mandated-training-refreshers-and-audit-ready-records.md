# Designing mandated training, refreshers and audit-ready records

**Goal:** everyone gets the training the law and your risks require, actually changes how
they work, and you can prove it to an inspector in minutes.

*For site inductions and toolbox-talk delivery see
`law-government.occupational-health-and-safety-practice.safety-training-and-culture.inductions-toolbox-talks-and-measuring-safety-culture`.
This page covers the programme: requirements, design, refreshers and records.*

## 1. Build the requirements matrix
- List every **legal/regulatory source** that applies: workplace safety regulator (OSHA,
  HSE, Safe Work Australia/state regulators), sector rules (DOT, FAA, food safety,
  healthcare), environmental permits, employment law (e.g. harassment-prevention training
  mandated by some US states), data protection, plus **customer/contract and insurer
  requirements**, and your own **risk assessment** findings.
- For each: who must be trained (role, task, exposure), **content**, **when** (before
  assignment / within X days / annually), **trainer qualification**, **evaluation**
  required (e.g. a practical evaluation for powered industrial trucks), and **record**
  required.
- Matrix = roles × required courses with frequencies. *Requirements are
  jurisdiction-specific and change; have EHS/legal confirm the matrix yearly.*

## 2. Design training people retain
- Start from **what people must do differently** (behaviours), not the regulation's text.
- **Scenario-based and hands-on** beats lecture: more engaging methods (practice,
  simulation, dialogue) are associated with better knowledge and safety outcomes (Burke et
  al. 2006). Use real near-misses from your site (anonymised).
- **Language and literacy:** deliver in a language and vocabulary workers understand —
  OSHA, for instance, expects training "in a manner employees understand".
- Short modules (10–20 min) for e-learning; keep hazard-specific practical parts in person.
- **Assessment:** knowledge check for awareness training; **practical demonstration** for
  equipment and procedures (lockout, respirator fit, forklift). Set a pass standard and
  remediation path.
- Avoid "click-next" e-learning for high-risk tasks; it satisfies records, not risk.

## 3. Refresher scheduling
- Frequencies come from the regulation where stated (e.g. annual, every 3 years), else
  from risk: high-severity, low-frequency tasks need more frequent refreshers or drills.
- **Triggers** besides the calendar: incident or near-miss, observed unsafe practice,
  new equipment/process, a change in law, return from long absence.
- Use a **grace window** and automated reminders (60/30/7 days); escalate overdue items to
  managers; restrict task access when lapsed for safety-critical authorisations.
- Make refreshers **different** from the original (new scenarios, a drill), not a re-watch.

## 4. Audit-ready records
Each record should show: **who** (unique ID), **what** (course + version/date of
content), **when**, **duration**, **trainer/provider and their qualification**, **method**,
**assessment result**, and signature/e-attestation. Keep sign-in sheets and materials
tied to the version taught.
- Store in an **LMS or HR system** with reports by person, course, role and overdue
  status. Test the report before an inspector asks.
- Retention periods vary by regulation (some require duration of employment plus years);
  *check each source in the matrix*.
- Contractors and temps: decide whether host or agency trains and records — get copies.

## 5. Show it works
Track leading indicators (observed safe behaviours, near-miss reports, audit scores)
alongside completion %. 100% completion with no behaviour change is a design problem.

## Gotchas
- **Compliance theatre:** completion targets met, behaviour unchanged.
- Old content after a regulation or process change — version control the courses.
- Generic off-the-shelf courses with no site-specific hazards.
- Records missing the trainer or assessment result — often what gets cited.
- New hires working before required training is complete.

## Verify
- [ ] A requirements matrix lists source, roles, content, frequency, evaluation and record for each course.
- [ ] High-risk procedures include a practical evaluation, not only e-learning.
- [ ] Training is delivered in a language/level workers understand.
- [ ] Refreshers are scheduled with reminders and event triggers; lapses escalate.
- [ ] You can pull a complete, versioned record for any worker in minutes.
