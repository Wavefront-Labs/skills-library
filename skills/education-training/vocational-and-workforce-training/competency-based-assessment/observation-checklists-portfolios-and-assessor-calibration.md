# Observation checklists, portfolios and assessor calibration

**Goal:** a "competent / not yet competent" decision that two different assessors would
make the same way, based on evidence an auditor could follow.

## 1. Start from the standard, not the training
- Take the **occupational standard** (unit of competency, apprenticeship standard,
  employer competency) and list its **performance criteria**, required **knowledge**, and
  **conditions** (tools, environment, time, volume).
- Map each criterion to at least one assessment method. Gaps here are the most common
  audit finding.

## 2. Principles and rules (the quality test)
Assessment should be **fair, flexible, valid and reliable**. Evidence must be:
- **Valid** — shows the actual skill described (a quiz isn't evidence of welding).
- **Sufficient** — enough instances/contexts to infer consistent performance.
- **Authentic** — the candidate's own work.
- **Current** — recent enough to show they can do it *now*.
*(This four-and-four framing is from Australian VET regulation; other systems use
similar language. Check your regulator's current wording.)*

## 3. Observation checklists
- One line per **observable behaviour**, written so an observer can tick it: "Isolates
  supply and verifies dead with an approved tester before touching conductors" — not
  "works safely".
- Mark **critical items** (safety, legal): failure = not yet competent regardless of the rest.
- Include the **conditions** (realistic workplace or simulated), any time limit, and the
  **benchmark** product/standard.
- Columns: Satisfactory / Not yet / Comment (evidence of what was seen). Comments for
  every "Not yet" — and for critical items, even when satisfactory.
- Add **oral questions** for underpinning knowledge and "what if" contingencies with
  model answers.
- Observe on **more than one occasion** or task variant to meet sufficiency.

## 4. Evidence portfolios
- Index mapping each piece of evidence → criteria it covers.
- Mix types: direct observation, work products with photos, supervisor **third-party
  reports** (with contactable verifier), logbooks, questioning records.
- **Authenticity:** candidate declaration plus verification (assessor calls a sample of
  third-party referees; asks the candidate to explain their own evidence).
- **Currency:** set a window (often the last few years for fast-changing fields — *set
  per industry*).

## 5. Recognition of prior learning (RPL)
Same standard, same rules — just different evidence. Steps: self-assessment against
criteria → evidence portfolio → **competency conversation** (structured interview) →
**practical challenge** for gaps or doubtful evidence → decision with gap training if needed.
Don't grant RPL on job titles or years of experience alone.

## 6. Assessor calibration (consistency)
- **Before use:** validate tools with industry experts (does the checklist reflect real work?).
- **Calibration session:** assessors independently mark the same recorded performance or
  portfolio, then compare and discuss differences until judgement converges; record
  agreed interpretations ("'neat' means…").
- **Moderation/validation after use:** sample completed assessments (e.g. a percentage
  per assessor per period) and have a second assessor re-judge; track agreement.
- Assessors need current industry competence plus assessor qualification where required.

## Gotchas
- **Vague criteria** ("demonstrates professionalism") — unassessable and inconsistent.
- **Single-occasion sign-off** — fails sufficiency.
- Assessor also being the trainer and friend — bias; use moderation.
- Ticking every box without comments; audits ask "how do you know?".
- Simulated environment missing real conditions (noise, pressure, real customers).

## Verify
- [ ] Every criterion maps to at least one method in a mapping document.
- [ ] Checklists use observable behaviours and mark critical items.
- [ ] Evidence meets valid, sufficient, authentic, current — and you can say how.
- [ ] RPL decisions include a competency conversation and practical check.
- [ ] Assessors have calibrated and a moderation sample is reviewed each period.
