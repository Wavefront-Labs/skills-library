# Demonstration, supervised practice and error correction in the shop

**Goal:** learners perform a practical skill safely and to standard, with you able to see
and fix errors before they become habits.

## 1. Safety gates come first
- **No machine access until passed**: a written safety test (aim for 100%, retake allowed)
  *and* a supervised practical check on that specific machine. Log both, per learner, per
  machine.
- Shop rules posted and taught: PPE by zone, hair/jewellery/loose clothing, no working
  alone, housekeeping, emergency stops, first aid and eyewash locations.
- **Lockout/tagout** before any maintenance or blade/bit change on powered equipment.
- Check guards and e-stops before each session; you are the last line of defence.
- *Exact requirements depend on your regulator (OSHA, HSE, Safe Work Australia) and
  institution; follow their rules where stricter than this page.*

## 2. A demonstration they can see
- **Group of ≤ 6–8** around a bench, everyone with a clear view (use a camera to a
  screen for fine work like soldering). Stand so the learner's view matches *their*
  working orientation — right-handed learners watching a right-handed demo face-on see
  it mirrored.
- **Sequence:** show the finished standard product first → demonstrate at full speed once
  (what good looks like) → demonstrate slowly, step by step, naming the **key point** of
  each (hand position, angle, speed, sound/smell cues) → demonstrate again with a common
  error and its result.
- Use **sensory cues** experts rely on: "listen for the steady bacon-frying sound" (MIG
  welding), "feel the bit bite, then ease off".
- Keep it short (≈5–10 min); attention for watching is limited. Check understanding:
  a learner talks you through the steps before anyone touches the machine.

## 3. Structured supervised practice
- Learners move through the motor-learning stages: **cognitive** (thinking every step,
  slow, errors) → **associative** (refining) → **autonomous**. Early practice needs close
  supervision and simplified tasks.
- **Part-task then whole-task:** isolate a hard element (strike and maintain an arc on
  scrap) before the full job (a fillet weld on a test piece).
- **Deliberate practice:** short focused repetitions on one aspect with immediate
  feedback beat long unfocused production runs.
- Circulate on a **planned loop** so you see every station every few minutes; position
  yourself to see the whole room, back to a wall.
- Use **test pieces and destructive checks** (cut and etch a weld, bend test) so learners
  see inside their own work.

## 4. Error correction
- **Stop immediately** for anything unsafe; correct quietly, demonstrate the safe way,
  watch them do it.
- For quality errors: ask the learner to inspect their piece against the standard first
  ("What do you see compared to this one?").
- Feedback = **one specific correction at a time**, tied to the cause ("travel speed too
  fast — see the narrow, ropey bead; slow to about this pace"), then let them try again
  straight away. Praise specifically what improved.
- Track recurring errors across the group; reteach to everyone if many share it.
- Gradually reduce feedback frequency so learners build their own error detection.

## Gotchas
- **Demo from the wrong side** / too large a group — half the group didn't see it.
- Talking while demonstrating fine motor work so fast learners can't follow either.
- Fixing the piece *for* the learner.
- Over-correcting — five things at once means none get fixed.
- Learners hiding mistakes for fear of grading; treat early errors as information.
- Fatigue and end-of-session rushing are when injuries happen; keep cleanup time.

## Verify
- [ ] Every learner has logged safety test + machine check before access.
- [ ] Every learner could see the demo and talked back the steps before practising.
- [ ] Practice is broken into part-tasks with test pieces checked against a standard.
- [ ] You saw each station regularly and corrected one thing at a time.
- [ ] Learners can identify their own defects by the end of the unit.
