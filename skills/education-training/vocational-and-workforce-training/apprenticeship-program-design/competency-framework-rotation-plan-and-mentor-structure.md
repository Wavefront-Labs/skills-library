# Competency framework, rotation plan and mentor structure for an apprenticeship

**Goal:** a programme where an apprentice moves through planned, supervised real work
and linked classroom learning, and completes able to do the whole job to a defined
standard.

*National systems differ: US Registered Apprenticeship (DOL or state agency approval),
England's apprenticeship standards with independent end-point assessment, Germany's
dual system, Australia's training packages. Map these steps onto your regulator's
templates; registration requirements are not covered in detail here.*

## 1. Job analysis → competency framework
1. Run a **DACUM-style workshop**: 6–10 expert workers (not managers) list the job's
   **duties** (broad areas) and **tasks** (observable units with a start and end).
   Typical result: 8–12 duties, 60–150 tasks.
2. For each task add the **knowledge, skills, tools, safety** points and the standard of
   performance ("to manufacturer torque spec, first time, in X minutes").
3. Check against any existing **national occupational standard** — adopting it can make
   registration and portability easier.
4. Group tasks into **levels** (foundation → intermediate → advanced) that match wage
   steps.

## 2. Choose the progression model
| Model | How completion is judged | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Time-based | hours of on-the-job learning (US min. 2,000 h) | simple to run; uneven competence |
| Competency-based | demonstrated competencies, any pace | needs solid assessment |
| Hybrid | competencies + a hour range | common compromise |
Tie **wage progression** to milestones; it's the strongest retention lever.

## 3. Rotation plan (on-the-job learning)
- Map each duty to the **department, crew or job type** where it's done often enough to
  learn. Some tasks are rare — plan simulations or special assignments for those.
- Sequence: safety and tool basics first; supervised production work early (motivation);
  complex diagnosis and planning later.
- Example 3-year electrical: Y1 residential rough-in and service, Y2 commercial and
  controls, Y3 industrial, troubleshooting and leading small jobs.
- Publish a **rotation calendar** per cohort; protect it from "we need them on the line".

## 4. Related technical instruction (classroom)
- Align modules to the rotation so theory arrives just before or during the practice
  (a college or training provider often delivers; US commonly ~144 h/year recommended —
  *check current registration rules*).
- Build a feedback loop: instructors see rotation logs; supervisors see module topics.

## 5. Mentor structure
- **Ratio:** e.g. 1 mentor : 1–2 apprentices on site (regulators may set a ratio for
  safety — *check yours*).
- Select mentors for willingness and communication, not just seniority; give them
  **train-the-trainer** preparation (see
  `education-training.vocational-and-workforce-training.train-the-trainer.converting-smes-into-workplace-trainers`)
  and time in their workload.
- Mentor duties: daily coaching, signing task logs, monthly progress conversation,
  escalating concerns.
- A programme coordinator holds quarterly reviews with apprentice, mentor and instructor.

## 6. Completion standards
- Define the final assessment: competency sign-off against the framework, a practical
  test, a licensing exam, or an independent end-point assessment.
- Specify **evidence**: task logbook, observed assessments, portfolio, exam results (see
  `education-training.vocational-and-workforce-training.competency-based-assessment.observation-checklists-portfolios-and-assessor-calibration`).
- Track **completion rate, time to completion, retention after 12 months** and reasons
  for leaving.

## Gotchas
- **Apprentice as cheap labour** stuck on one repetitive task — the rotation plan is the
  defence.
- Frameworks written by managers or HR without front-line workers miss real tasks.
- Mentors with no time allocation quietly stop mentoring.
- Classroom content out of step with the job site.
- Early drop-out (first 3–6 months) is where most attrition happens; front-load support.
  *(Draft: figures above are typical ranges, not regulator-verified for every system.)*

## Verify
- [ ] Framework built from a job analysis with current workers and checked against a standard.
- [ ] Every competency has a place (rotation, simulation or class) where it's learnt.
- [ ] Rotation calendar and RTI schedule are aligned and published.
- [ ] Mentors are trained, have time and sign task logs.
- [ ] Completion evidence and wage milestones are defined in writing.
