# Blueprint mapping, practice exams and readiness judgement

**Goal:** get candidates to pass on the first sitting by studying in proportion to what
the exam actually tests, and only sending them when the evidence says they're ready.

## 1. Get the official blueprint
- Download the **candidate handbook / content outline / exam specification** from the
  certifying body (not a prep vendor). It lists **domains**, their **% weighting**, number
  of items, time, allowed references, and often the task statements tested.
- Note: version/effective date (blueprints change — e.g. code-cycle updates for
  electrical exams), **open or closed book**, calculator rules, and whether unscored
  pretest items are included.
- Know the format: computer-based, adaptive or linear, scenario items, performance-based
  labs (some IT certs).

## 2. Map the blueprint to a study plan
Build a table per domain:
| Domain | Weight | # items | Candidate baseline % | Gap | Study hours |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
Allocate hours roughly by **weight × gap** — a 25%-weighted domain where the candidate
scores 50% beats a 5% domain at 30%. Map every training module and question in your bank
to blueprint codes so you can report coverage.

## 3. Baseline and weak-area drilling
- **Diagnostic** practice test mapped to domains, timed.
- **Weak-area drilling:** short sets (10–20 items) on one sub-domain, with full
  rationales reviewed for *every* item — including correct guesses.
- Keep an **error log**: concept gap, misread, calculation, reference-lookup failure, time.
- Mix in **spaced, interleaved review** of earlier domains each week.

## 4. Open-book / code-book skills (trade licensing)
Many trade exams allow the code book (e.g. NEC for US electrical exams; *check your
jurisdiction's edition and permitted tabs/highlighting*). Train:
- **Index navigation** — find the article from keywords in under a minute.
- Permitted **tabbing and highlighting** of the most-used tables.
- Time budget: candidates who look up every question run out of time; memorise the
  frequent items.

## 5. Timed full practice exams
- At least 2–3 full-length, **timed** mocks under test conditions, spaced through the
  last third of preparation.
- Use **fresh** questions; repeated items measure memory of the item, not competence.
- Review by domain and by error type; feed back into drilling.

## 6. Readiness judgement
Before the candidate books (or before you advise them to sit):
- Recent fresh-item mock scores **consistently above the pass mark with a margin**
  (practitioners often aim ~10 points above; *the real cut score is set by the
  certifying body and scaled scores don't map 1:1 to percent correct*).
- No blueprint domain far below the rest (some exams report or require per-domain
  performance).
- Finishing within time with a few minutes to review.
- Third-party practice exam difficulty can differ from the real one — prefer the
  certifying body's official practice test if one exists.
If not ready: delay. A failed attempt costs fees, waiting periods and confidence.

## 7. Logistics
Eligibility (hours, education, application approval), ID rules, testing-centre vs online
proctoring requirements, retake policy and wait period, accommodations requests (apply
early with documentation).

## Gotchas
- **Studying from the course, not the blueprint** — over-preparing favourite topics.
- **Brain-dumps** (leaked live items): unethical, usually violates the candidate
  agreement, and can lead to revoked certification. Don't use or recommend them.
- Outdated blueprint or code edition.
- Treating practice-test % as the real score.
- Ignoring time pressure until the last week.

## Verify
- [ ] Current official blueprint and handbook are on file with effective dates.
- [ ] Study hours per domain follow weight × gap, and all content is blueprint-mapped.
- [ ] Error logs and domain scores drive weekly drilling.
- [ ] ≥2 timed, fresh full mocks completed with a margin above the pass mark.
- [ ] Eligibility, ID and accommodation logistics are confirmed before booking.
