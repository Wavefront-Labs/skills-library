# Writing aims, significance and budget justification

**Goal:** a proposal a tired panel member can score highly after reading the first page,
and that survives the detailed reviewer reading the rest. Funder specifics (NIH, NSF,
ERC, UKRI, ARC…) change often — always work from the current call document; this skill
is the transferable structure. Institutional routing and budget sign-off are covered in
`business-finance.research-administration.pre-award-administration.proposal-budget-and-institutional-routing`.

## 0. Read the call like a contract
Extract into a checklist: eligibility, page/character limits, required sections,
font/margin rules, **the scoring criteria and their weights**, budget caps and
ineligible costs, deadline (internal research-office deadline is often 5–10 working
days earlier). Non-compliant proposals are frequently returned unreviewed.

## 1. The aims page (write it first, rewrite it last)
A one-page structure that works for most funders:
1. **Hook:** the problem and why it matters (1–2 sentences, concrete stakes).
2. **What is known / the gap:** the specific missing knowledge blocking progress.
3. **Long-term goal → objective of this proposal → central hypothesis** (or central
   question for non-hypothesis-driven work), and the rationale/preliminary data that
   make it plausible.
4. **2–3 aims**, each a short bold title plus 2–3 lines of approach and expected outcome.
   Aims should be **related but not dependent** — if Aim 1 fails, Aims 2–3 must still
   produce results. Interlocking aims are a classic reviewer criticism.
5. **Payoff:** what will be different when you finish (knowledge, tool, method).

Show this page to a colleague outside your subfield. If they can't say back the gap and
the aims in 30 seconds, rewrite.

## 2. Significance and innovation
- Map every paragraph to a named review criterion, using the funder's own words as
  sub-headings where allowed. Reviewers score against the criteria sheet; make their job easy.
- Significance = consequence of success for the field (and, where scored, society:
  NSF "broader impacts", UKRI/Horizon "impact" pathways). Avoid "this will cure X".
- Innovation = what is new (concept, method, application), stated plainly. "Novel" with
  no specifics scores nothing.

## 3. Approach
For each aim: rationale, design, methods, sample size/power or data sources, analysis,
**expected results, potential pitfalls and alternative strategies**, and timeline.
Include a Gantt or milestone table. Preliminary data should show feasibility of the
riskiest step, not everything you have ever done.

## 4. Budget justification
- Every line traces to an activity in the approach: "0.5 FTE postdoc, Years 1–3, runs
  Aim 2 assays" — reviewers check staff effort against workload.
- Use institutional rates (salary scales, fringe/on-costs, indirect/overhead) from your
  research office; don't guess. Inflate multi-year salaries per local policy.
- Equipment: quote, why existing kit can't do it, who else will use it.
- Travel: named conferences/fieldwork with per-trip estimates.
- Under-budgeting signals inexperience as much as over-budgeting signals padding.

## 5. Resubmission
- Read the summary statement/panel feedback twice, a day apart. Categorise every point:
  fix, partially fix, respectfully disagree with evidence.
- Write the response/introduction (often one page) as: thanks, the main changes, then
  point-by-point. Mark changes in the text if the funder allows. Never argue tone.

## Gotchas
- Jargon density: panels include non-specialists; define acronyms once, cut the rest.
- Missing letters of support/collaboration and data-management plans sink otherwise good bids.
- Uncertain: page limits, criteria names and resubmission rules differ by funder and
  year — confirm in the current call.

## Verify
- [ ] Compliance checklist from the call is complete.
- [ ] Aims page states gap, objective, hypothesis/question, independent aims, payoff.
- [ ] Each review criterion is visibly addressed.
- [ ] Every approach section has pitfalls and alternatives.
- [ ] Every budget line points to an activity; rates came from the research office.
