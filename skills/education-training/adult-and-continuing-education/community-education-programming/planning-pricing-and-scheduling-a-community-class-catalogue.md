# Planning, pricing and scheduling a community class catalogue

**Goal:** a term catalogue of evening/weekend classes (languages, crafts, cooking,
computing, fitness, personal finance) that fills, breaks even or meets its subsidy
target, and keeps good instructors and learners coming back. Applies to community
colleges' non-credit arms, adult/community learning services, recreation departments
and independent centres.

## 1. Choose what to run from evidence
- Look back 2–3 terms per course: enrolments vs capacity, cancellations, waitlists,
  repeat learners, evaluation scores. Classify: **keep**, **grow** (waitlisted — add a
  section), **fix** (good topic, weak delivery/time slot), **drop** (cancelled twice).
- New courses: learner suggestion box/survey, what's selling at nearby providers, local
  trends, partner requests (library, employer, health service). Pilot as a one-day
  taster before committing to a 10-week course.
- Keep a balance across subjects, days and price points; ensure some accessible/free
  provision if your mission or funding requires it (e.g. targeted community learning).

## 2. Recruit and brief instructors
- Sources: past learners who became experts, local businesses and makers, retired
  professionals, referrals from current tutors.
- Check: subject expertise, a short teaching demo or reference, required background checks
  (especially for programmes with under-18s or vulnerable adults), insurance and
  qualifications for higher-risk activities (fitness, food handling, power tools).
- Give a one-page tutor agreement: pay basis, minimum enrolment and cancellation rules
  (and whether they're paid if cancelled), materials, room rules, safeguarding/incident
  reporting, evaluation. Pay model choices: hourly rate, per-learner rate, or revenue share.
- Course description template from tutors: who it's for, what you'll make/learn, what to
  bring, level, accessibility notes.

## 3. Price to a break-even minimum
For each course:
```
direct costs = tutor pay + room + materials (if included) + share of overhead/marketing
break-even learners = direct costs ÷ fee per learner
```
- Set the **minimum enrolment** at or near break-even (often 6–10 for typical classes);
  set capacity by room and safety.
- Check the fee against local comparators; consider concessions (seniors, low income)
  and how they're funded. Separate materials fees make prices look clearer.
- Example: tutor $40/h × 2 h × 8 weeks = $640; room $15/session = $120; overhead $100
  → $860. At $120/learner, break-even ≈ 8 learners.

## 4. Build the term schedule
- Fix term dates around public holidays and school terms; 6–10-week courses plus one-day
  workshops are common.
- Spread popular subjects across days/times; avoid two courses aimed at the same
  audience in the same slot. Offer some daytime courses for retirees and shift workers.
- Publish the catalogue 4–6 weeks before start (print still matters for older audiences;
  online with live availability for everyone else).

## 5. Run go/no-go and registration
- **Go/no-go date** ~5–7 days before start: below minimum → cancel or combine, notify
  learners and tutor immediately, offer transfer or full refund. Publish this policy.
- Waitlisted courses: open a second section if a tutor and room are available.
- Clear refund/transfer rules (e.g. full refund up to 7 days before start).

## 6. Evaluate and iterate
Short end-of-course survey (rating, what to improve, "what would you take next?"),
tutor debrief, and an end-of-term review of fill rates and margins feeding the next
catalogue.

## Gotchas
- Cancelling late erodes trust more than cancelling early.
- Under-pricing to fill classes can make the programme unsustainable.
- Uncertain: example rates are illustrative only; local pay, room costs and subsidy rules vary.

## Verify
- [ ] Each course classed keep/grow/fix/drop from data.
- [ ] Every tutor has a signed agreement and required checks.
- [ ] Each course has a calculated break-even and published minimum.
- [ ] Go/no-go date and refund policy published.
- [ ] End-of-term review completed before planning the next catalogue.
