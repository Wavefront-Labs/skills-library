# Placing and preparing adults for the GED or HiSET

**Goal:** each learner passes their high-school-equivalency (HSE) credential in the
fewest attempts and weeks, with momentum kept by early wins. Details below reflect the
GED (US) and HiSET as commonly delivered; **test content, fees, passing scores and
which test a state offers change — check the official GED Testing Service / HiSET and
state adult-education sites before advising learners.**

## 1. Know the test structure
- **GED:** four separately scheduled subjects — Mathematical Reasoning, Reasoning Through
  Language Arts (RLA, including a written extended response), Science, Social Studies.
  Scores on a 100–200 scale; 145 per subject passes (at time of writing). Computer-based,
  at a test centre or online-proctored in many states.
- **HiSET:** five subtests (Language Arts–Reading, Language Arts–Writing incl. essay,
  Mathematics, Science, Social Studies); computer or paper depending on site.
- Both reward **reading and reasoning with provided information** more than memorised
  facts: science and social studies are largely reading graphs, passages and data.

## 2. Diagnose and place
- Intake: goals (job, college, military, personal), last grade completed, time available
  per week, prior attempts and scores.
- Use the **official practice tests** (GED Ready; HiSET practice tests): they predict
  readiness and give skill-level reports. Lower-level learners may first need
  adult-basic-education work — a learner reading well below roughly 9th-grade level is
  usually not ready for HSE content yet (rough guide).
- Plan subject order to get an **early win**: often the subject they're closest to
  passing (frequently Social Studies or Science for strong readers), leaving Math —
  most often the hardest — with enough time.

## 3. Teach what the tests reward
- **Reading for evidence:** main idea, inference, author's purpose, comparing two texts,
  reading charts/maps/tables. These skills carry across RLA, Science and Social Studies —
  teach them together.
- **Extended response / essay:** for GED RLA, learners analyse two passages and argue which
  position is better supported, citing evidence. Teach a fixed structure: claim →
  2–3 evidence paragraphs quoting the texts → explanation → brief conclusion. Practise
  timed (GED RLA's extended response is 45 minutes at time of writing).
- **Math:** number sense, ratios/percent, linear equations, functions, geometry with the
  provided formula sheet, data/statistics. Teach the formula sheet and the on-screen
  calculator (GED uses a TI-30XS emulator) explicitly — learners lose points fumbling them.
- Science/Social Studies: core vocabulary (hypothesis, variable, amendment, supply and
  demand) and practice with data and primary-source excerpts, not content lectures.

## 4. Accelerated review and readiness
- Use practice-test skill reports to target the 3–5 weakest skills per subject.
- Short timed sets build pacing; review every wrong answer for *why*.
- **Book the test when the official practice test says likely to pass**, not after a
  fixed number of weeks. Many programmes have vouchers or discounted retakes — check.

## 5. Test-day readiness and accommodations
- Walk through ID requirements, account setup, online-proctoring room rules (clear desk,
  webcam) or test-centre location.
- Learners with documented disabilities can apply for accommodations (extra time,
  breaks, screen reader) — the application takes weeks; start early.
- Test anxiety: rehearse under timed conditions, teach a skip-and-return strategy.

## 6. Keep momentum
Celebrate each subject passed; schedule the next subject within a couple of weeks.
Attendance drops after setbacks — call after any failed attempt and re-plan from the score report.

## Gotchas
- Teaching content coverage instead of reasoning with given information.
- Learners taking the test without a "likely to pass" practice score burn fees and confidence.
- Uncertain: subject timings, passing scores and state availability of GED vs HiSET.

## Verify
- [ ] Each learner has a subject order with an early win and practice-test baselines.
- [ ] Essay structure practised timed with evidence from both passages.
- [ ] Calculator and formula sheet drilled.
- [ ] Tests booked only after "likely to pass" practice results.
- [ ] Accommodation requests started early where needed.
