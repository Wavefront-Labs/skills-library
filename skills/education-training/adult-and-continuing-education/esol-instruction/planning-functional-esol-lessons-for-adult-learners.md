# Planning functional ESOL lessons for adult learners

**Goal:** adults who can do the things they need to do in English — make a GP
appointment, talk to their child's teacher, follow safety instructions at work, pass a
citizenship interview — with confidence growing each week.

## 1. Place learners sensibly
- Short intake interview plus a placement task covering **speaking/listening and
  literacy separately**: a fluent speaker may be unable to read, and a university-educated
  newcomer may read well but barely speak.
- Identify **pre-literate or low-literacy-in-first-language learners** early: they need
  literacy-level classes (letter formation, sound–letter links, left-to-right, reading
  numbers and their own details) and progress differently. Putting them in a standard
  beginner class leaves them lost.
- Use your system's levels (e.g. CEFR A1–B2, UK ESOL Entry 1–3/Level 1–2, US NRS ESL
  levels) for placement and reporting.

## 2. Find needs, then choose tasks
Ask the group (with visuals for beginners) what they need English for this term.
Typical themes: health, housing, work and job search, children's school, shopping and
money, transport, emergencies, citizenship. Pick one theme for 2–4 weeks and derive
**real-world tasks** from it: "call the surgery and book an appointment", "report a
repair to a landlord".

## 3. Lesson shape (a reliable default)
1. **Context:** a picture, realia (a real appointment card) or short story about the
   situation; elicit what learners already know and say.
2. **Model:** a short, natural dialogue or text — listen first, then see it.
3. **Language focus:** pull out the key chunks ("I'd like to make an appointment", "Is
   there anything on Tuesday?"), vocabulary and one grammar point that the task needs.
   Teach chunks before rules at low levels.
4. **Controlled practice:** drills, pair repetition, gap-fills, matching. Model
   pronunciation: word stress and sentence stress matter more than individual sounds
   for being understood.
5. **Freer practice / task:** role-play the real call with variations (no appointments
   free, wrong number); for higher levels, a real task (fill the actual form).
6. **Review and can-do check:** "Today I can book an appointment" — learners tick it.
Keep teacher talk short and graded; instructions demonstrated, not just explained.

## 4. Multilevel classes (common in community settings)
Same theme, tiered tasks: beginners match pictures to words and say the key phrase;
intermediate learners role-play; stronger learners write the follow-up message. Group
by level for practice, mix for authentic exchange.

## 5. Workplace and citizenship variants
- **Workplace:** analyse the job's language with the employer (safety signs, shift
  handovers, asking for clarification). Teach "checking" language ("Do you mean…?") —
  vital for safety. Agree outcomes with the employer and learners.
- **Citizenship/naturalisation:** practise the interview format, the civics question bank
  and reading/writing sentences as required by the specific test — check the current
  official materials for your country (e.g. USCIS in the US), as they are revised.

## 6. Learners' lives
Many adult ESOL learners are refugees, carers or shift workers; trauma, fatigue and
irregular attendance are normal. Keep each lesson self-contained enough to join mid-unit,
avoid topics that probe trauma (e.g. "describe your journey here"), and know local
referral services.

## Assessment
Track can-do statements per learner, plus mandated tests on your funder's schedule.
Record real-world wins learners report.

## Gotchas
- Grammar-first lessons with no task leave learners unable to use what they "know".
- Forgetting literacy needs of strong speakers, or speaking needs of strong readers.
- Uncertain: level frameworks and citizenship test content vary by country and year.

## Verify
- [ ] Learners placed separately for oral skills and literacy; pre-literate learners identified.
- [ ] Each unit is built from a real task learners named.
- [ ] Lessons move model → practice → task with short teacher talk.
- [ ] Can-do checks recorded each lesson.
- [ ] Workplace/citizenship content checked against current official sources.
