# Assessing and teaching adult literacy and numeracy classes

**Goal:** adults make measurable progress toward their own goals (reading to their
children, passing a work test, handling money and forms) in a class that protects
their dignity. This is the classroom/programme version; for one-to-one beginner
reading tutoring see
`languages-linguistics.literacy-and-reading.adult-literacy.tutoring-adult-beginner-readers-one-to-one`.

## 1. Intake: goals first, then levels
- Start with a private conversation: why now, what they want to read/write/calculate,
  past schooling experience (often painful), work and family commitments, any known
  learning difficulty, vision/hearing (get eyes checked — uncorrected vision is common).
- Then a **standardised placement** if your funder requires one (e.g. CASAS or TABE in US
  adult education, mapped to NRS levels; initial assessment against Entry Level/Level 1–2
  national standards in England's Functional Skills). Use what you're mandated to use;
  explain to the learner it's for placement, not pass/fail.
- Then a short **diagnostic** to find which component is weak — a total reading score
  hides it:
  - **Decoding/word reading:** read a list of real and nonsense words.
  - **Fluency:** read a passage at their level aloud for 1 minute.
  - **Vocabulary and comprehension:** questions on a passage read silently.
  - **Writing:** a short piece on a familiar topic.
  - **Numeracy:** number sense, the four operations, fractions/decimals/percentages,
    measurement, reading tables and graphs — in everyday contexts.
- Record all of this in an individual learning plan (ILP) with 2–3 goals the learner
  chose, in their words.

## 2. Teach the weak component explicitly
- Adults with weak decoding need **systematic, explicit phonics** too — with adult
  materials, never children's books. Fluency: repeated and paired reading of short
  adult texts. Comprehension: teach strategies (predicting, questioning, summarising)
  with think-alouds.
- Numeracy: build from concrete (money, measuring tapes, recipes, payslips) to abstract;
  ask learners how they *already* do mental calculations — many have effective informal
  methods to build on.

## 3. Use functional, authentic materials
Bus timetables, medicine labels, job adverts, tenancy letters, payslips, school
newsletters, workplace safety signs. The skill transfers better and learners see the
point. Pre-teach key vocabulary before the text.

## 4. Run a mixed-level class
- Short whole-group opener on a shared theme (e.g. "renting"), then **differentiated
  tasks** on the same theme at 2–3 levels, then share back.
- Group by component need, not just overall level, for targeted mini-lessons.
- Volunteers/assistants work with small groups on rehearsed routines.

## 5. Dignity-preserving correction
- Never ask someone to read aloud to the whole group without their consent.
- Correct the pattern, privately or in writing: note recurring errors and teach them as
  a mini-lesson to everyone who needs it.
- Praise specifically ("you sounded out *apartment* in chunks — that's the strategy").
- Learners' adult knowledge is real; the gap is a skill, not intelligence.

## 6. Track progress and retention
- Review ILP goals every 4–6 weeks; celebrate real-life wins (filled in a form alone).
- Post-test on the funder's schedule (often after a set number of instruction hours).
- Attendance is the biggest predictor of gains — call absent learners, adapt timing,
  and help with barriers (childcare, transport) through referrals.

## Gotchas
- Children's materials infantilise learners and drive drop-out.
- Undiagnosed dyslexia or other learning difficulty: refer for assessment where
  available, and teach multisensory, explicit methods meanwhile.
- Uncertain: assessment instruments and level frameworks are country- and funder-specific.

## Verify
- [ ] Each learner has an ILP with their own goals and a component-level diagnosis.
- [ ] Lessons target the diagnosed weak component explicitly.
- [ ] Materials are adult and functional.
- [ ] No one is put on the spot reading aloud without consent.
- [ ] Progress reviewed against goals and the required post-test.
