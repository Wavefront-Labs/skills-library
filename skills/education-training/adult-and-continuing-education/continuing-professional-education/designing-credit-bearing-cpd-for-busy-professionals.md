# Designing credit-bearing CPD for busy professionals

**Goal:** a continuing-education offering that (a) qualifies for the credits
professionals need to keep their licence or registration, (b) fits around a working
week, and (c) actually changes something they do. Accreditation rules are set by each
profession's body or accreditor and differ in detail — always design against the
current standards of the specific body (e.g. ACCME and its partners for medicine,
NASBA for US accountancy CPE, state bars for CLE, IACET for general CEUs, national
regulators/professional bodies elsewhere). For CME content production specifically see
`media-communication.scientific-and-medical-communication.medical-education-content.needs-assessed-cme-modules-with-fair-balance-and-independence`.

## 1. Start from a practice gap
- Needs assessment = the difference between current and desired practice, with evidence:
  audit data, incident reports, regulatory changes, new guidelines, practitioner surveys,
  exam pass-rate data, employer feedback.
- Write the gap in one sentence: "Many small-firm accountants are unsure how the new
  lease standard changes client balance sheets." Most accreditors require you to document
  this; it also stops you building courses nobody needs.

## 2. Write measurable objectives
Use observable verbs tied to practice ("calculate", "select", "apply", "draft"), not
"understand" or "be aware of". Two to four objectives per hour of content is plenty.
Objectives drive the assessment and are often printed on the certificate/brochure.

## 3. Choose a busy-professional format
- **Short and modular:** 30–90-minute units, stackable into larger credit bundles.
- **Formats:** live webinar (scheduled lunch/evening), self-paced online with
  knowledge checks, half-day workshop, case-based small group, journal-based learning,
  conference session. Blend: pre-read → live case discussion → applied task at work.
- Put cases and decisions at the centre — professionals learn from "what would you do?"
  more than from slides.
- Accessibility: captions, transcripts, mobile-friendly, downloadable materials.

## 4. Get the credit maths and records right
- Credit is usually based on **instructional time** with defined units — e.g. a CEU is
  commonly 10 contact hours (IACET), US accountancy CPE commonly uses a 50-minute credit
  hour, and many bodies count only instructional time (no breaks, intros, Q&A rules vary).
  Check your body's definitions and rounding rules.
- Self-paced credit often requires a pilot-tested time estimate or word-count formula
  and a passing post-test (e.g. 70% is common) — confirm the requirement.
- **Attendance verification:** for live online, polling checks or engagement prompts at
  intervals; for in-person, sign-in/out. Keep records for the period your accreditor
  requires (often 5–6 years).
- Certificates show: participant, provider (with accreditation statement exactly as
  worded by the accreditor), title, date, credits and type.

## 5. Protect independence (where required)
Many health-profession accreditors require: disclosure and mitigation of faculty and
planner financial relationships with ineligible companies, content free of commercial
bias, separation of promotion from education, and written agreements for commercial
support. Even where not required, disclose conflicts.

## 6. Evaluate beyond satisfaction
- Immediate: post-test on objectives, and "what will you change?" commitment question.
- Follow-up (4–8 weeks): did they make the change; what got in the way? Feeds the next
  needs assessment.

## Becoming an accredited provider
Expect an application with documented policies (needs assessment, planning, evaluation,
records, complaints, conflict of interest), sample activity files, fees, and periodic
reaccreditation. Plan 6–12 months; some bodies require a track record first.

## Gotchas
- Counting breaks and welcome sessions as credit time — a common audit finding.
- Accreditation statement wording altered on marketing — accreditors specify it verbatim.
- Uncertain: unit definitions, pass marks and record-retention periods vary by body.

## Verify
- [ ] Documented practice gap with evidence.
- [ ] Objectives use observable verbs and map to assessment.
- [ ] Credit calculation matches the body's current rules.
- [ ] Attendance/completion records and certificates meet requirements.
- [ ] Conflicts disclosed; evaluation includes intended practice change.
