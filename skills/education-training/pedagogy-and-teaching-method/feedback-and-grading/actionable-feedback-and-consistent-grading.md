# Actionable feedback and consistent grading

**Goal:** feedback that changes what the student does next, and grades that two teachers
would agree on — without spending your weekends writing comments nobody reads.

## 1. Separate the two jobs
- **Feedback** is for improvement: specific, about the task, followed by action.
- **Grading** is for reporting: consistent, criterion-referenced, defensible.
When a grade and comments come back together, many students read the grade and ignore the
comments. Where policy allows, return comments first, grade later (or not at all on drafts).

## 2. Feedback students can act on
Good feedback answers three questions (Hattie & Timperley): *Where am I going? How am I
going? Where to next?* In practice:
- **Tie it to a criterion** the student has seen: "Your explanation of osmosis uses
  'water moves' but doesn't mention concentration gradient (criterion 2)."
- **Make the next step a task, not a judgement:** not "more detail needed" but "Rewrite your
  second paragraph adding one piece of evidence from source B."
- **One or two targets**, not ten. Fix the most important thing first.
- **Task and process level**, not praise of the person ("you're so clever").
- Bake in **improvement time** (sometimes called DIRT): 10–15 minutes next lesson where
  students do the redraft. Feedback without time to act on it is mostly wasted.

## 3. Feedback at scale
- **Whole-class feedback:** read the set, noting (a) common errors, (b) great examples,
  (c) spelling/vocabulary slips, (d) individuals to see. Teach to that sheet next lesson;
  students then fix their own work. Often far quicker than individual comments.
- **Codes:** a key (e.g. T1 = "add a counter-argument", T2 = "quote embedded, not dropped in")
  that students translate into their own action.
- **Live marking:** circulate with a pen during independent work; one comment at the moment
  of error beats a paragraph a week later.
- **Exemplars:** show an anonymous strong and weak answer side by side and ask students to
  identify the difference before they revise.

## 4. Grading consistently
1. **Use the criteria or mark scheme** every time; read it again before each batch.
2. **Anchor papers:** before grading, pick 2–3 scripts at key boundaries and agree their marks.
3. **Mark question-by-question** across the whole set, not student-by-student, for long tests —
   it reduces halo effects.
4. **Anonymise** where possible (cover names or use candidate numbers) to cut bias.
5. **Shuffle** the pile between questions so the same students aren't always marked when you're tired.
6. **Moderate:** a colleague blind-marks a sample (say 5–10 scripts across the range). Discuss
   any disagreements of more than one grade and adjust the whole set if a pattern appears.
7. For essays and open work, **comparative judgement** (judging pairs, "which is better?")
   can be more reliable than rubric scoring; tools exist for running it at scale.

## Gotchas
- **Feedback as evidence for observers** — dense comments written to show you marked. Policy
  should be about impact, not ink; check your school's.
- **Vague criteria** ("excellent analysis") produce inconsistent grades; see
  education-training.curriculum-and-assessment-design.rubric-design.writing-and-calibrating-analytic-rubrics.
- **Drift** within a batch: the 30th script is graded harsher or softer than the 1st. Re-check
  your first five at the end.
- **Averaging** early failed attempts with later mastery can misreport current attainment;
  check whether your system is cumulative or standards-based.
- *Uncertainty:* the evidence on written versus verbal feedback, and on grades-with-comments,
  is mixed; the strongest finding is that students must use the feedback.

## Verify
- [ ] Each comment names a criterion and a concrete next action.
- [ ] Students had scheduled time to act on feedback, and you saw the improvement.
- [ ] Grades were set against anchor papers, with names hidden where possible.
- [ ] A colleague blind-marked a sample; disagreements over one grade were resolved.
- [ ] Your marking time per set dropped (whole-class feedback/codes) without losing impact.
