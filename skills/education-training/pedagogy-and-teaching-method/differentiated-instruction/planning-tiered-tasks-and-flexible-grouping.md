# Planning tiered tasks and flexible grouping

**Goal:** every student in a mixed class works on the same important learning at a level of
challenge that moves them forward — without you writing thirty lesson plans.

## 1. Find out where they are (pre-assessment)
Before the unit or lesson, run a quick diagnostic on the **prerequisites** and the new skill:
3–6 questions, an exit ticket from last lesson, or a hinge question. Sort responses into
three piles: *secure*, *partial*, *not yet*. That sort — not a fixed "ability" label — is your
grouping for this lesson only.

## 2. Keep one objective; vary the route
The most common mistake is giving lower-attaining students a *different, easier goal*.
Differentiate the **support** and the **depth**, not the destination:
| Lever | Not yet | Partial | Secure |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Content** access | Worked example + vocabulary card | Faded worked example | Problem only |
| **Process** | Teacher table, step-by-step | Pair work with checklist | Independent, then explain to a peer |
| **Product** | Sentence starters / partial frame | Frame with prompts removed | Open response, plus a "why" extension |
Everyone attempts the core question; the secure group goes deeper (a non-routine problem,
justify, generalise), not just "more of the same".

## 3. Build a tiered task quickly
1. Write the **core task** that meets the objective.
2. Make a **scaffolded version** by adding supports: a worked example at the top, chunked
   steps, a word bank, a partially completed model, fewer but representative items.
3. Make a **stretch version** by removing supports and adding depth: a twist, an error to
   diagnose, or a transfer context.
4. Same layout and same title for all three so students don't advertise their tier.
Let students **move up** a tier mid-lesson when ready; tiers are doors, not walls.

## 4. Group flexibly
- Regroup per lesson or per skill from fresh evidence; never fix groups for a term.
- Mix grouping types across a week: readiness groups, interest groups, mixed pairs, whole class.
- Run a **teacher table**: the 4–6 students who are "not yet" work with you for 10 minutes of
  targeted re-teaching while others start the task. Everyone else needs a clear, self-running task
  first, or you'll be interrupted constantly.

## 5. Plan for specific needs
- **EAL/ELL:** pre-teach key vocabulary with visuals; allow first-language talk for thinking;
  keep cognitive demand high while lowering language load.
- **SEND/IEP:** apply the accommodations in the student's plan (e.g. extra time, reduced copying,
  enlarged text) — these are obligations, not optional extras. See
  education-training.special-education-and-iep-planning for plan-writing.
- **Highly able:** depth and complexity, not acceleration into next year's content by default.

## Gotchas
- **"Learning styles" matching** (visual/auditory/kinaesthetic) is not supported by evidence;
  don't differentiate on it.
- **Workload trap.** Three tiers every lesson is unsustainable. Differentiate the 1–2 key tasks
  per week where the gap matters most, and use whole-class scaffolds (models, examples) that help
  everyone.
- **Fixed low groups lower expectations**, and the effect compounds. Re-sort from evidence.
- **Over-scaffolding**: remove supports as the student improves, or they stay dependent.
- *Uncertainty:* evidence on within-class differentiation is mixed and hard to separate from
  general good teaching; UK guidance now prefers the term "adaptive teaching" and cautions
  against creating many separate tasks. Treat this method as a practical frame, not a proven recipe.

## Verify
- [ ] Groups came from a pre-assessment done this week, not from last term's set.
- [ ] All tiers aim at the same objective; the stretch tier adds depth, not volume.
- [ ] Tiered sheets look alike; students can move up during the lesson.
- [ ] A teacher-table group got targeted re-teaching while others worked independently.
- [ ] Accommodations in individual plans were applied.
- [ ] The exit check shows the "not yet" group moved forward.
