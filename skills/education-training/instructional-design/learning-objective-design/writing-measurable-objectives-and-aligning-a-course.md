# Writing measurable objectives and aligning a course

**Goal:** every objective names something a learner will visibly do, every activity
practises an objective, and every assessment item tests one — so the course teaches what
the job actually needs.

## 1. Check that training is the answer (needs analysis)
Requests arrive as solutions ("we need a course on X"). Ask:
- **What should people be doing that they aren't?** Get the observable performance gap and how
  it's measured (errors, complaints, time, sales).
- **Why?** Classic causes: lack of knowledge/skill (training helps), unclear expectations, missing
  tools or information, no feedback, incentives that reward the wrong thing (training doesn't help).
  A quick test (after Mager): *could they do it if their life depended on it?* If yes, it's not
  a skill gap.
- **Who** are the learners, what do they already know, where and when will they learn?
Write a one-page needs statement and get the sponsor to agree it before designing.

## 2. Task-analyse the performance
Interview or observe 2–3 competent performers. List the steps, decisions, cues, common errors and
the knowledge each step needs. Prioritise by **frequency × consequence of error** — that ranking
decides what gets practice time.

## 3. Write objectives (ABCD format)
- **A**udience — who.
- **B**ehaviour — an observable verb.
- **C**ondition — given what tools/information/situation.
- **D**egree — the standard (accuracy, time, criteria).
Example: "Given a customer complaint email and the refund policy (C), a new support agent (A) will
draft a reply that applies the correct refund tier (B) with no policy errors on 4 of 5 sample
cases (D)."
Banned verbs: *understand, know, appreciate, be aware of, learn* — they can't be observed.

## 4. Pitch the level (revised Bloom)
| Level | Example verbs |
|---|---|
| Remember | list, recall, identify |
| Understand | explain, classify, summarise |
| Apply | use, calculate, carry out |
| Analyse | diagnose, compare, distinguish |
| Evaluate | judge, critique, justify |
| Create | design, compose, plan |
Match the level to the job: if the job requires *diagnosing* faults, an objective at "identify
the parts" is too low. Remember-level objectives are often **enabling objectives** under a
higher-level terminal objective.

## 5. Align (backward design)
Build a matrix, one row per objective:
| Objective | Assessment (evidence) | Practice activity | Content needed |
|---|---|---|---|
Fill **assessment before activities** (Wiggins & McTighe's stage 2 before stage 3). Then check:
- Every objective has an assessment at the **same level and condition** (an "apply" objective
  isn't tested by a recall quiz).
- Every activity serves an objective; content no objective needs is cut or moved to a reference.
- Practice resembles the real task conditions as closely as feasible.

## Gotchas
- **Objective inflation**: 25 objectives for a one-hour module. Aim for 3–5 terminal objectives
  per hour of instruction (*rule of thumb, not a standard*).
- **Writing objectives from the content** ("cover chapter 3") rather than from the task.
- **Learner-facing vs designer-facing** — the ABCD version is for you and the sponsor; give learners
  a plain-language version ("By the end, you'll be able to…").
- **Stakeholder content creep**: SMEs want everything included. The matrix is your defence — no
  objective, no screen.
- *Uncertainty:* Bloom levels are a planning aid, not a strict hierarchy; experts disagree on where
  some verbs sit, so judge by what the learner must actually do.

## Verify
- [ ] A signed-off needs statement shows training addresses a real skill/knowledge gap.
- [ ] Each objective has audience, observable behaviour, condition and degree.
- [ ] No objective uses "understand", "know" or "be aware of".
- [ ] The alignment matrix has no empty assessment or activity cells.
- [ ] Assessments match each objective's Bloom level and conditions.
