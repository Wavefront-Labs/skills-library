# DURC screening, risk-mitigation planning and publication review

**Goal:** identify the small fraction of legitimate research whose knowledge, products or
technologies could be directly misapplied to cause significant harm, and manage that risk
proportionately — without blocking ordinary science.

*Policy is in flux. The 2024 US policy (replacing the 2012/2014 DURC policies and the P3CO
framework, with "Category 1" DURC and "Category 2" PEPP research) was due to take effect in
2025, and a 2025 executive order on gain-of-function research directed further changes.
**Confirm which federal policy and agency terms currently apply before every review.**
Other countries use export-control, biosecurity and funder rules. This page describes a
generic institutional process; it deliberately contains no technical content.*

## 1. Roles
- **PI:** first-line identification and disclosure; implements mitigation.
- **Institutional review entity (IRE / ICDUR):** often the IBC with added expertise, or a
  separate committee; includes biosafety, biosecurity, the relevant science, and someone
  able to weigh benefits (e.g. public health).
- **Institutional contact** for liaison with funders/agencies.
- Funding agency: may review and approve the risk-mitigation plan.

## 2. Screening (scope test)
Apply the current policy's scope as a two-step check:
1. **Agent/toxin or research type in scope?** The policy lists agents (historically select
   agents and certain risk-group pathogens and toxins) and, for PEPP, research expected to
   enhance pandemic potential.
2. **Expected experimental outcome in scope?** The policy lists outcome types (e.g. that
   would enhance harmful consequences, disrupt immunity or countermeasure effectiveness,
   increase transmissibility, alter host range, enable evasion of detection, or
   reconstitute an eradicated agent — *paraphrased; use the policy's text*).
If both → formal review. Also screen **outside the list** using judgement: other fields
(AI-enabled design, chemistry, synthetic biology) can raise dual-use concerns that
funders or institutions now ask about.
Record a screening outcome for *every* in-scope-agent project, including "not DURC".

## 3. Risk assessment (committee)
Ask, in plain terms and documented:
- **What could be misused** — the knowledge, a product (material, strain, model), or a
  technology/method?
- **By whom and how plausibly** — what capability, resources and access would a misuser
  need beyond the publication or material? Is this a meaningful uplift over what's
  already available?
- **Consequence scale** — breadth and severity of harm to public health, agriculture,
  environment, security.
- **Benefits** — scientific and public-health value, and whether a lower-risk design
  could give the same benefit.
Keep the discussion at the level of *consequences and access*. Committee papers should
not reproduce or elaborate hazardous methods.

## 4. Risk-mitigation plan
Agree with the PI, in writing, proportionate measures, for example:
- **Design modifications** that keep the scientific aim with less risk (e.g. attenuated
  or surrogate systems where valid).
- Physical/biosafety containment and **biosecurity**: access controls, personnel
  reliability/insider-risk measures, inventory and material-transfer controls.
- **Information controls**: data handling, who sees sensitive details, export-control
  checks for foreign collaborators.
- **Communication plan** (below).
- Monitoring: periodic re-review, and immediate re-review if results are unexpected.
The plan may need funder approval before work starts.

## 5. Publication and communication review
Before submission, presentation or preprint: consider whether methods or results provide
meaningful misuse uplift; options include fuller context on benefits and safeguards,
moving specific details to controlled-access supplements, timing coordination with public
health bodies, or — rarely — not publishing particular details. Engage journals (several
have dual-use review processes). Avoid blanket secrecy: openness benefits defence and
countermeasure development too.

## Gotchas
- Treating DURC review as a biosafety (accident) review — it is about **deliberate misuse**.
- Screening only at proposal stage; risk often appears in **unexpected results**.
- Over-classifying: flagging everything erodes researcher trust and committee capacity.
- Committee minutes that themselves become an information hazard.
- Using an out-of-date policy version (high risk right now).

## Verify
- [ ] The currently applicable policy and its scope lists were checked this review cycle.
- [ ] Screening outcome is recorded for all in-scope projects, with reasons.
- [ ] Risk assessment covers misusable element, plausibility, consequence and benefit.
- [ ] A written, proportionate mitigation plan is agreed and (if required) funder-approved.
- [ ] A communication/publication review step is scheduled before dissemination.
