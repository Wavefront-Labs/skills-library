# Review-level determinations and vulnerable-population safeguards

**Goal:** route each submission to the right level of review, with a documented rationale,
and apply the additional protections the regulations require for specific populations.

*For the primary-reviewer workflow on a full protocol (approval criteria, consent mark-up,
stipulations), see
`religion-philosophy.applied-ethics-and-bioethics.research-ethics-review.reviewing-a-protocol-as-an-irb-member`.
Scope here: US 45 CFR 46 (2018 Requirements). FDA-regulated studies (21 CFR 50/56) follow
different rules — e.g. FDA has no general equivalent of most Common Rule exemptions.
Other countries use different frameworks. Institutional SOPs govern; this is not legal advice.*

## 1. Decision sequence (document each answer)
1. **Is it research?** Systematic investigation designed to contribute to generalisable
   knowledge (§46.102(l)). QI, public-health surveillance and some journalism/oral
   history may fall outside — §46.102(l)(1)–(4) carves out specific activities.
2. **Human subjects?** A living individual about whom the investigator obtains
   information/biospecimens through **intervention or interaction**, or obtains, uses,
   studies, analyses or generates **identifiable** private information or identifiable
   biospecimens. Truly de-identified existing data → often not human-subjects research.
3. **FDA-regulated?** (drug, device, biologic test article) → apply 21 CFR 50/56 too.
4. **Exempt?** Check §46.104(d) categories (1)–(8): e.g. (1) normal educational practices,
   (2) surveys/interviews/observation of public behaviour, (3) benign behavioural
   interventions with adults, (4) secondary research use of identifiable info under
   conditions, (5) federal demonstration projects, (6) taste and food quality, (7)–(8)
   broad-consent storage/secondary use. Several require **limited IRB review**
   (privacy/confidentiality protections). Subpart restrictions apply (e.g. (2)
   interaction with children is limited; (3) is adults only; prisoners mostly excluded).
5. **Expedited?** Must be **no more than minimal risk** *and* fit one of the OHRP
   expedited categories (1)–(9) (e.g. (5) existing data/specimens, (6) recordings,
   (7) behavioural research, (2) limited blood draws within volume/frequency limits).
   Reviewed by the chair or a designated experienced member; the reviewer cannot
   disapprove — only refer to the convened board.
6. **Otherwise → convened (full board)** with quorum including a non-scientist.

Minimal risk: harm/discomfort probability and magnitude not greater than ordinarily
encountered in daily life or routine physical/psychological exams (§46.102(j)).

## 2. Who decides exemptions
OHRP recommends investigators **not** self-determine exemptions; most institutions have
IRB staff or a designee make and record the determination. Keep the category and rationale.

## 3. Additional safeguards
- **Subpart B — pregnant women, fetuses, neonates:** preclinical/clinical data on risks,
  risk to the fetus minimal unless direct benefit, consent rules (father's consent in
  certain fetus-benefit-only cases), no inducements to terminate.
- **Subpart C — prisoners:** only four permitted categories of research (plus an
  epidemiology waiver); board must include a **prisoner representative**; parole
  decisions unaffected; certification to OHRP for HHS-funded research. Applies when a
  participant **becomes** incarcerated mid-study too.
- **Subpart D — children:** classify each study by risk/benefit (§46.404 minimal risk;
  §46.405 greater than minimal with prospect of direct benefit; §46.406 minor increase
  over minimal, no direct benefit, generalisable knowledge about the condition; §46.407
  otherwise — requires federal panel). Record the category. Obtain **parental
  permission** (one or both parents depending on category) and the child's **assent**
  where capable; plan for re-consent at the age of majority.
- Others needing judgement (no subpart): adults lacking capacity (state law on legally
  authorised representatives), students/employees of the investigator, economically or
  educationally disadvantaged groups.

## 4. Continuing review (2018 rule)
Generally not required for expedited studies or those that have reached data analysis /
follow-up only, unless the IRB documents why; still required for most convened-board
studies. *Institutions may require check-ins anyway.*

## Gotchas
- Calling coded data "de-identified" when the investigator holds the key.
- Exempting research with children under category (3) — not allowed.
- Minimal-risk procedures with no fitting expedited category → convened, by rule.
- Forgetting the prisoner check when someone is incarcerated during follow-up.
- Pre-2018 vs 2018 requirements: older studies may still follow the pre-2018 rule.

- [ ] Research and human-subjects questions are answered in writing first.

- [ ] Subpart B/C/D findings are documented with the category (e.g. §46.405).
- [ ] Permission/assent, prisoner representative and limited-review steps are in place where required.
- [ ] FDA and non-US requirements were checked when relevant.
