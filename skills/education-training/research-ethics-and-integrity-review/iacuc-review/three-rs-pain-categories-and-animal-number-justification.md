# 3Rs, pain categories and animal-number justification in IACUC review

**Goal:** approve only animal work that is scientifically justified, uses the fewest
animals that can answer the question, and causes the least pain and distress possible —
with clear endpoints staff can apply.

*US framing: Animal Welfare Act/USDA (covered species), PHS Policy (PHS-funded work, all
vertebrates) and the Guide. UK (ASPA, AWERB), EU Directive 2010/63 and Australia (Code)
differ in categories and process. Follow your institution's SOPs.*

## 1. Review route
Each protocol goes to **full committee review** (convened quorum) or **designated member
review** (DMR) — DMR only if no member requests full review. A DMR reviewer may approve,
require modifications or refer to full committee, but **cannot withhold approval** alone.
Include the attending veterinarian's input on any procedure affecting pain or health.

## 2. Test the 3Rs
- **Replacement:** has the investigator shown that non-animal methods (cell culture,
  organoids, in silico, lower sentience organisms) can't answer this question? For USDA
  painful procedures (category E and many D), a **written alternatives search** is
  expected: databases searched, dates, keywords, and the result interpreted — not a
  pasted list of hits.
- **Reduction:** statistically justified numbers (below); pilot studies; sharing tissues
  and controls; longitudinal imaging instead of terminal time-points.
- **Refinement:** least invasive methods, appropriate anaesthesia/analgesia, training
  and habituation, enrichment, social housing, earliest humane endpoint.

## 3. Pain categories (USDA)
| Category | Meaning |
|---|---|
| B | bred/held, not yet used |
| C | no more than momentary/slight pain; no drugs needed |
| D | pain/distress relieved by appropriate anaesthetics, analgesics or tranquillisers |
| E | pain/distress **not** relieved — requires written scientific justification |
Challenge mis-assignment: surgery is D, not C; withholding analgesia "because it may
confound results" needs **evidence**, not assumption, and makes it E. Category E is
reported to USDA annually for covered species. *(Category letters are USDA reporting
terms; other systems use mild/moderate/severe.)*

## 4. Animal-number justification
Look for:
- **Power analysis** for hypothesis-testing experiments: effect size (and where it came
  from — pilot, literature), variability (SD), α, power (typically 0.8), test, groups,
  and the resulting *n* per group; plus expected attrition.
- For breeding colonies: a breeding scheme showing genotype ratios and how many animals
  are produced to get the needed experimental animals (often the biggest hidden number).
- For pilot/feasibility or tissue-harvest studies: explain the rationale (not power) and
  keep numbers small.
- A table: experiment × group × n × procedures × pain category, summing to the total.
Red flags: round numbers with no rationale ("10 per group"); n chosen "based on lab
practice"; totals that don't add up.

## 5. Humane endpoints
Require **objective, observable** criteria with a monitoring frequency and a named
responder: e.g. weight loss >20% from baseline, body-condition score ≤2, tumour diameter
limit, hunched posture with inactivity, inability to reach food/water. Death as an
endpoint needs strong justification. Specify euthanasia method consistent with the
**AVMA Guidelines for the Euthanasia of Animals** (current edition).

## 6. Amendments and oversight
- **Significant changes** (species, numbers beyond a threshold, new procedures, pain
  category, personnel competency, endpoints) need committee or DMR review; OLAW allows some
  minor changes by veterinary verification under an IACUC-approved policy.
- **Semiannual programme review and facility inspection**, deficiency classification
  (significant vs minor) with correction plans and dates.
- Report serious non-compliance or suspensions as required (OLAW/USDA).

## Gotchas
- Generic alternatives searches done once and copied across protocols for years.
- Under-powered studies are a 3Rs failure too — animals used with no reliable answer.
- Analgesia plans missing dose, route, frequency and duration.
- Personnel listed without documented training on the procedures.
