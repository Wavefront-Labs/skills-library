# IBC protocol review and containment-level assignment

**Goal:** each registered project is classified correctly, assigned containment that
matches its actual risk (not just the agent's name), run by trained people with workable
procedures, and monitored for incidents.

*US framing: the NIH Guidelines bind institutions receiving NIH funding for recombinant or
synthetic nucleic acid research; many institutions apply them to all such work and extend
IBC review to pathogens and biological toxins. Select agents add separate federal rules.
Other countries: e.g. WHO LBM risk-based approach, national gene-technology regulators.
This page is about committee review process, not laboratory technique.*

## 1. Classify under the NIH Guidelines
Identify which Section III category applies; this sets **who must approve and when**:
| Section | Examples (paraphrased) | Approval |
|---|---|---|
| III-A | deliberate transfer of drug resistance to agents that would compromise disease control | IBC + NIH Director (RAC/OSP process) |
| III-B | cloning toxin molecules with high vertebrate toxicity | NIH OSP + IBC |
| III-C | human gene transfer (clinical) | IBC (+ IRB); specific requirements |
| III-D | RG2–4 agents as host-vector systems, infectious virus work, whole animals/plants above minimal | IBC approval **before** initiation |
| III-E | e.g. some low-risk viral constructs, certain transgenic rodents | IBC notice at initiation |
| III-F | exempt (e.g. many E. coli K-12 systems) | exempt from the Guidelines (institution may still register) |
*Category text is condensed — read the current Guidelines for each determination.*

## 2. Risk assessment → containment
1. **Agent hazard:** start from the **risk group** (Appendix B of the NIH Guidelines;
   BMBL agent summaries). Risk group ≠ biosafety level automatically.
2. **Modify for the work:** what the construct does (gene inserted — oncogene, toxin,
   immune modulator), tropism changes, replication competence, concentration and volume,
   aerosol-generating procedures, sharps, animal work (ABSL), route of exposure.
3. **Personnel and facility:** training, immune status/medical surveillance needs,
   available equipment (biosafety cabinet certification, centrifuge safety cups).
4. **Assign BSL/ABSL** and specific practices; up-tier if the procedure raises risk (e.g.
   large-volume culture), down-tier only with a documented rationale.
Example reasoning: a replication-incompetent, third-generation lentiviral vector is
commonly handled at BSL-2 (sometimes "BSL-2+ practices"); an insert encoding an oncogene
increases concern for skin/sharps exposure. *(Practices vary by institution; follow your
IBC's written policy.)*

## 3. What else the committee checks
- Lab-specific **SOPs**: decontamination agents and contact times, spill response,
  waste stream, transport between labs.
- **Training records** for every listed person; PI's oversight capability.
- **Inspection** of the lab by the biosafety officer before first approval at BSL-2+.
- Animal work: cage labelling, bedding handling, shedding period — coordinate with the
  IACUC.
- Human gene transfer: alignment with IRB review and clinical site readiness.

## 4. Incident oversight
- Require prompt reporting to the biosafety office of spills, exposures, and loss of
  containment. Under the NIH Guidelines, significant problems, violations and
  research-related accidents/illnesses are reported to **NIH OSP** (generally within 30
  days, and immediately for certain higher-containment incidents — *check current
  Section IV-B text*).
- Committee reviews root cause and corrective actions, not just the event.
- Track trends across labs (repeat sharps injuries, BSC failures).

## 5. Committee mechanics
Membership per NIH Guidelines: ≥5 members incl. **two community members** not affiliated
with the institution; expertise as needed (biosafety officer for BL3/BL4 or large-scale
work, plant/animal experts). Minutes must be made available to the public on request —
write them accordingly. Re-review on a set cycle and on amendments (new agent, new insert,
scale-up, new species).

## Gotchas
- Assigning BSL by agent name alone without considering procedures and inserts.
- Registrations that drift: new constructs added without amendment.
- "Exempt" assumed by the PI rather than determined by the IBC/biosafety office.
- Ignoring non-recombinant pathogens and toxins because the Guidelines don't cover them.
