# Drafting complaints and escalating to review and ombudsman

**Goal:** get a wrong decision changed or poor treatment put right for the person you
advocate for, by using the right route, writing clearly, meeting deadlines and
escalating calmly until the process is exhausted. *Routes, names and time limits differ
by country, sector and service; always read the specific body's procedure.*

## 1. Complaint, review or appeal?
| Problem | Usual route |
|---|---|
| A **decision** you disagree with (benefit refused, care hours cut, school place) | Internal review/reconsideration → external appeal/tribunal |
| **How** you were treated (delay, rudeness, lost papers, policy not followed) | Complaints procedure → ombudsman |
| Unsafe care, professional misconduct | Regulator / professional body (and complaint) |
| Unlawful decision with no other route | Legal advice on judicial-review-type challenge — refer to a lawyer quickly; time limits are short |
Often you run both: appeal the decision *and* complain about the delay.
Ombudsmen usually require you to finish the body's own complaints process first and
generally don't look at matters that have a tribunal route.

## 2. Before drafting
- Get instructions: what outcome does the person want? (Apology, decision changed,
  money back, process fixed, compensation, assurance it won't happen again.)
- Build a **chronology:** date, what happened, who, evidence reference.
- Find the **standard** that wasn't met: the body's own policy, service charter, legal
  duty, published timescale. "They broke their own rule" is the strongest complaint.
- Note **time limits:** many complaint schemes expect complaints within about 12 months;
  reviews of decisions may be as short as a month. Diarise immediately.

## 3. Drafting the complaint
Keep it to one or two pages, with appendices.
1. **Heading:** "Formal complaint — [person's name, reference]". Say you act with consent.
2. **Summary:** two or three sentences of what went wrong.
3. **What happened:** numbered, dated points from the chronology.
4. **What should have happened:** the policy/standard each point breached.
5. **Impact:** concrete effects on the person (missed meals, debt, distress, lost
   school days).
6. **Remedy sought:** specific and reasonable.
7. **Response requested** within the body's published timescale; ask for it in writing.
Tone: firm, factual, unemotional. Avoid adjectives; let facts carry weight. Send by
a trackable route; keep copies.

## 4. Escalating
- **No response in time:** polite chaser citing their timescale; then escalate to the
  next stage anyway.
- **Response unsatisfactory:** go to stage two/the senior manager, explaining point by
  point what the response didn't address. Don't restate the whole complaint.
- **Ombudsman:** once internal stages are exhausted (or the body has unreasonably
  delayed), submit with the chronology, complaint, responses and why they're inadequate.
  Ombudsmen look for maladministration and injustice; frame it that way.
- **Appeals/tribunals:** submit grounds tied to the legal test; get specialist advice where possible.

## 5. Persistence without burning out the person
Processes are slow and designed for persistence. Keep the person informed; check they
still want to continue at each stage; share the load (you do the writing, they approve).
Acknowledge partial wins in writing and decide whether to accept or push further.

## Gotchas
- Complaining about everything dilutes the strong points — lead with the best two or three.
- Accepting an informal phone resolution without written confirmation.
- Missing a short review deadline while pursuing a slower complaint route.
- Signing a settlement that waives other rights without understanding it — get advice.
- Hostile wording gets a defensive reply; precise wording gets action.

## Verify
- [ ] Correct route(s) chosen: review/appeal for decisions, complaint for service failure.
- [ ] Chronology built; each failing linked to a specific standard.
- [ ] Complaint states impact and a specific remedy, with response deadline.
- [ ] All time limits diarised; chasers and escalations sent on time.
- [ ] Person consulted at each escalation and outcome confirmed in writing.
