# Repairs disputes and eviction response for renters

**Goal:** help tenants and residents get homes repaired, keep their tenancy where
possible, and use tenancy processes properly. **Tenancy law is highly local** (state,
province, country, even city) and changes often — notice periods, valid grounds, deposit
rules and tribunal names below are examples only. Check current official guidance and
refer to a housing lawyer or specialist tenancy service early, especially once
possession or court papers appear.

## 1. Triage: what's urgent?
| Situation | Urgency |
|---|---|
| Lockout, belongings removed, utilities cut off by landlord | Same day — may be illegal eviction; police/specialist legal help |
| Court/tribunal papers or a hearing date | Days — legal advice now; don't miss the date |
| Notice to leave / eviction notice | Days to check validity and options |
| Dangerous disrepair (no heat in winter, gas leak, electrical danger, serious damp) | Urgent repair route |
| Ordinary repairs, deposit disputes, rent increases | Weeks, but diarise deadlines |

## 2. Repairs and conditions
1. **Document:** dated photos and video, a diary of the problem and its effects (e.g.
   mould and a child's asthma), copies of all messages.
2. **Report in writing** to the landlord/agent (email or app, so there's a record):
   the problem, when it started, impact, request for repair by a reasonable date.
   Oral reports are hard to prove later.
3. **Chase** after the deadline, referring to the landlord's repair obligations in local law.
4. **Escalate:** local council/housing inspectorate (environmental health) for hazards,
   social housing complaints process and housing ombudsman, or the tenancy tribunal for
   a repair order or compensation.
5. **Don't withhold rent** unless a lawyer confirms the local law allows it — it
   commonly creates arrears that become grounds for eviction.
Where local law gives protection against **retaliatory eviction** after a repair
complaint, written records are what make it usable.

## 3. Negotiating with landlords and agents
- Keep it factual and polite; many landlords respond to a clear written request.
- Separate issues: repairs, rent, behaviour complaints.
- Propose specifics ("a contractor visit by Friday, access arranged with 24 hours'
  notice"). Confirm any verbal agreement by email.
- For arrears: a realistic repayment plan offered early, plus maximising income
  (benefits check, hardship funds), often prevents eviction.

## 4. Responding to an eviction notice
1. **Read it carefully:** type of notice, grounds, date, who served it, how.
2. **Check validity** against local rules: correct form, notice period, required
   documents (in some places deposit protection, safety certificates or licensing must
   be in order first), service method. Invalid notices are common — a specialist should confirm.
3. **The tenant generally doesn't have to leave on the notice date**; in most systems
   only a court/tribunal order and an authorised officer can remove them. *Confirm locally.*
4. Explore options: negotiate more time, arrears plan, defence at hearing, rehousing
   help (apply to the local authority's homelessness service early — leaving voluntarily
   can affect eligibility in some systems).
5. **Attend every hearing.** Defaults are the commonest way tenants lose.

## 5. Deposits/bonds
Check whether it was protected/lodged as required. Request return in writing; dispute
deductions with move-in/out photos and inventory. Use the scheme's dispute process or tribunal.

## Gotchas
- Tenants moving out under pressure before getting advice.
- Agreeing to "cash for keys" without understanding rights lost.
- Ignoring post — hearing notices get missed.
- Discrimination (disability, race, family status, benefits) in letting may be unlawful; note it.

## Verify
- [ ] Urgency triaged; legal advice sought once court papers or lockout arise.
- [ ] Repairs reported in writing with evidence and deadlines; escalated if ignored.
- [ ] No rent withheld without legal confirmation.
- [ ] Eviction notice checked for validity; hearing dates diarised and attended.
- [ ] Rehousing/homelessness service contacted early if eviction is likely.
