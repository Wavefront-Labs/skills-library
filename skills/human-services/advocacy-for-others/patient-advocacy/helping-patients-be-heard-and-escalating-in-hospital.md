# Helping patients be heard and escalating in hospital

**Goal:** make sure a patient's questions, wishes and concerns actually reach the people
making decisions about their care, and that concerns get escalated fast when something
seems wrong. The advocate is **not** a clinician: you don't interpret results, second-
guess diagnoses or advise on treatment — you help the patient (or their chosen
representative) ask, understand and decide. *Rights, escalation schemes and consent law
vary by country and hospital; check local policy.*

## 1. Before appointments and ward rounds
- Help the patient write their **top three questions** and any symptoms or changes.
- Useful question sets: "What are my options? What are the benefits and risks of each?
  What happens if I do nothing?" (Ask 3 Questions / BRAN: Benefits, Risks,
  Alternatives, Nothing).
- Find out when ward rounds happen and ask that the patient (or advocate) can be present
  or receive a call.
- Bring the medication list, previous letters, and any advance directive.
- Arrange a **professional interpreter** where needed — not family members, especially children.

## 2. Consent and treatment wishes
- Valid consent needs information, understanding, voluntariness and capacity. If the
  patient doesn't understand, ask the clinician to explain again in plain language, or
  later with the advocate present.
- Patients can refuse treatment (if they have capacity), ask for time, or ask for a
  **second opinion** — say so calmly to staff.
- **Advance care directives** and appointed health proxies/attorneys: make sure copies are
  on the record and that the team knows. Remind staff of documented wishes, e.g. about
  resuscitation, when relevant.
- If capacity is in question, ask how it was assessed and what support was given.

## 3. Escalating concerns
For **deterioration** ("she's not herself", rising pain, confusion, breathing changes):
1. Tell the nurse looking after the patient, specifically and early.
2. If not reassured, ask for the nurse in charge, then the doctor.
3. Many hospitals have a **patient/family-activated rapid response** route (e.g.
   Martha's Rule in England, "Call for help"/REACH-type schemes elsewhere) — find the
   number on admission and use it if worry persists.
Use a structured script (SBAR-like): "I'm worried about [patient]. Since this morning
[change]. They usually [baseline]. I'd like a doctor to review them in the next [time]."
Record names and times.

For **non-urgent concerns** (communication, dignity, delays): ward manager → patient
liaison service (e.g. PALS in the NHS) → formal complaint
(`human-services.advocacy-for-others.complaints-and-appeals-advocacy.drafting-complaints-and-escalating-to-review-and-ombudsman`)
→ health ombudsman or commissioner, or the professional regulator for misconduct.

## 4. Discharge
- Ask early about the discharge plan: where to, what support, equipment, medications,
  follow-up.
- If a discharge is unsafe (no care at home, unmanaged needs), say so in writing to the
  ward and discharge team, asking for a reassessment; record names and times.
- Ask for the discharge summary and medication list in plain language.

## 5. Records
Patients usually have a right to access their records. Request in writing; check
timescales. Correct factual errors through the provider's process.

## Gotchas
- Arguing clinical judgement — focus on communication, process and wishes.
- Escalating angrily: firm, specific and calm gets faster action.
- Assuming the next-of-kin is the decision-maker — legal authority depends on documents and local law.
- Staff changing every shift: keep a single sheet of key facts and wishes for each handover.
- Privacy rules: get the patient's consent for staff to discuss with you.

## Verify
- [ ] Patient's questions written and asked; answers understood in plain language.
- [ ] Consent conversations included options, risks and "do nothing"; interpreter used if needed.
- [ ] Advance directive/proxy documents are on the record.
- [ ] Concerns escalated in steps, with names, times and a clear request.
- [ ] Discharge plan safe and confirmed in writing.
