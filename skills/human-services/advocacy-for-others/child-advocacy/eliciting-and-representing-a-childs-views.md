# Eliciting and representing a child's views

**Goal:** make sure children and young people — especially those in care, in child-
protection processes, with special educational needs, or facing exclusion — have their
own views heard and taken seriously by the adults deciding about them. The child
advocate represents **what the child wants** (their wishes and feelings), which is
different from a guardian or social worker representing what's in their *best interests*.
*Roles, entitlements and legal frameworks vary by country; check local standards.*

## 1. Your role, explained to the child
In plain words, at their level:
- "I'm here to help you say what you think and make sure grown-ups listen."
- "I don't work for social services / the school." (Be honest about who funds you if asked.)
- "You decide what I say. I won't share things you tell me unless I'm worried
  someone is hurting you or you're not safe — then I'd have to tell someone, and I'd
  tell you first if I can."
- "You can stop seeing me if you want."
Check understanding by asking them to explain it back.

## 2. Eliciting views: match the method to the child
- **Under ~7 / limited verbal language:** play, drawing, puppets, picture cards, "three
  houses" (house of worries, house of good things, house of dreams), smiley-face scales.
  Observe and describe rather than interpret.
- **7–12:** activity alongside talk (walking, games, drawing), sentence starters ("The
  best thing about living here is…"), sticky notes, "if you had a magic wand…".
- **Teenagers:** treat as near-adults; messaging between meetings may suit them better;
  respect if they don't want to talk today.
- **Disabled or non-verbal children:** use their communication system (AAC, signing,
  objects), ask people who know them well what signs mean, and observe in different settings.
Tips: meet somewhere they choose; short sessions; open questions; avoid leading ones
("You'd like to see your mum more, wouldn't you?"); several meetings before big
decisions; check whether they want their views passed on as theirs or anonymously.

## 3. Representing them
- Agree **exactly what to say** and how: written in their words, their drawing attached,
  a letter or recording from them, or them attending with your support.
- In meetings (care reviews, child-protection conferences, school SEN/exclusion
  meetings), present their views *before* professionals summarise them, and as theirs:
  "Jordan has asked me to say…" Don't soften or reinterpret.
- Push for the child to attend part or all of meetings if they want to, with
  adjustments (shorter, fewer people, a pre-meeting with the chair).
- **Feed back** to the child afterwards: what was decided and whether their view changed
  anything — and why not, if not. Children notice tokenism.

## 4. Education advocacy
For special needs plans and exclusions: help the child (and parents) say what helps them
learn, what's hard, and what they want. Ensure their own views section of the plan is
genuinely theirs. In exclusion hearings, the child's account of events must be heard.

## 5. Independent visitors / befriending
Volunteer independent visitors (for children in care, in some systems) build a long-term,
non-professional relationship: regular outings, consistency, being someone not paid to
see them. Reliability matters most — don't cancel.

## 6. Safeguarding
If a child discloses abuse: listen, stay calm, don't question in detail or promise
secrecy, record their exact words, and report immediately per procedure.

## Gotchas
- Speaking to carers and professionals more than to the child.
- Gathering views once for a major decision; views change.
- Using the child's words selectively to support the outcome you favour.
- Over-promising outcomes you can't deliver.

## Verify
- [ ] Role and confidentiality limits explained and understood by the child.
- [ ] Method suited to age and communication; no leading questions.
- [ ] Views presented in the child's words, agreed in advance, labelled as theirs.
- [ ] Child told the outcome and why.
- [ ] Any disclosure recorded verbatim and reported immediately.
