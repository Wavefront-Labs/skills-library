# Writing shift notes and incident reports

**Goal:** records that tell the next worker what they need, show progress toward the
person's goals, and would stand up if read by the person, their family, a regulator or
a court. Assume the person will read them — they often have the right to.

## 1. Shift notes: the rules
- **Objective:** what you saw, heard, did. "Jo said 'I don't want to go' and stayed in
  her room until 11:00" not "Jo was difficult".
- **Specific:** times, amounts, durations. "Drank 2 cups of water (approx. 500 ml)".
- **Person-first and respectful:** no labels ("the feeder", "a runner"), no
  opinions on personality, no humour.
- **The person's voice:** quote them where it matters; record their choices.
- **Timely:** written during or immediately after the shift.
- **Correctable, not alterable:** errors corrected by the method your system uses
  (single line through on paper, initialled; an addendum electronically). Never
  delete, white-out or back-date. Late entries are labelled as late.
- **Only what's relevant:** support given, observations, goal progress, issues
  reported and to whom. Not gossip about family members.

A simple structure: **Support provided → Observations (health, mood, behaviour) →
Goal progress → Issues and actions (who told, when)**.

## 2. Link to goals
Every plan should have the person's goals. Write against them:
- Goal: "Cook my own dinner twice a week." → "Chose spaghetti; followed picture
  recipe; needed verbal prompts for steps 4 and 7; drained pasta with hand-under-hand
  support. Said 'I did it'."
This becomes the evidence for **plan reviews**: funding schemes such as Australia's
NDIS rely on documented progress and support needs, so vague notes can cost the person
support. Record both progress and continuing need honestly.

## 3. Incident reports
An incident is anything that caused or could have caused harm: falls, injuries,
behaviours of concern, medication errors, allegations, restrictive practice use,
property damage, missing person, near misses.
1. **Make safe** and get help first.
2. Notify your supervisor **verbally** immediately.
3. Write the report the same shift on the organisation's form:
   - date, time, location, people involved (initials or as the system specifies);
   - what happened before, during, after, in sequence;
   - injuries observed (no diagnosis), first aid given;
   - who was notified and when;
   - direct quotes;
   - witnesses.
4. Separate **facts** from your opinion; if you include an opinion, label it.
5. One report per incident; each worker who witnessed it writes their own account —
   don't collaborate on wording.
Some incidents are externally **reportable** within hours (see
`human-services.disability-support.disability-safeguarding.dignity-of-risk-and-abuse-reporting`).

## 4. Privacy
- Use only approved systems — not personal phones, WhatsApp, or notebooks taken home.
- Access only records you need for your work.
- Don't name other residents in a person's notes; use initials or the system's
  convention.
- Disclose to third parties only with consent or legal authority.

## Gotchas
- "Had a good day" tells nobody anything.
- Copy-pasting yesterday's note is detectable and undermines the record.
- Writing a note to protect yourself rather than to describe events reads that way.
- Under-reporting incidents to avoid paperwork removes the evidence for better support.

## Verify
- [ ] Notes objective, specific, dated, timed, signed, completed this shift.
- [ ] At least one entry linked to a named goal.
- [ ] Incidents verbally reported and written up the same shift, facts separate from opinions.
- [ ] Corrections made without deleting the original.
- [ ] Records kept only on approved systems.
