# On-the-job coaching and fading

**Goal:** the employee does the job to the employer's standard and is supported by
their manager and co-workers, not by you. A job coach who is still there every shift
after months has usually failed to fade. Programme names and funding rules (e.g.,
Disability Employment Services in Australia, Access to Work in the UK, vocational
rehabilitation in the US) differ — check what your role covers.

## 1. Before day one
- **Disclosure** is the employee's decision: what the employer and co-workers are
  told, by whom, in what words. Agree it in writing. You can't share diagnosis or
  history without consent.
- Learn the job **yourself**: shadow it or do it, write a task analysis of each duty,
  note the workplace's unwritten rules (break etiquette, who to ask, dress).
- Agree with the employer: your role, when you'll be on site, who the employee's
  supervisor is, how feedback flows.

## 2. Teaching the job
- Use **systematic instruction**: task analysis + prompting hierarchy + fading (see
  `human-services.disability-support.daily-living-skill-building.task-analysis-prompting-and-fading`).
- Teach the way the workplace does it, at production speed eventually — accuracy first,
  then speed.
- Build in natural cues: the job board, the timer on the oven, the colleague who
  starts break.
- Teach the social side too: greeting, asking for help, handling a complaint,
  calling in sick properly.

## 3. Task adaptation and adjustments
When a task is a barrier, change the task, not the goal:
- Checklists, colour-coding, picture sequences, jigs and templates.
- Re-ordering duties, swapping tasks with a co-worker, different equipment.
- Schedule or environment changes: quiet workstation, regular breaks, consistent
  shifts.
Many jurisdictions require employers to make **reasonable adjustments/
accommodations**; government schemes may fund equipment. Help the employer see
adjustments as practical, usually low-cost, and good for others too.

## 4. Employer and co-worker liaison
- The **supervisor** gives instructions and feedback; you support them to do that
  well, rather than becoming the employee's boss.
- Coach co-workers on how to explain tasks to this person (with consent).
- Raise problems early and specifically; don't hide performance issues, which
  surface later as dismissal.
- Respect the business: avoid busy periods for coaching conversations; follow site
  safety rules.

## 5. Fading
- Plan from the start: e.g., full shift → half shift → check-in at start/end →
  weekly visit → phone contact.
- Fade when the employee meets the standard **and** a natural support is in place.
- Monitor after fading: job changes, new managers and rosters are classic points of
  breakdown — re-enter briefly if needed.

## Gotchas
- Standing next to the employee all day makes them look less capable to colleagues.
- Doing the work for them to "keep the job" hides the gap.
- Workplace bullying, underpayment or unsafe tasks are not coaching issues —
  escalate through your organisation, and inform the employee of their rights.
- Agree on whether you wear a visible "support" identity on site.

## Verify
- [ ] Disclosure agreed with the employee, in writing.
- [ ] Task analysis written from doing the job yourself.
- [ ] Adjustments documented and agreed with the employer.
- [ ] Supervisor and a co-worker are now the main supports.
- [ ] Fading schedule followed; post-fade check-ins planned.
