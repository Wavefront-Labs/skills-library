# Mapping services and decoding eligibility

**Goal:** go from "I need help with X" to "I've contacted the service most likely to
help, and I know what they'll ask". Service systems are fragmented and named
inconsistently; most people give up at the first wrong door. This is general
self-help reference, not legal or professional advice — free advice services
(below) can check your specific situation.

## 1. Name the need, not the service
Write one sentence per problem, in plain words: "I can't afford rent this month",
"Mum can't shower safely alone any more", "My son needs a diagnosis assessment".
Separate them — each may go to a different system (housing, aged care, health,
income support, disability, legal). Note which is **most urgent**.

## 2. Where to search
In roughly this order:
1. **General information and referral lines** — e.g., 211 in the US and Canada,
   Citizens Advice in the UK, and community information services elsewhere. They exist
   to tell you which door to knock on.
2. **Government portals** for the relevant system (aged-care, disability,
   income-support, housing websites often have eligibility checkers).
3. **Your GP / primary care clinic** — many health and care services need a
   referral from them.
4. **Local council, library or neighbourhood centre** — local services that aren't
   online.
5. **Condition- or group-specific charities** (dementia, carers, autism, refugees) —
   often the best navigators for their area.
6. Peers who've been through it (see
   `human-services.service-navigation-and-self-advocacy.peer-navigation.walking-alongside-as-a-peer-navigator`).
Directories go out of date: phone to confirm a service still runs and is taking
people.

## 3. Decoding eligibility
For each service, find and write down:
- **Who it's for:** age, diagnosis/condition, residency or visa status, location
  (catchment/postcode), income or assets (**means test**), level of need.
- **How you get in:** self-referral, or referral needed from a GP/other agency;
  is there an **assessment** first?
- **What it costs**: free, means-tested fee, co-payment.
- **Waiting time.**
Words to decode: "assessment" (someone decides if you qualify and how much),
"means-tested" (depends on income/assets), "catchment" (only your area), "case
managed" (you'll have a named worker), "brokerage" (small funds for one-off needs).
If you can't tell whether you qualify, **ask** — or apply anyway; rules are often
broader than they look, and only a formal decision tells you for sure.

## 4. Choose the entry point
Prefer a door that leads to several services: an assessment that unlocks a package, a
case manager, a hub. For urgent safety issues (no food tonight, nowhere to sleep,
violence at home), go straight to crisis lines and emergency services first;
navigation can wait until you're safe.

## 5. Make the first contact count
Have ready: your one-sentence need, ID/reference numbers, a short history, what
you've already tried. Ask: "Am I eligible?", "What happens next and when?", "If not
you, who?", and the **name** of the person you spoke to. Log it (see
`human-services.service-navigation-and-self-advocacy.own-case-paperwork.keeping-your-own-case-file-and-contact-log`).

## Gotchas
- "We don't do that" without a referral onwards — always ask who does.
- Eligibility based on old information (income last year, past diagnosis) — check
  what period they use.
- Online forms can time out; draft long answers elsewhere first.

## Verify
- [ ] Each need written as one plain sentence, urgency ranked.
- [ ] At least one information/referral service contacted.
- [ ] Eligibility, entry route, cost and wait recorded for each candidate service.
- [ ] Every "no" came with an onward suggestion.
- [ ] Contacts logged with names and dates.
