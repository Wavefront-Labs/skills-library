# Keeping your own case file and contact log

**Goal:** be able to answer, in two minutes, "what did they say, when, who, and what's
due next?" Agencies lose documents, staff change, and memories fade; your own record
is often the deciding evidence in a review or complaint. Works for yourself or for
someone you care for (with their consent or legal authority).

## 1. Set up the file (one afternoon)
One section per **system** (e.g., income support, housing, health, aged/disability
care, school, legal). For each, the same five parts:
1. **Front sheet:** your reference/client number, the service's contacts (names,
   direct lines, emails, addresses), your worker's name.
2. **Contact log** (below).
3. **Correspondence**, newest on top.
4. **Evidence** (reports, letters of support, bank statements), with an index.
5. **Forms submitted** — a copy of every one.
Paper: a ring binder with dividers. Digital: one folder per system, files named
`YYYY-MM-DD_from-or-to_what` (e.g., `2026-10-03_from-housing_decision-letter.pdf`), so
they sort by date. Scan paper with a phone scanning app.

## 2. The contact log
A table, one row per contact, filled in **during or straight after**:

| Date/time | Who (name, role) | How (phone/email/visit) | What I asked | What they said/agreed | Next step + due date | Ref no. |

- Get the **name** every time ("Can I take your name, please?").
- Note call-back promises and wait times ("held 47 min").
- For important calls, follow up in writing the same day ("As discussed...").
A notebook kept by the phone works; so does a spreadsheet or notes app.

## 3. Deadlines and to-dos
A single list or calendar for everything across all systems: review deadlines,
reporting dates, appointments, renewals, document expiry (ID, concession cards,
plans), promised call-backs. Set reminders a week **and** a day before. Missing a
deadline is the costliest paperwork error (see
`human-services.service-navigation-and-self-advocacy.appeals-and-complaints.challenging-a-decision-reviews-appeals-ombudsman`).

## 4. Sending things
- Keep a copy of everything before you send it.
- Prefer channels with proof: online portal receipt (screenshot it), email with a
  read-receipt or reply, tracked/recorded post with the receipt kept, or a stamped
  copy if you hand-deliver.
- Never send the only original of anything important.

## 5. Chronology (for disputes)
When something becomes a dispute, write a one-page **timeline**: date — event —
document reference. Advisers, advocates, ombudsmen and tribunals all work faster from
this, and it shows gaps and delays clearly.

## 6. Security and privacy
- These files hold sensitive data: store paper securely; password-protect devices;
  back up digital files (an encrypted cloud service or a second drive).
- Share selectively: give advisers copies, not the whole binder.
- If you're managing a family member's affairs, keep their file separate from yours
  and keep the authority document (consent form, power of attorney) at the front.

## 7. Keep it going (15 minutes a week)
Open all post the day it arrives (unopened letters are where deadlines hide), file
it, update the log and calendar. Weekly review: anything overdue from them? Chase it.

## Gotchas
- Logs written days later lose the detail that matters ("they said it'd be fine").
- Phone screenshots scattered in a camera roll aren't a file — move and name them.
- Shredding too early: keep benefit and care records for several years — check local
  guidance on how long.

## Verify
- [ ] One section per system, each with a front sheet.
- [ ] Every contact logged with name, date and next step.
- [ ] All deadlines in one calendar with reminders.
- [ ] Copies and proof of sending for every submission.
- [ ] Backups exist; sensitive papers secure.
