# Hygiene, illness exclusion, allergies and emergency drills

**Goal:** fewer infections passed around, no allergy or medication errors, a space that's
checked daily, and staff who know exactly what to do in an emergency.

Educational reference, not medical advice. **Exclusion periods, medication rules and drill
requirements come from your local public-health authority and regulator** — keep their current
guidance on the wall. Hold current paediatric first aid, including anaphylaxis and asthma.

## 1. Hygiene routines
- **Handwashing** (soap and water, scrub at least 20 seconds, per CDC): before, during and after
  preparing food, before and after eating, after toileting, after changing nappies or cleaning up a
  child who has used the toilet, after blowing a nose, coughing or sneezing, and after touching
  an animal. Soap and water is the best option in most situations; use sanitiser (at least 60%
  alcohol, CDC) only when soap and water are not readily available.
- Cleaning schedule: high-touch surfaces, toys that are mouthed (set the frequency in your
  policy), nappy areas after each change, bodily-fluid spills with the right product and gloves.
- Cough/sneeze into elbow; tissues binned; noses wiped and hands washed.

## 2. Illness and exclusion
- Know your authority's **exclusion table**. In England (UKHSA guidance on gov.uk), people with
  diarrhoea and vomiting stay away from the setting until 2 days after it has stopped; other
  jurisdictions differ, so check locally.
- Children who are too unwell to join in — whatever the cause — should go home.
- Child becomes ill at care: separate with supervision, comfort, record symptoms and times,
  call the family to collect. **Call emergency services** for any life-threatening emergency,
  including suspected anaphylaxis.
- Notify the family community of notifiable or outbreak illnesses per public-health
  instructions without identifying the child.
- Staff are excluded under the same rules.

## 3. Allergies and food
- Each allergic child has a **current action plan with photo**, visible to staff, plus their
  medication on site and in date.
- At every meal: check each child's plan; serve allergic children first and separately;
  use clean utensils; read labels every time (recipes change).
- Watch for cross-contact: shared play-dough (wheat), cooking activities, birthday food from home.
- Anaphylaxis (NHS): use the adrenaline auto-injector, call 999 (or your local emergency number),
  lie the child down with legs raised (raise shoulders or sit up slowly if breathing is hard), do not
  stand or walk them, and if symptoms have not improved after 5 minutes use a second auto-injector.

## 4. Medication
- Only with written parental authorisation and in original labelled packaging, per policy.
- Two-person check where policy requires: right child, medicine, dose, time and route; record
  immediately with signature.
- Never give non-prescribed medicine on a phone request without your policy's process.

## 5. Childproofing and daily checks
Daily walk-round before children arrive (indoor and outdoor): gates latched, sharp/broken
items, small objects for under-3s, cords and blind strings, unsecured furniture that could
tip, chemicals locked away, outdoor area for litter, animal waste, sharps, water.

## 6. Emergency drills
- Practise evacuation and lockdown/shelter-in-place at the frequency your regulator requires (no figure given here),
  at different times of day (including rest time).
- Know assembly points, who carries the roll, emergency kit and medications, how to move
  non-walking babies (evacuation cots/carriers).
- Debrief each drill: time taken, what went wrong, fixes made.

## Gotchas
- Fever-reducing medicine given at home can mask fever at drop-off; ask.
- Expired adrenaline injectors are common — set reminders.
- New/relief staff are the weak point for allergies and drills; brief them every shift.
- The exclusion period above is England only; follow your own authority's table.

## Verify
- [ ] Handwashing and cleaning schedules followed and signed.
- [ ] Current exclusion table displayed and applied consistently.
- [ ] Allergy action plans current, medication in date, every meal checked.
- [ ] Medication given only with authorisation and recorded immediately.
- [ ] Daily safety check completed; drills done on schedule and debriefed.
