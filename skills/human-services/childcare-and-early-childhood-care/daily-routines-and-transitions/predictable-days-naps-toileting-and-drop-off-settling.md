# Predictable days: naps, toileting, mealtimes and drop-off settling

**Goal:** a day children can predict, with transitions that don't turn into battles — while
fitting each family's own routines.

Educational reference for carers; your service's policies and each family's agreed plan come first.

## 1. Make the day predictable
- Keep the **order** of the day consistent even if times flex: arrive → play → snack → outside
  → lunch → rest → play → home.
- Use a **visual schedule** (photos or pictures) at child height; point to what's next.
- Children cope with change better when told in advance: "After lunch today we have a visitor."

## 2. Transitions
Most behaviour flashpoints happen between activities.
- **Warning:** "Five more minutes, then pack-up." Then "Two more minutes." Use a sand timer or
  a song for younger children — time words mean little to toddlers.
- **Give a job:** carry the basket, hold the door, be the line leader.
- **Reduce waiting:** don't line children up long before they need to move.
- **Bridge with a ritual:** a tidy-up song, a hand-washing rhyme.
- Offer choices inside the transition: "Do you want to hop or tiptoe to the bathroom?"

## 3. Naps and rest
- Follow the family's sleep pattern and your safe-sleep rules for infants (see infant-care).
- Wind-down: dim light, quiet story, same order every day.
- Not every child sleeps; offer **quiet rest** (books, a soft toy) for non-sleepers.
- Don't wake or keep awake against an agreed plan without talking to the family — but do raise
  it if a child's nap is affecting their wellbeing.
- Check sleeping children per policy.

## 4. Toilet learning
- Follow the **child's readiness and the family's approach** — signs include staying dry
  longer, interest, telling you about wet/soiled nappies, able to pull pants up and down.
- Regular, low-pressure toilet offers (after meals, before outside, before rest).
- **Accidents are normal:** neutral tone, change them privately, no shame or punishment.
- Keep spare clothes labelled; send soiled clothes home sealed, with a note.
- Regression often follows big changes (new sibling, moving house) — tell the family, adjust.
- Persistent pain, blood, or very sudden regression → suggest the family see a health professional.

## 5. Mealtimes
- Sit with children and eat calmly together where possible; it's a social time.
- **Adult decides what and when; child decides whether and how much.** No forcing, bribing or
  "clean plate" rules.
- Offer new foods repeatedly without pressure.
- Allergies and dietary/cultural requirements checked against each child's plan *every* meal.

## 6. Drop-off and pick-up
- Short, warm, predictable goodbyes. Encourage parents to **say goodbye, not sneak out** —
  sneaking increases anxiety next time.
- Have a settling activity ready; a key person greets the child.
- For new children, agree a gradual settling-in plan (shorter days at first, a comfort item).
- Text or call the parent if a child settles, so they don't worry all day (if policy allows).
- At pick-up, release children only to authorised adults (check ID if unfamiliar).

## Gotchas
- Rigid timetables break for real children — consistent *sequence* matters more than clock time.
- Toilet-training methods differ across families and cultures; align with them, don't impose yours.
- A child who suddenly struggles with drop-off may be signalling something at home or at care —
  stay curious and record observations.

## Verify
- [ ] Visual schedule up and used; children can say what comes next.
- [ ] Warnings given before each transition.
- [ ] Rest-time plan per child matches the family's agreement and safe-sleep rules.
- [ ] Accidents handled privately and neutrally; families informed.
- [ ] Every meal checked against allergy and dietary plans.
- [ ] Children released only to authorised adults.
