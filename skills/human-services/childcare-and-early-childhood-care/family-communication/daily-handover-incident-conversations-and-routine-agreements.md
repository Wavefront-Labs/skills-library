# Daily handover, incident conversations and routine agreements

**Goal:** parents and guardians always know how their child's day went, hear about problems
from you first and accurately, and agree with you on how routines are handled.

Educational reference; follow your service's communication, incident and privacy policies.

## 1. Daily handover (both directions)
**At drop-off, ask:** How did they sleep? Eaten? Any illness, medication, bumps at home?
Anything different today (who's collecting, a big event at home)? Record what matters.

**At pick-up, tell:** one specific positive moment, then the practical facts (food, sleep,
nappies/toileting), then anything that needs follow-up. Specific beats generic: "She built a
tower taller than herself and was so proud" beats "Good day."

Keep it short at the door — parents are often rushed. For bigger topics: "Can we find ten
minutes tomorrow to chat about naps?"

## 2. Written day notes
- App, communication book or sheet — consistent format daily.
- Facts: times, amounts, observations. Don't put sensitive concerns or other children's names
  in shared notes.
- For in-home care, a shared notebook or message thread agreed with the family.

## 3. Incident conversations (injuries, biting, behaviour)
- **Tell the family the same day, in person or by phone** — not via a note only, and for any
  significant injury, immediately (follow policy).
- Order: the child is OK (or what care they needed) → what happened, factually → what you did →
  what happens next. Have the incident form ready to sign.
- Don't blame other children or name them (privacy).
- Don't minimise ("just a little bump") or dramatise; describe.
- Acknowledge the parent's feelings: "It's upsetting to see a mark on your child."
- If you made a mistake, say so plainly and describe what you'll change.

## 4. Routine agreements
Write down what you've agreed with each family, especially for: sleep (times, settling
method), food (allergies, cultural/religious requirements, preferences), toileting approach,
comfort items, screen time, behaviour language, who may collect.
- Revisit as the child grows; date each update.
- If a request conflicts with safety (unsafe sleep) or regulations, explain why kindly and
  offer an alternative — and involve your supervisor.

## 5. Disagreements and complaints
- Listen fully before responding; summarise their concern back.
- Separate what you can change from what you can't, and why.
- Move heated conversations away from the door and other families.
- Follow up in writing on anything agreed. Escalate to a supervisor per complaints policy.

## 6. Raising developmental or wellbeing concerns
Share observations, not diagnoses: "We've noticed he rarely responds to his name and is using
few words compared with earlier. Have you noticed anything similar? A check with your child
health nurse or doctor could help." Do this privately, with a supervisor where possible.

(Safeguarding concerns are different — see child-safeguarding; do **not** discuss a suspected
abuse concern with the family before following your reporting procedure.)

## Gotchas
- Separated parents: know the court orders or agreements on file — who can collect and who
  can receive information.
- Language barriers: use interpreters or translated materials for anything important, not the child.
- Only ever reporting problems damages the relationship; share good news regularly.

## Verify
- [ ] Two-way handover at drop-off and pick-up, with a specific positive each day.
- [ ] Day notes factual and free of other children's names.
- [ ] Every incident told to the family the same day and documented with signature.
- [ ] Written, dated routine agreement for each child.
- [ ] Custody/authorisation details on file and checked.
