# Ratios, headcounts, room flow and excursions

**Goal:** every child accounted for every minute, staff where they're needed, and a room
that runs without constant crowd control.

Educational reference. **Ratios, qualification requirements and excursion rules are set by
your regulator** and vary by country, state and child age — the figures you must use are
theirs, not anything here.

## 1. Ratios: all day, not on average
- Know your legal ratio for each age group and your service's (sometimes stricter) policy.
- **Breaks, toilet trips, a staff member walking a child to the office, a phone call** — each
  can silently drop you out of ratio. Plan cover before someone leaves the room.
- Mixed-age rooms: the ratio for the youngest children usually drives the calculation.
- Say out loud when you leave and return: "Leaving the room, back in 2 — Sam's got
  the reading corner."

## 2. Headcount discipline
- **Count at every transition:** inside→outside, before and after rest, before leaving any
  space, on and off transport, at regular intervals during excursions.
- **Count names, not just numbers.** "Twelve" can hide one missing child and one extra visitor's
  child. Use the sign-in sheet or a roll and match faces to names.
- Reconcile with sign-in/sign-out records; children signed out by a parent but still listed
  is a common confusion.
- Two-person check before declaring an area empty (outdoor area, bathrooms, under tables,
  cubbies).

## 3. Room flow
- **Zones:** quiet (books, rest), active (blocks, movement), messy (art, sensory), eating.
  Keep messy near water, quiet away from traffic paths.
- Staff positioned so every zone is visible; communicate when moving.
- Stagger transitions (half the group washes hands while the other half finishes tidying)
  to avoid crowding and waiting.
- Enough materials to reduce conflict; rotate resources to keep interest.

## 4. Mixed-age dynamics
- Protect babies and toddlers from older children's play (separate floor space, small items
  out of reach).
- Give older children leadership roles (helper, reader) and their own challenge.
- Watch for younger children being excluded or dominated.

## 5. Excursions
Before:
- Risk assessment (route, venue, water, roads, toilets, weather, crowds), written permission
  from families, extra adults to meet the excursion ratio.
- Emergency kit: first-aid kit, medications (inhalers, adrenaline injectors) and action
  plans, emergency contact list, phone charged.
- Children identifiable (bright hats/vests), but **no names visible on clothing** in public.

During:
- Each adult has a named group and knows their names.
- Headcounts at departure, arrival, every movement, and before leaving.

## 6. Missing child — act immediately
1. Raise the alarm; one person keeps supervising the rest of the group.
2. Search systematically, starting with hazards (water, roads, exits) and hiding spots.
3. **Within a few minutes** if not found: call police/emergency services and the family,
   notify management. Don't wait longer to "be sure" — follow your procedure's timing.
4. Record and report to the regulator per requirements.

## Gotchas
- Visitors, volunteers and students usually don't count toward ratio unless your regulator
  says they do.
- End of day is high risk: fewer staff, tired children, parents arriving — keep counting.
- Head-counting by clicker without names misses swaps.

## Verify
- [ ] Ratio maintained continuously, including breaks and toilet trips.
- [ ] Name-based headcounts at every transition, reconciled with sign-in records.
- [ ] Zones and staff positions give full visibility.
- [ ] Excursion: risk assessment, permissions, kit, groups assigned, counts logged.
- [ ] Every staff member can recite the missing-child procedure.
