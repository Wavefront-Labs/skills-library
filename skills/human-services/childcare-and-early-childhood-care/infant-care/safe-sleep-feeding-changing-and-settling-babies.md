# Safe sleep, feeding, changing and settling babies

**Goal:** keep a baby in your care safe, fed, clean and calm — following the family's routines
and your setting's policies, and recognising when something needs a parent or a doctor.

This is educational reference for carers, not medical advice. Your service's policies and
your local health authority's guidance take precedence; get infant first-aid/CPR training.

## 1. Safe sleep — non-negotiable
Every sleep, including naps and in your arms-then-down transitions:
- **On the back**, every time, from birth. Once a baby rolls both ways by themselves they can
  stay in the position they choose, but still go down on their back.
- **Firm, flat, level mattress** in a cot/crib/bassinet meeting current safety standards.
- **Nothing else in the cot:** no pillows, bumpers, loose blankets, soft toys. Use a
  correctly sized infant sleeping bag or a firmly tucked light blanket to chest height,
  feet at the bottom of the cot.
- **Face and head uncovered;** don't overheat (no hats indoors for sleep).
- **Never** let a baby sleep on a sofa, armchair, adult bed, beanbag, in a car seat or
  sling out of the car/while unsupervised, or on an inclined product.
- Check sleeping babies regularly (setting policy will set the interval — often every
  10–15 minutes) and look at breathing and colour, not just "asleep".

If a parent asks for something unsafe (tummy sleeping, a pillow), explain politely you
can't, cite your policy, and escalate to your supervisor; get medical-exception requests in
writing from the child's doctor.

## 2. Feeding
**Bottles (formula or expressed breast milk):**
- Follow the formula tin exactly — too much powder can dehydrate, too little underfeeds.
- Warm by standing in warm water; **never microwave** (hot spots). Test on inner wrist.
- Hold the baby semi-upright; **never prop a bottle** (choking, ear infections).
- Label expressed milk with name and date; store and discard per your policy; never feed one
  family's milk to another child — if it happens, notify per procedure.
- Discard leftover milk from a feed; don't reuse later.

**Solids (usually from around 6 months, per family plan):**
- Seated upright and supervised for every bite. Know choking hazards: whole grapes, nuts,
  popcorn, hard raw veg, round slices of sausage — cut small/lengthways.
- **No honey under 12 months** (infant botulism).
- Allergies: follow each child's action plan; know where adrenaline injectors are.

## 3. Nappy changing
1. Everything ready before you start; **one hand on the baby at all times** on a raised surface.
2. Gloves per policy; front to back for girls; clean skin folds; barrier cream per parent.
3. Dispose, clean the mat, **wash your hands** (and the baby's if they touched).
4. Note unusual stools, blood, very few wet nappies (possible dehydration) or rash — tell
   the parent at handover, or sooner if the baby seems unwell.

## 4. Settling
- Check basics: hungry, wet, tired, too hot/cold, wind, pain.
- Calm, slow handling; rocking, shushing, white noise, a walk.
- **If you're at your limit, put the baby safely in the cot and step away for a few
  minutes. Never shake a baby.** Call a colleague or the parent.

## When to escalate immediately
Breathing difficulty, blue or grey colour, floppiness, unresponsiveness, fever in a young
baby (very young infants with any fever need prompt medical assessment), seizure, a
bulging soft spot, refusing several feeds → call emergency services/follow your emergency
plan, then the parent.
