# Nanny and babysitter practice in the family home

**Goal:** the children are safe and happy, the house runs the family's way, and you and the
family have clear expectations — with no regulator or colleague to fall back on.

Educational reference. Employment, tax, registration and background-check requirements for
in-home carers differ widely by country — check local rules (uncertain; not covered here).

## 1. First visit: get the information
Before you are alone with the children, have (ideally written in a family handbook):
- **Emergency:** parents' numbers, a backup adult, the home address said exactly as you'd
  tell emergency services, nearest hospital, children's doctor.
- **Health:** allergies and action plans (where adrenaline injectors/inhalers live),
  medications with written dosing instructions and consent, conditions, recent illness.
- **House:** how to lock/unlock doors, alarm codes, where the first-aid kit, torch and fuse
  box are, pets' rules, out-of-bounds areas (pool, workshop, balcony).
- **Routines:** meals, bedtime steps, screen-time rules, comforters, how each child settles.
- **Rules:** discipline approach the family uses, visitors (usually none without permission),
  outings allowed, foods allowed.
- **Who may collect** the children and how you verify them.

Do a walk-through for hazards: medicines, cleaning products, blind cords, pool gates, stairs, window locks.

## 2. Working within house rules
- The family's rules apply even if you'd do it differently — consistency matters more for
  children than whose method is used.
- Raise disagreements privately with parents, never in front of the children.
- **Safety beats house rules:** unsafe sleep, unrestrained car travel or unsupervised water
  are lines you don't cross; explain why.

## 3. Lone-working safety
- Phone charged and on you; no headphones while children are awake.
- Don't open the door to strangers; don't tell callers you're alone with children.
- Babysitting at night: know how to get home safely; agree transport in advance.
- Have a plan for if *you* become unwell (backup adult).

## 4. School runs and outings
- **Car seats:** correct seat for the child's age/size, fitted properly; check every trip.
  Never leave a child in a car alone. Know your own insurance position if using your car.
- Walking: hands held near roads; agreed routes; children in sight in parks.
- Tell the family where you are going if it's not routine.

## 5. Privacy and professionalism
- **Don't post photos of the children** or the home on social media without explicit
  permission; don't share household details with others.
- Agree phone and screen use for yourself during work.
- What you see in a family's home is confidential — except safeguarding concerns, which
  you must report (see child-safeguarding).

## 6. Work agreement
For ongoing nanny work, a written agreement covering hours, duties (childcare only, or
housework too?), pay, overtime, holidays, sick leave, notice, and a review date prevents most
conflicts. Keep a simple log of hours.

## Daily handover
At the end of a shift: food, sleep, mood, any bumps, anything broken, messages — written or
by text (see family-communication).

## Gotchas
- **Scope creep:** more household tasks gradually added; revisit the agreement.
- Bath time and water: never leave a young child alone in the bath, even to answer the door.
- Teen babysitters: families should confirm they're mature enough and have emergency basics.

## Verify
- [ ] Emergency, health and house information received before working alone.
- [ ] Hazard walk-through done; medicines and chemicals out of reach.
- [ ] House rules known and followed; safety lines explained if challenged.
- [ ] Car seats correct; never a child alone in a car or bath.
- [ ] No photos posted; written work agreement in place for ongoing roles.
