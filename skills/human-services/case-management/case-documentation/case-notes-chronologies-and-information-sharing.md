# Case notes, chronologies and information-sharing decisions

**Goal:** a record that a colleague could pick up tomorrow, that the client could read
without feeling betrayed, and that would stand up in a review, complaint or court.

General professional reference. Privacy law, retention periods and recording standards are
jurisdiction- and agency-specific.

## 1. Case notes: the essentials
Write **the same day** (memory degrades fast; late notes look like reconstruction).
Each note:
- Date, time, type of contact (phone/visit/office), who was present, duration.
- **Purpose** of the contact.
- **What happened / what was said** — facts and direct quotes for anything significant.
- **Assessment** — your professional view, *labelled as such*.
- **Actions** — who will do what, by when.

A simple structure is DAP (Data, Assessment, Plan); some agencies use SOAP. Pick the
agency standard and stick to it.

### Fact vs opinion
| Weak | Better |
|---|---|
| "Client was aggressive." | "Client raised voice, said 'You people never help', left before end of session." |
| "Home was dirty." | "Kitchen benches covered in used dishes; strong odour; no food in fridge." |
| "Mum seems unable to cope." | "Worker's assessment: mother appears overwhelmed — she said 'I can't do this' twice and cried; based on this and missed appointments, risk to routine care is increasing." |

Describe behaviour, then interpret. Avoid jargon, diagnosis you're not qualified to make,
and loaded words ("non-compliant", "manipulative").

## 2. Write as if the client will read it
In many jurisdictions clients can request their records. Respectful, accurate language
protects everyone. If you can't justify a sentence to the client's face, rewrite it.

## 3. Chronologies
For complex or safeguarding cases keep a running chronology separate from day-to-day notes:
```
Date | Event (fact) | Source | Significance / action taken
```
Include significant events only: incidents, disclosures, moves, hospitalisations, court
dates, missed key appointments, changes of worker. Patterns (escalating incidents, repeated
missed health appointments for a child) often only become visible in a chronology.

## 4. Recording information-sharing decisions
Every time you share or decline to share information, record:
- What was shared, with whom, when, how.
- **The basis:** consent (and its scope), or a legal/safeguarding basis without consent.
- If without consent: why, who you consulted (supervisor), and whether the client was told.
- Requests you **declined** and why.

The decision not to share can be as consequential as sharing; both need a rationale.

## Gotchas
- **Copy-paste notes** repeat stale facts and are spotted in audits; write fresh.
- Third-party information (what a neighbour said) should be attributed and may be exempt from
  client access — know your agency's rules.
- Don't record in personal notebooks or messaging apps; use the case system.
- Corrections: add a dated amendment; never delete or overwrite the original entry.
- Recording formats named here are common but not universal (uncertain per agency).

## Verify
- [ ] Notes written same day with date, contact type, people present.
- [ ] Facts, quotes and professional assessment clearly separated.
- [ ] Language you would be comfortable reading aloud to the client.
- [ ] Chronology maintained for complex/safeguarding cases.
- [ ] Every information share (or refusal) recorded with its basis and consultations.
