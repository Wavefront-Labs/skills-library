# Progress reviews, reassessment and planned closure

**Goal:** each open case is either moving, consciously paused, or being closed on purpose —
never quietly drifting.

General professional reference; review intervals and closure rules come from your agency.

## 1. Run a review (scheduled)
At each review date in the plan, with the client:
1. **Go through each goal:** done / in progress / stuck / no longer wanted.
2. For stuck items ask *why*: wrong step, wrong service, barrier (transport, childcare,
   money), or the goal wasn't really theirs.
3. **Ask about changes** since last time: housing, health, relationships, legal, safety.
4. Update the plan: close finished goals, rewrite stuck ones, add new ones.
5. Re-score any outcome measure your agency uses (same tool, same way, so change is real).
6. Set the next review date.

Write a short review note: what changed, what was decided, next date.

## 2. Reassess when the situation changes
Don't wait for the scheduled date. Triggers for reassessment:
- Crisis (hospital admission, arrest, eviction notice, violence incident).
- New household member, a baby, separation, bereavement.
- New risk indicators or a disclosure.
- A key service closes the client or declines the referral.
- Repeated missed contacts.

A plan written for last month's situation is the wrong plan.

## 3. Disengagement
When a client stops responding:
1. Try varied contact methods over a defined period (phone, text, letter, outreach visit,
   via a consented third party) — use only *safe* methods if family violence is a factor.
2. **Consider risk first:** for a high-risk client, silence may be the warning sign; escalate
   per procedure (welfare check, supervisor) rather than just closing.
3. Document every attempt.
4. If closing for non-engagement, send a closure letter saying the door is open and how to return.

## 4. Planned closure
Close when goals are met, the client chooses to, they move out of area, or another service
takes over as lead. Good closure:
- **Discuss it ahead** (not at the last visit) so the client can prepare.
- Review what was achieved — clients often underestimate their progress.
- **Exit plan:** who they contact for what; early-warning signs and what to do; re-referral route.
- Confirm handovers to ongoing services are complete.
- Close every referral in the tracker and write a closing summary that would let a future
  worker pick up quickly.

## Gotchas
- **Case drift:** cases stay open "just in case" for months, inflating caseloads and hiding
  real need. Flag any case with no plan activity in your agency's threshold (e.g. 6–8 weeks).
- **Closing by drift** — the client vanished and the file was closed with no attempts recorded.
  Indefensible if something goes wrong.
- **Abrupt closure** after a long relationship can feel like abandonment; taper contact.
- Outcomes recorded inconsistently (different tools, different raters) produce meaningless data.
- Specific intervals here are illustrative (uncertain — confirm against your agency standard).

## Verify
- [ ] Every open case has a current plan and a review date in the future.
- [ ] Reviews recorded with goal status, changes and next date.
- [ ] Reassessment done after any trigger event.
- [ ] Disengagement: risk considered, attempts documented, supervisor consulted where high risk.
- [ ] Closed cases have a closing summary, exit plan and closure letter.
