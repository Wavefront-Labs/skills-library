# Entitlement mapping and benefit application support

**Goal:** the client receives every payment and concession they qualify for, applications
succeed first time where possible, and nothing is lost to a missed deadline.

General professional reference. Benefit rules are jurisdiction-specific and change often —
always check the current official guidance or a welfare-rights service. Do not give formal
legal or financial advice beyond your role.

## 1. Do a full entitlement map, not just the obvious claim
People routinely miss secondary entitlements. For each client, list:
- Main income payment (unemployment, disability, carer, age, parenting, etc.)
- Supplements: rent assistance, housing support, child payments, energy rebates
- Concession cards and what they unlock (transport, health, utilities, phone)
- Health-related: prescription costs, travel to treatment, aids and equipment
- One-off/crisis: hardship grants, advance payments, emergency relief
- Non-government: utility hardship schemes, charity funds, education grants

Use a reputable online benefits calculator or welfare-rights checklist where your country
has one, then confirm with the official source.

## 2. Build the evidence pack
Most refusals come from thin evidence, especially for disability/illness claims.
- **Medical evidence should describe *function*, not just diagnosis:** what the person can't do,
  how often, for how long. Ask clinicians to address the actual criteria; give them the criteria.
- Include ID, income, rent, care responsibilities, and statements from carers/support workers.
- Keep copies of everything submitted, with date and method (upload receipt, reference number).

## 3. Application support
- Help complete forms *with* the client; use their words for how their condition affects them.
- Clients often understate difficulty ("I manage") — ask about bad days, not best days.
- Note every reference number, call date, staff name and what was said.

## 4. Authority to act
Agencies usually won't discuss a client with you without formal authority. Get the client
to set up a nominee/authorised-representative arrangement or signed consent in the agency's
own format. Know its limits (enquiry only vs. acting on their behalf).

## 5. Deadlines and decisions
Track in your system:
- Claim lodged / appointments / documents due
- Decision date and **review/appeal deadlines** (often short — weeks, not months)
- Reporting obligations (income changes, address changes) that, if missed, cause debts or suspension

On a refusal: read the reasons, check against the criteria, gather missing evidence, request
internal review in time, and **refer to a welfare-rights or legal service** for appeals.

## 6. Overpayments and debts
Don't advise a client to just accept a debt. Check whether it's correct, whether hardship
waivers or repayment arrangements apply, and refer to specialist advice.

## Gotchas
- Missing a mandatory appointment can suspend payments — help clients diarise these.
- Online-only systems exclude people without devices, data or digital skills; help them get access.
- A change in one payment can knock out a linked concession — check knock-on effects.
- Programme names and thresholds change frequently; this skill deliberately avoids specific
  figures (uncertain by design — verify locally each time).

## Verify
- [ ] Entitlement map completed across income, supplements, concessions, health and one-offs.
- [ ] Evidence describes function against the criteria; copies kept.
- [ ] Authority-to-act in place and its scope recorded.
- [ ] All decision, review and reporting deadlines in the case system.
- [ ] Refusals reviewed within deadline and referred to welfare rights where appropriate.
