# Collaborative case plan with goals and review dates

**Goal:** a short written plan that the client recognises as theirs, that says who does what
by when, and that has a built-in date to check whether it is working.

General professional reference; use your agency's plan template and consent forms.

## 1. Start from the client's priority, not yours
Ask: "Of everything we talked about, what matters most to sort first?" If they pick
something you think is secondary (a driver's licence over debt), start there unless it
leaves a safety risk unaddressed — early wins build the engagement that later goals need.
Safety issues are the exception: they go in the plan regardless, explained honestly.

## 2. Write goals in their words, then make them workable
- Keep the client's phrasing as the goal: *"Get my kids back for weekends."*
- Under it, break into steps that are **specific, small and dated** (SMART is a fine check):
  - "Book parenting course intake — client — by 12 Nov"
  - "Write support letter for family court — worker — by 15 Nov"
- 2–4 goals at a time. A plan with nine goals is a wish list.

## 3. Name an owner for every action
Each line: **action / who / by when / how we'll know it's done.** Split actions between
client, worker and other services. If every action belongs to the worker, the plan builds
dependence; if every action belongs to the client, you may be off-loading work they can't
yet do. Aim for a mix that moves toward client-led over time.

## 4. Choose services deliberately
- Match services to the step, check eligibility and waitlist *before* promising.
- Offer choices where they exist (two counselling options, not one).
- **Consent per referral:** the client agrees to each service and to what information is
  shared with it. Record it. Consent can be withdrawn; say so.

## 5. Set the review date now
- Put a review date in the plan (commonly 4–12 weeks; follow agency policy).
- Also define **triggers for early review:** crisis, housing change, new risk, a service
  declining the referral.
- Give the client a copy in plain language (or a photo of it on their phone).

## Template
```
Client goal (their words): ____________________
Why it matters to them: ____________________
| Step | Who | By when | Done when |
|------|-----|---------|-----------|
Services involved + consent recorded: Y/N, date
Risks / safety actions: ____________________
Review date: ______   Early review if: ______
Signed / agreed (client + worker): ______
```

## Gotchas
- **Worker-written plans** that the client "signs off" fail quietly — they miss appointments
  for goals they never chose. Write it *with* them, ideally on screen or paper between you.
- **Vague actions** ("engage with mental health") can't be reviewed. Name the service and step.
- **Mandated clients** (court, child protection) have non-negotiable conditions: say which
  parts are mandated and which are theirs to shape. Pretending it's all voluntary erodes trust.
- Literacy, language and cognitive load: keep the client copy short; use pictures or audio if needed.
- Don't promise a service you haven't confirmed has capacity.

## Verify
- [ ] Each goal is in the client's words and they can say it back.
- [ ] Every action has an owner, a date and a "done when".
- [ ] Consent recorded for each referral and information share.
- [ ] Safety/risk actions present where risk was identified.
- [ ] Review date and early-review triggers written; client has a copy.
