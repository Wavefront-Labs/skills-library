# Homelessness to stable tenancy: the case pathway

**Goal:** get the person safely housed tonight, into a longer-term home as fast as possible,
and still housed a year later.

General professional reference. Housing systems, eligibility, and tenancy law differ a lot
between countries and regions — always check local rules; specifics here are illustrative.

## 1. Tonight first
- Where will they sleep tonight? Is it safe? Crisis/emergency accommodation, refuge (if
  fleeing violence), or a safe interim with family/friends.
- Basic needs: food, phone charge, medication, a safe place for documents.
- Risk screen (health, violence, self-harm, exploitation). Young people, people fleeing
  violence and people leaving institutions often have specific pathways — use them.

## 2. Gather the paperwork early
Applications stall on documents. Start a checklist in session one:
- ID (photo ID, birth certificate), income evidence, bank statements, benefit letters
- Rental history/references, prior tenancy outcomes
- Evidence supporting priority (medical letters, violence-related evidence via a specialist
  service, disability support letters)
Help replace lost ID immediately — it's often the slowest item.

## 3. Run parallel pathways
Social/public housing waitlists can be long. Don't rely on one route:
- **Social/public housing application** with priority evidence attached.
- **Private rental** with whatever products exist locally (bond loans, rent subsidies, head
  leasing).
- **Supported or transitional housing** where support needs are high.
- **Housing First** programmes where available: housing without readiness conditions, with
  wraparound support.

## 4. Private rental: preparing and liaising
- Help prepare a rental application/resume: references, income, a short cover note.
- With consent, contact landlords/agents as a support worker — knowing there's a worker
  attached reassures some landlords.
- Watch for discrimination (benefit recipients, children, race, disability) — record and
  refer to tenancy advocacy if it occurs.
- **Check affordability honestly:** rent above ~30–50% of income (rule of thumb, varies)
  risks quick arrears.

## 5. Move-in and the first 90 days (sustainment)
Most tenancies that fail do so early. Plan:
- Essentials on day one: bed, fridge, utilities connected in the client's name, rent payment
  set up (automatic where possible).
- Explain the lease plainly: rent dates, visitors, repairs, inspections, notice rules.
- Weekly contact in month one, then taper.
- **Early warning signs:** missed rent, complaints from neighbours, too many visitors staying,
  isolation, stopped answering. Act on the first one.
- Link to the community: GP, local services, groups — loneliness drives abandonment of tenancies.

## 6. Eviction prevention
Arrears or a notice: contact the landlord early with a repayment plan; check hardship funds;
refer to tenancy legal advice **before** any hearing date. Don't give legal advice yourself.

## Gotchas
- "Housing ready" demands (sobriety, treatment completed) before housing often keep people
  homeless longer; evidence for Housing First is strong in many settings.
- **Utility debts and old tenancy records** can block new tenancies — surface them early.
- Crisis accommodation that's unsafe for the client will be abandoned; involve them in the choice.
- Rent affordability thresholds and programme names are jurisdiction-specific (uncertain).

## Verify
- [ ] Safe place to sleep tonight confirmed and recorded.
- [ ] Document checklist started; missing ID replacement under way.
- [ ] At least two housing pathways active.
- [ ] Move-in checklist done (utilities, rent payment, lease explained).
- [ ] Sustainment plan with contact schedule and early-warning signs agreed.
- [ ] Any notice or arrears referred to tenancy legal help in time.
