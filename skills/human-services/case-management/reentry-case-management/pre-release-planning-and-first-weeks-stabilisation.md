# Pre-release planning and first-weeks stabilisation

**Goal:** the person walks out with somewhere to go, money, medication, ID and a named
contact — and gets through the high-risk first weeks without losing housing, breaching
conditions or overdosing.

General professional reference. Parole/probation conditions, discharge procedures and
leaving-care entitlements are set by law and local agencies; work within them and never
advise a client to breach conditions.

## 1. Start before release (ideally 4–12 weeks out)
Engage *inside* the institution where allowed (in-reach visits, video calls, joint meetings
with prison/hospital/care staff). Map:
- **Release/discharge date and time** — and whether it might change (early release, delayed
  discharge). Late-Friday releases leave no services open; flag them.
- **Accommodation** for night one and beyond (see housing-case-management).
- **Income:** lodge benefit claims pre-release where the system allows, so money arrives quickly.
- **ID:** release with photo ID if at all possible — without it, banking, benefits and housing stall.
- **Health:** medication supply on release, prescriptions, GP or clinic appointment booked,
  mental-health and drug/alcohol treatment continuity.
- **Legal conditions:** parole/probation reporting times and places, curfews, exclusion zones,
  no-contact orders. Get them in writing.
- **Family and relationships:** who's safe to reconnect with; any child-contact arrangements.

## 2. The day-one plan (one page, client keeps a copy)
```
Release: date/time/where      Who meets you: name, phone
Tonight you sleep at: _______  Getting there: transport/fare
Report to parole/probation: where, by when
Medication: what you have, how many days, pharmacy
Money: what you have, when first payment arrives
Phone: charged/credit? Key numbers saved
Day 2–3 appointments: _______
If something goes wrong call: _______ (24h option too)
```
**Meet the person at the gate or the hospital door** where possible. Gate pick-up
dramatically reduces day-one drift.

## 3. First 72 hours to first weeks
- Daily contact in the first days, then taper.
- Practical errands together: bank, benefit office, pharmacy, phone, ID.
- Check-in on conditions compliance — missed first reports are a common early breach.
- **Overdose risk is sharply higher in the first weeks after prison release** because
  tolerance has dropped. Provide harm-reduction information and naloxone where available,
  and link to treatment. (Well documented internationally; confirm local naloxone access.)
- Suicide risk is also elevated in the post-release period — screen and act per procedure.
- Watch for old networks pulling the person back; help build new routines (work, study, groups).

## 4. Leaving out-of-home care
Young care leavers often lose support abruptly at a set age. Know local leaving-care
entitlements (extended support, financial help, transition workers) and start planning
well before the birthday, not after.

## Gotchas
- **Release dates move.** Confirm 48 hours out and on the morning.
- "Discharged to no fixed address" from hospital should trigger escalation, not acceptance.
- Clients often agree to every plan inside and abandon it outside — involve them in realistic choices.
- Don't become an enforcer of conditions; be clear about what you must report (agency rules)
  and say it upfront.
- Timeframes above are typical practice, not standards (uncertain — check local programme).

## Verify
- [ ] Night-one accommodation, ID, medication, money and phone confirmed before release.
- [ ] Written day-one plan held by client; gate/door meeting arranged.
- [ ] Conditions known in writing; first reporting appointment diarised.
- [ ] Overdose and suicide risk addressed; naloxone/harm-reduction info given where relevant.
- [ ] Daily contact scheduled for first days; taper plan set.
