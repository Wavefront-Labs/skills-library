# Caseload triage and protecting the quiet cases

**Goal:** every client on your list gets attention proportionate to their need, crises get
handled, and the clients who don't make noise still get seen.

General professional reference. Caseload caps and weighting tools are set by agencies and
funders; use theirs.

## 1. Know your whole list
Keep one live view of all open cases with: client, priority, last contact date, next
planned action/date, review date, open referrals. If you can't produce this in two minutes,
fix that before anything else (see case-management-systems).

## 2. Weekly caseload review (30–45 min, same time each week)
Go through every case and RAG it:
- **Red** — active risk, crisis, or a deadline this week (court, eviction, benefit cut-off).
- **Amber** — progressing but needs a planned action this week.
- **Green** — stable, next contact can wait until its scheduled date.

Then:
1. Schedule red and amber actions into the week *now*.
2. **Contact-gap check:** sort by last contact. Anyone beyond your threshold (e.g. 3–4 weeks
   for amber/green) gets a contact this week, even a short call.
3. Any case overdue for review goes into the diary.

## 3. Protect routine time
Crises expand to fill the week. Block fixed time for: home visits, writing notes (daily —
same day), referral follow-ups, and reviews. Treat those blocks as appointments.

## 4. Crisis-day rules
When a crisis takes the day:
- Cancel *by priority*: postpone greens first, never silently skip a red.
- Tell clients you're rescheduling — a no-show by the worker damages trust more than a
  rescheduled call.
- At day's end, note what was displaced and re-slot it.
- Tell your supervisor or team lead if a crisis displaces red/amber work; cover may be needed.

## 5. The quiet-case problem
Quiet clients — compliant, not chasing, not in crisis — are the most likely to be missed.
Their silence may mean stability, or isolation, deterioration or giving up. Safeguards:
- The contact-gap check above, run religiously.
- Scheduled reviews regardless of noise.
- Ask quiet clients directly: "Is there anything you haven't been telling me because you
  didn't want to bother me?"

## 6. Use supervision for load, not just cases
Bring a caseload summary (counts by RAG, overdue reviews, cases you're stuck on) to
supervision. If load is unsafe, say so in writing — that's a professional responsibility,
not a personal failing. Ask for cases to be reallocated, closed or held centrally.

## Gotchas
- **Raw case counts mislead.** Ten complex cases can outweigh thirty simple ones; use a weighting
  if your agency has one, or at least count reds separately.
- **Notes backlog** is a hidden risk: if you're off sick, no one can pick up your cases.
- Working unpaid overtime to cope hides the real staffing problem from managers.
- Burnout signs (dreading certain clients, cynicism, missed details) — raise early.
- Threshold numbers here are examples (uncertain; agency-specific).

## Verify
- [ ] One current list of all open cases with last-contact and next-action dates.
- [ ] Weekly RAG review done and red/amber actions diarised.
- [ ] No case beyond contact-gap threshold without a scheduled contact.
- [ ] Case notes no more than one working day behind.
- [ ] Caseload summary taken to supervision; unsafe load escalated in writing.
