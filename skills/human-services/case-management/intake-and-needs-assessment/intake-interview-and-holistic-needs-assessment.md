# Intake interview and holistic needs assessment

**Goal:** in one or two contacts, decide whether your service is the right door, understand
the person's situation across their whole life (not just the presenting problem), catch
risk early, and record a priority you could defend to a supervisor.

This is general professional reference. Your agency's intake form, eligibility rules,
risk tools and consent wording take precedence; where they differ from this, follow them
and raise the gap in supervision.

## 1. Before the interview
- **Screen eligibility first, briefly.** Catchment, age, income or referral criteria — five
  minutes on the phone saves a person retelling their story to a service that cannot help.
  If ineligible, give a *warm* onward referral (see referral-and-coordination), not just a number.
- Read what already exists (referral letter, prior closed files) so you don't make them repeat it.
- Book enough time (60–90 min is typical for a full intake), a private room, and an interpreter
  if needed — **never use the client's child or partner as interpreter** for intake.

## 2. Open the interview
1. Who you are, what the service can and cannot do, roughly how long this takes.
2. **Consent and confidentiality limits, in plain words:** what you record, who sees it, and
   the exceptions (serious risk to self or others, child-protection concerns, court orders).
   Say this *before* disclosures, not after.
3. Ask what *they* want help with today — their words go in the record verbatim.

## 3. Assess across domains
Work conversationally, not down the form. Common domains:

| Domain | Example questions |
|---|---|
| Housing | Where did you sleep last night? Is it safe? Can you stay there next week? |
| Income & debts | What comes in each fortnight/month? Any arrears, fines, cut-off notices? |
| Health & mental health | Any conditions, medications, current GP/clinician? |
| Substance use | Any alcohol or drug use affecting things right now? |
| Safety | Is anyone hurting or frightening you? Anyone you're worried about? |
| Family & caring | Who depends on you? Who do you depend on? |
| Legal | Any court dates, orders, police contact, visa issues? |
| Education/work | What would you like to be doing? |
| Strengths & supports | What has got you through before? Who's on your side? |

Record **strengths** alongside needs — plans built only on deficits stall.

## 4. Screen for risk every time
- Ask directly about suicide/self-harm thoughts and family violence when indicators appear —
  asking does not plant the idea. Use your agency's screening tool if it has one.
- Any child in the household: consider wellbeing and safety concerns; know your reporting duty.
- **Immediate danger → stop the intake and follow your emergency/risk procedure, then tell
  your supervisor the same day.** Intake can resume later.

## 5. Prioritise
Rate urgency on explicit criteria, e.g.:
- **Priority 1 (same day/48 h):** homeless tonight, safety risk, imminent eviction or cut-off,
  no food/medication.
- **Priority 2 (2 weeks):** deteriorating but currently safe.
- **Priority 3 (waitlist):** stable, needs are real but not time-critical.

Write the *reason* for the rating. Waitlisted clients need a re-contact date and a
"call us if X happens" instruction — waitlists are where risk hides.

## Gotchas
- **The presenting problem is rarely the whole problem.** A rent-arrears referral often sits on
  top of a gambling, health or violence issue disclosed only once trust exists.
- **Over-assessing:** a 40-page form in session one re-traumatises. Get what you need to act;
  deepen later.
- **Tick-box consent** isn't informed consent. Check understanding.
- Clients may minimise to avoid being judged "too complex" or overstate to get priority —
  look for consistency, not deception.
- Thresholds and timeframes above are illustrative; agencies vary (uncertain — confirm locally).

## Verify
- [ ] Eligibility confirmed or warm onward referral made.
- [ ] Consent and confidentiality limits explained and documented before disclosure.
- [ ] All domains touched, strengths recorded, client's own goal in their words.
- [ ] Risk screened; any immediate risk escalated per procedure and to supervisor.
- [ ] Priority rating with written rationale; waitlist re-contact date set.
