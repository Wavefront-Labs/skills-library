# Attendance casework and home-school liaison

**Goal:** get a struggling student back into learning by finding out *why* they are
struggling, then aligning school and family around a plan. General professional
reference; attendance law, legal-action thresholds and safeguarding roles differ by
country and state.

## 1. Read the data first
- Pull the attendance record: overall %, pattern (Mondays? after weekends at Dad's?
  particular lessons? mornings only?), trend over terms. Many systems call under 90%
  "persistent" or "chronic" absence — check your definition.
- Look at behaviour logs, grades, nurse visits, prior referrals, and sibling attendance.
  Patterns point to causes.

## 2. Find the function of the absence
Common drivers, each needing a different response:
- **Anxiety / emotionally based school avoidance:** the student wants to attend but
  can't; forcing them in without support usually backfires. Graded return, a safe base,
  a trusted adult, adjusted timetable, CAMHS/health referral where indicated.
- **Unmet learning needs:** undiagnosed or unsupported SEN; get an assessment.
- **Bullying or peer conflict:** investigate with pastoral staff.
- **Family pressures:** young carer duties, illness, poverty (no uniform, no fare),
  housing instability, family violence, parent's own school trauma.
- **Disengagement / truancy:** the student chooses to be elsewhere — who with, doing what?
  Exploitation risk must be considered.
Ask the student first, alone, in a low-pressure setting.

## 3. Home-school liaison
- Make first contact warm and non-punitive ("we've noticed and we want to help"). Home
  visits often get further than letters.
- Be the translator both ways: school language → plain language for families; family
  context → actionable information for teachers (with consent and need-to-know).
- Agree a written **attendance plan**: specific supports, who does what, the student's
  goals, a review date. Celebrate small gains.
- Use interpreters for parents with limited English; don't use the student.
- Escalation to formal/legal attendance measures follows your system's rules and is
  usually the last tier, not the first.

## 4. Student crises
Disclosures of abuse, self-harm, suicidal talk, or a student in acute distress go
through the **school's safeguarding procedure** — usually to the designated safeguarding
lead the same day — and mandatory-reporting duties where they apply. Don't promise
secrecy; tell the student what happens next. For suicide risk, follow your district
protocol (see `human-services.social-work.crisis-intervention.immediate-safety-de-escalation-and-suicide-risk-screening`).

## 5. Referring on
Know the local map: family support, housing, food banks, youth mental health, disability
services, youth workers. Warm referral (introduce, follow up) beats a phone number on a
sticky note.

## Gotchas
- Treating every absence as truancy; anxiety-driven avoidance worsens under punishment.
- Sharing family details with teaching staff beyond need-to-know.
- Losing the student's voice when adults negotiate.
- Pressure to improve the school's statistics distorting the child's interests.
- Complex cases (exploitation, child protection, mental-health crisis): take them to
  supervision and the school's wellbeing/multidisciplinary team.

## Verify
- [ ] Attendance pattern analysed, not just the headline %.
- [ ] Student interviewed alone; hypothesised cause written down.
- [ ] Family contacted in a non-punitive way, interpreter if needed.
- [ ] Written plan with named supports and review date.
- [ ] Any safeguarding concern passed to the DSL the same day and recorded.
