# Designing and co-facilitating a support group

**Goal:** run a group where members get something they couldn't get one-to-one —
mutual aid, normalising, skills practice — safely and with a clear purpose.
General professional reference. Groups that aim to *treat* a clinical condition need
appropriately qualified clinical leadership; check scope with your supervisor.

## 1. Design
- **Purpose in one sentence** ("Parents of teens learning de-escalation skills, 6 weeks").
- **Type:** support, psychoeducational, skills, task, or therapeutic. It drives size,
  structure and facilitator qualifications.
- **Open or closed:** closed groups build trust and allow a curriculum; open groups suit
  drop-in support but need a strong ritual for welcoming newcomers.
- **Size:** roughly 6–10 for discussion-based groups; psychoeducational groups can be larger.
- **Logistics:** accessible venue, consistent time, childcare/transport, interpreter,
  online platform with privacy settings if remote.
- **Evaluation:** decide up front what you'll measure (attendance, a short pre/post scale,
  members' own goals).

## 2. Screening and preparation
Meet each prospective member beforehand: explain purpose and format, hear their goals,
check fit. Screen out (and refer elsewhere) people in acute crisis, people whose presence
could endanger others (e.g. a perpetrator and survivor from the same relationship), or
whose needs the group can't meet. Explain confidentiality and its limits now.

## 3. The first session
- Welcome and introductions with a low-risk prompt.
- Co-create a **group agreement**: confidentiality (and its limits), one voice at a time,
  right to pass, phones, lateness, contact outside the group.
- Clarify roles of facilitators. Set the shape of each session (check-in, content,
  practice, check-out).

## 4. Stages of group life (watch for them)
Tuckman: forming, storming, norming, performing, adjourning. Garland, Jones & Kolodny add
pre-affiliation and power-and-control phases. Practically:
- **Early:** members test safety; be more directive and structured.
- **Conflict:** challenges to the facilitator or each other are normal — name the process,
  don't punish it.
- **Working:** step back; let members help each other.
- **Ending:** prepare from a few sessions out; review progress, acknowledge loss, plan
  what comes next.

## 5. Difficult dynamics
- **Monopoliser:** acknowledge, then redirect — "Thanks, I'd like to hear from others on this."
- **Silent member:** invite gently, use pairs/rounds, accept the right to pass.
- **Scapegoating:** interrupt it; reflect what the group is doing; protect the member.
- **Disclosure of risk in group:** acknowledge, contain, and follow up individually the
  same day under your safeguarding/crisis protocol.
- **Subgrouping:** vary pairings; bring outside-group alliances back to the agreement.

## 6. Co-facilitation
Plan roles before each session (who leads content, who watches the room). Agree signals
for stepping in. **Debrief after every session**: what happened, member concerns,
your reactions, plan for next time. Disagree in private, not in front of the group.
Bring co-working tensions and members of concern to supervision.

## Verify
- [ ] Purpose, type, size and evaluation decided before recruitment.
- [ ] Every member screened and prepared; unsafe combinations avoided.
- [ ] Group agreement written with members in session one.
- [ ] Risk disclosures followed up individually the same day.
- [ ] Debrief after every session; ending planned in advance.
