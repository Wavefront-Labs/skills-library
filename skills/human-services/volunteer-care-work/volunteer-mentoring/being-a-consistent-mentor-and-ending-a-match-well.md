# Being a consistent mentor and ending a match well

**Goal:** be a steady adult in someone's life for the length of the match — encouraging
their own goals, showing up reliably, and leaving them stronger rather than let down.
Volunteer reference; your scheme's rules on contact, activities and safeguarding come first.

## 1. Consistency is the intervention
Research on youth mentoring consistently suggests matches that end early (often in the first
months) can leave young people worse off than no match — especially those who've already
experienced adults leaving. So:
- Only sign up for the length you can genuinely commit to (many schemes ask for 12 months).
- Meet at the agreed frequency, same time if possible. Cancel only when you must, with notice.
- Expect to be tested (no-shows, short answers). Keep turning up.

## 2. The first months
- Low-pressure activities side by side (a walk, a game, food) — talking is easier when
  you're doing something.
- Learn their interests; remember details and ask about them next time.
- Let the relationship grow before pushing goals. Trust first, goals second.

## 3. Encouraging without rescuing
- Help them set **their own** goals (school, work, a skill, confidence) and break them
  into small steps. Celebrate effort as well as results.
- Ask, don't tell: "What do you think you could try?" "What's worked before?"
- **Avoid rescuing:** doing it for them, lending money, solving every problem, or taking
  sides against parents/teachers. Rescuing can feel kind but undermines their agency
  (the "drama triangle" trap: rescuer → victim → persecutor).
- You're not a parent, therapist or caseworker. Bigger problems → your match coordinator,
  who can link to the right service.

## 4. Boundaries and safeguarding
- Meet only in the settings and ways your scheme allows (public places, community sites,
  approved activities). Follow rules on transport and being alone together.
- **Contact** only through approved channels; no private social-media messaging unless the
  scheme explicitly allows and monitors it. Keep messages appropriate and visible.
- Gifts and money: follow policy (usually token only).
- With adult mentees (e.g. ex-offenders, people in recovery): equally clear boundaries
  around money, relationships and home visits.
- **Disclosures:** listen, don't promise secrecy, tell your coordinator the same day;
  immediate danger → emergency services. Record what was said as soon as you can.

## 5. Use your match support
Regular check-ins with your coordinator aren't paperwork — they are where you get help when
you're stuck, worried or tempted to rescue. Raise concerns early.

## 6. Ending well
- Plan endings with the coordinator; give the mentee notice (weeks, not days).
- Review together what they've achieved; give them something to keep (a card, a photo, a
  written note about their strengths).
- Be honest about what happens next (contact after closure is usually not allowed or is
  limited by scheme rules).
- Unplanned endings (you move, they drop out): still do a closure conversation or letter
  through the scheme if at all possible.

## Verify
- [ ] Commitment length matches what you can genuinely give.
- [ ] Meetings happen at the agreed frequency, cancellations with notice.
- [ ] Goals are the mentee's, not yours; you ask more than you tell.
- [ ] All contact via approved channels and settings.
- [ ] Ending planned with notice and a closure conversation.
