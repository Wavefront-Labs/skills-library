# Safe home visits, errands and volunteer driving

**Goal:** give practical help in people's homes and communities — a visit, the weekly
shop, a lift to an appointment — in a way that keeps both of you safe and stays inside
what your scheme covers. Volunteer reference only; your scheme's policies and insurance
define what you can do.

## 1. Before you go
- Know the visit details from your coordinator: address, who lives there, pets, access
  needs, any known risks.
- **Lone-working check-in:** tell the scheme (or buddy) when you're going in and when you
  expect to leave; check out afterwards. Know the escalation if you don't check out.
- Phone charged, ID badge, scheme contact number saved.

## 2. At the door and in the home
- Show your ID badge; say who you are and which scheme sent you. Never pressure to be let in.
- Wipe feet/offer to remove shoes; ask where to sit. It's their home and their rules.
- Note your exits; if anything feels unsafe (aggression, intoxication, an unknown person,
  a dangerous dog), make an excuse and leave. Report it.
- Don't go into rooms you're not invited into; don't look through post or papers.

## 3. Shopping and errands
- Agree the list in writing (or a photo of it) and a spending limit.
- **Money:** follow your scheme rule exactly. Typically: no use of the person's bank card or
  PIN; cash counted in front of them; **receipt and change returned** and both counted back.
  Some schemes use a log sheet both sign.
- Prescription collection: only if the scheme permits it; pharmacies may need authorisation.
- Don't buy alcohol or anything outside the agreed list without checking with your coordinator.

## 4. Volunteer driving
- Check your **insurance** covers volunteer driving (many insurers require you to tell
  them; it often isn't a premium change, but confirm). Valid licence, roadworthy car,
  any checks the scheme requires.
- Know the passenger's mobility needs; don't lift them (help only as trained); fold and
  load wheelchairs only if you're shown how.
- Mileage expenses at your scheme's rate; don't accept payment from passengers unless the
  scheme says so — it can affect insurance.
- Wait times and hospital pick-ups: agree with the passenger and scheme what happens if an
  appointment overruns.

## 5. Staying within the scheme's limits
Common "no"s: personal care, medication handling, ladders and DIY beyond simple tasks,
gardening with power tools, lending money, witnessing documents, giving financial or legal
advice, visiting outside arranged times. When asked: "I'm not allowed to do that, but I'll
ask the coordinator who can help."

## 6. Noticing and reporting
Signs of concern: cold or unsafe home, no food, falls, confusion, unpaid bills, doorstep
scammers or rogue traders, someone controlling their money, injuries, fear. **Tell your
coordinator the same day.** Emergency (someone collapsed, a fire, immediate danger) →
emergency services first. Don't investigate and don't promise secrecy.

## Verify
- [ ] Check-in and check-out done for every visit.
- [ ] ID shown at the door; you'd leave if it felt unsafe.
- [ ] Shopping receipt and change returned and counted back.
- [ ] Insurance confirmed for volunteer driving.
- [ ] You know the scheme's "no" list and passed any concern on the same day.
