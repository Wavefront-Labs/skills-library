# Befriending visits and telephone companionship

**Goal:** be a reliable, warm presence for someone who is lonely — regular, genuine company
that they look forward to — while staying within what your scheme asks of you. This is
general volunteer reference; your scheme's handbook and coordinator come first.

## 1. Before the first visit
- Read the referral information your coordinator shares (interests, health, communication
  needs, what they hope for). Ask what to avoid.
- Agree the plan: day, time, length (often about an hour), where (home, café, by phone).
- Many schemes do the first meeting with the coordinator present. Bring nothing but yourself
  — or something small to talk about (a photo of your dog, a local paper).

## 2. Building rapport
- **Be on time, every time.** Reliability *is* the relationship. If you must cancel, tell
  them (and the scheme) as early as possible.
- **Listen more than you talk.** Ask open questions: "What was this street like when you
  moved here?" "Tell me about that photo."
- **Reminiscence** works well for many older people; let them lead, and be gentle if
  memories are painful.
- Find shared activities: cards, a walk, gardening talk, football results, music, puzzles.
- It's fine to share a bit about yourself — that's friendship — but keep the focus on them
  and keep your address and personal finances private.
- For people with dementia: short sentences, one question at a time, don't correct or
  quiz, go with their reality where kind and safe.

## 3. Telephone befriending
- Same time each week; let them know if your number shows as withheld.
- Allow silences; you don't have to fill them. Smile — it's audible.
- Hearing difficulties: speak clearly and a bit slower, not louder; ask if another time of
  day suits better.
- Note anything to follow up next call ("How did the hospital appointment go?").

## 4. Boundaries (what most schemes ask)
- No lending or borrowing money, no handling their bank cards, no gifts beyond token ones.
- No personal care, medication, or decisions on their behalf.
- Don't swap personal phone numbers or visit outside arranged times unless your scheme allows.
- You're a friend-in-a-role: if they become dependent ("you're the only one I've got"),
  tell your coordinator — they can widen the person's support.

## 5. Noticing and raising concerns
Over months, you'll see changes others miss: weight loss, confusion, an unheated house,
unopened post, a new "friend" handling their money, bruises, fear of someone. **Tell your
coordinator the same day.** If someone is in immediate danger or seriously unwell, call
emergency services first. Don't investigate yourself and don't promise to keep secrets.

## 6. Sustaining and ending
- Regular check-ins with your coordinator; use volunteer support sessions.
- Endings happen (you move, they go into care, they die). Plan with the coordinator,
  give notice, say a proper goodbye. Grief is normal for volunteers too — ask for support.

## Verify
- [ ] Visit/call at the agreed time, every time; cancellations communicated early.
- [ ] You know your scheme's boundaries on money, gifts and contact.
- [ ] You know exactly who to tell about a concern, and you'd do it the same day.
- [ ] You've had a check-in with your coordinator in the agreed period.
- [ ] Any ending is planned, not a disappearance.
