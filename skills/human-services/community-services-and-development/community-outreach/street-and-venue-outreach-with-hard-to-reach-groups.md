# Street and venue outreach with hard-to-reach groups

**Goal:** reach people whom services miss — rough sleepers, isolated migrants, people
who use drugs, young people out of school — and connect them to help *they* want, at a
pace they can trust. General professional reference; follow your organisation's lone-working,
safeguarding and data rules.

"Hard to reach" is often "easy to ignore": the service is hard to reach for them. Start
from that assumption.

## 1. Map before you go
- Where are people, when? Talk to local businesses, transport staff, faith venues, existing
  services, and above all people with lived experience. Peer outreach workers open doors.
- Pick consistent **routes and times** — predictability builds trust.
- Know what you can offer *on the spot* (water, food, hygiene kits, harm-reduction supplies,
  phone charging, information) and what you can link to.

## 2. Safety
- Work in pairs where possible; follow the lone-working policy (check-in times, tracking
  app, code word for "send help").
- Carry ID, phone, first-aid kit, naloxone if trained and your service permits.
- Dynamic risk check on approach: exits, intoxication, groups, dogs, weather.
- You don't have to engage every time. Walk on if it feels wrong; note it for the team.

## 3. Making contact
- Approach from the front, at a distance, crouch or sit to their level if appropriate.
- Introduce yourself, your organisation, why you're there. Ask permission: "Is it OK if
  I stop for a minute?"
- **Respect a no.** Coming back next week without pressure is the work.
- Offer something useful and no-strings first. Remember names and details next time.
- Listen more than you inform; ask what would help them today.

## 4. From contact to connection
- Build towards one small goal they choose (ID replacement, a GP, a shower, benefits).
- **Warm handover:** go with them, or introduce by phone with them present; follow up to
  check they got there. A leaflet alone usually fails.
- Use motivational interviewing approaches for ambivalence.
- Be honest about what you can't do and what waiting times really are.

## 5. Recording
Keep a brief contact log (date, location, outcome, agreed next step) in your agency's
system, with consent for identifiable records. Many people won't give names at first —
use an agreed descriptor. Aggregate data (contacts, referrals, outcomes) shows need to funders.

## Gotchas
- One-off "blitz" outreach that is never repeated burns trust.
- Promising what services won't deliver.
- Leaving people with concerns about immediate health risk (hypothermia, overdose,
  untreated wounds) without escalating to emergency or health services.
- Safeguarding concerns (children, exploitation, adults at risk) still go through your
  safeguarding lead — outreach is not exempt.
- Exhaustion: outreach exposure is heavy; use debriefs and supervision.

## Verify
- [ ] Routes and times set from local mapping including lived-experience input.
- [ ] Lone-working and check-in procedure followed every shift.
- [ ] Contacts recorded with consent; next step noted.
- [ ] Referrals made as warm handovers and followed up.
- [ ] Health and safeguarding concerns escalated the same shift.
