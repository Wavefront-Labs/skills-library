# Facilitating inclusive community meetings and workshops

**Goal:** a meeting where the people affected actually shape the outcome, the quiet get
heard, decisions are clear, and tension is used rather than avoided. General reference;
statutory consultations or formal governance meetings may have their own rules.

## 1. Design before the day
- **Purpose and outcome:** what will exist at the end (a ranked list, a decision, three
  volunteers)? If there's no outcome, it's a briefing, not a workshop.
- **Who needs to be there** and what stops them: time of day, childcare, food, venue
  access (step-free, hearing loop, toilets), language/interpreters, transport costs.
- **Decision rule, decided in advance:** who decides, and how — consensus, majority vote,
  advisory input to a committee. Tell participants at the start.
- **Agenda as flow:** open (welcome, purpose) → diverge (ideas) → groan zone (wrestle
  with differences) → converge (prioritise, decide) → close (next steps).

## 2. Opening the room
- Welcome people at the door. Name tags.
- Purpose, outcome, decision rule, timings, how input will be used.
- Short **agreements**: one voice at a time, respect, phones, right to pass, confidentiality
  if personal stories are shared.
- An opening round with an easy question so everyone has spoken once.

## 3. Methods that widen participation
- **Rounds:** everyone speaks in turn, briefly.
- **Think-pair-share:** silent thinking, then pairs, then the group. Great for the quiet.
- **Small groups** with a scribe, reporting back. Mix or self-select deliberately.
- **World Café:** rotating tables on a question. **Open Space:** participants set the agenda.
- **Visual prioritising:** dot-voting; then discuss the top items, not the vote tally only.
- **Gradients of agreement / fist-to-five** for testing consensus quickly.

## 4. Dominant voices and conflict
- **Dominant voice:** thank them, summarise, then invite others ("Let's hear from someone
  who hasn't spoken"). Use rounds to structure equal time.
- **Off-topic:** a visible **parking lot** for issues to follow up — and actually follow up.
- **Tension:** name it neutrally ("There are two strong views here"), reflect each side's
  underlying interest, look for what both can live with. Separate people from issues.
- **Hostility or abuse:** restate the agreements; take a break; speak privately; ask the
  person to leave if needed. If you're running a meeting on a contested issue, plan
  safety (a second facilitator, a host, venue staff) in advance.
- As facilitator, stay neutral on content. If you have a stake, get an independent facilitator.

## 5. Closing
Summarise decisions and actions (who, what, by when), read them back, explain how and
when people will hear what happened. Quick evaluation (one word, or a show of hands).
Circulate notes within a week in accessible formats.

## Gotchas
- Hidden decision rules — people feel tricked when the committee later overrules them.
- Too much content, no time for convergence.
- Report-backs that repeat everything; ask for top two points only.
- Inaccessible written materials (small print, jargon, English only).

## Verify
- [ ] Purpose, outcome and decision rule stated at the start.
- [ ] Access barriers addressed (time, venue, language, childcare).
- [ ] Everyone spoke at least once (opening round).
- [ ] Decisions and actions read back with owners and dates.
- [ ] Notes and parking-lot follow-ups sent within a week.
