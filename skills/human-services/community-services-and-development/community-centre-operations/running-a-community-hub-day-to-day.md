# Running a community hub day to day

**Goal:** a building that feels open to everyone, runs smoothly, and stays safe when
something goes wrong. General reference; fire, health-and-safety, licensing and insurance
requirements are set locally — your landlord, insurer and regulator decide the minimums.

## 1. Space and timetable
- One **master booking calendar** (shared, single owner) — rooms, times, set-up needs,
  hirer contact, key-holder.
- **Hire agreement** for external users: purpose, times including set-up/clear-up, capacity,
  insurance (public liability), safeguarding policy if working with children or adults at
  risk, damage and cancellation terms, emergency procedures.
- Balance the **activity mix**: free/low-cost community use vs income-generating hire;
  daytime vs evening; groups for different ages and cultures. Review termly against who's
  using the hub and who isn't.
- Leave buffer time between bookings for cleaning and changeover.

## 2. Front desk and welcome
- Greet everyone within seconds, by name if known. A warm welcome is the service.
- Know the building's offer and the local map of services; keep an up-to-date leaflet
  rack and directory.
- Signing-in for visitors per your policy (fire roll call, safeguarding), without making
  drop-in users feel processed.
- Accessibility: step-free access, accessible toilet, hearing loop, seating, clear signage,
  quiet space if possible.
- **House rules** displayed in plain language: respect, no abuse, no weapons/drugs,
  children supervised.

## 3. Opening and closing checks
**Open:** alarm off, walk-round (hazards, overnight damage), lights/heating, fire exits
clear and unlocked, first-aid kit stocked, accident book present, today's bookings known.
**Close:** all rooms cleared and checked (toilets too), windows and doors secured, appliances
off, fire exits checked, alarm set, key-holder log signed. Lone closing → follow lone-working check-in.

## 4. Handling incidents
- **Medical:** first aider responds; call emergency services when in doubt; record.
- **Fire/evacuation:** alarm, evacuate to assembly point, roll call from sign-in, never re-enter.
  Run drills at least as often as your local rules require.
- **Aggression:** keep yourself safe; stay calm, speak low and slow, give space, offer
  choices, don't argue rules mid-crisis; get a colleague; ask the person to leave; call police
  if there's a threat or violence. Move others away.
- **Safeguarding concern** (a child alone, a disclosure, someone at risk): designated
  safeguarding lead, same day.
- **Afterwards:** incident report within 24 hours (facts, people, actions, outcome),
  debrief staff and volunteers, review whether a ban, risk plan or repair is needed. Bans
  should be time-limited with a clear route back where safe.

## 5. Keeping it running
Maintenance log and contractor list; regular checks (fire alarm tests, emergency lighting,
electrical testing, water hygiene) on a calendar; cleaning rota; petty cash with receipts and
two-person counts; key register. Annual review of policies with staff and users.

## Gotchas
- Double-bookings from multiple calendars.
- Hirers running children's activities with no safeguarding policy or checks.
- Fire exits blocked by stored chairs.
- A "regulars' club" that makes newcomers feel unwelcome — watch the culture.
- Serious or repeated incidents: discuss with your manager or committee; don't carry them alone.

## Verify
- [ ] Single booking calendar and signed hire agreements with insurance on file.
- [ ] Opening and closing checklists completed and signed daily.
- [ ] First-aid cover and fire drills meet local requirements.
- [ ] Every incident has a written report within 24 hours and a debrief.
- [ ] Activity mix reviewed against who uses the hub at least termly.
