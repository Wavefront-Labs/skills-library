# Logic models, delivery and outcome evaluation

**Goal:** a programme that does something people need, can show whether it worked,
and gets better each cycle. General professional reference; funders and commissioners
often prescribe their own templates and indicators — use theirs where required.

## 1. Start from evidence of need
Use your needs assessment (see `human-services.community-services-and-development.community-needs-assessment.needs-assessment-asset-mapping-and-community-listening`)
and the evidence base for what works. Check whether someone nearby already runs this —
partnering may beat duplicating.

## 2. Build a logic model
Read right-to-left first (what change do we want?), then left-to-right:
- **Inputs:** staff, volunteers, money, venues, partners.
- **Activities:** what you do (weekly cooking club, 1:1 mentoring).
- **Outputs:** counts of activity (sessions run, people attending).
- **Short-term outcomes:** knowledge, skills, confidence.
- **Medium/long-term outcomes:** behaviour, circumstances, wellbeing.
- **Assumptions and external factors:** what has to be true for this to work.
A **theory of change** adds the causal story ("if..., then..., because...") — write it in
one paragraph and test it with participants.

## 3. Objectives
SMART: specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, time-bound. "By month 6, 60% of
participants report increased confidence cooking on a budget (pre/post scale)." Choose
targets you can defend, not ones that win the bid.

## 4. Delivery
- **Recruitment:** through trusted referrers and community connectors, not just flyers.
  Remove barriers: timing, childcare, transport, language, food, cost.
- **Engagement and retention:** welcome, consistency (same faces), reminders, follow up
  people who drop out and ask why.
- **Delivery plan:** session plans, staffing rota, safeguarding arrangements, accessibility,
  incident process.
- **Risk register:** top risks (staff leaves, venue lost, low uptake), likelihood, impact, mitigation.

## 5. Evaluation
RBA's three questions: **How much did we do? How well did we do it? Is anyone better off?**
- Outputs answer the first; quality measures (satisfaction, completion, waiting times) the
  second; outcome measures the third.
- Collect baseline data at entry; same measure at exit and follow-up.
- Mix numbers with stories (case studies, quotes with consent).
- Keep the burden light: a few good measures beat many unused ones.
- Attribution is hard; say "contributed to" unless you have a comparison group.

## 6. Reporting to funders
Report against agreed indicators, explain variance honestly (under-recruitment and why),
share learning, and show what you changed. Funders generally trust candour more than
uniformly green dashboards — though this varies; talk to your funder early about problems.

## Gotchas
- Counting outputs and calling them outcomes.
- Measures chosen after delivery to make results look good.
- Designing for "the community" without community members in the design.
- No exit strategy when funding ends — plan sustainability from the start.
- Programmes involving children or adults at risk need safeguarding policy, vetting and
  a named safeguarding lead before launch.

## Verify
- [ ] Need evidenced; duplication checked.
- [ ] Logic model and one-paragraph theory of change written and tested with users.
- [ ] SMART objectives with baseline measures planned.
- [ ] Barriers to participation addressed in the delivery plan.
- [ ] Evaluation answers all three RBA questions; reports explain variance honestly.
