# Needs assessment, asset mapping and community listening

**Goal:** know what a community needs *and* what it already has before you design anything,
with the community shaping the picture rather than just being surveyed. General
professional reference; ethics approval may be needed if findings will be published as research.

## 1. Define the community and the question
- Geography (a neighbourhood, a postcode area) or identity/interest (a diaspora group,
  carers, young people)? Write the boundary down.
- What decision will this inform? A needs assessment with no decision attached becomes a
  report on a shelf.

## 2. Start with what exists (secondary data)
Census and population statistics, deprivation indices, health and education data, crime
data, service-use figures, previous local reports. Note the gaps: small groups (recent
migrants, Traveller communities, LGBTQ+ people) are often invisible in official data.

## 3. Asset mapping (ABCD)
Map what's strong, not just what's wrong:
- **Individuals:** skills, passions, knowledge (a "capacity inventory").
- **Associations:** clubs, faith groups, informal networks, sports teams, WhatsApp groups.
- **Institutions:** schools, libraries, clinics, businesses.
- **Physical assets:** halls, parks, empty shops.
- **Economic and cultural assets.**
Walk the area with residents; mark assets on a shared map. Assets are what solutions
get built from.

## 4. Consultation and listening
Mix methods to reach different people:
- **Surveys** (paper and online, translated) for breadth.
- **Focus groups** for depth, with groups who'd talk more freely together.
- **One-to-one conversations / listening campaigns:** trained residents ask neighbours
  open questions ("What do you love here? What worries you? What would you change?").
- **Go to where people are:** school gates, markets, places of worship, laundrettes.
- **Creative methods** for those excluded by forms: photo-voice, mapping, drawing with children.
Pay or compensate participants' time where you can; reimburse childcare and travel.

## 5. Analyse honestly
Triangulate: what do data, assets and voices agree on? Where do they conflict? Note
who you didn't hear from. Prioritise with the community (dot-voting, a community event),
not only by professional judgement.

## 6. Close the loop
Report back in accessible formats (one-page summary, event, translated versions): what
you heard, what will happen, what won't and why. This is the step most often skipped —
and the main cause of **consultation fatigue**.

## Gotchas
- Consulting on decisions already made (tokenism, low rungs on Arnstein's ladder).
- Hearing only the usual voices — the confident, the English-speaking, the organised.
- A deficit-only report that stigmatises the area.
- Gatekeepers speaking "for" a community; triangulate.
- Collecting personal data without a clear purpose, consent and secure storage.
- Complex or contested findings: discuss with your manager and partners before publishing.

## Verify
- [ ] Community boundary and the decision to be informed written down.
- [ ] Secondary data gathered, with its gaps named.
- [ ] Asset map exists alongside the needs list.
- [ ] At least three engagement methods; under-heard groups actively reached.
- [ ] Findings fed back to participants in accessible formats.
