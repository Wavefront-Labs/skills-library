# Building referral pathways and interagency networks

**Goal:** a person who walks into any service gets to the right one — quickly, without
retelling their story five times — because the services know each other, trust each
other and have agreed how to pass people on. General reference; information-sharing must
follow data-protection law and each agency's policies.

## 1. Map the system
List services relevant to your population: who they serve, eligibility, how to refer, wait
times, opening hours, languages. Ask service users where they got stuck — the map from
their side is usually different. Keep a living directory with a named owner and review date.

## 2. Build a referral pathway
For each frequent route (e.g. family support → housing advice):
1. **Criteria:** who is eligible, who isn't, what to do when someone falls between.
2. **Method:** form, phone, secure email, shared platform. One method, documented.
3. **Minimum information** and the consent needed to share it.
4. **Response standard:** acknowledgement within X days; what the referrer will hear back.
5. **Feedback / closed loop:** receiving service tells the referrer the outcome
   (accepted, waitlisted, declined with reason).
6. **Escalation:** who to call when a referral stalls.
Write it on one page. Pilot it, then review after three months with both sides.

## 3. Warm referral
Where possible: introduce the person by phone with them present, share agreed information so
they don't repeat it, book the first appointment together, check they got there.
"No wrong door" means any service can start the handover.

## 4. Running an interagency network
- Clear purpose (shared learning? case coordination? joint planning?) — say which.
- Regular rhythm, rotating host, short agenda: service updates, gaps and blockages, one
  learning topic, actions.
- Bring frontline staff, not only managers — they know where pathways break.
- Track recurring blockages and take them to people who can fix them (commissioners, senior managers).
- Case-specific discussion only under an information-sharing agreement and with consent or
  a lawful basis; otherwise discuss anonymised scenarios.

## 5. Joint programmes and place-based work
- **Partnership agreement / MOU:** purpose, roles, contributions, governance, decision-
  making, data sharing, how disputes are handled, how the partnership ends.
- **Collective impact** conditions as a check: common agenda, shared measurement, mutually
  reinforcing activities, continuous communication, and a backbone organisation to hold it.
- Share credit publicly; agree who speaks to funders and media.

## Gotchas
- Pathways that live in one person's head and vanish when they leave.
- Referral black holes: no feedback, so referrers stop referring.
- Information sharing done informally without consent or agreement — a breach risk.
- Power imbalances: large statutory agencies dominating small community groups; build in equal voice.
- Safeguarding concerns don't wait for the network meeting — follow your own agency's
  procedure immediately; multi-agency safeguarding processes run alongside.

## Verify
- [ ] Service map exists, owned, dated, with users' input.
- [ ] Each key pathway written on one page with criteria, method, consent and response time.
- [ ] Receiving services feed back outcomes to referrers.
- [ ] Network has a stated purpose and an action log; blockages escalated.
- [ ] Joint work governed by a written agreement including data sharing.
