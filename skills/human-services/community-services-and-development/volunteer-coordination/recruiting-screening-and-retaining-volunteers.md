# Recruiting, screening and retaining volunteers

**Goal:** the right volunteers in the right roles, safely, and staying because the
experience is worth their time. General reference; vetting schemes (DBS in England and
Wales, Working With Children Checks in Australian states, police checks elsewhere) and
volunteer employment-law boundaries vary by country.

## 1. Design the role first
Write a **role description**: purpose, tasks, time commitment, location, skills needed,
support provided, who they report to, and any checks required. Say what the role is
*not* (e.g. "befrienders don't handle money or give personal care"). A clear role is the
single best retention and safeguarding tool.

## 2. Recruit
Where people already are: volunteer centres and platforms, local groups, universities,
employer volunteering schemes, your own service users (lived experience volunteers are
valuable). Be specific in adverts ("2 hours a week, Tuesday mornings, chatting with
older people") — vague asks get vague responses. Recruit for diversity of age, background
and language to match your community.

## 3. Screen (proportionate to risk)
- Application or expression of interest, then an **informal interview** (two-way: fit and
  motivation, and their questions).
- **References** — at least one, ideally two, for roles with any responsibility.
- **Background checks** where the role legally requires or warrants them (contact with
  children or adults at risk); know the level of check each role needs.
- Safer recruitment habits: explore gaps, ask why they're interested, state your
  safeguarding commitment in adverts.
- Decline kindly but clearly where the fit is wrong; offer an alternative role if one exists.

## 4. Match and induct
- Match on availability, skills, interests, language and personality (for 1:1 roles).
- **Induction:** organisation purpose, role boundaries, safeguarding and how to report a
  concern, confidentiality, health and safety, lone-working, expenses, who to call.
- Shadowing before solo work. A short **volunteer agreement** (not an employment
  contract — avoid contract-like obligations that could create employment status where
  that's a legal risk in your jurisdiction).

## 5. Support and supervise
Regular check-ins proportionate to the role (monthly call, group debriefs). Make it easy to
raise concerns. Pay **out-of-pocket expenses** promptly; unpaid expenses quietly exclude
low-income volunteers. Group sessions for role-specific learning.

## 6. Retain and recognise
- People stay for meaning, connection and feeling useful. Tell them the impact.
- Thank individually and specifically; recognise during national volunteers' weeks and anniversaries.
- Offer growth: training, new responsibilities, references for jobs.
- **Exit conversations**: ask leavers why and what would have helped.

## 7. When things go wrong
Address problems early, privately, against the role description. Have a written problem-
solving procedure. Safeguarding concerns about a volunteer go straight to your safeguarding
lead, and may involve suspension pending inquiry and referral to the relevant vetting body.

## Gotchas
- No role description, so boundaries drift (lending money, personal care, home visits off-scheme).
- Checks done once and never renewed per policy.
- Volunteers used to replace paid roles — damages trust and may breach agreements.
- Over-recruiting with no work to give them.

## Verify
- [ ] Every role has a written description including limits and checks needed.
- [ ] References and required checks completed before unsupervised contact.
- [ ] Induction covers safeguarding reporting and boundaries; shadowing done.
- [ ] Expenses reimbursed promptly; check-ins happen at the agreed frequency.
- [ ] Leavers asked why; retention reviewed at least yearly.
