# Drop-in and detached youth work with safe boundaries

**Goal:** build trusting relationships with young people where *they* choose to engage, use
those relationships for informal learning and support, and keep young people safe.
General reference; age ranges, vetting checks and reporting duties vary by country.

## 1. The core of the practice
Youth work is **voluntary participation** — young people choose to be there. That makes
the relationship the method. Start where they are: their interests, their language,
their spaces. Informal education happens through conversation, activities and shared
experience, not lectures.

## 2. Drop-in sessions
- **Plan anyway:** staffing ratio, activity mix (open access + one structured offer),
  issue focus if any, who's on door/welcome.
- **Welcome every young person by name** where you can. Notice who's new, who's missing.
- Clear, short **house rules** agreed with young people (respect, no weapons, no drugs,
  how conflict is handled), displayed and applied consistently.
- **Session recording:** numbers, notable conversations, concerns, follow-ups — in the
  agency system, the same day.

## 3. Detached (street-based) work
- Map where young people gather and when; consistent routes and times.
- **Always in pairs**, with lone-working check-ins, ID, phone, first aid.
- Approach openly, introduce yourselves and your project; respect "no". Return regularly.
- You're a guest in their space; avoid acting like enforcement.
- Use **contextual safeguarding** thinking: harm can come from places and peer groups
  (parks, shops, online), not only families. Feed observations about unsafe locations to
  your safeguarding lead and partners.

## 4. Youth voice and participation
Hart's ladder warns against manipulation, decoration and tokenism. Higher rungs: young
people consulted and informed, then sharing decisions, then initiating. Practical routes:
young people on interview panels, planning their programme, running a project budget,
youth forums that actually change something.

## 5. Professional boundaries
- Friendly, not a friend. No personal social media contact; use only agency-approved
  channels and devices, with messages visible to the organisation.
- No lifts alone in your car, no one-to-one in closed rooms without your organisation's
  safeguards (visible windows, colleague aware), no gifts or money outside policy.
- Self-disclosure only when it helps the young person and is brief.
- Never promise confidentiality you can't keep.

## 6. Disclosures
Listen calmly; don't interrogate or lead; reassure them they did the right thing; explain
what happens next; **record their words**, date and time; tell your designated
safeguarding lead the same day; where there is immediate danger, call emergency services.
Watch for signs of sexual or criminal exploitation (new phones, older "friends", unexplained
money, going missing).

## Gotchas
- Inconsistent rule enforcement between workers — young people notice instantly.
- Becoming the one adult they trust and then leaving abruptly; plan endings.
- Banning a young person without a route back.
- Treating risk-taking behaviour only as a discipline issue; ask what's behind it.
- Any concern about a colleague's conduct goes to your manager or designated person —
  never keep it to yourself.

## Verify
- [ ] Session plan, staffing and lone-working arrangements in place.
- [ ] House rules co-created and applied consistently.
- [ ] Contact with young people only via approved channels.
- [ ] Disclosures recorded verbatim and passed to the safeguarding lead the same day.
- [ ] Evidence that young people shaped at least one decision this term.
