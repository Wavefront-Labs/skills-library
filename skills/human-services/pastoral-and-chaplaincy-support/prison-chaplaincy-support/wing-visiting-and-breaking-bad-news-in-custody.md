# Wing visiting and breaking bad news in custody

**Goal:** offer prisoners consistent, non-judgemental pastoral presence in an environment
built around control and suspicion — including at their worst moments, like hearing of a
death outside — while staying within security rules. Religious services and rites are a
separate part of the chaplain's role. *Rules, roles and duties vary by prison system;
the establishment's security and safeguarding instructions always come first.*

## 1. The setting
- **Security first:** complete key and security training; follow rules on what you carry,
  keys, cell entry (often only with officers present or after a risk check), and what you
  can pass in or out (usually nothing without authorisation).
- **Statutory visits** in many systems: chaplaincy sees new arrivals (reception/induction),
  people in segregation, healthcare and those on suicide/self-harm monitoring — check your
  establishment's list.
- **Faith rights:** prisoners usually have rights to practise their religion; chaplaincy
  often facilitates for all faiths and none.

## 2. Wing and cell visiting
- Be predictable: same days, same times. Consistency builds trust in a place where little is reliable.
- Introduce yourself through the door flap or at the cell door; ask if they'd like a chat.
- **Non-judgemental listening:** you're not there to assess guilt or the offence. People
  may want to talk about it or never mention it — follow their lead.
- Common themes: family contact, children, guilt, shame, anger at the system, fear,
  boredom, loss of identity, faith questions.
- Use the listening core (`human-services.non-clinical-counselling.supportive-listening.active-listening-reflection-and-holding-silence`).

## 3. Confidentiality — and its limits in custody
Explain clearly: you'll keep what they say private *except* risk of harm to themselves or
others, threats to security (escape, drugs, weapons), or safeguarding concerns. Some
systems require reporting of further offences. Saying this up front prevents a later
sense of betrayal.

## 4. Breaking bad news (a death or serious illness outside)
In many prisons chaplains deliver this news. Typical steps (follow local procedure):
1. **Verify** the information with the family or hospital before telling.
2. **Plan:** check the prisoner's current risk status with staff; choose a private room,
   not a cell door; arrange for an officer the prisoner trusts nearby; allow time.
3. **Tell plainly:** warn ("I'm afraid I've got some very sad news"), then say it clearly —
   "Your mother died this morning" — not euphemisms.
4. **Stay:** allow any reaction (silence, rage, collapse). Don't leave quickly.
5. **Practicalities:** phone call to family, compassionate visits or escorted funeral
   attendance (an application the chaplain often supports; outcomes depend on risk
   assessment), religious needs, a memorial in chapel.
6. **Risk:** bereavement is a known trigger for self-harm and suicide in custody — ensure
   staff are informed and, if indicated, suicide/self-harm monitoring is opened. Follow up
   the same day and next morning.

## 5. Self-harm and suicide
Know the signs and the procedure (e.g. opening an ACCT document in England and Wales, or
local equivalents). If someone tells you they're thinking of ending their life, you must
act — tell them you will. See
`human-services.non-clinical-counselling.crisis-line-work.handling-a-crisis-call-from-rapport-to-safe-ending`
for asking directly.

## 6. Sentence milestones and release
Support around sentencing, parole hearings, transfers and anniversaries. Release often
brings anxiety: housing, money, family, temptation, stigma. Connect to resettlement
services and community faith groups or mentoring schemes that welcome ex-prisoners.

## Gotchas
- **Conditioning/manipulation:** requests to pass messages, post letters, bring items or
  "just make a phone call" are serious security breaches. Say no warmly, every time, and
  report attempts per policy.
- Over-identifying with prisoners against staff, or with staff against prisoners.
- Making promises about parole, transfers or funerals you can't control.
- Vicarious trauma from hearing about violence; use supervision.

## Verify
- [ ] Security training complete; local rules on entry and items followed.
- [ ] Statutory visits done and recorded.
- [ ] Confidentiality limits explained before disclosures.
- [ ] Bad news verified, delivered privately and plainly, with risk flagged and follow-up.
- [ ] All conditioning attempts refused and reported.
