# Pastoral presence at scenes, notifications and vigils

**Goal:** offer calm, compassionate presence to people at the worst moments of their
lives — at disaster scenes and family centres, alongside police delivering a death
notification, and at community vigils — without getting in the way of the response or
causing further harm. Scene prayers and memorial rites are covered in
`religion-philosophy.chaplaincy-and-pastoral-ministry.crisis-and-disaster-ministry.scene-prayer-and-post-tragedy-memorials`;
this is the emotional-support side. *Deployment rules, credentialing and notification
procedures vary by agency and country; work only within your agency's.*

## 1. Deploy properly — never self-deploy
- Be **credentialed** with a recognised agency (police/fire chaplaincy, a disaster spiritual-care team, Red Cross/Red Crescent-type organisation) and trained in incident command basics.
- Report to the assigned point, sign in, take your briefing: role, location, risks,
  who you report to, shift length, how to escalate concerns.
- Wear identification. Bring water, food, weather gear; be self-sufficient.
- Untrained clergy arriving uninvited add to scene congestion and can retraumatise survivors.

## 2. Psychological First Aid (PFA) — the core approach
Evidence-informed, non-intrusive support in the early hours and days:
- **Look:** safety, urgent needs, people with serious distress reactions.
- **Listen:** approach, introduce yourself, ask about needs and concerns, help them calm.
- **Link:** help with basic needs and information, connect to loved ones and services.
Do **not** push people to recount events (single-session psychological debriefing of
survivors is not recommended). Offer practical comfort: a blanket, a phone charge, a
seat away from the cameras, accurate information about what's happening next.

## 3. Accompanying a death notification
Usually police lead; the chaplain supports. Typical principles (follow your force's procedure):
1. **In person, in pairs, promptly** — before the media or social media reaches the family.
2. **Confirm identity** of the deceased and next of kin beforehand; get facts straight.
3. At the door: introduce, ask to come in, ask them to sit.
4. The officer says it **plainly**: "I'm very sorry, your son Daniel was killed in a
   car crash this afternoon." Use the name and the words "died" or "killed".
5. Then stay: allow reactions; answer questions honestly (or say you'll find out);
   don't give graphic details unless asked.
6. **Don't leave them alone** — help call a relative, friend or faith leader; explain
   next steps (viewing/identification, coroner/medical examiner process, liaison officer).
7. Leave written contacts. Follow up as agreed.
Chaplains help most by being present, practical, and quiet. Offer prayer only if wanted.

## 4. Family assistance / reception centres
Families waiting for news of missing people: keep them informed honestly and regularly;
separate them from media; attend to children and older people; be with families when
officials deliver news. Respect religious and cultural needs (prayer space, dietary,
rituals around the dead, who may touch the body).

## 5. Community vigils and memorials
- Work with community leaders and families: who speaks, which faiths, inclusion of
  people of no faith, the families' wishes over the institution's.
- Keep it short, inclusive, accessible; plan for strong emotion and media presence.
- Watch for people in acute distress at the edges; have support people circulating.

## 6. Supporting responders
- Be present at staging areas, rest points, and after shifts. Small talk, coffee, a word
  at shift end.
- Normalise reactions; encourage rest, food, sleep and peer support; watch for those who
  don't speak at all or are drinking heavily.
- Refer per agency protocol (peer-support teams, EAP, trauma services).

## Gotchas
- Giving theological explanations for the disaster.
- Promising outcomes ("they'll find her") or speculating on causes.
- Talking to media about victims or families.
- Your own exposure: rotate, debrief, use supervision; cumulative trauma builds over deployments.

## Verify
- [ ] Credentialed, deployed via incident command, signed in and briefed.
- [ ] PFA used: look, listen, link — no forced recounting.
- [ ] Death notification in person, plain words, family not left alone, follow-up arranged.
- [ ] Vigils planned with families and community leaders, inclusive of all faiths and none.
- [ ] Responders offered support; own debrief and rest taken.
