# Arriving, being present and leaving a pastoral visit

**Goal:** make a pastoral visit — in hospital, hospice, care home, prison or at home —
that leaves the person feeling accompanied and respected, whatever their faith or none.
This is the emotional-support side of the visit. Prayer, sacraments and rites belong to
your tradition's ministry (see `religion-philosophy.chaplaincy-and-pastoral-ministry`
skills) and are offered, never assumed. Pastoral visiting is not counselling or therapy;
know when to refer (see
`human-services.non-clinical-counselling.boundaries-and-referral.recognising-clinical-red-flags-and-warm-referral`).

## 1. Before you go in
- **Check in with staff** (nurse in charge, officer on the wing, care-home staff): is
  this a good time? Any infection control, risks, communication needs, recent bad news?
- **Read what you're allowed to read** — referral note, faith recorded — but don't
  assume the recorded faith is current or important to them.
- **Settle yourself:** a breath, set aside your own agenda. Many chaplains use a brief
  personal prayer or pause before each door.
- Wash hands / follow infection-control rules; ID badge visible.

## 2. Arriving
- Knock, wait, make eye contact, introduce yourself and role simply: "Hello, I'm Sam,
  one of the chaplaincy team. We visit everyone on the ward who'd like a chat — would you
  like some company for a few minutes?"
- **Ask permission** to sit and to stay. Accept "no" gracefully; offer to come back.
- **Position:** sit if invited, at eye level, at a respectful distance, where they can see
  you without turning (especially if they're in pain or bed-bound).
- Mind dignity: draw the curtain if they want, wait if care is under way, don't sit on beds.

## 3. Being present
- Open gently: "How are you doing today?" or "How has it been in here?" — then follow
  their lead. Many people start with practical things (food, noise, family); that's the
  door, not small talk to get past.
- Use the listening core (`human-services.non-clinical-counselling.supportive-listening.active-listening-reflection-and-holding-silence`):
  reflect feelings, allow silence, don't rush to reassure.
- **Faith, any or none:** listen for what sustains them — family, nature, music, faith,
  memories. Ask open questions: "What's been helping you get through?" If they mention
  faith, explore it in their terms. Offer prayer or a reading only if it fits and they
  want it: "Would it be helpful if I said a prayer, or would you rather not?"
- **Presence without words** is often the gift: holding silence by a dying person, sitting
  with someone too tired to talk. Touch (a hand on a hand) only with clear consent and cultural fit.

## 4. Sensing when to go
Signs: tiredness, glancing at the door or phone, staff arriving, repeated short answers,
emotion that needs privacy. Most visits are 10–20 minutes; very ill people may manage 5.
Better to leave them wanting more than exhausted.

## 5. Leaving well
- Signal: "I'll let you rest soon."
- Briefly reflect something they shared; thank them.
- Say whether and when you'll return — and only promise what you'll do.
- Offer a blessing or prayer if it fits and is welcome.
- Tell staff anything they need to know (with consent, or per safeguarding if there's risk).

## 6. After
Brief notes per policy: date, that a visit happened, any requests (e.g. "would like a
priest/imam", "wants to see the chaplain again Friday"). Not intimate content. Take a
moment for yourself before the next door — visiting is draining.

## Gotchas
- Evangelising or offering your beliefs to someone in a vulnerable state — an abuse of the role.
- Assuming the person wants religion because you're a chaplain, or doesn't because they ticked "none".
- Filling silence with chatter about yourself.
- Visiting while ill — you can infect vulnerable people.

## Verify
- [ ] Checked with staff before entering; infection control followed.
- [ ] Introduced yourself and asked permission to visit and to sit.
- [ ] Followed their lead; prayer offered only if welcome.
- [ ] Left within their energy; any return promised is diarised.
- [ ] Notes and handovers made per policy; requests passed on.
