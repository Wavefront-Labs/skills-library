# Embedded availability and confidential support in units and workplaces

**Goal:** be a trusted, independent, confidential person *inside* an organisation —
a factory, retail centre, police or fire service, sports club or military unit — whom
people can talk to about anything, from family worries to the things they saw at work.
Religious services and ritual are separate parts of the role. *Military chaplains work
under their nation's doctrine and law; confidentiality privileges, non-combatant status
and duties differ by country and service — know yours.*

## 1. Earning trust by being around
- **Walk the floor** regularly: canteen, break rooms, shift changes, training exercises,
  the gym, the night shift. Predictable presence, not appointments, is how people
  come to talk.
- Learn the work — job titles, pressures, jargon, rhythms. Respect comes from showing up
  in the cold and the noise.
- Most conversations start as small talk; listen for the moment it turns.
- Be visibly **independent** of management/command: you don't report on individuals,
  you're not part of discipline or performance.

## 2. Confidential listening and its limits
- Agree confidentiality boundaries with the organisation *and* explain them to people:
  typically risk to life, serious harm to others, child safeguarding, and (in some
  settings) serious security or legal matters. Some militaries grant chaplains a special
  confidentiality standing — *check the actual rules before relying on it.*
- Meet privately when needed, but somewhere visible enough to be safe for both of you.
- Never pass on information about individuals to managers without consent (outside the limits).
- Use the listening core: `human-services.non-clinical-counselling.supportive-listening.active-listening-reflection-and-holding-silence`.

## 3. Common themes and what helps
- **Family, relationships, money, health worries** — listen; signpost to EAP, welfare
  officers, debt advice, counselling.
- **Shift work, isolation, long hours** — normalise, help them name what's sustaining or missing.
- **Workplace conflict, bullying** — listen, help them consider options (HR, union,
  grievance); don't intervene without consent.
- **Redundancy and job loss** — be visible on announcement days; offer group and
  individual time; help with the shock and sense of identity loss; link to outplacement and benefits advice.

## 4. Military specifics
- **Pre-deployment:** help members and families prepare — wills and practicalities are
  admin's job, but fears and relationships are yours. Make contact details known to families.
- **During deployment:** presence with troops; welfare links with home; support after
  casualties.
- **Post-deployment/reintegration:** watch for difficulty readjusting, relationship
  strain, drinking, anger, withdrawal.
- **Moral injury:** distress from acts that violated deeply held moral beliefs (doing,
  failing to prevent, or witnessing). It shows as guilt, shame, betrayal, loss of faith
  or trust. Listen without minimising or condemning; explore meaning and repair; refer to
  mental-health services when trauma symptoms are present — moral injury and PTSD can coexist.

## 5. After a workplace incident or death
Be present early; support the people closest; help managers communicate humanely;
attend or lead a memorial if wanted. Structured psychological support follows
organisational protocol — see
`human-services.pastoral-and-chaplaincy-support.crisis-and-disaster-chaplaincy.pastoral-presence-at-scenes-notifications-and-vigils`.

## 6. Working with the organisation
- Agree a clear role description: access, reporting line (for the chaplaincy itself,
  not individuals), confidentiality, how you'll report general themes (e.g. "morale
  low on nights", anonymised).
- Serve people of all faiths and none. Proselytising destroys trust and usually breaches the contract.

## Gotchas
- Becoming management's informal informant — or a channel for grievances you then own.
- Only talking to the people who already like chaplains.
- Mental-health crises: refer per protocol and stay alongside.
- Burnout from being "always on"; set boundaries and use supervision.

## Verify
- [ ] Regular, predictable presence across shifts and locations.
- [ ] Confidentiality limits agreed with the organisation and explained to individuals.
- [ ] No information about individuals passed on without consent (outside limits).
- [ ] Signposting list current (EAP, welfare, debt, mental health, families).
- [ ] Moral injury and trauma signs recognised and referred where needed.
