# Spiritual-needs screening and meaning and hope conversations

**Goal:** find out, gently, what gives a person meaning, hope and strength — and what's
troubling them at that level — then support it or connect them to the right person.
"Spiritual" here is broad: religious faith, but also meaning, purpose, connection,
values, legacy and peace, for people of any faith or none. This is pastoral and
educational, not clinical: existential distress can coexist with depression or anxiety,
which need clinical assessment.

## 1. When and how to raise it
- Usually after rapport, not as an opening question (see
  `human-services.pastoral-and-chaplaincy-support.pastoral-visiting.arriving-being-present-and-leaving-a-pastoral-visit`).
- Natural entry points: "What's been helping you through this?" "Where do you find
  strength when things are hard?"
- Avoid jargon. "Spirituality" can feel loaded; "what keeps you going" rarely does.

## 2. Brief screening frameworks
Use these as a mental map, not a form to read out.

**FICA** (Puchalski):
- **F**aith/meaning: "Do you consider yourself spiritual or religious? What gives your
  life meaning?"
- **I**mportance/influence: "How important is that to you, especially now?"
- **C**ommunity: "Are you part of a community that supports you?"
- **A**ddress in care: "Is there anything about this you'd like us to know or help with?"

**HOPE** (Anandarajah & Hight):
- **H**: sources of hope, meaning, comfort, strength, peace, love, connection.
- **O**: organised religion — part of their life? helpful?
- **P**: personal spirituality and practices.
- **E**: effects on care and end-of-life issues.

Record briefly per policy: e.g. "Catholic, faith very important, would like the parish
priest called"; or "No religion; strength from family and garden; worried about being a burden."

## 3. Signs of spiritual distress
- "Why me?" / "What did I do to deserve this?"
- Feeling abandoned or punished by God; anger at God.
- Loss of meaning or purpose ("What's the point?"), feeling a burden.
- Guilt, need for forgiveness, unfinished business, broken relationships.
- Fear of death or of what comes after.
- Conflict between beliefs and treatment choices.
These are invitations to explore, not problems to solve. "What's the point?" can also be
a sign of depression or suicidal thinking — ask directly if unsure, and refer per protocol.

## 4. Meaning and hope conversations
- **Life review:** "Tell me about your life — what are you most proud of? What mattered
  most?" Often brings perspective and peace.
- **Legacy:** "What would you want your grandchildren to know about you?" Some services
  use structured approaches (e.g. dignity therapy) to create a written legacy document — *requires training*.
- **Hope reframed:** hope shifts from cure to other things — a good day, a visit, being
  comfortable, being remembered. "What are you hoping for now?" Don't take hope away or
  inflate it.
- **Forgiveness and reconciliation:** help them think through whether and how to reach out;
  never pressure.

## 5. Sitting with doubt and anger
Don't defend God or argue. Lament is part of most traditions; sometimes saying so helps:
"Many people of faith have felt exactly that." Stay with them in the question.

## 6. Connecting to their tradition
If they want religious support, connect them with a faith leader from *their* tradition
(chaplaincy teams keep contact lists), arrange religious items, dietary needs,
prayer times, rituals around death. Don't impersonate another faith's role.

## Gotchas
- Imposing your own framework of meaning.
- Treating "no religion" as "no spiritual needs".
- Rushing to reassurance ("God has a plan") that closes the conversation.
- Recording intimate details where many staff will read them.

## Verify
- [ ] Spiritual needs explored with plain, open questions after rapport.
- [ ] Sources of meaning and any distress identified and briefly recorded.
- [ ] Doubt and anger met with presence, not argument.
- [ ] Possible depression or suicidal thinking checked and referred.
- [ ] Requested faith or community connections actually arranged.
