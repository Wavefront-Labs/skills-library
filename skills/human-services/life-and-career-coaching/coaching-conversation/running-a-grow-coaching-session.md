# Running a GROW coaching session

**Goal:** help a client make progress on something they care about, in one session,
by asking good questions and listening closely — so the insight and the plan are
*theirs*. Coaching assumes a client who is broadly well and resourceful; it does not
treat mental-health conditions (see
`human-services.life-and-career-coaching.coaching-contracting-and-ethics.engagement-contracting-and-referring-beyond-coaching`).

## 1. Before you start
- Re-read your notes from last time and any actions they committed to.
- Clear your head for two minutes. Coaching presence — full attention, curiosity, not
  rehearsing your next question — matters more than technique.

## 2. GROW, in practice
GROW isn't a strict sequence; you loop back (often from Options to Reality). Timings
are for a ~50-minute session.

### G — Goal for the session (5–10 min)
Agree what *this conversation* is for, not just the life goal.
- "What would you like to get from our time today?"
- "If this session were really useful, what would you leave with?"
- "How will you know we've got there?"
A vague topic ("my career") needs narrowing ("decide whether to apply for the team lead role").

### R — Reality (10–15 min)
Facts, feelings, what's been tried.
- "What's happening now?" "What have you tried so far? What happened?"
- "What's in your control here?" "Who else is involved?"
- "What's the real challenge here for you?" (Stanier's focus question)
Listen for assumptions ("I can't", "they'd never") — reflect them back or question them gently.

### O — Options (10–15 min)
Widen before narrowing.
- "What could you do?" … then "**And what else?**" — ask it two or three times; the
  best option is rarely the first.
- "What would you do if you knew you couldn't fail / had no constraints?"
- "What would someone you admire do?"
Only offer your own idea after theirs run out, labelled as one option among many:
"Can I offer an idea? Feel free to discard it."

### W — Will / Way forward (5–10 min)
- "Which option(s) will you take forward?" "What's the first step, and when?"
- "On a scale of 1–10, how committed are you to that?" Below 7: "What would make it an 8?"
- "What might get in the way? What will you do then?"
- "How will I know you've done it?" — agree how actions get reported back.

## 3. Closing
Ask "What was most useful today?" — it consolidates learning and tells you what to keep
doing. Note agreed actions in their words.

## 4. Question craft
- Short, open, one at a time: "What", "how", "what else". Fewer "why"s (they can sound
  like judgement).
- Leave silence after a good question; the client's thinking happens there.
- Use their words, not your paraphrase, for key phrases.
- Notice energy shifts (pace, tone, sighs) and name them: "Your voice dropped when you
  mentioned your manager."

## Alternatives
CLEAR (Contract, Listen, Explore, Action, Review) and OSKAR (solution-focused: Outcome,
Scaling, Know-how, Affirm, Review) are common variants. Use whichever keeps you
client-led.

## Gotchas
- **Telling dressed as asking** ("Have you thought about just talking to her?"). If you
  have an answer in mind, the question is leading.
- Skipping G: sessions without a clear goal wander and end with no action.
- Too much R: endless exploration of the problem can deepen stuckness.
- Over-ambitious W: one achievable step beats five aspirational ones.
- If emotion overwhelms the session repeatedly, pause and consider whether coaching is the right support.

## Verify
- [ ] The session goal was agreed and measurable by the client.
- [ ] Client generated at least three options before choosing.
- [ ] Clear action, date and commitment score recorded in their words.
- [ ] You talked for well under a third of the session.
- [ ] Session closed with "what was most useful?"
