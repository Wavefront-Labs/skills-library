# Three-way contracting and 360 feedback debriefs

**Goal:** coach a leader on *themselves* — how they lead, decide and are experienced by
others — in an engagement paid for by their organisation, so that change is real,
visible to stakeholders, and the client's confidentiality survives. Core session craft is
in `human-services.life-and-career-coaching.coaching-conversation.running-a-grow-coaching-session`.

## 1. Who's the client?
Three parties: the **coachee** (the leader), the **sponsor** (line manager, often with
HR), and you. The organisation pays and wants a business outcome; the coachee owns the
conversations. Make this explicit before session one.

## 2. The three-way contracting meeting (60 minutes)
Agree, in the room together:
- **Objectives:** two or three development goals, framed as observable behaviour
  ("delegates decisions on budgets under X", "chairs leadership team so others speak
  more"), not traits ("be more strategic").
- **Evidence:** how change will be noticed — follow-up stakeholder pulse, sponsor's
  observations, specific metrics.
- **Confidentiality:** session content stays with the coachee. What *is* shared, and by
  whom: typically progress against objectives, reported **by the coachee** in a later
  three-way, plus attendance. Write this down.
- **Sponsor's role:** regular feedback to the coachee between sessions; mid-point and
  final three-ways.
- **Logistics:** number of sessions (often six to twelve over six to nine months),
  cancellation policy, what happens if the coachee leaves or the role changes.
- **Red line:** coaching is not a disguised performance-management step. If the sponsor
  is building a case to exit the leader, decline or insist that's transparent to the coachee.

## 3. Gathering 360 feedback
- **Survey-based:** a validated multi-rater instrument; anonymity needs enough raters
  per group (typically at least three) or comments get attributed.
- **Interview-based:** 6–10 stakeholders, 30 minutes each, same questions: "What should
  they keep doing? Start? Stop? What's one thing that would make the biggest difference?"
  Promise anonymity; report themes, not quotes that identify people.

## 4. Debriefing the feedback (90 minutes, in person if possible)
1. **Set up:** remind them it's perception data, not truth, and it's a gift — people took time.
2. **Let them read first** (or walk through), then ask: "What stands out? What surprises
   you? What's familiar?"
3. **Strengths first, genuinely.** Leaders skip to the negatives and stay there.
4. **Look for themes across rater groups:** gaps between self-rating and others'; between
   boss, peers and direct reports.
5. **Expect reaction:** defensiveness ("they don't see the pressure I'm under"), hurt,
   or hunting for who said what. Reflect it, let it land, don't argue or rush. Sometimes
   the real work starts in the next session.
6. **Narrow to one or two focus areas** — the ones with most leverage and that the
   leader cares about.
7. **Plan stakeholder follow-up:** thank raters, share the focus areas, ask for ongoing
   suggestions. Visible effort shifts perception.

## 5. Derailers
Strengths overused under stress become derailers: confidence → arrogance, high standards
→ micromanaging, diplomacy → avoiding conflict. Name the pattern, find the triggers,
practise an alternative behaviour in low-stakes settings.

## 6. Closing the engagement
Final three-way: coachee reports progress; sponsor gives observations; agree how
development continues. A short repeat pulse survey on the focus behaviours gives
evidence. Don't promise ROI figures you can't support.

## Gotchas
- Sponsors asking "how's it going?" informally — answer only on process (attendance), and
  redirect to the coachee.
- Becoming the leader's ally against their organisation, or the organisation's informant.
- Interview data that includes allegations of misconduct (harassment, fraud) can't stay
  confidential coaching material; agree in the contract how that's handled.
- Clinical issues (heavy drinking, depression) are outside executive coaching — refer.

## Verify
- [ ] Written three-way agreement: objectives, evidence, confidentiality, roles, sessions.
- [ ] Objectives phrased as observable behaviours.
- [ ] 360 anonymity protected (rater minimums, themed reporting).
- [ ] Debrief covered strengths, narrowed to one or two focus areas, and planned stakeholder follow-up.
- [ ] Engagement closed with a three-way and evidence of change.
