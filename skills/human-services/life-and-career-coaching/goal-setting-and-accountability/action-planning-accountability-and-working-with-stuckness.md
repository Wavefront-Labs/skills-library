# Action planning, accountability and working with stuckness

**Goal:** help a coaching client move from "I want to" to visible progress between
sessions — and when they keep not doing it, treat that as useful information rather
than failure. For the in-session question flow see
`human-services.life-and-career-coaching.coaching-conversation.running-a-grow-coaching-session`;
for self-directed habit tools see `mind-self.habit-and-self-management` skills.

## 1. Shape goals that pull, not push
- **Whose goal is it?** Goals set by a manager, partner or parent ("I should lose weight")
  stall. Ask: "If nobody else cared, would you still want this? What would it give you?"
- **Approach, not avoidance:** "Feel confident speaking in meetings" beats "stop being
  so quiet".
- **Three layers:** an *outcome* goal (win the role), *performance* goals (within their
  control: present twice in team meetings), and *process* goals (prepare three points
  the night before). Clients control the last two; aim actions there.
- **SMART-enough:** specific and time-bound for actions; outcome goals can stay a little
  inspirational. Don't over-engineer.

## 2. Between-session actions
- **One to three actions, small enough to start this week.** Ask the 1–10 commitment
  question; below 7, shrink the action until it's an 8.
- **If–then plans:** "If it's Tuesday after the stand-up, then I'll message Priya to book
  the call." Linking an action to a cue reliably raises follow-through.
- **Mental contrasting (WOOP):** Wish, Outcome, Obstacle (internal — "I'll feel
  awkward"), Plan ("If I feel awkward, I'll send it anyway and take a walk").
- Client writes the action in their own words; you keep a copy.

## 3. Accountability without policing
Agree the mechanism at the start of the engagement:
- Start each session: "What's happened since we last spoke?" — before checking the
  list, so wins they didn't plan for surface.
- Optional midweek check-in (a short message from the client, not chasing from you).
- Review as learning: "What worked? What got in the way? What did you notice about
  yourself?" — never "Why didn't you do it?"
- Celebrate partial progress specifically. Track progress visibly (a scale, a
  simple log) so momentum is felt.

## 4. Working with stuckness
When the same action fails two or three sessions running:
1. **Check the goal still matters.** Circumstances change; dropping a goal is a legitimate outcome.
2. **Check the size.** Most non-completion is actions that are too big or vague.
3. **Look for the competing commitment** (Kegan & Lahey's "immunity to change"):
   - "What are you doing or not doing instead?"
   - "If you imagine doing the opposite, what's the worry?"
   - "So you may also be committed to… (not looking foolish / keeping the peace)."
   - "What big assumption makes that commitment feel necessary?"
   Then design a small, safe **test** of the assumption rather than more willpower.
4. **Check for something bigger.** Persistent low energy, hopelessness, sleep problems
   or anxiety that blocks everything may need a GP or therapist rather than more coaching.
   Raise it gently and refer if needed.

## 5. Reviewing the whole engagement
Every four to six sessions, revisit the original goals: scale progress (0–10 then vs
now), what's changed, what to keep, drop or add. Clear endings matter — a client who
leaves with a self-coaching plan has learned the method, not just reached a goal.

## Gotchas
- Owning their goals for them ("we need to get you to…") breeds dependence.
- Accountability via guilt works briefly, then clients start cancelling sessions.
- Perfectionists turn actions into tests; reframe actions as experiments.
- Over-planning can itself be a form of avoidance.

## Verify
- [ ] Goals are the client's own, framed as approach, with process-level actions.
- [ ] Every action scored at least 7/10 commitment and has an if–then cue.
- [ ] Sessions open with what happened, and review treats misses as learning.
- [ ] Repeated non-completion explored for size, relevance and competing commitments.
- [ ] Engagement reviewed against original goals at regular intervals.
