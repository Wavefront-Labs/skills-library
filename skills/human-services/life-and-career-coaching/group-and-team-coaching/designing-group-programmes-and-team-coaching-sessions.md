# Designing group programmes and team coaching sessions

**Goal:** coach more than one person at a time. Two different things share the name:
- **Group coaching:** individuals with *separate* goals learning alongside each other
  (a cohort of new managers, women returners, people changing career).
- **Team coaching:** a *team* with a shared purpose, where the client is the team's
  way of working together, not each individual.
Contract and design differently for each. One-to-one craft is in
`human-services.life-and-career-coaching.coaching-conversation.running-a-grow-coaching-session`.

## 1. Group coaching programme design
- **Size:** 4–8 for depth (everyone gets airtime); up to ~12 with breakout pairs/trios.
- **Shape:** a kick-off, then 4–8 sessions of 90–120 minutes every two to four weeks,
  plus optional 1:1s at start and end. Between-session peer pairs keep momentum.
- **Session pattern:** check-in round (progress on actions) → short input on a theme
  (10–15 min, not a lecture) → hot-seat coaching for one or two members while others
  observe and offer questions → pairs/trios practise → check-out with actions.
- **Hot seat:** the member states a challenge; you (and then peers) ask only questions
  for 10–15 minutes; peers may then offer "If I were you I might…" ideas, which the
  member chooses from; member says what they'll take.

## 2. Peer-coaching circles / action learning sets
A set of 4–6 meets regularly; one presents a problem; others ask open questions only,
no advice, for a fixed time; presenter commits to action; report back next time. The
coach facilitates the process and, over time, hands it over so the group runs itself.

## 3. Team coaching
1. **Contract with the leader and the team.** The team leader is a member, not the
   client. Agree goals, confidentiality (what's shared with the wider organisation), and
   that the leader will be challenged too.
2. **Diagnose:** short interviews or a survey across members and key stakeholders:
   purpose clarity, roles, decision-making, trust, how conflict is handled, how others
   experience the team.
3. **Feed back themes** (anonymised) to the whole team and let them make sense of them.
4. **Work on a few priorities** over several sessions: a team charter (purpose,
   commitments, how we disagree, how we decide), meeting redesign, role clarity,
   stakeholder relationships. Coach *live* — interrupt patterns as they happen ("I notice
   the same two people have spoken for ten minutes").
5. **Review** progress against the diagnostic.

## 4. Psychological safety
Members need to feel they can speak without punishment. Build it: leader goes first in
admitting mistakes, explicit agreements, curiosity about dissent, no surprises
afterwards. If a member's job is at risk because of what's said, the room isn't safe —
don't pretend otherwise.

## 5. Group contracting essentials
- Confidentiality is a *group promise* you can't fully guarantee; say so.
- Agree: attendance expectations, what can be shared outside ("themes, not stories"),
  cameras on for virtual sessions, how to raise concerns.
- For organisation-funded groups, agree what the sponsor hears (usually attendance and
  overall themes only).

## 6. Dynamics to watch
- Dominant members and quiet ones (use rounds, pairs, written reflection).
- Rank in the room: a senior person in a peer group suppresses honesty — mix carefully.
- Rescuing: peers jumping to advice. Hold the "questions first" rule.
- Conflict avoidance in teams: surface it gently ("What's not being said here?").
- Scapegoating one member for team problems.

## Gotchas
- Running a group like a workshop with lots of content and little coaching.
- Team coaching that's actually the leader's agenda in disguise.
- A team member disclosing something sensitive about a colleague — interrupt and redirect to the process.
- Virtual groups: shorter sessions (60–90 min), more structure, breakouts for depth.

## Verify
- [ ] Clear about group vs team coaching; contract reflects which.
- [ ] Group size and session shape give everyone real airtime.
- [ ] Questions-first rule held in hot seats and circles.
- [ ] Team coaching started from a diagnostic and has a charter or agreed priorities.
- [ ] Confidentiality agreement and sponsor reporting are explicit.
