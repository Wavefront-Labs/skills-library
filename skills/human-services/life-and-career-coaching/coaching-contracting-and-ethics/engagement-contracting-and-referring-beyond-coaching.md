# Engagement contracting and referring beyond coaching

**Goal:** put a clear, ethical frame around every coaching relationship so both sides
know what's on offer, what's private, and what happens when coaching isn't the right
help. Coaching is largely **unregulated** in most countries — anyone can use the title —
so professional bodies' codes (ICF, EMCC, AC) and your own contract do the work a
licensing board would do elsewhere. *Check local law; this isn't legal advice.*

## 1. Chemistry session (free, 20–30 minutes)
- Hear what they want; explain how you work and what coaching is and isn't.
- Assess fit: is this a coaching issue? Do you have relevant experience? Do you like
  working together?
- Screen gently: current crises, therapy, health issues relevant to goals.
- It's fine — good, even — to say "I'm not the right coach for this" and suggest others.

## 2. The written agreement
Cover at least:
- **Parties** and, for sponsored work, the sponsor (see
  `human-services.life-and-career-coaching.executive-coaching.three-way-contracting-and-360-feedback-debriefs`).
- **Purpose and goals** (initial; they'll evolve).
- **Practicalities:** number, length and frequency of sessions, medium, fees,
  invoicing, cancellation/late policy, between-session contact.
- **Confidentiality and its limits:** risk of serious harm to self or others, child
  safeguarding, illegal activity where disclosure is required, and supervision
  (discussed anonymously).
- **Records:** what you keep, where, how long, who can see them; the client's right of access.
- **Roles:** client responsible for decisions and results; coach for process. No
  guaranteed outcomes.
- **Ending:** either party can end; how notice works; what happens to records.
- **Complaints:** how to raise one, and the professional body you belong to.

## 3. Records and data
Keep notes minimal and factual (goals, actions, themes), stored securely
(encrypted device, locked cabinet). Retain only as long as your policy says (many
practitioners use 6–7 years for financial records and less for session notes — *check
your jurisdiction*). Don't keep health details unless needed and consented.

## 4. Coaching vs therapy — where's the line?
| Usually coaching | Usually therapy / clinical |
|---|---|
| Goals, performance, direction | Symptoms, diagnosis, treatment |
| Functioning reasonably well | Daily functioning significantly impaired |
| Present and future focus | Extensive work on the past or trauma |
| Client wants to grow | Client needs to heal or stabilise |
The edges blur (confidence, stress). Ask yourself: is the issue preventing them using
coaching? Would a mental-health professional handle this better? Does continuing
serve them, or my income?

## 5. Referring beyond coaching
Triggers: suicidal thoughts, self-harm, signs of depression, anxiety disorders,
trauma, addiction, eating disorders, domestic abuse; also legal, financial or medical
questions. Steps:
1. Name what you've noticed, kindly and specifically.
2. Explain why another professional could help more.
3. Offer concrete options (GP, therapist directories, specialist services, EAP).
4. Agree whether to pause coaching or continue alongside — some clients do both, with
   clearly separate focus.
Clinical red flags and warm handover are detailed in
`human-services.non-clinical-counselling.boundaries-and-referral.recognising-clinical-red-flags-and-warm-referral`.

## 6. Conflicts and boundaries
- Coaching friends, family, your own staff, or two people in the same team creates
  conflicts. Decline or name it and agree safeguards.
- No romantic or sexual relationships with clients (codes typically extend this past the engagement).
- Don't sell clients other products of yours without disclosing the interest.
- Have **supervision** and professional indemnity insurance.

## Gotchas
- Verbal agreements fail at the first disagreement about fees or confidentiality.
- "Life coaching" that drifts into weekly trauma processing is unethical and risky.
- Testimonials and case studies need explicit consent and anonymisation.
- Calling yourself "certified" implies a credential — state which body and level.

## Verify
- [ ] Chemistry session held; fit and scope assessed.
- [ ] Signed agreement covering confidentiality limits, records, fees, ending, complaints.
- [ ] Notes minimal, secure, with a retention period.
- [ ] Coaching-vs-therapy check done when issues touch mental health; referral offered when needed.
- [ ] Supervision, insurance and ethics code in place and named to clients.
