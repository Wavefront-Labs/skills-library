# Wheel-of-life reviews and sustainable habit change

**Goal:** help a broadly well client improve how their life feels — energy, balance,
health behaviours, routines — through change they sustain after coaching ends. Life
and wellness coaches **do not diagnose, treat or prescribe** (diet plans for medical
conditions, exercise programmes for injuries, anything for mental illness); those belong
to doctors, dietitians, physiotherapists and therapists. Self-directed habit techniques
are in `mind-self.habit-and-self-management` skills.

## 1. Intake and scope check
- Ask about health conditions, medication, current treatment and any eating or
  mood history *before* setting wellbeing goals. If someone wants weight loss and
  describes restricting or purging, or wants motivation but describes months of low
  mood, refer first (see
  `human-services.non-clinical-counselling.boundaries-and-referral.recognising-clinical-red-flags-and-warm-referral`).
- For big exercise or diet changes with a health condition, the client checks with
  their doctor first. Put this in the coaching agreement.

## 2. The wheel of life
Draw a circle with 8 segments: e.g. health, career, money, relationships/partner,
family and friends, fun, personal growth, environment/home (let clients rename them).
- Score each 1–10 for current satisfaction (centre = 0).
- Ask: "What do you notice?" "Which segment, if it went up two points, would lift
  others?" (Sleep or health often does.) "What would a 7 look like there?"
- Pick **one or two** segments to work on. Re-score every few sessions; the visual
  change motivates.
Variations: score importance too, and target high-importance/low-satisfaction areas.

## 3. Motivation: work with ambivalence
Most people are ambivalent ("I want to exercise, but I'm exhausted"). Pushing the
pro-change side makes them defend the other side.
- Ask for their reasons: "Why might you want to change this?" "How would life be
  different?" "On a scale of 0–10, how important is it? Why not lower?" — the
  "why not lower" answer is their own argument for change.
- Reflect **change talk** ("You want to keep up with your kids") more than sustain talk.
- Support **autonomy, competence and connection** (self-determination theory): their
  choice, achievable steps, and people who help.

## 4. Designing habits that last
- **Tiny:** start ridiculously small (two minutes of walking, one glass of water)
  and grow once automatic. Consistency before intensity.
- **Anchor:** attach to an existing routine ("After I put the kettle on, I…").
- **Environment:** make it easy (shoes by the door) and the competing behaviour harder.
- **Celebrate** immediately — a small, genuine sense of success wires the habit.
- **Track simply:** a tick on a calendar; reviewing patterns beats judging misses.
- **Identity:** "I'm someone who moves every day" is sturdier than "I'm trying to exercise".

## 5. Lapses and plateaus
Lapses are expected — plan for them in advance: "When you miss a few days, what will you do?"
A useful rule: never miss twice. Review a lapse as data (what triggered it, what to
adjust), not as proof of failure. Plateaus often mean it's time to change the goal,
not to push harder.

## 6. Balance and sustainability
Watch for clients adding goals to an overloaded life. Ask what they'll stop doing to
make room. Rest, relationships and fun are legitimate coaching goals too.

## Gotchas
- Giving specific diet, supplement or training prescriptions — out of scope and potentially harmful.
- Wellness culture can feed shame or disordered eating; avoid weight-centric praise.
- Big "new me" plans fail; one tiny habit that sticks beats ten that don't.
- Clients who repeatedly can't start may be dealing with depression, ADHD or burnout — explore and refer as needed.

## Verify
- [ ] Health and mood history checked; medical sign-off where needed; scope stated in agreement.
- [ ] Wheel of life scored and one or two focus areas chosen by the client.
- [ ] Client's own reasons for change elicited and reflected.
- [ ] Each habit is tiny, anchored, environment-supported and tracked.
- [ ] A lapse plan exists and the wheel is re-scored periodically.
