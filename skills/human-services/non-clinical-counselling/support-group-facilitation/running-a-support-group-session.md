# Running a support-group session

**Goal:** run a 60–120 minute peer or mutual-support group (carers, bereaved parents,
people living with a condition, people in recovery) where everyone who wants to speak
is heard, nobody is harmed, and members leave feeling less alone. The facilitator
holds the *structure*; the members provide the support. This is not group therapy —
no one is being treated, and the facilitator is not the expert in the room.

## 1. Before the group
- **Venue:** private, accessible, chairs in a circle, tissues, water, a clock you can
  see and they can't stare at. Online: waiting room on, cameras optional, a co-host
  watching chat.
- **Co-facilitate if you can.** One leads, one watches the room (who's upset, who's
  trying to speak) and can step out with someone who leaves distressed.
- **New members:** a short call or chat beforehand — what the group is, what it isn't,
  confidentiality, and whether it's the right fit (someone in acute crisis may need
  individual support first).

## 2. Group agreement (co-created in the first session, re-read when new people join)
Typical items:
- What's said here stays here (and its limits: risk to life or a child).
- One person speaks at a time; no cross-talk or fixing unless invited.
- Speak from your own experience ("I" statements).
- Right to pass.
- Phones away; start and finish on time.
- Respect differences (beliefs, choices, ways of coping).
Write it on a card or screen. When it's the *group's* agreement, enforcing it is
pointing to what everyone signed up to — not you policing.

## 3. A reliable session shape
1. **Welcome** and housekeeping (exits, toilets, finish time); reminder of the agreement.
2. **Check-in go-round:** name plus one line ("how your week's been, in a word or a
   sentence"). Sets a fair share and surfaces who needs time.
3. **Main sharing** — open floor, a theme, or a speaker followed by discussion.
4. **Check-out go-round:** one thing to take away, or one thing for the week ahead.
5. **Close on time**, then stay ten minutes for anyone who needs a word.

## 4. Balancing airtime
- **Dominating member:** first, use structure — go-rounds, a "let's hear from someone
  who hasn't spoken yet". Then a gentle interruption with their name and a summary:
  "Sam, you've said something important about feeling invisible — I'd like to see who
  else connects with that." If it persists, talk to them privately, warmly, between sessions.
- **Quiet member:** invite, don't pressure: "Jo, anything you'd like to add? Passing is fine."
  Silence may be how they take part.
- **Advice-givers:** "Can I check — would it help to hear suggestions, or just to be heard?"

## 5. Holding heavy disclosures
When someone shares something raw (a death, abuse, suicidal feelings):
- Slow down; acknowledge it directly and thank them for trusting the group.
- Don't move on as if it hadn't happened, and don't let the group pile in with advice.
- If there's risk, check in privately at the break or end, using the crisis approach in
  `human-services.non-clinical-counselling.crisis-line-work.handling-a-crisis-call-from-rapport-to-safe-ending`
  and your organisation's procedure.
- Check how others are affected: "That was a lot to hear — how's everyone doing?"

## 6. Closing a group well
End each session with something steadying (the check-out, a reading the group chose,
a breath). When a member leaves for good, or the group itself ends, mark it: a goodbye
round, not a silent drop-off.

## Gotchas
- Groups develop a "culture": cynicism or competition over who's suffered most spreads fast. Name it early.
- Romantic or business relationships between members can split a group; have a policy.
- Don't let the group become your own support — get supervision or a facilitator peer group.
- Online groups: screen for people joining to recruit, sell or troll; remove fast.

## Verify
- [ ] Group agreement visible and re-read when new members join.
- [ ] Check-in and check-out go-rounds happened; started and finished on time.
- [ ] Nobody took more than a fair share without being redirected.
- [ ] Heavy disclosures acknowledged and followed up privately; risk handled per procedure.
- [ ] Facilitator debriefed with co-facilitator or supervisor.
