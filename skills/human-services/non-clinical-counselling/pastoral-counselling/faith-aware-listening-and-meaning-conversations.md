# Faith-aware listening and meaning conversations

**Goal:** support someone through a life difficulty — conflict, loss, a hard decision,
a crisis of meaning — in a way that takes their faith seriously as a resource (or as
part of the struggle), without turning the conversation into doctrine-teaching, ritual,
or clinical therapy. Rites, prayer and sacraments sit in
`religion-philosophy.chaplaincy-and-pastoral-ministry` skills; this is the listening and
guidance side. Pastoral counselling by clergy or lay ministers is **non-clinical**:
it doesn't diagnose or treat mental illness, and should know when to refer.

## 1. Frame the conversation
- **Agree what this is:** a pastoral conversation, how long, how many times (open-ended
  "come whenever" arrangements drift into dependency). A short series — say three to six
  conversations — with a review is a common, healthy pattern.
- **Name confidentiality honestly**, including its limits: safeguarding of children
  and vulnerable adults, risk to life. Seal-of-confession rules, where they apply, are a
  separate denominational matter — know your tradition's position and your legal duties
  before you're in the room. *(Varies by tradition and jurisdiction.)*
- **Mind the power:** you may represent God, the community, or authority to them. They
  may tell you what they think you want to hear, or fear judgement. Say plainly that
  they can disagree with you.

## 2. Listen first, theology later (if at all)
Use the core listening moves (`human-services.non-clinical-counselling.supportive-listening.active-listening-reflection-and-holding-silence`).
Then explore how faith is *working* in this situation:
- "Where is your faith in all this — is it helping, or making it harder?"
- "What does your tradition say to you about this? And what do *you* think?"
- "Is there a story, prayer or text that's been on your mind?"
Let them bring the religious language; mirror it. Don't assume their beliefs match
your tradition, even inside your own congregation.

## 3. Common themes
- **Doubt:** treat it as part of a faith journey, not a problem to fix with arguments.
  Avoid answering a lament with an apologetic.
- **Guilt and shame:** separate realistic guilt (something to repair) from
  disproportionate or scrupulous guilt (which may be a mental-health symptom — e.g.
  religious-flavoured OCD — and warrants referral).
- **Forgiveness:** don't press someone to forgive an abuser or to reconcile. Forgiveness
  is not the same as trust or contact. Safety comes first.
- **"Why did God let this happen?":** stay with the question; ready-made answers
  ("God needed another angel") often wound.
- **Decision-making:** help them weigh options against their own values and discernment
  practice; don't decide for them.

## 4. Premarital and relationship conversations
Typical topics: expectations, family of origin, money, conflict styles, children,
faith practice as a couple, sex and intimacy (at the couple's comfort level). Structured
inventories exist and can help. **Screen for coercive control or abuse** — speak to each
person separately at least once; never offer couple work where one partner is afraid
of the other.

## 5. When to refer
Refer (warmly; see `human-services.non-clinical-counselling.boundaries-and-referral.recognising-clinical-red-flags-and-warm-referral`) for:
suicidal thoughts, self-harm, psychosis (including religious content that is new,
intense and out of keeping with their tradition), severe depression, trauma,
addiction, eating disorders, domestic abuse needing specialist support, or anything
needing more than about six conversations. Keep offering pastoral support *alongside*
clinical care — it isn't either/or.

## Gotchas
- Prescribing more prayer or faith for depression can add shame — it is not a substitute for treatment.
- Spiritual abuse (control through religious authority) is real; listen for it,
  including in your own community.
- Counselling congregants you also lead, employ or socialise with creates dual roles;
  name them and consider referring to a colleague.
- Meet in safe settings (visible, interruptible), follow your body's safeguarding policy.

## Verify
- [ ] Number of sessions, purpose and confidentiality limits agreed.
- [ ] The person's own beliefs and language guided the conversation, not your doctrine.
- [ ] No pressure to forgive, reconcile or "pray it away".
- [ ] Abuse screened for in couple work, each partner seen alone at least once.
- [ ] Clinical red flags referred on, with pastoral support continuing.
