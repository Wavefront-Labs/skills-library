# Running a safe home-care visit

**Goal:** a visit that delivers what the care plan says, leaves the person and their
home as they want it, and keeps you safe while working alone. Your provider's
procedures (check-in app, lone-worker policy, no-response protocol) take priority over
anything here.

## 1. Before you leave
- Read the plan and the last notes for **this** client: tasks, time allowed, access
  instructions (key safe code comes from the secure system, never written on paper),
  pets, risks (smoking, aggression history, hoarding, hazards).
- Phone charged, ID badge, gloves/aprons, hand gel, any equipment.
- Check-in method ready (app, phone call). Your coordinator needs to know where you
  are.

## 2. Arrival
1. Knock/ring and identify yourself clearly ("Hi, it's Sam from the care service").
   Show ID — many older people are rightly wary of strangers.
2. **Check in** on the system.
3. Quick scan: does the person look and sound as usual? Is the home as usual (heating
   on, food in the fridge, post not piling up)? Note changes.
4. Ask how they are and what matters to them today; agree the order of tasks.

## 3. If no one answers
Do **not** just leave. Typical steps (follow your provider's exact protocol):
knock again, call their phone, look through windows for anyone on the floor, check
with neighbours if allowed, **ring your office**, who will contact family/next of kin.
If you see someone collapsed or there is any doubt, call emergency services. Log
every step with times. A missed visit with no answer may be a person who has fallen.

## 4. During the visit
- **It's their home:** ask before opening cupboards, moving things, or using
  appliances. Do tasks their way where safe ("how do you like the bed made?").
- Domestic tasks: do what's in scope — light cleaning, laundry, meal prep, shopping —
  and involve the person where they can.
- Work through tasks in plan order but leave time for conversation; for many clients
  you are their only visitor that day.
- Food safety: check dates in the fridge, discard only with consent, report a bare
  fridge.
- Don't accept gifts, money, or be added to wills; don't lend or borrow; don't run
  personal errands off-plan. Report offers to your supervisor.

## 5. Working alone safely
- Trust your instinct: if you feel unsafe (aggressive person in the home,
  intoxication, weapons, dangerous dog), **leave** and phone your office. Your safety
  is not a lower priority than finishing tasks.
- Park facing out, keep keys and phone on you, know the exit.
- Use the lone-worker check-in/out or panic function as your service requires.
- Manual handling: you can't safely do a two-person task alone — report it rather
  than attempt it.

## 6. Leaving
- Check the person is comfortable, safe and has what they need in reach: drink,
  phone, personal alarm pendant **on**, walking aid.
- Doors locked as agreed, key back in the key safe, heating/stove off as appropriate.
- **Check out** on the system.
- Write notes: tasks done/not done and why, what you observed, anything reported.
  Factual, dated, signed (see
  `human-services.disability-support.support-planning-and-documentation.writing-shift-notes-and-incident-reports`).

## Gotchas
- Running late: call the office, who tell the client — waiting anxiously for a carer
  is distressing.
- Family may ask you to do things outside the plan; politely refer them to the office.
- Never take photos in a client's home on a personal phone.

## Verify
- [ ] Checked in and out; location known to the office throughout.
- [ ] No-answer protocol followed fully, if needed, with times logged.
- [ ] Changes in the person or home reported the same day.
- [ ] Person left safe with alarm, drink and aids in reach.
- [ ] Notes completed before the next visit.
