# Dignified personal care routine for older adults

**Goal:** help an older person wash, dress, groom and use the toilet in a way that keeps
them safe, keeps as much control in their hands as possible, and leaves them feeling
respected rather than handled. This is general training-level reference; the person's
own **care plan** and your employer's procedures always override it.

## 1. Before you start
- **Read the care plan** for this person: mobility/transfer method, skin or wound notes,
  continence aids, preferences (shower vs wash, time of day, gender of carer), and any
  "do not" instructions. If it is missing or out of date, tell your supervisor first.
- **Gather everything first** — towels, clothes chosen *with* the person, products,
  gloves, aids. Leaving someone wet or exposed while you fetch things is the most common
  avoidable lapse.
- **Environment:** warm room (older skin loses heat fast), door closed, curtains drawn,
  non-slip mat down, water tested on your inner wrist then by the person.
- **Hand hygiene** and gloves for contact with body fluids; change gloves between
  "dirty" and "clean" tasks.

## 2. Consent, every time
Explain what you are about to do *before* doing it, in short sentences, and wait for
agreement — a nod or "yes" counts; silence or pulling away does not. Consent given
yesterday is not consent today. If the person declines, do not force or trick: offer a
different time, a different approach (wash at the basin instead of shower), or a
different carer, then **record the refusal and tell your supervisor**. Repeated refusal
of hygiene is a care-planning issue, not something to win.

## 3. Doing it with the person, not to them
- **Maximise independence:** hand them the flannel, let them do face and front; you do
  back and feet. Slower is fine — doing it for them erodes function (see
  `human-services.disability-support.daily-living-skill-building.task-analysis-prompting-and-fading` for prompting).
- **Keep covered:** expose only the area being washed; a towel over the lap in the
  shower chair makes a big difference.
- **Order:** cleanest to dirtiest (face, then body, genital/perianal area last), front to
  back for perineal care.
- **Dressing:** weak or painful side goes into clothing **first** and comes out **last**.
  Offer two choices of outfit rather than an open question if decisions are hard.
- **Oral care and dentures:** clean dentures out of the mouth over a basin of water
  (dropped dentures break), check gums for sores, follow the plan for overnight storage.
- **Grooming:** hair, shaving (electric razor unless the plan says otherwise, especially
  for anyone on blood thinners), nails — many services restrict toenail cutting to
  podiatry for people with diabetes or poor circulation. Check before you cut.

## 4. Toileting and continence support
- Respond to requests promptly; "hold on" leads to accidents and distress.
- Offer regular prompted toileting if the plan says so — at set times, not "do you need to go?" asked once.
- Use the continence product the plan specifies, correctly sized; doubling up pads
  traps moisture and damages skin rather than adding protection.
- Clean and pat dry; apply barrier cream only as directed.
- Use neutral language ("let's get you fresh"), never "nappy".

## 5. Observe and report — you are the eyes
Each session is a skin and wellbeing check. Report the same shift, in writing and verbally:
- redness that does not fade after pressure is relieved, blisters, broken skin,
  bruising (especially unexplained or patterned — see
  `human-services.aged-care.elder-safeguarding.recognising-and-escalating-elder-abuse`);
- new confusion, drowsiness, pain on movement, swelling;
- urine that is dark, strong-smelling or bloody, new incontinence, constipation;
- reduced ability compared with last time.
Sudden change in continence or confusion can signal infection and needs a clinician,
not just a pad change. Do not diagnose — describe what you saw.

## Gotchas
- **Falls in the bathroom** are the big risk: never leave someone standing or on a
  shower chair alone unless the plan says they are safe to be.
- **Hot water:** scald risk is higher with reduced sensation; check the plan for any
  water-temperature limits your service sets (often around 43 °C at the outlet —
  unconfirmed for all jurisdictions).
- **Cultural and gender preferences** about who provides intimate care are legitimate
  and should be in the plan; if not, ask and record.
- Never perform clinical tasks (wound dressing, catheter care, medication) unless you
  are trained and it is delegated in your role.

## Verify
- [ ] Care plan read; equipment gathered before starting.
- [ ] Consent sought for each step; refusal recorded and escalated, not overridden.
- [ ] Person kept covered and warm; did what they could themselves.
- [ ] Skin, continence and wellbeing changes reported and documented that shift.
- [ ] Gloves changed and hands washed between tasks and after.
