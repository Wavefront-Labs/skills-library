# Assisted eating, hydration and texture-modified meals

**Goal:** make sure the person eats and drinks enough, safely and with enjoyment.
Malnutrition and dehydration are common and under-noticed in older adults; choking and
aspiration are the acute dangers. Diet textures and swallowing advice come from a
**speech pathologist / speech and language therapist** and dietitian — your job is to
follow that plan precisely and report what you see.

## 1. Before the meal
- Check the **mealtime plan**: diet texture, fluid thickness, allergies, cultural or
  religious requirements, likes/dislikes, equipment (lipped plate, built-up cutlery,
  two-handled cup), whether supervision or full assistance is needed.
- Toilet, hand-washing, glasses on, **dentures and hearing aids in**.
- Position: **upright at 90°**, feet supported, head slightly forward (chin tilted
  down, not back). In bed: sat fully up, not slumped.
- Calm setting: TV off, no rushing, no clinical tasks during the meal.

## 2. Texture-modified food and thickened drinks
Many services use the **IDDSI** framework: drinks levels 0 Thin to 4 Extremely thick;
foods levels 3 Liquidised to 7 Regular (with 7EC "easy to chew"). Practical rules:
- Serve exactly the prescribed level — "a bit of normal food as a treat" is a
  common cause of choking incidents.
- Thicken drinks with the product and the measure stated; stir, wait the stated
  time, re-check (some thickeners keep thickening).
- Watch for mixed textures (cereal in milk, soup with lumps, jelly that melts) —
  usually unsafe for people on modified diets unless the plan allows.
- Present pureed food attractively and separately so the person knows what it is.

## 3. Assisting someone to eat
- **Sit at eye level** beside them, not standing over.
- Tell them what each mouthful is; offer choice of next item.
- Small spoonfuls (a teaspoon is often right); wait until the previous mouthful is
  swallowed — watch the throat move and check the mouth is clear.
- Let them hold the cup or spoon with your hand guiding if they can — "hand-under-hand"
  preserves skill and dignity.
- Allow 30–45 minutes if needed; keep food warm.
- Keep upright for **at least 30 minutes after** eating (time varies — check plan).

## 4. Hydration
Older people often lose thirst sensation. Offer drinks frequently through the day, not
just at meals; leave a drink within reach and in a cup they can manage. Use preferred
drinks; jelly, soup and fruit count. Fill in fluid charts **at the time**, not end of
shift. Signs to report: dark urine, dry mouth, new confusion, dizziness, constipation.

## 5. Stop and report — swallowing warning signs
Coughing or choking during meals, wet or gurgly voice after swallowing, food pocketed
in the cheek, prolonged chewing, watery eyes, chest infections, refusing food. These
need a swallow reassessment. If someone is **choking**, follow your first-aid training
and call for help immediately.

## 6. Weight and intake
Report reduced intake over several meals, loose clothes/dentures, and any weight loss
recorded. Many services use a screening tool such as MUST or MNA run by clinical staff.

## Gotchas
- Rushing because the next resident is waiting causes aspiration — escalate staffing
  pressure, don't speed up.
- Thin liquids are most likely to be aspirated; tea poured "a bit thinner" is not a
  small change.
- Respect a capable person's informed choice to eat outside their plan (sometimes
  called "eating and drinking with acknowledged risk") — this must be documented and
  agreed with the clinical team, never decided by you alone.

## Verify
- [ ] Texture, fluid level, allergies and preferences checked on the plan.
- [ ] Upright posture, dentures/glasses in, sat at eye level.
- [ ] Paced spoonfuls, mouth clear between, upright afterwards.
- [ ] Fluids offered regularly; charts completed in real time.
- [ ] Swallow warning signs and intake/weight changes reported same shift.
