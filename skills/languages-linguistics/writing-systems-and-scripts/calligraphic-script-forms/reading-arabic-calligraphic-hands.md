# Reading Arabic calligraphic hands

**Goal:** identify which hand a text is written in and read it — on inscriptions,
manuscripts, documents and signage — as a matter of script literacy rather than art
appreciation. Assumes you already read printed Arabic script.

> Draft. Hand descriptions follow standard calligraphy histories in broad terms; dating
> and regional sub-styles are specialist territory. Reading Persian/Urdu/Ottoman text
> also needs those languages.

## 1. Identify the hand first
- **Naskh:** the book hand and basis of most print typefaces. Compact, rounded, clear,
  on a baseline. If you read print, you read Naskh.
- **Thuluth:** large, monumental, with tall vertical strokes, sweeping curves, letters
  **stacked** and interlaced. Used on mosque friezes, headings, panels.
- **Kufic** (several kinds): angular, horizontally extended, often **without dots or
  vowels** in early examples. **Square (banna'i) Kufic** builds text into geometric grids
  on tiles and brick — may read in a spiral or around a square.
- **Muhaqqaq / Rayhani / Tawqi':** older formal hands; Muhaqqaq has long horizontals
  and shallow curves (classic for large Qur'ans).
- **Diwani:** Ottoman chancery hand — highly cursive, letters slanting and crowding
  together, often unvocalised; **jali Diwani** fills the gaps with ornamental dots.
- **Ruqʿah:** quick everyday handwriting hand; short, minimal, many dots joined into dashes.
- **Nastaʿliq:** Persian/Urdu hand; lines **slope down from right to left** within each
  word, long sweeping horizontals, heavy hanging bowls.
- **Maghribi:** North African/Andalusian; rounded open bowls, distinctive **fāʾ with
  dot below and qāf with one dot above**.

## 2. Reading strategies
1. **Find the baseline(s).** In Thuluth and Diwani, letters climb above and dip below it;
   in Nastaʿliq each word has its own sloping baseline. Trace each word's flow from its
   start (rightmost) to end.
2. **Unstack:** in compositions, a letter written above another may come *after* it in
   reading order. Follow connecting strokes, not vertical position.
3. **Recognise ligatures:** lām-alif (لا), and in formal hands many letter pairs
   (e.g. lām-mīm, initial yā'-mīm combinations) merge into one shape.
4. **Separate text from ornament:** vowel signs (ḥarakāt), sukūn and shadda are often
   placed for balance, not necessarily directly over their letter; decorative fillers
   (small marks, mini letters) occupy gaps.
5. **Dotless text:** in early Kufic and some decorative work dots (iʿjām) are absent,
   so ب ت ث ن ي look alike. Use **context and known formulae**.
6. **Start from formulae:** much inscriptional text is predictable — the basmala, Qur'anic
   verses, shahāda, names of God and the Prophet, dates, makers' and patrons' titles.
   Identifying one known phrase anchors the rest.
7. **Verify against a source text:** for Qur'anic inscriptions, find the verse and
   compare letter by letter.

## 3. Practical kit
- Good photos at multiple angles/lighting; for friezes, panoramas in reading order.
- Reference alphabets and specimen sheets for each hand.
- A transcription log: your reading, uncertain letters marked, the hand, and the anchor
  phrase used.

## Gotchas
- Reading vertical position as order.
- Assuming the text is Arabic: it may be Persian, Ottoman Turkish or Urdu.
- Square Kufic mirrors or rotates text to fit the grid.
- Maghribi fāʾ/qāf dots differ from Eastern conventions — easy misreads.
- Diwani ornamental dots taken for letter dots.
