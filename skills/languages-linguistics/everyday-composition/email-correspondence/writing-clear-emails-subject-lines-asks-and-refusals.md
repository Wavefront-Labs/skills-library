# Writing clear emails: subject lines, asks and refusals

**Goal:** the reader knows from the preview pane what you need, can answer in one reply,
and doesn't misread your tone. (Inbox management itself is covered in
`computing-software.general-digital-literacy.email-clients.managing-email-and-calendar`.)

## 1. Subject line = the email in miniature
- Say the **topic and the action**: "Approval needed by Fri: venue deposit £400" beats
  "Quick question".
- Prefixes help busy readers: *Action:*, *FYI:*, *Decision:*, *Re-sent:*.
- **Change the subject** when the thread changes topic, or start a new thread.

## 2. Lead with the point (bottom line up front)
- First two lines: what you need, from whom, by when. Background comes *after*.
- One email, one main ask. Two unrelated asks → two emails (the second ask gets lost otherwise).
- Make replying easy: yes/no questions, numbered options ("Reply 1 or 2"), proposed times.
- Use short paragraphs, and bullets for lists of three or more.
- Put **dates with weekdays** ("Thu 22 Oct") and time zones for cross-region readers.

## 3. Tone calibration
Email strips voice and face, so neutral reads cooler than intended and short reads curt.
- Open with a human line for people you know; skip it for quick operational notes.
- Soften commands to requests ("Could you send…" rather than "Send…") unless urgency is real.
- Remove **"just", "sorry to bother", "per my last email"** — the first two undercut you,
  the third reads as passive aggression.
- Read it as the **most stressed version of the recipient** would. If a sentence could sting,
  rewrite it or say it by phone.
- Match formality to the reader's last email; mirror their greeting style.
- Don't send angry: draft, save, reread after an hour.

## 4. Asking well
- Why them, what exactly, how long it takes them, by when, and what happens next.
  "Could you review the two-page budget (attached, ~15 min) by Wednesday so I can submit Thursday?"
- Attach the thing, or link it with access already granted — check permissions.

## 5. Declining gracefully
- Thank, decline clearly in the **first or second sentence**, give a short reason if
  helpful (not a long justification), and offer an alternative only if real.
  "Thanks for thinking of me. I can't take this on this quarter. Sam on the data team may be
  a good fit."
- Avoid "maybe later" unless you mean it; it creates a future obligation.

## 6. Follow-ups and threads
- Follow up after a sensible interval (often 3–5 working days), **replying in the same thread**
  with a one-line restatement of the ask, so they don't have to scroll.
- **Reply-all** only when everyone needs the reply; move side topics off-thread.
- Use **Bcc** for large lists of people who don't know each other (privacy).
- Summarise long threads before adding someone new: "Context in 3 lines: …".

## Gotchas
- Attachments mentioned but not attached — many mail clients warn; read the warning.
- Auto-complete picks the wrong recipient; check the To line before sending sensitive mail.
- Humour and sarcasm misfire across cultures and in translation.
- Email is a record: write as though it may be forwarded.

## Verify
- [ ] Subject states the topic and the action/date.
- [ ] Ask and deadline are in the first two lines.
- [ ] Every request can be answered with one reply.
- [ ] Re-read once "as the stressed recipient": no sentence stings.
- [ ] Recipients, attachments and permissions checked before sending.
