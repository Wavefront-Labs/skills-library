# Self-editing in passes: structure, sentences and proofreading

**Goal:** turn a draft into clean, tight prose without a second person — by checking one
kind of problem at a time, largest first, and using tricks that make your own text look
unfamiliar. (Professional proofreading of typeset pages with standard marks is a separate
skill under media-communication publishing.)

## 0. Get distance
Your brain reads what you meant, not what you wrote. Before editing: leave it overnight (or
at least an hour), then change its look — print it, change the font and width, or read it on
another device.

## 1. Structural pass (the big things)
- Write the **purpose in one sentence**. Does the text deliver it? Is the main point in the
  first paragraph (or where the genre expects it)?
- **Reverse outline:** one line per paragraph saying what it does. Move, merge or cut to fix
  order, repetition and gaps.
- Cut whole sections that don't serve the reader. This saves more words than any
  sentence-level edit — do it before polishing sentences you may delete.

## 2. Paragraph pass
- Each paragraph: one job, stated early.
- Transitions show the logic (*so, however, for example*); a missing link usually means a
  missing step in the argument.
- Break paragraphs over ~150–200 words in everyday writing.

## 3. Sentence pass (tightening)
- **Cut throat-clearing:** "It is important to note that", "In order to" → "To",
  "due to the fact that" → "because".
- **Un-bury verbs** (nominalisations): "make an assessment of" → "assess".
- **Prefer active voice** where the actor matters: "The committee rejected it" not "It was
  rejected". Keep passive when the actor is unknown or irrelevant.
- **Split long sentences** at "and", "which" or a semicolon if they exceed ~30 words.
- **Hunt your habits:** everyone overuses something ("just", "really", "very", "actually",
  "in terms of"). Search for them and delete most.
- **Read aloud.** Stumbles and breath shortages mark sentences to rewrite.

## 4. Proofreading pass (accuracy)
Do this last, and slowly, ideally in several mini-passes:
- **Text-to-speech:** let the computer read it while you follow — it doesn't skip missing or
  doubled words.
- **Targeted searches** for your known errors: `its`/`it's`, `their/there/they're`,
  `affect/effect`, double spaces, "the the".
- **Numbers, names and dates** checked against the source; totals add up.
- **Consistency:** spelling variety (UK/US), capitalisation of headings, serial comma, number
  style. Keep a short **style sheet** of decisions for long documents.
- **Links and attachments** actually work.
- Read **backwards sentence by sentence** for typos in short, high-stakes text (a CV, a sign).
- Spellcheck and grammar tools catch some errors and miss correctly spelled wrong words
  ("form" for "from", "manger" for "manager"). Use them, but don't stop there.

## Gotchas
- Editing sentences before the structure is settled wastes time on text you'll cut.
- Doing all passes at once: you'll catch fewer errors of every kind.
- Last-minute edits introduce new errors — proofread the edited area again.
- Headings, captions and the first line are where typos survive longest; check them separately.

## Verify
- [ ] Purpose sentence written; reverse outline shows every paragraph serving it.
- [ ] Draft is shorter after the sentence pass (often 10–20%).
- [ ] Read aloud once; listened once with text-to-speech.
- [ ] Targeted searches for personal error patterns run.
- [ ] Names, numbers, dates and links verified against sources.
