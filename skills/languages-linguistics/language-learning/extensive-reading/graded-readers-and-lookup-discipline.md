# Graded readers and lookup discipline

**Goal:** read a lot, fast and with pleasure, because volume is what grows vocabulary and
reading fluency — and stop reading the way most learners do, i.e. slowly decoding hard
texts with a dictionary on every line.

## 1. Two kinds of reading — keep them apart
| | Extensive | Intensive |
|---|---|---|
| Level | Easy: ~98% of words known | Hard: ~90–95% |
| Speed | Fast, no stopping | Slow, analytic |
| Lookups | Rare (see rules below) | Many, plus grammar notes |
| Volume | Most of your reading time | Short passages |
| Purpose | Fluency, recycling, incidental learning | Learning new items |
Most learners only do intensive reading. A useful split is roughly 3:1 extensive to intensive.

## 2. Finding your level
- Take a page; count unknown words (not names). 1–2 per page of ~250 words ≈ extensive
  level; 5+ ≈ intensive only.
- **Graded readers** are written within a fixed headword count (e.g. 400–3,000 headwords)
  and labelled by level. Major publishers' scales don't line up exactly; use the page test,
  not the label. Read several at one level, then move up when they feel trivial.
- After graded readers: young-adult fiction, familiar genres, translations of books you have
  already read in your own language, series (same characters and vocabulary).

## 3. Lookup discipline (extensive mode)
Look a word up only if: it appears repeatedly **and** you can't follow the plot without it.
Otherwise guess from context and continue. Tools that make lookups nearly free
(pop-up dictionaries like Yomitan for Japanese, built-in e-reader dictionaries, LingQ/Readlang-
type readers) are useful but tempt over-lookup — set a ceiling, e.g. 5 lookups per chapter.
Mark instead of looking up; review marked words after the session.

## 4. Narrow reading
Read many texts on **one topic or by one author** in a row. Topic vocabulary repeats, so
each new text is easier and words get the 10+ encounters they need to stick. The same works
with news: follow one ongoing story across sources.

## 5. Tracking
Log pages (or words) per week and reading speed. Speed on easy material is a good fluency
measure: count words read in 5 minutes with full understanding. Native adult silent
reading is often cited around 200–300 wpm for English; L2 readers start far lower.

## Gotchas
- **Dropping books is fine.** Abandoning a boring or too-hard book is the method working.
- **Classics are a trap** early on: archaic vocabulary and long sentences.
- **Parallel texts** (L1 alongside) tempt you to read only the L1 column; cover it.
- **Logographic scripts** (Chinese, Japanese) need character knowledge on top of vocabulary;
  graded readers with furigana/pinyin support help, but wean off the support layer.
- Reading silently does not train pronunciation; add audio versions where available.

## Verify
- [ ] Your extensive book passes the page test (≤2 unknowns per ~250 words).
- [ ] You read at least as many pages extensively as intensively each week.
- [ ] Lookups per chapter stay under your ceiling.
- [ ] Reading speed on easy material has risen over the last two months.
- [ ] Words met in narrow reading turn up as recognised without a card ever being made.
