# Conversation practice and circumlocution

**Goal:** keep talking smoothly in the target language at your current level — fewer long
pauses, fewer abandoned sentences — and get around gaps instead of stopping at them.
Fluency here means speed and flow with language you *already know*; accuracy and range are
trained elsewhere.

## 1. Survival toolkit: learn these first (verbatim)
- **Stalling/fillers**: the target language's "well…", "let me think", "how do you say…".
- **Repair**: "sorry, again please?", "slower please", "do you mean…?", "what's the word for…?"
- **Circumlocution frames**: "it's a thing you use for ___", "it's like a ___ but ___",
  "the opposite of ___", "a person who ___", "the place where ___".
Drill them until automatic — they are your bridge over every gap.

## 2. Circumlocution in practice
When a word is missing, don't switch language or freeze. Describe:
category + function + feature. "Ladder" → "a thing, wood or metal, you climb to go up
to the roof." Then ask: "what's that called?" — and write the word down afterwards.
Practise deliberately: pick 10 random objects a day and define each without naming it.

## 3. Structuring tutor / exchange sessions
- **Tutor (paid)**: say up front what you want — "mostly me talking, correct only past
  tenses, write corrections in chat for me to review later". Otherwise many tutors talk too much
  or correct everything.
- **Language exchange (tandem)**: split time strictly (e.g. 30 min each language, timer on).
  Exchanges drift into the stronger shared language without a rule.
- Prepare 2–3 topics with key vocabulary beforehand. Topic preparation is not cheating; it
  is how fluency is built.
- Record (with permission) and re-listen once; note 3 things to fix.

## 4. Fluency drills
- **4/3/2**: tell the same story/explanation for 4 minutes, then 3, then 2 (to a partner or
  a recorder). Repetition under time pressure pushes speed and chunking.
- **Self-talk**: narrate what you're doing in the target language during routine tasks.
- **Retelling**: summarise an episode/article you consumed aloud, 2 minutes, no notes.
- **Shadow then speak**: shadow a clip, then answer a question about it in your own words.

## 5. Measuring fluency
On a monthly 2-minute recording on a familiar topic, measure:
- words per minute (count / 2);
- number of pauses over ~1 second;
- number of abandoned or restarted sentences.
Rising speed and falling long pauses on the same kind of task = progress.

## Gotchas
- **Waiting until "ready"**: speaking skill doesn't develop by input alone; start early with
  scripted topics.
- **Over-reliance on one partner**: you learn their speech patterns and they adapt to yours;
  rotate partners so you face unfamiliar speakers.
- **Anxiety**: start with written chat or voice notes, then audio calls, then video/face to face.
- **Translating in your head** from L1 slows everything; use chunks you've heard rather than
  building sentences word by word.
- Corrections given mid-sentence break flow; ask for them at the end or in chat.

## Verify
- [ ] You can use each survival phrase without hesitation in a role-play.
- [ ] Given 10 unknown object names, you describe each recognisably within ~20 seconds.
- [ ] Monthly recording shows higher words/minute and fewer >1 s pauses than the previous one.
- [ ] Sessions have a stated focus and you leave with a written list of corrections.
- [ ] You've held a 15-minute conversation with someone new without switching language.
