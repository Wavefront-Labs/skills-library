# Building a home immersion routine

**Goal:** get many hours of target-language input and use per week without moving
abroad — and, if you do move, actually get them. Volume of *understood* input is the
engine; immersion is just how you make that volume happen by default.

## 1. Environment switch (one afternoon)
- Phone, computer and streaming-service interface language → target language. Do the phone
  last if you're a beginner; recovery from a misread settings menu can be painful.
  Note how to switch back first.
- Streaming: set audio and subtitles to the target language per profile; create a separate
  profile so recommendations become TL content.
- Follow TL creators on the platforms you already scroll. Replace, don't add.
- Browser extensions that show dual subtitles exist; useful early, but turn the L1 line off
  as soon as you can.

## 2. Active vs passive input
- **Active**: full attention, content you understand (>~80–90%). This drives acquisition.
- **Passive**: background audio during chores/commute, ideally of content you've already
  watched actively (re-listening). Unknown content as background is mostly noise for
  beginners.
Rule of thumb: build the habit on active time first; passive time is a top-up.

## 3. Domain sequencing
Start where context carries meaning and vocabulary repeats:
1. Content you know in L1 (rewatch a favourite series dubbed/original).
2. Slice-of-life and children's/teen content; vloggers with a narrow topic.
3. Your hobby domain (sports, cooking, games) — strong motivation and repetitive vocabulary.
4. Dramas, sitcoms, podcasts with two hosts.
5. News, documentaries, then fast unscripted talk, comedy and regional varieties last.
Stay in a domain until it feels easy, then add the next.

## 4. Logging
Keep a simple log: date, minutes, active/passive, source. Weekly totals reveal what's real.
Practitioner communities often quote hundreds to low thousands of hours for comfortable
comprehension; *these numbers vary hugely with language distance and are not research
findings — use the log for consistency, not as a scoreboard.*

## 5. Output and contact
Immersion by input alone delays speaking. Add: weekly conversation sessions, writing a short
daily journal, and TL comments on content you watch. Correct outputs with a tutor or
native-speaker feedback platforms.

## 6. Abroad: avoiding the bubble
Living in the country does not guarantee immersion. Common failures: English-speaking
workplace, expat friend group, everyone switching to English when you hesitate. Counter it:
join TL-only activities (clubs, volunteering, classes in a hobby), say up front "I'd like to
practise — can we stay in [language]?", choose a homestay or TL-speaking housemates.

## Gotchas
- **Too hard too soon**: hours of incomprehensible native TV feel like immersion and teach little.
- **Content guilt**: entertainment you enjoy beats "worthy" content you abandon.
- **Burnout**: going 100% TL overnight often collapses after a few weeks; ramp up.
- **Script languages**: TL subtitles in a script you can't read yet add nothing; learn the
  script/kana first.
- Watching only one genre leaves big vocabulary gaps (e.g. great at crime-drama, lost at the bank).

## Verify
- [ ] Devices/media are switched and you know how to switch them back.
- [ ] Your log shows active input on most days, with totals rising or stable.
- [ ] Most of your active content is understood well enough to follow without pausing.
- [ ] You've moved up at least one domain in the past two months.
- [ ] Some output (speaking/writing) happens weekly alongside the input.
