# Transcribing interviews to a style guide

**Goal:** produce a transcript that matches the client's or study's conventions,
is accurate to the audio, and flags every uncertainty honestly.

> Draft. Conventions summarised from common commercial and research transcription
> guidelines; your client's style guide always overrides. Official court records are
> a law-government matter, not covered here.

## 1. Agree the style before you start
Ask (or check the brief) for:
- **Verbatim level:**
  - *True verbatim* — every um, uh, false start, repetition, stutter, and often
    non-verbal sounds `[laughs]`. Used for legal, conversation analysis, some research.
  - *Clean (intelligent) verbatim* — remove fillers, false starts and stutters;
    keep meaning, grammar as spoken (don't "improve" it). Most business and research work.
  - *Edited* — light grammar tidying for publication. Only if asked.
- **Speaker labels:** names, roles (`Interviewer:`/`Participant 3:`) or initials;
  anonymisation rules.
- **Timestamps:** none, every speaker change, or every N seconds/minutes; format
  `[00:12:34]`.
- **Tags:** exact wording for inaudible, unclear, crosstalk, pauses, redactions.
- **Spelling variety** (US/UK) and number style.
- **Deliverable format** (.docx, .txt, with or without header).

## 2. Set up
- Player with **keyboard/foot-pedal control**, rewind-on-pause (2–3 s), variable speed
  (oTranscribe in a browser, Express Scribe, or the transcription view of your editor).
- Closed-back headphones.
- Pre-listen to the first 2–3 minutes: identify speakers, accents, audio problems.
- Build a **glossary** from any briefing material: names, places, jargon.

## 3. The passes
1. **Typing pass** at 70–100% speed: type continuously, don't stop for every doubt —
   mark doubtful spots with a placeholder like `[?? 00:14:02]`.
2. **Research pass:** look up names, terms, places mentioned; resolve placeholders.
3. **Review pass** at normal speed with the transcript in front of you: fix errors,
   check speaker labels, apply style.
Typical effort: 3–4 hours of work per audio hour for clear two-speaker audio, more for
groups, accents or poor audio (rough industry rule of thumb).

## 4. Tagging uncertainty (the honesty rule)
- Inaudible: `[inaudible 00:23:15]` — always timestamped so the reader can check.
- Best guess: `[unclear: payroll? 00:23:40]` — never type a guess as if certain.
- Crosstalk: `[crosstalk]` then transcribe what you can attribute.
- Unknown speaker: `Speaker 3 (unidentified):`.
- Redactions (if required): `[name redacted]`.

## 5. Difficult audio
- Slow to 60–75% speed for fast talk; increase for drawled speech you've already grasped.
- Apply **EQ/noise reduction to a copy** (e.g. a high-pass around 80–100 Hz removes rumble;
  noise reduction in Audacity) — never to the deliverable audio.
- Isolate one channel if speakers were recorded on separate mics.
- Listen to the problem spot **in context** (5–10 s before) rather than on a tight loop.
- Ask the client for speaker lists or prepared notes; don't invent.

## 6. Speaker labelling tips
- Label on every change of speaker, even for one-word interjections.
- Backchannels ("mm-hm", "right") in clean verbatim: often omitted unless they answer
  a question — check the guide.
- Focus groups: note seat positions/voices early; a voice profile list helps.

## Gotchas
- Cleaning up grammar in clean verbatim (changing meaning or the speaker's voice).
- Homophones and auto-correct ("their/there", product names auto-capitalised).
- Inconsistent tags (`[inaudible]`, `(inaudible)`, `[unintelligible]` mixed).
- Timestamps drifting because the player was at a different speed — use the player's
  real-time position.
- Leaving placeholders `[??` in the delivered file.

## Verify
- [ ] Search the file for your placeholder marker: zero hits.
- [ ] Spot-check three random 1-minute segments against audio: no content errors.
- [ ] Every inaudible/unclear tag has a timestamp.
- [ ] Speaker labels and tags exactly match the style guide.
- [ ] Spelling of all names/terms checked against the glossary or research.
