# Deaf-space etiquette — attention, turn-taking and the unwritten rules

**Goal:** be a considerate participant in Deaf-led settings (clubs, events, workplaces,
classes) and avoid the hearing habits that read as rude in a visual culture.

> Draft. Norms vary by country, generation and community; this reflects widely shared
> conventions in ASL- and BSL-using communities. When in doubt, watch what Deaf people
> around you do, and ask.

## 1. Getting someone's attention
Pick the least intrusive method that works:
- **Wave** in their peripheral vision — a small wave if they're near, bigger if far.
- **Tap** on the shoulder or upper arm, firmly but briefly (one or two taps). Not on the
  head, back or face, and not a poke.
- **Table/floor tap:** stamp lightly or tap the table so they feel the vibration
  (works on wooden floors/tables).
- **Light flash:** flick the room lights briefly to get a whole room's attention —
  normal at meetings and events, not for one person.
- **Ask a neighbour** to relay the tap if the person is out of reach.
Don't throw things, wave inches from their face, or shout.

## 2. Eye gaze and turn-taking
- **Eye contact is the channel.** Looking away while someone signs is like covering
  your ears; it reads as not listening. Watch the face, not the hands — hands are read
  in peripheral vision.
- **Backchannel** visibly: nods, facial responses, short signs ("yes", "really?", "wow").
- **Taking a turn:** raise or move your hands slightly into signing position, catch the
  signer's eye; don't start signing at someone who isn't looking.
- In groups, sit in a **circle or horseshoe** so everyone sees everyone. Rearranging
  chairs to improve sightlines is normal and welcome.
- **Lighting:** don't sit with a bright window behind you; your face disappears in silhouette.

## 3. Moving through conversations
- Walking between two signers is acceptable and often preferred to waiting forever — go
  through **quickly**, without stopping, maybe a small "excuse me" sign. Lingering or
  ducking dramatically is more disruptive.
- Don't stand chatting in doorways or block lines of sight.

## 4. Name signs
- Name signs are **given by Deaf people**, usually after they know you. Don't invent your
  own; introduce yourself by fingerspelling your name.
- Name signs may be descriptive (a trait) or initialised (first letter plus location/
  movement); conventions differ between communities.

## 5. Language and voice
- In a Deaf space, **sign**, even if your signing is basic. Speaking with another hearing
  person in front of Deaf people excludes them; if you must speak, sign what you're saying
  or explain afterwards.
- Don't ask "can you lip-read me?" as a substitute for signing or writing. Lip-reading
  catches only part of speech, even for skilled readers.
- If the person writes or uses a phone, follow their lead.
- Terminology: many people identify as **Deaf** (capital D: culturally Deaf, sign-language
  users) vs *deaf* (audiological). Use "Deaf" or "hard of hearing" as the person prefers;
  avoid "hearing impaired" and "deaf and dumb".

## 6. With interpreters present
- Talk **to** the Deaf person, not to the interpreter ("tell her..."). Look at the person.
- Pace yourself; the interpreter is a few seconds behind.
- The interpreter is not your conversation partner, assistant or advocate.

## 7. Directness and information-sharing
Many Deaf communities value directness (comments on appearance, frank questions) and
thorough information-sharing — explaining why you're leaving, rather than slipping out.
Long goodbyes are common. Don't take directness as rudeness.

## Gotchas
- Turning away mid-conversation (to look at a phone, the TV) without signing "wait".
- Over-exaggerated slow mouthing — distorts lip patterns.
- Touching hands to stop someone signing.
- Assuming one Deaf person can speak for the whole community.

## Verify
- [ ] You got attention with a wave or tap, not voice.
- [ ] You maintained eye contact while others signed and gave visible backchannel.
- [ ] Seating gave everyone clear sightlines; no one backlit.
- [ ] No spoken side-conversations in Deaf company.
- [ ] You fingerspelled your name and didn't claim a self-made name sign.
