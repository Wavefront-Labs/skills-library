# Remote interpreting — setup, audio discipline and turns at a distance

**Goal:** interpret over a platform or phone line with the same accuracy as on site,
despite worse sound, no shared booth and no body language — without damaging your
hearing.

## 1. The three formats
- **RSI** (remote simultaneous): conferences on Zoom's interpretation feature or
  dedicated platforms (KUDO, Interprefy, Interactio, and others — check current names).
- **VRI** (video remote, mostly consecutive/dialogue): healthcare, courts, sign language.
- **OPI** (over-the-phone): short dialogue calls, often unscheduled, no visuals at all.

## 2. Home-booth setup (do this once, test before every job)
- **Wired Ethernet**, not Wi-Fi. A backup connection (phone hotspot) ready.
- **Wired USB headset with a boom microphone** — not earbuds, not the laptop mic, not
  Bluetooth (latency and dropouts). Many standards bodies and platforms list approved
  headset requirements; check them.
- A **quiet room**: soft furnishings to cut echo, door shut, notifications off, pets out.
- Second screen for the glossary/chat; the platform on the main screen.
- Power: laptop plugged in; ideally a UPS for the router.
- Close VPNs, backups and cloud sync that compete for bandwidth.

## 3. Protecting your hearing
Remote audio is often compressed and inconsistent; **acoustic shock** (sudden loud
noise in the headset) and long-term fatigue are documented risks for interpreters.
- Keep headset volume as low as workable; don't compensate for bad sound by turning it up.
- Use a headset or sound limiter that caps peaks where possible.
- If the speaker's sound is unusable (no headset, echo, cutting out), **say so** via the
  technician or moderator. Interpreting from bad sound is how errors and injuries happen.
  AIIC guidance supports refusing to continue under unsafe sound — check your contract.

## 4. RSI with a virtual boothmate
- Agree a **back channel** before the meeting (platform chat, a separate messaging app
  or a call) for numbers, terms and handover cues.
- Handover: say "ready" in the back channel ~1 minute before; outgoing interpreter
  finishes a sentence; incoming unmutes *just* as outgoing mutes. Some platforms have a
  handover button — use it rather than both being live.
- Know where the **relay** channel and the **floor** are, and how to switch your output
  language (retour) — practise on the platform's test room.
- Turns are often shorter remotely; RSI is commonly reported as more tiring than booth work.

## 5. VRI and OPI turn management
Nobody can see you raise a hand, so:
- Open with a clear introduction including your interpreter ID if required.
- Ask the provider to **speak in short segments** and to address the other person
  directly.
- Use a verbal interrupt: "Interpreter speaking — may I interpret that part?"
- On phone: confirm who is on the line; ask for names before starting; flag background
  noise or a speakerphone.
- On video: ask that the camera shows the faces of both parties where possible.
- Long silences: say "Interpreter: still listening" so parties don't think the line dropped.

## 6. When the tech fails
- Have the client's emergency contact (phone/email) before you start.
- If you drop: rejoin, then state in the meeting what was missed.
- Keep a written log of failures and timing — useful for billing and for the
  organiser's report.

## Verify
- [ ] Wired network, wired USB boom-mic headset, quiet room tested on the platform.
- [ ] Headset volume low; peak protection in place; bad sound reported, not tolerated.
- [ ] Back channel and handover signals agreed with your boothmate.
- [ ] You can switch relay and output channels without looking it up.
- [ ] Verbal turn cues used; parties know you are there.

*Uncertainty:* platform features and names change often; ISO/AIIC requirements should
be checked against current editions.
