# Ground-to-air symbols, signal mirrors and smoke for rescue

**Goal:** make yourself visible and understood to searchers when you have no working
radio or phone — and read their response. Visibility is everything: searchers fly over
people who don't stand out.

## 1. The rule of three and contrast
- **Three of anything** — fires, whistle blasts, gunshots, mirror flashes, piles of rock —
  in a line or triangle is a widely recognised distress signal.
- Everything below depends on **contrast**: colour against background, straight lines and
  right angles (nature rarely makes them), and **movement**.

## 2. Ground-to-air symbols (ICAO/IAMSAR code)
| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **V** | Require assistance |
| **X** | Require medical assistance |
| **N** | No / negative |
| **Y** | Yes / affirmative |
| **↑** (arrow) | Proceeding in this direction |
Also lay **SOS** if you don't remember the code. Make symbols:
- **Large:** at least ~2.5 m (8 ft) long — bigger is better (aim for 3–6 m where you can).
- **Contrasting:** dark branches on snow, stamped trenches with shadows, light rocks on dark
  ground, a space blanket or bright clothing.
- **In the open**, away from tree cover and on the clearest flat ground.
- Raised or trenched so they cast **shadows**, which outline them from the air.

## 3. Body signals
- **Both arms raised in a Y** = need help / pick us up.
- **One arm up, one arm down** (diagonal) = all OK / don't need help. Don't wave cheerfully
  — waving can be read as "we're fine".
- Lie down in the shape of the symbol only if injured and nothing else is possible.

## 4. Signal mirror
A mirror flash is visible far beyond where you can see the aircraft (survival manuals cite
tens of kilometres in good sun; treat as optimistic).
1. Hold the mirror near your eye; extend the other arm and make a **V with two fingers**
   around the target (aircraft, boat, ridge).
2. Tilt the mirror until the **reflected spot of light** falls on your fingers, then
   wiggle slightly so the flash sweeps across the target.
3. Mirrors with a sighting hole: look through the hole and put the bright aim-dot on the target.
4. **Sweep the horizon** regularly even when nothing is in sight — aircraft spot you first.
Stop flashing once acknowledged: continuous flashing can dazzle pilots.

## 5. Smoke and fire
- Prepare **three fires** in a triangle, ready to light (dry tinder protected) — searchers
  may pass in minutes.
- Smoke must contrast with the **background**: **white smoke** (add green leaves, damp moss)
  for dark forest; **dark smoke** (rubber, oil — sparingly) for snow or desert.
- At night, flame is the signal; by day, smoke.
- Control fire risk: clear around the fire site, never in dry grassland or under canopy.

## 6. Reading the aircraft
- **Rocking wings** (by day) or flashing lights by night: message seen and understood.
- **A full right-hand circle** (some codes): message not understood.
A low pass or dropped supplies means you're located — stay put unless directed.

## Gotchas
- Leaving your signal site to "find help" makes you harder to find; most rescues happen where people stayed.
- Fluorescent clothing hung in trees is invisible from above; spread it on open ground.
- Wind disperses smoke flat — build bigger, and light it when aircraft are heard.
- Some symbols vary between older references; V, X, N, Y and the arrow are the stable core.

- [ ] A practice symbol is ≥ 2.5 m, in the open, and visible from a high viewpoint.
- [ ] You can put a mirror flash onto a distant target using the V-finger method.
- [ ] You know which smoke colour to make for your current terrain.
