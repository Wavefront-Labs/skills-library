# Tactile tracking and building braille reading speed

**Goal:** read contracted (grade 2) braille by touch smoothly enough that the meaning,
not the decoding, holds your attention — and keep improving speed with deliberate drills.

## 1. The cell and the touch
- A braille cell is two columns of three dots: **dots 1-2-3** down the left, **4-5-6**
  down the right. Characters are named by their dots (`a` = 1, `b` = 1-2, `l` = 1-2-3).
- Read with the **pads** of the index fingers (the most sensitive area, just behind the
  tip), fingers slightly curved, wrists relaxed and roughly level.
- Use **light, even pressure.** Pressing hard flattens sensitivity and, on paper braille,
  flattens the dots themselves. If you can feel the paper's grain more than the dots,
  lighten up.
- Move **horizontally in one steady glide.** The classic fault is *scrubbing* — rubbing up
  and down or back and forth over a cell. It feels like it helps; it actually halves speed.
  If a character is unclear, keep going and let the word context resolve it; re-read the
  word only if the sentence then fails to make sense.

## 2. Two-handed tracking
Efficient readers share the line between hands:
1. Both index fingers start together at the left margin.
2. Around mid-line the **right hand continues reading** while the **left hand drops down to
   find the start of the next line**.
3. As the right finishes the line, the left is already reading the next one — the
   **return sweep** costs almost nothing.
Keep the other fingers resting lightly on the line to stay oriented. Beginners often
read with one finger; moving to two hands is the single biggest early speed gain, but
expect a temporary dip of a week or two while it feels awkward.

## 3. Reading contracted braille in context
- UEB grade 2 uses roughly 180 contractions and short-forms. Recognise them as **word
  shapes**, not letter-by-letter puzzles: `k` alone = *knowledge*, `p` alone = *people*,
  `abv` = *above*.
- Watch the **context rules**: the same cell can be a whole-word contraction standing
  alone and a groupsign inside a word (e.g. dots 1-2-3-4-6 is *and* alone or within words).
- Lower-cell signs (dots 2-3-5-6 only) are the easiest to misread because they sit low in
  the cell — slow down on first meeting a text dense in *en, in, be, con, dis*.
- Readers who learned the older EBAE code: UEB dropped contractions such as *ble, com,
  dd, to, into, by* as separate signs and changed several symbols. Expect a short
  re-adjustment period.

## 4. Building speed
- **Measure:** time a 1-minute read of unfamiliar, age-appropriate text; words read ÷ 1 =
  wpm. Record weekly. Rough reference points (vary a lot between studies and readers —
  treat as uncertain): many adult contracted-braille readers sit around 90–120 wpm; skilled
  readers exceed 150.
- **Repeated reading:** read the same short passage 3–4 times, timing each; then a new one.
- **Daily volume beats intensity:** 15–20 minutes of easy material daily outperforms a weekly
  hour of hard material.
- **Phrase reading:** practise reading aloud in phrase chunks to stop sub-vocalising letter by letter.
- **Mix media:** paper braille and refreshable displays feel different (display pins are
  firmer and evenly spaced); practise on whichever you'll actually use.

## Gotchas
- Cold or dry fingers lose sensitivity — warm hands first; hand cream immediately before
  reading leaves a film that dulls dots.
- Heavily used paper and thermoform copies have worn dots; a slow read may be the page.
- Fatigue sets in fast for learners: stop before accuracy drops rather than practising errors.
- Calluses from manual work reduce sensitivity; some readers switch to a less-used finger.

## Verify
- [ ] Hands glide left-to-right without scrubbing on a recorded reading.
- [ ] The left hand finds the next line before the right finishes the current one.
- [ ] You can name 20 random whole-word contractions on sight within 2 s each.
- [ ] Weekly 1-minute wpm is logged and trending upward over a month.
- [ ] Comprehension check: you can retell a passage read at your target speed.
