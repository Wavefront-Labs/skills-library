# Press pools, credentials and hostile-environment planning

**Goal:** you're accredited, positioned, insured and able to file — and someone knows
where you are and what to do if you don't check in.

## 1. Credentials and accreditation
- Event accreditation (summits, sport, elections, royal events) often closes **weeks**
  before; applications usually need an outlet letter or assignment proof, ID and
  sometimes security vetting. Freelancers: get a commissioning letter.
- Press cards (national union or police-recognised schemes, where they exist) help at
  cordons but grant no special legal rights in most places. *Schemes differ by country.*
- Foreign assignments: correct **journalist visa** where required — working on a tourist
  visa can mean detention or deportation. Check equipment import rules (drones, sat
  phones, body armour may be restricted or illegal).

## 2. Press pools
When access is limited (a head of state's office, a courtroom, a military aircraft),
organisers admit a **pool**: a few photographers who must share their pictures with all
member outlets.
- Know the pool rules: who distributes, how fast, credit format ("POOL/Agency"),
  embargoes, restricted angles.
- Pool duty means shooting the **record** first (the handshake, the signing) before
  anything interpretive.
- File to the pool distribution immediately; your own outlet gets it at the same time
  as everyone else.

## 3. Embeds and fixers
- **Embeds** (with military, police, aid groups): read the embed agreement — it may
  restrict what you can photograph (casualties, faces, positions) and when you can
  publish. Decide in advance with your editor whether the terms are acceptable.
- **Fixers/local producers** make the work possible and carry real risk; their
  exposure lasts after you leave. Pay fairly and promptly, credit them if they want,
  include them in the risk plan and insurance, and never pressure them into danger.

## 4. Hostile-environment plan
Before deploying to conflict, unrest or disaster zones:
1. **Training:** hostile-environment and first-aid training (HEAT/HEFAT) before first
   deployment; refresh periodically.
2. **Risk assessment:** threats (crossfire, kidnap, arrest, crowds, disease, road
   travel, which is often the biggest killer), mitigations, and the point at which you
   withdraw. Many outlets require a written one; freelancers should write one anyway.
3. **Insurance** that explicitly covers war/conflict zones, medical evacuation and
   repatriation — standard travel insurance usually excludes them.
4. **Communications and check-ins:** agreed times and channels with a safety contact
   at home; what happens after a missed check-in (wait X minutes, call fixer, call
   embassy/employer).
5. **Proof-of-life** questions and next-of-kin details lodged with your contact.
6. **Kit:** trauma kit (tourniquet you're trained on), protective equipment
   appropriate to the threat, cash in small notes, copies of documents.
7. **Digital security:** minimal data on devices crossing checkpoints; encrypted
   messaging; consider what your photos reveal about sources.

## 5. Filing from the field
- Set up outlet FTP/upload credentials and test them **before** you leave.
- Bandwidth plan: local SIM, hotel Wi-Fi, satellite terminal (BGAN or a consumer LEO
  service where legal); downsize JPEGs for first send, follow with full resolution.
- File small sets early; wires value the first usable frame.

## Gotchas
- Satellite devices can be illegal or draw suspicion in some countries.
- Credential lanyards visible in a crowd can make you a target; tuck them away.
- *Uncertain:* legal and security specifics vary per country and change fast;
  consult current CPJ/RSF advisories and your outlet's security team.

## Verify
- [ ] Accreditation/visa in hand; equipment rules checked.
- [ ] Pool rules (distribution, credit, embargo) known before the event.
- [ ] Written risk assessment with withdrawal triggers; insurance covers the zone.
- [ ] Check-in schedule and missed-check-in procedure agreed with a named contact.
- [ ] Filing route tested end to end before deployment.
