# Links, backtiming and self-op show driving

**Goal:** a live show that sounds effortless — every link purposeful, every hard post
hit to the second, no dead air — with you operating the desk alone.

## 1. The clock
Most music and talk stations run a **format (hot) clock**: fixed positions each hour for
news, ads, idents, features and songs. Before the show, know your **hard posts**
(news at :00 is the classic — it does not wait for you) and soft posts (features you
can shift).

## 2. Writing and doing links
- **One idea per link.** Tease, info, or personality — not all three.
- Structure: hook → content → forward promote/next element. "Coming up after this…"
  gives listeners a reason to stay.
- **Write the first and last line; ad-lib the middle.** The ending is where links drift.
- Length: on music stations typically short (often 15-45 seconds — station-dependent);
  on talk, as long as it stays interesting.
- Talk to one listener ("you"), not "everyone out there".
- **Back-announce** usefully: title and artist, or not at all if the format omits it.

## 3. Talking up to the vocal
Playout systems (e.g. Myriad, Zetta, WideOrbit, or similar) show each song's **intro
time** (to the vocal) and **out/ending** type. Start talking when the intro starts; aim
to finish on the last beat before the vocal ("hitting the post"). Never talk over vocals.
Know the end type: a **cold** ending needs your next element fired immediately; a **fade**
lets you segue.

## 4. Backtiming to a hard post
To hit the news at exactly :00:
1. Total the remaining items from now to the post (songs, ads, links).
2. Compare to time remaining. Many playout systems display this as an over/under figure.
3. **Under (gap):** add a link, a short song or a station ident, or let a song play to its
   full ending.
4. **Over:** drop a song, edit a link, or **fade** the last song early under your voice
   or the news jingle.
5. A **backtimed bed** (instrumental started at a calculated time so it ends exactly on
   the post) is a classic tool: start it at post time minus its duration, fader down, and
   bring it up under your last link.

Rule: lock the last 3-5 minutes before a hard post early, not in the final 30 seconds.

## 5. Driving the desk
- **Pre-fade listen (PFL/cue)** every source before it goes live — phone lines, guest
  mics, the next item if it's not from playout.
- Keep levels peaking consistently on the station's meter; voice usually needs riding up
  and down slightly as you lean in.
- Mic off when not talking; **assume every open mic is live**.
- Have an **emergency item** ready (a long song or a pre-recorded segment) that you can
  fire instantly if something fails.
- Know the station's **silence detector** behaviour: it may switch to automated backup
  after some seconds of dead air.

## 6. Phone-ins
- A producer or call screener notes caller name, location and point. Self-op: screen
  yourself during songs.
- Run through a **profanity delay** (commonly several seconds) and know the **dump**
  button: it removes the last chunk of audio from air. Check after dumping that the delay
  rebuilds before relying on it again.
- Open with a clear question; end calls warmly but firmly ("Thanks, Sam") and fade the line.
- Don't let callers defame named people — interrupt, clarify on air.

## Verify
- [ ] Hard posts in the hour written down before air.
- [ ] Intros talked up without crashing vocals.
- [ ] News/post hit within ~1-2 seconds.
- [ ] Every new source PFL'd before going live.
- [ ] Emergency item loaded and known.
- [ ] Profanity delay active and dump tested during phone-ins.
