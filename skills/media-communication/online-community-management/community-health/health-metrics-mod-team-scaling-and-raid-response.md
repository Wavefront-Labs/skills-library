# Health metrics, mod-team scaling and raid response

**Goal:** know whether the community is getting healthier or sicker, grow the people who
run it before they burn out, and survive the two shocks every community meets eventually —
internal splits and external raids.

## 1. A small health dashboard (monthly)
Member count is vanity. Track:
- **Active contributors** — members who posted/replied in the period (not just joined).
- **New-member activation** — % of joiners who post within their first 7–30 days.
- **Retention** — % of last month's contributors who contributed again this month.
- **Time to first response** on questions/new posts; and % of posts with no reply.
- **Contributor concentration** — share of content from the top 10 members (high and
  rising = fragile).
- **Moderation load** — reports per 1,000 messages, actions taken, appeals overturned.
- **Sentiment sample** — read 30 random threads a month; numbers miss tone.
Most platforms give basic insights (e.g. Discord Server Insights for eligible servers,
Discourse admin reports); spreadsheets fill the gaps. Compare trends, not absolutes.

## 2. Lifecycle stages and what each needs
- **Inception:** founder-driven; personally invite people, answer everything, seed content.
- **Establishment:** rituals, first rules, first volunteer helpers.
- **Maturity:** members create most content; staff curate, program, and develop leaders.
- **Mitosis/decline:** sub-groups want their own spaces; activity may fall. Support healthy
  splits rather than fight them.
(Stage models like the Community Roundtable's or Rich Millington's are frameworks, not laws.)

## 3. Scaling the volunteer mod team
- **Ratio**: there's no universal number; watch queue age and mod burnout. When reports
  sit unanswered for hours in peak time, or the same 2 mods do 80% of actions, recruit.
- **Recruit** from regulars who already help (answer questions, report well, de-escalate),
  not from applicants who most want power. Trial period (e.g. 30 days) with limited
  permissions and a mentor.
- **Handbook + precedent log** (see
  media-communication.online-community-management.moderation-policy.writing-community-guidelines-and-an-enforcement-ladder).
- **Structure** at size: leads, shifts covering time zones, specialist roles (events,
  onboarding, appeals).
- **Burnout prevention**: term limits or sabbaticals, no-guilt stepping down, private
  space to vent, and recognition. Volunteers quit over unresolved internal conflict and
  feeling unsupported by owners more than over workload.
- See also law-government.platform-and-community-governance.volunteer-moderation-leadership.recruiting-leading-and-retaining-a-volunteer-mod-team.

## 4. Managing splits
Disagreements over direction, a beloved mod removed, a paid tier introduced — splits happen.
- Name the disagreement openly; hold a structured consultation (survey, town hall, thread
  with clear rules).
- Explain decisions and reasoning; dissenters accept outcomes better when the process was fair.
- If a group leaves to start its own space, let them go graciously; banning mentions of the
  new community usually backfires.

## 5. Raid response playbook
A raid is a coordinated influx to spam, harass or shock.
1. **Lock down:** raise verification level / require account age, pause invites, enable
   slow mode, restrict posting to established roles, close new threads.
2. **Clean up:** bulk-remove raid content and ban raid accounts (moderation bots and
   built-in tools support mass actions — confirm what your platform offers).
3. **Protect members:** tell them briefly what's happening and not to engage; shield any
   targeted person.
4. **Report** to the platform with IDs and screenshots.
5. **Recover:** reopen gradually; review how they got in (a public invite link, a viral post)
   and fix it.
Write this down *before* the first raid and drill it with the mod team.

## Gotchas
- Celebrating member count while activation and retention fall.
- Mod teams that are all in one time zone.
- One owner holding every admin permission — a single point of failure (or of compromise).

## Verify
- [ ] Dashboard has activation, retention, response time and concentration, trended monthly.
- [ ] Mod recruiting is triggered by queue/burnout signals, with trial and mentor.
- [ ] Mods have a documented way to step back.
- [ ] Raid playbook written, permissions in place, and rehearsed.
- [ ] At least two trusted people hold owner/admin access.
