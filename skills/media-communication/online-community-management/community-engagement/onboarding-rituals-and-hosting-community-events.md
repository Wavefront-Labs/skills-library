# Onboarding, rituals and hosting community events

**Goal:** new members make a first contribution quickly, regulars have reasons to come back
weekly, and the community's best moments are created deliberately rather than by luck.

## 1. Onboarding: get to the first contribution
Most people who join never post. The aim of onboarding is to make the first contribution
easy and rewarded.
- **One clear starting point** — a welcome message or channel that says what the community is
  for, the 3 most important rules, and one thing to do now ("Introduce yourself: what are
  you working on?").
- **Introduction prompt** with a template (name/handle, what brought you, one thing you
  want to learn). Templates lower the effort and produce better intros.
- **Greet every introduction** in the first weeks of a community, and as long as volume
  allows — a human reply within a day noticeably raises the chance people return
  (practitioner experience; I'm not aware of a definitive study).
- **Low-stakes first tasks:** reacting to a poll, posting a photo of their setup, answering
  "what are you working on this week?".
- Hide complexity: new members don't need all 40 channels or subforums on day one.

## 2. Rituals: the weekly rhythm
Recurring, predictable formats do most of the engagement work:
- **Weekly threads**: "What are you working on?", "Win of the week", "Ask anything Monday",
  "Feedback Friday".
- **Showcase/spotlight**: a channel or monthly post featuring members' work; recognition is
  the strongest motivator in volunteer communities.
- **Challenges**: themed monthly prompts with a deadline and a showcase at the end.
- **Office hours** with staff or experts at a fixed time.
Pick 2–3 and run them reliably for months; a ritual that appears irregularly isn't a ritual.

## 3. Hosting an AMA (ask me anything)
1. **Book** the guest 3–4 weeks ahead; agree topic, time (pick the community's peak hours),
   duration (typically 60–90 minutes), format (text thread, voice/stage, video).
2. **Announce** a week ahead and again on the day; open a question-collection thread 2–3
   days before so people in other time zones can take part.
3. **Brief the guest** on the community's norms and level, how the platform works, and that
   some questions will be critical.
4. **During**: a host introduces the guest, a moderator filters/queues questions, removes
   trolling, and keeps a backup list of good questions for quiet moments.
5. **After**: post a summary or transcript, thank the guest publicly, and answer leftover
   questions if they agree.
Same pattern works for workshops, watch-alongs, game nights and live critiques.

## 4. Surfacing members
- Pin or repost excellent contributions (with credit and permission).
- Give regulars small roles: greeter, event co-host, wiki editor — the path to future mods.
- Use member names in announcements ("Thanks to @…, who built the new FAQ").

## 5. Reviving a quiet space
- **Diagnose**: is the purpose unclear, the space too fragmented (too many channels for the
  activity level), or have key regulars left?
- **Consolidate** channels/categories so activity is visible in one place.
- **Seed** conversations yourself for a few weeks with genuine questions; personally invite
  5–10 past active members back with a specific reason.
- **Restart one ritual** and keep it going.
- If nothing works, it's fine to archive the space or merge it.
Expect participation inequality: a small share of members create most content
(often summarised as 90-9-1). Design for lurkers too — they benefit and may later post.

## Gotchas
- Welcome bots with walls of text nobody reads.
- Events scheduled in the organiser's timezone only.
- Over-programming a small community until it feels like a marketing calendar.
- Staff talking more than members.

## Verify
- [ ] A new member can see what to do first within 30 seconds of joining.
- [ ] Introductions get a human reply.
- [ ] 2–3 rituals running on a fixed schedule for the last month.
- [ ] Each AMA has a question-collection thread, a moderator and a post-event summary.
- [ ] Member work is featured at least monthly.
