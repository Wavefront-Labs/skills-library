# Editorial calendar and repurposing

**Goal:** replace "what do we post this week?" panic with a plan: a few clear content
pillars, a cadence you can sustain for a year, repeatable formats, and a habit of
getting 4–6 outputs from each substantial piece.

## 1. Pillars before posts
Write 3–5 **content pillars** — the recurring subjects your audience comes to you for
(e.g. for a local-food publication: restaurant openings, home cooking, growers,
food policy). Every planned piece maps to one pillar. Pieces that fit none are a
warning sign, not a creative opportunity.

## 2. Cadence: pick what survives a bad month
- Decide cadence per channel from **capacity, not ambition**: count hours per piece × pieces
  per week, compare to hours actually available, then cut 20% for sickness and news.
- Consistency beats volume. One reliable weekly newsletter outperforms an erratic daily one
  for habit and deliverability.
- Publish on fixed days/times so readers (and you) build the habit; adjust using
  audience analytics after ~8 weeks of data.

## 3. Formats and series: reduce decisions
Design 3–6 **recurring formats** with a fixed structure, length and production checklist:
"Five-minute explainer", "Long read (monthly)", "Q&A with a practitioner",
"Reader mailbag", "Weekly roundup". Formats make pieces faster to produce, easier to
commission, and recognisable to readers.
A **series** is a bounded run (e.g. 6 parts) on one subject — good for launches and for
building a topic cluster (see
`media-communication.content-creation-and-audience-growth.editorial-seo.search-intent-and-evergreen-refresh`).

## 4. Building the calendar
Tool: a shared sheet, Airtable, Notion or Trello is enough. One row per piece with:
`working title | pillar | format | channel(s) | owner | status | due draft | publish date | peg | repurpose plan | link`
Status column: Idea › Assigned › Drafting › Editing › Scheduled › Published.
Fill it in three layers:
1. **Tentpoles** (fixed dates for the year): seasons, holidays, known events, anniversaries,
   awareness days relevant to your pillars, your own launches.
2. **Pegs** (6–8 weeks out): announced reports, conferences, releases.
3. **Evergreen backlog**: pieces with no date, used to fill gaps and for slow weeks.
Keep a **buffer** of 2–3 finished evergreen pieces at all times. Leave ~20% of slots
open for news and reactive content.

Run a 30-minute weekly planning meeting: review last week's results, confirm next two
weeks, move ideas from backlog. Monthly: look a quarter ahead.

## 5. Repurposing: one piece, many outputs
Start from the deepest piece (long article, interview, video). Plan derivatives *at
commissioning*, so the right assets are captured (quotes, B-roll, photos):
| From a long article | Output |
|---|---|
| Key finding | Social post / thread with a chart |
| Best quote | Quote card |
| Section | Newsletter lead item |
| Q&A section | Short video or audio clip |
| Whole piece, updated | Evergreen refresh in 6–12 months |
Rules:
- **Adapt, don't paste.** Each platform needs its own hook, length and format (see
  `media-communication.content-creation-and-audience-growth.platform-distribution.per-platform-packaging-and-ranking-literacy`).
- Stagger derivatives over days/weeks rather than firing everything on day one.
- Track the derivative back to the source piece in the calendar so you can see total reach.

## Gotchas
- Calendars die when they're only filled by one person. Give each row an owner.
- Planning more pieces than the editing capacity creates a draft graveyard; editing is
  usually the bottleneck, not writing.
- Awareness-day content is crowded; only do it if you have something specific to add.

## Verify
- [ ] Every scheduled piece maps to one pillar and one format.
- [ ] The next 2 weeks are fully scheduled; the next quarter has tentpoles.
- [ ] At least 2 finished evergreen pieces sit in the buffer.
- [ ] Each substantial piece in the calendar has a written repurpose plan.
- [ ] Cadence was kept for the last 8 weeks without all-nighters.
