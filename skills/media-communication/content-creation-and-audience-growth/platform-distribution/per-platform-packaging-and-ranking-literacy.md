# Per-platform packaging and ranking literacy

**Goal:** stop treating feeds as a bulletin board. Understand, in general terms, what
each platform's ranking optimises for, package the same story natively for each, and
survive the next algorithm change without panic.

**Uncertainty note:** platform ranking details are proprietary and change frequently.
Below is what platforms have *publicly said* plus widely observed patterns. Treat
specific tactics as hypotheses to test on your own account.

## 1. What feeds broadly optimise for
All major feeds predict **"will this person engage / keep watching / come back?"** from
early signals and then widen distribution if a post performs with an initial audience.
- **Video (YouTube, TikTok, Reels, Shorts):** watch time and retention, rewatches,
  satisfaction signals (YouTube surveys), shares. The first 1–3 seconds decide most.
- **Text/social (LinkedIn, Facebook, Threads, X, Bluesky):** meaningful comments, shares,
  dwell time; relationship to the poster. Bluesky's default following feed is
  chronological; custom feeds vary.
- **Reddit:** community votes within a subreddit; each subreddit's rules matter more than
  any algorithm. Self-promotion rules are strict.
- **Outbound links:** several platforms have been observed to show posts with external
  links less, since they take people off-platform. *Strength varies and is disputed;
  test it.* A common workaround is a native summary post with the link in the first
  comment or bio — check platform rules, as some now frown on this too.

## 2. Packaging per platform (same story, different wrappers)
| Platform | Package |
|---|---|
| YouTube long-form | Thumbnail + title as one promise; hook in first 15 s; chapters |
| Shorts / Reels / TikTok | Vertical 9:16, burned-in captions (many watch muted), payoff early, ≤60 s often |
| LinkedIn | First 2 lines before "see more" carry the hook; short paragraphs; document/carousel posts |
| Instagram feed | Carousel: slide 1 = hook, last slide = takeaway/CTA; 4:5 crops |
| Threads / X / Bluesky | One sharp sentence; a thread for depth; image or chart helps |
| Facebook | Native video and photos; groups for communities |
| Reddit | Answer the community's question in-post; follow sub rules; disclose affiliation |
General rules: the **hook** states the specific, surprising or useful thing — not "New
article out!". Write native captions; don't paste the article's dek everywhere. Add
alt text and captions (accessibility and muted autoplay).

## 3. Posting strategy
- **Test one variable at a time** (hook style, format, time) over ~10 posts before concluding.
- Post when *your* audience is online (platform analytics show this), not generic "best times".
- Reply to early comments in the first hour; conversation is a positive signal on most text feeds.
- Re-share evergreen work periodically with a new hook — most followers never saw it.
- Don't spray identical posts across 8 platforms at once; pick 2–3 where your audience is
  and do them well.

## 4. Surviving algorithm changes
- **Diversify:** no single platform should be >50% of your traffic. Convert followers to
  owned channels (newsletter, site, podcast feed) — that is the only audience you keep.
- When reach drops: check it's not you first (format change, posting gaps, a policy
  strike/demotion notice), then compare to peers, then read the platform's official
  creator blog. Don't chase rumours.
- Keep a dated change log of your own experiments and known platform changes so you can
  separate cause from noise.

## 5. Ethics and rules
- No engagement bait ("comment YES"), fake engagement or bought followers — platforms
  explicitly demote it, and it corrupts your analytics.
- Label sponsored or affiliated content per platform tools and local ad rules.

## Verify
- [ ] Each platform you use has its own native version of the last big story.
- [ ] Video versions have burned-in captions and a payoff in the first seconds.
- [ ] You can name, from your own data, which format performs best per platform.
- [ ] No platform supplies >50% of traffic, or there's a plan to change that.
- [ ] A change log records experiments and their results.
