# Signup funnels and list health for newsletter growth

**Goal:** grow an email audience that actually reads — when nothing is being sold — by
fixing where people sign up, what happens in their first week, and who stays on the
list. Platform setup and DNS authentication:
`media-communication.content-creation-and-audience-growth.substack.newsletter-platform-setup-and-deliverability`.

## 1. The funnel, measured
`visitors → see a signup prompt → sign up → confirm (if double opt-in) → open welcome → still active at 90 days`
Instrument each step. A typical weak point is that most visitors never *see* a prompt.
Rough benchmarks for editorial sites (*wide variance; use your own baseline*): inline
forms convert well under 1% of pageviews; a dedicated landing page sent warm traffic can
convert 20–40%+.

## 2. Signup placements that work
- **Inline, mid-article** after the reader has got value (after paragraph 4–6), with a
  specific promise: "Every Friday: the week's three biggest stories in 5 minutes."
- **End of article** — the reader just finished; highest-intent moment.
- **A dedicated landing page** you link from social bios, podcast notes and talks.
- **Exit-intent / scroll pop-ups**: they convert, they also annoy. Delay them (e.g. after
  60% scroll), show once per visitor per few weeks, and never on mobile as a full-screen
  interstitial (Google penalises intrusive mobile interstitials).
- Copy rules: say **what, how often, and a sample** ("See last week's issue"). Generic
  "Subscribe to our newsletter" underperforms.
- A **lead magnet** (guide, dataset, archive access) can lift signups but brings people
  who wanted the freebie — watch their 90-day retention separately.

## 3. The welcome sequence (first 7–10 days)
1. **Immediately:** welcome + what to expect + "reply and tell me why you subscribed"
   (replies improve inbox placement and give you audience research).
2. **Day 2–3:** best-of — 3–5 of your strongest past pieces.
3. **Day 5–7:** the "how to get the most from us" email: other newsletters, podcast,
   community, ask them to move you to Primary tab / add to contacts.
Then hand off to the regular issue.

## 4. Reading opens and clicks honestly
- Apple Mail Privacy Protection (since 2021) pre-loads images, so Apple Mail users look
  like they opened everything. Opens are now *inflated and directional only*.
- Use **clicks**, **replies**, and **"active in last 90 days" (any click)** as health metrics.
- Per-issue: compare to your own trailing average, not industry benchmarks.
- Unsubscribe spikes after an issue = content or frequency mismatch; read the reasons.

## 5. List hygiene and sunsetting
- Remove hard bounces immediately (platforms do this automatically).
- **Sunset policy:** subscribers with no clicks in ~120–180 days get a re-engagement
  email ("Still want this? Click to stay"), then are removed if they don't respond.
- A smaller, engaged list delivers better than a big dead one — spam filters judge you
  on engagement and complaints.
- Never add people who didn't ask (event attendees, contacts). In the EU/UK this is
  also a GDPR/PECR issue.

## 6. Cross-promotion
- **Swaps:** a short recommendation in each other's newsletter with a similar-sized
  publication. Match audiences, not just size.
- **Platform recommendation networks** (Substack Recommendations, Beehiiv Boosts,
  Ghost recommendations).
- **Referral programmes** (rewards at 1/3/5 referrals) work best for daily, habit-forming
  newsletters; less so for monthly ones.
- Guest appearances: write a piece or appear on a podcast with a direct signup link.

## Gotchas
- Buying lists, "co-registration" or scraped emails: wrecks deliverability; don't.
- Growth from a giveaway → mass unsubscribes and spam complaints a month later.
- Changing cadence silently (weekly → daily) drives complaints; announce it.

## Verify
- [ ] You know your signup conversion per placement (not just total subscribers).
- [ ] A fresh test signup receives all welcome emails on schedule.
- [ ] Health is reported with clicks/90-day-active, not raw open rate.
- [ ] A written sunset rule exists and was run in the last quarter.
- [ ] Every subscriber source on the list can be explained as an opt-in.
