# Building press lists and sends in media databases

**Goal:** a small, accurate list of the right journalists, a send that looks
individually written, and a record of who responded — using the platform as a
research tool, not a spam cannon.

*Feature names and screens differ between vendors and change often; the workflow
below is the common pattern across Cision, Muck Rack, Roxhill, Meltwater and similar.
Check your platform's help centre for exact steps.*

## 1. Searching for contacts
Search by combinations of:
- **Beat/topic** (e.g. "health policy", "fintech") — vendor-tagged, often broad;
- **Outlet type and tier** (national daily, trade, broadcast, podcast, newsletter);
- **Geography and language**;
- **Role** (reporter, editor, producer, freelancer, columnist);
- **Recent coverage keywords** — the most useful filter; platforms that index
  journalists' articles (Muck Rack, Roxhill and others) let you search "who wrote
  about X in the last 90 days".
Then **read** each candidate's recent pieces before adding them. Vendor beat tags lag
reality; journalists change beats and outlets frequently.

## 2. Building and maintaining lists
- Keep lists **small and purpose-built** (10–40 for most pitches; *rule of thumb*).
  One giant "all media" list produces irrelevance and opt-outs.
- Name lists clearly: `2026-10 Nurse survey - UK health + national news`.
- Add notes per contact: preferences ("no phone calls", "prefers exclusives",
  "embargo-friendly"), past coverage, last interaction.
- Verify emails before big sends: bounce reports, the outlet's site, the journalist's
  social bio. Fix or flag incorrect records with the vendor.
- Remove opt-outs immediately and keep them suppressed across lists.

## 3. Sending
- Use the platform's send tool or your own email — the platform route usually adds
  tracking and logs to contact history.
- **Personalise**: merge fields for first name at minimum; better, a custom first line
  per journalist for the top tier. Send top-tier targets individually, not via merge.
- Plain, readable formatting; text in the body, assets by link (see the press-kits skill).
- Test-send to yourself and check merge fields, links and the embargo line.
- Send in a sensible window for the audience's time zone; avoid blasting late on
  Friday unless news demands it.
- Follow pitching etiquette from
  `media-communication.media-relations-and-press-liaison.media-pitching.writing-and-targeting-a-media-pitch`.

## 4. Tracking and reporting
- Opens are unreliable (privacy features inflate them); treat replies, coverage and
  clicks on assets as the real signals.
- Log responses and outcomes against each contact; tie coverage back to sends via the
  platform's monitoring module or your own tracker.
- After each campaign: who covered, who declined and why, who bounced — update the list.

## 5. Data protection
Journalists' work contact details are personal data in many jurisdictions (e.g. GDPR
in the EU/UK). Commonly relied-on basis: legitimate interest for relevant professional
contact, with a clear opt-out and relevance to their beat. Don't export lists into
unmanaged spreadsheets or share them with clients without a basis. *Not legal advice —
confirm with your data-protection lead.*

## Gotchas
- Duplicate contacts across outlets (freelancers) → double sends; de-duplicate by email.
- Platform contract terms often restrict exporting data or sharing lists outside the
  licence holder.
- Generic newsdesk addresses are fine for breaking news; for features, find the person.

## Verify
- [ ] Every contact on the list has recent relevant coverage you've read.
- [ ] List size fits the story; notes and preferences recorded.
- [ ] Test send checked for merge fields, links, embargo line.
- [ ] Opt-outs suppressed; data-protection basis confirmed.
- [ ] Responses and coverage logged; list cleaned after the campaign.
