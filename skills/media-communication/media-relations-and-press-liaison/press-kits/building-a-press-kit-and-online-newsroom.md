# Building a press kit and online newsroom

**Goal:** a journalist on deadline can get every fact, name, image and contact they
need without emailing you — and everything they take is accurate and cleared for use.

## 1. Core contents
| Item | What it is | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Fact sheet** | One page of key facts: what, who, when, where, numbers | Bullets; dated; sources for figures |
| **Backgrounder** | 1–2 pages of history and context | Neutral tone, no sales language |
| **Bios** | Short (50-word) and long (200-word) versions for each spokesperson | Correct titles; pronunciation if tricky |
| **Boilerplate** | The standard "About X" paragraph | Same text everywhere; reviewed yearly |
| **Latest release(s)** | The news this kit supports | Link to newsroom archive |
| **Images** | Headshots, product/location photos, logos | High-res and web-res; captions; credits |
| **B-roll** | Unedited video footage for broadcasters to cut | No music, natural sound, slate/shot list |
| **Contacts** | Press office name, email, mobile, out-of-hours | Phone answered by a person |
Event-specific kits add: schedule, venue map, accreditation and filming rules.

## 2. Images that get used
- Supply **high resolution** (print typically wants several thousand pixels on the
  long edge; *confirm outlets' requirements*) plus a smaller web version.
- Variety: landscape and portrait orientations, with space for headlines; people doing
  something, not only posed groups.
- **Captions** for every file (who, what, where, when) and **credit line** as it
  must appear.
- **Rights cleared**: you need a licence that allows editorial use by third parties.
  Check photographer contracts and model releases; state the terms ("Free for
  editorial use in coverage of X; no commercial use; credit required").
- Embed IPTC caption/credit/copyright in files so data travels with them.
- Logos as vector (SVG/EPS) and PNG with transparent background, plus brand usage notes.

## 3. B-roll
- 2–5 minutes of usable shots, each held 10+ seconds: wide establishing, mid, close,
  details, interviews (clean audio, subject identified on a slate or in a shot list).
- Common broadcast-friendly formats (e.g. 1080p or 4K, H.264/ProRes as requested);
  offer a download link, not just a streaming video.
- Shot list with timecodes and a caption sheet.

## 4. Online newsroom
A press page on your website, linked from the footer:
- latest news first, searchable archive with dates;
- press contacts at the top;
- media library with filterable, downloadable assets and their usage terms;
- fact sheet, boilerplate, leadership bios;
- RSS or email alerts for subscribers;
- accessible: alt text, text-based releases (not just PDFs), mobile-friendly.
Avoid login walls for basic assets — journalists won't register on deadline.

## 5. Keeping it current
- Owner and review dates: leadership changes, new logos, updated figures. Out-of-date
  bios and numbers are the most common press-kit errors.
- Remove assets you no longer have rights to (licences expire; people leave).
- Archive old releases rather than delete; journalists link to them.

## Gotchas
- Huge zip files emailed unrequested fill inboxes; send links.
- A fact sheet that contradicts the release (different numbers) undermines both — check
  them together.
- Images of minors or patients need specific consent; check before including.
- *Uncertain:* format and resolution expectations vary by outlet; ask your key targets.

## Verify
- [ ] Fact sheet, backgrounder, bios, boilerplate and contacts all dated and consistent.
- [ ] Every image has caption, credit and stated editorial-use terms; rights confirmed.
- [ ] B-roll has a shot list and clean natural sound.
- [ ] Newsroom page loads without login; assets download at full resolution.
- [ ] Review date set; out-of-rights assets removed.
