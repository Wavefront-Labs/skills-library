# Writing and targeting a media pitch

**Goal:** the right journalist reads the pitch in ten seconds, sees a story their
audience wants, and replies — and still opens your emails next time.

## 1. Is it a story?
Test against news values: **new**, **significant** to the outlet's audience, **timely**
(peg to an event, season, data release), **human** (a person affected), **surprising**
or **conflict/tension**, **local** relevance. If you can't write the headline the
journalist would run, there's no pitch yet. "Company launches product" is rarely a
story; "One in three nurses now…, new survey finds" can be.

## 2. Targeting
- Pitch **people, not outlets**. Read the reporter's last 10 pieces; pitch only what
  fits their beat and angle. A media database helps find them (see
  `media-communication.media-relations-and-press-liaison.media-database-platforms.building-press-lists-and-sends-in-media-databases`),
  but always check their recent work yourself.
- Match format: trade press wants detail and data; national news wants significance
  and people; broadcast wants pictures and a talker; podcasts want a guest with a view.
- Respect lead times: monthly magazines plan months ahead; daily news works hours to days.

## 3. Exclusive vs broad offer
- **Exclusive:** one outlet gets the story first (often with interview access) in
  return for better treatment. Offer it to your top target with a reply deadline; if they
  pass, move to the next. Never offer the same exclusive to two people at once.
- **Embargoed broad release:** many outlets get it in advance under embargo for
  simultaneous publication. Get agreement to the embargo **before** sending details.
- **Straight release:** for news that's routine or already public.

## 4. Writing the pitch email
- **Subject line** = the headline, specific: "Data: 1 in 3 nurses plan to quit within
  a year (embargoed 14 Oct)".
- **Body under ~150 words** (*common practice, not a rule*):
  1. One line why this matters to *their* readers (personalised, referencing their
     coverage if genuine).
  2. The news in 2–3 sentences with the key fact/number.
  3. What you can offer: spokesperson, case study, data, images/b-roll, timing.
  4. Contact details incl. a mobile.
- Paste text in the body, link to assets; no attachments on first contact.
- No "just checking you got my press release" framing; no hype words ("revolutionary").

## 5. Follow-up
- One follow-up after 1–3 working days if no reply, adding something new (an extra
  data point, availability of the spokesperson), not "bumping".
- Phone only for genuinely time-sensitive news and with reporters who welcome calls.
- A "no" is information: ask what would be useful in future, then stop.
- Track every pitch: who, when, response.

## 6. When they say yes
Deliver exactly what was offered, fast: spokesperson briefed and available, facts
checked, images cleared. Missing a reporter's deadline does more damage than never
pitching.

## Gotchas
- Mass mail-merged pitches with the wrong name or beat get you blocked.
- "Off the record" must be agreed before you speak, not after.
- Data claims need a methodology note (sample, dates, who ran it).
- Privacy: use only publicly listed work contact details (consider GDPR where it
  applies to journalist data).

## Verify
- [ ] Headline-style news hook written; passes at least two news-value tests.
- [ ] Each target reporter's recent work read and fits the angle.
- [ ] Exclusive/embargo terms agreed before details were shared.
- [ ] Pitch under ~150 words with the key fact, offer and contact.
- [ ] One value-adding follow-up max; every pitch logged.
