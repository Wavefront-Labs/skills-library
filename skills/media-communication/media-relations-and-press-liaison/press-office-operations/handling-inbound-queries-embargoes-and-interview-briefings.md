# Handling inbound queries, embargoes and interview briefings

**Goal:** every journalist gets an accurate answer before their deadline, nothing is
said that wasn't cleared, and spokespeople walk into interviews prepared.

## 1. Taking a query
Log it immediately (a shared tracker or the media platform's inbox) with:
- journalist, outlet, contact, **deadline** (ask — "what time do you need it?"),
- exactly what they asked (copy their questions verbatim),
- what they already have / what the story is,
- format wanted (statement, interview, background, data).
Don't answer substantively on the first call unless it's a simple fact you're sure of.
"Let me get you an accurate answer by 3 pm" is a good reply.

## 2. Triage
- **Routine factual** (opening times, published numbers): answer directly.
- **Comment request**: draft statement → subject expert checks facts → approver signs
  off (named in your sign-off matrix) → send.
- **Sensitive** (legal, safety, personnel, regulatory, crisis signs): escalate to the
  head of comms and legal at once; consider a holding statement.
- **Not ours**: redirect to the right organisation with contact details.
Always reply, even if only to decline comment — and say it clearly so it's quoted
accurately ("X declined to comment" vs "did not respond" are very different lines).

## 3. Statements
- Short (often 2–4 sentences), attributable to a named spokesperson or "a spokesperson
  for X".
- Answer the question asked where possible; never lie, never speculate.
- Keep a statement library for recurring issues, version-dated.
- **Holding statement** for fast-moving events: what you know, what you're doing,
  when you'll update.

## 4. Attribution ground rules
Agree before speaking, every time:
- **On the record** — quotable, attributable to you by name/title.
- **Background / not for attribution** — usable, attributed to e.g. "a company source".
- **Off the record** — not for publication; usually only for guidance. Some
  journalists or outlets don't accept it, and terms are interpreted differently across
  countries — confirm what the reporter understands by it.
Default assumption: everything is on the record.

## 5. Embargoes
- Get the reporter's agreement to the embargo **before** sending material. Put the
  embargo date, time and **time zone** in the subject line and at the top of the
  document.
- Use embargoes only for genuinely scheduled news (results, studies, launches).
- If an embargo breaks: decide quickly whether to lift it for everyone (usually yes),
  notify all recipients, and log who broke it.

## 6. Brokering interviews
Ask the reporter: topic, angle, other people interviewed, format (live/recorded,
length), deadline, photographer/camera. Then prepare a **briefing note** for the
spokesperson:
- journalist profile and recent coverage, the likely angle;
- 3 key messages with proof points;
- likely and hostile questions with answers;
- facts and figures; what not to say (legal limits);
- logistics (time, place, length, who's attending).
Prep call beforehand; practise **bridging** from a difficult question back to a key
message without dodging it entirely. Sit in on (or record) the interview where agreed.

## 7. Out of hours
Run a rota with a phone number on the website; hand over open queries in writing.

## Gotchas
- Spokespeople going "off script" after the formal interview ends — the mic may still be on.
- Asking to approve copy before publication is generally refused; you may offer to
  check facts or technical accuracy.
- *Uncertain:* attribution conventions differ by country and outlet; confirm per reporter.

## Verify
- [ ] Every query logged with verbatim questions and deadline; none missed.
- [ ] Statements approved per the sign-off matrix before sending.
- [ ] Attribution terms agreed before any non-public comment.
- [ ] Embargo agreed in advance with date, time and time zone stated.
- [ ] Spokesperson had a briefing note and prep call before every interview.
