# Vérité shooting, on-camera interviews and releases

**Goal:** come back from the field with scenes that can be edited, sound that's usable,
interviews that are honest and emotional, and paperwork that lets the film be broadcast.

## 1. Vérité: shoot for the edit
- **Sound first.** Audiences forgive shaky pictures, not bad sound. Put radio lavaliers on
  main characters, and keep an on-camera shotgun or boom for everyone else. Record a
  separate backup track where possible; monitor with headphones constantly.
- **Cover scenes, not moments:** for each scene get a wide (establishing geography),
  mediums and close-ups on faces and hands, reaction shots, and cutaways (objects, details)
  that let the editor compress time.
- **Hold shots** — 10 seconds at least before moving; sudden pans and zooms are unusable.
- **Anticipate:** position yourself where the action will go (the door they'll come
  through), not where it was.
- **Long takes** with stable framing beat constant reframing; let things play out.
- Shoot **arrivals and departures**, and **establishing shots** of every location at
  different times of day. Editors always need them.
- **Room tone**: 30–60 seconds of silence in every location.

## 2. On-camera interviews
- **Location:** quiet (switch off fridges, AC — and remember to switch them back on),
  depth behind the subject, meaningful but not distracting background.
- **Eyeline:** subject looks just off-lens at the interviewer sitting beside the camera
  (classic documentary look). Looking straight down the lens (via a teleprompter-style
  rig, sometimes called an Interrotron) creates direct address — a deliberate choice.
- **Light** soft from one side (key) with some fall-off; a small LED panel and a bounce do a lot.
- **Two cameras** (wide and tight) make editing easier but aren't essential.
- **Technique:** start with easy questions; ask open questions ("Tell me about the day…");
  ask them to include the question in their answer ("When the letter arrived, I…") so bites
  stand alone; stay silent after an answer — the best material often follows the pause.
  Don't make "uh-huh" noises over their answers; nod instead.
- Ask the emotional questions once trust is there, usually later in the interview.
- End with "Is there anything I should have asked?"

## 3. Small-crew logistics
- A self-shooting director or a 2–3 person crew (camera, sound, producer/director) is
  standard for vérité. Small crews change behaviour less.
- **Daily checklist:** batteries charged, cards formatted *after* verified backup, spare
  lav mics, gaffer tape, release forms, location permits, first-aid kit.
- **Data wrangling every night:** two copies on separate drives, checksum-verified copy
  tools where possible, a log of cards and drives.
- **Risk assessment** for hazardous locations (crowds, protests, hostile environments,
  remote areas); many broadcasters and insurers require it.

## 4. Releases and consent
- **Appearance/contributor release** for anyone featured: name, signature, date, what
  they're consenting to (the film and its promotion, all media, worldwide, in perpetuity is
  common in broadcaster templates). Get it signed before or at filming — chasing afterwards
  is painful.
- **Minors:** parent/guardian consent, plus the child's own assent; follow broadcaster rules.
- **Location releases** for private property; **filming permits** for public locations
  where required (rules vary by city and country).
- **Filming in public:** in many jurisdictions incidental passers-by in public don't need
  releases, but rules differ (some countries have stronger image rights — e.g. France,
  Quebec). If unsure, blur or get consent. Check with the commissioner's legal team.
- **Verbal on-camera consent** ("Do you agree to be filmed for this documentary?")
  recorded at the start is a useful backup, not a replacement where a written release is
  required.

## Gotchas
- Gorgeous pictures with a camera mic recording wind.
- Filming the climax with no wide shot to establish where we are.
- Formatting a card before checking the backup copy.
- Promises made on location ("we won't use that") that nobody wrote down.

## Verify
- [ ] Every main character mic'd; headphones monitored; room tone recorded.
- [ ] Each scene has wide, medium, close, reaction and cutaway coverage.
- [ ] Interview answers stand alone without the question.
- [ ] Two verified copies of all media before cards are reused.
- [ ] Signed releases for every featured contributor and private location.
