# Anonymous restaurant review method

**Goal:** review a restaurant the way an ordinary paying customer experiences it, eat
enough of the menu to judge it, describe food precisely, and publish a verdict that is
fair, fact-checked and legally safe.

## 1. Anonymity and independence
- Book under another name and phone number; pay with a card that doesn't carry your
  byline name if your outlet arranges this. *Practices vary — follow your outlet's policy.*
- Don't announce yourself, accept comped dishes, or attend the press launch of a place
  you'll review. If recognised and comped, pay for it or note it in the review.
- The outlet pays. Free meals for review are a conflict of interest at most serious
  publications.
- Wait a few weeks after opening (commonly 4–6 weeks) unless covering an opening as news;
  give the kitchen time to settle. Say if you visited early.

## 2. Visits and ordering for coverage
- **Two or more visits** where possible (different days, ideally one busy service). One
  visit is a snapshot; many outlets require two or three for a starred review.
- Bring 1–3 companions and order across the menu: different starters, mains and
  desserts, at least one signature dish, one simple dish (it reveals technique), a
  vegetarian dish, drinks including something non-alcoholic.
- Taste everyone's plate. Note portion size, temperature, seasoning, texture, timing.

## 3. Notes without blowing cover
Keep brief notes on your phone (looks like texting) or in the bathroom; photograph the
menu and dishes discreetly if your outlet uses your photos (many send a photographer
later, with the restaurant's knowledge, after the visits). Record:
- Every dish name exactly as on the menu, price, what arrived.
- Wait times (seated → order → first course → bill).
- Service moments, noise level, comfort, accessibility, cleanliness.
- Total bill per head with a typical drink.

## 4. Vocabulary: precise over purple
- **Taste dimensions:** salt, acid, sweetness, bitterness, umami, heat — balance and
  which dominates.
- **Texture:** crisp vs crunchy, tender vs soft, gelatinous, chewy (as praise or fault).
- **Technique:** properly rested meat, emulsified sauce that's split, pastry under-baked
  ("soggy bottom" is a cliché but a real fault), over-reduced stock.
- **Temperature and timing:** lukewarm plates, dishes arriving staggered.
Describe what you ate so a reader could almost taste it; then judge it. Avoid stock
words: *moreish, unctuous, cooked to perfection, divine*.

## 5. Writing and the verdict
Structure: setting/scene hook › who's behind it and the concept › the food, best to
worst, with specifics › service and room › value › verdict and stars.
**Stars/scores:** define them for your outlet (e.g. what separates 3 from 4) and apply
consistently across reviews; compare with your past ratings before submitting. See
`media-communication.criticism-and-review.review-methodology.scoring-systems-and-disclosure-policy`.

## 6. Fairness, fact-check and legal care
- Call the restaurant **after** your final visit to check facts: chef's name, opening
  date, hours, prices, sourcing claims. You don't have to reveal the verdict.
- Distinguish fact from opinion: "the chicken was undercooked and pink at the bone"
  (fact you must be sure of) vs "the chicken was joyless" (opinion). Allegations about
  hygiene or illness are factual claims with real defamation risk — *seek legal review*.
- One bad dish is a bad dish; a pattern across visits is a verdict.
- Small, new, owner-run places: severity should be proportionate; consider whether
  the review serves readers or just punishes.

## Gotchas
- Reviewing a menu that's since changed; check before publishing.
- Ignoring price: the same dish judged differently at £12 and £45.
- Companions' opinions crowding out yours.

## Verify
- [ ] At least two paid, unannounced visits (or a single visit stated clearly).
- [ ] Menu coverage includes signature, simple and vegetarian dishes.
- [ ] Every dish named and priced as on the menu; facts confirmed with the restaurant.
- [ ] Factual criticisms (hygiene, safety) are documented or removed after legal check.
- [ ] Star rating is consistent with your outlet's definitions and recent reviews.
