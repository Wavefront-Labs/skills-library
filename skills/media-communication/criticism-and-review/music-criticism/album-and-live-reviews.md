# Album and live reviews

**Goal:** tell readers what a record or show sounds and feels like — in words people
who haven't heard it can follow — and whether it matters, on the timetable of album
releases and overnight gig deadlines.

## 1. Listening for an album review
- **At least three full listens** in different contexts: once attentive on good
  headphones/speakers, once in the background (what sticks?), once with the lyrics.
- Note track by track: tempo/feel, standout moments with timestamps, production choices,
  lyrical lines worth quoting, where attention drifted.
- Read the credits: producer, mixer, collaborators, samples. They explain a lot of sound.
- Promo streams are often watermarked and private; never share or rip them.

## 2. Describing sound in prose (the core skill)
Readers can't hear your review. Translate with concrete, layered description:
- **Instrumentation and texture:** "a single detuned synth pad under a bone-dry vocal".
- **Rhythm and tempo:** "a half-time drum pattern that makes a 140 bpm song feel slow".
- **Production/space:** reverb, compression, stereo width, how close the vocal sits,
  lo-fi vs polished.
- **Vocal delivery:** register, grain, phrasing, how the singer handles the hook.
- **Structure:** a song that withholds its chorus; a track that never resolves.
- **Analogy** sparingly: one well-chosen cross-sense metaphor beats five adjectives.
Avoid empty praise words (*lush, sonic, soundscape, banger*) unless followed by the
specific thing that earns them.

## 3. Genre context without lazy comparisons
- Place the record in the artist's arc (what changed since the last album?) and the
  scene or tradition it draws on.
- Comparisons ("sounds like X meets Y") are a shortcut; use at most one, make it precise
  ("the guitar tone of early-'90s shoegaze, not the volume"), and be aware of reflexively
  comparing artists only to others of the same gender or ethnicity.
- Know the genre's own values: judging a drill track by singer-songwriter criteria is a
  category error.

## 4. Structure (album, 400–800 words)
Hook (a moment, a line, a sound) › where the artist is now › the record's main move and
whether it works, with 2–4 tracks as evidence › weaknesses › verdict and score if used.
Name standout tracks; readers often only sample a few.

## 5. Live reviews
- Get confirmation of press access and photo pass (separate) from the promoter/publicist.
- Notes during the show: setlist (or check a setlist site later and verify), the moment
  the room changed, crowd size/response, sound quality, stagecraft, support act.
- Write that night or early morning; editors often want copy before noon next day.
- Describe the event *for people who weren't there*: what happened, how it felt, how it
  compared with the record.
- Don't review a show you left early without saying so.

## 6. Timing the album cycle
Singles → announcement → pre-release review window → release day (most reviews land
release week) → touring. Embargoes on reviews are common for major releases; confirm the
exact time with the publicist. Later "slow" reviews or reappraisals are legitimate
formats with different expectations.

## 7. Ethics
Disclose friendships, past work for the artist or label, or free travel. Don't let
access (interviews, guest list) soften a verdict; if it does, don't take the access.

## Gotchas
- Reviewing the press release: the bio's claims ("their most personal record yet") are
  marketing; test them against the music.
- Lyric quotes: quote briefly and accurately — lyrics are tightly controlled
  copyright; long quotes may need permission (*jurisdiction-dependent*).
- Mis-crediting a featured artist or producer; check against official credits.

## Verify
- [ ] Album heard ≥3 times; standout tracks named with reasons.
- [ ] Every evaluative adjective is followed by a concrete sonic detail.
- [ ] At most one comparison, and it's specific.
- [ ] Live review: setlist and support act verified; time of leaving noted if early.
- [ ] Embargo, credits and spellings checked.
