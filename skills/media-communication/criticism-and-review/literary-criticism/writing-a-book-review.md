# Writing a book review

**Goal:** a review that describes what the book is, judges how well it achieves it with
evidence from the text, and places it among other books — fair to the author, useful
to the reader, and clean on quotation and conflict-of-interest rules.

## 1. Reading for review
- Read the whole book. Reviewing from the first half is the commonest unforgivable sin.
- Annotate with page numbers: passages that show the style at its best and worst,
  structural turns, factual claims (for nonfiction), recurring images.
- For nonfiction, check the notes/bibliography: what sources does it rest on, and are the
  big claims supported? Spot-check one or two.
- Read (or re-skim) one comparator: the author's previous book or the obvious peer.

## 2. Find the judgement
Three questions (adapted from long critical tradition): *What is the author trying to
do? How well have they done it? Was it worth doing?* Your answer to all three is the
backbone. Don't review the book you would have written.

## 3. Structure
- **Short review (400–800 words):** hook › what the book is (premise or argument, form)
  › 2–3 evidenced judgements › one reservation › verdict and who should read it.
- **Review essay (1,500+ words):** opens with a broader question the book raises,
  places it in a tradition or debate (often with 2–3 other books), then reads closely.
  The book under review must still get substantive, specific attention.

## 4. Quoting fairly
- Quote to **show**, not to pad: a sentence that proves your claim about the prose.
- **Quote from the final text.** Galleys/advance copies say "uncorrected proof — do not
  quote without checking against the finished book". Check quotations against a final
  copy or confirm with the publicist before publication.
- Never trim a quote so it says the opposite (ellipses that change meaning); keep
  context honest. If you criticise a passage, quote enough for readers to judge.
- Quotation for criticism and review is generally permitted under fair dealing (UK and
  others) and fair use (US) with acknowledgement, in reasonable amounts. *Not legal
  advice: long excerpts, poems or song lyrics quoted in full may need permission.*

## 5. Galleys, embargoes, timing
- ARCs/galleys arrive by post or via NetGalley/Edelweiss-type platforms, typically
  2–4 months before publication. Your editor usually times reviews to publication week.
- Some big titles carry **embargoes** (no review before date X). Confirm in writing;
  breaking one damages your outlet's relationship with the publisher.
- Don't resell review copies before publication; many outlets forbid it outright.

## 6. Conflicts of interest
Decline or disclose if you: know the author personally, share an agent/publisher in a
way that matters, have reviewed them harshly in a public feud, blurbed the book, or have
a competing book on the same subject. When in doubt, tell your editor before accepting.
(The NBCC and many outlets publish ethics guidance — follow your outlet's.)

## 7. Craft notes
- Describe the prose so readers can hear it: sentence length, register, the dominant
  device ("long, clause-stacked sentences that keep deferring the verb").
- For fiction, limit plot to what's needed; no spoilers of the ending without warning.
- For nonfiction, separate *is the argument correct?* from *is it well made?*
- Negative reviews: be specific, punch at the book, not the author's identity.

## Verify
- [ ] Whole book read; the review states a judgement on its aims and execution.
- [ ] Every quotation checked against final text (or confirmed with publicist), page noted.
- [ ] Book details correct: title, author, translator, publisher, pages, price, date.
- [ ] Conflicts considered and disclosed or cleared with the editor.
- [ ] Embargo date confirmed; no unwarned spoilers.
