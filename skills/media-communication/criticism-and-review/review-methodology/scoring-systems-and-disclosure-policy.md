# Scoring systems and disclosure policy

**Goal:** make an outlet's review scores mean the same thing across reviewers and years,
make product comparisons repeatable, and be transparent about how products were
obtained and how the outlet earns money from them.

## 1. Choosing a scale
| Scale | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| 5 stars | Readers understand it; forces decisions | Compressed: 3 vs 4 does a lot of work |
| 10-point (with halves or decimals) | Finer distinctions | Decimal precision implies false accuracy |
| 100-point | Aggregator-friendly | Scores tend to cluster; little meaning |
| Verdict labels (Buy / Wait / Skip) | Actionable | Loses nuance between good and great |
| No score | Prose carries the judgement | Readers skim for a number anyway |
Whatever you pick, **publish what each point means** ("3 = good, with clear
reservations; recommended to fans of the genre"). Expect drift toward the top over time;
recalibrate periodically by reviewing your score distribution.

## 2. Rubrics and calibration
- Rubrics help **consumer-product** reviews (a laptop: performance, battery, display,
  build, value), less so arts criticism, where a weighted formula produces absurd results.
- If you weight sub-scores, publish the weights and check the overall still matches
  the reviewer's judgement; let the editor override with a stated reason.
- **Calibration:** periodically, have several reviewers independently score the same items
  and compare; discuss disagreements and update the score definitions. Track each
  reviewer's average score — persistent outliers need a conversation.
- Keep a **reference set**: past items at each score level that define the scale.

## 3. Comparative product testing
1. **Protocol written before testing:** what's measured, how, with which tools, in what
   conditions (e.g. battery test: fixed screen brightness, Wi-Fi on, a looping web script).
2. **Same conditions for every unit**; record firmware/software versions.
3. **Repeat** measurements several times and report the median; note variance.
4. Buy retail units where you can; manufacturer samples can be cherry-picked.
   *Whether a given sample was selected is usually unknowable; disclose sample sources.*
5. Long-term updates: revisit after months of use for durability.
6. Publish the method page and link it from every review.
Best-of lists should state how many products were tested and when; mark lists
"Updated <date>" when re-tested, not just re-dated.

## 4. Disclosure and ethics policy (publish it)
Cover, in plain words:
- **How items were obtained:** purchased, loaned (returned after), gifted (kept/donated).
  State it in each review.
- **Affiliate links/commerce:** that the outlet may earn a commission; that it doesn't
  affect verdicts; that editorial decides coverage, not commerce teams.
- **Travel and events:** who paid.
- **Pre-publication review:** manufacturers/publishers never see or approve reviews.
- **Corrections and score changes:** when and how a score can change (e.g. major patches),
  with a visible note.
- **Conflicts:** reviewers declare relationships; recusal rules.
Regulators: in the US the FTC Endorsement Guides say a material connection should be
disclosed clearly and conspicuously; free products generally call for disclosure, affiliate
relationships should be disclosed on the site, "paid link" next to an affiliate link is
described as adequate ("affiliate link" alone and a "buy now" button are not), and the closer
the disclosure is to the recommendation the better (a hyperlinked disclosure is inadequate). In the UK the ASA/CAP
Codes apply to advertising; read the code text for your case. *Not legal advice; rules differ by country and change.*
Put disclosures **near the top/next to the link**, not only in a footer.

## Gotchas
- A 7/10 that reads like a pan (or a rave) — editors must catch score/prose mismatch.
- Changing scales breaks archive comparability; document the switch date.
- Sponsored content styled like reviews without labelling is a regulatory and trust risk.

## Verify
- [ ] Each score level has a published definition and reference examples.
- [ ] Score distribution reviewed on a set schedule.
- [ ] A written, versioned test protocol exists for each product category.
- [ ] Every review states how the item was obtained; affiliate disclosure is near links.
- [ ] Policy page is public and linked from reviews.
