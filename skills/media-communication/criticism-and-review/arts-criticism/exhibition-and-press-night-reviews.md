# Exhibition and press-night reviews

**Goal:** criticism of live and in-person work — exhibitions, theatre, dance, opera —
that makes readers see what was in the room, judges both the work and the
curation/production, and meets press-night deadlines.

## 1. Exhibitions: seeing first, reading second
- **First walk-through without the wall texts or audio guide.** Note what you're drawn
  to, what you skip, how the rooms flow. Then a second pass with the texts and catalogue.
- Spend real time with 3–5 key works: scale, surface, materials, how light falls, how
  the work changes as you move. Photographs flatten all of this — say what the
  reproduction misses.
- Judge the **curation** separately from the art: the argument of the show (stated in the
  intro panel), selection, sequencing, hang, lighting, loans secured or missing, whether
  wall texts help or lecture.
- Note practicalities readers need: dates, price, booking, accessibility, crowding.
- Press views (before opening) are quieter than public days; mention if crowding is
  likely to change the experience.

## 2. Describing visual work in prose (ekphrasis)
Go from the general to the specific: what it is (medium, size), what's depicted or
made, then the detail that makes it work ("the blue isn't flat: under it you can see the
earlier, warmer ground showing through at the edges"). Use comparison to other works
only when it clarifies. Avoid curatorial jargon (*interrogates, problematises,
liminal*) unless you then say concretely how.

## 3. Theatre and dance: the press night
- Press night (or a designated press performance) is usually after previews; reviews
  are typically embargoed until after the curtain. Don't review previews unless your
  outlet's policy and the production allow it, and say so if you do.
- Collect: running time, interval(s), creative team (writer, director, designer,
  lighting, sound, choreographer, composer), cast list. Check names against the programme.
- **Notes in the dark:** brief keywords, not sentences; moments, lines, images, staging
  transitions, audience reactions.
- Judge: the play/choreography itself (new work) or the interpretation (revival/
  repertory); direction and staging choices; performances (specific moments, not
  adjective lists); design — set, lighting, costume, sound — as storytelling.
- For dance: describe movement concretely — weight, speed, use of the floor, partnering,
  how the ensemble relates to soloists, relationship to the music.
- **Understudies:** if one appears, say so and review what you saw.

## 4. Writing on deadline
Overnight reviews are often filed within an hour or two of curtain (or by morning).
Prepare the factual box (venue, dates, running time, creatives, booking) before you go.
Structure: a vivid opening image › what this production is and why now › 2–4 evidenced
judgements › performances › design › verdict/stars. Lengths commonly 400–700 words.

## 5. Spoilers and fairness
- New plays: don't reveal twists or endings; classics: fair to discuss.
- Review the work as made, not the production you'd have staged.
- Fringe/small companies: judge on ambition and execution, but don't inflate stars —
  readers pay real money.

## 6. Ethics
Declare friendships and professional ties (having worked with the company, sat on a
funding panel). Comp tickets are industry norm for press performances; gifts and paid
travel follow your outlet's policy. Don't leave at the interval and review the whole.

## Gotchas
- Reviewing the press release's thesis instead of the show's actual argument.
- Ignoring access: no step-free route, or an exhibition with no seating — readers need it.
- Misspelled creative names are the most common correction; check the programme.

## Verify
- [ ] Exhibitions: you did a text-free first walk and name 3+ works with specifics.
- [ ] Curation/production judged separately from the work itself.
- [ ] Factual box complete and checked against the programme or press release.
- [ ] Understudies, preview status or partial attendance disclosed.
- [ ] No unwarned spoilers for new work; embargo respected.
