# Reviewing a video game

**Goal:** a review that judges how a game plays and whether it's worth readers' money
and hours — with enough playtime to be fair, honest caveats about the build you played,
and disclosure of how you got access.

## 1. Before you start
- Record: platform(s) played, hardware (PC specs, console model, handheld), build/version
  number, how code was obtained (publisher-provided key, purchased), and any review
  guidelines/embargo letter received.
- Read the review guidelines: they often restrict *spoilers*, not opinions. If a
  guideline asks you to avoid criticising something, that's not acceptable — escalate to
  your editor.
- Turn on recording/screenshots; capture moments as evidence and for art.

## 2. How much to play
- **Finish the critical path** of narrative/campaign games where at all possible. If you
  didn't, say how far you got and why.
- Open-world/RPGs: main story plus a meaningful sample of side content; note hours.
- Roguelikes/strategy/sim: play until you understand the systems' depth — many runs,
  multiple difficulty levels or factions.
- Multiplayer/online: you need live servers with real players. Pre-launch sessions are
  artificial; publish a **review in progress** and update after launch week.
State total hours in the review.

## 3. What to evaluate (and how to describe it)
- **Core loop:** what you do minute-to-minute, and whether it stays interesting at hour 2,
  10, 40.
- **Feel:** controls, responsiveness, input latency, camera, animation priority ("you
  can't cancel the attack once committed, which makes combat deliberate — and, against
  groups, frustrating").
- **Systems and progression:** depth, balance, grind, difficulty curve, fairness of failure.
- **Structure and pacing**, writing and performance (story games), art and audio direction.
- **Accessibility:** remappable controls, subtitle options, colour-blind modes, difficulty
  and assist options, motion settings. Report what exists, specifically.
- **Monetisation:** price, editions, microtransactions, battle passes, how hard the game
  pushes them, whether progress feels gated.
- **Performance:** frame rate (target and real), resolution modes, loading, crashes,
  save corruption. PC: test at least on recommended specs; note Steam Deck behaviour if
  relevant to your audience.
Use mechanics vocabulary that clarifies (i-frames, crafting loop, metroidvania gating)
and explain it briefly for general outlets.

## 4. Patches and live-service caveats
- Review builds differ from launch builds. Say whether a **day-one patch** was applied;
  if you played pre-patch, test the patch before publishing or note it.
- Live-service games change constantly: date the review, note the version, and decide
  with your editor when (or whether) to re-review.
- Don't excuse current problems on the promise of future fixes; review what ships.

## 5. Structure and verdict
Hook › what it is (genre, premise, price, platforms) › how it plays › what's great
› what's broken or tiresome › who it's for › verdict/score. If your outlet scores,
the number must agree with the text (see
`media-communication.criticism-and-review.review-methodology.scoring-systems-and-disclosure-policy`).

## 6. Review events and disclosure
Publisher-hosted review events (travel, hotel, supervised play) shape impressions and
limit testing. Disclose if travel/accommodation was paid by the publisher; many outlets
forbid accepting it. Preview events are not reviews: don't score from them.

## Gotchas
- Reviewing difficulty you didn't engage with ("too hard") without trying assists.
- Ignoring the platform most readers will use (e.g. reviewing only on a high-end PC).
- Reporting a bug once as a pattern — reproduce it before calling it widespread.

## Verify
- [ ] Hours played, completion status, platform, hardware and build stated.
- [ ] Day-one patch status confirmed; online components tested live or labelled in-progress.
- [ ] Accessibility and monetisation covered specifically.
- [ ] Performance claims backed by repeatable observation.
- [ ] Access and any paid travel disclosed; score matches the prose.
