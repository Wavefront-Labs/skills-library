# Reporting interview: preparation, questions and ground rules

**Goal:** leave every interview with accurate, quotable material on terms both sides
understood *before* anything was said — and without having burned the source.

## 1. Prepare (the interview is won here)
- **Know what you need.** Write the one or two sentences of your story this person can
  confirm, deny or colour. Everything else is bonus.
- **Read them first:** past quotes, filings, social posts, previous coverage. Never ask a
  question whose answer is in a public document — ask them to *explain* the document.
- **Question ladder:** 6–10 questions, ordered from easy/factual → explanatory → the hard
  one. Put the hard one late but not last; leave time for the answer and a follow-up.
- **Write the must-ask question verbatim.** Under pressure people soften it into
  something answerable with "no comment" or a yes.

## 2. Ground rules — agree them before the material, not after
Terms differ between newsrooms and countries, so define them out loud:
| Term | Usual meaning (check your outlet's policy) |
|---|---|
| On the record | Name + quote usable |
| Not for attribution / background | Usable, attributed to a description ("a senior official") |
| Deep background | Usable as your own knowledge, no attribution |
| Off the record | Not publishable; often not even usable to get it elsewhere |

**Gotchas:**
- Default is **on the record** once you've identified yourself as a reporter — but say so.
- A source can't retroactively declare something off the record. Whether you honour a
  request after the fact is an editorial call; flag it to your editor, don't decide alone.
- Agree the exact **description** for background sources ("a person familiar with the
  talks") in the interview; vague descriptions get fought over later.
- Promising anonymity binds your outlet. Many require an editor to know the source's
  identity — tell the source that before they rely on you.

## 3. Asking
- **Open questions** ("Walk me through…", "What happened next?") produce quotes; closed
  ones produce confirmation. Use closed questions only to nail specifics: dates, numbers,
  spellings, titles.
- **One question at a time.** Double-barrelled questions let them answer the easier half.
- **Use silence.** After an answer, wait 3–5 seconds. People fill gaps, often with the
  best line.
- **Follow up on vagueness:** "What do you mean by 'concerns'?" "Who specifically?"
- **The accountability question:** state the fact, then ask. "The audit says X was
  overspent by 40%. Why?" — not "Is it true there were problems?"
- **Hostile or evasive:** stay level, restate the question once in plainer words, then
  record the non-answer. "She declined to say whether…" is itself reportable.
- **Reluctant sources:** explain what the story is and what happens if they don't talk
  (it runs without their side). Don't threaten; don't overpromise.
- **End with:** "What haven't I asked that I should?" and "Who else should I talk to?"
  Then confirm spellings and title.

## 4. Recording and notes
- Recording law varies: in some jurisdictions one-party consent is enough, others need
  all parties (several US states; check locally — *unverified per jurisdiction here*).
  Simplest rule: ask "Mind if I record so I quote you accurately?"
- Record **and** take notes with timestamps of good quotes; recorders fail.
- Transcribe key quotes the same day; memory rewrites quotes within hours.

## 5. Afterwards
- Quote checking: many outlets let you read back *facts* (numbers, technical claims), not
  quotes for approval. Know your outlet's policy before offering.
- File notes and recordings where your outlet's retention policy says — they may be your
  defence later.

## Verify
- [ ] Ground rules were stated and agreed before sensitive material, and noted.
- [ ] The must-ask question was asked in its sharp form, and the answer (or refusal) recorded.
- [ ] Every quote you'll use is on tape or in contemporaneous notes.
- [ ] Names, titles and spellings confirmed with the source.
- [ ] Any anonymity promise is known to and approved by your editor.
