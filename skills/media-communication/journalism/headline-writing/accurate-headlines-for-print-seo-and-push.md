# Accurate headlines for print, SEO and push

**Goal:** display copy that a reader can trust without reading the story, that fits the
space, and that gets found and opened — in that priority order.

## 1. Start from the story's single most important fact
Read the whole piece (not just the lede — the nuance is often in graf 6). Write one
sentence: *who did what, with what consequence.* That sentence is your raw headline.

## 2. Universal rules
- **The headline must be supported by the copy.** If the story says "may", the head
  can't say "will". Qualifiers in the copy must survive into the head.
- Active voice, present tense for past events ("Council closes library"), infinitive for
  future ("Council to close library"). Conventions differ by outlet — check style.
- Attribute contested claims in the head: "Police say…", or quote marks around the claim
  (in many styles, quote marks signal attribution, not sarcasm — know your house rule).
- Avoid: question headlines you'd answer "no" to; names the reader won't know (use a
  description); puns on tragedy; abbreviations not in common use.
- **Head + standfirst/dek work as a pair:** the head hooks, the dek supplies the second
  key fact or the stakes. Don't repeat words between them.

## 3. Per-surface specifics
| Surface | What matters | Typical constraint |
|---|---|---|
| Print | Fit the count, line breaks that read naturally | Set by the page template; count units/characters per deck |
| Web/SEO title | Key terms people actually search, near the front | Search results often truncate around 60 characters (approximate; varies by pixel width) |
| Social | Curiosity + clarity; it appears with an image | Platform card truncation varies |
| Push alert | The news itself, standalone, no tease | Short — lock-screen shows only the first line or two |
| Caption | Who, what, where, when of *the picture*, present tense | Must match what's visible |

**SEO gotcha:** write for the reader first, then make sure the proper noun and the key
term (e.g. "interest rates", the team name) are in the first half. Keyword stuffing reads
as spam and many publishers' CMS let you set a separate SEO title — use it rather than
mangling the display head.

**Push gotcha:** push alerts are the highest-stakes copy you'll write — they can't be
edited once sent. Never push unconfirmed casualties, names of the dead before family
notification policy clears them, or anything single-sourced. Get a second pair of eyes.

**Line breaks in print:** don't split a name, a number from its unit, or end a line on a
preposition/article if avoidable ("Minister to / quit" reads better than "Minister to
quit over / the").

## 4. A/B testing
- Test **framings**, not accuracy: both variants must be fully supported by the copy.
- Let the test reach your tool's significance threshold; early leaders often flip.
- Clicks aren't everything — watch engaged time/scroll. A head that wins clicks but
  loses readers immediately is overpromising.

## 5. Captions
Identify people left to right where needed, state what they're doing, when and where.
Never infer emotion or intent you can't see ("mourners" when you don't know who they are).
Credit the photographer/agency per house style.

## Verify
- [ ] Head is fully supported by the copy, including qualifiers.
- [ ] Contested claims are attributed.
- [ ] Fits the print count / SEO title length / push length for its surface.
- [ ] Dek adds a new fact; no repeated words with the head.
- [ ] Push alert read by a second editor before sending.
- [ ] Caption names and details checked against the photo's metadata/caption sheet.
