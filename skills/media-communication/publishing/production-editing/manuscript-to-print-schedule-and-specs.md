# Manuscript to print: schedule and specs

**Goal:** a correct book, printed and in the warehouse (and ebook delivered) on time
for publication, with no surprises in cost or quality.

## 1. Build the schedule backwards
Start from the **publication date** and work back. Typical milestones (durations are
illustrative — your printer, list and format set the real numbers):
| Milestone | Typical lead before pub |
|---|---|
| Books in warehouse / stock to distributors | several weeks |
| Printer delivery (print + bind + ship) | add a few weeks, more for colour or overseas |
| Files to printer (print-ready PDFs + cover) | before that |
| Final proofs approved, index in | before files |
| Second proofs / revises | 1-2 weeks each round |
| First proofs (author + proofreader) | 2-4 weeks |
| Typesetting | 2-4 weeks |
| Copyedit + author review | 4-8 weeks |
| Manuscript handover (transmittal) | start |
Build **float** in: author review and indexing are the usual over-runs. Share the schedule
with everyone, with named owners per milestone.

## 2. Transmittal / handover
Collect at handover: final manuscript files, art/figure files and permissions clearances,
front and back matter (title page, copyright page, dedication, acknowledgements), series
info, design brief, special characters list, and agreed **extent** (page count) and **trim
size**. Missing permissions found at proof stage cause the worst delays.

## 3. Typesetting and proofs
- Brief the typesetter: design spec/template, style sheet, heading levels, special
  elements, fonts, image placement.
- **First proofs** go to the author and a proofreader (and an indexer, who needs final
  pagination — so index work happens on first or second proofs).
- **Collate** corrections into one set. Gate later rounds: after first proofs, only
  essential corrections. Many contracts charge authors for excessive late changes.
- Check revises: every correction made, nothing new broken, pagination stable for the index.

## 4. Print specs
Agree with the printer (get a written quote against these):
- **Trim size**, **extent** (must suit the printer's signature/section sizes — often
  multiples of 16 or 32 pages; *confirm with your printer*), paper stock and weight,
  colour (1-colour/4-colour), **binding** (paperback/perfect bound, cased/hardback),
  cover finish (matte/gloss laminate, spot UV, foil), jacket, print run.
- **Spine width** depends on extent and paper — get it from the printer *after* extent is
  final, then finalise the cover.
- Files: **print-ready PDF** to the printer's spec (often a PDF/X standard), bleed (often
  3 mm / 0.125 in), crop marks as required, images at adequate resolution, fonts embedded,
  correct colour profile.
- Request a **proof** from the printer (digital and, for colour-critical work, a hard copy)
  and check it against the files.

## 5. Metadata and identifiers
- ISBN per format/edition (paperback, hardback, ebook, audio each get their own).
- Library cataloguing data where applicable (e.g. CIP programmes in some countries).
- Metadata (title, contributors, description, price, BISAC/Thema subjects, dimensions,
  pub date) to distributors and retailers, often as ONIX feeds, **months ahead** — retailer
  listings and pre-orders depend on it.

## 6. Ebook and digital
Commission ebook conversion from final files (or export from layout) after final proofs;
validate EPUB and check on devices before delivery (see ebook-production skills).

## 7. Vendor management
Keep a single tracker: each vendor, deliverable, due date, status, cost vs quote. Confirm
dates in writing. Escalate early — a slipped proof round is recoverable; a missed press
slot may not be.

## Verify
- [ ] Backward schedule from pub date shared with owners and float.
- [ ] Permissions cleared before typesetting.
- [ ] Spine width from printer based on final extent.
- [ ] Print-ready PDF passes printer preflight; printer proof approved.
- [ ] ISBNs and metadata live with retailers before pre-order window.
- [ ] Ebook validated and checked on devices.
