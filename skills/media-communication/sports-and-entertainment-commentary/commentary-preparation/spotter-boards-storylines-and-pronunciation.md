# Spotter boards, storylines and pronunciation

**Goal:** everything you might need is findable in under two seconds during live play,
and you use maybe a fifth of it — the right fifth.

## 1. The spotter board
A one-page (or two-panel) chart per team, laid out the way the team lines up on the
field so your eye maps it to the picture.
- **Per player:** shirt number (large), name as said on air plus phonetic spelling,
  position, 2–3 short facts (season numbers, a recent moment, a personal detail cleared
  for broadcast). Keep it to what you could say in a 5-second window.
- **Colour-code** by category (e.g. one colour for stats, one for storylines, one for
  injuries/returns) so you can find the type of fact you need.
- **Bench/substitutes** in a separate strip; move them in when they come on.
- Team block: coach, formation, form, head-to-head, league position, what's at stake.
- Format: paper/laminated card still dominates in many booths; tablets work but
  glare, sleep timers and touch accidents bite. *Choice is personal; keep a paper
  backup either way.*
- Update from the **official team sheet** when it's released (often about an hour
  before in football — check the competition's rules) and re-check numbers.

## 2. Storylines
Write the 3–5 storylines of the event, ranked: title race, a record within reach, a
return from injury, a rivalry, a debut. For each, have:
- a one-line way to raise it;
- the trigger in the game that would make it relevant (e.g. "if X scores → record");
- the stat that proves it.
Prep that is triggered by action surfaces naturally; prep recited in a lull sounds like
reading.

## 3. Stat pack
- Sources: the league/governing body's official stats, the broadcaster's research team,
  club media notes. Cite the source if challenged.
- Check definitions (what counts as an assist, a "shot on target") differ between providers.
- Pre-calculate milestones ("needs 2 more for 100").
- Round for speech; keep the precise figure on the card.

## 4. Pronunciation
- Ask the person or their team's media officer; many teams publish a pronunciation guide.
- Listen to the player say it (interviews) rather than guessing from spelling — local
  pronunciation and "anglicised" broadcast pronunciations can both exist; follow the
  player's preference and the broadcaster's style guide.
- Write a simple phonetic respelling you can read at speed (e.g. CAPS for stress:
  "a-ZEE-zee") rather than IPA, unless you read IPA fluently.
- Say the hard names aloud ten times before the broadcast.

## 5. Meetings and access
- **Production meeting** with producer/director: running order, planned replays and
  graphics, sponsor reads, where storylines will be supported by graphics.
- Team access (training, coaches, media officer) gives you facts nobody else has;
  respect what's off the record.
- Share notes with your co-commentator so you don't double up.

## Gotchas
- Late changes: shirt numbers swapped, a player withdrawn in warm-up — re-check before air.
- Personal details (family, illness) only if public or cleared.
- Over-preparation shows as a list of facts; aim to know the story, not recite the card.
- *Uncertain:* team-sheet timings and media-access rules vary by competition.

## Verify
- [ ] Spotter board laid out by formation, numbers checked against the official sheet.
- [ ] Every player has a phonetic respelling confirmed from a reliable source.
- [ ] 3–5 ranked storylines each with a trigger and a stat.
- [ ] Milestones pre-calculated; stat definitions noted.
- [ ] Production meeting attended; notes shared with co-commentator; paper backup ready.
