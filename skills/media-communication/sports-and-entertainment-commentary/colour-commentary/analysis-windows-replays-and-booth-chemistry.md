# Analysis windows, replays and booth chemistry

**Goal:** every time you speak, the viewer learns *why* something happened or what to
watch next — in the time available, without stepping on the call.

## 1. Your job vs the lead's
The lead (play-by-play) owns **what** and **who**. You own **why**, **how** and
**what next**. If you're repeating what the lead said or what the picture shows, cut it.
See `media-communication.sports-and-entertainment-commentary.play-by-play.calling-live-action-at-speed`.

## 2. The analysis window
Speak in the gaps: stoppages, set-piece setups, between points, between deliveries,
during replays. Before you start, estimate the window (a throw-in might give 5 seconds;
a VAR check 60) and **size the thought to fit**:
- **5 s:** one observation. "He's been drifting inside all half."
- **15 s:** observation + reason. "…because their full-back keeps tucking in."
- **30 s+ (replay):** observation, evidence on screen, consequence.
Start with the point, then the support — if play restarts, the lead can cut you off
and the point has still been made. Stop when the ball goes live; finish the thought
later if it matters.

## 3. Talking to the replay
- Know what the replay will show before it rolls (the director/producer may tell you
  on talkback, or the first frames will). Say "Watch the left-back here…" to direct
  eyes **before** the key moment, then explain as it happens.
- **Telestrator** (Piero, Viz, etc.): draw sparingly — one circle, one arrow. Talk while
  drawing; freeze-frame on the decisive moment. Practise the controls before air;
  drawing too slowly wastes the window.
- Admit when a replay changes your view. "I was wrong live — he did get a touch."

## 4. What makes insight
- **Specific over general:** "keeps his hips open so he can play either way" beats
  "great technique".
- **Anticipate:** tell viewers what to watch for next ("if they bring on a second
  striker, watch the press change"). Proven right, it's the best moment in your career;
  proven wrong, own it.
- **Lived experience:** what the player is feeling, what the dressing room is saying —
  the thing only a former practitioner knows. Use it; don't lean on "in my day".
- Criticism: fair, about the decision not the person; no speculation on private matters.

## 5. Booth chemistry
- Agree signals: a hand on the arm or a look for "I've got something", a raised
  finger for "hold on, play's live". Watch the lead, not just the monitor.
- Let the lead finish the big call and the lay-out before adding anything.
- Disagree with each other — briefly, warmly. Real discussion beats two people agreeing.
- Use names; ask each other questions; don't save all the best material for yourself.
- In prep, share storylines so you don't both raise the same stat.

## Gotchas
- Talking over the restart is the most common analyst error — listen to the lead's
  pitch rising and stop.
- Jargon: explain once ("the half-space — the channel between full-back and centre-back").
- Conflict of interest: disclose ties to clubs/players if the broadcaster requires it.
- *Uncertain:* timings above are illustrative; they vary by sport and director.

## Verify
- [ ] Listening back: every contribution adds why/how/what-next, not description.
- [ ] No analysis running over live play.
- [ ] Replays were set up ("watch…") before the key frame.
- [ ] Telestrations limited to one or two marks per replay.
- [ ] At least one forward-looking "watch for" per half or period.
