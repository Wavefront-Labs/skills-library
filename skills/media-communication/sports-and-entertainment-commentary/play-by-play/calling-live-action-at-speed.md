# Calling live action at speed

**Goal:** the audience always knows who has it, where, and what's at stake — and the
biggest moment gets the biggest (but not the longest) call. Prep comes from
`media-communication.sports-and-entertainment-commentary.commentary-preparation.spotter-boards-storylines-and-pronunciation`.

## 1. Radio vs TV: different jobs
- **Radio** — you are the picture. Describe continuously: who, where on the field
  (direction of play stated: "attacking left to right from the main stand"), what
  they did, the result. Silence is dead air.
- **TV** — the picture is already there. **Caption, don't describe**: add the name,
  the significance, then get out. Talking over every touch is the classic TV error.
- Streaming with no co-commentator tends toward the radio end; mixed audiences may be
  listening without watching.

## 2. Identification at speed
- Learn players by more than numbers: build, gait, hair, boots, position habits —
  numbers are often hidden.
- **Name the player when they receive**, not after they pass. If unsure, describe
  the action ("worked out to the far side…") and add the name a beat later once sure.
- A wrong name corrected quickly is forgiven; a confident wrong name on a goal is
  replayed forever. When in doubt on the decisive moment, call the action and team
  first, the name after the replay.

## 3. The reset
Every couple of minutes on radio, and after every break and every score on TV: the
**score, time and situation** ("Twenty-two minutes gone, still nil-nil, and they're
down to ten men"). People join constantly.

## 4. Pitching excitement
- Have a **range**: your conversational baseline must be low enough to leave room to rise.
- Rise with the danger, not the noise: voice lifts as the attack builds, peaks at the
  moment of result.
- **The big call:** short. Name, action, result — then **lay out**. Let the crowd and
  pictures carry it for a few seconds (TV) before you add the line. On radio, lay out
  for less time — a second or two of crowd, then confirm the score.
- Don't script catchphrases word-for-word; a prepared idea about the stakes ("first
  title in 40 years") is fine — keep the words natural.
- Never call it before it's certain (offside flags, video review, photo finish).
  "He's scored — but the flag is up…" is better than a premature celebration.

## 5. Working with the producer and the booth
- Talkback/IFB in one ear: keep talking while listening; acknowledge with action, not
  words on air.
- **Hit cues:** "Out in 30" means wrap your thought, not start a new story. Count backs
  should end cleanly on the line ("…and we'll be back after this").
- Hand to the analyst after the action ends, not mid-attack; a look or a name ("…Sam?")
  is the handover. See the colour-commentary skill for the analyst side.
- Read promos and sponsor reads as written, at natural pace — they're contractual.

## Gotchas
- Stats mid-play distract; save them for stoppages.
- Commentary positions may have a broadcast delay vs the stadium screen; call off
  your monitor if the feed is what viewers see, but watch the field for context.
- Injuries: describe neutrally, no speculation on severity; follow the producer on
  whether to show replays.
- *Uncertain:* conventions differ by sport and broadcaster (e.g. cricket's slower
  rhythm, horse racing's continuous call order); learn the house style.

## Verify
- [ ] Listening back: within any 10-second window, a newcomer knows who has possession.
- [ ] Score/time reset present after every break and roughly every few minutes (radio).
- [ ] Big moments: short call, then a lay-out; no premature calls.
- [ ] Producer cues hit; no story started inside a count.
- [ ] Wrong identifications logged and fixed in prep for next time.
