# Studio hosting, throws and filling time

**Goal:** the show feels effortless while you're quietly managing the clock, the
gallery's instructions and three opinionated guests at once.

## 1. Know the rundown cold
Before air, have the **running order/rundown**: each item, its duration, source (studio,
outside broadcast, VT/package, commentary), and the **hard points** (live kick-off, ad
breaks, network junctions). Mark which items are **flexible** (panel chat, extra
replay) and which are **fixed** (sponsor reads, legal lines, kick-off). Flexible items
are your accordion.

## 2. Opening and closing
- Open: the occasion and the stakes in one sentence, then the guests by name and why
  they're here. Script the first 15 seconds; ad-lib afterwards.
- Close: result/stakes recap, what's next and when, thanks, sign-off **on time** —
  the director will be counting you out.

## 3. Throws and handovers
- A **throw** tells the viewer where they're going and gives the next voice a clean start:
  "Let's go down to pitch-side, where Alex is with the manager." Then stop talking.
- Throw to a VT/package with its intro line and the agreed out-word if it's live-voiced.
- Receiving the show back ("back to you in the studio"): acknowledge briefly, move on.
- Throwing to commentary: keep it short; the commentators have their own opening.

## 4. Moderating a panel
- Brief each pundit on the topics and the order before air; give each a lane.
- Ask **short, specific questions** to a named person: "Sam, was that a penalty?"
  Open questions to the whole panel produce silence or a pile-up.
- Manage airtime: bring in the quiet one, cut in politely on a monologue ("Let me
  bring Jo in on that…").
- Disagreement is good television; personal attacks are not — step in.
- Keep the viewer's question in mind: what would they ask if they were sitting here?

## 5. The clock: back-timing
Talkback/IFB will give counts ("one minute to the break", "wrap", "30, 20, 10…").
- **Back-time**: know the time you must hit (the break, kick-off) and work out how long
  you have from now. Many hosts keep a stopwatch or the gallery clock in view.
- On "wrap", finish the current sentence and go to the planned out-line; don't start
  a new question. Have a pre-written 10-second and 30-second version of your link to
  the break.
- Be ready to **drop** a flexible item on instruction, or **stretch** it.

## 6. Filling when things slip
Rain delays, overrunning matches, lost lines, late kick-offs. Prepare a **fill bank**
before every show:
- 2–3 panel discussion topics with a clip each;
- archive moments relevant to the day;
- a "what to watch for" segment;
- stats and storylines from the commentary prep.
Tell the viewer plainly what's happening ("we've lost the pictures from the stadium —
we'll go back as soon as they're restored"); never guess the cause on air. Follow the
director; they'll tell you what sources are available.

## Gotchas
- Earpiece discipline: you'll hear the gallery talking over the guests — don't react
  visibly; nod lightly to show you've heard.
- Legal: avoid comments on ongoing disciplinary/legal cases; follow the editor's line.
- Live mics: assume every mic is live in the studio.
- *Uncertain:* cue conventions and terms differ between broadcasters; learn the
  gallery's vocabulary in rehearsal.

## Verify
- [ ] Rundown annotated with fixed vs flexible items and hard points.
- [ ] Scripted open/close and 10 s / 30 s break links ready.
- [ ] Each pundit briefed on lanes and topics.
- [ ] Fill bank prepared with at least 10 minutes of material.
- [ ] Show hit every hard point on air (check the log).
