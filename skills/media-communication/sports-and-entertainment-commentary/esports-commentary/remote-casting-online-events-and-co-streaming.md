# Remote casting, online events and co-streaming

**Goal:** a clean, in-sync remote cast of an online tournament that reads the game well
and stays inside the organiser's and publisher's rules. Core on-air craft (play-by-play,
colour, observer work) is in
`sports-recreation.esports.casting-and-shoutcasting.play-by-play-colour-and-observer-craft`;
this skill covers the online-event and remote layer.

## 1. Home caster setup
- **Wired ethernet**, not Wi-Fi. Close cloud sync and updates during the show.
- Dynamic or broadcast mic on an arm, closed-back headphones (no speaker bleed into
  your mic), a pop filter. Check levels against the producer's target and keep the
  same distance from the mic all show.
- Feed in: the producer usually sends a programme/observer feed and a talkback channel
  (e.g. Discord or a dedicated intercom). Feed out: your mic to the production, often
  via a browser-based contribution tool (e.g. VDO.Ninja) or a dedicated app — use
  whatever the production specifies.
- Two screens: programme feed, plus notes/stats. A third for chat is optional and
  distracting.
- Run a **tech check** an hour or more before air: audio level, latency, camera framing
  if on cam, backup internet (phone tether) tested.

## 2. Delay and sync
- **Spectator/observer delay** (often minutes in competitive titles, set by the
  organiser/game to prevent ghosting) means you cast a delayed game; never relay
  information from players' own streams or socials — they're on a different clock and
  it's an integrity problem.
- Duo casters in different homes hear each other with latency; agree hand signals in
  a back-channel (a text "→" to hand over) and leave slightly longer gaps than in a booth.
- If your feed and your partner's drift, the producer should re-sync; tell them in
  talkback rather than on air.

## 3. Reading game state as a duo
- Before air, agree the **frame** for each map/game: win conditions, economy or gold
  state, objective timers. The play-by-play caster tracks the immediate fight; the
  analyst tracks the state that will decide it.
- Call the **state shift** out loud: "that's the first time they've had full buys
  together" — it tells viewers why the next minute matters.
- Hype is a budget: save the top of your range for decisive moments, and for the
  series, not just the round.

## 4. Series format and downtime
Online events have long, unpredictable gaps: technical pauses, player disconnects,
remakes, draft/veto phases.
- Have a **downtime bank**: team form, head-to-head, player storylines, meta talk,
  a replay you can talk over.
- In a technical pause, say what's known ("a technical pause from Team A, we'll
  bring you back when it's resolved"), never speculate about cheating or a player's
  conduct.
- Recap the series score at every map start and after every pause.

## 5. Co-streaming and rights
- Co-streaming (re-broadcasting the official feed with your own commentary on your
  channel) is **permitted only where the rights holder allows it**, usually with
  conditions: delay minimums, no ad overlays on the feed, branding retained, sometimes
  approval lists. *Rules vary by publisher, league and event and change often —
  read the current terms for that event.*
- Organisers' talent contracts may forbid casting the same event elsewhere or
  promoting competing sponsors.
- Disclose sponsorships and any ties to teams.

## Gotchas
- Discord notifications and system sounds leak into the mic — mute them.
- Pronunciations of player handles matter as much as real names; ask the teams.
- Chat is not your producer; resist responding to it mid-play.

## Verify
- [ ] Tech check passed with wired connection, tested backup, clean audio, no bleed.
- [ ] Back-channel hand signals agreed with co-caster.
- [ ] No information used from outside the delayed official feed.
- [ ] Downtime bank ready; tech pauses handled without speculation.
- [ ] Co-stream (if any) matches the rights holder's current terms.
