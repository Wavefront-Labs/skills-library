# Sequencing reorg and layoff communications

**Goal:** every affected person hears news that affects them from the right person, in
the right order, before they read it anywhere else — and knows what happens next.

## 1. Stakeholder map
List every group and how the change affects them: directly affected individuals,
their managers, their teams, the rest of the organisation, unions/works councils/
employee representatives, the board, customers, suppliers, regulators, media, and
the local community for big site changes. For each: what they need to know, when,
from whom, and through which channel.

## 2. Legal and consultation first
Collective redundancies, relocations and some policy changes trigger **legal
consultation duties** in many countries (e.g. collective consultation in the UK, works
council consultation in much of the EU, WARN Act notices in the US for certain
layoffs). These dictate the order and timing of communications. *Not legal advice —
HR and employment lawyers must set the timetable; comms writes to it.*
Listed companies may also have market-disclosure obligations that govern timing.

## 3. The sequence (a typical layoff/reorg day)
1. **T-days:** leadership and managers who will deliver news are briefed under
   confidentiality, with materials. Rehearse the hard conversations.
2. **T-0, first:** affected individuals told **personally** (one-to-one or small
   group with manager + HR), with written details in hand.
3. **T-0 + short gap (minutes–hours):** leader's all-staff message and/or all-hands.
4. **Same time:** manager team meetings for remaining staff.
5. **Same day:** external statement ready (press, customers) — published when the news
   is out internally, or held reactive.
6. **Following days/weeks:** FAQ updates, office hours, check-ins, progress updates.
Avoid Friday-afternoon or pre-holiday announcements where possible, and avoid
cutting system access mid-conversation.

## 4. Manager talking points
Managers carry most of the load. Give them:
- a 1-page **briefing**: what's happening, why, timeline, what it means for their team;
- **key messages** (3–5) in plain language, and words to avoid;
- a **script** for the individual conversation (first sentence states the outcome);
- likely questions with answers, and "I don't know — I'll find out by <date>";
- who to escalate to and where support is (EAP, HR, outplacement).
Tell managers about their own position **before** asking them to deliver news.

## 5. FAQ drafting
- Write from employees' worries, in their words: pay, notice, benefits, visas, role,
  reporting lines, systems access, "am I next?".
- Answer directly; if undecided, say when it will be decided.
- Version-date it; update as questions arrive and flag what's new.
- Separate FAQs for affected and non-affected staff where answers differ.

## 6. Rollouts and policy shifts (non-layoff change)
Use the same map and cascade, but spread over weeks: awareness (why) → detail (what and
when) → readiness (training, how-to) → reinforcement (reminders, fixing problems).
Frameworks like ADKAR (awareness, desire, knowledge, ability, reinforcement) help check
you're not stopping at "awareness".

## Gotchas
- Calendar invites titled "Restructure" leak the news; use neutral titles.
- Email to a laid-off person's work address may become inaccessible — use personal
  email or printed letters for follow-up as HR advises.
- Prepare for leaks: have a holding statement ready from day one of planning.

## Verify
- [ ] Stakeholder map complete with owner, timing and channel per group.
- [ ] Legal/HR confirmed consultation and disclosure timetable.
- [ ] Affected people told personally before any broad announcement.
- [ ] Managers briefed (including about themselves) with script, Q&A and escalation path.
- [ ] FAQ version-dated and updated; follow-up communications scheduled.
