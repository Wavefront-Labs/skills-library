# All-hands run-of-show and Q&A moderation

**Goal:** a meeting that starts and ends on time, gives remote and in-room staff the
same experience, and has a Q&A that answers the real questions.

## 1. The run-of-show (ROS)
A shared document or sheet, one row per segment:
| Time | Seg | Who | Content | Source | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10:00:00 | Open | Host | Welcome, agenda | Cam 1, slide 1 | Walk-in music fades at :59 |
| 10:02:00 | CEO update | CEO | 3 points | Slides 2–9 | Clicker to CEO |
| 10:14:00 | Video | — | Customer story 2:30 | Playback | Host intro line |
| … | Q&A | Host + panel | Pre-submitted + live | Slido screen | Hard stop 10:58 |
Include **clock times**, not just durations; mark fixed hard stops. Typical 60-minute
shape: open (2), main update (10–15), 1–2 short segments (5–10 each), recognition (5),
Q&A (20–25), close (2). Protect the Q&A time — overruns usually eat it, which staff notice.

## 2. Speaker notes and transitions
- Give each speaker their slot length and the **one point** they must land.
- Write the **transitions** yourself: the host's handover line names the next speaker
  and why they matter ("To explain what the new pricing means for support, here's Jo").
- Speakers bring bullet notes, not full scripts, unless wording is sensitive
  (results, legal matters).
- Rehearse at least the opening, handovers and any tech (video, demo, remote speaker).

## 3. Q&A preparation
- **Collect questions in advance** (form or a Q&A tool such as Slido, Mentimeter or the
  platform's built-in Q&A) and allow **anonymous** submissions — people ask the real
  questions anonymously. Upvoting surfaces what most people want.
- Cluster submitted questions into themes; prepare answers for the top themes with
  the leaders who will answer.
- Prepare for the hard questions you know are out there even if not submitted
  (pay, layoffs, a recent incident). If the honest answer is "we can't share that yet",
  write why and when.

## 4. Moderating live
- The moderator (not the CEO) chooses questions. Read them **verbatim**, including
  pointed wording; softening an upvoted question destroys trust.
- Alternate between room, remote and pre-submitted questions.
- Direct each question to the right person by name.
- Keep answers short; follow up in writing on anything unanswered ("We'll publish
  answers to the remaining questions by Friday") — and actually do it.
- If a question names an individual or concerns a personal HR matter, acknowledge and
  take it offline.

## 5. Hybrid staging
- One platform (Teams town hall, Zoom webinar, a livestream) with a producer who isn't
  presenting.
- Cameras on speakers, not the slides only; put slides and speaker side by side.
- **Room mic for questions** — without it, remote viewers hear nothing.
- Turn on **live captions**; record and publish with captions for other time zones.
- Remote-first touches: say the time zone, greet remote staff, take a remote question
  early.

## Gotchas
- A shared ROS with timings that nobody watches is useless: assign a timekeeper with
  cards or a back-channel to the host.
- Muting/attendee permissions: set webinar mode so attendees can't unmute by accident.
- Recordings of sensitive content: check who can access them and for how long.
- *Uncertain:* tool features (anonymous Q&A, caption languages) vary by platform and
  licence tier.

## Verify
- [ ] ROS with clock times, owners, sources and a protected Q&A block.
- [ ] Transitions written; tech rehearsed.
- [ ] Questions collected in advance with anonymity; top themes prepared.
- [ ] Moderator reads questions verbatim; unanswered ones get a published follow-up.
- [ ] Captions on, room mic used, recording shared appropriately.
