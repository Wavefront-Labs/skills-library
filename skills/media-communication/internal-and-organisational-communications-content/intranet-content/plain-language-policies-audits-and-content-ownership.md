# Plain-language policies, audits and content ownership

**Goal:** a staff member can find the answer to "how do I…?" in one search and trust
it's current.

## 1. Write for tasks, not departments
Most intranet visits are someone trying to do something (book leave, claim expenses,
get a laptop fixed). Structure around those tasks:
- **Page title = the task** in the user's words: "Claim expenses", not "Finance
  Department — Reimbursement Procedures".
- First lines: who it applies to and the short answer.
- Numbered steps for processes; one action per step; link to the form/tool at the
  step where it's used.
- "If something goes wrong" / "Who to contact" at the end.
Look at intranet search logs — the top searches are your list of most important pages.

## 2. Plain-language policies
Policies have to be precise *and* understood. Approach:
1. Keep the formal policy text where legal/HR needs it, but **lead with a plain summary**
   ("What this means for you") of 3–6 bullets.
2. Use "you" and "we"; active voice ("Your manager must approve…", not "Approval shall
   be obtained…").
3. Replace legalisms: "must" over "shall"; "before" over "prior to"; "about" over "in
   relation to".
4. Define terms once, near where they're used.
5. Tables for rules that vary (by country, grade, contract type).
6. Test: ask two people from the audience to find a specific answer; watch where
   they struggle.
Have HR/legal approve that the plain version doesn't change the meaning.

## 3. Content audit
Run one on any intranet that has grown for a few years.
1. **Inventory:** export every page with URL, title, owner, last-updated date and page
   views (SharePoint, Confluence and most platforms can export or report this).
2. **Assess** each: keep, update, merge, archive or delete. Common rule-of-thumb
   triggers: not updated in 12+ months, no views in 6 months, duplicate topics,
   no owner. Look for **ROT** — redundant, outdated, trivial.
3. Prioritise the top ~20% of pages by traffic and task importance; those carry most
   of the value.
4. Redirect deleted pages' URLs to the replacement so links and bookmarks don't break.
*Thresholds are conventions; set your own based on how fast your content changes.*

## 4. Ownership model
- Every page has a **named owner** (a role, not just a person, so it survives
  departures) and a **review date**.
- Automated reminders before the review date; pages past review get a visible
  "may be out of date" banner, then are archived if no response.
- A small central team owns standards, navigation and the homepage; content owners
  own accuracy.
- A simple style guide (voice, formatting, page templates) that publishers actually
  use — templates beat rules.

## 5. Findability
- Use the platform's **promoted results / best bets** for top searches.
- Add synonyms staff actually use ("holiday" = "annual leave" = "PTO").
- Fewer, better pages beat many partial ones; one canonical page per topic.

## Gotchas
- PDFs and Word attachments hide content from search and mobile users; prefer pages.
- "Last updated" dates can be bumped by trivial edits; review dates must reflect a real
  check.
- Migrations are the best time to delete, not to copy everything across.

## Verify
- [ ] Top search terms each lead to a single, task-titled page.
- [ ] Every policy page has a plain summary approved by its owner.
- [ ] Audit inventory complete with a decision for every page; redirects in place.
- [ ] Each page has an owner role and review date; reminders automated.
- [ ] Two-person usability check passed for the most important tasks.
