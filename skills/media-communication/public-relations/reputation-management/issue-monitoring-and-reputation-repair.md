# Issue monitoring and reputation repair

**Goal:** know how you are perceived by the groups that matter, spot issues while they are
still small, and when damage happens, repair it in a way that actually holds. Reputation is
roughly *behaviour + what others say about it*; comms can only shift the second part so far.

## 1. Map whose opinion matters
List 5–10 stakeholder groups (customers, staff, regulators, investors, local community,
trade press, critics/activists). For each: what they currently believe about you, what you
want them to believe, and the evidence they'd accept. This keeps "reputation" from becoming
a vague feeling.

## 2. Keep an issues register
A spreadsheet beats a slide. Per issue:
- description and owner;
- **likelihood × impact** score (1–5 each);
- current status (latent / emerging / live / dormant);
- **trigger signals** that move it up (a regulator consultation, a lawsuit filed, a
  journalist asking, a spike in complaints);
- agreed position and the facts that back it;
- pre-approved lines and who may speak.
Review monthly; review weekly anything "emerging". Issues usually become crises because the
signals were visible and nobody owned them.

## 3. Monitoring
- **Media:** keyword alerts for org name, leaders, products, and the issue words themselves
  (paid monitoring tools or free alerts; free tools miss a lot of paywalled and broadcast
  coverage).
- **Social / forums:** listening queries including misspellings and slang; watch
  complaint-heavy places (review sites, subreddits, employee-review sites).
- **Search:** what page one returns for your name and "<name> + complaint/scam/lawsuit".
- **Direct signals:** complaint volumes, staff surveys, customer-service themes — often
  earlier than press.
Report trends, not counts: share of voice, tone over time, which narratives are rising.
Automated sentiment scores are noisy (sarcasm, mixed posts) — sample-read real items before
believing a shift.

## 4. Narrative upkeep (the quiet, unglamorous part)
- Keep a short **core narrative**: who you are, what you do, the proof points. Refresh proof
  points yearly.
- Build **third-party advocates** before you need them: customers, academics, partners,
  industry bodies who will speak because they believe it. Never script or pay them covertly
  — undisclosed paid endorsement breaches advertising rules in many places and destroys
  credibility when found.
- Make sure your own channels (website, profiles, Wikipedia talk pages via proper
  conflict-of-interest disclosure, not direct editing) are accurate.

## 5. Repairing damage
1. **Diagnose honestly**: is the criticism substantially true? If yes, comms cannot fix it
   alone; the operational fix comes first.
2. **Own it** where deserved (see media-communication.public-relations.crisis-communications.holding-statements-and-the-first-24-hours
   for the apology pattern).
3. **Change something visible** and measurable — policy, leadership, process, refunds.
4. **Show progress repeatedly** at intervals (30, 90, 180 days) with evidence others can check.
5. **Let others say it**: independent audits, regulators' findings, customers' stories.
6. For search results: publish genuinely useful, accurate content; request corrections of
   factual errors from publishers with evidence. Removal of legitimate critical coverage is
   generally neither possible nor wise; "reputation management" firms promising it may use
   fake reviews or legal threats that backfire.
Expect recovery to take months to years; trust drops fast and rebuilds slowly.

## Gotchas
- Measuring "mentions" and calling it reputation.
- Answering every critic — sometimes engagement amplifies a small issue.
- Astroturfing (fake grassroots support) — almost always discovered.
- Treating staff as an afterthought: employees are the most trusted source on what a
  company is really like.

## Verify
- [ ] Each priority stakeholder has a current-vs-target perception written down.
- [ ] Issues register reviewed on schedule; every live issue has an owner and trigger list.
- [ ] Monitoring covers media, social, search and internal signals.
- [ ] Repair plans include an operational change, not just messaging.
- [ ] No undisclosed paid advocacy or fake reviews anywhere in the plan.
