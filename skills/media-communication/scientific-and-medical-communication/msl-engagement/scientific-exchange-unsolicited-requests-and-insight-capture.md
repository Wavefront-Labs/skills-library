# MSL scientific exchange, unsolicited requests and insight capture

**Goal:** peer-level scientific conversations with healthcare professionals (HCPs) that
are genuinely useful to them, stay inside the line between medical and promotional
activity, are documented properly, and feed real insight back to the company.

**Big caveat:** rules differ by country (e.g. US FDA framework and the PhRMA Code; the UK
ABPI Code; EFPIA codes across Europe; local laws elsewhere) and by company SOP. Your
company's medical affairs SOPs and compliance team are the authority; this is the general
shape of the job.

## 1. The line: medical vs. promotional
- MSLs sit in **medical affairs**, not sales. They do not have sales targets, should not be
  directed by commercial teams on whom to see for sales purposes, and do not promote.
- **Proactive** discussion is normally limited to approved (on-label) information and
  materials approved through medical (not promotional) review, plus disease-state and
  scientific topics as permitted by local rules.
- **Off-label** information (unapproved uses, doses, populations) may only be shared in
  response to a genuine **unsolicited request** from the HCP — the MSL must not prompt it.
- Materials used in the field must be medically approved; personal slides or unapproved
  reprints are a common compliance finding.

## 2. Planning an interaction
- Objective drawn from the **medical plan** (e.g. understand treatment pathways in a
  disease area, gather feedback on trial design, support an investigator site), not a sales
  call plan.
- Read the HCP's recent publications, trial involvement and interests. KOLs (key opinion
  leaders) — often called scientific experts or thought leaders now — expect peer-level
  discussion.
- Prepare approved resources: product label, approved slide decks, published data,
  medical information request forms.
- Bring questions: what the HCP sees in practice, unmet needs, data gaps.

## 3. Handling an unsolicited question
1. Confirm it's **genuinely unsolicited** (not prompted by you or a sales colleague) and
   specific.
2. Respond **only to the question asked**, in a balanced, non-promotional, scientific way —
   including limitations and safety data — using approved response documents or published
   evidence.
3. Many companies route off-label requests to **Medical Information**, which provides a
   written, approved response; the MSL logs the request with the HCP's specific question
   in their words.
4. Make clear when information concerns an unapproved use.
5. Document: date, HCP, the question, what was provided, by whom.
6. Any **adverse event** or product complaint mentioned must be reported to
   pharmacovigilance within the company's timeline (often 24 hours) — even in passing.

## 4. Documenting interactions
Log every interaction in the CRM (Veeva CRM/Medical or equivalent): attendees, topics,
materials used, unsolicited requests, follow-ups. Keep notes factual. Transfers of value
(meals, honoraria for advisory boards) may be disclosable under transparency laws (e.g. the
US Open Payments system; EFPIA disclosure codes) — follow SOPs.

## 5. Insight capture
An **insight** is not just "Dr X liked the data". Good insights are:
- **New** to the company, and **actionable**;
- specific: what the HCP said, the context, why it matters;
- themed across many conversations (one comment is an anecdote; ten from different
  regions is a signal).
Example: "Several hepatologists in the region said they delay starting therapy because the
monitoring schedule needs an extra clinic visit — affects real-world uptake; consider
evidence on alternative monitoring." Feed insights through the formal process to medical
strategy; avoid passing raw field intelligence directly to sales.

## Gotchas
- A sales rep "introducing" an off-label question for the MSL to answer — not unsolicited.
- Using a favourite unapproved slide deck.
- Forgetting the AE report when a doctor mentions a side effect over coffee.
- Insights that are really opinions about competitors' sales tactics.

## Verify
- [ ] Every interaction maps to a medical-plan objective, not a sales target.
- [ ] Only medically approved materials used proactively.
- [ ] Off-label discussions only in response to documented unsolicited requests.
- [ ] AEs reported within the SOP timeline.
- [ ] Insights logged with context and themed for medical strategy.
