# Conference abstracts, posters and GPP-compliant manuscript support

**Goal:** accurate, complete, timely scientific publications — written efficiently with
professional medical-writing support, where authorship and funding are transparent and the
science (not the sponsor's commercial interest) drives the content.

## 1. The rules you work within
- **ICMJE authorship:** each author must (1) contribute substantially to conception/design
  or data acquisition/analysis/interpretation; (2) draft or critically revise the work;
  (3) approve the final version; and (4) agree to be accountable for it. All four.
  Others who helped are acknowledged, not listed as authors.
- **GPP 2022** (for industry-sponsored research): authors control content and have access
  to the data; medical writers support but don't replace authors; writing support and its
  funding are disclosed in the acknowledgements; publish results regardless of outcome,
  including negative results; avoid duplicate/redundant publication.
- **Reporting guidelines** (EQUATOR Network): CONSORT for randomised trials, STROBE for
  observational studies, PRISMA for systematic reviews. Use the checklist while drafting.
- **Disclosures:** every author's conflicts (ICMJE disclosure form or journal equivalent)
  and the study's funding source.

## 2. Conference abstracts
- Read the congress **instructions** first: word/character limit (often ~250–300 words or a
  character count including spaces), required headings, whether tables are allowed,
  **embargo policy** (many congresses embargo data until presentation and forbid prior
  publication), and **encore** rules (re-presenting data shown elsewhere — usually allowed
  only if declared, and sometimes not at all).
- Structure: **Background** (one or two sentences: the gap), **Methods** (design, population,
  intervention, primary endpoint, analysis), **Results** (numbers — primary endpoint first,
  with effect estimates and CIs or p-values, key safety findings), **Conclusions** (what the
  data support, no more).
- Write results with real numbers, not "significantly improved". Include the denominator.
- Conclusions must match the primary endpoint; secondary or subgroup findings are labelled
  as such.
- Timeline: draft to authors with time for review cycles before the deadline — typical
  submission deadlines fall several months before the meeting.

## 3. Posters
- One clear **take-home message** in the title or a top banner; readers spend seconds per
  poster.
- Mostly figures; minimal text in short bullet points; large fonts (readable from ~2 m).
- Logical flow left-to-right or top-to-bottom: background → methods → results → conclusions.
- A QR code to a PDF or plain-language summary is common (check congress policy on QR
  codes and supplementary materials).
- Disclosures and writing-support acknowledgement on the poster too.

## 4. Manuscript support
1. **Kick-off with authors**: agree target journal, key messages from the data, author
   order, timeline. Record decisions.
2. **Outline** following the reporting guideline and journal's instructions.
3. **Draft** from the clinical study report/statistical outputs, checking every number
   against the source tables. Keep a **data trace** (which table each number came from).
4. **Author review rounds**: comments consolidated, conflicts resolved by authors, not by
   the sponsor's commercial team. Document approvals.
5. **Submission**: cover letter, disclosures, acknowledgement of writing support ("Medical
   writing support was provided by <name> of <agency>, funded by <sponsor>").
6. Response to reviewers: authors decide; the writer drafts.

## 5. Keeping it non-promotional
- No brand names where generic names suffice (journals often require generics).
- No overstated claims, no selective reporting of favourable endpoints.
- Commercial/marketing functions should not direct content; route through medical
  affairs/publications governance.

## Gotchas
- Honorary or ghost authorship — both breach ICMJE/GPP.
- Breaking a congress embargo through a press release or social post.
- Numbers in the abstract that differ from the final analysis.
- Spin in conclusions when the primary endpoint wasn't met.

## Verify
- [ ] Every listed author meets all four ICMJE criteria and approved the final text.
- [ ] Writing support and funding disclosed.
- [ ] Every number traces to a source table.
- [ ] Abstract follows congress format, word limit and embargo/encore rules.
- [ ] Conclusions reflect the primary endpoint; negative results not hidden.
