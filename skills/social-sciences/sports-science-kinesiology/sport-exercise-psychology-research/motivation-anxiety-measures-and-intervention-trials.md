# Motivation and anxiety measures, and intervention trials, in sport and exercise

**Goal:** produce research evidence (not applied consulting) about psychological
factors in sport and exercise, with measures and designs that hold up to scrutiny.

## 1. Choose measures from theory
| Construct | Common instruments | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Exercise motivation (SDT) | BREQ-3 (behavioural regulation in exercise) | Regulations on the autonomy continuum; RAI index is debated — consider modelling regulations separately |
| Sport motivation (SDT) | SMS-II | Factor structure varies across samples; check fit |
| Achievement goals | TEOSQ, AGQ-S | Task/ego or 2×2/3×2 models |
| Competitive state anxiety | CSAI-2 / CSAI-2R | Intensity + **direction** (facilitative/debilitative) scale often added |
| Trait sport anxiety | SAS-2 | Somatic, worry, concentration disruption |
| Self-efficacy | Task-specific scales (Bandura's guide) | Build them for the specific skill |
| Mental toughness | Several competing scales | Construct definition contested |
Check validation evidence for your population (age, level, language) and report
reliability (omega) in your sample — see social-sciences.psychology.psychometrics.

## 2. Measurement timing
State anxiety changes quickly: administer at a fixed time relative to competition
(e.g. 30–60 min before), and note that pre-competition questionnaires intrude on
athletes' routines — keep them brief and agree timing with coaches.
Retrospective recall of pre-competition anxiety is biased.

## 3. Intervention trials
- **RCT** with an attention-control (not just no-treatment) group — contact time and
  expectancy effects are big in psychological interventions.
- Randomise at the right level (team-level → cluster RCT with cluster-adjusted analysis).
- Primary outcome declared in advance (performance, behaviour, or psychological
  variable); register the trial (e.g. ClinicalTrials.gov, ISRCTN, OSF) and report with CONSORT.
- **Manipulation check/fidelity:** did athletes actually practise imagery/self-talk?
  Logs, recordings, quizzes.
- Follow-up beyond post-test — adherence and maintenance effects often fade.
- Exercise adherence studies: objective activity (accelerometers) over self-report,
  which overestimates activity.

## 4. Single-case designs
Common with elite athletes where n is small: **multiple-baseline across participants**
(staggered intervention start), A-B-A-B withdrawal (rarely appropriate for skills that
can't be "unlearned"). Need stable baselines (≥ 3–5 points), visual analysis plus
statistics (e.g. Tau-U, randomisation tests); follow What Works Clearinghouse–type standards.

## 5. Psychophysiological methods
- **HRV:** standardise posture, breathing, time of day and recording length (≥ 5 min
  short-term; ultra-short 60 s RMSSD is used in field settings with caveats). Report
  RMSSD/ln-RMSSD and device validation (chest strap > optical wrist sensors for accuracy).
- **Salivary cortisol:** strong diurnal rhythm — fix sampling times; account for
  awakening response, food, caffeine; 20–30 min lag after a stressor.
- **EEG/eye tracking** in skilled performance (e.g. quiet eye): lab-to-field validity
  issues; report preprocessing pipelines.
Physiological markers are not direct readouts of an emotion — triangulate with self-report and behaviour.

## 6. Ethics and boundaries
Distinguish research from service delivery; athletes may feel coerced by coaches —
recruit independently and keep individual data from selectors. Have referral routes
for clinical issues (e.g. disordered eating, depression).

## Gotchas
- Performance outcomes are noisy; use sport-specific objective measures with known reliability.
- Small, single-team samples with many outcomes invite false positives.
- Uncertain: instrument recommendations evolve; check latest validation papers for revised versions.

## Verify
- [ ] Each measure tied to a theory and validated for the population; sample reliability reported.
- [ ] Timing of state measures standardised.
- [ ] Attention-control group, preregistered primary outcome, CONSORT reporting.
- [ ] Fidelity of intervention measured.
- [ ] Psychophysiological recording conditions standardised and devices validated.
