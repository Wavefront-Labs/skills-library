# Ethnography of speaking and language ideology

**Goal:** document how people actually use language in social life — who may say what,
to whom, how, and with what consequences — and what people *believe* about languages
and ways of speaking.

## 1. Choose speech events, not just words
Identify locally recognised **speech events** (a sermon, a bargaining session, a
gossip circle, a court hearing, a TikTok live). Ask people to name genres; local
genre labels are your first data.

## 2. Hymes' SPEAKING grid
For each event, record:
- **S**etting/scene, **P**articipants (speaker, addressee, overhearers), **E**nds
  (goals, outcomes), **A**ct sequence (form and order), **K**ey (tone: serious,
  mocking), **I**nstrumentalities (channel, language, variety, register),
  **N**orms (of interaction and interpretation), **G**enre.
It is a checklist for fieldnotes, not a theory. Gotcha: filling the grid without
noticing *who is absent or forbidden to speak*.

## 3. Recording naturally occurring talk
- Elicited speech (interviews, word lists) tells you what people think they say;
  recordings of real interaction show what they do. Get both, label which is which.
- Consent covers everyone audible, including bystanders; agree in advance how to
  handle recordings that capture non-consenting people (delete or never transcribe).
- Use a good external mic, record WAV at ≥44.1 kHz / 16-bit, log date, place and
  participants immediately. Video matters for gesture, gaze and turn-taking.
- Keep raw files untouched; work on copies.

## 4. Transcription
Transcription is analysis — your choices (spelling, punctuation, line breaks) shape
the reader's view of speakers. Use ELAN for time-aligned multi-tier transcripts
(speaker tiers, translation tier, gloss tier); Praat for acoustic detail (pitch,
duration). Represent non-standard speech consistently and avoid "eye dialect"
spellings that make speakers look uneducated. A minute of talk can take 10–60
minutes to transcribe depending on detail and languages involved — budget for it.

## 5. Key analytic concepts
- **Indexicality:** a form points to a social identity or stance (a vowel, a slang
  term, an honorific). Track which forms index which personae and who notices.
- **Language ideology:** beliefs about language ("our dialect is lazy", "real men
  speak plainly"). Irvine & Gal's processes: *iconisation* (linguistic feature seen as
  natural sign of a group's essence), *fractal recursivity* (an opposition replayed at
  other levels), *erasure* (ignoring inconvenient variation).
- **Metapragmatic talk:** people commenting on speech ("don't talk to elders like
  that") — the best evidence of norms.
- **Code-switching and register shifts** as social moves, not deficiencies.
- **Verbal art/performance:** heightened responsibility to an audience for display.

## 6. Writing it up
Present transcripts with line numbers, original language and translation in parallel,
and a transcription key. Make claims about specific lines.

*Uncertainty:* recording-format thresholds above are common archival practice (e.g.
language archives); check your target archive's deposit requirements.

## Verify
- [ ] Each analysed event has a SPEAKING-style description and local genre name.
- [ ] Recordings logged, consented, and raw copies preserved.
- [ ] Elicited vs naturally occurring data clearly distinguished.
- [ ] Transcription conventions stated; translations aligned with originals.
- [ ] At least one claim about ideology supported by metapragmatic talk.
