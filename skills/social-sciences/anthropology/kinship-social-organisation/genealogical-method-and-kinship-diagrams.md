# Genealogical method and kinship diagrams

**Goal:** record who is related to whom, what people *call* each other, and how
descent, marriage and residence organise groups — in a notation other anthropologists
can read.

## 1. Collecting genealogies (Rivers' method)
- Start with one person (**Ego**) and work outward: parents, siblings, spouses,
  children, then each parent's siblings and their children, up 3–4 generations.
- Ask with **biological/genealogical** questions ("your mother's brother's children")
  *and* separately record the **local term** used for each person. Never collapse them
  — the gap between the two is the analysis.
- Record per person: name (pseudonymised later), sex/gender, alive/dead, residence,
  marriage(s), clan/lineage, and anything locally significant (land, office).
- Collect from several Egos and cross-check; genealogies are political, and people
  forget or omit low-status, adopted or out-married kin. Note disagreements.
- Ask about **fictive and adoptive kin**, step-relations and divorces explicitly — they
  rarely surface unprompted.

## 2. Standard notation
Diagram symbols: △ male, ○ female, ◇ / □ unknown or other; filled or struck-through
symbol = deceased; `=` (or a bracket underneath) marriage; `≠` or a slashed `=`
divorce; vertical line = descent; horizontal line above siblings = siblinghood. Ego is
usually shaded. Generation levels sit on the same row (G+1, G0, G-1).

Kin-type abbreviations (English convention): F father, M mother, B brother, Z sister,
S son, D daughter, H husband, W wife, e/y elder/younger, ♂s/♀s man-speaking/woman-
speaking. Chain them: **FBD** = father's brother's daughter (a parallel cousin);
**MBD** = mother's brother's daughter (a cross cousin). Gotcha: Z = sister, not S
(son) — a common slip. Some texts use Sb (sibling), P (parent), C (child), E (spouse)
for gender-neutral types.

## 3. Analysing terminology
Group kin types that share one term. Classic cousin-terminology types:
- **Eskimo/lineal:** cousins distinguished from siblings (English).
- **Hawaiian/generational:** all same-generation kin = sibling terms.
- **Iroquois/bifurcate merging:** parallel cousins = siblings; cross cousins distinct.
- **Sudanese/descriptive:** nearly every cousin type has its own term.
- **Crow/Omaha:** skewed across generations (matrilineal/patrilineal emphasis).
Treat these as heuristics — real systems mix, and the typology is critiqued for
Western bias. Report the actual mappings, not just a label.

## 4. Descent, marriage, residence
Separate: descent (patrilineal, matrilineal, cognatic/bilateral, double), group
form (lineage — traceable links; clan — stipulated common ancestor), marriage rules
(exogamy, preferred cross-cousin marriage, bridewealth/dowry), and residence
(patri-/matri-/neolocal). Check stated rules against what people actually do; the
genealogies give you the counts.

## 5. Tools
Hand-drawn diagrams are fine in notebooks; for publication use genealogy software
(GenoPro, or general diagram tools) and keep a spreadsheet of individuals and links.
The open-source PUCK software supports kinship network analysis (*unconfirmed* how
current it is — check before relying on it).

## Ethics
Genealogies expose paternity, adoption and stigma. Store with pseudonyms and avoid
publishing real-name trees without explicit community agreement.

## Verify
- [ ] Each person has both a kin-type label (e.g. MBS) and the local term recorded.
- [ ] At least two Egos collected per household/lineage and discrepancies noted.
- [ ] Diagram uses standard symbols with a legend and marked Ego.
- [ ] Stated rules compared against observed marriages/residences with counts.
- [ ] Real names absent from shared diagrams.
