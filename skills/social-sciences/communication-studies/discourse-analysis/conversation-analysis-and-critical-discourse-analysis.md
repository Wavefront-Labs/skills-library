# Conversation analysis and critical discourse analysis

**Goal:** show how social actions (requesting, blaming, legitimating, excluding) are
accomplished in specific stretches of talk and text, with claims anchored in the data.

These are two different traditions. **CA** builds from participants' own displayed
orientations in recorded interaction and avoids importing outside context unless
participants make it relevant. **CDA** explicitly links texts to power and ideology.
Pick one per study or justify combining them.

## 1. Data
- CA needs **recordings of naturally occurring interaction** (calls, consultations,
  meetings, family dinners), with video when gaze and gesture matter. Interviews are
  interactions too, but analyse them as interviews.
- CDA works on texts and talk: news, policy documents, speeches, social media,
  often built as a corpus with selection criteria stated.

## 2. Jefferson transcription (CA)
Key symbols (Jefferson glossary; check the full list):
- `[ ]` overlap onset/end; `=` latching (no gap);
- `(0.5)` timed pause in seconds; `(.)` micropause under ~0.2 s;
- `:` sound stretch; `-` cut-off; underline/CAPS emphasis/loudness;
- `°quiet°`; `>fast<` `<slow>`; `↑↓` pitch shifts; `.hhh` inbreath; `hh` outbreath;
- `(( ))` transcriber comments; `( )` unclear hearing.
Transcribe in detail only for the extracts you analyse; a rough content log covers the
rest. Expect very slow progress for full detail (often well over 20 minutes per minute
of talk). Tools: ELAN, Transana, or a text editor with a foot pedal and Audacity.

## 3. CA analysis
1. Build a **collection** of instances of a phenomenon (e.g. how doctors deliver bad
   news) across recordings.
2. Analyse each by **sequence**: what action does each turn do, and how does the next
   turn display its understanding (the **next-turn proof procedure**)?
3. Core machinery: **turn-taking** and transition-relevance places; **adjacency pairs**
   (question–answer, offer–acceptance/refusal); **preference organisation** —
   dispreferred responses come with delays, hedges, accounts; **repair** (self-/other-
   initiated); **sequence expansion** (pre-sequences, insert sequences).
4. Check **deviant cases** — instances that don't fit should show participants
   orienting to the norm being broken.
Gotcha: interpreting motives ("she was nervous") instead of showing what the talk does.

## 4. Critical discourse analysis (Fairclough-style three levels)
1. **Text:** vocabulary, transitivity (who does what to whom; agent deletion via
   passives and nominalisations: "jobs were lost"), modality, metaphors, presuppositions.
2. **Discursive practice:** production and consumption — genres, **intertextuality**
   (whose voices are quoted, how), recontextualisation.
3. **Social practice:** relation to institutions, ideology, power.
Wodak's discourse-historical approach adds historical context and argumentation
strategies; van Dijk a socio-cognitive layer. CDA's risk: cherry-picking texts to
confirm a political reading. Mitigate with systematic sampling, corpus-assisted
methods (keywords, collocations) and explicit counter-reading.

## 5. Multimodal discourse
Images, layout and video also mean: Kress & van Leeuwen's grammar of visual design
(gaze, framing, salience, information value of left/right, top/bottom).

## 6. Presenting analysis
Number transcript lines; quote extracts in full; make every claim with reference to
line numbers. Anonymise names, places and voices in recordings.

## Verify
- [ ] Tradition (CA, CDA, multimodal) declared and its assumptions respected.
- [ ] Transcription conventions specified; analysed extracts transcribed in detail.
- [ ] CA claims grounded in next-turn evidence; deviant cases examined.
- [ ] CDA corpus selection criteria stated; text-level features linked to social context.
- [ ] Extracts line-numbered and anonymised.
