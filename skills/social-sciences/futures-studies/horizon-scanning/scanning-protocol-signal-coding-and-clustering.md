# Horizon scanning — protocol, signals, clusters

**Goal:** a systematic, repeatable search for early signs of change whose outputs
(emerging issues) are traceable to evidence and useful to a decision or research question.

Organisational quick-start (weak signals into futures wheels) is in
business-finance.strategy-and-foresight.strategic-foresight.horizon-scanning-weak-signals-and-futures-wheels.
This skill treats scanning as a documented research method.

## 1. Write a scanning protocol
- **Domain and question:** what change matters, for whom ("threats and opportunities for
  adult education provision, 10–20 years").
- **Horizon** and **geography**.
- **Taxonomy** for coding (STEEPLE or domain-specific categories).
- **Source panel** (below), cadence (weekly/monthly), team roles, and inclusion criteria for
  what counts as a signal.
- **Record format** and storage (shared database or spreadsheet; dedicated tools exist).

## 2. Sources: deliberately beyond the mainstream
Mix: preprints and conference proceedings, patent filings, start-up funding news,
regulatory consultations, niche/fringe communities and blogs, art and design, foreign-language
media, practitioner interviews, other foresight units' outputs. A panel of scanners with
different backgrounds catches more than one expert. Mainstream news mostly reports trends
already visible — useful for context, poor for weak signals.

## 3. What counts as a signal
A **weak signal** is an early, often ambiguous indicator of a possible emerging issue. One
useful model (Hiltunen) has three dimensions: the **signal** (the observable), the
**issue** (the change it might indicate), and the **interpretation** (what it means to the
receiver). Record all three separately — the same item means different things to different users.

## 4. Signal record (one per item)
| Field | Example content |
|---|---|
| ID, date found, scanner | S-0412, 2026-03-02, initials |
| Title and one-line summary | — |
| Source and link, source type | preprint / patent / interview… |
| Category (taxonomy) | Technological; Social |
| Possible issue it signals | — |
| Novelty (1–5), potential impact (1–5), evidence strength (1–5) | scored by the scanner |
| Time to mainstream (guess) | <5, 5–10, >10 years |
| Related signals | IDs |
Keep scoring rubrics written down so different scanners score consistently; calibrate on a
shared sample periodically.

## 5. From signals to emerging issues
- **Cluster** related signals (affinity mapping in a workshop; or text-based clustering of
  summaries as a first pass). A cluster with signals from several independent source types
  is stronger.
- Name each cluster as a **change statement** ("Employers bypassing degrees with
  micro-credentials"), with evidence, drivers, counter-signals, and possible implications.
- Prioritise on an impact × uncertainty (or impact × novelty) grid; keep low-scoring items
  in the library — weak signals by definition look unimportant early.

## 6. Automated support
Text mining, topic modelling, bibliometric bursts and patent-class growth help detect volume
changes; LLM-based summarisation speeds triage. They find what is already growing; human
scanning is still needed for novelty and interpretation. Log queries and tool versions for
reproducibility.

## 7. Output and feedback
Short issue briefs per cluster; feed into scenarios
(social-sciences.futures-studies.scenario-planning.scenario-construction-consistency-and-validation)
or trend analysis. Review hits and misses yearly: which issues materialised, which sources
contributed — and drop sources that never do.

## Gotchas
- Confirmation bias: scanners collect what fits their existing view; rotate sources and people.
- Signal inflation: a viral story counted many times from echo sources.
- Without a decision user, scanning becomes a newsletter nobody reads.

## Verify
- [ ] Written protocol: question, horizon, taxonomy, sources, cadence, record format.
- [ ] Source panel includes non-mainstream and non-domain sources.
- [ ] Signals recorded with signal/issue/interpretation separated and scored with a rubric.
- [ ] Clusters stated as change statements with evidence and counter-signals.
- [ ] Periodic retrospective on hits/misses and source value.
