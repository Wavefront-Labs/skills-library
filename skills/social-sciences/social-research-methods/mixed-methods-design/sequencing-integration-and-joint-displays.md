# Mixed-methods design — sequence, integrate, display

**Goal:** a study where the qualitative and quantitative strands answer a question
neither could answer alone, with integration designed in from the start rather than
two parallel reports stapled together.

## 1. Write the mixed-methods question
Besides the qual and quant questions, write one question that needs both, e.g. "To what
extent do patients' accounts of barriers explain the drop in uptake between clinics?"
If you cannot write it, you probably have a multi-method project, not a mixed-methods one.

## 2. Choose a core design
| Design | Sequence | Purpose | Integration point |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Convergent** | QUAN + QUAL at the same time | Compare/corroborate two views of one phenomenon | **Merging** results |
| **Explanatory sequential** | QUAN → qual | Explain survey/experiment results | **Connecting**: quant results select cases and shape the interview guide |
| **Exploratory sequential** | QUAL → quan | Develop an instrument or intervention, then test it | **Building**: themes become items/variables |
Complex designs (intervention trials with embedded qual, multistage evaluations) are built
from these cores. Notation: capitals = priority, arrows = sequence, plus = concurrent
(`QUAN → qual`).

## 3. Sampling across strands
- Explanatory: pick cases from the quant data purposively — extreme residuals, typical
  cases, people who changed most. Record the rule in the protocol.
- Convergent: decide whether qual participants are a subset of the quant sample (allows
  case-level merging) or a separate group (only results-level comparison).
- Exploratory: the quant sample must be independent of qual participants when testing the
  new instrument.

## 4. Integration techniques
- **Merging** — put the results side by side by topic and judge fit.
- **Connecting** — use one strand's results to sample for the other.
- **Building** — turn qual themes into survey items (then pilot them; see
  social-sciences.social-research-methods.survey-design.questionnaire-wording-sampling-and-weighting).
- **Embedding** — collect qual data within a trial to explain implementation.
- **Data transformation** — quantitise qual codes (presence per case) or qualitise quant
  data (profiles) — with caution about what counts mean in purposive samples.

## 5. Joint displays
A table or figure that holds both kinds of evidence and a column of **meta-inferences**.
Example (convergent, by theme):

| Domain | Survey result | Interview theme (illustrative quote) | Fit | Meta-inference |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cost | 62% cite cost as barrier | "It's not the fee, it's the day off work" | Expansion | Cost = lost income more than fees |
| Trust | No difference by arm | Trust built by named nurse in arm B only | Discordance | Measure lacked sensitivity, or effect is local |

Other forms: case-level displays (each row a participant with scores and themes),
statistics-by-theme, side-by-side bar charts annotated with quotes.

## 6. Judge fit honestly
- **Confirmation** — both say the same.
- **Expansion** — they cover different parts of the phenomenon.
- **Discordance** — they disagree: report it, then investigate (re-analyse, collect more,
  question the measure). Discordance is a finding, not a failure.

## Gotchas
- Equal-status claims while the qual strand is five interviews.
- Paradigm statements without consequences; say what your stance changes in practice.
- Integration left to the discussion section — reviewers look for it in methods and results.
- Timelines: sequential designs take longer than people budget; ethics may need an
  amendment for phase 2.

## Verify
- [ ] A mixed-methods question exists that needs both strands.
- [ ] Core design named, notation given, priority justified.
- [ ] Integration point(s) and technique specified in the protocol.
- [ ] Cross-strand sampling rule written down.
- [ ] At least one joint display with explicit meta-inferences.
- [ ] Fit (confirmation/expansion/discordance) stated for each domain.
