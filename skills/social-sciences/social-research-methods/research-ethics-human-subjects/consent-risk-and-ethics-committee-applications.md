# Human-subjects ethics for social research — risk, consent, review, data

**Goal:** an application the committee approves first time, and a study that would
still look right if a participant read every document you wrote about them.

Clinical-trial consent (GCP, regulated products) is covered separately:
health-medicine.clinical-research-operations.informed-consent-and-participant-protection.informed-consent-process-and-documentation.

## 1. Classify the study first
- **Is it research?** Systematic investigation designed to produce generalisable knowledge
  (US definition). Service evaluation and audit may fall outside; get that decision in
  writing from the committee, do not self-certify.
- **Risk level:** *minimal risk* means harm no greater than ordinary daily life. Under the
  US Common Rule, many anonymous surveys, interviews and educational tests qualify for
  **exempt** review; identifiable sensitive data usually need **expedited** review;
  anything above minimal risk goes to the **full board**. Other countries use
  proportionate/low-risk routes with similar logic. Check your institution's categories.
- **Who reviews:** every institution whose staff collect data, plus sites (schools,
  prisons, health services) that often have their own approval.

## 2. Risks social research actually creates
Psychological distress (trauma topics), social/legal harm from disclosure (immigration
status, illegal behaviour, sexuality), economic harm (employer learns views), group
harm (stigmatising findings), and **researcher safety** (lone fieldwork, home visits).
For each: likelihood, severity, mitigation. Write a **distress protocol**: signs to watch,
pause/stop rules, referral list, follow-up.

## 3. Consent
Information sheet (plain language, ~reading age 12 for general public) covering: purpose,
what participation involves and how long, risks and benefits, voluntariness and right to
withdraw (and **until when** data can be withdrawn — e.g. before anonymisation or
publication), confidentiality and its limits (mandatory reporting duties), data storage,
retention and sharing, funder, contacts including an independent complaint route.
- Consent form: separate, initialled boxes for optional elements (audio recording, quotes,
  archiving, recontact).
- **Online/anonymous surveys:** an information page plus "I agree" button, no signature.
- **Oral consent:** script plus recorded or witnessed confirmation, where literacy or
  risk of a signed document argues against writing.
- **Waivers / alteration** (e.g. deception studies, archival data): justify that research is
  impracticable otherwise, risk is minimal and rights are not adversely affected.

## 4. Deception
Allowed only when necessary, risk minimal, no information that would change willingness
to take part withheld, and a **debrief** that explains the deception and offers withdrawal
of data. Many economics journals and labs prohibit deception altogether — check the field norm.

## 5. Vulnerable groups
Children (parental permission + child **assent**, age-appropriate materials), prisoners,
people with impaired capacity (follow local capacity law), people in dependent
relationships (students of the researcher, employees): recruit through a third party and
remove any hint of coercion. Gatekeepers grant access, not consent.

## 6. Data protection
- Lawful basis under GDPR for EU/UK work is typically *public task* or *legitimate
  interests* for research, with a separate Article 9 condition for special-category data
  (health, ethnicity, politics, religion, sexuality) — consent for ethics is not
  automatically the GDPR basis. Confirm with your data protection officer.
- Do a DPIA for large-scale or sensitive data. Pseudonymise at collection; keep the key
  separate; encrypt devices; state retention period.
- "Anonymous" quotes can be identifying in small communities — composite details or
  check-back with participants.
- Online/social media data: public ≠ fair game; follow AoIR guidance on context and
  paraphrase quotes that are searchable.

## Gotchas
- Incentives large enough to be coercive for low-income participants.
- Promising "complete confidentiality" when you have mandatory reporting duties.
- Changing instruments after approval without an amendment.

## Verify
- [ ] Research/not-research and review level decided by the committee in writing.
- [ ] Risk table with mitigations; distress and safety protocols attached.
- [ ] Information sheet states withdrawal deadline and confidentiality limits.
- [ ] Vulnerable-group procedures and gatekeeper letters included.
- [ ] Data management plan: lawful basis, storage, retention, sharing, anonymisation.
- [ ] Amendment process noted for later changes.
