# Compiling a catalogue raisonné entry

**Goal:** produce one entry in the definitive, documented list of an artist's works (or of
one medium of their work) — accurate enough that collectors, museums and courts will rely
on it, and transparent enough that later scholars can correct it.

## 1. Set the scope before you start
Decide and state: which media (paintings only? prints, drawings, sculpture?), date range,
and whether you include workshop, collaborative, lost and destroyed works. Also decide
the policy for **doubtful** and **rejected** works — most modern catalogues list rejected
works with reasons, which saves endless re-submissions.

## 2. Locate the works
- Start from the artist's own records: account books, studio inventories, photographs,
  letters, the estate's archive.
- Dealer stock books and gallery archives, auction catalogues (annotated copies record
  buyers and prices), exhibition catalogues, collection catalogues.
- The Getty Provenance Index and similar databases for older sales.
- Public calls for owners to come forward; many works are in private hands. Expect a
  steady flow of submissions, including fakes.

## 3. Fields in a typical entry
- **Catalogue number** (permanent; keep numbers stable across editions, using suffixes
  like 123a for additions).
- **Title**, with variant titles used historically; **date** (with "c." and the basis);
  **medium and support**; **dimensions** in cm (H × W), and inches if the field expects it.
- **Inscriptions**: signature, date, labels, stamps, stencils on the back — transcribed
  exactly, with location ("lower right").
- **Provenance**: chain of owners from the artist to today, with dates and how each
  transfer happened (sale, gift, inheritance). Gaps are stated, not smoothed over.
  1933–45 European provenance must be checked against looted-art databases.
- **Exhibition history**: chronological, with venue, dates and catalogue number.
- **Literature**: every publication that discusses or reproduces it, with page/plate.
- **Related works**: studies, versions, copies, prints after it.
- **Condition and technical notes** where available.
- **Commentary**: dating argument, subject, attribution reasoning, unresolved questions.
- **Image**: good colour image, plus reverse if informative.

## 4. Attribution and dating decisions
Combine documentation, connoisseurship (comparison with secure works) and technical
evidence (pigment analysis, canvas weave, X-radiography, infrared reflectography). Record
how the decision was made and by whom. Distinguish clearly: "by the artist", "with
workshop", "attributed to", "circle of", "after". Dating usually rests on dated
anchor works plus style and documented exhibition dates — give the logic.

## 5. Legal and practical cautions
- Inclusion or exclusion moves market value by large sums; authors and committees have
  been sued. Get legal advice on disclaimers and on whether to issue opinions at all.
  *(Jurisdiction-specific — I'm not certain of current case law; confirm.)*
- Keep owner anonymity on request ("private collection"), but record identities
  confidentially for your own files.
- Clear image rights and reproduction permissions early; they are slow and costly.
- Online catalogues can be updated — publish a change log and keep superseded data.

## Gotchas
- Old exhibition catalogues sometimes misidentify works or reuse titles; match by
  dimensions, labels and photos, not titles.
- A label on the back proves the stretcher was there, not necessarily the painting.
- Auction houses' provenance lines are often abbreviated or optimistic; verify each link.
- Artists reused titles and painted multiple versions — expect near-duplicates.

## Verify
- [ ] Scope and inclusion/rejection policy are written down.
- [ ] Each entry has physical data, inscriptions, provenance, exhibitions and literature.
- [ ] Provenance gaps are explicitly marked; 1933–45 checked where relevant.
- [ ] Attribution and dating arguments are stated with evidence and decision-makers.
- [ ] Catalogue numbers are stable; changes are logged.
- [ ] Legal advice and image permissions are in place before publication.
