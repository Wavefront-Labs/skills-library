# Managing inward and outward loans

**Goal:** objects move between institutions and come back in the same condition, with
every party's obligations written down and the object's location known at every moment.

## 1. Timeline (work backwards from opening)
Typical lead time for international loans is **12–18 months**; domestic 6–12. Many
lenders refuse requests under six months. Rough sequence: request letter → lender's
curatorial and conservation review → facility report exchange → approval by lender's
board → loan agreement → insurance/indemnity → transport booking → packing → courier →
installation → de-installation → return → closing paperwork.

## 2. Request and facility report
- The borrower sends a formal request naming each object (lender's number), the
  exhibition, venues and dates, and a **standard facility report** (building, security,
  fire suppression, environmental control, handling staff, loading bay, insurance).
- Lenders set conditions: RH/temperature ranges (often around 45–55% RH and 18–22 °C
  for mixed collections, but each object's own requirement wins), light limits (e.g.
  50 lux for works on paper and textiles), case requirements, max display duration.
  Confirm what the venue can *actually* hold, with logger data, before agreeing.

## 3. The loan agreement
Covers: objects, loan period, venues, conditions of display, credit line wording,
photography and reproduction rights, insurance, transport, courier, packing, who pays
for what, cancellation, governing law. Lender's form usually prevails for outgoing loans
from big institutions; read the borrower's terms anyway for conflicts.

## 4. Insurance and indemnity
- **Nail-to-nail** (wall-to-wall): cover from the moment the object leaves the lender's
  wall until it returns. Values are agreed by the lender; check they are current.
- **Government indemnity** schemes (e.g. UK GIS, US Arts and Artifacts Indemnity) can
  replace commercial insurance for eligible borrowers; they have conditions (security,
  approval deadlines, deductibles). Apply early.
- For foreign loans into some countries, ask whether **immunity from seizure** protection
  applies or needs applying for — rules are national.

## 5. Condition, packing and couriers
- **Condition report** at every handover: outgoing at lender, on arrival, at close,
  on return. Same format, photos of known damage, numbered diagrams. Unpack only after
  crates acclimatise (commonly 24 h — follow the lender).
- Packing by specialist fine-art shippers: crate design matched to fragility (cushioning,
  thermal insulation, travel frames).
- **Courier**: lender's staff member accompanies high-value or fragile objects, supervises
  unpacking and installation, signs the condition report. Borrower pays per diems and
  travel; virtual couriering is now sometimes accepted — confirm with the lender.

## 6. Location control
Every move is recorded in the CMS: object, from, to, date, by whom. For inward loans,
create a loan record and temporary location records too — a lent object not in your
system is an object you can lose.

## Gotchas
- Credit line typos annoy lenders more than anything else; get approval in writing.
- Changes of venue or dates after signature need a written amendment and an insurance update.
- Status: figures above (lead times, RH, lux, acclimatisation) are common defaults, not rules.

## Verify
- [ ] Signed agreement and insurance/indemnity certificate exist before the object moves.
- [ ] Facility report conditions match measured gallery conditions.
- [ ] Condition reports exist for every handover, signed both sides.
- [ ] Every movement is logged; object location is known today.
- [ ] Return is signed off and the loan record closed.
