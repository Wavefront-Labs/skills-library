# Due diligence, accessioning and deaccessioning

**Goal:** every object in the permanent collection is there by a documented decision,
with clear legal title and a defensible provenance; every object that leaves goes by an
equally documented, ethical route.

## 1. Object entry is not acquisition
Anything arriving (offered gift, enquiry, loan, purchase on approval) gets an **entry
record and a temporary number** (e.g. `T.2026.014`) and a signed receipt stating it is
*not* yet the museum's. Most collection mess starts with an object left on a shelf for
years with no paperwork: it becomes "found in collection" and nobody can prove title.

## 2. Test against the collecting policy
Your collections development policy (Spectrum calls it an acquisition and disposal
policy; UK Accreditation requires one) says what you collect and why. Ask:
- Does it fit a stated theme, period or geography? Does it duplicate what you hold?
- Can you **store, conserve, insure and document** it for ever? A car, a piano or a
  hazardous object (asbestos, radioactive dial paint, firearms) has real lifetime cost.
- Are there restrictions (donor wants it always displayed, kept together, returned if
  not shown)? Refuse permanent-display conditions; they will be broken.

## 3. Due diligence: provenance and title
- **Ownership history**: get the donor/vendor's written account, documents, photos,
  receipts, export licences. Look for gaps, especially 1933–1945 for European art and
  anything archaeological or ethnographic after **1970** (the UNESCO Convention date
  most codes use as the threshold; some institutions apply 1970 strictly, others later
  national dates — check yours).
- **Databases**: check stolen-art registers (Art Loss Register, Interpol, national
  police lists) and Nazi-era databases (e.g. German Lost Art Foundation) where relevant.
- **Legal title**: the giver must own it and have the right to give it (not the
  executor's cousin; not jointly owned without consent). Natural-history, ivory and
  human-remains items carry extra law (CITES, national acts) — confirm locally.
- Record what you checked, when, and what you found, **including nothing**. "No
  results" with a date is evidence of diligence; silence is not.
If doubt remains, say no. An unclear object is a future claim.

## 4. Decide and transfer title
Take it to the accession body (committee, director, trustees as your policy states).
Minute the decision. Then the **transfer of title**: a signed deed of gift, bill of sale
or bequest documentation, naming the object precisely and assigning copyright/IP where
possible (if the donor holds copyright in an artwork or archive, ask for a licence;
without it you may not be able to photograph it for the web).

## 5. Accession
- Assign the permanent **accession number** (e.g. `2026.14.1` — year.lot.item; use the
  scheme already in your register, never invent a parallel one).
- Enter it in the **accession register** (bound, or a locked CMS register with audit
  trail) — this is the legal record; don't delete entries, annotate them.
- Mark the object, photograph it, create the catalogue record, and record location.

## 6. Deaccessioning and disposal
- Grounds must be in the policy (out of scope, duplicate, beyond repair, hazardous,
  repatriation, legally required). Professional codes (Museums Association, AAM) treat
  **financially motivated disposal** as a serious breach except in narrow cases —
  check the current code before even discussing a sale.
- Check original gift conditions and whether a donor or funder must be consulted.
- Prefer transfer to another public collection; document each step, the method and
  the recipient; keep the record permanently and mark the register "deaccessioned".

## Gotchas
- **Long-term loans** with no paperwork are not gifts. Treat as undocumented loans.
- A donor's tax valuation is their business; don't provide appraisals for gifts you
  receive (conflict of interest under most codes).
- Status of this skill: practice varies by country and governing body; thresholds and
  database names here should be confirmed against your national code.

## Verify
- [ ] Entry record and receipt exist for everything on site that isn't accessioned.
- [ ] Policy fit and lifetime care cost are recorded for the decision.
- [ ] Provenance research and database checks are written up, with dates and results.
- [ ] Signed transfer of title (and IP licence if relevant) is on file.
- [ ] Register entry, number, marking, photo and location are complete.
- [ ] Any disposal cites a policy ground and is permanently recorded.
