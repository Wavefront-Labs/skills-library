# Searching record platforms and vetting hints

**Goal:** find the records that exist online for an ancestor — across several platforms
with different holdings — and accept only those you have verified.

## 1. Know what each platform is good at
*Holdings change constantly; this is a general orientation, not a catalogue.*
- **FamilySearch:** free (account required); huge record collections, many **browse-only**
  image sets not indexed by name, a library **catalogue** of microfilmed records, a
  shared single world tree, and a Research Wiki. Also an experimental **full-text search**
  over machine-read documents (e.g. deeds, probate) — powerful for unindexed material.
- **Ancestry:** largest DNA database; strong US, UK and other collections; private and
  public user trees; hints.
- **findmypast:** strong for UK and Ireland (parish records, newspapers via the British
  Newspaper Archive, 1921 census of England and Wales).
- **MyHeritage:** strong European and Scandinavian content, record matching, DNA.
- Free volunteer sites (FreeBMD, FreeREG, FreeCEN, Find a Grave, BillionGraves, national
  archives' own catalogues) fill gaps.

## 2. Search broad, then narrow
1. Start with **name + approximate year (±2–5) + place** at the broadest level (county/state).
2. Too many results? Add a family member (spouse, parent) or narrow the place.
3. Too few? Remove fields. Then try variants: initials, nicknames (Polly for Mary, Bill for
   William), Latin forms in church records, maiden vs married names, phonetic/Soundex options,
   and **wildcards** (e.g. `Sm*th`; rules on minimum letters vary by site).
4. Search **without a first name** in a known place to catch mis-indexed entries.
5. Search the **collection directly** (card catalogue / record set page) rather than the global
   search; collection-specific fields (e.g. ship name, regiment) appear there.
6. Browse unindexed image sets page by page when an index fails — the record may be there.

## 3. Vetting hints and matches
- A hint is a **suggestion** from an algorithm, often based on other users' trees. Before
  attaching:
  - Open the **image**, not just the transcription; check name, age, place, family members.
  - Ask: does this fit everything else I know? Could it be a same-name person nearby?
  - Classify it (original/derivative, primary/secondary information — see
    `humanities.genealogy-and-family-history.records-research.applying-the-genealogical-proof-standard`).
- Treat other users' trees as **clues** — look at what sources they cite, and verify those.
- Record what you rejected and why, so you don't re-check it next year.

## 4. Cross-platform strategy
- Keep a **master tree off-platform** (see
  `humanities.genealogy-and-family-history.family-tree-software.managing-a-tree-and-gedcom-exchange`) or
  designate one as master; sync deliberately rather than letting hints edit it.
- **DNA:** test at Ancestry (no raw-data upload), then download raw data and upload to
  MyHeritage, FTDNA and GEDmatch where free uploads are offered. *Upload policies and fees
  change; check current terms.*
- Save images and citations locally; subscriptions lapse.

## Gotchas
- Indexes have errors: misread handwriting, wrong ages, shifted lines. If a record "should" exist, look at neighbours or browse.
- FamilySearch's shared tree can be edited by anyone; watch changes to your ancestors.
- Ages in censuses drift; birthplaces change between censuses. Don't over-filter.
- Platforms may restrict images by location (e.g. some FamilySearch images viewable only at
  affiliate libraries).

## Verify
- [ ] Searched at least two platforms plus relevant free sites for each key record type.
- [ ] Every attached record was checked against its image.
- [ ] Rejected hints logged with reasons.
- [ ] Master tree and local copies of images/citations kept outside any single subscription.
