# Context recording and writing the excavation report

**Goal:** produce a record from which a stranger, decades later, could rebuild the site's
sequence — and then publish it so it is actually findable.

## 1. The context sheet
Whatever the form, each context record should give:
- **Number, site code, area, type** (deposit/cut/masonry/timber/skeleton).
- **Description in a fixed order.** For deposits: compaction, colour (Munsell if the project
  uses it — note light conditions), composition (e.g. "silty clay"), inclusions (type, size,
  frequency %), thickness, and how its edges/boundaries look (sharp/clear/diffuse).
  For cuts: shape in plan, corners, break of slope top, sides, break of slope base, base,
  orientation, dimensions.
- **Relationships** (directly above/below/cuts/cut by/same as).
- **Interpretation** — kept separate from description, with a confidence word.
- **Method and conditions:** how dug (trowel/mattock/machine), weather, light, recorder initials, date.
- Cross-references: plan, section, photo, sample and small-find numbers.

Describe first, interpret second. "Dark silty clay with 10% charcoal flecks" survives a
change of mind; "hearth" does not.

## 2. Plans, levels, photos
- Single-context plans at 1:20 on drafting film, or digital equivalents (total station,
  GNSS, or photogrammetry/structure-from-motion orthophotos). Every plan ties to the site
  grid with at least two known points.
- Levels relative to a site **temporary benchmark** tied to a national datum; record the TBM value.
- Photographs: clean, shaded or evenly lit surface; scale bar(s) and north arrow; a photo
  board with site code/context *or* a register entry — but never a board covering the feature.
  Shoot RAW+JPEG or high-quality JPEG; log in a photo register.
- With SfM, place and survey ground control points; keep the project files and the
  control coordinates, not just the orthophoto.

## 3. Registers must reconcile
Context, plan, section, photo, sample, small-find and drawing registers. At the end of each
week check: every number issued is used or voided; every sheet referenced exists. Gaps found
after backfill are permanent.

## 4. From archive to report
1. **Assessment** (post-excavation assessment): quantify what you have — contexts, finds by
   class, samples — say what each can tell you, and propose the analysis and budget. (UK
   MoRPHE structure; other jurisdictions differ.)
2. **Analysis:** specialist reports, phased matrix, dating.
3. **Report structure:** summary; background and aims; methods (including what wasn't
   dug and why); results by phase; specialist reports; discussion against the research
   aims; archive location; references; figures (location map, site plan, phase plans,
   key sections, finds illustrations).
4. **Deposition:** archive to the receiving museum/repository in their format; register the
   report in the national index (e.g. OASIS in the UK/Ireland). Commercial "grey literature"
   reports are only useful if they are registered and findable.

## Gotchas
- Digital-only records need format choices that last: PDF/A, TIFF, CSV, open GIS formats;
  proprietary project files alone are a risk.
- Report the negative: areas with no archaeology, and methods limits (machine stripped too deep, flooding).
- Living-people data (landowners, finders) stays out of public reports.
- Repositories often refuse unsorted or unlabelled boxes; check their deposition standard
  before excavation begins, not after.

## Verify
- [ ] Each context sheet separates description from interpretation and cites plan/photo/sample numbers.
- [ ] All registers reconcile; no orphan or missing numbers.
- [ ] Every plan and level ties to the grid and a recorded datum.
- [ ] Report states methods, limits and archive location; registered in the relevant index.
- [ ] Archive formats meet the receiving repository's standard.
