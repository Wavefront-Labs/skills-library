# Skeletal inventory and age/sex estimation

**Goal:** produce a standard, comparable record of an archaeological skeleton — what is
present, who they may have been, and what happened to them — while treating the remains
with the respect that law and descendant communities require.

## 0. Ethics and law first
- Excavation of human remains needs the right licence/permit (e.g. a Ministry of Justice
  licence in England and Wales; NAGPRA consultation for Native American remains in the US;
  equivalents elsewhere). *Requirements vary and change; confirm locally.*
- If remains could be **recent** (under ~70–100 years, clothing, dental work, plastic) stop:
  it is a police/coroner matter.
- Follow descendant-community and repository policies on photography, sampling and
  destructive analysis (aDNA, isotopes, radiocarbon). Ask before sampling, not after.

## 1. Inventory and preservation
- Record every element present by side, with completeness (e.g. >75%, 25–75%, <25%) on a
  skeletal diagram plus a table. Teeth on a dental chart (FDI or Universal — state which).
- Record surface preservation (e.g. a 0–5 grade such as McKinley's), fragmentation, and
  taphonomic damage (root etching, gnawing, staining, excavation damage — new breaks are
  lighter-coloured).
- Commingled deposits: count **MNI** from the most frequent unique element by side and age.

## 2. Sex estimation (adults only)
- **Pelvis** is most reliable: Phenice traits (ventral arc, subpubic concavity, ischiopubic
  ramus ridge), greater sciatic notch width, preauricular sulcus.
- **Skull**: nuchal crest, mastoid process, supraorbital margin, glabella, mental eminence —
  scored 1–5 per Buikstra & Ubelaker.
- Report as female / probable female / indeterminate / probable male / male. Pelvis
  outweighs skull when they disagree. Do not sex juveniles from morphology.
- This is **biological sex estimation**; it says nothing about gender identity in life.

## 3. Age at death
- **Juveniles:** dental formation and eruption (e.g. AlQahtani's London Atlas), diaphyseal
  lengths, epiphyseal fusion. These are the most precise ages you will get.
- **Adults:** pubic symphysis (Suchey–Brooks phases), auricular surface (Lovejoy/Buckberry–Chamberlain),
  sternal rib ends, cranial suture closure (weak). Combine methods; report broad categories
  (e.g. young adult 18–25, middle 26–35, mature 36–45, older 46+). Adult methods
  systematically **underage older people** — "46+" is often all you can honestly say.

## 4. Stature and pathology
- Stature from complete long bones (femur best) with a regression formula **matched to the
  population** (Trotter & Gleser equations are common; note which table). Give the ± range.
- Palaeopathology: describe the lesion (location, distribution, bone formation vs destruction,
  healed or active) *before* offering a differential diagnosis. Dental disease (caries, calculus,
  enamel hypoplasia) is recorded routinely.

## Gotchas
- Lift skeletons into labelled bags by body region; small hand and foot bones get lost on site.
- Never wash fragile bone with brushes under running water; dry-brush or damp-sponge.
- An "osteological paradox": a cemetery is a sample of the dead, not the living population.
  Frailty and selection bias distort mortality and disease rates.

## Verify
- [ ] Legal authority and community/repository permissions documented.
- [ ] Inventory diagram + table, dental chart system stated, preservation graded.
- [ ] Sex and age methods named; results given as ranges/categories with confidence.
- [ ] Stature formula and population reference stated.
- [ ] Lesions described before diagnosed; sampling approvals on file.
