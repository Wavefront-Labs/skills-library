# Classifying and quantifying pottery and lithics

**Goal:** turn bags of sherds and flakes into comparable data — what they are, how many,
when, and what they were used for — so assemblages can date contexts and be compared
between sites.

## 1. Processing before analysis
- Wash pottery gently with a soft brush in clean water — **not** if residues, paint, or
  friable/low-fired fabrics are present, and never wash material set aside for residue,
  lipid or use-wear analysis. Dry slowly out of direct sun.
- Mark or label each item (or bag) with site code + context; keep the context link absolute.
- Lithics for use-wear: don't wash vigorously, don't mark the edges, handle minimally.

## 2. Pottery: fabric, form, decoration
- **Fabric** from a fresh break, under ×10–×20 magnification: inclusion type, size,
  frequency and sorting; firing colour sequence in the core/margins. Assign to the regional
  type series code if one exists rather than inventing names.
- **Form** from rims, bases, handles — rim diameter with a rim chart, and the percentage of
  circumference present.
- Record decoration, surface treatment, sooting/residue, and joins between contexts
  (cross-context joins are stratigraphic evidence).

### Quantify more than one way
| Measure | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|
| Sherd count | Quick | Biased by breakage — fragile wares look more common |
| Weight | Quick | Biased toward thick, heavy wares |
| Rim EVE (sum of rim % / 100) | Least biased for comparing types | Needs rims; slower |
| MNV (minimum vessels) | Intuitive for small assemblages | Subjective, does not scale |
The pottery standard recommends at least count and weight per fabric per context, with
EVEs where resources allow.

## 3. Lithics: technology first, then typology
- Sort into **debitage** (flakes, blades, chips, cores) and **tools** (retouched pieces).
  Record raw material, cortex %, platform type, termination, dorsal scar pattern, burning, patination.
- Blade vs flake: a blade is at least twice as long as wide with parallel sides/arrises — a
  rough technological indicator, not a date by itself.
- Reconstruct the **chaîne opératoire** (reduction sequence): are cores being worked on site,
  or are finished tools arriving? Refits are gold — try them on concentrated scatters.
- Classify tools with the regional typology; note use-wear only from microscope study,
  not from eyeballing edge damage (plough and trampling damage mimic use).

## 4. Dating and interpretation
- A context is dated by its **latest** securely stratified type (terminus post quem), tempered
  by residuality — a context full of abraded Roman sherds with one fresh medieval sherd is medieval.
- Record **abrasion** and sherd size: fresh large sherds suggest primary deposition;
  small abraded ones suggest reworking.
- **Seriation** orders assemblages by changing type frequencies; it gives relative order
  only and needs independent anchors.

## Gotchas
- Mixing specialists' type codes across regions silently breaks comparisons; cite the series.
- Don't throw away "undiagnostic" body sherds before they're counted and weighed.
- Burnt flint and fire-cracked rock: count and weigh even if discarded; they mark activity.
- Uncertain identifications should be recorded as such ("?Samian") — not promoted to certain.

## Verify
- [ ] Every item still traceable to its context.
- [ ] Pottery quantified by at least count and weight per fabric per context.
- [ ] Fabric codes cite a published type series.
- [ ] Lithics recorded for technology (not just tool type); refits attempted where dense.
- [ ] Context dates state the latest type and a residuality judgement.
