# Underwater wreck survey and recording

**Goal:** find, map and record a submerged site to the same standard as one on land, under
time limits set by air, cold and visibility — and without destroying it in the process.

## 0. Law, permits, safety
- Most countries protect wrecks and submerged sites; designated sites often need a licence
  even to dive on them. The UNESCO 2001 Convention's Annex sets the professional norm:
  in-situ preservation first, no commercial exploitation. *Check the specific national regime.*
- Commercial/scientific diving falls under occupational diving regulations in many
  countries (e.g. HSE Diving at Work in the UK), which differ from recreational rules.
  Dive planning, a supervisor and a dive log are not optional. **This skill does not teach diving.**

## 1. Finding sites (remote sensing)
- **Side-scan sonar:** acoustic image of the seabed; good for upstanding wrecks. Run lines
  with ~50–100% overlap; shadows give height.
- **Multibeam echosounder:** bathymetry — gives a 3D surface of the wreck mound.
- **Magnetometer:** ferrous material (anchors, cannon, iron hulls); towed close to the bed.
- **Sub-bottom profiler:** buried structure and sediment layers.
- Target lists from geophysics are then checked by divers or ROV, graded by confidence.

## 2. Diver survey techniques
- **Baseline-offset:** lay a tape baseline along the site, measure perpendicular offsets to
  points. Quick, but errors grow with offset length; keep offsets short and right-angles honest.
- **Trilateration (DSM — Direct Survey Method):** measure distances from several fixed
  control points to each point; software (e.g. Site Recorder — *current availability
  unconfirmed*) computes positions and reports error. Better accuracy, slower.
- **Drawing frames** (gridded 1 m or 2 m) for detailed plans; drafting film and pencil work underwater.
- Write slates in pencil; transcribe within hours of surfacing, while memory is fresh.

## 3. Underwater photogrammetry
- Overlap ~70–80% forward and ~60% side, shot in a regular lawnmower pattern at a fixed
  height. Use scale bars and control targets fixed and measured on site.
- Shoot in the clearest water and best light; avoid stirring sediment (good buoyancy,
  frog kick). Colour-correct consistently or keep RAW.
- Models are only as accurate as their control — record the control network and error.

## 4. Excavation and recovery
- **Airlifts** (rising air-driven suction) in deeper water, **water dredges** in shallow;
  discharge through a sieve/spoil bag so finds are caught. Work stratigraphically like on land.
- **Waterlogged finds** (wood, leather, textile, rope) must stay wet from seabed to lab: lift
  in water-filled containers, keep cool and dark, have a conservator and treatment budget
  (e.g. PEG impregnation, freeze-drying) agreed **before** lifting. Iron concretions need
  conservation too; don't break them open.
- If there is no conservation plan, record in situ and leave it.

## Gotchas
- Visibility and current vary hourly; plan short, specific tasks per dive.
- Tidal sites: work windows around slack water; record tide heights to correct depths.
- Sand moves: a wreck exposed this season may be buried next; monitor rather than assume.
- Don't publish exact coordinates of vulnerable unprotected wrecks — looting follows.
