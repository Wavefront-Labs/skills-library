# Single-context excavation and the Harris matrix

**Goal:** take a site apart in the reverse order it was put together, one stratigraphic unit
at a time, so the sequence can be rebuilt on paper after the ground is gone. Excavation
destroys; the record is the only thing that survives.

## 1. Units: deposits and cuts
- A **context** is a single action in the past: a deposit (layer, fill, wall, floor) or a
  **cut** (the interface left by digging a pit, ditch, posthole). Cuts get their own number
  even though they have no volume.
- A pit is therefore at least two contexts: the cut and one or more fills. Number the cut
  first in your head, but excavate the fills top-down.
- Give a number only when you can define the edges. Uncertain? Record "possibly same as
  [n]" rather than merging or splitting by guesswork.

## 2. The excavation cycle
1. **Clean** the area with a trowel (pulling the edge towards you, blade near-flat) until
   colour and texture changes are visible. Damp the surface if it dries out — many edges
   only show when wet. Shade the surface; harsh sun flattens contrast.
2. **Define** the latest context(s): the ones with nothing on top of them. Plan before you dig.
3. **Record** (sheet, plan, levels, photo — see
   `humanities.archaeology.site-recording-and-reporting.context-recording-and-writing-the-excavation-report`).
4. **Remove** that context only. Bag finds by context number. Take samples per the sampling
   strategy (see `humanities.archaeology.environmental-archaeology.environmental-sampling-and-flotation`).
5. **Record the surface revealed**, then repeat.

Features cut into natural are often **half-sectioned**: dig one half, draw the section,
then remove the other half (or not, per the project design). Draw sections at 1:10;
plans commonly 1:20.

## 3. Relationships and the Harris matrix
For every context, record what is **directly** above and below it, and what cuts or is cut
by it. Only direct physical relationships — not "it's earlier than everything in the pit".

Building the matrix:
- Each context is a box; a line joins it to the context *immediately* below.
- Remove **redundant** links: if A>B and B>C, don't also draw A>C.
- "Same as" contexts (one layer split by a wall) are linked as equivalents.
- Then group into **sub-groups / groups / phases** for interpretation — keep the raw matrix
  separate from the phased one.

Software helps (e.g. Stratify, Harris Matrix Composer, or matrix tools in recording systems)
but check it: it will happily draw a loop if you entered a contradiction. *Tool names are
examples; availability and maintenance status unconfirmed.*

## Gotchas
- **Loops** (A above B above A) mean a recording error — resolve on site while you can still look.
- **Residual** finds (old pottery redeposited in a later fill) are common; **intrusive** finds
  (later material pushed down by roots, animals, posts) less so but deadly for dating. A
  context dates no earlier than its *latest* reliable find.
- Don't dig in spits (arbitrary 10 cm levels) where stratigraphy is visible; spits are for
  homogenous deposits only, and must be declared.
- Over-cutting a feature edge destroys the very relationship you need. Leave a little, then trim.
- Spoil heaps too near the edge collapse into the trench; keep at least 1 m back, more for deep cuts,
  and shore or step trenches over about 1.2 m deep (local safety law decides the exact figure).

## Verify
- [ ] Every context has a unique number, type (cut/deposit) and direct relationships recorded.
- [ ] Cuts are numbered separately from their fills.
- [ ] Matrix has no loops and no redundant links; all contexts appear.
- [ ] Sections and plans tie to the site grid and levels datum.
- [ ] Finds and samples are bagged to a single context, never "from the pit".
