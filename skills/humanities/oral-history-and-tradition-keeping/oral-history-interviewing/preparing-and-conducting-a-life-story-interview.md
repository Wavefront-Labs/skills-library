# Preparing and conducting a life-story interview

**Goal:** record a 1–3 hour interview in which the narrator tells their own story in their
own words, with audio good enough to archive and share, and the narrator glad they did it.

## 1. Prepare
- **Background research**: the places, events, work and community the narrator lived
  through. You need enough to follow up, not to lecture.
- **Pre-interview** (phone or visit, not recorded or with permission): explain the project,
  how the recording will be used and archived, the consent/release process, and agree time
  and place. Learn names and dates to get right. Don't let them tell the best stories now —
  they'll be flat second time.
- Send the consent information in advance (see
  `humanities.oral-history-and-tradition-keeping.consent-and-ethics.informed-consent-release-and-restrictions`).

## 2. Question design
Build a **guide**, not a script: themes in rough chronological order (childhood, family,
school, work, community, key events, reflection).
- **Open questions**: "Tell me about…", "What was it like…", "Describe a typical day…",
  "How did you feel when…".
- **Avoid** yes/no, leading ("It must have been awful?"), double-barrelled and jargon questions.
- Prepare **follow-ups**: "What happened next?", "Can you give me an example?", "Who else was
  there?", "How do you know that?" (gently, for hearsay vs witnessed).
- End with reflection: "Looking back…", "What would you want people to know?" and "Is there
  anything I haven't asked that I should have?"

## 3. Recording setup
- **Recorder**: a dedicated field recorder (or a good phone app with external mic). Record
  **WAV, 24-bit/48 kHz** (or at least 16-bit/44.1 kHz) — uncompressed for the archive.
  *Note:* check your archive's preferred specification.
- **Microphones**: lavalier mics on narrator and interviewer give the cleanest sound;
  otherwise a mic close to the narrator (~30 cm), on a stand, not on the table being tapped.
- **Room**: soft furnishings, away from fridges, clocks, traffic, TVs, pets. Turn off
  phones (or flight mode). Sit at a slight angle, close.
- **Test** levels on a short chat; peaks around −12 dB; wear headphones to monitor.
- Record a **spoken label** at the start (who, where, date, project) and 30 s of room tone.
- Spare batteries and cards; back up the file the same day to two places.
- Video adds richness and burden (lighting, consent for image, storage); decide deliberately.

## 4. Listening technique
- **Let silence work**: wait 5+ seconds after an answer; narrators often add the important part.
- Minimal verbal encouragement on tape ("mm" overlaps speech); nod instead.
- Follow the narrator's thread; return to your guide later. The interview is theirs.
- Note names and terms to clarify spelling at the end, rather than interrupting.
- Watch fatigue: break every hour or so; two sessions is often better than one long one.

## 5. Difficult memories
- Let narrators control depth; say beforehand that they can skip any question, pause or stop.
- If distress arises: pause, acknowledge, offer a break or stopping — don't push for detail.
- Don't act as therapist; have information on support services ready where topics are
  traumatic (war, abuse, displacement).
- Look after yourself too — interviewers absorb distress; debrief with a colleague.
- Memory is constructed: contradictions and "errors" are data about meaning (Portelli), but
  note factual claims to check separately.

## 6. Close
Thank them; complete the release form (or confirm when you will); note restrictions they
ask for; arrange to return a copy. Write an **interview summary/log** the same day.

## Gotchas
- Talking too much — the commonest interviewer fault. Listen back to your first interviews.
- Recording in a café or kitchen with a fridge hum.
- Losing the file: no backup.
- Forgetting the release; the recording may then be unusable.

## Verify
- [ ] Pre-interview done and consent information sent in advance.
- [ ] Guide uses open questions and has follow-ups and a closing reflection.
- [ ] Audio is WAV, levels checked, spoken label and room tone recorded.
- [ ] Narrator was told they can skip, pause or stop; support info on hand for difficult topics.
- [ ] Release completed, restrictions noted, file backed up twice the same day.
