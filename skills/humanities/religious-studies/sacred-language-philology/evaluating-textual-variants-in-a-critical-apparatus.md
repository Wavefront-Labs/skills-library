# Evaluating textual variants in a critical apparatus

**Goal:** when manuscripts of a sacred text disagree, decide which reading is most likely
earliest (or best explains the others), and report the decision so a reader can check
it. This is the groundwork for any serious translation or exegesis.

## 1. Learn to read the apparatus
A critical edition prints a main text plus footnotes (the apparatus) listing variant
readings and the witnesses supporting each, in compressed sigla. Before anything else,
read the edition's introduction: sigla for manuscripts, versions (translations) and
church fathers/early citations; symbols for omission, addition, transposition; and what
the apparatus includes (a **selective** apparatus omits many variants). Examples:
Nestle–Aland for the Greek New Testament, BHS/BHQ for the Hebrew Bible, Göttingen editions
for the Septuagint. *Exact sigla vary by edition — always use the key for the edition
in front of you.*

## 2. Gather the evidence for one variation unit
Write out each reading in full, with its supporting witnesses, dates and families. Look up
anything significant in the manuscripts themselves where digitised (many major codices
are online) — apparatus entries occasionally err.

## 3. External evidence — who supports each reading
- **Date** of the witnesses — but an early manuscript can copy a corrupt text, and a late
  one a good text. Date of the *text*, not just the parchment, matters.
- **Families/text types**: group manuscripts by shared readings (in NT studies, the
  traditional Alexandrian, Western, Byzantine groupings; Hebrew Bible work weighs the
  Masoretic text against the Dead Sea Scrolls, the Samaritan Pentateuch and the Septuagint's
  Hebrew source). Witnesses are weighed, not counted — 500 copies of one exemplar are
  one voice.
- **Geographical spread** of independent support.
- **Stemmatics** (Lachmann's method) reconstructs a family tree from shared errors where
  transmission was mostly closed; contaminated traditions (copyists using several
  exemplars) resist this. The CBGM, now used for the Editio Critica Maior of the NT,
  models coherence between witnesses computationally.

## 4. Internal evidence — which reading explains the others
- **Transcriptional probability** — what would a scribe likely do?
  - Unintentional: haplography (writing once what should be twice), dittography
    (writing twice), homoeoteleuton (eye skipping between similar endings), confusion
    of similar letters (e.g. Hebrew dalet/resh), mishearing in dictation.
  - Intentional: smoothing grammar, harmonising with parallel passages, clarifying,
    doctrinal correction.
- Canons: the **harder reading** (*lectio difficilior*) is often preferred because scribes
  tended to smooth difficulties; the **shorter reading** (*lectio brevior*) was
  traditionally preferred, though recent studies suggest scribes omitted more than
  assumed, so this canon is now weaker.
- **Intrinsic probability** — what fits the author's style, vocabulary and argument.
- The best reading is the one that best explains how all the others arose.

## 5. Report the decision
State the variation unit, readings, evidence, your choice, and a confidence grade (some
editions use A–D ratings). For translation, footnote significant variants ("Other ancient
authorities read…").

## Traditions with different textual cultures
- **Qur'an**: distinguish manuscript variants (early codices, palimpsests such as the
  Sanaa manuscript) from the canonical readings (*qirāʾāt*) transmitted with chains of
  authority — they are different phenomena.
- **Buddhist and Hindu texts** circulated in multiple languages and recensions with strong
  oral transmission; "original text" may be the wrong target. Some editors aim for a
  recension or a stage rather than an archetype.

## Gotchas
- The main text of a critical edition is a hypothesis, not a manuscript.
- Don't use a single canon mechanically; they conflict.
- Confessional stakes can bias choices — note when a variant is doctrinally loaded.
- *I haven't verified current consensus on every canon; check the latest handbook.*

- [ ] Witnesses were weighed by family and independence, not counted.
- [ ] Each reading's origin is explained by a plausible scribal process.
