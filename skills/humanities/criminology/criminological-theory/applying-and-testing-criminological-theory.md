# Applying and testing criminological theory

**Goal:** use theories of crime as scholarly tools — to explain a pattern, generate testable
predictions, or critique policy assumptions — rather than as labels dropped into an essay.
This is academic social science; it is not guidance on policing or investigation.

## 1. Know the main families and what each explains
- **Strain / anomie** (Merton; Agnew's General Strain Theory) — crime as a response to
  blocked goals or negative experiences and emotions (anger, frustration).
- **Social learning / differential association** (Sutherland; Akers) — crime learned
  through association with others who define it favourably.
- **Social control / bonds** (Hirschi 1969) — asks why people *don't* offend: attachment,
  commitment, involvement, belief. **Self-control theory** (Gottfredson & Hirschi 1990)
  — low self-control formed early in life.
- **Labelling** (Lemert, Becker) — official reactions to rule-breaking produce deviant
  identities and further deviance (secondary deviance).
- **Rational choice / routine activities** (Cornish & Clarke; Cohen & Felson) — crime
  occurs when a motivated offender meets a suitable target without capable guardianship;
  underpins situational prevention research.
- **Life-course and developmental** (Sampson & Laub's age-graded informal control;
  Moffitt's adolescence-limited vs life-course-persistent) — explains onset, persistence
  and desistance over a life.
- **Critical, feminist, and green/cultural criminologies** — question who defines crime,
  how power, gender and race shape law and enforcement.
Each answers a different question (why individuals offend; why rates vary; why acts are
criminalised). Match the theory to your question.

## 2. Derive testable propositions
Turn the theory into claims that could be wrong. E.g. GST: "Adolescents experiencing
victimisation will report more delinquency, and the effect will be partly mediated by
anger." State the mechanism, not just the correlation.

## 3. Operationalise
Specify measures: self-report delinquency scales, official records, validated instruments
(e.g. the Grasmick et al. self-control scale), neighbourhood measures (collective
efficacy). Name the dataset (e.g. Add Health, NLSY, the Pathways to Desistance study, the
Cambridge Study in Delinquent Development) and check whether its measures actually capture
the theory's concepts. *Dataset contents are from memory — confirm variables in the
codebook.*

## 4. Design and analysis considerations
- Cross-sectional data can't establish temporal order; longitudinal designs are preferred
  for causal claims.
- Watch for **selection** (e.g. "peers cause delinquency" vs delinquent youths choosing
  delinquent peers) — consider fixed effects, instrumental variables or
  quasi-experiments.
- Test competing theories in the same model where possible (strain vs control vs
  learning), rather than confirming one in isolation.
- Report effect sizes and uncertainty, not just significance.

## 5. Critique and integrate
Evaluate: scope (which crimes, which populations), empirical support (meta-analyses where
available, e.g. Pratt & Cullen 2000 on self-control), falsifiability, policy implications
and unintended consequences. Consider integrated theories but beware of models so broad
they explain nothing.

## Gotchas
- Theories built on samples of urban boys in the US or UK may not generalise to women,
  other countries or white-collar crime.
- Official data measure enforcement as much as offending (see crime-data skill).
- Don't treat a theory's popularity as evidence; check the meta-analytic record.
- Avoid individualising explanations that ignore structural conditions — and vice versa.

## Verify
- [ ] The theory chosen matches the question (individual, rate, or criminalisation).
- [ ] Propositions specify a mechanism and could be falsified.
- [ ] Measures are tied to the theory's concepts and checked in the codebook.
- [ ] Design addresses temporal order and selection.
- [ ] At least one competing theory is tested or discussed.
- [ ] Scope limits and policy implications are stated.
