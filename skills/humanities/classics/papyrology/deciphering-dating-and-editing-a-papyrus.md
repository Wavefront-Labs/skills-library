# Deciphering, dating and editing a papyrus

**Goal:** turn a fragment into a published text: what it physically is, what it says
(with honest uncertainty), when and where it was written, and how it relates to
known texts. Transcription signs follow the Leiden conventions described in
humanities.classics.epigraphy.squeezes-leiden-conventions-and-inscription-editing.

## 1. Handle and image safely
- Papyrus is brittle; most collection pieces are mounted between glass or in frames.
  Don't remove mounts, flatten folds or separate layers yourself — that's a
  conservator's job (humidification, unfolding).
- Work from **high-resolution images** (600+ ppi, colour scale), and request
  **multispectral/infrared** images for faded or carbonised ink — infrared often
  reveals carbon ink invisible in visible light.
- Check papyri.info and the holding institution's catalogue: it may already be
  published, or part of a known roll.

## 2. Describe the physical object
- Dimensions (cm), colour, condition, margins.
- **Fibre direction**: write side "along the fibres" (→) or "across the fibres" (↓).
  Literary rolls are normally written on the side with horizontal fibres (recto) first;
  reuse on the back (verso) is common and can date the front.
- **Kolleseis** (joins between sheets) and their spacing; column widths and line
  spacing; margins; sheet height. For literary texts, reconstruct column layout to
  estimate how much is lost.

## 3. Decipher
- Identify the type: **documentary** (letter, contract, receipt, petition, account —
  usually cursive, abbreviated, formulaic) or **literary/subliterary** (bookhand,
  separate letters, often a known author).
- Make a letter-by-letter **diplomatic transcript** first; build an alphabet table of
  this scribe's forms from secure readings and use it for doubtful ones.
- Documents follow formulae: search **papyri.info** (DDbDP texts) for phrase parallels
  of the type (e.g. a lease's clause order). For literary texts, search the TLG for
  strings of secure letters; many fragments are identified this way.
- Mark every uncertain letter with an underdot; don't read what you expect.

## 4. Date and place
- Documents often date themselves: regnal years, months, consular or indiction dates —
  convert carefully (Egyptian, Macedonian, Roman calendars; regnal year conventions).
- Undated pieces: compare the hand with **securely dated documentary hands** (dated
  documents in plate volumes and online collections). Give a range (e.g. "late II–
  early III AD"); literary hands are harder to date than documentary ones.
- Provenance: archaeological record if any; otherwise internal evidence (place names,
  officials). Acquisitions via the antiquities market need provenance scrutiny —
  some recent "discoveries" turned out to be forged or illicit.

## 5. Publish
Standard edition layout: heading (inventory number, dimensions, provenance, date),
introduction (physical and palaeographic description, content, date), text with line
numbers, apparatus (corrections, alternative readings), translation, line-by-line
commentary. Cite with the **Checklist** abbreviations (`P.Oxy. LXXIV 4970`,
`BGU I 27`) and give **Trismegistos (TM)** numbers. Corrections to published texts go
to the **Berichtigungsliste**.

## Gotchas
- Images on screen exaggerate or hide ink; check doubtful readings on the original if
  allowed.
- Abbreviations and symbols in documentary texts are many; use the papyri.info lists.
- Status: draft; handling and imaging rules follow each collection's policy.

## Verify
- [ ] Physical description includes fibres, kolleseis and dimensions.
- [ ] Diplomatic transcript precedes the edited text; uncertain letters dotted.
- [ ] Formulaic and literary parallels searched (papyri.info, TLG).
- [ ] Date argued from internal dating or named dated parallels, as a range.
- [ ] Checklist citation, TM number and inventory number given; provenance checked.
