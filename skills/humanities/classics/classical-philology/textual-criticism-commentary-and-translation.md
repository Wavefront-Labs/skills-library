# Textual criticism, commentary and translation

**Goal:** read an ancient text as a reconstructed object — know where the words come
from, judge doubtful readings, and explain the passage in a commentary note or a
translation that a specialist would trust.

## 1. Start with the right edition
Use the current **critical edition** (Oxford Classical Texts, Teubner, Budé, or a major
single-author edition) — not a Loeb or online text — for anything you cite. Check
*L'Année philologique* for newer editions and textual articles. Perseus/TLG texts are
often older editions; use them for searching, then verify in the critical edition.

## 2. Read the apparatus criticus
- The **conspectus siglorum** (list of sigla) at the front tells you which letter is
  which manuscript (e.g. `M` = Codex Mediceus), family symbols (`ω` = consensus of
  the main family), and date.
- A typical entry: `23 ἔβη Ω : ἔφη P, Bentley` — line 23, the printed reading `ἔβη`
  is in Ω; `P` and the scholar Bentley have `ἔφη`. Latin abbreviations: `codd.`
  (manuscripts), `del.` (deleted by), `coni.` (conjectured), `add.` (added), `om.`
  (omitted), `fort.` (perhaps), `recte` (correctly).
- Signs in the text: `⟨ ⟩` editorial addition, `[ ]` deletion (in some editions;
  check the preface), `† †` (**obeli**/crux) — corrupt, no convincing remedy.

## 3. Weigh readings
- **Stemma** (Lachmann's method): readings descend from an archetype; shared errors
  group manuscripts. Agreement of independent branches outweighs number of copies.
- Internal criteria, used together and with caution:
  - **lectio difficilior potior** — the harder reading is likelier original (scribes
    simplify), but nonsense is not "difficult".
  - **usus scribendi** — what the author normally writes (check with TLG/PHI searches).
  - **palaeographical plausibility** — how one reading could become the other (letter
    confusion in minuscule or uncial, eye-skip/saut du même au même).
  - **metre** — a reading that breaks the metre is suspect.
- A conjecture should explain the corruption as well as make sense.

## 4. Scan
Mark long and short syllables (length by nature or position), find caesurae, then check
the metre: dactylic hexameter (epic), elegiac couplet, iambic trimeter (tragic
dialogue), lyric metres (consult a metrical handbook). Metre often settles a textual
question or shows emphasis.

## 5. Write a commentary note
Format: line number, **lemma** (the word(s) in bold or followed by `]`), then the note.
```
4 ἀμφὶ δέ] "and around (him)": adverbial ἀμφί, common in Homer (LSJ s.v. A.I);
cf. Il. 2.41. The variant ἀμφὶ δ' ἄρ' (P) is metrically equivalent but unattested in...
```
Cover in order of need: text, grammar/syntax, meaning of words (LSJ, OLD, *TLL*),
parallels, realia, literary point. Cite by standard references (author, work, book.line).

## 6. Scholarly translation
Accuracy first: tense and aspect, particles (Greek μέν/δέ, γάρ), word order emphasis.
Keep difficult readings visible with a note rather than translating around them. State
which edition you translate.

## Gotchas
- Online texts silently omit apparatus and may reproduce outdated readings.
- Line numbering differs across editions (especially prose: use standard Stephanus,
  Bekker or chapter.section references).
- Status: draft; abbreviation lists vary slightly by series — read the preface.
