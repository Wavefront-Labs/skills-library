# Reconstructing an ownership chain and documenting gaps

**Goal:** produce a provenance for one object — every known owner and transfer, each tied
to evidence, with gaps named rather than smoothed over.

## 1. Start with the object itself
Before any archive, examine (or get photographs of) **every surface**: front, back,
stretcher, frame, mount, base, underside, inside covers.
- **Labels**: dealer, framer, exhibition, shipping, customs, collection-inventory labels.
  Photograph before anything is removed; note partial or overpainted ones.
- **Stamps and stencils**: canvas suppliers' stamps date the support; customs and
  export stamps give places and dates.
- **Inscriptions and numbers**: chalk sale lot numbers, dealer stock numbers (often
  pencilled on the stretcher), inventory numbers.
- **Collectors' marks** on drawings and prints: identify them in **Lugt** (*Les Marques de
  collections*, searchable online).
Record each as: location on object, transcription, photo reference, interpretation,
confidence.

## 2. Work backwards and forwards from the firm points
- **Catalogue raisonné** of the artist (if one exists) — the starting chain, but check its
  sources; older ones copied each other's errors.
- **Exhibition history** — catalogues place an object with a lender on a date.
- **Sale catalogues** — buyer and price are often only in **annotated copies** (auction
  house archives, libraries). Lot descriptions and dimensions let you match objects.
- **Getty Provenance Index** — sales, dealer stock books (e.g. Goupil, Knoedler) and
  archival inventories. Partial coverage; see
  `humanities.provenance-and-art-authentication.provenance-and-loss-databases.searching-registries-and-interpreting-hits-and-absences`.
- **Dealer archives** (stock books, photo archives, correspondence) — Knoedler at the Getty,
  Duveen, Wildenstein (access restricted), and many in national libraries.
- **Collector papers, wills and probate inventories**, insurance schedules, photographs of
  interiors.

## 3. Matching: is it the same object?
Titles change and descriptions are vague. Match on **dimensions (allow for frame vs sight
size and unit conversion), medium, subject details, signature/date, and labels** linking
document to object. A stock number on the stretcher matching a stock-book entry is strong;
a matching title alone is weak. Grade each link.

## 4. Write it in standard form
Common museum convention (AAM / many US and UK museums):
- Chronological, earliest first, one owner per paragraph/entry.
- **Semicolon** between owners when transfer is documented directly; **period** when the
  link is not known to be direct.
- Dates and places for each; method of transfer (sale, gift, by descent) in parentheses;
  "[dealer]" after dealers' names; lot numbers and sale dates for auctions.
- Use "possibly" / "probably" for uncertain links, and footnote the evidence.
- Bracket gaps explicitly (e.g. "1910–1947, whereabouts unknown").
*Note:* conventions vary slightly by institution; follow the house style.

## 5. Gap analysis
List each gap with: dates, likely location, what records might fill it, and risk. For
continental European works, **1933–1945** gaps are high-risk and require spoliation
research (see `humanities.provenance-and-art-authentication.restitution-research.nazi-era-and-colonial-loss-due-diligence`).
Archaeological and ethnographic objects without documentation before **1970** (UNESCO
Convention) raise trafficking risk.

## Gotchas
- **Provenance can be forged** too: fake labels, invented collections, doctored catalogues
  (e.g. Drewe/Myatt inserting forged records into archives). Test documents as hard as the object.
- "Private collection" in a catalogue is not a provenance link.
- Family tradition is a lead, not evidence.
- Name variants and spellings, changing dealer firm names, and reused stock numbers cause
  false matches.

## Verify
- [ ] Every surface was examined and every mark recorded with photo and transcription.
- [ ] Each link cites a source and has a confidence grade.
- [ ] Object-to-document matches rest on more than title alone.
- [ ] The written provenance follows house punctuation conventions for direct vs uncertain links.
- [ ] Every gap is listed with dates, risk and next records to search.
