# Cross-border insolvency: recognition and coordination

**Goal:** make an insolvency proceeding in one country effective where the debtor's
assets, creditors and counterparties are elsewhere, and keep parallel proceedings from
fighting each other. The core framework is the **UNCITRAL Model Law on Cross-Border
Insolvency (1997)**, enacted in the US as Chapter 15 of Title 11 and in other states with
local modifications. Within the EU a separate regulation applies; confirm it on EUR-Lex.
Jurisdiction: UNCITRAL texts and US Chapter 15, checked current on 2026-10-24; reference content, not legal advice.

## 1. Start with COMI
**Centre of main interests (COMI)** decides where the *main* proceeding belongs.
- Presumption: the registered office (companies) or habitual residence (individuals) is the COMI in the
  absence of evidence to the contrary (US §1516(c)).
- Rebutted by evidence of where the debtor actually runs its affairs — head office
  functions, where creditors deal with it, where the board meets; the test varies by court.
- Timing: courts usually assess COMI at the filing of the recognition application or
  the opening of the foreign proceeding (approaches differ). **COMI shifts** shortly
  before filing attract scrutiny for forum shopping.
- Recognition as a *non-main* proceeding requires an **establishment** in the forum (US §1517(b)(2); see
  the §1502 definition); a *main* proceeding is one pending where the debtor has its COMI (§1517(b)(1)).

## 2. Recognition application (Model Law pattern)
Evidence typically required:
1. Certified copy of the decision commencing the foreign proceeding and appointing the
   foreign representative (US §1515(b)(1)), or a certificate from the foreign court affirming the
   proceeding's existence, or other evidence the court accepts.
2. Statement identifying all foreign proceedings known (§1515(c)).
3. English translations of the documents in (1) (§1515(d)).
The proceeding must be a collective, judicially supervised insolvency proceeding.
In the US, recognition of a **main** proceeding applies the automatic stay
and the §§363, 549 and 552 limits on transfers of the debtor's US property (§1520(a)).
Further relief is discretionary, at the foreign representative's request and where necessary to
effectuate the chapter: stays, examination of witnesses and taking of evidence, and entrusting
distribution of US assets to the foreign representative where US creditors are sufficiently protected
(§1521). The Model Law also contains a public-policy exception.

## 3. Things recognition does not automatically do
- **Enforce foreign judgments**, especially avoidance or default judgments against third
  parties. UK courts held in *Rubin v Eurofinance* ([2012] UKSC 46) that foreign insolvency-related judgments fall under the ordinary
  common-law rules and that the Model Law has no provision for enforcing them; UNCITRAL's 2018 Model Law on
  Recognition and Enforcement of Insolvency-Related Judgments addresses this. Check whether the target state
  has adopted it.
- **Discharge English-law debts:** the rule in *Antony Gibbs & Sons v La Societe Industrielle* (1890) holds that a foreign
  insolvency discharge only discharges liabilities governed by the law of the foreign forum unless the
  creditor submitted to that proceeding. The Court of Appeal reaffirmed it in *Bakhshiyeva v Sberbank*
  (2018); it has been criticised.

## 4. Coordination
- **Protocols** between courts and office-holders: information sharing, joint hearings,
  allocation of asset sales and claims processes.
- **Group insolvencies:** rely on protocols and, where adopted, the 2019 UNCITRAL Model Law on
  Enterprise Group Insolvency.
- **Synthetic secondaries:** in some regimes the main office-holder undertakes to respect local
  priorities to avoid a full secondary proceeding; confirm availability locally.

## 5. Asset tracing
Freezing orders, disclosure orders against banks, letters of request, recognition-based
evidence-taking (US §1521(a)(4)), and cooperation with
local counsel early — limitation and security deposit rules differ by forum.

## Gotchas
- Not every country has adopted the Model Law; the ones that have differ in reciprocity
  requirements and excluded entities.
- Local secured creditors and employees' local priorities are usually protected.
- File recognition early: assets move fast once creditors learn of the insolvency.
