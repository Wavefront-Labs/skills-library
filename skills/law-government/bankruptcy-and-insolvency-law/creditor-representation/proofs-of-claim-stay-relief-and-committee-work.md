# Creditor representation: claims, stay relief and committee work

**Goal:** protect a creditor's recovery once a counterparty enters insolvency —
preserve the claim, maximise its priority, and get leverage in the process. Jurisdiction: United States
federal (11 U.S.C.), checked current on 2026-10-24; other systems differ (see end). Reference content, not
legal advice.

## 1. First 48 hours after notice of filing
1. **Stop collection.** The automatic stay voids most post-filing collection acts; wilful violations can cost an injured individual actual damages, costs and fees (§362(k)(1)); setoff of
   pre-filing debts is also stayed (§362(a)(7)). Freeze dunning, setoff and repossession until reviewed.
2. **Pull the docket:** first-day motions, DIP order (look for lien challenges, deadlines,
   critical-vendor terms), creditor matrix, schedules.
3. **Classify the client's position:** secured (and is the security *perfected*?),
   priority, administrative, unsecured, contingent; any setoff or recoupment rights.
4. **Goods recently shipped?** A reclamation demand must be in writing within 45 days after the debtor
   received the goods, or within 20 days after the case begins if the 45 days run past the filing (§546(c));
   the value of goods received by the debtor within 20 days before the case began and sold in the ordinary
   course gets administrative priority (§503(b)(9)). Calendar these immediately.
5. **Executory contract?** If the client still owes performance under a live contract,
   the debtor may assume or reject it; on assumption, defaults must be cured — file a cure
   objection if the scheduled cure amount is wrong.

## 2. The proof of claim
- **Bar date** is set by order or the Rules; untimely claims are disallowed unless an exception applies
  (§502(b)(9)). A governmental unit's claim is timely if filed before 180 days after the order for relief
  (or any later date the Rules allow).
- Attach the supporting documents (contract, invoices, account statement, security
  documents and evidence of perfection). A claim signed and filed under the Rules is prima facie
  evidence of its validity and amount (Rule 3001(f)).
- Split the claim correctly: secured portion (up to collateral value), priority,
  administrative, and general unsecured. Interest, fees and costs: an oversecured
  creditor may recover them as allowed by its agreement or state law (§506(b)); a claim is secured only up
  to the value of the collateral interest (§506(a)(1)).
- In Chapter 11 a claim scheduled by the debtor and not listed as disputed, contingent or unliquidated is
  deemed filed (§1111(a)) — but check the amount, and filing anyway is the safer habit.

## 3. Relief from the automatic stay
Grounds (US §362(d)): **cause**, including lack of adequate protection; or no equity in
the property and the property is not necessary to an effective reorganisation. Build
the record: valuation evidence, insurance lapses, missed payments, depreciation. Under §362(e)(1)
the stay terminates 30 days after a relief request unless the court, after a hearing, orders it continued —
know the timing rules before you file.
Adequate protection can be periodic payments, replacement liens, or an equity cushion.

## 4. Committee work
- The US trustee appoints the unsecured creditors' committee (§1102(a)(1)), ordinarily from the persons
  willing to serve who hold the seven largest claims (§1102(b)(1)). Members owe fiduciary duties to the class, not
  themselves, and must handle material non-public information carefully (trading walls).
- Committee levers: investigate pre-petition liens and insider transactions, object to
  DIP terms, push sale processes, negotiate plan recoveries, seek standing to sue.

## 5. Priority fights and plan voting
- Challenge other creditors' liens (perfection gaps, preference periods) where it lifts
  your client's recovery.
- Read the plan's classification and treatment: is your class impaired? Is it fairly
  compared to similar claims? Vote, and object to confirmation if the plan fails the
  best-interests or absolute-priority tests.

## Gotchas
- Missing the bar date is usually fatal; diary it on receipt of notice.
- Receiving a payment in the 90 days before filing creates preference exposure — check
  ordinary-course and new-value defences before demanding more.
- Unperfected security interests are avoidable by the trustee; check filings early.
- Setoff requires mutuality and is itself stayed — seek relief, don't self-help.

## Other jurisdictions
Other systems differ on claims procedure, bar dates and priority lists; confirm locally.

## Verify
- [ ] Bar date, reclamation and cure-objection deadlines are diarised.
- [ ] The claim is split by priority with documents attached.
- [ ] Perfection of any security is confirmed from public records.
- [ ] Preference exposure from recent payments has been assessed.
