# Corporate reorganisation: plan and confirmation roadmap

**Goal:** steer a distressed company through a court-supervised reorganisation so a
plan is confirmed that the court can lawfully approve over dissent. Structure follows
US Chapter 11 (the textbook model); equivalent tools elsewhere are noted. Jurisdiction: United States federal
(11 U.S.C.), checked current on 2026-10-24; section numbers are US, so always check current local rules
and case law in the venue. Reference content, not legal advice.

## 1. Before filing
- **Liquidity forecast:** a 13-week cash flow is the first document everyone asks for.
  It drives DIP sizing and whether cash collateral alone will do.
- **Restructuring support agreement (RSA):** lock up key creditor classes before filing
  where possible. A prepackaged case solicits votes before filing; a pre-negotiated case still
  solicits after filing, which §1125(b) allows only once the court has approved a disclosure statement.
- **Venue:** choice of venue affects judges, local practice and precedent; check current venue law.

## 2. First-day motions (day 1–2)
Typical set: joint administration, cash management, payment of wages and benefits (subject
to the §507(a)(4) priority cap, $17,150 for cases filed from 1 April 2025 per the Code's adjustment note, and its 180-day window), critical vendors, utilities adequate assurance, insurance, taxes,
**cash collateral** and **DIP financing**. For the first 21 days Rule 6003 bars certain relief (employing professionals, using or selling
estate property including paying pre-petition claims, assuming or assigning contracts) unless needed to avoid
"immediate and irreparable harm"; the rest waits for the final hearing.
- **DIP financing gotchas:** roll-ups of pre-petition debt, milestones that force a quick
  sale, liens on avoidance actions, waivers of surcharge rights. Creditors' committees
  attack these; expect to give a challenge period for lien investigation.

## 3. Building the plan
1. **Classification:** substantially similar claims go together; separately classifying
   similar claims to manufacture an accepting class is attacked as gerrymandering.
2. **Treatment:** each class impaired or unimpaired. Unimpaired classes are conclusively presumed to accept
   (§1126(f)); classes receiving nothing are deemed to reject (§1126(g)).
3. **Disclosure statement:** "adequate information" for a hypothetical investor typical of the
   holders of claims or interests (§1125(a)(1)) — projections, liquidation analysis, valuation, risk
   factors. Court approval is needed before post-filing solicitation (§1125(b)).
4. **Voting:** a class accepts if creditors holding at least two-thirds in amount **and** more than
   one-half in number of the allowed claims actually voted accept (§1126(c)). The court may designate
   votes not cast in good faith (§1126(e)). Insiders' acceptances don't count toward the required
   impaired accepting class (§1129(a)(10)).

## 4. Confirmation checklist (US §1129)
- **Best interests:** each dissenting holder in an impaired class gets at least what it
  would get in liquidation. Needs a credible liquidation analysis.
- **Feasibility:** confirmation not likely to be followed by liquidation or further
  reorganisation — projections plus evidence of exit financing.
- **At least one impaired class accepts** (excluding insiders).
- **Priority claims:** administrative and certain priority claims get cash equal to the allowed amount on
  the effective date unless the holder agrees otherwise (§1129(a)(9)(A); tax claims have their own
  rules in §1129(a)(9)(C)).
- **Cramdown** (§1129(b)) over a dissenting impaired class: the plan must not discriminate unfairly and
  must be fair and equitable. For a dissenting unsecured class, the **absolute priority rule**
  (§1129(b)(2)(B)) means no junior claim or interest may receive anything unless the class is paid the
  allowed amount of its claims in full. Check §1129(b)(2)(A) for secured classes and case law on
  "new value" contributions by old equity.
- **Third-party releases:** non-consensual releases of non-debtors were held unavailable
  in Chapter 11 by the US Supreme Court (Harrington v. Purdue Pharma, 27 June 2024, 5-4); the Court did
  not call consensual releases into question, and what counts as consent (opt-in/opt-out) is contested. Check current case law in the venue.

## 5. After confirmation
Effective-date conditions (exit financing closes, new governance documents filed),
claims reconciliation, distribution mechanics, and a litigation trust for retained causes
of action where relevant.

## Gotchas
- Valuation is the fight: every cramdown and best-interests argument rests on it.
- Missing a milestone in the DIP can hand control to the lender.
- Critical-vendor payments need a real evidentiary basis, not a list of favourite suppliers.

## Other jurisdictions
Other systems have their own court-supervised restructuring tools (for example UK schemes of arrangement
and restructuring plans, and Canadian CCAA proceedings); confirm each regime's current law locally.
