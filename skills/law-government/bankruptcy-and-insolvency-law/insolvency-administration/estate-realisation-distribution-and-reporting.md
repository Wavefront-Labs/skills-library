# Insolvency administration: realisation, distribution and reporting

**Goal:** turn an insolvent estate into the maximum net return for creditors, distributed
in the right order, with a record that survives scrutiny by creditors, regulators and
the court. Applies to trustees, liquidators and administrators; terms and priority lists
vary by jurisdiction. Generic practice guidance, checked against the UK HMRC priority rules only (2026-10-24);
not legal advice, and an insolvency practitioner should confirm the local rules.

## 1. Day one: secure and inventory
- **Take control:** bank mandates, premises, keys, books and records, cloud accounts,
  domain names. Change passwords and notify banks to freeze accounts.
- **Insurance:** confirm cover on property immediately — the estate bears the loss.
- **Inventory** with photos and independent valuation where value is material.
- **Identify third-party interests:** retention of title, leased equipment, consigned
  goods, trust money. Releasing an asset you didn't own is the office-holder's liability.
- **Employees:** statutory notices and claims to any government guarantee scheme (names vary by country).

## 2. Realisation decisions
For every asset ask: **value to the estate, net of costs and secured claims?**
- Fully encumbered asset with no equity → abandon or hand to the secured creditor;
  don't spend estate money preserving value for someone else unless you can surcharge.
- Sale methods: going-concern sale (best value but needs funding to trade), auction,
  private treaty, pre-pack (in some systems; requires heightened transparency).
- **Causes of action are assets:** preferences, fraudulent transfers, director claims
  (wrongful/insolvent trading), unpaid share capital. Decide pursue / assign / fund /
  drop on a documented cost-benefit basis. Litigation funding and assignment rules vary.
- Record why each decision was made at the time — hindsight complaints are common.

## 3. Claims adjudication
- Require proof with supporting documents; adjudicate in writing with reasons.
- Test related-party claims hard; subordination rules for insiders and shareholder loans
  differ by system.
- Convert foreign-currency claims at the rate specified by law (often the date of
  the insolvency order).
- Reserve for disputed claims before any interim distribution.

## 4. The distribution waterfall (generic order — confirm locally)
1. Fixed-charge / secured creditors from their collateral (net of realisation costs
   chargeable to them).
2. Costs and expenses of the administration, including the office-holder's remuneration
   as approved.
3. Preferential / priority claims (commonly employee wages and some taxes; in the UK, for insolvencies
   starting after 1 December 2020, certain amounts HMRC holds from VAT and PAYE-type deductions rank as
   secondary preferential debts, ahead of floating-charge holders).
4. Any ring-fenced fund for unsecured creditors carved out of floating-charge
   realisations (UK "prescribed part"; confirm the current cap in the Insolvency Act 1986 and its orders).
5. Floating-charge holders.
6. Unsecured creditors pari passu.
7. Statutory interest, then subordinated claims, then shareholders.
Run the numbers in a spreadsheet with each tier's total, rate of return and reserves;
reconcile to the bank account before paying.

## 5. Reporting
Statutory reports, receipts-and-payments accounts and creditor updates on the prescribed
timetable; remuneration approval on the basis allowed (time costs, percentage or fixed fee).
Explain significant decisions, especially decisions not to pursue claims. Report director
conduct to the regulator where required locally.

## Gotchas
- Trading on without a funded budget makes the office-holder exposed for new liabilities.
- Commingling estate money with anything else is a career-ending error; use a dedicated
  estate account.
- Paying a dividend before resolving priority disputes invites personal liability.
- Conflicts: check prior relationships with the debtor, directors and major creditors
  before accepting appointment.

## Verify
- [ ] All assets insured, inventoried and third-party claims checked.
- [ ] Each realisation or abandonment decision has a dated cost-benefit note.
- [ ] Waterfall spreadsheet ties to the bank and includes disputed-claim reserves.
- [ ] Statutory reporting deadlines are diarised and met.
