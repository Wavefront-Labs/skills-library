# Licence condition enforcement: from warning to revocation

**Goal:** bring a licensee back into compliance — or remove the licence — using the
lowest effective step, through a process fair enough that the decision holds on appeal or
judicial review. Licensing regimes (alcohol/premises, taxis, gambling, childcare, waste
carriers, professions) each have their own statute, so this skill gives the common
administrative-law skeleton. Models: England & Wales local and national licensing, and US
state agencies acting under an Administrative Procedure Act. Powers, penalty amounts,
notice periods and appeal routes are regime-specific — always read the enabling statute
and the authority's published enforcement policy first.

## 1. Know exactly what is enforceable
- Pull the **current** licence with all conditions, variations and any attached plans.
- Split conditions into: clear and measurable ("CCTV recording retained 31 days") vs vague ("promote good order"). Vague conditions are hard to enforce — note this for the next review rather than stretching them.
- Identify the powers available: advice, warning, statutory notice, fixed/civil penalty, suspension, variation of conditions, revocation, prosecution, and who is delegated to use each.

## 2. Monitor
- Sources: inspections, licensee returns, complaints, police/partner intelligence, test purchases, data matching.
- Log every breach on the licensee file with date, source, condition breached and evidence reference — the **pattern** matters later.

## 3. Escalation ladder
| Step | Use when | Record |
|---|---|---|
| Advice / informal visit | First, minor, quickly fixable breach | Note of advice given |
| Warning letter | Clear breach; licensee needs written notice of consequence | Condition breached, evidence, action required, deadline, what happens next |
| Action plan / meeting | Repeated breaches, cooperative licensee | Agreed actions, review date |
| Penalty notice / civil penalty | Regime allows; breach proven; deterrence needed | Statutory form, amount, payment/appeal rights |
| Review/variation of conditions | Conditions inadequate to address the problem | Proposal and reasons |
| Suspension | Serious risk or persistent breach; time to fix | Period, scope, reasons, lift criteria |
| Revocation | Licensee unfit or unable to comply; public protection requires it | Full reasoned decision |
| Prosecution | Serious offence; public interest | Separate criminal evidence standards |
Apply the enforcement policy factors: seriousness, harm, history, intent, cooperation, deterrence, consistency with comparable cases.

## 4. Fair process for adverse action
1. **Notice:** tell the licensee in writing what is alleged, the evidence relied on, the action contemplated and how to respond (a "show cause" or "minded to" letter).
2. **Opportunity to respond:** a reasonable period (often 14–28 days, or as set by statute) and access to the material.
3. **Hearing** where the regime requires it: impartial decision-maker, evidence exchanged in advance, right to representation, record kept.
4. **Reasoned decision:** findings of fact, the conditions/law applied, why lesser steps are insufficient, the decision, effective date and appeal rights.
5. **Urgent action:** many regimes allow immediate suspension where public safety demands it, followed promptly by a full hearing — use only with documented urgency.

## 5. After the decision
- Serve correctly; record service.
- Monitor compliance with the decision (operating while suspended is itself an offence in most regimes).
- Appeals (magistrates' court/tribunal in England; administrative law judge/court in US states) are usually a rehearing or review on the record — your file is the case.

## Gotchas
- Skipping straight to suspension without documented lesser steps or urgency.
- Decision-maker who also investigated (bias/fairness challenge).
- Relying on evidence the licensee never saw.
- Inconsistent treatment of comparable licensees.

## Verify
- [ ] Condition breached is current, clear and cited verbatim.
- [ ] Breach log shows the pattern with evidence references.
- [ ] Chosen step justified against the enforcement policy factors.
- [ ] Notice, opportunity to respond and (if required) hearing provided.
- [ ] Written reasons and appeal rights served and recorded.
