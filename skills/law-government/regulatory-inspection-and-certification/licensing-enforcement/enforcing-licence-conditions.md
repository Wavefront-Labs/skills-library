# Enforcing licence conditions

**Goal:** detect breaches of licence or permit conditions, respond in a way that is
proportionate and consistent, and take licence action that survives appeal when lesser
steps fail.

Works alongside `law-government.public-administration-and-civil-service.licensing-and-permitting.processing-licence-applications`
(grant side). Penalty powers, notice periods and appeal routes are set by the regime's
statute and the regulator's published enforcement policy, which must be followed.

## 1. Monitoring without visiting
Most licence compliance can be checked on paper:
- **Returns and reports** required by conditions (annual returns, data submissions,
  certificates, insurance renewals). Diarise due dates and chase on day one of lateness.
- **Data analysis:** compare returns between licensees and over time; outliers are leads.
- **Intelligence:** complaints, other agencies, media, whistleblowers. Record source and
  reliability.
- Trigger an inspection when paper evidence can't settle it.

## 2. Evidence the breach
For each alleged breach: the exact condition wording, the facts showing breach, the
evidence (documents, admissions, inspection notes), dates, and the licensee's explanation.
Check the condition is clear and was actually in force on the date (variations, transfers).

## 3. Choose the response
Use your enforcement policy's factors: seriousness and risk, harm caused, intent or
recklessness, history, cooperation, whether the breach is continuing, and deterrence.
Typical ladder:
1. **Advice / informal letter** – minor, first-time, quickly remedied.
2. **Warning letter** – records the breach and the consequence of repetition.
3. **Formal caution** (where available) – requires admission; affects future decisions.
4. **Penalty notice / civil or administrative penalty** – fixed or variable amount;
   calculated using the published method; allows representations and appeal.
5. **Licence review: variation, suspension, revocation** – the gravest step; also used
   where a licensee is no longer fit and proper.
6. **Prosecution** for offences, in parallel or instead.
Record why you chose the step and why not the next one down.

## 4. Fair procedure for penalties and licence action
- **Notice of intent / show-cause:** the proposed action, the grounds and evidence relied on,
  and a reasonable period for representations.
- Consider representations genuinely; adjust or withdraw if they show you were wrong.
- **Final notice:** decision, reasons, amount or terms, effective date, appeal rights; whether
  the action is suspended pending appeal.
- Interim suspension without prior hearing usually requires urgency and a statutory power;
  follow with a prompt review.

## 5. Consistency and publication
Keep a decisions register to compare like cases. Publish outcomes where policy requires,
which also deters.

## Gotchas
- Enforcing a condition that's ambiguous or was never validly imposed.
- Penalty amount not following the published calculation.
- Skipping the representations stage because the case "is obvious".
- Delay: long gaps between breach and action weaken proportionality arguments.

## Verify
- [ ] Condition wording and date in force confirmed.
- [ ] Breach evidence assembled and licensee's explanation recorded.
- [ ] Response chosen against the policy factors, rationale written.
- [ ] Notice of intent issued, representations considered, final notice with appeal rights.
- [ ] Decision logged in the consistency register.
