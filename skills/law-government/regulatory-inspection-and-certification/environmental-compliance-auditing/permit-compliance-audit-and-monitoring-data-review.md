# Permit compliance audit and monitoring data review

**Goal:** determine, with evidence, whether a facility is meeting every condition of its
environmental permits and the rules that apply directly, and report findings graded so the
right ones get fixed first. Works for regulator compliance assessments and for internal
or third-party (consultant) audits. Models: US federal programmes (Clean Air Act Title V,
Clean Water Act NPDES, RCRA), England's Environmental Permitting Regulations and EU IED
installations. Limits, monitoring methods and reporting deadlines live in each permit and
rule — never audit from memory.

## 1. Define scope and build the obligations register (before site)
1. Collect: all permits and variations, applications (often incorporated by reference), consent orders, rules referenced by the permit, previous audit/inspection reports, complaint logs.
2. Break each permit into **atomic obligations** in a register: ID, source clause, requirement (limit, frequency, method), evidence needed, owner.
3. Add general duties not in the permit (waste duty of care/manifests, spill reporting, chemical storage, stormwater).
4. Set the audit period (e.g. last 12 or 24 months) and sampling plan for records.

## 2. Monitoring and emissions data review
| Check | How |
|---|---|
| Completeness | Every required parameter, at every required frequency, at every point — count expected vs actual results |
| Method | Accredited lab/method as specified (e.g. EPA methods, MCERTS-certified instruments in UK); holding times, chain of custody |
| CEMS quality | Calibration/drift checks, QAL2/QAL3 or RATA records, data availability %, substitution rules |
| Limits | Compare each result against the correct averaging period (hourly, daily, monthly average, 95th percentile) |
| Reporting | DMRs/annual returns filed on time and matching raw data |
Re-calculate a sample of averages from raw data; transcription and averaging errors are common. Flag every **exceedance** and check whether it was reported within the required notification time.

## 3. Site verification
- Walk each emission point, discharge, storage area and waste store against the register and the permit's site plan.
- Check physical controls: bunding/secondary containment (commonly ≥110% of largest tank — check the rule that applies), drain labelling, abatement equipment running and maintained, sampling points accessible.
- Interview operators: what happens on an upset, who reports.
- Look for unpermitted sources, changed processes not covered by a variation, and "temporary" arrangements that became permanent.

## 4. Grade the findings
Use the scheme the audit is under; otherwise a common three-tier grading:
- **Major/Category 1:** actual or potential significant environmental impact, or permit limit breach.
- **Minor/Category 2–3:** procedural or reporting failure with low impact.
- **Observation/opportunity:** not a breach.
(England's Compliance Classification Scheme uses categories 1–4 with scores feeding operator risk/fees — check current weighting.)
Each finding: requirement → condition observed → evidence → grade → root-cause hint.

## 5. Report and follow-through
- Executive summary with counts by grade; list of exceedances with dates.
- Corrective-action table with owners and dates; verify closure on next audit.
- Internal audits in the US: consider privilege and EPA Audit Policy self-disclosure timing (disclosure windows apply) — involve counsel before circulating findings.

## Gotchas
- Auditing against the original permit, not the current variation.
- Comparing a spot result to an averaged limit (or vice versa).
- Missing obligations hidden in the application documents incorporated by reference.
- Treating "no data" as "compliant" — missing monitoring is itself a breach.

## Verify
- [ ] Register covers every condition of the current permit versions.
- [ ] Expected vs actual monitoring counts reconciled.
- [ ] Sample of averages recalculated from raw data.
- [ ] Each finding graded with evidence reference.
- [ ] Exceedance notification timeliness checked.
