# Permit compliance audit (environmental)

**Goal:** determine, condition by condition, whether a permitted site complies with its
environmental permit, with evidence that supports any enforcement and a grading that drives
proportionate action.

Permitting regimes vary (e.g. US Clean Air Act/Clean Water Act permits such as NPDES; EU
industrial emissions permits with BAT conclusions; national waste licences). The permit
itself is the primary standard; regulations and guidance fill gaps.

## 1. Build the audit checklist from the permit
Extract every condition into a table: condition number, requirement, type (emission limit,
operating technique, management system, monitoring, reporting, record-keeping), how to test
it, evidence needed. Typical categories:
- **Emission/discharge limits:** parameter, limit value, averaging period, monitoring point,
  method standard.
- **Operating techniques:** containment, abatement equipment, raw materials, waste handling.
- **Management:** environmental management system, competence, maintenance, complaints,
  accident management plan.
- **Monitoring and reporting:** frequency, method, accreditation, report deadlines,
  notification of exceedances.

## 2. Desk review of monitoring data
- Compare reported results with limits using the **correct averaging period** (hourly, daily,
  monthly percentiles) and any compliance rules (e.g. percentage of samples allowed above).
- Check data completeness: gaps, invalidated periods, substitution rules for CEMS.
- **Data integrity:** look for impossibly flat values, values just under limits, timestamps
  out of pattern, calibration records missing, lab certificates inconsistent with reports.
- Check exceedances were notified within the required time.

## 3. Site verification
- Walk the process: check abatement is running (pressure drops, temperatures, flow meters),
  bunds and containment intact, storage labelled, drainage to the correct system.
- Witness or take independent samples where justified (see
  `law-government.regulatory-inspection-and-certification.regulatory-sampling-and-evidence.sampling-chain-of-custody-and-records`).
- Inspect monitoring equipment: location representative, calibration up to date, sample
  lines and probes clean.
- Interview operators: are procedures followed on night shifts and weekends?

## 4. Grade findings
Use a category scheme (many regulators publish one) based on potential environmental
impact: e.g. a condition breach that could have a major effect vs a minor administrative
failure. Record for each: condition, evidence, category, root cause (if known), required
action and date.

## 5. Report and follow-up
Issue a compliance report promptly; escalate per enforcement policy (advice, warning,
enforcement notice, suspension, prosecution). Repeat or deliberate breaches, and data
falsification, warrant escalation regardless of impact. Track actions to closure.

## Gotchas
- Assessing against the wrong limit version after a permit variation.
- Ignoring averaging periods and declaring compliance or breach incorrectly.
- Relying only on operator self-monitoring without integrity checks.
- Unpermitted emission points not on the permit plan; look for them.

## Verify
- [ ] Checklist covers every permit condition, current version.
- [ ] Monitoring compared using the right averaging and compliance rules.
- [ ] Data integrity tests done and recorded.
- [ ] Site checks confirm equipment operation and containment.
- [ ] Each finding graded with evidence and action date.
