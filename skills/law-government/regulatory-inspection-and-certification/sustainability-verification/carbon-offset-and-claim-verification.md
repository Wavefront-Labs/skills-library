# Carbon-offset and sustainability claim verification

**Goal:** give an independent opinion, under a named scheme standard and assurance level,
on whether a project's emission reductions (or another sustainability claim) are real,
measured correctly and not counted twice.

Reference points: ISO 14064-3 (validation and verification of GHG statements) and ISO 14065
(requirements for validation/verification bodies); scheme rules such as Verra VCS, Gold
Standard, CDM-derived methodologies, Article 6 mechanisms, and green-building or sourcing
schemes with their own audit manuals. Scheme rules change frequently and integrity criteria
(e.g. high-level integrity principles) are evolving; treat this as a method outline and
check the current scheme version. Status: draft; detail varies heavily by methodology.

## 1. Validation vs verification
- **Validation:** before or at the start: is the project design, baseline and monitoring plan
  consistent with the methodology and scheme rules?
- **Verification:** periodically afterward: did the project achieve the claimed reductions
  in the monitoring period, measured per the validated plan?
Confirm the assurance level (**reasonable** vs **limited**) and the **materiality**
threshold (often a percentage of total reductions, set by the scheme).

## 2. Validation tests
- **Eligibility:** project type and methodology version allowed; start date and crediting
  period rules.
- **Additionality:** would the activity have happened anyway? Typical tools: regulatory
  surplus (not required by law), investment analysis (not financially viable without credits),
  barrier analysis, common-practice analysis. Test the inputs; sensitivity analysis matters.
- **Baseline:** plausible, conservative, uses approved data sources.
- **Leakage:** emissions shifted outside the boundary.
- **Permanence** (forestry/land): reversal risk assessment and buffer contributions.
- **Safeguards:** stakeholder consultation, social and environmental requirements.

## 3. Verification tests
- Trace reported figures from source data (meters, logbooks, satellite data, sample plots)
  through calculations to the monitoring report. Recalculate a sample independently.
- Check instrument calibration, data gaps and how they were filled (conservatively?).
- Site visit where required: equipment operating as described, sample plots measured.
- **Double counting:** credits not also claimed under another scheme or by a host country
  where that would be a double claim; registry checks.

## 4. Findings and opinion
Raise findings as corrective action requests (must be resolved), clarification requests, and
forward action requests (for next period). Issue the opinion only when residual misstatement
is below materiality. Disclose limitations in scope.

## 5. Claim substantiation beyond carbon
Product or corporate sustainability claims (recyclable, carbon neutral, sustainably sourced)
face consumer-protection rules on green claims in many jurisdictions. Verify the claim wording
against the evidence and the scheme's claim rules; a verified dataset can still back a
misleading claim.

## Gotchas
- Accepting the project's investment analysis without testing assumptions.
- Using the wrong methodology version.
- Conflict of interest from prior consulting on the same project.

## Verify
- [ ] Scheme, methodology version, assurance level and materiality recorded.
- [ ] Additionality, baseline, leakage and permanence each tested with evidence.
- [ ] Sample recalculated from source data.
- [ ] Registry checked for double issuance or claims.
- [ ] All corrective action requests closed before opinion.
