# Food hygiene inspection and rating

**Goal:** assess whether a food business controls its hazards, act immediately on imminent
risks, and give a rating or score that is consistent across inspectors.

The legal basis varies: e.g. the FDA Food Code adopted (with local amendments) by US states
and counties, EU/UK hygiene regulations with national rating schemes, and Codex Alimentarius
general principles underpinning many others. Scoring systems (letter grades, 0–5 ratings,
point deductions) are scheme-specific.

## 1. Before the visit
Check registration/permit, previous inspection and rating, complaints, any food-poisoning
notifications. Most routine inspections are unannounced. Bring probe thermometer
(calibrated), sanitising wipes, torch, camera, sampling kit.

## 2. What to look at, in order of risk
1. **Temperature control:** cold holding (FDA Food Code: TCS food at 41 °F / 5 °C or below; GB chilled limit 8 °C per local-authority guidance citing the Food Safety and Hygiene (England) Regulations 2013 Sch 4),
   hot holding (FDA Food Code: 135 °F / 57 °C or above, roasts 130 °F / 54 °C; GB: 63 °C or above, up to 2 hours below 63 °C allowed per FSA/GOV.UK guidance; note 135 °F is 57 °C, not 63 °C), cooking and cooling records, reheating.
   Probe actual food, not just display readouts.
2. **Cross-contamination:** raw/ready-to-eat separation in storage, equipment and handling;
   use of separate boards and complex equipment (vacuum packers, slicers) is a frequent cause
   of outbreaks.
3. **Personal hygiene:** handwashing facilities stocked and accessible; ill-worker exclusion policy.
4. **Cleaning and disinfection:** correct chemical strength and contact time; visible soiling.
5. **Pests:** droppings, gnaw marks, proofing, pest-control contract records.
6. **Allergen management:** ingredient information, labelling of prepacked-for-direct-sale
   food, staff knowledge, cross-contact controls.
7. **Management system:** HACCP-based plan proportionate to the business; monitoring records
   that match reality; training.
Compare the plan, the records and what you observe; records that are too perfect warrant questions.

## 3. Critical violations and immediate action
Where there is an imminent health risk (e.g. sewage backflow, no hot water, serious
infestation, unsafe food being served), use the available power: voluntary closure, emergency
prohibition, permit suspension. Seize or detain unsafe food under the relevant procedure.
Document precisely; such actions often need court confirmation quickly (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).

## 4. Sampling
Sample for a reason (complaint, outbreak investigation, survey programme, verifying a claim).
Follow the formal procedure if results may be used for enforcement: correct containers,
temperature during transport, division into parts where required, labelling, chain of custody,
accredited laboratory.

## 5. Scoring or rating consistently
- Apply the scheme's scoring guidance literally; don't let a friendly owner move the score.
- Base "confidence in management" on evidence (records, training, past compliance).
- Explain the rating, the appeal or re-rating route, and what would improve it.
- Calibrate through regular consistency exercises with colleagues.

## 6. Follow-up
Written report listing contraventions with legal references and required actions;
improvement notices with dates for significant breaches; revisit for critical issues.

## Gotchas
- Uncalibrated thermometers undermine every temperature finding.
- Advising a specific supplier or product is outside your role.
- Ignoring the cooling stage, a classic source of outbreaks.

## Verify
- [ ] Temperatures probed in food; thermometer calibration recorded.
- [ ] Each risk area assessed and recorded.
- [ ] Imminent risks dealt with using the correct legal power.
- [ ] Samples taken under formal procedure where enforcement possible.
- [ ] Score matches the scheme guidance; report and appeal route sent.
