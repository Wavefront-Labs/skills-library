# Food hygiene inspection, rating and escalation

**Goal:** judge whether a food business is producing safe food, record it in a way the
public rating and any enforcement can rest on, and escalate fast when there is an
imminent risk. Models: England/Wales (Food Law Code of Practice, FHRS) and US local
health departments applying the FDA Food Code. Scoring schemes, notice names and
frequencies differ by country, state and even county — use the local scheme's forms.

## 1. Frequency and preparation
- Businesses get an intervention risk rating from the local scheme. Higher-risk premises (e.g. caterers to vulnerable groups, high-risk processes like sous-vide, vacuum packing, raw/ready-to-eat handling) are visited most often; take the frequency for each band from the current Food Law Code of Practice or your state or local rules.
- Review: last report, complaints, food poisoning notifications, approvals, sampling results.
- Kit: calibrated probe thermometer (and a sanitiser/test strip), torch, camera, sample kit, forms.

## 2. The walk-through (follow the food)
1. **Delivery and storage:** cold-chain temperatures (gov.uk: chilled food must be kept at 8°C or below, a legal requirement in England, Wales and Northern Ireland and a recommendation in Scotland; fridges set to 5°C or below; US cold-holding limit: see the FDA Food Code), raw below ready-to-eat, date control, pest proofing.
2. **Preparation:** separation of raw and ready-to-eat (equipment, surfaces, staff), *E. coli* O157 controls, allergen handling.
3. **Cooking/cooling/reheating:** probe core temperatures; check cooling times and hot-holding temperatures against the current local code (not confirmed here).
4. **Hygiene:** handwash basins stocked and accessible, cleaning chemicals meeting the disinfection standard your regulator requires, contact times known.
5. **Structure:** surfaces cleanable, ventilation, drainage, pests.
6. **Management:** HACCP-based food safety management system, records actually kept, staff training, allergen information to consumers.
Talk to food handlers; ask them to show you how they do it.

## 3. Score and rate
- **UK FHRS:** the officer checks three elements — food handling (preparation, cooking, reheating, cooling, storage), the physical condition of the business (cleanliness, layout, lighting, ventilation, pest control, facilities) and how food safety is managed (processes, training, systems) — and gives a rating from 5 (very good) to 0 (urgent improvement is necessary). The guidance does not state element weights. The rating is given at the inspection or within 14 days.
- **US:** record priority / priority foundation / core violations (or critical/non-critical in older codes); many jurisdictions publish letter grades or scores.
- Correct-on-site (COS) items still get recorded.

## 4. Escalate proportionately
| Situation | Typical action |
|---|---|
| Minor contraventions | Written report/letter with timescales |
| Significant contraventions, no imminent risk | Formal notice (UK) / re-inspection with violation follow-up (US) |
| Imminent risk to health (sewage, rodent infestation, no hot water, gross cross-contamination) | Voluntary closure or emergency prohibition under the local legal route (UK); permit suspension/summary closure (US) |
| Unsafe food | Detention/seizure, voluntary surrender, recall liaison |
| Serious, repeated breaches | Simple caution or prosecution per enforcement policy |
Document why voluntary closure was offered and accepted voluntarily — a pressured "voluntary" closure can be challenged.

## 5. After the visit
- Report to the operator within the local service standard; explain the rating and the right to reply, appeal or request a re-visit. FHRS guidance: appeal within 21 days of notification, result within 21 days of receipt; right to reply has no deadline; re-visit requests are not available for a 5; ratings 0-4 are published 3-5 weeks after inspection.
- Revisit within the timescale; record closure.
- Food poisoning links: liaise with public health/epidemiology before alerting the business.

## Gotchas
- Uncalibrated thermometer — the reading is worthless as evidence.
- Rating the décor rather than the risk.
- Missing allergen control: one of the most common serious failures now.
- Accepting records at face value: check they match what you observe.
