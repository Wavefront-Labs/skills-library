# Risk-based inspection visits

**Goal:** inspect the right sites at the right frequency, carry out each visit within your
legal powers, gather evidence that would support enforcement, and leave the duty-holder
clear on what must change and by when.

Powers of entry, warrant requirements, notice periods and enforcement tools come from the
specific statute under which you act (and sometimes a cross-cutting code, e.g. regulators'
codes requiring proportionality and transparency). This is the generic method.

## 1. Plan: risk-rate and schedule
- Score each regulated entity on **inherent hazard** (what could go wrong, how badly, to how
  many) and **confidence in management** (compliance history, quality of systems,
  complaints, incidents). Frequency follows the combined score.
- Reserve capacity for reactive work: complaints, incidents, intelligence.
- Announced visits suit system audits; unannounced visits test day-to-day conditions. Use
  whichever the statute permits and the purpose needs.

## 2. Prepare
- Read the file: previous findings, outstanding actions, permit/licence conditions, complaints.
- Define scope and the standards you will test against (statute, regulations, conditions,
  approved codes).
- Kit: ID/warrant card, PPE, camera, sampling kit, notebook, any forms.
- Know your powers: entry (times, with or without notice, dwelling restrictions), what you
  may examine, copy, sample, photograph or seize; when a warrant is required.

## 3. On site
- **Opening meeting:** identify yourself and the power you're using; explain scope; ask who
  is in charge and who you should speak to.
- **Observe first**, then interview and check records; compare what people say, what records
  show and what you see.
- **Evidence:** contemporaneous notes (date, time, who, what, exact words for admissions),
  photos with context, copies of documents, samples with chain of custody (see
  `law-government.regulatory-inspection-and-certification.regulatory-sampling-and-evidence.sampling-chain-of-custody-and-records`).
- If you may move to formal investigation of an offence, be aware of cautioning or interview
  rules in your jurisdiction; switch modes explicitly.
- Deal immediately with serious and imminent risk: use prohibition/stop powers if available.

## 4. Closing meeting
Summarise findings, distinguish legal requirements from advice, state what you expect and the
timescale, and say what happens next. Don't negotiate the legal standard; you can be flexible
on how and when.

## 5. Report and act
- Write the report promptly. For each finding: requirement, observation, evidence, risk,
  required action, deadline.
- Choose a proportionate response using an **enforcement pyramid**: advice → written warning
  → formal notice → penalty/sanction → prosecution or licence action. Escalate where risk is
  serious, the breach deliberate, or earlier steps ignored. Record why you chose the level.
- Schedule follow-up and close actions only on evidence.

## Gotchas
- Exceeding your powers (wrong time, no warrant for a dwelling) can make evidence inadmissible.
- Mixing advice and legal requirements in the report.
- Inconsistent outcomes for similar breaches; use your enforcement policy.

## Verify
- [ ] Visit chosen by documented risk rating.
- [ ] Legal power identified and respected.
- [ ] Notes contemporaneous; photos and samples traceable.
- [ ] Report findings each linked to a requirement and evidence.
- [ ] Enforcement level justified against the policy; follow-up diarised.
