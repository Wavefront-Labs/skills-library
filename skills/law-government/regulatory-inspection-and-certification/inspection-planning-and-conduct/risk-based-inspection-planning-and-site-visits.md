# Risk-based inspection planning and site visits

**Goal:** spend scarce inspector time where harm is most likely, and make every visit
produce findings and evidence that survive challenge. Models used: the OECD inspection
toolkit and UK Regulators' Code principles, with US federal/state agency practice noted.
Powers of entry, notice rules and enforcement tools are statutory and vary by
regime — always read the enabling act for the regime you inspect under.

## 1. Build the risk-rating model
1. Maintain a register of every regulated entity (licence/permit number, activity, size, location, history).
2. Score each on **hazard** (inherent severity of what could go wrong) × **likelihood** (compliance history, management confidence, complaints, age of last inspection).
3. Map scores to frequency bands, e.g.:

| Band | Typical profile | Indicative interval |
|---|---|---|
| A (high) | Severe hazard, poor history | 6–12 months, plus reactive |
| B | Severe hazard, good history / moderate hazard, poor history | 12–24 months |
| C | Moderate hazard, good history | 24–36 months |
| D (low) | Low hazard | Intelligence-led only / self-assessment |

(Intervals are illustrative; many regimes set statutory minimum frequencies — check.)
4. Reserve 20–40% of capacity for **reactive** work: complaints, incidents, referrals, intelligence.
5. Re-score after every visit so the model learns.

## 2. Prepare the visit
- Read the file: permit/licence conditions, last report, outstanding notices, complaints, monitoring returns.
- Define the **scope** and 3–5 priority topics (do not try to inspect everything).
- Decide announced vs unannounced. Unannounced suits hygiene, conditions-of-operation and complaint follow-ups; announced suits document-heavy audits where the right people must be present.
- Confirm your **authority**: warrant card/credentials, the statutory power of entry, time-of-day limits, whether a warrant is needed for domestic premises or refusal (US: administrative-search rules and consent; UK: statutory powers usually allow entry at reasonable times).
- Kit: notebook, camera, sample kit, PPE, forms, copy of the relevant law.

## 3. On site
1. **Arrival:** identify yourself, show credentials, state the power you act under and the purpose. Ask for the responsible person.
2. **Opening meeting (5–10 min):** scope, route, who will accompany you, what you may sample or copy.
3. **Walk-through:** observe operations as they are, not as staged. Follow the process flow (goods in → process → dispatch/waste).
4. **Evidence:** contemporaneous notes (time, location, who said what, verbatim where it matters), photographs with a scale and context shot, document copies, samples under protocol (see regulatory-sampling-and-evidence skill).
5. **Interviews:** distinguish informal questions from formal interviews under caution or compelled interviews — the rules on admissibility and self-incrimination differ sharply by jurisdiction. If an offence is suspected, stop and switch to the formal procedure.
6. **Closing meeting:** summarise findings, distinguish legal requirements from advice, state next steps and deadlines. Never promise an outcome you are not authorised to decide.

## 4. Choose the response (enforcement pyramid)
Advice → written warning → formal notice (improvement/compliance order) → prohibition/suspension → penalty or prosecution. Apply the regulator's published enforcement policy: proportionality, consistency, targeting, transparency. Record why you chose the level.

## 5. Write the report (within the policy deadline, often 5–15 working days)
- Facts first: date, attendees, areas inspected, scope limits.
- Each finding: requirement (cite the clause/condition) → observation → evidence reference → risk → required action and deadline.
- Separate **legal contraventions** from **good-practice recommendations**.
- File notes, photos and samples with a reference index so another officer can follow the trail.

## Gotchas
- Exceeding your power of entry or scope taints the evidence.
- Accepting hospitality or being steered around the site by the operator.
- Mixing advice and requirements in the report — the duty holder cannot tell what is enforceable.
- Risk models that never update become "inspect the usual suspects".

## Verify
- [ ] Visit priority traceable to a documented risk score or reactive trigger.
- [ ] Legal authority for entry confirmed and recorded.
- [ ] Every finding links to a requirement and an evidence item.
- [ ] Enforcement choice justified against the published policy.
- [ ] Report issued within deadline and risk score updated.
