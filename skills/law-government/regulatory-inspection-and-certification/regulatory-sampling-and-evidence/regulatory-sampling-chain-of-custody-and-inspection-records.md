# Regulatory sampling, chain of custody and inspection records

**Goal:** collect samples and records so that a result, photograph or note can be relied
on in a notice appeal, licence hearing or prosecution without a credible attack on how it
was obtained. Models: England & Wales (statutory formal sampling, PACE-style note-taking)
and US environmental/food agency practice. Specific sampling regulations (number of parts,
who gets one, deadlines to notify) differ by regime — read the regime's sampling rules
before the visit.

## 1. Decide: informal or formal sample
| | Informal / surveillance | Formal / official |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Intelligence, screening | Enforcement evidence |
| Procedure | Simple record | Full statutory protocol |
| Division | Single | Often divided into parts (e.g. three: one to the analyst, one to the owner, one retained) |
| Use in court | Weak | Designed for it |
If enforcement is plausible, take it formally the first time — you rarely get the same conditions again.

## 2. Sampling protocol (write it before going)
1. **Method:** the standard method or the regime's prescribed method (sample size, container type, number of increments, composite vs grab).
2. **Lab first:** confirm an accredited laboratory (ISO/IEC 17025, accredited for that test) or the statutory analyst; ask for containers, preservatives and **holding times** (e.g. microbiology often needs testing within 24–36 hours chilled; check per test).
3. **QA samples:** field blanks, trip blanks, duplicates as the method requires.
4. **Kit:** clean/sterile containers, tamper-evident seals and bags with unique numbers, labels, cool boxes with temperature logger, gloves, camera.

## 3. Taking the sample
- Photograph the sampling point and context before and after.
- Use aseptic/clean technique; change gloves between points.
- Divide into parts in front of the owner where the regime requires; offer their part and record acceptance or refusal.
- Seal each part; record seal numbers; have the owner witness/sign if possible.
- Label: unique sample ID, date/time, location, sampler, description, preservation.
- Record temperature at collection where relevant.

## 4. Chain of custody
A **custody form** travels with the samples. Every transfer gets a line: released by, received by, date/time, condition of seals, storage temperature. Rules:
- No gaps — if a sample sat in a vehicle or fridge, record where and how it was secured.
- Keep custody to as few people as practical.
- The lab records receipt condition and seal integrity; broken seals must be reported, not "fixed".
- Retain the custody form with the lab report as one exhibit bundle.

## 5. Contemporaneous notes and records
- Write at the time or as soon as practicable; record the time you made the note.
- Bound notebook or approved electronic system with audit trail; no blank lines, no erasures — strike through once, initial.
- Record exactly what was said for any admission, verbatim in quotation marks, and who was present.
- Distinguish **observation** ("the probe read 14.2°C at 10:42 in the walk-in chiller") from **opinion** ("inadequate cooling").
- Under PACE-style rules, a conversation becomes an interview once an offence is suspected — caution and formal recording apply. US: Miranda applies to custodial interrogation, rarely to regulatory visits, but agency policy may set its own rules.

## 6. Photographs and exhibits
- Wide (context) → medium → close-up, with a scale and the sample/exhibit label in shot.
- Keep originals unaltered with metadata; work on copies. Log each image (number, time, subject).
- Give every exhibit a reference (officer initials + number) used consistently in notes, reports and statements.
- Remember disclosure: notes, photos and unused material may have to be disclosed to the defence.

## Gotchas
- Holding time exceeded — result unusable.
- Owner's part not offered or not recorded.
- Seal numbers not written down at the scene.
- Editing photos, or deleting "bad" ones (looks like concealment).

## Verify
- [ ] Protocol cites the method and the lab's accreditation scope.
- [ ] Seals, IDs and division recorded at the scene; owner's part offered.
- [ ] Custody form complete with no time gaps; seals intact on lab receipt.
- [ ] Notes timed, unaltered, observations separated from opinions.
- [ ] Photo log and exhibit references consistent across all documents.
