# Sampling, chain of custody and inspection records

**Goal:** every sample result, photograph and note you rely on can be shown to be what you
say it is, taken how you say, and unaltered, so it is admissible and persuasive if
enforcement is contested.

Formal sampling procedures are often prescribed by statute or regulation (e.g. food law
requiring division into parts; environmental regulations prescribing methods). Laboratory
requirements typically include accreditation to ISO/IEC 17025 for the test. Evidence rules
(hearsay, exhibits, disclosure) are jurisdiction-specific.

## 1. Before sampling
- Know why you're sampling (enforcement, surveillance, complaint) and choose formal procedure
  if results may support enforcement.
- Confirm the method standard: sampling point, volume, container type, preservatives,
  temperature, **maximum holding time** to analysis. Book the lab in advance.
- Use clean, lab-supplied containers; record batch numbers.

## 2. Taking the sample
- Record: date, time, exact location (grid reference, photo of the point), conditions
  (weather, flow, process state), who was present, method used.
- **Split/tripartite samples** where required or prudent: divide into identical parts; one
  for your lab, one offered to the duty-holder, one retained for a referee analysis.
  Offering the part is often a legal precondition to using the result.
- Seal each container with a numbered tamper-evident seal; label with unique ID, date, time,
  your initials; get the duty-holder's representative to witness or sign if procedure allows.
- Take field blanks and duplicates where the method requires QA.

## 3. Chain of custody
A custody form travels with the samples and records each transfer: from whom, to whom, date,
time, condition, seal intact (Y/N). Store securely (locked cool box, refrigerator with log).
Any unrecorded gap is a point of attack. The lab records receipt condition and temperature.

## 4. Contemporaneous notes
- Write during or immediately after the event, in a bound notebook or approved electronic
  system with audit trail.
- No blank lines or gaps; cross out errors with a single line and initial; never erase or
  tear out pages.
- Record exact words for significant statements and admissions, attributing them.
- Note times throughout.

## 5. Photographs and video
- Wide shot for context, mid shot, close-up; include a scale and an ID card or label in shot.
- Keep originals unaltered (hash or write-protected storage); work only on copies.
- Log each image: number, time, location, what it shows, who took it. Check camera date/time
  is correct at the start.

## 6. Exhibits
Give each item an exhibit reference; record continuity until it is produced at any hearing.
Retain for the statutory or policy period.

## Gotchas
- Exceeding holding time invalidates many analyses.
- Failing to offer the duty-holder their part of a formal sample.
- Unsealed or unlabeled samples left in an unlocked vehicle.
- Edited photos, or metadata showing a wrong date.

## Verify
- [ ] Method, container, preservation and holding time match the standard.
- [ ] Split parts offered/retained and recorded.
- [ ] Custody form complete with every transfer and seal check.
- [ ] Notes contemporaneous, timed, unaltered.
- [ ] Photo originals preserved with a log; exhibits referenced.
