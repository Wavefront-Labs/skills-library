# Plan review and staged construction inspection

**Goal:** confirm a building is designed and built to the adopted code, catching defects at
the stage when they are still visible and cheap to fix, and issue occupancy only on evidence.

Which code applies (e.g. a locally amended edition of the International Building/Residential/
Fire Codes in the US; national building regulations and approved documents elsewhere) depends
on the jurisdiction and the date the application was made. Always confirm the edition and
local amendments in force for this permit.

## 1. Plan review
1. **Classify:** occupancy/use group, construction type, height, area, occupant load. Almost
   every later requirement depends on these; get them right first.
2. **Fire and life safety:** means of escape/egress (number, width, travel distance, dead
   ends), compartmentation and fire-resistance ratings, protection of openings and
   penetrations, sprinklers and alarms where required, fire service access.
3. **Structure:** design loads, engineer's calculations and stamps where required.
4. **Accessibility, energy, ventilation, plumbing, electrical** as within your remit
   (some are separate trades or agencies).
5. Issue written review comments citing the specific code section; approve when resolved.
**Alternative methods / performance-based solutions** are lawful in most codes if
demonstrated equivalent; require the analysis in writing and record the decision.

## 2. Staged inspections
Typical stages (vary by code and project): footing/foundation before concrete; under-slab
services; framing/structure; rough-in of mechanical, electrical and plumbing; insulation;
fire-stopping and fire-resistance assemblies before they are concealed; drainage testing; final.
At each:
- Inspect against the **approved plans**, not just the code. Unapproved changes need revised approval.
- Look at what will be hidden next. Once boarded over, fire-stopping and connections can't be checked.
- Record pass, pass with conditions, or fail; list corrections with code references.

## 3. Correction notices
Clear, specific, referenced: location, the defect, the code section, required correction,
reinspection requirement. Stop-work orders for serious or unpermitted work.

## 4. Fire code inspection of existing buildings
Periodic or complaint-driven: exits unobstructed and signed, doors self-closing and not wedged,
alarm and sprinkler maintenance records, fire extinguishers serviced, storage of hazardous
materials, occupant load not exceeded. Focus on things that kill in fires: blocked exits,
disabled systems, compromised compartmentation.

## 5. Occupancy sign-off
Issue a certificate of occupancy/completion only when all stages are passed, required tests
and commissioning certificates (fire alarm, sprinkler, emergency lighting, smoke control) are
on file, and life-safety systems are operational. Temporary occupancy, if allowed, should list
conditions and an expiry.

## Gotchas
- Wrong occupancy classification cascades into wrong egress and fire requirements.
- Passing a stage on a contractor's photo of something you were meant to see.
- Approving deviations on site verbally.
- Penetrations through rated walls added by later trades after fire-stopping inspection.

## Verify
- [ ] Code edition and local amendments identified for this permit.
- [ ] Classification recorded and checked by a second reviewer on complex buildings.
- [ ] Every stage inspected before concealment; results recorded.
- [ ] Corrections closed out on reinspection.
- [ ] Occupancy issued only with life-safety certificates on file.
