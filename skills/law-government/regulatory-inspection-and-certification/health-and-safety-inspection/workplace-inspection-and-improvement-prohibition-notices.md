# Workplace inspection and improvement/prohibition notices

**Goal:** inspect a workplace as the safety regulator and land on the proportionate,
legally sound response — advice, notice or prosecution — with a notice that will survive
appeal. Models: Great Britain (HSE / local authorities under HSWA 1974) and US federal
OSHA. Other systems (e.g. Australian WHS regulators, EU labour inspectorates) have close
analogues but different notice names, time limits and appeal routes — check locally.

## 1. Triggers and preparation
| Trigger | Typical approach |
|---|---|
| Planned/programmed (sector priority, high-risk activity) | Topic-focused: e.g. work at height, machinery guarding, silica |
| Complaint from worker/public | Investigate the specific concern; protect the complainant's identity |
| Reported incident (GB RIDDOR; US fatality/hospitalisation reporting) | Incident-triggered visit; preserve evidence; consider investigation level |
| Referral/intelligence | Scope from the referral |
Before going: check prior notices, incident history, sector guidance (HSE ACoPs/guidance; OSHA standards in 29 CFR 1910/1926) and the duty holder's size.

## 2. On site
1. Identify yourself and your power (GB s.20 HSWA powers; US: present credentials, hold an opening conference, seek consent or a warrant).
2. Involve worker representatives / safety reps where they exist (US: employee representative may accompany the walkaround).
3. Walk the process; watch actual work. Focus on the **significant** risks, not housekeeping trivia.
4. Gather evidence: notes, photographs, measurements, document copies (risk assessments, training records, maintenance logs), witness accounts.
5. Closing conference: tell the duty holder what you found and what you will do.

## 3. Decide the response — measure the risk gap
GB practice (EMM logic): compare the **actual risk** you saw with the **benchmark risk** the law accepts (what compliance with the standard/guidance would leave). The size of the gap, plus duty-holder factors (history, attitude, standards), points to the enforcement level:
- Small gap → verbal/written advice.
- Substantial gap, contravention → **improvement notice**.
- Risk of serious personal injury → **prohibition notice** (immediate or deferred) — does not require a proven contravention, only the risk.
- Serious gap with aggravating factors → consider prosecution in addition.
US: classify violations (other-than-serious, serious, willful, repeat, failure-to-abate); issue citations with proposed penalties (penalty maxima are inflation-adjusted annually — check the current figures). Imminent danger may require seeking a court order if the employer will not remove workers voluntarily.

## 4. Draft the notice / citation
An improvement notice must state: the provision contravened, the inspector's opinion, the **reasons**, and a compliance date not earlier than the appeal period (GB: 21 days — check current rules). A prohibition notice states the activity, the risk, and (if applicable) the contravention and the matters to remedy.
Drafting rules:
- Name the activity and location precisely ("use of the unguarded band saw in Workshop 2"), not "machinery".
- Cite the exact regulation; give reasons in plain words.
- Schedule of works is optional guidance — do not lock the duty holder into one fix unless necessary.
- Realistic compliance dates: the shortest that's achievable for the risk.
- Serve on the right legal person; record the method and time of service.

## 5. Follow-up and appeals
- Diary the compliance date; revisit or obtain evidence of compliance; record closure.
- Failure to comply is itself an offence (GB) / failure-to-abate (US).
- Appeals: GB to an employment tribunal (an improvement notice is suspended pending appeal; a prohibition notice is not unless the tribunal directs). US: notice of contest within 15 working days to OSHRC; informal conference often resolves first.
- Cost recovery schemes may apply (GB Fee for Intervention when a material breach is found).

## Gotchas
- Vague notices ("ensure machinery is safe") get cancelled on appeal.
- Compliance date set inside the appeal window.
- Treating a prohibition notice as needing a breach — it rests on risk.
- Forgetting to record what the duty holder said in mitigation.

## Verify
- [ ] Inspection trigger and scope recorded.
- [ ] Risk gap assessed against a named benchmark (standard, ACoP, guidance).
- [ ] Enforcement level matches the published policy and is reasoned.
- [ ] Notice cites the provision, gives reasons, specifies activity, date and service.
- [ ] Compliance follow-up diarised and closed out.
