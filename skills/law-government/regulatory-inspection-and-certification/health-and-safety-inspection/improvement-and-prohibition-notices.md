# Improvement and prohibition notices (OHS inspection)

**Goal:** use the right enforcement instrument for the risk found, draft it so the
duty-holder knows exactly what to do, and make it robust on appeal.

Instruments differ by system: UK-style **improvement** and **prohibition notices**; US OSHA
**citations** with classifications (OSHA's penalties page lists Serious, Other-Than-Serious, Posting Requirements, Failure to Abate, and Willful or Repeated) and penalties; other
systems use compliance orders and stop-work orders under their own law. The thinking below maps across; forms, time limits and appeal routes do not. For GB the instruments are in Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 ss 21 (improvement), 22 (prohibition), 24 (appeals) and 33(1)(g) (offence), all checked on legislation.gov.uk.
For the duty-holder side see the occupational-health-and-safety-practice field.

## 1. Pick the instrument
- **Prohibition / stop-work:** the activity involves, or will involve, a **risk of serious
  personal injury** (HSWA s 22(2)); no contravention of law is needed in GB, the risk is enough.
  The notice takes effect at the end of the period specified or, if it so declares, immediately (s 22(4)).
- **Improvement / compliance order:** a contravention exists, or has occurred in circumstances making it likely to continue or be repeated (HSWA s 21). The period to put right may not end earlier than the period for appealing under s 24.
- **Advice or written warning:** lower risk, first instance, cooperative duty-holder.
- **Prosecution / penalty:** consider in parallel for serious failures, harm caused, or
  disregard of earlier action. A notice doesn't preclude prosecution.
Record the risk assessment behind your choice (likelihood, severity, people exposed).

## 2. Draft the notice
A robust notice:
- Names the correct duty-holder (legal entity, not a site name) and is served correctly.
- States your opinion and its basis: the specific statutory provision contravened (for
  improvement) or the activity and the risk (for prohibition).
- Describes the activity or matter precisely: location, equipment, process.
- Gives a realistic compliance date (improvement) or effect (immediate/deferred).
- Optionally includes a schedule of remedial measures. Make it clear whether these are
  required or one way to comply; prescriptive schedules can be attacked if unreasonable.
- States appeal rights and that appeal does or doesn't suspend the notice (GB, HSWA s 24(3): an appeal suspends an improvement notice; it suspends a prohibition notice only if the tribunal so directs on the appellant's application).

## 3. Incident-triggered visits
- Secure the scene in coordination with police where a fatality is involved; preserve
  equipment and records.
- Identify immediate risks to others and act first.
- Obtain reporting records (e.g. statutory incident reports), risk assessments, training
  records, maintenance logs; interview witnesses separately and early.
- Determine root causes, including management failings, not just the immediate act.

## 4. Follow-up
Revisit or obtain evidence of compliance by the date. Extensions: only on request before
expiry, with reasons. Non-compliance with a notice is usually itself an offence (GB: HSWA s 33(1)(g), confirmed).

## 5. Appeals
Expect challenges to: whether there was a contravention or serious risk, the
reasonableness of time given, and the remedial schedule. Keep notes, photos and the risk
reasoning to defend each.

## Gotchas
- Wrong legal entity or defective service.
- Vague descriptions ("unsafe machinery") that the recipient can't act on.
- Too short a compliance period for engineering fixes.
- Missing interim controls when giving long periods.

## Verify
- [ ] Instrument matches the assessed risk and is justified in writing.
- [ ] Notice cites provision or risk, describes the matter precisely, and states dates and appeal rights.
- [ ] Served on the right entity by a valid method.
- [ ] Follow-up diarised; compliance confirmed on evidence.
