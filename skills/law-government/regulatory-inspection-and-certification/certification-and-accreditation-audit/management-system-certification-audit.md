# Management-system certification audit

**Goal:** decide on evidence whether an organisation's management system conforms to the
standard and is effective, and write findings precise enough that the certification
decision and the client's corrective action both follow from them.

Reference framework: ISO 19011 (auditing guidelines), ISO/IEC 17021-1 (requirements for
certification bodies) and ISO/IEC 17011 (accreditation bodies), plus the sector scheme
(ISO 9001, 14001, 45001, 27001 and others). Audit-time tables, grading terms and timelines
are set by the scheme and accreditation body; check the current rules.

## 1. The cycle
- **Stage 1:** readiness. Review documented information, scope, key processes, legal
  requirements, internal audit and management review. Decide if stage 2 can proceed and plan it.
- **Stage 2:** implementation and effectiveness on site (or remote where allowed).
- **Certification decision** by someone not on the audit team.
- **Surveillance** audits (typically annual) and **recertification** (typically every three
  years). Special audits after complaints, major changes or suspensions.

## 2. Plan
- Confirm scope (sites, activities, exclusions with justification).
- Allocate audit time per scheme rules; sample sites and processes, focusing on risk and
  previous findings.
- Team competence matches the sector codes; no impartiality conflicts (e.g. no consultancy
  to the client in the recent period).

## 3. Audit technique
- Follow **audit trails**: from a requirement (e.g. control of nonconforming product) through
  documents, records and interviews to observed practice, and back.
- Sample objectively; note what you looked at (record IDs, dates) even when it conforms.
- Process approach: inputs, outputs, controls, performance indicators, risks.
- Interview the people doing the work, not just managers.

## 4. Writing nonconformities
Each nonconformity statement has three parts:
1. **Requirement** (clause of the standard or the client's own procedure).
2. **Evidence** (objective: what was seen, where, which records).
3. **Statement of nonconformity** (how the evidence fails the requirement).
Grade:
- **Major:** absence or total breakdown of a required element, or a situation raising
  significant doubt that the system can achieve intended outcomes. Blocks certification until
  corrected and verified.
- **Minor:** a lapse not affecting the system's capability.
- **Opportunity for improvement:** not a nonconformity; never disguise advice as a finding, and
  don't prescribe solutions (impartiality).

## 5. Closing and follow-up
Closing meeting: present findings and grading, explain corrective-action timelines, confirm
recommendation. Review the client's root-cause analysis and corrective actions; verify majors
by evidence or follow-up visit before recommending.

## Gotchas
- Findings with no traceable evidence are unappealable only in theory; they get overturned.
- Grading by audit pressure ("we're out of time, call it minor").
- Auditing documents instead of practice.
- Acting as consultant (impartiality breach).

## Verify
- [ ] Scope, audit time and team competence/impartiality confirmed.
- [ ] Audit trails documented with sampled record IDs.
- [ ] Each nonconformity has requirement, evidence and statement; grade justified.
- [ ] No prescriptive solutions offered.
- [ ] Majors verified closed before recommendation.
