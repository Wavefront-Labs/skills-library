# Management-system certification audit and nonconformity grading

**Goal:** reach a defensible certification decision on whether an organisation's
management system conforms to the standard and is effectively implemented, with
nonconformities graded consistently. Model: the international ISO/IEC 17021-1 regime used
by accredited certification bodies worldwide (accreditation bodies such as UKAS, ANAB, DAkkS
operating under ISO/IEC 17011 and IAF rules). Scheme-specific rules (e.g. automotive
IATF 16949, food GFSI schemes, aerospace AS9100) add or tighten requirements — follow the
scheme rules where they differ.

## 1. The certification cycle
| Audit | Purpose | When |
|---|---|---|
| Stage 1 | Readiness: documentation, scope, legal requirements, internal audit and management review done, site conditions, plan stage 2 | Before stage 2 (gap usually weeks, not months) |
| Stage 2 | Full conformity and effectiveness of implementation | Initial certification |
| Surveillance 1 & 2 | Sample of requirements; always internal audit, management review, complaints, corrective action, use of marks | At least annually; first within 12 months of certification decision |
| Recertification | Whole system over the cycle | Before expiry of the 3-year certificate |
Special audits: short-notice for complaints, scope extensions, major changes.

## 2. Plan the audit
1. Confirm scope wording, sites, shifts, outsourced processes, headcount — they drive **audit time** (IAF MD 5 tables; adjustments must be justified and recorded).
2. Multi-site: sampling only where the scheme allows it and the central function controls all sites.
3. Build a process-based **audit plan**: each process/department, the clauses tested, auditor, time slot. Send it in advance.
4. Check auditor competence for the technical area (codes/sectors) and impartiality (no consultancy to this client in the restricted period — commonly 2 years).

## 3. Conduct
- **Opening meeting:** scope, plan, confidentiality, grading method, appeal route.
- Gather evidence by interview, observation and records, using **audit trails**: pick an output (a shipped order, an incident, a design change) and trace it back through the processes. Sample records rather than accept the procedure.
- Test **effectiveness**, not only paperwork: are objectives met, are corrective actions closing causes, does management review drive decisions?
- Record objective evidence for every conformity and nonconformity (document IDs, dates, who said what).

## 4. Grade findings
| Grade | Test (ISO/IEC 17021-1 concept) |
|---|---|
| **Major nonconformity** | Affects the capability of the system to achieve intended results — e.g. a requirement not addressed at all, a systemic breakdown, or doubt that effective process control exists |
| **Minor nonconformity** | Does not affect that capability — an isolated lapse |
| **Opportunity for improvement** | No requirement breached; never phrase as a recommended solution (that is consultancy) |
Write each nonconformity as: **requirement** (clause) – **finding** (what is missing) – **evidence**. Several minors on the same clause may together amount to a major.

## 5. Close and decide
- **Closing meeting:** present findings, confirm understanding, explain timelines.
- Majors: correction and corrective action **verified** before certification/recertification (commonly within 90 days, sometimes requiring a follow-up visit — check scheme rules); certification cannot be granted with open majors.
- Minors: accepted corrective-action plan, verified at the next audit.
- Report goes to an independent **certification decision-maker** who did not audit.
- In surveillance, unresolved majors lead to suspension; continued failure to withdrawal.
Accreditation assessment of a certification body mirrors this one level up: the accreditation body audits the CB's office processes and witnesses its auditors.

## Gotchas
- Grading by how "bad it feels" rather than by effect on system capability.
- Accepting a procedure as evidence of implementation.
- Writing solutions into findings (impartiality breach).
- Reducing audit days without a recorded justification — an accreditation finding.

## Verify
- [ ] Audit time calculated and justified per the applicable IAF/scheme rules.
- [ ] Audit plan covers every clause due in this cycle point.
- [ ] Each nonconformity has requirement, finding and evidence.
- [ ] Major/minor grading checked against the capability test.
- [ ] Majors verified closed before any certification decision.
