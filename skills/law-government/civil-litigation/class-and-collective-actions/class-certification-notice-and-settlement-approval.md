# Class actions: certification, notice and settlement approval

**Goal:** get a class certified (or defeated), give constitutionally adequate notice,
and get a settlement approved that survives objectors and appeal. US federal Rule 23 is
the textbook model; other systems are noted briefly at the end and differ significantly.

## 1. Rule 23(a) prerequisites
1. **Numerosity** — joinder impracticable (courts decide case by case).
2. **Commonality** — at least one common question whose answer drives resolution of all
   claims (*Wal-Mart v. Dukes*, 2011 — a shared grievance isn't enough).
3. **Typicality** — named plaintiffs' claims arise from the same course of conduct.
4. **Adequacy** — representatives and class counsel have no conflicts and will vigorously
   prosecute.
Plus implied requirements in many circuits: a definable and **ascertainable** class
(standards vary by circuit).

## 2. Rule 23(b) type
- **(b)(2)** — injunctive or declaratory relief for the class as a whole;
  individualised monetary relief not allowed (*Dukes*).
- **(b)(3)** — damages; requires **predominance** of common questions and **superiority**
  of class treatment. Damages model must measure damages attributable to the theory of
  liability (*Comcast v. Behrend*, 2013). Requires notice and opt-out.
- **(b)(1)** — limited fund or risk of inconsistent obligations (rare).

## 3. Certification fight
- The court conducts a "rigorous analysis" and may resolve merits questions that overlap
  with Rule 23 issues; expert evidence is often subjected to Daubert at this stage.
- Defence themes: individualised issues (reliance, causation, damages, consent,
  arbitration agreements and class waivers), uninjured class members (standing), conflicts within the class.
- Interlocutory appeal of a certification ruling is discretionary (Rule 23(f)) and
  must be sought within 14 days after the order (45 days where the United States, a US agency or officer is a party).

## 4. Class definition
Define by objective criteria (purchased X between dates in state Y), not by merits
("people harmed by"), to avoid fail-safe classes. Subclasses address conflicts.

## 5. Notice
(b)(3): the best notice practicable, including individual notice to all members
identifiable with reasonable effort — mail, email, digital media as appropriate. Plain-
language content: what the case is, the class definition, claims, right to opt out and
how, binding effect, right to appear through counsel. Use a notice administrator and
document reach.

## 6. Settlement approval (Rule 23(e))
1. **Preliminary approval** — the court reviews the proposal under Rule 23(e)(1) before notice goes to the class.
2. Opt-out and objection period.
3. **Fairness hearing** — factors: adequate representation, arm's-length negotiation,
   adequacy of relief, equitable
   treatment of members. Courts scrutinise **coupon** settlements, clear-sailing
   fee clauses, reversions to the defendant, and **cy pres** distributions.
4. Fee award — percentage-of-fund or lodestar cross-check.
CAFA requires notice of the proposed settlement to the appropriate federal and state officials within 10 days of
filing, and final approval cannot issue until 90 days after that notice (28 U.S.C. 1715(b), (d)).

## 7. Mass torts and MDL
Cases filed across districts may be centralised before one judge for pretrial purposes
by the Judicial Panel on Multidistrict Litigation (28 U.S.C. §1407). Not a class action: individual claims, bellwether trials, global
settlement programmes, common-benefit fees.

## Gotchas
- Arbitration agreements with class waivers can end a consumer or employee class.
- Settling individually with named plaintiffs before certification raises mootness and
  ethics questions.
- FLSA collectives are **opt-in** with different standards from Rule 23.

## Other systems
England & Wales: representative actions (CPR 19.8) and group litigation orders (CPR 19.22).
Other regimes (Competition Appeal Tribunal collective proceedings, Canada, Australia, the EU Representative
Actions Directive) differ significantly; check the current local rules.
