# Complaint and answer drafting that survives dismissal

**Goal:** draft the pleading that frames the whole case — every claim pleaded with
facts covering each element, every defence preserved — so the case gets past the
motion to dismiss and isn't constrained later. US federal rules (FRCP) are the main
textbook model; state courts and other countries differ (see end).

## 1. Before writing: the elements chart
For each cause of action, list its **elements** from the leading authority (pattern jury
instructions are a convenient checklist), then the **facts and evidence** that support each.
Any element with no supporting fact is either a gap to investigate or a claim to drop.
Rule 11 (and equivalents) require factual contentions to have, or be likely to have after
discovery, evidentiary support.

## 2. Complaint structure
1. **Caption** — court, parties (correct legal names and capacities), case number.
2. **Jurisdiction and venue** — the statutory basis (federal question, diversity with
   amount in controversy and each party's citizenship), personal jurisdiction facts, and venue facts.
3. **Parties** — who they are and why each defendant is responsible.
4. **Facts** — numbered paragraphs, one fact each, chronological, specific (dates, amounts,
   documents). Write so each paragraph can be admitted or denied cleanly.
5. **Counts** — one count per claim per defendant group; incorporate facts by reference
   sparingly (wholesale incorporation of all prior counts invites challenge).
6. **Prayer for relief** — each remedy sought (damages, injunction, declaratory relief,
   fees with the basis, interest).
7. **Jury demand** if wanted — deadlines are short and the right can be waived.

## 3. Pleading standards
- **Plausibility** (*Twombly* 2007, *Iqbal* 2009): legal conclusions get no weight;
  factual allegations must make liability plausible, not merely possible.
  Write "On 3 March, Smith emailed Jones stating the shipment was rejected" — not
  "Defendant breached the contract".
- **Particularity** for fraud and mistake (Rule 9(b)): who, what, when, where, how.
  Some statutes add more.
- Plead **in the alternative** where facts are uncertain (contract and unjust enrichment).

## 4. The answer
- Respond to **every** paragraph: admit, deny, or lack knowledge sufficient to form a
  belief (which operates as a denial). A failure to deny can operate as an admission.
- Deny precisely: admit the parts that are true; "denies the remaining allegations".
- **Affirmative defences** must be pleaded or risk waiver: limitations, statute of frauds,
  release, payment, waiver, estoppel, failure to mitigate, arbitration agreement, etc.
  Don't plead a boilerplate list without any factual basis — courts increasingly strike them.
- Certain defences (lack of personal jurisdiction, improper venue, insufficient service)
  are **waived** if omitted from the first Rule 12 motion or answer.
- **Compulsory counterclaims** arising from the same transaction or occurrence must be stated in the
  pleading (Rule 13(a)(1)).

## 5. Amendment
Leave to amend is freely given when justice requires (Rule 15(a)(2)); once a scheduling order sets a deadline, the
schedule can be modified only for good cause (Rule 16(b)(4)). **Relation back** (Rule 15(c)) saves claims against limitations when they arise
from the same conduct — and in limited cases for new parties.

## Gotchas
- Wrong defendant entity name or a dissolved company — check the corporate registry.
- Limitation periods and pre-suit notice requirements (government claims, medical
  malpractice, some consumer statutes) can bar the claim before you plead it.
- Exhibits attached to the complaint can be used against you at the motion stage.

## Other systems
Some US states require fact pleading (ultimate facts). England & Wales: claim form and particulars of claim under
CPR Part 16, verified by a statement of truth (CPR Part 22).
