# Retaining, preparing and challenging expert witnesses

**Goal:** get reliable expert evidence admitted for your side and exclude or undermine
the other side's. US federal practice (FRE 702, FRCP 26) is the model; state courts may
use *Frye* ("general acceptance") or their own variants, and England & Wales use CPR
Part 35, under which it is the expert's duty to help the court on matters within their expertise (CPR 35.3(1)).

## 1. Do you need one, and what kind?
Map each element that requires specialised knowledge (causation, standard of care,
damages, industry custom, technical function). Then decide:
- **Consulting (non-testifying) expert:** helps you understand the case; generally protected
  from discovery. Useful early.
- **Testifying expert:** their report, opinions and considered materials are discoverable.

## 2. Choosing the expert
- Real expertise in the precise question (a general engineer is not a specialist in
  battery thermal runaway).
- **Background check:** prior testimony, publications, prior exclusions (search
  verdict databases and Daubert-challenge records), statements that contradict your
  position, disciplinary history.
- Teaching ability: can they explain it simply to a jury?
- Conflicts with the other party; fee terms; availability through trial.

## 3. Engagement and work product
Engagement letter through counsel (scope, rates, confidentiality). In US federal court,
drafts of reports and most attorney-expert communications are protected (Rule 26(b)(4)(B)-(C)), except communications about compensation, facts or data the attorney
provided and the expert considered, and assumptions the attorney gave. Assume
anything else may be disclosed and write accordingly. State rules may protect less.

## 4. The report (US Rule 26(a)(2)(B))
Must include: complete statement of opinions with basis and reasons; facts or data
considered; exhibits; qualifications with publications in the last 10 years; prior
testimony in the last 4 years; compensation. Information not disclosed as Rule 26(a) requires cannot be used at trial unless the failure was substantially justified or harmless (Rule 37(c)(1)).
Make sure the methodology is stated step by step — the report is what a Daubert motion attacks.

## 5. Admissibility: Rule 702 / Daubert
The proponent must show **that it is more likely than not** (the wording of Rule 702 as amended in 2023) that:
the expert is qualified; testimony will help the trier of fact; it is based on sufficient
facts or data; it is the product of reliable principles and methods; and the opinion
reflects a reliable **application** of those methods to the facts.
Factors (*Daubert*, *Kumho Tire*): testability, peer review, error rate, standards,
general acceptance — flexibly applied, including to non-scientific experts.
**Attack lines:** analytical gap between data and conclusion ("ipse dixit"), cherry-picked
data, methodology developed for litigation, failure to consider alternative causes,
untested assumptions supplied by counsel, outside qualifications.

## 6. Depositions
**Defending your expert:** rehearse cross on weaknesses; they should concede what's true,
stay within their field, and not volunteer. **Deposing theirs:** lock in all opinions
("Have you given me every opinion you will offer at trial?"), the bases, what they didn't
do, assumptions, and fees — then you can confine them at trial.

## 7. Trial
Qualify efficiently (or accept stipulation), teach with demonstratives, and have the expert
explain the method before the conclusion. On cross of their expert: assumptions, omitted
data, compensation and repeat-witness patterns, and contradictions with literature.

## Other systems
CPR 35: experts must help the court on matters within their expertise (35.3(1)); the court may direct that
evidence on an issue be given by a single joint expert (35.7(1)) or direct discussions between experts (35.12).
Other systems differ; check local rules.

## Gotchas
- Late or missing disclosure risks exclusion (Rule 37(c)(1)).
- Giving the expert a privileged document can waive privilege over it.
- An expert who testifies on legal conclusions ("the defendant was negligent") invites
  exclusion of that opinion.

## Verify
- [ ] Each element needing expertise has an expert with matching qualifications.
- [ ] Report contains all required components and a stepwise methodology.
- [ ] Rule 702 factors addressed for your experts; attack plan for theirs.
- [ ] Disclosure deadlines diarised, including rebuttal.
