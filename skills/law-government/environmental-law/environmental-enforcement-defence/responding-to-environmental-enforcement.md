# Responding to environmental enforcement

**Goal:** contain legal, financial and reputational exposure from a regulator's
inquiry, inspection or prosecution — and come out with achievable compliance terms.

## 1. Inspections and information requests
- Have an inspection protocol: who greets inspectors, who accompanies them, take
  duplicate samples and photos, note what was viewed and said, mark confidential
  business information.
- Know the authority: statutory information requests (e.g. CAA § 114, CWA § 308)
  are compulsory, with criminal penalties for false statements. Answer accurately,
  narrowly and on time; negotiate scope and deadlines in writing.
- Employees interviewed may need separate counsel; give Upjohn-type warnings where
  company counsel attends.
- Preserve documents once enforcement is foreseeable.

## 2. Notice of violation / warning letter
Read it for: the precise legal provision, the facts alleged, the period, and the
regulator's ask. Respond with facts and law: wrong facts, inapplicable rule,
compliance already restored, mitigating circumstances. Request a conference before
positions harden.

## 3. Self-disclosure
US EPA's Audit Policy (April 2000; see epa.gov/compliance/epas-audit-policy): gravity-based penalty reduced by 100% (or 75% if discovery was not systematic) for
violations discovered through a systematic audit or compliance management system,
voluntarily disclosed within 21 days, corrected within 60 days, and not repeated —
with exclusions for repeat violations, serious actual harm, imminent and substantial endangerment and violations of orders. Many states have audit
privilege/immunity laws. Weigh disclosure against citizen-suit and criminal risk with care.

## 4. Penalty calculation and negotiation
US civil penalty policies generally = **economic benefit** of noncompliance (delayed
or avoided costs, modelled via the BEN model) + **gravity** (harm potential, extent,
duration), adjusted for culpability, cooperation, history, and **ability to pay**.
Attack the inputs: violation duration, costs avoided, discount rates.
Settlement tools: compliance schedules, stipulated penalties for future breaches,
**supplemental environmental projects** (*uncertain*: US DOJ policy on SEPs has
changed repeatedly — check current policy), injunctive relief scope, termination criteria.

## 5. England & Wales tools
Advice and guidance → warning → enforcement/suspension notices → civil sanctions
(enforcement undertakings, variable monetary penalties) → prosecution. Enforcement
undertakings (offer to restore, compensate, donate) can avoid prosecution if accepted.
On conviction, the Sentencing Council guideline uses culpability × harm categories and
company turnover bands, so fines for large organisations can be very large.

## 6. Criminal exposure
Many environmental statutes have strict-liability or negligence-based offences;
knowing violations carry imprisonment (US CWA/CAA felonies). Signs a matter may be
criminal: investigators with law-enforcement credentials, search warrants, grand jury
subpoenas, interest in individual employees. Bring in white-collar counsel; manage
parallel civil/criminal proceedings.

## 7. Citizen suits
US statutes allow citizen suits after 60-day notice (e.g. CWA § 505); diligent
government prosecution can bar them. Resolving with the regulator first can pre-empt them.

## Gotchas
- Oral statements to inspectors are evidence; brief site staff.
- A consent order with unachievable deadlines generates stipulated penalties later.
- Uncertain: enforcement priorities and policies change with administrations and
  regulators; confirm current penalty policies.

## Verify
- [ ] Inspection protocol followed and split samples/notes kept.
- [ ] Information responses accurate, scoped and timely.
- [ ] Self-disclosure eligibility assessed (and timing met if used).
- [ ] Penalty inputs (duration, economic benefit) independently checked.
- [ ] Consent terms technically achievable; criminal exposure assessed.
