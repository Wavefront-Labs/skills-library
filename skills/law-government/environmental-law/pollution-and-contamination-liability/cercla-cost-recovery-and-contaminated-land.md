# CERCLA cost recovery and contaminated land

**Goal:** identify who pays to clean up contamination, protect clients buying or
selling affected property, and recover costs from others.

## 1. US Superfund (CERCLA) basics
**Four classes of potentially responsible parties (42 U.S.C. § 9607(a), text confirmed)**:
1. Current owner/operator of the facility;
2. Owner/operator at the time of disposal;
3. **Arrangers** of disposal (generators) — requires intent to dispose (*Burlington Northern*);
4. **Transporters** who selected the site.
Liability is **strict**, **retroactive**, and **joint and several** unless a party proves
a reasonable basis for divisibility (*Burlington Northern*, 2009 (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)). Causation is
minimal: release or threatened release of a hazardous substance causing response costs.

## 2. Defences and protections
- Statutory defences (§ 107(b)): act of God, act of war, act of an unrelated third
  party (narrow) — rarely succeed.
- **Bona fide prospective purchaser**, **contiguous property owner** and **innocent
  landowner** protections — all require **All Appropriate Inquiries** before
  acquisition (Phase I ESA per ASTM E1527-21 (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked); under 40 CFR 312.20 certain components must be conducted or updated within 180 days before acquisition and the whole inquiry within one year; the 180-day figure is confirmed in the eCFR) plus continuing obligations (reasonable steps, cooperation,
  land-use controls compliance).
- De minimis/de micromis settlements for small contributors.
- Petroleum is excluded from CERCLA's "hazardous substance" definition (42 U.S.C. § 9601(14), confirmed) — but state laws and RCRA may cover it.
- PFOA and PFOS were designated CERCLA hazardous substances in 2024 (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked). *Uncertain:*
  EPA announced reconsideration in 2025 (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) — verify current status.

## 3. Getting money back
- **§ 107 cost recovery**: party that incurred response costs sues PRPs (joint and
  several; limitation under § 9613(g)(2): 3 years after completion of a removal action, 6 years after initiation of physical on-site construction of a remedial action; confirmed).
- **§ 113(f) contribution**: after being sued or settling with the government;
  equitable allocation (Gore factors (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)); 3-year limitation from judgment or qualifying settlement (§ 9613(g)(3), confirmed).
  Which one you can use matters (*Atlantic Research*, 2007; *Territory of Guam*, 2021 (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)).
- Response costs must be "necessary and consistent with the National Contingency Plan".
- Settling with EPA gives contribution protection against other PRPs' claims.

## 4. Transactional protection
Phase I (and Phase II sampling where indicated), environmental indemnities with caps,
survival periods and baskets, escrows, pollution legal liability insurance,
allocation of known vs. unknown conditions, access and remediation covenants.

## 5. England & Wales: Part 2A EPA 1990
Local authority (or Environment Agency for special sites) identifies contaminated
land (significant harm/significant pollution of controlled waters). Liability rests
first with **Class A** persons (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) — those who caused or knowingly permitted the
contamination; if none can be found, **Class B** — current owners/occupiers. Detailed
exclusion and apportionment tests apply. Much contamination is in practice dealt with
through planning conditions on redevelopment.

## 6. Toxic torts (private claims)
Nuisance, trespass, negligence, strict liability (*Rylands v Fletcher*; foreseeability
required — *Cambridge Water*), and US state statutory claims. Key battles: general
and specific causation (expert admissibility under *Daubert*/Rule 702), latency and
discovery-rule limitation, medical monitoring (recognised in some states only),
property diminution.

## Gotchas
- Buying contaminated property without timely AAI loses BFPP protection.
- Corporate successors and parents (direct operator liability — *Bestfoods* (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)) may be liable.
- Uncertain: state superfund statutes and PFAS rules vary and are changing rapidly.
