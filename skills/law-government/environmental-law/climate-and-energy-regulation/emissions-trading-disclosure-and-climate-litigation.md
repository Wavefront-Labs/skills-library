# Emissions trading, disclosure and climate litigation

**Goal:** keep clients compliant with carbon-pricing and disclosure regimes, steer
low-carbon projects through approvals, and assess climate litigation risk on both sides.

## 1. Emissions trading: the compliance cycle
Most cap-and-trade schemes (EU ETS, UK ETS, California-Québec, RGGI for power,
China's national ETS) share a cycle:
1. **Coverage**: is the installation/activity in scope (thresholds by capacity or
   emissions; sectors such as power, industry, aviation, maritime in the EU ETS)?
2. **Permit / monitoring plan** approved by the regulator.
3. **Monitoring, reporting, verification (MRV)**: annual emissions report verified
   by an accredited verifier.
4. **Allocation**: free allocation (benchmarks, leakage lists) and/or auction purchases.
5. **Surrender** allowances equal to verified emissions by the deadline (EU ETS:
   30 September of the following year since 2024). Shortfall → fixed excess-emissions
   penalty per tonne *plus* the obligation to surrender the missing allowances.
Advise on registry account controls, offset/credit eligibility limits, and
transaction tax/accounting.
**CBAM** (EU): importers of covered goods (steel, aluminium, cement, fertilisers,
electricity, hydrogen) report embedded emissions in the transitional phase and buy
CBAM certificates from 2026 (definitive period). *Uncertain:* thresholds and timelines
were amended in 2025 — check current rules.

## 2. Climate disclosure
| Regime | Notes |
|---|---|
| ISSB IFRS S2 | baseline adopted or being adopted in many jurisdictions (e.g. UK SRS, Australia, Japan) |
| EU CSRD/ESRS | double materiality; scope and timing cut back by the 2025 Omnibus — *uncertain*, verify current thresholds |
| US SEC rule (2024) | stayed and the SEC stopped defending it in 2025 — treat as not in force |
| California SB 253 / SB 261 | GHG (scope 1–3) and climate-risk reporting for large entities doing business in California; first reports from 2026 (litigation ongoing) |
Practice points: governance of disclosures, controls over GHG data, safe harbours for
scope 3 and forward-looking statements, consistency with marketing claims
(greenwashing enforcement under consumer and securities law).

## 3. Renewable and grid projects
Typical legal path: site control (leases/easements) → permits (land use, wildlife,
water, aviation) → impact assessment → grid interconnection queue → offtake (PPA)
→ financing. Wildlife conflicts (eagles, bats, protected habitats) and
interconnection delays are common critical-path risks; see
`law-government.environmental-law.conservation-and-wildlife-law.protected-species-consultation-and-habitat-law`.

## 4. Climate litigation theories
- **Against governments**: human-rights/constitutional duties to set adequate targets
  (*Urgenda*; *KlimaSeniorinnen* — ECHR art. 8; German Constitutional Court 2021);
  challenges to project approvals for failing to assess emissions; the 2025 ICJ
  advisory opinion says states have obligations under international law to prevent
  significant harm to the climate system.
- **Against companies**: tort/nuisance and consumer-fraud suits (US state AG and
  municipal cases), corporate-duty cases (*Milieudefensie v Shell* — 2021 order
  overturned on appeal in 2024 as to a specific reduction percentage), securities
  and greenwashing claims, directors' duties.
- Defences: justiciability/political question, standing, causation and
  attribution, federal displacement/pre-emption (US).

## Gotchas
- Allowance surrender is a hard legal duty — late verification causes penalties.
- Disclosure regimes interact with liability: what you say in sustainability reports
  is discoverable and actionable.
- Uncertain: this area changes month to month; date-stamp any advice.

- [ ] ETS coverage, monitoring plan and surrender deadline confirmed for each installation.
- [ ] CBAM reporting obligations checked for imports.
- [ ] Applicable disclosure regimes mapped with current thresholds.
- [ ] Marketing claims consistent with disclosures.
- [ ] Litigation exposure reviewed against current case law.
