# Obtaining and defending environmental permits

**Goal:** get a permit that lets the project operate as designed, with conditions the
operator can actually meet — and protect it on appeal.

## 1. Applicability: which permits, from whom?
Build a permit matrix for the project: medium (air, water discharge, stormwater,
wetlands/dredge-and-fill, waste, abstraction), regulator (federal/national,
state/regional, local), trigger thresholds, and lead times.
- US examples: Clean Air Act New Source Review/PSD for major sources (pre-construction)
  and Title V operating permits; NPDES for point-source discharges; CWA s.404 (Army
  Corps) for fill in waters of the US (scope narrowed by *Sackett v. EPA*, 2023).
- England & Wales: single Environmental Permitting regime (installations, waste,
  discharges); EU-derived BAT requirements under the Industrial Emissions Directive.
Watch for **synthetic minor** strategies (accept enforceable limits to stay below
major-source thresholds) and **aggregation** rules (related activities treated as one source).

## 2. Pre-application
Meet the regulator early: confirm scope, data needs, modelling protocols, monitoring
expectations, timeline, and whether an impact assessment is triggered (see
`law-government.environmental-law.environmental-impact-assessment-law.screening-scoping-and-adequacy-challenges`).
Agree baseline monitoring before it's needed — seasonal data can add a year.

## 3. The application
- Completeness is the first gate; incomplete applications reset clocks.
- Technical basis: emissions inventory or discharge characterisation, control
  technology (BACT/LAER/BAT) analysis, dispersion or mixing-zone modelling,
  site condition report.
- Keep confidentiality claims narrow and justified; public registers are the default.
- Engineering and legal teams must agree on operating scenarios (maximum capacity,
  startup/shutdown) — permits limit what you describe.

## 4. Negotiating the draft permit
Review each condition for: legal basis, technical feasibility, monitoring method and
frequency, averaging period, reporting deadline, exceedance consequences,
startup/shutdown/malfunction handling, flexibility for changes. Ask for compliance
schedules for new requirements. Get any "permit shield" language right (protection
for compliance with included terms, where available).

## 5. Public participation
Draft permits usually go to public comment (and hearings when requested). Raise every
issue you might appeal **during** comment — most regimes bar issues not preserved
(US 40 C.F.R. § 124.19 for EPA permits). Respond to community concerns substantively;
environmental justice analysis increasingly expected (status varies by agency and administration).

## 6. Appeals
- Applicant or objector appeal routes: US EPA permits → Environmental Appeals Board
  then federal court; state permits → state boards/courts. England & Wales → appeal
  to the Secretary of State (Planning Inspectorate), judicial review for third parties.
- Deadlines are short (EPA permit appeals to the Environmental Appeals Board: 30 days after notice of the decision, 40 CFR 124.19(a)(3)); some conditions are stayed pending appeal, others not.

## 7. Living with the permit
Variations for changes, transfers on sale (regulator consent often needed),
renewal applications well ahead of expiry, surrender with site condition proof.

## Gotchas
- Construction before a pre-construction permit is a violation even if the permit later issues.
- Conditions you accept now set precedent for your other sites.
- Uncertain: thresholds and procedural rules differ by state and change with
  rulemaking — confirm current regulations.

## Verify
- [ ] Permit matrix complete with triggers, regulators and lead times.
- [ ] Application deemed complete; operating scenarios match engineering design.
- [ ] Every draft condition reviewed for basis, feasibility and monitoring.
- [ ] All appealable issues raised in the comment period.
- [ ] Renewal, variation and transfer dates diarised.
