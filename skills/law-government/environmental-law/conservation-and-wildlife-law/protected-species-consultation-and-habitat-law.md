# Protected-species consultation and habitat law

**Goal:** get projects through species and habitat protections lawfully — or use those
protections to protect species — knowing where the legal triggers and choke points are.

## 1. US Endangered Species Act
- **Listing (s.4)**: endangered or threatened, based on best available science;
  petitions trigger a 90-day (to the maximum extent practicable) and a 12-month finding (16 U.S.C. 1533(b)(3)). **Critical habitat** designated
  "to the maximum extent prudent and determinable" (1533(a)(3)(A)), after considering economic, national-security and other impacts, with exclusion possible if benefits of exclusion outweigh inclusion unless extinction would result (1533(b)(2)). *Weyerhaeuser v. USFWS*, No. 17-71 (27 Nov 2018): unoccupied areas must be
  "habitat" to be designated, and exclusion decisions are reviewable.
- **Take prohibition (s.9)**: take = harass, harm, pursue, hunt, shoot, wound, kill,
  trap, capture, collect. "Harm" includes significant habitat modification that
  actually kills or injures wildlife (*Babbitt v. Sweet Home*, 515 U.S. 687 (1995)). Applies to everyone.
  Threatened species' protections depend on the applicable 4(d) rule (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
- **Section 7 consultation** (federal nexus — federal permit, funding, land):
  agency determines "may affect" → informal consultation / concurrence ("not likely to
  adversely affect") or formal consultation → **biological opinion**: jeopardy or
  adverse modification? If no jeopardy, an **incidental take statement** with
  reasonable and prudent measures exempts take that complies. Formal consultation concludes within 90 days of initiation, extendable by agreement, and the
  Service delivers the opinion within 45 days after concluding (50 CFR 402.14(e), (l)).
- **Section 10 (no federal nexus)**: incidental take permit backed by a **habitat
  conservation plan** (minimise and mitigate to the maximum extent practicable,
  adequate funding, and the assurances for changed or unforeseen circumstances in 50 CFR 17.22(b)(5) ("no surprises"); 16 U.S.C. 1539(a)(2)).
- Related: Migratory Bird Treaty Act (DOI's position on incidental take has changed repeatedly: check the current interpretation) and Bald and Golden Eagle Protection Act permits (current permit rules not verified here).

## 2. EU/UK habitats law
- **Habitats Regulations Assessment** for plans/projects likely to have a significant
  effect on a European/Natura 2000 site (in the UK now "national site network"):
  screening → **appropriate assessment** → consent only if no adverse effect on site
  integrity beyond reasonable scientific doubt (precautionary: *Waddenzee*, C-127/02, 7 Sep 2004, "no reasonable scientific doubt"; Habitats Directive art 6(3)).
  Otherwise only via IROPI (imperative reasons of overriding public interest) +
  no alternatives + compensatory measures.
- Mitigation can't be counted at the screening stage (*People Over Wind*, C-323/17, 12 Apr 2018).
- **European protected species** (e.g. great crested newts, bats): derogation licences
  need a listed purpose, no satisfactory alternative, and no detriment to favourable
  conservation status (Directive art 16(1); strict protection under art 12). Recent English planning reforms
  (e.g. environmental delivery plans) are not verified here: check current law.
- Domestic designations: SSSIs (consent for operations likely to damage).

## 3. Private conservation tools
- **Conservation easements/covenants**: US conservation easements (perpetual,
  tax-deduction scrutiny by IRS (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)); England's statutory conservation covenants under
  the Environment Act 2021 with responsible bodies (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
- **Biodiversity net gain** (England: 10% mandatory since 12 Feb 2024 per GOV.UK, with exemptions that GOV.UK says change from Aug 2026 for small sites), mitigation and conservation banking (US), offset agreements — check legal
  security, duration (minimum 30 years for BNG habitats per GOV.UK), monitoring and enforcement.

## Gotchas
- Survey seasons (bats, amphibians, nesting birds) can delay projects by a year — plan early.
- Listing a species mid-project can impose new obligations; watch candidate lists.
- ESA implementing regulations (50 CFR 402 and others) have been revised several times; confirm the text in force (402.14 read from eCFR 2026-10-15).

## Verify
- [ ] Listed/candidate species and designated habitats screened for the site.
- [ ] Federal nexus identified → s.7; or s.10 HCP route chosen.
- [ ] HRA screening done without counting mitigation; AA conclusion documented.
- [ ] Licences/permits in hand before works affecting protected species.
- [ ] Offsets/covenants legally secured and monitored.
