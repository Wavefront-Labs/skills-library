# Resolving boundary, easement and covenant disputes

**Goal:** establish what each neighbour actually owns and may do, then resolve the
dispute at a cost proportionate to the land involved — these disputes routinely cost
more than the strip of land at stake. England & Wales and US common-law states are the
models; prescription periods, adverse-possession rules for registered land and covenant
modification procedures vary, so confirm locally.

## 1. Early triage (proportionality first)
- Value the land and the practical issue (access, parking, a fence line, a view).
- Ask what the client actually needs: a usable access, a clear title for sale, or to stop
  a building project. Many matters end with a boundary agreement or a granted easement.
- Preserve evidence: dated photographs, old fences, aerial imagery, witness statements
  from long-term neighbours.

## 2. Boundaries
1. Collect title documents: conveyance plans, transfer plans, filed plans; note that many
   registered plans show **general boundaries** only.
2. Construe the conveyance that first split the land: words first, then plan, then
   extrinsic evidence of what the parties intended on the ground (features existing at
   that date).
3. Instruct a qualified land/boundary surveyor (single joint expert where possible).
4. Options: **boundary agreement** recorded with the registry; application to determine
   the exact boundary; court proceedings or tribunal referral if contested.

| Evidence | Weight |
|---|---|
| Words of the root conveyance | Primary |
| Conveyance plan ("for identification only" plans carry less weight) | Strong, depends on wording |
| Physical features at the date of the split | Strong |
| Later conduct and fences | Supporting, or adverse possession |

## 3. Easements
- **Express** grant/reservation: construe the words; check scope (vehicles, times,
  purposes) and route.
- **Implied**: necessity, common intention, quasi-easements on division of land, statutory
  conversion of rights on conveyance (E&W) — fact-sensitive.
- **Prescription** (E&W: 20 years' uninterrupted use (Prescription Act 1832 s.2) as of right — without force, secrecy or
  permission; US: varies by state; use must be open, continuous,
  adverse). Permission defeats it — look for licences, signs, payments.
- Issues: excessive use, obstruction, relocation, maintenance contribution, abandonment
  (hard to prove).

## 4. Restrictive covenants
- Who has the benefit (annexation, assignment, building scheme)? Is the burden binding
  (registered, notice)? Breach already occurred?
- Remedies for the beneficiary: injunction (act fast — delay weakens the claim), damages
  in lieu.
- For the burdened owner: indemnity insurance (do not approach beneficiaries first), release,
  or application to modify/discharge (E&W Upper Tribunal Lands Chamber; US: changed
  conditions doctrine, courts).

## 5. Adverse possession
- Unregistered/US: open, notorious, exclusive, continuous, hostile possession for the
  statutory period (varies; some states require tax payment or colour of title).
- E&W registered land: application after 10 years' possession (Land Registration Act 2002 Sch 6 para 1); the registered owner is notified
  (para 2) and if they object the applicant succeeds only if a para 5 condition is met — check current conditions.
- Defending: show permission, interruptions, or acts of ownership.

## 6. Resolution
Letter of claim with plan and evidence → expert-led site meeting → mediation (strongly
expected) → registry or tribunal determination → court (quiet title, declaration,
injunction, damages for trespass or nuisance). Build costs warnings into advice early.

## Gotchas
- Moving a fence or removing a structure unilaterally escalates and may be trespass.
- Treating a registered general-boundary plan as precise.
- Granting written permission to a neighbour's use — it stops prescription (useful for owners).
- Delay before seeking an injunction against building works.

## Verify
- [ ] Root conveyance identified and construed; surveyor's report on file.
- [ ] Each claimed right classified (express, implied, prescriptive) with evidence.
- [ ] Covenant benefit and burden traced.
- [ ] Limitation and possession periods calculated.
- [ ] Proportionality and settlement options advised in writing.
