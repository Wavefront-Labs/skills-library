# Examining and curing title

**Goal:** tell the client, in writing, exactly what they will own, subject to what, and
how each problem will be removed, insured over or accepted before money changes hands.
Two models: **recording systems** (most US states — title proved by a chain of recorded
deeds, protected by title insurance) and **registration systems** (England & Wales,
Torrens jurisdictions — the register largely is the title). Search periods, marketable-title
acts and registration rules vary by state and country.

## 1. Gather the evidence
- **Registered land:** official copies of the register and title plan; copies of
  documents referred to on the register (old conveyances with covenants, leases, easements).
- **Recording systems:** search the grantor-grantee (or tract) index back to a sovereign
  patent or the period set by local title standards or marketable-title act (often
  several decades — check); pull each deed, mortgage, release, lien, judgment, probate
  and lis pendens. Or obtain a **title commitment** from an insurer.
- Survey or plan; tax and utility searches; physical inspection notes (occupiers, paths,
  overhead lines) because possession and some rights are not on any record.

## 2. Build and test the chain
For each link record: instrument, date, grantor/grantee (exact names), legal description,
execution and acknowledgement, recording reference. Test:
- Names match from one link to the next (marriage, misspellings, entity name changes).
- Legal description is consistent and closes.
- Each grantor had capacity and authority (corporate resolutions, trustee powers,
  personal representative's authority, powers of attorney valid at signing).
- Gaps (a death with no probate, a missing deed) and wild deeds.

## 3. Read the encumbrances

| Item | Ask |
|---|---|
| Mortgages/charges | Will they be discharged at completion? Payoff letters obtained? |
| Liens (tax, mechanics', judgment) | Amount, priority, release route |
| Easements | Benefit or burden? Location? Affects proposed use or building? |
| Covenants/restrictions | Still enforceable? Breached already? Restrict intended use? |
| Leases and occupiers | Rights that bind the buyer (overriding interests, possession) |
| Options, pre-emption rights, pending litigation | Can they defeat the transaction? |

For a US title commitment, review **Schedule B** exceptions line by line and request
removal or affirmative endorsements; standard exceptions (survey, parties in possession)
can usually be deleted with a survey and owner's affidavit.

## 4. Classify and cure each defect

| Defect | Typical cure |
|---|---|
| Unreleased old mortgage | Obtain release/discharge from lender or successor; statutory discharge procedure |
| Name/description error | Corrective deed, scrivener's affidavit, rectification of register |
| Missing heir/probate gap | Probate or heirship affidavits; quitclaims from heirs |
| Breach of covenant | Retrospective consent, release, indemnity insurance, application to modify/discharge |
| Boundary/encroachment | Boundary agreement, easement grant, survey endorsement |
| Fundamental doubt | Quiet title action / rectification proceedings |

Agree with the other side who cures what by when; put it in the contract as a
condition or a completion requirement.

## 5. Report and insure
- Title report: what is owned, rights benefiting it, every encumbrance with your
  advice, required cures, and what the insurance will and will not cover.
- Check the pro forma title policy or indemnity policy wording against the defect; an
  indemnity policy can be voided by contacting the person who could enforce.

## Gotchas
- Searching only the current owner's name.
- Assuming a covenant is unenforceable because it is old.
- Contacting a covenant beneficiary before buying indemnity insurance.
- Off-record rights: prescriptive easements, occupiers, unregistered short leases.

## Verify
- [ ] Chain complete for the required period with no unexplained gaps.
- [ ] Every encumbrance and Schedule B exception addressed in writing.
- [ ] Each defect has an agreed cure, owner and deadline.
- [ ] Title policy/indemnity wording matches the risk.
- [ ] Post-completion register or recorded title checked for clean result.
