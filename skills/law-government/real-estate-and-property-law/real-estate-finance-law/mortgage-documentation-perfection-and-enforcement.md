# Mortgage documentation, perfection and enforcement

**Goal:** give the lender enforceable, first-priority security over real estate and its
income, and know exactly how to realise it on default; for borrowers, keep the terms
workable. US (mortgage/deed of trust states) and England & Wales are the models;
recording, foreclosure and borrower-protection rules differ sharply between states and
countries.

## 1. The document set

| Document | Job |
|---|---|
| Term sheet / commitment | Commercial terms, CPs, fees |
| Loan or facility agreement + promissory note | Debt, interest, covenants (LTV, DSCR, debt yield), events of default |
| Mortgage / deed of trust / legal charge | Security over land and buildings |
| Assignment of leases and rents | Captures income; lender can collect on default |
| Security over accounts, fixtures and personal property | US UCC-1 financing statements including fixture filing |
| Guaranties | Completion, non-recourse carve-out ("bad boy"), environmental indemnity |
| SNDAs and tenant estoppels | Leases survive foreclosure if tenants attorn; confirms rent and defaults |
| Intercreditor / subordination | Ranks mezzanine or second-lien debt |

## 2. Conditions precedent checklist
Title report and lender's title insurance (or E&W certificate of title / report on title),
survey, zoning confirmation, insurance with lender named as loss payee/mortgagee,
valuation, environmental report, corporate authorities and legal opinions, KYC, payoff
letters for existing debt, and signed security documents in recordable form.

## 3. Perfection and priority
- **US:** record the mortgage/deed of trust in the county where the land lies; priority
  generally follows recording under the state's race, notice or race-notice statute.
  File UCC-1s (state level and fixture filing in the real estate records). The title policy
  insures the priority subject to listed exceptions.
- **E&W:** register the legal charge at HM Land Registry within the buyer/lender's
  priority search period; for a company borrower, also register the charge at Companies
  House within the statutory period (21 days beginning with the day after creation, Companies Act 2006 s.859A(4)) or it is void against a
  liquidator, administrator or creditor (s.859H(3)).
- Check future-advance and modification rules: increasing the loan or extending the term
  can lose priority to intervening interests in some jurisdictions.

## 4. Intercreditor essentials
Payment waterfall, standstill periods, cure rights and purchase options for the junior
lender, limits on amendments to senior documents, and (for US mezzanine) the junior's
right to foreclose on equity pledges under UCC Article 9 subject to the intercreditor.

## 5. Default and enforcement
1. Reservation-of-rights letter; avoid course-of-dealing waivers.
2. Demand and acceleration notices exactly as the documents and statute require.
3. Choose remedy:
   - **Non-judicial foreclosure** (power of sale under a deed of trust) — faster where
     permitted; strict notice and sale procedures.
   - **Judicial foreclosure** — court action; slower; may allow deficiency judgment.
   - **Receiver** — court-appointed (US) or LPA/fixed-charge receiver appointed by deed
     (E&W) to collect rents and sell.
   - Mortgagee in possession — usually avoided (liabilities).
   - Consensual: deed in lieu, discounted payoff, restructuring with forbearance.
4. Borrower protections: residential consumer rules, redemption rights, anti-deficiency
   statutes, bankruptcy automatic stay.

## Gotchas
- Wrong legal description or unsigned acknowledgment makes a mortgage unrecordable.
- Missing the corporate charge registration deadline in E&W.
- Lender taking control of the property and inheriting landlord or environmental liability.
- Collecting rents before the assignment of rents is enforceable under local law.
