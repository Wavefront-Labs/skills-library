# Conveyancing: from instruction to registration

**Goal:** the buyer gets good title, registered, with the funds moving safely and no
surprises discovered after the point of no return.

Terminology and sequence vary. England and Wales and similar systems use a two-stage
**exchange** (binding contract) then **completion**; much of the US uses a signed purchase
agreement with contingencies and an escrow **closing**; civil-law systems often route the
deed through a notary. Taxes, deadlines and registration rules are jurisdiction-specific.

## 1. Instruction and identity
- Identify the client and the property precisely (title number, legal description, plan).
- Anti-money-laundering checks: client identity, source of funds and source of wealth.
- Check authority: all registered owners must sign; companies need authorised signatories;
  attorneys need a valid power.

## 2. The contract and the title
- **Seller side:** prepare the draft contract and evidence of title; complete property
  information forms honestly (misrepresentation exposure).
- **Buyer side:** examine title (see
  `law-government.real-estate-and-property-law.title-examination.chain-of-title-and-encumbrances`),
  raise enquiries on anything unclear, and check special conditions (deposit, completion date,
  condition of property, chattels, vacant possession).

## 3. Searches and due diligence
Typical: local authority/municipal (planning, roads, enforcement), drainage and water,
environmental, flood, mining or other regional risks, and a survey or inspection. Commercial
and rural deals add more (rights of way, environmental permits, tenancies). Report findings
to the client in writing before they commit.

## 4. Finance and the point of commitment
- Mortgage offer received and its conditions satisfied; lender's instructions followed (you
  often act for the lender too).
- At **exchange** (or when contingencies lapse), the buyer is bound and usually forfeits the
  deposit if they fail to complete. Never exchange without: satisfactory searches, finance in
  place, buildings insurance arranged where risk passes on exchange, and the client's
  express instruction.

## 5. Completion / closing
- Pre-completion searches (registry priority search, bankruptcy/insolvency search) so no new
  entry appears in the gap.
- Funds: completion statement agreed; money sent by verified means only.
- On receipt of funds, seller releases keys and executed transfer; seller's lender is paid
  off and releases its charge.

## 6. Post-completion
Pay transfer taxes (stamp duty, land transfer tax) and file within deadlines; register the
transfer and new mortgage at the land registry within the priority period. Send the client
the registered title.

## Gotchas
- **Payment diversion fraud:** emails "updating" bank details are the most common loss.
  Confirm account details by phone to a known number before every transfer; tell clients you
  will never change details by email.
- **Seller identity fraud** on unencumbered, empty or rented-out properties; verify more
  closely.
- Missing a registration priority period can let another entry rank ahead.
- Unauthorised works (no planning or building consent) found after exchange.

## Verify
- [ ] Client ID, source of funds and authority to sell/buy checked.
- [ ] Title examined; enquiries answered in writing; search results reported to client.
- [ ] Finance conditions satisfied and insurance in place before commitment.
- [ ] Bank details verified by phone; completion statement agreed.
- [ ] Taxes paid and registration lodged within deadlines.
