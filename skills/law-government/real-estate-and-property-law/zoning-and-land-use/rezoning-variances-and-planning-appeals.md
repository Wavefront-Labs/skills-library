# Rezoning, variances and planning appeals

**Goal:** obtain the land-use approval a project needs — or defeat or condition one a
client opposes — on a record strong enough to survive appeal. US local zoning and the
England & Wales plan-led system are the models; procedure, tests and deadlines are set
by state enabling acts, local codes and national rules, so read the governing ordinance
first.

## 1. Diagnose the approval needed

| Problem | US route | Character | E&W route |
|---|---|---|---|
| Use not allowed in the district | Rezoning (map amendment) or text amendment | Legislative — broad discretion, plan consistency | Planning application (departure from plan) |
| Use allowed only with conditions | Special/conditional use permit | Quasi-judicial — criteria in code | Planning application with conditions |
| Dimensional rule can't be met (setback, height, parking) | Area variance (practical difficulty) | Quasi-judicial | Usually within planning judgment |
| Use barred and unique hardship | Use variance (unnecessary hardship — hard) | Quasi-judicial | — |
| Existing use pre-dates rules | Nonconforming use/lawful use certificate | Evidential | Lawful development certificate |

Pre-application meeting with planning staff: learn their concerns and the decision-maker's.

## 2. Build the application and record
- Plan consistency analysis: comprehensive/local plan policies, zoning code criteria —
  quote each criterion and show compliance. In E&W the decision must accord with the
  development plan unless material considerations indicate otherwise.
- Technical reports: traffic, drainage, environmental, heritage, design, noise, viability.
- Community engagement before filing; record changes made in response.
- For quasi-judicial matters, the decision must rest on evidence in the record:
  witnesses, expert testimony, cross-examination where allowed; no ex parte contact with
  decision-makers.

## 3. Hearings
1. Confirm notice (published, mailed, posted) was correct — defective notice voids approvals.
2. Short presentation tied to the criteria; experts available for questions.
3. Address opponents' points with evidence, not adjectives.
4. Propose conditions you can live with; draft them yourself.
5. Ensure findings of fact are adopted that track each criterion.

## 4. Agreements and exactions
- US development agreements and conditions must have an **essential nexus** and **rough
  proportionality** to the project's impacts (*Nollan/Dolan* line; applies to monetary
  exactions too). E&W: planning obligations (s.106) must meet statutory tests (necessary,
  directly related, fairly and reasonably related in scale); community infrastructure levy
  is separate.
- Record the agreement as binding on successors where needed.

## 5. Appeals and review
- **US:** administrative appeal (e.g. to a zoning board) then judicial review in court —
  typically on the record, asking whether the decision was supported by substantial
  evidence, followed procedure and applied the law. Deadlines are short and set by state or local law —
  check. Legislative rezonings get deferential review; watch spot-zoning and
  uniformity challenges.
- **E&W:** appeal against refusal or non-determination to the Planning Inspectorate within
  the time limit for the application type (gov.uk gives up to 6 months from the decision letter for a refusal or conditional grant, and 28 days where an enforcement notice preceded the refusal; check other application types), by
  written representations, hearing or inquiry. Statutory challenge or judicial review
  in the High Court within a short strict window (six weeks for planning judicial review, CPR 54.5(5)) on legal grounds only.

## Gotchas
- Self-created hardship defeats most variances.
- Missing the exhaustion requirement — courts refuse cases not taken through local appeals.
- Conditions or exactions accepted without objection may waive later challenge.
- Approval lapses if the permission is not implemented within its time limit.
