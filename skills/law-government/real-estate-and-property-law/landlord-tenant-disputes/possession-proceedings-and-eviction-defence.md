# Possession proceedings and eviction defence

**Goal:** for a landlord, recover possession lawfully without the case failing on a
technicality; for a tenant, find every procedural and substantive defence and any
counterclaim. The same checklist serves both.

Residential tenancy law is heavily statutory and changes often (notice periods, permitted
grounds, prescribed forms, pre-action requirements, temporary moratoria). Commercial
forfeiture follows different rules. Always check the current statute, forms and court rules
for the property's location and tenancy type. Tenants facing eviction should get advice
quickly; many places offer free housing duty or legal aid schemes.

## 1. Identify the tenancy
Type of tenancy (fixed term, periodic, protected/regulated, licence, lodger, public/social
housing) determines which grounds and notices apply and whether a court order is needed at
all. Get the written agreement, start date, rent history and any renewals.

## 2. The notice: most cases are won or lost here
Check:
- **Correct form** (prescribed form where required) and current version.
- **Correct ground** stated, with the facts relied on.
- **Notice period** calculated correctly, including service rules (deemed service dates,
  ending on a rent day where required).
- **Correct parties and address** (all tenants, the landlord as on the agreement).
- **Preconditions** the statute ties to the notice: deposit protected and information given,
  required certificates or licences supplied, pre-action letters sent. A failed precondition
  can make the notice invalid even where the ground is strong.

## 3. Grounds
- **Rent arrears:** compute precisely from a rent statement; check payments credited, housing
  benefit timing, disputed charges. Mandatory arrears grounds often depend on the arrears at
  the date of notice *and* the hearing.
- **No-fault** possession (where it still exists): heavily procedural; in some jurisdictions
  abolished or restricted.
- **Breach of tenancy** (nuisance, damage): evidence-based; court may have discretion and
  consider reasonableness.

## 4. Defences and counterclaims (tenant)
- Invalid notice or failed preconditions.
- **Disrepair / habitability:** counterclaim for damages, often set off against arrears;
  some regimes allow rent withholding or repair-and-deduct under strict conditions.
- **Deposit:** unprotected deposit penalties and possession restrictions where they apply.
- **Retaliatory eviction** protections after complaints about conditions.
- Discrimination, disability (proportionality), or public-authority landlord duties.
- Request time to pay or a suspended/postponed order where the court has discretion.

## 5. Hearing and enforcement
- Bring the agreement, notice, proof of service, rent statement and evidence of preconditions.
- After an order, only the lawful enforcement officer (bailiff, sheriff, marshal) may remove
  occupiers. **Self-help evictions** (changing locks, removing belongings, cutting utilities)
  are unlawful in most residential regimes and can be criminal.

## Gotchas
- Notice dated or served wrongly by one day.
- Arrears figure includes non-rent charges.
- Joint tenants not all served.
- Landlord harassment during the process damages the landlord's case and exposes them to claims.

## Verify
- [ ] Tenancy type and governing regime identified.
- [ ] Notice form, ground, period, parties and service checked against current rules.
- [ ] Statutory preconditions (deposit, certificates, pre-action) evidenced.
- [ ] Arrears schedule reconciled; counterclaims assessed.
- [ ] Enforcement only via court officer.
