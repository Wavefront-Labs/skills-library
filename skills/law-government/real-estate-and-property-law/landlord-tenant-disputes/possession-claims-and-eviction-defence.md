# Possession claims and eviction defence

**Goal:** recover possession lawfully and quickly (landlord) or prevent, delay or
condition it while pursuing the tenant's own claims (tenant). Most possession cases are
won or lost on **notice validity and procedure**, not the merits. Models: England
(residential and commercial) and US state summary-eviction procedure; residential
tenancy law is heavily statutory, local and frequently reformed — confirm the rules in
force on the notice date.

## 1. Classify the tenancy (day 1)
- Residential or commercial? Statutory regime (in England, the type of tenancy determines
  the grounds; the Renters' Rights Act 2025 ended assured shorthold tenancies and section 21 "no-fault"
  possession for most private tenancies from 1 May 2026 (SI 2026/421); check the transitional rules
  for notices served before that date). US: state statute plus any local
  just-cause or rent-stabilisation ordinance; federally backed or subsidised housing adds
  notice requirements.
- Get the tenancy agreement, rent ledger, all notices served, deposit records, safety and
  licensing certificates, and correspondence about repairs.

## 2. Check the notice

| Test | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Correct prescribed form/content | Wrong form usually means start again |
| Correct ground and particulars | Court can only order possession on grounds stated |
| Notice period | Varies by ground; count carefully, add service days |
| Service method and evidence | Certificate of service; follow lease clause |
| Pre-conditions met | Deposit protected and information given, licensing, safety certificates (residential England); pre-action protocols for rent arrears (social landlords) |

For **US** non-payment: a pay-or-quit notice (period set by state law), then an
unlawful-detainer/summary-possession complaint. For **commercial forfeiture** (E&W):
statutory notice for non-rent breaches; tenant can seek **relief from forfeiture**.

## 3. Landlord's claim
1. Issue in the correct court using the prescribed claim and particulars forms; attach
   tenancy, notice, proof of service, rent schedule.
2. Choose route: accelerated/paper procedures where available; otherwise a hearing.
3. Seek arrears, use and occupation charges/mesne profits, costs.
4. At hearing: proof of each element; mandatory grounds vs discretionary grounds where the court weighs reasonableness.

## 4. Tenant's defence and counterclaims
- Notice and procedural defects (most powerful).
- **Deposit**: unprotected or late-protected deposit can bar certain possession routes
  and attract a statutory penalty (England: 1 to 3 times the deposit, Housing Act 2004 s.214(4)).
  US: remedies for wrongful withholding are set by state statute.
- **Disrepair / habitability**: counterclaim for damages and set-off against arrears;
  evidence: photos, complaints log, environmental health reports.
- **Retaliation**: many regimes bar eviction following a repair complaint or protected
  activity.
- Discrimination, disability adjustments, payment of arrears before the hearing,
  housing benefit delays, and applications to adjourn or suspend the order on terms.

## 5. After judgment
- Order types: outright, suspended on terms (pay current rent plus a sum off arrears),
  money judgment only.
- Enforcement only by court officers (warrant/writ of possession, sheriff/marshal).
  **Self-help eviction** (changing locks, removing belongings, cutting utilities) is
  unlawful and in many places criminal.
- Tenant options: application to set aside, stay, or appeal within short time limits.

## 6. Settlement
Typical terms: surrender date, arrears waived or payment plan, deposit release, mutual
releases, neutral reference, consent order recording it. Get it on the court file.

## Gotchas
- Serving notice before the deposit or licensing pre-conditions are cured.
- Counting notice periods from the wrong day; ignoring weekends/holidays rules.
- Accepting rent after a forfeiture breach can waive the right to forfeit.
- Tenants missing the hearing — attendance alone often secures an adjournment or suspension.

## Verify
- [ ] Tenancy type and governing regime identified for the notice date.
- [ ] Notice form, period, ground and service each checked against the statute.
- [ ] Deposit, licensing and safety pre-conditions documented.
- [ ] Counterclaims (disrepair, deposit, retaliation) assessed and pleaded.
- [ ] Any order enforced only through the court officer; settlement terms in a consent order.
