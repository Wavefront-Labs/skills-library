# Challenging takings and claiming compensation

**Goal:** for a property owner facing acquisition, first test whether the taking can be
stopped or narrowed, then maximise the compensation actually payable, supported by
valuation evidence that survives cross-examination. Models: US eminent domain
(federal constitutional floor plus state statutes, which often add protections) and
England & Wales compulsory purchase. Heads of claim, procedure and fee recovery differ
widely — check the governing statute.

## 1. First meeting checklist
- Identify the acquiring authority, enabling power, project, and stage (planning,
  offer, petition filed / CPO made, confirmed, vesting).
- Diary every deadline: objection period, response to offer, statutory challenge window
  (E&W: six weeks from first publication of notice of confirmation or making of the order, Acquisition of Land Act 1981 s.23(4)), deadline to answer a condemnation
  petition, claim time limits.
- Map interests: freehold, leases, mortgages, options, business occupiers — each may
  have a separate claim.
- Advise the client not to sign possession or "right of entry" agreements without review.

## 2. Challenge the taking (where realistic)

| Ground | US | E&W |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Public use (construed broadly after *Kelo*; many states narrowed it by statute) | Compelling case in the public interest; enabling power covers the purpose |
| Necessity / extent | Taking more than needed; bad faith | Land not needed; alternatives not considered |
| Procedure | Failure to negotiate in good faith, defective notice, environmental review gaps | Defective notices, failure to follow procedure, irrationality |
| Forum | Objections in the condemnation action; inverse condemnation for de facto or regulatory takings | Objection → public inquiry or written procedure → statutory challenge in High Court |

Even unsuccessful objections can win design changes, accommodation works or undertakings.

## 3. Compensation heads
- **Market value** of land taken, at highest and best use, ignoring the effect of the
  scheme itself (US project-influence rule; E&W **no-scheme** principle).
- **Partial takings:** severance damages / injurious affection to the remainder, less
  any special benefits where the jurisdiction allows offset.
- **Disturbance**: relocation, business losses, professional fees (E&W, statutory
  payments such as home loss and basic/occupier's loss — check rates); US Uniform
  Relocation Act benefits for federally funded projects, often separate from just
  compensation.
- Interest from the date of taking; advance payments on account where available.

## 4. Build the valuation evidence
1. Retain an appraiser/valuer early; insist on site-specific highest-and-best-use analysis
   (zoning probability, development potential, assemblage).
2. Comparable sales adjusted with explained, supportable adjustments; income and cost
   approaches as cross-checks.
3. Business-loss experts (forensic accountants) for disturbance claims.
4. Check the authority's appraisal for scope assumptions (before/after methodology,
   wrong date of valuation, project influence baked in).
5. Expert reports must comply with court/tribunal expert duties; the expert's duty is to
   the tribunal, not the client.

## 5. Resolve
- Negotiate with written offers and counter-offers; many jurisdictions shift costs or
  fees if the final award exceeds the last offer by a margin — know the rule.
- Trial: US often before a jury on value; E&W Upper Tribunal (Lands Chamber).
- Settlement documents: price, interest, date of possession, accommodation works,
  tax treatment (e.g. US involuntary conversion deferral), release scope.

## Gotchas
- Accepting relocation or early-entry payments that waive compensation claims.
- Valuing the property as presently used when a higher use is reasonably probable.
- Overlooking tenants', lenders' and business occupiers' separate claims.
- Missing short statutory challenge windows.

## Verify
- [ ] Power, stage and every deadline recorded.
- [ ] Objection/challenge grounds assessed in writing.
- [ ] All compensation heads listed with evidence for each.
- [ ] Expert reports compliant and tested against the authority's appraisal.
- [ ] Offer/fee-shifting strategy and tax treatment advised.
