# Running a trust as trustee

**Goal:** administer a trust so that every decision is within the deed, made for the
right reasons, recorded, and defensible if a beneficiary later complains. US (UTC states)
and England & Wales are the models; trustee duties are broadly similar across common-law
systems but information rights, accounting rules and tax filings differ.

## 1. Taking office (first 60 days)
- Read the whole deed and every amendment, deed of appointment and letter of wishes;
  summarise: beneficiaries, distribution standards, powers, restrictions, trustee
  succession, governing law, termination events.
- Accept office in writing; confirm co-trustees and how decisions are made (unanimity or
  majority).
- Take control of assets: retitle accounts and property into the trustees' names; obtain
  statements and valuations; review the predecessor's accounts and seek a handover
  release or note concerns.
- Notify beneficiaries as required (UTC: inform qualified beneficiaries of the trust's
  existence and their right to information within a set period — check).
- Register the trust where required (e.g. UK Trust Registration Service).

## 2. Investment
- Apply the **prudent investor** standard: portfolio-level risk/return, diversification
  unless a special reason, costs, needs of income and remainder beneficiaries.
- Write an **investment policy statement**; take and review proper advice (E&W statutory
  duty to obtain and consider advice in most cases).
- Delegate to managers only within the power to delegate; monitor them.
- Special assets (family business, residence, concentrated stock) — check whether the deed
  permits retention and document the reasons.

## 3. Distribution decisions
For each discretionary decision:
1. Identify the power and its standard (absolute discretion, HEMS, sole benefit).
2. Gather facts: the beneficiary's needs and other resources (if the deed says consider
   them), effect on other beneficiaries, tax, means-tested benefits.
3. Consider the letter of wishes without treating it as binding.
4. Decide; record a **distribution memo** or minute with reasons and documents.
5. Pay to the beneficiary or directly to providers where protective.
Never fetter discretion by promise; never distribute to yourself as trustee-beneficiary
without the deed's permission and any required independent co-trustee.

## 4. Records and accounting
- Separate trust bank account; no commingling.
- Allocate receipts and expenses between **income and principal (capital)** under the deed
  or default statute; consider power to adjust or unitrust conversion where available.
- Annual accounts/reports to beneficiaries entitled to them: opening balance, receipts,
  disbursements, distributions, gains/losses, closing assets at value, trustee fees.
- Keep minutes of decisions and copies of advice.

## 5. Tax

| Item | Example |
|---|---|
| Trust income tax return | US Form 1041 (and beneficiary K-1s); UK Trust and Estate Tax Return (SA900) via Self Assessment (paper by 31 October, online by 31 January) |
| Periodic/exit charges | UK relevant-property trusts: ten-year and exit IHT charges |
| Withholding/reporting | Foreign beneficiaries, international reporting regimes |

Time distributions with tax in mind (e.g. US distributable-net-income carry-out).

## 6. Conflicts and difficult beneficiaries
- Self-dealing is presumptively voidable; get deed authority, beneficiary consent with
  full disclosure, or court approval.
- Respond to information requests promptly and courteously; disclose what the law
  requires, protect what it allows (e.g. reasons for discretionary decisions in E&W are
  often protected).
- Seek court directions on genuine doubt rather than guess.

## 7. Termination
On the termination event: final accounting, tax clearances, reserve for liabilities,
distribution, receipts and releases or court discharge.

## Gotchas
- Leaving assets in the settlor's or deceased's name.
- Paying for a beneficiary's request without considering others' interests.
- No written record of why a distribution was made or refused.
- Missing ten-year charge or annual return deadlines.

## Verify
- [ ] Deed summary and acceptance on file; assets retitled.
- [ ] Investment policy written and reviewed annually.
- [ ] Every distribution has a dated memo with reasons.
- [ ] Accounts issued; income/principal allocation explained.
- [ ] Tax returns and registrations up to date.
