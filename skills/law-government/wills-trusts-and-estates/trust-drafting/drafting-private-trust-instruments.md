# Drafting private trust instruments

**Goal:** a trust deed that a trustee can administer for decades without going to court,
that delivers the settlor's intended tax and protective results, and that can adapt when
law and family circumstances change. US (Uniform Trust Code states) and England & Wales
are the models; tax treatment of trusts differs radically between systems, so pair every
draft with a tax review (see the estate-and-gift-tax-planning skill).

## 1. Choose the structure

| Need | Structure | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Probate avoidance, incapacity management | Revocable living trust (US) | No asset protection or estate-tax saving while revocable; must be funded |
| Move growth out of estate, protect beneficiaries | Irrevocable discretionary trust | Gift-tax/IHT entry charges possible; settlor must give up control and benefit |
| Beneficiary on means-tested benefits | Third-party special/supplemental-needs trust | Funded by others; no payback; distributions supplement not replace benefits |
| Beneficiary's own funds, means-tested benefits | First-party trust under the US Medicaid exception (payback to the state) | Strict statutory requirements — check |
| Life insurance | ILIT | See estate-and-gift-tax skill |

## 2. Core drafting decisions
- **Parties and property:** settlor, initial trustees, initial trust fund (schedule),
  power to add property.
- **Beneficiaries:** defined classes (include adopted/step/later-born?), power to add or
  exclude beneficiaries (with tax review — can create unwanted settlor-interested or
  grantor status), default beneficiaries if all fail.
- **Distribution standard:** mandatory income; discretionary; or limited to an
  **ascertainable standard** (health, education, maintenance and support). A beneficiary
  -trustee with powers beyond an ascertainable standard may be treated as owning the fund
  for tax purposes (US) — restrict or use an independent trustee.
- **Spendthrift clause** (US) / protective trust (E&W) to keep the fund from beneficiaries'
  creditors; know the statutory exceptions (child support, certain government claims).
- **Trust term:** within the perpetuity period (E&W: 125 years under the Perpetuities and Accumulations Act 2009 s.5, in force 6 April 2010, for the instruments within
  s.1; US varies by state). Specify vesting events and ages.

## 3. Trustee provisions
- Appointment, resignation, removal (who holds the power), succession order, minimum
  number, corporate trustee qualifications.
- **Powers:** investment (with or without restriction to statutory duty of care),
  delegation, borrowing, holding residences, business interests, digital assets,
  dealing with trustees' own conflicts.
- Exculpation and indemnity (statutes limit how far these go), compensation, bond waiver,
  accounting and information duties (UTC requires informing qualified beneficiaries; the
  right can be limited only to a degree).

## 4. Flexibility tools
- Trust protector or appointor with defined powers (change situs, amend administrative
  terms, remove trustees) — define fiduciary status explicitly.
- Limited or general powers of appointment for beneficiaries (tax consequences).
- Decanting and variation powers; change of governing law and place of administration.
- Letter of wishes (E&W/offshore) — non-binding guidance kept separate from the deed.

## 5. Special-needs drafting points
- Sole and supplemental purpose; prohibit distributions that reduce benefits unless the
  trustee judges it worthwhile.
- No beneficiary power to compel distributions; independent trustee.
- First-party trusts: state the payback clause and beneficiary age limits as the statute requires.

## 6. Execution and funding
Execute with required formalities (some US states require will-like formalities for
revocable trusts holding testamentary dispositions). Then **fund**: retitle accounts and
real estate, assign personal property, update beneficiary designations. Register where
required (UK Trust Registration Service: generally within 90 days of creation or of becoming liable to tax for newer trusts; other deadlines apply, see gov.uk).

## Gotchas
- Unfunded revocable trusts — the will's pour-over sends everything through probate.
- Beneficiary-trustees with unlimited discretion over their own distributions.
- Medicaid-sensitive beneficiary given a right to demand income.
- Boilerplate powers that conflict with the tax plan (e.g. accidental grantor-trust triggers).

## Verify
- [ ] Structure choice and tax consequences documented.
- [ ] Every beneficiary class and contingency, including ultimate default, defined.
- [ ] Distribution standards reviewed against trustee identity.
- [ ] Trustee succession works without court involvement.
- [ ] Execution formalities met; funding checklist completed; registration done.
