# Adult guardianship: petitions, oversight and alternatives

**Goal:** protect an adult who cannot manage some decisions, using the **least
restrictive** arrangement that works, and — if a court appointment is needed — get the
right scope, the right person, and a compliant administration afterwards. US state
guardianship/conservatorship (UGCOPAA-influenced) and England & Wales (Mental Capacity Act
2005, checked current on 2026-10-24) are the models; not legal advice; terminology, evidence standards and forms vary.

## 1. Screen for alternatives first

| Need | Less restrictive option |
|---|---|
| Finances, if capacity remains to sign | Durable/lasting power of attorney; revocable trust; joint signatory or representative payee for benefits |
| Health decisions | Healthcare agent, advance directive, statutory surrogate/next-of-kin rules |
| Support but not substitution | Supported decision-making agreement (check whether your state recognises it) |
| Single transaction | Single-order or protective arrangement instead of full guardianship |

Many courts require the petition to explain why alternatives are insufficient.

## 2. Assess capacity
- Capacity is **decision-specific and time-specific**. E&W statutory test (MCA 2005 ss2-3): an impairment of, or disturbance in the functioning of, the mind or brain causing inability to
  understand, retain, use or weigh relevant information, or communicate a decision. Presume capacity; take all practicable steps to
  help the person decide; an unwise decision alone does not show incapacity (s1).
- Obtain a professional assessment covering each domain (finances, residence, medical,
  contact, marriage/voting where relevant), with functional examples.
- Interview the person yourself; record their wishes and values.

## 3. Petition / application
1. Petitioner standing; proposed guardian/conservator/deputy (suitability, conflicts,
   criminal and credit checks).
2. Facts showing incapacity in specific domains and the harm without protection.
3. Proposed **scope**: limited powers tailored to the needs (residence, healthcare,
   property) rather than plenary.
4. Assets and income schedule; proposed bond or security.
5. Required notice: personal service on the respondent, notice to family and agencies.
6. Respondent's rights: counsel, court visitor or guardian ad litem, independent
   evaluation, attendance at hearing. Check your state's evidence standard.

E&W: application to the Court of Protection with evidence of the person's capacity; the Mental Capacity Act 2005 requires acting in the person's best interests
and considering whether the purpose can be met in a less restrictive way (s1).

## 4. Emergencies
Temporary/emergency guardianship or urgent interim orders exist for imminent harm
(financial exploitation, unsafe discharge). Keep them narrow and short; expect a prompt
return hearing.

## 5. After appointment
- Letters/order and bond filed; notify banks, benefits agencies, care providers.
- **Inventory** within the deadline (deadline set by the order or state law — check); separate account titled to
  the protected person.
- Decisions follow the person's wishes and values where possible (substituted judgment
  or best-interests standards).
- **Annual reports and accountings** (E&W: check the deputy's reporting duties); court approval for major transactions (sale of home, gifts, change of
  residence to restrictive settings).
- Review and **restoration**: seek modification or termination when capacity improves.

## Gotchas
- Plenary orders when only financial help was needed.
- Family conflict pushed into the guardianship — consider a professional fiduciary.
- Using the person's funds for the guardian's benefit or commingling.
- Overlooking an existing valid power of attorney that makes proceedings unnecessary.

## Verify
- [ ] Alternatives considered and reasons recorded.
- [ ] Capacity evidence is domain-specific and current.
- [ ] Respondent served, informed of rights and heard.
- [ ] Order scope limited to proven needs.
- [ ] Inventory, bond, accounts and annual reports diarised.
