# Administering an estate from grant to distribution

**Goal:** collect the deceased's assets, pay debts and taxes in the right order, and
distribute the balance to the right people with a clean release, without leaving the
personal representative (PR) personally liable. England & Wales (checked current on 2026-10-24) and US (UPC-style
informal probate) are the models; not legal advice; court involvement, deadlines and notice rules vary by
state and country.

## 1. First 30 days
- Locate the latest original will (and codicils); check will registries. If none,
  identify heirs under the intestacy rules.
- Secure assets: property insurance (notify insurer of vacancy), vehicles, valuables,
  stop direct debits, redirect mail, notify banks to freeze accounts.
- Register the death; obtain several certified death certificates.
- Identify who may act: named executor or administrator by priority; check conflicts
  (PR who is also a creditor or claimant).
- Keep receipts for funeral expenses.

## 2. Inventory and valuation
- Date-of-death values for every asset: bank balances with accrued interest, securities
  (valuation rules for listed shares — check method), real estate (formal valuation),
  business interests, personal effects, digital assets, debts owed to the deceased.
- Separate **probate assets** from non-probate assets (joint property, beneficiary
  designations, trust assets) — the latter may still count for tax.
- Record liabilities: mortgages, cards, taxes, care fees, guarantees.

## 3. Obtain the grant
- E&W: grant of probate (will with executor) or letters of administration; file the
  inheritance tax account/return as required before or with the application.
- US: petition for letters testamentary/administration; informal or formal procedure;
  bond unless waived; **small-estate affidavit** routes below state thresholds (check).
- Caveats or objections pause the grant — see the contested-estates skill.

## 4. Creditors
1. Publish/serve notices: E&W statutory advertisements under Trustee Act 1925 s27 protect a PR who waits the notice
   period (not less than two months) before distributing; US probate codes set
   publication and mailed-notice periods and claim bars.
2. Evaluate each claim: allow, compromise, or reject in writing within the time allowed.
3. **Insolvent estates** follow a statutory priority order that differs by jurisdiction; paying a beneficiary first exposes the PR.

## 5. Taxes

| Return | Typical timing |
|---|---|
| Deceased's final personal income tax return | Following normal deadline |
| Estate income tax returns during administration | Annually while open |
| Estate/inheritance tax return | E&W IHT: payment due by the end of the sixth month after death (gov.uk), interest thereafter; US federal estate tax return: 9 months after death (§6075(a); state taxes differ) |

Obtain tax clearance or closing letters before final distribution where available.

## 6. Accounts and distribution
- Keep a cash ledger from day one: receipts, payments, assets sold, gains/losses.
- Pay legacies, then residue; in-kind distributions need written agreement on values.
- Produce **estate accounts** (capital and income) for residuary beneficiaries; obtain
  signed approval and **receipts and releases** before final distribution, or seek court
  approval if a beneficiary refuses.
- Retain a reserve for late tax adjustments until clearances arrive.
- Close the estate: final account filed where the court requires it; PR discharged.

## Gotchas
- Distributing before creditor periods expire or tax is agreed.
- Commingling estate money with the PR's own funds.
- Missing a beneficiary-designated asset that should fall into the estate.
- Selling the house without the power or consents required.

## Verify
- [ ] Will and grant match; PR authority established.
- [ ] Inventory complete with date-of-death values and evidence.
- [ ] Creditor notices published and claim periods expired.
- [ ] All tax returns filed and clearances obtained.
- [ ] Accounts approved, releases signed, distribution recorded.
