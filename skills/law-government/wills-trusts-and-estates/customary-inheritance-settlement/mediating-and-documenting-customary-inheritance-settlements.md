# Mediating and documenting customary inheritance settlements

**Goal:** reach an inheritance settlement the family accepts as legitimate under its
custom, that respects constitutional and statutory protections, and that a court, land
registry or bank will recognise. Models are plural legal systems in sub-Saharan Africa
(e.g. South Africa, Kenya, Nigeria), with lessons that carry to Pacific and other
customary regimes; custom varies by community and is living, not fixed, so establish it
for **this** family and place rather than assuming a textbook rule. For Islamic
succession shares see
`law-government.religious-and-customary-law.islamic-law-practice.calculating-islamic-inheritance-shares`;
for recording land rights see
`law-government.religious-and-customary-law.customary-land-tenure.documenting-and-recording-customary-land-rights`.

## 1. Establish the applicable law
- Domicile, community, type of marriage(s) (civil, customary, religious; monogamous or
  polygynous), whether a valid will exists, and what statute says about which regime
  governs (some systems apply statutory intestacy to all estates; others route customary
  estates differently).
- Identify **constitutional limits**: courts in several countries have struck down rules
  excluding women or daughters (e.g. male primogeniture in *Bhe*; exclusion of daughters
  in *Ukeje*). Discriminatory custom may be unenforceable even if the family follows it.
- Separate **personal estate** from **family or communal property** (ancestral land, lineage
  houses) which may not pass by individual inheritance at all.

## 2. Map heirs and dependants
Draw a family tree with all spouses and their houses, children (including those born
outside marriage and adopted under custom), dependants and creditors. Flag the vulnerable:
widows, minors, persons with disabilities, children of junior houses. Confirm who holds
custodial roles under custom (e.g. a head of family as administrator, not owner).

## 3. Prepare the family council
- Agree convenor, venue, language, and who attends: elders or traditional authority as
  respected facilitators, each house represented, women and youth heard directly (not only
  through male relatives).
- Circulate in advance a plain inventory of assets and debts, and the legal position in
  simple terms, including what formal law guarantees.
- Agree ground rules: no decisions while key members are absent; minors represented by
  someone without a conflicting interest.

## 4. Run the mediation
1. Opening by the facilitator: purpose, custom, legal limits.
2. Each house states needs and expectations.
3. Deal first with urgent needs: widow's residence and maintenance, school fees, funeral
   debts.
4. Allocate assets: homestead/residence rights, land use rights, livestock, businesses,
   bank money, personal effects; record whether each allocation is ownership, use or
   custodianship.
5. Test the draft against formal law: does any heir receive less than the statutory
   minimum or protected right? If so, obtain fully informed, voluntary consent or revise.
6. Close with a reading-back in the family's language.

## 5. Document it

| Document | Content |
|---|---|
| Minutes of the family council | Date, place, attendees by house, facilitator, decisions, dissent |
| Settlement agreement | Each heir's share, nature of right, conditions, timeline, dispute route |
| Signatures | Each adult heir; marks/thumbprints with witness attestation where needed; interpreter's certificate |
| Supporting evidence | Death certificate, marriage evidence, family tree, asset list |

Then take the formal steps: letters of administration or registration with the
customary/estates authority, court approval where minors' interests are compromised,
land or title transfers, bank releases.

## Gotchas
- Treating the head of family as owner rather than custodian.
- Settlements signed by widows or daughters under pressure — likely to be set aside and
  ethically indefensible.
- Overlooking a civil marriage that brings statutory intestacy rules into play.
- Leaving the settlement unregistered so land disputes reopen a generation later.

## Verify
- [ ] Governing regime and constitutional limits written down.
- [ ] Complete family tree; vulnerable heirs identified and represented.
- [ ] Minutes and agreement signed, witnessed and interpreted.
- [ ] Settlement checked against statutory protections.
- [ ] Formal grant, registration and transfers completed.
