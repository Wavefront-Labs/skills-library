# Designing and coordinating an estate plan

**Goal:** help a client decide who gets what, who decides for them if they cannot, and
who looks after their dependants — and make every asset actually pass the way the plan
says. Most failed plans fail on **coordination** (titling and beneficiary designations),
not drafting. US and England & Wales practice are the models; the document names and
formalities differ, the counselling method does not.

## 1. Engagement and ethics
- Identify the client(s). Joint representation of spouses/partners: explain in the
  engagement letter how confidences and conflicts are handled and what happens if
  interests diverge.
- Adult children or carers in the room: see the client alone; note who instructs.
- Scope: which documents, which assets, whether you advise on tax and on foreign property.

## 2. Discovery
Use a written questionnaire, then interview:
- Family: spouses/partners (married or not), children and their circumstances (minors,
  disabilities, addictions, divorces, estrangement), prior relationships with continuing
  obligations.
- Assets with **how each is held**: sole, joint (which form), trust, beneficiary-designated
  (retirement, life insurance, transfer-on-death accounts), business interests, foreign
  assets, digital assets.
- Existing documents: wills, trusts, powers of attorney, marital agreements.
- Health, age, values, charitable wishes, worries.

## 3. Map the flow
Produce an **asset-flow chart**: for each asset, what happens on first death, second
death and incapacity, under the current set-up and under the proposed plan.

| Asset | Held as | Passes by | Today goes to | Plan goes to | Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Home | Joint tenancy | Survivorship | Spouse | Spouse, then trust | None / sever if blended family |
| Pension/401(k) | Designation | Contract | Ex-spouse (old form!) | Spouse/trust | New designation |

The gaps between "today" and "plan" are the to-do list.

## 4. Core document set

| Purpose | US | England & Wales |
|---|---|---|
| Dispose of probate assets | Will (often pour-over to revocable trust) | Will |
| Manage assets on incapacity | Durable financial power of attorney; revocable trust | Lasting power of attorney (property & financial affairs) — registered |
| Health decisions | Healthcare proxy/agent; living will/advance directive; HIPAA release | LPA (health & welfare); advance decision to refuse treatment |
| Minor children | Guardian nomination in will | Testamentary guardian appointment |
| Dependants with special needs | Supplemental-needs trust | Discretionary/disabled person's trust |

Choose agents and fiduciaries for competence, availability and family dynamics; name
alternates.

## 5. Counselling hard choices
- Blended families: balance spouse security with children's inheritance (life-interest
  or QTIP-type trusts, separate funds, life insurance).
- Unequal treatment: record reasons; consider family-provision claim risk.
- Family business: separate ownership from management; buy-sell agreements.
- Give options in plain language with pros/cons; avoid overselling complexity.

## 6. Implement and maintain
1. Execute documents with formalities (see the will-drafting skill for wills).
2. Funding/coordination letter listing each retitling and designation change, who does it,
   and by when; confirm completion.
3. Register LPAs (E&W) — they cannot be used until registered.
4. Review triggers: births, deaths, marriage, divorce, moves, major asset changes, tax law
   changes; suggest a review every few years.

## Gotchas
- Outdated beneficiary designations overriding the will.
- Joint accounts with one child "for convenience" that pass to that child alone.
- Choosing the same person as agent, executor and trustee without a check.
- Leaving the client to retitle assets without follow-up.

## Verify
- [ ] Engagement letter defines client(s) and conflicts approach.
- [ ] Asset-flow chart completed for death and incapacity scenarios.
- [ ] All core documents executed with alternates named.
- [ ] Every designation and title change confirmed in writing.
- [ ] Review date and triggers recorded with the client.
