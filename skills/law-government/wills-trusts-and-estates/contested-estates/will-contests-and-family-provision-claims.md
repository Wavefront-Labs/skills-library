# Will contests and family-provision claims

**Goal:** identify which inheritance claim (if any) is viable, preserve it before short
time limits run, gather the evidence that decides these cases (medical records, the
will file, witness accounts), and resolve it — usually by mediated settlement — at a
cost proportionate to the estate. England & Wales and US states are the models; standing,
time limits, presumptions and family-provision regimes differ widely.

## 1. Classify the claim

| Claim | Core question | Key evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Lack of testamentary capacity | Did the testator meet the capacity test when signing? | GP/hospital records, will file, witnesses, expert retrospective assessment |
| Want of knowledge and approval (E&W) | Did the testator know and approve the contents? (suspicious circumstances shift burden) | Instruction notes, who prepared the will, reading-over |
| Undue influence | Was the will the product of coercion overpowering free will? | Isolation, dependency, beneficiary's role in instructions; US: presumption from confidential relationship plus participation |
| Forgery / fraud / improper execution | Was it signed and witnessed properly, by the testator? | Handwriting expert, attesting witnesses |
| Rectification / construction | Clerical error or ambiguity? | Drafter's file |
| Family provision (E&W, many Commonwealth systems) | Does the will/intestacy fail to make reasonable financial provision for an eligible applicant? | Applicant's needs and resources, estate size, relationship, conduct |
| Elective/forced share (US, civil-law systems) | Is the surviving spouse (or heirs) entitled to a fixed share regardless of the will? | Augmented estate calculation |
| Fiduciary claims | Has the executor/trustee mismanaged, self-dealt, or failed to account? | Accounts, bank records |

## 2. Protect the position early
- **Time limits:** E&W family provision claims generally within six months of the date representation (the grant) is first taken out (Inheritance (Provision for Family and Dependants) Act 1975 s.4)
  (late permission possible but uncertain); rectification also short; US will contests
  often within a few months of probate notice — check the statute.
- E&W: enter a **caveat** to stop a grant while investigating; request the will file and
  the drafter's account (a *Larke v Nugus* request); obtain medical records.
- US: file objections in the probate proceeding before admission or within the contest
  period; consider requesting a formal testacy proceeding.
- Check any **no-contest clause** and its enforceability (varies by state; "probable
  cause" exceptions common) before advising a beneficiary to sue.

## 3. Build the case
1. Chronology of the testator's health, relationships and every will made (earlier wills
   matter: if the last will fails, the previous one usually applies).
2. Witness statements from the drafter, attesting witnesses, carers, doctors.
3. Expert: retrospective capacity opinion from records; handwriting expert if forgery.
4. Family provision: full financial disclosure for the applicant; estimate of estate net
   value after costs (which can consume small estates).

## 4. Resolve
- Pre-action correspondence with the evidence and a realistic outcome range.
- **Mediation** is the norm and courts expect it; prepare a costs-exposure schedule.
- Settlement terms: deed of variation or court order (tax-efficient variations may be
  available within time limits — check), releases, costs, payment from estate or parties.
- Trial: probate claims are fact-heavy; credibility of the drafter's evidence is often
  decisive.

## 5. Fiduciary litigation
Demand accounts; seek removal/replacement of a PR or trustee, surcharge for losses,
disgorgement of profits from self-dealing, or court directions. Interim administrators
can protect assets during a dispute.

## Gotchas
- Missing the six-month (or shorter) window while negotiating informally — protect it with
  a standstill agreement or issue.
- Suing on capacity with no medical evidence of impairment.
- Ignoring that a successful challenge revives an earlier will that is no better.
- Costs rules: losing parties may bear costs personally; estate costs are not a given.
