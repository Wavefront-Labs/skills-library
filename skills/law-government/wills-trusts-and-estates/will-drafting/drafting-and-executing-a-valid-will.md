# Drafting and executing a valid will

**Goal:** a will that says what the testator wants, is formally valid where it will be
proved, works in every survivorship scenario, and comes with a file that defeats a
later capacity or undue-influence challenge. England & Wales (Wills Act 1837, checked current on 2026-10-24) and US state law are the models; not legal advice; formalities, revocation rules and forced-heirship regimes
differ — where assets or domicile are abroad, check the other system too.

## 1. Take instructions properly
- See the testator **alone** for at least part of the meeting; note who arranged the
  appointment and who attended. Instructions passed through a beneficiary are a red flag.
- Record: family tree (marriages, children including stepchildren, prior relationships),
  assets and how each is held (joint, beneficiary-designated, trust, foreign), existing
  wills, lifetime gifts, dependants with claims, domicile/residence.
- Ask why: reasons for unequal shares or exclusions go in a file note (and sometimes a
  separate signed statement).

## 2. Capacity and safeguards
- Classic common-law test (*Banks v Goodfellow*, 1870): understands the act and its effects,
  the extent of property, the claims of those who might expect to benefit, and no disorder
  of mind poisoning those judgments (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked). The law reports were not read; only secondary commentary was available. Check
  your state's test in the US.
- Where the testator is elderly or unwell, follow the **"golden rule"** (from *Kenward v Adams*): obtain a medical
  practitioner's capacity assessment, ideally witnessing the will, and record it (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked). Only secondary commentary was read.
- Contemporaneous, detailed attendance notes are the best defence to a contest.

## 3. Draft the dispositive scheme
Typical order: revocation clause → appointment of executors (and substitutes) → guardians
for minor children → specific gifts → pecuniary legacies → residuary gift → administrative
powers → trusts → signature/attestation.
- **Residue first in your thinking**: most of the estate usually passes there.
- Survivorship clause (e.g. beneficiaries must survive by a stated period) and gift-over
  for each gift; check the default lapse/anti-lapse rules if not overridden.
- Define classes precisely ("my children" — include later-born, adopted, step?); state
  distribution *per stirpes* or per capita.
- Specific gifts: describe precisely; say what happens if the asset is sold (ademption)
  and who bears tax and debts.
- Avoid precatory language ("I wish") where you mean a binding gift.
- Coordinate with assets outside the will: joint property and beneficiary designations
  pass outside it.

## 4. Execution

| | England & Wales | US (typical UPC state) |
|---|---|---|
| Writing | Required | Required |
| Testator signs | Signs, or acknowledges the signature, before 2 or more witnesses present at the same time (Wills Act 1837 s9) | Check the state's wills statute |
| Witnesses | A gift to an attesting witness or their spouse is void (s15) | Interested witness rules vary |
| Extras | Attestation clause customary; no particular form required (s9) | Check whether a self-proving affidavit is available |
| Informal wills | Not valid under s9 | State law varies |

Supervise execution yourself where possible: check each page, initials, date, no staples
removed, and record who was present. Videoconference witnessing counted as presence in England and Wales only for wills made between 31 January 2020 and
31 January 2024 (s9(2)); check the current rule locally.

## 5. After signing
- Store the original securely (firm vault or official will registry); give the client a copy.
- Diary review triggers: marriage (revokes a will in E&W unless made in expectation of that marriage, s18), divorce (in E&W the former spouse is treated
  as having died on the date of dissolution, s18A, unless the will shows a contrary intention), births, deaths, moving
  country, big asset changes.
- Use a **codicil** for small changes; re-execute a new will for anything substantial.

## Gotchas
- Witness who is a beneficiary's spouse.
- Assuming a foreign property passes under this will — forced heirship or local
  formalities may apply; consider a separate situs will.
- Residuary gift that fails, leaving partial intestacy.
- Sending the unsigned draft home for self-execution.

## Verify
- [ ] Attendance notes show the testator's own instructions and reasons.
- [ ] Capacity assessed and recorded; golden rule followed where indicated.
- [ ] Every gift has a survivorship rule and gift-over; residue fully disposed of.
- [ ] Execution formalities met and recorded; original stored and registered.
- [ ] Non-probate assets and foreign assets coordinated.
