# Preparing and pursuing official applications

**Goal:** submit an application (benefit, permit, licence, visa, registration) that a
decision-maker can approve on first reading, and know what to do if it stalls or is refused.

This is general method, not advice on a particular scheme. Every scheme has its own rules,
forms and deadlines, and they change; use the official guidance current on the day you apply.
For high-stakes matters (immigration, large benefits, criminal-record issues) get advice from
a qualified adviser or a free advice service.

## 1. Find the real criteria
The form is not the law. Find the eligibility rules: the official guidance page, the
decision-maker's manual (many agencies publish them), or the regulation itself. Write a
list: *condition → what proves it*. This list is your application plan.

## 2. Evidence each condition
- Prefer documents the agency says it accepts; supply the specific one, not a substitute.
- Translations, certified copies and date ranges are frequent rejection reasons. Check
  whether "recent" means 3 months, 6 months or something else.
- Where you can't get a document, explain why and offer alternatives in a short covering letter.
- Answer every question. "N/A" is better than blank; blank fields often mean "incomplete".
- Be accurate. Errors you notice later should be corrected in writing straight away; an
  inaccurate application can be refused or treated as misrepresentation.

## 3. Submit with proof
- Keep a full copy (scan or photo) of everything sent, in order.
- Online: save the confirmation page or reference number and screenshot the final summary.
- Post: use tracked or recorded delivery, or get a dated receipt over the counter.
- Note the date: deadlines (and the agency's own target times) usually run from receipt.

## 4. Chase constructively
- Find the published processing time. Contact them only after it has passed, quoting your reference.
- Keep a log: date, channel, name or ID of who you spoke to, what they said.
- Ask specifically: "Is anything missing? When will a decision be made?"
- If stuck, use the formal complaint process; then the relevant ombudsman or elected
  representative where available. Many systems allow you to request your file (subject
  access or FOI) to see what they have.

## 5. If refused
- Read the reasons: which condition failed, and why.
- Note the **deadline** for challenge immediately; they can be short (sometimes days or weeks).
- Many systems require an internal review (reconsideration) before an appeal to a tribunal
  or court. Ask for it in writing and address each reason with new evidence or argument.
- Sometimes a fresh application with better evidence is faster than an appeal; sometimes it
  loses back-dated entitlement. Find out which before choosing.

## Gotchas
- Applying under the wrong category (e.g. visa type) can't usually be fixed mid-process.
- Missing a challenge deadline is often final.
- Unofficial "agents" charging to fill free forms: check they're authorised, and never hand
  over login credentials.

## Verify
- [ ] You have a condition → evidence list and every item is covered.
- [ ] A complete copy and proof of submission are kept.
- [ ] Processing time and your chase log are recorded.
- [ ] On refusal, you know the challenge route and its deadline.
