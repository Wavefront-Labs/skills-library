# Grant administration and compliance (intergovernmental)

**Goal:** spend grant money from a higher level of government on what the award allows,
prove it, and pass audit without disallowances or clawbacks.

Examples here lean on US federal practice (2 CFR 200, the "Uniform Guidance", and the Single
Audit) because it is well documented; EU structural funds, Commonwealth-to-state grants and
national-to-municipal transfers use similar concepts with different rules. Check the
specific award and governing regulations.

## 1. Read the award like a contract
Build a one-page compliance matrix from the grant agreement, programme statute, and
cross-cutting rules:
- **Purpose and scope** of allowable activities.
- **Period of performance** (costs outside it are unallowable, including pre-award costs
  unless authorised).
- **Budget and categories**, and how much you may move between them without approval.
- **Match / cost share** requirements and what counts (cash vs in-kind, valuation rules).
- **Reporting:** financial and performance reports with due dates.
- **Special conditions** and cross-cutting requirements (procurement, civil rights,
  environmental review, wage rules, data).

## 2. Cost allowability
A cost is generally allowable if it is necessary and reasonable for the award, allocable to
it, consistent with the cost principles and your own policies, adequately documented, and
not used to meet another award's match. Keep the documentation with the charge (invoice,
timesheet, approval, procurement file). Payroll charged to a grant needs time and effort
records that reflect actual work.

## 3. Cash management
Draw down funds to reimburse or for immediate needs only, as the award requires. Excess cash
on hand can create interest liabilities and audit findings. Reconcile drawdowns to the
ledger monthly.

## 4. Subrecipients
If you pass funds on to a lower level (counties, cities, nonprofits):
- Decide whether each is a **subrecipient** (carries out part of the programme) or a
  **contractor** (provides goods/services); obligations differ.
- Flow down all required terms in the subaward.
- **Risk-assess** each subrecipient and set monitoring accordingly: desk reviews, site visits,
  review of their audits, follow-up of findings.
- You are responsible for their compliance in the eyes of the funder.

## 5. Reporting and closeout
Diarise every report. Late or inaccurate reports are an easy finding and can lead to
payment holds. At closeout: final financial report, liquidate obligations within the
allowed window, account for equipment and unspent funds.

## 6. Audit and disallowance
Prepare for audit continuously. If costs are questioned, respond with documentation; if
disallowed, there is usually an appeal or negotiation route, and repayment may come from
non-grant funds.

## Mandates
Intergovernmental administration also covers mandates (requirements imposed with or without
funding). Track mandate compliance and reporting just as rigorously; unfunded-mandate rules
in some jurisdictions affect whether obligations are enforceable.

## Gotchas
- Charging costs before the start date or after the end date.
- Using the same expense as match for two awards.
- Treating a subrecipient as a vendor to avoid monitoring.

## Verify
- [ ] Compliance matrix exists with dates, budget, match and special conditions.
- [ ] Sample charges have full documentation and fall in the period.
- [ ] Subrecipients risk-assessed with monitoring records.
- [ ] Report calendar up to date; drawdowns reconciled.
