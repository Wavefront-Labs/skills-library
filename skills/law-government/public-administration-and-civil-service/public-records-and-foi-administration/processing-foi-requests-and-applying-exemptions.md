# Processing FOI requests and applying exemptions

**Goal:** answer information requests lawfully, on time and with the maximum
disclosure the law permits, withholding only what an exemption genuinely protects —
and keeping records well enough that you can find things. Models: UK FOIA 2000 and
US federal FOIA. State, provincial and other national laws differ on deadlines, fees,
exemptions and appeal bodies. For the requester's side see
`law-government.constitutional-and-administrative-law.freedom-of-information-practice.foi-requests-exemptions-and-appeals`.

## 1. Log and triage (day 0–2)
1. Log every request: date received (the clock usually starts the next working day),
   requester, wording, unique reference. UK and US federal both use **20 working days**
   as the standard response time (extensions exist — UK for public-interest
   consideration, US for "unusual circumstances").
2. Is it a valid request? (Writing, name, address for reply, description of information.)
   Route personal-data requests about the requester to the data-protection subject-access process.
3. Ambiguous? Seek **clarification** promptly — in many regimes the clock pauses or
   restarts on clarification. Offer advice and assistance to narrow it.

## 2. Search and cost
- Identify likely holders; send a search tasking with a deadline (e.g. 5 working days)
  and require a written search record: systems, search terms, date ranges, results.
- Estimate effort. UK: an appropriate-limit costs refusal (s.12) is possible where
  estimated search time exceeds a set hourly-cost cap (check current figures);
  you must still advise how to narrow. US: fees depend on requester category; fee waivers
  for public-interest requests.

## 3. Apply exemptions document by document
1. Read every responsive record; segregate exempt from non-exempt parts — disclose the
   rest (US "reasonably segregable" duty; UK partial disclosure).
2. For each withholding, identify the exemption and its test:
| Test type | UK examples | US examples |
|---|---|---|
| Class-based / absolute | Information accessible elsewhere, court records, personal data (third parties, largely) | Statutory bars (Exemption 3) |
| Prejudice / harm | Law enforcement, commercial interests, policy formulation (qualified) | Deliberative process (Exemption 5), law enforcement (Exemption 7) |
| Public-interest balance | Required for qualified exemptions | "Foreseeable harm" standard since 2016 |
3. Write the reasoning down at the time: what harm, how likely, why the public interest
   (where applicable) favours withholding. Generic boilerplate loses appeals.
4. Neither confirm nor deny (UK NCND; US "Glomar") only where confirming existence
   would itself reveal exempt information.

## 4. Redaction and response
- Redact with a proper tool that removes underlying text and metadata; mark each
  redaction with the exemption code.
- Response/refusal notice: information disclosed; information withheld; exemption and
  reasons; public-interest reasoning; internal review/administrative appeal rights and
  deadline; regulator or court route (UK Information Commissioner; US OGIS mediation and
  federal court).
- Publish to a **disclosure log** where policy allows.

## 5. Internal review / appeal
- Conducted by someone senior and not involved; reconsider search adequacy, not just exemptions.
- UK guidance expects reviews normally within 20 working days (40 at most in complex cases);
  US administrative appeals have statutory decision times — check them.

## 6. Records management underpins it all
- Maintain a retention and disposal schedule; destroy only per schedule, never after a request arrives.
- Altering, blocking or destroying requested records with intent to prevent disclosure
  is a criminal offence in the UK (s.77) and serious misconduct elsewhere.

## Gotchas
- "Vexatious" (UK s.14) refers to the request's burden or purpose, not the requester's
  tone; use sparingly and evidence it.
- Treating embarrassment as harm.
- Searching only the main records system and missing email, messaging apps and personal accounts used for business.
- Unredacted metadata or tracked changes in released documents.

## Verify
- [ ] Request logged with correct start date and deadline.
- [ ] Search record documents systems, terms and results.
- [ ] Each withholding has an exemption, harm reasoning and (where needed) public-interest test.
- [ ] Redactions are irreversible and labelled.
- [ ] Response states review and appeal rights; sent on time.
