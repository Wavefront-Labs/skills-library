# Running polling, the count and results certification

**Goal:** an election whose result is accurate, provably so, and accepted by the
losers. Models: US state and county administration (poll books, provisional ballots,
canvass, audits) and UK practice (Returning and Presiding Officers, ballot paper
accounts, verification then count). Election law is highly specific to each state and
election type — deadlines, ID rules, observer rights and recount triggers come from
your statute and official manual, not from habit.

## 1. Before voting (weeks −12 to −1)
1. **Timeline** from the statute backwards: notice of election, nominations, roll
   close, postal/absentee issue, ballot proofing, staff training, equipment testing.
2. **Ballot proofing:** candidate names, order, party descriptions checked against
   nominations by two people; candidates sign off where the law provides.
3. **Logic & accuracy testing** of tabulators in public, with a pre-marked test deck
   whose expected results are known; seal and log equipment afterwards.
4. **Polling places:** accessibility audit, signage, layout preserving ballot secrecy,
   contingency sites, power and connectivity backup.
5. **Training:** scenario-based — voter not on the roll, wrong polling place, ID
   disputes, assistance requests, disruptive person, equipment failure.

## 2. Voting day
- Open on time; record seal numbers and zero tapes / empty ballot box shown to observers.
- Check each voter against the poll book; mark the roll; issue one ballot.
- Voters not found: follow the statutory route (e.g. US **provisional ballot** under
  HAVA, later verified; in the UK, refer to the electoral registration office for
  clerical-error corrections). Never just turn them away.
- Log every incident with time and action taken.
- At close: anyone queueing at close can usually vote (check the rule); seal boxes;
  complete the **ballot paper account** (issued + spoiled + unused = supplied).

## 3. Chain of custody
Every container of ballots and every memory device moves with: seal number, from/to,
two signatures, time. Breaks in the chain are the first thing a challenger examines.

## 4. Verification and count
1. **Verify/reconcile first:** ballots in each box vs the ballot paper account or poll
   book count; investigate discrepancies before counting votes.
2. Count (by hand or tabulator) under observation; adjudicate doubtful ballots
   against the statutory voter-intent rules, showing each to candidate agents.
3. Recount procedures: triggered by margin thresholds or request rules defined in law.

## 5. Canvass, audit, certification
- Reconcile: voters checked in vs ballots cast vs ballots counted, per precinct.
- Resolve provisional and postal ballots within the statutory window.
- **Audit:** a risk-limiting audit (RLA) samples paper ballots until there is strong
  statistical evidence the reported winner is correct, or escalates to a full hand count.
- Certify within the deadline; publish precinct-level results, the reconciliation report
  and audit results. Certification is typically a ministerial (non-discretionary) duty.

## Gotchas
- An unexplained one-ballot discrepancy erodes trust more than a big but documented one.
- Last-minute polling-place changes without notice disenfranchise voters.
- Treating observers as adversaries — give them clear rules and good sightlines.
- Social media rumours: publish procedures and timelines in advance; correct quickly.

## Verify
- [ ] Statutory timeline met at every milestone.
- [ ] L&A testing done publicly; seals logged.
- [ ] Ballot paper accounts and poll-book reconciliation balanced or explained.
- [ ] Chain-of-custody logs complete for all ballots and media.
- [ ] Audit completed; results certified on time and published with reconciliation.
