# Polling and count operations

**Goal:** every ballot issued, cast, spoiled and counted is accounted for, so the
certified result can withstand a recount or an election petition.

Electoral law is highly jurisdiction-specific (who presides, ID rules, how doubtful
ballots are judged, recount thresholds, certification deadlines). This describes the
control principles common to paper-ballot systems; machine and electronic systems add
their own audit procedures. Always run from the official handbook for the election.

## 1. Before polling day
- **Roll/register freeze:** know the cut-off and which late changes (corrections, court
  orders) are still applied. Every station gets the correct extract.
- **Materials check:** ballot papers counted and serial ranges recorded per station; seals,
  ballot boxes, forms, accessibility aids. Two people sign for every handover.
- **Staff training:** identity rules, assisted voting, what to do with someone not on the
  roll (provisional/tendered ballot rules vary), and when to call the returning officer.

## 2. At the polling station
- Open: show the empty box to anyone present, seal it, record seal numbers.
- For each voter: verify on the roll, apply the ID rule exactly (no stricter, no looser),
  mark the roll, issue one ballot. Record assistance and tendered/provisional ballots.
- Keep observers and candidates' agents within their legal rights; record complaints
  verbatim with time.
- Close at the statutory time; anyone in the queue at close may vote in most systems.

## 3. Ballot paper account (the core control)
At close, complete the reconciliation before boxes leave:
`ballots received = ballots issued + spoilt/cancelled + unused`
and `ballots issued` should match the count of marks on the roll. Seal unused and spoilt
papers separately. Record seal numbers on the box and the form; both officials sign.
Unreconciled differences must be explained in writing now, not at the count.

## 4. Chain of custody
Boxes travel sealed, with two people or under escort, with a transfer log. Postal and
absentee ballots have their own verification step (identifier checks, envelopes) and
their own log. A gap in custody is the classic ground for a challenge.

## 5. The count
- **Verification first:** open each box, count papers face down, compare to the ballot
  paper account. Investigate discrepancies before sorting by candidate.
- **Sorting and counting** in bundles of a fixed size, double-checked.
- **Doubtful ballots:** set aside and adjudicate by the returning officer in front of agents,
  applying the statutory test (generally whether the voter's intention is clear and the
  voter not identifiable). Record each decision by category.
- **Recount:** follow the legal threshold or request rules; recount the same way, not faster.

## 6. Declaration and certification
Announce the provisional result only after totals reconcile. Certification (canvass) may
involve a separate review of all station returns. Retain sealed materials for the statutory
period; they are evidence in any petition.

## Gotchas
- Fixing a number at the count to make it balance destroys the audit trail. Record the
  discrepancy instead.
- Inconsistent ID enforcement between stations is a legal and reputational risk.
- Agents' complaints not recorded at the time are hard to answer later.

## Verify
- [ ] Every station's ballot paper account balances or has a signed explanation.
- [ ] Seal numbers match between station form, transport log and count.
- [ ] Doubtful ballot decisions recorded by category with agents present.
- [ ] Materials retained and sealed for the statutory period.
