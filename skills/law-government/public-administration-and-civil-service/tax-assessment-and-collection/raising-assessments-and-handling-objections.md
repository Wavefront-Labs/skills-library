# Raising assessments and handling objections

**Goal:** issue an assessment that is within power, in time, properly evidenced and
properly notified, and review objections on their merits so that only good assessments
go on to collection or litigation.

This is the administrator's view; the taxpayer's view is in
`law-government.tax-law.tax-controversy.audit-defence-and-tax-appeals`. Assessment powers,
time limits and objection procedures are set by each tax code and differ by tax type.

## 1. Is there a power to assess, and is it in time?
- Identify the type: **self-assessment adjustment**, **amended assessment** after audit,
  **default/estimated assessment** where no return was filed, or a **penalty assessment**.
  Each may have a different statutory basis and different limits.
- **Time limits:** normal limitation period; extended periods for careless or deliberate
  understatement or fraud usually require the authority to prove the behaviour. Don't use
  an extended period without evidence that meets that burden.
- Check that a statutory precondition (e.g. a discovery of new information, an open enquiry
  window) is met where the code requires one.

## 2. Evidence the figure
- Every adjustment ties to a document, a reconciliation, or a stated methodology
  (e.g. bank-deposit analysis, industry ratios for estimated assessments).
- An estimated assessment must be a genuine best judgement, not a punitive number. Write
  down the method so a reviewer can reproduce it.
- Separate the tax, interest (usually automatic) and penalties (usually requiring their
  own findings on behaviour and mitigation).

## 3. The notice
A notice of assessment typically must state the taxpayer, period, tax, amount, due date,
and the right and deadline to object or appeal. Defects in service (wrong address, wrong
entity) can invalidate the assessment or stop the objection clock running. Record how and
when it was served.

## 4. Reviewing an objection
- Check the objection is valid: in time, in the prescribed form, stating grounds. Late
  objections may be accepted on reasonable excuse; decide that question explicitly.
- Review by someone not involved in raising the assessment where possible.
- Consider each ground; ask for evidence; be willing to reduce or cancel. An assessment the
  authority can't defend at tribunal costs more to keep than to fix.
- Decision letter: outcome per ground, reasons, revised figures, and the next appeal step.

## 5. Collection
- Many systems suspend collection of disputed amounts during objection, or allow it only
  with security; check the rule.
- Escalate proportionately: reminder, payment plan (time-to-pay/installment agreement),
  then enforcement (garnishment, liens, distraint), each with its own notice requirements.
- Consider hardship and vulnerability policies before enforcement.

## Gotchas
- Assessing the wrong legal entity (trading name, group member) is fatal.
- Extended time limits invoked without evidence of the required behaviour.
- Penalties raised automatically without findings or consideration of reductions.
- Enforcement on an amount still under valid objection.

## Verify
- [ ] Statutory power and type of assessment identified; within time limit.
- [ ] Each adjustment has evidence or a written method.
- [ ] Notice served correctly with appeal rights; service recorded.
- [ ] Objection reviewed independently, every ground answered.
- [ ] Collection status consistent with dispute status.
