# Raising assessments, handling objections and collecting tax debt

**Goal:** collect the right tax at the lowest cost to taxpayer and state: correct
assessments raised on evidence and in time, objections reviewed honestly, and debt
collected proportionately. Models: US IRS and UK HMRC procedure, with OECD good
practice. Time limits, notice requirements and enforcement powers are statutory and
differ by tax and country — the figures below are typical examples (US federal and UK income-tax rules checked on 2026-10-24), not
universal rules. Not tax or legal advice. For the taxpayer's side see
`law-government.tax-law.tax-controversy.audit-defence-appeals-and-tax-court-petitions`.

## 1. Return processing and risk selection
1. Validate returns on receipt (arithmetic, identity, required schedules); correct
   obvious errors by the statutory correction route with notice to the taxpayer.
2. Match third-party data (employer, bank, platform, customs) and flag mismatches.
3. Score risk; select cases for desk checks or audits by risk, not by convenience.
   Record selection reasons — they matter if selection is challenged.

## 2. Raising an assessment
1. **Check the time limit first.** Typical examples: US (IRC §6501) — generally 3 years from filing,
   6 years for an omission of more than 25% of gross income, unlimited for fraud or no return; UK income tax and CGT
   (Taxes Management Act 1970 ss.34 and 36) — generally 4 years, 6 for carelessness, 20 for deliberate behaviour.
2. Gather evidence: records, explanations from the taxpayer (with a fair chance to
   respond), third-party information obtained lawfully.
3. Compute tax, interest and penalties separately; penalties need their own findings
   on behaviour (careless, deliberate, concealed; reasonable excuse; disclosure).
4. Issue the statutory notice (e.g. US notice of deficiency — the "90-day letter", §§6212-6213; UK assessment or closure notice)
   with reasons, calculation and appeal rights.

## 3. Objections and review
- Objection received in time? If late, consider the statutory late-appeal criteria.
- Review by an officer **not involved** in the original decision (e.g. HMRC statutory
  review, IRS Independent Office of Appeals).
- Ask: is the law applied correctly? Is the evidence adequate? Would a tribunal uphold it?
  Concede weak points early; litigation risk is a legitimate settlement factor where
  policy allows.
- Postponement of disputed tax pending appeal: apply the statutory rules.

## 4. Collection
| Stage | Tool | Safeguard |
|---|---|---|
| Early | Reminders, phone contact, payment plans (time-to-pay / installment agreement) | Affordability assessment |
| Mid | Offsets against refunds, wage/bank garnishment or levy, liens | Prior notice; hearing rights where the law provides them |
| Late | Seizure, insolvency petitions, director/officer liability where law allows | Proportionality; vulnerability checks |
| End | Write-off when uncollectable or collection statute expires (US: generally 10 years from assessment, §6502(a)(1)) | Documented reasons |
Prioritise by amount, age and taxpayer behaviour; contact early — debt collectability
falls sharply with age.

## Gotchas
- An assessment issued one day late is void, however strong the merits.
- Penalties assessed without a behaviour finding are routinely overturned.
- Reviewers who just defend the original officer — the review becomes worthless.
- Aggressive enforcement against vulnerable taxpayers creates complaints and political risk.

## Verify
- [ ] Time limit confirmed and recorded before the notice issues.
- [ ] Assessment notice includes reasons, calculation and appeal rights.
- [ ] Penalty findings on behaviour documented separately.
- [ ] Objection reviewed by an independent officer within deadline.
- [ ] Collection action proportionate, preceded by required notices, and logged.
