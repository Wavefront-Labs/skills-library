# Registering vital events and correcting the register

**Goal:** record every birth, death and marriage once, promptly and accurately, so
people have legal identity and the state has reliable vital statistics. Models: the
UN Principles for vital statistics systems, England & Wales registration practice, and
US state vital-records offices. Deadlines, who may register, evidence and correction
powers differ by country and state; examples below (e.g. England & Wales: births
within 42 days, deaths generally within 5 days) must be checked against current law.

## 1. Registering an event
| Step | Birth | Death | Marriage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who informs | Parent(s); others if parents can't (hospital, occupier) | Relative, person present, occupier, administrator | Officiant/registrar at the ceremony |
| Core evidence | Hospital/midwife notification; parents' ID; marriage or parentage evidence for second parent's details | Medical certificate of cause of death (or coroner/medical-examiner release) | Notice/licence, ID, capacity evidence, witnesses |
| Key data | Name, sex, date/place, parents | Name, date/place, cause, occupation, informant | Parties, status, ages, witnesses |
Procedure:
1. Verify the informant is qualified and identify them.
2. Take the particulars **from the informant**, read them back, and have them check spelling.
3. Record in the register (paper or electronic) and have the informant sign or confirm.
4. Issue certificates (short or full forms) and any onward notifications (health,
   statistics, population register, social security, passport/ID agencies).

## 2. Late and delayed registration
- Past the statutory window, an extended procedure applies: more evidence (baptismal,
  school, medical, community-leader affidavits), sometimes a supervisor's approval or
  court order, and fees or penalties (many countries waive these to reach the unregistered).
- Mobile registration drives, school-based and health-facility registration are the
  main tools for closing coverage gaps (SDG 16.9, legal identity for all).
- Unregistered children are at risk of statelessness — escalate cases with
  nationality doubts to the appropriate authority.

## 3. Correcting the register
1. **Classify** the error: clerical (typo, transposition), factual error on the
   informant's statement, or a change of status (legitimation, adoption, gender
   recognition, parentage order) — each has a different legal route.
2. Clerical errors: usually corrected by the registrar with evidence and a marginal note
   signed and dated; the original entry is **never erased**.
3. Material errors (e.g. paternity, date of birth): stronger evidence, statutory
   declarations, supervisor or court involvement.
4. Status changes: re-registration or an annotated new entry under the specific statute;
   original entries may be sealed.
5. Issue new certificates reflecting the corrected entry; notify downstream agencies.

## 4. Fraud and data protection
- Common risks: births registered for non-existent children (benefit or identity fraud),
  deaths of living persons, sham marriages. Use data-matching with health notifications.
- Flag deceased persons' records to prevent identity theft ("death flagging" to passport,
  tax and credit systems).
- Certified copies: control blank security paper; log issues; verify requesters'
  entitlement where access is restricted.

## 5. Quality for statistics
- Monitor completeness (registrations vs expected events), timeliness and the share of
  deaths with ill-defined causes; feed back to doctors on certificate quality.
- Reconcile with census counts to estimate under-registration.

## Gotchas
- Accepting particulars from a third party when the informant is available.
- Overwriting an entry instead of annotating it.
- Correcting a "typo" that actually changes identity (date of birth, parentage).
- Not notifying downstream agencies after a correction.

## Verify
- [ ] Informant qualified and identified; particulars read back.
- [ ] Event registered within the statutory period or late procedure followed.
- [ ] Corrections classified and made via the correct legal route with marginal notes.
- [ ] Certificates issued from controlled stock; downstream notifications sent.
- [ ] Completeness and timeliness indicators reviewed periodically.
