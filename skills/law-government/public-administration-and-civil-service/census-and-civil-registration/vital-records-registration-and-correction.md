# Vital records registration and correction

**Goal:** create a register entry that is accurate on the day, legally made, and can be
corrected later through the right route without breaking its evidential value.

Civil registration law varies widely: who may register, deadlines, whether registration
is centralised or local, whether entries can be amended or only annotated. The UN
Principles and Recommendations for a Vital Statistics System describe the common model;
local statute and the registrar's handbook govern.

## 1. Registration: the four questions
For every event, establish before you write anything:
1. **Event and place** within your district/jurisdiction (registering out of district is a
   common error and may make the entry invalid or require transfer).
2. **Qualified informant:** the law lists who may register (parent, person present at the
   death, hospital, etc.). An unqualified informant cannot be accepted even if helpful.
3. **Time limit:** normal period, late registration period, and beyond that (often needing
   a higher authority, statutory declaration or court order).
4. **Evidence required:** medical notification of birth, medical certificate of cause of
   death or coroner/medical examiner release, marriage schedule signed by officiant and witnesses.

## 2. Taking particulars
- Read particulars back to the informant before signing. Most errors are spelling,
  dates and parents' details.
- Record names exactly as the informant states them, within legal naming rules; don't
  "correct" spellings.
- Parentage rules (e.g. registering an unmarried father, same-sex parents) are statutory and
  have changed in many jurisdictions; check the current rule, not habit.
- Deaths: a death referred to a coroner or medical examiner usually cannot be registered
  until they release it. Never register with a cause you were told informally.

## 3. Correction vs re-registration
Distinguish:
- **Clerical error** made by the registrar: often correctable by the registrar with evidence.
- **Error of fact** (wrong date, wrong parent): usually needs documentary evidence, a
  statutory declaration, and sometimes approval by a central authority or a court.
- **Change, not error** (later legitimation, adoption, gender recognition, name change):
  usually handled by re-registration, annotation, or a separate register, not by
  "correcting" the original.
Corrections are normally made by marginal note or linked record, leaving the original
visible to authorised staff. Never overwrite or delete.

## 4. Certificates and access
- Issue certified copies only of what the register says, including annotations.
- Access rules (who may obtain a full birth certificate, whether death records are public)
  vary; adoption and gender-recognition records are typically restricted.
- Identity fraud often uses death and birth records; follow the verification steps for
  requests.

## 5. Link to statistics and the population register
Registration feeds vital statistics and, in many states, the population or identity
register. Coding of cause of death (ICD) and transmission deadlines matter downstream; late
or incomplete records distort statistics and benefits systems.

## Gotchas
- Out-of-district or out-of-time registration without the required authority.
- "Correcting" a lawful change of status rather than annotating it.
- Accepting a cause of death that the medical certificate doesn't support.

## Verify
- [ ] Event in district, informant qualified, within time or authorised.
- [ ] Particulars read back and signed.
- [ ] Correction route matches error type; original preserved with annotation.
- [ ] Certificate reproduces entry plus annotations; requester entitled to that type.
