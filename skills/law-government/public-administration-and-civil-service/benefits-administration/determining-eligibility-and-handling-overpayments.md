# Determining eligibility and handling overpayments

**Goal:** make benefit decisions that are correct, evidenced, explained and quick —
and, when money has been paid wrongly, recover it lawfully without pushing people into
hardship. Models: US federal/state programmes (Social Security, SNAP, Medicaid) and UK
social security (e.g. Universal Credit). Eligibility rules, deadlines and recovery
powers are programme-specific and change often — always work from the current
operating manual (they are mostly public: e.g. SSA's POMS, DWP's Decision Makers' Guide).

## 1. Build the eligibility decision
For every claim, work through the statutory conditions as a checklist:
| Condition type | Examples | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Identity & residence | Citizenship/immigration status, residence, presence | ID, status checks via data matching |
| Category | Age, disability, caring, unemployment, household composition | Medical evidence, work-capability assessments, declarations |
| Means | Income, capital, earnings period, deductions, disregards | Payslips, bank statements, real-time income data |
| Conduct / ongoing | Work-search, reporting changes | Commitments, attendance records |
Steps:
1. Identify the **relevant period** and the claim date (backdating rules matter).
2. Request missing evidence once, clearly, with a deadline; record what was asked.
3. Apply each rule to the evidence; where evidence conflicts, make and record a
   finding of fact on the balance of probabilities.
4. Calculate the award (or nil) with a worksheet another officer can re-run.

## 2. The decision notice
Must state: decision, effective date, amount and how calculated, the facts found, the
rule applied, and **appeal rights with the deadline** (e.g. UK mandatory reconsideration
generally within one month; US SSA reconsideration generally within 60 days of
receipt — check the programme). Plain language; translations and accessible formats
on request.

For terminations or reductions in US means-tested programmes, due process usually
requires advance notice and an opportunity for a hearing before benefits stop
(*Goldberg v Kelly*, 1970); timely appeal may trigger "aid paid pending".

## 3. Overpayments
1. **Establish the overpayment:** period, correct vs paid amount, cause.
2. **Classify the cause** — it often decides recoverability:
   | Cause | Typical treatment (varies) |
   |---|---|
   | Official/agency error, claimant couldn't reasonably have known | Often not recoverable or waivable |
   | Claimant error / late reporting | Recoverable |
   | Suspected fraud | Separate referral; don't interview under caution unless trained and authorised |
3. Issue an **overpayment notice**: amount, period, reason, calculation, right to
   dispute and to request waiver, repayment options.
4. Consider **waiver/write-off** (e.g. SSA tests whether the person was without fault
   and recovery would defeat the purpose of the programme or be against equity).
5. Recovery: deductions from ongoing benefit within statutory caps, repayment plans,
   pausing for hardship; avoid duplicate recovery across agencies.

## 4. Appeals and quality
- On reconsideration, look at the decision afresh, not to defend it.
- Prepare appeal bundles with every document relied on, in date order.
- Sample-check decisions monthly (e.g. 5–10% per officer) for accuracy; track
  overturn rates by reason and feed them into training.

## Gotchas
- Treating the claimant's silence as a "no" when the request letter went to an old address.
- Recovering an overpayment caused by the agency's own processing error without
  checking whether the law allows it.
- Decision notices with no reasons — they generate appeals and lose them.
- Automated data matches treated as proof without human verification.

## Verify
- [ ] Each statutory condition has a recorded finding and evidence.
- [ ] Calculation worksheet attached and reproducible.
- [ ] Notice gives reasons, amounts, effective date and appeal deadline.
- [ ] Overpayment cause classified; waiver considered; recovery within caps.
- [ ] Decision sampled in QA where selected; overturns analysed.
