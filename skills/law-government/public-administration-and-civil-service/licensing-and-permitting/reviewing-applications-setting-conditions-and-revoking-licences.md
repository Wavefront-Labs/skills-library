# Reviewing applications, setting conditions and revoking licences

**Goal:** decide licence and permit applications on time, on the statutory criteria
only, with conditions that actually work — and take enforcement action that will
stand up on appeal. Models: England & Wales (e.g. premises licensing under the Licensing
Act 2003), EU authorisation-scheme principles (Services Directive), and US state and
municipal licensing with constitutional due process. The governing statute defines
criteria, consultees, timescales and appeal routes for each licence type — read it
for every regime you administer.

## 1. Intake and completeness (days 0–10)
1. Check the application against the prescribed form and list of required documents,
   fee, plans, certificates (insurance, competence, criminal record checks where lawful).
2. Decide **complete / incomplete** quickly and in writing; list exactly what is missing.
   In many regimes the statutory clock starts on completeness — and some provide
   **deemed approval** if the authority misses the deadline.
3. Log the dates: received, complete, consultation end, decision due.

## 2. Assessment
- Apply **only** the statutory criteria (e.g. under the Licensing Act 2003: crime and
  disorder, public safety, public nuisance, protection of children). Irrelevant
  considerations make the decision unlawful.
- Consult the statutory consultees (police, fire, environmental health, planning)
  and publicise as required (site notice, newspaper, online register).
- Treat objections on their merits: relevant to criteria, evidence-based, not merely
  "we don't want it".
- Check the applicant's fitness and compliance history where the regime allows.
- Where a hearing is required (contested applications), give notice, disclose the
  material relied on, and let the applicant respond.

## 3. Writing conditions
A good condition is **necessary, relevant, precise, enforceable and proportionate**.
| Weak | Better |
|---|---|
| "Noise shall be kept to a minimum." | "Amplified music shall not be audible at the boundary of any residential property after 23:00, assessed by an authorised officer." |
| "Adequate CCTV." | "CCTV covering all entrances, recording continuously during opening hours, retained 31 days, footage provided to police on request within 48 hours." |
Avoid duplicating other legal duties (fire safety, employment) unless the regime requires.

## 4. Renewals and variations
- Diary renewal reminders 60–90 days ahead; process late applications per the statute
  (some licences lapse automatically).
- Variations that change risk (hours, capacity, activities) get the same scrutiny as new
  applications; minor variations may have a lighter route.

## 5. Suspension and revocation
1. Gather evidence: inspection reports, complaints, police data, breach notices.
2. Use the **enforcement ladder** unless the risk is serious: advice → warning →
   additional conditions → suspension → revocation.
3. **Notice of intended action / show-cause letter:** allegations, evidence, proposed
   action, deadline to respond (e.g. 14–28 days), right to a hearing.
4. Interim (immediate) suspension only where the statute permits and the risk is
   imminent; follow up with a prompt full hearing.
5. Decision letter: findings, criteria, why lesser steps are inadequate, effective date,
   appeal rights and deadline.

## Gotchas
- Refusing on grounds outside the statute (moral disapproval, competition) — quashed on appeal.
- Conditions copied from a template that don't fit the premises.
- Missing the statutory clock and creating a deemed grant.
- Revocation letters that don't explain why lesser measures would not work.

## Verify
- [ ] Completeness decision issued and clock dates logged.
- [ ] Every reason for refusal or condition maps to a statutory criterion.
- [ ] Conditions pass the necessary/precise/enforceable/proportionate test.
- [ ] Enforcement followed notice, response, hearing and reasoned decision.
- [ ] Register updated; appeal deadline communicated.
