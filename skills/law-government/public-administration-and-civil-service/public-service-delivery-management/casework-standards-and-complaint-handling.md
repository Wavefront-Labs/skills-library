# Casework standards and complaint handling

**Goal:** a service where cases are handled consistently, files show what happened and why,
and complaints are resolved early and fed back into fixing the cause.

Ombudsman principles in many countries (e.g. "Principles of Good Administration" style
guidance) converge on the same ideas: get it right, be customer-focused, open and accountable,
fair and proportionate, put things right, seek continuous improvement. Local complaint
deadlines and ombudsman jurisdiction vary.

## 1. Casework standards
Set and publish a short standard every caseworker can follow:
- **Acknowledge** within a fixed time with a reference and expected timescale.
- **File notes:** date, what was done, what was decided and why, next action and owner. If
  it isn't on the file, a reviewer will assume it didn't happen.
- **Single owner** per case where possible; hand-offs recorded.
- **Decision checks:** sample a percentage of decisions for quality review, higher for new
  staff and complex case types; track error types, not just error rates.
- **Reasonable adjustments and vulnerability:** record needs (format, contact method,
  representative) and act on them every time, not only at first contact.

## 2. Managing backlog
- Measure age of cases, not only volume. A falling queue can hide old cases.
- Triage: urgent (risk of harm, income loss, statutory deadlines) first; then oldest first.
- Avoid "cherry-picking" easy cases to hit throughput targets; it ages the hard ones.
- Assisted-digital routes for people who can't use online services must have equal priority.

## 3. Complaints process (typically two stages)
- **Stage 1 – frontline resolution:** quick, informal, within days. Many complaints are a
  missing explanation or an unreturned call; fix it and record it.
- **Stage 2 – investigation:** by someone independent of the original decision, within a
  set period (often 20 working days, varies). Define the complaint points with the
  complainant at the start.
- Separate **complaints about service** (delay, rudeness, errors) from **disagreement with a
  statutory decision**, which normally goes to review or appeal. Signpost appeals clearly but
  still deal with any service failures in the complaint.

## 4. Investigation and response
For each complaint point: what should have happened (the standard), what did happen
(evidence), was there a failing, what was the impact, what remedy. Write in plain language,
apologise specifically where at fault, and explain the next step (ombudsman) with its time limit.

## 5. Remedies
Put the person back where they would have been: correct the decision, pay arrears with
interest where policy allows, make a modest payment for distress or time and trouble where
appropriate, and fix the process. Record remedies so they're consistent across similar cases.

## 6. Learning
Code complaints by root cause; review the top causes monthly with service owners; report
what changed. Ombudsmen look for evidence of learning, not just resolution.

## Gotchas
- Defensive responses that don't answer the actual points escalate cases.
- Treating "outside policy" as a reason not to look at service failure.
- Losing reasonable-adjustment flags when cases move teams.

## Verify
- [ ] Every case file shows decisions with reasons and a next action.
- [ ] Backlog reported by age; urgent triage criteria written.
- [ ] Complaint points agreed and answered one by one, independent at stage 2.
- [ ] Remedy recorded; root cause coded and reviewed.
