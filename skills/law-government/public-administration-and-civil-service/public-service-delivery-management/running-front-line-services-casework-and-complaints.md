# Running front-line services, casework and complaints

**Goal:** a statutory service (counter, phone, online, casework) where people get the
right outcome first time, cases move at a predictable pace, and complaints are fixed
and learned from. Models: UK service and ombudsman standards and US federal customer-
experience guidance; the statutory duties of your service (deadlines, decision rights,
equality and accessibility duties) override any generic target.

## 1. Understand demand before redesigning
1. Sample 200–500 contacts across channels for 2 weeks. Classify each as **value
   demand** (what the service exists for) or **failure demand** (chasing, "what's
   happening?", re-explaining, errors).
2. Failure demand is often 30–60% of contacts in struggling services — fix the cause
   (unclear letters, missing status updates, rework) instead of adding call-handlers.
3. Map the end-to-end journey including back-office hand-offs and waiting time.

## 2. Channel design
| Channel | Best for | Must have |
|---|---|---|
| Online | Routine, high-volume transactions | Accessibility (e.g. WCAG AA), plain language, save-and-return, status tracking |
| Phone | Complex queries, reassurance | Call-back option, scripts for triage not for conversations |
| Counter / face-to-face | Identity checks, vulnerable people | Appointments plus walk-in capacity, interpreters |
| Assisted digital | People who can't use online alone | Human help, not a dead end |
Never make online the only route to a statutory entitlement.

## 3. Casework standards
- A written **casework standard** per case type: what "complete" means, decision rights,
  timescales, evidence required, templates, escalation triggers.
- Allocate by skill and complexity; cap open caseloads; track **age of work in
  progress**, not just throughput.
- Quality assurance: check a sample (e.g. 5–10% per caseworker monthly, more for new
  staff) against the standard; feed results into coaching.
- Vulnerability: flags and adjustments recorded and honoured across channels.

## 4. Measure what matters
- End-to-end time (median and 90th percentile), first-contact resolution, error/rework
  rate, failure-demand share, user satisfaction, complaint rate, and cost per transaction.
- Display measures as run charts over time rather than monthly targets; targets drive
  gaming (closing and reopening cases, cherry-picking).

## 5. Complaint handling (two-stage model)
| Stage | Who | Typical timescale |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Frontline resolution | Staff at point of service | Within ~5 working days |
| 2. Investigation | Independent of the original decision | Within ~20 working days (with updates if longer) |
| Then | Ombudsman | Per ombudsman rules |
(Timescales follow common ombudsman model procedures — check your own.)
Good practice:
1. Acknowledge, define the complaint points with the complainant, agree the outcome they seek.
2. Investigate facts; distinguish complaints from appeals (a disagreement with a
   statutory decision goes to the appeal route).
3. Response: each point, finding (upheld/not upheld), reasons, apology where warranted,
   **remedy** (put things right: correct the decision, pay financial redress for loss or
   distress where policy allows), and signposting to the ombudsman.
4. Log root causes; report trends to management quarterly; close the loop with service changes.

## Gotchas
- Answering "where's my case?" calls faster instead of sending proactive status updates.
- Defensive complaint responses that ombudsmen later criticise.
- Digital-by-default without assisted routes — an equality and accessibility risk.
- Average times hiding a long tail of very old cases.

## Verify
- [ ] Demand analysis done; failure-demand causes listed and owned.
- [ ] Every channel accessible; a non-digital route exists.
- [ ] Casework standards written; QA sampling running.
- [ ] Measures cover time, quality, demand and satisfaction, shown as trends.
- [ ] Complaints answered point by point with remedies; trends reported.
