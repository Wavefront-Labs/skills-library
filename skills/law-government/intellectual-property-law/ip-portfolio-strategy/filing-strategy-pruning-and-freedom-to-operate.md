# Filing strategy, pruning and freedom to operate

**Goal:** spend the IP budget on rights that protect revenue or create leverage, drop
the ones that don't, and know before launch whether someone else's patents block the
product. Timelines here follow the Paris Convention and PCT, which are near-universal;
national fees, grace periods and opinion-letter conventions vary.

## 1. Patent, secret, publish — decide per invention
| Choose | When |
|---|---|
| **Patent** | Invention visible in the product or detectable by reverse engineering; infringement provable; commercial life fits the patent term (check the term in each country) |
| **Trade secret** | Process or know-how hidden inside the factory or server; infringement undetectable; long value life |
| **Defensive publication** | Want freedom to use, don't need exclusivity, budget limited |
Map each invention to a business reason: protects a product line, blocks a competitor's
design-around, supports licensing or standards work, or adds to a fundraising/
acquisition story. No reason → don't file.

## 2. Filing timeline
1. **Priority filing** (often a domestic or provisional application) — sets the date.
   Avoid public disclosure before it.
2. Within **12 months** (Paris Convention Art. 4C(1); 6 months for designs and trademarks):
   file abroad claiming priority or file a **PCT** application to defer country decisions.
3. PCT search report and written opinion show the art early — re-evaluate.
4. National/regional phase entry, at **30 months** from priority for most PCT offices (WIPO
   PCT FAQ; some offices set a different period, so check the PCT Applicant's Guide for
   each) — the big cost decision. Pick countries by: where you
   manufacture, major markets, where competitors manufacture, where enforcement is
   practical. Translation costs dominate in some regions.
5. Continuations/divisionals to keep options open on commercially important families.
Keep a docket of all deadlines: missing a priority or national-phase deadline is often
unrecoverable or only recoverable with strict criteria.

## 3. Portfolio review and pruning
Run a review at least yearly and before each maintenance/annuity payment:
- Classify each family: **core** (covers current products / competitor products),
  **option** (future roadmap), **leverage** (licensing/defence), **dead** (abandoned tech).
- Claim-to-product mapping: does any claim actually read on a product, ours or theirs?
  Without a map, value is guesswork.
- Drop countries before drop families: keep the strongest jurisdictions.
- Consider selling or licensing non-core patents instead of abandoning.
- Record decisions and reasons; abandonment is usually irreversible.

## 4. Landscape analysis
- Define the technology space; search by classification codes and keywords (see
  law-government.intellectual-property-law.patent-search-platforms).
- Cluster results by assignee, sub-technology and filing trend; identify **white space**
  (few filings) and crowded areas.
- Use it for R&D direction, competitor monitoring and acquisition targets. A landscape is
  statistical — it's not an FTO opinion.

## 5. Freedom-to-operate (FTO)
1. Define the product precisely by feature, and the countries of making, selling, using.
2. Search **in-force and pending** patents in those countries (pending claims can change).
3. Screen by independent claims: does every element read on the product? Note
   doctrine-of-equivalents risk.
4. For hits: check legal status, expiry, validity (prior art), licence availability,
   and **design-around** options.
5. Report a risk grade per patent with recommended action. Engage counsel for formal
   opinions on high-risk patents Plan privilege handling.

## Gotchas
- Inventors' conference papers, demos and investor decks are public disclosures.
- FTO is a snapshot — re-run before launch in new countries or after major design changes.

## Verify
- [ ] Each invention has a recorded patent/secret/publish decision and business reason.
- [ ] Priority-year and national-phase deadlines docketed for all families.
- [ ] Portfolio review classified every family and mapped claims to products.
- [ ] FTO covers every launch country, pending applications included, with risk grades.
- [ ] Abandonments and design-around decisions documented.
