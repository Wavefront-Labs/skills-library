# Secrecy programmes, NDAs and misappropriation claims

**Goal:** make sure the client's valuable information legally *is* a trade secret
(because they took reasonable steps to keep it secret), and when it walks out the door,
move quickly with evidence. US federal (DTSA) and state (UTSA-based) law, the EU Trade
Secrets Directive and common-law breach of confidence (UK, Commonwealth) share the core
definition; remedies and procedure differ.

## 1. The definition drives everything
Under the US Defend Trade Secrets Act (18 U.S.C. 1839(3)) a trade secret is business,
scientific, technical, economic or engineering information where the owner has taken
**reasonable measures** to keep it secret and it derives independent economic value from
not being generally known or readily ascertainable. Other systems use similar elements;
check the local statute.

## 2. Building a secrecy programme
1. **Inventory**: list categories of secrets (formulas, source code, customer lists with
   pricing, supplier terms, process parameters, roadmaps). Rank by value.
2. **Access control**: need-to-know permissions, logging, MFA; segregate crown jewels.
3. **Marking**: label confidential documents — but don't mark everything, or the label
   means nothing.
4. **Agreements**: employee confidentiality and invention-assignment agreements; NDAs
   with contractors, partners, prospective investors (note many investors refuse NDAs —
   limit what you disclose).
5. **Training and policies**: acceptable use, personal devices, cloud storage, AI tool
   use (pasting secrets into external tools can be a disclosure).
6. **Onboarding**: new hires certify they brought nothing from former employers —
   protects against being the *defendant*.
7. **Offboarding**: exit interview, return-of-property certification, revoke access the
   same day, review recent download/email/USB activity for high-risk departures.

## 3. NDA drafting checklist
- Definition of Confidential Information (marked + reasonably understood as confidential).
- Standard exclusions: already known, public through no fault, independently developed,
  received from a third party lawfully.
- Use restriction (purpose only) *and* disclosure restriction.
- Term: confidentiality for trade secrets should last as long as they remain secret, not
  a short fixed period.
- Return/destruction, compelled disclosure procedure, remedies (injunctive relief).
- Watch **residuals clauses** (allowing use of information retained in unaided memory) —
  they can gut protection.
- US: include the DTSA **whistleblower immunity notice** (or a cross-reference to a policy
  document with it) in employee and contractor agreements entered into or updated after
  11 May 2016; without it the employer may not be awarded exemplary damages or attorney
  fees against that individual (18 U.S.C. 1833(b)(3)).

## 4. Responding to misappropriation
Speed matters — evidence disappears and delay undermines urgency for injunctions.
1. **Preserve and investigate**: forensic images of the departed employee's devices and
   accounts (by a qualified examiner, maintaining chain of custody); log reviews.
2. **Identify the secrets with particularity**; "everything in the engineering drive"
   will not do.
3. Cease-and-desist letters to the individual and new employer (careful: overreaching
   letters can create claims against the client).
4. **TRO / preliminary injunction**: likelihood of success, irreparable harm, evidence of
   taking (downloads before departure are classic), return of materials and forensic
   inspection as relief.
5. US DTSA allows an **ex parte civil seizure** order "only in extraordinary
   circumstances" (18 U.S.C. 1836(b)(2)); a DTSA action must be brought within 3 years after
   the misappropriation is discovered or should have been discovered (18 U.S.C. 1836(d)).
6. Remedies: check the statute for damages, exemplary damages and fees.

## Gotchas
- Non-compete rules vary by jurisdiction and change; check current law and don't rely on
  them as the main protection.
- Under the DTSA, improper means does not include reverse engineering or independent
  derivation (18 U.S.C. 1839(6)).
- Litigating can expose the secret: seek protective orders and sealed filings.

## Verify
- [ ] Inventory of trade secrets exists and the crown jewels have tighter access.
- [ ] Every employee/contractor has signed confidentiality terms (with DTSA notice in the US).
- [ ] Offboarding includes same-day access revocation and activity review.
- [ ] In a dispute: forensic preservation done and secrets identified with particularity.
- [ ] Injunction papers show specific evidence of taking and irreparable harm.
