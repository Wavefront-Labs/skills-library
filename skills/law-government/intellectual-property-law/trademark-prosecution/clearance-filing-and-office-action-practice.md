# Clearance, filing and office-action practice

**Goal:** pick a mark that can be registered and used without infringing, file it with
the right scope, and get it through examination and opposition. Rules are territorial:
registration in one country gives no rights elsewhere. US (USPTO/TTAB) terms with EU
(EUIPO) and UK equivalents noted; verify current fees and procedures per office.

## 1. Distinctiveness first (cheapest knockout)
Place the mark on the **spectrum**: generic (never protectable) → descriptive (needs
acquired distinctiveness/secondary meaning) → suggestive → arbitrary → fanciful
(strongest). Clients love descriptive names; explain early that "FastPrint" for printing
may face a descriptiveness refusal (in the US, 15 U.S.C. 1052(e)). Also screen for
geographic descriptiveness, surnames and deceptive terms (15 U.S.C. 1052(e)) and other
statutory bars.

## 2. Clearance searching
1. **Knockout search**: the office's register (identical and near-identical marks in
   related classes), plus a web/app-store/domain search. Cheap and kills obvious losers.
2. **Full search** (commercial search report): phonetic equivalents, translations,
   design marks, common-law (unregistered) uses, company names. Required before major
   investment.
3. **Risk opinion**: assess likelihood of confusion for each hit — similarity of marks
   (sight, sound, meaning, commercial impression), relatedness of goods/services, trade
   channels, strength of the earlier mark, evidence of actual confusion. Grade risk, don't promise.

## 3. Drafting the application
- **Owner**: the entity that controls the nature and quality of the goods — often the
  operating company, not the founder. Check the office's ownership rules before filing.
- **Goods/services**: use Nice classes and the office's acceptable ID wording where
  possible. Too broad → refusal or vulnerability to non-use challenge; too narrow →
  poor protection. Cover current use plus genuine near-term plans.
- **Filing basis** (US): use in commerce (with specimen showing the mark as used) or
  intent to use (later statement of use, with extension deadlines). The EU does not require
  use to register, but an EU trade mark registered for more than five years can be
  revoked for non-use (Art. 58(1)(a) EUTMR; EUIPO guidelines).
- Word mark vs design (logo) mark: consider filing both.
- International: consider the Madrid Protocol (one application designating many
  countries, dependent on the base mark for five years per WIPO's Madrid guide —
  "central attack" risk; check WIPO for any change) versus national filings. Watch
  priority deadlines (6 months for trademarks under Paris Convention Art. 4C(1)).

## 4. Office actions
Calendar the deadline (US: 3 months from issue, extendable by 3 months on a timely
request and fee, 37 CFR 2.62(a); the Office action may set a different period). Typical refusals and responses:
- **Likelihood of confusion (US §2(d), 15 U.S.C. 1052(d))**: argue differences in marks
  and goods, narrow the ID, or obtain a consent agreement from the prior owner.
- **Descriptiveness (15 U.S.C. 1052(e))**: argue suggestiveness or claim acquired
  distinctiveness under 1052(f) with evidence of use (five years of substantially
  exclusive and continuous use may be accepted as prima facie evidence).
- **Specimen refusal** and **ID / classification** issues: check the Office action and the
  office's current guidance.

## 5. Oppositions
After publication, third parties may oppose (US: within 30 days after publication,
extendable by a further 30 days on written request before the period ends and then for
good cause, 15 U.S.C. 1063(a); EUIPO: 3 months from publication). Defending: assess the
opponent's priority and evidence; consider coexistence agreements, ID narrowing, or a
cooling-off period (EUIPO provides 2 months) to settle.
Monitor the register with a **watch service** to oppose others' confusingly similar marks.

## Gotchas
- Registrations need maintenance filings (US, non-Madrid registrations: Section 8 declaration
  between the 5th and 6th years; renewal between the 9th and 10th years and every 10 years
  after, each with a 6-month grace period) — diarise them.
