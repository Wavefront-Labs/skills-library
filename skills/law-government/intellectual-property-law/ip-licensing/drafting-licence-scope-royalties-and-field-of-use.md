# Drafting licence scope, royalties and field of use

**Goal:** a licence that says exactly what rights pass, pays the licensor in a way that
can be calculated and audited, and doesn't fall apart under competition law,
insolvency or a change of control. Contract law and IP law are national; the structure
below is common to most common-law practice. Competition law varies — check it for exclusive or restrictive terms.

## 1. Define what is licensed
- **Licensed IP** schedule: patent numbers (and families, continuations), registered
  marks, copyrighted software by version, know-how described concretely (documents,
  training days). Vague "all related IP" clauses breed disputes.
- Decide whether future filings and **improvements** are included, and whose.
- Confirm the licensor owns or controls the IP (chain of title, co-owner consents,
  government/university rights, prior exclusive licences).

## 2. The grant
Write the grant clause as a precise sentence: *Licensor grants Licensee a [non-exclusive /
exclusive / sole] licence under the Licensed IP to [make, use, sell, import, reproduce,
distribute…] Licensed Products in the Field in the Territory during the Term.*
- **Exclusive** excludes even the licensor; **sole** excludes others but not the licensor.
  Exclusive licensees may get standing to sue (varies by jurisdiction and drafting).
- **Field of use**: define by application/market ("human therapeutics", "automotive
  sensors"), not vaguely. Field splits let a licensor license the same patent to several
  companies.
- **Territory**: tie to where products are made, sold or used; mind exhaustion/parallel
  import rules, which differ by jurisdiction.
- **Sublicensing**: allowed? to whom? with what flow-through obligations and share of
  sublicence income?
- Reserved rights: licensor's research use, academic publication (university deals).

## 3. Money
| Element | Purpose | Drafting point |
|---|---|---|
| Upfront fee | Access value | Non-refundable, non-creditable? |
| Milestones | Share development risk | Objective trigger events |
| Running royalty | Share commercial upside | Rate × defined **royalty base** |
| Minimum annual royalty | Diligence | Exclusive deals especially |
| Sublicence share | Income from sublicensees | Define what counts as income |

- **Royalty base**: "Net Sales" defined with listed deductions (returns, taxes, freight,
  discounts), capped if needed. Combination products need an allocation formula.
- **Royalty stacking**: licensee may need several licences; consider an anti-stacking
  reduction with a floor.
- **Term of royalties**: under *Kimble v. Marvel* (decided 22 June 2015) the Supreme Court
  declined to overrule *Brulotte*, so a patent holder cannot collect royalties for use
  after the patent expires. The opinion notes workarounds such as deferring payments for
  pre-expiry use, basing royalties on non-patent rights (for example a licensed trade
  secret), and non-royalty structures. Other jurisdictions treat this differently.
- Reports (quarterly, product-level), currency conversion, late interest, withholding tax
  and gross-up.
- **Audit rights**: independent accountant, frequency limit, look-back period, cost
  shifting if underpayment exceeds an agreed threshold.

## 4. Risk allocation and exit
- Warranties: licensors typically warrant ownership and authority but disclaim validity
  and non-infringement of third-party rights; negotiate.
- Indemnities, insurance, liability caps (with carve-outs).
- Enforcement: who may sue infringers, who controls, how recoveries split.
- **Diligence obligations** (commercially reasonable efforts, milestones) with
  termination or conversion to non-exclusive for failure.
- Assignment/change of control; insolvency (under US Bankruptcy Code §365(n), if a trustee rejects
  a licence of IP where the debtor is licensor, the licensee may elect to retain its rights; check jurisdiction).
- Termination effects: sell-off period, return of know-how, survival clauses.

## Gotchas
- **Grant-backs** of licensee improvements and no-challenge clauses can raise competition
  issues in some systems; check the applicable competition rules.
- Tax: royalty flows across borders can trigger withholding and transfer-pricing issues.

## Verify
- [ ] Licensed IP listed specifically; licensor's title confirmed.
- [ ] Grant sentence states rights, exclusivity, field, territory and term.
- [ ] Net Sales and every payment trigger defined and calculable.
- [ ] Post-expiry royalty and competition-law issues checked for the jurisdiction.
- [ ] Diligence, audit, enforcement and termination mechanics present.
