# Canvass, phone bank, voter ID and GOTV operations

**Goal:** talk to the right voters, record what they say, persuade the persuadable, and
make sure every supporter actually votes. Field work is where the campaign plan's vote
goal (see campaign-management) gets delivered.

## 1. Universes, not lists
From the voter file (see voter-file-and-campaign-tech):
- **ID / persuasion universe:** likely voters whose support is unknown or soft.
- **GOTV universe:** identified or modelled supporters with less-than-certain turnout.
- **Do-not-contact:** strong opponents (don't waste contacts or remind them to vote).
Prioritise by modelled support and turnout scores where available.

## 2. Scripts
Door script skeleton (~2 minutes):
1. Greeting and name: "Hi, I'm [name], a volunteer with [campaign]."
2. **ID question:** "On a scale of 1 to 5, where 1 is definitely [candidate] and 5 is
   definitely [opponent], where are you?"
3. Branch: 1s → volunteer ask + voting plan; 2–4 → issue question + persuasion
   (listen, share the relevant message point, personal story); 5s → thank and leave.
4. **Voting plan** for supporters: when, how (postal, early, election day), how
   they'll get there. Plan-making measurably raises turnout in the research literature.
5. Record responses immediately in the app.
**Deep canvassing** (longer, non-judgemental exchange of personal stories) has evidence
of durable persuasion on some issues but needs more training and time per door.

## 3. Volunteer operation
- Recruit continuously: every supporter ID'd gets a volunteer ask.
- Shift structure: welcome, 15-minute training with role-play, pairs for new volunteers,
  turf of ~2–3 hours, debrief and **ask for the next shift** before they leave.
- Leaders: promote reliable volunteers to team leaders who own a neighbourhood.
- **Relational organising:** volunteers contact people they know — higher trust and
  contact rates.
- Safety: pairs after dark, a check-in system, no entering homes, guidance on dogs
  and hostile residents.

## 4. Metrics (weekly, against goals)
Doors knocked, contacts (conversations), contact rate (contacts ÷ attempts; doors are
often roughly 20–30% in many contexts — verify locally), IDs by support level,
volunteer shifts filled and flaked, phone and text attempts and contacts.
Compare against the contact numbers your vote goal requires; if behind, adjust
early, not in the last week.

## 5. GOTV
- Timeline: begins when **postal/early voting** opens — chase banked ballots: who has
  requested, who has returned (where that data is published or available).
- Election day: staging locations in each area with a staging lead, walk lists of
  supporters who haven't yet voted, **knock-up** waves through the day, rides to polls
  if lawful, phone/text banks for remaining supporters.
- Poll observers/tellers where allowed, collecting who has voted to remove them from
  lists. Follow observer rules exactly.
- Final push in the last hours before polls close, focusing on the highest-support
  unvoted names.

## Legal limits
Rules on calling and texting (consent, automated dialling, do-not-call lists), data
protection on voter data, electioneering distance from polling places, rides and
incentives, and canvasser identification **vary by jurisdiction** — brief volunteers on
the local rules. Never offer anything of value in exchange for voting.

## Gotchas
- Talking only to friendly neighbourhoods because it's pleasant.
- Data not entered the same day — GOTV lists then chase people who already said no.
- Over-contacting the same supporters while untouched turf remains.

## Verify
- [ ] Universes defined; strong opponents excluded from GOTV.
- [ ] Script records support level and voting plan.
- [ ] Every shift ends with a re-booking ask; leaders own turfs.
- [ ] Metrics tracked weekly against contact goals derived from the vote goal.
- [ ] GOTV plan covers early/postal chase and election-day staging, within local law.
