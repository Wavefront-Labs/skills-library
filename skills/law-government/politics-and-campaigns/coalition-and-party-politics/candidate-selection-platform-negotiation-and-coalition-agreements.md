# Candidate selection, platform negotiation and coalition agreements

**Goal:** choose credible candidates, agree what the party stands for, and — where no
party wins outright — form and sustain a governing arrangement without losing your own
members. Party structures and formation conventions **vary widely by country**; party
rules are often legally enforceable contracts with members, so follow them to the letter.

## 1. Candidate selection
Methods range from leadership appointment, to selection committees, to member ballots,
to open primaries (where state law runs them, as in much of the US).
Running a fair process:
- **Publish rules and timetable** before nominations open: eligibility, nomination
  requirements, hustings, voting method, appeals.
- **Vetting/due diligence:** identity and eligibility to stand (citizenship, residency,
  disqualifications), declarations of interests, criminal records where lawful,
  financial issues (bankruptcy, tax), social media history, past statements,
  business conflicts. Ask candidates to self-disclose anything that could embarrass the
  party; undisclosed issues found later are usually grounds for deselection.
- **Diversity** goals and any legal permissions for positive action (e.g. all-women
  shortlists are lawful in some countries, not others).
- Independent returning officer for internal ballots; secure membership lists; clear
  freeze date for voter eligibility.
- Handle complaints and appeals through the published procedure.

## 2. Platform / manifesto
- Process: policy commissions or committees, member consultation, conference votes or
  leadership sign-off per the party's rules.
- Test policies for cost (see budget-and-fiscal-policy-analysis), deliverability and
  appeal to target voters.
- Keep a distinction between **firm pledges** (costed, specific) and aspirations —
  pledges become coalition bargaining chips and accountability yardsticks.
- Manage internal factions by giving them a visible stake while protecting the core message.

## 3. Coalition and support negotiations
After an inconclusive election:
- **Prepare before polling day:** negotiation team mandated by the party, red lines,
  priority list, policy costings, analysis of each possible partner's manifesto and
  internal politics, and the constitutional conventions on who gets the first attempt
  to form a government.
- **Forms of agreement:** full coalition (shared ministries), **confidence-and-supply**
  (support on confidence votes and budgets in exchange for policy commitments), or
  issue-by-issue minority government.
- **Negotiate**: exchange priorities; trade across issues (portfolios vs policies);
  cost every concession; write as you go.
- Agreement contents: policy programme, portfolio allocation, budget principles,
  **coordination mechanisms** (coalition committee, consultation rules, who speaks
  for government), how disagreements are resolved, agreed "agree to differ"
  areas, duration and review, exit procedure.
- **Ratification:** many parties require member or council approval — build that into
  the timetable.

## 4. Managing the coalition
- Regular leadership meetings; a coalition committee to resolve disputes early.
- Credit-sharing: junior partners often lose support if their wins aren't visible.
- Collective responsibility and its exceptions.
- Track commitments against the agreement; publish progress.

## 5. Party management
Membership growth and data protection, branch structures, finances (party funding law
applies), discipline and complaints procedures that are fair and timely, and
succession planning.

## Gotchas
- Breaking your own selection rules invites legal challenge and bitter splits.
- Red lines announced publicly before the election limit room to negotiate.
- Coalition agreements that are too detailed age quickly; include a review mechanism.

## Verify
- [ ] Selection rules, vetting and appeals published before nominations.
- [ ] Candidates self-disclosed and were vetted against a checklist.
- [ ] Manifesto separates costed pledges from aspirations.
- [ ] Negotiation mandate, red lines and costings agreed before polling day.
- [ ] Agreement covers coordination, dispute resolution, review and ratification.
