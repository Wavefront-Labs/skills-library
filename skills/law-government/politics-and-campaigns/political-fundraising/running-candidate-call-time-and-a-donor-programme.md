# Running candidate call time and a donor programme

**Goal:** raise the money in the campaign plan, on schedule, from a broad and legally
permissible donor base — mostly through the candidate personally asking. Models: US
federal and state candidate campaigns (where call time is central) and UK candidates and
parties (where spending limits and permissible-donor rules shape fundraising). Limits,
prohibited sources and reporting thresholds vary by jurisdiction and are often indexed
each cycle — check the current figures before printing any donation form.

## 1. Write the finance plan
Break the money goal into sources with monthly targets:
| Source | Typical tools |
|---|---|
| Candidate's personal network | call time, personal letters |
| Major donors | call time, one-to-one meetings, host committees |
| Events | receptions with hosts who each commit to raise an amount |
| Small-dollar / grassroots | email, SMS, digital ads, online platforms (e.g. ActBlue/WinRed-class tools in the US) |
| Institutional / party | political committees, party funds where lawful |
Front-load: early money buys staff and credibility. Track raised vs plan weekly.

## 2. Build the prospect list
- Start with the candidate's own contacts: phone, email, social media, alumni, colleagues,
  past causes. Most first-time candidates underestimate this list — aim for hundreds of names.
- Add donors to like-minded past candidates and causes from **public** disclosure
  records — but check the rules: some jurisdictions restrict using disclosure data to
  solicit (US federal law restricts using FEC-reported individual contributor information
  for soliciting contributions — take advice before using it).
- Research each prospect: capacity (giving history), connection to the candidate, issue
  interest. Set a specific **ask amount**.

## 3. Run call time
- Block fixed hours in the candidate's calendar (in competitive US races, commonly
  several hours a day) and protect them.
- A call-time manager prepares **call sheets**: name, contact, connection, giving history,
  ask amount, notes from last contact.
- Script: catch up briefly, explain why the race matters and why now, **make a specific
  ask** ("Can you contribute X today?"), then stop talking.
- Record outcome immediately: yes (amount and how they'll give), maybe (follow-up date),
  no. Send the donation link or pledge follow-up within the hour.
- Benchmarks: dials per hour, contacts, pledges, dollars pledged vs collected.

## 4. Events and hosts
Recruit a host committee where each host commits to raise an amount from their own
network. The event itself mainly collects pledges already made. Budget events tightly;
low-dollar events often lose money and are for supporters, not fundraising.

## 5. Small-dollar programme
Build the list (sign-up petitions, events, ads), test subject lines and asks, deadline-driven
appeals around reporting dates, and recurring monthly options. Avoid deceptive
pre-checked recurring boxes and false urgency — regulators and platforms have acted against them.

## 6. Compliance on every gift
- Collect required donor information (US: name, address, employer, occupation above a
  threshold, with "best efforts" follow-up; UK: details to check the donor is permissible).
- Enforce contribution limits per donor, per election (primary and general are often
  separate elections in the US).
- Refuse prohibited sources: foreign nationals (barred in US federal law and generally in
  the UK via permissibility rules), straw-donor or conduit gifts made in someone else's
  name, corporate gifts where banned, anonymous gifts above thresholds.
- Return or refund problem contributions within the required time and document it.
See law-government.politics-and-campaigns.campaign-finance-compliance.disclosure-reporting-and-contribution-limit-tracking.

## Gotchas
- The candidate dodging call time — the most common reason campaigns miss targets.
- Counting pledges as cash; chase collection relentlessly.
- Soliciting on government property or with official resources, prohibited in many jurisdictions.
- Reimbursing anyone for a donation (an illegal straw-donor scheme).

## Verify
- [ ] Finance plan by source and month, tracked weekly.
- [ ] Prospect list researched with specific ask amounts.
- [ ] Call time scheduled, staffed with call sheets and logged.
- [ ] Every contribution screened for limits, permissibility and required donor details.
- [ ] Pledge follow-up and refund procedures in writing.
