# Donor programme, call time and fundraising events

**Goal:** hit the fundraising targets in the campaign plan on the cash-flow schedule,
with every contribution legally acceptable. Money and compliance are inseparable — see
campaign-finance-compliance for limits, permissible sources and reporting, which **vary
greatly by jurisdiction** (some systems cap donations tightly, ban corporate money, or
provide public funding; others allow large individual gifts).

## 1. The fundraising plan
- Target by month (from the budget cash-flow), split by source: candidate's network,
  major donors, small-dollar/digital, events, party/organisations (where lawful),
  candidate self-funding.
- The **candidate's own network** usually provides the early money — the "first circle":
  family, friends, colleagues, professional contacts, past donors to similar candidates.
- Early money matters: it buys staff and signals viability to later donors.

## 2. Prospect list and research
- Build the list from the candidate's phone, email, social contacts, alumni/professional
  networks, and public donor records where lawful to use (some jurisdictions restrict
  using public finance reports for solicitation — check).
- For each prospect: relationship to the candidate, giving capacity, giving history,
  issue interests, the **ask amount**, and status. Compliance check: permissible donor?
  already given this cycle?

## 3. Call time (the core of candidate fundraising)
- Block **fixed daily hours** in the candidate's schedule — for many campaigns it's the
  single biggest use of candidate time. Protect it.
- A call-time manager prepares **call sheets**: name, numbers, relationship, notes,
  prior gifts, the specific ask, and talking points.
- Script: personal connection → why I'm running and why it's winnable (numbers) → the
  **specific ask** ("Can I count on you for 500?") → **silence**, let them answer →
  confirm amount, method and date → thank.
- The call-time manager dials, logs outcomes, and sends **same-day follow-up** (link or
  remittance details). Pledges uncollected within a week often disappear.
- Track calls/hour, contacts, pledges, collected — and the conversion between them.

## 4. Small-dollar and digital
- Email and SMS lists built through sign-ups, petitions and events (with consent under
  local data and anti-spam laws).
- Clear, urgent, honest asks tied to milestones (reporting deadlines, ad buys).
  Misleading "match" claims and pre-checked recurring boxes have drawn regulatory and
  platform action — avoid them.
- **Recurring** monthly giving smooths cash flow.
- Use the payment platform common in your market (e.g. ActBlue/WinRed in US
  partisan politics) that captures required donor details.

## 5. Events
- A **host committee** of people who each commit to raise a set amount (sell tickets/
  bundle) — events are fundraising by the hosts, not by the door.
- Tiered pricing, the candidate's short remarks and a direct ask, a follow-up for
  non-attendees ("sorry you missed it").
- In-kind costs (venue, food) are usually contributions themselves and count toward limits.

## 6. Stewardship
Thank within 48 hours (personal for major donors), regular updates, invitations, and
re-asks at the right intervals. Previous donors are the best prospects; lapsed donors
are the cheapest to reactivate.

## Gotchas
- Accepting a gift before checking permissibility; refunds after the fact are messy and
  may need reporting.
- Contributions in someone else's name (straw donors) are illegal in most systems — watch
  for clusters of identical gifts from one employer or family.
- Public-employee or official-resource solicitation restrictions in many jurisdictions.

## Verify
- [ ] Monthly targets by source tied to the cash-flow plan.
- [ ] Every prospect has an ask amount and a compliance check.
- [ ] Call time scheduled daily; pledges followed up the same day.
- [ ] Digital asks honest; recurring giving offered with explicit consent.
- [ ] Every gift recorded with required donor information before deposit.
