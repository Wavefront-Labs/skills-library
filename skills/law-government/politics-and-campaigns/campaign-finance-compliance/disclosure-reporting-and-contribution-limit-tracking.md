# Disclosure reporting and contribution-limit tracking

**Goal:** a campaign whose every pound or dollar in and out is lawful, recorded and
reported on time, so that no report is late, no excessive or prohibited contribution is
kept, and any audit is routine. Models: US federal candidate committees (FECA, FEC
reports) and UK parliamentary candidates (RPA 1983 spending limits, PPERA donation
rules). State, provincial and local regimes differ widely; thresholds, limits and
deadlines are indexed or amended frequently — always pull the current figures from the
regulator for the specific election.

## 1. Set up correctly at the start
- **Register** when the law requires (US federal: a candidate generally registers once
  raising or spending passes a statutory threshold, and designates a principal campaign
  committee; UK: a candidate appoints an election agent, who is legally responsible for
  spending returns).
- Appoint a treasurer/agent and a deputy; record who may authorise spending.
- Open a dedicated campaign bank account; never mix personal funds without documenting
  them as contributions or loans.
- Choose compliance software or a well-structured ledger with donor and payee records.

## 2. Screen and record every contribution
For each receipt record: date received, amount, method, donor name and address, and
(US) employer and occupation where required; (UK) evidence that the donor is
permissible. Then:
1. **Permissibility:** reject foreign nationals and other prohibited sources (corporations
   and unions directly at US federal level; impermissible donors in the UK), gifts in
   the name of another, and cash above thresholds.
2. **Aggregation:** add the gift to the donor's running total for the same election (US
   primary and general are separate). Above the limit? Seek a written
   **redesignation** or **reattribution** where the rules allow, otherwise refund within
   the deadline (US: 60 days for many fixes — confirm in current FEC rules).
3. **Itemisation:** flag donors whose aggregate passes the itemisation threshold so they
   appear on the report.
4. **Deposit** within the required time (US federal: 10 days).
In-kind contributions (goods, services, free premises) count too — value them at
market value.

## 3. Track spending
- Every payment backed by an invoice or receipt, with purpose.
- **Spending limits** (UK: statutory limits for the regulated period, split into
  long and short campaign for parliamentary elections; many US states and
  localities set limits for publicly financed candidates): maintain a running total
  against the limit, including notional spending (things used but not paid for) and
  shared costs apportioned between candidates.
- Personal-use prohibitions: campaign funds may not pay candidates' personal expenses
  that would exist without the campaign (US federal rule; similar rules elsewhere).

## 4. File disclosure reports
- Build a filing calendar: regular (quarterly/monthly), pre-election and post-election
  reports, plus 24- or 48-hour notices for large late contributions where required.
- Reconcile the ledger to bank statements before every filing; cash on hand must match.
- File electronically where mandatory; keep the submission confirmation.
- UK: the agent submits the spending return and declaration within the statutory period
  after the result, with invoices for payments above the threshold.

## 5. Disclaimers / imprints
All campaign materials and paid digital ads carry the required "paid for by" disclaimer
(US) or imprint with promoter's name and address (UK, now including digital material).
Check sizes and wording rules for each medium.

## 6. Audit and enquiry response
- Keep records for the required retention period (often several years).
- If the regulator writes (US: Request for Additional Information; UK: Electoral
  Commission enquiry), respond by the deadline with documents; amend reports where errors
  are found; consider voluntary disclosure of significant errors — usually treated better
  than discovery.
- Engage election counsel for anything involving potential knowing violations.

## Gotchas
- Joint-account cheques attributed to one spouse only, creating an excessive contribution.
- Forgetting in-kind contributions and notional spending.
- Last-minute large gifts that trigger fast-notice obligations over a weekend.
- Spending before the agent is appointed and records exist.

## Verify
- [ ] Registration, treasurer/agent and dedicated bank account in place.
- [ ] Every contribution screened for permissibility, aggregated and deposited on time.
- [ ] Running spending totals against any legal limit, including notional and in-kind items.
- [ ] Filing calendar complete; each report reconciled to the bank.
- [ ] Disclaimers/imprints on all materials; records retained for the statutory period.
