# Registration, contribution checks, disclosure reports and audit readiness

**Goal:** every penny in and out of the campaign is lawful, recorded and reported on
time. Campaign-finance law is **intensely jurisdiction-specific** (national, state/
provincial and local rules can all apply to the same campaign) and changes between
elections. This skill is a working method; confirm every number and deadline with the
current guidance of the relevant regulator (e.g. the US FEC or state ethics/elections
boards, the UK Electoral Commission, Elections Canada, national electoral bodies) and,
for anything unclear, an election lawyer.

## 1. Before the first gift
- **Register** the candidate/committee/agent with the regulator by the trigger
  (often on raising or spending a threshold amount, or on declaring candidacy).
- Appoint the **treasurer / election agent** — usually legally responsible, sometimes
  personally liable. They need authority to reject gifts and spending.
- Open a **dedicated bank account**; never mix with personal or business funds.
- Build the **compliance calendar**: reporting periods, filing deadlines,
  pre-election and late-contribution reports, regulated spending periods.
- Pick software that captures required donor fields and produces the regulator's
  report format.

## 2. Contribution checks (every gift, before deposit)
- **Permissible source?** Common prohibitions: foreign nationals, corporations or
  unions directly (in some systems), government contractors, anonymous gifts above a
  small threshold, contributions in another person's name. In the UK, donations above
  the reporting threshold must come from "permissible donors" (e.g. registered electors,
  UK companies carrying on business); in the US, federal candidates can't accept
  corporate treasury money. Check your regime's list.
- **Within limits?** Aggregate per donor per election/period (primary and general may
  be separate). Track aggregates automatically.
- **Required information captured?** Name, address, and often occupation/employer above
  a threshold; US federal committees must show "best efforts" to obtain missing
  information.
- **In-kind** contributions (goods, services, discounted venues, staff time paid by
  someone else) are valued at market rate and count toward limits.
- **Problems:** refund or return excessive or impermissible gifts within the regulator's
  time limit, and document it.

## 3. Spending
- Every payment authorised by the treasurer, with an invoice/receipt and purpose.
- Spending limits (common outside the US; also in some US public-financing schemes) —
  track by category and regulated period, including **notional** spending (in-kind use
  of goods). Joint materials with other candidates may need apportioning.
- **Imprints/disclaimers** ("paid for by…", promoter details) on printed and digital
  material where required.
- Personal use of campaign funds is generally prohibited.

## 4. Disclosure reports
- Reconcile to bank statements before filing; cash-on-hand must tie out.
- Itemise contributions and expenditures above thresholds; report debts and loans.
- File **on time** — late filing alone often attracts automatic penalties.
- Amend promptly when errors are found; explain amendments.
- Late large contributions near the election may need fast-track reporting
  (e.g. 48-hour notices in US federal races).

## 5. Audit readiness
Keep for the statutory period (often several years): deposit records with donor
details, cheque/card copies, invoices, receipts, contracts, payroll, the compliance
check for each gift, and reconciliations. Assume a regulator or opponent will
scrutinise every line.

## Gotchas
- Fundraising platforms' payouts net of fees — record gross contributions and fees.
- Loans from the candidate may have special reporting and repayment limits.
- Coordinated spending with outside groups can turn their spending into your
  contribution.
- Post-election: debt retirement, surplus use and termination filings.

## Verify
- [ ] Registered; treasurer/agent appointed; segregated bank account open.
- [ ] Every contribution checked for source, aggregate limit and donor data before deposit.
- [ ] Impermissible/excess gifts returned within the deadline and documented.
- [ ] Reports reconciled to bank statements and filed on time.
- [ ] Records retained for the statutory period.
