# Writing a campaign plan and win number

**Goal:** a written plan that turns "we want to win" into a number of votes, a list of
who they come from, the money and people needed to reach them, and dated weekly
benchmarks the team can be held to. Models: US candidate campaigns (local to
congressional) and UK constituency/council campaigns. Election law — spending limits,
nomination rules, imprint rules, postal/early voting — differs by jurisdiction; read
the election authority's candidate guidance first.

## 1. Research the race
- Past results for the seat for the last 3-4 comparable elections (same office, same
  cycle type — presidential/general vs midterm/local turnout differ hugely).
- Registered/eligible voters, demographics, geography (precincts/wards/polling districts).
- Your candidate's and opponents' strengths, weaknesses and money.
- Key dates: nomination deadline, registration deadline, start of early/postal voting,
  election day, reporting deadlines.

## 2. Calculate the win number
1. **Projected turnout** = registered voters x expected turnout rate, based on
   comparable past elections adjusted for trends (new registrations, competitive top
   of ticket).
2. **Win number** = projected turnout x 50% + 1 vote in a two-way race; in multi-candidate
   first-past-the-post races, estimate the share needed to beat the strongest rival
   (often well below 50%).
3. **Vote goal** = win number plus a safety margin (commonly 5-10%).
4. Example: 40,000 registered x 35% = 14,000 votes; win number 7,001; vote goal ~7,500.
Run-off, ranked-choice and proportional systems need different maths — model the
specific counting rules.

## 3. Build the vote goal from universes
| Universe | Who | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Base | reliable supporters who will vote | minimal contact, recruit as volunteers/donors |
| GOTV | supporters with lower turnout likelihood | turnout contact, postal/early vote push |
| Persuasion | likely voters who are undecided or soft | message contact, repeat touches |
| Ignore | committed opponents | do not contact |
Sum expected votes: base + (GOTV universe x expected turnout lift) + (persuasion
universe x expected win rate). If the sum is below the vote goal, expand universes or
contact intensity.

## 4. Budget and cash flow
- Fundraising targets by month, set conservatively.
- Typical spend categories: staff, field (materials, data), paid media (digital, mail,
  broadcast), events, compliance, overheads. Proportions depend on race size and media
  market; field-heavy for small districts, media-heavy for large.
- Front-load what builds capacity (data, staff, volunteers); back-load persuasion and GOTV
  media to the voting window — but book scarce media and print slots early.
- Keep within legal spending limits where they apply (UK candidates face statutory limits;
  US federal candidates generally do not, but contribution limits apply — see the
  political-fundraising and campaign-finance-compliance skills).

## 5. Staffing and governance
Candidate, campaign manager, finance lead, field lead, communications lead, treasurer
or agent (legally required in many systems). Define who signs off spending, messages
and public statements. Volunteers need named coordinators.

## 6. Timeline and benchmarks
Work back from election day: GOTV period (final 4 days, or the full early-voting window),
persuasion phase, name recognition phase, launch. Set weekly benchmarks: money raised,
doors knocked, calls made, volunteers active, IDs collected, postal-vote sign-ups. Review
every week and reallocate.

## Gotchas
- Using presidential-year turnout for an off-year race (overstates the win number).
- Treating total contacts as voters persuaded; contact rates are often 20-30% of attempts.
- Running out of cash in the final fortnight when it matters most.
- Forgetting that early/postal votes are banked weeks before election day.

## Verify
- [ ] Turnout projection documented with comparable past elections.
- [ ] Win number and vote goal calculated under the actual counting rules.
- [ ] Universes sized and sum to the vote goal.
- [ ] Monthly budget, cash flow and legal limits checked.
- [ ] Dated timeline and weekly benchmarks owned by named people.
