# Casework, bill management and whip counts

**Goal:** run the core work of a legislator's office — solving constituents' problems
with government, moving the member's priorities through the legislature, preparing the
member for committee, and knowing the votes before they are cast. Models: US Congress
and state legislatures; UK House of Commons MPs' offices. Chamber rules, ethics codes
and staff-funding rules (e.g. separation of official and campaign work) are specific to
each legislature — read the current manual.

## 1. Constituent casework
1. **Intake:** record the constituent's issue, the agency involved, reference numbers,
   what they have already tried and the outcome they want.
2. **Consent:** obtain written privacy consent before contacting an agency on their
   behalf (US federal agencies generally require a signed Privacy Act release; UK
   bodies have their own data-protection expectations).
3. **Route** the enquiry to the agency's legislative/congressional liaison unit, not the
   general line; be factual, attach the consent, and ask for a status update or review —
   offices can expedite and clarify, not override lawful decisions.
4. **Track** in a case management system: dates, contacts, next follow-up (typically every
   2-3 weeks), outcome.
5. **Close** with a letter to the constituent explaining the result; tag cases by agency and
   issue so patterns can feed legislative or oversight work.
Set expectations at intake about what the office can and cannot do (e.g. courts and
independent tribunals are generally off-limits).

## 2. Correspondence
Batch incoming mail by issue; maintain approved form responses per issue reflecting the
member's position; personalise where the constituent shares a personal story. Track
turnaround (many offices target 2-3 weeks).

## 3. Moving a bill
- **Before introduction:** legislative counsel drafts; gather a cost estimate where
  needed; recruit original co-sponsors (ideally across parties) and outside supporters.
- **Committee:** brief the chair and ranking member's staff; request a hearing; prepare
  for markup with a manager's amendment and responses to expected amendments.
- **Floor:** understand the procedure (rules for debate and amendment, unanimous consent,
  suspension, programme motions, private members' bill ballot and sitting days in
  Westminster); line up speakers; prepare a one-page explainer and section-by-section.
- **Other chamber and conference/ping-pong:** identify a counterpart sponsor early.
Keep a bill tracker: status, next step, decision-makers, open issues, allies, opponents.

## 4. Committee hearing preparation
Hearing memo for the member: purpose, witnesses with public bios and positions, key
issues, 5-8 suggested questions in priority order (short; one point each), and the member's
time limit. Include follow-up questions for the record.

## 5. Whip counts
1. List every voting member; mark each **Yes / Lean yes / Undecided / Lean no / No**, with the
   source and date of the information.
2. Count only firm commitments as yes; treat "leans" as uncertain.
3. Identify what undecided members need (amendment, district concern, cover from a
   group) and who is best placed to ask them.
4. Re-count before the vote; absences and pairing change the threshold. Know the
   threshold for this vote (simple majority of those voting, absolute majority,
   supermajority, cloture).
5. If the count is short, delay or change the bill rather than lose publicly.

## Gotchas
- Using official staff time or resources for campaign work — prohibited under most ethics rules.
- Promising constituents an outcome the office cannot deliver.
- Treating "I'll look at it" as a yes in a whip count.
- Losing institutional memory: document every case and bill file.

## Verify
- [ ] Casework intake, signed consent and follow-up dates recorded for every case.
- [ ] Correspondence turnaround tracked; form letters match current positions.
- [ ] Bill tracker current with next procedural step and decision-makers.
- [ ] Hearing memos delivered at least a day before the hearing.
- [ ] Whip count sourced, dated, re-checked against the correct vote threshold.
