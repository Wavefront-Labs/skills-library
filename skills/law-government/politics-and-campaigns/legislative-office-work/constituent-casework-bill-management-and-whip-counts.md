# Constituent casework, bill management and whip counts

**Goal:** serve constituents effectively and turn the member's priorities into
legislation, using the office's limited time and political capital well. Procedures and
terminology **vary by legislature** (Westminster parliaments, US Congress and state
legislatures, other assemblies); learn your chamber's standing orders/rules.

## 1. Constituent casework
- **Intake:** confirm the person is a constituent (most offices only take their own),
  record the issue, desired outcome, agency involved, deadlines, and documents.
- **Consent:** get a signed **privacy release/authority** before contacting an agency
  on someone's behalf — agencies require it (e.g. under privacy laws).
- Contact the agency's dedicated legislative/MP liaison channel, not the public line.
  Ask for status, the decision basis, and whether anything is missing; request
  reconsideration where justified. The office can expedite and clarify; it usually
  **can't overturn** a lawful decision or interfere in court cases.
- **Track** every case in the office's CRM with status, next action date, and outcome.
  Close with a letter to the constituent.
- **Spot patterns:** many cases on one agency or rule signal a policy problem — feed
  them to the legislative team.
- Boundaries: no special favours, no involvement in active litigation or procurement
  decisions, and follow ethics rules on donors (casework must not depend on donations).

## 2. Correspondence
Templates per issue (cleared positions), personalised opening, fast turnaround targets,
and tallies of mail volume by issue for the member.

## 3. Moving a bill
1. **Policy and drafting:** work with legislative counsel to draft (see policy-making-
   and-analysis.legislative-drafting). Cost it if needed.
2. **Coalition:** cosponsors (ideally cross-party), stakeholder support letters,
   endorsements; answer opponents' objections early.
3. **Committee:** the committee chair controls the agenda in most systems — get the bill
   scheduled. Prepare witness lists, testimony, and amendments to address concerns.
4. **Floor/later stages:** track timetabling, amendments filed by others, procedural
   hurdles (closure/cloture, report stage), and the other chamber.
5. Keep a one-page **bill status sheet**: stage, next event, supporters, opponents,
   open issues.
For private members'/backbench bills, procedural time is scarce — consider attaching
the policy as an amendment to a government bill or pursuing non-legislative routes.

## 4. Committee and hearing preparation
Briefing memo for the member: purpose of the hearing, witnesses and their positions,
key facts, **5–7 prioritised questions** with likely answers and follow-ups, the
member's goal (a commitment, a fact on the record, a clip). Time limits are short; the
best questions are one line.

## 5. Vote counting (whip counts)
- List every member; categorise: **yes, lean yes, undecided, lean no, no**, absent.
- Sources: public statements, prior votes on similar issues, party position, direct
  conversations — record the date and source of each assessment.
- Work the **undecided and leaners**: what do they need (a change in the text, a
  district concern addressed, cover from a stakeholder)?
- Count conservatively — a "probably" isn't a yes. Know the threshold (simple majority
  of those voting, absolute majority, supermajority) and attendance risks.
- Update the count daily near the vote; don't call the vote until you have the numbers
  (if you control timing).

## Gotchas
- Casework promises the office can't keep damage trust; be clear about limits.
- Staff political activity on official time or resources is restricted in many systems.
- Leaking whip counts can lose votes.

## Verify
- [ ] Every case has a signed release, tracker entry and closing letter.
- [ ] Casework patterns reported to the legislative team monthly.
- [ ] Bill status sheet current; committee scheduling secured or alternative route chosen.
- [ ] Hearing brief has prioritised one-line questions and a stated goal.
- [ ] Whip count sourced and dated for every member; threshold confirmed.
