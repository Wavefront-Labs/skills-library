# Bill tracking, member targeting and amendment strategy

**Goal:** change legislative outcomes for a client — pass, amend or stop a bill — by
being in the right room, at the right stage, with an ask that decision-makers can say
yes to. Procedure differs greatly (US Congress and states, Westminster parliaments, EU
Parliament/Council). Before any lobbying, check registration and gift rules — see
law-government.lobbying-government-affairs.lobbying-disclosure-compliance.

## 1. Know the procedure cold
- Map the bill's path: introduction → committee referral → hearings → committee
  amendments (markup / committee stage / report stage) → floor → second chamber →
  reconciliation (conference, ping-pong) → executive signature or veto.
- Identify **choke points**: committee chair's control of the agenda, rules committees
  or business managers, budget/appropriations scoring, deadlines (session end, cross-over
  dates in US states).
- Most amendments succeed early (drafting and committee stage); late-stage changes are
  far harder.

## 2. Bill tracking
For each bill on the client's list, maintain:
| Bill | Stage/next event | Sponsor(s) | Committee | Client position | Ask | Risk | Owner |
Use the legislature's official site and alerts plus a commercial tracking service; read
the **actual text** of each version and amendment, not summaries. Track companion bills
and riders in must-pass legislation.

## 3. Mapping and targeting
- **Decision map**: sponsor, chair and ranking member, committee members, leadership,
  swing members, relevant executive officials. Note party, district/constituency interests
  (employers, constituents affected), past votes and statements, and who influences them.
- Build a **vote count**: yes / lean yes / undecided / lean no / no. Spend most effort on
  the undecided and leaners on the committee of jurisdiction.
- **Staff** do much of the substantive work: committee counsel and policy advisers draft
  text and brief members. Build credibility with them first.
- Constituent connection matters: a local employer or constituent in the room is often
  more persuasive than a professional advocate (see
  law-government.lobbying-government-affairs.grassroots-advocacy-campaigns).

## 4. The meeting and the ask
- Prepare a **one-pager**: issue, client position, the specific ask (vote, co-sponsor,
  amendment, letter), local impact data, contact. Leave it behind.
- Be accurate and candid — reputation for reliable information is a lobbyist's main asset.
  Acknowledge the other side's arguments.
- Ask for something specific and achievable; follow up promptly with what you promised.

## 5. Amendment strategy
- Identify the narrowest change that solves the client's problem: definitions,
  exemptions, effective dates, thresholds, safe harbours, delegation to the regulator.
- Draft actual text in the legislature's style (amend at page/line or clause level) — a
  ready-made, technically sound amendment is far more likely to be adopted; the
  legislature's drafting office will often refine it.
- Find a **sponsor** for the amendment, ideally a committee member; anticipate budget
  impact scoring and opposition.
- Fallbacks: report language or committee statements on intent, guidance commitments from
  ministers, delayed implementation.

## 6. Hearing testimony
Coordinate witnesses: credible, ideally constituents or technical experts; written
testimony within page limits and deadlines; oral statement short with one data point and
one story; prep for hostile questions.

## Gotchas
- Lobbying on the wrong version: texts change between stages.
- Surprising a friendly member with opposition from your coalition partner — tell allies first.
- Gifts, meals and travel: tightly regulated; ask compliance before any hospitality.

## Verify
- [ ] Every tracked bill has a current stage, next event, position and owner.
- [ ] Decision map and vote count up to date for the committee of jurisdiction.
- [ ] Each meeting has a specific ask and leave-behind; follow-ups logged.
- [ ] Amendments exist as drafted text with a sponsor.
- [ ] Lobbying activity logged for disclosure reporting.
