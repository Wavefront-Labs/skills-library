# Registration, reporting, gift and revolving-door rules

**Goal:** an organisation that lobbies at full intensity with zero compliance surprises.
Lobbying law is fragmented: national, state/provincial and city regimes often apply
simultaneously, with different definitions and thresholds. The US federal outline below
illustrates the components; thresholds are adjusted periodically and every regime differs.
Verify against current law and official guidance for each jurisdiction where you act.

## 1. Who must register?
Map every jurisdiction and level of government you contact, then for each regime apply
its definitions of **lobbying contact**, **lobbying activities**, **lobbyist** and
**covered official**.
- US federal (LDA): an individual is a lobbyist if they make more than one lobbying
  contact and spend at least 20% of their time for that client/employer on lobbying
  activities in a quarterly period; registration is triggered when spending/income
  thresholds are met, generally within 45 days of the first contact or engagement.
  In-house organisations and outside firms register differently.
- **Foreign principals**: agents of foreign governments/political parties/certain foreign
  entities may need to register under FARA (US) or similar foreign-influence schemes —
  stricter than ordinary lobbying law, with criminal penalties. Screen every foreign client.
- UK: the statutory register covers only **consultant lobbyists** communicating with
  ministers and permanent secretaries; voluntary and other codes cover more.
- EU: the Transparency Register is effectively required for meetings with many
  Commission, Parliament and Council officials.
- State and local regimes may cover **procurement lobbying** and have far lower thresholds.

## 2. Track and report
- Build tracking into daily work: contact logs (who, which official, issue, bill/rule
  numbers), time sheets showing lobbying vs non-lobbying time, and expense coding.
- Report on time: US LDA quarterly activity reports (LD-2: issues, specific bills,
  agencies contacted, lobbyists, income/expenses) and semi-annual contribution reports
  (LD-203: political contributions and certain event/honoring payments, with gift-rule
  certification). Other regimes: monthly, quarterly or per-meeting reports.
- Reconcile reports with finance records and with what other filers (outside firms,
  coalition partners) report for the same client.
- Keep records for the retention period; audits do occur (the US GAO audits LDA filings
  annually; some states audit randomly).

## 3. Gifts, hospitality and contributions
- Legislative and executive gift rules restrict what officials can accept; registered
  lobbyists often face stricter limits (US House and Senate rules generally bar gifts from
  registered lobbyists and their clients, with narrow exceptions; executive branch rules
  separate).
- Pre-clear every meal, ticket, travel, event invitation, and charitable donation linked
  to an official. "Widely attended event" and similar exceptions are technical.
- **Pay-to-play**: political contributions may disqualify the company from contracts
  (e.g. municipal securities, state contracting rules). Pre-clear contributions,
  including by executives and their spouses where covered.
- **Contingency fees** for lobbying are banned in many jurisdictions.

## 4. Revolving door
- Hiring former officials: check post-employment restrictions on the individual (US
  18 U.S.C. §207 — lifetime bars on particular matters they worked on, one- or two-year
  cooling-off periods for senior officials; agency-specific and ethics-pledge rules may
  add more). Obtain an ethics opinion from their former agency where possible.
- Screen and wall off as needed; document assignments.
- Employees leaving for government: recusal obligations on their side; no promises tied
  to official acts.

## 5. Compliance programme
Written policy; training on hire and annually; designated compliance owner; pre-clearance
workflow; quarterly certifications by lobbyists; outside-firm engagement letters requiring
compliance and information sharing; incident escalation and voluntary correction of
errors.

## Gotchas
- "Grassroots" and coalition spending may be reportable in some states.
- Executives casually contacting officials count as lobbying contacts.
- Late filing penalties accrue daily in some states.

- [ ] Foreign-principal screening completed for every client.
- [ ] Contact logs, time and expense data reconcile with filed reports.
- [ ] Every gift, event and contribution pre-cleared and logged.
- [ ] Former officials screened against post-employment rules with written advice.
