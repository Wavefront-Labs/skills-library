# Building and running advocacy coalitions

**Goal:** multiply influence by speaking with many voices — trade associations, ad hoc
coalitions, unexpected allies — without losing control of the message, breaching
competition law, or getting tied to positions the organisation can't support. Coalition
practice is broadly similar across democracies; antitrust and disclosure obligations
vary by jurisdiction.

## 1. Lead, join, or stay out?
| Option | Best when | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| Work through existing **trade association** | Industry-wide issue, broad agreement | Slow, lowest-common-denominator positions |
| Form an **ad hoc coalition** | Specific issue, members across sectors | Control and focus, but you fund and run it |
| Join someone else's coalition | Aligned goal, others leading | Less control over message |
| Go alone | Company-specific issue or divergent interests | No cover, but clear position |
Decide by issue. Officials value breadth: a coalition of manufacturers, unions, consumer
groups and local governments is more persuasive than ten companies from one sector.

## 2. Forming an ad hoc coalition
- **Purpose statement**: one issue, a defined objective, a time horizon. Scope creep kills
  coalitions.
- **Membership**: core funders + broader supporters; recruit **unlikely allies** whose
  presence changes the narrative (e.g. environmental group backing an energy project).
- **Governance** (written, even if short): steering committee, decision rule for positions
  and public statements (consensus vs majority; who can veto), spokesperson authority,
  how members can dissent or exit, dues/cost-sharing formula, who hires consultants.
- **Name and branding**: coalition names should not mislead; disclose funders where law or
  good practice requires. Concealing industry funding behind a citizen-sounding front
  ("astroturfing") is reputationally dangerous and in some places legally reportable.

## 3. Positions and message
- Draft a **joint position paper** with the core ask and supporting evidence; agree
  red lines and fallback positions privately.
- Use **sign-on letters** for breadth: many organisations, a short clear ask.
- Message discipline: shared talking points; members report meetings back; no member
  undercuts the coalition in private meetings with officials (agree what happens if
  someone wants a side deal).
- Assign members to targets based on relationships and geography.

## 4. Grasstops and validators
- **Grasstops**: influential individuals with personal relationships to decision-makers
  (local business leaders, former officials, community figures). Map relationships across
  the membership and deploy them deliberately.
- **Third-party validators**: academics, think tanks, retired regulators whose independent
  analysis supports the position — must be genuinely independent and disclosed if paid.

## 5. Antitrust guardrails
Competitors meeting together carries competition-law risk.
- Have antitrust counsel review the coalition's scope; adopt written **antitrust
  guidelines**; circulate agendas in advance; keep minutes; counsel attends sensitive
  meetings.
- Never discuss prices, costs, wages, capacity, bids, customers, markets or
  commercial strategy. Collect any needed data through a third party, aggregated and
  historical.
- Petitioning government is generally protected (in the US, the *Noerr-Pennington*
  doctrine), but discussions around it are not automatically protected.

## 6. Running and closing
- Regular cadence of calls; shared tracker of meetings and outcomes; clear budget reports.
- Reassess after major milestones: continue, re-scope, or wind down. Close formally:
  final report, settle accounts, archive records.
- Check whether coalition spending must be reported under lobbying laws (see
  law-government.lobbying-government-affairs.lobbying-disclosure-compliance).

## Gotchas
- Trade association positions diverging from members' public commitments (e.g. on
  climate) — monitor and address through governance or exit.
- One dominant funder making the coalition look like a single company's front.
- Unminuted competitor side conversations.

## Verify

- [ ] Written governance: decisions, spokesperson, funding, exit.
- [ ] Joint position and talking points agreed; meeting reports shared.
- [ ] Antitrust guidelines adopted, agendas and minutes kept.
- [ ] Funding transparency and lobbying-disclosure obligations checked.
