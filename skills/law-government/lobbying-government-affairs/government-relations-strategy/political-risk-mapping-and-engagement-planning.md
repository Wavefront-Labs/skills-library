# Political risk mapping and engagement planning

**Goal:** turn government relations from reacting to whatever arrived this week into a
deliberate programme focused on the few issues that matter most to the organisation,
with clear asks, relationships built before they're needed, and measurable results.
The method is jurisdiction-neutral; legal limits on lobbying, political spending and
contact with officials are not — check them for each country and level of government.

## 1. Issue scan
Gather every policy issue that could affect the organisation over 1–3 years:
legislation, regulation, procurement, tax, trade, licences/permits, enforcement trends,
elections and leadership changes, public sentiment. Sources: business units (ask what
keeps them up at night), monitoring services, trade associations, party platforms, regulator
agendas, court cases. See
law-government.lobbying-government-affairs.political-intelligence-and-monitoring.

## 2. Prioritise
Score each issue on:
- **Impact** on the organisation (revenue, cost, licence to operate, reputation) — quantify
  where possible with business-unit input;
- **Likelihood and timing** of government action;
- **Influenceability**: can the organisation realistically change the outcome?
Plot on a matrix; pick 3–5 **priority issues** for active campaigns, a second tier for
monitoring and association-led work, and drop the rest. Revisit quarterly and after elections.

## 3. Positions and asks
For each priority issue, write a one-page position:
- the problem framed in terms of the public interest (jobs, safety, consumer benefit,
  competition), not just the company's interest;
- the specific policy ask (text change, regulatory approach, funding, timing);
- evidence and proof points; likely opponents and their arguments; acceptable compromises
  (the "landing zone").
Clear internal sign-off (legal, business, communications, leadership) so the organisation
speaks consistently.

## 4. Stakeholder mapping
- List decision-makers and influencers: ministers/members, staff, officials, regulators,
  local governments, opposition parties, think tanks, unions, NGOs, media, other companies.
- Plot **power vs position** (support–oppose) and **power vs interest**; note relationships
  between them.
- Identify gaps: important stakeholders with no relationship. Build relationships ahead
  of need — site visits, briefings, constituency events, technical expertise offered
  without an ask.
- Engage across parties; today's opposition is tomorrow's government.

## 5. Engagement plan
For each priority issue: objective, key decision points on the political calendar,
targets, messengers (executives, employees, customers, coalition partners), channels
(direct lobbying, regulatory submissions, coalitions, grassroots, media), sequencing,
budget, owner, and compliance checks. Keep it in a shared system (stakeholder CRM)
logging every contact — this also feeds disclosure reporting.

## 6. Governance and reporting
- Board/executive oversight of political activity: approved policy on lobbying, political
  contributions (where lawful), trade-association memberships and alignment of their
  positions with the company's stated positions.
- **KPIs**: outcome measures (policy changes achieved, risks avoided with estimated value),
  plus process measures (relationships established, submissions made). Be honest about
  attribution — many factors drive outcomes.
- Quarterly reporting to leadership; post-mortems on wins and losses.

## Gotchas
- Trying to work 30 issues at once; credibility and resources spread thin.
- Business units contacting officials independently with inconsistent messages.
- Trade associations taking positions that conflict with the company's public commitments
  — a growing source of reputational risk.
- Ignoring sub-national government, where many permits and rules are decided.

## Verify
- [ ] Issue scan includes input from every business unit.
- [ ] 3–5 priority issues chosen by impact, likelihood and influenceability.
- [ ] Each priority issue has a signed-off position with a specific ask and landing zone.
- [ ] Stakeholder map covers all parties and levels of government with relationship owners.
- [ ] Engagement plan, contact log and KPIs reported to leadership quarterly.
