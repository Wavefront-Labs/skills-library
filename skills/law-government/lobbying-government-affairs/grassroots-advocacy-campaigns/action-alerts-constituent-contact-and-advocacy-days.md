# Action alerts, constituent contact and advocacy days

**Goal:** make officials hear from real people they represent, at the moment it matters,
in a form that persuades — personal, local, specific. Constituent pressure works because
elected officials and staff weigh it differently from professional lobbying. Software
tools (e.g. Quorum, FiscalNote's advocacy products, and others) handle matching and
delivery; features change, so evaluate current capabilities. Some jurisdictions regulate
or require disclosure of paid grassroots lobbying — check before launching.

## 1. Build the advocate base before you need it
- Sources: employees, customers, members, suppliers, retirees, community partners — with
  clear **opt-in** consent for advocacy communications (data protection and anti-spam
  laws apply; employee participation must be voluntary).
- Capture **verified address** to match each advocate to their legislative districts;
  without district matching, contacts lose most of their value.
- Segment by district, issue interest, role (employee/customer), and past activity.
- Keep the list warm: occasional updates and wins, not only asks.

## 2. Action alerts that work
- **Timing**: tie to a decision point — committee vote, floor vote, comment deadline,
  district recess. Alerts without urgency underperform.
- **Targeting**: only advocates whose officials matter for this vote (committee members,
  swing votes). Mass alerts to all districts dilute impact and fatigue the list.
- **Ask**: one action, one specific request ("vote no on HB 123 Section 4"), with the why
  in two sentences.
- **Personalisation**: templates help people start, but identical form emails are
  discounted by offices (many filter them as a single campaign). Prompt advocates to add
  one sentence about how the issue affects them, their job or their town.
- Channels by impact (rough order): in-person meetings in the district > personalised
  calls > personalised emails/letters > social media mentions > form emails. Patch-through
  calls connect advocates directly to offices.
- Measure: open/click rates, completed actions per district, and — more importantly —
  staff feedback and vote outcomes.

## 3. Story bank
- Collect constituent stories with consent: who they are, where they live, the concrete
  effect of the policy, a photo or short video if they agree.
- Tag by district, issue and theme; verify facts; get release forms for public use.
- Use stories in meetings, testimony, leave-behinds and media. A local story with a
  number ("we'd cut 40 jobs in [town]") moves staff more than national statistics.

## 4. Advocacy days (fly-ins / lobby days)
1. Pick dates when legislators are in session and the issue is live; book meetings
   4–6 weeks ahead, by district.
2. Group advocates by district so each meeting has constituents; assign a team lead
   per group.
3. Training session: the ask, the facts, the one-pager, how to tell their story in
   90 seconds, how to handle questions they can't answer ("I'll get back to you"),
   etiquette with staff (staff meetings are normal and valuable).
4. Meeting reports: each group records who they met, the response, commitments and
   follow-ups — collected the same day.
5. Follow-up: thank-you notes from each advocate referencing their story; send promised
   information; invite officials for a site visit back home.
Virtual advocacy days are cheaper and broaden participation; in-person still has more
impact in many offices.

## 5. District engagement
Town halls, site visits to facilities, letters to the editor in local papers, meetings
in district offices during recess — officials are often more accessible at home.

## Gotchas
- Fake or inflated constituent contacts destroy credibility and can break the law.
- Pressuring employees to participate — coercion risks and legal limits in some places.
- Over-mailing the list until engagement collapses.

## Verify
- [ ] Advocates opted in and matched to verified districts.
- [ ] Each alert is tied to a decision point and targeted to relevant districts only.
- [ ] Alerts prompt personalisation; results tracked by district.
- [ ] Story bank entries have consent and verified facts.
- [ ] Advocacy day meetings grouped by district with same-day reports and follow-up.
