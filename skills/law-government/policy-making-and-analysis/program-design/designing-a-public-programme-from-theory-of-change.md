# Designing a public programme from a theory of change

**Goal:** turn an agreed policy aim into a programme (a grant scheme, benefit, service
or intervention) whose logic is explicit, whose eligibility rules hit the intended
group, and which can actually be delivered on time. Jurisdiction-neutral; the UK Five
Case Model and US federal grant practice are reference points. Procurement, grant and
benefit law differ by jurisdiction — involve legal and finance early.

## 1. Write the theory of change
Work backwards from the long-term outcome:
1. **Impact** — the change in society (e.g. fewer young people not in education,
   employment or training).
2. **Outcomes** — the changes in participants that cause it (skills, job offers).
3. **Outputs** — what the programme produces (training places filled, mentoring hours).
4. **Activities** and **inputs** — what is done and with what money, staff and partners.
Write the **assumptions** between each link ("employers will hire completers") and the
evidence for each. Weak links are where the programme will fail; they are also what
the evaluation must test.

## 2. Turn it into a logic model table
| Inputs | Activities | Outputs | Short-term outcomes | Long-term outcomes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| budget, staff, partners | recruit, train, place | numbers served | measurable change at 6-12 months | change at 2-5 years |
Add an indicator and a data source for each output and outcome now (this is the
foundation for the policy-evaluation skill,
law-government.policy-making-and-analysis.policy-evaluation.building-an-evaluation-framework).

## 3. Choose the delivery model
| Model | Good when | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Direct delivery by government | control and consistency matter; statutory function | capacity, speed of hiring |
| Grants to local government / NGOs | local knowledge, flexibility | variable quality, reporting burden |
| Contracted provider (incl. payment-by-results) | clear measurable outputs | gaming, cream-skimming, contract management cost |
| Vouchers / cash transfer | users can choose; market exists | supply constraints, fraud |
| Regulation or mandate on others | low public cost | compliance and enforcement |
Map the **delivery chain** from the funding department to the end user and name the
accountable person at each link.

## 4. Design eligibility and targeting
- Define the target population and estimate its size from data, not guesses.
- Choose criteria that are **verifiable** with data the administrator can access
  (age, income band, residence, existing benefit receipt). Every extra criterion adds
  admin cost and reduces take-up.
- Decide between universal, categorical, means-tested and discretionary eligibility,
  and model inclusion errors (wrong people in) against exclusion errors (right people out).
- Plan for **take-up**: automatic enrolment or data-matching beats application forms;
  means-tested schemes often have far from full take-up.
- Design cliff edges out (tapers instead of hard income cut-offs where possible).

## 5. Plan implementation
- Phases: design, build (systems, guidance, staff), pilot, scale.
- A realistic timeline with critical path; IT and recruitment are usually the longest.
- Governance: senior responsible owner, programme board, RACI chart for decisions.
- **Risk register**: risk, likelihood, impact, owner, mitigation — top risks typically
  being delivery capacity, fraud and error, demand above forecast, and legal challenge.
- Budget profile by year with contingency (commonly 10-20% for novel programmes; follow
  your finance rules).

## 6. Pilot before scaling
Pilot in a few sites with a clear question (does the assumption hold? is the process
workable?) and a decision point: scale, adapt or stop. Do user research with real
applicants before writing the forms.

## Gotchas
- Logic models with arrows but no assumptions — they look fine and test nothing.
- Eligibility that depends on data no-one holds, forcing paper evidence and depressing take-up.
- Funding announced before delivery partners have capacity.
- No exit or continuation plan when time-limited funding ends.

## Verify
- [ ] Theory of change with explicit, evidenced assumptions at each link.
- [ ] Indicator and data source for every output and outcome.
- [ ] Delivery model chosen with reasons; delivery chain mapped with owners.
- [ ] Eligibility criteria verifiable; inclusion/exclusion errors and take-up estimated.
- [ ] Implementation timeline, governance, risk register and pilot decision point agreed.
