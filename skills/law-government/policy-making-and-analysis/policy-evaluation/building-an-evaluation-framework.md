# Building an evaluation framework

**Goal:** decide, before a policy launches, how you will know whether it was delivered
as intended, whether it worked, and whether it was worth the money — and secure the data
and design to answer those questions credibly. Models: the UK Magenta Book and US federal
practice under the Evidence Act (agency learning agendas and evaluation plans).
Statutory review duties (e.g. post-implementation review clauses) vary by jurisdiction
and by Act.

## 1. Start from the theory of change and the decision
Take the programme's logic model (see
law-government.policy-making-and-analysis.program-design.designing-a-public-programme-from-theory-of-change)
and ask: which decisions will the evaluation inform (continue, scale, redesign, stop),
and when? Evaluation results that arrive after the spending review are wasted.

## 2. Write the evaluation questions
| Strand | Questions |
|---|---|
| **Process** | Was it delivered as designed? Who was reached? What barriers did staff and users meet? |
| **Impact** | What difference did it make to outcomes compared with what would have happened anyway? For whom? |
| **Economic** | Did benefits exceed costs? Cost per outcome compared with alternatives? |
Limit to 5-8 priority questions; long lists dilute budgets.

## 3. Choose the impact design (the counterfactual)
| Design | Use when | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Randomised controlled trial | you can randomise access (lottery, phased rollout, oversubscription) | highest internal validity |
| Regression discontinuity | eligibility uses a sharp cut-off score | strong near the cut-off |
| Difference-in-differences | comparison areas/groups with parallel pre-trends | moderate-strong |
| Matching / synthetic control | rich data on participants and non-participants | moderate; depends on observables |
| Theory-based (contribution analysis, process tracing, realist) | no comparison group possible; complex systems | explains how/why; weaker on size of effect |
| Before-and-after only | last resort | weak — cannot separate trends from impact |
Decide this **before** rollout: phased or randomised rollout is cheap to build in and
impossible to add afterwards. Ethical review is needed where randomising access to
services.

## 4. Define indicators and data
- For each outcome, one or two indicators that are specific, measurable, attributable
  and timed (e.g. "% of participants in employment 12 months after starting").
- Record the **baseline** value and data source; agree data-sharing with administrative
  data holders early (often the longest lead time — months).
- Power calculation for impact designs: is the sample large enough to detect the
  expected effect size? If not, change the design or scope of the claim.
- Collect cost data by activity from day one for the economic strand.

## 5. Plan the pilot evaluation
Pilots should test feasibility and the riskiest assumptions, with pre-set **progression
criteria** (e.g. "at least 60% of referred users enrol"; "no increase in error rate").
Do not over-claim impact from small pilots.

## 6. Post-implementation review
Typically 3-5 years after commencement (or as the legislation requires): did the measure
achieve its objectives, were costs as predicted, are there unintended effects, is it
still needed? Compare with the original impact assessment.

## 7. Governance and publication
Name an evaluation lead independent of delivery, a steering group, a budget (often cited
as roughly 1-5% of programme spend — a rule of thumb, not a requirement), a timeline of
interim and final reports, and a commitment to publish findings, including null results.

## Gotchas
- Choosing outcome measures after seeing the data (pre-register where possible).
- Attributing national trends to the programme in a before-after comparison.
- Selection bias: volunteers differ from non-volunteers.
- Measuring outputs (people trained) and reporting them as outcomes (people employed).

## Verify
- [ ] Evaluation questions tied to named decisions and dates.
- [ ] Process, impact and economic strands planned.
- [ ] Counterfactual design chosen and built into rollout; power checked.
- [ ] Indicators with baselines, data sources and data-sharing agreements.
- [ ] Pilot progression criteria and post-implementation review date set; publication committed.
