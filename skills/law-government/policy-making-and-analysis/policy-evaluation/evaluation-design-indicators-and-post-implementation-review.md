# Evaluation design, indicators and post-implementation review

**Goal:** credible evidence on whether a policy was delivered as intended, whether it
caused the outcomes, for whom, at what cost — built in from the start, not bolted on.

Reference guides: UK HM Treasury **Magenta Book**, OECD-DAC evaluation criteria
(relevance, coherence, effectiveness, efficiency, impact, sustainability), US federal
evidence-act guidance. Requirements to evaluate **vary by jurisdiction** and funder.

## 1. Evaluation questions (decide early)
From the theory of change (see program-design), ask:
- **Process:** Was it delivered as designed? Who was reached? What got in the way?
- **Impact:** What difference did it make compared with what would have happened
  otherwise? For whom, and under what conditions?
- **Value for money:** Were the outcomes worth the cost? Compared with alternatives?
Prioritise 3–6 questions that decision-makers actually need answered, and by when.

## 2. Indicators and data
- For each outcome: an indicator, definition, data source, baseline, frequency,
  owner. Make them SMART; prefer administrative data you already collect (linked
  records are cheaper and more complete than surveys).
- **Collect the baseline before launch** — the most common irretrievable mistake.
- Data-sharing agreements and privacy/ethics approval take months; start them early.

## 3. Choosing an impact design
The question is the **counterfactual**. Options, strongest internal validity first:
- **Randomised controlled trial (RCT):** random assignment to receive the programme (or
  receive it earlier). Feasible more often than people think — oversubscribed
  programmes, phased rollouts, encouragement designs.
- **Quasi-experimental:**
  - *Difference-in-differences* — compare change over time between participating and
    similar non-participating areas/groups; needs parallel pre-trends.
  - *Regression discontinuity* — eligibility cut-offs (age, score, income) compare
    people just above and below.
  - *Matching* (e.g. propensity scores) — comparable non-participants on observed
    characteristics; vulnerable to unobserved differences.
  - *Interrupted time series* — sustained trend change at implementation.
- **Theory-based** (contribution analysis, realist evaluation, process tracing) where a
  comparison group is impossible (national, complex, systemic interventions): test
  each link in the causal chain and rule out rival explanations.
Size the study: a **power calculation** tells you whether the sample can detect a
plausible effect; an underpowered evaluation will likely report "no significant
effect" whatever the truth.

## 4. Process and economic strands
- Process: interviews with staff and users, observation, management data on reach and
  fidelity, take-up analysis by group.
- Economic: cost per output/outcome, cost-effectiveness vs alternatives, or a full cost-
  benefit analysis using the impact estimate.
- **Mixed methods** explain *why* the impact numbers look as they do.

## 5. Pilots
Agree before the pilot what results would justify scale-up, modify or stop — otherwise
pilots become launches. Be wary of pilot-site enthusiasm effects not replicating at scale.

## 6. Post-implementation review (PIR)
Often required for regulations (e.g. review clauses, statutory reviews). Cover:
objectives, whether they were achieved, actual vs estimated costs and benefits,
unintended effects, whether the regulation is still needed, options to amend/repeal.
Proportion effort to the policy's scale.

## Reporting
Lead with findings and confidence; describe methods and limitations honestly; publish
(including null or negative results); give a management response with actions.

## Gotchas
- Evaluating outputs (people trained) and calling it impact.
- Changing the programme mid-evaluation without recording it.
- Commissioning after launch: baseline lost, randomisation impossible.

## Verify
- [ ] Evaluation questions agreed before launch, tied to decisions.
- [ ] Indicators have baselines, sources and owners; data-sharing in place.
- [ ] Impact design justified, with power calculation where quantitative.
- [ ] Process and VfM strands planned.
- [ ] Scale-up/stop criteria for pilots agreed in advance; results published.
