# Ministerial briefings, research reports and parliamentary question answers

**Goal:** give a busy decision-maker exactly what they need, accurately, in the time
they have — often five minutes in a car between meetings.

## 1. Universal rules
- **Bottom line up front (BLUF)** / pyramid principle: conclusion or ask first, then
  supporting points, then detail.
- Know the **reader**: their position, prior statements, political sensitivities, what
  they already know. Check what they said last time — consistency matters.
- Short sentences, plain words, numbers with sources and dates, no unexplained acronyms.
- Every fact **checked against a source** you could show; a wrong number in a
  minister's mouth becomes a correction on the public record.
- Clearance: route through the required sign-offs (policy lead, legal, analysts,
  press office, private office) — build the time in.

## 2. Types of briefs
**Decision note / submission:** issue, recommendation, timing, background, options,
considerations (legal, financial, handling, equality), annexes. (See policy-analysis
for options appraisal.)

**Meeting brief:** who you're meeting (role, organisation, interests, history with
the department), purpose and objectives for the meeting ("what we want out of it"),
likely asks from them and suggested responses, **lines to take**, background, a short
bio. One to two pages plus annexes.

**Lines to take / Q&A:** short, quotable statements of the position; then anticipated
hostile questions with crisp answers; "if pressed" lines; things **not** to say
(unannounced policy, commercial-in-confidence figures).

**Event/visit brief:** logistics, attendees, running order, speech/remarks, media,
and sensitivities (local disputes, ongoing litigation).

## 3. Answering parliamentary questions (PQs)
Conventions **vary by legislature** (e.g. Westminster written and oral questions,
Question Period in Canada, question time in Australia; congressional hearings in the US).
Common practice:
- **Answer the question asked** — directly, accurately, completely. Ministerial codes
  in many Westminster systems require accuracy and correcting inadvertent errors promptly.
- Written answers: a direct first sentence, then context; give the data if held; if
  not held or disproportionate cost to obtain, say so in the standard form your
  legislature accepts.
- Check consistency with previous answers on the topic (search the record).
- Oral questions: a short topline answer plus a **supplementaries pack** — the
  likely follow-ups from that member and others, with figures, recent announcements
  and attack/defence lines.
- Meet the deadline; late answers are tracked and published in some systems.

## 4. Research reports (think-tank / policy unit)
- Structure: summary with key findings and recommendations (2 pages max), introduction
  and question, methods, findings, discussion, recommendations (specific, addressed
  to someone, costed if possible), annex.
- Be transparent about methods, funding and limitations; independence is your capital.
- **Landscape scan:** who's doing what (government, opposition, think-tanks,
  academics, other countries), the current evidence, live debates, upcoming decision
  points. Table form works.
- Launch plan: target audiences, briefing events, op-eds, social summaries.

## 5. Formats that save the reader time
Headline numbers in a box; one chart that tells the story; options in a table; a
"what we need from you" line; page numbers and annex references.

## Gotchas
- Burying the ask on page 3.
- Political content in civil-service briefs: civil servants in most systems must stay
  impartial — party-political material comes from political advisers.
- Briefs leak or get released under freedom-of-information laws; write accordingly.

## Verify
- [ ] Ask or conclusion in the first two lines.
- [ ] Every number sourced and dated; consistency with past statements checked.
- [ ] Meeting brief covers objectives, their likely asks, and lines to take.
- [ ] PQ answer answers the actual question and matches the record.
- [ ] Clearances obtained before deadline.
