# Writing a ministerial or executive briefing

**Goal:** give a busy decision-maker exactly what they need for a meeting, a decision, a
public appearance or a question in the legislature — accurate, sourced, short and in
the format their office expects. Models: Westminster-style government departments
(ministerial submissions, parliamentary questions) and US legislative and executive
practice (briefing memos, congressional inquiries). Formats, deadlines and conventions
are set by each private office or committee — always use the current template.

## 1. Clarify the commission (five minutes, saves hours)
Ask or infer: Who is the reader? What is the **use** (decide, meet, speak, answer a
question, understand)? What is the deadline and the page limit? What has the reader
already seen? Is it for publication (PQ answers, research papers) or internal only?

## 2. Rapid research scan
1. Your own organisation's past briefings, published positions and previous answers —
   consistency matters, and contradicting a previous ministerial statement is a
   classic error.
2. Official statistics and the latest data release (note release dates; embargoes apply).
3. Legislation and current policy documents.
4. Independent evidence: government research units, audit offices, legislative
   research services, academic reviews, reputable think tanks (note their stance).
5. Stakeholder positions and recent media coverage.
Keep a source log: every figure in the brief links to a source and date.

## 3. Choose the format
| Format | Length | Core sections |
|---|---|---|
| Decision note / submission | 2-4 pages | Issue, recommendation, timing, background, options, risks, handling, clearances |
| Meeting brief | 1-3 pages + annexes | Purpose and objectives, who's attending (public roles only), key messages, lines to take, likely asks and responses, background |
| Speaking note | 1-2 pages | Key messages in spoken style, short sentences |
| Q&A / defensive lines | as needed | Anticipated hostile questions with short factual answers |
| Research report / think-tank paper | 10-40 pages | Summary, context, evidence, analysis, options or conclusions, references |

## 4. Write it
- **Bottom line up front:** the first paragraph says what the reader needs to know or
  decide, and by when.
- Use headings that carry meaning ("Costs are rising faster than forecast") rather than
  labels ("Costs").
- One idea per paragraph; numbered paragraphs make discussion easy.
- Separate fact from judgement: "The data show..." vs "We assess...".
- **Lines to take** are short, quotable, accurate and cleared; include "If pressed"
  lines and things **not** to say.
- Round numbers sensibly and give the date and source.

## 5. Answering parliamentary / legislative questions
- Answer the question asked, fully and accurately; legislative codes typically require
  ministers to give accurate and truthful information and to correct errors promptly.
- Check the deadline (written questions often have short set turnaround times —
  check the current rules for your legislature).
- Draft the answer, plus a background note for the minister explaining sources and
  sensitivities.
- If information is not held or cost-prohibitive to gather, say so in the approved form.
- Check consistency with previous answers on the same topic.

## 6. Clear and quality-assure
Get legal, finance, communications and other affected teams to clear text in their
area. Do a final read for accuracy of every number, names and titles, and sensitivity
(would you be comfortable if it were published or released under freedom-of-information law?).

## Gotchas
- Burying the decision required on page three.
- Stale figures — a new statistics release the morning of the meeting.
- Briefing on what the department wants to say rather than what the reader will be asked.
- Partisan content in civil-service briefs; politically impartial officials must keep
  party-political material out (rules vary by system).

## Verify
- [ ] Reader, purpose, deadline and format confirmed.
- [ ] Bottom line and any decision required in the first paragraph.
- [ ] Every figure sourced and dated; consistent with prior statements.
- [ ] Lines to take and hostile Q&A included where the reader will face questions.
- [ ] Cleared by relevant teams; accurate enough to be published.
