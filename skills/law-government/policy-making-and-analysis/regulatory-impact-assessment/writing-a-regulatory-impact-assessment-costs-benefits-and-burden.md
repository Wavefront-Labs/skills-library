# Writing a regulatory impact assessment: costs, benefits and burden

**Goal:** a transparent estimate of what a proposed rule will cost and achieve, compared
with alternatives, so decision-makers (and scrutiny bodies) can judge whether it's worth it.

Most OECD governments require some form of RIA. Method guides differ in detail — e.g.
UK Green Book and Better Regulation guidance (scrutinised by the Regulatory Policy
Committee), US OMB Circular A-4 for significant federal rules (reviewed by OIRA), the
EU Better Regulation toolbox. **Check your jurisdiction's current guidance**: discount
rates, thresholds and required sections change.

## 1. Problem and rationale
What is the problem (with evidence), why does government need to act (market failure,
externality, information asymmetry, equity), and what are the objectives (measurable)?

## 2. Baseline (counterfactual)
Describe what happens **without** the new rule, including trends and other rules already
coming. Every cost and benefit is measured relative to this. The commonest RIA error is
counting changes that would have happened anyway.

## 3. Options
Do nothing (baseline), non-regulatory options (guidance, information, voluntary codes,
self-regulation), and regulatory variants (different stringency, scope, timing,
exemptions). Explain why discarded options were rejected.

## 4. Costs
- **Direct compliance costs:** capital (equipment, IT), ongoing operational costs.
- **Administrative burden** with the **Standard Cost Model**: for each information
  obligation, cost = time per action × hourly wage (plus overheads) × frequency ×
  number of affected businesses. E.g. 2 hours × 30/hour × 4 times a year × 5,000
  firms = 1.2 million a year.
- **Familiarisation/transition costs** (reading guidance, training, changing systems)
  — one-off.
- **Government costs:** enforcement, IT, staffing.
- **Indirect costs:** reduced competition, innovation, prices passed to consumers.
Separate one-off vs annual; state who bears them.

## 5. Benefits
- Monetise where credible: avoided harm (health, accidents, environmental damage),
  time saved, fraud avoided. Health and safety benefits often use a **value of a
  statistical life** or QALY values set by central guidance — use the official figures.
- Describe non-monetised benefits qualitatively with the best available indicators
  (don't silently drop them, and don't over-monetise speculative ones).

## 6. Bringing it together
- Appraisal period (often 10 years; longer for infrastructure), constant prices,
  **discount** future values at the official social discount rate to a **net present
  value** (NPV = Σ (Bₜ − Cₜ)/(1+r)ᵗ). Report benefit-cost ratio too.
- **Sensitivity analysis:** vary key assumptions (take-up, unit costs, effect size);
  report a low/central/high range and the switching values ("benefits exceed costs
  only if compliance exceeds 60%").
- Show **who wins and who loses**: distributional analysis by income group, region,
  business size; specific tests for small/micro businesses and, where required,
  equality, competition, trade, environmental impacts.

## 7. Evidence, consultation and review
Cite data sources; show assumptions explicitly in a table; test them in consultation
(ask affected businesses for real cost data). Set out monitoring and a **post-
implementation review** date with success indicators.

## Gotchas
- Transfers (taxes, fees, payments between groups) are not net social costs; report
  them separately.
- Double counting (e.g. counting both lower injury numbers and the lower insurance
  premiums they cause).
- Assuming 100% compliance overstates both costs and benefits.
- Unverified figures: discount rates and VSL values here are deliberately not given —
  take the current official values.

## Verify
- [ ] Baseline explicit; options include non-regulatory alternatives.
- [ ] Admin burden built with SCM inputs that can be checked.
- [ ] One-off vs ongoing costs separated; transfers excluded from net value.
- [ ] NPV uses the official discount rate; sensitivity and switching values reported.
- [ ] Small-business and distributional impacts addressed; review date set.
