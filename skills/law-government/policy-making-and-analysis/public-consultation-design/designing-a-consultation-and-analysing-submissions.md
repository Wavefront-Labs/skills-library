# Designing a consultation and analysing submissions

**Goal:** get evidence and views that genuinely shape a decision, from the people
affected (not just the loudest), and show them what changed as a result.

## 1. Purpose and legal frame
- Decide **what is open to influence**. If the decision is made, don't consult on it —
  inform. The IAP2 spectrum (inform, consult, involve, collaborate, empower) helps set
  expectations.
- Consult **early enough** that options are still formative. In English public law the
  "Gunning principles" (formative stage, sufficient reasons, adequate time,
  conscientious consideration) are the classic test; other systems have statutory
  requirements — e.g. US federal **notice-and-comment** rulemaking under the
  Administrative Procedure Act. **Legal duties to consult vary by jurisdiction**;
  failing them can invalidate the decision.

## 2. Stakeholder mapping
List affected and interested groups; place them on a **power/interest grid**. Look
specifically for **seldom-heard** groups (people with disabilities, low literacy,
minority languages, rural, young people, small businesses without trade associations)
and plan targeted routes to them: partner organisations, in-person sessions, easy-read
versions, translated summaries.

## 3. The consultation document
- Plain-language summary up front: what we propose, why, what we want to know,
  deadline, how responses will be used.
- Enough evidence and options (including costs and impacts) for informed responses.
- **Question design:**
  - Neutral wording ("To what extent do you agree or disagree…" with a balanced scale),
    not leading ("Do you agree we should protect children by…").
  - One issue per question; no double-barrelled questions.
  - Pair closed questions with an open "why?".
  - Ask for evidence: "Please provide data on costs to your organisation."
  - Keep it short; prioritise the questions you'll actually use.
- Respondent information: individual vs organisation, sector, size, region — so
  analysis can segment. Explain data protection and whether responses are published.
- **Duration:** commonly around 12 weeks for major consultations in many
  administrations; shorter can be justified; extend over holidays.

## 4. Channels
Online form (structured data), email/post for long submissions, events and workshops,
targeted interviews, and for contested value questions consider **deliberative**
methods (citizens' panels or assemblies with balanced information and facilitation).

## 5. Analysing responses
1. Log every response with metadata; remove personal data per policy.
2. **Identify campaign responses** (identical or template text) — count them and report
   their number and content, but analyse them as one argument with a count, not
   thousands of independent views.
3. Quantitative questions: counts and percentages **of those who answered** — and say
   clearly that consultation respondents are self-selected, not a representative sample.
4. Open text: build a **coding framework** (themes and sub-themes) from a first sample,
   code all responses (manually or with software assistance plus human checking),
   double-code a sample to check consistency, add themes as they emerge.
5. Weigh **arguments and evidence**, not just volume. A single well-evidenced
   submission can matter more than a thousand assertions.
6. Segment by respondent type.

## 6. Government response
Publish: who responded, the main themes per question, what you've decided, **what
changed and why**, and what didn't change and why. Publish within a reasonable time;
silence breeds cynicism.

## Gotchas
- Online-only consultations skew to organised and digitally confident groups.
- Reporting "70% opposed" from self-selected respondents as public opinion is misleading.
- Undisclosed pre-decision makes the whole exercise legally and politically vulnerable.

## Verify
- [ ] Decision still formative; legal consultation duties checked.
- [ ] Seldom-heard groups mapped with targeted channels.
- [ ] Questions neutral, single-issue, with evidence prompts.
- [ ] Campaign responses identified; coding double-checked.
- [ ] Published response explains what changed and why.
