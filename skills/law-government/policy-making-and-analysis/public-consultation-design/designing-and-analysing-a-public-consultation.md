# Designing and analysing a public consultation

**Goal:** collect evidence and views that genuinely shape a decision, in a way that is
fair, accessible and defensible if challenged. Models: England & Wales (Cabinet Office
Consultation Principles plus the common-law Gunning principles) and US federal
notice-and-comment rulemaking under the Administrative Procedure Act. Whether consultation
is legally required, its minimum length and its form vary by jurisdiction and statute —
check the enabling legislation first.

## 1. Decide purpose, scope and legal footing
- What is still genuinely open? Consulting on a decision already made is both pointless
  and, in England & Wales, a ground for judicial review.
- Is consultation required by statute, by a legitimate expectation (past practice,
  published promise) or voluntary? Statutory duties may specify consultees and timing.
- Choose the level of participation (IAP2 spectrum: inform, consult, involve,
  collaborate, empower) and be honest with participants about which it is.

## 2. Map stakeholders
Grid each group by **interest** and **influence/affectedness**; deliberately add
seldom-heard groups (people with disabilities, low-literacy users, small firms, rural
residents, young people). For each, note the best channel (online form, workshop, trade
body, community organisation, translated materials).

## 3. Build the consultation document
1. Executive summary and how to respond (deadline, address, formats).
2. Context and the problem.
3. **Options or the proposal, with reasons** — enough information for an intelligent
   response (Gunning principle two), including the draft impact assessment if one exists.
4. Questions, numbered and placed beside the relevant text.
5. Privacy notice: how responses will be used and published; confidentiality requests.
Offer accessible formats (easy-read, large print, translations as needed).

## 4. Write good questions
| Do | Avoid |
|---|---|
| "To what extent do you agree that X? Please explain." | "Do you agree that we should protect children by X?" (leading) |
| One issue per question | Double-barrelled questions |
| Closed scale plus free text | Only yes/no |
| Ask for evidence: "What would this cost your organisation?" | Abstract opinion only |
Keep it to the minimum number of questions — 10-20 is typical; long forms depress response rates.

## 5. Timing and channels
Common practice is around 12 weeks for major policy (UK guidance now emphasises
proportionality rather than a fixed period; US federal rules commonly allow 30-60 days
— check your rules). Extend for holiday periods. Pilot the questions with 3-5 people
from different groups. Run events in parallel and record them in a standard template so
that they can be analysed alongside written responses.

## 6. Analyse submissions
1. Log every response with an ID, respondent type and channel.
2. Build a **coding frame** from a sample of ~50 responses, then code the rest; double-code
   a 10% sample to check consistency between analysts.
3. Report closed questions as counts and percentages, **by respondent type**, and say
   clearly that a consultation is not a representative poll.
4. Identify **organised campaign responses** (identical or near-identical text); count
   them separately and report them transparently rather than discarding them.
5. Pull out new evidence and arguments, which matter more than volume.
Text-analysis software can speed up clustering, but a human must check the coding.

## 7. Publish the response
State what you heard, what you will change and why, and what you will not change and
why (conscientious consideration — Gunning principle four). Aim to publish within about
12 weeks of closing, or as your rules require, and alongside any final decision.

## Gotchas
- Asking only about the preferred option when the law or fairness requires alternatives
  to be explained (see *Moseley*).
- Counting responses as votes.
- Failing to consult a statutorily named body.
- Promising anonymity you cannot deliver under freedom-of-information or records law.

## Verify
- [ ] Legal basis, scope and open questions written down before launch.
- [ ] Stakeholder map includes seldom-heard groups and matched channels.
- [ ] Questions piloted; neutral, single-issue, with free text.
- [ ] Coding frame documented and double-coded sample checked.
- [ ] Published response explains changes and non-changes with reasons.
