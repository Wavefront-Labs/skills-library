# Religious divorce: the get process

**Goal:** guide a client through obtaining a valid get so that both parties are free to
remarry under Jewish law, in step with the civil divorce, and know the remedies if one party
refuses.

Practice differs between Orthodox, Conservative/Masorti and other movements and between
batei din; in Israel, rabbinical courts have state jurisdiction over Jewish marriage and
divorce. This is an orientation outline; procedure is decided by the beth din handling the
case. Status: draft.

## 1. Why it matters
Under halakha a marriage is ended by the husband giving, and the wife receiving, a get of
her own free will (broadly; consent of the wife has been required since medieval rabbinic
enactment in Ashkenazi practice). A civil divorce does not end the religious marriage. If a
woman remarries without a get, a later relationship is treated as adulterous in Orthodox
halakha and children of it may face *mamzer* status, a serious and largely irreversible
consequence. Hence timing and validity are critical.

## 2. The procedure in outline
1. **Open a file** with a recognised beth din; both parties provide identity, Hebrew names,
   the ketubah and marriage details. Names and spellings must be exact.
2. **Hearing/appointment:** usually short. A **sofer** (scribe) writes the get specifically
   for this couple, on the husband's instruction, before qualified witnesses.
3. **Delivery:** the husband (or his appointed agent, *shaliach*) hands the get to the wife
   (or her agent). Either party may appoint an agent so they need not meet.
4. The beth din keeps the get and issues a **certificate (petur)** confirming the divorce.
5. The wife generally must wait (commonly about 92 days) before remarrying; certain
   remarriage restrictions apply (e.g. a kohen may not marry a divorcee).

## 3. Coordinating with the civil divorce
- Raise the get early in the civil negotiation; once civil assets are settled, leverage to
  secure cooperation disappears.
- Some civil systems give tools: e.g. in England and Wales the court can delay the final
  divorce order until a religious divorce is obtained (Divorce (Religious Marriages) Act 2002
  provisions); New York's "get laws" address removal of barriers to remarriage. Check local law.
- Settlement agreements can include a clause obliging cooperation with the get.

## 4. Get refusal (agunah)
A spouse who refuses (often the husband, sometimes the wife) can leave the other unable to
remarry. Options: beth din summons and a *seruv* (contempt order) leading to communal
pressure; civil-law remedies above; damages or maintenance obligations under a halakhic
prenup; in Israel, rabbinical-court sanctions. Coercion outside lawful channels risks invalidating
the get and civil/criminal liability.

## 5. Prevention: halakhic prenuptial agreements
Several batei din endorse prenups (e.g. obligating support payments until a get is given, and
agreeing to beth din jurisdiction). Recommend them to couples marrying.

## Gotchas
- Errors in names or the couple's identity can invalidate the get; use the beth din's forms.
- Assuming civil divorce suffices.
- Movement differences: some Orthodox batei din won't recognise gets from other courts.

## Verify
- [ ] Recognised beth din engaged; names and ketubah details confirmed.
- [ ] Get timing built into the civil divorce strategy.
- [ ] Certificate (petur) received and kept.
- [ ] Client told of the waiting period and remarriage restrictions.
- [ ] If refusal: lawful remedies identified (seruv, civil statute, prenup).
