# Navigating the get (religious divorce) process

**Goal:** get a Jewish marriage religiously dissolved cleanly, in step with the civil
divorce, so that neither spouse — especially the wife — is left unable to remarry.
Halakha is the same everywhere in principle, but practice differs between Orthodox,
Conservative/Masorti and Reform communities and between batei din; civil remedies
differ by country (US, England & Wales and Israel used below). Work with the beth din
the community will recognise.

## 1. Why it matters
- Under halakha a marriage ends only by the husband's free delivery of a **get** and
  the wife's acceptance (or by death). A civil decree alone does not end it.
- A woman who remarries without a get is, in Orthodox law, in an adulterous union, and
  children of that union may be *mamzerim* with lasting status consequences. A man
  who refuses leaves his wife an **agunah** ("chained woman", strictly a *mesurevet get*).
- So: secure the get **before or alongside** the civil decree, never "later".

## 2. Before the ceremony
1. Choose a recognised beth din; ask which communities and the Israeli Chief
   Rabbinate accept its gittin (critical if either party may live in Israel).
2. Provide: the ketubah (marriage contract), civil marriage certificate, Hebrew names
   of both parties and their fathers, any name variants and nicknames used, ID.
   Spelling of names matters — errors can invalidate the get.
3. Agree logistics: both present, or one appointing an agent (*shaliach*) — a get can
   be sent by agent for delivery to the wife abroad.
4. If the beth din is also asked to resolve money or custody (*din Torah*), sign an
   arbitration agreement (*shtar borerut*); keep that separate from the get itself.

## 3. The ceremony (typically 1–2 hours)
- A panel (usually three rabbis), a scribe (*sofer*) and two qualified witnesses.
- The husband instructs the sofer to write the get **for this wife**; witnesses sign.
- Both confirm they act voluntarily. A get coerced unlawfully is invalid (*get
  me'useh*), so pressure must come in forms halakha accepts.
- The husband drops the get into the wife's cupped hands; she walks a short distance
  and returns it. The beth din files it and later issues each party a **ptur**
  (certificate of release).
- The wife traditionally waits about 92 days before remarrying; a kohen may not marry
  a divorcee.

## 4. When a spouse refuses
| Tool | Where | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Halakhic prenuptial agreement (e.g. RCA/BDA form) | US, UK, elsewhere | Obligates daily support payments while a get is withheld; arbitration by beth din |
| Seruv (contempt order) | Community | Public censure, exclusion from synagogue honours |
| Divorce (Religious Marriages) Act 2002 | England & Wales | Court can refuse the final civil order until both declare the religious divorce is done |
| Get statutes (DRL s.253 and 236 factors) | New York | Plaintiff must swear barriers to remarriage removed; refusal can weigh in equitable distribution |
| Rabbinical court sanctions | Israel | Exclusive jurisdiction; licence, passport, bank and even imprisonment sanctions against refusers |
| Coercive control / economic abuse framing | Various | Some courts treat get refusal as abuse — jurisdiction-dependent |
A woman can also refuse to accept a get; men face a slower remedy (*heter me'ah rabbanim*)
in Ashkenazi practice. Treat both sides even-handedly.

## Gotchas
- Settling the civil divorce first removes leverage; build the get into the
  settlement terms (timing, cooperation, who pays the beth din fee).
- A Reform or non-Orthodox document may not be accepted by Orthodox authorities or in
  Israel — check before relying on it.
- Paying a spouse to give a get is common but can be attacked as extortion in some
  courts; document it as part of a global settlement.
- Never draft in a way that makes the get conditional on unresolved matters after
  delivery — conditions can create doubt over validity.

## Verify
- [ ] Beth din chosen and recognised by the communities and states that matter.
- [ ] Hebrew names and variants confirmed in writing before the ceremony.
- [ ] Civil settlement ties the get's timing to the civil process.
- [ ] Ptur received and copies stored; civil court informed where required.
- [ ] If refusal: remedies list above mapped to the parties' jurisdictions.
