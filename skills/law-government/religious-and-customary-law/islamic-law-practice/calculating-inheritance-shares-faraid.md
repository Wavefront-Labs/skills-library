# Calculating inheritance shares (fara'id)

**Goal:** produce a distribution of a Muslim decedent's estate that follows the applicable
school and the personal-status law in force, and show the working.

Classical rules are largely agreed among the Sunni schools, with notable differences in some
configurations; Twelver Shia (Ja'fari) rules differ structurally (e.g. classes of heirs).
Many states codify a version in personal-status legislation that departs from classical
positions (e.g. obligatory bequests for orphaned grandchildren in several countries). In
non-Muslim-majority states, the result usually takes effect only through a valid civil will
or the heirs' agreement. A qualified scholar or specialist lawyer should confirm any real
distribution. Status: draft.

## 1. Order of deductions from the estate
1. Reasonable funeral expenses.
2. **Debts** (including unpaid dower, *mahr*, owed to a wife).
3. **Bequests (wasiyya)** up to **one third** of the remainder. Majority Sunni view: no
   bequest to an heir unless the other heirs consent; Ja'fari view permits bequests to heirs
   within the third.
4. The balance goes to heirs.

## 2. Identify heirs and exclusions
List surviving relatives. Barriers to inheriting commonly recognised: homicide of the
decedent, and difference of religion (with some modern-state variations). Then apply
**exclusion (hajb)**: e.g. a son excludes brothers; a father excludes siblings; a nearer
heir in the same line excludes a more distant one.

## 3. Fixed-share heirs (Sunni outline)
Key shares (conditions abbreviated; check the full rule for each case):
- **Husband:** 1/2 if no child or son's child; otherwise 1/4.
- **Wife (or wives sharing):** 1/4 if no child; otherwise 1/8.
- **Daughter:** alone 1/2; two or more share 2/3; with a son, she becomes residuary taking
  half a son's portion.
- **Mother:** 1/6 if there is a child or two or more siblings; otherwise 1/3 (with the
  special *'umariyyatan* cases where she takes a third of the remainder after the spouse).
- **Father:** 1/6 if there is a son; with only daughters, 1/6 plus residue; with no children,
  residuary.
- Others (grandparents, uterine siblings, full and consanguine sisters) have defined shares
  in particular configurations.

## 4. Residuaries (asaba)
After fixed shares, the remainder goes to the nearest male agnatic relatives (sons, then
son's sons, father, brothers, etc.), with females taking alongside their male counterpart at
half the male portion where the rule applies.

## 5. Adjustments
- **'Awl (increase of denominator):** where fixed shares exceed the whole, reduce all
  proportionally by enlarging the common denominator.
- **Radd (return):** where shares are less than the whole and no residuary exists, return the
  surplus proportionally to fixed-share heirs (in most Sunni views excluding spouses).

## 6. Worked shape (example)
Heirs: wife, one son, one daughter. Wife 1/8 (child present). Remainder 7/8 to son and
daughter at 2:1 → son 7/12, daughter 7/24. Common denominator 24: wife 3, son 14, daughter 7.

## Gotchas
- Applying Sunni rules to a Ja'fari estate or vice versa.
- Ignoring state codification (obligatory bequest, modified shares).
- Forgetting the dower as a debt.
- Treating the distribution as self-executing in a civil jurisdiction.

- [ ] Heirs listed; exclusions applied and noted.
