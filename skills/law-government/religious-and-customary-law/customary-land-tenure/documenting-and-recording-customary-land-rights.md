# Documenting and recording customary land rights

**Goal:** turn rights that exist under custom into a record the state and third parties
respect — without freezing out the people custom already protects (women, younger
siblings, pastoralists, migrants). Examples draw on sub-Saharan African statutory
schemes (Uganda, Tanzania, Ghana-style customary land secretariats) and the FAO VGGT;
Pacific, Latin American and Asian systems differ, and every statute defines who can
certify, what is registrable and whether rights become transferable. Read the enabling
law first.

## 1. Decide what is being recorded
| Option | Records | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Community/territory boundary | Outer boundary of the group's land, held by a body (village council, CPA, trust) | Internal rights stay informal |
| Household parcel certificate | Use/occupation parcels within the community | Elites and men captured as sole holders |
| Layered rights register | Primary holders plus secondary rights (grazing, water, gathering, paths, seasonal use) | Harder to map; often the most accurate |
Most failures come from recording only individual "ownership" where custom has
overlapping bundles of rights.

## 2. Method (systematic, community-wide)
1. **Legal and social scoping (weeks 1–4):** enabling statute, competent authority,
   prescribed forms and fees; map customary authorities, clans/lineages, existing
   disputes and outside claims (concessions, protected areas, investors).
2. **Community entry and consent:** public meetings in the local language; explain
   costs, effects (taxability, transferability, mortgageability) and the right to
   decline. Record the community decision (minutes, attendance, sex-disaggregated).
3. **Establish a land committee** with mandated representation of women and minority
   users; agree written community rules (who may hold, inheritance, sales to outsiders).
4. **Boundary harmonisation with neighbours first:** joint boundary walks with
   neighbouring communities; GPS points or high-resolution imagery; signed boundary
   agreements. Disputed segments are flagged, not forced.
5. **Parcel adjudication:** walk each parcel with claimant, neighbours and elders;
   record holder(s), how acquired (inheritance, allocation, gift, purchase), spouses and
   dependants, secondary users. Default to **co-recording spouses** where law permits.
6. **Public display / objection period:** post maps and lists in the community for the
   statutory period (often 30–90 days — check the law); hear objections at a recorded
   meeting or before the customary tribunal.
7. **Certification and registration:** forms signed by the competent authority; copies
   to holder, community registry and district/state registry. Record the legal basis.
8. **Maintenance:** a simple process and fee for recording later transfers and
   inheritances — without it the register is obsolete within a few years.

## 3. Handling disputes during recording
- Resolve through the customary forum first, document the outcome, then record.
- Unresolved disputes: record the parcel as "under dispute" with parties named;
  never award by default to the claimant present on the day.
- Large outside claims (investor, state) → escalate to legal support; do not let the
  exercise legitimise an unconsented acquisition (FPIC principles may apply).

## Gotchas
- Certificates in the household head's name alone can strip widows and daughters of
  rights custom gave them.
- Converting customary rights to freehold can make land saleable and trigger
  distress sales; explain this before consent.
- Pastoral and seasonal rights disappear if the method only records cultivated plots.
- Donor-project timetables push speed over legitimacy; disputes then resurface.

## Verify
- [ ] Enabling statute, authority and forms confirmed.
- [ ] Community consent minuted; committee composition includes women and minority users.
- [ ] Outer boundaries agreed with every neighbour or flagged as disputed.
- [ ] Each parcel record names all holders, spouses and secondary rights.
- [ ] Public objection period run for the statutory length; objections resolved or noted.
- [ ] A maintenance process for later transfers exists.
