# Preservation, issue selection and habeas

**Goal:** get a conviction or sentence reviewed on the issues most likely to win,
inside unforgiving deadlines, and keep collateral remedies open.

## 1. Deadlines are jurisdictional (or nearly)
- US federal criminal notice of appeal: 14 days after judgment (FRAP 4(b)).
  State deadlines vary and are often short.
- England & Wales: notice of appeal or application for leave to the Court of Appeal within 28 days of the
  conviction, verdict or finding (Criminal Appeal Act 1968 s.18(2)); the Court of Appeal can extend time (s.18(3)).
- File the notice even if issue analysis is unfinished; you can't fix a late notice.
- Collateral review has its own limits — e.g. AEDPA's one-year period (28 U.S.C. 2244(d)(1) for state prisoners; 2255(f) for federal prisoners), with
  tolling rules that are easy to lose.

## 2. Read the whole record
Every transcript (including sidebars, jury selection, instructions, sentencing),
every motion and ruling, every exhibit. Issues hide in the instruction conference.

## 3. Preservation → standard of review
For each candidate issue note: was it raised, on what ground, and was a ruling obtained?
| Status | Typical review |
|---|---|
| Preserved legal question | de novo |
| Preserved discretionary ruling (evidence) | abuse of discretion |
| Fact finding | clear error / substantial evidence |
| Unpreserved | plain error (US FRCrP 52(b): a plain error that affects substantial rights may be considered even though it was not brought to the court's attention) |
Then harmless-error: even a real error loses if the court finds it didn't affect the verdict.

## 4. Pick issues — fewer is stronger
Rank by (merit × standard of review × remedy). A strong preserved issue reviewed de
novo with a new-trial remedy beats five plain-error arguments. Counsel may winnow
issues (*Jones v. Barnes*), but discuss choices with the client. Lead with your best
issue.

## 5. The brief
Statement of the case with record cites for every fact; standard of review stated
for each issue; argument that confronts adverse authority; specific remedy requested
(reversal, new trial, resentencing, remand). Appellate judges read the facts
section for fairness — overreaching there costs credibility everywhere.

## 6. Post-conviction / collateral review
- **Ineffective assistance (*Strickland*):** deficient performance + prejudice
  (reasonable probability of a different result). Usually needs facts outside the
  trial record (affidavits, trial counsel's file) — hence raised collaterally.
- **Brady violations, newly discovered evidence, actual innocence, unlawful sentence.**
- **Federal habeas for state prisoners (§ 2254):** exhaust state remedies first;
  AEDPA deference — relief only if the state decision was contrary to or an
  unreasonable application of clearly established Supreme Court law, or based on an
  unreasonable fact determination. Procedural default is the main trap.
- The **Criminal Cases Review Commission** (England, Wales and Northern Ireland) investigates possible
  miscarriages of justice and can refer cases to the appeal courts.

## Gotchas
- Raising a claim on direct appeal can bar or affect relitigating it collaterally.
- Successive habeas petitions require circuit authorisation first (28 U.S.C. 2244(b)(3)(A)).
- Uncertain: tolling, exhaustion and default doctrines are dense and change through
  case law — check current circuit authority.

## Verify
- [ ] Notice of appeal filed; collateral limitation dates calendared.

- [ ] Harmlessness addressed for each issue.
