# Building a theory of defence

**Goal:** arrive, as early as possible, at one coherent explanation of the evidence
that is consistent with innocence (or a lesser offence), and organise investigation,
motions and client advice around it.

## 1. First 72 hours
- Interview the client privately; explain privilege before taking facts.
  Get timeline, witnesses, phones, cameras, receipts — anything that decays.
- **Preserve evidence**: send preservation letters for CCTV, phone records, body-worn
  camera, 911/999 audio. Many systems overwrite video in days to weeks.
- Handle custody/release first (see `law-government.criminal-law.bail-and-pretrial-release.bail-application-and-detention-hearing`).
- Tell the client not to discuss the case on recorded jail calls or social media.

## 2. Map the prosecution's case
Make the same elements matrix a prosecutor would: element → evidence → source →
weakness. Defence needs only one element to fail beyond reasonable doubt.
Review all discovery personally; do not rely on police summaries.

## 3. Candidate theories (pick one, not all)
| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| Factual innocence | misidentification, alibi, wrong person |
| Element failure | no intent, no possession, value below threshold |
| Justification/excuse | self-defence, duress, necessity, insanity |
| Procedural | unlawful search/confession → suppression guts the case |
| Lesser offence | yes it happened, but it is manslaughter not murder |
Test each against *every* bad fact. The winner explains the bad facts instead of
ignoring them. Mutually inconsistent theories ("I wasn't there; it was self-defence")
destroy credibility with juries.

## 4. Direct investigation by the theory
Brief the investigator with specific questions. Interview prosecution witnesses with
a witness present (so you are not the only impeachment witness). Consider experts
early (forensics, eyewitness identification, mental state) — funding requests take time.

## 5. Motions as strategy
Identify suppression targets (stop, search, warrant, interrogation, identification
procedure). A win may end the case; a loss still yields sworn officer testimony usable
at trial. See `law-government.criminal-law.criminal-motions-practice.motion-to-suppress-and-pretrial-motions`.

## 6. Notice obligations
Many jurisdictions require advance notice of alibi, insanity/mental-state, or expert
defences (US federal: FRCrP 12.1 alibi notice, due within 14 days after the government's written request, and Rule 12.2
insanity and mental-condition notice); missing a deadline can exclude the defence. Check local rules on day one.

## 7. Client counselling
Some decisions belong to the client and others to counsel after consultation (check your
jurisdiction's professional-conduct rules for the list). Advise in writing on
plea offers, likely sentence range on conviction vs. offer, and collateral
consequences (immigration — *Padilla v. Kentucky* in the US). Record the client's
decisions and your advice.

## Gotchas
- Interviewing a witness alone makes you a potential witness.
- An affirmative defence may shift a burden of production (or persuasion) onto the
  defence — the allocation varies by jurisdiction and defence.
- Do not present evidence you know to be false; client perjury rules differ by
  jurisdiction and need careful handling.
- Uncertain: discovery scope and timing vary widely (open-file states vs. narrow rules).

## Verify
- [ ] Preservation requests sent for all time-limited evidence.
- [ ] Theory stated in one or two sentences and survives every bad fact.
- [ ] Notice deadlines for alibi/expert/mental-state defences calendared.
- [ ] Suppression issues identified and decided on.
- [ ] Written advice to client on offers and client-reserved decisions on file.
