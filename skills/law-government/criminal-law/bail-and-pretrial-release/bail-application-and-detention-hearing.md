# Bail application and detention hearing

**Goal:** secure the least restrictive release the law allows (defence) or justify
detention or conditions with evidence (prosecution) at the first appearance —
the hearing that most shapes how a criminal case ends.

## 1. Know the test in your court
- **US federal (18 U.S.C. § 3142):** release on personal recognisance or conditions
  unless no condition or combination will reasonably assure appearance and community
  safety. The statute requires clear and convincing evidence for the community-safety ground and states no
  standard for flight, so check circuit authority. Rebuttable presumptions of detention apply
  for listed offences (e.g. drug offences with a maximum of 10 years or more, offences under 18 U.S.C. 924(c),
  listed terrorism offences, offences with a minor victim; 18 U.S.C. 3142(e)(3)).
- **US states:** rules vary from money-bail schedules to no-money-bail regimes with
  risk-assessment tools; read the state's statute and constitution.
- **England & Wales (Bail Act 1976):** for imprisonable offences the court need not grant bail if there are
  substantial grounds to believe the defendant would abscond, offend on bail, or
  interfere with witnesses (Schedule 1 exceptions).

## 2. Factors courts weigh
Nature and seriousness of the offence; weight of the evidence; criminal record, especially failures to appear
and bail breaches; community ties (residence, family, employment, length of time in
the area); immigration status; mental health/substance use; risk to identified people.

## 3. Defence: walk in with a release plan
A plan defeats the abstract "risk" argument:
- Verified address (who lives there, will they let probation visit).
- Employment or school letter; childcare responsibilities.
- Proposed sureties/third-party custodians — vet them, prepare them to testify.
- Conditions you will accept: reporting, curfew, electronic monitoring, surrender
  of passport, no-contact orders, treatment, drug testing.
- Ability to pay: if money bail is involved, argue what the client can actually
  afford — unaffordable bail is detention by another name.

## 4. Prosecution: evidence, not adjectives
Proffer specific facts (prior FTAs with dates, threats on record, weapons found,
foreign assets). Hearsay and proffer are often permitted at detention hearings, but
credibility still matters. Link each risk to why no condition can manage it.

## 5. The hearing
- Ask for a continuance only if it serves the client (US federal: up to 3 days for
  the government, 5 for the defence, excluding weekends and holidays, and the defence limit can be
  exceeded for good cause; the defendant is detained meanwhile).
- Use the hearing to probe the case: cross-examining the agent gives early discovery,
  but watch that the client doesn't make statements.
- Request written findings on detention (a US federal detention order must include them, 18 U.S.C. 3142(i)).

## 6. If you lose
Review/appeal routes: US federal — motion to the district judge to revoke or amend a
magistrate's order; reopening on new information. England & Wales — renewed
application with new arguments. Track custody time limits
and speedy-trial clocks.

## Gotchas
- Breaching a condition can mean revocation plus a separate offence.
- Conditions that are unrelated to identified risk are challengeable as excessive.
- Uncertain: money-bail rules and risk-tool use are changing quickly in many US
  states — check current statute and local practice.
