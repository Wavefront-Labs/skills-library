# Motion to suppress and pre-trial motions

**Goal:** use pre-trial motions to exclude unlawfully obtained evidence, narrow or
end the case, and force disclosure — and preserve every issue for appeal.

## 1. Calendar first
Rule-based deadlines (e.g. US FRCrP 12(b)(3): suppression, defects in the
indictment, severance and Rule 16 discovery motions must be raised by pretrial motion; a late motion is untimely
unless good cause is shown, Rule 12(c)(3)).
Local rules often set shorter dates. Missing one is the most common avoidable loss.

## 2. Motion to suppress (US framework) (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)
The case-law framework below (standing, Franks, exceptions to the warrant requirement, Miranda, remedies and their
limits) is the author's summary and was not checked against the opinions; read the current authority before relying on it.
Work through in order — the prosecution will:
1. **Standing:** did *this* defendant have a reasonable expectation of privacy (or a
   property interest) in the place/thing searched? No standing, no motion.
2. **Was there a search or seizure?** Stops, arrests, phone/data access, tracking.
3. **Warrant?** If yes: probable cause on the four corners, particularity, scope of
   execution. A *Franks* hearing requires a substantial preliminary showing of a
   deliberate or reckless falsehood/omission material to probable cause.
4. **No warrant?** Prosecution must fit an exception: consent, exigency, search
   incident to arrest, automobile, plain view, inventory, Terry stop-and-frisk.
5. **Statements:** custody + interrogation without Miranda warnings/waiver;
   voluntariness; invocation of counsel/silence honoured?
6. **Remedy and its limits:** fruit of the poisonous tree vs. independent source,
   inevitable discovery, attenuation, good-faith exception.
Draft: facts with record cites (reports, body-cam timestamps), the rule, the
application, the specific evidence to suppress. Request an evidentiary hearing.

## 3. Other common motions
- **Dismiss:** defective charging document, limitation period, double jeopardy,
  speedy-trial violation, selective/vindictive prosecution, grand jury abuse.
- **Discovery / Brady-Giglio demand** (FRCrP 16(a)(1) lists categories the government must disclose on request;
  Brady is a separate due-process duty that the rule does not codify): list specific categories (impeachment
  material, informant benefits, prior statements, lab bench notes). Specific
  requests sharpen later materiality arguments.
- **Severance** of counts or co-defendants (prejudicial joinder; *Bruton* confessions).
- **In limine:** exclude prior convictions, other-act evidence, prejudicial photos,
  expert opinion beyond qualifications; obtain rulings before opening.
- **Change of venue** for prejudicial publicity.

## 4. The hearing
The defence often bears the initial burden on standing; the prosecution bears it on
warrant exceptions and Miranda waiver. Cross the officer against the report and
video. Even a loss locks testimony for trial impeachment.

## Outside the US
England & Wales: a confession must be excluded under PACE s.76(2) unless the prosecution proves beyond
reasonable doubt that it was not obtained by oppression or in circumstances likely to make it unreliable; other
prosecution evidence can be excluded under s.78(1) if admitting it would have such an adverse effect on the
fairness of the proceedings that it ought not to be admitted. Check your jurisdiction's equivalent.

## Gotchas
- Conditional pleas preserving suppression issues for appeal exist in some
  jurisdictions only, and need prosecution/court consent.
- Testifying at a suppression hearing: in US federal court the testimony cannot be
  used as evidence of guilt at trial (*Simmons*), but may be usable to impeach in some
  circuits — advise the client.
- Uncertain: state constitutions sometimes give broader protection than federal law.
