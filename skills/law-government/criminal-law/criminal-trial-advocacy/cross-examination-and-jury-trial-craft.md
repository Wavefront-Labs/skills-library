# Cross-examination and jury trial craft

**Goal:** present one theme consistently from jury selection to closing, and protect
the record for appeal along the way.

## 1. Theme and theory first
Write the theory (what happened, legally) and a one-line theme the jury can repeat
("He trusted the wrong friend"). Every phase serves it. Draft the closing *first*;
then build openings and examinations to deliver what the closing needs.

## 2. Voir dire / jury selection
- Purpose: find and remove jurors who cannot be fair on *your* issues, not to "win" jurors.
- Use open questions ("How do you feel about…") to get jurors talking; challenge for
  cause by getting the juror to commit, in their words, that they could not be fair.
- Peremptories cannot be used on race grounds (*Batson v. Kentucky*, 1986);
  have a neutral reason ready for each strike.
- Note: jury selection practice differs in other systems; check local rules.

## 3. Opening statement
Tell the story in present tense from the chosen point of view; preview evidence,
not argument. Promise only what you are sure to prove — opponents will quote broken
promises in closing. Defence may reserve opening in some courts; usually unwise.

## 4. Direct examination
Open, non-leading questions (who/what/where/how/describe). Headline topics
("Let's talk about the night of the 4th"). Front weaknesses yourself. Short,
chronological chapters. Use exhibits to anchor memory.

## 5. Cross-examination — the core rules
1. Leading questions only; one fact per question.
2. Ask only questions you know the answer to, or where any answer helps.
3. Don't ask "why" or let the witness explain.
4. Don't ask the one question too many — save the conclusion for closing.
5. Chapters: lock in helpful facts first, impeach last.

**Impeachment by prior inconsistent statement:** commit (today you said X) →
credit (you gave a statement on [date], to [officer], it was fresh, you signed it) →
confront (read the line, have them confirm). Know your jurisdiction's foundation
and extrinsic-evidence rules (FRE 613 in US federal court: the witness need not be shown the statement, but extrinsic proof of it waits until the witness has had a chance to explain or deny it).

## 6. Objections
Object timely and specifically ("Objection, hearsay"), stand, get a ruling. If
evidence is excluded, make an **offer of proof** to preserve the issue. Use motions
in limine to settle big evidentiary fights before trial. Don't object to harmless
things — juries notice.

## 7. Closing argument
Walk the jury instructions element by element; tie each to evidence. Argue only from the
evidence admitted and the reasonable inferences from it. Address reasonable doubt concretely.

## Gotchas
- Opening a door (e.g. character) lets the other side in on rebuttal.
- Failing to object usually limits appeal to plain-error review.
- Uncertain: evidence codes, order of closings and rebuttal rights vary by jurisdiction.

## Verify
- [ ] Theme fits in one sentence and appears in opening, key crosses and closing.
- [ ] Every cross question is leading and has a known answer.
- [ ] Impeachment exhibits marked and foundation steps scripted.
- [ ] Offers of proof made for every excluded item you need on appeal.
- [ ] Proposed jury instructions filed and objections to the charge on the record.
