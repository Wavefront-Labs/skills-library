# M&A: structure, due diligence, purchase agreement and closing

**Goal:** move a deal from term sheet to closing with the risks found, priced and
allocated in the documents. US and UK private-company practice are the textbook models;
public-company deals add takeover rules, and every jurisdiction has its own merger-control
and foreign-investment regime.

## 1. Structure
| Structure | What transfers | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| Share/stock purchase | The entity, with all liabilities | Contracts and permits usually stay in place | Buyer inherits everything; needs all sellers |
| Asset purchase | Chosen assets and assumed liabilities | Cherry-pick; leave liabilities | Consents to assign contracts; transfer taxes; employee transfer rules (e.g. TUPE in the UK) |
| Merger (US; e.g. reverse triangular) | By operation of law | Squeezes out minority with statutory vote | Appraisal rights; consents still triggered by some contracts |
Tax drives structure (e.g. asset step-up vs seller's preference for share sale) — involve
tax advisers early.

## 2. Due diligence
- **Request list** by workstream: corporate/capitalisation, material contracts (change of
  control, exclusivity, assignment), IP ownership and licences, employment and benefits,
  litigation, regulatory/permits, real estate, data protection, tax, environmental,
  compliance (anti-bribery, sanctions), finance and debt.
- Use a **virtual data room**; track Q&A; record what was disclosed and when.
- Output: a **red-flag report** focused on findings that affect price, structure, conditions,
  specific indemnities, or walk-away — not a summary of every document.
- Map each material finding to a contractual response: price reduction, pre-closing
  covenant, closing condition, specific indemnity, or accept.

## 3. Purchase agreement: key terms
- **Price mechanics:** **completion accounts** (post-closing adjustment for working capital,
  debt and cash) vs **locked box** (price fixed off a historic balance sheet with leakage
  protection; common in the UK/Europe). **Earn-outs** — define metrics precisely and the
  buyer's operating covenants; disputes are common.
- **Representations and warranties:** fundamental (title, capacity, capitalisation) vs
  business reps. Qualifiers: knowledge, materiality; "materiality scrape" for indemnity.
- **Indemnification:** survival periods, basket (deductible vs tipping), cap (often a
  percentage of price for general reps, higher for fundamental), escrow/holdback, specific
  indemnities for known issues, sandbagging clause (pro- or anti-; default rule varies by
  state).
- **R&W / W&I insurance:** shifts warranty risk to an insurer; excludes known issues and
  some categories — read the exclusions against your diligence findings.
- **Covenants:** conduct of business between signing and closing; efforts to obtain approvals
  (with any "hell or high water" commitment on antitrust remedies); non-compete/non-solicit.
- **Conditions:** regulatory clearances (merger control such as US HSR filing, EU/UK
  regimes; foreign investment such as CFIUS or UK NSIA mandatory notifications), third-party
  consents, no MAE, bring-down of reps.
- **Termination:** outside date, break fees.

## 4. Disclosure schedules / letter
Sellers disclose exceptions against reps; buyers push for specific, fairly disclosed
items (general disclosure of the whole data room is a negotiating point, more accepted in
UK practice). Cross-check schedules against diligence findings.

## 5. Closing
A **closing checklist** (document, responsible party, status, signatories): resignations,
board and shareholder approvals, consents, payoff letters and lien releases, funds flow
memo, escrow agreement, share transfer forms or stock powers, registry filings, and
post-closing items (filings, notifications, integration).

## Gotchas
- Change-of-control clauses in key customer contracts discovered late.
- Gun-jumping: integrating or controlling the target before clearance breaches merger-
  control law.
- Funds flow errors — verify wire instructions by phone (wire fraud is common).

## Verify
- [ ] Structure chosen with tax and consent analysis documented.
- [ ] Every red-flag finding mapped to a contractual protection or accepted risk.
- [ ] Regulatory and foreign-investment filings assessed with timelines.
- [ ] Closing checklist and funds flow reconciled and wire details verified.
