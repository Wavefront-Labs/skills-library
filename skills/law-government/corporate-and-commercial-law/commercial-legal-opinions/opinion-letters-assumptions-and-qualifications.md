# Commercial legal opinions: assumptions and qualifications

**Goal:** give a written opinion that states precisely what the firm is confirming, on
what basis, and with what limits — so the recipient can rely on it and the firm isn't
exposed for more than it intended. US customary practice (TriBar Opinion Committee
reports, ABA guidance) and UK/European banking practice are the models; customary
practice differs by market and is part of what the opinion means.

## 1. Types
- **Closing opinions** delivered to the other side in financings and M&A: corporate status,
  capacity, due authorisation, execution, no conflicts with constitution/law/key contracts,
  no consents required, **enforceability**.
- **Reasoned opinions** on uncertain questions: tax, true sale, non-consolidation, regulatory
  characterisation. Use graded levels — "will" (high confidence), "should", "more likely
  than not" — with stated meaning.
- **Risk memoranda** for clients: not opinions to third parties; analysis of options and
  probabilities.
- **Negative assurance (10b-5) letters** in securities offerings: not an opinion but a
  statement that nothing came to counsel's attention indicating misstatement.

## 2. Before agreeing to give it
- Check the opinion is **customary** for the transaction and that you're the right counsel
  (local law matters go to local counsel).
- Scope: which laws? (e.g. "the laws of the State of New York and the federal laws of the
  United States" — exclude securities, tax, antitrust unless addressed).
- **Addressees and reliance:** named addressees only; permitted assigns limited; no reliance
  for other purposes.
- Run conflicts and get **opinion committee** review per firm policy — most firms require
  second-partner sign-off.

## 3. Diligence (the opinion backup)
- Constitutional documents certified by an officer and confirmed with public registries
  (good standing certificates dated close to closing).
- Board/shareholder resolutions approving the transaction and signatories.
- Officer's certificate on factual matters (no litigation, no other agreements breached,
  incumbency).
- Review the material contracts you opine against for no-conflict opinions — or limit to
  scheduled agreements.
- Keep a file showing how each opinion paragraph is supported.

## 4. Standard assumptions
Genuineness of signatures, authenticity of documents, conformity of copies, legal capacity
of natural persons, due authorisation and enforceability against the **other** parties,
no fraud or mutual mistake, and facts in certificates are accurate. **Don't assume what
you are opining on** (e.g. assuming due authorisation of your own client while opining on
it). Assumptions should be ones the recipient would accept as reasonable.

## 5. Qualifications (exceptions)
- **Bankruptcy exception:** enforceability subject to insolvency, reorganisation and similar
  laws affecting creditors' rights generally.
- **Equitable principles exception:** remedies like specific performance and injunctions are
  discretionary.
- Specific provisions not covered (often): penalties/liquidated damages, indemnities against
  own negligence or securities liabilities, exculpation, choice-of-forum provisions,
  severability, waivers of rights that can't be waived, interest provisions that may be
  usurious.
- In UK practice, an enforceability opinion often expressly means "obligations of a type the
  English courts would enforce" — not that each obligation will be enforced as written.

## 6. Drafting habits
- Each opinion paragraph short and in customary form; avoid improvising language that
  may be read more broadly.
- Date it and state it speaks only as of that date with no duty to update.
- Distinguish "to our knowledge" (define whose knowledge and what inquiry).

## Gotchas
- Opining on foreign law without local counsel.
- Opinions on "no litigation" without a docket search or certificate.
- Letting the recipient's counsel expand reliance late in the closing.

- [ ] Scope of law, addressees and reliance stated and appropriate.
- [ ] Backup file supports every opinion paragraph.
- [ ] Assumptions don't assume the matters opined on.
- [ ] Opinion committee or second-partner review completed.
