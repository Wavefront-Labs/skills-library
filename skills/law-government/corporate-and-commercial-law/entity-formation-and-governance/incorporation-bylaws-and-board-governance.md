# Incorporation, bylaws and board governance

**Goal:** set up a business entity correctly and keep it in good order, so that
ownership is clear, decisions are valid, and liability protection holds. Delaware and the
UK Companies Act 2006 are the textbook models; every jurisdiction has its own filing
and governance rules. Tax consequences of entity choice need a tax adviser.

## 1. Choosing the entity
| Factor | Corporation (US C-corp / UK Ltd) | LLC / partnership-type |
|---|---|---|
| Liability | Limited | Limited (LLC/LLP) |
| Investors | Preferred by VCs (stock classes, options) | Flexible economics; less familiar to VCs |
| Formality | Board, officers, minutes | Operating agreement can reduce formality |
Consider where the business operates — foreign qualification/registration is
needed in states/countries where it does business.

## 2. Formation steps (typical)
1. Check name availability; reserve if needed.
2. File the **certificate/articles of incorporation** (US) or register with Companies House
   using articles of association (model articles or bespoke) (UK). Include authorised shares
   (US) and classes.
3. Appoint a **registered agent/office**.
4. **Organisational actions** (US: incorporator's or initial board consent): adopt bylaws,
   elect directors/officers, issue founder stock, adopt fiscal year, authorise bank
   account, approve indemnification agreements, set up option plan if needed.
5. **Founder stock:** purchase agreements with vesting and IP assignment. In the US, an
   **83(b) election** must be made not later than 30 days after the transfer (26 U.S.C.
   83(b)(2)) where stock is subject to vesting.
6. Obtain tax IDs; register for applicable taxes.
7. **Beneficial ownership** filings — check the current UK Companies House requirements. US
   Corporate Transparency Act: FinCEN's final rule, effective 14 August 2026 per FinCEN's
   BOI page, exempts US-formed companies; entities formed under foreign law and registered
   to do business in a US state or tribal jurisdiction remain reporting companies. Check
   the FinCEN page for current deadlines.

## 3. Bylaws / articles: what to look at
Board size and election, quorum and voting, notice periods, written consents, officer roles,
indemnification and advancement, share transfer restrictions, amendment procedures. Stay
consistent with the charter — charter prevails if they conflict.

## 4. Board governance
- **Duties:** care and loyalty (US), and in the UK the codified duties in ss171–177
  (including promoting the success of the company, avoiding conflicts, declaring interests).
- **Business judgment rule** (US) protects informed, good-faith, disinterested decisions —
  so the **process** matters: adequate information, time to consider, documented discussion.
- **Conflicts:** disclose; interested directors recuse; use independent approval or
  shareholder ratification (DGCL §144 safe harbour; §144 was amended in 2025 (85 Del. Laws
  c. 6) and now has separate paths for interested-director and controlling-stockholder
  transactions — read the current text).
- **Resolutions and minutes:** record attendance, quorum, disclosures of interest, materials
  considered, the resolution text, and votes. Unanimous written consents for routine items
  (where permitted).

## 5. Shareholder agreement
Covers what the constitution doesn't: pre-emption on new issues, transfer restrictions,
ROFR, **drag-along** and **tag-along**, board appointment rights, reserved matters/
protective provisions, information rights, deadlock resolution, leaver provisions,
non-competes (enforceability varies sharply — e.g. California).

## 6. Ongoing compliance
Annual reports/confirmation statements, franchise taxes (Delaware's can be surprisingly
high under the authorised-shares method — use the assumed par value capital method),
statutory registers, maintaining the cap table, separating personal and company finances
(commingling risks veil piercing).

## Gotchas
- Issuing shares without board approval creates defective stock (fixable in Delaware via
  ratification under DGCL §204, but costly).
- Missing IP assignments from founders and early contractors.
- Different classes of shares not authorised in the charter.

## Verify
- [ ] Entity choice reviewed for tax and investor fit.
- [ ] Organisational consent covers bylaws, officers, stock issuance and banking.
- [ ] Founder vesting, IP assignment and any 83(b) elections done on time.
- [ ] Minute book, registers and cap table are complete and consistent.
