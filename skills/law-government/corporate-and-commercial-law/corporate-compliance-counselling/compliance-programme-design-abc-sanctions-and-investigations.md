# Compliance programme design: bribery, sanctions, competition and investigations

**Goal:** give a company a compliance programme that prevents and detects misconduct and
that regulators will credit when something goes wrong. Key reference frameworks: US DOJ
*Evaluation of Corporate Compliance Programs* (updated periodically), UK Ministry of Justice
guidance on Bribery Act "adequate procedures", and equivalent guidance elsewhere.

## 1. Risk assessment first
Map the business: countries (corruption indices), sales channels (agents, distributors,
joint ventures), government touchpoints (licences, customs, state-owned customers),
products (export-controlled items), market position (competition risk), payment flows.
Rank risks by likelihood and impact. **Every control should trace back to a risk** —
regulators ask how the programme was tailored.

## 2. Programme elements (common to most frameworks)
1. **Tone from the top** and board oversight, with a compliance function that has
   resources, independence and direct access to the board.
2. **Policies:** code of conduct; anti-bribery including gifts, hospitality, facilitation
   payments, charitable/political donations; sanctions; competition; conflicts.
3. **Third-party due diligence:** risk-based onboarding, beneficial ownership,
   red flags (unusual commissions, requests for cash or offshore payment, government
   connections), contractual audit and termination rights, monitoring.
4. **Training** targeted by role and risk.
5. **Speak-up/whistleblowing** channels with anti-retaliation protection (check the EU
   Whistleblowing Directive and local whistleblower laws).
6. **Monitoring and testing:** data analytics on payments, audits.
7. **Discipline and incentives** applied consistently.
8. **Investigation and remediation** process; root-cause analysis.

## 3. Anti-bribery
- **US FCPA:** anti-bribery provisions (foreign officials) plus books-and-records and internal
  controls provisions for issuers. Check current DOJ enforcement policy.
- **UK Bribery Act 2010:** the corporate **failure to prevent bribery** offence (s7) carries a
  defence that the organisation had **adequate procedures** in place. The UK also has a
  **failure to prevent fraud** offence (Economic Crime and Corporate Transparency Act 2023,
  s199) in force from 1 September 2025 for large organisations, meaning those meeting two of
  three tests (more than 250 employees, more than GBP 36 million turnover, more than GBP 18
  million total assets; ss201-202, which also set group tests) — check scope.

## 4. Sanctions and export controls
- Screen customers, suppliers and counterparties against lists (OFAC SDN, UK OFSI, EU,
  UN) and apply ownership rules (OFAC's **50 Percent Rule** covers entities owned 50 percent or more in
  the aggregate by one or more blocked persons, but speaks only to ownership, not control;
  UK/EU ownership-and-control tests differ). Screen at onboarding and on list updates.
- The statute of limitations for IEEPA and TWEA violations was extended from five to ten
  years on 24 April 2024, and OFAC's recordkeeping requirement now runs 10 years (final rule
  21 March 2025). Keep records accordingly.
- Export controls (US EAR/ITAR, EU dual-use regulation, UK export control orders):
  classify products, check end-use and end-user, licence requirements.

## 5. Competition
Train sales staff on no-go conduct: price-fixing, market allocation, bid-rigging, and
information exchange with competitors (including via trade associations). Prepare a
**dawn-raid protocol** for regulator inspections.

## 6. Internal investigations
- Triage the allegation; decide who investigates (in-house vs external counsel) and
  reporting lines (board committee if senior people are implicated).
- **Preserve** data immediately; consider local data-protection and labour-law limits on
  reviewing employee communications.
- **Privilege:** structure the investigation to protect it (lawyer-led, for legal advice);
  privilege rules differ by country, so take local advice.
- Interviews: **Upjohn warning** (counsel represents the company, not the employee; the
  company may waive privilege).
- Decide on **self-reporting** with counsel — credit regimes differ by agency and country.

## Gotchas
- "Paper programmes" — policies with no testing or data — get little credit.
- Screening only at onboarding misses later list designations.
- Deleting data after an issue surfaces can be obstruction.

## Verify
- [ ] Documented risk assessment underpins each control.
- [ ] Third-party diligence is risk-tiered and records retained.
- [ ] Sanctions screening covers ownership rules and list updates.
- [ ] Investigation protocol covers preservation, privilege and reporting lines.
