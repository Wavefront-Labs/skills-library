# Workplace incident investigation and root-cause analysis

**Goal:** establish what happened and why — down to the system failures, not just the
person nearest the event — and fix those causes, while meeting legal reporting duties
and protecting evidence. Models: GB (HSG245; RIDDOR reporting) and US (OSHA 29 CFR 1904
recording/reporting). Other jurisdictions have their own reportable categories and
deadlines — confirm them before you need them.

## 1. First hour
1. Make safe: treat the injured, stop the process, prevent escalation.
2. **Preserve the scene**: barrier it, stop clean-up, don't restart or repair equipment. In many jurisdictions the regulator may require the scene left undisturbed for a fatality or major incident (except to save life or prevent further harm).
3. Note who was present; separate witnesses before accounts converge.
4. Photograph and video everything before anything moves.
5. Notify management, and legal counsel if the incident is serious (privilege strategy differs by jurisdiction — decide early whether the investigation runs under counsel).

## 2. Report to the regulator on time
| Jurisdiction | Examples (verify current rules) |
|---|---|
| GB RIDDOR | Deaths and specified injuries reported without delay; over-7-day incapacitation within 15 days; certain occupational diseases and dangerous occurrences |
| US OSHA | Work-related fatality within 8 hours; inpatient hospitalisation, amputation or loss of an eye within 24 hours; recordable cases logged on OSHA 300 within 7 calendar days |
Also: insurers, client, workers' compensation, and any industry-specific regulator.

## 3. Gather evidence
- **People:** interview each witness separately, soon, somewhere neutral. Open questions ("Talk me through what you were doing"), then clarify. Reassure on purpose (learning). Record and have them read back.
- **Position:** sketch/measure the scene, positions of people, equipment, materials.
- **Parts:** retain failed components with labels; independent testing if needed.
- **Paper:** risk assessments, procedures, permits, training records, maintenance logs, rosters/hours (fatigue), CCTV, control-system data logs.

## 4. Analyse
1. Build a **timeline / sequence of events** — the backbone.
2. Identify **causal factors**: each event or condition without which the incident would not have happened.
3. Ask why for each, down through levels:
   - Immediate cause (the unsafe act/condition).
   - Underlying causes (procedure, training, supervision, equipment design, maintenance).
   - Root/organisational causes (planning, resourcing, production pressure, culture, management of change).
4. Tools: 5 Whys (quick, single chain), fishbone (brainstorm categories), causal-factor charting/ICAM or TapRooT-type methods (complex events), bowtie to check which barriers failed.
5. Human factors: classify errors (slip, lapse, mistake, violation) and ask what made them likely. A **just-culture** test separates blameless error and at-risk behaviour from reckless conduct — discipline is rarely the corrective action.

## 5. Corrective actions
- For each root cause, an action that changes the system; rank by the hierarchy of controls.
- SMART: owner, due date, success measure.
- Look across: where else could this happen (other sites, shifts, similar equipment)?
- Verify effectiveness later (e.g. 3–6 months), not just completion.

## 6. Report and learn
Report: summary, sequence, causes by level, actions, lessons. Share an anonymised safety alert. Update risk assessments and procedures. Track trends with near misses — they are the free lessons.

## Gotchas
- Stopping at "operator error" or "failed to follow procedure".
- Investigator from the same line management judging their own decisions.
- Leading questions contaminating witness accounts.
- Actions that are only "retrain" and "remind".

## Verify
- [ ] Scene preserved and photographed before disturbance.
- [ ] Regulator and other notifications made within the legal deadlines.
- [ ] Timeline built from multiple evidence sources.
- [ ] Each causal factor traced to an organisational cause.
- [ ] Actions mapped to root causes, owned, dated and checked for effectiveness.
