# Workplace risk assessment: hazard ID, risk matrix and hierarchy of controls

**Goal:** a risk assessment that actually reduces harm — not a form filed to satisfy an
auditor. Almost every OHS regime (UK Management Regulations, EU Framework Directive,
Australian WHS laws, OSHA's general duty clause (OSH Act s.5(a)(1): employers must provide a workplace free from recognized hazards likely to cause death or serious physical harm, confirmed) and programme guidance, ISO 45001 clause
6.1 (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)) expects some version of this cycle; the legal wording and required records
**vary by jurisdiction**.

## 1. Scope it
One assessment per **activity or task** (e.g. "replacing pallet racking beams"), not per
building. List who is exposed: employees, contractors, visitors, the public, and
vulnerable groups (new and young workers, pregnant workers, lone workers, people with
disabilities).

## 2. Find the hazards — go and look
- **Walk the task with the people who do it.** Watch it done; ask "what's the worst
  thing that's happened or nearly happened here?"
- Break the task into steps (job safety analysis / JHA) and ask at each step:
  struck by/against, caught in/between, fall from height, slip/trip, manual handling,
  electrical, stored energy, hazardous substances, noise/vibration, heat/cold,
  vehicles, fire/explosion, biological, psychosocial (violence, fatigue, stress).
- Use existing data: incident and near-miss logs, first-aid records, safety data
  sheets, manufacturer manuals, absence data, previous inspections.
- Include **non-routine work**: maintenance, cleaning, breakdowns, start-up/shutdown —
  where a disproportionate share of serious incidents happen.

## 3. Rate the risk
A typical 5×5 matrix: likelihood (1 rare – 5 almost certain) × severity (1 first aid –
5 fatality/permanent disability) = score 1–25, banded low/medium/high/intolerable.
Calibration rules:
- Rate the **realistic worst credible outcome**, not the most frequent.
- Define bands in words your organisation agrees ("likely = has happened here in the
  last year") or two assessors will score the same task 6 and 16.
- Rate **current** risk with existing controls, then **residual** risk after new controls.
- Don't let a low likelihood score talk you out of controlling a fatal hazard; high
  severity hazards deserve controls even when rare.

## 4. Choose controls — the hierarchy
1. **Eliminate** (don't do it: ground-level assembly, remote inspection by drone).
2. **Substitute** (less hazardous chemical, smaller pack sizes, lower voltage).
3. **Engineering** (guarding, interlocks, local exhaust ventilation, guardrails,
   lifting aids).
4. **Administrative** (procedures, permits, training, signage, rotation, supervision).
5. **PPE** (five levels and order confirmed against the NIOSH hierarchy of controls) — last line, because it depends on fit, wear and behaviour every time.
Usually you combine levels. Ask whether each control is reasonably practicable (the
UK/Australian "SFAIRP/ALARP" test (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) weighs risk against cost, time and effort;
other regimes frame it differently).

## 5. Record and act
Minimum record: task, hazards, who's harmed and how, existing controls, risk rating,
further actions, **owner and due date**, residual rating, assessor, date, review date.
Communicate it to the people doing the work — a toolbox talk, not just a shared drive.
Review after any incident, change of process/equipment/people, or when controls may not be working or workers report problems (UK HSE five steps: identify hazards, assess risks, control, record, review; employers of 5 or more must record significant findings, confirmed on hse.gov.uk). Fixed review intervals such as annually are (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).

## Gotchas
- Generic, copy-pasted assessments are a common finding after serious incidents.
- Specific hazards often have their own mandated assessments (hazardous substances,
  manual handling, noise, display screens, confined spaces, fire). Link to them.
- Actions without owners don't happen; track them like any other work.
- Assessments are disclosable after an incident (jurisdiction-dependent (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)); write facts, not opinions.

## Verify
- [ ] Assessed the task in person with workers, including non-routine steps.
- [ ] Every hazard has a rating with agreed band definitions.
- [ ] Controls considered top-down; PPE is not the only control for high risks.
- [ ] Each action has an owner and date; residual risk recorded.
- [ ] Workers briefed; review date set.
