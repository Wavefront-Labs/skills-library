# Workplace risk assessment and the hierarchy of controls

**Goal:** identify what could hurt people at work, judge how likely and how serious it
is, put in controls that reduce the risk, and record them so they can be reviewed.

Legal anchor (Great Britain): regulation 3 of the Management of Health and Safety at Work
Regulations 1999 requires every employer to make a "suitable and sufficient" assessment of
the risks to employees at work and to other people affected by the undertaking, to
identify the measures needed to comply with the relevant statutory provisions. The
assessment must be reviewed if there is reason to suspect it is no longer valid. Other
regimes (the US, EU, Australia, ISO 45001) differ in detail: check your local duties.

HSE's five steps: identify hazards, assess the risks, control the risks, record your
findings, review the controls.

## 1. Identify hazards
- Look at how people work and how plant and equipment are used, what chemicals and
  substances are used, safe and unsafe work practices, and the state of the premises.
- Use accident and ill-health records to find less obvious hazards.
- Include non-routine operations such as maintenance, cleaning or changes in production cycles.
- Include hazards to health: manual handling, chemicals, work-related stress.
- For each hazard, consider how employees, contractors, visitors or the public might be
  harmed, including workers with particular requirements (young workers, migrant
  workers, new or expectant mothers, people with disabilities).
- Involve employees; they usually have good ideas.

## 2. Assess the risks
Decide how likely it is that someone could be harmed and how serious it could be. For
each hazard decide who might be harmed and how, what is already being done to control
it, what further action is needed, who will carry it out and by when.

## 3. Control the risks: the NIOSH hierarchy
NIOSH describes five levels in preferred order of general effectiveness:
1. **Elimination:** remove the hazard at the source; best used at design stage.
2. **Substitution:** a safer alternative; compare the new risks of the substitute with the original.
3. **Engineering controls:** reduce or prevent contact with the hazard (modified equipment
   or workspace, barriers, ventilation). The most effective are part of the original
   design, block the hazard at source, cannot be bypassed and need minimal user action.
4. **Administrative controls:** work practices that reduce duration, frequency or intensity
   of exposure (training, job rotation, rest breaks, limiting access, adjusting line speeds).
5. **PPE:** gloves, safety glasses, hearing protection, hard hats, respirators. Do not rely
   on PPE alone when other effective controls are available; it works only when used
   correctly and consistently, and a PPE programme should include hazard assessment,
   selection, inspection and replacement, training and monitoring.
Elimination, substitution and engineering controls work without significant human
interaction; administrative controls and PPE need ongoing effort from workers and
supervisors, and are useful while other controls are being implemented.

**Reasonably practicable (HSE):** you are not expected to eliminate all risks, but must do
everything reasonably practicable, balancing the level of risk against the measures needed
to control it in terms of money, time or trouble.

## 4. Record and review
HSE: if you employ 5 or more people, you must record significant findings: the hazards,
who might be harmed and how, and what you are doing to control the risks. Do not rely
purely on paperwork; control the risks in practice. Review controls to check they work,
and when they may no longer be effective, when staff, process, substances or equipment
change, or after problems, accidents or near misses. Update the record.

## Gotchas
- Generic assessments copied between sites.
- Jumping to training or PPE because they are cheap, when higher controls are available.
- Actions with no owner or date.

## Verify
- [ ] Non-routine and maintenance tasks included.
- [ ] Vulnerable groups considered.
- [ ] Each hazard has controls chosen from the top of the hierarchy where possible.
- [ ] Actions have owners and dates; review triggers set.
- [ ] Significant findings recorded (required for 5 or more employees in GB).
