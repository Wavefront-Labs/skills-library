# Contractor prequalification and multi-employer site coordination

**Goal:** when several employers work on one site, make sure nobody's hazards hurt anybody
else's people and that every contractor is competent, controlled and supervised — without
the client either abdicating responsibility or micromanaging. Models: GB (general duties to
non-employees and to cooperate/coordinate; CDM 2015 for construction with defined client,
principal designer and principal contractor roles) and US (OSHA's multi-employer citation
policy: creating, exposing, correcting and controlling employers can each be cited). Legal
role definitions and notification thresholds differ elsewhere — check.

## 1. Plan the work and define roles
- Scope the job and its hazards before tendering; the client knows the site hazards (asbestos, live services, process risks) and must share them.
- Name the **controlling/coordinating party**: principal contractor (CDM), host employer, or the site owner. Write it into contracts.
- Construction in GB: check whether the project is notifiable (duration/person-day thresholds — check current figures) and that a construction phase plan exists before work starts.

## 2. Prequalify
| Area | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Safety management | Policy, risk assessment examples, procedures for the work type |
| Performance | Injury rates (e.g. TRIR/EMR in US, RIDDOR history in GB), enforcement notices, prosecutions, last 3 years |
| Competence | Qualifications, licences/cards (e.g. CSCS in UK, OSHA 10/30 in US), supervisors' experience |
| Insurance | Employer's liability / workers' compensation, public liability |
| Subcontracting | How they select and control their own subcontractors |
Third-party schemes (SSIP members in UK; ISNetworld, Avetta in US/international) reduce duplication but are a floor — add job-specific checks for high-risk work. Scale effort to risk: a window cleaner is not a demolition contractor.

## 3. Contract and pre-start
- Contract terms: comply with site rules, provide RAMS, notify incidents, right to stop work and remove personnel, subcontracting needs approval.
- Review **RAMS (risk assessment and method statements)** for this job: specific, sequenced, with named competent persons, rescue plans and plant certificates. Reject generic ones.
- Pre-start meeting: interfaces, permits, isolations, welfare, emergency arrangements, communication lines.

## 4. On site
1. **Site induction** for every worker (site hazards, rules, emergency, reporting) with a check of understanding and a record/ID badge.
2. **Daily coordination**: start-of-shift meeting or plan-of-the-day; map **simultaneous operations** (SIMOPS) — e.g. hot work below scaffolding, lifting over work areas.
3. **Permit-to-work** for high-risk activities (hot work, confined space, live services, excavation, lifting, work at height), issued by the site's authorised people, cross-checked for conflicts.
4. **Monitoring**: planned inspections and audits of contractor work against their RAMS; escalate deviations — coach, stop, remove.
5. Sign-in/out for roll-call.

## 5. Close-out and review
- Hand-back of areas and isolations; as-built and safety information to the client.
- Performance review: incidents, observations, compliance with RAMS, cooperation. Feed into the approved contractor list (rate, retain, remove).

## Gotchas
- "They're the experts" — handing over without any monitoring (legal exposure for the client/controlling employer remains).
- Prequalification paperwork never compared with who actually turns up.
- Sub-subcontractors arriving unvetted.
- Two contractors each assuming the other provided edge protection or a fire watch.

## Verify
- [ ] Coordinating party named in contracts; client hazard information shared.
- [ ] Prequalification scaled to risk and recorded.
- [ ] RAMS job-specific and approved before start.
- [ ] Every worker inducted; daily coordination and permits cover SIMOPS.
- [ ] Contractor performance reviewed and approved list updated.
