# Contractor prequalification, site induction and multi-employer coordination

**Goal:** contractors do the work safely on your site, and nobody's work endangers
anyone else's — because shared sites are where responsibilities fall into gaps.

## Know the legal frame
Most regimes put duties on **both** client/host and contractor, and expect them to
cooperate and coordinate: e.g. Australian WHS "overlapping duties" between PCBUs; OSHA's
multi-employer citation policy (creating, exposing, correcting and controlling
employers); the UK CDM 2015 regulations for construction (client, principal designer,
principal contractor); EU Framework Directive cooperation duties. **The exact allocation
varies by jurisdiction** — get it written into contracts.

## 1. Prequalification (before award)
Scale with risk of the work. Ask for and check:
- safety policy, management system, and named competent safety advice;
- injury and enforcement history (rates, prosecutions, notices) — trend, not one number;
- insurance (employer's/workers' comp and public liability) and licences (electrical,
  gas, asbestos, scaffolding, crane etc.);
- training/competency records for the people actually coming;
- sample risk assessments and method statements for similar work;
- subcontracting arrangements — who will really be on site?
Third-party schemes (e.g. CHAS/SafeContractor in the UK, ISNetworld or Avetta in North
America) reduce paperwork but **don't replace** job-specific review.

## 2. Pre-start: job-specific planning
- Share **your** site hazard information with them (asbestos register, live services,
  process hazards, traffic routes, other works happening).
- Require **RAMS** (risk assessment and method statement; called **SWMS** for
  Australian high-risk construction work). Review for the actual task and site: who,
  what equipment, sequence, isolations, permits, exclusion zones, rescue plan. Reject
  generic ones.
- Agree permits needed under your permit-to-work system and who issues them.
- Name the contractor's on-site supervisor and your contract owner/host.

## 3. Site induction and access
Site-specific induction for every individual (not just the supervisor): emergency
arrangements, site rules, hazards, permits, reporting, welfare. Issue access only after
induction; check competency cards at the gate for licensed tasks. Language barriers:
use interpreters or translated material.

## 4. Coordination during work
- **Daily coordination meeting** (or permit board review) for all contractors on site:
  what's happening where today, conflicts, lifts, isolations, hot work, road closures.
- Manage **simultaneous operations (SIMOPS)**: e.g. no hot work near a tank cleaning
  job; no overhead work above another crew.
- A single traffic management plan and shared welfare/first-aid arrangements.
- Clarify who leads in an emergency on a multi-contractor site.

## 5. Monitoring and close-out
- Field inspections and permit audits against the agreed RAMS; stop work for serious
  deviations. Record observations and close them out with the contractor.
- Incidents involving contractors are investigated jointly.
- **Post-job performance review** feeds the approved-contractor list: safety, quality,
  cooperation. Poor performance means re-qualification or removal.

## Gotchas
- "We hired a competent contractor" doesn't transfer all your duties.
- Sub-subcontractors arrive unvetted; require approval for any subcontracting.
- Contractors on short jobs (an afternoon) still get inductions and permit control.
- Labour-hire/agency workers often need host-employer training too.

## Verify
- [ ] Prequalification proportional to risk, including licences and subcontractors.
- [ ] Job-specific RAMS/SWMS reviewed against site hazards and accepted in writing.
- [ ] Every worker inducted before access; licences checked.
- [ ] Daily coordination covers SIMOPS, permits and emergency leadership.
- [ ] Field monitoring and post-job review recorded.
