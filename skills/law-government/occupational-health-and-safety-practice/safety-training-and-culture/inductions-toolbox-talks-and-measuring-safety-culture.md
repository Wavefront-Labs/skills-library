# Inductions, toolbox talks and measuring safety culture

**Goal:** people know how to work safely, feel able to speak up, and leaders act on
what they hear — measured, not assumed.

## 1. Training needs and the matrix
- Start from the risk assessments: each control that relies on people knowing or doing
  something implies a training need.
- Build a **training matrix**: roles down the side, courses across the top, cells
  showing required / held / expiry date. Include legally required items (first aid,
  forklift, work at height, confined space, asbestos awareness, etc. — the list
  **varies by jurisdiction** and sector).
- **Competence ≠ attendance.** For high-risk tasks, add a practical assessment by a
  competent person, and record it.

## 2. Inductions
Day-one essentials, short and practical (people retain little from a two-hour slide deck):
- emergency procedures, assembly point, first aiders, how to report incidents and hazards;
- site rules, PPE and where to get it, restricted areas, permits;
- the top 3–5 hazards of *their* job and the controls;
- who their supervisor is and the **stop-work** right;
- a walk around the area, then a quick check of understanding (questions, not a signature).
Follow with job-specific training and buddying; new starters are over-represented in
injuries in many datasets.

## 3. Toolbox talks (tailgate / pre-start)
- 5–15 minutes, at the work location, at shift start.
- One topic, tied to today's work or a recent incident/near miss.
- **Ask, don't lecture:** "What could go wrong on this lift today?" Get the crew to
  identify hazards and controls.
- Record topic, date, attendees, and any concerns raised — and close those out visibly.
- Rotate who leads; supervisors and workers, not only the safety officer.

## 4. Behaviour and leadership practices
- **Safety conversations / leadership walkarounds:** managers go to the work, observe,
  ask what makes the job hard or risky, and fix something. Track actions.
- **Behaviour-based safety (BBS)** programmes (peer observation checklists, feedback)
  can help but are criticised when they focus on worker behaviour while ignoring
  conditions and management decisions. Pair observations with fixing system causes,
  and never use them for discipline.
- **Stop-work authority:** written policy, explicit protection from reprisal, and
  managers publicly thanking people who use it.
- **Just culture:** consistent response to error vs. at-risk vs. reckless behaviour,
  so reporting isn't punished.

## 5. Measuring culture and climate
- **Safety climate surveys** (validated instruments, e.g. NOSACQ-50, or your own short
  pulse) covering management commitment, worker involvement, trust in reporting,
  peer norms. Survey anonymously; report results back with actions within weeks.
- **Maturity models** (e.g. the Hearts and Minds ladder: pathological → reactive →
  calculative → proactive → generative) for facilitated workshop discussion.
- Behavioural indicators: near-miss reports per headcount, % of reports closed with
  feedback, leadership walkarounds completed, stop-work calls, training currency.
- Rising near-miss reporting after an initiative is usually good news.

## Gotchas
- Incentives tied to injury-free days encourage under-reporting.
- Translate and adapt for literacy and language; use pictures and demonstrations.
- Contractors and temps need the same induction quality.

## Verify
- [ ] Training matrix derived from risk assessments, with expiry tracking.
- [ ] High-risk competence checked practically, not just by attendance.
- [ ] Inductions include stop-work right and a comprehension check.
- [ ] Toolbox talks are interactive and their concerns tracked to close.
- [ ] Culture measured with a survey and leading indicators; results fed back.
