# Workplace emergency plan: evacuation, drills and first response

**Goal:** when something goes wrong, everyone knows what to do in the first minutes,
everyone is accounted for, and the responders get what they need on arrival.

## 1. Scenario list (from your risk assessment, not a template)
Typical: fire, medical emergency, chemical spill/gas release, explosion, structural
failure, utility loss, severe weather/flood/earthquake, violent intruder/active threat,
bomb threat, confined-space or work-at-height rescue, pandemic. Rank by likelihood and
consequence; write a short procedure per scenario that applies.

**Rescue is part of the plan.** For confined spaces, work at height (suspension in a
harness becomes dangerous within minutes) and machinery entrapment you need an
on-site rescue capability; you cannot rely on public services arriving in time.

## 2. The plan's core content
Many regimes set minimums (e.g. OSHA 29 CFR 1910.38 for emergency action plans; fire
safety orders/codes elsewhere; **requirements vary by jurisdiction and building type**).
Cover:
- how emergencies are **reported and alarms raised** (and what each alarm sounds like);
- **evacuation**: routes, exits, refuges, assembly points (upwind, away from vehicle
  access), who stays to shut down critical processes and when they leave;
- **roles**: incident controller, wardens/marshals per zone, first aiders, people meeting
  emergency services with site plans and hazardous materials information;
- **accounting for people**: visitor sign-in, contractor lists, roll-call method at the
  assembly point, how "missing" is reported to the fire service;
- **PEEPs** (personal emergency evacuation plans) for people who need assistance —
  agreed with the person, including visitors' arrangements;
- **shelter-in-place** (chemical release, severe weather) and **lockdown** (violent
  intruder) — different from evacuation, and must not be confused in alarm signals;
- communications: call trees, mass notification, who talks to media and families;
- site plans marking utilities shut-offs, hazardous stores, fire equipment, AEDs.

## 3. First aid and medical response
Do a **first-aid needs assessment**: headcount, shift patterns, hazards, distance to
hospital, remote/lone workers. It sets number of trained first aiders, kit contents and
locations, eyewash/showers near corrosives, and whether to have an **AED**. Time to an
AED matters in cardiac arrest; place them where they can be reached quickly and check
them on the maker's schedule (a monthly visual check is common (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)).

## 4. Drills and exercises
- **Evacuation drills** at least annually is common practice, not set by OSHA 1910.38 (no drill interval there); local fire codes may set one (check), on different shifts, sometimes with a blocked exit.
  Time it, observe stragglers and door behaviour, check roll-call accuracy.
- **Tabletop exercises** for scenarios you can't drill live (active threat, major
  spill): walk leaders through an evolving scenario and test decisions and communications.
- Joint exercises with the local fire service for high-hazard sites.
- **Debrief** every drill and real event; update the plan within a set time.

## 5. Keep it alive
Review after changes (layout, occupancy, processes), after drills/incidents, and
annually. Train new staff at induction; refresh wardens. Keep printed copies at the
assembly point and security desk — not only on the network that may be down.

## Gotchas
- Assembly points too close to the building or in the fire-engine access route.
- Visitor and contractor lists not available at roll-call.
- Wardens who are always on day shift — check night and weekend cover.
- Lifts are not evacuation routes unless designed as evacuation lifts.

## Verify
- [ ] Scenario list derived from site risks; rescue arrangements where needed.
- [ ] Roles named for every shift; PEEPs agreed for those who need them.
- [ ] Roll-call method accounts for visitors and contractors.
- [ ] First-aid needs assessment done; AEDs checked.
- [ ] Drill held, timed and debriefed; plan updated.
