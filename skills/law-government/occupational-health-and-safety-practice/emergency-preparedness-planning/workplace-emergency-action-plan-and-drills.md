# Workplace emergency action plan and drills

**Goal:** when something goes wrong, everyone knows what to do, can get out (or stay put)
safely, and the first response contains the harm until emergency services take over —
proven by drills, not assumed. Models: US OSHA (emergency action plans; written, kept in the workplace and available to employees; an employer with 10 or fewer employees may communicate it orally, per 1910.38(b)), England & Wales Fire Safety Order
(responsible person's fire risk assessment and emergency arrangements) and GB first-aid
rules. Site-specific regimes (major-hazard sites, healthcare, schools, mines) add more
detailed requirements — check for them.

## 1. Pick the scenarios
Start from the risk assessment and the location:
| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Fire/explosion | Building fire, hot-work fire, dust/gas explosion |
| Hazardous materials | Chemical spill, gas leak, toxic release from neighbour |
| Medical | Cardiac arrest, serious injury, mass casualty |
| Natural | Flood, severe storm, earthquake, heat |
| Security | Active threat/violence, bomb threat, civil disturbance |
| Utility/IT | Power loss, critical system failure (links to business continuity) |
| Task-specific | Confined-space rescue, work-at-height rescue (suspension), trench collapse |
Rank by likelihood × consequence; plan in depth for the top ones and generically for the rest. Never rely on emergency services for rescues they cannot reach in time (e.g. suspension trauma, confined spaces) — plan your own.

## 2. Write the plan (keep it usable)
1. **Raising the alarm:** how, by whom, what the signals mean (evacuate vs shelter vs lockdown).
2. **Roles:** incident controller, fire wardens/marshals per area, first aiders, spill team, person meeting emergency services, roll-call lead — with deputies on every shift.
3. **Evacuation:** routes and alternatives, refuges, assembly points (far enough and not blocking emergency vehicles), roll-call method (visitor and contractor sign-in included).
4. **Shelter-in-place and lockdown** procedures where relevant.
5. **People needing assistance:** personal emergency evacuation plans (PEEPs) for anyone who cannot self-evacuate; don't rely on the fire service to rescue from refuges.
6. **Critical shutdowns:** who isolates gas, process, power, and how.
7. **Communications:** contact list, external notifications, family/media lines.
8. **Floor plans** posted with "you are here", exits, extinguishers, alarm points, first-aid kits, AED.

## 3. First-aid and medical arrangements
Do a first-aid needs assessment: headcount, hazards, shifts, remoteness, distance to hospital. Decide number of trained first aiders, kit contents, AED placement (aim for a few minutes' reach), eyewash/safety showers where corrosives are used (within seconds of the hazard; US practice follows ANSI Z358.1, confirm the exact distance and flow in that standard (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)). OSHA expects trained responders where medical help is not in near proximity.

## 4. Drill programme
- Fire evacuation drill at least annually is a common practice, not an OSHA 1910.38 requirement (that section requires training and plan review, with no drill interval); some fire codes set intervals (check yours).
- Vary: unannounced, blocked exit, missing person, night shift.
- Tabletop exercises for scenarios you can't fully drill (active threat, toxic release) with managers and emergency services.
- Time it: alarm to all-clear-out, and roll-call completion.

## 5. After-action review
Within days: what was supposed to happen, what happened, why the difference, what to change. Update the plan, retrain, re-drill the weak point. Also review after real events, layout or occupancy changes, and annually.

## Gotchas
- Plans that name one person with no deputy for nights and holidays.
- Assembly point in the path of fire appliances or downwind of the chemical store.
- No roll-call for visitors and contractors.
- Never drilling the scenario most likely to kill (e.g. a rescue from height).

## Verify
- [ ] Scenarios derived from the risk assessment, including task-specific rescues.
- [ ] Every role has trained deputies on every shift.
- [ ] PEEPs exist for everyone who needs one.
- [ ] Drills held to plan, timed and reviewed with actions closed.
- [ ] Plan document, posted floor plans and contact lists current.
