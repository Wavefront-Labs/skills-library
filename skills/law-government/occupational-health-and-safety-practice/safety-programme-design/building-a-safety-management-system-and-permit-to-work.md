# Building a safety management system and permit-to-work

**Goal:** turn scattered rules into a working system — clear policy, accountable roles,
procedures people actually follow, controlled high-risk work and a feedback loop — scaled
to the organisation. Models: ISO 45001, HSE HSG65 (Plan-Do-Check-Act), OSHA Recommended
Practices and ANSI Z10. Legal minimums differ: e.g. GB requires a written policy with 5+
employees and consultation with workers; some US states mandate written injury and illness
prevention programmes (e.g. California IIPP) while federal OSHA mostly mandates
programme elements standard by standard; many countries mandate joint committees above a
headcount — check the local thresholds.

## 1. Plan: policy, roles, objectives
1. **Policy statement** signed by the top executive: commitment, objectives, consultation, legal compliance, continual improvement. One page.
2. **Organisation:** named responsibilities from board to supervisor to worker; competent safety advice (internal or external); who can stop work (everyone).
3. **Arrangements** index: how each risk area is managed (risk assessment, contractors, training, maintenance, emergencies, incidents, health surveillance) — the document map.
4. **Objectives** with measurable targets and an annual plan.
Start from a gap analysis against ISO 45001 clauses or HSG65 — and the risk profile — rather than writing 60 procedures nobody needs.

## 2. Do: safe work procedures
- Write procedures for high-risk and non-routine tasks first (risk assessment drives this).
- Format: purpose, scope, hazards, controls, step-by-step with key safety points, PPE, emergency actions, sign-off. Keep it to what the worker needs on the job; photos beat paragraphs.
- Test the draft by watching someone do the task with it.
- Version control, review date, owner.

## 3. Permit-to-work design
Use for work where normal controls are suspended or extra coordination is needed: hot work, confined spaces, work on isolated plant/electrical systems, work at height in hazardous areas, excavation, line breaking.
Elements of a sound PTW system:
| Element | Design point |
|---|---|
| Roles | Issuer (authorised, competent), performing authority, area authority, isolating authority |
| Hazard assessment | Task-specific, attached or referenced |
| Isolations | Cross-referenced to lockout/tagout certificate; verified dead/zero energy |
| Precautions | Gas testing results, fire watch, barriers, standby person |
| Communication | Briefing signed by every worker on the permit |
| Validity | Time-limited (often one shift); revalidation rules |
| Interaction | Conflicting permits identified on a permit board/register |
| Hand-back | Work complete, site safe, isolations removed in order, signed |
Audit live permits regularly — most PTW failures are "permit in drawer, work elsewhere".

## 4. Consultation and committees
- Safety committee: management and worker representatives (often at least equal worker representation where law requires a joint committee), terms of reference, quarterly or monthly meetings, minutes with actions and owners.
- Involve elected/union safety reps in risk assessments, procedure changes and incident reviews.
- Give workers a simple route to raise hazards and see the response.

## 5. Check and act
- **Leading indicators:** inspections completed, actions closed on time, permits audited, training completion, near misses reported.
- **Lagging:** injury rates (e.g. TRIR/LTIFR), ill-health, enforcement.
- Internal audit annually against the system; **management review** by top management decides changes.
- Learn from incidents (see incident-investigation skill) and feed changes back into procedures.

## Gotchas
- A system written for certification, not for the work.
- Procedures longer than the task.
- Permit issuers who never visit the job.
- Measuring only injury rates — they reward under-reporting.

## Verify
- [ ] Signed policy and named responsibilities exist and are communicated.
- [ ] High-risk tasks each have a tested procedure.
- [ ] PTW covers isolations, gas testing, conflicts, validity and hand-back.
- [ ] Committee meets with minuted actions closing.
- [ ] Management review held with decisions recorded.
