# Workstation and manual-handling ergonomics assessment

**Goal:** find the jobs most likely to cause musculoskeletal disorders, quantify the risk
with a recognised tool, and redesign the work so the risk falls — not just tell people to
"lift properly". Models: GB (Manual Handling Operations Regulations, DSE Regulations,
HSE MAC/RAPP/ART tools) and US (no federal ergonomics standard — OSHA relies on the
General Duty Clause and guidelines; some states have rules; NIOSH tools are the common
method). Check local legal duties; the tools work everywhere.

## 1. Prioritise
- Use injury/illness records, first-aid logs, worker discomfort surveys (body-map questionnaires such as the Nordic questionnaire) and observation to rank jobs.
- Flag risk factors: force, awkward posture, repetition, duration, vibration, contact stress, cold, pace set by machine, poor recovery time.
- Talk to the workers about the hardest part of the job.

## 2. Pick the right tool
| Situation | Tool |
|---|---|
| Two-handed lifting/lowering | Revised NIOSH Lifting Equation (RWL, Lifting Index); HSE MAC |
| Pushing/pulling | Liberty Mutual (Snook) tables; HSE RAPP |
| Repetitive upper-limb tasks | HSE ART; Strain Index; OCRA |
| Whole-body posture snapshots | REBA (whole body), RULA (upper limb) |
| Computer work | DSE workstation checklist |
Video the task to score it accurately and to show before/after.

## 3. Manual handling: NIOSH lifting equation in practice
RWL = LC × HM × VM × DM × AM × FM × CM, with load constant 23 kg (51 lb) multiplied by reduction factors for horizontal distance, vertical height, travel distance, asymmetry (twist), frequency and coupling (grip). Lifting Index = actual load ÷ RWL.
- LI ≤ 1: most healthy workers protected.
- LI > 1: increased risk; LI > 3 commonly treated as high risk for many workers.
Measure at origin and destination; if significant control is needed at destination, assess both. The equation does not cover one-handed, seated, kneeling, team or very fast lifts — use another method.
GB route: first avoid hazardous manual handling, then assess (MAC colour bands: green/amber/red/purple for each factor), then reduce.

## 4. DSE / workstation assessment
Check, with the user in their usual posture:
- Chair: adjustable seat height so forearms are roughly horizontal at the keyboard; lumbar support; feet flat (footrest if not).
- Screen: top at or slightly below eye level, about an arm's length away, perpendicular to windows; two screens set by usage.
- Keyboard/mouse close, wrists neutral; laptop on a stand with separate keyboard for long use.
- Breaks/task variation; eye-test entitlement where the law provides it (GB DSE users).
- Home and hybrid workers: self-assessment checklist plus follow-up for problems.

## 5. Choose controls (hierarchy again)
1. Eliminate the lift/reach: conveyors, gravity feeds, redesign layout so work is between knee and shoulder height, close to the body.
2. Mechanical aids: hoists, vacuum lifters, trolleys with good castors, adjustable-height benches, tool balancers.
3. Load changes: smaller/lighter packs, handles.
4. Work organisation: rotation that actually changes muscle groups, rest breaks, pace control.
5. Training last — technique training alone has weak evidence of reducing injury.
Re-score after the change and record the reduction.

## Gotchas
- Using the lifting equation outside its assumptions.
- Back belts or "kinetic lifting" training as the main control.
- Rotation between jobs that load the same body parts.
- DSE checklist done by the user with no follow-up on issues raised.

## Verify
- [ ] Jobs prioritised from records and worker input.
- [ ] Tool matched to the task type, scored from video/measurements.
- [ ] Controls address the specific risk factors that scored highest.
- [ ] Post-change re-score shows reduced risk.
- [ ] Findings and actions recorded with owners and review date.
