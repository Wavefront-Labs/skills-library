# Workstation and manual-handling ergonomic assessment

**Goal:** find the tasks most likely to cause musculoskeletal disorders (MSDs) and fix
the work, not the worker. MSDs are among the most common occupational illnesses in most
industrialised economies.

## 1. Find where to look
- Injury/first-aid logs, absence data, early-reporting of discomfort, and a short
  **body-map discomfort survey** (mark where it hurts, how often, which task).
- Risk factors to flag on a walk-through: **force** (heavy lifts, pushing, pinch grips),
  **posture** (reaching, twisting, bending, overhead work, static postures),
  **repetition** (cycles under ~30 s, same motion most of the shift), **duration**,
  **vibration**, cold, and psychosocial pressure (pace, targets).
- Talk to workers; they usually know which job hurts.

## 2. Display screen / office workstation
Check, with the person in their normal position:
- **Chair:** feet flat (or footrest), thighs roughly horizontal, lumbar support at the
  small of the back, armrests not blocking the desk.
- **Desk height** so elbows are about 90° with shoulders relaxed and wrists straight.
- **Screen:** an arm's length away, top of the screen at or slightly below eye level
  (lower for bifocal/varifocal wearers), perpendicular to windows to cut glare.
- Keyboard and mouse close, at elbow height; laptops need a separate keyboard, mouse
  and raised screen for sustained use.
- Breaks/changes of activity; sit-stand desks help only if people actually alternate.
Many jurisdictions require DSE assessments for habitual users (e.g. UK DSE Regulations)
and include home workers — **requirements vary**.

## 3. Manual handling
- First ask: **can the handling be avoided** (conveyor, pallet truck, hoist, delivering to
  point of use, smaller packs)?
- **NIOSH Revised Lifting Equation** for two-handed lifts: Recommended Weight Limit
  RWL = 23 kg × HM × VM × DM × AM × FM × CM (horizontal distance, vertical height,
  vertical travel, asymmetry, frequency and coupling multipliers, each ≤1).
  **Lifting Index** LI = load ÷ RWL; LI > 1 indicates elevated risk for some workers,
  LI > 3 high risk for most. Use the published tables for the multipliers.
- Quick screening tools: the UK HSE **MAC** (lifting, carrying, team handling), **RAPP**
  (pushing/pulling) and **ART** (repetitive upper-limb tasks) — colour-coded scores
  that are easy to explain to managers.
- Posture snapshot tools: **REBA** (whole body) and **RULA** (upper limb) — score a
  photographed worst-case posture.
- Push/pull: measure initial and sustained force with a force gauge; maintain wheels and
  floors.

## 4. Controls — redesign first
1. Eliminate/mechanise (lift assists, vacuum lifters, turntables).
2. Redesign the workplace: work heights between knee and shoulder (ideally
   hip–elbow) for frequent lifts, reduce reach distances, tilt bins, adjustable fixtures.
3. Reduce load: smaller packaging, two-person lifts as a last resort.
4. Job design: rotation across genuinely different muscle groups, micro-breaks,
   realistic pace, gradual onboarding for new/returning workers.
5. Training in handling technique supports — but rarely solves — the problem.
Pilot changes with the workers, then re-score.

## Gotchas
- Scores from tools are risk *indicators*, not safe/unsafe verdicts; apply judgement.
- Back belts and wrist splints are not evidence-based preventive controls.
- Pregnant workers, young workers and those returning from injury need individual assessment.

## Verify
- [ ] Priority tasks chosen from data and worker input, not guesswork.
- [ ] DSE users assessed in their actual position (including home workers where required).
- [ ] High-risk lifts scored with NIOSH/MAC-type tools; LI or scores recorded.
- [ ] Controls start with elimination/redesign; tasks re-scored after change.
