# Union recognition, bargaining duty and ULPs

**Goal:** advise unions or employers through organising, bargaining and disputes
without committing — or overlooking — an unfair labour practice.

## 1. The rights (US private sector, NLRA)
**Section 7**: employees may organise, bargain collectively and engage in
**protected concerted activity** for mutual aid or protection — union or not
(e.g. co-workers jointly complaining about pay on social media). Supervisors,
managers, independent contractors, agricultural and domestic workers are excluded.
Public-sector employees fall under state law; railways/airlines under the Railway Labor Act.

## 2. Unfair labour practices (s.8)
Employer (8(a)): interference, restraint or coercion (threats, interrogation,
promises, surveillance — "TIPS"); domination of a union; discrimination for union
activity; retaliation for NLRB participation; refusal to bargain.
Union (8(b)): coercing employees, causing discrimination, refusal to bargain,
secondary boycotts, certain picketing.
**ULP charges must be filed within 6 months** (s.10(b)). The NLRB Regional Office
investigates; the General Counsel issues complaints; ALJ → Board → court of appeals.

## 3. Recognition
- **Voluntary recognition** on majority showing (cards).
- **Petition for election** (with a showing of interest from a substantial number of employees in the unit, 29 CFR 102.61(a)(7)) → hearing on the
  **appropriate unit** (community of interest) → secret-ballot election.
- *Cemex* (2023) (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked): when a union demands recognition with majority support, the
  employer must recognise or promptly petition; ULPs that would set aside the
  election can lead to a bargaining order. In March 2026 the Sixth Circuit (Brown-Forman v. NLRB, 2-1) declined to enforce a
  Cemex bargaining order, holding the Board exceeded its adjudicatory authority in creating
  it (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked); check the circuit and current status. Board doctrine also shifts with Board composition.
- Great Britain: statutory recognition via the CAC (Schedule A1) for employers with
  21 or more workers (Sch A1 para 7) — a declaration on majority membership (para 22) or a
  ballot won by a majority of the workers voting (para 29(3), as substituted from 6 April 2026
  by the Employment Rights Act 2025; the earlier test also required 40% of the unit).

## 4. The duty to bargain
- Meet at reasonable times, confer in good faith; no obligation to agree.
- **Mandatory subjects**: wages, hours, other terms and conditions — must bargain
  before changing. **Unilateral change** to a mandatory subject without bargaining to
  impasse is a per se violation (*Katz*). Permissive subjects can be proposed but
  not insisted on to impasse.
- Information requests: employers must provide relevant information (e.g. payroll
  data; financials when pleading inability to pay).
- Impasse: after genuine deadlock, employer may implement its last offer —
  "impasse" is fact-intensive and often litigated.
- First-contract and successor-employer obligations have their own rules.

## 5. Strikes and lockouts
- **Economic strikers** can be permanently replaced (*Mackay Radio*) but not fired;
  **ULP strikers** are entitled to reinstatement.
- No-strike clauses and contract-term limitations apply; health-care institutions
  need 10 days' notice (s.8(g)).
- Lockouts are lawful in support of a bargaining position after impasse in many circumstances.
- UK: industrial action is protected only if the union follows ballot and notice
  rules (TULRCA Part V) (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) — technical defects remove immunity. *Uncertain:* thresholds
  under the Employment Rights Act/Bill reforms may have changed; verify.

## Gotchas
- Supervisor statements during a campaign are attributed to the employer — train
  them first.
- Handbook rules (confidentiality, social media, civility) can violate s.7 if they
  chill protected activity; standard set by *Stericycle* (2023) (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) — check current law.
- Uncertain: NLRB precedents swing between administrations.
