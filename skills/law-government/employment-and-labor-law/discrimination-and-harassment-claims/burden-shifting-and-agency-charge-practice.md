# Burden shifting and agency charge practice

**Goal:** frame or defend a discrimination, harassment or retaliation claim under the
right legal test, inside the administrative deadlines that gate court access.

## 1. Administrative exhaustion (US federal)
- File a **charge with the EEOC** within **180 days** of the act, extended to
  **300 days** where a state or local agency enforces a law banning employment discrimination on the same basis
  (for age claims the extension needs a state law and agency; a local law alone does not extend it).
  Federal-sector employees have a different process (contact an EEO counsellor within 45 days of the discrimination).
- Each discrete act (termination, failure to promote) has its own clock;
  hostile-environment claims are timely if one act falls in the period (*Morgan*, 2002). (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)
- After a **right-to-sue notice**, suit must be filed within **90 days**.
- Claims in court must be "like or related" to the charge — draft the charge broadly
  and accurately. (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)
- Great Britain: you must notify Acas before making an employment-tribunal claim (taking part in
  conciliation is voluntary). Equality Act 2010 s.123(1) now gives **6 months** starting with the date of the act
  (substituted by the Employment Rights Act 2025 with effect from 1 October 2026; the earlier limit was 3 months), or
  such other period as the tribunal thinks just and equitable. Check the transitional provisions for acts before
  that date and how early conciliation affects time.

## 2. Disparate treatment — McDonnell Douglas (circumstantial cases) (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)
1. **Prima facie case:** protected class; qualified; adverse action; circumstances
   suggesting discrimination (e.g. replaced by someone outside the class, comparators
   treated better).
2. Employer articulates a **legitimate, non-discriminatory reason** (burden of production only).
3. Employee shows the reason is **pretext** (inconsistent explanations, departures
   from policy, comparator evidence, timing, biased remarks by decision-makers).
Note *Muldrow* (2024): a transfer or other term-and-condition change need show only
"some harm", not significant harm. Mixed-motive and "but-for" causation standards
differ by statute (ADEA and Title VII retaliation require but-for causation).

**Great Britain** uses a two-stage reverse burden (Equality Act s.136): facts from
which discrimination could be inferred → employer must prove it did not discriminate.

## 3. Disparate impact (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)
Neutral practice with disproportionate effect on a protected group → employer shows
job-relatedness/business necessity → plaintiff shows a less discriminatory
alternative. Statistics and expert evidence drive these. (GB equivalent: indirect
discrimination with a proportionality justification.)

## 4. Harassment (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)
- **Hostile environment:** unwelcome conduct based on protected characteristic,
  severe *or* pervasive enough to alter conditions — judged objectively and subjectively.
- **Employer liability:** supervisor harassment resulting in tangible action → strict
  liability; otherwise the **Faragher/Ellerth** affirmative defence (reasonable care to
  prevent and correct + employee unreasonably failed to use the procedure). Co-worker
  harassment → negligence (knew or should have known, failed to act).
- Policies, training and prompt investigations are the defence's evidence.

## 5. Retaliation (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)
Protected activity (complaint, charge, participation) → materially adverse action
(would dissuade a reasonable worker — *Burlington Northern*) → causal link (timing
plus other evidence). Retaliation is often easier to prove than the underlying
discrimination; counsel employers on it the moment a complaint arrives.

## Gotchas
- State laws often cover smaller employers, more characteristics and longer deadlines.
- Accommodation claims (disability, religion, pregnancy under the PWFA) are distinct
  from disparate treatment — analyse separately.
- Uncertain: causation standards and pleading rules keep evolving in the circuits;
  check current authority.
