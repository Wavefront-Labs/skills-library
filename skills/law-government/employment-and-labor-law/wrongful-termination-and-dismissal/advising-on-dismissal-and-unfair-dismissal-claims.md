# Advising on dismissal and unfair-dismissal claims

**Goal:** get terminations right before they happen, and assess claims after, using
the framework the jurisdiction actually applies.

## 1. Two different questions
- **Wrongful dismissal** (common law, contract): was the contract breached — usually
  by failing to give the contractual/statutory notice? Remedy ≈ pay for the notice
  period. Summary dismissal without notice needs a repudiatory breach (gross misconduct).
- **Unfair dismissal** (statute, e.g. GB): was the reason fair and the process
  reasonable? Separate claim, separate remedies.

## 2. US: at-will and its exceptions
Termination for any reason or none is lawful unless it falls into an exception (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked):
- **Statutory**: discrimination, retaliation, whistleblower statutes, FMLA, NLRA
  protected concerted activity, jury service, military service (USERRA).
- **Public policy** (most states): refusing to break the law, exercising a legal
  right (e.g. workers' compensation claim).
- **Implied contract**: handbook or assurances of job security (state-specific).
- **Good faith and fair dealing**: recognised in a minority of states.
- Mass layoffs: **WARN Act** — 60 days' notice for covered plant closings/mass layoffs (20 CFR 639.5, confirmed);
  several states have stricter "mini-WARN" laws.

## 3. Great Britain: unfair dismissal
1. **Eligibility**: employee status and usually 2 years' continuous service (no
   minimum for automatically unfair reasons such as whistleblowing, pregnancy, union
   activity). ERA 1996 s 108(1) currently says two years (checked on legislation.gov.uk). Employment Rights Act 2025 (c. 36) section 25 substitutes six months in s 108(1) and omits s 124 (the compensatory-award limit); SI 2026/559 reg 3 brings section 25 into force on 1 January 2027, subject to its transitional provisions (all confirmed on legislation.gov.uk). Until then two years applies: check the effective date of termination and read regulation 4.
2. **Potentially fair reason** (ERA s.98): capability, conduct, redundancy, statutory
   illegality, or "some other substantial reason".
3. **Reasonableness**: did the employer act within the **band of reasonable
   responses** (*Iceland Frozen Foods v Jones*, EAT 1982, confirmed on BAILII)? For misconduct, *British Home Stores v Burchell*: genuine belief,
   reasonable grounds, reasonable investigation.
4. **Procedure**: follow the Acas Code — investigate, written notice of allegations
   with evidence, hearing with right to be accompanied, decision, appeal. Unreasonable
   failure can adjust awards by up to 25% (TULRCA s 207A, confirmed).
5. **Redundancy**: genuine redundancy, fair selection pool and criteria, consultation
   (collective consultation duties at 20 or more redundancies at one establishment within 90 days, TULRCA s 188, confirmed; s 188(A1) now also refers to a "threshold number" under s 195A inserted by the 2025 Act, so re-check), search for
   alternative roles.
Remedies: basic award + compensatory award, reinstatement rarely; the compensatory-award limit in s 124 is omitted by the 2025 Act from 1 January 2027 (confirmed above).

## 4. Constructive dismissal
Employee resigns in response to the employer's repudiatory breach (e.g. pay cut,
demotion, breach of trust and confidence) and doesn't delay so long as to affirm the
contract (*Western Excavating v Sharp*, CA 1977, confirmed on BAILII). In the US, "constructive discharge" mostly matters
inside discrimination/retaliation claims — conditions so intolerable a reasonable
person would resign.

## 5. Pre-termination checklist (employer side)
- Documented, consistent reason; same treatment as comparators.
- Recent protected activity? (complaint, leave, injury claim) — high retaliation risk.
- Protected characteristics and accommodation history reviewed.
- Contract: notice, bonus/equity vesting, covenants, garden leave.
- Final pay timing rules (some US states require pay on the last day).
- Offer a release/settlement agreement only with lawful formalities.

## Gotchas
- A fair reason with an unfair process is still unfair dismissal in GB.
- Inconsistent explanations at termination become pretext evidence later.
- Uncertain: qualifying periods, caps and notice rules change; verify current figures.

## Verify
- [ ] Wrongful vs unfair (or at-will exception) analysed separately.
- [ ] Reason documented and consistent with comparators.
- [ ] Procedure matches the applicable code; appeal offered.
- [ ] Protected-activity and characteristic risks screened.
- [ ] Notice, final pay and collective-consultation/WARN duties checked.
