# Classification, overtime and pay audits

**Goal:** determine who is owed what for hours worked, and find exposure before a
plaintiff's lawyer does — or prove it if you are that lawyer.

## 1. Employee or contractor?
- **FLSA economic-realities test**: is the worker economically dependent on the
  business or in business for themselves? Factors: opportunity for profit/loss,
  investment, permanence, control, integral to the business, skill and initiative.
  The US DOL's guidance on this test has changed more than once: check the current
  rule and the controlling circuit's case law (not verified this review).
- **ABC test** (used by some states for some purposes, e.g. California; list of states unconfirmed, check each state's statute):
  presumed employee unless (A) free from control, (B) work outside the usual course of
  business, and (C) independently established trade.
- **UK**: three statuses — employee, *worker* (gets minimum wage, holiday pay) and
  self-employed (*Uber v Aslam* [2021] UKSC 5 (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)).
Label in the contract is not decisive anywhere.

## 2. Exempt or non-exempt (US FLSA)
White-collar exemptions (executive, administrative, professional, plus computer and
outside sales) generally require **salary basis + salary level + duties**.
- Salary level: **$684/week** in 29 CFR 541.600(a) as published in eCFR on 2026-10-15
  (the CFR cites 91 FR 27835, May 15, 2026); this figure has moved repeatedly, so re-check
  before relying on it. Many states (e.g. CA, NY, WA, CO) set higher thresholds (not verified here).
- Duties tests are about actual primary duty, not job title.
- Day-rate workers are not salaried even at high pay unless the salary-basis rules
  are met (*Helix Energy Solutions v. Hewitt*, US Supreme Court, 22 Feb 2023: day-rate pay
  qualifies only under 29 CFR 541.604(b), however high the pay).
- Improper deductions from salary can destroy the exemption.

## 3. Overtime math
FLSA: 1.5 × the **regular rate** for hours over 40 in a workweek (fixed, recurring
7-day period; no averaging across weeks (29 CFR 778.104)). The regular rate includes most
non-discretionary bonuses, shift differentials and commissions:
```
regular rate = total straight-time compensation for the week / total hours worked
OT due (hourly worker with bonus) = base OT + 0.5 × (bonus ÷ hours) × OT hours
```
Daily overtime exists in some states (California: 1.5x over 8 hours in a workday, double time over
12, and seventh-consecutive-day rules; DIR overtime FAQ). Compensable time includes pre/post-shift integral tasks, short breaks
(5 to about 20 min, 29 CFR 785.18), and often travel during the workday.

## 4. Litigation
- FLSA **collective actions** are opt-in (§ 216(b)); state-law claims often proceed as
  opt-out Rule 23 class actions — "hybrid" cases.
- Limitations: 2 years, 3 for wilful violations (29 U.S.C. 255(a)); liquidated (double) damages (216(b)) unless
  the employer shows good faith and reasonable grounds (260); mandatory reasonable fees for prevailing employees (216(b)).
- Arbitration agreements with class waivers are generally enforceable (*Epic
  Systems v. Lewis*, US Supreme Court, 21 May 2018), except where carved out (e.g. California PAGA representative claims, partly).

## 5. Pay-practice audit (under privilege)
Sample timekeeping vs. access logs; rounding rules; meal-break auto-deductions;
regular-rate inclusion of bonuses; exempt roles' actual duties; contractor roster;
final-pay timing; pay-stub contents; recordkeeping retention (29 CFR 516.5: payroll records 3 years).
Engage counsel so the audit is privileged; remediate prospectively and evaluate back pay.

## Gotchas
- Employees cannot waive FLSA rights by private agreement without DOL or court approval
  (circuit-specific rules).
- Auto-deducted meal breaks are a frequent collective-action trigger.
- Uncertain: thresholds and tests change often at federal and state level.

## Verify
- [ ] Each worker's status tested under every applicable test (federal + state).
- [ ] Exempt roles meet salary basis, level and actual duties.
- [ ] Regular rate includes non-discretionary pay.
- [ ] Workweek defined and state daily-overtime rules applied.
- [ ] Audit run under privilege with remediation plan.
