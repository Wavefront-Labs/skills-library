# Drafting employment contracts and restrictive covenants

**Goal:** documents that set clear terms, survive challenge, and don't accidentally
create rights the employer didn't intend.

## 1. Know the default regime first
- **US:** employment is presumptively **at-will** (Montana is the main statutory
  exception). Contracts mainly add terms (pay, equity, IP, covenants) and must be
  careful not to undercut at-will status ("permanent position", guaranteed tenure).
- **UK/EU/Commonwealth:** statutory minimum notice and unfair-dismissal protection
  after qualifying service; a written statement of key particulars is mandatory (in
  the UK, from day one). Contract notice can exceed but not undercut statutory minimums.

## 2. Contract checklist
Parties and start date; job title and duties (with flexibility clause); place of work
and remote rules; hours; pay, bonus (discretionary vs contractual — say which);
benefits; holiday; sickness pay; probation; notice; garden leave; confidentiality;
IP assignment (note statutory carve-outs for employee inventions in some US states,
e.g. California Labor Code § 2870); data protection notice; restrictive covenants;
governing law and forum; dispute resolution (arbitration where lawful); entire
agreement; variation.

## 3. Restrictive covenants
| Type | Typical enforceability |
|---|---|
| Confidentiality | generally enforceable; can't bar whistleblowing or protected disclosures |
| Non-solicitation of clients/staff | more readily enforced if limited to contacts the employee dealt with |
| Non-dealing | between the two |
| Non-compete | most scrutinised |
Rules of thumb (common-law reasonableness test): protect a **legitimate interest**
(trade secrets, client connections), no wider than necessary in **time, geography,
scope**, tailored to the role. Then check local law:
- Several US states ban or heavily restrict non-competes (California is the best known; others set income thresholds or sector limits; check the state statute). The FTC's 2024
  nationwide rule was set aside in court (Ryan, LLC v. FTC); the FTC voted on 5 Sept 2025 to dismiss its appeals
  and removed the rule from the CFR effective 12 Feb 2026, so state law governs.
- Draft covenants as cascading, severable clauses tailored to the role; confirm current
  England and Wales case law and any reform proposals on non-competes.
- Consideration: a mid-employment covenant may need fresh consideration in some states.

## 4. Handbooks and policies
State clearly that the handbook is **not contractual** (US: disclaimer, conspicuous)
unless you intend specific parts to be. Keep disciplinary and grievance procedures
consistent with statutory codes (e.g. Acas Code in Great Britain — failure to follow
can raise awards by up to 25%). Version and date every policy; record employee acknowledgement.

## 5. Severance / settlement agreements
- Release of claims must meet formalities: US age-discrimination waivers under OWBPA
  need written, specific reference to ADEA, advice to consult a lawyer, **21 days** to
  consider (45 for group programmes, with disclosures), and **7 days** to revoke.
- UK settlement agreements must be in writing, identify claims, and the employee
  must have received independent advice from a relevant adviser.
- Some rights can't be waived (e.g. certain wage claims in the US without DOL/court
  supervision, statutory rights to report to regulators).
- Confidentiality/non-disparagement clauses must not gag reporting of harassment or
  crimes in many jurisdictions (e.g. US Speak Out Act for pre-dispute sexual
  harassment NDAs).

## Gotchas
- "Discretionary" bonus clauses applied as fixed in practice may become contractual.
- Choice-of-law clauses rarely override mandatory local employee protections.
- Uncertain: covenant law changes frequently; check current statute before signing.

## Verify
- [ ] Contract consistent with the default regime (at-will or statutory notice).
- [ ] All mandatory particulars included.
- [ ] Each covenant tied to a legitimate interest and limited in time/area/scope.
- [ ] Handbook disclaimer and version control in place.
- [ ] Release formalities (review/revocation periods, advice) satisfied.
