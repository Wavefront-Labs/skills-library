# Section 1983 claims: liability, immunities and remedies

**Goal:** bring (or defend) a damages or injunction claim against government officials
and entities for violating federal rights. The US framework (42 U.S.C. §1983) is the
textbook model; other systems are noted at the end. Immunity doctrine is unsettled and
circuit-specific — research the circuit.

## 1. Elements
1. A person acting **under color of state law** (state and local officials, private
   parties in joint action with the state).
2. Deprived the plaintiff of a right secured by the **Constitution or federal law**.
3. **Causation** — the defendant's own conduct caused the deprivation; no vicarious
   liability. Plead each defendant's personal involvement.
Federal officials: §1983 doesn't apply; *Bivens* damages claims are now available only in
very narrow contexts (*Egbert v. Boule*, decided 8 June 2022) — look for statutory alternatives (FTCA). (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)

## 2. Identify the right (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)
Match facts to the constitutional provision and its test:
- Excessive force by police in an arrest → Fourth Amendment objective reasonableness
  (*Graham v. Connor*).
- Pretrial detainee conditions → Fourteenth Amendment due process (objective standard for
  force, *Kingsley*); convicted prisoners → Eighth Amendment deliberate indifference.
- False arrest → no probable cause; malicious prosecution → Fourth Amendment claim
  recognised in *Thompson v. Clark* (2022).
- Retaliation for speech → First Amendment retaliation test.

## 3. Municipal liability (*Monell*) (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)
Cities and counties are "persons" but liable only when a **policy or custom** caused the
violation: an official policy; a widespread practice; a decision by a final policymaker; or
**failure to train/supervise** amounting to deliberate indifference (usually needs a pattern
of similar violations). States and state agencies are not "persons" for damages; the
Eleventh Amendment bars damages against states, but *Ex parte Young* allows prospective
injunctions against state officers.

## 4. Immunities (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)
- **Qualified immunity** shields officials sued individually unless they violated a
  **clearly established** right — a precedent with closely similar facts in the controlling
  jurisdiction (or a robust consensus). Courts can decide either prong first. Raised at
  motion to dismiss and summary judgment; denial is immediately appealable (collateral
  order), which delays cases. Not available to municipalities.
- **Absolute immunity:** judges (judicial acts), prosecutors (advocacy functions), legislators
  (legislative acts), witnesses (testimony).
- Some states have enacted state-law causes of action limiting qualified immunity for state
  claims — consider pleading them.

## 5. Procedural bars (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)
- **Heck v. Humphrey:** no §1983 damages claim that would necessarily imply the invalidity of
  an outstanding conviction or sentence.
- **Limitations:** borrowed from the state's personal-injury statute; accrual is federal law.
- **PLRA** for prisoners: exhaustion of administrative grievances, physical-injury
  requirement for emotional damages, fee caps.
- Abstention doctrines when state proceedings are ongoing (*Younger*).

## 6. Remedies
Compensatory damages (proof of actual injury; nominal damages otherwise), punitive damages
against individuals (not municipalities), injunctions, declaratory relief. **Attorney fees**
for a prevailing party under §1988 (the court "may allow" a reasonable fee) — makes many cases viable.

## 7. Evidence
Body-worn and dash camera video, CAD/dispatch records, use-of-force reports, internal
affairs files (often contested — seek protective order), prior complaints for *Monell*,
training materials. Send preservation letters early — video retention periods can be short.

## Other systems
UK: Human Rights Act 1998 s7 claims against public authorities (one-year limit, extendable if the court
considers it equitable, s7(5)). Other systems differ.
