# FOI requests, exemptions and appeals

**Goal:** get public records released — or, for a public body or third party, handle
disclosure lawfully. US FOIA (5 U.S.C. §552) and the UK Freedom of Information Act 2000
are the two textbook models; US states, Canadian ATI/ATIP regimes, Australia and many
others follow similar patterns with different deadlines and exemptions.

## 1. Drafting the request
- **Identify the right body** and its FOI channel (portal or email).
- **Describe records specifically:** record type, subject, date range, custodians/offices,
  keywords. "All emails between the Planning Director and Acme Ltd from Jan–Mar 2025
  mentioning 'Riverside site'" beats "all information about Riverside".
- Ask for **electronic format** and, where useful, data in spreadsheet form.
- Narrow requests avoid cost-limit refusals (UK: the appropriate limit is £600 for authorities listed in Part I of Schedule 1 to the Act and £450 for others —
  check current figures) and long US processing queues.
- US: request a **fee waiver** (disclosure likely to contribute significantly to public understanding of
  government operations and not primarily in the requester's commercial interest, 5 U.S.C. 552(a)(4)(A)(iii)).
- Environmental information in the UK goes under the **EIR 2004** (SI 2004/3391; exceptions are in regulation 12).
  A request for your own personal data is absolutely exempt under FOIA s.40(1) and is dealt with under data
  protection law instead.

## 2. Deadlines
- UK: promptly and within 20 working days (FOIA s.10(1)); where a qualified exemption's public interest test is
  being considered, such time as is reasonable in the circumstances (s.10(3)).
- US FOIA: 20 working days (excluding weekends and holidays), extendable by up to 10 working days in unusual
  circumstances (5 U.S.C. 552(a)(6)).
Diary every deadline and chase in writing.

## 3. Exemption analysis
US FOIA has nine exemptions; commonly invoked:
- **(b)(5)** deliberative process / attorney-client / work product — the deliberative-process
  privilege does not apply to records created 25 years or more before the request, and an agency may withhold only if
  it reasonably foresees that disclosure would harm an interest protected by an exemption (5 U.S.C. 552(a)(8)(A), (b)(5)).
- **(b)(6)** personnel, medical and similar files; **(b)(7)** law-enforcement records.
- **(b)(4)** trade secrets and confidential commercial or financial information; **(b)(1)** classified; **(b)(3)** other statutes.
UK: absolute exemptions vs **qualified** ones subject to the **public interest test** (FOIA s.2)
(e.g. s35 policy formulation, s36 prejudice to effective conduct, s43 commercial interests);
s40 personal data; s12 cost; s14 vexatious.
Under the UK Act the duty to confirm or deny can also be disapplied by an exemption (s.2(1)).

## 4. Challenging a refusal
- **UK:** internal review (where the authority offers one) → complaint to the Information Commission
  (FOIA s.50, whose current text refers to the Commission, which has replaced the Information Commissioner) →
  appeal to the Tribunal (s.57) → Upper Tribunal on a point of law (Tribunals, Courts and Enforcement Act 2007 s.11).
- **US:** administrative appeal (the agency must allow at least 90 days after an adverse determination, 5 U.S.C.
  552(a)(6)(A)(i)(III)), then suit in federal district court; the court decides the matter de novo and the burden is
  on the agency (552(a)(4)(B)). Ask for a document-by-document justification of each withholding. The court may
  assess reasonable attorney fees against the United States where the complainant has substantially prevailed
  (552(a)(4)(E)).
- Arguments: exemption doesn't apply; harm not foreseeable; public interest favours release;
  inadequate search (ask for search terms and locations searched).

## 5. Acting for bodies or third parties
Public bodies: document searches, apply exemptions record by record, consult affected third
parties. Third parties: representations to the body
seeking non-disclosure of commercial information.

## Gotchas
- Asking questions ("why did you decide…") rather than for records — FOI covers recorded
  information, not explanations.
- Overbroad requests get refused or take years; split into tranches.
- Released information is generally released to the world.

## Verify
- [ ] Request is specific (records, dates, custodians, format) and sent to the right body.
- [ ] Response deadline and appeal deadlines diarised.
- [ ] Each withholding mapped to a specific exemption and challenged on its test.
- [ ] Search adequacy questioned where relevant.
