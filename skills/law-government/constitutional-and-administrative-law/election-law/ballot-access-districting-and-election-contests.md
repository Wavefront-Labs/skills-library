# Election law: ballot access, districting and election contests

**Goal:** litigate or advise on the rules of elections under severe time pressure, where
deadlines are fixed by the election calendar. US law is the main model (most variable
and litigated area — state law differs enormously); UK election petitions are noted at
the end. Doctrine in this area moves quickly; check the latest decisions each cycle.

## 1. Ballot access
- Build a calendar from the state election code: filing periods, petition signature
  requirements, residency/age qualifications, challenge deadlines, ballot printing and
  mailing deadlines (federal law requires sending absentee ballots to overseas and military voters at least 45 days before
  federal elections when requested in time, 52 U.S.C. 20302(a)(8), subject to a hardship waiver).
- **Challenges** to nomination petitions: signature validity, circulator requirements,
  formatting defects (see petition-and-ballot-campaigns skill). Short statutory windows,
  often days.
- **Constitutional challenges** to burdens on candidates and voters: the
  *Anderson-Burdick* balancing test — severe burdens get strict scrutiny; reasonable,
  nondiscriminatory restrictions are justified by important regulatory interests. (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)

## 2. Timing: the Purcell principle (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)
Federal courts are reluctant to change election rules close to an election (*Purcell v.
Gonzalez*, 2006). Sue early; late-filed challenges often lose on timing regardless of merits.
Expedited schedules and emergency appeals are the norm.

## 3. Redistricting (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)
- **One person, one vote:** congressional districts must be as nearly equal as practicable;
  state legislative districts generally tolerate deviations under about 10% total.
- **Racial gerrymandering** (Fourteenth Amendment, *Shaw v. Reno* line): race predominated
  over traditional principles without satisfying strict scrutiny. *Alexander v. South
  Carolina NAACP* (2024) raised the bar for proving racial predominance where partisanship
  is the defence.
- **VRA §2 vote dilution:** *Gingles* preconditions — a minority group sufficiently large and
  geographically compact for a majority-minority district, politically cohesive, and white
  bloc voting usually defeating its preferred candidates — then the totality of
  circumstances. *Allen v. Milligan* was decided on 8 June 2023 and *Louisiana v. Callais* (No. 24-109) on 29 April 2026;
  Callais addresses §2 vote-dilution claims, so read it before relying on earlier summaries of the *Gingles* framework.
- **Partisan gerrymandering** claims are non-justiciable in federal court (*Rucho*, 2019) but
  may succeed under state constitutions.
- Evidence: demographers, map-drawing simulations (ensemble analysis), ecological inference
  for racially polarised voting.

## 4. Voting-rights claims
Challenges to voter ID, registration purges, polling-place closures, mail ballot rules:
constitutional claims (*Anderson-Burdick*), VRA §2 vote-denial claims (*Brnovich*, 2021), National Voter Registration Act (systematic list-maintenance programs must be completed at least 90 days
before a federal primary or general election, 52 U.S.C. 20507(c)(2)(A)), and state constitutional claims.

## 5. Recounts and contests
- **Recounts:** automatic thresholds or requested recounts with deposit requirements;
  rules on voter-intent standards for ballots; observers.
- **Election contests:** statutory grounds (fraud, misconduct, ineligible votes cast,
  error) and the requirement usually to show the outcome would have changed. Very short
  filing windows after certification.
- Preserve ballots, envelopes, logs and equipment records immediately.
- Equal protection limits on standardless recount procedures (*Bush v. Gore*, expressly
  limited to its circumstances but still cited). (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)

## Other systems
UK: a parliamentary election petition must be presented within 21 days after the return is made to the Clerk of
the Crown, with longer periods where later payments or illegal practices are alleged (Representation of the People
Act 1983 s.122); local election petitions are tried by an election court (s.130). Parliamentary constituencies must
have an electorate within 5% of the UK electoral quota (Parliamentary Constituencies Act 1986, Schedule 2 rule 2(1)),
subject to the listed exceptions.

## Gotchas
- Laches: delay in bringing a claim can defeat it even within limitation periods.
- State and federal claims may have different forums and speeds — choose deliberately.
- Communications with election officials may be discoverable.
