# Rulemaking: comment letters and rule challenges

**Goal:** shape an agency rule while it's proposed and, if needed, set up and bring a
successful challenge to the final rule. US federal practice under the Administrative
Procedure Act (APA §553) is the model; UK consultations on secondary legislation and
EU "have your say" consultations work differently and are noted briefly.

## 1. Track early
- Watch agency regulatory agendas (the Unified Agenda), advance notices (ANPRMs),
  petitions, and stakeholder meetings. Influence is greatest before the proposal.
- Once a **notice of proposed rulemaking (NPRM)** is published in the Federal Register, the
  comment period starts (the agency sets its length). Request an extension early if needed, with
  reasons.

## 2. The comment letter
Comments are the record. A court reviewing the rule looks mostly at what the agency had
before it and how it responded.
- **Structure:** executive summary of positions → who you are and your interest →
  issue-by-issue analysis keyed to the preamble and regulatory text sections →
  specific alternative language → appendix of data.
- **Be specific and evidentiary:** data, studies, cost estimates, operational examples. The
  agency must respond to *significant* comments; vague opposition doesn't oblige a
  response. (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)
- **Legal objections:** statutory authority (after *Loper Bright*, 2024, courts interpret
  statutes independently rather than deferring) (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked), major questions doctrine where relevant,
  constitutional problems, conflicts with other statutes.
- **Procedural objections:** inadequate notice, missing data the agency relied on (ask that
  it be placed in the docket), deficient Regulatory Flexibility Act or Paperwork Reduction
  Act analysis, flawed cost-benefit analysis.
- **Alternatives:** propose less burdensome alternatives with evidence. Failure to consider
  significant alternatives supports an arbitrary-and-capricious argument.
- File by the method the NPRM directs (usually an online docket) before the deadline; save the
  tracking number and PDF.

## 3. Procedural compliance review of the final rule (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)
Check:
- **Logical outgrowth:** is the final rule something commenters could have anticipated from
  the proposal? If not, notice was inadequate.
- **Response to significant comments** — did the agency address yours?
- **Good cause** claims for skipping notice-and-comment or the 30-day delay before a rule takes effect
  (5 U.S.C. 553(d) requires 30 days unless an exception applies or the agency finds good cause and publishes it).
- **Reasoned explanation** for changes in position (*FCC v. Fox*: acknowledge the change and
  give good reasons; consider reliance interests).
- Congressional Review Act submission; the CRA disapproval window can matter politically.

## 4. Challenging the rule
- **Forum and timing:** some statutes route challenges directly to a court of appeals with
  short deadlines (e.g. 28 U.S.C. 2344: 60 days); otherwise district court under the APA within the general
  six-year period (28 U.S.C. 2401(a); see *Corner Post*, decided 1 July 2024, on when the period starts). (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)
- **Standing:** concrete injury traceable to the rule; associations need a member with standing.
- **Grounds:** contrary to statute / exceeds authority, arbitrary and capricious (*State Farm*:
  failed to consider an important aspect, explanation counter to the evidence),
  procedural defects, constitutional claims.
- **Preservation:** many courts require that issues were raised in comments (issue
  exhaustion) — another reason the comment letter matters.
- Remedy: vacatur, remand without vacatur, or stays (APA §705).

## Other systems
UK and EU consultation practice differs; check the relevant government's current guidance.

## Gotchas
- Mass form comments carry little legal weight; one detailed comment with data carries more.
- Submitting confidential data without the agency's confidentiality procedure makes it public.
