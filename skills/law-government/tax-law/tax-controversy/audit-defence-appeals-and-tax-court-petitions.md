# Audit defence, appeals and tax-court petitions

**Goal:** take a tax dispute from the first audit letter to the cheapest acceptable
resolution, never losing a right by missing a deadline. US federal practice (checked current on 2026-10-24) is the model,
with England & Wales (HMRC) equivalents noted; not tax advice; every authority has its own forms, time
limits and forums, so confirm them for the jurisdiction in play.

## 1. Triage the opening letter (day 1)
- Identify the stage: correspondence audit, field exam, notice of proposed adjustment,
  "30-day letter", **notice of deficiency ("90-day letter")**, or collection notice. Each
  carries a different clock.
- Diary every deadline twice. A missed petition deadline usually forfeits the
  pre-payment forum entirely.
- Check the **assessment statute of limitations** (US §6501: generally 3 years from filing,
  6 years for an omission of more than 25% of gross income, no limit for fraud or if no return was filed). It shapes your leverage.
- Obtain a power of attorney (US Form 2848, the IRS Power of Attorney and Declaration of Representative; UK agent authorisation) before speaking to the
  authority.
- Consider privilege early: practitioner privilege is narrower than attorney-client and
  usually does not apply in criminal matters. If an accountant must work the numbers for
  counsel, engage them through counsel (engage them through counsel).
- Screen for criminal exposure (badges of fraud, unreported income). If present, stop
  routine cooperation and involve criminal tax counsel.

## 2. Run the examination
1. Agree scope, timetable and a single point of contact with the examiner.
2. Answer each **Information Document Request (IDR)** precisely and only within scope;
   log everything produced (Bates-number it).
3. Prepare the client-side narrative and technical position memo in parallel.
4. Present facts before law: most adjustments are factual and are lost on documents.
5. Before agreeing to extend the statute (a written consent under §6501(c)(4)), weigh it: refusing can
   trigger an unfavourable, rushed assessment; consider a restricted consent limited to
   the issues under exam.

## 3. Administrative appeal
- On a 30-day letter, file a written **protest** (check the current IRS rules, Publication 5, on when a formal written protest rather than a small-case request is needed). Contents: taxpayer
  details, years, each disputed adjustment, facts, law, penalties of perjury statement.
- Appeals officers weigh **hazards of litigation**: present a realistic percentage view of
  each issue. Split-issue and percentage settlements are common.
- UK: request a **statutory review** by HMRC or appeal to the tax tribunal; the deadline is normally 30 days from the date
  of HMRC's decision letter (and a further 30 days from the review conclusion letter), and late appeals need a reasonable excuse.

## 4. Choose the litigation forum

| Forum (US) | Pay first? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| US Tax Court | No | Petition within 90 days of mailing of the notice of deficiency (150 if addressed outside the US); deadline strictly applied; no jury |
| District Court | Yes (full payment rule) | Only forum with a jury; refund claim first, then suit |
| Court of Federal Claims | Yes | No jury; different precedent |

UK equivalent: the tax tribunal, after any HMRC statutory review.
Forum choice turns on cash, precedent in each circuit, need for a jury and discovery style.

## 5. Litigate toward settlement
- Settlement remains possible after a petition is filed; ask whether the case can be referred to Appeals.
- Stipulate facts where possible; prepare expert valuation early.
- Penalties: build the **reasonable cause / good faith** record (reliance on advice,
  disclosure) separately from the merits.
- Document any settlement in the correct closing form (for example a US Form 870-AD Appeals agreement, closing agreement
  or stipulated decision) and check interest computations.

## Gotchas
- Calendar the 90-day petition date from the notice's mailing date, not receipt.
- A refund claim must be filed within its own limitations period before a refund suit.
- Volunteering documents outside an IDR's scope invites new issues.
- Agreeing to an open-ended statute extension without a review date.

## Verify
- [ ] Every deadline (protest, petition, refund claim, statute) diaried with source.
- [ ] Power of attorney filed; privilege structure decided before experts engaged.
- [ ] Criminal-exposure screen done and recorded.
- [ ] Protest/petition lists every disputed item, including penalties.
- [ ] Forum chosen with written reasons; settlement documented in a binding closing form.
