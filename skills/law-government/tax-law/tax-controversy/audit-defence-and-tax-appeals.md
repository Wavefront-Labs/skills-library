# Audit defence and tax appeals

**Goal:** limit an audit to its proper scope, resolve it on the best available terms, and,
where the authority won't move, preserve and pursue the taxpayer's appeal rights.

Examples use US federal practice (IRS examination, Appeals, Tax Court), checked current on 2026-10-24, as one
well-known path; UK and other systems have different steps and deadlines. The principles transfer; dates don't.
Not tax advice; a qualified tax adviser should handle a real dispute. The
revenue-authority side is in
`law-government.public-administration-and-civil-service.tax-assessment-and-collection.raising-assessments-and-handling-objections`.

## 1. On the audit notice
- Confirm the period, taxes and issues in scope; get the notice in writing.
- Take over communications (power of attorney / agent authorisation). Clients talking freely
  to auditors is the most common unforced error.
- Do a **privileged internal review** first: re-examine the return and records to find the
  weak points before the authority does. Where accountants assist, structure the engagement
  to preserve privilege where the system allows (in the US, by engaging the accountant through counsel).

## 2. Managing information requests
- Respond fully, accurately and on time; negotiate scope and deadlines in writing.
- Provide what is requested, organised and indexed. Don't volunteer unrelated material; never
  withhold or alter documents (obstruction and penalty exposure).
- Log every document produced (Bates numbers or equivalent).
- Track the **limitation period**. Authorities may ask to extend it; agree only to limited
  extensions (restricted to specific issues) where possible, and understand the trade-off:
  refusing may trigger an immediate assessment.

## 3. Framing the issues
For each proposed adjustment: the facts, the law (statute, regulations, cases, guidance), the
burden of proof and who bears it, and the hazards of litigation for both sides. Write a
response to the proposed adjustment that the next decision-maker can lift into their file.
Address **penalties** separately: reasonable cause, good faith, reliance on professional
advice, adequate disclosure.

## 4. Administrative appeal
Most systems offer review by an independent unit (IRS Independent Office of Appeals,
statutory review, objection officers). They can weigh litigation risk and settle on
percentages, which examiners usually cannot. File the protest/objection within the deadline,
setting out facts, law and argument per issue. Consider mediation or other ADR where offered.

## 5. Litigation
- **Jurisdictional deadlines** are strict (e.g. the 90-day period, 150 days if the notice is addressed outside the US, counted from the mailing of the
  statutory notice of deficiency, to petition the US Tax Court: §6213(a)). Missing them may force a pay-first refund suit.
- Choose forum where there's a choice (prepay or not, judge vs jury, precedent).
- Build the record: stipulations of fact, witnesses, experts (valuation, transfer pricing).
- Settlement remains possible throughout; tribunals often encourage it.

## Gotchas
- Missing a jurisdictional deadline is usually fatal and not curable by argument.
- Broad, open-ended limitation extensions.
- Inconsistent explanations across the audit, which damage credibility on appeal.
- Ignoring state/provincial or related-year consequences of a federal settlement.
