# Private rulings and voluntary disclosure

**Goal:** obtain binding certainty on a future transaction where the stakes justify it, and
when a past position is wrong, regularise it on the best terms available before the
authority finds it.

Procedures, fees, timelines and binding effect vary: e.g. US private letter rulings (the IRS publishes the
procedures and user fees each year in the first revenue procedure of the year, Rev. Proc. 2026-1 for 2026), pre-filing agreements
and APAs; other countries run their own ruling and clearance systems. Disclosure programmes change often.
Always read the current procedure document. Checked 2026-10-24 against IRS pages; not tax advice.

## 1. Is a ruling worth it?
Seek one when: the transaction is large or irreversible, the law is genuinely uncertain, a
counterparty or lender requires comfort, or a structure depends on one step. Don't seek one
when: the answer is clear, the authority won't rule in that area (no-rule lists), the timeline
won't fit, or you'd prefer not to put the facts in front of the authority. Consider an informal
**pre-submission conference** to test the waters, sometimes anonymously.

## 2. Drafting the request
- **Facts:** complete, accurate, specific; include all material facts, including unhelpful
  ones. A ruling protects only the facts stated; omission voids it.
- **Representations** the taxpayer makes, often in a signed declaration.
- **Ruling requested:** precise statements of the conclusions sought.
- **Law and analysis:** statute, regulations, cases, guidance; address contrary authority.
- **Procedural items:** fee, power of attorney, penalties-of-perjury declaration where required,
  disclosure consents.
Respond promptly to follow-up questions; delays can lead to withdrawal. If the authority
signals an adverse ruling, you can often withdraw (the fact of withdrawal may be noted).

## 3. Advance pricing agreements
For transfer pricing: unilateral, bilateral or multilateral APAs agree a method for covered
transactions over several years. Bilateral APAs prevent double tax but take longer. Need
functional analysis, financial data, critical assumptions, annual compliance reports.

## 4. Voluntary disclosure
For past non-compliance (unreported income, unregistered for VAT/sales tax, offshore accounts):
- Act **before** the authority makes contact; most programmes exclude taxpayers already under
  audit or investigation.
- Quantify the exposure (tax, interest, penalties) for the look-back period the programme uses.
- Choose the route: formal programme with reduced penalties and (sometimes) criminal-prosecution
  protection, or simply filing amended returns ("quiet disclosure"), which may carry more risk
  in some systems.
- Where criminal exposure is possible, involve criminal tax counsel first and structure for
  privilege.
- Be complete; a partial disclosure can forfeit protection.

## 5. Penalty abatement
Request relief on grounds such as reasonable cause, first-time abatement (US: returns of the same type filed on time for the
previous three years and no penalty, or any penalty removed, in that period) or equivalent
administrative waivers, statutory exceptions. Provide evidence (illness, reliance on advice,
disaster). Ask before paying where possible; otherwise claim a refund.

## Gotchas
- Ruling relied on despite facts changing.
- Disclosure made after an audit letter arrived, losing programme protection.
- Assuming a ruling for one taxpayer protects another; it generally does not.

## Verify
- [ ] Cost/benefit of a ruling assessed; no-rule list checked.
- [ ] Request states all material facts and precise rulings sought.
- [ ] Transaction executed as described in the ruling.
- [ ] Disclosure eligibility confirmed before contact; exposure quantified; criminal risk assessed.
- [ ] Penalty relief requested with evidence.
