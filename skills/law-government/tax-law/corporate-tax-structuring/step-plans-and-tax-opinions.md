# Step plans and tax opinions

**Goal:** turn a commercial reorganisation into a sequenced set of legal steps whose tax
consequences are identified, defensible and documented, and give (or rely on) an opinion at
the level the facts support.

Rules referenced (US reorganisation provisions such as IRC §§ 368 and 355; UK reconstruction
and clearance regimes; EU directive-based reliefs) are examples. Every jurisdiction has its own
reliefs, anti-avoidance rules and opinion conventions. Jurisdiction: US federal (IRC) for the checked
provisions, as of 2026-10-24. Not tax advice; a qualified tax adviser must review any real plan.

## 1. The step plan
A table, one row per step:
- **Step number and date/sequence constraint** (must precede/follow which steps).
- **Legal action** (e.g. "HoldCo transfers 100% of Target shares to NewCo in exchange for NewCo
  shares").
- **Parties and jurisdictions.**
- **Tax analysis per jurisdiction:** income/gains, relief relied on and its conditions, basis
  or base cost carried over, transfer taxes/stamp duty, VAT, withholding, losses and attribute
  effects.
- **Documents** needed (board resolutions, transfer instruments, valuations, filings).
- **Conditions and risks** (holding periods, continuity tests, clearance obtained or not).
Draft the plan before the documents and keep it the master; if a document changes, the plan
row changes.

## 2. Stress-test against anti-avoidance
- **Step transaction / substance over form:** would the authority collapse steps and tax the
  end result? Identify independent business purpose for each step and avoid pre-arranged
  binding commitments where relief depends on independence.
- **Business purpose and continuity** requirements (of interest, of business enterprise) in
  reorganisation reliefs.
- **GAAR/anti-abuse rules** and principal purpose tests in treaties.
- **Timing:** reliefs often require holding periods before or after; record why a step is
  timed as it is.
- **Disclosure regimes** (reportable transactions and other mandatory-disclosure rules; check each jurisdiction).

## 3. Rulings and clearances
Where available and worthwhile, seek an advance ruling or statutory clearance on key steps
(see `law-government.tax-law.tax-ruling-and-guidance-practice.private-rulings-and-voluntary-disclosure`).
Accurate, complete facts are essential; a ruling is only as good as the facts it states.

## 4. Opinion levels (US-style vocabulary; others vary)
- **Will:** no reasonable doubt on the conclusion.
- **Should:** high confidence, some risk.
- **More likely than not:** greater than 50%.
- **Substantial authority / reasonable basis:** lower thresholds for return positions; in the US the
  §6662(d)(2)(B) penalty reduction turns on substantial authority, or adequate disclosure with a reasonable basis.
An opinion states facts and representations relied on, assumptions, law, analysis, conclusion
and limitations. Get **representations** in a signed certificate from management; if any is
wrong, the opinion falls away. Don't opine above what the authorities support.

## 5. Closing
Execute documents in the plan's order; a closing agenda with sign-offs per step; post-closing
filings and elections within deadlines; retain evidence of business purpose and valuations.

## Gotchas
- Documents executed out of order or on the same day without recorded sequence.
- Valuations missing where relief depends on market value.
- A plan changed late without re-running the tax analysis.
- Ignoring transfer taxes and VAT that can exceed the income tax saved.

## Verify
- [ ] Every step has legal action, tax result per jurisdiction and documents.
- [ ] Anti-avoidance tests addressed with recorded purpose.
- [ ] Rulings/clearances obtained or risk accepted in writing.
- [ ] Opinion level matched to authority; representations signed.
- [ ] Closing executed in sequence; filings and elections calendared.
