# Obtaining and keeping exempt status

**Goal:** get a nonprofit recognised as tax-exempt on the first application, and keep it
exempt by running it within the rules that most often cost organisations their status.

Examples are US federal (IRC § 501(c)(3) and related provisions); other systems differ
(e.g. charity registration with the Charity Commission and HMRC recognition in England and
Wales; registered charities under the Income Tax Act in Canada; ACNC and DGR status in
Australia). State/provincial registration, solicitation and property-tax exemptions are
separate. This is general information, not legal or tax advice; consult a qualified tax adviser and the IRS or your national regulator.

## 1. Organisational test: get the documents right
- **Purposes clause** limited to exempt purposes (charitable, educational, religious,
  scientific, etc.). Broad "any lawful purpose" language fails.
- **Dissolution clause** dedicating assets to exempt purposes on winding up.
- Prohibitions on private inurement and political campaign activity; limits on lobbying.
- Governing documents filed under the relevant nonprofit corporation or trust law.

## 2. The application
- US: Form 1023, submitted electronically on Pay.gov with the user fee (IRS), within 27 months of formation for exemption to relate back to formation (IRS; churches and public charities with gross receipts normally under $5,000 are excepted). A streamlined Form 1023-EZ for small organisations (IRS page: $50,000 or less gross receipts, $250,000 or less total assets) existed; confirm it is still available and its current limits and fee on irs.gov (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
- Describe activities concretely: what, who benefits, how selected, how funded, who is paid and
  how much. Vague narratives trigger rounds of questions.
- Disclose related-party arrangements and justify them as at fair market value.

## 3. Public charity vs private foundation
Private foundations face excise taxes and strict rules: self-dealing prohibitions, minimum
distributions, excess business holdings, jeopardising investments, taxable expenditures.
Public charity status requires meeting a public support test (broad public/government
support) or being a church, school, hospital or supporting organisation. Track support
annually; failing the test for the required period can tip an organisation into foundation
status.

## 4. Operating within the rules
- **Private inurement/benefit:** no net earnings to insiders; compensation reasonable and
  approved using comparability data by independent board members (the US "rebuttable
  presumption" procedure for excess benefit transactions). Document it.
- **Political campaign activity:** 501(c)(3)s must not support or oppose candidates. Issue
  advocacy and nonpartisan voter education are allowed with care.
- **Lobbying:** must not be a major part of activities (IRS lobbying page); the § 501(h) election gives
  objective expenditure limits.
- **UBIT:** income from a trade or business regularly carried on and not substantially related
  to exempt purposes is taxable; exceptions (volunteer labour, donated goods, passive income
  like dividends, most rents and royalties, subject to debt-financing rules). Silo by activity.
- **Governance:** conflict-of-interest policy, minutes, document retention, whistleblower policy.

## 5. Annual compliance
File the annual information return (Form 990 series) on time; three consecutive missed years
means automatic revocation in the US (IRS: effective on the due date of the third missed return). Make returns and the exemption application available
for public inspection as required. Renew state registrations.

## Gotchas
- Filing the application after the 27-month window without realising exemption won't relate back.
- Founder-controlled boards approving the founder's pay.
- Endorsing a candidate from the pulpit or in a newsletter.
- Commercial side businesses growing until they threaten exemption itself.

## Verify
- [ ] Purposes and dissolution clauses meet the organisational test.
- [ ] Application describes activities and related-party dealings concretely.
- [ ] Public support tracked or foundation rules applied.
- [ ] Compensation approved with comparables by independent directors.
- [ ] Annual return and state filings calendared.
