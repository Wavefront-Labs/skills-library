# Obtaining and keeping charitable tax exemption

**Goal:** form a charity whose documents qualify for exemption, get the exemption
recognised, and keep it through annual compliance, sound governance and careful handling
of commercial income. US federal 501(c)(3) is the model, with England & Wales charity
contrasts. Fees, thresholds and form versions change — check current IRS/regulator figures. Jurisdiction: US federal
(IRC §501(c)(3)), checked 2026-10-24. Not legal or tax advice.

## 1. Draft qualifying formation documents
- **Organizational test:** purposes clause limited to exempt purposes (charitable,
  educational, religious, scientific…); prohibition on private inurement, campaign
  intervention and substantial lobbying; **dissolution clause** sending assets to another
  exempt body. State law may also require specific language.
- Bylaws: board size (independent majority recommended), quorum, officer roles, conflict
  of interest policy, compensation review procedure.
- Obtain an employer identification number and state charitable-solicitation
  registrations before fundraising.

## 2. Apply for recognition
1. Prepare **Form 1023**; the IRS requires Form 1023-series applications to be submitted electronically on Pay.gov with the
   user fee (check the IRS page for the current form, fee and any streamlined option).
2. File within 27 months of formation so exemption is retroactive to formation.
3. The narrative of activities is the core: who does what, how often, funded how, for
   whose benefit. Vague narratives draw development letters.
4. Provide three to five years of projected budgets consistent with the narrative.
5. Choose the **public charity** classification sought (donor-supported, fee-for-service,
   supporting organisation) or accept private-foundation status.
UK analogue: charity registration and tax recognition follow separate UK rules; check gov.uk.

## 3. Public charity or private foundation

| | Public charity | Private foundation |
|---|---|---|
| Support | Broad public support under the Code's public support tests | Typically one family or company |
| Excise taxes | Check the rules for the class | General private foundation excise taxes apply (IRC chapter 42) |
| Key rules | Excess benefit (intermediate sanctions, §4958) | Private foundation rules; check each |

Track the support fraction annually; failing it tips the charity into foundation status.

## 4. Annual compliance
- File the **Form 990 series** (990-N e-Postcard for organisations normally having gross receipts of $50,000 or less; 990-EZ or 990
  by size; 990-PF for private foundations) by the 15th day of the 5th month after year-end (§6072(e)).
- **Three consecutive years of non-filing revokes exemption automatically** (§6033(j)); reinstatement
  requires an application.
- Renew state registrations and audit requirements.

## 5. Governance that protects exemption
- Approve executive compensation using the **rebuttable presumption** procedure (Treas. Reg. §53.4958-6): approval in
  advance by an authorised body with no conflicted members, reliance on comparability data, and
  contemporaneous documentation. Excess-benefit transactions bear §4958 excise taxes: 25% on the disqualified person,
  200% if not corrected, and 10% on managers who knowingly approve (capped at $20,000 per transaction).
- A 501(c)(3) may not participate or intervene in any political campaign, and no substantial part of its activities may be
  lobbying (§501(c)(3)); §501(h) lets eligible public charities measure lobbying by dollar limits instead.
- Gift acceptance and grant-making policies; grants to individuals need objective selection.

## 6. Unrelated business income
Income is **unrelated business taxable income** if from a trade or business, regularly
carried on, and not substantially related to the exempt purpose. Certain
kinds of income are excluded; see the IRS page on exceptions and exclusions and check debt-financed income
separately. File Form 990-T if gross income from an unrelated business is $1,000 or more. If unrelated activity grows
large, consider moving it to a taxable subsidiary.

## Gotchas
- Purposes clause copied from a non-US template — fails the organizational test.
- Founders' family members on payroll without a documented comparability study.

- Fiscal sponsorship arrangements without control over funds.

## Verify
- [ ] Purposes and dissolution clauses meet federal and state requirements.
- [ ] Application filed within the retroactivity window; determination letter on file.
- [ ] Public support test computed annually.
- [ ] 990-series returns diaried; three-year revocation risk impossible.
- [ ] Compensation minutes, conflict disclosures and UBI analysis on file.
