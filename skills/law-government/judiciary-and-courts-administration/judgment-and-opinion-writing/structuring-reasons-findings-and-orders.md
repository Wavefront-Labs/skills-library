# Structuring reasons, findings and orders

**Goal:** a decision that tells the loser why they lost, shows an appellate court each
step, and ends in orders that can be enforced without further argument. Conventions
(numbered paragraphs, neutral citations, separate findings and conclusions, single
vs seriatim opinions) vary by jurisdiction and court level — follow the court's practice
directions and style guide.

## 1. Know your readers
1. **The parties** — especially the losing party, who needs to see their arguments were
   heard.
2. **The appellate court** — needs findings, reasons, and where discretion was exercised.
3. The profession and public — for precedent and legitimacy.
The duty to give adequate reasons (recognised widely as part of procedural fairness)
means explaining the *why* on the main issues, not answering every point.

## 2. A reliable structure
1. **Introduction / summary** (1–3 paragraphs): who the parties are, what they want, the
   outcome in a sentence. Many courts now start with the result.
2. **Issues**: numbered list of the questions the decision answers. Everything after
   follows this numbering.
3. **Background**: undisputed facts and procedural history, briefly. Don't narrate the
   whole trial.
4. **Law**: only the rules needed, stated accurately with authority.
5. **Analysis, issue by issue**: for each issue —
   - the parties' positions (fairly stated, at their strongest);
   - findings of fact on disputed matters, with the evidence relied on and why contrary
     evidence was rejected;
   - application of law to those findings;
   - conclusion on the issue.
6. **Conclusion and orders**.
(For appellate opinions: standard of review stated per issue, then the analysis.)

## 3. Findings of fact
- State findings as findings ("I find that…"), not as a summary of testimony ("X said…").
  Summarising evidence without resolving it is a common appellate complaint.
- Tie each important finding to the evidence (exhibit, transcript page) and to the
  standard of proof.
- Explain credibility determinations with reasons beyond demeanour (see
  law-government.judiciary-and-courts-administration.judicial-decision-making).
- In systems requiring separate "findings of fact and conclusions of law", label them
  clearly and don't mix legal conclusions into factual findings.

## 4. Writing style
- Plain language; short paragraphs, each with one point; numbered paragraphs for citation.
- Headings that are the issues, or answers to them.
- Quote sparingly and precisely; check every citation and pinpoint.
- Explain technical terms; avoid sarcasm, rhetorical flourishes and gratuitous criticism
  of parties or counsel.
- Anonymise per rules (children, victims of sexual offences, protected persons).
- Proofread for internal consistency: dates, names, amounts, defined terms.

## 5. Orders
Orders must be **self-contained and enforceable**:
- Who must do what, by when, and the consequence of non-compliance (where appropriate,
  with penal notices as required by local rules).
- Exact sums with the basis for interest, the date it runs from, and costs.
- Declarations stated precisely; injunctions specific about prohibited conduct (vague
  injunctions are unenforceable or overturned).
- Dispose of every claim and counterclaim; say what is dismissed.
- Consent orders: check they are within the court's power and don't bind non-parties.

## 6. Multi-judge courts
Coordinate: majority reasons should command a clear majority on each issue. Concurrences
should say what they add; dissents should engage the majority's reasoning. Avoid
fragmented outcomes where no rationale has majority support, unless unavoidable.

## Gotchas
- Delay: long-delayed judgments can themselves be grounds of complaint or appeal in some
  systems; set internal deadlines.
- Copying a party's submissions wholesale undermines the appearance of independent reasoning.
- Inconsistency between reasons and formal order — the order is what's enforced.

## Verify
- [ ] Outcome stated near the start; issues numbered and followed throughout.
- [ ] Each disputed fact resolved with evidence cited and contrary evidence addressed.
- [ ] Each issue's legal reasoning is complete and citations checked.
- [ ] Orders are specific, enforceable and match the reasons.
- [ ] Anonymisation and publication rules applied.
