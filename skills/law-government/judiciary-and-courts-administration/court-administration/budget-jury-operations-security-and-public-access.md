# Budget, jury operations, security and public access

**Goal:** a court that has the people and money its workload demands, summons jurors
efficiently, keeps everyone safe, and is genuinely open and usable by the public — while
protecting judicial independence. Governance varies widely: unified state judiciaries,
ministry-run courts, court services agencies. Adapt to who controls budget and staff.

## 1. Governance and independence
- Know the split of authority: chief/presiding judge (judicial policy, assignments) vs
  court administrator (operations, budget, staff). Document delegations.
- Administration serves adjudication: no administrative decision should influence how a
  case is decided (e.g. case assignment must be neutral — random or rule-based).
- Use a performance framework (e.g. the International Framework for Court Excellence or
  CourTools) to set goals and report publicly.

## 2. Budget and staffing
- Base requests on **workload, not filings alone**: weighted caseload studies assign time
  values to case types to estimate judge and staff need (judicial need model).
- Budget lines: personnel (usually the bulk), interpreters, jurors, technology, facilities,
  security, court-appointed counsel (where funded by the court).
- Track cost drivers monthly; prepare for mid-year shortfalls with contingency plans that
  don't stop constitutionally required services.
- Present budget requests with outcomes: time-to-disposition, backlog, access improvements.
- Staffing: cross-training, succession planning for clerical expertise, clear job
  classifications.

## 3. Jury operations
- **Source lists**: inclusive (e.g. voter + driver + other lists merged and de-duplicated)
  to produce representative pools; review undeliverable and non-response rates by area.
- **Yield**: share of summoned people who are qualified and available. Improve with
  updated addresses, follow-up on non-responses, reasonable deferral options.
- **Utilisation**: avoid summoning far more than needed — track jurors summoned vs
  actually sent to courtrooms and vs used in selection. One-day/one-trial systems reduce
  burden.
- Juror experience: clear instructions, online questionnaires, comfortable facilities,
  payment handled promptly; monitor complaints.
- Monitor representativeness (demographics) where law permits.

## 4. Security and continuity
- Security committee with judges, administrator, law enforcement/court security.
- Threat assessment and incident reporting for judges and staff (including online
  threats and doxxing); protect judges' personal information where law provides.
- Screening at entrances; separate circulation for judges, in-custody defendants and
  public where facilities allow; duress alarms; training (active threat, de-escalation).
- **Continuity of operations (COOP)** plan: essential functions (e.g. custody hearings,
  protective orders), alternate sites, remote hearing capacity, data backup and
  cyber-incident response. Test it.

## 5. Public access and access to justice
- **Open justice**: public hearings, published calendars, accessible records subject to
  privacy rules (sealing, redaction of personal data, protected categories).
- **Language access**: qualified/certified interpreters provided per law; translated forms
  and signage; track interpreter availability as a caseflow factor.
- **Disability access**: accommodations process (physical access, hearing loops, remote
  participation); designated coordinator.
- **Self-help**: centres, plain-language forms, online guided interviews, navigators —
  especially for family and housing cases with high self-representation.
- Measure access and fairness via court-user surveys.

## Gotchas
- Cutting interpreters or self-help first in budget crises — legally risky and
  amplifies delay.
- Over-summoning jurors to be "safe", wasting money and public goodwill.
- Security plans that exist only on paper; untested COOP plans fail.

## Verify
- [ ] Budget request based on weighted workload with outcome measures.
- [ ] Jury yield and utilisation tracked; source lists reviewed for representativeness.
- [ ] Security assessment and COOP plan current and exercised in the last year.
- [ ] Interpreter, disability-accommodation and self-help services funded and measured.
- [ ] Case assignment method documented and neutral.
