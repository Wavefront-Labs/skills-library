# Writing bench memos and draft opinions

**Goal:** give the judge accurate, neutral, efficient support — so the judge can decide
quickly and well — while maintaining the confidentiality and impartiality of chambers.
Title and role vary (law clerk, judicial assistant, judge's associate, registry clerk);
duties and ethics codes for clerks differ by court. Always follow your judge's
preferences, which beat any generic template.

## 1. First pass on every file
- **Jurisdiction and timeliness**: is the court competent? was the appeal/motion filed in
  time? is the order appealable? A jurisdictional defect ends the analysis — flag it first.
- **Standard of review** (appellate) or the governing legal test (trial motions).
- **Record check**: which documents are actually in the record; note anything the parties
  cite that isn't.
- **Conflicts**: check parties and counsel against the judge's conflict list and your own
  (former employer, future employer you've accepted an offer from). Report immediately.

## 2. The bench memo
Purpose: prepare the judge for argument or decision. Typical structure:
1. **Caption + one-paragraph summary**: what's at stake and your bottom-line recommendation.
2. **Questions presented**, numbered.
3. **Facts and procedural history**, with record cites — neutral, including facts that
   hurt your recommendation.
4. **Standard of review / burden**.
5. **Analysis per issue**: each side's best argument, the controlling authority (read
   it yourself — never rely on the parties' characterisations), your assessment, and
   open questions.
6. **Recommendation** with alternatives ("if the court disagrees on issue 1, then…").
7. **Suggested questions for oral argument** — targeted at weaknesses in each side's case.
Keep it as short as the case allows; flag uncertainty honestly ("I could not find
authority on X in this circuit").

## 3. Drafting opinions
- Get the judge's decision and reasoning first; ask for the outline if unclear. The
  clerk drafts, the judge decides.
- Write in the judge's voice and style; study their prior opinions for structure,
  tone, and pet conventions.
- Follow the court's opinion format and citation style (e.g. Bluebook in the US, OSCOLA
  in the UK, local style guides).
- Use the structure in law-government.judiciary-and-courts-administration.judgment-and-opinion-writing.
- Mark every factual statement with a record cite in the draft so the judge can verify.

## 4. Cite-checking (non-negotiable)
- Every authority: pull the actual source, confirm the proposition, pinpoint, quotation
  accuracy, and subsequent history (overruled? reversed? superseded by statute?) using a
  citator.
- Check record cites against the record.
- Generative AI tools can fabricate citations; many courts now regulate their use —
  follow the court's policy and verify every reference against primary sources.

## 5. Orders and the record
- Process proposed orders: check they match the ruling, parties, dates and amounts; flag
  discrepancies to the judge rather than editing substance.
- Track deadlines for decisions under submission and statistical reporting.
- Registry/clerk-of-court work: docket entries accurate and timely; sealed and
  confidential documents correctly restricted; certified copies only from the official record.

## 6. Ethics
- **Confidentiality** of deliberations and drafts is absolute — no discussion outside
  chambers, including after the clerkship, about how decisions were reached.
- No ex parte communications with parties; route calls to the clerk's office for
  procedural matters only.
- Avoid public commentary, social-media posts or political activity related to pending
  matters.
- Post-clerkship restrictions may limit appearing before the judge for a period.

## Gotchas
- Advocating for a result in the "facts" section — the judge needs neutral facts.
- Relying on headnotes or summaries instead of reading the authority.
- Editing an order's substance without telling the judge.

## Verify
- [ ] Jurisdiction, timeliness and standard of review addressed first.
- [ ] Bench memo states recommendation, both sides' best arguments, and record cites.
- [ ] Every authority and record cite checked against the source and citator.
- [ ] Draft opinion reflects the judge's decision and style.
- [ ] Conflicts screened and confidentiality maintained.
