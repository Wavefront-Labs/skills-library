# Recusal, extrajudicial activity and conduct complaints

**Goal:** keep a judge's impartiality — and its appearance — beyond reproach, deciding
recusal questions correctly and early, and handling complaints properly. The Bangalore
Principles (independence, impartiality, integrity, propriety, equality, competence and
diligence) are the international reference; binding codes are national or state-level
(e.g. ABA Model Code adopted with variations by US states, 28 U.S.C. §455 for US federal
judges, judicial conduct guides in Commonwealth jurisdictions). Use the code that binds you
and its advisory opinions.

## 1. Conflict screening (prevent before it arises)
- Maintain a **conflict list**: financial interests (shares, funds where applicable),
  family members' interests and employers, former firm and clients, organisations served,
  close friends who litigate.
- Run new cases against it at assignment (many case-management systems automate this).
- Re-check when parties or counsel change or new corporate parents are disclosed.
- Divest problem holdings where practical rather than recusing repeatedly.

## 2. The recusal decision
Two questions:
1. **Mandatory disqualification grounds** in the governing code/statute — e.g. personal
   knowledge of disputed facts, prior involvement as lawyer in the matter, financial
   interest (sometimes however small), a close relative as party/lawyer/material witness.
2. **Apparent bias**: would a fair-minded and informed observer conclude there is a real
   possibility (or reasonable apprehension) of bias? Formulations differ slightly by
   jurisdiction; the US constitutional floor (*Caperton*) applies in extreme cases such as
   disproportionate campaign support.
Process:
- **Disclose** on the record any relationship or information the parties might reasonably
  consider relevant, even if you believe recusal isn't required.
- Some systems allow parties to **waive** certain grounds after disclosure, outside the
  judge's presence; others don't. Follow the code.
- Decide promptly and give brief reasons. Neither recuse lightly (there is a duty to sit)
  nor sit when in doubt on a genuine ground.
- Don't let the timing of a recusal motion become tactical: consider whether it was
  brought promptly.
- Prior adverse rulings against a party are generally not grounds for recusal.

## 3. Extrajudicial activities
Typical limits (check your code):
- **Political**: no partisan activity, endorsements or fundraising (elected judiciaries
  have special campaign rules).
- **Teaching, writing, speaking** about the law: encouraged, but no comment on pending or
  impending cases, and avoid prejudging issues likely to come before you.
- **Organisations**: no membership in groups practising invidious discrimination;
  limits on fundraising and serving on boards of organisations that litigate.
- **Gifts and hospitality**: restrictions and reporting requirements; never from parties
  likely to appear.
- **Financial and business activities**: restrictions on active business roles; reporting
  of financial interests.
- **Social media**: assume anything posted is public and permanent; avoid "friending" or
  following lawyers/parties who appear before you in ways that suggest closeness; no
  commentary on cases; check advisory opinions — guidance continues to develop.
- **Ex parte communications** about pending matters are prohibited except narrow
  administrative/emergency exceptions; if one occurs, disclose it to all parties.

## 4. Responding to a conduct complaint
- Complaints go to a judicial conduct body (commission, council, chief justice office).
  Most are dismissed as challenges to decisions — appeal, not discipline, is the remedy
  for legal error.
- If a complaint is referred to you: respond promptly, factually and respectfully; obtain
  the transcript/recording; avoid contacting the complainant; consider advice from
  counsel or a judges' association.
- Consider whether the complaint itself requires recusal from the complainant's ongoing case.
- Seek **advisory opinions** in advance when unsure — they often provide a safe harbour.

## Gotchas
- Forgetting spouse/children's interests and employers.
- Commenting publicly about a case after decision while appeals are pending.
- Treating disclosure as optional because you're sure you're impartial — the test is appearance.

## Verify
- [ ] Conflict list current and run against every new case and party change.
- [ ] Recusal analysis covers mandatory grounds and the apparent-bias test, with reasons recorded.
- [ ] Relevant relationships disclosed on the record.
- [ ] Extrajudicial and online activities checked against the code and advisory opinions.
- [ ] Any ex parte contact disclosed; complaints answered factually with the record.
