# Screening and running family mediation

**Goal:** help separating parents reach durable arrangements for children and money
while making sure no one is mediating under fear or pressure. Models: England & Wales
(MIAMs, Family Mediation Council standards) and US court-connected and private
practice (AFCC standards; several states mandate custody mediation). Legal effect of
the outcome differs: in E&W a mediated summary is not binding until made a consent
order; in the US it is typically incorporated into a court judgment.

## 1. Individual intake (always separately)
1. Meet each party alone (the MIAM in E&W, an intake call elsewhere) before any joint session.
2. Explain mediation, its voluntariness, confidentiality and its limits (child
   safeguarding disclosures; in E&W financial disclosure is open, not privileged).
3. **Screen for domestic abuse and coercive control** using a structured tool, asking
   about: physical violence, threats, fear of the other party, control of money or
   movement, monitoring, sexual coercion, harm to children, substance misuse, current
   orders or police involvement.
4. Decide: suitable for joint mediation / suitable only with adaptations (shuttle,
   separate arrivals, support person, online with separate links) / **not suitable**
   — refer to legal advice, support services or court, and sign any exemption form.
   Re-screen at every session; abuse often surfaces later.

## 2. Choose the model
| Model | Use when |
|---|---|
| Joint face-to-face | Screened safe, broadly able to communicate |
| Shuttle (separate rooms) | High conflict or imbalance but safe enough to mediate |
| Hybrid with lawyers present | Complex finances or significant imbalance in knowledge |
| Child-inclusive | Children (often age ~10+, mediator assessed) want a voice; both parents consent |

## 3. Parenting sessions
- Start with the children's routines and needs, not each parent's "rights".
- Work through a **parenting plan**: weekday and weekend pattern, holidays and special
  days, handovers, schooling, health decisions, communication rules, introducing new
  partners, relocation, how to resolve future disagreements and review the plan.
- Child-inclusive practice: a separately trained mediator meets the child, agrees with
  the child what may be shared, then feeds back to parents. The child is not asked to choose.

## 4. Financial sessions
1. Full disclosure first — e.g. Form E-style **open financial statement** in E&W,
   financial affidavits in the US: income, assets, debts, pensions, with documents.
2. Build a shared schedule of assets and liabilities (joint vs sole, values, dates).
3. Identify needs (housing for each household, child support, income gap), then
   options, then test each against each party's budget.
4. Pension and tax issues → recommend specialist advice before agreement.

## 5. Recording the outcome
- **Memorandum of Understanding** (proposals, not binding) plus the open financial
  summary; parties take independent legal advice; a lawyer drafts the consent order or
  settlement agreement for court approval.
- Parenting plans may stay informal or be embodied in orders, depending on law and wishes.

## Gotchas
- Joint intake meetings: a frightened party won't disclose abuse in front of the other.
- Equal "airtime" does not cure imbalance in financial knowledge — slow down and
  ensure documents are explained.
- Proposals reached without full disclosure can be set aside later.
- Agreeing child-support figures that conflict with statutory formulas may be unenforceable.

## Verify
- [ ] Separate screening completed and recorded; re-screened each session.
- [ ] Model adapted to risk, or case declined and referrals made.
- [ ] Financial disclosure complete with supporting documents.
- [ ] Parenting plan covers routine, holidays, decisions, communication and review.
- [ ] MoU states parties should take independent legal advice before it becomes binding.
