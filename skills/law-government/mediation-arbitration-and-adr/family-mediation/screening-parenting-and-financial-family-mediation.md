# Screening, parenting and financial family mediation

**Goal:** help separating parents or partners reach workable arrangements for children
and money, while making sure mediation is safe and the outcome is not the product of
fear or hidden assets.

## 1. Intake: always separate, always screen
- See each person **alone** first (in England and Wales this is the MIAM; other
  systems have equivalent intake assessments). Never screen with both present.
- Screen for domestic abuse, including **coercive control**, financial control,
  threats, stalking, child protection concerns, substance misuse and mental-health
  crisis. Ask direct, behaviour-based questions ("Has your partner ever stopped you
  seeing friends or accessing money?"), not "is there abuse?".
- Outcomes: proceed jointly; proceed with adaptations (shuttle, separate arrival and
  departure times, video from separate locations, a support person); or **decline**
  and signpost to legal advice, protective-order routes and support services. Record
  the decision and reasons. Screening is ongoing — re-check if something surfaces later.
- Child-protection disclosures override confidentiality; know your local reporting
  duty before the first session.

## 2. Parenting sessions
- Put the child at the centre literally: a photo or a chair can help some parents.
- Work from the practical calendar outward: school week, weekends, holidays,
  festivals, birthdays, handovers (where, who drives), communication between homes,
  new partners, relocation, decision-making on school/health/religion.
- Use developmental norms (a toddler and a 14-year-old need different schedules) but
  don't prescribe. Build in a **review date**.
- **Child-inclusive mediation:** a separately qualified mediator meets the child (with
  both parents' consent, typically from around age 10, varying by jurisdiction and
  practice standard) and feeds back only what the child agrees to share. The child
  is heard; the child does not decide.

## 3. Financial sessions
- **Full and frank disclosure first, negotiation second.** Use a standard financial
  statement (e.g. Form E in England and Wales, or your jurisdiction's equivalent) with
  supporting documents: 12 months bank statements, payslips/tax returns, pension
  valuations, property valuations, mortgage statements, business accounts.
- Build one **shared asset and liability schedule** on screen: joint, A's, B's,
  with values, sources and disputed items flagged. Agreeing the numbers often defuses
  half the conflict.
- Then income and needs budgets for both households, then options (sale vs. buy-out,
  pension sharing/offsetting, maintenance level and term). Get pension and tax input
  from specialists; mediators should not give advice on it.
- Recommend each party takes **independent legal advice** before finalising.

## 4. Managing power imbalance in the room
Watch for one party deferring, looking for permission, or agreeing too fast. Tools:
balance speaking time, summarise the quieter party's interests, caucus, slow down,
reality-test "can you live with this in two years?", or stop mediation.

## 5. Output
- A **Memorandum of Understanding** (proposals) plus, for finances, an Open Financial
  Statement of the disclosed figures. In most systems these are **not** binding until
  turned into a court order or a properly executed agreement by lawyers.
- Parenting plans are often non-binding but can be put to the court.

## Gotchas
- Mediator privilege and its exceptions (child harm, fraud) **vary by jurisdiction**.
- Some courts require attendance at intake before filing; exemptions often apply for
  abuse — know them so you can tell people accurately.
- Avoid drafting "agreements" that read as binding; use the MoU label.
- Unverified: the age guideline for child-inclusive work varies by accreditation body.

## Verify
- [ ] Each party screened separately; decision and adaptations recorded.
- [ ] Financial figures supported by documents and on one agreed schedule.
- [ ] Parenting plan covers term time, holidays, handovers, communication and review.
- [ ] Parties told the MoU is non-binding and advised to get independent legal advice.
