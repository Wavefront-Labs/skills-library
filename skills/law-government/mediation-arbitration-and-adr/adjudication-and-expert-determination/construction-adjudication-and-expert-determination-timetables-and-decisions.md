# Construction adjudication and expert determination: timetables and decisions

**Goal:** produce a binding (at least temporarily) decision within days or weeks, on the
documents, without giving a losing party a ground to resist enforcement.

## Two different animals
| | Construction adjudication | Expert determination |
|---|---|---|
| Source | Statute in many countries (e.g. UK Construction Act 1996, Australian/NZ/Singapore security-of-payment acts) or contract (FIDIC/NEC dispute boards) | Contract only |
| Decider acts as | Quasi-judicial neutral, natural justice applies | Expert using own skill and judgment; often no hearing |
| Finality | Binding until finally resolved by court/arbitration ("pay now, argue later") | Usually final and binding, challengeable only for narrow grounds (outside the remit, fraud, sometimes manifest error) |
| Typical disputes | Interim payments, variations, extensions of time, loss and expense | Completion accounts, share/asset valuation, technical measurements, rent review |

## Construction adjudication — the timetable (UK statutory example; varies by jurisdiction)
1. **Notice of adjudication** served by the referring party (Housing Grants Act 1996 s.108 and the Scheme para 7); nominating body appoints
   you (or named adjudicator). Accept promptly — the referral must follow within 7 days.
2. **Referral notice** received → the 28-day clock starts (extendable by 14 days with
   the referring party's consent, longer only with both).
3. Issue **directions** immediately: response date, reply, any site visit or meeting,
   page limits, how documents are exchanged, your fee basis.
4. Decide, and **communicate the decision within the deadline**. A decision made outside the
   time allowed risks challenge.
Other jurisdictions run on different (often shorter) clocks and limit what a respondent
may raise; check the statute before the first direction.

### Jurisdiction checks (do these on day one)
- Is there a **construction contract** within the statute (exclusions: some residential
  occupiers, certain process-plant work, etc.)?
- Is there a **crystallised dispute** (a claim made and rejected or not admitted)?
- **One dispute only** unless parties agree otherwise.
- Same or substantially the same dispute already decided? You can't re-decide it.
- Correct appointment under the contract/scheme?
If a party challenges, you can't finally decide your own jurisdiction, but you should
investigate, give a non-binding view and usually proceed; a party that participates
should reserve its position.

### Natural justice in a 28-day process
Courts enforce decisions robustly but will refuse where there was a **material breach**:
deciding on a point neither party raised without inviting comment, ignoring a defence
properly put, or private contact with one side. When you rely on your own knowledge or
an approach not argued, tell both parties and give them a short window to respond.

## Expert determination
- Read the clause carefully: the **remit** (what exactly the expert decides), whether
  reasons are required, procedure (often left to the expert), costs, and whether the
  expert acts "as an expert and not as an arbitrator".
- Agree a short procedural letter: submissions, information requests, site access,
  timetable, fees.
- Investigate actively — that is the point of appointing an expert.
- Stay **within the remit**: answering a different question is the classic ground for
  setting a determination aside.
- Accounting/valuation determinations: state the basis of preparation, the accounting
  policies applied, and reconcile to the figures in dispute.

## Writing the decision
Short, issue by issue: the question, each party's position, your finding and reasons,
the sum and date for payment, interest, and who pays your fees. Correct only clerical
slips afterwards ("slip rule"), promptly.

## Verify
- [ ] Jurisdiction points checked and recorded on day one.
- [ ] Directions issued the day of referral; deadline and any extensions in writing.
- [ ] Any point not argued by the parties was put to them before deciding.
- [ ] Expert stayed within the contractual remit.
- [ ] Decision delivered in time with payment terms and fee allocation.

## Checked

Checked 2026-10-25. Jurisdiction: England and Wales (Housing Grants, Construction and Regeneration Act 1996 and the Scheme for Construction Contracts); other jurisdictions are described as differing.

Confirmed on legislation.gov.uk: Housing Grants, Construction and Regeneration Act 1996 s.108(2) (notice at any time; timetable aimed at referral within 7 days of notice; decision within 28 days of referral, extendable by up to 14 days with the referring party's consent or longer by agreement after referral) and s.108(3) (decision binding until finally determined). Scheme for Construction Contracts (England and Wales) Regulations 1998 Pt I para 7(1) (referral within seven days of the notice of adjudication) and para 19(1) (decision within 28 days of receipt of the referral notice, 42 days with the referring party's consent, or longer by agreement; wording amended 1 October 2011). The statement that a late decision is a nullity was a case-law claim that could not be read and was softened.
