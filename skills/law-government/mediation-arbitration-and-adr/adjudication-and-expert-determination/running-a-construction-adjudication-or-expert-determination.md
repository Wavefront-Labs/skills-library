# Running a construction adjudication or expert determination

**Goal:** deliver a binding decision within days or weeks, not years, that survives
an enforcement hearing. Model: UK statutory adjudication under the Housing Grants,
Construction and Regeneration Act 1996 and the Scheme for Construction Contracts.
Similar "security of payment" regimes exist in Australian states, New Zealand,
Singapore, Ireland and elsewhere with different timetables and scope. Expert
determination is purely contractual everywhere — the clause is the rulebook.

## Part A — Statutory construction adjudication (UK model)
### 1. On appointment
1. Check the **notice of adjudication**: identifies parties, the dispute, the redress sought.
2. Check jurisdiction before accepting and again on receipt of the referral:
   - a "construction contract" within the Act (excluded operations, residential occupier exclusion);
   - a single dispute that has **crystallised** (claim made and rejected or ignored);
   - correct appointment route (named adjudicator or nominating body, e.g. RICS, CIC, TeCSA);
   - no prior decision on the same dispute.
3. The **referral notice** must arrive within 7 days of the notice of adjudication.
4. Accept in writing with terms and fees; disclose conflicts.

### 2. Timetable (default under the Act/Scheme — confirm contract terms)
| Day | Step |
|---|---|
| 0 | Referral received |
| ~7–14 | Response from responding party (set by adjudicator) |
| ~17–21 | Reply / further submissions, site visit or meeting if needed |
| 28 | Decision due |
| +14 | Extension with the referring party's consent; longer only with both parties' consent |
Issue directions on day 1: dates, page limits, format, communication rules (copy all).

### 3. Natural justice under time pressure
- Give each side a fair opportunity to answer the material against it — especially new
  evidence in a late reply.
- If you use your own expertise or an assessor, tell the parties and invite comment.
- Consider all defences genuinely raised, including set-off where admissible.
- Don't decide on a basis nobody argued.

### 4. The decision
- Answer the dispute referred and nothing more: sums due, by whom, when, interest,
  fees allocation. Give brief reasons unless the parties agree otherwise.
- Correct only clerical slips under the slip rule, promptly.
- Decisions are binding until finally determined by litigation, arbitration or
  agreement; courts generally enforce summarily and refuse to revisit merits
  (*Macob*, 1999). Successful resistance is mostly on jurisdiction or a material
  breach of natural justice.

## Part B — Expert determination
1. Read the clause: what is to be determined (a valuation, completion accounts, a
   technical question), how the expert is appointed, procedure, timescale, whether
   reasons are required, who pays.
2. Agree terms of engagement with both parties; confirm independence.
3. Set a procedure: written submissions, documents, site inspection, meetings — no
   formal hearing unless the clause provides one.
4. Act as an **expert, not an arbitrator**: you may investigate using your own knowledge.
5. Decide exactly the question asked. A determination is generally final unless the
   expert departed from instructions in a material way or the clause allows challenge
   for manifest error or fraud (*Jones v Sherwood*). Questions of law: some clauses
   reserve them to the court — check.

## Gotchas
- Accepting a "dispute" that was never put to the other side → no jurisdiction.
- Taking more than 28 days without valid consent risks the decision being challenged as made out of time.
- "Kitchen sink" referrals: you may manage by directions but must still decide.
- Experts who adopt arbitral procedure inadvertently create arguments about status.

## Verify
- [ ] Jurisdiction checks done and recorded at appointment and on referral.
- [ ] Directions issued; extensions agreed in writing.
- [ ] Every new point given to the other side for comment.
- [ ] Decision on time, answers only what was referred, states sums, dates, fees.
- [ ] For expert determination: question decided matches the contractual mandate.

Confirmed on legislation.gov.uk: Housing Grants, Construction and Regeneration Act 1996 s.108(2) (notice at any time; timetable aimed at referral within 7 days of notice; decision within 28 days of referral, extendable by up to 14 days with the referring party's consent or longer by agreement after referral) and s.108(3) (decision binding until finally determined). Scheme for Construction Contracts (England and Wales) Regulations 1998 Pt I para 7(1) (referral within seven days of the notice of adjudication) and para 19(1) (decision within 28 days of receipt of the referral notice, 42 days with the referring party's consent, or longer by agreement; wording amended 1 October 2011). The statement that a late decision is a nullity was a case-law claim that could not be read and was softened.
