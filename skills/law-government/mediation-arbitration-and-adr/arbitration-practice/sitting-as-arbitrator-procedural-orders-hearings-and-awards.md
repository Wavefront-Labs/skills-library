# Sitting as arbitrator: procedural orders, hearings and awards

**Goal:** conduct an arbitration that is fair, efficient and ends in an award that
survives a set-aside challenge and is enforceable where the winner needs it.

## 1. Before accepting
- **Conflict check and disclosure.** Disclose anything that might give rise to
  justifiable doubts about independence or impartiality, judged from the parties'
  eyes. The **IBA Guidelines on Conflicts of Interest** (red/orange/green lists) are
  the common reference. Duty to disclose continues throughout.
- Confirm availability for the realistic timetable; institutions increasingly ask.
- Read the arbitration clause: seat, rules (ICC, LCIA, SIAC, AAA-ICDR, UNCITRAL ad
  hoc...), number of arbitrators, language, governing law, any time limits.

## 2. Jurisdiction
Under most modern laws (based on the UNCITRAL Model Law) the tribunal rules on its own
jurisdiction (*kompetenz-kompetenz*), subject to court review at the seat. Objections
must usually be raised no later than the statement of defence. Decide whether to
bifurcate: worth it when a jurisdictional win would end the case and the issue is
cleanly separable.

## 3. Case management conference and Procedural Order No. 1
Hold an early CMC (video is fine) and issue **PO1** covering:
- parties, counsel, contacts, communication method;
- applicable rules, seat, language, the **lex arbitri**;
- the procedural timetable (written submissions, document production, witness
  statements, expert reports, hearing dates, post-hearing briefs);
- evidence rules — usually "guided by" the IBA Rules on the Taking of Evidence;
- document production via a **Redfern schedule** (request, justification, objection,
  tribunal decision in four columns);
- format of submissions, bundles, hearing logistics, transcripts, interpreters;
- tribunal secretary (if any) and their tasks, with party consent;
- costs: deposits, and how costs will be allocated.
Agree as much as possible; decide the rest promptly.

## 4. The hearing
- Pre-hearing conference ~2–4 weeks before: witness order, time allocation (a
  **chess-clock** split is common), sequestration of witnesses, hearing bundle.
- Witness statements stand as evidence-in-chief; hearing time is for
  cross-examination. Consider **expert conferencing** (hot-tubbing) for technical issues.
- Treat parties equally and give each a **full opportunity to present its case** —
  the core ground for set-aside and refusal of enforcement. Ask at the close: "Does
  either party have any objection to the procedure so far?" and record the answers.

## 5. The award
- Structure: parties and tribunal; procedural history; the agreement to arbitrate and
  jurisdiction; issues; each side's case; analysis and reasons per issue; interest;
  costs; dispositive section (*the operative part*) stating exactly who pays what by when.
- Check the formal requirements of the seat and the rules: in writing, signed, reasons
  (unless waived), date and seat stated. ICC awards go through institutional scrutiny.
- Write so a court reading only the award can see every claim was addressed — **infra
  petita** (not dealing with a claim) and **ultra petita** (going beyond the
  submission) both invite challenge.
- Enforcement abroad is usually under the **New York Convention**; its Article V
  grounds (invalid agreement, no proper notice or opportunity to present the case,
  excess of mandate, irregular tribunal composition, public policy) are your checklist.

## Gotchas
- Don't decide on a legal theory neither party argued without inviting submissions.
- Unilateral contact with one party about the merits is forbidden.
- Time limits for the award (some rules and laws impose them) can be fatal if missed.
- Seat-specific mandatory law varies; confirm local requirements (uncertain areas
  flagged per seat).

## Verify
- [ ] Disclosure made and recorded; no unaddressed objections.
- [ ] PO1 issued with full timetable and evidence rules.
- [ ] Each party confirmed it had a full opportunity to present its case.
- [ ] Every claim and counterclaim decided; operative part is precise.
- [ ] Award meets the seat's form requirements and was signed and dated in time.
