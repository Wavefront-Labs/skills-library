# Running a commercial mediation from intake to settlement

**Goal:** get two businesses (usually with lawyers) from entrenched positions to a
signed, enforceable settlement in one day or less, without the mediator deciding anything.

## 1. Before the day (where most settlements are actually won)
- **Agreement to mediate**, signed by every party and counsel: confidentiality,
  without-prejudice status of everything said, the mediator cannot be called as a
  witness, fees and cancellation, who attends. Use a provider template (CEDR, JAMS,
  AAA-ICDR or your national body) rather than drafting your own.
- **Authority check:** confirm in writing that each side sends someone with *full*
  settlement authority (not "authority up to X"). Missing authority is the commonest
  cause of a wasted day. For insurers/public bodies, get the approval route by phone.
- **Pre-mediation calls** with each side separately (30–60 min): what do they really
  need, what has been offered already, what stops settlement, who are the
  personalities, are there non-money issues (future supply, reference, apology,
  publicity).
- **Position papers + bundle:** short (10 pages) papers exchanged, plus a confidential
  note to the mediator only if useful. Read everything; know the costs-to-trial figure.

## 2. The day
1. **Opening joint session** (optional and increasingly skipped when tempers are
   high): mediator explains process and confidentiality; each side opens briefly.
   Coach parties to speak to the *other principal*, not to you.
2. **First caucuses** — explore, don't negotiate yet. Ask: "If this went to trial,
   what is your realistic range, and what will it cost to get there?" Build their
   **BATNA/WATNA** with them (best/worst alternative). Include irrecoverable costs,
   management time, delay, enforcement risk and interest.
3. **Shuttle phase.** Carry offers only with explicit permission ("what can I tell
   them?"). Everything said in caucus is confidential unless released — keep a written
   note of what each side has let you share.
4. **Reality-test without taking sides:** questions, not verdicts. "How will the judge
   deal with the limitation point?" lands better than "you'll lose on limitation".

## 3. Impasse tools (in rough order of escalation)
- **Re-frame the pie:** payment timing, instalments, credits on future orders,
  non-disparagement, mutual releases, tax treatment.
- **Brackets:** "If they moved to 800, would you move to 1.2m?" — narrows without
  committing either side.
- **Principals-only meeting** without lawyers (with consent).
- **Mediator's proposal:** a number put to both sides privately; each answers yes/no
  to the mediator alone; only a double yes is revealed. Use once, late.
- **Adjourn with a deadline** rather than declare failure; many settle within a week.

## 4. Closing
- **Write it before anyone leaves.** At minimum signed heads of terms; better, a full
  settlement agreement or (in litigation) a consent order/stay terms. Cover: amount,
  dates, mode of payment, who pays costs, releases (scope!), confidentiality,
  default consequences, governing law, and what happens to the pending proceedings.
- Lawyers draft; the mediator checks the draft matches what was agreed, not its merits.
- Cross-border: a settlement may be enforceable under the **Singapore Convention on
  Mediation** where the states involved have ratified it — check the current list.

## Gotchas
- Confidentiality and privilege of mediation communications **vary by jurisdiction**
  (e.g. US state mediation-privilege statutes, the EU Mediation Directive, English
  without-prejudice rules); don't promise more than the governing law gives.
- Never give legal advice to either party; refer them to their counsel.
- Disclose any conflict (prior work for a party or firm) before appointment.
- Fatigue settles cases and also produces regret; check terms are understood.

## Verify
- [ ] Signed agreement to mediate and confirmed full authority for every party.
- [ ] Pre-mediation call held with each side; you know their BATNA and non-money interests.
- [ ] You only conveyed information you were permitted to share.
- [ ] Signed written terms covering payment, releases, costs and the proceedings.
- [ ] Uncertain point flagged: enforceability and privilege checked for the governing law.
