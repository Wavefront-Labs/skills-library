# Running a commercial mediation day

**Goal:** take two businesses (and their lawyers) from entrenched positions to signed,
enforceable settlement terms in one day — or, failing that, a narrowed dispute and a
clear next step. Model: facilitative-evaluative mediation as practised in US and
English commercial work. Confidentiality and enforceability rules vary (US Uniform
Mediation Act states, without-prejudice privilege in England & Wales, the EU Mediation
Directive, Singapore Convention for cross-border settlements) — check the seat.

## 1. Before the day (1–3 weeks out)
1. **Mediation agreement** signed by parties, counsel and mediator: confidentiality,
   without-prejudice status, mediator immunity and non-compellability, fees and split,
   authority to settle, who attends.
2. **Position papers and bundle:** each side's short statement (often ≤10 pages),
   key documents, and optionally a confidential note for the mediator only.
3. **Pre-mediation calls** with each side separately: what really drives the dispute,
   costs spent and to come, who has authority (insist the decision-maker attends, not
   a junior with a cap), any commercial relationship worth preserving.
4. Plan the room: one plenary room plus a private room per party; a schedule that
   allows a long afternoon (most movement happens after 3 p.m.).

## 2. Structure of the day
| Time | Stage | Mediator's aim |
|---|---|---|
| 09:00 | Private hellos | Re-establish rapport; check authority |
| 09:30 | Joint session (optional) | Ground rules; short openings by principals, not lawyers' speeches; skip if hostility will harden positions |
| 10:30 | First caucuses | Explore interests, risks, costs; no numbers yet |
| 12:00 | Opening offers | Encourage a credible first offer; manage reaction to an "insulting" number |
| 14:00 | Negotiation rounds | Shuttle; package non-monetary terms |
| 16:00+ | Impasse work | Techniques below |
| Close | Drafting | Signed terms before anyone leaves |

## 3. Caucus technique
- Ask: "What happens if this doesn't settle today?" → walk through the litigation
  path, time to trial, costs (including irrecoverable costs), enforcement risk.
- Build a **decision tree** with counsel: probability of each issue × outcome, minus
  costs → a risk-weighted value (the BATNA). Let them do the maths; you ask questions.
- Confirm what may be shared with the other side. Default: nothing from caucus is
  disclosed without express permission.
- Look for value beyond money: payment timing, future supply, apology, confidentiality,
  non-disparagement, credit notes, reference letters.

## 4. Breaking impasse
- **Bracketing:** "If they came to X, would you come to Y?" — narrows the range without commitment.
- **Conditional offers:** "We'll move to 700 if you move below 900."
- **Split the issues:** settle liability items one at a time; leave quantum to last.
- **Mediator's proposal:** a single number put to both privately; each answers yes/no
  confidentially; only mutual yes is disclosed. Use once, late.
- **Time-out or adjournment** with a short deadline, rather than declaring failure.

## 5. Settlement terms
Draft in the room: parties, amount, payment date and method, what is released
(claims known and unknown, related parties), costs, confidentiality, tax treatment,
mechanism if payment is missed (consent order / stay with liberty to restore), signatures
of principals. Cross-border: note whether the Singapore Convention applies.

## Gotchas
- A participant without real authority stalls the last hour — confirm in the pre-call.
- Evaluating too early makes you look partial; evaluate (if at all) only when asked
  and in caucus.
- "Agreement in principle" without signed terms often unravels overnight.
- Never carry a fact disclosed in confidence across rooms without permission.

## Verify
- [ ] Mediation agreement signed; authority to settle confirmed in writing.
- [ ] Each side has a written risk analysis of its litigation alternative.
- [ ] All non-monetary terms captured, not only the headline number.
- [ ] Settlement signed by principals with a default mechanism.
- [ ] Confidential material returned or destroyed per the agreement.
