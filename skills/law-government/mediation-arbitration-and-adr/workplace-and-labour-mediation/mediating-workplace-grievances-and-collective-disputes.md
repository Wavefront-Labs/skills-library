# Mediating workplace grievances and collective disputes

**Goal:** resolve conflict at work at the lowest level that will hold — two colleagues,
an employee and the organisation, or an employer and a union at the bargaining table —
without undermining statutory rights or the grievance process. Models: UK (Acas
conciliation, internal mediation) and US (labour mediation under the NLRA framework,
grievance mediation in union contracts). Statutory steps and time limits vary and are
revised — check current rules before advising.

## 1. Triage: which process?
| Situation | Process | Not suitable when |
|---|---|---|
| Two colleagues in conflict, team breakdown | Interpersonal workplace mediation | Allegations of harassment or discrimination needing investigation, safety risk |
| Employee grievance against management | Grievance mediation (before or alongside formal grievance) | Employee wants a formal finding; whistleblowing |
| Claim heading to tribunal / court | Statutory conciliation (e.g. Acas early conciliation in Great Britain, a precondition for most tribunal claims) | — |
| Union–employer contract talks stalled | Collective conciliation / labour mediation | Parties want an imposed outcome → arbitration |
Mediation must be voluntary for individuals and must not pause statutory time limits
unless the law says so — tell parties about limitation deadlines.

## 2. Interpersonal and grievance mediation (typically one day)
1. **Separate pre-meetings** (60–90 min each): their account, impact, what "better" looks
   like, fears about meeting the other person. Check for power and safety issues.
2. **Joint meeting:** each speaks uninterrupted; mediator summarises; agenda built from
   both lists; move from past events to future working arrangements.
3. **Working agreement:** concrete behaviours (how to communicate, meeting protocols,
   how work is allocated, how to raise future issues), review date (e.g. 4–6 weeks).
4. Confidentiality: the agreement belongs to the parties; managers receive only what
   both agree, often just "mediation took place; agreement reached".

## 3. Collective bargaining disputes
1. Get the **bargaining history**: expired agreement, proposals tabled, costings, any
   strike ballot or notice periods. (In the US, notice duties under NLRA s.8(d) run
   before contract expiry — check timing and the role of the federal or state mediation
   service, whose capacity has changed in recent years.)
2. Meet each team separately first; identify the true priorities behind the list
   (often 2–3 items matter; the rest are trading stock).
3. Run joint sessions on non-economic items first to build momentum; park economics
   until the end and cost every package on a shared basis.
4. **Interest-based bargaining** option: joint problem statements, shared data,
   option generation, criteria for choosing.
5. Impasse: side-bar meetings of chief negotiators only, mediator's packages, short
   cooling-off, or agreed fact-finding / interest arbitration.
6. Document a **tentative agreement** (TA) item-by-item with initials and dates;
   remind teams it is subject to ratification.

## 4. Settlements of individual claims
- Use the statutorily recognised form (e.g. Acas COT3 or a compliant settlement
  agreement with independent advice in Great Britain; in the US, releases that comply
  with ADEA/OWBPA timing for age claims).
- Include return-to-work terms or agreed references, tax treatment, confidentiality
  limited as the law now requires (whistleblowing and harassment carve-outs).

## Gotchas
- Using mediation to avoid investigating a serious harassment complaint.
- Managers attending "to observe" — it changes the room; agree attendees in advance.
- In collective talks, a mediator who leaks a team's bottom line loses both sides.
- Unratified TAs presented to members as "final" can trigger rejection.

## Verify
- [ ] Process matched to the dispute; unsuitable cases redirected.
- [ ] Parties told about statutory deadlines that mediation doesn't pause.
- [ ] Working agreement has specific behaviours and a review date.
- [ ] Collective packages fully costed; TAs signed item by item.
- [ ] Individual settlements in a legally valid form with required advice.
