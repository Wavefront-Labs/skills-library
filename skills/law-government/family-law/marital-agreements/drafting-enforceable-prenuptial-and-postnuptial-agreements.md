# Drafting enforceable prenuptial and postnuptial agreements

**Goal:** an agreement that does what the clients intend and survives a challenge
years later, when one spouse has every incentive to attack it.

## 1. The challenge grounds — draft against each
Courts typically refuse enforcement for:
1. **Involuntariness** — duress, undue pressure, last-minute signing.
2. **Inadequate disclosure** — no fair and reasonable disclosure of property and
   obligations (and no valid waiver of disclosure).
3. **Unconscionability** — at signing (most UPAA states) and in some states also at
   enforcement ("second look").
4. **Lack of independent legal advice** — required or heavily weighted in some
   states, mandatory when waiving spousal support in others (e.g. California).
5. **Formal defects** — must be in writing and signed; some states require
   notarisation or witnesses.
6. **Public-policy limits** — child support and custody can't be pre-determined.

## 2. Process safeguards (most failures happen here)
- **Timing**: deliver the draft well before the wedding — weeks, not days. California
  requires 7 days between presentation and signing; others assess timing on the facts.
- **Separate counsel** for each party; the weaker party's lawyer should be chosen and
  instructed independently (payment by the other party is common but disclose it).
- **Disclosure**: attach schedules of assets, liabilities and income with values and
  supporting documents; include tax returns where income matters. Keep proof of
  delivery. Waivers of disclosure are risky; avoid if possible.
- **Plain-language explanation** and an acknowledgment clause (read, understood, had
  advice, signed voluntarily) — helpful but not a cure for real defects.
- Translation/interpreter where either party isn't fluent in the drafting language.

## 3. Substantive drafting
- Define **separate vs marital/community property**, including income from and
  appreciation of separate property, contributions of marital effort to a separate
  business, commingled accounts, and the marital home.
- Spousal support: waiver, cap, or formula (e.g. by years married); consider
  fairness at enforcement — a sliding scale is more defensible than a total waiver.
- Death provisions coordinate with estate plan (elective-share waivers; ERISA spousal
  rights can only be waived by a spouse *after* marriage — re-sign plan waivers post-wedding).
- **Sunset** or review clauses (e.g. on birth of a child or after N years).
- Choice of law and forum; severability; dispute resolution.
- Debts, tax filing, and the treatment of gifts between spouses.

## 4. Postnuptial and separation agreements
Postnups: many US states apply stricter scrutiny (spouses owe fiduciary duties to
each other); UPMAA treats pre- and post-marital agreements alike. Consideration may be
questioned in some states. Separation/marital settlement agreements are usually
merged into or incorporated in the divorce decree — decide which; merger affects
enforcement (contempt vs contract) and modifiability.

## 5. England & Wales
Nuptial agreements are not strictly binding, but courts give effect to an agreement
freely entered into by each party with full appreciation of its implications, unless
it would be unfair to hold the parties to it in the circumstances (*Radmacher*).
Needs (especially of children) are the usual reason for departure. Best practice:
independent advice, disclosure, signing well before the wedding.

## Gotchas
- A spouse-to-be's lawyer who drafts for both sides creates a ready-made challenge.
- Agreements ignored in practice (e.g. separate accounts commingled) undermine themselves.
- Uncertain: about half of US states have adopted some version of the UPAA; standards
  differ — draft to the strictest plausible forum.

## Verify
- [ ] Draft delivered with adequate time before signing; dates documented.
- [ ] Each party had independent counsel.
- [ ] Disclosure schedules attached and receipt acknowledged.
- [ ] No child-support or custody terms; support terms tested for fairness at enforcement.
- [ ] Execution formalities met; ERISA waivers re-signed after marriage.
