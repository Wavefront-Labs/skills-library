# Adoption consents and parentage orders

**Goal:** create a legal parent–child relationship that is valid, final and
recognised everywhere the family will live — by adoption or by parentage determination.

## 1. Which route?
| Route | Typical use |
|---|---|
| Stepparent adoption | spouse adopts partner's child; other legal parent consents or rights terminated |
| Agency adoption | licensed agency places child after relinquishment |
| Private/independent | placement arranged directly (not allowed in some states/countries) |
| Foster-care adoption | after termination of parental rights in a child-welfare case |
| Intercountry | Hague Convention or non-Hague procedures plus immigration |
| Second-parent / confirmatory | secures a non-biological parent's status (recommended even with a birth certificate, for interstate/international recognition) |
| Parentage judgment | assisted reproduction or surrogacy, no adoption needed in many states |

## 2. Consents and relinquishment
- Who must consent: legal parents (birth mother; legal/presumed father; an
  acknowledged father), the agency/guardian, and often an older child (age threshold set by statute).
- **Timing**: many US states bar consent until a set time after birth (state-specific); England & Wales: a mother's consent to adoption is ineffective if given less
  than 6 weeks after birth (Adoption and Children Act 2002 s.52(3)).
- **Revocation windows** vary from none to weeks; after that, consent can usually be
  set aside only for fraud or duress.
- **Unmarried fathers**: rights depend on registration in a putative father registry,
  acknowledgment, or relationship with the child; give notice as the statute requires.
  Defective notice is the most common reason adoptions unravel.
- If a parent won't consent: grounds for dispensing with consent / terminating rights
  (abandonment, unfitness, failure to support or communicate), proven to a high standard.

## 3. ICWA and interstate placements
- **Indian Child Welfare Act** applies where the child is a member of, or eligible for
  membership in, a federally recognised tribe: notice to the tribe, placement
  preferences, heightened consent formalities (consent executed in writing and recorded before a court; a consent given before or within
  10 days after birth is not valid, 25 CFR 23.125), tribal court jurisdiction in some cases. Upheld in *Haaland v. Brackeen* (2023).
  Ask about tribal heritage at intake, every time.
- **ICPC**: approval from both states before a child crosses state lines for
  adoptive placement — leaving early can jeopardise the adoption.

## 4. Home study, placement, finalisation
Home study (background checks, interviews, home visit) before placement in most
cases; post-placement supervision; then a finalisation hearing and **adoption decree**.
Obtain the amended birth certificate; for intercountry adoptions, handle immigration
and citizenship (US Child Citizenship Act), and recognition/re-adoption where useful.

## 5. Assisted reproduction and surrogacy
- Gestational surrogacy agreements: enforceable by statute in many US states
  (UPA 2017 model) with requirements — independent counsel for each party,
  medical/psychological screening, executed before embryo transfer. Some states
  permit **pre-birth parentage orders**; others restrict or prohibit compensated surrogacy.
- England & Wales: the surrogate is the legal mother at birth; intended parents apply
  for a **parental order** (HFEA 2008 s.54) within 6 months beginning with the day of birth
  (late applications sometimes allowed); the surrogate's agreement is ineffective if given less than 6 weeks after the birth (s.54(7)); only expenses reasonably incurred may be paid unless the court authorises (s.54(8)).
- International surrogacy: citizenship and parentage recognition problems are common
  — plan before conception.

## Gotchas
- A voluntary acknowledgment of parentage can be challenged only within short windows.
- Payments to birth parents beyond permitted expenses can void adoptions or be criminal.
- Uncertain: consent timing, revocation periods and surrogacy rules differ widely
  by state and country; verify the statute where each step occurs.
