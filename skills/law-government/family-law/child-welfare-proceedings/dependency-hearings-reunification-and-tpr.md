# Dependency hearings, reunification and TPR

**Goal:** represent a parent, a child, or the state agency through a child-protection
case on statutory clocks that run from the day of removal. (US structure described
first; England & Wales care proceedings noted after.)

## 1. Stages and clocks (typical US)
1. **Removal** — emergency, or by court order.
2. **Shelter/detention hearing** — promptly (timing set by state law): is continued removal necessary
   to protect the child? Were reasonable efforts made to prevent removal? Consider
   in-home safety plans and relative placement.
3. **Adjudication** — did abuse/neglect as defined by statute occur? (preponderance in
   most states; clear and convincing in some; ICWA requires clear and convincing for
   foster placement).
4. **Disposition** — placement and **case plan** (services, visitation, conditions for return).
5. **Review hearings** — at least every 6 months.
6. **Permanency hearing** — within 12 months of entering care: reunification, adoption,
   guardianship, placement with a fit relative, or another planned permanent living arrangement.
7. **TPR** — ASFA generally requires the agency to petition when a child has been in
   foster care **15 of the most recent 22 months**, subject to exceptions (relative
   care, compelling reasons, services not provided).

## 2. Reasonable efforts (and ICWA active efforts)
Agencies must make reasonable efforts to prevent removal and to reunify (unless
aggravated circumstances excuse them — e.g. torture, prior TPR of a sibling). For
Indian children, **active efforts** — a higher, hands-on standard — are required.
Parent's counsel: challenge generic case plans, unavailable services, waitlists,
inaccessible locations, and missing visitation; get findings of "no reasonable
efforts" on the record — it matters for TPR and federal funding.

## 3. Representing each party
- **Parent**: meet early and often; get services started immediately (they drive
  the timeline); document compliance; push for frequent, less-supervised visitation;
  identify relatives for placement; coordinate with any parallel criminal case
  (Fifth Amendment risk in testimony and evaluations).
- **Child** (attorney or guardian ad litem/CASA, depending on the state model):
  client-directed vs best-interests role — know which you are; visit the child in
  placement; monitor school, health, sibling contact, and placement stability.
- **Agency**: evidence-based petitions, timely notice to all parents (including
  alleged fathers) and relatives, documentation of efforts, concurrent planning.

## 4. Termination of parental rights
Requires a statutory ground (abandonment, failure to remedy conditions, chronic
substance abuse, aggravated circumstances) **and** best interests. Constitution
requires at least **clear and convincing evidence** (*Santosky*); ICWA requires proof
**beyond reasonable doubt** with qualified expert testimony. 

## 5. England & Wales care proceedings
Local authority applies under Children Act 1989 s.31; the **threshold criteria**
(child suffering, or likely to suffer, significant harm attributable to care not
reasonable to expect from a parent) must be proven on the balance of probabilities.
Interim care orders on reasonable grounds; Public Law Outline; statutory **26-week**
target for completion; children's guardian from Cafcass.

## Gotchas
- Missed hearings or service starts early in the case often decide it.
- Admissions at adjudication can be used in criminal cases — advise carefully.
- Uncertain: hearing timelines, standards and counsel models vary by state; check local
  statute and court rules.

## Verify
- [ ] Every statutory hearing deadline calendared from removal date.
- [ ] Reasonable/active efforts findings sought or challenged at each hearing.
- [ ] Case plan specific, achievable and services actually available.
- [ ] Relatives and all parents identified and noticed; ICWA inquiry done.
- [ ] TPR grounds and burden of proof analysed early, not at the petition stage.
