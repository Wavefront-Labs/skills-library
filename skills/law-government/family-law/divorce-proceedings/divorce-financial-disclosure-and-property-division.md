# Divorce: financial disclosure and property division

**Goal:** dissolve the marriage and divide property on a complete, verified financial
picture — producing a decree that can actually be implemented.

## 1. Jurisdiction, grounds, and timing
- **Jurisdiction**: residency or domicile requirements (US state residency periods vary; England & Wales applies statutory jurisdiction tests, check the current rules). Cross-border
  couples: forum choice can change outcomes dramatically — analyse early.
- **Grounds**: every US state offers a no-fault ground (irreconcilable differences /
  irretrievable breakdown); some still allow fault grounds that may affect alimony or
  property in a few states. England & Wales: no-fault only since April 2022
  (statement of irretrievable breakdown; 20-week wait before conditional order).
- Waiting periods and separation requirements vary by state.
- Temporary orders: interim support, exclusive use of the home, status-quo orders
  restraining asset transfers (automatic in some states on filing).

## 2. Financial disclosure — the core of the case
- Mandatory sworn financial statements (e.g. state financial affidavits; Form E in
  England & Wales) with supporting documents: tax returns, payslips, bank, brokerage,
  pension, business accounts, loans, credit cards, property valuations.
- Discovery tools: requests for production, subpoenas to banks/employers, depositions,
  questionnaires. Look for: unexplained transfers, cash businesses, crypto, deferred
  bonuses, shifting income to entities, new debts.
- Non-disclosure can lead to adverse inferences, cost penalties, or setting aside a
  final order later (*Sharland*, *Gohil*, UKSC 2015).

## 3. Characterise, value, divide
1. **Characterise**: marital/community vs separate (pre-marital, gifts, inheritances).
   Separate property can become marital by **commingling** or **transmutation**;
   preserve it by **tracing** with records. Appreciation of separate property during
   marriage may be marital if due to marital effort (rules vary).
2. **Value**: pick a valuation date (filing, separation, trial — state-specific).
   Experts for businesses (fair market vs fair value; goodwill personal vs enterprise),
   real property, pensions (present value or deferred division).
3. **Divide**:
   - **Community property** states (AZ, CA, ID, LA, NV, NM, TX, WA, WI; elective in
     some others): presumptively equal division, with variations (CA strict 50/50; TX
     "just and right").
   - **Equitable distribution** (other states): fair, not necessarily equal; statutory
     factors (duration, contributions incl. homemaking, economic circumstances,
     dissipation).
   - England & Wales: discretionary s.25 factors; "yardstick of equality" (*White*) and
     principles of needs, compensation and sharing (*Miller; McFarlane*).
4. **Debts** are allocated too; creditors aren't bound by the decree.

## 4. Implementation
- Retirement plans: **QDRO** for ERISA plans (separate order approved by the plan
  administrator); pension-sharing orders in England & Wales. Draft and pre-approve with
  the plan — many decrees go wrong here.
- Real estate deeds, refinancing deadlines, title transfers, tax consequences
  (basis, capital gains, transfer-tax exemptions incident to divorce).
- Final decree/consent order language specific enough to enforce.

## Gotchas
- Dissipation claims need evidence of spending for non-marital purposes after breakdown.
- Tax effects (e.g. pre-tax vs post-tax assets) make "equal" splits unequal.
- Uncertain: characterisation and valuation-date rules differ greatly; check local law.

## Verify
- [ ] Jurisdiction and forum options analysed; temporary/status-quo orders in place.
- [ ] Sworn disclosure complete and cross-checked against documents.
- [ ] Every asset characterised with tracing evidence for separate claims.
- [ ] Valuations dated consistently and experts engaged where needed.
- [ ] QDRO/pension orders and transfers drafted for implementation.
