# Support calculation, imputation and modification

**Goal:** get the right number on the order — then keep it right when life changes,
and collect it when it isn't paid.

## 1. Child support guidelines
Every US state must have presumptive guidelines (federal requirement for IV-D funding,
reviewed at least every 4 years). Three models:
- **Income shares** (most states): combine both parents' incomes, read the basic
  obligation from a schedule, split by each parent's share of combined income.
- **Percentage of obligor income** (a few states): fixed percentage by number of children.
- **Melson formula** (a few states): self-support reserve, then primary support, then
  standard-of-living share.
Add-ons: health insurance, uninsured medical, childcare, sometimes education/extracurriculars.
Adjustments: shared-parenting credit above an overnight threshold; multiple-family
adjustments; low-income self-support reserve.
Great Britain: the Child Maintenance Service applies a statutory gross-income formula
with reductions for shared care nights; courts handle top-ups above the cap,
school fees and disability costs.

## 2. Determining income
- Gross income definitions are broad: wages, bonuses, commissions, self-employment
  net (with add-backs for personal expenses, excessive depreciation), rental,
  investment, some benefits.
- **Imputation**: where a parent is voluntarily unemployed/underemployed, impute based
  on earning capacity (work history, qualifications, job market) — the federal guideline
  rule (45 CFR 302.56(c)(1)(iii)) requires considering the parent's specific circumstances
  (assets, work history, skills, health, local job market). Evidence: vocational experts, job-search logs.
- Self-employed and closely held businesses: get tax returns, general ledgers, bank
  statements; trace personal expenses through the business.

## 3. Deviations
The guideline amount is presumptively correct; deviation requires written findings
that it would be unjust or inappropriate, using statutory factors (extraordinary
needs, high income beyond the schedule, special custodial arrangements).

## 4. Spousal support (alimony/maintenance)
Usually discretionary, with statutory factors: length of marriage, standard of living,
each party's earning capacity and contributions (including career sacrifices), age
and health, assets after property division, child-care responsibilities.
Types: temporary (pendente lite), rehabilitative (to retrain), bridge-the-gap,
durational, and (increasingly limited) permanent. Some states use formulas or
duration caps tied to marriage length. US federal tax: for divorce instruments
executed after 2018, alimony is neither deductible nor taxable income.
England & Wales: periodical payments with a "clean break" duty to consider termination.

## 5. Modification
Requires a **substantial (material) change in circumstances** since the last order —
job loss, significant income change, change in custody, child's needs. Child support
modifications typically are not retroactive before the filing/notice date — file
promptly. Guideline-based percentage thresholds trigger review in many states.
Agreements labelled non-modifiable may bind for spousal support but not child support.

## 6. Enforcement
Income withholding orders (default in US), interception of tax refunds, licence
suspension, passport denial, liens, credit reporting, contempt. Interstate: **UIFSA**
— one controlling order, registration for enforcement elsewhere. Internationally,
the 2007 Hague Child Support Convention.

## Gotchas
- Arrears generally can't be retroactively forgiven by a court in the US (Bradley
  Amendment); act before arrears accrue.
- Imputing income without evidence is a common appeal reversal ground.
- Uncertain: guideline schedules and alimony statutes are revised frequently by state.

- [ ] Add-ons and shared-parenting adjustments applied.
- [ ] Deviations supported by written findings.
- [ ] Modification filed promptly after change; enforcement tools selected.
