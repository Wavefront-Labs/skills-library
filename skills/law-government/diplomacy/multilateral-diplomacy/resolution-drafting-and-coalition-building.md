# Resolution drafting and coalition building

**Goal:** get your priorities into an adopted text in an international organisation
or conference — usually by consensus — and avoid procedural ambushes.

## 1. Anatomy of a resolution
- **Preambular paragraphs (PPs):** context; begin with participles in italics —
  *Recalling*, *Reaffirming*, *Noting with concern*, *Recognizing*. Not operative.
- **Operative paragraphs (OPs):** numbered actions; begin with present-tense verbs —
  *Decides*, *Requests* the Secretary-General, *Calls upon*, *Encourages*, *Urges*.
  Verb strength is negotiated: *decides* > *urges* > *calls upon* > *encourages* >
  *invites*. Only certain bodies' decisions bind (e.g. Security Council under
  Chapter VII); most resolutions are recommendations.
- **Agreed language:** text adopted previously by the same body is the currency of
  compromise. Keep a searchable library; citing "agreed language from resolution
  X/Y, OP7" settles many disputes.
- Programme-budget implications: OPs requesting reports or meetings may carry
  costs reviewed by the budget committee — check before adding them.

## 2. The negotiation cycle
1. **Penholder / main sponsor** circulates a zero draft.
2. **Informal consultations ("informals")** paragraph by paragraph; facilitators run them.
3. Revised drafts; bilateral side-meetings on hard paragraphs.
4. **Silence procedure:** text deemed agreed unless someone "breaks silence" by a
   deadline. Breaking silence is a significant act — coordinate before doing it.
5. Tabling, co-sponsorship, adoption in committee then plenary.

## 3. Coalitions
- Map positions: regional groups, negotiating blocs (e.g. G77 and China, the EU
  speaking as one, Arab Group, small island developing states), cross-regional core groups.
- Work the bloc coordinator; blocs negotiate collectively and individual members may
  not deviate publicly.
- Co-sponsor count signals legitimacy; cross-regional sponsors matter more than numbers.
- Trade: support on their paragraph for support on yours — keep a ledger.

## 4. Consensus, votes and procedure
- Consensus means no formal objection, not unanimity of enthusiasm; "join consensus"
  with an **explanation of position** to register reservations.
- If a vote is called: recorded vs. non-recorded, paragraph votes, amendments voted
  before the text. **Explanations of vote** before or after (not by sponsors in GA
  practice after voting on their own text — check body's rules).
- Procedural motions to know: point of order, adjournment of debate, **no-action
  motion** (kills a draft without a substantive vote), motions take precedence in a
  fixed order set by the rules of procedure.

## 5. Chairing / facilitating
Publish a timetable; work from the text not general statements; use "friends of
the chair" small groups for hard issues; produce a chair's compromise text late,
when fatigue makes it acceptable; keep everyone informed to avoid "process" complaints.

## Gotchas
- An unnoticed adjective in a PP can become "agreed language" against you next year.
- Late-night drafts: verify the final version circulated is the one agreed.
- Uncertain: rules and voting practices differ between UN organs, specialised
  agencies and treaty conferences of the parties; read the specific rules of procedure.

## Verify

- [ ] Agreed-language sources cited for contested text.
- [ ] Bloc positions mapped and coordinators engaged.
- [ ] Budget implications checked.
- [ ] Procedural scenario (vote, amendments, no-action) planned with EOV drafted.
