# Precedence, credentials and immunities

**Goal:** run the formal machinery of diplomatic relations so that nobody is
offended by accident, and privileges and immunities are applied correctly — neither
denied nor stretched.

## 1. Accrediting a head of mission
1. Sending state asks for **agrément** for the proposed ambassador (VCDR art. 4);
   the receiving state need not give reasons for refusal. Keep requests confidential.
2. On arrival the ambassador presents a **copy of credentials** to the foreign
   minister, then the **letters of credence** to the head of state at a ceremony.
3. **Precedence among heads of mission** within each class runs from the date and
   time of taking up functions (art. 13 & 16) — either credentials presentation or
   notification of arrival with copy, depending on the receiving state's uniform
   practice. The longest-serving ambassador is usually **doyen** (dean) of the corps;
   some Catholic-majority states give the apostolic nuncio that role.

## 2. Precedence and seating
- Each state publishes or maintains its own **domestic order of precedence** (head of
  state, head of government, speaker, chief justice, ministers…). Foreign
  dignitaries are slotted in by rank and the host's practice.
- Seating: guest of honour to the host's right; alternate by rank around the table;
  co-host opposite the host in "French" or "English" style seating. Prepare the
  plan from the precedence list, then adjust for language and politics — and record why.
- Flags: host flag in the position of honour on home soil per national rules;
  check flag codes for order, size and half-masting.

## 3. Visits
Classify the visit (state, official, working, private) — it determines honours,
ceremonies and who meets the plane. Build a minute-by-minute programme; recce every
venue; agree gifts, interpreters, media access, security and medical cover; brief
on forms of address and cultural sensitivities.

## 4. Privileges and immunities (receiving-state side)
| Who | Core protection |
|---|---|
| Diplomatic agents | inviolability of person (art. 29); full criminal immunity; civil immunity with exceptions — private immovable property, succession, private professional/commercial activity (art. 31) |
| Family members forming part of household | same, if not nationals (art. 37(1)) |
| Administrative & technical staff | criminal immunity; civil immunity only for official acts (art. 37(2)) |
| Consular officers | functional immunity for official acts; personal inviolability limited (VCCR art. 41, 43) |
Mission premises, archives and the diplomatic bag are inviolable (arts. 22, 24, 27).
Remedies for abuse: request a **waiver** (only the sending state can waive, art. 32),
declare **persona non grata** (art. 9) at any time without reasons, or require the
person's withdrawal. Immunity is not impunity from the sending state's jurisdiction (art. 31(4)).

Administer it: maintain the diplomatic list, issue ID cards that state the level of
immunity, notify police of arrivals/departures, handle traffic and tax exemptions.

## Gotchas
- Nationals and permanent residents of the receiving state get much narrower
  immunity (art. 38).
- Immunity continues for official acts after functions end; personal immunity lapses
  after a reasonable period to leave (art. 39(2)).
- Uncertain: domestic precedence lists, honours and seating customs are national;
  reconcile with the host's protocol office every time.

## Verify
- [ ] Agrément obtained before announcement; credentials sequence followed.
- [ ] Precedence list current and seating plan checked against it.
- [ ] Visit category set and programme recce'd.
- [ ] Each accredited person's immunity category recorded correctly on ID.
- [ ] Abuse cases routed to waiver request or PNG, not to arrest.
