# Planning a public diplomacy campaign

**Goal:** change something specific in what a foreign audience knows, thinks or does,
in support of a policy goal — not just "raise awareness" — and be able to show it worked.

## 1. Start from the policy objective
Write it as: *[Audience] will [know/believe/do X] by [date], which supports
[policy goal].* Example: "University leaders in country B will see our country as a
first-choice research partner, increasing joint grant applications". If you can't
link it to a policy goal, it is promotion, not diplomacy.

## 2. Audience analysis
- Segment narrowly (students, regional journalists, diaspora, business chambers,
  youth on a given platform) — "the public" is not an audience.
- What do they already think? Use polling, media monitoring, social listening,
  local staff knowledge.
- Where do they get information and whom do they trust?

## 3. The toolkit (Cull's categories, simplified)
| Tool | Use when |
|---|---|
| **Listening** | always first; feeds everything else |
| **Advocacy** (press, speeches, op-eds, social media) | short-term explanation of policy |
| **Cultural diplomacy** (arts, language, heritage) | long-term affinity |
| **Exchanges** (scholarships, visitor programmes) | building elite networks over decades; alumni are the payoff |
| **International broadcasting** | reach where media is closed (usually a separate agency) |
Credibility often rises when *you* step back — local partners, alumni, scientists,
artists speak more credibly than the embassy.

## 4. Message and messengers
One core message, three supporting points, local proof stories. Test wording with
local staff for translation traps and connotations. Match messengers to segments.

## 5. Media engagement
- Know the local media landscape and ownership; build relationships with
  journalists before you need them.
- Offer access: interviews, background briefings (agree attribution rules —
  on record, background, deep background, off the record — explicitly).
- Rapid response protocol: who can speak, approval times, holding lines.
- Digital: platform-specific content; respond in the local language; keep a record of
  official accounts and authorised posters.

## 6. Measurement
- **Outputs**: events held, participants, articles placed (necessary, not sufficient).
- **Outcomes**: change in attitudes (pre/post surveys), behaviour (applications,
  partnerships, alumni activity), media framing shift.
- Use a simple logframe or theory of change; set baselines before launch.

## Gotchas
- Messages that contradict your government's actions fail; public diplomacy can't
  outrun policy.
- Host-country sensitivities: funding local NGOs or influencers may breach host law
  on foreign agents/funding — check.
- Uncertain: attribution-rule definitions vary between countries' press corps;
  always agree terms with the journalist in advance.

## Verify
- [ ] Objective names an audience, a change and a policy link.
- [ ] Audience segmented with baseline data.
- [ ] Messengers chosen for credibility with each segment.
- [ ] Media attribution terms and approval chain set.
- [ ] Outcome indicators and baseline defined before launch.
