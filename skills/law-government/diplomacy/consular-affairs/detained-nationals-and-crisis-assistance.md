# Detained nationals and crisis assistance

**Goal:** protect nationals in trouble abroad within the limits of international law
and the host state's sovereignty — and manage the expectations of families and media.

## 1. Arrest and detention (VCCR art. 36)
- Host authorities must inform a detained foreign national **without delay** of the
  right to have their consulate notified, and notify the consulate if the person
  requests it. Some bilateral treaties make notification **mandatory** regardless of
  the detainee's wishes — check lists of such agreements.
- Consular officers have the right to communicate with, visit and arrange legal
  representation for the detainee, but must not act if the detainee expressly objects.
- The ICJ held in *LaGrand* and *Avena* that art. 36 creates individual rights; how
  domestic courts remedy violations varies (US courts generally do not suppress
  evidence for breach — *Sanchez-Llamas*, 2006).

## 2. The first prison visit
Checklist: identity and nationality confirmed (dual nationals — the host state may
not recognise your nationality); welfare and medical needs; allegations of
mistreatment (record, and ask consent before raising); charges and next hearing;
lawyer (provide a list of local lawyers — not a recommendation); family contact
consent; privacy waiver for sharing information; explain local process and timelines.
Report the visit in a standard format.

## 3. What consuls can and cannot do
Can: visit, monitor welfare, raise mistreatment or fair-trial concerns, provide
lawyer lists, liaise with family, help transfer funds, issue emergency travel
documents, help after deaths abroad (registration, repatriation options).
Cannot (normally): get someone out of prison, interfere in judicial proceedings,
pay legal fees or fines, give legal advice, investigate crimes. Say this early to families.

## 4. Crisis response
- **Before:** registration of nationals (warden/registration schemes), contact trees,
  assembly points, liaison with like-minded missions, travel advice levels.
- **During:** staffing a crisis cell, call-handling scripts, consolidated nationals
  database, coordination with host authorities and other states for departure options
  (commercial first; charter or military-assisted evacuation as last resort; costs
  may be recoverable from evacuees depending on national policy).
- **After:** accounting for all registered nationals, welfare follow-up, lessons log.

## 5. Notarial and documentary services
Authenticating signatures, certifying copies, administering oaths, issuing
emergency passports, and (where national law allows) registering births and deaths
abroad. Note the **Apostille Convention** removes legalisation for documents between
member states — consular legalisation is then not required.

## Gotchas
- Privacy law at home may bar telling a family anything without the detainee's consent.
- Dual nationals in their other country of nationality may get little or no
  consular access.
- Uncertain: the scope of services and fees is set by each state's own law and
  policy; the convention sets the floor, not the menu.

## Verify
- [ ] Notification received/requested and access visit made promptly.
- [ ] Mistreatment allegations recorded and raised only with consent.
- [ ] Family told clearly what consular staff can and cannot do.
- [ ] Privacy waiver on file before sharing information.
- [ ] Crisis plan has registration data, assembly points and partner-mission contacts.
