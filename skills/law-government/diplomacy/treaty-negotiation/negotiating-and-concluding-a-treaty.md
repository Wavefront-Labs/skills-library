# Negotiating and concluding a treaty

**Goal:** take an agreement from instructions to binding effect without losing
content in the drafting or legal validity in the formalities.

## 1. Mandate and authority
- Get a written **negotiating mandate**: objectives, red lines, fallback positions,
  who can approve deviations. Ministries of foreign affairs, the line ministry and
  the treasury often each have stakes — settle internal positions first.
- Know who has authority: heads of state/government and foreign ministers act
  without **full powers**; others need an instrument of full powers to adopt or sign
  (VCLT art. 7). Check full powers early — absence delays signing ceremonies.
- Decide early: is this meant to be **legally binding** (treaty) or a political
  commitment (many MOUs)? Wording signals it: "shall/agree" vs. "will/intend";
  "enter into force" vs. "come into effect". Some states differ on how they classify
  MOUs — state your understanding explicitly.

## 2. Running the text
- One **single negotiating text** held by the chair or one party; all changes tracked.
- **Brackets** mark unagreed language; footnote who holds which alternative.
  Nothing is agreed until everything is agreed.
- Agree **ad referendum** (subject to capital's approval) when you lack authority on
  the spot; say so clearly.
- Keep a negotiating record: it may later be used as supplementary means of
  interpretation (VCLT art. 32).
- Final clauses are not boilerplate: entry-into-force trigger (number of
  ratifications, date), depositary, reservations, amendment, withdrawal, duration,
  authentic languages, dispute settlement.

## 3. Closing the text
- **Initialling** authenticates the text (art. 10) but normally does not bind.
- Multilateral conferences often adopt a **Final Act** recording the outcome.
- Legal-linguistic scrub: all language versions checked for consistency before signature.

## 4. From signature to binding
| Step | Effect |
|---|---|
| Signature subject to ratification | no binding obligation yet, but duty not to defeat object and purpose (art. 18) |
| Ratification / acceptance / approval / accession | expression of consent to be bound (arts. 14-16) |
| Deposit or exchange of instruments | consent becomes effective |
| Entry into force | as the treaty provides (art. 24) |
| Registration with the UN Secretariat | art. 102 UN Charter; unregistered treaties can't be invoked before UN organs |
Domestic steps sit between signature and ratification: parliamentary approval,
implementing legislation (dualist systems), constitutional review. Map them for
your state before signing — they drive the timeline.

## 5. Reservations
Permitted unless prohibited by the treaty or incompatible with its object and
purpose (art. 19). Other parties may object. Interpretive declarations are not
reservations but are sometimes argued to be. Draft deliberately.

## Gotchas
- "Provisional application" (art. 25) can bind before entry into force — check
  domestic legality.
- Inconsistent language versions lead to disputes; designate which texts are authentic.
- Uncertain: domestic ratification procedures differ greatly (e.g. US Senate advice
  and consent for Article II treaties vs. executive agreements; UK CRaG Act laying
  procedure) — verify for the state concerned.
