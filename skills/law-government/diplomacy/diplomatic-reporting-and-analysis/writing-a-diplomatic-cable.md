# Writing a diplomatic cable

**Goal:** tell busy readers in capital, in the first paragraph, what happened, why it
matters to them, and what (if anything) they should do — with sourcing they can weigh.

## 1. Know what is worth reporting
Report what capital cannot get from the news: private views of decision-makers, the
meaning behind public events, coming decisions, and risks to your interests. VCDR
art. 3(1)(d) allows ascertaining conditions "by all lawful means" — reporting is
not espionage, and contact work must stay overt.

Cable types: **spot report** (event, same day), **meeting readout**, **scene-setter**
(before a visit), **analytical/think piece**, **response to tasking**.

## 2. Structure
1. **Subject line**: informative, not cute ("Coalition likely to collapse before
   budget vote — PM's adviser").
2. **Summary (BLUF)**: 3–5 sentences — the news, its significance, outlook, and any
   action requested. Many readers stop here.
3. **Body**: numbered paragraphs; one idea each; chronology only where it matters.
4. **Comment**: clearly labelled, the post's judgement. Keep fact and assessment
   separate — readers must be able to tell what was said from what you think.
5. **Action request / recommendation** where relevant.

## 3. Sourcing
- Describe the source's access and reliability ("a senior official in the finance
   ministry with direct involvement" vs. "a well-connected journalist").
- Distinguish: said to us directly; reported second-hand; public statement; our inference.
- **Protect sources.** Use protective handling caveats per your service's rules; avoid
  identifying detail where it isn't needed. Assume leaks are possible (mass cable
  leaks have exposed contacts).

## 4. Analytic quality
- Use calibrated language for likelihood ("likely", "almost certainly", "we cannot
   judge") and be consistent — some services use a formal probability yardstick.
- State key assumptions and what would change your view (indicators).
- Offer alternative explanations for ambiguous events.
- Link to your country's interests and policy, not just local drama.

## 5. Style
Short words, active voice, no unexplained acronyms, names in full on first mention
with title. Avoid adjectives as analysis. Respect the reader's time: most cables
should fit on two screens.

## 6. Classification and distribution
Classify at the lowest level appropriate to the most sensitive paragraph (paragraph
markings where the system requires them); choose distribution to reach who needs it
without over-broadcasting. Clear the draft with the head of section/mission per post rules.

## Gotchas
- Burying the news in paragraph 7 means it is not read.
- Over-classification limits reach; under-classification risks sources.
- Reporting your own lobbying without its result reads as self-promotion — report outcomes.
- Uncertain: format conventions (headers, caveats, paragraph markings) are
  service-specific; follow your ministry's handbook.

## Verify
- [ ] Summary stands alone and states significance and outlook.
- [ ] Every claim attributed with access/reliability indicated.
- [ ] Comment section clearly separate from reporting.
- [ ] Likelihood language consistent; indicators named.
- [ ] Classification and handling caveats correct; source identifiers minimised.
