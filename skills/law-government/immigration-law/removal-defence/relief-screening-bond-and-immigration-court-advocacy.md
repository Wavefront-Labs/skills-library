# Relief screening, bond and immigration-court advocacy

**Goal:** keep the client in the country lawfully — by contesting removability, winning
release, and proving eligibility for the best available relief — while preserving every
issue for appeal. Terms are US (EOIR immigration courts, BIA). UK appeals (First-tier
Tribunal) and other systems differ substantially; the triage logic transfers.

## 1. First meeting: read the charging document
On the **Notice to Appear** (NTA), check:
- **Factual allegations** (citizenship, manner of entry, convictions) — are they true?
- **Charges** of removability — do they legally follow from the facts? Is the person
  charged as inadmissible (never admitted) or deportable (admitted)? That choice changes
  burdens and available relief.
- Defects: wrong name/identity, missing time/place information (litigated issue in the
  US — check current precedent), service problems.
- Is there any **claim to citizenship** (derivation/acquisition through a parent)? A
  citizen cannot be removed; screen this first. See
  law-government.immigration-law.citizenship-and-naturalisation.

## 2. Relief screening — check everything
Use a written checklist on every case; relief is often hiding in family history.
- **Asylum / withholding / CAT protection** (fear of return).
- **Cancellation of removal** (INA 240A) — for permanent residents (5 years as an LPR,
  7 years of continuous residence after admission in any status, no aggravated felony
  conviction) and for non-permanent residents (10 years of continuous physical presence,
  good moral character, and "exceptional and extremely unusual hardship" to a qualifying
  citizen or LPR spouse, parent or child). Watch the "stop-time" rule: service of a notice
  to appear or commission of certain offences ends the period.
- **Adjustment of status** before the judge via a family or employment petition.
- Special categories: special immigrant juvenile status, U visa (crime victims),
  T visa (trafficking), VAWA self-petitions, TPS.
- **Prosecutorial discretion** / dismissal or administrative closure (availability
  shifts with government policy).
- **Voluntary departure** — the fallback, requested at the right stage; check the
  statutory conditions.
- Termination: contest removability itself.

## 3. Detention and bond
- Determine whether detention is discretionary or **mandatory**; check the current
  statute and precedent for the client's category.
- Bond packet: confirm who bears the burden under current precedent. Evidence: fixed address, sponsor letters with status proof,
  family ties, employment, community letters, rehabilitation evidence, prima facie
  eligibility for relief (a strong case to stay = reason to appear).
- Propose an amount and conditions; a bond decision can often be appealed or re-sought
  on changed circumstances.

## 4. Hearings
- **Master calendar** (short procedural): admit/deny allegations, concede or contest
  charges, designate country of removal (or decline), identify relief, get filing
  deadlines. Never concede removability reflexively if it's contestable.
- Meet court filing deadlines strictly (application, evidence, witness list) — late
  evidence can be excluded.
- **Individual (merits) hearing**: prepare direct examination as a story; prepare the
  client and witnesses for government cross; have interpreter-tested exhibits; know the
  judge's prior rulings if published practice allows.
- Object on the record, request what you need, and get rulings stated clearly — issues
  not raised below are often waived on appeal.

## 5. After the decision
- Appeal deadlines are short (US: a Notice of Appeal, Form EOIR-26, must reach the BIA
  within 10 calendar days of an Immigration Judge's decision, or 30 calendar days where the
  judge adjudicated an asylum application and did not deny it as barred under INA
  208(a)(2)(A)-(C); 8 CFR 1003.38(b)). Diarise on decision day.
- Motions to reopen/reconsider have their own time and number limits.
- **In absentia** orders: a missed hearing can lead to a removal order. Make sure the
  court and agency have the client's current address; file change-of-address forms
  immediately.

## Gotchas
- Criminal pleas taken without immigration advice can eliminate relief — coordinate
  with criminal counsel.
- Filing a weak application can create statements used later; screen carefully before filing.

## Verify
- [ ] Allegations and charges reviewed; citizenship claim screened.
- [ ] Full relief checklist completed and documented, including fallback voluntary departure.
- [ ] Detention category determined; bond packet filed if eligible.
- [ ] All court deadlines diarised; address on file current.
- [ ] Objections and issues preserved; appeal deadline calendared on decision day.
