# Naturalisation eligibility, good moral character and the interview

**Goal:** file a naturalisation application only when it will succeed and won't expose
the client to removal, and catch clients who are already citizens by descent. US terms
(N-400, good moral character); other countries use similar tests (residence periods,
"good character", language and knowledge tests) with different numbers. Verify current
requirements for the jurisdiction.

## 1. Check citizenship already held — first
Before naturalising, ask whether the client **already is a citizen**:
- **Acquired** at birth abroad through a citizen parent (rules depend on birth date and
  parents' residence history — use the transmission chart for the applicable period).
- **Derived** as a child when a parent naturalised (conditions such as being under 18, a
  permanent resident, and in the citizen parent's custody; the law in force at the time
  applies).
If yes, the right filing is a citizenship certificate or passport, not naturalisation.

## 2. Statutory eligibility (US outline)
| Requirement | Typical rule (verify) |
|---|---|
| Permanent residence | 5 years (3 if married to and living with a citizen spouse) |
| Continuous residence | An absence of more than 6 months but less than 1 year is presumed to break continuity (rebuttable); 1 year or more breaks it unless an approved Form N-470 applies |
| Physical presence | At least half of the statutory period inside the country (INA 316(a)) |
| Local residence | At least 3 months in the state or USCIS district before filing (INA 316(a)) |
| English & civics | Tested at interview; age/residence-based and disability exemptions exist |
| Early filing | Check the current USCIS Policy Manual for the early-filing window |

Build a **travel table** from passports and entry/exit records; clients routinely
under-count trips.

## 3. Good moral character (GMC) screen
GMC is assessed over the statutory period, but conduct before it can still matter.
Ask every client — in writing, with examples — about:
- Any arrest, citation, or charge, **even if dismissed, expunged or juvenile**. Get
  certified dispositions.
- Permanent and conditional bars to good moral character (see the USCIS Policy Manual,
  Volume 12, Part F) and any other conduct the Policy Manual lists.
- Unpaid taxes or failure to file; unpaid child support.
- **False claims to citizenship** or voting in elections — severe consequences.
- How the green card was obtained: if permanent residence was obtained by error or
  fraud, applying can lead to **removal** instead of citizenship.

When the screen surfaces a removability issue, the right advice may be "don't file" —
the application invites a full review of the immigration history.

## 4. Preparing the application and interview
- Answer every question truthfully and consistently with previous filings; the officer
  will compare. Explain "yes" answers with documents.
- Practise the civics questions and reading/writing sentences using the agency's
  current official materials.
- Rehearse the interview: the officer reviews the application line by line under oath;
  the client should understand each answer and be able to explain any arrests or trips.
- Bring originals: green card, passports, dispositions, tax transcripts, marriage/divorce
  records.

## 5. After the interview
- Possible outcomes: approved, continued (more evidence or retest), or denied. A US denial
  can be challenged by an administrative hearing request; check the deadline on the
  denial notice.
- Status remains permanent resident until the **oath**; don't travel in ways that
  jeopardise continuity in the meantime, and report any new arrest before the oath.

## Gotchas
- Dual citizenship: some home countries strip citizenship on naturalising elsewhere —
  client should check.
- Rules for physical presence differ between countries.

## Verify
- [ ] Acquired/derived citizenship ruled out (or claimed) before naturalising.
- [ ] Residence and physical-presence calculation done from a full travel table.
- [ ] GMC questionnaire completed with certified dispositions for every arrest.
- [ ] Original green card basis reviewed for removal risk.
- [ ] Client practised current civics/English tests and the line-by-line review.
