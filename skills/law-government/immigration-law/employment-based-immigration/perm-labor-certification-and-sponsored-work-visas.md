# PERM labour certification and sponsored work visas

**Goal:** move a foreign worker from job offer to sponsored status and (where wanted)
permanent residence, while keeping the employer audit-proof. Described with US
terminology (PERM, LCA, H-1B); analogues include the UK sponsor licence / Skilled
Worker route and Canada's LMIA. Details, timelines and wage rules differ a lot by
system and change often — treat numbers here as illustrative and verify current rules.

**US H-1B changes to check (as read on 2026-10-24 from USCIS and the Federal Register):** A presidential proclamation of 19 September 2025 required an additional USD 100,000 payment for certain H-1B petitions filed on or after 21 September 2025. USCIS reports that on 8 June 2026 the District of Massachusetts vacated the agency guidance implementing the payment (State of California v. Mullin), that the First Circuit denied a stay on 24 July 2026, and that DHS said it would comply with the order while considering next steps and still plans to collect the payment if the order is lifted. Other litigation is reported; the USCIS H-1B page was last updated 21 September 2026 and may be out of date. A final rule (published 29 December 2025, effective 27 February 2026) replaced the random cap lottery with wage-level-weighted selection (level IV gets 4 entries, III 3, II 2, I 1) from the FY2027 registration season. Confirm the current position on the USCIS H-1B page before advising, and have an immigration attorney review any filing strategy.

## 1. Define the job before anything else
The job description drives the wage level, the recruitment, and the eligibility category.
- Write the **actual minimum requirements** (education, experience, special skills) —
  not the candidate's CV. Requirements tailored to the foreign worker are the classic
  PERM audit/denial issue.
- If the worker is already employed by the sponsor, the employer generally cannot require
  more training or experience than the worker had at hire, with exceptions (20 CFR
  656.17(i)(3)) — check before drafting.
- Pick the occupational classification (US: SOC code) honestly; it sets the prevailing
  wage.

## 2. Prevailing wage
- US: request a prevailing wage determination from the Department of Labor for PERM;
  the offered wage must meet or exceed it. For H-1B, the Labor Condition Application
  (LCA) attests to paying the higher of actual or prevailing wage.
- Wage level is driven by requirements: adding years of experience or special skills
  can push the job to a higher wage level. That is the core "prevailing wage strategy"
  trade-off — never understate genuine requirements to lower the wage.

## 3. Labour-market test (recruitment)
For PERM, the employer must show no able, willing, qualified and available US worker
applied. Typical structure (verify current regulation):
- Mandatory steps for professional occupations (20 CFR 656.17(e)(1)): a 30-day job order
  with the state workforce agency and two print advertisements (generally Sunday
  editions; a professional-journal ad can replace one in some cases).
- Additional steps for professional roles: choose three of the ten listed options
  (employer website, job fairs, job search sites, campus recruiting, etc.); only one may
  fall entirely within the 30 days before filing.
- **Timing windows**: the mandatory steps must fall at least 30 and no more than 180 days
  before filing, and no step may be more than 180 days old at filing. Calendar them
  backwards from the target filing date.
- Every applicant must be reviewed in good faith and rejected only for lawful,
  job-related reasons, documented in a **recruitment report**.

## 4. Filing and the green-card chain
- PERM certification → immigrant petition (I-140) → adjustment or consular processing,
  gated by the **priority date** and the monthly Visa Bulletin. For some nationalities in
  EB-2/EB-3 the wait is many years; advise clients on that up front.
- Keep the worker's non-immigrant status valid throughout.

## 5. Employer compliance (the part clients forget)
- **Public access file** for each LCA (US), retained for the required period.
- **PERM audit file**: recruitment evidence, résumés, the recruitment report, the
  prevailing wage determination — retained for 5 years from the date of filing (20 CFR 656.10(f)).
- Pay the required wage from the start, including for non-productive time due to an
  employer decision (20 CFR 655.731(c)(7)).
- Material changes (worksite move outside the area, job duties, hours) may require an
  amended petition or new LCA.
- Form I-9 (US) employment verification for every hire, not just sponsored workers.
- UK/other sponsor regimes have their own reporting duties and sanctions; check the
  current sponsor guidance.

## Gotchas
- Who pays which cost: in US PERM the employer must not seek or receive payment for
  activities tied to the labor certification; the worker may pay their own costs
  (20 CFR 656.12(b)). Check the exceptions in the regulation.
- Annual lotteries/caps (e.g. H-1B) have fixed registration windows; missing one can
  mean waiting a year.

## Verify
- [ ] Job requirements are the employer's genuine minimums, not tailored to the beneficiary.
- [ ] Offered wage ≥ current prevailing/required wage for the correct classification and area.
- [ ] Recruitment steps dated inside their regulatory windows; recruitment report complete.
- [ ] Public access / audit files exist and retention dates are diarised.
- [ ] Client has a written timeline including priority-date backlog estimates.
