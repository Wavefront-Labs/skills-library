# Asylum declaration, country evidence and credibility

**Goal:** present a protection claim so that the decision-maker can find each element of
the refugee definition proved by credible testimony and corroborated by objective
evidence. The definition is rooted in the 1951 Refugee Convention; procedure, deadlines and how particular social groups are defined vary sharply by
jurisdiction (and in the US, by circuit and by shifting precedent). Check current law.

## 1. The elements — build the theory first
A refugee has a **well-founded fear** of **persecution** **on account of** (nexus) race,
religion, nationality, political opinion or membership of a **particular social group**,
and the state is the persecutor or is **unable or unwilling to protect**. Then check:
- **Past persecution** (may create a presumption of future fear in some systems) vs
  future risk only.
- **Internal relocation**: could the person safely and reasonably live elsewhere in the
  country?
- **Bars**: persecutor bar, serious crimes, firm resettlement, security grounds, and
  filing deadlines (US affirmative asylum: within 1 year of the date of last arrival,
  unless changed circumstances materially affecting eligibility or extraordinary
  circumstances explain the delay and the claim was filed within a reasonable time).
- **Fallback protection**: withholding of removal and Convention Against Torture
  protection. Their standards differ from asylum's; check the current statute and
  regulations. Plead all that apply.
Write a one-page theory memo: protected ground(s), persecutor, harms, why the state
won't protect, why relocation fails. If the social group is used, define it precisely
(check the current tests in the relevant circuit).

## 2. The declaration
- Chronological, first person, in the client's voice — not lawyerly prose.
- Concrete sensory detail: dates (or anchors like "the rainy season after my son was
  born"), places, names, what was said, injuries, how they escaped.
- Show **why** they were targeted (evidence of nexus: what persecutors said, who else was
  targeted).
- Address weaknesses openly: gaps, delays in leaving, earlier inconsistent statements
  (e.g. at the border or airport interview) and why.
- Read it back to the client in their language via a competent interpreter; they sign
  only what they confirm. Keep drafts and interpreter notes.

## 3. Country-conditions evidence
- Sources: government human-rights reports, UNHCR guidance, credible NGO reports, academic
  experts, reputable journalism. Use recent material and cite page-specific passages.
- Index with **highlighted excerpts** tied to each element (persecution of this group,
  state failure to protect, no safe relocation).
- Anticipate the adjudicator's go-to sources (often the government's own report) and
  address passages that cut against the claim.
- Expert declarations are strongest when the expert reviews the specific facts.

## 4. Corroboration and credibility
- Seek corroboration that's reasonably available: medical/psych evaluations documenting
  injuries and trauma, membership cards, arrest warrants, letters from witnesses (with
  ID copies), news reports. Explain why missing items are unavailable.
- Credibility is assessed on consistency (internal and with prior statements), detail
  and plausibility. Trauma affects memory: peripheral details, dates and sequence are
  often unreliable — a psychological evaluation can explain this.
- Prep sessions: practise direct and cross-examination, including hostile questions;
  never script answers. Teach the client to say "I don't know" or "I don't remember."

## Gotchas
- Interpreter errors at earlier interviews are a frequent source of "inconsistencies";
  request transcripts/notes and compare.
- Documents from the home country may be checked for authenticity; never submit anything
  of doubtful origin without saying so.
- Contacting the home government (e.g. for a passport) can undermine a fear claim.
- Confidentiality: never disclose that the person applied to anyone in the home country
  when gathering evidence.

## Verify
- [ ] Theory memo maps facts to every element, bar and fallback form of protection.
- [ ] Declaration is detailed, chronological, read back in the client's language, and addresses prior statements.
- [ ] Country evidence is current, indexed and excerpted by element, including adverse passages.
- [ ] Filing deadline compliance (or exception) documented.
- [ ] Client has rehearsed cross-examination with the actual interpreter language.
