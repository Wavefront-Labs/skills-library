# Building a visa petition and answering an RFE

**Goal:** file a petition that an adjudicator can approve without asking for anything
more, and when a Request for Evidence (RFE) does arrive, answer exactly what was asked
in one complete response. Framed around US practice (USCIS), where vocabulary is most
standardised; the method transfers to UK (UKVI), Canadian (IRCC) and Australian
systems, but forms, standards and deadlines differ — check the current rules for the
jurisdiction and category every time.

## 1. Start from the elements, not the documents
Every category has a regulatory checklist (statute + regulation + agency policy manual).
Before collecting a single document, build an **eligibility matrix**:

| Element (cite the rule) | Facts that satisfy it | Primary evidence | Secondary evidence | Gaps |
|---|---|---|---|---|

- One row per element, including the boring ones (valid status of the beneficiary,
  employer's ability to pay, filing fee, signatures).
- For criteria-based categories (e.g. EB-1A extraordinary ability: a one-time major
  achievement or at least 3 of the 10 criteria in 8 CFR 204.5(h)(3)), pick the strongest
  criteria and prove them thoroughly instead of touching all of them thinly. The record
  must also tell a coherent story.
- Note the standard: in most US benefit filings it is **preponderance of the evidence**
  ("more likely than not"), and the burden is always on the benefit requestor (USCIS
  Policy Manual Vol. 1, Part E, Ch. 4). You don't need certainty; you need each element
  more likely true than not, documented.

## 2. Assemble evidence an officer can navigate
- **Cover/petition letter** that walks the matrix: element → facts → "see Exhibit 7,
  p. 3". Officers have minutes per file; make the path obvious.
- **Exhibit index** with tabs, page numbers, and a one-line description of each exhibit's
  relevance.
- Primary evidence first (contracts, pay records, degree certificates, published
  articles); expert letters and declarations support but rarely substitute.
- **Translations**: any foreign-language document submitted to USCIS needs a complete
  English translation certified by the translator as complete and accurate and as
  competent to translate (8 CFR 103.2(b)(3)); other systems may require certified or
  sworn translators.
- Consistency audit: dates, job titles, salaries and names must match across forms,
  letters, CV and pay records. Inconsistency is the commonest trigger for RFEs and fraud
  referrals.
- Confirm the current form edition, fee and filing address/online channel on the agency
  site on the day you file.

## 3. Reading an RFE
- Diary the **response deadline** immediately (USCIS allows a maximum of 84 calendar days
  for most form types, plus 3 days if the notice was mailed, so up to 87; Forms I-539 and
  I-601A are capped at 30 days; the notice governs — verify on the notice itself).
  Regulations prohibit officers from granting extensions to respond to an RFE. A NOID
  response period is 30 days, plus 3 if mailed.
- Distinguish RFE (more evidence wanted) from **NOID** (Notice of Intent to Deny — a
  proposed denial you must rebut) and from an outright denial (which needs a motion or
  appeal, with its own short deadline).
- Break the RFE into numbered issues. Agencies often use templated language; separate
  the template boilerplate from the actual concern.

## 4. Writing the response
1. Return the RFE notice on top (US practice requires it with the response).
2. Cover letter with one heading per issue, mirroring the RFE's numbering.
3. For each issue: state the legal standard, show how the new and existing evidence
   meets it, and politely correct a misstated standard with a citation to the rule or
   policy manual.
4. Submit **everything in one package** — USCIS treats a partial response as a request
   for a decision on the existing record and does not wait for more or issue a second RFE.
5. Don't introduce new facts that contradict the original filing; if the original was
   wrong, explain the correction expressly.

## Gotchas
- A petition can't usually be "fixed" by changing material facts after filing (e.g.
  the job offered or the employer); that may require a new filing. Rules vary by category.
- Get specific, signed expert letters tied to the facts of the case.
- Agency processing times and premium-processing availability change frequently —
  confirm before advising a client on timing.
- Unauthorized practice: in many jurisdictions only licensed lawyers or accredited
  representatives may prepare filings for others.

## Verify
- [ ] Every regulatory element has a matrix row with at least one exhibit.
- [ ] Names, dates, titles and wages are consistent across all documents.
- [ ] Current form edition, fee and filing channel confirmed on the filing day.
- [ ] Every foreign document has a translation and certificate.
- [ ] RFE response answers each numbered issue, includes the notice, and goes in before the deadline as a single package.
