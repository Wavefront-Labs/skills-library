# Family petitions, adjustment of status and consular processing

**Goal:** get a relative to permanent residence by the right path, with relationship
evidence that survives an interview and no surprise inadmissibility issue at the end.
US framework (I-130 / I-485 / NVC); spouse and partner routes elsewhere (UK appendix
FM, Canadian spousal sponsorship, Australian partner visas) share the same logic —
relationship proof, financial requirement, admissibility — with different rules.

## 1. Classify the relationship first
- **Immediate relatives** of US citizens (spouses, children, and parents of citizens aged
  at least 21; INA 201(b)(2)(A)(i)): not subject to the annual numerical caps.
- **Preference categories** (other children, siblings, relatives of permanent residents):
  annual caps, so the **priority date** and Visa Bulletin govern; waits range from a few
  years to decades depending on category and country.
- Age-out risk: a child turning 21 can drop to a slower category. US law has partial
  protection (Child Status Protection Act calculation) — run it, don't assume.
- Status changes can move the beneficiary to another category; recheck eligibility after
  any change in the petitioner's status or the beneficiary's marital status.

## 2. Choose the processing path
| | Adjustment of status (in-country) | Consular processing (abroad) |
|---|---|---|
| Who | Beneficiary physically present and generally lawfully admitted/paroled | Beneficiary abroad, or ineligible to adjust |
| Work/travel meanwhile | Interim work and travel permits available | Waits outside the country |
| Risk | Departing without travel permission can abandon the case | Leaving may trigger unlawful-presence bars |

The decisive question: **was the beneficiary inspected and admitted or paroled, and do
they have unlawful presence?** In US law (INA 212(a)(9)(B)), departing after more than 180
days but less than 1 year of unlawful presence triggers a 3-year bar, and 1 year or more a 10-year bar. A provisional unlawful-presence waiver
(I-601A) can sometimes be obtained before departure. Never advise anyone to travel until
this is mapped.

## 3. Relationship evidence (marriage cases)
Officers test whether the marriage is **bona fide** (entered for a genuine shared life,
not for immigration). Build a layered record:
- Legal: marriage certificate, divorce decrees ending all prior marriages for both spouses.
- Commingling: joint lease/mortgage, joint bank accounts with real activity, insurance
  beneficiaries, joint tax returns.
- Shared life: photos across time and with family, travel records, communications,
  children's birth certificates.
- Affidavits from people who know the couple — supportive but weaker.
Prepare the couple for an interview (and in doubtful cases separate interviews) on
everyday details: routines, home layout, how they met. Inconsistent answers, not thin
paperwork, sink most cases.

## 4. Financial sponsorship
- US: the petitioner files an **affidavit of support (I-864)**, generally showing income
  at or above 125% of the federal poverty guidelines for household size (100% for a
  sponsor on active duty in the armed forces sponsoring a spouse or child); a joint
  sponsor (no more than one) can fill a gap. It is a legally enforceable contract that
  the sponsored person, federal and state governments and other agencies can enforce —
  explain that to the sponsor.
- Other systems use their own income rules; check the current official guidance.

## 5. Inadmissibility screen
Before filing, ask every client about: criminal history (any arrest, even dismissed),
prior immigration violations, prior removal, misrepresentation, health grounds, and
claims to citizenship. Some grounds are waivable (often requiring "extreme hardship" to
a qualifying relative), some aren't. See the crimmigration skill
law-government.immigration-law.immigration-crimes-intersection for criminal issues.

## Gotchas
- Prior marriages not legally ended make the new marriage void: get final decrees.
- Conditional residence applies where the marriage is less than 24 months old when
  residence is obtained (US); the joint petition to remove conditions is due in the
  90-day period before the second anniversary, and status terminates if it is not filed
  without good cause (8 U.S.C. 1186a).
- Policy manuals and fees change; confirm the current edition before each filing.

## Verify
- [ ] Category, priority-date position and age-out risk calculated in writing.
- [ ] Entry history and unlawful presence mapped before choosing adjustment vs consular.
- [ ] Relationship evidence spans legal, financial and social layers across time.
- [ ] Sponsor income meets the current threshold or a joint sponsor is lined up.
- [ ] Inadmissibility questionnaire completed and any waiver need identified.
