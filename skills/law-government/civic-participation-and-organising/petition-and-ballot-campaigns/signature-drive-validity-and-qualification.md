# Signature drive: validity management and qualification

**Goal:** collect enough **valid** signatures, in the right legal form, by the deadline,
to qualify a measure, candidate or official petition. Most drives that fail don't fail
for lack of signatures — they fail on validity and form. Rules are set by each state,
province or country and change often; this is a generic method, not a legal checklist
for any particular place.

## 1. Read the rules before printing anything
Build a one-page requirements sheet from the statute, regulations and the election
official's handbook:
- **Number required** and the base (e.g. a percentage of votes cast for governor at the
  last election) — get the official figure in writing.
- **Distribution requirements** (a minimum from each county or district).
- **Deadline**, and earliest circulation date (often after title/summary approval).
- **Petition format:** exact text, font sizes, required statements, the full text of the
  measure attached, numbering, colours. Format defects can invalidate whole sheets.
- **Signer eligibility:** registered voter? in which jurisdiction? registered by when?
- **Circulator rules:** residency or registration requirements (some invalidated by
  courts), affidavit/declaration on each sheet, pay-per-signature bans in some places,
  badges or disclosure of paid status.
- **Single-subject** and pre-circulation legal review requirements.
- **Challenge procedure:** who can challenge, how, and deadlines.

## 2. Plan the number
Target = required valid signatures ÷ expected validity rate, plus a buffer.
- Raw validity rates commonly run in the 65–80% range; a **cushion of 25–50%** over the
  requirement is typical. Your own check data beats rules of thumb — measure early.
- Duplicates are a big source of invalidity; some places strike *both* signatures.
- Budget: paid circulation costs per signature rise sharply near deadlines.

## 3. Circulator operations
- Train every circulator on: who can sign (registered, right jurisdiction), the signer
  writing their own details legibly, address as registered, no signing for others,
  one signature per person, completing the circulator declaration.
- Use **turf assignments** and daily reporting (sheets in, signatures, hours).
- Never let circulators fill in or "fix" signer data — that can be forgery.

## 4. Internal validation
- Check a sample (or all) signatures against the voter file as they come in.
- Track validity rate **by circulator** — a falling rate flags training issues or fraud.
  Remove bad circulators' sheets and report suspected fraud to authorities.
- Fix sheet-level defects (missing declarations, notarisation where required) before filing.

## 5. Filing and verification
- File by county/district as required; keep copies or scans and a log of sheet counts.
- Officials often verify by **random sample** with a statistical threshold: a high
  projected rate qualifies, a low one fails, a middle band triggers a full count.
- Prepare to **defend**: observers at verification, a legal team for challenges, and
  your own validation data as evidence.

## Petitions to legislatures
Official e-petitions to parliaments (e.g. UK Parliament petitions — threshold triggers
for a government response and possible debate) are simpler: one signature per person,
verified via email and postcode. The craft is outreach and timing, not validity.

## Gotchas
- Changing the measure text after circulation starts usually means starting over.
- Paying per signature where prohibited can void signatures and expose the campaign.
- Personal data on petitions is regulated — secure sheets and follow data protection
  rules where they apply.
