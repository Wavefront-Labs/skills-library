# Running a commercial or investor-state arbitration

**Goal:** steer a cross-border dispute from the arbitration clause to an enforceable award.
Covers commercial arbitration (ICC, LCIA, SIAC, HKIAC, UNCITRAL ad hoc) and investor-state
arbitration under investment treaties (ICSID or UNCITRAL). Institutional rules are
revised periodically — always work from the version in force for your case (often the
one in force at commencement unless agreed otherwise).

## 1. Start with jurisdiction
- **Arbitration agreement**: scope ("arising out of or in connection with"), parties
  bound (non-signatories?), pre-arbitration steps (negotiation/mediation tiers and
  whether they're conditions), institution, rules, **seat**, language, number of
  arbitrators.
- The **seat** decides the procedural law (*lex arbitri*) and which courts supervise and
  can set aside the award. Not the same as the hearing venue.
- Investor-state: is the claimant a protected **investor** (nationality, corporate
  structuring timing — restructuring after a dispute is foreseeable may be abusive)?
  Is there a protected **investment**? Consent conditions in the treaty (cooling-off
  periods, local-litigation requirements, fork-in-the-road, time limits, denial of
  benefits).
- Check limitation periods under the governing law or treaty.

## 2. Commencement and the tribunal
- Request/notice of arbitration per the rules: parties, agreement, summary of claims,
  relief, nominations. Pay the advance on costs.
- **Arbitrator selection**: expertise, availability, nationality limits for chairs,
  prior decisions on the key issues, and **conflicts** (use the IBA Guidelines on Conflicts
  of Interest; arbitrators must disclose). Party-appointed arbitrators are independent —
  ex parte contact is limited to pre-appointment interviews within accepted bounds.
- Urgent relief: **emergency arbitrator** procedures or courts at the seat for interim
  measures.

## 3. Procedure
- **First case management conference / Procedural Order No. 1**: timetable, written
  rounds (memorial-style with evidence attached vs pleadings), bifurcation of jurisdiction
  or quantum, document production rules, hearing logistics, confidentiality, tech.
- **Document production**: commonly under the IBA Rules; requests must be narrow and
  specific, relevant and material, often in a **Redfern schedule** (request, reasons,
  objections, tribunal ruling). Common-law-style broad discovery is not the norm.
  Adverse inferences are available for unjustified non-production.
- **Witness statements** stand as direct evidence; witnesses are then cross-examined.
  Prepare witnesses ethically (rules on witness preparation vary by counsel's home bar).
- **Experts**: party-appointed (quantum, industry, foreign law); consider **witness
  conferencing** (hot-tubbing). Quantum models (DCF vs comparables vs sunk costs) often
  decide the economic result.

## 4. Merits in investment cases
Standard treaty claims: expropriation (direct/indirect, compensation standard), fair and
equitable treatment (legitimate expectations, due process, arbitrariness), full
protection and security, national/MFN treatment, umbrella clauses. Defences: police
powers, necessity, treaty exceptions, investor misconduct/illegality. Tribunals aren't
bound by precedent but cite each other heavily.

## 5. Award and afterwards
- Awards must meet formal requirements of rules and seat. Ask for costs submissions —
  "costs follow the event" is common but discretionary.
- **Set-aside** only at the seat, on limited grounds (Model Law Art 34: invalid agreement,
  no proper notice/opportunity, excess of mandate, improper composition, non-arbitrability,
  public policy). Short deadlines (commonly 3 months).
- **Enforcement** abroad under the **New York Convention** (Art V grounds, similarly narrow).
- ICSID awards: no national court set-aside; only ICSID **annulment** and enforcement as
  final judgments in member states (execution still subject to sovereign immunity rules).
- Asset tracing and state immunity strategy are often harder than winning.

## Gotchas
- Missing a multi-tier clause step can lead to inadmissibility or a stay.
- Third-party funding often must be disclosed; may trigger security-for-costs applications.
- Confidentiality is not automatic in every system; agree it.

## Verify
- [ ] Clause/treaty conditions satisfied; seat and applicable rules version identified.
- [ ] Arbitrator conflicts checked against IBA Guidelines.
- [ ] Procedural Order No. 1 settles timetable, production and evidence rules.
- [ ] Every claim and defence supported by witness, document or expert evidence.
- [ ] Set-aside/enforcement forums and deadlines mapped before award.
