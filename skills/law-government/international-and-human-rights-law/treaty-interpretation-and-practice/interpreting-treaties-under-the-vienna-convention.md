# Interpreting treaties under the Vienna Convention

**Goal:** answer "what does this treaty require of this state, and can anyone rely on it
here?" with the method international courts and tribunals actually apply. The Vienna
Convention on the Law of Treaties (VCLT) rules on interpretation are accepted as
customary international law, so they apply even to states not party to the VCLT.
Domestic effect is entirely a matter of each state's constitutional law.

## 1. Is the state bound, and on what terms?
1. Find the authoritative text and status at the **depositary** (for multilateral
   treaties often the UN Secretary-General — UN Treaty Collection).
2. Check: signature vs **ratification/accession** (signature alone generally doesn't
   bind, though it carries a duty not to defeat object and purpose before ratification,
   VCLT Art 18); **entry into force** generally and for that state; any withdrawal.
3. **Reservations and declarations**: read every reservation and every objection by
   other states. A reservation modifies the relationship between the reserving state and
   accepting states; reservations incompatible with object and purpose are impermissible
   (Art 19). Distinguish genuine **interpretive declarations** from disguised reservations
   by substance, not label.
4. Protocols, amendments and successive treaties on the same subject (Art 30) — which
   version binds which pair of states?

## 2. The interpretive method (Arts 31–33)
Art 31 is a single combined operation, not a ranked list:
- **Ordinary meaning** of the terms
- in their **context** (the whole text, preamble, annexes, and related agreements/
  instruments made in connection with conclusion)
- in light of the treaty's **object and purpose**
- in good faith.
Also take into account (Art 31(3)):
- **subsequent agreements** between the parties on interpretation;
- **subsequent practice** establishing the parties' agreement on interpretation;
- **relevant rules of international law** applicable between the parties (systemic
  integration).
A **special meaning** applies if the parties intended it (Art 31(4)) — e.g. defined terms.

**Supplementary means** (Art 32) — preparatory work (*travaux préparatoires*) and the
circumstances of conclusion — may be used to confirm the Art 31 meaning, or to determine
it when Art 31 leaves it ambiguous/obscure or produces a manifestly absurd or
unreasonable result. In practice tribunals consult travaux often; frame them as
confirmation.

**Plurilingual treaties** (Art 33): each authentic language is equally authoritative; if
texts diverge, adopt the meaning that best reconciles them in light of object and purpose.

## 3. Writing the analysis
Structure per contested term:
1. Text in each authentic language.
2. Ordinary meaning (dictionaries are a starting point only).
3. Context: neighbouring provisions, defined terms, preamble.
4. Object and purpose.
5. Subsequent agreements/practice; relevant international law.
6. Travaux and circumstances of conclusion (confirmation).
7. Interpretations by treaty bodies, courts and tribunals (persuasive authority; weight
   depends on the body and the treaty).
8. Conclusion and the strongest counter-reading.

Human-rights treaties are often read as "living instruments" with effective, evolutive
interpretation by their courts — note this when a regional court's case law applies.

## 4. Domestic effect
- **Monist** tendencies: ratified treaties become part of domestic law, sometimes ranking
  above statutes (e.g. several civil-law constitutions).
- **Dualist**: treaties need implementing legislation (e.g. UK; many Commonwealth states).
  Unincorporated treaties may still guide statutory interpretation.
- US: treaties are "supreme law" but courts distinguish **self-executing** from
  non-self-executing provisions; implementing legislation may be needed.
- Check whether individuals can invoke the treaty in court, and whether a later statute
  overrides it domestically (the international obligation remains — Art 27: internal law
  is no excuse).

## Gotchas
- Using an unofficial translation or an outdated consolidated text.
- Forgetting a state's reservation or a later protocol.
- Treating treaty-body general comments as binding; they're authoritative guidance of
  varying weight, contested by some states.
