# Documenting violations to evidential standards

**Goal:** collect information about violations so that it is accurate, safe for the
people who gave it, and usable later by courts, prosecutors or UN mechanisms — without
contaminating future criminal investigations. Draws on widely used professional
protocols (Istanbul, Minnesota, Murad Code, Berkeley Protocol, OHCHR monitoring
methodology). Legal admissibility rules belong to the eventual forum and vary.

## 1. Plan before going to the field
- **Mandate and questions**: which violations, period, place, actors, and which legal
  framework (human-rights law, humanitarian law, criminal law) the findings will speak to.
- **Risk assessment**: for witnesses, staff, communities, data. "Do no harm" overrides
  collection goals; if you can't protect a source, don't interview them.
- Coordinate with other investigators: repeated interviews of the same survivors cause
  re-traumatisation and inconsistent statements that defence counsel will exploit.
- Decide your **standard of proof** up front (UN commissions commonly use "reasonable
  grounds to believe"); report against it consistently.

## 2. Interviewing witnesses and survivors
- **Informed consent**: purpose, who will see the information, possible uses (including
  court), risks, the right to refuse or stop, and whether their identity will be shared.
  Record consent, specifically for each use (e.g. sharing with a prosecutor).
- Private, safe setting; trained interpreters bound by confidentiality; gender of
  interviewer considered, particularly for sexual violence (follow the **Murad Code**).
- Open-ended free recall first, then clarifying questions; avoid leading questions and
  never show other witnesses' accounts. Distinguish what was seen from what was heard.
- Capture sources of knowledge, times, places, descriptions of perpetrators (uniforms,
  insignia, vehicles, language), and other witnesses.
- Refer to medical, psychosocial and protection services; don't promise outcomes.
- Notes: contemporaneous, verbatim where possible, signed/dated; keep originals.

## 3. Physical and medical evidence
- Don't collect physical evidence or exhume unless trained and mandated — you may destroy
  it for later forensic work. Photograph in place with scale and location data.
- Medical documentation of torture and ill-treatment follows the **Istanbul Protocol**;
  unlawful deaths, the **Minnesota Protocol**.

## 4. Digital and open-source material
- Preserve originals with **hash values** and metadata; capture full web pages with
  timestamps and URLs; record who captured what, when and how.
- **Verify**: source identification, geolocation (landmarks, satellite imagery), chronolocation
  (shadows, weather, events), consistency with other sources. Treat everything as unverified
  until checked.
- Follow the Berkeley Protocol for documentation and analyst wellbeing (graphic content).

## 5. Analysis and corroboration
- Build an incident database: each allegation, sources, corroboration, reliability score,
  and gaps. Corroboration should come from independent sources.
- Assess patterns (similar incidents, methods, units) which matter for systematic or
  widespread findings.
- Attribute responsibility cautiously; distinguish established facts from allegations.

## 6. Reporting
- State methodology, standard of proof, limitations (access denied, sample bias).
- Protect identities: remove details that could identify sources unless consent and safety allow.
- Keep the evidence archive separate from the public report, with access controls and a
  plan for transfer to accountability mechanisms (with consent).

## Gotchas
- Insecure communications and cloud storage can expose sources — use encryption and
  minimise what's stored on devices crossing checkpoints.
- Paying witnesses or offering inducements undermines credibility.
- Publishing names or images can trigger reprisals.

## Verify
- [ ] Risk assessment completed and documented before interviews.
- [ ] Every interview has recorded, use-specific informed consent.
- [ ] Digital items hashed, metadata preserved, chain of custody logged.
- [ ] Each finding states its sources, corroboration and the standard of proof applied.
- [ ] Public report checked for identifying details; archive secured.
