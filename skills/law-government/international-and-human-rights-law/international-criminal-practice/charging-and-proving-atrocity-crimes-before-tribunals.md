# Charging and proving atrocity crimes before tribunals

**Goal:** structure an atrocity-crimes case so every element — including the contextual
element and the accused's link to the crimes — is proved by admissible evidence, or
(for the defence) show where that chain breaks. Framed on the International Criminal
Court (Rome Statute), with differences from ad hoc tribunals (ICTY/ICTR legacy) and
domestic universal-jurisdiction courts flagged; procedure and case law vary by forum.

## 1. Jurisdiction and admissibility (ICC)
- **Jurisdiction**: temporal (after entry into force for the state), territorial or
  nationality link (or Security Council referral, or Art 12(3) declaration), subject
  matter (Arts 5–8 bis).
- **Complementarity** (Art 17): inadmissible if a state with jurisdiction is genuinely
  investigating/prosecuting the same person for substantially the same conduct — unless
  unwilling or unable. Also **gravity**.
- Ad hoc tribunals had primacy instead; domestic universal-jurisdiction cases depend on
  national statutes and immunities.

## 2. The crimes — contextual elements first
| Crime | Contextual element |
|---|---|
| **Genocide** | Specific intent to destroy, in whole or in part, a national, ethnical, racial or religious group as such |
| **Crimes against humanity** | Widespread **or** systematic attack directed against a civilian population, pursuant to a state or organisational policy (ICC), with knowledge of the attack |
| **War crimes** | Conduct in the context of and associated with an international or non-international armed conflict; protected persons/objects; classification of the conflict matters for which crimes apply |
| **Aggression** | Leadership crime; special jurisdictional regime |
Then the **underlying acts** (killing, deportation, persecution, rape and other sexual
violence, attacking civilians, etc.) per the Elements of Crimes, plus mental element
(Art 30 default: intent and knowledge, unless otherwise provided).

## 3. Modes of liability — linking the accused
Senior accused are rarely physically present at crimes, so the case lives here:
- **Commission**: direct, joint (co-perpetration — essential contribution to a common plan),
  indirect (through another person or an organised power apparatus) (Art 25(3)(a)).
- **Ordering, soliciting, inducing**; **aiding and abetting**; **common purpose
  contribution** (Art 25(3)(d)).
- **Command/superior responsibility** (Art 28): effective control, knew or should have
  known (military) / consciously disregarded (civilian superiors), failure to prevent,
  repress or submit for prosecution. Causation element debated at ICC.
- Ad hoc tribunals used **joint criminal enterprise** (JCE) — different from ICC co-perpetration.
Charge alternative modes where the evidence supports them, within fair-notice limits.

## 4. Evidence
- **Crime-base evidence**: victims and eyewitnesses, forensic exhumations, medical
  records, imagery, documents.
- **Linkage evidence**: orders, communications intercepts, organisational charts,
  insider witnesses, logistics records, public speeches.
- **Open-source/digital evidence**: preserve with hashes and metadata, document capture
  and chain of custody, verify geolocation and time (Berkeley Protocol methodology).
- Witness protection and trauma-informed interviewing; avoid re-traumatisation and
  multiple statements that create inconsistencies.

## 5. Disclosure and fairness
- Prosecution must investigate incriminating and exonerating circumstances equally
  (Art 54(1)(a)) and disclose exculpatory material as soon as practicable (Art 67(2)).
  Late disclosure is a leading cause of delays and stays.
- Defence: test reliability, challenge provenance of digital evidence, use expert
  military/organisational analysis to contest effective control, and pursue its own
  investigations — resources are a constant constraint.

## 6. Victims
At the ICC, victims may participate through legal representatives (views and concerns,
Art 68(3)) and claim **reparations** after conviction (Art 75, Trust Fund for Victims).
Victim applications require identity and harm links to charged crimes.

## Gotchas
- Charging crimes against humanity without proving the policy element (ICC requirement).
- Misclassifying the conflict, which changes applicable war crimes.
- Witness contamination by NGOs' earlier interviews; obtain prior statements.

- [ ] Each mode of liability has element-by-element linkage evidence.
- [ ] Digital evidence preserved with verifiable chain of custody.
