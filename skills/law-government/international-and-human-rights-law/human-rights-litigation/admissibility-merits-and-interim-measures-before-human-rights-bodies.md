# Admissibility, merits and interim measures before human-rights bodies

**Goal:** get a complaint past admissibility (where most applications die), argue the
merits in the body's own analytical framework, obtain urgent protection when life or
irreparable harm is at stake, and turn a win into real change. Forums include the
European Court of Human Rights (ECtHR), the Inter-American system (Commission then Court),
the African Commission and Court, and UN treaty bodies (e.g. the Human Rights Committee
under the ICCPR Optional Protocol). Each has its own time limits and forms — check the
current rules of procedure.

## 1. Choose the forum
- Which treaties has the state ratified, and has it accepted individual complaints
  (optional protocols / declarations)?
- **Binding force**: ECtHR and Inter-American Court judgments are binding; UN treaty-body
  "views" are authoritative but contested as non-binding by some states.
- Speed, remedies, backlog, and whether a matter already examined by another
  international body bars a new complaint (rules differ; some states made reservations).

## 2. Admissibility checklist (typical elements)
- **Jurisdiction**: state party; act within its jurisdiction (including extraterritorial
  effective control cases); events after the treaty's entry into force for that state
  (or continuing violations).
- **Victim status**: directly affected (or indirect/potential victims in narrow cases).
  Organisations generally can't complain on behalf of others without authority.
- **Exhaustion of domestic remedies**: every effective, available remedy pursued, with the
  Convention complaint raised *in substance* before domestic courts. Exceptions for
  ineffective or unavailable remedies — argue them with evidence.
- **Time limit**: ECtHR — **4 months** from the final domestic decision (reduced from
  6 months by Protocol 15 for final decisions from 1 February 2022) (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked); Inter-American —
  generally 6 months from notification of final decision (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked); UN bodies vary (some within
  a "reasonable time"; e.g. abuse-of-right rules for long delays). Verify for the forum.
- Not manifestly ill-founded; ECtHR "no significant disadvantage" criterion (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
- **Complete application**: ECtHR Rule 47 (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) strictly requires the official form fully
  completed with copies of key documents — incomplete forms don't stop the clock.

## 3. Merits framing
Use the body's test, not domestic-law language:
- **Qualified rights** (e.g. private life, expression, assembly, religion): interference →
  prescribed by law (accessible, foreseeable) → legitimate aim → **necessary in a
  democratic society / proportionate**; state's **margin of appreciation** varies by
  subject and European consensus.
- **Absolute rights** (torture/inhuman treatment): no justification; prove severity and
  attribution; **procedural limb** — duty to investigate effectively.
- **Positive obligations**: state knew or ought to have known of a real and immediate risk
  and failed to take reasonable measures.
- Anchor facts in the domestic record; use NGO and UN reports for context; cite the
  forum's leading judgments and their own paragraph-level reasoning.
- Remedies: just satisfaction (ECtHR Art 41 — pecuniary/non-pecuniary/costs, claimed in
  time and itemised), and in the Inter-American system broad reparations (restitution,
  guarantees of non-repetition).

## 4. Interim measures
- ECtHR **Rule 39** (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked): imminent risk of irreparable harm, typically to life or of ill-treatment
  (expulsion/extradition cases most commonly). Send by the designated urgent channel with
  the decision date, removal date, and supporting evidence; mark it urgent.
- Inter-American **precautionary measures** (Commission) and **provisional measures**
  (Court); UN bodies' interim measures requests. States are expected to comply; breach
  can itself be a violation.

## 5. Implementation follow-up
Judgments are executed under supervision (e.g. Council of Europe Committee of Ministers;
Inter-American Court compliance hearings). Submit communications on execution, push
general measures (law changes), and coordinate with NGOs and national human-rights
institutions.

## Gotchas
- Raising a right at the international level that was never argued domestically → non-exhaustion.
- Missing the time limit by counting from the wrong decision.
- Anonymity and protection of applicants must be requested expressly.

## Verify
- [ ] State has ratified and accepted individual complaints for the chosen forum.
- [ ] Exhaustion shown with domestic decisions where the right was raised in substance.
- [ ] Time limit computed from the correct final decision with a buffer.
- [ ] Application form complete per the forum's strict rules.
- [ ] Interim measures request (if any) includes dates, risk evidence, and urgent channel.
