# Recruiting and leading a volunteer moderator team

**Goal:** a team of unpaid moderators that enforces the community's rules consistently,
handles conflict (including among themselves), doesn't burn out, and survives the
departure of its founders. Applies to subreddits, Discord servers, forums, fan and hobby
communities, and open-source project spaces. The hosting platform's own rules (e.g.
Reddit's Moderator Code of Conduct, Discord's guidelines) sit above yours, and
illegal content goes to the platform and, where required, authorities — volunteers
are not a substitute for that.

## 1. Know how many moderators you need
Look at activity: posts/messages per day, reports per day, peak hours and time zones,
languages. A rough heuristic is enough moderators that the queue is checked at least
every few hours across your main time zones — and never fewer than three, so one person's
absence doesn't stop moderation.

## 2. Recruit
- Look first at trusted regulars: people who report accurately, help newcomers and stay
  calm in arguments.
- Use a short public application: time zone and hours available, experience, answers to
  3-5 realistic scenarios ("a long-time member posts a borderline joke; reports pile up —
  what do you do?"), and why they want to help.
- Avoid recruiting only friends; seek diversity of time zone, perspective and community segment.
- **Trial period** (e.g. 30 days) with limited permissions and a mentor; review decisions
  together before granting full permissions.

## 3. Write the mod handbook
1. The public rules with internal interpretation and examples.
2. Standard actions per rule (remove, warn, timeout/mute, temporary ban, permanent ban) —
   a simple ladder keeps decisions consistent.
3. Removal reasons/templates so users always get a reason and a route to appeal.
4. Escalation: when to involve the head moderator, when to report to platform admins
   (threats, self-harm, illegal content, doxxing, ban evasion), and never to handle it alone.
5. Privacy: what moderators may and may not share outside the team.
6. Conduct expectations for mods: no using powers in personal disputes, recuse when involved.

## 4. Keep decisions consistent and visible
- Use the platform's mod log and a shared notes system on repeat users.
- Weekly or fortnightly check-in (async is fine) to discuss hard cases and update the handbook.
- Publish rule changes to the community with reasons; invite feedback in a meta thread.
- Give users an appeal path to a different moderator than the one who acted.

## 5. Handle conflict inside the team
- Disagree in private mod channels, never in public threads.
- For contested decisions, use a simple rule: e.g. majority of active mods, head mod breaks ties.
- If a moderator repeatedly breaches the handbook: private conversation, written
  expectations, then removal by the agreed process. Document each step.

## 6. Prevent burnout
- Schedules or "on-duty" rotations so nobody feels responsible 24/7.
- Encourage breaks and leave of absence without stigma.
- Route the most distressing content to the platform quickly rather than having volunteers
  view it repeatedly; offer to rotate people away from difficult areas.
- Recognise contributions (public thanks, flair, small perks if the community has them).

## 7. Plan succession
- At least two people with top-level permissions, so the community is not lost if one leaves.
- Remove inactive moderators on a stated schedule (e.g. after 60-90 days of inactivity,
  with notice).
- Document everything (handbook, bots and tools, passwords stored in a shared secure
  password manager, never in chat).
- Grow future leads by rotating responsibilities (events, rule reviews, onboarding).

## Gotchas
- Founders hoarding top permissions, then disappearing.
- Silent removals with no reason, which breed distrust and conspiracy theories.
- Moderators debating users publicly about a ban.
- Treating volunteers as staff: demanding hours or targets kills goodwill.

## Verify
- [ ] Coverage across time zones with at least three active moderators.
- [ ] Application, scenario questions and trial period with mentor in use.
- [ ] Mod handbook with action ladder, templates, escalation and conduct rules.
- [ ] Mod log, regular case review and user appeal path to a different moderator.
- [ ] Burnout safeguards, inactivity policy and two-person top-level access in place.
