# Sponsored-research agreement key terms — protect the institution's mission

**Goal:** sign research agreements that pay the full cost, keep the freedom to publish and
to keep doing research, give the sponsor fair access to results, and don't expose the
institution to liability it can't bear. You negotiate for the institution; the PI is a key
stakeholder but not the signatory.

*Positions below reflect common university practice, especially in the US; your
institution's policies and template set the actual red lines.*

## 1. Before negotiating
Read the PI's statement of work and budget; ask the PI what results, materials and data will
come out, whether students are involved (publication matters for theses), whether there's
any existing related IP or other sponsors in the same area (conflicting obligations), and
whether any material, data or technology is **export-controlled**. Check conflicts of
interest (PI equity in the sponsor).

## 2. Scope and payment
- Statement of work attached and specific; deliverables described as **research effort**,
  not guaranteed results ("best efforts", no warranty of outcome).
- **Budget at full cost** including the institution's industry F&A rate; most universities
  don't discount F&A for industry.
- **Fixed-price** vs **cost-reimbursable:** fixed price is simpler for the sponsor; agree what
  happens to residual balances. Payment schedule front-loaded or milestone-based; avoid
  payment only on "acceptance" of results.
- Termination: either party on notice (e.g. 30–60 days); sponsor pays costs incurred and
  non-cancellable commitments, including student support through the term.

## 3. Intellectual property
- **Background IP** stays with its owner; no licence implied except as needed to do the project.
- **Foreground (project) IP:** typical university position — the institution owns inventions
  made by its personnel; joint inventions jointly owned. Sponsor gets a **non-exclusive
  research licence** and/or an **option to negotiate** an exclusive or non-exclusive
  commercial licence within a fixed window (e.g. 6 months from disclosure) on reasonable terms.
- Resist sponsor ownership of all results or pre-agreed royalty-free exclusive licences unless
  the institution's policy allows (and price it).
- If federal funds are also involved, **Bayh-Dole** obligations take priority over anything
  given to the sponsor.

## 4. Publication
Non-negotiable for most universities: the right to publish. Accept a **review period**
(commonly 30–60 days) for the sponsor to identify its confidential information (which is
removed) and patentable inventions (publication delayed a further limited period, often
up to 60–90 days, for filing). No sponsor approval or veto over content. Theses must not be
held up indefinitely.

## 5. Confidentiality
Mutual, limited to information marked confidential, with standard exclusions (public,
already known, independently developed, received from third party), a defined term (e.g.
3–5 years). Keep confidential information out of the research results where possible —
results must be publishable.

## 6. Liability
- Universities generally won't give broad indemnities; sponsor indemnifies for its use of
  results and products. Mutual indemnity for own negligence where permitted; **public
  institutions** may be legally barred from indemnifying.
- Disclaim warranties on results; cap liability.
- Governing law: public institutions often must use their own state/country law, or remain silent.

## 7. Related agreements
- **Subawards** out to collaborators: use standard templates (e.g. the FDP subaward forms
  in the US) and flow down prime terms.
- **MTAs:** for incoming/outgoing materials — check reach-through rights on results, publication
  restrictions, and use the **UBMTA** where both parties are signatories.
- **DUAs:** define the dataset, permitted use, security requirements, re-identification
  ban, retention/destruction, and any regulatory regime (HIPAA, GDPR).

## Gotchas
- Sponsor "standard" templates written for vendor contracts (work-for-hire, all IP assigned).
- Agreeing to restrictions that conflict with existing awards or prior licences in the lab.
- Foreign-national researchers and export-controlled deliverables without a licence review.
- PI starting work before signature — costs may be unrecoverable and IP terms unclear.

## Verify
- [ ] SOW, budget at full cost and payment schedule attached; no guarantee of results.
- [ ] IP ownership, research licence and option window defined; background IP excluded.
- [ ] Publication right with a capped review/delay period.
- [ ] Confidentiality mutual, marked, time-limited; liability and indemnity within policy.
- [ ] Export control, COI and conflicting-obligation checks recorded.
