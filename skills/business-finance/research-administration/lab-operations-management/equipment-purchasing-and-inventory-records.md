# Equipment, purchasing and inventory records — keep the lab running and auditable

**Goal:** the lab gets what it needs on time, every purchase is charged to the right account
by the rules, every asset can be found, and the paperwork that safety, audit and sponsors ask
for already exists.

*Thresholds below are illustrative; your institution's procurement and asset policies set the
real numbers.*

## 1. Purchasing that survives an audit
- **Know the routes:** catalogue/punch-out suppliers (pre-negotiated, fastest), purchasing card
  for small purchases, purchase orders above the card limit, formal quotes or tenders above
  higher thresholds. Splitting an order to stay under a threshold is a policy breach.
- **Quotes / competition:** above the competitive threshold collect the required quotes or
  write a **sole-source justification** (unique capability, compatibility with existing
  equipment) — specific, not "PI prefers this brand".
- **Charge correctly at the point of purchase:** the account must be active, the cost allowable
  and allocable to that project, and within the project period. Record which project the
  item is for on the order. Shared consumables are split by a documented method.
- **Receive and match:** check deliveries against the order, record receipt, and approve the
  invoice only for what arrived (three-way match where the system supports it).
- Watch for items needing approvals before ordering: controlled substances, radioactive
  material, select agents, biological materials (often needs an MTA), lasers, export-controlled
  items, hazardous chemicals with storage limits.

## 2. Equipment inventory
- Items at or above the **capitalisation threshold** (for federal awards, equipment has a useful life over one year and a per-unit cost at or above the lesser of the institution's capitalisation level or $10,000; 2 CFR 200.1) become capital assets: tag them and record
  description, manufacturer, model, serial number, asset tag, cost, funding source (and %
  if federally funded), acquisition date, location, custodian, condition.
- Federal awards require a **physical inventory at least every two years** and reconciliation
  to the records (2 CFR 200.313(d)(2)); many institutions do it annually.
- Moves, loans to other labs, disposals and transfers when a PI leaves go through the asset
  office — **title** to equipment bought on a grant may belong to the institution or the
  sponsor; don't sell or give it away informally.
- Keep a separate list of **sensitive or attractive items** below the threshold (laptops,
  cameras, pipettes in quantity) — they go missing.

## 3. Service and maintenance
For each major instrument: service contract (dates, coverage, renewal cost), calibration
schedule and certificates, maintenance and fault log, user training records and a booking
calendar. Budget renewals in the multi-award forecast; contracts often renew annually at
fiscal year-end.

## 4. Chemical and consumable stock
- **Chemical inventory** with location, quantity, hazard class and the current **safety data
  sheet** (SDS) accessible; reconcile at least annually and dispose of expired stock through
  the hazardous-waste route.
- Reorder points for consumables used constantly (tips, gloves, media) — a simple min/max list
  stops urgent rush orders.

## 5. People logistics
- **Joiner checklist:** access card, building and lab-specific safety training, accounts and
  data storage, equipment training, ordering rights, protocol approvals added (animal,
  human-subjects, biosafety).
- **Leaver checklist:** return keys/cards and equipment, data handover and archiving, notebook
  retention, remove from protocols and approvals, cancel P-card, final payroll distribution.

## 6. Recharges
If the lab provides services to others (a recharge or core facility), rates must be based on
actual costs and reviewed periodically; charge all users, including your own lab, the same
rate. Don't build up surpluses beyond what the institution's policy allows.

## Gotchas
- Equipment ordered in the last weeks of a grant: auditors ask if it benefitted the project.
- Gifts of equipment from companies without going through the institution (title, liability,
  conflicts).
- Personal-card purchases claimed later for grant items — often non-reimbursable.
- Inventory spreadsheets with no serial numbers; you can't match assets to records.

## Verify
- [ ] Each order used the right route, with quotes or sole-source justification above threshold.
- [ ] Charges recorded to an active, appropriate project at purchase time.
- [ ] Capital equipment tagged, recorded with funding source, and physically inventoried within two years.
- [ ] Service contracts, calibrations and maintenance logs current for major instruments.
- [ ] Chemical inventory reconciled with SDS available; joiner/leaver checklists used.
