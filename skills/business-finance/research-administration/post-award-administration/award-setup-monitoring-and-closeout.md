# Award setup, monitoring and closeout — keep the grant clean from start to finish

**Goal:** spend the award on allowable costs, within its terms and timeline, with
documentation an auditor can follow — and close it out without late reports, disallowed
costs or unspent money nobody noticed.

US federal examples (2 CFR 200 Uniform Guidance, NIH Grants Policy Statement). Non-US and
private sponsors set their own terms — **the award document governs.**

## 1. Award setup (first two weeks)
Read the **notice of award** and everything it incorporates by reference. Extract into the
award record: budget period and project period, awarded amount by year and category,
F&A rate and base, **restrictions and special conditions** (e.g. funds restricted until
an IRB/IACUC approval is in place), reporting schedule, prior-approval requirements,
carryover rules, payment method, and cost-share commitments.
- Compare the award with the proposal: if the budget was cut, agree a revised budget and
  scope with the PI now.
- Open the grant account(s) in the finance system with correct start/end dates, budget
  lines, F&A rate and fund code; set up subawards (issue subaward agreements with the
  flow-down terms).
- Hold a short kick-off with the PI: key dates, rules on purchases, travel, personnel changes.

## 2. Monthly monitoring
- **Burn rate:** spent + committed vs elapsed time. 40% spent at 75% of the period means
  under-spending (risk of losing funds or of a rushed year-end spend); 90% at 50% means overrun.
- **Allowability review** of charges: reasonable, allocable to this project, consistent with
  policy, adequately documented. Watch for costs that belong to another award or to
  general departmental budgets.
- **Payroll:** confirm people are charged at the effort they actually work. Payroll
  distribution changes should be made promptly.
- **Cost transfers** moving charges onto a grant must be timely (institutional policies
  often set a 90-day window) and justified in writing — frequent late transfers are an
  audit red flag.
- **Subrecipient monitoring:** review invoices against budget and progress; risk-assess subrecipients.

## 3. Effort reporting
Certify effort (or use another compliant payroll-validation approach) periodically:
salary charged to each award must reasonably reflect effort. Reductions in committed effort for key personnel can require sponsor prior approval (NIH sets a threshold: confirm the current figure in the NIH Grants Policy Statement, which was not retrievable for this review). Effort must never exceed 100% across all activities.

## 4. Changes during the award
Many routine changes are allowed under **expanded authorities** (NIH, many federal awards):
rebudgeting within limits, pre-award costs up to 90 calendar days before the award date and a **one-time extension of up to
12 months** with written notice at least 10 calendar days before the end of the period of performance, unless the
agency restricts it (2 CFR 200.308(f)-(g), eCFR 2026-10-15; the extension may not be used solely to spend unobligated balances; confirm sponsor-specific terms). Changes in scope, key personnel absence, foreign
components and second extensions need **prior approval** — request it in writing early.

## 5. Reporting
Track progress reports (e.g. annual RPPR for NIH), financial reports, invention reports and
other deliverables on a calendar with internal deadlines. Financial reports reconcile to the
general ledger.

## 6. Closeout
Under the Uniform Guidance, final financial, progress and invention reports are
due **within 120 calendar days** after the end of the period of performance (2 CFR 200.344); subrecipients
must report and liquidate obligations within 90 calendar days (or earlier as agreed) so the prime can meet it.
Steps: stop new commitments near end date; clear pending charges and encumbrances; remove
unallowable or late charges; reconcile cash drawn vs expenses; collect final subaward
invoices; submit reports; return unobligated funds; close the account; retain records
(**3 years** from submission of the final financial report, 2 CFR 200.334, longer if litigation, claims or audit are open).

## Gotchas
- Spending on equipment in the last weeks — auditors question whether it benefitted the project.
- Charging salary for people who left; payroll keeps running on the old distribution.
- Special conditions (restricted funds) missed at setup.
- Assuming an extension is automatic when the terms say prior approval (the 10-day notice and the one-time 12-month limit are in 200.308).
- Closing the financial account before late invoices and subaward finals arrive.

## Verify
- [ ] Award terms, restrictions and reporting dates abstracted into the award record.
- [ ] Account set up matching budget, dates and F&A; subawards issued with flow-downs.
- [ ] Monthly burn-rate and allowability review done; cost transfers timely and justified.
- [ ] Effort certified and consistent with payroll charges.
- [ ] Closeout reports submitted within the deadline and cash reconciled.
