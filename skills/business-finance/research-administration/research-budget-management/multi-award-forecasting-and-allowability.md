# Multi-award forecasting and allowability — run a lab's money, not one grant

**Goal:** a PI with several awards, start-up funds and discretionary accounts knows, for
the next 12–24 months, who is paid from what, when each award runs out, where the gaps
are, and that every charge would survive an audit.

## 1. The forecast model (spreadsheet or the institution's tool)
**Rows:** each person (salary + fringe), plus major non-personnel lines (supplies, animal
costs, core facilities, equipment, travel, subawards).
**Columns:** months for the next 24.
**Funding tabs:** one per award/account with start and end dates, remaining balance by
category, F&A treatment, and restrictions.

For every person and month, assign a **percentage distribution** across accounts that adds
to 100% of their salary. Then compute per account: projected spend per month vs remaining
balance → **projected end balance at award end**.
- Apply salary increases on the actual effective date and fringe rate changes on the new
  fiscal year.
- Include known events: new hires, students graduating, postdocs' fellowship start dates,
  sabbaticals, big equipment purchases.

## 2. Read the forecast
- **Overrun:** an award projected negative before its end date → move people or costs to
  other *appropriate* accounts, cut spend, or request rebudgeting.
- **Underrun:** a large projected balance → is the work behind? Plan a no-cost extension
  or appropriate spending; rushed year-end buying is an audit red flag.
- **Funding gap:** months where a person's salary has no allocable source → bridge funding
  request, a new application timed to land before the gap, or a staffing decision made early
  and humanely.
- **Effort consistency:** each person's charging should reflect the work they actually do
  on each project. Moving someone onto an award just because it has money is not allowable.

## 3. Allowability: the core tests (US federal, 2 CFR 200.403)
A cost charged to an award must be:
1. **Allowable** — not on the unallowable list (see the selected items of cost in 2 CFR 200 Subpart E) and permitted by the award terms;
2. **Allocable** — benefits *this* project, in proportion to the benefit. A shared supply
   order is split across projects on a reasonable basis, not dumped on the fullest grant;
3. **Reasonable** — what a prudent person would pay;
4. **Consistently treated** — the same kind of cost is not charged directly in some cases and
   recovered through F&A in others.

The same section also requires adequate documentation and that the cost falls in the approved budget period.
**Administrative and clerical salaries** and general office supplies are normally indirect
(covered by F&A); under 2 CFR 200.413(c) they may be charged directly only if essential to the award, specifically identifiable with it, and not also recovered as indirect costs. *Check the award terms and institutional policy.*

## 4. F&A awareness
Indirect cost recovery funds institutional infrastructure; some institutions return part of
it to departments or PIs. Know which accounts carry F&A (most sponsored) and which don't
(start-up, gifts, some foundations) — moving costs between them changes institutional recovery
and may need approval.

## 5. Discretionary and start-up funds
Use them for what grants can't pay for (bridge salary, pilot data, unallowable-but-legitimate
costs within institutional policy). Know their expiry dates: start-up funds often have one.

## 6. Monthly routine
Reconcile ledger charges to the forecast; correct payroll distributions promptly; update the
forecast; send the PI a one-page summary (balances, projected end balances, gaps, decisions
needed).

## Gotchas
- Forecasting with budgeted salaries instead of actual payroll.
- Forgetting fringe or F&A on the direct-cost spend.
- Late cost transfers to "clean up" balances near award end.
- Ignoring the award's own budget-category restrictions (e.g. participant support can't be rebudgeted freely).

## Verify
- [ ] Every person's salary is 100% allocated each month to named accounts.
- [ ] Projected end balance for every award computed; overruns/underruns flagged.
- [ ] Funding gaps at least 6–12 months out identified with an action.
- [ ] Charges pass allowable/allocable/reasonable/consistent tests; admin costs treated correctly.
- [ ] Forecast reconciled to the ledger this month and summarised for the PI.
