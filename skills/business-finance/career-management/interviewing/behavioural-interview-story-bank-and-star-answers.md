# Behavioural interview story bank and STAR answers

**Goal:** walk into any "tell me about a time..." interview with rehearsed, true,
specific stories you can adapt on the spot, instead of improvising generalities.

## 1. Extract the competencies
From the job description (and the company's published values or leadership
principles, if any) list 6-10 things they will probe: e.g. stakeholder conflict,
delivering under ambiguity, influencing without authority, a failure, a data-driven
decision, leading a team, customer focus, prioritisation.

## 2. Build 8-12 stories, each reusable for 2-3 competencies
Write each as **STAR**:
- **Situation** (1-2 sentences): context, stakes, when.
- **Task**: what *you* were responsible for.
- **Action** (the bulk, ~60%): what *you* did, step by step, including the judgment
  calls. Say "I", not "we"; interviewers are scoring your contribution.
- **Result**: the number or outcome, plus what you learned or would change.
Target **90 seconds to 2 minutes** spoken. Longer and you lose the panel; shorter and
there's no evidence. Make a grid: stories × competencies, so you can see coverage gaps.

Include at least one **genuine failure** with a real cost and what you changed
afterwards. "I work too hard" answers are scored as evasive.

## 3. The fixed openers and closers
- **"Tell me about yourself"**: 60-90 seconds. Present → past (2 relevant highlights)
  → why this role now. Not your life story.
- **"Why us / why this role"**: something specific you couldn't say about a competitor.
- **"Weakness"**: a real, job-relevant but non-fatal one, plus the concrete fix in
  progress.
- **Salary question early**: deflect politely to the range for the role if you can;
  see salary-negotiation skill. (Some jurisdictions ban employers asking salary
  history; rules vary by state/country, verify locally.)
- **Your questions** (have 4-5, ask 2-3): what success looks like at 6-12 months,
  the biggest challenge for the team, how decisions are made, why the role is open.

## 4. Formats and their twists
- **Phone/video screen**: recruiter checks fit, salary, logistics. Keep it brief and
  positive. For video: camera at eye level, test link 10 minutes before, notes off-screen.
- **Panel**: answer to the asker, glance around the room when concluding; note names.
- **Case interview** (consulting, strategy, some PM roles): different skill; structure
  the problem aloud (issue tree), state assumptions, do math out loud. See
  business-finance.strategy-and-foresight.management-consulting.
- **Technical/work sample**: clarify the problem before solving; narrate trade-offs.

## 5. Rehearse properly
Say the stories **out loud**, timed, ideally recorded or to a friend who interrupts
with "what exactly did *you* do?" and "how did you measure that?". Reading notes
silently doesn't transfer.

## 6. After the interview
Within 24 hours send a short thank-you to each interviewer (or via the recruiter):
one specific callback to the conversation, one line on fit. Log the questions you got
and which stories you used, to refine the bank for the next round.

## Gotchas
- Hypothetical answers ("I would...") to a "tell me about a time" question score low;
  give the real past example.
- Badmouthing a former employer or colleague is a common silent rejection reason.
- Over-rehearsed scripts sound robotic; memorise the beats, not the words.
- Confidential details from your current employer: anonymise and round numbers.

## Verify
- [ ] Each target competency has at least two candidate stories in the grid.
- [ ] Every story has a measurable or observable result and fits in 2 minutes aloud.
- [ ] One real failure story ready.
- [ ] 60-90 s "tell me about yourself" rehearsed and timed.
- [ ] Questions to ask prepared; thank-you notes sent within 24 h.
