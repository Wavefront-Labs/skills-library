# Informational interviews and referral outreach

**Goal:** get real conversations with people in the roles and companies you want, and
turn some of them into referrals, without being the person who asks strangers for a job.

## 1. Who to contact, in order of warmth
1. Former colleagues, managers, classmates (reconnect before you need them).
2. Second-degree contacts via a **warm intro**: ask the mutual contact; give them a
   forwardable 3-line blurb so it costs them 30 seconds.
3. Shared affiliation: alumni of your school, past employer, professional association,
   bootcamp. Reply rates are noticeably higher than fully cold (practitioner experience).
4. Fully cold: people with the target title at target companies.

## 2. The outreach message (under ~100 words)
- Why them specifically (their role, a talk or post, shared background).
- What you want: **advice, 20 minutes**, a concrete topic. Not "a job", not "pick your brain".
- Make it easy: offer two time windows or "happy to do it by email if easier".
Example shape: "Hi <name>, I'm a <current role> exploring a move into <target>. I saw you
moved from <X> to <Y> at <company>. Could I ask you 3-4 questions about how you made
that switch? 20 minutes on a call any time next week, or email if simpler. Thanks."
Follow up once after ~a week if no reply; then let it go.

## 3. Running the informational interview
- Be on time, keep to 20 minutes, you run the agenda.
- Good questions: what a typical week looks like; what separates strong from average
  in the role; what they'd do in your position; how hiring works on their team;
  **who else should I talk to?** (the most valuable question: it grows the network).
- Don't ask for a job. If it goes well they'll often offer help; if not, at the end:
  "If a suitable role opens, would you be comfortable pointing me to the right person?"
- Send a thank-you the same day with one specific thing you'll act on.

## 4. Earning a referral
Many companies pay employees a referral bonus and route referred candidates past the
first screen, so a referral is both valuable and a favour with the referrer's
reputation attached. Ask only after a real conversation, for a **specific posting**,
and send: the job link/ID, your tailored resume, and 3-4 lines on why you fit that they
can paste into the referral form.

## 5. LinkedIn profile that supports all this
- Headline: target positioning, not just current title ("Financial analyst | FP&A,
  forecasting, Power BI").
- Recognisable photo, custom URL, About section in first person with 2-3 proof points.
- Experience bullets reuse your resume achievements.
- "Open to work": visible-to-recruiters-only setting is discreet; the public green
  banner is a choice some hiring managers read negatively, others don't care (opinions split).
- Connection requests to strangers: add a short note where your plan allows; the
  number of personalised notes on free accounts has been limited and changes, so check.

## 6. Upkeep (the part everyone skips)
Keep a simple list (spreadsheet or CRM-like notes): name, how you met, last contact,
something personal. Each month, touch 5-10 people: congratulate a new role, share
something useful, report back on advice they gave. Help others first; networks run on
reciprocity, and asking only when job-hunting is visible.

## Gotchas
- Mass copy-paste messages are obvious and get ignored.
- Taking 45 minutes when you asked for 20 burns the bridge.
- Never misstate how well you know someone when asking for an intro.
- Introverts: written outreach and small 1:1s work as well as events.

- [ ] Outreach template under 100 words with a specific ask.
- [ ] Each conversation ended with "who else should I talk to?" and a same-day thanks.
- [ ] Referral requests include job link, resume and a pasteable blurb.
