# Targeted job-search pipeline

**Goal:** turn a search from "refresh job boards and hope" into a measured pipeline
where you know which channel produces interviews and fix the stage that leaks.

## 1. Define the target before applying anywhere
Write one sentence: **role title(s) + level + sector + location/remote + salary floor**.
If you can't, you're not ready to apply; you'll scatter applications and get
generic rejections. Two adjacent targets is fine; five is not a target.

## 2. Build a target-employer list (30-60 names)
Sources: competitors of your current/last employer, companies where people with your
target title work (LinkedIn search by title + location), industry association member
lists, funding announcements (for growth hiring), local business journals.
Tier them: A (would love), B (good fit), C (fine). A-tier gets networking effort, not
just an online application.

## 3. Use every channel, weighted by what converts
Rough order of interview yield per hour (practitioner consensus, not hard data; your
field may differ):
1. **Referrals** from someone inside, see professional-networking skill
   (business-finance.career-management.professional-networking).
2. **Direct approach** to a hiring manager at an A-tier company.
3. **Specialist recruiters** in your niche.
4. **Applications via company careers pages**, tailored.
5. **Mass job boards** (Indeed, LinkedIn Easy Apply): highest volume, lowest yield.
   Many postings get hundreds of applicants within days; apply within the first 2-3
   days of posting or deprioritise.

## 4. Track it in a spreadsheet
Columns: company, role, link, date found, channel, contact, date applied, stage
(applied / screen / interview / final / offer / rejected / ghosted), next action,
next-action date, notes. Each week compute:
- applications → screens (low? resume or targeting is off)
- screens → interviews (low? your pitch or salary expectations mismatch)
- interviews → offers (low? interview prep, see the interviewing skill)
Fix the leakiest stage first. A typical healthy screen rate for tailored applications
is often quoted at 10-20%; treat that as a rough yardstick only.

## 5. Weekly quotas (activity you control)
Example for a full-time search: 5-10 tailored applications, 5 networking messages,
2 conversations, 1 recruiter touchpoint, and review of the tracker on Friday. Outcomes
lag activity by 2-6 weeks; judge the plan on activity first.

## 6. Working with recruiters
- **Agency/contingency recruiters** are paid by the employer when you're hired; they
  work for the client, not you. Be clear on your floor and what you won't do.
- Ask which companies they will send your CV to **before** they do. Two agencies
  submitting you to the same employer can get you disqualified, and duplicate
  submissions create fee disputes.
- **Retained search** (executive) firms come to you; reply even if not interested.
- Never pay a recruiter to find you a job; in many jurisdictions charging candidates
  is restricted, and it's a common scam pattern.

## 7. Follow-up cadence
- After applying with a named contact: one note after ~1 week.
- After an interview: thank-you within 24 hours; check in after the stated decision
  date passes, then once more a week later. Then move it to "ghosted" and stop.

## Gotchas
- Applying to everything feels productive and wrecks your conversion rate.
- Unemployed searches typically take months; budget for it and protect energy by
  capping daily board-scrolling.
- Fake job postings and "pay for training" offers exist; verify the company domain and
  never send ID documents or bank details before a real offer.

## Verify
- [ ] One-sentence target written down.
- [ ] Target list of 30+ employers, tiered.
- [ ] Tracker shows channel and stage for every application.
- [ ] You can name your weakest conversion stage and what you're changing.
- [ ] No recruiter submits you anywhere without your prior OK.
