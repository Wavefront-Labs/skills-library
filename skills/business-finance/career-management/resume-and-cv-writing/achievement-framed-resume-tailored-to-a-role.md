# Achievement-framed resume, tailored to a role

**Goal:** a one- or two-page document that (a) an applicant tracking system parses
cleanly and ranks as relevant, and (b) a recruiter skimming for about 10 seconds can
see you have done this job's core work before, with evidence.

## 1. Build a master resume once
Keep a long private document listing *every* role, project and result with numbers
(revenue, time saved, error rate, team size, budget, users). You tailor *from* this;
you never write a tailored version from scratch at 11pm.

## 2. Write bullets as achievements, not duties
Pattern: **verb + what you did + measurable result + (how / context)**.
- Duty: "Responsible for month-end close."
- Achievement: "Cut month-end close from 9 to 5 working days by automating 14
  reconciliations in Excel Power Query."
If you have no hard number, use scale (how many, how often, how big) or a
before/after state. Never invent figures; an interviewer will ask how you measured it.
Lead with the strongest 3-4 bullets per recent role; older roles get 1-2.

## 3. Tailor to each posting (15-20 minutes, not an hour)
1. Paste the job description and highlight the **hard requirements**, repeated nouns
   (tools, certifications, domains) and the first 3 responsibilities.
2. Make sure each one you genuinely have appears **in the posting's own wording**
   somewhere: summary, skills line or a bullet. "Stakeholder management" and
   "managing stakeholders" may not match in every ATS.
3. Reorder bullets so the most relevant come first; cut ones that don't serve this role.
4. Rewrite the 2-3 line summary at the top to name the target title and your
   strongest matching evidence.

## 4. Format for parsing (the ATS gotchas)
- Single column, standard headings ("Experience", "Education", "Skills").
- No text in headers/footers, text boxes, tables or images. Many parsers drop them,
  including your contact details if you put them in the page header.
- Plain fonts, real bullet characters, dates in a consistent format (e.g. "Mar 2022 – Jun 2024").
- Submit .docx or text-based PDF as the posting asks; a scanned PDF is unreadable.
- Test: copy-paste your PDF into a plain text editor. If the order is scrambled or
  text is missing, a parser will see the same mess.
Note: claims that "75% of resumes are rejected by ATS" are widely repeated but poorly
sourced; most systems (Workday, Greenhouse, Lever, Taleo) rank and filter, and humans
still decide. Optimise for both, don't panic about either.

## 5. Resume vs CV, by region (uncertain at the edges)
- US/Canada: "resume", 1-2 pages, no photo, no date of birth.
- UK/Ireland/Australia: "CV" means the same 2-page document.
- Academic/medical CV: full publication and grant record, any length.
- Some continental European and Asian markets still expect a photo or personal
  details; check local norms for the specific country rather than assuming.

## 6. Cover letter: evidence, not a resume recap
Three short paragraphs: why this role/organisation (specific), one or two stories
proving you can do the hardest part of the job, a plain close. Under 300 words.
If the posting says optional, a strong one still helps for career changers or when
your resume needs context (gaps, relocation, a pivot).

## Gotchas
- Same resume to 100 postings gets fewer interviews than a tailored one to 20.
- Employment gaps: one honest line ("Career break: family care, 2021-2022") beats silence.
- Listing every tool you've touched dilutes the ones that matter; keep the skills line
  to what you'd be happy to be tested on.
- File name: `Firstname-Lastname-Resume-<Role>.pdf`, not `resume_final_v7.pdf`.

## Verify
- [ ] Every bullet in the top two roles starts with a verb and most carry a number or scale.
- [ ] Each hard requirement you meet appears in the posting's own words.
- [ ] Plain-text paste test keeps order and contact details.
- [ ] A friend shown the resume for 10 seconds can name the role you're targeting.
- [ ] No figure you couldn't explain in an interview.
