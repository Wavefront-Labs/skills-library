# Skill-gap plan, promotions and pivots

**Goal:** make career moves on purpose, roughly every 2-3 years, instead of reacting
to whatever lands in your inbox.

## 1. Set a direction you can test
Write 2-3 candidate "next roles" as **real job titles** you could search for today, not
abstractions ("something more strategic"). For each, note: what the day actually
involves, pay range, who does it now. Talk to two people in each role before deciding
(informational interviews, see professional-networking).
Check against three filters: energy (do you like the work itself?), market (are
there openings at your level and location?), economics (pay, stability, ramp-up cost).

## 2. Skill-gap analysis against a real target
1. Collect 5-10 postings or a public levelling framework (many companies publish
   career ladders; internal ones are better) for the target role.
2. List requirements; mark each **have / partial / gap** with evidence.
3. Rank gaps by how often they appear × how hard they are to close.
4. Close gaps through **work evidence first** (a stretch project, a cross-team
   initiative), then courses or certifications. Hiring managers weigh "did it" over
   "studied it" in most fields; regulated professions (accounting, nursing, law) are
   the exception where the credential is the gate.
Put it in a one-page development plan: gap, action, evidence it will produce, date.
Review quarterly.

## 3. Getting promoted internally
- Learn the **actual mechanism**: who decides, when (calibration cycles), what the
  written criteria for the next level are. Ask your manager directly.
- Operate at the next level for a sustained period before asking. Most promotion
  systems reward demonstrated scope, not potential.
- Keep a **brag document**: dated wins, metrics, feedback received, mapped to the
  next-level criteria. Update it weekly (five minutes); you will not remember in
  January what you did in April.
- Get a **sponsor**: someone senior who will argue for you in the room where you are
  not. A mentor advises you; a sponsor spends their credibility on you.
- Make your manager's job easy: hand them a draft promotion case in their format.
- If the answer is "not this cycle", ask: "What specifically would I need to show,
  by when?" and get it in writing. Two cycles of moving goalposts is data.

## 4. Pivots: de-risk before you jump
- **Adjacent moves** (same industry new function, or same function new industry)
  succeed far more often than double jumps. Chain two adjacent moves if needed.
- Translate your experience into the target field's vocabulary on your resume.
- Test cheaply: side project, volunteer work, secondment, part-time course, a contract.
- Plan the money: a pivot often costs a pay step for 1-2 years; know your runway.
- Internal transfer is usually the cheapest pivot: you keep your reputation.

## 5. Personal positioning
One sentence you'd want a colleague to say about you when you're not there ("the
person who fixes broken data pipelines in finance"). Make your work, LinkedIn
headline and internal reputation consistent with it.

## Gotchas
- Chasing titles over skills can strand you in a role you can't perform or leave.
- Waiting to be noticed rarely works; promotions need an explicit ask.
- Certifications without application are weak signals outside regulated fields.
- Long-range plans beyond ~3-5 years are mostly fiction; keep the direction, revisit
  the plan yearly.

## Verify
- [ ] Two or three target roles named as searchable titles.
- [ ] Gap table with evidence, ranked, and dated actions.
- [ ] Brag document updated within the last two weeks.
- [ ] You can name your sponsor (or know you lack one).
- [ ] Pivot tested in a low-cost way before committing.
