# Co-founder agreement and equity split — found the company so a breakup doesn't kill it

**Goal:** before real money or real code exists, agree in writing who owns what, who
decides what, and what happens when someone leaves. Most early startup deaths are
team failures, and the cheapest time to plan for them is day one.

## 1. Have the hard conversation first (before any percentages)
Sit down and answer, in writing, for each founder:
- **Role and decision rights** — who has the final call on product, on hiring, on money?
  "We decide everything together" breaks on the first real disagreement; name a tie-break.
- **Commitment** — full-time from when? Salary expectations, runway each person can
  survive on, any outside jobs or side projects.
- **Contribution so far** — prior code, a prototype, a customer list, cash. Value it
  roughly; don't let it dominate a decade of future work.
- **Exit scenarios** — what if one of you wants out in month 8? Gets ill? Is underperforming?
- **Ambition** — lifestyle business or venture-scale? Mismatched ambition is a common
  slow-motion breakup.

## 2. Split equity deliberately
- Weight **future** contribution over past: the company is mostly unbuilt.
- An equal split is defensible when founders start together, full-time, with comparable
  roles; it avoids a resentment tax. Unequal splits are fine when commitment or risk
  genuinely differs (one founder full-time a year earlier, one funding the first year).
- Reserve room: investors will usually ask for an **employee option pool** (size it from your hiring plan, not a rule of thumb) and that pool usually
  comes out of founders' pre-money ownership.
- Never leave "we'll figure out equity later". Later, everyone has a different memory.

## 3. Put founder shares on vesting — including your own
- Common pattern: **4-year vesting with a 1-year cliff**, implemented
  as *reverse vesting* — founders own the shares now, but the company can repurchase
  unvested shares at cost if a founder leaves.
- Credit prior work by vesting some shares immediately or starting the clock earlier.
- **Acceleration:** *single-trigger* (on sale alone) is disliked by acquirers; *double-
  trigger* (sale **and** termination without cause) is the more common founder ask.
- Vesting protects the founders who stay: without it, a co-founder who leaves in
  month 3 keeps a third of the company forever.

## 4. Assign the IP to the company
Every founder signs an **IP assignment / proprietary information and inventions
agreement** transferring pre-incorporation work (code, designs, domain names) to the
company. Investors check this in due diligence; missing assignments are a classic
deal-delayer. Watch for work done on an employer's laptop or time — many employment
contracts claim it.

## 5. Incorporate in a structure investors accept
- In the US, venture investors commonly expect a **Delaware C-corp**; an LLC can
  suit a bootstrapped or lifestyle business but often needs converting before a priced round.
- Outside the US the equivalent is usually a private limited company (Ltd, GmbH, Pty Ltd);
  the vesting mechanism differs (e.g. leaver provisions in the shareholders' agreement
  rather than reverse vesting). *Unconfirmed by jurisdiction — get local counsel.*

## 6. The 83(b) deadline (US)
If founder shares are subject to vesting, a US founder normally files an **83(b)
election with the IRS within 30 days of the stock grant**. Without it, tax under section 83(a) is generally measured when the shares vest, on the excess of their value then over the price paid, potentially a large bill on illiquid stock. The 30-day period is set by statute (26 U.S.C. 83(b)(2)). Send it with tracked delivery, keep the proof,
and give the company a copy.

## Gotchas
- Equity promised by email or chat to an early advisor or friend is a cap-table landmine;
  every grant goes through the board and is documented.
- Don't issue founder shares at a price that looks wrong later; buying them early at a
  nominal price is what keeps the 83(b) tax near zero.
- A "silent" founder who keeps a full-time job but holds equal equity is a frequent
  investor objection.
- Templates (accelerator standard documents, law-firm startup packs) are fine starting
  points; have a lawyer review anything non-standard.

## Verify
- [ ] Written answers to roles, decision tie-break, commitment and exit scenarios.
- [ ] Cap table shows every founder's shares, vesting start date, cliff and acceleration terms.
- [ ] Signed IP assignment from every founder, covering pre-incorporation work.
- [ ] (US) 83(b) filed within 30 days for every founder with vesting stock, proof retained.
- [ ] You can say exactly what happens to a founder's shares if they leave next month.
