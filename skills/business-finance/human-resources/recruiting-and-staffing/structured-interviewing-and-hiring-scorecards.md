# Structured interviewing and hiring scorecards

**Goal:** hire on evidence of the abilities the job actually needs, with every
candidate asked the same core questions and scored against the same anchored scale.
Structured interviews and work samples are among the better predictors of job
performance in the selection research; unstructured "chats" are among the weakest
and most bias-prone. Employment law on questions, records and adverse impact differs
by country — **check with your HR/legal team**.

## 1. Job analysis → 4–6 competencies
Talk to the hiring manager and a top performer: what does success look like at 6
and 12 months? Translate into a few competencies, each with a definition and
observable behaviours (e.g. "Prioritisation: chooses work by impact, says no with
reasons, re-plans when scope changes"). Separate **must-haves** from trainable
**nice-to-haves**; remove proxies that aren't requirements (specific degree, years
of experience).

## 2. Build the scorecard
For each competency:
- 2–3 **behavioural questions** ("Tell me about a time you had to drop a commitment
  to deliver something more important. What did you do?").
- 1 **situational question** ("Two stakeholders each need your team next week...").
- **Anchored rating scale** (1–4 or 1–5) with written examples of what a 1, 3 and 5
  answer sounds like. Without anchors, ratings measure the interviewer, not the
  candidate.
- Follow-up probes for the STAR structure: Situation, Task, **Action (what *you*
  did)**, Result.

## 3. Design the loop
- Assign each competency to **one or two interviewers**; nobody covers everything.
- Include a **work sample** where practical: a short realistic task, the same for
  everyone, time-boxed, with a marking guide. Avoid unpaid multi-day "projects".
- Train interviewers once: the questions, the anchors, legal no-go topics (age,
  family plans, religion, health, etc., per local law), and note-taking.
- Offer adjustments for disabled candidates on request.

## 4. Running the interview
- Same opening and core questions for every candidate; probes may vary.
- Take notes of **evidence** (what they said they did), not impressions.
- Leave time for their questions; candidate experience affects acceptance and brand.

## 5. Scoring and debrief
- Each interviewer **scores independently and submits before the debrief**, with
  evidence for each rating. Reading others' feedback first anchors you.
- Debrief: go competency by competency; the person with the evidence speaks first;
  the most senior person speaks last.
- Decide against the bar, not against the other candidates in this batch alone.
- Record the decision rationale in the ATS.

## 6. References and offer
Use structured reference questions tied to the competencies, with consent. Prepare the
offer with compensation (see business-finance.human-resources.compensation-and-benefits.salary-bands-and-market-benchmarking),
close quickly, and hand over to onboarding.

## Gotchas
- **"Culture fit"** without definition becomes "like us". Use defined values
  behaviours or "culture add".
- **Halo effect:** one strong answer colours all ratings — score each competency
  separately.
- **Hypotheticals only:** situational questions without behavioural ones reward
  good talkers.
- **Changing questions mid-process** makes candidates incomparable.
- Track pass-through rates by stage and demographic group (where lawful) to catch
  adverse impact.

## Verify
- [ ] Competencies come from a job analysis and are 4–6 in number.
- [ ] Every question has an anchored rating scale.
- [ ] Each competency assessed by named interviewers; work sample used where practical.
- [ ] Scores submitted independently before the debrief.
- [ ] Decision rationale recorded; stage pass-through rates monitored.
