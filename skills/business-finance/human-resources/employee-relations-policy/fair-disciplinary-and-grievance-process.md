# Running a fair disciplinary or grievance process

**Goal:** a case outcome that is reasonable, consistent with past cases and defensible
if challenged — reached through a process the employee recognises as fair. The
structure below follows widely used fairness principles (the UK Acas Code is a good
written model); legal requirements on notice, representation, timing and dismissal
**vary by country and contract — check your policy and local law**. For
investigations run under legal privilege see
law-government.employment-and-labor-law.workplace-investigations-counsel.conducting-a-privileged-workplace-investigation.

## 1. Triage (first 1–3 days)
- What is alleged, by whom, about whom; is it **conduct**, **capability/performance**,
  a **grievance**, or a **whistleblowing/protected disclosure** or harassment
  complaint with its own procedure?
- Can it be resolved **informally** (a conversation, mediation)? Many minor issues
  should be.
- **Risk steps:** is anyone at risk? Is evidence at risk? Consider precautionary
  **suspension on full pay** only when necessary, briefly, and stated as not a
  judgement. Alternatives: temporary change of duties or location.
- Check for overlapping issues (a grievance raised during a disciplinary).

## 2. Investigation
- Appoint an **investigator who isn't the decision-maker** and has no involvement.
- Terms of reference: the specific allegations, scope, timeframe.
- Collect documents/data first (emails, logs, CCTV within data-protection rules),
  then interview: complainant, witnesses, then the respondent, who gets the
  allegations in enough detail to answer.
- Interview notes are signed/agreed; keep questions open and non-leading.
- **Report:** allegations, evidence for and against each, findings of fact on the
  balance of probabilities, and whether there is a case to answer. Not the sanction.

## 3. Hearing
- Written invitation with: allegations, possible outcomes (including if dismissal is
  possible), copies of evidence, date with reasonable notice, the **right to be
  accompanied** (where it applies — e.g. colleague or union rep in the UK).
- Hearing: present the case, let the employee respond, call witnesses where relevant,
  adjourn before deciding.
- Consider mitigation: length of service, record, personal circumstances,
  consistency with how similar cases were treated.

## 4. Outcome
- Proportionate sanction: no action, informal warning, written warning, final written
  warning, dismissal (with or without notice for gross misconduct) — per policy.
- Written decision with reasons, warning duration, and **right of appeal** with a
  deadline.
- For grievances: findings on each point raised, what will be done, appeal right.

## 5. Appeal
Heard by someone more senior and uninvolved where possible; can be a review or a full
rehearing per policy. Communicate the final outcome in writing.

## Case file
Chronology, all correspondence, investigation report, notes, evidence, decisions,
appeal. Store securely with restricted access; apply retention rules.

## Gotchas
- **Same person investigating and deciding** undermines fairness.
- **Pre-judged outcomes** ("we just need to get the paperwork done") are obvious in
  the record.
- **Inconsistency** with previous similar cases — check the case log.
- **Delay**: long, unexplained gaps cause harm and look unfair.
- **Retaliation** against complainants or witnesses — warn managers explicitly.
- Health, disability or pregnancy links may engage discrimination law; take advice.

## Verify
- [ ] Issue triaged to the right procedure; informal route considered.
- [ ] Investigator and decision-maker are different, uninvolved people.
- [ ] Employee received allegations, evidence and notice before the hearing.
- [ ] Right to be accompanied offered where applicable.
- [ ] Written outcome with reasons and appeal right; case file complete.
