# Collective bargaining: preparation and costing

**Goal:** reach a collective agreement the organisation can afford and run, without
avoidable disputes, by preparing harder than the other side expects. The legal
framework (duty to bargain in good faith, protected industrial action, works-council
rights, approval of agreements) differs sharply between countries — e.g. US NLRA (29 U.S.C. 158(d): a mutual obligation to meet and confer in good faith, without compelling agreement or a concession), UK TULRCA 1992 (recognition, ss. 70A-70C; industrial action ballots, s. 226) —
**get labour-law advice for your jurisdiction before you start**.

## 1. Start 6–9 months before expiry
- **Review the current agreement clause by clause** with line managers: what costs
  money, what blocks operations, what causes grievances. Pull grievance and
  arbitration history.
- **Data pack:** headcount by classification, pay and allowance distribution, overtime,
  absence, turnover, comparable settlements in the sector/region, inflation, the
  organisation's financial outlook.
- **Relationship map:** who leads for the union, their constituency's priorities,
  internal politics, their recent settlements elsewhere.
- Identify **legal steps and deadlines** (notice to bargain, cooling-off periods,
  ballot requirements).

## 2. Costing — every proposal, both sides
Build a costing model before tabling anything:
- **Base pay increase** compounds: 3% then 3% is 6.09% over two years, plus
  **roll-up** costs that move with base (overtime, shift premiums, holiday pay,
  pension contributions, payroll taxes). Roll-up varies with the pay structure; compute yours.
- Show cost per year and **cumulative over the agreement**, and in cents/hour or per
  employee terms that bargaining teams use.
- Non-wage items have costs too: extra leave days (cover or lost output), changes to
  overtime triggers, rostering rules, redundancy terms.
- Cost the union's claims the same way, quickly, during bargaining — you'll need
  answers in hours, not weeks.

## 3. The mandate
Get written approval from the board/executive for: target and walk-away **total
cost**, must-have changes (flexibility, rostering), items you can trade, and the
point where you'd accept industrial action. Know your **BATNA** (best alternative if
no agreement) and theirs.

## 4. At the table
- Small, consistent team: lead negotiator, note-taker, a costing analyst, an
  operations manager who knows the work.
- Agree ground rules (meeting schedule, communications, confidentiality).
- **Interest-based** approaches (exploring underlying interests before positions)
  often help on complex non-wage issues; positional bargaining usually remains for
  the wage number.
- Package items; never agree a single issue in isolation unless it's final.
  "Nothing is agreed until everything is agreed."
- Keep accurate notes of every offer — good-faith obligations and later disputes
  depend on the record.
- Don't communicate directly with employees in a way that bypasses or undermines the
  union where the law prohibits it.

## 5. Contingency and close
- Plan for **industrial action**: service continuity, safety-critical cover, lawful
  responses, customer communications. Don't plan unlawful replacement or
  victimisation.
- Draft agreement language carefully; ambiguous clauses become grievances. Have the
  final text checked for cost and legal compliance.
- **Ratification** by members (and in some regimes, a tribunal or authority) before
  it takes effect. Then brief managers on what changed.

## Gotchas
- Forgetting roll-up and compounding understates cost badly.
- Back-pay for late settlements.
- Side letters and "custom and practice" can bind as much as the agreement.
- Leaks of the mandate destroy your position; limit who knows it.

## Verify
- [ ] Clause review, grievance history and data pack done before first meeting.
- [ ] Every proposal costed annually and cumulatively, including roll-up.
- [ ] Written mandate with walk-away and trade-offs approved.
- [ ] Offer record kept for every session.
- [ ] Contingency plan ready; final text cost- and legally-checked before ratification.
