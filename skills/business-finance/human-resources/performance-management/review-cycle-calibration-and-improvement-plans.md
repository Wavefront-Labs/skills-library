# Review cycle, calibration and improvement plans

**Goal:** every employee knows what's expected, hears honest feedback through the
year, gets a rating that means the same across managers, and — if underperforming —
gets a real chance to improve before any formal consequence.

## 1. Expectations
- At the start of the period: **3–5 goals** (outcomes, measurable, owned) plus the
  **behaviours/competencies** for their level. OKRs, SMART goals or a mix — be
  consistent.
- Record them in the HRIS/performance tool; adjust when priorities change and note why.
- **Regular one-to-ones** (e.g. every 1–2 weeks) with feedback in the moment. The
  formal review should never be the first time someone hears about an issue.

## 2. The review
- Inputs: goal outcomes, examples of behaviour across the **whole period** (managers
  should keep notes — otherwise recency bias dominates), self-assessment, peer
  or 360 input where useful.
- Write evidence, not adjectives: "shipped the billing migration two weeks early,
  cutting failed payments 30%" rather than "great team player".
- Rating scale: 4–5 points with definitions. Separate **what** (results) from **how**
  (behaviours) if your culture needs both.

## 3. Calibration
Purpose: make ratings consistent across teams and catch bias.
- Managers submit **proposed ratings with evidence** before the session.
- Group by comparable population (same level/function), 6–10 managers, facilitated
  by HR. Review outliers and the boundaries (who's on the edge of a rating).
- Look at the **distribution** by team and by demographic group (where lawful); ask
  why a team is all "exceeds" or why one group gets lower ratings.
- Challenge common biases: recency, halo, leniency/severity, similar-to-me,
  attribution of visibility vs impact (remote and quieter people).
- Use guideline distributions as a check, not a forced curve; forced ranking tends to
  damage collaboration.
- Record the changes and the reason.

## 4. Conversation and pay link
Deliver the review in a conversation, not an email. If ratings feed pay (merit
matrix; see business-finance.human-resources.compensation-and-benefits.salary-bands-and-market-benchmarking),
explain how. **Promotions** are decided separately against the next level's criteria,
with evidence of already operating at that level — not as a reward for a high rating.

## 5. Performance improvement plans (PIPs)
Use when informal support hasn't worked:
1. Check causes first: unclear expectations, resources, health or disability (may
   require reasonable adjustments), personal circumstances, poor management.
2. Written plan: the specific gap with examples, the measurable standard required,
   support offered (training, coaching, more frequent check-ins), duration (commonly
   30–90 days), and the possible consequence if not met.
3. Weekly documented check-ins with honest progress.
4. Outcome: successful (close it in writing), extended (once, with reason), or move
   to a formal capability process per policy and law.
A PIP that's designed to fail is obvious in the record and legally risky.

## Gotchas
- Ratings inflation: if most people "exceed", ratings carry no information.
- Surprise in the annual review = management failure during the year.
- Goals set and never revisited.
- Calibration that only pushes ratings down to fit a budget.
- Local law may require consultation (e.g. works councils) before introducing or
  changing rating systems — unverified per country.

## Verify
- [ ] Goals and level expectations recorded at period start.
- [ ] Reviews cite evidence across the whole period.
- [ ] Calibration done with distributions checked by team and group; changes logged.
- [ ] Promotions decided against next-level criteria, separately from ratings.
- [ ] Any PIP is specific, supported, time-boxed and documented weekly.
