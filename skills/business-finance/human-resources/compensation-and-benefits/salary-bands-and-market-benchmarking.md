# Salary bands and market benchmarking

**Goal:** a pay structure where every role sits in a defensible range based on the
market, people are placed in it consistently, and differences in pay can be explained
by legitimate factors. Pay-transparency and equal-pay law is changing quickly (EU Pay
Transparency Directive, US state posting laws, UK gender pay gap reporting) —
**confirm current obligations for each jurisdiction you employ in**.

## 1. Job architecture first
- Group roles into **job families** (engineering, sales, finance) and **levels** with
  written criteria (scope, complexity, autonomy, impact). Typical: individual
  contributor levels and a parallel management track.
- Every employee maps to one job + level. Titles follow levels, not negotiation.

## 2. Market pricing
- Use **reputable salary surveys** (provider surveys with large participant sets)
  matched on job content and level, not title alone. Match to the right market:
  industry, company size/revenue, geography.
- Take a market percentile you've chosen as policy (e.g. **P50** for most roles, P75
  for hard-to-hire skills). Write the policy down.
- **Age the data** to a common effective date using the survey's movement
  projections (surveys are often 6–12 months old).
- Blend 2–3 sources; flag matches with low sample sizes.
- Free job-board salary data is useful as a sense check, not as the benchmark.

## 3. Build the bands
- **Midpoint** = market rate at your chosen percentile for the level.
- **Range spread** (max/min − 1): commonly ~30–40% for junior roles, ~50–60% for
  senior (heuristic). With the midpoint centred: min = 2 × midpoint ÷ (2 + spread),
  max = min × (1 + spread). A 50% spread gives 80%–120% of midpoint.
- **Midpoint progression** between levels typically 10–20%; overlap between adjacent
  bands is normal.
- Geographic differentials as separate structures or % adjustments by location tier.

## 4. Place and manage people
- **Compa-ratio** = salary ÷ band midpoint. ~0.8–0.9 learning the role, ~1.0 fully
  proficient, >1.1 sustained top performance or long tenure.
- **Merit matrix:** increase % depends on performance rating *and* position in range
  (higher % for high performers low in range), within the merit budget.
- **Below min (green circle):** bring to minimum within a set time.
  **Above max (red circle):** freeze base or pay lump sums until the band catches up.
- New-hire offers inside the range, set against internal peers, not candidate's past pay
  (salary history questions are banned in many places).

## 5. Pay equity review (at least annually)
- Regress pay on legitimate factors (level, location, tenure in role, performance) and
  check whether gender/ethnicity (where data lawfully held) remains significant.
- Review **unexplained gaps** person by person; budget for remediation.
- Do privileged analysis with counsel where the law allows; regulators may ask for it.

## Gotchas
- **Title inflation** to justify pay breaks the structure.
- **Compression:** new hires at market above long-serving staff — review
  incumbents when you raise offers.
- **Stale surveys** in fast markets understate pay.
- Bonus, equity and benefits matter: benchmark **total cash** and **total
  compensation** separately.
- Publishing ranges (required in some places) exposes inconsistent placements —
  clean up first.

## Verify
- [ ] Every employee mapped to a job family and level.
- [ ] Market data matched on content/level, aged, from multiple sources; percentile
      policy written.
- [ ] Bands have midpoint, min, max, and documented spreads and progression.
- [ ] Compa-ratio and red/green-circle cases reviewed each cycle.
- [ ] Annual pay-equity analysis done; gaps explained or remediated.
