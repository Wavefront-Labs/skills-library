# Rolling forecast and variance commentary

**Goal:** each month, tell leadership what happened, why, what it means for the
year, and what they should decide — with a forecast they've learned to trust.

## 1. The monthly cycle (typical)
- **WD3–4:** actuals locked by accounting.
- **WD4–6:** variance analysis and first-draft commentary; business partners ask
  budget holders "why" while it's fresh.
- **WD6–8:** forecast update with budget holders; review with the CFO.
- **WD8–10:** pack issued; decisions logged.

## 2. Driver-based rolling forecast
- Forecast a fixed horizon ahead (commonly **12–18 months**, rolling one month each
  cycle) rather than only "to year end". The year-end view is a by-product.
- Forecast **drivers**, not line items: headcount × loaded cost, customers × ARPU,
  units × price, conversion rates, days of working capital. A budget holder can argue
  with "we'll hire 4 not 6", not with "people costs 2.31m".
- Keep **detail where it matters** (top 10–20 lines that drive 80% of variance);
  trend or % of revenue for the rest.
- Separate **run-rate** from **one-offs** and **known changes** (price rise in March,
  new site in Q3). Label each.
- Lock a version each month (`FC-Jun`) so you can compare forecasts over time.

## 3. Variance analysis
Compare actuals against **budget** (the commitment) and **last forecast** (the
expectation). Variance vs forecast tells you whether your forecasting works; variance
vs budget tells you how the year compares with the plan.
- Use a consistent sign convention: favourable = positive for profit.
- Set **thresholds** (e.g. >5% and >a fixed amount) to decide which lines get
  commentary.
- Split variances into **volume, price/rate, mix, timing, one-off** where possible
  (see business-finance.accounting.management-accounting.standard-costing-and-variance-analysis).
- **Timing vs permanent:** marketing spend that slipped from May to June is timing;
  a cancelled campaign is permanent. Only permanent variances change the full-year
  forecast.

## 4. Writing commentary
Pattern per material variance: **what (number) → why (driver) → so what (full-year
impact) → now what (action and owner)**.
> Revenue 120k (4%) below forecast: enterprise deal slipped to July (timing, 90k) and
> SMB churn ran 0.4pt above plan (permanent, 30k). Full-year forecast reduced 150k.
> Retention team pilot on at-risk accounts from July (owner: VP CS).

Avoid: restating the table in words ("costs were higher than budget"), unexplained
jargon, and "timing" as a catch-all that never reverses.

## 5. The pack
One page up front: headline P&L and cash vs budget/forecast, full-year outlook, top
3–5 messages, decisions needed. Then a **bridge (waterfall) chart** from budget to
forecast, KPI trend charts, then detail by department.

## Gotchas
- **Sandbagging and optimism:** track forecast accuracy (e.g. absolute % error at
  3-month horizon) by budget holder and show it; bias becomes visible.
- **Reforecasting the budget away:** never overwrite the budget column.
- **Accrual quality** drives variance quality; partner with accounting on cut-off.
- **Too many lines** — a 400-line pack nobody reads explains nothing.
- **Headcount** is the biggest cost driver for most companies; reconcile FP&A
  headcount to HR's system every month.

## Verify
- [ ] Actuals in the pack tie to the locked ledger.
- [ ] Every material variance has what / why / so-what / now-what.
- [ ] Timing vs permanent split stated; full-year forecast reflects only permanent.
- [ ] Forecast version saved and accuracy tracked against prior versions.
- [ ] Decisions requested are explicit and get logged with owners.
