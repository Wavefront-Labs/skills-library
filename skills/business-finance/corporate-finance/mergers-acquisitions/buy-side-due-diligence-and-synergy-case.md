# Buy-side due diligence and synergy case

**Goal:** know what you're really buying — sustainable earnings, true net debt,
normal working capital, and deal-breaking risks — and price synergies you can
actually deliver, so the price you pay and the protections in the purchase agreement
reflect it. This is a corporate acquirer's checklist; legal diligence, tax structuring
and regulatory clearance need specialist advisers.

## 1. Organise the work
- A **diligence request list** by workstream (financial, commercial, tax, legal, HR,
  IT, operations, ESG), each with an owner on your side.
- Track Q&A through the **virtual data room** log; every answer goes in the log, not
  in emails.
- Weekly **red-flag report** to the deal sponsor: issue, impact on value, proposed
  treatment (price, SPA protection, walk away).

## 2. Quality of earnings (QoE)
Rebuild EBITDA from the trial balance and adjust to a **normalised, run-rate** figure:
- Remove one-offs (litigation, restructuring, a lost contract's exit costs) — but
  challenge "one-offs" that happen every year.
- Owner-related costs and non-arm's-length related-party transactions at market rates.
- Pro forma effects of changes already made (new pricing, closed site) — only with evidence.
- **Accounting policy differences** vs yours (capitalised development, revenue recognition).
- Check **cash conversion**: EBITDA that doesn't turn into operating cash is suspect.
Tie revenue to invoices and cash receipts on a sample; look at customer
concentration, churn and pricing trends by cohort.

## 3. Net debt and working capital
Most deals price on **enterprise value**, then bridge to equity:
`Equity price = EV − net debt − debt-like items ± (actual NWC − NWC peg)`
- **Debt-like items:** unpaid taxes, deferred revenue for which cash was already
  received (judgement), pension deficits, deferred consideration on old deals,
  accrued bonuses, customer deposits, leases (depending on approach), overdue
  creditors. Each one you miss is a straight transfer to the seller.
- **NWC peg:** the "normal" working capital, usually an average of the last 12 monthly
  balances (seasonality matters). A seller who stretches payables before closing
  inflates cash you're paying for.
- Mechanism: **completion accounts** (true-up after closing) vs **locked box** (price
  fixed at a past balance sheet date, with "leakage" protection). Locked box needs
  stronger diligence on that balance sheet.

## 4. Synergy case
- List synergies by line: **cost** (procurement, duplicate overhead, facilities) and
  **revenue** (cross-sell, pricing). Each needs an owner, a baseline, a timing curve
  and the **one-off cost to achieve** it.
- Haircut revenue synergies heavily or exclude them from the price; they're the least
  reliable.
- Include **dis-synergies** (customer losses, retention bonuses, TSA costs).
- Rule of thumb: don't pay away more than a minority of the synergy value; the
  premium paid is a cost you bear whether synergies arrive or not.

## 5. Into the deal documents
Each red flag goes to one of: price adjustment, specific indemnity, warranty,
condition precedent, escrow/retention, or walk away. Hand integration planners the
diligence findings before signing — day-1 issues (systems, payroll, key people) are
found in diligence.

## Gotchas
- Management-prepared "adjusted EBITDA" accepted at face value.
- Seasonal NWC: a peg from a single month is almost always wrong.
- Change-of-control clauses in key customer, supplier and debt contracts.
- Key-person dependency and retention not costed.
- Exact SPA mechanics and warranty/indemnity norms vary by jurisdiction and market
  practice — confirm with counsel.

## Verify
- [ ] Normalised EBITDA bridge from reported, each adjustment evidenced.
- [ ] EV-to-equity bridge lists every debt-like item considered.
- [ ] NWC peg uses 12+ months and reflects seasonality.
- [ ] Synergies have owners, timing, cost to achieve, and a haircut for revenue.
- [ ] Every red flag mapped to an SPA treatment or walk-away decision.
