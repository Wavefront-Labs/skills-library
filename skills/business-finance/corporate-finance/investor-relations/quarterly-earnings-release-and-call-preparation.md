# Quarterly earnings release and call preparation

**Goal:** publish results that the market understands the first time, deliver a call
with no surprises for management, and never disclose material information to some
investors before others. Disclosure rules here are summarised for US (Reg FD, SEC) and
EU/UK (MAR) at a high level — **your securities counsel sets the actual rules**.

## 1. Timeline (results ~4–6 weeks after quarter end, adjust to your cycle)
- **Quarter end:** quiet period starts (no guidance conversations; meetings limited
  to public information). Insider trading blackout already in force.
- **T−15 working days:** consensus collected from covering analysts; draft release
  skeleton and script outline; key messages agreed with CEO/CFO.
- **T−10:** first full draft of release, script, slides; Q&A document started.
- **T−5:** numbers final from controller; audit committee/board review; legal review.
- **T−2:** rehearsal with mock Q&A — including hostile questions.
- **T−0:** release via regulated channel (newswire / RNS / 8-K) *before* the call and
  before market open (or after close); website updated simultaneously; call held.
- **T+1 to T+10:** callbacks and roadshow meetings on public information only;
  perception feedback gathered.

## 2. The release
- Headline numbers first: revenue, profit measure, EPS, cash flow, guidance.
- **Non-GAAP / alternative performance measures:** each one defined, reconciled to the most directly comparable GAAP/IFRS measure (US Regulation G, 17 CFR 244.100, requires presenting that measure with a reconciliation), and used consistently quarter to quarter. Changing definitions is a credibility hit.
- Segment table, balance sheet, cash flow.
- **Forward-looking statements** language (safe harbor in the US) and risk factor
  reference.

## 3. Guidance
- Decide the philosophy and keep it: annual vs quarterly, ranges vs points, which
  metrics. Ranges should be achievable at the midpoint.
- Compare your view with consensus. A large gap that you *don't* address is itself a
  message; material updates go out by public release, never by quiet nudges to
  individual analysts.

## 4. Script and Q&A
- Script: CEO strategy/highlights (5–7 min), CFO numbers and guidance (8–10 min).
  Same messages as the release, in plain words.
- **Q&A document:** 40–80 anticipated questions with agreed answers and the data
  behind them, sorted by likelihood. Sources: last quarter's questions, analyst notes,
  short-seller reports, peers' calls, anything that moved vs consensus.
- Assign who answers what. Agree "we don't comment on" lines (M&A rumours, individual
  customers) and use them consistently.

## 5. Disclosure discipline
- Material, non-public information goes out to everyone at once through the
  regulated channel. If something material slips in a private meeting, Regulation FD requires prompt public disclosure: as soon as reasonably practicable, and no later than the later of 24 hours or the start of the next NYSE trading day after a senior official learns of the disclosure (17 CFR 243.101(d)). EU/UK MAR has its own disclosure and delay rules. **Confirm with counsel.**
- Keep a log of investor meetings, attendees and topics.
- Only authorised spokespeople talk to investors.

## Gotchas
- Restating a prior quarter's KPI without flagging it.
- Slides that contain numbers not in the release.
- Off-script guidance by an executive on the call ("we're comfortable with consensus").
- Website, wire and filing out of sync by minutes — markets notice.
- Assuming one country's disclosure rules apply to a dual-listed company.

## Verify
- [ ] Release, slides, script and filing show identical numbers.
- [ ] Every non-GAAP measure defined and reconciled to GAAP/IFRS.
- [ ] Q&A pack covers consensus gaps and last quarter's questions.
- [ ] Rehearsal done; spokespeople and no-comment lines agreed.
- [ ] Simultaneous public release via the regulated channel; meeting log kept.
