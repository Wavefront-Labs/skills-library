# Spreading financial statements and writing the credit memo

**Goal:** turn a borrower's accounts into a standardised spread, analyse whether they
can repay, and write a credit memo that lets an approver decide in ten minutes.

## 1. Gather
- 3 years of annual accounts (audited/reviewed if available) plus year-to-date
  management accounts and the same period last year.
- Debt schedule (lender, balance, rate, repayment, security), aged debtors and
  creditors, projections, tax returns for small/owner-managed businesses.
- Note the accounts' basis (audited vs compiled), year-end, and any qualified
  opinion or going-concern note — read the notes, not just the primary statements.

## 2. Spread
Map each line into the bank's standard template (usually in the loan-origination
system or a spreadsheet) so borrowers are comparable:
- Income statement: revenue, COGS, gross profit, opex, EBITDA, depreciation, interest,
  tax, net profit.
- Balance sheet: cash, receivables, inventory, fixed assets, payables, short- and
  long-term debt, equity.
- **Normalise:** strip one-offs (asset sale gains, restructuring), add back excess
  owner salary/related-party rent at market terms, treat leases consistently.
  Record every adjustment and why.
- Tie-out: assets = liabilities + equity in every year; retained-earnings movement
  reconciles to profit less dividends. A spread that doesn't balance is wrong.

## 3. Analyse — tell the story behind the ratios
- **Growth and margins:** revenue trend, gross and EBITDA margin. Falling gross margin
  with rising revenue = buying growth.
- **Working capital:** debtor days (receivables ÷ revenue × 365), inventory days,
  creditor days; **cash conversion cycle** = DSO + DIO − DPO. Lengthening debtor days
  can signal collection issues or aggressive revenue recognition.
- **Cash flow:** operating cash flow vs EBITDA (persistent big gap = earnings quality
  problem), capex vs depreciation, free cash flow.
- **Repayment capacity:** DSCR on existing + proposed debt, base and downside.
- **Leverage and liquidity:** debt/EBITDA, current ratio, quick ratio, tangible net worth.
- Compare to sector benchmarks if your bank has them.

## 4. Qualitative assessment
Management depth and track record, customer concentration (one customer > ~20% of
revenue is a risk), supplier dependence, industry outlook, ownership/group structure,
account conduct with your bank (overdraft usage, returned items).

## 5. Risk rating
Apply the bank's rating model (scorecard or PD model). Know which inputs drive it and
where you're overriding with judgement — overrides need written justification.

## 6. The credit memo
Structure:
1. **Request & recommendation** — amount, facility, purpose, tenor, pricing, security,
   rating; approve/decline. One paragraph.
2. **Key risks and mitigants** — 3–5 bullets, honestly stated.
3. Borrower background and management.
4. Financial analysis with the spread summary table.
5. Repayment sources: primary (operating cash), secondary (collateral), tertiary (guarantees).
6. Security, covenants, conditions precedent.
7. Policy exceptions, each flagged and justified.
Write so the conclusion is first and every claim is supported by a number in the spread.

## Gotchas
- Relying on projections the borrower made without stress-testing them.
- Missing off-balance-sheet obligations (guarantees given, leases, earn-outs) — check notes.
- Year-end window dressing: compare year-end to average balances from management accounts.
- Stale accounts: an 18-month-old balance sheet tells you little.

## Verify
- [ ] Spread balances every year and each normalisation is documented.
- [ ] Cash conversion cycle and operating cash flow vs EBITDA examined.
- [ ] DSCR shown for base and downside including the new facility.
- [ ] Memo leads with request/recommendation and lists risks with mitigants.
- [ ] Every policy exception is explicit, not buried.
