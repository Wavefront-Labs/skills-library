# Setting up and running a village savings and loan group (VSLA)

**Goal:** help a community group of roughly 15–30 people save together, lend to each
other from their own savings, and share out the fund transparently each cycle,
without outside capital.

*Draft: based on the widely used VSLA methodology promoted by NGOs (e.g. the model
CARE helped spread) and its common variants; groups adapt the rules, and
practitioners' training manuals should be followed where a programme exists.*

## 1. Form the group
- Members self-select: people who know and trust each other, live close, and can
  attend weekly. Typically 15–30 members.
- Elect a management committee (chair, record-keeper, box-keeper, two money-counters),
  ideally by secret ballot.
- Write a **constitution** together: meeting day, share price, minimum/maximum
  shares per meeting, loan interest rate, maximum loan size, loan term, fines for
  lateness/absence, social fund contribution, cycle length (commonly 9–12 months).

## 2. The core mechanics
- **Shares:** members buy 1–5 shares at each meeting at the fixed share price. Savings
  vary by how many shares each buys, not by changing the price.
- **Social fund:** a small fixed contribution each meeting, for emergencies (funeral,
  illness) as grants, kept separately.
- **Loans** from the loan fund (accumulated shares + interest + fines): usually up
  to ~3× the borrower's savings, for 1–3 months, with a flat monthly service charge
  agreed by the group (e.g. 5–10% per month in many programmes — high by bank
  standards but the interest returns to members). *(Ranges vary widely; the group sets them.)*
- **Cash box with three locks;** three different members hold the keys, and the
  box-keeper is not a key-holder. Money is counted aloud in the meeting.

## 3. Meeting procedure (same order every time)
1. Open; roll call; fines.
2. Social fund contributions.
3. Share purchases — recorded in each member's passbook (stamps or entries)
   and the group ledger.
4. Loan repayments.
5. New loan requests, approved by the group, recorded.
6. Count the cash balance aloud; record it; lock the box.
Transparency is the control: everything happens in front of everyone.

## 4. Share-out at cycle end
- All loans must be repaid before share-out.
- Total fund ÷ total shares = **value per share**; each member receives shares held ×
  value per share. Members see their return (often well above the share price).
- The group then decides whether to start a new cycle, change the share price or rules.

## 5. ROSCAs (rotating savings) — the simpler cousin
Each member pays a fixed amount each period; one member takes the whole pot each
round until everyone has had it. No lending, no interest, no records beyond the
order — but early recipients effectively borrow from later ones, so order is decided
by lottery, need or bidding.

## Gotchas
- **Elite capture:** a few members taking most loans; cap loan sizes relative to savings.
- **Cash at home** belonging to the group is a theft risk for the box-keeper; consider
  mobile-money group wallets where available, with the same multi-signatory principle.
- **Outside loans** injected into young groups often destabilise them; most programmes
  advise at least one full cycle on own savings first.
- Literacy: use stamps/pictorial passbooks so every member can check their record.

## Verify
- [ ] Written constitution with share price, loan rules, fines and cycle length.
- [ ] Three-lock box with keys held by three members other than the box-keeper.
- [ ] Every transaction recorded in passbook and ledger during the meeting.
- [ ] Cash counted aloud and recorded at every meeting.
- [ ] Share-out computed as fund ÷ shares and paid only after all loans repaid.
