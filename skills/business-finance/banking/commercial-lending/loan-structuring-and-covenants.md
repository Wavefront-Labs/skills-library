# Structuring a business loan and setting covenants

**Goal:** fit the loan's shape (amount, tenor, amortisation, security, covenants) to
how the borrower's cash actually flows, so it can be repaid from operations and the
lender gets early warning if it can't.

## 1. Match the facility to the purpose
| Need | Typical facility | Tenor / repayment |
|---|---|---|
| Seasonal working capital | Revolving credit / overdraft, often against a borrowing base | 1 year, renewable; cleans up seasonally |
| Receivables/inventory funding | Asset-based line (advance rates on eligible debtors/stock) | Revolving |
| Equipment | Term loan or lease | Amortises over ≤ asset's useful life |
| Property | Commercial mortgage | Longer amortisation, often a balloon |
| Acquisition / growth capex | Term loan, maybe delayed-draw | 3–7 years amortising |
Golden rule: **don't fund long-term assets with short-term money**, and don't let
the loan outlive the asset securing it.

## 2. Size it from repayment capacity
- Start with normalised cash flow available for debt service — commonly EBITDA minus
  unfunded capex minus cash taxes (definitions vary by bank).
- **DSCR** (debt service coverage) = cash flow ÷ (interest + scheduled principal).
  Many lenders look for ≥1.25x for business loans. *(Indicative; set by credit
  policy.)*
- **Leverage** = total debt ÷ EBITDA, judged against sector norms.
- Run a downside case (e.g. revenue −15%, margin −2 pts). The structure should still
  service debt, or covenants should trip before cash runs out.

## 3. Security and support
- Fixed and floating charges / general security agreement over business assets;
  specific mortgages over property; assignment of key contracts or insurances.
- **Guarantees** from owners or parent companies for small/owner-managed businesses.
- Value collateral at forced-sale, not book value; apply haircuts (receivables and
  stock far more than property).
- Perfect security properly (registrations, priority searches); unregistered security
  may be void against a liquidator.

## 4. Covenants
- **Financial maintenance covenants** (tested quarterly on management accounts or
  annually on audited): minimum DSCR / interest cover, maximum leverage, minimum
  net worth or liquidity.
- **Set headroom of ~15–30%** below the base-case forecast so normal volatility
  doesn't breach, but a genuine deterioration does. *(Common practice range.)*
- **Information covenants:** monthly/quarterly management accounts, annual audited
  statements within X days, compliance certificate signed by a director.
- **Negative covenants:** limits on further borrowing, asset disposals, dividends,
  change of control, giving security to others (negative pledge).
- Define every term precisely in the facility agreement (what's in "EBITDA"?
  one-offs? leases?); loose definitions are where disputes arise.

## 5. Pricing
Margin over a reference rate reflecting risk rating, security and tenor, plus
arrangement fee, commitment fee on undrawn revolvers, and early-repayment terms.
Check the deal meets the bank's return-on-capital hurdle — pricing is constrained by
policy, not just by negotiation.

## Gotchas
- Covenants set without a forecast test: a breach in quarter one damages the
  relationship and forces a waiver.
- Bullet repayments without a credible refinancing route.
- Ignoring the borrower's working-capital cycle; a growing business can be profitable
  and run out of cash.
- Guarantee enforceability rules (independent advice requirements for personal
  guarantors in some jurisdictions).

## Verify
- [ ] Facility type and tenor match the purpose and asset life.
- [ ] Base and downside DSCR computed from normalised cash flow.
- [ ] Security valued at realisable value and its perfection steps listed.
- [ ] Each financial covenant has a definition, test date and stated headroom.
- [ ] Reporting obligations and negative covenants are written into the term sheet.
