# Running a sell-side M&A process (investment banking advisory)

**Goal:** sell a company for the best achievable value and terms, with deal certainty,
by running a controlled competitive auction rather than a bilateral negotiation.

*Draft: describes the common broad-auction structure in mid-market and large-cap deals;
timelines and documents vary by market, size and regulator.*

## 1. Preparation (4–8 weeks)
- **Engagement letter:** scope, success fee (often a percentage of enterprise value,
  sometimes tiered/ratcheted), retainer, exclusivity, tail period.
- **Understand the business and the seller's goals:** price vs speed vs certainty vs
  employees/legacy; full or partial sale; management rollover.
- **Valuation range** using trading comparables, precedent transactions and DCF (plus
  LBO analysis to estimate what financial sponsors can pay).
- **Buyer list:** strategics and sponsors, ranked by fit and ability to pay; typically
  dozens of names for a broad auction, a handful for a targeted one.
- **Materials:** a blind **teaser** (1–2 pages, no name), the **confidential
  information memorandum (CIM)**, a management presentation, and an operating model.
- Encourage vendor due diligence (financial, tax, sometimes legal) to front-load issues.

## 2. First round (4–6 weeks)
- Send teasers; buyers sign an **NDA** (watch non-solicit and standstill clauses) to get
  the CIM.
- **Process letter** sets rules: deadline, format of indications of interest (IOIs) —
  headline price or range, structure, financing, key assumptions, approvals needed.
- Field buyer questions through the bank; log every contact.

## 3. Second round (6–8 weeks)
- Shortlist several bidders based on price *and* certainty.
- Open the **virtual data room** (staged: more sensitive data later, clean-team
  arrangements for competitor buyers).
- Management presentations, site visits, expert sessions.
- Circulate a **draft SPA** (sale and purchase agreement); bidders return mark-ups with
  binding offers. The mark-up is part of the bid — heavy changes = lower real value.

## 4. Evaluate and negotiate
Compare bids on a like-for-like **equity value** basis: enterprise value adjusted for
net debt, working-capital peg, debt-like items; locked-box vs completion-accounts
mechanism; deferred/contingent consideration; financing certainty (committed papers?);
regulatory/antitrust risk; warranty and indemnity scope (W&I insurance?).
Keep tension: grant exclusivity only when terms are near-final.

## 5. Signing to closing
Conditions precedent (regulatory approvals, third-party consents), completion
mechanics, funds flow memo. The bank coordinates lawyers, accountants and the seller's
management throughout.

## Gotchas
- **Confidentiality leaks** damage staff, customer and supplier relationships; use code
  names and need-to-know lists.
- **Headline price traps:** a high price with a punitive working-capital peg or
  heavy SPA mark-up can be worth less than a lower clean bid.
- **Management fatigue:** running the business while selling it — protect performance
  in the deal period; a missed forecast mid-process re-trades price.
- **Insider information** in listed situations: wall-crossing procedures and insider
  lists are regulatory obligations.
- **Conflicts:** banks advising sellers may also offer staple financing; disclose and manage.

## Verify
- [ ] Seller objectives and walk-away terms are written down before launch.
- [ ] Every buyer contact is under NDA and logged.
- [ ] Process letters specify exactly what bids must contain.
- [ ] Bids compared on equity value, mechanism, certainty and SPA mark-up — not headline EV.
- [ ] Exclusivity granted only with near-final terms and a short deadline.
