# Coverage determination and reserving on a property/liability claim

**Goal:** decide whether a claim is covered, set a realistic reserve early, quantify
the loss, and settle fairly and promptly — with a file that survives audit,
complaint or litigation.

*Draft: generic adjusting practice. Regulatory deadlines (acknowledgement, decision,
payment) and fair-claims rules differ by jurisdiction — your handling standards
override the timings here.*

## 1. First notice of loss (FNOL) — first 24–48 hours
- Acknowledge the claim; capture date, place, cause, what's damaged/injured,
  parties, police/fire reference, photos.
- **Mitigation advice:** tell the insured to prevent further damage (emergency
  repairs, drying) and keep damaged items and receipts. Unmitigated loss may not be
  recoverable.
- Set an **initial reserve** (see §4) even on limited facts.

## 2. Coverage analysis — read the policy, in order
1. **Policy in force** on the loss date; premium paid; correct named insured.
2. **Insuring agreement:** is this the kind of loss/event the policy responds to?
3. **Exclusions:** does any apply? Burden of proving an exclusion usually sits with
   the insurer — collect evidence for it.
4. **Conditions:** notice given in time, cooperation, warranties complied with.
   In many jurisdictions a breach only defeats the claim if it's material/causative
   — check local law.
5. **Limits, sub-limits, deductibles**, co-insurance/average (underinsurance).
6. **Other insurance** that may share the loss.
If coverage is uncertain, investigate under a **reservation of rights** (where used)
rather than denying early or paying and regretting.

## 3. Investigate and quantify
- Inspect (adjuster, or desk-based for small claims with photos/video).
- Establish **cause** (expert if needed: engineer, forensic accountant, fire investigator).
- Quantify on the policy's basis: **replacement cost vs actual cash value**
  (depreciation), repair vs replace, business-interruption loss from accounts.
- Get competing estimates for large repairs; check for pre-existing damage.
- Identify **subrogation** potential (a negligent third party) — preserve evidence.

## 4. Reserving
- Reserve = best estimate of the ultimate cost of the claim (indemnity + expenses),
  not the first estimate received or the amount you hope to pay.
- Revisit at every significant development and on a diary (e.g. every 30–90 days).
- Separate indemnity and expense reserves; document the basis of each change.
- **Avoid stair-stepping** — many small increases over time distort the insurer's
  reported results; move straight to the realistic number when information arrives.

## 5. Decide and settle
- Communicate the decision in writing, citing the policy wording relied on for any
  decline or partial payment, and how to complain/appeal.
- Negotiate with evidence, not splitting the difference. Get a signed
  discharge/release for full and final settlements.
- Pay promptly once agreed; record recoveries (subrogation, salvage).

## Gotchas
- **Admitting liability** to a third party on the insured's behalf — never; and
  instruct the insured not to.
- **Waiver by conduct:** acting as if covered (e.g. taking over defence) without
  reservation can waive policy defences.
- **Fraud indicators** go to the SIU via the referral process; don't confront the claimant.
- Vulnerable claimants need adjusted handling; check your conduct rules.

## Verify
- [ ] Policy in force, insuring clause, exclusions, conditions and limits checked in order.
- [ ] Initial reserve set within 48 h and reviewed on diary.
- [ ] Cause established and quantum computed on the policy's valuation basis.
- [ ] Decline/partial letters cite specific wording and complaint routes.
- [ ] Subrogation and salvage considered and recorded.
