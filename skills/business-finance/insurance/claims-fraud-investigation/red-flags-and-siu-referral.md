# Red flags and SIU referral of suspect claims

**Goal:** spot claims that may be fraudulent, investigate proportionately and lawfully,
and decide whether to pay, refer, decline or report — without treating honest
claimants as suspects.

*Draft: generic special investigations unit (SIU) practice. Legal duties (mandatory
fraud reporting, privacy limits on surveillance and data sharing, fair-claims rules)
vary by jurisdiction; follow your insurer's SIU procedures and legal advice.*

## 1. Red flags — indicators, not proof
Look for **clusters**, never a single flag:
- **Timing:** loss soon after inception or cover increase; just before expiry or a
  missed premium; late notification without explanation.
- **Claimant behaviour:** pressure for quick cash settlement, unusual familiarity with
  claims process, reluctance to provide documents or allow inspection, only
  contactable by mobile/email, changes in story.
- **Documents:** receipts from businesses that don't exist, sequential invoice
  numbers from "different" suppliers, metadata inconsistencies, photos reused from the
  internet (reverse image search), altered dates.
- **Loss circumstances:** damage inconsistent with stated cause, items claimed with no
  proof of ownership, financial distress of the insured, prior similar claims.
- **Networks (motor/injury):** same repairers, medical providers, solicitors,
  witnesses or phone numbers recurring across unrelated claims.
Automated scoring and network analytics flag claims; a human decides.

## 2. Triage and referral
Claims handler → documents the specific indicators → refers through the SIU referral
form. SIU accepts, returns with guidance, or declines. Keep normal handling going and
**meet regulatory timelines** while investigating; delay is not a tactic.

## 3. Investigation toolkit (proportionate to value and suspicion)
- Desk checks: claims-history databases/industry fraud registers where lawful,
  policy history, open-source and social-media review (public information only),
  company and land registries.
- Document verification directly with the issuer (supplier, hospital, police).
- **Recorded interview / statement** of the claimant, conducted fairly and without
  inducement; allow representation where applicable.
- Experts: forensic engineers, fire investigators, vehicle examiners, document examiners.
- Surveillance only where legally permitted and justified, with authorisation recorded.

## 4. Decision
- **No fraud found** → return to normal handling promptly; nothing hostile in tone.
- **Exaggeration** → pay the genuine part where law/policy permits, or decline entirely
  where the policy's fraud condition and law allow forfeiture. *(Jurisdiction-dependent.)*
- **Fraud proven** on balance of evidence → decline, potentially void/cancel the
  policy, recover prior payments, and report to the regulator/fraud bureau/police
  where required or appropriate.
- Write the decision citing evidence and policy terms; legal review before alleging fraud.

## 5. Records and feedback
Case file with chain of evidence, interview records, authorisations and decision.
Feed confirmed patterns back to underwriting and to the detection rules.

## Gotchas
- **Confirmation bias:** after a referral, investigators seek fraud; set out what
  evidence would *clear* the claim too.
- **Defamation and bad-faith exposure** from unfounded allegations or unreasonable delay.
- **Pretexting** (lying about who you are to get information) is unlawful in many places.
- Vulnerable claimants: language barriers and distress mimic "inconsistent stories".

## Verify
- [ ] Referral lists specific indicators, not "gut feel".
- [ ] Regulatory claim deadlines still met during investigation.
- [ ] Each investigative step was proportionate and lawfully authorised.
- [ ] Decision letter cites evidence and policy conditions; legal review done for fraud declines.
- [ ] Outcome fed back to detection rules and underwriting.
