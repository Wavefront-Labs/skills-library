# Programme budget and burn rate

**Goal:** a donor-funded programme that spends its budget on time, on eligible costs,
within flexibility limits — with no surprise underspend in month 10 or disallowed costs
at audit. Each institutional donor (e.g. USAID-type US federal awards, ECHO, FCDO, UN
agencies, foundations) has its own rules; the **grant agreement and its annexes win
over any rule of thumb here**.

## 1. Build the budget from the workplan
- Start from activities in the logframe/workplan (see
  logframe-and-program-design.theory-of-change-and-logframe), not from last year's budget.
- Each line: description, unit, quantity, unit cost, **duration/frequency**, % charged
  to this grant, total, and a **budget note** explaining the basis (quotes, salary
  scales, market surveys).
- Typical structure: staff (with % time allocation), travel, supplies/commodities,
  equipment, contracts/sub-grants, other direct costs, then **indirect cost recovery**
  (a percentage set by the donor or a negotiated rate).
- Shared costs (office rent, vehicles, security) charged by a **documented, consistent
  allocation method** (e.g. staff headcount or time) — the same method across donors.
- Add **inflation and FX** assumptions for multi-year or multi-currency budgets.
- Sanity checks: cost per beneficiary vs comparable programmes; support costs vs
  programme costs; every line tied to an activity.

## 2. Know the eligibility rules before you spend
For each grant keep a one-page **compliance summary**: eligible period, ineligible cost
types (common examples: alcohol, fines, entertainment, costs before start date,
items already funded by another donor), procurement thresholds and required
quote/tender procedures, nationality/source rules for goods, equipment ownership and
disposal, visibility requirements, **budget flexibility** (e.g. movements between lines
up to a set percentage without approval — *percentages vary by donor*), and reporting
deadlines and formats. Brief programme and logistics staff on it; most disallowances
come from people who never saw the rules.

## 3. Monthly budget-vs-actual (BvA)
For each line: budget, actual to date, commitments (POs raised, contracts signed),
balance, % spent vs % of time elapsed. Review with the programme manager monthly:
- **Burn rate** = spend per month. **Expected burn** = remaining budget ÷ remaining months.
- **Forecast to completion** = actual + commitments + planned spend for remaining months.
- Flag lines >10% over or under the forecast trajectory (threshold is a house choice).
Underspend is as dangerous as overspend: unspent funds may be returned, and donors read
low burn as poor delivery. Front-loaded procurement or late staff hiring are typical causes.

## 4. Act on variances early
- Within flexibility: realign and document the reason.
- Beyond it: submit a **realignment or modification request** with justification well
  before the change is implemented and before the donor's last date for requests.
- Consider a **no-cost extension** if delays are genuine — request it early, with a
  revised workplan and budget.

## 5. Donor financial reporting and audit readiness
- Report in the donor's template and currency, using the agreed **exchange-rate method**
  (e.g. actual rate of transfer, monthly accounting rate — fixed per agreement).
- Reconcile the donor report to the general ledger every time; differences need a
  written reconciliation.
- Keep a **supporting-document file per transaction**: approval, quotes, contract,
  invoice, receipt, payment proof, distribution list. Audit samples will ask.
- Sub-grantees: pre-award capacity assessment, financial reports with spot checks,
  and funds advanced in tranches against liquidation.

## Gotchas
- Charging 100% of a staff member to two grants is the fastest way to a finding: keep timesheets.
- Interest earned on advances may have to be returned or reported.
- Closeout: costs must be incurred within the period; final reports and refunds have hard deadlines.

## Verify
- [ ] Every budget line has a note and links to a workplan activity.
- [ ] A compliance summary exists per grant and staff have been briefed.
- [ ] Monthly BvA with forecast-to-completion reviewed and signed off.
- [ ] Variances beyond flexibility have approved requests on file.
- [ ] Donor report reconciles to the ledger; supporting documents complete for sampled transactions.
