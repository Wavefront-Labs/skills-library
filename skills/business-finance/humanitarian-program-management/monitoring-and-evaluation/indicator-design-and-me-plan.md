# Indicator design and the M&E plan

**Goal:** a monitoring and evaluation system that tells managers in time whether the
programme is working, satisfies the donor, and is light enough for field teams to
actually run. Most M&E failures are too many indicators, vague definitions and data
nobody looks at.

## 1. Start from the logframe
Take the results chain (see logframe-and-program-design.theory-of-change-and-logframe):
activities → outputs → outcomes → impact. Choose indicators per level:
- **Output:** what was delivered ("# households receiving cash transfers, by sex of
  household head"). Easy to count, says nothing about effect.
- **Outcome:** change in behaviour, access or condition ("% of households with
  acceptable Food Consumption Score"). This is what matters.
- **Impact:** long-term, usually not attributable to one project; often borrowed from
  national data.
Aim for **2–3 indicators per result**, ~10–20 total for a mid-size project. Use
standard sector indicators (cluster or donor indicator registries) where they exist —
comparable and already defined.

## 2. Make each indicator unambiguous: the reference sheet
For every indicator, one page (often called a PIRS):
- **Precise definition** — numerator, denominator, what counts ("acceptable" per which threshold).
- **Unit and disaggregation** — sex, age bands, disability (e.g. Washington Group
  questions), location, displacement status.
- **Data source and method** — survey, distribution lists, observation.
- **Frequency** and **who collects / who analyses**.
- **Baseline value and date; targets** per period.
- **Known limitations** and data-quality risks.
Two staff reading the sheet should calculate the same number from the same data.

## 3. Baselines and targets
Measure the baseline before or at start-up with the same method you'll use at
endline. If time doesn't allow, use the earliest round of monitoring and say so.
Targets come from need, budget and realistic coverage — not round numbers. For
percentage indicators, check the sample size gives a margin of error narrower than
the change you're trying to detect.

## 4. The M&E plan and routine monitoring
A table: indicator | source | method | frequency | responsible | reporting use. Plus:
- **Activity monitoring** — distribution and attendance records, reconciled against
  registration lists.
- **Post-distribution monitoring (PDM)** — phone or in-person survey of a random sample
  of recipients within weeks: received the full entitlement? Problems? Use of
  assistance? Safety and dignity issues?
- **Feedback and complaints mechanism** — hotline, help desk, suggestion boxes; log,
  categorise, respond, and track closure time. Sensitive complaints (fraud, sexual
  exploitation) go to a separate confidential channel.
- **Data-quality assessment** quarterly: validity, reliability, timeliness, precision,
  integrity — spot-check against source documents.

## 5. Use the data
Monthly review meeting with programme staff: indicator dashboard vs targets, PDM
findings, complaint trends → decisions recorded (adapt targeting, fix a vendor, move
staff). If nobody can name a decision changed by M&E data last quarter, the system is
only feeding reports.

## 6. Evaluations
Write the Terms of Reference early: purpose and users, key questions (often framed
around OECD-DAC criteria — relevance/coherence, effectiveness, efficiency, impact,
sustainability), methods, timing, budget (rule of thumb several percent of programme
budget for M&E overall — *donor norms vary*), and independence. Plan a management
response: which recommendations are accepted, by whom, by when.

## Gotchas
- Counting the same beneficiary in several activities inflates "people reached";
  keep unique-ID registration.
- Percentages without denominators hide collapsing coverage.
- Donor indicator definitions sometimes differ from cluster definitions — map them.

## Verify
- [ ] Every logframe result has 2–3 indicators with reference sheets.
- [ ] Each indicator has baseline, targets, disaggregation and an owner.
- [ ] PDM and a feedback mechanism are scheduled and resourced.
- [ ] Data-quality checks are dated and documented.
- [ ] At least one programme decision per quarter traces to M&E evidence.
