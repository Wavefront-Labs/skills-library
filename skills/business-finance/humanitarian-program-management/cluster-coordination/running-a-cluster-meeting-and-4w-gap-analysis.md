# Running a cluster meeting and 4W gap analysis

**Goal:** get many agencies working in one sector (water, shelter, food security,
health, protection…) to cover needs without gaps or duplication — through meetings that
make decisions and a "who does what, where" dataset everyone trusts. Structures and
templates follow the IASC cluster approach as applied in each country (coordination
teams publish country-specific templates and timelines; use those).

## 1. The 3W/4W/5W dataset
Columns vary by response; the core is:
- **Who** — organisation (and implementing partner, to avoid double counting between
  donor-funded lead and local implementer).
- **What** — activity, from a fixed **activity list** tied to the sector's response-plan
  indicators.
- **Where** — admin level (usually admin 2 or 3) using official **P-codes**, not
  free-text place names.
- **When** — status (planned / ongoing / completed) and dates (the 4th W).
- **For whom** (5W) — people reached, disaggregated by sex/age/disability where possible.
Practice that makes it work:
1. **One template, locked dropdowns** (P-codes, activity list). Free text kills aggregation.
2. **Fixed monthly deadline** and one named IM focal point per partner.
3. **Clear counting rule:** report *people reached*, not services delivered, and
   specify how to avoid counting the same person twice across months (e.g. report
   new individuals monthly plus cumulative unique where possible).
4. Validate on receipt: impossible numbers, wrong P-codes, reached > target population.

## 2. Gap analysis
Join the 4W with the **needs/severity data** (from the needs overview or recent
assessments) at the same admin level:
- **Coverage** = people reached (or targeted) ÷ people in need, per area and activity.
- Flag: high severity + low coverage (**gaps**), many partners + high coverage
  (**possible duplication** — check, may be legitimate if needs are large),
  planned activities stalled for months (**ghost presence**).
- Map it: coverage choropleth plus partner-count map. One map communicates what a
  10-tab spreadsheet doesn't.
- Treat results as *indicative* — 4W completeness is never 100%; state the reporting rate.

## 3. The cluster meeting
**Before:** circulate agenda and the updated gap maps 2 days ahead; collect agenda
items; confirm interpretation if meeting multilingual (and include local NGOs — hold
meetings in a language and location they can access).
**Agenda (60–90 min):**
1. Action points from last meeting — done / not done.
2. Situation update (2–3 key changes only).
3. **Gap analysis and decisions** — who can cover gap areas? Agree names and dates.
4. Technical item (standards, a joint position, a guidance note).
5. Funding/allocation updates, inter-cluster issues.
6. AOB.
**After:** minutes within 48 hours with an **action-point table** (action, owner,
deadline), plus updated contact list and products posted on the shared platform.
Meetings that only receive updates should become emails.

## 4. Information sharing and inter-agency issues
- Share products on the response's common platform; respect **data-protection** rules —
  no beneficiary-level personal data in 4Ws.
- Escalate to inter-cluster coordination: multi-sector gaps, access constraints,
  pipeline breaks, and issues needing the humanitarian coordinator.
- Pooled-fund allocations should follow the gap analysis; document the logic for
  transparency.

## Gotchas
- Partners under-report when they fear the data will be used against them (e.g. to
  redirect funds); explain how data are used.
- Sub-national hubs often have better information than the capital meeting — link them.
- Coordinating ≠ commanding: the cluster lead persuades; it rarely directs.

## Verify
- [ ] 4W template uses P-codes and a fixed activity list; reporting rate is published.
- [ ] Coverage vs need calculated per area; gaps and possible duplication flagged on a map.
- [ ] Each meeting produces an action table with owners and deadlines within 48 h.
- [ ] Local and national NGOs can attend and participate in their language.
- [ ] No personal data in shared coordination products.
