# Rapid multi-sector needs assessment

**Goal:** within days of a shock (flood, displacement, conflict escalation), produce a
defensible picture of who needs what, where and how badly, so the response can be
prioritised — and avoid the classic failure of collecting data nobody analyses.
Frameworks referenced (MIRA, MSNA, JIAF) are inter-agency tools whose current versions
evolve; check the latest guidance on the coordination platform for your response.

## 1. Coordinate before you collect
- Check with the relevant cluster/sector or the inter-cluster coordination group: is a
  joint assessment planned? **Duplicate assessments burden communities** and waste
  access windows. Join or share rather than run parallel.
- Agree geography, timeline, and who analyses and publishes.

## 2. Secondary data review (day 0–2)
Before fieldwork, compile what's already known: pre-crisis baselines (census,
DHS/MICS-type surveys, previous assessments), situation reports, displacement
tracking, satellite damage maps, market prices, media. Structure it by sector and
geography and note **information gaps** — these define what primary data must answer.
Often 60–80% of the initial picture can come from SDR.

## 3. Analysis plan first, questionnaire second
Write the **key questions** (e.g. "Which displaced sites lack safe water?") and for
each: indicator, disaggregation (sex, age, disability, displacement status), data
source, and how it will be analysed. Every question in the tool must map to this plan;
cut anything that doesn't. Short tools get honest answers; 90-minute surveys don't.

## 4. Methods and sampling
- **Key informant interviews (KIIs):** community leaders, health workers, teachers,
  women's group representatives — fast, site-level, but biased toward gatekeepers.
  Deliberately include women, minority and disability-focused informants.
- **Focus group discussions:** separate by sex and age; good for priorities and
  protection concerns (never ask about individual incidents of violence in a group).
- **Direct observation** checklist: shelter damage, water points, latrines, markets.
- **Household surveys:** only if time, access and skills allow a proper sample. Rapid
  assessments typically use **purposive** sampling — results are *indicative*, not
  statistically representative. Say so in the report.
- Digital collection (KoboToolbox, ODK) with built-in skip logic and constraints;
  test the form in the field language before deployment.

## 5. Protection and ethics
Informed consent; no names or GPS of individuals in shared datasets; secure devices;
referral pathways ready for anyone disclosing urgent needs (health, GBV, child
protection). **Do no harm:** don't raise expectations of aid you can't deliver — explain
what the assessment is for.

## 6. Analysis: severity and priority
1. Clean daily; reconcile enumerator differences.
2. Aggregate by area and population group.
3. **Severity scoring:** rate each sector/area on a scale (e.g. 1 minimal → 5
   catastrophic, in the spirit of JIAF-style scales) using pre-agreed thresholds,
   combining indicators and triangulating sources (SDR + KII + observation).
4. Rank **priority** = severity × number of people affected × gap in current response
   (from 3W/4W data, see cluster-coordination).
5. Note confidence per finding (high/medium/low) based on source quality and coverage.

## 7. Output (one week, not one month)
A 2–4 page brief: context, methodology and limitations, key findings by sector with
severity, priority areas and groups, recommendations, information gaps, plus the
anonymised dataset shared through the coordination platform. Present to the cluster
meeting; late assessments are history, not planning tools.

## Verify
- [ ] Assessment agreed with the cluster/coordination body; no duplicate exercise.
- [ ] SDR completed and gaps defined before the tool was drafted.
- [ ] Every questionnaire item maps to the analysis plan.
- [ ] Sampling approach and its limits stated in the report.
- [ ] Consent, anonymisation and referral pathways in place.
- [ ] Findings include severity, numbers affected, confidence and priorities.
