# Theory of change and logframe

**Goal:** move from an assessed need to a programme design whose logic a donor,
a field team and an evaluator can all test — and keep it alive as conditions change.
Donor templates differ (some call levels goal/purpose/outputs, others
impact/outcome/output; some require a results framework instead of a logframe), so
adapt the vocabulary to the template you're writing for.

## 1. Problem analysis
From the needs assessment (see needs-assessment.rapid-multi-sector-needs-assessment),
build a **problem tree** with the affected community and partners where possible:
- **Core problem** in the middle ("Children under 5 in district X have high acute malnutrition").
- **Causes** below (immediate → underlying → root): poor diet diversity, diarrhoea from
  unsafe water, lost livelihoods, limited health-service access.
- **Effects** above.
Flip it into an **objective tree** (negative states → positive results). Then
**choose the slice** you'll address based on mandate, expertise, other actors' coverage
and budget. Saying what you *won't* do is part of the design.

## 2. Theory of change
A narrative plus diagram: "**If** we do these activities, **then** these outputs; **if**
these outputs exist **and** [assumptions hold], **then** these outcomes, contributing to
[impact]." The value is in making **assumptions explicit**: "Households will spend cash
on food rather than repaying debt" — "Markets can supply increased demand without price
spikes". Each risky assumption should be testable through monitoring, and some may
need their own activity (e.g. a market assessment before cash).

## 3. The logframe (4×4 matrix)
Rows: **Goal/Impact**, **Outcome(s)**, **Outputs**, **Activities**.
Columns: **Narrative summary**, **Indicators**, **Means of verification**, **Assumptions/risks**.

Write results as **states achieved**, not actions: Output "500 households have access
to a functioning water point within 500 m" — not "build water points".
Indicators and MoV follow monitoring-and-evaluation.indicator-design-and-me-plan.

**Check the logic both ways:**
- **Vertical (if–then–and):** Activities + assumptions → Outputs; Outputs + assumptions →
  Outcome. If outputs are all delivered and the outcome still wouldn't follow, an
  output or assumption is missing.
- **Horizontal:** every result has indicators; every indicator has a feasible means
  of verification you've budgeted for.
- Usually **one outcome** (or very few) per project; several unrelated outcomes means
  several projects.
- Assumptions are things **outside** your control; if it's inside, it's an activity.

## 4. From logframe to proposal
The logframe drives the rest of a donor proposal: activity list → workplan (Gantt) →
budget lines (see aid-budgeting-and-donor-compliance.programme-budget-and-burn-rate) →
staffing → M&E plan → risk matrix (likelihood × impact, mitigation, owner). Also
expected: targeting criteria and beneficiary numbers (with double-counting avoided),
cross-cutting issues (gender, protection, disability inclusion, environment),
accountability to affected people, localisation/partner role, exit or sustainability
strategy. Keep narrative, logframe, workplan and budget **mutually consistent** —
reviewers check numbers across sections.

## 5. Adaptive revision
Review the ToC and assumptions at least every 6 months, or when context shifts
(access lost, prices spike, new displacement). Changes to activities within the
same outcome may be a budget realignment; changes to outcomes, targets or locations
usually need **formal donor approval** — request it before you implement, with
evidence from monitoring.

## Gotchas
- "Increased awareness" as an outcome without a behaviour or practice indicator rarely convinces.
- Copy-pasted logframes from a previous context carry wrong assumptions.
- Targets must match budget: 10,000 households at a cost per household the budget can't cover is an instant red flag.

## Verify
- [ ] Problem tree built with community/partner input; slice chosen and justified.
- [ ] ToC names its key assumptions, each monitored or mitigated.
- [ ] Logframe passes the vertical if–then–and test and the horizontal indicator/MoV test.
- [ ] Results written as achieved states; activities are not outputs.
- [ ] Narrative, logframe, workplan, budget and targets agree with each other.
