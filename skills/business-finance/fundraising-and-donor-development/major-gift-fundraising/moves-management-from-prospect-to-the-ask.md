# Moves management: from prospect to the ask

**Goal:** turn a list of capable people into a managed portfolio where each prospect
has a next step, and asks happen when the donor is ready, for a specific amount and
purpose. "Major gift" thresholds vary hugely: $1,000 at a small charity, $100,000+ at
a university. Define yours from your gift data (often the top ~10% of donors by value).

## 1. Identify and research
- Sources: existing donors with rising or consistent giving, board and volunteer
  networks, event attendees, wealth screening of your database (vendors such as
  DonorSearch, iWave, WealthEngine screen against public records).
- Qualify on **Linkage** (a connection to you or someone close), **Ability** (capacity:
  property, public company holdings, giving to others, business ownership) and
  **Interest** (in your mission specifically). Ability without interest is the most
  common false lead.
- Research ethically: public information only, kept factual and relevant to the gift;
  donors can see their records under data-protection laws (e.g. GDPR subject access),
  so write nothing you wouldn't show them. UK charities have faced regulator fines for
  undisclosed wealth screening; check your privacy notice covers it.

## 2. The portfolio
A full-time gift officer typically carries roughly 100-150 prospects (commonly cited
range; smaller for very high-level gifts). Each record in the CRM has: stage,
**next move**, date, target ask amount, purpose, and who else is involved.
Stages: **identification → qualification → cultivation → solicitation → stewardship**.
Review the portfolio monthly; anyone without a move in 90 days needs one or should be
released.

## 3. Discovery and cultivation
- **Discovery visit** (qualification): an in-person or video meeting to learn their
  interests and history with the cause. Ask more than you tell: "What drew you to
  us?" "What would you most like to see change?" "How do you like to give?"
- **Cultivation moves**: meaningful contact that deepens involvement: site visits,
  meeting programme staff or beneficiaries (with their consent), advisory roles,
  personal impact reports. Aim for a planned move every 4-8 weeks, not random newsletters.
- Listen for readiness signals: asking about needs, costs, naming opportunities, or
  "how can I help?"

## 4. The ask
- Prepare: amount (based on capacity *and* past giving; often a stretch multiple of
  their largest gift), the specific project, who asks (often a peer volunteer plus
  staff), the setting, and anticipated objections.
- Make it **specific and in person**: "Would you consider a gift of $50,000 over two
  years to fund the reading programme at two new schools?"
- **Then stop talking.** Silence is part of the ask.
- Responses: yes; yes but less (accept graciously, ask about timing or a pledge);
  "need to think / talk to my spouse or adviser" (agree a follow-up date); no (ask what
  would interest them; the relationship continues).
- Confirm in a letter or gift agreement: amount, purpose, payment schedule, recognition,
  any restrictions.

## 5. Stewardship (where the next gift starts)
Thank within 48 hours (call from a leader for large gifts), honour every agreed
restriction and recognition, send impact reports on the schedule promised, invite
to see results. Donors who feel their gift made a difference give again; restricted
funds spent off-purpose damage trust and may create legal liability.

## Gotchas
- Asking too early, or never asking, are the two classic failures; the second is more common.
- Vague asks ("anything you can do") yield small gifts.
- One gift officer holding all the relationship: involve the CEO/board so it survives staff turnover.
- Planned gifts (bequests) need specialist advice; mention them, don't draft them.
