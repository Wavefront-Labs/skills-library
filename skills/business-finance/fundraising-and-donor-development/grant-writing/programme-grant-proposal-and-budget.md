# Programme grant proposal and budget

**Goal:** a proposal a programme officer can score highly in ten minutes and defend to
their board, from a funder you've checked is actually likely to fund you. (Research
grants to universities follow different rules; not covered here.)

## 1. Research and qualify funders before writing
- Sources: Candid's Foundation Directory (US), 360Giving GrantNav (UK, open data on
  published grants), government grant portals (e.g. Grants.gov), community foundations,
  funders' own past-grant lists and US 990-PF filings (show actual grantees and amounts).
- Qualify on: mission and priority fit (their words), geography, typical grant size
  (ask for something in their usual range), whether they accept unsolicited proposals,
  deadlines, and whether they fund **your type of cost** (some won't fund core costs,
  capital or individuals).
- Fit score each prospect; drop low fits. Twenty tailored proposals beat sixty generic ones.
- If allowed, **call or email the programme officer** with a two-line project summary.
  Their steer saves weeks.

## 2. Structure (follow the funder's form exactly)
When there's no template, a common order:
1. **Summary** (write last): who, what, for whom, how much, outcomes. One paragraph.
2. **Need statement**: evidence of the problem locally (data with sources, beneficiary
   voice), why now, why it isn't already solved. Specific beats dramatic.
3. **Programme description**: activities, who delivers, timeline, how participants are reached.
4. **Outcomes and evaluation**: what changes, measured how, by when (e.g. "70 of 100
   participants improve reading age by ≥1 year, measured by <named assessment> at entry
   and exit"). A **logic model** (inputs → activities → outputs → outcomes → impact) or
   theory of change if they ask.
5. **Organisational capacity**: track record, staff, partners, governance.
6. **Budget and budget narrative**.
7. **Sustainability**: how the work continues after this grant.
Use the funder's vocabulary and priorities verbatim where truthful; reviewers score
against their criteria grid.

## 3. The budget
- Line items: staff (salary × FTE % × months, plus employer on-costs), programme
  supplies, travel, evaluation, equipment, and **indirect/overhead costs**.
- Don't under-claim overheads: chronic underfunding of core costs is a known sector
  problem ("full cost recovery"). Know each funder's indirect-cost policy (some cap at
  e.g. 10-15%; US federal awards use negotiated or de minimis rates, check current rules).
- The **budget narrative** explains each line's basis; every activity in the narrative
  must appear in the budget and vice versa.
- Show other funding sources (match, in-kind) and total project cost.
- Totals must add up across every table; mismatches are a common reason for doubt.

## 4. Writing craft
Plain language, short paragraphs, headings matching the criteria, active voice, no
jargon the reviewer must decode. One real beneficiary story (consented and anonymised)
plus data. Have someone outside the project read it for clarity, and someone else
check every number.

## 5. Submit and after
- Submit at least a day early; portals fail on deadline day.
- Track: funder, ask, deadline, status, decision date, reporting dates.
- **Rejected**: ask for feedback; many funders give it and you may be invited back.
- **Awarded**: put reporting dates in the calendar immediately; read the grant
  agreement terms (restrictions, permitted variances, publicity requirements).
- **Reports**: outcomes against targets (honest about misses and what you learned),
  spend against budget, a story. Reliable reporting is the strongest renewal argument.

## Gotchas
- Asking for 3× the funder's typical grant usually means a fast no.
- Restricted funds must be spent as agreed; get written approval for budget changes.
- Outputs (sessions delivered) aren't outcomes (lives changed); funders know the difference.
- Boilerplate reuse is fine; forgetting to change the funder's name isn't.

## Verify
- [ ] Funder fit checked against priorities, geography, size and cost types.
- [ ] Each section maps to a scoring criterion in the guidelines.
- [ ] Outcomes have a number, a measure and a date.
- [ ] Budget totals reconcile across all tables and match the narrative.
- [ ] Reporting dates in the calendar on award.
