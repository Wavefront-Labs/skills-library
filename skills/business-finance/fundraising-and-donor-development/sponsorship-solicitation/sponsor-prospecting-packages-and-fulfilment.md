# Sponsor prospecting, packages and fulfilment

**Goal:** sponsorship revenue that renews, because the sponsor got measurable business
value. Sponsorship is a **commercial exchange** (money for benefits), unlike a donation;
companies usually fund it from marketing budgets, not philanthropy budgets.

## 1. Inventory what you can actually offer
List your assets and their reach before writing any package:
- Audience: size, demographics, location, profession, how engaged (attendance, email
  open rates, social following, web traffic). Sponsors buy access to *your audience*.
- Visibility: event signage, stage mentions, program/website logos, email features.
- Engagement: speaking slots, booth/exhibit space, product sampling, hospitality
  tickets, employee volunteering days, co-branded content.
- Naming: buildings, rooms, programmes, awards, scholarships (usually longer-term,
  higher value; overlaps with major gifts).
- Data access: be careful; sharing attendee personal data needs consent under privacy laws.

## 2. Prospect by fit
Good prospects: companies who sell to your audience, local employers wanting
recruitment or community visibility, suppliers to your organisation, companies with
CSR themes matching your mission, past sponsors of similar events (check other
organisations' sponsor walls and reports).
Screen out reputational risks with a written **gift acceptance / sponsor policy**
(e.g. sectors you won't accept, conflicts with your mission). Decide before money is offered.

## 3. Design packages, but sell custom
- Tiers (e.g. presenting, gold, silver, supporter) with clear, limited benefits;
  exclusivity (one presenting sponsor, category exclusivity) is what commands top prices.
- Price on value delivered and market comparables, not just on the cost of the event.
- Use tiers as a menu, then **build a custom proposal** after a discovery call:
  "What are you trying to achieve this year? Who do you need to reach? How do you
  measure success?" Then match assets to those goals.

## 4. The pitch and proposal
Short deck or document: audience profile with numbers, the sponsor's objectives restated,
proposed benefits mapped to those objectives, price, deadline for confirmation, and
past sponsor results or testimonials. Approach 6-12 months before an event; corporate
budgets are often set in the prior year (timing varies by company).

## 5. Contract it
A sponsorship agreement covering: fee and payment dates, every benefit with quantity and
deadline, logo and brand-use approvals, exclusivity, cancellation/force majeure, morality
or reputation clauses both ways, term and renewal option.
Tax (uncertain per jurisdiction): in the US, payments where the sponsor gets only
acknowledgment are generally "qualified sponsorship payments", but substantial return
benefits such as advertising with qualitative or comparative claims or calls to action
may create unrelated business income (UBIT). In the UK, sponsorship can be a VATable
supply. Get your accountant to review the benefit structure.

## 6. Fulfilment and stewardship
- A **fulfilment tracker**: every promised benefit, owner, due date, proof (photo,
  screenshot, attendance count).
- Assign one relationship manager per sponsor; check in before, during and after.
- Send a **fulfilment report** within ~30 days: photos, reach numbers, leads or
  impressions, employee participation, thank-you. Then discuss renewal while results
  are fresh.

## Gotchas
- Overselling benefits you can't deliver (e.g. logo on materials already printed).
- Ignoring competitor conflicts between sponsors in the same category.
- Treating sponsors like donors (vague impact talk) instead of business partners (metrics).
- Naming rights without a removal clause can trap you with a damaged name.

## Verify
- [ ] Asset inventory with audience numbers exists.
- [ ] Sponsor acceptance policy approved by the board.
- [ ] Each proposal states the sponsor's own objectives and maps benefits to them.
- [ ] Signed agreement lists every benefit; tracker shows proof of delivery.
- [ ] Fulfilment report sent and renewal conversation held.
