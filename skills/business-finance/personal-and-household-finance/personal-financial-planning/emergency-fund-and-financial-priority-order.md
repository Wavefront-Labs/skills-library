# Emergency fund and the financial priority order

**Goal:** decide where the next dollar of surplus goes, in a defensible order, and size
the emergency fund that protects everything else. Jurisdiction-neutral: account names
differ (401(k)/IRA in the US, super in Australia, workplace pension/ISA in the UK,
RRSP/TFSA in Canada) — check local rules and tax treatment; this is general education,
not personal advice.

## 1. Snapshot first: net worth and cash flow
- **Net worth** = everything owned at realistic sale value − everything owed. List
  each debt with balance, interest rate (APR) and minimum payment.
- **Monthly surplus** from your budget (see
  household-budgeting.zero-based-household-budget). The priority order below allocates
  this surplus; without a surplus, the first job is creating one.
- Repeat the snapshot every 6–12 months; the trend matters more than the number.

## 2. The priority order (a default, not a law)
1. **Starter buffer** — about one month of essential expenses (or a fixed small sum)
   in cash. Stops the next car repair from going on a credit card.
2. **Capture any employer match** on retirement contributions. A 50–100% instant return
   beats almost anything else. (Check vesting rules: some matches are forfeited if you
   leave early.)
3. **High-interest debt** — credit cards, payday loans, buy-now-pay-later in arrears,
   typically anything above ~8–10% APR. See
   personal-debt-management.debt-repayment-strategy.
4. **Full emergency fund** (sizing below).
5. **Insurance gaps** — before investing heavily, confirm you could survive the
   big-loss events (section 4).
6. **Retirement saving** toward a target rate (a common rule of thumb is 10–15% of
   gross income including employer contributions; *uncertain* — appropriate rates vary
   widely by age, pension system and starting point).
7. **Named goals** — house deposit, education, car replacement — each with its own
   account, amount and date.
8. **Moderate-interest debt** (mortgage, low-rate car loans) — overpay only once the
   above are covered; this is partly a preference call.

Steps 3 and 4 are sometimes swapped: someone with unstable income or no family
safety net may sensibly build the full fund first.

## 3. Sizing the emergency fund
Essential monthly expenses (housing, utilities, food, insurance, minimum debt
payments, transport) × months:
- **3 months** — dual stable incomes, renters, good job market.
- **6 months** — single income, dependants, homeowner.
- **9–12 months** — self-employed, commission-based, specialised field with long job
  searches, or health issues.
Keep it **liquid and boring**: a high-interest savings account separate from everyday
spending, not shares or crypto (they fall exactly when jobs disappear). Name the
account "Emergency" — it measurably reduces raids.

What counts as an emergency: job loss, urgent medical or repair costs, unavoidable
travel. Not: sales, holidays, predictable annual bills (those are sinking funds).
Refill it before resuming step 6 after you use it.

## 4. Insurance-needs check
- **Income protection / disability**: your future earnings are usually your largest
  asset. Check what employer or state cover exists, waiting period and benefit period.
- **Life insurance**: needed if someone depends on your income. Rough sizing: debts to
  clear + years of dependants' support + education goals − existing assets. Term cover
  is usually far cheaper than whole-of-life.
- **Health, home/contents, car, liability** per local norms. Raise excesses
  (deductibles) once the emergency fund can absorb them — premiums drop.

## 5. Major-purchase test
Before a big purchase (car, renovation, upgrade): does it delay steps 1–5? Can you pay
cash, or is the financing rate below what the money earns elsewhere? Total cost of
ownership over the holding period, not the sticker price. Wait 48 hours on anything
above a threshold you set (e.g. one week's take-home).

## Gotchas
- Tax wrappers change the order: tax-advantaged retirement accounts can beat paying
  moderate debt; check local contribution caps and early-withdrawal penalties.
- Don't hold a huge emergency fund *and* 20% credit-card debt beyond the starter buffer.
- Inflation erodes idle cash; review the target size yearly.

## Verify
- [ ] Net worth and every debt's rate are written down and dated.
- [ ] Emergency-fund months chosen with a stated reason, held in a separate liquid account.
- [ ] Employer match fully captured.
- [ ] No debt above ~10% APR while investing beyond the match.
- [ ] Income-protection and life cover checked against dependants.
