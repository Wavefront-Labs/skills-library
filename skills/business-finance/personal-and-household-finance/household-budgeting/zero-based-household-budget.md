# Zero-based household budget — give every dollar a job, then keep it honest

**Goal:** a monthly plan where income minus planned spending, saving and debt payments
equals zero, built from what the household *actually* spends, with a review habit that
keeps it true. Method-agnostic: works in a spreadsheet, a budgeting app (YNAB, Actual,
Monarch and similar) or cash envelopes.

## 1. Baseline before you plan (one evening)
1. Export the last **3 months** of statements for every account and card (CSV if the
   bank offers it). One month lies: it misses quarterly bills and has odd one-offs.
2. Tag every transaction into 12–20 categories. Fewer than ~10 hides leaks; more than
   ~25 and nobody maintains it. Typical set: housing, utilities, groceries, eating out,
   transport, insurance, phone/internet, subscriptions, kids, health, personal, gifts,
   debt payments, savings, irregular/annual.
3. Average each category over the 3 months. That is your **baseline**, not your target.
4. List every **irregular expense** from the last 12 months (car registration,
   insurance premiums, school fees, holidays, gifts at holidays, annual software).
   These are the usual reason budgets "fail" in month four.

## 2. Build the plan
Start with **net (take-home) income** you can count on. Variable earners: use the
lowest month of the last 6–12, and treat anything above it as a bonus to assign when it
lands.

Assign in this order:
1. **Fixed essentials** — housing, utilities, insurance, minimum debt payments.
2. **Sinking funds** — annual total of each irregular expense ÷ 12, moved monthly into
   a separate savings account or sub-account. A $1,200 annual insurance bill is $100 a month.
3. **Savings goals** — emergency fund first (see personal-financial-planning), then
   retirement and named goals. "Pay yourself first": automate the transfer for payday.
4. **Variable essentials** — groceries, fuel. Set to the baseline, then trim 5–10% if
   you need to, not 40%. Crash cuts break in week two.
5. **Discretionary** — eating out, hobbies, personal money. Give each adult a no-questions
   personal allowance; it prevents most budget arguments.
6. Whatever is left gets assigned (extra debt, savings) until **unassigned = 0**.

If the plan is negative, cut discretionary first, then question fixed costs (insurance
quotes, phone plans, subscriptions), then income. Rough sanity check only: the
50/30/20 rule (needs/wants/savings) is a heuristic, not a target, and fails in
high-rent cities.

## 3. Run it
- **Timing:** budget per pay cycle if pay is fortnightly and bills cluster; otherwise
  monthly. Note that fortnightly pay gives two "three-paycheque" months a year — plan
  them as bonus months.
- **Weekly 10-minute check-in:** categorise new transactions, compare to plan, move
  money between categories *consciously* if one runs over ("roll with the punches" is
  allowed; pretending is not).
- **Envelope rule for problem categories:** if eating out or groceries keep blowing
  out, switch that one category to a prepaid card or cash.
- Keep a small **buffer** category (1–3% of income) for things you forgot.

## 4. Monthly review (30 minutes, both partners)
1. Actual vs plan by category — note the three biggest variances and why.
2. Adjust next month's plan to reality; repeated overspend means the plan is wrong,
   not that you failed.
3. Check sinking-fund balances against upcoming bills.
4. Record **savings rate** = (savings + extra debt principal) ÷ net income. Track the
   trend, not the single month.

## Gotchas
- **Credit cards:** budget the purchase when it is made, not when the card bill is
  paid, or you double-count or miss spending.
- **Refunds and reimbursements** go back into the category they came from.
- **Subscriptions** hide in annual renewals; search statements for the merchant names
  every quarter.
- Transfers between your own accounts are not spending — exclude them from category totals.
- App bank-sync breaks silently; reconcile balances against the bank monthly.

## Verify
- [ ] Baseline built from ≥3 months of real transactions, not memory.
- [ ] Every irregular annual cost has a monthly sinking-fund line.
- [ ] Planned income − all assignments = 0.
- [ ] Savings transfers are automated on payday.
- [ ] Account balances reconcile to the bank at month end.
- [ ] A dated review note exists for last month with variances and changes.
