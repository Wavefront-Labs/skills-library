# Researching and negotiating a big purchase

**Goal:** for anything above a threshold you set (an appliance, laptop, furniture, a
used car), end up with the right item, at a good total price, from a seller you can
return it to — without spending more time than the saving is worth.

## 1. Define the need before browsing
Write 3–5 **must-haves** (measurable: "fits a 60 cm cavity", "16 GB RAM", "seats 5")
and a few nice-to-haves. Set a budget **ceiling** and a **walk-away** price. Browsing
first lets marketing set your requirements.

## 2. Research (stop when the answer stops changing)
- **Shortlist 2–4 models** from independent testing (consumer-testing organisations,
  specialist reviewers who measure things) rather than star ratings alone.
- **Read reviews critically:** sort by most recent and by 2–3 stars (the useful
  middle); look for repeated failure modes after 6–18 months. Distrust bursts of
  five-star reviews on the same days and reviews that mention a free product.
- **Total cost of ownership:** price + delivery/installation + consumables (ink,
  filters, subscriptions) + running cost (energy rating × your usage) + likely resale.
  A cheaper printer with dear cartridges is the textbook trap.
- **Model-number games:** big retailers sometimes sell near-identical variants under
  retailer-specific model numbers, which blocks easy price comparison. Compare specs
  line by line.

## 3. Timing
- Use a **price-history tracker** (browser extension or site) for online goods: a
  "sale" price that equals the 90-day normal price isn't a sale.
- Typical patterns (*vary by country and category*): end-of-season clearances, new
  model launches discount the outgoing model, major sales events (Black Friday, Boxing
  Day, end of financial year), month/quarter end for dealers chasing targets.
- If you need it now, buy now; waiting six weeks to save 5% on a broken fridge is
  false economy.

## 4. Negotiate as a buyer
Haggling works best with commissioned staff, independent shops, bundles, floor stock
and anything secondhand. It rarely works on small items in big chains — but **price
matching** often does (check the policy and bring the competitor's live listing).
1. **Anchor with evidence:** "Retailer X has it at $Y delivered — can you match or
   beat that?"
2. **Ask open, then stay quiet:** "What's the best you can do on this?" Silence does
   much of the work.
3. **Trade on more than price:** free delivery, installation, removal of the old unit,
   accessories, longer return window, display model at a further discount.
4. **Negotiate the total, not the payment.** Monthly-payment framing hides cost —
   especially on cars and furniture finance.
5. **Be willing to walk.** Leave your number; call-backs are common.
6. Get the agreed price and extras **in writing** on the invoice.

## 5. Decline the traps
- **Extended warranties:** usually poor value — they overlap with manufacturer
  warranty and statutory consumer guarantees (e.g. Australian Consumer Law, UK
  Consumer Rights Act, EU 2-year legal guarantee — *check local scope*). Self-insure
  with your emergency fund unless the item is fragile, expensive to repair and used hard.
- **Interest-free / buy-now-pay-later:** fine only if paid in full before the period
  ends; deferred-interest deals may charge backdated interest.
- **Add-on bundles** (cleaning kits, "protection" sprays, gap insurance at dealer markup).
- **Countdown timers and "only 2 left"** pressure.

## 6. After purchase
Keep receipt, serial number and warranty terms in one folder. Inspect and test inside
the return window. Pay by credit card for big items where available: card chargeback
and some statutory protections can help if the seller fails (*rules differ by card
network and country*).

## Verify
- [ ] Written must-haves, ceiling and walk-away price before shopping.
- [ ] Shortlist compared on total cost of ownership, not sticker price.
- [ ] Price checked against history and at least two competitors.
- [ ] At least one ask made (price, match or extras) before paying.
- [ ] Extended warranty decision made against statutory rights, not sales pressure.
- [ ] Return window date noted; item tested within it.
