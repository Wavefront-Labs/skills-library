# Beneficiary designations and estate organising — the household side

**Goal:** make sure what you own goes where you intend, quickly and with minimal
conflict, by understanding which assets the will actually controls and getting the
paperwork into a state your executor can use. This is the planning-and-organising craft
for a household; wills, trusts and tax are jurisdiction-specific legal work — have a
lawyer draft or review documents. Uncertain details are flagged.

## 1. Inventory: what exists and how it's held
Build one table: asset, approximate value, **how it's titled** (sole name, joint,
nominated beneficiary, held in trust, company), and institution/contact. Include debts,
insurance policies, retirement/pension accounts, business interests, vehicles,
digital accounts (email, photos, crypto, domain names) and sentimental items.

## 2. The key insight: many assets bypass the will
Common categories that usually pass **outside** the will (check locally):
- **Retirement/pension accounts and life insurance** with a beneficiary designation or
  nomination. In some systems (e.g. Australian super) the trustee may have discretion
  unless the nomination is *binding* and current — binding nominations can lapse after a
  fixed period in some funds. *Verify with each fund.*
- **Jointly owned property held as joint tenants** (right of survivorship) passes to the
  surviving owner automatically; tenants-in-common shares go through the estate.
- **Payable-on-death / transfer-on-death** bank and brokerage registrations (where offered).
- **Assets in a trust or company** follow the trust deed or company constitution.
The classic failure: a will divides everything equally among three children, but the
biggest asset is a pension still naming an ex-spouse. **The designation wins.**

## 3. Make the allocation decisions
- **Who gets what:** equal vs equitable (a child who was a carer; earlier gifts already
  made; a beneficiary with disabilities whose means-tested benefits could be lost on a
  direct inheritance — a specialist trust may be needed).
- **Contingent beneficiaries:** name a backup for every designation, or the asset may
  fall into the estate or intestacy rules.
- **Minors:** they generally can't receive assets directly; plan a guardian (for the
  children) and a trustee or custodian (for the money), and an age for control.
- **Executor:** someone organised, trusted and likely to outlive you; a backup; consider
  a professional if the family is fractious or the estate complex.
- **Powers of attorney** (financial and medical) for incapacity — the will does nothing
  while you're alive.

## 4. Gifting during life
Give while living when it helps now (house deposit, education) and you can afford it
without jeopardising retirement. Watch: gift-tax or inheritance-tax thresholds and
look-back periods (e.g. UK seven-year rule on lifetime gifts; US annual exclusion and
lifetime exemption — *figures change yearly, check current values*), means-tested
aged-care or pension rules that count recent gifts, and fairness between children
(record gifts and whether they're advances on inheritance).

## 5. The document pack
One place (physical folder plus a secured digital copy) with: will and where the
original is held, powers of attorney, the asset inventory, copies of beneficiary
nominations with dates, insurance policies, a contact list (lawyer, accountant,
adviser), password-manager emergency access or instructions, and a **letter of wishes**
for sentimental items and funeral preferences. Tell the executor where it is.

## 6. Review triggers
Marriage, divorce or separation (in some places these revoke or alter a will
automatically — *check*), birth or death in the family, moving country or state, a
major asset purchase or sale, and every 3–5 years regardless.

## Gotchas
- Updating the will doesn't update beneficiary designations — do each one separately
  with each institution and keep the confirmation.
- Adding a child as joint owner of your account "for convenience" can make it theirs
  outright, expose it to their creditors, and override the will.
- Home-made wills fail on witnessing formalities more than on content.

## Verify
- [ ] Inventory lists title/ownership type for each asset.
- [ ] Every designation checked, dated, consistent with the will, with a contingent named.
- [ ] Executor, guardian and attorneys named, with backups, and they've agreed.
- [ ] Documents drafted or reviewed by a qualified local lawyer.
- [ ] Executor knows where the document pack is.
