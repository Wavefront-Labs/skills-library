# Two-axis scenarios, wind-tunnelling and signposts

**Goal:** stress-test a strategy against several genuinely different plausible
futures, so you find no-regret moves, hedges and early warnings, instead of betting
everything on one forecast. Scenarios are **not predictions** and are not "best /
base / worst" cases of one variable.

## 1. Set the focal question and horizon
A decision-linked question: "Should we commit €200M to a new plant in region X by 2028?"
Horizon long enough for real change (often 5-15 years; shorter for fast-moving sectors).

## 2. Map driving forces
Brainstorm with a diverse group (not only strategy staff; include outsiders) using
**PESTLE/STEEP**: political, economic, social, technological, legal, environmental.
Aim for 30-60 forces. Sort each into:
- **Predetermined elements**: high impact, fairly certain (demographics already in
  motion, signed regulation). These appear in *every* scenario.
- **Critical uncertainties**: high impact on the focal question, high uncertainty.
Plot all forces on impact × uncertainty; take the top-right cluster.

## 3. Choose two axes
Pick two critical uncertainties that are **independent of each other** and each with
a clear range (e.g. "carbon price: low/patchy ↔ high/global"; "customer demand shifts
to service models: slow ↔ fast"). Test combinations; choose the pair yielding four
distinct, decision-relevant worlds. If two candidates are correlated, the quadrants
collapse into two.

## 4. Write the four scenarios
For each quadrant: a memorable name, a narrative of how we got there from today
(events in sequence), what the world looks like at the horizon for customers,
competitors, suppliers, regulators, and key quantified indicators. 1-2 pages each.
Plausibility check: every scenario must be internally consistent and defensible to a
sceptic; avoid making one obviously "good" and one "doom". Include predetermined
elements in all four.
(Alternatives to the 2×2: deductive matrices with more variables, or inductive
event-based storylines. The 2×2 "intuitive logics" approach is the most common in
business; it's not the only valid one.)

## 5. Wind-tunnel the strategy
For each scenario ask:
- How does our current strategy perform? Which assumptions break?
- What would we do differently if we knew this scenario was coming?
Then classify options:
- **No-regret moves**: good in all scenarios: do now.
- **Options/hedges**: small investments preserving the ability to act (pilots, land
  options, partnerships).
- **Big bets**: only good in one or two scenarios; make explicitly with eyes open.
- **Contingent plans**: pre-agreed responses triggered by signposts.

## 6. Signposts and monitoring
For each scenario, list 3-6 **leading indicators** (observable, measurable, early) that
would show it unfolding: e.g. a regulatory consultation published, a price threshold
crossed, a competitor's capex announcement. Assign an owner and a review cadence
(quarterly dashboard) and tie each to the contingent plan it triggers.

## 7. Workshop logistics
Typically 2-3 workshops: drivers & axes (1 day), scenario building (1 day), implications
(half to 1 day). Facilitator neutral to the outcome; pre-read interviews with leaders
("what keeps you up at night about 2035?") surface uncertainties early.

## Gotchas
- Choosing axes that are really the same uncertainty.
- Scenarios as an intellectual exercise disconnected from a decision: no one uses them.
- Leaders privately picking a favourite scenario and planning only for it.
- Not refreshing: revisit scenarios when signposts move or every 1-2 years.

## Verify
- [ ] Focal question tied to a real decision and horizon.
- [ ] Predetermined elements separated from critical uncertainties.
- [ ] Two axes are independent; four scenarios are distinct and plausible.
- [ ] Strategy options classified as no-regret, hedge, bet, or contingent.
- [ ] Signposts have owners, thresholds and linked actions.
