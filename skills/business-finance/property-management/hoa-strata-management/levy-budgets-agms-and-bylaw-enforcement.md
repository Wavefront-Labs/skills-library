# Levy budgets, AGMs and bylaw enforcement

**Goal:** an association whose money is adequate and transparent, whose meetings are
valid, and whose rules are applied fairly, so owners trust the committee and decisions
survive challenge. The manager administers; the **board/committee decides**. Governing
law differs a lot (US state HOA/condo acts, Canadian provincial strata acts, Australian
state strata schemes, UK leasehold/commonhold); read your statute and the governing
documents (declaration/CC&Rs, bylaws, rules) first.

## 1. The annual budget and levies
1. **Operating budget**: insurance (often the biggest and fastest-rising line), common
   utilities, cleaning, landscaping, repairs, management fee, audit/accounting, legal,
   contingency. Base it on 2-3 years of actuals plus known contract changes.
2. **Reserve / sinking fund**: contributions based on a **reserve study** (component
   list with remaining useful life and replacement cost: roof, lifts, paving, painting,
   plant). Commonly updated every 3-5 years with an annual review; several jurisdictions
   now mandate studies or depreciation reports for some buildings. Check yours.
3. Allocate levies per the governing documents (unit entitlement/lot entitlement,
   equal shares, or by size).
4. Board approves; distribute to owners within the required notice period, and adopt at
   the meeting if the statute requires owner approval.
Underfunded reserves lead to **special assessments** that owners can't afford, delayed
repairs, and in extreme cases unsafe buildings. Explain this plainly when the board is
tempted to keep levies flat.

## 2. Collections
Clear due dates, automated reminders, late fees only as the documents/statute allow,
a written collections policy applied identically, payment plans recorded in writing,
and escalation (liens, debt recovery) only after the statutory notices. Report arrears
to the board monthly.

## 3. Meetings: AGM and board
- **Notice**: deliver to every owner in the required form and period (statute or bylaws
  set this, e.g. a minimum number of days) with the agenda, budget, financial statements,
  proposed motions and proxy forms.
- **Quorum** and **voting**: check thresholds (ordinary vs special/supermajority
  resolutions for things like bylaw changes or major spending). Verify proxies and
  owner eligibility (some statutes bar voting for owners in arrears).
- **Minutes**: decisions, vote counts, who moved/seconded, conflicts declared. Circulate
  within the required time; keep them permanently.
- Board meetings: open or closed per law; record decisions on contracts, enforcement
  and spending. Directors with a conflict (e.g. a relative's contracting firm) must
  declare and abstain.
- Invalid notice or quorum can void resolutions, including levies.

## 4. Bylaw enforcement: consistent and documented
1. Complaint or observation logged with date, evidence (photos), and rule cited.
2. Friendly first contact (many issues end here).
3. Formal written notice: the rule, the breach, a cure period, the owner's right to be
   heard (many statutes require a hearing before fines).
4. Hearing before the board if requested; decision in writing.
5. Fines or other remedies only as authorised, applied the same way to everyone,
   including board members. Escalate to tribunal/court only on legal advice.
Selective enforcement can make rules unenforceable and creates claims of discrimination.
Accessibility requests (e.g. reasonable accommodation for an assistance animal) override
some rules under disability law.

## 5. Common-property upkeep
Maintenance plan aligned with the reserve study; contractors with insurance; tender
large works with at least three quotes; distinguish common property from owner-responsibility
items using the plan/declaration (balconies, windows and pipes are frequent disputes).
Keep insurance adequate (rebuild valuations periodically) and claims documented.

## Gotchas
- Spending reserve funds on operating costs (often prohibited or restricted).
- Board acting beyond its authority without an owner vote.
- Missing statutory disclosures to buyers (resale certificates/estoppel letters).

## Verify
- [ ] Budget includes reserve contributions tied to a current reserve study.
- [ ] Meeting notices, quorum and votes meet statute and bylaws; minutes filed.
- [ ] Enforcement log shows notice, hearing opportunity and consistent fines.
- [ ] Arrears and reserve balance reported to the board monthly.
- [ ] Conflicts of interest declared and recorded.
