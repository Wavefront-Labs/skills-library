# Service-charge budget, occupier relations and the asset plan

**Goal:** a building that runs safely within budget, occupiers who renew, and an owner
whose business plan for the asset is delivered. You act for the **owner**, while being
the occupiers' main day-to-day contact; that dual role is the job.

## 1. Know the leases before the building
Abstract every lease (see lease-administration): service-charge clause (what's
recoverable, apportionment method, caps, exclusions, sinking fund rights, year end,
reconciliation deadline), repair obligations, insurance, landlord's services, access.
**What the lease says decides what you can recover**, not what seems fair.

## 2. Service-charge / CAM budget
1. Start from last year's actuals and the planned preventive maintenance (PPM) schedule.
2. Cost heads: cleaning, security, M&E maintenance, utilities to common areas, lift
   contracts, insurance (if recoverable), management fee, compliance testing, reserves.
3. **Apportion** per lease: floor area (net internal/rentable), weighted by use, or
   fixed percentages. Note US "CAM" leases may include gross-up provisions for occupancy
   and caps on controllable expenses.
4. Check recoverability line by line: improvements vs repairs is the classic dispute;
   capital upgrades are usually not recoverable unless the lease says so.
5. Issue budget to occupiers before the year starts (often with a covering explanation
   of material changes); bill quarterly or monthly on account.
6. Year end: **reconcile** actual vs budget, issue certified statements within the
   lease deadline, credit or demand balances. In England & Wales the RICS professional
   statement sets expected practice (transparency, timely reconciliation); other markets
   have their own norms.
Landlord pays the share for vacant units ("void costs"); budget for it.

## 3. Occupier relations
- Welcome pack: contacts, emergency procedures, access, deliveries, rules.
- Regular check-ins (e.g. quarterly with each occupier's facilities contact; annually
  with decision-makers ahead of lease events). Log issues and resolution times.
- Communicate works in advance (noise, shutdowns) and hold occupier meetings for
  multi-let buildings, especially on budget and major projects.
- Watch for **risk signals**: arrears, reduced headcount, sublet enquiries, "we're
  reviewing our space". Feed them to the owner's asset manager early.

## 4. Facilities and compliance oversight
- Appoint and manage FM contractors on clear specifications and KPIs (response times,
  PPM completion %). Audit monthly.
- Statutory compliance calendar (examples; jurisdiction-specific): fire risk assessment
  and alarm/emergency lighting testing, lift inspections, water hygiene/legionella,
  electrical installation testing, gas safety, asbestos register and management plan,
  accessibility, energy ratings (e.g. UK MEES minimum EPC for letting).
- Keep a building **H&S file** and contractor permit-to-work system.

## 5. Delivering the asset plan
The owner's asset manager sets strategy (hold/refurbish/re-let/sell); you deliver:
- Annual **business plan**: occupancy, lease events, capex programme (lifecycle
  replacement: roof, plant, lifts), ESG upgrades, NOI target.
- Monthly/quarterly owner report: rent collected and arrears, service charge vs budget,
  compliance status, occupier issues, leasing pipeline, capex progress, risks.
- Separate **landlord costs** (capex, void costs, non-recoverables) from service charge
  clearly in both budgeting and reporting.

## Gotchas
- Recovering costs the lease doesn't permit invites disputes and refunds with interest.
- Late reconciliations erode trust and can bar recovery under some leases.
- Mixing sinking-fund money with operating cash; hold it as the lease and local rules require.
- Uncertain: market conventions (CAM vs service charge, gross vs net leases) differ by
  country; confirm the local standard before applying any template.

## Verify
- [ ] Every cost head checked against each lease's recoverability clause.
- [ ] Budget issued before year start; reconciliation by the lease deadline.
- [ ] Compliance calendar with last/next test dates and certificates on file.
- [ ] Occupier issue log shows response and resolution times.
- [ ] Owner report separates recoverable and landlord costs.
