# Arrears, reconciliation, deposits and owner statements

**Goal:** every pound/dollar received is in the right ledger, every owner and occupier
can see why they owe or are owed what they are, and client money is never mixed with
the agent's own. Errors here become complaints, regulator findings or lost claims.

## 1. Ledgers and demands
- One **tenant ledger** per tenancy/lease: charges (rent, service charge, insurance,
  utilities recharges) and receipts, running balance.
- Raise demands on the lease schedule (e.g. quarter days in UK commercial leases;
  monthly in most residential). Demands must contain any statutorily required content
  (e.g. landlord name and address in England & Wales; residential service-charge
  demands need the prescribed summary of tenants' rights). A non-compliant demand may
  mean the sum isn't legally due yet.
- Allocate receipts to specific charges (oldest first unless the payer specifies);
  unallocated cash is the most common ledger mess.

## 2. Bank reconciliation, daily or weekly
Match bank lines to ledger receipts; investigate unidentified receipts within days
(contact payers, check references). Reconcile client accounts **at least monthly**
(many regulators/schemes require it) and have a second person review and sign.

## 3. Arrears process (staged, consistent, documented)
| Day | Action |
|---|---|
| 1-3 | Friendly reminder (automated email/SMS) |
| 7 | Personal call; find the reason; offer a payment plan if hardship |
| 14 | Formal letter: amount, breakdown, consequences, contact for help |
| 21-30 | Notify owner/guarantor; final written demand |
| 30+ | Legal route per lease and law, on owner's instruction |
Residential: follow statutory pre-action steps exactly (notice forms, periods, and in
some jurisdictions pre-action protocols requiring engagement before court). Commercial:
remedies may include forfeiture, rent deposit draw-down, guarantor claims or
statutory recovery procedures; take legal advice. Record every contact. Hardship
signposting (debt advice services) is good practice and in some places expected.
Report arrears ageing (0-30, 31-60, 61-90, 90+) to owners monthly.

## 4. Service-charge year-end reconciliation
1. Close the year's actual expenditure per cost head; accrue unpaid invoices.
2. Compare to budget; explain material variances in writing.
3. Apportion actuals to each occupier per the lease method; subtract on-account payments.
4. Produce certified statements (accountant/surveyor certification where the lease
   requires) by the lease deadline.
5. Issue **balancing charges or credits**; credits are refunded or carried forward as
   the lease says.
6. Keep invoices available for inspection; occupiers often have statutory or lease
   rights to inspect supporting documents.
Residential leasehold in England & Wales: costs must be reasonable, and major works or
long-term agreements over thresholds need statutory consultation first or recovery
gets capped. Check equivalents elsewhere.

## 5. Deposits
- Hold per local law: protection scheme registration and prescribed information within
  the deadline (England & Wales residential), separate trust accounts and interest rules
  (many US states), caps on amount (several jurisdictions).
- At end of tenancy: compare check-out to check-in inventory; deductions only for
  damage beyond fair wear and tear, unpaid rent or as the lease allows, itemised with
  evidence; return within the statutory period. Use the scheme's dispute service if contested.

## 6. Owner statements
Monthly (or as contracted): rent due and received per unit, arrears, expenses paid with
invoices attached, management fees, reserves/float held, net remitted, and tax
withholding where applicable (e.g. UK Non-Resident Landlord Scheme). Pay owners on a
fixed date; year-end summary for their tax return.

## Gotchas
- Client money in the agent's office account (a serious breach in most regimes).
- Fees deducted before they're due under the management agreement.
- Late service-charge reconciliation can bar recovery under some leases/statutes.

## Verify
- [ ] Every receipt allocated; no unidentified cash older than two weeks.
- [ ] Client account reconciled and signed off monthly by two people.
- [ ] Arrears actions logged per stage with dates.
- [ ] Year-end statements issued by the deadline with variance notes.
- [ ] Deposits protected/held lawfully and returned within the statutory period.
