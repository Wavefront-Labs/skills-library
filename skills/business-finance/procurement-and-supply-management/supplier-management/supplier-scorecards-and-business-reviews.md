# Supplier scorecards and quarterly business reviews

**Goal:** manage key suppliers after contract award with objective performance data,
regular reviews that produce improvement actions, and early warning of supply risk.

## 1. Segment first
Not every supplier needs a scorecard. Typical segmentation:
- **Strategic / critical** (high spend or high supply risk): full scorecard, quarterly
  business review (QBR), executive sponsor, risk monitoring.
- **Important:** lighter scorecard, semi-annual review.
- **Transactional:** exception-based management only.
Usually the top 10–20 suppliers get the full treatment.

## 2. Design the scorecard
- 5–8 KPIs, each with a definition, data source, target and weight. Example:

| KPI | Definition | Target | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| On-time in-full (OTIF) | Order lines delivered complete by promised date ÷ all lines | ≥ 95% | 30% |
| Quality | Defective PPM or % rejects at goods receipt | ≤ 500 PPM | 25% |
| SLA attainment (services) | SLAs met ÷ SLAs measured | 100% | — |
| Responsiveness | Median hours to resolve tickets/queries | ≤ 24 h | 15% |
| Commercial | Invoice accuracy; cost-reduction commitments delivered | ≥ 98% | 15% |
| Innovation / ESG | Agreed initiatives delivered; ESG data supplied | qualitative 1–5 | 15% |
- Use **your system data** (ERP receipts, QA records, ticketing) not supplier
  self-reporting where possible. Agree definitions with the supplier upfront — disputes
  about the number waste reviews.
- Mix lagging metrics with a leading one or two (e.g. capacity utilisation,
  forecast accuracy shared).
- Two-way: include a "customer score" — how well *you* forecast, pay on time, give
  clear specs. Suppliers often fail because buyers are hard to serve.

## 3. Run the QBR
Agenda (60–90 min):
1. Scorecard review — trends, not just this quarter.
2. Root cause on misses (supplier's analysis, e.g. 5-whys or 8D for quality).
3. Open actions from last review.
4. Upcoming demand/forecast and changes on both sides.
5. Improvement and innovation ideas; cost-reduction pipeline.
6. Risk topics (capacity, financial health, sub-tier issues).
Output: action list with owners and dates; send within 48 hours.

## 4. Escalation ladder for persistent underperformance
1. Corrective action plan (CAP) with dated milestones.
2. Increased review frequency; executive escalation.
3. Contract remedies — service credits, withheld volume for new business.
4. Dual-sourcing / transition planning.
5. Termination per contract.
Document each step; remedies you never invoke tend to be treated as waived in practice.

## 5. Supply-risk monitoring
For critical suppliers: financial health (credit scores, late filings), single-site
dependence, geopolitical/climate exposure, cyber posture, key sub-tier suppliers,
news alerts. Keep a risk register with mitigations (safety stock, alternate source,
business-continuity plan evidence).

## Gotchas
- Scorecards nobody discusses — data without a review cadence changes nothing.
- Weighting everything equally hides the KPI that matters most.
- All-green scorecards with unhappy users: check the KPI reflects what users experience.
- Supplier development (helping them improve) is often cheaper than switching.

## Verify
- [ ] Suppliers segmented; full scorecards only for strategic/critical ones.
- [ ] Each KPI has definition, source, target and weight agreed with the supplier.
- [ ] QBRs held on schedule with actions circulated within 48 h.
- [ ] Underperformers have a dated CAP and a documented escalation path.
- [ ] Critical suppliers have current risk assessments and mitigations.
