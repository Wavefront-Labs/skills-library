# Planning and running a buyer-side purchase negotiation

**Goal:** negotiate a supply agreement that gets the right price, payment terms,
service levels and risk allocation — prepared well enough that you know when to walk
away and what you'll trade.

## 1. Prepare (most of the result is decided here)
- **Objectives per issue:** for each negotiable term write your **target** (realistic
  best), **opening** (ambitious but defensible) and **walk-away** (limit).
- **BATNA:** your best alternative if no deal — another bidder, incumbent extension,
  in-sourcing, delay. Improve it before you negotiate; a weak BATNA can't be hidden for long.
- **Estimate theirs:** their alternatives, capacity utilisation, quarter-end
  pressure, how much they need this reference customer. Their likely walk-away
  defines the zone of possible agreement (ZOPA).
- **Fact base:** should-cost model or cost breakdown (materials, labour, overhead,
  margin), competitive bids, market indices, benchmark rates.
- **Tradeables list:** things cheap for you but valuable to them (longer term, volume
  commitment, faster payment, reference, forecast visibility) and vice versa.
- **Team roles:** lead negotiator (only person who concedes), note-taker, technical
  expert. Agree internally who can approve going past walk-away — no one in the room.

## 2. The issues to cover
- **Price** and its mechanics: unit price, volume tiers/rebates, **price adjustment**
  (index-linked, capped, review dates), currency.
- **Payment terms:** days, early-payment discount, milestones vs arrears. (Paying
  suppliers too slowly can breach prompt-payment rules in some jurisdictions and
  hurts small suppliers.)
- **Service levels and remedies:** measurable SLAs, measurement method, service
  credits, termination for persistent failure.
- **Risk allocation:** liability cap (often a multiple of annual fees) and
  exclusions, indemnities (IP, data, injury), insurance, warranties, data protection.
- **Exit:** term, renewal, termination for convenience, transition assistance.
Agree with legal which clauses are non-negotiable *before* the meeting.

## 3. In the room
- Open with your package, anchored by the fact base ("benchmark rates for this role are X").
- **Trade, never give:** every concession is conditional — "if we commit to 3 years,
  can you hold price with a CPI cap?" Use "if… then…" phrasing.
- Negotiate the **whole package**; avoid closing issues one by one (salami-slicing
  leaves nothing to trade at the end).
- Silence after an offer; let them fill it.
- Summarise agreements in writing at the end of each session.

## 4. Close and hand over
- Confirm all terms in a written summary; legal turns it into contract language.
- Check the final draft against the agreed summary — drafting drift is common.
- Hand a negotiation record (why each term landed where it did) to the contract
  manager.

## Gotchas
- **Revealing budget** early anchors the price at your budget.
- **Deadline pressure** is yours if your contract is expiring: start 6–9 months before
  expiry for significant contracts. *(Rule of thumb.)*
- Winning price but losing on liability or exit terms — often worth more.
- Over-squeezing a strategic supplier until they deprioritise you.
- Unbalanced relationships: if you're a small customer, lean on standard terms and
  competition rather than bespoke demands.

## Verify
- [ ] Target, opening and walk-away recorded for every key term.
- [ ] BATNA defined and actively improved; supplier's position estimated.
- [ ] Tradeables list prepared; all concessions were conditional.
- [ ] Liability, indemnity, SLA remedies and exit agreed, not just price.
- [ ] Final contract checked against the written negotiation summary.
