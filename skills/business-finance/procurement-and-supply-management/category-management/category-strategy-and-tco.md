# Category strategy and total-cost-of-ownership analysis

**Goal:** manage a spend category (e.g. IT hardware, facilities services, packaging)
as a multi-year portfolio — understanding demand, market and total cost — instead of
buying it one purchase order at a time.

## 1. Category profile (the fact base)
- **Spend cube:** 12–24 months of spend by supplier, business unit, sub-category,
  region. Clean supplier names (parent/child) and classify consistently (e.g. UNSPSC
  or your own taxonomy). Expect ~10–20% of spend to be miscoded on first pass.
- **Demand:** who buys, how much, why, specifications used, forecast growth.
- **Contracts:** expiry dates, terms, coverage — share of spend on contract vs off.
- **Stakeholder needs and pain points** from interviews with the main budget holders.

## 2. Supply market analysis
- Market structure (number of suppliers, concentration), cost drivers (raw materials,
  labour, energy, FX), capacity, trends, innovation, risk (single sources, geography).
- **Porter's five forces** from the buyer's side tells you your leverage.
- **Kraljic matrix** — position sub-categories by profit impact × supply risk:
  - *Leverage* (high impact, low risk) → compete hard, tender, consolidate.
  - *Strategic* (high, high) → partnerships, long-term agreements, joint development.
  - *Bottleneck* (low, high) → secure supply, find alternatives, buffer stock.
  - *Non-critical* (low, low) → simplify: catalogues, p-cards, automation.

## 3. Total cost of ownership
TCO = acquisition + operating + support/maintenance + downtime/risk + end-of-life − residual.
Worked example, one laptop over 3 years:

| | Supplier A | Supplier B |
|---|---|---|
| Purchase price | 900 | 800 |
| Extended warranty | included | 150 |
| Power (3 yrs) | 90 | 120 |
| Support/repairs (3 yrs) | 120 | 210 |
| Residual value | −100 | −50 |
| **TCO** | **1,010** | **1,230** |
Cheapest price, most expensive to own. Get cost inputs from real data (helpdesk
tickets, energy specs, finance's depreciation and disposal records) and state
assumptions; run sensitivity on the uncertain ones.

## 4. Strategy and plan
- **Demand levers:** standardise specifications, cut variants, change policy
  (e.g. 4-year refresh instead of 3), reduce consumption. Often bigger than price levers.
- **Supply levers:** consolidate suppliers, tender, renegotiate, change commercial
  model (rent vs buy, outcome-based), develop new suppliers, partner.
- Pick levers per Kraljic segment; quantify savings and risk impact per initiative.
- Write a **category plan** (one page summary): objectives, KPIs, initiatives with
  owners and timeline, aligned to contract expiry dates (the windows where you can act).
- Get budget-holder sign-off — category strategies without stakeholder ownership fail.

## 5. Review cycle
Quarterly: spend under management, savings tracking (agreed with finance on how
savings are measured — avoided cost vs budget reduction), supplier performance,
market changes. Refresh the full strategy every 1–3 years.

## Gotchas
- **Savings that never hit the budget:** price savings with volume growth; agree the
  measurement method with finance up front.
- Treating a strategic category as leverage and tendering away a key partner.
- Ignoring maverick (off-contract) spend: the best contract saves nothing if nobody uses it.
