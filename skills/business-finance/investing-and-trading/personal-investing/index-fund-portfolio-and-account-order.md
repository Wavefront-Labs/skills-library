# Index fund portfolio and the order of accounts

**Goal:** a simple, low-cost, diversified portfolio in the most tax-efficient accounts
available to you, set up so it runs automatically and survives your own emotions.
General education, not personal advice — account names, limits and tax rules
**depend on your country and change yearly**; check the current official limits.

## 1. Before investing
- **High-interest debt first.** Paying off a 20% credit card is a guaranteed 20% return.
- **Emergency fund:** commonly 3–6 months of essential expenses in an easy-access
  savings account. Money you need within ~3–5 years shouldn't be in shares.
- Know your goal and horizon: retirement in 30 years and a house deposit in 3 are
  different portfolios.

## 2. Order of accounts (typical priority)
1. **Employer retirement plan up to the full match** — free money (US 401(k) match,
   UK workplace pension employer contribution, Australian super contributions).
2. **Tax-advantaged personal accounts:** US IRA/Roth IRA, UK ISA/SIPP, Canada
   TFSA/RRSP, and equivalents. Choose between tax-now-relief (traditional) and
   tax-free-later (Roth-type) based on whether you expect a higher tax rate now or
   in retirement.
3. More into the workplace plan up to its limit, if its funds are low-cost.
4. **Taxable brokerage account** for the rest.

## 3. Choose the funds
- **Broad index funds or ETFs**: a global (or home + international) equity fund and a
  bond fund. One-fund options: a **target-date** or balanced/lifestrategy fund that
  rebalances for you.
- **Costs matter more than almost anything you control:** look at the expense ratio
  / TER (broad index funds are often well under 0.2% a year (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)), platform fees, and
  trading costs. A 1% annual fee compounds into a large share of your final pot over
  decades (computed: at 5% gross for 30 years, a 1% fee leaves 75% of the fee-free pot, a 0.2% fee 94%).
- Equity/bond split: a common rule is more equity the longer the horizon and the
  steadier your income; the real test is whether you'd hold through a 30–50% fall in
  the equity part without selling.
- Prefer accumulating (dividends reinvested) funds in tax wrappers where available.

## 4. Choose a broker/platform
Regulated in your country with investor protection (e.g. SIPC in the US, FSCS in
the UK — coverage limits differ (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)), low fees for your account size, offers the accounts
and funds above, fractional shares or fund investing for small amounts.

## 5. Automate
- Monthly automatic contribution the day after payday (**dollar-cost averaging**).
  Lump sums historically did better more often than not, but drip-feeding is fine if
  it's what gets you invested.
- Rebalance once a year, or when allocation drifts by more than ~5 points; use new
  contributions to do it.

## Behavioural traps
- **Selling after a crash** — the most expensive mistake; write your plan down now.
- **Chasing last year's winner** (fund, sector, coin).
- **Checking daily** — increases the urge to act.
- **Overconfidence** from a few good stock picks; keep "fun money" under ~5–10%.
- **Home bias** — your country is a slice of the world market.
- **Tips and guaranteed returns** from strangers or social media are a scam signal.

## Gotchas
- Contribution limits and deadlines for tax wrappers are annual; missed allowances
  usually don't carry forward (some do — check).
- Over-contributing to a tax wrapper can trigger penalties.
- Selling in a taxable account realises gains; know the local rules on wash sales /
  bed-and-breakfasting.

## Verify
- [ ] High-interest debt cleared and an emergency fund in place.
- [ ] Getting the full employer match.
- [ ] Tax-advantaged allowances used before the taxable account.
- [ ] Total annual cost (fund + platform) known, in money terms.
- [ ] Contributions automated; a written rebalancing and "don't sell in a crash" plan.
