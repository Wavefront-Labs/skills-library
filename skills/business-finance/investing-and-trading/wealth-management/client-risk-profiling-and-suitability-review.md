# Client risk profiling and suitability review

**Goal:** recommend a portfolio you can show is suitable — matched to the client's
goals, finances and attitude to risk — and keep it suitable as their life changes.
Regulatory regimes differ (US Reg BI / fiduciary duty, EU/UK MiFID II suitability,
UK Consumer Duty, other national rules); this is the common practice, **your firm's
compliance manual governs**.

## 1. Fact-find (know your client)
- **Goals:** each with amount, date and priority (retire at 62 on X a year; school
  fees from 2031; leave the house to children).
- **Financial position:** income, expenses, assets, debts, pensions, insurance,
  emergency fund, expected inheritances, business interests.
- **Tax position** and residency; account types available.
- **Knowledge and experience** with investments.
- **Circumstances that create vulnerability:** health, bereavement, cognitive
  decline, low financial resilience — these change how you communicate and check
  understanding.
- Verify identity and source of wealth per anti-money-laundering rules.

## 2. Three dimensions of risk — profile all three
1. **Risk tolerance (attitude):** the client's willingness to accept volatility,
   from a validated psychometric questionnaire plus conversation. Ask how they
   behaved in 2008, 2020 or 2022, not only hypotheticals.
2. **Risk capacity (ability):** how much loss they can absorb without damaging their
   goals or lifestyle — horizon, income security, other assets. A cautious
   millionaire may have high capacity; an adventurous 64-year-old with no other
   savings has little.
3. **Risk required (need):** the return needed to reach the goals. If it exceeds what
   tolerance and capacity allow, the conversation is about goals, savings or timing
   — not about more risk.
The recommended risk level is generally the **lowest** of tolerance and capacity
that still meets the need, or a documented discussion of the shortfall.

## 3. Map to a portfolio
- Use the firm's model portfolios or risk bands; record expected volatility and a
  plausible worst-year loss in currency, not just percent ("in a bad year this could
  fall by about 80,000").
- **Tax-aware structuring:** use tax-advantaged accounts first; **asset location** —
  hold tax-inefficient assets (bonds, high-yield) in sheltered accounts where rules
  allow; harvest losses where permitted; watch wash-sale-type rules.
- Liquidity buckets: near-term spending (1–3 years) in cash/short bonds.
- Costs: total cost (product + advice + platform) shown in money terms.

## 4. Document suitability
The suitability report links each recommendation to the client's stated goals,
risk profile and circumstances, explains alternatives considered, costs and risks,
and is written so the client can understand it. "Client is balanced, so balanced
portfolio" is not enough.

## 5. Annual (or event-driven) review
- Re-confirm circumstances: job, health, family, goals, need for income.
- Re-run risk profile if anything material changed or every few years.
- Performance vs the agreed benchmark and goal progress (are they on track?).
- Rebalance; check fees and tax position; refresh the plan.
- Record the review even if "no change".

## Gotchas
- **Questionnaire-only profiling** — scores swing with recent market moods.
- **Joint clients** with different risk attitudes: profile both, agree explicitly.
- **Concentrated positions** (employer stock) count toward risk even if held outside.
- **Drawdown/decumulation** clients face sequence-of-returns risk; capacity for loss
  shrinks.
- Product-specific rules (complex products, leverage) usually need an
  appropriateness test in addition to suitability.

## Verify
- [ ] Fact-find complete, including vulnerability and source of wealth.
- [ ] Tolerance, capacity and need assessed separately; conflicts discussed.
- [ ] Recommended portfolio's downside shown in currency terms.
- [ ] Suitability report ties each recommendation to the client's own facts.
- [ ] Review held at least annually and after life events, with notes kept.
