# Whistleblower intake to investigation report

**Goal:** take an allegation of misconduct inside the organisation, establish the facts
fairly and proportionately, reach findings the evidence supports, and fix the cause —
while protecting the person who reported it.

## 1. Intake (first 48 hours)
- Log the report in a case-management system with restricted access; assign a case number.
- **Acknowledge** to the reporter. Under the EU Whistleblower Directive (2019/1937, Art. 9), internal reporting procedures must provide for acknowledgement within **7 days** and feedback within a reasonable time not exceeding **3 months** from the acknowledgement. National transposition laws and non-EU rules vary. *Check local law.*
- Capture: what, who, when, where, how the reporter knows, evidence they hold, who else
  knows. Ask if they fear retaliation.
- Keep identity confidential on a strict need-to-know basis; anonymous reports are valid —
  keep a two-way channel open if the hotline allows.

## 2. Triage
Decide: is it in scope (misconduct vs a grievance for HR)? How serious (fraud, bribery,
safety, sanctions, harassment by senior staff)? Who must not be involved (conflicts —
anyone implicated or close to the subject)? Is there a **legal or regulatory duty** to
notify (regulator, police, auditor, insurer)? Should counsel lead to protect **privilege**?
Serious or senior-level matters typically go to the audit committee and external counsel.

## 3. Plan and preserve
Write **terms of reference**: allegations to be investigated (specific), scope, investigator(s),
who they report to, timeline. Then the plan: evidence sources, interviewees and order,
data-protection constraints.
**Preserve evidence before interviews:** legal hold notices, mailbox and device preservation,
system logs, documents — quietly, so the subject isn't tipped off to destroy material.

## 4. Gather evidence
- Documents and data first: emails, chat, expense claims, approvals, access logs. Work with
  forensic IT for collection; keep chain of custody.
- **Interviews:** witnesses first, subject last (once you know what to ask). Two people —
  one asks, one takes notes. Explain the purpose, confidentiality, no retaliation, and, in
  the US where counsel interviews employees, the **Upjohn** warning (counsel represents the company, not the employee; *Upjohn Co. v. United States*, 449 U.S. 383 (1981)). Follow local rules on employee representation (e.g. works
  council, union representative, right to be accompanied).
- Open questions, then specific; put the evidence to the subject and let them respond.
- Write notes promptly; have the interviewee review/sign if your process requires it.

## 5. Findings
For each allegation conclude **substantiated / not substantiated / inconclusive** against a
stated standard (internal investigations commonly use the balance of probabilities).
Reason from evidence: what's corroborated, what's contradicted, credibility assessments with
reasons. Separate findings of fact from conclusions about policy breach; disciplinary
decisions belong to management/HR, not the investigator.

## 6. Report, remediate, close
Report: terms of reference, method, evidence summary, findings per allegation, root cause
and **control failures**, recommendations. Remediation goes beyond discipline — fix the
control that allowed it, consider restitution, disclosure, and training. Give the reporter
feedback on the outcome to the extent confidentiality allows. Close the case with all
records retained per policy.

## 7. Protect against retaliation
Monitor the reporter's treatment (performance ratings, assignments, dismissal) for a period
after the report. Retaliation is a separate serious breach, and in many jurisdictions illegal.

## Gotchas
- Investigating before preserving evidence; tipping off the subject.
- Investigator with a conflict or reporting line to the subject.
- Scope creep without updating the terms of reference — or too narrow scope that ignores obvious related issues.
- Leading questions and "gotcha" interviews; they make findings challengeable.
- Revealing the reporter's identity through the questions asked.
- Data-protection and cross-border transfer rules for evidence (e.g. GDPR).

## Verify
- [ ] Report logged, acknowledged within the required time, access restricted.
- [ ] Triage recorded: seriousness, conflicts, notification duties, privilege decision.
- [ ] Terms of reference written; evidence preserved before interviews.
- [ ] Each allegation has a finding with stated standard and reasoned evidence.
- [ ] Root cause and control fixes tracked; reporter given feedback and monitored for retaliation.
