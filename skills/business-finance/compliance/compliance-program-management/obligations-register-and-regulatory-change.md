# Obligations register and regulatory change — know what you must comply with

**Goal:** be able to answer, for any rule that applies to the firm, "who owns it, what
control meets it, and how do we know it's working?" — and keep that answer current as
rules change. Reference: ISO 37301 (compliance management systems) is said to describe this cycle (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked). ISO.org returned 403 and the standard is paywalled;
regulators expect the substance whatever the framework.

This is the in-house operational side. For legal advice on what a law requires, work with
counsel (see law-government.corporate-and-commercial-law.corporate-compliance-counselling).

## 1. Build the obligations register
- **Inventory sources:** licences and permissions held, applicable laws and regulations,
  regulator rules and guidance, codes the firm has signed up to, contractual commitments
  with regulatory effect, and conditions imposed by regulators.
- **Break them into obligations** — one row per thing someone must *do or not do*, written
  in plain language, with the citation. "Firms must keep records of X for 5 years" is an
  obligation; "Part 7 of the Act" is not.
- For each obligation record: business areas affected, **accountable owner** (a named senior
  person), linked **policy/procedure**, linked **controls**, compliance risk rating
  (impact × likelihood of breach), and last monitoring result.
- Use a GRC tool or a well-structured spreadsheet; the structure matters more than the software.

Gaps show up immediately: obligations with no owner, no policy, or no control.

## 2. Regulatory change management
1. **Horizon scanning** — subscribe to regulator publications, consultations, enforcement
   notices, industry body updates; legal-update services for multi-jurisdiction firms.
2. **Triage** each item weekly: relevant? to whom? effective date?
3. **Impact assessment** with the business: which obligations change, which policies,
   processes, systems, training and reports need updating, cost and lead time.
4. **Implementation plan** with owners and dates working back from the effective date;
   track in the same way as projects.
5. **Close out** — update the register, policies and controls; confirm with evidence
   that the change is live; post-implementation check after a few months.
Respond to consultations where a proposal materially affects you.

## 3. Compliance risk assessment and monitoring plan
- Annually rate compliance risk by business area/obligation group (inherent risk, control
  strength, residual risk), using incidents, complaints, audit findings and regulatory focus.
- Build a **risk-based monitoring plan**: higher-risk areas get more frequent and deeper
  testing (file reviews, transaction sampling, surveillance, thematic reviews). Document
  each test's scope, sample, results and actions.
- Track breaches and near misses; decide on regulator notification per the rules and
  timescales that apply (some regimes require prompt notification of significant breaches).

## 4. Reporting
Quarterly to senior management and the board (or its risk/audit committee): material
regulatory changes and implementation status, monitoring results, breaches and
notifications, open actions and overdue items, regulator interactions, emerging risks,
and an overall view of whether compliance risk is within appetite.

## Gotchas
- An obligations register that's a list of laws, not actionable obligations.
- "Compliance owns compliance" — the business owns its obligations; compliance advises,
  monitors and challenges.
- Regulatory change logged but never closed with evidence.
- Monitoring that only tests what's easy to test, not what's high-risk.
- Overlapping obligations across jurisdictions recorded inconsistently; map them to a common
  control where possible.

## Verify
- [ ] Every obligation has a citation, owner, policy and control link.
- [ ] Regulatory change items triaged weekly, with impact assessments and dated plans.
- [ ] Monitoring plan derived from a written compliance risk assessment.
- [ ] Breach log maintained; notification decisions documented.
- [ ] Board report shows change status, monitoring results, breaches and overdue actions.
