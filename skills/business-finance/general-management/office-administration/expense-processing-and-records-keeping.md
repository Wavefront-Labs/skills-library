# Expense processing and records keeping — the office admin's audit-proof routine

**Goal:** get staff expenses reimbursed quickly and correctly, and keep business
records so that anything can be found and defended later (by an auditor, a tax
authority or the next person in your job).

## 1. Expense claims: check before you approve
For every claim line confirm:
1. **Receipt present and legible**, showing supplier, date, amount, and the tax
   amount where you reclaim sales tax/VAT/GST. A card-terminal slip alone usually
   doesn't qualify as a tax invoice; many jurisdictions require a proper tax invoice
   above a threshold. *(Thresholds vary by country — check your tax authority.)*
2. **Within policy:** category limits (meals, hotel per night, mileage rate), class of
   travel, pre-approval where required.
3. **Business purpose** stated ("client meeting — <client code>, project X"), and
   attendees for entertainment. Tax authorities disallow entertainment without it.
4. **Not already claimed or paid on a company card.** Duplicates are the most common
   error; search by amount + date + supplier.
5. **Correct coding:** cost centre, GL account, project, tax code.
6. **Approver is the claimant's manager, not the claimant**, and never someone the
   claimant approves in turn on the same claim (circular approval).
Return non-compliant claims with the exact fix needed; don't silently edit amounts.

## 2. Mileage and per diems
Use the published rate the organisation adopted (often the tax authority's rate).
Require start/end and purpose; for repeat trips, check distance against a map once.

## 3. Turnaround and cut-offs
Publish a monthly cut-off (e.g. claims in by the 25th are paid in that month's run).
Set a late-claim age after which extra approval is needed — late claims are a known fraud
and accrual problem.

## 4. Records keeping: file so it can be found
- **One naming convention** for every file: `YYYY-MM-DD_supplier-or-party_doc-type_ref`
  — dates first so files sort chronologically.
- **Folder structure by function then year**, e.g. `Finance/Expenses/2026/`,
  `HR/Contracts/`, `Legal/Leases/`. Keep it shallow (3–4 levels).
- **Single source of truth:** the scanned/e-filed copy in the shared system is the
  record; no personal-drive copies.
- **Retention schedule:** a table of record types × how long × who may destroy.
  Periods are set by your tax authority. US example (IRS): keep records until the period
  of limitations for the return runs out, generally 3 years; 6 years if income left off the
  return exceeds 25% of the gross income shown; 7 years for a worthless-securities loss or
  bad-debt deduction; indefinitely if no return was filed or the return was fraudulent;
  4 years for employment tax records; property records until the limitation period expires
  for the year of disposal. Also check whether insurers or creditors need longer. Verify
  periods for your own jurisdiction and sector.
- **Destruction log:** what was destroyed, when, by whom, under which schedule line.
  Never destroy anything subject to a legal hold or pending dispute.
- **Personal data:** restrict HR and payroll folders; apply privacy-law retention
  limits (keeping personal data *too long* can also be a breach).

## 5. Monthly routine
- Reconcile expenses paid to the expense report total and to the GL.
- Chase missing receipts on company-card transactions (card statement vs receipts).
- Sweep the scan inbox into the filing structure; fix misnamed files.
- Review access lists on sensitive folders.

## Gotchas
- Splitting one purchase into several claims to stay under an approval limit
  ("split claims") — look for same-day, same-supplier lines.
- Foreign-currency claims: use the card statement amount actually charged, not a
  converted receipt amount.
- Photos of receipts are fine only if your tax authority accepts digital copies —
  most now do, but some require originals kept for a period.

## Verify
- [ ] Every approved claim line has receipt, purpose, policy check and coding.
- [ ] No claimant approved their own or a circular claim.
- [ ] Any document can be located quickly by name search.
- [ ] A written retention schedule exists and the destruction log is current.
- [ ] Company-card transactions all have receipts by month end.
