# Decision meetings: agendas, decision protocols and action tracking

**Goal:** run business meetings that end with clear decisions and owned actions,
in less time, and make sure the follow-through actually happens.

## 1. Should this be a meeting?
Meet only for **decide, solve, or align** — things that need live discussion.
Status updates go in writing. If you can't state what will be different after the
meeting, cancel it.

## 2. Build the agenda (send 24–48 h ahead)
For each item: **topic · desired outcome (decide / input / inform) · owner · time box ·
pre-read**. Example:
```
1. Q3 vendor shortlist — DECIDE — Priya — 20 min — pre-read: scoring sheet
2. Office move timeline — INPUT — Sam — 15 min
3. New expense policy — INFORM — Lee — 5 min
```
- Put decision items first, while energy is high.
- Pre-reads: short (1–2 pages), sent with the agenda, and stated as read — open with
  silent reading for 5 minutes if people won't pre-read.
- Invite only people who decide, hold needed information, or must execute.
  Others get the minutes.

## 3. Name the decision protocol before discussing
Say how the decision will be made *before* the debate:
- **Leader decides after input** (fastest; most business decisions).
- **Consent** — proceed unless someone has a reasoned, paramount objection.
- **Consensus** — everyone agrees (slow; use for values/high-commitment items).
- **Vote** — rarely right in a management team; creates losers.
Also state the **fallback**: "if we can't converge in 20 minutes, Priya decides."

## 4. Facilitate
- Open: purpose, outcomes, timebox, protocol. Appoint a note-taker and timekeeper
  (not the facilitator).
- Diverge then converge: collect options/concerns (round-robin or silent writing so
  quieter people contribute), then evaluate against agreed criteria.
- **Parking lot** for off-topic items; revisit at the end.
- Test for agreement explicitly: "Proposal is X. Any objections?" or fist-to-five.
- At time-box end: decide, extend once deliberately, or defer with an owner and
  date for what's missing.

## 5. Minutes that matter
Record only **decisions** (with rationale in one line), **actions** (what · who ·
by when), and **open questions**. Not a transcript. Read actions back in the last
5 minutes. Circulate within 24 hours.

## 6. Action tracking
- Keep a running action log (shared sheet or task tool) across meetings, not
  buried in each meeting's notes.
- One named owner per action; "the team" owns nothing.
- Open the next meeting by reviewing overdue actions (2–5 minutes).
- Close or reassign stale actions; a log full of dead items is ignored.

## Workshops (half-day+)
Same principles, plus: a session plan with timings and methods per block, materials
prepared, breaks every ~90 minutes, and a clear output artefact (e.g. prioritised
list, draft plan) defined in advance.

## Gotchas
- Re-litigating decisions next meeting — minutes with rationale prevent it.
- HiPPO effect: senior person speaks first and anchors everyone; have them speak last.
- "Decide" items with the decision-maker absent — reschedule the item.
- Hybrid meetings: run everyone through the same video tool so remote people aren't
  second-class.

## Verify
- [ ] Agenda went out in advance with outcome type, owner and timebox per item.
- [ ] Decision protocol and fallback stated before each decision item.
- [ ] Minutes list decisions, actions (owner + date) and open questions only.
- [ ] Minutes circulated within 24 h; overdue actions reviewed at the next meeting.
