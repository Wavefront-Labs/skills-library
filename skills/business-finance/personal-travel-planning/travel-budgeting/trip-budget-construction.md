# Trip budget construction

**Goal:** know before you book what the trip will roughly cost, choose trade-offs
deliberately, pay abroad without leaking 3–8% to fees, and know mid-trip whether you're
on track.

## 1. Split costs into fixed and daily
**Fixed (per trip):** flights, long-distance transport, travel insurance, visas/ETAs,
vaccinations, gear, pre-booked tours and passes, airport transfers, pet-sitting or
kennels at home.
**Daily (per day or per night):** accommodation, food, local transport, entry fees,
SIM/data, incidentals.
Trip budget = fixed + (daily × days) + contingency.

## 2. Estimate the daily rate
- Price **accommodation** from real listings for your dates (not "from" prices); it's
  usually the single largest daily item.
- **Food:** count meals × typical local prices at your chosen style (street food /
  casual / restaurant). Supermarket breakfasts and picnic lunches move this a lot.
- **Activities:** list the A-tier items from your itinerary with actual ticket prices.
- **Local transport:** passes vs per-ride; city cards only pay off if you'll actually
  hit enough included sights — do the arithmetic.
- Cross-check against recent traveller cost reports for the destination, adjusting for
  your style. Expensive destinations can be 3–4× cheaper ones for the same comfort level.

## 3. Contingency
Add **10–15%** of the total (more for long or remote trips). It absorbs price surprises,
a missed connection, an extra night. If unused, it's a bonus — not a target.

## 4. Trade-off levers (biggest first)
1. **Dates:** shoulder season and avoiding school holidays often cut flights and rooms
   substantially (*varies by destination*).
2. **Fewer bases / longer stays:** weekly rates, fewer transfer costs.
3. **Accommodation type:** apartment with kitchen vs hotel; location trade-off (cheaper
   room, but add the transport cost and time).
4. **Flight flexibility:** nearby airports, mid-week days (see booking-logistics).
5. **Splurge deliberately:** pick one or two things that matter and fund them by
   cutting where you don't care.

## 5. Currency and payment plan
- **Card with no foreign-transaction fee** as the main payment method (many standard
  cards charge ~2–3%), plus a **backup card on a different network** kept separately.
- Multi-currency travel cards/accounts can give near-mid-market rates; check ATM
  withdrawal limits and fees.
- **Always decline Dynamic Currency Conversion (DCC):** when a card terminal or ATM
  offers to charge you "in your home currency", choose the **local currency**. DCC rates
  commonly carry a markup of several percent.
- ATMs: use bank-owned machines, withdraw larger amounts less often, decline the
  operator's conversion, and watch for local operator fees.
- Carry some local cash for markets, tips, small towns and transit; how much depends
  on the country's card acceptance.
- Notify banks if they still require it; enable app alerts so you spot fraud fast.
- Learn tipping norms per country and budget them (significant in some countries,
  unusual or even awkward in others).

## 6. Track while travelling
- Simple method: note daily spend in an app (travel budget apps, a spreadsheet, or a
  shared-expense app like Splitwise for groups) each evening — two minutes.
- Compare **running average per day** to the planned daily rate. One expensive day is
  fine; a running average 20% over by day four means adjust now.
- Separate fixed pre-paid costs from on-trip spending so the daily figure stays honest.
- For groups, agree up front how shared costs split and settle up weekly, not at the end.

## Verify
- [ ] Fixed and daily costs listed separately with real quotes for accommodation and transport.
- [ ] Contingency of ≥10% included.
- [ ] Primary card has no FX fee; backup card on another network packed separately.
- [ ] Everyone in the party knows to decline DCC.
- [ ] Daily running average reviewed at least every few days on the trip.
