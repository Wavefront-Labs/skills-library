# Day-by-day itinerary and pacing

**Goal:** turn "two weeks in X" into a route and daily plan that fits the time, energy
and interests of the people actually going — with slack built in so one delay doesn't
collapse the trip.

## 1. Frame the trip (before any map)
- **Hard constraints:** dates, total nights, budget band (see
  travel-budgeting.trip-budget-construction), mobility, kids' ages, dietary or medical needs.
- **Trip style per traveller:** pace (relaxed / medium / packed), interests ranked,
  tolerance for early starts and long transit days.
- **Fixed points:** an event, a festival, a booked tour, a friend's wedding. These pin
  the calendar; plan around them.

## 2. Research and tier the wish list
Collect candidates from guidebooks, official tourism sites, maps and trip reports.
Put each on a shared map (Google My Maps or similar) so geography is visible. Then tier:
- **A — the reason you're going** (2–4 per destination).
- **B — would be sad to miss.**
- **C — nice if passing.**
For each A item note: opening days (museums often close one weekday), timed-entry or
advance-booking requirements, seasonal closures, and how long it really takes.

## 3. Route and nights per base
- Draw the shortest sensible loop or line through clusters of A items. Avoid
  backtracking; consider **open-jaw** flights (fly into one city, out of another) —
  often similar cost and saves a full travel day.
- **Rule of thumb for bases:** minimum 2 nights anywhere (1-night stops lose most of
  the day to check-in/out); 3+ nights for a base you'll do day trips from. Moving every
  day feels like a commute, not a holiday.
- Count **transit time door to door**, not timetable time: hotel → station → wait →
  ride → station → hotel. Use a route planner (Rome2Rio-style aggregators, then the
  operator's own site to confirm).
- Too many bases? Cut a destination, not the nights. The most common itinerary error
  is breadth over depth.

## 4. The day template
A sustainable day for most travellers:
- **One anchor** (an A item) in the morning when energy and crowds are better.
- **One secondary** (B item) or a neighbourhood wander after lunch.
- **Downtime** mid-afternoon — essential with kids, older travellers or heat.
- Evening: dinner and something light.
Group by neighbourhood to cut transit. A **packed** traveller might do two anchors; plan
for the slowest person in the group, not the keenest.

## 5. Build in slack
- **Arrival day after a long flight = low-stakes.** Plan nothing bookable; for big
  time-zone shifts, expect 1–2 groggy days (a common estimate is about a day per
  time zone crossed for full adjustment — *rough*; eastward is usually harder).
- **One buffer/free day per week**, or a half-day every few days.
- Don't book a non-refundable tour on the morning after a long transfer.
- **Departure:** stay near the airport, or within easy reach, before an early or
  international flight — especially if it's the last leg home.

## 6. Make it usable
One document per trip: dates, base addresses, bookings with confirmation numbers and
cancellation deadlines, the day plan, offline maps downloaded, opening hours. Share it
with travel companions and someone at home.

## Gotchas
- Public holidays at the destination close attractions and fill transport.
- "Walkable" on a map ignores hills, heat and 25,000-step days.
- Booked-out timed entries (popular galleries, monuments) can sell out months ahead —
  check these first, then build around the slots you get.

## Verify
- [ ] Every base has ≥2 nights unless deliberately a transit stop.
- [ ] Each transfer has a door-to-door time estimate.
- [ ] Every A item has its closing day and booking requirement checked.
- [ ] Arrival day is light; there is at least one free block per week.
- [ ] The itinerary document is shared and available offline.
