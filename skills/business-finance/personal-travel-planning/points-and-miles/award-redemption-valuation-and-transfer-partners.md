# Award redemption valuation and transfer partners

**Goal:** turn loyalty points into trips worth more than the cash alternative, without
stranding points in the wrong programme. Programme rules, partners and award charts
change often — treat every specific figure here as *illustrative* and check the live
programme terms before acting.

## 1. Know your currencies
- **Programme currencies:** an airline's miles or a hotel chain's points — redeemable
  only in that programme (and its partners).
- **Transferable/flexible currencies:** bank or card points that convert into several
  airline and hotel programmes. More valuable because you choose the programme later.
  **Transfers are generally one-way and irreversible** — the cardinal rule.
- Note expiry rules (inactivity windows) and family-pooling options for each account.

## 2. Valuing a redemption: cents per point (cpp)
`cpp = (cash price of the same trip − taxes/fees paid on the award) ÷ points used × 100`
Use the cash price **you would actually have paid** (often economy, not the business
fare you'd never buy). Compare against:
- the **fixed cash-out value** of the currency (e.g. pay-with-points or statement
  credit) — the floor; and
- a personal baseline you set for "good" redemptions per currency.
Also count what you lose: earning miles on a cash fare, flexibility, and the time hunting.

## 3. Award search before transfer
1. Find the route on the operating airline, then identify which **alliance partners'**
   programmes can book that same seat (Star Alliance, oneworld, SkyTeam, plus
   bilateral partners).
2. Search award availability on a partner's site or an award-search tool. Partners
   often see only **saver** (lowest-level) inventory, which differs from what the
   operating airline sells to its own members.
3. Compare **price in miles + cash** across programmes: same seat can cost very different
   amounts, partly because some programmes pass on **carrier-imposed surcharges**
   ("fuel surcharges") — sometimes hundreds per ticket on certain carriers or routes.
4. Only when you've **seen bookable space** at an acceptable price, transfer the exact
   number of points needed (most transfers are instant or within a day; a few take
   longer — *check before relying on it for scarce space*).
5. Book immediately; hold options exist with some programmes.

## 4. Earning strategy
- **Sign-up bonuses** on cards usually dwarf ongoing earn; meet minimum spend only with
  normal spending, and only if you pay in full monthly. Interest wipes out rewards.
- Concentrate on one or two flexible currencies plus the programme of your home
  airline rather than sprinkling across ten programmes.
- Use shopping portals and dining programmes as minor top-ups, not a reason to spend.
- Some jurisdictions have restricted card rewards and fees; availability varies widely by country.

## 5. Elite status decisions
Status buys lounge access, upgrades, bag allowances, better irregular-operation
handling. Worth chasing only if you'd fly most of the required amount anyway.
**Never fly extra "mileage runs"** without pricing them against simply buying the
benefits (lounge pass, paid bag, extra legroom). Ask about **status matches or
challenges** when switching airlines or hotel chains.

## Gotchas
- **Dynamic award pricing** means fixed charts are increasingly gone; good-value
  redemptions often cluster in partner awards or off-peak dates.
- **Devaluations** happen with little notice — earning and burning beats hoarding.
- Award tickets can have different change/cancel fees and stopover rules from cash tickets.
- Names on the award must match passports; some programmes restrict booking for
  non-family "redemption nominees".
- Taxes and surcharges are paid in cash; include them in cpp.

## Verify
- [ ] Bookable award space confirmed in the target programme before any transfer.
- [ ] cpp calculated against the realistic cash alternative, net of fees and surcharges.
- [ ] Redemption beats the currency's fixed cash-out value.
- [ ] Cards earning points are paid in full every month.
- [ ] Expiry dates for all accounts recorded.
