# Scoring and due diligence

**Goal:** decide grants consistently, fairly and defensibly — comparing applications on
the same published criteria, checking the organisation can be trusted with the money,
and documenting why each decision was made.

## 1. Eligibility screen (staff, fast)
Binary checks against the published criteria: legal status, geography, size band, fit,
completeness. Record the failed criterion for each ineligible application — it feeds
the decline letter and tells you if the call was unclear. Borderline cases: one
documented decision rule, applied to everyone.

## 2. The scoring rubric
Use the **same criteria and weights published in the call**. Typical set: need and
relevance, approach/quality of design, organisational capacity, equity/inclusion,
value for money, learning/sustainability. For each criterion write **anchored
descriptors** per score:
- 1 — Not addressed / major weaknesses.
- 2 — Weak; significant gaps.
- 3 — Adequate; meets expectations with minor gaps.
- 4 — Strong; clear, evidenced.
- 5 — Exceptional; compelling evidence and design.
Anchors stop "a 4 for me is a 3 for you". Ask for a **one-line justification per
score** — it is the audit trail and the source for applicant feedback.

## 3. Reviewers and calibration
- **2–3 independent reviewers per application** where volume allows; include people
  with lived experience or regional knowledge, not only staff.
- **Conflict of interest:** each reviewer declares relationships (board seats, employment,
  family, consulting, competing bids) per application before seeing it; conflicted
  reviewers don't score or attend that discussion. Log declarations.
- **Calibration session:** everyone scores the same 2–3 sample applications, then
  discusses differences before the main round.
- Watch for drift and known biases: favouring polished writing (penalises small and
  non-native-language applicants), halo effect from famous organisations, order
  effects (late-in-the-day harshness). Consider partial blinding of organisation
  names at early stages.
- **Moderation:** flag applications where reviewer scores differ by ≥2 points on a
  criterion; resolve through discussion, not averaging.

## 4. Panel and decision
Rank by weighted score, then apply **portfolio considerations** openly (geography,
focus-area balance, organisation size) — and document when the portfolio overrides rank.
Fund fewer at a meaningful size rather than shaving every budget.

## 5. Due diligence (proportionate to risk and size)
- **Legal and registration:** charity or company register, governing documents, good standing.
- **Financial health:** last accounts/audit, reserves in months of spending,
  income concentration (dependence on one funder), going-concern notes, unexplained deficits.
- **Governance:** board independence and size, key policies (finance, conflict of
  interest, whistleblowing).
- **Safeguarding** policy and practice where working with children or vulnerable adults.
- **Screening:** sanctions and anti-terrorism-financing lists for organisation and key
  people, especially for cross-border grants; adverse media search.
- **Reference calls** with another funder or partner.
- For high-risk or large grants: site visit or video call with leadership and finance staff.
Outcome: proceed / proceed with conditions (e.g. tranche payments, a finance
mentor, more frequent reporting) / decline. Conditions should address an identified risk.

## 6. Award recommendation and agreement
A one-page **award memo** per grant: summary, score and reviewer highlights, due-diligence
findings and conditions, amount, duration, payment schedule, reporting requirements,
alignment with strategy. The grant agreement mirrors the memo: purpose, amount,
payment schedule, reporting, permitted budget variance, restrictions, termination,
and return of unspent or misused funds.

## 7. Declines
Send promptly and kindly; offer brief criterion-based feedback where capacity allows
(two or three sentences from reviewer justifications). Never imply resubmission will
succeed unless it's true.

## Verify
- [ ] Every application has eligibility outcome and reason recorded.
- [ ] Rubric with anchored descriptors matches the published criteria and weights.
- [ ] COI declarations logged; conflicted reviewers excluded.
- [ ] Large score divergences moderated and resolved with notes.
- [ ] Due diligence completed at a depth matched to grant risk, with conditions linked to findings.
- [ ] Award memos and decline letters issued with reasons.
