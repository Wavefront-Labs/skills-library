# Designing a grant programme and call for proposals

**Goal:** a programme whose money, mechanisms and process fit what it wants to change,
and a call that attracts the right applicants while wasting as little of everyone
else's time as possible. Legal constraints on who a funder may fund (charity law,
expenditure responsibility, cross-border rules) vary by jurisdiction — check with
counsel when funding non-charities or overseas.

## 1. Strategy before process
- **Problem and change sought:** what will be different in 5–10 years, for whom.
- **Landscape scan:** who else funds this, where the gaps are, what grantees say they
  lack (talk to 10–20 practitioners — cheap and decisive).
- **Theory of change and the funder's role:** money alone, or money plus convening,
  advocacy, research? Where is this funder's comparative advantage?
- **Focus areas:** 2–4 at most for a typical programme budget. Many focus areas with
  small budgets each = scattered grants with little cumulative effect.
- **Budget envelope:** annual amount, expected number of grants, typical size and
  length — these three numbers must be consistent.

## 2. Choose funding mechanisms
| Mechanism | Fits when | Watch |
|---|---|---|
| **General operating / core** | Aligned organisations; you trust their strategy | Harder attribution; needs good due diligence |
| **Project** | Specific new work, clear deliverables | Under-funds overheads — allow realistic indirect costs |
| **Multi-year** | Change takes years; stability matters | Locks budget; build in annual check-ins |
| **Capacity-building** | Strengthening systems, leadership | Let grantees define needs |
| **Small/rapid grants** | Community groups, emergencies | Proportionate (light) process |
| **Regranting / participatory** | Shifting decisions to communities | Governance and conflict-of-interest design |
| **Programme-related investments / loans** | Revenue-generating work | Needs different expertise |
Trust-based approaches (multi-year unrestricted funding, simplified reporting) are
widely advocated to reduce burden; choose deliberately, not by fashion.

## 3. Eligibility criteria
Write them as tests an applicant can self-check: legal status, geography, size (e.g.
annual budget band), focus-area fit, track record, exclusions (individuals, capital
works, religious activity, lobbying — as relevant). Each criterion should be **needed**:
unnecessary criteria (e.g. requiring three years of audited accounts) silently exclude
small, new or community-led organisations — test criteria against the population you
actually want to reach.

## 4. Process design: proportionate to grant size
- **Open call / invited / rolling** — open calls are fairer but generate volume.
- **Two-stage**: short expression of interest or letter of inquiry (1–2 pages), then full
  proposals from a shortlist. Saves applicants most of the work when success rates are low.
- **Rule of thumb:** expected applicant effort shouldn't be wildly out of proportion to
  the grant value × probability of success. Publish expected success rates.
- Accept existing materials (their strategy, budget, annual report) instead of bespoke forms.
- Accessibility: plain language, alternative formats, possibility of video/oral applications.

## 5. Writing the call
Include: purpose and what success looks like; who's eligible and who isn't; grant size,
duration, number expected; what costs are covered (including overheads); assessment
criteria **with weights** (same as reviewers will use — see
application-review-and-award.scoring-and-due-diligence); timeline with decision date;
process stages; contact for questions and an FAQ/info session; how decisions are communicated.

## 6. Pilot and revise
Run the first round as a pilot: track applications, eligibility rejection rate,
applicant-reported hours, diversity of applicants, review time. A high ineligible rate
means the call is unclear; a flood of low-fit proposals means the focus is too vague.

## Verify
- [ ] Budget envelope, grant size and grant count are mutually consistent.
- [ ] Each eligibility criterion is justified and tested for unintended exclusion.
- [ ] Application burden is proportionate; two-stage used when success rate is low.
- [ ] Call publishes weighted criteria, timeline and decision date.
- [ ] Pilot metrics captured and reviewed before round two.
