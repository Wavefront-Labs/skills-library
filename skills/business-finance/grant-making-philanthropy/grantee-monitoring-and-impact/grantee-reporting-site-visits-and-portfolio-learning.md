# Grantee reporting, site visits and portfolio learning

**Goal:** know whether funded work is happening and what it's achieving, help grantees
when things go off track, and learn across the portfolio — while keeping the reporting
burden in proportion to the money. Funders often over-collect and under-read reports;
reverse that.

## 1. Proportionate reporting
- Set requirements at award, matched to grant size and risk: a small grant may need one
  short final report (or a call); a large multi-year grant may need annual narrative
  and financial reports.
- **Accept the grantee's own reports** to their board or other funders where possible.
- Ask fewer, better questions: What did you do? What changed and for whom? What didn't
  work and what are you changing? What would help from us? How was the money spent vs
  budget, and why any significant variance?
- **Financial report:** spend vs approved budget by main line; explanation of
  variances beyond the agreed tolerance; unspent balance.
- **Read every report within a set time** (e.g. 30 days), respond with a short note, and
  log key data. Unread reports teach grantees the process is theatre.

## 2. Site visits and check-in calls
- Purpose: understand context and relationship, not audit. Agree the agenda with
  the grantee; ask what they want you to see; don't make them host a performance.
- Meet beyond leadership where appropriate — frontline staff and, with care and consent,
  participants. Keep the group small; cover your own costs.
- Useful questions: What surprised you this year? What's the hardest constraint? If
  you had 10% more money or 10% less, what would change?
- Write a visit note within a week: observations, risks, follow-ups. Share relevant
  parts back.

## 3. Red flags and responses
Signals: reports late repeatedly, leadership turnover, unexplained large variances,
sudden loss of other funders, safeguarding or fraud allegations, cash-flow distress.
Response ladder: conversation first (most issues are capacity, not malice) → agreed
remediation plan → changed payment terms (smaller tranches) → independent review →
suspension/termination and recovery per the grant agreement for serious misuse.
Safeguarding and fraud allegations follow your organisation's incident policy
immediately, including any regulator reporting duties (*jurisdiction-specific*).

## 4. Impact measurement without overreach
- Build a **light outcomes framework** per programme: 3–6 outcomes the strategy cares
  about, each with one or two indicators that grantees already track or can track
  cheaply. Avoid inventing bespoke metrics per grant that can't be aggregated.
- **Contribution, not attribution:** most foundation grants are one of many inputs.
  Ask "what's the evidence our support plausibly contributed?" (contribution analysis)
  rather than claiming sole credit.
- Use **mixed evidence**: counts, outcome indicators, case stories, grantee reflections,
  external data and, for big bets, commissioned evaluations.
- Fund grantees' own measurement capacity if you want better data — don't just ask for it.

## 5. Portfolio-level learning
Once or twice a year, per programme:
1. Aggregate: where grants are, sizes, completion, outcome indicators, grantee types.
2. Synthesise themes from reports and visit notes (what works, recurring barriers).
3. Compare against the programme's theory of change — which assumptions held?
4. **Ask grantees anonymously** how the funder performs (responsiveness, burden, helpfulness);
   independent grantee perception surveys exist for this.
5. Record **decisions**: change focus, eligibility, grant sizes, reporting, or exit
   plans. Share a short "what we learned" note back with grantees.

## Gotchas
- Asking for "impact" from a one-year small grant invites inflated claims.
- Changing reporting templates mid-grant without notice is a hidden burden.
- Ending funding abruptly harms grantees; plan exits with notice and, if possible, tapered or bridge funding.

## Verify
- [ ] Reporting requirements recorded at award and proportionate to size and risk.
- [ ] Every report read and acknowledged within the set window.
- [ ] Visit notes filed within a week, with follow-ups tracked.
- [ ] Red-flag cases have a documented response and outcome.
- [ ] Annual portfolio review records at least one strategy decision and is shared with grantees.
