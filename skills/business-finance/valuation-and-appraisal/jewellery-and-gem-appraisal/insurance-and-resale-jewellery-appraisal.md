# Insurance and resale jewellery appraisal

**Goal:** a correctly identified, graded and valued piece with the right value type for
the purpose — usually **retail replacement** for insurance, **fair market / resale**
for estates, divorce or sale — documented well enough that an insurer or buyer can
rely on it. Gem identification needs gemmological training and instruments; anything
uncertain goes to a gem lab.

## 1. Purpose and value type
- **Insurance replacement:** cost to replace with a piece of like kind and quality from a
  typical retail source. Avoid inflated replacement values: premiums rise and insurers
  may only pay what replacement actually costs them.
- **Fair market value / resale:** what a willing buyer pays in the secondary market
  (estate jewellers, auction) — often a fraction of retail for modern commercial jewellery.
- **Liquidation / scrap (melt):** metal weight × purity × spot price, minus refiner
  margin; plus whatever dealers pay for stones. The floor.
The gap between replacement and resale is the most common source of shock for owners;
explain it in the report.

## 2. Examine and identify
- **Metals:** hallmarks/stamps (e.g. 750 = 18 kt, 585 = 14 kt, 925 sterling, 950 platinum),
  maker's marks; confirm with testing (acid, XRF) — stamps can be wrong. Weigh on a
  calibrated scale (grams or dwt).
- **Stones:** identify species and variety (refractometer, polariscope, spectroscope,
  microscope). Distinguish natural, **lab-grown** (needs advanced lab screening for
  diamonds — visual inspection is not enough), and simulants (cubic zirconia, moissanite,
  glass, doublets).
- **Treatments:** heating, fracture filling, oiling of emeralds, diffusion, irradiation,
  coatings — strongly affect value and care; disclose known or suspected treatments.
- **Construction and condition:** prong wear, solder repairs, worn shanks, chips,
  abrasion on facet edges.

## 3. Grade
- **Diamonds:** the 4Cs — carat weight (measure and estimate if mounted; state
  "estimated"), colour, clarity, cut (proportions, polish, symmetry), plus fluorescence.
  A grading report from a major lab (e.g. GIA, IGI and others) helps; verify that the
  report number matches any laser inscription and that measurements match the stone.
  Grading standards differ between labs — *don't treat grades from different labs as
  interchangeable*.
- **Coloured gemstones:** hue, tone, saturation, clarity, cut, size, treatment and
  sometimes **origin** (some origins carry premiums); origin reports come only from
  specialist labs.
- **Pearls:** type (natural, cultured, freshwater/saltwater), lustre, surface, shape,
  size, matching.

## 4. Price
- Replacement: current wholesale/retail price guides and supplier quotes for comparable
  stones and mounting, plus labour for manufacture — matched to the piece's quality
  and market tier.
- Resale: recent auction and estate-dealer results for comparable items; designer and
  signed pieces (major maisons) and period jewellery can command premiums over intrinsic value.
- **Lab-grown diamond prices** have fallen far below natural and continue to move;
  date-stamp the valuation and recommend more frequent updates.
- Always state the **effective date**; metal and stone prices move.

## 5. Documentation
Per item: photograph (front, side, marks, against a scale), full description (metal,
weight, stones with measurements, estimated weights, grades, treatments), plotting or
notes of inclusions for identification, value type and amount, effective date,
methodology, limiting conditions (e.g. "stones graded while mounted"), appraiser
qualifications and signature. Recommend updates every 2–5 years (*insurer
requirements vary*) and secure storage of the report separate from the jewellery.

## Ethics
No percentage-of-value fees; disclose if you might buy or sell the item; don't
appraise outside competence (antique cameos, natural pearls, high-value coloured
stones may need specialists).

## Verify
- [ ] Value type matches purpose; replacement vs resale explained to the client.
- [ ] Metal purity tested, not only read from stamps; weight recorded.
- [ ] Each stone identified, natural vs lab-grown vs simulant determined or referred to a lab.
- [ ] Treatments disclosed; mounted-stone grading limitations stated.
- [ ] Photos, measurements and effective date in the report.
