# Valuing antiques and contents by purpose

**Goal:** give a defensible value for movable goods — furniture, art, silver, ceramics,
collectibles, general household contents — where the **purpose** drives which value
you report. The same Georgian chest can carry an insurance replacement value two or
three times its probate value, and both can be correct. Professional bodies (e.g.
ASA, ISA, AAA in the US; RICS and other national bodies elsewhere) and USPAP-type
standards govern formal reports; authentication beyond your competence must be
referred to specialists.

## 1. Purpose sets the value type
| Purpose | Usual value type | Market |
|---|---|---|
| Insurance scheduling | **Retail replacement value** — cost to buy a comparable item new or from a retail dealer, in a reasonable time | Retail (dealers, galleries) |
| Probate / estate tax, charitable donation | **Fair market value** — price between willing buyer and seller, both informed, neither compelled | The **most common market** where such items sell to the public — often auction (*tax authority rules define this; check current guidance*) |
| Divorce / equitable distribution | Usually fair market value; sometimes agreed otherwise | Per jurisdiction or agreement |
| Sale advice | **Auction estimate** (low–high range) or marketable cash value net of fees | Auction / private sale |
| Liquidation / house clearance | Orderly or forced liquidation value | Dealers, clearance |
State the value type, **effective date** and market in the report. Many disputes are
really a mismatch between these.

## 2. Identify and describe (the object comes first)
- Category, maker/artist, period, origin, materials and technique, dimensions, marks
  and signatures (photograph them), edition numbers.
- **Condition report:** damage, restoration, replacements (e.g. new handles, re-veneering,
  re-lined canvas), missing parts. Use good raking light and UV where appropriate.
  Condition can halve or destroy value in collector markets.
- **Provenance and documents:** receipts, prior appraisals, exhibition or literature
  history, export/import paperwork. Good provenance adds value; gaps in sensitive
  categories (antiquities, wartime-era art, ivory, protected species materials) can
  make an item **unsaleable** or illegal to trade — flag, don't guess.
- If attribution is uncertain, say so ("attributed to", "circle of", "in the style of" —
  these qualifiers carry real price differences).

## 3. Research comparables
- **Auction records** (price databases and auction house archives): hammer vs price
  including buyer's premium — know which the database shows and stay consistent.
- Dealer retail asking prices for insurance replacement (asking ≠ sold; note it).
- Choose comparables close in maker, model, size, condition and date of sale; adjust for
  differences and for **market trend** (many "brown furniture" categories have fallen
  sharply over recent decades while some design and contemporary categories rose).
- Note currency and regional market differences.

## 4. General household contents
Room-by-room inventory; group low-value items into lots ("kitchen contents, general")
and itemise anything above a threshold (for insurance, commonly items over a policy's
single-item limit need listing). Photos of every room and significant item.
Second-hand household goods often have **very low** fair market value — set
expectations kindly.

## 5. Ethics and independence
No fee as a percentage of value; don't offer to buy what you appraise; disclose any
interest; stay within competence. Donation and tax appraisals may require specific
qualifications and signed forms — *check current tax authority requirements*.

## 6. The report
Client, intended use and users, value type and definition, effective date, scope of
inspection, each item (description, condition, photos, value), methodology and market,
assumptions and limiting conditions, signed certification. Insurance schedules should
be reviewed every 3–5 years or after market moves.

## Verify
- [ ] Purpose → value type → market chain is explicit in the report.
- [ ] Each significant item photographed with marks and a condition note.
- [ ] Comparables consistent on hammer vs buyer's-premium-inclusive prices, dated and adjusted.
- [ ] Attribution qualifiers and provenance gaps stated honestly; restricted materials flagged.
- [ ] No conflict of interest; fee independent of value.
