# Sales comparison approach and adjustments

**Goal:** a supportable opinion of market value for land and buildings, led by the
sales comparison approach (the primary approach for most residential property) and
cross-checked with income and cost approaches where they apply. Professional practice
is governed by standards (USPAP in the US, RICS Red Book/IVS in many other countries)
and licensing; this is the method, not a substitute for those requirements.

## 1. Define the problem
Client and intended use, intended users, **type of value** (usually market value),
**effective date**, property rights (fee simple, leasehold), extraordinary assumptions
(e.g. "as if complete"). Value for a mortgage lender, a divorce and a tax appeal can
differ only because the definition or date differs — get it in writing.

## 2. Inspect and analyse
- Measure **gross living area** using the standard your market uses (e.g. ANSI Z765 in
  US residential; IPMS/local codes elsewhere — *measurement conventions differ*;
  basements and finished attics are typically reported separately).
- Record condition and quality ratings, layout, updates, defects, site features, zoning,
  easements, flood zone, view, external nuisances.
- **Highest and best use:** legally permissible, physically possible, financially
  feasible, maximally productive — as vacant and as improved. Usually the current use;
  when it isn't (e.g. a teardown), the whole valuation changes.

## 3. Select and verify comparables
- Search recent arm's-length sales (often within 6–12 months, closer in time and
  distance is better) similar in location, size, age, quality, condition, site, style.
- **Verify** each: price, date, concessions (seller-paid costs), financing, condition at
  sale, whether arm's-length (not a family transfer or distressed sale) — via agent,
  party or listing history.
- Use 3–6 comps, plus active and pending listings as market-direction evidence.

## 4. Adjust: comp → subject
Adjust **the comparable's price** toward the subject: if the comp is *better*, adjust
*down*; if *worse*, adjust *up*. Order commonly used: property rights → financing/
concessions → conditions of sale → **market conditions (time)** → location → physical
characteristics.
Support each adjustment with market evidence:
- **Paired sales:** two sales differing mainly in one feature isolate its value.
- **Time adjustment** from repeat sales or a price index trend per month.
- **Grouped data / regression** on a larger data set for GLA, bedrooms, garage.
- Cost-to-cure only where the market pays it.
Typical guides: net adjustments ideally modest relative to price (around 15% net / 25%
gross is a long-cited lender rule of thumb — *not a standard, and not a hard limit*).
Large adjustments mean the comp is weak; find a better one or explain.

## 5. Reconcile within the approach
Look at the range of adjusted prices. Weight comps with the **fewest and smallest
adjustments**, best verification and most similar location — don't just average.
State why.

## 6. Cross-check with other approaches
- **Income approach** (rentals, commercial): direct capitalisation — value = net
  operating income ÷ capitalisation rate (cap rate derived from comparable sales) — or
  discounted cash flow for multi-year leases.
- **Cost approach:** land value + replacement/reproduction cost new − depreciation
  (physical, functional, external). Strong for new or special-purpose buildings, weak
  for old ones.
Final **reconciliation:** weight approaches by data quality and relevance to how
buyers in this market price this property type.

## 7. Report
Scope of work, property description, HBU, data and adjustments with support, reasoning,
final value and date, assumptions and limiting conditions, certification. A reader
should be able to follow each number to its source.

## Verify
- [ ] Type of value, effective date, intended use and property rights stated.
- [ ] Each comp verified as arm's-length with concessions noted.
- [ ] Adjustments directionally correct (comp better → down) and each has market support.
- [ ] Final value weighted toward least-adjusted, best-verified comps with reasons.
- [ ] Other approaches considered and either used or excluded with explanation.
