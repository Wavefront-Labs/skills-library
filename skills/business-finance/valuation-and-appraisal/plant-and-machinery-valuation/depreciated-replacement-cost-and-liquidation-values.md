# Depreciated replacement cost and liquidation values

**Goal:** value machinery and equipment (production lines, CNC machines, earthmoving
plant, vehicles, process equipment) on the premise the client needs — continued use,
insurance, or liquidation for a lender — with each figure traceable to cost or market
evidence. Professional practice follows standards such as IVS/RICS Red Book or
USPAP with machinery & equipment credentials (e.g. ASA M&E); definitions below
paraphrase common usage and *exact definitions should be taken from the governing standard*.

## 1. Premise of value (decides everything)
- **Fair market value in continued use:** assumes the assets stay installed and
  working as part of an operating business — includes installation, freight and
  integration costs.
- **Fair market value in exchange / removal:** assets sold individually and removed.
- **Orderly liquidation value (OLV):** gross amount realisable from a sale with a
  reasonable period to find buyers (often stated as a few months), seller compelled,
  as-is where-is.
- **Forced liquidation value (FLV):** quick sale, typically a properly advertised auction
  in a compressed timeframe.
- **Insurable value / reinstatement:** cost to replace, often new for old, per policy.
Lenders usually want **net OLV** (after selling costs) for asset-based lending. Say
whether values are gross or net of commissions, removal, and holding costs.

## 2. Inspection and the asset register
- Start from the fixed-asset register but **verify physically** — registers miss
  disposals and include ghost assets. Tag or photograph each item with its nameplate
  (maker, model, serial, year).
- Record condition, hours/km/cycles, maintenance records, capacity and options,
  modifications, whether operating at inspection, and site/removal difficulty
  (foundations, cranes needed, hazardous fluids).
- Group low-value items into lots; itemise anything material.

## 3. Cost approach: depreciated replacement cost (DRC)
1. **Replacement cost new (RCN):** current price of a modern equivalent asset with the
   same utility, from dealer quotes, manufacturer lists or cost indices applied to
   historical cost (trending — weaker; indices drift from real equipment prices).
   For continued use, add freight, installation and commissioning.
2. **Physical deterioration:** commonly via age-life: effective age ÷ total economic life
   (e.g. a press with effective age 10 years and 25-year life → 40% physical
   depreciation). Adjust effective age for condition and maintenance, not just
   calendar age.
3. **Functional obsolescence:** excess operating cost or lost capacity versus the modern
   equivalent (energy use, manual vs automated, oversized capacity).
4. **Economic (external) obsolescence:** industry-wide factors — overcapacity, regulation,
   low utilisation; often measured by an under-utilisation or income shortfall at the
   business level.
DRC = RCN × (1 − physical) − functional − economic (apply consistently; percentages
can also be applied multiplicatively).

## 4. Market approach: used-equipment comparables
- Auction results, dealer sale prices and asking prices (discount asking to expected
  transaction levels) for the same make/model/year/hours.
- Adjust for condition, hours, options, location (shipping cost to the market), and
  date of sale.
- Highly liquid categories (trucks, mainstream construction plant, forklifts) have deep
  markets — weight the market approach heavily. Specialised or custom process equipment
  has few comps — rely on cost, but expect liquidation values far below DRC.

## 5. Liquidation values
OLV and FLV come from market evidence for the realisation scenario: auction results
for similar assets, recovery rates in comparable sales, net of auctioneer commission
(often charged to buyer and/or seller), de-installation, rigging, transport,
decontamination, site holding costs and time. Specialist custom equipment may realise
little above scrap.

## 6. Report
Premise and definitions, effective date, inspection scope, asset listing with
identifiers, methods per category, RCN sources, depreciation basis, market evidence,
the values (and gross/net basis), assumptions (e.g. clear title, no liens), and limiting
conditions. Lenders often require OLV, FLV and FMV side by side.

## Verify
- [ ] Premise of value matches the client's use and is defined in the report.
- [ ] Assets physically verified against the register with nameplate data.
- [ ] RCN sourced; physical, functional and economic obsolescence each considered.
- [ ] Market comps used where liquid markets exist, adjusted for hours and condition.
- [ ] Liquidation values stated gross or net with costs listed.
