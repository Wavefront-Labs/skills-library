# Pre-approval to application — converting mortgage leads

**Goal:** move a borrower from "I'm thinking about it" to a complete, lodged
application with the fewest dropped leads, while giving advice you'd be happy to
defend to a compliance reviewer. Written for a loan officer, mortgage adviser or
broker; the regulated steps (credit licensing, best-interest duty, disclosure
forms) differ by country — check your own regime before you script anything.

## 1. Speed to lead
- Call inbound and portal leads **within 5 minutes** where you can. Contact rates
  fall away sharply after the first hour; the borrower is usually shopping 2–4
  lenders at once. (The often-quoted "5 minutes" figure comes from web-lead
  studies; treat the exact multiplier as indicative.)
- Sequence for no-answer: call → SMS ("This is <name> from <lender>, you asked
  about a home loan, when suits for 10 minutes?") → call again same day → email
  with a booking link → day 2 and day 5 calls. Stop after ~6 touches and move to nurture.

## 2. The qualifying call (15–20 minutes)
Cover these, in roughly this order, and write the answers down:
1. **Purpose and timing** — purchase (found a property? auction/closing date?),
   refinance (why now?), equity release. Timing decides urgency.
2. **Price/loan size and deposit** — gives you the loan-to-value (LVR/LTV).
   Above ~80% usually means mortgage insurance or a different product; say so early.
3. **Income and employment** — type (PAYG/salaried, self-employed, contractor,
   commission/overtime), tenure, probation. Self-employed usually needs 1–2 years'
   tax returns; flag it now, not at document stage.
4. **Commitments** — other loans, car finance, BNPL, credit-card *limits* (lenders
   assess the limit, not the balance), child support.
5. **Credit history** — ask directly: "Anything on your credit file I should know
   about — defaults, late payments, hardship?" Surprises kill deals late.
6. **What matters to them** — lowest repayment, offset/redraw, fixed certainty,
   extra repayments, speed.
Then give a straight read: "On what you've told me, you look serviceable at
around $X; the thing to firm up is Y."

## 3. Rate vs cost conversation
- Compare on **total cost**, not headline rate: the comparison rate / APR,
  upfront and ongoing fees, cashback, and the cost of features they'll actually use.
- Show 2–3 options in a one-page table (rate, comparison rate/APR, monthly
  repayment, fees, key features, why it suits). Fewer options close faster.
- Fixed vs variable: explain break costs on fixed in plain words; never predict rates.
- Price matching: know your pricing authority before the call and ask for it
  internally once, with the competitor's written offer in hand.

## 4. From "yes" to a complete application
- Send a **single checklist** the same day (ID, payslips, bank statements, tax
  returns, contract of sale, existing loan statements). Give a due date.
- Chase on a fixed cadence (day 2, day 4, day 7). Most stalls are one missing
  document — name it specifically.
- Pre-assess before lodging: run serviceability on the lender calculator with
  buffered rates; resolve red flags (undisclosed debts, gambling transactions,
  irregular income) before submission, not after a decline.
- Lodge, then tell the borrower the next milestone and when you'll update them.

## 5. Refinance win-back (retention)
- Trigger a call when a fixed term is ~90 days from expiry, a discharge request
  arrives, or rate checks show the client is >0.5% above new-customer pricing.
- Open with the facts: "You're on X; new customers get Y; I can get you to Z."
  Retention pricing is usually cheaper than losing the loan.

## Gotchas
- Never quote approval as certain — "pre-approval" is conditional on valuation
  and full assessment. Put that in writing.
- Credit-card limits and BNPL accounts quietly destroy serviceability; ask.
- Multiple hard credit enquiries in a short window can hurt the file; don't
  lodge with several lenders "to see".
- Keep notes of *why* you recommended the product — many regimes require it.

## Verify
- [ ] Every new lead had first contact attempted within the hour.
- [ ] Qualifying notes cover purpose, LVR, income type, commitments, credit, priorities.
- [ ] Options were compared on comparison rate/APR and fees, not rate alone.
- [ ] One document checklist sent with a due date; chase dates diarised.
- [ ] Recommendation reasons recorded; conditional nature of pre-approval disclosed.
