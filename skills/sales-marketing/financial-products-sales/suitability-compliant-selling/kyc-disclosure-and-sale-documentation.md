# KYC, disclosure and sale documentation — selling inside the rules

**Goal:** make every financial-product sale one you can defend: the client was
known, the product suited them, the required disclosures were given, the
contact was lawful, and the file proves it. The *principles* below are common
across regimes; the *exact* rules are not — US Reg BI and FINRA 2111, UK COBS and
Consumer Duty, EU MiFID II, Australia's best-interest duty and anti-hawking
rules (Corporations Act s992A) all differ. **Status draft:** confirm every
specific against your own compliance manual; nothing here is legal advice.

## 1. Know your client — inside the sales conversation
Weave these into discovery rather than reading a form at the end:
- **Identity and source of funds** (AML/KYC) — firm-approved ID process.
- **Financial situation** — income, assets, liabilities, emergency savings.
- **Objectives and time horizon** — what the money is for and when it's needed.
- **Risk tolerance and capacity for loss** — two different things: a client can
  *want* risk but be unable to *afford* a loss.
- **Knowledge and experience** of the product type (needed for appropriateness
  tests on complex products in many regimes).
- **Vulnerability indicators** — bereavement, illness, cognitive decline,
  language barriers, pressure from a third party. Slow down, involve a
  trusted person, escalate per policy.

## 2. Suitability test before you recommend
Ask three questions and write the answers:
1. Does the product meet the stated objective and horizon?
2. Can the client bear the risks and costs, including worst case?
3. Is there a reasonably available alternative that is clearly better (cheaper,
   simpler) for them? If yes, why aren't you recommending it?
If you can't answer (3) honestly, don't make the sale.

## 3. Disclosure scripts
- Use the firm's approved script verbatim for mandated statements (capacity in
  which you act, who pays you, fees and commissions, conflicts, cooling-off rights,
  key risks). Ad-libbing mandated wording is a common audit finding.
- Deliver key documents (KID/KIID, PDS, Form CRS, fee schedule) **before** the
  client commits, and record when and how.
- Check understanding with an open question: "Can you tell me in your own words
  what happens to your money if the market falls 20%?"

## 4. Cold calling and unsolicited contact
- Check do-not-call registries and your internal suppression list before every
  campaign (e.g. US TCPA/DNC, UK TPS, Australian DNCR). Respect calling-hour limits.
- Some products may not be sold off an unsolicited call at all (e.g. Australia's
  anti-hawking regime for retail financial products). If the client didn't
  request contact about that product, offer information and book a later
  meeting at their request, rather than selling on the call.
- Identify yourself and the firm at the start; stop immediately on "not interested".

## 5. The file note (write it the same day)
Include: date, attendees, how contact started (solicited or not), KYC facts,
objectives in the client's words, options considered and rejected with reasons,
the recommendation, risks and fees disclosed, documents provided, client
questions and answers, and next steps. Write it so a stranger reading it in three
years would reach the same recommendation.

## Gotchas
- "The client insisted" doesn't cure an unsuitable sale; document an
  execution-only or insistent-client process if your firm permits one.
- Recorded calls are evidence for *both* sides — script accordingly.
- Incentive schemes tied to product volume are a red flag regulators look for.
- Switching or replacement sales (moving a client out of an existing product)
  usually carry extra comparison and disclosure duties.

## Verify
- [ ] KYC captured both risk tolerance and capacity for loss.
- [ ] Suitability questions 1–3 answered in writing, including the alternative.
- [ ] Mandated disclosures given verbatim and documents delivered before commitment.
- [ ] Contact was solicited or lawful under DNC/anti-hawking rules.
- [ ] Same-day file note a third party could follow.
