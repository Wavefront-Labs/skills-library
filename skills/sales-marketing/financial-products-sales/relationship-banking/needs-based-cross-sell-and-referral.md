# Needs-based cross-sell and specialist referral

**Goal:** deepen each client relationship (more of their banking with you, lower
attrition) by finding real needs and either meeting them or handing them to a
specialist who closes — without the product-pushing that has landed banks in
regulatory trouble (the US fake-accounts scandals are the cautionary tale).

## 1. Tier the book
Export your portfolio and sort by revenue (or balances if revenue isn't
available). Typical split:
- **Tier A** (top ~20% by value): proactive contact quarterly, full review yearly.
- **Tier B**: twice-yearly contact, review every 1–2 years.
- **Tier C**: digital/automated contact, respond to triggers only.
Add a column for **products held** and one for **obvious gaps** (salary not
credited here, no savings account, business client with no merchant facility,
loan elsewhere visible in statements).

## 2. Watch for trigger events
Needs appear around events, not on your sales calendar. Set alerts or a weekly
scan for:
- large deposit or a big outgoing transfer (property, investment elsewhere);
- salary change, new employer, regular payments to another lender;
- business: sudden turnover growth, new payroll, overdraft hard on limit,
  card payments to a competitor's merchant service;
- life events mentioned in conversation: baby, marriage, inheritance, retirement, sale of business.
Call within a week with a specific reason: "I noticed a large deposit — happy to
talk about where it earns most, no pressure."

## 3. The portfolio review
Agenda (30–45 minutes, sent beforehand):
1. What's changed for you/your business this year?
2. Plans for the next 12 months (purchases, hires, expansion, retirement).
3. Walk through current products — fees, rates, usage. Fix anything wrong first;
   it earns the right to talk about more.
4. Gaps you've identified, framed as questions: "Where do your customers pay you
   by card today?"
5. Agreed actions with owners and dates.

## 4. Warm referral to specialists
The handoff is where most cross-sell leaks:
- Qualify enough to make it worth the specialist's time: need, timing, rough
  size, decision maker.
- Get permission: "Would it help if I introduced you to <name>, who does this
  every day?"
- Do a **three-way introduction** (call or email) with context so the client
  doesn't repeat themselves. Book the meeting before you hang up if you can.
- Log the referral in the CRM with the expected value; follow up with both
  sides after the meeting. Specialists keep working with RMs whose referrals convert.

## 5. Retention
Attrition signals: falling balances, salary credit stops, direct debits moving
out, complaints, a new relationship manager change. Call — don't email — within
days. Ask what's prompting the change and what it would take to stay; escalate
pricing exceptions with a written competitor offer.

## Gotchas
- Products-per-customer targets drive bad behaviour; only sell what the client
  will use. Opened-but-unused accounts are a conduct risk, not a win.
- Respect product boundaries: RMs often can't give investment or insurance
  advice — refer instead of improvising.
- Don't let tier C clients feel abandoned: a broken service fix beats a cross-sell call.
- Document the identified need for each sale; reviewers ask for it.

## Verify
- [ ] Book tiered with a contact cadence and a gaps column.
- [ ] Trigger-event scan runs weekly and leads to calls within a week.
- [ ] Reviews follow an agenda that fixes existing issues before new products.
- [ ] Referrals are three-way intros, logged, and followed up.
- [ ] Every product sold has a documented need; no unused accounts opened.
