# Adviser roadshows and platform placement — institutional distribution

**Goal:** get a fund or financial product bought at scale through the people who
choose for others — advice networks, platforms, investment consultants and
institutional allocators. You rarely sell to the end investor; you sell
*access* (being on the list) and then *conviction* (advisers choosing you
from the list).

## 1. Map the gatekeepers, in order
The usual chain (names vary by market):
1. **Research/ratings** — fund research houses and rating agencies. Many advice
   groups can only use rated products.
2. **Platforms / wraps** — the product must be on the platform menu for advisers
   to transact.
3. **Approved product lists (APLs)** of dealer groups/networks, set by their
   investment committee.
4. **Investment consultants** for institutional money (pension funds,
   endowments) — they shortlist managers for their clients.
5. **Advisers** who actually allocate.
Build a spreadsheet per channel: gatekeeper, decision process, review cycle,
contact, status, next action. Missing step 1–3 makes step 5 pointless.

## 2. Getting listed
- Keep a **due-diligence pack** ready: standard DDQ answers, investment process
  description, team bios and tenure, risk management, track record (GIPS-compliant
  if you claim it), fees, capacity, operational/ESG policies.
- Respond to research house and consultant RFPs on time, answering the question
  asked; generic marketing copy scores badly.
- Learn each APL's review calendar — submitting a month after the committee met
  can cost a year.
- After listing, confirm the product is actually *visible and transactable*
  (correct share class, minimums, model portfolio inclusion).

## 3. The adviser roadshow
Advisers attend for education and CPD hours, not pitches. Format:
- 45–60 minutes: a market or technical topic of genuine use (sequence risk,
  tax changes, portfolio construction) with your product as one worked example,
  not the whole talk. Check whether the session can be accredited for CPD.
- Cities/venues sequenced to minimise travel; 10–30 advisers per session.
- Pre-event: personal invites from the BDM to the top 30% of each territory by
  current or potential flows.
- Post-event within 48 hours: slides, a one-page product summary, and a call to
  book a one-to-one. Track attendees → meetings → first flows.

## 4. Working the territory
- Segment advisers by flows (current and potential); focus 70% of time on the
  top segment, maintain the rest digitally.
- In one-to-ones ask how they build portfolios and where a gap exists; position
  the product into their model, not as a standalone idea.
- Report weekly: meetings, new advisers using the product, net flows, redemptions
  and reasons.

## Gotchas
- Wholesale/institutional material may not be suitable for retail audiences —
  marking and distribution controls matter (product governance and target-market
  rules in several regimes).
- Performance claims must match approved, compliant figures; a slide with
  unapproved numbers can sink a relationship with compliance-heavy networks.
- Losing a rating or being removed from an APL drives redemptions quickly;
  keep researchers informed of team or process changes *before* they hear elsewhere.
- **Uncertain:** listing timelines and fee-for-rating practices vary a lot by
  market; confirm with your distribution head.
