# CMA and the listing presentation

**Goal:** leave the seller's kitchen table with a signed agency agreement at a
price the property can actually achieve and a fee you're proud of. Overpriced
listings won to flatter the seller go stale, cost you marketing and reputation,
and sell for less in the end. Agency agreements, disclosure and commission rules
differ by country and state — **status draft:** check yours (e.g. US practice
changed after the 2024 NAR settlement).

## 1. Before the appointment
- Pull the property's records: title/ownership, land size, last sale price and
  date, zoning, building approvals.
- Drive the street; note condition of neighbours and anything a buyer will notice.
- Ask the seller on the phone: why selling, timing, where they're moving, who
  else they're talking to, what they think it's worth.

## 2. Build the CMA
Pick comparables:
- **Sold** in the last 3–6 months (stretch to 12 in slow markets), same
  neighbourhood/school zone, similar type, size (± ~20%), beds/baths, condition.
- 3–6 best comps; plus **active** listings (your competition) and **expired/withdrawn**
  (what the market rejected).
Adjust each comp for differences (extra bathroom, garage, renovated kitchen, lot
size, view) using local norms, and note your reasoning. The result is a **range**,
not a single number. Add days-on-market and list-to-sale price ratios for the area.

## 3. Presentation structure (45–60 minutes)
1. **Walk through first**, asking questions; let them tell you what they love.
2. **Their goals**: price, timing, minimal disruption, privacy.
3. **Market evidence**: comps on a map with photos; active competition; what
   buyers are paying now.
4. **Price recommendation**: a range and a strategy (list price, price guide,
   auction, offers-over — depends on market/region).
5. **Marketing plan**: photography, floor plan, video, portals, signboard,
   database, open homes, timeline (week by week to sale).
6. **Preparation**: 3–5 high-impact fixes (declutter, paint, garden, minor repairs, staging).
7. **Your evidence**: recent results nearby, list-to-sale ratio, average days on
   market, testimonials.
8. **Fee and agreement**, then ask for the listing.

## 4. The pricing conversation
- Let the comps do the talking: "Here's what buyers paid for these three. Yours
  sits between them because…"
- When their number is higher: "If we list at that, buyers will compare it with
  these active homes at that level — here's what they offer." Show the cost of
  overpricing: longer days on market, price reductions, lower final price.
- Agree a review point: "If we've had fewer than X enquiries or no offers in 3
  weeks, we'll review the price."

## 5. Defending your commission
- Never discount on first ask; ask what's driving it.
- Tie fee to net result: a better agent achieving 2% more on a $800k home is
  $16k — more than most fee differences.
- Trade if you must: lower fee for a sole/exclusive agency, a longer term, the
  seller paying marketing upfront, or a tiered fee (higher % above a target price).

## 6. Vendor management after listing
Weekly report: enquiries, inspections, feedback themes (verbatim), price
opinions from buyers, portal views, recommended action. Call — don't just email.
Bad news early is how you earn the price conversation later.

## Gotchas
- Using asking prices instead of sold prices as comps.
- Quoting a price to "buy" the listing — illegal underquoting/overquoting rules exist
  in some markets (e.g. Victoria and NSW in Australia).
- Forgetting disclosure duties (material facts, defects) in the presentation.

## Verify
- [ ] 3–6 adjusted sold comps plus actives and expireds; range stated.
- [ ] Seller's goals recorded before the price talk.
- [ ] Marketing plan with week-by-week timeline and prep list delivered.
- [ ] Fee held or traded for something; agreement signed.
- [ ] Weekly vendor report scheduled with a price-review trigger.
