# Running an open home that converts

**Goal:** turn foot traffic into offers for this property — and into leads for
your next sale. An open home where the agent stands at the door and hands out
brochures wastes most of its value.

## 1. Prepare the property (day before and hour before)
Give the seller a checklist:
- Declutter surfaces, remove personal photos and valuables (and prescription
  medicines), lock away keys and documents.
- Clean thoroughly — kitchen, bathrooms, windows; fix small visible defects.
- Lights on in every room, blinds open, comfortable temperature, fresh air (no
  strong scents).
- Pets and owners out. Cars off the driveway.
- Garden: mowed, edges trimmed, entrance swept, front door clean.
Arrive 20–30 minutes early: walk through, check lights, place signage
(directional signs at nearby corners, where permitted), set out brochures with
floor plan and key facts.

## 2. Register every visitor
- Name, phone, email, and "buying, selling or both?" Use a tablet app or the
  agency CRM sign-in; paper if necessary.
- Many jurisdictions have privacy rules on collecting and using this data —
  state what it's for. Some also require ID or registration for safety reasons.
- Ask a qualifying question while registering: "Have you seen many in the area?"
  "Do you have a place to sell?"

## 3. Work the room
- Greet everyone, give a 20-second overview (best features, what's unique), then let
  them look — buyers want space.
- Position yourself centrally (kitchen/living area) where you can see movement
  and be found.
- If two agents: one at entry, one inside.
- Answer questions with facts; if you don't know, note it and get back to them.
- Avoid statements you can't back up (on price, zoning, building condition) —
  misrepresentation risk.

## 4. Read buyer signals
Strong interest:
- long stays, returning to a room, measuring or photographing;
- asking about settlement dates, inclusions, council rates/HOA fees, offers received;
- couples discussing furniture placement;
- second visit, or bringing a parent/builder.
Weak interest: quick walk-through, focusing on faults with no follow-up questions.
Ask open questions on the way out: "What did you think?", "How does it compare to
others you've seen?", "Where would you see the value?" Record each answer.

## 5. Follow up within 24 hours
- Call every registered buyer (not just the keen ones): thank them, ask feedback,
  ask what price they'd consider, ask if they want a second inspection or a
  contract/disclosure pack.
- Tag in CRM: hot / warm / cold, plus what they're looking for — future buyer for
  other listings.
- Sellers among visitors = appraisal leads; book a market appraisal.

## 6. Report to the vendor
Same day or next morning: number of groups, feedback themes (verbatim quotes),
buyer price indications, second-inspection requests, next steps. Honest feedback
supports later pricing conversations.

## Private showings
Same principles, one-on-one: confirm identity and qualification beforehand,
meet them there, let them lead the walk, debrief immediately afterward.

## Safety
Tell the office where you are and the time; work in pairs when possible; keep
your phone and car keys on you; don't go into confined spaces (basement, garage)
with a stranger ahead of you blocking the exit.

## Verify
- [ ] Seller prep checklist done; property checked 20+ minutes before.
- [ ] Every group registered with contact details and buying/selling status.
- [ ] Signals and feedback recorded per group.
- [ ] All registrants called within 24 hours and tagged in CRM.
- [ ] Vendor report sent with verbatim feedback and next steps.
