# Tenant qualification and incentive negotiation

**Goal:** fill the space with a tenant who will pay reliably for the full term,
at the best **net effective rent** for the owner — and keep good tenants at
renewal. Residential tenancy law (screening limits, deposits, rent increases,
anti-discrimination) is strict and local; commercial leasing is more freely
negotiated. **Status draft:** check your jurisdiction's rules before applying steps.

## 1. Prospecting tenants
- **Residential**: portal listing with good photos and floor plan, clear rent and
  availability date, group or private viewings, quick application process.
- **Commercial**: target by use — who needs this size, location and fit-out?
  Approach tenants in nearby buildings with leases expiring in 12–24 months
  (lease-expiry data from your database or providers), tenant-rep brokers, signage,
  and businesses outgrowing current space.

## 2. Qualify
Residential (within the law; criteria applied identically to every applicant):
- Identity, income (common rule of thumb: rent ≤ ~30% of gross income, or income
  ≥ ~2.5–3× rent — **uncertain, varies by market**), employment, rental history and
  references, tenancy-database checks where lawful.
- Never screen on protected characteristics (race, religion, family status,
  disability, etc.); some places ban source-of-income discrimination too.
Commercial:
- **Covenant strength**: financial statements (2–3 years), trading history,
  business plan for start-ups, parent company or director **personal guarantees**.
- Use fits zoning/permitted use and the building (loading, hours, exhaust, power).
- Security: deposit or **bank guarantee** typically 3–6 months' rent for weaker covenants.

## 3. Face rent vs incentives
Landlords often protect the **face rent** (headline, used for valuations and
future reviews) and give incentives instead: rent-free months, fit-out
contribution, reduced rent early on. Compare offers on **net effective rent**:
```
Net effective rent ≈ (Total rent over term − value of incentives) ÷ term
```
Example: 5-year lease at $100k/yr face = $500k; 6 months free ($50k) + $50k
fit-out contribution → ($500k − $100k) ÷ 5 = **$80k/yr effective** (20% incentive).
More rigorous: discount cash flows (present value). Know the owner's minimum
effective rent and whether they prefer to protect face rent.

## 4. Negotiating heads of terms
Agree before lawyers draft: parties, premises, term and options to renew,
commencement and rent-start dates, rent and review mechanism (fixed %, CPI,
market review), outgoings/service charges, incentives (and clawback if the tenant
leaves early), security, permitted use, fit-out and make-good obligations,
assignment/subletting, break clauses. Trade:
- longer term ↔ bigger incentive;
- stronger covenant/guarantee ↔ lower security;
- tenant fit-out works ↔ rent-free during fit-out.

## 5. Rent reviews and renewals
- Diarise reviews and option dates 6–12 months ahead (commercial options usually
  require notice within a strict window).
- Market reviews: prepare evidence (recent comparable leases on an effective basis).
- Renewal: start 6–9 months out for commercial, 2–3 months for residential;
  a vacancy (lost rent + re-letting costs + new incentive) usually costs more than a
  modest concession to keep a good tenant.
- Residential increases: follow statutory notice periods and frequency limits.

## Gotchas
- Comparing offers on face rent alone.
- Inconsistent screening of applicants = discrimination risk.
- Missing an option-exercise or review deadline can lose the right.
- Make-good clauses vague at heads of terms → disputes at lease end.

## Verify
- [ ] Qualification criteria written and applied identically; lawful checks only.
- [ ] Commercial covenant assessed with financials and appropriate security.
- [ ] Offers compared on net effective rent.
- [ ] Heads of terms cover term, rent, reviews, incentives with clawback, security and make-good.
- [ ] Review and renewal dates diarised with lead time.
