# Geographic farm and expired-listing plan

**Goal:** never depend on luck for next quarter's listings. Prospecting is
the agent's equivalent of planting: the work done now produces appraisals in
3–12 months. Pick a few channels and work them consistently.

## 1. Choose a farm area
- **Size**: ~500–2,000 homes you can realistically touch monthly.
- **Turnover rate** = sales in last 12 months ÷ total homes. Aim for ≥ ~5–6%;
  below that, there aren't enough sales to win (**uncertain:** a common rule of
  thumb, not a standard).
- **Competition**: is one agent dominant? A fragmented market is easier to enter.
- **Average price** that suits your business and commission.
- Ideally somewhere you live or know well.
Projected listings ≈ homes × turnover × your target share. 1,000 homes × 6% × 15%
share ≈ 9 listings/year — set expectations accordingly; share takes 1–3 years to build.

## 2. Farm touch plan (12 months)
At least one touch per month, mixed:
- Monthly market update (letterbox or mail): recent sales, prices, days on market —
  useful information, not just "I'm an agent".
- "Just listed / just sold" cards within 48 hours of each event in the area.
- Door-knocking around each listing and sale ("We just sold #12; we have buyers who
  missed out — are you or any neighbours thinking of selling?").
- Community presence: local events, school/sports sponsorship, a local
  social-media group with genuine content.
- Quarterly phone calls where lawful (check do-not-call registers).
Log every touch and response in the CRM by address.

## 3. Sphere of influence (SOI)
Past clients, friends, family, contacts. They are the cheapest source of
listings and referrals.
- Load everyone into the CRM with tags (past client, purchase date, referral source).
- Touch plan: e.g. "33 touches a year" style programs — mix of calls (4+/year),
  market updates, home-purchase anniversary, birthday, useful annual items (tax
  depreciation reminder, local events).
- Ask for referrals explicitly and specifically: "Who do you know who's talked
  about moving this year?"
- Thank every referral (handwritten note, small gift where lawful and disclosed).

## 4. Expired and withdrawn listings
- Daily: pull expired/withdrawn listings from MLS or portal data in your area.
- Research before calling: price history, days on market, photos, marketing
  quality — find the specific reason it didn't sell (price, presentation, marketing,
  access).
- Approach (call or visit where lawful, letter otherwise):
  "Your home was on the market for 94 days. I looked at why it might not have
  sold — would it help if I shared what I found?" Bring a short analysis, not a pitch.
- Don't criticise the previous agent; focus on what you'd do differently.
- Check the listing has genuinely expired (no exclusive agreement still in force)
  before soliciting.
- FSBO (for sale by owner): offer buyer leads or a free appraisal; follow up weekly.

## 5. Measure the pipeline
Weekly: contacts made, conversations, appraisals booked, appraisals done, listings
won. Track source for each listing to see which channel earns its time.

## Gotchas
- Inconsistent farming (3 months then stop) wastes the investment.
- Do-not-call, spam, privacy and door-to-door rules apply to prospecting — check locally.
- Generic marketing without local data is ignored.
- Soliciting a property still under exclusive agency may breach professional rules.

## Verify
- [ ] Farm chosen with turnover ≥ target and size you can touch monthly.
- [ ] 12-month touch calendar mixing mail, door, digital and calls.
- [ ] SOI loaded and tagged in CRM with a touch plan and referral asks.
- [ ] Expireds researched and approached daily, after confirming expiry.
- [ ] Weekly pipeline metrics by source.
