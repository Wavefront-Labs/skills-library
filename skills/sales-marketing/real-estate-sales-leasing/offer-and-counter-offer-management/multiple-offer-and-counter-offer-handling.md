# Multiple-offer and counter-offer handling

**Goal:** as the listing agent, get the seller the best combination of price
and certainty, through a process buyers see as fair, and convert verbal
agreement into a binding contract before it slips. Contract forms, cooling-off
periods, deposit norms and rules on disclosing other offers differ heavily by
jurisdiction — **status draft:** check yours before relying on any step.

## 1. Receive offers in writing
Ask for every offer on the local standard form (or in writing): price, deposit,
conditions/contingencies and their deadlines, settlement/closing date,
inclusions/exclusions, buyer's finance evidence (pre-approval letter), expiry of
the offer. Verbal offers are information, not offers.

## 2. Compare on net value and certainty
Build a comparison table for the seller:
| | Offer A | Offer B |
|---|---|---|
| Price | $812k | $805k |
| Deposit | 5% | 10% |
| Finance condition | 21 days | none (cash) |
| Inspection condition | 14 days | 7 days |
| Sale of their home | yes | no |
| Settlement | 90 days | 45 days (seller wanted 60) |
| Pre-approval seen | yes | proof of funds |
Talk through risk: a higher price subject to the sale of another home may be worth
less than a lower, unconditional one. Let the seller decide; record advice.

## 3. Run a multiple-offer process
When there are 2+ serious buyers:
- Tell every interested party that multiple offers exist (as local rules allow)
  and give the **same** deadline and instructions ("best and final by 5 pm
  Thursday, on the form, with finance evidence").
- Don't disclose one buyer's offer details to another unless the seller instructs
  and the law permits.
- Options for the seller: accept the best; counter one buyer; or send **multiple
  counter-offers** (where permitted — make clear the seller can only be bound by one,
  often requiring seller's final acceptance).
- Escalation clauses (buyer offers $X above any competing offer up to a cap) —
  require proof of the competing offer; check legality and form locally.

## 4. Counter-offers
- Counter on what matters to the seller: price, conditions shortened, deposit, dates.
- Keep it simple — change 1–3 terms; complex counters stall.
- Set a short expiry (e.g. 24 hours) and track it.
- Coach both sides on movement: "They've come up $10k and shortened finance to
  14 days; I think there's another $5k if we can give them a 60-day settlement."

## 5. From agreement to exchange/binding contract
- Get signatures fast — send contracts for e-signature same day; slow paperwork is
  where gazumping (seller accepting a higher late offer) or buyer's remorse happens
  in markets where agreement isn't binding until exchange.
- Collect the deposit per contract terms; confirm receipt.
- Diarise every condition deadline; chase reports (finance, inspection) days before.
- Back-up offers: keep the runner-up warm (in writing as a back-up offer where allowed).

## 6. When conditions bite
Inspection finds defects → get the report, ask for the buyer's specific request,
cost it (quotes), and negotiate a price reduction, credit or repair. Keep
the seller calm and the deal moving; the alternative is relisting.

## Gotchas
- Inventing competing offers to push a buyer is unethical and illegal in many places.
- Losing track of offer expiry times — an expired acceptance isn't an acceptance.
- Contract terms the agent writes beyond the standard form = practising law; use
  solicitors/conveyancers.
- Fiduciary duty runs to your client (usually the seller); treat buyers honestly
  but don't advise them against the seller's interest.

## Verify
- [ ] Every offer in writing with price, deposit, conditions, dates and finance evidence.
- [ ] Comparison table given to the seller with risk discussed and advice recorded.
- [ ] Same deadline and instructions given to all buyers in a multiple-offer process.
- [ ] Counter-offers simple with short expiries.
- [ ] Contracts signed, deposit received and all condition dates diarised.
