# Buyer qualification to settlement

**Goal:** get your buyer the right property at a fair price with no nasty
surprises after they move in — and spend your time on buyers who are ready,
able and committed. Practices differ widely: US buyer agents now generally need
a **written buyer agreement before touring** (post-2024 NAR settlement
practice); in the UK and much of Australia, buyer's agents are a paid
specialist service. **Status draft:** follow your local law and forms.

## 1. Qualify the buyer (first conversation)
- **Motivation and timing**: why buy, why now, deadline (lease end, school term)?
- **Finance**: lender pre-approval in hand (amount, conditions, expiry)? Cash?
  Need to sell first? Send them to a lender/broker before touring if not.
- **Decision makers**: everyone who must agree, present for key viewings.
- **Experience**: first-time buyers need more process explanation.
- **Commitment**: are they working with other agents? Agree terms in writing
  (agreement, fee, duration, who pays).

## 2. The brief
Write it down and get it confirmed:
- Must-haves (non-negotiable) vs nice-to-haves; dealbreakers.
- Areas/school zones, commute limits, property type, beds/baths, parking, outdoor space.
- Budget **including** purchase costs (transfer/stamp duty, legal, inspections,
  lender fees, moving) — buyers often forget these.
Revisit after the first 5–10 viewings; real preferences emerge.

## 3. Search and shortlist
- Set saved searches/alerts on MLS/portals; tap off-market sources (agent
  networks, pre-market listings).
- Shortlist with a short note per property: fit to brief, concerns, price
  evidence (recent comparable sales).
- Fewer, better viewings: 3–6 per session, planned route, 20–30 minutes each.

## 4. Showings
- Point out what a buyer misses: water stains, cracking, drainage, roof age, noise,
  orientation/light, neighbouring developments.
- After each, rate against the brief while it's fresh.
- Second viewing at a different time of day before any offer.

## 5. Offer advice
- Run a mini-CMA (see cma-and-listing-presentation) to set a value range.
- Gauge seller motivation and competition from the listing agent: days on
  market, other offers, settlement preferences.
- Recommend price **and** terms: deposit size, conditions/contingencies (finance,
  building/pest inspection, appraisal, sale of their home), settlement date,
  inclusions. Strong clean terms can beat a slightly higher price.
- Agree the buyer's walk-away before submitting. In auctions, set bidding limits in advance.

## 6. Conditions to settlement
Timeline with dates and owners:
- Inspections booked immediately (building, pest, strata/HOA records, survey).
- Finance formal approval and lender valuation/appraisal.
- Legal/conveyancing review of contract and title; searches.
- Negotiate repairs or price adjustments from inspection findings within the
  condition period.
- Pre-settlement/final walk-through: inclusions present, agreed repairs done.
- Keep the buyer updated weekly; missed condition deadlines can make the contract
  unconditional or let the seller terminate.

## Gotchas
- Showing homes above the pre-approval amount creates heartbreak.
- Advising on legal matters or structural issues beyond your expertise — refer.
- Dual agency/representation conflicts: disclose and follow local rules.
- Letting a condition date pass unnoticed.

## Verify
- [ ] Buyer has pre-approval and a signed agreement before touring (where required).
- [ ] Written brief with must-haves, areas and total budget including costs.
- [ ] Offers backed by comparable sales and include recommended conditions.
- [ ] Condition deadlines in a dated timeline with owners.
- [ ] Final walk-through completed before settlement.
