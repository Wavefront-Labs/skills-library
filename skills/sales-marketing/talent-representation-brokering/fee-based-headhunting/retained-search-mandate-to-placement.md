# Retained search: from mandate to placement

**Goal:** turn a hiring need into a signed fee agreement, a strong shortlist and an
accepted offer that sticks past the guarantee period.

Fee levels quoted are common market ranges, not fixed rates; they vary by country,
seniority and sector. Recruitment is regulated in some jurisdictions (agency licensing,
data-protection rules for candidate data) — confirm what applies to you.

## 1. Win the mandate
- **Intake meeting** with the hiring manager (not just HR): why the role exists, what
  success looks like at 12 months, must-haves vs nice-to-haves, salary band, the
  interview process and who decides, why previous hires failed.
- Push back on unrealistic specs early: "Three of these five must-haves together are rare
  at this salary — which two matter most?"
- Choose the model:
  | Model | Fee basis (typical) | When |
  |---|---|---|
  | Contingency | % of first-year base (often ~15–25%) on hire only | volume / mid-level |
  | Retained | similar or higher %, paid in stages (e.g. thirds: start, shortlist, hire) | senior / confidential / hard to fill |
  | Exclusive contingency | contingency with a time-boxed exclusive | middle ground |
- **Terms of business** signed before you present anyone: fee %, what it's calculated on
  (base only? guaranteed bonus?), payment terms, guarantee/rebate (replacement or partial
  refund if the hire leaves within e.g. 3–6 months), ownership period for introduced
  candidates, off-limits agreement (you won't poach from this client).

## 2. Source
- Map the market: list target companies, then target titles in them.
- Boolean search (LinkedIn, job boards, search engines), e.g.
  `("head of finance" OR "finance director") AND (SaaS OR software) NOT recruiter`
- Referrals: ask strong people "who's the best you've worked with?"
- Respect your off-limits list — never approach staff of current clients.

## 3. Sell the opportunity to candidates
Lead with *their* motive, not the job ad: ask what would make them move (scope, money,
location, boss, growth). Then match the role to it honestly. Qualify hard on salary
expectations, notice period, and other processes they're in.

## 4. Shortlist and present
- Typically 3–5 candidates, each with a one-page write-up: fit against the must-haves,
  motivation, compensation now and expected, notice, risks.
- Get candidate consent before sending their CV anywhere; record it.
- Debrief both sides within 24 h after every interview.

## 5. Broker the offer
- Pre-close the candidate before the offer: "If they offer X with Y start date, will you
  accept?" Get a yes or the real objection.
- Pre-close the client: tell them the number that will land.
- Deliver the offer verbally, then in writing from the client; handle the gap in one
  round if possible.
- **Counteroffer prep**: before resignation, ask the candidate what they'll say when their
  employer counters. Remind them why they decided to move.

## 6. After placement
Check in at start date, week 1, month 1 and before the guarantee ends. Invoice on start
date per terms. Ask the client for the next mandate.

## Gotchas
- Two agencies sending the same CV — the ownership clause and timestamped submissions decide.
- Fee on "first-year compensation" vs "base salary" can differ by a lot; define it.
- Presenting candidates without consent damages trust and may breach data rules.
- Skipping backchannel references on senior hires; do them with permission.

## Verify
- [ ] Signed terms of business before the first CV is sent.
- [ ] Written spec with ranked must-haves agreed with the hiring manager.
- [ ] Candidate consent logged for every submission.
- [ ] Candidate pre-closed and counteroffer discussed before resignation.
- [ ] Guarantee-period check-ins scheduled in the calendar.
