# Submission packages and pitch calls — selling a client to buyers

**Goal:** get a buyer (publisher, casting director, club, promoter, employer) to say
"send it over" and then "let's talk terms", by matching the right client to the right
buyer with a package they can judge in two minutes.

The method is the same across literary, acting, music, sports and model agencies; the
package contents differ (manuscript vs reel vs stats). Industry norms below are typical,
not universal — check your market's conventions (unconfirmed for every territory).

## 1. Position the client before you pitch anyone
Write three lines you can say from memory:
- **What they are, in buyer language**: "a 45-minute clean comedy headliner", "a
  left-footed centre-back, 23, two seasons of top-flight minutes", "upmarket book-club
  fiction, 95k words".
- **Comps / comparables**: 2–3 recent, successful, *similar-sized* comparables
  ("for readers of X and Y" — titles from the last ~3–5 years, not all-time megahits).
- **Why now**: an award, a viral clip, contract expiry, a trend the buyer is chasing.
If you can't write these, you aren't ready to submit — the buyer will ask exactly this.

## 2. Build the buyer list (quality over spray)
Spreadsheet columns: buyer · person · what they've bought recently · fit reason ·
submission rule (exclusive? simultaneous? agented-only?) · date sent · status · next action.
- Research each person's *recent* acquisitions or castings; pitch individuals, not imprints
  or generic inboxes.
- Tier the list: A (best fit, best terms), B, C. Go out to tier A first in one wave so you
  can create competition and a deadline; hold B/C for a second round.
- Never send the same project to two people at the same buying house unless their rules
  allow it — it's a common way to burn a relationship.

## 3. The submission package
Keep the front page to **one page**; everything else is attached or linked.
- Subject / header: `Submission: <TITLE / CLIENT> — <one-line hook> (<format/position>)`.
- Pitch paragraph (≤150 words): hook, positioning line, comps, why this buyer.
- Credentials: 3–5 bullets of proof (sales, stats, credits, awards, audience numbers).
- Materials: manuscript/sample, showreel (best 60–90 s first), highlight reel, EPK,
  headshots/comp card, stats sheet. Use view-tracked links where possible so you know
  if it was opened; check links work in a private browser window before sending.
- Availability / status line: rights available, contract situation, dates free.

## 4. The pitch call (90 seconds, then listen)
1. Permission + reason: "Got two minutes? I have something that fits your X list."
2. Positioning line and comps.
3. One proof point that matters to *this* buyer.
4. Ask: "Can I send it today?" or "Would you see them for the role?"
5. Then ask what they're looking for now — log it; it feeds the next pitch.
Don't oversell. Buyers remember agents whose submissions match the description.

## 5. Follow-up and creating a decision
- Follow up once after a reasonable read window (often 2–6 weeks for manuscripts, days
  for casting/bookings), with *new* information if you have it.
- When one buyer shows interest, tell the others on the same wave: "We have interest; I'd
  like to hear from everyone by <date>." Only say this when it's true.
- Log every pass with the reason. Three passes for the same reason = fix the package or
  the positioning, not the list.

## Gotchas
- Pitching a client to a buyer who just bought something too similar — check recent deals.
- Attachments blocked by corporate filters; many buyers prefer links or a pasted sample.
- Exclusivity: if a buyer asked for an exclusive look, honour the window in writing.
- Your client must approve the list — some have buyers they refuse to work with.

## Verify
- [ ] Positioning line, comps and "why now" written and client-approved.
- [ ] Buyer list has a named person and a fit reason for every row.
- [ ] Package front page fits on one page; all links open in a private window.
- [ ] Tracker shows date sent, status and next action for every submission.
- [ ] Every pass has a logged reason, reviewed after each wave.
