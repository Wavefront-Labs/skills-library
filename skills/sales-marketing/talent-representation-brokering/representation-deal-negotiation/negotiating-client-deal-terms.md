# Negotiating client deal terms

**Goal:** close the best *total* deal the client will actually sign — not just the
biggest headline number — and know in advance when to walk away.

Applies to book, talent, music-booking, sports and model deals. Commission rates and
standard clauses named here are common market norms, not rules; they vary by country,
union agreements and sport governing body (e.g. agent-fee caps), so confirm locally.

## 1. Prepare with the client (before the first call)
Build a **term sheet grid** — one row per term, columns: *ideal · target · walk-away ·
priority (1–3) · tradeable?*
Typical rows:
- **Money up front**: fee, advance, signing bonus, guarantee, day rate.
- **Money later**: royalties/backend %, escalators (rate steps up at sales thresholds),
  performance bonuses, appearance fees.
- **Payment schedule**: on signing / delivery / publication / per match / per show.
- **Rights and scope**: territory, formats, term length, options on next work,
  first-negotiation vs first-refusal, reversion if unexploited.
- **Control**: approvals, credit/billing, exclusivity, non-competes.
- **Rider / conditions**: travel, accommodation, tech, hospitality, insurance.
- **Exit**: termination, buy-out or release clauses, cancellation fees.
Write the client's **BATNA** (best alternative — another offer, staying put, self-publishing)
in one line. Your walk-away comes from it, not from wishful thinking.
Get the client's sign-off on walk-away points *in writing* (an email is fine).

## 2. Open and anchor
- Let the buyer state a number first if you lack market data; anchor first if you know
  the market well (comparable deals, previous fees).
- Anchor with a reason: "Their last tour grossed X per night; we're at Y."
- Ask for the total package in writing before negotiating any single term — piecemeal
  agreement leaves you nothing to trade later.

## 3. Trade, don't concede
- Every give gets a get: "We can accept a lower advance if the royalty escalates at
  10k copies" / "lower fee if travel is business class and billing is top line".
- Concede in shrinking steps (e.g. 10% → 5% → 2%) — it signals you're near your floor.
- Move value between rows the client cares little about and rows they care a lot about.
- Park sticking points and come back; don't let one clause end the call.

## 4. Running competition (auctions, multiple offers)
- Set a clear process: "best bids by Friday 5pm", rules on rounds, and whether the
  highest bid wins or the client chooses on total terms.
- Report bids honestly; inventing an offer is the fastest way to lose a buyer for good
  and may breach professional codes.

## 5. Walking away
If the offer stays below walk-away after a fair process: say so calmly, thank them,
leave the door open. A real walk-away only works if you'd actually do it — that is
why step 1 matters.

## 6. Close
- Send a **deal memo** within 24 h listing every agreed term, then let the lawyer or
  contracts team draft. Compare the long-form contract line by line against the memo.
- Confirm commission and payment routing: many agencies receive monies and pay out
  net of commission — make that explicit in the client agreement.

## Gotchas
- Headline number wins, backend loses: an "accounting" definition of net profit can make
  backend worthless; ask how it is calculated.
- Options and non-competes can cost more than the fee — they block the next deal.
- Never negotiate a term the client hasn't approved, and never let the buyer go around you.
- Currency, tax withholding and who pays VAT/GST change the real number.

## Verify
- [ ] Term-sheet grid with ideal/target/walk-away signed off by the client.
- [ ] BATNA written down before the first call.
- [ ] Every concession recorded with what was gained for it.
- [ ] Deal memo sent within 24 h; long-form contract checked against it.
- [ ] Commission and payment flow confirmed in writing.
