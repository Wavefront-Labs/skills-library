# Discussion guide and moderation — focus groups and IDIs

**Goal:** get honest, deep explanations of *why* consumers think and behave as
they do, and turn hours of talk into a few findings the business trusts.
Qual tells you why and how; it does not tell you how many — never quote
percentages from groups.

## 1. Choose the format
- **Focus group**: 6–8 people, 90–120 minutes. Good for reactions, language,
  social norms. Bad for sensitive topics and individual journeys.
- **In-depth interview (IDI)**: one person, 45–60 minutes. Good for decisions,
  sensitive topics, B2B.
- **Triads/dyads**: 2–3 people (friends, couples) for shared decisions.
- **Online** (video groups, bulletin boards over several days) for geography and diaries.
Plan at least 2 groups per key segment so one odd group doesn't drive conclusions.

## 2. Recruit properly
Screener: category behaviour, demographic mix, exclude market research/marketing
industry workers and anyone who did a group in the last 6 months, check
articulateness with an open question. Over-recruit by 1–2 per group for no-shows.

## 3. Write a funnel discussion guide
Timed sections, broad to narrow:
1. **Warm-up (10 min)** — introductions, ground rules (no right answers,
   recording, observers), easy question about themselves.
2. **Category context (15–20 min)** — how they use/buy the category, in their words.
3. **Brand/experience (20 min)** — unaided first, then specific brands.
4. **Stimulus (30–40 min)** — concepts, ads, packs; individual written reaction
   **before** discussion to avoid groupthink. Rotate stimulus order between groups.
5. **Projectives (10 min)** — see §5.
6. **Wrap-up (5 min)** — "If you could tell the company one thing…"
Write questions as open prompts with planned probes; the guide is a map, not a script.

## 4. Moderation technique
- Open questions: "Tell me about the last time…", "Walk me through…".
- Probes: "What do you mean by ___?", "Why is that important?", "Can you give an
  example?", "Who sees it differently?" Use their words back to them.
- **Laddering**: attribute → consequence → value: "Why does that matter to you?"
  repeated 3–4 times.
- Silence: wait 5 seconds after an answer; the second answer is often the real one.
- Neutral face and voice; never show which answer you like or defend the client.
- Manage dynamics: invite the quiet ("Sam, you're nodding — what's your take?"),
  park the dominant ("Thanks — let's hear from others first").

## 5. Projective techniques
Use when people can't or won't say it directly:
- **Personification**: "If this brand were a person, who would they be?"
- **Collage/picture sort**: choose images that feel like the brand.
- **Bubble drawings**: what is this shopper thinking vs saying?
- **Obituary/letter**: "Write a breakup letter to your bank."
Always ask them to explain their choice; the explanation is the data.

## 6. Synthesis
- Debrief with observers right after each group (15 min): surprises, themes, quotes.
- Transcribe; code transcripts into themes (a spreadsheet of quote × theme × segment
  works). Look for patterns across groups and differences between segments.
- Report as: finding → evidence (2–3 verbatim quotes) → implication. Separate
  what consumers said from your interpretation.

## Gotchas
- Clients behind the mirror react to single quotes; remind them it's one voice.
- Leading stimulus order: the first concept shown benefits or suffers — rotate.
- Paid respondents please the moderator; ask for criticism explicitly.
- Obtain consent for recording and observation; handle recordings per privacy law.

## Verify
- [ ] ≥ 2 groups or ~6+ IDIs per key segment; screener excludes industry and repeat respondents.
- [ ] Guide is timed, funnelled, with individual write-down before group discussion.
- [ ] Probes and laddering used; quiet participants drawn in.
- [ ] Projectives explained by participants.
- [ ] Findings backed by multiple quotes across groups; no percentages reported.
