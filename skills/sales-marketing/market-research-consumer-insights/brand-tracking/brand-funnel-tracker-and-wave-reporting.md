# Brand-funnel tracker and wave reporting

**Goal:** measure the brand's health over time consistently enough that a
movement in the numbers means something changed in the market — and report it
so the brand team knows what to do. The enemy of a tracker is change: to the
questionnaire, sample or method.

## 1. Core measures (keep them few and fixed)
Funnel, asked in this order:
1. **Unaided (spontaneous) awareness** — "Which brands of ___ can you think of?"
   (first mention = top-of-mind).
2. **Prompted (aided) awareness** — logo/name list.
3. **Consideration** — "Which would you consider next time?"
4. **Usage** — past 3/12 months; main brand.
5. **Preference / recommendation** (e.g. NPS-style 0–10 likelihood to recommend).
Plus:
- **Image attributes** — 10–15 brand associations in a pick-any grid (brands ×
  statements). Include **category entry points** (situations/needs that cue buying:
  "for a quick lunch at work") to measure mental availability.
- **Ad/communication awareness** — prompted with stills or descriptions of recent
  activity, with a fake "control" ad to estimate false recognition.

## 2. Sampling design
- **Continuous** fieldwork (equal sample every week) beats bursts: it catches
  campaign effects and avoids seasonal artefacts.
- Typical: 100–250 completes/week in the category; report on 4- or 8-week
  rolling bases (e.g. 4 × 150 = 600).
- Fixed quotas (age, gender, region, category users) every week; same panel
  provider and screener throughout. Panel changes break trend lines — if forced,
  run both in parallel for 4–8 weeks to calibrate.

## 3. Questionnaire discipline
- Lock the core; changes go at the **end** of the survey so they don't influence
  core answers. Add topical modules there.
- Keep brand lists stable; adding a brand changes prompted awareness of others.
- Randomise brand order within lists; keep question order fixed.

## 4. Reading movement
- Compare rolling period vs previous non-overlapping period (and vs same period
  last year for seasonal categories). Overlapping rolling periods share sample —
  don't test them against each other.
- Significance at 95% on the base, but also require **persistence** (two
  consecutive readings in the same direction) before calling a change.
- Normalise image scores for brand size: big brands get more of every
  association. Use expected-vs-actual (based on row and column totals) to see
  what's *distinctive* about each brand.
- Compare with **norms** where you have them (your own history, agency databases).

## 5. Wave/quarterly report
One page up front:
- Headline: what moved, what didn't, how confident.
- Funnel chart with conversion ratios (consideration ÷ awareness, etc.) — the
  biggest drop-off ratio vs competitors is usually the problem to fix.
- Link to activity: campaigns, pricing, PR, competitor launches, on a timeline.
- 2–3 actions or questions for the brand team.
Appendix: bases, method notes, all measures by brand and segment.

## Gotchas
- Sample of 50/week reported weekly is noise — stakeholders will chase it.
- Online panel quality drifts; monitor speeders and fake-ad recognition.
- Changing the wording of the awareness question invalidates the history.
- **Uncertain:** weekly sample sizes depend heavily on category incidence and budget.

## Verify
- [ ] Core funnel and image measures fixed, at the start of the survey.
- [ ] Continuous sampling with stable quotas, provider and brand list.
- [ ] Changes called only when significant *and* persistent on non-overlapping periods.
- [ ] Image scores normalised for brand size.
- [ ] Report leads with headline, funnel ratios, activity timeline and actions.
