# Account rounding from an exposure review

**Goal:** sell the additional lines an existing client actually needs, using
information already on file, so the account becomes multi-line (more protected,
more profitable, and much less likely to leave). Industry rule of thumb: retention
rises markedly with each additional policy held — treat the exact uplift
figures you see quoted as indicative, not law.

## 1. Mine what you already have
Run a report of mono-line clients (one policy) and sort by premium. For each,
scan the file for clues:
- **Home insurance** client → car, contents of a rental, valuables, umbrella
  liability, landlord cover if they own a second property.
- **Car/fleet** client → business use implies a business: liability, property, BI.
- **Business property** client → public/general liability, BI, cyber (do they
  take card payments or hold customer data?), management liability, staff-related
  covers (workers' compensation where not state-run), professional indemnity if
  they give advice or design.
- **Life/protection** client → income protection, trauma/critical illness, partner cover.
Note exposures with evidence ("website takes online orders → cyber exposure").

## 2. Prioritise
Score each opportunity: client severity (how bad an uninsured loss would be) ×
likelihood × fit with your insurer panel. Work the top 20 accounts first. One
well-argued additional line beats a list of ten.

## 3. Make the approach
- Best moments: pre-renewal review, after a claim (the client has just seen the
  value of cover), after a business change (new premises, hires, contract).
- Lead with the exposure, not the product: "You've started selling online — if
  your customer database were breached, the notification and recovery costs
  would land on you. Your current policy doesn't respond to that."
- Use a real, anonymised claim example from your book or insurer case studies.
- Give a ballpark price early; vague prices stall decisions.

## 4. Add lines without a full remarket
- Prefer adding to the **existing insurer** (package policy, or a stand-alone line
  with the same carrier) when terms are competitive — one renewal date, one
  relationship, often a multi-policy discount.
- Align expiry dates where possible (short-period or pro-rated policy to the main
  renewal) so the whole account renews together.
- If the incumbent can't write the new line, place it separately; don't reopen
  the main policy to the market just to add a line.

## 5. Record declines
If the client declines, record the exposure explained and the date. Re-raise it
annually at review. This protects you and the client — an uncovered loss
followed by "nobody told me" is the classic E&O claim against an agent.

## Gotchas
- Don't bundle cover the client doesn't need to qualify for a discount.
- Commercial clients often have covers arranged elsewhere (through an
  association, a lender, a parent company) — ask before quoting.
- Check licence scope: a general insurance agent may not be authorised for life
  products, and vice versa; refer instead.
- **Uncertain:** retention-uplift statistics per extra policy vary by source and market.

## Verify
- [ ] Mono-line report run and each top account annotated with evidenced exposures.
- [ ] Approaches timed to reviews, claims or business changes.
- [ ] Pitch opens with the exposure and a priced option.
- [ ] Expiry dates aligned where practical; no unnecessary remarket.
- [ ] Declined recommendations documented and diarised for the next review.
