# Market submission and binding — placing the risk

**Goal:** turn a client's needs analysis into bound cover on good terms, by
presenting the risk well to insurers whose appetite fits it, then managing
quotes, declines and conditions through to a clean bind. Written from the
broker/intermediary side; tied agents with one carrier use the same steps
minus the market selection.

## 1. Build a submission underwriters want to quote
An underwriter reads dozens a day; complete, clear submissions get quoted first.
Include:
- **Proposal/application form** fully completed and signed (no "see attached" gaps).
- **Risk narrative** (half a page): what the business does, revenue split by
  activity, years trading, management experience, key risk controls.
- **Claims history** — usually 5 years, with insurer loss runs if available;
  explain large losses and what changed afterwards.
- **Schedules** — locations, sums insured, vehicles, payroll, as applicable.
- **Risk improvements** — sprinklers, alarms, training, contracts, certifications —
  with photos or reports.
- **What you want**: limits, excess, key extensions, expiry date, target premium
  if you have one, and the date you need terms by (allow 2–3 weeks for commercial).

## 2. Match to appetite
- Maintain an appetite grid: insurer × class × size × territory × hazards
  they avoid. Update it from underwriter conversations and declines.
- Approach 3–5 markets that genuinely fit; blasting everyone wastes goodwill and
  can "burn" the market (an insurer that has seen the risk elsewhere may decline).
- Agree with the client which markets you'll approach, especially in
  hard-to-place classes.

## 3. Handling declines and odd terms
- Ask every declining underwriter *why* (appetite? claims? missing information?).
  "Missing info" is fixable; resubmit with it.
- Common conditions: higher excess, specific exclusions, survey required,
  warranties (e.g. "alarm must be set when unattended") — explain warranties
  carefully; breaching one can void cover for related claims.

## 4. Comparing and presenting quotes
Build a side-by-side: insurer (and financial strength rating), premium
including taxes/fees, limits, sub-limits, excesses, key extensions, key
exclusions, subjectivities, policy wording differences. Recommend one with
reasons; the cheapest is often thinner cover. Disclose your remuneration as required.

## 5. Subjectivities and binding
- **Subjectivities** are conditions before or after binding (signed proposal,
  survey within 30 days, risk improvement completed). List each with owner and
  due date; missing a post-bind subjectivity can let the insurer change or cancel terms.
- **Bind** only on a written instruction from the client, in writing to the
  insurer, before the inception date/time. Confirm the bind back in writing and
  issue a cover note/certificate if needed (lender, landlord, contract).
- Check the issued policy schedule against the quote — names, addresses,
  limits, excesses, endorsements. Errors are common and the client won't spot them.

## Gotchas
- Material facts disclosed to you must reach the insurer; a late discovered
  non-disclosure can void the policy and land on you.
- Don't let cover lapse while waiting for terms: arrange a short extension or
  hold cover with the expiring insurer.
- Quotes expire (often 30 days); note validity dates.
- **Uncertain:** binding authority and documentation practice varies by firm and
  market; follow your delegated authority limits.

## Verify
- [ ] Submission complete: form, narrative, 5-year claims, schedules, risk controls, terms needed.
- [ ] Markets selected from an appetite grid and agreed with the client.
- [ ] Quote comparison covers limits, excesses, exclusions and subjectivities, not just price.
- [ ] Written client instruction and written bind before inception.
- [ ] Policy schedule checked against the quote; subjectivities diarised.
