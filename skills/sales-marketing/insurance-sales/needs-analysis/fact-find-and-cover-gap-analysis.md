# Fact-find and cover gap analysis

**Goal:** recommend insurance from the client's actual exposures, not from
whatever product you sell most. A good fact-find finds the gap the client
didn't know about, sizes it, and leaves a written trail showing why each
recommendation follows. Applies to personal lines, life/protection and small
commercial; the document names (statement of advice, demands-and-needs
statement, needs analysis form) depend on your jurisdiction and licence.

## 1. Before the meeting
- Ask for **current schedules/policy documents**, not just "who are you with".
  The schedule shows sums insured, excesses/deductibles, endorsements and exclusions.
- For businesses: last year's turnover, wage roll, asset register or rough
  values, contracts with insurance clauses (leases and customer contracts often
  *require* specific cover and limits).

## 2. The discovery interview
Work through exposures, not products:
- **People** — who depends on the income? debts? what happens financially if
  the earner dies, is disabled, or is off work for 6 months?
- **Property** — buildings, contents, stock, vehicles, equipment; how values
  were set and when.
- **Liability** — what could you be sued for? public/general liability,
  professional advice, products sold, directors' decisions, cyber/data.
- **Income** — business interruption: how long would it take to be trading
  again after a fire? (Owners almost always underestimate; ask about lead times
  on key equipment.)
- **Obligations** — lender, landlord or contract requirements.
Ask "what's the worst thing that could happen to you financially?" and let them answer.

## 3. Gap analysis
Build a table: exposure | current cover | limit/sum insured | gap/issue | priority.
Common findings:
- **Underinsurance** — sums insured not updated with rebuild costs or inflation.
  Many property policies apply *average/co-insurance*: insure for 60% of value
  and a partial claim may pay only ~60%. Check whether the policy has such a clause.
- **Indemnity period too short** on business interruption (12 months is common;
  rebuilds often take longer).
- **Missing lines** — no professional indemnity for an adviser business, no cyber
  for a business holding customer data, no income protection for a self-employed earner.
- **Excess/deductible mismatch** — set higher than the client could actually pay.
- **Exclusions that bite** — flood, unoccupancy periods, subcontractor work.
For life/protection, size the need: debts + income replacement for N years +
future costs (education) − existing assets and cover. Show the arithmetic.

## 4. Prioritise and recommend
Rank by severity × likelihood. Present the top 3 gaps first with costed options;
let the client choose which to address and record declined recommendations
("Client declined BI cover after discussion of 6-month rebuild scenario").

## 5. Document the advice basis
Record: information relied on, client objectives, gaps found, options
presented, recommendation and reasons, what was declined and why, and any
assumptions (e.g. "rebuild cost estimated from online calculator, not a
professional valuation"). Send the client a copy.

## Gotchas
- Duty of disclosure rules differ (some regimes put the onus on the insurer to
  ask questions); explain to the client what they must tell the insurer, and the
  consequence of not doing so.
- Don't set sums insured yourself as fact — recommend a valuation for high values.
- Overinsurance wastes premium too; flag it.
- **Uncertain:** formulas for life cover need vary by firm; use your approved calculator.

## Verify
- [ ] Current schedules reviewed, not just named insurers.
- [ ] Exposures covered across people, property, liability, income, obligations.
- [ ] Gap table built with sums insured checked for underinsurance/average.
- [ ] Recommendations prioritised; declined cover recorded.
- [ ] Advice basis and assumptions documented and sent to the client.
