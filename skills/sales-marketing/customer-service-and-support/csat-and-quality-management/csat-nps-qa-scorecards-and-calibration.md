# CSAT, NPS and quality management

**Goal:** reliable measures of how customers experience support, a quality standard
everyone scores the same way, and coaching that actually moves the numbers.

## 1. Choose the customer metrics
| Metric | Question (typical) | Scale | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CSAT** | "How satisfied were you with the support you received?" | 1–5 (CSAT % = 4s+5s ÷ responses) | Per-interaction quality |
| **CES** | "How easy was it to get your issue resolved?" | 1–7 agree/disagree | Friction, process problems |
| **NPS** | "How likely are you to recommend us to a friend?" | 0–10 (NPS = %9–10 − %0–6) | Relationship/brand level |
Use CSAT (and optionally CES) after tickets; NPS is better as a periodic relationship
survey than after every contact. Always add one open question: "What's the main reason
for your score?" — the verbatims are where the insight is.

**Survey hygiene:** send within hours of resolution; 1–2 questions; mobile-friendly;
track response rate (low rates bias toward extremes); exclude spam/duplicate tickets;
report at agent level only once each agent has enough responses (e.g. 30+ per period)
to be meaningful.

## 2. Build a quality (QA) scorecard
Scores what customers can't see: accuracy, policy, process. 6–10 criteria, grouped:
- **Resolution:** correct answer; issue fully resolved or correct next step; first-contact resolution where possible.
- **Process/compliance:** identity verification, required disclosures, correct tagging, notes.
- **Communication:** clarity, tone/empathy, personalisation, grammar (written).
- **Critical fails** (auto-fail): data-protection breach, rudeness, wrong refund amount.
Define each criterion with what "meets" looks like, plus examples. Use yes/no or a
3-point scale — fine-grained 1–10 scales reduce agreement between reviewers.

Sample fairly: e.g. 4–8 contacts per agent per month, randomly selected plus some
targeted (low CSAT, long handle time, escalations).

## 3. Calibration sessions
Purpose: everyone who scores (QA analysts, team leads) applies the scorecard the same way.
1. Pick 3–5 contacts; each reviewer scores them independently beforehand.
2. Meet (45–60 min); compare scores criterion by criterion.
3. Discuss disagreements; agree the correct score and **update the criterion
   definition** with the example.
4. Track variance between reviewers over time — aim to shrink it (e.g. within a few
   percentage points of the agreed score).
Run monthly, or whenever the scorecard or policies change.

## 4. From scores to coaching
- Weekly 1:1 (15–30 min) per agent, focused on **one behaviour** at a time.
- Use a real interaction: listen/read together, ask the agent to self-assess first.
- Name the behaviour, its impact, the alternative: "When you said 'that's policy', the
  customer pushed back. Try explaining the reason: 'We do this to protect your account.'"
- Agree a practice goal; check it in the next sample.
- Celebrate good contacts publicly; coach problems privately.

## 5. Close the loop on detractors
Route low CSAT/NPS responses to a lead within 1–2 business days; call back where useful.
Tag reasons (agent, process, product, policy) — most low scores are about product or
policy, not the agent; send those to the owning teams.

## Gotchas
- Rewarding agents purely on CSAT encourages giveaways and survey-begging.
- QA and CSAT disagreeing is normal; investigate patterns, not single cases.
- Agents gaming handle-time metrics at the expense of resolution.

## Verify
- [ ] CSAT survey live with open-text reason; response rate tracked.
- [ ] Scorecard with defined criteria, examples and critical fails.
- [ ] Monthly calibration held; reviewer variance tracked.
- [ ] Each agent has a weekly coaching session on one behaviour.
- [ ] Detractor responses followed up and tagged by cause.
