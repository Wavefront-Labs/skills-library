# Service recovery — fix, compensate in proportion, close the loop

**Goal:** after something goes wrong, the customer ends up confident in you again, at
a cost proportionate to the failure — and the same failure happens less often.

## 1. Order of operations
1. **Fix the actual problem first** (re-ship, repair, rebook, correct the bill). A
   voucher without a fix is an insult.
2. **Apologise and own it** — specific, no blaming other departments or suppliers in
   front of the customer.
3. **Compensate in proportion** (section 2), if warranted.
4. **Follow through** on every promise and confirm it's resolved.
5. **Feed back** the cause internally (section 4).

## 2. Size the gesture with a recovery matrix
Agree a matrix with management so front-line staff can act without asking. Example
structure (values are illustrative — set your own by margin and customer value):
| Severity | Example | Typical response |
|---|---|---|
| Minor inconvenience | Short delay, small error fixed quickly | Apology + fix |
| Moderate | Missed delivery date, second contact needed | Fix + small gesture (shipping refund, small credit) |
| Major | Wrong item at an event deadline, repeated failures | Fix + meaningful credit or partial refund |
| Severe | Financial loss, safety issue, repeated failures over weeks | Fix + escalate to manager + tailored remedy |
Adjust for: the customer's actual loss (time, money, stress), how many times they've had
to contact you, and their lifetime value. Ask "what would make this right for you?" —
often the request is smaller than you'd offer.

Give front-line agents **authority** up to a set limit without approval; slow approvals
erase the goodwill of the gesture.

## 3. Follow through
- Promise only what you control, with a date. Put a task/reminder in the helpdesk.
- Contact the customer *before* the deadline, even if only to say it's in progress.
- After the fix: a short check-in ("Did the replacement arrive okay?"). This is where
  recovery is won.
- Note the outcome on the customer record so the next agent knows the history.

## 4. Close the loop to the business
- Tag every recovery case with a **root-cause category** (carrier, product defect,
  billing error, policy confusion, agent error, website bug).
- Weekly/monthly: top causes by volume and by goodwill cost; send to the owning team
  with examples.
- Track whether fixes reduced contacts in that category.
Recovery spend is a measure of upstream failures; the cheapest recovery is prevention.

## 5. Measuring recovery
- Post-resolution CSAT on recovery cases specifically.
- Retention/repurchase of recovered vs non-recovered customers (cohort comparison).
- Goodwill spend per case and as % of revenue.
- Repeat-contact rate on the same issue.

## Gotchas
- The "service recovery paradox" (recovered customers more loyal than if nothing went
  wrong) is real in some studies and absent in others; don't rely on it. Aim not to fail twice.
- Inconsistent gestures between agents breed "ask for the manager" behaviour — the matrix fixes this.
- Over-compensation teaches some customers to complain for credit; log gestures per customer.
- Legal entitlements (statutory refunds, consumer-law remedies) are not goodwill —
  never present them as favours.

## Verify
- [ ] Problem fixed before or alongside any compensation.
- [ ] Gesture matched the recovery matrix (or a recorded exception).
- [ ] Follow-up task created and customer contacted after the fix.
- [ ] Case tagged with root cause.
- [ ] Monthly root-cause report sent to owning teams.
