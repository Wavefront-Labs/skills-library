# Returns, refunds and warranty claims — intake, triage, saves

**Goal:** process every return correctly and quickly, keep the customer where you
fairly can (exchange or credit), and catch fraud without treating honest customers
like suspects.

## 1. Know the two layers of rules
1. **Statutory rights** — what the law gives customers regardless of your policy:
   remedies for faulty, misdescribed or not-as-described goods, and in many places
   cooling-off / withdrawal rights for online and distance sales (e.g. the EU's
   14-day withdrawal right; Australia's consumer guarantees; rules vary by country
   and state). Your policy cannot remove these.
2. **Store policy** — your extras, typically change-of-mind returns within a window,
   condition requirements, receipt rules, restocking fees where lawful.
Front-line staff must know which layer a case falls under; mixing them up is the most
common legal mistake ("no refunds on sale items" is unlawful for faulty goods in many markets).

## 2. Intake (ask, don't assume)
- Order/receipt lookup (order number, card, email, loyalty account).
- **Reason:** faulty, damaged in transit, wrong item, not as described, doesn't fit,
  changed mind. Record a **reason code** — it drives analytics and supplier claims.
- Item condition, tags/packaging, accessories, date of purchase/delivery.
- What they want: refund, exchange, repair, credit.

## 3. Triage
| Situation | Default route |
|---|---|
| Faulty within warranty / statutory period | Repair, replace or refund per law; check what the customer is entitled to choose |
| Damaged/wrong item shipped | Replace or refund; arrange free return label; carrier claim internally |
| Change of mind within policy | Refund or exchange per policy condition rules |
| Outside policy, no fault | Decline politely or offer exception (section 4) |
| High-value warranty claim | Create **RMA**, inspect/test before final decision |
Refund to the **original payment method** by default; it reduces fraud and is required
in some jurisdictions.

## 4. Exceptions judgement
Consider: customer history (first return vs serial), item value, how far outside policy,
whether the item is resaleable, and the cost of saying no (a chargeback, a bad review).
Use the authority level you've been given; log the reason for every exception so
exceptions stay consistent.

## 5. Saves
Before processing a change-of-mind refund, offer what solves the underlying issue:
- Wrong size/colour → **exchange** (check stock, offer to ship before return arrives
  for trusted customers).
- Didn't meet expectations → a better-fit alternative, or a how-to fix ("the setting
  is under…") for products that weren't set up correctly.
- **Store credit** with a small bonus, where policy allows.
Never pressure; offer once, then process the refund cheerfully.

## 6. Fraud signals (flag, don't accuse)
Item returned isn't the one sold (serials, wear, a different product in the box),
"wardrobing" (worn and returned), receipt doesn't match, very high return frequency,
refund requested to a different card. Follow your loss-prevention procedure; escalate
quietly and keep the conversation neutral.

## Gotchas
- Telling a customer to "contact the manufacturer" for a faulty item when the law makes
  the retailer responsible.
- Not photographing damage on receipt — supplier and carrier claims then fail.
- Slow refunds after receipt drive chargebacks; publish and meet a refund time.
- Restocking fees are restricted or banned for some situations; check before charging.

## Verify
- [ ] Each case classified as statutory or policy; statutory rights never refused.
- [ ] Reason code and condition recorded, damage photographed.
- [ ] Refund to original payment method unless an exception is logged.
- [ ] Exchange/credit offered once on change-of-mind returns.
- [ ] Exceptions and fraud flags logged with reasons.
