# Reserves, vendors and post-sale deals

**Goal:** bring in good property, agree reserves that let it sell, keep the vendor
informed and calm, and turn unsold lots into sales or a clean plan.

## 1. Winning the consignment
- Visit or review photos quickly; speed and a specific, evidenced view beat a vague
  high number. Bring 2–3 comparables and explain your estimate.
- Explain *why* your sale suits this property: the buyers you reach, the sale's
  timing, marketing (catalogue feature, online platforms), and recent results.
- Resist "buying" the consignment with an inflated estimate. It wins the item and
  loses it on sale day, along with the vendor relationship.
- Put the offer in writing: estimate, proposed reserve, seller's commission, extra
  charges (photography, insurance, lotting, unsold/"buy-in" fees), payment timing.

## 2. Agreeing the reserve
- The **reserve** is the confidential minimum below which the lot won't sell. Common
  practice keeps it **at or below the low estimate** — check your house rules and any
  sector regulation (estimates above reserve are restricted in some markets).
- Open with what comparables support; let the vendor state their number and the reason
  behind it (debt, sentiment, what they paid). Address the reason, not just the figure.
- Offer **discretion**: authority to sell up to, say, 10% below reserve if bidding
  stalls just short (record the exact percentage in writing).
- If the vendor insists on an unrealistic reserve, say plainly what you expect to
  happen, offer a lower-fee or later-sale option, or decline the lot.
- Record reserve, discretion and any special instructions on the signed
  **consignment agreement** and in the clerk's sale sheet.

## 3. Through the sale
- Confirm receipt, lot number, catalogue entry and photos with the vendor before
  publication; correct errors now, not on sale day.
- Share interest signals honestly (watchers, condition-report requests, absentee bids)
  without promising results.
- Re-visit reserves a few days before the sale if pre-sale interest is weak; a small
  cut then beats a "no sale" result that's visible to the market.

## 4. Unsold (passed / bought-in) lots
1. Contact the vendor the same day or next morning; lead with the facts (highest bid,
   number of bidders).
2. Options in order of value: **post-sale deal** with the underbidder or any buyer at
   or near the highest bid (where conditions of sale allow after-sale offers);
   **re-offer** in a later sale with a lower reserve; move to a timed online sale;
   collection by the vendor (fees per agreement).
3. Approach the underbidder promptly while interest is warm; after-sale deals usually
   carry the same buyer's premium and commission terms (state your house's terms).

## 5. Settlement
Send a vendor statement: hammer price, commission, charges, net proceeds, payment date
(often after the buyer has paid and any cooling-off/withdrawal period has passed).
Pay on time — prompt settlement is the best consignment marketing there is.

## Gotchas
- A reserve above the low estimate signals trouble; many lots that fail are lots where
  the house conceded this at intake.
- Never disclose a reserve to bidders; "it's reserved" is all they need.
- Verbal reserve changes made the day before the sale get lost — get them in writing
  and update the clerk's sheet.
- Fee structures and reserve rules vary by country and sector; treat percentages here
  as examples.

## Verify
- [ ] Signed consignment agreement records estimate, reserve, discretion and all fees.
- [ ] Reserve at or below low estimate (or documented exception).
- [ ] Vendor checked catalogue entry before publication.
- [ ] Every passed lot had vendor contact within 24 hours and a decided next step.
- [ ] Vendor statement sent and paid by the promised date.
