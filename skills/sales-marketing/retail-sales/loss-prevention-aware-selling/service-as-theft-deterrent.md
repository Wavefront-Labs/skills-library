# Service as theft deterrent

**Goal:** lower shrink without treating customers as suspects. Attentive service is the
cheapest loss-prevention tool a store has: people who intend to steal want anonymity,
and good service takes it away.

## 1. Greet everyone, every time
- Acknowledge each person entering within ~10 seconds with eye contact. It's welcoming to
  shoppers and signals "we see you" to someone planning theft.
- Re-approach periodically with something useful ("Can I get you another size?"). A
  helpful approach and a deterrent approach are the same approach.

## 2. Zone the floor
- Split the floor into zones with a named person each shift, including blind spots (corners,
  tall fixtures, near exits and fitting rooms).
- Keep sight lines open: low fixtures in the middle, tall ones on walls; mirrors at blind
  corners.
- Never leave the floor or tills unattended; announce coverage hand-offs ("Covering
  zone 2 while I'm on break?").

## 3. High-shrink merchandise
Know your store's list (commonly razor blades, fragrances, cosmetics, electronics
accessories, spirits, branded apparel). Handle per policy:
- **Display:** keep near the counter or in view; display empty boxes or one unit; lock
  high-value items with key-release.
- **Tagging:** EAS tags, spider wraps or safer boxes as specified. Deactivate/remove at
  the till every time — a false alarm on an innocent customer is an embarrassment for
  both of you.
- **Lock-and-key service:** retrieve and walk the item to the till for the customer, offering
  it as service ("I'll bring it to the counter for you").

## 4. Fitting rooms
Count items in and out (common limit 5–8 per visit); use number tags; check the room
after each use for empty hangers and removed tags. Report hanger/tag finds to a
manager rather than chasing a customer.

## 5. If you suspect theft
- **Do not confront, accuse, detain, touch or chase.** Store policy and the law on
  detention vary by jurisdiction and are often restrictive; physical intervention risks
  injury and liability. Leave it to trained loss-prevention staff or police per policy.
- Service-based intervention is usually allowed: approach and offer help ("Can I take
  that to the counter for you?" / "Would you like a bag for those?"). Often the item gets
  put back.
- Note: description, time, direction, items. Write an incident report the same day.

## 6. Incidents and safety
Use your incident system for every theft, attempt and suspicious pattern — data drives
staffing, fixture and tagging decisions. If anyone is threatening, step back, prioritise
safety, alert a manager or call emergency services. No item is worth an injury.

## Gotchas
- Profiling by appearance is wrong, discriminatory and ineffective; focus on behaviour
  (watching staff instead of product, concealment moves, repeated trips to blind spots).
- Internal theft and admin errors (mis-scans, wrong deliveries) are a large share of
  shrink in many industry surveys — accurate processes matter as much as the floor.
- Over-locking stock hurts sales; review the list against shrink data.

## Verify
- [ ] Every customer greeted; zones assigned and covered each shift.
- [ ] High-shrink list known; tagging and display match policy.
- [ ] Fitting-room counts done and rooms checked after each use.
- [ ] No staff confrontation; suspicions handled by service approach and reporting.
- [ ] Incident reports filed the same day.
