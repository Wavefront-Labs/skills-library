# Attach and step-up at the point of sale

**Goal:** more value per customer — higher average transaction value (ATV) and units per
transaction (UPT) — while the customer leaves feeling better served, not upsold.

**Definitions:** *upsell / step-up* = a better version of what they're buying;
*cross-sell / attach* = something that goes with it.

## 1. Lock the primary sale first
Never introduce add-ons before the customer has decided on the main item. Offering a
£200 model when they've just said yes to the £120 one reopens the decision and can lose
both. Order: decide → confirm → then add.

Exception: **step-up** belongs during matching, not at the till — show it as one of
three options (good-better-best) while they're still choosing.

## 2. Step-up with good-better-best
- Present the three tiers side by side; name the **one concrete difference** per step
  that matters for their stated need ("The middle one has a battery that lasts the full
  day you said you need").
- Let the price gap be visible; say the difference out loud as a small number ("It's £30
  more"), not the total.
- If they choose the cheapest, endorse it. Trust is what brings them back.

## 3. Attach what the product needs ("completes")
Build an **attach list** for your top 20 products — the item that makes the product
work or last:
| Main item | Natural attach |
|---|---|
| Phone | case, screen protector, charger (if not included) |
| Shoes | care spray, socks, insoles |
| Printer | ink/toner, paper, cable |
| Paint | brushes, tape, dust sheets |
| Bike | lock, lights, helmet |

Script: "Most people get a [case] with this — do you have one already?" It's a
question, not a push. Physically bring it to the counter; seeing beats hearing.

## 4. Warranties and protection plans — do it honestly
- Explain what it covers (accidental damage? only defects?), how long, and how it differs
  from the statutory/consumer-law rights they already have (in many jurisdictions,
  goods must be fit for purpose for a reasonable time regardless of warranty). Misstating
  this can breach consumer protection law.
- Offer once, clearly. If they decline, move on.

## 5. Bundles and thresholds
- Pre-built bundles (product + 2 accessories at a small saving) make attaching easy.
- Free-delivery or gift thresholds: "You're £8 off free delivery — these socks would get
  you there."

## 6. Track it
Daily per associate: ATV, UPT, attach rate for target categories (e.g. % of phone sales
with a case). Share as coaching data, not a leaderboard that rewards pushing.

## Gotchas
- **Add-on fatigue:** one or two suggestions per transaction; more feels like a script.
- Upselling customers who've signalled a tight budget damages trust and conversion.
- Bundled items need to be in stock at the counter, or the moment passes.
- Commission-driven warranty pushing produces returns and complaints; watch refund rates.
- Unverified: attach-rate benchmarks vary widely by category; set targets on your own baseline.

## Verify
- [ ] Main item confirmed before any add-on is mentioned.
- [ ] Good-better-best options shown with one stated difference per step.
- [ ] An attach list exists for top products and the items are at the counter.
- [ ] Warranty descriptions are accurate and distinguish them from statutory rights.
- [ ] ATV, UPT and attach rate tracked weekly against the starting baseline.
