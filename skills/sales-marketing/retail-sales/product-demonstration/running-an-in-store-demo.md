# Running an in-store demo

**Goal:** show the product doing the one thing the customer cares about, let them try it,
and turn interest into a purchase decision on the spot.

## 1. Set up the station (before the doors open)
- **Working unit, charged/plugged/filled**, plus a spare. A demo that fails in front of a
  customer is worse than no demo. Test it twice.
- Consumables ready (ingredients, paper, test fabric, batteries), wipes and a bin.
- **Stock within reach** — boxed units on or beside the station so a "yes" goes straight
  to the till. Price clearly visible.
- Position at a crossing point of traffic, with room for 2–4 people to stand without
  blocking the aisle. Check fire exits are clear.
- If sampling food: follow your local food-hygiene rules (gloves, temperature, allergen
  labels); many retailers require a hygiene certificate.

## 2. Script a 60–90 second demo
Write it, rehearse it out loud, time it.
1. **Hook** (5–10 s): a visible result or question. "Want to see a stain come out in ten seconds?"
2. **Three benefits, each shown, not told.** Use feature → so what → for you:
   "It has a 2,000 W motor *(feature)*, so it crushes ice in seconds *(advantage)*, so your
   smoothies are ready before you leave for work *(benefit)*."
3. **The proof moment:** the dramatic bit (the blend, the clean, the photo in the dark).
4. **Hand over:** "Here — you try it." The customer's hands on the product is the most
   important line in the script.
5. **Trial close:** "Can you see that working in your kitchen?" / "Which colour would suit you?"

Three benefits max. Long feature lists lose people.

## 3. Read and adapt
- Ask one discovery question early ("What are you using now?") and pick the benefits
  that match.
- Watch body language: leaning in, asking price or specs = buying signals → close.
  Arms folded or checking phone → shorten and let them go.
- If a crowd forms, talk to the group, then pick off the most engaged person afterwards.

## 4. Close from the demo
- Answer objections with the product: "Is it loud?" → switch it on.
- Offer the decision: "Shall I grab you a boxed one?" If there's a demo-day offer, mention
  it once, accurately.
- Not now? Give a leaflet/QR code or note their name for follow-up if the store permits.

## 5. Log the session
Demos run, people engaged, units sold, top objections. Over a week this tells you which
hook and benefits work, and gives the brand useful feedback.

## Gotchas
- Never demo with a unit you haven't tested that day.
- Don't claim performance you can't show (advertising/consumer-protection rules apply to
  spoken claims too).
- Cleaning between users matters — especially beauty, headphones and food.
- Fatigue: a script repeated 80 times a day goes flat. Vary the hook every hour.
- Unverified: "60–90 seconds" is a common rule of thumb, not a measured optimum; adjust to
  product complexity.

## Verify
- [ ] Unit tested twice, spare ready, stock and prices within reach.
- [ ] Script timed under 90 seconds with exactly three shown benefits.
- [ ] Every demo includes the customer handling the product.
- [ ] Every demo ends with a trial close or a direct offer.
- [ ] Session log kept: demos, engagements, units sold, objections.
