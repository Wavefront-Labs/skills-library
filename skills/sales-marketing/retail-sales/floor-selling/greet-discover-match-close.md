# Greet, discover, match, close — selling on the retail floor

**Goal:** turn browsers into buyers without the pushiness that makes people leave. The
customer should feel helped, not sold to.

## 1. Approach (the first 30 seconds)
- **Acknowledge within ~10 seconds** of entry: eye contact, smile, "Hi, come in" — even if
  you're busy. Then give them space to decompress.
- **Approach when they signal:** touching or picking up a product, looking around (for help
  or a size), or returning to the same item twice.
- **Open with a non-business or observational line**, not "Can I help you?" (which invites
  "Just looking"). Examples: "Those just came in this week — the lighter one's softer",
  "Are you shopping for yourself today or someone else?"
- If they say "just looking": "Great — I'm [name], shout if you want a size or a demo," and
  step away. Return later with something useful (a size, a related item).

## 2. Discover (2–3 questions, then listen)
Ask open questions about **use, not product**:
- "What will you mainly use it for?"
- "What are you using now — what do you like or dislike about it?"
- "Is there a budget you'd like to stay around?" (ask plainly; it saves both of you time)
- "When do you need it by?"
Repeat back the key need in one sentence: "So something waterproof for daily cycling,
under about £120." This alone builds trust.

## 3. Match (narrow to three)
- Show **no more than three options**: one that matches the brief, one a step up, one a
  value pick. Too many choices stalls the decision.
- Translate features into their need: "It's fully taped at the seams — so the rain you
  get on that commute won't come through the stitching."
- **Put it in their hands** (try on, hold, switch on). Ownership feeling sells.
- Trial close: "How does that feel compared to the other one?" Their answer tells you
  which to drop.

## 4. Handle the objection
Common ones and a response pattern — acknowledge, ask, answer:
- **Price:** "Fair point. Is it the total, or are you comparing it to something?" Then show
  value (lasts longer, warranty) or move to the value option. Don't discount unprompted.
- **"I'll think about it":** "Of course. Is there anything about it you're unsure of?" Often
  a hidden question (fit, returns). Mention the returns policy if it reduces risk.
- **"Cheaper online":** know your price-match policy; otherwise point to service,
  immediate availability, and help with setup or returns.

## 5. Close and hand off
- Assumptive, low-pressure close: "Shall I take that to the counter for you?" or "Which
  colour are you going with?"
- At the till: confirm the item, mention one relevant add-on (see
  sales-marketing.retail-sales.upselling-cross-selling), explain returns and care.
- If they don't buy: offer to hold it, note it in clienteling, or give a card. A good
  no today is a sale next week.

## Gotchas
- Hovering and following kills sales and can feel like suspicion.
- Pre-judging budget by appearance is both wrong and costly.
- Talking more than the customer in discovery = you're pitching, not discovering.
- Don't knock competitors or other products in the store.
- Commission pressure shows; customers notice when your pick is always the dearest.

## Verify
- [ ] Every customer acknowledged within ~10 seconds.
- [ ] You can state each customer's need in one sentence before showing product.
- [ ] Shown no more than three options, one in their hands.
- [ ] Price objections answered with value before any discount.
- [ ] Track your conversion (sales ÷ customers served) and average sale weekly.
