# Wholesale, job lots and second-hand sourcing

**Goal:** buy stock that your specific buyers will take, at a cost that leaves margin after
all costs — and avoid the dead stock, fakes and unsafe goods that sink small traders.

## 1. Start from your buyers, not the bargain
Before buying, write: who buys at my pitch, what they pay for things, what sells out, what
they ask for that I don't have. A cheap lot of the wrong thing is expensive.
Rule of thumb: you need enough markup to
cover waste, unsold stock and day costs — many traders target at least 2× cost on general
goods (see sales-marketing.street-informal-trade.market-pricing.day-pricing-and-end-of-day-clearance);
calculate your own from break-even.

## 2. Sources and how to work them
- **Wholesale produce/flower markets:** go early (often before dawn); prices fall later but
  the best quality is gone. Build a relationship with two or three wholesalers — regulars
  get better stock and credit.
- **Cash-and-carry wholesalers:** require a trade account (often proof of business); good for
  branded consumables.
- **Job lots / liquidation / returns pallets:** ends of lines, overstocks, customer returns sold
  by the pallet. Ask for a **manifest** (item list); "unmanifested" pallets are a gamble.
  Returns pallets include faulty items — budget for a percentage you can't sell.
- **Direct imports** (online B2B marketplaces): low unit costs but minimum order quantities,
  shipping, import duty and VAT/sales tax, and long lead times. **Order samples first.**
  Use the platform's buyer protection/escrow; check supplier history.
- **Second-hand:** charity shops, car boots, estate/house clearances, online marketplaces. Know
  values from recent *sold* (not listed) prices on marketplaces.
- **Makers/local producers:** consignment or sale-or-return can reduce your risk.

## 3. Judge a buy in 2 minutes
- Can I sell this at my pitch, at a price ≥ my target markup, within a few weeks?
- Condition: check samples from the bottom of the box, not the top.
- Sizes/colours mix: a lot of all-XXL or one colour sells slowly.
- Storage and transport: does it fit in my van/car and store?
- Seasonality: is the season ending?
Start small with a new line; scale only after it sells.

## 4. Legal and safety checks
- **Counterfeits:** branded goods far below wholesale price are likely fake; selling them
  is illegal and stock can be seized.
- **Product safety:** electrical items, toys, cosmetics and children's products often need
  safety marks/compliance (e.g. CE/UKCA in Europe/UK, other marks elsewhere). Second-hand
  electricals and upholstered furniture may have specific rules. Check local regulations.
- **Stolen goods:** too-cheap, no-receipt deals from strangers are a risk; get receipts.
- Keep **purchase records** for tax.

## 5. Track sell-through per line
Record cost, quantity, sold, date sold out. Lines that sell through quickly at a good margin
get re-bought; anything with stock left after several weeks gets cleared and not re-bought.

## Gotchas
- Import landed cost surprises: duty + tax + shipping + customs broker can double the unit cost.
- "Mixed lot" photos show the best items.
- Cash flow: don't tie all your money in one big lot.
- The 2× markup figure is a common heuristic, not a rule — calculate yours.

## Verify
- [ ] You can describe your buyers and their price range before sourcing.
- [ ] Every new line tested in small quantity first.
- [ ] Landed cost includes shipping, duty, tax and fees.
- [ ] No counterfeits; safety-regulated items checked for required marks.
- [ ] Purchase receipts kept; sell-through tracked per line.
