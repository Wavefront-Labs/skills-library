# Hawking rounds and event vending

**Goal:** sell quickly to people where they are — beaches, queues, stadium stands, festivals,
commuter routes — carrying only what sells, with fast and safe payment handling and the
right permission.

## 1. Permission
- Roving sellers usually need a specific licence (e.g. a pedlar's or street-trading licence;
  names and rules differ by country and city), and many zones ban roving trade entirely.
- Events and venues control vending inside their boundary — you need the organiser's or
  concessionaire's approval and usually a fee or commission. Selling inside without it gets
  you removed.
- Food and drink need food-hygiene registration and often temperature control.
- Carry your licence/badge visibly.

## 2. Plan the round
- **Match product to moment:** cold drinks and ice creams in heat, umbrellas and ponchos in rain,
  snacks and programmes before kick-off, glow items after dark at festivals.
- **Map the route and timing:** where crowds gather and wait (queues, beach entries, stadium
  gates 60–90 minutes before start, half-time concourses). Walk it before trading day.
- **Restock points:** a car, locker or partner stall where you refill; plan when to return.
- Check weather forecasts and event schedules the night before.

## 3. Carry the right stock
- **Narrow range:** 1–5 items you can describe in one call. Speed beats choice on foot.
- Weight and balance: a cooler box on a strap, backpack, tray or cart; know your carrying limit
  for a full day — fatigue is the real constraint.
- Pre-pack bundles and change-friendly prices ("2 for £5").
- Keep a small reserve of best-sellers; know your sell-out signal for returning to restock.

## 4. Serve in under 30 seconds
1. Call the product and price in one short line: "Cold water, two pounds!"
2. Make eye contact, approach the hand that's raised.
3. Hand the item first only if you're comfortable; many vendors take payment as they hand over.
4. Take payment, give change from a pre-sorted pouch, thank, move.
Round prices and a pre-counted float of common change make it fast.

## 5. Cash and card on foot
- **Card/mobile payments:** a pocket card reader paired to your phone (common providers
  include Square, SumUp, Zettle — fees and availability vary by country); or QR-code payment
  apps common in many markets. Test signal at the venue — stadiums often have poor coverage;
  check whether your reader supports offline payments and the risk if they later decline.
- **Cash:** apron or body pouch with notes separated by value; remove large notes regularly to a
  secure inner pocket or drop at your restock point. Check large notes for counterfeits.
- Never count big cash in public; record takings at restock points.

## 6. Safety and conduct
Don't block exits, aisles or traffic. Avoid aggressive selling — it gets vendors banned. Work
with a partner in crowded or late settings; hydrate and use sun protection; know the
venue's lost-person and first-aid points.

## Gotchas
- Unlicensed roving trade is often confiscated on the spot.
- Ice melts, chocolate melts, phones die: plan for heat and battery (power bank).
- Big venue crowds make change slow — price for round notes/coins.
- Licensing names and card-reader providers are examples; confirm locally.

## Verify
- [ ] Licence/venue approval and food registration (if needed) in hand.
- [ ] Route mapped to crowd timings with restock points.
- [ ] Range limited to items that sell in seconds; prices round.
- [ ] Card reader tested at the venue; offline behaviour understood.
- [ ] Cash separated, large notes removed regularly, takings recorded at each restock.
