# Setting up a stall that pulls buyers

**Goal:** passers-by notice the stall from a distance, understand what you sell in a glance,
stop, touch, and buy — on a pitch you're allowed to trade from.

## 1. Pitch selection
- **Permission first:** most markets and streets need a licence or a pitch fee paid to the
  market operator or local council; trading without one risks fines and confiscated stock.
  Rules vary widely by city and country.
- **Footfall line:** stand at the busiest route at the times you'll trade — count people
  passing in 5 minutes at two candidate spots if you have a choice.
- Best spots: near entrances, corners where two flows cross, next to a popular food stall
  (queues = captive browsers). Avoid dead ends, behind pillars, downhill of drains.
- Regulars at fixed markets get the best pitches; turn up consistently to earn one.
- Check sun direction (shade for you and your goods) and wind exposure.

## 2. Layout and display
- **Front table at waist height**, stepped up toward the back with crates, boxes or
  risers so everything is visible from 3 m away. Flat displays get ignored.
- **Abundance:** full, neat stacks sell; three sad items don't. Restock from under the table
  as you sell.
- **Colour and blocks:** group by colour or type; put your eye-catchers (brightest,
  most unusual, best value) at the front corners where people approach.
- **Touchable:** let people pick things up; keep fragile/high value nearer you.
- **Space to stop:** leave the front open so people can stand without blocking the aisle.
- Kit list: gazebo/umbrella with weights (sandbags/water weights — gazebos blow over),
  trestle table, cloth to floor (hides stock), crates, clips, bags, float, card reader,
  phone power bank, rain covers, bin bags.

## 3. Signage and prices
- One **big name/what-you-sell sign** readable from 10 m.
- **Price every item or every group** — many buyers won't ask. Big, clear numbers: "3 for £5"
  beats a price list. Use a pricing approach from
  sales-marketing.street-informal-trade.market-pricing.day-pricing-and-end-of-day-clearance.
- Show you take card ("Card accepted" sign) — many buyers carry no cash now.

## 4. The pitch and patter
- **Be standing, facing the aisle, not on your phone.** A seated trader looks closed.
- **Open with a call** that names the offer: "Fresh strawberries, two punnets a fiver!"
  Short, rhythmic, repeated; vary it so it doesn't drone.
- **A crowd pulls a crowd:** demonstrate (slice the fruit, show the gadget working, give a
  taste) to stop the first two people; others stop to see what they're looking at.
- Speak to the person who's touching something: "That one's handmade, feel the weight."
- Close quickly: bag it while you talk, offer the bundle ("Take three, I'll do it for ten").

## Gotchas
- Food sampling and selling needs food-hygiene registration in many places.
- Don't block neighbours' frontage or argue over boundaries — markets run on goodwill.
- Weather kills trade and stock; pack waterproof covers and take wet days seriously.
- Keep cash in a body bag/apron, not a box on the table.

## Verify
- [ ] Licence/pitch fee sorted with the operator or council.
- [ ] Pitch chosen on counted footfall; gazebo weighted.
- [ ] Display tiered and full, visible from 3 m, eye-catchers at the front corners.
- [ ] Every item or group priced; card accepted and signed.
- [ ] You're standing, calling, and demonstrating at least every few minutes.
- [ ] End of day: note sales by hour and item to improve next week's layout.
