# Day pricing and end-of-day clearance

**Goal:** price so the day covers all costs and makes a profit, sells most of what you
brought, and doesn't leave you carrying spoiling or bulky stock home.

## 1. Know your daily costs and break-even
```
day_costs  = pitch fee + transport + help/wages + bags/packaging + card fees + food
item_cost  = what you paid per unit (incl. delivery/share of waste)
break_even_sales = day_costs / average_margin_%
```
Example: £60 day costs, 50% average margin → you need £120 of sales just to break even.
Write this number down; it tells you whether a quiet day justifies staying.

## 2. Set the floor: cost-plus
- Unit floor = item cost + enough margin to cover a share of day costs.
- Markup vs margin: a 100% markup on £1 cost = £2 price = 50% margin. Don't confuse them.
- Typical markups vary hugely by goods: perishables need a higher markup to cover waste;
  second-hand goods are priced on what they'll fetch, not cost.

## 3. Check what the market bears
- Walk the market before opening: note competitors' prices for the same or similar items.
- Shop and supermarket prices nearby set a ceiling for commodities (fruit, batteries).
- Unique/handmade items can price above cost-plus — test high first; you can come down,
  going up is hard.
- Watch the first hour: selling out fast = too cheap; nothing moving with interest = too dear.

## 4. Use selling price points
- **Round numbers** speed cash trade and change: £1, £2, £5, £10 (or local equivalents).
- **Multi-buys:** "1 for £2, 3 for £5" lifts units per customer and moves stock.
- **Price ladders:** have a cheap entry item, a mid seller and a premium piece on every table.

## 5. Events and seasons
- Festivals, holidays, sports events and tourist peaks: higher footfall, often higher
  willingness to pay; bring more stock, consider a modest price uplift on in-demand items.
- Off-season and bad weather: fewer buyers; drop slow lines, bundle more.
- Seasonal goods (decorations, summer items): start high early in the season, step down as
  the date passes; the day after a holiday, seasonal stock is near worthless.

## 6. End-of-day clearance (perishables especially)
- Decide your **clearance time** (often the last 60–90 minutes) and the stages:
  1. Multi-buy deepens ("3 for £4").
  2. "Everything on this table £1."
  3. Final 15–20 minutes: bowl/box deals at near cost.
- Compare clearance price to the alternative: tomorrow's value (spoilage), carrying it home,
  or waste. Selling at cost beats binning.
- Don't start clearance too early or regulars learn to wait.
- Non-perishables: clear only if storage or transport is the problem.

## Gotchas
- Forgetting card-processing fees (typically a few % per transaction — check your provider).
- Under-pricing to beat the neighbour starts a race to the bottom; compete on quality and display.
- Track waste: if you clear 30% every day, you're bringing too much.
- Some markets regulate pricing displays and units (weights must use legal units); check rules.
