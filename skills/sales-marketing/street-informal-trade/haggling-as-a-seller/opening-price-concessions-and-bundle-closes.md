# Opening price, concessions and bundle closes

**Goal:** close the sale at a price above your floor, with the buyer feeling they got a good
deal — and coming back. Where haggling is expected (many markets and bazaars), the ritual
is part of the product.

## 1. Know your numbers before anyone asks
For each item or line:
- **Cost** (what you paid, plus a share of pitch fee and transport).
- **Floor:** the lowest price you'll accept today. Write it in code on the tag or in your head.
- **Target:** the price you'd be happy with.
- **Opening:** your first price. In haggling cultures, open with room — often noticeably
  above target, but not absurd, or serious buyers walk. Where fixed prices are the norm
  (much of Northern Europe/US retail markets), keep the gap small.

## 2. Read the buyer
- **Locals vs tourists, regulars vs one-off:** regulars deserve your real price; they'll
  be back weekly.
- **Interest signals:** handling the item repeatedly, asking about it, coming back.
- **Their budget hints:** "What's your best price?" means they're buying; "I'm just looking"
  means build interest before talking price.
- Let them make the first counter after your opening. Don't negotiate against yourself
  ("150... OK, 130") before they've said a number.

## 3. Concession rhythm
- Concede in **shrinking steps**: e.g. 150 → 135 → 128 → 125. Shrinking steps signal the floor
  is near; equal steps invite more pushing.
- **Get something for every concession:** "I can do 130 if you take it now / pay cash / take two."
- Pause before conceding; a quick drop looks like you priced too high.
- Stay friendly and use humour; it's a game both sides enjoy when it's respectful.

## 4. Bundle closes (protect your price)
Rather than cutting unit price, add value:
- "Take two for 220 instead of 125 each."
- "At 125, I'll throw in the small one."
- Round to a convenient number: "Call it 100 for both."
Bundles move more stock and keep your headline price intact for the next buyer.

## 5. When they walk away
- A walk-away is often theatre. If their last offer was above your floor, call a final
  number as they leave: "OK — 120, last price, only for you." Once.
- If it's below your floor, let them go, politely: "Sorry, I can't do that one — come back
  if you change your mind." Many do.
- Never chase twice; it destroys your floor for everyone watching.

## Gotchas
- Others are listening — the price you give one buyer becomes the price for the crowd.
- Don't drop below cost to "win"; you lose money on every win.
- Haggling norms differ by culture and market; read neighbouring stalls first.
- Watch counterfeit notes when cash deals close fast; check large notes.

## Verify
- [ ] Each line has a known cost, floor, target and opening.
- [ ] You let the buyer counter before you moved.
- [ ] Concessions came in shrinking steps, each traded for something.
- [ ] Bundles offered before cutting unit price.
- [ ] End-of-day: average sale price vs target recorded per line.
