# Matching packages and upselling trips

**Goal:** turn an enquiry into a booking that fits what the traveller actually wants,
at the highest value they're happy to pay — and have them thank you for the upgrade.

## 1. Qualify the enquiry (5–10 minutes, before searching anything)
Ask and note:
- **Who**: number of travellers, ages (child ages on travel date matter for pricing),
  mobility or dietary needs, occasion (honeymoon, anniversary, milestone birthday).
- **When**: dates, flexibility (± days), school-holiday constraints.
- **Where / what kind**: destination or "somewhere warm"; beach, city, adventure, cruise.
- **Budget**: total or per person, and whether flights are included in it. Ask directly:
  "So I don't show you things you'd never book, what would you like to spend in total?"
- **Must-haves vs nice-to-haves**: all-inclusive, adults-only, direct flights, pool, kids' club.
- **Past trips**: best and worst holiday and why — the fastest way to learn their taste.
- **Departure airport** and luggage needs.

## 2. Quote good / better / best
Present three options, not ten:
- **Good**: meets every must-have, within budget.
- **Better**: the one you'd recommend — usually slightly above budget, with a clear reason.
- **Best**: the stretch option (upgraded room, premium cabin, better location).
For each: one-line *why this fits you* ("adults-only, 2 min walk to the beach you asked
about"), total price, what's included, what isn't. People commonly choose the middle option
when the comparison is clear.

## 3. Upgrade selling — sell the experience, then the price difference
- **Room / cabin**: sea view, balcony cabin, suite, club-level. Frame per person per night:
  "The balcony cabin is about <X> per person per day more — that's your private
  breakfast spot every morning."
- **Flights**: premium economy / business for long-haul, extra legroom seats, flexible fares.
- **Board basis**: half-board → all-inclusive with a quick daily-spend comparison.
- **Occasion extras**: tell the hotel/cruise line about honeymoons and anniversaries;
  many offer touches, and paid packages exist.
Offer upgrades when they matter most: at quote stage, and again before final balance
(availability permitting).

## 4. Tours and excursions
Recommend 2–3 relevant ones, not a catalogue. Mention when pre-booking is wise (popular
sights selling out, cruise excursions with limited places). Be honest when an independent
option is fine.

## 5. Convert: options, deadlines, deposits
- Many tour operators and cruise lines let you place an **option** (a courtesy hold) for a
  short period — often 24–72 h; terms vary. Place it, tell the client exactly when it expires.
- Explain prices are live and may change until booked.
- Ask for the booking: "Shall I secure it with the deposit today so you don't lose the
  room?" Confirm deposit amount, balance due date and cancellation terms in writing.
- Send the confirmation and check names exactly match passports.

## Gotchas
- Searching before qualifying → endless requotes.
- Quoting per-person prices when the client thinks in totals (or vice versa) — state both.
- Upgrade pushed against a stated budget with no reason — erodes trust.
- Name mismatches with passports can mean costly changes or denied boarding.
- Consumer-protection rules for packages (e.g. EU/UK Package Travel Regulations, ATOL in the
  UK) affect how you sell and document — check your licensing obligations (not detailed here).

## Verify
- [ ] Enquiry form complete: who, when, where, budget, must-haves, past trips.
- [ ] Quote offers three options, each with a fit reason and full price.
- [ ] Upgrades offered with a per-day/per-person framing.
- [ ] Option expiry, deposit, balance date and cancellation terms sent in writing.
- [ ] Booking names checked against passports.
