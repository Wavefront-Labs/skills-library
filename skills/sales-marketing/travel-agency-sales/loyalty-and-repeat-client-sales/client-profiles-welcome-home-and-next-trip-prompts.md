# Client profiles, welcome-home calls and next-trip prompts

**Goal:** make rebooking with you easier and more personal than booking anywhere else,
so a one-off client becomes a client for life.

## 1. The client profile (fill it during the first booking, update after every trip)
Fields in your CRM / agency system (or a structured note if you have nothing else):
- Travellers: names as on passports, dates of birth, passport expiry dates.
- **Loyalty numbers**: airline frequent-flyer, hotel, car hire, cruise past-guest numbers.
- Preferences: seat (aisle/window), bed type, room location (high floor, quiet), board basis,
  dietary, mobility, pace (relaxed vs packed), budget band.
- **Key dates**: birthdays, anniversaries, school-holiday patterns, usual travel months.
- Trip history: where, when, what they loved, what disappointed.
- Wish list: "one day we want to..." destinations.
- Communication preference and marketing consent (store it; respect it — GDPR or local
  privacy law may apply).

## 2. Use loyalty programs for the client
- Add their loyalty numbers to every booking (PNR/booking notes) so they earn points and
  status benefits.
- Offer to enrol them in programs they don't belong to, explaining the benefit.
- Cruise lines in particular reward repeat guests; mention their past-guest tier and
  any onboard-booking or past-guest offers you can apply (terms vary by line).
- If your agency has its own program (points, member rates, priority service), enrol at
  first booking.

## 3. Pre-trip touches
- Send a "countdown" note (e.g. 2 weeks out) with a tip for the destination.
- Notify the hotel/cruise of occasions from the profile.

## 4. Welcome-home call or message (within ~2–5 days of return)
Script:
1. "Welcome back — how was it?" Listen; note highs and lows in the profile.
2. Fix anything that went wrong (chase supplier compensation, log the complaint).
3. Ask permission for a review or testimonial if they're happy.
4. Ask for referrals: "Who do you know who'd enjoy a trip like this?"
5. Plant the next trip: "You mentioned <wish-list place> — when are you thinking?"
Calls beat emails here; keep the email as a fallback.

## 5. Dated next-trip prompts
Create tasks in the CRM, not reminders in your head:
- **Booking window prompt**: if they always travel in July, contact them in January/February
  (or when that year's prices/itineraries release).
- **Anniversary of last trip**: "A year ago you were in <place>..."
- **Key dates**: milestone birthdays and anniversaries 6–12 months ahead.
- **Passport expiry**: remind ~9–12 months before (many countries require several months
  of validity at entry).
- **Supplier triggers**: new itineraries, sales, early-booking offers that match their profile.
Every prompt should mention something personal from the profile — that's what direct
booking sites can't do.

## 6. Measure
- Repeat-client rate (clients booking again within 12–24 months).
- Referrals received and converted.
- % of returned clients who got a welcome-home contact within a week.

## Gotchas
- Profiles started and never updated; update at booking and at welcome-home every time.
- Loyalty numbers missing from bookings — clients notice lost points.
- Generic newsletters as the only contact; they don't drive rebooking on their own.
- Marketing to people who didn't consent.

## Verify
- [ ] Every client has a profile with preferences, loyalty numbers and key dates.
- [ ] Loyalty numbers attached to every booking component.
- [ ] Welcome-home contact made within a week of return and logged.
- [ ] At least one dated next-trip task exists for every active client.
- [ ] Repeat-client rate reviewed quarterly.
