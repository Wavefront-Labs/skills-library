# Commission structures and service fees

**Goal:** know what each booking earns before you quote it, use preferred-supplier
deals without short-changing the client, and charge fees where commission doesn't pay
for your time.

Commission percentages below are typical ranges reported in the trade, not guarantees;
they vary by supplier, market, consortium and host agency. Check your own agreements.

## 1. How the money flows
- **Commission model**: the supplier (tour operator, cruise line, hotel, car hire) pays the
  agency a percentage of the commissionable fare — often around 10–16% for packages and
  cruises, varying for hotels. Taxes, port charges and fees are usually *non-commissionable*.
- **Overrides**: extra percentage points paid when the agency (or its consortium/host
  agency) hits volume targets with a supplier, or on promoted sailings/products.
- **Net rates + markup**: some suppliers (wholesalers, DMCs, some hotels) give net prices;
  you set the selling price. Check local rules on price display and disclosure.
- **Airlines**: many airlines pay little or no base commission on standard tickets for
  most agencies; agencies typically charge a ticketing fee instead.
- **Host agency split** (for independent advisors): the host takes a share of commission
  in return for supplier access, systems and accreditation; splits vary widely.
- **Timing**: commission is often paid after travel (or after final payment), not at booking.

## 2. Know your earnings per quote
Before quoting, note for each option: commissionable amount, rate, any override, expected
commission, payment timing. A simple quote sheet column `Est. commission` keeps it visible.

## 3. Preferred-supplier steering — within client interest
- Preferred suppliers (via your consortium or host) give higher commission, better
  support, amenities for clients and sometimes better availability.
- **Rule**: steer only between options that genuinely fit the brief. If a non-preferred
  supplier is clearly better for this client, book it. Recommending a worse fit for
  commission damages repeat business and may breach consumer-protection or disclosure
  rules in some places.
- Use preferred perks as a client benefit: "Booking through us adds <onboard credit /
  breakfast / upgrade on arrival> through our partner program."

## 4. When to charge a service fee
Charge (and say so up front) when commission doesn't cover the work:
- Complex multi-stop itineraries with lots of non-commissionable parts.
- Air-only tickets.
- Clients who request extensive planning before committing.
- Changes and cancellations requiring significant rework.
Common forms: **planning/consultation fee** (often credited against the booking),
**per-ticket fee**, **change fee**, or an annual membership for frequent clients.
Put fees in your terms and conditions, list them on the quote, and get written acceptance.

## 5. Track and reconcile commission
Spreadsheet or agency system with: booking ref, supplier, client, travel date, gross
commissionable amount, rate, expected commission, date expected, date received, amount
received, variance.
- Monthly: chase anything unpaid ~30–60 days after travel; send the supplier the booking
  reference, travel dates and expected amount.
- Cancellations and amendments change commission — update the expected amount.
- Bookings made by clients directly after your quote usually earn nothing; that's why a
  planning fee for heavy research is sensible.

## Gotchas
- Quoting a price "including our commission" in a way that misleads — be transparent.
- Assuming taxes and port fees are commissionable.
- Not registering the booking under your agency ID (IATA/CLIA/TIDS or the supplier's agent
  code) → no commission.
- Commission clawback if the client cancels inside the penalty period without insurance.

## Verify
- [ ] Each quote shows expected commission and timing internally.
- [ ] Preferred supplier chosen only when it fits the brief as well as alternatives.
- [ ] Service fees listed in T&Cs and accepted in writing before work starts.
- [ ] Booking made under your agency ID/agent code.
- [ ] Commission reconciliation reviewed monthly; overdue items chased.
