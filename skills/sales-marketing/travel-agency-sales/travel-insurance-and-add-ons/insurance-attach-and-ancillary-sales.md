# Insurance attach and ancillary sales

**Goal:** every client is offered travel insurance at the right moment with the facts
they need to decide, the decision is recorded, and useful extras are sold when the
client is most receptive.

Selling or arranging insurance is regulated in many countries (e.g. you may need to be an
appointed representative of an insurer or authorised broker, and specific disclosures are
mandatory). This skill covers the sales craft; **confirm your licensing and the exact
wording you're allowed to use** with your insurer/compliance team — not verified here.

## 1. Offer insurance at booking — always, to everyone
Timing matters: cancellation cover typically starts protecting the client from when the
policy is bought, so the deposit moment is the natural point.
Script outline:
1. **Why now**: "Your deposit is non-refundable from today. If you had to cancel because
   of illness, would you want that covered?"
2. **What it covers** (from the policy summary): cancellation/curtailment, emergency
   medical and repatriation, baggage, delay, personal liability. Mention limits and excess.
3. **What it doesn't**: common exclusions — undeclared pre-existing medical conditions,
   certain activities (e.g. some winter sports or diving unless added), travel against
   government advice. Point to the policy document.
4. **Medical declaration**: the client must declare pre-existing conditions accurately;
   tell them clearly that non-disclosure can void the policy.
5. Offer the right level (single trip vs annual multi-trip; standard vs premium; add-ons
   like winter sports, cruise cover, gadget cover).
Don't advise in ways you aren't authorised to (e.g. "you don't need to declare that").

## 2. If they decline
- Ask whether they have cover elsewhere (annual policy, bank account, credit card). Suggest
  they check it covers cancellation and medical limits suitable for the destination.
- **Record the decline**: many agencies use a signed or emailed insurance-decline form.
  It protects the agency if a claim situation arises later.
- Offer again at final balance — many clients reconsider when the full amount is due.

## 3. Ancillaries at booking
Offer only what fits the trip, as a short checklist rather than a hard sell:
- **Airport transfers** (shared vs private; private for families, late arrivals, mobility needs).
- **Car hire** — check driver ages, licence requirements (an International Driving Permit
  may be needed in some countries), excess and excess-waiver options.
- **Airport parking / hotel the night before** for early flights.
- **Seats, extra baggage, priority boarding**, lounge access.
- **Experiences** pre-booked where they sell out.

## 4. Pre-departure contact (often ~2–4 weeks before travel)
A call or email with: document check (passport validity rules for the destination, visas/ETAs,
vaccination info pointers), balance confirmation, transfer and car-hire reminders,
upgrade availability, insurance check ("Do you have your policy number handy?"), and an
offer for anything not yet booked. Keep notes on what was accepted.

## 5. Measure
- **Insurance attach rate** = bookings with insurance sold or confirmed-held / all bookings.
- Ancillary revenue per booking.
- % of declines with a recorded decline form.

## Gotchas
- Offering insurance only to "older" clients or long trips — offer to everyone, consistently.
- Reading the policy wording from memory; always use the current policy summary.
- Selling car hire without checking the driver's licence validity abroad.
- Forgetting that a decline isn't documented — the most common agency exposure.

## Verify
- [ ] Insurance offered at deposit for every booking using approved wording.
- [ ] Medical-declaration duty explained; client told to read the policy.
- [ ] Decline recorded in writing when insurance isn't taken.
- [ ] Ancillaries checklist offered at booking; pre-departure contact scheduled.
- [ ] Attach rate reviewed monthly.
