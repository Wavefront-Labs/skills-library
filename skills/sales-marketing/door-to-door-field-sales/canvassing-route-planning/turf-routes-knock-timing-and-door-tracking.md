# Canvassing route planning — turf, timing, tracking, re-knocks

**Goal:** spend the shift talking to likely buyers instead of walking past empty
houses — and turn every door you knock into data for the next pass.

## 1. Choose the turf
Score candidate neighbourhoods on:
- **Fit for the offer:** home ownership (for home-improvement, solar, security),
  property age, housing type (single-family vs apartments with access controls),
  income range, presence of the problem you solve.
- **Density:** doors per street-kilometre. Dense single-family streets = more knocks per hour.
- **Rules:** municipalities with permit restrictions, gated communities, no-knock registries.
- **History:** past sales, cancellations, previous canvassing dates (from your app).
Data sources: census/statistics agency data, public property data where available,
your CRM's past sales heat map.

## 2. Cut turf for each rep/shift
- One shift's turf ≈ expected doors per hour × hours, with a buffer. Typical pace is
  roughly 20–40 doors per hour depending on spacing and conversation length (track your own).
- Cut along natural boundaries (major roads, rivers) so reps don't cross busy roads.
- Plan a **loop**: one side of a street then back the other, or one side per pass on
  busy roads; finish near the car/meeting point.
- Assign turf in the canvassing app (e.g. SPOTIO, SalesRabbit, Knockio-type tools let
  managers draw areas and assign them).

## 3. Knock timing
- Best contact rates are generally when people are home and not busy: late afternoon
  to early evening on weekdays, late morning to afternoon on weekends — **within legal
  hours** for the area.
- Avoid dinner hour in your local culture if contact rates show it; weather shifts
  behaviour (people are home on rainy days but less receptive at the door).
- Use your own data: contact rate by hour and day after a few weeks beats general advice.

## 4. Track every door
Record status immediately, with a standard code set, e.g.:
| Code | Meaning | Next action |
|---|---|---|
| NH | Not home | Re-knock different time/day |
| NI | Not interested | Re-knock after a long gap (e.g. 3–6 months) or never if firm |
| CB | Callback requested | Scheduled appointment |
| S | Sale | Onboarding |
| NS | No soliciting sign / do not knock | Never knock |
| X | Not qualified (renter, already has product) | Skip |
Add notes (dog, gate code not shared, best time). GPS-pinned entries prevent duplicate knocks.

## 5. Metrics per shift
- **Doors knocked**, **contacts** (someone answered), **pitches**, **sales**.
- Contact rate = contacts ÷ doors; close rate = sales ÷ pitches.
- Low contact rate → change timing or turf; low pitch-to-contact → work on the opener;
  low close rate → work on pitch/close.

## 6. Re-knock strategy
- Not-homes: re-knock at a **different time of day and day of week** within 1–2 weeks;
  the second pass often reaches a large share of first-pass not-homes.
- Pass 3 only if contact or sales rates justify it.
- Callback appointments get priority routing.
- Leave approved leave-behind materials only where legal (some places restrict
  mailbox use — e.g. in the US, only postal mail may go in mailboxes).

## Gotchas
- Logging outcomes at the end of the day loses accuracy; log at each door.
- Overlapping turfs between reps annoy residents and waste time.
- Burning out a neighbourhood with too-frequent passes triggers complaints and bans.
- Safety: share live location with the team; plan routes away from hazards after dark.

## Verify
- [ ] Turf chosen with a written fit/density/legal score.
- [ ] Each rep has a non-overlapping, assigned turf loop sized to the shift.
- [ ] Knocks happen inside legal hours; timing adjusted from contact-rate data.
- [ ] 100% of doors coded in the app with GPS; do-not-knock doors flagged.
- [ ] Not-homes re-knocked at a different time within two weeks.
