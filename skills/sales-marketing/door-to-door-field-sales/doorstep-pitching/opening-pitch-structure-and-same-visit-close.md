# Doorstep pitching — opening, pitch, same-visit close

**Goal:** earn the next 30 seconds, then the next five minutes, then a decision — at a
stranger's door, without pressure tactics that breach trust or the law.

## 0. Legal first (check before you knock)
Door-to-door selling is regulated almost everywhere, differently by place:
- **Permits/licences:** many towns require a solicitation permit; carry it and your ID badge.
- **Hours:** local and national rules limit knocking times (commonly no early mornings,
  evenings or certain days) — know yours.
- **"No soliciting"/"Do not knock" signs:** respect them; in many places it's a legal
  requirement, everywhere it's good practice.
- **Cooling-off rights:** most jurisdictions give doorstep buyers a cancellation period
  (e.g. the US FTC Cooling-Off Rule — 3 business days for many sales; EU/UK distance and
  off-premises rules — 14 days). You must tell the buyer in writing. Never discourage cancellation.
- Leave immediately when asked.

## 1. The approach (first 10 seconds)
- Knock or ring, then **step back** a pace and stand at an angle — less threatening than
  square-on at the door.
- Clipboard or tablet visible, lanyard ID visible; smile; no sunglasses.
- Open with **who you are + why their street + a local reference**, not a product:
  "Hi, I'm Sam with <Company>. We're doing the <service> for a few of your neighbours —
  the Garcias two doors down started last week — and I'm just letting the rest of the
  street know while we're here."
- Neighbour proof (only if true!) and a reason you're here today beat any clever line.
- A **pattern interrupt** (an unexpected but honest remark) can break the reflex "no
  thanks" — e.g. "I'm not here to sell you anything today unless it saves you money, fair
  enough?" Use sparingly and only honestly.

## 2. The pitch (2–5 minutes)
1. **One qualifying question** tied to their situation: "Have you had any problems with
   ants this season?" / "Who's your provider now?"
2. **Problem → simple solution → proof:** what you do in one sentence, why it matters
   for *their* house, one number or neighbour example.
3. **Offer** with a reason to act today that is real (crew in area this week, local promo).
4. **Check-in question:** "Does that make sense for your place?"
Keep the product demo physical where possible (sample, photo on tablet, quick bill
comparison). Stay on the step unless invited in.

## 3. Close on the same visit
- **Assumptive transition:** "The crew's here Thursday or Friday — which is better?"
- Handle one or two objections (see
  `sales-marketing.door-to-door-field-sales.objection-handling-in-the-field.brush-offs-stalls-and-referral-turns`).
- Paperwork: complete it accurately, explain price, terms and the cancellation right,
  leave copies. A rushed contract becomes a cancellation.
- If not today: ask for a specific callback time, or a referral.

## 4. After every door
Log the outcome immediately in the canvassing app/sheet (not home, no, callback, sale,
sign) — see `sales-marketing.door-to-door-field-sales.canvassing-route-planning.turf-routes-knock-timing-and-door-tracking`.

## Gotchas
- Inventing neighbour references or "inspection" pretexts is deceptive and often illegal.
- Talking too long before a question; aim to ask within the first 20–30 seconds.
- High-pressure closes produce cancellations, chargebacks and complaints that cost the
  territory its permit.
- Safety: don't enter homes alone if company policy forbids it; leave if you feel unsafe;
  share your route.

## Verify
- [ ] Permit, ID and knock hours confirmed for this territory.
- [ ] Opener names company, reason and a true local reference within 10 seconds.
- [ ] A qualifying question asked before the offer.
- [ ] Every sale had cancellation rights explained in writing and copies left.
- [ ] Each door outcome logged before the next knock.
