# Appointment setting — qualify, book, confirm

**Goal:** booked appointments that "sit" — the right people, at the agreed time, expecting
the conversation — so closers spend their time selling, not driving to empty houses.

Common in setter–closer teams (solar, home improvement, security, B2B SDR → AE).

## 1. Qualify on the spot (2–3 minutes)
Ask naturally, in conversation, not as an interrogation. Typical criteria:
- **Fit:** do they have the problem/need, and does the offer apply (e.g. homeowner,
  roof direction for solar, company size for B2B)?
- **Authority:** who else decides? Married/partnered homeowners usually decide together —
  "Who else would want to be part of a decision like this?"
- **Timing:** is anything happening that makes now relevant (bill increase, renovation,
  contract renewal)?
- **Budget/means** where relevant, phrased gently ("Are you open to options that
  are paid monthly?").
Frameworks like **BANT** (budget, authority, need, timing) are a useful checklist.
Don't book unqualified people to hit a set target; they don't sit and they waste closer time.

## 2. Ask for the appointment
- Give a reason for the meeting and what they get from it: "The specialist will measure
  up and show you the actual numbers for your house — takes about 45 minutes."
- **Alternative-choice:** "Is Tuesday at 6 or Thursday at 6 better, when both of you are home?"
- Insist (politely) on **all decision-makers present**; a one-legged appointment rarely closes.
- Confirm length and what to have ready (recent bills, measurements, account access).

## 3. Lock the commitment
- Enter it in the shared calendar/CRM on the spot; send a calendar invite or text
  confirmation while you're still with them if possible.
- Repeat back: day, date, time, who's attending, address, the name of who's coming.
- Get the best mobile number and permission to text reminders (consent rules for
  texts vary — follow your company's compliance policy).
- Ask a micro-commitment: "If something comes up, will you text me so we can move it
  rather than miss it?"

## 4. Confirm and remind
Typical sequence (adapt to sales cycle):
1. Immediately: confirmation text/email with closer's name and photo, time, what to expect.
2. The day before: confirmation call or text asking for a reply ("Reply YES to confirm").
3. 1–2 hours before: "<Closer> is on the way" text.
Unconfirmed appointments get a call; if no response, reschedule rather than send a closer blind.

## 5. Hand-off notes to the closer
Write: who lives there/attends, the need in their words, objections already raised,
qualifying answers, rapport details (dog's name, kids' sport), anything promised.
Closers who walk in knowing this convert better and customers don't repeat themselves.

## 6. Measure
- **Set rate** (appointments ÷ conversations), **sit/show rate** (held ÷ booked),
  **close rate on sits**. A rising set rate with falling sit or close rate signals
  over-booking of weak leads.
- Track no-show reasons and fix the pattern (weekend slots, booking too far ahead —
  appointments set many days out tend to show less).

## Gotchas
- Booking too far out (more than a few days) increases no-shows; book soon.
- Promising things the closer can't deliver ("free system", "guaranteed savings").
- Setting appointments with a renter or a teenager at the door.
- Setter pay structures that reward sets, not sits, encourage junk bookings.

## Verify
- [ ] Each booking meets the written qualifying criteria.
- [ ] All decision-makers confirmed for the slot.
- [ ] Appointment in the CRM with confirmation sent at booking.
- [ ] Day-before confirmation and on-the-way message sent.
- [ ] Hand-off notes complete; sit and close rates tracked by setter.
