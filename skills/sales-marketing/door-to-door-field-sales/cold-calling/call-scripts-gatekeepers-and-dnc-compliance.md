# Cold calling — scripts, gatekeepers, compliance

**Goal:** more conversations that end in a next step, from a calling list that is
legally callable, using a script that sounds like a person.

## 1. Compliance before the first dial
Rules differ by country, and between consumer (B2C) and business (B2B) calls:
- **Do-not-call registers:** scrub your list before calling and at the intervals the
  law requires — e.g. the US National Do Not Call Registry (plus state lists), UK TPS
  (consumers) and CTPS (businesses), Canada's DNCL, Australia's Do Not Call Register.
- **Internal do-not-call list:** record every "don't call me again" immediately and honour it.
- **Calling hours:** e.g. US Telemarketing Sales Rule allows consumer calls only between
  8 am and 9 pm local time of the called party; other countries have their own limits.
- **Autodialers, prerecorded messages and texts** to mobiles often need prior consent
  (US TCPA; UK PECR). Manual dialling vs automated systems can change the rules — get
  legal advice for your setup.
- **Caller ID:** display a real, callable number.
- **Recording:** some jurisdictions require all-party consent; announce recording if unsure.
Fines are often per call. When in doubt, ask compliance before launching a list.

## 2. Build the script (one page)
1. **Opener (first ~10 seconds):** name, company, and a reason for calling *them*.
   - Permission-based: "Hi Alex, it's Jordan from <Company>. This is a cold call — can I
     have 30 seconds to tell you why I called, then you can decide if we keep talking?"
     Honest openers like this tend to beat "How are you today?".
2. **Hook / reason:** one relevant problem or trigger ("We help dental practices cut
   no-shows — I noticed you've opened a second location").
3. **Qualifying questions (2–3):** open questions about their current situation.
4. **Value statement** tailored to the answer, with one proof point.
5. **Ask for a small next step:** a 15-minute meeting, a site visit, sending a quote —
   with two time options.
6. **Objection notes:** 4–5 common brush-offs and a one-line response each (see
   `sales-marketing.door-to-door-field-sales.objection-handling-in-the-field.brush-offs-stalls-and-referral-turns`).
Write it as bullet prompts, not a paragraph to read aloud. Practise until you can
deliver it naturally; record calls (with consent) and review.

## 3. Gatekeepers
- Be polite, brief and confident: "Hi, it's Jordan from <Company>, could you put me
  through to Alex please?" Using first name and a calm tone works better than pitching
  the receptionist.
- If asked "what's it about?": give a short, honest, specific reason; ask for their help
  ("Who handles appointment scheduling there?").
- Treat gatekeepers as allies — learn their name, best times, and the decision-maker's
  correct title. Never lie about a prior relationship.

## 4. Voicemail
Under ~25 seconds: name, company, one-line reason relevant to them, number said twice
slowly, and a reason to call back. Expect few callbacks; voicemail mostly raises
recognition for your next attempt or email.

## 5. Cadence and productivity
- Block calling time (e.g. 2 × 90 minutes), list prepared beforehand, no email in between.
- Multi-touch cadence for B2B: several call attempts across 2–3 weeks mixed with email
  and LinkedIn, varied times of day.
- Track dials, connects, conversations, meetings booked; review connect-to-meeting rate weekly.
- Use a CRM/dialler that logs outcomes and enforces DNC flags automatically.

## Gotchas
- Rushing the opener or reading robotically ends calls in seconds.
- Pitching features before learning anything about the prospect.
- Calling consumers on mobile with an autodialer without consent (US) is high-risk.
- Bad data (wrong numbers) wastes time — verify lists and remove disconnected numbers.

## Verify
- [ ] List scrubbed against applicable DNC registers and internal DNC list within the required window.
- [ ] Calling hours and recording-consent rules checked for every region called.
- [ ] Script fits one page, opener states reason in under 10 seconds.
- [ ] Every call ends with a next step requested.
- [ ] Dials, connects and meetings booked logged; weekly review done.
