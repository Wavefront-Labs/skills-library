# Fee, term, exclusivity and renewal terms

**Goal:** a sponsorship deal both sides will renew — the rights holder gets a fee that
reflects value, the brand gets protected, measurable rights — with no ambiguity left for
the lawyers to fight over later.

## 1. Prepare (most of the result is decided here)
- **Your walk-away (BATNA):** the alternative if this deal fails — another sponsor
  in the category, another property, or spending the budget on media.
- **Reservation price:** the lowest fee you'll take (rights holder) or highest you'll pay
  (brand), derived from valuation (see
  sales-marketing.sponsorship-and-brand-deals.sponsorship-packaging.rights-inventory-tiers-and-valuation).
- **Their likely constraints:** budget cycle, fiscal year, existing contracts, internal
  approvers, competitor pressure.
- **Tradeables list:** variables you can move other than price — term length, payment
  timing, exclusivity scope, hospitality, content, value-in-kind (VIK), renewal rights.

## 2. Open and trade
- **Anchor credibly:** the rights holder opens with the full-value package and its
  justification; the brand opens by restating objectives and a budget envelope, not a number
  pulled from air.
- **Trade, don't give:** every concession gets something back. "We can reduce the fee 10%
  if you commit to three years" / "We'll pay the full fee if exclusivity covers all
  non-alcoholic beverages, not just soft drinks."
- Concede in shrinking steps; it signals you're nearing your limit.
- **Value-in-kind** (product, services, media) should be valued at cost-to-the-receiver,
  not the sponsor's retail price, and capped as a % of the deal.

## 3. Clauses that matter (term-sheet checklist)
- **Fee and payment schedule** — instalments tied to dates or delivery; late payment terms.
- **Term** — start/end, season alignment.
- **Category exclusivity** — exact category definition, carve-outs for existing partners
  and venue/league deals, and remedy if breached.
- **Rights delivered** — a schedule listing every asset, quantity and spec.
- **Performance / make-goods** — if an asset isn't delivered (broadcast cancelled, event
  shortened), equivalent replacement value or a pro-rata fee reduction.
- **Performance bonuses** — e.g. bonus on championship, attendance or audience thresholds;
  define measurement source.
- **Escalators** — annual fee increase (fixed % or index-linked) in multi-year deals.
- **Renewal** — right of first negotiation (exclusive talking window) or right of first
  refusal (sponsor can match a rival offer); the latter is more restrictive for the rights holder.
- **Termination & morals clauses** — scandal, insolvency, relegation, cancellation; both
  directions.
- **IP and approvals** — use of marks, approval turnaround times, ambush protection.
Get a lawyer to draft from the agreed term sheet; jurisdiction matters.

## 4. Renewal negotiation
Start 6–12 months before expiry (budgets get set early). Lead with the results recap (see
sales-marketing.sponsorship-and-brand-deals.sponsorship-roi-measurement.media-value-brand-lift-and-renewal-case),
then propose evolution of rights, not just a price increase.

## Gotchas
- Vague category definitions are the most common source of disputes.
- Agreeing price before scope means renegotiating scope downward later.
- Long silent gaps in negotiation often mean internal approval problems — ask directly.
- This is general practice, not legal advice; contract law varies.

## Verify
- [ ] Walk-away, reservation price and tradeables written before the first meeting.
- [ ] Every concession recorded with what was received in return.
- [ ] Term sheet covers fee, schedule, exclusivity definition, rights schedule, make-goods,
  bonuses, escalators, renewal, termination and IP.
- [ ] Lawyer-drafted contract matches the term sheet line by line.
- [ ] Renewal talks diarised 6–12 months before expiry.
