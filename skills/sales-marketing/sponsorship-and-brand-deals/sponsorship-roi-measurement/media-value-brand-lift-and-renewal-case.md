# Media value, brand lift and the renewal case

**Goal:** an evidence-based answer to "was it worth it?" that a finance director accepts —
and the basis for renewing, renegotiating or exiting.

## 1. Set the baseline before the deal starts
- Agree objectives and KPIs at contract stage (awareness, consideration, sales, leads,
  hospitality pipeline).
- Run a **pre-wave brand survey** in the target audience and capture baseline sales/leads in
  the relevant region/channel. Without a baseline, lift can't be shown.

## 2. Exposure and media value (measure, then discount)
- Exposure = how often and how clearly the brand was seen: broadcast seconds on screen,
  logo clarity/size, social impressions, attendance.
- **Media value equivalent** = exposure converted at comparable media cost (e.g. on-screen
  seconds × 30-second spot rate ÷ 30, or impressions × CPM ÷ 1,000).
- Apply a **quality discount**: a logo partially visible for 2 seconds among many others is
  worth far less than a spot ad. Specialist measurement vendors use computer vision and
  weighting; if doing it yourself, document the discount you apply.
- Report media value as context, never as the ROI on its own — it's an opportunity-cost
  proxy, not revenue.

## 3. Brand lift
- **Pre/post waves** among the target audience: unaided/aided awareness, sponsor recall,
  favourability, consideration, purchase intent.
- Use a **control**: compare people exposed to the sponsorship (fans, attendees) with
  similar non-exposed people; the difference is lift. Without a control, a general
  market trend looks like sponsorship lift.
- Check **sponsor misattribution**: fans often name a competitor or past sponsor — measure
  correct recall.
- Sample sizes: enough per cell for the differences you need to detect (a survey vendor or a
  power calculation can tell you; a few hundred per cell is a common floor).

## 4. Activation attribution
- Unique promo codes, QR codes, landing URLs and UTM-tagged links per activation.
- Sampling: count samples and capture opt-ins; follow up for conversion.
- Hospitality: list of hosted accounts → pipeline and revenue influenced in CRM (agree the
  attribution rule — e.g. hosted within 90 days before an opportunity moved).
- Regional sales test: compare sales in the sponsorship's market vs matched markets.

## 5. Build the renewal business case (2 pages)
1. Objectives and KPIs agreed at the start.
2. Results vs each target, with method notes.
3. Total cost = rights fee + activation + staff time + VIK.
4. Return: incremental gross profit attributed (conservative), brand-lift value if your
   organisation has a model for it, pipeline influenced.
5. **ROI** = (return − cost) ÷ cost, plus **return on objectives** where money isn't the goal.
6. Recommendation: renew as is, renew with changed rights/price, or exit — and which assets
   underdelivered (lever for negotiation, see
   sales-marketing.sponsorship-and-brand-deals.rights-fee-negotiation.fee-term-exclusivity-and-renewal-terms).

## Gotchas
- Inflated media-value figures destroy credibility with finance; be conservative.
- Measure the same way every year for comparability.
- Rights holders: share data proactively — sponsors who can't prove value don't renew.
- Lift from a single season can be noisy; look at multi-year trends.

## Verify
- [ ] Baseline survey and sales captured before the sponsorship launched.
- [ ] Media value reported with a documented quality discount, not as ROI.
- [ ] Brand lift measured against a control group, including correct sponsor recall.
- [ ] Every activation has a tracking mechanism.
- [ ] Renewal case covers total cost, results vs targets, ROI/ROO, and a recommendation.
