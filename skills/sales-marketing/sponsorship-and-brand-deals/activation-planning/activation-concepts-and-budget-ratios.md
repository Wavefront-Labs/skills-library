# Activation concepts and budget ratios

**Goal:** turn bought rights (logos, designations, access) into something fans notice and
act on. A sponsorship with no activation budget is mostly a logo — and logos alone rarely
justify renewal.

## 1. Start from objectives, not assets
Pick one primary objective per audience:
- **Awareness/affinity:** fans recall and like the brand more.
- **Sales:** trial, purchase, sign-ups at or around the property.
- **B2B:** client entertainment, pipeline.
- **Employees:** engagement, recruitment, pride.
Write measurable targets (e.g. 20,000 samples, 3,000 opt-ins, 40 client hosts) — they become
the ROI case (see
sales-marketing.sponsorship-and-brand-deals.sponsorship-roi-measurement.media-value-brand-lift-and-renewal-case).

## 2. Budget activation on top of the fee
- Industry discussion commonly cites spending roughly **1:1 or more** on activation per unit
  of rights fee (often quoted up to 2–3:1 for big deals). These ratios come from industry
  surveys and vary widely — treat as a sanity check, not a rule.
- If the budget can't fund meaningful activation, negotiate fewer rights for a lower fee
  and keep money to activate.
- Split budget across: experiential, content production, paid media to amplify, hospitality,
  staffing, measurement (often 5–10% of activation).

## 3. Concept test — use the rights only you have
Good activation answers: "What can we do *because* we're the partner that no non-sponsor
could do?" Checklist for each concept:
- Uses an exclusive right (access, marks, talent, venue, data)?
- Adds value to the fan experience rather than interrupting it (free water on a hot day,
  phone charging, a better view, behind-the-scenes content)?
- Has a clear call to action (sample, scan, enter, buy)?
- Extends beyond the event (content, retail tie-in, social)?
- Measurable?
Brainstorm 10, score these five 1–3, pick the top 2–3.

## 4. Leverage across channels
- **On-site:** booth/zone, sampling, branded experiences, signage with a CTA.
- **Content:** player/talent content series, behind-the-scenes, social collabs with the property.
- **Retail:** on-pack promotions, point-of-sale with the property's marks, prize draws
  (check promotion/lottery law per jurisdiction).
- **Paid media:** ads using the "official partner" designation to reach non-attendees.
- **Hospitality:** guest list tied to business goals (target accounts), host plan, follow-up.
- **Internal:** staff tickets, volunteering, launch events.

## 5. Plan delivery
Timeline back from the event: concept sign-off (often 3–6 months out), rights-holder approvals
(brand use, site plans), suppliers, permits, staffing and training, risk assessment, contingency
for weather/cancellation, measurement set-up (QR codes, unique URLs, counters).

## Gotchas
- **Approvals take time** — properties and leagues control mark usage; build in 2+ rounds.
- **Ambush marketing:** competitors may market around the event; protected by contract and
  sometimes event-specific law, but plan counters (better activation, visible designation).
- Hospitality without a guest strategy becomes free tickets for staff favourites.
- Logo-only activation underperforms; don't confuse rights with activation.

## Verify
- [ ] One objective and numeric target per audience.
- [ ] Activation budget agreed separately from the rights fee with a measurement line.
- [ ] Each chosen concept scores well on exclusivity, fan value, CTA, extension, measurability.
- [ ] Rights-holder approvals and permits scheduled with buffer.
- [ ] Measurement mechanisms (codes, URLs, counts, surveys) ready before day one.
