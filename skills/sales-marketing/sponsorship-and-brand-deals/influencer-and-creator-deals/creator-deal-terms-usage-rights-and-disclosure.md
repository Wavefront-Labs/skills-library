# Creator deal terms, usage rights and disclosure

**Goal:** a written deal where both brand and creator know exactly what's delivered, how it
can be used, for how long, and what it costs — and audiences are told it's an ad.

## 1. Price the deliverables
- **Base fee per deliverable** (e.g. 1 Reel, 3 stories, 1 TikTok, 1 YouTube integration).
  Creators often quote from a rate card; common starting heuristics key off typical views or
  followers, but rates vary hugely by niche, platform and audience quality — compare quotes
  and past performance, not a universal formula.
- **Add-ons priced separately:** usage rights, whitelisting/paid amplification, exclusivity,
  raw footage, extra revisions, rush turnaround.
- **Performance elements:** affiliate commission or bonus on code sales/views; define the
  data source (platform insights screenshots within X days, or tracked links).

## 2. Usage rights — the term most often missed
Organic posting on the creator's channel ≠ the brand reusing the content. Specify:
- **Where:** brand's organic social, website, email, paid ads, out-of-home, TV.
- **How long:** e.g. 30/90/180 days or 12 months; perpetual costs a lot more.
- **Territory:** worldwide or named countries.
- **Edits:** can the brand cut, crop, add text?
Usage often adds a significant percentage on top of the base fee per period — negotiate it
explicitly; unlicensed reuse of creator content in ads is a common dispute.

## 3. Whitelisting / partnership ads
Brands run ads through the creator's handle (Meta partnership ads, TikTok Spark Ads with an
authorisation code). Agree: duration, spend cap (optional), who can see comments, and that
the creator can revoke access when the term ends. Charged separately from base fee.

## 4. Exclusivity
Define the competitor set precisely (named brands or a category), the window (e.g. 30 days
before/after posting), and the platforms. Broad exclusivity blocks the creator's income —
price it accordingly.

## 5. Process terms
- **Brief** and **creative concept** approval before shooting.
- **Draft review:** number of revision rounds (often 1–2), turnaround times both ways.
- **Posting window** and minimum live period (e.g. stays up 6 months).
- **Payment:** amount, currency, deposit (creators often ask 50% up front), net terms
  (e.g. net 30), late-payment interest, kill fee if the brand cancels after work starts.
- **Morals and termination** clauses in both directions; FTC/ASA compliance obligations.
- Who holds copyright (usually creator retains, licensing usage to brand).

## 6. Disclosure (non-negotiable)
- Paid, gifted or affiliate relationships must be disclosed clearly and up front — e.g.
  "#ad" or "Ad" at the start of the caption, spoken/on-screen in video, plus the platform's
  paid-partnership label. Hidden in hashtags or below "more" is not enough under US FTC
  Endorsement Guides or UK ASA/CMA guidance; other countries have similar rules.
- Brands can share liability for non-disclosure — the contract should require it and the
  brand should check.
- Claims must be truthful; creators shouldn't say they use the product if they don't.

## Gotchas
- Verbal or DM agreements leave usage and payment unclear — always a written agreement,
  even a one-page one.
- Insights screenshots can be faked; ask for screen recordings or platform-native sharing.
- Agencies/managers may take a commission — know who you're paying.
- Not legal advice; check local advertising and contract rules.

## Verify
- [ ] Every deliverable, platform, date and live period is listed.
- [ ] Usage rights specify channels, duration, territory and edits, priced separately.
- [ ] Whitelisting and exclusivity terms defined with windows and competitor set.
- [ ] Approval rounds, payment schedule, kill fee and termination written.
- [ ] Disclosure method agreed and checked on the live post.
