# Concession ladder planning — giving to get

**Goal:** move from your opening position toward agreement without giving
value away: every concession is planned in advance, gets something back, and
shrinks in size so the buyer believes you're near your limit.

## 1. List your tradeables before the meeting
Make a table: variable | cost to you | value to them | your range.
Typical variables in a sale: price, volume, contract length, payment terms,
delivery date, payment schedule, service level, training, warranty, exclusivity,
returns, case study/reference rights, launch timing, upfront payment.
Look for **low cost to you, high value to them** (give these) and **high value
to you, low cost to them** (ask for these): e.g. a reference customer
testimonial costs them little and is worth a lot to you.

## 2. Build the ladder
From anchor to walk-away, plan 3–5 steps with **decreasing size**:
```
Anchor   $100,000
Step 1   − $4,000   (if 3-year term)
Step 2   − $2,000   (if paid annually upfront)
Step 3   − $1,000   (if signed this quarter)
Target ≈ $93,000   Floor $90,000 (walk-away; never planned to reach)
```
Decreasing steps signal you're converging; equal or growing steps signal there's
more to get. Most non-price steps should come *before* the floor is near.

## 3. Trade with if–then
Always condition: **"If you can ___, then I can ___."** The *if* goes first
so they hear the requirement before the gift.
- "If you can commit to three years, I can bring the annual price to $96k."
- Never: "OK, I'll knock off 5%." An unconditional concession teaches the buyer that
  asking works.
- When they ask for something, respond with a question: "What would that make
  possible for you?" — sometimes a cheaper variable solves the same need.

## 4. Package, don't salami-slice
- Negotiate the whole package, not one item at a time; agreeing price first and
  then terms lets them reopen everything.
- Use **"nothing is agreed until everything is agreed."**
- Offer **two or three equivalent packages** (MESOs — multiple equivalent
  simultaneous offers): e.g. lower price + longer term, or higher price + shorter
  term + more training. Their choice reveals priorities and feels like control.

## 5. Protect the floor
- Know your floor in numbers *and* in total deal value (price × volume − cost
  of extras); extras can sink margin even when the headline price holds.
- Get approval authority up front so you don't concede to avoid a delay.
- Near the floor, slow down: smaller steps, more conditions, "I'll need to take
  that to my director" (only if true).
- Be willing to walk away when you reach the floor — your BATNA makes that credible.

## 6. Track the trades
Keep a running log in the meeting: what you gave, what you got. Summarise
before closing so nothing slips in afterward (see trial-closes-and-nibble-defence).

## Gotchas
- A big first concession destroys the ladder — the buyer expects more of the same size.
- Splitting the difference too early halves your remaining room.
- Free extras (training, delivery) have real costs; price them on your side of the table.
- Some buyers are scored on discount size; trade a visible price move for less-visible
  terms that cost you little.

## Verify
- [ ] Tradeables table with cost-to-you and value-to-them completed.
- [ ] Ladder planned with decreasing steps and a floor in total deal value.
- [ ] Every concession made in if–then form.
- [ ] Package negotiated as a whole; equivalent options offered.
- [ ] Trade log summarised and agreed before close.
