# Named-account planning and multi-threading

**Goal:** grow a small set of big accounts deliberately — knowing where the money is,
who decides, and which deal comes next — instead of reacting to inbound requests.

## 1. Pick the accounts
Score candidates on: fit with your best customers (industry, size, tech stack), a known
trigger (new leader, funding, M&A, regulation), reachable relationships, and potential
size. A rep can usually plan a handful to a couple of dozen accounts properly;
more than that becomes a list, not a plan.

## 2. One-page account plan (refresh quarterly)
- **Snapshot**: revenue, divisions, strategy statements (annual report, earnings call,
  CEO letters — public companies say their priorities out loud).
- **Their top 3 initiatives** this year and how you map to each.
- **Current footprint**: what they buy today, where, contract dates, health.
- **Whitespace**: see §3.
- **Power map**: see §4.
- **Next 2–3 opportunities** with value, sponsor, next step and date.
- **Risks**: renewal risk, competitor footholds, unhappy users.
- **Asks**: what you need internally (exec time, SE, partner, budget for an event).

## 3. Whitespace analysis
Grid: rows = business units / regions / sites; columns = your products or use cases.
Mark each cell: *own* · *competitor* · *open* · *not relevant*. Open cells next to cells
you already own are the cheapest expansion (reference inside the same company).

## 4. Power map and multi-threading
List people by: name/role, business unit, influence (high/med/low), stance
(champion / supporter / neutral / sceptic / blocker), relationship strength with you,
what they care about, last contact.
- **Multi-thread target**: at least 3–5 active relationships per open opportunity
  across levels (user, manager, economic buyer, IT/security, procurement). A deal
  resting on one champion dies when that person leaves or goes quiet.
- Get introductions through the champion ("Who else should hear this?") rather than
  going around them.
- Use different value messages per persona: users = time and friction; managers =
  team metrics; executives = initiative outcomes and risk.

## 5. Executive alignment
Pair one of your executives with one of theirs. Give your exec a brief: account plan,
the customer exec's priorities, the specific ask for the meeting. Keep these meetings
rare and purposeful (e.g. twice a year, around planning and renewal).

## 6. Land and expand
- **Land**: a focused first use case that can succeed quickly and visibly.
- **Prove**: agree success metrics; report them (QBR — quarterly business review) to the
  sponsor and their boss.
- **Expand**: use the proof in the next unit or use case on the whitespace grid; ask the
  sponsor for the introduction.
- **Renew early**: start renewal conversations well before the date (often ~90+ days
  for enterprise contracts), with results in hand.
Measure: net revenue retention, number of business units live, stakeholders engaged.

## 7. Coordinate with marketing (ABM)
Share the plan with marketing for targeted content, events and ads to the named contacts;
agree which signals (site visits, content downloads, event attendance) trigger outreach.

## Gotchas
- Plans written once for a QBR and never reused — keep it short so you'll update it.
- Treating title as power; check who actually influenced past purchases.
- Going over the champion's head without warning them.
- Expansion pitched before the first use case has delivered.

## Verify
- [ ] Each named account has a current (this quarter) one-page plan.
- [ ] Whitespace grid exists and the next opportunity comes from it.
- [ ] Every open opportunity has 3+ active stakeholders logged with stance and last contact.
- [ ] Executive sponsor pairing set for top accounts, with a brief.
- [ ] Renewal start dates calendared well ahead of contract end.
