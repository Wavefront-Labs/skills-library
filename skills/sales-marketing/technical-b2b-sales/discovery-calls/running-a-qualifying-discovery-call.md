# Running a qualifying discovery call

**Goal:** leave a 30–45 minute call knowing whether this is a real opportunity, what the
problem costs the buyer, who decides, and with a calendared next step — or with a clean
disqualification.

## 1. Prepare (15 minutes)
- Company: size, recent news (funding, hires, earnings), tech stack hints (job ads list
  tools), likely trigger for the call.
- Person: role, tenure, what they own; check if they came from a customer of yours.
- Write 3 hypotheses: "They probably struggle with X because Y." You will test, not pitch, them.

## 2. Open with an upfront contract (first 2 minutes)
"Thanks for the time. We've got 30 minutes. I'd like to understand what prompted you to
look, how you handle it today, and what a good outcome looks like. Then I'll share what's
relevant, and at the end we'll decide together whether a next step makes sense —
it's fine if it doesn't. Anything you want to add?"
This sets permission to ask hard questions and to agree a next step.

## 3. Question flow (SPIN order, talk ≤30% of the time)
- **Situation** (keep short — research covers most): "Walk me through how this works today."
- **Problem**: "Where does that break down?" "How often?"
- **Implication**: "What happens downstream when it does?" "Who else feels it?"
- **Need-payoff**: "If that were fixed, what would it change for you?"
Follow up with "Tell me more", "Can you give me an example from last month?", and
silence — the second answer is usually the real one.

## 4. Quantify the pain
Turn it into numbers the buyer states themselves:
`hours lost per week × people affected × loaded hourly cost × 52` or revenue at risk,
error cost, compliance exposure. Write their numbers down and read them back:
"So roughly 3 analysts × 6 hours a week — about 900 hours a year. Fair?"

## 5. Capture qualification (MEDDPICC fields)
| Field | Question that gets it |
|---|---|
| Metrics | "How would you measure success?" |
| Economic buyer | "Who signs off on spend like this?" |
| Decision criteria | "What will you compare options on?" |
| Decision process | "What are the steps from here to a decision? Security review? Legal?" |
| Paper process | "How does procurement work for a new vendor?" |
| Identify pain | (§3–4) |
| Champion | "Who besides you wants this fixed?" — test: will they get you a meeting? |
| Competition | "What else are you looking at, including doing nothing or building it?" |
You won't fill all of them on call one. Leave gaps visible in the CRM, not guessed.

## 6. Stakeholder map
List names, roles, stance (supporter / neutral / blocker / unknown), and what each cares
about. Ask: "Who would be upset if we did this?"

## 7. Close the call on a dated next step
"Based on this, a 45-minute session with you and <ops lead> to see X on your data makes
sense. Does Tuesday 10am work?" Book it before you hang up; send a recap the same day
with their pain in their words, the numbers, and the agreed next step.

## Gotchas
- Demoing on call one before you know the problem — it turns discovery into a pitch.
- Accepting "I'll circle back internally" with no date — that's usually a no.
- Mistaking a friendly contact for a champion; a champion has influence and acts.
- Disqualifying is a win: no budget, no pain, no access → close it out and move on.

## Verify
- [ ] Upfront contract stated in the first two minutes.
- [ ] Pain quantified with the buyer's own numbers, read back to them.
- [ ] CRM has MEDDPICC fields filled or explicitly marked unknown.
- [ ] Stakeholder map with at least the economic buyer named or flagged as a gap.
- [ ] Next meeting booked in the calendar and recap sent same day.
