# Replies, triage and crisis judgement

**Goal:** every message that deserves an answer gets one quickly, in a consistent voice;
complaints reach someone who can fix them; and a bad moment doesn't become a crisis because
of how you replied.

## 1. Triage matrix (decide in seconds)
| Message type | Action | Target time (example) |
|---|---|---|
| Praise / tag / UGC | Like + short personal reply; ask permission to reshare | same day |
| Product question | Answer or link; tag product team if unknown | within business hours |
| Complaint (order, product, service) | Acknowledge publicly, move to DM, hand to support | 1–2 h in hours |
| Safety issue / legal threat / injury | Don't debate; escalate immediately to named lead | immediately |
| Spam / scams / impersonation | Hide or delete, block, report | as seen |
| Abuse / hate | Hide/remove per policy, block, report; don't engage | as seen |
| Troll / bait | Usually ignore; reply once factually if misinformation spreads | — |
Set response-time targets you can staff; publish hours in the bio if not 24/7.

## 2. Brand voice at speed
- One-page voice guide: 3 adjectives (e.g. warm, plain, a little playful), words we use
  and avoid, emoji policy, how to apologise, sign-off convention (initials help humanise).
- **Saved replies** in your inbox tool for top 15 situations — as starting points, always
  personalised (name, detail). Copy-paste replies get noticed and screenshotted.

## 3. Complaints: public acknowledge, private resolve
1. Public: "Sorry about this, [name] — that's not what we want. We've sent you a DM to sort it."
2. DM: ask for order number/details (never ask for card numbers or passwords in DMs).
3. Create a support ticket or hand off via your integrated helpdesk; tell the customer
   who will contact them and when.
4. Close the loop: follow up publicly if resolved and they're happy.
Don't delete negative-but-legitimate comments — it usually escalates.

## 4. Moderation rules
Write and publish community guidelines (pinned or on website). Use platform tools: hidden
words/keyword filters (Instagram and Facebook offer them; TikTok has comment filters),
restrict/limit features, and hiding rather than deleting where it de-escalates.

## 5. Crisis ladder
Spot it: a sudden spike in mentions or negative sentiment (social listening alert), a
complaint going viral, a news story, an employee incident.
- **Level 1 – issue:** a few complaints on one topic → handle normally, flag to the team.
- **Level 2 – heating up:** volume rising, influencers/press asking → pause scheduled posts,
  notify comms lead, use one holding reply: "We're aware and looking into it. Update by [time]."
- **Level 3 – crisis:** safety, legal, discrimination, data breach, mass outrage → stop all
  scheduled content, comms/legal approve every word, single official statement, then
  consistent replies pointing to it.
Rule: never argue, joke, or speculate about facts during levels 2–3.

## Gotchas
- **Pause scheduled posts** in a crisis — a cheerful promo post mid-crisis is a classic own goal.
- Replying from a personal account by mistake (multi-account apps) happens; double-check.
- Promising refunds/compensation publicly sets a precedent; follow policy.
- Response-time targets above are illustrative; benchmark against your own data.

## Verify
- [ ] A triage matrix and response-time targets are written and staffed.
- [ ] Voice guide and personalised saved replies exist for top situations.
- [ ] Every complaint has a support ticket and a closed loop.
- [ ] Community guidelines published; keyword filters configured.
- [ ] Crisis levels, named approvers and "pause all scheduled posts" step documented.
