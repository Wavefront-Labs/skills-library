# Creator vetting, outreach and seeding

**Goal:** a roster of creators whose audiences actually buy from you, built through
fair, well-briefed relationships rather than one-off sponsored posts. For negotiating paid
deals and usage rights, see
sales-marketing.sponsorship-and-brand-deals.influencer-and-creator-deals.creator-deal-terms-usage-rights-and-disclosure.

## 1. Find candidates
- Start with people already mentioning or tagging you (tagged posts, mentions, UGC).
- Search hashtags and keywords your customers use; look at who comments thoughtfully on
  competitors' and adjacent brands' posts.
- Creator discovery tools/marketplaces (e.g. platform creator marketplaces, third-party
  databases) speed this up; features and pricing change — trial before buying.
- Favour **micro (roughly 10k–100k) and nano (<10k)** creators for niche trust and cost;
  tiers are industry conventions, not fixed definitions.

## 2. Vet in 10 minutes per creator
- **Audience fit:** ask for (or view via tool) audience country, age, gender split. A UK
  brand with a 70% non-UK audience creator is a mismatch.
- **Engagement rate** = (likes + comments) ÷ followers per post, averaged over ~10 recent
  posts. Compare to similar-size accounts in the niche rather than a universal number.
- **Red flags for fake/bought audience:** sudden follower spikes, generic comments ("Nice
  pic 🔥") from empty accounts, very high followers with tiny views on video.
- **Brand safety:** scroll 3–6 months of content and captions; search their name + "controversy".
- **Content quality:** would you be happy running their content as an ad?
Log in a sheet: handle, platform, followers, ER, audience fit, rate card, status, notes.

## 3. Outreach that gets replies
Personal, short, specific:
> Hi [name] — loved your [specific post] on [topic]; the [detail] is exactly how our
> customers use [product]. We'd like to send you [product] to try, no obligation to post.
> If you enjoy it, we'd love to talk about working together. Could I get a shipping address?
Use their listed business email or platform DMs. Follow up once after ~5–7 days. Never
mass-blast templates with wrong names.

## 4. Seeding / gifting programme
- Send with **no posting obligation** (be clear about it); include a handwritten note and
  a simple card with key facts and the brand handle.
- Track: shipped date, delivered, posted (Y/N), link, views, engagement, discount code uses.
- Even unpaid gifted posts usually require **ad/gift disclosure** in many jurisdictions
  (US FTC, UK ASA/CMA guidance, EU national rules) — tell creators how to disclose.

## 5. Brief for paid or ambassador work
One page: objective, key messages (max 3), must-dos (disclosure, tags, link), don'ts (claims
you can't substantiate, competitor mentions), deliverables and dates, approval process,
and room for the creator's own style — over-scripted content underperforms.

## 6. Grow the relationship
Promote top performers to an ambassador programme: quarterly product, early access, affiliate
commission or retainer. Engage with their content, share results with them, pay on time.

## Gotchas
- Follower count ≠ sales; track codes/links per creator.
- Creators shouldn't receive confidential launch info without an NDA.
- Health, finance and kids' products have stricter advertising rules — check before briefing.
- Engagement benchmarks quoted online vary widely; use your own niche comparisons.

## Verify
- [ ] Each creator logged with audience fit, ER, brand-safety check.
- [ ] Outreach personalised with a specific reference; one follow-up only.
- [ ] Seeding tracked from shipment to post and code uses.
- [ ] Disclosure instructions given for every gifted or paid post.
- [ ] Top performers identified for ongoing ambassador terms.
