# Hooks, formats and analytics iteration

**Goal:** get more of the right people to see and act on each post by giving each feed's
ranking system the signals it rewards — and learning from your own numbers weekly.

**Caveat:** platforms change ranking systems often and publish only partial explanations.
Treat the patterns below as current working hypotheses (as of late 2026) to test, not facts.

## 1. What every feed broadly rewards
Ranking systems estimate how likely a viewer is to engage. Strong signals across most
platforms: **watch time / retention**, **shares (especially sends in DMs)**, **saves**,
**comments with substance**, and repeat engagement from the same people. Weak signals:
raw likes from low-engagement audiences, engagement bait ("comment YES") — which platforms
say they demote.

## 2. The hook (first 1–3 seconds or first line)
Short-video viewers decide almost instantly. Patterns that work:
- **State the payoff:** "3 mistakes making your coffee bitter."
- **Pattern interrupt:** unexpected visual or motion in frame 1; no logo intro.
- **Open loop:** "I tested every cheap blender so you don't have to — number 3 surprised me."
- **On-screen text** echoing the hook (many watch muted).
For text platforms (LinkedIn, X, Threads) the first line before "see more" is the hook —
make it a complete, specific claim.

## 3. Format by platform (test, then trust your data)
| Platform | Strong formats to test |
|---|---|
| TikTok | 15–60 s native vertical video, trending sounds where commercially licensed, captions |
| Instagram | Reels for reach to non-followers; carousels for saves; stories for existing followers |
| YouTube Shorts | Vertical under the Shorts length limit, loops well, strong title |
| LinkedIn | Text posts with a clear point of view, document/carousel posts, native video |
| Facebook | Video and Reels; groups for community |
Make it native: vertical 9:16, platform's own text tools, no other platform's watermark.

## 4. Timing and frequency
- Start with your analytics' "when your followers are online" view; post ~30–60 minutes
  before the peak.
- Test frequency in blocks (e.g. 3 posts/week for 4 weeks, then 5/week for 4 weeks);
  compare reach per post and total reach, not just totals.

## 5. Weekly analytics loop (30 minutes)
1. Export last week's posts with: reach/views, average watch time or retention %,
   completion rate, shares, saves, comments, follows, link clicks.
2. Rank by **shares + saves per 1,000 views** and by **retention**; reach is often an
   outcome of these.
3. For the top and bottom 3, write one line on why (hook, topic, format, length).
4. Pick **one variable** to test next week (e.g. hook style) and keep the rest constant.
5. Repost or remix winners in a new format; retire consistently weak topics.
Look at the retention graph: a steep drop in the first 3 seconds = hook problem; a mid-video
cliff = pacing or a slow section.

## Gotchas
- Chasing virality with off-brand content grows the wrong audience — followers who never buy.
- Deleting and reposting underperformers is said to hurt by some practitioners; evidence is
  anecdotal. Prefer remixing.
- Small samples mislead: wait for 5–10 posts per variant before concluding.
- Business accounts may not have access to all trending audio; check licensing.

## Verify
- [ ] Every short video has a payoff hook in the first 1–3 seconds with on-screen text.
- [ ] Formats chosen per platform, native aspect ratio, no cross-platform watermarks.
- [ ] Posting time based on your own audience-online data.
- [ ] Weekly loop done: ranked by shares/saves and retention, one-variable test chosen.
- [ ] Reach and share rate trend compared month over month.
