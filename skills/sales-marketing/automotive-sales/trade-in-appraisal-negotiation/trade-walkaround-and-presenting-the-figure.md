# Trade-in appraisal and negotiation

**Goal:** value the customer's car accurately, present the number so it's believed,
and keep the trade negotiation from muddying the price of the new vehicle.

## 1. Gather information early (without valuing)
During needs analysis, note: year, make, model, trim, mileage, ownership history,
service records, accidents, keys, and **payoff** (amount owed and lender). Ask what
they think it's worth — and where that number came from (an online estimate, a private
listing, a friend). That's the expectation you'll be managing.

## 2. The appraisal walkaround (customer present)
Walk the car *with* the customer, calmly noting condition aloud and on an appraisal
sheet. This lets them see the reasons for the figure before they hear it.
- **Exterior:** panel damage, paint mismatch or overspray (prior repairs), windscreen chips,
  tyre tread depth and brand match, kerbed wheels.
- **Interior:** stains, tears, odours (smoke, pets), warning lights on start-up, all
  electrics working.
- **Mechanical/test drive:** brakes, transmission shifts, noises, alignment pull.
- **Paperwork:** service history, title/registration status, number of keys.
- Run a vehicle-history report and check for open recalls where available.
Don't criticise the car harshly — it's their car. State facts.

## 3. Value to actual cash value (ACV)
The used-car manager (or you, if trained) builds the number:
1. **Market data:** wholesale/auction values (e.g. Manheim MMR in the US), guide
   books (Black Book, Kelley Blue Book or regional equivalents), and live retail
   listings for similar cars in your area.
2. Estimate **retail resale price** if you keep it, minus **reconditioning** (tyres,
   brakes, detailing, repairs), minus holding costs and target gross profit → ACV.
   Or, if it will go straight to auction: auction value minus fees and transport.
3. Record the reasoning on the appraisal — you'll use it when presenting.

## 4. Present the trade figure
- Present ACV with the evidence: "Similar cars are selling at auction for around X;
  it needs tyres and a windscreen, so our number is Y." Show the market report if helpful.
- Compare like with like: an online retail listing price isn't what a dealer pays for
  a car needing recon. Explain the gap between private-sale and trade value, plus the
  convenience and (in many places) sales-tax credit of trading in — check local tax rules.
- If they're upside down (owe more than ACV), say so clearly; negative equity rolled
  into a new loan must be disclosed in finance documents.

## 5. Keep the numbers separate
Negotiate **price of new car**, **trade value**, and **finance terms** as distinct
figures. Inflating the trade (over-allowance) while holding the new-car price looks
generous but is the same money moved around — and causes mistrust when customers
compare. Many desks still use this tactic; the transparent approach is clearer and
better for repeat business.

## Gotchas
- Appraising without the customer present makes the figure feel arbitrary.
- Book values lag the market in volatile periods; trust current auction data.
- Missing second key, lien/title issues and warning lights cost real money — price them.
- Never promise a trade value before the used-car manager confirms it.
- Odometer and title disclosures are legally regulated; complete them exactly.

## Verify
- [ ] Payoff, lender and customer's expected value recorded early.
- [ ] Walkaround done with customer; condition notes on appraisal sheet.
- [ ] ACV built from current wholesale/retail data minus recon, with reasons written down.
- [ ] Trade figure presented with evidence; negative equity disclosed if present.
- [ ] Final deal sheet shows price, trade and finance as separate lines.
