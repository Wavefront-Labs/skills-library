# F&I: menu selling with compliant disclosure

**Goal:** arrange finance and offer protection products in a way customers understand,
that holds up to regulators and audits, and still delivers product penetration.

Consumer-credit and insurance-sales law is highly jurisdiction-specific (e.g. US
federal Truth in Lending plus state rules; UK FCA rules on commission disclosure;
Australian add-on insurance reforms). Treat this as process; get your store's
compliance training for the actual rules.

## 1. Before the customer sits down
- Receive the signed deal from the desk: price, trade, payoff, down payment, term.
- Pull credit (with signed authorisation), review lender approvals and stipulations
  (proof of income, residence, insurance).
- Prepare the **menu**: 3–4 columns from most protection to base payment, each listing
  products, term and payment. Price products from your published rate cards.

## 2. Interview first (3–5 minutes)
Ask about: how long they keep cars, annual mileage, how far they commute, who services
the car, whether a surprise repair bill would hurt, kids/pets (interior protection),
road conditions (tyres/wheels). Link each later product to an answer here.

## 3. Present the menu
1. Show **every** option side by side, including the base payment with no products.
   Showing the base payment openly is the core of compliant menu selling.
2. Walk top to bottom once, briefly: what each product covers, what it doesn't, cost
   in total and per month.
3. Let the customer choose; answer objections with their interview answers ("You said
   you'll keep it eight years — the factory warranty ends at three.").
4. Common products: vehicle service contract (extended warranty), **GAP** (covers the
   gap between insurance payout and loan balance after a total loss — most relevant
   with low deposits, long terms or negative equity), tyre & wheel, appearance/paint
   protection, prepaid maintenance, key replacement.

## 4. Finance options
- Present the rate and term the customer qualifies for. If your market permits a
  dealer rate markup (reserve), know the lender's cap and your store policy; many
  regulators now require disclosure or restrict discretionary markups.
- Show total cost of credit, not just payment. Longer terms lower the payment and
  raise total interest — say so.

## 5. Avoid payment packing
**Payment packing** = quoting a payment that secretly includes products, then "finding"
room for them. It's deceptive and a common enforcement target. Rules:
- Never quote a payment with products before the menu has been shown.
- Every product is itemised with its own price on the contract.
- Products are optional and the customer initials accepting or declining each.

## 6. Paperwork
Contract figures must match the menu and deal sheet exactly. Customer signs the finance
contract and product contracts; give copies; record declined-product acknowledgements.
Fund the deal promptly with all lender stipulations.

## Gotchas
- Telling a customer a product is required for approval when it isn't is illegal in
  most jurisdictions.
- GAP is poor value where the loan balance is already below the car's value — be honest.
- Cancellation/refund rights for products exist in many places; know the process.
- PVR targets tempt shortcuts; audits look for identical product counts across all deals.

## Verify
- [ ] Interview completed; each sold product links to a stated need.
- [ ] Menu showed the base (no-product) payment.
- [ ] No payment quoted with products before menu presentation.
- [ ] Contract matches menu and deal sheet line by line; declines initialled.
- [ ] Rate markup (if any) within lender cap, store policy and local disclosure rules.
