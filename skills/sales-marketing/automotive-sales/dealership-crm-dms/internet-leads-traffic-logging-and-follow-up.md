# Dealership CRM/DMS: leads, traffic logging and follow-up

**Goal:** no lead or walk-in goes unrecorded or unfollowed, and managers can see
exactly where every deal stands.

Roles: the **CRM** (VinSolutions, DealerSocket, Elead, etc.) handles customers, leads,
tasks and desking; the **DMS** (CDK, Reynolds & Reynolds, etc.) holds inventory,
deals, accounting and service. They integrate, but screens and field names differ by
vendor and by how your store configured them — learn your store's setup.

## 1. Internet lead response
Leads arrive from your website, OEM site and third-party portals, usually as
ADF/XML-formatted leads routed into the CRM.
1. **Respond fast** — within minutes during business hours. Response speed strongly
   affects contact rates (a widely repeated finding; exact numbers vary by study).
   Use an auto-responder as acknowledgement only, then a personal reply.
2. **First reply:** answer their actual question (availability, price, trade), confirm
   the specific VIN/stock number is available, include a photo or video walkaround,
   and ask for an appointment with two specific time options.
3. **Phone + email + text** (where they've consented — texting rules are strict in
   many markets, e.g. TCPA in the US).
4. Log every touch in the CRM, not your personal phone.

## 2. Showroom traffic logging
Every walk-in ("up") gets logged on arrival or within minutes: name, contact,
vehicle of interest, source (how they heard), salesperson, trade info. Then
stage updates: demo drive → write-up → T.O. → sold/lost with reason.
Unlogged ups are invisible to follow-up and distort close-rate reporting.

## 3. Desking tool
In the CRM desking module (or DMS deal screen):
- Select the stock unit, add trade (appraisal), payoff, down payment, rebates.
- Pull lender programmes/rates and terms; generate a worksheet with payment options.
- Every pencil saved as a version; manager approvals recorded.
- Once sold, the deal pushes to the DMS for F&I and contracting.
Make sure the stock number and VIN match in both systems — mismatches delay funding.

## 4. Follow-up cadence
A typical unsold-customer plan (adapt to store process):
| Day | Action |
|---|---|
| 0 (same day) | Thank-you text/email with figures discussed |
| 1 | Phone call — answer open question, offer next step |
| 3 | Email with something new (similar car, video, incentive) |
| 7 | Call |
| 14, 30 | Email/call; then monthly nurture |
Sold customers: delivery follow-up at day 1 and 7, first-service reminder, review request.
Build these as **CRM process templates** so tasks auto-generate; work the daily task
list to zero.

## 5. Reporting
Weekly: lead response time, contact rate, appointment set rate, **show rate**,
close rate, by source and salesperson. Lead-source ROI (cost per sale by portal)
drives where the marketing budget goes.

## Gotchas
- Duplicate customer records split history; search before creating a new one.
- Marking leads "lost" to clear the task list hides real pipeline.
- Consent fields for marketing calls/texts must be accurate — fines are per message in some regimes.
- Integrations sometimes fail silently; spot-check that portal leads actually arrive.

## Verify
- [ ] Median internet-lead response time measured and within store target.
- [ ] Every walk-in logged with source and outcome.
- [ ] Desking pencils saved and approved in the system.
- [ ] Follow-up process templates active; daily task list cleared.
- [ ] Weekly funnel report by lead source reviewed.
