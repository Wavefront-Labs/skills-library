# Test-drive conversion — from lot visitor to write-up

**Goal:** turn an "up" (walk-in customer) into a written offer, using the drive as the
moment they emotionally take ownership.

## 1. Meet and greet (first 2 minutes)
- Approach within a minute or two, unhurried; introduce yourself, ask their name and
  use it. Offer a drink and a seat — customers who sit down stay longer.
- Lower the guard: "Are you just starting to look, or have you narrowed it down?"
  Don't pitch yet.

## 2. Needs analysis (5–10 minutes)
Ask, and write the answers down where they can see:
- What are you driving now, what do you like/dislike about it?
- Who drives it, what for, how far? (commute, kids, towing, parking constraints)
- Must-haves vs nice-to-haves (AWD, seats, tech, fuel type).
- Timing: what's prompting the change now?
- Trade-in? (note it; don't appraise yet — see trade-in skill)
- Budget is better framed as "a monthly range you're comfortable with" or total —
  record it, don't negotiate it now.
Then select **one** vehicle (two at most). Too many choices delays decisions.

## 3. Walkaround
A structured walkaround (many dealers teach a six-point version: front, engine bay,
passenger side, rear, driver side, interior) tying **features to their stated needs**:
"You said the kids' car seats are a fight — the rear doors open to nearly 90°, let me
show you." Let them touch, open, sit. Skip features they don't care about.

## 4. The demo drive
- Pre-plan a **demo route** of ~10–15 minutes: some smooth road, a corner, a highway
  merge for acceleration, a parking manoeuvre, and a quiet safe spot to switch drivers.
- Copy their licence and (per dealership policy) confirm insurance before the drive.
- You drive first out of the lot (if policy), showing one or two features; switch and
  let them drive the rest. Talk less: ask "How does that feel compared with yours?"
- Pair phone, set seat and mirrors — small ownership cues.
- End by parking the car in a "sold" area or at the front door, not back in the row.

## 5. Post-drive close
1. **Trial close** while they're still in the car: "Is this the one that fits what you
   need?" A yes moves on; a hesitation is information ("What's holding you back?").
2. Summarise the needs it meets, using their words from the needs analysis.
3. Move to the desk: "Let's put some numbers together so you can see if it works."
4. Get commitment to a write-up (offer sheet) before introducing the manager — see
   `sales-marketing.automotive-sales.deal-closing-and-desking.four-square-and-manager-turnover`.

## Gotchas
- Talking price before the drive anchors the conversation on money instead of fit.
- Letting the customer pick a random car off the lot skips needs analysis and often
  leads to a second visit (or a competitor).
- An unplanned route (traffic, roadworks) kills the experience — drive it yourself weekly.
- Safety and legal: licence checks, minimum age and solo-drive rules are dealership
  and jurisdiction specific — follow your store's policy.
- Log every up in the CRM, sold or not, so follow-up happens.

## Verify
- [ ] Needs analysis notes written and used in the walkaround.
- [ ] One (max two) vehicles selected before the drive.
- [ ] Planned route covering acceleration, cornering, parking; customer drove most of it.
- [ ] Trial close asked before leaving the car.
- [ ] Customer logged in CRM with outcome and next step.
