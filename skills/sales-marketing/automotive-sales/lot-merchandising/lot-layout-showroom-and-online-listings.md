# Lot merchandising — physical lot and online listings

**Goal:** every car looks its best wherever the buyer first meets it — and most first
meetings are now the online listing, so treat that as the real front line.

## 1. Time to line
Track **time from acquisition to "frontline ready"** (recon done, photos up, priced).
Each idle day costs depreciation and holding cost. Many stores target a few days for
used cars; set your own target and review weekly.

## 2. Physical lot
- **Front line** (road-facing): your most attractive, high-margin or high-demand units,
  colour-mixed (alternate bright and neutral), clean and evenly spaced.
- **Lining:** straight rows, aligned bumpers or wheels, wheels turned the same way,
  same distance from the kerb. A neat lot signals a careful dealer.
- Group by type (SUVs, trucks, budget, certified) so walk-ins self-navigate; signs help.
- Daily: wash/dust, check tyre pressure, batteries (jump packs ready), remove clutter
  from interiors, all keys tagged and locatable.
- **Window stickers / buyer's guides:** many jurisdictions mandate specific window
  labels on new and used cars (e.g. US FTC Used Car Buyers Guide) — check yours.

## 3. Showroom
- 3–6 cars typically, depending on floor size: a hero new model, a high-trim spec to
  show features, a high-volume seller, and something seasonal.
- Rotate on a schedule (e.g. every 2–4 weeks) so repeat visitors see change.
- Doors unlocked, keys accessible, interiors spotless, battery maintainers on display cars.

## 4. Online listings (the vehicle detail page)
**Photos** — a consistent set for every car, in the same order and setting:
1. Front three-quarter (the thumbnail), side, rear three-quarter, rear, front.
2. Dashboard/odometer on, front seats, rear seats, boot/cargo, key features (screen,
   sunroof, wheels), and any **damage honestly**.
Aim for 20–40+ photos on used cars (portal guidance varies). Shoot in even light,
clean background, camera at roughly headlight height.
**Data:** VIN, trim, options, odometer, colour, history report, service records, any
certified pre-owned status. Missing options are lost search filters.
**Description:** 3–5 lines on what makes this car worth seeing (one owner, new tyres,
full history) — not a pasted brochure.
**Price:** market-based. Portals like CarGurus show deal badges ("Great", "Fair" etc.)
comparing your price to similar listings; Autotrader and others have price indicators
too. Check where you land before publishing.

## 5. Aged inventory
Review days-in-stock weekly. At set thresholds (e.g. 30/45/60 days — store-specific),
re-shoot photos, rewrite the description, move it to the front line, reprice, or
wholesale it. Old stock rarely improves with time.

## Gotchas
- Stock photos on used cars hurt trust and lead conversion.
- Advertised price must match the lot price and include or clearly state fees per local law.
- Dirty interiors in photos (or a visible competitor's plate frame) cost more clicks than price.
- Listing data synced from the DMS overwrites manual edits — fix it at the source.

## Verify
- [ ] Time-to-line measured and within target.
- [ ] Lot lined and cleaned daily; required window labels on every vehicle.
- [ ] Showroom rotation scheduled.
- [ ] Every listing has full photo set in standard order, complete data, honest damage shots.
- [ ] Aged-inventory review done weekly with an action per car.
