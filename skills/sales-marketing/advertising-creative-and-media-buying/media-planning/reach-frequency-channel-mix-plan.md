# Media planning — reach, frequency and channel mix

**Goal:** turn a campaign objective and a budget into a defensible plan that says
*who* sees the ads, *how often*, *where*, *when*, and *what it costs* — before anyone
negotiates a single rate.

## 1. Start from the objective, not the channels
Write one line for each:
- **Business goal** (e.g. +8% trial in a new region in Q2).
- **Communication task:** awareness (reach-led), consideration (frequency-led) or
  response (conversion-led). This decides the reach/frequency balance.
- **Target audience** in buyable terms: the definition you can actually buy against
  in TV ratings, panel data or platform targeting (e.g. "adults 25–54, HH income top
  40%"), not a persona. Note how big it is — you need the universe for every number below.

## 2. Set reach and frequency targets
- **Reach** = % of the audience exposed at least once. **Average frequency** = exposures
  per reached person. **GRPs (TRPs for a target)** = reach × frequency.
- Common working targets (rules of thumb, not laws — validate with your own
  brand data): launch/awareness 60–80% reach at 3+ effective frequency over 4 weeks;
  established brand maintenance 40–60% reach at 1–2/week.
- Reach builds with diminishing returns: doubling GRPs never doubles reach. Use the
  agency's reach curve tool or platform reach estimator rather than guessing.

## 3. Choose the channel mix
Score each candidate channel against: audience coverage, cost per thousand (CPM) or
per rating point (CPP), ability to carry the message (sight/sound/motion/length),
lead time, minimum spend, and measurement. Typical roles:
| Channel | Strength | Watch out |
|---|---|---|
| Linear TV / BVOD | Fast mass reach | High entry cost, shrinking young reach |
| CTV / online video | Targeted reach, frequency control | Fragmentation, fraud/viewability |
| Radio / audio | Cheap frequency, near-purchase timing | No visuals |
| Out-of-home | Local reach, brand fame | Weak measurement |
| Print | Trust, detail, niche titles | Declining circulation |
| Paid social / search | Precise targeting, response | Short attention, auction prices |
Lead with the channel that delivers most reach of the target per dollar; add a second
to extend reach (duplication between channels is lower than within one) and a third
for frequency near the point of sale. More than 4–5 channels on a modest budget
usually means none reaches threshold.

## 4. Flight the budget
- **Continuity:** steady weight; for always-bought categories.
- **Flighting/burst:** on-off weeks; concentrates weight above threshold.
- **Pulsing:** a low base with bursts at seasonal peaks or launches.
Lay it out week-by-week in a flowchart (rows = channels, columns = weeks, cells =
GRPs or budget). Keep 5–10% unallocated as a contingency/optimisation reserve.

## 5. Sanity-check the maths
- Impressions = universe × reach × frequency. Cost = impressions / 1000 × CPM.
- Back-solve: if the plan needs 400 TRPs at a CPP of `<rate>`, does that fit the budget?
  If not, cut channels or weeks, not reach below threshold in every channel.
- Compute cost per reached person and, for response plans, a target cost per acquisition.

## Gotchas
- Mixing audience definitions across channels makes reach figures un-addable. Use one
  target definition throughout and say where a channel can only approximate it.
- Platform "reach" is usually de-duplicated only within that platform; cross-channel
  reach needs a model or a panel. Present combined reach as an estimate.
- A plan below minimum effective weight everywhere is invisible; spending more in
  fewer places almost always beats spreading thin.
- Creative wear-out: high frequency on one execution can reverse results — plan
  rotation when frequency exceeds ~8–10 per flight (rough figure; varies by format).

## Verify
- [ ] Objective, target audience (with universe size) and KPI written down.
- [ ] Reach and frequency targets stated per flight and justified.
- [ ] Every channel has a role and reaches its own minimum weight.
- [ ] Flowchart totals equal the budget, including a contingency line.
- [ ] Cost per reached person / CPA computed and compared with past campaigns.
