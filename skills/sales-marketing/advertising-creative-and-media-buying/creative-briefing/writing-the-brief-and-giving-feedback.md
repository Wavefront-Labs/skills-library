# Creative briefing — the brief, the feedback, the approval

**Goal:** get advertising that does the job by giving creatives one clear problem,
then judging the work against that problem instead of personal preference.

## 1. Write the brief (one page, hard limit)
Fill these boxes in plain words. If a box takes a paragraph, you haven't decided yet.
1. **Why are we advertising?** The business problem in one sentence
   ("lapsed buyers think we're only for special occasions").
2. **Who are we talking to?** A real person in a real moment, plus the media/target
   definition. What do they currently think, feel, do?
3. **Insight:** a human truth about them that creates the opening ("weeknight dinner
   is a negotiation with tired kids").
4. **Single-minded proposition (SMP):** the *one* thing the ad must say. One idea, no
   "and". Test: can a stranger repeat it after hearing it once?
5. **Reasons to believe (RTBs):** 1–3 proof points that make the SMP credible.
6. **What should they think/feel/do after seeing it?** The measurable response.
7. **Tone:** 3 adjectives, plus one thing the brand is *not*.
8. **Mandatories:** logo, legal lines, offer terms, formats and lengths, deliverables
   list (e.g. 30s + 15s + 6s cutdowns, 1:1 and 9:16 social, OOH 48-sheet).
9. **Budget, timing, approvals:** production budget range, key dates, who signs off.

Brief the team in person or on a call; never just email it. Answer questions, then
revise the brief if the questions show a gap.

## 2. Check the brief before it goes out
- Does the SMP compete with the RTBs (four messages pretending to be one)? Cut.
- Is the target the same as in the media plan? Mismatch here wastes the buy.
- Has the person who will approve the work signed the brief? If not, expect the
  brief to be rewritten at the first presentation.

## 3. Give feedback that improves the work
At each review (concepts → scripts → production → final):
1. **Restate the objective first**, then react: "Against the brief, does this make
   lapsed buyers see us as weeknight food?"
2. **Lead with what works** and must be kept — creatives need to know what not to break.
3. **Describe problems, not solutions:** "The product doesn't appear until second 25
   and the proposition feels hidden" — not "add a pack shot at 0:05". Let them solve it.
4. **Separate** must-fix (off-brief, legal, factual) from consider (preference).
5. **Consolidate** stakeholder comments into one voice before sending; contradictory
   feedback from five people is the main cause of mediocre work.
6. Limit rounds: agree up front, e.g. two rounds at concept, two at edit.

## 4. Approve against the brief
Score the final against the boxes: SMP clear? Target addressed? Tone right? Mandatories
present? Then decide. If it fails the brief, reject on that basis; if it passes the brief
but you don't love it, that's usually a reason to approve, or to admit the brief was wrong.

## Gotchas
- "Make the logo bigger" style notes signal the brand cue is weak — say that instead.
- Changing the brief after concepts is a new brief; reset timings and possibly fees.
- Pre-testing (see copy-testing skills) is input, not a substitute for judgement.
- Brief formats differ by agency; the boxes above are a common core, not a standard.

## Verify
- [ ] Brief fits on one page; SMP is one idea with no "and".
- [ ] Approver signed the brief before briefing.
- [ ] Each feedback round restated the objective and split must-fix from consider.
- [ ] Feedback was consolidated into one document per round.
- [ ] Final approval was checked box-by-box against the brief.
