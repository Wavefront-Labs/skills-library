# Media buying negotiation — rates, added value and makegoods

**Goal:** buy the plan's audience at the lowest real cost per target exposure, with
contract terms that protect you when the seller under-delivers.

## 1. Prepare before you call anyone
- **Buy brief** from the plan: target audience, GRPs/impressions per week, flight
  dates, formats/lengths, dayparts or placements, budget per vendor, must-haves
  (e.g. category exclusivity, programme adjacency) and nice-to-haves.
- **Benchmarks:** last year's actual CPP/CPM by vendor and daypart, agency pool
  rates if you have them, and the seller's rate card (treat the card as the opening
  anchor, not the price).
- **Your leverage:** total spend across the seller's portfolio, flexibility on
  dates or dayparts (unsold inventory is perishable), early commitment, payment terms.
- **Walk-away:** the maximum CPM/CPP per vendor above which you shift money elsewhere.
  Have a real alternative vendor priced — leverage without one is a bluff.

## 2. Run the negotiation
1. Send the brief to 2–4 competing vendors with a response deadline. Ask for a
   proposal *in the target audience's terms* (cost per target rating point or per
   thousand in target), not total-audience ratings.
2. Compare on **cost per target exposure after audience guarantees**, not headline price.
3. Counter on the elements you value most; trade, don't concede: "We can move 20% of
   weight into late fringe if the CPP drops to `<x>`."
4. Ask for **added value** last, once price is near settled: bonus spots, sponsorship
   billboards, digital extensions, premium positions, research.
5. Confirm everything in writing the same day; verbal deals drift.

Timing context (varies by market): US national TV sells much of its inventory in
the **upfront** (spring/summer commitment for the coming season) with remaining
**scatter** bought nearer air date; many other markets work on annual agency deals
plus spot buys. Confirm local practice before assuming either model.

## 3. Lock the terms that matter
- **Audience guarantee** on the agreed demo and the measurement source.
- **Makegood policy:** under-delivery compensated with equivalent audience
  (makegoods / ADUs), at your approval, inside the flight where possible.
- **Cancellation windows** and penalties (broadcast often requires notice weeks ahead;
  digital IOs vary).
- **Brand safety / placement exclusions**, category separation from competitors.
- **Pre-emption** rights: whether cheaper spots can be bumped by higher bidders.
- **Billing** basis (actuals vs ordered) and payment terms.

## 4. Post-buy and makegoods
- After each flight (or weekly for long campaigns), run a **post-buy analysis**:
  ordered vs delivered GRPs/impressions per vendor, spots that ran out of agreed
  dayparts, pre-empted or missing spots, viewability for digital.
- Claim shortfalls promptly with the evidence. Reject makegoods in weak dayparts or
  after the campaign window when timing matters — ask for cash credit instead.
- Log each vendor's delivery accuracy; it is leverage for the next negotiation.

## Gotchas
- A low CPM on a broad audience can be expensive in target terms — always convert.
- Added value that doesn't reach your target (2 a.m. bonus spots) is not value.
- Accepting a makegood without checking the substitute schedule often means poorer
  inventory than you paid for.
- Over-committing in an upfront removes flexibility if sales or creative change; keep
  some budget for scatter or opportunistic buys (proportions vary by brand).
- Rate terminology and guarantee practice differ by country and medium; the steps
  above are the general pattern, not a specific market's trading rules.

## Verify
- [ ] Every vendor proposal compared on cost per target exposure.
- [ ] Walk-away price and an alternative vendor existed before each negotiation.
- [ ] Contract states guarantee, makegood, cancellation, pre-emption and billing terms.
- [ ] Post-buy completed and every shortfall claimed or credited.
- [ ] Vendor delivery scorecard updated for next season.
