# Visual identity briefing — commission, judge, roll out

**Goal:** get an identity system that expresses the positioning, is distinctive and
practical across every touchpoint — by directing designers well, not by designing it
yourself.

## 1. Before the brief: audit
- **Touchpoint inventory:** every place the brand appears (website, app, packaging,
  signage, vehicles, uniforms, documents, social, email, merch, event). Photograph or
  screenshot each; count them (it sizes the rollout).
- **Asset audit:** which existing assets (colour, symbol, shape, character, sound) do
  customers already recognise? Quick recognition tests (show the asset without the
  name, ask which brand) tell you what to keep. Throwing away recognised assets is
  the most expensive rebrand mistake.

## 2. Write the identity brief
- **Why now:** the business reason (merger, repositioning, outdated, inconsistent).
- **Positioning and personality** (link the positioning document).
- **Audiences** and where they meet the brand most.
- **Keep / evolve / replace** decisions from the asset audit.
- **Deliverables:** logo suite (primary, secondary, icon/favicon, mono), colour palette
  with values (e.g. Pantone, CMYK, RGB, HEX), typography (with licensing for web, app,
  print), graphic elements, imagery/illustration style, motion/sound if needed,
  guidelines, and source files.
- **Practical constraints:** smallest size (app icon, embroidery), print processes,
  accessibility contrast (WCAG), languages/scripts.
- **Budget, timeline, decision-makers, review stages.**

## 3. Evaluating the work
At each presentation, judge against criteria agreed in the brief — not taste:
1. **Strategic fit:** does it express the positioning and personality?
2. **Distinctiveness:** would it be confused with a competitor? Lay it alongside
   competitor identities.
3. **Versatility:** show it at favicon size, one colour, on dark and light, on the
   messiest real touchpoint (a delivery van, a spreadsheet header).
4. **Ownability:** symbol trademark-searchable? (legal check before final)
5. **Longevity:** fashion-driven choices date quickly.
Feedback format: what works and must stay → problems described against criteria →
questions. Avoid "make it pop"; describe the problem, let designers solve it.
Limit stakeholders in the room; collect wider views in structured form afterwards.

## 4. Plan the rollout
- Prioritise touchpoints by visibility and cost: digital first (fast, cheap), then
  high-visibility physical, then natural replacement cycles (stationery, uniforms as
  stock runs out).
- Decide **big-bang vs phased** launch; phased saves money but runs two identities
  side by side — set an end date.
- Build an **asset library** (DAM or shared drive) with approved files and the guidelines;
  train internal teams and brief suppliers.
- Budget for the long tail: signage and vehicles often cost more than the design.

## Gotchas
- Judging a logo in isolation on a big screen; identities live small and in context.
- Committee design by many stakeholders producing a safe, generic result.
- Forgetting font licences for web/app use — a legal and cost surprise.
- Rebranding to fix a product or service problem.

## Verify
- [ ] Touchpoint inventory and asset-recognition audit completed.
- [ ] Brief lists deliverables, constraints and evaluation criteria.
- [ ] Work tested small, mono, on real touchpoints and next to competitors.
- [ ] Trademark check and font licensing confirmed before sign-off.
- [ ] Rollout plan with priorities, end date for old identity and asset library live.
