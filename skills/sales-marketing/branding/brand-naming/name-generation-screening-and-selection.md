# Brand naming — generate, screen, select

**Goal:** a name that fits the positioning, is easy to say and remember, works in your
markets, and can actually be owned (trademark, domain, handles).

## 1. Naming brief (half a page)
- What is being named (company, product, sub-line) and its place in the brand
  architecture (stand-alone or endorsed by the parent?).
- Positioning and 3–5 personality words.
- Markets and languages it must work in.
- Constraints: length, must/must-not contain words, legal classes (Nice
  classification of goods/services), domain requirement (.com essential or not).
- Competitor names (to avoid echoing them).
- Decision-makers and the decision date.

## 2. Generate widely (aim for 300–1,000 raw candidates)
Work across the spectrum of name types — each trades memorability against ease of
legal protection:
| Type | Example style | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Descriptive | "General Motors" | Clear, weak/hard to trademark |
| Suggestive | "Pinterest" | Hints at benefit; often registrable |
| Arbitrary | "Apple" for computers | Real word, unrelated meaning; strong |
| Invented/coined | "Kodak" | Most protectable; needs marketing to give meaning |
Techniques: word lists by theme (benefit, metaphor, origin, sound), compounds,
blends/portmanteaus, foreign-language roots (check meaning carefully), truncations,
altered spellings (use sparingly — spelling confusion costs), and name-generator or
AI tools for volume. Run sessions solo and in groups; don't judge during generation.

## 3. Screen in stages
1. **Strategic fit** (cut to ~50): matches the brief and personality?
2. **Linguistic** (cut to ~20): easy to pronounce from spelling, one obvious spelling
   from sound ("radio test": say it, ask someone to type it), no negative meanings or
   slang in target languages (use native speakers), no unfortunate abbreviations.
3. **Legal knockout** (cut to ~5–10): search trademark registers for identical/similar
   marks in your classes — e.g. USPTO, EUIPO, WIPO Global Brand Database, plus your
   national office. Also search company registers and the web.
4. **Digital availability:** domain (exact or with a sensible modifier), main social
   handles, app stores.
5. **Full clearance** for 2–3 finalists by a trademark attorney before any commitment.
A knockout search is not clearance; only a professional opinion reduces legal risk.

## 4. Choose and sell the name
- Present finalists **in context**: on a logo-less mock-up of packaging, website header,
  email signature, spoken in a sentence — not as words on a slide.
- Restate the brief before showing names; judge against it.
- Expect initial dislike of unfamiliar names; familiarity grows. Ask "which best does
  the job?" not "which do you like?"
- Have a backup cleared name ready in case of late legal issues.

## 5. Secure it
File trademark applications in key markets and classes, register domains (and common
misspellings), and claim handles — before the public announcement.

## Gotchas
- Falling in love with a name before legal screening — most candidates fail clearance.
- Names that work in one market can be vulgar or confusing in another.
- Descriptive names feel safe but are hard to defend and blend in.
- "Available .com" sometimes means a premium resale price — budget for it.

## Verify
- [ ] Naming brief signed off by the decision-maker before generation.
- [ ] Hundreds of candidates generated across at least three name types.
- [ ] Shortlist passed native-speaker linguistic checks in each target market.
- [ ] Knockout searches done; finalists cleared by a trademark attorney.
- [ ] Trademark filed, domain and handles secured before launch.
