# Brand voice, guidelines and governance

**Goal:** anyone in the company (or an agency) can produce on-brand words and visuals
without asking the brand team every time — and off-brand work gets caught early.

## 1. Define the voice
1. Collect 30–50 real pieces of brand writing (site, emails, support macros, social,
   packaging). Mark what sounds like "us" at our best, and what doesn't.
2. Pick **3–4 voice traits** derived from the positioning and personality, each with
   its boundary — a **voice chart**:
| Trait | We are | We are not | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Plain-spoken | Direct, everyday words | Blunt or curt | "You'll get it Tuesday." | "Delivery is anticipated…" |
| Warm | Friendly, human | Gushing | Use "we" and "you" | Exclamation marks everywhere |
3. **Tone flexes, voice doesn't:** show how tone adjusts by context (celebration, error
   message, complaint reply, legal notice) on a simple scale, e.g. more playful ↔ more
   serious.
4. Write **before/after rewrites** of real copy — the most useful section of any voice guide.

## 2. Write the guidelines
Keep them short and practical; a 120-page PDF nobody opens fails. Typical contents:
- **Brand foundations:** purpose, positioning, personality (one page).
- **Voice and tone:** chart, tone scale, before/afters, word list (preferred terms,
  banned words, product naming and capitalisation, inclusive-language rules).
- **Editorial style:** spelling standard, dates, numbers, punctuation choices — or point
  to an external style guide and list only your exceptions.
- **Visual identity:** logo usage (clear space, minimum size, colourways, misuse
  examples), colour values, typography, imagery, layout grids, accessibility rules.
- **Templates:** slides, documents, social formats, email — ready to use.
- **Contacts and approval process.**
Publish as a searchable web page or brand portal (many DAM/brand-portal tools exist)
rather than only a PDF, so updates reach everyone.

## 3. Governance
- **Tiered approval:** high-risk work (campaigns, packaging, new sub-brands) reviewed by
  the brand team; low-risk work (social posts, internal docs) self-checked against a
  checklist using templates.
- **Brand champions** in each department/region: trained, first point of contact.
- **Onboarding:** brief new staff and every agency on the guidelines; include a short
  writing exercise.
- **Audits:** quarterly sample of output across channels scored against the guidelines
  (e.g. 1–5 on voice, visual, accuracy). Share results, fix templates where errors cluster.
- **Change log** and version number on the guidelines; announce changes.

## 4. Applying the voice to AI and support tools
Put the voice chart, word list and before/afters into prompt templates or style settings
for any AI writing tools and into support macro libraries — those produce most of the
words customers see.

## Gotchas
- Traits like "innovative, passionate, trusted" are values, not a voice; they don't tell
  a writer what to do. Use "we are / we are not" pairs.
- Guidelines written only by designers often skip the words; voice gets neglected.
- Policing without templates creates resentment; make the right thing the easy thing.
- Guidelines go stale; assign an owner and a review date.

## Verify
- [ ] Voice chart with 3–4 traits, each with "not" boundaries and examples.
- [ ] Tone scale and at least five before/after rewrites included.
- [ ] Guidelines published in a searchable, versioned place with templates.
- [ ] Approval tiers, champions and an audit schedule defined.
- [ ] Voice rules loaded into support macros and AI writing prompts.
