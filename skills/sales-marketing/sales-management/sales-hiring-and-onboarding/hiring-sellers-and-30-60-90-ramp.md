# Hiring sellers and the 30-60-90 ramp

**Goal:** hire people who will actually sell in your motion, and get them to full
productivity faster with a ramp plan that's measured, not hoped.

## 1. Write the scorecard before the job ad
List 4–6 competencies with what "good" looks like for *this* role:
- e.g. discovery questioning, resilience/prospecting volume, written communication,
  coachability, deal management, product aptitude.
- Plus hard facts: deal size and cycle they've sold, inbound vs outbound, customer type.
Every interviewer scores the same competencies 1–4 with evidence notes.

## 2. Structured interviews
- Behavioural questions with follow-ups: "Walk me through your biggest deal last year —
  how did it start, who was involved, what nearly killed it?" Probe for numbers and their
  own actions ("we" vs "I").
- **Verify claims:** quota attainment and ranking can be inflated. Ask for specifics and
  check with references (with candidate consent).
- Same questions for every candidate for the same role — fairer and comparable.
  Check local employment law on what you may ask.

## 3. The audition (the most predictive step)
- **Role-play / mock call:** give a short brief 1–2 days ahead (fictional prospect,
  product one-pager). Run a 20–30 min discovery call. Score: questions asked, listening,
  next step secured.
- **Coachability test:** give one piece of feedback and re-run a 5-minute segment. Did they
  apply it?
- For SDRs, a written cold email; for senior AEs, a deal-plan presentation.

## 4. Decide
Debrief together, scorecards submitted **before** discussion (prevents anchoring).
Hire against the scorecard, not "culture fit" gut feel.

## 5. The 30-60-90 ramp plan
Give the hire a written plan on day one:
**Days 1–30 — Learn**
- Product, ICP, competitors, pricing; shadow 10+ calls; CRM and tool setup.
- **Certifications:** pitch delivery, discovery role-play, demo — each signed off by the manager.
**Days 31–60 — Do with support**
- Own calls with manager/peer present; build own pipeline; first opportunities.
- Leading indicators: meetings booked, opportunities created, call scorecard scores.
**Days 61–90 — Own**
- Full activity targets; first closes in short-cycle sales; ramped quota begins (see
  sales-marketing.sales-management.territory-and-quota-design.carving-territories-and-setting-quotas).
Ramp length scales with sales cycle: a transactional SDR may ramp in 1–3 months, an
enterprise AE often 6+ months — measure your own.

## 6. Early-tenure retention
- Weekly 1:1s during ramp; a buddy outside the reporting line.
- Early wins engineered: hand a few warm leads or a small existing account.
- Watch for the 3–6 month dip: if leading indicators are on track, reassure; if not, coach
  specifically now rather than waiting for the quota miss.

## Gotchas
- Hiring the best talker ≠ the best seller; the role-play reveals listening.
- Industry experience matters less than motion fit (outbound hunter vs account farmer).
- Throwing new hires straight into quota with no ramp burns them and leads.
- Unverified: time-to-productivity norms vary hugely by company; track yours (months to
  first full-quota month).

## Verify
- [ ] A written scorecard existed before interviews; every interviewer used it.
- [ ] Each finalist did a role-play and a coachability re-run.
- [ ] Reference checks probed attainment claims.
- [ ] New hire received a 30-60-90 plan with certifications and leading indicators.
- [ ] Time-to-productivity and 12-month retention tracked per hiring cohort.
