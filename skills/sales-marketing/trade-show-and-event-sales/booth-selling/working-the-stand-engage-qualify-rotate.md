# Working the stand: engage, qualify, rotate

**Goal:** talk to as many *right* visitors as possible, qualify each in under a minute,
hand the good ones a clear next step, and release the rest politely — for every hour
the hall is open.

## 1. Pre-show briefing (the evening before or first thing)
Brief every staffer on one page:
- Show goal: e.g. "120 qualified leads, 25 booked meetings, 3 demo-theatre sessions a day".
- Who we want: target industries, roles, company sizes. Who we don't (students,
  competitors, other vendors selling to us — be courteous, keep it short).
- The 3 qualifying questions (§3) and how to log answers in the lead app.
- Opening lines, the 20-second pitch, the next-step offers (demo slot, meeting with SE,
  follow-up call).
- Rota, breaks, booth captain's name, where spare scanners/chargers/literature are.
- Rules: no phones, no eating, no sitting behind tables, no huddling with colleagues.

## 2. Aisle engagement — without pouncing
- **Stand at the edge of the booth, angled toward the aisle**, not behind a desk. Open
  hands, eye contact, smile. One or two steps into the aisle at most.
- **Open with a question, not "Can I help you?"** (answer: "just looking"):
  - "What brings you to the show this year?"
  - "What are you hoping to find here?"
  - "Have you seen <the thing on our screen> before?"
  - Read the badge — role and company give you a tailored opener.
- If they slow down at a display, let them look for a few seconds first, then open.

## 3. 30-second qualification
Three questions, adapt to your product:
1. **Role / relevance**: "What's your part in <area> at your company?"
2. **Need / project**: "Are you working on anything around <problem> right now?"
3. **Timing / authority**: "Is that something you're looking at this year? Who else is involved?"
Then score on the spot (hot / warm / cold — see
sales-marketing.trade-show-and-event-sales.lead-capture-and-scoring.badge-scan-notes-and-floor-scoring)
and offer the matching next step: hot → book a meeting now; warm → short demo or send
specific material; cold → friendly goodbye, optional scan for newsletter only if they agree.

## 4. Disengaging politely
Long conversations with poor-fit visitors cost real leads. Use a closing line:
"I don't want to keep you — the best next step is <X>. Can I scan your badge so we
can send it?" Then step back and turn toward the aisle. For competitors: be polite,
don't give pricing or roadmap.

## 5. Traffic peaks and lulls
- **Peaks** (opening hour, after keynotes, during breaks): every staffer engaged; booth
  captain directs overflow — "I'll be with you in two minutes; meanwhile watch this demo."
  Hand waiting visitors something useful (QR to a short video, a seat at the demo).
- **Lulls**: reset the booth, charge devices, update lead notes, walk adjacent aisles
  only if the show permits it (many forbid soliciting outside your stand — check the
  exhibitor rules).

## 6. Staffing rotations
- Shifts of roughly 2–3 hours on the floor, then a real break off the stand. Energy drops
  noticeably later in long shifts.
- Overlap shifts by ~15 minutes for hand-over of hot conversations.
- Mix roles per shift: at least one person who can demo, one who can talk commercials.
- Staffing level rule of thumb: a staffer per ~5 m² / 50 sq ft of open booth space,
  adjusted for expected traffic (a commonly quoted rule, not a standard).
- Booth captain checks leads per staffer per shift and rebalances.

## Gotchas
- Staff clustered together chatting — visitors read it as "closed".
- Scanning everyone who walks by — inflates numbers, buries real leads.
- Giant bags of brochures; offer to send material instead.
- Forgetting comfortable shoes and water — fatigue kills afternoon performance.

## Verify
- [ ] Every staffer had the briefing sheet and can recite the 3 qualifying questions.
- [ ] No tables or barriers between staff and aisle; no phones in use on the floor.
- [ ] Each conversation ends with a scored lead and next step, or a polite release.
- [ ] Rota followed with breaks; hand-over notes at shift change.
- [ ] Leads per staffer per shift reviewed at the end of each day.
