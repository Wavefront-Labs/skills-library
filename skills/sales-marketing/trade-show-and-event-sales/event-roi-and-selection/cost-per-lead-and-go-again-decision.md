# Cost per lead and the go-again decision

**Goal:** know, in money, what each show produced against what it cost, and make the
next year's show list and booth sizes from evidence rather than habit.

## 1. Capture the full cost (most teams undercount)
| Bucket | Items |
|---|---|
| Space & sponsorship | stand space, sponsorship package, speaking slot |
| Build | booth design/rental, graphics, furniture, AV, power, internet, rigging |
| Show services | drayage/material handling, installation & dismantling labour, cleaning, lead-retrieval licences |
| Shipping & storage | freight both ways, storage between shows |
| People | travel, hotels, meals; *optionally* staff time at loaded cost |
| Marketing | pre-show promotion, giveaways, hospitality/dinners, printed material |
| Post-show | follow-up campaign costs |
Decide once whether staff time is included and apply it consistently across shows.

## 2. Track the right outputs
Tag every lead with the event's CRM campaign at import; associate opportunities with it.
- Total leads, **qualified** (hot + warm) leads, meetings held.
- **Pipeline sourced**: new opportunities whose first touch was this event.
- **Pipeline influenced**: existing opportunities where a contact engaged at the event.
- Revenue closed from sourced pipeline (measure at 3, 6, 12 months — B2B cycles are long).

## 3. The core ratios
```
Cost per lead            = total cost / all leads
Cost per qualified lead  = total cost / (hot + warm leads)
Cost per meeting         = total cost / meetings held
Pipeline-to-cost         = sourced pipeline / total cost
ROI (closed)             = (closed revenue x gross margin - total cost) / total cost
```
Compare with other channels' cost per qualified lead and per opportunity (paid search,
webinars, outbound) — that's the real benchmark. Don't count influenced pipeline as if
the event created it; report it separately.

## 4. Booth size and sponsorship decisions
- Size to staffing and goals: estimate peak visitors per hour you can actually talk to
  (staffers × conversations per hour) — extra floor space nobody staffs adds cost, not leads.
- Location within the hall often matters as much as size: near entrances, food, main
  aisles, or the sessions your buyers attend.
- Sponsorship add-ons (speaking slot, badge-lanyard branding, app listings): ask the
  organiser for last year's attendee breakdown by role/industry and what similar sponsors
  measured. A speaking slot with your target audience usually beats generic logo placement.
- Alternative: attend without a booth — book meetings in advance, hold a side dinner or
  meeting room. Often far cheaper per meeting for enterprise targets.

## 5. Go-again scorecard (score each show 1–5, weight to taste)
- Cost per qualified lead vs channel average.
- Pipeline-to-cost ratio.
- Audience fit: % of visitors in target roles/industries (from your scans + organiser data).
- Strategic value: key accounts met, partner meetings, competitor presence, press.
- Trend: attendance and your results vs prior years.
Outcomes: **go again same size**, **go bigger**, **go smaller / attend only**, **drop**.
Decide before the organiser's rebooking deadline — many sell next year's space on site,
with early-booking discounts that create pressure to sign before the data is in.

## Gotchas
- Judging a show at 30 days — enterprise pipeline needs months to show up.
- Leads not tagged to the campaign at import → attribution impossible later.
- Comparing shows with different cost definitions.
- Ignoring hidden costs like drayage and labour, which can be a large share of the bill.

## Verify
- [ ] Full cost sheet completed with every bucket, same definitions for all shows.
- [ ] All leads and opportunities tagged to the event campaign.
- [ ] Cost per qualified lead and pipeline-to-cost reported and compared to other channels.
- [ ] Results reviewed at 3/6/12 months, not only right after the show.
- [ ] Go-again scorecard completed before committing to rebooking.
