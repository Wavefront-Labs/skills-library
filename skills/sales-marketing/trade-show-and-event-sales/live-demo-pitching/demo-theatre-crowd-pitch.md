# Demo-theatre crowd pitch

**Goal:** turn passers-by into a standing audience, hold them through a short demo,
and convert the end of the session into scanned, qualified leads.

## 1. Schedule and signage
- Run sessions at fixed times (e.g. on the hour and half-hour), posted on a sign/screen
  at the booth and in the show app if exhibitors can list sessions.
- Time sessions to footfall: right after keynotes end and during coffee/lunch breaks.
- 7–12 minutes is a common length for a standing crowd; longer only with seating.

## 2. The script (example 10 minutes)
| Min | Segment | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 0–1 | **Hook** | A problem the audience recognises, stated as a question or a surprising number. "Hands up if month-end still means spreadsheets." |
| 1–2 | Promise | "In the next 8 minutes you'll see X done in Y." |
| 2–7 | Demo — 2–3 moments | Each moment: problem → show it working → outcome. Biggest "wow" early, not saved for the end. |
| 7–8 | Proof | One customer result, one sentence. |
| 8–9 | Call to action | "Stay for a 2-minute chat and a badge scan to get <offer>; or book a 1:1 demo." |
| 9–10 | Q&A / transition | Staff move in to talk to individuals while presenter takes questions. |
Write it, rehearse it at least 3–5 times out loud with the real equipment, and keep a
printed copy at the station.

## 3. Equipment
- Headset mic (hands free) and a small speaker if the show allows amplified sound —
  most have decibel limits; check the exhibitor manual.
- Large screen at head height or above so the back row sees it.
- Demo runs offline or on a hard-wired connection; show Wi-Fi is often overloaded. Have a
  recorded video fallback ready to play instantly.
- Clicker/remote, spare batteries, charged laptop with power.

## 4. Drawing and holding a crowd
- **Seed the crowd**: a few staff stand in front at the start — people join groups that
  already exist. Staff step aside as real visitors arrive.
- **Announce**: "Demo starting in 2 minutes — the one about <benefit>." Staff invite
  people in the aisle by name from their badge.
- Start on time even with a small crowd; motion and voice attract more.
- Speak to the person furthest away; ask simple participation questions (show of hands).
- Keep slides minimal; the live product is the draw.

## 5. Converting at the end
- Offer something worth a scan: a recording, a guide, a 1:1 slot, a draw for a prize
  (if you use a prize, capture its leads separately — they're lower intent).
- Staff with scanners stand at the exits of the audience area, not behind the presenter.
- Qualify each person quickly (the same three questions as the booth) and score.

## 6. Hecklers and competitors in the audience
- **Hostile or loaded question**: stay calm, restate it neutrally, answer briefly and
  factually, offer to continue one-to-one after the session. Don't argue in front of the crowd.
- **Competitor staff** (badge shows their company): treat them like anyone else, avoid
  sharing roadmap or pricing, don't call them out. If they disrupt, the booth captain
  steps in politely.
- **Demo breaks**: say so lightly ("live demos keep us honest"), switch to the recording,
  keep the narrative going.

## Gotchas
- Mic volume too high for neighbours; complaints can lead to show staff shutting you down.
- Presenter blocked by the screen, or screen too low for a standing crowd.
- Ending without a clear call to action — the crowd simply disperses.
- Same presenter all day; rotate presenters to keep energy up.

## Verify
- [ ] Session times posted and aligned to show breaks.
- [ ] Script rehearsed on the real kit; offline/video fallback tested.
- [ ] Sound level within the show's limit.
- [ ] Staff positioned to scan at the end of each session; prize leads tagged separately.
- [ ] Leads per session recorded to compare time slots and presenters.
