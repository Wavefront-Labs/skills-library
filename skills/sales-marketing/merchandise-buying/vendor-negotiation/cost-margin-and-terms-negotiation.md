# Cost, margin and terms negotiation with vendors

**Goal:** buy at a cost that delivers the planned margin, with terms that
protect you when the product doesn't sell — while keeping suppliers you'll need
next season. The retail buyer's version of negotiation: value is in *terms* as
much as in unit cost.

## 1. Work back from retail
Decide the selling price first (from price architecture), then:
```
Target landed cost = Retail price (ex sales tax/VAT) × (1 − target IMU%)
```
Landed cost = supplier price (e.g. FOB) + freight + duty + insurance + handling.
Example: retail £30 incl. 20% VAT → £25 ex VAT; target IMU 60% → landed cost ≤ £10;
if freight+duty ≈ 18% of FOB, target FOB ≈ £8.47.
Know this number before the meeting. Your **walk-away** cost is where the line
no longer earns its space.

## 2. Prepare
- **Cost breakdown** for key lines: fabric/material, trim, labour, overhead, margin.
  Ask suppliers for open-costing on big volumes; use commodity price moves
  (cotton, freight) as evidence.
- **Vendor scorecard**: sales, margin, sell-through, on-time delivery, quality
  returns, markdown % — your leverage and their weak spots.
- Your **offer**: volume, phasing, exclusivity, payment speed, store space,
  promotion support. What's cheap for you but valuable to them?

## 3. Trade, don't concede (see concession-trading)
Price/terms levers, roughly in order of value to a buyer:
- **Unit cost** — volume tiers, simplification (fewer colours/trims), longer lead times.
- **Markdown money / markdown support** — supplier funds part of the
  clearance loss if sell-through misses an agreed level by a date.
- **Returns to vendor (RTV)** or sale-or-return for new/unproven lines.
- **Rebates** — volume or growth rebates paid at period end; agree thresholds and
  how they're measured.
- **Settlement discount** — e.g. 2% for payment within 10 days, if your cash allows.
- **Payment terms** — longer terms improve cash flow (but be fair to small suppliers;
  some jurisdictions have prompt-payment rules or grocery codes of practice).
- **Exclusives** — exclusive colourway/style/period in return for commitment.
- **Marketing/launch support** — samples, displays, co-op advertising.
Trade explicitly: "If I give you 30% more volume on the core, I need £0.40 off and
markdown support below 60% sell-through by week 10."

## 4. Range review with the vendor
Each season: review last season's performance line by line, agree what to
drop/continue/improve, share next season's direction and volumes, then negotiate
new lines. Suppliers given forward visibility invest in your business.

## 5. Put it in writing
Purchase order and supplier terms agreement: costs, quantities, delivery
windows (and penalties/cancellation rights for late delivery), quality standards,
markdown support conditions, rebate scales, payment terms, compliance (ethical
sourcing, product safety). Ambiguous rebate terms are the usual dispute.

## Gotchas
- Squeezing cost so hard that quality, compliance or delivery fails costs more later.
- Retroactive demands (deductions not in the agreement) breach trust and, in some
  sectors, regulations — e.g. grocery supply codes.
- Watch total landed cost: a cheaper FOB from a slower, riskier origin can lose on freight and markdown.
- **Uncertain:** typical IMU and markdown-money norms vary greatly by category and retailer.

## Verify
- [ ] Target landed cost derived from retail and IMU before the meeting; walk-away set.
- [ ] Scorecard and cost breakdowns prepared.
- [ ] Every concession traded for a return; terms beyond price negotiated.
- [ ] Range review held with forward volumes shared.
- [ ] All terms (markdown support, rebates, penalties) written and agreed.
