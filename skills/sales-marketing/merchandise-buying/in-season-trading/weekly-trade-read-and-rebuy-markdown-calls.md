# Weekly trade read, rebuys and markdown calls

**Goal:** every week, find what's winning and back it, find what's failing and
act early — because a markdown taken in week 4 at 20% off costs far less than one
taken in week 12 at 50% off.

## 1. Monday reports (per category, per option)
Pull last week and season-to-date:
- **Units and sales value** vs plan and last year.
- **Rate of sale (ROS)** = units sold ÷ weeks on sale ÷ stores stocked (units per
  store per week) — fairer than raw units across options with different distribution.
- **Sell-through %** = units sold ÷ (units sold + stock on hand) [some use
  ÷ units received — be consistent].
- **Weeks of cover** = (stock on hand + on order due) ÷ current weekly sales.
- Full-price vs promoted sales split; availability (% stores in stock by size).
Rank options by ROS within category: top 10 and bottom 10.

## 2. Read best sellers
For each top option ask:
- Is it selling evenly across stores and sizes, or one region/size?
- Cover: < ~4 weeks with weeks left in the season → **rebuy** candidate.
- Can the supplier repeat in time? Lead time + selling weeks remaining decides
  the quantity; rebuying stock that lands after peak creates the next markdown.
- Can you extend it (new colour, sister style) next drop?
Rebuy quantity ≈ forecast weekly sales × weeks of selling after delivery − stock
expected to remain at delivery. Check OTB (see otb-calculation-and-reforecast).

## 3. Read worst sellers
- Diagnose before discounting: wrong price? poor display or location? missing sizes?
  only in weak stores? not yet delivered to all stores? bad weather?
- Fix the cheap causes first: move to a better fixture, remerchandise, transfer to
  stores where it sells (**store transfers**), add to a promotion.
- If ROS still lags after 2–3 weeks of fixes: set a markdown.

## 4. Markdown triggers and cadence
Typical rule set (**uncertain:** tune to your category and season length):
- Projected end-of-season sell-through < target (e.g. 70%) at current ROS → first
  markdown 20–30%.
- After 2–3 weeks, if ROS doesn't lift enough to clear → second step 40–50%.
- Final clearance near season end: deeper, consolidated to fewer stores or outlet/online.
- Check margin impact: markdown cost = units × (old price − new price). Use any
  vendor markdown support agreed.
Early, shallow markdowns usually beat late deep ones; avoid marking down
best-sellers in categories you'll promote anyway.

## 5. The weekly trade meeting (45–60 min)
Attendees: buyer, merchandiser/planner, allocator, visual merchandising, sometimes
marketing. Agenda:
1. Headline: week vs plan and LY, by category.
2. Top/bottom options with diagnosis.
3. Decisions: rebuys, cancels, transfers, markdowns, VM changes, promotions.
4. Next week's deliveries and launches.
5. Actions log with owner and deadline — reviewed first thing next week.

## Gotchas
- Raw unit rankings favour widely distributed options — use ROS per store.
- Broken sizes depress sales and look like a weak line; check availability.
- Markdown in one channel only (e.g. online) without matching stores creates price complaints.
- Weather and calendar shifts (Easter dates) distort week-on-week comparisons; compare to LY carefully.

## Verify
- [ ] Weekly report shows ROS, sell-through, cover and availability per option.
- [ ] Best sellers with < 4 weeks' cover assessed for rebuy against lead time and OTB.
- [ ] Worst sellers diagnosed and cheap fixes tried before marking down.
- [ ] Markdown triggers applied with margin cost computed.
- [ ] Trade meeting decisions logged with owners and reviewed next week.
