# Comp shopping and trend translation

**Goal:** decide which trends to back, when, and how hard — then translate them
into product your customer will actually buy at your price. A buyer's trend
job is commercial, not editorial: the question is never "is it cool?" but "how
many units, in which version, at which price, in which stores?"

## 1. Gather signals on a cadence
- **Trend services** (WGSN, Trendalytics, Heuritech, etc., or free equivalents):
  macro themes, colour and material forecasts 12–24 months ahead. Use as
  direction, not a shopping list.
- **Runway and designer** collections (Vogue Runway, show reports): where silhouettes
  and colours start.
- **Social and search**: TikTok, Instagram, Pinterest Trends, Google Trends —
  look for **rising search volume over several weeks**, not a single viral spike.
  Compare against last year's same period.
- **Own data**: what is selling at full price faster than plan, and what
  new-in tests outperform.
- **Customer-facing staff**: what customers ask for that you don't stock.

## 2. Comp shopping (structured, not browsing)
Monthly per category, visit 4–6 competitors in store and online (leader, direct
competitor, aspirational, value). Record in a template:
- new arrivals, key items, colours, fabrics, fits;
- price architecture (entry/core/top price);
- depth (how much stock, how many sizes on the floor);
- what's on markdown already (what *didn't* work);
- presentation and space given to the trend.
Photograph where allowed; tag each item with date, store, price. Look for the
same item appearing across several competitors — that's the market committing.

## 3. Place each trend on the lifecycle
- **Emerging** (designers, early adopters): test small, in flagship/online only.
- **Rising** (fast-fashion leaders adopting, search climbing): commit in
  key items, A-grade stores.
- **Peak/mass** (everyone has it, value retailers too): carry core versions,
  be careful with depth — peak is when markdown risk starts.
- **Declining** (markdowns everywhere): exit.
Your position depends on your customer: a mass-market buyer typically enters
at "rising", a premium/fashion-forward one earlier.

## 4. Translate into commercial product
For each backed trend, define:
1. The **key items** (2–5) that carry it in your category.
2. The **commercial version**: adjust silhouette, length, fabric weight and
   colour for your customer's body shape, climate, lifestyle and price point.
3. Where it sits in good-better-best (see range-architecture-and-size-curves).
4. Unit commitment and test-vs-chase plan; agree a re-order trigger
   (e.g. sell-through > 30% in 2 weeks → chase).
5. Supplier with lead time short enough to chase.
Present to the range review as a mood board plus a line list with units and margin.

## 5. Seasonal direction call
Summarise in one page: 3–5 trends to back (with lifecycle stage), 2–3 to
avoid or exit, colour story, and the evidence for each. Revisit mid-season.

## Gotchas
- Social virality ≠ your customer: check age and region of the audience.
- Copying a competitor's exact item risks IP claims and arrives late — translate, don't clone.
- Trend items need short lead times; long-lead factories suit core, not trend.
- **Uncertain:** the lifecycle-stage cues are heuristic; validate against your own sell-through history.
