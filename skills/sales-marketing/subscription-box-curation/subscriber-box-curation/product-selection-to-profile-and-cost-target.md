# Product selection to profile and cost target

**Goal:** each box makes subscribers feel they got more than they paid for, fits who they
are, and still leaves the business a margin after shipping.

## 1. Work out the cost-per-box target
```
box_price (after discounts, ex-tax)
− payment fees (~2–4%, check provider)
− fulfilment (pick, pack, packaging, inserts)
− shipping
− target contribution margin
= max product cost per box (landed: incl. inbound freight, duty)
```
Example: £30 box → £0.90 fees, £3 packing, £4.50 shipping, £8 margin → about **£13.60** for
products. This number rules every decision.

## 2. Perceived value: the curation math
- **Retail value (RRP sum) vs price:** many boxes aim for contents with RRP well above the box
  price (often quoted as 1.5–3×); achievable through brand partnerships, sampling deals and
  bulk buying. Don't claim inflated RRPs — consumer law covers misleading reference prices.
- **One hero item** (full-size, recognisable, photographable) makes the unboxing; 2–4
  supporting items; 1 small surprise (sample, treat, note).
- **Mix of known and discovery:** at least one familiar brand for trust, and one find they
  wouldn't have discovered.
- Avoid filler that screams "filler" (stickers, flyers as items).

## 3. Subscriber profiles
- Onboarding **preference quiz**: size, skin/hair type, taste, experience level, dislikes,
  allergies, dietary needs. Keep it short (5–10 questions) and store answers as structured
  fields, not free text.
- Segment into a manageable number of **variants** (often 2–6) rather than per-person boxes:
  e.g. "light / dark roast", "dry / oily skin". Each variant needs its own pick list and stock.
- Use ratings and reviews from previous boxes per segment to steer the next selection.

## 4. Allergies, restrictions and safety
- Food/cosmetic boxes: track allergens per item; route subscribers with declared allergies
  to a safe variant. Label clearly; follow local food-labelling rules.
- Cosmetics/supplements: ensure items comply with local regulations (ingredient listing,
  product notifications) — importers carry responsibility in many jurisdictions.
- Swap rules: if an item runs short, pre-approve substitutes of equal value.

## 5. Selection process (monthly)
1. Shortlist 10–20 candidates per slot from suppliers and brand pitches.
2. Score each: fit to segment, perceived value vs cost, availability in volume, shipping
   weight/fragility, novelty vs past boxes, brand willingness to co-promote.
3. Build 2–3 draft boxes per variant; cost them in a spreadsheet against target.
4. Test pack one physical box: fit, weight band (postage tiers!), breakage, unboxing photo.
5. Lock quantities with buffer (often +3–5% for damages and late subscribers).

## Gotchas
- **Postage tiers:** 10 g or 1 cm over a band can add significant cost per box; weigh the test box.
- Repeating the same category every month causes fatigue — track item categories per subscriber.
- Brand-sampling deals may require data sharing or reviews; check privacy law and disclosure.
- Ratios above are common practice, not verified benchmarks.

## Verify
- [ ] Max landed product cost per box calculated and respected.
- [ ] Each box has a hero item, supporting items and a surprise; RRP claims honest.
- [ ] Variants map cleanly to quiz answers; allergen routing tested.
- [ ] Test box packed and weighed within the target postage band.
- [ ] Post-box ratings collected per item and segment.
