# Feature-of-the-month and limited drops

**Goal:** the monthly pick (a book, a wine, a coffee, a sneaker drop) feels like a find that
most members love — balancing safe broad appeal against the discovery members joined for.

## 1. Keep a candidate pipeline 3–6 months ahead
- Sources: publisher/producer catalogues and advance copies, trade shows, distributor
  lists, member suggestions, staff picks, trend signals (searches, social chatter).
- Log every candidate in a sheet: title/product, category/genre, price/cost, release date,
  availability at volume, exclusivity possible, notes from whoever tried it.
- Everyone on the selection panel must actually read/taste/try finalists.

## 2. Score with a rubric
Score 1–5 each:
| Criterion | Question |
|---|---|
| Broad appeal | Will most members like it? (past ratings for similar picks) |
| Discovery | Would members have found it on their own? Is it new to them? |
| Brand fit | Does it fit our club's identity and promise? |
| Story | Can we tell a compelling story (maker, author, origin)? |
| Supply & cost | Can we get enough, on time, within cost target? |
| Variety | Different from the last 3 picks (genre, region, style)? |
Weight broad appeal and supply higher for mainstream clubs; discovery higher for niche ones.

## 3. Balance discovery vs appeal over a sequence
Plan the year as a mix rather than each month alone: e.g. alternate a crowd-pleaser with a
bolder pick, or follow a 3-month pattern. Watch ratings per pick type to tune the mix —
if bold picks drive skips and cancellations, dial them back or offer choice.

## 4. Member choice, alternates and skips
- Many clubs offer **a choice of 3–5 picks** each month with a default; this cuts dissatisfaction
  and returns. Decision deadline a week or so before shipping.
- **Skip the month** option reduces cancellations (see
  sales-marketing.subscription-box-curation.member-retention-offers.churn-signals-pause-flows-and-win-backs).
- Track pick-share: if one option takes most votes every month, your lineup is unbalanced.

## 5. Limited drops and exclusives
- **Exclusive editions** (special cover, signed copy, private label, small-batch bottling)
  need early negotiation with the producer: quantity, cost, timeline, exclusivity window.
- **Drop mechanics:** announce date/time, quantity (honestly), per-member limit, early access
  for longest-standing members as a perk. Use a queue/waitlist to manage traffic.
- Sell-out speed is a signal: instant sell-out = you could have ordered more or priced higher;
  leftover stock = rethink pick or volume.
- Any scarcity claim ("only 500 made") must be true — misleading urgency can breach consumer law.

## 6. Feedback loop
After each pick: rating (1–5), "would you have chosen this yourself?", skip/cancel rates
that month, social mentions. Review quarterly against the rubric scores to calibrate the panel.

## Gotchas
- Release-date slips break the schedule; keep an alternate ready per month.
- Panel taste drifts from member taste — always check ratings, not panel enthusiasm.
- Exclusive editions often require minimum orders; budget for the leftovers.
- The rubric weights are suggestions; calibrate against your ratings data.

## Verify
- [ ] Pipeline covers the next 3–6 months; finalists tried by the panel.
- [ ] Each pick scored with the rubric; variety vs last 3 picks checked.
- [ ] Alternate ready for each month in case of supply slips.
- [ ] Member choice/skip deadlines communicated.
- [ ] Ratings and skip rates reviewed per pick and fed into the next selection.
