# Theme calendar, lead times and reveals

**Goal:** a year of box themes that feel timely, each sourced on time without panic buys,
and each revealed in a way that pulls in new subscribers before the cut-off.

## 1. Draft the 12-month theme calendar
- List fixed dates: seasons, holidays and gifting peaks relevant to your audience (they
  differ by country and hemisphere), back-to-school, awareness months, your anniversary.
- Assign one theme per box; mark which are **acquisition peaks** (often the gift season) —
  put your strongest theme and biggest hero item there.
- Mix theme types: seasonal ("autumn cosy"), occasion ("self-care for exam season"),
  discovery ("Japan"), collaboration (guest curator or brand).
- Avoid two similar themes back-to-back; rotate product categories.

## 2. Schedule backwards from the ship date
For each box, build a critical path (example timings — replace with your suppliers' real
lead times):
| Milestone | Weeks before ship |
|---|---|
| Theme locked, brief to buyers | 16–24 (longer for custom/imported) |
| Samples in hand, selection signed off | 12–16 |
| Purchase orders placed | 10–14 (imports: add ocean freight + customs, often 6–10 weeks) |
| Custom packaging/inserts artwork final | 8–10 |
| Goods received and QC'd | 3–4 |
| Subscriber cut-off / final count | 2–3 |
| Pack | 1–2 |
| Ship | 0 |
- Custom printed boxes, tissue and inserts often have the longest lead times and minimum order
  quantities — order generic packaging for the year and theme with inserts.
- Keep **one buffer theme** with stock-on-hand items as a fallback for a late supplier.

## 3. Volume forecasting
Order quantity = current active subscribers + expected net new before cut-off + buffer
(damages, replacements; often a few %). Use last year's growth around the same date; gift
seasons can spike sign-ups sharply.

## 4. Reveal and teaser sequencing
Typical sequence for a box (adjust to your audience):
1. **Theme announce** (4–6 weeks before cut-off): name and mood image — creates curiosity.
2. **Teasers** (weekly): one item silhouette, a blurred photo, a clue, a maker story.
3. **Hero reveal** (1–2 weeks before cut-off): the headline item with "join by [date] to get
   this box". This is usually the sign-up spike.
4. **Cut-off reminder** (48 hours before).
5. **Spoilers** after shipping: full contents for those who want them, with a spoiler warning;
   some subscribers love surprise and some want to know.
6. **Unboxing UGC push** when boxes land: hashtag, prompt card in the box.
Keep spoilers consistent: decide policy (e.g. full reveal only after delivery starts).

## 5. Review
After each box: on-time ship %, cost vs target, sign-ups during reveal window, ratings,
social mentions. Feed into the next year's calendar.

## Gotchas
- Social posts showing items before the hero reveal leak the surprise — brief partners and
  influencers on embargo dates.
- Hemisphere matters: "summer" boxes shipped internationally may arrive in winter.
- Seasonal items left over after the date are hard to reuse; buy tight.
- Lead times above are illustrative, not verified supplier data.

## Verify

- [ ] Each box has a dated critical path from real supplier lead times.
- [ ] Fallback theme with on-hand stock exists.
- [ ] Order quantities include forecast growth and buffer.
- [ ] Reveal sequence and spoiler policy scheduled; partners know embargo dates.
