# Bundle and multi-buy offers at the point of sale

**Goal:** raise average transaction value (ATV) at the moment of payment with
offers customers are glad they took — the right add-on, priced and explained
clearly — rather than pressure that annoys them and drives returns.

## 1. Pick the mechanic
- **Fixed bundle** — named items at one price (phone + case + screen protector).
  Best when items are naturally used together.
- **Multi-buy** — 3-for-2, 2 for $10, buy-one-get-one (BOGO). Best for consumables
  and stock-up items.
- **Mix-and-match** — any 3 from a group. Gives choice; set up the group carefully.
- **Spend threshold** — free gift or $ off over $X (place the threshold ~20–30% above
  current ATV — **uncertain:** tune with your own data).
- **Add-on prompt** — a suggested item at checkout (in store: by the cashier or at
  the till display; online: "frequently bought together", order bump).

## 2. Price it so it works for both sides
- Customer saving must be **clear and real**: show "save $8" against the separate prices.
- Check margin: bundle price − sum of item costs. A 3-for-2 on a 40%-margin item
  gives ~10% margin on the three; is the extra unit sold worth it?
- Put the discount on the higher-margin or slower item where possible.
- Avoid bundles that cannibalise full-price sales of the hero item.

## 3. Set up correctly in POS/e-commerce
- Define the bundle or mix-and-match group by SKU, quantity rule, price and dates.
- Decide how the discount is **apportioned** across items (matters for returns and
  for category sales reporting): usually pro-rata to item price.
- Returns rule: returning one item from a bundle — refund the item's apportioned
  price, or reverse the deal? Write it down and configure the POS accordingly.
- Test: correct items qualify, wrong ones don't, quantities of 2 and 4 on a
  3-for-2 behave as intended, stacking with other offers follows the rules.

## 4. Checkout add-on prompts that feel like help
At the till or in the online cart:
- **Relevant** — tied to what's in the basket ("Batteries aren't included with
  this — do you need some? It takes AA").
- **One** suggestion, not three.
- **Value framed**: "It's in the 3-for-2 — you've got two, the third is free"
  (often customers simply didn't notice).
- **Easy no**: accept "no thanks" immediately; don't repeat.
- Online: pre-selected add-ons ("opt-out") are restricted or banned in many places —
  use opt-in.
Script for staff: notice → relevant suggestion → reason → accept the answer.

## 5. Measure
- **Attachment rate** = transactions including the add-on ÷ transactions with
  the qualifying item.
- ATV and units per transaction vs a control period/store.
- Margin per transaction, not just revenue.
- Returns rate on bundled items (high returns = pushed, not wanted).
- Staff-level attachment (coaching, not punishment).

## Gotchas
- Multi-buys on a single unit the customer intended may create waste and returns.
- "Unadvertised" multi-buys that only trigger at the till frustrate customers who
  missed them — sign them on shelf.
- Unit-price and reference-price display rules apply to bundles in many places.
- Pressure selling of add-ons like extended warranties is regulated in some
  jurisdictions — follow your compliance rules.

## Verify
- [ ] Mechanic matches the product type; savings shown clearly.
- [ ] Bundle margin calculated and acceptable.
- [ ] POS setup tested for qualification, quantities, stacking and returns apportionment.
- [ ] Add-on prompts are single, relevant, value-framed and opt-in.
- [ ] Attachment rate, ATV, margin and returns tracked vs a baseline.
