# Tier benefits, redemption and the enrolment pitch

**Goal:** make the loyalty program work for the customer in the 60 seconds at
the counter: they get the points, discounts and offers they're entitled to,
non-members hear a quick reason to join, and the data captured is accurate and
consented. Program rules come from head office; this is how to execute them well.

## 1. Know your program cold
One-page crib sheet per store/site:
- Earn rate (e.g. 1 point per $1), excluded items (gift cards, tobacco, some
  promotions), bonus multipliers running now.
- Tiers, thresholds and benefits (member pricing, free delivery, birthday offer,
  priority service), and how tier status is shown (app card, POS flag).
- How targeted/personal offers appear (app wallet, auto-applied at POS, coupon code).
- Redemption rules: minimum points, value per point, partial redemption, on which items.
- Who can adjust points and how (missing transaction claims, goodwill).

## 2. At every transaction
1. **Ask early** — "Are you a member?" before scanning, so member prices and
   offers apply to everything. Asking at the end causes re-rings.
2. **Identify** by app/card scan, phone or email lookup; confirm name to avoid
   crediting the wrong account.
3. **Check offers** — on many systems personalised offers must be "activated" in
   the app first; prompt the customer if the POS shows available offers.
4. **Mention the balance or a benefit** ("You've got $15 in rewards — want to use it
   today?" / "You're $40 from Gold").
5. **Receipt** shows points earned; point the customer to it.

## 3. Redemption and problems
- Offer not applying: check eligibility (item, minimum spend, expiry, activation,
  one per member) before overriding; override only within authority, with reason code.
- Points missing: most programs allow a claim with receipt within N days —
  take details or show the customer the self-service route; don't promise a
  manual credit you can't authorise.
- Returns: points earned are usually reversed and redeemed rewards may or may
  not be reinstated — know the rule and say it before processing.
- Shared/family accounts: follow the program's rules; don't add points to a staff
  member's or friend's account (common fraud flag).

## 4. The enrolment pitch (15 seconds)
Lead with **today's** benefit, then the ongoing one, then make it easy:
> "You'd save $6.50 on this today with member pricing — it's free and takes
> 30 seconds with your phone number. Want me to set it up?"
- Tailor to the basket (a big basket = immediate saving; a parent = birthday or
  kids' offers).
- If no: "No problem" — and move on. One ask per visit.
- Use the program's fast enrolment (QR code, SMS link) rather than typing a long form at the till.

## 5. Consent and data quality
- Read back email/phone; typos make the account useless.
- Marketing consent must be **separate** and freely given; tell customers
  what they'll receive. Pre-ticked boxes or bundled consent are not valid in many
  jurisdictions (e.g. GDPR/UK GDPR).
- Never sign customers up without their knowledge to hit enrolment targets —
  common and serious misconduct.

## Gotchas
- Asking for membership after payment means re-rings or angry customers.
- Staff enrolment targets drive fake or duplicate accounts; managers should check
  data quality, not just counts.
- Tier status mismatches between app and POS — know who to call.
- **Uncertain:** activation requirements and claim windows vary by program.

## Verify
- [ ] Crib sheet covers earn, tiers, offers, redemption and adjustment rules.
- [ ] Membership asked before scanning on every transaction.
- [ ] Offer issues checked against eligibility before any override.
- [ ] Enrolment pitch leads with today's saving; one ask per visit.
- [ ] Contact details read back; marketing consent separate and explicit.
