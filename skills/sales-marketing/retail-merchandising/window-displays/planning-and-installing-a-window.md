# Planning and installing a window — the window as a selling tool

**Goal:** a passer-by stops, understands the offer in about three seconds, and walks in.
A window that wins awards but doesn't move footfall or the featured product has failed.

## 1. Brief the window (one paragraph)
Answer before buying a single prop:
- **What must it sell?** 1–3 hero products with enough stock depth to last the window.
- **One message:** a season, a launch, a price event, a gift idea. One window, one story.
- **Who walks past** and from which direction, on foot or by car? A window read from a car
  needs bigger shapes and less text.
- **Budget and timing:** install date, live period, strike date.

## 2. Design for the street, not the stockroom
- Stand **across the street and 3 m back** — the real viewing distances. Photograph the
  empty window from both directions of travel.
- **Focal point** near eye level (roughly 1.4–1.6 m above the pavement) and slightly
  off-centre toward the oncoming foot traffic.
- **Layer depth:** background (colour/backdrop), mid-ground (props/mannequins), foreground
  (the hero product, closest to the glass). Pyramid composition and odd-number groupings.
- **Scale up:** small products need a big framing device (oversize prop, repeated
  product in quantity, a block of colour) or they vanish.
- **Words:** fewer than ~7 on the glass/backdrop; legible from across the street. If
  showing prices, make them consistent and accurate (pricing law applies in windows too).
- Sketch to scale on graph paper or a phone photo with overlays; list every prop and fixing.

## 3. Light it
- Windows compete with daylight glare: at day, a bright interior and directional
  spots on the hero product help; at night, the window is the brightest thing on the street.
- Aim spotlights at the product, not the glass (reflections) or into viewers' eyes.
- Put window lights on a **timer** running into the evening (check local rules on
  overnight illumination; some places restrict it).
- Check for **sun fading:** south/west-facing windows (northern hemisphere) bleach fabric and
  packaging within weeks. Use UV film or rotate product; display samples, not saleable stock.

## 4. Install and strike
- Clean the glass inside and out before and after. Floor covering down first, backdrop next,
  then props back to front, product last.
- Fix everything that could topple onto the glass: fishing line, clear tape on the base,
  weighted stands. Nothing leaning against the glass.
- Check from outside after each layer; adjust from the street view, not inside.
- Capture photos day and night for the record and for head office.

## 5. Changeover calendar
Plan the year: typically 6–12 changes (seasons, key holidays, launches, sale). Work back
from each install date: concept 8–10 weeks out, props ordered 4–6 weeks, product
reserved 2 weeks, install out of trading hours. Small stores often refresh a hero product
weekly within a longer theme to keep regulars looking.

## Gotchas
- Featuring a product the store has 3 of creates disappointed customers — reserve stock.
- Dust, dead insects and fading kill a window by week three; schedule a weekly clean.
- Props bought for one window rarely reuse; design some modular pieces (plinths, cubes).
- Unverified: industry figures on "window conversion" vary; count footfall yourself
  (a door counter or a manual 15-minute tally) for a before/after comparison.

## Verify
- [ ] From across the street you can name the story and the hero product in 3 seconds.
- [ ] Hero products have stock to last the window period.
- [ ] Lights aim at product, timers set, no glare from the pavement.
- [ ] Nothing can fall onto or lean against the glass.
- [ ] Day and night photos taken; footfall and featured-item sales tracked against the prior window.
- [ ] Next changeover date and lead-time milestones are on the calendar.
