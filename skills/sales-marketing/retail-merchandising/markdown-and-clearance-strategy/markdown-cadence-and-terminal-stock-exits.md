# Markdown cadence and terminal-stock exits

**Goal:** clear seasonal or slow stock before it ages, at the highest blended margin, and
free cash and space for new product. Markdowns are a cost of a buying decision — the job
is to take them early and small, not late and deep.

## 1. Find candidates weekly
For each line (style/SKU) compute:
```
sell_through_%  = units_sold / (units_sold + units_on_hand)     # since launch
weeks_of_cover  = units_on_hand / avg_weekly_units_last_4wks
weeks_left      = weeks until season end / planned exit date
```
Flag a line when `weeks_of_cover > weeks_left` — at current rate it won't sell out in
time. Also compare sell-through to plan (e.g. a 12-week season with an 80% target should
be roughly 40% through by week 6; your planning team sets the curve).

## 2. Markdown in steps, not one plunge
- **First markdown** early and shallow: often 20–30% off, once a line falls behind plan.
  Early cuts sell at a higher price than late deep ones.
- Review after **1–2 weeks**. If rate of sale doesn't lift enough, take the **second step**
  (e.g. to 40–50%), then a **final** (60%+) near the exit date.
- Price to clear ends (".99/.00") consistently; follow your jurisdiction's rules on
  "was/now" reference pricing (many require the "was" price to have genuinely applied
  for a period — check local consumer law).
- Respond to elasticity: if a 25% cut doubled weekly units, it's working; if units rose
  10%, the product has a problem price won't fix (size, colour, fit) — move to exit.

## 3. Clearance zoning in store
- Consolidate markdowns into **one clearance area** at the back or side: it pulls shoppers
  through full-price stock and protects the full-price look up front.
- Size-sort clearance racks (shoppers search by size first), mark the % clearly on signage.
- Consolidate broken size runs from several stores into one (or online) so they sell.
- Remove full-price neighbours from clearance fixtures to avoid confusion at the till.

## 4. Terminal stock exits
When it's still left after the final markdown:
1. **Outlet stores / online outlet** — keeps margin in-house, protects main brand pricing.
2. **Jobbers / liquidators / off-price buyers** — sell the lot by the pallet at a fraction
   of cost; get quotes from 2–3, specify whether they may resell in your region (brand
   protection clause).
3. **Donation** — may carry tax relief depending on jurisdiction; confirm with finance.
4. **Hold for next season** only for genuinely seasonless basics; carrying costs and ageing
   are real.
Never let terminal stock sit: it costs space, handling and insurance.

## 5. Measure the real outcome
Track **markdown cost** = (original price − sale price) × units, as % of sales; and
**realised margin** per line. Feed the result back into buying: lines that always need
deep markdowns are bought too deep.

## Gotchas
- Promotions and markdowns are different: a promotion is temporary and returns to full
  price; a markdown is permanent. Don't mix them in reporting.
- Price-matching or system errors: check the POS shows the new price before signage goes up.
- Discounts like 30% don't stack the way staff assume ("extra 20% off sale" = 44% off
  total, not 50%). Work out the final price and margin before announcing.
- Numbers above are common practice ranges, not universal rules; calibrate on your history.

## Verify
- [ ] Weekly report flags lines with weeks of cover > weeks left.
- [ ] Each flagged line has a dated first, second and final markdown step.
- [ ] Clearance sits in one zone, size-sorted, with POS prices checked.
- [ ] "Was/now" pricing complies with local rules.
- [ ] Terminal stock has an exit route and buyer before season end.
- [ ] Markdown cost % and realised margin reported and fed back to buying.
