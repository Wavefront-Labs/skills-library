# Focal points and fixture styling — a floor that sells without a salesperson

**Goal:** a customer walking in sees a clear story within three seconds, moves deeper into
the store, and can find and pick up product without wrecking the display.

## 1. Walk the store as a customer first
- Stand just inside the door. The first 1.5–3 m is the **decompression zone**: shoppers are
  adjusting and miss most of what's there. Don't put key product or small signage in it.
- Note the **first sight line** (usually straight ahead or slightly right; many shoppers in
  right-hand-traffic countries drift right — treat that as a tendency, not a law, and watch
  your own traffic).
- Mark 3–5 **focal points**: the end of each main sight line, the back wall (to pull people
  through), and the cash wrap queue.
- Sketch it: door, aisles, fixtures, focal points. Every focal point must be visible from
  where a shopper actually stands, not from the stockroom door.

## 2. Build each focal point as one story
One message per focal point: one trend, one colour story, one price promise, or one
use-occasion ("back to school", "summer hosting").
- **Pyramid (triangle) layout:** tallest item at the back centre, stepping down to the
  sides and front. The eye travels up to the apex and down across the product.
- **Rule of three / odd numbers:** groups of 3 or 5 read as styled; even rows read as stock.
- **Repetition:** the same item repeated in a block reads as confidence and abundance.
- Leave **negative space** — a table that's 100% covered reads as a markdown bin.
- Light it: the focal point should be the brightest thing on its sight line. Re-aim
  spotlights after every change (a classic miss).

## 3. Fixture-by-fixture rules of thumb
- **Nesting/feature tables:** the hero product at the highest tier, folded stacks in the
  middle, accessories at the front edge for impulse. Fold stacks to a consistent width
  (use a folding board), spine facing the customer, max ~8–10 items high so it doesn't topple.
- **Walls:** read left to right, light to dark (or by size small→large); the
  **eye-to-chest band (~1.2–1.6 m)** is prime — put the best margin there. Faced-out
  hero at the top of each bay, hanging depth below.
- **Colour blocking:** group by colour in vertical bands on walls (horizontal bands get
  broken when a size sells out). Run warm→cool or follow a spectrum; neutrals at the ends.
- **Mannequins:** dress them in product that's actually on the adjacent fixture, fully
  accessorised (cross-merchandising). Group of 3 at different heights/poses. Sign "as worn"
  with prices if your brand does that.
- **Cross-merchandise** complementary items at the point of the main purchase (belts by
  trousers, dip by crisps).

## 4. Store standards — what makes it last past Tuesday
Write a one-page standard per fixture type with a photo of "right": fold width, hanger
direction (all hooks facing the same way, usually "question mark" toward the back),
size order (S→XL left→right / top→bottom), sizing cubes visible, price tickets in the
same position. Do a daily **recovery walk** (opening and after peaks) against it.

## Gotchas
- **Overstuffed rails** kill sales: aim for a hand's width of space every few hangers.
- **Out-of-stock heroes:** a mannequin wearing an item you can't sell is a complaint
  generator. Check depth before choosing hero product (rule of thumb: enough to last the
  display period, often 2–4 weeks — confirm with your allocator).
- **Head office plans vs. your box:** corporate visual guidelines assume a standard floor
  plate; adapt to your sight lines and note deviations with photos.
- **Safety:** keep fire exits, sprinklers clearances and aisle widths (accessibility rules
  vary by jurisdiction — check local code) clear; no heavy product on top tiers.
- Untested: the commonly quoted "right turn" and dwell-time numbers vary by study; measure
  your own floor with sales per fixture before and after.

## Verify
- [ ] From the doorway you can name the story of each focal point in one sentence.
- [ ] Every focal point is lit and visible from the shopper's path, not hidden by fixtures.
- [ ] Tables follow a pyramid; walls are colour-blocked vertically with best sellers at eye level.
- [ ] Mannequin outfits are in stock on the adjacent fixture in a full size run.
- [ ] A written standard with photos exists and the recovery walk is done daily.
- [ ] You compared sales per fixture for 2 weeks before vs after the change.
