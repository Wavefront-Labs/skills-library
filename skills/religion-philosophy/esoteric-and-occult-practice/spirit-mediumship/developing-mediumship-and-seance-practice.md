# Developing mediumship and séance practice

**Goal:** develop and practise mediumship the way Spiritualist churches and training
centres teach it — gradually, in a supervised circle, with evidential messages and
firm ethics toward sitters, many of whom are grieving.

Spiritualism is a religion in its own right (e.g. the Spiritualists' National Union in
the UK, the National Spiritualist Association of Churches in the US) with services,
hymns and healing as well as mediumship. Its training centres (e.g. the Arthur Findlay
College, Stansted) run structured courses.

## 1. The development circle
Mediumship is traditionally developed in a **closed circle** led by an experienced
medium, meeting weekly at a fixed time, with the same members, often for years.
A typical session (60–90 min):
1. **Opening prayer** or invocation, asking for protection and guidance.
2. **Attunement/meditation** (15–20 min): slow breathing, expanding awareness,
   "sitting in the power".
3. **Exercises:** e.g. psychometry (holding an object and describing impressions),
   linking with a partner and giving what comes, describing a "communicator".
4. **Feedback** from the leader: what was evidential, what was guesswork or
   imagination.
5. **Closing prayer** and **closing down:** consciously drawing awareness back,
   grounding (feet on the floor, eat or drink something).

## 2. The senses of mediumship (Spiritualist terms)
Clairvoyance (seeing), clairaudience (hearing), clairsentience (feeling/sensing),
claircognisance (knowing). Developers learn which is strongest for them and to report
impressions accurately in the mode received.

## 3. Giving an evidential message
Structure taught in many circles:
1. **Establish the link:** "I'd like to come to the lady in the blue scarf."
2. **Evidence first:** specific, verifiable facts about the communicator — appearance,
   personality, manner of passing, names, places, shared memories. Aim for details
   the medium could not plausibly guess.
3. **Ask for yes/no confirmation**, not leading information; don't fish.
4. **The message:** what the communicator wants to say — usually love, reassurance,
   recognition of current events.
5. **Close the link** and thank both.
Good practice: give what you get without embroidering; if it isn't accepted, don't
force it.

## 4. Platform demonstration
In a Spiritualist service: hymns, prayer, address (a short talk on philosophy), then
the demonstration — typically 3–6 messages in 20–30 minutes, each 3–5 minutes —
closing hymn and prayer. Speak clearly, include the whole congregation, keep each
message positive.

## 5. Séance (sitting) practice
Private sittings are usually 30–60 minutes, one-to-one. Explain the process at the
start (you cannot guarantee a particular person will communicate). Physical
mediumship séances (cabinet, darkness, trumpet) are a specialist and contested area;
attend only those run by established, accountable organisations.

## Ethics
- Many sitters are recently bereaved: be gentle; don't encourage reliance or repeated
  sittings; know local grief-support services and refer.
- Never give health diagnoses, predictions of death, legal or financial advice.
- Fees should be clear and modest; consumer-protection laws (e.g. UK Consumer
  Protection from Unfair Trading Regulations 2008, which replaced the Fraudulent
  Mediums Act) apply.
- Confidentiality of what sitters share.
- Development and mental health: stop and seek help if experiences become
  intrusive, frightening or blur everyday functioning.

## Gotchas
- Developing alone without an experienced leader.
- Fishing ("I'm getting an M… does M mean anything?") instead of evidence.
- Skipping the closing down.
- Taking every impression as spirit rather than imagination — circles train discernment.

## Verify

- [ ] Your messages lead with specific evidence and ask only yes/no confirmation.
- [ ] Every session ends with a deliberate closing down and grounding.
- [ ] Your practice statement covers bereaved sitters, no predictions and fee clarity.
- [ ] You know local referral options for grief and mental-health support.
