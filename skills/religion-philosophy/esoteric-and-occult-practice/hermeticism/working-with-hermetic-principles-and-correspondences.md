# Working with Hermetic principles and correspondences

**Goal:** know the actual sources of the Hermetic tradition, build a correspondence
table you can use in practice, time work by planetary days and hours, and take up the
contemplative practice the Hermetic texts themselves describe.

## 1. Know your sources
- **Corpus Hermeticum** (Greek, roughly 1st–3rd c. CE, Egypt): dialogues of Hermes
  Trismegistus. Treatise I, *Poimandres*, describes a vision of creation and the
  soul's ascent. Translated into Latin by Marsilio Ficino (1471), launching
  Renaissance Hermeticism. Modern translation: Copenhaver.
- **Asclepius** (Latin): includes the passage on animating statues.
- **Emerald Tablet** (*Tabula Smaragdina*; Arabic, roughly 8th–10th c.): short,
  alchemical; source of "that which is below is like that which is above".
- **The Kybalion** (1908, "Three Initiates", widely attributed to William Walker
  Atkinson): the "seven Hermetic principles" (mentalism, correspondence, vibration,
  polarity, rhythm, cause and effect, gender). It is a New Thought text, not an
  ancient one — useful to many practitioners, but label it accurately.
- **Agrippa, *Three Books of Occult Philosophy*** (1533): the great Renaissance
  synthesis of correspondences.

## 2. The principle of correspondence in practice
The cosmos is ordered so that things share qualities across levels — planets,
metals, colours, plants, days, parts of the body. Practice uses these links to align
the practitioner with a quality.
Build a table:

| Planet | Day | Metal | Colour (one common set) | Qualities |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sun | Sunday | gold | gold/yellow | vitality, success |
| Moon | Monday | silver | silver/white | dreams, intuition |
| Mars | Tuesday | iron | red | courage, conflict |
| Mercury | Wednesday | quicksilver | orange or mixed | intellect, speech |
| Jupiter | Thursday | tin | blue/purple | growth, benevolence |
| Venus | Friday | copper | green | love, art |
| Saturn | Saturday | lead | black | discipline, endings |

Colour sets differ between sources (Golden Dawn, Agrippa, others); record which you use.

## 3. Planetary hours
Traditional method:
1. Find local sunrise and sunset times.
2. Divide daylight into 12 equal "hours" and night into 12 (so their length varies
   with season).
3. The first hour after sunrise belongs to the day's ruler (Sunday → Sun).
4. Subsequent hours follow the **Chaldean order**: Saturn, Jupiter, Mars, Sun, Venus,
   Mercury, Moon, then repeat.
Example: Wednesday's first hour is Mercury, second Moon, third Saturn.
Several apps compute this; check one hour by hand to confirm the app's method.

## 4. Applying it
- Choose the planet matching your intent (study → Mercury).
- Work on its day and in its hour; use its colour, metal, incense; recite a prayer or
  hymn from the tradition (Ficino wrote planetary hymns for this purpose — his
  "natural magic" for health of spirit).
- Record the working and results.

## 5. Contemplative practice of the texts
- **Reading as ascent:** read *Poimandres* slowly, one section per day, followed by
  silence. The texts present knowledge (*gnōsis*) of God and self as the goal.
- **Hymns and silent prayer:** Corpus Hermeticum treatises end with hymns (e.g. the
  Secret Hymn in CH XIII); recite and then sit in silence ("speech through silence").
- **Macrocosm/microcosm reflection:** at day's end, review which planetary qualities
  were strong or weak in you.

## Gotchas
- Presenting the Kybalion as ancient Egyptian teaching.
- Mixing correspondence tables from different systems mid-working.
- Using fixed 60-minute "planetary hours" — traditional hours are unequal.
- Ignoring that much "Hermeticism" in modern occultism is via the Golden Dawn rather
  than the Greek texts.
