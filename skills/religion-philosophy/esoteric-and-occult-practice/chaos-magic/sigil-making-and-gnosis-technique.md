# Sigil-making and gnosis technique

**Goal:** perform the basic chaos-magic cycle — sigil, gnosis, launch, forget, record —
and use "belief as a tool" in a disciplined, testable way.

Chaos magic developed in 1970s England (Peter Carroll's *Liber Null*, Ray Sherwin,
the Illuminates of Thanateros) on foundations from the artist Austin Osman Spare
(early 20th century). It emphasises technique and results over any fixed cosmology.

## 1. Write the statement of intent
- One sentence, present or desire form: "It is my will to finish my thesis draft
  by June." Many chaos magicians use "It is my will to…" or "This is my wish to…".
- Specific, achievable, and with an obvious way to know if it happened.

## 2. Build the sigil (Spare's letter method)
1. Write the sentence in capitals.
2. Strike out repeated letters (keep only the first instance of each), and in many
   versions all vowels too. E.g. "IT IS MY WILL TO FINISH MY THESIS DRAFT BY JUNE"
   → consonants without repeats: T S M Y W L F N H D R B J (practitioners vary on
   treating Y).
3. Combine the remaining letter shapes into one glyph: overlap, rotate, mirror —
   until it no longer reads as letters.
4. Simplify and stylise; add a border if you like.
Variants: **mantric** sigils (scramble remaining letters into a nonsense chant) and
**pictorial** sigils (combine simple images).

## 3. Achieve gnosis and charge
"Gnosis" here means a state where the conscious mind's chatter stops and attention
is total.
- **Inhibitory gnosis:** deep stillness — long meditation, staring at the sigil until
  the eyes blur, quiet darkness, slow and steady breathing.
- **Excitatory gnosis:** high arousal — fast dancing, drumming, spinning, intense
  breathwork, climax.
At the peak, focus completely on the sigil (or chant the mantric sigil), then
release it — burn the paper safely, or put it away.

## 4. Banish with laughter and forget
Immediately break the state: laugh out loud, change activity, do something mundane.
Then **forget the sigil** — don't brood on whether it's working. A common practice is
to make several sigils at once, put them away for weeks, then charge them at random
so the conscious mind can't track which is which.

## 5. Paradigm adoption
Chaos magic treats belief systems as tools. Exercise: for a month, adopt a paradigm
fully (a pantheon, a superhero mythos, a planetary system), perform its rituals with
total sincerity, then shift to another. Record what each made possible. Don't
borrow a living religion's sacred practices casually — many practitioners choose
fictional or invented systems for this reason.

## 6. The results diary
Chaos magic's empirical spirit makes the diary central:
- Date, intent, sigil, method of gnosis, depth (1–5).
- Outcome when known, and timeframe.
- Review every 3 months: what technique correlates with your perceived results?
Be honest about misses and coincidence.

## Safety
- Excitatory methods: stay physically safe (clear space, hydration, no driving after
  spinning).
- Avoid gnosis via drugs, dangerous breath-holding or sleep deprivation; they carry
  real health risks.
- Paradigm-hopping and intense practice can be destabilising: keep ordinary routines,
  and pause and seek support if you notice anxiety, dissociation or loss of sleep.
- Burning sigils: in a metal bowl, outdoors or over a sink, with water at hand.

## Gotchas
- Lusting for result (Spare's phrase) — obsessing about the outcome is said to block
  the working; hence the forgetting.
- Intent too vague to evaluate.
- Charging while distracted — no gnosis, no launch.
- Treating "belief as a tool" as licence to appropriate closed traditions.
