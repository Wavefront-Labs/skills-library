# The inner Great Work and plant spagyrics

**Goal:** use the alchemical framework as a disciplined practice of
self-transformation, read its symbolic language, and carry out the classic beginner's
laboratory exercise (a plant spagyric) safely as an embodied meditation on the stages.

## 1. The language
- **Tria prima** (Paracelsus): **Sulphur** (soul, the essential/volatile oil, quality),
  **Mercury** (spirit, life, the volatile liquid), **Salt** (body, the fixed residue).
- **Solve et coagula** — dissolve and recombine; the core rhythm.
- **The stages of the Great Work** (*magnum opus*), in the common four-colour scheme:
  1. **Nigredo** (blackening) — breakdown, putrefaction, confronting what is dead.
  2. **Albedo** (whitening) — washing, purification, clarity.
  3. **Citrinitas** (yellowing) — dawning illumination (often dropped in later sources).
  4. **Rubedo** (reddening) — integration, the Stone, the union of opposites.
  Other texts list seven or twelve operations (calcination, dissolution, separation,
  conjunction, fermentation, distillation, coagulation). Note the scheme your source uses.

## 2. Inner practice
Many esoteric schools (and, in a psychological reading, C. G. Jung) treat the stages
as a map of inner work:
- **Calcination/nigredo:** a written inventory of habits, attachments and pride to be
  "burned" — honest, specific, private.
- **Dissolution/albedo:** practices that wash: confession or examination of
  conscience in your religious frame, daily meditation, simplification of life.
- **Conjunction:** reconciling opposites in yourself (reason/feeling, discipline/
  play) — journal dialogues between them.
- **Rubedo:** embodiment — living the change in action, service, creativity.
Keep a laboratory notebook for the inner work just as you would for the outer:
date, stage, practice, observations.

## 3. Contemplating emblems
Alchemical emblem series (*Rosarium Philosophorum* 1550, *Splendor Solis* c. 1530s,
Michael Maier's *Atalanta Fugiens* 1617) teach through images. Method: one plate per
week; describe every element literally; identify standard symbols (the green lion,
the king and queen, the ouroboros, the rebis); ask which stage it shows and what it
asks of you.

## 4. A plant spagyric (ritual exercise)
A classic first "outer" work that mirrors separation, purification and recombination.
Use a common, non-toxic culinary herb such as rosemary or lemon balm.
1. **Separation (Mercury + Sulphur):** dry ~30 g of herb; cover with ~250 ml of a
   high-proof drinking spirit in a clean jar; seal; keep warm (not hot) and dark,
   shaking daily for 2–4 weeks. Strain: the liquid tincture carries the plant's
   "Mercury and Sulphur".
2. **Calcination (Salt):** burn the spent herb to ash **outdoors** in a fireproof
   metal or ceramic vessel (never indoors; the residue may still contain alcohol —
   let it dry fully first). Continue heating until the ash turns grey-white.
3. **Purification:** dissolve the ash in distilled water, filter, and evaporate to
   obtain white salt crystals.
4. **Conjunction:** add the purified salt back to the tincture. Seal and rest.
Many practitioners time steps to planetary days/hours of the plant's ruler.
**Safety:** alcohol is highly flammable — no open flames near the jar or during
straining; burn ash only outdoors with water at hand. This is a ritual/contemplative
exercise: **don't ingest** the product or give it to others as medicine; herbal and
alcohol interactions with health conditions and medications are real. Never attempt
historical metal or mercury work — toxic.

## Gotchas
- Reading alchemical texts literally as chemistry, or as purely psychological — the
  tradition held both together.
- Skipping the nigredo: transformation narratives start with dissolution.
- Indoor calcination and fumes.
- Mixing colour/stage schemes from different authors.
