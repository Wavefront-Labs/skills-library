# Running a faith-formation class

**Goal:** learners finish able to *believe, pray and practise* as the tradition
expects at their stage — not just pass a quiz — and are ready for the rite the
course leads to (confirmation, reception, bar/bat mitzvah, conversion, completion of
a Qur'an juz').

## 1. Start from the end
Write down what the learner must be able to do at the finish. Typical outcome areas:
1. **Know:** core beliefs (creed, articles of faith, the Shema and its meaning).
2. **Pray:** the prayers expected of an adult member, from memory where required.
3. **Practise:** how to take part in worship, observe fasts, keep festivals.
4. **Belong:** who to go to, how to serve in the community.
5. **Rite-specific:** e.g. chanting a Torah portion; reciting Al-Fatiha correctly
   with tajwid; knowing the baptismal promises.
Check your authority's published requirements (diocesan guidelines, the
denomination's catechism, the beit din's conversion expectations, the madrasa
syllabus).

## 2. Build the course
- **Length:** match to the rite and tradition: confirmation programmes often run one
  to two years; adult reception (OCIA/RCIA) roughly a liturgical year; conversion to
  Judaism often a year or more; madrasa is ongoing.
- **Sequence:** concrete practice before abstraction. Teach how to pray and take
  part in worship early; the doctrines then have a lived reference.
- Each session tied to the liturgical calendar where possible (teach about Lent in Lent).

## 3. A dependable session (60–90 min)
1. **Welcome and prayer** (5 min) — model the prayer you want them to learn.
2. **Review** last week's memory item (5–10 min), out loud, low pressure.
3. **Teach one thing** (15–20 min): text, story or doctrine, with a primary source in
   hand (scripture, catechism paragraph, Mishnah, hadith).
4. **Engage** (15–20 min): discussion questions, pair work, or for children, craft and
   role-play.
5. **Practise** (10–15 min): rehearse the gesture, chant, recitation or prayer.
6. **Send** (5 min): one thing to do at home this week; closing prayer.

## 4. Memorisation and recitation
- Small chunks, daily repetition; cards or recordings sent home.
- For recitation in a sacred language (Hebrew, Arabic, Sanskrit, Church Slavonic),
  pair learners with a fluent reader and record the teacher's model so they can
  practise pronunciation at home.
- Check each learner individually, not just as a choir — weak voices hide.

## 5. Mentors and families
Assign each learner a sponsor, godparent or mentor who attends some sessions and
worship with them. For children, send parents a short weekly note: what was taught,
the memory item, a question to ask at dinner.

## 6. Readiness
Before the rite, hold a personal conversation (not an exam) with each learner: do
they understand what they are committing to, and do they want it freely? Most
traditions treat free consent as essential. Defer gently if not ready.

## Safeguarding
Two adults present with minors; background checks per your body's policy; no private
messaging with children outside approved channels.

## Gotchas
- Teaching the course as a lecture series; formation needs practice and worship.
- Assuming families practise at home — many don't; build practice into class.
- Converts and adult learners carry questions and past wounds; leave room.
- Passing someone who can recite but has not chosen.

## Verify
- [ ] Written outcomes match your tradition's published requirements.
- [ ] Each session includes prayer, one teaching point, and practice.
- [ ] Each learner has been individually heard reciting the required texts.
- [ ] Each learner has a mentor/sponsor and family has been kept informed.
- [ ] A readiness conversation took place before the rite.
