# Officiating weddings, funerals and initiations

**Goal:** carry out the rite correctly by your tradition's books, satisfy any civil
law that rides on it (marriage registration, burial permits), and leave the family
feeling cared for at one of the most significant moments of their lives.

## The common workflow
1. **Intake meeting** — in person, unhurried. Gather names (with pronunciation),
   dates, family situation, faith background and wishes.
2. **Check eligibility** under your tradition: e.g. prior marriages and annulments,
   whether a non-member may be baptised or buried in the community's rite, conversion
   status for a Jewish wedding, wali (guardian) and witnesses for a nikah.
3. **Paperwork** — civil and religious (see below). Start it at the first meeting.
4. **Plan the rite** from the authorised text; note what is fixed and what may be chosen
   (readings, music, words of the couple, eulogists).
5. **Rehearse** where there is movement (weddings, baptisms with sponsors).
6. **Officiate.**
7. **Register and follow up** — sign and return documents; visit or call afterwards.

## Weddings
- **Legal authority:** in most jurisdictions only registered or authorised celebrants
  may solemnise a legally binding marriage. Confirm you are authorised *there* —
  many places require local registration of clergy from elsewhere.
- **Notice periods:** many countries require advance notice (e.g. a Notice of Intended
  Marriage lodged at least one month before in Australia; a licence obtained before the
  ceremony in US states, with expiry and sometimes waiting periods). Check your
  jurisdiction's current rules.
- **Religious documents:** ketubah signed by witnesses before the chuppah; nikah
  contract with mahr agreed and witnesses present; church marriage register entries.
- **Rehearsal (day before):** walk the procession, positions, ring exchange; tell the
  couple exactly what they will repeat; give the photographer the "no-flash" moments.
- **On the day:** arrive 45+ min early, confirm licence and rings are present; sign and
  witness straight after the ceremony before anyone leaves.

## Funerals
- Coordinate with the funeral director immediately: date, venue, burial or cremation,
  and your tradition's timing requirements (Jewish and Muslim burials are ideally very
  prompt, often within 24 hours where possible).
- Visit the family; listen for the person's story; ask who will speak and for how long.
- Plan: gathering, readings, prayers, eulogy/remembrance, commendation, committal.
- Eulogies: give each speaker a time limit (3–5 min) and ask for a printed copy in case
  they cannot continue — you or a relative can read it.
- At the graveside, stand where the family can see you; keep the committal brief in
  bad weather.
- Calendar follow-ups: one week, one month, first anniversary (and tradition-specific
  observances such as shloshim, the 40th-day memorials in several traditions, or
  Requiem Masses).

## Initiations (baptism, naming, confirmation and similar)
- Prepare parents/sponsors: what promises they make and what they mean.
- Confirm sponsor eligibility rules in your tradition.
- Rehearse position, water/oil handling and who holds the child.
- Record it in the register; issue a certificate.

## Gotchas
- Mispronouncing a name at a funeral or wedding is remembered for decades — write it
  phonetically on your notes.
- Unsigned or late-filed marriage paperwork can leave a couple not legally married.
- Family conflict surfaces at funerals; agree in advance who decides.
- Mixed congregations: briefly explain what is happening ("We now stand for…").

- [ ] Civil paperwork lodged in time; you are authorised in this jurisdiction.
- [ ] Order of service built from the authorised rite and shared with participants.

- [ ] Registers signed and returned; follow-ups calendared.
