# From vocational discernment to ordination

**Goal:** test a sense of call honestly with the community, complete the formation
the tradition requires, and come to ordination (or vows, or semikhah) ready and
freely consenting. Written for candidates and for the clergy who accompany them.

Every tradition runs its own process and its published handbook is authoritative.
What follows is the shared shape; durations are typical, not rules.

## 1. Discernment (6–24 months)
Practices that most traditions use:
- **Regular spiritual direction** with an experienced director (monthly is common).
- **A rule of prayer** you actually keep: daily office, salah on time, daily study —
  formation tests consistency more than intensity.
- **Serve now** in the community in a lay role (reader, catechist, visitor). A call
  usually shows up as fruitful service others recognise.
- **Journal** what draws you and what you resist; review with your director.
- **Ask the community.** Many traditions hold that a call is confirmed by others,
  not only felt inwardly. Ask your clergy and two or three mature members for honest
  feedback.
Questions to sit with: Am I running toward this or away from something? Would I be
content serving as a layperson? Do others see these gifts in me?

## 2. Formal application and candidacy
Typical steps (names vary — postulancy, aspirancy, candidacy, enquirer):
1. Letter or meeting with your local clergy; their sponsorship.
2. Application to the diocese, conference, seminary, religious order or yeshiva.
3. Background checks, medical and often psychological assessment.
4. A discernment panel or committee interview.
5. Acceptance into a formation programme.
Keep a folder with every letter, decision date and requirement — candidates often
spend years in process and records go missing when staff change.

## 3. Formation (often 3–7 years)
Components common across traditions:
- **Academic:** scripture, theology/law, history, languages (Greek, Hebrew, Latin,
  Classical Arabic), homiletics.
- **Spiritual:** a structured rule of life, retreats, direction, community prayer.
- **Pastoral:** field placements; Clinical Pastoral Education (CPE) units are required
  by many US denominations and chaplaincies.
- **Human:** maturity, boundaries, healthy relationships, financial responsibility.
- **Community:** monastic novitiate or seminary residence with regular evaluations.
Ask for written evaluations each year and act on them; surprises at the final stage
usually trace back to ignored feedback.

## 4. Preparing for the rite
- Pre-ordination retreat (often several days of silence).
- Rehearse the rite: positions, prostration where used, responses, vesting.
- Prepare vows/promises: read them slowly beforehand; know what you are promising
  (obedience, celibacy, stability, faithful teaching — as applicable).
- Invite those who formed you; arrange your first service/Mass/sermon.

## 5. After ordination
The first two to three years are formation too: seek a mentor, keep direction, and
continue your rule of prayer when the workload tempts you to drop it.

## Gotchas
- Treating discernment as a decision already made; real discernment allows "no".
- Neglecting finances: seminary debt shapes ministry options for years.
- Hiding struggles from formators until they become disqualifying.
- Losing personal prayer under academic pressure.

## Verify
- [ ] You have a spiritual director and a rule of life you keep.
- [ ] Your community (clergy and members) has given honest, documented feedback.
- [ ] Every candidacy requirement is listed with a status and date.
- [ ] Annual formation evaluations are on file and acted on.
- [ ] You can state, in your own words, each promise the rite asks of you.
