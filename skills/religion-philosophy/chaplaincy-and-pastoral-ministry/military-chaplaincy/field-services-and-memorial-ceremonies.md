# Field services and memorial ceremonies — worship and remembrance where the unit is

**Goal:** provide brief, portable worship and blessing in the field, and a dignified
memorial for the fallen, without breaching operational security, command authority or
the right of every service member to their own faith — or none.

## 1. The two duties
A military chaplain *performs* the rites of their own faith and *provides* for everyone
else (by arranging another chaplain, a lay leader, materials or time). Both are
obligations. Rules and terminology differ by nation and service — check your
chaplaincy's regulations.

## 2. Field worship (15–30 minutes)
1. **Clear it with the commander** and the operations cell: time, place, numbers.
   Gatherings are targets; follow force-protection guidance on dispersal and cover.
2. **Announce it as voluntary.** Never parade troops to worship; attendance must not
   affect anyone's standing.
3. **Kit that packs small:** field altar kit or stole, a pocket service book, printed
   cards (no unit names or locations on them), wafers and wine in sealed containers if
   your tradition celebrates in the field, a red-lens torch for night.
4. **Shape** (adapt to your tradition): gathering sentence → confession or quiet
   reflection → one short reading → a few minutes of address → prayers for the unit,
   families and the fallen → blessing or dismissal. Keep it under 30 minutes.
5. **Opsec:** no photographs, posts or prayer requests naming missions, locations or
   casualties before notification.

## 3. Rites before deployment or a mission
- **Unit blessing:** brief, inclusive wording at a command parade if the commander
  wants it, e.g. a prayer for "courage, judgement and safe return", closing with a
  plain "Amen". Offer faith-specific blessings separately to those who ask.
- **Individual:** confession or Communion for Christians who request it; Catholics may
  want a priest for confession. In imminent danger with no time for individual
  confession, Catholic priests may give general absolution under church rules — that is
  a priest's decision.
- Many service members also ask for religious medals, a pocket New Testament, a Qur'an
  or other texts; keep a small stock of several traditions.

## 4. Casualties and the dying in the field
- Your role is non-combatant: do not carry arms if your nation's rules forbid it.
- With the wounded, pray briefly and only once medics allow; do not obstruct treatment.
- Catholics: a priest anoints and gives Viaticum; if none is present, any chaplain can
  pray an act of contrition with them. Muslim casualties may wish to hear or say the
  shahada; Jewish casualties the Shema. Others: ask, or simply hold a hand.

## 5. Memorial ceremony for the fallen
1. **Confirm next of kin are notified** before any public ceremony names the dead.
2. **Plan with the commander and the deceased's friends.** Elements commonly include:
   - the **battlefield cross** or memorial display (inverted rifle, helmet, boots,
     identity tags) — form varies by nation;
   - a tribute by the commander and a friend;
   - scripture or a reading the deceased's faith would recognise, and a short address;
   - **final roll call** (the name called three times, unanswered), a bugle call such as
     Last Post or Taps, a moment of silence;
   - a prayer of commendation, and the opportunity to file past and salute.
3. **The ramp ceremony** (casket loaded onto aircraft) is short: words of committal,
   honour guard, salute. Keep words to one or two minutes.
4. **Respect the deceased's faith:** a Muslim service member's body is ideally prepared
   and buried quickly by co-religionists; a Jewish service member's community may want
   a rabbi and early burial — liaise with mortuary affairs and the family.

## Gotchas
- Inclusive public prayer is expected at command events; sectarian content belongs in
  voluntary worship.
- Troops often confide things they will not tell command; privileged communication
  rules apply, with exceptions — know your service's policy.
- Your own grief is real; arrange debrief with a senior chaplain after casualties.

## Verify
- [ ] Commander approved time and place; force-protection guidance followed.
- [ ] Worship announced as voluntary; nothing coercive in attendance.
- [ ] No operational details in printed material or prayers.
- [ ] Next of kin notified before any name was used publicly.
- [ ] Members of other faiths had access to their own provision or a referral.
