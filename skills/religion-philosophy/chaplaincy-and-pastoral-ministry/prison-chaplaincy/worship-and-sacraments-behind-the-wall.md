# Worship and sacraments behind the wall — ministry inside security rules

**Goal:** deliver regular worship, sacraments and faith-specific provision to people in
custody so that every registered faith is served fairly, and nothing you bring, say or
carry compromises security or trust.

## 1. Know the frame you work inside
- **Security first, always.** Read your establishment's chaplaincy and security
  instructions before your first session; national rules (and your faith body's) vary
  — check both. You will be trained on keys, radios, alarms and incident reporting.
- **Faith registration:** most systems record each person's religion and use it for
  access to services and diets. People may change registration through the formal
  process; you facilitate, not decide.
- **Multi-faith duty:** the chaplaincy team as a whole serves every faith and none.
  Keep the rota visible: Christian services, Friday Jumu'ah, Jewish, Sikh, Hindu,
  Buddhist, Pagan and other groups as numbers and visiting ministers allow.

## 2. Bringing items in
1. **Pre-clear everything** through security: books, rosaries, prayer beads, oil,
   candles, incense, recorded music. Many prisons ban open flame and glass — use LED
   candles and plastic containers.
2. **Sacramental wine** is alcohol: it usually must be logged in, kept in a locked
   chaplaincy store, measured, and consumed only by the celebrant or administered
   under the rules your prison approves (some allow intinction or non-alcoholic wine).
   Never leave any unattended.
3. **Hosts, Qur'ans, prayer mats, tefillin, kara or turbans** — confirm what people may
   hold in cell and what stays in the chapel or faith room.
4. Count items out and back in at the end of each session.

## 3. A corporate worship session
1. Collect your list of attendees from the wing or movements staff; only those on it
   attend. Officers will be present or nearby.
2. Start on time; movement windows are fixed.
3. **Shape (Christian example):** greeting → song or psalm → confession → reading →
   short address (5–10 min) → prayers, inviting names of family and victims → Lord's
   Prayer → Communion if a priest or minister is presiding → blessing.
4. Avoid passing objects hand to hand that staff have not seen; prayer requests on
   paper go through the agreed route, not into pockets.
5. Do not carry messages, letters or phone numbers in or out for anyone. This is the
   most common way chaplains are manipulated.

## 4. Individual ministry and sacraments
- **Confession** for Catholics is heard by a priest. The seal binds the priest
  absolutely within the church; be clear in advance with the person about the limits
  of confidentiality outside the sacrament (threats of harm, escape, risk to children
  must be reported per policy — know your rules).
- **Communion in cell or in segregation:** check with the officer in charge, use the
  hatch or an escorted visit as permitted, keep words short.
- **Statutory visits:** in many systems a chaplain must see each new arrival and
  people in segregation or on suicide-watch daily or within set times. Record each one.
- **Other faiths:** a Muslim may need the right Ramadan meal times, a prayer mat and
  qibla direction in cell; a Jewish person kosher food and candles for Shabbat (often
  battery); a Sikh the five articles of faith subject to security review. Arrange the
  visiting imam, rabbi, granthi or priest for rites only they can give.
- **Death in custody or bereavement:** break news with staff present per policy,
  offer prayer, and help arrange escorted or virtual attendance at a funeral.

## Gotchas
- Conditioning: kindness that bends a rule once becomes leverage. Report gifts and
  approaches; your colleagues are your protection.
- Never promise outcomes (parole, transfer); write letters only within policy.
- Volunteers you bring in must be vetted and briefed — you answer for them.

## Verify
- [ ] Every item brought in was cleared, logged and counted back out.
- [ ] Sacramental wine stored and accounted for per local rules.
- [ ] Each registered faith on the rota has a session or visiting minister.
- [ ] Statutory visits (reception, segregation, at-risk) recorded on time.
- [ ] No message, item or contact detail carried for anyone.
