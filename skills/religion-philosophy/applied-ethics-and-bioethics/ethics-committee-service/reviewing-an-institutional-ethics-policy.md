# Reviewing an institutional ethics policy — the committee workflow

**Goal:** take a policy (new, expiring, or triggered by a hard case) through a
committee and out the other side as a clear, lawful, usable document that frontline
staff can follow at 3 a.m., with a recorded rationale and a review date.

Committees typically do three jobs: **education**, **policy review/development**, and
**case consultation/review**. This skill covers policy; the bedside workflow is in
`religion-philosophy.applied-ethics-and-bioethics.clinical-ethics-consultation.running-a-bedside-ethics-consult`.
Not legal advice — legal/risk counsel must review any policy that touches law.

## 1. Scope the job (chair + one or two members, before the meeting)
Write a one-page brief:
- **Trigger:** scheduled review, new law/regulation, accreditation finding, a case that
  exposed a gap, staff complaints.
- **Policy question:** e.g. "When may a clinician decline to provide CPR the surrogate
  requests?" One question per policy where possible.
- **Owner:** which department signs it off (ethics usually advises; medical executive,
  nursing or board often approves — check your bylaws).
- **Deadline** and the committee meeting(s) it will need.

## 2. Map law, standards and stakeholders
| Input | What to collect |
|---|---|
| Law & regulation | Statutes, case law, regulator guidance (jurisdiction-dependent) |
| Accreditation | e.g. Joint Commission standards in the US (check current edition) |
| Professional guidance | Specialty-society statements on the topic |
| Peer policies | 3–5 comparable institutions' policies, to compare structure not copy text |
| Internal data | Cases, incident reports, consult logs from the last 1–3 years |
| Stakeholders | Who must follow it, who is affected (patients, families, nurses, interpreters, chaplaincy, legal, risk) |
Invite affected groups that are not on the committee to comment — especially nursing
and patient/family advisors.

## 3. Structured deliberation (one agenda item, 30–45 minutes)
1. Restate the policy question and constraints (what law already decides).
2. Walk one or two **concrete cases** through the current text: where does it fail?
3. Name the values in tension (autonomy, beneficence, non-maleficence, justice,
   professional integrity, staff conscience).
4. List options; for each record: ethical rationale, feasibility, legal risk, burden on staff.
5. Test against **accountability for reasonableness** (Daniels & Sabin): is the
   rationale public, relevant, revisable on appeal, and enforced?
6. Seek consensus; record dissent and its reasons in the minutes rather than forcing unanimity.

## 4. Red-line the draft
Check every policy for:
- **Purpose and scope** — who and which settings it covers.
- **Definitions** — terms like "surrogate", "capacity", "potentially inappropriate treatment".
- **Step-by-step procedure** with named roles, time frames and escalation points.
- **Dispute resolution** — second opinion, ethics consult, transfer attempt, appeal.
- **Documentation** — what goes in the chart and where.
- **Conscientious objection** route that does not abandon the patient.
- **Plain language**: can a new nurse follow it without the committee in the room?
- **References** and review date (commonly every 2–3 years; check institutional rule).

## 5. Approve, launch, monitor
Send to legal/risk review → approving body → publish. Pair launch with education
(a short case-based session or one-page summary). Track use: consult requests,
incidents, staff feedback. Put the next review date in the committee calendar.

## Gotchas
- Drafting by committee produces vague compromise text; one or two authors draft,
  the committee critiques.
- Peer policies are a guide, not a template — law differs across borders and states.
- A policy written in reaction to one case overfits it; test with at least two different cases.
- Members with institutional roles (risk, administration) have interests; declare them.
- Committee recommendations are usually advisory; be clear who actually decides.

## Verify
- [ ] One-sentence policy question and named approving body.
- [ ] Law, accreditation and professional guidance mapped and cited.
- [ ] Draft walked through at least two concrete cases.
- [ ] Minutes record rationale and any dissent.
- [ ] Legal/risk review completed; education plan and review date set.
