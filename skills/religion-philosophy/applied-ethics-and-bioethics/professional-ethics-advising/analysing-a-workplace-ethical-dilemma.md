# Analysing a workplace ethical dilemma — an advisor's working method

**Goal:** help a professional or manager move from "this feels wrong" to a reasoned,
documented decision they can defend to their profession, their organisation and the
people affected — and, just as often, help them act on what they already know is right.

Not legal advice. Where law, regulation or a licensing body governs the matter
(fraud, safety, discrimination, client confidentiality), route to legal/compliance
first; ethics analysis starts where the law runs out or is silent.

## 1. Sort the problem type (first 10 minutes)
| Type | Signal | Typical route |
|---|---|---|
| **Compliance** | A rule clearly applies; pressure to break it | Code/policy, compliance, speak-up channel |
| **Temptation** | Right is clear, self-interest pulls the other way | Coaching to act (Giving Voice to Values) |
| **True dilemma** | Two legitimate duties conflict (e.g. confidentiality vs preventing harm) | Full analysis below |
| **Uncertainty** | Values agreed, facts unclear | Get facts before ethics |
Most "dilemmas" are the first two. Treating a temptation as a dilemma invites rationalisation.

## 2. Establish facts and the governing rules
- What exactly happened or is proposed? Who knows what, and since when?
- What do the **organisation's code of conduct**, the **professional code** (e.g. bar,
  engineering, accounting, medical bodies) and contracts say? Quote the clause.
- What does law require? (Jurisdiction-dependent — check with counsel.)
- What is the **deadline** and what is irreversible?

## 3. Map stakeholders
For each: interest at stake, power, vulnerability, voice in the decision. Include
the absent ones — customers, the public, future employees, the person being reported.

## 4. Run the lenses
Use several; agreement across lenses gives confidence, disagreement shows where judgement is needed.
- **Consequences:** which option produces most benefit and least harm, for whom?
- **Duties and rights:** which option respects the rights of all and keeps promises/obligations?
- **Justice/fairness:** does it treat like cases alike, distribute burdens fairly?
- **Virtue/character:** what would a person of integrity in this role do?
- **Common good / relationships:** what sustains trust in the team, profession, market?
Quick heuristic checks: **publicity** (front-page test), **reversibility** (would I
accept it if I were on the receiving end?), **generalisation** (if everyone in my role did this?).

## 5. Generate options — at least three
Beat the false binary. Typical middle options: raise it with the manager first;
disclose partially; recuse; seek consent; delay to gather facts; escalate through
the formal channel; document and proceed with safeguards.

## 6. Recommend and plan the voice
For the chosen option: who says what to whom, when, with what evidence; the
likely pushback and the reply (Gentile's scripting approach); fallback if blocked
(escalation path, hotline, regulator — check whistleblower protections locally).

## 7. Document
One page: question, facts, rules cited, stakeholders, options considered, rationale,
decision, who decided, date. This protects the person and creates institutional memory.

## Gotchas
- The advisor is not the decision-maker; say so and keep the client's agency.
- Rationalisations to name out loud: "everyone does it", "it's not my job",
  "no one gets hurt", "it's legal so it's fine", "just this once".
- Confidentiality of the advice itself may be limited — tell the person up front
  what you would be obliged to report.
- Retaliation risk is real; check protections before advising someone to escalate.
- Codes often conflict (employer vs profession); professional duties to the public
  usually take precedence in the codes themselves — check the specific code.

## Verify
- [ ] Problem typed (compliance / temptation / dilemma / uncertainty).
- [ ] Relevant code clauses and legal constraints quoted or ruled out.
- [ ] Stakeholder map includes absent parties.
- [ ] At least three options compared across at least three lenses.
- [ ] Recommendation has an action script and a fallback.
- [ ] Decision record written and dated.
