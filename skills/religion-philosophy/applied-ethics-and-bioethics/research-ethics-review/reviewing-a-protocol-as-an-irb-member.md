# Reviewing a protocol as an IRB member — primary reviewer workflow

**Goal:** arrive at the meeting with a defensible recommendation (approve, approve with
modifications, defer, disapprove) and a short list of specific, fixable stipulations,
grounded in the regulatory approval criteria rather than personal taste.

Framed on the US Common Rule (45 CFR 46) and the Belmont principles — respect for
persons, beneficence, justice. Other jurisdictions (UK HRA/RECs, Australia's National
Statement, EU CTR) use similar logic with different rules; apply the ones your
committee operates under. Animal research goes to an IACUC/AWERB (3Rs: replace,
reduce, refine) and is not covered here.

## 1. Read in the right order (budget 1–3 hours)
1. Application summary and **study population**.
2. Protocol: aims, design, procedures, schedule — mark which procedures are
   **research-only** versus standard care (only research risks count).
3. Consent documents, recruitment materials, questionnaires.
4. Investigator's brochure / device info, data management plan, DSMB charter if any.

## 2. Confirm the review level
| Level | When (US) |
|---|---|
| Exempt | Fits a category in 46.104 (e.g. certain educational tests, surveys, secondary data) — often determined by IRB staff |
| Expedited | No more than **minimal risk** *and* fits an OHRP expedited category |
| Full board | Everything else, including greater-than-minimal-risk studies |
**Minimal risk** = harm/discomfort no greater than ordinarily encountered in daily life
or routine exams. FDA-regulated studies add 21 CFR 50/56 requirements.

## 3. Test the approval criteria (45 CFR 46.111)
Write yes/no/needs-change against each:
1. Risks are **minimised** (sound design; uses procedures already being done where possible).
2. Risks are **reasonable in relation to** anticipated benefits to subjects and the
   importance of the knowledge. A study too weak to answer its question has no
   benefit to offset risk — check sample size and design.
3. **Equitable selection** of subjects; no convenience-driven targeting of vulnerable groups.
4. Informed consent will be **sought** from each subject or legally authorised representative.
5. Consent will be appropriately **documented** (or waiver justified).
6. Adequate **data-safety monitoring** where appropriate.
7. Adequate **privacy and confidentiality** protections.
8. **Additional safeguards** for vulnerable subjects — Subparts B (pregnancy, fetuses,
   neonates), C (prisoners), D (children: assent plus permission; risk categories in 46.404–46.407).

## 4. Mark up the consent form
- Begins with a concise **key information** section (purpose, duration, procedures,
  main risks, benefits, alternatives — required under the 2018 rule).
- Reading level appropriate (many IRBs target roughly 6th–8th grade; check local guidance).
- Voluntary participation and right to withdraw without penalty stated plainly.
- Research procedures clearly separated from usual care; therapeutic misconception avoided.
- Compensation for injury, costs, payment (not coercive or undue in amount/schedule).
- Data and specimen future use, return of results, contacts for questions.
- Waiver or alteration requests justified against 46.116(f) criteria.

## 5. Write the review
Recommendation, then numbered stipulations, each tied to a criterion:
"Stipulation 3 (46.111(a)(7)): State where identifiable data are stored and who has access."
Separate **required** changes from **suggestions**. Flag scientific concerns only where
they affect risk-benefit.

## Gotchas
- Reviewing the science you would have done instead of the study proposed.
- Counting standard-care risks as research risks (or the reverse).
- Payment: large completion bonuses can pressure people to stay; prorate.
- Since the 2018 rule, many expedited studies no longer require continuing review —
  but FDA-regulated studies still do (check current rules and local SOPs).
- Declare conflicts of interest and leave the room for that item.
- Cross-border studies may need multiple committees; ICH-GCP and Helsinki are often
  cited by sponsors but local law governs.

## Verify
- [ ] Review level confirmed and justified.
- [ ] Every 46.111 criterion marked with evidence from the documents.
- [ ] Research-only procedures identified and their risks weighed.
- [ ] Consent form has key information first and covers voluntariness and withdrawal.
- [ ] Stipulations are numbered, specific, and cite the criterion.
- [ ] Conflict of interest declared or none.
