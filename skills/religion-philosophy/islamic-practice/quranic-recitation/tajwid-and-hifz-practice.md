# Tajwid and hifz — reciting and memorising the Quran

**Goal:** recite the Quran as worship with correct pronunciation (tajwid), and build a
memorisation routine (hifz) that does not collapse under forgotten earlier pages.

> Draft. Tajwid terminology here is for the Hafs 'an 'Asim reading, the most widely
> printed. Other readings (e.g. Warsh, common in North and West Africa) differ in
> elongations and some vowels. Recitation is traditionally learned from a teacher, by ear;
> this page supports that and does not replace it.

## 1. Setup
- **One mushaf, always the same print.** Visual memory of where each verse sits on the page
  matters. The 15-line "Madani" layout (604 pages, each juz = 20 pages) is the most
  common; South Asian 13/16-line prints differ. Do not switch mid-hifz.
- **Colour-coded tajwid mushaf** is useful while learning the rules.
- **Teacher (shaykh/ustadha)** to hear you recite: online or at a mosque. An
  *ijazah* is a teacher's certified chain of transmission. Ask for one for advanced study;
  it is not needed to start.
- **Reference reciters** for listening: choose one clear, measured reciter in your reading
  (murattal style, not elaborate mujawwad) and imitate them.
- Wudu is required to touch the mushaf in most schools; reciting from memory does not need it.

## 2. Core tajwid rules to drill first
1. **Makharij** — articulation points. Distinguish the hard letters: ح/ه, ع/ء, ق/ك, ص/س,
   ض/د, ط/ت, ظ/ذ. Most early errors are here.
2. **Noon sakinah / tanween** rules: *izhar* (clear, before throat letters), *idgham*
   (merge into ي ر م ل و ن), *iqlab* (becomes م before ب), *ikhfa* (hidden, nasal, before
   the remaining 15 letters).
3. **Ghunnah** — 2-count nasalisation on doubled ن and م.
4. **Madd** — elongation: natural madd 2 counts; connected madd (madd muttasil) 4–5;
   separated (munfasil) 2–5 by reading; necessary madd (lazim) 6.
5. **Qalqalah** — the slight "echo" on ق ط ب ج د when they carry sukun.
6. **Waqf** — stopping signs (م must stop, لا do not stop, ج either). Stopping mid-phrase
   can alter meaning; restart a phrase from a sensible point.

## 3. A hifz routine
Use three tracks every day:
- **Sabaq (new):** e.g. 5–10 lines. Listen to it ×5, read it looking ×10–20, then recite
  from memory until you have 3 error-free repetitions. Recite to your teacher next day.
- **Sabqi (recent):** the last 7–20 pages, every day.
- **Manzil (old):** rotate through everything already memorised, e.g. 1 juz per day
  once you have more than a few juz.
Best times are after Fajr for new material and recitation in prayer for retention:
reciting memorised sections in your sunnah or night prayers is the traditional way to
keep hifz alive.

## Gotchas
- **New pages crowding out revision** is the main cause of failed hifz. If old pages are
  shaky, pause sabaq and only revise.
- Similar verses (*mutashabihat*) get confused — keep a list and drill the differences.
- Learning tajwid rules on paper without being heard: your ear will not catch your own
  makharij errors. Get corrected.
- Speed over correctness: tarawih-style speed is for those already fluent.
- Children and adults memorise differently; adults usually need more sabqi time.

## Verify
- [ ] A teacher has heard you recite al-Fatiha and confirmed makharij and madd counts
      (al-Fatiha is required in every prayer, so it comes first).
- [ ] You can name the noon-sakinah rule at each occurrence on a random page.
- [ ] You can recite yesterday's sabaq without looking, with no errors, before starting new.
- [ ] Your manzil rotation reaches every memorised juz within its cycle.
