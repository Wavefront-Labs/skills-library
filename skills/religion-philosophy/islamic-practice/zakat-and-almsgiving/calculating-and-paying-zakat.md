# Calculating and paying zakat

**Goal:** pay the obligatory annual alms correctly — on the right assets, at the right
rate, on time, to eligible recipients — as an act of worship, not just a donation.

> Draft. Rulings on pensions, shares, debts and jewellery differ between schools and
> contemporary scholars. Use a reputable zakat calculator (national zakat bodies and
> major Islamic charities publish them) and ask a scholar for complex portfolios or
> businesses. Shia practice also includes **khums** (one-fifth of annual surplus),
> not covered here.

## 1. Are you liable?
Zakat on wealth is due when an adult Muslim's net zakatable wealth stays at or above the
**nisab** for one full lunar year (**hawl**, ~354 days).
- **Gold nisab:** 85 g of gold (some use 87.48 g).
- **Silver nisab:** 595 g of silver (some use 612.36 g).
Silver gives a much lower threshold, so more people pay and the poor benefit more; many
charities default to it for cash. Check today's metal price when you calculate.

## 2. Fix your zakat date
Pick the Hijri date your wealth first reached nisab (or any fixed date if unknown, e.g.
1 Ramadan) and calculate on that date every year. Record it. If the date is tracked in the
Gregorian calendar, many scholars adjust the rate to ~2.577% to compensate for the longer year.

## 3. Add up zakatable assets (value on the zakat date)
- Cash in hand and in all bank accounts, including savings.
- Gold and silver (jewellery: Hanafi include it; other schools exempt regularly worn
  jewellery — follow your school).
- Money owed to you that you expect to receive.
- Investments: shares held for trading at full market value; long-term shares often by the
  zakatable portion of the company's assets (or ~25–30% of value as a common proxy).
- Business stock at sale value; business cash.
- Pensions and retirement funds: views differ (accessible portion only, or on withdrawal).
**Not zakatable:** your home, car, furniture, personal clothing, tools of your trade.

## 4. Subtract and calculate
1. Subtract debts **due now** (bills, the next instalment of a long loan — most scholars do
   not subtract the whole mortgage).
2. If the remainder ≥ nisab: **zakat = 2.5% × net zakatable wealth.**
   Example: £12,000 net, nisab (silver) currently below that → £300 due.
Agricultural produce (5% or 10%) and livestock follow separate tables.

## 5. Who may receive it
Quran 9:60 names eight categories (**asnaf**): the poor, the needy, zakat administrators,
those whose hearts are to be reconciled, freeing captives, debtors, in the cause of God,
and stranded travellers. Zakat may not go to your parents, grandparents, children,
grandchildren or spouse (you already owe them support), nor in most views to non-eligible
causes like mosque construction — use sadaqah for those.
- Give with intention that it is zakat; the recipient need not be told.
- When giving through a charity, choose a **zakat-designated fund** with a stated policy.
- Pay promptly on the due date; paying early in instalments through the year is allowed.

## 6. Zakat al-fitr and sadaqah
- **Zakat al-fitr (fitrana):** due per household member at the end of Ramadan, before the
  Eid prayer: one *sa'* (~2.5–3 kg) of a staple food, or its local cash value as set by your
  mosque.
- **Sadaqah:** voluntary charity, any amount, any time, any good cause.

## Gotchas
- Forgetting the hawl date and drifting — write it in your calendar with a reminder.
- Using the gold nisab by default when you hold mostly cash: decide your nisab basis
  consciously.
- Counting money already earmarked for a debt due today twice.
- Missed past years remain owed: estimate and pay them.

## Verify
- [ ] Zakat date recorded; calculation done on it.
- [ ] Nisab basis (gold/silver) chosen and priced today.
- [ ] All accounts, gold/silver, receivables and investments listed; exempt items excluded.
- [ ] 2.5% paid to an eligible recipient or a zakat-designated fund, with intention.
- [ ] Zakat al-fitr paid before the Eid prayer.
