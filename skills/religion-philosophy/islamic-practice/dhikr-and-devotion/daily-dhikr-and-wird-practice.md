# Daily dhikr and wird

**Goal:** establish a steady practice of remembrance of God (dhikr) — short formulas
recited with attention at fixed times — and know how to take part in a communal or
Sufi gathering.

> Draft. The formulas below are widely shared across Sunni practice. Sufi orders
> (tariqas such as Qadiri, Shadhili, Naqshbandi, Tijani) give their own **wird** (fixed
> daily litany) through a shaykh; Shia devotion has its own litanies (e.g. the tasbih of
> Fatimah is shared, while other duas are particular). Follow your own teacher's
> wording and counts where they differ.

## 1. After each obligatory prayer (about 2 minutes)
1. *Astaghfirullah* ×3.
2. *Allahumma antas-salam wa minkas-salam, tabarakta ya dhal-jalali wal-ikram.*
3. **Tasbih:** *SubhanAllah* ×33, *Alhamdulillah* ×33, *Allahu akbar* ×33 (34 in some
   narrations), completing 100 with *La ilaha illallahu wahdahu la sharika lah...*
4. Ayat al-Kursi (Quran 2:255).
5. Personal **dua** with hands raised, in any language.

## 2. Morning and evening adhkar
Recited after Fajr until sunrise and after Asr until Maghrib (some say until night).
A standard collection such as *Hisn al-Muslim* ("Fortress of the Muslim") gives the texts
with sources; typical items: the last three surahs ×3, *Sayyid al-istighfar*, *Bismillahi
alladhi la yadurru...* ×3, *SubhanAllahi wa bihamdihi* ×100. Use a pocket book or an app
offline so you are not drawn into notifications.

## 3. Building a routine
- Start small and constant: post-prayer tasbih + morning/evening adhkar for 40 days before
  adding more. Consistency is valued above volume.
- Daily anchors: **istighfar** ×100, **salawat** on the Prophet ×100 (e.g.
  *Allahumma salli 'ala Muhammad*), **tahlil** (*La ilaha illallah*) ×100.
- **Counting:** on the fingers of the right hand (the sunnah method — the thumb touches
  each finger joint in turn; a common way is 15 joints per pass), or a **misbaha/subha**
  of 33 or 99 beads with a marker; a digital counter ring also works.
- Pronounce the words, at least moving the lips — silent "thinking" alone is not counted
  as spoken dhikr by most scholars.
- Attention: slow down when the mind wanders rather than racing to the count.

## 4. Communal and Sufi gatherings
- A **majlis/halaqa** may recite litanies together, sing **qasidas** (devotional poems,
  e.g. the Burda) and salawat. A **hadra** is a standing, rhythmic group dhikr; **sama'**
  is listening to devotional music/poetry (accepted in many orders, not all).
- **Taking a wird:** a seeker usually takes initiation (bay'ah) from a shaykh, who
  assigns the litany and its counts. Do not adopt another order's wird and its
  conditions without permission — orders treat this seriously.
- Etiquette: wudu, clean clothes, sit in the circle where shown, follow the leader's pace
  and volume, do not record without asking, men and women may sit separately.

## Gotchas
- Overloading early (thousands of repetitions) usually ends in dropping everything.
- Racing the count until words blur loses the point and, for Arabic formulas, the meaning.
- Unsourced "special" litanies with promised rewards circulate online; stick to an
  established collection or your teacher.
- Do not recite Quran or dhikr in the toilet; dhikr itself needs no wudu, but it is recommended.

## Verify
- [ ] Post-prayer tasbih done after all five prayers today.
- [ ] Morning and evening adhkar completed inside their windows.
- [ ] Counts tracked (fingers, beads or counter) without guessing.
- [ ] Any wird you recite was given by your teacher or is from an established collection.
