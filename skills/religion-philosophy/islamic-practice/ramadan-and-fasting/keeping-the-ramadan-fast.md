# Keeping the Ramadan fast

**Goal:** fast the month of Ramadan validly and sustainably, as an act of worship —
from dawn to sunset with intention — and keep its nights, its make-up rules and its
voluntary fasts.

> Draft. Rulings below are the broadly shared core; schools differ on details (e.g.
> what counts as eating forgetfully, injections, fidyah amounts). Ask a local imam or
> scholar for edge cases, and a doctor for medical ones.

## 1. When the month begins and ends
Ramadan is the 9th month of the lunar Hijri calendar, so it moves ~11 days earlier each
solar year. Communities start it either on **local moon sighting** (hilal), on a sighting
anywhere, or on an **astronomical calculation** — which is why neighbouring mosques can
begin a day apart. Decide whose announcement you follow before the month, and follow the
same authority for Eid al-Fitr at the end.

## 2. The daily fast
- **Intention (niyyah):** a heart intention to fast; many schools require it each night
  before dawn (Maliki: once for the month).
- **Suhoor:** a pre-dawn meal, strongly recommended even if small. Stop at the start of
  **Fajr** (true dawn). Timetables often show an earlier "imsak" time (~10 min before)
  as a safety margin.
- **The fast:** from Fajr to Maghrib, no food, drink (including water), smoking or
  sexual intercourse. Also guard the tongue and temper — the fast's spiritual aim.
- **Iftar:** break the fast promptly at sunset (Maghrib), traditionally with dates and
  water, with a dua, then pray Maghrib before the main meal.

**Does not break the fast:** eating or drinking genuinely by mistake (stop when you
remember), brushing teeth without swallowing, showering, eye drops (most views), blood
tests. **Breaks it:** deliberate eating/drinking, deliberate vomiting, menstrual bleeding
starting. Deliberate intercourse during the day requires **kaffarah** (expiation, classically
60 consecutive days of fasting) in addition to making up the day.

## 3. Who is exempt
- Children before puberty (many practise half-days to learn).
- The sick, travellers (by school-defined distance), pregnant or breastfeeding women where
  fasting risks harm, menstruating women: they **make up (qada)** missed days later, before
  the next Ramadan.
- The elderly or chronically ill who cannot fast: pay **fidyah**, feeding one poor person
  per day missed (mosques publish a local amount).
- Diabetics on insulin or sulfonylureas, kidney patients and others on timed medication:
  see a doctor *before* Ramadan; the risk is hypoglycaemia and dehydration. Islamic law
  does not require fasting that causes harm.

## 4. The nights
- **Tarawih:** voluntary night prayers after Isha, often reciting the whole Quran over the
  month in the mosque (8 or 20 rak'ahs by tradition).
- **Laylat al-Qadr:** sought in the odd nights of the last ten (21st, 23rd, 25th, 27th,
  29th). Extra prayer, Quran and dua; some do **i'tikaf**, secluding in the mosque for the
  last ten days.
- Before Eid prayer, pay **zakat al-fitr** for each household member.

## 5. Voluntary fasts
Six days of Shawwal, Mondays and Thursdays, the 13th–15th of each lunar month, the Day of
Arafah (for those not on hajj), and Ashura (10th Muharram, with the 9th or 11th). Fasting on
the two Eid days is forbidden.

## Gotchas
- Heavy, salty suhoor = thirst by noon. Favour water, slow carbs, fruit; cut caffeine
  gradually the week before to avoid withdrawal headaches.
- In high latitudes, summer fasts can exceed 18–20 hours; communities follow local times,
  nearest-moderate-country times, or Mecca times by different rulings — check yours.
- Keep a written record of missed days to make up.

## Verify
- [ ] You know which moon authority you follow for start and Eid.
- [ ] Intention made; suhoor finished before Fajr, not before imsak by guesswork.
- [ ] Missed days logged with reason (qada vs. fidyah).
- [ ] Zakat al-fitr paid before the Eid prayer.
