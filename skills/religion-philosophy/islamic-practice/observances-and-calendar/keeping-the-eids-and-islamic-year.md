# Keeping the Eids and the Islamic year

**Goal:** know when the Islamic observances fall, carry out the two Eids properly
(including the Eid al-Adha sacrifice), and keep halal observance in daily life.

> Draft. Observance of Mawlid, Ashura and some other dates differs between
> communities: Sunni and Shia practice of Ashura is very different, and some Sunni
> scholars do not celebrate Mawlid. The descriptions below report what communities do;
> follow your own.

## 1. The Hijri calendar
- 12 lunar months of 29 or 30 days; the year is ~354 days, so every date moves ~11 days
  earlier in the Gregorian year and cycles through the seasons over ~33 years.
- A month begins with the sighting of the new crescent (or by calculation, per your
  community). Put **provisional** dates in your calendar and confirm from your mosque the
  evening before.
- Islamic days begin at **sunset**: "the night of" a date is the night *before* its daytime.

| Month | Main observance |
|-------|-----------------|
| 1 Muharram | Islamic New Year; **Ashura** on the 10th |
| 3 Rabi' al-Awwal | **Mawlid** (Prophet's birthday), 12th (Sunni) / 17th (many Shia) |
| 7 Rajab | Isra' and Mi'raj (27th, widely) |
| 8 Sha'ban | mid-Sha'ban night (Shab-e-Barat, 15th) |
| 9 Ramadan | the fast; Laylat al-Qadr in the last ten nights |
| 10 Shawwal | **Eid al-Fitr** (1st) |
| 12 Dhul-Hijjah | hajj; Day of Arafah (9th); **Eid al-Adha** (10th–13th) |

## 2. Eid day, step by step
1. Ghusl, best clothes, perfume (men); eat dates before Eid al-Fitr prayer, but delay food
   until after the prayer on Eid al-Adha.
2. Pay **zakat al-fitr** before the Fitr prayer.
3. Recite the **takbirat** on the way: *Allahu akbar, Allahu akbar, la ilaha illallah...*
   (for Eid al-Adha, also after each prayer from Fajr on the 9th to Asr on the 13th).
4. **Eid prayer:** 2 rak'ahs in congregation in the morning (after sunrise), with extra
   takbirs (number differs by school: e.g. 3+3 Hanafi, 7+5 Shafi'i/Maliki/Hanbali), then the
   sermon. No adhan or iqamah. Arrive early — mosques and parks often run several sittings.
5. Greet (*Eid mubarak*), visit family, give gifts to children, take a different route home.

## 3. Qurbani (udhiyah) for Eid al-Adha
- Sacrificed after the Eid prayer, until sunset of the 12th or 13th by school.
- One sheep/goat per person or household; one-seventh share of a cow or camel.
- Animal must meet age and health minimums (e.g. sheep ~1 year, or 6 months if well-grown,
  per school). Customarily divided in thirds: household, relatives/friends, the poor.
- In most Western countries, book through a licensed abattoir or a charity that slaughters
  abroad; home slaughter is generally illegal. Those sacrificing avoid cutting hair and
  nails from 1 Dhul-Hijjah until it is done.

## 4. Ashura and Mawlid
- **Sunni:** fast on 10 Muharram plus the 9th or 11th.
- **Shia:** the first ten days of Muharram mourn the martyrdom of Husayn at Karbala —
  majlis gatherings with recitation, marthiya and latmiya, processions; Arba'een
  follows 40 days later.
- **Mawlid:** gatherings of salawat, recited biographies (sira) and poetry, and food.

## 5. Halal observance day to day
- Avoid pork, alcohol (including in cooking and many vanilla extracts), and meat not
  slaughtered according to Islamic rules (zabiha) — rulings on "people of the book" meat and
  on stunning vary; know your own position.
- Check **E-numbers and additives** (gelatine, carmine E120, some emulsifiers) and use a
  trusted halal certifier's logo rather than a generic "halal" sticker.

## Gotchas
- Booking Eid leave on a calculated date: request both possible days.
- Mixing up "night of" vs. day of an observance.
- Qurbani orders close days before Eid — book early.
