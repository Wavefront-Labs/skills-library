# Keeping Shabbat — candles to havdalah

**Goal:** prepare for and keep Shabbat so that the 25 hours from Friday sunset to
Saturday nightfall are restful and sacred, with the kitchen, lights and logistics
settled in advance.

> Draft. The rules below follow traditional (Orthodox) halacha in outline; Conservative,
> Reform and other movements observe Shabbat with different scope — e.g. many permit
> driving to synagogue or electricity use. Detailed melacha questions (cooking, carrying,
> electronics) depend on your rabbi's rulings.

## 1. Friday: prepare
- **Times:** look up local **candle-lighting** (typically 18 minutes before sunset; 40 in
  Jerusalem by custom) and **havdalah/Shabbat end** (nightfall, ~42–72 min after sunset by
  custom) from a zmanim calendar or your synagogue.
- **Cook everything** before candle lighting. Food to be served hot goes on a **blech**
  (metal sheet over a low flame/burner) or an electric **hot plate** with no controls, set up
  *before* Shabbat. A slow cooker (for cholent) is set and its knob covered.
- **Lights and appliances:** set **Shabbat timers** for lamps; put the oven into
  **Shabbat mode** (certified models); unscrew or tape the fridge light.
- Tear toilet paper, prepare tissues, set the table, put out two **challot**, wine/grape
  juice, the kiddush cup, candles and matches. Put phones and work items away
  (**muktzeh**, not handled on Shabbat).

## 2. Friday evening
1. **Candle lighting** (at least two candles; often by the woman of the house): light, draw
   hands in three times, cover the eyes, say the blessing (*...lehadlik ner shel Shabbat*),
   then uncover the eyes. Once lit, Shabbat has begun for her.
2. **Kabbalat Shabbat** and Maariv at synagogue or at home.
3. At the table: sing **Shalom Aleichem**; bless the children; **kiddush** over a full cup of
   wine (at least ~86–150 ml by differing opinions); wash hands with a cup (netilat yadayim),
   blessing, then silence until **hamotzi** over the two challot (lechem mishneh); salt and share.
4. Meal with **zemirot** (songs) and words of Torah; **Birkat HaMazon** after.

## 3. Saturday
Morning service (with Torah reading), then **kiddush** and the second meal; afternoon
rest, study, walks; Mincha; **seudah shlishit** (third meal) before sunset.

## 4. The 39 melachot in practice
The categories of creative work from building the Tabernacle. In everyday terms, avoid:
cooking and heating food; lighting or extinguishing fire (including, in traditional
practice, switching electrics); writing and erasing; tearing and cutting to size; sewing;
building; and **carrying** between private and public domains — unless the area has an
**eruv**. Check your community's eruv status every week (hotlines/websites post it).
Saving life (**pikuach nefesh**) overrides all Shabbat rules.

## 5. Havdalah (Saturday night)
After nightfall (three medium stars), with a full cup of wine, **besamim** (spices) and a
**multi-wick candle**: blessings over wine, spices, flame (hold fingernails to the light) and
the separation blessing; drink, and extinguish the candle in the remaining wine.

## Gotchas
- Lighting candles late: lighting after sunset is not permitted in traditional practice;
  set a Friday alarm an hour ahead.
- Forgetting to cover the slow-cooker knob or set the timers.
- Winter Fridays: Shabbat can start mid-afternoon — leave work early.
- Assuming the eruv is up: it is checked weekly and can be down.

## Verify
- [ ] Candle-lighting and end times written down for this week.
- [ ] All cooking done; hot food on blech/hot plate; timers set; fridge light off.
- [ ] Two challot, wine, cup, candles ready.
- [ ] Eruv status checked before carrying.
- [ ] Havdalah set (wine, spices, braided candle) for after nightfall.
