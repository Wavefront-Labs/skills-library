# Mezuzah, mikvah and ritual handwashing

**Goal:** sanctify the home and the body through three regular practices — the
mezuzah on the doorposts, immersion in a mikvah, and the ritual washing of hands —
done correctly in their details.

> Draft. Family purity (taharat hamishpacha) is a detailed area of halacha; this page
> is an outline only — couples learn it with a qualified teacher (kallah/chatan teacher)
> and ask a rabbi or yoetzet halacha their questions. Non-Orthodox movements vary widely
> in mikvah use.

## 1. Mezuzah
**Buy a kosher scroll, not just a case.** The *klaf* is handwritten on parchment by a
certified **sofer** (scribe) with the Shema and *V'haya im shamoa* (Deut. 6:4–9, 11:13–21).
Printed "scrolls" sold inside decorative cases are not kosher. Buy from a reputable seller
who has the scroll checked (by a second expert, often also by computer).
**Affixing:**
1. Which doors: every doorway to a room used for living (bedrooms, kitchen, living room,
   front door) — not bathrooms or very small closets. Doorways need two doorposts and a lintel.
2. Where: the **right-hand** doorpost as you enter the room, at the bottom of the
   **upper third** of the doorway height, in the outer handbreadth of the doorpost.
3. Angle: Ashkenazim slant it with the top toward the room; Sephardim fix it vertical.
4. Roll the klaf from the end toward the beginning so *Shema* is on the outside first;
   wrap in plastic for outdoor doors.
5. Say the blessing *...likbo'a mezuzah* once, then affix the first (most important) one,
   followed by the rest without interruption.
**Timing:** in an owned home, immediately; in a rented home outside Israel, within 30 days
(by custom, many do it at once). A **chanukat habayit** (housewarming with Torah words) is
customary. Have scrolls checked **twice every seven years** — ink cracks and fades.

## 2. Mikvah
A **mikvah** is a pool of naturally collected rainwater (or connected to it) built to strict
specifications. Uses: women after niddah (menstrual separation) under family purity laws,
conversion, before a wedding, and (custom) men before Shabbat or Yom Kippur; also
utensils (tevilat kelim) in a separate utensil mikvah.
**Preparation (women's mikvah, outline):**
1. Book with the mikvah; it opens after nightfall for monthly immersion.
2. Before immersing: bathe, shampoo and comb hair, remove nail polish, jewellery, contact
   lenses, make-up, plasters — nothing may intervene (**chatzitzah**) between body and water.
   Most mikva'ot provide a full preparation room and checklist.
3. A trained attendant (**balanit**) checks for chatzitzah and witnesses the immersion.
4. Immerse fully (all hair under water), with body relaxed, eyes and lips lightly closed;
   say the blessing; immerse again per custom.
Mikvah visits are private; staff keep discretion.

## 3. Netilat yadayim (ritual handwashing)
**On waking (negel vasser):** with a cup or basin beside the bed, pour alternately right,
left, three times each; say *Modeh ani* on waking and the handwashing blessing (usually with
the morning blessings).
**Before eating bread:**
1. Hands clean and dry, rings removed.
2. Fill a two-handled **washing cup** (at least ~86 ml, a revi'it).
3. Pour on the right hand twice (or three times by custom), covering to the wrist; then the left.
4. Rub hands, lift them, say *...al netilat yadayim*, dry thoroughly.
5. No talking until you have said **hamotzi** and eaten bread.
**Mayim achronim:** a little water on the fingertips after the meal, before Birkat HaMazon
(customs differ). **Asher yatzar** after using the toilet, with ordinary handwashing.

## Gotchas
- Fancy mezuzah case with a printed paper inside — ask to see the klaf's certification.
- Mezuzah placed on the left or too low.
- Chatzitzah forgotten (stray hair tie, nail polish) delaying immersion.
- Talking between handwashing and hamotzi.

## Verify
- [ ] Every required doorway has a checked, handwritten mezuzah on the right side, upper third.
- [ ] Check dates for mezuzot recorded.
- [ ] Mikvah preparation checklist completed; appointment booked after nightfall.
- [ ] Washing cup by the sink; washing before bread done right–left with blessing.
