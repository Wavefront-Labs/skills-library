# Chevruta, parashah and daf yomi

**Goal:** build a sustainable Torah-study practice — study as an act of worship
(talmud Torah), not just reading — using the weekly portion, a study partner and,
if you choose, a fixed daily cycle.

> Draft. Cycle dates below are approximate; check a current luach or the cycle
> organisers. Which texts are studied, and by whom, varies by community (e.g.
> women's Talmud study is mainstream in Modern Orthodox and non-Orthodox settings,
> less so in some Haredi ones).

## 1. Before you start
- Say **Birkat HaTorah** (the blessings over Torah study) each morning — they are in the
  siddur and cover the whole day's learning.
- Fix a **time** (*keva*) rather than "when I can": e.g. 20 minutes after Shacharit or before
  bed. Regularity is the classic ideal (*asei Toratcha keva*).

## 2. The weekly parashah
- The Torah is read in 54 portions over the year, restarting at Simchat Torah; your
  synagogue's luach names this week's.
- **Shnayim mikra v'echad targum:** the traditional practice of reading the portion twice
  in Hebrew and once in the Aramaic Targum (or with Rashi), finished before the Shabbat
  morning meal.
- Starter method: read an aliyah a day (7 per week) with **Rashi** in a bilingual Chumash
  (Koren, Artscroll Stone, JPS, Hertz). Note one question per day; bring one idea to the
  Shabbat table.

## 3. Chevruta (study in pairs)
- Two people read the text aloud, translate, and argue it out — the beit midrash method.
- **Roles:** alternate reading; one proposes an explanation, the other challenges; then
  check the commentary.
- Choose a partner slightly above or level with you; meet at the same time weekly
  (in person, by phone or video). Online matching programmes exist via many yeshivot and
  community organisations.
- Keep a shared notebook of open questions; ask a teacher those you cannot resolve.

## 4. Daily cycles
| Cycle | Pace | Commitment |
|------|------|------------|
| **Daf Yomi** | one two-sided folio of Babylonian Talmud a day; 2,711 pages | ~7½ years; the current (14th) cycle began Jan 2020 and ends around mid-2027 |
| **Amud Yomi** | one side a day | double the time, gentler |
| **Mishnah Yomit** | two mishnayot a day | ~6 years for the whole Mishnah |
| **Halacha Yomit** | short daily law (e.g. Mishnah Berurah or Shulchan Aruch) | practical |
| **Rambam Yomi** | Mishneh Torah, 1 or 3 chapters a day | 1 or 3 years |
Talmud for beginners: the **Steinsaltz/Koren** or **Artscroll Schottenstein** editions give
translation and explanation; **Sefaria** (free, web/app) has texts with linked commentaries;
daily shiurim are available as podcasts. A 30–60 minute class is typical for a daf.

## 5. Siyum
On completing a tractate, book of Mishnah or other unit, hold a **siyum**: learn the last
passage aloud, recite the *Hadran* and the special Kaddish, and share a festive meal.

## Gotchas
- Falling behind daf yomi and quitting: either switch to amud yomi or skip ahead with the
  cycle and fill in later — staying with the community's pace keeps motivation.
- Reading translation only: try at least the key terms in Hebrew/Aramaic.
- Studying alone with no one to ask — find a teacher or chevruta early.
