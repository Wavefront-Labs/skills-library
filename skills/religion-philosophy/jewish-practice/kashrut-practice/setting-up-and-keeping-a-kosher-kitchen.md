# Setting up and keeping a kosher kitchen

**Goal:** run a home kitchen that keeps kashrut reliably — food sourced kosher, meat
and dairy fully separated, and utensils made and kept kosher — with systems simple
enough that guests and children follow them too.

> Draft. Standards differ (e.g. waiting times, which certifications are accepted,
> dishwasher and glass rules, kitniyot at Passover). Make your kitchen with a local
> rabbi; kashering an existing kitchen especially should be done with one present the first time.

## 1. The three categories
- **Meat (fleishig/basari):** kosher-slaughtered (shechita) meat and poultry from permitted
  animals, salted/soaked to remove blood (bought already done), and anything cooked with them.
- **Dairy (milchig/chalavi):** milk and dairy products, and anything cooked with them.
- **Pareve:** neither — eggs, fish, fruit, vegetables, grains. Pareve goes with either, but
  fish is not cooked or eaten together with meat in traditional practice.
Non-kosher (**treif**): pork, shellfish, birds of prey, any mixture of meat and milk.

## 2. Sourcing: read the hechsher
Buy packaged goods with a reliable **hechsher** (certification symbol: e.g. OU, OK, Kof-K,
Star-K, KLBD, local batei din). Next to the symbol: **D** or "Dairy" = dairy; **DE** = made on
dairy equipment; **M** or "Meat" = meat; **Pareve** = neither; **P** = kosher for Passover (not
"pareve"). A plain **K** is usually just the manufacturer's claim. Meat and wine always need
supervision.

## 3. Laying out the kitchen
- Two sets of everything that touches hot or sharp food: pots, pans, utensils, crockery,
  cutlery, colanders, chopping boards, sponges, dish racks, tea towels.
- **Colour-code**: e.g. red = meat, blue = dairy, green/yellow = pareve; label drawers.
- Separate sinks if possible; otherwise a basin or rack per category so dishes never touch
  the sink itself.
- Ovens: commonly one oven used for meat and pareve with dairy baked covered, or a separate
  dairy oven/toaster oven — ask your rabbi. Dishwashers are generally designated to one category.
- Pareve set (a few pots and knives) avoids recategorising every vegetable dish.

## 4. Daily rules
- **Waiting after meat before dairy:** 6 hours (most Ashkenazi and Sephardi), 3 hours
  (some German/Dutch custom), 1 hour (Dutch custom); after dairy before meat, rinse the mouth
  and eat something neutral (longer after hard cheeses).
- A mistake (dairy spoon in a meat pot): stop, set both aside, and ask — the answer depends
  on temperature, proportions and timing.
- **Check produce for insects**: leafy greens, herbs, broccoli, strawberries — soak in
  soapy water, rinse, inspect over a light box; or buy certified pre-checked.
- **Eggs:** check for blood spots before using.
- **Bishul Yisrael / Pat Yisrael:** some standards require a Jew to take part in cooking
  or lighting the oven — follow yours.

## 5. Making utensils kosher
- **Tevilat kelim:** new metal and glass food utensils from a non-Jewish manufacturer are
  immersed in a mikveh (a utensil mikveh, often at the synagogue) with a blessing before use.
- **Kashering**, by how the item was used:
  **Hagalah** — immersion in boiling water (metal pots, cutlery; clean and unused for 24 hours
  first); **Libun** — direct fire/self-clean cycle (grills, oven racks);
  **Iruy** — pouring boiling water (counters, sinks). Ceramics, most plastics and
  non-stick generally cannot be kashered.

## 6. Passover turnover
Remove or sell **chametz**, deep-clean, kasher counters/sinks/ovens (or cover them), and
bring out dedicated Passover dishes. Search for chametz the night before, burn it the morning
before Pesach.

## Gotchas
- "DE" products eaten straight after a meat meal — they're treated as dairy by most.
- Wooden boards and sponges crossing categories.
- Guests "helping" with the wrong colour — label clearly.

## Verify
- [ ] Every pot, utensil and board is colour-coded or labelled.
- [ ] Pantry items carry an accepted hechsher; D/M/Pareve markings checked.
- [ ] New metal/glass utensils immersed before use.
- [ ] Your waiting time is known to all in the household.
