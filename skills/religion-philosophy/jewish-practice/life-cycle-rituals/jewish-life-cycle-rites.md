# Jewish life-cycle rites

**Goal:** plan and take part in the major Jewish life-cycle rites — birth, coming of
age, marriage, death and mourning — knowing the order of events and the decisions
that have to be made early.

> Draft. Orthodox, Conservative, Reform and other communities differ (e.g. egalitarian
> roles under the chuppah, bat mitzvah format, who counts for a minyan, interfaith
> weddings, cremation). Your rabbi and, for death, the community's chevra kadisha or
> Jewish funeral director are the first calls.

## 1. Birth
- **Brit milah** for a boy on the **8th day** (counting the birth day; Jewish days start at
  sunset, so a baby born after dark counts from the next day). Held even on Shabbat or Yom
  Kippur if it is the 8th day; postponed only for the baby's health — the mohel and doctor decide.
  Book a **mohel** right after birth. Roles: **kvatter** (brings the baby in), **sandak**
  (holds the baby). The Hebrew name is announced, then a festive meal.
- **Girls:** named in synagogue at a Torah reading soon after birth, or at a **simchat bat**
  ceremony.
- **Pidyon haben:** redemption of a firstborn son (from a mother's first pregnancy, not by
  C-section, neither parent from a kohen or levi family) on day 31, with five silver coins given to a kohen.

## 2. Bar and bat mitzvah
A boy becomes bar mitzvah at **13**, a girl bat mitzvah at **12** (13 in many non-Orthodox
communities), automatically. The celebration marks it:
- Start preparation ~12 months ahead: Torah trope (cantillation), the Torah portion
  and/or **haftarah** for the date, the blessings, a short **d'var Torah** (speech).
- Book the Shabbat date with the synagogue early; date is set by the Hebrew birthday.
- In Orthodox practice girls usually mark it with a speech, study project or celebration
  rather than reading Torah in the main service.
- Begin tefillin (boys) about a month before.

## 3. The wedding
1. **Ketubah** (marriage contract) chosen and checked by the rabbi; signed by two
   kosher witnesses before the ceremony.
2. **Bedeken:** groom veils the bride.
3. **Chuppah** (canopy). Under it: blessings over wine (**erusin**); the groom gives a plain
   ring (no stones) worth more than a perutah, placing it on the bride's right index finger
   and saying *Harei at mekudeshet li...* before witnesses; the ketubah is read; the **sheva
   brachot** (seven blessings) are recited; the groom **breaks a glass**.
4. **Yichud:** the couple spend a few minutes alone.
5. A week of **sheva brachot** meals follows in traditional practice.
Many couples fast on the wedding day until the ceremony. Arrange the civil licence
separately.

## 4. Death and mourning
1. Call the **chevra kadisha** / Jewish funeral home immediately; burial is as soon as
   possible, ideally within 24 hours, except on Shabbat/festivals.
2. **Tahara:** ritual washing and dressing in white **tachrichim** (shrouds) by the chevra
   kadisha; a plain wooden coffin. Traditional practice does not embalm, display the body, or
   cremate.
3. At the funeral, mourners (for parent, spouse, child, sibling) do **keriah** — tearing a
   garment or a ribbon. Mourners and attendees help fill the grave with earth.
4. **Shiva:** 7 days at home — low seats, mirrors covered, a candle burning, prayer services
   with a minyan, visitors bring food. Not observed on Shabbat publicly; festivals cut it short.
5. **Sheloshim** (30 days) and, for a parent, 11 months of **Mourner's Kaddish** daily in a minyan.
6. **Yahrzeit** each year on the Hebrew date of death: candle, Kaddish; tombstone
   **unveiling** usually within the first year.

## Gotchas
- Brit date errors from ignoring sunset — calculate with the rabbi.
- Bar/bat mitzvah portion assigned for the wrong Shabbat because of a leap year.
- Hospitals and coroners can delay burial — tell them early that a Jewish burial is needed.
