# Undertaking temporary ordination and the alms round — living the vinaya for a season

**Goal:** go through a short-term ordination (common in Thailand, Myanmar, Laos and
Cambodia, often for one rains retreat) prepared, and keep the discipline and daily
alms round correctly enough that you support rather than burden the monastery.

## 1. Before you ask
1. **Find a monastery and a preceptor (upajjhaya)** willing to ordain you. Visit as a
   lay guest first, keeping the **eight precepts** (no food after noon, no
   entertainment or adornment, celibacy) for a few days or weeks.
2. Many Western forest monasteries require a period as an **anagarika** (white-robed
   postulant) of months before novice ordination; Thai village temples may ordain within days.
3. **Requirements for full ordination (upasampada):** male, at least 20 years old (counted
   from conception in some communities), parental consent, free of debt, not
   evading the law or military duty, and free of the disqualifying conditions asked in
   the ceremony. Novices (*samanera*) can be younger. Full bhikkhuni ordination
   exists in some Theravada lineages and is contested in others — ask the community.
4. In Thailand the candidate (*nak*) shaves head and eyebrows and is dressed in white;
   in Myanmar boys' novice ordination (*shinbyu*) is a family festival.

## 2. The ceremony, in outline
1. Bow three times to the preceptor; offer the robes (presented by family on a tray).
2. Request the going forth (*pabbajja*) in Pali, as taught by the monastery.
3. Receive the robes, change, take the **three refuges** and the **ten precepts** → you are a samanera.
4. For upasampada a quorum (at least five bhikkhus outside the "middle country") sits;
   two teachers (*acariya*) question you on the obstructions; the motion is recited
   three times and, with silence as assent, you are a bhikkhu. The time is noted to
   fix your seniority.
Exact Pali wording differs between the Thai Mahanikaya and Dhammayut orders and
between Sri Lankan and Burmese lineages — learn the text from your preceptor.

## 3. The discipline
- Samanera: ten precepts (the eight, with the seventh split in two, plus not accepting gold and silver, i.e. money).
- Bhikkhu: the **227 rules** of the Theravada patimokkha (East Asian Dharmaguptaka
  has 250 for monks; Tibetan Mulasarvastivada 253). Learn first the four *parajika*
  (sex, theft, killing a human, false claims to attainment), which end monkhood.
- No eating between noon and next dawn; food must be formally offered.
- Attend the fortnightly **patimokkha recitation** on uposatha days after confessing offences.
- **Vassa:** three months from the day after the Asalha full moon (around July);
  monks stay in one residence, overnight absences only under the allowed exceptions.

## 4. The alms round (pindapata)
1. At first light, robe both shoulders, bowl in its sling or held in front, walk in
   seniority order, barefoot where custom allows.
2. Walk slowly and silently, eyes down about a plough's length ahead. Don't ask for
   anything or show preference.
3. Stop when a donor waits; open the lid; the donor (shoes off, often kneeling) places
   food in the bowl. Close the lid; in Thailand a short blessing may be chanted.
4. Return, and food is offered again within the monastery before the meal.

## Gotchas
- **Women and monks:** in Thai and many Theravada communities a monk does not take an
  item directly from a woman's hand; she sets it on a cloth the monk holds out or on
  the ground/table before him. Monks avoid being alone with a woman.
- Lay helpers (*kappiya*) handle money; never pay a monk in cash hand to hand.
- **Disrobing** is a formal declaration before another person, not just changing
  clothes; family often marks it with a merit-making.
- Japanese priests in most schools marry and live by different rules — this skill
  is Theravada-centred.

## Verify
- [ ] Preceptor agreed and you have stayed as an eight-precept guest first.
- [ ] You know the four parajika and the noon rule.
- [ ] You can do the alms round silently, in order, receiving without asking.
- [ ] You know how women offer to monks in your community.
- [ ] You know how and before whom to disrobe formally.
