# Setting up a shrine and daily puja — a small, steady act of homage

**Goal:** establish a simple, respectful home shrine and a 10–20 minute daily
puja (homage, offering, refuge, chanting, dedication) that fits the tradition
you practise in, and that a visiting teacher or monastic would find in good order.

## 1. Place the shrine
1. Choose a clean spot **at or above head height when seated** — a high shelf or
   table, never on the floor, never in a bathroom or where feet will point at it from the bed.
2. Face the shrine so you are not pointing your feet at it when you sit; many
   households have it face east or the main door, but cleanliness and height matter more.
3. Put the **Buddha image (buddha-rupa)** highest and centre. Below or beside it:
   texts (Dharma) and, if you have one, a stupa or image of a teacher (Sangha).
   Nothing sits above the Buddha. Books on the shrine are never stacked under feet or bags.
4. In Japanese homes the shrine is a **butsudan** cabinet; Jodo Shinshu households
   centre Amida Buddha and keep ancestors' name tablets per their temple's guidance.

## 2. The offerings
- **Theravada (Thai, Sri Lankan, Burmese):** flowers (impermanence), a candle or
  oil lamp (wisdom), and three sticks of incense (Buddha, Dharma, Sangha). Water
  and, in the morning, a little food or rice may be offered and later removed.
- **Tibetan:** a row of **seven offering bowls** left to right — drinking water,
  washing water, flowers, incense, light, perfume, food — usually all filled with
  clean water, poured without gaps between bowls and emptied at dusk. Bowls are never
  set down empty: wipe, stack upside down, refill next morning.
- **Zen/Chan:** often one incense stick, a candle, a flower and a cup of water or tea.
Offer with **both hands** or the right hand supported by the left. Renew flowers
before they wilt; fresh is the point.

## 3. The order of the daily rite
1. Wash hands, remove shoes, settle the mind.
2. Light the candle, then the incense from the candle (don't blow out a flame with
   your breath — wave it or pinch it).
3. **Three prostrations.** Theravada five-point bow: kneel, palms joined at the
   chest, raise to the forehead, lower forearms and forehead to the floor, three
   times. Tibetan practice uses standing or full-length prostrations, often in
   counts toward 100,000 in ngondro — follow your lama.
4. **Homage** (Theravada, recited three times):
   *Namo tassa bhagavato arahato samma-sambuddhassa* — "Homage to the Blessed One,
   the Worthy One, the Perfectly Self-awakened One."
5. **Refuge** (each line three times):
   *Buddham saranam gacchami. Dhammam saranam gacchami. Sangham saranam gacchami.*
   Mahayana households may recite the Three Refuges in the vernacular or in Sino-Japanese;
   Tibetan practitioners often recite the refuge and bodhicitta verse ("Sang gye cho dang...").
6. **Precepts** (lay Theravada): the five precepts, e.g. *Panatipata veramani
   sikkhapadam samadiyami* and the four following.
7. **Chanting or sitting:** a short text (e.g. Metta Sutta, Heart Sutra, a mantra
   round on a mala) and/or a period of meditation.
8. **Dedication of merit:** close by sharing the merit with all beings, then three
   prostrations.

## Gotchas and etiquette
- Never point your feet at the shrine or the image; sit with legs tucked to the side.
- Don't use the shrine as a shelf for keys, phones or decorative clutter; don't place
  a Buddha image as garden decor or in a bathroom.
- Dispose of old offerings respectfully — food to people, birds or the garden, flowers
  to compost — not straight into the bin in front of the shrine.
- Incense ash and wax are fire risks: use a sand-filled bowl and never leave flames unattended.
- Wording, pronunciation and number of repetitions differ by temple and lineage —
  ask the resident teacher and copy their chanting book.

## Verify
- [ ] The Buddha image is highest, clean and not in a disrespectful place.
- [ ] Offerings are fresh, given with both hands and cleared before they spoil.
- [ ] You can do the sequence: light, three prostrations, homage, refuge, chant, dedicate.
- [ ] Refuge formula recited correctly three times each line for your tradition.
- [ ] Your feet never point toward the shrine during the practice.
