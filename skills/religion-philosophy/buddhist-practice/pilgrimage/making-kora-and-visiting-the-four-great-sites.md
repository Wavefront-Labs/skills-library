# Making kora and visiting the four great sites — pilgrimage as practice

**Goal:** go on pilgrimage as devotion rather than sightseeing: know what each site
commemorates, perform circumambulation, prostrations and offerings correctly, and
behave so that local practitioners are glad you came.

## 1. The four great sites
In the Mahaparinibbana Sutta the Buddha names four places a devoted person should
see with reverence:
1. **Lumbini** (southern Nepal) — the birth. Maya Devi Temple with the marker stone,
   the Ashokan pillar and the sacred pool.
2. **Bodh Gaya** (Bihar, India) — the awakening. The Mahabodhi Temple, the Bodhi Tree
   and the *Vajrasana* (diamond seat) beneath it.
3. **Sarnath** (near Varanasi) — the first teaching. The Dhamek Stupa in the Deer Park.
4. **Kushinagar** (Uttar Pradesh) — the final passing (parinirvana). The reclining
   Buddha in the Mahaparinirvana Temple and the Ramabhar cremation stupa.
Groups often add Rajgir (Vulture's Peak), Nalanda, Shravasti and Vaishali.
**Season:** October–March. Winter at Bodh Gaya brings large Tibetan prayer festivals
(e.g. Kagyu and Nyingma Monlam) — wonderful, but book lodging months ahead.

## 2. A simple practice for each site
1. Arrive early, before crowds; remove shoes where required (at the Mahabodhi complex
   footwear is left at the entrance; rules on phones and cameras change — check on arrival).
2. **Three prostrations** facing the main image or stupa.
3. **Circumambulate (kora / pradakshina) clockwise**, keeping the shrine on your
   **right** — three times at minimum; Tibetan pilgrims often do 108. (Bon practitioners
   go anticlockwise; Buddhists always clockwise.)
4. **Offer**: flowers, a butter or oil lamp where lamp houses are provided, incense,
   a khata (white scarf) in Tibetan custom, or robes and food to resident monks.
5. **Recite** something that fits the place: the refuges and Metta Sutta at Lumbini; the
   Heart Sutra or meditation under the Bodhi Tree; the Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta at
   Sarnath; the Buddha's last words, *vayadhamma sankhara, appamadena sampadetha*
   ("all conditioned things pass away; strive on with diligence"), at Kushinagar.
6. **Dedicate the merit** before you leave.

## 3. Kora in Tibetan and Himalayan places
- Around stupas such as **Boudhanath** and **Swayambhunath** (Kathmandu), walk the
  circuit clockwise and spin each prayer wheel **clockwise** with your right hand as
  you pass, reciting *Om mani padme hum*.
- **Prostration kora**: full-length prostration, stand, step to where your hands
  reached, repeat — some pilgrims complete whole circuits this way.
- **Mount Kailash** (Tibet): the outer kora is about 52 km over a pass near 5,600 m,
  usually walked in three days. Permits are needed through the Tibet travel system;
  altitude illness is a real risk — acclimatise and travel with an experienced operator.

## Gotchas and etiquette
- Never walk anticlockwise around a stupa or step over offerings and people prostrating.
- Don't climb on stupas or statues, or pose with your back to an image, touch its head
  or point your feet at it when you sit.
- At the Bodhi Tree, don't pull leaves from branches; fallen leaves may be picked up
  where guards allow.
- Dress modestly: shoulders and knees covered.
- Give to beggars and monasteries as you wish, but use official donation desks rather
  than pressure-selling "guides".
- Practices at mixed sites (Thai, Sri Lankan, Tibetan, Japanese temples side by side)
  differ — join in their form or sit quietly; ask the resident monks.

## Verify
- [ ] You know what each site commemorates and have a recitation ready for each.
- [ ] Every circuit was clockwise with the shrine on your right; prayer wheels turned clockwise.
- [ ] Shoes, dress and camera rules checked at each site.
- [ ] Offerings and prostrations made; merit dedicated at the end.
- [ ] For high-altitude kora: permits, acclimatisation and a guide arranged.
