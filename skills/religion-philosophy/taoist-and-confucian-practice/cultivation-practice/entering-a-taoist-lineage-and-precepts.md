# Entering a Taoist lineage and keeping precepts

**Goal:** understand how a person actually becomes a Taoist practitioner within the
religion — teacher, refuge, name, precepts — and where **neidan** (internal alchemy)
fits, so that cultivation is pursued as a religious path inside a lineage rather
than as a self-taught technique. Breath and movement methods themselves (qigong,
taiji) are out of scope here.

## 1. Two main paths
| | **Quanzhen** (Complete Perfection) | **Zhengyi** (Orthodox Unity) |
|---|---|---|
| Clergy | celibate, monastic, vegetarian, live in temples (guan) | married, householder priests, ritual specialists |
| Centre | Baiyun Guan (Beijing); Longmen branch is the largest | Longhushan (Jiangxi), Celestial Masters |
| Entry | refuge, then monastic ordination with precepts | training in a family/teacher lineage, ordination by conferral of a **lu** (register) |
| Cultivation | neidan and meditation central | ritual mastery central; personal cultivation alongside |
Lay followers of both can take refuge without becoming clergy.

## 2. Finding a teacher (shifu)
1. Start at an **active, recognised temple**: in mainland China, temples registered
   with the Chinese Taoist Association; elsewhere, temples with a verifiable lineage
   (ask: which lineage and generation? who ordained the teacher?).
2. Attend liturgy and lectures for months; ask the temple's guest master (知客) about
   lay study.
3. Expect a relationship of years, not a weekend course. A teacher who sells
   "initiation" online with no temple or lineage is not what the tradition means.

## 3. Guiyi (taking refuge)
- The lay entry rite: you take refuge in the **Three Treasures** of Taoism —
  **Dao, Scriptures (jing), and Teachers (shi)** — before the deities, with a
  teacher who becomes your refuge master.
- You typically receive a **Taoist name** whose first character comes from the
  lineage's **generation poem (派詩)**: e.g. the Longmen lineage poem of 100
  characters gives each generation its character, so your name shows your
  generation.
- You receive a refuge certificate; bring a donation and a red envelope by custom.

## 4. Precepts (jie)
- **Five basic precepts** for lay and novices (traditionally attributed to Laojun):
  no killing, no stealing, no sexual misconduct, no false speech, no intoxicants.
- Quanzhen clerical ordination: the **three-altar great precepts** (三壇大戒) —
  Initial Perfection (初真戒), Intermediate Ultimate (中極戒) and Celestial Immortal
  (天仙大戒) — conferred in large ordination assemblies held at intervals of years,
  after a period of temple life.
- Keep a simple daily **self-examination**: evening review of conduct against the
  precepts. Some lineages use merit/demerit ledgers (功過格).

## 5. Neidan as religious path
- Neidan treats the body as the furnace in which **jing (essence), qi and shen
  (spirit)** are refined, classically staged as: laying the foundation → refining
  essence into qi → refining qi into spirit → returning spirit to emptiness.
- In the tradition it rests on **precepts, scripture study and the teacher's oral
  instruction (口訣)**. Classical texts (e.g. *Wuzhen pian*, *Cantong qi*) are
  deliberately coded; reading them alone is not considered sufficient.
- Daily form for a lay student is usually modest: morning and evening recitation,
  quiet sitting as instructed, precepts, and service to the temple.

## Gotchas
- Don't self-ordain or claim a lineage name you were not given — lineage
  communities check.
- Commercial "Taoist master" certificates abroad often have no temple behind them;
  verify.
- Intensive internal practice without supervision is warned against by teachers
  themselves.
- Uncertain: precise rites of refuge and ordination differ by temple and are taught
  orally; outline only.

## Verify
- [ ] You can name your teacher's lineage, generation and temple.
- [ ] Refuge (guiyi) taken and name recorded with its generation character.
- [ ] The five precepts kept and reviewed daily.
- [ ] Neidan practice only as instructed by the teacher.
