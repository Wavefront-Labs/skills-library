# Keeping the lunar ritual year

**Goal:** know what the household and temple do at each major point of the Chinese
ritual year in Taoist and popular-religion practice, find the dates each year, and
prepare offerings in time. Regional custom (Taiwan, Fujian, Guangdong, Malaysia,
Singapore) varies; the shape below is common ground.

## 1. Getting dates
- Festival dates are on the **lunar calendar (農曆)**; Gregorian dates move each
  year. Use an almanac (**Tongshu/Nongmin li**), a lunar calendar app, or your
  temple's printed calendar.
- **Qingming** and the Winter Solstice are **solar terms** — fixed-ish Gregorian dates
  (4–5 April; 21–22 December).
- A leap month is added some years; deity birthdays are not repeated in a leap month
  (common practice; check your temple).

## 2. The core cycle
| Lunar date | Observance | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 12/23 or 12/24 | Sending off the **Kitchen God (Zaojun)** to report to Heaven | sweet offerings (malt candy, sticky cakes), burn his paper image or paper horse; clean the house |
| 12/30 (New Year's Eve) | reunion | offerings to ancestors and household gods; family dinner; stay up |
| 1/1 | New Year | temple visit at midnight or first morning; first incense (頭香) is prized |
| 1/4 | welcoming the gods back (incl. Kitchen God) | new image or incense at the stove |
| 1/9 | **Jade Emperor's birthday** (拜天公) | Hokkien/Taiwanese: midnight offering table to Heaven outdoors, sugar cane, three-tiered offerings |
| 1/15 | **Lantern Festival / Shangyuan** | Heaven Official's day; lanterns, tangyuan, temple rites |
| 3/23 | **Mazu's birthday** | processions and pilgrimages (Taiwan: Dajia Mazu pilgrimage) |
| 5/5 | Duanwu | protective talismans and herbs over doors; dragon boats |
| 7th month, peak 7/15 | **Ghost Festival / Zhongyuan / pudu** | offerings to wandering spirits outside, ancestors inside; temple **pudu** rites |
| 8/15 | Mid-Autumn | moon offerings; Earth God's birthday in some places |
| 9/9 | Double Ninth (Chongyang) | ancestral graves visited (Cantonese practice), honouring elders |
| 10/15 | **Xiayuan** | Water Official's day |
Plus each temple's **own deity's birthday (神誕)**: its biggest day, with opera,
processions and feasts.

## 3. Running the 7th-month offering (household/shop)
1. Set a table **outside** the door/shopfront on the chosen day (often 7/15, or a day
   in the month set by the community).
2. Offer cooked dishes, rice, fruit, snacks, drinks, incense; candles; a basin of
   water and towel for spirits to wash.
3. Burn spirit money and paper clothing for the spirits in a burner.
4. Leave offerings for the time the incense burns, then clear them.
5. Many avoid during this month: moving house, weddings, swimming at night,
   whistling at night, sitting in the front row at street opera (reserved for spirits).

## 4. Preparing for a deity birthday at your temple
- Volunteer slots fill a month ahead: offerings, opera stage, procession palanquin
  bearers, lion dance troupes, catering.
- Households bring **birthday buns (壽桃)**, noodles and fruit; the temple may
  read donor names in a petition.
- Processions require city permits and traffic management; firecrackers may be
  restricted.

## Gotchas
- Don't convert lunar dates by memory — they drift ~11 days a year with leap months.
- Burning restrictions: many cities ration or ban street burning in the 7th month;
  use community burners.
- The Kitchen God send-off timing differs: northern custom on 12/23, southern on 12/24.
- Uncertain: regional dates for the Earth God and some officials' days vary; check
  your temple's calendar.
