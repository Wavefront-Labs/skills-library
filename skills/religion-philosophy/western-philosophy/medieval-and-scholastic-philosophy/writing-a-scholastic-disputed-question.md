# Writing a scholastic disputed question

**Goal:** reason through a contested question in the format of the medieval
university **disputatio**, as preserved in Aquinas's *Summa Theologiae* articles: a
method that forces you to state the strongest case against your view, cite
authority, give your own answer, and then dissolve each objection by distinction.
Useful for studying Aquinas and the scholastics, and as a discipline for any hard question.

## 1. The shape of an article
| Part | Latin | What goes there |
|---|---|---|
| Question | *Utrum…* ("Whether…") | a yes/no question |
| Objections | *Videtur quod…* ("It seems that…") | usually 2–5 arguments for the answer you will **reject** |
| On the contrary | *Sed contra* | one authority or brief argument for the other side |
| I answer that | *Respondeo dicendum quod…* | your determination, with the distinctions it needs |
| Replies | *Ad primum… ad secundum…* | an answer to each objection in turn |

Read a model first: *ST* I q.2 a.3 ("Whether God exists") — two objections, a
scriptural sed contra, the five ways in the respondeo, and two replies.

## 2. Step by step
1. **Frame the question** as *Whether X is Y*. It must be answerable yes/no and
   genuinely disputed. Bad: "About free will". Good: "Whether the will is moved of
   necessity by the good".
2. **Write the objections first**, in their strongest form — each a short syllogism
   with a premise you will later address. Use real sources (Aristotle, scripture,
   Augustine, a modern author) as the scholastics did.
3. **Sed contra:** one authoritative statement on the other side. In Aquinas it is
   usually a quotation, not his full argument; it signals the direction of the
   answer, not the proof.
4. **Respondeo:** the body.
   - Begin by **defining terms and drawing the distinction** the problem turns on
     (*distinguendum est*): e.g. necessity of coercion vs necessity of nature;
     *per se* vs *per accidens*; in act vs in potency.
   - State your thesis and argue it; draw on principles your audience accepts
     (Aristotle's physics and metaphysics, for a medieval audience).
5. **Replies:** for each objection, identify exactly which premise fails, or grant
   it in one sense and deny it in another ("*concedo… nego…*" / "this is true of X
   but not of Y"). Never ignore an objection; a reply "*patet ex dictis*" ("this is
   clear from what was said") is allowed only if the respondeo really did answer it.
6. **Check** that every distinction in the replies was prepared in the respondeo.

## 3. Key working tools
- **Distinction**: the main scholastic move — find the ambiguous term and split it.
- **Analogy of being**: terms said of God and creatures neither univocally nor
  equivocally, but analogically (Aquinas, *ST* I q.13 a.5).
- **Authority and reason**: authorities are interpreted ("*exponendum est*"), rarely
  rejected outright; reverent exposition is part of the method.
- **Faith and reason** (Aquinas): natural reason can prove some truths (preambles of
  faith, e.g. God's existence); others are known by revelation only (e.g. the
  Trinity), and reason answers objections against them without proving them.

## 4. Related genres
- ***Lectio***: line-by-line commentary on a set text (Aristotle, Peter Lombard's
  *Sentences*).
- ***Quaestiones disputatae***: fuller records of actual disputations (Aquinas's
  *De veritate*, *De malo*).
- ***Quodlibeta***: open sessions where any question could be put to the master.

## Gotchas
- Don't build straw-man objections; the scholastic standard was to state them better
  than their proponents.
- Objections are not always the master's opponents' actual words; often composed
  for the exercise.
- Know whose position you reconstruct: Aquinas, Scotus and Ockham differ sharply
  (e.g. on univocity of being, universals).
- Translations: the English Dominican Fathers translation of the *Summa* is free and
  widely used but dated in vocabulary.

## Verify
- [ ] Question phrased "Whether…" and answerable yes/no.
- [ ] Objections strong, each with an identifiable premise.
- [ ] Respondeo introduces the distinction(s) the replies rely on.
- [ ] Every objection receives its own reply.
