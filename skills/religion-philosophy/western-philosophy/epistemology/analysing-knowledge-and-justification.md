# Analysing knowledge and justification

**Goal:** work through the central problems of epistemology with the standard moves
on each, so you can place a view, construct the relevant case, and argue for or
against it. Uses the analysis–counterexample loop from
religion-philosophy.western-philosophy.analytic-philosophy.conceptual-analysis-and-thought-experiments.

## 1. Knowledge after Gettier
- **JTB:** S knows p iff p is true, S believes p, S is justified.
- **Gettier case recipe:** (1) S has a justified false belief q; (2) S validly infers
  p from q; (3) p happens to be true for an unrelated reason. Result: justified true
  belief, but luck — intuitively not knowledge. **Fake barn county** (Goldman/Ginet)
  gives a non-inferential version.
- **Main fixes** — and the case that troubles each:
| Fix | Condition | Pressure case |
|---|---|---|
| No false lemmas | not inferred from a falsehood | fake barns (no inference) |
| Sensitivity (Nozick) | if p were false, S wouldn't believe p | violates closure; fails for "I'm not a brain in a vat" |
| Safety (Sosa, Pritchard) | in nearby worlds where S believes p, p is true | disputes over "nearby" |
| Reliabilism (Goldman) | belief from a reliable process | generality problem; clairvoyant (BonJour) |
| Virtue epistemology | true *because of* competence | credit in testimony cases |
| Knowledge-first (Williamson) | knowledge is unanalysable | gives up the project |

## 2. The regress of justification
If belief A is justified by B, and B by C… either:
1. **Foundationalism:** stop at basic beliefs (perceptual seemings, self-evident
   truths) justified non-inferentially. *Challenge:* what justifies the basic ones?
2. **Coherentism:** justification is mutual support in a system. *Challenge:*
   isolation — a coherent fairy tale.
3. **Infinitism** (Klein): an infinite non-repeating chain. *Challenge:* finite minds.
Place a view by asking it: where does the chain end, and why is that point justified?

## 3. Internalism vs externalism
- **Internalism:** justifiers are accessible to the subject on reflection (evidence
  you can cite). Pressure: the **new evil demon** — your deceived twin seems equally
  justified, so reliability can't be what matters.
- **Externalism:** justification depends on facts (reliability) the subject needn't
  access. Pressure: **Norman the clairvoyant** — reliable but irrational-seeming.
State which side a theory is on before criticising it; many debates are people
talking past each other here.

## 4. Scepticism
**Closure-based argument (brain in a vat):**
1. I don't know I'm not a brain in a vat.
2. If I know I have hands, I know I'm not a brain in a vat (closure).
3. So I don't know I have hands.
Replies map onto premises:
- Deny 3 → **Moorean**: I know I have hands, so I know I'm not envatted (deny 1).
- Deny 2 → **Nozick/Dretske**: closure fails.
- **Contextualism** (DeRose, Lewis): "know" shifts standard by conversation; the
  sceptic raises the standard.
- **Externalist** replies: if I'm not envatted, my perceptual beliefs are reliable
  and so known.
Also know the **Agrippan trilemma** (regress, circularity, dogmatic stopping) — the
regress problem from section 2 in sceptical dress.

## 5. Sources of knowledge
Perception (direct realism vs representationalism), testimony (reductionism — needs
independent reasons to trust — vs anti-reductionism — default entitlement), memory,
a priori reasoning. For testimony, test a view on a child learning from a parent.

## Gotchas
- "Certainty" and "knowledge" are not the same; most epistemologists are fallibilists.
- Closure must be stated carefully (known entailment, competent deduction).
- Don't confuse the internalism/externalism debate in epistemology with the one in
  philosophy of mind (content externalism).
