# Practising Confucian self-cultivation

**Goal:** understand the core Confucian concepts well enough to read the classics
accurately, and practise the tradition the way its own thinkers framed it — as daily
self-cultivation (*xiushen* 修身) in relationships, not just as doctrine.

## 1. Core vocabulary
- **Ren 仁** — humaneness, the virtue of being fully human toward others.
- **Li 禮** — ritual propriety: ceremonies, etiquette and the right forms of conduct
  through which ren is expressed and trained.
- **Yi 義** — rightness, doing what the situation morally requires.
- **Zhi 智** — wisdom, discernment. **Xin 信** — trustworthiness.
- **Xiao 孝** — filial piety, care and respect for parents and ancestors.
- **Junzi 君子** — the exemplary person (contrasted with the *xiaoren*, petty person).
- **Zhengming 正名** — rectifying names: roles and words should match reality.
- **Tian 天** — Heaven; **ming 命** — what is decreed, beyond our control.

## 2. A reading path
1. **Analects (Lunyu)** — start here; short sayings. Read with a translation that
   includes commentary (e.g. Slingerland's, which excerpts traditional commentators).
2. **Mencius (Mengzi)** — human nature is good; the four sprouts (compassion, shame,
   deference, right-and-wrong) must be cultivated like plants.
3. **Great Learning (Daxue)** — the eight steps from investigating things to bringing
   peace to the world; self-cultivation is the root.
4. **Doctrine of the Mean (Zhongyong)** — equilibrium, sincerity (*cheng*).
5. **Xunzi** — the contrasting view that human nature must be shaped by ritual and
   teaching.
6. **Neo-Confucians:** Zhu Xi (systematiser; *li* 理 principle in all things,
   "investigation of things") and Wang Yangming (unity of knowledge and action,
   innate knowing *liangzhi*).
Zhu Xi's arrangement of the Four Books was the basis of imperial examinations for
centuries; reading them in his order is itself a traditional practice.

## 3. Practices
- **Daily triple self-examination** (Analects 1.4, Zengzi): each evening ask — Have I
  been loyal in what I did for others? Trustworthy with friends? Have I practised what
  I was taught? Write brief answers.
- **Practising li in ordinary acts:** greetings, how you receive guests, punctuality,
  mourning customs. The point is the inward attitude trained through outward form.
- **Attending to relationships:** the five relationships (ruler–subject,
  parent–child, husband–wife, elder–younger sibling, friend–friend) as the field of
  practice. Modern practitioners often reinterpret the hierarchical ones; note how
  your source does.
- **Recitation and memorisation** of passages, a traditional study method.
- **Quiet-sitting (jingzuo 靜坐)** — Neo-Confucian seated reflection, recommended by
  Zhu Xi as a complement to study, distinct from Buddhist meditation in aim.
- **Investigation of things:** pick one matter a day (a text, a task, a
  relationship) and examine it until its principle is clear.
- **Ancestor rites and honouring teachers** — in families that keep them.

## 4. Reading a passage well
1. Read the line in context; note who is speaking (Confucius, a disciple).
2. Identify key terms; check how your translation renders them.
3. Read one traditional commentary.
4. Ask what it would ask *of you* this week.

## Gotchas
- Reducing Confucianism to "obedience" — the texts repeatedly praise remonstrating
  with superiors and put yi above mere compliance.
- Treating translations of *ren* or *li* as settled; they are contested.
- Confusing *li* 禮 (ritual) with *li* 理 (principle) — different characters and tones.
- Reading Neo-Confucian metaphysics back into the Analects.

- [ ] You have read at least the Analects with commentary.
- [ ] A daily self-examination journal is kept for at least two weeks.
- [ ] You can distinguish Mencius' and Xunzi's views of human nature.
