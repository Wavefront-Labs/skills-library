# Naming, anand karaj and antam sanskar

**Goal:** arrange and take part in the three main Sikh life-cycle rites as set out in
the Sikh Rehat Maryada (SRM), knowing what is religious requirement and what is
Punjabi family custom.

## 1. Naam karan (naming)
1. After the birth, when mother and child are able, the family comes to the
   gurdwara (or holds a diwan at home with the Guru Granth Sahib).
2. Kirtan, then ardas giving thanks and asking for a name.
3. The granthi takes a **hukamnama**; the **first letter of the first word** of the
   shabad is announced. The family chooses a name starting with that letter.
4. **Singh** is added for a boy, **Kaur** for a girl.
5. Karah prashad is distributed. SRM also mentions giving the child amrit-touched
   water (from a kirpan-stirred bowl with Japji Sahib) by some sangats' custom.
**Dastar bandi** (first turban tying, often at puberty) is a later family/gurdwara
observance, not an SRM rite.

## 2. Anand karaj (marriage)
**Before the day**
- SRM: both partners must be Sikh. Many gurdwaras enforce this and will not host a
  mixed-faith anand karaj; check the host gurdwara's policy early.
- Book the gurdwara and granthi/ragis; agree start time — the ceremony is usually
  held in the **morning** (before noon by custom).
- Civil registration: in the UK/US/Canada you usually need a separate civil
  registration unless the gurdwara is registered for marriages. India: Anand
  Marriage Act registration where states have rules. Check locally.
- Dowry and astrology-based date selection are rejected by SRM.

**Order of service**
1. **Milni** — meeting of the two families (custom), then the diwan with kirtan.
2. Couple sits before the Guru Granth Sahib, bride on the groom's left (custom).
3. An **ardas** for the couple; the couple and parents stand.
4. Granthi takes a hukamnama.
5. The bride's father (or a relative) places one end of the **palla** (scarf worn by
   the groom) in the bride's hand.
6. **Lavan**: the granthi reads each of Guru Ram Das's four lavan (Suhi raag, ang
   773–774); the ragis then sing it while the couple walks **clockwise** around the
   Guru Granth Sahib, groom leading. Four readings, four circuits; the couple bows
   on returning each time.
7. **Anand Sahib** (six pauris), ardas, hukamnama, karah prashad.

## 3. Antam sanskar (funeral)
1. At death, family and sangat recite *Waheguru* and gurbani; loud wailing is
   discouraged by SRM — the emphasis is acceptance of hukam.
2. The body is washed and dressed by family, **five Ks left on** for an amritdhari.
3. **Cremation** is the norm (burial or other disposal permitted where cremation is
   impossible). At the funeral home/crematorium: Kirtan Sohila is recited and ardas
   is said before cremation.
4. Ashes are scattered in flowing water (many go to **Kiratpur Sahib**) or a local
   river/sea where permitted. **No headstones or memorials** to the deceased (SRM).
5. A **sehaj path** (or akhand path) begins at home or the gurdwara and ends with a
   **bhog** ceremony, typically within ~10 days, with kirtan, ardas and langar.

## Gotchas
- Wedding timing: lavan cannot start until the granthi is ready and the Guru Granth
  Sahib is in prakash; build slack before the planned start.
- The palla is held, not tied; don't let the photographer stop the circuits.
- Funerals: crematorium slots are short (30–60 min in the UK) — agree in advance
  how much kirtan fits; finish ardas before the committal.
- Uncertain: customs such as the chunni ceremony, the dohli departure, or annual
  barsi observances vary by family and region; they are not SRM requirements.

## Verify
- [ ] Naming: hukamnama taken, first letter recorded, Singh/Kaur added.
- [ ] Wedding: gurdwara policy checked, civil registration arranged, lavan
  order and clockwise circuits understood.
- [ ] Funeral: Kirtan Sohila and ardas planned, sehaj path booked, ashes destination legal.
