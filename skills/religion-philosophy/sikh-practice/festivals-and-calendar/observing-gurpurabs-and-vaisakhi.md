# Observing gurpurabs and Vaisakhi

**Goal:** know when the Sikh holy days fall, what the standard pattern of observance
is, and how a sangat runs the big public events (nishan sahib change, nagar kirtan).

## 1. Getting the dates right
- Most dates follow the **lunar Bikrami calendar** and move each year against the
  Gregorian calendar (e.g. Guru Nanak's prakash purab is on Kartik Puranmashi,
  usually in November).
- The **Nanakshahi calendar** (solar, adopted 2003) fixes dates; the SGPC reverted
  to a Bikrami-based calendar in 2010, while some diaspora bodies still use the
  original Nanakshahi dates. **Result: two gurdwaras in the same city may celebrate
  on different days.** Check your gurdwara's own calendar, or the SGPC jantri.
- Vaisakhi is solar: **13 or 14 April**.

## 2. Main observances
| Day | Commemorates | Typical observance |
|---|---|---|
| Vaisakhi (Khalsa Sajna Divas) | founding of the Khalsa, 1699 | nishan sahib change, amrit sanchar, nagar kirtan |
| Gurpurab of Guru Nanak | birth of Guru Nanak | akhand path, prabhat pheris, all-night diwan in some places |
| Gurpurab of Guru Gobind Singh | birth of Guru Gobind Singh | akhand path, kirtan, langar |
| Shaheedi purabs (Guru Arjan, Guru Tegh Bahadur, Sahibzade) | martyrdoms | kirtan, katha; *chhabeel* (cold sweet drinks) for Guru Arjan in summer heat |
| Bandi Chhor Divas | Guru Hargobind's release from Gwalior fort | coincides with Diwali; diwan, lights at gurdwara |
| Hola Mohalla | martial gathering at Anandpur Sahib | day after Holi; martial displays (gatka) |
| Gurgaddi Divas of Guru Granth Sahib | Guru Granth Sahib installed as Guru (1708) | diwan |

## 3. The standard gurpurab pattern
1. **Akhand path** begins ~48 hours before (see the nitnem skill for how it runs),
   timed so the **bhog** falls on the morning of the gurpurab.
2. **Diwan** with kirtan and katha on the life of the Guru; the special ardas.
3. **Langar** for all — often the biggest of the year; plan for 2–3× normal numbers.
4. **Prabhat pheris** (early-morning kirtan processions through the neighbourhood)
   in the days before, by local custom.

## 4. Nishan sahib change (Vaisakhi)
- The flagpole is lowered, the old **chola** (saffron/blue cloth) removed, the
  pole washed (traditionally with water and milk/yoghurt), dried and re-clothed, and
  the new nishan raised during ardas with jaikaras.
- Safety: tall poles need a pulley system or a cherry-picker hire; never let
  volunteers climb. Brief the team and keep the sangat clear of the drop zone.

## 5. Organising a nagar kirtan
1. **Permits:** apply to the local council/police for a road procession, typically
   8–12 weeks ahead (local rules vary). Agree route, timings and stewarding.
2. **Order of procession:** the Guru Granth Sahib on a decorated vehicle (palki),
   preceded by the **panj pyare** carrying nishans, sevadars sweeping the road
   ahead, kirtan jathas, gatka groups, then the sangat.
3. Rota: stewards in hi-vis, water, first aid, langar stalls along the route, waste
   collection.
4. Granthi and chaur sahib sevadar ride with the Guru Granth Sahib.

## Gotchas
- Calendar mismatch is the commonest error — always confirm the date the host
  gurdwara uses before publicising.
- Bandi Chhor Divas is observed on Diwali's date but its meaning is Sikh; avoid
  describing it simply as "Sikh Diwali" to the sangat.
- Fireworks policies are increasingly restricted — check venue rules.
- Uncertain: routes, permit lead times and procession order details are local; figures here are
  indicative only.

## Verify
- [ ] Date confirmed against your gurdwara's calendar (Bikrami or Nanakshahi).
- [ ] Akhand path start time calculated so bhog lands on the day.
- [ ] Langar scaled up for gurpurab numbers.
- [ ] For a nagar kirtan: permit granted, stewards briefed, palki order set.
