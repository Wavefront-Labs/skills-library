# Keeping daily nitnem

**Goal:** establish and sustain the daily recitation of gurbani (nitnem) set out in
the Sikh Rehat Maryada, with naam simran, and understand how longer readings of the
Guru Granth Sahib (sehaj path, akhand path) are kept.

## 1. The nitnem banis (SRM)
| When | Banis | Rough time at steady pace |
|---|---|---|
| Amrit vela (early morning, before dawn) | Japji Sahib, Jaap Sahib, Tav Prasad Savaiye (10 sudha savaiye), Chaupai Sahib, Anand Sahib | 40–60 min |
| Evening (sunset) | Rehras Sahib | 15–25 min |
| Night (before sleep) | Kirtan Sohila | 5 min |
Ardas follows the morning and evening banis. Some Sikh orders (e.g. Taksal,
Nihang) add banis or use longer versions of Chaupai or Rehras — follow the maryada
of the sangat you belong to; SRM is the widely accepted baseline.

## 2. Setting up a routine
1. **Get a gutka** (prayer book with the nitnem banis) in Gurmukhi, with
   transliteration if you need it. Many use an app (e.g. iGurbani, Nitnem apps);
   the content is the same.
2. **Bathe first** (ishnaan) at amrit vela, then sit in a clean place with head
   covered.
3. **Start small if new:** Japji Sahib daily plus Kirtan Sohila at night, then add
   the remaining banis one at a time over weeks. Consistency matters more than length.
4. **Read aloud or in a low voice** — recitation is meant to be heard by yourself,
   not just scanned.
5. Follow with **naam simran**: repeating *Waheguru* (the gurmantar), aloud or
   silently, often synchronised with breath, for a set time (start with 10 minutes).
6. Close with **ardas** and a hukamnama if you have access to one (many read the
   daily hukamnama from Harmandir Sahib online).

## 3. Handling a gutka
- Keep it wrapped in a cloth (rumal) and stored on a high, clean shelf; don't put it
  on the floor, a bed you sleep on, or take it into the bathroom.
- Wash hands before handling. Never write in it.
- A worn-out gutka is not thrown away: return it to a gurdwara, which arranges
  respectful disposal (customarily by cremation — *agan bhet*).

## 4. Sehaj path and akhand path
- **Sehaj path**: a complete reading of all 1430 angs of the Guru Granth Sahib at
  your own pace — days, weeks or months. Can be done at home if a saroop or (for
  personal reading) a set of pothis/screen is available. Track your ang number.
- **Akhand path**: a continuous, uninterrupted reading of all 1430 angs by a relay
  of readers, ~48 hours, usually for a family occasion. Readers change over without
  a break in the recitation; it is normally arranged through the gurdwara, with
  trained pathis, and ends with **bhog** (concluding readings, Anand Sahib, ardas,
  hukamnama, karah prashad).

## 5. Learning correct pronunciation
- Gurbani vowel marks change meaning; learn **santhiya** from a granthi or a
  recorded santhiya course, bani by bani.
- Listening to a clear recitation while following the text is the fastest way to
  correct errors.

## Gotchas
- Speed-reading to "finish" defeats the purpose; if short of time, read fewer banis
  attentively rather than all of them rushed — but keep them daily.
- Missed amrit vela? Read the morning banis when you can; most teachers advise not
  skipping the day entirely. (Practice advice, not SRM text.)
- Different gutkas may end Chaupai Sahib or Rehras at different points depending on
  the maryada they follow — notice which you have.
- Akhand path booking dates fill fast around gurpurabs; ask early.

## Verify
- [ ] You can list the five morning banis, Rehras and Kirtan Sohila and when each is read.
- [ ] Nitnem has been read on most days this week, followed by naam simran and ardas.
- [ ] Your gutka is stored and handled respectfully.
- [ ] You can explain the difference between sehaj and akhand path.
