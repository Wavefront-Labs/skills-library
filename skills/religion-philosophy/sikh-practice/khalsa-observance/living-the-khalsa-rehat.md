# Living the Khalsa rehat

**Goal:** understand and keep the discipline (rehat) of an initiated Sikh — from
preparing for amrit sanchar to daily observance of the five Ks and the prohibitions —
as set out in the Sikh Rehat Maryada (SRM). Sangats such as the Damdami Taksal or
Nihang orders keep stricter or slightly different maryadas; follow the panj pyare
who initiate you.

## 1. Preparing for amrit sanchar
- Any person, of any background, sex or caste, old enough to understand the
  commitment, may take amrit. No fee.
- Arrange through a gurdwara that holds amrit sanchar (often around Vaisakhi, the
  anniversary of the Khalsa's founding in 1699, and on other set dates).
- Before the day: begin keeping the five Ks and the nitnem (see
  religion-philosophy.sikh-practice.scripture-recitation.keeping-daily-nitnem),
  stop any prohibited conduct, and bathe and wash your hair the morning of the rite.
- Bring: clean clothes, all five Ks worn (some gurdwaras provide a kirpan/kara set),
  head covered with a turban/dastar or chunni (many sangats expect women to keep a
  keski or dastar; practice varies).

## 2. What happens at amrit sanchar (outline)
- Held in a closed room before the Guru Granth Sahib. Present: the **panj pyare**
  (five initiated Sikhs), a granthi, and those being initiated. Others leave.
- The panj pyare prepare **khande di pahul**: water and patashe (sugar) in an iron
  bowl, stirred with a khanda while reciting the five banis (Japji Sahib, Jaap Sahib,
  Tav Prasad Savaiye, Chaupai Sahib, Anand Sahib).
- Each initiate receives amrit in cupped hands to drink, sprinkled on the eyes and
  on the hair, answering "*Waheguru Ji Ka Khalsa, Waheguru Ji Ki Fateh*" each time.
- The panj pyare explain the rehat; men take the name **Singh**, women **Kaur** if
  not already. A hukamnama is taken and names may be given from it.
Do not record the rite.

## 3. The five Ks (panj kakar)
| K | What | Practical care |
|---|---|---|
| Kesh | uncut hair (all body hair) | wash regularly; tie and cover with dastar/keski |
| Kanga | small wooden comb | kept in the hair; comb twice daily |
| Kara | iron/steel bracelet | worn on the wrist at all times |
| Kachera | cotton undershorts with drawstring | change one leg at a time so you're never without |
| Kirpan | sword/blade in a gatra (sling) | worn always; never drawn casually |
**Legal note:** kirpan carriage rules differ by country, airport and school (UK,
Canada and others have specific exemptions; US states vary). Check before travel —
in-cabin flights generally refuse a kirpan above a few cm. Not legal advice.

## 4. The four kurehats (cardinal prohibitions, SRM)
1. Cutting or removing hair from the body.
2. Eating **kuttha** meat (ritually slaughtered; many Sikhs interpret this as all meat).
3. Sexual relations outside marriage.
4. Using **tobacco** (SRM adds all intoxicants to the code of conduct).
Breaking a kurehat means the person must re-take amrit after **tankhah**.

## 5. Breaches and tankhah
- Minor lapses or a kurehat: go to the **panj pyare** at a gurdwara, confess
  honestly; they assign a **tankhah** (penance: extra paath, seva, etc.).
- Complete it, then ardas is done; for kurehats amrit is taken again.

## Gotchas
- Don't stop wearing the kirpan for convenience; agree a work/school arrangement
  (smaller kirpan, worn under clothing) in writing ahead of time.
- Rehat details (e.g. whether eggs are allowed, the length of Chaupai Sahib)
  vary between maryadas — ask your panj pyare, not the internet.
- Uncertain: some gurdwaras require initiates to commit to particular nitnem
  additions; SRM's list is the minimum widely recognised.

## Verify
- [ ] All five Ks worn and maintained daily.
- [ ] Morning and evening nitnem kept.
- [ ] You can state the four kurehats and what to do on a breach.
- [ ] Kirpan legal position checked for your country, workplace and travel.
