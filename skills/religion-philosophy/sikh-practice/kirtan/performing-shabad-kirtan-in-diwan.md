# Performing shabad kirtan in the diwan

**Goal:** sing gurbani as worship in the sangat so that the words are heard clearly
and correctly, in a way the congregation can follow and join. Kirtan here is an act
of worship before the Guru Granth Sahib, not a concert.

## 1. Ground rules (Sikh Rehat Maryada)
- Only **gurbani** (the Guru Granth Sahib), or the compositions of **Bhai Gurdas**
  and **Bhai Nand Lal** as their explanation, may be sung in kirtan in the diwan.
- In the gurdwara, SRM says only Sikhs perform kirtan to the sangat. Anyone may
  sing along from the floor.
- **Do not alter gurbani** to fit a tune: no added words, no rearranging lines, no
  repeating fragments in a way that changes meaning. Tune serves text.
- No film-song or popular tunes for shabads — strongly discouraged by most
  sangats and raagi tradition (local enforcement varies).

## 2. Choosing the shabad
1. Every shabad in the Guru Granth Sahib sits under a **raag heading** (31 main
   raags, e.g. Sri, Asa, Gujri, Sorath, Bilaval, Ramkali, Basant). Use the raag
   where you can; some raags are traditional for a time of day or season
   (Asa in the early morning — *Asa di Var*; Basant in spring).
2. Match the occasion: anand karaj uses the Lavan; funerals use shabads of acceptance
   (vairag); gurpurabs use shabads of the Guru being remembered.
3. Find the **rahao** line ("pause") — it carries the central theme and is the
   refrain the sangat sings back. Mark it in your pothi or on screen before starting.
4. Prepare the **Gurmukhi text with correct pronunciation**: lagaan-matran (vowel
   marks) change meaning. If unsure, check a santhiya (pronunciation) recording or
   your granthi before you sing.

## 3. Structure of a single shabad
1. Optional brief **alaap** in the raag, or a short instrumental lead-in.
2. Sing the **rahao** line first, sangat repeats it.
3. Sing each **ank (verse)** in turn, returning to the rahao after each.
4. Keep the tempo steady enough to be heard; build energy, don't race.
5. End on the rahao. Many jathas then say **"Waheguru Ji Ka Khalsa, Waheguru Ji Ki
   Fateh"**.
Typical slot: 20–40 minutes for a jatha of 3, 3–5 shabads.

## 4. Roles in a jatha
| Role | Instrument | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Lead ragi | harmonium (or tanti saaz: dilruba, taus, rabab) | sets raag, tempo, leads lines |
| Second ragi | harmonium | doubles/answers, holds drone if needed |
| Tabla / jori player | tabla or jori | taal (e.g. teentaal 16 beats, dadra 6, keherva 8) |
Set the harmonium's scale (Sa) to the lead's comfortable range — commonly C or C#
for men, G–A for women — and agree it before the slot.

## 5. Microphones and the screen
- One mic per voice at mouth height, harmonium mic aimed at the reed bellows side,
  tabla mic between drums. Do a level check *before* the diwan, not during.
- If the gurdwara projects gurbani (SikhiToTheMax or similar software), the
  sevadar needs your shabad's **first line or page (ang) number** in advance so the
  screen follows you.

## Gotchas
- Kirtan sits behind the Guru Granth Sahib's dignity: face the Guru/sangat, sit on
  the stage only once seated with head covered.
- The sangat sings the rahao — if it is too high or too fast, they drop out. Lower Sa.
- Never stop mid-shabad for a technical problem if you can avoid it; continue
  unaccompanied.
- Uncertain: whether women perform kirtan *inside Harmandir Sahib* is a live and
  changing question; in local gurdwaras women's jathas are normal.

## Verify
- [ ] Every line sung is gurbani (or Bhai Gurdas / Bhai Nand Lal); no altered words.
- [ ] Rahao identified and used as refrain.
- [ ] Pronunciation checked against santhiya for any uncertain words.
- [ ] Sa agreed, mics checked, screen operator has ang numbers before you start.
- [ ] The sangat could join the refrain.
