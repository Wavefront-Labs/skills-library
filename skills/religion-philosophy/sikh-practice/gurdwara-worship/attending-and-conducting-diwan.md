# Attending and conducting a gurdwara diwan

**Goal:** take part correctly in the congregational service (diwan) in the darbar
hall of a gurdwara, whether as a first-time visitor or as a sevadar helping run it,
following the order set out in the Sikh Rehat Maryada (SRM). Local custom varies
between gurdwaras and between jathebandis; where a step is local practice it is
marked as such.

## 1. Entering (everyone, Sikh or not)
- **Cover your head** before entering the darbar hall: turban, patka, chunni or a
  scarf. Most gurdwaras keep a box of head scarves at the door.
- **Remove shoes** and leave them on the racks; wash hands (many also rinse feet).
- **No tobacco, alcohol or intoxicants** on the premises, ever.
- Walk up the centre toward the Guru Granth Sahib on the takht under the palki
  (canopy), **kneel and bow (matha tekna)**, touching the forehead to the floor. An
  offering (money in the golak, or food items for langar) is customary but optional.
  Non-Sikh visitors may bow or simply stand respectfully for a moment.
- **Sit cross-legged on the floor**, facing the Guru; never point your feet toward
  the Guru Granth Sahib or turn your back on it while near it. Men and women usually
  sit on separate sides (custom, not a scriptural rule). Gurdwaras with chairs for
  elderly or disabled sangat place them at the back or sides.
- Keep phones silent; ask before photographing.

## 2. Order of a typical diwan
1. **Prakash** (morning): the Guru Granth Sahib is carried on the head from the
   sukhasan room, placed on the manji sahib, the rumalas folded back and the
   scripture opened. Everyone stands while it is carried.
2. **Kirtan** of gurbani by ragis or the sangat (see
   religion-philosophy.sikh-practice.kirtan.performing-shabad-kirtan-in-diwan).
   Morning diwan typically includes **Asa di Var**.
3. **Katha** (exposition) or announcements — local practice.
4. **Anand Sahib** — SRM specifies the first five and the last pauri (six pauris)
   before ardas.
5. **Ardas**: the sangat stands, faces the Guru, hands joined. One person leads.
   It opens with the invocation from Guru Gobind Singh's *Var Sri Bhagauti Ji*,
   remembers the Gurus, the martyrs and the Khalsa, then the specific petition of
   the day, and closes asking for *Nanak naam chardi kala, tere bhane sarbat da
   bhala*. At "Bolo ji Waheguru" the sangat responds **"Waheguru"**; the ardasia
   says *"Jo bole so nihal"* and the sangat answers **"Sat Sri Akal"**.
6. **Hukamnama (vak)**: the granthi opens the Guru Granth Sahib at random and reads
   the shabad beginning at the top of the left-hand page (if the shabad started on the
   previous page, go back to its start), read through to its end. Sit to hear it.
   Many gurdwaras display or post the day's hukamnama.
7. **Karah prashad** is distributed: receive it seated, with **both hands cupped**,
   right over left. Accept it — refusing is discourteous; ask for a small amount if needed.
8. **Sukhasan** (evening): after Kirtan Sohila and ardas, the Guru Granth Sahib is
   closed, wrapped in rumalas and carried to the sukhasan room.

## 3. Sevadar duties around the Guru Granth Sahib
- Whoever sits behind the Guru (granthi or trained Sikh) has bathed, has the head
  covered, and waves the **chaur sahib** while the scripture is open.
- **Rumalas** are kept clean and changed; the takht never left with scripture open
  and unattended.
- Before opening or closing, ask the head granthi — the exact handling of the
  saroop (hand placement, how far rumalas are folded) is taught in person and
  varies; do not improvise.

## Gotchas
- Kneeling and leaving immediately is fine; walking out *during ardas or hukamnama*
  is not — wait for it to finish.
- Karah prashad contains ghee, wheat and sugar — mention allergies quietly to the
  sevadar rather than refusing in the moment.
- Mixed-language services: a Punjabi diwan may give an English hukamnama translation
  afterwards; ask a sevadar where it is posted.
- Uncertain: some gurdwaras ask visitors to leave the darbar before certain rites
  (e.g. amrit sanchar); this is not part of an ordinary diwan.

## Verify
- [ ] Head covered and shoes off before entering; nothing intoxicating carried in.
- [ ] You can name the order: prakash → kirtan → Anand Sahib → ardas → hukamnama → karah prashad.
- [ ] You stood for ardas and gave the "Waheguru" and "Sat Sri Akal" responses.
- [ ] You know how the hukamnama page and shabad are chosen.
- [ ] Prashad received seated with cupped hands.
