# Jain fasting: from ekasana to upvas and beyond

**Goal:** undertake Jain fasts as tapas — austerity aimed at shedding karma and
loosening attachment to the body — choosing a level you can keep, taking the vow
properly, and breaking it correctly.

> Draft. Names and rules follow common Svetambara usage; Digambara and other
> communities use some different terms and rules. Water rules in particular vary.
> Anyone with diabetes, heart or kidney disease, pregnancy, an eating disorder, or on
> regular medication should talk to a doctor before any fast beyond one day; Jain
> teaching itself holds that austerity should be within one's capacity.

## 1. The ladder of fasts (lightest to strictest)
| Fast | What it means (common Svetambara form) |
|------|----------------------------------------|
| **Navkarsi / porsi** | no food or water until 48 min after sunrise / a quarter of the day |
| **Biyasana** | two meals only, eaten seated in one sitting each, during daylight |
| **Ekasana** | one meal, in one sitting, during daylight |
| **Ayambil** | one meal of plain, bland food — no milk products, oil, ghee, sugar, spices, fruit or green vegetables (e.g. boiled grains, lentils, dry rotis) |
| **Upvas (tivihar)** | no food from sunrise to the next sunrise; only **boiled water**, drunk between sunrise and sunset |
| **Upvas (chauvihar)** | no food and no water for the whole period |
| **Chhath / attham** | 2 / 3 consecutive upvas |
| **Atthai** | 8 consecutive upvas — commonly undertaken during Paryushana |
| **Masakshaman** | a month of upvas — rare, under close guidance |

The **Ayambil Oli** (twice yearly, nine days) and **Varshitap** (alternate-day fasting over
about a year) are well-known extended observances.

## 2. Taking the vow
- Decide the fast the evening before and take the **pachchakhan** (vow formula) — ideally
  in front of a monk or nun, otherwise by reciting it yourself — specifying type and end time.
- The previous evening, finish eating before sunset (chauvihar the night before is usual).
- During the fast: spend time in samayika, temple visits, scripture and pratikramana,
  rather than just "not eating". Avoid heavy work and heat.

## 3. Boiled water (ukaalelu pani)
In tivihar fasts, only water that has been boiled (traditionally boiled for some minutes
and used within a set period) is drunk, and only during daylight. Boiling is understood to
reduce the living beings in the water. Prepare it in the morning and keep it covered.

## 4. Breaking the fast (parna)
- Break at the stated time — typically after sunrise plus 48 minutes on the day after.
- Start gently: boiled water, then something light (e.g. a thin dal water, moong, a little
  rice or a light sweet tea), and eat a normal meal later. After multi-day fasts,
  reintroduce food over a day or two.
- Breaking the fast of a person who completed a long tapas is often a family and community
  occasion (with gifts and a procession for long fasts).

## 5. Sallekhana / santhara
The rite of gradually giving up food at the end of life, when death is near and
unavoidable, undertaken voluntarily with the permission and guidance of a spiritual
teacher and in full awareness. It is understood within Jainism as the opposite of
suicide — a peaceful acceptance of death. It is not a fast for the living to practise.
Its legal status has been contested in Indian courts; anyone encountering it should follow
the guidance of the sangha and the law where they live.

## Gotchas
- Jumping straight to multi-day upvas without building up via ekasana and single upvas.
- Dehydration in chauvihar fasts in hot weather — choose tivihar if needed.
- Large meals at the parna cause stomach upset; keep it light.
- Taking unboiled water by habit during a tivihar fast.

## Verify
- [ ] Fast type chosen within your capacity; doctor consulted if you have a medical condition.
- [ ] Pachchakhan taken with defined start and end.
- [ ] Water rules for your fast type clear (boiled, daylight only, or none).
- [ ] Parna planned: time known, light first food prepared.
