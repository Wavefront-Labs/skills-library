# Jain diet and daily ahimsa

**Goal:** practise non-violence (ahimsa) toward all living beings in the concrete
places it shows up — food, water, kitchen, movement and livelihood — at the level of
observance you have taken on.

> Draft. Strictness varies widely between families and individuals; many Jains keep
> the core (no meat, fish, eggs) and add further restrictions gradually or on holy days
> (tithis such as the 8th and 14th of each lunar fortnight). Jain-labelled products
> and restaurant "Jain options" are not uniformly certified — ask.

## 1. The reasoning that drives the rules
Jain teaching classifies living beings by the number of senses they have, from
one-sensed beings (plants, water, earth, fire and air bodies) to five-sensed beings.
Harm is minimised by avoiding the most complex beings entirely and reducing harm to
simpler ones. That explains rules that otherwise look arbitrary.

## 2. The Jain diet
**Never:** meat, fish, eggs (and anything containing them: gelatine, rennet-set cheese,
animal fats, many sweets and capsules).
**Avoided (abhakshya):**
- **Honey** — collecting it harms bees.
- **Alcohol** and fermented foods kept overnight (micro-organisms multiply).
- **Root vegetables (kandmool / anantkay):** potatoes, onions, garlic, carrots, beets,
  radish, ginger root — uprooting kills the whole plant, and roots are held to contain
  innumerable beings. Dried ginger (sunth) and turmeric powder are often accepted.
- Figs and some many-seeded fruits; mushrooms and other fungi.
- **Leftovers** past their time limit; food cooked the night before.
Dairy is accepted in traditional practice; some Jains now choose vegan diets on the same ahimsa grounds.

## 3. Eating before sunset (ratri bhojan tyag)
Eat the last meal before sunset; after dark, more insects are drawn to food and cooking.
**Chauvihar** = nothing at all after sunset, including water; **tivihar** allows water.
Check sunset times daily in a Jain panchang or app.

## 4. Water and the kitchen
- Filter drinking water through a fine cloth; keep water covered.
- Check grains and flour for insects; sieve flour; do not keep flour too long.
- Cook in a clean kitchen; avoid letting food sit uncovered; dispose of waste so it does not
  rot where insects gather.
- Restaurant/outside food: ask for "Jain" (no onion, garlic, potato) and check for eggs,
  gelatine and animal rennet in packaged items.

## 5. Careful daily conduct
- Walk watching the ground; do not crush insects; brush them away gently.
- Do not kill pests — use deterrents and remove insects outdoors.
- Avoid unnecessary use of fire, water, electricity and plants (these are living bodies too).
- Livelihood: Jains traditionally avoid trades involving large-scale harm (weapons,
  pesticides, animal slaughter, leather).
- Non-violence of speech and thought counts too: harsh words, anger and harmful intent are harm.

## 6. The lay vows (anuvratas)
The five **minor vows** scale the monastic great vows down for householders:
1. **Ahimsa** — no intentional harm to mobile beings; minimise harm to others.
2. **Satya** — truthfulness.
3. **Asteya** — no stealing.
4. **Brahmacharya** — fidelity within marriage.
5. **Aparigraha** — limiting possessions to a stated amount.
Supplemented by three gunavratas and four shikshavratas (limits on travel and
consumption, samayika, fasting, hospitality to ascetics). Vows are often taken formally
before a monk or nun, with personally chosen limits.

## Gotchas
- "Vegetarian" in restaurants often includes eggs (cakes, mayonnaise) and onions/garlic.
- Hidden animal ingredients: E120 (carmine), E441 (gelatine), E904 (shellac), some E471.
- Pressing your own level of observance on guests or relatives — practice is personal.
