# Keeping Paryushana and the Jain year

**Goal:** know when the main Jain observances fall and how to keep them — above all
Paryushana/Das Lakshana, the annual season of fasting, scripture and forgiveness.

> Draft. Dates follow the lunar **panchang**, and Svetambara and Digambara communities
> (and sub-sects within them) can differ by a day or more. Get dates from your own
> temple or sangha's printed panchang or app each year.

## 1. Reading the calendar
- Months are lunar, divided into bright (shukla) and dark (krishna) fortnights; days are
  **tithis**. Jain era: **Vira Nirvana Samvat** (counted from Mahavira's nirvana; the
  Vikram Samvat is also widely used).
- Monthly holy days: the 8th and 14th tithis of each fortnight (and often the 5th), when
  many observe extra restrictions (green vegetables avoided, fasting, pratikramana).

## 2. Paryushana and Das Lakshana (Bhadrapada, ~Aug–Sep)
**Svetambara Paryushana — 8 days**
1. Daily: temple puja, pratikramana, fasting at your level (ekasana, upvas, atthai for
   the strong), and discourses.
2. Around day 5 the reading of Mahavira's birth from the **Kalpa Sutra** is celebrated
   (the 14 dreams of his mother Trishala are displayed and honoured).
3. Day 8 — **Samvatsari:** the annual pratikramana (about 3 hours), a fast for many, and
   the asking of forgiveness.
**Digambara Das Lakshana — 10 days**, usually starting as Paryushana ends: one day
devoted to each of the ten virtues — forgiveness, humility, straightforwardness,
contentment/purity, truth, self-restraint, austerity, renunciation, non-attachment,
celibacy — with puja and discourses on the *Tattvartha Sutra*.

**Kshamavani / Michchhami Dukkadam:** after Samvatsari (or at the end of Das Lakshana),
ask forgiveness of everyone you may have hurt in thought, word or deed in the past year —
in person, by letter or message: *"Michchhami Dukkadam"* — and forgive in return. Do it
specifically with the people you had real friction with, not just a group post.

## 3. Other major days
| Observance | When (approx.) | Practice |
|-----------|----------------|----------|
| **Mahavir Janma Kalyanak** (Mahavir Jayanti) | Chaitra shukla 13 (Mar–Apr) | processions with the image, abhisheka, readings, charity |
| **Akshaya Tritiya** | Vaishakha shukla 3 | breaking of the year-long varshitap fast (with sugarcane juice) |
| **Ayambil Oli** | twice yearly, 9 days (Chaitra, Ashvin) | ayambil meals, worship of the Navpad |
| **Diwali** | Kartika amavasya (Oct–Nov) | Mahavira's **nirvana**: fasting, the night spent in meditation and recitation, lamps as the light of knowledge; next day Jain new year and Gautam Swami's enlightenment |
| **Gyan Panchami** | Kartika shukla 5 | honouring scriptures and knowledge: cleaning and displaying sacred texts |
| **Kartik Purnima** | Kartika full moon | pilgrimage season opens (e.g. Shatrunjaya) after chaturmas |
Each tirthankara's five **kalyanakas** (conception, birth, renunciation, omniscience,
liberation) are also marked in temples.

## 4. Planning tips
- Paryushana falls during **chaturmas**, when monks and nuns are resident — check the
  upashraya timetable for discourses and group pratikramana.
- Arrange work leave for Samvatsari; the pratikramana runs into the evening.
- If fasting, tell family who cook, and plan the parna for the morning after.

## Gotchas
- Using a generic Hindu calendar: Jain dates (especially Paryushana) can differ.
- Treating Diwali at a Jain household as a firework party; the emphasis is quiet observance.
- Forgetting to reciprocate forgiveness promptly — the exchange matters.
