# Darshan and the eightfold puja in a Jain temple

**Goal:** worship the tirthankaras in a temple (derasar/basadi) correctly: arrive
ritually prepared, take darshan, and perform the eight offerings, understanding each
as an aid to one's own spiritual effort rather than a request to the Jina.

> Draft. This follows the common **Svetambara Murtipujak** pattern. **Digambara**
> temples use the eight substances (ashtadravya) too, but with a sung text, offerings
> placed on a plate, and different rules on who may touch the image. **Sthanakvasi** and
> **Terapanthi** Jains do not worship images at all. Every temple posts its own rules:
> follow them and ask the pujari when unsure.

## 1. Preparation
- Bathe; wear clean, unstitched or freshly washed puja clothes reserved for worship
  (men often a dhoti and upper cloth, women a sari or long dress). No leather — belts,
  wallets, watch straps stay outside.
- Do not eat beforehand where possible; no food or drink inside.
- Women during menstruation customarily do not perform puja (temple rules vary).
- Bring or buy the materials: water/milk, sandalwood paste, flowers, incense, a ghee lamp,
  unbroken rice (akshat), sweets, fruit.

## 2. Entering and darshan
1. Say **nissihi** three times as you enter — renouncing worldly matters for this time.
2. Bow and look at the image (murti) — **darshan**, seeing the Jina's calm, detached form.
3. Circumambulate three times clockwise (pradakshina), if space permits.
4. Recite the **Navkar (Namokar) Mantra**: *Namo Arihantanam, Namo Siddhanam, Namo
   Ayariyanam, Namo Uvajjhayanam, Namo Loe Savva Sahunam...*
5. Cover the mouth with a cloth (mukhkosh) folded eight times before touching the image, so
   breath does not reach it.

## 3. The eight offerings, in order
The first three are offered on the image (anga puja), the rest in front of it (agra puja).
1. **Jal puja** — abhisheka/prakshal: pour water (or milk-water mix) over the image, then dry
   it gently with soft cloths.
2. **Chandan puja** — sandalwood paste touched with the ring finger on the image's nine
   points (toes, knees, wrists, shoulders, head, forehead, throat, chest, navel).
3. **Pushpa puja** — fresh whole flowers (no petals torn, none dropped on the floor).
4. **Dhoop puja** — incense, waved at the left side of the image.
5. **Deepak puja** — ghee lamp, waved at the right side.
6. **Akshat puja** — unbroken rice arranged on the table as a **swastika** with three dots
   above it and a crescent and dot at the top (the four states of existence, the three jewels,
   and liberation).
7. **Naivedya puja** — sweets placed on the swastika: the aspiration to be free of the need
   to eat.
8. **Phal puja** — fruit placed above: the fruit of liberation (moksha).
Then perform **chaityavandan** (a seated set of hymns and bows), and leave walking backwards a
few steps so as not to turn your back to the image.

## Gotchas
- Offerings are not taken back: what is offered is left for the temple.
- Use only filtered water and avoid stepping on ants or dropping flowers — ahimsa rules
  apply inside the temple too.
- Do not touch the image unless you have bathed and changed into puja clothes; visitors in
  street clothes take darshan only.
- Photography is often prohibited near the inner sanctum.

## Verify
- [ ] Bathed, in clean puja clothes, no leather on you.
- [ ] Said nissihi on entering; took darshan and recited the Navkar Mantra.
- [ ] Offered the eight items in order: water, sandalwood, flowers, incense, lamp, rice,
      sweets, fruit.
- [ ] Rice swastika laid with the three dots and the crescent.
- [ ] Followed the temple's posted rules on touching the image.
