# Jain tirtha yatra (pilgrimage)

**Goal:** plan and perform a pilgrimage to a Jain tirtha as devotion and austerity —
not tourism — keeping the site's rules and your own vows along the way.

> Draft. Step counts, distances and timings below are approximate and change with
> local regulations. Check the managing trust's current notices (e.g. Anandji
> Kalyanji Pedhi for Shatrunjaya and Girnar; the relevant Digambara/Svetambara trusts
> at Shikharji) before travelling.

## 1. What a tirtha is
A **tirtha** ("ford", crossing place) is a site sanctified by events in the lives of
the tirthankaras — especially where they attained liberation (nirvana-bhumi) — or by
great temples. Visiting one is understood as an aid to one's own spiritual effort; the
journey's austerities are part of the practice.

## 2. Key tirthas and their practice
**Shatrunjaya (Palitana, Gujarat)** — the foremost Svetambara tirtha, ~900 temples on
the hill.
- Climb of roughly 3,500–3,800 stone steps; start before dawn to avoid heat.
- No food is eaten on the hill and **no one stays overnight** there; descend by evening.
- Devotees often climb barefoot and fast (upvas or ekasana) on yatra days.
- The **Navanu yatra** — 99 ascents over a season — is a famous austerity. The hill is
  closed to yatra during chaturmas; the season reopens on **Kartik Purnima**.

**Sammed Shikharji (Parasnath Hill, Jharkhand)** — where 20 of the 24 tirthankaras
attained nirvana, sacred to both main traditions.
- The **parikrama** is about 27 km round trip on foot, visiting the shrines (tonks) on the
  summit ridge; pilgrims set off around 2–4 a.m. and return by afternoon.
- Palanquins (dolis) carried by porters are available for those who cannot walk.

**Shravanabelagola (Karnataka)** — Digambara centre with the 57-foot monolithic statue of
**Bahubali (Gommateshwara)**.
- About 600–650 steps up Vindhyagiri, climbed barefoot.
- The **Mahamastakabhisheka** — anointing the statue from scaffolding with water, milk,
  sugarcane juice, sandal paste, turmeric and flowers — is held roughly every 12 years
  (most recently 2018); expect huge crowds and booked accommodation.
Others widely visited: Girnar, Pavapuri (Mahavira's nirvana), Dilwara (Mount Abu),
Ranakpur, Taranga.

## 3. Practical planning
- **Lodging:** stay in a **dharamshala** run by the temple trust; book early in season.
  Many have a **bhojanshala** serving Jain food before sunset.
- **Clothing:** puja clothes for temple worship (bathe first), comfortable modest
  clothes for climbing; no leather on the hill or in temples.
- **Kit:** water (boiled, if fasting tivihar), torch for the pre-dawn start, small cloth
  bag for puja materials, sun protection.
- **Sangh yatra:** some travel in a group led by a mendicant, on foot over days,
  observing the **chhari-palit** discipline (six practices, including walking barefoot,
  one meal, sleeping on the ground and daily pratikramana).
- Train beforehand: daily stair-climbing for a few weeks avoids injury on long ascents.

## 4. On the day
1. Bathe, take a pachchakhan for the day's fast/eating limit.
2. Climb mindfully: watch your step for insects, keep conversation spiritual or silent.
3. At each main temple: darshan, puja if prepared, chaityavandan.
4. Descend in good time; break the fast (parna) below the hill if fasting.

## Gotchas
- Starting too late on Shatrunjaya or Shikharji — midday heat and rules mean you may
  not complete the circuit.
- Bringing food or leather items up the hill.
- Monsoon closure and Mahamastakabhisheka crowds: check dates before booking travel.
