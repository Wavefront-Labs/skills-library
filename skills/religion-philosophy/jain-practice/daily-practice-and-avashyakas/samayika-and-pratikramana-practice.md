# Samayika, pratikramana and the six daily essentials

**Goal:** build a lay Jain daily routine around the six **avashyakas** (essential
duties), centred on a 48-minute period of equanimity (samayika) and a reflection-and-
repentance rite (pratikramana).

> Draft. The six avashyakas are common to Jain traditions, but the recited texts and
> lengths differ: Svetambara Murtipujak, Sthanakvasi and Terapanthi use different
> pratikramana sutras in Ardhamagadhi; Digambara lay practice puts more weight on
> puja and svadhyaya and treats the avashyakas somewhat differently. Learn the texts
> from your own sangha's book or teacher.

## 1. The six essentials
1. **Samayika** — equanimity: a set period of renunciation of all harmful activity.
2. **Chaturvimshati-stava** — praise of the 24 tirthankaras (the *Logassa* hymn).
3. **Vandana** — veneration of the teachers (guru-vandan).
4. **Pratikramana** — "turning back": reviewing and repenting faults against the vows.
5. **Kayotsarga** — standing or sitting motionless, abandoning attachment to the body,
   usually timed by silent repetitions of the Navkar Mantra or Logassa.
6. **Pratyakhyana** — a vow renouncing something for a set time (food, drink, an activity).

## 2. Doing a samayika (48 minutes)
- **Time:** one *muhurta* = 48 minutes. Use a timer; many keep a sand timer.
- **Place:** a quiet, swept spot — at home, in an upashraya, or in the presence of monks or nuns.
- **Kit (Svetambara/Sthanakvasi):** a woollen mat (asan), a **muhapatti** (mouth-cloth)
  and a **charavala** (small soft brush to clear insects from the seat). White clothes
  are customary.
1. Sweep the seat gently with the charavala.
2. Take the vow: recite the *Karemi Bhante* sutra, renouncing harmful activity of mind,
   speech and body for the period.
3. Spend the time in scripture reading (svadhyaya), mantra japa, meditation or silent
   reflection. No phone, no household talk, no getting up for chores.
4. Close with the prescribed concluding sutras when the 48 minutes are complete.

## 3. Pratikramana
- **Daily:** evening (devasi) for the day's faults; morning (raisi) for the night's.
- **Fortnightly** (pakshik), **four-monthly** (chaumasi) and **annual** (samvatsari, the
  last day of Paryushana) are longer; the annual one may take ~3 hours.
- The structure: review conduct against the vows (ahimsa, truth, non-stealing, chastity,
  non-possession and the subsidiary vows), recite the confession sutras, and ask
  forgiveness of all living beings: **Michchhami Dukkadam** ("may my wrongdoing be fruitless").
- Doing it in the upashraya with a monk, nun or experienced lay leader reciting aloud is
  the easiest way to learn; booklets in Gujarati, Hindi and English transliteration exist,
  and recordings help with pacing.

## 4. Daily pratyakhyana examples
- **Navkarsi:** nothing to eat or drink until 48 minutes after sunrise.
- **Porsi:** until a quarter of the day has passed.
- **Chauvihar:** no food or water from sunset until next sunrise (a stricter version of
  the general Jain practice of not eating after dark).
- **Tivihar:** as above, but water allowed.
Take the vow consciously each morning or evening, ideally in front of a monk or nun or
by reciting the formula yourself; break it only at its stated time.

## Gotchas
- Pratikramana without understanding becomes mechanical recitation; read a translation
  alongside the original.
- Cutting a samayika short undoes its vow: start only when you can give the full 48 minutes.
- Sunrise/sunset times for vows come from a Jain panchang or app, not a guess.
- Different sangha texts mean you may not be able to follow along elsewhere — bring your book.

## Verify
- [ ] Today included at least one 48-minute samayika, timed.
- [ ] Evening pratikramana done (or at least the Michchhami Dukkadam reflection).
- [ ] A pratyakhyana taken this morning with a defined end time, and kept.
- [ ] You can name all six avashyakas and what each is for.
