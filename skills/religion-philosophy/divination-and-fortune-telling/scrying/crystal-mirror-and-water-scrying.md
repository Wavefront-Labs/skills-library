# Crystal, mirror and water scrying

**Goal:** sustain a relaxed, unfocused gaze into a reflective or luminous surface
long enough for images or impressions to arise, then record and interpret them
consistently.

Scrying is old and widespread — Elizabethan magus John Dee's obsidian mirror and
crystal, bowl divination in the ancient Near East, flame gazing in many folk
traditions. Technique is broadly shared; interpretation is individual.

## 1. Choose a medium
- **Crystal ball:** clear quartz or glass, 5–10 cm. Inclusions give the eye something
  to rest on. Glass is fine to start.
- **Black mirror:** obsidian, or make one by painting the *back* of a picture-frame
  glass with matte black paint (3 coats) so the front gives a deep, non-distorted
  reflection.
- **Water:** a dark bowl (black ceramic or glass) filled with still water; a drop of
  ink darkens it.
- **Flame:** a single candle in a draught-free room.

## 2. Set up the space
- Dim, indirect light: one candle *behind* you or to the side, so no bright reflection
  sits on the surface.
- Medium on a dark cloth at slightly below eye level, ~30–50 cm away.
- Sit upright and comfortable; phone silenced; a notebook and pen within reach.
- Fix a session length — 10–15 minutes at first, building to 20–30.
- Many practitioners open with a prayer, invocation or simply stating the question
  aloud, and close formally; follow your own tradition's practice.

## 3. The gaze
1. Breathe slowly for a minute (e.g. four counts in, four out).
2. Look *into* the surface, not at it — as if focusing a few centimetres behind it.
3. Let the eyes soften; don't stare hard. Blink normally.
4. Typical progression reported by practitioners: the surface seems to cloud or
   mist; colours or light shifts appear; then (sometimes, with practice) shapes,
   symbols or scenes.
5. Don't chase an image; noticing it too eagerly often dissolves it. Return to the
   soft gaze.

## 4. Record immediately
After the session (or by speaking into a voice recorder during it) note:
- Date, time, medium, question.
- Colours, shapes, symbols, scenes, and the feelings attached.
- Anything heard or "known" rather than seen.
Write first, interpret later — interpretation during the session breaks the state.

## 5. Interpret
- Personal symbolism first: what does this image mean *to you*?
- Traditional colour conventions (many sources; not universal): white/silver clouds
  = favourable, dark clouds = unfavourable, rising movement = yes, descending = no.
- Look for patterns across sessions in your journal.
- Hold readings lightly; a vague image can support many stories.

## 6. Practise
Short daily sessions beat long weekly ones. Many people see nothing but mist for
weeks; that's normal.

## Safety
- Never leave a flame unattended; keep hair and cloth away.
- Eye strain: stop if eyes water or ache; don't use sunlight on a crystal (a sphere
  focuses sunlight and can start fires).
- If sessions bring distressing imagery, sleep disturbance or blur the line between
  vision and reality, stop and talk to someone you trust or a health professional.

## Gotchas
- Bright reflections of the room or a lamp — move the light.
- Staring until the eyes dry out.
- Interpreting mid-session.
- Expecting cinematic visions early.

## Verify
- [ ] Light source is behind or beside you with no hot spot on the surface.
- [ ] Session has a set length, a question and an opening/closing.
- [ ] Impressions recorded before interpreting.
- [ ] Journal kept across sessions to spot recurring symbols.
- [ ] Fire and eye-safety precautions in place.
