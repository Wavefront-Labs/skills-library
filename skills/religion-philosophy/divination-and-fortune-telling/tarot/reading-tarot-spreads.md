# Reading tarot spreads

**Goal:** move from a question to a coherent reading that treats the spread as a
whole story, not a list of memorised card meanings, and deliver it to a querent
(the person asking) responsibly.

## 1. Know the deck's structure
- **78 cards:** 22 Major Arcana (0 The Fool to XXI The World) for large themes;
  56 Minor Arcana in four suits of Ace–10 plus four court cards.
- **Suits** (Rider–Waite–Smith associations; other systems differ): Wands — will,
  energy, work; Cups — emotion, relationships; Swords — thought, conflict, truth;
  Pentacles/Coins — body, money, material life.
- **Decks:** Rider–Waite–Smith (RWS, illustrated pips, most beginner books use it);
  Thoth (Crowley/Harris, different titles e.g. Lust for Strength, astrological
  attributions); Tarot de Marseille (non-scenic pips, read more by number and suit).
  Learn meanings for the deck you use.

## 2. Frame the question
Good questions are open and about the querent's own choices:
"What do I need to understand about changing jobs?" rather than "Will I get the job?"
Avoid reading on third parties' private lives, health diagnoses, or legal outcomes.

## 3. Shuffle and lay out
1. Shuffle (overhand or riffle) while the querent focuses on the question; some
   readers let the querent shuffle.
2. Cut into three piles with the left hand and restack (a common convention, not a rule).
3. Decide **before** shuffling whether you read reversals; if yes, turn part of the
   deck during shuffling so some cards come out inverted.
4. Lay cards face down in the spread's order, then turn them one by one.

### Three-card spread
Positions: 1 situation / 2 challenge / 3 advice — or past / present / likely outcome.
Best for daily practice and short questions.

### Celtic Cross (common RWS-tradition layout)
1. Present situation · 2. Crossing (challenge) laid across 1 · 3. Foundation (below)
· 4. Recent past (left) · 5. Possibilities/conscious aim (above) · 6. Near future
(right) · 7. Self · 8. Environment/others · 9. Hopes and fears · 10. Outcome (staff of
four cards up the right side, 7 at the bottom). Position names vary between books —
pick one version and stay consistent.

## 4. Read the spread as a whole
1. **Scan first:** count Major Arcana (many = big life forces), dominant suit,
   repeated numbers (several 5s = conflict/change), many court cards (other people
   involved).
2. **Read each card in its position:** card meaning × position meaning.
3. **Connect:** look at where figures face, colour and imagery flow, contrasts
   between foundation and outcome.
4. **Reversals:** read as blocked, internalised, delayed or excessive versions of the
   upright meaning — not simply the opposite.
5. **Summarise** in two or three sentences, ending with what the querent can do.

## 5. Running a client reading
- State plainly what a reading is and isn't; in some jurisdictions practitioners
  must state readings are "for entertainment" — check local consumer law.
- Don't make medical, legal or financial predictions; refer to professionals.
- Don't create dependency (repeat readings on the same question the same day) or
  upsell fear ("there is a curse I can remove for a fee" is a classic scam pattern).
- Let the querent respond; ask "How does that fit?"
- Keep readings confidential.

## 6. Building skill
Daily one-card draw with a journal: card, first impression, what happened. After a
month, compare. Learn one suit per week through its imagery and numbers rather than
rote keywords.

## Gotchas
- Reading the book instead of the cards in front of you.
- Changing reversal policy mid-reading.
- Death (XIII) almost never means literal death in modern practice — say so before
  the querent panics.
- Asking the same question repeatedly until a preferred answer appears.

## Verify
- [ ] Question is open, about the querent, and not medical/legal/third-party.
- [ ] Reversal policy and spread positions decided before shuffling.
- [ ] Reading notes the overall pattern (Majors, suits, numbers) before single cards.
- [ ] The summary ends with an actionable reflection, not a fixed fate.
- [ ] Ethical limits stated to the client; reading kept confidential.
