# Elder Futhark rune casting

**Goal:** draw or cast runes and read them as a coherent answer, while knowing which
parts of the practice are historically attested and which are modern.

## A note on history
The Elder Futhark (c. 2nd–8th centuries CE) is well attested as an alphabet. How (or
whether) it was used for divination is uncertain: Tacitus' *Germania* describes
casting marked lots on a white cloth but does not say the marks were runes. Most rune
meanings in use today come from the medieval rune poems (Old English, Norwegian,
Icelandic — written for later rune rows) and from 20th-century authors. Modern
practice, including within heathenry/Ásatrú, is a living tradition built on these;
present it honestly as such.

## 1. The 24 runes in three aettir
**Freyr's aett:** Fehu (wealth, cattle) · Uruz (aurochs, strength) · Thurisaz (giant,
thorn — force, conflict) · Ansuz (a god/Odin — speech, inspiration) · Raidho (ride,
journey) · Kenaz (torch — knowledge, craft) · Gebo (gift, exchange) · Wunjo (joy).
**Hagal's aett:** Hagalaz (hail, disruption) · Nauthiz (need, constraint) · Isa (ice,
stillness) · Jera (year, harvest, cycles) · Eihwaz (yew, endurance) · Perthro
(uncertain meaning — often lot-cup, fate, mystery) · Algiz (elk/protection) · Sowilo
(sun, success).
**Tyr's aett:** Tiwaz (Tyr — justice, sacrifice) · Berkano (birch, growth, birth) ·
Ehwaz (horse, partnership) · Mannaz (humankind, self) · Laguz (water, intuition) ·
Ingwaz (Ing — gestation, completion) · Dagaz (day, breakthrough) · Othala
(inheritance, home).
Sound values are well established; meanings are interpretive.

## 2. Making or choosing a set
Wood (traditionally fruit-bearing trees per Tacitus), stone, or bone; 24 pieces of
similar size so none can be felt in the bag. Many practitioners carve or paint their
own as a dedication. The "blank rune" is a 1980s addition (Ralph Blum) — many
traditional practitioners omit it; decide and be consistent.

## 3. Draws
- **One rune:** daily guidance or a focal theme. Draw blind from a bag.
- **Three runes** (left to right or in a row): situation · challenge/action · outcome;
  or Norns layout — Urd (what has become), Verdandi (what is becoming), Skuld (what
  shall be).
- **Five-rune cross:** centre = present, left = past influences, right = future,
  top = help, bottom = obstacle.

## 4. A nine-rune cast
1. Spread a white or plain cloth (the traditional image from Tacitus).
2. Focus on the question; take nine runes blind.
3. Hold them, then scatter them gently onto the cloth from a low height.
4. Read only face-up runes. Those in the centre are most central to the matter;
   toward the edge, more peripheral. Runes touching or clustered are read together.
5. Face-down runes are ignored or read as hidden factors — choose a convention.

## 5. Merkstave (reversed/inverted)
Runes that are not symmetric can land reversed; many readers read these as blocked
or shadow aspects. Nine runes (Gebo, Hagalaz, Nauthiz, Isa, Eihwaz, Ingwaz, Dagaz,
Sowilo and Jera, depending on carving style) cannot reverse — learn which in your set.

## Gotchas
- Mixing Elder Futhark meanings with Younger Futhark or Anglo-Saxon Futhorc runes.
- Using runes with white-supremacist symbolic history (Othala variants, Algiz in some
  contexts, the Wolfsangel — not a Futhark rune) carelessly; many heathen
  organisations actively reject this misuse. Be aware how symbols read in public.
- Overclaiming ancient pedigree for modern meanings.
