# Reading tea leaves and coffee grounds

**Goal:** prepare a cup that leaves readable patterns, read it by zones, and turn the
shapes into a coherent reading. Tea-leaf reading is a British/Irish and wider European
folk practice; coffee-cup reading (*kahve falı* in Turkey, also common in Greece, the
Levant, the Balkans and Armenia) is a social tradition often done among friends and family.

## Tea leaves
### Preparation
- **Tea:** loose-leaf with small to medium leaves (a broken-leaf black tea or Earl
  Grey works well). Very fine dust tea clumps; large whole leaves don't form shapes.
- **Cup:** a plain, light-coloured, wide-mouthed teacup with a saucer; avoid patterns
  inside the cup.
- Brew directly in the cup, or in an unstrained pot so leaves pour through. About half
  a teaspoon of leaves per cup.

### The ritual
1. The querent drinks while thinking about the question, leaving about a teaspoon of
   liquid with the leaves.
2. Holding the handle (left hand is a common convention), swirl the cup three times
   clockwise, so the leaves climb the sides.
3. Turn the cup upside down onto the saucer and leave it ~1 minute to drain.
4. Turn it upright with the **handle pointing toward the querent**.

### Zones
- **Handle** = the querent and their home; symbols near it are close to them.
- **Rim** = present and near future; **sides** = coming weeks/months; **bottom** =
  the distant future or deep matters (some readers read the bottom as negative).
- Read clockwise from the handle around the cup for a time sequence.

## Turkish/Greek coffee
### Preparation
- Very finely ground coffee (powder-fine, "Turkish grind") boiled in a cezve/briki
  with water (and sugar if wished), poured unfiltered into small cups.
- Drink from one side only, leaving the thick sludge.

### The ritual
1. Place the saucer on top of the cup; make a wish or think of the question.
2. Swirl gently (often three times, toward oneself), then flip cup and saucer together
   so the cup is upside down on the saucer.
3. Let it cool and dry, typically 5–10 minutes — reading a wet cup smears shapes.
4. Lift the cup. Read the **cup** (the querent's life — handle side often personal
   matters, opposite side others/outside world; the rim is near-term) and then the
   **saucer** (sometimes read as the answer or home).
5. Customs vary by region: some don't read one's own cup, some leave a coin on the
   saucer, some read only after a wish is made.

## Symbols (traditional associations — readers vary)
- Bird: news · Ring: commitment or completion · Key: solution, opportunity · Snake:
  enmity or wisdom (context) · Heart: love · Anchor: stability, journey ending ·
  Tree: growth · Fish: luck, abundance (especially in coffee reading) · Road or
  lines: journey; straight = smooth, wavy = obstacles · Dots: money · Letters/numbers:
  initials, times.
- Size: large symbols = important; clear shapes = definite; fuzzy = uncertain.
- First impressions count — name what you see before reaching for a symbol list.

## Building the reading
1. Scan the whole cup; note the 3–5 clearest shapes.
2. Place each in its zone (near/far, close to handle or not).
3. Tell the story in time order, linking symbols.
4. End with the overall tone and anything the querent can act on.

## Gotchas
- Using teabags (leaves too fine) or filtered coffee (no grounds).
- Reading too soon — wait for drainage/drying.
- Handle orientation forgotten, which flips the timeline.
- Treating a cultural social practice as a solemn fortune when the setting is casual —
  match the tone of the occasion.

## Verify
- [ ] Right tea/grind used; a teaspoon of liquid left before swirling.
- [ ] Cup inverted and drained/dried the proper time.
- [ ] Handle (or reading side) oriented and zones identified before reading.
- [ ] 3–5 clear symbols noted with position and size.
- [ ] Reading told as a sequence with an overall tone.
