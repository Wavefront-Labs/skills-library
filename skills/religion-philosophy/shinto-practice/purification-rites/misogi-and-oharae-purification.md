# Misogi and oharae — Shinto purification

**Goal:** take part in the main Shinto purification rites — from the twice-yearly Great
Purification to misogi in cold water — understanding their place in restoring a
clean, bright state before the kami.

> Draft. Practice differs between shrines and lineages; misogi in particular is taught
> by specific shrines and groups (e.g. the Misogi-kyō tradition and some shrines that run
> regular sessions) with their own sequence and chants. Do **not** attempt waterfall or
> sea misogi without an experienced leader — cold-water shock and slippery rocks are real
> dangers.

## 1. The idea
**Kegare** (impurity, pollution) — through death, illness, wrongdoing (**tsumi**) or simply the
wear of daily life — dulls one's original bright, sincere heart (*makoto*). **Harae/harai**
(purification) removes it. Purification precedes almost every Shinto act: temizu at the
basin, the priest's **shubatsu** with the haraegushi wand before any ceremony.

## 2. Oharae — the Great Purification (30 June and 31 December)
Held at shrines nationwide to purify the past half-year:
- **Hitogata / katashiro:** a paper doll in human shape, given out at the shrine office in the
  weeks before. Write your name and age on it, **breathe on it** and **stroke your body**
  with it (transferring impurity), then return it with a small offering. The priests purify
  it in the rite and burn it or float it away.
- **Nagoshi no harae** (30 June) — **chinowa kuguri:** walking through a large ring of
  woven reed or grass (chinowa) set up at the shrine gate, in a figure of eight:
  1. Bow; pass through, turn **left**, walk round back to the front.
  2. Bow; pass through, turn **right**, walk round.
  3. Bow; pass through, turn **left** again, then pass through once more and proceed to
     the hall to pray.
  A short verse is often recited; the shrine posts the order and words.
- **Toshikoshi no harae** (31 December): the year-end purification before New Year.
- The priests recite the **Ōharae no kotoba** (Great Purification prayer). Many lay
  practitioners also learn the short **harae-kotoba** for daily use before their kamidana.

## 3. Misogi (purification with water)
Typical structure of a guided session (sea, river or waterfall):
1. **Preparation:** fasting or a light meal beforehand; white clothing (men a white
   fundoshi and headband, women a white robe) provided by the group; remove jewellery.
2. **Warm-up exercises** on the shore, done together with shouts: **torifune** (a rowing
   movement), **furutama** (shaking the clasped hands before the belly to settle the spirit),
   **otakebi** (calling out), and sprinkling salt and sake on the body and into the water.
3. **Entering the water** on the leader's signal, while chanting (e.g. *harae-tamae,
   kiyome-tamae*) — standing under the waterfall or immersed to the chest in the sea for a
   counted time.
4. **Leaving the water** together; drying, warming, then prayer at the shrine.
Safety: medical clearance for heart conditions or high blood pressure; never alone; know water
temperature and currents; limit time in winter (often only a minute or two).

## 4. Everyday purifications
- **Yakubarai:** a gokitō to purify and protect in one's **yakudoshi** (unlucky ages, e.g.
  25 and 42 for men, 19 and 33 for women in common reckoning — check the shrine's chart,
  which uses kazoedoshi age).
- **Salt:** sprinkling salt over oneself after a funeral before entering the house; piles of
  salt (*morijio*) at entrances.
- **Kibuku:** a period of mourning when visits to shrines are customarily avoided.

## Gotchas
- Discarding the hitogata instead of returning it to the shrine.
- Walking through the chinowa in a straight line or in the wrong direction.
- Joining a winter misogi without a warm-up, guidance or medical clearance.

## Verify
- [ ] Hitogata collected, used (name, breath, stroke) and returned before 30 June / 31 December.
- [ ] Chinowa passed in the left–right–left figure of eight.
- [ ] Misogi only with an experienced leader, warm-up done, time limits kept.
- [ ] Yakudoshi checked against the shrine's chart for this year.
