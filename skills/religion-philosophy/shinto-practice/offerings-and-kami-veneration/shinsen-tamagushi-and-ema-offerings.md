# Shinsen, tamagushi and ema — making offerings to the kami

**Goal:** make the principal Shinto offerings correctly — food offerings (shinsen), the
sakaki-branch offering (tamagushi) and votive plaques (ema) — understanding them as
acts of gratitude and relationship with the kami.

> Draft. Arrangements below follow common Jinja Honchō practice and home custom;
> shrines and families differ in detail (left/right placement, which items). Follow the
> priest's direction at a shrine and your household's custom at home.

## 1. Shinsen (food offerings)
**Basic home or small-shrine set** — offered fresh each morning (or at least on the 1st and
15th of the month):
- **Rice** (uncooked washed rice, or cooked rice), **salt**, **water**; and **sake** (omiki)
  on special days, usually a pair of bottles.
- At larger shrine rites: fish, seaweed, vegetables, fruit, sweets, sometimes rice cakes
  (mochi) — the sea and mountain produce of the region.
**Arrangement (common home layout):** on a **sanbō** (small wooden stand) or tray, rice in
the centre, salt and water to either side (rice → sake → salt/water in order of rank from the
centre). With two rows: rice centre, sake behind, salt and water in front. Use white
unglazed dishes (kawarake) or plain porcelain kept only for offerings.
- Offer with a bow; raise the tray to eye level when carrying it.
- Take offerings down (osagari) later the same day or the next morning and **eat them** —
  sharing the kami's food is the **naorai**; don't throw them away if they are edible.

## 2. Tamagushi hōten (offering a sakaki branch)
Given at formal prayers, weddings, funerals and festivals. A **tamagushi** is a sakaki branch
with paper streamers (shide) attached.
1. Step forward and bow to the priest, who hands you the branch.
2. Receive it with the **right hand from above holding the base** (stem), and the **left hand
   from below supporting the leafy end**; hold it at chest height, slightly raised to the left.
3. Walk to the offering table; bow lightly.
4. Rotate the branch **clockwise** so the stem points toward you; bring the left hand down to
   hold the stem with both hands; pause for a silent prayer.
5. Move the right hand to support the middle, then rotate the branch **clockwise 180°** so the
   stem now points toward the altar.
6. Place it on the table, stem toward the kami.
7. Step back one pace; **two bows, two claps, one bow** — at a **funeral** or memorial, clap
   **silently** (*shinobite*), bringing the hands together without sound.
8. Step back, bow to the priest and family, return to your seat.
Watch the person before you if unsure.

## 3. Ema (votive plaques)
1. Buy an ema (wooden plaque) at the shrine office; designs follow the year's zodiac animal or
   the shrine's kami.
2. Write the wish on the **back** (the plain side), clearly and specifically — e.g. a school's
   name for exam success, "safe delivery" with a due date. Write your name (and, often,
   address or initials only, for privacy).
3. Hang it on the **ema-kake** rack near the hall of worship; it will later be ritually burned.
Many write ema in any language; keep wishes positive and about yourself or loved ones.

## 4. Money offerings
- **Saisen:** coins or notes in the offering box at the hall.
- **Hatsuho-ryō / tamagushi-ryō:** paid for ceremonies, in a white envelope with your name.

## Gotchas
- Leaving shinsen to spoil on the kamidana — replace daily and eat what is taken down.
- Turning the tamagushi anticlockwise or placing it with leaves toward the altar.
- Loud claps at a Shinto funeral (use silent clapping).
- Writing other people's private information on a public ema.

## Verify
- [ ] Rice, salt and water fresh on the tray today; sake on 1st/15th or special days.
- [ ] Tamagushi received right-hand-over-stem, turned clockwise, placed stem toward the kami.
- [ ] Funeral context: silent claps.
- [ ] Ema written on the back, hung on the rack.
