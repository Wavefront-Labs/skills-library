# Shrine visit and formal prayer

**Goal:** pay respects at a Shinto shrine (jinja) correctly, and arrange and take part
in a formal prayer ceremony (gokitō / kigan) conducted by a priest.

> Draft. The forms below follow the widely taught Jinja Honchō (Association of Shinto
> Shrines) standard. Some shrines differ — e.g. Izumo Taisha and Usa Jingū use **four**
> claps; Ise's inner rites differ — and every shrine posts its own instructions; follow
> those. Fees and booking practices vary.

## 1. Arriving
1. **Torii:** bow lightly once before passing through the gate; you are entering the
   kami's precinct.
2. **Sandō** (approach path): walk to one side — the centre (*seichū*) is customarily left
   for the kami.
3. Visiting while in mourning (roughly 50 days after a close relative's death, by custom)
   is traditionally avoided; many wait until the period ends.

## 2. Temizu (purification at the water basin)
1. Take the ladle in the **right** hand, fill it; pour over the **left** hand.
2. Switch hands; pour over the **right** hand.
3. Switch back; pour water into the cupped **left** hand and rinse your mouth (never drink
   from the ladle); spit discreetly to the side, not into the basin.
4. Rinse the left hand again; tip the ladle upright so the remaining water runs down the
   handle; replace it face down.
Use one ladleful for the whole sequence.

## 3. Praying at the hall of worship (haiden)
1. Bow lightly; drop a coin offering (**saisen**) gently into the box — the amount is up to
   you (5 yen, *go-en*, is a popular pun on "good connection").
2. Ring the **bell** (suzu) by shaking the rope, if there is one.
3. **Nirei nihakushu ippai:** two deep bows (90°); two claps at chest height (right hand
   slightly lower); hold your palms together for a silent prayer — many first give name and
   address, then thanks, then the request; one final deep bow.
4. Bow lightly and step aside.

## 4. Formal prayer (gokitō)
For purposes such as safe childbirth, a child's blessing, warding off misfortune in an
unlucky year (*yakudoshi*), business prosperity, car safety, exam success:
1. Go to the **shamusho** (shrine office) or the gokitō reception. Many shrines take walk-ins
   during set hours; larger ones allow reservation.
2. Fill in the form: name(s), address, date of birth, purpose.
3. Pay the **hatsuho-ryō** (offering) — often ¥5,000–10,000+, sometimes tiered. Use new notes
   in a white envelope (noshi-bukuro), writing 初穂料 (hatsuho-ryō) and your name.
4. Wait in the waiting room; you will be led into the hall. Dress neatly (no hats; jacket
   for formal occasions).
5. **The ceremony** (about 15–30 minutes):
   - **Shubatsu** (purification): the priest waves the **haraegushi** (wand with paper
     streamers) over you; bow your head.
   - The drum may sound; the priest offers food and sake to the kami.
   - **Norito:** the priest recites a prayer in classical Japanese naming you and your purpose.
   - **Tamagushi hōten:** you may be asked to offer a sakaki branch at the altar (see the
     offerings skill).
   - Possibly a **miko** dance (kagura) with bells.
   - Bow when the priest bows.
6. Afterwards you receive an **ofuda/omamori** and often a small gift (sake, sweets); a
   **naorai** (sharing in the offering) may be a sip of sake.

## 5. Other things at the shrine
**Omamori** (amulets) for specific purposes; **omikuji** (fortunes) — tie bad ones to the
provided rack; **goshuin** (shrine stamp) in a dedicated book, received after praying.

## Gotchas
- Drinking from the ladle or putting it back face up.
- Photographing inside the honden or during gokitō without permission.
- Using four claps at a two-clap shrine (or vice versa) — read the posted sign.
- Folded or used notes for the hatsuho-ryō.

## Verify
- [ ] Bowed at the torii; walked to the side of the sandō.
- [ ] Temizu done left–right–mouth–left with one ladleful.
- [ ] Prayer: two bows, two claps, silent prayer, one bow (or the shrine's posted form).
- [ ] For gokitō: form filled, offering in a white envelope, appropriate dress.
