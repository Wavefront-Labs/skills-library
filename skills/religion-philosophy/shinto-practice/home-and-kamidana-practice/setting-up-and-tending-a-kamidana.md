# Setting up and tending a kamidana

**Goal:** install a **kamidana** (household Shinto altar) in the right place, enshrine the
ofuda correctly, and keep a simple daily practice of offering and prayer through the year.

> Draft. Guidance follows common Jinja Honchō and shrine-shop practice; families and
> regions differ, and apartments force compromises. When unsure, ask the shrine where
> you receive your ofuda — priests are used to these questions.

## 1. What you need
- **Miyagata:** a small wooden shrine (one-door or three-door), or a simple modern ofuda
  stand for small spaces.
- **Ofuda:** paper/wood talismans from shrines — usually the **Jingū Taima** (from Ise,
  available at most local shrines), one from your **ujigami** (local guardian shrine), and
  optionally one from another shrine you revere (*sūkei jinja*).
- A shelf (the kamidana itself), a pair of **sakaki** vases, a **shimenawa** rope with shide
  paper streamers, offering dishes for rice, salt and water (and a pair of sake flasks), and
  optionally a pair of lamps.

## 2. Placement
- **High:** above eye level, so you look up to it.
- **Facing south or east** (the front of the miyagata faces that way).
- **Bright, clean, calm room** — a living room is typical.
- **Avoid:** above a doorway people pass under constantly, facing the toilet, directly opposite
  or below a Buddhist **butsudan** (if you have both, place them in different spots or on
  different walls, with the kamidana higher).
- **Upstairs room above it?** Shops sell a small card reading 雲 (*kumo*, "cloud") or 天 (*ten*,
  "heaven") to attach to the ceiling above the kamidana, signifying nothing is above it.
- Fix the shelf securely to studs; earthquakes are a real hazard.

## 3. Enshrining the ofuda
- **Three-door miyagata:** centre — **Jingū Taima**; to the viewer's right — **ujigami**;
  viewer's left — sūkei jinja.
- **One-door miyagata:** stack them, front to back — Jingū Taima at the front, ujigami
  behind, others behind that.
- Handle ofuda with clean hands; never write on them or place them face down.

## 4. Daily practice (2–3 minutes, ideally in the morning)
1. Wash hands and rinse mouth.
2. Place fresh **rice, salt and water** (water in a lidded vessel — open the lid when offering)
   on the shelf: rice in the centre, salt and water on either side.
3. Stand before the kamidana; optionally recite the short **harae-kotoba** or the
   *kamidana-haishi* prayer.
4. **Two bows, two claps, one bow** (as at a shrine).
5. Silent thanks and prayer for the household.
6. Take the offerings down later and use them (eat the rice, use the salt in cooking, water for
   plants) — that is sharing with the kami.

## 5. Monthly and yearly
- **1st and 15th of the month:** change the **sakaki** (fresh branches — or replace water and
  trim every few days in summer), add sake, wipe the shelf.
- **Year end:** clean the kamidana (*susu-harai*), hang a new shimenawa, and receive **new
  ofuda** at the shrine. Return the old ones to the shrine's collection point
  (*kosatsu-osamejo*) at hatsumōde to be ritually burned; do not discard them in rubbish.
- **Mourning:** during the period after a family death, many cover the kamidana with white
  paper and pause offerings (often for ~50 days) — follow your shrine's guidance.

## Gotchas
- Mounting below eye level or in a corridor.
- Leaving sakaki to wither — change it or use artificial sakaki only if you must.
- Putting both a kamidana and a butsudan facing each other.
- Keeping year-old ofuda indefinitely; renew annually.

## Verify
- [ ] Kamidana above eye level, facing south or east, securely fixed.
- [ ] Ofuda in order: Jingū Taima centre/front, ujigami right/behind.
- [ ] Rice, salt and water fresh today; daily two bows-two claps-one bow.
- [ ] Sakaki changed on the 1st and 15th; ofuda renewal planned for year end.
