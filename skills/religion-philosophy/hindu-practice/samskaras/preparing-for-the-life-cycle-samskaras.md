# Preparing for the life-cycle samskaras

**Goal:** organise the main samskaras (sacraments that sanctify each stage of life)
so the family knows what to prepare, when, and what each step means. Texts list
sixteen (*shodasha samskara*) though the number and details vary by region,
community and sampradaya. A family **purohit** (priest) conducts them and is the
authority on your family's form.

## Common preparation for any samskara
1. Consult the purohit early. Give names, gotra, nakshatras.
2. Fix a **muhurta** (auspicious time) from the panchang — the priest usually does this.
3. Get the **samagri** (materials) list: typically ghee, rice, turmeric, kumkum,
   coconuts, betel leaves and nuts, flowers, fruits, a havan kund and wood for the
   sacred fire, new clothes.
4. Arrange venue, seating around the fire, and food for guests.
5. Many rites open with Ganesha puja, sankalpa and a homa (fire offering).

## Childhood rites
- **Namakarana (naming):** commonly on the 11th or 12th day after birth (customs vary).
  The father or priest whispers the name in the baby's right ear; the name's first
  syllable is often chosen from the birth nakshatra.
- **Annaprashana (first solid food):** around six months; the child is fed a little
  sweet rice (kheer), often by a maternal uncle or father.
- **Chudakarana / mundan (first haircut):** often in the first or third year; head
  shaved, sometimes at a temple or pilgrimage site, with the hair offered.
- **Vidyarambha:** beginning learning — writing the first letters, often on
  Vijayadashami.

## Upanayana (sacred thread)
Traditionally for boys of communities that keep it (often between ages 8 and 16;
some communities today also perform it for girls). The child receives the
**yajnopavita** (sacred thread worn over the left shoulder), is taught the
**Gayatri mantra** by the father/guru (often under a cloth, privately), and begins
daily *sandhyavandana*. Afterward the initiate keeps the thread's rules (e.g.
changing it annually on Upakarma). Prepare the child beforehand in the mantra's
pronunciation and the commitment involved.

## Vivaha (wedding)
Rites vary greatly by region; common key steps:
1. **Ganesha puja** and pre-wedding rites (haldi, mehndi; varied by community).
2. **Kanyadaan** — the bride's parents give her hand.
3. **Panigrahana** — the groom takes the bride's hand.
4. **Mangalsutra / sindoor** — in many communities, the groom ties the mangalsutra
   (marriage necklace) and/or applies sindoor.
5. **Agni** — the sacred fire as witness; offerings of puffed rice (*laja homa*).
6. **Saptapadi** — seven steps (or seven circlings) around the fire, each with a
   vow. In many traditions the marriage is complete with the seventh step.
Planning: a ceremony of 1–3 hours; give the priest the order of extras you want
included; provide printed explanations for guests. Civil registration is separate
in most countries (in India, registration under the relevant marriage law; abroad,
by an authorised celebrant).

## Antyeshti (last rites)
1. At death: place the body on the floor with head to the south (common custom),
   lamp at the head, Ganga water/tulsi in the mouth if possible; chant the divine name.
2. Cremation as soon as practicable, ideally within about a day. The chief mourner
   (traditionally the eldest son; today often any child) performs **mukhagni**
   (lighting the pyre) or presses the crematorium button; outside India, funeral
   homes accommodate this.
3. Ashes collected and later immersed in a river (*asthi visarjan*), ideally the
   Ganga; overseas, local rivers/sea per regulations or sent to India.
4. A mourning period (often 10–13 days) with daily rites; the 12th or 13th day
   rites (*sapindikarana*, feeding Brahmins/guests) end the period.
5. Annual **shraddha** on the death tithi, and Pitru Paksha observances.

## Gotchas
- Booking a priest late in festival or wedding seasons.
- Overseas cremation timing and ash-scattering rules — check local law.
- Assuming one region's wedding order applies to all.
- Not explaining rites to guests from other backgrounds.

## Verify
- [ ] Purohit engaged; muhurta fixed; samagri list in hand.
- [ ] Family knows each step's meaning and who performs it.
- [ ] Legal requirements (marriage registration, cremation permits) handled separately.
- [ ] Post-rite observances (thread rules, mourning days, shraddha dates) calendared.
- [ ] Guests given a short explanation where helpful.
