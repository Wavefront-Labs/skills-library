# Japa, kirtan and satsang

**Goal:** practise bhakti — loving devotion to a chosen form of God — through its
three everyday disciplines: repeating the divine name (japa), singing it together
(kirtan and bhajan), and keeping company with fellow devotees (satsang).

Bhakti traditions are many: Vaishnava (Krishna, Rama), Shaiva (Shiva), Shakta
(the Goddess), and saint-poet lineages (Mirabai, Tulsidas, the Alvars and Nayanars,
Kabir). Your lineage or guru, where you have one, sets the specific mantra and rules.

## 1. Ishta-devata and mantra
- The **ishta-devata** is the form of God you are drawn to and devote yourself to.
- A mantra may be given in initiation (*diksha*) by a guru — traditionally such
  mantras are kept private. Public name mantras need no initiation, for example:
  "Om Namah Shivaya", "Om Namo Narayanaya", "Sri Ram Jai Ram Jai Jai Ram", and the
  Hare Krishna maha-mantra ("Hare Krishna Hare Krishna, Krishna Krishna Hare Hare /
  Hare Rama Hare Rama, Rama Rama Hare Hare").

## 2. Japa with a mala
- **Mala:** 108 beads plus a larger *meru* (guru) bead. Rudraksha is traditional for
  Shaiva practice, tulsi wood for Vaishnava, crystal or sandalwood in others.
- **Method:**
  1. Sit upright, ideally at the same time daily (early morning is favoured).
  2. Hold the mala in the **right hand**, draped over the middle (or ring) finger;
     move beads with the thumb toward you. The index finger traditionally doesn't
     touch the beads.
  3. One repetition per bead, starting next to the meru.
  4. At the meru, **don't cross it**: turn the mala around and go back the other way.
  5. Many keep the mala in a cloth bag and don't let it touch the floor.
- **Voice:** aloud, whispered, or mental — traditions rank them differently; begin
  aloud or whispered to steady attention.
- **Count:** commit to a number of rounds daily (1, 4, 16 rounds — ISKCON initiates
  vow 16 rounds of the maha-mantra) and keep it.
- When the mind wanders, gently return to the sound and meaning of the name.

## 3. Kirtan and bhajan
- **Kirtan:** call-and-response chanting of divine names; a leader sings a line, the
  group repeats. Builds from slow to fast and returns to stillness.
- **Bhajan:** devotional songs with lyrics, often by saint-poets, sung by a group or
  soloist.
- **Instruments:** harmonium (drone and melody), kartals (hand cymbals),
  mridanga/dholak/tabla. A simple kirtan needs only a harmonium and kartals.
- **Leading kirtan:**
  1. Pick a short, well-known chant; give the words (sheet or screen).
  2. Start at a comfortable pitch — a common beginner key on the harmonium is around
     C or D.
  3. Keep each melody simple; repeat until the group has it before speeding up.
  4. End with a slow repetition and a few moments of silence.

## 4. Satsang
"Company of truth": a regular gathering of devotees. A typical format (60–90 min):
opening prayer, kirtan/bhajan, reading from scripture (Bhagavata Purana, Ramayana,
Gita, a saint's poems) with discussion or a talk, arati, prasad shared.
Keep it hospitable to newcomers: share words and meanings.

## 5. The inner side
Bhakti emphasises surrender (*sharanagati*), humility, and remembering God in daily
actions — offering food, work and relationships to the ishta. Simple practice:
before each meal, mentally offer it; before sleep, recall the day with gratitude.

## Gotchas
- Crossing the meru bead.
- Treating japa as a numbers race; quality of attention matters.
- Publicising an initiated mantra that the lineage keeps private.
- Kirtan leaders choosing complex songs the group can't follow.

## Verify
- [ ] Ishta-devata and mantra chosen (or given by your guru).
- [ ] Daily japa at a fixed time and count; meru not crossed.
- [ ] You can lead a short call-and-response kirtan.
- [ ] You attend or host a regular satsang.
- [ ] Daily acts (meals, work) consciously offered.
