# Darshan, archana and abhisheka at the temple

**Goal:** visit a Hindu temple and take part in its worship correctly — from
etiquette at the door to requesting priestly rites — whether at a large South Indian
temple, a North Indian mandir or a diaspora temple serving many traditions.

Temple worship is governed by the temple's own tradition (Agamic rules in many South
Indian temples, Pancharatra or Vaikhanasa in many Vaishnava ones, local custom
elsewhere). Watch what regulars do and follow the temple's posted guidance.

## 1. Before entering
- Bathe beforehand; wear modest, clean clothing (some temples require traditional
  dress — e.g. dhoti for men and sari/salwar for women — and post the rule).
- Remove footwear at the designated place.
- Wash hands and feet if a tap is provided.
- Carry an offering if you wish: flowers, fruit, coconut, betel leaves, a garland —
  temples usually sell them outside.
- Phones silent; photography usually forbidden inside the sanctum.

## 2. Order of worship
1. Ring the entrance bell (where present) on entering.
2. Many temples have a set order: first Ganesha, then the main deity, then the
   subsidiary shrines. Follow the flow of devotees or signs.
3. **Darshan** — the central act: seeing and being seen by the deity in the
   sanctum (*garbhagriha*). Stand or sit facing the deity, palms joined, eyes open
   to behold the deity; pray silently or recite a mantra.
4. **Pradakshina** — walk clockwise around the sanctum or the whole temple,
   keeping the deity on your right; odd numbers of rounds (1, 3, 5…) are customary.
   At Shiva temples, many don't cross the channel (*somasutra*) where abhisheka
   water flows and so turn back rather than completing the circle.

## 3. Archana (name-chanting offering)
1. Buy an archana ticket at the counter (if the temple uses them) and bring an
   offering plate.
2. Give the priest (*pujari/archaka*) your name, **gotra** (paternal lineage, if
   known; "I don't know" is acceptable at most temples) and **nakshatra** (birth star).
3. The priest chants the deity's 108 names (*ashtottara*) on your behalf, offering
   flowers or kumkum, then waves arati.
4. Receive the plate back with blessed flowers and prasad.

## 4. Abhisheka (ritual bathing)
Ritual bathing of the murti with water, milk, curd, honey, ghee, sugar, coconut
water, sandalwood and others, accompanied by Vedic chanting (e.g. Rudram for Shiva).
Devotees book and sponsor it in advance; you'll be told when to arrive, what
materials to bring (or to pay for), and where to sit. Witness quietly; receive
*theertham* (blessed water) afterwards.

## 5. Receiving blessings
- **Arati:** hold palms over the flame when the plate is brought, then touch eyes/head.
- **Theertham:** receive in the right palm (left supporting below), sip, wipe hand on
  head.
- **Vibhuti/kumkum:** apply to the forehead.
- **Shatari** (Vaishnava temples): the priest touches a crown to your head.
- **Prasad:** accept with the right hand; eat or take home — don't discard it.
- An offering to the hundi (donation box) is customary.

## 6. Festivals and processions
At festivals (*utsavam*), festival images (*utsava murti*) are taken out in
processions, sometimes on great chariots (*ratha*). Join by walking with the
procession, offering coconuts or arati as it passes your home, or helping pull the
chariot ropes when invited.

## Gotchas
- Pointing feet toward the deity when sitting.
- Touching the murti or entering the sanctum — only priests do in most temples.
- Some temples restrict entry (e.g. to Hindus only) — respect posted rules.
- Using the left hand for offerings or prasad.

## Verify
- [ ] Dressed appropriately, footwear removed, phone silenced.
- [ ] Darshan and clockwise pradakshina done in the temple's order.
- [ ] Archana requested with name, gotra (if known) and nakshatra.
- [ ] Arati, theertham and prasad received with the right hand.
- [ ] Posted temple rules (dress, entry, photography) followed.
