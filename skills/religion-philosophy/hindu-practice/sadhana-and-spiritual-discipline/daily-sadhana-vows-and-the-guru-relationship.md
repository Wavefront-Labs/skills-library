# Daily sadhana, vows and the guru relationship

**Goal:** establish a sustainable daily spiritual discipline (*sadhana*), deepen it
with vows and austerity (*tapas*) appropriate to your life, and approach the
guru–disciple relationship with both devotion and discernment.

This skill covers sadhana as religious practice. Postural and breathing technique
practised as exercise belongs elsewhere.

## 1. The ethical foundation
Patanjali's *Yoga Sutra* lists **yamas** (restraints: non-violence *ahimsa*,
truthfulness *satya*, non-stealing *asteya*, *brahmacharya* — celibacy or moderation
by life stage, non-possessiveness *aparigraha*) and **niyamas** (observances:
purity *shaucha*, contentment *santosha*, austerity *tapas*, study *svadhyaya*,
surrender to God *ishvara pranidhana*). Many teachers start every disciple here: an
evening review against one yama per week.

## 2. A daily sadhana
**Brahma muhurta** — the period of about 96 minutes before sunrise — is traditionally
favoured. A sample 45–60 minute routine:
1. Wake, bathe, clean clothes.
2. Sit at your shrine; light a lamp.
3. Prayer to the guru and Ganesha.
4. **Japa** — set number of mala rounds of your mantra.
5. **Meditation** on the ishta-devata or the mantra's meaning (15–20 min).
6. **Svadhyaya** — a page of scripture.
7. Offer the fruits of the practice to God.
Initiated dvija men (and others in some communities) also perform
**sandhyavandana** at the junctions of day (dawn, noon, dusk) with Gayatri japa.
**Rule:** choose a routine you can keep on your worst day; add when it is stable.
Keep a simple log (date, time, rounds, notes).

## 3. Vows (vrata) and tapas
Tapas — "heat" — is disciplined effort that purifies. Common forms:
- **Fasting:** Ekadashi or a weekly fast.
- **Mauna** (silence): a few hours or one day a week; carry a note explaining.
- **Chaturmasya:** a four-month period during the monsoon (from Ashadha Shukla
  Ekadashi, Devshayani) when many take extra vows — giving up a food, extra japa;
  sannyasis stay in one place.
- **Sleep on the floor, simple food, a japa target** for a set period.
Structure each vow: sankalpa (what, how long), keep it, close it formally. Don't
take vows that damage health; consult your guru and, for fasting, a doctor if
you have medical conditions.

## 4. Guru and disciple
The guru–shishya relationship is central to many traditions: the guru gives
**diksha** (initiation) and mantra, guides practice, and connects the disciple to a
**parampara** (lineage).
Before seeking initiation:
- Observe the teacher over time — months or more. The tradition itself advises
  testing a guru (and the guru tests the disciple).
- Check the lineage: who was their teacher? Is the community accountable?
- Look for consistency between teaching and conduct; how the community treats
  questions, money and people who leave.
- **Red flags:** demands for large sums or property, isolation from family, sexual
  advances, punishment for questioning, claims that ordinary ethics don't apply.
After diksha: keep the mantra private if instructed, follow the daily commitment,
offer *seva* (selfless service) in the ashram or community, and attend Guru Purnima.

## 5. Stages of life
Classical *ashrama* stages (student, householder, forest-dweller, renunciant) shape
expectations: householders' sadhana fits around family duties; *sannyasa*
(renunciation) is a formal initiation within an order — a major step taken with the
order's guidance.

## Gotchas
- Starting with a two-hour routine and quitting in a week.
- Austerity as self-punishment or pride rather than devotion.
- Revealing an initiated mantra.
- Ignoring red flags because of a teacher's charisma.

## Verify
- [ ] A daily sadhana you have kept for 30 days, with a log.
- [ ] One yama/niyama under weekly review.
- [ ] Each vow has a sankalpa, duration and formal close, and is safe for your health.
- [ ] Any guru has been observed over time and checked against the red flags.
- [ ] Seva is part of your practice.
