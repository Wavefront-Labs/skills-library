# Gita recitation and svadhyaya

**Goal:** make scripture a daily devotional and contemplative practice — reciting
correctly, understanding what you recite, and letting it shape life — rather than
reading it like an ordinary book.

## 1. Choosing texts
- **Bhagavad Gita** — 700 verses in 18 chapters, mostly in *anushtubh* metre (four
  quarters of eight syllables). The most widely recited text.
- **Ramayana** — Valmiki's Sanskrit epic, or Tulsidas's Awadhi *Ramcharitmanas*,
  recited in homes and temples; the *Sundara Kanda* is often recited on its own.
- **Stotras** — hymns such as the *Vishnu Sahasranama* (1,000 names), *Lalita
  Sahasranama*, *Hanuman Chalisa* (40 verses in Awadhi).
- **Vedic texts** — Rudram, Purusha Sukta, the Upanishads; chanted with pitch accents
  and traditionally learned only orally from a teacher.

## 2. Sanskrit pronunciation essentials
- Learn Devanagari or the IAST transliteration (ā, ī, ū long; ṛ vocalic r; ṭ ḍ ṇ
  retroflex; ś ṣ distinct sibilants; ṃ anusvara; ḥ visarga — an echo of the vowel).
- Long vowels are held for **twice** the short ones; this matters for metre.
- Aspirated consonants (kh, gh, th, dh, ph, bh) are single sounds with a breath, not
  English "th".
- Use a recording by a recognised teacher (e.g. from a Gita-teaching institution or
  traditional pathashala) and repeat line by line.

## 3. Daily Gita recitation
1. Fixed time and seat; light a lamp if that's your practice.
2. Opening invocation (many recite the *Gita Dhyanam* verses).
3. Recite a portion: one chapter a day completes the Gita in 18 days; or a set of
   verses, building up memorisation.
4. Read the meaning of the verses with a traditional commentary (e.g. Shankara,
   Ramanuja or Madhva — commentaries reflect different Vedanta schools — or a modern
   teacher in your lineage).
5. Pick one verse to carry through the day.
6. Close with a prayer; many conclude a chapter with the colophon ("Om tat sat…").
Memorisation: two lines at a time, ten repetitions, review previous days' verses
first.

## 4. Parayana (complete recitation)
A full reading of a text over a set time as a vow — e.g. the *Ramcharitmanas* in 9
days (*navahna*) or a month, the *Sundara Kanda* on Tuesdays/Saturdays, the
*Vishnu Sahasranama* daily. Set a schedule, keep it, and conclude with arati and
prasad. Group recitation (*akhand path* of the Manas: continuous, in shifts over
about 24 hours) is common at festivals.

## 5. Vedic chanting
Vedic recitation uses three pitch accents: **udatta** (raised), **anudatta**
(lowered, marked with a line under the syllable) and **svarita** (falling, marked
with a vertical stroke above). Chanters use a hand movement to track them.
Tradition holds Vedic chanting must be learned orally from a teacher (*guru-mukha*),
with mnemonic recitation patterns (*pada, krama, jata, ghana*) used to preserve it
exactly. Note: some communities restrict who may chant certain Vedic texts — learn
the norms of your tradition.

## 6. Svadhyaya
In the *Yoga Sutra* (2.1, 2.32) svadhyaya — "self-study" — includes scripture study
and mantra recitation as purification. Practice: after recitation, a short journal
entry — what the text said, what it asked of me, where today I fell short or
followed it.

## Gotchas
- Rushing long vowels and breaking the metre.
- Reading only one modern paraphrase as "the Gita".
- Attempting Vedic svaras from text alone.
- Starting a parayana schedule you can't keep — begin smaller.

## Verify
- [ ] You can read your chosen text's script or IAST with long/short vowels distinguished.
- [ ] Daily recitation at a fixed time, with meaning studied from a named commentary.
- [ ] A memorisation log with review of earlier verses.
- [ ] Any parayana has a written schedule and completion rite.
- [ ] Vedic chanting learned from a teacher, not from text alone.
