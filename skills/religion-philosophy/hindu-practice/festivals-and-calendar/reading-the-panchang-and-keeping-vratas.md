# Reading the panchang and keeping vratas

**Goal:** know when observances fall *for your location*, and keep the main fasts,
vows and festivals of the year in the way your family or tradition does.

## 1. The panchang's five limbs
A panchang (almanac) gives, for each day:
1. **Tithi** — lunar day: 30 per lunar month, 15 in the waxing fortnight
   (*shukla paksha*) and 15 in the waning (*krishna paksha*). A tithi is defined by
   the moon–sun angle (12° each), so it starts at varying clock times.
2. **Vara** — weekday.
3. **Nakshatra** — the moon's lunar mansion (27).
4. **Yoga** — a combination of sun and moon longitudes (27).
5. **Karana** — half a tithi.
It also lists sunrise, muhurta (auspicious windows) and inauspicious periods such as
Rahu Kalam.

## 2. Practical rules that trip people up
- **Location matters.** Tithi at sunrise usually governs the day's observance, and
  sunrise differs by place, so an Ekadashi can fall on different civil dates in India
  and North America. Use a panchang calculated for your city (temple calendars,
  established panchang publishers and apps let you set location).
- **Month systems:** **amanta** (month ends at new moon; common in South and West
  India) vs **purnimanta** (ends at full moon; common in North India). Festival dates
  agree; month *names* for the dark fortnight differ.
- **Adhik maas:** a leap month inserted roughly every 32–33 months keeps the
  lunisolar calendar aligned; festivals shift accordingly.
- Regional solar calendars (Tamil, Bengali, Malayalam, etc.) set New Year and some
  festivals.

## 3. Ekadashi fast
The 11th tithi of each fortnight — about 24 a year, observed especially by
Vaishnavas.
1. Many eat a single light meal on the evening before (Dashami).
2. On Ekadashi: abstain from grains and beans (rice, wheat, lentils); levels range from
   fruit-and-milk only to a full water fast (*nirjala*). Choose a level appropriate to
   your health — fasting rules traditionally exempt the sick, pregnant, elderly and
   children.
3. Extra japa, scripture reading, temple visit; staying awake part of the night on
   major Ekadashis.
4. **Parana** (breaking the fast) next day **within the window** the panchang gives
   — typically after sunrise on Dvadashi and before its end, avoiding the first
   quarter called *Hari Vasara*.

## 4. Structure of a vrata (vow)
1. **Sankalpa** — formal intention: deity, purpose, duration.
2. **Observance** — fast, puja, reading of the vrata katha (the story behind it).
3. **Completion** — *udyapana*: a closing puja, feeding others, gifts.
Common vratas: Monday fasts for Shiva, Tuesday or Saturday for Hanuman, Karva Chauth,
Satyanarayan puja on full moons.

## 5. Major festivals (dates by panchang)
- **Navaratri** (Sharad, autumn): nine nights of the Goddess from Ashvin shukla
  pratipada; ghatasthapana (installing a sacred pot) on day 1, kanya puja on day 8 or
  9, Dussehra/Vijayadashami on day 10. In Bengal, Durga Puja with pandals.
- **Diwali** (Deepavali): centred on the new moon of Kartika (amanta Ashvin);
  five days in many regions — Dhanteras, Naraka Chaturdashi, Lakshmi Puja on the
  amavasya evening at the auspicious window, Govardhan Puja, Bhai Dooj. Clean and
  light the home with diyas.
- **Holi:** Holika Dahan bonfire on Phalguna purnima night; colours next day.
- Also: Janmashtami, Ganesh Chaturthi, Maha Shivaratri (night vigil), Rama Navami,
  Pongal/Makar Sankranti (solar, around 14 January).

## 6. Make a family calendar
At the start of each year, take a location-specific panchang and list: Ekadashis,
family vratas, festivals, ancestral *shraddha* dates (by tithi), and muhurtas for
planned events. Share with the household.

## Gotchas
- Using an India-calculated date abroad.
- Breaking a fast outside the parana window.
- Mixing amanta and purnimanta month names when reading festival instructions.
- Strict fasts without considering health and medications.
