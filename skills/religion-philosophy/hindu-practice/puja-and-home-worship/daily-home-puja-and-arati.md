# Daily home puja and arati

**Goal:** offer daily worship at a home shrine with the attitude of welcoming and
honouring the deity as a beloved guest, following a simple, widely used sequence.

Home puja varies enormously by family, region, sampradaya (tradition) and the deity
worshipped (Vishnu/Krishna, Shiva, Devi, Ganesha and others). Many families follow
the practice they inherited, and that practice comes first. What follows is a common
shape; a family elder or priest can give your lineage's specifics and mantras.

## 1. The shrine
- A clean, quiet spot, ideally facing east or north (common Vastu guidance), raised
  above floor level — a shelf, cabinet mandir or small room.
- **Murti** (consecrated image) or framed pictures of the chosen deity/deities;
  Ganesha is commonly included and worshipped first.
- **Puja items:** a bell, a lamp (diya, ghee or oil with a cotton wick), incense,
  a small water pot and spoon (*achamani*), kumkum and turmeric, sandalwood paste,
  rice grains (*akshata*), fresh flowers, a plate (*thali*) and fresh food to offer.
- Many homes keep a **tulsi** (holy basil) plant, especially in Vaishnava households.

## 2. Before you begin
- Bathe and wear clean clothes; many avoid worship after eating and keep footwear
  away from the shrine.
- Clean the shrine; remove the previous day's flowers (put them in a garden or
  compost rather than the bin, as many families do).
- Morning (after bathing, before eating) and/or evening at lamp-lighting time are
  usual.

## 3. A short daily puja (panchopachara — five offerings)
1. **Achamana** — sip water three times from the right palm (or sprinkle) for
   purification.
2. **Ring the bell** and light the lamp.
3. **Sankalpa** — a brief statement of intention: who you are, the day, that you
   offer this worship.
4. **Invoke** the deity with a mantra or prayer (e.g. "Om Gam Ganapataye Namaha" for
   Ganesha first, then your chosen deity's name mantra).
5. The five offerings:
   - **Gandha** — sandalwood paste/kumkum applied to the murti or picture.
   - **Pushpa** — flowers.
   - **Dhupa** — incense, waved clockwise.
   - **Dipa** — the lamp.
   - **Naivedya** — fresh vegetarian food (fruit, sweets, cooked rice), sprinkled with
     water, offered with a mantra and a moment's pause.
6. **Arati** — circle the lit lamp (or camphor) clockwise before the deity while
   singing an arati song (e.g. "Om Jai Jagdish Hare", widely sung in North India) and
   ringing the bell. Then pass your palms over the flame and touch them to your eyes
   and head.
7. **Pradakshina** — turn clockwise in place (if you can't walk around the shrine).
8. **Prayer and prostration** (*pranam*), and a moment of silent prayer.
9. **Prasad** — the offered food becomes blessed food; share it with the household.
The full **shodashopachara** (16 offerings) adds welcoming, seat, foot-washing,
bathing (*abhisheka*), clothing, sacred thread and others — used on festival days.

## 4. Simplified practice
On busy days: light the lamp, offer a flower and water, chant a short mantra (e.g.
108 repetitions of the deity's name on a mala), bow. Consistency matters more than
elaborateness.

## Gotchas
- Food tasted before offering is not offered (it's considered already eaten).
- Lamps unattended near curtains or flowers; use a brass diya on a tray and keep it
  away from cloth.
- Camphor flares — hold away from the face; use small pieces.
- Some practices (e.g. touching consecrated murtis, menstruation customs, onion and
  garlic in offerings) vary by family and community; follow your own or ask.

## Verify
- [ ] Shrine clean, items assembled, old flowers removed respectfully.
- [ ] Purification, lamp, sankalpa and invocation done before offerings.
- [ ] Five offerings made with naivedya untouched before offering.
- [ ] Arati performed clockwise with the lamp; lamp never left unattended.
- [ ] Prasad shared; the practice kept consistently, even in short form.
