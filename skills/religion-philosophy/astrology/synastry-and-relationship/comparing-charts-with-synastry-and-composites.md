# Comparing charts with synastry and composites — reading the relationship, not a score

**Goal:** describe how two people tend to interact — attraction, ease, friction,
long-term glue — from their two charts and a composite, in a balanced way that helps
them understand the relationship rather than deciding it for them.

## 1. Data and consent
- Birth date, time and place for **both** people, each checked as for a natal chart
  (see religion-philosophy.astrology.natal-charting.casting-and-delineating-a-natal-chart).
  If one time is unknown, skip that person's Ascendant, MC, house overlays and the
  composite angles.
- Read both natal charts first; synastry is meaningless without knowing each person.
- A partner's chart is their personal data. Prefer both parties consenting, and do
  not use synastry to tell a client to stay in or leave a relationship, or to judge
  someone who has not asked.

## 2. Software
- **Bi-wheel:** astro.com (Extended Chart Selection › "Synastry chart / bi-wheel"),
  astro-seek synastry calculator, Solar Fire (*Chart › Bi-wheel*), with an
  **inter-aspect grid** turned on.
- **Composite:** midpoint composite (most common) in the same programs. The
  **Davison** relationship chart (a real chart for the midpoint in time and space) is
  an alternative; some astrologers use both.
- Use the same zodiac, house system and node type for both people.

## 3. Inter-aspects (A's planets to B's planets)
1. **Orbs:** tighter than natal — commonly about 5–6° for the luminaries and 3–4° for
   other planets; schools vary. Inter-aspects to the Ascendant/Descendant axis use
   about 2–3°.
2. **Key contacts, in rough order of weight:**
   - Sun–Moon (especially conjunction, opposition, trine): basic compatibility of
     identity and needs; traditionally a marriage indicator.
   - Venus–Mars: physical attraction and romantic spark.
   - Moon–Moon and Moon–Venus: emotional comfort, domestic ease.
   - Mercury–Mercury and Mercury to the other's Sun/Moon: how well they talk.
   - **Saturn contacts** to personal planets: commitment and duration, but also
     restriction or a sense of being judged — read who is the Saturn person.
   - Outer planets (Uranus, Neptune, Pluto) to personal planets: excitement and
     instability, idealisation and confusion, intensity and control respectively.
   - Nodal contacts (planet conjunct the other's North or South Node): sense of fate
     or familiarity.
3. **Direction matters:** read each contact both ways ("her Mars to his Moon": she
   activates, he feels). Grid both A→B and B→A.

## 4. House overlays
Place A's planets in B's houses and vice versa. They show which area of life each
person activates for the other (A's Venus in B's 7th: A feels like a partner to B;
A's Saturn in B's 1st: B may feel restricted or steadied). Use the person's own
house system; overlays are sensitive to birth time.

## 5. Composite chart
Read it as a natal chart for the relationship itself: the composite Sun (purpose),
Moon (emotional climate), Ascendant (how the couple presents), Venus, Saturn, and
the house emphasis. The composite has no "owner"; it describes the shared entity.
Note that midpoint composites can place Mercury and Venus in odd positions relative
to the Sun; take that with caution.

## 6. Synthesis and delivery
List supportive and challenging themes, keeping only those confirmed by two or more
factors (e.g. Saturn–Moon inter-aspect + Saturn on composite Ascendant = seriousness
and duty). Present both sides, describe challenges as dynamics to work on, and avoid
"compatibility percentages" (some apps produce them; they are crude point counts).

## Gotchas
- Sun-sign compatibility alone ignores nearly everything that matters here.
- Mixing house systems or zodiacs between the two charts.
- Treating one hard aspect as a deal-breaker; long relationships typically show many.
- Unknown birth times silently produce wrong overlays and composite angles.

## Verify
- [ ] Both natal charts checked and read first; birth time quality noted for each.
- [ ] Inter-aspect orbs stated and contacts read in both directions.
- [ ] House overlays done only where birth times are reliable.
- [ ] Composite type (midpoint or Davison) named.
- [ ] Reading is balanced and leaves the decision with the clients; consent for both charts.
