# Judging a horary question — answering one question from the chart of its asking

**Goal:** give a clear yes/no (or where/when/who) answer to a single sincere question
by casting the chart for the moment it was understood, following the traditional
rules in a fixed order.

## 1. Take the question
- **One sincere, specific question** that matters to the querent. "Will I get the job
  I interviewed for on Tuesday?" works; "Tell me about my career" does not.
- **The time:** most practitioners use the moment *the astrologer receives and
  understands* the question (when an email or message is read; when it is asked in
  person). Use the astrologer's location; some use the querent's — state your rule.
- Do not re-ask the same question hoping for a different chart; the first chart stands.
- Ethics: refuse questions about another person's death or health, and questions
  you would not answer in a natal reading.

## 2. Software settings
Solar Fire, Astrogold, Morinus (free), or astro.com. Use the **tropical zodiac**,
**Regiomontanus** houses (Lilly's system; some modern horarists use whole-sign or
Alcabitius), the seven traditional planets plus the nodes and Part of Fortune, and
traditional rulerships (Mars rules Scorpio, Saturn Aquarius, Jupiter Pisces). Outer
planets are optional colour, not significators. Show essential dignities.

## 3. Considerations before judgment
Lilly lists cautions that the chart may not be "radical" (fit to judge). Check:
1. **Ascendant earlier than 3°** — too early, the matter is not yet ripe.
2. **Ascendant later than 27°** — too late; the matter is already decided or the
   querent already knows.
3. **Moon void of course** — makes no further major aspect before leaving its sign
   (traditional exceptions when in Taurus, Cancer, Sagittarius or Pisces): "nothing
   will come of it" is the usual reading.
4. **Moon in the Via Combusta** (about 15° Libra–15° Scorpio).
5. **Saturn in the 1st or 7th** (harm to the querent or the astrologer's judgment).
Practitioners differ on whether these forbid judgment or simply describe the
situation; many modern horarists read them as descriptive.

## 4. Assign significators
- **Querent:** ruler of the 1st house, with the Moon as co-significator.
- **Quesited:** ruler of the house that rules the matter — 2nd money/possessions, 5th
  children/pregnancy, 6th illness and employees, 7th partner/opponent, 10th job/career,
  11th friends and hopes. For other people's affairs use **derived houses** (the
  partner's money = 2nd from 7th = 8th).
- If one planet rules both houses, judge by the Moon and by planets in those houses.

## 5. Judge
1. **Perfection:** does the querent's significator make an applying aspect to the
   quesited's significator (or vice versa) before either changes sign? Conjunction,
   sextile, trine = yes, with ease; square = yes, with difficulty; opposition = it may
   happen with regret, or not at all.
2. **Translation of light:** a faster planet separates from one significator and
   applies to the other, carrying the matter. **Collection:** a slower planet receives
   applying aspects from both.
3. **Obstacles:** **prohibition** (another planet aspects first), **refranation**
   (a significator turns retrograde before perfection), **combustion** (within about
   8.5° of the Sun).
4. **Reception and dignity:** mutual reception helps even without an aspect; a
   significator in detriment or fall shows weakness of that party.
5. **Timing:** degrees to perfection, scaled by sign mode and house: cardinal/angular
   = shorter units (days/weeks), mutable/succedent = medium, fixed/cadent = longer
   (months/years). Use the context to pick the unit.

## Gotchas
- Sloppy significators: decide them before looking for the answer you want.
- Forgetting to count only applying aspects within the current signs.
- Using modern rulers as significators changes the answer.
- Lost-object questions use the 2nd house and the Moon; direction comes from
  sign/house placement, which conventions disagree on.

## Verify
- [ ] Question recorded verbatim with the time and place you used.
- [ ] Considerations before judgment noted, and how you treated them.
- [ ] Significators named before judging, with derived houses where needed.
- [ ] Answer cites the specific perfection or obstacle that decides it.
- [ ] Timing gives a unit chosen from mode, house and context.
