# Reading transits, progressions and solar returns — a layered annual forecast

**Goal:** describe the main themes and timing windows of a coming period (usually 12
months) for one person, built from a verified natal chart and ranked by importance
rather than listed as every passing aspect.

## 1. Prerequisites
- A natal chart with a reliable birth time (see
  religion-philosophy.astrology.natal-charting.casting-and-delineating-a-natal-chart).
  Without a time, use only transits to planets, not to angles or houses, and skip
  progressions of the angles.
- The forecast window and the client's **current place of residence** (needed for
  the solar return).

## 2. Transits (the outer layer)
1. In the software, produce a **transit listing**: astro.com (Extended Chart Selection
   › "Transits" / personal daily horoscope), astro-seek transit calendar, or Solar
   Fire's *Dynamic Reports* › Transits. Set the period and include stations.
2. **Prioritise by planet speed:** Pluto, Neptune, Uranus, then Saturn, then Jupiter.
   Ignore Sun, Mercury, Venus and Mars transits for an annual overview; use them for
   timing *within* an outer-planet window. Transits of the Moon are daily noise.
3. **Orbs:** commonly 1–2° for outer planets (some use up to 3° applying), 2–3° for
   Saturn and Jupiter. Schools differ; tighter orbs give sharper timing.
4. **Retrograde passes:** a slow planet often makes the same aspect three times
   (direct, retrograde, direct). Report the full window from first to last exact hit,
   and note **stations** within about 1° of a natal point as especially strong.
5. **Ranking:** transits to the Sun, Moon, Ascendant, MC and chart ruler outrank
   transits to other points. Hard aspects (conjunction, square, opposition) are
   usually more eventful than trines and sextiles.
6. Cycles worth naming: Saturn return (about ages 29 and 58), Uranus opposition
   (about 40–42), Jupiter return (every ~12 years), nodal return (~18.6 years).

## 3. Secondary progressions (the inner layer)
"A day for a year": the chart for N days after birth describes age N.
- Track the **progressed Moon** (about 1° per month, ~2.5 years per sign, a full
  cycle every ~27–28 years): its sign and house give the emotional backdrop; its
  aspects to natal planets time shorter phases.
- Note progressed Sun sign changes (roughly every 30 years), progressed planets
  changing direction (a progressed station is a multi-year theme), and progressed
  New/Full Moons (the lunation cycle).
- Progression orbs are tight: about 1°, applying.
- Some practitioners use **solar arc directions** alongside or instead; keep the
  technique consistent across clients.

## 4. Solar return (the annual snapshot)
1. Cast the chart for the exact moment the Sun returns to its natal degree, minute
   and second, for the place where the client **will be** at that birthday (some
   schools insist on the birthplace — state your choice).
2. Read: the return Ascendant and its ruler, angular planets, the house holding the
   return Sun (the year's focus), and the return Moon.
3. Make sure the software uses the same zodiac (tropical vs sidereal; some use a
   precession-corrected return) you intend.

## 5. Synthesis
Build a month-by-month table: outer transits (multi-month windows), progressed Moon
phases, solar return emphasis, then fast-planet triggers. A theme matters when two
layers agree (e.g. Saturn transiting the 10th, progressed Moon entering Capricorn,
return MC ruler angular). Write in terms of themes and likely areas of activity, not
fixed events. Do not forecast deaths, illnesses, legal outcomes or investment results.

## Sun-sign horoscopes (column writing)
Media horoscopes use **solar houses**: the reader's Sun sign is treated as the 1st
house, and the month's ingresses, lunations and retrogrades are read through those
houses. Keep it clearly general; it is not a personal forecast.

## Gotchas
- Using a mean-time ephemeris with the wrong time zone shifts exact dates by a day.
- Over-listing: forty transits in a report hide the three that matter.
- Relocated solar returns change the angles dramatically; record which place you used.
- Progressed angles depend on the method (e.g. Naibod vs solar arc for the MC); state it.

## Verify
- [ ] Every reported transit has a date window, including all retrograde passes.
- [ ] Outer and slow planets ranked above fast ones; orbs stated.
- [ ] Progressed Moon sign/house and any progressed stations noted.
- [ ] Solar return location and zodiac setting recorded.
- [ ] Each headline theme is supported by at least two layers; no medical/financial/legal predictions.
