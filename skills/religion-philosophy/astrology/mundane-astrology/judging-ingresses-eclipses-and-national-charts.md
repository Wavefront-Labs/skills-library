# Judging ingresses, eclipses and national charts — mundane forecasting for a country

**Goal:** produce a forecast of the main collective themes for one nation (or the
world) over a period, by layering the slow cycles, the season's ingress chart,
eclipses and the nation's own foundation chart.

## 1. Decide scope and reference data
- **Unit:** a country, a city, or the world as a whole. For a country you need its
  **capital** (latitude/longitude) and, ideally, a **national (foundation) chart**.
- **National charts are contested.** Most nations have several candidate charts
  (independence, constitution, union, revolution). The US "Sibly" chart for 4 July
  1776 is widely used but its time is disputed; the UK is often read from 1 January
  1801 (Act of Union). Choose one, record its source, and test it against past events
  before forecasting with it. Nicholas Campion's *Book of World Horoscopes* collects
  the alternatives.

## 2. Software
Solar Fire (*Chart › Ingress* and eclipse search), Astrogold, Morinus, Janus, or
astro.com/astro-seek ephemerides and eclipse tables. Tropical zodiac; houses usually
Placidus or Regiomontanus for ingresses (whole-sign in Hellenistic-style work).
Eclipse path maps come from NASA's public eclipse catalogue.

## 3. Work from slowest to fastest cycle
1. **Great conjunctions:** Jupiter–Saturn conjunctions (~every 20 years) set the
   generational tone; their element shifts roughly every 200 years (the 2020
   conjunction in Aquarius opened an air series). Also note Saturn–Uranus,
   Saturn–Neptune, Saturn–Pluto and Uranus–Pluto cycles and their phases for
   structural and ideological shifts.
2. **Ingress chart:** cast the chart for the moment the Sun enters 0° Aries (the
   **Aries ingress**) for the capital. If a fixed sign rises, it is often read for
   the whole year; a cardinal sign means also casting the Cancer, Libra and Capricorn
   ingresses for each quarter; mutable signs, two charts. (Conventions vary.)
3. **Eclipses:** list the year's solar and lunar eclipses with their degrees.
   Traditional approaches weight places where the eclipse is visible or on the
   central path, and eclipses falling on angles or key points of a national chart.
   Duration of effect is often taken from the eclipse length (Ptolemy: years for a
   solar eclipse's hours, months for a lunar eclipse's), though modern astrologers
   simply watch the 6–12 months after.
4. **Lunations:** New and Full Moon charts for the capital time shorter episodes.

## 4. Read the ingress with mundane house meanings
- 1st: the people, general condition. 2nd: economy, national wealth. 3rd:
  communications, transport, neighbours. 4th: land, agriculture, opposition party,
  housing. 5th: leisure, children, speculation. 6th: public health, workforce,
  armed services. 7th: foreign relations, open enemies, war. 8th: deaths, debt,
  foreign trade. 9th: law, religion, universities, long-distance trade. 10th:
  government, the head of state. 11th: parliament, allies. 12th: prisons, hospitals,
  hidden enemies.
- Look for angular planets, the Ascendant ruler's condition, and the Moon's next
  applications (the course of events).

## 5. Test against the national chart and synthesise
Overlay ingress, eclipse and transit positions onto the national chart. A theme
earns a headline when the same houses or planets are triggered by two or more layers
(e.g. eclipse on national 10th-house ruler + ingress Saturn on the MC = pressure on
government). Write about tendencies and areas of stress, give windows not dates, and
check the method against at least two past years before publishing.

## Gotchas
- Using the wrong national chart (or an unrectified time) makes house readings arbitrary.
- Forgetting to recast the ingress for the capital's exact location and time zone.
- Hindsight bias: it is easy to "find" events after the fact; record forecasts in advance.
- Publishing predictions about named individuals' deaths or health, or about markets,
  is poor practice and can cause real harm — keep to collective themes.

## Verify

- [ ] Great-conjunction/outer cycles, ingress(es), eclipses and lunations listed.
- [ ] Ingress cast for the capital; rising-sign convention for its duration stated.
- [ ] Each headline theme confirmed by at least two layers.
