# Masbuta and Mandaean ritual life

**Goal:** understand and take part in the core rites of the Mandaean (Sabian-Mandaean)
faith — baptism in living water above all — as a community member, with the
practical arrangements diaspora communities now need.

> Draft. Mandaean rites are performed by priests from texts in Mandaic (the *Ginza Rabba*,
> the *Qolasta* prayerbook and priestly manuals); laypeople do not perform the priestly
> parts. Details and naming differ between Iraqi, Iranian (Ahvaz) and diaspora
> communities (Australia, Sweden, the US, Netherlands). The faith does not accept
> converts. Confirm specifics with your community's priests (tarmida, ganzibra).

## 1. Living water
Central to Mandaean practice is **yardna** — "living", i.e. naturally flowing, water,
understood as coming from the world of light. Rites need a river, stream, or (in diaspora)
a purpose-built **mandi** pool fed with flowing water. Still or tap water in a bath does not
serve for baptism.
Diaspora communities use agreed river sites (often requiring local council permission) or
build a mandi with circulation; check with your mandi committee for the current site.

## 2. Masbuta (baptism)
Unlike a once-only rite, **masbuta** is repeated throughout life: traditionally every Sunday,
at feasts, before and after marriage, after birth, after impurity, and when a person feels
the need.
1. Bathe first and arrive fasting from the morning meal if your community asks it.
2. Wear the white ritual dress, the **rasta** (several garments including a belt, the
   *himyana*, and a stole), prepared and worn in a pure state.
3. Enter the river, facing roughly north (toward the world of light), and kneel or stand
   as the priest directs.
4. The priest immerses you three times, signs the forehead with water three times,
   gives you water to drink three times, places a myrtle wreath (klila) on the head, and
   lays his hand on you while reciting.
5. On the bank: anointing with sesame oil, a sacramental meal of bread (pihta) and water
   (mambuha), and the **kushta** (ritual handclasp of truth) with the priest.

## 3. Lay ablutions
- **Rishama:** a daily self-ablution in flowing water (or water poured from a jug in
  diaspora practice) before prayer, washing face, hands, ears, nose, legs and so on in a
  prescribed order with short prayers, ideally before sunrise.
- **Tamasha:** a self-immersion three times in flowing water, after events causing
  impurity (e.g. touching a corpse, after childbirth or menstruation for women, nocturnal
  emission).

## 4. Ritual meals and the dead
- **Lofani** and **zidqa brikha**: blessed ritual meals shared in memory of the dead and
  for the community.
- **Masiqta** ("raising up"): the death mass performed by priests to help the soul ascend to
  the world of light, with ritual bread and oil; held soon after death. Funerals are prompt;
  mourning includes avoiding certain foods for a period.

## 5. The year
| Feast | Practice |
|-------|----------|
| **Dehwa Rabba** (Great Feast, New Year) | families stay indoors for the first 36 hours, keeping purity; visiting follows |
| **Parwanaya / Panja** (five intercalary days, spring) | the most important baptism period — many go to the river every day; meals for the dead |
| **Dehwa Hnina** (Little Feast) and **Dehwa d'Mabarakta** | baptism and visiting |
The Mandaean calendar is 365 days without leap-year correction, so festival dates drift
relative to the Gregorian calendar: use the community's printed calendar.

## Gotchas
- Treating any water source as acceptable; it must be flowing.
- Wearing a rasta that has been made impure (e.g. stored with ordinary laundry).
- Arranging a river baptism without checking permits, safety and water quality in diaspora sites.

## Verify
- [ ] Baptism site confirmed as flowing water and approved by the priest/mandi committee.
- [ ] Rasta clean, complete and kept separately.
- [ ] Rishama done before prayer; tamasha after impurity.
- [ ] Current-year dates for Parwanaya and Dehwa Rabba taken from the community calendar.
