# Baha'i prayer, Feast and Fast

**Goal:** keep the core personal and community laws of Baha'i devotional life —
daily obligatory prayer, the Nineteen Day Feast and the annual Fast — on the right
days and in the right form.

> Draft. Details below follow the Kitab-i-Aqdas and widely published Baha'i guidance;
> for questions on exemptions and local arrangements, consult your Local Spiritual
> Assembly. Feast and Holy Day dates come from the Badi calendar — use an official
> Baha'i calendar or app each year rather than fixed Gregorian dates.

## 1. Obligatory prayer (from age 15)
Choose **one** of the three each day:
| Prayer | When |
|-------|------|
| **Short** | once, between noon and sunset |
| **Medium** | three times: morning, noon, evening, with prescribed movements |
| **Long** | once in 24 hours, at any time, with standing, kneeling and prescribed gestures |
- Perform **ablutions** first: wash the hands and face with the short verses given for each.
- Face the **Qiblih** — the Shrine of Baha'u'llah at Bahji, near Akka, Israel. Use a
  compass app or a fixed bearing for your town.
- Say it alone, in a clean place, in any language (from an authorised translation). It is not
  said congregationally.
- Exemptions include illness, danger, and women during menstruation (who may instead repeat
  a short verse 95 times).
Also recite the Greatest Name, **Allah-u-Abha**, 95 times daily after ablutions; prayer
beads or counting on the fingers are commonly used. Reading the sacred writings morning and
evening is enjoined.

## 2. The Badi calendar
19 months of 19 days, plus 4 or 5 intercalary days (**Ayyam-i-Ha**) before the last month,
giving a solar year that starts at **Naw-Ruz** (the spring equinox as reckoned in Tehran,
about 20–21 March). Days run sunset to sunset.

## 3. The Nineteen Day Feast
Held on (or near) the first day of each Baha'i month, by the local community. Three parts:
1. **Devotional:** readings from the Baha'i writings and prayers (often from the Bab, Baha'u'llah
   and Abdu'l-Baha); music is welcome.
2. **Administrative:** consultation — news from the Local Spiritual Assembly, the community's
   suggestions and questions, recorded and passed to the Assembly.
3. **Social:** food and fellowship.
The administrative portion is for registered Baha'is; guests are generally welcome to the
devotional and social parts or at other gatherings. Hosts rotate; the host prepares readings,
food and a welcoming space.

## 4. The Fast (month of 'Ala, about 2–20 March)
- From sunrise to sunset: no food or drink (and no smoking).
- Applies from 15 to 70 years of age. Exempt: travellers (per conditions), the ill,
  pregnant or nursing women, women in menstruation, those doing heavy labour.
- Get up early for a pre-dawn meal and prayers; many take the time for extra reading and reflection.
- The Fast ends with **Naw-Ruz**, the Baha'i new year — a feast day.

## 5. Holy days
Eleven holy days, on nine of which work should be suspended — among them Naw-Ruz, the
first, ninth and twelfth days of **Ridvan** (late April–early May), the Declaration of the Bab,
the Ascension of Baha'u'llah, the Martyrdom of the Bab and the Twin Holy Birthdays (autumn,
on lunar reckoning). Ask your employer or school ahead for leave.

## Community practice
Many communities run **devotional gatherings**, children's classes and **study circles**
(e.g. the Ruhi sequence) open to all. Contributions to the Baha'i funds are voluntary and
confidential, and only accepted from Baha'is; **Huququllah** is a specific spiritual law on
surplus wealth, paid to designated representatives.

## Gotchas
- Mixing prayers: don't combine parts of the short and long obligatory prayers; choose one.
- Twin Holy Birthdays and some other days move each year — check the calendar.
- Saying the obligatory prayer facing Mecca or another direction by habit.

## Verify

- [ ] Allah-u-Abha repeated 95 times.
- [ ] Current year's Feast and Holy Day dates in your calendar.
- [ ] Fast observed sunrise to sunset in 'Ala unless exempt.
