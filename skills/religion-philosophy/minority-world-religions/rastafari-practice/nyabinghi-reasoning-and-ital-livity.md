# Nyabinghi, reasoning and ital livity

**Goal:** take part in Rastafari devotional life — the Nyabinghi gathering, reasoning,
ital diet and the holy days — in the way the community itself practises it, as a
**livity** (way of life) rather than a set of beliefs alone.

> Draft. Rastafari has no central authority; practice varies across the **mansions**
> (houses) — notably the **Nyabinghi** (House of Nyabinghi), **Bobo Ashanti**
> (Ethiopia Africa Black International Congress) and the **Twelve Tribes of Israel** —
> and among unaffiliated Rastas. Ganja use as a sacrament is subject to local law, which
> varies (Jamaica's 2015 reform allows sacramental use by Rastafari; many countries do not);
> nothing here is legal advice. Follow the elders of your house.

## 1. Livity basics
- **Jah** (God) and the divinity or special role of **Haile Selassie I** (Emperor of
  Ethiopia) are central, interpreted differently across houses; Marcus Garvey is honoured
  as a prophet.
- Daily Bible reading (KJV is common); the Twelve Tribes encourage reading a chapter a day,
  through the Bible.
- **Language:** "I-and-I" for the self and community, emphasising the divine within each
  person; "overstanding" etc. Use it naturally if it is yours.
- **Dreadlocks:** understood by many as part of the **Nazarite vow** (Numbers 6) — no razor
  on the head. Not all Rastas wear them, and wearing them does not make one Rasta.
- Colours: red, gold and green (often with black).

## 2. The Nyabinghi issembly (binghi / groundation)
A gathering for worship, drumming and chanting, from one night to several days, often around
holy days.
- **Drums:** the **bass** drum keeps a slow, heavy heartbeat pulse; the **funde** keeps a
  steady syncopated pattern; the **repeater (akete)** improvises the lead. New drummers start
  on the funde and learn from elders.
- **Chants:** many adapted from hymns and psalms ("Holy Mount Zion", "Rivers of Babylon" and
  others). Join the chorus; leading is for the experienced.
- A tabernacle (tent/shelter) and fire are kept; elders open and close with prayer; dress is
  modest, women often in long skirts with heads covered (especially in Nyabinghi and Bobo
  houses).

## 3. Reasoning
A structured, respectful discussion of scripture, history, Africa and daily life, often
seated in a circle.
- Speak one at a time; let elders open; give "a word" rather than a lecture.
- Where the **chalice** (sacramental pipe) or spliff is used, it is blessed with a prayer
  before lighting and passed in a set direction; it is a sacrament, not recreation.
  Declining is acceptable.

## 4. Ital
**Ital** ("vital") food is natural, whole, and as unprocessed as possible:
- Mostly or wholly vegetarian/vegan; no pork in any house; many avoid shellfish and
  scavenger fish.
- Little or no **salt** (especially no table salt), no additives, no alcohol.
- Cooked in clay or wood rather than aluminium by some; grown locally where possible.
Typical dishes: ital stew (vegetables, coconut milk, pimento, thyme, scotch bonnet),
callaloo, run-down, roasted breadfruit.

## 5. Holy days
| Date | Observance |
|------|------------|
| 7 January | Ethiopian Christmas |
| 21 April | **Grounation Day** — Haile Selassie's visit to Jamaica, 1966 |
| 25 May | African Liberation Day |
| 23 July | Haile Selassie's birthday |
| 17 August | Marcus Garvey's birthday |
| 2 November | Coronation Day (1930) |
Most are marked with Nyabinghi issemblies.

## Gotchas
- Treating Rastafari as a fashion or music genre; dreadlocks and colours carry meaning.
- Assuming one house's rules (e.g. Bobo Ashanti's distinctive turbans and robes,
  sabbath and purity rules) apply to all.
- Bringing alcohol, meat or salted food to an issembly.

## Verify
- [ ] You know which house's customs apply at the gathering you attend.
- [ ] Drum and chant roles learned from elders before leading.
- [ ] Reasoning: one voice at a time, elders open; sacrament only where lawful and offered.
- [ ] Ital meal prepared without salt, meat or additives.
