# Lalish pilgrimage and Yazidi rites

**Goal:** observe the main rites of the Yazidi (Ezidi) faith — daily prayer, the
life-cycle rites tied to Lalish, and the feasts of the year — and behave correctly as
a pilgrim or invited guest at the sanctuary.

> Draft. Yazidi religious knowledge is transmitted orally through sacred hymns
> (*qewl*) sung by the **qewal** class, and practice varies between communities in
> Iraq (Sinjar/Shingal, Sheikhan), Syria, Turkey, Armenia, Georgia and the diaspora
> (notably Germany). Dates follow the community's own (Julian-based) calendar.
> Many communities are still recovering from the 2014 genocide; diaspora practice has
> adapted, e.g. by holding rites in local community centres. Check details with a
> sheikh or pir of your community.

## 1. Roles every Yazidi holds
Society is divided into three endogamous groups: **sheikhs**, **pirs** and **murids**
(laity). Every Yazidi has, by heredity, an assigned **sheikh** and **pir** who perform
their rites and to whom they owe respect and dues, and chooses a **brother or sister of
the hereafter** (*birayê axretê*), from a sheikh family, who stands by them in this life and
the next. Know who your sheikh, pir and hereafter-sibling are before any rite.

## 2. Daily devotion
- Prayers (*dua*) are said at **sunrise and sunset**, facing the sun, which is honoured as
  a sign of the divine light.
- Wednesday is the holy day of the week; customs for it (e.g. resting from some work) vary by community — unconfirmed in detail here.
- Respectful conduct toward the sacred: thresholds are kissed, not stepped on, at shrines;
  fire and water are not polluted.

## 3. Rites tied to Lalish
**Lalish**, in the Sheikhan valley of Iraqi Kurdistan, holds the tomb of Sheikh Adi and is
the faith's holiest place; Yazidis aim to visit at least once in life.
- **Mor kirin (baptism):** the child (and, in diaspora, sometimes adults) is baptised with
  water from **Kaniya Spî**, the White Spring at Lalish, by a sheikh or pir. Water may be
  brought abroad for families who cannot travel.
- **Bisk:** a boy's first haircut, by the brother of the hereafter, with locks kept.
- **Pilgrim etiquette at Lalish:**
  1. Remove shoes and walk **barefoot** throughout the sanctuary area.
  2. **Do not step on thresholds**; step over and kiss them.
  3. In the shrine, tie a knot in the coloured silk cloths and untie one tied by another
     pilgrim — a wish made and one fulfilled.
  4. Try to throw a folded cloth onto a sacred stone ledge — landing it is good fortune.
  5. Dress modestly; ask before photographing people or inner spaces.

## 4. The feasts of the year
| Feast | When (approx.) | Practice |
|-------|----------------|----------|
| **Çarşema Sor / Sere Sal** (Red Wednesday, New Year) | first Wednesday of Nisan in the Yazidi (Julian) calendar, mid-April | eggs dyed bright colours, flowers (red) placed over doorways, visits to graves, candles lit at Lalish |
| **Cejna Cemaiya** (Feast of the Assembly) | about 6–13 October | the great annual gathering at Lalish: processions, the sacrifice of a bull, qewl recitation |
| **Rojiyên Êzî** (Fast of Ezi) | three days in December, followed by a feast | fasting from dawn to sunset |
| Other fasts and feasts | through the year | some fasts are kept mainly by religious specialists |

## 5. Death
Burial is quick; the body faces east. A sheikh and the hereafter-sibling take part; a
**berat** (small ball of clay from Lalish) is often placed with the deceased.

## Gotchas
- Conversion into the faith is not practised; marriage is within the community and group.
- Exact feast dates shift with the Julian-based calendar — confirm locally each year.
- Mislabelling Tawûsî Melek with outsiders' slanders is deeply offensive; use the community's own terms.
