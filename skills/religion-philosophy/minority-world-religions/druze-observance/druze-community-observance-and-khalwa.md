# Druze community observance and the khalwa

**Goal:** understand and take part respectfully in the observable religious life of
Druze (al-Muwahhidun, "the unitarians") communities — as a community member who is
not (yet) initiated, as a family member, or as a guest.

> Draft, and deliberately limited. The Druze faith is esoteric: its scriptures (the
> *Rasa'il al-Hikma*, Epistles of Wisdom) and doctrine are revealed only to initiated
> members, and the community does not accept converts. This page describes only
> what is openly practised and visible. It cannot, and should not, describe the
> content of initiated worship. Local customs in Lebanon, Syria, Israel and Jordan differ.

## 1. Two circles of the community
- **Uqqal / ajaweed** ("the knowledgeable"): men and women who have asked for and been
  admitted to religious knowledge after a period of tested conduct. They study the
  scriptures, attend the full religious gatherings, and keep strict rules of life.
- **Juhhal** ("the uninitiated"): the majority. They are fully Druze by birth, but do not
  have access to the scriptures. Their religious life centres on good conduct, community
  and family, and on respect for the uqqal.
Becoming an uqqal is a personal decision, usually in adulthood; the request is made to the
religious leaders of the local khalwa, who judge readiness by conduct over time.

## 2. Conduct expected of the religious
Uqqal typically:
- Dress distinctively: men a black or dark robe (or plain dark clothes) with a white
  turban or white cap, often a moustache and beard; women a long dark dress and a white
  head veil (**mandil**) that can cover the mouth.
- Abstain from alcohol, tobacco and pork; speak truthfully; avoid frivolity, music
  and entertainment considered unsuitable; keep strict honesty in trade.
- Many follow particular rules on food and accepting hospitality.
Core values stressed for all Druze include truthfulness, protecting fellow believers,
and accepting God's will; these are widely cited publicly.

## 3. The khalwa and the Thursday evening gathering
- The **khalwa** (or **majlis**) is a plain prayer and meeting house, without images or
  ornament; larger khalwat are also retreat centres in the mountains.
- The weekly gathering is on **Thursday evening** (the eve of Friday). The first part,
  with general religious exhortation, may be open to all community members; the uninitiated
  then leave, and the uqqal continue with study and devotion.
- Men and women sit in separate sections, often separated by a curtain.
- If you are a juhhal community member attending: dress very modestly, sit where shown,
  stay silent, and leave without question when asked. Outsiders should attend only by invitation.

## 4. Feasts and shrines
- **Eid al-Adha** is the principal festival: visiting family, honouring elders, giving
  to the needy, and visits to graves; the uqqal observe a period of devotion before it.
  Druze do not keep the Ramadan fast or the hajj.
- **Ziyara** (visit) to the shrines (maqamat) of prophets and saints honoured by the
  community — e.g. the tomb of **Nabi Shu'ayb** near Tiberias, with a major annual visit
  in late April in Israel. Shrines are kept by families or the community; remove shoes,
  dress modestly, and leave a donation if customary.

## 5. Funerals and condolence
- Funerals are prompt; the religious lead the prayers. Women and men often gather separately.
- Visitors come to offer condolence over several days; bring nothing elaborate, sit,
  offer brief words, accept coffee, and leave after a short time.
- Belief in reincarnation (the soul passing immediately to a new body) shapes the tone of
  mourning; follow the family's lead.

## Gotchas
- Asking an uqqal to explain the scriptures — this is not discussed with outsiders or juhhal.
- Treating Druze practice as a branch of mainstream Islam; the community defines itself distinctly.
- Photographing religious elders, khalwat or women without explicit permission.

## Verify
- [ ] You know whether you are attending as initiated, uninitiated member or guest, and what is open to you.
- [ ] Modest dress; seating and departure from the khalwa as directed.
- [ ] Eid al-Adha visits and condolence calls kept brief and family-led.
- [ ] No requests for, or attempts to obtain, the closed scriptures.
