# Walking the Camino de Santiago — route, credencial, Compostela, and the inner journey

**Goal:** walk (or cycle) to the tomb of St James in Santiago de Compostela as a
Christian pilgrimage, meeting the Cathedral's requirements for the **Compostela**
certificate and giving the journey a spiritual shape, not only a hiking one. Rules are
set by the Pilgrim's Office in Santiago and occasionally change — **check its current
published requirements before you go**.

## 1. Choose a route and a starting point
| Route | Classic start | Approx. distance | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Camino Francés | Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port | ~780 km, 30–35 days | Busiest, best infrastructure. **Sarria** (~115 km) is the most popular start for the minimum. |
| Camino Portugués | Porto (~240 km) or Tui (~115 km) | 10–12 days from Porto | Coastal and central variants. |
| Camino del Norte | Irún | ~820 km | Coastal, hillier, quieter. |
| Camino Primitivo | Oviedo | ~320 km | Mountainous; the oldest documented route. |
| Camino Inglés | Ferrol | ~115 km | Starting from A Coruña (~75 km) requires adding distance walked elsewhere — check. |
Plan 20–25 km a day for most walkers; build in a rest day every week or so.

## 2. Get the credencial (pilgrim passport)
1. Obtain it before you start, from the Cathedral's official channels, a pilgrims'
   association or confraternity in your country, or at major starting points (churches,
   albergues, pilgrim offices).
2. It identifies you as a pilgrim and gives access to pilgrim **albergues** (hostels).
3. Collect **sellos** (stamps) from albergues, churches, cafés and town halls along the way:
   at least **one a day**, and **two a day for the last 100 km** (cycling: the last 200 km),
   with dates.

## 3. Compostela requirements (as commonly published — verify)
- Walk at least the **last 100 km** to Santiago (on foot or horseback), or cycle at least the
  **last 200 km**; sailing pilgrims have special rules.
- The distance must be continuous and end in Santiago; walking 100 km elsewhere and taking
  a bus to the city does not count.
- Undertaken with a **religious or spiritual motivation**. Those walking for other reasons
  may receive a separate certificate of welcome.
- At the **Pilgrim's Office** near the cathedral, present the stamped credencial (online
  pre-registration and a QR queue ticket are now usual). The Compostela is free, in Latin,
  with your name Latinised; a distance certificate is available for a small fee.

## 4. The pilgrimage itself
1. **Pilgrim blessing:** many parishes bless pilgrims before departure, and churches along
   the route (e.g. Roncesvalles) hold evening pilgrim Masses with a blessing.
2. **A daily rhythm:** morning prayer before setting off, a rosary or psalm while walking,
   visiting churches that are open, evening Mass or Vespers where available.
3. **An intention:** many pilgrims carry intentions for others, or a stone to leave at the
   **Cruz de Ferro** on the Francés route.
4. **Arrival:** attend the **Pilgrims' Mass** in the cathedral (times vary; check the
   cathedral's schedule), visit the tomb of St James in the crypt, and embrace the
   apostle's statue behind the altar. The **botafumeiro** (giant censer) swings on major
   feasts and some other occasions, not daily.
5. **Holy Years:** when 25 July (St James) falls on a Sunday, the Holy Door is opened and a
   plenary indulgence is offered on the usual conditions (confession, Communion, prayer
   for the Pope's intentions). 2027 is such a year — expect crowds.

## Gotchas
- **Blisters and tendons** end more pilgrimages than anything else: walk your boots in
  beforehand, keep a pack under about 10% of body weight, tape hot spots at once.
- Public albergues are first-come and fill early in summer and Holy Years; many don't
  take bookings.
- Stamps from the last 100 km must be clearly dated; missing days cause questions at the office.
- Luggage-transfer services are fine for the Compostela; the walking still has to be yours.
- Respect churches as places of worship: quiet, covered shoulders, and no flash during services.

## Verify
- [ ] Your route's final stage covers at least 100 km on foot (200 km by bike) into Santiago.
- [ ] You carry a credencial and have one stamp per day, two per day in the last 100 km.
- [ ] Accommodation strategy fits the season (albergues vs. booked rooms).
- [ ] You know the Pilgrim's Office registration process and the Pilgrims' Mass times.
- [ ] You have a daily practice of prayer and an intention for the journey.
