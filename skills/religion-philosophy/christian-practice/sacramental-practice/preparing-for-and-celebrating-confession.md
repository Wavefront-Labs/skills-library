# Going to confession — preparation, the rite, and what to say

**Goal:** make an individual confession calmly and correctly, knowing what happens at
each step and what you are expected to say, whether it is your first time or your first
in many years. The steps below follow Catholic practice; other traditions are noted at the end.

## 1. Before you go
1. **Find a time.** Most Catholic parishes list confession times in the bulletin or on
   their website (often Saturday afternoon); you can also ask a priest for an appointment.
   Penitential services with many priests are common in Advent and Lent.
2. **Examine your conscience.** Sit quietly and review your life since your last
   confession. Many people use the Ten Commandments, the Beatitudes or a printed
   "examination of conscience" leaflet. Note:
   - **grave (mortal) sins** — Catholic teaching asks that these be confessed by kind and,
     as far as you can remember, by number;
   - venial sins may be confessed too, and regular confession of them is encouraged.
3. **Learn or carry an Act of Contrition.** One widely used form: "My God, I am sorry for
   my sins with all my heart. In choosing to do wrong and failing to do good, I have sinned
   against you whom I should love above all things. I firmly intend, with your help, to do
   penance, to sin no more, and to avoid whatever leads me to sin. Our Saviour Jesus Christ
   suffered and died for us. In his name, my God, have mercy." Any sincere prayer of sorrow
   in your own words is acceptable.

## 2. The rite (Catholic, individual confession)
1. Choose **behind the screen** (anonymous) or **face to face**; both are permitted.
2. Make the Sign of the Cross. The priest may greet you or read a short scripture.
3. Begin: **"Bless me, Father, for I have sinned. It has been [time] since my last
   confession."** If it is your first confession or you have been away a long time, say so —
   the priest will help.
4. Confess your sins plainly and briefly. End with something like "For these and all my
   sins, I am sorry."
5. Listen to any counsel and accept the **penance** (usually prayers or an action).
6. Pray the **Act of Contrition** when invited.
7. The priest extends his hand and gives **absolution**, ending: "…I absolve you from your
   sins in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit." Answer **"Amen."**
8. He may dismiss you with "Give thanks to the Lord, for he is good"; answer "His mercy
   endures for ever." Leave and do your penance promptly.

## 3. Other traditions
- **Orthodox:** confession usually happens in the church before an icon of Christ or the
  Gospel book, with the priest standing beside the penitent; at the absolution prayer the
  priest lays his stole (epitrachelion) over the penitent's head. Practice of how often to
  confess before Communion differs between jurisdictions — ask your priest.
- **Anglican/Episcopal:** private confession is available but optional ("all may, none must,
  some should"); the Book of Common Prayer 1979 has the "Reconciliation of a Penitent" rite.
- **Lutheran:** individual confession and absolution is retained in the Small Catechism,
  though general confession at the Sunday service is the more common practice today.

## Gotchas
- **The seal is absolute** in Catholic law: the priest may never reveal what is confessed.
- You do not need to give long background stories or name other people.
- Forgotten sins are forgiven with the rest; mention a grave one next time if you recall it.
- Catholics must confess grave sins at least once a year (traditionally around Easter) and
  should not receive Communion with unconfessed grave sin; check details with a priest.
- Language: if your parish offers confessions in several languages, the schedule says which.

## Verify
- [ ] You have examined your conscience and know which sins you will name.
- [ ] You can say the opening line and how long since your last confession.
- [ ] You have an Act of Contrition ready (memorised, on a card, or in your own words).
- [ ] You responded "Amen" to the absolution and know what your penance is.
- [ ] Penance completed soon after leaving.
