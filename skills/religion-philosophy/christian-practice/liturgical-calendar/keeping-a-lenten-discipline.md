# Keeping Lent — the rules, the season's shape, and a personal plan you'll keep

**Goal:** know exactly which days carry obligations in your tradition, who they bind, and
build a realistic personal discipline of prayer, fasting and almsgiving (Matthew 6:1–18)
that lasts from Ash Wednesday to Easter. Rules below are general; bishops' conferences
and dioceses adjust them, so **check your diocese or parish** each year.

## 1. The shape of the season (Western churches)
- **Ash Wednesday** opens Lent, 46 days before Easter Sunday (40 days of fasting plus the six
  Sundays, which are not counted as fast days).
- **Sundays of Lent:** the Gloria and Alleluia are dropped at Mass; violet vestments, with
  rose permitted on the Fourth Sunday (Laetare).
- **Holy Week** begins on Palm Sunday. In Catholic reckoning Lent ends before the evening
  Mass of the Lord's Supper on Holy Thursday, which begins the **Easter Triduum** (Holy
  Thursday evening, Good Friday, Holy Saturday/Easter Vigil, Easter Sunday).
- Easter falls on the Sunday after the first ecclesiastical full moon on or after 21 March,
  so Lent moves each year; look up the dates early.

## 2. Catholic obligations (Latin Church; check local norms)
1. **Abstinence from meat:** from age **14** on Ash Wednesday, Good Friday and all Fridays of
   Lent. Fish, eggs and dairy are allowed. If a solemnity (e.g. St Joseph, 19 March, or the
   Annunciation, 25 March) falls on a Friday, abstinence does not bind.
2. **Fasting:** from age **18 until the 59th birthday is complete** (i.e. through age 59), on Ash
   Wednesday and Good Friday. In US practice this means one full meal, plus two smaller
   meals that together do not equal a full meal; no snacking between.
3. **Exemptions:** illness, pregnancy, breastfeeding, heavy manual labour, and other real
   need excuse; nobody is obliged to endanger health. A pastor can dispense.
4. **Paschal fast:** keeping the Good Friday fast through Holy Saturday until the Vigil is
   encouraged but not required.
5. **Eucharistic fast (all year):** no food or drink, except water and medicine, for **one
   hour before receiving Communion**. The elderly, sick and their carers are exempt.
6. **Easter duty:** receive Communion at least once in the Easter season, and confess grave
   sins at least yearly.

## 3. Other traditions
- **Orthodox Great Lent** begins on **Clean Monday**, with a stricter traditional fast:
  no meat, dairy, eggs or fish with backbones; wine and oil traditionally limited on
  weekdays. Practice varies a great deal by jurisdiction and household — follow your
  priest's guidance. Orthodox Pascha is usually on a different date from Western Easter.
- **Anglican/Episcopal:** the BCP 1979 names Ash Wednesday and Good Friday as fast days and
  the weekdays of Lent as days of special devotion; the content of the fast is left to the
  individual.
- **Lutheran, Methodist and many other Protestant churches** observe Ash Wednesday (often
  with imposition of ashes) and Holy Week services; fasting is voluntary.

## 4. Build your personal plan
1. Choose one practice under each heading, specific and measurable:
   - **Prayer:** e.g. daily Mass once a week, the Stations of the Cross on Fridays, 15
     minutes of scripture each morning.
   - **Fasting:** give up one real attachment (alcohol, social media after 9 pm, snacks).
   - **Almsgiving:** set an amount or a cause; redirect what the fast saves.
2. Write it down on Shrove Tuesday and tell one person.
3. Mark the calendar: Ash Wednesday, Fridays, a confession date, Holy Week services.

## Gotchas
- Whether Sundays "don't count" for personal sacrifices is popular custom, not a rule.
- Meat-based broths and gravies: strict and lenient views exist; follow your pastor.
- Outside Lent, Catholic Fridays are still penitential days; many conferences allow
  another penance in place of abstinence.
- A plan too ambitious to keep by the second week is the most common failure.

## Verify
- [ ] You know this year's dates of Ash Wednesday, Palm Sunday and Easter (or Pascha).
- [ ] You know whether fasting and abstinence bind you by age and health.
- [ ] Every Friday of Lent is marked.
- [ ] Your plan has one concrete prayer, fasting and almsgiving practice.
- [ ] Holy Week services are in your calendar.
