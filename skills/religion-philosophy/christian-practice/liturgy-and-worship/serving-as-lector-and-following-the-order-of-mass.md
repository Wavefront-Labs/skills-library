# Serving as lector at Mass — know the order, find the right page, proclaim clearly

**Goal:** read the scripture at a Catholic Mass so the assembly hears it clearly, at
the right moment, with the right words before and after — without hunting for pages
or guessing where to stand. Local customs vary a lot; your parish's liturgy
coordinator has the final word on movements and who does what.

## 1. Know where you are in the Order of Mass
The Roman Rite Mass has four parts. The lector's work sits in the second.
1. **Introductory Rites** — entrance procession, Sign of the Cross, greeting,
   Penitential Act, Kyrie, Gloria (Sundays outside Advent and Lent, solemnities,
   feasts), Collect (opening prayer). Sit after the Collect's "Amen".
2. **Liturgy of the Word** —
   - First Reading (usually Old Testament; from Acts during the Easter season)
   - Responsorial Psalm (often sung by a cantor; if not, the lector may read it)
   - Second Reading (Sundays and solemnities; from the New Testament letters or Revelation)
   - Gospel Acclamation (Alleluia; replaced by another acclamation in Lent)
   - Gospel — read only by a deacon or priest, never the lector
   - Homily, Profession of Faith (Creed), Universal Prayer (Prayers of the Faithful)
3. **Liturgy of the Eucharist** — preparation of the gifts, Eucharistic Prayer, Communion Rite.
4. **Concluding Rites** — blessing and dismissal.

## 2. Find the right readings
- Sundays run on a **three-year cycle**: Year A (Matthew), Year B (Mark), Year C (Luke);
  John fills parts of Lent and Easter. Weekdays use a two-year cycle (Year I / Year II)
  for the first reading. A Catholic liturgical calendar (an "Ordo") or your bishops'
  conference website gives the day's lectionary number.
- Check for a solemnity or feast that displaces the Sunday, and for **long and short
  forms** of a reading — ask which one the celebrant wants.
- Practise aloud at least twice, in the translation your country uses (in the US the
  Lectionary uses the NABRE-based text; elsewhere it differs). Look up how to say
  difficult names in advance.

## 3. Proclaiming
1. Go to the ambo after the assembly is seated and settled. Bow to the altar on the way if
   that is your parish custom.
2. Begin with the heading as printed: **"A reading from the Book of the Prophet Isaiah."**
   Do not say "The first reading is…", and do not read the italic summary line or the
   citation (chapter and verse).
3. Read slower than feels natural, look up at the ends of sentences, and let commas
   breathe. Microphones help volume but not pace.
4. After the last verse, pause, look up, and say **"The word of the Lord."** The people
   reply **"Thanks be to God."** (2011 English translation.)
5. Keep a few seconds of silence before the psalm begins — the GIRM explicitly asks for
   moments of silence in the Liturgy of the Word.
6. If you also read the psalm, read the response once, signal the people to repeat it,
   then the verses with the response after each.
7. Where there is no deacon, the lector may carry the **Book of the Gospels** (not the
   Lectionary) in the entrance procession, place it on the altar, and may announce the
   intentions of the Universal Prayer. The priest says the opening and closing prayers.

## Gotchas
- The Lectionary stays at the ambo; the Book of the Gospels is the only book carried in
  procession.
- The Gloria and Alleluia are absent in Lent; Advent omits the Gloria — don't be thrown
  when your cue changes.
- Some books in the Lectionary print the response to the psalm only once; know it by heart.
- Holy days, vigils and the Easter Vigil (up to seven Old Testament readings) have their
  own pages and often a rota of several readers.
- Dress modestly and arrive 15–20 minutes early to check the book is open at the right
  ribbon and the microphone is on.

## Verify
- [ ] You know the lectionary number and which form (long/short) you are reading.
- [ ] You have read the passage aloud twice and checked every proper name.
- [ ] You start with "A reading from…" and do not read citations or summaries.
- [ ] You close with "The word of the Lord" and pause before the psalm.
- [ ] You know whether you also read the psalm, carry the Gospel book, or read the intentions.
