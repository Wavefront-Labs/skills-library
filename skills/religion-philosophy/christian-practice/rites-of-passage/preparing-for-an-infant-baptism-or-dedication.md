# Infant baptism or dedication — booking it, choosing sponsors, knowing the rite

**Goal:** take a family from "we'd like the baby baptised" (or "dedicated") to a
well-prepared celebration: the right conversation with the parish, eligible godparents,
paperwork, and everyone knowing what happens and what they will be asked to say.
Requirements vary by parish and diocese — **always ask your church office first**.

## 1. Which rite does your church celebrate?
- **Infant baptism:** Catholic, Orthodox, Anglican/Episcopal, Lutheran, Methodist,
  Presbyterian and Reformed churches baptise infants on the faith of parents and sponsors.
- **Dedication:** Baptist, many Pentecostal and other evangelical churches practise
  **believer's baptism** for those old enough to profess faith themselves, and offer a
  service of **infant dedication** for babies instead — a blessing and parental pledge,
  with no water.

## 2. Practical preparation
1. **Contact the parish** early (6–8 weeks is typical). Usually at least one parent should
   be a registered or practising member; if you live elsewhere, your home parish may need
   to give permission.
2. **Attend the preparation session** most parishes require for parents (and sometimes
   godparents) — often one or two evenings.
3. **Bring documents:** the child's birth certificate; for godparents, some Catholic
   parishes ask for a sponsor certificate from the godparent's own parish.
4. **Choose godparents/sponsors.** In Catholic canon law: at least one, and at most two
   (one male and one female); aged at least 16; Catholic, confirmed, having received First
   Communion, living a life of faith; not the child's parent. A baptised non-Catholic
   Christian may stand as a "Christian witness" alongside a Catholic godparent. Anglican
   practice traditionally asks for three (two of the same sex as the child), but this
   varies — check.
5. **Decide the date** — many parishes baptise during or after Sunday Mass on set weekends;
   Easter Vigil and the Baptism of the Lord are traditional days.
6. **Bring:** a white garment (or the parish provides one), a baptismal candle if not
   supplied, and a towel.

## 3. The order of the rite (Catholic, outline)
1. **Reception at the door:** the celebrant asks the child's name and what the parents ask
   ("Baptism"); parents and godparents accept their responsibility; the child is signed
   with the cross on the forehead.
2. **Liturgy of the Word:** readings, homily, intercessions, litany of saints (often short).
3. **Prayer of exorcism and anointing** with the oil of catechumens (may be omitted in some places).
4. **Blessing of water; renunciation of sin and profession of faith** — parents and
   godparents answer "I do" to each question.
5. **Baptism:** by immersion or by pouring water three times, with the Trinitarian formula
   (Matthew 28:19): **"N., I baptise you in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of
   the Holy Spirit."**
6. **Explanatory rites:** anointing with **chrism** on the crown of the head, clothing with
   the **white garment**, the **candle** lit from the Paschal candle and given to a parent or
   godparent, and optionally the Ephphatha (touching ears and mouth).
7. **Conclusion:** procession to the altar, Lord's Prayer, blessing of the mother, father and all.

## 4. Variations
- **Orthodox:** baptism is usually by triple full immersion, followed immediately by
  **chrismation** and, at the next Liturgy, Holy Communion; the formula is said in the third
  person: "The servant of God N. is baptised in the name of the Father…". One godparent of
  the child's sex is common.
- **Anglican, Lutheran, Methodist:** usually within the main Sunday service; the whole
  congregation promises to support the child.
- **Dedication (Baptist and others):** parents bring the child forward, promise to raise
  them in the faith, the congregation pledges support, and the pastor prays a blessing.

## Gotchas
- **Emergency baptism:** in danger of death, any person (even not a Christian) may baptise
  by pouring water and saying the Trinitarian formula; inform the parish afterwards so it is recorded.
- The formula and water must go together; "christening" names alone are not baptism.
- Photography rules differ; ask before the day.
- Most churches recognise any baptism with water and the Trinitarian formula and will not
  rebaptise; keep the **baptismal certificate** — it is needed for marriage and other sacraments later.

## Verify
- [ ] The church office has confirmed the date, preparation session and required documents.
- [ ] Godparents (or sponsors) meet your church's criteria and have any certificates needed.
- [ ] Parents and godparents know the promises they will make.
- [ ] You know whether the child will be immersed or have water poured, and have clothing/towel ready.
- [ ] A baptismal certificate (or dedication record) was issued afterwards.
