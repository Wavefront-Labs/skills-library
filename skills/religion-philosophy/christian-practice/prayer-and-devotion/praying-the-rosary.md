# Praying the rosary — the bead map, the mysteries, the prayers

**Goal:** pray a full rosary (five decades, about 15–20 minutes) without losing your
place, choosing the right set of mysteries and keeping the vocal prayers as a frame for
meditating on scenes from the life of Christ and Mary. This is Catholic practice; some
Anglicans and Lutherans use different prayer-bead patterns (see Gotchas).

## 1. The bead map
A standard rosary is a loop of five **decades** (groups of ten small beads, each
separated by one larger bead) joined to a short pendant with a crucifix, one large bead,
three small beads and another large bead.

## 2. Opening (on the pendant)
1. **On the crucifix:** Sign of the Cross, then the **Apostles' Creed**.
2. **First large bead:** the **Our Father**.
3. **Three small beads:** three **Hail Marys**, traditionally for an increase of faith, hope and charity.
4. **Next space or large bead:** the **Glory Be** ("Glory be to the Father, and to the Son,
   and to the Holy Spirit, as it was in the beginning, is now, and ever shall be, world
   without end. Amen.").

## 3. Each of the five decades
1. **Announce the mystery** ("The first Joyful Mystery: the Annunciation"). A short
   scripture verse may be read.
2. **Large bead:** Our Father.
3. **Ten small beads:** ten Hail Marys, holding the mystery in mind rather than the words.
4. **After the tenth:** Glory Be. Many add the **Fatima prayer**: "O my Jesus, forgive us
   our sins, save us from the fires of hell, lead all souls to heaven, especially those in
   most need of thy mercy."

## 4. The mysteries and their days
Following the 2002 scheme of *Rosarium Virginis Mariae*:
| Set | Days | The five mysteries |
|---|---|---|
| Joyful | Monday, Saturday | Annunciation; Visitation; Nativity; Presentation in the Temple; Finding in the Temple |
| Sorrowful | Tuesday, Friday | Agony in the Garden; Scourging; Crowning with Thorns; Carrying of the Cross; Crucifixion |
| Glorious | Wednesday, Sunday | Resurrection; Ascension; Descent of the Holy Spirit; Assumption; Coronation of Mary |
| Luminous | Thursday | Baptism of Jesus; Wedding at Cana; Proclamation of the Kingdom; Transfiguration; Institution of the Eucharist |

Common seasonal variation: Joyful on Sundays of Advent and Christmas, Sorrowful on Sundays of Lent.

## 5. Closing
1. **Hail, Holy Queen** (Salve Regina).
2. Often the versicle "Pray for us, O holy Mother of God" / "That we may be made worthy of
   the promises of Christ", and the closing collect ("O God, whose only-begotten Son…").
3. Optional prayers for the Pope's intentions (an Our Father, Hail Mary, Glory Be) — part
   of the conditions for the rosary indulgence.
4. Sign of the Cross.

## 6. Praying in a group
A leader says the first half of each prayer and everyone answers with the second half
("Holy Mary, Mother of God…" for the Hail Mary; "Give us this day…" for the Our Father;
"As it was in the beginning…" for the Glory Be). Leaders often rotate by decade. Parishes
commonly pray the rosary before weekday Mass, at wakes, and through October (the month of the rosary).

## Gotchas
- You can pray one decade or one set of mysteries; the full 20-mystery round is not required daily.
- Lost count? Fingers work; the beads are an aid, not a rule.
- Since 2002, "five decades" means one set of mysteries — older books speak of 15-decade rosaries.
- Wording of the Fatima prayer and Hail Holy Queen varies slightly between countries and
  booklets; follow what your group uses.
- **Anglican prayer beads** (33 beads in "weeks" of seven) and the Lutheran "Pearls of
  Life" are separate devotions without the Marian prayers — don't conflate them.
