# Lectio divina — slow, prayerful reading of a short scripture passage

**Goal:** read a few verses of the Bible not for information but as prayer — letting one
word or phrase speak, responding to God, and resting in silence — in a repeatable shape
you can do alone each day or lead for a small group. The practice comes from Benedictine
monasticism and is now used widely by Catholics, Anglicans and many Protestants.

## 1. Prepare
1. **Pick a short passage** — 5 to 15 verses. Good sources: the Gospel for the coming
   Sunday or today's Mass readings (from a lectionary app or missal), a psalm, or a
   continuous reading through one Gospel. Narratives and psalms suit beginners better than
   dense argument (e.g. Romans).
2. **Set time and place:** 20–30 minutes, a quiet spot, phone silenced. Use a physical
   Bible or printout so you are not pulled into notifications.
3. **Settle:** a few slow breaths and a brief prayer asking for openness, e.g. "Speak,
   Lord, for your servant is listening" (1 Samuel 3:10).

## 2. The steps (Guigo II's "ladder", plus the modern fifth rung)
1. **Lectio — read.** Read the passage slowly, ideally aloud and more than once. Notice the
   word or phrase that catches your attention. Stop there; you are not trying to cover the text.
2. **Meditatio — reflect.** Repeat that word or phrase gently. Ask: why does it stand out?
   What does it touch in my life today? Imagination is welcome — place yourself in the scene.
3. **Oratio — respond in prayer.** Speak to God honestly about what came up: thanks,
   confession, a request, a question. Words can be few.
4. **Contemplatio — rest.** Let go of words and thinking; remain quietly in God's presence
   for a few minutes. When thoughts wander, return with the word from step 1.
5. **Actio — carry it out** (added in many modern presentations). Choose one small concrete
   response for today, or carry the phrase with you.

The steps are a guide, not a timer: people often move back and forth between them.

## 3. Leading group lectio (about 40 minutes, 4–10 people)
1. Open with a brief prayer and explain the shape so nobody feels put on the spot.
2. **First reading** (one voice, slowly). Silence 1–2 minutes. Each person shares
   **only a single word or phrase**, no commentary.
3. **Second reading** (a different voice). Silence 3 minutes. Prompt: "Where does this touch
   your life today?" Share briefly; others listen without responding or correcting.
4. **Third reading.** Silence 3 minutes. Prompt: "What is God inviting you to do or be this
   week?" Share, then each person prays briefly for the person on their right.
5. Close with the Lord's Prayer or a Glory Be.
Sharing is always optional — "I'll pass" is fine.

## Gotchas
- This is not Bible study. Background reading and commentaries are valuable but belong at
  another time; don't let a lectio group turn into a debate about interpretation.
- Short beats long: a whole chapter makes it hard to linger.
- Dryness is normal. Faithfulness to the time matters more than a "result".
- Keep a notebook of the phrases you received; patterns over weeks are revealing.
- Different authors label and number the stages differently (four or five, sometimes with
  "silencio" first); the substance is the same.

## Verify
- [ ] The passage was short enough to read aloud at least twice.
- [ ] You stayed with one word or phrase rather than analysing the whole text.
- [ ] There was spoken or silent prayer in response, and some time of wordless rest.
- [ ] You left with one concrete response or phrase to carry.
- [ ] In a group, everyone heard three readings with silence, and sharing stayed brief and uncorrected.
