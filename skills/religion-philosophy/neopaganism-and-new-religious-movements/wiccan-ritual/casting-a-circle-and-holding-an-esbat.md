# Casting a circle and holding an esbat

**Goal:** conduct a Wiccan ritual — typically a full-moon **esbat** — with a coherent
structure: a cleansed, bounded sacred space, the elements and deities welcomed,
the rite's purpose carried out, and everything closed and released properly.

> Draft. This is a generic outline in the style common to eclectic Wicca and derived
> from British Traditional Wicca. **Gardnerian** and **Alexandrian** covens are initiatory and
> use oath-bound texts that are not public; their exact forms will differ. Quarter
> correspondences below are the common Northern-hemisphere set; many Southern-hemisphere
> practitioners swap North and South (fire/earth). Adapt words to your tradition.

## 1. Before the night
- Decide the purpose: celebration of the moon, a healing working, divination, study.
- Check the **full-moon time** (an almanac or moon app); esbats are usually held the night
  of or nearest the full moon.
- Bathe, ideally with salt or herbs, and wear robes, a ritual outfit or nothing (skyclad)
  per your tradition.
- **Altar** (often in the north or east): Goddess and God candles or images, **athame**
  (black-handled knife, used to direct energy, never to cut physical things), **wand**,
  **chalice** of wine or juice, **pentacle** (disc), bowls of salt and water, incense, cakes
  (bread, biscuits) and the working's items.
- Safety: fire-safe candle holders, nothing hanging above flames, a water jug nearby,
  ventilation for incense; outdoors, check fire bans.

## 2. The rite, step by step
1. **Cleanse the space:** sweep with a besom (broom) widdershins from the centre outward;
   consecrate salt and water and asperge (sprinkle) the circle; cense it.
2. **Cast the circle:** walk **deosil** (sunwise, clockwise in the Northern hemisphere) from
   the east or north, pointing the athame or wand at the boundary, visualising a sphere of
   light, three rounds. Say a casting formula, e.g. *"I conjure thee, O circle of power..."*
   Once cast, people do not walk through the boundary — "cut a door" with the athame and
   seal it again if someone must leave.
3. **Call the quarters:** face each direction in turn, deosil, light its candle and call the
   element: **East — Air, South — Fire, West — Water, North — Earth**.
4. **Invoke the Goddess and God:** by name or title, with spoken invocation. At an esbat the
   Goddess as moon is central; in covens a priestess may perform **Drawing Down the Moon**.
5. **The working:** the purpose of the night — raising energy (chant or dance), spellwork,
   meditation, divination, reading the **Charge of the Goddess**, etc.
6. **Cakes and ale:** bless the cakes and the wine/juice (often by symbolically lowering the
   athame into the chalice — the Great Rite in token), share them, and set aside a portion
   for the earth as an offering.
7. **Thank and dismiss** the Goddess and God, then the quarters in reverse order (North,
   West, South, East), extinguishing each candle.
8. **Open the circle:** walk widdershins, drawing the energy back; declare it open:
   *"The circle is open but unbroken."*
9. **Ground:** eat, drink water, touch the floor or earth.

## 3. Ethics and good practice
- The **Wiccan Rede** — "An it harm none, do what ye will" — and, for many, the **threefold
  law** guide workings; do not work on others without consent.
- Keep a **Book of Shadows** or journal: date, moon phase, purpose, what was done, results.
- In groups: agree consent rules (especially for skyclad work or touch), minors only with
  parental consent, and no alcohol pressure — juice in the chalice is fine.

## Gotchas
- Leaving candles burning after the circle is opened.
- Calling the quarters in the wrong direction for your hemisphere tradition — be consistent.
- Overly long improvised invocations; write and rehearse them.
- Walking through a cast circle without cutting a door.

## Verify
- [ ] Space cleansed, circle cast deosil, quarters called in order.
- [ ] Deities invoked and later thanked; quarters released in reverse.
- [ ] Circle opened; everyone grounded.
- [ ] All flames out; offerings left respectfully outdoors.
- [ ] Journal entry written.
