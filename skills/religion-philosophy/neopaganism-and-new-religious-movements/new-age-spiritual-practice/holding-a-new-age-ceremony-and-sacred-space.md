# Holding a new age ceremony and sacred space

**Goal:** create and hold a well-run ceremonial gathering in contemporary new age
spirituality — an intention or moon circle, a sound or meditation ceremony, a study
group on a channelled teaching — that is welcoming, safe and coherent.

> Draft. "New age" covers a loose, non-institutional field of practices, many
> borrowing from other traditions; practitioners often describe themselves as
> spiritual rather than religious. This page describes common practice. It makes no
> claims about the efficacy of any practice and does not cover health treatment. Where a
> practice originates in a living indigenous or religious tradition, see the gotchas.

## 1. Setting up sacred space
- **Room:** quiet, warm, with mats or chairs in a circle; dim, steady light (LED candles
  where open flames are not allowed); phones off or in a basket.
- **Altar or centrepiece:** a cloth, a candle, seasonal flowers or natural objects, crystals
  or objects participants bring, an image or symbol for the theme. Keep it simple and
  invite people to place something.
- **Clearing the space:** ring a bell or bowl, use sound, open windows, or burn incense or
  locally grown herbs (rosemary, lavender, garden sage). Check for smoke sensitivities and alarms.

## 2. Running an intention or moon circle (60–120 min)
1. **Arrival:** greet each person; explain the shape of the evening and that every part is
   optional.
2. **Opening:** a short grounding — three slow breaths, a body scan or a short guided
   visualisation (5 minutes).
3. **Theme:** new moon = beginnings and intentions; full moon = release and gratitude.
   Offer a short reading or reflection.
4. **Sharing round with a talking piece:** only the person holding the object speaks; others
   listen without comment or advice. Give a time guide ("about two minutes each").
   Passing is always allowed.
5. **Personal practice:** journal prompts (e.g. "What am I calling in?" / "What am I
   releasing?"); write intentions on paper; release papers by safely burning in a fireproof
   bowl or by tearing and composting.
6. **Sound or silence:** singing bowls, chime, a chant, or ten minutes of silence.
7. **Closing:** a final round of one word each; thank the group; blow out candles; tea and
   snacks to help people ground.

## 3. Channelled-teaching study groups
Groups studying texts such as *A Course in Miracles* often read a passage aloud, sit
in silence, then share reflections, and practise the text's daily lessons between meetings.
Facilitate by keeping to the text, rotating reading, and avoiding positioning anyone as a
personal authority over others' experience.

## 4. Safety, consent and ethics
- **Trauma-informed:** offer eyes-open options in meditation; avoid forced touch; tell people
  how to step out; have a co-facilitator who can sit with anyone distressed.
- **Breathwork:** intense breathing can cause dizziness, tingling or panic; screen for
  pregnancy, epilepsy, cardiovascular conditions and psychiatric history, and keep
  sessions gentle unless trained.
- **Ceremonial substances:** do not offer psychoactive substances; even ceremonial-grade
  cacao contains stimulants — screen for heart conditions and MAOI-type medications.
- **Money:** be transparent about fees; never pressure people into further paid courses.
- **Not therapy:** signpost to professional help when someone discloses a crisis.

## Gotchas
- **Appropriation:** sacred rites of living traditions (e.g. sweat lodges, pipe ceremonies,
  Native American "smudging" with white sage) belong to those communities; do not run them
  without being authorised by that community. Use your own or openly shared practices.
- Crowded sharing rounds that run over — set the time per person at the start.
- Open flames near loose fabrics and altar cloths.

## Verify
- [ ] Space prepared, altar simple, smoke and fire risks checked.
- [ ] Shape of the ceremony explained; every part optional.
- [ ] Talking-piece sharing round kept to time; passing allowed.
- [ ] Screening done for breathwork or cacao; no psychoactives offered.
- [ ] Practices borrowed from living traditions checked for permission or replaced.
