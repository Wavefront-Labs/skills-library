# Handfasting, wiccaning and croning — pagan rites of passage

**Goal:** design and conduct the major life-transition ceremonies of modern Paganism
so that they are meaningful to the people involved, recognised where they need to be
(legally), and run smoothly on the day.

> Draft. Forms vary across Wicca, Druidry, Heathenry and eclectic Paganism; each couple
> or family shapes their own. Legal recognition of Pagan weddings depends entirely on
> jurisdiction (e.g. in Scotland authorised Pagan celebrants can conduct legal
> marriages; in England and Wales a separate civil registration is currently needed;
> in the US it depends on state officiant rules). Check the current law where you marry.

## 1. Handfasting (Pagan wedding)
**Planning**
- Decide the term: a **year and a day** (a trial or renewable commitment, in some
  traditions), "for this life", or "for this life and beyond".
- Choose the celebrant (priest/priestess, Druid, or Pagan celebrant) and check whether
  they can solemnise legally; otherwise book the civil registration separately.
- Outdoor site: permission, weather plan, fire rules.
- **Cords:** 3 or more cords (often colours chosen for meaning), about 1.5–2 m each,
  braided or kept separate; or a single long cord or ribbon.

**Outline (in a circle or grove)**
1. Open the sacred space (circle cast or grove opened).
2. Welcome and statement of purpose; deities or ancestors invited if the couple wishes.
3. Questions of intent and the couple's **vows**, written by them.
4. **Binding:** the couple join hands (often right to right and left to left, making the
   infinity shape) and the celebrant wraps the cords loosely, with a blessing per wrap;
   the knot is tied and the hands are slipped out, leaving the knot intact as a keepsake.
5. Exchange of rings or tokens; sharing a cup; the blessing.
6. **Jumping the broom (besom):** together, into the new life.
7. Thanks and closing; feast.
Rehearse the binding — cord wrapping is fiddly under pressure.

## 2. Wiccaning / paganing / naming
A blessing and presentation of a child to the gods, elements and community. It does
**not** commit the child to the religion; the choice is theirs when older.
1. Sacred space opened.
2. Parents present the child and announce the name.
3. The child is presented to each **element** (a feather or breath for air, a candle carried
   safely past for fire, a drop of water, a touch of earth or salt) and blessed.
4. **Guide-parents** (Pagan godparents) make promises of support.
5. Gifts and wishes from guests; close.
Keep it short (20–30 minutes); toddlers' attention runs out.

## 3. Coming of age, croning and saging
- **Coming of age:** for teenagers, often around first menstruation or an agreed age, with
  a challenge, a vigil or a mentoring conversation, and recognition by the community.
- **Croning** (women) and **saging** (men) — or **eldering** for any gender — celebrate
  entering elderhood, often after 50 or at menopause or retirement: the person reflects on
  their life, may "cross a threshold" or step through an arch, receives a crown, staff or
  cloak, and shares wisdom; the community honours them.

## 4. Pagan funerals and memorials
- Many families combine a civil or crematorium service with a Pagan rite: words of
  passing to the Summerlands (or the ancestral halls, for Heathens), the elements
  thanked, a **crossing-over** blessing.
- Natural or woodland burial suits many Pagans; check the provider allows readings and
  candles. Write wishes in advance and give them to next of kin.

## Gotchas
- Assuming a handfasting is legally binding — do the civil part.
- Cords tied tight around hands — keep them loose for the slip-out.
- Open flames near babies' blankets and long sleeves.
- Guests unfamiliar with paganism: a printed order of ceremony or a quick explanation helps.

- [ ] Naming: elements set up safely, guide-parents briefed.
