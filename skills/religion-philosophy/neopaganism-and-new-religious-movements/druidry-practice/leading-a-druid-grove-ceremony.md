# Leading a Druid grove ceremony

**Goal:** lead an outdoor seasonal ceremony in the style of modern (revival-derived)
Druidry — one that is welcoming to newcomers, rooted in nature, and has a clear
shape from opening to close.

> Draft. Modern Druidry is diverse. The outline below follows the widely published
> open-ceremony pattern of the **Order of Bards, Ovates and Druids (OBOD)** and similar
> British groups. **ADF (Ár nDraíocht Féin)** uses a different "Core Order of Ritual"
> (summarised in section 4). Groups use their own wording; many texts are inherited from
> the 18th-century revival (e.g. the Gorsedd prayer attributed to Iolo Morganwg).

## 1. Preparation
- **Which festival:** the eight festivals of the wheel of the year — the solstices and
  equinoxes (Alban Arthan, Alban Eilir, Alban Hefin, Alban Elfed in OBOD usage) and the
  four fire festivals (Samhuinn, Imbolc, Beltane, Lughnasadh). See
  `religion-philosophy.neopaganism-and-new-religious-movements.wheel-of-the-year.celebrating-the-eight-sabbats`.
- **Site:** a grove, hilltop, stone circle (respect heritage-site rules — no fires, no
  wax or offerings left on stones) or a garden. Get landowner permission; check fire rules.
- **Roles:** a ceremony leader; four people for the quarters; someone with a sword or staff
  for peace; a bard for a poem or song. Brief newcomers before starting.
- **Kit:** a central fire or candle lantern, a horn or bell, a sword (or staff), seasonal
  symbols, bread and mead/juice, offerings that are biodegradable and safe for wildlife.

## 2. Ceremony outline
1. **Gathering and procession:** participants process to the circle, entering sunwise.
2. **Purification:** the circle is blessed with water and with incense or smoke.
3. **Casting the circle:** the leader walks sunwise, marking the circle with a staff or
   sword, saying words of protection.
4. **Peace to the quarters:** the leader (or quarter officers) calls in each direction —
   *"May there be peace in the East"*, then South, West, North — and all respond, or a
   bearer half-draws the sword and asks *"Is there peace?"* three times, answered *"There
   is peace."*
5. **The Druid's Prayer** (Gorsedd prayer), said together: *"Grant, O God/Goddess/Spirit,
   thy protection; and in protection, strength..."* — groups choose the divine name.
6. **The awen chant:** three long intoned "aaa-ooo-enn" sounds, together — the breath of
   inspiration.
7. **Seasonal rite:** words for the festival, a short drama, lighting or tending the fire,
   blessing seeds or the harvest.
8. **Eisteddfod:** anyone may offer a poem, song, story or tune — a hallmark of Druid gatherings.
9. **Offerings and sharing:** bread and drink blessed and passed; a portion given to the earth.
10. **Thanks and close:** thanks to the spirits of place, ancestors and deities; peace to the
    quarters again or thanks to each direction in reverse; the circle is unwound. A
    closing such as *"We swear by peace and love to stand..."* is common.

## 3. Personal practice alongside
Daily meditation outdoors, studying a tree or bird, journalling, learning **ogham**, and
grove study courses (OBOD's Bardic, Ovate and Druid grades are a correspondence course).

## 4. ADF's core order (contrast)
Opening prayer and offering to the **Earth Mother**; establishing the **Fire, Well and Tree**
(the sacred centre); opening the gates with the **Gatekeeper**; offerings to the **Kindreds**
(gods, ancestors, nature spirits) and the deities of the occasion; taking an **omen**
(often ogham or runes); receiving the blessing (return flow); thanks and closing.

## Gotchas
- Ceremonies that run too long in the cold; aim for 45–60 minutes.
- Leaving tea-light cases, glitter or bread on monuments.
- Insider jargon with newcomers — explain each step briefly as you go.

## Verify
- [ ] Permission and fire rules for the site confirmed.
- [ ] Roles assigned and briefed; written words for the quarters and festival.
- [ ] Peace called, Druid's Prayer and awen chanted together.
- [ ] Eisteddfod time included.
- [ ] Site left with no trace except biodegradable offerings.
