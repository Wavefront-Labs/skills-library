# Leading a vow renewal or commitment ceremony — honouring a bond that already exists

**Goal:** mark an ongoing relationship — 10, 25 or 50 years on, after a hard season,
or a commitment the law doesn't register — with a ceremony that looks back as much as
forward, and that nobody could mistake for a legal wedding.

## 1. Know the legal edge
- A renewal or commitment ceremony has **no legal effect**. Say so in your agreement
  and, gently, in the welcome.
- Don't use a jurisdiction's prescribed marriage words (e.g. Australia's monitum or
  "lawful wedded" vow lines), don't sign anything that looks like a marriage
  certificate, and don't say "by the authority vested in me". In Australia, purporting
  to solemnise a marriage without authority is an offence under the Marriage Act; other
  places have similar rules (jurisdiction-dependent, check the registrar).
- Common reasons for a commitment ceremony: the couple can't or won't marry legally,
  a celebration after a register-office or overseas legal wedding, or relationships the
  law doesn't recognise (e.g. more than two partners). Treat each descriptively and
  without judgement.
- If the couple *are* legally married elsewhere on another day, describe the event as a
  celebration of that marriage, not a second one.

## 2. Consultation (45–75 minutes)
1. What are you marking — an anniversary, a recovery, a milestone, a first public
   ceremony?
2. What has changed since you first made promises? What has held?
3. Which original vows still fit? Which would you rewrite?
4. Who is part of it — children, grandchildren, the original wedding party?
5. Anything from the first wedding to echo — the reading, the music, the dress, the venue?
6. Tone: intimate and private, or a big party with speeches?
7. Hard history (illness, infidelity, loss of a child): acknowledge, allude, or leave out?

## 3. Running order (15–25 minutes)
1. Entrance — often together, or escorted by their children (2 min).
2. Welcome and what this ceremony is (and isn't) (2 min).
3. Their story then and now — a "then / now" contrast works well (4–5 min).
4. Reading or a tribute from a child or friend (2–3 min).
5. Original vows read back — by the celebrant, or a recording from the wedding (1–2 min).
6. **Renewed or commitment vows** (3–4 min).
7. Ring blessing, new rings, or engraving reveal (2 min).
8. Symbolic act — handfasting, a family candle, a time capsule (3 min).
9. Declaration and presentation (1 min).

## 4. Templates
Renewed vow:
```
[NAME], [NUMBER] years ago I promised you [ORIGINAL PROMISE].
I've learned that what that really meant was [WHAT IT TURNED OUT TO MEAN].
Today I promise you again: [NEW PROMISE].
```
Declaration (safe wording):
```
Having heard [NAME A] and [NAME B] renew their promises in front of the people
who love them, I invite you all to celebrate this [NUMBER]th year — and the next.
```
Commitment declaration:
```
[NAME A] and [NAME B] have made their commitment to each other here today.
Please welcome them as the partners they have chosen to be.
```

## Gotchas
- Older couples may have mobility or hearing limits — seat them, and use a good mic.
- Adult children's tributes can drift into roast or grievance; ask for scripts ahead.
- If one partner is more invested than the other, the consultation is where you
  notice. Write vows each can say honestly.
- Don't over-promise "forever" language to couples who've survived a rupture unless
  they ask for it; "every day we choose this" often lands better.
- Commitment ceremonies for couples blocked from legal marriage carry grief as well as
  joy; leave room for it.

## Verify
- [ ] Written agreement states the ceremony is non-legal.
- [ ] Script contains no prescribed legal marriage wording or legal-looking forms.
- [ ] Both partners approved their vows and the story section.
- [ ] Original-wedding elements (readings, recordings) sourced and tested.
- [ ] Ceremony timed aloud within about 25 minutes.
