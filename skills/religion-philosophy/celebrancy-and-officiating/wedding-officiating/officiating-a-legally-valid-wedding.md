# Officiating a legally valid wedding — a ceremony the couple loves that the registrar also accepts

**Goal:** deliver a ceremony that feels personal *and* is legally binding — the right
notice, the right words, the right signatures, returned on time. A beautiful wedding
with a missing legal element is not a marriage.

Everything legal below is **jurisdiction-dependent — check the registrar** (or the
county clerk / registry of births, deaths and marriages) before every wedding. Rules
change, and some examples here may already be out of date.

## 1. First contact: can you legally marry them here?
- Are you authorised where the ceremony takes place (not where you live)?
- Has either party been married before? You will need the divorce order or death
  certificate sighted.
- Ages, names exactly as on ID, any name changes, interpreter needed?
- Location type: some jurisdictions restrict *where* a civil wedding may happen.

## 2. Legal checkpoints by jurisdiction (examples, not a complete list)
- **Australia:** the Notice of Intended Marriage (NOIM) must reach the celebrant at
  least one month and no more than 18 months before the date (shortening needs a
  prescribed authority). Sight birth certificate/passport and photo ID. Both parties
  sign the declaration of no legal impediment before the ceremony. The celebrant must
  say the **monitum** (s46 wording, including "I am duly authorised by law to
  solemnise marriages according to law…"), and each party must say the s45(2) vow
  words: "I call upon the persons here present to witness that I, [FULL NAME], take you,
  [FULL NAME], to be my lawful wedded [wife/husband/spouse]". Two witnesses aged over
  18. Lodge the paperwork with the state/territory registry within 14 days.
- **England & Wales:** notice is given at a register office, normally at least 28
  days before. An independent celebrant cannot legally solemnise; the couple does a
  short register-office ceremony and you lead the celebration. Humanist weddings are
  legal in Scotland, Northern Ireland, Jersey and Guernsey but, as far as I know at time
  of writing, not yet in England & Wales — reform has been debated, so confirm.
- **United States:** state-by-state. The couple gets a licence from the county clerk
  (waiting periods and validity windows vary, e.g. 30–90 days). Some places require
  officiant registration (e.g. New York City, Nevada). The officiant returns the signed
  licence by a deadline — commonly 5–30 days depending on the state. Witness numbers
  vary from none to two.

## 3. Consultation (60–90 minutes)
Ask, and write the answers down:
1. How did you meet? What does marriage mean to you two?
2. Tone: solemn, light, funny, spiritual, religious elements, none?
3. Who should be named or honoured (including people who have died)?
4. Readings, readers, music cues, symbolic act (rings, handfasting, sand)?
5. Vows: written by them, repeat-after-me, or both? Who goes first?
6. Things to avoid: exes, family tensions, words they dislike ("obey", "man and wife").

## 4. Running order (about 20–30 minutes)
1. Processional (3–5 min) — agree the cue word with the musician.
2. Welcome and acknowledgement (2 min).
3. Their story (3–5 min).
4. Reading (2–3 min).
5. **Legal words** — e.g. the Australian monitum (1 min).
6. Vows: legal vow lines + personal vows (3–5 min).
7. Ring exchange (2 min).
8. Optional symbolic act (2–4 min).
9. Declaration: "By the authority vested in me… I now pronounce you married" (adjust to
   local wording) and the kiss (1 min).
10. Signing with witnesses (5 min) — music plays.
11. Presentation and recessional (2 min).

## 5. Script skeleton
```
Welcome, everyone. We're here to celebrate [NAME A] and [NAME B]...
[STORY — 300–500 words]
[LEGAL WORDS — copied verbatim from the official source, not paraphrased]
[NAME A], please repeat after me: "I call upon the persons here present..."
```
Print the legal lines in bold on a separate card in your folder.

## Gotchas
- Never paraphrase prescribed words, even to make them "flow" — they are the contract.
- Check spellings on every form against ID; a typo can mean a correction application.
- Rehearse the mic: if guests can't hear the vows, witnesses arguably didn't either.
- Bring a spare pen and printed forms; carry the paperwork home yourself.
- Same-sex marriage, minimum ages and proxy rules differ — never assume.

## Verify
- [ ] Authorisation valid for this venue's jurisdiction.
- [ ] Notice lodged in time and ID/previous-marriage documents sighted.
- [ ] Prescribed words appear verbatim in the script and were spoken audibly.
- [ ] Correct number of adult witnesses signed.
- [ ] Paperwork lodged/licence returned before the deadline; confirmation kept.
