# Leading a secular community gathering — congregation without creed

**Goal:** host a regular non-religious gathering (a secular assembly, humanist
meeting or ethical-society platform) that people come back to — because it offers
what congregations traditionally do: belonging, reflection, music, ideas and mutual
help — without asking anyone to believe anything.

## 1. Decide what the gathering is for
Write one sentence and test every segment against it. Established models to borrow from:
- **Sunday Assembly** — upbeat, pop songs sung together, a talk, a reflection, cake;
  motto "Live Better, Help Often, Wonder More".
- **Ethical Society / Ethical Culture** — a "platform" address on ethics or society,
  more lecture-like, often with discussion.
- **Humanist group meetings** — talks, discussion, social action, seasonal events.
- **Unitarian Universalist** services sit on the boundary — some congregations are
  largely non-theist and keep a liturgical shape.
Respect each model on its own terms; don't present your format as "church for atheists"
if your members dislike the comparison — ask them.

## 2. Running order (60–75 minutes, monthly or fortnightly)
| Min | Segment | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 | Welcome and intro | Host explains the format for newcomers every time |
| 5–10 | Song 1 (everyone) | Lyrics on screen; pick songs people already know |
| 10–15 | Reading / poem | A member, rotating |
| 15–20 | "Life story" or member spotlight | 3–5 minutes, scripted in advance |
| 20–25 | Song 2 | |
| 25–45 | Main talk on the theme | Guest or member, 15–20 min max |
| 45–48 | **Moment of reflection** | Silence, guided prompt, or candle — no prayer framing |
| 48–53 | Community notices and "help often" | Volunteering, members in need, events |
| 53–58 | Song 3 / closing song | Upbeat finish |
| 58–60 | Close and invitation to stay | Point to tea, cake, conversation |
| 60+ | Social time | Where belonging actually happens |

## 3. Host script fragments
```
Welcome to [GROUP NAME]. Whatever you believe or don't, you're welcome here.
This is a gathering to [PURPOSE]. Nobody will ask you to believe anything —
you're also free to just sit and listen.

Reflection: Let's take a minute of quiet. You might think about [PROMPT —
someone who helped you this month / something you wondered about this week].
[60 seconds silence] Thank you.
```

## 4. Themes through the year
Plan 6–12 months ahead. Common anchors: new year intentions, Darwin Day (12 Feb),
equinoxes and solstices, World Humanist Day (21 June), remembrance in November,
winter festival/gratitude in December. Theme each gathering: "courage", "awe",
"failure", "home".

## 5. The celebrant's wider role
- Members will ask for weddings, namings and funerals — have a list of accredited
  celebrants or be one (see related skills in this field).
- **Pastoral care:** set up a small, trained group for hospital visits and bereavement
  support, with clear boundaries and referral routes to professionals.
- Mark members' deaths at the next gathering with a short remembrance.

## 6. Volunteers and operations
Roles: host, music lead, tech (screen, mic, livestream), welcome team, refreshments,
safeguarding lead, treasurer. Rotate the host role to avoid burnout and personality cults.
Keep a one-page run-sheet with cue times, and a checklist for venue setup and close-down.

## Gotchas
- Newcomers feel excluded by in-jokes and assumed songs; repeat the format explanation.
- Talks drift into attacking religion — set a speaker brief: constructive, about living well.
- Safeguarding applies if children attend or you offer pastoral visits (jurisdiction-
  dependent: background checks, policies — check local requirements and any charity law).
- Charitable or tax status and music licensing for public performance vary by country;
  check before collecting donations or streaming songs.
- Grief and loneliness bring people in — train welcomers to notice and follow up.

## Verify
- [ ] Purpose sentence written and every segment serves it.
- [ ] Run-sheet with cue times circulated to volunteers before the day.
- [ ] Speaker and life-story contributors briefed and timed.
- [ ] Safeguarding, licensing and accessibility checked for the venue.
- [ ] Newcomer follow-up done within a week; attendance and feedback noted.
