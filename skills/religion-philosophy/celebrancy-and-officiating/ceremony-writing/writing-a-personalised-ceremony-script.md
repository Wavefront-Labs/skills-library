# Writing a personalised ceremony script — from interview notes to words worth hearing

**Goal:** a script that sounds like *these* people, lands in the time available, and
uses ritual that means something to them — for any ceremony type (wedding, funeral,
naming, renewal). Applies alongside the legal requirements in
`religion-philosophy.celebrancy-and-officiating.wedding-officiating.officiating-a-legally-valid-wedding`.

## 1. Mine the notes for specifics
Highlight in your consultation notes:
- **Concrete details** — the burnt first dinner, the blue Corolla, the dog called Pickle.
- **Their phrases** — words they actually use, quoted.
- **Tensions resolved** — distance, illness, a hard year — the story's turning point.
- **Values** in their own terms ("we're a team", "always say yes to the adventure").
If you have no concrete details, go back and ask; generic ceremonies come from thin notes.

## 2. Set a word budget
Spoken pace is roughly 130–150 words per minute, slower for emotional content.

| Section | Wedding (25 min) | Funeral tribute (10 min) | Naming (20 min) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Welcome | 150–200 | 150 | 150 |
| Story / tribute | 400–600 | 1,200–1,500 | 300 |
| Reflection on meaning | 150–250 | — | 150 |
| Symbolic act narration | 100–200 | 100 | 150 |
| Close | 80–120 | 120 | 80 |
Celebrant words are only part of the time: music, readings, vows and signing take the rest.

## 3. Shape the story
A simple arc works for most ceremonies:
1. **Hook** — a single scene ("It was raining in Bristol and [NAME A] had lost one shoe...").
2. **Background** — who they were before.
3. **Turning point** — the meeting, the decision, the obstacle.
4. **Now** — what the relationship or life looks like.
5. **Bridge** — why it brings everyone here today.
Keep humour affectionate; never a joke at someone's expense who can't answer back.

## 4. Choose a symbolic act that fits
Ask: does it express something true about them, is it visible to guests, and can it
be done in under five minutes?
- **Handfasting** — cords tied around joined hands; a Celtic / folk custom now widely used
  in civil and pagan ceremonies. Good for couples who like hands-on, tactile ritual.
- **Sand ceremony** — two colours poured into one vessel; works on beaches, wind permitting.
- **Unity candle** — two tapers light one; check venue fire rules (often banned outdoors).
- **Ring warming** — rings passed among guests to hold a wish; needs a reliable minder.
- **Wine box / letter box** — sealed for a future anniversary.
- **Tree planting, stones, flowers** — good for funerals and namings.
Introduce each act with one sentence of meaning; avoid inventing fake ancient history.
Where an act comes from a living culture or religion the family doesn't belong to
(e.g. jumping the broom, a tea ceremony, Indigenous smoking ceremonies), ask whether
it belongs to them; if they have heritage ties, involve someone from that tradition.

## 5. Script template
```
WELCOME
Welcome, everyone. My name is [CELEBRANT] and I'm honoured to [PURPOSE].
[ACKNOWLEDGEMENT OF COUNTRY / ABSENT LOVED ONES — if requested]

STORY
[HOOK SCENE]. [BACKGROUND]. [TURNING POINT]. [NOW].

MEANING
[1–2 paragraphs in their language about what this commitment / life / child means]

SYMBOLIC ACT
[ONE-SENTENCE MEANING]. [STAGE DIRECTION in italics]

CLOSE
[Declaration / farewell / welcome line]
```

## 6. Edit by ear
- Read the whole script aloud at speaking pace with a timer.
- Cut sentences you trip over; spoken lines prefer short sentences and names over pronouns.
- Mark pauses (`//`) and pronunciations in the margin.
- Send a draft for approval; mark surprises (things one partner hasn't seen) clearly.

## Gotchas
- Clichés ("two become one", "journey") — keep at most one, and earn it.
- Inside jokes exclude 90% of the room; explain or cut.
- Legal wording must stay verbatim even when the rest is creative.
- Big print (16pt+), numbered pages, in a folder — loose sheets blow away.

## Verify
- [ ] At least three concrete details only these people could own.
- [ ] Timed read-aloud fits the slot, with music and signing allowed for.
- [ ] Symbolic act chosen for meaning, checked against venue rules, origin respected.
- [ ] Draft approved; legal wording (if any) unchanged.
- [ ] Final print is large-type, paginated, with pronunciation notes.
