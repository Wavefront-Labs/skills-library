# Pressing plants and submitting biological records

**Goal:** turn finds into evidence (voucher specimens and photos) and records that
recording schemes can verify and use.

## 1. When to collect at all
- Only collect where legal and with landowner permission; never protected
  species or from protected sites without the right consent.
- Rule of thumb used by many botanists: don't collect unless the population is
  large (often quoted as >20 plants) and take a portion, not the whole plant.
- Photos alone suffice for many common species; vouchers matter for critical
  groups, new site records and anything the recorder asks for.

## 2. Pressing plants
1. Collect the **full set of characters:** flowers, fruit, basal and stem leaves,
   underground parts if not damaging. A specimen without fruit may be unidentifiable.
2. Arrange flat in a folded sheet of newsprint inside the press; show both leaf
   surfaces (turn one leaf over) and open one flower.
3. Stack: cardboard corrugate – blotter – newsprint folder – blotter – corrugate.
4. Strap tightly; change blotters **daily for the first few days** (or use
   gentle warm airflow) — slow drying causes mould and blackening.
5. Bulky fruits/cones: dry separately and store in a labelled packet with the sheet.
6. Mount on archival paper with gummed strips or linen tape; avoid sticky tape.
Succulent plants dry poorly; a quick freeze or brief immersion in alcohol before
pressing is sometimes used — check the specific method for the group.

## 3. Drying fungi
Fungi can't be pressed. Dry them whole or halved on a food dehydrator or warm
airflow (around 40–50 °C is commonly used) until brittle; store in sealed bags with
the spore print and label. Note fresh colours and smell first — they are lost on drying.

## 4. Diagnostic photography
- Whole plant/habitat shot, then close-ups of the features keys use (flower front
  and side, leaf both sides, stem, fruit, ligule or sori, fungal gills and base).
- Include a scale (ruler) in at least one frame.
- Plain, contrasting background for small parts; diffuse light (shade or flash
  with diffuser) to avoid blown highlights.

## 5. The record: what, where, when, who
Every usable record has:
- **What:** scientific name (with the taxonomic authority/checklist you followed);
  confidence level or "agg." for aggregate species.
- **Where:** a site name **and** a grid reference or GPS coordinate with precision
  stated (e.g. 10 m, 100 m, 1 km). Don't over-state precision.
- **When:** date (single day or date range).
- **Who:** recorder and determiner (who identified it, if different).
Useful extras: abundance, habitat, substrate/host, phenology, voucher held (where),
photo link.

## 6. Labels
Herbarium label: scientific name, locality, grid ref, altitude, habitat, date,
collector and number, determiner. Write labels in pencil or archival ink at the
time of collecting; unlabelled specimens are effectively worthless.

## 7. Submitting
Submit to the relevant **recording scheme** or local recorder (county recorder,
national plant or fungus recording society, local environmental records centre),
or via an online portal they accept. Many schemes also pull verified data from
iNaturalist (see
natural-sciences.botanical-fungal-identification.inaturalist.making-research-grade-observations).
Expect queries from verifiers; answer with photos or the voucher.

## Verify
- [ ] Collection was legal, permitted and from a robust population.
- [ ] Specimen includes the characters the key needs; dried without mould.
- [ ] Label written at collection with grid ref and date.
- [ ] Record has what, where (with precision), when and who.
- [ ] Submitted to a scheme that will verify and share it.
