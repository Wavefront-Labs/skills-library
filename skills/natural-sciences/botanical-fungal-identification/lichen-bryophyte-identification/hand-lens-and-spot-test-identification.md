# Hand-lens and spot-test identification

**Goal:** get lichens and bryophytes to genus and many to species with a hand lens,
a few chemical reagents and an understanding of which characters matter.

## 1. Hand-lens technique
- Use a **10× to 20×** lens. Hold the lens **close to your eye**, then bring the
  specimen (or your head) toward it until in focus — not the lens toward the specimen.
- Work in good, side-on light; a small LED torch angled across the surface shows
  texture.
- **Wet bryophytes** (a spray bottle or breath) — dry mosses shrivel and leaf
  shapes become unreadable. Lichens often show colours better wet as well, but
  spot-test on dry material.

## 2. Lichens: growth form first
- **Crustose:** crust fixed tightly to the surface; can't lift it without the substrate.
- **Foliose:** leaf-like lobes with distinct upper and lower surfaces; lower surface
  colour and rhizines (root-like threads) are important.
- **Fruticose:** shrubby or hanging, often round in cross-section (e.g. beard lichens).
- **Squamulose**, **leprose** (powdery) and dimorphic forms (e.g. Cladonia with
  basal squamules plus podetia).
Then look for:
- **Apothecia** (disc/cup fruiting bodies) and perithecia (tiny flask-shaped dots).
- **Soredia** (powdery granules) and **isidia** (tiny cylindrical outgrowths) —
  vegetative propagules; position and presence are key characters.
- Substrate: bark (which tree), rock (acid vs calcareous), soil, wood.

## 3. Spot tests
Apply a tiny drop with a fine glass capillary or toothpick to the **cortex** and, by
scraping, the **medulla** (the white inner layer); watch for colour change within
seconds.
- **K:** ~10% potassium hydroxide solution. Yellow, red, or purple reactions.
- **C:** fresh household bleach (sodium hypochlorite); replace often — it degrades.
- **KC:** K applied first, then C on the same spot.
- **Pd (para-phenylenediamine):** yellow to red reactions; **toxic and a strong
  sensitiser** — gloves, tiny quantities, and many recorders now use the
  more stable Steiner's solution form. Treat as hazardous.
- **UV (long-wave, ~365 nm torch):** some lichen substances fluoresce.
Record as e.g. "cortex K+ yellow, medulla C−". Test a known control to check
reagents still work. K and Pd are corrosive/harmful: eye protection and gloves.

## 4. Mosses vs liverworts
- **Mosses:** leaves spirally arranged, often with a midrib (nerve); capsules on a
  persistent wiry stalk (seta) with a lid and teeth.
- **Leafy liverworts:** leaves in 2–3 ranks, often lobed, never with a nerve;
  capsule stalks are short-lived and translucent.
- **Thalloid liverworts:** flat ribbon-like thallus, no leaves.
- **Acrocarpous** mosses: upright, unbranched, capsules at the tip, forming cushions.
- **Pleurocarpous** mosses: creeping, branched, capsules from the side, forming mats.
Key moss characters: leaf shape, margin teeth, nerve length (stopping short,
reaching tip, excurrent as a hair point), leaf cell shape (needs ×40+ or microscope).

## 5. Collecting
Take small samples only, where legal and permitted; never from protected sites
without consent. Air-dry in paper packets labelled with date, location, substrate.
Many bryophytes and some lichens need microscopy for species-level ID.

## Verify
- [ ] Growth form and substrate recorded before chemistry.
- [ ] Reproductive structures and propagules noted.
- [ ] Spot tests done on cortex and medulla, with a working control and fresh C.
- [ ] Safety kit (gloves, eye protection) used for K and Pd.
- [ ] Bryophytes examined wet; moss vs liverwort decided by leaf arrangement and nerve.
