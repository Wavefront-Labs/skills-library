# Building an accord from a brief

**Goal:** go from a commercial brief ("fresh green pear for a shower gel, mass
market, cost ≤ X/kg") to a stable, compliant formula that wins evaluation —
by building small accords first and growing them deliberately.

## 1. Decode the brief
- **Application base** (shower gel, candle, beverage, baked snack): it decides
  stability, solubility, volatility and legal limits more than the target profile does.
- **Target and benchmark:** a market product, a reference sample, or a mood board.
  Get a sample of the benchmark in base.
- **Cost ceiling, dosage** (fragrance commonly ~0.5–2% in wash-off products;
  flavours often ppm–0.5% depending on application) and **regulatory region**.
- **Constraints:** naturals-only, vegan, allergen-free claims, halal/kosher, no
  specific materials.

## 2. Analyse the target
- Smell/taste the benchmark at several dilutions and over time (top, heart, dry-down
  for fragrance; onset, body, linger for flavour).
- **GC-MS** identifies volatile components; **GC-olfactometry** shows which peaks
  actually smell — the largest peak is often not the most important. Odour impact
  depends on concentration relative to **odour threshold** (odour activity value).
- Write a descriptor map: e.g. "fruity-pear (esters), green (cis-3-hexenol type),
  waxy, soft musk base".

## 3. Build the minimal accord
- Start with **2–5 materials** that carry the core character. Make dilutions (e.g.
  10% and 1% in a neutral solvent such as DPG or ethanol for fragrance, appropriate
  food-grade carrier for flavour) so trace materials can be dosed accurately.
- Vary ratios systematically — a small ladder (e.g. 3:1, 1:1, 1:3) of two key
  materials teaches more than random tweaking. Keep a numbered trial log; never
  overwrite a formula version.
- Smell on blotters/strips (fragrance) at fixed times (immediately, 15 min, 1 h,
  overnight); taste flavours in the actual base at use level, not in water only.

## 4. Grow into a full formula
- Add materials to fix a specific defect ("needs lift", "too sharp", "dry-down flat"),
  one change at a time.
- Balance volatility: top notes fade fast; base notes and fixatives extend wear.
- Check **cost per kg** as you go; swapping a costly natural for a captive or
  reconstitution may be necessary.
- Rest/macerate the concentrate (often days) before final judgement.

## 5. Test in base
- Incorporate at target dose and check: **discolouration** (vanillin and
  indoles brown in some bases), **pH stability** (esters hydrolyse in alkaline bases;
  aldehydes oxidise), **solubility/clarity**, viscosity effects in surfactant
  systems, and odour/flavour stability under accelerated conditions (e.g. 40 °C for
  weeks, light exposure).
- Evaluate against benchmark with a small trained panel using **blind, coded
  samples**; use discrimination tests (triangle) or descriptive profiling as appropriate.

## 6. Compliance
- **Fragrance:** IFRA Standards set restrictions per product category; labelled
  allergens and their thresholds depend on region (e.g. EU cosmetic regulation).
  Check the IFRA certificate of every raw material.
- **Flavour:** only permitted flavouring substances for the target market;
  natural-labelling rules differ by region; check maximum levels for restricted substances.
- Keep a full trace of raw-material specifications for the dossier.

## Gotchas
- Olfactory fatigue: take breaks, smell coffee beans or your own skin, limit to
  a few evaluations per session.
- Blotter ≠ base ≠ skin. Decide only in the application.
- Raw-material lots vary, especially naturals; re-check at scale-up.

## Verify

- [ ] Trial log numbered; one change per iteration.
- [ ] Final candidate evaluated blind against benchmark in the real base.
- [ ] Stability in base checked (colour, odour, clarity) under accelerated conditions.
- [ ] IFRA/flavour regulatory compliance and allergen labelling confirmed.
