# Safe solar observing and eclipses

**Goal:** see sunspots, prominences and eclipses with zero risk to eyes and equipment.
Solar retinal injury is painless, can happen in seconds through optics, and can be
permanent. Every rule below exists because people got it wrong.

## 1. Non-negotiables
- **Never** look at the Sun through any telescope, binoculars or camera viewfinder without
  a proper **front-mounted** solar filter covering the full aperture.
- **Eyepiece "sun filters"** (screw-in dark glass, once sold with cheap scopes) crack from
  heat — throw them away.
- **Cap or remove the finderscope.** An uncapped finder can burn skin, clothing or
  eyes instantly.
- Sunglasses, smoked glass, stacked sunglasses, CDs, exposed film: **not safe**.
- Inspect filters before every use (AAS: discard if scratched, punctured or torn): hold up to a bright lamp — any pinhole, scratch or
  tear means discard. Fix filters to the scope so wind can't blow them off (tape them).
- Supervise children and the public at every instrument at all times.

## 2. Safe ways to look
- **Eclipse glasses / handheld viewers** meeting **ISO 12312-2**, from a reputable
  supplier (the AAS keeps a list of suppliers verified to meet ISO 12312-2; counterfeit history (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)). Naked-eye only — never with them behind optics (AAS: concentrated sunlight can damage the filter and enter your eyes).
- **Projection:** pinhole projector, a colander, or a small refractor/binocular projecting
  onto white card (only with cheap, non-cemented optics; a covered finder; never look
  through it; don't leave unattended).
- **White-light full-aperture filter** (glass or polymer film like Baader AstroSolar, with
  the correct "visual" density OD 5, per retailer listings; check the maker datasheet, and note the ISO 12312-2 norm covers naked-eye viewers, not telescope front filters) — sunspots, faculae, granulation in good seeing.
- **Herschel wedge** ((gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)) — for refractors only, with its built-in ND filters; stunning
  white-light detail; never on reflectors/SCTs (heat).
- **Hydrogen-alpha** dedicated telescopes or filter systems — prominences, filaments,
  surface texture. Sealed design from a known maker; don't improvise.
- **Calcium-K** — mostly for imaging; the deep violet is hard for the eye to see.

## 3. Total and partial solar eclipses
- **Partial phases** (and annular eclipses — the ring is still dangerous): filters on, always.
- **Totality only:** when the Sun is *completely* covered (diamond ring and Baily's beads
  have ended) it is safe to remove filters and look with the naked eye or binoculars at the
  corona. Put filters **back on as soon as the Sun's edge reappears** (second diamond ring).
  Know the totality duration at your exact location (eclipse maps/apps) and set an alarm.
- Outside the path of totality there is **no** safe unfiltered moment.
- Cameras and phones: filter over the lens for partial phases; remove only during totality.
  Practise the sequence beforehand — people waste totality fiddling with gear.

## 4. Lunar observing (the relaxing half)
- The best detail is along the **terminator** (day–night line), where low shadows reveal
  relief. Full Moon is flat and glaring — a neutral density or polarising "moon filter"
  helps comfort (not a safety issue).
- Learn a few landmarks per phase: e.g. the Apennine mountains and Copernicus around first
  quarter; Tycho's rays near full (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
- **Lunar eclipses** are completely safe to watch with any optics; note the Danjon
  brightness and colour.

## Gotchas
- Leaving a telescope pointed at the Sun with the filter on, then someone else removes it.
- Swapping eyepieces in a Herschel wedge setup without checking ND filters are in.
- A filter that fits loosely and blows off in a gust.
- Believing "it's only 90% partial — surely that's safe" (it isn't).
- *Uncertainty note:* filter product recommendations change; check AAS/maker guidance
  before buying.

## Verify
- [ ] Every optic pointing sunward has a full-aperture certified filter or is a dedicated solar scope.
- [ ] Finders capped/removed; filters inspected and secured.
- [ ] Eclipse glasses ISO 12312-2 from a vetted supplier.
- [ ] Totality start/end times known for your exact site; filter-back-on alarm set.
- [ ] Someone is responsible for each public instrument at all times.
