# Specimen cleaning, labelling and storage

**Goal:** turn a pile of field finds into a collection where every specimen is clean
but undamaged, traceable to its locality, and stored so it doesn't decay.

## 1. Identify before you clean
Cleaning methods that are fine for quartz destroy others. Know the species first:
- **Water-soluble or water-sensitive:** halite, sylvite, borax, some sulphates — dry
  brush only (or alcohol for some).
- **Soft or fragile:** gypsum, calcite habits, fluorite (cleaves with thermal shock),
  fibrous minerals, delicate crystal sprays.
- **Acid-sensitive:** all carbonates (calcite, malachite, rhodochrosite) — no acid.
- **Ultrasonic danger:** fractured, cleavable, included or aggregate specimens can
  shatter or lose crystals; never use on fluorite, opal, malachite, or anything glued.

## 2. Cleaning ladder (gentlest first)
1. Dry soft brush, air blower.
2. Lukewarm water + a drop of dish soap, soft toothbrush; rinse; dry slowly (avoid
   temperature shock — no hot water on cold specimens).
3. Dental picks and wooden skewers for clay in pockets.
4. **Iron staining** on quartz/silicates: oxalic acid solution (commonly a few % in
   water, soaking days to weeks, warm not hot). Wear gloves and eye protection; oxalic
   acid is toxic. Neutralise and rinse thoroughly (days of soaking in fresh water),
   otherwise residues crystallise later. Don't use on carbonates or on matrix you want
   to keep.
5. Mechanical (air-abrasive, scribes) — specialist work.
Test on an inconspicuous spot or a low-value specimen first. Record any treatment on
the label/database ("oxalic-cleaned"); buyers and museums want to know.

## 3. Number and label everything
- Give each specimen a **unique catalogue number**, applied to the specimen itself:
  a small dab of white paint or a paper micro-label under a reversible acrylic
  (Paraloid B-72) layer on an inconspicuous face, numbered with archival ink.
- **Label** (archival paper, pigment ink) travels with the specimen: catalogue number,
  species (and associates), **locality** as precise as you know it (mine/pit, district,
  region, country; GPS for your own finds), date collected, collector, source if bought
  or traded, previous labels.
- Keep the **original dealer/collector labels** — provenance adds value and science.
- Mirror it in a database or spreadsheet: number, species, locality, size, date,
  source, price, treatment, storage location, photo filename.
- A specimen whose label is lost is reduced to a pretty rock — this is the most
  common irreversible loss in private collections.

## 4. Storage and display
- **Boxes:** acid-free card trays or clear plastic boxes; cushion with inert foam
  (polyethylene), not cotton wool (snags crystals). Store heavy over light.
- **Humidity:** keep most collections at moderate RH (~40–55%). **Pyrite/marcasite**
  decay (white sulfate bloom, acidic odour) is driven by humidity — store vulnerable
  pieces sealed with silica gel, and isolate infected ones (the acid damages labels and
  neighbours). Some hydrated minerals need the opposite: they dehydrate and crumble if
  too dry.
- **Light:** realgar, proustite, pyrargyrite, vivianite, some fluorites and amethyst
  fade or alter in light; keep them in the dark and display LED-lit only briefly.
- **Radioactive minerals** (uraninite, autunite): sealed container, ventilated, away
  from living areas; label clearly; keep them few.
- **Toxic:** label arsenic/mercury/lead minerals; wash hands after handling.
- Display cabinets: closed glass keeps dust off; check for off-gassing materials in
  new cabinets (some woods and adhesives emit acids).

## Gotchas
- Superglue repairs are permanent and devalue specimens; use reversible adhesives.
- Masking tape and ballpoint on specimens degrade within years.
- Moving house is when collections lose labels — box each specimen with its label.

## Verify
- [ ] Species and sensitivities checked before any wet or chemical cleaning.
- [ ] Every specimen carries a catalogue number and a matching label and database row.
- [ ] Locality as specific as known; treatments recorded.
- [ ] Pyrite, light-sensitive, toxic and radioactive pieces stored appropriately.
