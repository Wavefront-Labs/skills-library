# Gold panning and reading a stream

**Goal:** recover fine gold (and heavy gem minerals like sapphire or garnet) from stream
gravels efficiently, by sampling where they concentrate and panning without losing them.

## 1. Where to dig: read the water
Gold is ~19× heavier than water and ~7× heavier than quartz sand; it drops wherever
the current slows, and works **down** to bedrock.
- **Inside bends** of meanders, at the upstream head of gravel bars.
- **Behind obstructions:** downstream of large boulders, below waterfalls and riffle
  drops, in plunge pools at low water.
- **Bedrock cracks and crevices** — the best traps. Clean them with a crevice tool,
  brush and spoon.
- **Old high-water channels** and benches above the current stream.
- Where black sand (magnetite, hematite, ilmenite) collects, other heavies tend to also.
Start in a stream with a known history of gold (state geological survey reports, old
mining districts). Sample-pan in several spots; move to where colours appear.

## 2. Kit
Plastic pan with riffles (green or black — gold shows well), a classifier/sieve
(~1/2" or 1/4" mesh) on a bucket, trowel/shovel, crevice tools, snuffer bottle, vials,
magnet in a bag, tweezers, loupe. Optional: small sluice where legal.

## 3. Panning technique
1. **Classify:** sieve material into the pan so it's ~3/4 full of fines; check the
   oversize for nuggets before discarding.
2. **Soak and break up** clay underwater with your fingers; clay balls carry gold away.
3. **Stratify:** hold the pan level just under water and shake vigorously side to side
   — heavies sink to the bottom. Do this repeatedly between stages.
4. **Wash off the top:** tilt the pan forward (riffles toward the far edge, down) and
   use a gentle forward-and-back dip so water sweeps light material over the lip.
   Re-level and shake again every few washes.
5. Work down until **black sand** remains. Then with a little water, swirl gently to
   spread the concentrate in a fan; gold lags behind the black sand.
6. Pick colours with the snuffer bottle. Save concentrates in a bucket to process later
   with a magnet (remove magnetite) or a finishing table.
Practice by seeding a pan with lead shot or a few small fragments of known weight and
checking you keep them all.

## 4. Gold or not?
| Candidate | Test |
|---|---|
| **Gold** | Stays bright yellow in shade and from any angle, soft (dents with a pin), heavy — lags in the pan |
| Pyrite | Brassy, brittle (crushes to black powder), forms cubes |
| Mica / weathered biotite ("fool's gold" flakes) | Flashes then dims as you rotate it; light — washes out easily |
| Chalcopyrite | Brassy-greenish, brittle |
Gem gravels: sapphires look like dull rounded glassy pebbles (often blue-grey) in the
heavy concentrate; garnet is red-brown; zircon very heavy and adamantine. Hold rough to
light, check hardness carefully — many "gems" are glass or quartz.

## 5. Claims and law
- **US:** much BLM and Forest Service land is open to recreational panning, but
  existing **mining claims** belong to someone — check the BLM MLRS database and look for
  posts/markers. Many states require permits for any suction dredge or motorised
  equipment; some streams are closed to protect fish spawning.
- **Australia:** a **fossicking licence** is needed in most states, with designated
  fossicking areas and hand-tool limits.
- Elsewhere: assume landowner/state permission is required.
- Etiquette: fill holes, don't undercut banks, don't move into another prospector's
  working spot, pack out rubbish.

## Gotchas
- Fine "flour" gold floats on surface tension — a drop of dish soap or jet-dry helps.
- Mercury from historic workings can turn up in old diggings: don't handle shiny liquid
  beads, report them.
- Water flow and temperature: winter/spring floods are dangerous; low flow in late
  summer exposes the best bedrock.

## Verify
- [ ] Land status and claims checked; licence/permit held where required.
- [ ] Sampled at obstructions, inside bends and bedrock, not random gravel.
- [ ] Test pan with known heavies retains all of them.
- [ ] Gold confirmed by malleability and constant colour, not just sparkle.
