# Glovebox operation and atmosphere maintenance

**Goal:** keep a shared glovebox below ~1 ppm O₂ and H₂O so air-sensitive metal
complexes survive — and avoid the handful of mistakes that wreck the atmosphere
or the purifier for everyone.

## 1. Before you bring anything in
- Check the O₂ and H₂O readings and the logbook. Don't open anything sensitive if
  the box is above its spec (often < 1 ppm each; some sensors read misleadingly
  low after a long time without calibration).
- Everything entering must be **dry and degassed**: glassware oven-dried (≥ 120 °C,
  several hours) and brought in hot or cooled under vacuum; solvents from a
  purification system or dried over sieves/sodium and degassed (freeze-pump-thaw ×3);
  solids dried under vacuum.
- No open water, no aqueous solutions, no unsealed protic solvents.

## 2. Antechamber cycling
- **Large antechamber:** typically 3 evacuate/refill cycles, with the first
  evacuation long (≥ 15–30 min) for porous items (paper, Kimwipes, septa, plastics).
- **Small (mini) antechamber:** 3 cycles of a few minutes each.
- Loosen caps on vials/bottles so they don't burst or trap air; tape over
  septum holes; never bring sealed volatile liquids under vacuum without venting.
- Leave both doors closed and the antechamber under vacuum when idle (or as your
  group specifies).

## 3. Working inside
- Move slowly: fast hand movements pump air past glove seals and spike O₂.
- Keep the box pressure slightly positive (a few mbar), not ballooning the gloves.
- Cap solvents immediately; volatile solvent vapour loads the purifier.
- Use a dedicated balance and keep it clean; spilled pyrophoric/air-sensitive solids
  should be quenched by the agreed procedure, not swept into the bin inside.

## 4. Purifier poisons — the big one
The purifier (copper catalyst for O₂, molecular sieves for H₂O) is poisoned or
saturated by:
- **Halogenated solvents** (DCM, CHCl₃), **thiols/sulfur compounds**, **phosphines
  and amines** at high vapour levels, **volatile metal species**, and large solvent loads.
Standard practice: switch **circulation off** (or purge the box with fresh inert gas)
while using such solvents, then purge before turning circulation back on. Check
your box's rules — some boxes have a solvent trap or are designated "no halogenated".

## 5. Maintenance
- **Regeneration** of the purifier (heating under a forming gas such as N₂ or Ar
  with a few % H₂, then cooling under vacuum) when O₂/H₂O can no longer be pulled down; done by
  a trained person per manufacturer program; log it.
- **Sensors:** O₂ sensors need periodic calibration; a classic qualitative check is a
  bulb test (a light bulb with the glass broken, filament exposed — survives on in a good
  box) or a solution of a highly air-sensitive indicator such as Cp₂TiCl or a Na/benzophenone
  ketyl (deep purple/blue = dry and O₂-free).
- **Gloves:** inspect for pinholes and swelling (solvent attack); replace by the
  glove-change procedure without opening the box to air. Wear nitrile gloves inside
  the box gloves.

## Gotchas
- Porous items (paper towels, filter paper, stir bars with rough coatings) carry a
  lot of water; give them overnight under vacuum.
- Ether peroxides, pyrophoric waste and sharps accumulate in shared boxes; take waste out regularly.
- *Uncertainty note:* cycle times, ppm targets and indicator choices vary by box model
  and group policy — the group's SOP overrides these defaults.

## Verify
- [ ] O₂/H₂O readings within spec before and after your session (logged).
- [ ] All items antechamber-cycled the agreed number of times.
- [ ] Circulation off/purged around halogenated or sulfur-containing solvents.
- [ ] Gloves checked; no waste or open solvents left inside.
- [ ] Your air-sensitive compound shows no decomposition (colour, NMR) over the work period.
