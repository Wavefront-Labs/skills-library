# Soil profile description and horizon designation

**Goal:** produce a profile description that another soil scientist could use to
classify the soil, compare sites, and interpret your lab results by horizon — the
foundation of any soil-research site characterisation.

## 1. Site and pit
- Record location (GPS), landform and slope position, slope %, aspect, parent material,
  vegetation/land use, drainage class, and surface features (stones, crusts).
- Dig a pit ~1 m wide to ≥1–1.5 m or to bedrock/restrictive layer (or use a hydraulic
  probe/auger where a pit isn't possible — note the method). Face the described wall to
  the sun; don't stand on or pile spoil above it. **Deep pits collapse**: shore or step
  pits deeper than ~1.2 m (local excavation regulations apply).
- Clean the face with a knife from top to bottom; pick a section to expose natural
  structure, and photograph with a tape and colour card.

## 2. Delineate horizons
Look for changes in colour, texture, structure, consistence, roots, and reaction to
acid. Mark boundaries with nails; record depth (cm, from the mineral surface per USDA
convention), and boundary **distinctness** (abrupt <2 cm, clear, gradual, diffuse) and
**topography** (smooth, wavy, irregular, broken).

## 3. Name them (USDA designations; WRB similar)
- **Master horizons:** O (organic), A (mineral, organic-enriched surface), E (eluvial,
  bleached), B (subsoil alteration/accumulation), C (little-altered parent material),
  R (hard bedrock).
- **Suffixes** describe what happened: Ap (ploughed), Bt (clay accumulation — look for
  clay films on ped faces), Bw (colour/structure development only), Bk (carbonates —
  fizz, white masses), Bg (strong gleying), Bs (sesquioxides, podzols), Cr (soft bedrock).
- Numbers subdivide (Bt1, Bt2); prefixes mark lithologic discontinuities (2C).
Designations are interpretations; record the observations they rest on.

## 4. Describe each horizon
- **Colour:** Munsell chart, moist (and dry if possible) — hue, value/chroma (e.g.
  10YR 4/3). Read in diffuse daylight, not sunglasses or late evening light.
- **Redoximorphic features:** iron concentrations (orange/red mottles) and depletions
  (grey, chroma ≤2): abundance, size, contrast. They record seasonal saturation.
- **Texture by feel:** moisten, knead, ribbon. Gritty = sand; silky = silt; sticky,
  long ribbon = clay. Estimate class (e.g. silty clay loam) and % clay; calibrate your
  hand against lab-measured samples — field texture is ±5–10% clay at best.
- **Coarse fragments:** % by volume and size class.
- **Structure:** grade (weak/moderate/strong), size, type (granular, blocky, platy,
  prismatic, columnar, single grain, massive).
- **Consistence:** moist friability, wet stickiness and plasticity.
- **Roots, pores, effervescence** (dilute HCl), pH (field kit), clay films, concretions.

## 5. Sample
Bag ≥0.5–1 kg per horizon (by horizon, not fixed depths, for classification), labelled
with site, horizon and depth. Take undisturbed cores of known volume for **bulk density**
in each major horizon — required to convert % carbon to stocks (t C/ha). Record the
coarse-fragment correction.

## 6. Classify
Use lab results (particle size, organic C, CEC, base saturation, pH) with the field
description to key out the soil in **USDA Soil Taxonomy** or **WRB**. Diagnostic horizons
(argillic, cambic, mollic epipedon) have quantitative criteria — field calls are
provisional until lab data confirm.

## Gotchas
- Wet vs dry Munsell colour can differ by 1–2 value units; always say which.
- Smeared pit faces hide structure — pick at the face.
- Fixed-depth sampling (0–10, 10–30 cm) suits carbon monitoring; horizon sampling suits
  classification. Decide which the study needs; doing both is common.

## Verify
- [ ] Site data, photos with scale, and pit depth recorded.
- [ ] Each horizon: depth, boundary, designation, Munsell (moist), texture, structure,
      consistence, redox features, roots, effervescence.
- [ ] Horizon samples plus bulk density cores labelled and logged.
- [ ] Classification marked provisional until lab data are in.
