# Complete checklists and trip planning in eBird

**Goal:** submit checklists that are scientifically useful (complete, with honest
effort) and use eBird's output to find birds.

## 1. Choose the right protocol
- **Stationary:** you stayed within about 30 m (100 ft) of the starting point at one
  fixed location; record duration. eBird recommends Stationary checklists under 3 hours.
- **Traveling:** you went more than 30 m from the start; record duration and distance.
  eBird recommends keeping Traveling checklists under 5 miles (8 km), and says limiting
  them to less than one hour or one mile gives even more precision. Split long outings
  into several checklists.
- **Incidental:** your primary focus was not birding.
- **Historical:** you know the date but not the start time, duration or distance.
- Specialised protocols exist (pelagic, banding, nocturnal flight call count).

## 2. "Complete" means one specific thing
Answer **Yes** to "Are you submitting a complete checklist?" only if you reported
**every species you were able to identify** by sight or sound, not just the interesting
ones. On a complete checklist a missing species implies a count of zero. If
you skipped gulls or only noted new birds, answer **No**.

## 3. Counts
Give exact counts when you can and your closest estimate when you can't; use **X**
(identified but not counted) sparingly, since a best estimate is more informative.
Use 1 only when exactly one individual was observed.

## 4. Location hygiene
- Use an existing **hotspot** when your effort was mostly within it; otherwise drop
  a personal location pin where you actually were.
- Without Mobile Tracks, place the location near the middle of your route.
- Mobile app: enable tracking so the protocol, distance and route record automatically.

## 5. Flagged records and comments
Automated filters set expected maximum counts by species, date and region; a record
that exceeds them is flagged for a volunteer reviewer. Do not remove a flagged species
or lower the count: recount, and if the number holds, keep it and explain how you
counted. Write species comments describing the bird and how you ruled out lookalikes
("Seen well" is not enough) and add photos or recordings when possible. Reviewers may
contact you. Unconfirmed records are visible only to the observer. For true rarities,
also submit to the relevant records committee
(see natural-sciences.birding.listing-twitching.documenting-a-rare-bird-record).

## 6. Sensitive species
Sensitive species are hidden from checklist views (except to the observers) and from
site-level output such as hotspot bar charts; species maps show them only at grid-cell
level. Report them in eBird, but keep specific locations private elsewhere and avoid
giving exact coordinates or directions. Hiding a checklist removes it from all outputs
and from scientific use, so unhide it once the species is no longer at risk.

## 7. Trip planning
Use eBird's Explore tools (hotspot pages, bar charts, species maps, alerts) to find birds.
Gotcha: frequencies are biased by observer effort; a species "missing" at a quiet hotspot
may simply be unsurveyed. Check eBird's help pages for the current names of each tool.

## Verify
- [ ] Protocol matches what you did; effort (time, distance, party size) entered.
- [ ] "Complete" answered honestly.
- [ ] Every species has a count, with X used sparingly.
- [ ] Location pin reflects where you birded.
- [ ] Flagged species have comments and media.
