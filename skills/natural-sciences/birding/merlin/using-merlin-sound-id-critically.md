# Using Merlin Sound ID critically

**Goal:** get the benefit of Merlin's automated suggestions while avoiding the
main failure: accepting a wrong suggestion into your list or eBird.

## 1. Setup
- Sound ID works without cell service. Keep **location services on** even when
  offline, so suggestions reflect the birds likely in your area.
- Sound ID identifies birds without any Bird Pack downloaded, but then lists only
  species names. Photos and species information need the **Bird Pack** for the area,
  so a travelling birder should download the destination pack before losing signal.

## 2. Sound ID in the field
- Approach slowly and **do not move while recording**: your own movements and
  handling noise are picked up louder than the bird and cause false hits.
- Recordings are not saved automatically. After you stop, tap **Save** to keep one;
  it is stored on your phone ("Sound ID Sessions") and is not sent to the Cornell Lab
  automatically.
- Quiet, distant birds and overlapping choruses reduce accuracy; a dawn chorus gives
  many simultaneous candidates, some wrong.

## 3. Treat suggestions as hypotheses
Merlin's list is based on the sounds you recorded combined with birds likely in your
area, and Cornell states it relies on you to confirm what you are hearing or seeing.
Before accepting a suggestion:
1. Compare your bird with Merlin's example sounds and species details.
2. Ask whether the species is plausible here and now (habitat, season, eBird bar charts).
3. **Try to see the bird.** Watching the singing bird is the best way to learn and confirm.

## 4. Failure patterns to expect (judgement, not Cornell figures)
- Mimics and similar-sounding species can trigger the wrong suggestion.
- Non-bird sounds (frogs, insects, gates, alarms, human whistling) can look like bird song.
- **One brief detection** of a rare or out-of-season species is the most likely false positive.
- Absence of a suggestion is not absence of the bird.

## 5. Photo ID and Step-by-Step
Give Photo ID a clear crop of the whole bird and the right date and place; poor light
and angle produce confident-looking wrong answers. Treat Step-by-Step results the
same way: as candidates to check against the species details.

## 6. Reporting
Don't add a species to eBird solely from a Merlin suggestion you could not verify.
Sound ID recordings are not shared automatically: export a saved recording and add it
to your eBird checklist manually if you report a sound-only bird, and say in the
comments that it was heard. (See
natural-sciences.birding.ebird.complete-checklists-and-trip-planning.)

## 7. Use it to learn
Use each confirmed suggestion as a lesson: watch the bird sing while the app labels
the sound, then try without the app next time (see
natural-sciences.birding.birdsong-identification.learning-songs-and-calls-by-ear).

## Verify
- [ ] Location services on; pack installed if you want photos and species info offline.
- [ ] For each species you report, you compared the sound with reference recordings.
- [ ] Rare or unexpected suggestions confirmed by sight or a reviewed recording.
- [ ] Recordings saved for anything flagged.
