# Separating confusion species

**Goal:** reach a defensible identification of a bird that resembles one or more
other species, using features you can actually see, in an order that stops
colour and expectation from leading you astray.

## 1. Order of observation (do it the same way every time)
1. **Size and shape against a known reference** — "slightly bigger than the
   House Sparrow beside it", not "about 15 cm". Absolute size estimates on a lone
   bird are close to worthless, especially over water or against sky.
2. **Structure (GISS/jizz):** bill length and shape relative to head, head shape,
   neck, wing length relative to tail, **primary projection** (how far the
   wingtips extend beyond the tertials), tail length and shape, leg length.
   Structure is stable across plumages; colour is not.
3. **Behaviour:** tail-pumping, wing-flicking, feeding style, flight pattern
   (undulating, direct, flap-glide), habitat and height used.
4. **Plumage by feather tract** — work methodically: crown, supercilium, eye-stripe,
   ear coverts, malar, throat, breast, flanks, mantle, wing coverts (wing-bars
   are the tips of greater/median coverts), tertials, primaries, rump, tail pattern,
   undertail coverts.
5. **Bare parts:** bill colour (base vs tip), leg colour, eye/orbital ring.
6. **Call** — often decisive (see natural-sciences.birding.birdsong-identification.learning-songs-and-calls-by-ear).

## 2. Build a comparison table for the pair
For any recurring confusion pair in your area, write a 3-column table:
feature / species A / species B, ranked by **reliability**, not by how eye-catching
the feature is. Field guides differ in which feature they lead with; regional
specialist papers and identification articles usually rank them properly. Classic
pattern: the feature everyone mentions (e.g. leg colour in some warbler pairs) is
the least reliable, and the one that actually works is structural (wing formula,
primary projection) or vocal.

## 3. Age, sex and moult — rule them in before ruling species out
- Ask "what age/plumage am I looking at?" before "which species?". Juveniles of
  one species often resemble adults of another.
- Moult can make a bird look structurally wrong: missing or growing primaries
  shorten the wing; worn feathers lose pale fringes so wing-bars vanish by late summer.
- Gulls and large raptors take multiple years to reach adult plumage — label the
  cycle (first-cycle, second-cycle) before comparing.

## 4. Traps that cause most wrong IDs
- **Lighting:** backlight darkens everything; bright sun bleaches; evening light
  warms colours; shade in a canopy turns greens grey.
- **Expectation bias:** you see what is common or what you want. Ask "what else
  could this be?" and name at least one alternative.
- **Aberrant birds:** leucism, melanism, staining (iron-rich water turns waterfowl
  rusty), oiled birds, feather damage.
- **Hybrids** in ducks, gulls and some passerines — if features are mixed, it may be one.
- **Escapes** from collections for exotic-looking waterfowl and parrots.
- **Single field mark IDs** — never rest an ID on one feature unless the literature
  says it is diagnostic for that age and season.

## 5. Notes that survive a 5-second view
Write or voice-record **immediately, before opening a field guide** — guides
contaminate memory. A quick sketch (egg for body, circle for head, labelled
arrows) beats prose. Record what you did *not* see too ("no wing-bars visible",
"tail not seen"). Photograph if possible (see
natural-sciences.birding.bird-photography-for-id.taking-diagnostic-bird-photos).

## 6. Leave it unidentified when it should be
"Phylloscopus sp." or "large gull sp." is a correct record. Logging a guess as a
species contaminates databases and your own learning.

## Verify
- [ ] You assessed structure before colour and used a size reference.
- [ ] Age/plumage was assigned before species.
- [ ] At least one alternative species was explicitly considered and eliminated
      with a feature known to be reliable.
- [ ] Notes/sketch made before consulting a guide.
- [ ] If the elimination rested on a single feature, you checked it is diagnostic
      for that age and season — otherwise the record is logged to genus or "sp.".
