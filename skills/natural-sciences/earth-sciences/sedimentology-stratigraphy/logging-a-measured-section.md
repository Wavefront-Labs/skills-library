# Logging a measured section

**Goal:** a bed-by-bed graphic log with true thicknesses and enough observation that
facies and depositional environments can be argued from it — and the log can be
correlated with others.

## 1. Choose the line
Walk the whole exposure first. Pick the line with the most continuous, least-weathered
exposure; offset along a marker bed where you must (note every offset on the log).
Establish **way-up** before starting — logs are drawn base at the bottom.

## 2. Measure true thickness
- On gentle dips, a **Jacob's staff** (1.5 m pole with a clinometer set to the dip) gives
  true stratigraphic thickness directly: tilt it perpendicular to bedding and step up.
- Tape along the outcrop: true thickness = measured distance × sin(angle between the
  measuring line and bedding). For a horizontal tape across vertical-ish strata this is
  close to measured; on slopes, correct properly.
- Record cumulative height every bed boundary. Thin beds (<5 cm) can be lumped as
  "interbedded" units with a stated ratio (e.g. 70:30 sand:mud).

## 3. What to record per bed
- **Grain size** with a grain-size card and hand lens (Wentworth: clay < 4 µm, silt
  4–63 µm, very fine sand 63–125 µm ... granule 2–4 mm, pebble 4–64 mm). The log's
  horizontal axis *is* grain size — that's what makes fining/coarsening trends visible.
- Lithology, colour, sorting, rounding, composition.
- **Base contact**: sharp, erosional (scoured, with relief), gradational, loaded.
- **Sedimentary structures**: cross-bedding (planar/trough, set thickness), ripple
  lamination, parallel lamination, hummocky cross-stratification, bioturbation index,
  mudcracks, rootlets, soft-sediment deformation.
- Fossils, trace fossils, concretions, nodules.
- **Paleocurrents**: measure foreset dip directions or trough axes; ~25+ readings per
  unit for a rose diagram. Restore for tectonic tilt if bedding dips more than ~10–15°.
- Samples and photos, keyed to height.

## 4. Draw the log
Standard layout: height column, lithology column with width = grain size, symbols for
structures, columns for paleocurrent, fossils, samples, notes. Draw it **at the outcrop**,
then fair-copy (SedLog, Strater or by hand). Keep the field version.

## 5. Facies and interpretation
1. Group beds into **facies** by repeated combinations of grain size + structures +
   fossils (e.g. "Facies B: medium sandstone, trough cross-bedded, erosive base, mud clasts").
2. Interpret each facies as a **process** (traction current, suspension settling,
   sediment gravity flow) before naming an environment.
3. Look at **facies successions**: fining-upward packages with erosive bases suggest
   channel fills; coarsening-upward ones suggest prograding deltas/shorefaces.
4. Apply **Walther's law**: facies stacked conformably were deposited side by side; a
   sharp jump across an erosion surface breaks that assumption — look for a sequence
   boundary or flooding surface.
5. Correlate logs on a **datum** (a flooding surface or ash bed is better than a
   lithological top, which is often diachronous).

## Gotchas
- Weathered mudstones look homogeneous; dig back to fresh rock or the structures vanish.
- Recessive (eroded-back) mudstones get under-measured; check thickness against the
  staff, not the visible face.
- "Cross-bedding" on a joint-oblique face shows apparent dip; measure on 3D faces.
- Interpreting environment from a single bed. Environments come from stacking patterns.
- *Uncertainty note:* bioturbation and paleocurrent sample-size rules of thumb vary by text.

## Verify
- [ ] Log base, top and every offset are tied to map grid refs.
- [ ] Sum of bed thicknesses equals the independently measured total (±5%).
- [ ] Every bed has grain size, base contact and structures recorded (or "not seen").
- [ ] Facies table defines each facies by observations, not by environment names.
- [ ] Paleocurrent data are tilt-corrected where bedding dips significantly.
