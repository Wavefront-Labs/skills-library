# Audiometry and spirometry surveillance

**Goal:** detect early, work-related change in hearing and lung function in exposed workers,
classify it consistently, and turn each abnormal result into both an individual action and a
workplace control review.

## 1. Define who, what and how often
Surveillance follows the risk assessment; it is not a substitute for exposure control.
- **Trigger:** a hazard with a recognised health effect and a valid test (noise above the
  upper action value, respiratory sensitisers such as isocyanates or flour, silica,
  skin sensitisers, statutory lists such as lead or ionising radiation).
- **Baseline** before or at the start of exposure; **periodic** testing at the frequency
  the regulation or guidance sets (commonly annual at first; some regimes relax to every
  2-3 years once stable, check current national rules).
- **Records:** keep individual health records separately from clinical notes; retention
  periods are long (often decades for some exposures; check the regulation) and jurisdiction-specific.

## 2. Audiometry
- Calibrated audiometer, booth meeting background-noise limits, trained tester; test
  0.5, 1, 2, 3, 4 and 6 kHz (OSHA minimum, 1910.95(h)(1)); 8 kHz is useful for spotting notch recovery.
- **Baseline** after a quiet period (US OSHA: at least 14 hours without workplace noise, hearing protectors may substitute; baseline within 6 months of first exposure at or above the action level; annual audiogram for workers at or above 85 dBA 8-h TWA, 1910.95(g)).
- **Classify each periodic test**:

| Scheme | Key definition |
|---|---|
| US OSHA STS | Average shift of **10 dB or more at 2, 3 and 4 kHz** in either ear vs baseline (age correction via Appendix F permitted); employer may retest within 30 days; worker told in writing within 21 days (1910.95(g)(7), (8), (10)) |
| US recordability | Work-related STS **and** average hearing level 25 dB or more above audiometric zero at 2, 3, 4 kHz in the same ear (29 CFR 1904.10; no age correction for the 25 dB test; retest within 30 days allowed) |
| UK HSE categorisation | Sum of thresholds at 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 kHz compared with age/sex tables: category 1 acceptable, 2 mild (warning), 3 poor (referral), 4 rapid loss; plus a unilateral-loss check (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) |

- A **4 kHz notch with recovery at 8 kHz** suggests noise; asymmetry, conductive gaps,
  sudden or rapid loss need ENT referral to exclude non-occupational causes.

## 3. Spirometry
- Meet **ATS/ERS 2019** quality: at least three acceptable blows, the two highest FEV1
  and FVC each within 150 mL (100 mL if FVC is 1.0 L or less; secondary sources, ATS/ERS text (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)); grade sessions and record the device and technician.
- Interpret single tests against the **lower limit of normal (LLN, z-score below -1.645 (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked))**
  using current reference equations (GLI; recent ATS guidance favours race-neutral GLI
  Global (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)), not fixed 80%-predicted cut-offs.
- **Longitudinal decline** is more sensitive than one test: investigation thresholds for FEV1 decline vary by scheme (a figure near 15% is often quoted (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)); use a consistent method (e.g. spirometry longitudinal data analysis software).
- Pair with a **symptom questionnaire**; work-related symptoms in a sensitiser-exposed
  worker warrant **serial peak flow** (e.g. at least four readings a day for several weeks, analysed with OASYS (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)) and specialist referral even when spirometry is normal.

## 4. Respond to abnormal results
1. **Confirm** (retest, check quality, exclude temporary threshold shift or acute illness).
2. **Individual action:** explain the result, refer clinically where indicated, review
   protective equipment fit and use, consider restriction or redeployment for sensitisation.
3. **Notify** the employer of fitness implications without disclosing clinical detail
   (consent and confidentiality rules apply); report where law requires (e.g. UK RIDDOR, US OSHA 300 log).
4. **Group analysis:** aggregate anonymised results by area and job; a cluster means the
   controls have failed and triggers a hygiene review.

## Gotchas
- Changing audiometer, booth, spirometer or reference equations creates artificial shifts;
  document changes and avoid re-baselining to hide deterioration.
- Revising a baseline is only legitimate for persistent shifts and per the scheme's rules.
- Normal spirometry does not exclude occupational asthma.
- Surveillance without feedback to exposure control is a wasted programme.

## Verify
- [ ] Each worker in the programme has a documented exposure trigger and a baseline.
- [ ] Test quality criteria met and recorded for every session.
- [ ] Results classified with a named scheme and compared with baseline.
- [ ] Every abnormal result has an individual action and a workplace review logged.
- [ ] Group trend analysis performed at least annually and fed back to management.
