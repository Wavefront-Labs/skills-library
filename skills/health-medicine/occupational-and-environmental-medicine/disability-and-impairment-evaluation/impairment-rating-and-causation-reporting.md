# Impairment rating and causation reporting

**Goal:** answer the questions the instructing party actually asked (diagnosis, causation,
impairment, prognosis, work capacity) with a reproducible method and an impartial,
evidence-cited report that survives cross-examination.

## 1. Set up the evaluation
- Read the **letter of instruction**: who instructs, the jurisdiction, the legal test and
  the edition of the AMA Guides (or other schedule) the system mandates. Systems differ
  widely; using the wrong edition invalidates the rating.
- Make the role clear to the examinee: an independent evaluator, not a treating doctor;
  no therapeutic relationship; the report goes to the instructing party. Obtain consent.
- Review all records chronologically before the examination; list every document relied on.

## 2. Confirm maximum medical improvement (MMI)
Rate only when the condition is stable and further recovery is not expected with or without
treatment (use the MMI definition in the edition or scheme the instructions mandate). If not at MMI, say so, estimate when, and give interim
work capacity instead of a rating.

## 3. Rate impairment (AMA Guides Sixth Edition method) (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)
1. Choose the **diagnosis-based impairment (DBI) grid** for the body region and assign the
   **class (0-4)**; the default grade within the class is the middle one (C).
2. Score the **grade modifiers**: functional history (GMFH, often using a validated
   instrument such as QuickDASH or a pain-disability questionnaire), physical examination
   (GMPE) and clinical studies (GMCS).
3. Apply the **net adjustment formula**: (GMFH - CDX) + (GMPE - CDX) + (GMCS - CDX), where
   CDX is the diagnosis class; move the grade accordingly within the class. A modifier used
   to place the class is not reused as an adjuster.
4. Convert regional to **whole person impairment (WPI)** and combine multiple impairments
   with the **combined values chart** (A + B(1 - A)), never by simple addition.
5. Show the arithmetic so another evaluator can reproduce it. Inter-rater reliability is
   modest, so transparency matters.
Distinguish **impairment** (loss of body function or structure) from **disability**
(limitation in activities and participation, ICF framework), which depends on occupation
and environment and is often a legal, not purely medical, decision.

## 4. Analyse causation
- Establish the **diagnosis** first; causation of an unproven diagnosis is meaningless.
- Weigh the **exposure evidence** (nature, intensity, duration, timing) against the
  epidemiology, using **Bradford Hill** considerations (strength, consistency, temporality,
  biological gradient, plausibility, alternative explanations).
- Address **individual factors**: pre-existing degeneration, non-occupational exposures,
  timing of onset relative to the event.
- State the opinion against the **legal standard** given (often "more likely than not" or
  balance of probabilities, i.e. greater than 50%; the precise test varies by jurisdiction,
  e.g. material contribution or aggravation).
- **Apportionment** between causes or pre-existing impairment follows the jurisdiction's
  rules; explain the basis or say it cannot be done with reasonable medical probability.

## 5. Write the report
Standard structure: instructions and questions; sources reviewed; history (in the
examinee's words where relevant); examination findings including measurement method;
investigations; diagnosis; answers to each question with reasoning; range of opinion and
uncertainties; prognosis and work capacity; required expert declaration (e.g. under UK CPR 35.10(2), a statement that the expert
understands and has complied with their duty to the court).

## Gotchas
- **Waddell signs** and inconsistency indicate non-organic contribution or distress, not
  malingering; use formal symptom-validity measures cautiously and describe findings
  neutrally (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
- Range-of-motion ratings require the prescribed technique (inclinometer or goniometer,
  repeated measurements, validity criteria); pain-limited effort undermines them.
- Do not rate a condition that is not ratable under the mandated edition, or stack
  ratings for overlapping conditions in the same region.
- Stay inside expertise; defer questions outside it rather than speculate.

- [ ] Causation opinion tied to evidence and to the stated legal standard.
