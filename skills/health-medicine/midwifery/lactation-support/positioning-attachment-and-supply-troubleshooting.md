# Positioning, attachment and supply troubleshooting

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** observe a feed, diagnose the actual problem (attachment, transfer, supply,
or a baby/parent medical issue), and leave the family with a specific, time-limited
feeding plan that protects both the baby's intake and milk supply.

## 1. Observe a whole feed
Before advising, watch from the start: baby's feeding cues (rooting, hand to mouth —
crying is late), positioning, attachment, sucking pattern, swallowing, how the feed
ends, and the parent's comfort. Use a structured tool (e.g. UNICEF BFI breastfeeding
assessment). Ask about pain, nappies, weight, and the parent's goals.

## 2. Positioning — CHIN
Whatever the hold (laid-back/biological nurturing, cradle, cross-cradle, rugby/football,
side-lying):
- **C**lose — baby held close, chest to chest.
- **H**ead free — supported at the shoulders/neck so the head can tilt back.
- **I**n line — ear, shoulder and hip aligned; not twisting.
- **N**ose to nipple — so the baby tips the head back and reaches up, leading with the
  chin.

## 3. Attachment signs (deep latch)
Wide open mouth; chin pressed into the breast, nose clear; more areola visible above
the top lip than below; cheeks full and rounded (not dimpled); sucking changes from
rapid to slow, rhythmic sucks with pauses and **audible swallowing**; no ongoing pain
after the first few sucks; nipple comes out round, not pinched or lipstick-shaped.

## 4. Is the baby getting enough?
Wet nappies (≥ 6 heavy by day 5), yellow stools (≥ 2/day after day 4), weight loss
< 10% and regaining birth weight by ~2 weeks, baby content after most feeds, alert
when awake. These outrank "my breasts feel soft" or "baby feeds a lot" — cluster feeding
is normal, especially evenings and growth spurts.

## 5. Common problems
- **Nipple pain/damage:** almost always shallow attachment — fix the latch first.
  Persisting pain: check for tongue-tie (restricted tongue lift/extension), vasospasm
  (blanching, colour change), thrush (burning pain both sides, baby oral signs — often
  over-diagnosed), dermatitis.
- **Perceived low supply** (common) vs **true low supply**: confirm with intake signs
  and weight. Causes of true low supply: infrequent or ineffective removal, separation,
  supplementation without expressing, retained placenta, PPH/Sheehan, insufficient
  glandular tissue, breast surgery, thyroid disease, PCOS, some medications (oestrogen
  contraception, pseudoephedrine).
  Fix: more frequent effective removal (8–12 times/24 h), breast compression, switch
  sides, skin-to-skin, **hand expression or pumping after feeds**. Galactagogue drugs
  (e.g. domperidone) only via prescriber with cardiac risk check.
- **Engorgement:** frequent feeding, reverse-pressure softening of the areola before
  latching, cold compresses after feeds, minimal pumping to comfort.
- **Mastitis spectrum** (ABM 2022): continue feeding; avoid aggressive massage and
  over-pumping; NSAIDs, ice, supportive bra; fever/systemic symptoms persisting
  12–24 h → antibiotics via prescriber; abscess → ultrasound and drainage.

## 6. Supplementation when needed
Medical indications (e.g. ≥ 10% weight loss with poor transfer, hypoglycaemia,
jaundice with poor intake). Order of preference: expressed breastmilk → donor milk →
formula. Small volumes matched to baby's age (e.g. ~5–15 mL per feed day 1–2, rising —
per ABM protocol #3). Use cup, syringe, supplementer or **paced bottle feeding**. Always
pair supplementation with expressing to protect supply.

## 7. Feeding plan (write it down)
Feed frequency, positions to try, expressing schedule, supplement volume and method,
red flags (fewer wet nappies, lethargy, jaundice worsening, fever), and the date/time
of reassessment (often 24–48 h) with weight check. Refer to an IBCLC or infant-feeding
specialist for complex cases.

## Gotchas
- Advice without watching a feed is guesswork.
- Nipple shields can help short-term but need follow-up for transfer and supply.
- Formula top-ups without expressing reduce supply and create the problem they treat.
- Respect the parent's informed decisions, including combination or formula feeding.

## Verify
- [ ] A full feed observed with a structured tool.
- [ ] Positioning (CHIN) and attachment signs checked and corrected.
- [ ] Intake confirmed by nappies, weight and baby behaviour.
- [ ] Problem diagnosed with cause; red flags explained.
- [ ] Written plan with expressing, supplementation (if any) and reassessment date.
