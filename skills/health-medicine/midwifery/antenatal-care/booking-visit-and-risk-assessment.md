# Booking visit and antenatal risk assessment

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** at the first ("booking") appointment, place the pregnancy on the right
care pathway — midwife-led or obstetric-led — offer screening as an informed choice,
and set up every routine visit to catch the conditions that change outcomes:
hypertension, growth restriction, diabetes, mental illness and abuse.

Schedules below follow NICE NG201 and NG133 (UK); local schedules differ.

## 1. Booking visit (early in pregnancy)
**History:**
- LMP, cycle regularity, contraception; confirm dating with ultrasound (crown–rump
  length 11+2–14+1 weeks).
- Obstetric history: each previous pregnancy's outcome, mode of birth, complications
  (pre-eclampsia, PPH, preterm birth, SGA baby, stillbirth, shoulder dystocia, perineal
  injury, GDM).
- Medical: hypertension, diabetes, renal, thyroid, epilepsy, cardiac, VTE, autoimmune,
  mental health (current or past severe illness, family history of perinatal
  psychosis), BMI, medications (check teratogenicity — e.g. valproate, isotretinoin,
  warfarin, ACE inhibitors), smoking/alcohol/substances, folic acid use.
- Social: support, housing, work, **domestic abuse** (ask privately, without partner
  present), safeguarding, interpreter needs.

**Measure and offer:**
- BP, BMI, urine dipstick for protein (and culture for asymptomatic bacteriuria).
- Bloods: full blood count, blood group and antibodies, haemoglobinopathy screen (per
  family origin questionnaire), infectious diseases (HIV, hepatitis B, syphilis;
  rubella immunity per local policy).
- Dating scan 11+2 to 14+1 weeks; anomaly scan 18+0 to 20+6 weeks. Trisomy screening per
  local pathway.
- Ask about mental health at the first contact and at every visit.
- Vaccines: discuss flu, pertussis and RSV immunisation per national guidance (NG201 refers to the UKHSA Green Book for timing).

## 2. Risk triage → pathway and preventive actions
- **Aspirin 75 to 150 mg daily from 12 weeks** until birth if any high-risk factor for
  pre-eclampsia (hypertensive disease in a previous pregnancy, CKD, autoimmune disease
  such as SLE or antiphospholipid syndrome, type 1 or 2 diabetes, chronic hypertension)
  or more than one moderate factor (nulliparity, age 40 or over, interval over 10 years,
  BMI 35 or over at first visit, family history of pre-eclampsia, multi-fetal pregnancy).
  Verify dose locally.
- **GDM risk** -> refer for an oral glucose tolerance test between 24+0 and 28+0 weeks;
  risk factors and earlier testing are in the NICE diabetes in pregnancy guideline.
- **Fetal growth risk** -> serial growth scans per local pathway rather than fundal
  height alone.
- VTE risk assessment at booking and with every admission.
- Folic acid dose: use your unit's guideline for raised neural tube defect risk.
- Refer to obstetric/specialist care: pre-existing medical disease, previous serious
  complication, multiple pregnancy, significant mental illness, substance use etc.

## 3. Routine visits (NICE: 10 for nulliparous, 7 for parous women)
At each: BP and urine protein, symptoms review, mental health and wellbeing, plans.
After 24 weeks discuss fetal movements; reduced movements need same-day assessment.
After 24+0 weeks offer **symphysis fundal height**, no more often than every 2 weeks
unless regular growth scans are done, plotted on a growth chart. If small for dates,
offer a growth scan.
After 36+0 weeks offer abdominal palpation to identify possible breech presentation.

## 4. Same-day referral triggers (examples)
- Raised BP or new proteinuria, especially with headache, visual disturbance,
  epigastric pain or sudden oedema (NG133 thresholds; 30 mg/mmol protein:creatinine is significant).
- Vaginal bleeding; ruptured membranes; abdominal pain; reduced fetal movements;
  pruritus without rash (cholestasis); suicidal thoughts.

## Gotchas
- Dipstick protein "+" needs quantification (protein:creatinine ratio of 30 mg/mmol or more is significant).
- Wrong cuff size inflates BP in high-BMI patients.
- Asking about abuse with a partner present is unsafe — use routine private time.
- A "low-risk" label is re-assessed at every visit, not fixed at booking.

## Verify
- [ ] Dating confirmed; full history including mental health, abuse and medications.
- [ ] Screening offered and decisions documented as informed choices.
- [ ] Aspirin, folic acid dose, OGTT, growth-scan and VTE decisions recorded.
- [ ] Pathway (midwife-led vs obstetric-led) documented with reasons.
- [ ] Each visit records BP, urine, SFH (from 24 weeks), movements and wellbeing.
