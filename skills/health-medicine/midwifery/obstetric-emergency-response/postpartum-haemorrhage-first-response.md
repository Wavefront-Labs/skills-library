# Postpartum haemorrhage first response

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** stop the bleeding and resuscitate in parallel during the first minutes of a
PPH, using a fixed sequence the whole team knows — before an obstetrician or
anaesthetist arrives.

Doses below are standard textbook regimens for illustration; follow your unit's PPH
protocol and verified drug references. Not for use without drill training.

## Definitions and recognition
- PPH: blood loss ≥ 500 mL within 24 h of birth (RCOG minor 500–1000, major > 1000);
  ACOG uses ≥ 1000 mL or bleeding with hypovolaemia signs (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
- **Measure, don't estimate:** weigh swabs/pads (1 g ≈ 1 mL), use calibrated drapes.
  Visual estimates underestimate large losses.
- **Shock index** (HR/systolic BP) ≥ ~0.9–1.0 suggests significant loss (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked). Pregnant
  physiology hides shock until late; tachycardia and anxiety are early signs, hypotension
  is late.

## The first minutes (do in parallel, call out loud)
1. **Call for help:** emergency bell/obstetric emergency call; assign a scribe and
   time-keeper. State "postpartum haemorrhage, estimated X mL".
2. **Rub up the fundus** (massage) and **empty the bladder** (catheter) — a full bladder
   prevents contraction.
3. **ABC:** high-flow oxygen, lie flat, **two large-bore IV cannulas** (14–16G), bloods
   (FBC, coagulation incl. fibrinogen, crossmatch, U&E, lactate), warmed fluids
   — crystalloid then blood as soon as available (volumes and unit counts per local protocol (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)).
4. **Uterotonics** (atony is the commonest cause, about 70% (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)):
   - Oxytocin 5 IU slow IV (repeat once), then infusion e.g. 40 IU in 500 mL (RCOG-style regimen (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)).
   - Ergometrine 0.5 mg IV/IM (UK regimen, or syntometrine (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked); the US methylergonovine label gives 0.2 mg IM, or IV slowly over at least 60 s, repeatable at 2-4 h) — **contraindicated in hypertension/
     pre-eclampsia**.
   - Carboprost 250 µg IM (US label: repeat at intervals of 15 to 90 minutes, total not to exceed 2 mg, i.e. 8 doses) — **caution in asthma**.
   - Misoprostol 800 µg sublingual (useful where injectables unavailable (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)).
5. **Tranexamic acid 1 g IV** (WOMAN trial: second 1 g if bleeding continued after 30 min or restarted within 24 h; death due to bleeding was reduced, most clearly when given within 3 h of birth, RR 0.69). Give as soon as possible after bleeding onset; the 10-minute infusion time is local practice (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
6. **Bimanual uterine compression** if bleeding continues while drugs take effect.

## Find the cause — the 4 Ts
- **Tone:** boggy uterus → massage, uterotonics.
- **Trauma:** well-contracted uterus but bleeding → inspect perineum, vagina, cervix
  with good light; apply pressure, repair; consider uterine rupture/inversion
  (inversion: shock out of proportion to blood loss, no palpable fundus).
- **Tissue:** check placenta and membranes for completeness; retained products → manual
  removal in theatre.
- **Thrombin:** coagulopathy (pre-eclampsia/HELLP, abruption, amniotic fluid embolism,
  sepsis, massive loss) — fibrinogen < 2 g/L predicts severe PPH (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked); activate the massive
  haemorrhage protocol.

## Escalation
If bleeding continues despite the above: senior obstetrician and anaesthetist,
massive transfusion protocol, transfer to theatre for examination under anaesthesia,
intrauterine balloon tamponade, compression sutures, interventional radiology,
hysterectomy. In community/birth-centre settings: emergency ambulance transfer early,
with the woman lying flat, IV access and ongoing uterine massage/compression.

## After
- Close observation (MEOWS chart), fluid balance, Hb check, VTE prophylaxis once safe,
  iron replacement.
- Debrief the person and family; document timings, drugs, volumes and team members.
- Team debrief and incident report per local policy.

## Drills (all obstetric emergencies)
Run regular multi-professional in-situ drills (PROMPT-style) for PPH, **shoulder
dystocia** (call for help, McRoberts, suprapubic pressure, then internal manoeuvres/
posterior arm — HELPERR), **cord prolapse** (call for help, relieve pressure on the cord
by elevating the presenting part or filling the bladder, knee–chest position, expedite
birth) and **newborn resuscitation** (see newborn life support algorithm). Keep a PPH
box (drugs, cannulas, bloods bottles, algorithm card) checked daily.

## Gotchas
- Ergometrine in a hypertensive patient can cause stroke — check BP first.
- Oxytocin as a rapid large bolus causes hypotension — give slowly.
- Losing track of cumulative blood loss across a shift change; keep a running total.
- A "contracted uterus" doesn't exclude bleeding from trauma or concealed haematoma.

## Verify
- [ ] Help called and roles (scribe, time-keeper) assigned within the first minute.
- [ ] Fundal massage, bladder emptied, two IV lines and bloods taken.
- [ ] Uterotonics given in sequence with contraindications checked.
- [ ] Tranexamic acid given within 3 h of birth.
- [ ] 4 Ts assessed; blood loss measured cumulatively; escalation documented.
