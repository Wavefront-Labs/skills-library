# Postnatal check and warning signs

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** at each postnatal contact, systematically check recovery from birth, pick
up the conditions that kill or harm in the postnatal period (haemorrhage, sepsis,
pre-eclampsia, VTE, suicide), and make sure the person knows which symptoms need urgent
help.

## 1. Ask first
- How are you feeling? Pain (and analgesia use), sleep, support at home.
- Bleeding: amount (pads per hour), clots, smell.
- Passing urine (voided since birth? volume), bowels, flatus continence.
- Headache, visual disturbance, epigastric pain, breathlessness, chest pain, calf
  pain/swelling, fever/rigors, wound or perineal pain worsening.
- Feeding and newborn concerns (link to newborn checks).
- Mood: anxiety, low mood, intrusive thoughts, thoughts of self-harm.

## 2. Observations
- Temperature, pulse, respiratory rate, BP, SpO₂ — plot on an obstetric early-warning
  chart (MEOWS) and escalate per triggers.
- Persistent tachycardia is a warning sign for haemorrhage, sepsis or PE even with a
  normal BP.

## 3. Uterus and lochia
- Fundus should be firm and central, involuting steadily.
- Lochia changes from red to pale over days. Sudden or very heavy, or persistent or
  increased, bleeding (NG194) could indicate retained placental tissue or endometritis.

## 4. Perineum and wounds
Inspect (with consent, good light) using **REEDA**: Redness, Oedema, Ecchymosis,
Discharge, Approximation. Check suture line integrity and haematoma. For caesarean
wounds: dressing, redness, discharge, dehiscence. Advise hygiene, analgesia and pelvic
floor exercises; refer OASI (3rd/4th degree) follow-up per pathway. NG194 notes that
perineal pain persisting or worsening in the first weeks can be associated with depression. Severe perineal pain out of proportion → haematoma.

## 5. Bladder and bowels
Urinary retention after birth (especially after epidural, instrumental birth) can be
silent — measure void volumes or bladder scan if not voided (follow your unit's time limit). New faecal
incontinence or urgency → assess for unrecognised anal sphincter injury.

## 6. Mental health
- Use the screening tool your unit uses (e.g. EPDS) with its local thresholds; **any
  self-harm thoughts** need same-day assessment.
- Transient "baby blues" differ from postnatal depression and from **postpartum
  psychosis** (confusion, mania, delusions, rapid change) — psychosis is an **emergency**.
- Past severe mental illness → specialist perinatal mental health plan.

## 7. Other checks before discharge
VTE risk reassessment and prophylaxis (NG194 points to the NICE VTE guideline and RCOG
guidance), anti-D for RhD-negative with RhD-positive baby per local policy,
rubella/MMR if non-immune, contraception, and a BP medication plan for hypertensive
disorders. The first midwife visit is within 36 hours of transfer of care, and a GP
review is at 6 to 8 weeks after birth (NG194).

## 8. Teach the warning signs (written and verbal)
Seek urgent help for: sudden or very heavy bleeding, or large clots; fever,
shivering, foul discharge; severe headache, visual changes, epigastric pain; chest pain
or breathlessness; calf pain/swelling; wound breakdown; thoughts of self-harm or
feeling disconnected from reality. Give the 24/7 number.

## Gotchas
- Sepsis in postnatal women may present with diarrhoea/vomiting and abdominal pain —
  don't dismiss as gastroenteritis.
- Normal BP at discharge doesn't exclude later postpartum pre-eclampsia.
- Partners or family may be the first to notice psychosis — include them in education.
- Cultural practices (e.g. confinement periods) affect follow-up access; plan visits accordingly.

## Verify
- [ ] Observations charted with early-warning score; escalation done if triggered.
- [ ] Uterus, lochia, perineum/wound (REEDA), bladder and bowels assessed.
- [ ] Mood screened; self-harm question asked; psychosis risk considered.
- [ ] Anti-D, VTE, immunisation and contraception addressed.
- [ ] Warning signs taught with written information and contact number.
