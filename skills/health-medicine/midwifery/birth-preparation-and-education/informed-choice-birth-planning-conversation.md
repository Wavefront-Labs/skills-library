# Informed-choice birth-planning conversation

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement or current local guidance. UK framing (NICE NG235 Intrapartum care).

**Goal:** help expectant parents make decisions about birth that fit their values,
using balanced, numeric information, and capture them as flexible birth preferences
the labour team can actually use.

## 1. Set up the conversation
- Timing: NICE says to discuss preferences and choices for labour and birth as early as
  possible in pregnancy and to record them; revisit when circumstances change.
- Ask first: "What matters most to you about your birth?" "What worries you?" "What
  have you heard or experienced before?" Previous traumatic birth or fear of birth
  (tokophobia) -> offer specialist support/perinatal mental health.
- Check who will be with them and what language and format they need.

## 2. Present options, not a default
For each decision, cover: what the options are (including doing nothing / waiting),
benefits and risks **in absolute numbers** with a consistent denominator, what is
uncertain, and how it fits their values. Use decision aids where available.

**Place of birth (low-risk women).** Any woman may choose home, a freestanding midwifery
unit, an alongside midwifery unit or an obstetric unit; support the choice.
- Multiparous: planning home or a midwifery-led unit is associated with a lower rate of
  interventions and no difference in baby outcome compared with an obstetric unit.
- Nulliparous: midwifery-led units are associated with fewer interventions and no
  difference in baby outcome; planning birth at home is associated with a small increase in
  serious medical problems for the baby (NICE table: 9 per 1,000 at home vs 5 per 1,000 in
  the other settings, about 4 more per 1,000). In the same table, transfer to an obstetric
  unit was 450 per 1,000 for planned home birth. Quote local transfer times.
- NICE lists medical conditions and other factors (its tables 6 to 9) that raise risk or
  call for further consideration of setting; use them in the assessment.

**Pain relief** (NICE NG235):
- Latent first stage: breathing exercises, a shower or bath, and massage may reduce pain.
  Aromatherapy, yoga and acupressure are not offered or advised for latent-phase pain, but
  support the choice if she wants them.
- Support women who choose breathing and relaxation or massage by birth companions.
- TENS is not provided by the NHS; support the choice if she wants to use it.
- **Epidural:** available only in obstetric units (so transfer if elsewhere); more effective
  than opioids; may not be fully effective; not associated with long-term backache, a
  longer first stage or more unplanned caesarean birth; associated with a longer second
  stage and more forceps or ventouse birth; needs more intensive monitoring and IV access,
  so mobility may be reduced.

## 3. Consent and capacity
A person with capacity may decline any intervention, even if the team disagrees; record
the discussion, offer ongoing care, and escalate to a senior midwife or obstetrician for
support, not coercion. Care outside guidance (for example home birth with risk factors)
-> individualised plan agreed with the senior team.

## 4. Write the birth preferences
- One page, headed by name and key medical facts (for example GBS status, allergies,
  previous caesarean).
- Sections: environment, birth companions, monitoring, pain relief order of preference,
  positions/water, interventions, third stage (NICE describes active and physiological
  management as options), skin-to-skin, feeding.
- Include "**if things change**" preferences: assisted birth, caesarean, baby needing
  neonatal care.
- Word them as preferences, not demands; the plan is a communication tool.

## 5. Antenatal education sessions
Cover signs of labour and when to call, coping strategies, comfort measures, partner role,
newborn feeding and safe sleep. Interactive practice beats lectures. Include reasons to
call urgently per your unit's guidance.

## Gotchas
- Quoting relative risk only ("double the risk") without baseline misleads.
- Presenting the unit's routine as the only option undermines consent.
- Over-specific plans set families up for disappointment; frame flexibility positively.
- Document the conversation, not just the outcome.

## Verify
- [ ] Values and fears elicited before information given.
- [ ] Options presented including "wait/do nothing", with absolute numbers.
- [ ] Right to decline explained; decisions recorded in notes.
- [ ] Written birth preferences include "if things change" scenarios.
