# Intermittent auscultation in low-risk labour

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** monitor the fetus in low-risk labour with intermittent auscultation (IA)
precisely enough to detect a developing problem, while supporting physiological birth
and mobility — and convert to continuous CTG or obstetric review promptly when indicated.

Intervals and thresholds follow NICE NG229 (fetal monitoring in labour) and NG235 (intrapartum care); check your unit's guideline.

## 1. Is IA appropriate?
NG229 offers IA to women at low risk of complications in established first stage. Offer
continuous CTG when a new intrapartum risk factor develops, e.g. meconium, maternal
pyrexia (38 °C or above on one reading, or 37.5 °C or above on 2 readings 1 hour apart),
suspected chorioamnionitis or sepsis, hypertension, fresh vaginal bleeding, maternal pulse
over 120 on 2 occasions 30 minutes apart, contractions over 2 minutes or 5+ in 10
minutes, confirmed delay, regional analgesia or oxytocin. Document the risk assessment
and the person's informed choice.

## 2. Technique
1. Use a Pinard stethoscope or Doppler; palpate the abdomen to find the fetal back first.
2. **Palpate the maternal pulse** to tell the two heartbeats apart. NICE says hourly in
   first stage and simultaneously in second stage, when it is easier to auscultate the
   maternal rate by mistake.
3. Listen **immediately after a palpated contraction** for at least **1 minute**.
4. Record as a single rate (bpm), noting accelerations or decelerations heard.
5. If no fetal heartbeat is detected, offer urgent real-time ultrasound to check viability.

## 3. Frequency
| Stage | Frequency |
|---|---|
| First contact in suspected or established labour | Offer auscultation at first contact and each further assessment |
| Established first stage | At least once every **15 minutes**, each for at least 1 min |
| Second stage | At least once every **5 minutes**, each for at least 1 min |

## 4. Normal vs abnormal
- A baseline usually between 110 and 160 bpm is expected.
- **Concern (NG229):** a rise of 20 bpm or more from the start of labour (plotted on the
  partogram), or any **deceleration** heard.
- Then **listen more frequently** (e.g. after 3 consecutive contractions) and do a full
  review. If concerns are confirmed: summon help, advise continuous CTG, and transfer
  from midwifery-led to obstetric-led care if safe and appropriate.
- Return to IA if CTG was started only because of IA concerns and the trace is normal
  after 20 minutes, unless the woman chooses to stay on CTG.

## 5. Labour progress alongside IA
- Latent phase: support at home where appropriate; analgesia, hydration, reassurance.
- Established first stage: regular painful contractions with progressive cervical
  change from **4 cm**. Offer VE about every 4 hours (or if concerned); record on a
  partogram/labour care guide.
- Suspect delay: dilatation under 2 cm in 4 hours (first labours; also for later labours,
  or a slowing of progress). Examine again 2 hours later and diagnose delay if progress
  is under 1 cm; then consider amniotomy if membranes are intact.
- Observations (NG235 first stage): contractions half-hourly, pulse hourly, temperature,
  BP and respiratory rate 4-hourly.
- Second stage: follow NG235 on timing and obstetric review.

## 6. Documentation
Time-stamped entries: FHR rate, maternal pulse, accelerations/decelerations,
contractions, actions taken and who was informed. A "fresh eyes" or buddy review
process is often used for CTG; some units apply it to IA.

## Gotchas
- Doppler can pick up the maternal aorta — if FHR ≈ maternal pulse, reposition.
- Listening between contractions or for under 1 minute misses decelerations; listen after a contraction.
- Epidural, oxytocin or pyrexia changes the risk category — switch to CTG.
- Don't let the monitoring choice stop movement; mobile/waterproof Dopplers allow IA in
  water and upright positions.

- [ ] Risk assessment documents IA suitability and informed choice.
- [ ] Each IA entry has FHR, maternal pulse and timing relative to contraction.
- [ ] Frequency matches stage (at least every 15 min first stage, every 5 min second stage).
- [ ] Abnormal findings led to more frequent listening and escalation to CTG.
- [ ] Progress and maternal observations recorded on the partogram.
