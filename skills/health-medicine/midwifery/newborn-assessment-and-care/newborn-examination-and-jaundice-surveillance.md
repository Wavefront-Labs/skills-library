# Newborn examination and jaundice surveillance

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** confirm a newborn is well, detect the conditions screening targets (eye,
heart, hip, testes abnormalities; critical congenital heart disease; significant
jaundice), and set up feeding and safety for home.

## 1. Before you touch the baby
Review maternal and birth history: gestation, risk factors for infection (GBS,
prolonged rupture of membranes, maternal fever), blood group/antibodies (haemolysis
risk), family history (hip dysplasia, congenital cataract, deafness, heart disease),
breech presentation, birth weight and any resuscitation.

## 2. Structured examination (within 72 h — NIPE in the UK)
Warm room, quiet baby, parent present. Work head to toe, noting:
- **General:** colour, tone, posture, cry, activity, dysmorphic features.
- **Head:** fontanelles, sutures, caput vs cephalhaematoma (doesn't cross sutures),
  head circumference.
- **Eyes:** **red reflex** with ophthalmoscope in a dim room — absent/white/asymmetric
  reflex → urgent ophthalmology (cataract, retinoblastoma).
- **Mouth:** palate inspected *with a light and tongue depressor* (posterior cleft is
  easily missed by palpation alone), tongue-tie.
- **Heart:** rate, rhythm, murmurs, **femoral pulses** (weak/absent → coarctation),
  colour, perfusion.
- **Lungs/abdomen:** work of breathing, organomegaly, masses, umbilicus.
- **Genitalia:** **both testes** palpated in the scrotum; hypospadias; ambiguous genitalia
  → urgent senior review (never assign sex before assessment).
- **Hips:** Barlow (adduct and push posteriorly — dislocatable) and Ortolani (abduct and
  lift — relocatable) with the baby relaxed; asymmetric creases are unreliable. Risk
  factors or abnormal exam → hip ultrasound per programme timing.
- **Spine:** midline defects, sacral dimples, hair tufts.
- **Skin:** birthmarks, bruising, jaundice. Neurology: tone, Moro, suck, grasp.

## 3. Pulse oximetry screening for CCHD (where used)
After 24 h (or per local protocol earlier), SpO₂ right hand and either foot.
AAP 2020 algorithm: pass if ≥ 95% in both and difference ≤ 3%; fail if < 90% in either;
otherwise repeat once. Failed → echocardiography/neonatal review same day. Protocols
differ (UK units vary).

## 4. Feeding and weight
- Feeds ≥ 8 times in 24 h; wet nappies increasing (≥ 6/day by day 5); stools changing
  to yellow by day 4–5.
- Weight loss up to ~7% normal in the first days; **≥ 10%** → feeding assessment,
  consider medical review, check sodium/bilirubin per guidance. Regain birth weight by
  ~2 weeks.

## 5. Jaundice surveillance
- Visual inspection in good natural light (blanch skin) — unreliable for level,
  especially in darker skin; check sclerae and gums.
- **Jaundice < 24 h of age is pathological** → urgent serum bilirubin and medical review.
- After 24 h: transcutaneous bilirubin (TcB) if ≥ 35 weeks; serum bilirubin if TcB high
  or above threshold region.
- Plot on **gestation- and hour-specific thresholds** (NICE treatment threshold graphs
  or AAP 2022 phototherapy thresholds with neurotoxicity risk factors: < 38 weeks,
  albumin < 3 g/dL, haemolysis/G6PD deficiency, sepsis, clinical instability).
- Prolonged jaundice (> 14 days term, > 21 days preterm) → check conjugated bilirubin;
  **pale stools or dark urine** suggest biliary atresia — urgent referral.

## 6. Screening and safety
- Newborn blood spot (heel prick) at the programme's time (UK day 5; US 24–48 h).
- Hearing screen; vitamin K (consented at birth); BCG/hepatitis B per risk/programme.
- **Safe sleep:** on back, own flat firm surface, smoke-free, room-sharing without
  bed-sharing especially if parents smoke, drank alcohol or took sedatives; car-seat
  safety.

## Gotchas
- Murmurs in the first 24 h are common and many serious defects have none — femoral
  pulses and oximetry matter more.
- Undescended testes bilaterally plus hypospadias → consider disorders of sex development.
- Sleepy, poorly feeding jaundiced babies feed less, which raises bilirubin further.

## Verify
- [ ] History reviewed for infection, haemolysis, hip and family risks.
- [ ] Red reflex, palate (visualised), femorals, testes, hips and spine examined.
- [ ] Oximetry screen done/recorded where used.
- [ ] Weight and feeding assessed; ≥ 10% loss actioned.
- [ ] Bilirubin plotted on hour-specific thresholds; screening and safe sleep covered.
