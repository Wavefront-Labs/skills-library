# Adult cardiac arrest management (ALS/ACLS)

**Goal:** maximise perfusion with uninterrupted high-quality CPR, deliver early
defibrillation for shockable rhythms, and find and treat the reversible cause — while one
person keeps the whole team oriented.

## 1. Set up the team (first minute)
- Confirm arrest: unresponsive, not breathing normally, no definite pulse (check ≤10 s (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)).
- **Team leader** stands back, hands-off, and assigns roles: compressions (with a relief
  rotation every 2 minutes), airway, defibrillator, access/drugs, recorder/timer.
- Use **closed-loop communication** and state the plan out loud ("next rhythm check at 2:00,
  adrenaline is due").

## 2. High-quality CPR
- Rate 100–120/min, depth 5–6 cm, chest-compression fraction >80% (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked); keep the pre-shock pause under 5 s and continue compressions while the defibrillator charges (RCUK/ERC 2025, confirmed).
- 30:2 is used when ventilating by BVM or when a supraglottic airway leaks; with a working advanced airway give continuous compressions and 10 ventilations/min (RCUK/ERC 2025). Avoid hyperventilation.
- Waveform capnography: confirms tube placement, monitors CPR quality (persistently low
  EtCO2 (the <10 mmHg figure often cited is (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked); ERC 2025 gives a physiology-guided target of at least 25 mmHg) suggests poor CPR or poor prognosis — do not use alone to stop),
  and an abrupt rise can signal ROSC.

## 3. Rhythm pathways (2-minute cycles)
**Shockable (VF/pulseless VT):**
1. Shock (biphasic energy per device; escalate if the device allows), resume CPR immediately
   for 2 minutes without a pulse check.
2. After the **3rd shock**: adrenaline (epinephrine) 1 mg IV/IO and amiodarone 300 mg IV/IO.
3. Further adrenaline 1 mg every 3–5 minutes (alternate cycles); amiodarone 150 mg after the
   5th shock. Lidocaine is an accepted alternative where amiodarone is unavailable.
   (Doses per RCUK/ERC and AHA adult algorithms — verify against local formulary/guideline;
   AHA timing of first adrenaline in shockable rhythms differs slightly.)
4. Witnessed, monitored VF (e.g., cath lab): up to 3 stacked shocks may be considered.

**Non-shockable (PEA/asystole):**
1. Adrenaline 1 mg IV/IO **as soon as access is obtained**, then every 3–5 minutes.
2. Two-minute CPR cycles with rhythm checks; look hard for a reversible cause — PEA usually has one.

## 4. Reversible causes: 4 Hs and 4 Ts
- **Hypoxia** — airway, oxygen, tube position.
- **Hypovolaemia** — haemorrhage, fluids/blood.
- **Hypo-/hyperkalaemia and metabolic** — calcium, insulin-dextrose, bicarbonate as indicated.
- **Hypo-/hyperthermia** — core temperature; prolonged CPR and rewarming in hypothermia.
- **Thrombosis** (coronary or pulmonary) — consider thrombolysis for suspected PE and then
  continue CPR for 60–90 minutes after the thrombolytic (RCUK/ERC 2025).
- **Tension pneumothorax** — finger thoracostomy/needle decompression.
- **Tamponade** — pericardiocentesis; in trauma, resuscitative thoracotomy if skilled.
- **Toxins** — specific antidotes, lipid emulsion for local anaesthetic toxicity.
Point-of-care ultrasound during the rhythm check can identify tamponade, RV dilatation or
hypovolaemia — but must not extend the pause beyond 10 seconds.

## 5. After ROSC
ABCDE again; 12-lead ECG (STEMI → emergent PCI pathway); SpO2 94–98% (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked); normocapnia; avoid
hypotension (MAP target commonly ≥65 mmHg (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)); check glucose; actively prevent fever
(temperature control strategy per current guideline); arrange CT head/chest if cause unclear
and ICU admission. Defer neuroprognostication (generally ≥72 hours (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)).

## 6. Stopping
Consider stopping when asystole persists despite treatment of reversible causes, with no
eCPR candidacy, after team discussion. Respect valid DNACPR/advance directives. Debrief the team.

## Gotchas
- **Pauses kill**: pulse checks that drift to 20–30 seconds, airway attempts during
  compressions, and shocks delayed for charging. Charge during compressions.
- Agonal breathing is not breathing.
- Hyperventilation raises intrathoracic pressure and lowers coronary perfusion.
- Do not interrupt CPR to place an advanced airway; supraglottic or BVM is fine early.
- Fatigue degrades compressions within ~2 minutes even when the provider feels fine.

## Verify
- [ ] Roles assigned, timer running, recorder logging shocks, drugs and rhythm checks.
- [ ] Compressions at 100–120/min, 5–6 cm, with rotation every 2 minutes.
- [ ] Correct pathway followed and drug timing matches local algorithm.
- [ ] 4 Hs and 4 Ts each actively considered and documented.
- [ ] Waveform capnography in use once an advanced airway is placed.
- [ ] Post-ROSC bundle started (ECG, oxygenation/ventilation targets, BP, glucose, temperature).
