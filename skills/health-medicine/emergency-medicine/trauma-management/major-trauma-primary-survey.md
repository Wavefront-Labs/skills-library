# Major trauma primary survey (<C>ABCDE)

**Goal:** identify and treat what kills first, in order, within minutes of arrival —
with a coordinated team doing tasks in parallel and a clear decision on the patient's next
destination (CT, theatre, angiography or ICU).

## 1. Before the patient arrives
- Pre-alert (e.g., ATMIST: Age, Time, Mechanism, Injuries, Signs, Treatment).
- Brief the team: leader, airway, two procedural doctors, nurses, scribe; personal
  protection; assign roles out loud.
- Prepare: blood in the room or massive haemorrhage protocol on standby, rapid infuser,
  warmed fluids, airway kit, chest drain/thoracostomy set, pelvic binder, CT notified.
- Handover from pre-hospital: hands off, everyone listens, then transfer.

## 2. The survey (treat as you find; team works in parallel)
**<C> Catastrophic haemorrhage:** direct pressure, haemostatic dressings, **tourniquet**
for limb bleeding (note time applied), junctional devices.

**A – Airway with cervical spine protection:** assess patency and voice; manual in-line
stabilisation when indicated; suction; definitive airway (RSI) for GCS ≤8, airway threat
(burns, expanding haematoma), or failing ventilation — anticipate a difficult airway.

**B – Breathing:** expose the chest; RR, SpO2, expansion, auscultation, percussion,
surgical emphysema. Tension pneumothorax → immediate decompression (needle at 4th/5th
intercostal space anterior axillary line or 2nd ICS midclavicular per local practice, or
finger thoracostomy in ventilated patients), then chest drain. Massive haemothorax → drain
and consider thoracotomy. Open pneumothorax → vented seal.

**C – Circulation and haemorrhage control:** the five sites — "blood on the floor and four
more": **chest, abdomen, pelvis, long bones** (plus external). Large-bore IV/IO, bloods with
crossmatch, gas (lactate, base deficit), coagulation/viscoelastic testing where available.
- **Pelvic binder** at the level of the greater trochanters in suspected pelvic injury.
- **Damage control resuscitation**: blood products early in a balanced ratio (e.g.,
  1:1 RBC:plasma, platelets per protocol), minimise crystalloid, **permissive hypotension**
  (commonly SBP ~80–90 mmHg or palpable radial pulse) **except** in traumatic brain injury.
- **Tranexamic acid** 1 g IV over 10 minutes within 3 hours of injury, then 1 g over 8
  hours (CRASH-2 regimen — verify against local formulary/guideline).
- Calcium replacement during massive transfusion; check ionised calcium.

**D – Disability:** GCS (record components), pupils, limb movements, glucose. Head injury:
avoid hypoxia (SpO2 <90%) and hypotension; target normocapnia.

**E – Exposure and environment:** log-roll with spinal precautions, examine back, perineum;
**prevent hypothermia** (warm room, warmed products, cover the patient). The lethal triad of
hypothermia, acidosis and coagulopathy drives mortality.

## 3. Adjuncts and decisions
- eFAST, chest X-ray, pelvic X-ray (if not proceeding straight to CT), ECG, urinary catheter
  after excluding urethral injury signs, gastric tube when indicated.
- **Stable or responding** → whole-body CT (common UK practice in adult major trauma).
- **Non-responder / unstable** → do not go to CT; theatre (laparotomy, pelvic packing) or
  interventional radiology per local system; consider REBOA/thoracotomy only in trained centres.
- Re-run the primary survey after every deterioration and every transfer.

## Gotchas
- Elderly patients with low-energy mechanisms have major injuries with normal vitals;
  anticoagulants worsen bleeding.
- Tachycardia can be absent (beta-blockers, pacemakers, athletes); lactate and base deficit help.
- Never "spring" the pelvis repeatedly — it dislodges clot.
- Crystalloid boluses dilute coagulation factors and worsen acidosis.
- Distracting injuries hide spinal and abdominal trauma; complete the secondary survey later
  and a tertiary survey within 24 hours.

## Verify
- [ ] Team briefed, roles allocated, MHP status known before arrival.
- [ ] Catastrophic haemorrhage controlled; tourniquet times documented.
- [ ] Airway, breathing and chest decompressions addressed in order.
- [ ] All five bleeding sites assessed; binder applied when indicated.
- [ ] TXA given within 3 hours if bleeding; blood products balanced; calcium checked.
- [ ] Temperature measured and actively maintained.
- [ ] Clear destination decision (CT / theatre / IR / ICU) documented with the leader's rationale.
