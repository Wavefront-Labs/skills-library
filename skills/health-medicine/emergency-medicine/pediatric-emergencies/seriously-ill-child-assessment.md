# Assessing and stabilising the seriously ill child

**Goal:** recognise compensated physiological failure in a child before it decompensates,
and deliver correctly weight-dosed treatment without calculation errors.

## 1. From the doorway: Pediatric Assessment Triangle
Three observations, no touching required:
- **Appearance** (TICLS: Tone, Interactiveness, Consolability, Look/gaze, Speech/cry) —
  best indicator of brain perfusion and oxygenation.
- **Work of breathing:** abnormal sounds (stridor, grunting, wheeze), recession, nasal
  flaring, head bobbing, tripoding.
- **Circulation to skin:** pallor, mottling, cyanosis.
Any abnormal side = potentially seriously ill. Abnormal appearance + circulation = shock;
abnormal appearance alone = consider CNS/metabolic causes (check glucose).

## 2. ABCDE with age in mind
- Children compensate with **tachycardia and vasoconstriction**; **hypotension is a
  pre-terminal sign**. Capillary refill >2 s, cool peripheries, tachycardia and reduced
  urine output signal shock while BP is still normal.
- Approximate normal ranges (vary by source — use your local chart/PEWS):
  - Infant <1 yr: RR 30–40, HR 110–160 (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) (NICE amber HR thresholds: >160 under 12 months, >150 at 12-24 months, >140 at 2-5 years, confirmed)
  - 1–2 yr: RR 25–35, HR 100–150
  - 2–5 yr: RR 25–30, HR 95–140
  - 5–12 yr: RR 20–25, HR 80–120
  - >12 yr: RR 15–20, HR 60–100
- Hypotension rule of thumb (1–10 yr): systolic BP < 70 + (2 × age in years) mmHg (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
- Bradycardia in a hypoxic child is a peri-arrest sign — oxygenate and ventilate first.
- Always measure glucose; hypoglycaemia is common and easily missed.

## 3. Weight first, then every dose
- Use a **measured weight** when possible; otherwise a length-based tape (e.g., Broselow) or
  age-based formula. APLS formulas: 0–12 months (0.5 × age in months) + 4; 1–5 yr (2 × age) + 8;
  6–12 yr (3 × age) + 7 (kg) (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked). Formulas under- or over-estimate in individual children.
- Write the weight on the resus board and use a pre-calculated drug chart or app.
- Typical resuscitation values (verify against local formulary/guideline):
  - All doses in this list are (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
  - Fluid bolus: 10 mL/kg balanced crystalloid (APLS/ERC; some guidelines use 20 mL/kg), reassess after each.
  - Hypoglycaemia: 2 mL/kg 10% glucose.
  - Cardiac arrest adrenaline: 10 micrograms/kg IV/IO (0.1 mL/kg of 1:10,000).
  - Defibrillation: 4 J/kg.
  - Anaphylaxis IM adrenaline (1 mg/mL): <6 months 100–150 µg, 6 months–6 yr 150 µg, 6–12 yr 300 µg, >12 yr 500 µg (UK
    RCUK age bands; weight-based 10 µg/kg elsewhere).

## 4. Sepsis and shock
- Red flags (NICE "red" features in under-5s): pale/mottled/ashen/blue, no response to social
  cues, does not wake or stay awake, weak/high-pitched cry, grunting, RR >60, moderate/severe
  chest indrawing, non-blanching rash, bulging fontanelle, neck stiffness, status epilepticus, focal neurological signs or seizures, age <3 months with temperature ≥38°C (NICE NG143 Table 2, confirmed; reduced skin turgor is an AMBER feature, with CRT ≥3 s, SpO2 ≤95%, RR >50 at 6-12 months or >40 above 12 months).
- Access: IV or **intraosseous** within minutes (do not persist with failing IV attempts).
- Antibiotics within 1 hour for septic shock (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked); cultures if they do not delay treatment.
- After ~40–60 mL/kg of fluid (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) without improvement (or earlier with signs of overload such as
  hepatomegaly or crackles), start inotropes/vasopressors (adrenaline or noradrenaline,
  peripheral/IO acceptable initially) and involve paediatric critical care.

## 5. Communicate
Involve parents — they notice changes first. Escalate early to senior paediatric/PICU
input and retrieval services.

## Gotchas
- Fever and crying cause tachycardia — reassess when settled and afebrile before reassuring.
- A quiet, floppy child is more worrying than a screaming one.
- Ten-fold dosing errors (decimal points, mg vs micrograms) are the commonest serious
  paediatric drug error; use two-person checks.
- Respiratory rate falling in a tiring child can look like "improvement".
- Consider non-accidental injury when history and findings do not match.

## Verify
- [ ] PAT documented; abnormal sides acted on.
- [ ] Vitals compared with age-specific ranges/PEWS, including capillary refill and glucose.
- [ ] Weight measured or estimated with method recorded; doses double-checked.
- [ ] Fluid boluses given in aliquots with reassessment after each.
- [ ] Antibiotics within 1 hour if septic shock suspected.
- [ ] Senior paediatric/PICU escalation made where indicated; parents updated.
