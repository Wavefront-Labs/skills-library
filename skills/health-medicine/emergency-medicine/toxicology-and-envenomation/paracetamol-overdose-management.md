# Paracetamol (acetaminophen) overdose management

**Goal:** identify every patient at risk of hepatotoxicity, start acetylcysteine (NAC) early
enough to prevent it (MHRA: virtually 100% effective in preventing liver damage when given within 8 hours of the overdose), and recognise the patient heading for acute liver failure.

## 1. Establish the ingestion
- Time of ingestion (single acute, **staggered** over >1 hour, or **unknown**), formulation
  (immediate vs modified-release), total dose in mg/kg using actual body weight (many
  guidelines cap the weight used, e.g., at 110 kg (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) — check local; the US label caps at 100 kg), co-ingestants, chronic
  alcohol use or malnutrition.
- Always assess intent and suicide risk; this is often a self-harm presentation.
- Contact the regional poisons centre / TOXBASE for anything unusual (massive dose,
  modified-release, very high level, pregnancy, paediatric).

## 2. Decontamination
Activated charcoal (commonly 50 g adult / 1 g/kg child (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)) is considered within ~1–2 hours of a
significant ingestion if the airway is protected and the patient is cooperative (time window
varies by guideline; longer for very large or modified-release ingestions).

## 3. Bloods and the nomogram
- **Single acute ingestion with known time ≥4 hours ago:** paracetamol level at 4 hours or
  later (levels before 4 hours are uninterpretable), plus ALT, INR, creatinine, glucose,
  venous gas/lactate.
- Plot on the nomogram used in your region:
  - **UK:** single treatment line joining 100 mg/L at 4 hours (MHRA/CHM guidance, 2012, confirmed).
  - **US (Rumack–Matthew):** treatment line from 150 mcg/mL at 4 hours (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) (the label refers to the revised Rumack-Matthew nomogram; the figure itself is not in the label text).
  - **Australia/NZ:** 150 mg/L at 4 hours line (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
  At or above the line → NAC. Below the line with normal ALT → generally no NAC (if timing reliable).
- **Do not wait for the level** to start NAC if presentation is ≥8 hours after a potentially
  toxic ingestion, or if the dose is clearly toxic and the level will not be back before 8 hours.
- **Staggered or unknown time:** the nomogram cannot be used — treat with NAC if the
  total dose is potentially toxic (UK uses ≥75 mg/kg in 24 h for staggered (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked); thresholds vary; MHRA says treat staggered overdoses without delay and do not use the nomogram),
  or if paracetamol is detectable or ALT is raised.

## 4. Acetylcysteine regimens (verify against local formulary/guideline)
- **Traditional 3-bag (21-hour):** 150 mg/kg over 1 hour (historically 15 minutes), then
  50 mg/kg over 4 hours, then 100 mg/kg over 16 hours (US label and MHRA, confirmed: the 60-minute first infusion reduces adverse reactions). The US label also lists a two-bag regimen of 200 mg/kg over 4 hours then 100 mg/kg over 16 hours.
- **SNAP / two-bag (~12-hour):** 100 mg/kg over 2 hours, then 200 mg/kg over 10 hours (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) — used in
  Scotland and some UK/Australian centres; associated with fewer adverse reactions (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
- Oral NAC regimens are used in parts of North America.
- At completion, repeat paracetamol level, ALT and INR: continue NAC if ALT is rising or
  significantly raised, INR elevated, or paracetamol still detectable (exact stop criteria vary).

## 5. Adverse reactions
Non-IgE **anaphylactoid reactions** (flushing, urticaria, bronchospasm, hypotension) are
common, especially in the first bag and with lower paracetamol levels. Stop or slow the
infusion, give antihistamine ± bronchodilator, then restart — this is **not** a contraindication to further NAC.

## 6. Liver failure and referral
Discuss with a liver unit early if: INR rising (e.g., >2 at 24 h, >3 at 48 h (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)), metabolic
acidosis, raised creatinine, hypoglycaemia, encephalopathy, or hypotension despite fluids.
**King's College criteria** (paracetamol): arterial pH <7.30 after resuscitation, **or** all
of INR >6.5, creatinine >300 µmol/L and grade III–IV encephalopathy (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked). Lactate thresholds are
included in some modified versions.

## Gotchas
- A 4-hour level drawn at 3 hours is not a 4-hour level.
- Patients are often uncertain about times; when in doubt, treat as unknown-time.
- Massive ingestions (very high levels, early coma, early lactic acidosis) may need increased
  NAC dosing and toxicologist input; fomepizole and haemodialysis are emerging options.
- Modified-release preparations can produce late peaks — repeat levels.
- Early ALT can be normal even in severe poisoning; it rises after ~24 hours (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).

## Verify
- [ ] Time, pattern and mg/kg dose documented; weight measured.
- [ ] Level drawn ≥4 hours post-ingestion and plotted on the correct regional nomogram.
- [ ] NAC started without delay when ≥8 hours or staggered/unknown with toxic dose.
- [ ] Regimen and doses checked against local protocol and weight.
- [ ] End-of-treatment level, ALT and INR reviewed before stopping.
- [ ] Psychosocial/self-harm assessment completed before discharge.
