# Rapid-sequence intubation (RSI) in the emergency department

**Goal:** secure the airway on the first attempt without hypoxia or haemodynamic collapse,
with a pre-briefed plan B, C and D so that a failed intubation never becomes a failure to
oxygenate.

## 1. Decide and assess
- Indications: failure to maintain or protect the airway, failure of oxygenation or
  ventilation, anticipated deterioration (burns, expanding haematoma), or need for
  procedures/transfer in an agitated, unsafe patient.
- **Anatomically difficult:** LEMON (Look, Evaluate 3-3-2, Mallampati if feasible,
  Obstruction, Neck mobility); difficult BVM (MOANS) and SGA (RODS); difficult front of
  neck (SHORT). 
- **Physiologically difficult:** hypoxaemia, hypotension/shock, severe metabolic acidosis,
  RV failure. "Resuscitate before you intubate": fluids/blood, push-dose or infused
  vasopressor ready, optimise oxygenation.

## 2. Prepare (use a written checklist)
- Team roles: intubator, airway assistant, drugs, cricoid/monitoring, team leader if staffing allows.
- Equipment: suction (on, under pillow), video and direct laryngoscopes, bougie and stylet,
  two tube sizes, SGA, BVM with PEEP valve, **waveform capnography** connected, front-of-neck
  kit (scalpel, bougie, size 6.0 cuffed tube) opened or immediately available.
- Position: ramped / ear-to-sternal-notch, head up 20–30° if not contraindicated.
- **Preoxygenate** for ≥3 minutes with tight-fitting facemask or NIV (target EtO2 ≥85–90%
  if measured); add **apnoeic oxygenation** with nasal cannulae (15 L/min or high-flow nasal oxygen).
- Verbalise the plan: Plan A (VL ± bougie, max 2–3 attempts), Plan B (SGA), Plan C
  (facemask ventilation, 2-person), Plan D (front-of-neck access).

## 3. Drugs (typical adult starting doses — verify against local formulary/guideline)
- **Induction:** ketamine 1–2 mg/kg (often reduced to ~0.5–1 mg/kg in shock); etomidate
  0.3 mg/kg; propofol 1–2 mg/kg (reduce substantially or avoid in haemodynamic instability).
  Fentanyl as an adjunct in some protocols (e.g., raised ICP, aortic dissection), dose-reduced
  in the unstable.
- **Neuromuscular blockade:** rocuronium 1.2 mg/kg (higher dose shortens onset; reversal with
  sugammadex 16 mg/kg for immediate reversal) or suxamethonium 1.5 mg/kg (contraindicated
  in hyperkalaemia risk: burns or crush >24–72 h, denervation, neuromuscular disease, known
  hyperkalaemia, history of malignant hyperthermia).
- Post-intubation sedation and analgesia must be **started immediately** to avoid awareness
  under paralysis.

## 4. Perform
1. Give induction then paralytic as rapid pushes; wait for full relaxation (~45–60 s for
   high-dose rocuronium).
2. Video laryngoscopy is recommended as first-line in critically ill adults by DAS guidance;
   use a bougie liberally.
3. Watch SpO2 continuously: abort the attempt and reoxygenate (BVM/SGA) at a pre-agreed
   threshold (e.g., SpO2 falling below ~93%, or any rapid fall).
4. **Confirm** with sustained waveform capnography (≥ several breaths) — "no trace = wrong
   place" (except possibly in cardiac arrest with absent CPR), plus bilateral chest rise and
   auscultation. Note tube depth at teeth (~21–23 cm typical adult; varies).

## 5. Failed airway
- After the maximum attempts, **declare failure aloud**, place a second-generation SGA (Plan B).
- If oxygenation fails with SGA and facemask → **CICO**: declare it, ensure full paralysis,
  perform **scalpel-bougie-tube cricothyroidotomy** without delay.

## 6. Post-intubation
Ventilator settings (lung-protective ~6–8 mL/kg ideal body weight, PEEP 5+), chest X-ray for
depth, gastric tube, sedation/analgesia, recheck BP (post-intubation hypotension is common),
document grade of view, attempts and adjuncts.

## Gotchas
- **Peri-intubation arrest** is driven by hypoxia, hypotension and acidosis, not by the laryngoscopy itself.
- Severe metabolic acidosis: matching the patient's minute ventilation after intubation is critical — apnoea time worsens acidosis.
- Fixation errors: repeated attempts with the same technique; change something every attempt or move on.
- Cricoid pressure may worsen the view — release it if laryngoscopy is difficult.
- Capnography may be low but present in arrest; absent trace still demands a recheck.

## Verify
- [ ] Indication documented; anatomical and physiological difficulty assessed.
- [ ] Checklist completed, Plans A–D verbalised, front-of-neck kit available.
- [ ] Preoxygenation ≥3 min plus apnoeic oxygenation; haemodynamics optimised first.
- [ ] Drug doses checked against weight and haemodynamic status.
- [ ] Tube confirmed with sustained waveform EtCO2; depth recorded.
- [ ] Post-intubation sedation, ventilation and BP plan in place.
