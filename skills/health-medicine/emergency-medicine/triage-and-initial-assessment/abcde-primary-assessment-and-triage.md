# ABCDE primary assessment and triage

**Goal:** within the first minutes of contact, decide how sick this patient is, fix anything
immediately life-threatening, and place them in the right queue — so the patient who will
die without action is never waiting behind the one who merely looks uncomfortable.

## 1. The "doorway" look (seconds)
Before any numbers, answer: **sick or not sick?** Look for altered or absent responsiveness,
work of breathing (accessory muscles, tripoding, inability to speak sentences), colour
(pallor, mottling, cyanosis), diaphoresis, and posture. A clinician's gestalt of "sick" is a
valid trigger to move the patient straight to resus, regardless of the triage score.

## 2. ABCDE primary assessment (treat as you find)
Work in order; do not move on until the problem found is addressed or delegated.
1. **A – Airway:** talking normally = patent. Listen for stridor, gurgling, snoring; look
   for burns, swelling, vomit. Simple manoeuvres (head tilt/chin lift, jaw thrust, suction,
   adjuncts) and call for airway help early.
2. **B – Breathing:** rate, SpO2, chest expansion, auscultation, trachea. Oxygen to target
   (commonly 94–98%, or 88–92% if at risk of hypercapnic failure — check local guideline).
   Think tension pneumothorax, severe asthma, pulmonary oedema.
3. **C – Circulation:** HR, BP, capillary refill (>2 s centrally is abnormal), skin
   temperature, urine output, ECG monitoring, IV/IO access with bloods (include lactate and
   glucose). Look for bleeding. Hypotension is a late sign of shock.
4. **D – Disability:** AVPU or GCS, pupils, **glucose** ("don't ever forget glucose"),
   lateralising signs, seizure activity.
5. **E – Exposure:** full exposure with dignity — rashes (non-blanching), wounds, swelling,
   temperature; prevent hypothermia.
Reassess ABCDE after every intervention and whenever the patient changes.

## 3. Assign a triage category
Use your system's scale consistently; most are 5-level:
- **ESI (US):** level 1 needs immediate life-saving intervention; level 2 is high-risk,
  confused/lethargic, or severe pain/distress; levels 3–5 are sorted by predicted resources
  (≥2, 1, none), with danger-zone vitals up-triaging a level-3 patient.
- **Manchester Triage System (UK/Europe):** presentation flowchart + discriminators
  (life threat, haemorrhage, pain, conscious level, temperature, acuity) → red/orange/
  yellow/green/blue with target times.
- **CTAS (Canada)** and **ATS (Australasia):** 5 categories with maximum time-to-
  physician targets (ATS category 1 immediate, 2 within 10 min, 3 within 30 min, 4 within
  60 min, 5 within 120 min — verify current local targets).

## 4. Add an early warning score
NEWS2 (RR, SpO2, supplemental O2, systolic BP, HR, consciousness/new confusion,
temperature) gives an aggregate: 0–4 low, 5–6 or any single parameter scoring 3 prompts
urgent review, ≥7 emergency response (escalation thresholds vary locally). Pair with a
sepsis screen (suspected infection + red-flag physiology). qSOFA (RR ≥22, altered
mentation, SBP ≤100) predicts poor outcome but is **not** sensitive enough to rule sepsis out.

## 5. High-risk presentations that should not wait in the queue
Chest pain with ECG changes or ongoing pain (ECG within 10 minutes of arrival), stroke
symptoms within the treatment window, anaphylaxis, suspected sepsis with abnormal
physiology, GI bleeding with tachycardia, severe pain, suicidal intent with means, and
the immunocompromised febrile patient (e.g., neutropenic).

## Gotchas
- **Normal vitals do not mean safe.** Beta-blockers mask tachycardia; elderly and young fit
  patients compensate until they crash; a normal SpO2 can hide a rising RR.
- **Respiratory rate** is the most predictive and the most often guessed vital sign — count it.
- Triage is a **snapshot**; patients in the waiting room deteriorate. Set reassessment
  intervals by category and act on repeat observations.
- Anchoring on the referral letter or "frequent attender" label causes missed diagnoses.
- Language barriers, intoxication and cognitive impairment hide symptoms; up-triage when unsure.

## Verify
- [ ] Sick/not-sick decision made and patient placed in an appropriate area.
- [ ] ABCDE completed with each abnormality treated or escalated, and glucose checked.
- [ ] Full set of observations including a counted respiratory rate; NEWS2 (or local score) recorded.
- [ ] Triage category assigned per local scale, with the discriminator documented.
- [ ] ECG within 10 minutes for chest pain; sepsis screen where infection suspected.
- [ ] Reassessment interval set and escalation route clear to the team.
