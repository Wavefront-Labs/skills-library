# Informed consent process and documentation

*General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.*

**Goal:** a participant (or their legally authorised representative) makes a voluntary,
understood decision before any study-specific procedure, and the record proves it — who,
which version, when, how, and that understanding was checked.

## 1. Before the conversation
- Use only the **current IRB/EC-approved** consent form (and translations); check the
  version/date in the footer against the approval letter.
- Confirm the person obtaining consent is **delegated and trained**. EU CTR Art 29(2)(c) requires
  a prior interview with a member of the investigating team appropriately qualified under the law of the Member State; some
  countries require a physician (verify locally).
- Assess capacity informally; if doubtful, use a structured tool (e.g. MacCAT-CR or a
  locally approved capacity assessment) and follow the protocol's LAR pathway.

## 2. Required content (check the form covers it)
21 CFR 50.25(a) basic elements, mirrored in ICH E6(R3) and EU CTR Art 29:
purpose, duration and procedures (experimental ones flagged); foreseeable risks and
discomforts; benefits; alternatives; confidentiality and who may inspect records;
compensation/treatment for injury (if more than minimal risk); contacts for questions,
rights and injury; voluntariness and right to withdraw without penalty. Add 50.25(b)
elements as applicable (unforeseeable risks, pregnancy, costs, new findings) and the
ClinicalTrials.gov statement (50.25(c), applicable clinical trials). Under the revised Common Rule (45 CFR 46.116) a concise **key
information** section comes first; whether FDA rules require the same is not confirmed here, so check 21 CFR 50 current text.
EU: data-protection (GDPR) information is distinct from consent to participate.

## 3. The conversation
1. Private setting, interpreter if needed (not a family member for key content where avoidable).
2. Walk through the key information; avoid jargon (a plain-language target such as ~8th-grade reading level is common practice, not a regulatory figure).
3. Give time — take-home and a second visit for complex trials; no pressure, no undue
   inducement.
4. **Teach-back:** "Can you tell me in your own words what happens if you join?" Cover
   randomisation/placebo, main risks, visits, withdrawal. Re-explain gaps.
5. Answer all questions; participant signs and dates personally; person obtaining consent
   signs; give a signed copy.

## 4. Special situations
| Situation | Rule of thumb (verify jurisdiction) |
|---|---|
| Cannot read | **Impartial witness** present for the whole discussion, signs (ICH E6(R3); EU CTR Art 29(1) for subjects unable to write) |
| Language with no translated form | US: short form + oral presentation + witness + IRB-approved summary (21 CFR 50.27(b)(2)); the 8 basic elements of 50.25(a) are presented orally |
| Lacks capacity | LAR per state/national law; EU CTR Art 31 adds conditions; seek assent where possible |
| Minors | Parental permission + child assent (21 CFR 50 Subpart D; EU CTR Art 32); re-consent at majority |
| Emergency | Only under 21 CFR 50.24 exception or EU CTR Art 35 deferred consent, as approved |
| Pregnant/breastfeeding | Extra conditions (EU CTR Art 33; 45 CFR 46 Subpart B) |
| Dependent relationships | Students, employees, patients of the investigator: neutral consenter, extra emphasis on voluntariness |

## 5. Documentation and re-consent
- Consent **process note** in source: date/time, version, who was present, questions asked,
  understanding confirmed, copy given, consent obtained **before** first procedure.
- **Re-consent** when an approved amendment changes risks, procedures or anything that may
  affect willingness to continue — at the next visit or sooner if safety-related.
- eConsent: identity verification and 21 CFR Part 11-compliant e-signature (US) or national rules (EU).

## Gotchas
- Signed form ≠ informed consent; inspectors look for the process note.
- Wrong version signed = deviation; re-consent on the right version and report.
- Backdating or pre-printed dates are serious findings.
- Withdrawal of consent: clarify scope (treatment only vs all follow-up vs data use).
- Screening labs drawn "as standard of care" the same day invite scrutiny — document clinical need.
