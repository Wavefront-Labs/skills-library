# Acute metabolic decompensation: first response

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** recognise a possible inborn error of metabolism (IEM) crisis early, capture
diagnostic samples before treatment erases the evidence, stop catabolism and remove
toxins — while getting a metabolic specialist on the phone within the first hour.

Doses below are commonly cited starting points for illustration; use the patient's
**emergency letter**, the specialist centre's protocol and verified references.

## 1. Recognise
Suspect an IEM when:
- A previously well **neonate** becomes lethargic, feeds poorly, vomits, has seizures or
  encephalopathy after a symptom-free interval ("sepsis" with negative cultures).
- Older child/adult: recurrent encephalopathy, vomiting, ataxia, or decompensation
  triggered by fasting, infection, surgery, high-protein intake or postpartum period.
- Labs: **hyperammonaemia**, unexplained high-anion-gap metabolic acidosis,
  hypoglycaemia without (or with inappropriately low) ketones, respiratory alkalosis
  with encephalopathy (urea cycle), raised lactate, liver dysfunction.
- **Abnormal newborn screen** result: follow the condition-specific **ACMG ACT sheet** —
  some (e.g. MCADD, MSUD, urea-cycle, galactosaemia) require same-day contact with the
  family and clinical assessment.
- **Measure ammonia in every encephalopathic neonate** — free-flowing sample, on ice,
  analysed within ~30 min (falsely high from haemolysis or delay).

## 2. Critical samples (before or at the start of treatment)
Blood: glucose, ammonia, blood gas, lactate, electrolytes/AG, liver and renal function,
CK, **plasma amino acids**, **acylcarnitine profile** (dried blood spot), free/total
carnitine, ketones (beta-hydroxybutyrate), insulin/cortisol if hypoglycaemic.
Urine: **organic acids**, ketones, (orotic acid if hyperammonaemic). Freeze extra
plasma and urine. Don't delay treatment to collect them.

## 3. Treat: stop catabolism and toxin load
- **Stop protein intake** (typically for no more than 24–48 h, then reintroduce per
  specialist to avoid catabolism of own protein). For fat-oxidation disorders, avoid
  fasting and long-chain fat; for MSUD, leucine control.
- **High-glucose IV fluids:** e.g. 10% dextrose with electrolytes at a rate giving
  glucose ~8–10 mg/kg/min in neonates (higher may be needed); add insulin if
  hyperglycaemic rather than reducing glucose. Not in pyruvate dehydrogenase deficiency
  without specialist input.
- Add IV lipid (if fatty-acid oxidation disorder excluded) for calories as advised.
- Correct acidosis cautiously; treat infection and other triggers.
- Avoid valproate (can worsen urea-cycle and mitochondrial disorders) and hypotonic
  fluids where cerebral oedema is a risk.

## 4. Hyperammonaemia
- Urgent specialist discussion if ammonia > ~100 µmol/L in neonates (> ~80 beyond
  neonatal period); thresholds vary.
- **Nitrogen scavengers** (sodium benzoate, sodium phenylacetate/benzoate combination),
  **L-arginine** (except in arginase deficiency) and **carglumic acid** (NAGS deficiency,
  some organic acidaemias) per protocol — loading then maintenance.
- **Haemodialysis/CRRT** — prepare early when ammonia is very high (e.g. > ~250–500
  µmol/L) or rising despite treatment; peritoneal dialysis and exchange transfusion are
  much less effective. Transfer to a centre with paediatric dialysis.
- Monitor ammonia every 2–4 h, neurological status, signs of raised intracranial pressure.

## 5. Known patients
Most diagnosed patients carry an **emergency letter/card** with their condition-specific
regimen. Follow it, and don't let "they look well" delay starting dextrose at triage
when the letter says so.

## Gotchas
- Normal glucose does not exclude an IEM; normal ammonia once doesn't either — repeat
  with clinical change.
- Treating as sepsis is right, but not *instead of* checking ammonia and glucose.
- Lactate rises in many conditions; ratio with pyruvate and clinical context matter.
- Carnitine supplementation is condition-specific (helpful in organic acidaemias,
  debated in some fatty-acid oxidation disorders) — ask.

## Verify
- [ ] Ammonia, glucose, gas and AG checked in any unexplained encephalopathy.
- [ ] Critical samples collected and stored before/at treatment start.
- [ ] Protein stopped and high-dextrose fluids running at a calculated rate.
- [ ] Specialist contacted; dialysis readiness assessed for hyperammonaemia.
- [ ] Emergency letter (if any) followed; monitoring intervals set.
