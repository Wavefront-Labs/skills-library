# NIPT result interpretation and follow-up

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** explain what a cell-free DNA (cfDNA) result actually means for *this*
pregnancy, and route screen-positive, no-call and incidental results to the right
diagnostic step — never to irreversible decisions on screening alone.

## 1. What cfDNA measures
Fragments in maternal plasma — the "fetal" fraction comes mostly from the **placenta**
(cytotrophoblast). Hence it is a **screening** test: results can reflect placental,
maternal or vanishing-twin DNA rather than the fetus. Offered from early in pregnancy, subject to the lab's minimum gestation (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).

## 2. Screen-positive: calculate the PPV, don't quote sensitivity
Sensitivity/specificity for trisomy 21 are both reported as very high (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked), yet PPV depends on prior risk
(maternal age, gestation) and condition prevalence:
- Trisomy 21: highest PPV of the common targets, rising with maternal age (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
- Trisomy 18 and 13: lower than trisomy 21 (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
- Sex chromosome aneuploidies (e.g. 45,X): PPV often low (45,X especially
  low due to mosaicism and maternal age-related X loss) (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
- Microdeletions (e.g. 22q11.2) and genome-wide CNVs: PPV variable and often low; many
  guidelines don't recommend routine screening for these.
Use a PPV calculator (e.g. Perinatal Quality Foundation/NSGC) with the patient's age
and gestation. Use the lab's and calculator's values for the numbers.

## 3. Diagnostic follow-up
- **Amniocentesis** (usually from the second trimester) (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) tests fetal-derived cells — preferred when
  confined placental mosaicism (CPM) is a concern (e.g. trisomy 13, rare autosomal
  trisomies, sex chromosome results).
- **CVS** (first trimester) gives earlier results (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) but samples placenta, sharing the CPM
  issue; abnormal mosaic CVS may need confirmatory amniocentesis.
- Tests: rapid aneuploidy (QF-PCR/FISH) plus karyotype or **chromosomal microarray**
  (CMA recommended when ultrasound anomalies present).
- Detailed ultrasound — but a normal scan does not exclude aneuploidy.
- **No irreversible management on cfDNA alone.** Offer genetic counselling promptly.

## 4. No-call / low fetal fraction
- Causes: early gestation, high maternal BMI, low-molecular-weight heparin, certain
  pregnancies (e.g. IVF, twin), and **aneuploidy itself** (trisomy 13/18 and triploidy
  associated with low fetal fraction).
- Management: offer **diagnostic testing** and detailed ultrasound, *or* a single redraw
  (success rate per lab) (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked). Don't simply repeat indefinitely.

## 5. Unexpected and maternal findings
- Multiple aneuploidies or genome-wide abnormal patterns can signal **maternal
  malignancy** → refer for evaluation per lab/guideline advice.
- Maternal CNVs or maternal sex chromosome mosaicism may explain discordant results.
- Vanishing twin can produce a false-positive or a fetal-sex discordance.
- Fetal-sex discordance with ultrasound → consider disorders of sex development, sample
  issues, transplant/twin.

## 6. Negative results
High negative predictive value for screened trisomies, but **does not** screen for most
genetic disease, neural tube defects (offer ultrasound ± maternal serum AFP) or
structural anomalies. Residual risk persists.

## Related reproductive genetics
Carrier screening (ideally preconception; expanded panels per ACMG 2021 tier
recommendations (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)), preimplantation genetic testing (PGT-M for known familial variants,
PGT-A aneuploidy screening with limited evidence of benefit in some groups) — separate
counselling topics.

## Gotchas
- Quoting "99% accurate" to a 25-year-old with a positive trisomy 13 screen is misleading.
- Twin pregnancies: performance reduced; sex chromosome results often not reportable.
- Ensure the result report's gestational age and sample date match the pregnancy dating.
