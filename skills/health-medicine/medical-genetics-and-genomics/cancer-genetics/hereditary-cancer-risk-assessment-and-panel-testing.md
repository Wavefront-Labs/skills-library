# Hereditary cancer risk assessment and panel testing

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** identify who likely carries a cancer-predisposition variant, test the most
informative person with the right panel, interpret the result (including negatives),
and give carriers and relatives an actionable surveillance plan.

## 1. Cancer-focused pedigree
For each relative (both sides, three generations): cancer type/primary site, age at
diagnosis, bilateral/multiple primaries, current age or age/cause of death, ethnicity
(e.g. Ashkenazi Jewish founder variants), prior genetic testing results. Verify key
diagnoses with records when possible — "stomach cancer" is often ovarian or colon.

## 2. Red flags / testing criteria (summary, verify current NCCN/NICE) (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)
- Breast cancer ≤ 50; triple-negative breast cancer (any age, some criteria ≤ 60);
  male breast cancer; ovarian/fallopian/peritoneal cancer at any age; pancreatic cancer;
  metastatic or high-risk prostate cancer.
- Multiple primaries in one person; multiple relatives on the same side.
- Colorectal/endometrial cancer with **mismatch-repair (MMR) deficiency** on tumour
  (IHC loss or MSI-high) not explained by MLH1 promoter hypermethylation/BRAF V600E.
- Known familial pathogenic variant.
- Tumour sequencing showing a likely germline variant (e.g. BRCA1/2 with high VAF).
- Many guidelines now support testing **all** patients with ovarian, pancreatic or
  metastatic prostate cancer, and broadening criteria for breast cancer.

## 3. Risk models when criteria are borderline
- **CanRisk/BOADICEA** (breast/ovarian; includes pedigree, PRS, mammographic density),
  **Tyrer–Cuzick/IBIS** (breast lifetime risk for screening decisions).
- **PREMM5** (Lynch probability; testing often recommended at ≥ 2.5%) (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
- Models estimate carrier probability or cancer risk — know which you're quoting.

## 4. Who to test first and which panel
- Test the **affected relative** most likely to carry the variant (youngest diagnosis,
  most typical cancer) — an uninformative negative in an unaffected person leaves risk
  unresolved.
- Known familial variant → single-site test.
- Otherwise multigene panel matched to the phenotype: breast/ovarian (BRCA1, BRCA2,
  PALB2, ATM, CHEK2, RAD51C, RAD51D, BRIP1, TP53, PTEN, CDH1, STK11) (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked), Lynch (MLH1, MSH2,
  MSH6, PMS2, EPCAM), plus pancreatic/GI genes as indicated. Larger panels add VUS rate
  and moderate-penetrance genes with less clear management.

## 5. Interpreting results
- **Pathogenic/LP:** follow gene-specific guidelines (e.g. BRCA1/2: breast MRI from
  ~25–30, risk-reducing salpingo-oophorectomy by ~35–40 for BRCA1 and ~40–45 for BRCA2,
  discuss mastectomy; Lynch: colonoscopy every 1–2 years starting ~20–30 depending on
  gene, discuss hysterectomy/BSO, aspirin). Ages are approximate — verify per gene (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
  PARP inhibitor eligibility for some cancers.
- **Negative in a family with a known variant:** true negative → population risk for
  that syndrome (family history may still warrant screening).
- **Negative with no known familial variant:** uninformative; manage by family-history
  risk models.
- **VUS:** manage by history; do not use for surgery decisions.
- **Mosaic/low VAF TP53** variants may reflect clonal haematopoiesis — confirm on
  another tissue (e.g. skin fibroblasts).

## 6. Cascade and documentation
Family letter with exact variant; offer testing to first-degree relatives (and
appropriate age for childhood-onset syndromes like Li–Fraumeni, FAP). Document
surveillance plan with ages and intervals and who coordinates it.

## Gotchas
- Somatic tumour results ≠ germline; tumour-only BRCA findings need germline confirmation.
- Lynch IHC: MLH1/PMS2 loss is commonly sporadic (promoter methylation); MSH2/MSH6
  loss is more suggestive of germline (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
- Bone-marrow transplant recipients and haematological cancers: blood-based germline
  testing is invalid — use cultured fibroblasts.
- Insurance pre-authorization often requires documenting which criterion is met.

## Verify

- [ ] Criterion or model result documented to justify testing.
- [ ] Most informative person tested; panel fits phenotype.
- [ ] Result category interpreted correctly (true vs uninformative negative, VUS).
- [ ] Gene-specific surveillance plan and cascade letter provided.
