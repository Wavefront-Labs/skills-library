# Structured polypharmacy review

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** leave every patient on a list where each drug has a current indication, an
acceptable benefit/harm balance for *this* patient's goals and time horizon, and a
monitoring plan — and change no more than the patient can safely absorb at once.

## 1. Get the true list (reconciliation)
- Use at least two sources: patient/carer interview ("show me the bags"), pharmacy
  dispensing record, previous discharge summary, GP/primary-care list.
- Include OTCs, supplements, eye drops, inhalers, patches, PRNs and drugs taken "only
  sometimes". Ask what they actually take, not what is prescribed.
- Record dose, frequency, start date and prescriber where known.

## 2. Pair each drug with an indication
Write a two-column table: drug → condition/target. Flags:
- **No indication found** → candidate to stop (after checking with the prescriber).
- **Indication without a drug** (START-type omission: e.g. no anticoagulant in AF with
  high stroke risk, no statin after MI) → candidate to add.
- **Drug treating a side effect of another drug** → possible prescribing cascade
  (e.g. amlodipine → ankle oedema → furosemide; cholinesterase inhibitor → urinary
  incontinence → antimuscarinic; NSAID → hypertension → extra antihypertensive).

## 3. Screen systematically
- **Explicit criteria:** Beers (US) or STOPP/START (Europe) — benzodiazepines/Z-drugs,
  strong anticholinergics, long-term PPI without indication, sulfonylureas with
  hypoglycaemia risk, NSAIDs with CKD/HF/anticoagulation.
- **Renal and hepatic dosing:** recalc doses against current eGFR/CrCl (many drug
  labels use Cockcroft–Gault CrCl, not eGFR — DOACs, some antibiotics, gabapentinoids).
- **Interactions:** run an interaction checker, but triage by clinical consequence:
  QT-prolonging combinations, serotonergic stacking, "triple whammy" (ACE-I/ARB +
  diuretic + NSAID), CYP3A4/P-gp inhibitors with DOACs or statins, additive
  hyperkalaemia (RAAS blockade + spironolactone + trimethoprim).
- **Cumulative burden:** anticholinergic burden score, sedative load, falls-risk drugs.

## 4. Prioritise against goals and time-to-benefit
Ask what matters most to the patient (function, symptom relief, longevity, fewer
pills). Weigh **time-to-benefit** against life expectancy: tight glycaemic targets or
primary-prevention statins may not pay off in a frail patient with limited prognosis.
When guidelines for different diseases conflict, say so explicitly rather than
summing them.

## 5. Deprescribe safely
- Change **one or two drugs at a time**, so you can attribute any effect.
- **Taper** drugs with withdrawal or rebound: benzodiazepines (e.g. ~10–25% dose
  reduction every 2–4 weeks), beta-blockers, PPIs (rebound acid), antidepressants,
  gabapentinoids, corticosteroids (adrenal suppression).
- Agree a monitoring plan: what symptom or value would prompt a restart, and when you
  will check.
- Communicate the change to every prescriber and the pharmacy; update the list in the
  record so it doesn't get "reconciled" back on the next admission.

## Gotchas
- Hospital is a poor place to stop long-term drugs blindly: acute illness distorts BP,
  renal function and glucose. Document the reason and hand over the plan.
- "Sick-day" rules (temporarily withholding metformin, SGLT2i, ACE-I/ARB, diuretics,
  NSAIDs during dehydrating illness) are different from permanent deprescribing.
- Interaction checkers over-alert; ignoring all alerts is as dangerous as acting on all.
- Patients may hold strong beliefs about a drug — negotiate; a stopped drug restarted
  secretly is worse than a documented compromise.

## Verify
- [ ] Reconciled list from ≥2 sources, including OTCs and PRNs.
- [ ] Every drug has an indication or a documented decision.
- [ ] Doses checked against current renal function with the correct equation.
- [ ] High-consequence interactions reviewed and acted on or justified.
- [ ] Changes limited in number, with taper, monitoring date and restart criteria.
- [ ] All prescribers and the pharmacy informed; record updated.
