# Admission-to-discharge ward workflow

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** every admitted adult gets a complete admission, a daily plan that moves them
forward, a timely escalation if they deteriorate, and a safe discharge that the next
clinician can follow.

## 1. Admission: the checklist that prevents day-3 surprises
- **Working diagnosis + differential** and the specific question this admission answers.
- **Medication reconciliation** from at least two sources (patient/family, pharmacy
  record, GP list, pill bottles). Decide for each drug: continue, hold (and why), stop.
- **Code status / treatment escalation plan** documented on day one, not at 2 a.m.
- **Standard risk assessments**: VTE (e.g. Padua or local tool) with prophylaxis or a
  reason not to; falls; pressure injury; delirium risk; nutrition screen (e.g. MUST).
- **Orders**: diet, fluids (with a stop/review time), monitoring frequency, glucose
  plan, lines and catheters with an indication.
- **Estimated discharge date (EDD)** and anticipated barriers (home support, rehab,
  pending tests).

## 2. The daily round — same order every patient
1. Overnight events, nursing concerns, observations trend (not just the last set).
2. Examine; review **ins/outs, weight, new results** against yesterday.
3. Problem-based plan: for each active problem, "status → today's action → criterion
   for next step" (e.g. "IV → oral antibiotics when afebrile 24–48 h and tolerating oral").
4. **Line/catheter check:** does each device still have an indication? Remove if not.
5. **Medication review:** IV-to-oral switch, held meds to restart, new interactions.
6. **Discharge check:** EDD still valid? What single task blocks discharge today?
Write the note as SOAP or problem list; put the plan where nights will look first.

## 3. Deterioration: score, escalate, document
- Use the local early-warning system (e.g. **NEWS2** in the UK: aggregate 5–6 or any
  single parameter scoring 3 → urgent review; ≥7 → emergency/critical-care response).
  Check your institution's thresholds — they differ.
- Escalation call in **SBAR** form: situation, background, assessment, recommendation,
  plus an explicit ask ("I need you to see the patient within 30 minutes").
- Reassess after each intervention and record the time; set a review time before
  leaving the bedside.
- Common reversible culprits on the ward: sepsis, hypovolaemia or fluid overload,
  hypoxia, hypoglycaemia, opioid/sedative excess, urinary retention, delirium triggers,
  PE, bleeding on anticoagulation.

## 4. Discharge: planned from admission
- Criteria-led discharge: write the clinical criteria so a nurse or junior can
  discharge when met.
- **Discharge summary** contents: diagnosis (and what was excluded), key results,
  procedures, medication changes **with reasons**, pending results and who chases them,
  follow-up appointments, red-flag advice given.
- Reconcile medications again; flag high-risk drugs (anticoagulants, insulin, opioids).
- Teach-back with the patient: they explain their new medicines and when to return.

## Gotchas
- "Held" medications are the most commonly forgotten at discharge — keep a held-meds
  list in every note.
- Pending results at discharge are a known safety gap; assign a named owner.
- Weekend handover without a plan for each sick patient creates Monday deterioration.
- Day-of-week and pharmacy cut-off times often decide the real discharge time; start
  discharge medications the day before.

## Verify
- [ ] Admission note has med rec, code status, VTE assessment and EDD.
- [ ] Each daily note has a problem list with explicit next-step criteria.
- [ ] Every line and catheter has a documented current indication.
- [ ] Deterioration episodes show score, escalation, intervention and review times.
- [ ] Discharge summary lists med changes with reasons and owners for pending results.
