# Preoperative risk assessment and optimisation

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** answer the surgeon's actual question — "is this patient's risk acceptable and
what should we do to lower it?" — with a stepwise assessment and specific orders, not
"cleared for surgery".

## 1. Clarify the question and the surgery
- What procedure, how urgent (emergency / time-sensitive / elective), what anaesthesia,
  expected blood loss and fluid shifts. **Emergency surgery** → proceed; your job is
  perioperative management, not testing.
- Surgical risk class: low (<1% major adverse cardiac events — e.g. cataract,
  superficial, endoscopic) vs elevated (intraperitoneal, intrathoracic, major vascular).

## 2. Stepwise cardiac assessment (ACC/AHA-style)
1. **Active cardiac condition?** (ACS, decompensated HF, significant arrhythmia, severe
   symptomatic valve disease) → delay elective surgery and treat.
2. **Estimate risk** with RCRI (1 point each: high-risk surgery, ischaemic heart
   disease, HF history, cerebrovascular disease, insulin-treated diabetes, creatinine
   >2 mg/dL / ~177 µmol/L). 0–1 points ≈ low risk. The six RCRI predictors and the creatinine > 2.0 mg/dL cut-off match Lee 1999 (confirmed from abstracts; original major-cardiac-complication rates 0.4-0.5%, 0.9-1.3%, 4-7%, 9-11% for 0, 1, 2, 3 or more points). NSQIP calculators are an alternative.
3. Low risk → proceed.
4. Elevated risk → **functional capacity**: ≥4 METs (climb a flight of stairs, walk
   uphill briskly) or DASI >34 (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) → proceed without further testing.
5. Poor/unknown capacity → consider BNP/NT-proBNP (ESC/CCS approach) or stress testing
   **only if the result would change management** (e.g. revascularisation independent of
   surgery). Routine pre-op echo/stress testing in stable patients does not reduce events.
Guideline details differ between ACC/AHA, ESC and CCS — name the one you apply.

## 3. Medications: give an instruction per drug
- **Continue** most: beta-blockers (do not start high-dose de novo on the day), statins,
  most antihypertensives.
- **Often hold**: ACE-I/ARB on the morning of surgery (hypotension risk; practice
  varies), diuretics on the day, SGLT2 inhibitors **3 days before** (ertugliflozin 4; FDA 2020 label change, confirmed from news coverage of the label) for
  euglycaemic ketoacidosis risk, metformin on the day.
- **Insulin**: commonly reduce basal by ~20–25% the night before (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked); hold prandial while
  fasting. Use local protocol.
- **Antiplatelets**: never stop dual antiplatelet therapy after a recent stent without
  the cardiologist; aspirin decisions depend on stent/bleeding risk.
- **Anticoagulants**: DOAC interruption by drug, renal function and bleed risk (e.g.
  apixaban/rivaroxaban roughly 1 day off for low-bleed-risk, 2 days for high-bleed-risk
  with normal renal function; dabigatran longer as CrCl falls (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)). Warfarin: stop ~5 days
  before; **bridging** only for high thrombotic risk (mechanical mitral valve, recent
  stroke/VTE) — BRIDGE (Douketis, NEJM 2015; 1,884 patients) showed no bridging was non-inferior for arterial thromboembolism (0.4% vs 0.3%) with less major bleeding (1.3% vs 3.2%); patients with CHADS2 5-6 were under-represented.
  Verify intervals against current ACCP/local tables.

## 4. Non-cardiac risks to name
Pulmonary (OSA screen with STOP-BANG, COPD optimisation), renal (baseline creatinine,
avoid nephrotoxins), delirium risk in older adults, frailty, anaemia (treat iron
deficiency weeks ahead), glycaemic control (HbA1c), VTE plan.

## 5. The consult note
Lead with a one-line answer, then: risk estimate with tool used, recommendations as a
numbered list of orders (drug, dose change, timing), post-op monitoring (troponin
surveillance if your guideline recommends it), and who to call. **Avoid "cleared"** —
write "acceptable risk to proceed; recommendations below".

## Gotchas
- Re-read recommendations against the surgical date; a "hold 3 days" order written on
  the wrong day is useless.
- Co-management needs explicit role division: who orders fluids, anticoagulation restart,
  glucose.
- Restarting anticoagulation post-op depends on surgical haemostasis — agree it with the
  surgeon in writing.

## Verify
- [ ] Urgency, surgical risk class and risk tool are documented.
- [ ] Any test ordered has a stated management consequence.
- [ ] Every home medication has an explicit pre-op and post-op instruction.
- [ ] Anticoagulant stop/restart dates are written as calendar dates.
- [ ] The note answers the question asked without the word "cleared".
