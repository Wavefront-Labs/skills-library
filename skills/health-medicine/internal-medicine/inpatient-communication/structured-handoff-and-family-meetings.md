# Structured handoff and family meetings

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** make the plan survive shift changes and make difficult conversations
produce a clear, documented, patient-centred decision.

## Part A — Handoffs (I-PASS)
Give each patient in the same order, ideally face-to-face with a written copy:
- **I — Illness severity:** stable / "watcher" / unstable. Say it first; it sets attention.
- **P — Patient summary:** one-liner (age, key comorbidities, reason for admission,
  hospital day), key events, current active problems and plan, code status.
- **A — Action list:** concrete tasks with owner and time ("check 18:00 potassium;
  if < 3.5 replace per protocol").
- **S — Situation awareness and contingencies:** *if/then* statements: "If he spikes
  > 38.5, blood cultures ×2 and broaden to pip-tazo; if BP < 90 systolic, 500 mL bolus
  and call ICU outreach."
- **S — Synthesis by receiver:** the receiver summarises back and asks questions.

Tips: hand over in a quiet place; protect the time; update the written tool the same
day; flag pending results that someone must chase. For a quick escalation call
(nurse → doctor, team → consultant) use **SBAR** (situation, background, assessment,
recommendation) and end with an explicit request.

## Part B — Family / goals-of-care meeting
### Before
- Agree among the team on the medical facts, prognosis range and a recommendation.
  Disagreements in front of the family destroy trust.
- Identify the decision-maker (patient with capacity, or legally recognised
  surrogate) and any advance directive.
- Book a private room, enough chairs, interpreter (professional, not family), bedside
  nurse, and 30–60 min with pagers handed off.

### During (a workable script)
1. **Introductions and purpose:** "We're meeting to update you and talk about what
   comes next."
2. **Ask** what they understand: "What have you been told so far?"
3. **Tell** in small chunks, plain language, with a warning shot: "I'm afraid I have
   some serious news." Pause. Give the headline before the details.
4. **Ask** again: "What questions do you have?" Check understanding.
5. **Respond to emotion** before more facts — **NURSE**: Name ("this is a lot"),
   Understand, Respect ("you've been at her bedside every day"), Support ("we'll stay
   with you through this"), Explore ("tell me more about what worries you most").
6. **Explore values:** "If she could hear this, what would matter most to her?"
   "What would she consider unacceptable?" — substituted judgement, not "what do you
   want us to do?"
7. **Recommend** a plan based on those values ("Given what you've told us, I'd
   recommend…"). Families often find a recommendation less burdensome than an open menu.
8. **Summarise and next steps:** what will happen today, who to contact, when you
   will meet again.

### After
Document in a dedicated note: who attended, information shared, the family's
understanding, values expressed, decision (e.g. code status, time-limited trial with
clear end points), and follow-up. Update orders (code status, consults e.g. palliative
care) immediately and hand over the outcome.

## Consult etiquette (briefly)
When asking another service: state a specific question, urgency and your callback
number. When consulting: answer the question asked, give concrete recommendations,
and call the primary team for anything urgent rather than relying on the note.

## Gotchas
- "Do you want everything done?" is a false choice; ask about goals, then translate
  into treatments.
- Silence after bad news is useful; don't fill it with statistics.
- Prognosis is better given as ranges and "hope for the best, prepare for the worst"
  than a single number.
- Code status conversations held without discussing the overall picture produce
  unstable decisions.
- Legal surrogate rules differ by jurisdiction; check local law and policy.

## Verify
- [ ] Every handoff has severity, one-liner, actions with owners and if/then contingencies.
- [ ] Receiver synthesised back; pending results have an owner.
- [ ] Family meeting pre-briefed with a team consensus and recommendation.
- [ ] Emotion acknowledged before information; values explored.
- [ ] Decision documented and orders updated the same day.
