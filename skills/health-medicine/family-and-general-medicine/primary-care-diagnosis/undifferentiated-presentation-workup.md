# Working up an undifferentiated presentation in primary care

**Goal:** turn a vague complaint ("tired all the time", "dizzy", "not right") into either a
working diagnosis, a safe period of watchful waiting, or a timely referral, without missing
the serious-but-rare cause and without over-investigating the benign-but-common one.

## 1. Frame the consultation (first 2 minutes)
- Open question, then **listen without interrupting** for the first ~60–90 s; the key
  clue is often volunteered early.
- Ask **ICE**: ideas ("what do you think is going on?"), concerns ("what worries you most?"),
  expectations ("what were you hoping we'd do today?"). A hidden fear (cancer, a parent's
  illness) changes the explanation you must give.
- Clarify the timeline: onset, duration, trajectory (better, worse, fluctuating), and
  **why now** — why did they come today rather than last month?

## 2. Murtagh's five-question safe diagnostic strategy
1. What is the **probability diagnosis** (the common cause in this age/setting)?
2. What **serious disorders must not be missed** (cardiovascular, neoplasia, severe infection)?
3. What conditions are **often missed** (pitfalls: e.g. depression, drug side-effect,
   pregnancy, foreign body, early hypothyroidism, coeliac disease)?
4. Could this be one of the **masquerades** (depression, diabetes, drugs, anaemia,
   thyroid/endocrine, spinal dysfunction, UTI)?
5. Is the patient **trying to tell me something** (psychosocial, abuse, health anxiety)?
Write the differential in these buckets in the notes; it documents your reasoning.

## 3. Red-flag screen (always, briefly)
Unintentional weight loss (commonly >5% over 6–12 months), night sweats, fever, new pain
waking from sleep, bleeding from any orifice, progressive neurological deficit, new
symptoms over ~50 years old, immunosuppression, and a "first-ever/worst-ever" pattern.
Map positives to the relevant urgent pathway (e.g. NICE NG12 suspected-cancer criteria in
the UK; national equivalents elsewhere — thresholds differ by country).

## 4. Examine with intent
Examine to confirm or refute the top 2–3 items, plus vital signs every time (pulse, BP,
temperature, respiratory rate, SpO2, weight/BMI). Record negatives that matter
("no lymphadenopathy, no hepatosplenomegaly").

## 5. Proportionate investigation
- Test only when the result would change management. A typical first-line panel for
  fatigue: FBC, U&E/creatinine, LFTs, glucose/HbA1c, TSH, ferritin, CRP or ESR, ±
  coeliac serology, ± pregnancy test. Exact panels vary by guideline.
- Avoid "screening" panels in low-probability presentations: false positives generate
  cascades of further tests and anxiety.
- Consider pre-test probability: a normal test in a high-probability patient may still
  need follow-up.

## 6. Use time as a diagnostic tool — safely
Watchful waiting is legitimate if: no red flags, the patient is stable, and a **specific
safety-net** is given:
- *What* to look out for (named symptoms), *how long* before review (e.g. 2–4 weeks),
  and *how* to get help sooner (urgent line, out-of-hours, emergency).
- Document the safety-net verbatim in the record.
- Book the follow-up or make it the patient's explicit task — not "come back if needed".

## Gotchas
- **Anchoring** on the first label, especially one from a previous clinician or a
  referral letter. Ask "what else could this be?" before closing.
- **Frequent attenders** get serious diseases too; re-examine when the story changes.
- **Medication review** is diagnostic: check new drugs, OTC, supplements and recent dose changes.
- Older adults present atypically (confusion, falls, "off legs" for sepsis or MI).
- Language barriers: use a professional interpreter, not family.
- Third consultation for the same unexplained symptom = consider a fresh pair of eyes,
  investigation or referral (a common rule-of-thumb, not a formal standard).

## Verify
- [ ] ICE elicited and the patient's main concern explicitly addressed.
- [ ] Differential documented under probability / not-to-miss / pitfalls / masquerades.
- [ ] Red-flag screen recorded, including negatives.
- [ ] Each test ordered has a stated reason and a plan for who acts on the result.
- [ ] Safety-net advice (what, when, how) given and documented; follow-up booked.
