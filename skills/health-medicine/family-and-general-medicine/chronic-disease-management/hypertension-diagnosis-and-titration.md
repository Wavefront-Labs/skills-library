# Hypertension: diagnosis, workup and step-wise titration in primary care

**Goal:** a confirmed diagnosis (not a single clinic reading), a documented cardiovascular
risk and end-organ assessment, and BP brought to target within a few months using a
predictable step-wise regimen the patient can adhere to.

## 1. Measure properly
- Seated 5 min, back supported, arm at heart level, correct cuff size (bladder ~80% of arm
  circumference), no talking, no caffeine/smoking in the prior 30 min (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
- First visit: measure **both arms**; if the difference is >15 mmHg, repeat; if it persists use the arm with the higher reading (NICE NG136 1.2.1); consider vascular disease.
- Check for postural drop in older adults, diabetics and anyone with dizziness.

## 2. Confirm out of office
- Clinic BP 140/90 to 180/120 (NICE NG136 1.2.3-1.2.4) → offer **ABPM** (at least 2 readings per hour while awake, average of at least 14) or, if unsuitable, **HBPM** (two consecutive readings at least 1 min apart, twice daily, at least 4 and ideally 7 days; discard day 1, average the rest). Confirm hypertension if ABPM daytime or HBPM average is ≥135/85 (1.2.8).
- NICE stage 1: clinic 140/90-159/99 with ABPM/HBPM average 135/85-149/94; stage 2: clinic ≥160/100 and <180/120.
- **ACC/AHA** labels ≥130/80 as hypertension (stage 1). The 2025 AHA/ACC guideline (Hypertension 2025;82:e212, replacing 2017) uses PREVENT risk to time drug treatment at stage 1 and favours starting two first-line agents, ideally a single-pill combination, when BP is more than 20/10 mmHg above goal (secondary sources: ACC summary pages, Healio (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)). Thresholds and targets differ between guidelines (NICE NG136 as updated 2023, ESC 2024, AHA/ACC 2025): use the one your service adopts, document it, and confirm the current version.
- Clinic ≥180/120: assess for target organ damage; NICE 1.5.2 advises same-day specialist assessment if retinal haemorrhage or papilloedema, or life-threatening symptoms (new confusion, chest pain, heart failure signs, AKI).

## 3. Baseline workup
Urine albumin:creatinine ratio and dipstick for haematuria, creatinine/eGFR and
electrolytes, HbA1c, lipids, ECG (LVH, AF), fundoscopy where relevant, and a 10-year CV risk
score (QRISK3, PCE, SCORE2 by region). Ask about NSAIDs, decongestants, oral contraceptive,
liquorice, alcohol, sleep apnoea symptoms.

**Screen for secondary causes** when: age <40, resistant hypertension, sudden worsening,
hypokalaemia (think primary aldosteronism — aldosterone:renin ratio), paroxysms (phaeo),
renal bruit or flash pulmonary oedema.

## 4. Step-wise drug therapy (NICE-style ladder; check local formulary)
1. **Step 1:** ACEi or ARB (NICE 1.4.32) if type 2 diabetes (any age or family origin; ARB preferred over ACEi for Black African/African-Caribbean origin, 1.4.30), or age <55 and not of Black African/Caribbean origin; otherwise a CCB (e.g. amlodipine). Some guidelines favour starting
   with a **two-drug single-pill combination** for most patients (ESH).
2. **Step 2:** ACEi/ARB + CCB, or + thiazide-like diuretic (indapamide, chlortalidone).
3. **Step 3:** ACEi/ARB + CCB + thiazide-like.
4. **Step 4 (resistant):** confirm elevated BP out of office, assess postural hypotension and discuss adherence first (1.4.47); then if K⁺ ≤4.5 mmol/L consider low-dose spironolactone (monitor sodium, potassium and renal function within 1 month, 1.4.50), otherwise consider an alpha- or beta-blocker (1.4.51); seek specialist advice if still uncontrolled on 4 drugs (1.4.52).
- Never combine ACEi + ARB (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
- Review interval after each change: local practice (NICE does not fix one in the passages checked); aim to reach target within ~3 months (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).

## 5. Targets (typical)
NICE NG136 clinic targets: below 140/90 under 80 years (hypertension with or without type 2 diabetes), below 150/90 at 80+; ABPM/HBPM equivalents are in the guideline's tables. AHA/ACC 2025 sets <130/80 for most adults when tolerated (secondary source (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked); ESC 2024: 120-129 systolic for most (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)). Individualise for frailty and postural hypotension.

## Gotchas
- **White-coat** effect: treating on clinic readings alone overtreats; **masked**
  hypertension (normal clinic, high home) is missed without HBPM.
- ACEi/ARB: check creatinine and K⁺ 1–2 weeks after starting/uptitrating; a creatinine
  rise up to ~30% is usually tolerated, larger rises need review (renal artery stenosis) (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
- ACEi cough → switch to ARB. Angioedema → stop and do not switch casually.
- ACEi/ARB/spironolactone are teratogenic or unsuitable in pregnancy — check contraception plans.
- Amlodipine ankle oedema is dose-related, not fluid overload; diuretics don't fix it.
- Non-adherence is the commonest cause of "resistant" hypertension.

## Verify
- [ ] Diagnosis confirmed with ABPM/HBPM averages recorded.
- [ ] Urine ACR, eGFR, HbA1c, lipids, ECG and CV risk score documented.
- [ ] Secondary causes considered where criteria met.
- [ ] Bloods rechecked after ACEi/ARB/diuretic/spironolactone changes.
- [ ] Target stated, review interval set, annual review booked once controlled.
