# Airway, ASA grading, fasting and medication planning

> General clinical/professional reference, not patient-specific advice. Medication-hold timings and fasting rules change frequently; check current society and local guidance.

**Goal:** arrive on the day with no surprises — a predicted airway plan, known risks,
correct fasting, the right medicines held or continued, and an informed patient.

## 1. History essentials
- Previous anaesthetics: difficult airway, PONV, awareness, malignant hyperthermia
  (personal **or family**), pseudocholinesterase deficiency.
- Cardiorespiratory: angina, heart failure, valve disease, arrhythmia, devices,
  COPD/asthma control, OSA.
- **Functional capacity:** can climb two flights of stairs / ≥ 4 METs without symptoms;
  the DASI questionnaire is more reliable than self-estimate.
- Bleeding history, reflux, diabetes control (HbA1c), renal/liver disease, frailty,
  cognition, alcohol/substance use, allergies, pregnancy possibility.

## 2. Airway examination (combine predictors — each alone is weak)
- Mallampati class (sitting, tongue out, no phonation), mouth opening (< 3 cm
  concerning), thyromental distance (< 6 cm), mandibular protrusion (upper lip bite
  test), neck extension, dentition, beard, neck circumference/radiotherapy.
- **Difficult mask ventilation** predictors: beard, obesity, edentulous, snoring/OSA,
  age > 55, neck radiation.
- Document a plan A/B/C if difficulty is predicted (see
  `health-medicine.anesthesiology.airway-management` skills).

## 3. Risk grading
- **ASA physical status** I–VI (+E for emergency) — a description of health, not a
  risk score for the operation. Use the ASA's published examples to grade consistently.
- Cardiac: surgical risk + patient factors (RCRI, or calculators such as NSQIP).
  Testing only if it would change management; poor/unknown functional capacity with
  elevated risk → consider BNP/NT-proBNP or further testing per guideline.
- **STOP-Bang ≥ 3** suggests OSA risk; ≥ 5 high risk — plan monitoring and opioid-sparing.

## 4. Fasting (healthy elective adults, ASA 2023 basis)
- **Clear fluids until 2 h** (carbohydrate drinks allowed in many pathways).
- Breast milk 4 h; infant formula/non-human milk 6 h; light meal 6 h; fatty meal 8 h.
- Avoid unnecessarily long fasts — they cause dehydration, hypoglycaemia, distress.
- Delayed gastric emptying (gastroparesis, opioids, GLP-1 RA, labour, obesity with
  symptoms) changes the risk — consider gastric ultrasound and full-stomach precautions.

## 5. Medication plan (verify current guidance)
- **Usually continue:** beta-blockers, statins, most antiepileptics, inhalers, thyroid
  medicines, most psychiatric medicines.
- **Often hold:** ACE inhibitors/ARBs morning of surgery (practice varies); diuretics
  on the day; **SGLT2 inhibitors** typically 3 days before (ertugliflozin 4) — euglycaemic
  ketoacidosis risk; insulin reduced per protocol.
- **GLP-1 RAs:** guidance is evolving (2023 advice to hold weekly agents 1 week was
  softened in later multisociety guidance toward individual risk assessment and liquid
  diet 24 h) — follow local policy.
- **Anticoagulants:** DOAC hold by drug, renal function and bleeding risk (e.g. PAUSE
  protocol); warfarin ~5 days with INR check; bridging rarely needed except high-risk
  mechanical valves. Antiplatelets with recent stents → cardiology discussion.
- Herbal supplements: ask; several affect bleeding.

## 6. Choosing and consenting the technique
Match technique to surgery and patient (GA, regional, sedation, combined). Discuss
common and serious risks, alternatives, and what will happen on the day.

## Gotchas
- "Normal airway" documented without an exam.
- Patients told "nothing after midnight" — overly long fasts.
- Missing a family history of MH or suxamethonium apnoea.
