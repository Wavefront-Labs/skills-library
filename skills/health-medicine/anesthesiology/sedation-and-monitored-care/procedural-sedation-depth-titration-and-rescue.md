# Procedural sedation: depth titration and rescue

> General clinical/professional reference, not patient-specific advice. Who may give which agents and at what depth is set by credentialing and local policy.

**Goal:** a comfortable, still patient who keeps their airway and breathing — and a
practitioner able to rescue them from **one level deeper** than intended.

## 1. The continuum
| Level | Response | Airway | Breathing | CVS |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Minimal (anxiolysis) | Normal to voice | Unaffected | Unaffected | Unaffected |
| Moderate ("conscious") | Purposeful to voice/light touch | No intervention | Adequate | Usually maintained |
| Deep | Purposeful to repeated/painful stimulus | May need help | May be inadequate | Usually maintained |
| General anaesthesia | Unrousable | Often needs intervention | Often inadequate | May be impaired |
Depth drifts: patients slide from moderate to deep with a stimulus pause (e.g. scope
withdrawn). Withdrawal from pain is not "purposeful response".

## 2. Patient selection and preparation
- Assess like any anaesthetic: airway, ASA, OSA, cardiorespiratory reserve, fasting
  (procedure urgency vs aspiration risk), allergies, prior sedation problems.
- High-risk (ASA ≥ 3, difficult airway, severe OSA, morbid obesity, extremes of age,
  long or prone procedures) → anaesthesia-led care.
- Consent, IV access, oxygen, suction, bag-valve-mask, airway adjuncts, SGA,
  reversal agents, defibrillator; a separate person monitoring the patient.

## 3. Monitoring
SpO2, NIBP, ECG (as indicated), **waveform capnography** for moderate and deeper
sedation — it detects apnoea and hypoventilation minutes before SpO2 falls, especially
with supplemental oxygen. Record observations at regular intervals (e.g. every 5 min).

## 4. Agents and titration (typical adult approach)
- **Midazolam** 0.5–1 mg increments, wait 2–3 min between (peak effect is slower than
  people expect); elderly start lower.
- **Fentanyl** 25–50 µg increments; synergy with benzodiazepines → apnoea risk.
- **Propofol** (by trained practitioners): small boluses (e.g. 10–20 mg) or infusion;
  narrow window between moderate and GA; no reversal agent.
- **Ketamine** 0.5–1 mg/kg dissociative sedation (common in ED/paediatrics); preserves
  airway reflexes mostly; emergence reactions, laryngospasm, vomiting.
- **Dexmedetomidine**: cooperative sedation, bradycardia/hypotension, slow onset.
- **Remimazolam** (where licensed): short-acting benzodiazepine, reversible with flumazenil.
Titrate to effect; don't stack doses before the previous one peaks.

## 5. Rescue from oversedation
1. **Stimulate** and stop further drugs; tell the proceduralist to pause.
2. Airway manoeuvres: jaw thrust, chin lift, adjuncts; increase oxygen.
3. Bag-mask ventilation if apnoeic/hypoventilating.
4. Reverse: **naloxone** (titrated, e.g. 40–100 µg IV increments to avoid abrupt pain/
   withdrawal; repeat as needed — shorter half-life than many opioids), **flumazenil**
   200 µg then 100 µg increments (seizure risk in chronic benzodiazepine users).
5. Escalate to anaesthesia/advanced airway if not rapidly improving.
Laryngospasm (ketamine, secretions): jaw thrust with pressure at the laryngospasm notch,
CPAP with 100% O2, small dose of propofol, then suxamethonium if needed.

## 6. Recovery and discharge
Observe until back to baseline; use a scoring system (e.g. Aldrete/PADSS). After reversal
agents, observe long enough for resedation risk (often ≥ 2 h after naloxone). Discharge
with a responsible adult, written instructions, no driving/legal decisions for 24 h.

## Gotchas
- Supplemental O2 masks hypoventilation on pulse oximetry — watch capnography.
- The proceduralist cannot also monitor sedation safely.
- Elderly patients reach peak benzodiazepine effect late; most oversedation follows a redose.

## Verify
- [ ] Pre-sedation assessment and fasting/urgency decision documented.
- [ ] Rescue equipment and reversal agents checked before starting.
- [ ] Capnography used for moderate/deep sedation; obs recorded.
- [ ] Doses and times charted; target depth stated.
- [ ] Discharge criteria met and instructions given.
