# Difficult airway algorithm and front-of-neck access

> General clinical/professional reference for trained airway practitioners, not patient-specific advice. Practise the algorithm and FONA in simulation; local equipment and guidelines take precedence.

**Goal:** keep the patient oxygenated. Intubation is a means, not the goal. Most airway
deaths in audits (NAP4) involved failure to plan, too many attempts, and delayed
front-of-neck access.

## 1. Plan before you start
- Predict difficulty (see `health-medicine.anesthesiology.preanesthetic-assessment.airway-asa-fasting-and-medication-planning`).
- If difficulty is predicted and the patient is cooperative: consider **awake tracheal
  intubation** (flexible scope or video laryngoscope with topical anaesthesia ± light
  sedation).
- Verbalise plans A–D to the team; equipment (VL, bougie, SGA sizes, scalpel kit) open
  or at hand; **capnography connected**.
- Position: ear-to-sternal-notch (ramp in obesity), head-up; preoxygenate; apnoeic
  oxygenation via nasal cannula/HFNO.

## 2. Plan A: tracheal intubation
- **Maximum 3 attempts** (+1 by a more experienced operator) — each attempt changes
  something: device (video laryngoscope early), position, external laryngeal
  manipulation, bougie/stylet, full paralysis.
- Confirm with **sustained waveform capnography** — "no trace = wrong place" (except in
  cardiac arrest with absent circulation, where flat trace still mandates checking).
- Oxygenate with mask between attempts; SpO2 falling → stop and oxygenate.

## 3. Plan B: supraglottic airway
- Declare failed intubation. Insert a **second-generation SGA** (gastric drain port);
  max 3 attempts (size/type change).
- If oxygenating: **stop and think** — wake the patient, intubate through the SGA with a
  fibreoptic scope, proceed with the SGA (rarely), or tracheostomy/FONA electively.

## 4. Plan C: final face-mask attempt
Two-person, two-handed mask with oropharyngeal/nasopharyngeal airways, **full
neuromuscular blockade** (relieves laryngospasm, improves compliance). If oxygenation
works → wake the patient (sugammadex for rocuronium if appropriate) or plan definitive airway.

## 5. Plan D: CICO and front-of-neck access
- **Declare "can't intubate, can't oxygenate"** out loud — this triggers FONA.
  Ensure full paralysis.
- **Scalpel–bougie–tube** (DAS technique):
  1. Extend neck, laryngeal handshake to identify the cricothyroid membrane.
  2. Transverse stab incision through the membrane with a size 10 blade, cutting edge
     toward you; rotate 90° (sharp edge caudal).
  3. Slide bougie alongside the blade into the trachea; railroad a lubricated **6.0
     cuffed tube**; inflate, ventilate, confirm with capnography.
  4. **Impalpable membrane:** 8–10 cm vertical midline skin incision, blunt dissection
     with fingers to identify the larynx, then proceed as above.
- Cannula techniques have higher failure rates in audits; most guidelines favour scalpel.

## 6. After the event
Document what happened and what worked; tell the patient, write a **difficult airway
letter/alert** for the record and the GP; report per local systems.

## Human factors
Fixation on intubation is the classic failure. Use a shared mental model (Vortex: three
lifelines — face mask, SGA, tube — each "best effort" then move on; FONA when all fail).
Assign a timekeeper and someone watching SpO2.

## Gotchas
- Repeated DL attempts traumatise the airway and convert "can't intubate" into CICO.
- Fear of FONA delays it; decide by SpO2 trajectory, not by the number of attempts alone.
- Oesophageal intubation still happens — trust capnography over chest sounds.

## Verify
- [ ] Plans A–D verbalised; VL, SGA, scalpel kit available; capnography connected.
- [ ] Attempts limited and each one changed something.
- [ ] Tube confirmed with sustained ETCO2 waveform.
- [ ] CICO declared promptly; FONA practised in simulation in the last year.
- [ ] Airway alert documented and communicated.
