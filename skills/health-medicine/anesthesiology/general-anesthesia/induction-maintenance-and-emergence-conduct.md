# Induction, maintenance and emergence

> General clinical/professional reference for trained anaesthesia providers, not patient-specific advice. Doses are typical adult ranges and must be adjusted to the patient and local formulary.

**Goal:** unconscious, still, physiologically stable during surgery; awake, breathing,
strong and comfortable afterwards.

## 1. Before induction
- WHO sign-in, machine check done, suction working, airway plan A/B/C with equipment
  in the room, IV running, monitoring (SpO2, ECG, NIBP or arterial line, capnography
  ready, temperature for > 30 min cases, **quantitative NMB monitor** if relaxant).
- **Preoxygenate** to end-tidal O2 ≥ ~0.85–0.9 (3 min tidal breathing or 8 deep
  breaths (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)), head-up in obese patients; consider high-flow nasal oxygen for apnoeic
  oxygenation in difficult or high-risk airways.

## 2. Induction choices (typical adult IV doses)
- **Propofol** US label (Diprivan): 2–2.5 mg/kg for most adults under 55 who are ASA I-II; about 1–1.5 mg/kg for elderly, debilitated or ASA III-IV patients. Most hypotension of the three.
- **Etomidate** US label (Amidate): 0.2–0.6 mg/kg, usual 0.3 mg/kg over 30-60 s; haemodynamically stable; adrenal suppression debate.
- **Ketamine** US label (Ketalar): IV induction 1–4.5 mg/kg, average about 2 mg/kg; maintains sympathetic tone; may still drop BP in
  catecholamine-depleted patients.
- Opioid co-induction (e.g. fentanyl 1–2 µg/kg (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)) blunts the laryngoscopy response.
- **RSI** (full stomach): no or gentle mask ventilation per modern practice,
  rocuronium 0.6–1.2 mg/kg (US label, rapid sequence intubation, intubating conditions in most patients in under 2 minutes) or suxamethonium 1–1.5 mg/kg (common practice; the US Anectine label gives an average adult dose of 0.6 mg/kg, optimum range 0.3–1.1 mg/kg, and 1 mg/kg for emergency intubation in older children, so confirm the dose against your formulary); cricoid pressure use is debated.
- **Post-induction hypotension** is common and associated with harm: reduce doses,
  have a vasopressor drawn up (phenylephrine/metaraminol, ephedrine, noradrenaline).

## 3. Maintenance
- **Volatile:** titrate to age-adjusted MAC (≥ ~0.7 MAC with opioids usually prevents
  awareness (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)); end-tidal agent alarms on.
- **TIVA/TCI:** propofol by target-controlled infusion (Marsh/Schnider/Eleveld models);
  **use processed EEG (BIS etc.)** with TIVA plus paralysis — NAP5 linked awareness to
  TIVA without depth monitoring and disconnected lines (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked). Keep the IV cannula visible.
- Haemodynamics: MAP generally ≥ 65 mmHg or within ~20% of baseline (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked); treat cause
  (depth, volume, vasodilation, bleeding, surgical compression).
- Ventilation: VT 6–8 mL/kg predicted body weight, PEEP 5+ (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked), recruitment as needed.
- Temperature: active warming; hypothermia increases bleeding and infection.
- PONV prophylaxis by risk (Apfel factors): dexamethasone, 5-HT3 antagonist, TIVA,
  avoid nitrous oxide in high-risk.

## 4. Neuromuscular blockade
- Quantitative monitoring (TOF ratio) is now the standard of care in many guidelines;
  qualitative "4 twitches felt" cannot detect residual block.
- Reverse: **sugammadex** for rocuronium/vecuronium (US label: 2 mg/kg once the second twitch of TOF has reappeared; 4 mg/kg at 1-2 post-tetanic counts with no TOF response; 16 mg/kg for immediate reversal about 3 minutes after rocuronium 1.2 mg/kg), or neostigmine (+ glycopyrrolate) only once
  recovery is established (TOF count 4 (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)). **Extubate at TOF ratio ≥ 0.9** (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).

## 5. Emergence
- Stop agent at the right time (context-sensitive), analgesia established, PONV covered.
- Suction oropharynx under direct vision; oxygen 100% (or lower FiO2 to limit
  atelectasis per practice).
- Extubation criteria: TOF ≥ 0.9, regular adequate spontaneous breathing, eye opening
  to command or deep extubation plan, haemodynamically stable, normothermic.
- Higher-risk extubations follow a structured plan (DAS extubation guidelines).
- Handover to recovery using a structured format.

## Gotchas
- Elderly and frail: induction doses are reduced (propofol label: about 1-1.5 mg/kg); arm–brain time is slow, so wait before redosing.
- Volatile off, TIVA not started (or line tissued) during transfer — awareness window.
- Sugammadex and hormonal contraception: the label requires an additional non-hormonal method for 7 days after administration.

## Verify
- [ ] Preoxygenation end-tidal O2 target reached.
- [ ] Vasopressor ready before induction; MAP maintained.
- [ ] Depth monitored (MAC alarms or processed EEG with TIVA).
- [ ] TOF ratio ≥ 0.9 documented before extubation.
- [ ] PONV prophylaxis given by risk; structured handover done.
