# Perioperative anaphylaxis, malignant hyperthermia and massive haemorrhage

> General clinical/professional reference for trained anaesthesia teams, not patient-specific advice. Use your institution's cognitive aids in the moment; doses are typical adult values.

**Goal:** recognise early, call for help early, and run the right algorithm with a
coordinated team. In crises, memory fails — read the card.

## 1. Crisis team basics
- **Declare the crisis** aloud and call for help (and the senior anaesthetist).
- Assign roles: leader (hands off), airway, drugs, reader of the cognitive aid,
  scribe/timekeeper, runner (MH kit, blood, defibrillator).
- **Closed-loop communication**: "Give adrenaline 50 micrograms IV" → "50 micrograms
  adrenaline given."
- Tell the surgeon what's happening and what you need (stop, pack, finish quickly).

## 2. Perioperative anaphylaxis
- Clues: unexplained hypotension (commonest and sometimes only sign), bronchospasm,
  falling ETCO2, tachycardia or bradycardia, rash (often absent early, and drapes hide it).
  Common culprits: neuromuscular blockers, antibiotics, chlorhexidine, patent blue dye.
- Actions:
  1. Remove likely triggers (stop the infusion, remove chlorhexidine-coated lines); 100% O2.
  2. **Adrenaline IV** titrated: 50 µg boluses (adult) repeated; start infusion if > 2–3
     boluses needed. IM 0.5 mg if no IV access/monitoring.
  3. Rapid fluid (crystalloid 20 mL/kg), legs up, consider vasopressin or glucagon
     (on beta-blockers) for refractory cases.
  4. Bronchospasm: increase volatile, salbutamol.
  5. Cardiac arrest → ALS with full-dose adrenaline.
- **Mast cell tryptase** at ~1 h (as soon as resuscitation allows), at 2–4 h, and
  baseline ≥ 24 h. Refer to allergy clinic; report to national systems.

## 3. Malignant hyperthermia
- Clues: **unexplained rising ETCO2** despite increased ventilation, tachycardia,
  masseter spasm, generalised rigidity, metabolic acidosis, hyperkalaemia, late
  hyperthermia. Triggers: volatile agents, suxamethonium.
- Actions:
  1. Stop volatile, **hyperventilate with 100% O2 at high fresh gas flow**; insert
     activated charcoal filters if available; switch to TIVA.
  2. **Dantrolene** 2.5 mg/kg IV, repeat 1 mg/kg boluses until signs resolve (up to
     ~10 mg/kg). Traditional vials (20 mg) take many people to reconstitute — assign
     helpers; newer formulations reconstitute faster where available.
  3. Treat hyperkalaemia (insulin–glucose, calcium), acidosis, arrhythmias (avoid
     calcium channel blockers with dantrolene), active cooling, urine output target.
  4. ICU monitoring ≥ 24 h (recrudescence), CK and myoglobin; refer family for testing.

## 4. Massive haemorrhage
- **Activate the massive transfusion protocol** early; communicate with the blood bank.
- Tranexamic acid 1 g early (trauma/obstetric pathways specify timing).
- Balanced transfusion (e.g. RBC:FFP close to 1:1 initially in trauma; platelets),
  then guide with **viscoelastic tests** (ROTEM/TEG) and labs; fibrinogen
  replacement (concentrate or cryoprecipitate) for low fibrinogen.
- Avoid the lethal triad: warm everything, correct calcium (citrate), acidosis.
- Surgical control is the definitive treatment: ask for packing, pressure, damage control.

## 5. Cardiac arrest under anaesthesia
Often witnessed, monitored and reversible: check causes (hypoxia, hypovolaemia,
anaphylaxis, LAST, tension pneumothorax, high spinal, vagal reflexes, drug error,
gas embolism) while running ALS; stop anaesthetic agents; ETCO2 guides CPR quality.

## Gotchas
- Anaphylaxis treated as "anaesthetic hypotension" with repeated small vasopressor doses.
- Treating ETCO2 rise as a CO2 absorber problem without considering MH.
- Starting MTP late — first-pack lag time is long.

## Verify
- [ ] Cognitive aids accessible in every theatre; staff know the MH kit location.
- [ ] Crisis declared, roles assigned, scribe recording times.
- [ ] Tryptase samples timed and labelled after suspected anaphylaxis.
- [ ] Dantrolene stock and reconstitution plan checked regularly.
- [ ] Debrief, incident report and patient/family follow-up done.
