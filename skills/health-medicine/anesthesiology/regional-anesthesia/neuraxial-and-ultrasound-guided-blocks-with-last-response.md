# Neuraxial and ultrasound-guided blocks, with LAST response

> General clinical/professional reference for trained practitioners, not patient-specific advice. Anticoagulant timing tables are updated periodically — check the current ASRA/ESRA or national version before every case where it matters.

**Goal:** a block in the right place, on the right side, without nerve injury,
haematoma or local anaesthetic toxicity — and a team ready if toxicity occurs.

## 1. Before the needle
- Consent including failure, nerve injury (transient common, permanent rare), and
  block-specific risks (e.g. phrenic palsy with interscalene).
- **Anticoagulation check** (neuraxial and deep/non-compressible blocks): time since
  last dose, renal function, platelets. Typical examples — verify in current tables:
  prophylactic LMWH 12 h, therapeutic LMWH 24 h, apixaban/rivaroxaban 72 h (longer in
  renal impairment), clopidogrel 5–7 days, aspirin alone acceptable.
- Monitoring, IV access, resuscitation drugs and **lipid emulsion** immediately available.
- **Stop Before You Block:** immediately before needle insertion, re-confirm the side
  with the surgical site mark — wrong-side blocks happen after positioning and delays.

## 2. Local anaesthetic dosing
- Calculate the maximum dose per kg **lean** body weight and total across all sites
  (surgeon infiltration counts). Common upper guides: lidocaine 4.5 mg/kg plain (7 with
  adrenaline), bupivacaine/levobupivacaine 2–2.5 mg/kg, ropivacaine 3 mg/kg — lower in
  elderly, heart failure, liver disease, pregnancy, infants.
- Use the smallest effective volume; fractionate injection with aspiration every 3–5 mL.

## 3. Spinal and epidural technique
- Positioning (sitting or lateral, flexed); identify level (Tuffier's line ≈ L4 or L4/5,
  often inaccurate) — ultrasound helps in obesity or abnormal anatomy.
- Full aseptic technique: mask, sterile gloves, chlorhexidine allowed to dry
  (neurotoxic if carried into the CSF).
- Spinal: pencil-point needle (25–27G) reduces post-dural puncture headache; free CSF
  flow before injecting; hyperbaric solutions spread with position.
- Epidural: loss of resistance (saline), catheter 4–5 cm in space, test dose per local
  practice, then incremental dosing.
- Assess and document block height (cold/pinprick) and motor block.

## 4. Ultrasound-guided peripheral blocks
- Probe and approach chosen so the needle stays **in plane** where possible;
  **never advance without seeing the tip**.
- Hydrodissect with small volumes to confirm tip position; injection should spread
  around, not into, the nerve. High injection pressure or pain/paraesthesia → stop.
- Common blocks: interscalene (shoulder; diaphragm palsy), supraclavicular/
  infraclavicular/axillary (upper limb), femoral and adductor canal (knee),
  popliteal sciatic (foot/ankle), fascial plane blocks (ESP, TAP, serratus).

## 5. LAST: recognise and treat
- Signs: perioral tingling, metallic taste, tinnitus, agitation → seizures → arrhythmia,
  bradycardia, cardiovascular collapse (may present as cardiac arrest without warning).
- **Stop injecting, call for help, airway with 100% O2**, control seizures
  (benzodiazepine; avoid large propofol doses with cardiovascular instability).
- **Lipid emulsion 20%:** bolus 1.5 mL/kg over 2–3 min, infusion 0.25 mL/kg/min;
  repeat bolus / double infusion if unstable; max ~12 mL/kg.
- Cardiac arrest: CPR with **reduced adrenaline doses (≤ 1 µg/kg)**, avoid vasopressin,
  calcium channel blockers, beta-blockers and lidocaine; consider ECMO; prolonged resuscitation.

## Gotchas
- Missing infiltration doses from the surgeon in the total.
- Catheters removed without re-checking anticoagulant timing.
- Persistent or new motor block after an epidural: urgent MRI for haematoma/abscess.

## Verify
- [ ] Anticoagulant timing checked against current table and documented.
- [ ] Stop Before You Block performed with side mark visible.
- [ ] Total LA dose calculated and within limit.
- [ ] Needle tip visualised; injection pressure low; no paraesthesia.
- [ ] Lipid emulsion and LAST checklist in the block area.
