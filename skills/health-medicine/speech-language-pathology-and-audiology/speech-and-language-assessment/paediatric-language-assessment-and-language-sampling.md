# Paediatric language assessment and language sampling

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** decide whether a child has a language disorder (not just a difference), describe
its profile, and set baselines that therapy goals can be measured against.

## 1. Before testing
- **Case history:** milestones, family history of language/literacy difficulty,
  languages at home (who speaks what, how much), schooling, concerns from parents and
  teachers, functional impact.
- **Hearing:** confirm a recent hearing test (or screen). Untreated otitis media with
  effusion or undetected hearing loss mimics language disorder.
- Note other factors (autism features, intellectual disability, neglect) — CATALISE
  uses **DLD** when no known biomedical cause explains it; co-occurring factors are
  recorded, not used to exclude support.

## 2. Standardised tests — use them correctly
- Choose a test **normed on a population that includes children like this one**
  (age, dialect, language exposure). E.g. CELF-5, PLS-5, CASL-2, TOLD, or local norms.
- Report **standard scores with confidence intervals and percentiles**, not age
  equivalents (age equivalents are statistically weak and misleading).
- Typical cut-offs: ~ −1.25 to −1.5 SD below the mean for clinical concern, but the
  test's **sensitivity/specificity at its recommended cut score** matters more —
  check the manual.
- Administer exactly as the manual says; any deviation (repetition, prompting,
  translation) invalidates the norms — report as descriptive only.

## 3. Language sample (the ecological measure)
- Collect **50–100 utterances** (or a set time, e.g. 10 min) in conversation and in
  narrative retell (story elicitation) — narratives are more sensitive in school age.
- Record (audio/video), transcribe in a standard format (e.g. SALT conventions:
  utterance segmentation by C-units for school-age).
- Measures:
  - **MLU in morphemes** (Brown's rules) for younger children (approx. MLU ≈ age in
    years until ~ 4–5, very rough).
  - Number of different words (NDW), grammatical errors per utterance, % utterances with
    errors, subordination index, narrative macrostructure.
- Compare to database norms (SALT) where available, or track against itself over time.

## 4. Bilingual and culturally diverse children
- Assess **both languages** (with an interpreter trained in assessment, not a family
  member) and describe the exposure history.
- A disorder shows in **all** languages; low scores in only the less-exposed language
  usually reflect exposure, not disorder.
- Dialect features (e.g. African American English) are not errors — score with
  dialect-sensitive rules.
- **Dynamic assessment** (test → teach → retest): children with typical learning
  ability respond quickly to brief mediated teaching; poor modifiability supports
  disorder. Useful when norms don't fit.

## 5. Pulling it together
Integrate: standardised scores, sample measures, observation, parent/teacher report,
**functional impact**. Diagnosis needs evidence of impairment **and** functional
impact. Write recommendations as specific, measurable baselines.

## Gotchas
- Reporting age equivalents to parents ("he talks like a 3-year-old").
- Testing in English only for a child who is mostly exposed to another language.
- Skipping the hearing check.
- Over-relying on one score; low scores on a biased test are not diagnostic.

## Verify
- [ ] Hearing status confirmed.
- [ ] Test norms match the child; standard scores with CIs reported.
- [ ] Language sample collected, transcribed and analysed with stated measures.
- [ ] Both languages considered with a qualified interpreter if needed.
- [ ] Conclusion states impairment + functional impact + measurable baselines.
