# Clinical swallow evaluation and IDDSI diet decisions

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** decide safely what a patient can eat and drink now, know when the bedside exam
isn't enough, and avoid the harms of over-restriction as well as aspiration.

## 1. Before offering anything by mouth
- Chart review: diagnosis, chest status (temperature, CXR, oxygen), alertness, ability to
  sit upright, respiratory rate, tracheostomy/cuff status, nutrition and hydration route,
  medications affecting swallow (sedatives, antipsychotics, anticholinergics).
- **Alertness and positioning** are prerequisites: drowsy patients don't get trials.
- Oral care status — poor oral hygiene raises aspiration-pneumonia risk more than
  thin water does in many patients.

## 2. Oral-motor and cranial-nerve exam
Lips, tongue (strength, range, fasciculation), palate elevation, dentition/dentures,
secretions management, voice quality (wet, breathy), volitional cough and swallow,
dysarthria. Laryngeal elevation on dry swallow (fingers on hyoid/thyroid).

## 3. Trial hierarchy
- Start with what is safest for the presentation — often teaspoon of water or ice chip,
  then sips, then cup/straw, then purée, soft and bite-sized, regular solids.
- Watch: oral containment and transit time, number of swallows per bolus, residue,
  **cough, throat clear, wet voice, breathlessness, SpO2 fall** (≥ 2–3% is a weak sign)
  during and after.
- **Silent aspiration is missed by bedside exam** — no cough does not mean no aspiration,
  especially after stroke, in neurological disease, and post-intubation.

## 4. When to request instrumental assessment
- Suspected silent aspiration (recurrent chest infections, unexplained fever), unclear
  pharyngeal phase, considering long-term non-oral feeding, needing to test a strategy.
- **VFSS:** whole swallow, oral to oesophageal entry, bolus flow, timing; radiation.
- **FEES:** bedside, no radiation, good for secretions, residue, laryngeal anatomy and
  fatigue over a meal; whiteout moment hides the swallow itself.
- Score with the **Penetration-Aspiration Scale** (1 = no entry; 2–5 penetration;
  6–8 aspiration, 8 = silent aspiration). Describe residue (e.g. Yale scale) and which
  strategies changed it.

## 5. IDDSI levels (international texture standard)
- **Drinks:** 0 thin, 1 slightly thick, 2 mildly thick, 3 moderately thick (also food
  level 3 liquidised), 4 extremely thick (also food level 4 puréed). Test drinks with
  the **IDDSI flow test (10 mL syringe, 10 s)**.
- **Foods:** 3 liquidised, 4 puréed, 5 minced & moist, 6 soft & bite-sized,
  7 easy-to-chew / regular. Test with the fork-pressure and spoon-tilt tests.
- Thickened fluids reduce penetration for some patients but **reduce intake and
  hydration** and are disliked; reassess regularly.
- **Free-water protocols** (water between meals after oral care) may be appropriate for
  selected patients — follow local protocol.

## 6. Strategies and therapy
- Compensatory: upright 90°, chin tuck (only if shown to help on instrumental exam),
  small sips/bites, slow rate, double swallow, supervised meals.
- Rehabilitative: effortful swallow, Mendelsohn, Shaker/head-lift, EMST, tongue-pressure
  training (IOPI), McNeill programme — choose from the physiological deficit.

## 7. Eating and drinking with acknowledged risks
When restricting oral intake conflicts with the person's wishes or quality of life (e.g.
advanced dementia, end of life), document a shared decision with the patient/family and
team, with the risks and mitigations explicit.

## Gotchas
- "Passed" bedside swallow = no aspiration (it doesn't).
- Ordering the wrong IDDSI level because staff use old terms ("nectar", "honey").
- Using chin tuck routinely without evidence it helps this patient.
- Forgetting oral care, medication form (crushing, liquids) and hydration monitoring.
