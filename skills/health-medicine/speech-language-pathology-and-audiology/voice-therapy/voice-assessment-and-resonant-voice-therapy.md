# Voice assessment and resonant voice therapy

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** know what is wrong with the larynx before treating the voice, measure the
voice reproducibly, and teach efficient phonation that carries into daily speaking.

## 1. Laryngeal examination comes first
- **Voice therapy should not start without a laryngeal view** by ENT/laryngology (or an
  SLP within scope) — AAO-HNS: visualise the larynx when hoarseness persists beyond
  4 weeks, or sooner with concerning features.
- **Red flags → urgent ENT:** smoker/ex-smoker with persistent hoarseness, haemoptysis,
  stridor, dysphagia/odynophagia, neck mass, referred otalgia, recent neck/chest
  surgery or intubation (vocal fold palsy), weight loss.
- Stroboscopy shows vibratory behaviour (mucosal wave, closure pattern) — needed for
  subtle lesions and differentiating nodules from cysts/polyps.

## 2. Measure the voice (baseline)
- **Perceptual:** CAPE-V (overall severity, roughness, breathiness, strain, pitch,
  loudness on 100-mm scales) or GRBAS (0–3).
- **Patient-reported:** VHI-10 (> 11 abnormal), or V-RQOL; singing VHI for singers.
- **Acoustic:** **CPP / CPPS** (cepstral peak prominence — robust in dysphonic voices,
  unlike jitter/shimmer); record with a fixed mouth-to-mic distance and the same
  protocol each time (e.g. Praat, ADSV).
- **Aerodynamic:** maximum phonation time (adult typically > ~15 s), s/z ratio.
- Vocal load history: daily voice use, occupation, singing, noise, hydration, reflux
  symptoms, smoking, medications (inhaled steroids → fungal laryngitis).

## 3. Therapy principles
- Target **efficient phonation**: maximum output with minimal effort and laryngeal
  collision.
- **Resonant voice therapy (e.g. Lessac-Madsen, LMRVT):** easy onset, forward oral
  vibration sensation ("buzz" on lips/nose), hum → "mm-hmm" chants → words → phrases
  → conversation.
- **Semi-occluded vocal tract (SOVT) exercises:** lip trills, straw phonation (in air or
  water — "straw in water" adds pulsing), humming; glides across range for 2–5 min,
  several times a day.
- **Circumlaryngeal massage / laryngeal reposturing** for muscle tension dysphonia —
  can produce dramatic change in one session.
- **Vocal function exercises** (Stemple): sustained /i/, glides, pitch steps — strength
  and balance.
- **Vocal hygiene** (adjunct, not therapy on its own): hydration, voice rest breaks,
  amplification, reducing throat clearing (swallow or sip instead), reflux management
  if indicated.

## 4. Structure and carryover
- 4–8 sessions typical for nodules/MTD; weekly with daily home practice (short, frequent).
- Hierarchy: isolated tasks → structured speech → conversation → real environments
  (classroom, phone, stage). Record before/after for self-monitoring.
- Re-measure perceptual, VHI-10 and CPP at discharge; re-scope if no change or a lesion
  was present (e.g. nodules should reduce).

## Gotchas
- Treating "hoarseness" without a laryngeal diagnosis — can delay cancer diagnosis.
- Relying on jitter/shimmer in moderate–severe dysphonia (unreliable).
- Prescribing total voice rest for long periods — usually unhelpful beyond short
  post-surgical rest.
- Vocal hygiene leaflets without behavioural practice → little change.

## Verify
- [ ] Laryngeal exam documented; red flags excluded or referred.
- [ ] Baseline CAPE-V/GRBAS, VHI-10, CPP and MPT recorded with a fixed protocol.
- [ ] Home programme written with frequency.
- [ ] Carryover tasks in real settings practised.
- [ ] Outcome re-measured; re-scope arranged if lesion or no improvement.
