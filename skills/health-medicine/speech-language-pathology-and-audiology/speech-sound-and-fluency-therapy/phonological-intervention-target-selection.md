# Phonological intervention: target selection and approach

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** pick the treatment targets and approach that produce the most system-wide
change in a child's speech, and prove it with generalisation probes.

## 1. Classify the profile first
- **Articulation (phonetic):** consistent distortions of a few sounds (e.g. /s/ lisp,
  /r/); motor placement issue. → traditional/motor approaches.
- **Phonological delay/disorder:** rule-based patterns (fronting, stopping, cluster
  reduction, final consonant deletion) — delayed if typical-but-late, disorder if
  unusual patterns (e.g. backing, initial consonant deletion).
- **Inconsistent phonological disorder:** same word produced differently on repeated
  naming (≥ 40% inconsistency on a 25-word test) → core vocabulary approach.
- **Childhood apraxia of speech (CAS):** inconsistent errors on repeated productions,
  disrupted coarticulation, inappropriate prosody (lexical stress errors) → motor
  approaches (e.g. DTTC, ReST, NDP3) with high-frequency practice. Status: diagnosis of
  CAS should rest on multiple features; don't diagnose on one sign.

## 2. Baseline measures
- Single-word test + connected speech sample.
- **PCC** (percentage consonants correct), list of patterns with % occurrence,
  phonetic inventory, **stimulability** for each error sound, intelligibility rating
  (e.g. Intelligibility in Context Scale with parents).

## 3. Selecting targets — two philosophies
- **Developmental/traditional:** stimulable, early-developing, high-impact patterns
  first (fast success, intelligibility gains).
- **Complexity approach (Gierut):** non-stimulable, later-developing, more marked
  targets (e.g. clusters like /str/, sounds absent from inventory) → can produce wider
  **system-wide generalisation**, especially in children with many errors.
- Pick 2–3 targets by intelligibility impact, pattern frequency and child's response.

## 4. Approaches
- **Minimal pairs:** contrast target vs error (key–tea for fronting) — child learns
  meaning changes. Good for 1–2 patterns.
- **Maximal oppositions / treatment of the empty set:** contrast with sounds differing
  in multiple features — suits severe inventories.
- **Multiple oppositions:** one error sound collapsing many targets (e.g. /t/ for
  /k, s, ʃ, tʃ/) → treat several contrasts at once.
- **Cycles (Hodson):** each pattern for a fixed time, then cycle on, for highly
  unintelligible children; includes auditory bombardment.

## 5. Dose and structure
- **Dose matters:** aim for high trials per session (often 70–100+ productions in
  ~30 min for phonological approaches; ≥ 100 for motor approaches) and 2–3 sessions per
  week where feasible. Low dose (a few productions with lots of games) is a common
  failure mode.
- Home practice with clear lists and brief daily sessions.

## 6. Measure generalisation
- Untreated **probe word lists** for target and related sounds every 3–4 sessions.
  Progress = change on untreated words and in connected speech, not just in therapy
  words.
- If no change after ~ 6–8 sessions, revise target, approach or dose.

## Gotchas
- Treating a CAS profile with a purely linguistic approach at low dose.
- Choosing only easy stimulable targets for a child with a large system-wide disorder.
- Counting therapy-word accuracy as progress without probes.
- Ignoring hearing and oral structure checks.

## Verify
- [ ] Profile classified (articulation / phonological / inconsistent / CAS).
- [ ] Baseline PCC, patterns, stimulability and intelligibility recorded.
- [ ] Target-selection rationale written down.
- [ ] Session dose counted and recorded.
- [ ] Generalisation probes administered on a schedule.
