# Hearing aid fitting and real-ear verification

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** prove the aid delivers prescribed, audible, comfortable sound in this person's
ear, and that it improves the situations they care about.

## 1. Candidacy and goals
- Audiogram, speech-in-noise score (e.g. QuickSIN — predicts benefit and need for
  remote microphones), uncomfortable loudness levels if recruitment suspected.
- **COSI** (Client Oriented Scale of Improvement): 3–5 specific listening goals, ranked.
- Medical clearance where required (asymmetric loss, conductive loss, ear disease).
- **Cochlear implant referral** when aided speech perception is poor — adult criteria
  vary by country (e.g. NICE: severe–profound loss ≥ 80 dB HL at 2 and 4 kHz and poor
  aided speech scores); don't keep fitting stronger aids indefinitely.

## 2. Choose the device and coupling
- Style by loss and dexterity: receiver-in-canal with open dome for mild–moderate high-
  frequency loss; closed/power domes or custom moulds as low-frequency loss increases
  (occlusion vs feedback trade-off).
- Rechargeable vs disposable batteries, telecoil, Bluetooth streaming, remote mic.

## 3. Prescription
- **NAL-NL2** (adults typical) or **DSL v5** (children typical; adult version exists).
  Enter correct age, experience (new/experienced), mono/binaural, language.
- The manufacturer's **"first fit" is not the prescription** — first-fit settings are
  often under target at high frequencies, especially for new users.

## 4. Real-ear verification (REM)
1. Otoscopy; calibrate the probe tube; mark insertion depth (~ 5 mm past the canal
   tip of the aid, roughly 25–31 mm from intertragal notch in adults; check with
   your system).
2. Measure **REUG/REUR** if required by the system.
3. Insert aid without disturbing the probe; run the **ISTS** speech signal at
   soft (50–55 dB SPL), average (65) and loud (75–80) levels.
4. Adjust gain per frequency band to match targets (commonly within ± 5 dB at 250–2k Hz
   and ± 8 dB at 3–4 kHz as a quality benchmark).
5. Check **MPO** with an 85–90 dB swept tone — below UCL and not causing discomfort.
6. Run feedback calibration after adjustment (it can reduce high-frequency gain — re-check).
- Children: use **RECD** with coupler measures when real-ear testing isn't feasible.

## 5. Counselling and follow-up
- Insertion/removal, cleaning (wax guards, domes), battery/charging, phone use.
- Expect **acclimatisation**; some fitters start slightly under target for first-time
  users and step up over weeks — document deliberate deviations.
- Follow-up at 2–6 weeks: data-logging (hours/day, environments), fine-tune complaints
  (own voice boomy → venting/low-frequency gain; tinny → high-frequency reduction only
  if still audible).
- Outcomes: COSI rated, and a standard questionnaire (e.g. IOI-HA, GHABP).

## 6. Tinnitus
Sound enrichment via aid, counselling (CBT-based approaches have best evidence),
address sleep and anxiety; urgent referral if pulsatile or unilateral with neuro signs.

## Gotchas
- Skipping REM — studies show many first fits are well below target.
- Probe tube not deep enough → wrong high-frequency readings.
- Feedback manager silently cutting high-frequency gain.
- Not offering remote microphones to people with poor speech-in-noise.
- Use < 2 h/day on data-logging ignored at follow-up.

## Verify
- [ ] COSI goals documented before fitting.
- [ ] REM at soft/average/loud with ISTS; MPO checked; deviations explained.
- [ ] Feedback calibration followed by re-check.
- [ ] Follow-up booked; data-logging and COSI reviewed.
- [ ] CI candidacy considered when aided speech scores are poor.
