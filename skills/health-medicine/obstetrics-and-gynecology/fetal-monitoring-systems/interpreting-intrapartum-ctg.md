# Interpreting an intrapartum CTG

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** read the cardiotocograph in a consistent order, classify it with your
unit's system, and act — while remembering the CTG is one part of the clinical picture.

## 1. Setup and technical checks
- **Paper speed:** know your unit's; misreading speed distorts every feature.
- Correct date/time on the monitor; patient identifiers on the trace.
- **Confirm the signal is fetal:** palpate the maternal pulse and record it. In the
  second stage, differentiate fetal from maternal rate at least every 5 minutes;
  accelerations then are most likely maternal pulse (NG229).
- Toco transducer at the fundus; contractions also palpated.
- Poor trace quality: reposition the tocodynamometer or transducer, or use a fetal scalp
  electrode, always with maternal heart rate monitoring (it may pick up the maternal rate).

## 2. Read in order (NICE NG229 features)
1. **Risk:** antenatal and intrapartum risk factors, reviewed hourly with previous traces.
2. **Contractions:** fewer than 5 in 10 minutes is the reference; 5 or more in 10
   minutes (less resting time) is a concern.
3. **Baseline rate:** mean over 10 minutes when stable, excluding accelerations and
   decelerations; usually **110 to 160 bpm**. Compare with earlier traces.
4. **Variability:** usually 5 to 25 bpm, measured as highest minus lowest in a 1-minute
   segment between contractions. Reduced (under 5 bpm) for 30 to 50 minutes is amber,
   over 50 minutes is red; over 25 bpm for more than 10 minutes, or sinusoidal, is red.
   Absent variability is very concerning: review the whole picture with a low threshold
   for expedited birth. Opioids and quiet periods can reduce variability.
5. **Accelerations:** rises of 15 bpm or more lasting 15 seconds or more. Their presence
   is reassuring; use them alongside the four features.
6. **Decelerations:** falls of more than 15 bpm below baseline lasting 15 seconds or more.
   - **Early:** uncommon, benign, usually head compression.
   - **Variable:** very common and often normal in uncomplicated labour (cord
     compression); worry about concerning characteristics, repetition, or other CTG changes.
   - Longer and later decelerations, with a rising baseline or changed variability, mean
     higher risk of compromise.
   - Acute bradycardia, or a single prolonged deceleration of 3 minutes or more, is red.
7. **Overall category** and plan.

## 3. Classify with your system
- **NICE NG229:** grade each of the 4 features (contractions, baseline, variability,
  decelerations) white, amber or red, then classify the trace as normal, suspicious or
  pathological. Pathological = any 1 feature red, or 2 or more amber.
- Other systems (FIGO, NICHD) differ; use your unit's system consistently.

## 4. Respond
- Conservative measures for suspicious/pathological traces: support position change or
  mobilising, review oxytocin and contraction frequency, and look at maternal observations.
  Follow your unit's intrauterine resuscitation protocol.
- Escalate: urgent obstetric review when variable decelerations persist with other CTG
  changes, or decelerations last over 30 minutes with a rising baseline or reduced
  variability. Consider expedited birth depending on trace, risk factors and stage.
- Second stage: onset of hypoxia is more rapid; a baseline rise of 20 bpm or more is red.
  For concerns get obstetric review, consider discouraging pushing and stopping
  oxytocin unless birth is imminent, and agree a timed plan.
- **Acute bradycardia or a prolonged deceleration of 3 minutes or more** needs urgent action.

## 5. Document
Hourly systematic review with features, classification and plan; NG229 asks for an
in-person "fresh eyes" review of every hourly assessment for women on CTG; mark events
(VE, epidural, oxytocin changes, position changes) on the trace or record.

## Gotchas
- **Rising baseline over hours** with reducing variability is an early sign of
  infection or evolving hypoxia, even if each hour looks "normal".
- Opioids and quiet periods reduce variability, but review other risk factors and look for a rising baseline.
- CTG has high false-positive rates; don't use it to the exclusion of the clinical picture.

Thresholds follow NICE NG229; follow your unit's guideline.
