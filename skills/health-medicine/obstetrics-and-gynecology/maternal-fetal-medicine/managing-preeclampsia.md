# Managing preeclampsia

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** recognise preeclampsia promptly, control blood pressure to prevent stroke,
prevent eclampsia, watch for maternal and fetal deterioration, and time delivery —
the only cure.

## 1. Diagnose
New hypertension (≥140/90 on two occasions, after 20 weeks) **plus** one or more of:
- Proteinuria: urine protein:creatinine ratio ≥30 mg/mmol (~0.3 mg/mg) or 24 h ≥300 mg (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked). (NICE NG133 1.2.6 confirms the 30 mg/mmol protein:creatinine threshold.)
- Maternal organ dysfunction: creatinine rise, raised transaminases, RUQ/epigastric
  pain, neurological signs (severe headache, visual scotomata, clonus), low platelets,
  haemolysis.
- Uteroplacental dysfunction: fetal growth restriction, abnormal Dopplers, stillbirth.
Proteinuria is **not required** in many current definitions.
Placental growth factor testing (PlGF or sFlt-1:PlGF ratio) helps rule in/out in
suspected preterm preeclampsia where available.

## 2. Severe features (escalate, admit)
BP ≥160/110, severe headache or visual disturbance, epigastric/RUQ pain, platelets
<100 × 10⁹/L, rising creatinine, transaminases ≥2× normal, pulmonary oedema, clonus,
HELLP syndrome (Haemolysis, Elevated Liver enzymes, Low Platelets), eclampsia.

## 3. Blood pressure
- **Severe BP (≥160/110) is an emergency — treat promptly (within 30–60 minutes (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked))** to reduce
  stroke risk. NICE NG133: labetalol first line, nifedipine if labetalol unsuitable, methyldopa if neither suits; other regimens (IV hydralazine) per local protocol.
- Target once on treatment: **135/85 or less** (NICE NG133, confirmed); other guidelines accept up to 140–150/90–100 (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
- Labetalol avoided in asthma.
- Avoid ACE inhibitors/ARBs in pregnancy.

## 4. Magnesium sulfate
For severe preeclampsia/when delivery is planned in severe disease and for eclampsia:
- Loading **4 g IV over 5–15 min**, then **1 g/h infusion** for 24 h after delivery
  or the last seizure (Zuspan regimen; local regimens vary) (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked). NICE NG133 only says to offer IV magnesium sulfate (before 34 weeks surveillance); take regimen from the local guideline.
- Eclamptic seizure on magnesium: further 2–4 g bolus (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
- Monitor: reflexes (patellar), RR (>12/min), urine output (>~25–30 mL/h), SpO₂ (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
  Toxicity: loss of reflexes first, then respiratory depression → stop infusion,
  **calcium gluconate 10 mL of 10% IV** over 10 min (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
- Reduce dose in oliguria/renal impairment.

## 5. Monitoring
- Maternal (NICE NG133 Table 2): BP every 15–30 min until below 160/110 when severe, then at least 4 times daily as an inpatient; at least every 48 h for non-severe disease; FBC, renal and liver function twice a week (3 times a week if severe); fluid balance; symptoms.
- **Fluid restriction** (commonly ~80 mL/h total (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)) — pulmonary oedema is a leading
  cause of death; avoid fluid boluses unless haemorrhage.
- Fetal: NICE NG133: fetal heart auscultation each appointment, ultrasound at diagnosis and every 2 weeks if normal, CTG at diagnosis then only if indicated.
- Antenatal corticosteroids if early birth is planned (NICE refers to its preterm labour guideline for gestation limits).

## 6. Timing of delivery
- **<34 weeks:** continue surveillance (with IV magnesium sulfate and corticosteroids per NICE NG133 Table 3) unless there are indications for planned early birth, e.g. uncontrollable BP, deteriorating labs, eclampsia, pulmonary oedema, abruption,
  non-reassuring fetal status.
- **34⁺⁰–36⁺⁶ weeks:** NICE NG133: continue surveillance unless indications for early birth; if considering planned early birth, weigh maternal and fetal condition, risk factors and neonatal unit availability.
- **≥37 weeks:** NICE NG133 Table 3 says to initiate birth within 24 to 48 hours (confirmed).
Vaginal birth is often possible; mode by obstetric factors.

## 7. Postpartum
Risk persists — eclampsia can occur postpartum. Continue BP checks; adjust
antihypertensives (e.g. enalapril, amlodipine compatible with breastfeeding per local
guidance); review at ~2 weeks and 6–8 weeks (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked). Lifelong cardiovascular risk counselling.
Aspirin next pregnancy: NICE NG133 1.1.2 advises 75–150 mg daily from 12 weeks until birth for women with prior hypertensive disease of pregnancy.

## Gotchas
- Headache in a hypertensive pregnant patient is preeclampsia until proven otherwise.
- HELLP can occur with only mild hypertension.
- Avoid NSAIDs postpartum in severe preeclampsia with renal impairment.

Uncertainty note: definitions and targets from ISSHP, ACOG and NICE NG133; regimens
differ — follow local guideline.
