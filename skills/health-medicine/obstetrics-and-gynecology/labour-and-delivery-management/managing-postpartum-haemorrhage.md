# Managing postpartum haemorrhage

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** prevent PPH where possible, recognise it early by measuring loss, and run
resuscitation and cause-directed treatment in parallel with a team.

## 1. Prevention
- **Active management of the third stage:** prophylactic uterotonic (oxytocin 10 IU
  IM is the common standard (FIGO 2022 table lists 10 IU IM for vaginal birth); carbetocin 100 µg IV/IM where used, FIGO 2022), delayed cord clamping, controlled cord traction.
- Identify risk antenatally and on admission: previous PPH, placenta praevia/accreta,
  multiple pregnancy, polyhydramnios, macrosomia, prolonged labour, anaemia,
  anticoagulants, fibroids. Group and save, IV access for high-risk.

## 2. Recognise
- PPH commonly defined as ≥500 mL after vaginal birth (≥1,000 mL major) (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) — but treat
  any bleeding with signs of shock.
- **Quantify blood loss** (weigh swabs and pads, measure collected blood) — visual
  estimation underestimates large losses.
- Pregnant patients compensate: tachycardia and narrowing pulse pressure come before
  hypotension. **Shock index** (HR ÷ systolic BP, FIGO 2022 definition confirmed) ≥~0.9–1.0 suggests significant loss (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).

## 3. First actions (call for help, then everything in parallel)
1. Call for help: senior obstetrician, anaesthetist, midwife coordinator; activate
   major obstetric haemorrhage protocol if large/ongoing; assign a scribe.
2. ABC, high-flow oxygen, lie flat, keep warm.
3. **Two large-bore cannulas**; bloods: FBC, coagulation incl. **fibrinogen**,
   crossmatch, lactate/blood gas (point-of-care viscoelastic testing if available).
4. Warmed crystalloid while awaiting blood; give blood early in major PPH.
5. **Rub up the fundus**; empty the bladder (catheter).

## 4. Find the cause — the 4 Ts
- **Tone** (~70%+ (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)): boggy uterus → uterine massage, bimanual compression, uterotonics.
- **Trauma:** tears (cervix, vagina, perineum), haematoma, uterine rupture, inversion
  → examine under good light, repair.
- **Tissue:** retained placenta/membranes → inspect placenta for completeness;
  manual removal in theatre.
- **Thrombin:** coagulopathy (preeclampsia/HELLP, abruption, amniotic fluid embolism,
  dilution) → correct with blood products guided by labs.

## 5. Drug ladder for atony (check contraindications)
- **Oxytocin** 5 IU slow IV (may repeat) and infusion 40 IU in 500 mL isotonic crystalloid (RCOG 2016 as tabulated in FIGO 2022, confirmed; the 4 h rate is (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)).
- **Ergometrine** IM/IV or syntometrine — **avoid in hypertension/preeclampsia**; dose 500 mcg (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) (FIGO lists 0.2 mg for ergonovine/methylergonovine).
- **Carboprost** 250 mcg IM, repeated every 15–90 min as needed to a total of 2 mg (FIGO 2022 table, confirmed) — **avoid in asthma**.
- **Misoprostol** 800 mcg sublingual (WHO/FIGO 2022, confirmed).
- **Tranexamic acid 1 g IV** over 10 min within 3 h of birth, second 1 g if bleeding continues after 30 min or restarts within 24 h; do not give more than 3 h after birth (WHO 2017 update, confirmed via search and FIGO 2022).

## 6. Escalate
If bleeding continues: theatre for examination under anaesthesia, intrauterine
balloon tamponade, compression sutures (e.g. B-Lynch), interventional radiology
embolisation, uterine artery ligation, and hysterectomy — **early rather than late**
when other measures fail. Cell salvage where available.

## Gotchas
- Concealed bleeding (broad ligament haematoma, intraperitoneal after caesarean)
  presents with shock out of proportion to visible loss.
- Fibrinogen <2 g/L predicts progression to severe PPH (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) — replace (cryoprecipitate or
  fibrinogen concentrate) per protocol.
- Keep the patient warm; hypothermia worsens coagulopathy.
- Debrief the patient and team afterwards; document times and drugs given.

Uncertainty note: drug doses summarised from RCOG Green-top 52, WHO and FIGO guidance;
regimens vary — confirm with local protocol.

## Verify
- [ ] Prophylactic uterotonic given in third stage.
- [ ] Blood loss quantified, not estimated.
- [ ] Help called and protocol activated at threshold.
- [ ] 4 Ts systematically assessed.
- [ ] TXA given within 3 h; escalation decisions timed and documented.
