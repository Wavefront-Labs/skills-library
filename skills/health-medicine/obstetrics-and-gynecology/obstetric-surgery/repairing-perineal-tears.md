# Repairing perineal tears

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** classify every perineal injury correctly — above all, never miss an
obstetric anal sphincter injury (OASI) — and repair first- and second-degree tears
with a technique that minimises pain and dehiscence.

## 1. Examine systematically after every vaginal birth
- Good light, adequate analgesia, patient in lithotomy if needed.
- Inspect labia, vagina (including apex), perineum.
- **Rectal examination before repair in every case of perineal trauma**: finger in
  the anal canal, thumb on the perineum — pill-rolling to feel the external anal
  sphincter (EAS) bulk; ask the patient to squeeze. Look for a gap, retraction, or
  "dimple". A torn sphincter may look intact through skin.

## 2. Classify (Sultan / RCOG)
| Degree | Injury |
|---|---|
| 1st | Perineal skin/vaginal epithelium only |
| 2nd | Perineal muscles, not anal sphincter |
| 3a | <50% EAS thickness torn |
| 3b | >50% EAS thickness torn |
| 3c | EAS and internal anal sphincter (IAS) torn |
| 4th | Sphincter complex **and** anorectal mucosa |
| Rectal buttonhole | Rectal mucosa torn, sphincter intact — repair and document separately |
When unsure between degrees, classify **higher**.

## 3. Repair a second-degree tear (or episiotomy)
- Analgesia: check epidural/top up, or local infiltration (e.g. lidocaine 1%, within
  maximum dose per kg).
- Suture: **rapidly absorbable polyglactin 910 (e.g. 2-0)**.
- **Continuous non-locking technique** for all layers is associated with less pain
  than interrupted sutures:
  1. Anchor **~1 cm above the apex** of the vaginal tear; continuous suture down to the hymenal ring.
  2. Re-approximate perineal muscles (continuous), aligning the bulbospongiosus and
     transverse perineal muscles.
  3. Close skin with a **subcuticular** continuous suture, avoiding tension.
- Leaving well-apposed skin unsutured is an acceptable option in some guidelines.
- First-degree tears that are well apposed and not bleeding may be left unsutured with consent.

## 4. OASI (3rd and 4th degree)
- **Repair in theatre**, by an appropriately trained clinician, with good light,
  regional or general anaesthesia.
- Anorectal mucosa: interrupted or continuous 3-0 polyglactin.
- IAS: separate repair with interrupted mattress sutures (e.g. 3-0 PDS/polyglactin) where identified.
- EAS: full-thickness tears with **overlapping** or **end-to-end** repair; partial
  (3a) with end-to-end. Monofilament (PDS 3-0) or braided polyglactin 2-0 per guideline.
- Post-op: broad-spectrum antibiotics, laxatives (stool softeners), avoid constipation,
  analgesia (avoid codeine-induced constipation), physiotherapy referral, follow-up
  at ~6–12 weeks with symptom review and endoanal ultrasound/manometry where available.
- Counsel about next birth based on symptoms and investigations.

## 5. After every repair
- **Swab, needle and instrument count** before and after.
- **Rectal examination after repair** to confirm no suture through the rectal mucosa.
- Document degree, technique, suture material, counts, rectal exam findings, and analgesia given.
- Advice: hygiene, pain relief, signs of infection or breakdown, pelvic floor exercises.

## Gotchas
- A retained swab is a never event — count every time.
- Tight skin sutures cause long-term dyspareunia.
- Vaginal haematoma presents with severe pain and rectal pressure hours later — re-examine.

Uncertainty note: technique summarised from RCOG Green-top 29 and NICE intrapartum
guidance; suture choices vary by unit.

## Verify
- [ ] Rectal exam done before and after repair.
- [ ] Tear classified using Sultan categories; higher grade chosen if uncertain.
- [ ] OASI referred for theatre repair.
- [ ] Counts correct and documented.
- [ ] Post-repair advice and follow-up given.
