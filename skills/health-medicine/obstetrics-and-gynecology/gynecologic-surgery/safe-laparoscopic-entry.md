# Safe laparoscopic entry

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** establish pneumoperitoneum and place ports without injuring bowel or major
vessels — about half of laparoscopic complications occur at entry, often before the
procedure proper has started.

## 1. Pre-op choice of entry site and method
- **Umbilical Veress** or **direct optical/trocar** entry: common default in
  normal-BMI patients without prior midline surgery.
- **Open (Hasson) entry:** previous laparotomy, suspected adhesions, pregnancy, very
  thin patients, or surgeon preference.
- **Palmer's point** (left upper quadrant, ~3 cm below the left costal margin in the
  midclavicular line): previous midline incisions, umbilical hernia/mesh, large
  pelvic mass, failed umbilical entry. Empty the stomach first (orogastric tube);
  avoid with splenomegaly or prior LUQ surgery.
- Review imaging and operative notes; mark likely adhesion zones.

## 2. Veress technique at the umbilicus
1. Patient **flat** (not Trendelenburg — tilt brings the great vessels closer to the umbilicus).
2. Empty bladder.
3. Check Veress spring action and patency.
4. Lift the abdominal wall (or not — evidence is mixed); insert at the base of the
   umbilicus **perpendicular** in thin patients, angling towards the pelvis
   (~45°) in normal BMI; perpendicular and deeper in obesity, where the umbilicus
   drifts caudally relative to the aortic bifurcation.
5. Feel two "clicks" (fascia, peritoneum); limit attempts (commonly ≤3, then change method).
6. **Confirm intraperitoneal placement:** initial insufflation pressure
   **<8–10 mmHg** (low, fluctuating with breathing). Avoid lateral movement of the needle.
7. Insufflate to an entry pressure of **20–25 mmHg** for primary trocar insertion
   (greater splinting), then **reduce to ~12–15 mmHg** working pressure.

## 3. Primary trocar
Insert with controlled force and a twisting motion, aimed towards the pelvis
(away from the sacral promontory vessels). Immediately inspect directly beneath the
entry site and the whole peritoneal cavity (360°) for bleeding or bowel content.

## 4. Secondary (lateral) ports — under direct vision
- **Transilluminate** the abdominal wall and **visualise the inferior epigastric
  vessels** laparoscopically (lateral to the umbilical ligament, running towards
  the deep inguinal ring).
- Place lateral ports **lateral to the rectus sheath**, roughly 5 cm above the
  symphysis and 8 cm from the midline (conventional landmarks; individualise).
- Insert perpendicular, then angle towards the pelvis, watching the tip all the way.
- Fascial closure for ports ≥10 mm to prevent port-site hernia.

## 5. Recognise entry injuries
- **Vascular:** sudden haemodynamic collapse, blood in the cavity or Veress hub,
  expanding retroperitoneal haematoma. **Leave the trocar in place** (tamponade),
  call for vascular surgeon, convert to laparotomy, get blood.
- **Bowel:** faecal smell, visible mucosa, gas from Veress with asymmetric
  insufflation. Repair or call general surgery; leave instrument in place to mark
  the site. Many bowel injuries are missed intraoperatively — have a low threshold
  for re-look if a patient isn't improving post-op (pain, fever, tachycardia day 1–3).
- **Gas embolism:** sudden EtCO₂ drop, hypotension — stop insufflation.
- **Preperitoneal insufflation:** high pressures, poor view — convert method.

## Gotchas
- Raising the patient into Trendelenburg before entry is a classic vascular-injury setup.
- Obese patients: the umbilicus is not a reliable landmark for the aortic bifurcation.
- Failed Veress twice → open or Palmer's point; don't persist.

Uncertainty note: technique summarised from RCOG/BSGE entry guidance and AAGL
literature; local practice and evidence vary.

## Verify
- [ ] Entry method chosen with documented reason (previous surgery, BMI).
- [ ] Patient flat, bladder and (for LUQ) stomach emptied.
- [ ] Veress opening pressure <8–10 mmHg confirmed.
- [ ] 360° inspection after primary port.
- [ ] Inferior epigastric vessels seen before lateral ports placed.
