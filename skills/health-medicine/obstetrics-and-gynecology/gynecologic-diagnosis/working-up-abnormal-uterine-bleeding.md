# Working up abnormal uterine bleeding

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** find the cause of abnormal uterine bleeding (AUB) using a structured
framework, never miss pregnancy or endometrial cancer, and match investigations to
age and risk.

## 1. Rule out pregnancy and assess stability
- **Pregnancy test in everyone who could be pregnant** — ectopic pregnancy and
  miscarriage present as "abnormal bleeding".
- Heavy acute bleeding: vital signs, haemoglobin, resuscitate; acute medical
  management (e.g. tranexamic acid, high-dose progestogen or combined hormones per
  protocol) or surgical options if unstable.

## 2. Characterise the bleeding
- Pattern: heavy menstrual bleeding (HMB — impacts quality of life; flooding, clots
  >~2.5 cm, changing protection every 1–2 h (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)), intermenstrual, postcoital, irregular,
  prolonged, absent periods.
- Age and menopausal status: **postmenopausal bleeding** (≥12 months after last
  period) is its own pathway.
- Associated: pelvic pain, dyspareunia (adenomyosis, endometriosis), pressure
  symptoms (fibroids), signs of hyperandrogenism (PCOS), thyroid symptoms, galactorrhoea.
- Bleeding history: easy bruising, gum bleeding, PPH, family history — screen for
  **von Willebrand disease**, especially if HMB since menarche.
- Medications: anticoagulants, hormonal contraception/HRT, tamoxifen, SSRIs.
- Cervical screening status.

## 3. PALM-COEIN (FIGO)
**Structural (PALM):** Polyp, Adenomyosis, Leiomyoma (fibroid — submucosal matters most), Malignancy/hyperplasia.
**Non-structural (COEIN):** Coagulopathy, Ovulatory dysfunction (PCOS, thyroid,
hyperprolactinaemia, perimenopause, adolescence), Endometrial, Iatrogenic, Not yet
classified. More than one can coexist.

## 4. Examination and tests
- Abdominal and pelvic exam; **speculum** to see the cervix (polyp, lesion, ectropion,
  infection). Bimanual for size, mobility, tenderness.
- **Bloods:** FBC for all women with HMB (NICE NG88 1.2.6, confirmed); coagulation testing (e.g. von Willebrand) when HMB since menarche or personal/family history suggests it (1.2.7); NICE says do **not** do female hormone testing for HMB (1.2.9). Targeted TSH, prolactin or androgens apply to other presentations (e.g. suspected PCOS or thyroid disease); chlamydia/gonorrhoea NAAT for intermenstrual/postcoital bleeding (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
- **Imaging:** NICE NG88 1.3.3: choose hysteroscopy or ultrasound as first-line from history and examination; offer outpatient hysteroscopy if history suggests submucosal fibroids, polyps or endometrial pathology (persistent intermenstrual bleeding or risk factors); ultrasound if hysteroscopy is declined. Triggers for transvaginal ultrasound and saline infusion sonography are (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).

## 5. Endometrial sampling — who needs it
- Commonly: age ≥45 with AUB (thresholds vary, e.g. ≥40–45 (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)), or younger with risk
  factors (NICE 1.3.10: consider biopsy at hysteroscopy for persistent intermenstrual bleeding or high-risk women) — **unopposed oestrogen** (obesity, PCOS, anovulation), tamoxifen, Lynch
  syndrome, failed treatment, persistent intermenstrual bleeding.
- Pipelle biopsy in clinic; hysteroscopy with directed biopsy if inadequate sample or persistent symptoms.

## 6. Postmenopausal bleeding
Urgent cancer pathway: TVUS for **endometrial thickness** — ≤4 mm (some use ≤3 mm) (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)
has low cancer risk if bleeding doesn't recur; above threshold → biopsy/hysteroscopy.
Any **persistent or recurrent bleeding needs sampling regardless of thickness**.
Women on HRT or tamoxifen: thresholds differ — follow local pathway.

## Gotchas
- A normal pipelle doesn't exclude focal lesions (polyps, focal cancer).
- Abnormal cervix on exam → colposcopy referral, not just cytology.
- Iron deficiency from HMB is common and under-treated — check and replace.
- Adolescents with HMB from menarche: up to a notable fraction have a bleeding disorder.

Uncertainty note: framework per FIGO PALM-COEIN; age and thickness thresholds from
NICE NG88 and ACOG guidance vary.

## Verify
- [ ] Pregnancy excluded.
- [ ] Bleeding pattern and bleeding-disorder screen documented.
- [ ] Speculum exam done and cervical screening status checked.
- [ ] PALM-COEIN category assigned (one or more).
- [ ] Endometrial sampling done when age/risk criteria met; PMB on urgent pathway.
