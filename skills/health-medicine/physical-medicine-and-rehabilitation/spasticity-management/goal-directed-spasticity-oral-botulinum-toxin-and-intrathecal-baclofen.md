# Goal-directed spasticity management: oral agents, botulinum toxin and intrathecal baclofen

> General clinical/professional reference, not patient-specific advice; doses must be checked against current product information and local protocols.

**Goal:** treat spasticity only where it causes a problem the person or carer cares
about, choose the least burdensome effective modality, and measure whether the goal was met.

## 1. Assess — is spasticity the problem?
- Distinguish spasticity (velocity-dependent tone) from **contracture**, weakness, pain,
  dystonia and clonus. Contracture won't respond to toxin.
- Measures: **Modified Ashworth** (0–4), **Tardieu** (R1 catch vs R2 full passive range — a
  big R2–R1 gap = dynamic, toxin-responsive; small gap = fixed), passive ROM, and
  **Goal Attainment Scaling** for the agreed goals.
- Goals are either **active** (improved reach, gait, grasp release) or **passive** (hygiene
  in the palm/axilla, dressing, positioning, pain, carer burden). Passive goals are more
  reliably achieved.
- Look for **noxious triggers** that worsen tone: UTI, constipation, pressure injury,
  ingrown toenail, fracture, tight clothing.

## 2. Non-drug foundation
Stretching and positioning programme, standing, splints/orthoses, serial casting for
dynamic shortening, and strengthening of antagonists. Every injection plan needs this
follow-through or the effect is wasted.

## 3. Oral agents (generalised spasticity)
- **Baclofen:** start low (e.g. 5 mg two–three times daily), titrate; sedation, weakness;
  reduce dose in renal impairment; **never stop abruptly** (seizures, hallucinations).
- **Tizanidine:** sedation, hypotension, hepatotoxicity — check LFTs; major interaction
  with CYP1A2 inhibitors (ciprofloxacin, fluvoxamine).
- **Dantrolene:** acts on muscle (less sedation); hepatotoxicity risk, monitor LFTs.
- Gabapentin/pregabalin, benzodiazepines (sedation, dependence), cannabinoids in MS.
- Oral drugs often cause more weakness and sedation than benefit; reassess at each step.

## 4. Botulinum toxin (focal/multifocal)
- **Units are not interchangeable** between products (onabotulinumtoxinA, abobotulinumtoxinA,
  incobotulinumtoxinA). Always prescribe by brand.
- Pick muscles from the pattern: e.g. flexed elbow — biceps, brachialis, brachioradialis;
  clenched fist — FDS, FDP; adducted/internally rotated shoulder — pectoralis major,
  latissimus dorsi, subscapularis; equinovarus foot — gastrocnemius, soleus, tibialis posterior.
- Localise with **ultrasound, EMG or e-stim** rather than anatomy alone for deep or small muscles.
- Respect the product's maximum total dose per session and minimum re-injection interval
  (commonly ≥ 12 weeks) to limit antibody formation and spread.
- Effect starts in ~3–7 days, peaks ~2–6 weeks, wanes ~3–4 months. Review at about 4–6 weeks.
- Warn about weakness, dysphagia/breathing difficulty with distant spread (urgent advice).
- **Phenol/alcohol nerve or motor point blocks** (e.g. obturator, musculocutaneous) suit
  large muscles or when toxin dose limits are reached; risk of dysaesthesia with mixed nerves.

## 5. Intrathecal baclofen (ITB)
- For severe generalised (often lower-limb) spasticity uncontrolled or intolerant of oral agents.
- **Screening trial bolus** (e.g. 50 mcg, then higher if needed) with assessment over
  4–8 h; then pump implantation and slow titration.
- Requires a team for refills every few months, an alarm plan and education.
- **Baclofen withdrawal** (pump empty, catheter kink/fracture): itch, rebound spasticity,
  fever, hypotension/hypertension, confusion → can progress to rhabdomyolysis and death.
  Treat as an emergency: high-dose oral baclofen bridge, benzodiazepines, and urgent pump
  interrogation. Overdose: drowsiness, respiratory depression.
- Other surgical options: selective dorsal rhizotomy (selected children with CP), tendon
  lengthening/transfers.

## Gotchas
- Injecting toxin to "relax" a contracture that needs casting or surgery.
- Treating a tone flare without looking for the UTI or pressure sore behind it.
- Removing spasticity a person uses to stand or transfer — worse function.
- Mixing toxin units across brands.
- Missing early ITB withdrawal because it looks like sepsis.

## Verify
- [ ] Problem attributed to spasticity (not contracture/weakness) with measures recorded.
- [ ] SMART/GAS goals agreed and baseline scored.
- [ ] Oral drug choices checked for renal/hepatic issues and interactions.
- [ ] Toxin: brand, muscles, units, localisation method and total dose within limits recorded.
- [ ] Stretch/splint/orthotic follow-through arranged; review date set.
- [ ] ITB patients and carers know withdrawal signs and who to call.

*Uncertainty note:* starting/titration doses and toxin maximums vary by product, country
and age group; verify before each prescription.
