# Functional assessment and the rehabilitation prescription

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** turn a diagnosis into a measured functional baseline, realistic goals and a
written prescription the therapy team can execute without guessing.

## 1. Frame the problem with the ICF
Write one line for each WHO ICF domain — it stops the plan collapsing into the diagnosis:
- **Body structure/function (impairment):** e.g. L hemiparesis MRC 2/5 arm, 3/5 leg; neglect.
- **Activity limitation:** e.g. needs moderate assist sit-to-stand; dependent stairs.
- **Participation restriction:** e.g. cannot return to job as driver, lives alone.
- **Contextual factors:** home (stairs, bathroom access), carers, insurance/funding, goals.

## 2. Measure, don't describe
- **FIM** (18 items, 1–7 each, total 18–126; motor 13 items, cognitive 5). 7 = complete
  independence, 6 = modified independence (device/extra time), 5 = supervision/setup,
  4 = minimal assist (patient does ≥ 75 %), 3 = moderate (50–74 %), 2 = maximal (25–49 %),
  1 = total (< 25 %). FIM is licensed and US IRFs now report **Section GG** (CARE items,
  scored 1–6 plus activity-not-attempted codes) instead — know which your unit uses.
- **Barthel Index** (0–100) — quick, public-domain, ceiling effect in higher-functioning patients.
- **Modified Rankin Scale** (0–6) for stroke outcome; not sensitive to inpatient change.
- Condition-specific: Berg Balance, 10-m walk test, 6-minute walk, Timed Up and Go,
  ASIA/ISNCSCI (SCI), Ashworth (spasticity), MoCA (cognition).
- Score on the **first 3 days** (IRF rules use a 3-day admission window) and at discharge;
  score what the patient *actually does*, not what they could do.

## 3. Prognosis and setting
Combine diagnosis-specific predictors (e.g. stroke severity/NIHSS, SCI level/AIS grade,
age, premorbid function, cognition, continence, social support) to choose the setting:
acute inpatient rehab (US IRF typically requires tolerating ~3 h/day therapy, ≥ 2
disciplines, physician oversight), sub-acute/skilled nursing, home-based or outpatient.
State the expected length of stay and discharge destination at admission.

## 4. Goals
SMART and in the patient's words where possible: "Walk 50 m indoors with a quad stick
and supervision in 3 weeks so she can reach the bathroom at night." Use **Goal
Attainment Scaling** (−2 to +2) for individualised goals the scales miss. Each
discipline owns specific goals; the team conference reviews them weekly.

## 5. Write the prescription
Every therapy order should contain:
1. **Diagnosis and relevant comorbidities** (cardiac limits, diabetes, osteoporosis).
2. **Precautions:** weight-bearing status (NWB / TTWB / PWB % / WBAT / FWB), hip or
   spinal precautions, sternal precautions, BP/HR parameters ("hold if SBP > 180 or
   HR > 120"), seizure, falls, aspiration, autonomic dysreflexia.
3. **Discipline and frequency:** PT, OT, SLP, psychology, orthotics — e.g. "PT 60 min
   BID, 5–6 days/week".
4. **Specific treatments/goals:** gait training with device, ADL retraining, spasticity
   stretching programme, cognitive-communication therapy.
5. **Equipment** to assess: wheelchair, orthoses, bathroom equipment.
6. **Review date.**

## Gotchas
- Scoring FIM from the chart instead of observation → inflated admission scores and
  "no gain".
- Omitting weight-bearing or cardiac parameters — therapists will (rightly) not proceed.
- Goals written as impairments ("increase strength") rather than activities.
- Ignoring cognition and mood: depression and executive deficits are the commonest
  reason a physically capable patient fails to progress.
- Discharge planning that starts in week 3 instead of on day 1.

## Verify
- [ ] ICF summary written, including home and social context.
- [ ] Baseline scores recorded with the scale named (FIM vs Section GG vs Barthel).
- [ ] Setting, expected stay and discharge destination documented.
- [ ] Each discipline has SMART goals and a review date.
- [ ] Prescription states precautions, parameters, frequency and equipment.

*Uncertainty note:* FIM/Section GG scoring rules and IRF admission criteria are
US-specific and change with CMS rule updates; check the current manual.
