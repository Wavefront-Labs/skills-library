# Residual limb care, K-level candidacy and prosthetic prescription

> General clinical/professional reference, not patient-specific advice; component choice and funding rules vary — work with a certified prosthetist and local policy.

**Goal:** get a healed, well-shaped residual limb with full range, match the prosthesis to
the person's realistic functional level, and keep them walking (or wheeling) safely.

## 1. Pre-prosthetic phase
- **Ideally see the person before surgery:** level choice (preserve knee whenever viable —
  transtibial walking costs far less energy than transfemoral), expectations, peer visit.
- **Wound and shaping:** rigid removable dressing (transtibial) protects and reduces
  oedema and knee flexion contracture; then **shrinker** or figure-of-eight wrapping
  (distal-tight) once wound allows, worn ~23 h/day.
- **Contracture prevention:** transtibial — keep the knee extended (no pillow under the
  knee, stump board in the chair); transfemoral — avoid hip flexion/abduction (no pillow
  between legs or under the stump), prone lying 15–30 min a few times a day.
- Strengthen hip extensors/abductors, core, contralateral limb; transfers and wheelchair
  skills; protect the remaining foot (in vascular/diabetic patients the other limb is at
  high risk — daily inspection, footwear, podiatry).
- Early walking aids (e.g. pneumatic post-amputation mobility aid) where available.

## 2. Candidacy and K-level
- **K0:** no ability/potential to ambulate or transfer safely with a prosthesis.
- **K1:** transfers and household walking on level surfaces at fixed cadence.
- **K2:** limited community walker; low barriers (kerbs, stairs, uneven surfaces).
- **K3:** community walker with variable cadence; vocational/therapeutic/exercise demands.
- **K4:** exceeds basic ambulation — high impact/energy (child, active adult, athlete).
- Inform with the **Amputee Mobility Predictor (AMPPRO/AMPnoPRO)**, 6-minute walk,
  timed up-and-go, cognition (to don/doff and manage the limb), cardiorespiratory reserve,
  contralateral limb status, and the person's goals. Not everyone should be fitted; a
  wheelchair-first plan can be the right outcome.

## 3. Prescription (lower limb)
- **Socket:** transtibial — total surface bearing or patellar-tendon-bearing;
  transfemoral — ischial containment or quadrilateral, sub-ischial in selected users.
- **Interface/suspension:** silicone/gel liner with pin lock, lanyard, suction or
  elevated vacuum; sleeves; belt for some transfemoral users. Pin locks can cause distal
  "milking" skin problems.
- **Foot:** SACH or single-axis (K1), multiaxial/flexible keel (K2), dynamic-response
  carbon (K3–K4), microprocessor ankle for some.
- **Knee (transfemoral):** locking or weight-activated stance-control for K1–K2; polycentric;
  hydraulic/pneumatic swing control and **microprocessor knees** for K3 (and evidence for
  fall reduction in some K2 users).
- Plan for **sock-ply management**: the limb keeps shrinking for ~6–18 months; check-socket
  then definitive socket.

## 4. Gait training and common deviations
- Lateral trunk lean (weak abductors, short prosthesis, painful socket), vaulting
  (prosthesis too long, poor knee flexion), circumduction, terminal impact, knee buckling
  (foot too far dorsiflexed/posterior, weak hip extensors) and foot whip. Diagnose with the
  prosthetist; fix alignment or fit before blaming the person.

## 5. Pain
- **Phantom sensation** is near-universal and benign; **phantom pain** affects most at some
  point. Options: mirror therapy/graded motor imagery, desensitisation, gabapentinoids,
  TCAs; evidence is modest — set expectations.
- **Residual limb pain:** look for socket fit problems, neuroma (Tinel, ultrasound —
  injection or targeted muscle reinnervation), bone spur, infection, ischaemia, referred
  spinal pain.

## Gotchas
- Pillow under the transtibial knee "for comfort" → fixed flexion contracture that blocks fitting.
- Prescribing by aspiration rather than demonstrated function (or the reverse — under-prescribing a capable user).
- Neglecting the remaining foot in diabetic/vascular amputees.
- Skin breakdown from volume change while the person keeps the same sock ply.

## Verify
- [ ] Wound, shape, volume and range documented; contracture prevention charted.
- [ ] K-level justified with named measures and stated goals.
- [ ] Prescription names socket, interface, suspension, foot/knee with rationale.
- [ ] Gait deviations reviewed jointly with the prosthetist.
- [ ] Phantom vs residual pain distinguished; contralateral limb plan in place.

*Uncertainty note:* K-levels are a US Medicare framework; other health systems use
different funding categories, though the functional logic is similar.
