# NCS/EMG study design for radiculopathy, neuropathy and entrapment

> General clinical/professional reference, not patient-specific advice and not a substitute for formal electrodiagnostic training, lab-specific normal values or current AANEM guidance.

**Goal:** turn a referral question into a focused study that localises the lesion
(root, plexus, nerve, junction, muscle), grades it (axonal vs demyelinating, acute vs
chronic, severity) and answers the clinical question in plain words.

## 1. Before the needle
- **Re-take a 2-minute history and exam.** The referral diagnosis is a hypothesis; a
  "carpal tunnel" referral with weak finger abduction is a different study.
- **Write the differential** (e.g. CTS vs C6/C7 radiculopathy vs proximal median lesion
  vs polyneuropathy) and design the study to separate them.
- **Timing:** fibrillations/positive sharp waves need roughly 2–3 weeks (proximal
  muscles earlier, distal up to 4–6 weeks) after axonal injury; Wallerian degeneration
  lowers distal CMAP/SNAP amplitudes from about day 7–11. A study at day 3 can be falsely
  reassuring — say so in the report or defer.
- **Contraindication check:** anticoagulation (generally not a bar for most muscles;
  avoid deep or compressible-difficult sites such as paraspinals if INR high — follow lab
  policy), lymphoedema, implanted devices for NCS stimulation near leads.
- **Limb temperature:** warm to ≥ 32 °C (hand) / ≥ 30 °C (foot). Cold slows conduction
  and *raises* amplitudes — a classic false "demyelinating" picture.

## 2. Core protocols (adapt to question)
**Carpal tunnel syndrome**
- Median and ulnar sensory and motor of the symptomatic hand; if routine is normal, a
  sensitive internal comparison (median–ulnar palmar or ring-finger, or median–radial
  thumb comparison, or a combined sensory index).
- Needle APB if motor abnormal or severe; screen C6–C7 and a non-median C8 muscle to
  exclude radiculopathy/proximal lesion.
- Study the other hand when bilateral symptoms or polyneuropathy is possible.

**Ulnar neuropathy at the elbow**
- Ulnar motor recording ADM (and FDI if needed) with **elbow flexed ~70–90°** and an
  across-elbow segment ≥ 10 cm; slowing of > ~10 m/s across elbow vs forearm or a
  significant drop in amplitude suggests focal lesion. Inching if localisation unclear.
- Needle FDI, and FCU/FDP-4; check a C8 non-ulnar muscle (APB/EIP) to exclude C8/lower trunk.

**Radiculopathy**
- NCS mainly to *exclude* mimics; **SNAPs are preserved** in radiculopathy (lesion is
  proximal to the dorsal root ganglion) — a low SNAP points to plexus or nerve.
- Needle screen: about 6 limb muscles covering all roots plus paraspinals; finding
  abnormality in ≥ 2 muscles of the same root via different nerves is the usual
  threshold. Paraspinals may be abnormal after spine surgery — interpret with care.
- A normal EMG does not exclude a purely sensory or demyelinating radiculopathy.

**Polyneuropathy**
- At minimum: sural sensory and peroneal (fibular) motor in one leg, plus an upper limb
  sensory/motor; bilateral if asymmetry. Distal-to-proximal gradient, length dependence.
- Classify: axonal (low amplitudes, near-normal velocities) vs demyelinating (velocity
  < ~70–75 % LLN, distal latency > ~130 % ULN, conduction block, temporal dispersion,
  prolonged/absent F-waves). Uniform vs non-uniform demyelination separates inherited
  (CMT1) from acquired (CIDP). **Sural sparing** with abnormal upper-limb SNAPs suggests
  acquired demyelinating (e.g. GBS).

## 3. Reporting
- Tables with values and lab reference ranges, temperature, then an **impression** in
  plain language: location, pathophysiology, severity, chronicity, and the clinical
  correlation statement ("consistent with a moderate median neuropathy at the wrist").
- State what was *not* tested and any limitation (early timing, poor cooperation, cold).

## Gotchas
- Martin–Gruber anastomosis can mimic conduction block or make the APB CMAP bigger with
  elbow stimulation; look for an initial positive deflection.
- Accessory deep peroneal nerve mimics a peroneal conduction block at the ankle.
- Absent sural SNAPs over age ~60–70 may be normal; use age-adjusted references.
- Over-calling radiculopathy from paraspinal fibrillations alone.
- Submaximal stimulation masquerading as low amplitude — always confirm supramaximal.

## Verify
- [ ] Differential written before testing; protocol covers each alternative.
- [ ] Temperature recorded and adequate.
- [ ] Timing since onset noted and limitation stated if early.
- [ ] Each abnormal finding localised and classified (axonal/demyelinating, acute/chronic).
- [ ] Impression answers the referral question in plain language.

*Uncertainty note:* numeric cut-offs (velocity %, segment slowing, amplitude drops) vary
between labs and references; use your lab's validated normal values.
