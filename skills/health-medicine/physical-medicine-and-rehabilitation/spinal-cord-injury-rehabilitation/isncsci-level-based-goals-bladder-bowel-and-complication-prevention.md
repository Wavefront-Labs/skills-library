# ISNCSCI, level-based goals, bladder/bowel and complication prevention in SCI

> General clinical/professional reference, not patient-specific advice; always defer to current Consortium for Spinal Cord Medicine / local guidelines and the treating team.

**Goal:** classify the injury precisely, set realistic level-based goals, establish
bladder and bowel programmes, and prevent the complications that kill or re-admit people
with SCI.

## 1. Classify (ISNCSCI)
- Test 10 key muscles per side (C5–T1, L2–S1) graded 0–5, light touch and pin-prick at
  28 dermatomes per side, plus **deep anal pressure** and **voluntary anal contraction**.
- **Sensory and motor levels** per side; the **neurological level of injury** is the most
  caudal segment with normal sensation and antigravity (≥ 3) motor function, with all
  levels above normal.
- **AIS:** A = complete (no sacral sparing S4–5); B = sensory incomplete; C = motor
  incomplete with < half key muscles below NLI grade ≥ 3; D = ≥ half grade ≥ 3; E = normal
  (only if previously deficient). Motor incomplete needs voluntary anal contraction *or*
  sacral sensory sparing with motor function > 3 levels below the motor level.
- Exam at **72 h – 1 week** is more prognostic than the day-0 exam (spinal shock, sedation).
  Use the free ISNCSCI calculator to avoid classification errors.

## 2. Prognosis and level-based goals (approximate, motor-complete)
- **C1–C3:** ventilator dependence likely; power chair with head/breath control; directs care.
- **C4:** may wean ventilation; power chair; dependent for most ADLs.
- **C5:** elbow flexion; power chair (manual on flat with adaptations); feeding with orthoses.
- **C6:** wrist extension → tenodesis grasp; some independent transfers with board,
  manual chair, possible self-catheterisation (especially men), driving with adaptations.
- **C7–C8:** triceps → independent transfers, manual chair, largely independent ADLs.
- **T1–T9:** independent wheelchair level; standing only for exercise.
- **T10–L2:** possible household ambulation with KAFOs (high energy cost).
- **L3 and below:** community ambulation with AFOs ± aids likely.
- Conversion: AIS A rarely becomes ambulatory; pin-prick sparing in a dermatome/sacral
  area substantially improves walking prognosis for AIS B. Most motor recovery occurs
  in the first 6–9 months, plateauing by ~12–18 months.

## 3. Neurogenic bladder
- Above-conus lesions → detrusor overactivity ± detrusor–sphincter dyssynergia (high
  pressure, renal risk); conus/cauda equina → areflexic bladder.
- **Clean intermittent catheterisation** every 4–6 h, volumes < ~400–500 mL, is the
  default when hand function or a carer allows; anticholinergics (or beta-3 agonist,
  botulinum toxin to detrusor) to keep pressures low. Indwelling/suprapubic catheter when
  CIC isn't feasible.
- Baseline urodynamics and **renal ultrasound**; lifelong surveillance (commonly annual).
- Don't treat asymptomatic bacteriuria.

## 4. Neurogenic bowel
- Upper motor neuron bowel: daily/alternate-day programme at a consistent time after a
  meal (gastrocolic reflex), suppository and **digital stimulation**.
- Lower motor neuron (areflexic) bowel: firmer stool, daily or twice-daily **manual
  evacuation**; avoid straining.
- Goal: predictable evacuation in < ~60 min, no incontinence between.

## 5. Complication prevention
- **Autonomic dysreflexia** (injury at or above T6): sudden BP rise ≥ 20–40 mmHg over
  baseline, headache, flushing above / pallor below, bradycardia. Sit upright, loosen
  clothing, check bladder (catheter kink/blocked → change), then bowel (anaesthetic gel),
  then skin. If SBP ≥ 150 mmHg persists, a rapid short-acting antihypertensive per
  protocol. Baseline BP in tetraplegia may be ~90–110 systolic — 130 can be a crisis.
  Give the person a wallet card.
- **Pressure injuries:** turn schedule (commonly 2-hourly in bed initially), pressure
  mapping for the seat cushion, pressure-relief every 15–30 min in the chair, daily skin
  checks with a mirror; nutrition.
- **VTE:** pharmacological prophylaxis (typically LMWH) once bleeding risk allows, for ~8–12
  weeks in motor-complete injuries.
- **Orthostatic hypotension:** binders, stockings, gradual tilt, midodrine if needed.
- Also: heterotopic ossification (hip; rising ALP, warm swelling), spasticity, osteoporosis,
  respiratory (assisted cough), shoulder preservation in manual chair users, mood.

## Gotchas
- Calling AIS A from the acute exam before sacral testing is done.
- Missing AD because "BP 140" looks fine in someone whose baseline is 95.
- Over-promising walking in AIS A because of zone-of-partial-preservation gains.
- Antibiotics for every positive urine dip in a catheterised patient.

## Verify
- [ ] ISNCSCI fully documented including sacral exam; AIS and NLI checked with calculator.
- [ ] Goals match level and completeness, and the person agreed them.
- [ ] Bladder method, schedule, medications and surveillance plan recorded.
- [ ] Bowel programme with timing and method in place.
- [ ] AD education given (T6 and above); skin, VTE and orthostatic plans charted.

*Uncertainty note:* functional outcomes by level are population averages; age, body
habitus, comorbidity and incomplete injuries shift them substantially.
