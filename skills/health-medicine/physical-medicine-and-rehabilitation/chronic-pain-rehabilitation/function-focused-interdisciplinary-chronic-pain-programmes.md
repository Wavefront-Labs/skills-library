# Function-focused interdisciplinary chronic pain programmes

> General clinical/professional reference, not patient-specific advice; prescribing and tapering decisions must follow current national guidance and the individual's clinical context.

**Goal:** shift the target from "zero pain" to valued function, combine exercise,
psychology and education in a coordinated programme, use procedures and medicines only
where they serve that function, and rationalise drugs that do more harm than good.

## 1. Assess (biopsychosocial)
- Pain mechanism: nociceptive, neuropathic (screen: DN4, painDETECT), **nociplastic**
  (widespread, sensory hypersensitivity, fatigue, sleep and cognitive symptoms), or mixed.
- **Rule out red flags and missed treatable pathology** before labelling chronic pain:
  malignancy, infection, inflammatory arthritis, fracture, progressive neurology.
- Measures to anchor the plan: **BPI** (intensity + interference), **PEG** (quick 3-item),
  **Pain Catastrophising Scale**, **Pain Self-Efficacy Questionnaire**, PHQ-9/GAD-7, sleep,
  function (e.g. ODI for back pain, PROMIS physical function), and risk stratification such
  as **STarT Back** for low back pain.
- Map **yellow flags** (fear-avoidance, catastrophising, passive coping), blue/black flags
  (work and system factors), substance use, trauma history.
- Medication history including all opioids, sedatives and gabapentinoids; current
  morphine milligram equivalents (MME); PDMP check where available.

## 2. Programme design
- **Interdisciplinary** = shared goals and team meetings, not parallel referrals. Core:
  physician, physiotherapist, psychologist, occupational therapist ± nurse/pharmacist.
- Components: graded exercise (aerobic + strengthening, quota-based not pain-contingent),
  **pacing** to break boom-bust, **graded exposure** for feared activities, **CBT or ACT**,
  pain neuroscience education, sleep hygiene/CBT-I, vocational planning and return to work.
- Intensity: high-intensity functional restoration programmes (several weeks, many hours
  per week) have the best evidence for disabling pain; lower-intensity group programmes for
  less complex cases. Match intensity to STarT-type risk.
- Goals: specific, functional, personally valued (walk to the shops, 4-hour shift, lift
  grandchild); measured at baseline, discharge and follow-up.

## 3. Interventional selection
- Use procedures as a **window for rehabilitation**, not a stand-alone cure; pair each one
  with an active plan.
- **Facet-mediated pain:** diagnostic medial branch blocks (commonly two, with ≥ 50–80 %
  relief thresholds by protocol) before **radiofrequency ablation**.
- **Radicular pain:** epidural steroid injection gives short-term relief for selected
  radiculopathy; little role in axial non-specific back pain.
- **SI joint:** diagnostic blocks; RFA evidence mixed.
- **Spinal cord stimulation:** for refractory neuropathic pain (e.g. persistent pain after
  spine surgery, painful diabetic neuropathy) after psychological screening and a trial.
- Avoid repeated procedures without functional gain.

## 4. Medication rationalisation
- First-line by mechanism: exercise and education for all; topical NSAIDs/short NSAID
  courses for nociceptive flares; **TCA/SNRI or gabapentinoid** for neuropathic pain;
  SNRIs/TCAs for some nociplastic pain. NICE advises against starting opioids, gabapentinoids
  and most other analgesics for chronic primary pain.
- **Opioids:** do not escalate for chronic non-cancer pain; reassess benefit vs harm at
  higher doses (CDC 2022 flags caution around ≥ 50 MME/day). Co-prescribe naloxone where risk
  is elevated; avoid opioid + benzodiazepine combinations.
- **Tapering** when harms exceed benefit: collaborative, slow (e.g. ~10 % of the dose per
  month for long-term users, faster if shorter duration), with psychological support;
  never abrupt discontinuation; screen for opioid use disorder and offer buprenorphine/
  methadone treatment if present.

## Gotchas
- Chasing pain scores while function and mood deteriorate.
- "Interdisciplinary" programmes where clinicians never meet or give conflicting messages.
- Forced rapid opioid tapers → withdrawal, illicit use, suicide risk.
- Sequential injections with no exercise plan between them.
- Dismissing nociplastic pain as "not real" — it erodes engagement.

## Verify
- [ ] Red flags excluded; mechanism(s) named.
- [ ] Baseline function, psychological and risk measures recorded.
- [ ] Goals are functional and owned by the person.
- [ ] Each intervention has selection criteria met and an attached active plan.
- [ ] Medication list reviewed: MME, sedatives, naloxone, taper plan if indicated.
- [ ] Outcomes re-measured at discharge and follow-up.

*Uncertainty note:* MME thresholds, block criteria and taper rates vary between
guidelines and countries; these figures are orientation only.
