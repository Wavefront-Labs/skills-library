# Stroke and TBI recovery staging and rehab planning

> General clinical/professional reference, not patient-specific advice; follow current national stroke/TBI guidelines and the multidisciplinary team's assessment.

**Goal:** know where the person is on the recovery curve, pick measures that track it,
screen for the hidden impairments, dose therapy enough to matter, and plan a safe discharge.

## 1. Stage the recovery
**Stroke**
- Severity: **NIHSS** at onset (prognostic); function: **mRS**, Barthel/FIM.
- Motor recovery: **Brunnstrom stages** (flaccid → synergy → isolated movement) and the
  **Fugl-Meyer** upper/lower limb score. Most spontaneous recovery is in the first ~3 months,
  steepest in the first weeks; early shoulder abduction and finger extension within ~72 h
  predict useful arm function (basis of tools like PREP2).
**TBI**
- Acute severity: GCS, duration of loss of consciousness, and **post-traumatic amnesia**
  (PTA). PTA duration is a strong outcome predictor (roughly: > 4 weeks = severe).
- Track PTA daily with the **GOAT** or Westmead scale; emergence ≈ two consecutive
  passing scores.
- Behaviour/cognition stage: **Rancho Los Amigos** levels I–X; agitation in Rancho IV is
  expected — manage environment first (low stimulation, consistent staff, sleep), drugs
  sparingly (avoid typical antipsychotics/benzodiazepines where possible; they may slow
  recovery).
- Disorders of consciousness: use the **CRS-R** repeatedly (≥ 5 assessments) before
  labelling vegetative/unresponsive wakefulness; misdiagnosis is common.

## 2. Screen the hidden impairments early
- **Dysphagia:** formal swallow screen before any oral intake; SLT assessment; instrumental
  study (VFSS/FEES) if silent aspiration suspected.
- **Neglect:** line bisection, star cancellation; affects safety and ADLs more than weakness.
- **Aphasia/cognitive-communication:** SLT; give information in aphasia-friendly format.
- **Mood:** post-stroke depression affects about one-third; screen (e.g. PHQ-9 adapted, or
  observational tools if aphasic). Also fatigue, emotionalism, anxiety.
- **Continence, pain (hemiplegic shoulder, central post-stroke pain), vision, sleep.**
- **TBI extras:** endocrine (hypopituitarism), post-traumatic headache, paroxysmal
  sympathetic hyperactivity, post-traumatic seizures, hydrocephalus if plateau/decline.

## 3. Therapy content and dose
- **Task-specific, repetitive, progressive** practice drives recovery; volume matters
  (many guidelines target ≥ 3 h/day of therapy across disciplines in intensive inpatient
  rehab when tolerated; NICE 2023 suggests at least 3 h/day, 5 days/week).
- Upper limb: CIMT for those with some wrist/finger extension; mirror therapy, robot-
  or FES-assisted practice as adjuncts.
- Gait: early mobilisation (but **avoid very early, high-dose out-of-bed mobilisation
  within 24 h** — AVERT), treadmill/body-weight support for selected people, AFO for foot drop.
- Spasticity and hemiplegic shoulder: positioning, support, early treatment of focal
  spasticity (see `health-medicine.physical-medicine-and-rehabilitation.spasticity-management.goal-directed-spasticity-oral-botulinum-toxin-and-intrathecal-baclofen`).
- Secondary prevention continues in rehab: BP, antithrombotics, lipids, AF, smoking.

## 4. Discharge planning (start on admission)
- Set an estimated discharge date and destination early; agree goals with the person and
  family.
- Home assessment, equipment, carer training, community/early-supported discharge.
- **Driving:** advise legal restrictions (vary by jurisdiction; often at least 1 month
  after stroke for private licences); vision fields and cognition count.
- Return to work: graded, with employer adjustments; cognitive fatigue is the usual barrier.
- Written plan: medications, secondary prevention, who to call, follow-up for spasticity,
  mood and cognition.

## Gotchas
- Discharging "independent" a person with unrecognised neglect or poor insight.
- Sedating Rancho IV agitation into a prolonged confusional state.
- Feeding before swallow screen; aspiration pneumonia sets rehab back weeks.
- Reading early plateau as final recovery — re-assess after medical issues are treated.
- Labelling DoC from a single examination.

## Verify
- [ ] Stage and severity recorded with named scales; reassessed at defined intervals.
- [ ] Swallow, neglect, communication, mood and continence screens done.
- [ ] Therapy dose and content documented against goals.
- [ ] Driving, work and secondary prevention advice given.
- [ ] Discharge plan names destination, supports and follow-up.

*Uncertainty note:* therapy-dose targets and driving rules differ between countries and
guideline editions; check the current local version.
