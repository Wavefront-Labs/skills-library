# Visual field defect localisation

**Goal:** from a reliable field test and the bedside signs, place the lesion at the
retina/optic nerve, chiasm, or retrochiasmal pathway, and decide the urgency and imaging.

## 1. Get a trustworthy field
- **Static automated perimetry** (e.g. Humphrey 24-2 or 30-2 SITA; 10-2 for central or
  paracentral defects). **Kinetic Goldmann** perimetry suits poor reliability, peripheral
  defects and functional loss.
- Check reliability indices (fixation losses, false positives, false negatives; device
  thresholds vary). Correct refraction for near, and note small pupils or lid artefacts.
- Always test **both eyes** and view them side by side as the patient sees them
  (left eye's field on the left).

## 2. Apply the meridian rules
- **Respects the horizontal meridian** (nasal step, arcuate, altitudinal): retina or
  optic nerve (nerve fibre layer arrangement, vascular supply).
- **Respects the vertical meridian**: chiasm or behind it.
- **Monocular** defect: anterior to the chiasm. **Binocular** defect respecting the
  vertical: chiasmal or retrochiasmal.

## 3. Pattern-to-site table
| Pattern | Likely site | Classic causes |
|---|---|---|
| Central/caecocentral scotoma | Macula or optic nerve | Optic neuritis, toxic/nutritional, Leber hereditary optic neuropathy |
| Arcuate, nasal step | Optic nerve head | Glaucoma, drusen |
| Altitudinal | Optic nerve or retinal vascular | Anterior ischaemic optic neuropathy, branch artery occlusion |
| Enlarged blind spot | Disc | Papilloedema, peripapillary disease |
| **Junctional scotoma** (central defect one eye + superotemporal defect other eye) | Junction of optic nerve and chiasm | Compressive lesion |
| **Bitemporal hemianopia** | Chiasm | Pituitary adenoma (often superior first), craniopharyngioma (often inferior first), meningioma |
| Incongruous homonymous hemianopia | Optic tract | Tumour, demyelination; may have RAPD contralateral to tract lesion |
| Superior homonymous quadrantanopia ("pie in the sky") | Temporal lobe (Meyer's loop) | Stroke, tumour, temporal lobectomy |
| Inferior homonymous quadrantanopia | Parietal lobe | Stroke, tumour |
| Congruous homonymous hemianopia, often macular sparing | Occipital cortex | Posterior cerebral artery stroke |
Congruity (how similar the two eyes' defects are) rises the more posterior the lesion.

## 4. Corroborate with bedside signs
- **RAPD**: present in unilateral or asymmetric optic nerve disease; absent with
  symmetrical bilateral disease and usually with pure cortical lesions.
- **Colour desaturation** and reduced acuity point to the optic nerve.
- Disc appearance: swelling (acute neuritis, ischaemia, papilloedema), pallor (chronic,
  including band or "bow-tie" atrophy in chiasmal/tract lesions).
- Associated neurology: headache, endocrine symptoms (pituitary), hemiparesis, aphasia,
  neglect (parietal).

## 5. Urgency and imaging
- Acute homonymous defect: treat as **stroke** pathway.
- Older patient with altitudinal loss plus headache, jaw claudication or raised
  inflammatory markers: consider **giant cell arteritis** same day.
- Chiasmal or tract pattern: **MRI brain with pituitary/orbital sequences** (with
  contrast unless contraindicated); check pituitary function; pituitary apoplexy is an
  emergency.
- Bilateral disc swelling with enlarged blind spots: urgent assessment for raised
  intracranial pressure.
Imaging protocol and urgency per local neuroradiology pathway.

## Gotchas
- Glaucoma and chiasmal disease coexist; a nasal step does not rule out a vertical
  meridian defect elsewhere.
- Lid droop gives a superior defect; uncorrected refraction or a small pupil depresses the
  whole field.
- Spiral or tubular fields that do not expand with distance suggest functional loss,
  but organic disease must be excluded first.
- A 24-2 can miss small central scotomas; run a 10-2 when the history is central.

## Verify
- [ ] Field test reliable, both eyes tested, displayed as seen by patient.
- [ ] Meridian rules and congruity applied to localise.
- [ ] RAPD, acuity, colour and disc findings consistent with the site.
- [ ] Urgency triaged (stroke, GCA, raised ICP, apoplexy considered).
- [ ] Appropriate imaging requested with the localised region specified.
