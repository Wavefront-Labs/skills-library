# Glaucoma diagnosis and treatment escalation

**Goal:** confirm glaucoma (or ocular hypertension), classify the angle, stage damage,
set an individual target IOP, and escalate treatment when progression or pressure
demands it.

## 1. Assemble the diagnostic set
- **IOP** by Goldmann applanation, ideally at different times of day; **central corneal
  thickness** (pachymetry) to interpret it.
- **Gonioscopy** to classify open vs closed angle (Shaffer or Spaeth grading); look for
  pigment, pseudoexfoliation, recession, synechiae.
- **Optic disc**: vertical cup-disc ratio relative to disc size, neuroretinal rim
  (ISNT rule: rim typically thickest Inferior > Superior > Nasal > Temporal), notching,
  disc haemorrhage, peripapillary atrophy, asymmetry between eyes.
- **OCT** RNFL and ganglion cell analysis; beware the "red disease" of normative
  databases (false positives in myopes, small or tilted discs).
- **Standard automated perimetry** (e.g. Humphrey 24-2 SITA, add 10-2 for central
  involvement); require reliable, repeatable defects before labelling.

## 2. Classify
| Category | Pattern |
|---|---|
| Ocular hypertension (OHT) | Raised IOP, normal disc and field |
| POAG | Open angle, characteristic disc/field damage |
| Normal-tension glaucoma | As POAG with IOP within normal range; exclude compressive/vascular causes |
| Primary angle-closure suspect / PAC / PACG | Per ISGEO classification by angle contact, PAS/raised IOP, and neuropathy |
| Secondary | Pseudoexfoliation, pigment dispersion, neovascular, steroid-induced, uveitic |
Stage severity by field loss (e.g. Hodapp-Parrish-Anderson mild/moderate/severe).

## 3. Set a target IOP
Base it on baseline IOP, stage, rate of progression, life expectancy and the other eye.
A common starting point is a **20-30% reduction from baseline**, more for advanced
disease; revise when progression is shown. Targets are guides, not end points.

## 4. Escalation ladder
1. **First line**: prostaglandin analogue drop (e.g. latanoprost) **or SLT**; the LiGHT
   trial supports SLT as a first-line option for OAG/OHT.
2. **Add or switch**: beta-blocker (timolol; check asthma, bradycardia), alpha-agonist
   (brimonidine; allergy common), topical carbonic anhydrase inhibitor, rho-kinase
   inhibitor where available. Prefer combination bottles to aid adherence; consider
   preservative-free drops for surface disease.
3. **Surgery** when maximum tolerated therapy fails, progression continues, or
   adherence is impossible: **MIGS** (often combined with cataract surgery) for
   mild-moderate disease; **trabeculectomy with antimetabolite** (mitomycin C) or
   **tube shunt** for lower targets or refractory disease.
4. **Angle closure**: laser peripheral iridotomy or lens extraction (EAGLE trial
   supports clear lens extraction in selected PACG/high-IOP PAC); acute attacks are
   emergencies managed per protocol.
Doses and contraindications per current formulary/verified reference.

## 5. Monitoring for progression
Use event- and trend-based analysis (Guided Progression Analysis, visual field index
slope). Several reliable fields early (often cited as around six in the first two years)
improve detection of fast progressors. Review interval scales with risk and stage.

## Gotchas
- Adherence and drop technique failure masquerade as treatment failure; ask and observe.
- Systemic beta-blockade plus topical timolol reduces effect and adds side effects.
- Steroid response: check IOP after starting topical or intravitreal steroids.
- Learning effect inflates early field defects; repeat before acting.

## Verify
- [ ] IOP with CCT, gonioscopy, disc, OCT and reliable fields documented for both eyes.
- [ ] Diagnosis and angle classification recorded with stage.
- [ ] Target IOP written and reviewed against progression.
- [ ] Treatment choice checked against contraindications and adherence.
- [ ] Progression analysis method and follow-up interval set.
