# Systematic eye examination

**Goal:** examine every eye in the same order so that nothing is missed, the findings
are recorded in a comparable way, and sight-threatening conditions are triaged correctly.

## 1. History framing
Onset (sudden vs gradual), laterality, pain, redness, photophobia, discharge, flashes and
floaters, curtain, diplopia (monocular vs binocular), headache and jaw claudication in the
over-50s, contact lens wear, trauma, systemic disease (diabetes, hypertension, autoimmune),
drugs (steroids, hydroxychloroquine, ethambutol, anticholinergics) and family history of
glaucoma.

## 2. Function before structure
Do functional tests **before** bright lights, drops or contact tonometry.
1. **Visual acuity** each eye, with current correction, then **pinhole**. Record Snellen
   (e.g. 6/9, 20/30) or logMAR/ETDRS. If below chart: count fingers, hand movements,
   perception of light. Pinhole improvement suggests refractive error.
2. **Near vision** and, where relevant, colour (Ishihara plates) and red desaturation.
3. **Pupils**: size, shape, direct/consensual reflex, **swinging flashlight test for a
   relative afferent pupillary defect (RAPD)**. Check before dilating; record if not done.
4. **Confrontation fields** (red target for subtle defects) and Amsler grid for macular
   symptoms.
5. **Ocular motility**: alignment (cover test), versions in nine positions, saccades,
   nystagmus.

## 3. Slit-lamp anterior segment
Work front to back: lids and lashes, conjunctiva (injection pattern, papillae vs
follicles), cornea (fluorescein staining under cobalt blue, Seidel test if leak
suspected), anterior chamber (depth, **cells and flare** graded per the SUN scheme,
hypopyon, hyphaema), iris (transillumination, rubeosis), lens (opacity grading, e.g.
LOCS III, pseudoexfoliation).
- **Van Herick** estimate of peripheral chamber depth before dilating; a shallow
  peripheral chamber warrants gonioscopy first.

## 4. Intraocular pressure
**Goldmann applanation tonometry** is the reference standard (topical anaesthetic plus
fluorescein). Rebound or non-contact tonometers are fine for screening. Normal population
range is commonly quoted as roughly 10-21 mmHg; thin corneas underestimate and thick
corneas overestimate IOP (central corneal thickness via pachymetry).

## 5. Dilated fundus examination
Dilate unless contraindicated (narrow angle risk, neuro observation of pupils,
iris-fixated lenses). Agent and regimen per local protocol.
- **Slit-lamp biomicroscopy** with a 78D/90D lens: disc (cup-disc ratio, rim, ISNT rule,
  haemorrhage), macula, vessels.
- **Binocular indirect ophthalmoscopy** with indentation for the periphery when flashes,
  floaters or trauma are present.
- Direct ophthalmoscope is a fallback; its field is small.

## 6. Triage summary
| Finding | Typical action |
|---|---|
| Sudden painless loss + RAPD | Same-day assessment (vascular occlusion, GCA, optic neuropathy) |
| Red painful eye, high IOP, mid-dilated pupil | Emergency (acute angle closure) |
| Corneal infiltrate in contact lens wearer | Same-day (microbial keratitis) |
| Flashes, floaters, curtain | Urgent dilated periphery check (retinal tear/detachment) |
| Chemical injury | Irrigate first, examine after |
Urgency definitions vary by service; follow local referral pathways.

## Gotchas
- Dilating before checking pupils loses the RAPD, the single most useful sign of optic
  nerve disease.
- Topical anaesthetic for tonometry abolishes pain cues: ask about pain and
  photophobia, and check corneal sensation where relevant, before instilling it.
- Contact lenses must be removed for staining and for accurate tonometry.
- Always examine **both** eyes; the "good" eye often holds the diagnosis (e.g.
  fellow-eye narrow angle, asymmetric discs).

## Verify
- [ ] Acuity and pinhole recorded for each eye with correction stated.
- [ ] Pupils and RAPD documented before dilation.
- [ ] Angle depth estimated before dilating; IOP recorded with method.
- [ ] Disc, macula and periphery documented for each eye, or reason not done.
- [ ] Red flags triaged per local pathway.
