# Strabismus assessment and measurement

**Goal:** characterise a misalignment precisely (direction, size, constancy, comitance,
cause) and identify amblyopia and binocular potential, so that refractive, orthoptic or
surgical decisions rest on reproducible measurements.

## 1. History
Age of onset, constant vs intermittent, family history, birth history and development,
diplopia (adults), head posture, old photographs, previous patching, glasses or surgery.
**New-onset** strabismus in a child or adult with diplopia needs exclusion of
neurological, orbital or ocular causes (retinoblastoma behind a childhood squint is the
classic warning; check red reflexes and fundus).

## 2. Observation and corneal reflexes
- Abnormal head posture: face turn, head tilt (fourth nerve palsy), chin up/down.
- **Hirschberg** corneal light reflex (roughly 1 mm decentration approximates 7 degrees,
  about 15 prism dioptres) when cover testing is impossible; **Krimsky** uses prisms to
  centre the reflex.
- Look for pseudostrabismus from epicanthic folds.

## 3. Cover testing (near and distance, with and without glasses)
1. **Cover-uncover test**: movement of the uncovered eye = **tropia**; movement of the
   covered eye on uncovering = **phoria**.
2. **Alternate cover test**: dissociates to reveal the total deviation.
3. **Prism cover test / alternate prism cover test (APCT)**: neutralise movement to
   quantify in **prism dioptres (PD)**. Base-out for eso, base-in for exo, base-down over
   the hypertropic eye.
Record at 1/3 m and 6 m, in primary position, and in key gaze positions for
incomitance; note control of intermittent exotropia (e.g. Newcastle Control Score).

## 4. Motility and comitance
- Versions and ductions in the nine positions; grade under/overactions (e.g. -4 to +4).
- **Comitant** (same deviation all gazes, usually childhood) vs **incomitant** (paretic
  or restrictive, e.g. thyroid eye disease, orbital fracture).
- **Parks-Bielschowsky three-step test** for vertical deviations (which eye is higher,
  worse in which horizontal gaze, worse on which head tilt).
- **Hess or Lees screen** charts document incomitance and track change; forced duction
  tests distinguish restriction from paresis.
- A and V patterns: measure up- and down-gaze.

## 5. Refraction and sensory status
- **Cycloplegic refraction** in children (agent per local protocol): uncorrected
  hyperopia drives **accommodative esotropia**; full correction may straighten the eyes.
- **Visual acuity** with age-appropriate tests (preferential looking, Kay pictures,
  crowded logMAR) to detect **amblyopia**; treat with glasses first, then occlusion or
  atropine penalisation (PEDIG evidence).
- Binocular function: Worth four-dot, Bagolini, stereoacuity (Frisby, TNO, Lang),
  fusional amplitudes, and in adults a diplopia field.

## 6. Planning
Surgery when the deviation is stable, refractive and amblyopia treatment optimised, and
measurements reproducible on at least two visits. Dose tables (mm of recession/resection
per PD) differ between surgeons and units; use your department's tables. Adjustable
sutures help in adults and restrictive cases. Botulinum toxin is an option in selected
cases.

## Gotchas
- Measuring without the glasses the patient wears, or without full cycloplegic correction,
  misses accommodative components.
- Intermittent exotropia measures larger at distance and after prolonged occlusion
  ("patch test"); measure fully dissociated.
- Variable, fatiguable deviations with ptosis: think **myasthenia**.
- Postoperative diplopia risk in adults with no fusion: assess with prism trial first.

## Verify
- [ ] Deviation type, direction and size in PD recorded near and distance, with/without glasses.
- [ ] Comitance assessed; vertical deviations three-stepped.
- [ ] Cycloplegic refraction and amblyopia status documented.
- [ ] Binocular function measured; neurological red flags considered.
- [ ] Stable measurements on repeat visits before surgical planning.
