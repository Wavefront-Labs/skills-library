# Microbial keratitis management

**Goal:** recognise an infective corneal ulcer early, obtain the right samples, start
effective empirical treatment the same day, and identify the organisms that need
different therapy.

## 1. History and risk factors
- **Contact lens wear** (especially overnight wear, swimming or showering in lenses,
  tap-water case rinsing) is the commonest risk in high-income settings.
- **Trauma**, especially with vegetable matter (fungal risk), ocular surface disease,
  previous surgery, topical steroid use, immunosuppression.
- Pain out of proportion to signs suggests **acanthamoeba**; recurrent episodes with
  reduced corneal sensation suggest **HSV**.

## 2. Examine and document
Slit-lamp: infiltrate size (two perpendicular diameters), epithelial defect with
fluorescein, depth, thinning, satellite lesions, feathery margins (fungal), ring
infiltrate or radial perineuritis (acanthamoeba), dendrites with terminal bulbs (HSV
epithelial), anterior chamber reaction and **hypopyon height**. Draw or photograph for
comparison. Check corneal sensation before anaesthetic where HSV is possible.

## 3. Sample before treating (when feasible)
- **Corneal scrape** under topical anaesthetic (preservative-free) with a blade or
  needle from the leading edge and base: Gram stain and culture (blood, chocolate,
  Sabouraud agar; non-nutrient agar with E. coli overlay for acanthamoeba), plus PCR
  where available for HSV, fungi and acanthamoeba.
- Send **contact lenses, case and solution** for culture.
- Small peripheral infiltrates may be treated empirically per local protocol; central,
  large or atypical ulcers should be scraped.
- In vivo confocal microscopy can support fungal or acanthamoeba diagnosis.

## 4. Empirical treatment
| Suspected organism | Typical first-line approach |
|---|---|
| Bacterial (default) | Intensive topical broad-spectrum: fluoroquinolone monotherapy or fortified antibiotics (e.g. cephalosporin plus aminoglycoside) for severe ulcers |
| Fungal (filamentous) | Topical natamycin (MUTT favoured it over voriconazole for filamentous fungi); add or switch per sensitivities |
| Acanthamoeba | Biguanide (PHMB) and/or diamidine/chlorhexidine; prolonged course |
| HSV epithelial | Topical or oral antiviral; avoid steroids in epithelial disease |
Initial frequency is typically hourly day and night for 24-48 h, then tapered by
response. Agents, concentrations and frequency per current local protocol/verified
reference. Add cycloplegia for comfort.

## 5. Steroids and review
- Adjunctive topical steroid for bacterial keratitis is controversial: SCUT showed no
  overall benefit and possible harm with Nocardia; consider only after organism is known
  and the ulcer is improving on effective therapy. **Avoid in fungal and early
  acanthamoeba.**
- Review daily initially: infiltrate size, epithelial healing, hypopyon, thinning.
- Failure to improve at 48-72 h: re-scrape, consider biopsy, review sensitivities,
  revisit diagnosis.

## 6. Escalation
Impending or actual perforation: tissue adhesive and bandage contact lens for small
perforations, **therapeutic keratoplasty** for larger or progressive cases. Corneal
cross-linking (PACK-CXL) is investigational for some ulcers.

## Gotchas
- Patching an infected eye or leaving contact lens wearers on steroids worsens outcome.
- Preserved anaesthetic or fluorescein before scraping can reduce culture yield.
- Antibiotic toxicity (epitheliopathy) after days of intensive drops mimics non-healing.
- Acanthamoeba is often misdiagnosed as HSV for weeks.

## Verify
- [ ] Risk factors, lens history and corneal sensation documented.
- [ ] Ulcer dimensions and hypopyon recorded with drawing/photo.
- [ ] Scrape and cultures sent (or empirical decision justified).
- [ ] Empirical regimen matches suspected organism and local protocol.
- [ ] Daily review plan and escalation criteria set.
