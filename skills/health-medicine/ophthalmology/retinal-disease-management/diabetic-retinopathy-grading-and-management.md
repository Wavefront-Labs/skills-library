# Diabetic retinopathy grading and management

**Goal:** grade retinopathy and maculopathy separately and reproducibly, then match each
grade to the right follow-up interval or treatment.

## 1. Examine and image
Dilated fundus exam or two-field (or widefield) colour photography, plus **OCT of the
macula** whenever maculopathy is suspected. Fluorescein angiography (FFA) or OCT
angiography helps define ischaemia and neovascularisation when unclear.

## 2. Grade retinopathy (severity)
International Clinical Diabetic Retinopathy scale (simplified from ETDRS):
| Grade | Key features |
|---|---|
| No apparent DR | No abnormalities |
| Mild NPDR | Microaneurysms only |
| Moderate NPDR | More than microaneurysms, less than severe |
| Severe NPDR | **4-2-1 rule**: haemorrhages in all 4 quadrants, venous beading in 2+, or IRMA in 1+ |
| PDR | Neovascularisation (NVD/NVE), vitreous or preretinal haemorrhage |
UK screening uses R0/R1/R2/R3 (R3a active, R3s stable treated) plus M0/M1 and photocoagulation (P) codes; map carefully between systems.

## 3. Grade maculopathy separately
- **Centre-involving DMO (CI-DMO)** on OCT: thickening involving the central subfield.
- **Non-centre-involving DMO**: observe or consider focal laser depending on proximity.
- ETDRS "clinically significant macular oedema" (CSME) is the older clinical definition
  still used for focal laser decisions.

## 4. Match grade to action
| Finding | Usual action |
|---|---|
| None / mild NPDR, no DMO | Routine rescreen (interval per programme, often 1-2 years) |
| Moderate NPDR | Closer review (months), optimise systemic control |
| Severe NPDR | Specialist review; consider PRP especially if follow-up unreliable or type 2 with high risk |
| PDR | **PRP** and/or anti-VEGF; urgency rises with NVD, vitreous haemorrhage, rubeosis |
| CI-DMO with reduced vision | **Intravitreal anti-VEGF** first line |
| Non-clearing vitreous haemorrhage, tractional detachment threatening macula | Vitrectomy referral |
Intervals and thresholds vary by programme and guideline; check current local pathway.

## 5. Anti-VEGF practicalities
- Agents include ranibizumab, aflibercept, bevacizumab (off-label in many regions) and
  faricimab; choice and dosing per current local protocol and formulary.
- Typical pattern: loading phase of monthly injections then **treat-and-extend** or PRN
  based on OCT thickness and acuity.
- Intravitreal steroid (dexamethasone or fluocinolone implant) for pseudophakic or
  anti-VEGF-resistant DMO; monitor IOP and cataract.
- Injection technique: povidone-iodine antisepsis, lid speculum, inject through pars
  plana (roughly 3.5-4 mm posterior to limbus); check perfusion/hand-motion vision after.

## 6. Systemic partnership
HbA1c, blood pressure and lipid control slow progression; communicate grade to the
diabetes team. Rapid HbA1c reduction can transiently worsen retinopathy ("early
worsening"), including with some newer glucose-lowering agents; examine before and after
intensification where indicated.

## Gotchas
- Pregnancy accelerates retinopathy; increase surveillance per pregnancy pathway.
- Cataract surgery can worsen DMO; treat or stabilise macula pre-operatively.
- Fibrovascular proliferation may contract after anti-VEGF ("crunch") causing traction
  detachment; plan vitrectomy timing in advanced PDR.
- Endophthalmitis after injection presents with pain, reduced vision and hypopyon,
  usually within days: same-day assessment.

## Verify
- [ ] Retinopathy and maculopathy graded separately using a named scale.
- [ ] OCT obtained for any suspected macular involvement.
- [ ] Action and interval match grade and local pathway.
- [ ] Anti-VEGF agent, dosing regimen and consent per current protocol.
- [ ] Systemic risk factors communicated to diabetes care team.
