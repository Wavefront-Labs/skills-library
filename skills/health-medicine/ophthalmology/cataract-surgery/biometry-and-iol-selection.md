# Biometry and IOL selection

**Goal:** choose an intraocular lens (IOL) power and type that lands the patient close to
the agreed refractive target, and catch the measurement errors that cause refractive
surprise.

## 1. Agree the refractive target
- Discuss distance (emmetropia, often a slight myopic target such as about -0.25 D),
  near (myopic), or **monovision**; consider occupation, hobbies and the fellow eye.
- **Anisometropia**: a large inter-eye difference after first-eye surgery is poorly
  tolerated; plan second-eye timing or a compromise target.
- Record the target explicitly in the notes and on the lens selection sheet.

## 2. Acquire biometry
- **Optical biometry** (partial coherence interferometry or swept-source OCT based,
  e.g. IOLMaster-class devices) is first line; **immersion A-scan ultrasound** when the
  lens is too dense or fixation is poor (contact A-scan compresses the cornea and
  shortens axial length).
- Measure **both eyes**: axial length (AL), keratometry (K), anterior chamber depth,
  and on newer devices lens thickness and white-to-white.
- Remove contact lenses beforehand for long enough to let the cornea stabilise (often
  quoted as around 1-2 weeks for soft and longer for rigid lenses; check local policy).

## 3. Quality checks (repeat if any fail)
| Check | Typical trigger to repeat/verify |
|---|---|
| Inter-eye AL difference | More than about 0.3 mm without explanation |
| Inter-eye mean K difference | More than about 0.9 D |
| Mean K outside normal range | Roughly below 41 D or above 47 D |
| Signal quality / SNR | Below device threshold |
| AL outside normal | Short (<22 mm) or long (>26 mm) eyes need formula care |
Thresholds are commonly cited values; use your service's standard operating procedure.

## 4. Choose the formula
- **Average eyes**: most modern formulas perform similarly.
- **Short eyes** (AL < ~22 mm): Hoffer Q, Haigis or newer formulas (Barrett Universal II,
  Kane) preferred over SRK/T.
- **Long eyes** (AL > ~26 mm): SRK/T or modern formulas; consider Wang-Koch AL adjustment
  with older formulas to avoid hyperopic surprise.
- Use **optimised lens constants** (A-constant or equivalents) for the device and lens
  model, ideally from ULIB-type databases or your own outcomes audit.

## 5. Choose the lens type
- **Monofocal** standard; **toric** when regular corneal astigmatism is significant
  (often around 1.0-1.5 D or more; use a toric calculator that accounts for posterior
  corneal astigmatism and surgically induced astigmatism).
- **Multifocal/EDOF**: counsel on halos, glare and contrast loss; avoid with macular
  disease, glaucoma or irregular corneas.
- Plan a **backup lens** (sulcus-fixated power adjustment, three-piece) for posterior
  capsule rupture.

## 6. Special cases
- **Post-refractive surgery** (LASIK, PRK, RK): standard keratometry misestimates true
  corneal power; use dedicated formulas (e.g. Barrett True-K, Haigis-L, ASCRS calculator)
  and warn of higher surprise risk.
- **Silicone oil-filled eyes**: optical biometry or ultrasound velocity correction needed.
- **Keratoconus**: K values unreliable; consider conservative target.

## Gotchas
- Wrong-eye or wrong-lens error is a never event: use a WHO-style checklist and verify
  patient, eye, lens model and power at time-out.
- Dry eye corrupts keratometry and toric planning; treat ocular surface and remeasure.
- Changing lens model on the day without recalculating with its own constant.

## Verify
- [ ] Refractive target agreed and documented, fellow eye considered.
- [ ] Both eyes measured; inter-eye differences within limits or explained.
- [ ] Formula appropriate to axial length; constants optimised for the lens.
- [ ] Toric/premium decisions justified; backup lens planned.
- [ ] Lens details confirmed at surgical time-out.
