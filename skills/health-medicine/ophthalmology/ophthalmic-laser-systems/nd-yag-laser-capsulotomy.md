# Nd:YAG laser capsulotomy

**Goal:** clear a visually significant posterior capsule opacification (PCO) with the
least energy, no lens damage and no avoidable pressure spike or retinal complication.

## 1. Select the case
- Symptoms (blur, glare, reduced contrast) with PCO on retroillumination and visual
  function consistent with the opacity; exclude other causes (macular disease,
  refractive change, dry eye).
- Rule out active inflammation; record IOP, and examine the retinal periphery and
  macula where possible (higher detachment risk in high myopes and younger patients).
- Consider waiting several months after cataract surgery unless clearly indicated; local
  policies vary.
- Consent: IOP spike, inflammation, IOL pitting, CME, retinal detachment (rare),
  floaters.

## 2. Prepare
- **Laser safety**: designated controlled area, warning sign, door control, trained
  operator; Nd:YAG at 1064 nm is invisible, so check that the aiming beam and goggles
  appropriate to the wavelength are in place for others in the room.
- Pre-treatment IOP prophylaxis (alpha-agonist such as apraclonidine or brimonidine is
  commonly used) per local protocol.
- Dilate if needed for a larger opening; topical anaesthetic and a capsulotomy contact
  lens (e.g. Abraham) to improve focus, stability and energy density.

## 3. Settings and focus strategy
- Mode: Q-switched, single pulse per burst to start.
- **Start low** (often around 1 mJ or a little more, depending on the device) and titrate
  up only as needed; thicker fibrotic membranes need more energy.
- **Posterior offset/defocus**: focus slightly behind the capsule (device-dependent
  offset setting) so the plasma expands away from the IOL and pitting is reduced.
- Aim the two aiming beams into a single sharp spot on the capsule.
Exact energies and offsets vary by platform; follow manufacturer guidance and local SOP.

## 4. Pattern
- **Cruciate**: start at the 12 o'clock periphery and work toward the centre along
  vertical then horizontal lines, keeping the visual axis free of early pits; good for
  most PCO.
- **Circular / "can-opener"**: series of shots outlining a round opening, leaving a
  hinge or freeing a disc that falls inferiorly; can reduce central pitting but large
  free fragments may float.
- Aim for an opening at least as large as the scotopic pupil (often about 3-4 mm or
  more, smaller for some multifocal lens designs per lens guidance).
- Record total shots and cumulative energy.

## 5. Aftercare
- Check IOP about 1 hour post laser (or per local protocol); higher risk with glaucoma,
  sulcus lenses, high cumulative energy.
- Short topical steroid course is used by some units; per local protocol.
- Advise urgent return for new flashes, floaters, curtain, pain or worsening vision.

## Related platform notes
The same laser family delivers **laser peripheral iridotomy** for angle closure;
frequency-doubled 532 nm Nd:YAG is used for **SLT** and **panretinal photocoagulation**
with entirely different settings and lenses. Never assume settings carry over.

## Gotchas
- Focusing on the anterior capsule or IOL rather than posterior capsule causes pitting
  and wasted energy.
- Excess energy on a silicone IOL pits readily; hydrophobic acrylic tolerates more but
  still pits.
- Treating vitreous condensation or anterior hyaloid instead of capsule: reassess focus.
- Silicone oil eyes and lens-capsule fluid (capsular bag distension) need specific plans.

## Verify
- [ ] PCO is the cause of symptoms; retina and IOP checked; consent documented.
- [ ] Laser area safety controls in place; IOP prophylaxis given per protocol.
- [ ] Lowest effective energy, posterior offset used, adequate opening achieved.
- [ ] Shots and total energy recorded; post-laser IOP checked.
- [ ] Return advice for retinal detachment symptoms given.
