# Specimen grossing, margins and synoptic reporting

*General professional reference. Follow your laboratory's SOPs and the current CAP/RCPath/ICCR protocol for each organ; staging editions and required data elements change.*

**Goal:** turn a resection specimen into a correct, complete diagnosis — the right patient,
the right orientation, margins you can trust, enough sections to answer every
staging question, and a report the oncologist can act on without phoning you.

## 1. Before the knife: identity and history
- Two identifiers on the container match the requisition; laterality and specimen site
  match. Discrepancy = stop and resolve before cutting (never "fix" labels yourself).
- Read the clinical history, imaging and prior biopsies: the question determines the sampling
  (e.g. post-neoadjuvant tumour bed needs mapping; a tiny prior biopsy cancer may be hard to find).
- Note **cold ischaemia time** (removal to formalin) and **fixation time** — they affect
  immunohistochemistry and biomarkers (e.g. breast ER/PR/HER2 guidance: cold ischaemia
  ≤ 1 h, formalin 6-72 h).

## 2. Fixation
- 10% neutral buffered formalin, roughly 10:1 volume to tissue. Formalin penetrates
  slowly (~1 mm/h), so **open hollow organs and bread-loaf solid organs** promptly.
- Fresh tissue for frozen section, flow cytometry, microbiology, cytogenetics or tumour
  banking must be triaged **before** fixation — know which cases need it (e.g. suspected lymphoma).

## 3. Orientation and inking
- Use the surgeon's sutures/clips and a diagram; if orientation is unclear, ask the surgeon
  and document it.
- **Ink every surgically relevant margin** with a colour code before cutting; blot dry so
  ink stays on the surface and does not track into cracks.
- Record the ink key in the gross description.

## 4. Gross description and sampling
- Describe: specimen type, dimensions (three axes), weight where relevant, lesion size
  (three dimensions), colour/consistency, relationship and **distance to each margin**,
  and other findings.
- Sample to the organ protocol: tumour (including deepest invasion), closest margins
  (perpendicular sections for close margins; en face shave for distant ones — know which
  your protocol expects), relationship to adjacent structures, background tissue, and **all
  lymph nodes** (many protocols set minimum node counts, e.g. ≥ 12 for colorectal resection).
- Keep a **block key** (which cassette holds what). A good block key lets a reviewer
  reconstruct the specimen without the tissue.
- Photograph complex specimens.

## 5. Microscopic assessment
- Diagnosis and histologic type (current WHO classification), grade per the organ-specific
  system (e.g. Nottingham for breast, Gleason/Grade Group for prostate).
- Depth/extent of invasion, lymphovascular and perineural invasion, margin status with
  distance, nodes (number examined and positive, extranodal extension), treatment effect.
- Ancillary tests: IHC with appropriate controls; biomarkers required by protocol (e.g.
  mismatch repair in colorectal and endometrial cancer, HER2 in breast/gastric).

## 6. Staging and synoptic reporting
- Assign **pTNM** using the current AJCC/UICC edition your institution has adopted, with
  prefixes (y for post-therapy, r for recurrence, m for multiple).
- Use the **synoptic checklist** (CAP/RCPath/ICCR): required elements in a fixed format
  improve completeness; free text adds nuance.
- Communicate **critical or unexpected findings** (e.g. unexpected malignancy, transplant
  rejection, organisms in an immunocompromised patient) directly and document the call.

## 7. Frozen section (intraoperative consultation)
Answer the surgeon's specific question (diagnosis? margin? adequate tissue?) quickly;
know the limits (fatty tissue freezes poorly; some diagnoses should be deferred to permanents).
Keep frozen-permanent correlation as a QA metric.

## Gotchas
- Inking after cutting creates false-positive margins.
- Under-fixed tissue gives misleading biomarker results.
- Pieces from small biopsies can be lost in processing — use biopsy bags/sponges and count fragments.
- Specimen mix-ups (floaters, carry-over) happen at the bench; one specimen open at a time.

## Verify
- [ ] Identifiers, site and laterality matched before cutting.
- [ ] Cold ischaemia and fixation times recorded.
- [ ] Margins oriented, inked, colour key documented.
- [ ] Sampling meets the organ protocol, including node count; block key complete.
- [ ] Synoptic report complete with pTNM edition stated.
- [ ] Critical results communicated and documented.
