# Hospital autopsy and cause-of-death formulation

*General professional reference for the consented (hospital/academic) autopsy. Authorisation, retention of tissue, and when a death must be referred to a coroner or medical examiner are governed by local law — follow your jurisdiction and institution. Forensic autopsy is a separate discipline.*

**Goal:** answer the clinicians' and family's questions about why a person died, produce
an accurate cause-of-death statement, and feed what is learned back into care — treating
the deceased and their family with respect throughout.

## 1. Before the examination
- **Authority:** confirm valid consent from the appropriate person (or legal authority for
  a medicolegal case) and its **scope** — full, limited (e.g. chest only), or no retention of
  organs/tissue. Work strictly within it.
- **Is this a reportable death?** Unexpected, unnatural, injury-related, in custody,
  procedure-related or of unknown cause deaths generally must go to the coroner/medical
  examiner first. If in doubt, ask before starting.
- **Read the chart:** clinical course, procedures, imaging, cultures, drugs, devices, the
  clinicians' specific questions. Talk to the clinical team.
- **Risk assessment:** known or suspected high-risk infection (TB, blood-borne viruses,
  prion disease, novel agents), radioactive implants or seeds, implanted devices — adapt the
  facility, PPE and technique; some cases require specialised suites.

## 2. Examination framework
- **External examination:** identification, documentation of medical devices and their
  positions (lines, tubes, drains, pacemakers), surgical wounds, and relevant findings.
- **Internal examination:** systematic, organ by organ, by an established method (e.g.
  en masse, en bloc, or organ-by-organ evisceration) chosen for the questions asked.
  Weigh and measure organs; compare to reference ranges adjusted for body size.
- Confirm device placement in situ before removal (e.g. line tips, tube positions).
- Answer each clinical question directly; dissect the relevant system in detail (e.g.
  coronary arteries in cross-section at short intervals, pulmonary arteries for emboli).

## 3. Ancillary sampling
- **Histology** from all major organs and every lesion — many key diagnoses are microscopic.
- **Microbiology** collected aseptically and early (blood from heart or great vessel after
  surface sterilisation, lung tissue, CSF); interpret alongside post-mortem translocation and contamination.
- **Toxicology**, vitreous humour chemistry (e.g. glucose/ketones, electrolytes), genetics
  (e.g. sudden cardiac death in the young), as indicated.
- Retention only within consent and legal authorisation; document what is retained and its fate.

## 4. Formulating the cause of death
Use the WHO/ICD structure on the certificate:
- **Part I:** the causal sequence — immediate cause at the top (line a) back to the
  **underlying cause** (the disease or injury that started the chain) at the bottom. Each line
  should be "due to (or as a consequence of)" the line below.
- **Part II:** other significant conditions contributing to death but not part of the chain.
- **Mechanisms are not causes:** "cardiac arrest", "respiratory failure" or "multi-organ failure"
  alone are not acceptable; name the disease that caused them.
- **Manner of death** (natural, accident, suicide, homicide, undetermined, pending) is
  recorded where the jurisdiction requires it; a non-natural manner usually means the case belongs to the medicolegal system.

## 5. Clinicopathologic correlation and reporting
- Provisional anatomical diagnosis promptly (often within a few working days); final
  report after histology and ancillary results.
- Write a **clinicopathologic summary**: how the findings explain the clinical course and answer each question.
- Classify major discrepancies between clinical and autopsy diagnoses (e.g. Goldman
  classes) for mortality and morbidity review — autopsies still reveal clinically missed diagnoses.
- Communicate findings to the clinical team; offer families an explanation through the clinician or a family meeting, in plain, gentle language.

## Gotchas
- Starting a case that should have gone to the coroner/medical examiner can invalidate the investigation.
- Removing devices before documenting their position loses key evidence.
- Post-mortem cultures are often contaminated; single skin-flora isolates rarely mean sepsis.
- Mechanism-only death certificates are a common certification error.

## Verify
- [ ] Authority and scope confirmed; reportability assessed.
- [ ] Clinical questions listed before starting.
- [ ] Risk assessment done; PPE and facility appropriate.
- [ ] Histology and indicated ancillary samples taken within consent.
- [ ] Cause-of-death sequence ends in an underlying disease, not a mechanism.
- [ ] Clinicopathologic summary sent and discrepancies fed to mortality review.
