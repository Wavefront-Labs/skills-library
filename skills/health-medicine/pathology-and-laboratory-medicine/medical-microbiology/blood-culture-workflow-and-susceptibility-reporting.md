# Blood-culture workflow and susceptibility reporting

*General professional reference. Breakpoints, expert rules and reporting policies change annually (EUCAST) or periodically (CLSI); use the version your laboratory has adopted and your local antimicrobial reporting policy.*

**Goal:** turn a positive bottle into actionable information as fast as possible — Gram
stain, identification, susceptibility — and report it in a way that steers prescribers
to the right narrow drug, not just any active drug.

## 1. Quality starts at collection
- **Volume** is the main driver of sensitivity: adults typically 20-30 mL per set (split
  aerobic/anaerobic), 2-3 sets per episode; paediatric volumes by weight. Monitor bottle fill as a lab metric.
- **Contamination rate** target commonly < 3% (lower achievable with initial specimen
  diversion devices and phlebotomy teams). Single-set culturing doubles ambiguity.
- Bottles to the analyser promptly (delays reduce yield, especially if incubated off-instrument).

## 2. The positive bottle: Gram stain and the first call
- Gram stain and **phone the clinical team** promptly (positive blood culture is a
  critical result in most labs). Record who was told and when.
- Report **time to positivity** and number of positive sets — they help separate
  contaminants from true bacteraemia (contaminants often flag later and in one set only).
- Gram-stain morphology guides empiric therapy: clusters (staphylococci), chains/pairs
  (streptococci/enterococci), Gram-negative rods, yeasts. Yeasts and *S. aureus* trigger specific pathways.
- Subculture to appropriate media; direct rapid tests from the broth where validated.

## 3. Rapid identification
- **MALDI-TOF MS** from early growth (short-incubation subculture) or directly from the
  bottle (extraction protocols) — same-day identification.
- **Multiplex PCR panels** on positive bottles: organism ID plus key resistance genes
  (mecA/C, vanA/B, CTX-M, KPC, NDM, VIM, IMP, OXA-48-like) within ~1-2 hours. Genotype
  does not always equal phenotype — confirm with AST.
- Pair rapid results with **stewardship action** (real-time advice); speed without a
  feedback loop rarely changes outcomes.

## 4. Antimicrobial susceptibility testing (AST)
- Methods: disk diffusion, broth microdilution (reference), gradient strips, automated systems;
  EUCAST **rapid AST** direct from bottles (short incubation, specific breakpoints) where validated.
- Interpret with **current breakpoints**: S, I (EUCAST: susceptible, increased exposure),
  R; note **ATU** (area of technical uncertainty) where EUCAST defines one.
- Apply **expert rules** and intrinsic resistance (e.g. *Klebsiella* and ampicillin;
  enterococci and cephalosporins; AmpC producers and third-generation cephalosporins).
- Phenotypic and genotypic confirmation for ESBL, AmpC and **carbapenemases** (immunochromatographic
  or PCR) — carbapenemase type drives choice of newer agents and infection-control action.
- QC strains with every run per standard.

## 5. Reporting
- **Selective/cascade reporting:** report narrow-spectrum agents first; release broad
  ones only when needed (resistance, allergy, site). This is a stewardship tool, endorsed
  by IDSA/SHEA and EUCAST guidance.
- Add **interpretive comments**: "*S. aureus* in blood is never a contaminant — recommend
  repeat cultures and ID consultation", "Likely contaminant if single set; correlate clinically".
- Notify infection prevention of alert organisms (MRSA, VRE, CPE, *C. auris*).
- Produce a **cumulative antibiogram** annually per CLSI M39 (first isolate per patient per
  period, ≥ 30 isolates per organism for reliable percentages).

## Gotchas
- Contaminant-looking organisms (coagulase-negative staphylococci) can be real in prosthetic devices — count sets and context.
- Automated systems can miss inducible resistance (e.g. clindamycin D-test for staphylococci).
- A susceptible report does not mean the drug reaches the site (e.g. CNS, urine-only agents in blood).
- Breakpoint changes alter susceptibility rates year to year; flag them when publishing the antibiogram.

## Verify
- [ ] Blood-culture volume, set number and contamination rate monitored.
- [ ] Gram stain phoned and documented with time and recipient.
- [ ] Rapid ID/resistance genes reported with stewardship follow-up.
- [ ] AST interpreted with the current breakpoint version and expert rules.
- [ ] Cascade reporting and interpretive comments applied.
- [ ] Alert organisms notified to infection prevention.
