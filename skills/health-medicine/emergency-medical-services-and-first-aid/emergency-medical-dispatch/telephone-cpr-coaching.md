# Telephone CPR coaching (dispatcher-assisted CPR)

**Goal:** get a frightened untrained caller doing effective chest compressions within
about a minute of answering, because the dispatcher is effectively the first responder
in most out-of-hospital cardiac arrests.

## 1. Recognise arrest fast — two questions
Your local protocol (MPDS, criteria-based dispatch, other) sets exact wording; the core
is:
1. **"Is the person awake?"** (conscious/responsive)
2. **"Are they breathing normally?"**
Unconscious **and** not breathing normally → treat as **cardiac arrest**. Start CPR
instructions immediately and dispatch at highest priority in parallel.
- Benchmarks (AHA T-CPR): **recognition within 60 s** and **first compression within
  90 s** of call answer (check local KPIs).

## 2. Listen for agonal breathing
Callers describe agonal breathing as "gasping", "snoring", "barely breathing", "making
noises", "breathing every few seconds". **These are arrest.** If unsure, ask the caller
to put the phone near the mouth, or to describe the breaths ("Tell me 'now' every time
they breathe"). Brief seizure-like activity at collapse followed by unresponsiveness also
indicates arrest.

## 3. Coaching script (adult, hands-only)
Keep instructions short and imperative; repeat as needed.
1. "Put the phone on speaker and next to them."
2. "Get them flat on their back on the floor."
3. "Kneel beside their chest."
4. "Put the heel of your hand in the centre of their chest, other hand on top."
5. "Lock your elbows, push down hard and fast — at least 5 centimetres / 2 inches."
6. "Let the chest come all the way up between pushes."
7. **Count aloud with them:** "One, two, three, four…" at **100–120/min**; many centres
   use a metronome.
8. "Don't stop until help takes over."
- Default for adults: **compression-only** instructions — easier, faster, as effective
  in early cardiac arrest.
- Rescue breath instructions are used for children, drowning and asphyxial arrests per
  local protocol, if the caller is willing.

## 4. AED and responders
- If a **second person** is present: "Send them to get the AED from [location] now"
  — check public-access AED registries.
- Alert community first responders / smartphone app networks (e.g. GoodSAM,
  PulsePoint) where available.
- When the AED arrives: "Turn it on and do what it says," then resume coaching.
- Direct someone to open doors, secure pets, and meet the crew.

## 5. Sustaining quality
- Give **feedback** on rate ("faster", "keep that speed").
- Encourage swapping rescuers about every **2 minutes** if available.
- Ask "Is the chest going down?" — callers often push too gently.
- Stay on the line until crew arrival and handover.

## 6. Special cases (per protocol)
- **Pregnancy, obesity, bed:** still floor if possible.
- **Obvious death** criteria (decomposition, decapitation, etc.) — use protocol
  exclusion wording.
- **DNR/advance directive** present: follow local policy on verification.
- **Choking** leading to unresponsiveness: proceed to CPR.

## Gotchas
- **Accepting "they are breathing"** without asking "normally" — misses agonal gasps.
- **Pulse checks by callers** are unreliable; do not ask.
- **Over-questioning** before starting instructions (address and callback number are
  essential; most else can follow).
- Callers stop to answer questions or hang up — tell them to keep going and keep the
  line open.
- Language barriers: use interpreter services but start with simple demonstrative
  phrases.
- Bystander's emotional distress: calm, firm, repetitive instructions work best; offer
  reassurance afterward (calls are traumatic for dispatchers too — debrief).

## Verify
- [ ] Address/location confirmed first.
- [ ] Consciousness and normal breathing asked explicitly; agonal breathing treated as
      arrest.
- [ ] Highest-priority response dispatched in parallel.
- [ ] Hands-only instructions started within about 90 s of answering.
- [ ] Rate coached at 100–120/min with counting or metronome.
- [ ] AED and first responders mobilised; caller kept on line until crew arrival.
- [ ] Call reviewed against T-CPR time metrics.
