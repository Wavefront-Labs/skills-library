# Field hypothermia management and evacuation

**Goal:** stop further heat loss, avoid triggering a cardiac arrest by rough handling,
rewarm what can safely be rewarmed in the field, and get the severely hypothermic
patient to a centre capable of ECLS (ECMO/bypass) rewarming.

## 1. Recognise and stage
Core temperature is often unavailable in the field (oral and tympanic readings are
unreliable in cold); stage clinically. The **revised Swiss staging** (ICAR) uses
**likelihood of hypothermia** by consciousness:
- **Conscious, shivering** — likely mild (roughly 35–32 °C).
- **Impaired consciousness, not shivering** — likely moderate (roughly 32–28 °C).
- **Unconscious** — likely severe (<28 °C), arrest risk rising sharply below about
  30 °C and especially <24 °C. (Bands are approximate — trauma, intoxication and
  hypoglycaemia confuse the picture.)
- **No vital signs** — hypothermic cardiac arrest possible.
Key sign: **stopping shivering while still cold is deterioration**, not improvement.

## 2. Handle gently, keep horizontal
- Move the patient **horizontally** and gently, especially from cold water — rescue
  collapse after removal from water is linked to hydrostatic and postural blood pressure
  loss.
- Avoid rough movement, unnecessary limb exercise or walking in moderate/severe cases;
  a cold myocardium is irritable and can fibrillate.

## 3. Stop heat loss — the hypowrap
1. Get off the ground: insulating layer (pads, packs, branches) beneath.
2. Shelter from wind and wet.
3. Remove wet clothing **only if shelter is available** and it can be done quickly;
   otherwise wrap over it with a vapour barrier.
4. Layers (inside → out): dry insulation/sleeping bag → **vapour barrier** (plastic,
   foil blanket, tarp) → windproof outer. Cover the head and neck, leave a face opening.
5. Add **heat sources** (chemical heat pads, warm water bottles, wrapped) to the chest,
   axillae and back — not directly against skin, to avoid burns.

## 4. Rewarming by stage
- **Mild, conscious, can swallow:** high-calorie food and warm sweet drinks; active
  movement once dry and insulated is acceptable. Monitor for 30 min before letting them
  continue.
- **Moderate/severe:** nothing by mouth if impaired; insulation plus active external
  heat to the trunk; no rubbing of limbs; evacuate. Monitor airway and breathing
  carefully.

## 5. Cardiac arrest in hypothermia (summary — follow current WMS/ERC)
- Check breathing and pulse for up to **1 minute** — bradycardia and slow shallow
  breathing are easy to miss.
- Start CPR if no signs of life, unless obviously lethal injury, chest wall
  incompressible, or the airway packed with snow with prolonged burial (avalanche
  algorithms apply).
- **Delayed or intermittent CPR** is accepted when continuous CPR during a dangerous
  evacuation is impossible (e.g. <28 °C: 5 min CPR alternating with ≤5 min without;
  <20 °C: 5 min CPR with ≤10 min without — per ICAR/WMS; verify current version).
- Shocks and adrenaline are commonly withheld or spaced below about 30 °C per ERC —
  in the remote setting the priority is transport to ECLS.
- "Not dead until warm and dead" — survival with good neurology after prolonged arrest
  has been reported.

## 6. Evacuation decision
- **Evacuate:** any patient who is not shivering, has altered consciousness, cannot
  rewarm in the field, or has associated trauma/illness. Request helicopter or rescue
  team with clear grid reference and stage information.
- **Destination:** suspected severe hypothermia or arrest → **ECLS-capable centre** even
  if farther, where feasible.
- Mild cases that fully rewarm, can walk, eat and think clearly may self-evacuate with
  supervision.

## Gotchas
- **Afterdrop** — core temperature may keep falling after rescue; rough handling and
  limb warming worsen it.
- **Hypoglycaemia** coexists and mimics deeper hypothermia.
- **Heat packs on bare skin** cause burns in poorly perfused skin.
- **Alcohol** and **massage** are harmful folklore.
- Hypothermia complicates most trauma — "trauma triad" (hypothermia, acidosis,
  coagulopathy); insulate every injured patient.

## Verify
- [ ] Stage estimated from consciousness and shivering, not a peripheral thermometer.
- [ ] Patient moved horizontally and gently; insulated from the ground.
- [ ] Hypowrap complete with vapour barrier and wind layer; head covered.
- [ ] Heat sources wrapped and on the trunk only.
- [ ] Oral intake only if fully alert and able to swallow.
- [ ] Evacuation decision made and ECLS destination considered for severe cases.
