# Structured ambulance-to-hospital handover

**Goal:** transfer responsibility for a patient with no lost information — handovers are
a recognised source of error, and a structured format gets the critical facts heard the
first time by a busy receiving team.

## 1. Pre-alert (en route, by phone or radio)
- Use when the patient is time-critical or needs a pathway: trauma team, stroke, STEMI,
  sepsis, resus bay, paediatrics, obstetrics.
- Keep it short — about 30–60 s — in a fixed format. **ATMIST** is common for trauma
  (also used for medical with I = illness):
  - **A**ge and sex
  - **T**ime of incident / onset
  - **M**echanism (or Medical complaint)
  - **I**njuries found (or Illness/key findings)
  - **S**igns — vitals: RR, SpO2, HR, BP, GCS, temperature, glucose, and trend
  - **T**reatment given, plus **ETA**
- Include call-sign and any special needs (bariatric, infectious, security, family).

## 2. The hands-off verbal handover (at the bedside)
- **Before moving the patient** (unless unsafe), ask for **one moment of silence**:
  "Can I have everyone's attention for a 30-second handover?" The team listens; nobody
  touches the patient except for immediate life threats.
- Deliver in the same format (ATMIST, or **SBAR/ISBAR** for medical: Identify,
  Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation; or **IMIST-AMBO** in Australia:
  Identification, Mechanism/Medical complaint, Injuries/Information, Signs, Treatment,
  Allergies, Medications, Background, Other).
- Lead with the **single most important point** ("This is a time-critical head injury
  with falling GCS").
- State **trend**, not just last numbers ("GCS 14 at scene, now 11").
- Finish with **outstanding concerns** and invite questions.

## 3. Physical transfer
- Agree who leads the move; count ("ready, brace, slide" or local wording) with spinal
  precautions if indicated.
- Hand over lines, drains, tourniquet times, splints, pelvic binder, airway device
  position and ETCO2.
- Transfer monitoring without gaps for unstable patients.

## 4. Written handover and documentation
- Complete the **patient care record / ePCR** promptly; leave a copy or ensure
  electronic transfer before leaving.
- Record times: incident, contact, key interventions, drug doses with times, handover
  time and the name/role of the receiving clinician.
- Hand over property, medications brought from home, advance directives, and next-of-kin
  details.

## 5. Interfacility transfers (variant)
- Before leaving the sending unit: confirm clinician-to-clinician acceptance, receiving
  bed, documents, imaging, infusions with rates, ventilator settings, escorting
  clinician competence, and contingency plan for deterioration en route.

## 6. Hospital offload delays
If the patient waits on the ambulance stretcher, responsibility arrangements vary by
system; follow local escalation policy, keep reassessing and recording observations, and
escalate deterioration immediately.

## Gotchas
- **Talking while the team is moving the patient** — nobody hears; get the hands-off
  pause.
- **Burying the critical fact** at the end of a chronological story.
- **Omitting treatment times** (last analgesic, tourniquet applied, antibiotics) leads
  to duplicate or missed doses.
- **Different formats** for pre-alert and verbal confuse receivers — use the same one.
- Patient's own concerns and safeguarding information are often lost; include them.
- Avoid jargon and unexplained abbreviations across professional groups.

## Verify
- [ ] Pre-alert given in structured format with ETA for time-critical patients.
- [ ] Hands-off pause obtained; verbal handover under about 60 s for critical cases.
- [ ] Most important finding stated first; trends reported.
- [ ] Treatments with times, allergies and medications included.
- [ ] Receiving clinician named; handover time documented.
- [ ] ePCR transferred and property/documents handed over.
