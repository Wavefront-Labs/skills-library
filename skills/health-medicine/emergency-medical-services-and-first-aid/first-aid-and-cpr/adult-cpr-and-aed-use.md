# Adult CPR and AED use (lay and workplace rescuer)

**Goal:** turn the minutes before professional help arrives into survival — early
recognition, early call, high-quality compressions and a shock within minutes are the
links that matter most. Every minute without CPR and defibrillation in VF arrest
substantially lowers survival.

## 1. Check danger and responsiveness
- **Danger:** traffic, electricity, fire, gas. Do not become a second casualty.
- **Response:** shout and tap the shoulders firmly. No response → proceed.
- **Open the airway:** head tilt, chin lift.
- **Breathing:** look, listen, feel for no more than **10 seconds**. Not breathing, or
  breathing **abnormally** (agonal gasps — slow, noisy, irregular) = cardiac arrest.
  Brief seizure-like jerking at collapse can also be arrest.

## 2. Call and get the AED
- Call the emergency number (112/999/911 depending on country); use speakerphone so
  the dispatcher can coach you while you work.
- Send a bystander for the nearest AED. If alone with a phone, call first, then start
  CPR; do not leave the casualty to look for an AED unless one is very close.

## 3. Chest compressions
- Casualty on their back on a firm surface; kneel beside the chest.
- Heel of one hand on the **centre of the chest (lower half of the sternum)**, other hand
  on top, fingers interlocked, arms straight, shoulders over hands.
- **Depth 5–6 cm** (about 2 inches, not more than 2.4 inches per AHA); **rate
  100–120/min**; allow **full recoil**; keep interruptions as short as possible.
- **Trained and willing:** 30 compressions to **2 rescue breaths**, each breath about
  1 second, just enough to make the chest rise. Use a pocket mask or face shield if
  available.
- **Untrained or unwilling to breathe:** continuous compression-only (hands-only) CPR.
- Swap compressors about every **2 minutes** (at AED analysis) — quality falls before
  the rescuer notices fatigue.

## 4. Using the AED
1. Switch it on and follow the voice prompts.
2. Bare the chest; dry it; shave or rip away heavy hair if pads won't stick.
3. **Pad positions:** one below the right collarbone, one on the left side below the
   armpit (mid-axillary line, V6 position). Front-back placement is acceptable and is
   preferred on some devices for small children.
4. Keep compressing while pads go on if a second rescuer is present.
5. **"Analysing — do not touch":** hands off, everyone clear.
6. **Shock advised:** say "Stand clear!", check visually, press shock, then **resume
   compressions immediately** for 2 minutes — do not check for a pulse.
7. **No shock advised:** resume CPR immediately; the AED will reanalyse at 2 minutes.

## 5. Continue until
- Professional help takes over; the person shows signs of life (purposeful movement,
  eyes opening, normal breathing); or you are physically unable to continue.
- If breathing normally returns: place in the **recovery position**, leave the AED pads
  on, and keep reassessing breathing — re-arrest is common.

## Special situations (summary — check your course material)
- **Drowning and children:** start with **5 rescue breaths** before compressions in
  most European-style curricula (ERC/RCUK); AHA sequence differs slightly.
- **Pregnancy (visibly pregnant):** manual left uterine displacement if a second person
  is available.
- **Implanted pacemaker/ICD:** place pads at least ~8 cm away from the device lump.
- **Water/metal surfaces:** move out of standing water; a wet chest must be dried.
- **Suspected opioid overdose:** give naloxone if trained and available, but do not
  delay CPR.

## Gotchas
- **Agonal breathing mistaken for life** is the commonest reason CPR is never started.
- **Shallow compressions** and leaning on the chest (incomplete recoil) are the
  commonest quality failures — push hard, let go fully.
- **Long hands-off pauses** before and after shocks reduce shock success; aim for under
  10 seconds.
- Fear of hurting the casualty: rib fractures happen and are acceptable; no CPR is
  fatal.
- Medication patches on the pad site: remove and wipe the skin.
- Guideline specifics (rescue breath sequence, child pad sizes) differ between AHA,
  ERC and other councils; follow your certifying body.

## Verify
- [ ] Arrest recognised within 10 s, including abnormal/agonal breathing.
- [ ] Emergency call made early, on speaker, and an AED sent for.
- [ ] Compressions at 100–120/min and 5–6 cm depth with full recoil.
- [ ] AED pads correctly placed on a dry bare chest; nobody touching during analysis
      and shock.
- [ ] Compressions resumed immediately after each shock or no-shock decision.
- [ ] Rescuers rotated about every 2 minutes.
