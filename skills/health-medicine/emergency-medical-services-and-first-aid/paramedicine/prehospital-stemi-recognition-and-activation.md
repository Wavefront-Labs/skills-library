# Prehospital STEMI recognition and PCI activation

**Goal:** identify STEMI (and STEMI-equivalents) on a field 12-lead early enough to
bypass the local emergency department and activate the cath lab — the prehospital ECG
is the single biggest lever on reperfusion time.

## 1. When to acquire
- Any chest pain or discomfort, and atypical presentations: dyspnoea, epigastric pain,
  syncope, unexplained diaphoresis, especially in older, diabetic and female patients.
- Target: **12-lead within 10 minutes of patient contact** (common benchmark — check
  local KPI). Repeat every 15–30 min or with symptom change; an initially normal ECG
  does not exclude evolving MI.

## 2. Acquisition quality
- Limb electrodes on limbs (or proximal torso, consistently); precordial V1–V2 at 4th
  intercostal space either side of the sternum, V4 5th ICS mid-clavicular, V3 between,
  V5–V6 along the same horizontal plane.
- Patient still, supine or semi-recumbent; filter settings per manufacturer (monitoring
  filters distort ST segments — use diagnostic mode).
- Misplaced V1–V2 (too high) is a common cause of false patterns.

## 3. ST-elevation criteria (Fourth Universal Definition)
New ST elevation at the J point in **two contiguous leads**:
- **≥1 mm** in all leads other than V2–V3.
- **V2–V3:** ≥2 mm in men ≥40 years, ≥2.5 mm in men <40, ≥1.5 mm in women.
- Look for **reciprocal ST depression** — it strongly supports true STEMI over mimics.
- Territories: inferior (II, III, aVF), anterior (V1–V4), lateral (I, aVL, V5–V6).

## 4. Extra leads and equivalents
- **Inferior STEMI:** record **V4R** — ST elevation ≥0.5 mm (≥1 mm in men <30 per UDMI) suggests right-ventricular
  involvement (preload-dependent; caution with nitrates).
- **Posterior MI:** ST depression V1–V3 with tall R waves → record V7–V9 (≥0.5 mm
  elevation supportive).
- **LBBB or paced rhythm:** use **(modified) Sgarbossa** criteria (concordant ST
  elevation ≥1 mm; concordant ST depression V1–V3; excessively discordant ST elevation
  in Smith-modified version).
- **De Winter T waves**, **Wellens** pattern (critical LAD stenosis, often pain-free),
  and **aVR elevation with diffuse ST depression** — handle per local occlusion-MI
  pathway; criteria for activation on these vary (check local).

## 5. Mimics (do not ignore, do not over-call)
Benign early repolarisation, pericarditis (diffuse concave elevation, PR depression,
no reciprocal change except aVR), LV hypertrophy, LV aneurysm, Brugada, hyperkalaemia,
Takotsubo. When uncertain, **transmit the ECG** to the receiving cardiologist.

## 6. Treatment and activation (examples — verify against local formulary/guideline)
- **Aspirin** 300 mg chewed (UK) or 162–325 mg (US) unless contraindicated.
- **GTN/nitroglycerin** sublingual for pain if SBP adequate (commonly >90–100 mmHg),
  no RV infarct, no recent phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitor.
- Oxygen only if SpO2 below target (commonly <90–94%) — routine oxygen offers no
  benefit.
- Analgesia per protocol; defib pads on (VF risk is highest early).
- **Activate PPCI pathway** with a direct call; transport straight to a PCI-capable
  centre if within the system time window (commonly first medical contact to device
  ≤90–120 min). Where that is not achievable, some systems give **prehospital
  thrombolysis** with a checklist.
- Pre-alert, transmit ECG, document times (symptom onset, FMC, ECG, activation).

## Gotchas
- **Single-ECG reliance:** STEMI evolves — repeat.
- **Computer interpretation** misses inferior/posterior STEMI and over-calls
  repolarisation; read it yourself.
- **Nitrates in RV infarct** can cause profound hypotension.
- **Lead misplacement** and **monitoring filter** create artefactual ST changes.
- Cardiac arrest survivors with ROSC need a post-ROSC ECG; activation criteria differ.

## Verify
- [ ] 12-lead within ~10 min of contact, in diagnostic mode, leads correctly placed.
- [ ] Criteria checked lead-by-lead with reciprocal changes noted.
- [ ] Right-sided/posterior leads recorded when indicated.
- [ ] Aspirin given or contraindication documented; nitrates screened for RV infarct,
      hypotension and PDE-5 use.
- [ ] Cath lab/PPCI centre activated and ECG transmitted; key times recorded.
