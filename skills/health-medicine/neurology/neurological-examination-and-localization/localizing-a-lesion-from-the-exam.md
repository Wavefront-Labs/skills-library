# Localizing a lesion from the neurological exam

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** turn history and signs into a single anatomical answer — *where* is the
lesion — before asking *what* it is (vascular, inflammatory, neoplastic, degenerative…).
Localisation narrows the differential and decides which imaging or test is useful.

## 1. Frame the question from the history
- **Tempo:** seconds–minutes (vascular, seizure), hours–days (infection, inflammation),
  weeks–months (tumour, degeneration). Tempo predicts *what*; signs predict *where*.
- **Pattern:** focal vs diffuse, one lesion vs multifocal vs a system (e.g. all
  lower motor neurons, all large sensory fibres).
- Hypothesise a level before you touch the patient: cortex, subcortex, brainstem,
  cord, root, plexus, nerve, junction, muscle.

## 2. The exam, in a fixed order
1. **Mental status:** alertness, orientation, attention (months backwards), language
   (fluency, comprehension, repetition, naming), neglect. Aphasia or neglect = cortex.
2. **Cranial nerves:** pupils and fields (confrontation by quadrant), eye movements
   (pursuit and saccades, look for nystagmus and diplopia direction), facial
   sensation, facial power (forehead spared = UMN face; forehead involved = LMN/CN VII),
   hearing, palate, tongue, SCM/trapezius.
3. **Motor:** inspection (wasting, fasciculations), tone (catch, spasticity, rigidity),
   pronator drift, power graded MRC 0–5 by named muscle (shoulder abduction, elbow
   flexion/extension, wrist and finger extension, finger abduction, hip flexion, knee
   flexion/extension, ankle dorsi/plantar flexion).
4. **Reflexes:** biceps C5–6, brachioradialis C6, triceps C7, knee L3–4, ankle S1;
   plantar response; Hoffmann; clonus.
5. **Sensation:** pin (spinothalamic), vibration and joint position (dorsal columns),
   compare side to side and distal to proximal; hunt for a level on the trunk.
6. **Coordination:** finger-nose, heel-shin, rapid alternating movements.
7. **Gait:** normal, tandem, heels/toes, Romberg (positive = proprioceptive loss, not cerebellar).

## 3. Localisation rules
| Finding | Points to |
|---|---|
| Weakness + spasticity, brisk reflexes, upgoing plantar, pyramidal pattern (extensors weak in arm, flexors in leg) | UMN: cortex to cord |
| Wasting, fasciculations, hypotonia, reduced reflexes | LMN: anterior horn, root, nerve |
| Fatigable weakness, ptosis/diplopia, normal sensation and reflexes | Neuromuscular junction |
| Proximal symmetric weakness, normal sensation | Muscle |
| Cranial nerve on one side + limb signs on the other ("crossed") | Brainstem, at the level of that nerve |
| Sensory level on trunk, bilateral leg signs, bladder involvement | Spinal cord |
| Face + arm > leg, aphasia or neglect | Cortex (MCA territory) |
| Face, arm and leg equally, no cortical signs | Internal capsule / pons (lacunar) |
| Dermatomal pain/sensory loss + one reflex lost | Root |
| Mixed motor/sensory in one limb, not one root or nerve | Plexus |
| Stocking-glove, distal symmetric | Length-dependent polyneuropathy |

Single-lesion first ("Occam"); invoke multifocal disease only when one lesion can't
explain everything.

## Gotchas
- **Acute UMN lesions** (early stroke, spinal shock) can be flaccid and areflexic;
  spasticity and hyperreflexia appear over days to weeks.
- **Functional overlay** does not exclude organic disease — look for positive signs
  (Hoover's sign, give-way weakness) but still examine fully.
- A **"normal" cranial nerve exam** done from across the room misses subtle INO,
  field cuts and facial asymmetry; test each eye and quadrant.
- Neck lesions can give leg-only signs; a "lumbar" problem with brisk knee jerks needs
  cervical/thoracic imaging, not lumbar.
- Document what you found, not your interpretation only ("MRC 4/5 R hip flexion,
  R plantar extensor"), so trend comparisons are possible.

Uncertainty note: the reflex root values above are the conventional teaching
assignments; individual variation and overlap exist.

## Verify
- [ ] You can state the level (e.g. "left pons" or "right C6 root") in one phrase.
- [ ] Every abnormal sign is explained by that level, or you've named why not.
- [ ] Tempo has been used to rank the cause (*what*) separately from site (*where*).
- [ ] Power is recorded by named muscle and MRC grade; reflexes and plantars recorded.
- [ ] The chosen investigation images the localised level, not just the symptomatic region.
