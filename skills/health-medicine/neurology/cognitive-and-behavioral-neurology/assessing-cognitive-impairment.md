# Assessing cognitive impairment

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** decide whether the patient has delirium, dementia, mild cognitive impairment,
or something else (depression, sensory loss, medication effect) — and if dementia, which
syndrome — using history, a structured test and a short targeted workup.

## 1. Delirium first
Delirium is acute, fluctuating, and centred on **inattention**; it is a medical
emergency and can sit on top of dementia.
- Screen with the **4AT** (alertness, AMT4, months backwards, acute change) or **CAM**
  (acute onset/fluctuation + inattention + either disorganised thinking or altered
  consciousness).
- If positive: find the cause (infection, drugs — anticholinergics, opioids,
  benzodiazepines — retention, constipation, hypoxia, metabolic, pain, withdrawal) and
  defer dementia diagnosis until it resolves.

## 2. History — the collateral matters most
- Interview someone who knows the patient well, **separately**. Patients with
  Alzheimer disease often under-report; worried-well patients over-report.
- Ask: first symptom, tempo (insidious vs stepwise vs rapid), function (finances,
  medications, driving, cooking), personality/behaviour change, hallucinations,
  sleep (REM behaviour disorder), falls, continence.
- **Rapidly progressive** (weeks–months) = different workup: CJD, autoimmune
  encephalitis, tumour — refer urgently.

## 3. Structured testing
- **MoCA** (30 points; add 1 if ≤12 years education; <26 commonly abnormal) is
  more sensitive to MCI and executive dysfunction than the MMSE.
- **ACE-III** (100 points) gives domain sub-scores useful for subtyping.
- Scores are influenced by education, language, culture, hearing and vision — record
  these. Never diagnose from a score alone; it's one data point with function.

## 4. Reversible / contributing causes
Bloods: FBC, electrolytes incl. calcium, renal/liver, thyroid, B12/folate, glucose;
syphilis and HIV where risk. **Structural imaging** (CT or MRI) to exclude tumour,
subdural haematoma, normal pressure hydrocephalus (gait + incontinence + cognition),
and assess vascular burden. Review drugs for anticholinergic load. Screen for depression and sleep apnoea.

## 5. Syndrome by early profile
| Early feature | Suggests |
|---|---|
| Episodic memory loss (repeats, misplaces, doesn't recall with cues) | Alzheimer disease |
| Fluctuations, visual hallucinations, RBD, parkinsonism | Dementia with Lewy bodies |
| Disinhibition, apathy, loss of empathy, food changes; memory relatively spared | Behavioural-variant FTD |
| Progressive word-finding/grammar or word-meaning loss | Primary progressive aphasia |
| Stepwise decline, executive slowing, vascular risk, imaging burden | Vascular |

## Gotchas
- In suspected DLB, avoid typical antipsychotics — severe sensitivity reactions.
- MCI: cognitive decline with **preserved independence**; counsel and follow up rather than label as dementia.
- Capacity is **decision-specific and time-specific**; a dementia diagnosis doesn't
  remove capacity for every decision.
- Raise driving, safety, advance care planning and carer support early.

Uncertainty note: cut-offs are conventional screening thresholds; validation varies by
population and language.

## Verify
- [ ] Delirium screen documented before attributing to dementia.
- [ ] Collateral history obtained.
- [ ] Test score recorded with education, language and sensory caveats.
- [ ] Reversible-cause bloods and imaging requested.
- [ ] Syndrome named or referral made; capacity and safety issues addressed.
