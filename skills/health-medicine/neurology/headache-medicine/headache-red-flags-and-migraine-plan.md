# Headache red flags and a migraine plan

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** separate the rare dangerous secondary headache from the common primary one,
then give a migraine patient a plan that actually works: correct acute use, a clear
preventive threshold, and protection against medication overuse.

## 1. Red-flag screen (SNOOP-style)
| Flag | Concern |
|---|---|
| **S**ystemic: fever, weight loss, cancer, immunosuppression | Infection, metastasis |
| **N**eurological deficit, confusion, papilloedema, seizure | Mass, stroke, IIH, encephalitis |
| **O**nset sudden — peak in <1 minute (thunderclap) | SAH, RCVS, dissection, CVST |
| **O**lder: new headache >50 | Giant cell arteritis (ESR/CRP, jaw claudication), mass |
| **P**attern change, positional, Valsalva-triggered, waking from sleep | Raised/low ICP, mass |
| Pregnancy/postpartum | CVST, preeclampsia, pituitary apoplexy |

Thunderclap: CT within 6 h of onset is highly sensitive for SAH; beyond that, or if
clinical suspicion stays high, LP for xanthochromia or CTA per local pathway.

## 2. Diagnose migraine positively
ICHD-3 migraine without aura: ≥5 attacks, 4–72 h, with 2 of (unilateral, pulsating,
moderate–severe, worse with routine activity) and 1 of (nausea/vomiting, or
photophobia **and** phonophobia). A bilateral, pressing headache can still be migraine
if other features fit. Aura: reversible visual/sensory/speech symptoms spreading over
≥5 min, each lasting 5–60 min.

## 3. Acute treatment plan
- Treat **early**, while pain is mild.
- Step or stratify: NSAID (e.g. ibuprofen 400–600 mg, naproxen 500 mg) ± antiemetic;
  **triptan** if NSAIDs fail; triptan + NSAID combination for stubborn attacks.
- Triptan failure on one doesn't predict failure on another — try 2–3 before
  abandoning the class. Contraindicated in coronary, cerebrovascular or uncontrolled
  hypertensive disease. Gepants or lasmiditan are alternatives where triptans are unsuitable.
- Avoid opioids and butalbital: poor efficacy and high overuse risk.

## 4. Prevention threshold
Offer prevention if ≥4 headache days/month with disability, attacks poorly
responsive to acute treatment, or acute-medication use near overuse limits.
- First-line oral options (choose by comorbidity): propranolol, amitriptyline,
  topiramate (teratogenic — contraception counselling; avoid in pregnancy potential
  where possible), candesartan.
- Trial each at target dose for **8–12 weeks** with a diary before judging.
- CGRP monoclonal antibodies / gepants or onabotulinumtoxinA (chronic migraine) after
  oral failures, subject to local access criteria.

## 5. Medication-overuse headache
Suspect if headache ≥15 days/month with simple analgesics on ≥15 days/month, or
triptans/combination analgesics/opioids on ≥10 days/month for >3 months. Educate,
start prevention, and withdraw the overused drug.

## Gotchas
- "Sinus headache" and "tension headache" labels hide a lot of migraine.
- Oral contraceptives with oestrogen: avoid in migraine **with aura**
  (stroke risk) — check current UKMEC/US MEC.
- A headache diary (days, severity, meds taken) is the single best management tool.

Uncertainty note: thresholds summarised from ICHD-3 and common national guidance; drug
choices and access criteria vary by country.
