# Managing status epilepticus

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** stop the seizure fast with a time-staged protocol and find the cause, because
the longer convulsive status runs, the more drug-resistant it becomes.

## Definition that drives action
- Treat as status when a convulsive seizure lasts **5 minutes or more** (the definition used
  in the American Epilepsy Society guideline), without recovery of consciousness.
- Start a clock at onset and record every drug and time.

## Stage 0: stabilise
- Airway positioning, oxygen, suction; don't force anything into the mouth.
- Check capillary glucose and treat if low.
- IV/IO access and bloods (electrolytes, renal and liver function, FBC, antiseizure drug
  levels if on them, toxicology, pregnancy test where relevant).

## Stage 1: benzodiazepine, adequate dose
- IV lorazepam 0.1 mg/kg (maximum 4 mg per dose) is an established first-line option
  (AES guideline, Level A; IV diazepam is equivalent), **or**
- IM midazolam 10 mg (adults and children over 40 kg; 5 mg for 13-40 kg) when no IV,
  **or** rectal diazepam, intranasal or buccal midazolam where IV access is not available
  (all "probably effective", Level B).
- The AES-cited trials allowed a repeat dose if seizures continued after about 5 minutes.
  Under-dosing is a recognised failure; confirm the dose against your local protocol.

## Stage 2: second-line loading
The ESETT trial (adults and children, benzodiazepine-refractory) gave one of these over
10 minutes and found them broadly similar in efficacy:
- **Levetiracetam** 60 mg/kg (maximum 4500 mg)
- **Fosphenytoin** 20 mg PE/kg (maximum 1500 mg PE); watch blood pressure and rhythm
- **Valproate** 40 mg/kg (maximum 3000 mg); check pregnancy potential and liver disease
  before use

## Stage 3: refractory status
- Refractory-status treatment is outside the AES guideline: call ICU and anaesthesia early
  and follow your local neurocritical care protocol, with continuous EEG.

## Find the cause (in parallel)
Missed doses or low drug levels, alcohol withdrawal, hypoglycaemia, electrolyte
disturbance, CNS infection, stroke, tumour, trauma, toxins, autoimmune encephalitis,
eclampsia.

## Gotchas
- **Non-convulsive status**: patient not waking after the convulsions stop; get an EEG.
- Functional (dissociative) seizures can mimic status; treat if unsure and seek specialist
  advice before escalating to intubation.
- Paediatric dosing and local formularies differ; confirm before use.

## Verify
- [ ] Onset time and all drug times documented.
- [ ] Glucose checked within the first minutes.
- [ ] Benzodiazepine given at full weight-based dose.
- [ ] Second-line agent loaded if seizures persist.
- [ ] EEG arranged if consciousness doesn't recover.
- [ ] Cause actively investigated and treated.
