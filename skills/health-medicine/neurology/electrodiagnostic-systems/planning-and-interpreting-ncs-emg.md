# Planning and interpreting NCS/EMG

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** design an electrodiagnostic study as an extension of the neurological
exam — answering a specific localisation question — and interpret it without falling
for technical artifacts.

## 1. Plan from the question
Write the question before starting: "median neuropathy at wrist vs C6/C7
radiculopathy?", "length-dependent axonal vs demyelinating polyneuropathy?",
"myopathy vs NMJ?". Then pick nerves and muscles that discriminate the options.
- Polyneuropathy: at minimum, sural sensory, peroneal and tibial motor (with
  F-waves) in one leg, plus one upper limb; compare sides if asymmetric.
- Carpal tunnel: median vs ulnar sensory and motor, plus a sensitive comparison
  (e.g. median-ulnar palmar or ring-finger comparison).
- Radiculopathy: NCS to exclude mimic neuropathy, then needle EMG of muscles from
  the suspected myotome via **two different nerves**, plus paraspinals.

## 2. Technique that changes results
- **Limb temperature:** keep hands ≥32 °C, feet ≥30 °C (common targets). Cold slows
  velocity and **raises** amplitudes — falsely "demyelinating".
- Supramaximal stimulation (increase ~20% beyond the level giving a maximal response).
- Measure distances carefully with a tape; errors over short segments distort velocity.
- Watch for stimulus spread to neighbouring nerves and anomalous innervation
  (Martin-Gruber anastomosis can mimic conduction block in the forearm).

## 3. Reading NCS
| Measure | Mainly reflects |
|---|---|
| CMAP / SNAP **amplitude** | Number of conducting axons (axonal loss lowers it) |
| Distal latency, **conduction velocity** | Myelin (demyelination slows it) |
| **Conduction block** (proximal CMAP amplitude drop, often >50%, without dispersion) | Focal demyelination |
| F-wave latency | Proximal segments, roots |

- **Axonal:** low amplitudes, velocity normal or mildly slow (not below ~75% of lower limit of normal).
- **Demyelinating:** velocities <~70–75% of lower limit, distal latencies >~130% of
  upper limit, conduction block, temporal dispersion, prolonged/absent F-waves.
- **Preganglionic (root) lesion:** SNAPs preserved despite sensory symptoms (the
  dorsal root ganglion is outside the lesion). Postganglionic (plexus/nerve): SNAPs low.

## 4. Needle EMG
- At rest: fibrillations/positive sharp waves (active denervation, ~2–3 weeks after
  injury; earlier in paraspinals), fasciculations, myotonic discharges.
- On activation: large, long, polyphasic units with reduced recruitment = chronic
  neurogenic; small, short, polyphasic units with **early** recruitment = myopathic.
- NMJ: repetitive nerve stimulation (decrement >10% at 3 Hz) and single-fibre EMG.

## 5. Report
Summarise the data table, then an impression that names the lesion's site, type
(axonal/demyelinating), chronicity (active/chronic), severity, and answers the question.

## Gotchas
- Studies done in the first week after acute injury may look normal distal to a lesion
  (Wallerian degeneration not yet complete).
- Sural SNAPs can be absent in healthy older adults; interpret with age norms.
- Anticoagulation: needle EMG is generally feasible but avoid deep muscles where
  bleeding can't be compressed; follow local policy.
- Normative values are lab- and equipment-specific — use your lab's reference tables.

Uncertainty note: demyelination cut-offs vary between published criteria (e.g. EFNS/PNS
for CIDP); numbers here are approximations.

## Verify
- [ ] Question written before the study; nerves and muscles chosen to answer it.
- [ ] Limb temperature documented and within range.
- [ ] Amplitude, latency, velocity and F-waves compared to lab norms.
- [ ] Root vs nerve logic applied (SNAP preservation).
- [ ] Impression states site, type, chronicity and severity.
