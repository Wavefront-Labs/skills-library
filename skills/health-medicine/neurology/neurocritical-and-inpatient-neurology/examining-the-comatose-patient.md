# Examining the comatose patient

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** decide quickly whether coma is **structural** (needs imaging and possibly
surgery) or **metabolic/toxic/diffuse** (needs labs and antidotes), while treating the
reversible causes before you've finished thinking.

## 1. Stabilise and give the cheap fixes
- ABC; protect airway (GCS ≤8 is a common, not absolute, intubation trigger).
- **Capillary glucose** immediately. Thiamine before glucose in suspected alcohol use
  or malnutrition.
- Naloxone if opioid toxidrome (small pupils, low respiratory rate).
- Temperature, ECG, blood gas, electrolytes incl. sodium and calcium, renal/liver,
  ammonia, tox screen, blood cultures if febrile.
- Fever + coma: blood cultures and **empirical antimicrobials for meningitis/
  encephalitis (incl. aciclovir) now** — don't wait for CT or LP.

## 2. Score it
- **GCS** (E4 V5 M6): report components, not just the total ("E2 V1 M4").
- **FOUR score** (eye, motor, brainstem, respiration; 0–16) works in intubated
  patients and captures brainstem function.

## 3. The coma exam
1. **Observe:** posture, myoclonus (metabolic, post-anoxic), subtle twitching (non-
   convulsive status), breathing pattern.
2. **Pupils:** size, symmetry, light reaction.
   - Unilateral fixed dilated → CN III compression, uncal herniation until proven otherwise.
   - Pinpoint → pontine lesion or opioids.
   - Midposition fixed → midbrain.
   - **Reactive pupils with otherwise absent brainstem signs favour metabolic coma.**
3. **Eye movements:** resting gaze deviation (towards a hemispheric lesion, away from
   a pontine one, and away from an actively seizing hemisphere), oculocephalic (only if
   C-spine cleared) or cold caloric testing.
4. **Corneal, cough and gag reflexes.**
5. **Motor response to central pain:** localising, withdrawal, flexor (decorticate),
   extensor (decerebrate), none. Asymmetry suggests structural.

## 4. Structural vs metabolic
| Structural | Metabolic/toxic |
|---|---|
| Asymmetric signs, focal deficits | Symmetric findings |
| Pupils unequal/unreactive early | Pupils reactive until late |
| Progresses rostro-caudally | Myoclonus, asterixis, tremor |
Get urgent CT for anything structural-looking or unexplained.

## 5. Raised ICP / herniation — first-line measures
Signs: falling GCS, new unilateral pupil dilation, Cushing response (hypertension,
bradycardia, irregular breathing).
- Head of bed 30°, neck neutral, avoid tight ET-tube ties.
- Normocapnia; brief hyperventilation only as a bridge.
- Osmotherapy: **mannitol** ~0.25–1 g/kg or **hypertonic saline** per protocol;
  monitor sodium and osmolality.
- Treat fever, seizures and hyponatraemia; urgent neurosurgical review.

## Gotchas
- LP before imaging in a patient with focal signs, papilloedema, or reduced
  consciousness risks herniation — image first, but give antibiotics before either.
- Sedatives and neuromuscular blockers invalidate the exam; record what was given and when.
- Locked-in syndrome looks like coma — ask the patient to look up/blink.
- Prognosis after cardiac arrest needs a multimodal approach after ≥72 h, off sedation.

Uncertainty note: osmotherapy doses vary by protocol; confirm locally.

## Verify
- [ ] Glucose, thiamine/naloxone considerations documented.
- [ ] GCS components and pupils recorded with times.
- [ ] Brainstem reflexes and motor response tested.
- [ ] Structural vs metabolic call made, imaging done if structural.
- [ ] Antimicrobials given if infection possible.
