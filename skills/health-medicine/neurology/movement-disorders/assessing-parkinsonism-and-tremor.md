# Assessing parkinsonism and tremor

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** name the movement correctly first (phenomenology), then decide whether it is
Parkinson disease, essential tremor, drug-induced, or an atypical syndrome. Most
diagnostic errors in this field come from mislabelling the movement.

## 1. Name the movement
Watch the patient at rest, with arms outstretched, during finger-nose, writing and
walking. Video it (with consent) for later comparison.
- **Tremor:** rhythmic oscillation. Classify by *when* it occurs:
  - Rest (limb fully supported, disappears with movement, re-emerges after a delay
    with posture — "re-emergent") → suggests PD.
  - Postural/action, bilateral, often head or voice, family history, improves with
    alcohol → suggests essential tremor.
  - Intention (worsens approaching target) → cerebellar.
- **Bradykinesia:** not just slowness — **progressive decrement** in speed or
  amplitude with repetitive movement (finger taps, hand open-close, foot taps; 10–15
  repetitions). Slowness without decrement is not bradykinesia.
- **Rigidity:** velocity-independent resistance; activation manoeuvre (opening and
  closing the opposite hand) brings it out. Cogwheeling = rigidity plus tremor.

## 2. Parkinsonism → Parkinson disease?
Parkinsonism = bradykinesia plus rest tremor and/or rigidity (MDS criteria). For PD
look for supportive features: clear, sustained response to levodopa; asymmetric onset;
rest tremor; levodopa-induced dyskinesia; hyposmia; REM sleep behaviour disorder;
constipation predating motor signs.

## 3. Red flags for something else
| Feature | Think of |
|---|---|
| Early falls (within ~3 years), vertical gaze palsy (esp. downgaze), axial rigidity | PSP |
| Early severe autonomic failure (orthostasis, urinary), cerebellar signs, stridor | MSA |
| Apraxia, cortical sensory loss, alien limb, marked asymmetry | Corticobasal syndrome |
| Early hallucinations / fluctuating cognition before or within a year of parkinsonism | Dementia with Lewy bodies |
| Symmetric onset, on dopamine blockers (antipsychotics, metoclopramide, prochlorperazine) | Drug-induced |
| Lower-body predominance, broad-based gait, vascular risk | Vascular parkinsonism |
| Young onset (<40–50) | Wilson disease (check caeruloplasmin), genetic forms |

## 4. Levodopa trial
- Start low (e.g. levodopa/carbidopa 50/12.5 mg–100/25 mg three times daily) and
  titrate over weeks to a meaningful dose (~600 mg/day levodopa total) before calling
  it a non-response. Confirm local dosing references.
- Use an objective measure (MDS-UPDRS part III subset, timed finger taps, timed walk)
  before and after.
- Never stop levodopa abruptly — risk of parkinsonism-hyperpyrexia syndrome.

## Gotchas
- Essential tremor patients can develop mild rest tremor late; PD patients can have
  postural tremor. Use the whole picture, not one sign.
- DaT-SPECT distinguishes degenerative parkinsonism from ET or drug-induced, but not
  PD from MSA/PSP.
- Thyroid, caffeine, beta-agonists, valproate, lithium and anxiety enhance
  physiological tremor — check before diagnosing ET.
- Inpatient PD: give meds **on time**; withholding during "nil by mouth" causes rapid deterioration.

Uncertainty note: diagnostic criteria summarised from MDS clinical criteria; ages and
time cut-offs are conventional, not absolute.
