# Working up peripheral neuropathy

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** classify the neuropathy by pattern before ordering tests, so the workup is
targeted and the treatable or dangerous causes are not missed.

## 1. Answer four pattern questions
1. **Distribution:** length-dependent (stocking, then glove when leg symptoms reach
   ~knee level), non-length-dependent (proximal and distal, e.g. GBS/CIDP), or
   multifocal (individual named nerves — mononeuritis multiplex).
2. **Fibres:** motor, sensory (large fibre: vibration/proprioception, ataxia;
   small fibre: burning pain, pin/temperature loss, normal reflexes), autonomic, or mixed.
3. **Tempo:** acute (<4 weeks), subacute (4–8 weeks), chronic (>8 weeks).
4. **Symmetry:** symmetric vs asymmetric.

The common, low-urgency pattern is **chronic, symmetric, distal, sensory-predominant,
length-dependent**. Anything else raises the stakes.

## 2. Red flags — act the same day
- **Rapidly ascending weakness, areflexia** → suspect GBS: measure **FVC/NIF**
  serially (falling vital capacity, e.g. <20 mL/kg, is a common ICU trigger), check
  autonomic instability, admit. Treat with IVIG or plasma exchange; steroids don't help.
- **Painful asymmetric multifocal** deficits → vasculitis; urgent ESR/CRP, ANCA, nerve
  biopsy discussion.
- Prominent motor, proximal involvement, or progression over months → CIDP.
- Sensory ataxia with asymmetric arms/face involvement → sensory neuronopathy
  (paraneoplastic, Sjögren).

## 3. First-line blood panel (chronic length-dependent)
- **HbA1c** / glucose (oral glucose tolerance test if borderline — impaired glucose
  tolerance causes neuropathy too).
- **B12** (with methylmalonic acid if low-normal), folate.
- **Serum protein electrophoresis with immunofixation** — paraproteins are common
  and easily missed without immunofixation.
- Renal, liver and thyroid function, FBC.
- Consider: alcohol history, medications (chemotherapy, metronidazole, nitrofurantoin,
  amiodarone, isoniazid, excess pyridoxine), HIV, hepatitis C, copper.

These identify a cause in a large share of cases; a meaningful fraction stay idiopathic.

## 4. Nerve conduction studies and EMG
- Not required for classic mild diabetic neuropathy with an obvious cause.
- Order when: atypical pattern, motor-predominant, asymmetric, rapidly progressive,
  or to separate **axonal** (low amplitudes) from **demyelinating** (slow velocities,
  prolonged latencies, conduction block) — demyelinating disease opens treatable
  causes (CIDP, paraprotein-associated).
- NCS are **normal in pure small-fibre neuropathy**; use skin biopsy for
  intraepidermal nerve fibre density or autonomic testing.

## Gotchas
- Diabetes is common enough to coexist with a second cause — still check B12 (esp.
  on metformin) and SPEP.
- Early GBS can have normal NCS and normal CSF protein in the first week.
- Cord lesions (B12 subacute combined degeneration, cervical myelopathy) can mimic
  neuropathy — brisk knees with absent ankles or a sensory level means look at the cord.
- Foot care education is part of management, not an afterthought.

Uncertainty note: test panel reflects common neurology society recommendations; local
practice and yield estimates vary.

## Verify
- [ ] Distribution, fibre type, tempo and symmetry documented.
- [ ] Red flags excluded or acted on (FVC done if weakness is progressing).
- [ ] HbA1c, B12 and SPEP with immunofixation requested.
- [ ] Medication and alcohol history recorded.
- [ ] NCS ordered when pattern is atypical; small-fibre testing considered if NCS normal.
