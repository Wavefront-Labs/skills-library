# Running an acute stroke code

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** move a suspected acute ischaemic stroke from arrival to reperfusion decision
fast and safely, with every decision time-stamped. "Time is brain": reperfusion benefit
falls steeply with each minute of delay.

## 1. First 10 minutes (parallel, not sequential)
1. **Last known well (LKW):** the last time the patient was *seen* normal — not when
   symptoms were found. Wake-up stroke: LKW is bedtime unless imaging-selected.
2. **ABC, capillary glucose** (hypoglycaemia mimics stroke; treat and reassess; NICE
   NG128 aims to keep glucose between 4 and 11 mmol/L), IV access, bloods (do not wait
   for results except glucose, and INR if on warfarin or anticoagulant status unknown).
3. **NIHSS** by a certified examiner. Record the score and the items driving it.
4. **Weight** (estimated if needed) — thrombolytic dose is weight-based.
5. Straight to **non-contrast CT** plus **CT angiography** head and neck (perfusion
   if your pathway uses it for late windows). Don't hold CTA for creatinine in the
   acute setting in most pathways.

## 2. Thrombolysis decision
- Typical window: within **4.5 h** of LKW (later only with imaging selection per
  local protocol).
- Agents: alteplase or tenecteplase (NICE TA990) within a well-organised stroke service,
  once intracranial haemorrhage is excluded. **Take dose, exclusions and contraindications
  from your formulary, the product licence and current guideline.**
- Key exclusions to actively ask about: haemorrhage on CT, recent intracranial
  surgery or serious head trauma, recent major bleeding, anticoagulant use, glucose
  abnormality explaining symptoms, extensive established infarct (confirm the list locally).
- **BP:** NICE says consider lowering to 185/110 mmHg or lower in thrombolysis candidates.
  Post-lysis targets and antihypertensive choice per local protocol.
- Disabling deficit matters more than raw NIHSS — an isolated aphasia with NIHSS 2
  can be disabling.

## 3. Thrombectomy triage
- CTA shows **LVO** (ICA, M1, often proximal M2, basilar): call the
  neurointerventional team **immediately**, in parallel with lysis — don't wait to
  see if lysis "works".
- NICE NG128 offers thrombectomy to selected people last known well between 6 and 24
  hours before (including wake-up strokes), on imaging criteria; check the current guideline.
- Transfer patients: "drip and ship" — start lysis, then transfer without delay.

## 4. After the decision
- Neuro obs and NIHSS at the frequency your protocol sets; any deterioration,
  headache, vomiting or BP spike = stop infusion, urgent CT, coagulation and fibrinogen.
- Watch for **orolingual angioedema** (higher with ACE inhibitors).
- Antithrombotic timing after lysis per local protocol (repeat imaging to exclude bleed first).
- Swallow screen by a trained professional before any oral food, fluid or medication (NG128).

## Gotchas
- Mimics: seizure with Todd's paresis, hypoglycaemia, migraine aura, functional
  deficits, sepsis unmasking an old stroke. Lysing a mimic is low risk; delaying a
  true stroke is not — but document why you suspected stroke.
- Posterior circulation strokes score low on NIHSS (vertigo, ataxia, diplopia); do
  not let a low score talk you out of CTA.
- A fluctuating deficit can be a basilar occlusion or capsular warning syndrome — treat as urgent.
- Be cautious lowering BP aggressively in patients *not* being reperfused; follow your
  national guideline (NG128 has separate advice for systolic BP over 220 mmHg).

Verify doses and BP thresholds against your current national guideline and formulary.
