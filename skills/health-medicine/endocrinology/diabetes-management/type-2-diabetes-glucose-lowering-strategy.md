# Type 2 diabetes: glucose-lowering strategy and intensification

**Goal:** an individualised glycaemic target reached without hypoglycaemia, using agents
chosen first for cardiorenal and weight benefit, with timely (not inert) intensification.

## 1. Set the target
- Typical HbA1c target **<7% (53 mmol/mol)** for most non-pregnant adults (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
- Tighter (~6.5%, 48 mmol/mol) if achievable safely, e.g. short duration, no hypoglycaemia-prone drugs.
- Looser (~7.5–8%+, 58–64 mmol/mol) with limited life expectancy, frailty, recurrent
  hypoglycaemia, hypoglycaemia unawareness or major comorbidity (exact cut-offs vary by guideline (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)).
- Where CGM is used, add time in range targets (see
  health-medicine.endocrinology.diabetes-technology.cgm-ambulatory-glucose-profile-review).

## 2. Stratify before choosing drugs
Ask in this order:
1. **Established ASCVD or high CV risk?** → GLP-1 RA with proven CV benefit and/or SGLT2 inhibitor.
2. **Heart failure (HFrEF or HFpEF)?** → SGLT2 inhibitor.
3. **CKD (eGFR <60 or albuminuria, UACR ≥3 mg/mmol / ≥30 mg/g)?** → SGLT2 inhibitor
   (US labels: empagliflozin glycaemic use not recommended below eGFR 30 but data for heart failure/CKD use extend to eGFR 20; dapagliflozin glycaemic use not recommended below eGFR 45; check each current label) ± GLP-1 RA; ACEi/ARB and
   consider finerenone per nephrology/KDIGO.
4. **Weight a priority?** → GLP-1 RA or dual GIP/GLP-1 RA (tirzepatide) give the largest losses.
5. **Cost/access limits?** → metformin, sulfonylurea, pioglitazone, human insulin remain valid.
Cardiorenal agents are indicated for organ protection **independent of HbA1c and of metformin use**.

## 3. Foundation: metformin (where no contraindication)
- Start low with food (US label: 500 mg twice daily or 850 mg once daily with meals, increase 500 mg weekly or 850 mg every 2 weeks, maximum 2550 mg/day, doses above 2000 mg may be better tolerated three times daily); extended release helps GI intolerance. Verify against local formulary/guideline.
- Renal (US label): do not use if eGFR is below 30; initiation not recommended at eGFR 30 to 45; dose reduction at 30-45 is guideline practice, not label text I re-read.
- Label: measure haematological parameters annually and vitamin B12 every 2 to 3 years.

## 4. Intensify every ~3 months if off target
- Add rather than wait; consider **early combination** if HbA1c is ≥1.5% above target (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
- Common second agents: GLP-1 RA, SGLT2i, DPP-4i, pioglitazone, sulfonylurea. Do **not**
  combine a DPP-4 inhibitor with a GLP-1 RA (no added benefit (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)).
- Prefer a GLP-1 RA before insulin in most people needing injectable therapy.

## 5. Basal insulin start and titration
- **Start insulin early** if catabolic features (weight loss, ketosis), HbA1c >10% (86 mmol/mol)
  or glucose ≥300 mg/dL (16.7 mmol/L), or symptomatic hyperglycaemia (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
- Start basal (glargine, degludec, detemir or NPH at bedtime) at **10 units/day or
  0.1–0.2 units/kg/day** (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked). Verify against local formulary/guideline.
- Titrate by ~2 units every 3 days to a fasting target of about **80–130 mg/dL (4.4–7.2 mmol/L)**;
  reduce 10–20% for any unexplained hypoglycaemia (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
- **Overbasalisation** signals: basal >0.5 units/kg/day, bedtime-to-morning drop ≥50 mg/dL
  (~2.8 mmol/L), hypoglycaemia, high variability. Then add GLP-1 RA or prandial insulin
  (start 4 units or ~10% of basal with the largest meal) rather than pushing basal further (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
- Reduce or stop sulfonylurea when insulin is added or intensified.

## 6. Hypoglycaemia
- Level 1 <70 mg/dL (3.9 mmol/L); level 2 <54 mg/dL (3.0 mmol/L); level 3 = needs assistance (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
- Treat with ~15 g fast carbohydrate, recheck at 15 min, repeat (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked); glucagon available for
  anyone on insulin. Every level 2–3 episode should trigger a regimen review.

## Gotchas
- **Euglycaemic DKA** on SGLT2 inhibitors: glucose may be near normal. Teach sick-day rules
  (pause during acute illness, fasting, surgery; the lead time before planned surgery varies by label and local policy, check current label). The labels advise assessing for ketoacidosis regardless of blood glucose.
- GLP-1 RAs: hold/consider before procedures with aspiration risk per local anaesthesia policy;
  avoid with personal/family history of MTC or MEN2 (boxed warning on semaglutide labels: thyroid C-cell tumours in rodents); caution in prior pancreatitis.
- HbA1c misleads in anaemia, haemoglobinopathy, recent transfusion, pregnancy, advanced CKD — use CGM/fructosamine.
- Pioglitazone: fluid retention, avoid in heart failure.
- Therapeutic inertia: the commonest failure is not escalating for years.
- Always re-check diagnosis: lean, young, rapid insulin requirement or ketosis → test for
  autoimmune diabetes (GAD antibodies) or monogenic diabetes.

## Verify
- [ ] Individualised HbA1c (and TIR if CGM) target is documented with its rationale.
- [ ] ASCVD, heart failure and CKD status (eGFR + UACR) checked and reflected in agent choice.
- [ ] Metformin and SGLT2i doses match current eGFR.
- [ ] Insulin titration rule and hypoglycaemia plan (incl. glucagon) given in writing.
- [ ] Sulfonylurea reviewed when insulin started; sick-day rules explained.
- [ ] Review date set (~3 months) to avoid therapeutic inertia.
