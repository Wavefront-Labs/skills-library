# Male hypogonadism and testosterone therapy

**Goal:** treat men who have both symptoms and unequivocally low testosterone, identify
the cause (including pituitary disease), and avoid treating low numbers that reflect
illness, obesity or a bad sample.

## 1. Who to test
Specific symptoms/signs: reduced libido, fewer spontaneous erections, loss of body hair,
small or shrinking testes, gynaecomastia, low-trauma fracture, hot flushes, infertility.
Non-specific fatigue or low mood alone is a weak indication. Don't test during acute illness.

## 2. Confirm biochemically
- **Morning (≈07:00–11:00), fasting total testosterone**, repeated on a separate day.
- Lower limit of normal: ~264 ng/dL (9.2 nmol/L) in harmonised assays (Endocrine Society);
  AUA uses <300 ng/dL (10.4 nmol/L). Use the local laboratory's reference range.
- Measure **SHBG and calculate free testosterone** when total T is borderline or SHBG altered:
  low SHBG (obesity, insulin resistance, nephrotic syndrome, hypothyroidism) or high SHBG
  (ageing, HIV, liver disease, hyperthyroidism, anticonvulsants).

## 3. Classify and find the cause
- **LH and FSH**:
  - High = **primary** (testicular): Klinefelter (karyotype if small firm testes), prior
    orchitis, chemotherapy/radiotherapy, trauma, orchidectomy.
  - Low/inappropriately normal = **secondary** (hypothalamic–pituitary).
- For secondary: prolactin, ferritin/transferrin saturation (haemochromatosis), other
  pituitary axes (TSH/free T4, morning cortisol) and review of functional causes — obesity,
  opioids, glucocorticoids, anabolic steroid use, uncontrolled diabetes, severe illness.
- **Pituitary MRI** if total T is very low (<150 ng/dL / ~5.2 nmol/L), prolactin raised,
  other pituitary hormone deficits, or visual-field/headache symptoms.
- Functional causes: treat the cause first (weight loss, stop opioids) — T often recovers.

## 4. Before starting testosterone — contraindications/cautions
- **Desire for fertility in the near term** — exogenous testosterone suppresses spermatogenesis.
  Use gonadotropins (hCG ± FSH) in secondary hypogonadism, via a specialist.
- Breast or prostate cancer; palpable prostate nodule or PSA >4 ng/mL (>3 in high-risk men)
  without urology evaluation.
- Haematocrit >48% (>50% at altitude), untreated severe obstructive sleep apnoea,
  uncontrolled heart failure, MI or stroke within ~6 months, thrombophilia (thresholds per guideline).

## 5. Treat and monitor (verify against local formulary/guideline)
- Formulations: transdermal gel, IM testosterone enanthate/cypionate (shorter interval, peaks
  and troughs), long-acting IM undecanoate (e.g. every ~10–14 weeks after loading), others regionally.
- **Target mid-normal range** (~400–700 ng/dL, 14–24 nmol/L).
- Timing of levels: gel ~2–8 h post-application (after ≥1 week); enanthate/cypionate midway
  between injections; undecanoate just before the next injection.
- Check at **3–6 months, then annually**: testosterone, **haematocrit** (withhold/reduce if
  >54%), symptoms, PSA/DRE per local prostate monitoring policy, adherence.
- Re-evaluate benefit at 6–12 months; stop if no symptomatic improvement.

## Gotchas
- A single low result is not a diagnosis; ~30% normalise on repeat.
- Older men: modest age-related decline alone is not an indication. TRAVERSE (2023) showed
  CV non-inferiority in selected men but more AF, AKI and PE — counsel.
- Gel transfer to partners and children: cover site, wash hands.
- Anabolic steroid withdrawal produces prolonged secondary hypogonadism; manage specialist.
- Testosterone therapy masks the cause — do the pituitary workup first.

## Verify
- [ ] Two morning fasting total T values below range, with symptoms, documented.
- [ ] SHBG/free T used where relevant; LH/FSH classify primary vs secondary.
- [ ] Secondary causes (prolactin, iron, drugs, MRI when indicated) addressed.
- [ ] Fertility wishes asked and contraindications checked before prescribing.
- [ ] Monitoring schedule (T, haematocrit, PSA) booked; benefit review at 6–12 months.
