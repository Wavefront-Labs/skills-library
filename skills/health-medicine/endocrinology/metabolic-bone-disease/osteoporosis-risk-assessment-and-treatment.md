# Osteoporosis: risk assessment and treatment selection

**Goal:** identify who will fracture, treat the right people with the right-strength drug
in the right order, and avoid the avoidable harms (denosumab rebound, unchecked secondary causes).

## 1. Diagnose and stratify
- **DXA** (lumbar spine, total hip, femoral neck; forearm if spine/hip unusable):
  - **T-score ≤ −2.5** is the operational BMD definition of osteoporosis, but BMD is one risk factor among many: most people who fracture have a femoral neck T-score above −2.5.
  - For people not covered by the T-score definition (premenopausal women, younger men, children) use the Z-score and local guidance.
- A **hip or vertebral fragility fracture** establishes osteoporosis clinically regardless of T-score.
- **FRAX** (with femoral neck BMD where available) gives 10-year major osteoporotic and hip
  fracture probability. The UK (NOGG) uses age-dependent FRAX intervention thresholds; other
  countries use different thresholds (check local).
- **Vertebral fracture assessment** (VFA or lateral spine X-ray) in postmenopausal women and men ≥50 with
  ≥4 cm height loss, kyphosis, recent or long-term oral glucocorticoids or T-score ≤ −2.5 at spine or hip
  — silent fractures change risk class.
- **Very high risk** (consider anabolic first): in NOGG, a FRAX probability exceeding the intervention
  threshold by 60%; a recent vertebral fracture (within 2 years), two or more vertebral fractures,
  T-score ≤ −3.5 or high-dose glucocorticoids are also specialist-referral triggers. Definitions vary by guideline.

## 2. Exclude secondary causes before treating
Baseline: calcium (albumin-adjusted), phosphate, creatinine/eGFR, ALP, liver tests,
25-OH vitamin D, PTH, TSH, FBC; consider coeliac serology, serum protein electrophoresis/free
light chains (myeloma), testosterone and LH in men, 24-h urine calcium, and screen for
Cushing syndrome when clinically suspected. Review drugs: glucocorticoids, aromatase
inhibitors, androgen deprivation, excess thyroxine, some antiepileptics, PPIs.

## 3. Choose therapy
Optimise vitamin D (replete if deficient) and calcium intake before IV bisphosphonate or
denosumab (hypocalcaemia risk; it rises with renal impairment). Check doses against the BNF or local formulary.
- **High risk — antiresorptive first line**
  - Oral bisphosphonate (alendronate or risedronate, weekly forms available; take fasting, upright, with plain water).
  - Zoledronic acid IV once yearly (acute-phase reaction common after first dose).
  - Check renal function before bisphosphonates; follow the product information for the cut-off.
  - Denosumab SC every 6 months, but see Gotchas.
- **Very high risk — anabolic first**, then consolidate with an antiresorptive:
  - Teriparatide (daily SC, typically up to 24 months) or abaloparatide.
  - Romosozumab monthly for 12 months; cardiovascular events were more frequent than with alendronate in trials, so check the product information for contraindications.
- Sequencing matters: after teriparatide or romosozumab start alendronate, zoledronate or denosumab without delay; BMD gains
  are blunted when these anabolics follow denosumab or bisphosphonates.

## 4. Monitor and review
- Reassess fracture risk in treated people with FRAX and femoral neck BMD; use the same DXA machine where possible.
- **Bisphosphonate review (NOGG)**: plan at least 5 years oral or 3 years IV, then reassess. Continue longer
  (at least 10 years oral, 6 years IV) if age ≥70 at start, prior hip or vertebral fracture, oral prednisolone
  ≥7.5 mg/day or equivalent, or a fracture during the first years of treatment. After stopping, reassess FRAX at
  18 months (risedronate, ibandronate), 2 years (alendronate) or 3 years (zoledronate), sooner if a new fracture.

## Gotchas
- **Denosumab must not be stopped or delayed** without a follow-on antiresorptive
  (usually zoledronic acid timed to the off-set); rebound bone loss and multiple vertebral
  fractures can occur within months (NOGG: about 3-6 months after the last dose was due).
- Treat a new fragility fracture as a sentinel event — fracture liaison services exist for this reason.
- Osteonecrosis of the jaw: rare at osteoporosis doses; dental review before starting if
  major dental work planned. Atypical femoral fracture: ask about thigh/groin pain on long-term therapy.
- Osteomalacia (low vitamin D, high ALP, low phosphate) mimics low BMD — treat it first.
- Spine BMD falsely elevated by degenerative change, aortic calcification, vertebral fractures.

## Verify
- [ ] T- or Z-score used appropriately for age/sex; FRAX and vertebral imaging considered.
- [ ] Risk category (high vs very high) documented with its basis.
- [ ] Secondary-cause screen and medication review done; vitamin D and calcium replete.
- [ ] Drug choice matches renal function and cardiovascular history.
- [ ] Denosumab patients have a written plan for timely doses and any transition.
- [ ] Review/DXA date and treatment-duration plan recorded.
