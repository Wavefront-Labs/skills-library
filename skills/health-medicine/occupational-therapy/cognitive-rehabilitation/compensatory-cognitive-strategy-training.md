# Compensatory cognitive strategy training

**Goal:** reduce the everyday impact of memory, attention and executive impairment by
teaching strategies and building routines the person will actually use at home.

## 1. Assess cognition in function
- **Screens** (e.g. MoCA, ACE-III) flag impairment but do not predict task performance.
- **Performance-based functional cognition** tools - e.g. the Executive Function
  Performance Test (EFPT), Multiple Errands Test variants, kitchen tasks - show where and
  how tasks break down.
- Record error types: omission, sequencing, perseveration, distractibility, poor
  error detection, unsafe judgement.
- Assess **self-awareness** (intellectual, emergent, anticipatory - Crosson model); low
  awareness changes strategy choice.
- Screen for mood, fatigue, sleep, pain, hearing and vision, which all affect cognition.

## 2. Match strategy to impairment and awareness

| Problem | Strategy | Awareness needed |
|---|---|---|
| Prospective memory (forgetting to do things) | Smartphone alarms, calendars, whiteboard, pill organisers | Low-moderate (if routinised) |
| Learning new routines in severe amnesia | **Errorless learning**, **spaced retrieval**, vanishing cues | Low |
| Executive/planning failures | **Goal Management Training** (stop - define - list steps - learn - check), checklists | Moderate-high |
| Attention/distractibility | Reduce environmental load, one task at a time, time-pressure management | Moderate |
| Complex goals in mild-moderate impairment | Metacognitive approaches, e.g. CO-OP (Goal-Plan-Do-Check) | Moderate-high |

Internal strategies (visual imagery, mnemonics) generally suit milder impairment;
external aids are better supported for moderate-severe impairment.

## 3. Teach the strategy in stages
1. **Acquisition** - explain and demonstrate; person rehearses in clinic.
2. **Application** - use in a real but supported task (e.g. phone reminder for a
   medication time).
3. **Adaptation/generalisation** - use across tasks and settings without prompting.
Use the same wording, device and layout each time. Involve family/carers to prompt use
until habitual.

## 4. Errorless learning in practice
- Prevent errors during learning: give the answer or demonstrate before the person
  guesses; avoid "have a go".
- Fade support gradually (vanishing cues: give fewer letters/steps each trial).
- Combine with **spaced retrieval**: recall at increasing intervals (e.g. 30 s, 1 min,
  2 min, 4 min); reset to the last successful interval after an error.

## 5. Build routines and environments
- Fixed daily schedule anchored to existing habits (meds with breakfast).
- Single location for keys/wallet/phone; labels on cupboards; door checklist.
- Simplify technology: fewer apps, one calendar, consistent alarm tones.

## Gotchas
- Strategy use often fails to generalise; train in the real context and check at home.
- A strategy the person did not choose or does not see a need for will be abandoned;
  address awareness first.
- Cognitive "brain training" apps show limited transfer to daily function; do not
  substitute them for task-specific training.
- In progressive conditions (dementia), favour environmental and carer-led strategies
  and revise as abilities change.

## Verify
- [ ] Functional cognition observed, error types and awareness level recorded.
- [ ] Strategy chosen to fit impairment, awareness and the person's goals.
- [ ] Strategy taught through acquisition, application and generalisation stages.
- [ ] Carers trained and use consistent prompts.
- [ ] Use checked in the home/real setting at follow-up.
