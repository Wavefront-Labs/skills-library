# Home safety assessment and equipment prescription

**Goal:** make the person's home safe and usable for their priority tasks, with the
least intrusive effective solution, documented so that equipment services, funders and
contractors can implement it correctly.

## 1. Prepare
- Know the diagnosis and prognosis (stable, improving, progressive - this changes what
  to prescribe), transfer ability, mobility aid, cognition, vision, weight, carer input.
- Get the property layout and tenure (owner, rented, social housing - landlord consent
  may be needed).
- Plan who attends: the person, family/carer, sometimes physiotherapist or housing
  officer.

## 2. Assess in the home
Walk the person's actual routes and tasks: entry, stairs, bed, toilet, bath/shower,
kitchen, getting out in an emergency. A structured tool improves consistency:
**Westmead Home Safety Assessment**, **HSSAT**, or **SAFER-HOME**.
Record measurements needed for equipment: bed, chair and toilet heights; bath rim
height and internal width; door widths and turning space; step heights and number;
rail positions and wall construction.

## 3. Match solution to need (least intrusive first)
1. **Change the method** - teach a safer technique (e.g. sitting to wash).
2. **Rearrange the environment** - remove trip hazards, improve lighting, move items to
   reachable height, bed downstairs.
3. **Minor equipment/adaptations** - grab rails, stair rail (second rail), toilet frame
   or raised seat, bath board, shower chair, perching stool, chair/bed raisers,
   key-safe, pendant alarm.
4. **Major adaptations** - level-access shower, stairlift or through-floor lift, ramp,
   door widening, extension. Usually slower, means-tested or grant-funded (e.g. Disabled
   Facilities Grant in England; schemes differ elsewhere).

For hoists and slings: specify hoist type (mobile, ceiling track), sling type and size
from the manufacturer's chart, and train carers; this is a manual-handling risk task.

## 4. Wheelchair and seating basics
- Assess posture on a firm surface (pelvic tilt and obliquity, spinal curves, fixed vs
  flexible deformity) before measuring.
- Key measurements: hip width, seat depth (buttock to popliteal fossa, minus a small
  clearance), lower-leg length, back height, and elbow height. Typical clearance rules
  of thumb vary by service; follow local seating protocol.
- Choose a pressure-redistributing cushion according to tissue-viability risk (Braden
  or Waterlow) and the person's ability to self-relieve pressure.
- Check propulsion method, transfer method, transport and home access (door widths,
  turning circle).

## 5. Ramps and access
Ramp gradient limits come from building standards: **1:12** is the commonly cited
maximum in the US ADA standard, and UK guidance prefers gentler gradients for longer
runs. Check the current local standard and include landings and handrails.

## 6. Write the recommendation
State clinical need, functional problem, recommended solution, exact specification and
measurements, rationale, alternatives considered, urgency, and who installs/maintains.
Contractors need dimensions and positions, not just "grab rail by toilet".

## Gotchas
- Rails on plasterboard or stud walls need appropriate fixings; specify wall type.
- Progressive conditions (MND, MS, Parkinson's) need forward planning: a stairlift may
  soon be unusable when transfers fail.
- Equipment not trialled with the person is often unused; trial transfers on the day.
- Cluttered homes and pets are common fall causes; negotiate changes respectfully.
- Recheck capacity and consent if the person declines recommended changes; document risk
  discussions.

## Verify
- [ ] Home routes and priority tasks observed, with structured tool and measurements.
- [ ] Least intrusive effective solution chosen; prognosis considered.
- [ ] Equipment trialled and user/carers trained; sizes and weight limits checked.
- [ ] Wheelchair/cushion matched to posture, pressure risk and environment.
- [ ] Written specification is measurable and implementable, with follow-up booked.
