# ADL and IADL assessment and goal setting

**Goal:** produce a defensible picture of what the person needs to do, what they can do,
what gets in the way, and a small set of measurable goals that drive intervention.

## 1. Build the occupational profile first
Interview the person (and carer, with consent) before testing:
- **Roles and routines** — a typical day and week; what has stopped since onset.
- **Priorities** — what matters most to them, not to the team.
- **Context** — home layout, support network, cultural and financial factors, discharge
  destination and deadlines.
- **Prior level of function** — the baseline you are aiming back towards.

Frame findings with a model so nothing is missed: **PEO** (person-environment-occupation),
**MOHO** (volition, habituation, performance capacity) or the **ICF** (body function,
activity, participation, context).

## 2. Choose measures for the question

| Purpose | Typical tool | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Client-rated priorities and change | **COPM** | Up to 5 priority problems rated 1-10 for performance and satisfaction; a 2-point change is commonly cited as clinically meaningful |
| Basic ADL dependency (screen) | **Barthel Index** | 10 items; quick, ceiling effect in higher-functioning clients |
| Burden of care, rehab settings | **FIM** | 18 items, 7-level scale; requires credentialed training in many services |
| Quality of task performance | **AMPS** | Observed motor and process skills; needs calibrated, trained rater |
| IADL | **Lawton IADL**, kitchen/meal assessment | Self/proxy report vs observed performance can differ |
| Cognition in function | e.g. **PRPP**, **EFPT** | See cognitive rehabilitation skills |

Pick by setting and purpose; check licensing and training requirements locally.

## 3. Observe performance in context
- Observe the actual task (wash, dress, prepare a hot drink) rather than relying on
  report alone; people commonly over- or under-estimate ability.
- Record **level of assistance** (independent, set-up, supervision, minimal, moderate,
  maximal, dependent) and **type of cue** (verbal, gestural, physical).
- Note *why* performance breaks down: strength, range, balance, sensation, vision,
  sequencing, safety awareness, fatigue, pain or environment.
- Record safety risks (falls, burns, wandering) explicitly.

## 4. Analyse and formulate
Summarise strengths, barriers and the gap between capacity and required performance.
Separate *can't* (impairment), *doesn't* (habit, motivation, mood) and *shouldn't*
(risk) - each leads to a different intervention.

## 5. Set goals with the person
- Translate COPM priorities into **SMART** goals: who, does what, to what level of
  assistance, under what conditions, by when.
  Example: "Prepares a hot drink in own kitchen with supervision only, using a kettle
  tipper, within 2 weeks."
- For complex or heterogeneous caseloads, **Goal Attainment Scaling** (-2 to +2) allows
  comparison of individualised goals.
- Agree short-term steps towards each long-term goal and a review date.

## Gotchas
- Barthel/FIM scores describe dependency, not *why*; a score alone does not guide
  treatment.
- Assessing in an unfamiliar ward environment can underestimate (or overestimate) home
  performance.
- Fatigue, time of day, medication timing and pain change performance; record them.
- Proxy report from carers can reflect carer burden as much as client ability.
- Standardised tools are only valid if administered as per the manual.

## Verify
- [ ] Occupational profile documents priorities, roles, context and prior function.
- [ ] Measures chosen match purpose and setting; rater trained where required.
- [ ] At least one priority task observed with assistance level and breakdown reasons.
- [ ] Safety risks recorded and acted on.
- [ ] Goals are SMART, client-agreed, and have a review date and baseline score.
