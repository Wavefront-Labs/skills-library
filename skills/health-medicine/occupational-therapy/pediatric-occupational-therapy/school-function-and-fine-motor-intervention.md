# School function and fine-motor intervention

**Goal:** identify why a child struggles with school and self-care tasks and deliver
targeted, functional intervention, delivered largely through the people around them.

## 1. Clarify the referral question
Ask parents, teacher and (where possible) the child: which tasks are hard (writing,
scissors, dressing for PE, lunchbox, organisation)? Since when? In what settings? What
does the child want to get better at? Review developmental history, diagnoses (DCD,
autism, ADHD, cerebral palsy) and vision/hearing checks.

## 2. Assess with purpose-matched tools

| Question | Typical tools |
|---|---|
| Participation and performance at school | **School Function Assessment (SFA)**, teacher questionnaire, classroom observation |
| Motor coordination / DCD identification | **Movement ABC-2**, **BOT-2**, DCDQ (parent questionnaire) |
| Visual-motor integration | **Beery VMI** with visual perception and motor coordination subtests |
| Handwriting | Writing sample (legibility, speed, letter formation, spacing); speed tools vary by country |
| Self-care | Observation, parent report, PEDI-CAT |
| Child's own goals | **Perceived Efficacy and Goal Setting (PEGS)**, COPM for older children |

Use norms appropriate to the child's age and population; check the manual's normative
sample.

## 3. Observe the components that matter
- **Postural stability**: seated on a chair with feet flat, hips and knees about
  90 degrees, desk near elbow height.
- **Grasp**: mature dynamic tripod or quadrupod grasps are both functional; change grasp
  only if it causes pain, fatigue, illegibility or slowness.
- Bilateral coordination (paper stabilisation), in-hand manipulation, pressure,
  letter formation start points, copying from board vs near point.
- Attention, sensory and emotional factors (avoidance, frustration).

## 4. Intervene functionally
- **Task-oriented approaches** have the strongest support for DCD: CO-OP
  (Goal-Plan-Do-Check, child discovers strategies) and Neuromotor Task Training.
  Generic "process-oriented" training (e.g. isolated sensory or perceptual drills) has
  weaker evidence for functional change.
- **Handwriting:** explicit letter formation by families of letters with consistent
  verbal cues, short daily practice, lined or raised-line paper, slant board, pencil
  grips if they help. Consider keyboarding or assistive technology early when writing
  load blocks learning.
- **Self-care:** backward chaining for dressing and shoelaces, visual schedules,
  adapted clothing (elastic laces, Velcro).
- **Classroom adaptations:** extra time, reduced copying, printed notes, positioning,
  preferential seating.

## 5. Coach the family and school
Use a **coaching** model: agree goals, observe the routine, problem-solve together,
plan home/class practice, review. Write recommendations into the school's support
plan (e.g. IEP in the US, EHCP or SEN support plan in England); terminology and
legal duties vary by jurisdiction.

## Gotchas
- A low VMI score does not by itself explain poor handwriting; check speed, motivation,
  and language/literacy factors.
- Mixed-handed or left-handed writers need paper slanted the other way and a
  hand position that avoids smudging.
- DCD is a diagnosis requiring medical exclusion of other conditions; OT assessment
  contributes but criteria (e.g. DSM-5) must be met.
- Weekly clinic sessions without home/school practice rarely change performance.

## Verify
- [ ] Referral question and child/family/teacher goals documented.
- [ ] Standardised tools chosen for the question and normed for the child.
- [ ] Posture, grasp, bilateral and attentional factors observed in context.
- [ ] Intervention is task-oriented with a home/school practice plan.
- [ ] Recommendations shared with school in a usable, specific form; review date set.
