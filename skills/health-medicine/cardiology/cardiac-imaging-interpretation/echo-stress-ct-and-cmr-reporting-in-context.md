# Echo, stress, CT and CMR reporting in context

> General clinical/professional reference, not patient-specific advice. Grading cut-offs follow society documents that are revised periodically; confirm the version your lab uses.

**Goal:** turn images into a report that answers the clinical question, with the
measurements that drive decisions and the caveats that stop wrong ones.

## 1. Echocardiography reporting
- **LV:** size (indexed volumes), wall thickness, regional wall motion by segment,
  **LVEF** (biplane Simpson's or 3D preferred over eyeball), **global longitudinal strain**
  (more reproducible; GLS less negative than about −16% to −18% is abnormal; vendor-
  dependent). Cardio-oncology uses relative GLS change > 15% as significant.
- **Diastolic function / filling pressures:** E/A, average E/e′ (> 14 raised), septal/
  lateral e′, TR velocity (> 2.8 m/s), LA volume index (> 34 mL/m²) — apply the algorithm;
  indeterminate is a valid answer.
- **RV:** TAPSE (< 17 mm abnormal), S′, FAC; estimated PASP with RA pressure.
- **Aortic stenosis:** Vmax ≥ 4 m/s, mean gradient ≥ 40 mmHg, AVA < 1.0 cm² = severe.
  Discordant grading → check LVOT diameter measurement (squared error), alignment, then
  consider **low-flow low-gradient** (SVi < 35 mL/m²): dobutamine stress echo for reduced
  EF, CT calcium score (sex-specific thresholds) for preserved EF.
- **Mitral regurgitation:** integrate — vena contracta, EROA (primary MR severe ≥ 0.4 cm²),
  regurgitant volume, PISA, pulmonary vein flow, LA/LV size. Colour jet area alone misleads.
- Always state image quality, comparison with prior, and whether the question was answered.

## 2. Stress testing
- **Exercise ECG:** limited sensitivity; mainly for functional capacity and symptoms.
  ≥ 1 mm horizontal/downsloping ST depression is positive; Duke treadmill score for prognosis.
  Uninterpretable with LBBB, paced rhythm, digoxin, LVH with strain.
- **Stress echo:** new wall-motion abnormalities in ≥ 2 contiguous segments; LV dilation
  with stress and fall in EF are high-risk.
- **Nuclear MPI (SPECT/PET):** reversible defects = ischaemia, fixed = scar (or artefact —
  breast/diaphragm attenuation; check gated images and attenuation correction). Extent
  (≥ 10% ischaemic myocardium high risk), TID, PET myocardial blood flow reserve.
- Report: adequacy (target heart rate ≥ 85% predicted, or adequate pharmacological stress),
  symptoms, ECG, imaging findings, risk category.

## 3. CT coronary angiography
- Report per segment; **CAD-RADS 2.0**: 0 (none) to 5 (total occlusion), plus plaque
  burden (P1–P4) and modifiers (N non-diagnostic, HRP high-risk plaque, S stent, G graft,
  E exceptions). CAD-RADS 3 (50–69%) → consider functional test or CT-FFR; 4A/4B →
  invasive angiography as indicated.
- Heavy calcium and motion artefact overstate stenosis; say so.
- Report incidental extracardiac findings per local policy.

## 4. Cardiac MRI
- Volumes and function (reference standard), **late gadolinium enhancement** patterns:
  - Subendocardial/transmural in a coronary territory → infarct.
  - Mid-wall (septal) → non-ischaemic cardiomyopathy; prognostic.
  - Subepicardial (inferolateral) → myocarditis (with T2/oedema, T1/ECV by Lake Louise
    criteria 2018).
  - Diffuse subendocardial with abnormal gadolinium kinetics → amyloid.
- **Viability:** LGE transmurality < 50% suggests recovery potential with revascularisation
  (but REVIVED-BCIS2 questioned outcome benefit of revascularisation for ischaemic LV
  dysfunction — integrate with symptoms and ischaemia).
- Stress perfusion CMR as a functional test.

## Gotchas
- Reporting LVEF to the single percent from an eyeball estimate.
- Calling severe AS "moderate" because the gradient is low in low-flow states.
- Attenuation artefacts reported as inferior/anterior ischaemia.
