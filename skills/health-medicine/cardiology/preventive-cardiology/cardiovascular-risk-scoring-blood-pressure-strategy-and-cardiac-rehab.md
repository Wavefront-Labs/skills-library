# Cardiovascular risk scoring, blood-pressure strategy and cardiac rehab

> General clinical/professional reference, not patient-specific advice. Risk calculators and treatment thresholds are population-specific; use the ones validated for your region. For lipid-lowering detail see `health-medicine.endocrinology.lipid-and-metabolic-disorders.ldl-cholesterol-lowering-strategy`.

**Goal:** match treatment intensity to absolute risk, get blood pressure controlled
(most aren't), and get every eligible cardiac patient into rehabilitation.

## 1. Risk scoring
- Use the regional calculator: **PREVENT** (US, 2023; includes kidney function, no race,
  predicts ASCVD and heart failure, 10- and 30-year), Pooled Cohort Equations (older US),
  **SCORE2/SCORE2-OP** (Europe, by risk region), **QRISK3** (UK).
- Don't use primary-prevention calculators for people who already have ASCVD, familial
  hypercholesterolaemia (LDL ≥ 4.9 mmol/L / 190 mg/dL (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)), diabetes with organ damage
  in some frameworks, or CKD — they're already high risk.
- **Risk enhancers** (shift borderline/intermediate decisions): family history of
  premature ASCVD, **lipoprotein(a)** (measure once in a lifetime; ≥ ~125 nmol/L / 50 mg/dL
  raises risk (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)), persistent elevated hs-CRP, CKD, chronic inflammatory disease, HIV,
  pre-eclampsia or early menopause history, South Asian ancestry, metabolic syndrome.
- **Coronary artery calcium (CAC):** when the decision is uncertain at intermediate risk —
  CAC 0 (without strong risk enhancers or smoking) favours deferring statins; CAC ≥ 100
  or ≥ 75th percentile favours treatment (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
- Communicate risk in absolute terms and with lifetime perspective for younger patients.

## 2. Blood pressure
**Measure right** (most errors are technique): seated 5 min, back supported, feet flat,
arm at heart level, correct cuff size, no talking, two or more readings averaged.
Confirm office readings with **home BP or ABPM** (white-coat and masked hypertension).

**Targets** (verify current guideline): AHA/ACC 2025 <130/80 for most adults using PREVENT at stage 1, and ESC 2024 120–129 systolic for most treated adults (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked). NICE NG136 (fetched): confirm hypertension with clinic ≥140/90 plus ABPM daytime or HBPM average ≥135/85; treat to below 135/85 under age 80 and below 145/85 at 80 or over; individualise in frailty, orthostatic hypotension, very old.

**Treatment**
- Lifestyle: sodium reduction, potassium-rich diet (caution in CKD), weight loss,
  alcohol reduction, aerobic activity.
- First-line drugs: ACEi/ARB, dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker, thiazide-like
  diuretic. A **single-pill combination** is advised by US/ESC guidelines for most with BP ≥ 140/90 (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked); NICE NG136 uses a stepped approach (ACEi/ARB or CCB first, by age and ethnicity).
- Step: two-drug → three-drug (A + C + D) → add low-dose spironolactone for **resistant
  hypertension** (NICE step 4 if potassium ≤4.5 mmol/L; caution with low eGFR; check sodium, potassium, renal function within 1 month) after confirming adherence and ABPM.
- Screen for secondary causes: **primary aldosteronism** (renin/aldosterone ratio — far
  more common than once thought, especially resistant or hypokalaemic hypertension), OSA,
  renal disease, renal artery stenosis, drugs (NSAIDs, decongestants, oral contraceptives).

## 3. Cardiac rehabilitation
- **Refer automatically** after ACS, PCI, CABG, valve surgery, stable heart failure
  (HFrEF), and some with stable angina/PAD. Participation reduces mortality and
  readmissions, but uptake is often < 30–50% (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
- Components: exercise training (aerobic + resistance; intensity by symptom-limited
  exercise test or perceived exertion e.g. Borg 12–14 (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)), risk-factor management,
  psychological support, education, return-to-work advice.
- Home-based and hybrid programmes have comparable outcomes for many patients; offer them.

## 4. Lifestyle and other levers
Smoking cessation (varenicline/combination NRT; most impactful single action), diet
(Mediterranean-pattern), physical activity ≥ 150 min/week moderate (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked), weight management
(GLP-1 RA with CV outcome benefit in established ASCVD with obesity — SELECT trial),
diabetes agents with CV benefit, influenza vaccination.

## Gotchas
- Using a 10-year score in a 35-year-old with very high LDL → falsely reassuring.
- Uncontrolled BP blamed on "white coat" without home readings.
- Rehab referral left to the patient — make it at discharge, automatically.

## Verify
- [ ] Regionally validated risk score documented with enhancers considered.
- [ ] BP measured with proper technique and confirmed out-of-office.
- [ ] Single-pill combination considered; resistant hypertension screened for aldosteronism.
- [ ] Lp(a) measured at least once.
- [ ] Cardiac rehab referral sent for every eligible patient.
