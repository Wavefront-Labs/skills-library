# Reperfusion, antithrombotic strategy and cardiogenic shock

> General clinical/professional reference, not patient-specific advice. Drug doses, time targets and agent preferences differ between guidelines and regions; follow the local ACS pathway.

**Goal:** open the occluded artery fast, prevent re-thrombosis without causing major
bleeding, and recognise shock and mechanical complications before they're irreversible.

## 1. STEMI (and occlusion MI equivalents)
- Diagnosis from ECG + symptoms; don't wait for troponin (see
  `health-medicine.cardiology.ecg-interpretation.systematic-12-lead-reading-and-high-risk-patterns`).
- **Primary PCI** preferred if achievable within **120 min** of diagnosis (ESC: STEMI
  diagnosis to wire crossing); target ≤ 60–90 min in PCI-capable centres.
- Otherwise **fibrinolysis** within 10 min of diagnosis (if < 12 h symptoms and no
  contraindications), then transfer: rescue PCI if failed reperfusion (< 50% ST
  resolution at 60–90 min, ongoing pain), routine angiography 2–24 h if successful.
- Late presenters (12–48 h) with ongoing ischaemia, heart failure or instability still
  benefit from PCI.
- Resuscitated cardiac arrest with STEMI → immediate angiography.

## 2. NSTE-ACS timing
- **Immediate** (< 2 h): haemodynamic instability, refractory pain, life-threatening
  arrhythmia, mechanical complications, acute heart failure, recurrent dynamic ST changes.
- **Early** (< 24 h): confirmed NSTEMI, dynamic ST/T changes, GRACE > 140.
- Others: selective invasive strategy guided by ischaemia testing or anatomy.

## 3. Antithrombotic therapy
- **Aspirin** loading then low dose indefinitely.
- **P2Y12 inhibitor:** ticagrelor or prasugrel preferred over clopidogrel in most ACS
  patients managed invasively (prasugrel avoided with prior stroke/TIA; age ≥ 75 or
  < 60 kg dose-adjusted); clopidogrel with fibrinolysis, oral anticoagulation, or high
  bleeding risk. **Pre-treatment** before angiography in NSTE-ACS is no longer routine
  in ESC guidance.
- **Parenteral anticoagulation:** UFH during PCI; enoxaparin or fondaparinux in
  medically managed or pre-angiography settings per pathway.
- DAPT duration default 12 months; shorten (1–3 months then single antiplatelet) in
  **high bleeding risk** (ARC-HBR); triple therapy with an oral anticoagulant for AF kept
  short (often ≤ 1 week, up to 30 days), then OAC + clopidogrel.
- Radial access reduces bleeding; PPI with DAPT for GI-risk patients.

## 4. Cardiogenic shock
- Recognise early (SCAI stages A–E): hypotension or vasopressor need, hypoperfusion
  (lactate, oliguria, cool peripheries, altered mentation).
- Urgent echo (LV/RV function, mechanical complications), early revascularisation of
  the **culprit lesion only** in multivessel disease (CULPRIT-SHOCK).
- Vasopressor of choice usually noradrenaline; inotropes (dobutamine) for low output.
- Mechanical circulatory support (Impella, VA-ECMO, IABP) via a **shock team**; recent
  trial data support microaxial pump in selected STEMI shock, VA-ECMO benefit unproven —
  evidence evolving.

## 5. CCU care and complications
- Monitoring for arrhythmia (VF early), heart failure, re-infarction.
- **Mechanical complications** (days 1–7): papillary muscle rupture (acute MR, pulmonary
  oedema), ventricular septal rupture (new harsh murmur), free wall rupture (tamponade) →
  urgent surgery/shock team.
- Before discharge: echo for LVEF, secondary prevention (high-intensity statin ± ezetimibe,
  beta-blocker if reduced EF, ACEi/ARB, MRA if EF ≤ 40% with HF/diabetes), cardiac
  rehabilitation referral, smoking cessation, driving/work advice.

## Gotchas
- Transfer delays turning a lysis candidate into a late PCI — decide at first contact.
- Type 2 MI (supply–demand) treated with full ACS antithrombotics.
- NSAIDs during ACS increase risk; avoid.

- [ ] Antithrombotic regimen justified against bleeding risk; DAPT plan written.
- [ ] Shock screened (lactate, perfusion) and escalated to shock team when present.
