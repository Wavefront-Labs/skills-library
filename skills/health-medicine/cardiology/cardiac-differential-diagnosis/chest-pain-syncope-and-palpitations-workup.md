# Chest pain, syncope and palpitations workup

> General clinical/professional reference, not patient-specific advice. Troponin cut-offs are assay-specific; follow your laboratory's validated pathway.

**Goal:** catch the dangerous causes early, avoid over-investigating the benign ones,
and leave each patient with a clear next step.

## 1. Chest pain
**Immediate threats first** (ECG within 10 min): ACS, aortic dissection, pulmonary
embolism, tension pneumothorax, tamponade, oesophageal rupture.
- Dissection: tearing pain radiating to back, pulse/BP differential between arms,
  neurological deficit, new aortic regurgitation → CT angiography; don't give
  antithrombotics until excluded if suspected.
- PE: pleuritic pain, dyspnoea, tachycardia, risk factors → Wells/PERC, D-dimer, CTPA.
**ACS pathway**
- ECG: STEMI/equivalents → reperfusion pathway (see `health-medicine.cardiology.acute-coronary-care`).
- **High-sensitivity troponin** 0/1 h or 0/2 h algorithms (ESC) or single low value
  with symptom onset > 3 h → rule-out; rising pattern → rule-in; intermediate →
  observe, repeat. Troponin rise ≠ always MI (myocarditis, PE, sepsis, renal failure,
  type 2 MI).
- Risk scores (HEART, EDACS) guide discharge and outpatient testing; low-risk patients
  generally don't need urgent testing. Intermediate risk → CT coronary angiography or
  functional testing.
- Describe pain as cardiac, possibly cardiac or non-cardiac rather than "atypical".

## 2. Syncope
- Confirm it was transient loss of consciousness with spontaneous recovery (vs
  seizure, fall, hypoglycaemia, intoxication).
- Classify: **reflex** (prodrome, triggers — standing, pain, micturition, cough),
  **orthostatic** (on standing; drugs, volume depletion, autonomic failure),
  **cardiac** (exertional, supine, preceded by palpitations, no prodrome, structural
  heart disease, family history of sudden death).
- Everyone: history, examination including **lying/standing BP**, 12-lead ECG.
- High-risk features → admit/urgent evaluation: exertional syncope, chest pain,
  new heart failure, structural disease, abnormal ECG (Mobitz II/CHB, bifascicular
  block, long/short QT, Brugada pattern, pre-excitation, sustained VT, ARVC signs),
  family history of sudden death, syncope causing serious injury.
- Tools: Canadian Syncope Risk Score; echo if structural disease suspected; ambulatory
  monitoring/implantable loop recorder for recurrent unexplained episodes.
- Driving restrictions vary by jurisdiction and cause — counsel and document.

## 3. Palpitations
- Ask: onset/offset (abrupt suggests SVT), regular vs irregular, rate, triggers,
  vagal manoeuvres stopping it, syncope, exertional onset.
- 12-lead ECG at baseline (pre-excitation, long QT, ectopy) and **during symptoms**
  whenever possible — the diagnosis is the rhythm at the time.
- Monitor matched to frequency (Holter, patch, event recorder, smartwatch ECG).
- Check TFTs, Hb, electrolytes, caffeine/alcohol/stimulants; echo if structural concern.
- Red flags: syncope, exertional palpitations, family history of sudden death, known
  structural disease.

## 4. Dyspnoea and murmurs (brief)
- Dyspnoea: natriuretic peptide (BNP/NT-proBNP) to rule out heart failure; echo if raised.
- Murmurs: systolic ejection murmur radiating to carotids with soft S2 and slow-rising
  pulse → aortic stenosis; diastolic murmurs are always pathological; refer for echo
  when symptomatic, loud (≥ 3/6), diastolic, or with abnormal ECG.

## Gotchas
- Normal ECG and first troponin early after onset don't exclude MI — follow the pathway timing.
- Labelling exertional syncope as vasovagal.
- Women, older adults and diabetics more often present without classic chest pain.

## Verify
- [ ] ECG within 10 min for chest pain; immediate threats considered and documented.
- [ ] Troponin pathway followed with correct timings.
- [ ] Syncope classified; high-risk features screened; lying/standing BP recorded.
- [ ] Palpitations: symptom-rhythm correlation planned.
- [ ] Clear follow-up and driving advice documented.
