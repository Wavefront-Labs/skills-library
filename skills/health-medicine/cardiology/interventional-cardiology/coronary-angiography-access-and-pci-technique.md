# Coronary angiography, access and PCI technique

> General clinical/professional reference for trained interventional staff, not patient-specific advice or a substitute for supervised procedural training.

**Goal:** accurate anatomy, a revascularisation decision based on physiology and the
heart team, and an optimised stent result with no access complications.

## 1. Before the case
- Indication and consent (including stroke, bleeding, emergency surgery risk), renal
  function, contrast allergy history, anticoagulants, diabetes medicines.
- Contrast-associated AKI risk → hydrate per protocol, minimise contrast (track the
  volume; consider limits such as ~ 3× eGFR mL as a rough ceiling).
- Plan SYNTAX/heart-team discussion for left main or complex multivessel disease in
  elective cases.

## 2. Access
- **Radial first** (lower bleeding and vascular complications, mortality benefit in ACS).
  Check pulse, use ultrasound in difficult cases, spasmolytic cocktail, heparin.
- **Patent haemostasis** with a band (allow antegrade flow) to prevent radial occlusion.
- Femoral when needed (large-bore, CTO, failed radial): **ultrasound- and fluoroscopy-
  guided puncture** over the common femoral artery (above the bifurcation, below the
  inferior epigastric artery) — high sticks cause retroperitoneal bleeding.

## 3. Angiography
- Standard views: left system — RAO caudal, RAO cranial, AP cranial, LAO cranial, LAO
  caudal (spider); right — LAO, RAO, AP cranial. Image every segment in ≥ 2 orthogonal views.
- Watch pressure waveform for damping/ventricularisation (ostial disease) before injecting.
- Visual stenosis estimates are unreliable for intermediate (40–90%) lesions.

## 4. Lesion decisions
- **FFR ≤ 0.80 / iFR (or other non-hyperaemic ratio) ≤ 0.89** = functionally significant
  in stable disease; defer if above. Physiology avoids stenting lesions that won't benefit.
- In ACS, treat the culprit; non-culprit lesions per physiology/angiography and timing
  evidence (complete revascularisation in STEMI improves outcomes; not in shock acutely).

## 5. PCI technique
- Guide catheter for support and coaxial alignment; workhorse wire, anticoagulation
  with ACT targets per protocol (commonly ~250–300 s with UFH alone).
- Lesion preparation: predilate; **calcium** needs modification (non-compliant/cutting
  balloons, intravascular lithotripsy, atherectomy) — underexpanded stents fail.
- **Drug-eluting stents** sized to reference vessel; post-dilate with non-compliant balloons.
- **Intravascular imaging (IVUS/OCT)** for left main, long lesions, calcified or bifurcation
  disease and ACS ambiguity: guides size and confirms expansion, apposition and edge
  dissections; trials show fewer target-vessel events.
- Bifurcations: provisional side-branch strategy for most; two-stent strategies for true
  left main bifurcations with large diseased side branches.

## 6. Complications
- **No-reflow:** intracoronary adenosine, nitroprusside or verapamil; avoid aggressive
  post-dilation in thrombus-laden vessels.
- **Dissection:** stent if flow-limiting.
- **Perforation:** balloon tamponade proximally, reverse heparin if needed, covered stent
  (large vessel) or coils/fat (distal), pericardiocentesis for tamponade.
- **Stent thrombosis:** antiplatelet adherence; check for underexpansion.
- Access bleeding, pseudoaneurysm, radial occlusion — check before discharge.

## 7. Structural interventions (overview)
TAVI, transcatheter edge-to-edge repair, LAA occlusion and PFO closure are heart-team
decisions with dedicated imaging (CT, TOE) and procedure-specific training.

## Gotchas
- Treating an angiographically "tight" lesion without symptoms or physiology.
- Underexpanded stent in calcified disease — the commonest cause of restenosis/thrombosis.
- Contrast load accumulating in staged or complex cases.

## Verify
- [ ] Access site chosen deliberately; ultrasound used when indicated.
- [ ] Every segment in two orthogonal views.
- [ ] Physiology or imaging used for intermediate lesions.
- [ ] Stent expansion confirmed (imaging when indicated).
- [ ] Contrast volume, ACT and access-site checks documented.
