# GDMT optimisation and decompensation management

> General clinical/professional reference, not patient-specific advice. Doses, contraindications and monitoring must be checked for each patient; titration protocols vary.

**Goal:** get patients on all proven therapies at effective doses quickly, keep them
out of hospital, and recognise when they need devices or advanced therapies.

## 1. Classify
- **HFrEF:** LVEF ≤ 40%. **HFmrEF:** 41–49%. **HFpEF:** ≥ 50% with structural/
  functional abnormality and raised natriuretic peptides. **HFimpEF:** improved EF —
  continue therapy (stopping leads to relapse).
- Look for the cause: ischaemia, hypertension, valve disease, cardiomyopathies
  (amyloid in HFpEF with LVH, carpal tunnel, low voltage), arrhythmia, alcohol,
  chemotherapy, thyroid.

## 2. The four pillars in HFrEF
1. **ARNI** (sacubitril/valsartan) — preferred over ACEi/ARB; 36 h washout after ACEi.
2. **Evidence-based beta-blocker** (bisoprolol, carvedilol, metoprolol succinate,
   nebivolol).
3. **MRA** (spironolactone, eplerenone) — check K+ and creatinine.
4. **SGLT2 inhibitor** (dapagliflozin, empagliflozin) — benefits across EF range;
   expect a small, acceptable initial eGFR dip.
- **Start all four early, in low doses, often simultaneously or in quick sequence**, then
  up-titrate every 1–2 weeks to target or maximum tolerated (STRONG-HF: rapid up-titration
  after admission reduced events). Don't wait to reach target dose of one before starting the next.
- Tolerance issues: low BP — reduce diuretics/non-HF vasodilators first; hyperkalaemia —
  potassium binders allow continuation; creatinine rise up to ~30% is acceptable.
- Add-ons: ivabradine (sinus rhythm, HR ≥ 70 on max beta-blocker), hydralazine–nitrate
  (Black patients, ACEi/ARNI intolerance), IV iron for iron deficiency (ferritin < 100
  or 100–299 with TSAT < 20%), vericiguat after worsening event.
- **HFpEF:** SGLT2 inhibitor, diuretics for congestion, treat comorbidities (BP, AF,
  obesity — GLP-1 RA data in obese HFpEF), MRA/ARNI considered in lower range of EF.

## 3. Acute decompensation
- Identify precipitants (ischaemia, arrhythmia, infection, non-adherence, NSAIDs,
  uncontrolled BP) and profile (warm/cold, wet/dry).
- **IV loop diuretic** at 1–2.5× the home oral dose (furosemide equivalent).
- Check response early: spot urine sodium at 2 h (< 50–70 mmol/L suggests poor response)
  or urine output at 6 h (< 100–150 mL/h) → double the dose; then add sequential
  nephron blockade (thiazide-type, acetazolamide) for resistance; monitor electrolytes.
- **Decongest fully** before discharge (clinical exam, weight, natriuretic peptide);
  residual congestion predicts readmission.
- Cold/hypoperfused → inotropes and shock pathway.
- Continue/initiate GDMT in hospital unless shock or severe hyperkalaemia/AKI.

## 4. Devices
- **ICD** for primary prevention: LVEF ≤ 35% despite ≥ 3 months optimal GDMT, in
  ischaemic and selected non-ischaemic disease, life expectancy > 1 year.
- **CRT:** LVEF ≤ 35%, LBBB with QRS ≥ 150 ms (strongest benefit), sinus rhythm; weaker
  with non-LBBB or QRS 130–149.
- Reassess EF after GDMT optimisation before implanting — many improve.

## 5. Advanced therapies
Refer early when **I NEED HELP**: IV inotropes, NYHA III–IV/natriuretic peptide
persistently high, End-organ dysfunction, EF ≤ 25%, Defibrillator shocks, Hospitalisations
(> 1 in 12 months), Escalating diuretics, Low BP, Prognostic medication intolerance.
Options: LVAD, transplant, palliative care discussions alongside.

## Gotchas
- Leaving patients on starter doses for months.
- Stopping beta-blockers for mild decompensation (usually continue unless shock).
- Discharging on weight loss alone while still congested.

## Verify
- [ ] EF category and aetiology documented.
- [ ] All four pillars prescribed or contraindication recorded; titration plan dated.
- [ ] Diuretic response checked at 2–6 h in admitted patients.
- [ ] Device candidacy reassessed after ≥ 3 months GDMT.
- [ ] Advanced-HF triggers screened at each review.
