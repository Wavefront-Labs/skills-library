# Prolonged PT/APTT: interpretation and mixing studies

> General clinical/laboratory reference, not patient-specific advice. Reagent sensitivities, normal ranges and mixing-study cut-offs are laboratory-specific; reversal agents and factor concentrates must be dosed per local protocol with haematology advice.

**Goal:** turn an abnormal clotting screen into a specific explanation — sample artefact,
drug, deficiency, inhibitor or consumption — and decide whether it matters for bleeding,
thrombosis or a planned procedure.

## 1. Exclude the boring causes first
- **Pre-analytical:** under-filled citrate tube (wrong blood:anticoagulant ratio),
  heparin contamination from a line, delayed processing, **high haematocrit > 55%**
  (needs citrate adjustment), clotted sample.
- **Drugs:** warfarin (PT/INR), heparin (APTT; anti-Xa level for monitoring), DOACs
  (variable effect: rivaroxaban often prolongs PT, dabigatran prolongs APTT and thrombin
  time strongly; apixaban may leave both near-normal). Ask when the last dose was taken.
- Repeat on a fresh, correctly taken sample before investigating.

## 2. Read the pattern
| PT | APTT | Think of |
|---|---|---|
| ↑ | normal | factor VII deficiency, early vitamin K deficiency/warfarin, early liver disease |
| normal | ↑ | heparin; deficiency of VIII, IX, XI (bleeding) or XII (no bleeding); **lupus anticoagulant** (thrombosis, not bleeding); **acquired FVIII inhibitor**; von Willebrand disease (if FVIII low) |
| ↑ | ↑ | liver disease, vitamin K deficiency/warfarin excess, **DIC**, dilution after massive transfusion, common pathway deficiency (X, V, II, fibrinogen), DOACs, heparin overdose |
Add **fibrinogen (Clauss)** and **thrombin time** (heparin, dabigatran, low/dysfunctional fibrinogen).

## 3. Mixing study (50:50 with normal pooled plasma)
- **Corrects** → factor **deficiency** → specific factor assays.
- **Does not correct** → **inhibitor**:
  - **Lupus anticoagulant:** immediate non-correction; confirm with dRVVT and
    LA-sensitive APTT with phospholipid confirmation; associated with **thrombosis and
    pregnancy loss**, not bleeding. Repeat at **12 weeks** for antiphospholipid syndrome diagnosis.
  - **Acquired FVIII inhibitor (acquired haemophilia A):** may correct initially and
    then **prolong after incubation (1–2 h at 37 °C)** — time-dependent inhibitor.
    Elderly, postpartum, autoimmune disease or cancer; new extensive bruising or muscle
    bleeds with an isolated long APTT. **Haematological emergency**: avoid IM injections
    and invasive procedures, measure FVIII and Bethesda titre, bypassing agents/
    emicizumab per specialist.
  - Heparin contamination also fails to correct — check thrombin time/heparin neutralisation.

## 4. DIC
Triggers: sepsis, trauma, obstetric complications, malignancy (APL!).
Pattern: prolonged PT, **low/falling fibrinogen**, **falling platelets**, raised
D-dimer; schistocytes may be present. **ISTH overt DIC score ≥ 5** (platelets, D-dimer,
PT prolongation, fibrinogen). Treat the cause; replace products only for bleeding or
procedures (platelets, plasma, fibrinogen). Trend counts every 6–12 h.

## 5. Bleeding history still matters
A normal screen doesn't exclude a bleeding disorder: **von Willebrand disease**, mild
haemophilia, **platelet function disorders**, factor XIII deficiency and
hyperfibrinolysis can all have normal PT/APTT. Use a structured **bleeding assessment
tool (ISTH-BAT)** and request VWF antigen/activity, FVIII and platelet function testing
when the history is positive.
Conversely, an isolated prolonged APTT from **factor XII deficiency** or lupus
anticoagulant does not need correction before surgery.

## Gotchas
- Don't give plasma to "correct" a long INR before a procedure in a non-bleeding patient
  — modest INR rises (e.g. liver disease) don't predict bleeding.
- Vitamin K deficiency in malnutrition/antibiotics/cholestasis: responds to IV vitamin K within hours.
- Normal PT/APTT does not exclude a clinically relevant apixaban level.

## Verify
- [ ] Sample quality and anticoagulant drugs excluded before workup.
- [ ] Pattern (PT/APTT/fibrinogen/TT) recorded.
- [ ] Mixing study (immediate and incubated) used for isolated prolonged APTT.
- [ ] Possible acquired haemophilia escalated urgently.
- [ ] DIC scored and the trigger treated; bleeding history taken regardless of screen.
