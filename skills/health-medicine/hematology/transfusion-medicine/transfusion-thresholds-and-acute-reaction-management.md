# Transfusion thresholds and acute reaction management

> General clinical reference, not patient-specific advice. Thresholds, product doses and special requirements must follow national guidance and the local transfusion laboratory's policy. Massive haemorrhage: `health-medicine.anesthesiology.anesthetic-crisis-management.perioperative-anaphylaxis-malignant-hyperthermia-and-massive-haemorrhage`.

**Goal:** transfuse only when it helps, give the right product to the right patient,
and recognise a reaction in the first minutes when stopping the transfusion saves lives.

## 1. Red cells
- **Restrictive threshold Hb < 70 g/L** (target 70–90) for most stable inpatients,
  including ICU and GI bleeding.
- **Hb < 80 g/L** commonly used in **acute coronary syndrome** (trial evidence is mixed; the
  2023 MINT trial leaned towards a liberal strategy), orthopaedic and cardiac surgery per
  local practice.
- **Single-unit transfusion then reassess** (clinical state + repeat Hb) in non-bleeding
  patients — one adult unit raises Hb by ~10 g/L.
- Treat the cause: iron deficiency → iron, not blood, in stable patients.
- Chronically transfused patients (thalassaemia, MDS) follow individual thresholds.

## 2. Platelets
- Prophylaxis in reversible marrow failure: **< 10 × 10⁹/L** (not routinely in
  autologous transplant or chronic stable marrow failure in some guidance).
- Before procedures: thresholds commonly ~20 (CVC, ultrasound-guided), 40–50 (LP, most
  surgery), 50–100 (neurosurgery/posterior eye) — **check local tables**.
- Active bleeding: keep > 50 (> 100 for CNS/major trauma).
- **Don't transfuse** in TTP or HIT (thrombosis risk) unless life-threatening bleeding;
  ITP rarely, only with serious bleeding.

## 3. Plasma and cryoprecipitate
- **FFP** for bleeding with coagulopathy or massive haemorrhage — **not** to "correct" an
  INR before minor procedures in non-bleeding patients. Warfarin reversal uses
  **PCC + vitamin K**, not FFP.
- **Cryoprecipitate/fibrinogen concentrate** for fibrinogen < 1.5 g/L in bleeding
  (< 2 g/L in obstetric haemorrhage).

## 4. Special requirements (most incidents are about these)
- **Irradiated** components: prevent transfusion-associated GVHD — purine analogues
  (fludarabine), Hodgkin lymphoma, stem cell transplant, some CAR-T protocols, congenital
  T-cell immunodeficiency, HLA-matched or directed donations.
- **CMV-safe**: leucodepleted products are adequate in many countries; CMV-seronegative
  units kept for pregnancy/neonates per local policy.
- Antigen-negative units for patients with alloantibodies; extended matching in sickle
  cell disease.
Flag requirements on the patient's record and in the lab system.

## 5. Safety at the bedside
Two-sample rule or equivalent for group confirmation, **positive patient ID** at
sampling and administration (wristband vs component vs prescription), observations
before, at 15 minutes and after each unit. **Wrong blood in tube** is the root of
ABO-incompatible transfusion.

## 6. Acute reactions — stop first
**Any reaction: stop the transfusion, keep the line open with saline, check the patient
and unit ID, observations, call for help.** Then classify:
- **Fever < 2 °C rise, stable, no other signs** → febrile non-haemolytic; may restart
  slowly after review (paracetamol).
- **Urticaria only** → antihistamine; may continue cautiously.
- **Fever/rigors, hypotension, loin/back pain, haemoglobinuria, DIC in the first minutes**
  → **acute haemolytic reaction (ABO incompatibility)**: stop, resuscitate, return unit and
  samples to the lab (DAT, haemolysis screen, repeat group, cultures), renal and
  coagulation monitoring. Check for a second patient involved in the same mix-up.
- **Anaphylaxis** (hypotension, bronchospasm, angio-oedema) → **IM adrenaline** per
  anaphylaxis protocol; investigate IgA deficiency later.
- **Dyspnoea:** **TACO** (hypertension, raised JVP, positive fluid balance, raised BNP,
  responds to diuretics) vs **TRALI** (within 6 h, hypoxia, bilateral infiltrates,
  often **hypotension**, no overload; supportive ventilation; lab investigates donor
  antibodies).
- **High fever + rigors + hypotension** → **bacterial contamination** (platelets
  especially): culture the patient and the bag; broad-spectrum antibiotics.
Report serious reactions to haemovigilance (e.g. SHOT, national systems).

## Gotchas
- TACO is the leading cause of transfusion-related death in recent haemovigilance reports;
  elderly, small, cardiac and renal patients — slow rates, single units, consider diuretic.
- Don't premedicate routinely with antihistamine/steroids; it masks reactions.
- Two units "because it's easier" doubles exposure without a reassessment.
