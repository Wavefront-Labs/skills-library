# Anaemia classification and iron studies workup

> General clinical reference, not patient-specific advice. Reference ranges and ferritin cut-offs vary by laboratory and guideline; iron, B12 and folate replacement regimens must follow the local formulary.

**Goal:** find the cause of anaemia, not just its number — and recognise when
anaemia is the visible edge of something serious (GI cancer, haemolysis, marrow failure).

## 1. First pass
- Confirm the anaemia (Hb below sex- and age-specific range), look at the **full blood
  count** — are white cells and platelets normal? — and **compare with old results**.
- **MCV** sorts the differential; the **reticulocyte count** asks "is the marrow
  responding?" (high = loss or destruction; low = production failure).
- Ask the **blood film** for anything unexpected (see
  `health-medicine.hematology.blood-film-and-marrow-morphology.systematic-blood-film-review-and-urgent-findings`).

## 2. By MCV
**Microcytic (low MCV):** iron deficiency (commonest), thalassaemia trait, anaemia of
inflammation (late), sideroblastic anaemia (rare). **Mentzer index** (MCV ÷ RBC) < 13
hints at thalassaemia trait — confirm with haemoglobin electrophoresis/HPLC, especially
before treating a "non-responder" with iron.

**Normocytic:** acute blood loss, anaemia of inflammation, CKD, mixed deficiency
(micro + macro averaging out — RDW wide), haemolysis, early marrow infiltration, endocrine
(hypothyroidism).

**Macrocytic:** B12 or folate deficiency (megaloblastic, hypersegmented neutrophils),
alcohol, liver disease, hypothyroidism, drugs (methotrexate, hydroxycarbamide,
antiretrovirals), reticulocytosis, **myelodysplasia** (older patient, other cytopenias).

## 3. Interpreting iron studies
- **Ferritin < ~30 µg/L** = iron deficiency in almost any context (some guidelines use
  15 µg/L for strict diagnosis, 30–45 for higher sensitivity — check local).
- Ferritin is an acute-phase protein: in inflammation, CKD, liver disease or cancer it
  can be normal or high despite deficiency — then use **transferrin saturation (TSAT)
  < 20%** with ferritin up to ~100 µg/L (heart failure and CKD guidance uses similar criteria).
- High ferritin with high TSAT → think iron overload (haemochromatosis, transfusion).
- Serum iron alone is unreliable (diurnal and dietary variation).

## 4. Iron deficiency demands a cause
- **Men and postmenopausal women with iron-deficiency anaemia → bidirectional GI
  endoscopy** (OGD and colonoscopy or CT colonography) unless an obvious cause is
  confirmed; **coeliac serology** for all; urinalysis for haematuria.
- Premenopausal women: menstrual history, coeliac screen; GI investigation if over ~50,
  GI symptoms, or family history of colorectal cancer.
- Treat: oral iron (once daily or alternate-day dosing improves absorption and
  tolerance); **IV iron** for intolerance, malabsorption, ongoing loss, late pregnancy,
  CKD/heart failure, or need for rapid repletion. Expect Hb rise of ~20 g/L in 3–4 weeks.

## 5. Other key screens
- **B12/folate:** check both before starting folate alone (giving folate in B12
  deficiency risks neurological deterioration). Pernicious anaemia: intrinsic factor
  antibodies. Neurological signs → treat B12 urgently.
- **Haemolysis screen:** reticulocytes, **LDH** high, **haptoglobin** low, unconjugated
  bilirubin high, **DAT** (autoimmune), film (spherocytes, schistocytes).
  **Schistocytes + thrombocytopenia → treat as TMA (TTP/HUS) emergency.**

## 6. Pancytopenia and urgent referral
Two or more cytopenias, or anaemia with abnormal white cells/platelets → film review and
**same-day haematology discussion** if blasts, neutrophils < 0.5 × 10⁹/L, platelets
< 20–30 × 10⁹/L, or bleeding. Differential: B12/folate deficiency, hypersplenism, marrow
infiltration (leukaemia, lymphoma, myeloma, metastases), **aplastic anaemia**, MDS,
drugs, viral infection (HIV, parvovirus), sepsis. Bone marrow examination is usually needed.

## Gotchas
- Transfused patients' iron studies and haematinics are uninterpretable — send before transfusion.
- A normal Hb doesn't exclude iron deficiency (fatigue, restless legs with low ferritin).
- Iron "non-response": non-adherence, ongoing loss, malabsorption (coeliac, H. pylori,
  PPI), wrong diagnosis (thalassaemia, inflammation).

## Verify
- [ ] MCV category and reticulocyte response stated.
- [ ] Ferritin interpreted against CRP/inflammation; TSAT used where needed.
- [ ] Iron deficiency has a documented cause search (GI, coeliac, menstrual).
- [ ] B12 and folate both checked before folate replacement.
- [ ] Pancytopenia or schistocytes escalated to haematology same day.
