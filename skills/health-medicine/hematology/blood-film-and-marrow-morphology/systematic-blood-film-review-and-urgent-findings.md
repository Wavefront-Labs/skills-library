# Systematic blood film review and urgent findings

> General laboratory/clinical reference. Reporting thresholds, critical-result call lists and grading schemes are laboratory-specific; morphology alone rarely makes a final diagnosis — correlate with the count, clinical picture and confirmatory tests.

**Goal:** read every film the same way so nothing is missed, and recognise the handful
of findings that must be phoned through today.

## 1. Before the microscope
- Check the **FBC and flags** (analyser flags: blasts?, NRBC, platelet clumps, left shift),
  the clinical question, and previous results.
- Film quality: a good **feathered edge**, an even **monolayer** zone where red cells just
  touch, properly stained (Romanowsky — e.g. May-Grünwald-Giemsa or Wright).
  Old EDTA samples (> ~24 h) create artefacts (crenated cells, vacuolated neutrophils).

## 2. Low power (×10–×20)
- Scan the whole film including the **feathered edge and sides**: platelet clumps,
  large abnormal cells and blasts collect there; microfilariae, rouleaux and red cell
  agglutination are visible at low power.
- Estimate white cell density against the count.

## 3. High power / oil (×50–×100) in the monolayer
**Red cells** — size, colour, shape, inclusions:
- Hypochromic microcytes, pencil cells → iron deficiency; target cells + microcytes →
  thalassaemia or liver disease.
- **Spherocytes** → hereditary spherocytosis or autoimmune haemolysis (DAT).
- **Schistocytes (fragments)** → microangiopathy (TTP, HUS, DIC, HELLP, malignant
  hypertension, mechanical valve). Count/estimate per ICSH; ≥ ~1% in the right context
  is significant.
- **Teardrop cells + nucleated red cells + left-shifted myeloid cells**
  (leucoerythroblastic picture) → marrow infiltration or fibrosis.
- **Rouleaux** → paraprotein/inflammation; **agglutination** → cold agglutinins.
- Macro-ovalocytes + hypersegmented neutrophils (≥ 5 lobes) → megaloblastic anaemia.
- Inclusions: Howell-Jolly bodies (hyposplenism), basophilic stippling (lead, thalassaemia),
  Heinz/bite cells (oxidative haemolysis, G6PD), **parasites**.

**White cells** — differential and morphology:
- **Blasts** (high N:C ratio, fine chromatin, nucleoli); **Auer rods** = myeloid.
  Abnormal promyelocytes → APL (emergency).
- Toxic granulation, Döhle bodies, vacuolation → infection/sepsis.
- Mature small lymphocytes with **smudge cells** → CLL; atypical reactive lymphocytes →
  viral infection (EBV); cleaved/villous lymphocytes → lymphoma.
- Dysplastic neutrophils (hypogranular, pseudo-Pelger) → MDS.

**Platelets** — confirm the count:
- Low count with **clumps** at the feathered edge → **EDTA pseudothrombocytopenia**;
  repeat in citrate.
- Giant platelets → inherited macrothrombocytopenia, ITP (young platelets), MPN.

## 4. Same-day action findings
Phone and escalate per lab policy:
- **New blasts** or suspected acute leukaemia; abnormal promyelocytes (APL).
- **Schistocytes with thrombocytopenia** (± anaemia, raised LDH) → TMA/TTP: urgent
  haematology (plasma exchange decision, ADAMTS13 sent before treatment).
- **Malaria** or other parasites → species and parasitaemia %; ≥ 2% (or any *P.
  falciparum* with severity features) needs urgent treatment. Thick and thin films ×3
  over 48 h if first negative and suspicion persists; rapid antigen tests complement but
  don't replace microscopy.
- Leucoerythroblastic picture in a patient without known marrow disease.
- Severe neutropenia with sepsis features, very high counts with leukostasis risk.
- Unexpected spherocytes with rapidly falling Hb (warm AIHA).

## 5. Reporting
Describe what you see in standard terms (ICSH grading), give an integrated comment
("features consistent with microangiopathic haemolysis; suggest LDH, haptoglobin,
ADAMTS13 and urgent clinical review"), and recommend specific next tests rather than
a list of words.

## Gotchas
- Thick areas make normal red cells look spherocytic; thin tail areas flatten cells and
  lose central pallor.
- Smudge cells are increased by delayed processing; some labs add albumin to reduce them.
- Analyser differentials miss blasts at low counts — the film is the safeguard.

## Verify
- [ ] FBC, flags and history reviewed before reading.
- [ ] Low-power scan including feathered edge done.
- [ ] RBC, WBC and platelet morphology each commented on.
- [ ] Platelet count confirmed on film (clumps excluded).
- [ ] Urgent findings phoned and documented with time and recipient.
