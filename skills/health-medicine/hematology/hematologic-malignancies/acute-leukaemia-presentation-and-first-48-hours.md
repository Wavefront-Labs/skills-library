# Acute leukaemia: presentation and the first 48 hours

> General clinical reference, not patient-specific advice. Chemotherapy, ATRA, rasburicase and antimicrobial regimens are specialist decisions dosed from current protocols and the local formulary. Lymphoma pathways: `health-medicine.oncology.hematologic-oncology.lymphoma-diagnosis-staging-and-response`.

**Goal:** suspect acute leukaemia quickly, stop the early killers (bleeding from APL,
leukostasis, tumour lysis, sepsis), and get the diagnostic samples right first time,
because genetics now determine induction therapy.

## 1. Suspect it
- Symptoms of marrow failure: fatigue/anaemia, infections, bruising/bleeding; plus bone
  pain, gum hypertrophy (monocytic AML), lymphadenopathy/mediastinal mass (T-ALL), CNS signs.
- Count: any combination of cytopenias with or without high white count; **circulating
  blasts** on the film. Auer rods → myeloid.
- Same-day haematology referral; **don't wait for a marrow** to start supportive care.

## 2. Emergency 1: acute promyelocytic leukaemia (APL)
- Suspect with **DIC features** (low fibrinogen, prolonged PT, high D-dimer),
  bleeding, and **abnormal promyelocytes** (bilobed nuclei, heavy granules, faggot cells).
- **Start ATRA immediately on suspicion** — don't wait for PML-RARA confirmation. Early
  haemorrhagic death is the main cause of APL mortality.
- Aggressive product support (typical targets: fibrinogen > 1.5 g/L, platelets > 30–50 ×
  10⁹/L — verify against current protocol).
- Watch for **differentiation syndrome** (fever, weight gain, dyspnoea, effusions,
  hypotension, renal impairment) → corticosteroids per protocol.

## 3. Emergency 2: hyperleukocytosis and leukostasis
- WBC/blasts typically > 100 × 10⁹/L (lower in monocytic AML): hypoxia, infiltrates,
  confusion, visual change, priapism.
- **Cytoreduction** (hydroxycarbamide, early induction); leukapheresis in selected
  cases (evidence limited). **Avoid red cell transfusion** before cytoreduction if
  possible (raises viscosity).
- Spurious lab results: pseudohyperkalaemia, pseudohypoxaemia (cells consume O2 in the
  sample) — check with appropriate samples.

## 4. Emergency 3: tumour lysis syndrome (TLS)
- High risk: high WBC, ALL/Burkitt, bulky disease, high LDH, pre-existing renal impairment.
- Labs (Cairo-Bishop): **↑ urate, ↑ K⁺, ↑ phosphate, ↓ Ca²⁺**; clinical TLS adds AKI,
  arrhythmia or seizures.
- Prevention: **IV hydration** with urine output targets, **allopurinol** (intermediate
  risk) or **rasburicase** (high risk/established; contraindicated in **G6PD
  deficiency**). Monitor electrolytes every 6–8 h; renal team early for refractory hyperkalaemia.
- Don't treat asymptomatic hypocalcaemia with calcium (precipitates calcium phosphate).

## 5. Emergency 4: neutropenic sepsis
Fever ≥ 38 °C with neutrophils < 0.5 × 10⁹/L → **empirical broad-spectrum antibiotics
within 1 hour** per local policy; don't wait for the count if neutropenia is likely.

## 6. The diagnostic set (send before treatment where safe)
- **Bone marrow aspirate and trephine:** morphology, **flow cytometry**
  immunophenotype, **cytogenetics/karyotype**, FISH, **molecular panel** (AML: e.g. FLT3,
  NPM1, IDH1/2, CEBPA, TP53; ALL: BCR-ABL1/Philadelphia).
- Peripheral blood can substitute for flow/molecular when circulating blasts are high.
- HLA typing of patient (and siblings) early for transplant candidates.
- Baseline: echocardiogram (anthracyclines), viral serology, pregnancy test, fertility
  discussion, coagulation screen, LP for ALL (per protocol timing).
Genetic results (e.g. **FLT3** → FLT3 inhibitor added; CBF AML → gemtuzumab; Ph+ ALL →
TKI) change induction within days; ELN 2022 risk groups guide post-remission strategy and
transplant.

## Gotchas
- Fit vs unfit for intensive therapy is a key early decision (age, comorbidity, patient
  goals); lower-intensity regimens (e.g. venetoclax-based) exist.
- Platelet thresholds for procedures differ; LP and lines need product cover.
- Central lines in DIC/thrombocytopenia: ultrasound-guided, compressible sites.

## Verify
- [ ] Film reviewed for blasts/Auer rods/promyelocytes the same day.
- [ ] APL suspected → ATRA started and coagulopathy supported.
- [ ] TLS risk graded; hydration and urate-lowering started; electrolytes monitored.
- [ ] Neutropenic fever pathway in place.
- [ ] Marrow with flow, cytogenetics and molecular sent; HLA typing requested where relevant.
