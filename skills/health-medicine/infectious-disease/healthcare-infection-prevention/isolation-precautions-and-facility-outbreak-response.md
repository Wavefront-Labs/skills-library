# Isolation precautions and facility outbreak response

*General professional reference. Precaution categories, PPE specifications and notification duties vary by country and institution — follow your local IPC policy and public-health authority.*

**Goal:** stop organisms moving between patients and staff by matching precautions to
the transmission route, and when transmission happens anyway, find it fast, contain it
and prove it is over.

## 1. Standard precautions — for every patient, every time
Hand hygiene at the WHO **five moments** (before patient contact, before aseptic task,
after body-fluid exposure risk, after patient contact, after touching surroundings);
PPE for anticipated exposure; respiratory hygiene; safe sharps; cleaning of shared
equipment; safe injection practice. Alcohol rub is preferred **except** for
*C. difficile* and norovirus, where soap and water physically removes spores/virus.

## 2. Transmission-based precautions
| Category | Typical organisms | Room | PPE |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Contact** | MRSA, VRE, ESBL/CPE, *C. difficile*, scabies, *Candida auris* | Single room (or cohort) | Gloves + gown on entry; dedicated equipment |
| **Droplet** | Influenza, pertussis, meningococcus (first 24 h of therapy), mumps | Single room or cohort | Surgical mask within close range |
| **Airborne** | TB (pulmonary/laryngeal), measles, varicella/disseminated zoster | Airborne infection isolation room (negative pressure, door closed) | Fit-tested respirator (N95/FFP3) |

- Some pathogens need **combined** precautions (e.g. varicella: airborne + contact;
  SARS-CoV-2 per current local policy; VHF: specialist high-level protocols).
- **Aerosol-generating procedures** may require respirators regardless of category.
- Start precautions on **suspicion** (syndrome-based: diarrhoea → contact; vesicular rash → airborne
  + contact; cough + fever + travel/TB risk → airborne), not on the confirmed result.
- Discontinuation criteria differ (e.g. *C. difficile*: commonly 48 h symptom-free;
  TB: effective therapy plus clinical response/negative smears per policy). Write the criterion on the chart.
- Isolation harms too: fewer visits, more falls and adverse events, worse experience. Review daily.

## 3. Recognising an outbreak
Signals: a cluster above baseline (two or more linked cases of the same organism in
time and place), an unusual organism (*C. auris*, a new carbapenemase), or a single case
of a highly transmissible disease (measles, TB in a staff member). Surveillance data,
the lab and ward staff are the usual detectors — make it easy for them to call.

## 4. Running the investigation (classic steps, done in parallel)
1. **Confirm** it is real: same organism (typing/WGS where available), not a lab or
   reporting artefact (pseudo-outbreak — e.g. a contaminated bronchoscope or reagent).
2. **Convene the outbreak control team**: IPC, microbiology, ward leads, facilities,
   occupational health, communications; public health if notifiable.
3. **Case definition**: person, place, time, and clinical/lab criteria; tiers
   (confirmed / probable / possible).
4. **Line list** and **epidemic curve**: a point-source curve (single sharp peak)
   suggests a common exposure; propagated waves suggest person-to-person spread.
5. **Hypothesis and targeted sampling**: environment (sinks and drains for Gram-negatives,
   shared equipment), staff screening only when epidemiology implicates it.
6. **Control measures** start immediately, before the cause is proven: cohort cases and
   dedicated staff, enhanced cleaning (sporicidal/chlorine for *C. difficile*, norovirus;
   check agent compatibility for *C. auris*), restrict admissions/transfers, screen contacts,
   hand-hygiene and PPE audits.
7. **Communicate** with staff, patients and families; notify regulators as required.
8. **Declare over** after a defined period without new cases (commonly two maximum
   incubation periods; 48-72 h symptom-free for norovirus wards per local policy), then debrief and
   write lessons into policy.

## 5. Device-associated infection prevention (bundles)
- **Central line (CLABSI):** insertion checklist, maximal barrier precautions, chlorhexidine-alcohol
  skin prep, avoid femoral site where possible, daily review of need, scrub the hub.
- **Urinary catheter (CAUTI):** insert only for indication, aseptic insertion, closed drainage,
  bag below bladder, nurse-led removal protocols.
- **VAP:** head of bed 30-45°, daily sedation interruption and spontaneous breathing trial.
Measure compliance with each element, not just infection rates.

## Red flags — escalate to IPC lead immediately
Any carbapenemase producer or *C. auris*, measles or TB exposure on a ward, a single
case of a notifiable disease, more than one similar case in 48 h on one unit, a cluster
linked to a procedure or device.

## Gotchas
- Gloves are not hand hygiene; clean hands after removing them.
- A negative-pressure room with the door open is just a room.
- Sinks are a classic reservoir for CPE outbreaks; do not tip patient fluids into handwash basins.

## Verify
- [ ] Precautions started on clinical suspicion with the reason charted.
- [ ] Room type and PPE match the category; respirator wearers fit-tested.
- [ ] Discontinuation criterion documented and reviewed daily.
- [ ] Outbreak: case definition, line list and epi curve exist and are current.
- [ ] Control measures logged with start dates; end-of-outbreak criterion defined.
- [ ] Notifications to public health made where legally required.
