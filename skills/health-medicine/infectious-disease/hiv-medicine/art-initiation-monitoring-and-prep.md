# HIV: starting ART, monitoring and prevention (PrEP/PEP)

*General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement. HIV guidelines are living documents that change often; confirm regimens, doses and eligibility against current DHHS/EACS/WHO and local guidance.*

**Goal:** get every person with HIV onto suppressive ART quickly and safely, keep the
viral load undetectable, prevent opportunistic infections, and prevent new infections
with PrEP and PEP. Undetectable = untransmittable (U=U) is the message for patients.

## 1. Baseline at diagnosis
- HIV-1/2 confirmation, **CD4 count and %**, **HIV RNA (viral load)**, **genotypic resistance
  test** (reverse transcriptase/protease; integrase genotype if transmitted INSTI resistance
  is suspected).
- **Hepatitis B** (HBsAg, anti-HBs, anti-HBc) — determines whether the regimen must contain
  tenofovir + emtricitabine/lamivudine. Hepatitis C, syphilis, gonorrhoea/chlamydia (all sites),
  TB screening (symptom review ± IGRA), toxoplasma IgG, CMV IgG as indicated.
- Renal function, urinalysis, LFTs, lipids, glucose/HbA1c, FBC, pregnancy test.
- **HLA-B*5701** before any abacavir use.
- If CD4 < 100-200: cryptococcal antigen (CD4 < 100), fundoscopy (CD4 < 50, CMV retinitis) (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).

## 2. Starting ART
- Offer ART to everyone, regardless of CD4. **Rapid / same-day start** is endorsed by
  major guidelines once there are no clinical reasons to wait; the baseline genotype
  result can follow.
- Typical first-line choice: an integrase inhibitor (dolutegravir or bictegravir) with two
  NRTIs, or certain two-drug regimens in selected patients (not with HBV coinfection, high
  viral load or unknown genotype — check current criteria). Single-tablet regimens help adherence.
- **Delay ART** in **cryptococcal meningitis** (typically 4-6 weeks after antifungal start (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked))
  and consider timing carefully in **TB meningitis**; in other TB, start ART within ~2 weeks
  if CD4 < 50, otherwise within 8 weeks (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) (DHHS pages returned 403; verify current guidance).

## 3. Monitoring milestones
| When | Expect |
|---|---|
| 2-8 weeks after start | ≥ 1 log10 fall in viral load (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) |
| ~3-6 months | Viral load < 50 copies/mL (later if very high baseline) (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) |
| Then | Viral load every 3-6 months; extend once stable and adherent (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) |

- A confirmed viral load > 200 copies/mL is virological failure (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked): check adherence,
  interactions, absorption (cation chelation of INSTIs by antacids, iron, calcium), then
  resistance testing while still on the failing regimen.
- Blips (single < 200, then undetectable) are common and usually not failure.

## 4. Opportunistic infection prophylaxis (CD4-driven)
- CD4 < 200 (or oral thrush): *Pneumocystis* prophylaxis, usually TMP-SMX (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
- CD4 < 100 and toxoplasma IgG positive: TMP-SMX covers toxoplasma too (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
- Stop prophylaxis once CD4 recovers and viral load is suppressed on ART per guideline thresholds.

## 5. IRIS
Immune reconstitution inflammatory syndrome: paradoxical worsening of a known OI (TB,
crypto, MAC) or unmasking of an occult one, typically in the first weeks to months of ART,
highest risk with low baseline CD4. Exclude new infection, drug resistance and drug
toxicity first; usually continue ART. Steroids help in some settings (e.g. TB-IRIS) — specialist decision.

## 6. Drug interactions — check every time
Boosters (ritonavir, cobicistat) inhibit CYP3A: dangerous with inhaled/injected
fluticasone or triamcinolone (Cushing's), some statins, certain anticoagulants and
sedatives. Rifampicin lowers many ARVs. Check a dedicated interaction database
(e.g. the Liverpool checker) for every new prescription, including over-the-counter supplements.

## 7. Prevention: PrEP and PEP
- **PrEP**: oral tenofovir-based daily or event-driven (event-driven evidence is for
  anal sex in men who have sex with men (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)), or long-acting injectable cabotegravir/lenacapavir
  where available. Test HIV negative before starting and at each follow-up; monitor renal
  function and STIs; check HBV (stopping tenofovir can flare HBV).
- **PEP**: start as soon as possible, ideally within hours and no later than **72 hours**
  after exposure; 28-day three-drug course (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked); baseline and follow-up HIV testing. Transition
  to PrEP if exposure is ongoing.

## Red flags — urgent specialist input
New CD4 < 200 with respiratory symptoms (PJP), headache or confusion (cryptococcus,
toxoplasma), visual symptoms (CMV), acute HIV syndrome, suspected IRIS, pregnancy, HBV
coinfection when changing regimen, virological failure.

## Gotchas
- Acute HIV may test negative on antibody-only tests; use a 4th-generation Ag/Ab assay or RNA.
- Switching off tenofovir in HBV coinfection can cause a fatal hepatitis flare.
- Dolutegravir and bictegravir modestly raise creatinine by blocking tubular secretion, not by reducing GFR.

## Verify
- [ ] Baseline set done including HBV status, genotype and HLA-B*5701 (if abacavir).
- [ ] ART start date recorded; reasons documented if deferred (crypto/TB meningitis).
- [ ] Viral-load milestones met or failure workup started.
- [ ] OI prophylaxis matches current CD4.
- [ ] Interaction check completed for every co-prescription.
- [ ] Regimen and doses confirmed against the current guideline version.
