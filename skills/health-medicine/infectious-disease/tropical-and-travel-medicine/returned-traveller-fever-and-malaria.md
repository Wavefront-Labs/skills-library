# Fever in the returned traveller, and malaria

*General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement. Antimalarial choice and doses depend on species, severity, pregnancy and local availability — confirm against current national guidance and your ID/tropical service.*

**Goal:** exclude falciparum malaria the same day, recognise severe disease early, and
work through the rest of the differential by geography, exposure and incubation period —
while protecting staff when a viral haemorrhagic fever (VHF) is possible.

## 1. First question: could this be a VHF?
Before routine bloods, ask: travel in the last **21 days** (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) to an area with an active
Ebola, Marburg, Lassa or Crimean-Congo outbreak, or contact with a known case, bodily
fluids, or ill/dead animals? If yes, isolate and follow your national VHF risk-assessment
algorithm and call the infection specialist **before** sending samples to the routine lab.
Most patients flagged this way turn out to have malaria — so malaria testing proceeds under the protocol.

## 2. The travel history that matters
- **Where exactly** (country, region, urban vs rural) and **when** — dates of entry and return.
- **Exposures:** mosquito bites, freshwater swimming (schistosomiasis, leptospirosis),
  animal bites (rabies), tick bites/safari (rickettsia), unboiled water and street food
  (enteric fever), sexual contact, healthcare contact abroad (resistant organisms), visiting
  friends and relatives (VFR travellers carry higher risk — less prophylaxis, longer stays).
- **Prophylaxis and vaccines:** malaria chemoprophylaxis (agent, adherence) reduces but
  never excludes malaria; typhoid and hepatitis A vaccination status.

## 3. Use incubation period to prune the list
| Onset after return | Think |
|---|---|
| < 10 days | Dengue, chikungunya, Zika, rickettsia, influenza/COVID, enteric fever (can be longer), *P. falciparum* (≥ 7 days after first exposure) |
| 10-21 days | Malaria, enteric fever, leptospirosis, acute HIV, acute schistosomiasis (Katayama), VHF window |
| > 21 days | Malaria (especially *P. vivax/ovale* — relapse months later), TB, hepatitis, amoebic liver abscess, visceral leishmaniasis |

Remember the cosmopolitan causes too: pneumonia, UTI, pyelonephritis, cellulitis.

## 4. Malaria: test now, repeat if negative
- Any fever (or history of fever) within ~a year of travel to an endemic area = malaria
  test **on the same day**, with results within hours (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
- **Thick and thin blood films** (thick for detection, thin for species and parasitaemia %)
  plus a **rapid diagnostic test**. One negative set does not exclude it: **repeat every 12-24 h**, three sets in total, before calling it negative (CDC, confirmed). Follow every positive RDT with microscopy (CDC).
- Low platelets with fever after travel to Africa is malaria until proven otherwise (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).

### Severe malaria — assess every falciparum case
Criteria (WHO; summarise, check full list): impaired consciousness or seizures, shock,
respiratory distress/pulmonary oedema, acidosis or lactate > 5 (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked), hypoglycaemia, severe
anaemia, acute kidney injury, jaundice with other organ dysfunction, abnormal bleeding,
and high parasitaemia (UK commonly uses > 2% as a trigger for IV therapy; WHO thresholds differ) (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
- Severe or unable to take oral: **IV artesunate** is first-line (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked); source it urgently
  (some hospitals hold it centrally). Monitor glucose, and watch for delayed haemolysis 1-3 weeks later (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
- Uncomplicated falciparum: an oral artemisinin-combination therapy per national guidance (CDC's current page extends artemether-lumefantrine to 5 days, 10 doses, as of August 2026; confirm in the CDC treatment tables);
  admit initially in non-endemic settings because patients can deteriorate quickly.
- Non-falciparum: treat the blood stage, then consider **primaquine or tafenoquine** to clear
  *vivax/ovale* liver hypnozoites — **G6PD testing first** (haemolysis risk), and avoid in pregnancy.

## 5. Other high-yield differentials
- **Dengue:** fever, myalgia, rash, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia. Warning signs at defervescence
  (abdominal pain, vomiting, bleeding, rising haematocrit) = plasma leak; avoid NSAIDs.
- **Enteric fever:** blood cultures (more than one); stool less sensitive. Rising fluoroquinolone
  and multidrug resistance (e.g. XDR typhoid from Pakistan) shapes empiric choice — check guidance.
- **Rickettsia:** look for an eschar; doxycycline is the usual treatment.
- **Leptospirosis:** freshwater exposure, conjunctival suffusion, myalgia, AKI, jaundice.
- **Acute schistosomiasis:** fever, eosinophilia, freshwater exposure 2-8 weeks earlier.

## 6. Pre-travel consultation (brief)
Itinerary-based risk assessment, routine plus travel vaccines (hepatitis A, typhoid, yellow
fever certification where required, rabies, Japanese encephalitis), malaria chemoprophylaxis
matched to region and resistance, bite avoidance, and a plan to seek care for fever after return.

## Red flags
Impaired consciousness, any severe-malaria criterion, haemorrhage, VHF risk, shock,
eosinophilia with neurological signs, pregnancy with malaria. Involve ID/tropical medicine early.

## Gotchas
- "I took my prophylaxis" does not rule out malaria.
- Fever can be absent at presentation; the history of fever counts.
- Malaria and bacteraemia can coexist (especially non-typhoidal *Salmonella* in children).

## Verify
- [ ] VHF risk assessed before routine samples.
- [ ] Malaria films/RDT resulted the same day; three negative sets before excluding.
- [ ] Severe-malaria criteria checked and recorded for every positive case.
- [ ] G6PD checked before primaquine/tafenoquine.
- [ ] Differential tied to region, exposure and incubation period.
- [ ] Treatment confirmed with current national guidance and ID/tropical service.
