# Infective endocarditis: workup and management

*General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement. Antimicrobial regimens and durations depend on organism, valve type and current ESC/AHA guidance — confirm doses with your ID team, pharmacist and local formulary.*

**Goal:** suspect it early, prove it with properly taken cultures and imaging, and manage
it as a team — because the decisions that save lives in endocarditis are often surgical
and time-critical, not just the choice of antibiotic.

## 1. Who to suspect
- Fever + new or changed murmur; fever + prosthetic valve, cardiac device or prior IE.
- **Any *Staphylococcus aureus* bacteraemia** (IE in a meaningful minority); enterococcal or
  viridans/*S. gallolyticus* bacteraemia without another source (S. gallolyticus → colonoscopy).
- Fever + embolic events: stroke in a young patient, splenic/renal infarcts, septic
  pulmonary emboli (right-sided IE in people who inject drugs), vertebral osteomyelitis.
- Subacute: weight loss, night sweats, anaemia, splinter haemorrhages, raised ESR/CRP, haematuria.

## 2. Blood cultures before antibiotics
- **Three sets** from separate venepunctures, adequately filled (adult ~ 20-30 mL per set),
  ideally before the first dose. In a stable subacute patient, it is reasonable to wait for
  cultures rather than treat blindly.
- Persistently positive cultures are the hallmark of an endovascular source.
- **Culture-negative IE** (prior antibiotics most common): serology for *Coxiella* and
  *Bartonella*, consider *Brucella*, *Tropheryma whipplei*, fungi; send valve tissue for
  16S rRNA PCR and histology if operated.

## 3. Imaging
- **TTE first** in all suspected cases; **TEE** if TTE is negative but suspicion remains,
  for prosthetic valves or devices, for *S. aureus* bacteraemia (most), and to assess
  complications (abscess, fistula, perforation) before surgery.
- Repeat TEE in 5-7 days if the first is negative and suspicion persists.
- **[18F]FDG-PET/CT** (prosthetic valves > ~3 months post-implant, devices) and cardiac CT add
  diagnostic criteria in current guidelines; brain imaging and abdominal imaging look for emboli.

## 4. Diagnosis: modified Duke criteria
Major: typical organism in persistent blood cultures (or *Coxiella* serology), and imaging
evidence of endocardial involvement. Minor: predisposition, fever, vascular phenomena,
immunological phenomena, microbiological evidence short of major. Definite = 2 major, or
1 major + 3 minor, or 5 minor; possible = 1 major + 1 minor, or 3 minor. The 2023
Duke-ISCVID update adds organisms, PET/CT and intraoperative findings — check which version your service uses.

## 5. Management framework
- **Endocarditis team** (cardiology, cardiac surgery, ID/microbiology, imaging, neurology as needed)
  — associated with lower mortality in guidelines.
- Antimicrobials are **bactericidal, high-dose and prolonged**: commonly 4-6 weeks,
  counted from the first negative blood culture (or surgery, when cultures of the valve are positive).
  Regimens differ for native vs prosthetic valve, staphylococci vs streptococci vs
  enterococci; prosthetic staphylococcal IE typically adds rifampicin (once bacteraemia clears) ± gentamicin.
- Repeat blood cultures every 24-48 h until negative; document the first negative date.
- **Surgical triggers** (discuss urgently): heart failure from valve dysfunction (emergency),
  uncontrolled infection (abscess, fistula, enlarging vegetation, persistent bacteraemia
  despite appropriate therapy, fungal or multidrug-resistant organism), and embolic
  prevention (large vegetation, e.g. ≥ 10 mm with emboli; thresholds in current guidelines).
- Neurological complications change timing of surgery and anticoagulation — neurology/neurosurgery input.
- **Oral step-down:** POET supported partial oral therapy in stable left-sided IE with
  selected organisms after ≥ 10 days IV; 2023 ESC guidance accepts it for selected patients.
- **OPAT** when stable: line plan, weekly bloods (FBC, renal, LFTs, CRP, drug levels),
  named responsible clinician.
- Dental review and source control; addiction services and harm reduction for people who inject drugs.

## 6. The deep-infection cousins (same principles)
Vertebral osteomyelitis and prosthetic-joint infection share the playbook: get tissue
before antibiotics if the patient is stable (biopsy or multiple intraoperative samples),
long targeted courses, and surgery decides more than the drug (debridement, implant retention vs exchange).

## Red flags
New heart failure, conduction block on ECG (aortic root abscess), stroke or new neurology,
persistent bacteraemia > 72 h on therapy, prosthetic valve IE, fungal IE.

## Gotchas
- Starting antibiotics before cultures in a stable patient can make the diagnosis impossible.
- A negative TTE does not exclude IE, especially with prosthetic valves.
- Daily ECG can catch an evolving root abscess (PR prolongation).

## Verify
- [ ] Three culture sets before antibiotics (or reason documented).
- [ ] TTE done; TEE indication assessed and recorded.
- [ ] Duke classification documented with criteria listed.
- [ ] Endocarditis team review and surgical indication decision recorded.
- [ ] First negative culture date and planned end date written.
- [ ] Regimen and doses verified against current guideline and formulary.
