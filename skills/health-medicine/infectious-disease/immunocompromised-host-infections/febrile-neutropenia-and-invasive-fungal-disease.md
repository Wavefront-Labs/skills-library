# Febrile neutropenia and invasive fungal disease in the immunocompromised host

*General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement. Antimicrobial and antifungal choices, doses and level targets must be checked against current local haematology/transplant protocols and formulary.*

**Goal:** treat fever in a neutropenic patient as an emergency, then think in terms of
*which* immune defect the patient has, because that predicts the organisms — and the
signs of infection are often muted or absent.

## 1. Think "net state of immunosuppression"
| Defect | Typical cause | Organisms to fear |
|---|---|---|
| Neutropenia | Chemotherapy, marrow failure | Gram-negatives incl. *Pseudomonas*, viridans strep, *Candida*, *Aspergillus* (prolonged) |
| T-cell/cellular | Transplant immunosuppression, steroids, HIV, purine analogues | CMV, HSV/VZV, PJP, *Cryptococcus*, *Nocardia*, *Listeria*, TB, endemic fungi |
| Humoral / asplenia | Myeloma, CLL, rituximab, splenectomy | Encapsulated bacteria (pneumococcus, *H. influenzae*, meningococcus) |
| Barrier breach | Mucositis, central lines | Skin flora, gut organisms, *Candida* |

Transplant timeline helps: first month = surgical/nosocomial; 1-6 months = opportunists
(CMV, PJP) unless prophylaxis; later = community organisms, unless rejection needs more immunosuppression.

## 2. Febrile neutropenia — the emergency pathway
Definitions vary (commonly temperature ≥ 38.0-38.3 °C, and neutrophils < 0.5 × 10⁹/L or
expected to fall below). Many units treat **suspected** neutropenic sepsis on clinical
grounds without waiting for the count.
1. Assess and resuscitate; blood cultures (peripheral + each line lumen), urine, sputum,
   swabs of any lesion, chest imaging if symptomatic, lactate.
2. **Empiric IV antipseudomonal beta-lactam within 1 hour of arrival** (door-to-needle is
   an audited standard in many systems). Add Gram-positive cover (e.g. vancomycin) only for
   specific indications: haemodynamic instability, suspected line or skin/soft-tissue infection,
   pneumonia, MRSA colonisation, Gram-positive in blood. Follow local regimen.
3. Risk-stratify: the **MASCC** score (≥ 21 = low risk) or **CISNE** for solid-tumour
   outpatients identifies patients who may be managed with oral therapy/early discharge per protocol.
4. Persistent fever 4-7 days on broad antibiotics with expected ongoing neutropenia →
   invasive fungal workup (below) and empiric or pre-emptive antifungal strategy per local protocol.
5. Duration: continue until afebrile and recovering counts, or per protocol allowing
   earlier stopping when afebrile and clinically well (practice is shifting — check current evidence).

## 3. Invasive fungal disease (IFD) workup
- **High-resolution CT chest** early: nodules, halo sign, air-crescent (late), reverse halo
  (suggests mucormycosis).
- **Serum galactomannan** (*Aspergillus*) — lower sensitivity on mould-active prophylaxis;
  BAL galactomannan improves yield. **Beta-D-glucan** — broad (not mucorales or cryptococcus),
  prone to false positives (IVIG, albumin, haemodialysis membranes, gauze).
- Bronchoscopy with BAL (culture, galactomannan, PCR, PJP testing); biopsy when safe.
- Classify per **EORTC/MSGERC** definitions (proven / probable / possible) — needed for trials,
  useful for clinical communication.
- Mould-active azoles (voriconazole, posaconazole, isavuconazole) need **therapeutic drug
  monitoring** and interaction checks (strong CYP3A4 inhibitors — tacrolimus, sirolimus,
  vincristine, venetoclax). Mucormycosis needs a different agent (amphotericin-based first line)
  plus urgent surgical debridement. Suspected mucor is a same-day ENT/surgical referral.

## 4. CMV in transplant
Two strategies: universal **prophylaxis** vs **pre-emptive** monitoring with quantitative PCR and
treatment above a threshold. Highest risk: donor-positive / recipient-negative solid organ
transplants; recipient-positive allogeneic HSCT. Thresholds are assay- and centre-specific.

## 5. Prophylaxis strategy (summary)
PJP prophylaxis for transplant recipients and patients on prolonged high-dose steroids or
certain chemotherapy; antiviral (aciclovir/valaciclovir) for HSV/VZV in HSCT and some regimens;
mould-active azole prophylaxis for AML induction and GvHD; hepatitis B screening and
antiviral prophylaxis before rituximab or other anti-CD20 therapy.

## Red flags
Hypotension or rising lactate in neutropenia; sinus pain, facial numbness or black eschar
(mucor); new neurological signs; pneumonitis with hypoxia; perianal pain (avoid rectal exam/temperature).

## Gotchas
- No neutrophils → no pus, minimal infiltrate, often no meningism; fever may be the only sign.
- Steroids mask fever entirely.
- Drug-level interactions between azoles and calcineurin inhibitors cause rejection or toxicity within days.

## Verify
- [ ] Immune defect(s) named and linked to the differential.
- [ ] Febrile neutropenia: cultures + empiric antibiotic given within 1 h; time recorded.
- [ ] MASCC/CISNE documented before any outpatient pathway.
- [ ] Persistent fever triggered CT chest and fungal biomarkers.
- [ ] Azole levels and interactions checked.
- [ ] Prophylaxis reviewed against current protocol.
