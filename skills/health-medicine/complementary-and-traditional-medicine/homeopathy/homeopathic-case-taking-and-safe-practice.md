# Homeopathic case taking and safe practice

*General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.*

**Goal:** describe how homeopaths take a case and select a remedy, while ensuring the consultation never delays effective care and the patient consents knowing what the evidence shows.

## 1. The evidence, stated plainly
Major systematic reviews (NHMRC 2015; UK House of Commons Science and Technology Committee 2010) found no reliable evidence that homeopathy is effective for any health condition beyond placebo. Remedies diluted beyond about 12C are unlikely to contain a single molecule of the original substance. NHS England advises against routine prescribing. Any practice must be framed by this: the main risks of homeopathy are indirect — delayed diagnosis, abandoned effective treatment, and avoided vaccination.

## 2. Consent and claims
- Tell the patient the evidence position above before treatment and record that you did.
- Do not claim to treat, cure or prevent serious disease (cancer, infection, diabetes, asthma, mental illness); advertising law in many countries (e.g. UK CAP Code) prohibits such claims.
- Never offer "homeoprophylaxis" or nosodes as alternatives to vaccination, or remedies as malaria prophylaxis.

## 3. Red-flag screen — refer, do not "treat"
Fever in an infant, breathing difficulty, chest pain, signs of sepsis or meningitis, dehydration, unexplained weight loss or bleeding, new neurological symptoms, suicidal thoughts, any pregnancy complication. Also refer when there is no medical diagnosis for persistent symptoms. Ask whether the patient is under medical care and whether they have changed any prescribed medicine — advise them not to stop.

## 4. Case taking (as practitioners do it)
A 60–90 min first consultation records:
- Presenting symptoms with **modalities** (what makes better/worse: time, temperature, motion, position).
- **Mentals/generals:** temperament, fears, sleep, thirst, food desires, temperature preference.
- Strange, rare and peculiar symptoms, which homeopathic texts weight highest.
- Patient's own words, verbatim.

## 5. Repertorisation and selection
1. Choose 5–10 characteristic rubrics from a repertory (e.g. Kent) or software.
2. Tally remedies covering the most rubrics by grade.
3. Differentiate the top 3–5 by reading materia medica.
4. Potency (practitioner convention): low (6C–30C) for local/acute, higher or LM potencies for constitutional prescribing; usually single dose or short course, then wait and observe.

## 6. Product safety
- Low dilutions (mother tinctures, 1X–4X) may contain pharmacologically active or toxic material (e.g. belladonna, arsenic, nux vomica); check them like herbal products.
- Teething products with variable belladonna content have prompted FDA warnings; avoid in infants.
- Lactose/alcohol vehicles matter for intolerance or recovery.
- Buy from regulated manufacturers only.

## 7. Follow-up
Review at 4–6 weeks. If symptoms worsen ("aggravation"), do not reassure — reassess medically. If no improvement, say so and encourage medical review.

## Gotchas
- Natural fluctuation and regression to the mean create apparent responses; do not interpret improvement as proof.
- Children and vulnerable adults cannot weigh the evidence themselves; be especially cautious.
- Record every referral and the patient's response.

## Verify
- [ ] Evidence statement given and documented before treatment.
- [ ] Red flags screened; medical care status and medications recorded.
- [ ] No claims about serious disease or vaccination alternatives.
- [ ] Low-dilution products checked for active content.
- [ ] Follow-up and escalation plan recorded.
