# Naturopathic case taking to treatment plan

*General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.*

**Goal:** in a 60–90 minute first visit, build a whole-person picture, decide what must go to a medical practitioner, and leave the patient with a prioritised, measurable plan that works alongside — never instead of — their medical care.

## 1. Case history
| Domain | Ask |
|---|---|
| Presenting complaint | Onset, course, severity (0–10), what helps/worsens, prior diagnoses and tests |
| Systems review | Head-to-toe, including digestion, sleep, energy, mood, menstrual/reproductive |
| Medical | Diagnoses, medications, supplements, allergies, surgeries, family history |
| Lifestyle | 3-day diet recall, alcohol, tobacco, caffeine, movement, sleep hours, stress, work, relationships |
| Environment | Occupation exposures, housing (damp/mould) |
| Goals | What the patient wants to change, and by when |

Record a baseline outcome measure (MYMOP, or condition-specific tool).

## 2. Referral judgement — decide before planning
Refer to GP/emergency care for red flags: unexplained weight loss, bleeding, chest pain, breathlessness, new neurological deficit, lumps, persistent fever, suicidal ideation, signs of eating disorder, pregnancy complications, and any child who is unwell. Also refer when symptoms have no medical diagnosis yet. Stay within your scope and jurisdiction (naturopathic regulation varies widely — licensed primary-care NDs in some US states/Canada, voluntary registration elsewhere).

## 3. Apply the therapeutic order
Work from least to most invasive:
1. Establish conditions for health: sleep, diet, movement, stress, connection.
2. Stimulate self-healing: e.g. hydrotherapy, exercise progression.
3. Support weakened systems: targeted nutrition.
4. Address specific pathology: botanicals, supplements.
5. Refer for pharmaceutical or surgical care when needed.

## 4. Write the plan
- No more than 3–5 changes at the first visit; each as a SMART goal ("walk 20 min after dinner 5 days/week").
- Diet changes based on recall, not blanket exclusion diets; consult a dietitian for medically complex needs.
- Botanicals/supplements: give name, form, dose, duration, reason, and check interactions for each prescription drug (e.g. St John's wort with SSRIs/contraceptives; high-dose fish oil with anticoagulants). Doses: follow monograph or product label (check current references).
- Supplements with testing basis only — test vitamin D, iron, B12 via standard laboratory tests where indicated, not by unvalidated methods.

## 5. Follow-up
Review at 2–6 weeks: outcome measure versus baseline, adherence, adverse effects. Change one variable at a time. If no meaningful change after an agreed trial, reconsider and refer.

## Gotchas
- Avoid unvalidated tests (IgG food intolerance panels, live-blood analysis, hair mineral analysis for diagnosis, iridology): they mislead diagnosis and cost the patient.
- Never advise stopping prescribed medicines or vaccinations; liaise with the prescriber.
- Supplement stacks grow; audit the total list every visit.
- Be explicit about evidence levels: strong for lifestyle change, variable for most botanicals.

## Verify
- [ ] Full history incl. medications and supplements recorded.
- [ ] Red flags screened; referrals made and documented.
- [ ] Plan follows therapeutic order with ≤5 SMART goals.
- [ ] Interaction check per product.
- [ ] Baseline measure and review date set.
