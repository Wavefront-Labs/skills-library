# Safe acupuncture treatment session

*General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.*

**Goal:** deliver a needling session that is clinically indicated, consented, technically clean and safe, with an outcome you can measure, and recognise when the patient needs a doctor instead.

## 1. Screen before you needle
- **Red flags → refer, do not treat first:** unexplained weight loss, night pain unrelieved by position, fever with back pain, new neurological deficit, saddle anaesthesia or bladder/bowel change, chest pain, suspected DVT, history of cancer with new pain.
- **Relative contraindications / precautions:** anticoagulants or bleeding disorders (shallow needling, pressure on withdrawal); pregnancy (traditionally avoid LI4, SP6, BL60, BL67 and lumbosacral/abdominal points — the evidence for harm is weak, but follow your professional body's code); pacemaker (no electroacupuncture); lymphoedematous limb; uncontrolled epilepsy; needle phobia; immunosuppression; broken or infected skin at the site.
- Record medications, allergies (nickel), and a baseline outcome measure (0–10 NRS pain, or a validated tool such as MYMOP).

## 2. Evidence and consent
- State the evidence plainly: meta-analyses show acupuncture outperforms sham and usual care for some chronic pain (back, neck, osteoarthritis, headache), with modest differences over sham. NICE NG193 permits a course for chronic primary pain; NG59 does not recommend it for low back pain (verify current guidance in your jurisdiction).
- Consent covers: bruising, transient pain, drowsiness/fainting, rare pneumothorax and infection; number of sessions (typically a trial of 4–6, then review).

## 3. Point selection
Combine (a) local points at the painful area, (b) distal points on the involved channel, (c) pattern-based points from the TCM diagnosis (see `health-medicine.complementary-and-traditional-medicine.traditional-chinese-medicine-diagnosis.four-examinations-pattern-differentiation`), and (d) palpated tender (ashi) points. Typical session: 6–12 needles. Write the point list in the notes before needling.

## 4. Clean-needle technique
1. Hand hygiene; clean field; sterile single-use needles only, never reused.
2. Skin cleaning per local code (alcohol swab where required; let dry).
3. Insert with guide tube or freehand; do not touch the needle shaft.
4. Seek de qi (dull ache, heaviness) only as tolerated.
5. Sharps straight into a sharps bin; count needles in and out — record the count.

## 5. Safe depths over danger areas
| Region | Risk | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Thorax, supraclavicular fossa, GB21, upper back points (BL lateral line) | Pneumothorax | Oblique/transverse needling; shallow depth scaled to body habitus — thin patients may have under 1–2 cm to the pleura |
| ST9, neck | Carotid | Palpate and avoid the artery |
| CV points over a full bladder or pregnancy | Viscera | Empty bladder first; avoid in pregnancy |
| Eye region (BL1, ST1) | Haematoma | Fine needle, no manipulation, press on withdrawal |

## 6. Retention and adjuncts
- Retention 15–30 min; check the patient regularly; first session lying down (fainting risk).
- **Moxibustion:** keep a barrier or distance; never on numb skin or diabetic feet; avoid face; ventilate.
- **Cupping:** expect circular bruising (document and warn — may be mistaken for injury); no cupping over varicose veins, broken skin or in anticoagulated patients without care.
- **Electroacupuncture:** never across the chest/heart, never with a pacemaker.

## 7. Adverse events
- **Fainting:** remove needles, lie flat, legs raised, monitor.
- **Suspected pneumothorax** (pleuritic pain, breathlessness hours after thoracic needling): send to emergency care; it can present late.
- Needle-stick to the practitioner: follow the bloodborne-virus exposure protocol.
- Report serious events to your regulator/professional body.

## Gotchas
- Pneumothorax is the most-reported serious harm and is avoidable with depth control.
- Broken needles: never push a fragment deeper; mark the site and refer.
- Improvement must be judged against the baseline measure, not impression; stop after an unsuccessful trial.
- Do not use acupuncture to delay assessment of an undiagnosed condition.

## Verify
- [ ] Red-flag screen and medications documented; contraindications checked.
- [ ] Consent recorded including pneumothorax risk and course length.
- [ ] Point list, needle count in/out and depths over the thorax noted.
- [ ] Baseline and follow-up outcome scores recorded; review at 4–6 sessions.
- [ ] Adverse events (bruising, fainting) logged and reported where required.
