# Classical formula selection and modification

*General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.*

**Goal:** turn a TCM pattern diagnosis into a written, individually modified formula that is safe with the patient's medicines, sourced from tested supply, and reviewed at a set interval.

## 1. Prerequisites
- A pattern statement and treatment principle (`health-medicine.complementary-and-traditional-medicine.traditional-chinese-medicine-diagnosis.four-examinations-pattern-differentiation`).
- Biomedical diagnosis known or red flags excluded; full medication and supplement list; pregnancy/breastfeeding status; liver and kidney history.

## 2. Select the base formula
Match the formula's classical indication (pattern, tongue, pulse) to the patient, not the disease name. Examples of common pairings (from Formulas & Strategies):

| Pattern | Base formula |
|---|---|
| Spleen qi deficiency | Si Jun Zi Tang |
| Liver qi stagnation with blood deficiency | Xiao Yao San |
| Kidney yin deficiency | Liu Wei Di Huang Wan |
| Exterior wind-heat | Yin Qiao San |

Read the formula's structure: **jun** (chief), **chen** (deputy), **zuo** (assistant), **shi** (envoy). Modifications must not remove the chief.

## 3. Modify
- Add 1–4 herbs for secondary patterns or symptoms; remove herbs whose action is not needed or that are contraindicated.
- Adjust doses to the pattern's emphasis. Typical raw-herb daily totals are of the order of 60–150 g for decoction; granule extracts use much smaller gram doses by concentration ratio (check supplier ratio and current references — dosing varies widely by text and form).
- Keep the total formula commonly to 8–15 herbs; more herbs means more interaction risk.

## 4. Safety screen (do every time)
- **Banned/toxic:** never Aristolochia-containing herbs (nephrotoxic, carcinogenic; risk of substitution in Fang Ji, Mu Tong). Ma Huang (ephedra) and unprocessed Fu Zi (aconite) are restricted in many jurisdictions; check local law.
- **Interactions:** anticoagulants/antiplatelets with blood-moving herbs (Dan Shen, Dang Gui, Hong Hua) — reports of raised INR with warfarin; Gan Cao (liquorice) with antihypertensives, diuretics, digoxin (hypokalaemia, hypertension); herbs with sedatives; immunosuppressants. Check a dedicated interaction reference.
- **Pregnancy:** avoid blood-moving, purgative and toxic herbs; many practitioners decline to prescribe in the first trimester.
- **Hepatotoxicity:** reports with He Shou Wu (Polygonum multiflorum) among others; baseline and periodic liver function tests for long courses or at-risk patients.
- **Endangered species:** no CITES-listed animal or plant products; use substitutes.

## 5. Supply and preparation
- Buy only from suppliers providing certificates of analysis (identity, heavy metals, pesticides, microbial, aflatoxin).
- Decoction: soak 30 min, simmer 20–40 min; note herbs needing special handling (decoct first, add near end). Write the method on the label.
- Label: patient name, formula, herbs and amounts, dose, duration, practitioner contact.

## 6. Review
Review in 1–2 weeks for acute, 2–4 weeks for chronic; reassess the pattern and modify. Stop and refer on jaundice, dark urine, rash, palpitations or worsening symptoms. Report suspected adverse reactions via the national scheme (e.g. MHRA Yellow Card in the UK).

## Gotchas
- Pinyin names map to several species; prescribe with Latin pharmaceutical names to avoid substitution.
- Clinical trial evidence for most classical formulas is limited or low quality; say so when consenting.
- Patented pills from unregulated sources have been found adulterated with pharmaceuticals (steroids, sildenafil) and heavy metals.
- Patients often do not mention herbs to their doctor; encourage them to.

## Verify
- [ ] Formula matches the documented pattern; chief herb retained.
- [ ] Interaction, pregnancy, toxicity and CITES checks recorded.
- [ ] Supplier certificate of analysis on file.
- [ ] Label and preparation instructions complete.
- [ ] Review date set; LFT plan where indicated; patient told which symptoms mean stop.
