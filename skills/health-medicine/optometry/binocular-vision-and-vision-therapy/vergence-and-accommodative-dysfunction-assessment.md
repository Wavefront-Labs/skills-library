# Vergence and accommodative dysfunction assessment

**Goal:** turn near-work symptoms (eyestrain, headaches, intermittent blur or diplopia, words moving) into a classified diagnosis and a treatment choice backed by measurements.

## 1. Symptoms and exclusions
- Use a validated questionnaire such as the **CISS** (Convergence Insufficiency Symptom Survey) to quantify and track symptoms.
- First correct significant refractive error, and exclude strabismus, paresis (incomitance), and pathology. Acquired or incomitant deviation, new diplopia, or headache with neurological signs need medical referral.

## 2. Core measurements (with full correction)
| Test | What it gives |
|---|---|
| Cover test / alternating prism cover test, distance and near | Size and direction of phoria; recovery quality |
| **Near point of convergence** (repeat several times, note fatigue) | Remote NPC suggests CI; reference values vary (around 5-6 cm commonly used) |
| **Fusional (vergence) reserves** with prism bar or rotary prisms | Blur/break/recovery, positive and negative, distance and near |
| **Amplitude of accommodation** (push-up or minus-lens) | Compare with Hofstetter minimum: 15 - 0.25 x age |
| **Accommodative facility** (+/-2.00 D flippers) | Infacility (norms depend on age and method) |
| Accommodative lag (MEM or Nott retinoscopy) | High lag = insufficiency; low/lead = excess |
| AC/A ratio (calculated or gradient) | Guides classification and lens/prism choice |
| **Fixation disparity / associated phoria** (e.g. Mallett unit) | Indicates decompensation |

## 3. Classify
- **Convergence insufficiency**: exophoria greater at near, remote NPC, reduced positive fusional reserves at near, low AC/A.
- **Convergence excess**: esophoria greater at near, high AC/A, often with accommodative lag.
- **Divergence insufficiency/excess**: deviation greater at distance (rule out sixth nerve palsy for new eso at distance).
- **Accommodative insufficiency**: amplitude below age minimum, high lag, difficulty with minus on flippers.
- **Accommodative excess/infacility**: difficulty relaxing (plus) or switching.
- **Decompensated heterophoria**: symptoms plus failure of compensation criteria.

## 4. Compensation criteria
- **Sheard**: compensating fusional reserve (to blur, or break if no blur) should be at least twice the phoria. Most useful for exophoria.
- **Percival**: the operating point should lie in the middle third of the total vergence range. Most useful for esophoria.
- Aligning prism from fixation disparity is a practical prism estimate.

## 5. Choose management
| Diagnosis | First-line options |
|---|---|
| Symptomatic CI (children) | Office-based vergence/accommodative therapy with home reinforcement had the strongest evidence in CITT; home pencil push-ups alone performed less well |
| Convergence excess / high AC/A | Near plus addition (single vision readers or bifocals) |
| Accommodative insufficiency | Plus at near, accommodative therapy |
| Decompensated phoria not responding | Relieving prism (base in for exo, base out for eso), often split between eyes |
| Vertical phoria | Small vertical prism often well tolerated |

Re-measure the same parameters and the symptom score after a defined period (often several weeks) to judge response.

## Gotchas
- Uncorrected hyperopia or anisometropia mimics accommodative and vergence problems; refract first, with cycloplegia in children.
- A single NPC measurement can look normal; repeated measures reveal fatigue.
- Reading difficulty is not diagnostic of a binocular anomaly; avoid overstating vision therapy for learning difficulties.
- Prism can reduce fusional reserves over time (prism adaptation); use the smallest effective amount.

## Verify
- [ ] Refractive error corrected and strabismus/incomitance/pathology excluded.
- [ ] Phoria, NPC, reserves and accommodation measured at near and distance.
- [ ] Diagnosis matches a recognised pattern; Sheard/Percival applied.
- [ ] Symptom questionnaire baseline recorded.
- [ ] Management plan has a review date and the same outcome measures.
