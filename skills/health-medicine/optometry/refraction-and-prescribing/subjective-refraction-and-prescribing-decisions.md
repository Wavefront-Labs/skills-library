# Subjective refraction and prescribing decisions

**Goal:** find the most plus (least minus) correction that gives best acuity with comfortable binocular vision, then prescribe what will actually help the patient, not just the measured number.

## 1. Starting point
- **Retinoscopy** (working distance usually 67 cm, so subtract +1.50 D working-lens allowance) or autorefraction; compare with the habitual prescription.
- Consider **cycloplegic refraction** for children, suspected latent hyperopia, accommodative esotropia, or variable endpoints (agent and dosing per local protocol).

## 2. Monocular subjective (fog the other eye or occlude)
1. **Best vision sphere**: fog with plus, reduce plus in 0.25 D steps until acuity stops improving. Stop at the **maximum plus for best acuity**; "smaller and darker" means over-minussed.
2. **Duochrome** as a check: equal or slightly red-favoured endpoint; less reliable with reduced acuity or colour deficiency.
3. **Jackson cross-cylinder**: refine **axis first** (straddle the axis, rotate toward the preferred position), then **power** (keep spherical equivalent constant by changing sphere 0.25 D for each 0.50 D cylinder change).
4. Recheck sphere after cylinder.

## 3. Binocular balance
- Methods: alternate occlusion, **Humphriss immediate contrast** (fog one eye +0.75 to +1.00 D), prism dissociation, or polarised charts.
- Aim for equal clarity or let the dominant eye be marginally clearer; then binocular +0.25 D check to avoid over-minus.

## 4. Near addition
- Start from age-typical values, then refine with tentative add plus **range of clear vision** at the patient's actual working distance(s).
- Prescribe the **lowest add** that gives comfortable sustained reading with a centred range; too strong an add shortens the range.

## 5. Prescribing judgement
| Situation | Typical approach |
|---|---|
| Small change from habitual (around 0.25 D) and no symptoms | Often do not change |
| Large cylinder or axis change in an adult | Consider partial change; warn about adaptation |
| Anisometropia (roughly >2 D) | Watch for aniseikonia and prismatic imbalance; consider contact lenses |
| Low hyperopia, asymptomatic adult | Often not needed |
| Children | Follow age-based prescribing guidance (thresholds vary between guidelines) |
| Presbyopia | Discuss single vision, bifocal, progressive (PAL), occupational lenses |

## 6. Non-tolerance troubleshooting
1. **Verify the spectacles**: focimeter power and axis, prism, optical centres vs PD, segment/fitting cross height, base curve change, frame tilt and vertex distance.
2. **Recheck the refraction**, especially over-minus, cylinder axis and add.
3. Ask about the symptom pattern: blur at distance (over-plus/axis), swim or distortion (cylinder change, PAL fitting), headache (over-minus, vertical prism), diplopia (decentration-induced prism).
4. Consider binocular vision anomalies and ocular pathology if the numbers are right.

## Gotchas
- Over-minussing young patients is the most common error; always push plus.
- Changing JCC power without adjusting sphere shifts the circle of least confusion.
- Base curve or lens material changes can cause complaints even with identical prescriptions.
- Prentice's rule: decentration (cm) x power (D) = prism dioptres; anisometropes feel vertical prism when reading through PALs.

## Verify
- [ ] Endpoint is maximum plus for best acuity in each eye.
- [ ] Cylinder axis refined before power; spherical equivalent maintained.
- [ ] Binocular balance and final binocular plus check done.
- [ ] Add refined at the patient's real working distance.
- [ ] Prescribing decision considers habitual Rx, symptoms and adaptation.
- [ ] For non-tolerance, spectacles verified before re-refracting.
