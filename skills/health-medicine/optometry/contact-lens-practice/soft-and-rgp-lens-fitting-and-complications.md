# Soft and RGP lens fitting and complications

**Goal:** choose and fit a lens that is safe, comfortable and gives good vision, then catch complications early at aftercare.

## 1. Pre-fit assessment
- History: motivation, wearing schedule, hygiene, swimming/showering, previous lens problems, allergy, dry eye.
- Refraction (vertex-correct powers roughly above +/-4.00 D), **keratometry or topography**, HVID, pupil size, lid position and tension.
- Slit lamp baseline: lid eversion (papillae), corneal staining, limbal vessels, tear film (TBUT, meniscus). Treat blepharitis/dry eye first.
- Contraindications to address: active anterior eye disease, poor hygiene/compliance, uncontrolled dry eye.

## 2. Choose the modality
| Need | Usual option |
|---|---|
| Low/moderate sphere, occasional wear | Daily disposable (lowest infection and care burden) |
| Astigmatism (often >= 0.75 D) | Soft toric, or RGP |
| Presbyopia | Multifocal soft/RGP, or monovision |
| Irregular cornea (keratoconus, post-graft) | RGP, hybrid or scleral |
| Severe ocular surface disease | Scleral lens (specialist) |

Silicone hydrogels give higher oxygen transmissibility (Dk/t); extended wear raises keratitis risk regardless of material.

## 3. Soft lens fit evaluation (after settling, about 10-20 minutes)
- **Centration**: full corneal coverage in all gaze positions.
- **Movement**: roughly 0.2-0.5 mm on blink; **push-up test** for tightness.
- Tight: little movement, conjunctival indentation, fluctuating vision clearing after blink. Loose: edge stand-off, excessive movement, decentration.
- **Toric**: check rotation via markings; apply **LARS** (left add, right subtract) to compensate stable mislocation.
- Over-refract and confirm acuity.

## 4. RGP fit evaluation
- Initial base curve chosen from keratometry per manufacturer fitting guide.
- **Fluorescein pattern**: alignment centrally, adequate edge clearance band; central pooling = steep, central touch = flat.
- Look for lid attachment or interpalpebral position, smooth movement, 3-and-9 o'clock staining risk.
- **Scleral lenses**: assess central vault (OCT helps), limbal clearance and landing-zone blanching; specialist fitting.

## 5. Aftercare and complications
Grade findings with **Efron** or **CCLRU** scales and record changes over time.

| Finding | Concern | Action level |
|---|---|---|
| Painful red eye, focal infiltrate with overlying epithelial defect, AC reaction | **Microbial keratitis** (Pseudomonas, Acanthamoeba) | Stop wear; same-day urgent referral per local pathway |
| Small peripheral infiltrates, intact epithelium | CLPU / sterile infiltrative keratitis | Cease wear, review closely |
| Giant papillae, mucus, lens intolerance | CLPC/GPC | Change to daily disposable, reduce wear |
| Limbal vessel ingrowth | Hypoxia | Higher Dk/t, reduce hours |
| Generalised superficial punctate staining | Solution toxicity, dryness | Change care system |

Pain out of proportion to signs and a history of water exposure should raise suspicion of **Acanthamoeba**.

## Gotchas
- Patients often sleep in or overwear lenses; ask specifically, and about tap water and topping up solution.
- A soft lens that looks well centred may still be tight; always do the push-up test.
- RGP flare complaints often reflect a small optic zone or decentration, not power.
- Do not patch a contact lens-related corneal abrasion (Pseudomonas risk).

## Verify
- [ ] Keratometry/topography and baseline anterior eye recorded.
- [ ] Fit documented: centration, movement, push-up/fluorescein pattern.
- [ ] Over-refraction and acuity confirmed.
- [ ] Wear schedule, hygiene and water avoidance taught and checked back.
- [ ] Complications graded on a named scale; red-eye red flags handled urgently.
