# OCT and visual field interpretation

**Goal:** get trustworthy OCT and perimetry data and interpret it in the context of the clinical examination, avoiding the common traps of artefact-driven diagnosis.

## 1. OCT acquisition
- Explain and position: chin and forehead in, blink before capture, fixate the target. Dilation helps with small pupils and media opacity.
- Check **signal strength/quality index** against the device's threshold; repeat poor scans rather than interpreting them.
- Choose protocols: peripapillary **RNFL circle**, optic nerve head cube, **macular cube** (thickness map and line scans), ganglion cell analysis (GCL/GCIPL), anterior segment (angle, cornea, scleral lens vault), OCT-A where available.

## 2. Reading macular OCT
- Scroll through B-scans; do not rely on the thickness map alone.
- Identify layers from vitreous to choroid; locate fluid (intraretinal cysts, subretinal fluid, sub-RPE/PED), drusen, epiretinal membrane, vitreomacular traction, macular hole.
- **New fluid in a patient with AMD suggests neovascular activity** and needs fast-track referral per local pathway.

## 3. Reading RNFL/GCL reports
- Compare with the normative database (colour coding green/yellow/red) but remember: databases do not cover high myopes, very large/small discs, or all ethnicities well.
- **Red disease**: false abnormal from artefact, myopic tilted discs, peripapillary atrophy, poor centration, floaters.
- **Green disease**: normal-looking numbers despite real damage, e.g. ERM or oedema thickening the RNFL, or early focal loss averaged out.
- Check **segmentation lines** on the B-scan and the circle centration before trusting values.
- For progression use the same device and protocol with registered follow-up (e.g. guided progression analysis); device values are not interchangeable.

## 4. Visual field acquisition (standard automated perimetry)
- Correct near refraction for the bowl distance and age; trial lens close to the eye to avoid rim artefact; occlude the other eye.
- Common strategy: **24-2 SITA Standard** (or Faster/Fast) for glaucoma screening/monitoring; **10-2** for central involvement (advanced glaucoma, hydroxychloroquine screening alongside OCT).

## 5. Reading the field printout
1. **Reliability**: fixation losses, false positives (trigger-happy, artificially good field) and false negatives (fatigue or real damage). Device guidance sets thresholds; high false positives are especially misleading.
2. **Global indices**: mean deviation (overall loss), pattern standard deviation (localised loss), VFI.
3. **Glaucoma hemifield test**: compares superior/inferior clusters.
4. **Probability plots**: total and pattern deviation; pattern deviation removes generalised depression (cataract, small pupil).
5. **Pattern recognition**: arcuate/nasal step (glaucoma), respecting the vertical midline (chiasmal/post-chiasmal, which need neurological referral), central/centrocaecal (macula/optic nerve), enlarged blind spot.
6. **Structure-function correlation**: an inferior RNFL defect should match a superior field defect.

## Gotchas
- Learning effect: first fields are often worse; repeat before diagnosing.
- Lid droop, lens rim and wrong correction cause superior and peripheral artefacts.
- A "normal" OCT with a reproducible field defect still needs explanation.
- Corneal topography and fundus photos have their own quality checks (tear film, focus, exposure); a dry ocular surface distorts topography maps.

## Verify
- [ ] OCT signal quality above device threshold and segmentation checked.
- [ ] B-scans reviewed, not just maps.
- [ ] Normative database limitations considered.
- [ ] Field reliability indices acceptable; correct lens used.
- [ ] Defect pattern classified and correlated with structure.
- [ ] Progression assessed on the same device and protocol.
