# Low vision assessment and magnifier prescribing

**Goal:** help a person with irreversible vision loss do the specific tasks they care about, using the right magnification, lighting, technique and support services.

## 1. Goal-centred history
- List **concrete tasks** and their size/distance: reading post, medicine labels, TV, faces, cooking, mobility, phone use.
- Cause and stability of vision loss (AMD, glaucoma, diabetic retinopathy, inherited retinal disease), which affects scotoma pattern and prognosis.
- Hand function, tremor, cognition, hearing, mood (depression is common), falls, driving status, living situation.
- Ask about **Charles Bonnet syndrome** visual hallucinations; reassure and explain.

## 2. Measure function
| Test | Why |
|---|---|
| Distance acuity on a **logMAR chart** (ETDRS/Bailey-Lovie) at a reduced distance if needed | Accurate in low range; avoid "count fingers" where a chart reading is possible |
| **Near acuity and reading speed** (e.g. MNREAD or continuous text) | Gives threshold print size and critical print size |
| **Contrast sensitivity** (Pelli-Robson) | Predicts difficulty with faces, steps, and benefit of lighting/contrast |
| Visual fields (central and peripheral), Amsler | Locates scotomas; mobility implications |
| Careful **refraction** with larger steps (e.g. +/-1.00 D) and trial frame | Small gains in refraction are often worth a lot |
| Glare sensitivity | Need for filters/tints |

## 3. Calculate magnification
- **Kestenbaum rule** (starting point): add (D) = reciprocal of the best Snellen acuity fraction, e.g. 6/60 (20/200) gives +10 D for about N8/1M print. Refine with real reading material.
- Alternative: magnification = current print size threshold / target print size, with an **acuity reserve** (often 2-3x threshold) for fluent reading.
- Equivalent power links devices: magnification approximately = equivalent power / 4 (for a 25 cm reference distance; conventions vary by manufacturer).

## 4. Choose the device
| Device | Strengths | Limits |
|---|---|---|
| Spectacle magnifiers (high-add readers, prism half-eyes) | Hands-free, wide field | Very close working distance |
| Hand magnifier | Portable, flexible distance | Needs steady hand; field shrinks with distance |
| Stand magnifier (often illuminated) | Good for tremor; fixed focus | Patient needs correct reading add to view the virtual image |
| Telescopes | Distance spotting (TV, signs) | Narrow field; not for mobility |
| **Electronic magnifiers / CCTV**, tablets and phone accessibility | High magnification, contrast reversal | Cost, training |

Demonstrate with the patient's own tasks and let them try before supplying.

## 5. Non-optical and rehabilitation strategies
- **Lighting**: task lamp positioned close and to the side of the better eye, avoiding glare.
- Contrast and size: bold markers, large print, tactile markers, contrast tape on steps.
- **Eccentric viewing training** for central scotoma, developing a stable preferred retinal locus.
- Referral to rehabilitation, mobility and social services; sight-impairment certification/registration per local eligibility criteria.

## Gotchas
- Too much magnification shrinks the field and slows reading; aim for the minimum that reaches the goal.
- Stand magnifiers fail when the patient uses the wrong reading add.
- An unchanged acuity does not mean unchanged function; reassess goals.
- Fluctuating vision may signal active disease (e.g. wet AMD) needing medical review, not more magnification.

## Verify
- [ ] Specific task goals documented.
- [ ] logMAR distance, near threshold/critical print size and contrast recorded.
- [ ] Refraction optimised before magnification.
- [ ] Magnification calculated and confirmed on real tasks.
- [ ] Lighting, training and onward referrals arranged; follow-up booked.
