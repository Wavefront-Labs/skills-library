# Glaucoma detection and referral

**Goal:** identify patients with glaucoma, ocular hypertension (OHT) or angle-closure risk, and refer or co-manage with the right urgency without flooding services with false positives.

## 1. Risk profile
- Age, **family history** in a first-degree relative, African or Caribbean heritage (POAG), East Asian heritage and hyperopia (angle closure), high myopia, diabetes, vascular dysregulation/low blood pressure (normal-tension), long-term steroid use, pseudoexfoliation or pigment dispersion.

## 2. Measure IOP properly
- **Goldmann applanation** as reference; repeat any raised non-contact reading. Record time of day.
- **Pachymetry (CCT)**: thin corneas cause underestimation and are an independent risk factor; thick corneas cause overestimation. Avoid simple correction formulae as definitive.
- Statistical upper limit of "normal" IOP is about 21 mmHg, but glaucoma occurs below and many above never develop it; referral thresholds vary by guideline/jurisdiction (check current NICE/local pathway).

## 3. Angle assessment
- **Van Herick** at the slit lamp: peripheral AC depth relative to corneal thickness; grades of 1/4 or less suggest a potentially occludable angle.
- **Gonioscopy** (Shaffer or Spaeth grading) where available and before labelling an angle open.
- **Acute angle closure**: painful red eye, haloes, mid-dilated poorly reactive pupil, corneal oedema, very high IOP, nausea. **Emergency same-day referral**.

## 4. Optic disc and RNFL
Examine stereoscopically at the slit lamp with a condensing lens, after dilation if needed.
- Assess disc size first (vertical disc diameter), then cup:disc ratio in context; large discs have large cups.
- **ISNT rule** (rim usually thickest inferior > superior > nasal > temporal) as a guide; notching or thinning of inferior/superior rim is suspicious.
- **Disc haemorrhages**, bayoneting, peripapillary atrophy (beta zone), asymmetry of cup:disc between eyes (roughly 0.2 or more is noteworthy).
- **OCT RNFL/GCL** supports but does not replace clinical assessment; beware red disease and green disease (see `health-medicine.optometry.ocular-imaging-and-diagnostic-systems.oct-and-visual-field-interpretation`).

## 5. Visual fields
- Standard automated perimetry (e.g. Humphrey 24-2 SITA, or equivalent); repeat abnormal or unreliable fields before acting.
- Glaucomatous patterns: nasal step, arcuate defect, paracentral loss; check GHT and pattern standard deviation, and correlate with disc/RNFL location.

## 6. Referral decisions
| Finding | Typical urgency |
|---|---|
| Suspected acute angle closure | Emergency, same day |
| Very high IOP (thresholds per local pathway) | Urgent |
| Glaucomatous disc and/or reproducible field defect | Routine-to-soon per pathway |
| OHT alone, or narrow angle without symptoms | Per enhanced/repeat-measures pathway |

Include IOP with method and time, CCT, disc description or images, field printouts, angle findings and risk factors in referrals.

## Gotchas
- A single raised NCT reading is not a referral; repeat and confirm with applanation.
- Field learning effects make a first field unreliable; repeat before referring on fields alone.
- Normal-tension glaucoma is missed when only IOP is screened.
- Do not dilate a suspected occludable angle without a plan for post-dilation IOP checks and symptoms advice.

## Verify
- [ ] Risk factors documented.
- [ ] IOP repeated with applanation and time recorded; CCT measured.
- [ ] Angle graded (Van Herick and gonioscopy where possible).
- [ ] Disc described with size, rim, haemorrhage and asymmetry.
- [ ] Field reliable and correlated with structure.
- [ ] Referral urgency matches local pathway; acute angle closure sent same day.
