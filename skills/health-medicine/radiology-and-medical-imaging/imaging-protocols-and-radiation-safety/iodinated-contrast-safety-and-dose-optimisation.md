# Iodinated contrast safety and dose optimisation

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** give the right scan, with contrast only when it adds value, at the lowest dose
that answers the question — and be ready when a reaction happens.

## 1. Justify the request
- Does the imaging answer the clinical question and will it change management? Check
  appropriateness criteria (ACR AC, RCR iRefer).
- Could ultrasound or MRI answer it without ionising radiation (children, pregnancy,
  young women, repeated follow-up)?
- Under IR(ME)R-style regimes, justification is a legal duty of the practitioner, not
  just good practice.

## 2. Kidney function (iodinated IV contrast)
- ACR-NKF 2020: risk of contrast-associated AKI is **low at eGFR ≥ 30**; consider
  prophylaxis (IV isotonic fluid) at **eGFR < 30** not on dialysis, or AKI. Don't
  withhold a needed contrast scan in a sick patient because of creatinine alone.
- **Metformin:** continue if eGFR ≥ 30 and no AKI; hold for 48 h and recheck renal
  function if eGFR < 30 or AKI (ACR). Some regional guidance differs — check local.
- Dialysis patients: no need to schedule extra dialysis for contrast in most cases.

## 3. Contrast reactions
- **Allergic-like:** mild (urticaria, itch) → observe, antihistamine if needed;
  moderate (diffuse urticaria, bronchospasm, facial oedema) → treat and monitor;
  severe (anaphylaxis, laryngeal oedema, hypotension) → **IM adrenaline 0.5 mg
  (1 mg/mL) adult** in anterolateral thigh, repeat every 5 min as needed, oxygen, IV
  fluids, call the resuscitation team.
- **Physiological** (nausea, warmth, vasovagal: hypotension **with bradycardia**) —
  legs up, fluids, atropine if needed. Distinguish from anaphylaxis (tachycardia).
- Prior reaction: consider alternative imaging, switch contrast agent, and
  premedication (e.g. oral prednisolone 50 mg at 13, 7 and 1 h + antihistamine,
  ACR regimen) — **premedication does not prevent all breakthrough reactions**.
- "Shellfish allergy" and iodine/povidone sensitivity are **not** contraindications.
- Extravasation: elevate, cold compress, assess compartment; surgical review if
  large volume or skin compromise.

## 4. Gadolinium (MRI)
- Group II macrocyclic agents carry very low NSF risk even at low eGFR; still use the
  lowest dose and avoid in severe AKI unless essential. Gadolinium deposition: use
  only when it adds value.

## 5. Dose optimisation (CT)
- Read dose indices on every study: **CTDIvol** (per-slice intensity, phantom-based)
  and **DLP** (× scan length). Compare with national/local **DRLs** per protocol.
- Levers: limit scan length to the question; avoid unnecessary multiphase; automatic
  tube current modulation; kV selection (lower kV for CTA/children); iterative or
  deep-learning reconstruction; correct patient **centring** (off-centre raises dose
  and noise).
- Children: size-based protocols; image gently.
- **Pregnancy:** check status in 12–55 years; most diagnostic doses outside the pelvis
  are very low foetal dose — don't withhold needed imaging, but document and
  optimise.

## Gotchas
- Delaying a CT pulmonary angiogram in a deteriorating patient for creatinine.
- Calling a vasovagal reaction anaphylaxis (or vice versa) — check heart rate.
- Copying an adult protocol to a child.
- Repeating a multiphase study where single phase would answer the question.

## Verify
- [ ] Request justified; alternative modality considered.
- [ ] eGFR/AKI and metformin status checked when relevant.
- [ ] Reaction history, premedication and emergency kit/adrenaline available.
- [ ] Dose indices recorded and within DRL or excess explained.
- [ ] Pregnancy status documented where applicable.
