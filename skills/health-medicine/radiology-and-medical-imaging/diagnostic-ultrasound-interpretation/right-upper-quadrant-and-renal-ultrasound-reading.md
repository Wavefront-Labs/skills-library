# Right upper quadrant and renal ultrasound reading

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** turn a RUQ or renal scan into a confident answer — and know when the images
are too limited to give one.

## 1. Judge the acquisition first
Ultrasound is operator-dependent; you read what was captured.
- Were both organs seen in **two planes** with labelled images? Cine clips of the
  gallbladder and kidneys?
- Patient fasting ≥ 6 h (a contracted gallbladder after a meal looks thick-walled).
- Body habitus / bowel gas limiting? Say so in the report — a limited negative is not a
  negative.
- Correct gain and focus; harmonic imaging on.

## 2. Gallbladder
- **Gallstones:** echogenic, mobile, posterior acoustic **shadowing**. Non-shadowing
  mobile echoes → sludge or small stones; fixed non-shadowing → polyp (measure: ≥ 10 mm
  generally needs surgical opinion; 6–9 mm surveillance — check current local/ESGAR
  guidance).
- **Acute calculous cholecystitis** (combine): stones (often impacted at neck),
  **sonographic Murphy's sign** (maximal tenderness under the probe over the
  gallbladder), wall > 3 mm, pericholecystic fluid, distension. Wall thickening alone is
  non-specific (heart failure, hepatitis, ascites, hypoalbuminaemia, post-prandial).
- Analgesia before the scan can abolish Murphy's sign — check the request.
- Gas in the wall or lumen → emphysematous cholecystitis (urgent).

## 3. Bile ducts
- **CBD** measured inner-to-inner at the porta: typically ≤ 6 mm; allow ~ 1 mm per decade
  over 60 and larger post-cholecystectomy (≤ 10 mm often normal). Intrahepatic ducts
  > 2 mm or > 40% of adjacent portal vein → dilated.
- Distal CBD is often obscured by duodenal gas — dilated duct with no visible stone →
  MRCP/EUS.

## 4. Liver
- Echotexture vs right kidney: brighter than cortex → steatosis (grade by loss of
  diaphragm/vessel wall visibility). Nodular surface, caudate hypertrophy, splenomegaly,
  ascites → cirrhosis features.
- Focal lesion: anechoic + through-transmission = simple cyst; echogenic well-defined
  in low-risk patient = likely haemangioma (but confirm per pathway in cirrhosis or
  cancer history). Doppler: portal vein patency and direction.

## 5. Kidneys
- Length (adult ~ 10–12 cm; > 1.5–2 cm asymmetry is significant), cortical thickness,
  echogenicity (brighter than liver = medical renal disease).
- **Hydronephrosis:** mild/moderate/severe (or SFU grade 1–4 in children). Check
  bladder is not overdistended — re-scan post-void. Look for ureteric jets on colour
  Doppler.
- **Cysts:** simple (anechoic, thin wall, through-transmission) need no follow-up;
  septations, solid components or calcification → CT/MRI for Bosniak classification
  (Bosniak is a CT/MRI system; US is a triage tool, though contrast US is used in some
  centres).
- Stones: echogenic with shadow and **twinkle artefact** on colour Doppler.

## Gotchas
- Calling cholecystitis on wall thickness alone.
- Non-fasted patient → false thick wall, missed stones.
- Calling hydronephrosis with a full bladder.
- Mistaking an extrarenal pelvis or parapelvic cysts for hydronephrosis — follow the
  calyces.

## Verify
- [ ] Limitations stated (habitus, gas, fasting).
- [ ] Gallbladder: stones, wall, Murphy's, fluid all commented on.
- [ ] CBD measured with age/cholecystectomy context.
- [ ] Kidneys: length both sides, hydronephrosis grade, post-void if needed.
- [ ] Indeterminate findings given a specific next test.
