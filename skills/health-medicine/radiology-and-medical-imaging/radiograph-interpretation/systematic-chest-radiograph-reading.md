# Systematic chest radiograph reading

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** read every chest film the same way so the finding you weren't looking for
still gets seen. Most CXR misses are perceptual (seen in retrospect), not knowledge gaps.

## 1. Before the image: context and priors
- Confirm patient, date, projection (**PA vs AP**, erect vs supine).
- **Open the prior.** Comparison is the single most useful tool — a 1 cm nodule present
  for 3 years is a different problem from a new one.
- Read the clinical question, but don't let it narrow your search.

## 2. Technical adequacy (RIPE)
- **Rotation:** medial clavicle ends equidistant from spinous processes.
- **Inspiration:** ~6 anterior or 9–10 posterior ribs above the diaphragm.
- **Projection:** AP magnifies the heart — don't call cardiomegaly on AP.
- **Exposure:** vertebrae just visible behind the heart.

## 3. Lines and tubes first (they're the urgent ones)
- **ETT:** tip ~3–7 cm above the carina (neck neutral).
- **NG tube:** follows the midline, **bisects the carina**, crosses the diaphragm, tip
  in the stomach ≥ 10 cm beyond the GOJ, below the left hemidiaphragm. Anything
  else → do not feed; escalate. Misplaced NG feeding is a recognised never event.
- **CVC:** tip at cavo-atrial junction / lower SVC; check for pneumothorax after
  insertion.
- Chest drains, pacing leads, valves — note and check for complications.

## 4. Search pattern (ABCDE + review areas)
- **A — Airway:** trachea midline; carina; main bronchi.
- **B — Breathing (lungs/pleura):** compare zones side by side; look for consolidation,
  nodules, interstitial lines, **pneumothorax edge** (follow the apex — on a supine
  film look for the deep sulcus sign).
- **C — Cardiac/mediastinum:** CTR on PA (> 0.5 enlarged), mediastinal contour, hila
  (size, density — the left hilum is normally higher).
- **D — Diaphragm:** costophrenic angles (blunting needs ~200 mL erect), free gas under
  the diaphragm on erect film.
- **E — Everything else:** bones (ribs, clavicles, humeri, spine), soft tissues, breast
  shadows, surgical clips.

## 5. Review areas (where misses cluster)
Apices; **behind the heart**; hila; below the diaphragm (lung extends posteriorly);
**costophrenic angles**; peripheries for pneumothorax; bones. Look at these **last**,
deliberately, even if you've found something — satisfaction of search is the classic trap.

## 6. Signs that localise
- **Silhouette sign:** loss of the right heart border → right middle lobe / lingula on
  left; loss of hemidiaphragm → lower lobe.
- **Lobar collapse:** LLL — "sail" behind the heart; RUL — elevated horizontal fissure
  (Golden S sign suggests a central mass); LUL — veil-like haze, luftsichel.
- **Air bronchograms** → airspace process (pneumonia, oedema, sometimes tumour).
- Effusion vs collapse: effusion pushes the mediastinum away; collapse pulls toward.

## 7. Write it down
Lead with the answer to the question and any urgent findings; then other findings;
then recommendation (e.g. CT for a new nodule; follow-up film at 6 weeks after
pneumonia in smokers > 50 per local pathway).

## Gotchas
- Stopping after the first finding.
- Calling cardiomegaly or widened mediastinum on a supine AP film.
- Missing a pneumothorax on a supine ICU film (look for deep sulcus, sharp heart border).
- Not comparing with prior → reporting a stable scar as new.

## Verify
- [ ] Prior compared; projection noted.
- [ ] All lines/tubes positioned and commented on.
- [ ] Every review area checked after the main finding.
- [ ] Urgent findings communicated directly, not just reported.
- [ ] Recommendation is specific (what test, when).
