# Non-contrast CT head and stroke CT reading

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** answer the time-critical questions in order — bleed? established infarct?
vessel occlusion? salvageable tissue? — and get the answer to the stroke team in minutes.

## 1. Set up the viewer
- **Windows:** brain (W80/L40), **stroke** (narrow, ~W40/L40) to exaggerate grey–white
  contrast, subdural (W150–200/L50–80), bone (W2000/L500).
- Review axial thin and thick slices; coronal and sagittal reformats help with
  convexity subdurals and posterior fossa.

## 2. Non-contrast CT: "Blood Can Be Very Bad"
- **Blood:** intra-axial, extra-axial (EDH lentiform, SDH crescentic, crossing sutures),
  **subarachnoid** (sylvian fissures, interpeduncular cistern, occipital horns of
  lateral ventricles — easy to miss), intraventricular.
- **Cisterns:** effaced basal cisterns = raised ICP / herniation risk.
- **Brain:** grey–white differentiation, sulcal effacement, mass effect, midline shift.
- **Ventricles:** hydrocephalus (temporal horns dilate early).
- **Bone:** fractures (use bone window), sinuses for air-fluid levels.

## 3. Early ischaemic signs
- **Hyperdense vessel** (MCA "dot" or "string") — thrombus; compare with the other side
  and haematocrit.
- Loss of insular ribbon, obscured lentiform nucleus, cortical hypodensity, sulcal
  effacement.
- **ASPECTS** (anterior circulation): start at 10, subtract 1 for each of 10 MCA
  regions with early ischaemic change (caudate, lentiform, internal capsule, insula,
  M1–M3 at the ganglionic level, M4–M6 at the supraganglionic level). **ASPECTS ≥ 6**
  is the commonly used threshold in thrombectomy trials; lower scores are increasingly
  treated in selected patients — follow local protocol.

## 4. CTA (arch to vertex)
- Look for **LVO**: ICA terminus, M1, proximal M2, basilar. Follow each vessel; compare
  sides; MIPs help.
- Assess collaterals (good collaterals favour outcome), tandem cervical ICA lesions,
  arch access for the interventionalist, incidental aneurysm or dissection.
- Don't forget the **lung apices** on the CTA field.

## 5. CT perfusion — use with caution
- Core (low CBF, e.g. rCBF < 30%) vs penumbra (Tmax > 6 s). Mismatch selects late-window
  thrombectomy (DAWN/DEFUSE-3 criteria).
- Software maps can be wrong: motion, poor arterial input function, small lacunar
  infarcts invisible. **Check the source images** and the AIF/VOF curves.

## 6. Communicate
Phone the stroke team with: bleed yes/no, ASPECTS, occlusion site, perfusion summary.
Written report follows. Don't let thrombolysis wait for the formal report — the
team will act on the verbal result.

## Gotchas
- Missing a small SAH in the interpeduncular cistern or occipital horns.
- Calling a calcified vessel a hyperdense MCA (check bone window / Hounsfield ~ 40–60
  for clot vs > 100 for calcium).
- Missing basilar occlusion because the posterior circulation wasn't traced.
- Stroke mimics: hypoglycaemia, seizure, tumour, subdural — the CT may show the mimic.

## Verify
- [ ] Haemorrhage excluded on brain and subdural windows, all cisterns checked.
- [ ] ASPECTS scored region by region.
- [ ] Every major intracranial vessel and both cervical carotids/vertebrals traced on CTA.
- [ ] Perfusion source data and curves checked before quoting core volume.
- [ ] Time of verbal result documented.
