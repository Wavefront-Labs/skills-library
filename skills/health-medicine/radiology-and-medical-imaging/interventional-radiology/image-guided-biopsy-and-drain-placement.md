# Image-guided biopsy and drain placement

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** get diagnostic tissue or a working drain with the fewest passes, through the
safest route, with bleeding and infection risk managed before the needle goes in.

## 1. Is it the right procedure?
- Review all imaging; confirm the target still exists and is the **most accessible
  lesion that answers the question** (e.g. biopsy a liver metastasis rather than a deep
  pancreatic primary if it gives the same stage and diagnosis).
- Will the result change management? MDT agreement for oncology biopsies.
- Collection: is it drainable (liquid, > ~3 cm, not a phlegmon)? Small abscesses may
  settle with antibiotics or aspiration alone.

## 2. Bleeding risk and anticoagulation
- **SIR 2019:** classify the procedure as **low** (e.g. superficial biopsy, thoracentesis,
  paracentesis, drain exchange) or **high** bleeding risk (e.g. solid-organ biopsy,
  most new drains into organs, deep biopsies).
- High risk: INR ≤ 1.5–1.8 and platelets ≥ 50 × 10⁹/L per SIR thresholds; low risk:
  INR ≤ 2.0–3.0, platelets ≥ 20 × 10⁹/L. Check your local version — thresholds vary.
- Hold anticoagulants/antiplatelets per drug and renal function (e.g. DOACs commonly
  2–3 days for high-risk, longer with renal impairment); decide bridging with the
  prescriber. Don't stop antiplatelets in a recent coronary stent without cardiology.

## 3. Plan the path
- Ultrasound when the target is visible (real-time, no radiation, shorter); CT for deep,
  lung, bone, or gas-obscured targets.
- Shortest route that **avoids vessels (colour Doppler), bowel, pleura, and large
  central vessels**; through normal liver parenchyma before a subcapsular liver lesion
  to tamponade bleeding.
- Lung: avoid fissures and bullae; fewer pleural punctures = fewer pneumothoraces.

## 4. Doing it
- WHO checklist / time-out: patient, site, side, allergies, coagulation, consent.
- Coaxial technique for multiple cores through one capsule puncture. 18G cores are the
  common default; 20G when risk is higher.
- **Specimen handling:** cores in formalin for histology; fresh/saline for
  microbiology, flow cytometry (lymphoma), or molecular as requested — agree before you
  start. ROSE where available.
- **Drains:** **Seldinger** (needle → wire → dilate → drain) for deep or tricky targets;
  **trocar** (direct puncture with mounted drain) for large superficial collections.
  8–12 Fr for most abscesses; larger for thick pus. Aspirate to dryness, send pus for
  culture; lock the pigtail; secure well.

## 5. Aftercare
- Observation per protocol (e.g. 2–4 h for liver biopsy with vitals); chest x-ray or
  fluoroscopy after lung biopsy (post-biopsy-side down).
- Drain: flush (e.g. 10 mL saline 8–12-hourly), measure output, re-image if output stops
  with persistent sepsis. Remove when output < ~10–20 mL/day and clinical recovery
  (exclude fistula if output persistently high).

## Gotchas
- Biopsy of a necrotic centre → non-diagnostic; aim for enhancing periphery.
- Not sending a sample for microbiology when infection is in the differential.
- Drain through bowel or across pleura (empyema risk) — check the approach on CT.
- Forgetting to restart anticoagulation in the discharge plan.

## Verify
- [ ] Indication agreed; target and route reviewed on latest imaging.
- [ ] Bleeding-risk category, labs and anticoagulant hold documented.
- [ ] Time-out completed; specimen destinations confirmed.
- [ ] Post-procedure observation and imaging done; complications recorded.
- [ ] Drain care instructions and anticoagulation restart in the notes.
