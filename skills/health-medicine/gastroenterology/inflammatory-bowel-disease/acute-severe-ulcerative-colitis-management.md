# Acute severe ulcerative colitis (ASUC) management

> General clinical reference, not patient-specific advice. Steroid, infliximab and ciclosporin doses and monitoring must be taken from current guidelines and the local formulary; involve the IBD team early.

**Goal:** recognise ASUC as a medical emergency with real mortality, get IV steroids
and infection screens in on day 0, make a firm response decision by day 3, and move to
rescue therapy or surgery without drift.

## 1. Recognise it (Truelove and Witts)
**≥ 6 bloody stools/day** plus **at least one** of:
- pulse > 90/min, temperature > 37.8 °C, Hb < 105 g/L, ESR > 30 mm/h (CRP > 30 mg/L
  is commonly used in place of ESR).
Admit, ideally under a joint gastroenterology–colorectal team.

## 2. Day 0 bundle
- Bloods: FBC, U&E, LFT, CRP, albumin, magnesium; **hepatitis B, HIV, VZV, TB screen
  (IGRA)** now — you may need biologics within 72 h.
- **Stool: C. difficile** toxin, culture; stool cultures before steroids.
- **Flexible sigmoidoscopy** (unprepped, minimal insufflation) within ~24–72 h:
  confirms severity, **biopsies for CMV** (immunohistochemistry).
- **Abdominal X-ray:** transverse colon > 5.5 cm suggests toxic dilatation; mucosal
  islands also predict colectomy.
- **IV corticosteroids** (e.g. hydrocortisone or methylprednisolone; dose per guideline).
- **VTE prophylaxis with LMWH** — rectal bleeding is not a contraindication; IBD flares
  are strongly pro-thrombotic.
- Stop/avoid opioids, anticholinergics, antidiarrhoeals and NSAIDs (megacolon risk).
- Correct potassium and magnesium; nutritional assessment; stool chart (frequency,
  blood, nocturnal).

## 3. Day 3 decision
Assess objectively on day 3 of IV steroids:
- **Travis/Oxford criterion:** > 8 stools/day, **or** 3–8 stools/day with **CRP > 45
  mg/L** → ~85% colectomy risk without rescue.
- Other predictors: albumin < 30 g/L, mucosal ulceration depth, colonic dilatation.
Non-responders → **rescue therapy** or **colectomy**, decided that day with the
surgeon present. Don't extend IV steroids beyond ~7 days hoping for a late response.

## 4. Rescue therapy
- **Infliximab** (weight-based; accelerated/intensified dosing is used by some units for
  high inflammatory burden — evidence mixed, check local protocol), or
- **Ciclosporin** IV (needs magnesium and cholesterol checked, blood levels, renal and BP
  monitoring; seizure risk with low Mg/cholesterol); bridge to thiopurine or vedolizumab.
- Efficacy broadly similar in trials; infliximab easier to use and continue as
  maintenance. Sequential use of one after the other is high-risk and specialist-only.
- Small-molecule JAK inhibitors (e.g. tofacitinib) are used in some centres as
  rescue — evidence is emerging; **treat as specialist/off-label in many regions**.
- Reassess response 4–7 days after rescue; failure → colectomy.

## 5. Surgery
- **Indications:** failed rescue, toxic megacolon not settling within 24–72 h,
  perforation, massive haemorrhage.
- Usual operation: **subtotal colectomy with end ileostomy**, preserving the rectum for
  later pouch (IPAA) or ileorectal anastomosis.
- Delayed surgery increases complications; early stoma-nurse involvement and honest
  counselling reduce harm.

## Red flags — urgent surgical review
- Abdominal distension, reduced bowel sounds, tenderness or peritonism (steroids mask
  signs), new tachycardia, colon > 5.5 cm, rising lactate, haemodynamic instability.

## Gotchas
- C. difficile and CMV mimic or complicate flares: treat when found, but don't delay
  steroids awaiting results in typical ASUC.
- Low stool frequency on opioids can be false reassurance.
- Albumin and nutrition fall fast; a prolonged medical course worsens surgical outcome.
- Thiopurine-naive patient after ciclosporin: start maintenance early.

## Verify
- [ ] Truelove-Witts criteria documented on admission.
- [ ] C. difficile, stool cultures, CMV biopsies, viral/TB screen sent on day 0–1.
- [ ] LMWH prophylaxis prescribed.
- [ ] Day-3 Oxford assessment recorded with a decision.
- [ ] Surgeon involved from admission; colectomy not delayed after failed rescue.
