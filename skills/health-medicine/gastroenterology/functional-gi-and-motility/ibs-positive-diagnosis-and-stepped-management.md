# IBS: positive diagnosis and stepped management

> General clinical reference, not patient-specific advice. Drug availability, licensing and doses vary by country; check the local formulary.

**Goal:** make a confident, positive diagnosis of irritable bowel syndrome from history
plus a short set of tests — instead of an endless exclusion workup — and treat it in
steps matched to the dominant symptom.

## 1. Rome IV criteria
Recurrent abdominal pain on average **≥ 1 day/week in the last 3 months**, with **≥ 2**
of: related to defecation; change in stool frequency; change in stool form. Onset
≥ 6 months before diagnosis (criteria text confirmed on the Rome Foundation Rome IV page).
Subtype by **Bristol Stool Form** on days with abnormal stools (definitions (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)): **IBS-C**, **IBS-D**,
**IBS-M**, IBS-U. Subtype drives treatment and can shift over time.
Bloating and discomfort without pain → functional bloating/constipation/diarrhoea
rather than IBS (management overlaps).

## 2. Alarm features — investigate, don't label
- Age of onset > ~50 (60 in some guidelines), rectal bleeding, unintentional weight
  loss, iron-deficiency anaemia, nocturnal symptoms waking the patient, family history
  of colorectal cancer, IBD or coeliac disease, abdominal or rectal mass, raised
  inflammatory markers.
- Any alarm feature → appropriate referral (often colonoscopy; local fast-track criteria apply).

## 3. Minimal testing in typical cases
- FBC, CRP, **coeliac serology** (tTG-IgA with total IgA).
- **Faecal calprotectin** in diarrhoea to screen for IBD (ACG 2021 suggests serologic coeliac testing and calprotectin in IBS with diarrhoea; the age cut-off of ~40-50 is (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)).
- Faecal immunochemical test (FIT) where local pathways use it for lower-GI symptoms.
- Consider ovarian cancer screening (CA-125) in women with new persistent symptoms in
  the relevant age group (NICE suggests this (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)).
- Think of mimics in IBS-D: **bile acid diarrhoea** (SeHCAT or 7αC4, or trial of
  binder), **microscopic colitis** (older, female, NSAID/PPI/SSRI use — needs colonic
  biopsies), coeliac, lactose intolerance, giardia, thyroid.

## 4. Stepped management
**Step 1 — everyone**
- Explain the diagnosis as a real disorder of gut–brain interaction (visceral
  hypersensitivity, altered motility, central processing). A confident explanation is
  itself therapeutic.
- Regular meals, physical activity, **soluble fibre** (psyllium/ispaghula); avoid
  insoluble bran, which often worsens bloating.
- **Antispasmodic** or **peppermint oil** for pain.
- IBS-D: **loperamide** as needed. IBS-C: **osmotic laxative** (e.g. PEG).

**Step 2 — persistent symptoms**
- **Low-FODMAP diet** (ACG 2021: a limited trial) delivered by a dietitian: restriction, structured reintroduction,
  personalisation. Not indefinitely; avoid in disordered eating.
- **Low-dose tricyclic** (gut–brain neuromodulator, e.g. amitriptyline titrated) — best
  evidence for global symptoms and pain; take at night; more helpful in IBS-D.
  **SSRI** an alternative, particularly in IBS-C (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) (the ACG summary only endorses tricyclics).
- IBS-C: secretagogue (ACG 2021 recommends chloride-channel and guanylate-cyclase activators; e.g. **linaclotide**) or prucalopride depending on licence (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
- IBS-D: **rifaximin** (ACG 2021 recommends it); 5-HT3 antagonist (e.g. ondansetron, often off-label) and eluxadoline where available (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).

**Step 3 — refractory**
- **Gut-directed psychotherapy** (ACG 2021 suggests it), e.g. hypnotherapy or **IBS-specific CBT** — effective and durable; refer
  to trained therapists.
- Specialist review for overlap (dyssynergic defecation needs anorectal physiology and
  biofeedback; chronic pain syndromes).

## Gotchas
- Repeated normal colonoscopies reinforce illness worry; agree a plan and stop testing
  without new features.
- Opioids for IBS pain worsen it (narcotic bowel syndrome).
- "Food intolerance" IgG panels have no diagnostic value.
- Constipation with straining, digitation or incomplete evacuation may be an evacuation
  disorder, not slow transit — laxatives alone fail.

## Verify
- [ ] Rome IV criteria and subtype recorded.
- [ ] Alarm features asked and documented as absent (or acted on).
- [ ] Coeliac serology, FBC/CRP and calprotectin (if diarrhoea) done.
- [ ] Diagnosis explained; first-line diet and subtype-specific drug started.
- [ ] Review planned with a defined step-up (FODMAP, neuromodulator, psychological therapy).
