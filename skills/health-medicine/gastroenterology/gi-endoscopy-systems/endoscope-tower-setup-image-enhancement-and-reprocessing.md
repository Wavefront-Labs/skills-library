# Endoscope tower setup, image enhancement and reprocessing

> General reference. Menu names, buttons and reprocessing parameters differ by manufacturer, model and software version — **always follow the specific device instructions for use (IFU) and your decontamination unit's validated process.** Vendor details below are summarised from public material and not verified on every model.

**Goal:** a tower that works before the patient is sedated, image enhancement used
deliberately rather than randomly, and scopes that are traceably clean every time.

## 1. Pre-list tower check
- Processor, light source (often integrated in current platforms), monitor, recorder/
  image capture, **CO2 insufflator** (CO2 reduces post-procedure pain vs room air),
  water bottle / **water-jet pump**, suction with a fresh canister, electrosurgical
  generator if therapy is planned.
- Connect scope: light-guide plug, video connection, water bottle, suction. Check
  **angulation** in all directions, locks free, air/water and suction valves working,
  forceps channel patent.
- **White balance** if the platform requires it (many modern processors are automatic —
  check model). Wrong white balance makes mucosa and enhancement colours misleading.
- Patient ID entered on the processor so images land in the right record.
- Confirm the scope's reprocessing record (date/time, cycle passed) before use.

## 2. Image enhancement — what each mode is for
- **Olympus NBI (narrow-band imaging):** narrow blue/green light highlights surface
  vessels and pits; used for polyp characterisation (NICE/JNET), Barrett's and
  squamous neoplasia. **TXI** (on EVIS X1) enhances texture and colour for detection;
  **RDI** highlights deeper vessels/bleeding points.
- **Fujifilm BLI (blue light imaging)** for characterisation; **LCI (linked colour
  imaging)** for detection and inflammation (e.g. H. pylori gastritis appearance).
- **Pentax i-scan** (software modes 1–3/OE) for surface, tone and contrast enhancement.
- **Near focus / dual focus / magnification** for close characterisation.
- **CADe/CADx** modules add on-screen detection boxes or optical predictions; they assist,
  they don't replace inspection, and their false positives (folds, bubbles) need judgement.
Practical habit: detect in white light (or a detection mode), **characterise in
NBI/BLI at near focus**, photograph both.

## 3. Reprocessing chain (each step is a failure point)
1. **Bedside pre-clean** immediately after withdrawal: wipe insertion tube, suction
   detergent and air through channels, fit the soaking/channel cap. Dried biofilm is
   the root cause of many outbreaks.
2. Transport in a closed, labelled "dirty" container.
3. **Leak test** before immersion — a perforated sheath lets fluid into the scope and
   ruins it; a failed scope goes for repair, not reprocessing.
4. **Manual cleaning:** brush every channel with correct-size single-use brushes,
   flush, clean valves separately; this step removes the bioburden that disinfection
   cannot penetrate.
5. **Automated endoscope reprocessor (AER):** high-level disinfection (or sterilisation
   where policy requires) and rinse, with all channel adaptors connected.
6. **Drying and storage:** forced-air drying cabinet with HEPA-filtered air; store
   hanging/flat per cabinet design; respect maximum storage time before re-processing.
7. **Traceability:** scope serial number ↔ AER cycle ↔ patient record.

## 4. Duodenoscopes and linear EUS scopes
The **elevator mechanism** is hard to clean and has caused multi-drug-resistant outbreaks.
Follow the extra manual brushing steps, consider disposable-cap designs or single-use
duodenoscopes where policy recommends, and support microbiological surveillance culturing.

## Gotchas
- Water bottles must be sterile water and changed per policy (often daily).
- A video "no signal" is frequently a half-seated connector or a moist plug — dry and reseat.
- Enhancement modes drop brightness; increase light or move closer before judging a lesion.
- Don't use a scope whose reprocessing record is missing, regardless of urgency.

## Verify
- [ ] Angulation, air/water, suction, channel and CO2 checked before sedation.
- [ ] Patient ID on processor; images capture to the correct record.
- [ ] Lesions photographed in white light and enhancement mode.
- [ ] Pre-clean done at bedside; leak test passed; AER cycle logged.
- [ ] Scope traceable to patient and cycle; stored dry within time limits.
