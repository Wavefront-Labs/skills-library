# Colonoscopy quality and cold snare polypectomy

> General clinical reference, not patient-specific advice. KPI targets, surveillance intervals and anticoagulant handling differ between societies; follow local guidance.

**Goal:** a complete, clean, carefully inspected colon, every relevant lesion found and
correctly described, and small polyps removed completely with the lowest-risk technique.

## 1. Quality indicators you are measured on
Typical targets (check your national standard):
- **Caecal intubation** ≥ 90–95%, photo-documented (appendiceal orifice + ileocaecal
  valve, or terminal ileum).
- **Adenoma detection rate (ADR)** ≥ 25% overall in screening populations (higher
  targets increasingly used, e.g. 30–35%; varies by population and sex).
- **Mean withdrawal time** ≥ 6 min in negative procedures; many aim for 8–10.
- **Bowel prep** adequate in ≥ 90%: **Boston Bowel Preparation Scale** ≥ 2 in each
  segment (total ≥ 6). Inadequate prep → repeat, usually within a year, with a stronger
  regimen.
- Polyp retrieval, complication and comfort rates audited.

## 2. Technique that finds more
- Clean as you go (wash and suction pools), inspect behind folds, use position changes
  to distend each segment (e.g. left lateral for the right colon is common practice).
- Second look or retroflexion in the right colon and rectum.
- Image enhancement (NBI/BLI/i-scan) and distal attachments (cap, Endocuff) can help;
  computer-aided detection (CADe) raises ADR in trials — local availability varies.

## 3. Describe and triage every lesion
Record size (estimate against open snare or forceps), location, **Paris** morphology
(0-Ip, 0-Is, 0-IIa, 0-IIb, 0-IIc), and an optical classification (**NICE** or **JNET**).
- Sessile serrated lesions: pale, mucus cap, indistinct edge, right colon — easy to miss
  and to resect incompletely.
- **Features of deep submucosal invasion** (depressed area 0-IIc, NICE 3/JNET 3,
  disrupted pit/vessel pattern, non-lifting) → **do not snare**: biopsy the suspicious
  area, tattoo, photograph and refer for MDT/surgical or specialist resection.
- Large non-pedunculated lesions (≥ 20 mm) → plan EMR/ESD at a dedicated list rather
  than an ad-hoc attempt that scars the lesion.

## 4. Cold snare polypectomy (polyps < 10 mm)
- **CSP is the standard** for 3–9 mm (and usually diminutive ≤ 3 mm too, where cold
  forceps leave residual tissue). It avoids thermal injury and delayed bleeding.
- Position the polyp at 5–6 o'clock, deflate slightly, open a snare sized to the
  polyp, capture with a **1–2 mm rim of normal mucosa**, close firmly and steadily
  through to transection.
- Inspect the defect: a small protruding submucosal stalk ("cold snare defect
  protrusion") is common and usually benign; residual polyp at the edge → re-snare.
- Mild oozing usually stops; clipping is not routinely needed for small cold defects.
- Retrieve for histology (suction trap); multiple polyps from one segment can share a pot.

## 5. Larger and pedunculated lesions
- Pedunculated ≥ 10 mm: hot snare; consider prophylactic clip or loop on thick stalks
  (head ≥ 20 mm or stalk ≥ 10 mm) to reduce bleeding.
- 10–19 mm non-pedunculated: cold snare (piecemeal) or EMR per local practice.

## Complications
- **Immediate bleeding:** snare tip or clips; adrenaline injection as adjunct.
- **Delayed bleeding** (hours to ~2 weeks): mostly after hot resection of large right-sided
  lesions, especially on anticoagulants — repeat colonoscopy if ongoing.
- **Perforation:** recognise target sign / visible defect, close endoscopically with
  clips if small and the patient is stable, give antibiotics, involve surgeons early.

## Gotchas
- Antithrombotics: cold snare of small polyps is generally done on continued antiplatelet
  therapy; anticoagulant holding varies — **verify current BSG/ESGE or ASGE guidance and
  local protocol**.
- An incomplete prep hides flat lesions; don't sign off "no polyps" behind stool.
- Tattoo 2–3 cm **distal** to the lesion (and not into it) so the surgeon or endoscopist
  can still see and resect it.
