# Acute upper GI bleed: risk scoring and endoscopy timing

> General clinical reference, not patient-specific advice. PPI regimens, reversal agents and transfusion targets must be checked against current guidelines and the local formulary. Variceal bleeding has its own bundle — see `health-medicine.gastroenterology.hepatology.decompensated-cirrhosis-ascites-encephalopathy-and-varices`.

**Goal:** stabilise, sort the many low-risk patients from the few high-risk ones,
scope the right patient at the right time, stop active bleeding, and catch rebleeding.

## 1. Resuscitate first
- ABC, two large-bore cannulas, group and screen/crossmatch, FBC, U&E (urea rises with
  upper bleeding), clotting, LFTs, lactate.
- **Restrictive transfusion:** typically transfuse at Hb < 70 g/L targeting 70–90;
  higher threshold (~80, target ≥ 100) with significant cardiovascular disease. Massive
  bleeding follows the major haemorrhage protocol (see
  `health-medicine.anesthesiology.anesthetic-crisis-management.perioperative-anaphylaxis-malignant-hyperthermia-and-massive-haemorrhage`).
- Ask early: **cirrhosis/varices?** (changes the whole bundle), anticoagulants,
  antiplatelets, NSAIDs, previous ulcer or aortic graft (aorto-enteric fistula).

## 2. Risk-score
- **Glasgow-Blatchford score (GBS)** before endoscopy: urea, Hb, systolic BP, pulse,
  melaena, syncope, hepatic disease, cardiac failure.
  - **GBS 0–1** → suitable for outpatient management/endoscopy in most guidance.
  - Higher scores → admit; GBS predicts need for intervention, not mortality.
- **Rockall** (post-endoscopy) or **AIMS65** for mortality risk.

## 3. Pre-endoscopy
- **IV PPI** may be started pre-endoscopy (downstages lesions; does not change
  mortality) — don't let it delay endoscopy.
- **Prokinetic** (IV erythromycin ~30–120 min before) improves views if not contraindicated (QT).
- **Anticoagulants:** hold; reverse for life-threatening bleeding (warfarin: PCC + vitamin K;
  DOACs: specific reversal agent or PCC per local protocol). Discuss aspirin for secondary
  prevention with cardiology — usually continued or restarted early.
- Tranexamic acid is **not** recommended (HALT-IT: no benefit, more VTE).

## 4. Timing of endoscopy
- **Within 24 h** of presentation for admitted patients.
- Earlier (after resuscitation) for haemodynamic instability not responding to fluids.
  Very early (< 6 h) endoscopy did not improve mortality in a high-risk trial — resuscitate
  first, then scope; secure the airway in massive haematemesis or reduced consciousness.

## 5. Endoscopic findings and therapy (Forrest)
- **Ia** spurting, **Ib** oozing, **IIa** non-bleeding visible vessel → treat.
- **IIb** adherent clot → attempt removal and treat the underlying stigmata (individualise).
- **IIc** pigmented spot, **III** clean base → no endoscopic therapy; early feeding/discharge.
Therapy: **dual therapy** — adrenaline injection must be combined with a second modality
(clips, thermal coagulation, or sclerosant); adrenaline alone is inadequate. Haemostatic
powder as a bridge for diffuse or malignant bleeding; over-the-scope clips for difficult
or recurrent lesions.
After high-risk stigmata treated: **high-dose PPI** (bolus + infusion or intermittent
high-dose) for ~72 h, then oral twice daily (regimens vary — verify).

## 6. Rebleeding
- Repeat endoscopy for clinical rebleeding.
- Failure → **interventional radiology** (CT angiography then embolisation) before
  surgery in most centres; surgery for failed IR or perforation.

## 7. After the bleed
- Test and treat **H. pylori** (false negatives on PPI/acute bleed — retest if negative).
- Stop NSAIDs; long-term PPI if antithrombotics must continue.
- Lower GI bleed: **Oakland score** ≤ 8 → safe discharge pathway; unstable lower
  bleeding → CT angiography first (and consider an upper source — 10–15% of
  "lower" bleeds are upper).

## Gotchas
- Initial Hb can be normal in acute haemorrhage; tachycardia and urea matter more.
- Beta-blockers mask tachycardia; elderly decompensate silently.
- Melaena with a raised urea:creatinine ratio points upstream.

## Verify
- [ ] GBS documented; low-risk patients managed as outpatients where appropriate.
- [ ] Restrictive transfusion target written.
- [ ] Varices considered; anticoagulants addressed.
- [ ] Endoscopy within 24 h; Forrest grade recorded; dual therapy for high-risk stigmata.
- [ ] Rebleeding plan (IR/surgery) and H. pylori testing arranged.
