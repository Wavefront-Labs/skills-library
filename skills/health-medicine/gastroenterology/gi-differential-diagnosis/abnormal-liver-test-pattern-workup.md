# Abnormal liver test pattern workup

> General clinical reference, not patient-specific advice. Reference ranges, screening panels and referral thresholds vary by laboratory and region; follow local pathways.

**Goal:** turn "deranged LFTs" into a named pattern, a short list of causes, a
severity judgement and a clear next step — without either ignoring a mild persistent
abnormality or over-investigating a trivial one.

## 1. Is it really liver, and is it really new?
- **ALT** is the most liver-specific transaminase. **AST** also rises from muscle
  (exercise, rhabdomyolysis, myositis), red cells (haemolysis) and heart.
- **ALP** also comes from bone, placenta (pregnancy) and growth (adolescents). Confirm
  hepatic origin with a raised **GGT** (or ALP isoenzymes).
- **Isolated unconjugated bilirubin** with normal enzymes and no haemolysis is usually
  **Gilbert syndrome** — reassure, don't investigate endlessly.
- Look back at old results: chronicity changes the differential.

## 2. Classify the pattern (R factor)
`R = (ALT / ALT ULN) ÷ (ALP / ALP ULN)`
- **R ≥ 5 → hepatocellular:** viral hepatitis, alcohol, MASLD (formerly NAFLD), drugs,
  autoimmune hepatitis, ischaemic hepatitis, haemochromatosis, Wilson disease.
- **R ≤ 2 → cholestatic:** biliary obstruction (stones, malignancy), PBC, PSC, drugs,
  infiltration (metastases, granulomatous disease), sepsis.
- **2 < R < 5 → mixed.**
Clues: **AST:ALT > 2** suggests alcohol (or advanced fibrosis of any cause);
transaminases in the **thousands** point to ischaemia, viral, paracetamol/toxin, or
acute stone passage (transient).

## 3. Judge severity before aetiology
Synthetic function and the patient decide urgency, not the enzyme height:
- **INR prolonged (not explained by warfarin/vitamin K deficiency)**, rising bilirubin,
  **encephalopathy** → possible **acute liver failure**: same-day discussion with a liver
  unit. Hypoglycaemia and acidosis are late, bad signs.
- **Jaundice with cholestatic pattern** → urgent ultrasound for duct dilatation;
  fever + jaundice + pain = cholangitis (see pancreaticobiliary skills).
- Low albumin and platelets suggest **chronic** disease and possible cirrhosis.

## 4. Staged aetiology screen (typical "liver screen")
First line, guided by pattern:
- History: alcohol units, all medications incl. herbal/supplements and recent
  antibiotics (amoxicillin-clavulanate is a classic DILI cause), travel, sexual and
  injecting risks, metabolic syndrome features, autoimmune history.
- **Liver ultrasound.**
- Hepatitis B surface antigen, hepatitis C antibody (with reflex RNA); hepatitis A/E
  IgM if acute.
- Ferritin + transferrin saturation (ferritin alone is an inflammatory marker — a
  high ferritin with normal saturation is usually MASLD/alcohol, not haemochromatosis).
- Autoantibodies (ANA, smooth muscle, AMA) + immunoglobulins.
- Caeruloplasmin in younger patients (commonly < 40, local cut-offs vary);
  alpha-1 antitrypsin level.
Second line (specialist): MRCP, liver biopsy, genetic testing.

## 5. Stage fibrosis in chronic disease
For suspected MASLD or other chronic disease use a non-invasive score such as
**FIB-4** (age, AST, ALT, platelets). Low score → primary care risk-factor management;
indeterminate → elastography (e.g. transient elastography) or ELF test; high →
hepatology referral. Note FIB-4 is less reliable under ~35 and over ~65 years; cut-offs
differ by guideline — verify locally.

## Red flags — escalate same day
- INR > ~1.5 without anticoagulant, or any encephalopathy (acute liver failure).
- Paracetamol overdose history (staggered or deliberate) — treat per toxicology protocol.
- Cholangitis features; new jaundice with weight loss or a mass (possible malignancy).
- Transaminases > ~10× ULN with symptoms; pregnancy with abnormal LFTs (HELLP, acute
  fatty liver) — obstetric emergency.

## Gotchas
- A "normal" ALT does not exclude cirrhosis; burnt-out disease can have near-normal enzymes.
- Raised ALP with normal GGT in a teenager or pregnant patient is usually physiological.
- Statins rarely cause clinically important liver injury; stopping them reflexively
  for mild ALT rises is often unnecessary (confirm against local advice).
- Repeat a single mild abnormality before a full screen, but don't repeat indefinitely:
  persistent abnormality (commonly > 3–6 months) deserves the full workup.

## Verify
- [ ] Hepatic origin confirmed (GGT for ALP; CK considered for isolated AST).
- [ ] Pattern stated with an R value.
- [ ] INR, bilirubin and mental state checked before deciding urgency.
- [ ] Full drug/supplement and alcohol history recorded.
- [ ] Ultrasound and staged screen ordered to match the pattern.
- [ ] Fibrosis staged (FIB-4 ± elastography) for chronic disease; referral rule applied.
