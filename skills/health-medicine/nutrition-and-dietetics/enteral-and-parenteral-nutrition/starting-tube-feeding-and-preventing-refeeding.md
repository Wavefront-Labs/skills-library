# Starting tube feeding and preventing refeeding syndrome

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** get nutrition into a patient who can't eat enough, by the safest route, at
a rate that doesn't trigger refeeding syndrome.

## 1. Choose the route
- **Gut works → use it.** Enteral nutrition (EN) before parenteral (PN).
- Short term (<4–6 weeks): **nasogastric (NG)**; nasojejunal if gastric feeding fails
  (gastroparesis, aspiration, pancreatitis in some pathways).
- Long term: **gastrostomy (PEG/RIG)** or jejunostomy.
- **PN** only if the gut is non-functional, inaccessible, or insufficient (e.g.
  obstruction, high-output fistula, short bowel, prolonged ileus), usually via a
  dedicated central/PICC lumen.

## 2. Confirm NG position before every use
- **First-line: aspirate pH ≤5.5** (litmus/pH strip per policy; NHS Improvement 2016 alert (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)).
- If no aspirate or pH >5.5: **chest x-ray** read by a competent person, documenting
  the tube following the oesophagus, bisecting the carina, crossing the diaphragm,
  tip below the left hemidiaphragm (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
- **Never** use the "whoosh" test, litmus paper, or absence of respiratory distress.
- Check the external length mark each time. Misplaced NG feeding is a never event.

## 3. Grade refeeding risk (NICE CG32, recommendations 1.4.4-1.4.7, Box 1; checked on nice.org.uk)
High risk if **one** of: BMI <16; unintentional loss >15% within the last 3–6 months; little/no
intake >10 days; low K/PO₄/Mg before feeding. **Or two** of: BMI <18.5; loss >10% within
3–6 months; little/no intake >5 days; history of alcohol abuse or drugs incl.
insulin, chemotherapy, antacids, diuretics.
Extreme cases (e.g. BMI <14, negligible intake >15 days): NICE says use only 5 kcal/kg/day with continuous cardiac rhythm monitoring; get specialist input.

## 4. Start low, go slow, replace, monitor
- High risk: start at a maximum of **10 kcal/kg/day** (5 kcal/kg/day in extreme cases), increasing slowly to meet or exceed full needs by 4 to 7 days (NICE CG32). Little or no intake for >5 days: no more than 50% of requirements for the first 2 days, then increase if monitoring shows no refeeding problems.
- **Thiamine** immediately before and during the first 10 days of feeding: oral thiamin 200 to 300 mg daily, vitamin B co strong 1 or 2 tablets three times daily (or full-dose IV vitamin B preparation if necessary) and a balanced multivitamin/trace element supplement daily (NICE CG32).
- **Bloods:** K, PO₄, Mg at baseline and monitored frequently from the start (daily for the first days is common practice (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)); NICE advises supplementing potassium, phosphate and magnesium unless levels are high before feeding; replace per protocol and do not wait for levels to crash.
- Watch for fluid overload, oedema, arrhythmias, confusion.

## 5. Regimen maths (example)
Target 1,800 kcal, 80 g protein. Formula 1.5 kcal/mL, 60 g protein/L (arithmetic recomputed: 1,800 ÷ 1.5 = 1,200; 1.2 × 60 = 72; 1,200 ÷ 20 = 60):
1,800 ÷ 1.5 = **1,200 mL/day** → protein 1.2 L × 60 = 72 g (short by 8 g → add
protein module or a higher-protein formula). Continuous over 20 h = **60 mL/h** (with
4 h rest for gastric pH/medication). Add water flushes for fluid needs (e.g. 30–50 mL
before/after feeds and medications, adjusted to total fluid target).

## 6. Tolerance
- Diarrhoea: check drugs first (antibiotics, sorbitol-containing liquids, laxatives),
  C. difficile; fibre-containing feed; slow the rate.
- Vomiting/aspiration: head of bed ≥30–45° (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked), prokinetics, post-pyloric feeding.
- Gastric residual volume checks are no longer routine in many ICUs; follow local protocol.

## Gotchas
- Drugs and feeds interact (phenytoin, levothyroxine, some antibiotics need breaks;
  don't crush modified-release tablets) — involve pharmacy.
- Blocked tubes: flush with warm water; never use a guidewire to unblock.
- Hyperglycaemia is common at start — monitor glucose.

Note: refeeding criteria and starting rates follow NICE CG32 (UK), checked this session; the ASPEN 2020 consensus differs slightly (not re-read). Doses for electrolytes vary by protocol.

## Verify
- [ ] Route justified; NG position confirmed by pH or x-ray before use.
- [ ] Refeeding risk graded and documented.
- [ ] Starting rate matches risk; thiamine prescribed.
- [ ] K/PO₄/Mg monitored daily and replaced.
- [ ] Regimen meets energy, protein and fluid targets by day ~4–7.
