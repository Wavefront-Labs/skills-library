# Texture-modified diets with IDDSI

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** produce texture-modified food and thickened drinks that match the
prescribed IDDSI level every time — consistently safe for people with dysphagia and
still nutritious and appetising.

## 1. The IDDSI framework
Levels are prescribed by a speech and language therapist (or equivalent) after
swallow assessment.
| Level | Drinks | Foods |
|---|---|---|
| 0 Thin | Water-like | — |
| 1 Slightly thick | Thicker than water, flows through a straw | — |
| 2 Mildly thick | Flows off a spoon, sippable | — |
| 3 Moderately thick | Can be drunk from a cup; smooth | Liquidised (same) |
| 4 Extremely thick | Spoon-held, not drinkable | Pureed |
| 5 | — | Minced and moist (≤4 mm lumps adults) |
| 6 | — | Soft and bite-sized (≤1.5 cm pieces adults) |
| 7 | — | Easy to chew / Regular |
Transitional foods (e.g. some wafers that melt with moisture) have specific uses.

## 2. Testing — every batch
- **IDDSI Flow Test (drinks, levels 0–3):** 10 mL slip-tip syringe, fill to 10 mL,
  let flow for **10 seconds**, read the remaining volume: <1 mL = level 0; 1–4 mL = 1;
  4–8 mL = 2; >8 mL (some drips) = 3. Level 4 should not flow.
- **Fork drip test:** level 3 drips slowly through fork prongs; level 4 sits on the
  fork in a mound, a small tail may form.
- **Spoon tilt test (level 4, 5):** food holds its shape on the spoon and slides off
  easily with little residue when tilted; not sticky.
- **Fork pressure test (levels 5–7):** pressing with a fork (thumbnail blanches white)
  squashes the food; for level 5 lumps fit between fork prongs (~4 mm).
- Retest after holding hot/cold — thickness changes with time and temperature;
  gum-based thickeners are more stable than starch-based ones.

## 3. High-risk foods to exclude from levels 4–6
Mixed thin-and-thick textures (cereal in milk, soup with pieces, watery fruit),
stringy/fibrous (pineapple, celery, beans skins), hard/dry (nuts, crackers,
toast), crunchy, crumbly, sticky (peanut butter, sticky rice, mashed potato that's
too gluey), round/long shapes (grapes, sausages), skins and husks, floppy (lettuce),
juicy foods that release fluid.

## 4. Nutrition — don't let modification starve people
Pureeing adds water and dilutes energy and protein. Counter it:
- **Fortify** with milk powder, cream, butter, cheese, oils; enriched sauces.
- Offer smaller, nutrient-dense meals plus snacks within the texture level.
- Present food appealingly (moulded or piped purees, separate colours) — intake
  rises when it looks like food.
- Monitor weight and intake; dietitian review for anyone at nutrition risk.

## 5. Systems: allergens, labelling and handover
- Standardised recipes with yield, thickener quantities and test results.
- Each tray/meal labelled with name, **IDDSI level (number and colour)**, allergens.
- Allergen controls: ingredient specifications, 14 major allergens (UK/EU) or
  local regulatory list, segregation, cleaning, and staff checks at service.
- Diet code communicated from ward to kitchen and verified at plating and at the bedside.

## Gotchas
- Thickened drinks made "by eye" are inconsistent — use scoop measures and test.
- Bread, biscuits and jelly/ice cream (melt to thin liquid) are common errors at levels 4–5.
- Drinks thickened to a higher level than prescribed reduce fluid intake → dehydration.
- Medicines may need the pharmacist's advice — not all can be crushed into puree.

Uncertainty note: test descriptions are summarised from the IDDSI framework
(version current at writing); consult the official IDDSI testing methods.

## Verify
- [ ] Each modified item tested with the correct IDDSI test before service.
- [ ] High-risk foods excluded at the prescribed level.
- [ ] Fortification built into texture-modified recipes.
- [ ] Trays labelled with IDDSI level and allergens; bedside check done.
- [ ] Weight and intake monitored for residents on modified diets.
