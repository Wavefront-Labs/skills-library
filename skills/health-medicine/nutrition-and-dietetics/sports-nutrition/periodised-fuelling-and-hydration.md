# Periodised fuelling and hydration

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** match intake to the training and competition demands of each day, protect
health (energy availability, bone, hormones), and use only supplements that are
evidence-based and anti-doping safe.

## 1. Map the week
Get the training plan: sessions per day, type (endurance, high intensity, strength,
skill), duration, and key sessions or competitions. Nutrition is planned **around the
training**, not as a fixed daily diet ("fuel for the work required").

## 2. Carbohydrate by load (per kg body mass per day)
| Load | Approx. carbohydrate |
|---|---|
| Light / skill-based | 3–5 g/kg |
| Moderate (~1 h/day) | 5–7 g/kg |
| High (1–3 h/day moderate–high intensity) | 6–10 g/kg |
| Very high (>4–5 h/day) | 8–12 g/kg |
- **Before:** 1–4 g/kg in the 1–4 h pre-event; familiar, low-fibre/fat if gut-sensitive.
- **During:** events >60–90 min: ~30–60 g/h; >2.5 h: up to ~90 g/h using
  glucose+fructose mixes — **train the gut** in practice sessions first.
- **Carbohydrate loading** (events >90 min): ~10–12 g/kg/day for 36–48 h with taper.
- Deliberate low-carbohydrate sessions ("train low") are an advanced tool for
  specific adaptation sessions, not a default.

## 3. Protein
- ~1.2–2.0 g/kg/day total, in **~0.3 g/kg doses (≈20–40 g) every 3–4 h**, including
  after training and possibly before sleep. Higher end during energy restriction or injury.
- Leucine-rich sources (dairy, meat, eggs, soy) or larger servings of plant blends.

## 4. Hydration — measure, don't guess
- **Sweat rate (L/h)** = (pre-weight − post-weight in kg) + fluid drunk (L) − urine (L),
  divided by hours. Weigh nude/towel-dried.
- Aim to limit body-mass loss to **<2%** in most settings; avoid gaining weight
  during events (hyponatraemia risk from over-drinking).
- Rehydrate ~**1.25–1.5 L per kg** lost, with sodium for heavy/salty sweaters.
- Pre-exercise: ~5–10 mL/kg in the 2–4 h before; pale-yellow urine as rough guide.

## 5. Screen for low energy availability / RED-S
Energy availability = (intake − exercise energy expenditure) ÷ fat-free mass; <~30
kcal/kg FFM/day is linked to problems. Ask about menstrual function (amenorrhoea is
**not** normal for athletes), stress fractures, recurrent illness, fatigue,
performance plateau, low libido in males, disordered eating. Refer to medical/
psychology team when present.

## 6. Supplements — evidence and risk
- Strong evidence for specific uses: **caffeine** (~3 mg/kg, 30–60 min pre), **creatine**
  monohydrate (3–5 g/day), **nitrate/beetroot** (endurance), **beta-alanine**
  (1–4 min efforts), **sodium bicarbonate** (high-intensity; GI side effects),
  plus sports foods (gels, drinks, protein) and medical supplements for deficiency
  (iron, vitamin D) only when confirmed by testing.
- Any athlete subject to testing: **strict liability** — use only **batch-tested**
  products (e.g. Informed Sport or equivalent third-party certification); even then risk isn't zero.

## Gotchas
- Weight-making sports (rowing, combat, jockeys) need a structured, gradual plan —
  rapid dehydration cuts are dangerous.
- Young athletes: growth needs come first; avoid adult supplement practices.
- Teach portion-based plates (e.g. easy/moderate/hard day plates) — g/kg numbers don't translate to the kitchen alone.

Uncertainty note: figures from IOC consensus (2018), ACSM/AND/DC 2016 position stand
and IOC REDs 2023 statement; individual needs vary.
