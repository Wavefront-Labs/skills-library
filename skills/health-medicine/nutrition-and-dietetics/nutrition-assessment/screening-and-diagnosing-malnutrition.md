# Screening and diagnosing malnutrition

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** find the at-risk patient with a quick screen, then confirm and grade
malnutrition with a structured assessment so the intervention and its urgency are right.

## 1. Screen everyone (minutes)
Use a validated tool on admission and at set intervals (weekly inpatients, at
registration/annual review in community):
- **MUST** (adults): BMI score + unplanned weight loss in 3–6 months score + acute
  disease effect (no intake likely for >5 days). Total 0 = low, 1 = medium,
  **≥2 = high risk** → refer/treat.
- **NRS-2002** (hospital), **MNA-SF** (older adults), paediatric tools (STAMP,
  PYMS) for children.
A screen is a sorting step, not a diagnosis.

## 2. Assess (the dietitian's work-up)
**Anthropometry**
- Measured weight and height (recalled values are often wrong). If unweighable:
  MUAC (<23.5 cm suggests BMI <20 in adults), ulna length for height.
- **% weight loss** = (usual − current) ÷ usual × 100. Time frame matters: 5% in a
  month is more significant than 5% in a year.
- Adjust for oedema/ascites — fluid masks wasting.

**Diet history**
- 24-hour recall plus usual pattern; estimate % of requirements met; duration of reduced intake.
- Barriers: appetite, nausea, chewing/swallowing, mood, cost, access, cooking ability.

**Nutrition-focused physical exam**
- Muscle loss: temples, clavicles, shoulders, interosseous, quadriceps, calves.
- Fat loss: orbital, triceps, ribs.
- Handgrip strength (dynamometer) as functional measure.
- Micronutrient signs: angular stomatitis, glossitis, hair/skin/nail changes.

**Requirements estimate**
- Energy: predictive equations (e.g. Henry/Mifflin-St Jeor with activity/stress
  factors) or ~25–30 kcal/kg/day as a starting estimate in many adults; indirect
  calorimetry is the gold standard in ICU.
- Protein ~1.0–1.5 g/kg/day for most unwell adults (higher in critical illness, burns,
  wounds; adjust in kidney disease per stage and dialysis).
- Fluid ~30–35 mL/kg/day adults, adjusted for losses and restrictions.

## 3. Diagnose (GLIM)
Requires ≥1 **phenotypic** criterion (non-volitional weight loss; low BMI; reduced
muscle mass) **and** ≥1 **etiologic** criterion (reduced intake/assimilation;
disease burden/inflammation). Severity graded by weight-loss and BMI thresholds
(e.g. stage 2/severe: >10% in 6 months or >20% beyond 6 months; BMI <18.5 if <70 y).
Write as a nutrition diagnosis (PES): "Severe malnutrition related to reduced intake
from dysphagia as evidenced by 12% weight loss in 3 months and quadriceps wasting."

## 4. Biochemistry — interpret, don't over-read
- **Albumin and prealbumin are inflammation markers**, not nutrition markers; they
  fall with acute illness and fluid shifts. Don't diagnose malnutrition from them.
- Useful labs: electrolytes (K, Mg, phosphate — refeeding risk), glucose/HbA1c,
  renal and liver function, CRP (inflammation context), micronutrients where
  indicated (iron studies, B12, folate, vitamin D, zinc — interpret with CRP).

## Gotchas
- Obese patients can be malnourished — sarcopenic obesity is easy to miss.
- Missing weights are themselves a risk flag; get the patient weighed.
- High-risk screens without a documented plan = no screen.

Uncertainty note: thresholds summarised from BAPEN (MUST), ESPEN and GLIM consensus;
local adaptations exist.

- [ ] Muscle/fat exam and intake estimate documented.
