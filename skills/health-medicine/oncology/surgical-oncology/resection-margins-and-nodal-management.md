# Resection margins and nodal management

**Goal:** remove the cancer with clear margins and appropriate nodal staging, with no
more morbidity than the oncological benefit justifies, in the right order relative to
systemic and radiation therapy.

## 1. Decide resectability and intent at the MDT
- Classify as **resectable, borderline resectable, locally advanced/unresectable, or
  metastatic**, using tumour-specific criteria (for example vascular involvement in
  pancreatic cancer, CRM threat on MRI in rectal cancer).
- Weigh fitness: performance status, cardiopulmonary reserve, frailty, nutrition;
  prehabilitation where time allows.
- Selected **oligometastatic** disease (for example colorectal liver metastases) may be
  resected with curative intent; this is an MDT decision.

## 2. Plan the margin
Residual tumour (R) classification after resection:

| R | Meaning |
|---|---|
| R0 | No residual tumour; microscopically clear margins |
| R1 | Microscopic residual tumour at a margin |
| R2 | Macroscopic residual tumour left |

- Required clear distance varies by tumour and margin type: for example "no tumour on
  ink" is widely accepted for invasive breast cancer with breast conservation, while
  rectal CRM is considered involved at 1 mm or less, and sarcoma and melanoma use
  defined wider margins. Check current tumour-specific guidance.
- Resect **en bloc** with adherent structures rather than dissecting tumour off them;
  tumour spillage or rupture upstages risk (notably in sarcoma and GIST).
- Orientate the specimen with sutures/clips and annotate the request form so the
  pathologist can report each margin.
- Frozen section helps for selected margins (for example bile duct, ureteric) but not
  as a substitute for planning.

## 3. Manage the nodes
- **Sentinel lymph node biopsy (SLNB)** stages the clinically node-negative axilla or
  basin with less morbidity than full dissection (breast, melanoma, vulval,
  endometrial and others). Use dual tracer (radioisotope plus blue dye or alternative)
  per local protocol.
- **Formal lymphadenectomy** where nodes are clinically involved or standard for the
  tumour (for example D2 gastrectomy, mesocolic excision with central vascular ligation,
  TME for rectal cancer). Minimum harvested node counts are used as quality markers
  (for example at least 12 nodes for colon cancer staging).
- Trials have narrowed indications for completion dissection after a positive
  sentinel node in some settings (breast, melanoma); follow current evidence.

## 4. Sequence with other treatments
- **Neoadjuvant** chemotherapy/radiotherapy to downstage, test biology and treat
  micrometastases early (for example rectal, oesophageal, HER2-positive or
  triple-negative breast). Mark the tumour (clip) before neoadjuvant therapy so the site
  can be found after a complete response.
- **Organ preservation**: after clinical complete response in rectal cancer, a
  watch-and-wait programme with intensive surveillance may be offered in experienced
  centres.
- **Adjuvant** therapy decisions depend on final pathological stage (yp/pTNM) and
  margins; R1 may prompt re-excision or adjuvant radiotherapy.
- **Cytoreductive surgery with or without HIPEC** for selected peritoneal disease;
  peritoneal cancer index (PCI) and completeness of cytoreduction (CC score) guide
  selection and prognosis.

## Gotchas
- Excisional biopsy without a plan can compromise later definitive surgery; core first.
- Unclipped tumours that respond completely to neoadjuvant therapy can become
  unlocatable.
- Surgery on an unstaged patient risks a non-therapeutic laparotomy; stage first.
- A positive margin on the report may reflect specimen shrinkage or fragmentation:
  discuss with the pathologist before re-operating.

## Verify
- [ ] Resectability and intent agreed at MDT with staging complete.
- [ ] Margin goal and nodal strategy defined pre-operatively for the tumour type.
- [ ] Specimen orientated; R status and node count reported.
- [ ] Neoadjuvant/adjuvant sequencing documented; tumour marked if neoadjuvant.
- [ ] Post-op MDT review with final pathology and adjuvant decision.
