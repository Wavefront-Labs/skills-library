# Oncologic emergency recognition and initial management

**Goal:** spot the time-critical cancer emergencies early, start the first-hour actions,
and get the right specialist involved before irreversible harm occurs.

## 1. Neutropenic sepsis
- **Suspect in any patient within weeks of chemotherapy** who is febrile or unwell,
  even without fever (steroids, elderly, and sepsis can blunt fever). Definitions vary:
  commonly neutrophils below 0.5 x 10^9/L (or expected to fall) with temperature
  above 38 °C; check local definition.
- **Do not wait for the neutrophil count.** Give empirical broad-spectrum
  intravenous antibiotics within **1 hour** of presentation (door-to-needle), after
  blood cultures (peripheral and from any central line). Antibiotic choice per local
  protocol (typically anti-pseudomonal beta-lactam).
- Assess for sepsis (lactate, NEWS2/qSOFA) and source; escalate to critical care early.
- Risk-stratify for possible early discharge/oral switch with **MASCC** (score of 21 or
  more = low risk) or CISNE, per local policy.

## 2. Metastatic spinal cord compression (MSCC)
- Red flags: new thoracic or progressive back pain, band-like or nocturnal pain, pain on
  coughing/straining, radicular pain, then limb weakness, sensory level, sphincter
  disturbance. **Treat pain-only presentations as a window of opportunity.**
- **Whole-spine MRI** urgently, within the time targets of the local/national MSCC
  pathway (fastest when neurological deficit is present); multi-level disease is common.
- Start **dexamethasone** if neurological deficit, per local protocol (doses vary
  between guidelines), with gastroprotection and glucose monitoring.
- Nurse flat with spinal precautions if spinal instability is suspected (SINS score
  helps assess instability). Same-day discussion with the acute oncology/MSCC
  coordinator for **surgery vs radiotherapy**. Ambulatory status at treatment is the
  strongest predictor of outcome.

## 3. Tumour lysis syndrome (TLS)
- Highest risk: bulky, rapidly proliferating tumours (Burkitt, acute leukaemia with high
  white count, high-grade lymphoma), high LDH, pre-existing renal impairment.
- **Cairo–Bishop laboratory TLS:** two or more of hyperuricaemia, hyperkalaemia,
  hyperphosphataemia, hypocalcaemia within the defined window around treatment
  (thresholds or 25% change from baseline); **clinical TLS** adds AKI, arrhythmia or
  seizure.
- Prevention by risk: hydration, **allopurinol** for intermediate risk, **rasburicase**
  for high risk or established hyperuricaemia (contraindicated in G6PD deficiency;
  check first). Monitor electrolytes frequently; treat hyperkalaemia urgently; early
  renal input for dialysis.

## 4. Hypercalcaemia of malignancy
- Symptoms: thirst, polyuria, constipation, confusion, drowsiness, abdominal pain.
  Use adjusted (corrected) calcium; severity bands per local lab/guideline.
- Mechanisms: PTHrP (humoral, e.g. squamous cancers), osteolytic metastases, myeloma,
  calcitriol (some lymphomas). Check PTH to exclude coexisting hyperparathyroidism.
- Management: **intravenous fluid rehydration first**, then **bisphosphonate** (for
  example zoledronic acid, dose-adjusted for renal function) or **denosumab** if
  refractory or renal impairment; stop calcium-raising drugs. Treat the underlying
  cancer; recurrence is common.

## 5. Other emergencies to keep in mind
- **Superior vena cava obstruction:** facial/arm swelling, distended chest veins,
  breathlessness. Obtain histology if unknown; stenting gives rapid relief; treat cause.
- **Hyperviscosity** (Waldenström, myeloma) and **leucostasis** (very high blasts):
  urgent haematology.
- Raised intracranial pressure from brain metastases, malignant pericardial effusion,
  airway obstruction, immunotherapy toxicity (myocarditis, colitis).

## Gotchas
- Afebrile neutropenic sepsis kills; act on "unwell after chemo".
- Waiting for neurological signs in MSCC means waiting for permanent paralysis.
- Rasburicase interferes with uric acid samples at room temperature: follow lab
  handling instructions.
- Calcium falls slowly after bisphosphonate (days); fluids drive early improvement.

## Verify
- [ ] Antibiotics within 1 hour for suspected neutropenic sepsis; cultures taken.
- [ ] Suspected MSCC: whole-spine MRI arranged within pathway target, steroids considered,
      MSCC coordinator contacted.
- [ ] TLS risk assessed before cytotoxic therapy; prophylaxis matched to risk; G6PD
      checked before rasburicase.
- [ ] Hypercalcaemia: adjusted calcium, PTH, renal function; fluids then antiresorptive.
- [ ] Acute oncology team informed and escalation plan documented.
