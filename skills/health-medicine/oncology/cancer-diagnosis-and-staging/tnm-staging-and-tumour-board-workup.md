# TNM staging and tumour-board workup

**Goal:** move from "suspicious lesion" to a tissue-confirmed, correctly staged,
biomarker-annotated case that a multidisciplinary team can make a treatment decision on.

## 1. Plan the biopsy before you take it
- **Biopsy the lesion that stages highest** when it is safely accessible: a liver or bone
  lesion that proves metastatic disease answers diagnosis and stage in one procedure.
- **Core biopsy over fine-needle aspiration** when architecture, grade, or enough tissue
  for immunohistochemistry (IHC) and molecular testing is needed. Lymphoma generally
  needs excisional or generous core tissue, not FNA.
- Consider the **surgical path**: in suspected sarcoma or some resectable tumours the
  biopsy tract may need to be excised later, so discuss with the treating surgeon first.
- Request **reflex biomarkers** appropriate to the tumour (for example ER/PR/HER2 in
  breast, EGFR/ALK/PD-L1 and broader panels in non-small-cell lung cancer, MMR/MSI in
  colorectal). Panels change quickly; check current guideline lists.

## 2. Stage with the right system and prefix
TNM (AJCC/UICC, aligned but check edition differences) describes:

| Element | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **T** | Primary tumour size and/or local extent (Tis, T1–T4, TX) |
| **N** | Regional lymph-node involvement (N0–N3, NX) |
| **M** | Distant metastasis (M0, M1, often subdivided) |

Prefixes matter and must be recorded:
- **c** clinical (exam, imaging, biopsy before treatment)
- **p** pathological (after resection)
- **yc / yp** after neoadjuvant therapy
- **r** recurrence

TNM categories are combined into a **prognostic stage group** (0–IV); in some
tumours (for example breast, prostate) grade and biomarkers alter the group. Other
systems apply in specific settings: FIGO for gynaecological cancers, Lugano/Ann Arbor
for lymphoma, ISS/R-ISS for myeloma, Breslow/Clark data within melanoma TNM. Record the
edition used.

## 3. Staging investigations
- Tailor imaging to the tumour's spread pattern: CT chest/abdomen/pelvis is a common
  baseline; **PET-CT** where guidelines support it (for example lung, lymphoma,
  oesophageal, head and neck); **MRI** for local staging of rectum, prostate, brain,
  liver characterisation; bone scan where bone spread is likely.
- **Confirm unexpected M1 findings** with tissue when it changes intent; a solitary
  PET-avid lesion is not always metastasis.
- Baseline bloods including tumour markers only where validated (for example PSA,
  CA-125, AFP/hCG/LDH in germ-cell tumours); markers are not diagnostic alone.

## 4. Assess the patient, not just the tumour
- **Performance status:** ECOG 0–4 (or Karnofsky 0–100). ECOG 0–1 is usually
  required for intensive therapy and trials.
- Comorbidity, organ function (renal, hepatic, cardiac), frailty screen (for example G8)
  in older adults, and patient goals.

## 5. Present to the tumour board / MDT
Use a fixed structure so nothing is missed:
1. Age, performance status, key comorbidities, patient preferences.
2. Presentation and diagnostic pathway.
3. Histology, grade, biomarkers (pending ones flagged).
4. Imaging findings with stage written as, for example, cT2 cN1 cM0, stage group, edition.
5. **The specific question** for the meeting (resectability, intent, trial eligibility).
6. Outcome recorded with intent (curative vs palliative), sequence, and owner.

## Gotchas
- Mixing c and p staging, or omitting the y prefix after neoadjuvant therapy, makes
  outcomes and trial eligibility wrong.
- Staging edition changes alter stage groups; do not compare across editions loosely.
- Insufficient tissue is the commonest cause of delay; ask for extra cores at the first
  procedure.
- Imaging is reported in one hospital and discussed in another: re-review at the MDT.

## Verify
- [ ] Histological diagnosis confirmed and adequate for required biomarkers.
- [ ] TNM recorded with prefix (c/p/y), stage group, and edition.
- [ ] Staging imaging matches the tumour's guideline workup; equivocal M1 resolved.
- [ ] Performance status and organ function documented.
- [ ] MDT question explicit; outcome, intent, and responsible clinician recorded.
