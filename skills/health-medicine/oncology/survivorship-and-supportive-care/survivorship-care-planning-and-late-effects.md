# Survivorship care planning and late effects

**Goal:** control symptoms during treatment and convert "treatment finished" into a
written, risk-based plan for surveillance, late effects and recovery that the patient
and primary care can follow.

## 1. Before treatment: protect future options
- **Fertility preservation referral** before gonadotoxic chemotherapy, pelvic
  radiotherapy or gonadal surgery in anyone of reproductive age who may want children:
  sperm banking, oocyte/embryo cryopreservation, ovarian tissue cryopreservation;
  ovarian suppression is an adjunct with debated benefit. Refer early; oocyte
  stimulation takes around two weeks.
- Baseline assessments for planned cardiotoxic, nephrotoxic or ototoxic therapy (for
  example echocardiogram before anthracyclines or trastuzumab; audiometry where
  cisplatin is planned, per protocol).

## 2. During treatment: symptom control
- **Nausea and vomiting (CINV):** classify regimen emetogenicity (high, moderate, low,
  minimal) and give prophylaxis per MASCC/ESMO or ASCO guidance: high-emetic regimens
  commonly use an NK1 antagonist plus 5-HT3 antagonist plus dexamethasone, often with
  olanzapine. Treat anticipatory nausea and check for other causes (constipation,
  hypercalcaemia, brain metastases).
- **Pain:** assess type (nociceptive, neuropathic, bone); WHO analgesic ladder as a
  framework, opioid titration with laxative co-prescription; adjuvants for neuropathic
  pain; radiotherapy for painful bone metastases. Doses per verified reference.
- **Fatigue:** exclude treatable causes (anaemia, hypothyroidism, depression, sleep,
  medications); graded exercise has the best evidence.
- **Distress:** screen routinely (for example NCCN Distress Thermometer, holistic needs
  assessment) and refer to psycho-oncology as needed.

## 3. End of treatment: the treatment summary and care plan
A survivorship care plan should contain:
- Diagnosis, stage, histology, biomarkers, and dates.
- Treatments received with **cumulative doses** (anthracycline dose, radiotherapy
  fields and doses, surgery performed).
- Surveillance schedule for recurrence (which tests, how often, who orders them) and
  evidence that routine scans add value for that cancer.
- Specific **late-effect risks** and their screening.
- Symptoms that should prompt urgent review.
- Lifestyle advice: physical activity, smoking cessation, alcohol, weight; vaccinations.
- Named contacts and the division of responsibility between oncology and primary care.

## 4. Risk-based late-effect surveillance (examples)
| Exposure | Late effect | Typical surveillance |
|---|---|---|
| Anthracyclines, trastuzumab, chest RT | Cardiomyopathy, valve/coronary disease | Echocardiography schedule per cardio-oncology guidance; manage risk factors |
| Chest or mantle RT at young age | Breast cancer | Early breast screening (often MRI) per national programme |
| Neck RT | Hypothyroidism, carotid disease | Periodic thyroid function |
| Platinum agents | Ototoxicity, neuropathy, renal impairment | Hearing and renal checks |
| Alkylators, pelvic RT | Infertility, premature menopause | Hormone assessment, menopause management |
| Aromatase inhibitors, ADT, early menopause | Osteoporosis | Bone density (DXA), bone-protective therapy |
| Alkylators, topoisomerase II inhibitors | Therapy-related myeloid neoplasm | Blood count if symptomatic |
| Axillary/inguinal surgery or RT | Lymphoedema | Limb measurement, early referral |

Childhood and young-adult survivors need structured long-term follow-up (Children's
Oncology Group guidelines or national equivalents) because risks persist for decades.

## 5. Psychosocial recovery
Fear of recurrence, return to work, finances, sexual health, body image and
relationships are common unmet needs; address them explicitly and signpost services.

## Gotchas
- Cumulative doses not recorded at the end of treatment are hard to reconstruct later.
- New symptoms in survivors may be recurrence, a late effect or a second cancer; keep
  all three in the differential.
- Steroid-sparing antiemetic strategies matter for patients with diabetes or on
  immunotherapy.
- Survivorship plans fail when primary care never receives them.

## Verify
- [ ] Fertility discussed and referral offered before gonadotoxic treatment.
- [ ] Antiemetic prophylaxis matched to regimen emetogenicity.
- [ ] Treatment summary includes cumulative doses and RT fields.
- [ ] Late-effect surveillance tailored to actual exposures, with named owner.
- [ ] Copy sent to primary care; patient knows red-flag symptoms and contacts.
