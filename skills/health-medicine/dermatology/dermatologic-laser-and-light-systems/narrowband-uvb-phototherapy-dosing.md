# Narrowband UVB phototherapy dosing

**Goal:** deliver a course of 311 nm NB-UVB that clears or controls disease in the fewest
exposures, without burning, using a written protocol and calibrated equipment.

## 1. Indications and screening
- Common indications: chronic plaque and guttate **psoriasis**, **atopic dermatitis**,
  **vitiligo**, early-stage **mycosis fungoides**, chronic pruritus, prophylactic
  photohardening for polymorphic light eruption.
- Contraindications / cautions: xeroderma pigmentosum and other DNA-repair disorders,
  lupus or photo-aggravated dermatoses, history of melanoma or multiple keratinocyte cancers,
  inability to stand for the exposure time, recent ciclosporin or other
  immunosuppressant use (raises skin-cancer risk), arsenic or ionising-radiation exposure.
- Review drugs: photosensitisers (tetracyclines, thiazides, amiodarone, fluoroquinolones,
  NSAIDs, St John's wort). Reassess if a new drug starts mid-course.
- Record Fitzpatrick skin type, baseline photographs and a severity score (e.g. PASI, EASI).

## 2. Starting dose — two accepted approaches
**MED-based:** test 6–8 graded doses on unexposed skin (buttock/back), read erythema at
24 h; start at **50% of the MED** (as in the trial above; 50-70% is the commonly quoted range (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)). Most individualised; required by some protocols.

**Skin-type based** (values as reproduced in a 2024 open-access PMC trial article, Table 2; the original attribution is inconsistent and the claim that these are AAD 2019 values is (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked); the same table lists maximum doses of 2000 mJ/cm² for types I-II, 3000 for III-IV, 5000 for V-VI; check local protocol):

| Skin type | Starting dose | Increment |
|---|---|---|
| I | 130 | 15 |
| II | 220 | 25 |
| III | 260 | 40 |
| IV | 330 | 45 |
| V | 350 | 60 |
| VI | 400 | 65 |

UK/BPG-style protocols instead increase by a percentage (e.g. 10–20% per exposure).
**Vitiligo** is often started at a fixed low dose (e.g. ~200 mJ/cm²) regardless of skin
type, increased 10–20% per session to a faint persistent pink.

## 3. Frequency and course
- **2–3 sessions per week** on non-consecutive days (three weekly clears faster; two weekly
  needs fewer total exposures in some studies).
- Psoriasis clearance typically needs ~20–30 exposures; vitiligo needs months.
- Stop increasing once a maintenance dose is reached or the protocol's maximum
  (device/protocol dependent; see the maximum doses in the table note above).
- Maintenance after clearance is optional and its value debated.

## 4. Adjusting the dose
**Erythema** (ask and examine at every visit):
- Faint, asymptomatic pink → hold the dose (no increment).
- Well-defined, symptomatic erythema → omit treatment until settled, then restart at
  ~50% of the last dose (or as protocol).
- Painful/blistering burn → stop, treat, review protocol and dosimetry; incident report.

**Missed treatments** ((gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked), from the last dose given):
- 4–7 days → keep the same dose.
- 1–2 weeks → reduce by 25%.
- 2–3 weeks → reduce by 50% (or restart).
- >3 weeks → restart at the starting dose.

## 5. Safety and equipment
- **UV-protective goggles** for every exposure; shield male genitalia; cover the face if
  uninvolved; protect previously burned or photoaged sites.
- Calibrate cabinet irradiance with a radiometer regularly and on lamp change — dose =
  irradiance × time, so drift causes under- or over-treatment.
- Log every exposure (dose, time, reactions) and the **cumulative number of treatments**;
  long-term NB-UVB is associated with a much lower carcinogenic risk than PUVA, but
  patients with high cumulative exposure (often quoted as >500 treatments) warrant
  regular skin-cancer checks (thresholds vary — check local).

## Gotchas
- Patients changing position, clothing or topical products (tar, oily emollient applied
  thick) alter the delivered dose — standardise: thin emollient only, same garments.
- Subtle erythema is easy to miss in darker skin — rely on symptoms (tenderness, itch,
  burning) as well as colour.
- Starting a photosensitiser mid-course is a common cause of unexpected burns.
- Treating face/genital vitiligo and genital psoriasis requires separate protocol rules.

## Verify
- [ ] Indication, skin type, contraindications and drug history documented.
- [ ] Starting dose chosen from MED or a named skin-type table.
- [ ] Erythema and missed-dose rules applied at each visit and logged.
- [ ] Goggles worn and genital shielding used; device recently calibrated.
- [ ] Cumulative treatment count updated and skin checks planned if high.
