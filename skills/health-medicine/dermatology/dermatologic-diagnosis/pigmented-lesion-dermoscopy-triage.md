# Pigmented lesion triage with dermoscopy

**Goal:** decide, for each pigmented lesion, whether it is benign and can be left, needs
short-term monitoring, or must be excised/biopsied — using a reproducible algorithm
rather than gestalt alone.

## 1. Context before the scope
- History: new lesion in an adult (especially >50), reported change in size/shape/colour,
  bleeding, itch; personal or family history of melanoma; immunosuppression.
- Whole-skin examination first. Apply the **ugly duckling sign** — a lesion that looks
  different from the patient's other naevi is suspicious regardless of size.
- Clinical **ABCDE**: Asymmetry, Border irregularity, Colour variegation, Diameter >6 mm,
  Evolving. Evolution is the most important single feature; small (<6 mm) melanomas exist.

## 2. Step one — is it melanocytic?
Work through the **two-step algorithm** in order:
1. Melanocytic criteria: pigment network (or on acral skin, parallel patterns), aggregated
   globules, streaks, homogeneous blue pigmentation (blue naevus).
2. If absent, look for a non-melanocytic diagnosis: **BCC** (arborising vessels, leaf-like
   areas, blue-grey ovoid nests, spoke-wheels, ulceration), **seborrhoeic keratosis**
   (milia-like cysts, comedo-like openings, fissures/ridges, sharp demarcation),
   **haemangioma** (red-blue lacunae), **dermatofibroma** (central white patch with
   peripheral delicate network).
3. If none fits — treat as melanocytic by default ("featureless" lesions are not safe).

## 3. Step two — benign naevus or melanoma?
Use one validated method consistently:
- **ABCD rule of dermoscopy (Stolz)**: TDS = A×1.3 + B×0.1 + C×0.5 + D×0.5;
  <4.75 benign, 4.75–5.45 suspicious, >5.45 highly suggestive of melanoma.
- **7-point checklist**: major (2 points each) — atypical network, blue-white veil,
  atypical vascular pattern; minor (1 point each) — irregular streaks, irregular
  dots/globules, irregular blotches, regression structures. Score ≥3 → excise
  (some revised versions use ≥1 to raise sensitivity — check your protocol).
- **3-point checklist** (screening for non-experts): asymmetry of colour/structure,
  atypical network, blue-white structures; ≥2 → refer.

Melanoma-specific structures to name explicitly: atypical network, negative (inverse)
network, irregular streaks, off-centre blotch, blue-white veil, regression (white
scar-like areas, peppering), shiny white lines, atypical/polymorphous vessels,
peripheral brown structureless areas.

Site-specific patterns:
- **Acral**: parallel furrow / lattice / fibrillar = benign; **parallel ridge pattern** =
  suspicious for acral melanoma.
- **Face (chronically sun-damaged)**: asymmetric perifollicular pigmentation, annular-granular
  pattern, rhomboidal structures → suspect lentigo maligna.

## 4. Decide and act
| Finding | Action |
|---|---|
| Clearly benign pattern, stable history | Reassure; no follow-up needed |
| Mildly atypical flat lesion, no melanoma-specific structures | Short-term digital monitoring (~3 months) |
| Multiple atypical naevi / high-risk patient | Total-body photography + sequential dermoscopy (6–12 monthly) |
| Any melanoma-specific structure, positive checklist, or patient-reported change you cannot refute | Excision biopsy |
| Raised, nodular, or changing lesion | Excise — never monitor |

Biopsy technique: full-thickness excision with ~2 mm clinical margin to fat is preferred
for suspected melanoma; avoid shave biopsy if a deep lesion is possible (it can transect
and understage Breslow thickness). For large facial lesions where excision is impractical,
incisional/punch from the darkest or most raised area is acceptable — state this on the
request.

## Gotchas
- **Nodular, amelanotic and desmoplastic melanomas** fail ABCD/checklists — use the
  "EFG" rule (Elevated, Firm, Growing >1 month) and excise.
- Short-term monitoring is only for **flat** lesions; any change on follow-up → excise.
- Pressure from a contact dermatoscope blanches vessels — use polarised non-contact
  mode to assess vasculature.
- Spitzoid patterns in adults are excised; in children, management varies by centre.
- Dermoscopy improves accuracy only with training; a scored checklist does not replace
  histology.
