# Upper-face botulinum toxin: glabella, forehead, crow's feet

**Goal:** soften dynamic upper-face lines with natural brow position and no eyelid or
brow ptosis, by matching dose and point placement to each patient's muscle anatomy.

## 1. Selection and consent
- Dynamic lines respond best; static etched lines improve only partly (may need filler or
  resurfacing).
- **Contraindications**: pregnancy or breastfeeding, neuromuscular disease (myasthenia
  gravis, Lambert–Eaton, ALS), infection at site, known hypersensitivity (incl. albumin
  or cow's-milk protein for some products). Caution with aminoglycosides and other
  neuromuscular-blocking drugs.
- Screen for **body dysmorphic disorder** and unrealistic expectations. Explain onset
  2–5 days, peak ~2 weeks, duration ~3–4 months.
- Photograph at rest and in full animation (frown, raise brows, smile).

## 2. Product and dilution
- Units are **product-specific and not interchangeable** (abobotulinumtoxinA units are
  roughly 2–3× onabotulinumtoxinA units; incobotulinumtoxinA roughly 1:1 — conversion
  ratios are approximate).
- Common onabotulinumtoxinA reconstitution: 100 U in 2.5 mL preservative-free saline =
  4 U per 0.1 mL. Use 30–32G needles; record lot numbers.

## 3. Glabellar complex (corrugators, procerus)
- Labelled onabotulinumtoxinA dose: **20 U total as 5 × 4 U** — one procerus point,
  and on each side a medial corrugator (body) point and a lateral corrugator (tail) point.
  Men and strong frowners often need more (verify against product labelling/local protocol).
- Inject the lateral corrugator points **≥1 cm above the bony orbital rim**, superficially,
  roughly at or medial to the mid-pupillary line.
- Keep a finger on the orbital rim to prevent diffusion downward.

## 4. Forehead (frontalis)
- Labelled dose: 20 U as 5 × 4 U in a horizontal row, **always treated with the
  glabella** (treating frontalis alone lets depressors pull the brow down).
- Stay **≥1.5–2 cm above the brow** (higher in low-browed or older patients with
  compensatory frontalis use for dermatochalasis). Lower total doses (e.g. 6–15 U)
  in light patterns give a more natural result.
- Leave lateral frontalis partly active unless you want to drop the lateral brow;
  over-treating medial-only gives a "Spock" (Mephisto) peaked lateral brow — correct with
  1–2 U above the peak.

## 5. Lateral canthal lines (orbicularis oculi)
- Labelled dose: **12 U per side as 3 × 4 U** (24 U total), 1.5–2 cm lateral to the
  lateral canthus / ≥1 cm outside the orbital rim, intradermal-to-superficial bleb.
- Stay above the zygomatic arch and away from zygomaticus major to avoid a lip droop
  or asymmetric smile.

## 6. Aftercare and review
- Stay upright ~4 h, avoid rubbing the area; animate the treated muscles if desired.
- Review at **2 weeks**; small top-ups at review only. Minimum interval between full
  treatments ~12 weeks to reduce antibody (non-responder) risk.

## Gotchas
- **Upper eyelid ptosis** (levator diffusion): onset 2–10 days, lasts weeks. Treat with
  **apraclonidine 0.5%** eye drops (stimulates Müller's muscle) as an off-label temporising
  measure; check local guidance.
- **Brow ptosis** from forehead doses too low or too high; worst in patients with heavy
  lids who rely on frontalis.
- Asymmetric baseline brows are common — photograph and point them out before treating.
- Bruising: avoid visible vessels; anticoagulants/fish oil/ginkgo increase it.
- Counterfeit or improperly stored product causes failures and harm — source only from
  licensed suppliers.

## Verify
- [ ] Contraindications and expectations screened; consent and photos recorded.
- [ ] Product, dilution, lot number and units per point documented.
- [ ] Glabellar points ≥1 cm above orbital rim; forehead points ≥1.5–2 cm above brow.
- [ ] Forehead treated together with glabellar depressors.
- [ ] Two-week review booked; ptosis advice given.
