# Infantile hemangioma: risk stratification and propranolol

**Goal:** identify, early in infancy, which hemangiomas threaten life, function or
appearance or signal an underlying syndrome, and get them treated before the
rapid-growth window closes.

## 1. Confirm the diagnosis
- Typical course: absent or a faint precursor (telangiectasia, pallor, bruise-like patch)
  at birth, appears in the first weeks, **rapid proliferation mostly in weeks 1–8**, with
  ~80% of final size reached by ~3 months; slow involution over years.
- Morphology: superficial (bright red, lobulated), deep (blue, warm, subcutaneous), or mixed;
  focal, multifocal, segmental or indeterminate.
- **Distinguish** from congenital hemangiomas (RICH/NICH — fully formed at birth,
  GLUT1-negative, do not respond well to propranolol), vascular malformations (present at
  birth, grow proportionately — e.g. port-wine stain), pyogenic granuloma, kaposiform
  haemangioendothelioma (firm, purpuric, Kasabach–Merritt platelet consumption).
- Ultrasound with Doppler if deep and uncertain; biopsy (GLUT1) rarely needed.

## 2. Stratify risk (AAP 2019 framework)
**High risk → specialist assessment, ideally by ~1 month of age:**
- **Life-threatening**: "beard-area" segmental (airway hemangioma — stridor, hoarse cry);
  **≥5 cutaneous hemangiomas** (screen with liver ultrasound for hepatic hemangiomas,
  which can cause high-output heart failure and hypothyroidism).
- **Functional**: periocular (amblyopia from ptosis, astigmatism — urgent ophthalmology),
  nasal tip, lip, ear canal, breast bud in girls, perineal/feeding interference.
- **Ulceration risk**: lips, neck folds, perineum/perianal, axillae; segmental morphology.
- **Structural associations**:
  - **PHACE** — large segmental facial hemangioma (commonly >5 cm): Posterior fossa,
    Hemangioma, Arterial (cerebrovascular), Cardiac (coarctation), Eye anomalies, ± sternal
    defects. Needs MRI/MRA head and neck, echocardiogram, eye exam.
  - **LUMBAR** — segmental lumbosacral/perineal hemangioma: spinal dysraphism,
    urogenital and anorectal anomalies → spinal ultrasound (<3 months) or MRI.
- **Disfigurement**: central face, especially nose/lip; any facial lesion >2 cm;
  large or pedunculated lesions likely to leave fibrofatty residuum.

Low-risk lesions (small, superficial, trunk/limbs) → reassure, photograph, review
during growth phase. Tools such as the **IHReS** score can help primary-care triage.

## 3. Treatment
- **Oral propranolol** first-line for high-risk lesions: target **2–3 mg/kg/day**, usually
  divided twice daily, titrated up over 1–2 weeks (licensed paediatric solution labelling
  ends at ~3.4 mg/kg/day) — verify against local formulary/guideline.
  - Give **with or just after feeds**; withhold if not feeding or vomiting (hypoglycaemia).
  - Baseline: HR, BP, cardiac/respiratory exam; ECG if bradycardia, arrhythmia, family
    history of congenital heart block or maternal connective-tissue disease.
  - Inpatient initiation for infants <5 weeks corrected age, preterm, poor weight gain,
    or significant comorbidity (local criteria vary).
  - Usual duration **≥6 months**, often to ~12 months of age; taper or stop, watch for
    rebound growth (~10–25%).
- **Topical timolol 0.5% gel-forming solution** for small, thin, superficial lesions.
- Ulcers: barrier care, non-adherent dressings, analgesia, treat infection; ulceration
  itself is an indication for propranolol.
- Pulsed-dye laser / surgery for residual telangiectasia or fibrofatty tissue later.

## Gotchas
- Delayed referral is the main failure: by 5 months most growth (and damage) has happened.
- Propranolol masks hypoglycaemia signs and can worsen bronchospasm — hold during
  wheezy illness.
- In suspected PHACE, image cerebral arteries before propranolol where possible
  (theoretical stroke risk with severe arteriopathy); start at low dose under specialist care.
- "Hemangioma" present fully formed at birth is probably congenital, not infantile.

## Verify
- [ ] Diagnosis consistent with infantile (not congenital/malformation/KHE) hemangioma.
- [ ] Location, size, morphology and number documented with photos.
- [ ] High-risk features checked; PHACE/LUMBAR/liver work-up ordered where indicated.
- [ ] Propranolol dose, feeding advice and hypoglycaemia education documented.
- [ ] Review interval matched to the growth phase.
