# Chairside CAD/CAM crown workflow

**Goal:** a well-fitting, correctly contacted single crown in one visit — which depends far
more on preparation, retraction and scan quality than on the software.

## 1. Prepare for the mill
- Follow the block manufacturer's minimum thicknesses (typical full-contour lithium
  disilicate ~1.5 mm occlusal/~1.0–1.2 mm axial; monolithic zirconia less — check the IFU).
- **Rounded** internal line angles and cusp tips; no sharp edges, grooves or thin "feather"
  margins — burs have a finite diameter and will over-mill them (poor fit, rocking).
- Shoulder or heavy chamfer, smooth and continuous; no undercuts along the insertion path.
  (See the prosthodontics crown-preparation skill for reduction principles.)

## 2. Retraction and field control
- Cord (single or double) ± haemostatic agent; margin must be **visible, dry and free of blood**.
  The scanner records only what it can see — light cannot pass through gingiva or saliva.
- Cheek and tongue retraction; dry with air; avoid blowing debris into the sulcus.

## 3. Scanning strategy (adapt to your scanner's recommended path)
1. **Preparation arch:** start occlusal, roll to lingual/palatal, then buccal, in a
   continuous path; include adjacent teeth fully (needed for contacts) — typically a
   quadrant is enough.
2. **Opposing arch** over the same region.
3. **Buccal bite** registration in maximum intercuspation, on one or both sides.
4. Review: rotate the model; fill **data holes** at the margin, contacts and occlusal
   surfaces; rescan only the missing area (rescanning everything raises stitching error).
- Pre-op scan of the unprepared tooth (or a "copy" mode) helps reproduce existing anatomy.

## 4. Design
- **Margin line:** trace on the scan at high magnification; check against the colour view;
  correct auto-detected lines that jump onto gingiva or cord.
- **Insertion axis:** set so undercuts are minimal; software blocks out the rest.
- Parameters (defaults are usually reasonable; adjust with experience): cement spacer
  (commonly tens of µm, e.g. around 80–120 µm depending on system), proximal contact
  strength, occlusal contact strength, minimum thickness for the chosen material.
- Check the virtual crown: contacts present, occlusion not heavy, emergence profile not
  overcontoured, thickness map above the minimum everywhere.

## 5. Mill and process
- Select block material and shade (shade taken before preparation).
- **Lithium disilicate (e.g. e.max CAD):** milled in the softer pre-crystallised "blue" state,
  tried in if desired, then **crystallised** in the furnace (with stain/glaze in one or two
  firings per IFU). Program temperatures are product-specific.
- **Zirconia:** milled oversize then **sintered** (speed-sintering furnaces for chairside);
  shrinkage is compensated by the software — use the matched block data.
- Remove sprue, adjust contacts on the fired restoration with fine diamonds, polish or glaze.

## 6. Try-in and cementation
- Check seat (silicone fit-checker if in doubt), contacts (floss snaps), margins with an explorer,
  occlusion before cementation where safe (ceramics are brittle before bonding).
- **Lithium disilicate:** hydrofluoric acid etch (commonly ~5% for ~20 s; per IFU), rinse,
  silane; adhesive resin cement, or self-adhesive cement on retentive preps.
- **Zirconia:** do **not** HF-etch; alumina air abrasion (low pressure) then an
  **MDP-containing** primer/cement; conventional cements possible on retentive preps.
- Clean intaglio of saliva contamination before bonding (cleaning paste or per IFU).
- Remove excess cement, re-check occlusion, polish adjustments.

## Gotchas
- Blood or saliva over the margin = a guessed margin and a poorly fitting crown.
- Sharp prep features cause rocking crowns after milling.
- Buccal bite scanned with the patient not fully closed gives high occlusion.
- Adjusting a glazed crown without re-polishing leaves a rough, wear-causing surface.
- Mixing up IFU steps for different ceramics (e.g. HF on zirconia) weakens the bond.

## Verify
- [ ] Margin continuous and clearly visible in the scan on all sides.
- [ ] Thickness map meets the material minimum; contacts and occlusion checked in software.
- [ ] Correct firing/sintering program for the block used.
- [ ] Clinical fit: seated, contacts snap, margins closed, occlusion adjusted.
- [ ] Surface treatment and cement matched to the material per IFU.
