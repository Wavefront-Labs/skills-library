# Risk-based caries prevention

**Goal:** stop new lesions and arrest early ones by giving each patient the intensity of
prevention their risk demands — and recalling them at an interval that matches it.

## 1. Assess risk (every examination)
Use a structured tool (CAMBRA, ADA Caries Risk Assessment form, or a local equivalent) and
record the category: **low, moderate/medium, high** (some tools add "extreme").
- **Disease indicators:** new cavities or active (white, chalky, rough) lesions in the last
  ~3 years, restorations placed for caries recently, lesions into dentine on radiograph.
- **Risk factors:** frequent sugar intake (more than ~4 intakes/day as a working flag),
  sugary drinks/bottle at night, visible plaque, low saliva (medication, radiotherapy,
  Sjögren's), deep fissures, appliances, low fluoride exposure, social deprivation, special needs.
- **Protective factors:** twice-daily fluoride toothpaste, fluoridated water, regular care.
Past caries experience is the single strongest predictor.

## 2. Fluoride toothpaste (adapted from Delivering Better Oral Health — verify local guidance)
| Age / risk | Strength | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Under 3 years | ≥1000 ppm F | smear |
| 3–6 years | 1000–1500 ppm F | pea-sized |
| 7 years and adults | 1350–1500 ppm F | pea-sized/full brush |
| High risk, 10+ years | 2800 ppm (prescription) | per Rx |
| High risk, 16+ years | 5000 ppm (prescription) | per Rx |
Brush twice daily, last thing at night and one other time; **spit, don't rinse**. Supervise
young children's brushing.

## 3. Professional fluoride
- **Fluoride varnish** (5% NaF, 22,600 ppm F): DBOH advises at least twice yearly for all
  children from 3 years and 2 or more times yearly for higher-risk children and adults.
  Apply a thin layer to dried surfaces; advise no brushing for ~4 h and soft food (check the IFU).
- Avoid in patients with a history of allergy to varnish components (e.g. colophony)
  per product labelling.
- **Silver diamine fluoride** (38%) arrests cavitated lesions where restoring is not
  feasible; stains lesions black — consent for aesthetics.

## 4. Fissure sealants
- **Resin-based sealants** on permanent molars (and premolars at risk) as soon as practical
  after eruption, especially in moderate/high-risk children; can also seal non-cavitated
  occlusal lesions.
- Technique: clean, isolate (rubber dam or cotton rolls with suction), etch ~20–30 s
  (per IFU), rinse, dry to frosty enamel, apply, cure, check retention with an explorer
  and occlusion. Moisture contamination is the main cause of failure.
- **Glass ionomer** sealant where isolation is poor (partially erupted molars) — lower
  retention but still releases fluoride.
- Check retention at every recall; reseal lost sealants.

## 5. Diet and hygiene coaching
- Reduce the **frequency** of sugars; confine them to mealtimes; no sugary drinks between meals
  or at bedtime; sugar-free medicines.
- Use a short diet diary for high-risk patients; set one or two specific goals (motivational
  interviewing style), not a lecture.
- Disclose plaque; teach a technique the patient can repeat; interdental cleaning for adults.

## 6. Recall intervals (NICE CG19)
Set by risk at each review: **3–12 months for under-18s, 3–24 months for adults**. High-risk
patients at the shorter end; record the reasoning.

## Gotchas
- Classifying as low risk while active lesions are present — disease indicators override.
- Rinsing after brushing washes away topical fluoride benefit.
- Bitewings at intervals matched to risk are part of prevention (see the dental-imaging skill).
- High-fluoride toothpastes are prescription products with age limits; check ingestion risk.
- A sealed fissure with a lost sealant is unprotected — check, don't assume.

## Verify
- [ ] Risk category recorded with the factors that drove it.
- [ ] Toothpaste strength and amount advised for age and risk.
- [ ] Varnish/sealants delivered as indicated and documented with product.
- [ ] One specific diet/hygiene goal agreed and recorded.
- [ ] Recall interval set within NICE ranges and justified by risk.
