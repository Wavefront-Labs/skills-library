# Non-surgical periodontal therapy (scaling and root planing)

**Goal:** convert bleeding, deep pockets into shallow, non-bleeding sites by controlling risk
factors and removing subgingival biofilm and calculus — the foundation that every later
periodontal step depends on.

## 1. Diagnose: stage and grade (2017 AAP/EFP)
From a full six-point chart (PD, recession, CAL, BOP, mobility, furcation) and radiographs:
- **Stage** (severity/complexity, by worst interdental CAL): I 1–2 mm; II 3–4 mm;
  III ≥5 mm (or tooth loss from periodontitis ≤4 teeth, PD ≥6 mm, furcation II/III,
  vertical defects ≥3 mm); IV as III plus ≥5 teeth lost or rehabilitation needs
  (masticatory dysfunction, <20 teeth, bite collapse, drifting).
- **Grade** (rate/risk): A, B, C — by % bone loss ÷ age (<0.25, 0.25–1.0, >1.0), with
  modifiers **smoking** (<10/day → at least B; ≥10/day → C) and **diabetes**
  (HbA1c <7.0% → at least B; ≥7.0% → C).
- Extent: localised (<30% of teeth) vs generalised; molar-incisor pattern noted.

## 2. Step 1 — cause-related therapy (everyone)
- Explain the disease, its chronic nature and the patient's role.
- **Oral hygiene instruction:** interdental brushes sized to each space are the main tool;
  floss where spaces are too tight; record plaque and bleeding scores (common target under
  ~20%, targets vary).
- **Risk factors:** smoking cessation advice/referral; liaise on diabetes control.
- Supragingival scaling, removal of plaque-retentive factors (overhangs, open margins).

## 3. Step 2 — subgingival instrumentation
1. Local anaesthesia as needed; quadrant-wise or full-mouth within 24 h — outcomes are
   broadly similar, choose by patient and logistics.
2. **Ultrasonic/piezo** for bulk deposits and furcations (fine tips, lavage), then **hand
   curettes** for finish: Gracey 5/6 anteriors/premolars, 7/8 posterior buccal/lingual,
   11/12 posterior mesial, 13/14 posterior distal. Blade face ~70–80° to the root.
3. Work in overlapping vertical/oblique strokes until the root feels smooth with an
   explorer; aim to remove calculus and biofilm, not to plane away cementum.
4. Post-op advice: sensitivity and recession as tissues shrink are expected.
5. **Adjuncts:** systemic antibiotics are **not** routine. They may be considered in
   specific situations (e.g. generalised Stage III in young adults) — a commonly cited
   regimen is amoxicillin 500 mg + metronidazole 400–500 mg three times daily for 7 days,
   given with instrumentation; verify against local formulary/guideline and stewardship rules.

## 4. Re-evaluate (about 6–12 weeks later)
- Rechart fully. **Endpoint of therapy:** no sites ≥5 mm with BOP and no PD ≥6 mm (EFP);
  a ≤4 mm site without BOP is a "closed pocket".
- Residual 4–5 mm with BOP → repeat instrumentation (Step 3 may begin here).
- Residual ≥6 mm, infrabony or furcation defects → consider **surgical** access/regenerative
  surgery or specialist referral.
- Poor hygiene or uncontrolled risk factors → repeat Step 1 rather than escalate.

## 5. Supportive periodontal care
Recall every **3–12 months** set by risk (stage, grade, residual pockets, smoking, plaque
control). Each visit: rechart BOP/PD, reinforce OHI, re-instrument bleeding sites.

## Gotchas
- Instrumenting before OHI is in place gives poor, short-lived results.
- Burnished calculus feels smooth but is still there — check with an explorer and radiographs.
- Reevaluating too early (before ~4–6 weeks) misreads healing tissue.
- Recession and black triangles after success are normal; warn patients beforehand.
- A narrow isolated deep pocket may be endodontic or a root fracture, not periodontitis.

## Verify
- [ ] Six-point chart with CAL and BOP recorded; stage, grade and extent written.
- [ ] Plaque and bleeding scores recorded and trending down.
- [ ] Smoking/diabetes addressed and documented.
- [ ] Re-evaluation chart compared with baseline; residual sites listed with plan.
- [ ] Supportive-care interval set and justified.
