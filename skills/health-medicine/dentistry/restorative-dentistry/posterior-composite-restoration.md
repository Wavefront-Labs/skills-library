# Posterior (Class II) composite restoration

**Goal:** a sealed, well-contacted, well-contoured composite that survives — most failures
are secondary caries at the gingival margin and fracture, both largely technique-driven.

## 1. Before you start
- Confirm diagnosis (cavitated or radiographically into dentine) and that composite fits:
  isolation achievable, margins in enamel ideally, patient caries risk controlled.
- If the gingival margin is subgingival/on root dentine and isolation is doubtful,
  consider a GIC/RMGIC base at the box floor (open/closed sandwich) or a different material.
- Pre-op occlusion: mark contacts with articulating paper so you don't place margins on
  a centric stop if avoidable.
- Shade-select **before** isolation; teeth dehydrate and lighten within minutes.

## 2. Preparation
1. Local anaesthesia; **rubber dam** (single-tooth isolation is rarely enough for Class II —
   isolate at least one tooth either side).
2. Protect the adjacent tooth (metal matrix strip or wedge guard) before opening the box.
3. Access via the marginal ridge; remove caries guided by hardness and staining, peripheral
   dentine to hard, with **selective removal** to firm/leathery dentine over the pulp in deep
   lesions to avoid exposure.
4. No bevel needed on the occlusal; a light bevel/finish on the proximal enamel is
   optional. Round internal angles; no retention grooves needed.

## 3. Matrix
- **Sectional matrix + ring + wedge** gives better contacts and contours than a circumferential band.
- Wedge firmly from the wider embrasure (usually lingual/palatal); confirm seal at the
  gingival margin — no overhang possible later.
- Burnish the band against the adjacent contact.

## 4. Adhesive protocol (follow the specific IFU)
- **Etch-and-rinse:** 35–37% phosphoric acid ~15 s on dentine, ~30 s enamel; rinse, keep
  dentine moist (blot, do not desiccate); primer/adhesive scrubbed ~20 s; air-thin; cure.
- **Self-etch / universal:** scrub on, air-thin, cure. Add **selective enamel etching**
  (phosphoric acid on enamel margins only) for better enamel bond.
- Check light output with a radiometer periodically; hold the tip close and perpendicular.

## 5. Placement and curing
- Build the proximal wall first, converting Class II to Class I.
- **Conventional composite:** oblique increments ≤2 mm, cure each (typically 20 s at
  ≥1000 mW/cm²; check IFU for shade/depth).
- **Bulk-fill:** up to 4–5 mm per IFU, usually with a conventional capping layer.
- A flowable liner at the gingival floor can improve adaptation; keep it thin.
- Remove ring and matrix, then **post-cure from buccal and lingual**.

## 6. Finish and check
- Remove flash with a scalpel/finishing strip at the gingival margin; floss — it should
  snap through and not shred.
- Adjust occlusion in centric and excursions; polish.
- Bitewing at next recall to check for overhangs/voids.

## Gotchas
- **Contamination** (saliva, blood, handpiece oil) after etching ruins the bond — re-etch.
- **Over-drying dentine** in etch-and-rinse collapses collagen → post-op sensitivity.
- **Open contacts** come from loose wedges and thin bands; food impaction will follow.
- **Shrinkage stress** is highest in deep, high C-factor cavities — increments help.
- Curing light tips with cured resin on them drop output dramatically.

## Verify
- [ ] Rubber dam isolation was maintained throughout bonding and placement.
- [ ] Floss passes with a firm snap; no catch at the gingival margin.
- [ ] Occlusion adjusted; patient bites evenly with no high spots.
- [ ] Adhesive used per its IFU (etch mode, times, curing).
- [ ] Radiograph/visual check shows no gingival overhang or void.
