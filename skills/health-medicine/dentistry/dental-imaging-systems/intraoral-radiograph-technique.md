# Intraoral radiograph technique (bitewings and periapicals)

**Goal:** a diagnostic image first time, at the lowest dose consistent with the clinical
question — every retake doubles the dose for nothing.

## 1. Justify and select
- No routine "new patient full set". Each exposure answers a clinical question after
  examination (selection criteria from FGDP(UK) or ADA/FDA guidance).
- **Bitewing recall intervals** by caries risk (approximate; verify against current local
  criteria): high risk ~6 months (children and adults); moderate ~12 months; low risk
  ~12–18 months for children and ~24 months for adults.
- Periapicals for endodontic, periapical, trauma and specific periodontal questions.
- Record the justification and the report — an unreported image is an unjustified exposure.

## 2. Dose optimisation (ALARA / ALADAIP)
- **Rectangular collimation** matched to the receptor — a large dose saving over round
  collimation (often quoted at roughly half or more; depends on set-up).
- Use **beam-aiming holders** so the collimator aligns with the receptor.
- Digital receptors with exposure settings set for the patient size and the region; use
  manufacturer/physicist-recommended exposure charts.
- Lead aprons are not routinely needed for intraoral imaging; thyroid shielding advice
  differs between jurisdictions — follow current local regulation/physicist guidance.

## 3. Paralleling periapical
1. Receptor in the holder, placed **parallel to the long axis** of the teeth; move it away
   from the teeth toward the midline (palate/floor of mouth) to gain parallelism.
2. Anterior: vertical (portrait) receptor; posterior: horizontal (landscape).
3. Align the beam-aiming ring so the tube is perpendicular to tooth and receptor; include
   ≥2–3 mm beyond the apices.
4. **Bisecting angle** only when anatomy prevents paralleling (shallow palate, gag reflex,
   tori) — expect some distortion.

## 4. Bitewing
- Horizontal bitewings with a bitewing holder; receptor centred on the contact areas of
  premolars or molars (two per side in adults usually).
- Beam directed **through the contact points** horizontally and with a slight positive
  vertical angulation (around +5–10° in many textbooks; with holders, follow the ring).
- **Vertical bitewings** for periodontal bone levels where pockets are deep.

## 5. Receptor handling
- Sensors: barrier sleeve; don't bite or bend cables; check for dead pixels.
- **PSP plates:** barrier envelope, avoid bending/scratches, scan promptly (latent image
  fades), erase before reuse; inspect plates regularly and retire damaged ones.

## 6. Common errors and fixes
| Error | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Overlapping contacts | horizontal angle not through contacts | re-angle beam along the contact line |
| Cone cut (clear area) | beam not covering receptor | align ring/collimator with receptor |
| Elongation | too little vertical angle (bisecting) | increase angle |
| Foreshortening | too much vertical angle (bisecting) | decrease angle |
| Apices missing | receptor not deep enough / tilted | reposition deeper, use holder |
| Blur | patient or tube movement | stabilise, shorter exposure |
| Light/dark image | wrong exposure setting | match chart to region/size |
| Reversed image pattern | receptor placed back-to-front | correct orientation (pattern varies by system) |

## 7. Quality assurance
Grade every image (e.g. diagnostically acceptable/not acceptable per UK IRR17-linked
guidance) and audit retake rates; investigate rising rejects. Equipment checks per the
physicist's schedule.

## Gotchas
- Shallow receptor placement is the commonest cause of missing apices.
- Overlap on bitewings hides interproximal caries — the core reason the image was taken.
- Over-exposed digital images can look "fine" after processing; check exposure indices.
- Retaking without fixing the cause gives the same error and double dose.

## Verify
- [ ] Justification and clinical question recorded before exposure.
- [ ] Holder and rectangular collimation used; exposure matched to patient/region.
- [ ] Contacts open on bitewings; apices plus 2–3 mm visible on periapicals.
- [ ] Image graded, reported and stored with the record.
- [ ] Retake (if any) documented with cause.
