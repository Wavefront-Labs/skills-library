# Mental state examination and diagnostic formulation

> General clinical/professional reference, not patient-specific advice. Diagnostic
> criteria should be checked against the current DSM-5-TR or ICD-11 text used in your
> jurisdiction.

**Goal:** produce an assessment another clinician could act on: what is happening
now (MSE), why it's happening (formulation), what it most likely is (ranked
differential with organic causes excluded), and what is risky (risk summary — see
`health-medicine.psychiatry.risk-assessment-and-crisis-care.structured-risk-formulation-and-safety-planning`).

## 1. History structure
Presenting complaint in the patient's words → history of presenting illness (onset,
course, triggers, functional impact) → past psychiatric history (diagnoses,
admissions, detentions, self-harm, medication trials with dose/duration/response) →
medical history and medications → substance use (quantify) → family history →
personal/developmental and social history (housing, finances, relationships,
forensic) → premorbid personality.
- **Collateral** from family, GP, previous records: essential for psychosis, mania,
  cognitive decline and when insight is limited. Record source and consent basis.

## 2. Mental state examination (observe, then ask)
| Domain | Record |
|---|---|
| Appearance & behaviour | self-care, eye contact, psychomotor change, abnormal movements, catatonic signs |
| Speech | rate, volume, quantity, pressure, latency |
| Mood & affect | subjective (quote) vs objective; range, reactivity, congruence |
| Thought form | linear, circumstantial, tangential, flight of ideas, loosening, thought block |
| Thought content | preoccupations, overvalued ideas, delusions (type, conviction), obsessions, **thoughts of harm to self/others** |
| Perception | hallucinations (modality, 2nd/3rd person, command), illusions, depersonalisation |
| Cognition | orientation, attention, memory; formal screen (MoCA/ACE-III) if indicated |
| Insight & judgement | awareness of illness, attitude to treatment |
Use descriptive language, not labels ("believes neighbours have implanted a device"
rather than "paranoid").

## 3. Exclude organic and substance causes
Red flags for a medical/neurological cause: first episode over ~40–45, acute onset,
fluctuating consciousness or disorientation, visual hallucinations, focal neurology,
headache, seizures, recent head injury, systemic illness, atypical features.
- Baseline workup commonly: FBC, U&E, LFT, glucose, TFT, calcium, B12/folate, CRP,
  urine drug screen; consider HIV/syphilis serology, ECG (before antipsychotics),
  CT/MRI and EEG when indicated. **Local first-episode protocols vary.**
- Delirium first in any acutely confused patient (see
  `health-medicine.psychiatry.consultation-liaison-psychiatry.delirium-workup-and-capacity-assessment`).
- **Catatonia** is often missed; screen with Bush-Francis items and consider a
  supervised benzodiazepine challenge per local protocol.

## 4. Differential diagnosis
- Use hierarchical reasoning: organic → substance-induced → psychotic → mood → anxiety
  /trauma → personality and neurodevelopmental. Disorders co-occur; list comorbidities
  separately.
- Common confusions: bipolar depression vs unipolar (ask about prior hypomania; family
  history), schizophrenia vs psychotic depression, ADHD/autism vs personality disorder,
  PTSD vs psychosis (flashback phenomena), dementia vs depression.
- Apply criteria (DSM-5-TR or ICD-11) explicitly and state which criteria are met or
  uncertain.

## 5. Formulation (4P × biopsychosocial)
A grid of **predisposing, precipitating, perpetuating, protective** factors across
biological, psychological and social domains, ending with a one-paragraph narrative
that links them and points to interventions. Structured interview-to-formulation
technique overlaps with
`health-medicine.clinical-psychology.diagnostic-interviewing-and-formulation.structured-interview-to-formulation`.

## 6. Plan and communication
Working diagnosis + differential, investigations, risk summary, immediate management,
legal status/capacity if relevant, follow-up, and who was informed. Share the
formulation with the patient in plain language where possible.

## Gotchas
- Interpreters, not family members, for assessments in another language.
- Cultural context: some beliefs are normative within the patient's culture — ask.
- A calm interview doesn't negate a collateral account of dangerous behaviour.
- "Medically cleared" notes from busy settings may be cursory; check what was done.

## Verify
- [ ] All MSE domains recorded descriptively, including harm thoughts.
- [ ] Collateral sought (or documented why not).
- [ ] Organic red flags reviewed and baseline investigations ordered.
- [ ] Differential ranked with criteria referenced; comorbidity listed.
- [ ] 4P formulation written and linked to the plan; risk summary present.
