# Withdrawal management, agonist therapy and relapse prevention

> General clinical/professional reference, not patient-specific advice. **Withdrawal
> regimens, OAT induction and titration doses, and relapse-prevention drug dosing must
> be taken from the current local protocol/formulary and national guideline.**
> Controlled-drug prescribing rules differ by jurisdiction.

**Goal:** keep people safe through withdrawal, retain them in effective treatment
(especially opioid agonist therapy, which reduces mortality), and treat co-occurring
mental illness alongside the substance use.

## 1. Assessment
- Quantify use per substance (amount, route, frequency, last use), dependence features,
  previous withdrawals (seizures, DTs), overdoses, injecting and BBV status, social
  situation, children/safeguarding, driving.
- Screening tools: **AUDIT/AUDIT-C** for alcohol; urine/oral fluid drug testing to
  support, not replace, history.
- Physical: liver disease, nutrition, injection-site infections, cognitive screen.
- Use **motivational interviewing**; goals may be abstinence or reduced harm.

## 2. Alcohol withdrawal
- Onset typically 6–24 h after last drink; seizures commonly 12–48 h; **delirium
  tremens** typically 48–72 h — medical emergency.
- Assess severity with **CIWA-Ar**; symptom-triggered benzodiazepine regimens reduce
  total dose where staff can monitor; fixed regimens where they can't (per protocol).
- **Thiamine** (parenteral in high-risk/suspected Wernicke's) **before glucose**;
  Wernicke's triad (confusion, ataxia, ophthalmoplegia) is often incomplete.
- Community detox excluded by history of seizures/DTs, very high intake, serious
  comorbidity, poly-substance use or lack of support → inpatient.

## 3. Benzodiazepine dependence
- Abrupt cessation risks seizures; convert to a long-acting agent and taper slowly
  (often over weeks to months) per guideline.

## 4. Opioid use disorder
- **Opioid agonist therapy** (methadone, buprenorphine incl. long-acting injectable
  forms) is first-line and reduces overdose death; detox alone has high relapse and
  **lost tolerance → overdose** risk.
- Withdrawal severity with **COWS**. Buprenorphine induction usually begins once in
  moderate withdrawal to avoid **precipitated withdrawal** (more of a problem after
  fentanyl — newer low-dose/"micro-induction" approaches exist; follow local guidance).
- Methadone: QTc and interaction checks; supervised consumption initially per local
  rules; slow titration given accumulation risk in early treatment.
- **Take-home naloxone** for everyone at risk and their contacts.
- Pregnancy: continue/start OAT; do not withdraw.

## 5. Relapse-prevention medicines (alcohol)
- **Acamprosate**, **naltrexone** (avoid if on opioids), **disulfiram** (supervised,
  with cardiac/hepatic cautions), nalmefene for reduction where licensed. Combine with
  psychosocial support.
- Stimulants and cannabis: no strongly established pharmacotherapy; contingency
  management and psychosocial interventions have best evidence.

## 6. Dual diagnosis
- Treat both conditions in parallel; don't defer mental-health treatment until
  abstinence.
- Substance-induced vs independent disorders: symptoms preceding use, persisting
  ~4 weeks after cessation, or family history favour independent.
- Prescribing caution: sedative interactions, overdose toxicity, stimulants in ADHD
  with SUD (consider risk of diversion).

## 7. Red flags / escalate
DTs, Wernicke's, withdrawal seizures, mixed sedative overdose, hepatic decompensation,
injection-related sepsis/endocarditis, emergent suicidality (see
`health-medicine.psychiatry.risk-assessment-and-crisis-care.structured-risk-formulation-and-safety-planning`).

## Gotchas
- Post-detox and post-prison release are high overdose-mortality periods.
- Breath alcohol > 0 doesn't preclude withdrawal in heavy chronic drinkers.
- Stigmatising language ("addict", "dirty urine") damages engagement.

## Verify
- [ ] Use quantified per substance; withdrawal history recorded.
- [ ] Validated severity scale used during withdrawal.
- [ ] Thiamine given where indicated before glucose.
- [ ] OAT offered for OUD; naloxone supplied.
- [ ] Co-occurring mental illness assessed and treated in parallel.
- [ ] All doses checked against the current local protocol.
