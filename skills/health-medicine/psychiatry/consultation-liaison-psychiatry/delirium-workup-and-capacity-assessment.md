# Delirium workup and capacity assessment

> General clinical/professional reference, not patient-specific advice. Capacity law
> differs by jurisdiction (e.g. Mental Capacity Act in England and Wales, other
> statutes elsewhere). **Any drug use in delirium must follow current local guidance
> and formulary, at the lowest effective dose.**

**Goal:** answer the referring team's actual question — is this delirium, can they
decide, what's the psychiatric contribution — with a plan the ward can carry out.

## 1. Clarify the question
Call the referrer: what decision or behaviour prompted the referral, how urgent, what
they expect. "Please see — low mood" often hides "patient refusing dialysis".

## 2. Delirium: screen, then hunt the cause
- **Acute onset and fluctuating** course, **inattention**, plus disorganised thinking
  or altered arousal. Hypoactive delirium (quiet, withdrawn) is commonly misread as
  depression.
- Screen: **4AT** (ward), **CAM/CAM-ICU** (ICU). Collateral on baseline cognition is
  essential — delirium on dementia is common.
- Causes are usually multiple. Systematic search:
  - Drugs: anticholinergics, opioids, benzodiazepines, steroids, dopaminergics,
    recent starts/stops, withdrawal (alcohol, benzodiazepines).
  - Infection, hypoxia, metabolic (Na, Ca, glucose, urea, LFTs), urinary retention,
    constipation, pain, dehydration, sleep deprivation.
  - Neurological: stroke, seizures (non-convulsive status → EEG), CNS infection,
    subdural.
- ICU management of sedation and delirium: see
  `health-medicine.critical-care-medicine.sedation-analgesia-and-delirium.analgesia-first-sedation-and-delirium-bundle`.

## 3. Delirium management
- Treat the causes; review the drug chart line by line.
- Non-drug measures: orientation, glasses/hearing aids, family presence, continuity of
  staff, sleep hygiene, mobilisation, hydration, avoid ward moves and catheters.
- Medication (short-term antipsychotic or, in alcohol withdrawal, benzodiazepine) only
  for severe distress or risk, after non-drug measures; avoid antipsychotics in Lewy
  body dementia/Parkinson's; check QTc. **Doses per local protocol.**
- Follow-up: delirium predicts dementia and mortality; flag cognitive review after
  discharge.

## 4. Capacity assessment
- Capacity is **decision- and time-specific**; presume capacity; support the person to
  decide (information format, timing, interpreter, hearing aids).
- Common functional test (Appelbaum / MCA formulation): can the person
  1. **understand** the relevant information,
  2. **retain** it long enough to decide,
  3. **use or weigh** it (appreciate how it applies to them),
  4. **communicate** a choice?
  (Under the MCA, an impairment of mind or brain must also cause the inability.)
- Disagreement with medical advice ≠ incapacity. Depression, psychosis and delirium can
  impair "use and weigh" specifically — document how.
- Document: the decision, information given, the person's own words on each element,
  and the conclusion. If lacking capacity: best-interests process, advance decisions,
  attorneys/proxies, least restrictive option; escalate high-stakes disputes (legal
  advice, court).
- Mental-health legislation treats mental disorder; capacity legislation covers
  physical treatment decisions in many jurisdictions — know the interface locally.

## 5. Other common liaison work
- **Depression in medical illness**: somatic symptoms overlap; weight cognitive
  symptoms (hopelessness, guilt). Check interactions and organ function before
  prescribing.
- **Steroid-induced** mood and psychotic symptoms: dose-related; reduce if possible.
- **Functional neurological disorder / somatic symptom disorder**: positive diagnosis
  (signs such as Hoover's), clear explanation, avoid repeated investigation, link to
  therapy/physio. Validate symptoms as real.
- Self-harm in ED: assessment per
  `health-medicine.psychiatry.risk-assessment-and-crisis-care.structured-risk-formulation-and-safety-planning`.

## Gotchas
- "Sundowning" is delirium until proven otherwise in a new admission.
- Capacity assessments done by the psychiatrist alone when the treating team holds the
  information — the decision-maker usually assesses; liaison advises.
- Haloperidol in an undiagnosed Parkinsonian patient can cause severe reactions.

## Verify
- [ ] Referrer's question restated in the note.
- [ ] Delirium screen result and baseline cognition recorded.
- [ ] Cause checklist (drugs, infection, metabolic, neuro, retention, pain) completed.
- [ ] Capacity note names the decision and evidence for each element.
- [ ] Clear, ward-actionable plan with review date.
