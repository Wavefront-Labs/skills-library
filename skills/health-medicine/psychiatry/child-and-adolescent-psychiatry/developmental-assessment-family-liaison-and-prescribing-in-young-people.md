# Developmental assessment, family liaison and prescribing in young people

> General clinical/professional reference, not patient-specific advice. Licensing,
> consent law (e.g. Gillick/Fraser in England, mature-minor doctrines elsewhere) and
> safeguarding procedures vary by jurisdiction. **Paediatric doses and monitoring must
> be checked against the current paediatric formulary (e.g. BNFc) and guideline.**

**Goal:** assess the young person in their developmental, family and school context,
keep them safe, and use medication only where evidence supports it — with careful
monitoring.

## 1. Developmental framing
- Ask what is typical for age: tantrums at 3, risk-taking at 15, sleep phase delay in
  adolescence. Symptoms count when they are **out of keeping with developmental stage,
  persistent and impairing** across settings.
- Developmental history: pregnancy/birth, milestones, early temperament, attachment,
  language, social communication, school progress, medical history (epilepsy, head
  injury), family psychiatric history.

## 2. Multi-informant assessment
- See the young person **alone** for part of every assessment (especially adolescents)
  and with carers. Explain confidentiality and its limits first.
- Gather school reports and teacher rating scales (e.g. SDQ, Conners, Vanderbilt);
  informants often disagree — that is data, not error.
- Adolescents: **HEEADSSS** (Home, Education/employment, Eating, Activities, Drugs,
  Sexuality, Suicide/depression, Safety) for a psychosocial sweep.
- Consider neurodevelopmental conditions (ADHD, autism, learning disability) behind
  many presentations of "behaviour" or "anxiety"; formal autism assessment uses
  structured tools (ADOS-2, ADI-R) within a multidisciplinary team.

## 3. Safeguarding
- Abuse, neglect, domestic violence, exploitation (criminal/sexual), online harm,
  bullying. Any disclosure or concern → follow local child-protection procedure the
  same day; document verbatim.
- Self-harm in young people: assessment per
  `health-medicine.psychiatry.risk-assessment-and-crisis-care.structured-risk-formulation-and-safety-planning`,
  adapted for age and involving carers where safe.

## 4. Family and school liaison
- Family work is usually treatment, not background: parenting interventions for
  younger children with behavioural problems; family-based therapy for adolescent
  anorexia nervosa.
- School: reasonable adjustments, attendance plans for school avoidance, education
  health and care plans (or equivalent).

## 5. Early psychosis recognition
- Prodrome: social withdrawal, declining school performance, suspiciousness, odd
  beliefs, perceptual changes, sleep disruption. Cannabis use raises risk.
- Refer early to an **early intervention in psychosis** service; organic screen as in
  adults. Distinguish from trauma-related perceptual experiences and autism-related
  social difficulties.

## 6. Prescribing principles
- Psychological and family interventions first for many conditions (mild–moderate
  depression, anxiety, behaviour problems).
- **ADHD**: stimulants (methylphenidate, lisdexamfetamine) and non-stimulants have good
  evidence. Baseline and regular **height, weight (on growth chart), BP, pulse**; ask
  about cardiac history/family sudden death; appetite and sleep monitoring; diversion
  risk.
- **Depression**: fluoxetine has the strongest evidence in adolescents and is usually
  first choice when medication is indicated, alongside psychological therapy;
  regulator warnings about **emergent suicidality** mean closer early review.
- **Antipsychotics**: young people are more susceptible to weight gain, metabolic
  change and hyperprolactinaemia — monitor closely.
- Off-label use is common; document rationale and consent.
- "Start low, go slow", one change at a time, structured rating scales.

## 7. Transition
Plan transfer to adult services from ~17 (timing varies), with joint meetings — a
known high-risk point for disengagement.

## Gotchas
- Anxiety and ADHD/autism overlap heavily; treating only the anxiety stalls progress.
- Restrictive eating presenting as "stomach pain" — check weight trajectory and
  physical risk (bradycardia, electrolytes) urgently.
- Parents may want medication or reject it for reasons unrelated to the child's
  needs — explore.

## Verify
- [ ] Young person seen alone; confidentiality limits explained.
- [ ] Developmental history and at least two informants (home + school).
- [ ] Safeguarding considered and actioned per local procedure.
- [ ] Psychological/family interventions offered before or alongside medication.
- [ ] Growth/BP/metabolic monitoring recorded on charts for any medication.
- [ ] Doses verified against the current paediatric formulary.
