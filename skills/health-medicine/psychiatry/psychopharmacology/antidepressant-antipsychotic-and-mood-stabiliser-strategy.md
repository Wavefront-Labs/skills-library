# Antidepressant, antipsychotic and mood-stabiliser strategy

> General clinical/professional reference, not patient-specific advice. **All doses,
> plasma-level targets, monitoring intervals and switching schedules must be verified
> against the current local formulary, product information and national guideline
> (e.g. Maudsley, NICE, BNF or equivalent) before prescribing.**

**Goal:** give each medicine a fair trial, change strategy for reasons rather than
impatience, and monitor the predictable harms.

## 1. Principles
- Confirm diagnosis and **adherence** before calling anything treatment-resistant
  (pill counts, levels, collateral).
- **Adequate trial**: therapeutic dose for an adequate duration — commonly ~4 weeks at
  dose for antidepressants before judging non-response (partial response may justify
  longer), and ~4–6 weeks at dose for antipsychotics.
- One change at a time; measure response with a scale (PHQ-9, MADRS, PANSS, YMRS).
- Check interactions (CYP450, QTc stacking, serotonergic load) and comorbidity
  (renal, hepatic, cardiac, pregnancy/contraception).

## 2. Depression
- First line commonly an SSRI; choice by side-effect profile, interactions, prior
  response, overdose toxicity and patient preference.
- **Non-response**: increase dose (if tolerated) → switch (within or across class) →
  **augment** (e.g. second-generation antipsychotic such as aripiprazole/quetiapine,
  lithium, or combining with mirtazapine) → consider ECT/TMS/esketamine per service
  (see `health-medicine.psychiatry.neurostimulation-systems.ect-and-tms-patient-selection-and-course-management`).
- **Switching**: direct switch, taper-and-switch or cross-taper depending on agents;
  MAOI switches need washout periods; fluoxetine's long half-life matters. Use a
  switching reference table.
- **Discontinuation symptoms** are common (esp. paroxetine, venlafaxine); taper
  gradually, sometimes over months.
- Under-25s and early treatment: monitor for emergent suicidality and agitation.

## 3. Psychosis
- Choose by side-effect profile: metabolic (olanzapine, clozapine), EPS/prolactin
  (haloperidol, risperidone, amisulpride), QTc, sedation.
- **Long-acting injectables** for adherence-dependent relapse.
- **Treatment-resistant schizophrenia** (non-response to ≥ 2 adequate trials incl. one
  SGA): **clozapine** — the only drug with clear superiority. Mandatory
  **neutrophil monitoring** under the national scheme; watch myocarditis (early weeks),
  constipation/ileus (can be fatal), seizures, hypersalivation; smoking status changes
  clozapine levels substantially.
- Baseline and periodic **metabolic monitoring**: weight/BMI, waist, BP, HbA1c/glucose,
  lipids; ECG at baseline where indicated.

## 4. Bipolar disorder and mood stabilisers
- **Lithium**: narrow therapeutic index; serum level 12 h post-dose; regular
  levels, renal function, TFT, calcium. Toxicity risk rises with dehydration, NSAIDs,
  ACE inhibitors/ARBs, thiazides. Toxicity signs: coarse tremor, ataxia, confusion,
  vomiting → stop and check level urgently.
- **Valproate**: highly teratogenic — strict pregnancy-prevention requirements in
  people of childbearing potential (and evolving regulator advice regarding male
  patients); LFTs, FBC.
- **Lamotrigine**: bipolar depression prophylaxis; slow titration to reduce
  Stevens–Johnson risk; interaction with valproate (dose adjustment).
- Avoid antidepressant monotherapy in bipolar disorder (switch/rapid cycling risk).

## 5. Emergencies to recognise
| Syndrome | Clues | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Neuroleptic malignant syndrome | rigidity, fever, autonomic instability, altered consciousness, high CK | stop antipsychotic, medical emergency |
| Serotonin syndrome | clonus (inducible/ocular), hyperreflexia, agitation, hyperthermia after serotonergic change | stop agents, medical emergency |
| Lithium toxicity | as above | urgent level, renal team |
| Clozapine myocarditis | tachycardia, fever, chest pain, raised troponin/CRP in first ~8 weeks | stop, cardiology |
| Agranulocytosis | fever/sore throat on clozapine/carbamazepine | urgent FBC |

## Gotchas
- QTc prolongation stacks: antipsychotics + citalopram/escitalopram + methadone +
  ondansetron, with hypokalaemia.
- Older adults: lower starting doses, anticholinergic burden, hyponatraemia with SSRIs.
- Pregnancy: decisions are risk–risk; involve perinatal psychiatry.

## Verify
- [ ] Diagnosis and adherence confirmed before declaring non-response.
- [ ] Each trial's dose, duration and outcome measure documented.
- [ ] Switching/augmentation plan written with the reference used.
- [ ] Monitoring (metabolic, ECG, lithium/clozapine bloods) scheduled and actioned.
- [ ] Doses verified against current formulary/guideline for this patient.
