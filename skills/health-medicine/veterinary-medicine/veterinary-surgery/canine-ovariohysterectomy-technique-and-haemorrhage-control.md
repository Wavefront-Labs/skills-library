# Canine ovariohysterectomy: technique and haemorrhage control

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** a routine spay with complete ovarian removal, secure pedicles and no
ureteric injury — and a calm plan if a pedicle drops.

## 1. Pre-operative
- Timing: breed-size dependent (large/giant breeds — evidence on orthopaedic disease
  and some cancers informs later neutering; discuss with owner). Avoid surgery in
  oestrus/early pregnancy when practical (larger vessels, friable tissue).
- Clinical exam, weight, PCV/TP ± chemistry in older dogs; pre-anaesthetic plan with
  analgesia (see veterinary anaesthesia skill); urinate before surgery (small bladder).
- Ovariectomy (OVE) is an accepted alternative to OHE in healthy uterus — no increased
  pyometra risk in studies when all ovarian tissue is removed.

## 2. Approach
- Dorsal recumbency, clip/prep widely. Ventral midline incision in the **cranial third**
  of the caudal abdomen (umbilicus to ~ 1/3 toward pubis) — more cranial in deep-chested
  dogs because ovaries are cranial; cats more caudal.
- Incise the linea alba (tent with forceps to avoid viscera; the bladder/spleen lie
  close).

## 3. Locate the uterus and ovary
- Spay hook along the body wall dorsally, rotate medially, or follow the uterine body
  from the bladder neck. Confirm by following to the bifurcation.
- **Break or stretch the suspensory ligament** (index finger, close to the body wall,
  caudal traction) to exteriorise the ovary — avoid tearing the ovarian vessels.
- Make a window in the broad ligament caudal to the ovarian pedicle.

## 4. Pedicle ligation (three-clamp technique)
- Place three clamps across the pedicle **proximal to the ovary** (check the whole
  ovary is distal — look at the ovarian bursa).
- Remove the clamp **closest to the body wall** and ligate in its crush groove
  (absorbable, e.g. 2-0/0 polydioxanone or polyglactin per size); second ligature
  (transfixing or encircling) between that ligature and the next clamp.
- Transect between the remaining clamps; **check the stump for bleeding** while
  holding with thumb forceps, before releasing into the abdomen. Inspect the excised
  ovary to confirm it's complete.

## 5. Uterine body
- Ligate the uterine vessels separately in large dogs, then the uterine body
  (cranial to the cervix) with encircling + transfixing ligatures. Check for bleeding
  before release. Large or diseased uterus (pyometra) needs more care: gentle handling,
  avoid spillage, lavage if contaminated.

## 6. Dropped pedicle
- **Don't blindly clamp** — risk of ligating the ureter.
- Extend the incision cranially. Left pedicle: retract the descending colon medially to
  use its mesentery as a retractor (mesocolon). Right pedicle: retract the duodenum
  medially (mesoduodenum). Suction, find the bleeding stump, re-ligate under direct
  vision. Ureter lies dorsal and close — identify it.
- Assess blood loss; transfuse if needed.

## 7. Closure
Linea alba with a strong absorbable suture (simple continuous or interrupted, bites
~ 5–10 mm from the edge incorporating the external rectus sheath), subcutaneous layer,
skin/intradermal. Count swabs before closure.

## Complications to know
Haemorrhage (pedicle, broad ligament in oestrus), **ovarian remnant syndrome** (signs of
oestrus later — incomplete removal, usually right ovary), **stump pyometra**, ureteral
ligation, urinary incontinence (later), incisional hernia, retained swabs.

## Gotchas
- Incision too caudal → struggle to reach ovaries → tearing.
- Clamps across the ovary → remnant.
- Releasing a pedicle without checking for bleeding.
- Blind clamping in a pool of blood.

## Verify
- [ ] Whole ovary identified on each excised pedicle.
- [ ] Each stump checked for bleeding before release.
- [ ] Swab count correct before closure.
- [ ] Linea closed with holding layer included.
- [ ] Post-op analgesia, monitoring and owner instructions documented.
