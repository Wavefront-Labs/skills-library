# In-clinic haematology, blood smear and cytology

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** never act on an analyser number the smear contradicts, classify anaemia
correctly, and get diagnostic aspirates from masses and lymph nodes.

## 1. Sample handling
- Clean venepuncture (jugular for most), smooth draw — haemolysis and clotting ruin
  results. Fill **EDTA** to the correct volume (under-filling shrinks cells, alters PCV).
- **Make a blood smear within minutes** (cell morphology deteriorates in EDTA).
- Label immediately; run within the analyser's stability window.
- Analyser QC: run controls per maker schedule and log; patient results without valid QC
  are unreliable (ASVCP point-of-care QA).

## 2. Always read the smear
- **Platelets:** check the **feathered edge for clumps** — clumping is very common in cats
  → falsely low counts. Estimate: platelets per 100× oil field × ~ 15,000–20,000/µL (≥ 10–
  12/field is roughly adequate).
- **WBC:** estimate (~ 10× field count) and do a 100-cell differential; look for left
  shift (bands), toxic change, blasts, atypical lymphocytes.
- **RBC:** polychromasia, anisocytosis, **spherocytes** (dogs — immune-mediated
  haemolysis), Heinz bodies (cats — oxidant toxins, e.g. onion, paracetamol),
  parasites (Mycoplasma haemofelis, Babesia), schistocytes (DIC, haemangiosarcoma).
- **Saline agglutination test** if clumping seen: 1 drop blood + several drops saline;
  persistent agglutination = immune-mediated.

## 3. Classify anaemia
- **Regenerative vs non-regenerative** — the first branch. Reticulocyte count:
  dog > ~ 110 × 10⁹/L, cat (aggregate) > ~ 50 × 10⁹/L suggests regeneration (lab
  reference ranges vary). Takes **3–5 days** after acute loss to appear — early
  "non-regenerative" may be pre-regenerative.
- Regenerative: blood loss (check total protein — falls with loss, normal with
  haemolysis) or haemolysis (spherocytes, autoagglutination, icterus).
- Non-regenerative: chronic disease, kidney disease, marrow disease, iron deficiency
  (microcytic, hypochromic — chronic GI blood loss in dogs).

## 4. Fine-needle aspiration
- 22–25G needle; **non-aspiration ("woodpecker") technique** for vascular or lymphoid
  tissue; aspiration with 5–10 mL syringe for firm tissues. Redirect several times
  without exiting.
- Expel onto slide, **gentle squash prep** — avoid crushing (lymphoid cells rupture).
  Make several slides; air dry fast.
- Lymph nodes: sample **peripheral nodes other than mandibular** when possible
  (mandibular nodes are often reactive from oral inflammation).
- Stain with rapid Romanowsky (Diff-Quik type); **mast cell granules may stain poorly**
  with rapid stains — keep an unstained slide for the lab.

## 5. Basic cytology read
- **Cellularity and quality** (intact cells, blood contamination).
- **Inflammation:** neutrophils (degenerate → look for bacteria), macrophages,
  eosinophils.
- **Neoplasia** by cell shape: round cells (lymphoma, mast cell, histiocytoma,
  plasmacytoma, TVT), epithelial (clusters/sheets), mesenchymal (spindle, single cells,
  low yield). Criteria of malignancy: anisokaryosis, high N:C ratio, prominent/multiple
  nucleoli, mitoses.
- Lipoma: lipid droplets, slide never fully dries — but don't call every soft lump fat
  without aspirating it.
- **Send to a clinical pathologist** whenever unsure, and always for anything that
  determines surgery or prognosis.

## Gotchas
- Treating "thrombocytopenia" in a cat with clumped platelets.
- Missing spherocytes/agglutination because no smear was made.
- Calling early acute blood loss "non-regenerative".
- Aspirating only the mandibular node for lymphoma.

## Verify
- [ ] Smear made and reviewed for every CBC; platelet clumps checked.
- [ ] Analyser QC logged for the day.
- [ ] Anaemia classified with reticulocytes and total protein.
- [ ] Multiple good-quality FNA slides plus unstained slide sent when needed.
- [ ] Cytology impression stated with confidence level and referral where uncertain.
