# Reptile husbandry-led assessment and supportive care

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** find the husbandry cause behind most reptile disease, examine safely, and keep
the patient alive with heat, fluids and nutrition while the specific problem is treated.

## 1. Species first
Identify the exact species (and its natural habitat: desert, tropical forest,
temperate). Requirements differ widely — a bearded dragon, leopard gecko, ball python,
Mediterranean tortoise and red-eared slider need different temperatures, UV, humidity
and diet.

## 2. Husbandry audit (most important part of the history)
- **Enclosure:** size, substrate (impaction risk with loose sand/particles in some
  species), hides, ventilation.
- **Temperature gradient:** basking spot temp, cool end temp, night drop — measured
  with a **digital probe thermometer or IR gun**, not a stick-on dial. Heat source and
  thermostat. Keep within the species' **preferred optimal temperature zone (POTZ)**.
- **UVB:** type (T5 linear vs compact), distance, age of bulb (replace per maker,
  often 6–12 months), mesh blocking, Ferguson zone for species. Glass/plastic blocks UVB.
- **Humidity** and water provision (bathing, misting).
- **Diet:** insects (gut-loading, calcium dusting, variety), greens, whole prey for
  snakes (size, frequency), supplements and their frequency.
- Shedding history, defecation, recent brumation/hibernation, egg-laying (females
  without males can still produce eggs).

## 3. Handling and exam
- Lizards: support body, control tail-base and head; never grab tails (**autotomy** in
  geckos and many lizards). Snakes: support along length, control head in venomous/
  large species (two-person rule for large constrictors). Chelonians: hold shell sides.
- **Weigh on gram scales every visit** — drug dosing and trend.
- Exam: body condition (tail base fat in geckos, pelvic bones in dragons), hydration
  (sunken eyes, tacky mucus, skin tenting less reliable), oral cavity (stomatitis),
  vent, skin (retained shed, mites), limbs/jaw (soft jaw = MBD), coelomic palpation
  (eggs, masses).

## 4. Common presentations
- **Metabolic bone disease (nutritional secondary hyperparathyroidism):** lack of UVB
  and/or calcium; soft jaw, fractures, tremors. Fix UVB/diet; calcium per vet
  formulary; radiographs for bone density and fractures. Hypocalcaemic tremors are an
  emergency.
- **Dysecdysis (retained shed):** humidity too low, poor condition; warm soaks, humid hide;
  retained spectacles/toe rings (avascular necrosis risk).
- **Anorexia:** often low temperature, wrong season, pre-shed, gravid, or stress. Always
  correct temperature first.
- **Follicular stasis/egg retention:** gravid female without suitable laying site;
  radiograph/ultrasound; provide laying box; surgical if failing or ill.
- Respiratory disease in snakes/tortoises (open-mouth breathing, nasal discharge);
  culture, warm to upper POTZ.

## 5. Supportive care
- **Warm to upper POTZ** before treating — reptile metabolism and immune function depend
  on it; drugs work poorly in a cold patient.
- Fluids: soaks, oral, or SC/intracoelomic (e.g. ~ 10–25 mL/kg/day reptile-appropriate
  crystalloid — verify species formulary).
- Assisted feeding with recovery formula once hydrated and warm.
- Analgesia: reptiles feel pain — use species-appropriate opioid/NSAID per formulary.
- Hygiene: **Salmonella** carriage is common; hand-washing advice for owners, especially
  households with children < 5, pregnant or immunocompromised.

## Gotchas
- Treating disease while the vivarium stays too cold or UV-deficient.
- Old UVB tube or one behind glass.
- Grabbing a gecko's tail.
- Dosing by estimated weight.

## Verify
- [ ] Species identified; husbandry audit recorded with measured temperatures.
- [ ] UVB type, age, distance documented.
- [ ] Weight in grams recorded.
- [ ] Patient warmed to POTZ before drugs.
- [ ] Written husbandry corrections and Salmonella hygiene advice given.
