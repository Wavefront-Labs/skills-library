# Small-animal anaesthetic monitoring and pain scoring

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** keep a dog or cat physiologically stable from premed to full recovery and
treat pain by measuring it, not guessing.

## 1. Pre-anaesthetic assessment
- History, exam, weight (today's, in kg), **ASA physical status** (I–V, E for emergency).
  Risk rises with ASA ≥ III, extremes of size/age, and in cats and brachycephalics.
- Bloodwork per age/ASA (PCV/TP, glucose, renal at minimum in older patients).
- Fasting: short fast (e.g. 4–6 h for healthy adults; less for neonates/diabetics);
  water until premed.
- **Anaesthetic safety checklist** (adapted WHO): identity, procedure, consent, fasting,
  IV access, airway kit and tube sizes, machine leak test, oxygen, scavenging,
  emergency drug chart calculated for this weight.

## 2. Protocol logic (not doses)
- **Premedication:** sedative (e.g. alpha-2 agonist in healthy patients, acepromazine,
  or benzodiazepine in compromised) **plus opioid** (full mu agonist for painful
  procedures). Doses from a current formulary for species and ASA.
- **Induction:** to effect (propofol, alfaxalone, ketamine + benzodiazepine) — titrate.
- **Maintenance:** inhalant (isoflurane/sevoflurane) — MAC-sparing with opioid,
  lidocaine (dogs; caution in cats), ketamine CRIs, and **local/regional blocks**
  (dental, epidural, ring, incisional).

## 3. Monitoring targets (typical)
| Parameter | Target |
|---|---|
| Heart rate | Dog ~ 60–140 (size dependent), cat ~ 120–200 |
| **Blood pressure** | MAP ≥ 60–70 mmHg (or Doppler systolic ≥ 90) |
| **EtCO2** | 35–45 mmHg |
| SpO2 | ≥ 95% |
| Temperature | Prevent < 36.5 °C |
| Depth | Eye position ventromedial, absent palpebral reflex, relaxed jaw |
- Record every 5 min. A trained person dedicated to monitoring — CEPSAF showed most
  deaths in the **first 3 hours after anaesthesia**, so monitoring continues into
  recovery.

## 4. Fixing common problems
- **Hypotension:** reduce inhalant (most common cause), check depth, give crystalloid
  bolus (e.g. 5–10 mL/kg dog, smaller in cats) if hypovolaemic, treat bradycardia
  (anticholinergic if appropriate), then inotropes/pressors (dopamine, dobutamine,
  norepinephrine) per protocol.
- **Hypercapnia (EtCO2 > 50–60):** ventilate (manual or ventilator).
- **Hypoxaemia:** check tube placement/disconnection, oxygen supply, ventilation.
- **Hypothermia:** start warming at premed — forced-air warmers, warmed fluids, limit
  wet prep, cover extremities. Never use non-medical heat sources directly on skin.
- **Cats:** intubation-related tracheal injury (over-inflated cuff), laryngospasm (spray
  lidocaine on larynx), fluid overload (lower rates).

## 5. Recovery
- Continue monitoring HR, RR, temperature, SpO2 until sternal and able to swallow.
- **Brachycephalics:** extubate late (when actively swallowing/head lifting), sternal,
  airway kit ready.
- Cats: extubate earlier (before swallowing strongly) to reduce laryngospasm risk.

## 6. Pain scoring
- Dogs: **Glasgow CMPS-SF** — intervention threshold ≥ 6/24 (or ≥ 5/20 without mobility).
- Cats: **Feline Grimace Scale** (≥ 4/10 → analgesia) or Glasgow feline CMPS.
- Score at regular intervals (e.g. every 1–2 h post-op), **and after giving analgesia**
  to confirm effect. Dysphoria vs pain: reassess if unsure; a response to analgesia
  confirms pain.
- Multimodal: opioid + NSAID (if normovolaemic, normal renal function, no
  contraindication) + locals + adjuncts (ketamine, gabapentinoids for chronic).

## Gotchas
- Calculating drugs on estimated weight.
- Treating hypotension with fluids only while inhalant is too deep.
- Stopping monitoring when the tube comes out.
- Assuming a quiet cat is a comfortable cat.

## Verify
- [ ] ASA and checklist completed; emergency drugs calculated.
- [ ] 5-min monitoring record with BP, EtCO2, SpO2, temp.
- [ ] Problems and interventions documented with response.
- [ ] Recovery monitored until sternal; brachycephalic plan used.
- [ ] Pain scores recorded on a validated scale with reassessment after analgesia.
