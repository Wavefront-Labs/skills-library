# Canine and feline chronic kidney disease: IRIS staging

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** confirm CKD (not an acute or prerenal problem), stage it properly, and target
the treatments that change outcome — protein, pressure, phosphate, hydration, diet.

## 1. Confirm before staging
- IRIS staging applies to **stable CKD** in a **well-hydrated** patient — at least two
  creatinine/SDMA values over weeks, consistent.
- Rule out prerenal azotaemia (dehydration — check urine specific gravity) and acute
  kidney injury or acute-on-chronic (pyelonephritis, ureteral obstruction in cats,
  toxins, leptospirosis in dogs).
- Baseline: CBC, chemistry, **SDMA**, electrolytes, phosphate, **USG**, urinalysis +
  **urine culture**, **UPC**, **blood pressure**, abdominal imaging (kidney size, stones,
  pyelectasia), T4 in cats (hyperthyroidism masks CKD).

## 2. IRIS stage (by fasting creatinine; SDMA refines)
| Stage | Dog creatinine (µmol/L) | Cat creatinine (µmol/L) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | < 125 | < 140 |
| 2 | 125–250 | 140–250 |
| 3 | 251–440 | 251–440 |
| 4 | > 440 | > 440 |
- (mg/dL: dog < 1.4, 1.4–2.8, 2.9–5.0, > 5.0; cat < 1.6, 1.6–2.8, 2.9–5.0, > 5.0.)
- Unconfirmed detail: the canine stage 2/3 boundary above follows the 2023 IRIS revision
  as recalled here; older sources give dog stage 2 as 125–180 µmol/L (1.4–2.0 mg/dL).
  Check the current table on the IRIS website before staging.
- Persistently increased **SDMA** (> 18 µg/dL) supports CKD at low creatinine (e.g. lean
  or muscle-wasted patients); SDMA ≥ 25 (stage 2) / ≥ 38 (3) / ≥ 45 (4) suggests the
  creatinine underestimates — consider staging up. Check the current IRIS table.

## 3. Substage
- **Proteinuria (UPC)** on inactive sediment, culture negative: dog < 0.2 non-
  proteinuric, 0.2–0.5 borderline, > 0.5 proteinuric; cat < 0.2, 0.2–0.4, > 0.4.
- **Blood pressure (systolic):** < 140 normotensive; 140–159 prehypertensive;
  160–179 hypertensive; ≥ 180 severely hypertensive (target-organ damage risk —
  eyes, brain, heart, kidney). Measure properly: quiet room, acclimatisation, 5–7
  readings, same cuff/site/operator; Doppler preferred in cats.

## 4. Treatment priorities
- **Hydration:** fresh water always; wet food, water fountains; subcutaneous fluids in
  later stages when dehydration recurs.
- **Renal diet** (restricted phosphate, moderate high-quality protein, omega-3) from
  IRIS stage 2 in cats and stage 3 (or proteinuric 2) in dogs — strongest evidence for
  survival. Transition slowly; appetite first.
- **Phosphate targets:** stage 2: 0.9–1.5 mmol/L; stage 3: < 1.6; stage 4: < 1.9 —
  diet first, then binders with food.
- **Proteinuria:** RAAS blockade (e.g. telmisartan or benazepril per label dose) when
  above threshold; recheck UPC, creatinine and potassium 1–2 weeks after starting.
- **Hypertension:** amlodipine first-line in cats; ACE-I/ARB ± amlodipine in dogs.
  Target systolic < 160 (ideally < 140).
- Also: anaemia (consider ESA in late stages), nausea/appetite (maropitant,
  mirtazapine/capromorelin per species licence), potassium supplementation in
  hypokalaemic cats, metabolic acidosis.

## 5. Monitoring and communication
- Recheck every 3–6 months stage 1–2, every 1–3 months stage 3–4, and after any drug
  change. Track weight, muscle condition score, appetite.
- Explain to owners: progressive disease, quality of life focus, signs to watch
  (vomiting, inappetence, lethargy, blindness), when to call.

## Gotchas
- Staging an unstable or dehydrated patient.
- Missing hypertension because BP wasn't measured or measured in a stressed animal.
- Starting ACE-I/ARB in a dehydrated patient → acute azotaemia.
- Forgetting T4 in cats and urine culture in all.

## Verify
- [ ] Two stable values; prerenal/acute causes excluded.
- [ ] Stage plus UPC and BP substage recorded.
- [ ] Diet, phosphate, BP, proteinuria targets set with dates.
- [ ] Recheck after any RAAS drug start (creatinine, K+, UPC).
- [ ] Owner plan documented, including quality-of-life discussion.
