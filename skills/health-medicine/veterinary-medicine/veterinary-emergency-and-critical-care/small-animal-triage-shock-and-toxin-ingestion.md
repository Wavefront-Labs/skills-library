# Small-animal triage, shock and toxin ingestion

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** sort who needs treatment in the next minute, reverse shock with measured
resuscitation, and decide quickly whether and how to decontaminate a poisoning.

## 1. Triage (first 60 seconds): major body systems
- **Respiratory:** effort, rate, noise, posture (orthopnoea), mucous membrane colour.
  Upper airway noise → airway; quiet tachypnoea with crackles → oedema/pneumonia;
  short shallow → pleural space (do TFAST, thoracocentesis can be diagnostic and
  therapeutic).
- **Cardiovascular:** HR, pulse quality, mucous membranes, CRT, extremity temperature.
- **Neurological:** mentation, seizures, posture.
- **Urinary:** palpable large painful bladder in a straining male cat = urethral
  obstruction (hyperkalaemia → ECG, bradycardia).
- Get permission to treat immediately ("can we take your pet to the back?"); history
  in parallel.

## 2. Shock recognition
- **Dogs:** tachycardia, pale membranes, prolonged CRT, weak pulses, cold extremities.
- **Cats:** often **bradycardia (< 160–180), hypothermia and hypotension** — the
  triad; tachycardia is less reliable.
- Point-of-care: PCV/TP, lactate, glucose, electrolytes, blood gas, BP (Doppler).
- **AFAST** for free abdominal fluid (haemoabdomen — splenic mass, trauma), **TFAST**
  for pleural/pericardial effusion, pneumothorax.

## 3. Resuscitation
- Oxygen (flow-by or mask; avoid stressing cats/dyspnoeic animals).
- IV/IO access. Isotonic crystalloid **boluses**: dogs ~ 10–20 mL/kg over 15 min,
  **cats 5–10 mL/kg** (smaller; cats overload easily), reassess after each bolus (HR,
  pulses, BP, lactate). The old "shock dose" (90 mL/kg dog) is a ceiling, not a goal.
- Warm hypothermic cats **while** resuscitating (cardiovascular response is blunted
  when cold).
- Haemorrhage: blood products, permissive hypotension in uncontrolled bleeding until
  surgical control.
- **GDV** (large deep-chested dog, non-productive retching, distended cranial abdomen):
  right-lateral radiograph (double bubble), fluids, gastric decompression
  (trocarisation/orogastric), then surgery with gastropexy.

## 4. Toxin ingestion — decision steps
1. **What, how much, when:** product name/packaging, active ingredient, mg/kg dose,
   time since ingestion. Call an animal poison control service when unsure.
2. **Decontaminate?** Emesis is useful generally **within ~1–2 h** (longer for some
   substances like chocolate or grapes that stay in the stomach).
   - **Don't induce emesis** with: caustics/corrosives, hydrocarbons, already
     symptomatic (seizures, depressed mentation, no gag), brachycephalic risk,
     rabbits/rodents/horses (can't vomit).
   - Dogs: apomorphine; cats: alpha-2 agonist (dexmedetomidine) — per formulary.
   - **Never** salt or hydrogen peroxide in cats (hydrogen peroxide also causes gastritis
     in dogs).
   - Activated charcoal for toxins that bind (not alcohols, xylitol, metals,
     caustics); watch **hypernatraemia** with repeated doses.
3. **Common toxins and the key action:**
   - **Xylitol (dogs):** hypoglycaemia within 30–60 min, liver failure at higher doses →
     serial glucose, dextrose, liver enzymes.
   - **Chocolate (theobromine):** dose-dependent — tachyarrhythmia, seizures.
   - **Grapes/raisins:** AKI risk unpredictable (tartaric acid hypothesis) → decontaminate
     and monitor renal values 48–72 h.
   - **Anticoagulant rodenticides:** clotting fails after **2–5 days** → PT at 48–72 h or
     prophylactic vitamin K1 for weeks.
   - **Lilies (cats):** all parts (incl. pollen, vase water) cause AKI → aggressive
     fluids early.
   - **NSAIDs, paracetamol (cats: methaemoglobinaemia), permethrin (cats: tremors),
     ethylene glycol** (antidote window hours).

## Gotchas
- Fluid-overloading a cat with dog-sized boluses.
- Missing hypothermic, bradycardic shock in cats.
- Inducing emesis in a seizuring or caustic exposure.
- Discharging a rodenticide ingestion without a PT recheck plan.

## Verify
- [ ] Triage category and major body systems documented on arrival.
- [ ] Bolus volumes and reassessment endpoints recorded.
- [ ] POCUS (AFAST/TFAST) findings recorded where indicated.
- [ ] Toxin dose in mg/kg calculated; decontamination decision justified.
- [ ] Monitoring plan with recheck times (glucose, renal, PT) given to owner.
